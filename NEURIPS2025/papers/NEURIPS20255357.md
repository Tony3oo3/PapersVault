## Problem

While increasing Transformer depth correlates with better LLM performance, it remains unclear whether deeper models use their extra layers to compose higher-order computations impossible in shallow models, or merely spread the same kind of computation over more layers. Prior work showed robustness to layer skipping and no benefit of depth for compositional generalization, yet interpretability studies found multi-layer circuits, leaving a tension unresolved about how depth is actually utilized.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255357/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The analysis takes pretrained LLMs (Llama 3.1 8B/70B/405B, Qwen 3 8B/14B/32B, OLMo 2 7B/13B/32B) and text prompts from GSM8K, MATH, and MQuAKE as input, and produces per-layer contribution metrics (relative L2 norms, cosine similarities), causal intervention heatmaps (layer-skipping effects on downstream computations and future-token predictions), Logitlens KL-divergence curves, residual-erasure and integrated-gradients visualizations per token, and a scalar 'depth score' quantifying how much later layers are engaged. For the cross-model comparison, it additionally trains L₁×L₂ linear maps between every layer pair of an independently trained shallow (Qwen 2.5 1.5B) and deep (Qwen 2.5 14B) model, producing a prediction-error matrix that reveals layer correspondence.

## Method

The paper employs a suite of causal interventions on the residual stream of pre-layernorm Transformers. First, it measures the relative L2 norm and cosine similarity of each sublayer's contribution (attention and MLP outputs) against the residual, revealing a sharp phase transition at the network midpoint where layers shift from erasing to strengthening existing features. Second, it performs layer-skipping interventions (setting h_{s+1} := h_s) and residual-erasure interventions (replacing a single token's residual with a batch mean) to quantify each layer's causal influence on downstream computations and future-token predictions, showing that the second half of layers has minimal effect on future computations while remaining important for the current token's probability distribution. Third, it trains linear probes mapping every layer of a shallow model to every layer of a deeper, independently trained model, finding a diagonal pattern that indicates the deeper model simply stretches the same computation into smaller steps. Finally, it trains standard Transformers and MoEUT from scratch on DeepMind Math (with and without modeling the question) to test whether the fixed-depth behavior is an architectural or training-objective artifact, finding that MoEUT and question-free training both increase depth utilization.

## Results

Across Llama 3.1 70B, Qwen 3, and OLMo 2 models, a sharp phase transition occurs at the middle of the network: the relative L2-norm contribution of sublayers to the residual stream drops substantially in the second half, and cosine similarity between sublayer outputs and the residual shifts from negative (erasing) to positive (strengthening). Causal interventions (layer skipping) confirm that the second half of layers has near-zero effect on future-token computations, while Logitlens KL-divergence and top-5 overlap show that prediction refinement begins at the same midpoint. The 'depth score' (weighted average of layer index by importance) is flat across MATH difficulty levels and MQuAKE hop counts, indicating no adaptive depth allocation. Linear maps trained between Qwen 2.5 1.5B and 14B residuals show a clear diagonal pattern, with same-relative-depth layers mapping best to each other.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary architectural comparison is between standard pre-layernorm Transformers and MoEUT (Mixture-of-Experts Universal Transformers, Csordás et al., NeurIPS 2024), both trained from scratch on the DeepMind Math arithmetic subset. Residual erasure interventions reveal that MoEUT uses more of its depth for processing the answer, and its depth usage is more input-dependent than the Transformer's. On the extrapolation split 'Mul/Div Multiple Longer,' MoEUT achieves 63% accuracy when not modeling the question versus 36% when it is, while the standard Transformer goes from 48% to 41%—a 27-point gap for MoEUT versus only 7 points for the Transformer. All models trained from scratch (both architectures) show increased depth usage with deeper computation steps, in contrast to the fixed-depth behavior observed in pretrained LLMs like Llama 3.1. The authors note that fine-tuning a pretrained Llama 3.2 3B on the same data does not substantially change its fixed-depth computation pattern, suggesting the behavior is baked in during pretraining rather than being a simple fine-tuning artifact.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on layer robustness and interpretability in Transformers (Lad et al. on stages of inference, Gromov et al. on removing half the layers, Sun et al. on layer swapping) by asking a sharper question: do deeper models perform genuinely novel compositional computation, or merely spread the same computation over more layers? It repurposes established causal intervention tools (layer skipping, residual erasure, integrated gradients) and Logitlens to probe this question systematically, and introduces a linear-projection correspondence test between independently trained shallow and deep models as a new diagnostic. The exploratory comparison with MoEUT positions the findings against an alternative architecture that may use depth more adaptively.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that the second half of layers primarily refines the output distribution rather than building reusable sub-results, and that computation depth is independent of problem complexity, directly challenges the core motivation for scaling depth in stacked Transformers and may help explain diminishing returns. It also casts doubt on latent-thinking approaches that assume the residual stream can be used for iterative compositional reasoning. Key open questions include whether the behavior is an artifact of the pretraining objective or the architecture itself, whether the residual width is the true bottleneck, and whether fine-tuning or architectural changes (e.g., MoEUT, mixed layernorm) can unlock deeper compositional use of layers.
