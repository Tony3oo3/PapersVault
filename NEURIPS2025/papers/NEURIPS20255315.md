## Problem

Current multimodal reward models are limited to direct scoring or shallow reasoning, producing unreliable reward signals in complex scenarios due to the absence of structured, multi-dimensional analysis. Training such reasoning via supervised fine-tuning is impractical because large-scale CoT reward annotations are scarce and expensive to produce manually.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255315/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For generation tasks, the model receives an image or video pair alongside a text prompt (instruction + caption); for understanding tasks, it receives an image or video, a query, and two candidate textual answers. The output is a structured CoT trace in which each candidate is independently scored along task-specific dimensions (e.g., semantic consistency, aesthetics, authenticity for generation; semantic accuracy, factual correctness, clarity for understanding), with dimension-wise scores aggregated arithmetically into a final preference judgment. This multidimensional scoring enforces alignment between intermediate reasoning and the final decision, addressing the common failure mode where reasoning appears implausible despite a correct prediction.

## Method

Built on the UnifiedReward VLM (LLaVA-OneVision backbone), the paper proposes a three-stage exploration-driven training pipeline to elicit latent long-CoT reasoning without large-scale CoT annotation. Stage 1 (Cold Start) distills ~5K CoT reward traces from GPT-4o on image generation preference data to teach the model the reasoning format and structure. Stage 2 (Rejection Sampling) prompts the model on large-scale unified multimodal preference data spanning image/video generation and understanding, retaining only correctly predicted trajectories for supervised fine-tuning to reinforce accurate reasoning patterns. Stage 3 (GRPO) applies Group Relative Policy Optimization on the remaining incorrectly reasoned samples, using verifiable format and accuracy rewards to guide the model toward correct reasoning through trial-and-error exploration. A key design choice is the multidimensional CoT scoring strategy, which decomposes evaluation into per-dimension scores that sum to the final judgment, ensuring that verifying the final answer implicitly validates the entire reasoning chain.

## Results

On VLRewardBench (image understanding), UnifiedReward-Think achieves 73.8% overall accuracy and 72.3% macro accuracy, surpassing the base UnifiedReward (67.5%/66.6%) by +6.3/+5.7 points, and outperforming GPT-4o (65.8%) and Gemini-1.5-Pro (67.2%) by 8+ points. On GenAI-Bench image generation, it reaches 72.5% diff accuracy vs. 70.9% for UnifiedReward and 68.4% for HPSv2. On video generation, it achieves 82.3% diff accuracy on GenAI-Bench and 80.5% on VideoGen-RewardBench, exceeding the next-best baseline (UnifiedReward at 77.2%/79.3%) by 5+ points. Notably, even without explicit CoT traces (w/o CoT), the model still outperforms all baselines, confirming internalized implicit reasoning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UnifiedReward-Think outperforms all 10 baselines across every benchmark. Against the base model UnifiedReward, CoT reasoning yields +6.3 points overall accuracy on VLRewardBench (73.8 vs. 67.5), +1.6 on image generation diff (72.5 vs. 70.9), and +5.1 on video generation diff (82.3 vs. 77.2). Against general-purpose LLMs, it beats GPT-4o by 8.0 points and Gemini-1.5-Pro by 6.6 points on VLRewardBench overall accuracy. Among specialized reward models, it surpasses the strongest image-generation baseline HPSv2 (68.4) by 4.1 points and the strongest video-generation baseline VideoReward (73.3/73.9) by 9.0/6.6 points on GenAI-Bench/VideoGen-RewardBench. The hallucination sub-task shows the largest relative gain (+14.6 over UnifiedReward, 72.7 vs. 58.1), suggesting CoT reasoning is most beneficial where fine-grained visual verification is needed. Even in the w/o-CoT setting, the model retains a 5.6-point advantage over UnifiedReward on VLRewardBench, validating the implicit reasoning hypothesis.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends UnifiedReward (Wang et al., 2025d), a unified multimodal reward model that handles both image/video generation and understanding tasks, by introducing long chain-of-thought reasoning into the reward decision process. The training methodology draws directly from DeepSeek-R1's GRPO framework (Guo et al., 2025), repurposing group-relative policy optimization with verifiable rewards from the LLM reasoning domain to the multimodal reward modeling setting. The key conceptual departure from prior reward models—which provide direct scores or shallow justifications—is the hypothesis that VLMs possess latent long-chain reasoning that can be elicited through a staged pipeline (cold-start distillation → rejection sampling → GRPO) rather than requiring large-scale manually annotated CoT reward data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that CoT reasoning in reward models yields both more accurate and more interpretable reward signals, and notably that the model retains improved performance even when CoT traces are suppressed at inference time, suggesting genuine internalization of reasoning structure. However, the authors acknowledge (citing Yue et al., 2025) that RL can only amplify capabilities already present in the base model, meaning the approach's ceiling is bounded by the VLM's pre-trained reasoning capacity. Open questions include whether scaling high-quality CoT supervision can push beyond this ceiling, and whether the multidimensional scoring strategy generalizes to reward tasks with more than two candidates or to open-ended generation evaluation.
