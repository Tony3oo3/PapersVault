## Problem

In depthwise-separable convolutions, the 1×1 pointwise convolution responsible for channel interaction suffers from quadratic parameter complexity (C²) and a limited receptive field, constraining the global expressiveness of lightweight CNNs. Prior discrete nmODE approaches have been applied to segmentation decoders but have not been generalized into a standalone lightweight backbone applicable across diverse vision tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254034/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The network consumes standard RGB images at task-specific resolutions (32×32 for CIFAR, 64×64 for Tiny-ImageNet, 256×256 for ImageNet-R) and outputs class labels, bounding boxes, or pixel-wise segmentation masks depending on the downstream head. Internally, the COS module operates on feature tensors of shape C×H×W, performing channelwise computation independently at each spatial location using a compact √C×√C transformation matrix rather than a full C×C weight matrix, which is the structural source of the parameter reduction.

## Method

The core contribution is the Channelwise ODE Solver (COS) module, which replaces the pointwise convolution in depthwise-separable blocks with a discrete neural-memory ODE solved via a novel Direct Differentiation Euler (DDE) algorithm. The DDE introduces per-layer learnable time increments Δt (initialized from a Normal distribution and clamped by ReLU6), allowing the solver to adaptively refine temporal resolution during training rather than relying on a fixed 1/L step size. Channel interaction is decomposed into a linear transformation via a compact √C×√C matrix Φ followed by L sequential DDE updates that map each channel vector toward the nmODE's global attractor, converting quadratic C² complexity into linear C·L cost. Two block variants are designed: COS-base (a direct drop-in replacement for MobileNetV1's pointwise conv) and COS-inv (an inverted-residual block where COS handles both channel expansion and projection, with bilinear interpolation enabling parameter-free scaling). The resulting MobileODEV1 and MobileODEV2 reduce channel-interaction parameters by 71.0% and 69.2% respectively while achieving higher accuracy than their MobileNet baselines across classification, detection, and segmentation.

## Results

MobileODEV1 (1.14M params) achieves 93.56% top-1 accuracy on CIFAR-10, 74.98% on CIFAR-100, 44.55% on ImageNet-R, and 61.92% on Tiny-ImageNet, outperforming MobileNetV1 (3.41M) on all four datasets with 66.6% fewer parameters. MobileODEV2 (1.52M) reaches 94.01% on CIFAR-10 and 46.59% on ImageNet-R, surpassing MobileNetV2 (2.48M) with 38.7% fewer parameters. The best variant, MobileODEV2+ViT (3.65M), achieves the highest accuracy across all four classification benchmarks (94.26%, 75.89%, 48.08%, 63.56%), exceeding MobileViTV2 (18.45M) by 1.34% on CIFAR-10 and 1.36% on ImageNet-R while using 80.2% fewer parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MobileODEV1 (1.14M) outperforms its direct baseline MobileNetV1 (3.41M) on all four classification datasets—by +0.39% on CIFAR-10, +0.21% on CIFAR-100, +2.32% on ImageNet-R, and +1.14% on Tiny-ImageNet—while using 66.6% fewer parameters. MobileODEV2 (1.52M) beats MobileNetV2 (2.48M) on CIFAR-10 (+0.24%), CIFAR-100 (+0.70%), and ImageNet-R (+3.58%), though it trails slightly on Tiny-ImageNet (63.46% vs. 63.53%). Against ShuffleNetV2 1.5× (2.69M), MobileODEV2 wins on CIFAR-10 (+1.21%), CIFAR-100 (+3.04%), and Tiny-ImageNet (+2.83%) but loses on ImageNet-R (46.59% vs. 47.24%). The ViT-enhanced variants dominate the transformer-based baselines: MobileODEV2+ViT (3.65M) surpasses MobileViTV2 (18.45M) by +4.93% on CIFAR-10, +10.11% on CIFAR-100, +1.36% on ImageNet-R, and +2.74% on Tiny-ImageNet, while MobileODEV1+ViT (2.82M) beats MobileViT (5.58M) by +4.16% on CIFAR-10 and +10.33% on CIFAR-100. MobileODEV2+ViT also edges out MobileOne-s0 (4.47M) on CIFAR-10 (+0.34%), CIFAR-100 (+0.31%), and ImageNet-R (+2.63%) with 18.3% fewer parameters.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the discrete nmODEs framework (Yi, 2023) from its prior applications in U-shaped segmentation decoders (He et al., 2024; Wang et al., 2024b) and bidirectional networks (Niu et al., 2024) to a general-purpose MobileNet-like backbone. It directly modifies MobileNetV1's depthwise-separable convolution by replacing the 1×1 pointwise convolution with a Channelwise ODE Solver (COS) built on a novel Direct Differentiation Euler (DDE) algorithm, and adapts MobileNetV2's inverted residual structure into a COS-inv block. The key departure from prior discrete nmODE work is the introduction of learnable, per-layer time increments (Δt) rather than the fixed 1/L step size used in all previous discretizations, which the authors show is critical for stability at depth. The MobileViT block is incorporated as an optional self-attention extension, following the hybrid CNN-Transformer trend of FastViT and RepViT.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~70% reduction in channel-interaction parameters while matching or exceeding baseline accuracy across classification, detection, and segmentation offers a concrete, drop-in alternative to pointwise convolutions for resource-constrained deployment, as demonstrated by the edge-computing breast-screening demo. However, the sequential nature of the L-layer DDE computation limits parallelism, capping practical L at 10 and constraining further accuracy gains; the authors acknowledge that indiscriminate increases in L hurt inference speed. Open questions include whether NAS (as in MobileNetV3/V4) can co-optimize the number of discrete layers and channel widths, and whether the bilinear-interpolation-based channel expansion in COS-inv can be replaced with more expressive (but still lightweight) alternatives to avoid the accuracy cliff observed at e≥9.
