## Problem

Existing contrastive learning methods for time series rely on hard negative sampling or uniform temporal decay functions, which fail to capture inherent physical correlations across sequences and cannot adapt to the varying non-stationarity and rates of change present in different sequences. Standard similarity metrics (cosine, Euclidean) overlook spectral structure, while DTW is computationally prohibitive and noise-sensitive for large-scale iterative training.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multimodal time series sensor data (acoustic, seismic, IMU) segmented into fixed-length windows of L readings across C channels, formatted as X_i ∈ R^{T×L×C}. For similarity computation, samples are transformed into time-frequency representations via STFT. The output is an H-dimensional embedding per sample, used for downstream classification via a linear probe appended to a Swin Transformer backbone that processes spectrogram patches.

## Method

AdaTS is a pluggable module with three components: (1) Time-Frequency Coherence (TFC) computes pairwise similarity via normalized cross-spectral density in the STFT domain, providing a physics-guided, FFT-efficient alternative to DTW; (2) an ordinal consistency loss enforces relative ordering of TFC-derived similarities through triplet margin constraints rather than absolute value matching, making it robust to noise and context-dependent metric distortions; (3) a dynamic temporal assignment mechanism computes a per-sequence dissimilarity statistic κ^n (mean negative log of adjacent-sample similarity) to modulate the temporal decay rate in the contrastive loss, so that rapidly changing sequences receive faster similarity decay while smoother sequences retain longer-range temporal structure. The combined AdaTS loss (λ_oc·L_OC + λ_t·L_T) is added to the base SSL framework's original objective, requiring only 3–7% additional pretraining overhead.

## Results

AdaTS, as a pluggable module, improves average contrastive learning accuracy by 7.3% (up to 13.7%) across four IoT datasets (ACIDS, MOD, PAMAP2, RWHAR). The largest gain comes from integrating AdaTS with TS2Vec (0.6817 → 0.8189 avg accuracy, +13.7%), while FOCAL+AdaTS achieves the best absolute performance at 0.9363 avg accuracy (+2.1% over FOCAL alone). On the UCR/UEA benchmark archives (125 univariate + 29 multivariate datasets), AdaTS-augmented TS2Vec consistently outperforms SoftCLT, though gains are smaller due to more stationary patterns. AdaTS introduces only 3–7% pretraining overhead and no additional inference cost.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AdaTS consistently outperforms all three SoftCLT-augmented baselines (TS2Vec+SoftCLT, TS-TCC+SoftCLT, FOCAL+SoftCLT) across all four datasets, with the largest margin on TS2Vec (0.8189 vs. 0.7630 avg accuracy, +5.6%). Against the strongest SSL baselines, FOCAL+AdaTS (0.9363 avg Acc) surpasses TNC (0.8429), CMC (0.8167), MAE (0.8089), and SimCLR (0.7092) by 9–23 percentage points. On the UCR/UEA archives, AdaTS-augmented TS2Vec outperforms SoftCLT across all 154 datasets, though the margin is smaller (typically 1–3%) since these benchmarks exhibit more stationary patterns. The supervised Informer baseline (0.9125 avg Acc) remains competitive but is outperformed by FOCAL+AdaTS (0.9363), demonstrating that AdaTS-enhanced SSL can match or exceed supervised approaches. Notably, AdaTS provides larger relative gains on simpler HAR datasets (PAMAP2, RWHAR) where ordinal consistency captures fine-grained activity similarities, while on high-dynamics vehicle datasets (ACIDS, MOD) the dynamic temporal assignment mechanism drives the improvement.

## Relation to Prior Work

AdaTS extends the soft contrastive learning line initiated by SoftCLT (Lee et al., ICLR 2024), which introduced temporal similarity decay for time series CL, by replacing its single global decay parameter with a per-sequence dynamic rate derived from adjacent-sample dissimilarity. It also departs from prior work that enforces absolute similarity alignment (e.g., DTW-based semantic matching) by proposing an ordinal consistency loss that preserves only the relative ordering of physical similarities, and from DTW-based similarity metrics by substituting Time-Frequency Coherence for its computational efficiency and noise robustness. The framework is explicitly positioned as a pluggable module atop existing CL backbones (TS2Vec, TS-TCC, FOCAL) rather than a standalone architecture, building on the broader self-supervised time series representation learning literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The pluggable design and the 13.7% average improvement on TS2Vec make AdaTS a practical drop-in enhancement for IoT time series CL pipelines, particularly in label-scarce and noisy deployment settings where the ordinal consistency and dynamic temporal assignment mechanisms provide additional supervisory signal. The finding that relative ordering of similarities is more robust than absolute values under noise and non-stationarity offers a useful design principle for future similarity-aware contrastive objectives. Open questions include whether the STFT-based TFC assumption of local stationarity limits performance on highly transient signals (where wavelets showed modest advantages), and whether the ordinal consistency oversampling problem can be addressed more fundamentally than through post-hoc distance regularization.
