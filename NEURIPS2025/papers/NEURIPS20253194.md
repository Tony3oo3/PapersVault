## Problem

Optimizing downstream reward functions in pre-trained diffusion models typically requires either differentiable proxy models (as in classifier guidance) or computationally expensive fine-tuning (as in RL-based or classifier-free guidance). This limits the use of non-differentiable reward feedback common in scientific domains (e.g., molecular docking scores, physics-based simulations) and prevents principled application to discrete diffusion models. The paper asks whether reward-optimized sampling can be achieved at inference time without either differentiability or fine-tuning.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-trained diffusion model (continuous, e.g., Stable Diffusion v1.5 with T=50 steps, or discrete masked diffusion with T=128), a non-differentiable reward function r: X→R, and optionally a conditioning signal (e.g., text prompt). At each denoising step it draws M candidate samples (M=10–20 in experiments) from the pre-trained denoising policy and selects one via importance-weighted categorical sampling. The output is a batch of N generated samples (images at 512×512, molecules as SMILES/graphs, or DNA/RNA sequences) that maximize the reward while remaining close to the pre-trained distribution, with naturalness measured by CLIP score or ELBO-based log-likelihood.

## Method

SVDD integrates soft value functions—defined as v_{t-1}(x) = α log E[exp(r(x_0)/α) | x_{t-1}]—into the standard diffusion inference loop. At each denoising step, it generates M samples from the pre-trained policy, assigns importance weights w = exp(v̂_{t-1}(x)/α), and selects one sample via categorical sampling, thereby approximating the value-weighted optimal policy p*_{t-1} without computing its intractable normalizer. The value function is estimated by posterior mean approximation (evaluating r on the pre-trained model's x̂_0 prediction, requiring no additional training) or Monte Carlo regression. Theoretically, SVDD is an instantiation of nested importance-sampling SMC, which avoids the unstable ratio-of-value-functions computation required by standard SMC. An enhanced variant, SVDD-R, adds a global resampling step that replaces low-value samples within the batch with high-value ones, reallocating compute to more promising trajectories at no additional forward-pass cost.

## Results

SVDD and its enhanced variant SVDD-R consistently achieve the highest reward scores across all six evaluation domains (image compressibility, image aesthetics, molecule QED/SA/docking, DNA enhancer activity, and RNA 5'UTR translational efficiency). For example, on molecule docking to Parp1, SVDD-R reaches a 50% quantile of 13.61 versus 7.15 for the pre-trained model and 10.00 for Best-of-N. On enhancer activity (HepG2), SVDD-R achieves a 50% quantile of 6.782 compared to 0.121 for the pre-trained model. Naturalness metrics (CLIP score for images, log-likelihood for molecules/sequences) remain reasonably preserved, with SVDD maintaining CLIP scores of 28.9–29.0 and log-likelihoods within ~100 of the pre-trained baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SVDD outperforms both DPS (Chung et al., 2022) and SMC-based methods (Wu et al., 2024) across all six domains on reward metrics. Against DPS, the gains are particularly large in scientific domains: on molecule docking to Parp1, SVDD achieves 11.40 (50% quantile) versus 7.35 for DPS, and on enhancer activity, SVDD reaches 5.353 versus 3.782 for DPS. Against SMC, SVDD also wins consistently: on molecule QED, SVDD scores 0.848 versus 0.667 for SMC, and on 5'UTR translational efficiency, SVDD achieves 1.214 versus 0.76 for SMC. The enhanced SVDD-R variant further improves upon SVDD in nearly all settings (e.g., 13.61 vs. 11.40 on Parp1 docking, 6.782 vs. 5.353 on enhancers) without additional forward passes. Notably, DPS and SMC struggle most in domains with non-differentiable rewards (molecule docking, compressibility), where SVDD's derivative-free design provides the largest relative advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

SVDD converts the soft value function and soft optimal policy framework from Uehara et al. (2024c)—originally developed to *interpret* RL-based fine-tuning of diffusion models—into a fine-tuning-free, inference-time algorithm. The algorithmic backbone is nested importance-sampling SMC (Naesseth et al., 2019), which the paper distinguishes from standard SMC guidance methods (Wu et al., 2024) by avoiding the unstable ratio of value functions and enabling per-sample parallelization. Conceptually, SVDD departs from classifier guidance (DPS, Chung et al. 2022) by eliminating the differentiability requirement, and from RL-based fine-tuning (Black et al. 2023, Fan et al. 2023) by operating entirely at inference time without modifying model weights.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The derivative-free, fine-tuning-free nature of SVDD is practically significant for scientific domains where reward functions (docking scores, physics-based simulations, biological activity assays) are inherently non-differentiable and where fine-tuning large foundation models is prohibitive. The unified treatment of continuous and discrete diffusion models addresses a gap that gradient-based guidance methods cannot fill. Key limitations include the M× computational or memory overhead relative to standard inference, and the observation that SVDD-R's CLIP scores on some image tasks appear anomalously high, suggesting potential reward-hacking that warrants further scrutiny. Open directions include protein sequence optimization, 3D molecule generation, and scaling to larger pre-trained models.
