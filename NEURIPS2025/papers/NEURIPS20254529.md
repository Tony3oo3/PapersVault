## Problem

Enabling LLMs to effectively seek accurate knowledge in complex multi-hop tasks remains challenging because (1) directly issuing a single complex query yields low retrieval coverage, and (2) even simple one-hop retrieval returns irrelevant content that misleads reasoning. Prior approaches cascade information-seeking pipelines and train LLMs for specific stages independently, but aligning retrieval, evidence extraction, and answer generation under a unified end-to-end objective has remained an open problem.

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural-language question and a Wikipedia passage corpus (Dec 2018 dump, retrieved via ColBERTv2 with top-K=5 documents per sub-query) as input. It produces a final short-span answer grounded in external evidence, along with an explicit search trajectory: a sequential sequence of (sub-query, retrieved documents, extracted evidence) tuples that records the full reasoning-and-retrieval process. The trajectory length is variable, bounded by a maximum step T, and terminates when the LLM emits an End token.

## Method

EXSEARCH formalizes three interleaved agentic actions—thinking (generating a sub-query from the evolving trajectory), search (triggering an external retriever), and recording (extracting fine-grained evidence from retrieved documents)—and trains the LLM to perform them end-to-end via a generalized expectation-maximization algorithm. In the E-step, the current LLM samples multiple search trajectories and assigns each an importance weight proportional to p(y|z,x;θ), reflecting how well the trajectory supports the correct answer. In the M-step, the LLM is updated by maximizing a re-weighted ELBO that decomposes into a learning-to-search loss (over sub-queries and evidence) and a learning-to-answer loss (over the final response), both weighted by the importance scores. This creates a self-incentivized loop where the model iteratively improves from its own generated trajectories, and the paper proves non-decreasing convergence of the objective across EM iterations.

## Results

EXSEARCH with Qwen-2.5-7B achieves an average F1 of 51.45, EM of 43.02, and Accuracy of 46.20 across four knowledge-intensive benchmarks (NQ, HotpotQA, MuSiQue, 2WikiQA), substantially outperforming GPT-4o (Avg F1 45.82) and LLaMA-3.3-70B (Avg F1 43.90) which rely solely on parametric knowledge. On HotpotQA, EXSEARCH reaches F1 62.59, surpassing RankRAG-70B (55.40) and Search-o1-32B (53.31) by 7.19 and 9.28 points respectively. The largest backbone tested (Mistral-2501-24B) pushes average F1 to 55.89 and EM to 44.75, demonstrating consistent scaling. In retrieval recall (Table 2), EXSEARCH achieves R@5 of 66.94 on HotpotQA and 40.16 on MuSiQue, far exceeding ColBERTv2 alone (51.79 and 17.91) and re-ranking methods like BGE (55.89 and 20.23).

## Baselines & Comparisons

EXSEARCH (Qwen-2.5-7B) outperforms all 26 baselines on average F1 across the four benchmarks. Against the strongest direct-reasoning LLMs, it gains +5.63 F1 over GPT-4o and +7.55 F1 over LLaMA-3.3-70B, despite using a 7B backbone versus 70B+ parameter models. Among advanced RAG methods, EXSEARCH surpasses RankRAG-70B by +7.19 F1 on HotpotQA (62.59 vs. 55.40) and by a wide margin on MuSiQue where RankRAG reports no results. Among iterative RAG baselines, EXSEARCH beats Search-R1 (trained with PPO) by +2.71 average F1 (51.45 vs. 48.74) while using a simpler EM-based self-incentivization loop rather than reinforcement learning. It also outperforms Search-o1-32B by +7.31 F1 on HotpotQA and GenGround by +6.41 F1 on HotpotQA. The only baseline that approaches EXSEARCH on individual metrics is Search-R1 on NQ F1 (54.26 vs. 56.37), but EXSEARCH still leads on EM (47.07 vs. 42.21) and all other datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper applies the generalized expectation-maximization (GEM) algorithm to the agentic search setting, treating search trajectories as latent variables in a variational framework. It departs from prior iterative RAG methods (Search-o1, Search-R1, Self-RAG) that either rely on reinforcement learning (PPO) for training or cascade separately optimized stages, by unifying thinking, searching, and recording into a single self-incentivized EM loop with importance-weighted trajectory learning. The conceptual lineage draws from variational inference and importance sampling in probabilistic modeling, repurposing these tools for LLM-based retrieval agents rather than traditional latent variable models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a theoretically grounded, simpler alternative to PPO-based training for agentic search, showing that a self-incentivized EM loop with convergence guarantees can match or exceed RL-trained methods like Search-R1. This is practically significant because it reduces the training complexity and computational cost of building agentic searchers. Key limitations include text-only operation, fixed retrieval at every reasoning step regardless of necessity, and reliance on rule-based answer-centric metrics that may not capture open-ended performance. Open questions include extending to multimodal retrieval, adaptive retrieval triggering, and whether the EM framework generalizes to tool-augmented agents beyond document search.
