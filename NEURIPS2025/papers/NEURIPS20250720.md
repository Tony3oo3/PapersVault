## Problem

Standard sequential modeling approaches assume stationary, regularly sampled data, yet real-world sensor data (e.g., UWB-based digital key trajectories) is frequently non-stationary and irregularly sampled, degrading model performance. Existing solutions for these challenges require substantial domain-specific model selection, and prior contrastive learning frameworks for time series are task-agnostic, decoupling representation learning from the downstream objective and thereby overlooking task-specific information.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250720/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts univariate or multivariate time series sequences of variable length N with optional per-element timestamps to capture irregular sampling; for irregularly sampled clinical data, static attributes are encoded via RoBERTa and concatenated with the latent embedding before the output head. The Siamese architecture simultaneously produces a masked and an unmasked latent representation per input, which are consumed by the contrastive loss, while the unmasked tower feeds a task-specific output head yielding sequence-level class labels, per-element anomaly labels, or scalar regression targets depending on the formulation.

## Method

STaRFormer is a Siamese network of N shared-parameter encoder-only Transformer blocks where the left tower performs the downstream task and the right tower reconstructs a dynamically masked version of the input. The key masking mechanism, DAReM, aggregates attention weights across all layers via attention rollout to compute per-element importance scores, then masks a region of size γ around the top-ζ most important elements (capped at φ fraction of the sequence), introducing synthetic non-stationarity and irregular sampling. A novel semi-supervised contrastive loss fuses a batch-wise (self-supervised) NT-Xent term with a class-wise (supervised) term, maximizing agreement between masked and unmasked embeddings both within the batch and within the same class, with three formulations covering sequence-level, element-level, and regression tasks. The total training objective is the sum of the task loss and a weighted fused contrastive loss (λ_CL · L_STAR-CL), and the approach is validated across 56 datasets spanning non-stationary spatiotemporal, irregularly sampled, and regular time series for classification, anomaly detection, and regression.

## Results

On the UEA multivariate time series benchmark (30 datasets), STaRFormer achieves 0.795 average accuracy (rank 1), 9 top scores, and an average rank of 2.8, improving over the previous state-of-the-art (ViTST, 0.790) by 0.5 percentage points. On the non-stationary DKT dataset, STaRFormer reaches 0.852 accuracy, outperforming TARNet (0.781) and TimesURL (0.724) by 7.1 and 12.8 percentage points respectively. On irregularly sampled PAM data, STaRFormer achieves 97.6% accuracy and 97.4% F1, surpassing ViTST (95.8% / 96.5%). On the TSR regression benchmark (19 datasets), STaRFormer attains the best average relative mean difference (−0.254) and 9 top scores out of 19.

## Baselines & Comparisons

STaRFormer outperforms all 16 baselines on the UEA benchmark, with the closest competitor being ViTST (0.790 vs. 0.795 avg. accuracy) and TARNet (0.755). On the non-stationary DKT dataset, STaRFormer (0.852) substantially surpasses TARNet (0.781) and TimesURL (0.724), while only marginally beating the plain Transformer (0.849) and LSTM (0.844). On Geolife, STaRFormer (0.932) exceeds the previous best ST-GRU (0.913) by 1.9 points. For irregularly sampled data (PAM), STaRFormer (97.6% acc., 97.4% F1) leads ViTST (95.8% / 96.5%) and Raindrop (88.5% / 89.8%) by a wide margin. On anomaly detection, STaRFormer achieves 0.789 F1 on Yahoo and 0.830 F1 on KPI, beating TimesURL (0.749 / 0.688) and TS2Vec (0.745 / 0.677). On the TSR regression benchmark, STaRFormer ranks first with 9 top scores, ahead of Rocket (7 top scores) and Inception (3 top scores).

## Relation to Prior Work

STaRFormer generalizes TARNet's dynamic masking scheme by replacing fixed top-k element masking with attention-rollout-guided regional masking (DAReM) that perturbs statistical properties around task-important regions. It departs from the dominant self-supervised pretraining paradigm for time series (TST, TimesURL, TS2Vec, InfoTS) by coupling representation learning directly with the downstream task through a semi-supervised contrastive objective that fuses batch-wise (self-supervised) and class-wise (supervised) NT-Xent losses. The Siamese architecture with shared encoder parameters and the use of attention rollout for importance scoring are inherited from prior work, but the specific combination of regional masking with dual-granularity contrastive learning during downstream training is novel.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap where real-world sequential data (automotive UWB trajectories, clinical signals) is frequently non-stationary and irregularly sampled, yet most strong baselines assume stationarity and regular intervals. The task-coupled design is a double-edged sword: it embeds task-specific information for better downstream performance but sacrifices the flexibility of universal representation learners like TimesURL, meaning the model must be retrained per task. Open questions include whether a task-agnostic variant is feasible, how the O(N²) attention-based masking scales to very long sequences, and whether the semi-supervised CL benefit persists in truly low-data regimes beyond what the UEA ablation suggests.
