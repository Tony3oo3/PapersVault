## Problem

Existing decentralized bilevel optimization methods require problem-specific hyperparameters—such as smoothness constants, strong convexity parameters, and the spectral gap of the communication graph—to set stepsizes, yet these quantities are typically unavailable in practice due to privacy constraints or computational infeasibility. This paper asks whether a single-loop decentralized bilevel algorithm can be designed that is entirely free of such problem-specific parameters while still matching the convergence rates of well-tuned state-of-the-art methods.

## Input / Output

Each of n agents holds a local nonconvex upper-level function f_i and a strongly convex lower-level function l_i, and communicates only with neighbors over a fixed doubly stochastic graph. The algorithm requires no knowledge of Lipschitz constants, strong convexity parameters, or graph spectral properties—only positive control coefficients (γ_x, γ_y, γ_v) and initial accumulator values. The output is the network-average primal variable x̄_T, which is an ε-accurate stationary point of the global upper-level objective Φ(x) after O(ε^{-1} log^4(1/ε)) iterations.

## Method

AdaSDBO employs AdaGrad-style adaptive stepsizes where each variable's stepsize is inversely proportional to the square root of its accumulated gradient norm, but with a hierarchical structure that respects bilevel interdependencies: the dual variable y uses stepsize 1/m^y, the auxiliary variable v uses 1/max(m^v, m^y), and the primal variable x uses the more conservative 1/(m^x · max(m^v, m^y)). To handle the decentralized setting, a lightweight stepsize-tracking mechanism synchronizes the scalar gradient-norm accumulators across agents via the communication graph at each iteration, bounding stepsize discrepancies without exchanging full-dimensional tracker states. The algorithm solves the three coupled subproblems (upper-level x, lower-level y, and Hessian-inverse auxiliary v) in a single loop using local gradients and neighbor averaging, achieving a convergence rate of Õ(1/T) that matches well-tuned methods up to a log^4(T) factor while requiring zero problem-specific hyperparameters.

## Results

AdaSDBO achieves a convergence rate of Õ(1/T) (specifically O(log⁴(T)/T)) and gradient complexity of O(1/ε · log⁴(1/ε)) for finding an ε-accurate stationary point, matching the state-of-the-art single-loop methods (AID, SLDBO) up to polylogarithmic factors. The key distinguishing result is that AdaSDBO requires zero problem-specific parameters (no μ, L, ρ_W, or β), whereas all competing methods require at least 2–4 such constants. In experiments, AdaSDBO converges faster than double-loop baselines (DBO, MA-DSBO) on synthetic data (p=50 and p=200) and maintains stable performance across stepsizes ranging from 10⁻³ to 10², a range far wider than any baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AdaSDBO matches the convergence rate O(1/T) and gradient complexity O(1/ε) of the best single-loop baselines AID [Ji et al., 2022] and SLDBO [Dong et al., 2023], up to a log⁴(T) factor, while eliminating all problem-specific parameter requirements. It strictly outperforms the centralized single-loop method FSLA [Li et al., 2022a] in both convergence rate (O(1/T) vs. O(1/√T)) and gradient complexity (O(1/ε) vs. O(1/ε²)). Against double-loop decentralized methods DBO [Chen et al., 2024a] and MDBO [Gao et al., 2023], AdaSDBO achieves the same or better convergence rate with significantly lower gradient complexity (O(1/ε) vs. O(1/ε²·log(1/ε))). In empirical experiments, AdaSDBO converges faster than DBO and MA-DSBO on synthetic data and achieves competitive test accuracy on MNIST and FMNIST across varying numbers of agents. On CIFAR-10 decentralized meta-learning, AdaSDBO achieves notably better training accuracy than SLDBO. The most striking practical advantage is stepsize robustness: AdaSDBO maintains stable performance over stepsizes from 10⁻³ to 10², whereas baselines exhibit much narrower stable regions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of decentralized bilevel optimization algorithms (DBO, MA-DSBO, MDBO, SLDBO) by replacing their fixed or problem-parameter-dependent stepsizes with AdaGrad-style accumulated gradient norms, making the first fully parameter-free single-loop decentralized bilevel method. The adaptive stepsize mechanism draws on the two-stage analysis framework of Ward et al. (2020) and Xie et al. (2020) for adaptive methods, but the authors must additionally handle the hierarchical coupling between primal, dual, and auxiliary variables unique to bilevel structure, as well as network-induced stepsize inconsistencies across agents. A key departure from prior adaptive methods in minimax or single-level settings is the hierarchical stepsize design (where the primal stepsize is inversely proportional to m^x · max(m^v, m^y)) and the lightweight scalar-based stepsize tracking mechanism that synchronizes gradient-norm accumulators without exchanging full-dimensional tracker states.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is substantial: decentralized bilevel optimization is increasingly needed for large-scale meta-learning, hyperparameter optimization, and resource allocation, yet existing methods require knowledge of smoothness constants, strong convexity parameters, and graph spectral gaps that are typically unavailable in real deployments. By eliminating all such dependencies while matching state-of-the-art convergence rates up to polylog factors, AdaSDBO significantly lowers the barrier to deploying bilevel optimization in heterogeneous, privacy-constrained networks. Key limitations include the deterministic full-gradient setting and the strongly convex lower-level assumption; extending to stochastic gradients and generally convex lower-level objectives remains open and would substantially broaden applicability.
