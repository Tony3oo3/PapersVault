## Problem

Existing image-based 2D-to-3D pose lifting methods establish a direct joint-to-joint mapping from 2D to 3D, making them highly sensitive to 2D pose estimation errors and unable to resolve depth ambiguity in self-occluded regions. These methods rely on in-plane image features without explicit depth modeling, so joints that are far apart in 3D but overlap in 2D projection receive identical (incorrect) depth, degrading accuracy especially under occlusion.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253015/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single RGB image, a predicted 2D pose (N_J × 2, normalized to [-1,1]) from an off-the-shelf estimator (CPN or HRNet), and multi-scale pyramid feature maps from a frozen HRNet-w32 backbone. It outputs 3D joint positions (N_J × 3) structured as a weighted ensemble over 3D anchors: each joint position is a softmax-weighted sum of anchor positions plus per-anchor-to-joint offsets. The 2D pose prior is used both to generate adaptive local anchors and to restrict feature sampling to small r×r regions around each joint, cutting GPU memory roughly in half compared to full-image processing.

## Method

PandaPose propagates the 2D pose prior into a 3D anchor space as a unified intermediate representation, replacing direct 2D-to-3D regression with anchor-to-joint ensemble prediction. Three key mechanisms differentiate it: (1) joint-wise adaptive 3D anchors, where K learnable 3D offsets per joint are generated from the 2D pose and added to the joint's projected 3D position at depth 0, combined with 256 global fixed anchors on the root-joint plane, reducing mean anchor-to-joint offset from 154.6 mm to 69.7 mm; (2) joint-wise depth distribution estimation, where a lightweight CNN+ASPP network predicts a per-joint categorical depth map (64 bins) supervised sparsely at GT joint locations, resolving the ambiguity that a single depth map cannot handle for occluded or 2D-proximate joints; (3) a 3D anchor-feature interaction decoder whose layers alternate depth cross-attention, inter-anchor self-attention, and 3D deformable cross-attention over features lifted to 3D via the outer product of depth distributions and sampled image features. The final 3D pose is produced by an MLP that predicts per-anchor offsets and softmax weights, yielding each joint as a weighted sum of (anchor position + offset) terms.

## Results

On Human3.6M, PandaPose achieves 39.8 mm MPJPE and 32.7 mm PA-MPJPE on the full test set, outperforming the previous image-based SOTA CA-PF (41.4 mm MPJPE) by 1.6 mm and matching the sequence-based SOTA KTPFormer (40.1 mm) with a single frame. On the challenging subset (occlusion/noisy 2D pose), PandaPose reduces MPJPE to 73.1 mm and PA-MPJPE to 69.9 mm, a 14.7% improvement over CA-PF. On MPI-INF-3DHP, PandaPose achieves 98.6% PCK, 75.8% AUC, and 31.8 mm MPJPE; on 3DPW (cross-dataset), it reaches 74.9 mm MPJPE and 46.9 mm PA-MPJPE, both state-of-the-art.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PandaPose outperforms all 18 compared baselines across all three benchmarks. Against the closest image-based competitor CA-PF [55], PandaPose reduces MPJPE by 1.6 mm on the full Human3.6M test set and by 9.3 mm (11.3%) on the challenging subset, with a 12.1 mm PA-MPJPE gain. It also surpasses the sequence-based SOTA KTPFormer [28] (40.1 mm) by 0.3 mm MPJPE while using only a single frame versus 243 frames. On MPI-INF-3DHP, PandaPose beats CA-PF by 0.6% PCK, 0.4% AUC, and 0.9 mm MPJPE on the full set, and by 9.8% PCK and 14.8 mm MPJPE on the challenging subset. On the cross-dataset 3DPW evaluation, PandaPose improves over HiPART [58] by 2.3 mm MPJPE and 1.9 mm PA-MPJPE, confirming strong generalization. The largest absolute gains appear under occlusion and noisy 2D pose conditions, where the adaptive 3D anchor and joint-wise depth distribution mechanisms provide the most benefit.

## Relation to Prior Work

PandaPose extends the anchor-to-joint regression paradigm from hand pose estimation (A2J, ICCV 2019; A2J-Transformer, CVPR 2023) to full-body 3D human pose lifting, replacing the fixed global anchor settings of those works with adaptive joint-wise local anchors guided by 2D pose priors. It departs from the dominant image-based lifting approaches (e.g., CA-PF) that establish direct 2D-to-3D joint mappings by instead using 3D anchors as an intermediate representation, and from prior 2D-to-3D feature lifting methods (e.g., DfA3D, OA-BEV) by predicting joint-wise depth distributions rather than a single depth map. The 2D pose prior based feature sampling strategy and the anchor-feature interaction decoder with 3D deformable cross-attention are novel architectural choices that integrate these components into a unified pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses two persistent pain points in single-image 3D pose lifting—error propagation from imperfect 2D pose inputs and depth ambiguity under self-occlusion—with a principled solution that is well-validated through extensive ablations across three benchmarks. The 14.7% PA-MPJPE reduction on the challenging Human3.6M subset and the demonstrated noise robustness (16% degradation vs. 56% for CA-PF under 5-pixel Gaussian noise) have practical implications for real-world deployment where 2D pose estimators are imperfect. Key limitations include the lack of temporal smoothness inherent to single-frame methods and the computational overhead of 3D feature lifting, leaving open the question of how to integrate temporal context without sacrificing the anchor-space formulation's robustness advantages.
