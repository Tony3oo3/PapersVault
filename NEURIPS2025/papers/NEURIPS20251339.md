## Problem

Existing vision foundation models are single-task specialists trained on massive datasets (e.g., SAM on 1B pixel-level annotations), while multi-task generalist models are typically trained from scratch with substantial compute and data overhead. The paper asks whether the powerful generative priors embedded in pre-trained text-to-image diffusion models can be repurposed to build a unified multi-task perception model that matches specialist performance with orders-of-magnitude less data and compute.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251339/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes an RGB image (VAE-encoded to 8× downsampled latents, then 2×2 patchified), a short text task prompt (e.g., 'image to depth', 'image to segmentation'), and optionally 1–5 point coordinates for interactive segmentation (processed through a 2-layer MLP with sin-cos positional encoding). All perception outputs are unified in RGB space: single-channel maps (depth, masks) are repeated to three channels, normal maps are used directly as RGB, and entity/instance segmentation masks are assigned random colors per instance and later separated via KMeans clustering. Inference runs 28 denoising steps on a 24 GB GPU with classifier-free guidance set to 2.

## Method

DICEPTION adopts the SD3 DiT architecture with flow matching, keeping the pre-trained weights and architecture essentially unchanged. The key mechanism is token-wise concatenation along the sequence dimension of four token groups—input image tokens, noisy target tokens, task prompt embeddings, and point embeddings—so the model conditions on all of them while the flow-matching loss is computed exclusively on the noisy tokens. Rotary Position Embeddings are applied independently to input and noisy tokens so that spatially corresponding positions share identical positional encodings, enabling the model to learn pixel-aligned correspondences without additional parameters. The paper's central empirical finding is that this minimal intervention (no new modules, no channel-wise concatenation, no task-specific heads) maximally preserves the generative prior and yields the best multi-task performance; more complex designs like channel-wise concatenation or U-Net backbones converge slower and produce worse results. For few-shot adaptation to novel tasks (lung segmentation, tumor segmentation, image highlighting), a rank-128 LoRA on all attention Q/K/V layers (<1% of parameters) fine-tuned on 50 images per task suffices, and pixel-aligned training significantly reduces artifacts in low-level tasks.

## Results

On depth estimation, DICEPTION (500K training samples) achieves AbsRel of 0.069 on KITTI, 0.061 on NYUv2, 0.072 on ScanNet, and 0.050 on ETH3D, matching or exceeding SOTA specialists like DepthPro and Metric3D v2 on most benchmarks while using orders of magnitude less data. For interactive segmentation, DICEPTION achieves mIoU on par with SAM-vit-h using only 0.06% of its training data (600K vs. 1B pixel-level annotated images). On surface normal estimation, DICEPTION reaches a mean angular error of 18.34° on NYUv2 and 18.84° on ScanNet, competitive with StableNormal (19.71°/17.25°) and Marigold (20.86°/18.46°). Few-shot adaptation with LoRA (50 images, <1% parameters) successfully extends the model to lung segmentation, tumor segmentation, and image highlighting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On depth estimation, DICEPTION outperforms all multi-task baselines (Painter, Unified-IO, 4M-XL, OneDiffusion, Genpercept) across nearly all five benchmarks, and is competitive with SOTA specialists: it beats DepthPro on ETH3D (0.050 vs 0.043 AbsRel) and Metric3D v2 on NYUv2 (0.061 vs 0.039), while remaining within ~0.02 AbsRel of DepthPro on KITTI (0.069 vs 0.055). For interactive segmentation, DICEPTION matches SAM-vit-h in mIoU using only 600K samples versus SAM's 1B, with SAM pulling ahead only on out-of-distribution datasets like WoodScape fisheye. On surface normal estimation, DICEPTION (18.34° mean on NYUv2) outperforms 4M-XL (37.28°) and Unified-IO (28.55°) by large margins and is within ~1° of StableNormal (19.71°). However, DICEPTION clearly underperforms on human keypoint estimation (57.8 AP vs. ViTPose's 78.3 and Painter's 72.5) and text-based instance segmentation (33.2 AP vs. Mask2Former's 50.1), which the authors attribute partly to post-processing errors in mask extraction. The single-task variant shows no significant gap versus the multi-task model, indicating no negative transfer across the six tasks at this data scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

DICEPTION repurposes the SD3 rectified-flow transformer (Esser et al., ICML 2024) — originally a text-to-image generator — as a unified backbone for six visual perception tasks, departing from prior multi-task models (Painter, Unified-IO, 4M) that train from scratch with task-specific encoders/decoders. Single-task diffusion-for-perception work (Marigold, GenPercept, StableNormal, GeoWizard) demonstrated that generative priors transfer to individual tasks, but DICEPTION is the first to show this extends to a fully shared-parameter multi-task setting via simple token-wise concatenation of input image, noisy tokens, task prompts, and point embeddings. The key design departure from concurrent work like One Diffusion is the explicit separation of conditioning tokens from the denoised target, and the finding that preserving the pretrained architecture (DiT + RoPE) without additional modules is what enables effective multi-task transfer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a single pretrained diffusion model can serve as a practical multi-task perception foundation with dramatically reduced data requirements (1.8M samples vs. SAM's 1B) and near-zero-cost task adaptation (50 images, <1% parameters via LoRA), which is practically significant for resource-constrained settings. However, notable gaps remain: pose estimation (AP 57.8 vs. 78.3 for ViTPose) and instance segmentation (AP 33.2 vs. 50.1 for Mask2Former) lag substantially behind specialists, and the authors acknowledge that multi-task mutual benefit has not yet been observed at their data scale. Open questions include whether scaling training data reveals cross-task synergy, whether the RGB-space unification generalizes to tasks with fundamentally different output structures (e.g., 3D point clouds), and whether the one-step denoising failure in multi-task settings can be resolved without reverting to single-task training.
