## Problem

Prior audio benchmarks (HARES, NOSS, SUPERB) are fragmented across speech and non-speech domains, rely on task-specific fine-tuning, and lack standardized measures of embedding efficiency. There is no unified, model-agnostic framework to assess whether a single audio embedding can support the full spectrum of real-world auditory capabilities—from voice search retrieval to bioacoustic monitoring—while quantifying the gap between current sound-based performance and text-based oracles.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251038/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark consumes raw audio signals (spoken queries of 1–62 s, environmental sound clips, birdsong soundscapes) optionally paired with text context (Wikipedia passages/pages, candidate hypothesis lists of up to 250 items). Outputs are task-specific: fixed-size or sequential embeddings, ranked candidate lists, extracted answer spans, class labels, verbatim transcriptions, salient-term timestamps, cluster assignments, or reconstructed waveforms. The framework is strictly model-agnostic, accepting any encoder output format (vectors, token sequences, discrete tokens) and universally reporting Compression Ratio and FLOPS alongside task metrics.

## Method

MSEB is an extensible, open-source evaluation library rather than a single model. It defines eight super tasks (Retrieval, Reranking, Reasoning, Classification, Transcription, Segmentation, Clustering, Reconstruction), each with a standardized evaluator and primary metric (MRR, mAP, gmean-F1, Accuracy, WER, NDCG, V-measure, FAD respectively), and supports three encoder paradigms: end-to-end, cascade (e.g., Whisper ASR → text embedding), and multi-tower collections. The library's core execution flow maps a Task (Dataset + Evaluator) to bulk encoder inference via a Runner interface, storing results in an Embedding Cache keyed by task identifiers, enabling separation of expensive encoding stages. Baselines employ Whisper Large v3 for ASR, Gemini Embedding and Gecko for text encoding, CLAP and Perch for direct audio encoding, HuBERT/Wav2Vec2 for speech clustering, and EnCodec for reconstruction. The novel SVQ dataset (177k+ spoken queries, 26 locales, 17 languages, 4 noise environments) is released as a single undivided collection with metadata derived from XTREME-UP, enabling all eight tasks to be evaluated from one source without task-specific fine-tuning.

## Results

MSEB establishes baselines across eight super tasks, revealing substantial performance headroom between sound-based and text-oracle inputs. Key headline numbers include: retrieval MRR varying widely by language (with sound consistently below text), reranking mAP from 0.86 (en-AU) to 0.11 (ml-IN), reasoning gmean-F1 dropping from 0.54 to 0.12 for Malayalam cross-language, transcription WER from 7.7% (Arabic, clean) to >100% (Malayalam), segmentation NDCG from 0.83 (en-AU) to 0.002 (ml-IN), clustering V-measure up to 0.97 (speaker ID, te-IN, spectrogram) and 0.68 (FSD50K, CLAP), and reconstruction FAD from ~15k (English, clean) to ~778k (FSD50K). The consistent finding is that current sound-based approaches significantly lag their text-modality counterparts across all tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Whisper Large v3 serves as the ASR backbone in all cascade baselines, with WER ranging from 7.7% (Arabic, clean) to over 100% (Malayalam), establishing the primary bottleneck for downstream tasks. Gemini Embedding consistently outperforms Gecko across retrieval, reranking, and classification, confirming that stronger text encoders yield better sound-embedding performance in cascade settings. For reasoning, Gemma 3 (generative) substantially outperforms the retrieval-based approach (Gemini Embedding with 0.8 threshold), with the retrieval method failing due to lack of task-specific fine-tuning. In non-speech domains, CLAP achieves 0.43 mAP on FSD50K classification and 0.68 V-measure on clustering, while Perch dominates bioacoustics with 0.66 mAP (NBP) and V-measure up to 0.53. Notably, for speaker clustering on SVQ, a raw spectrogram baseline matches or exceeds HuBERT and Wav2Vec2 in many locales (e.g., 0.97 V-measure for te-IN speaker ID), suggesting fundamental acoustic features suffice for clean-condition speaker discrimination. EnCodec reconstruction shows severe locale-dependent degradation, with FAD jumping from ~15k (English, clean) to ~490k (English, traffic) and ~778k (FSD50K), highlighting the lack of universal audio generative capability.

## Relation to Prior Work

MSEB positions itself as the audio analogue of MTEB (text) and MIEB (images), filling a gap left by prior audio benchmarks such as SUPERB, HARES, and NOSS, which focused primarily on speech-centric or non-semantic representations without covering information-access tasks. It builds directly on XTREME-UP for the textual metadata underpinning the new SVQ dataset, and leverages a diverse set of existing encoders (Whisper, Gemini Embedding, CLAP, Perch, HuBERT, Wav2Vec2, EnCodec, Gemma 3) as baselines rather than proposing a new architecture. The key departure from prior audio benchmarks is the inclusion of user-centric tasks (retrieval, reranking, reasoning) and the explicit measurement of embedding efficiency via compression ratio and FLOPS, combined with an evaluation protocol that does not require task-specific fine-tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark establishes concrete, quantified performance headrooms between sound-based cascades and text-based oracles across 26 locales and four noise conditions, making the gap a measurable target for future end-to-end audio models. A notable open question is whether current embedding vectors can achieve truly universal semantic representation, given the persistent cross-language gaps that shrink for shorter contexts but remain substantial for full-page retrieval. The finding that raw spectrograms rival or exceed HuBERT/Wav2Vec2 for speaker clustering in clean conditions challenges the assumption that sophisticated self-supervised pre-training is always necessary, though this is limited to the controlled SVQ recording setup. The benchmark's extensibility and the release of SVQ (177k queries, 17 languages) provide the community with a shared, large-scale evaluation substrate that should accelerate progress toward general-purpose auditory intelligence.
