## Problem

Existing test-time adaptation (TTA) methods for vision-language models fail to achieve robust, continuous knowledge accumulation from the test stream. Prompt-tuning methods reset per sample and discard prior information, while cache-based methods suffer from forgetting (correct entries overwritten by noisy pseudo-labeled samples) and blocking (confidently incorrect samples persistently occupy fixed-size cache slots, preventing later correct samples from entering). No prior approach can integrate information from every encountered test sample while remaining robust to pseudo-label noise.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images processed through a frozen CLIP visual encoder (ResNet-50 or ViT-B/16) and text prompts generated via prompt ensembling (e.g., 'a photo of [CLS]') encoded by the frozen text encoder. It operates strictly online with batch size 1, processing test samples sequentially in a stream. The output is a fused class probability distribution combining zero-shot textual logits with a cache-derived logit from a closed-form linear classifier, with per-sample adaptive weighting based on prediction entropy.

## Method

SCA formulates the reuse of all past test-sample features as a ridge-regularized least-squares problem whose closed-form solution depends only on two compact, size-independent statistics: the Gram matrix G = X^T X (second-order) and the cross-statistic C = X^T Y (first-order). These statistics are updated additively with each new sample (G_t = G_{t-1} + x_t^T x_t, C_t = C_{t-1} + x_t^T ŷ_t), so knowledge from every sample is retained without the fixed-capacity forgetting of feature caches. To handle noisy pseudo-labels, SCA generates dynamic soft labels by selecting a variable-size candidate class set Ω whose cumulative probability exceeds a threshold τ, then applying a softmax restricted to Ω — yielding sharper labels for confident samples and softer ones for uncertain ones. Finally, an instance-level adaptive fusion computes per-sample weights via softmax of negated prediction entropies for the cache and textual classifiers, eliminating the need for dataset-specific fusion hyperparameters.

## Results

On the cross-domain benchmark (10 datasets), SCA achieves 70.34% average top-1 accuracy with CLIP ViT-B/16, outperforming the strongest baseline DPE (69.40%) by 0.94% and TDA (67.53%) by 2.81%. With CLIP ResNet-50, SCA reaches 63.29% average, beating DPE (61.93%) by 1.36%. On the OOD benchmark (ImageNet + 4 variants), SCA achieves 66.16% average with ViT-B/16 (best among all methods) and 50.49% with ResNet-50 (slightly below DPE's 50.81%). SCA is over 10× faster than TPT (0.012s vs 0.218s per image) while being backpropagation-free.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SCA outperforms all 12 baselines on the cross-domain benchmark with both backbones. Against the strongest cache-based competitor DPE [15], SCA gains +0.94% (ViT-B/16) and +1.36% (ResNet-50) in average accuracy, with the largest per-dataset margins on DTD (+2.89%) and UCF101 (+2.99%). Against the prompt-tuning baseline TPT [12], SCA improves by +5.24% (ViT) and +5.63% (ResNet) while being >10× faster (0.012s vs 0.218s per image). Against the Bayesian method BCA [27], SCA leads by +1.75% (ViT) and +1.85% (ResNet). On the OOD benchmark with ViT-B/16, SCA achieves the best average (66.16%) and best OOD average (64.77%), edging out DPE (65.93%/64.43%) and BCA (65.37%/64.16%); with ResNet-50, SCA (50.49%) trails DPE (50.81%) by 0.32% but remains competitive. Notably, SCA uses a single set of fusion hyperparameters across all datasets, whereas DPE and TDA require per-dataset tuning of fusion coefficients for their best reported numbers.

## Relation to Prior Work

SCA sits at the intersection of two TTA-for-CLIP lineages: test-time prompt tuning (TPT, DiffTPT, Dynaprompt), which adapts per-sample but resets and thus cannot accumulate knowledge, and cache-based methods (TDA, DPE, DMN-ZS, BCA), which store a fixed number of raw features per class and are vulnerable to forgetting and slot-blocking. The paper's core departure is replacing the raw-feature cache with two compact, additively-updated statistics (a Gram matrix and a cross-statistic), reframing the cache as an online ridge-regression classifier that ingests every sample without eviction. This repurposes a classical least-squares solution in an online, label-free setting, and pairs it with a dynamic soft pseudo-labeling scheme and an entropy-weighted fusion rule that removes the need for per-dataset hyperparameter search.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating both the fixed-size cache bottleneck and the backpropagation overhead of prompt tuning, SCA offers a practical, training-free TTA recipe that a single hyperparameter set can deploy across diverse benchmarks—a meaningful reduction in tuning effort for practitioners. The controlled forgetting/blocking experiments provide a clearer diagnostic of why prior cache methods degrade over time, which should inform future cache-design choices. Open questions remain: the method is validated only in single-domain settings, the statistics matrix grows with feature dimension (d×d), and performance still trails an oracle-label upper bound, so extending to multi-domain streams and very high-dimensional encoders is a natural next step.
