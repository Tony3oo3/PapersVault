## Problem

Deep learning-based traffic classification methods degrade severely under label noise, which is pervasive in real-world traffic due to human annotation errors and flawed automated labeling. Existing label-noise-robust methods from computer vision or malicious-traffic detection fail to generalize to general traffic classification because of the inherent semantic complexity and obfuscated structure of traffic data, leaving a gap for a unified framework that detects, corrects, and classifies noisy traffic across diverse scenarios.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254270/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes raw network traffic flows (sequences of packets) formatted into fixed-size matrices via the MFR algorithm, along with their potentially noisy class labels. It produces three outputs: a partition of flows into clean and noisy sets, a refined dataset with corrected labels for high-confidence noisy flows, and a dual-granularity classifier that outputs both flow-level and packet-level class predictions. The framework operates on encrypted traffic where payload content is unavailable, relying solely on flow-level statistical and structural features.

## Method

FlowRefiner is a three-stage pipeline built on a pre-trained MAE-style Transformer encoder. First, a traffic semantics-driven noise detector extracts label-independent flow embeddings via the pre-trained encoder, clusters them with K-means at fine granularity (K = n×C clusters), and flags flows whose labels are not among the top-m majority labels in their cluster as noisy. Second, a confidence-guided label correction module fine-tunes a classification head on the isolated clean flows, then corrects high-confidence noisy labels (p ≥ τ_h) to the predicted class while retaining low-confidence ones (p ≤ τ_l) to preserve semantic diversity. Third, a cross-granularity robust classifier uses shared-weight encoders to jointly perform flow-level and packet-level classification, with a randomly selected packet per epoch serving as a local-granularity input; this dual-task design prevents the encoder from memorizing specific mislabeled flows by forcing it to learn patterns valid at both granularities.

## Results

FLOWREFINER achieves F1 scores of 93.34% (ISCXVPN), 99.71% (CrossPlatform), 96.02% (USTC-TFC), and 93.33% (Malware) at 5% label noise, and maintains 72.90%, 84.29%, 78.71%, and 72.61% respectively at 60% noise—where the majority of training samples are mislabeled. The noise detector identifies over 75% of noisy flows even at 60% noise, and the label correction module achieves >80% correction accuracy in most scenarios. On the real-world CICIDS2017 dataset, FLOWREFINER identifies 84.61% of noisy flows with 81.92% detection accuracy, significantly outperforming MCRe.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FLOWREFINER outperforms all 14 baselines across every dataset and noise level. Against the strongest pre-training baselines (YaTC, MAE, ET-BERT), the gap widens dramatically with noise: at 60% noise on ISCXVPN, FLOWREFINER achieves 72.90% F1 versus YaTC's 45.37% and ET-BERT's 36.21%, a margin of 27–37 points. MCRe, the only prior method targeting label noise in traffic, performs competitively on USTC-TFC (91.41% vs. 91.69% F1 at 40% noise) but degrades sharply on general classification tasks like ISCXVPN (70.55% vs. 72.90% at 60%) and Malware (56.21% vs. 72.61% at 60%), confirming its reliance on benign-malicious separability. Among general label-noise learning methods, DivideMix is the closest competitor (e.g., 68.88% vs. 72.90% on ISCXVPN at 60% noise), but FLOWREFINER leads by 2–10 F1 points across all settings, with the largest gaps on CrossPlatform (84.29% vs. 75.34% at 60%). Traditional methods (Appscanner, FS-Net) and loss-function-based approaches (GCE, SCE, LSR) show the most severe degradation under high noise, often dropping below 60% F1 at 40–60% noise.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the MAE-style pre-training paradigm (originally from vision) to extract label-independent traffic semantics via a Transformer encoder, then applies K-means clustering with majority-label voting to isolate noisy flows — a strategy that departs from loss-value-based noise filtering methods (Co-teaching, DivideMix) borrowed from computer vision. It generalizes the malicious-traffic-specific label noise framework MCRe to arbitrary multi-class traffic classification, and introduces a cross-granularity (flow + packet) multi-task classifier as a regularization mechanism against memorization. The confidence-guided label correction module is a novel intermediate step not present in prior traffic or vision label-noise methods, bridging detection and robust training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Label noise is pervasive in real-world traffic due to automated labeling errors, yet prior DL-based traffic classifiers silently assume clean labels; this paper provides the first unified detect-correct-classify pipeline for general (not just malicious) traffic, and the noise-detection output gives network operators actionable feedback for annotation quality. Key limitations include reliance on synthetic noise injection for most experiments (only one real-world noisy dataset, CICIDS2017), single-run results without error bars, and moderate computational cost (~3 min on an RTX 3090 per run). Open questions include whether the K-means + majority-vote detector generalizes to very high-dimensional or streaming traffic, how performance scales with dataset size beyond the ~2–5K samples used here, and whether the cross-granularity classifier's benefit persists when packet-level labels are independently noisy.
