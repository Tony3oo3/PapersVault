## Problem

LLM token embeddings are assumed to live in Euclidean space, yet the paper shows they exhibit strong hyperbolic (tree-like) structure: token frequencies follow a power-law distribution, high-frequency abstract tokens cluster near the origin while low-frequency specific tokens sit farther out, and prompt-level δ-hyperbolicity values are close to zero. Existing parameter-efficient fine-tuning methods like LoRA operate entirely in Euclidean space, and the standard workaround of mapping into tangent space for hyperbolic adaptation fails because consecutive exponential and logarithmic maps cancel, nullifying any geometric benefit.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard LLM token embeddings (vectors in R^d) from a frozen base model and produces a pair of low-rank adapter matrices A ∈ R^{r×d} and B ∈ R^{k×r} (r ≪ min(d,k)) that are injected into each Transformer layer's weight matrix. During inference, the adapter projects each token embedding into the Lorentz hyperbolic model, applies the low-rank transformation on the space-like component, and projects back to Euclidean space before the residual connection, so the overall input/output interface of the LLM remains unchanged (text in, text out).

## Method

HypLoRA introduces a Direct Lorentz Low-Rank Transformation (LLR) that performs the low-rank update directly on the hyperbolic manifold, bypassing tangent-space intermediaries that cause exp/log cancellation. Concretely, the Euclidean input x^E is mapped to the Lorentz model via the exponential map at the origin; the low-rank product BA is applied only to the space-like component x_s^H, and the time-like coordinate is set to √(‖BA x_s^H‖² + K) so the result stays on the hyperboloid; the output is then mapped back via the logarithmic map. This construction introduces higher-order, ‖x‖₂-dependent terms into the effective transformation (Proposition 1), giving the adapter an implicit sensitivity to the hierarchical radial structure of token embeddings. The curvature parameter K is learnable and initialized per model (e.g., 0.5 for Gemma/LLaMA3, 1.0 for Qwen), and the overall time complexity remains O(r·(d+k)) with only O(N) overhead from the space projections.

## Results

On arithmetic reasoning (Table 3), HypLoRA achieves micro-average accuracy gains of +0.5 to +7.5 points over LoRA across six base models, with the largest improvement on Qwen2.5-7B (88.3% vs. 80.8% M.AVG, +7.5) and Gemma3-4B (77.8% vs. 73.7%, +4.1). On commonsense reasoning (Table 4), HypLoRA improves average accuracy by +1.8 to +4.0 points over LoRA, with LLaMA3-8B reaching 84.8% vs. 80.8% and Qwen2.5-7B reaching 87.0% vs. 85.2%. The method uses the same parameter budget as LoRA (0.67%–1.04% of total parameters) while operating the low-rank update directly on the Lorentz hyperbolic manifold.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is standard Euclidean LoRA [31], which HypLoRA outperforms on M.AVG across all six base models in arithmetic reasoning (gains of +0.5 to +7.5 points) and on AVG across all three models in commonsense reasoning (gains of +1.8 to +4.0 points). The most dramatic improvement is on Qwen2.5-7B, where HypLoRA gains +7.5 points on M.AVG (88.3% vs. 80.8%), driven by +9.3 on GSM8K and +7.8 on SVAMP. The secondary baseline is DoRA [75], which the paper compares in the efficiency analysis: HypLoRA outperforms DoRA in both inference runtime and overall efficiency despite the additional hyperbolic projection overhead. In the hyperbolicity analysis (Tables 6–7), HypLoRA consistently achieves lower δ values (i.e., more tree-like representations) in the final hidden layer than both LoRA and DoRA, confirming that it better preserves the hierarchical geometry during fine-tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends LoRA (Hu et al., 2021) by performing the low-rank adaptation directly on the hyperbolic manifold rather than in Euclidean or tangent space, addressing a cancellation problem where consecutive exponential and logarithmic maps nullify the geometric benefit. It draws on the hyperbolic representation learning literature (Nickel & Kiela 2017, Ganea et al. 2018, Chami et al. 2019) and prior geometric analyses of embeddings (Reif et al. 2019, Puccetti et al. 2022), but is the first to systematically quantify δ-hyperbolicity of LLM token embeddings and to design a PEFT method that preserves hyperbolic structure throughout fine-tuning. The Direct Lorentz Low-Rank Transformation (LLR) departs from standard hyperbolic neural network layers by removing normalization, non-linear activations, and orthogonal constraints to maintain compatibility with frozen Euclidean LLM backbones.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first empirical evidence that pretrained LLM token embeddings exhibit strong tree-like (hyperbolic) geometry, with δ-values near 0.06–0.12 (close to ideal trees at 0.00 vs. sphere space at 0.99), and that this structure is preserved or enhanced by HypLoRA fine-tuning. The consistent but modest gains (typically 0.5–2.5 points over LoRA, up to ~7.5 on Qwen2.5-7B arithmetic) suggest hyperbolic inductive bias is beneficial but not transformative for current reasoning benchmarks. Open questions include whether the benefit extends to generation, code, or long-context tasks; how to automatically select curvature K per task; and whether the theoretical link between power-law token frequency and hyperbolic curvature (γ = 2/ζ + 1) holds beyond the simplified Poincaré disk derivation. The method is orthogonal to existing LoRA variants (DoRA, AdaLoRA, LoRA+) and could be composed with them.
