## Problem

Existing time series foundation models achieve strong zero- and few-shot generalization but rely on massive architectures (hundreds of millions to billions of parameters) and large-scale pre-training corpora (billions of time points), making them impractical for resource-constrained environments. This paper asks whether both model size and pre-training data scale can be substantially reduced simultaneously while preserving strong cross-domain generalization.

## Input / Output

The model consumes univariate time series of fixed lookback length L=512 (multivariate inputs are decomposed via channel independence), which are instance-normalized before processing. It produces future predictions at multiple horizons H ∈ {96, 192, 336, 720} through a multi-resolution strategy with separate linear prediction heads per horizon. During pre-training, the output is a reconstructed version of the masked input; during inference, a greedy scheduling algorithm generates arbitrary-length forecasts autoregressively.

## Method

SEMPO combines two novel modules within a 6.5M-parameter encoder-decoder Transformer. The Energy-aware Spectral Decomposition (EASD) module transforms each series via FFT, partitions the spectrum into high- and low-energy branches using a learnable energy threshold, then applies independently parameterized multi-band frequency masks to each branch before inverse FFT and patch-based projection—this prevents dominant high-energy components from overwhelming subtle low-energy signals during pre-training. The Mixture-of-Prompts enabled Transformer (MoPFormer) replaces large mixture-of-experts networks with a pool of 128 small learnable prompt vectors; a token-dependent adaptive router (linear-softmax gating) selects and blends relevant prompts per patch token, and the resulting structured key-value pairs are appended to the K and V matrices in self-attention, enabling parameter-efficient domain adaptation. Training follows a two-stage paradigm: (1) energy-aware self-supervised pre-training via MSE reconstruction on ~83M time points (no prompts involved), then (2) MoP tuning where the Transformer backbone is frozen and only the prompt pool and prediction heads are updated with a joint forecasting-plus-reconstruction loss.

## Results

On the TSLib zero-shot benchmark (7 datasets, horizons H∈{96,192,336,720}), SEMPO (6.5M params, 83M pre-training points) achieves the best MSE and MAE on 12 out of 14 dataset-metric pairs, yielding average reductions of 23.1% in MSE and 10.8% in MAE over all competing FMs. In the 5% few-shot setting, SEMPO outperforms 11 deep baselines with an average 32.3% MSE reduction. On GIFT-Eval (9 datasets), SEMPO achieves the best SMAPE/NRMSE in 6 out of 9 cases compared to the Chronos family.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In zero-shot forecasting, SEMPO (6.5M params, 83M pre-training points) decisively outperforms all large-scale FMs: it achieves 19.9% lower MSE than Chronos_L (710M, 84B), 19.2% lower than Time-MoE_L (453M, 309B), and 36.0% lower than Moment (385M, 1.13B), while using 300–4000× less pre-training data. Among lightweight FMs, SEMPO beats Moirai_S (14M, 27B) by a wide margin (e.g., 0.410 vs 0.448 MSE on ETTh1) and TTM (5M, 1B) in zero-shot. In the 5% few-shot setting, SEMPO wins 12/14 dataset-metric pairs; the sole exception is ETTh1 where TTM edges ahead (0.382 vs 0.406 MSE). SEMPO outperforms LLM-based FMs (Time-LLM, GPT4TS, S2IP-LLM) by 10.7–15.9% in MSE and all seven task-specific models (iTransformer, DLinear, PatchTST, TimesNet, Stationary, FEDformer, Autoformer) by 15–60%+ in MSE. The paper also shows that replacing MoP with a sparse MoE (8.5M params) or prefix tuning degrades zero-shot MSE from 0.350 to 0.375 and 0.382 respectively, confirming the design choices.

## Relation to Prior Work

SEMPO sits at the intersection of two active threads in time series foundation models: frequency-domain modeling (FEDformer, FilterNet, ReFocus) and sparse conditional computation (Time-MoE, Moirai-MoE). It departs from the prevailing 'scale up parameters and data' strategy by proposing that the bottleneck is not model capacity but data utilization—specifically, the systematic neglect of low-energy frequency components during pre-training. The EASD module extends prior frequency-axis masking by adding an energy-axis partitioning step, while MoPFormer repurposes the prefix-tuning mechanism as a lightweight substitute for large MoE expert networks, drawing on the observation that self-attention is itself a specialized MoE.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for deployment in resource-constrained settings: a 6.5M-parameter model trained on 83M time points outperforms 710M-parameter models trained on 309B time points, with 10× faster inference than the next-smallest FM. This challenges the implicit assumption that general time series forecasting requires billion-scale pre-training. Open questions include whether the channel-independence assumption limits performance on strongly coupled multivariate systems, whether the energy-aware partitioning generalizes to non-stationary or irregularly sampled series, and whether the prompt-based adaptation can be extended to distributional (probabilistic) forecasting rather than point estimates.
