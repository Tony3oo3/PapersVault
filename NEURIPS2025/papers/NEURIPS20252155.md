## Problem

Test-Time Discovery (TTD) requires models to identify and classify novel classes during inference while preserving known-class accuracy, but existing training-free TTD methods rely on frozen backbones and static feature representations, causing feature overlap between known and unknown classes and limiting long-term discriminative improvement. The core gap is the absence of a mechanism that can dynamically reshape the feature space to amplify inter-class discrepancies as new categories emerge in an unlabeled test stream.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252155/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sequential stream of 224×224 RGB images (unlabeled) drawn from a mixture of known and unknown classes, processed through a frozen ViT-B/16 backbone. At each step it outputs a class label if the maximum cosine similarity to any known or seen-class prototype exceeds a confidence threshold γ, otherwise it declares a new unseen class and initializes its prototype from the current sample's adapted feature. Over the test phase, the model also maintains per-class sample and feature queues (FIFO for unknowns) that are periodically renewed to refresh prototypes.

## Method

DAA is a lightweight trainable adapter inserted after the frozen ViT-B/16 backbone (initialized from L2P prompts). Before testing, a discrepancy-amplifying warm-up simulates unknown features via Mixup plus Gaussian noise and trains DAA with a dual loss: MSE to preserve known-class representations and a contrastive objective to push simulated-unknown features apart from known ones. At test time, a Short-Term Memory Renewal (STMR) mechanism periodically re-evaluates stored unknown samples, discards those reclassified as known, re-queues reliable ones with updated features, and recomputes class prototypes as queue means. Test-time DAA updates combine self-supervised pseudo-label losses (MSE for known, contrastive for unknown) with replay of known-class samples and renewal of seen-class samples from memory, balancing plasticity for novel classes against stability for known ones.

## Results

On real-time evaluation, DAA achieves the best HCA, ARI, NMI, and VM across all three datasets: 0.66/0.52/0.85/0.85 on CIFAR-100D, 0.63/0.40/0.88/0.88 on CUB-200D, and 0.63/0.41/0.88/0.88 on Tiny-ImageNetD. Known-class accuracy (KA) also leads at 80.81% (CIFAR-100D), 68.09% (CUB-200D), and 76.38% (Tiny-ImageNetD). In post-evaluation, DAA maintains top clustering scores (e.g., ARI 0.53 on CIFAR-100D, 0.42 on Tiny-ImageNetD) while keeping Known Forgetting (KF) low (3.10, 3.60, 2.39 respectively), confirming stable knowledge retention during test-time adaptation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The closest competitor is HM (Hashing Memory, the prior TTD method), which is training-free and updates only external prototypes. DAA outperforms HM on HCA, ARI, NMI, and VM across all three datasets in real-time evaluation (e.g., ARI 0.52 vs. 0.43 on CIFAR-100D; 0.40 vs. 0.34 on CUB-200D), and also leads on KA (80.81 vs. 79.17 on CIFAR-100D). In post-evaluation, HM retains a slight KA edge on CIFAR-100D (80.73 vs. 80.27) and lower KF on Tiny-ImageNetD (1.15 vs. 2.39), but DAA still wins on all clustering metrics. Against prompt-based continual learning baselines (L2P, DP, GMP), DAA achieves substantially higher KA and clustering scores—for instance, on CUB-200D, DAA reaches 68.09% KA versus 46.22% (L2P), 53.69% (DP), and 62.97% (GMP)—while also producing far lower KF (3.60 vs. 42.29, 5.85, 5.46). The on-the-fly discovery method PHE is outperformed on every metric across all datasets, with the largest gaps in ARI (0.40 vs. 0.28 on CUB-200D) and KA (68.09 vs. 44.66). Overall, DAA consistently achieves the best or near-best results on all reported metrics, with the most pronounced gains in clustering quality (ARI, HCA) over the training-free HM baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the L2P prompt-based ViT-B/16 backbone and the Test-Time Discovery (TTD) task recently formalized by Lyu et al. (2025), which proposed a training-free hash-memory approach (HM) for real-time novel class discovery. The key conceptual departure is replacing the frozen backbone with a trainable Discrepancy-Amplifying Adapter (DAA) that actively reshapes the feature space to separate known from unknown classes, combined with a Short-Term Memory Renewal (STMR) mechanism that selectively refreshes prototypes. This positions the work between TTA (which handles domain shifts but not class shifts) and NCD/GCD (which operate offline), filling the gap of real-time, model-adaptive discovery that prior training-free or offline methods cannot address.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TTD is a nascent task with direct relevance to deployed systems in healthcare, autonomous driving, and robotics that must handle previously unseen categories without retraining. The paper demonstrates that a lightweight trainable adapter can outperform both frozen-model memory methods and aggressive prompt-updating methods while keeping known-class forgetting low (KF ≈ 3–4 vs. 27–29 for L2P/DP), suggesting that targeted feature-space manipulation is a viable middle ground. Limitations include the additional warm-up computation, heuristic FIFO-based memory selection that may degrade under high noise, and evaluation restricted to three mid-scale image benchmarks; extending to multi-modal inputs, larger-scale datasets, and principled memory compression remain open.
