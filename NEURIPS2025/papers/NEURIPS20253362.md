## Problem

Traditional image editing depends on manual inputs (text prompts, masks, sketches, or physical dragging), making it inaccessible to users with motor or communication impairments. Existing brain-computer interface approaches for generative tasks are limited to single-modality signals (EEG or fMRI) and cannot capture the nuanced, multi-faceted cognitive states needed for complex instruction-based editing. This paper asks whether multimodal neural signals alone can drive instruction-based image editing, what each modality contributes, and how neural conditions complement or compare to natural-language instructions.

## Input / Output

The system takes an original RGB image (from SEED-Data-Edit, displayed at 1920×1080) together with synchronously recorded 4-channel EEG (250 Hz, downsampled to 32 Hz), 6-channel fNIRS (25 Hz), 4-channel PPG (25 Hz), and 6-axis head motion (12.5 Hz) signals, with optional speech as an additional condition. The output is a single edited image generated by a FLUX.1-dev-based DiT in 8 denoising steps with classifier-free guidance (w=4). Neural signals are collected via a wireless, lightweight BCI patch while participants conceive edits, supporting unconstrained head movement and simultaneous speech.

## Method

LoongX encodes each neural modality with a Cross-Scale State Space (CS3) encoder that combines an N=5-layer adaptive average pooling pyramid with a structured state space model (S3M) applied along both temporal and channel axes, yielding linear-complexity O(L log L) long-sequence encoding; the multi-scale and cross-shaped features are merged via an Adaptive Nonlinear Projection. A Dynamic Gated Fusion (DGF) module then binds content and condition embeddings through three sub-steps: per-channel gated mixing of instance-wise and layer-wise statistics, adaptive affine modulation (GAP + MLP producing scale/shift coefficients), and top-k dynamic channel masking (ρ=0.7). The fused latent conditions a DiT backbone (FLUX.1-dev, LoRA-finetuned) that predicts velocity for iterative image refinement. Training is two-phase: CS3 encoders are first pretrained with symmetric NT-Xent contrastive loss to align neural features with frozen T5-XXL/CLIP text embeddings on large-scale cognitive corpora plus L-Mind, then the full stack is jointly fine-tuned with a standard diffusion MSE velocity objective on 22,728 paired editing samples.

## Results

On the L-Mind test set (1,200 samples), LoongX using neural signals alone achieves CLIP-I 0.6605 and DINO 0.4812, surpassing the text-based OminiControl baseline (0.6558 and 0.4636 respectively), while also yielding lower pixel-level errors (L1: 0.2509 vs. 0.2632; L2: 0.1029 vs. 0.1161). When speech is combined with neural signals, LoongX reaches the highest CLIP-T score of 0.2588, exceeding the text-only baseline (0.2549). The neural-signal-only variant trails on CLIP-T (0.2436 vs. 0.2549), indicating that pure neural signals capture visual/structural intent well but are weaker at aligning with explicit textual semantics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole external baseline is OminiControl [48], a text/speech-conditioned DiT-based editing model, evaluated in both text-prompt and speech-prompt variants. LoongX with neural signals alone wins on all four image-similarity metrics (L1, L2, CLIP-I, DINO) against OminiControl (Text), with the largest margin on DINO (0.4812 vs. 0.4636, +3.8%) and L2 (0.1029 vs. 0.1161, −11.4%), confirming that multimodal neural signals carry sufficient semantic and structural information to drive editing without any linguistic input. However, LoongX (Neural) loses on CLIP-T (0.2436 vs. 0.2549), reflecting its weaker alignment with explicit text semantics. The hybrid LoongX (Signals+Speech) recovers this gap, achieving the best CLIP-T (0.2588) while maintaining competitive L1/L2, demonstrating that neural and linguistic conditions are complementary rather than redundant. Against OminiControl (Speech), LoongX (Neural) dominates across all metrics, suggesting that raw speech prompts are substantially less informative than decoded neural intent for image editing.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends brain-supervised generation (DreamDiffusion, MindEye, CMVDM) from single-modality image synthesis to multimodal instruction-based image editing, building on the DiT backbone (Peebles & Xie, ICCV 2023) and structured state-space models (Gu et al., ICLR 2022) for efficient long-sequence encoding. The closest prior work, Davis et al. (CVPR 2022), explored EEG-guided editing but was restricted to facial images and a single modality; LoongX generalizes to arbitrary images and integrates EEG, fNIRS, PPG, and head-motion signals through novel CS3 and DGF modules. The contrastive pre-training strategy aligns neural features with frozen text embeddings (T5-XXL, CLIP), borrowing the NT-Xent paradigm from vision-language alignment but applying it to neurophysiological signals for the first time in an editing context.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to demonstrate that multimodal neural signals alone can drive instruction-based image editing at performance comparable to text prompts, opening a path toward hands-free creative tools for users with motor or communication impairments. Key open questions include cross-subject generalization (only 12 participants, homogeneous young cohort), the limited spatial resolution of low-density EEG/fNIRS, and whether the small performance margins over text baselines hold under noisier real-world conditions. The authors also flag entity consistency and ambiguous-intent disambiguation as unresolved challenges, and suggest VR/XR integration and world-model alignment as promising future directions.
