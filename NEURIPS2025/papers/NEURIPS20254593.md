## Problem

Existing Graph Transformers rely on intricate, task-specific architectural designs to capture different types of node interactions (local, cluster, global), limiting their flexibility and making it difficult to combine complementary interaction levels. The paper asks whether a unified perspective exists that reduces GT design to attention mask construction, and how to efficiently integrate multi-level interactions on large-scale graphs where irregular masks prevent standard efficient-attention techniques.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254593/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an attributed graph with node feature matrix X ∈ R^{N×d_in} and binary adjacency matrix A, and augments it with virtual super nodes: P cluster-level nodes (one per METIS partition) and |Y| label-specific global nodes, each initialized by averaging features of their corresponding real nodes. It outputs a per-node class probability vector via a linear classifier on the final representation. Training uses cross-entropy loss computed jointly over labeled training nodes and the label-specific global virtual nodes, which serve as class-prototype anchors.

## Method

M³Dphormer is a Mixture-of-Experts Graph Transformer built on three theoretically grounded hierarchical masks: a local mask M^{l2}=A (1-hop adjacency), a cluster mask M^{c4} that restricts attention to node–cluster pairs within the same METIS partition, and a global mask M^{g3} where real nodes attend to label-specific virtual nodes that aggregate only same-label training nodes. A bi-level expert routing mechanism adaptively weights three MHA experts (one per mask): the first sigmoid gate allocates weight between the local expert and the remaining two, and the second gate splits the residual between cluster and global experts, with zero-initialized gates yielding initial weights [0.5, 0.25, 0.25] that prioritize local attention. For scalability, a dual attention computation scheme partitions the mask into regions and dynamically selects dense or sparse computation per region based on a sparsity threshold of 1/(3d_h), reducing peak memory from O(N²) to O(m) where m is the number of valid attention pairs.

## Results

M³Dphormer achieves state-of-the-art node classification accuracy across all 9 benchmark datasets. On homophilic graphs, it reaches 88.48% on Cora, 77.53% on CiteSeer, 89.96% on PubMed, 92.09% on Computer, 95.91% on Photo, and 73.54% on Ogbn-Arxiv. On heterophilic graphs, it achieves 44.34% on Squirrel, 47.09% on Chameleon, and 98.27% ROC-AUC on Minesweeper, with particularly large gains on Chameleon (+2.5% over the next best) and Minesweeper (+1.3% over PolyNormer).

## Baselines & Comparisons

M³Dphormer outperforms all 15 baselines across all 9 datasets. Against the strongest Graph Transformer baselines, it leads PolyNormer by 0.69% on Cora, 0.60% on CiteSeer, 0.48% on PubMed, 0.24% on Computer, 0.47% on Photo, 5.02% on Chameleon, and 1.29% on Minesweeper. Against CoBFormer (the closest GT competitor on several datasets), the margins are 0.33% on Cora, 0.48% on CiteSeer, and 2.64% on Minesweeper. On heterophilic graphs the advantage is most pronounced: M³Dphormer surpasses GCN-MoE (the best MoE-based GNN) by 1.32% on Squirrel and 2.52% on Chameleon, and exceeds PolyNormer by 1.29% on Minesweeper ROC-AUC. On homophilic graphs the gains over the best baselines are more modest (0.2–0.7%), consistent with the paper's observation that local interactions dominate in highly homophilic settings. The dual attention computation scheme also enables M³Dphormer to scale to Ogbn-Arxiv (116K nodes), where the dense attention variant runs out of memory.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new conceptual ground by showing that the diverse architectural choices in existing Graph Transformers (tokenized attention in NAGphormer, cluster partitioning in CoBFormer/Graph ViT, global attention in NodeFormer/SGFormer, sparse virtual nodes in Exphormer) can all be reinterpreted as implicit attention mask designs operating at local, cluster, or global interaction levels. Rather than proposing yet another bespoke architecture, it establishes a unified hierarchical mask framework and derives a theoretical design principle (large receptive field + high label consistency) that explains when each level is most useful. The resulting M³Dphormer model repurposes the Mixture-of-Experts paradigm from NLP (bi-level gating) and the sparse/dense attention trade-off from FlashAttention/Exphormer, but applies them in a graph-specific way guided by the mask hierarchy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a principled lens for GT design: instead of hand-crafting architectures for specific interaction types, practitioners can reason about which mask (or combination) suits their graph's homophily structure. The empirical finding that naive ensembles of multi-level masks underperform the best single mask on 5/7 datasets, while an Oracle selection dramatically outperforms all, highlights that adaptive integration—not mere aggregation—is the key unsolved challenge. Limitations include the focus on node classification under strong theoretical assumptions (orthogonal class prototypes, Gaussian representations), and the METIS-based partitioning introduces a non-trivial preprocessing dependency. Extending the framework to graph-level or edge-level tasks, and to settings where cluster structure is unknown or dynamic, remains open.
