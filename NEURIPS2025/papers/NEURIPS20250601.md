## Problem

Existing safety-driven unlearning methods for text-to-image diffusion models are fragile to downstream fine-tuning: even when adapted on entirely benign datasets, state-of-the-art methods can regress to near pre-unlearning harmfulness levels. This fragility arises because current objectives only suppress harmfulness at the current parameter state without accounting for nearby regions in parameter space reachable through fine-tuning, leaving the local neighborhood vulnerable to benign gradient signals that inadvertently reactivate unsafe behaviors.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained T2I diffusion model (e.g., SD v1.4), a set of harmful prompt-image pairs for the unlearning loss, preservation prompts for utility maintenance, and a pool of simulated fine-tuning configurations (learning rates from 1e-4 to 1e-6, 5–30 steps, SGD/Adam/AdamW, full-parameter or LoRA, standard or prior-preserved denoising loss) sampled during training. It outputs an unlearned diffusion model checkpoint that retains safety after downstream fine-tuning while preserving benign generation quality (FID, CLIP score, aesthetics) and personalization capability (CLIP-I, CLIP-T, DINO). Training requires ~1 GPU hour on a single A100 with peak memory of ~56 GB.

## Method

ResAlign introduces a resilient unlearning objective that explicitly penalizes the increase in harmfulness loss caused by simulated downstream fine-tuning, going beyond suppressing harmfulness at the current state. To compute the resulting hypergradient efficiently, the paper approximates the fine-tuning solution via a Moreau envelope formulation, which reduces the problem to solving a linear system (Ax=b) using Richardson iteration (5 steps) with Hessian-vector products computed via reverse-mode autodiff—avoiding storage of intermediate fine-tuning checkpoints. A meta-learning outer/inner loop structure samples diverse fine-tuning configurations (learning rate, optimizer, steps, loss function, parameter subset) at each inner iteration, aggregates the resulting hypergradients, and updates the base model, ensuring resilience generalizes across unseen adaptation settings. Theoretically, the additional term acts as an implicit penalty on the trace of the Hessian of the harmfulness loss, encouraging convergence to flatter, more stable regions of the loss landscape that are less sensitive to downstream parameter perturbations.

## Results

On the primary safety metric (Inappropriate Rate, IP), ResAlign achieves 0.0014 before fine-tuning and maintains 0.0186 (DreamBench++) and 0.0687 (DiffusionDB) after 200 steps of benign fine-tuning, compared to the original SD v1.4 baseline of 0.3598. All six unlearning baselines regress substantially after fine-tuning, with IP scores ranging from 0.10 to 0.33, approaching or exceeding the pre-unlearning model. For benign generation quality, ResAlign achieves FID 18.18, CLIP score 31.03, and Aesthetics score 5.98, remaining competitive with the original SD v1.4 (16.90/31.17/6.04) and most baselines, while LCFDSD-NG degrades severely (FID 47.21).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ResAlign outperforms all six unlearning baselines (ESD, SafeGen, AdvUnlearn, LCFDSD-NG, LCFDSD-LT) and the original SD v1.4 on IP after fine-tuning on both DreamBench++ and DiffusionDB. The gap is most dramatic for SafeGen (IP 0.3154/0.3344 vs. ResAlign's 0.0186/0.0687) and LCFDSD-NG (0.2238/0.2474), which nearly revert to the pre-unlearning level. AdvUnlearn, the strongest baseline before fine-tuning (IP 0.0183), degrades to 0.1038 on DreamBench++ and 0.2975 on DiffusionDB, still 5–4× worse than ResAlign. Against the two additional comparisons in Table 8, ResAlign (0.0186/0.0687) substantially outperforms IMMA (0.0752/0.1321) and Meta-Unlearning (0.1128/0.1515), which the authors attribute to their use of first-order Jacobian approximations and fixed fine-tuning configurations. On benign generation quality, ResAlign matches or slightly trails SD v1.4 in FID (18.18 vs. 16.90) but is competitive with most baselines, while LCFDSD-NG suffers a severe FID degradation (47.21).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the safety-driven unlearning line (ESD, SafeGen, AdvUnlearn, LCFDSD) by adding a resilience term that explicitly penalizes the recovery of harmful behaviors after downstream fine-tuning, rather than only suppressing harmfulness at the current parameter state. The computational machinery draws on the Moreau envelope-based implicit gradient formulation from Rajeswaran et al. (NeurIPS 2019) and the ME-based bilevel optimization framework from Liu et al. (ICML 2024), combined with a MAML-style meta-learning loop (Finn et al., ICML 2017) to generalize across diverse fine-tuning configurations. Conceptually, the paper departs from prior unlearning by treating the post-fine-tuning parameter state as an implicit function of the base parameters and optimizing through it, rather than treating unlearning as a single-step parameter modification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that even entirely benign fine-tuning can cause state-of-the-art unlearning methods to regress to near pre-unlearning harmfulness levels is practically critical: it means that 'safe base models' deployed on fine-tuning-as-a-service platforms may offer no meaningful safety guarantee to end users. ResAlign provides a model-agnostic, loss-agnostic solution that is computationally tractable (~1 GPU hour on a single A100), making it deployable in practice. However, the paper acknowledges that perfect resilience against adversarial fine-tuning on harmful data is likely unachievable, and the Moreau envelope approximation introduces a modeling gap that could matter for very long fine-tuning trajectories. Open questions include whether the flat-minima interpretation generalizes to other safety dimensions (violence, PII) and whether the meta-learning overhead can be further reduced for production settings.
