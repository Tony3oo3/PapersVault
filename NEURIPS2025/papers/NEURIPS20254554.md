## Problem

Scene Coordinate Regression (SCR) methods for LiDAR localization suffer from ambiguities when spatially distinct locations share highly similar local geometric structures, causing erroneous coordinate predictions and localization failure. Existing mitigations, such as semantic segmentation (LiSA), fail because geometrically similar regions often share similar semantic attributes, leaving the core disambiguation problem unsolved.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254554/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

During training, the method consumes a single voxel-downsampled LiDAR point cloud (0.25 m voxels on Oxford/QEOxford, 0.3 m on NCLT) together with a structured geospatial text string (e.g., 'District 99, West-Southwest') derived from ground-truth pose. At inference, only the LiDAR point cloud is required thanks to distillation. The network predicts per-point world scene coordinates, from which the 6-DoF pose (translation + rotation) is recovered via RANSAC.

## Method

GTR-Loc builds on the LightLoc sparse-convolution SCR backbone and introduces three components. A Geospatial Text Generator (GTG) discretizes position into M districts and heading into K direction bins, producing a stable, pose-aware text prompt that is invariant to scene dynamics. A LiDAR-Anchored Text Embedding Refinement (LATER) module uses a Transformer decoder with learnable prompts cross-attending to point cloud features to produce view-specific text embeddings, which are then encoded by a frozen CLIP text encoder. A Modality Reduction Distillation (MRD) strategy trains a LiDAR-text teacher and a LiDAR-only student simultaneously, with an L1 distillation loss on predicted coordinates, so that at inference the student achieves near-teacher accuracy without any runtime text generation. The overall loss combines L1 coordinate losses for both heads plus the distillation term, optimized with AdamW over 25–30 epochs.

## Results

On QEOxford, GTR-Loc achieves a mean position/orientation error of 0.75 m / 1.03°, outperforming the previous SOTA single-frame SCR method LightLoc (0.83 m / 1.12°) by 9.64% in position and 8.04% in orientation. On the original Oxford dataset, GTR-Loc reaches 2.59 m / 1.19° versus LightLoc's 2.67 m / 1.25°. On NCLT, GTR-Loc attains 1.40 m / 2.62°, ranking first in position accuracy among all single-frame methods. Inference runs at 29 ms (34 FPS) on Oxford/QEOxford and 48 ms (21 FPS) on NCLT, well within the respective 20 Hz and 10 Hz LiDAR scanning frequencies.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GTR-Loc outperforms all single-frame SCR baselines across all three datasets: on QEOxford it beats LightLoc (0.83 m / 1.12°) by 9.64% in position and 8.04% in orientation, LiSA (0.95 m / 1.14°) by 21% in position, and SGLoc (1.53 m / 1.60°) by 51% in position. Against single-frame APR methods, GTR-Loc surpasses FlashMix (2.31 m / 2.03°), HypLiLoc (3.89 m / 1.27°), and all PosePN variants by large margins in both position and orientation. The only method that beats GTR-Loc on any metric is DiffLoc (multi-frame APR, 3 frames), which achieves a lower orientation error (0.87° vs 1.03°) on QEOxford, though GTR-Loc still leads in position accuracy (0.75 m vs 1.86 m) while using only a single frame. On NCLT, GTR-Loc achieves the best position accuracy (1.40 m) among all single-frame methods, slightly ahead of LightLoc (1.46 m) and LiSA (1.47 m), while LiSA edges out GTR-Loc on orientation (2.31° vs 2.62°). Overall, GTR-Loc is the strongest single-frame method in position accuracy on all three datasets and competitive in orientation, with the notable advantage of requiring no pre-built 3D map and no multi-frame input at inference.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on LightLoc (CVPR 2025) as its SCR backbone, extending the scene-coordinate-regression paradigm by introducing structured geospatial text as a training-time regularization signal. It departs from prior text-in-SLAM work (TextSLAM, LP-SLAM, Text2Pos, Text2Loc) that uses free-form scene descriptions for place recognition, instead generating discrete pose-aware text (district ID + quantized direction) that directly encodes localization-relevant information. The use of a frozen CLIP text encoder and a Transformer-based fusion module connects the method to the broader vision-language model literature, while the Modality Reduction Distillation strategy is a novel application of knowledge distillation to eliminate the text dependency at inference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a fundamental and persistent limitation of SCR-based LiDAR localization—ambiguity in geometrically similar regions—by showing that structured textual cues can serve as effective regularization during training. The MRD strategy makes the approach practically deployable since no text generation is needed at inference, though the reliance on ground-truth poses for text construction during training remains a constraint. Open questions include whether the fixed 10×10 district grid and 16-direction discretization generalize optimally to arbitrarily large or irregularly shaped environments, and whether the text-regularization benefit persists when the scene undergoes significant long-term structural change. The moderate but consistent gains (~9–10% on QEOxford) suggest the approach is a useful incremental step rather than a paradigm shift for the field.
