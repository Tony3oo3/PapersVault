## Problem

Deep neural networks are widely deployed in safety-critical domains but are poorly calibrated and lack reliable uncertainty estimates, yet no single PyTorch-based library unifies the full spectrum of UQ techniques (ensembles, Bayesian NNs, conformal prediction, post-hoc calibration, deterministic models) with comprehensive evaluation metrics and multi-task support. Existing toolkits each cover only a narrow subset of methods, offer limited metric coverage, and lack the modularity needed to compose or rapidly prototype hybrid UQ approaches.

## Input / Output

The framework consumes RGB images (28×28 MNIST through 224×224 ImageNet, 512×1024 segmentation), tabular UCI features, univariate/multivariate time series, and tokenized text, all handled through Lightning-style datamodules with automatic splits, normalization, and on-the-fly corruption. Outputs are task-specific: class probabilities with per-sample uncertainty for classification, pixel-wise categorical distributions for segmentation, distribution parameters (Normal, Laplace, Cauchy, Student's-T) for regression, plus derived OOD scores (15 selectable criteria), calibration metrics (ECE, aECE), and selective-classification decisions (AURC, Risk@80Cov).

## Method

Torch-Uncertainty organizes UQ around task-specific Lightning routines (ClassificationRoutine, RegressionRoutine, SegmentationRoutine, PixelRegressionRoutine) that unify training, validation, and test-time evaluation in a single API call. Each UQ technique—Deep Ensembles, Packed-Ensembles, MIMO, BatchEnsemble, Masksembles, Variational BNNs, SWAG, SGLD, MC-Dropout, Laplace approximation, temperature scaling, TTA, conformal prediction, and Deep Evidential networks—is implemented as a swappable layer or model wrapper, enabling free composition (e.g., ensembles of Laplace approximations). The routines natively compute 26 metrics across seven categories (classification, OOD detection, selective classification, calibration, diversity, regression/depth, segmentation) and support multi-metric checkpointing via a CompoundCheckpoint object. The paper validates the framework by benchmarking ViT-B/16 on ImageNet-1k (with ImageNet-21k pre-training), UNet on MUAD segmentation, MLP on UCI regression, InceptionTime on UCR/UEA, and BERT on SST-2, demonstrating that Deep Ensembles consistently lead in accuracy, calibration, and OOD robustness while compact variants like Packed-Ensembles trade modest performance for 75% parameter reduction.

## Results

On ImageNet-1k with ViT-B/16, Deep Ensembles achieve the best top-1 accuracy (82.19% vs. 80.67% for the single model) and lowest NLL (0.65 vs. 0.71), with temperature scaling further reducing ECE to 0.01%. For OOD detection, Deep Ensembles lead with 92.05% FarOOD AUROC and 33.05% FPR95, outperforming the single model by +1.3 AUROC and −4.9 FPR95. On MUAD semantic segmentation with UNet, Deep Ensembles reach 74.93% mIoU (vs. 71.55% baseline) and the best selective classification (AURC 0.63%, Risk@80Cov 0.93%), while Packed-Ensembles achieve 71.87% mIoU with only 25% of the parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Deep Ensembles [51] consistently outperform all other methods: on ImageNet they achieve the highest accuracy (82.19%), lowest NLL (0.65), best FarOOD AUROC (92.05%), and best selective classification (AURC 4.41%, Risk@80Cov 8.49%); on MUAD segmentation they reach 74.93% mIoU and the best AURC (0.63%). Temperature Scaling [31] provides a small but consistent calibration gain (ECE drops from 0.03% to 0.01% for Deep Ensembles) without affecting accuracy, while TTA [74] severely degrades performance (accuracy drops from 80.67% to 75.12%, ECE worsens to 0.24%). Packed-Ensembles [52] and MiMo [34] offer parameter-efficient alternatives with competitive accuracy (79.23% and 80.59%) but weaker OOD detection (89.84% and 89.13% FarOOD AUROC vs. 92.05% for Deep Ensembles). On segmentation, MC Dropout [27] underperforms the baseline (68.80% vs. 71.55% mIoU) and worsens calibration (ECE 1.52% vs. 0.51%), while BatchEnsemble [90] achieves the best FPR95 (48.00%) but the worst mIoU (64.88%). Masksembles [20] and Variational BNNs [5] fall in the middle, with Masksembles at 67.62% mIoU and Variational BNNs showing competitive regression NLL (−0.047 on Boston) but higher FLOPS than deterministic baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper does not introduce a new UQ method but rather unifies and integrates a broad spectrum of existing techniques—deep ensembles, Bayesian neural networks (SWAG, SGLD, variational BNNs), conformal prediction, post-hoc calibration (temperature scaling, Laplace approximation, MC-Dropout), and compact ensemble variants (Packed-Ensembles, MIMO, BatchEnsemble, Masksembles)—into a single PyTorch/Lightning framework. It positions itself against existing UQ libraries (Lightning-UQ-Box, BLiTZ, GPyTorch, TorchCP, Bayesian-Torch, MAPIE, Fortuna, Uncertainty Baselines, NeuralUQ) by emphasizing three differentiators: domain generality across modalities, modular composability of UQ techniques, and an evaluation-centric design with 26+ built-in metrics. The methodological taxonomy follows Gawlikowski et al. [29], and the experimental protocols draw on standard benchmarks (ImageNet, MUAD, UCI, UCR/UEA) and the OpenOOD evaluation splits.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical value lies in reducing the substantial engineering overhead of implementing, training, and fairly comparing UQ methods across multiple robustness dimensions (calibration, OOD detection, selective classification, distribution shift) in a single reproducible pipeline. The authors acknowledge that the integration burden limits current comprehensiveness (four main tasks, specialization on computer vision, no Gaussian Process support), and that the modular architecture increases bug surface area. Open questions include whether the evaluation framework can become a community standard, whether the modular design scales to new modalities (e.g., 3D, audio), and whether the observed calibration degradation of Deep Ensembles under aggressive data augmentation (noted in the segmentation benchmark) reflects a broader, underappreciated interaction between training-time augmentations and UQ quality.
