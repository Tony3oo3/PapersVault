## Problem

Point cloud registration frequently fails in low-overlap scenarios where geometric features are subtle or incomplete, and existing color-enhanced methods (e.g., ColorPCR) still struggle when color differences are not visually distinct. The core gap is the lack of a tight, noise-robust integration of geometric structure, color information, and local distributional context that can establish correct correspondences even with minimal overlap.

## Input / Output

The method consumes two colored 3D point clouds (target P and source Q), each with per-point RGB values in [0,1]³, processed at multiple resolution levels from dense points down to superpoints. It outputs a rigid transformation T = {R ∈ SO(3), t ∈ R³} along with the estimated correspondences between the two clouds. Color is represented in HSV space for indoor scenes and LAB space for outdoor scenes, and the method operates on superpoint-level patches rather than individual points for the 3DGS encoding stage.

## Method

GeGS-PCR is a two-stage pipeline. In coarse registration, a dedicated color encoder (MLP with layer normalization) maps raw RGB to noise-robust high-dimensional color features, which are adaptively fused with geometric features via a learned scalar weight α at each feature-extraction level. The Geometric-3DGS module then encodes each superpoint's local neighborhood as a 3D Gaussian (position, covariance from differentiable covariance matrix, color, and transparency), and uses 3DGS position embeddings with self-attention to build a globally invariant geometric-color representation; LORA low-rank adaptation keeps the 3DGS parameterization computationally tractable. Coarse alignment is achieved by ICP-style iterative optimization over Gaussian distributions using a Mahalanobis-distance objective solved via SVD. In fine registration, fast differentiable rendering of the 3DGS under the estimated pose produces a weighted photometric loss (pixel weights decay exponentially with inter-cloud distance) that is jointly optimized with the geometric loss via Riemannian gradient descent on SE(3), refining the transformation to sub-centimeter accuracy.

## Results

On Color3DMatch (C3DM), GeGS-PCR achieves 99.5% Feature Matching Recall, 89.1% Inlier Ratio, and 97.9% Registration Recall at 5000 RANSAC samples. On the more challenging Color3DLoMatch (C3DLM, 10–30% overlap), it reaches 97.6% FMR, 70.3% IR, and 90.7% RR. On the outdoor ColorKitti benchmark, GeGS-PCR attains 99.9% Registration Recall with a Relative Translation Error of 5.7 cm and Relative Rotation Error of 0.13°, the best among all compared methods. Total inference time is 1.70 s (RANSAC) or 0.124 s (RANSAC-free), competitive with the fastest baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GeGS-PCR outperforms the closest color-based baseline ColorPCR [20] by +0.4% RR on C3DM and +4.2% RR on C3DLM, with IR gains of +1.3% and +2.3% respectively, confirming that the Geometric-3DGS module and joint photometric loss provide meaningful improvements over multi-stage geometric-color fusion alone. Against PEAL [19], the improvement is larger: +3.3% RR on C3DM and +9.0% on C3DLM. Compared to the geometry-only GeoTransformer [15], GeGS-PCR gains +5.9% RR on C3DM and +15.7% on C3DLM, highlighting the critical role of color in low-overlap scenarios. On the outdoor ColorKitti benchmark, GeGS-PCR (LGR) achieves the best RTE (5.7 cm) and RRE (0.13°), surpassing GeoTransformer-LGR (6.8 cm, 0.24°) and HRegNet (~12 cm, 0.29°). In RANSAC-free mode, GeGS-PCR matches or exceeds all baselines in RR while maintaining the fastest pose estimation time (0.032 s), demonstrating that the 3DGS-based coarse registration is both accurate and efficient.

## Relation to Prior Work

GeGS-PCR directly extends ColorPCR (CVPR 2024), which first demonstrated that multi-stage geometric-color fusion improves registration, by replacing its separate color processing with a dedicated color encoder and a Geometric-3DGS module that jointly encodes local neighborhood geometry, Gaussian distribution, and color into a unified representation. The transformer-based attention backbone follows GeoTransformer (CVPR 2022), but augments it with 3DGS position embeddings and a Mahalanobis-distance-based ICP for coarse alignment. The most notable departure is repurposing 3D Gaussian Splatting (originally a rendering technique) as a local feature encoder and differentiable rendering engine for registration, combined with LORA to control the parameter overhead this introduces.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses the persistent low-overlap registration problem by showing that Gaussian-distribution-aware color-geometry fusion can maintain 90%+ registration recall where purely geometric methods drop to 60–75%, and it introduces ColorKitti to extend evaluation beyond the limited indoor Color3DMatch/Color3DLoMatch benchmarks. However, the method's reliance on superpoint decomposition and full 3DGS parameterization creates memory and compute bottlenecks at scale, and the authors acknowledge that scene-level 3DGS registration and semantic-aware patch decomposition remain open. The modest but consistent gains over ColorPCR (especially the 4.2% RR improvement on C3DLM) suggest the 3DGS encoding captures complementary structure, but the complexity-to-benefit ratio may limit adoption outside carefully controlled settings.
