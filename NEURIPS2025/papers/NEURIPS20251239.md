## Problem

While prior work has shown that transformer models encode features in low-dimensional subspaces and that QK matrix singular vectors align with important residual features, no method had rigorously identified which low-dimensional features are *causal* for a specific attention pattern. The paper addresses the question: given a head attending to a token pair, what is the minimal set of upstream components and the minimal subspace such that removing their contribution would eliminate the attention? This gap prevents precise, single-pass circuit discovery and obscures model-wide coordination mechanisms.

## Input / Output

The method takes a frozen transformer model (GPT-2 small, Pythia-160M, or Gemma-2 2B) and a single text prompt, requiring only one forward pass with no counterfactual inputs. It produces: (1) low-dimensional signal subspaces defined by subsets of QK singular vectors (typically 5–10% of the R-dimensional head space), (2) a directed communication graph where edges represent causal upstream-to-downstream signal contributions, (3) prompt-specific circuits by aggregating and thresholding graphs across prompts, and (4) data-independent control signal clusters that explain attention-sink behavior. The method extends to models with attention bias terms and RoPE positional encoding.

## Method

The core technique introduces *relative attention*—the raw attention score minus the mean of all other scores at that destination position—which is linear in each of the query, key, and QK matrix, thereby converting the nonlinear Softmax causal problem into a tractable linear one. The relative attention is expanded in the singular value decomposition of the head's QK matrix, and the smallest set of terms whose sum exceeds the relative attention value is selected as the signal set (the sparse attention decomposition hypothesis). Residuals are then projected onto the corresponding left/right singular vector subspaces to isolate signal from noise, and the resulting signal contributions are distributed over the exact residual-stream decomposition (attention outputs, FFN outputs, biases, input embeddings) to attribute each upstream component's causal impact on the downstream attention. This yields a recursive tracing algorithm that builds communication graphs in a single forward pass, and reveals that a small number of data-independent control signals (fewer than a dozen per model) are the proximal cause of attention-sink behavior across 80%+ of attended token pairs.

## Results

Across GPT-2 small, Pythia-160M, and Gemma-2 2B on IOI, GT, and GP tasks, the paper shows that attention scores are computed using only 5–10% of the available head dimension R (3–6 singular vectors for R=64 models; 13–25 for R=256), confirming sparse attention decomposition is ubiquitous. Control signals are strongly predictive of attention-sink behavior with F-scores of 0.965 (GPT-2), 0.952 (Pythia), and 0.994 (Gemma-2), and more than 80% of attended token pairs are attributable to attention sinking. Intervention experiments confirm that ablating identified signals decreases task performance while boosting them improves it, with residual perturbations having <1% relative norm change and >0.99 cosine similarity. Circuit discovery requires only a single forward pass per prompt (≈1 min on CPU for GPT-2/Pythia, ≈1 hr for Gemma-2), versus many forward passes for patching-based methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its single-pass circuit discovery against Edge Pruning (EP) [61], ACDC [14], and EAP [62], using the first-reported circuit for each task/model as the reference baseline (Wang et al. [45] for GPT-2/IOI, Tigges et al. [63] for Pythia/IOI, Athwin et al. [60] for GPT-2/GP, Hanna et al. [59] for GPT-2/GT). Figure 4 and the Appendix K heatmaps (Figures 20–22) show precision, recall, and F1-scores for all method pairs; the paper reports that its circuits are broadly consistent with previously reported ones rather than strictly superior on any single metric. The primary advantage over all patching-based baselines (EP, ACDC, EAP) is computational: the proposed method requires only one forward pass per prompt and no counterfactual dataset, whereas patching methods require many forward passes and task-neutral activation patches. The method also avoids known weaknesses of patching such as the hydra effect (self-repair) and ambiguous counterfactuals. For attention-sink explanation, the identified control signals generalize and provide a mechanistic account of the phenomenon previously characterized only empirically by Xiao et al. [52] and Cancedda [53].

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the sparse attention decomposition line of work (Franco & Crovella 2024; Pan et al. 2024; Merullo et al. 2024), which showed that QK matrix singular vectors expose low-dimensional communication channels, by introducing a formal problem definition (attention-causal communication) and a provably causal identification method. The key methodological departure is the introduction of 'relative attention' as a linear ersatz for the nonlinear softmax, enabling exact linear decomposition of attention scores and single-forward-pass circuit tracing. This contrasts with the dominant activation-patching paradigm (ACDC, edge pruning, path patching) that requires many forward passes and counterfactual inputs, and with SAE-based feature discovery that ignores model parameters.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The discovery of control signals — a small set of data-independent features that coordinate large groups of attention heads and implement attention-sink behavior — is a genuinely new mechanistic finding with F-scores above 0.95 across three architectures, suggesting a previously unrecognized layer of global model organization. The single-forward-pass circuit discovery is a significant practical advance over multi-pass patching methods, though the approach remains heuristic and limited to attention (not fully handling MLPs). Open questions include the functional significance of the hierarchical head organization by control signal cluster, whether the method extends to MLP-mediated causality, and whether more formally guaranteed (non-heuristic) solutions to the attention-causal communication problem exist.
