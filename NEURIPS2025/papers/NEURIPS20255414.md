## Problem

Convolutional filters in sequential recommendation are fixed, applying identical weights across all positions and thus unable to capture position-specific temporal dynamics. Self-attention addresses this expressiveness gap but is computationally expensive and lacks inductive bias toward local sequential structure. Existing hybrid models combine both, yet the paper shows that a single time-variant convolutional filter can subsume both roles, eliminating the need for self-attention entirely.

## Input / Output

The model takes a user's chronological item interaction sequence, truncated or zero-padded to a fixed length N (50 for standard experiments, 200 for long-range), and maps it through item embeddings into a D-dimensional signal. It produces a score for every item in the catalog via inner product between the final sequence representation and item embeddings, from which a Top-r list (r ∈ {5, 10, 20}) is extracted. Notably, no positional embeddings are required because positional information is encoded inherently through the spectral decomposition of the underlying directed cyclic graph.

## Method

TV-Rec reinterprets a user sequence as a signal on a directed cyclic graph (DCG) and applies node-variant graph filters in the spectral domain, where each sequence position receives its own filter tap. The filter tap matrix H is factorized as H = C·B̄, where C is a position-dependent coefficient matrix (a function of time) and B̄ is a normalized basis matrix of m complex vectors, giving the model position-specific frequency responses while keeping parameter count manageable. The filtering operation is computed via the Graph Fourier Transform (equivalent to DFT on the DCG) and a Vandermonde matrix, making the entire encoder a linear operator that can be precomputed after training for O(n²d) inference. The architecture stacks L blocks of this filter layer followed by a GELU feed-forward network, each with residual connections and layer normalization. Training uses cross-entropy loss augmented with an orthogonal regularization term on the real and imaginary parts of the basis matrix B to encourage diverse, well-conditioned filter bases.

## Results

TV-Rec achieves an average 7.49% relative improvement over the strongest baselines across six benchmark datasets (Beauty, Sports, Yelp, LastFM, ML-1M, Foursquare) using HR@r and NDCG@r (r ∈ {5,10,20}) without negative sampling. The most dramatic gains appear on LastFM (19.13% on HR@10, 18.34% on NDCG@10) and Foursquare (22.17% on HR@10, 23.85% on NDCG@10), while improvements on e-commerce datasets (Beauty, Sports) are more modest (0.98–2.73%). On the long-range setting (N=200) for ML-1M and Foursquare, TV-Rec still leads all baselines with up to 11.27% gain in NDCG@20 on Foursquare. TV-Rec also outperforms three GNN-based sequential models (SR-GNN, GC-SAN, GCL4SR) on all six datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TV-Rec outperforms all 13 baselines across every dataset and metric. Against the strongest hybrid baselines, BSARec (self-attention + Fourier convolution) is the most frequent runner-up, with TV-Rec improving by 0.55–23.85% depending on dataset and metric; AdaMCT (self-attention + 1D CNN) is consistently outperformed as well. On ML-1M, LRURec (linear recurrent units) is the closest competitor on HR@10 (0.2848 vs. 0.2904) and HR@20 (0.3886 vs. 0.4079), but TV-Rec still leads by 1.97% and 4.97% respectively. On LastFM and Foursquare, TV-Rec's advantage over all baselines is particularly large (7–24%), suggesting the time-variant filter excels when user preferences shift rapidly or sequences are long. Among pure convolutional baselines (Caser, NextItNet, FMLPRec), TV-Rec leads by wide margins, confirming that fixed filters are insufficient. Against GNN-based sequential models (SR-GNN, GC-SAN, GCL4SR), TV-Rec achieves the highest HR@20 and NDCG@20 on all six datasets, with the largest gap on Foursquare (0.0314 vs. 0.0222 HR@20 for SR-GNN).

## Relation to Prior Work

TV-Rec builds directly on the node-variant graph filter formulation of Gama et al. (IEEE DSLW 2022), adapting it from the graph domain to sequential recommendation by reinterpreting user sequences as signals on a directed cyclic graph. It departs from the dominant hybrid paradigm (AdaMCT, BSARec) that pairs fixed convolutions with self-attention, arguing instead that a single time-variant spectral filter subsumes both: fixed convolutions become the special case where all position-specific filter taps are identical, and self-attention's global mixing is replaced by position-dependent frequency modulation. The paper also positions itself against GNN-based session recommendation (SR-GNN, GC-SAN, GCL4SR) by operating on sequence positions rather than item-transition graphs, avoiding iterative message passing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is a simpler, faster architecture that eliminates self-attention and positional embeddings while matching or exceeding hybrid models across six benchmarks, with the linear-operator filter precomputable at inference. The GSP framing provides a unifying theoretical lens under which several existing SR methods (Caser, FMLPRec, BSARec) appear as special cases, potentially guiding future filter design. Open questions include the connection to state space models (noted as future work), whether the DCG padding trick generalizes to non-uniform or variable-length sequences, and whether the approach extends beyond next-item prediction to other sequential tasks.
