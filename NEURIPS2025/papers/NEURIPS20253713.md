## Problem

Existing retrieval-augmented lifelong editing methods for MLLMs degrade in accuracy as the editable knowledge base grows, because they rely on single-channel retrieval that cannot precisely match relevant knowledge at scale. Moreover, the editable knowledge they store is typically limited to a single type, lacking the synergistic integration of unimodal, multimodal, and explicit label information needed to collaboratively guide the editing process toward reliable outcomes.

## Input / Output

At inference, CARML receives an image-text pair (v_q, x_q) as the edit query and produces the desired text response o_b from the retrieved knowledge entry. The query is encoded into three parallel embeddings (visual, textual, fused multimodal) via a frozen mexma-siglip2 encoder, and the knowledge base stores the same triple plus the target output for each historical edit. The base MLLM (BLIP-2-OPT 2.7B or LLaVA-V1.5 7B) remains fully frozen; only lightweight MLPs for the scope classifier and prompt generation are trained, with the final input to the MLLM being a concatenation of continuous prompt prefixes, the image embedding, and the text embedding.

## Method

CARML's core innovation is a two-stage pipeline: (1) Conflict-Aware Dynamic Retrieval, which queries three modality-specific embedding channels in parallel, then fuses them by computing per-modality information entropy (intra-modal uncertainty) and pairwise Jaccard-based conflict degrees over top-K candidate sets, producing dynamic softmax weights that re-rank all knowledge base entries to select the single most relevant entry; an edit scope classifier (two trainable MLPs trained with Focal Loss) then gates whether the query falls within the retrieved knowledge's editing scope. (2) Multi-Level Knowledge Guidance, which, when in-scope, prepends two types of continuous prompts to the MLLM input: a static knowledge prompt (MLP mapping of the retrieved text embedding) and a context-aware dynamic prompt (token-level cross-attention fusion of visual, textual, and multimodal prompt tokens, conditioned on the query embedding), plus an explicit logits enhancement that adds a one-hot bias to the desired output token's logit when the model's predicted probability for that token falls below a relevance threshold η.

## Results

On the averaged results across E-VQA, E-IC, and VLKEB with both BLIP-2-OPT and LLaVA-V1.5, CARML achieves 98.5% Reliability, 98.2% T-Generality, 97.0% M-Generality, 100.0% T-Locality, and 96.3% M-Locality at T=1000 sequential edits. At T=1, CARML reaches 99.6% Reliability and 99.4% M-Generality, already outperforming all baselines. The method maintains near-perfect performance (≥97% on all metrics) even as the knowledge base grows to 1000 edits, whereas most baselines degrade substantially (e.g., MEND drops to 19.0% Reliability, LEMoE to 38.5%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CARML outperforms all seven baselines on Reliability and both Generality metrics at every edit count (T=1, 100, 1000). Against the strongest baseline LiveEdit (CVPR 2025), CARML leads by 11.5 points on Reliability (98.5 vs. 87.0) and 20.6 points on M-Generality (97.0 vs. 77.4) at T=1000, though LiveEdit retains a slight edge on M-Locality (98.9 vs. 96.3). Intrinsic editing methods (TP, MEND) collapse dramatically at scale—MEND drops from 93.2% to 19.0% Reliability between T=1 and T=1000—while CARML stays above 98%. External knowledge-aided methods (SERAC, LEMoE, RECIPE) also degrade severely: RECIPE, the closest external baseline, falls to 61.7% Reliability at T=1000 versus CARML's 98.5%. On T-Locality, CARML achieves a perfect 100.0% at all edit counts, matching LiveEdit and exceeding RECIPE (91.2%) and all other baselines. The cross-task editing experiment (Table 4) further confirms CARML's advantage, achieving 94.5% Reliability versus LiveEdit's 89.0% when simultaneously editing E-VQA and E-IC samples.

## Relation to Prior Work

CARML extends the retrieval-augmented continuous prompt learning paradigm of RECIPE from unimodal LLMs to the multimodal setting, replacing its single-channel text retrieval with a conflict-aware multi-channel (visual, textual, fused) retrieval that dynamically weights modalities based on intra-modal uncertainty and inter-modal Jaccard conflict. It also adopts the reliability-generality-locality editing loss formulation from LiveEdit, the most recent MLLM-specific lifelong editing framework, but departs from LiveEdit's low-rank MoE expert routing in favor of parameter-free prompt-based guidance. Conceptually, the paper positions itself against both intrinsic editing methods (ROME, MEND, TP) that suffer catastrophic forgetting under sequential edits, and external memory methods (SERAC, LEMoE) that lack multi-level knowledge integration, arguing that reliable MLLM editing requires both precise cross-modal retrieval and coordinated implicit/explicit guidance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Lifelong multimodal knowledge editing is a pressing open problem as MLLMs are deployed in dynamic real-world settings requiring continuous factual updates without retraining. CARML's near-perfect retention at T=1000 edits demonstrates that retrieval-augmented, parameter-free editing can scale to large knowledge bases in the multimodal domain, offering a practical path for maintaining MLLM accuracy over time. However, the reliance on a hard logits-enhancement mechanism (essentially forcing the retrieved answer token) raises questions about whether the approach generalizes to open-ended or multi-token edits, and the evaluation is limited to VQA and caption tasks on 2.7B–7B models, leaving open whether the framework scales to larger architectures or more complex reasoning-based edits.
