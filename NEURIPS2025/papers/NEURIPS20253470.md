## Problem

Standard conformal prediction fixes a target coverage level and lets the prediction set size vary freely, which can yield impractically large or uninformative sets in high-stakes applications like medical diagnosis. This paper reverses the design: it fixes a (potentially data-dependent) size constraint rule on the prediction set and adapts the miscoverage level to satisfy it, while still providing valid marginal coverage guarantees. The open challenge is making the resulting coverage guarantee computable in practice without additional distributional assumptions.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a calibration set of n labeled examples, a single test feature, a user-specified size constraint rule T (mapping calibration data and test feature to an integer in {1,...,|Y|}), and a score function S (typically cross-entropy from a pre-trained classifier). It outputs a conformal prediction set of size at most T and a leave-one-out estimate of the marginal miscoverage, giving the practitioner a concrete coverage number (1 − α̂^LOO) to decide whether the set is trustworthy. The size constraint rule can be constant or fully data-adaptive, e.g., driven by local label entropy in a PCA-projected feature space.

## Method

Backward Conformal Prediction constructs conformal sets via e-values: the test e-variable is the ratio of the test score to the average of all n+1 scores, and the conformal set includes all labels whose e-value falls below 1/α̃. The key inversion is that the miscoverage level α̃ is chosen as the infimum over α such that the number of labels passing the e-value threshold does not exceed the size constraint T, making α̃ a data-dependent quantity. To make the marginal coverage guarantee P(Y_test ∈ Ĉ) ≥ 1 − E[α̃] actionable, the paper introduces a leave-one-out estimator α̂^LOO that averages pseudo-miscoverages computed by treating each calibration point as a pseudo-test score against the remaining n−1 scores. Under boundedness and stability assumptions on the score function and size constraint rule, the estimator converges at rate O_P(1/√n), and an explicit high-probability bound R_δ(n) allows practitioners to certify a minimum coverage threshold with probability at least 1−δ.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (EfficientNet-B0, 91.1% test accuracy), the leave-one-out estimator 1−α̂^LOO closely approximates the true marginal coverage 1−E[α̃] across calibration sizes n ∈ {100, 1000, 5000} and set-size constraints T ∈ {1, 2, 3} over N=200 runs, with the approximation improving as n grows. Empirical coverage consistently exceeds 1−E[α̃], confirming the marginal guarantee (2). On the Breast Cancer Wisconsin dataset (XGBoost, 97.7% test accuracy), standard conformal prediction with fixed α=0.02 produces 33 out of 200 (16.5%) uninformative size-2 prediction sets, whereas Backward Conformal Prediction with T=1 yields exclusively size-1 sets while maintaining valid coverage.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not benchmark against other conformal prediction methods in a head-to-head comparison; the implicit baseline is standard (split) conformal prediction with a fixed miscoverage level α. The key contrast is that standard conformal prediction cannot control set size: on the binary Breast Cancer Wisconsin task, even setting α to the estimated marginal miscoverage E[α̃] still yields 4 out of 200 uninformative size-2 sets, while Backward Conformal Prediction with T=1 guarantees all sets have size at most 1. On CIFAR-10, the paper validates that the LOO estimator tracks the true marginal miscoverage (Theorem 3.1) and that the empirical coverage rate exceeds the guaranteed 1−E[α̃], but does not report a direct accuracy or coverage comparison against competing size-control methods (e.g., Stutz et al. 2022, Ghosh et al. 2023, Kiyani et al. 2024) that are cited as related work. The contribution is thus primarily theoretical (validity of the LOO estimator under bounded scores and stability conditions) with empirical confirmation rather than a competitive benchmark.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper inverts the standard conformal prediction workflow: rather than fixing a coverage level and letting set size vary, it fixes a size constraint rule and adapts the miscoverage level accordingly. The theoretical foundation rests on Gauthier et al. [2025], which established post-hoc validity for e-value-based conformal sets, and on Vovk's conformal e-prediction framework. The paper departs from prior work on reducing conformal set sizes (Stutz et al., Ghosh et al., Kiyani et al.) by providing a principled, distribution-free mechanism for size control via e-values rather than learned or heuristic methods, and from adaptive-miscoverage work (Sarkar & Kuchibhotla, Cherian et al.) by deriving the miscoverage directly from a user-specified size constraint rather than learning it.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method addresses a practical gap in high-stakes applications (medical diagnosis, inventory forecasting) where overly large prediction sets are unactionable, while still providing computable marginal coverage guarantees via the novel leave-one-out estimator. The stability assumption (P3) required for the general data-dependent case is somewhat restrictive and its relaxation under regularity conditions remains open. Future directions include extending to regression, exploring dynamic anytime adjustments to the size constraint rule, and investigating whether the Markov-inequality-based conservatism can be tightened. The framework is modular and could be combined with other conformal prediction refinements.
