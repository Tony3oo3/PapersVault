## Problem

Standard SGD for convex problems converges to some global minimum but not necessarily the minimum-norm solution, and without a priori boundedness assumptions the iterates can be unstable or even explode. This paper asks how to design a regularized SGD variant that provably converges to the minimum-norm solution with quantified rates, while requiring no boundedness assumption on the iterates themselves.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252530/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on a convex, L-smooth objective over a separable real Hilbert space, consuming unbiased stochastic gradient estimates (e.g., mini-batch estimators for finite-sum problems) at each iteration alongside deterministic or predictable step-size and regularization schedules. A key unusual aspect is that no boundedness assumption is imposed on the iterates: the vanishing Tikhonov term λ_k X_k acts as a retracting force that guarantees almost sure boundedness as a consequence of the analysis rather than as a prerequisite. The output is the minimum-norm solution x_* with explicit L² and almost sure convergence rates parameterized by the polynomial decay exponents p and q of the regularization and step-size schedules.

## Method

The reg-SGD update is X_k = X_{k-1} − α_k(∇f(X_{k-1}) + λ_k X_{k-1} + D_k), where D_k is a martingale-difference noise satisfying an ABC-type second-moment condition. The analysis centers on an energy function E_k = f_{λ_{k+1}}(X_k) − f_{λ_{k+1}}(x_{λ_{k+1}}) and the decomposition ‖X_k − x_*‖ ≤ ‖X_k − x_{λ_k}‖ + ‖x_{λ_k} − x_*‖, where the first term is bounded by E_k/λ_k via strong convexity and the second by the regularization error ‖x_{λ_k} − x_*‖. The step-size and regularization schedules must satisfy competing conditions: λ_k must decay slowly enough to exploit the strong convexity of f_{λ_k} for tracking x_{λ_k}, yet fast enough that x_{λ_k} → x_*. For polynomial schedules α_k = C_α k^{−q} and λ_k = C_λ k^{−p}, the paper derives optimality-gap rates matching vanilla SGD and, under a Lojasiewicz condition, optimal strong convergence rates to x_* of order k^{−2ξ/(4ξ+3)} in L² and k^{−2ξ/(6ξ+3)} almost surely. The Robbins–Siegmund theorem is applied to the energy recursion to establish both L² and almost sure convergence without any boundedness assumption on the iterates.

## Results

The paper establishes that reg-SGD with polynomial schedules α_k = C_α k^{-q} and λ_k = C_λ k^{-p} converges to the minimum-norm solution x_* both in L² and almost surely, without any boundedness assumptions on the iterates. Under a Lojasiewicz condition with exponent ξ, the optimal L² rate is E[‖X_k − x_*‖²] = O(k^{−2ξ/(4ξ+3)}) (achieved at p = 1/(4ξ+3), q = (1+p)/2), and the optimal a.s. rate is ‖X_k − x_*‖² = O(k^{−2ξ/(6ξ+3) − ε}) (at p = 1/(6ξ+3), q = 2/3). For the optimality gap, reg-SGD recovers the same O(k^{−1/3}) rate as vanilla SGD [59] for q ∈ (0, 2/3] with λ_k = C_λ k^{−q/2}. In the Radon transform experiment (128×128 image, 32 angles, 100 rays), reg-SGD with optimal schedules (p=1/3, q=2/3) converges to the minimum-norm solution over 5×10⁶ iterations, while vanilla SGD fails to produce feasible reconstructions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is between reg-SGD and vanilla SGD (with theoretically optimal step-sizes). On the Radon transform image reconstruction task, vanilla SGD with α_k = 20k^{−1/2} fails to produce feasible reconstructions, while reg-SGD with optimal schedules (α_k = 20k^{−2/3}, λ_k = 0.01k^{−1/3}) converges to the minimum-norm solution x_* = A†y. In the ODE-based inverse problem (1D elliptic BVP, 64 observation points), SGD again converges in optimality gap but fails to reach the minimum-norm solution, whereas reg-SGD (α_k = 100k^{−2/3}, λ_k = 0.001k^{−1/3}) safely reconstructs x_*. In the toy example f(x₁,x₂) = ½(x₁+x₂−1)², SGD converges to arbitrary points on the minimum set while reg-SGD converges to x_* = (1/2, 1/2) from all initializations. In terms of optimality gap rates, reg-SGD exactly matches the O(k^{−1/3}) rate of vanilla SGD from Moulines & Bach [59], confirming that the regularization overhead does not degrade convergence speed. The key qualitative advantage is that reg-SGD is automatically bounded (the Tikhonov term acts as a retracting force), whereas vanilla SGD iterates can be unbounded without additional assumptions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the continuous-time analysis of Tikhonov-regularized gradient flows (Attouch, Cominetti, and subsequent work) to the discrete-time stochastic setting, building directly on the stochastic differential equation framework of Maulen-Soto et al. [56] and the deterministic iterative regularization theory of Bakushinsky & Goncharsky. It departs from standard SGD convergence analyses (Moulines & Bach, Sebbouh et al., Liu & Zhou) by incorporating a time-varying regularization parameter that simultaneously controls the step-size dynamics and selects the minimum-norm solution, rather than converging to an arbitrary minimizer. The ABC noise condition and Robbins-Monro step-size framework are inherited from the stochastic approximation literature, but the joint balancing of α_k and λ_k to achieve last-iterate convergence to x_* is the novel analytical contribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first rigorous discrete-time convergence guarantees for reg-SGD to the minimum-norm solution without boundedness assumptions, showing that the Tikhonov term acts as a built-in stabilizer that prevents iterate explosion — a property absent in vanilla SGD. The derived optimal polynomial schedules (e.g., p=1/3, q=2/3 for the Radon transform experiment) give practitioners concrete, theoretically grounded hyperparameter choices for ill-posed inverse problems and potentially for weight decay in deep learning. Key limitations include the restriction to smooth convex objectives in Hilbert space, the need for the Lojasiewicz condition to obtain strong convergence rates to x_*, and the open question of whether the O(k^{-1/2} log k) rate for q=1/2 can be improved. Future directions include extending to non-convex settings via gradient domination and exploring the connection to implicit regularization in neural network training.
