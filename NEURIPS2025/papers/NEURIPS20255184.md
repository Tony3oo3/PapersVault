## Problem

Causal effect estimation from observational data requires selecting and tuning from dozens of specialized estimators (meta-learners, DML, doubly robust methods, neural approaches), a process that demands substantial domain expertise and per-dataset hyperparameter optimization. No single ready-to-use model can amortize this entire workflow, inferring causal effects for new datasets out of the box without task-specific adjustment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255184/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, CausalPFN takes an observational dataset of N triplets (x⁽ⁿ⁾, t⁽ⁿ⁾, y⁽ⁿ⁾) as context tokens and a batch of query tokens (x, t) for which causal effects are desired. It outputs a 1024-bin quantized posterior predictive distribution over the conditional expected potential outcome (CEPO) for each query, from which point estimates (CEPO, CATE, ATE) and calibrated credible intervals are derived by sampling. The model requires only forward passes at test time—no per-dataset training, cross-validation, or hyperparameter tuning.

## Method

CausalPFN is a 20-layer PFN-style transformer encoder trained on a large library of simulated DGPs that satisfy strong ignorability by construction: base tables (OpenML or synthetic) are transformed by selecting covariates, relabeling two columns as μ₀(X) and μ₁(X), adding zero-mean noise, and generating binary treatments via a random sigmoid propensity model. The transformer uses asymmetric attention masking so that both context and query tokens attend only to context tokens, ensuring mutually independent CEPO-PPD predictions. Each query is projected to 1024 logits, softmaxed into a quantized histogram, and trained with a Gaussian-smoothed cross-entropy (histogram) loss that approximates the causal data-prior loss. The key insight is that by training on a rich prior over identifiable DGPs, the model implicitly learns posterior predictive inference—mapping raw observations directly to the CEPO-PPD—without ever explicitly computing the posterior, shifting all computational cost to pre-training.

## Results

CausalPFN achieves the best average CATE rank (2.30 ± 0.10) across all 310 benchmark tasks from IHDP, ACIC 2016, and Lalonde, outperforming the next-best method (T-Learner at 3.57 ± 0.16). On IHDP, CausalPFN attains a PEHE of 0.58 ± 0.07, roughly 3× lower than the next-best T-Learner (1.73 ± 0.30). For ATE estimation, CausalPFN is competitive with an average rank of 4.45 ± 0.19, trailing only T-Learner (4.31) and IPW (4.41). On uplift modeling (Qini scores), CausalPFN achieves the best average normalized score of 0.952 across five marketing RCTs, edging out X-Learner (0.944).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CausalPFN decisively outperforms all baselines on average CATE rank (2.30 vs. 3.57 for T-Learner, the runner-up), driven by large margins on IHDP (PEHE 0.58 vs. 1.73 for T-Learner) and both Lalonde cohorts. However, it loses on ACIC 2016 PEHE to X-Learner (0.92 vs. 0.60) and DA-Learner (0.92 vs. 0.72), and trails Forest DML on IHDP ATE relative error (0.20 vs. 0.08) and T-Learner/S-Learner on ACIC ATE (0.05 vs. 0.03). Among neural baselines (TarNet, DragonNet, RA-Net), CausalPFN dominates on every CATE metric, with PEHE roughly 2–3× lower. Against the non-causal TabPFN, CausalPFN wins on IHDP (0.58 vs. 0.95), Lalonde CPS (8.96 vs. 9.45), and Lalonde PSID (14.40 vs. 18.7), while TabPFN* (causally fine-tuned) narrows the gap on ACIC 2016 (0.47 vs. 0.92). For uplift modeling Qini scores, CausalPFN achieves the best average (0.952) over X-Learner (0.944), S-Learner (0.912), DA-Learner (0.881), and T-Learner (0.876), though it trails X-Learner on Criteo (0.859 vs. 1.000) and S-Learner on Hill(1) (0.992 vs. 1.000).

## Relation to Prior Work

CausalPFN directly extends the prior-fitted network (PFN) paradigm from Müller et al. (ICLR 2022) and its tabular instantiation TabPFN (Hollmann et al., 2023/2025) to causal effect estimation. Where PFNs amortize Bayesian posterior predictive inference for supervised regression/classification, CausalPFN amortizes the posterior over data-generating processes to predict conditional expected potential outcomes (CEPOs), CATEs, and ATEs. This departs from the long lineage of single-dataset causal estimators (meta-learners, DML, doubly robust methods, BART, GRF, neural CATE estimators) that each require per-dataset training, hyperparameter tuning, or cross-validation, and from prior amortized causal inference work (Nilforoshan et al., Zhang et al.) that either recovers causal graphs first or lacks identifiability guarantees in the prior.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CausalPFN offers a practical 'one model for all causal tasks' solution that eliminates the substantial domain expertise currently required to select and tune among dozens of specialized estimators, while providing calibrated uncertainty estimates out of the box. Key limitations include the untestable strong ignorability assumption, restriction to binary treatments, performance degradation on very large tables (context-length limits of PFN-style transformers), and the absence of finite-sample guarantees. Open directions include extending to continuous treatments, richer identification strategies (instrumental variables, front-door), scaling to longer contexts, and developing robustness theory for prior misspecification.
