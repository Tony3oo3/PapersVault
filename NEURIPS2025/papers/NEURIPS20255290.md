## Problem

Transformer architectures exhibit fundamental limitations in modeling periodicity—the recurring, invariant patterns that underpin structured knowledge acquisition and rule-based reasoning. The paper demonstrates that even a simple modular function reveals suboptimal periodicity fitting in Transformers despite sufficient data and capacity, and that this inefficiency compounds during large-scale language model training. The central question is whether explicitly incorporating periodicity modeling into the attention mechanism can improve both learning efficiency and downstream performance of LLMs.

## Input / Output

FANformer is a decoder-only model that consumes a sequence of token IDs, maps them to embeddings of dimension d_h, and produces next-token probability distributions over the vocabulary. The architecture is built on OLMo's framework with a 1.1B parameter configuration trained on 1 trillion tokens from the Dolma corpus. A hyperparameter p (default 0.25) controls the proportion of the hidden dimension allocated to periodicity modeling via the FAN layer, with the remainder handled by a standard linear projection.

## Method

The core contribution is the ATF (ATtention-Fourier) module, which modifies the QKV projection step of multi-head attention by first transforming the input X through a FAN layer variant: X_F = [cos(W_p X) || sin(W_p X) || (W_p̄ X + B_p̄)], where the activation function is replaced by the identity map. The Q, K, V projections are then computed as linear transforms of X_F rather than X directly, injecting frequency-domain periodic representations into the attention computation. Crucially, ATF(X) is mathematically equivalent to Attention(FANLayer'(X)), enabling seamless compatibility with FlashAttention and other standard attention optimizations. The full FANformer layer stacks Multi-head ATF with a SwiGLU feed-forward network under pre-norm residual connections, and the paper shows through ablation that the gains stem specifically from the FAN-based periodicity modeling in attention rather than from added depth or FFN modifications.

## Results

FANformer-1B (1.1B params, 1T tokens) achieves an average zero-shot accuracy of 65.6% across 8 core commonsense tasks, surpassing OLMo-1B (1.1B, 2T) by 8.8% relative improvement while using half the training data, and matching Qwen2.5-1.5B (1.5B, 18T) which is the current SOTA around 1B scale. In scaling experiments, FANformer requires only 69.2% of the model parameters or 79.7% of the training tokens to match standard Transformer performance. On the ULogic logical reasoning stress test, FANformer-1B achieves 38.2% accuracy versus 7.1% for Qwen2.5-1.5B and 0.0% for OLMo-1B (GPT-4: 65.1%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FANformer-1B (1.1B, 1T) decisively outperforms OLMo-1B (1.1B, 2T) by +5.2 points in average accuracy (65.6 vs. 60.4), achieving this with half the training tokens. It also surpasses Llama-3.2-1B (1.1B, 9T) by +5.2 points and TinyLlama-v1.1-3T (1.1B, 3T) by +6.2 points, all with fewer training tokens. Against 3B-parameter models trained on the same 1T tokens, FANformer-1B exceeds OpenLLaMA-v2-3B (62.0) by +3.6 points and StableLM-v2-3B (60.9) by +4.7 points, demonstrating that architectural efficiency can compensate for a 3x parameter gap. FANformer-1B is within 0.3 points of Qwen2.5-1.5B (65.9), the SOTA model in the ~1B class, despite using 18x fewer training tokens. On ULogic logical reasoning, FANformer-1B (38.2%) substantially outperforms Qwen2.5-1.5B (7.1%) and OLMo-1B (0.0%), though it still trails GPT-4 (65.1%). On code and math tasks, FANformer-1B improves over OLMo-1B on all three benchmarks (HumanEval 6.3 vs. 5.2, MBPP 5.4 vs. 3.1, GSM8K 15.7 vs. 8.9).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the Fourier Analysis Network (FAN) layer from Dong et al. (2024a) into the Transformer attention mechanism, specifically by replacing the standard linear QKV projection with a FAN-based projection that concatenates cos/sin features with a linear component. It positions itself within the broader line of Transformer architecture improvements (Differential Transformer, Selective Attention, Probabilistic Attention Keys) but targets a distinct bottleneck: the inability of linear projections in the time domain to capture periodic patterns. Unlike FNet, which replaces attention entirely with Fourier transforms and sacrifices performance, FANformer retains the full attention mechanism and only enriches the feature space feeding into it, making it compatible with existing optimizations like FlashAttention.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in the claimed 31% parameter reduction and 20% token reduction for equivalent performance, combined with minimal computational overhead (~1% latency, ~2% memory), making this an easily adoptable architectural tweak for existing LLM training pipelines. The elimination of the 'hole' phenomenon in rule-based reasoning tasks (modular addition, linear regression) and the strong ULogic results (38.2% vs. 0.0% for OLMo-1B) suggest a genuine shift from case-based to rule-based generalization, though these findings are demonstrated only at 1B scale. Key open questions include whether the periodicity framing is the correct mechanistic explanation (vs. simply a richer inductive bias in QKV space), whether gains persist at 7B+ scale, and whether the approach composes well with other architectural innovations like MoE or long-context extensions.
