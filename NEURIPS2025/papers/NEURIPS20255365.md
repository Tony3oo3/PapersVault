## Problem

Existing persistent homology descriptors for graphs, including RePHINE, still fail to capture basic structural information: on monochromatic graphs, RePHINE recovers the same information as vanilla PH and cannot separate simple non-isomorphic graphs. Neither PH-based methods nor spectral methods alone are sufficient to distinguish all graph structures, motivating a descriptor that provably combines both sources of information.

## Input / Output

The method takes a colored graph G=(V,E,c,X) together with a vertex filtration function f_v: X→R and an edge filtration function f_e: X×X→R_{>0}, and produces a SpectRe persistence diagram whose 0-dimensional tuples are (b, d, α, γ, ρ) and 1-dimensional tuples are (1, d, 0, 0, ρ), where ρ is the sorted list of non-zero eigenvalues of the graph Laplacian of the connected component at the vertex's death time or cycle's birth time. The diagram is vectorized via DeepSets (with a dedicated sub-network for the spectral component) and concatenated with the GNN's graph-level embedding before the classification head.

## Method

SpectRe augments each RePHINE tuple with the non-zero eigenvalues of the graph Laplacian of the connected component a vertex dies in (0-dim) or a cycle is born in (1-dim), leveraging the insight that tracking the graph Laplacian of filtered subgraphs captures all the expressivity that higher-order persistent Laplacians would offer. The paper proves SpectRe is strictly more expressive than both RePHINE and the Laplacian spectrum alone, and that RePHINE and LS are incomparable. For stability, the authors construct a novel bottleneck distance on RePHINE and SpectRe diagrams that accounts for the α, γ, and ρ components, showing RePHINE is globally stable while SpectRe is only locally stable (injectivity of the edge filtration is required), with an explicit upper bound on the extent of instability when injectivity is violated. For GNN integration, a dedicated DeepSet sub-network encodes the spectral list ρ per tuple, and the resulting topological embedding is concatenated with the GCN's graph-level representation at each layer.

## Results

On nine real-world graph classification/regression datasets, SpectRe+GCN achieves the highest mean accuracy in 8 out of 9 cases, with the largest margins on MUTAG (91.2% vs. 87.7% for RePHINE) and PTC-MM (61.7% vs. 57.8%). On synthetic isomorphism benchmarks, SpectRe perfectly separates all pairs of minimal Cayley graphs (100% accuracy across all sizes from 12 to 63 nodes), while RePHINE and PH¹ plateau at 49–95%. On the BREC benchmark, SpectRe achieves 100% separation on B(60) and R(50) subsets, matching RePHINE, but underperforms on E(100) (4% vs. 55%) and C(100) (5% vs. 3%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SpectRe is compared against three baselines: plain GCN (no topological descriptor), PH⁰ (vertex-level persistent homology), and RePHINE (the immediate predecessor that augments PH with vertex/edge color information). Against RePHINE, SpectRe wins on 7 of 9 real-world datasets, with the most significant gains on MUTAG (+3.5 pp) and PTC-MM (+3.9 pp), while RePHINE narrowly edges out SpectRe on ZINC (MAE 0.47 vs. 0.48). Against plain GCN, SpectRe provides substantial improvements across all datasets (e.g., +28 pp on MUTAG, +7 pp on NCI1), confirming that the spectral-topological signal is informative beyond what message-passing alone captures. On the synthetic Cayley graph benchmark, SpectRe achieves 100% separation for all graph sizes, whereas RePHINE and PH¹ are identical and fail on larger instances (dropping to 49% at c-63). On BREC, the picture is mixed: SpectRe matches RePHINE on B(60) and R(50) but is substantially worse on E(100) (4% vs. 55%) and C(100) (5% vs. 3%), suggesting that the spectral component can sometimes be less discriminative for certain structural regularities that RePHINE's color-based tuples capture more directly.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RePHINE descriptor (Immonen et al., NeurIPS 2023), which itself augmented classical persistent homology with vertex and edge color information, by further enriching each persistence tuple with the non-zero eigenvalues of the graph Laplacian of the connected component at the relevant birth/death time. A key theoretical departure is showing (Proposition 3.4) that the simple graph Laplacian spectrum is as expressive as the more elaborate persistent Laplacian spectrum, justifying the simpler construction. On the stability side, the paper generalizes the classical bottleneck distance framework of Cohen-Steiner et al. to the richer RePHINE and SpectRe diagram spaces, introducing new metrics that account for the additional color and spectral parameters.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SpectRe provides a principled, provably more expressive topological descriptor for graph learning that captures both topological (persistence) and spectral (Laplacian eigenvalue) structure simultaneously, with a clean integration pathway into GNNs via DeepSets. The discovery that SpectRe is only locally stable (not globally) is a non-trivial theoretical finding that clarifies the boundary conditions under which spectral-augmented persistence is well-behaved, and the explicit instability bound (Theorem 4.7) quantifies the worst-case degradation. Open questions include extending to biparameter or Vietoris-Rips filtrations, investigating spectral interactions with vertex-based filtrations, and reducing the O(n^5) worst-case eigendecomposition cost for large-scale deployment.
