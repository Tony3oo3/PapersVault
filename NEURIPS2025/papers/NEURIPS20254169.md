## Problem

On-policy RL for LVLMs is fundamentally bounded by the initial policy distribution because vision-text alignment training does not produce slow-thinking reasoning trajectories, so the model can only amplify existing behaviors. Directly distilling off-policy trajectories from external models introduces visual hallucinations due to mismatched visual perception abilities across models. SOPHIA addresses this by constructing a semi-off-policy behavior model that preserves the target LVLM's own visual understanding while borrowing slow-thinking reasoning from a separate language model.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a text query as input. The LVLM first generates a detailed image description (capturing spatial layouts, object relations, and fine-grained visual details) in an on-policy manner, which is then concatenated with the query to form a text-only prompt for the off-policy reasoning LLM. The output is a full slow-thinking reasoning trajectory ending with a final answer, where the trajectory is selected to be the shortest among correct ones to mitigate overthinking and redundant repetition.

## Method

SOPHIA constructs a semi-off-policy behavior model by combining on-policy visual understanding from the trainable LVLM (e.g., InternVL) with off-policy slow-thinking reasoning from open-source reasoning LLMs (QwQ-32B for easy questions, DeepSeek-R1 for hard ones). During sampling, the LVLM produces image descriptions, and the reasoning LLM generates N slow-thinking trajectories conditioned on those descriptions. Rewards are assigned at two levels: an outcome-based binary reward for reasoning correctness, and a visual reward propagated backward as the average outcome score across N trajectories, which strengthens the link between visual understanding quality and reasoning success. Policy updates use off-policy RL with importance sampling, but a theoretical lemma (Lemma A.3) shows that under a boundedness assumption on the policy ratio, the IS correction can be dropped with bias at most δ, simplifying the gradient to a REINFORCE-style update weighted by the composite reward. The visual transformer is frozen during training to preserve vision-language alignment, and a warm-up phase on visual QA data improves the quality of on-policy descriptions before SOPHIA training begins.

## Results

On the 38B scale, InternVL3.0-38B + SOPHIA achieves an average pass@1 accuracy of 55.51% across eight multimodal reasoning benchmarks, improving over the base model by 8.50%. On the most challenging benchmarks, SOPHIA reaches 49.08% on MathVision and 49.95% on OlympiadBench, surpassing GPT-4.1 (47.53% and 30.38%) and Claude-3.7-Sonnet (42.43% and 36.25%). Further on-policy RL on top of SOPHIA (SOPHIA+GRPO) pushes the average to 57.28%, with 50.40% on MathVision and 51.79% on OlympiadBench, approaching Claude-3.7-Sonnet on college-level tasks while surpassing most closed-source models on challenging scientific reasoning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SOPHIA (InternVL3.0-38B) achieves 55.51% average, outperforming GPT-4.1 (55.07%) and Claude-3.7-Sonnet (53.52%) overall, and decisively beating them on the hardest benchmarks: MathVision (49.08% vs. 47.53% and 42.43%) and OlympiadBench (49.95% vs. 30.38% and 36.25%). Among open-source 72B models, SOPHIA surpasses Qwen2.5-VL-72B (51.74%), QvQ-72B-preview (49.35%), Virgo-72B (49.03%), and MM-Eureka-Qwen-32B (50.89%) by 3.8–6.2 points on average, with the largest gaps on OlympiadBench (+15.5 to +19.7 points). Against the same-base-model fine-tuning baselines, SOPHIA improves over MPO (49.51%) by 6.0 points and over direct GRPO (50.98%) by 4.5 points, indicating that semi-off-policy sampling with reward propagation is more effective than either preference optimization or on-policy RL alone. The combined SOPHIA+GRPO pipeline reaches 57.28% average, surpassing all listed baselines including GPT-4.1, and matches or exceeds Claude-3.7-Sonnet on college-level questions (MMMU 70.22 vs. 70.44) while leading on challenging scientific reasoning (OlympiadBench 51.79 vs. 36.25). On the 8B scale (Table 2), InternVL3.0-8B+SOPHIA achieves 44.71% average, outperforming Qwen2.5-VL-7B (40.44%), MM-Eureka-8B (34.87%), and Mulberry-8B (24.92%), with a particularly large margin on OlympiadBench (38.67% vs. 16.28% and 3.27%).

## Relation to Prior Work

SOPHIA carves out a middle ground between two established but limited paradigms for LVLM reasoning: on-policy RL (GRPO, MM-Eureka, Vision-R1), which is fundamentally bounded by the model's initial policy distribution, and fully off-policy distillation, which introduces visual hallucinations due to perception mismatch across models. The key conceptual move is decomposing the behavior policy into an on-policy visual understanding component (the LVLM's own captioning) and an off-policy slow-thinking reasoning component (a separate LLM like QwQ or DeepSeek-R1), then using reward backpropagation to link the two. The theoretical grounding draws on importance sampling for off-policy optimization, with a novel lemma bounding the bias from approximating the IS ratio as 1 under a boundedness assumption.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SOPHIA provides a practical, scalable recipe for injecting slow-thinking reasoning into LVLMs without requiring human annotations or closed-source model access, and the finding that it yields a strictly better initialization for subsequent on-policy RL (whereas direct GRPO degrades the base model) suggests a new training pipeline: semi-off-policy first, then on-policy refinement. Limitations include reliance on a private 80K-question training set, evaluation on a single model family (InternVL), persistent struggles with long-range visual dependencies and fine-grained recognition, and the binary reward signal that cannot capture partial correctness. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to video or multi-image inputs, whether richer (non-binary) rewards would further improve performance, and whether the semi-off-policy decomposition can be made fully self-play (eliminating the external LLM).
