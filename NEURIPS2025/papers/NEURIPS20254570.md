## Problem

Adversarial optimization algorithms that search for flaws in agents' policies work well in zero-sum settings but fail catastrophically in cooperative or general-sum settings: agents are irrationally incentivized to self-sabotage, refusing to collaborate or actively destroying shared reward, which halts further learning. No existing framework prevents this self-sabotage across arbitrary adversarial optimization objectives in general-sum games.

## Input / Output

The method operates on a general-sum partially-observable stochastic game with k agents, taking as input the environment dynamics, per-agent observation spaces, and an adversarial optimization objective (e.g., minimize cross-play reward, maximize regret). It outputs trained base-agent policies that are guaranteed rational (best-responses to some co-policy) while satisfying the adversarial objective; manipulator agents are discarded after training. The method is agnostic to the underlying RL algorithm and works across grid-world (Overcooked, STORM) and card-game (Hanabi) environments.

## Method

RPG introduces a manipulator agent for each base agent, decoupling rationality from the adversarial objective: base agents only train to maximize their own reward against their manipulator (enforcing the rationality constraint), while manipulators use opponent-shaping to indirectly steer base agents toward policies that optimize the adversarial objective. Manipulator gradients are computed as higher-order gradients through the base agents' policy updates using Loaded DiCE, a surrogate loss that preserves all dependencies for unbiased autodiff. A partner-play regularization term (scaled by a small ε) mixes in rollouts against actual evaluation partners to prevent the manipulator from exploiting distribution shift. The algorithm performs N lookahead steps on base agents before evaluating the adversarial objective, allowing manipulators to anticipate multi-step learning dynamics and stabilize otherwise unstable equilibria.

## Results

In the unobserved STORM environment (Table 1), RPG-trained victims achieve substantially higher rewards against rational adversarial attacks: AT-RPG reaches 0.65 training reward and 0.88 against AP-RPG, while XPD-RPG reaches 0.98 training and 0.96 against AP-RPG. Non-RPG baselines (AT, PAIRED, XPD) collapse to near-zero training rewards due to self-sabotage, and the AP attack trivially achieves 0.0 against all victims. In Overcooked (cramped room), XPD-RPG maintains 240 cross-play reward (matching self-play), while CoMeDi drops to 2 and XPD to 1.25, confirming that prior diversity methods rely on sabotage rather than genuine incompatibility.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against self-play, XPD-RPG achieves comparable training reward (0.98 vs. 0.98 in STORM) but dramatically outperforms it in cross-play robustness: in complex Overcooked layouts (coordination ring, counter circuit), SP policies achieve near-zero cross-play rewards while XPD-RPG maintains high scores, and in Hanabi SP with high entropy converges to a single strategy rather than diverse ones. Against AP/AT (Gleave et al., 2019), the non-RPG versions completely fail in cooperative settings (AT trains to 0.0, AP trivially sabotages to 0.0), while AT-RPG recovers to 0.65 training reward and 0.88 against AP-RPG. Against PAIRED (Dennis et al., 2020), the original algorithm collapses to 0.13 training reward due to self-sabotage, whereas PAIRED-RPG reaches 0.93 and produces victims that score 0.84–0.85 against rational attacks. Against CoMeDi (Sarkar et al., 2024), XPD-RPG dominates in cross-play diversity: in Overcooked cramped room, CoMeDi's cross-play reward is 2 (vs. 240 for XPD-RPG), and in STORM CoMeDi drives cross-play to zero while XPD-RPG maintains positive reward. Against XPD/ADVERSITY (Cui et al., 2023) and LIPO (Charakorn et al., 2023), the same pattern holds: these baselines achieve deceptively low cross-play scores (1.25 in Overcooked) through sabotage rather than genuine strategic diversity, while XPD-RPG achieves 240 cross-play, indicating the environment contains little true diversity that requires incompatibility.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the adversarial optimization paradigm (adversarial policy, adversarial training, PAIRED) from zero-sum to general-sum multi-agent settings by introducing a rationality constraint formalized as RPO. The solution algorithm, RPG, repurposes opponent shaping (Foerster et al., 2017) in a novel way: manipulator agents take higher-order gradients through base agents' learning updates via Loaded DiCE (Farquhar et al., 2019), which is the first use of opponent shaping in the adversarial training context. The paper explicitly departs from prior self-sabotage fixes (ADVERSITY, CoMeDi) that relied on confusing observation distributions between self-play and cross-play, demonstrating that these approaches are fundamentally insufficient when sabotage is possible even with a single observation state.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RPO provides a general, algorithm-agnostic formalism that can be applied to any adversarial optimization objective in general-sum settings, unifying previously fragmented approaches to robustness and diversity. The key open question is the lack of convergence guarantees: while RPG empirically enforces rationality, there is no formal proof that it converges to a true RPO solution. Computational overhead is significant (roughly 3× slower than XPD due to higher-order gradients and additional agents), and the evaluation is limited to small 2-player cooperative games, leaving open whether the approach scales to larger populations or high-dimensional domains. Future work on model-free opponent shaping (Lu et al., 2022) could reduce the gradient computation cost.
