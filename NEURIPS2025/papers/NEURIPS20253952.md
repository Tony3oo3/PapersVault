## Problem

Existing MLLM-based video anomaly detection methods remain limited to shallow anomaly descriptions or simple question answering, lacking the ability to perform explicit step-by-step reasoning before answering. No prior work has defined a structured reasoning task for video anomalies that mimics the human cognitive process of perceiving, interpreting, and analyzing anomalous events, and no dataset or training procedure exists to elicit such reasoning from MLLMs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253952/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a video clip (uniformly sampled to 16 frames, each capped at 128×28×28 pixels) together with a structured prompt that explicitly requires the model to reason before answering. The output is a two-stage P2C-CoT (perception: global scene → local suspicious event; cognition: abnormality assessment → cause/consequence analysis) followed by a structured answer specifying the anomaly category, event description, temporal range, approximate spatial position, the reason for the anomaly judgment, and potential consequences. For normal videos the CoT collapses to a simplified two-step format.

## Method

Vad-R1 builds on Qwen2.5-VL-7B and is trained in two stages. Stage 1 performs SFT on 1,755 videos annotated with full P2C-CoT reasoning (generated by a Qwen-VL-Max → Qwen-Max pipeline with per-step prompts and human verification). Stage 2 applies AVA-GRPO, an extension of GRPO that adds an anomaly verification reward: for abnormal videos the model's predicted temporal segment is trimmed and re-fed, yielding a positive reward if the trimmed video is then classified normal; for normal videos a random beginning or ending segment is removed, yielding a negative reward if the trimmed video is then classified abnormal (penalizing temporal hacking). A length reward is also included to regularize output length. This self-verification mechanism allows RL training with only video-level weak labels (6,448 videos), avoiding the need for expensive per-video reasoning annotations during the RL stage.

## Results

On the Vad-Reasoning test set, Vad-R1 (7B) achieves BLEU-2 of 0.233, METEOR of 0.406, and ROUGE-2 of 0.194 for anomaly reasoning, and 87.5% accuracy, 86.2% F1, and 71.3% mIoU for anomaly detection. On the VANE benchmark, Vad-R1 reaches 78.0% on OpenSORA, 75.8% on Avenue, and 63.9% on Ped2, substantially outperforming the best prior method HAWK (52.0%, 36.4%, 38.9% respectively). In LLM-as-judge evaluations, Vad-R1 scores 0.734 on Reasonability, surpassing GPT-4o (0.724) and Gemini 2.5 Pro (not listed but implied lower).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Vad-R1 outperforms all open-source video MLLMs and reasoning MLLMs on both reasoning and detection metrics; for example, it improves BLEU-2 by 0.105 over VideoChat-R1 (0.128→0.233) and mIoU by 0.154 over Qwen2.5-VL (0.567→0.713). Against proprietary models, Vad-R1 surpasses GPT-4o by 0.079 BLEU-2 and 0.241 mIoU, and beats Gemini 2.5 Pro by 0.088 BLEU-2 and 0.077 mIoU. The only losses are on classification accuracy and F1 versus o4-mini (0.875 vs. 0.884 Acc; 0.862 vs. 0.875 F1), though Vad-R1 still leads o4-mini by 0.127 BLEU-2 and 0.069 mIoU. Compared to MLLM-based VAD methods, Vad-R1 achieves a 5.5× BLEU-2 improvement over HAWK (0.042→0.233) and a 36.2-point accuracy gain (0.513→0.875). On the VANE benchmark, Vad-R1 leads all baselines by 16.7–39.4 points across the eight sub-categories, with the largest margin on OpenSORA (78.0 vs. 52.0 for HAWK).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GRPO reinforcement learning framework from DeepSeekMath [50] by adding an anomaly verification reward (AVA-GRPO) that uses a self-verification mechanism with temporally trimmed videos, drawing on the temporal hacking observation from Video-UTR [81]. It builds on the MLLM-based VAD line (HAWK, Holmes-VAD/VAU) but departs from them by making reasoning a core part of the task rather than a post-hoc explanation, and on the DeepSeek-R1 paradigm of SFT-then-RL for reasoning. The P2C-CoT structure is novel in the video anomaly domain, though the perception-to-cognition progression mirrors general CoT design principles from LLM reasoning literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper introduces one of the largest structured-reasoning datasets for video anomaly understanding (Vad-Reasoning, 8641 videos, 360+ hours) and a self-verification RL mechanism that works with only video-level weak labels, reducing annotation cost. The finding that structured reasoning (P2C-CoT) substantially improves detection accuracy—not just explanation quality—suggests that forcing explicit temporal and causal analysis helps MLLMs attend to relevant segments, which could generalize to other video understanding tasks. Open questions include whether the approach scales to longer/more complex videos, whether the 37 fine-grained anomaly categories capture the full diversity of real-world anomalies, and whether the proprietary-model-generated CoT annotations introduce systematic biases that limit generalization.
