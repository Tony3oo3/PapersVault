## Problem

Existing pathology foundation models either aggregate patch features via MIL for slide-level classification or generate reports in a single forward pass, bypassing the systematic multi-scale examination strategy that pathologists use (scanning at low magnification, then progressively zooming into suspicious regions). This leads to weak supervision vulnerable to shortcut learning and a lack of interpretability, since no intermediate reasoning is exposed. The paper asks whether an agent-based system that autonomously navigates WSIs with dynamic magnification adjustment can replicate pathologist diagnostic logic while producing verifiable, step-by-step rationales.

## Input / Output

The model consumes gigapixel WSIs (initially 32× downsampled into a grid of 16000×16000-pixel regions at 40× magnification) and optional text questions for VQA scenarios. It produces a structured three-stage output: (1) region groupings with severity scores and examination flags, (2) a navigation plan as an autoregressive sequence of (x, y, m, o) tuples specifying normalized coordinates, relative magnification, and diagnostic focus, and (3) a holistic multi-scale reasoning chain culminating in a natural-language diagnostic report. Each individual view is processed at 1008×1008 resolution, lower than closed-source models (e.g., Gemini at 3072×3072), but the multi-view trajectory compensates through strategic navigation.

## Method

CPathAgent is an agent-based VLM built on LLaVA-OneVision with Qwen3-14B as the LLM backbone and CPath-CLIP as the vision encoder, connected via a two-layer MLP. The core diagnostic workflow decomposes into three stages: global screening (clustering thumbnail regions into diagnostic groups with priority scores), navigation planning (autoregressively generating a sequence of viewing steps with coordinates, magnification, and focus descriptions), and multi-scale multi-view sequence reasoning (processing the full cropped image sequence along the planned path in a single first-person reasoning pass). Training follows a three-stage progressive strategy: vision-language alignment, patch-level pathology instruction tuning (both inherited from CPath-Omni), and a critical third stage on 278K CPathAgent-Instruct samples that teach the agent-based navigation and reasoning capabilities. The training data is synthesized using Gemini-2.5-Pro guided by expert WSI reports from HistGen/TCGA, with a reference-guided pipeline that extracts region descriptions, generates multi-scale patch captions, and produces diverse navigation paths (temperature 0.8, 1–3 samples per region) to capture the inherent variability in pathologist examination strategies.

## Results

On the novel PathMMU-HR² benchmark (1,668 expert-validated VQA pairs requiring multi-scale reasoning over 16000×1600-pixel regions), CPathAgent achieves 88.6% overall accuracy, substantially outperforming the strongest general-purpose model Gemini-2.5-Pro (73.2%) by 15.4% and the pathology-specific SOTA CPath-Omni (71.7%) by 16.9%. On patch-level PathMMU, CPathAgent reaches 80.5% (Tiny) / 78.6% (All), surpassing Gemini-2.5-Pro (68.7%/67.5%) and CPath-Omni (72.4%/72.2%). For WSI classification across six TCGA cancer types, CPathAgent achieves 82.8% average balanced accuracy, outperforming traditional MIL (ABMIL: 79.9%, DSMIL: 76.8%) and all LMM baselines (best: CPath-Omni 77.4%, Gemini-2.5-Pro 72.1%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CPathAgent wins decisively across all three evaluation scales. On PathMMU-HR², it outperforms Gemini-2.5-Pro (73.2%) by 15.4% and CPath-Omni (71.7%) by 16.9%, with the largest gap on KICH (+22.4% over Gemini-2.5-Pro) and KIRC (+12.1%). On PathMMU patch-level VQA, CPathAgent (80.5% Tiny) beats the strongest general LMM Gemini-2.5-Pro (68.7%) by 11.8% and the pathology SOTA CPath-Omni (72.4%) by 8.1%. On WSI classification, CPathAgent (82.8% avg) surpasses ABMIL (79.9%) by 2.9%, CPath-Omni (77.4%) by 5.4%, and Gemini-2.5-Pro (72.1%) by 10.7%, with particularly strong margins on TCGA-BRCA (88.5% vs. 72.4% for Gemini-2.5-Pro) and TCGA-ESCA (97.1%, matching the upper bound). Notably, applying CPathAgent's agent-based prompting to Gemini-2.5-Pro improves its PathMMU-HR² score from 73.2% to 76.4% (+3.2%), confirming the navigation strategy itself is beneficial independent of the underlying model. On OOD CPTAC-Lung, CPathAgent (88.1%) beats CPath-Omni (82.3%) and PRISM (83.2%) while using 64× fewer training WSIs than TITAN (91.9%), though it remains slightly below TITAN.

## Relation to Prior Work

CPathAgent builds directly on the authors' own CPath-Omni framework (unified patch/WSI multimodal model) and the LLaVA-OneVision architecture, extending them with an agent-based navigation layer that departs from the static single-pass paradigm of both encoder-based MIL and LMM approaches. Conceptually, it draws from the broader AI agent literature (ReAct, WebGPT, AutoGPT) and applies the observe-reason-act loop to pathology, going beyond the closest prior agent-based pathology system PathFinder, which operates at a fixed magnification within predefined 512×512 patches. The key departure is introducing dynamic multi-scale zoom-in/out navigation and explicit verbalized reasoning, mimicking how pathologists progressively examine tissue from low to high magnification rather than aggregating features in a single forward pass.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The agent-based framing addresses two persistent criticisms of pathology AI—lack of interpretability and vulnerability to shortcut learning—by making the diagnostic process transparent and step-by-step. The PathMMU-HR² benchmark fills a genuine gap by providing expert-validated evaluation at the 'huge region' scale (16000×16000 px) that mirrors how pathologists actually focus their attention, a scale previously unbenchmarked between patches and full WSIs. Key limitations include reliance on synthetically generated navigation trajectories (not real pathologist recordings), a relatively small training set (5,254 WSIs), and OOD performance that still falls short of clinical deployment standards. Open questions include whether scaling to larger datasets and collecting genuine pathologist navigation data would close the ~9% gap to the synthetic upper bound, and whether the agent paradigm extends to prognostic or biomarker detection tasks.
