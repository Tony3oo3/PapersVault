## Problem

The original MetaBox (2023) was limited to single-objective optimization and RL-based MetaBBO, but the field has since expanded to four learning paradigms (RL, supervised learning, neuroevolution, in-context learning) and diverse optimization scenarios (multi-objective, multi-modal, large-scale, multi-task). No existing benchmark platform supports the full bi-level MetaBBO framework across these paradigms and problem types, leaving researchers without a unified, fair evaluation environment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255017/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The platform accepts a MetaBBO meta-level policy (parameterized by θ) and a problem instance sampled from a training distribution, then executes the nested bi-level optimization loop for T steps. Outputs include the optimized solution, a normalized performance metric (Perf), a learning efficiency score (Perf divided by training wall-clock time), an Anti-NFL generalization indicator, and structured JSON metadata per run containing full population trajectories (X), objective value sequences (Y), and wall-clock time (T). The 18 integrated testsuites span dimensions from 2D to 6440+ parameters and include both synthetic benchmarks and realistic tasks (protein docking, UAV path planning, hyperparameter optimization, neuroevolution).

## Method

MetaBox-v2 introduces a unified MetaBBO Template with a Basic Agent class featuring universal train and rollout interfaces, where a wrapper function transforms paradigm-specific learning objectives (rewards for RL, gradients for SL, fitness for NE, textual context for ICL) into a common data object, enabling 23 baselines across all four paradigms within one codebase. The Problem class is redesigned as a polymorphic Basic_Problem parent with an overridable eval() method, allowing seamless integration of multi-objective, multi-modal, large-scale, and multi-task problems. Two orthogonal parallelization schemes accelerate computation: a vectorized optimization environment (built on Tianshou) that batches low-level environments for parallel training signal collection, and a Ray-based distributed testing framework with four granularity modes (instance-level, run-level, instance×baseline, full N×B×R decomposition) achieving 10–40× speedups. Two novel evaluation metrics are introduced: Learning Efficiency (Perf(𝒜^(g),𝔻)/T^(g)) to measure how quickly a policy converges, and Anti-NFL (exponential of mean relative performance shift across B unseen testsuites) to quantify generalization robustness beyond the training distribution.

## Results

On the in-distribution bbob-10D test set (16 instances, 51 runs), MetaBBO baselines achieve the best optimization results on 14 of 16 instances, outperforming traditional BBO optimizers by orders of magnitude (e.g., LDE achieves 5.955E-01 on Sharp_Ridge vs. PSO's 1.905E+02). LDE ranks 1st on average, followed by DEDDQN-2019 (2nd) and RLDAS (3rd). Out-of-distribution, traditional BBO methods (DE, SHADE) demonstrate greater robustness across protein, UAV, and HPO-B testsuites, while MetaBBO baselines show significant performance oscillation. MetaBox-v2 accelerates training by up to 10× and testing by up to 40× over the original MetaBox, and no single baseline dominates both learning efficiency and Anti-NFL generalization robustness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In the in-distribution bbob-10D test, LDE (2021) achieves the best average rank (1st), with DEDDQN-2019 (2nd) and RLDAS (2024) close behind; all three MetaBBO-RL methods substantially outperform the best traditional BBO baseline SHADE (4th) by orders of magnitude on most instances (e.g., LDE: 5.955E-01 vs. SHADE: 1.442E+00 on Sharp_Ridge). However, out-of-distribution evaluation on protein, UAV, and HPO-B reveals that traditional BBO methods (DE, SHADE) maintain more consistent performance, while MetaBBO baselines exhibit significant degradation—DEDQN-2019, which ranks 2nd in-distribution, collapses on protein, indicating overfitting. In the extreme problem-shift neuroevolution test (Ant tasks with 4328–6440 parameters), GLEET's Transformer-based meta-policy achieves performance parity or superiority over CMA-ES and GLPSO, whereas MLP-based RLEPSO and LSTM-based LDE degrade sharply. For learning efficiency, RLDAS achieves the best performance-per-training-hour, while DEDDQN-2019 requires hundreds of hours; MetaBBO-NE (LES) and MetaBBO-ICL (OPRO) have the lowest efficiency due to nested evolution and expensive LLM calls. For generalization robustness (Anti-NFL), GLHF and LES score highest despite lower absolute performance, while RLDAS—despite strong in-distribution results—has among the lowest Anti-NFL due to its dimension-specific policy architecture. No single baseline dominates across all three axes (optimization performance, learning efficiency, and Anti-NFL), confirming a fundamental trade-off in current MetaBBO design.

## Relation to Prior Work

MetaBox-v2 is a direct architectural successor to MetaBox (Ma et al., NeurIPS 2023), the first open-source benchmark for RL-based single-objective MetaBBO. The key conceptual departure is the unification of all four MetaBBO learning paradigms (RL, SL, NE, ICL) under a single Basic Agent interface, replacing the original RL-specific agent class, and the polymorphic Basic_Problem class that enables seamless integration of multi-objective, multi-modal, large-scale, and multi-task problems. Positioning against established BBO benchmarks (COCO, PyPop7, EvoX), MetaBox-v2 is distinguished as the sole platform supporting the bi-level meta-learning training loop, which is essential for evaluating algorithm design policies rather than fixed optimizers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmarking study reveals a critical gap between MetaBBO's promise and practice: traditional hand-designed optimizers (DE, SHADE) demonstrate more robust out-of-distribution generalization than learned meta-policies, suggesting the field overestimates MetaBBO's transferability. The proposed Anti-NFL indicator and learning efficiency metric provide much-needed multidimensional evaluation tools beyond simple convergence metrics. Open questions remain about how to define problem similarity for meaningful generalization, how to address persistent overfitting in meta-training, and whether the efficiency-robustness trade-off observed here is fundamental or an artifact of current policy architectures.
