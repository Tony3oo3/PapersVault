## Problem

Iterative LLM reasoning was expected to improve truth-seeking, but emerging evidence suggests it may instead entrench prior beliefs. Prior work on confirmation bias in LLMs relied on synthetic tasks or domain-specific setups that cannot assess reasoning failures as they unfold in open-ended problems. The paper identifies the need for a principled, unsupervised, and domain-agnostic metric that can detect whether a model's belief updates are driven by new evidence or by predictable drift toward its prior.

## Input / Output

The method consumes LLM reasoning trajectories (CoT or multi-turn debate) on problem statements from forecasting, value-laden, and peer-review domains. A separate LLM judge (e.g., GPT-4o) assigns a belief probability in [0,1] at each reasoning step, treating the first step as the prior and the final step as the posterior. The output is a single Martingale Score (the OLS slope coefficient) computed across all problems in a setup, along with the per-step belief scores; the score is interpretable as the expected change in belief per unit increase in prior confidence.

## Method

The core insight is that Bayesian rationality implies the Martingale property: E[Δb | b_prior] = 0, so belief updates should be unpredictable from the prior alone. The Martingale Score operationalizes violations of this property as the OLS regression coefficient β̂₁ from regressing belief updates Δb = b_posterior − b_prior on prior beliefs b_prior across many problems. A positive, statistically significant M (via t-test) indicates that higher prior confidence systematically predicts larger positive updates — the signature of belief entrenchment. The paper proves that under the Martingale property, β₁ = 0 and the OLS estimator is both unbiased and consistent, giving the score a firm statistical foundation. Validation is performed by showing that |M| positively correlates with Brier Score (prediction error) in domains with ground truth, confirming that the unsupervised metric tracks actual accuracy loss.

## Results

Across 54 CoT experiments spanning 6 models, 3 prompts, and 3 domains, 51 show statistically significant positive Martingale Scores, confirming widespread belief entrenchment. Mean entrenchment varies by prompt condition: prior-conforming (M̄ = 0.082 ± 0.018), no-prompt (M̄ = 0.075 ± 0.014), and critical-thinking (M̄ = 0.072 ± 0.018, 95% CI). By domain, r/ChangeMyView shows the highest entrenchment (M̄_CoT = 0.103 ± 0.013), followed by OpenReview (0.086 ± 0.012) and Forecasting (0.037 ± 0.011). Critically, the Martingale Score predicts ground-truth accuracy: a score of 0.04 in forecasting already yields a Brier Score worse than random guessing (0.250), and the absolute Martingale Score explains a significant portion of Brier Score variance even after controlling for domain, model, prompt, and reasoning technique.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not benchmark against competing entrenchment metrics; instead, it compares experimental conditions to identify factors that amplify or mitigate belief entrenchment. Debate reasoning consistently reduces entrenchment relative to CoT (e.g., GPT-4o on Forecasting drops from +0.0018 to −0.0439; DeepSeek V3 from +0.0335 to −0.0929), with debate producing smaller, more conservative belief updates. Among models, DeepSeek R1 is the clear outlier with the lowest entrenchment (e.g., +0.0207 on Forecasting CoT vs. +0.0764 for Gemini 2.0 Flash under the same no-prompt condition), while GPT-4o, DeepSeek V3, Gemini 2.0 Flash, and both Llama 4 variants show comparable, moderate entrenchment. Prompt manipulation has a measurable but modest effect: prior-conforming prompts raise the mean Martingale Score to 0.082 versus 0.072 for critical-thinking prompts, with the gap being statistically significant in Forecasting but not in r/ChangeMyView or OpenReview. Judge consistency is validated across five evaluators (two human, three LLM), all showing strong correlation with the default GPT-4o judge (Pearson r ranging from 0.7152 to 0.8822, all p < 0.001), ruling out judge bias as an artifact.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper operationalizes the Martingale property from Bayesian statistics (Molavi, 2021) as a practical, regression-based metric for measuring belief entrenchment in LLM reasoning, departing from prior work that relied on synthetic tasks or domain-specific setups to detect confirmation bias (Schmidgall et al., 2024; Shi et al., 2024). It sits at the intersection of the Bayesian rationality evaluation literature (Gupta et al., 2025; Falck et al., 2024; Qiu et al., 2025a), which has shown LLMs deviate from ideal Bayesian updating, and the cognitive-bias-in-LLMs literature, which has documented sycophancy, conformity, and confirmation bias but lacked a domain-agnostic, unsupervised measurement tool. The key conceptual departure is framing belief entrenchment as a statistical property (predictability of belief updates from priors) rather than an individual-instance tendency, enabling evaluation in open-ended domains without ground truth.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Martingale Score provides the first unsupervised, domain-agnostic process-based metric for LLM reasoning quality, filling a gap left by outcome-based evaluations (e.g., Brier Score) that cannot diagnose *how* a model arrives at its answer. The finding that belief entrenchment is near-universal (51/54 CoT setups) and persists even under critical-thinking prompts suggests that current reasoning techniques do not fundamentally address confirmation bias, with implications for deploying LLMs in judgment-heavy domains like peer review or value-laden decision-making. Open questions include whether reinforced reasoning (e.g., DeepSeek-R1-style RL) can mitigate entrenchment, whether the metric extends to external evidence-seeking (not just internal reasoning), and whether it can serve as a training objective to actively reduce belief entrenchment.
