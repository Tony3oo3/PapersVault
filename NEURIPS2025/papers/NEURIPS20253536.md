## Problem

RL fine-tuning of LLMs is dominated by policy-gradient methods (on-policy, requiring costly fresh sampling) and bandit-based approaches (incapable of exploiting token-wise reward signals and requiring paired trajectories). Q-learning methods, which offer sample efficiency and off-policy learning, have seen little adoption in the LLM community because naively applying them to model logits ignores key LLM-specific constraints: the need for multiple networks, poor initialization from reference logits, and slow reward propagation under sparse sequence-level rewards.

## Input / Output

ShiQ consumes single prompt-completion-reward triplets (unranked, no pairing required) along with the reference model's logits, and supports both token-wise and sequence-level reward signals. The output is a set of fine-tuned LLM logits that are directly samplable via softmax at inference—no reference model, temperature parameter, or auxiliary value network is needed at deployment. The loss is token-level (one squared residual per token position), normalized by total token count like cross-entropy, making it drop-in compatible with standard LLM training loops.

## Method

ShiQ derives a sequence of four Bellman consistency equations, each resolving one LLM-specific obstacle while preserving the guarantee that the softmax over learned logits equals the KL-regularized optimal policy. First, a Q-function reparameterization (Thm. 2) absorbs the reference policy into the logits so that inference is a plain softmax. Second, potential-based reward shaping (Thm. 3) shifts the Bellman equation to operate on logit *differences* from the reference, making the reference logits a zero-gradient initialization when reward is zero. Third, a multi-step telescoping extension (Thm. 4, adapted from path consistency learning) replaces the one-step Bellman residual with a full-trajectory return, propagating sparse sequence-level rewards to every token in a single update without importance sampling. The final loss L_ShiQ is a single-network, off-policy, token-level squared Bellman residual that can be optimized by standard SGD on mini-batches, with no target network, no value head, and no paired data requirement.

## Results

On the single-turn Anthropic-Harmless/Helpful (HH) dataset with 7B Cohere R7B models, ShiQ and CoPG achieve comparable reward optimization and KL divergence behavior, while DPO and DRO exhibit a more limited reward range. On the multi-turn BFCL-V3 function-calling benchmark, ShiQ outperforms both multi-turn DPO and CoPG by leveraging full information about per-turn reward positions. In the synthetic fine-grained 5×5 grid-world MDP, only ShiQ consistently discovers an intermediate reward at (3,5) while still reaching the terminal goal, driving regret close to zero, whereas DPO and CoPG fail to exploit the intermediate signal. In the offline 3-armed bandit, ShiQ, CoPG, and ShiQ/init all converge to the analytically optimal policy, while DPO converges to a biased solution.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CoPG (Flet-Berliac et al., 2024), ShiQ matches performance on the single-turn HH dataset (comparable reward and KL curves) but clearly outperforms it on the multi-turn BFCL-V3 benchmark, where ShiQ's token-level, multi-step Bellman loss exploits per-turn reward positions that CoPG's sequence-level bandit formulation cannot capture. Against DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023), ShiQ wins across all settings: DPO converges to a biased policy in the offline bandit (due to non-Bradley-Terry preference simulation), fails to exploit intermediate rewards in the fine-grained MDP, and shows a narrower reward range on HH. Against DRO (Richemond et al., 2024), ShiQ achieves a wider reward range on the single-turn HH dataset while requiring only a single network (no separate value head), making it more memory-efficient. Against multi-turn DPO (Rafailov et al., 2024), ShiQ outperforms on BFCL-V3, benefiting from its ability to use unranked single-trajectory data rather than paired trajectories. A key structural advantage of ShiQ over all baselines is that it is a token-level loss (one square term per token) rather than a sequence-level loss, enabling it to propagate fine-grained reward signals that bandit-based methods fundamentally cannot process.

## Relation to Prior Work

ShiQ sits at the intersection of regularized MDP theory (Geist et al. 2019, Ziebart 2010), Q-function reparameterization (Vieillard et al. 2020b), potential-based reward shaping (Ng et al. 1999, Wiewiora 2003), and path consistency learning (Nachum et al. 2017), combining these into a single Bellman-consistency loss tailored to LLM fine-tuning. It departs from the dominant policy-gradient paradigm (PPO, REINFORCE) by being off-policy and token-wise, and from bandit-based alignment methods (DPO, CoPG, DRO) by exploiting the temporal structure of autoregressive generation rather than treating each completion as a single arm. Compared to prior Q-learning-for-LLM efforts (Guo et al. 2022, Yu et al. 2024), ShiQ eliminates the need for target networks, dueling architectures, or separate value heads, relying solely on the model's own logits with the reference model providing a natural initialization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ShiQ offers a theoretically grounded, single-network, off-policy alternative to policy-gradient fine-tuning that can exploit token-level reward signals and multi-turn structure—capabilities that bandit-based methods fundamentally lack. Its practical value is strongest in multi-turn and fine-grained-reward settings (e.g., function calling on BFCL-V3), where it outperforms multi-turn DPO and CoPG by leveraging full reward-position information. Key limitations include reliance on a reliable reward model, evaluation on a limited set of benchmarks (all 7B Cohere models), and exclusively offline data; extending to online/heterogeneous sampling, classical RL domains, and distillation are identified as open directions. The assumption of a trustworthy reward function remains a practical bottleneck that KL regularization only partially mitigates.
