## Problem

Existing post-hoc time series explanation methods compute saliency at the individual timestep level, which fragments local temporal dependencies and fails to capture the holistic influence of key subsequences (shapelets) that actually drive classification outcomes. While shapelets are widely recognized as the fundamental discriminative units in time series, no prior explanation method aligns its perturbation and attribution with these semantically meaningful patterns, resulting in scattered, less interpretable saliency maps.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250897/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

SHAPEX takes a trained black-box classifier (Transformer, LSTM, CNN, or MultiRocket) and a univariate time series of length T as input. During training, the SDD module is fit on the dataset to learn N shapelets of fixed length L; at inference, the learned shapelets produce activation maps that, after thresholding, define a set of shapelet-aligned segments. The output is a Shapley-value saliency score per segment, which is then distributed uniformly across constituent timesteps to yield a T-length saliency map compatible with standard evaluation metrics (AUPRC, AUP, AUR).

## Method

SHAPEX operates in two phases. In training, the Shapelet Describe-and-Detect (SDD) framework learns a compact set of shapelets via a descriptor (1D convolution using shapelets as kernels to produce activation maps) and a detector (peak-activation localization to extract matching subsequences), optimized jointly with three losses: classification expressiveness, morphological matching, and angular diversity. At inference, the learned shapelets segment the test series into shapelet-aligned regions; a Shapelet-Driven Segment-Level (SDSL) perturbation masks or retains entire segments using linear interpolation at boundaries to avoid artificial discontinuities. Shapley values are then computed over these segments as players, with a temporally-relational subset strategy restricting coalitions to locally connected segments to reduce complexity from O(N!) to O(N). The paper further proves that the resulting Shapley value is equivalent to a model-level Conditional Average Treatment Effect (CATE), providing a causal rather than merely correlational interpretation of the saliency scores.

## Results

On the four synthetic motif-based datasets (MCC-E, MCC-H, MTC-E, MTC-H), SHAPEX achieves AUPRC scores of 0.641, 0.811, 0.610, and 0.679 respectively, surpassing the second-best method by an average of 58.12% in AUPRC (largest gain: 146.07% on MTC-E) and by 21.09% in AUR. On the real-world ECG dataset, SHAPEX attains AUPRC 0.723, AUP 0.840, and AUR 0.696, outperforming the strongest baseline TIMEX++ (AUPRC 0.660, AUP 0.726, AUR 0.460). Across the full UCR Archive (112 datasets), SHAPEX consistently achieves the highest average occlusion AUROC and the most stable degradation curve under varying perturbation ratios.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SHAPEX outperforms all seven baselines on AUPRC across every synthetic dataset and the ECG dataset. The strongest competitor is TIMEX++, which achieves AUPRC 0.639 on MCC-H and 0.390 on MTC-H but is surpassed by SHAPEX by 27.2% and 73.9% respectively; on ECG, TIMEX++ reaches AUPRC 0.660 versus SHAPEX's 0.723. CoRTX is the second-strongest baseline on MCC-H (AUPRC 0.620) but falls well behind SHAPEX (0.811) and is particularly weak on MTC-E (0.188 vs. 0.610). TIMEX shows competitive AUP scores on MTC-E (0.890) and MTC-H (0.900) but lags significantly in AUPRC and AUR. Gradient-based methods (IG, WinIT, MILLET) consistently underperform, with WinIT and MILLET scoring below 0.35 AUPRC on most datasets. Dynamask is the weakest perturbation-based baseline, with AUPRC below 0.35 on all synthetic datasets. Notably, SHAPEX_SF (using ShapeFormer's shapelets instead of SDD) underperforms full SHAPEX, confirming that explanation-aware shapelet learning is critical.

## Relation to Prior Work

SHAPEX sits at the intersection of three research threads: the shapelet literature (Ye & Keogh, 2009; ShapeFormer, 2024), Shapley-value-based attribution (Shapley, 1953; SHAP, 2017), and the Describe-and-Detect paradigm from image anomaly detection (D2-Net, CVPR 2019). It departs from the dominant PHTSE line (TIMEX, TIMEX++, Dynamask, CoRTX, WinIT) by shifting the unit of perturbation from individual timesteps or equal-length windows to semantically coherent shapelet-aligned segments, and from prior shapelet-based classifiers by repurposing shapelets as explanation primitives rather than classification features. The theoretical contribution of equating the resulting Shapley value to a model-level CATE provides a causal-inference grounding that prior PHTSE methods, which expose only correlational importance, lack.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By being the first PHTSE work to conduct occlusion-based evaluation across the entire UCR archive (112 datasets), SHAPEX establishes a much more comprehensive benchmark for explanation quality than prior single-dataset comparisons. The causal framing (Shapley ≈ CATE) offers practitioners in high-stakes domains a stronger interpretive guarantee than correlation-based saliency, though the proof relies on SUTVA and ignorability assumptions that may not hold in all real-world settings. Key open questions include extending the framework to multivariate and irregular time series, automating the shapelet count/length/threshold hyperparameters that currently require manual tuning, and reducing the two-stage training overhead that limits plug-and-play usability.
