## Problem

Pretrained foundation models for physiological signals remain scarce: while EEG-specific encoders exist, no large-scale pretrained models have been developed for EMG or ECG, and existing time-series foundation models (Moment, OTiS) are not modality-aware. Physiological signals are strongly non-stationary, corrupted by motion artifacts and baseline drift, and exhibit high inter-subject variability, making standard time-domain or fixed-window frequency methods inadequate for capturing their multi-scale structure. Additionally, no unified framework exists for fusing heterogeneous multi-modal biosignals (EEG, EMG, ECG) with different sampling rates and temporal scales.

## Input / Output

The model ingests raw multi-channel physiological signals (12-lead ECG at 500 Hz, 8-channel surface EMG at 2 kHz, multi-channel EEG, or single-channel PPG) segmented into overlapping windows of 2048 (ECG) or 1024 (EMG) samples. The learnable wavelet front-end expands each C-channel input into (L+1)C subband channels before patching, so the Transformer operates on frequency-band tokens rather than raw time-domain patches. Downstream outputs include multi-label class predictions (arrhythmia, gesture, emotion, driving behavior), continuous heart-rate estimates, and frozen latent representations for linear probing in multi-modal fusion.

## Method

PhysioWave combines a learnable multi-level wavelet decomposition with a Transformer masked-autoencoder. The wavelet front-end maintains M candidate wavelet bases whose low-pass/high-pass taps are blended via a softmax-weighted Adaptive Wavelet Selector conditioned on the input; each decomposition level applies filtering, downsampling, and an attention-based per-channel soft gate (replacing hard U-Net skip connections) to re-insert fine-scale detail, followed by a Cross-Scale CAFFN that fuses current-level features with coarser-level context via multi-head attention. For self-supervised pretraining, Frequency-guided Masking (FgM) computes FFT-based spectral energy per patch, blends it with uniform noise (controlled by α), and masks the highest-scoring patches, forcing the RoPE-attention Transformer encoder to reconstruct information-rich frequency-band segments through a deliberately shallow decoder. In multi-modal settings, all modality-specific encoders are frozen and only lightweight classification heads plus softmax-normalized fusion weights are trained, enabling reliability-aware aggregation across heterogeneous signals.

## Results

On ECG arrhythmia classification, PhysioWave-Large (37M params) achieves 66.7% F1 / 94.6% AUROC on PTB-XL, 73.1% F1 / 96.1% AUROC on CPSC 2018, and 54.8% F1 / 98.3% AUROC on Chapman-Shaoxing, surpassing the 13B-parameter ECG-Chat by +10.8% F1 on PTB-XL. On EMG gesture recognition, the same 37M model reaches 94.50% accuracy / 94.56% F1 on EPN-612 and 93.19% / 93.59% on UCI EMG, outperforming Moment (385M) by +3.6% and +2.6% respectively. In multi-modal settings, fusing EEG+ECG+EMG lifts DEAP arousal accuracy by +7.3% and MPDB driving-behavior accuracy by +4.5% over single-modality baselines. The compact PPG variant (1.57M params) achieves 0.6209 activity-recognition accuracy, exceeding Pulse-PPG (28.5M) by +46.7% accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On ECG, PhysioWave-Large (37M) decisively outperforms ECG-Chat (13B) on PTB-XL F1 (66.7% vs 55.9%, +10.8%) and AUROC (94.6% vs 94.1%), while on CPSC 2018 it narrows the F1 gap to ECG-Chat from 80.1% to 73.1% but still trails by 7.0% F1, though it wins on AUROC (96.1% vs 95.7%). All three OpenECG variants (SimCLR, BYOL, MAE; 11M each) are surpassed by even the 5M-parameter PhysioWave-Small on PTB-XL F1 (65.8% vs 46.9–48.1%). On EMG, PhysioWave-Large beats Moment (385M) on EPN-612 (94.50% vs 90.87% accuracy) and UCI EMG (93.19% vs 90.45%), and surpasses OTiS (45M) on all three EMG benchmarks; however, on NinaPro DB5 the Small model (84.78%) slightly trails Moment (86.41%), though the Large model recovers the lead (87.53%). In multi-modal DEAP, the fused system outperforms TPRO-Net by 0.4% and BILSTM+attention / Bayesian pipelines by 7–22%; on MPDB it exceeds MMPNet and EEGNet by 9–17%. For EEG sleep staging, the enhanced PhysioWave (trained from scratch) surpasses pretrained EEGPT (0.7312 vs 0.6917 balanced accuracy) and LaBraM (0.6771) on Sleep-EDF. On PPG, the 1.57M-parameter PhysioWave-PPG outperforms Pulse-PPG (28.5M) by +63.1% F1 on activity recognition and achieves lower MAE (5.22 vs 3.705) than Deep PPG and NAS-PPG on heart rate regression.

## Relation to Prior Work

PhysioWave repurposes classical multi-resolution wavelet decomposition as a learnable, differentiable front-end to a Transformer encoder, departing from both fixed-filter DSP pipelines and the raw-patch tokenization used by generic time-series foundation models (Moment, OTiS). Its self-supervised objective extends the masked-autoencoder paradigm (MAE, HuBERT, BEiT) with a Frequency-guided Masking strategy that replaces random token occlusion with spectral-energy-driven selection, drawing on SpecAugment's frequency-masking intuition. In the biosignal-specific lineage, the paper positions itself as the first to provide large-scale pretrained encoders for EMG and ECG, complementing existing EEG foundation models (LaBraM, EEGPT) and the cross-modal ECG-Chat, while unifying all three modalities under a single multi-modal fusion framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By filling the long-standing absence of dedicated foundation models for EMG and ECG, PhysioWave gives the community reusable, modality-aware backbones that can be linearly probed or fine-tuned for a wide range of downstream clinical and BCI tasks, reducing the need for task-specific architecture design. The learnable wavelet front-end offers a principled, data-driven alternative to hand-crafted band-pass and notch filtering, potentially generalizing to new signal types without re-engineering the preprocessing pipeline. Open questions include whether the softmax-weighted logit fusion scales gracefully beyond three modalities, whether the wavelet decomposition's benefits persist at much larger model scales, and whether the from-scratch EEG/PPG results in the appendix (which lack the large-scale pretraining that drives the main ECG/EMG gains) can be matched with dedicated pretrained variants.
