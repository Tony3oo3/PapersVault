## Problem

Long-term BCI decoding suffers from channel-level variability across days caused by neuron loss, electrode shifts, and neuron drift, which degrades a Day-0-trained decoder's performance. Existing manifold-alignment and domain-adaptation methods struggle when neural signals exhibit significant drift or when a substantial fraction of channels change, and large-scale pretraining approaches (POYO, NDT2) require prohibitive training costs. The paper asks whether a lightweight, channel-level invariant representation can be learned that explicitly handles both missing and drifted neurons without requiring massive pretraining data.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-channel neural spike signals or EMG recordings organized as tensors X_d ∈ R^{N×C×T} (N samples, C channels, T time points), where C ranges from 8 (EMG muscles) to 192 (microelectrode array channels). It produces a channel-invariant representation aligned to a reference Day-0 space, which is then fed to a Day-0-trained downstream decoder (SVM for classification, Wiener filter for regression) to yield hand trajectories, 8-direction labels, EMG waveforms, speech word labels, or handwriting characters. A key constraint is that the decoder is trained exclusively on Day-0 data and never sees later-day signals; all cross-day adaptation is handled upstream by CRRL.

## Method

CRRL decomposes channel variability into two sub-problems solved by dedicated modules. The Rearrangement module (RA) uses a shared-parameter MLP to predict per-sample permutation matrices via Gumbel-Sinkhorn continuous relaxation, trained with a negative Pearson correlation loss (focusing on signal shape rather than amplitude) plus entropy regularization to push the doubly stochastic matrix toward a true permutation; at inference the Hungarian algorithm discretizes to an exact permutation. The Reconstruction module (RC) is a channel-based VQ-VAE where each channel's time series is embedded as a token, cross-channel multi-head attention captures inter-channel dependencies, a vector quantization layer discretizes the latent space into a learned codebook (exploiting the discrete nature of spike signals), and an MLP decoder reconstructs both time-domain and frequency-domain (amplitude and phase via DFT) features. Training is strictly two-stage: RA is trained first and frozen, then RC is trained on a 7:3 mixture of Day-0 and RA-rearranged Day-k data with random channel-segment masking. This separation allows each module to target a specific failure mode—permutation for drift, codebook reconstruction for loss—while keeping the total parameter count (~6.8M) well below large-scale pretraining baselines.

## Results

On the Center-Out (C) dataset, CRRL achieves R²/acc of 0.75/77.1% at [5,10) days, surpassing SD-Net (0.73/69.3%) and StabilizedBCI (0.59/56.6%), and retains 0.65/66.8% at [20,40) days. On the long-term ISO (J) dataset, CRRL reaches 0.47/51.1% at [65,100) days, outperforming NoMAD (0.31/39.7%) and SD-Net (0.20/34.5%). For the challenging Key (G) EMG regression, CRRL maintains 0.35 R² at [20,40) days, a 94% improvement over Cycle-GAN (0.20). On the speech dataset, CRRL recovers to 86% accuracy on day 21 while SD-Net and NoMAD decline to 62% and 42% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CRRL consistently outperforms all five cross-day baselines (StabilizedBCI, NoMAD, SD-Net, ADAN, Cycle-GAN) across every dataset and time period evaluated. The advantage grows with temporal distance: on ISO (J) at [65,100) days, CRRL achieves 0.47/51.1% R²/acc versus 0.31/39.7% (NoMAD) and 0.20/34.5% (SD-Net), a 51% and 135% relative improvement respectively. On the Key (G) EMG task, CRRL's 0.35 R² at [20,40) days represents a 94% gain over Cycle-GAN (0.20) and a 67% gain over ADAN (0.21). Against large-scale pretraining methods, CRRL without pretraining (using only 5 days of data) achieves 0.87 R² on Monkey C, approaching POYO-1's 0.97, while CRRL with sequential pretraining (0.975) surpasses both POYO-1 and NDT2 Multi (0.59/0.43 on M1-A/M2). The plug-in experiments further show that adding CRRL's RA module to Cycle-GAN boosts R² by 22.2% on Day 7 and maintains 122% higher accuracy by Day 56, confirming its compatibility with existing domain adaptation methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

CRRL builds on two well-established deep learning components — Gumbel-Sinkhorn differentiable permutation learning (Mena et al., 2018; Jang et al., 2017) and VQ-VAE discrete latent encoding (Van Den Oord et al., 2017) — and combines them into a two-stage pipeline specifically designed for the BCI cross-day decoding problem. It positions itself against two families of prior work: manifold-alignment methods (StabilizedBCI, NoMAD) that align low-dimensional neural manifolds, and domain-adaptation methods (SD-Net, ADAN, Cycle-GAN) that treat different days as different domains. The key architectural departure is explicitly modeling channel-level permutation (for drifted electrodes) and channel-level reconstruction (for lost neurons) as separate operations, rather than relying on implicit alignment in a shared latent space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Long-term BCI stability remains a critical bottleneck for clinical motor and speech rehabilitation, and CRRL demonstrates that a lightweight, plug-in channel-invariant representation can match or exceed large-scale pretraining methods (POYO, NDT2) while using only 5 days of calibration data. The method's modularity — being combinable with existing DA approaches like Cycle-GAN and ADAN — makes it practically useful for existing BCI pipelines. However, the paper acknowledges a slow residual performance decline over time, and the approach's dependence on a high-quality Day 0 reference (mitigated but not eliminated by multi-day averaging) remains an open practical concern for very long-term deployments.
