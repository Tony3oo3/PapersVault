## Problem

Existing federated clustering methods rely on model-specific local proxies (centroids, kernel factors, basis matrices) that degrade under Non-IID data distributions and inherit restrictive inductive biases from their centralized counterparts, limiting both robustness and generality. Additionally, privacy leakage during collaboration remains a fundamental concern, particularly under client collusion. No prior framework offers a unified, model-agnostic mechanism that simultaneously achieves lossless global structure recovery and information-theoretic privacy guarantees.

## Input / Output

Each client holds a local subset of n d-dimensional samples distributed across m clients, along with tunable hyperparameters l (data segments), t (noises), and a prime p for finite-field arithmetic. The method operates in a single communication round: clients share only Lagrange-encoded representations (not raw data or model parameters), and the server outputs an exact n×n global pairwise distance matrix D ∈ R^{n×n} that can be directly fed into any centralized clustering algorithm to produce the final partition π*. The one-shot design makes it applicable to scenarios like model markets where multi-round training is infeasible.

## Method

OmniFC reconstructs the global pairwise distance matrix via Lagrange coded computing in three steps: (1) each sample is mapped to a finite field F_p^d, split into l segments, and combined with t random noises to form a degree-(l+t−1) Lagrange polynomial; (2) each client evaluates the polynomial at its assigned point β_j to produce encoded representations z_{i,j}, which are shared peer-to-peer; (3) each client computes pairwise squared distances on its encoded dataset, and the server recovers exact distances by interpolating a degree-2(l+t−1) polynomial from m client reports (requiring m ≥ 2l+2t−1). The key insight is that the squared Euclidean distance between two encoded points is itself a polynomial in β of degree 2(l+t−1), enabling exact recovery regardless of how data is partitioned across clients. This makes the framework inherently immune to Non-IID heterogeneity and model-agnostic: the reconstructed D serves as direct input for distance-based methods (SC, DBSCAN, HC, KMed) or as a surrogate feature matrix for centroid-based methods (KM, FCM, NMF). Privacy is guaranteed information-theoretically: mutual information between any private sample and the encoded data observed by up to t colluding clients is zero.

## Results

OmniFC is evaluated using Kappa (primary) and NMI (supplementary) across 7 datasets under 5 Non-IID levels (p=0 to 1). OmniFC achieves the best Kappa in 27/35 SC-based, 24/35 KM-based, 22/35 FCM-based, and 30/35 NMF-based comparisons. Critically, OmniFC's scores are invariant to Non-IID severity (e.g., 0.95 Kappa on Iris at all p values), while baselines degrade sharply (e.g., FedSC drops from 0.95 to 0.31 on Iris at p=1). On the large-scale 10x_73k genomic dataset, OmniFC-SC achieves 0.89 Kappa versus 0.24 for FedSC at p=1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against FedSC [6] (federated spectral clustering), OmniFC-SC wins 27 out of 35 comparisons; the gap is most dramatic under high Non-IID (e.g., Iris p=1: 0.95 vs 0.31 Kappa; 10x_73k p=1: 0.89 vs 0.24). Against k-FED [1] and MUFC [3] (federated k-means variants), OmniFC-KM wins 24 out of 35 comparisons, with MUFC being the stronger baseline (9 wins) but still degrading under Non-IID (e.g., COIL-100 p=1: 0.52 vs OmniFC's 0.56). Against FFCM [2] (federated fuzzy c-means), OmniFC-FCM wins 22 out of 35; FFCM is the most competitive baseline in the FCM family (13 wins) but remains sensitive to heterogeneity. Against FedMAvg [7] (federated NMF), OmniFC-NMF dominates with 30 wins out of 35, as FedMAvg degrades severely (e.g., Iris p=1: 0.50 vs 0.95). For the generality extension (Table 2), OmniFC matches centralized KMed [29], DBSCAN [15], and HC [17] exactly on all 7 datasets, confirming lossless federated extension for pairwise-distance-dependent methods. Overall, OmniFC matches or exceeds centralized baselines (SC_central, KM_central, FCM_central, NMF_central) while remaining immune to Non-IID, whereas all federated baselines degrade as p increases.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Lagrange Coded Computing (LCC) [12] for its secret-sharing and privacy mechanism, but departs fundamentally from the dominant paradigm in federated clustering of aggregating model-specific local proxies (centroids for k-means, kernel factors for spectral clustering, basis matrices for NMF). Instead, it reframes the problem around lossless reconstruction of the global pairwise distance matrix—a model-agnostic representation—thereby decoupling the federated protocol from any particular clustering algorithm's inductive biases. The closest prior work, SecFC [23], also leverages LCC but is restricted to k-means and requires multi-round communication, whereas OmniFC provides a one-shot, unified extension mechanism applicable to spectral clustering, DBSCAN, hierarchical clustering, k-medoids, and beyond.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified distance-matrix perspective offers a clean theoretical and practical path to extending any centralized clustering method to the federated setting with guaranteed lossless reconstruction and information-theoretic privacy, while being inherently immune to Non-IID degradation—a persistent weakness of proxy-aggregation methods. However, the n×n distance matrix imposes quadratic memory and computation costs, necessitating subsampling for datasets beyond ~5000 samples, which limits scalability to truly large-scale deployments. The paper demonstrates preliminary feasibility for deep clustering (OmniFC-DCN) but leaves open how the framework handles high-dimensional or intrinsically complex data where shallow distance-based methods are less effective, and whether the one-shot protocol can be extended to iterative deep representation learning.
