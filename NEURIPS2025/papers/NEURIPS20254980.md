## Problem

Deployed text-to-image diffusion models accumulate outdated, incorrect, or biased assumptions over time, and users flag these failures post-deployment. Repairing such failures requires specialized expertise that service providers often lack, but sharing the model, user feedback, or repair algorithms with an external institute violates privacy regulations, IP protections, and business confidentiality. No prior framework enables a service provider and a repair institute to collaboratively fix a diffusion model while keeping all three parties' private information mutually hidden.

## Input / Output

The service provider supplies a 100-dimensional query key derived from aggregated user feedback and its 32 cross-attention layers' key/value projection matrices (each [320, 768], single-precision float). The institute supplies a repair database of n entries, each pairing a 100-dimensional semantic key with a [768, 768] fix matrix computed offline from source/destination prompt embeddings (shape [768, 77]) and a regularization hyperparameter. The output is a single [768, 768] fix matrix delivered to the service provider via oblivious transfer, which it right-multiplies onto every W_K and W_V layer locally; the repaired model is returned only to the provider, and the institute learns nothing about the query or model weights.

## Method

SURE co-designs a crypto-friendly editing algorithm with a lightweight two-party computation protocol. The key algebraic insight is refactoring the TIME closed-form update W' = (λW + W C*C^T)(λI + CC^T)^{-1} into W' = W · W_fix, where W_fix = (λI + C*C^T)(λI + CC^T)^{-1} depends only on institute-held embeddings and is computed entirely offline, decoupling model-specific weights from the repair logic. The online protocol has two stages: (1) a small 2PC circuit that privately computes argmin_j d(k_qry, k_j) to identify the matching fix index, and (2) a 1-out-of-n oblivious transfer that delivers only the selected W_fix matrix without revealing the index to the institute. Because the same fix matrix applies uniformly to all 32 layers and both K and V projections, the OT cost is constant regardless of model depth, and no matrix multiplication or inversion ever occurs inside the secure computation. This yields a 4-orders-of-magnitude speedup over a generic MP-SPDZ baseline (under 17 s vs. over 100 h for a full Stable Diffusion v1.4 repair), while a formal security proof in the (F_OT, F_2PC)-hybrid model guarantees that the institute learns nothing about the model or query, the provider learns only the single relevant fix, and the editing algorithm itself remains hidden.

## Results

SURE completes a full 32-layer repair of Stable Diffusion v1.4 in under 17 seconds (16.77 s at 3,000 database entries), with communication capped at 17.09 GB. In contrast, the generic 2PC baseline requires 167–189 hours and 76–106 TB of communication for the same task. This yields a runtime speedup of 4.07×10⁴ to 2.14×10⁵ (four to five orders of magnitude) depending on database size. The OT-based model repair phase remains nearly constant regardless of database size, while the 2PC matching phase accounts for most of the runtime.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares SURE against a single baseline: a generic 2PC protocol (implemented with MP-SPDZ's semi-honest variant) that executes the entire TIME editing formula as an arithmetic circuit inside secure computation. SURE wins decisively on both runtime and communication across all tested database sizes (500–3,000 entries), achieving a 4–5 order-of-magnitude speedup (e.g., 2.81 s vs. 167.36 h at 500 entries; 16.77 s vs. 189.44 h at 3,000 entries). Communication is reduced by roughly four orders of magnitude as well (17.09 GB vs. 105.86 TB at 3,000 entries). The dramatic gap stems from SURE's co-design: all matrix operations are shifted offline to local computation, leaving only a small 2PC matching circuit and a lightweight OT-based retrieval in the online phase, whereas the baseline must securely compute the full closed-form update (including matrix multiplications and inversions) for every layer inside 2PC.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the TIME editing algorithm (Orgad et al., ICCV 2023), which updates cross-attention key/value projection matrices via a closed-form formula to correct implicit assumptions in text-to-image diffusion models, and extends it to the more general UCE framework. The key departure is a co-design principle: rather than naively encoding the editing formula into a generic 2PC circuit (which the authors show takes >100 hours), they algebraically refactor the update so that all matrix operations are computed offline by the repair institute, reducing the online cryptographic workload to a small matching circuit and a single oblivious transfer. This positions the work at the intersection of knowledge editing in diffusion models and secure multi-party computation, drawing on OT (Rabin) and 2PC (Yao; Goldreich et al.) as building blocks but contributing a customized protocol rather than a generic application.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SURE is the first framework to make secure, mutually blind model repair practical for commercial-scale diffusion models (17 seconds for all 32 layers of SD v1.4), addressing a real deployment gap where service providers cannot share proprietary weights, user feedback is protected by GDPR, and repair institutes guard their IP. The co-design methodology—jointly optimizing the ML algorithm and cryptographic protocol—offers a template for other secure ML tasks where generic 2PC is infeasible. Key limitations include restriction to linear weight edits in text-to-image diffusion models, no batching for multiple simultaneous repairs, and reliance on a semi-honest threat model (malicious extension adds ~9× overhead). Open questions include extending the framework to LLMs, non-linear or architecture-changing edits, and amortizing costs across many concurrent repair requests.
