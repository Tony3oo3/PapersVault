## Problem

Translating natural language instructions into executable agent policies has historically required either costly in-domain supervision (labeling trajectories with language), reward engineering (which is prone to reward hacking), or test-time training for each new task. No prior approach achieves zero-shot, gradient-free policy inference from arbitrary language prompts or cross-embodied video demonstrations without any in-domain task-specific labels.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252794/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts either a natural language instruction (e.g., 'walk like a human') or a cross-embodied video demonstration (e.g., a YouTube clip of a human doing a cartwheel) as input, and produces a single closed-loop policy parameterized by a latent vector z in the BFM's skill space. The policy operates on 64×64 RGB image observations plus proprioceptive state in continuous-control environments (state dimensions 17–358, action dimensions 6–69). Critically, the output policy is obtained in closed form in ~25 seconds with no gradient updates, no environment interaction, and no per-task model training, as opposed to baselines that require 3–5 hours of test-time training per prompt.

## Method

RLZero decomposes language-to-policy into three stages: (1) **Imagine** — an unsupervised video generative model (InternVideo2 encoder + GRU, trained via the GenRL reconstruction objective without any text-task labels) produces a sequence of video frames from the language prompt; (2) **Project** — each imagined frame is grounded to a real observation from the offline dataset via SigLIP cosine-similarity retrieval over frame stacks of k=3 frames (to capture velocity/acceleration), yielding a sequence of proprioceptive states; (3) **Imitate** — a successor-measure-based BFM (Forward-Backward) pretrained on reward-free RND-collected offline transitions provides a closed-form solution to state-marginal distribution matching. The key theoretical result (Theorem 1) shows that minimizing KL divergence between the projected trajectory's state distribution and the policy's state distribution reduces to computing z_imit = E_{ρ^E}[φ(s)] using the shaped reward r(s) = ρ^E(s)/ρ(s), requiring no discriminator training, no gradient steps, and no environment interaction. This avoids the reward-hacking vulnerability of VLM-as-reward approaches and eliminates the need for per-task test-time training entirely.

## Results

On 25 language-to-behavior tasks across 4 DeepMind Control Suite environments, RLZero achieves an 83.2% GPT-4o-judged win rate over the TD3 base model and a distribution-matching return of 295.11, outperforming the strongest baseline GenRL (76.8%, 246.48) and VLM-reward offline RL methods (51.2% for IQL, 44.8% for video-language IQL). In cross-embodied video-to-behavior generation on 17 tasks, RLZero achieves 80% win rate on Stickman (2D humanoid) and 100% on HumEnv (3D SMPL humanoid) against SMODICE, with distribution-matching returns up to 4× higher (e.g., 199.90 vs 50.02 for a karate kick). Critically, RLZero requires ~25 seconds of inference per task with zero gradient steps, compared to ~3–5 hours of test-time training for GenRL.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RLZero outperforms all three baseline families on both win rate and distribution-matching return. Against VLM-reward offline RL (VLM-RL [62] with IQL or TD3), RLZero achieves 83.2% vs. 51.2% (image-language IQL) and 44.8% (video-language IQL) win rates, with distribution-matching returns roughly 2× higher (295.11 vs. 148.97/118.79); these baselines also require training a separate policy per task. Against GenRL [49], the closest competitor which uses model-based RL at test time, RLZero wins 83.2% vs. 76.8% and achieves a 20% higher distribution-matching return (295.11 vs. 246.48), while being orders of magnitude faster (~25 s vs. 3–5 h per task on an A40 GPU) and requiring no test-time training. In the cross-embodied setting, RLZero beats SMODICE [46] with 80% win rate on Stickman and 100% on HumEnv, with distribution-matching returns up to 4× higher (e.g., 199.90 vs. 50.02 for a karate kick), confirming that successor-measure-based zero-shot imitation surpasses DICE-style state-occupancy matching for observation-only imitation. A key structural advantage is that RLZero uses a single pretrained BFM for all tasks per environment, whereas every baseline trains a new policy model per prompt.

## Relation to Prior Work

RLZero composes three existing research threads into a novel pipeline: (1) the unsupervised video-generation approach from GenRL [49] for the 'imagine' step, (2) successor-measure-based unsupervised RL (Forward-Backward [76]) for a Behavior Foundation Model that supports closed-form policy retrieval, and (3) the shaped-reward distribution-matching trick from Pirotta et al. [57] that eliminates the need for a learned discriminator. The key departure from prior language-conditioned RL is that all components are trained without in-domain task labels or expert trajectories, and the final policy is obtained via a single closed-form linear regression rather than test-time gradient descent. This contrasts with VLM-as-reward methods (VLM-RL, Text2Reward) that remain vulnerable to reward hacking, and with GenRL, which still requires 3–5 hours of model-based RL per prompt.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By removing both in-domain supervision and test-time training, RLZero offers a practical path toward generalist agents that respond to natural-language commands in ~25 seconds with a single pretrained policy, sidestepping the reward-design bottleneck that has limited RL's accessibility. The cross-embodiment result—mimicking YouTube or AI-generated human videos on a 3D SMPL humanoid using an off-the-shelf BFM—suggests the framework could transfer to real robots given a suitable BFM. Open questions include whether the small video-generation model and SigLIP-based retrieval scale to more complex, high-dimensional embodiments, whether the GPT-4o-as-judge evaluation faithfully captures human preference, and whether the approach generalizes beyond the DeepMind Control Suite and HumEnv to contact-rich or dexterous manipulation tasks.
