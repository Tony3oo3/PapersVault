## Problem

Distribution testing has well-characterized sample complexities, but the cost of requiring algorithmic replicability—producing the same output on two independent sample sets with high probability—was largely unexplored. The only prior work (Liu and Ye, 2024) addressed uniformity testing under a restrictive symmetry assumption on the tester, leaving open whether near-optimal lower bounds hold for general testers and how replicability affects other canonical problems like closeness and independence testing.

## Input / Output

The tester receives i.i.d. sample access to one distribution over [n] (uniformity), two distributions over [n] (closeness), or one distribution over [n₁]×[n₂] (independence), and outputs a single accept/reject decision. The algorithm must simultaneously satisfy two constraints: ε-correctness (distinguishing the property from ε-far instances with probability ≥ 1−ρ) and ρ-replicability (agreeing on two independent sample sets with probability ≥ 1−ρ). The central quantity studied is the minimum sample complexity m as a function of n, ε, and ρ.

## Method

For lower bounds, the paper introduces a meta-distribution over hard instances and constructs a random walk on the sample-count space whose stationary distribution matches the expected acceptance probability. The key insight is that moderate replicability (1−1/polylog(n)) forces the acceptance probability under any individual instance to concentrate around its expectation, bypassing the symmetry assumption used in prior work; fast mixing (polylog(n) steps) is shown via spectral gap analysis of per-coordinate Poisson random walks. For upper bounds, the paper applies a random-thresholding strategy to high-probability testers: compute a test statistic Z and compare it to a uniformly random threshold in [0, R/2], so that variance concentration ensures replicability. For independence testing, where the flattening procedure inflates total variance, the paper introduces an averaged statistic Z_a (expectation over internal randomness) and bounds its variance by the expected non-singleton sample count via an Efron-Stein-style inequality, then uses a two-stage test that first verifies the non-singleton count is within bounds before applying the main closeness test.

## Results

The paper establishes tight (up to polylog factors) sample complexity characterizations for replicable distribution testing. For uniformity testing over [n], the sample complexity is $\tilde{\Theta}(\sqrt{n}\,\varepsilon^{-2}\rho^{-1} + \varepsilon^{-2}\rho^{-2})$ (Theorem 1.3), settling the main open problem from Liu and Ye [2024]. For closeness testing, matching upper and lower bounds of $\tilde{\Theta}(n^{2/3}\varepsilon^{-4/3}\rho^{-2/3} + \sqrt{n}\,\varepsilon^{-2}\rho^{-1} + \varepsilon^{-2}\rho^{-2})$ are proven (Theorems 1.4–1.5). For independence testing over $[n_1]\times[n_2]$, an upper bound of $\tilde{O}(n_1^{2/3}n_2^{1/3}/(\rho^{2/3}\varepsilon^{4/3}) + \sqrt{n_1 n_2}/(\rho\varepsilon^2) + 1/(\varepsilon^2\rho^2))$ is given (Theorem 1.6), conjectured to be nearly optimal.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary prior work is Liu and Ye [2024], which designed a replicable uniformity tester and proved a lower bound, but only against symmetric testers (those invariant under domain relabeling). This paper's lower bound framework removes the symmetry assumption entirely, yielding unconditional lower bounds against all testers via a random-walk-based concentration argument (Lemma 3.6), thus settling the open problem. For the algorithmic side, the paper builds on the high-probability testers of Diakonikolas et al. [2021], which achieve sample complexities of the same functional form after replacing $\rho^{-1}$ with $\sqrt{\log(1/\delta)}$; the key technical contribution is converting these into replicable testers via random thresholding and, for independence testing, an Efron-Stein-style variance bound on an averaged statistic (Lemma 2.9) that overcomes the variance inflation caused by the flattening procedure. The paper also draws on the averaging idea from Aliakbarpour et al. [2019] (differentially private testing) to stabilize the independence statistic. No direct empirical baselines exist, as the contribution is entirely theoretical.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the single prior work on replicable distribution testing (Liu and Ye, NeurIPS 2024, which addressed only uniformity testing under a symmetry assumption on the tester) into a systematic framework covering uniformity, closeness, and independence testing. The lower bound methodology departs from Liu and Ye's symmetry-based argument by introducing a random walk on the sample space whose mixing properties allow concentration of acceptance probabilities without any structural assumption on the tester. On the algorithmic side, the paper repurposes the high-probability testers of Diakonikolas et al. (STOC 2021) and the flattening technique of Diakonikolas and Kane (FOCS 2016), while borrowing the averaging-over-internal-randomness trick from the differentially private testing literature (Aliakbarpour et al., NeurIPS 2019) to control variance in the independence tester.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper settles the main open question from Liu and Ye by proving a near-optimal lower bound for replicable uniformity testing, and provides the first tight characterization (up to polylog factors) of replicable closeness testing. The lower bound framework—combining Poissonization, a product random walk with fast mixing, and an Efron-Stein-style variance bound—is general and likely applicable to other replicable testing problems. A striking open question is whether there exists a generic reduction between high-probability testers and replicable testers, given that the sample complexity formulas match up to the substitution ρ⁻¹ ↔ √(log(1/δ)); resolving this could unify two previously separate lines of work. The independence testing upper bound is conjectured to be near-optimal but lacks a matching lower bound.
