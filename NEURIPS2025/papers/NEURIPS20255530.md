## Problem

Standard LM pretraining requires centralized access to all training data and offers no mechanism to selectively include or exclude the influence of specific data sources at inference time. Data owners in regulated industries (healthcare, finance) cannot share their datasets due to privacy regulations, IP protections, or licensing constraints, yet existing distributed training approaches like federated learning suffer from performance degradation and high synchronization costs. FLEXOLMO addresses this by enabling independent, asynchronous expert training on locally maintained data while supporting fine-grained opt-in and opt-out of data sources during inference with no further training.

## Input / Output

The method takes a frozen public base model (OLMo 2 7B, 32 layers, hidden dim 4096) and independently trained expert modules (FFNs plus router embeddings) contributed by separate data owners, each trained on 50B tokens of their own dataset. At inference, the resulting 37B-parameter MoE (20B active, top-4 out of 8 experts) processes text input and produces generated text, with the ability to add or remove any expert module by simply including or excluding its router embedding row—enabling data opt-out at zero retraining cost. The final model is evaluated on 31 downstream tasks spanning general benchmarks (MMLU, BBH, MC9, GEN5) and domain-specific evaluations (math, code, news generation, poem generation, scientific literature).

## Method

FLEXOLMO replaces each transformer block's FFN with a router and n independently trained expert FFNs. During training, each data owner trains their expert using the frozen public model as a shared anchor: a two-expert MoE is constructed where the public expert and shared attention layers are frozen while only the new expert's FFN and its router embedding are updated on the owner's data, teaching experts to coordinate with the public model and by extension with each other. The router is decomposed into per-expert embedding vectors initialized from domain-specific document embeddings (via the GRIT embedder) and finetuned during coordinated training; at merge time, these embeddings are simply concatenated into the full router matrix, eliminating the need for joint training. A negative bias term is added to each expert's routing score to make the learned decision boundary more selective, improving multi-expert competition at inference. Optionally, a lightweight router tuning step on small proxy subsets of public data (selected via a binary classifier) can further improve routing quality without accessing closed data.

## Results

FLEXOLMO (8 experts, 37B total / 20B active parameters) achieves an average score of 52.4 across 31 downstream tasks, a 41% relative improvement over the public-only baseline (42.4). In the 4-expert ablation setting, FLEXOLMO scores 47.8 on average, outperforming the best prior merging baseline BTM (43.4) by 10.1% relative and the compute-matched Unrestricted MoE (46.3) by 1.5 points. The largest gains appear on domain-specific benchmarks: Math2 improves from 8.1 to 50.7, Code4 from 1.0 to 17.3, and BBH from 35.6 to 47.1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FLEXOLMO outperforms all prior model merging baselines. Against BTM (the strongest baseline at 43.4 avg), FLEXOLMO achieves 47.8 avg, a 10.1% relative improvement, with particularly large margins on Math2 (50.7 vs. 21.2) and BBH (47.1 vs. 43.6). Model Soup (weighted, 42.2 avg) and BTX (40.0 avg) trail FLEXOLMO by 5.6 and 7.8 points respectively, with BTX performing especially poorly on Code4 (6.4 vs. 17.3). Prompt-based routing is highly unstable, scoring 40.8 (OLMo router) and 40.0 (Llama router) on average. FLEXOLMO also surpasses the compute-matched Unrestricted MoE (1× FLOPs, 46.3 avg) by 1.5 points, though it falls short of the data-matched Unrestricted MoE (2× FLOPs, 51.5 avg) by 3.7 points, indicating a small performance cost for maintaining data separation.

## Relation to Prior Work

FLEXOLMO sits at the intersection of federated learning, model merging, and mixture-of-experts architectures, but departs from each in key ways. Unlike federated learning, it requires no synchronous aggregation or inter-client communication; unlike BTX/DEMix, it eliminates the need for joint training on a union of all datasets after merging; and unlike standard MoE, its motivation is modularity and data governance rather than compute efficiency. The core technical contribution is the 'coordinated training' strategy—freezing a public model as a shared anchor so independently trained experts learn to compose—and the domain-informed router whose per-expert embeddings can be concatenated at merge time without any joint optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work offers a practically viable path for regulated industries (healthcare, finance, legal) to benefit from domain-specific data in LM training while keeping that data local and supporting inference-time opt-out—a capability no prior method provides with architectural guarantees. The 41% average improvement over the public-only model and 10.1% over the best merging baseline (BTM) are well-supported by ablations across 31 tasks, and the synergy effect (combined experts outperforming all individuals even on tasks where no single expert helps) suggests the MoE routing captures complementary strengths that simpler ensembling misses. Open questions include how the approach scales to dozens or hundreds of experts, whether coordinated training generalizes across different base architectures, and whether the small but nonzero data-extraction risk (0.7%) is acceptable without mandatory DP training.
