## Problem

While Riemannian gradient flow (equivalent to Langevin dynamics) is well-established in Wasserstein space for minimizing KL divergence, the stochastic variants—SGD and SVRG—have no continuous-time (flow) counterparts in this setting. The core difficulty is that standard Euclidean continuous stochastic dynamics rely on Brownian motion, which is nontrivial to define on Riemannian manifolds, and the linear structure needed to pass from discrete updates to differential equations does not exist on general manifolds.

## Input / Output

The method takes a target distribution μ ∝ exp(−V(x)), an initial probability measure π₀, and a finite set of component distributions {μ_ξ} (e.g., Gaussians or Gaussian mixtures parameterized by ξ). It produces a deterministic Fokker-Planck equation governing the evolution of π_t in Wasserstein space, along with explicit convergence rate bounds on KL divergence and Riemannian gradient norm. Notably, the 'stochastic' flows are deterministic curves in the Wasserstein space—the randomness from the discrete index ξ_n is fully absorbed into the probability measure evolution, making the continuous formulation a PDE rather than an SDE on the manifold.

## Method

The key technical bridge is to represent each probability measure π_n by a random vector x_n ∈ ℝ^d in Euclidean space, so that the discrete Riemannian SGD/SVRG updates become Euclidean stochastic recursions. Taking the step-size η → 0 limit of these recursions yields an SDE in ℝ^d (e.g., a generalized Langevin equation with noise covariance Σ_SGD or Σ_SVRG), whose Fokker-Planck equation then defines the continuous flow in Wasserstein space. This circumvents the need to define Brownian motion on the manifold directly. The paper proves that the discrete dynamics approximate the continuous flow with error O(η), and establishes convergence rates—O(1/√T) for SGD and O(N^{2/3}/T) for SVRG in the non-convex case, and O(1/T) and O(e^{−γT/N^{2/3}}) under the log-Sobolev (Riemannian PL) inequality—that match their Euclidean counterparts. The framework also reveals that discrete SGLD and SVRG Langevin dynamics are precisely the discrete counterparts of the proposed continuous flows.

## Results

The paper establishes convergence rates for three continuous Riemannian flows in Wasserstein space minimizing KL divergence. For non-convex problems, the Riemannian GD flow achieves O(1/T) in gradient norm, the Riemannian SGD flow achieves O(1/√T), and the Riemannian SVRG flow achieves O(N^{2/3}/T), where N is the number of component functions. Under the log-Sobolev inequality (Riemannian PL condition), the GD flow converges as O(e^{-2γt}), the SGD flow as O(1/T), and the SVRG flow as O(e^{-γT/N^{2/3}}). In experiments (Figure 3) on Gaussian (N=100, d=2) and Mixture Gaussian (N=5, d=2) targets, Riemannian SVRG and GD show faster convergence than SGD under the same number of update steps, while SGD and SVRG are more efficient per gradient computation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's three proposed continuous flows are direct continuous-time analogues of three established discrete MCMC/sampling methods: Langevin dynamics (Riemannian GD), SGLD (Riemannian SGD), and SVRG Langevin dynamics (Riemannian SVRG). The key theoretical contribution is showing that the convergence rates of these continuous flows exactly match their Euclidean counterparts, which had not been previously established in the Wasserstein setting. In the empirical comparison (Figure 3), under the same number of update steps, Riemannian SVRG and Riemannian GD converge faster than Riemannian SGD in both KL divergence and Fisher divergence (gradient norm). However, under the same number of gradient evaluations, Riemannian SVRG is the most efficient, followed by Riemannian SGD, with Riemannian GD being the most expensive per step (O(N) vs. O(1)). The SVRG flow's advantage is most pronounced for large N, where its O(N^{2/3}·ε^{-1}) complexity substantially outperforms both GD's O(N·ε^{-1}) and SGD's O(ε^{-2}).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on continuous optimization in Wasserstein space—where gradient flow (equivalent to Langevin dynamics) was previously established by Santambrogio (2017) and others—by deriving the first continuous-time stochastic counterparts (SGD flow and SVRG flow) in this geometric setting. It builds directly on discrete Riemannian SGD (Bonnabel 2013) and Riemannian SVRG (Zhang et al. 2016), using the Wasserstein space's manifold structure (exponential map, parallel transport, Riemannian gradient) to bridge discrete updates to SDEs via the Fokker-Planck equation. The approach departs from prior continuous-flow derivations (e.g., Santambrogio's variational formulation) by using a step-size-limiting procedure on discrete dynamics, which the authors argue is essential for incorporating stochasticity. The resulting flows are shown to be the continuous limits of discrete SGLD (Welling & Teh 2011) and SVRG Langevin dynamics (Zou et al. 2018), unifying these MCMC methods under a single Riemannian-geometric framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides a principled Riemannian-geometric lens for analyzing stochastic sampling algorithms, showing that their convergence behavior in Wasserstein space mirrors Euclidean SGD/SVRG rates (O(1/√T) and O(N^{2/3}/T) for stationarity; O(1/T) and O(e^{-γT/N^{2/3}}) under log-Sobolev). The framework is general enough to potentially extend to other divergences, other Riemannian manifolds, or higher-order methods, though the paper only treats KL divergence in Wasserstein space. A key limitation is that the continuous flows are deterministic curves in probability space (all randomness is absorbed into the measure), making direct simulation nontrivial; practical use relies on the discrete SGLD/SVRG Langevin approximations. Open questions include whether the step-size-limiting approach generalizes to other stochastic methods (SPIDER, SARAH) and whether the framework yields new algorithmic insights beyond confirming known rates.
