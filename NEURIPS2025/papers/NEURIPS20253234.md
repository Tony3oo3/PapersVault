## Problem

Pre-trained one-step generators offer excellent quality and efficiency, but adapting them to new control conditions (structural constraints, semantic guidelines, external inputs) conventionally requires modifying the base model and re-running expensive diffusion distillation. This paper asks whether a lightweight, native approach can directly inject new controls into an already-trained one-step generator without access to original training images or any additional distillation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253234/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a frozen one-step generator (Stable Diffusion 1.5 pre-trained via Diff-Instruct) and a condition c sampled from a control signal model (Canny edges, HED boundaries, depth maps, low-resolution images, or image prompts encoded by OpenCLIP ViT-H/14). At inference, a single random Gaussian noise vector z is paired with an independently sampled condition c, and the adapted generator f_{θ,φ}(z, c) produces one image in a single forward pass (1 NFE). The only trainable component is a lightweight adapter (ControlNet or IP-Adapter architecture) operating in the noise space of the generator.

## Method

NCT introduces an adapter φ into the frozen one-step generator and trains it with two losses in the noise space. First, a noise consistency loss diffuses the initial noise z to two levels z_{t_n} and z_{t_{n-1}} via variance-preserved diffusion, then minimizes the distance between f_{θ,φ}(z_{t_n}, c) and a stop-gradient of f_{θ,φ}(z_{t_{n-1}}, c), forcing the adapter to exploit the condition c to produce consistent outputs across noise levels. Second, a boundary loss constrains f_{θ,φ}(z, c) to match f_θ(z) when c is the condition naturally associated with z, preventing the adapter from collapsing the output distribution. The two losses are combined as a constrained optimization problem solved via a primal-dual algorithm that dynamically adjusts the boundary loss weight λ. Theoretically, satisfying both conditions guarantees that the adapted generator maps independent (z, c) pairs to the target joint distribution p_θ(x)p(c|x), effectively performing conditional distribution matching without explicit distillation.

## Results

On controllable generation (Canny, HED, Depth, 8× Super Resolution), NCT achieves an average FID of 14.31 and average consistency of 0.078 with only 1 NFE, outperforming the 50-NFE ControlNet baseline (FID 15.22, consistency 0.093) and the 1-NFE JDM baseline (FID 15.26, consistency 0.085). For image-prompted generation on COCO-2017-5k, NCT achieves CLIP-T 0.593 and CLIP-I 0.821 with 1 NFE, matching or exceeding the 100-NFE IP-Adapter (CLIP-T 0.588, CLIP-I 0.828) at 100× lower computational cost.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NCT outperforms all three baselines on the primary controllable generation benchmark. Against the standard 50-NFE ControlNet, NCT reduces NFE by 50× while improving average FID from 15.22 to 14.31 and average consistency from 0.093 to 0.078. Against DI+ControlNet (which naively attaches a multi-step ControlNet to a one-step generator without retraining), NCT achieves substantially better FID (14.31 vs. 22.21) and consistency (0.078 vs. 0.119) at the same 1 NFE, confirming that simply reusing a diffusion-model ControlNet degrades quality. Against JDM (which requires an additional distillation phase to train a controllable one-step student), NCT wins on both average FID (14.31 vs. 15.26) and consistency (0.078 vs. 0.085) without any distillation step. For image-prompted generation, NCT slightly exceeds IP-Adapter on CLIP-T (0.593 vs. 0.588) but trails by 0.007 on CLIP-I (0.821 vs. 0.828), while operating at 100× fewer NFEs; it also marginally beats JDM on CLIP-T (0.593 vs. 0.585) at the cost of a small CLIP-I gap (0.821 vs. 0.826).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper transplants the consistency training paradigm from Consistency Models (which operates in image space across noise levels) into the noise space of a pre-trained one-step generator, using variance-preserved diffusion on the latent code itself. It adopts ControlNet's adapter architecture as the conditioning module and Diff-Instruct as the base one-step generator, but departs fundamentally from prior control-injection methods for one-step models (SDXS, JDM) by eliminating the need for any additional diffusion distillation. The key conceptual move is treating the coupled (noise, condition) pair as an interpolation path from fully-coupled to independent, and enforcing consistency of the generator's output along this path—effectively a noise-space analogue of the image-space consistency loss.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

NCT offers a practical, modular recipe for adding arbitrary new controls to existing one-step generators with a single forward pass, avoiding the computationally expensive re-distillation pipeline that prior methods require. The ablation revealing that the boundary loss is critical (FID collapses from ~14 to ~217 without it) highlights a non-obvious training instability specific to noise-space consistency. Open questions include whether the single-particle MMD estimation generalizes to higher-dimensional or more complex control spaces, whether the approach scales beyond the tested SD 1.5 base, and how it behaves with adversarially chosen or out-of-distribution conditions. The internally collected dataset and absence of error bars limit the strength of the empirical claims.
