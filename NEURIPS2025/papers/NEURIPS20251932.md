## Problem

Existing range-view LiDAR diffusion models (e.g., LiDARGen, R2DM) generate only depth and reflectance images, requiring a separate pretrained segmentation model to produce semantic labels in a two-step pipeline. This decoupled approach suffers from poor cross-modal consistency because the post-hoc semantic predictions cannot guide the generative process, and the independently trained models lack shared representations. Additionally, all existing evaluation metrics for LiDAR generation ignore semantic labels, making it impossible to assess the quality of generated labeled scenes.

## Input / Output

SPIRAL takes Gaussian noise as input in unconditional mode, or Gaussian noise plus an RGB-encoded semantic map in conditional mode, operating on range-view images of 64×1024 (SemanticKITTI) or 32×1024 (nuScenes) resolution. It simultaneously outputs a depth image, a reflectance image, and a per-pixel semantic segmentation map (19 classes for SemanticKITTI, 16 for nuScenes). The semantic output is not a single-step prediction but the result of progressive EMA smoothing across all denoising steps, yielding stable per-pixel confidence scores that also drive the closed-loop feedback mechanism.

## Method

SPIRAL is a 4-layer Efficient U-Net (61M parameters) built on a continuous DDPM framework with two output heads: one predicting the diffusion noise residual ε̂_t and one predicting the semantic map ŷ_t. During training, it alternates between unconditional steps (predicting both ε̂_t and ŷ_t with MSE + cross-entropy loss) and conditional steps (predicting ε̂_t given a ground-truth semantic map y with MSE loss only), sampled 50/50 via a random variable. At inference, a key innovation is the closed-loop mechanism: intermediate semantic predictions are smoothed via exponential moving average, and once more than 80% of pixels exceed a confidence threshold δ=0.8, the smoothed map is fed back as a conditional input, alternating between unconditional and conditional denoising steps to jointly refine geometry and semantics. This progressive, confidence-gated feedback loop is what distinguishes SPIRAL from both pure unconditional generators and two-step pipelines, enabling cross-modal consistency without requiring a separate segmentation network.

## Results

On SemanticKITTI, SPIRAL (61M params, NFE=256) achieves the best scores across all 12 metrics, with S-FRD of 382.87, S-FPD of 153.61, and S-JSD of 9.16, outperforming the best two-step baseline (R2DM + RangeNet++) by 31.03%, 56.33%, and 50.94% respectively. On nuScenes, SPIRAL similarly leads all metrics, improving S-FRD, S-FPD, and S-JSD by 49.03%, 67.84%, and 46.79% over the second-best method. SPIRAL also demonstrates strong generative data augmentation: with only 1% real labeled data, adding SPIRAL-generated samples boosts SPVCNN++ mIoU from 37.76% to 47.41%, and on out-of-distribution Robo3D fog scenes from 32.07% to 44.06%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPIRAL outperforms all three baselines across every metric on both SemanticKITTI and nuScenes. Against LiDARGen [73] (NFE=1160), SPIRAL reduces S-FRD from 1216.61 to 382.87 (68.6% improvement) and S-FPD from 710.79 to 153.61 (78.4% improvement) on SemanticKITTI, while using only 256 NFE steps versus 1160. Against LiDM [48], which only generates depth (no reflectance), SPIRAL achieves S-FPD of 153.61 vs. 522.47 and S-JSD of 9.16 vs. 21.03 on SemanticKITTI. Against R2DM [42] (the strongest two-step baseline), SPIRAL improves S-FRD by 31.03%, S-FPD by 56.33%, and S-JSD by 50.94% on SemanticKITTI, and by 49.03%, 67.84%, and 46.79% on nuScenes, all while using fewer parameters (61M vs. 31+50M or 31+97M). Notably, the larger segmentation model SPVCNN++ performs worse than the smaller RangeNet++ on generated scenes from LiDARGen and LiDM, indicating that post-hoc segmentation degrades with generation noise, a problem SPIRAL avoids through joint generation.

## Relation to Prior Work

SPIRAL extends the range-view LiDAR diffusion lineage initiated by LiDARGen (ECCV 2022) and refined by R2DM (ICRA 2024), which generate only depth and reflectance images, and by LiDM (CVPR 2024), which conditions generation on externally provided semantic maps. The key departure is unifying semantic prediction into the generative process itself: rather than a two-step pipeline (generate → segment), SPIRAL jointly outputs depth, reflectance, and semantic maps from a single Efficient U-Net backbone, with a closed-loop inference mechanism that feeds EMA-smoothed semantic predictions back as conditioning. The paper also extends the evaluation framework by introducing semantic-aware variants of FRD, FPD, JSD, and MMD, filling a gap where prior metrics ignored semantic labels entirely.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By producing labeled LiDAR scenes in a single forward pass with only 61M parameters, SPIRAL offers a practical path to reducing the expensive annotation bottleneck in autonomous driving perception, validated through generative data augmentation experiments that improve segmentation mIoU under low-data and out-of-distribution (fog, wet-ground) settings. The semantic-aware metrics provide a more holistic evaluation tool that the community can adopt. Open questions include whether the range-view representation limits 3D geometric fidelity compared to voxel-based methods, whether the closed-loop mechanism generalizes to more complex scene dynamics, and whether the approach scales to higher-resolution or multi-sensor configurations.
