## Problem

Existing theoretical analyses of KL-regularized RLHF and contextual bandits yield the same O(1/ε²) sample complexity as unregularized bandit objectives, failing to capture the fundamental statistical benefit that reverse-KL regularization provides. This paper asks whether there is a genuine distinction between regularized and unregularized bandit learning, and how the coverage of the reference policy π₀ affects the sample complexity of online RLHF.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes a context distribution d₀, a known reference policy π₀, a parametric reward function class R(θ,·,·) with bounded rewards in [0,B], a KL-regularization coefficient η, and either absolute reward signals (contextual bandit setting) or pairwise preference labels generated by a Bradley-Terry oracle (RLHF setting). It outputs a KL-regularized policy π̂ ∝ π₀·exp(ηR(θ̂,·,·)) together with a high-probability suboptimality gap bound of O(ε). The key structural requirement is a data coverage condition on π₀ (coefficient D) ensuring the in-sample reward error under d₀×π₀ controls out-of-sample error, rather than requiring explicit exploration or eluder-dimension assumptions.

## Method

The core technical contribution is a novel fine-grained decomposition of the suboptimality gap that exploits the strong convexity of the KL-regularized objective: by writing the gap as a quadratic form in the reward estimation error Δ(x,a) = R(θ̂,x,a) − R(θ*,x,a) via a first-order Taylor expansion of the log-partition function, the bound becomes O(η·E[Δ²]) rather than O(E[|Δ|]), yielding O(1/ε) instead of O(1/ε²) sample complexity. To realize this bound, the paper proposes a two-stage mixed-policy sampling algorithm (TMPS): stage 1 collects m = Θ(η²D²) samples from π₀ to obtain a rough reward estimate and an intermediate policy π̂₀^η; stage 2 collects n = Θ(η/ε) samples from π̂₀^η to refine the reward estimate, ensuring the final policy is well-covered by the intermediate one. This design achieves only an additive dependence on the coverage coefficient D² (as opposed to the multiplicative dependence in prior work), and matching lower bounds of Ω(η log N_R(ε)/ε) confirm the sharpness. The analysis extends to the RLHF setting with preference feedback, where the reward is estimated via MLE under the Bradley-Terry model, introducing an additional e^B factor from the sigmoid link function.

## Results

The paper establishes a sharp O(1/ε) sample complexity for KL-regularized contextual bandits and RLHF (when ε is sufficiently small), improving upon the O(1/ε²) bound from prior work (Xiong et al., 2024a; Song et al., 2024). The two-stage mixed sampling algorithm (TMPS) achieves O(max(η²D², η/ε) · log N_R(ε/δ)) samples with only an additive dependence on the coverage coefficient D², whereas prior methods require multiplicative dependence. A matching lower bound of Ω(η log N_R(ε) / ε) is proven, confirming the tightness of the upper bound. Synthetic experiments validate that mixed-policy sampling reduces the suboptimality gap by a large margin compared to offline-only sampling, and that the KL-regularization coefficient η significantly affects sample complexity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is against prior theoretical analyses of KL-regularized RLHF. Xiong et al. (2024a) and Song et al. (2024) both yield O(1/ε²) sample complexity by adapting standard bandit techniques that neglect the strong convexity induced by KL-regularization. This paper's novel fine-grained decomposition of the suboptimality gap exploits the KL-regularized objective's structure to achieve O(η/ε) — a strict improvement when ε is small. Under the local KL-ball coverage condition (Definition 2.11), Song et al. (2024) require Θ(C_ρKL²/ε²) samples, while this paper achieves Θ(C_ρKL · η/ε), which is tighter when η = o(C_ρKL/ε). The synthetic experiments (Figures 1–2) confirm that the two-stage mixed sampling strategy substantially outperforms pure offline sampling from π₀ in suboptimality gap, and that increasing η (stronger KL penalty) reduces the required sample size, consistent with the theoretical O(η/ε) scaling.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the KL-regularized RLHF formulation of Xiong et al. (2024a) and the local KL-ball coverage analysis of Song et al. (2024), while adapting the data coverage condition from the F-design framework of Agarwal et al. (2024). The key departure is methodological: prior work applied standard bandit analysis techniques that treat the KL term as a mere constraint, yielding O(1/ε²) sample complexity. This paper instead exploits the strong convexity of the KL-regularized objective (via a novel Taylor-expansion decomposition of the suboptimality gap) to derive a fundamentally sharper O(1/ε) bound, and converts the multiplicative dependence on the coverage coefficient into an additive one through a two-stage mixed sampling strategy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first theoretical demonstration that reverse-KL regularization in RLHF provides a genuine statistical benefit (improved sample complexity) rather than merely preventing overfitting, which was previously only an empirical observation. The matching lower bounds confirm the O(1/ε) rate is tight, and the additive coverage dependence suggests that a well-covered reference policy can substitute for explicit exploration. Open questions include whether the sharp bound extends to full MDPs (beyond the bandit setting), whether additive dependence can be achieved under the more natural local-coverage condition, and whether the planning oracle assumption (closed-form policy computation) can be relaxed for practical LLM-scale problems.
