## Problem

Diagonal state space models (S4D) achieve performance comparable to structured SSMs while being more computationally efficient, yet the role of their initialization schemes remains theoretically unexplained. Existing schemes initialize poles in continuous time and discretize with a learnable step Δ, which entangles decay rate and oscillation frequency, making the model's spectral response highly sensitive to Δ selection and leading to over-parameterization, non-uniform frequency coverage, and aliasing artifacts.

## Input / Output

The model consumes real-valued 1D sequences—serialized grayscale or color images (flattened from 32×32, 128×128, or 256×256 grids), 1-second audio waveforms at 16 kHz, or character-level text tokens—and produces either a class label (for classification) or a delayed copy of the input (for the continuous copying task). Internally, the hidden state evolves in the complex domain under a diagonal state matrix, and the output is obtained by taking the real part of the complex state projection, effectively implementing a real-valued convolution whose kernel is determined entirely by pole placement.

## Method

S4D-DFouT directly constructs the discrete-time state matrix by placing poles uniformly around the unit circle at angles 2πn/N, modulated by a learnable exponential decay factor ξ, thereby decoupling the decay rate from the oscillation frequency and eliminating sensitivity to the discretization step Δ. When ξ=0, the system reduces exactly to the Discrete Fourier Transform, providing a complete and non-redundant spectral basis that acts as a universal approximator. For layer-wise initialization, H parallel SSMs in each layer are synchronized with fixed phase offsets φ_h = 2π(h−1)/(NH), so their combined poles form a uniform grid of size NH on [0, 2π), ensuring every resonance band is represented exactly once and preventing redundant modes. A half-plane variant exploits Hermitian symmetry of real-valued inputs to halve the state dimension by placing poles only on the upper semicircle [0, π].

## Results

On the Long Range Arena (LRA) benchmark, S4D-DFouT achieves 87.89% accuracy on PathX-256 (65,536 tokens), the first method to solve this task from scratch without self-pretraining (S4+Self-Pretrain achieves 87.11% but requires extensive pretraining). On the remaining LRA tasks, S4D-DFouT is competitive with the strongest baselines (S5, Liquid-S4), winning on Image (88.48% vs. S5's 88.00%) but trailing slightly on ListOps (61.89% vs. 62.15%), Text (87.41% vs. 89.31%), and PathX-128 (94.17% vs. 98.58%). On Speech Commands at 8 kHz zero-shot resampling, S4D-DFouT reaches 93.85%, outperforming all other S4D variants by 2+ points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

S4D-DFouT is most competitive with S5 and Liquid-S4, the strongest SSM baselines on LRA. It wins on Image (88.48% vs. S5's 88.00%, Liquid-S4's 89.50%) and Pathfinder (94.30% vs. S5's 95.33%), but trails S5 on ListOps (61.89% vs. 62.15%), Text (87.41% vs. 89.31%), Retrieval (90.95% vs. 91.40%), and PathX-128 (94.17% vs. 98.58%). Against the original S4D variants (S4D-Lin, S4D-Inv, S4D-LegS), S4D-DFouT consistently improves by 1–4 points across most LRA tasks, with the largest gains on PathX-128 where S4D-Lin is computationally infeasible. The headline result is PathX-256: S4D-DFouT achieves 87.89% training from scratch, surpassing S4+Self-Pretrain (87.11%) which required extensive self-pretraining to handle sequences of this length. On Speech Commands at 8 kHz, S4D-DFouT (93.85%) outperforms all S4D baselines by 2+ points but remains slightly below S5 (94.53%). Overall, the paper's contribution is less about dominating every LRA task and more about demonstrating that discrete-domain initialization eliminates the need for task-specific Δ tuning while enabling scaling to previously intractable sequence lengths.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the diagonal state space model (S4D) line initiated by Gupta et al. (NeurIPS 2022) and Gu et al. (NeurIPS 2022), which showed that restricting the S4 transition matrix to be diagonal preserves performance while simplifying kernel computation to a Vandermonde multiplication. The key departure is moving initialization from the continuous-time domain (where poles are placed and then discretized via a learnable Δ) directly into the discrete domain, eliminating the entanglement between decay rate and resonant frequency that the authors show is inherent to all prior schemes (S4D-Lin, S4D-Inv, S4D-LegS). The layer-wise pole synchronization mechanism further differentiates the approach by treating an entire S4 layer as a coordinated spectral partition rather than a collection of independently initialized SSMs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central conceptual finding—that SSMs on serialized images learn 'local attention' kernels with peaks at row-stride offsets rather than truly global receptive fields—challenges the assumption that LRA tasks require genuinely long-range modeling and suggests the benchmark's difficulty may be overestimated. The first successful from-scratch training on PathX-256 (65,536-length sequences) without self-pretraining is a concrete milestone, though the margin over S4+Self-Pretrain (87.89 vs 87.11) is modest. Open questions include whether the discrete-domain initialization transfers to text and LLM settings (explicitly left unexplored), why it fails on permuted sCIFAR (65.7%), and whether the local-attention bias can be deliberately exploited or must be overcome for tasks without inherent spatial structure.
