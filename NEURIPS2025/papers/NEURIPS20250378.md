## Problem

Adapting large pathology foundation models (PFMs) for downstream clinical tasks like mutation prediction is computationally prohibitive: existing end-to-end fine-tuning approaches require tens of GPUs, while fixed-feature MIL pipelines fail to adapt PFM representations to the target task. No prior method enables task adaptation of large-scale ViT-based PFMs on a single GPU while resolving the optimization instability that arises from jointly training the foundation model and the MIL aggregator.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250378/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes H&E-stained whole slide images at native 20× or 40× resolution, extracting non-overlapping 224×224×3 tiles (448×448×3 tiles from 40× slides are resized to 224×224×3) after Otsu-based tissue segmentation. Only slide-level (bag-level) labels are available—no tile-level annotations—so predictions are made at the WSI level. Outputs include binary mutation probabilities (e.g., FGFR3 in bladder cancer, EGFR in lung adenocarcinoma), multi-label mutation scores for four actionable genes simultaneously, and survival time estimates via a multi-task logistic regression head.

## Method

TAPFM repurposes the ViT's internal CLS-to-token attention weights as MIL aggregation scores, eliminating the need for a separate attention-based aggregator. Training proceeds in two decoupled stages per iteration: (1) a linear classifier is updated via weighted cross-entropy on detached tile features and attention weights, and (2) the PFM is updated via a Task Adaptation Loss (TAL) that combines a feature alignment term (negative dot product of features with detached feature gradients) and an attention loss (dot product of attention weights with detached attention gradients), weighted by λ. This detached dual-graph design breaks the circular dependency inherent in joint PFM–aggregator optimization, yielding stable convergence on a single NVIDIA H100 GPU. The approach is evaluated with three PFMs (UNI, GigaPath, H-Optimus-0) and outperforms both fixed-feature and naively fine-tuned baselines across institutional and TCGA external validation cohorts.

## Results

On the binary FGFR3 mutation prediction task in BLCA, H-Optimus-0 (TAPFM) achieves AUC 0.8837 (institutional, N=194) and 0.8956 (TCGA, N=260), outperforming the best fixed-PFM baseline (H-Optimus-0+ABMIL, AUC 0.8412/0.8786) by 4.25/1.70 points and the best fine-tuned baseline (H-Optimus-0+VarMIL(FT), AUC 0.8526/0.8889) by 3.11/0.67 points. On LUAD EGFR prediction, H-Optimus-0 (TAPFM) reaches AUC 0.8503 (institutional, N=876) and 0.8590 (TCGA, N=438), surpassing the best fine-tuned baseline (0.8167/0.8543) by 3.36/0.47 points. For multi-label prediction of four actionable mutations (EGFR, KRAS, MET, ALK) in LUAD, H-Optimus-0 (TAPFM) achieves macro-average AUC 0.8485 (institutional) and 0.8415 (TCGA), consistently outperforming GigaPath (TAPFM) across all individual mutations including the rare MET and ALK targets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TAPFM outperforms all four external MIL aggregators (ABMIL, CLAM, DSMIL, VarMIL) across every PFM–dataset combination in both fixed and fine-tuned settings. Among the external MIL methods, ABMIL and VarMIL are consistently the strongest baselines (e.g., H-Optimus-0+ABMIL achieves AUC 0.8412 on BLCA institutional vs. 0.8365 for DSMIL and 0.8373 for CLAM), but TAPFM's ViT-attention-based aggregation surpasses even these by 3–8 AUC points depending on the PFM and task. The gap is largest for the smaller UNI model (TAPFM gains ~5 AUC points over UNI+ABMIL on BLCA) and narrows for H-Optimus-0 (~4 points on BLCA institutional, ~0.5–3 points on LUAD), suggesting the benefit of task adaptation is most pronounced when the PFM has more room to adapt. On the multi-label LUAD task, H-Optimus-0 (TAPFM) leads GigaPath (TAPFM) on all four mutations (e.g., EGFR 0.8665 vs. 0.8595, ALK 0.8702 vs. 0.8632 institutional AUC), confirming that the advantage is driven by the PFM quality rather than the aggregation method alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the ViT architecture's CLS token attention mechanism, repurposing it as a tile-importance scoring function for MIL aggregation in whole slide image analysis, thereby eliminating the need for separate external MIL aggregators (ABMIL, CLAM, DSMIL, VarMIL). It departs from prior PFM adaptation work (Campanella et al. 2025, Li et al. 2023) that requires multi-GPU distributed training by introducing a detached dual-gradient optimization that breaks the circular dependency between PFM and aggregator parameter updates, enabling stable single-GPU fine-tuning. The approach also extends the fixed-feature paradigm where PFMs serve as frozen encoders, showing that task-specific adaptation of the PFM itself yields consistent gains over both frozen and naively end-to-end fine-tuned configurations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TAPFM makes adaptation of large-scale pathology foundation models (ViT-giant class) practical on a single H100 GPU, removing the 24-GPU barrier that previously confined PFM fine-tuning to well-resourced institutions. The detached-gradient formulation provides a theoretically grounded resolution to the optimization instability that plagues joint PFM-aggregator training, with empirical confirmation that unified-graph training oscillates while the proposed approach converges smoothly. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond ViT-based PFMs, which specific transformer layers benefit most from adaptation, and whether multi-institutional validation with diverse scanning protocols will confirm the TCGA generalizability observed here. The method's extension to multi-label mutation prediction and survival analysis suggests a broader template for clinical task adaptation, though validation remains limited to two cancer types.
