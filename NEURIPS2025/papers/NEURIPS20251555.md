## Problem

Kernel methods face a fundamental scalability bottleneck: while EigenPro 3.0 achieved linear memory scaling by decoupling model size from training set size, its per-iteration time complexity remains quadratic in model size due to projecting the model back into the center span after every stochastic gradient step. This makes training large kernel models (e.g., 512K+ centers) prohibitively slow, requiring days to weeks even on modern GPUs. The paper addresses this by eliminating the per-step projection overhead.

## Input / Output

The method consumes pre-extracted feature embeddings (via MobileNetv2 or ResNet-18) paired with labels, a user-specified set of p model centers Z (which need not overlap with training data), and a positive semi-definite kernel (primarily Laplace, with Gaussian and NTK also tested). For multi-class tasks it trains one-vs-all binary regressors and outputs the argmax class. The projection period T is the key new hyperparameter, optimally set proportional to p/m (model size over batch size), and the algorithm also requires a Nyström subsample size s and preconditioner level q.

## Method

EigenPro 4 introduces delayed projection: instead of projecting the model back into the span of centers Z after every mini-batch update (as EigenPro 3 does), it accumulates T stochastic gradient steps before performing a single projection. During the delay, the model expands with 'temporary centers' from each mini-batch, whose weights are set once at insertion and never modified; the Nyström preconditioner weights receive additive updates; and the original center weights remain frozen. At step T, a single inexact projection (solved via EigenPro 2) compresses the expanded model back to the original p centers. The optimal T scales as (p/m)·√(2·T_ep2), yielding an amortized per-batch cost of O(p) FLOPS and O(p) memory, compared to O(p²) per batch for EigenPro 3. A Nyström approximate preconditioner suppresses top Hessian eigenvalues to accelerate convergence, and the algorithm empirically converges in fewer epochs than EigenPro 3 despite the delayed projection.

## Results

EigenPro 4 achieves up to 686× speedup over EigenPro 3 while maintaining comparable or superior test accuracy across all datasets and model sizes. At 1M model centers on CIFAR5M*, EigenPro 4 completes one epoch in 21 minutes (88.7% accuracy) versus >4 days for EigenPro 3 (88.7% accuracy). On LibriSpeech (10M samples), the speedup reaches >411× (70 min vs >20 days) with 89.5% accuracy. Falkon, the other baseline, runs out of memory for model sizes ≥256K on most datasets due to its O(p²) memory scaling.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against EigenPro 3, EigenPro 4 achieves dramatic speedups (4.6× at 64K centers up to >686× at 1M centers) while matching or slightly exceeding test accuracy on every dataset—for example, 88.7% vs 88.7% on CIFAR5M* and 89.5% vs 85.4% on LibriSpeech at 1M centers. The speedup grows with model size because EigenPro 3's per-iteration cost scales as O(p²) while EigenPro 4's amortized cost is O(p). Against Falkon, EigenPro 4 is faster at all model sizes where Falkon can run (e.g., 5 min vs 3 min at 64K on CIFAR5M*, but 12 min vs 240 min at 512K), and achieves higher accuracy (88.6% vs 86.7% at 512K on CIFAR5M*). Critically, Falkon hits out-of-memory errors for model sizes ≥256K on CIFAR5M, LibriSpeech, and WebVision due to its O(p²) memory requirement, whereas EigenPro 4 scales linearly in memory and handles 1M centers without issue. On ImageNet specifically, EigenPro 4 converges in ~3 hours (15 epochs) versus ~43 days for EigenPro 3 at 512K centers, while also reaching higher final accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the EigenPro line of work, building directly on EigenPro 3.0 (Abedsoltan et al., ICML 2023), which first decoupled kernel model size from training set size with linear memory scaling, and on EigenPro 2.0 (Ma & Belkin, 2019), which introduced the Nyström-based stochastic preconditioner. The key departure is the delayed projection mechanism: rather than projecting the model back into the center span after every stochastic gradient step (as EigenPro 3 does, incurring O(p²) cost), EigenPro 4 accumulates T steps of updates before a single amortized projection, reducing per-iteration time to O(p). This positions the method as a strict algorithmic improvement over EigenPro 3 while remaining in the same problem class, and as a memory-efficient alternative to Falkon (Rudi et al., 2017) and Nyström-based methods (NYTRO, ASkotch) that suffer quadratic memory in model size.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: model sizes of 1M+ centers become trainable on standard GPU hardware in minutes rather than days, opening the door to kernel methods at scales previously reserved for neural networks. The convergence analysis, however, only covers the idealized T=∞ exact-projection version; the finite-T, inexact-projection algorithm actually deployed lacks a formal convergence guarantee, leaving a gap between theory and practice. The paper also notes that the delayed-projection structure is naturally amenable to multi-GPU and distributed parallelization, a direction left for future work. For the broader kernel methods community, this work removes the primary computational barrier that has kept kernel machines from competing with deep learning at scale.
