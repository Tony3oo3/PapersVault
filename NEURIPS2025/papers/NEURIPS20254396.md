## Problem

No existing benchmark systematically and objectively evaluates the remote sensing capabilities of Large Vision-Language Models. Current evaluations rely on fragmented, single-task datasets that suffer from data leakage (reusing public datasets like DOTA and DIOR that appear in VLM training), coarse evaluation taxonomies, and rigid exact-match metrics that bias results. CHOICE addresses all three gaps by introducing a hierarchical, all-new, multi-task benchmark with objective multiple-choice evaluation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254396/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each problem pairs a remote sensing image (512×512 to 800×800 pixels, 0.1–30 m/pixel resolution, sourced from 50 cities across six continents via Landsat-8, Sentinel-1/2, and Google Earth Engine) with a text question. For 21 of 23 tasks, the model must select from 2–4 multiple-choice options (A/B/C/D); Visual Grounding requires outputting rotated bounding box coordinates [x₁,y₁,x₂,y₂] scored by IoU > 0.5, and Referring Expression Segmentation requires a pixel-wise mask. The final output is a per-task accuracy aggregated into 6 L-2 dimension scores and 2 L-1 capability scores.

## Method

CHOICE organizes evaluation into a three-level taxonomy: 2 L-1 capabilities (Perception, Reasoning) decomposed into 6 L-2 dimensions (Image-level Comprehension, Single-instance Identification, Cross-instance Discernment, Attribute Reasoning, Assessment Reasoning, Common Sense Reasoning) and 23 L-3 leaf tasks, totaling 10,507 problems. All RSIs are collected in-house from multi-source satellites across 50 randomly selected cities to eliminate data leakage, and problems are constructed via three complementary pipelines: label-driven (predefined categories with OSM-assisted collection), foundation-model-driven (YOLOv8, MTP, RingMo-SAM for instance attributes and rotated bounding boxes), and human-GPT-4 collaboration (for change detection and inference tasks). A 12-person quality control team verifies all labels and filters ambiguous distractors. Evaluation is zero-shot: LLM-based VLMs are prompted to output a single letter, while CLIP-based VLMs are scored by image-sentence similarity over augmented declarative choices, enabling uniform accuracy computation across 24 models (15 general-domain, 6 RSVLMs, 3 proprietary).

## Results

Across the 6 L-2 capability dimensions of CHOICE, no single VLM achieves consistently high performance. Qwen2-VL-72B leads in SII (0.702), CID (0.742), and ILC (0.855), while InternVL2-40B achieves the best AttR (0.694) and CSR (0.946). Among proprietary models, Gemini-1.5-Pro scores highest in ILC (0.867) and AssR (0.611), and GPT-4o-2024-11-20 leads in CSR (0.900). RSVLMs trail significantly, with VHM achieving the best SII among them (0.703) but falling below 0.45 in most reasoning dimensions, and GeoRSCLIP scoring as low as 0.198 in SII.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Qwen2-VL-72B outperforms GPT-4o-2024-11-20 by 7% in Change Detection (0.840 vs. 0.770) and by 15.3% in Physical Property reasoning (0.670 vs. 0.517), while InternVL2-40B surpasses all proprietary models in Attribute Reasoning (0.694 vs. 0.590 for Gemini-1.5-Pro) and Common Sense Reasoning (0.946 vs. 0.900 for GPT-4o). Among RSVLMs, VHM achieves the strongest perception scores (SII 0.703, OL 0.872, VG 0.922) but its reasoning scores (AttR 0.392, AssR 0.348) are far below those of comparable-parameter general-domain models like Ovis1.6-Gemma2-9B (AttR 0.598, AssR 0.645) and GLM-4V-9B (AttR 0.564, AssR 0.450). GeoRSCLIP, despite strong CSR (0.884), scores the lowest SII (0.198) and AttR (0.210) among all evaluated models, highlighting the trade-off between CLIP-based image-level matching and fine-grained instance perception. LHRS-Bot-nova shows the weakest AssR (0.120) of all 24 models, indicating severe limitations in resource and environmental assessment. Overall, no RSVLM consistently outperforms the top general-domain VLMs across all six dimensions, and proprietary VLMs (GPT-4o, Gemini-1.5-Pro) offer no clear advantage over Qwen2-VL-72B or InternVL2-40B in most remote sensing-specific tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the general VLM benchmarking tradition (MM-Bench, SEED-Bench, MMStar) into the remote sensing domain, while departing from existing RS-specific benchmarks (LHRS-Bench, EarthVQA, VRSBench, GeoChat-Bench) in three key ways: a hierarchical 23-task taxonomy versus 3–11 flat tasks, all-new data collected from 50 globally distributed cities to eliminate data leakage, and inclusion of multi-temporal and pixel-wise tasks absent from prior work. Conceptually, it repurposes the MCQ evaluation format from general VLM benchmarks but grounds it in remote-sensing-specific perception tiers (image-level, instance-level, cross-instance) and reasoning dimensions (attribute, assessment, common sense) that no prior RS benchmark had systematically organized.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CHOICE provides the first objective, hierarchical evaluation framework for RS VLMs, and its central finding—that domain-specific RSVLMs do not consistently outperform general-domain VLMs and that fine-grained perception plus advanced reasoning remain major bottlenecks for all models—challenges the prevailing assumption that domain-specific training is sufficient for remote sensing. Open questions include whether scaling RSVLM parameters and integrating general reasoning will close the gap, how the benchmark extends to SAR and multi-spectral modalities, and whether the MCQ format adequately captures the open-ended nature of real-world RS interpretation. The benchmark's all-new data design sets a methodological standard for future RS evaluation, addressing a data-leakage concern that has undermined prior comparisons.
