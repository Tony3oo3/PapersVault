## Problem

Deployed ML models can silently degrade when the input distribution shifts, but knowing *when* to retrain without labels is an open challenge because not all distribution shifts harm performance. Existing monitoring methods either require persistent access to training data at deployment, fail to distinguish deteriorating from benign shifts (inflating false positives), or lack provable guarantees on both true and false positive rates. This paper formalizes post-deployment deterioration (PDD) monitoring and seeks an algorithm that is label-free, training-data-free, and provably robust to non-deteriorating shifts while sensitive to deteriorating ones.

## Input / Output

At deployment, D3M consumes batches of m unlabeled inputs (RGB images, histopathology slides, or tabular clinical features) drawn from an unknown distribution Q_x and produces a single binary deterioration flag by comparing the batch's maximum disagreement rate to a (1−α)-quantile threshold from a pre-computed reference distribution Φ. The method is model-agnostic: it only requires that the base model's feature extractor supports gradient-based optimization, and it operates entirely via forward passes with no access to the original training set, making it suitable for edge or privacy-constrained deployment.

## Method

D3M couples a neural feature extractor with a Variational Bayesian Last Layer (VBLL) trained end-to-end via the ELBO, yielding a posterior predictive distribution over class logits. During a calibration stage, the method bootstraps held-out in-distribution samples, draws K posterior samples per input, applies temperature-scaled softmax and categorical sampling to generate diverse pseudo-predictions, and records the maximum disagreement rate against the mean model's predictions across T rounds to build a reference distribution Φ. At deployment, the same procedure is applied to unlabeled deployment data, and a deterioration flag is raised when the observed maximum disagreement rate exceeds the (1−α)-quantile of Φ. The key insight is that Bayesian posterior sampling over the last layer approximates the intractable search for well-performing hypotheses that maximally disagree with the base model, replacing gradient-based finetuning or training-data access with a single forward pass and stochastic sampling, while temperature scaling and categorical (rather than argmax) prediction extraction compensate for the limited diversity inherent to last-layer-only Bayesian treatment.

## Results

On the three benchmark datasets with known deteriorating shifts, D3M achieves TPRs of 0.38–0.69 (UCI Heart Disease), 0.40–0.74 (CIFAR-10.1), and 0.89–0.99 (Camelyon17) across query sizes of 10, 20, and 50, respectively. On the GEMINI clinical dataset, D3M maintains a low false positive rate (FPR) under non-deteriorating temporal shift while achieving competitive TPRs under deteriorating age-group shift at significance level α=0.05. The method's key differentiator is that all TPRs are achieved while maintaining ID FPR below α, and it requires no training data or gradient-based finetuning at deployment time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

D3M is most directly comparable to Detectron [19], the only other disagreement-based method that provides theoretical guarantees; Detectron achieves slightly higher TPRs at larger query sizes (e.g., 0.83 vs. 0.74 on CIFAR-10.1 at size 50; 1.0 vs. 0.93 on Camelyon17 at size 20), but D3M wins at the smallest query size on CIFAR-10.1 (0.40 vs. 0.37) and at size 50 on Camelyon17 (0.99 vs. 0.96). Critically, D3M is training-data-free at deployment while Detectron requires persistent access to the original training set and gradient-based finetuning, making D3M substantially more scalable for edge or privacy-constrained settings. Against distribution-divergence baselines (MMD-D, H-Div, CTST, Rel. Mahalanobis), D3M consistently achieves higher TPRs across all three benchmarks, with the largest margins on CIFAR-10.1 (e.g., 0.74 vs. 0.04 for CTST at size 50). Deep Ensembles [40] and BBSD [38] are outperformed by D3M on CIFAR-10.1 and Camelyon17 but remain competitive on UCI Heart Disease, where D3M exhibits notably higher variance (±0.19–0.33) due to the small dataset size and stochastic posterior sampling. On the GEMINI clinical dataset, D3M matches or exceeds all baselines in maintaining low FPR under non-deteriorating temporal drift while achieving competitive TPR under deteriorating age-shift, validating its selectivity in a realistic deployment scenario.

## Relation to Prior Work

D3M sits at the intersection of two prior research threads: the model-disagreement framework for shift detection (Yu & Aizawa 2019; Ginsberg et al. 2023; Rosenfeld & Garg 2023) and variational Bayesian last layers (Harrison et al. 2024). The key departure is that all prior disagreement-based monitors (notably Detectron) require persistent access to training data and gradient-based finetuning at deployment, while shift-detection baselines (MMD-D, H-divergence, BBSD) flag any distributional change without distinguishing deteriorating from benign drift. D3M replaces the intractable optimization over a well-performing hypothesis class with posterior sampling from a VBLL, achieving a training-data-free, finetuning-free monitoring protocol that provably controls false positives on non-deteriorating shifts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is substantial for regulated domains like healthcare, where labels are expensive or temporally delayed and storing training data at edge nodes is infeasible; the GEMINI results demonstrate that D3M can selectively alert on clinically meaningful deterioration while ignoring benign temporal drift. However, the theory assumes ground-truth labeling and the empirical results show notably higher variance than baselines at small query sizes, limiting immediate reliability in low-data regimes. Open questions include extending guarantees to noisy or adversarially poisoned labels, understanding how feature-representation quality modulates the variance, and whether the VBLL approximation can be tightened to fully recover the idealized D3M guarantees.
