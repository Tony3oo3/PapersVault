## Problem

Prior theoretical analyses of contrastive representation synchronization either assume d ≥ N or N → ∞, failing to capture the practically relevant regime d ≪ N ≪ 2^d (e.g., SigLIP2 uses d ≈ 10^3 with N ≈ 10^10). Moreover, previously identified optimal configurations (simplex structures, perfect cross-modal alignment) are too rigid to explain empirical phenomena such as the modality gap. This paper provides the first rigorous characterization of global minima for the sigmoid loss with trainable inverse temperature and bias, as used in SigLIP and SigLIP2, in the practical high-N regime.

## Input / Output

The method takes N paired samples (X_i, Y_i) — typically image-caption pairs — and two neural encoders f_θ: X → S^{d-1} and g_φ: Y → S^{d-1} that produce unit-norm embeddings U_i and V_i. The optimization jointly trains encoder parameters and the inverse temperature t and bias b (or equivalently the relative bias b_rel = b/t) to minimize the sigmoid contrastive loss. The output is a set of synchronized embeddings forming an (m, b_rel)-Constellation, where positive-pair inner products are separated from negative-pair inner products by a margin m, enabling perfect retrieval via nearest-neighbor search and, when m > |b_rel|, a provable linear modality gap between the two embedding sets.

## Method

The core contribution is a complete geometric characterization: a configuration is a global minimizer of the sigmoid loss with trainable t and b if and only if it satisfies the inner-product separation inequalities ⟨U_i, V_i⟩ ≥ m + b_rel and ⟨U_i, V_j⟩ ≤ −m + b_rel for all i ≠ j, defining what the authors call (m, b_rel)-Constellations. The paper connects these constellations to spherical codes, yielding explicit lower and upper bounds on the maximum number of pairs embeddable in dimension d for a given margin, and proves that when N > d and m > |b_rel|, the two modalities are linearly separable (the modality gap). A key practical contribution is a reparameterization of the sigmoid loss using the relative bias b_rel directly rather than the absolute bias b, which the authors show implicitly adds modality-dependent linear adapters (extending the Double-Constant Embedding Model), accelerates convergence under Adam, and allows freezing b_rel to guide training toward configurations with larger margins. For more than two modalities, a construction using regular simplex vertices in an appended subspace yields zero-loss configurations for arbitrary synchronization graphs.

## Results

The paper's main contributions are theoretical: it fully characterizes global minimizers of the sigmoid contrastive loss as (m, b_rel)-Constellations (Theorems 3.1–3.2), proves that constellations of exponential size exist in dimension d under the conditions m + b_rel < 1 and 3m < 1 + b_rel (Theorem 3.3), and establishes that a modality gap (linear separability of the two modalities) holds for any zero-loss configuration when N ≥ d+2 and m > |b_rel| (Theorem 3.6). Empirically, the authors verify these predictions on 8 SigLIP models from Hugging Face evaluated on the ImageNet validation set, observing perfect linear separability of image and text embeddings for all models. For the SigLIP2 B/16 384×384 model, the learned parameters are t ≈ 117.8, b ≈ −12.9, yielding a relative bias b_rel ≈ −0.11, consistent with the small-bias regime predicted by theory.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is primarily a theoretical paper, so comparisons are against prior theoretical characterizations and alternative loss parameterizations rather than competing methods on a benchmark. Against the prior work of Lee et al. (LCS24), which characterizes minimizers only in the d ≥ N regime and yields rigid simplex or antipodal structures, the present work extends to the practically relevant d ≪ N ≪ 2^d regime and shows a rich family of constellation solutions. In the ablation experiments on synthetic data (Section 3.5), the proposed relative-bias parameterization (L^{RB-Sig} with trainable t, b_rel) converges to zero loss faster and achieves a larger inner-product separation margin than the original bias parameterization (L^{Sig} with trainable t, b) used in SigLIP/SigLIP2, which tends to drive b_rel toward zero. Fixed-temperature baselines (both low t ≲ 10 and high t ≫ 10) fail to achieve the inner-product separation condition needed for reliable retrieval, confirming that trainable temperature and bias are essential. The modality gap result (Theorem 3.6) contrasts with the InfoNCE-based analysis of Wang & Isola (2020), which predicts near-perfect alignment (U_i = V_i), and instead explains the empirically observed disjoint embedding regions in CLIP and SigLIP.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the theoretical analysis of contrastive representation synchronization from the InfoNCE and sigmoid loss literature (Wang & Isola 2020, E & Wojtowytsch 2022, Lee et al. 2024) into the practically relevant regime d ≪ N ≪ 2^d, where prior results either assumed d ≥ N (yielding rigid simplex structures) or N → ∞ (yielding perfect alignment). The key conceptual departure is making inverse temperature and bias trainable, which was done empirically in SigLIP/SigLIP2 but not analyzed theoretically. The construction of zero-loss configurations draws on the Double-Constant Embedding Model of Lee et al. (2024) but replaces the simplex with spherical codes, connecting the problem to classical coding theory (Shannon, Wyner, Conway & Sloane).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first rigorous geometric characterization of what synchronized representations look like in the regime where real models actually operate, and shows that the empirically observed modality gap is a geometric necessity of the loss rather than an architectural artifact. The proposed relative bias parameterization offers a concrete, drop-in improvement for training dynamics, and the spherical code connection gives practitioners a quantitative tool for choosing embedding dimensions. Open questions include convergence guarantees for first-order methods in this regime, full resolution of the combinatorial capacity problem (Problem 1), and validation of the new parameterization on real large-scale data.
