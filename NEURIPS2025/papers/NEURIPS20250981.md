## Problem

Current LLMs handle tabular data by serializing tables into text sequences, which inherently destroys the structural semantics of tables—most critically, permutation invariance (the property that row/column reordering should not change meaning). The paper demonstrates via a new StructQA benchmark that even GPT-4 and table-specific models like TableLlama show answer robustness below 40% under simple row/column permutations, revealing a fundamental mismatch between the structured nature of tables and the sequential nature of text serialization.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-structured table (with hierarchical or flat layout, cells containing text, numbers, or dates) and a natural language question as input. The table is consumed in two parallel streams: (1) serialized into a formatted text sequence processed by the LLM's standard embedding layer, and (2) converted into a hypergraph and encoded by a separate table encoder. The output is a free-form text answer generated via next-token prediction, with the table structure representation injected as a learnable soft-prompt token preceding the serialized table text.

## Method

TAMO treats tables as an independent modality by encoding them through a hypergraph-enhanced table encoder built on HyperTrans, a hypergraph-structure-aware transformer. Tables are restructured as hypergraphs where leaf cells become nodes and branch cells (rows, columns, headers) become hyperedges, with Set Transformer multiset functions ensuring permutation invariance at each layer. A single-layer MLP alignment projector maps the pooled node and hyperedge representations into the LLM's embedding space, producing a compact table structure token. This structural embedding is prepended to the serialized table text as a soft prompt, allowing the LLM to perceive global relational context (row-column relationships, hierarchies) before processing fine-grained cell content. Training supports frozen-LLM (only encoder + projector learned), LoRA, and full SFT settings, with the LLM backbone (Llama2-7B) remaining architecturally unmodified.

## Results

On the frozen LLM setting, TAMO achieves an average relative improvement of +42.65% over pure text prompt tuning, with a peak gain of +86.06% on HiTab (48.86 vs. 26.26 accuracy). Under full SFT, TAMO_SFT+ reaches 71.60 on StructQA, 63.89 on HiTab, 45.81 on WikiTQ, 85.90 on WikiSQL, and 39.01 BLEU on FeTaQA, outperforming the text-only SFT baseline by +9.71% on average. The 7B-parameter TAMO_SFT+ surpasses GPT-4.1 and DeepSeek-R1 on StructQA (71.60 vs. 60.33 and 57.47) and WikiSQL (85.90 vs. 71.21 and 71.91), while remaining competitive on knowledge-intensive WikiTQ where larger models lead.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TAMO consistently outperforms all text-only baselines across every tuning setting: +42.65% average over prompt tuning (frozen), +28.27% over LoRA, and +9.71% over full SFT. Against TableLlama (the prior SOTA generalist table LLM), TAMO_SFT+ wins on all five datasets, most dramatically on StructQA (71.60 vs. 6.47) and WikiSQL (85.90 vs. 46.26). Compared to GPT-3.5 and GPT-4, TAMO_SFT+ surpasses both on 4 out of 5 datasets, with an average score improvement of +19.83 over GPT-3.5. Against the more recent GPT-4.1 and DeepSeek-R1, the 7B TAMO_SFT+ leads on structure-centric benchmarks (StructQA, WikiSQL) but trails on knowledge-intensive WikiTQ (45.81 vs. 68.14 and 75.76). Relative to specialist SOTA methods (CABINET, SeaD), TAMO_SFT+ is competitive on HiTab (63.89 vs. 64.71) and FeTaQA (39.01 vs. 40.50) but falls short on WikiTQ (45.81 vs. 69.10) and WikiSQL (85.90 vs. 92.07), where those methods use dataset-specific architectures or external modules.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of serializing tables into text for LLMs by treating tables as an independent modality, drawing a direct analogy to how vision (ViT/CLIP → GPT-4v/LLaVA) and audio (Whisper → SpeechGPT) integrate specialized encoders into multimodal LLMs. Architecturally, it builds on HyTrel's hypergraph transformer (HyperTrans) and Set Transformer's multiset functions to construct a permutation-invariant table encoder, but repositions these components from a standalone representation learner into a modality interface that projects structural embeddings into the LLM's semantic space via a lightweight MLP. It differentiates from TAPAS/TAPEX, which embedded structural awareness within encoder-only or encoder-decoder architectures, by designing a plug-and-play interface specifically for modern decoder-only LLMs without modifying their parameters.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The StructQA benchmark and its permutation-invariance diagnostic expose a fundamental blind spot in how the field has been evaluating table understanding: even GPT-4 and TableLlama show answer robustness below 40% under trivial row/column shuffling, suggesting that years of SFT on serialized tables have not taught models genuine structural comprehension. The plug-and-play design (frozen LLM + lightweight hypergraph encoder) makes the approach immediately deployable, and the 7B TAMO outperforming GPT-4 on structural tasks signals that architectural inductive bias can substitute for scale on structure-centric reasoning. Key open questions include extending to multi-turn and dynamic table editing, pretraining the table encoder on large-scale task-agnostic data to reduce cross-dataset transfer gaps, and systematically studying how different serialization formats interact with the structural modality.
