## Problem

Noisy label learning (NLL) and long-tailed learning (LTL) methods face a circular dependency: NLL requires unbiased model predictions to select and correct noisy labels and recover the true class distribution, while LTL methods like logit adjustment require the true distribution to recalibrate biased predictions. When noisy labels further distort the observed distribution relative to the true one (in consistent, relieve, or aggravate scenarios), this mutual dependency creates a deadlock that renders both method families ineffective.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254341/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, larger for real-world datasets) paired with noisy labels from long-tailed training sets, and operates as a plug-in wrapper around existing NLL methods (DivideMix, UNICON, DPC). Beyond standard class label predictions at inference, it learns a scalar adjustment parameter τ and a class prior estimate π(y) during training. A balanced clean subset D_bc is constructed internally from high-confidence clean samples via over-sampling to serve as the validation signal for the outer optimization loop.

## Method

Unlocker employs a bilevel optimization framework to iteratively break the NLL–LTL deadlock. The inner optimization trains the model using an NLL method with post-hoc logit adjustment applied to selection logits, enabling fairer noisy-label identification in tail classes, while also constructing the balanced clean subset D_bc and estimating the training-set class prior π(y). The outer optimization updates a learnable parameter τ via gradient descent to minimize the KL divergence between the model's class-averaged predictions on D_bc and a uniform distribution, adaptively calibrating adjustment strength to avoid over- or under-adjustment. An exponential moving average stabilizes the per-epoch adjustment vector α = τ·log π(y) against early-training fluctuations. The paper proves convergence by showing the outer objective is differentiable, bounded in [0, log C], and admits a closed-form least-squares solution for τ, reducing the bilevel problem to a single-level optimization.

## Results

On CIFAR-100, DivideMix+Unlocker achieves 71.95% accuracy under the consistent scenario (IR=10, joint 40% noise), 53.34% under the relieve scenario (IR=50, sym 60%), and 62.59% under the aggravate scenario (IR=50, t2h 40%), outperforming the base DivideMix by up to +16.98%. On WebVision-50, DivideMix+Unlocker reaches 83.79% Top-1 and 96.55% Top-5 accuracy, surpassing the previous best GSS (83.64%/95.86%). On Clothing1M, the method attains 76.94% accuracy, exceeding GSS (75.83%) and RCAL+ (74.97%). Direct integration of logit adjustment with NLL methods (NLL+LA) frequently causes training collapse, dropping to as low as 2.51% accuracy, whereas Unlocker's adaptive τ optimization avoids this failure mode.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Unlocker consistently outperforms all LTNLL baselines (RoLT, TABASCO, DaSC) across every scenario, with the largest gaps in the relieve setting where tail-class sample selection is most challenging (e.g., 53.34% vs. 14.76% for RoLT on IR=50, sym 60%). Compared to the base NLL methods, Unlocker yields substantial gains: DivideMix improves by +2.99% to +16.98%, UNICON by +1.56% to +8.42%, and DPC by +1.05% to +54.46% (the latter in the aggravate scenario where DPC alone collapses to 1.04%). Direct logit adjustment (NLL+LA) is highly unstable, frequently causing training collapse to near-random accuracy (e.g., 2.51% for DivideMix+LA at IR=100), while Unlocker's adaptive τ avoids this. On real-world benchmarks, DivideMix+Unlocker edges out GSS on WebVision-50 (83.79% vs. 83.64% Top-1) and Clothing1M (76.94% vs. 75.83%), and achieves the best Top-5 on ILSVRC12 (96.37% vs. 95.22% for GSS). On Red-MiniImageNet, the method surpasses GSS by 0.96% at IR=10/η=20% and by 0.56% at IR=100/η=40%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of noisy label learning (NLL) and long-tailed learning (LTL), building directly on logit-based noisy label selection/correction pipelines (DivideMix, UNICON, DPC) and logit adjustment (Menon et al., ICLR 2021). Its key conceptual departure is identifying a 'deadlock' between these two paradigms: NLL requires unbiased predictions to select noisy labels, while LTL requires true class priors to recalibrate biased predictions, creating a circular dependency that prior LTNLL methods (RoLT, TABASCO, DaSC) implicitly ignore by assuming observed and true distributions coincide. The proposed bilevel optimization framework generalizes logit adjustment by making the adjustment strength τ a learnable parameter optimized in an outer loop, rather than a fixed hyperparameter.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap: real-world noisy labels systematically alter the observed class distribution, invalidating the core assumption of existing LTNLL methods. The bilevel optimization framework is modular and can be plugged into any NLL method, making it broadly applicable. However, limitations remain: the balanced clean subset D_bc may contain impure samples that bias τ optimization, and the distribution estimation π(y) relies on the quality of noisy label correction. Open questions include how the approach scales to very large class counts, whether the EMA stabilization is necessary or masks deeper instability, and whether the bilevel structure can be replaced by a single-level surrogate without performance loss.
