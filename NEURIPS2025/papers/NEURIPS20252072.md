## Problem

Predicting the 3D pose of a query ligand in a protein binding site when a co-crystallized reference ligand (template) is available remains challenging: existing ligand-based alignment methods ignore protein context, while standard docking tools discard the geometric priors encoded in the template. No prior method combines template-conditioned generative alignment with differentiable, coordinate-level refinement, and no benchmark specifically evaluates template-guided docking across varying degrees of ligand similarity and flexibility.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252072/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a 2D molecular graph of the query ligand (heavy atoms with bond types, charges, hybridization), the 3D atomic coordinates of a co-crystallized template ligand bound to the same target, and optionally the protein binding pocket (residues within 12 Å of the template). It outputs a full 3D atomic coordinate set for the query ligand (including hydrogens reintroduced during the second PO phase), with 10 candidate poses generated per query and the top-ranked pose selected via a composite score combining shape/pharmacophore Tanimoto similarity to the template and a rescaled Vina docking score against the pocket.

## Method

FMA-PO is a two-stage pipeline. Stage 1 (FMA) is a flow-matching generative model that denoises a query conformation sampled from a harmonic prior (defined by the molecular graph Laplacian, ensuring bonded atoms remain spatially coherent) toward a pose aligned with the 3D template. The model is a GNN operating on a joint query-template graph containing atomic nodes, BRICS functional-group nodes, covalent edges, and dense inter-ligand RBF-encoded connections, processed by a Multi-Head Attention with Edge Bias encoder followed by a time-conditioned Vector Field Network that predicts the velocity field. Stage 2 (PO) refines the initial pose via gradient descent directly on all atomic coordinates, minimizing a weighted sum of a differentiable shape-based Tanimoto score, a pharmacophore-based Tanimoto score (Gaussian spheres of 1.7 Å radius per feature type), an optional protein pocket score (pharmacophore complementarity minus steric clash penalty), and GAFF2 internal energy (bonds, angles, torsions, Lennard-Jones, Coulomb). The PO protocol runs in two phases—first without hydrogens, then with hydrogens reintroduced—each containing four sub-steps with empirically tuned weight schedules that progressively shift emphasis from energy minimization to shape/pharmacophore alignment and pocket complementarity.

## Results

On AlignDockBench (369 protein-ligand template-query pairs, cross-docking setup), FMA-PO+ achieves a mean RMSD of 1.62 ± 1.33 Å and 77.78% of molecules with RMSD < 2 Å, outperforming all baselines. FMA-PO (single-pose refinement) reaches 1.86 ± 1.42 Å mean RMSD and 69.38% < 2 Å at a competitive runtime of 3.66 s per molecule, compared to 72.98 s for Vina and 20.68 s for rDock. FMA-PO+ incurs a higher runtime of 27.96 s due to refining all sampled poses before selection. The method maintains its advantage across all similarity and complexity strata, including low template similarity and high rotatable-bond counts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FMA-PO+ outperforms all six baselines on both mean RMSD and the fraction of molecules with RMSD < 2 Å. Against the closest alignment baselines, FMA-PO+ improves mean RMSD by 1.31 Å over FitDock (1.62 vs. 2.93) and by 0.92 Å over LS-align (1.62 vs. 2.54), while increasing the < 2 Å success rate by ~24 and ~23 percentage points respectively. Against the structure-based docking tools, the gains are larger: FMA-PO+ reduces mean RMSD by 2.90 Å versus rDock (4.52 Å) and by 1.77 Å versus Vina (3.39 Å), with corresponding < 2 Å improvements of ~43 and ~30 percentage points. ROSHAMBO, the weakest baseline, trails FMA-PO+ by 1.25 Å in mean RMSD and by 47.4 percentage points in the < 2 Å metric. Notably, FMA-PO (3.66 s) is roughly 20× faster than Vina and 6× faster than rDock while achieving substantially better accuracy, and FMA-PO+ (27.96 s) remains faster than Vina despite its multi-pose refinement. The paper further reports that FMA-PO+ maintains its lead across all similarity bins (Morgan fingerprint and Murcko scaffold) and complexity bins (atom count, rotatable bonds), whereas LS-align and FitDock degrade more sharply at low template similarity.

## Relation to Prior Work

FMA-PO sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: flow-matching-based molecular generation (Lipman et al. 2022; Hassan et al. 2025; Stark et al. 2024) and template-guided ligand alignment (ROCS, LS-align, FitDock, ROSHAMBO). The generative backbone borrows the harmonic prior and linear interpolation schedule from ET-Flow and Harmonic Flow, and the encoder architecture (MHAwithEdgeBias + Vector Field Network) is adapted from Qiao et al. 2024. The key departure is the two-stage design: a flow-matching model conditioned on a 3D template ligand (rather than a protein pocket, as in DiffDock or Harmonic Flow) produces an initial pose, which is then refined by a fully differentiable coordinate-level optimization integrating shape/pharmacophore Tanimoto scores, GAFF2 internal energy, and optional pocket complementarity—something no prior alignment or docking tool performs in a single differentiable pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By unifying ligand-based alignment with structure-based refinement in a differentiable framework, FMA-PO addresses a practical gap: many drug-discovery projects have a co-crystallized reference ligand but lack a high-quality pose for the query compound, and existing tools either ignore the template (Vina, rDock) or cannot handle low-similarity pairs well (LS-align, FitDock). The AlignDockBench benchmark, which deliberately includes low-similarity and high-flexibility pairs, fills a gap left by PoseBusters and FitDock's benchmark. Open questions include whether the approach extends to flexible-receptor (induced-fit) scenarios, whether conditioning on multiple templates or the full pocket improves accuracy, and whether the 28-second runtime of FMA-PO+ is acceptable for large-scale virtual screening. The absence of released code limits immediate reproducibility and community adoption.
