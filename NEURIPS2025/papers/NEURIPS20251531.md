## Problem

The generalization performance of large-margin halfspaces had a persistent logarithmic gap between the best known upper bound (Grønlund et al. 2020) and the matching lower bound, specifically a factor of √(ln n / ln(e/L_S^γ(w))) in the dependence on the empirical margin loss. This paper closes that gap by proving the first upper bound that is asymptotically tight across the full tradeoff between margin γ, empirical margin loss, sample size n, and failure probability δ.

## Input / Output

The bound takes as input a training set S of n labeled points in R^d × {−1,1} (assumed in a ball of radius R), a halfspace classifier (w, b) with w ∈ S^{d-1}, a margin threshold γ ∈ [Rn^{-1/2}, R], and a confidence parameter δ. It outputs a high-probability upper bound on the true misclassification probability L_D(w,b) expressed as L_S^γ(w,b) plus a correction term involving √(L_S^γ · (R²ln(e/L_S^γ)/(γ²n) + ln(e/δ)/n)) and additive terms ln(eγ²n/R²)/(γ²n) and ln(e/δ)/n. The bound is uniform over all halfspaces and all margins simultaneously, and the margin loss L_S^γ(w,b) is the fraction of training points where y(⟨w,x⟩+b) ≤ γ.

## Method

The proof extends the random discretization framework of Schapire et al. [1998] and Grønlund et al. [2020a], which maps each halfspace w to a finite grid via a Johnson-Lindenstrauss projection followed by random snapping to a k-dimensional lattice. The key novelty is a refined decomposition that uses exact equalities (rather than loose inequalities) when relating L_D(w) to the discretized margin loss, so that discretization-induced errors on the distribution side cancel with corresponding errors on the training-set side. The residual discretization error is then bounded via Rademacher complexity and the contraction inequality, where the Lipschitz constant of the relevant probability functions is shown to be O(exp(−γ²k/c)·(√k + γ^{-1})), yielding a √(exp(−γ²k/c)/(γ²n)) bound that is tight enough to match the lower bound when k = cγ^{-2}ln(e/L_S^γ(w)). A further technical contribution handles the probability that the discretized vector falls outside the grid by considering an infinite sequence of grids of increasing norm and applying Cauchy-Schwarz and Jensen's inequality to eliminate the resulting exp(−k/c) factor.

## Results

The paper proves the first asymptotically tight generalization bound for large-margin halfspaces (Theorem 2), closing the gap between the previous best upper bound (Grønlund et al. 2020a, Eq. 4) and the known lower bound (Theorem 1). The key improvement is replacing the $\ln n$ factor in the first-order term with $\ln(e/\mathcal{L}_S^\gamma(w))$, yielding a bound of the form $\mathcal{L}_D(w) \leq \mathcal{L}_S^\gamma(w) + c\bigl(\sqrt{\mathcal{L}_S^\gamma(w)\cdot(\ln(e/\mathcal{L}_S^\gamma(w))/(\gamma^2 n) + \ln(e/\delta)/n)} + \ln(e\gamma^2 n)/(\gamma^2 n) + \ln(e/\delta)/n\bigr)$. This matches the lower bound across the entire tradeoff between margin $\gamma$, empirical margin loss $\mathcal{L}_S^\gamma(w)$, sample size $n$, and confidence $\delta$. The bound also generalizes to non-homogeneous halfspaces and data in a ball of radius $R$ by replacing $\gamma$ with $\gamma/R$.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's main comparison is against the previous state-of-the-art upper bound by Grønlund et al. (2020a, Eq. 4), which had a $\ln n$ factor in the first-order term: $\sqrt{\mathcal{L}_S^\gamma(w)\cdot(\ln n/(\gamma^2 n) + \ln(e/\delta)/n)}$. The new bound (Theorem 2) replaces $\ln n$ with $\ln(e/\mathcal{L}_S^\gamma(w))$, which is strictly smaller and matches the lower bound from Grønlund et al. (Theorem 1) that contains $\ln(e/\tau)$ where $\tau = \mathcal{L}_S^\gamma(w)$. The gap between the old upper bound and the lower bound was a factor of $\sqrt{\ln n / \ln(e/\mathcal{L}_S^\gamma(w))}$, which is eliminated. For the hard-margin case ($\mathcal{L}_S^\gamma(w)=0$), the new bound recovers $\ln(e\gamma^2 n)/(\gamma^2 n) + \ln(e/\delta)/n$, matching the lower bound's $\ln(\gamma^2 n)/(\gamma^2 n)$ term up to constants. Earlier bounds by McAllester (2003) and Bartlett & Shawe-Taylor (1999) had additional $\sqrt{\ln n + \ln(e/\delta)/n}$ terms that do not vanish in the hard-margin case, and the Rademacher-based bound of Bartlett & Mendelson (2002) only matches the lower bound for constant $\mathcal{L}_S^\gamma(w)$ but not across the full range.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the random-discretization framework of Schapire et al. [1998] and the specific Johnson-Lindenstrauss + random-rounding approach of Grønlund et al. [2020a], which established the previous state-of-the-art upper bound for large-margin halfspaces. The key departure is a refined analysis that exploits exact equalities (rather than loose inequalities) in relating true and empirical losses under the randomized discretization, combined with a Rademacher-complexity/contraction-inequality treatment of the rounding-error terms. The random rounding technique itself follows Alon and Klartag [2017], and the overall proof structure builds on the Bernstein-inequality + union-bound machinery from Grønlund et al., but replaces the crude exp(-k/c) tail bound with an infinite-grid argument that eliminates the problematic exponential term.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This resolves a long-standing gap between upper and lower bounds for one of the most fundamental learning models, pinning down the exact asymptotic generalization rate as a function of margin, empirical margin loss, sample size, and confidence. The authors note that analogous logarithmic gaps persist for large-margin voting classifiers (Gao and Zhou [2013] vs. Grønlund et al. [2020b]) and that the techniques may transfer to kernel methods and SVM generalization bounds, where prior results are not first-order. The main limitation is that the bound requires γ ≥ n^{-1/2}, and the proof is highly technical with substantial portions deferred to appendices, making it difficult to adapt without deep familiarity with the machinery.
