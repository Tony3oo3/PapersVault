## Problem

Convergence of Riemannian ADMM for the original nonsmooth composite problem min_{x∈M} f(x)+h(Ax) has remained elusive because the changing tangent spaces on the manifold prevent the standard Euclidean technique of bounding dual-variable differences via optimality-condition differences. Existing Riemannian ADMM variants either lack convergence guarantees (MADMM) or sidestep the difficulty by smoothing the nonsmooth regularizer, fundamentally altering the problem structure. This paper asks whether one can prove convergence of the unsmoothed Riemannian ADMM while retaining the optimal O(ε^{-3}) iteration complexity.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes a smooth function f with Lipschitz-continuous gradient, a proper closed convex Lipschitz regularizer h, a bounded linear operator A, and a compact Riemannian submanifold M embedded in R^n, together with initial points (x₀, y₀, λ₀) and hyperparameters (γ₀, c_γ, ρ₀, c_ρ, c_τ). It outputs a triple (x_K, y_K, λ_K) where x_K lies on M and the triple satisfies the three KKT residual conditions (Riemannian stationarity, proximal optimality, and feasibility) to within ε. Crucially, each iteration requires exactly one Riemannian gradient evaluation of f and one proximal computation of h—no inner loops or exact subproblem solves—so the per-iteration cost is O(1) oracle calls.

## Method

ARADMM replaces the fixed penalty ρ in classical Riemannian ADMM with an increasing sequence ρ_k = c_ρ k^{1/3} and introduces a separate adaptive dual stepsize γ_{k+1} that is chosen as the minimum of a constraint-violation-dependent term and a decaying schedule c_γ/(k^{1/3} log²(k+1)). The x-update is a single Riemannian gradient step with stepsize τ_k = c_τ k^{-1/3} applied to the augmented Lagrangian (rather than an exact subproblem solve), while the y-update is a closed-form proximal step. The key technical insight is that the adaptive γ_{k+1} explicitly bounds ‖λ_{k+1}−λ_k‖ by a combination of the proximal residual (controlled by the Lipschitz constant of h) and the primal iterate difference ‖x_{k+1}−x_k‖, thereby controlling the dual ascent term in the augmented Lagrangian descent inequality without any smoothing. Summing the resulting descent lemma over K iterations and exploiting the summability of the adaptive schedules yields the O(ε^{-3}) complexity for an ε-approximate KKT point on any compact Riemannian submanifold.

## Results

ARADMM achieves an optimal O(ε^{-3}) iteration complexity for finding an ε-approximate KKT point without any smoothing of the nonsmooth regularizer, matching the best-known rate of smoothing-based Riemannian ADMM methods. In the sphere-manifold classification experiment (Table 4), ARADMM attains the best objective value (0.6464) and fastest runtime (0.42 s) at the largest scale (m=600, n=10000, σ²=10), compared to SOC (0.6469, 7.82 s), OADMM (0.6665, 1.07 s), and MADMM/RADMM (0.9216, ~0.7 s). In the DPCP robust subspace recovery task (Table 5), ARADMM reduces CPU time by 1–3 orders of magnitude (e.g., 0.0057 s vs. 1.48 s for SOC at p=4, n=30) while also achieving the lowest objective value.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ARADMM is compared against eight baselines across three problem settings. On the sphere-manifold classifier (Table 4), ARADMM achieves the best or near-best objective value in all four scales and is the fastest method in every case, outperforming SOC by up to 22× in CPU time (1.42 s vs. 30.7 s at the largest instance) and MADMM/RADMM by ~1.5–2× in both objective and runtime. On the DPCP robust subspace recovery problem (Table 5), ARADMM dominates all three ADMM baselines (SOC, MADMM, RADMM) in both objective value and CPU time, with speedups of 200–500× over SOC and 5–100× over MADMM. For sparse PCA (Tables 2–3), ARADMM outperforms the Riemannian subgradient method (RSG) and accelerated manifold proximal gradient (AManPG) in both objective quality and speed, and matches or slightly improves upon ALM-type methods (ALMSSN, ALMSRTR) while being 2–3× faster and producing sparser solutions. The key differentiator is that ARADMM achieves all these gains without any smoothing of the nonsmooth ℓ₁ regularizer, whereas RADMM and OADMM rely on smoothing-based formulations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Riemannian ADMM lineage initiated by MADMM [Kovnatsky et al., ECCV 2016], which first adapted ADMM to manifolds but lacked convergence guarantees. The key prior works—RADMM [Li et al., 2024] and OADMM [Yuan, 2024]—achieved convergence by smoothing the nonsmooth regularizer, with OADMM reaching O(ε⁻³) but restricted to the Stiefel manifold. ARADMM departs from this smoothing paradigm by introducing adaptive dual stepsizes and penalty parameters that bound multiplier differences via primal iterate changes, thereby establishing convergence for the original nonsmooth problem on general compact manifolds. The variable update ordering (y, x, λ) follows the stochastic ADMM framework of Huang et al. [23].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that smoothing is unnecessary for Riemannian ADMM convergence removes a key structural assumption from the field and opens the door to applying ADMM directly to problems where smoothing is intractable or undesirable (e.g., non-differentiable regularizers without smooth approximations). The O(ε⁻³) rate matches the best known for smoothing-based methods while offering lower per-iteration cost (one gradient + one proximal step). A stated limitation is that the analysis does not exploit the Kurdyka–Łojasiewicz inequality, which could yield sharper local convergence rates; extending the framework to non-compact manifolds or to settings where the linear coupling A is replaced by a nonlinear map also remains open.
