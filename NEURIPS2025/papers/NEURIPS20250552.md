## Problem

Existing point tracking benchmarks (TAP-Vid Davis, TAP-Vid Kinetics, and synthetic datasets) are dominated by static points (82–99%), contain very few track reappearances (≤1.02), and feature only 3–5 objects per video, making them poor proxies for real-world motion complexity. Standard metrics reported on these benchmarks conflate distinct failure modes—occlusion recovery, feature binding, and point discrimination—preventing a clear diagnosis of why and when trackers actually break.

## Input / Output

The benchmark provides 72 real-world video clips (sourced from MOSE, LVOS, and Ego4D) with 1,373 algorithmically selected point queries (gradient-based, random, and background) spanning an average of 221.6 frames per track. Models receive RGB video frames resized to their native resolution and then to 256×256 for evaluation, and must output per-frame 2D (or 3D) point coordinates along with occlusion visibility flags. Unlike TAP-Vid, query points are sampled algorithmically from object masks rather than hand-picked by annotators, eliminating salience bias.

## Method

ITTO introduces a two-phase human annotation pipeline (coarse MTurk labeling followed by iterative video-tool refinement by trained UpWork annotators) that achieves 88.3% of points within 2 pixels of ground truth, outperforming the TAP-Vid pipeline's 80%. The evaluation protocol partitions all tracks along two novel axes—average frame-to-frame motion (four tiers from <0.5% to >5% of frame diagonal) and reappearance frequency (three bins by occlusion-to-visible transitions)—and reports standard metrics (Average Jaccard, δ, occlusion accuracy) within each partition to expose model-specific failure modes. A complementary Pairwise Distance Variance (PDV) metric quantifies spatial coherence of tracks on the same object, distinguishing rigid from deformable motion. The paper evaluates ten SOTA 2D and 3D trackers (CoTracker3, LocoTrack, TAPIR, BootsTAP, TAPTR, SpatialTracker, SceneTracker, DELTA) and demonstrates that performance collapses on high-motion and high-reappearance tracks, revealing a stationary initialization bias and catastrophic, irrecoverable tracking forgetting beyond native context windows.

## Results

On ITTO, the best-performing tracker (LocoTrack) achieves only 36.0 Average Jaccard (AJ) and 52.8 δ, while CoTracker3 online reaches 35.3 AJ and 47.0 δ — a dramatic drop from CoTracker3's 64.4 AJ and 76.9 δ on TAP-Vid Davis. Performance collapses on high-motion tracks (>5% frame diagonal): CoTracker3's AJ falls from 55.9 (static) to 13.7, and δ from 68.4 to 22.1. Similarly, tracks with 3+ reappearances see CoTracker3's AJ drop from 50.1 to 13.7, revealing a fundamental inability to re-identify points after occlusion. All 11 evaluated SOTA models show significant degradation on ITTO relative to TAP-Vid, with the best 3D model (DELTA 3D) scoring only 29.1 AJ versus 64.2 on TAP-Vid Davis.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This benchmark paper evaluates 11 SOTA trackers (8 distinct methods) on ITTO and compares their performance against TAP-Vid Davis/Kinetics. LocoTrack [3] achieves the highest AJ (36.0) and δ (52.8) on ITTO, narrowly edging CoTracker3 online [17] (35.3 AJ, 47.0 δ), while CoTracker3 leads in occlusion accuracy (77.6 OA). The relative ranking of models shifts substantially between benchmarks: DELTA [23] was within 2% of CoTracker3 on TAP-Vid but drops by 6% on ITTO, and BootsTAP [8] was 1.8% ahead of CoTracker3 on TAP-Vid yet falls 15% behind on ITTO. TAPIR [9] and TAPTR [21] suffer the largest absolute drops, with AJ scores of only 17.6 and 29.3 respectively. Among 3D methods, DELTA 3D [23] outperforms SpatialTracker [34] and SceneTracker [31] on average, but SpatialTracker is more robust under high occlusion (AJ 17.2 vs. DELTA's 9.2 at 72–100% occlusion rate). The key finding is that all models exhibit a stationary bias and catastrophic forgetting: performance on static tracks remains near-perfect while high-motion and high-reappearance tracks see near-complete collapse across every method.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper does not extend a specific tracking method but instead carves new ground in the evaluation landscape for point tracking. It departs from the TAP-Vid benchmark family (Davis, Kinetics) and synthetic benchmarks (DynamicReplica, PointOdyssey) by identifying a systematic stationary bias—82–88% of tracks in existing real-world benchmarks are static, with near-zero reappearance counts—and sourcing videos from VOS datasets (MOSE, LVOS) and egocentric video (Ego4D) to capture the motion complexity those benchmarks miss. The multi-stage annotation pipeline (MTurk coarse labeling followed by UpWork video refinement) is positioned as an alternative to the algorithmic track initialization used in TAP-Vid, avoiding algorithmic bias in ground-truth generation. The paper also introduces partitioned evaluation axes (track motion tiers, reappearance frequency, PDV) that go beyond the standard AJ/δ/OA metrics to diagnose *why* and *when* trackers fail.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals a fundamental architectural gap: current SOTA trackers suffer catastrophic, irrecoverable failure once a track leaves the model's native context window, and cannot re-identify points after even short occlusions (average 12.5 frames for 3+ reappearances), pointing to the need for memory mechanisms, re-initialization steps, or object-pointer formulations. The finding that relative model rankings shift dramatically between TAP-Vid and ITTO (e.g., BootsTAP drops 15% relative to CoTracker3) suggests widespread overfitting to existing benchmark characteristics. Limitations include pixel-level annotation precision, exclusion of featureless regions, 2D-only evaluation, and limited domain coverage, leaving open questions about 3D generalization and whether the annotation pipeline scales to new domains.
