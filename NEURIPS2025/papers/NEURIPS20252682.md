## Problem

Multi-modal object ReID suffers from two intertwined challenges: fine-grained local noise (occlusion, frame loss, glare) degrades discriminative features within each modality, and heterogeneous modalities are difficult to fuse effectively. Existing methods largely assume balanced, stable quality across modalities and lack explicit modeling of both local-region uncertainty and sample-level noise intensity, leaving the model unable to adaptively suppress unreliable cues during fusion.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252682/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes three co-registered spectral images per identity (RGB, NIR, TIR), resized to 256×128 for persons or 128×256 for vehicles, and tokenizes each into 128 local patches plus one global class token via a shared CLIP ViT encoder (16×16 patching). The output is a single fused feature embedding per sample, obtained by concatenating the per-modality expert-aggregated representations, which is then used for standard mAP/Rank retrieval evaluation against a gallery set.

## Method

UGG-ReID introduces two coupled modules. First, the Gaussian Patch-Graph Representation (GPGR) parameterizes every token (global and local) as a Gaussian N(μ, σ²), builds a patch-graph whose edges are weighted by mean-vector similarity, and propagates both mean and variance through a graph convolutional network (GPGCN) with reparameterized sampling and KL-divergence regularization, thereby quantifying aleatoric uncertainty at the local-patch level and modeling structural dependencies among patches. Second, the Uncertainty-Guided Mixture of Experts (UGMoE) models sample-level uncertainty via a second Gaussian layer, routes each sample to the top-k expert with the lowest predicted variance, and—critically—extends each modality's gate to access C+M−1 experts (including experts from other modalities) so that uncertainty-aware routing simultaneously performs noise-adaptive expert selection and cross-modal interaction. The full framework is trained end-to-end with cross-entropy, triplet, KL, routing, and expert-balancing losses.

## Results

UGG-ReID achieves state-of-the-art performance across all five multi-modal object ReID benchmarks. On RGBNT201 (person ReID), it reaches 81.2% mAP and 86.8% R-1, surpassing the previous best (IDEA) by 1.0% mAP and 4.7% R-1. On Market1501-MM, it attains 85.4% mAP and 94.3% R-1. On the vehicle ReID benchmark WMVEID863 with severe flare interference, it achieves 72.6% mAP and 80.8% R-1, outperforming the runner-up (FACENeT) by 2.8% mAP and 3.8% R-1. The method also runs at 371.4 FPS with 103.2M parameters and 35.0G FLOPs, comparable to lighter baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UGG-ReID outperforms all 19 compared baselines on every reported metric across all five datasets. On RGBNT201, it leads IDEA (CVPR'25) by +1.0% mAP / +4.7% R-1, DeMo (AAAI'25) by +1.5% mAP / +5.0% R-1, and MambaPro (AAAI'25) by +2.3% mAP / +3.4% R-1. On Market1501-MM, it surpasses MambaPro by +1.3% mAP / +1.5% R-1 and DeMo by +1.8% mAP / +1.2% R-1. On the vehicle ReID benchmark WMVEID863 (severe flare), it beats FACENeT (INFFUS'25) by +2.8% mAP / +3.8% R-1 and MambaPro by +3.1% mAP / +3.9% R-1. On MSVR310, it exceeds ICPL-ReID (TMM'25) by +3.2% mAP and PromptMA (TIP'25) by +4.9% mAP / +13.5% R-1. The largest margins appear on R-1 for person ReID (up to +5.9% over PromptMA) and on mAP for vehicle ReID under flare (up to +3.8% over DeMo), indicating the uncertainty-guided design is most beneficial under high-noise conditions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads in multi-modal object ReID: (1) feature-fusion methods (PFNet, TIENet, MambaPro) that bridge cross-modal semantic gaps, (2) noise-robustness methods (EDITOR, IDEA, TOP-ReID) that suppress local interference, and (3) uncertainty-aware multi-modal learning (MAP, EAU, EUAR). It departs from prior work by replacing deterministic feature-vector graph nodes with Gaussian-distribution nodes (GPGR), enabling explicit quantification of local aleatoric uncertainty during message passing, and by coupling this with an uncertainty-guided MoE routing mechanism that allows cross-modal expert sharing. The uncertainty-aware routing constraint and expert-balancing regularizer are directly adapted from EUAR [31], but extended to a multi-modal ReID setting with a novel cross-modal top-k routing design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: most multi-modal ReID methods assume balanced modality quality and ignore heterogeneous local noise (occlusion, glare, frame loss), which degrades fusion robustness. By jointly modeling local and sample-level aleatoric uncertainty, UGG-ReID provides a principled mechanism for the network to down-weight unreliable regions and route noisy samples to appropriate experts. However, the authors acknowledge the framework may still struggle under extreme corruption where local cues are heavily missing, and future work points toward Bayesian inference and evidence theory for stronger robustness. The lack of statistical significance testing and the reliance on a single GPU for all experiments limit the strength of the empirical claims.
