## Problem

Existing LLM ensemble methods face two key limitations: generate-then-ensemble approaches incur first-token delay by waiting for all models to finish, while ensemble-while-generation methods struggle with long-range semantic collaboration across models. Moreover, all prior methods assume equal contribution from every model during aggregation, ignoring that different LLMs exhibit task-specific performance differences that should be reflected in their voting influence.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251752/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

SpecEM takes a text instruction and a set of N heterogeneous base LLMs (7B–72B parameters, e.g., Llama-3, Mistral, Qwen2, GLM-4, Gemma-2) as input, and produces a single generated text response. Generation proceeds in iterative rounds where each round produces a candidate segment of at most L=10 tokens; the selected segment is appended to the shared context before the next round begins. The verify-in-line mechanism concatenates all N candidate segments into one sequence with a custom attention mask and reset position IDs, so each model scores all candidates in a single parallel forward pass rather than N separate calls.

## Method

SpecEM reimagines speculative decoding for ensembling: in each iteration, all models draft a candidate segment in parallel (drafting stage), then mutually score every candidate by averaging token-level logits (verification stage), with the highest weighted-score segment broadcast as the next context. A verify-in-line attention mask blocks cross-candidate attention and resets position IDs so each model effectively scores its own candidate in isolation while processing the concatenated sequence in one pass. An online feedback mechanism based on multiplicative weight updates dynamically adjusts each model's verification weight ω_i^(k) = ω_i^(k-1)·e^(η·γ_i^(k)), where the reward γ_i^(k) counts how often model i's candidate outperforms others in peer evaluations, and the learning rate η = α·√(1/k)/N decays over rounds for stability. This ensures stronger generators progressively gain more influence as verifiers, eliminating the need for any trained fusion module or selection aggregator.

## Results

On the English FuseEval benchmark, SpecEM (7B–9B base models) achieves ROUGE-1 of 31.19, ROUGE-2 of 14.40, and GPT4-Rank of 3.98, surpassing the best single base model (Mistral-7B: ROUGE-1 27.75) by over 3 points and the strongest baseline (PairRank: ROUGE-1 28.21) by nearly 3 points. On MMLU and ARC-C, SpecEM(All) reaches 73.01% and 89.08% accuracy respectively, exceeding the best base model (Gemma-2-9B: 71.51% / 88.14%). When scaled to 24B–72B models, SpecEM achieves an average win rate of 54.52% on FuseEval and AlpacaEval 2.0, outperforming the strongest single model (Mistral-24B: 44.27%) by over 10 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SpecEM outperforms all five baselines across the majority of metrics. Against PairRank (Jiang et al., 2023b), SpecEM gains +2.98 ROUGE-1, +3.54 ROUGE-2, and reduces GPT4-Rank from 6.84 to 3.98 on English FuseEval. Against MBR (Freitag et al., 2023), the margin is even larger: +4.07 ROUGE-1 and +4.03 ROUGE-2. Against MOA (Wang et al., 2024), SpecEM leads by +3.58 ROUGE-1 and +2.93 ROUGE-2, though MOA slightly edges out on BLEU (5.12 vs. 5.81 is actually SpecEM winning). Against UniTE (Yao et al., 2025), SpecEM achieves +3.38 ROUGE-1 and +3.49 ROUGE-2, with UniTE showing particularly weak performance on Chinese FuseEval (win rate 19.13% vs. SpecEM's 56.77%). Against Majority Voting (Davani et al., 2022), SpecEM(All) surpasses it by +1.23 on MMLU (73.01 vs. 71.78) and +0.70 on ARC-C (89.08 vs. 88.38), while also dominating on IFEval where Majority Voting is not applicable.

## Relation to Prior Work

SpecEM repurposes the drafting-verification paradigm from speculative decoding (originally designed to accelerate single-model inference) into a multi-model collaboration framework, where all LLMs draft and verify each other's segments in parallel rather than a small model drafting for a large one. It departs from prior LLM ensemble methods in two key ways: (1) it operates at the segment level rather than token-level probability aggregation (as in DeePEn, EVA, UniTE) or full-response fusion (as in MOA, GenFuse), and (2) it introduces an online multiplicative-weights-update mechanism that dynamically reweights models during generation, whereas all prior ensemble methods assume static, equal contribution. The verify-in-line attention mask and position-ID trick is a practical engineering contribution that enables parallel scoring without redundant context re-encoding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, plug-and-play nature of SpecEM makes it immediately deployable with any collection of off-the-shelf LLMs without fine-tuning a fusion module, which is a significant practical advantage over methods requiring a trained aggregator. The dynamic reweighting addresses a real limitation of prior ensembles—static equal voting ignores task-specific model strengths—and the empirical validation that stronger generators are also stronger verifiers (Appendix A) provides a principled basis for the feedback mechanism. Key open questions include how to handle the degradation caused by weak models in early drafting rounds (the paper acknowledges this in its Limitations section) and whether adaptive or semantics-aware segmentation could replace the fixed-length L=10 constraint that occasionally causes semantic truncation.
