## Problem

There is no general-purpose, computationally efficient framework for differentially private statistical estimation when the data support is unbounded (e.g., ℝ^d), because bounding estimator sensitivity requires problem-specific analysis in each case. This paper introduces data truncation as a unified mechanism to control sensitivity, while correcting the resulting bias through maximum likelihood estimation on the truncated distribution.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes N i.i.d. samples from an unbounded-support exponential family (or Gaussian) distribution along with privacy parameters (ε, δ), accuracy target α, and a survival set S ⊆ ℝ^d. After truncation and a preprocessing step that pads/truncates to exactly n samples and shuffles them, the method runs DP-SGD over n² iterations. It outputs a parameter estimate θ̂ (or μ̂, Σ̂ for Gaussians) satisfying ‖θ̂ − θ⋆‖ ≤ α with probability 1−β, with sample complexity linear in the sufficient-statistic dimension m and proportional to 1/ε, 1/α², and 1/(αε).

## Method

The core mechanism truncates data to a survival set S_SGD defined as a ball in sufficient-statistic space, which bounds gradient sensitivity to O(√m + R) and enables DP-SGD. To correct truncation bias, the algorithm minimizes the truncated *empirical* NLL (rather than the population NLL) via DP-SGD, which requires a uniform convergence guarantee showing the empirical minimizer stays within α of the true parameter; the authors prove this at O(1/α²) samples by establishing a Polyak-Łojasiewicz condition on the truncated NLL over a carefully chosen projection set K. Unbiased stochastic gradients are obtained through rejection sampling from q_θ restricted to S_SGD. A recursive warm-start and preconditioning procedure, adapted from Biswas et al.'s Gaussian algorithms to truncated exponential families, removes the need for a prior parameter bound and avoids polynomial dependence on the condition number.

## Results

The paper is purely theoretical, with no empirical experiments. The headline results are sample complexity bounds: for general unbounded high-dimensional exponential families (Theorem 3.1), the algorithm achieves Õ(m log(1/ηβδ)/(λ²ε) + m log(1/β)/(λ⁴η⁴α²) + e^{O(1/λ²)}m log(1/βδ)/(λ²αε)) samples. For Gaussian mean estimation (Theorem 4.1), the sample complexity is Õ(d log(1/βδ)/ε + d log(1/β)/α² + d log(1/βδ)/(αε)), and for Gaussian covariance estimation (Theorem 4.2), it is Õ(d^{1.5} log(Λ/λβδ)/ε + d² log(1/βδ)/ε + d² log(1/β)/α² + d² log(1/βδ)/(αε)). These Gaussian rates match the known optimal sample complexities up to logarithmic factors.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines or experimental comparisons. The theoretical comparisons are as follows: for Gaussian mean and covariance estimation, the paper recovers the same optimal sample complexities (up to log factors) previously achieved by Aden-Ali et al. [3] (which required exponential running time) and Hopkins et al. [26, 27] (polynomial time via robust-to-private transformations), but through a more general truncation-based framework. For exponential family estimation, the paper provides the first efficient algorithm for unbounded high-dimensional multi-parameter families, whereas all prior DP exponential family works [6, 23, 37] were restricted to bounded or one-dimensional/one-parameter settings. The uniform convergence result (Lemma 3.6) improves upon Shah et al. [43], which required Ω(1/α⁴) samples, by achieving O(1/α²) via a Polyak-Łojasiewicz condition argument.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate literatures: truncated statistics (Daskalakis et al. [17], Lee et al. [34, 35]) and differentially private optimization (Bassily et al. [5]). The key conceptual move is repurposing truncation—originally a tool for handling censored data in non-private estimation—as a sensitivity-bounding mechanism for DP, then correcting the resulting bias via DP-SGD on the truncated negative log-likelihood. It departs from Shah et al. [43] by proving a stronger uniform convergence result (O(1/α²) vs. O(1/α⁴) samples) via a Polyak-Lojasiewicz argument, and extends the warm-start/preconditioning machinery of Biswas et al. [7] to the truncated exponential family setting. Prior DP exponential family work (Bernstein & Sheldon [6], McMillan et al. [37]) was restricted to bounded or one-parameter families, making this the first treatment of unbounded high-dimensional multi-parameter cases.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a general blueprint for DP algorithm design via truncated statistics, which could extend to other unbounded-support problems (regression, linear dynamics) where sensitivity bounding is the bottleneck. The improved uniform convergence guarantee for exponential family log-likelihoods is of independent interest beyond the DP setting. Key limitations include the exponential dependence on the condition number λ (an artifact of existing anti-concentration tools) and the requirement of constant survival mass ρ = Ω(1), which restricts applicability to truncation sets that retain a constant fraction of the distribution. Open questions include whether the λ-dependence can be removed for general exponential families and whether the truncation paradigm extends to more complex statistical tasks.
