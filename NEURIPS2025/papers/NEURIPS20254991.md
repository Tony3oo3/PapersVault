## Problem

Large vision-language models frequently hallucinate objects or misinterpret visual details because attention mechanisms fail to reliably focus on the most informative visual tokens, especially under ambiguity. Existing remedies either require resource-intensive training-stage fine-tuning or rely on heuristic inference-time adjustments that add cost without principled grounding. The paper asks whether uncertainty in visual token representations can be quantified at inference time and used to selectively suppress unreliable tokens, improving reliability without any parameter modification.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an RGB image (segmented into patches and encoded into 32–2000+ visual tokens depending on the backbone) concatenated with a text instruction, and produces autoregressively generated text such as image captions or VQA answers. It operates entirely at inference time on frozen LVLMs (LLaVA-1.5, InstructBLIP, LLaVA-NEXT), requiring no fine-tuning or additional supervision. The key intermediate artifact is a per-visual-token text-space projection distribution obtained via the logit lens, which drives all downstream uncertainty computation and dropout decisions.

## Method

DROPOUT DECODING projects each visual token's top-layer hidden state through the vocabulary matrix to obtain a text-space distribution (logit lens), then computes epistemic uncertainty as the KL divergence between each token's projection and the average projection across all visual tokens. During decoding, it formulates per-token dropout probabilities that scale linearly with normalized epistemic uncertainty, controlled by hyperparameters γ and δ that set the probability range. It samples K independent binary dropout masks (K=3 in practice), optionally retaining tokens whose projections contain the preliminary-prediction token, and runs K parallel forward passes with the masked visual contexts. The final output token at each step is selected by majority voting across the K candidates, with ties broken in favor of the candidate with the fewest dropped tokens. This ensemble-of-masked-contexts strategy diversifies the model's visual perspective, reducing the impact of any single misinterpreted token without altering model weights.

## Results

On MSCOCO (CHAIR benchmark), DROPOUT DECODING reduces object hallucination across all three LVLMs: on InstructBLIP, CHAIR_I drops from 7.90 (Greedy) to 6.63 and CHAIR_S from 27.87 to 24.53; on LLaVA-1.5, CHAIR_I drops from 12.83 to 11.73; on LLaVA-NEXT, CHAIR_S drops from 28.80 to 26.26. On THRONE, the method achieves the best F_all^1 (0.804, 0.814, 0.821) and P_all (0.790, 0.838, 0.842) across LLaVA-1.5, InstructBLIP, and LLaVA-NEXT respectively. On MMBench, DROPOUT DECODING scores 74.01 on LLaVA-1.5 (vs. 71.86 original) and 74.31 on LLaVA-NEXT, outperforming both VCD and OPERA on LLaVA-1.5.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DROPOUT DECODING outperforms both OPERA and VCD on CHAIR_S and CHAIR_I across all three models. Against OPERA, the gains are consistent: e.g., on InstructBLIP, CHAIR_I improves from 8.23 to 6.63 (−19.4%) and CHAIR_S from 28.07 to 24.53 (−12.6%). Against VCD, the advantage is more pronounced, as VCD actually worsens hallucination in several settings (e.g., CHAIR_S rises from 27.87 to 39.33 on InstructBLIP, and from 28.80 to 33.19 on LLaVA-NEXT), while DROPOUT DECODING reduces it. On THRONE, DROPOUT DECODING achieves the best F_all^1 and P_all for all three models, with the largest margin over OPERA on InstructBLIP (F_all^1: 0.814 vs. 0.805; P_all: 0.838 vs. 0.830). On MMBench, DROPOUT DECODING leads on LLaVA-1.5 (74.01 vs. OPERA 73.86, VCD 72.35) but is slightly below the original model on LLaVA-NEXT (74.31 vs. 74.57), while VCD degrades substantially on LLaVA-NEXT (69.65). The method also beats Beam Search on CHAIR metrics for all models, confirming that the uncertainty-guided dropout provides gains beyond standard decoding strategies.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes two well-established ideas in novel ways: classical dropout (Srivastava et al., 2014) is shifted from training-time parameter regularization to inference-time masking of input visual tokens, and the logit lens technique from mechanistic interpretability (Belrose et al., 2023) is used to project visual tokens into text space for uncertainty quantification. The uncertainty decomposition framework (Hüllermeier & Waegeman, 2021) is adapted to define per-token epistemic uncertainty as KL divergence from an average visual-textual distribution. Compared to prior hallucination-reduction methods like VCD (contrastive decoding with distorted visuals) and OPERA (attention-based penalties), Dropout Decoding operates purely on the input context without modifying model internals or requiring auxiliary models, positioning it as a lighter-weight, more general inference-time intervention.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method offers a training-free, plug-and-play approach to reducing object hallucinations in LVLMs, which is practically appealing given the resource cost of fine-tuning-based alternatives. However, the computational overhead is non-trivial (up to 45.7% throughput reduction with the preliminary forward pass), and the reliance on text-space projection quality as a proxy for visual uncertainty remains an open question—particularly for models with weak vision-text alignment. The finding that epistemic uncertainty, but not aleatoric uncertainty, correlates with visual token informativeness provides a useful diagnostic, but whether this holds across architectures and modalities (e.g., video) is untested. Future work could explore adaptive dropout rates, learned uncertainty estimators, or integration with other decoding strategies to reduce the ensemble cost.
