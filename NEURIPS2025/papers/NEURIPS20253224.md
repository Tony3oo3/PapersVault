## Problem

Existing end-to-end speech models cannot deliver the first streaming audio chunk upon completing the first LLM forward pass, resulting in high first-token latency that is a critical bottleneck for real-time speech interaction. While interleaved audio-text modeling better preserves language capabilities than parallel approaches, its sequential audio token prediction introduces further delay, especially at high audio token rates.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253224/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts speech input (encoded via GLM-4-Voice tokenizer or SenseVoiceSmall at 12.5 Hz) and text, and produces interleaved text and discrete audio tokens that are decoded into waveform by a GLM-4-Voice flow-matching decoder. In the fastest Turbo mode, a single LLM forward pass yields 1 text token plus 10 audio tokens from MCTP modules, enabling the first decodable audio chunk within ~50 ms. Four inference modes (Turbo, Boost, Balance, Vanilla) trade off generation speed against speech and language quality by varying how many tokens the main LLM versus MCTP modules produce per step.

## Method

VITA-Audio introduces lightweight Multiple Cross-modal Token Prediction (MCTP) modules that generate 10 audio tokens per forward pass directly from LLM hidden states and text embeddings, achieving zero audio token delay. The key insight, validated through attention-map analysis and masking experiments, is that LLM hidden states already encode sufficient contextual information for the corresponding audio tokens, so a simple text-to-speech mapping suffices without full LLM semantic modeling. Each MCTP module is a small Transformer block arranged in a cascaded chain (inspired by DeepSeek-V3's MTP), where each module conditions on the hidden state and output of the preceding one to progressively refine the cross-modal mapping. Training follows a four-stage progressive strategy: (1) audio-text alignment on ~100K hours of open-source ASR/TTS data, (2) single MCTP module training, (3) scaling to 10 cascaded MCTP modules, and (4) supervised fine-tuning on 1.4M speech QA pairs to enable speech-to-speech dialogue. Built on Qwen2.5-7B, the system achieves 3–5× inference speedup while matching or exceeding open-source baselines on ASR, TTS, and SQA benchmarks.

## Results

On Spoken Question Answering (SQA), VITA-Audio-Plus-Vanilla achieves a mean S→S score of 50.8 and S→T score of 55.5, outperforming all open-source baselines (Moshi: 12.5 S→S, GLM-4-Voice: 31.0 S→S, LUCY: 29.1 S→S) and approaching the proprietary MinMo (47.2 S→S). On TTS (Seed-TTS), VITA-Audio-Turbo at Stage 3 achieves 1.05% CER (Chinese) and 1.77% WER (English), surpassing CosyVoice2 (1.45% CER, 2.57% WER) and matching Seed-TTS (1.12% CER). On ASR, VITA-Audio-Plus-Vanilla(S1) achieves 6.68% WER on WenetSpeech test_meeting and 1.51% on AISHELL-1, competitive with Qwen2.5-Omni (7.71% and 1.13%). Inference speedup reaches 4.72× (Turbo mode, 7B) and 6.46× (Turbo mode, 72B) over vanilla autoregressive decoding, with first-audio-chunk latency reduced from 236 ms to 53 ms.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VITA-Audio-Plus-Vanilla outperforms all open-source speech models on SQA: it leads Moshi (12.5 S→S) by 38.3 points, GLM-4-Voice (31.0 S→S) by 19.8 points, and LUCY (29.1 S→S) by 21.7 points in mean S→S accuracy. Against the proprietary MinMo baseline, VITA-Audio-Plus-Vanilla trails by 6.4 points on S→T (55.5 vs. 60.7) but surpasses it by 3.6 points on S→S (50.8 vs. 47.2), indicating superior speech-to-speech alignment. On TTS, VITA-Audio-Turbo(S3) beats CosyVoice2 by 0.40% CER and 0.80% WER on Seed-TTS, and VITA-Audio-Plus-Vanilla achieves 1.13% CER, slightly below Seed-TTS (1.12%) but well below CosyVoice (3.63%). On ASR, VITA-Audio-Plus-Vanilla(S1) achieves 6.68% WER on WenetSpeech test_meeting, beating VITA-Audio-Vanilla (17.34%) and approaching Qwen2.5-Omni (7.71%), while on AISHELL-1 it reaches 1.51% WER, close to Qwen2.5-Omni's 1.13%. The key differentiator is inference speed: VITA-Audio-Turbo achieves 4.72× speedup at 7B scale with zero audio token delay, whereas baselines like GLM-4-Voice require 13 additional forward passes and Moshi requires 1, making VITA-Audio the only model with both zero audio token delay and zero audio generation delay.

## Relation to Prior Work

VITA-Audio adapts the Multi-Token Prediction (MTP) framework from DeepSeek-V3—originally designed for text semantic modeling—to the cross-modal audio-text mapping problem, repurposing it as lightweight MCTP modules that predict multiple audio tokens from LLM hidden states. It positions itself within the interleaved end-to-end speech paradigm (as opposed to the parallel paradigm of Moshi and Mini-Omni), which better preserves LLM language capabilities, but addresses the sequential-generation latency bottleneck that plagues all prior interleaved models (GLM-4-Voice, LUCY, Mini-Omni) by generating 10 audio tokens per forward pass instead of one. The key conceptual departure from prior work is the observation that LLM hidden states at individual text positions contain sufficient local context for audio generation, eliminating the need for the full autoregressive loop over audio tokens.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VITA-Audio's zero audio token delay (first audio chunk generated during the initial LLM forward pass) is a meaningful architectural first for end-to-end speech models, reducing perceived latency from ~236ms to ~53ms and enabling more natural real-time conversation. The 3–5× inference speedup at 7B scale while preserving TTS and SQA quality makes the interleaved paradigm practically viable for deployment. However, the paper acknowledges that the audio decoder remains a latency bottleneck, and the approach has only been validated on a single LLM backbone (Qwen2.5-7B) with a specific tokenizer (GLM-4-Voice / SenseVoiceSmall), leaving open whether the MCTP strategy generalizes to other architectures or higher audio token rates.
