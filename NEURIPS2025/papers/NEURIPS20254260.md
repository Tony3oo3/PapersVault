## Problem

Existing red-teaming methods for synthetic image detectors (SIDs) either require white-box access to proprietary detectors or perform expensive per-image optimization in the T2I latent space. PolyJuice addresses both gaps by being the first black-box, image-agnostic (universal) unrestricted adversarial attack on SIDs, enabling efficient red teaming even with API-only access to commercial detectors.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254260/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

PolyJuice takes a text prompt and a T2I generative model (SDv3.5, FLUX[dev], or FLUX[sch]) as input, along with pre-computed per-timestep steering directions δ_t that are found offline from 20K TP and 20K FN examples. The directions are computed at a base resolution of 256×256 and transferred to higher resolutions (512×512, 1024×1024) via spatial interpolation. The output is a set of adversarial synthetic images that the target SID misclassifies as real, with the steering applied at each denoising timestep during generation.

## Method

PolyJuice exploits a key observation: there is a measurable distribution shift in the T2I latent space between samples the SID correctly detects as fake (TP) and those it misses (FN). It identifies the steering direction via Supervised Principal Component Analysis (SPCA), which finds the subspace maximizing HSIC-based statistical dependence between T2I latents and SID hard labels, requiring only black-box label access. The steering direction δ_t is a convex combination of the top eigenvectors of ZHK_YYHZ^T weighted by their eigenvalues, computed once per timestep. At inference, each latent z_t is shifted by λ_t·δ_t (with λ_t searched via Optuna over a continuous interval of timesteps), universally steering all generated images toward the SID's failure modes without per-image optimization. Resolution transferability further reduces cost by interpolating low-resolution directions to higher resolutions, leveraging the resolution-invariance of KL-regularized autoencoder latents.

## Results

PolyJuice boosts the false negative rate (FNR, i.e., attack success rate) of T2I models against synthetic image detectors by up to 84 percentage points (SDv3.5 vs. RINE at 256×256: 15.3% → 99.7%). Averaged across three T2I generators (SDv3.5, FLUX[dev], FLUX[sch]) and three resolutions, PolyJuice achieves 89.4±6.8% FNR against UFD and 91.7±9.0% against RINE, compared to 59.6±21.8% and 53.7±21.1% for unsteered baselines. Even when the target SID is calibrated on the same T2I model used for the attack, PolyJuice still improves FNR by up to 72%. Calibrating SIDs with PolyJuice-generated attack samples reduces their FNR by up to 30%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is against unsteered T2I generation (no attack), where PolyJuice consistently outperforms across all T2I–SID pairs and resolutions, with the largest gains on SDv3.5 (up to +84 pts vs. RINE) and smaller but still substantial gains on FLUX models (up to +28 pts). The paper also compares against a transferred white-box attack: DiffPGD [Xue et al., NeurIPS 2023] applied to a surrogate ResNet-50 and then transferred to the black-box RINE detector. Against RINE with FLUX[dev], PolyJuice achieves 81.2% FNR versus 70.1% for the best DiffPGD variant (realistic x₀ⁿ) and 57.2% for the standard xⁿ variant. Against RINE with SDv3.5, the gap is even more dramatic: PolyJuice reaches 99.7% FNR while DiffPGD^t achieves only 32.1% (x₀ⁿ) and 23.5% (xⁿ). PolyJuice thus dominates the transferred white-box baseline by 11–68 percentage points, while requiring no gradient access and no per-image optimization.

## Relation to Prior Work

PolyJuice builds on the unrestricted adversarial (UA) attack paradigm, where generative models are used to synthesize attack images rather than perturbing clean ones. Prior UA attacks on diffusion/flow-matching models (DiffPGD, AdvDiff, DiffAttack) rely on white-box gradient access and per-image latent optimization; PolyJuice departs from this by replacing gradient-based, image-specific steering with a single distribution-based direction discovered via SPCA/HSIC on hard-label queries. The conceptual lineage also draws on classifier-guided steering in T2I models, but repurposes the steering mechanism to target an abstract property (realness) that the T2I model was never trained to condition on, using only the observed distribution shift between TP and FN samples in latent space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PolyJuice provides the first practical red-teaming tool for proprietary, API-only synthetic image detectors, enabling defenders to stress-test and improve SIDs without white-box access. The resolution transferability result (256×256 directions working as well or better at 512×512) offers a significant computational efficiency gain for high-resolution attacks. Key limitations include the assumption of linear dependence between latents and realness (nonlinear kernels are discussed but not implemented), the need for hyperparameter search over λ_t, and evaluation limited to COCO prompts and two primary SID models. Open questions include whether the approach extends to video generation, whether the linear SPCA direction remains effective as SIDs incorporate more sophisticated frequency-domain features, and how to defend against the identified failure modes (low brightness for RINE, warm tones for UFD).
