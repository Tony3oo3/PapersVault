## Problem

In Local SGD and its generalizations (FedOpt), the outer optimizer that aggregates client updates is a poorly understood component: no explicit expressions existed for the optimal inner-outer learning rate pair (η, γ), the bilevel structure's benefit in i.i.d. settings was unexplained, and the mechanism by which Nesterov acceleration in the outer loop improves convergence was unknown. Prior analyses forced the local stepsize η to scale as O(1/(LH)), precluding recovery of the tight Local SGD rate when η is larger.

## Input / Output

Theoretically, the analysis takes the FedOpt template with M i.i.d. nodes, H local steps per round, R communication rounds, and two stepsizes (inner η, outer γ), producing explicit convergence bounds and closed-form optimal (η*, γ*) via KKT conditions. A key unusual feature is that the stepsize constraint ηL(1+(γ−1)₊H) ≤ 1/4 is asymmetric, permitting γ > 1 when η is small. Empirically, the method trains Chinchilla-style decoder transformers (150M, 400M, 1B parameters) on C4 with batch size 512 and sequence length 1024, using AdamW with a cosine schedule as the inner optimizer and comparing three outer optimizers (SGD averaging, Nesterov/DiLoCo, Schedule-Free SGD), reporting validation perplexity.

## Method

The core technical contribution is a novel, tighter convergence analysis of Generalized Local SGD that uses online convex optimization regret to bound the client-drift inner product term, enabling the analysis to hold for all η (not just η = O(1/(LH))). This reveals the dual role of the outer learning rate γ: it interpolates between minibatch SGD (γ > 1) and vanilla Local SGD (γ = 1), and it compensates for an ill-tuned inner learning rate even in the noiseless regime. The analysis extends to momentum (showing the effective outer stepsize is γ/(1−μ)) and to Nesterov acceleration in the outer loop, where the drift terms achieve O(1/R²) dependence on communication rounds, improving upon FedAC's local acceleration. A data-dependent, high-probability guarantee further shows that γ < 1 becomes optimal in noise-dominated late-training regimes. Empirical validation at three model scales confirms that outer learning rates above 1 and acceleration consistently outperform simple averaging.

## Results

On C4 validation perplexity for Chinchilla-style transformers, Nesterov outer optimizer (DiLoCo) with γ=1.0 achieves 16.38 at 150M parameters, outperforming simple averaging (SGD γ=1) at 17.75 and SF-SGD (γ=2.0) at 16.88. At 400M, Nesterov with γ=0.7 reaches 13.71 perplexity versus 14.90 for SGD averaging, while Nesterov with γ=1.0 diverges (>30). All distributed methods with M=4 replicas and H=50 local steps outperform the standard data-parallel baseline (18.07 at 150M, 15.28 at 400M) but remain behind the higher-communication 4× batch-size data-parallel baseline (16.89 at 150M, 13.21 at 400M). The convex optimization experiments confirm the theory: the optimal outer learning rate γ decreases monotonically from 1.0 to 0.1 as gradient noise σ increases from 10⁻³ to 50.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three outer optimizers—simple averaging (SGD γ=1, equivalent to FedAvg/McMahan et al. 2017), Nesterov acceleration (equivalent to DiLoCo/Douillard et al. 2023), and Schedule-Free SGD (Defazio et al. 2024)—against two data-parallel baselines at matched FLOPs. Nesterov consistently outperforms simple averaging: at 150M it achieves 16.38 vs. 17.75 perplexity (γ=1.0), and at 400M it achieves 13.71 vs. 14.90 (γ=0.7), confirming the theoretical prediction that acceleration in the outer loop improves convergence. SF-SGD with an outer learning rate of 2.0 (greater than 1, as predicted by the theory) performs competitively with Nesterov at 150M (16.88 vs. 16.38) and 400M (13.95 vs. 13.71), validating the paper's claim that γ>1 is beneficial. However, all distributed methods lose to the higher-communication 4× batch-size data-parallel baseline (16.89 at 150M, 13.21 at 400M), which the authors attribute to decreasing cosine similarity between outer gradients as replica count increases. A notable finding is that Nesterov with γ=1.0 diverges at 400M (>30 perplexity) while γ=0.7 succeeds, illustrating the sensitivity of the outer learning rate to model scale and the need for careful tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the convergence analysis of Local SGD (Stich, 2019; Khaled et al., 2020; Woodworth et al., 2020) to Generalized Local SGD with a variable outer stepsize γ, building on the FedOpt template (Reddi et al., 2021) and the DiLoCo algorithm (Douillard et al., 2023). Prior analyses of Generalized Local SGD (Karimireddy et al., 2020; Jhunjhunwala et al., 2023; Sun et al., 2024) were forced to restrict the inner stepsize η to O(1/(LH)), preventing recovery of the tight Local SGD rate; this paper's key technical departure is a regret-based analysis that works for all η, revealing the dual role of γ. The paper also provides the first convergence guarantee for an accelerated outer optimizer (Nesterov) paired with an unaccelerated inner optimizer, a combination used in practice by DiLoCo but previously unanalyzed, and the first high-probability data-dependent bound for Local SGD.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides explicit optimal (η*, γ*) pairs for Generalized Local SGD and shows that γ > 1 can be beneficial—counterintuitively, the outer learning rate should sometimes exceed 1 to compensate for an ill-tuned inner rate. This gives practitioners concrete guidance for tuning DiLoCo-style distributed training systems and explains why Nesterov acceleration in the outer loop outperforms local acceleration (FedAC) in communication-round dependence. Key limitations include the restriction to i.i.d. data and convex objectives in the main theorems; extending the analysis to heterogeneous data and nonconvex settings, as well as understanding adaptive outer optimizers under client failures, are identified as important open problems.
