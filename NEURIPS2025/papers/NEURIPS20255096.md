## Problem

Standard RL training of LLMs samples n>1 completions per task but optimizes pass@1 (expected single-sample reward), under-utilizing the sampling budget and limiting exploration on harder problems. While pass@k (expected maximum reward over k samples) better captures the collective utility of a sample set, prior work only provided gradient estimators for the special case k=n, leaving robust optimization for arbitrary k≤n unaddressed.

## Input / Output

The method takes a vector of n scalar rewards (one per sampled completion for a given task, which can be binary correctness or continuous scores like % unit tests passed) and a target k ≤ n, and produces a transformed reward vector of the same dimension n via a multivariate function R^n → R^n. Unlike per-sample reward shaping, the transformation is jointly applied across all n rewards in the batch, with each output element depending on all input rewards through binomial-coefficient-weighted combinations. The transformed vector is then plugged directly into any standard policy gradient algorithm (e.g., PPO) as the per-sample advantage signal, requiring no architectural changes.

## Method

PKPO derives novel low-variance unbiased estimators for pass@k and its gradient by analytically averaging over all C(n,k) subsets of size k from the n samples. For continuous rewards, the key trick is assuming w.l.o.g. that rewards are sorted, which yields a closed-form estimator with weights μ_i = C(i-1, k-1), and a gradient estimator where each sample's coefficient s_i is a weighted sum of all rewards via a structured upper-triangular matrix M with specific diagonal and off-diagonal binomial entries. Two variance-reducing baselines are provided: a leave-one-out baseline s^(loo) that subtracts the mean over subsets excluding each element, and a k-1 sized subset baseline s^(loo-1) that generalizes TZSM25 to n>k, both computable in O(k + n log n) time. The method enables annealing k during training (e.g., starting at k=8 for exploration then switching to k=1 for consolidation), achieving strong pass@1 alongside significant pass@k gains without the trade-off inherent in prior approaches that couple k to the batch size.

## Results

On the challenging ARC-AGI-1 benchmark, PKPO with k_opt=8 achieves a cumulative solve rate of 84.14% (GEMMA2-9B) and 88.89% (LLAMA3.1-8B), compared to only 12.00% and 22.00% respectively for standard pass@1 optimization (k_opt=1). On MATH (GEMMA2-9B), k_opt=8 reaches 48.37% pass@16 versus 42.59% for k_opt=1, while on Coding (GEMMA2-9B) it reaches 71.91% pass@16 versus 60.98%. The annealing strategy (k_opt=8 → k_opt=1) achieves strong pass@k gains without sacrificing pass@1, e.g. maintaining 20.69% pass@1 while reaching 48.37% pass@16 on MATH.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PKPO dominates all baselines across every benchmark and model size tested. On ARC-AGI-1, the most dramatic setting, PKPO (k_opt=8) achieves 84.14% cumulative solve rate on GEMMA2-9B versus 22.00% for [TZSM25] and 24.67% for EntropyReg — a 4x improvement over the closest baseline, while standard pass@1 optimization (k_opt=1) stalls at 12.00%. On MATH and Coding benchmarks, PKPO with k_opt=8 consistently outperforms [TZSM25] by 3–5 points on pass@16 (e.g., 48.37 vs 44.82 on MATH GEMMA2-9B; 71.91 vs 66.41 on Coding GEMMA2-9B), and outperforms EntropyReg by a similar margin. The [TZSM25] baseline, which corresponds to the special case n=k in PKPO's formulation, is consistently outperformed because it couples the minibatch size to k, incurring higher estimator variance. EntropyReg, despite a hyperparameter sweep over 5 values, is both harder to tune and significantly outperformed, and it sacrifices pass@1 (e.g., 4.00% vs 26.67% on ARC-AGI-1) while providing no mechanism to target a specific k_eval.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the pass@k metric and its unbiased estimator from Chen et al. (2021) to continuous rewards, and generalizes the concurrent work of Tang et al. (2025) — which optimized pass@k only in the special case n=k — to arbitrary k≤n. The theoretical machinery draws on U-statistics (Hoeffding, Kolmogorov) to derive low-variance unbiased gradient estimators, and the variance-reduction baselines (leave-one-out, k-1 subset baselines) follow the standard policy-gradient baselining tradition. Conceptually, the work sits at the intersection of inference-time search (Best-of-N, pass@k evaluation) and training-time policy optimization, bridging a gap where prior methods either optimized pass@1 during training or restricted to the n=k case.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: PKPO is a simple drop-in reward transformation (a few lines of code) that can be applied to any existing PPO/GRPO pipeline, and the k-annealing trick (start high, end at 1) improves both pass@1 and pass@k simultaneously without the trade-off inherent in prior methods. The finding that pass@1 optimization literally stalls on hard problems (ARC-AGI-1) while pass@k optimization unblocks learning suggests a fundamental limitation of single-sample reward optimization for exploration. Open questions include extension to more complex inference-time search algorithms (beam search, self-consistency), scaling to larger models and harder benchmarks, and whether the variance advantage persists at very large n.
