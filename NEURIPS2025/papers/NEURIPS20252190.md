## Problem

Multi-view Gaussian process latent variable models (MV-GPLVMs) suffer from two coupled limitations: stationary kernels cannot capture input-varying or time-varying correlations across views, and the resulting kernel matrices incur O(N³) inversion costs that preclude large-scale multi-view datasets. The existing non-stationary Bivariate Spectral Mixture (BSM) kernel relaxes stationarity but imposes an identical-variance constraint on its spectral components and admits no closed-form random Fourier feature approximation, leaving it both less expressive and computationally intractable for multi-view settings.

## Input / Output

The model consumes V views of N observations, where each view v is an N×M_v matrix (e.g., MNIST images paired with their digit labels as a second view, or the real and imaginary parts of 32-antenna MIMO channel vectors from QUADRIGA simulations). It outputs a shared N×D latent representation X, per-view kernel hyperparameters (Q mixture components each with means μ_q1, μ_q2, variance vectors σ_q1², σ_q2², correlation ρ_q, and weight α_q), and variational parameters (per-point means and covariances). The RFF approximation reduces per-iteration cost to O(NL²V), where L is the number of random features per component, making the method scalable to N=10,000 observations.

## Method

The paper establishes a Universal Bochner's Theorem that generalizes the classical spectral-kernel duality by relaxing the positive semi-definite constraint on the bivariate spectral density, enabling a Next-Gen Spectral Mixture (NG-SM) kernel whose components are symmetric bivariate Gaussians with independent variances for w₁ and w₂. A closed-form RFF feature map is derived for this kernel (Theorem 2), and an unbiased differential estimator is constructed by stacking per-component feature maps scaled by √α_q, which introduces differentiability with respect to mixture weights without requiring discrete sampling. A two-step reparameterization trick exploits the correlation structure between w₁ and w₂ to sample from each bivariate Gaussian in O(D) rather than O(8D³), and the full model is trained via sampling-based variational inference that jointly optimizes kernel hyperparameters and latent representations by maximizing an ELBO with Monte Carlo reconstruction and analytic KL regularization, using the Adam optimizer.

## Results

On five multi-view datasets (BRIDGES, CIFAR, MNIST, 20 Newsgroups, YALE), NG-MVLVM achieves the best or near-best classification accuracy using both KNN and SVM classifiers on the learned unified latent representations. Headline numbers include 85.31% KNN / 87.49% SVM on BRIDGES, 82.72% KNN / 60.14% SVM on MNIST, 100% on 20 Newsgroups, and 83.87% KNN / 64.81% SVM on YALE. The method also demonstrates superior computational scalability, with wall-time growing linearly in dataset size N (Figure 3), unlike MV-DGPLVM which is prohibitively expensive. On the wireless channel compression task, NG-MVLVM achieves the lowest reconstruction MSE and highest KNN accuracy across all latent dimensions tested.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NG-MVLVM outperforms the closest GP-based baseline, MV-ARFLVM [Li et al., ICML 2024], on every dataset for both KNN and SVM, with the largest gaps on YALE (83.87% vs. 78.86% KNN; 64.81% vs. 56.72% SVM) and BRIDGES (85.31% vs. 83.42% KNN). Against MV-DGPLVM [Sun et al., TPAMI 2020], the advantage is dramatic: on MNIST, NG-MVLVM achieves 82.72% KNN versus 26.83% for MV-DGPLVM, and on CIFAR 36.02% vs. 22.15%, while also being orders of magnitude faster to train. The VAE-based baselines (MVAE [Wu & Goodman, NeurIPS 2018] and MMVAE [Mao et al., ICCV 2023]) are consistently outperformed on KNN across all datasets, though MVAE edges out NG-MVLVM by a narrow margin on YALE SVM (65.13% vs. 64.81%) and MMVAE slightly leads on CIFAR SVM (34.73% vs. 32.47%). The non-stationary GP baseline MV-NGPLVM is dominated on nearly all metrics, confirming that the NG-SM kernel's expressiveness advantage over a single Gibbs kernel is substantial. Overall, NG-MVLVM achieves the best or tied-best result on 18 out of 20 KNN/SVM dataset combinations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MV-GPLVM framework (Li et al. 2017) by replacing the default stationary RBF kernel with a new non-stationary NG-SM kernel, whose design is enabled by a novel Universal Bochner's Theorem that relaxes the positive semi-definite constraint on the bivariate spectral density. It builds directly on the BSM kernel (Remes et al. 2017), which first introduced non-stationary spectral kernels via the generalized Bochner's theorem but was limited by an identical-variance constraint and the intractability of closed-form random Fourier features. The scalable inference strategy draws on the RFF-based variational framework of ARFLVM (Li et al. 2024, ICML), but introduces a two-step reparameterization trick and an unbiased differential RFF estimator specifically tailored to the bivariate Gaussian mixture structure of the NG-SM kernel.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses two long-standing bottlenecks in multi-view GP latent variable models—limited kernel expressiveness and cubic computational complexity—simultaneously, which prior methods handled only one at a time. The universal approximation guarantee of the NG-SM kernel (any continuous kernel can be approximated with enough mixture components) is a meaningful theoretical advance, and the wireless channel compression application demonstrates practical relevance beyond standard image/text benchmarks. Open questions include how the method scales when the number of views V or mixture components Q grows large, whether the variational approximation q(W)=p(W) is tight enough for highly non-Gaussian posteriors, and whether the GP-based approach can compete with increasingly powerful neural multi-view methods on very large-scale datasets.
