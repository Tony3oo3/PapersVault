## Problem

Prior ObjectNav approaches complete partial semantic maps using deterministic, discriminative models with a strict one-to-one mapping, which inherently limits generalization to unseen environments. The paper argues that semantic map completion is inherently uncertain—multiple plausible full scenes correspond to the same partial observation—and that existing indoor datasets are too small and homogeneous for generative models to learn the latent distribution without external knowledge.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254439/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each timestep the agent receives egocentric RGB-D images and its pose; these are back-projected into a registered 3D point cloud, jointly segmented with a sparse convolutional network, and projected to a top-down semantic map of shape (N_c+2)×h×w. The observed region is cropped to a fixed 256×256 patch with Gaussian noise (σ=0.01) injected into unobserved cells to form the source sample. The flow model outputs a complete semantic map, from which the argmax grid in the target-object channel becomes a long-term waypoint; a Fast Marching Method local planner then converts this into discrete navigation actions (move_forward, turn_left, turn_right, stop).

## Method

GOAL formulates semantic map completion as a flow-matching generative problem implemented on a DiT backbone, with three key design choices. First, LLM-derived spatial priors (object-pair distances and confidence scores obtained via chain-of-thought prompting) are converted into 2D isotropic Gaussian fields and added to the unobserved regions of the ground-truth target map, distilling commonsense co-occurrence knowledge into the flow model during training only—no LLM is needed at inference. Second, instead of the standard independent coupling (Gaussian source + cross-attention conditioning), the paper constructs a data-dependent coupling by directly interpolating between the noise-injected partial map and the LLM-enhanced full map, eliminating conditioning modules and reducing inference time from 17.1 ms to 12.4 ms. Third, multi-frame RGB-D observations are aggregated into a unified point cloud and segmented jointly with a Sparse UNet, capturing geometric and temporal consistency that per-frame 2D segmentation misses. Training uses the OT displacement interpolant with an MSE velocity loss, and sampling is performed via Euler integration over n steps.

## Results

On Gibson, GOAL achieves 83.5% SR, 44.2% SPL, and 0.83 DTS, outperforming the previous best T-Diff (79.6% SR, 44.9% SPL, 1.00 DTS) and SGM (78.0% SR, 44.0% SPL, 1.11 DTS). On MP3D, GOAL reaches 41.7% SR, 15.5% SPL, and 4.84 DTS, surpassing T-Diff (39.6% SR, 15.2% SPL, 5.16 DTS) and SGM (37.7% SR, 14.7% SPL, 4.93 DTS). In the cross-dataset transfer setting (trained on MP3D, tested on HM3D), GOAL achieves 48.8% SR and 23.1% SPL, exceeding all prior methods including those trained directly on HM3D or using LLMs at inference time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GOAL outperforms all 17 compared baselines on the primary metrics. On Gibson, GOAL improves SR by 3.9 points over T-Diff (83.5 vs. 79.6) and 5.5 points over SGM (83.5 vs. 78.0), while also achieving the best DTS (0.83 vs. 1.00 and 1.11). On MP3D, GOAL leads T-Diff by 2.1 SR points (41.7 vs. 39.6) and SGM by 4.0 SR points (41.7 vs. 37.7), though zero-shot LLM-based methods UniGoal (41.0 SR, 16.4 SPL) and SG-Nav (40.2 SR, 16.0 SPL) achieve slightly higher SPL than GOAL's 15.5, which the authors attribute to their adaptive waypoint-update policy limiting path correction. In the HM3D transfer setting, GOAL (48.8 SR, 23.1 SPL) surpasses VoroNav (42.0 SR, 26.0 SPL) and ESC (39.2 SR, 22.3 SPL) on SR, and outperforms PONI (41.8 SR, 20.1 SPL) by 7 SR points, demonstrating that the generative flow model with LLM-distilled priors generalizes better than both discriminative and LLM-at-inference approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reframes semantic map completion in ObjectNav from a deterministic prediction problem (as in L2M, SSC-Nav, SGM) to a probabilistic generative one, adopting Flow Matching with a DiT backbone. It departs from standard diffusion/flow conditioning (independent Gaussian source + cross-attention) by constructing data-dependent couplings that directly bridge partially observed maps to LLM-enriched targets, eliminating extra conditioning modules. The LLM knowledge distillation—encoding spatial co-occurrence priors as 2D Gaussian fields injected only during training—is a novel use of LLMs in embodied navigation that avoids inference-time API calls, contrasting with zero-shot LLM-based methods (L3MVN, SG-Nav, UniGoal) that require LLM access at test time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The strong cross-dataset transfer (MP3D→HM3D, outperforming methods trained directly on HM3D and those using LLMs at inference) suggests that generative modeling of scene semantics captures distributional structure rather than memorizing dataset-specific layouts, a property deterministic predictors lack. However, the approach is limited to a fixed set of object categories (no open-vocabulary support), relies on simulated rectangular visibility masks that diverge from real fan-shaped observations, and the SPL improvement is modest due to the adaptive waypoint policy. Open questions include whether more advanced flow-matching techniques (consistency models, time-shifted training) would close the remaining gap, and whether the LLM distillation strategy transfers to other embodied tasks beyond ObjectNav.
