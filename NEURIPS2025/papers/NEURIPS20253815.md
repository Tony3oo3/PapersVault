## Problem

Existing RL methods for LLM reasoning face a credit assignment dilemma: token-level approaches (PPO) require a critic model that produces unreliable value estimates due to limited per-prompt data, while trajectory-level approaches (GRPO) assign a single coarse advantage to all tokens, preventing the model from identifying which specific tokens drive success or failure. This paper addresses the gap by introducing segment-level advantage estimation at an intermediate granularity that is fine enough for precise credit assignment yet coarse enough to enable accurate Monte Carlo estimation without a critic.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt (e.g., a math word problem) and a policy LLM, generating a chain-of-thought response token-by-token under a sparse binary reward (1 if the final answer matches ground truth, 0 otherwise). The generated token sequence is partitioned into contiguous segments, and the trained policy model is the output. For short CoT (SPO-chain), segments are defined by accumulating a fixed number of low-probability 'cutpoint' tokens; for long CoT (SPO-tree), segments are fixed-length token blocks organized in a tree structure where sibling nodes share identical prompts and token counts.

## Method

SPO partitions the generated sequence into segments and estimates each segment's advantage as the difference in state values at its boundaries, computed via Monte Carlo sampling from the current policy rather than a learned critic. For short CoT (SPO-chain), an adaptive cutpoint-based partition places boundaries where token probabilities drop below a threshold, and N independent rollouts from each boundary state estimate the value. For long CoT (SPO-tree), trajectories are organized in a tree where each node extends its parent by M tokens; values are computed bottom-up from leaf rewards, and advantages are estimated relative to sibling nodes, reusing all samples for both value estimation and policy optimization. A probability-mask strategy further refines credit assignment by assigning segment advantages only to low-probability tokens (those below threshold ρ) rather than uniformly across all tokens in a segment, concentrating gradient signals on the tokens most likely to influence the reasoning trajectory.

## Results

On GSM8K (short CoT), SPO-chain (int5) achieves the highest test accuracy, outperforming PPO and GRPO by 6–12 percentage points. On MATH500 (long CoT), SPO-tree achieves 0.736 accuracy at 2K context and 0.828 at 4K context, surpassing GRPO (0.62 and 0.752) by 11.6 and 7.6 percentage points respectively. On AIME24, SPO-tree reaches 0.10 at 2K and 0.333 at 32K, matching or exceeding all baselines. The probability-mask strategy alone boosts GRPO accuracy from 45.7% to 53.6% on GSM8K, demonstrating its independent value.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPO-chain (int5) outperforms all six short-CoT baselines on GSM8K: PPO, GRPO, RLOO, DPO, RestEM, and VinePPO, with 6–12 pp gains over PPO and GRPO and higher accuracy than VinePPO while requiring less episode generation time. SPO-tree dominates GRPO on MATH500 at both 2K (0.736 vs. 0.620) and 4K (0.828 vs. 0.752) contexts, confirming that segment-level advantages are critical for long-CoT training. Against DeepScaleR (trained with 8K→24K context), SPO-tree wins decisively at 2K (0.736 vs. 0.538) and 4K (0.828 vs. 0.744) but loses at 32K (0.848 vs. 0.878), suggesting GRPO-based training may not optimize token efficiency under tight context budgets. SPO-tree also outperforms STILL-3 at 2K (0.736 vs. 0.662) and 4K (0.828 vs. 0.794), and marginally at 32K (0.848 vs. 0.846). On AIME24, SPO-tree matches GRPO at 4K and 32K while substantially outperforming it at 2K (0.100 vs. 0.033). The probability-mask technique, when applied to GRPO alone, improves GSM8K accuracy from 45.7% to 53.6%, indicating that selective token-level credit assignment is beneficial even without segment-level advantages.

## Relation to Prior Work

SPO positions itself as a generalization of VinePPO (which the authors explicitly note is a special case), extending step-level MC advantage estimation to an arbitrary segment-level granularity that does not require semantic completeness (e.g., line breaks). It departs from PPO by eliminating the critic model entirely and from GRPO by replacing the single trajectory-level advantage with multiple segment-level advantages estimated via Monte Carlo sampling. The tree-based advantage estimation in SPO-tree is a novel contribution over VinePPO's chain-based MC, reusing sampled trajectories for both value estimation and policy optimization to dramatically reduce sampling cost in long-CoT settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The segment-level framework offers a practical, modular middle ground for credit assignment in LLM RL that avoids both the instability of critic models and the imprecision of trajectory-level signals, with the probability-mask technique providing a simple, transferable improvement applicable even to existing methods like GRPO. Key limitations include evaluation only up to 4K context for long-CoT (due to compute constraints), focus primarily on mathematical reasoning, and absence of error bars. Open questions include whether the approach scales to much longer contexts (32K+), generalizes to code generation and RLHF, and whether off-policy variants can further improve the sample efficiency of tree-based sampling.
