## Problem

Prior work on length generalization in LLMs, including Rule-Following Fine-Tuning (RFFT), is restricted to single-task settings where models must be separately fine-tuned on task-specific rule-following data for each new task. This is impractical and fails to exploit shared structures across tasks. The paper asks whether rule-following capability can be acquired as a transferable meta-ability through multi-task training, enabling robust length generalization on unseen tasks with minimal adaptation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251411/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives an explicit rule (written as Python code or natural language instructions) alongside the problem statement, and optionally a one-shot exemplar showing a short rule-following trace. The output is a structured execution trace where the model must recite the relevant rule before each action, describe all variable states before and after modification, and iterate through the full loop until termination, finally producing the answer. This format enforces step-by-step rule adherence rather than allowing the model to shortcut to a direct answer.

## Method

Meta-RFFT is a two-stage supervised fine-tuning pipeline. In the RF-pretraining stage, the model is fine-tuned on 310k rule-following samples across 74 diverse tasks (from LeetCode, Big-Bench Hard, and Symbolic Reasoning benchmarks) at lengths 1–15, learning to follow explicit rules and maintain iterative state across many computational structures. In the downstream adaptation stage, the model is adapted to a target task via minimal fine-tuning on only 5k samples (lengths 1–5) or via 1-shot in-context prompting, then evaluated on out-of-distribution lengths up to 30. The key insight is that multi-task RF-pretraining teaches shared computational primitives—particularly correct loop maintenance (entering, iterating, and exiting loops at the right time)—which are the primary source of length generalization failure in single-task RFFT. This meta-ability transfers to unseen tasks and even to rules expressed in natural language rather than code, without requiring task-specific architectural modifications.

## Results

On 12 unseen downstream tasks (8 LeetCode + 4 NUPA), the 32B Meta-RFFT model achieves 98% average accuracy at length 30 (ACC_Len30) and sustains ≥90% accuracy up to length 30 (Max_Len_90% = 30.00), i.e., 6× the training length. The 7B Meta-RFFT model reaches 77% ACC_Len30 and Max_Len_90% of 21.17. In the 1-shot in-context learning setting, the 32B RF-pretrained model achieves Max_Len_90% of 28.5 without any parameter updates, surpassing even the fine-tuned vanilla RFFT model by 10.3 lengths.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Meta-RFFT (32B) decisively outperforms all baselines on both metrics. Against the strongest long-CoT models, it beats DeepSeek-R1-671B by 14 percentage points on ACC_Len30 (98% vs. 84%) and by over 10 lengths on Max_Len_90% (30.0 vs. 19.17), despite being 21× smaller in parameters. It similarly surpasses QwQ-32B (same parameter size) by 19 points on ACC_Len30 (98% vs. 79%). Compared to the single-task vanilla RFFT [19], Meta-RFFT improves ACC_Len30 from 67% to 98% and Max_Len_90% from 18.17 to 30.00 on the 32B model, confirming that multi-task RF-pretraining is essential for robust cross-task transfer. Against the scratchpad baseline [32], the gap is even larger (98% vs. 41% ACC_Len30), showing that merely providing intermediate computations without explicit rules is insufficient. Finally, on Llama-3.1-8B, Meta-RFFT (ACC_Len30 = 0.38, Max_Len_90% = 11.67) substantially outperforms the instruction-tuned Tulu3 model (0.16, 0.67), demonstrating that standard instruction-following training does not confer the strict rule-adherence needed for length generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends RFFT (Hu et al., ICML 2024), which demonstrated that explicitly providing rules and execution traces in the input enables single-task length generalization in post-trained LLMs. Meta-RFFT generalizes this from single-task to a two-stage multi-task pipeline: a large-scale RF-pretraining phase across 74 diverse tasks, followed by minimal downstream adaptation (fine-tuning on 5k samples or 1-shot prompting). It departs from earlier positional-encoding and data-format approaches (Zhou et al., Kazemnejad et al., Cho et al.) that were limited to small transformers trained from scratch on single symbolic tasks and incompatible with pretrained LLMs. The paper also positions against long-CoT reasoning models (DeepSeek-R1, QwQ), showing they still fail at length generalization despite their strong general reasoning capabilities.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework demonstrating that rule-following is a transferable meta-ability: a model pretrained on diverse rule-following tasks can generalize to unseen tasks with only one exemplar, eliminating the need for task-specific fine-tuning. The 86-task dataset significantly broadens length generalization research beyond the narrow addition/sorting tasks that dominated prior work. However, the scope remains limited to controlled algorithmic and symbolic tasks; the authors acknowledge that extending to complex real-world long-horizon reasoning (legal judgment, multi-step workflows) and defining 'length' in such settings remains open. The finding that shared computational primitives like loop maintenance—not task-specific patterns—drive cross-task transfer provides a useful mechanistic explanation for why multi-task pretraining helps.
