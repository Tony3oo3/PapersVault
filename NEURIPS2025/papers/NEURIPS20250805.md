## Problem

State-of-the-art RL policies, even when trained to convergence, hit a performance ceiling on complex multi-agent tasks (Dec-POMDPs) where combinatorial action spaces and partial observability make zero-shot inference insufficient. While inference-time search strategies are well-established in single-agent combinatorial optimization, their role in multi-agent RL has been almost entirely unexplored, and no systematic study has examined how these strategies scale with time and compute budgets.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250805/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Given a converged policy checkpoint (IPPO, MAPPO, or SABLE) and a new Dec-POMDP instance, the inference strategy operates under a hard wall-clock time limit T_max and a compute budget B_max specifying the number of parallel rollouts permitted. The environment's reward function remains accessible during inference to score each attempted solution, and the strategy returns the single best-scoring sequence of joint actions found within budget. All experiments use a fixed NVIDIA A100 GPU, with compute controlled by varying batch size rather than hardware, and 128 independent seeds per configuration for statistical robustness.

## Method

The paper formalizes a unified framework for four inference strategies adapted to the multi-agent setting: stochastic joint-action sampling, Simulation-Guided Beam Search (SGBS) with greedy rollouts for node evaluation, online fine-tuning via policy gradient updates on the fixed instance, and COMPASS, which pre-trains a latent-conditioned policy (adding <2% parameters) and searches the 16-dimensional latent space with CMA-ES at inference time. The key finding is that COMPASS dominates across all 17 tasks and budget settings, achieving up to 126% improvement over zero-shot SOTA (SABLE) with only 30 seconds of search, and exhibits near-linear scaling with compute capacity. The study comprises 60,928 evaluated episodes across 4 strategies × 7 compute budgets × 17 tasks × 128 seeds, making it the largest empirical investigation of inference strategies for complex RL to date.

## Results

Across 17 complex multi-agent RL tasks, inference-time search with a 30-second budget and 64 parallel solutions yields an average 45% improvement over zero-shot state-of-the-art (SABLE), with a maximum gain of 126%. SABLE + COMPASS achieves close to 100% win-rate in all tasks where this metric is available, while zero-shot SABLE stagnates around 60% normalized performance. The study comprises 60,928 evaluated episodes (4 strategies × 7 compute budgets × 17 tasks × 128 seeds) on a single NVIDIA A100 GPU. On the single-agent Craftax-1B benchmark, a 30-second stochastic sampling search boosts performance from 18.8% to 25.8% of maximum score (a 37% relative gain).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three MARL base policies—SABLE (Mahjoub et al., 2025), IPPO (de Witt et al., 2020), and MAPPO (Yu et al., 2022)—each evaluated with four inference strategies (stochastic sampling, SGBS, online fine-tuning, COMPASS). SABLE consistently outperforms IPPO and MAPPO both zero-shot and with inference-time search, confirming its SOTA status even under the new evaluation paradigm. Among inference strategies, COMPASS dominates across nearly all tasks and budget settings, achieving close to 100% win-rate when paired with SABLE, while online fine-tuning shows high variance and is often outperformed by simple stochastic sampling under tight budgets. SGBS is competitive in the low-budget regime (below 100 seconds, fewer than 10 parallel attempts) but fails on Connector tasks. The key finding is that the choice of inference strategy matters as much as the base policy: SABLE + COMPASS outperforms SABLE zero-shot by 45% on average, and the gap widens with task complexity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies and extends four inference-strategy paradigms previously studied in isolation for single-agent combinatorial optimization—stochastic sampling, simulation-guided beam search (Choo et al., 2022), online fine-tuning (Bello et al., 2017; Hottung et al., 2022), and latent-space diversity search via COMPASS (Chalumeau et al., 2023b)—to the multi-agent Dec-POMDP setting. It departs from prior CO inference work in three key ways: (i) evaluating on genuinely hard, unsaturated multi-agent tasks rather than near-solved single-agent benchmarks, (ii) varying both time and compute budgets systematically rather than fixing a single budget, and (iii) drawing conceptual parallels to the LLM test-time compute scaling literature (Snell et al., 2025; Wu et al., 2025) to frame inference as a first-class scaling axis. The base policies (SABLE, IPPO, MAPPO) are taken from the MARL literature, and the paper's contribution is orthogonal to training-time improvements, focusing instead on what happens after convergence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical implication is significant: for any RL deployment where a few seconds to minutes of inference time are available, the choice of inference strategy can yield 45–126% performance gains over zero-shot execution, making inference a core component of the solution pipeline rather than an optional refinement. The contour maps and scaling curves provide practitioners with actionable guidance for allocating time and compute budgets. Key limitations include the focus on multi-agent tasks (single-agent evidence is limited to one Craftax result), the absence of out-of-distribution evaluation, and the fact that all tasks provide an exact or near-exact reward signal for scoring attempts. Open questions include how to best combine complementary inference paradigms, whether these scaling properties hold under distribution shift, and how to extend the analysis to settings where the reward is noisy or only partially observable.
