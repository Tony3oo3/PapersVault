## Problem

Existing missing-modality recovery methods for multimodal emotion recognition in conversations (MERC) rely on training with complete multimodal data, causing them to fail catastrophically under fixed missing protocol where a modality is entirely absent from a client's local dataset. This creates an inherent contradiction: generative recovery models need raw data of the missing modality to learn its distribution, yet that data is precisely what is unavailable. Additionally, centralized approaches that pool data across clients raise privacy concerns in real-world deployments.

## Input / Output

The method consumes pre-extracted utterance-level features from available modalities—DeBERTa-large (1024-d) for text, MA-Net (1024-d) for vision, and wav2vec-large (512-d) for audio—organized as conversation sequences of C utterances with speaker identity metadata. Under the fixed missing protocol, each of 3 federated clients retains at least one of the three modalities, yielding six possible absence patterns. The system produces two outputs: (1) recovered latent feature vectors for missing modalities (not raw data, for ethical reasons), and (2) emotion class predictions (4-class or 6-class for IEMOCAP; binary positive/negative for CMU-MOSI/MOSEI).

## Method

FedDISC integrates federated learning with conditional diffusion-based modality recovery through three coupled mechanisms. First, the DISC-Diffusion module constructs a conditional embedding by concatenating outputs from a Dialogue Graph Network (single-layer R-GCN over speaker and context graphs with a fixed window w=2) and a Semantic Conditioning Network (cross-attention between audio and visual features followed by self-attention), which then guides a U-Net noise-prediction network via cross-attention layers using classifier-free guidance. Second, the federated protocol has each client train a modality-specific diffusion model on its locally available modality; the server performs FedAvg-style aggregation per modality and broadcasts the global models to clients lacking that modality, enabling zero-shot cross-client recovery without local training data for the missing modality. Third, the Alternating Frozen Strategy (AFS) cycles between a recovery-training stage (classifier frozen, recovery module active) and a classifier-optimization stage (recovery frozen, classifier active) every E communication rounds to prevent gradient interference between generative and classification objectives. The paper further provides convergence proofs under PL smoothness and bounded SGD variance assumptions, establishing linear convergence rates for the alternating optimization.

## Results

On IEMOCAP4 (fixed missing protocol), FedDISC(P) achieves the highest ACC/WAF1 across all six incomplete modality conditions, outperforming the best SOTA baseline by 1.3%–7.5% in accuracy (e.g., 68.0/68.2 with only language available vs. SDR-GNN's 66.2/66.2; 78.0/78.1 with language+acoustic vs. CIF-MMIN's 74.3/75.6). On IEMOCAP6, FedDISC(P) slightly trails SDR-GNN by 0.9%–3.2% in a few settings, though it operates with only 1/3 of the data per federated client. Under the random missing protocol on CMU-MOSI and CMU-MOSEI, FedDISC(P) achieves the best average ACC/WAF1 (81.2/81.2 and 84.4/84.5 respectively) and shows the smallest performance degradation as missing rate increases from 0.0 to 0.7 (12.3% and 6.1% decline vs. 15.4%–27.7% and 7.4%–22.9% for baselines).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedDISC(P) decisively outperforms DiCMoR [18] across all conditions, with DiCMoR collapsing to 21.9–47.1% ACC on IEMOCAP4 (vs. FedDISC's 61–78%), confirming that flow-based generative recovery fails catastrophically under fixed missing protocol. Against IMDer [8] (diffusion-based but non-federated), FedDISC gains 5–12% ACC on IEMOCAP4, demonstrating the benefit of federated aggregation over single-client training. SDR-GNN [23] is the strongest graph-based baseline; FedDISC(P) beats it by 1.3–7.5% on IEMOCAP4 but trails by 0.9–3.2% on IEMOCAP6 in {l,v} and {l,a} settings, though FedDISC operates with only 1/3 of the data per client. CIF-MMIN [38] is competitive in bimodal settings (e.g., 74.3/75.6 on IEMOCAP4 {l,a}) but FedDISC(P) still leads by 3.7% ACC. GCNet [12] (latent-space recovery) is consistently outperformed by 2–12% ACC, and the non-recovery DCCAE [39] baseline lags by 10–20% on CMU-MOSI/MOSEI, confirming that explicit generative recovery is necessary under high missing rates.

## Relation to Prior Work

FedDISC sits at the intersection of incomplete multimodal emotion recognition (MERC) and federated learning, building on two distinct lineages: (1) explicit modality recovery via generative models (IMDer, DiCMoR, CIF-MMIN) and latent-space recovery (GCNet, SDR-GNN), and (2) multimodal federated learning (MFCPL, Yin et al.). The key departure is addressing the 'fixed missing protocol'—where a client entirely lacks a modality—by federated aggregation of modality-specific diffusion models, which prior recovery methods cannot handle because they assume each client trains on complete multimodal data. The DISC-Diffusion conditioning (Dialogue Graph Network + Semantic Conditioning Network) extends standard conditional diffusion by injecting structured conversational and cross-modal semantic priors, while the Alternating Frozen Strategy is a novel federated optimization protocol not present in prior FL-for-recovery work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap: real-world emotion recognition deployments (healthcare, customer service) involve heterogeneous sensor availability across clients, and existing recovery methods fail when a modality is entirely absent locally. The federated diffusion approach enables zero-shot cross-client modality recovery while preserving privacy, and the AFS strategy offers a reusable recipe for multi-objective federated training. However, the evaluation is limited to 3 clients and 3 modalities, assumes synchronous FL, and the diffusion sampling cost (2.13 min/epoch for DDPM) remains a deployment bottleneck. Open questions include scalability to many clients, asynchronous settings, and whether lighter-weight generative backbones (consistency models, GANs) can match the recovery quality at lower cost.
