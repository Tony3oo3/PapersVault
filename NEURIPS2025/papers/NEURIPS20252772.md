## Problem

Convex polyhedral classifiers are well-structured but too restrictive since their decision boundaries must be convex, while general piecewise-linear (ReLU) classifiers are too flexible, yielding disconnected parameter spaces and local minima even on separable data. This paper introduces starshaped polyhedral sets supported on a fixed simplicial fan as a middle ground that allows non-convex boundaries while retaining tractable geometric structure, and provides a complete combinatorial and geometric analysis of the resulting loss landscape.

## Input / Output

The method takes a binary labeled dataset X = {(x^(i), y^(i))} in R^d × {0,1} and a fixed complete simplicial polyhedral fan Δ with n generators, and produces a parameter vector a ∈ R^n_{>0} defining the star-shaped classifier c_a: R^d → {0,1}. The evaluation matrix A_X (mapping data points to fan coordinates) is sparse with at most d non-zero entries per row due to the simplicial structure. The classifier assigns label 0 to points inside the star (where f_a^Δ(x) ≤ 1) and label 1 to points outside, with the star being the union of simplices with vertices {0, (1/a_{i_1})v_{i_1}, ..., (1/a_{i_k})v_{i_k}} over each cone of the fan.

## Method

The classifier is defined by a continuous piecewise-linear function f_a^Δ(x) = ⟨[x]^Δ, a⟩ that is linear on each cone of a fixed simplicial fan, where [x]^Δ denotes the non-negative coordinates of x in the fan. The paper analyzes two loss functions: the 0/1-loss, whose sublevel sets decompose into chambers of a hyperplane arrangement (the data arrangement) and are star-convex when data is separable, and a log-likelihood loss built from an exponential CDF, which is concave and thus optimizable in polynomial time via convex optimization. Key theoretical results include: the VC dimension equals n (the number of fan rays), the log-likelihood optimum is unique under mild rank conditions on the data matrix, and the optimal parameter a*(λ) traces a straight line toward the origin as the rate parameter λ varies. When translations are allowed, the sublevel sets become semialgebraic (degree ≤ 2) and can be disconnected, with VC dimension bounded by O(d² log(d) k log(k)) for k maximal cones.

## Results

The paper is primarily theoretical, establishing that the VC dimension of starshaped polyhedral classifiers equals n (the number of rays in the fan), that the log-likelihood loss is concave (enabling polynomial-time optimization via Algorithm 1), and that sublevel sets of the 0/1-loss are star-convex for separable data. In a small-scale experiment on 500 synthetic 2D points with an 8-ray fan, Algorithm 1 achieves 0.852 accuracy, outperforming all standard baselines. The optimal rate parameter was found to be λ ≈ 0.83.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares Algorithm 1 against five standard binary classifiers on a single synthetic 2D dataset. SVMs with linear, polynomial, and sigmoid kernels, as well as logistic regression, all fail to separate the star-shaped boundary, collapsing to a single-class prediction and achieving only 0.718 (or 0.534 for sigmoid) accuracy. The SVM with an RBF kernel (0.780) and a small ReLU neural network with two hidden layers (0.824) capture some of the non-convex structure but still underperform Algorithm 1 (0.852) by 7.2 and 2.8 percentage points, respectively. The authors note that the experiments are limited to one small-scale 2D dataset and that the theoretical contributions (VC dimension bounds, concavity, star-convexity of sublevel sets) are the primary focus rather than empirical superiority.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalizes the line of work on convex polyhedral classifiers (Astorino & Gaudioso 2002, Manwani & Sastry 2010, Kantchelian et al. 2014) and the kite-based conic classifiers of Cevikalp & Triggs (2017) by allowing the decision boundary to be a possibly nonconvex starshaped polyhedral set supported on a fixed simplicial fan. It also positions itself against general ReLU networks (Brandenburg et al. 2024), arguing that the fixed-fan structure retains nonconvex expressivity while avoiding the disconnectedness and local-minima pathologies of unconstrained piecewise-linear parameter spaces. The log-likelihood analysis draws on the polytope reconstruction framework of Dostert & Jochemko (2023), and the VC-dimension bound for the translated case leverages Kupavskii's (2020) result on simplices.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a rare complete geometric and combinatorial characterization of a nonconvex classifier family: the VC dimension equals the number of fan rays, the log-likelihood is concave (hence polynomial-time optimizable), and sublevel sets of the 0/1-loss are star-convex for separable data. These results offer a principled middle ground between overly restrictive convex polyhedral separators and the combinatorially wild parameter spaces of ReLU networks. Key open questions remain: how to select the underlying fan, translation vector, and rate parameter in a task-adaptive way, and whether the framework scales beyond the small 2D experiments presented. The purely theoretical nature and the absence of a systematic fan-selection procedure limit immediate practical adoption.
