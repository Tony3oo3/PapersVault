## Problem

State-of-the-art neural scene representations (NeRF, ZipNeRF, 3DGS) produce high-quality view synthesis on curated benchmarks but degrade severely on real-world captures with per-image exposure variation, such as indoor-outdoor transitions or rooms with windows. Existing remedies either ignore exposure (causing floating artifacts), use GLO embeddings (producing over- or underexposed regions), or require HDR captures and professional 2D tonemapping software that is not 3D-consistent. The paper addresses the gap of learning a 3D-consistent, well-exposed scene appearance directly from standard RGB images with varying exposure, without post-processing or multi-exposure captures.

## Input / Output

The method takes as input a set of RGB images each annotated with its capture exposure time Δt (e.g., shutter speed), with no HDR data, target appearance, or additional labels required. It produces two outputs: a ZipNeRF-based neural scene representation that renders novel views, and a 3D neural exposure field that assigns an optimal exposure value to every point in space. Unusually, exposure is treated as a spatially-varying 3D property rather than a per-image or per-pixel quantity, and at test time the scene representation is conditioned on the learned 3D exposure field rather than any input exposure value.

## Method

The core contribution is a neural exposure field e_φ (a 4-layer MLP with 128 hidden units) that predicts an optimal exposure per 3D point, trained jointly end-to-end with a modified ZipNeRF scene representation. The key architectural insight is a latent exposure conditioning mechanism: the log of the exposure is added to the MLP bottleneck vector (which predicts log radiance) rather than to the radiance output as in HDRNeRF, yielding improved expressiveness and more stable optimization. The exposure field is trained by selectively backpropagating the input exposure only where rendered pixels are well-exposed (a Gaussian weight centered at 0.5) and well-saturated (RGB channel standard deviation), so the model only trusts pixels that are already correctly exposed in some view. A 3D smoothness regularizer penalizes exposure differences between nearby sample points to ensure spatial coherence. At test time, the scene representation is conditioned on the neural exposure field's per-point predictions, producing 3D-consistent, well-exposed novel views without any 2D tonemapping or reference image.

## Results

On the HDRNeRF dataset, NExF achieves 42.54 PSNR / 0.988 SSIM / 0.014 LPIPS on in-distribution exposures and 38.36 PSNR / 0.984 SSIM / 0.021 LPIPS on out-of-distribution exposures, outperforming the prior best RGB-only method HDRNeRF by 3.5 PSNR (ID) and 0.8 PSNR (OOD) while training in 11 minutes (vs. 542 min for HDRNeRF). On the Eyeful Tower room-level scenes, NExF reaches 26.48 PSNR / 0.876 SSIM / 0.234 LPIPS, improving over the best baseline (HDRNeRF*) by +3.7 PSNR and +0.04 SSIM, corresponding to a >55% MSE reduction. The method also trains 3× faster than the fastest competing baseline (HDR-GS at 34 min).

## Baselines & Comparisons

NExF outperforms all six baselines on the HDRNeRF exposure-reconstruction task. Against HDRNeRF [15] (the closest RGB-only competitor), it gains +3.5 PSNR on in-distribution and +0.8 PSNR on out-of-distribution exposures while training 49× faster (11 vs. 542 min). Against HDR-GS [6], which has access to full HDR captures during training, NExF still wins by +1.4 PSNR (ID) and +2.0 PSNR (OOD) in PSNR and by +0.006 SSIM (ID), though HDR-GS achieves a slightly lower LPIPS (0.011 vs. 0.014 ID). On the Eyeful Tower room-level scenes (Table 3), NExF (26.48 PSNR) beats HDRNeRF* (22.82), GLO/NeRF-W (21.20), Affine GLO/ZipNeRF (20.13), and Ignore Exposure (16.72) by 3.7, 5.3, 6.4, and 9.8 PSNR respectively. The gap over GLO-based methods is particularly large, confirming that per-view latent embeddings alone cannot handle strong exposure variation without producing over- or underexposed regions. NeRF [26] and 3DGS [18] without any exposure handling perform poorly (13.97 and 19.46 PSNR), underscoring the necessity of explicit exposure modeling for high-dynamic-range scenes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on ZipNeRF's grid-based radiance field backbone and HDRNeRF's log-domain exposure conditioning, but departs from both in key ways. Rather than applying 2D tonemapping (as in HDRNeRF/HDR-GS) or using per-image GLO embeddings (as in NeRF-W/ZipNeRF) to explain away exposure variation, it introduces a 3D neural exposure field that predicts an optimal exposure per spatial point, conditioned jointly with the scene representation via a novel latent-space mechanism (adding log exposure to the MLP bottleneck rather than to radiance). This shifts the exposure problem from a per-image 2D calibration to a 3D-consistent spatial prediction, eliminating the need for HDR captures, professional tonemapping software, or reference target appearances.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap that limits the deployment of neural view synthesis on real-world captures: strong per-image exposure variation (e.g., indoor/outdoor transitions) causes severe quality degradation in standard methods. By producing 3D-consistent, well-exposed novel views from RGB+exposure metadata alone, the approach removes dependencies on HDR pipelines and artist-created references, making high-quality view synthesis accessible for consumer captures. Limitations remain for extreme lighting, semi-transparency, and strong reflections, and the method assumes exposure metadata is available. Open questions include whether the 3D exposure field formulation extends to dynamic (4D) scenes or to settings where exposure metadata is unavailable.
