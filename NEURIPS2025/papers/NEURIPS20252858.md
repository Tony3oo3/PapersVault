## Problem

Existing multi-step RAG systems rely on manually designed prompts or heuristics to orchestrate retrieval, which are labor-intensive and do not scale to complex questions. Labeling reasoning-with-search trajectories for supervised fine-tuning is impractical due to cost and the open-ended nature of multi-hop reasoning. The paper addresses how to train LLMs to autonomously decide when and how to search as part of a reasoning chain, without any supervised data on intermediate reasoning steps.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252858/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a multi-hop question and interacts with an open-ended Wikipedia search environment (E5-base-v2 retriever over a Dec. 2018 Wikipedia dump, top-5 results per query). The output is an interleaved reasoning chain where the model generates thinking tokens, issues search queries enclosed in <search></search> tags, receives retrieval results in <result></result> tags, and continues reasoning until producing a final answer in \boxed{} format. Retrieval results are injected by the environment rather than generated by the model, and the rollout terminates at an end-of-sentence token.

## Method

ReSearch trains LLMs from scratch using Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), where each rollout is an iterative loop of text-based thinking, search queries, and environment-returned retrieval results. A key design choice is retrieval result masking: tokens inside <result></result> tags are excluded from the policy loss so the model is not biased toward memorizing retrieved content. The reward is a simple rule-based function combining F1 score on the boxed final answer with a format-correctness bonus (0.1 if format is valid but answer is wrong, 0 otherwise), requiring no learned reward model. Training uses only the MuSiQue training set (19,938 samples) for 2 epochs on Qwen2.5-7B/32B (base and instruct variants), built on the verl RL framework with FlashRAG as the retrieval backend. The paper shows that this minimal setup elicits emergent reflection and self-correction behaviors without any explicit prompting or heuristic design.

## Results

ReSearch achieves average improvements of 15.81% (EM) and 17.56% (LLM-as-a-Judge) over the best baseline for Qwen2.5-7B, and 14.82% (EM) and 15.46% (LJ) for Qwen2.5-32B, across four multi-hop QA benchmarks. The strongest configuration, ReSearch-Qwen-32B-Instruct, reaches 46.73% EM on HotpotQA, 44.90% EM on 2WikiMultiHopQA, 26.40% EM on MuSiQue, and 56.80% EM on Bamboogle. Notably, the model is trained only on the MuSiQue training set (19,938 samples) yet generalizes to all four benchmarks, with the largest absolute gains on MuSiQue (e.g., 22.30% EM vs. 8.69% for Iter-RetGen at 7B scale).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReSearch outperforms both multi-step RAG baselines (Iter-RetGen and IRCoT) on every benchmark and model size, with no losses. At the 7B scale, ReSearch-7B-Instruct beats Iter-RetGen by 9.16 EM on HotpotQA (43.52 vs. 34.36), 19.67 EM on 2Wiki (47.59 vs. 27.92), 13.61 EM on MuSiQue (22.30 vs. 8.69), and 20.80 EM on Bamboogle (42.40 vs. 21.60). IRCoT is more competitive on Bamboogle (24.80 EM) but lags significantly on 2Wiki (21.57 EM) and MuSiQue (6.99 EM). At the 32B scale, the gap widens further: ReSearch-32B-Instruct surpasses Iter-RetGen by 6.92 EM on HotpotQA, 11.26 EM on 2Wiki, 13.91 EM on MuSiQue, and 27.20 EM on Bamboogle. The naive baselines (No RAG and Naive RAG) are consistently the weakest, confirming that multi-step reasoning with search is essential for these tasks. Training from instruction-tuned models yields consistent gains over base models across all benchmarks, suggesting that instruction-following priors complement RL-based reasoning acquisition.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RL-for-reasoning paradigm established by OpenAI-o1 and DeepSeek-R1 by incorporating external search operations directly into the reasoning chain, using GRPO (from DeepSeekMath) as the optimization algorithm. It departs from prior multi-step RAG methods like IRCoT and Iter-RetGen, which rely on manually designed prompt heuristics to interleave retrieval and generation, by instead learning the search-reasoning policy end-to-end via reinforcement learning without any supervised reasoning-step annotations. The key conceptual shift is treating search queries and retrieval results as first-class tokens in the policy's rollout, with retrieval-result masking to prevent the policy from being biased toward environment-generated text.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstration that a simple F1-based reward suffices to elicit multi-step reasoning-with-search—including emergent reflection and self-correction—without any supervised trajectory data offers a scalable path to multi-step RAG that avoids the costly annotation of reasoning steps. However, the framework is currently limited to concise, objectively verifiable answers and a single Wikipedia knowledge base, leaving open whether the approach generalizes to longer-form responses, domain-specific corpora, or more complex tool-use scenarios. The generalization across four benchmarks from a single training set is encouraging but the baselines are prompt-based rather than RL-trained, so the true gap versus other RL-based multi-step RAG methods remains untested.
