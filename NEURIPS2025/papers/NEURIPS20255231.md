## Problem

Large VLMs (72B+) achieve state-of-the-art vision-language performance but are impractical for resource-constrained deployment, while smaller models (1B–8B) remain significantly weaker. Existing remedies—scaling, architectural modifications, or verbose 'think-answer' RL pipelines—either increase inference latency or restrict applicability to domains with predefined answer-parsing metrics. The paper asks whether a lightweight VLM can be trained to match or exceed large-model performance without architectural changes, without explicit reasoning chains, and across open-ended visual question answering tasks.

## Input / Output

The student VLM receives an RGB image and a text question and produces a free-form text response, with no intermediate 'think' tokens, preserving the original inference speed of the base model. The discriminator and LLM-as-a-Judge operate purely on natural-language text: the discriminator consumes a formatted prompt containing the question and a candidate response (Fig. 3) and outputs a single scalar via a linear head replacing the standard language head, while the judge (Qwen2.5-32B) receives the question, ground-truth answer, and generated response (Fig. 4) and returns a binary correctness verdict. Because all signals are text-based, RIL is agnostic to the image-embedding strategy or language tokenizer used by either student or teacher VLMs.

## Method

RIL unifies Dr.GRPO (an unbiased-advantage variant of GRPO) with a GAIL-style adversarial loop. A discriminator—initialized with the same architecture and parameters as the student VLM to mitigate the balance problem—is pre-trained to output near-zero for teacher responses and near-one for student responses, then continuously updated during RIL iterations. The student's total reward is the sum of two binary signals: a similarity reward (discriminator score < 0.5, indicating teacher-like style) and an answer reward (LLM-as-a-Judge binary correctness check), which together prevent the student from mimicking fluent but factually wrong outputs. During the GRPO update, the 2G responses per question comprise G student-generated and G pre-cached teacher-generated samples, so the student receives direct exemplars of correct answers even when it cannot produce them independently. Using multiple large teacher VLMs (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL-72B and InternVL3-78B simultaneously) enriches response diversity and strengthens both the discriminator and the GRPO advantage estimates, yielding consistent gains over single-teacher or RL-only baselines across 14 vision-language benchmarks.

## Results

On 14 vision-language benchmarks, Qwen2.5-VL-RIL-7B (using both Qwen2.5-VL-72B and InternVL3-78B as teachers) achieves 79.7 on MathVista, 80.4 on MM-Vet, 70.0 on BLINK, and 95.6 on ChartQA, surpassing GPT-4o (63.8, 69.1, 68.0, 85.7 respectively) on these metrics. InternVL3-RIL-8B reaches 77.8 on MathVista, 80.1 on MM-Vet, 74.8 on MMStar, and 88.1 on MMBench, outperforming GPT-4o and matching or exceeding several 72B+ open-source models. Compared to its own 72B teacher (Qwen2.5-VL-72B), the 7B RIL model gains +5.5 on MathVista and +3.5 on MM-Vet while remaining 10x smaller. The RIL-trained models also outperform same-size baselines (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL-7B baseline) by 4–12 points across most benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against closed-source models, Qwen2.5-VL-RIL-7B outperforms GPT-4o by +15.9 on MathVista (79.7 vs 63.8), +11.3 on MM-Vet (80.4 vs 69.1), +2.0 on BLINK (70.0 vs 68.0), and +10.0 on ChartQA (95.6 vs 85.7), while trailing on MMMU (65.7 vs 69.1) and MMMU-Pro (48.5 vs 51.9). InternVL3-RIL-8B similarly beats GPT-4o on MathVista (+14.0), MM-Vet (+11.0), and SEED (+3.4), but falls short on MMMU (68.6 vs 69.1) and RWQA (73.7 vs 75.4). Compared to same-family 72B teachers, the 7B RIL model surpasses Qwen2.5-VL-72B on MathVista (+5.5), MM-Vet (+3.5), BLINK (+5.6), and ChartQA (+6.1), while the 8B RIL model exceeds InternVL3-78B on MM-Vet (+1.2), MMStar (+2.3), and SEED (+1.8). Against same-size open-source baselines, RIL gains are substantial: Qwen2.5-VL-RIL-7B improves over Qwen2.5-VL-7B by +11.9 on MathVista, +8.6 on MM-Vet, and +13.6 on BLINK; InternVL3-RIL-8B improves over InternVL3-8B by +6.2 on MathVista, +1.6 on MM-Vet, and +7.2 on BLINK. The RIL models also outperform larger open-source models like Ovis2-16B (73.7 MathVista) and LLaVA-OV-72B (67.5 MathVista) on MathVista, while remaining competitive on most other benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

RIL unifies two previously separate training paradigms for VLMs: the GRPO/Dr.GRPO reinforcement learning framework (from DeepSeekMath and its R1-style successors) and the GAIL adversarial imitation learning framework (from robotics). It departs from the 'think-answer' RL paradigm (DeepSeek-R1, Vision-R1, R1-V) by eliminating intermediate reasoning tokens, and from conventional knowledge distillation (MiniLLM, DistilLLM, LLaVA-KD) by operating on natural-language text responses with a trainable discriminator rather than static KL-divergence on high-dimensional logits. The use of LLM-as-a-Judge for reward computation also distinguishes RIL from prior RL-for-VLM work that relies on rigid answer parsing, enabling application to open-ended visual questions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RIL provides a practical, architecture-agnostic recipe for closing the performance gap between 7–8B and 72–78B VLMs without increasing inference latency, which is critical for deployment on mobile and edge devices. The finding that multiple diverse teacher VLMs substantially outperform a single teacher, and that RIL synergizes with prior distillation-based training, opens avenues for iterative multi-stage training pipelines. Key limitations include the high training compute cost (256 A100s for discriminator pre-training, 8 A100s for the RIL loop), the current restriction to post-instruction-tuning alignment (not the initial visual instruction tuning phase), and the reliance on a large LLM-as-a-Judge (Qwen2.5-32B) for reward computation, which itself introduces cost and potential bias.
