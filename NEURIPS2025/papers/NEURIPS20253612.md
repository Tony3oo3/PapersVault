## Problem

Existing deep multi-modal clustering methods fuse all modalities at various levels, which causes information conflicts between modalities and forces high-quality modalities to align with noisy low-quality ones, degrading clustering performance. This paper asks whether one can instead identify a dominant (highest-quality) modality and selectively enhance it with diversity information from the remaining modalities, rather than performing indiscriminate fusion.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253612/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes pre-extracted multi-modal feature vectors (N samples across M modalities, each of dimension d_m) and a target number of clusters K as input. Before training, the dominant modality is selected by computing the average silhouette coefficient of K-means clustering on each modality's raw features. The output is a discrete cluster assignment matrix Q of shape N×K, produced by the DDC clustering module operating on the enhanced dominant modality representation.

## Method

DDMC replaces multi-modal fusion with dominant modality enhancement. Each modality is encoded by an independent variational encoder: the dominant modality yields a single feature D^dm, while each non-dominant modality yields two branches—diversity features Z^m and consistency features C^m—sharing encoder parameters. Feature-level diversity learning minimizes mutual information compression losses (Information Bottleneck) and applies contrastive alignment between D^dm and C^m to decouple shared from modality-specific information. Cluster-level diversity learning maximizes mutual information between cluster assignments of non-dominant and dominant modalities while enforcing balanced cluster sizes via a Sinkhorn-Knopp-based Uniform Distribution Constraint. The enhanced representation is formed by concatenating D^dm with all Z^m vectors, and final clustering is performed by Deep Divergence-based Clustering (DDC), which optimizes intra-cluster compression, inter-cluster separation, and orthogonality simultaneously.

## Results

DDMC achieves the best ACC and NMI across all five multi-modal datasets: 76.7/68.8 on Caltech-3V, 90.3/82.7 on Caltech-4V, 60.9/40.9 on ESP-Game, 58.7/36.5 on Flickr, and 49.5/28.3 on IAPR. Compared to the strongest baseline (SCMVC), DDMC gains an average of 3.88% in ACC and 3.52% in NMI across the five datasets. The largest margin appears on Caltech-4V, where DDMC improves ACC by 5.9 points and NMI by 7.4 points over SCMVC.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DDMC outperforms all 18 baselines on every dataset for both ACC and NMI. The strongest competitor is SCMVC (TMM'24), which leads on Caltech-3V (75.9 ACC) and Caltech-4V (84.4 ACC) but is surpassed by DDMC by 0.8 and 5.9 ACC points respectively; on ESP-Game, SMCMB (TBD'23) is the closest rival at 54.9 ACC, yet DDMC still leads by 6.0 points. Among deep multi-modal baselines, SEM (NeurIPS'23) achieves the best Caltech-4V NMI (75.3) but trails DDMC by 7.4 points, while MFLVC (CVPR'22) and SSLNMVC (TMM'25) remain competitive on IAPR (47.3 and 46.4 ACC) but fall 2.2–2.2 points short of DDMC. Traditional methods (CoregMVSC, RMKMC, SwMC, ONMSC) and single-modal baselines (KM, Ncuts, AvKM, AvNcuts) are consistently 10–40 points behind DDMC, confirming the advantage of the dominant-modality enhancement strategy over naive fusion or single-view clustering. Notably, DDMC's advantage is largest on Caltech-4V (the dataset with the most modalities), suggesting the method scales well with increasing modality count.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant fusion-based paradigm in deep multi-modal clustering (feature-level, decision-level, and mixed-level fusion) by proposing 'dominant modality enhancement instead of fusion.' It builds on DDC [28] as the clustering backbone, Information Bottleneck theory [24,25] for feature-level diversity extraction, and contrastive learning to decouple consistency from diversity in non-dominant modalities. The conceptual lineage draws an analogy to human perception (vision as dominant, other senses as supplementary), but the technical contribution is a two-level diversity learning framework (feature-level via mutual information + contrastive loss, cluster-level via UDC + mutual information) that extracts only the information in non-dominant modalities that is *not* already present in the dominant one.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper challenges a near-universal assumption in multi-modal clustering that all modalities must be fused, showing that selectively enhancing the highest-quality modality with diversity information from others can outperform fusion by 2–6% ACC over the best competitors and ~11% over a direct fusion ablation on ESP-Game. However, the method requires all modalities to be present and performs poorly on incomplete data, and the dominant-modality selection via silhouette coefficient is acknowledged as potentially suboptimal. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to high-dimensional deep features (beyond the hand-crafted descriptors used here), how to handle missing or highly imbalanced modalities, and whether the SI-based selection can be replaced by learned or adaptive criteria.
