## Problem

Existing multimodal molecular representation methods overlook fine-grained hierarchical taxonomic annotations (e.g., LOTUS Tree, MeSH) that capture multi-level functional semantics, rely on pairwise alignment anchored to a single modality that fails to model higher-order interdependencies, and neglect local correspondences between molecular substructures and their textual descriptions. These gaps limit the expressivity of learned representations for molecular property prediction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255801/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes three modalities per molecule: a SMILES string, a natural language functional description, and a Hierarchical Taxonomic Annotation (HTA) synthesized by GPT-4o from up to 32 PubChem classification systems. For local alignment, RDKit extracts functional groups (from a curated set of 85) whose embeddings are pooled and matched against corresponding text spans. The output is a 512-dimensional L2-normalized molecular embedding in a shared latent space, used downstream for property prediction via a linear probe.

## Method

TRIDENT jointly encodes SMILES (frozen MoLFormer), text descriptions, and HTA (shared frozen SciBERT or MolT5) through trainable 3-layer MLP projection heads into a common 512-dim space. Global alignment uses a volume-based contrastive loss (adapted from GRAM) that minimizes the parallelotope volume spanned by the three unit-normalized embeddings, with bidirectional retrieval (molecule→text+HTA and text+HTA→molecule) to enforce triadic consistency. A novel local alignment module applies InfoNCE contrastive loss between max-pooled functional group embeddings and their textual counterparts, capturing substructure-to-phrase correspondences. A momentum-based mechanism (β=0.9) dynamically reweights the global and local losses via exponential moving average, directing more capacity toward whichever alignment component currently has higher loss. Training freezes all encoder parameters and optimizes only projection layers on 47,269 curated triplets over 60 epochs.

## Results

On the MoleculeNet classification benchmark (8 tasks), TRIDENT(M-M) achieves a mean ROC-AUC of 78.46%, outperforming the strongest baseline Atomas (77.01%) by 1.45 points and MolFM (74.64%) by 3.82 points. On the TDC small-dataset benchmark, TRIDENT(M-S) reaches 95.08% AUC on DILI and TRIDENT(M-M) reaches 87.07% AUC on Carcinogens, both setting new state-of-the-art results. On MoleculeNet regression tasks, TRIDENT(M-M) achieves the best RMSE on ESOL (0.72) and FreeSolv (1.42), and matches the best on Lipophilicity (0.60). Across all 18 evaluated tasks (11 classification + 3 regression + 4 TDC), TRIDENT achieves the top or second-best result on the majority.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TRIDENT(M-M) outperforms all 13 baselines on the MoleculeNet average ROC-AUC (78.46%), with the closest competitor being Atomas (77.01%, +1.45 gap) and MolFM (74.64%, +3.82 gap). On individual MoleculeNet tasks, TRIDENT(M-M) wins on BBBP, Tox21, ToxCast, MUV, and HIV, while TRIDENT(M-S) additionally wins on ClinTox (95.75% vs. Atomas 93.16%) and Bace (84.19% vs. MolFM 83.90%). On the TDC small-dataset benchmark, TRIDENT(M-S) achieves 95.08% AUC on DILI, surpassing MoleculeSTM (91.20%) and MolXPT (91.67%) by over 3 points, and TRIDENT(M-M) leads on Carcinogens (87.07% AUC) and Skin Reaction (72.00% AUC). On regression tasks, TRIDENT(M-M) achieves the best RMSE on ESOL (0.72 vs. MolXPT 0.75) and FreeSolv (1.42 vs. Uni-Mol 1.48), and ties Uni-Mol on Lipophilicity (0.60). The largest margins are seen over single-modal baselines (MOLFORMER, KV-PLM, MegaMolBART) where gaps exceed 6–10 points, confirming the benefit of the tri-modal HTA input and volume-based alignment over SMILES-only or bi-modal approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

TRIDENT extends the GRAM volume-based contrastive framework [5]—originally designed for audio-video-text alignment—into the molecular domain, where modalities are structurally heterogeneous and include hierarchical taxonomic labels. It builds on the multimodal molecular representation line (MoleculeSTM, MoMu, MolFM, Atomas) but departs from the dominant pairwise, single-modality-anchored alignment paradigm by introducing a third modality (Hierarchical Taxonomic Annotations across 32 classification systems) and a geometry-aware triadic volume loss. The local functional-group alignment module draws on RDKit fragment extraction and InfoNCE-style contrastive objectives, while the momentum-based dynamic weighting is a novel integration mechanism not present in prior molecule-text models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The curated 47,269-triplet dataset spanning 32 taxonomic systems (LOTUS, MeSH, etc.) is a reusable resource that could benefit the broader molecular ML community beyond TRIDENT itself. The finding that hierarchical taxonomic annotations complement (rather than subsume) traditional functional descriptions suggests that multi-perspective, structured semantic enrichment is a promising direction for molecular representation learning. Key limitations include the reliance on GPT-4o for HTA synthesis (raising reproducibility and cost concerns), the exclusion of target/metabolite information that is critical for toxicity prediction, and the fact that all pre-training data derives from PubChem, potentially limiting generalization to novel chemical space.
