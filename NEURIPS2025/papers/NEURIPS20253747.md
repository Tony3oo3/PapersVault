## Problem

Existing gap-dependent regret bounds for episodic MDPs either use coarse variance quantities (H·Q*, which can reach H³) or suffer from poor horizon dependence (H³–H⁶ in the leading terms). The paper asks: what is the tightest regret bound that simultaneously exploits both variance structure and suboptimality gaps, achieving a 'best-of-three-worlds' guarantee that dominates variance-only, gap-only, and minimax bounds asymptotically?

## Input / Output

The analysis applies to the MVP algorithm operating on a finite-horizon tabular MDP with S states, A actions, horizon H, transition kernel P, reward distributions R (with bounded total reward ≤ H per trajectory), and initial distribution μ, over K episodes with confidence parameter δ. The output is a high-probability regret upper bound whose leading terms scale with (H² ∧ Var_max^c)/Δ_h(s,a) for each suboptimal (h,s,a) tuple and (H² ∧ Var_max^c)|Z_opt|/Δ_min for optimal tuples, where Var_max^c is the maximum conditional total variance. A matching lower bound is also established via a Bernoulli-bandit reduction.

## Method

The paper introduces a new variance metric, the maximum conditional total variance Var_max^c, which conditions the expected cumulative variance on trajectories visiting a specific (s,h) pair—capturing the randomness an algorithm encounters when learning any particular state-step. The core analytical technique is a novel weighted-sum argument over suboptimality gaps: by choosing weights w_h(s,a) = Var_h^*(s,a) and summing the per-step gap-surplus inequality across all episodes and steps, the authors derive an inequality of the form Σ u·n ≲ W·Σ v·√n, where W = H²ι ∧ Var_max^c. Applying Cauchy-Schwarz to this inequality yields the regret bound with H² dependence, avoiding the complex clipping-and-recursion machinery of prior work. A refined clipping scheme for optimal actions further tightens the Δ_min-dependent term to worst-case O(H²). The lower bound construction reduces each layer's decision at a low-frequency state to a Bernoulli bandit, proving that Var_max^c (not the unconditional Var_max) is necessary even when the latter is O(1).

## Results

The paper proves that MVP achieves a gap-dependent regret of Õ((Σ_{(h,s,a)∈Z_sub} (H² log K ∧ Var_max^c)/Δ_h(s,a) + (H² ∧ Var_max^c)|Z_opt|/Δ_min + SAH⁴(S∨H)) log K), where Var_max^c is a novel maximum conditional total variance. A matching lower bound of Ω(Σ_{(h,s,a)∈Z_sub} (H² ∧ Var_max^c)/Δ_h(s,a) · log K) is established, confirming tightness modulo log factors. The worst-case H-dependence in the gap-dependent terms is H², improving upon H³–H⁶ in all prior gap-dependent bounds, and the variance dependence uses Var_max^c ≤ HQ* rather than the coarser HQ* used previously.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MVP strictly improves upon all four compared baselines on both the H-dependence and the variance-dependence in the gap-dependent terms. Compared to StrongEuler (Simchowitz & Jamieson, 2019), MVP reduces the worst-case H-factor from H³ to H² and replaces the coarse HQ* variance with the tighter H² ∧ Var_max^c. Against Q-learning/UCB-H (Yang et al., 2021) and AMB (Xu et al., 2021), MVP eliminates the H⁶ and H⁵ worst-case factors entirely and introduces variance-awareness that those methods lack. Versus UCB-Advantage and Q-EarlySettled-Advantage (Zheng et al., 2024), which also use HQ* with an H² factor, MVP's H² ∧ Var_max^c term is strictly tighter since Var_max^c ≤ HQ*, and the per-gap summation structure (Σ 1/Δ_h) replaces the coarser SA/Δ_min aggregation. The matching lower bound confirms that the H² ∧ Var_max^c dependence cannot be improved and that the conditional variance Var_max^c (not the unconditional Var_max) is necessary for gap-dependent analysis.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two lines of work on problem-dependent regret in episodic MDPs: gap-dependent bounds (Simchowitz and Jamieson 2019, Yang et al. 2021, Xu et al. 2021, Zheng et al. 2024) and variance-dependent bounds (Zanette and Brunskill 2019, Zhou et al. 2023, Zhang et al. 2024). It analyzes the MVP algorithm from Zhang et al. [2024]—already known to be minimax-optimal and variance-aware—and derives a gap-dependent regret that simultaneously improves on both the variance quantity (replacing the coarse H·Q* with a new maximum conditional total variance) and the horizon dependence (achieving H² worst-case versus H³–H⁶ in prior gap-dependent results). The key technical departure is a novel weighted-sum analysis of suboptimality gaps that avoids the clipping-and-recursion machinery of Simchowitz and Jamieson, and a lower bound construction via Bernoulli bandits that proves the conditional variance is irreducible.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that the maximum conditional total variance (conditioned on visiting a specific state at a specific step) is fundamentally necessary for gap-dependent regret, while the unconditional total variance that suffices for minimax bounds is provably insufficient—a clean separation that clarifies the structure of problem-dependent RL. The H² worst-case horizon dependence matches the known lower bound, making the leading term tight up to log factors. However, the Δ_min-dependent term for optimal actions remains unmatched by the lower bound, and the analysis is restricted to tabular MDPs; extending the conditional-variance framework to linear or general function-approximation settings is an open direction the authors identify.
