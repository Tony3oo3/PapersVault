## Problem

While LVLMs have shown promising performance in video anomaly detection, it remains unclear whether their predictions are grounded in scene-aware contextual reasoning or driven by superficial visual-textual co-occurrence shortcuts learned during pretraining. This paper reveals a systematic hallucination phenomenon: LVLMs incorrectly flag normal scenes as anomalous when high-frequency objects (e.g., fire, gun, knife) appear in semantically benign contexts, and this shortcut reliance persists across architectures and model scales.

## Input / Output

The model takes a video clip (sequence of T frames) and a natural-language anomaly query as input. During inference, 8 frames are uniformly sampled per segment and each is scored via a law-enforcement-style prompt (rating 0–1 for suspiciousness); sparse frame scores are then densified using temporal attention weights from the decoding process to produce a continuous per-frame anomaly score sequence. The same framework also supports open-ended anomaly captioning at clip, event, and video granularity.

## Method

VAD-DPO reformulates video anomaly detection as a pairwise preference optimization problem, training the model to prefer semantically correct anomaly judgments over co-occurrence-driven shortcuts. The key mechanism is constructing counter-example preference pairs: visually similar video clips where the same object or interaction (e.g., a person hitting another) appears in both a normal context (boxing ring) and an anomalous one (street alley), with only the latter labeled as anomalous. The training objective combines a standard DPO contrastive loss with an anchored term (weighted by γ) that preserves the model's preference for semantically valid clips, preventing over-suppression of legitimate anomaly cues. Built on Qwen2-VL (2B) and trained for one epoch on 1,000 curated preference pairs from HIVAU-70K, MSAD, NWPU Campus, and Wan2.1-synthesized clips, VAD-DPO reduces average false-positive rates on shortcut probes from over 75% to 13.1% while achieving state-of-the-art frame-level AUC across six benchmarks.

## Results

VAD-DPO (2B parameters) achieves the highest frame-level AUC across all five main VAD benchmarks: 87.2% on ShanghaiTech, 86.2% on UCF-Crime, 88.5% on XD-Violence, 79.1% on NWPU Campus, and 85.4% on MSAD. The most notable gain is on NWPU Campus (+8.3% over Holmes-VAU), a dataset rich in scene-dependent anomalies, demonstrating that preference optimization effectively promotes scene-aware reasoning. On the hallucination probe sets, VAD-DPO reduces the average false positive rate from 75.0% (Holmes-VAU) to 13.1% and the false negative rate from 80.0% to 12.5%, an absolute reduction exceeding 60 percentage points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VAD-DPO (2B) outperforms all 11 baselines on every reported metric. Against the closest same-scale competitor Holmes-VAU (2B), VAD-DPO gains +2.0 to +8.3 AUC points across the five VAD benchmarks, with the largest margin on NWPU Campus (79.1 vs. 70.8), confirming superior scene-aware reasoning. VAD-DPO also surpasses substantially larger models: it beats Qwen2.5-VL-7B by 7.8 AUC on ShanghaiTech and LLaVA-1.5-13B by 10.9 AUC on the same dataset, despite using roughly 4–6× fewer parameters. On the hallucination probe sets (Table 5), VAD-DPO's average FPR of 13.1% is dramatically lower than all baselines (75.0–85.0%), and its FNR of 12.5% is the lowest reported (next best: LLaVA-NeXT-Video-34B at 57.5%). For reasoning quality on HIVAU-70K (Table A.1), VAD-DPO_Holmes achieves the highest BLEU (0.923 clip-level), CIDEr (0.688 clip-level), and ROUGE (0.561 clip-level) scores, outperforming Holmes-VAU and all 7B+ LVLMs. Generalization to four video understanding benchmarks (MVBench, PerceptionTest, EgoSchema, Video MME) shows consistent small gains (+0.2 to +0.8) over the base Qwen2-VL-2B, indicating that preference tuning does not degrade general video comprehension.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) from LLM alignment and the anchored multimodal variant (MDPO) for video anomaly detection, introducing a VAD-specific twist: preference pairs are constructed from visually similar but semantically contrasting clips to explicitly break co-occurrence shortcuts. It departs from prior LVLM-based VAD work (LAVAD, A-Guardian, AnomalyRuler, Hawk, Holmes-VAU), which either operates training-free or uses standard instruction tuning, by identifying and targeting a specific failure mode—object-label co-occurrence bias—that none of these methods address. The diagnostic probe methodology draws on co-occurrence analysis from NLP and object hallucination studies in VLMs, but applies it to the VAD-specific setting where the model correctly detects objects yet misclassifies their semantic significance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that even 32B-parameter LVLMs exhibit >50% false positive rates on carefully constructed normal scenes containing high-bias objects (fire, gun, blood) is a significant warning for anyone deploying LVLM-based VAD in safety-critical surveillance. VAD-DPO provides a practical, low-cost training recipe (1,000 preference pairs, ~16 GPU-hours on 2×A100) that reduces hallucination FPR from ~75% to ~13%, making it accessible to practitioners. Key open questions include whether the approach scales to 7B+ models, whether GRPO-style group optimization can further improve scene-aware reasoning, and how to address residual fairness risks from shortcut-driven bias in surveillance deployments.
