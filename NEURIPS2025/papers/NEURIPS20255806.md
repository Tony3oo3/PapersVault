## Problem

LLMs face two critical limitations in multi-document QA: (1) attention dilution weakens long-range dependencies between key paragraphs and the question/target, and (2) the 'lost-in-the-middle' phenomenon causes degraded performance when critical information appears mid-sequence. Existing remedies either truncate global token interactions (e.g., StreamingLLM, H2O) or require task-specific fine-tuning (e.g., LongAlign), leaving no universal, training-free solution that preserves full context while sharpening attention.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a prompt consisting of C concatenated paragraphs followed by a question, processed through a standard Transformer LLM with greedy decoding (max 32 new tokens). DSAS operates entirely within the forward pass by modifying attention score matrices in-place at selected layers (default: final 50%), requiring no architectural changes, additional parameters, or external retrieval. The output is a short factual answer evaluated by F1-score against a reference.

## Method

DSAS is a training-free, two-stage attention optimization plug-in. Stage 1 (Contextual Gate Weighting, CGW) computes a combined information-flow score per paragraph by averaging column-wise Top-K attention values from both the question and target positions, then applies Z-normalization with sigmoid scaling and a position-aware weight derived from the Gaussian CDF over token indices to counteract the U-shaped attention bias; the resulting gate weight multiplicatively scales attention scores between question/target tokens and each paragraph. Stage 2 (Reciprocal Attention Suppression, RAS) classifies paragraphs as key or irrelevant via a mean-threshold on gate weights, then bidirectionally suppresses attention scores between key and irrelevant paragraphs by scaling with the minimum of their weights, breaking cross-paragraph interference. The entire mechanism is inserted into the standard multi-head attention computation (Eq. 3) at the final 50% of layers, with hyperparameters K=10, α=1, β=0.7 fixed across all models and benchmarks.

## Results

DSAS achieves an average F1-score improvement of 4.2% over vanilla LLMs on both Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct and Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct across four Multi-doc QA benchmarks (HotpotQA, 2WikiMultiHopQA, MuSiQue, and LongBench). Gains range from +0.9% (Llama-3.2-3B) to +4.2% (Llama-3.1-8B, Qwen2.5-14B), with the largest per-task improvements reaching +6.6% on LongBench-MuSiQue for Llama-3.1-8B and +6.5% for Qwen2.5-14B. The method is training-free and requires no architectural modifications, operating as a plug-in on the final 50% of Transformer layers.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole external baseline is PINE (Wang et al., ICLR 2025), which re-assigns input paragraph positions to eliminate position bias. DSAS outperforms PINE on the average F1-score across all seven tested LLMs, with margins ranging from +0.6% (Llama-3.2-3B) to +1.9% (Qwen2.5-7B). The gap is most pronounced on medium-sized models: DSAS leads PINE by +1.4% on Llama-3.1-8B (45.5 vs. 44.1) and +1.3% on Qwen2.5-14B (51.7 vs. 50.4). On individual tasks, DSAS achieves the largest per-task advantage over PINE on LongBench-MuSiQue (+1.4% for Llama-3.1-8B, +2.6% for Qwen2.5-14B), suggesting its attention-sharpening mechanism is particularly effective on complex multi-hop reasoning. Notably, PINE occasionally degrades below vanilla (e.g., DeepSeek-R1-8B: 29.2 vs. 29.5 average), whereas DSAS never underperforms the vanilla baseline on any model or benchmark.

## Relation to Prior Work

DSAS carves new ground at the intersection of attention-flow interpretability (Simonyan et al. 2014; Wang et al. 2023) and long-context attention optimization. It departs from prior attention-pruning methods (StreamingLLM, LM-Infinite, H2O) that sacrifice global token interactions, and from position-reordering approaches like PINE (Wang et al. 2025) that alter input structure, by instead modifying attention scores in-place within the existing context window—requiring no architectural changes, no reordering, and no training. The position-aware weighting component draws on the U-shaped attention bias analysis of Hsieh et al. (2024), while the two-stage information flow analysis (paragraph→question, paragraph→target) extends the 'label words as anchors' perspective of Wang et al. (2023) to the multi-document setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DSAS offers a practical, training-free mechanism that practitioners can immediately apply to existing LLMs without retraining, which is valuable given the cost of fine-tuning large models. However, the improvements (0.9–4.2% F1) are modest, and the method's reliance on paragraph-level segmentation limits its direct applicability to tasks without natural paragraph boundaries (though Appendix F shows some generalization via fixed-length chunking). Open questions include whether the approach integrates cleanly with retrieval-augmented generation pipelines, how it behaves under adversarial or noisy document inputs, and whether the linear-time overhead becomes prohibitive at 128K+ token scales where the quadratic attention cost is already the bottleneck.
