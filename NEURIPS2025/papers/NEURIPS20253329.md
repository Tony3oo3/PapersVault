## Problem

Generative data augmentation (GDA) methods that condition synthesis on both image and text prompts struggle to balance fidelity and diversity: image embeddings used to guide generation become entangled with irrelevant visual details (backgrounds, environmental context), creating conflicts with text prompts that intend to modify those elements. Existing approaches either overfit to instance-level details, limiting diversity, or fail to preserve the class-discriminative concept, especially for fine-grained or abstract categories.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253329/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a real image from a fine-grained category (image prompt) and an LLM-generated text description of that class (text prompt, produced by Claude-3-Sonnet for inference or BLIP-2/MiniGPT-4 for training) and produces synthetic augmented images at 512×512 resolution (stored at 256×256). Fine-grained class names are randomly replaced with meta-class names (e.g., 'bulldog' → 'dog') to encourage robustness. The synthetic pool is approximately 5× the original training set size, with at least 250 samples per class under few-shot and long-tail settings.

## Method

The core contribution is a two-stage pipeline: (1) a Salient Concept-Aware (SCA) embedding model built on DINOv2-Base, trained with an ArcFace-style additive angular margin loss that enforces intra-class compactness and inter-class separation, thereby decoupling the salient concept from irrelevant visual context; (2) fine-tuning of SDXL where the SCA embedding is projected into the text feature space via a 4-block cross-attention projector and injected into every U-Net cross-attention layer alongside text features, with LoRA applied to both the text branch (Q/K/V) and all self-attention layers for domain adaptation. Multi-modal classifier-free guidance uses the average training embedding as the class-conditional null for image prompts rather than zero vectors. The key insight is that the angular margin in the embedding loss prevents the generative model from encoding confounding background or environmental attributes, allowing faithful concept preservation while still responding to diverse text edit instructions.

## Results

On conventional fine-grained classification across six FGVC datasets, the method achieves an average Top-1 accuracy of 92.97% (RN50) and 93.74% (ViT-B/16), outperforming the best baseline (Diff-Mix) by 0.73% and 0.55% respectively. Under long-tail settings (IN-LT and iNat), the improvement is dramatically larger: 6.48% and 6.52% overall, with tail-category accuracy jumping from 39.82% to 69.93% on IN-LT (+25.30%) and from 59.25% to 73.20% on iNat (+11.88%). In few-shot (10-shot) settings, the method gains 1.85% over the best baseline on ViT-B/16, and in OOD evaluation on Waterbird it outperforms peers by 5.68%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all five baselines across every dataset and evaluation setting. In conventional classification, Diff-Mix is the strongest competitor (92.06% avg on RN50, 93.19% on ViT-B/16), but the SCA method surpasses it by 0.91% and 0.55% respectively, with the largest per-dataset gains on CUB (+2.18% RN50) and Aircrafts (+1.19% RN50). DA-Fusion and Real-Guidance underperform even the vanilla baseline on several datasets (e.g., DA-Fusion drops to 87.96% on Aircrafts vs. 89.14% vanilla), confirming that naive diffusion-based editing without concept-aware embeddings can be counterproductive. CutMix and Mixup provide modest gains (0.3–0.7%) but are consistently outperformed by the proposed method. The advantage is most pronounced under long-tail and OOD settings, where the SCA method's ability to generate diverse yet concept-faithful samples for underrepresented classes yields 6.5% and 5.68% improvements over the best GDA baseline, respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the IP-Adapter/SuTI paradigm of using a pre-trained image embedding model to condition a diffusion model alongside text, but departs from prior GDA methods (DA-Fusion, Diff-Mix, DiffuseMix) that rely on per-subject optimization (DreamBooth, Textual Inversion) or SDEdit-based editing. The key conceptual departure is training a domain-specific salient concept-aware (SCA) embedding model with an ArcFace-style angular margin loss, rather than naively using off-the-shelf features from DINOv2 or CLIP, to disentangle the target concept from irrelevant background attributes. This is the first application of the dual-embedding personalized generation framework specifically to GDA without subject-specific fine-tuning, positioning the work at the intersection of metric learning and diffusion-based data augmentation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 6.5% long-tail improvement and 5.7% OOD improvement demonstrate that better concept isolation in the image embedding is a practical lever for robustness in data-scarce and imbalanced settings, which are common in real-world deployment. A notable limitation is that the synthesis model must be trained per dataset (up to ~3 days for 2.7M images), and the approach is optimized for object-centric FGVC tasks rather than complex scene understanding or text-rich tasks. Open questions include whether the SCA embedding strategy transfers across domains without retraining, whether it composes with emerging flow-matching or autoregressive generators, and whether the per-dataset training requirement can be relaxed through meta-learning or larger-scale pretraining.
