## Problem

While prior work has identified Fourier-based solutions and learning dynamics (e.g., grokking) for modular addition in neural networks, no systematic framework existed to explain how global optimal solutions relate to partial solutions or why gradient descent selects specific solution structures over others. This paper asks whether global solutions can be algebraically constructed from partial solutions that only satisfy subsets of the loss, and which solutions the training dynamics prefer.

## Input / Output

The network takes a pair of one-hot encoded elements (g₁, g₂) from a finite Abelian group G of size d and predicts the one-hot representation of their group product g₁·g₂. Weights are parameterized in the Fourier (character) basis of G, and the L2 loss applies a zero-mean projection P₁⊥ to both the network output and the target, removing the constant bias component. The quadratic activation σ(x)=x² is essential, as it makes the loss analytically expressible as a function of the Fourier-space weight coefficients.

## Method

The paper proves that the weight space of 2-layer networks across all widths forms a commutative semi-ring under concatenation (addition) and Khatri-Rao product (multiplication), and that the L2 loss decomposes into sum potentials (SPs) that are ring homomorphisms. This algebraic structure enables the CoGS framework: partial solutions satisfying only subsets of SP constraints can be composed into global solutions via ring operations, yielding explicit constructions of order-6 Fourier solutions, order-4/6 mixed solutions, and a d²-order perfect memorization solution. The paper further shows that gradient dynamics with weight decay favors lower-order solutions (an Occam's Razor effect) because algebraically connected solutions are topologically connected via zero-loss paths, and that infinite-width overparameterization asymptotically decouples SP dynamics, explaining why overparameterization improves performance.

## Results

The paper's headline empirical result is that approximately 95% of order-4 and order-6 solutions obtained by gradient descent (with weight decay 5×10⁻⁵, q=512 hidden nodes) can be exactly factorized into the CoGS theoretical constructions (2×2 and 2×3 components), with a factorization error of only ~0.04 (compared to solution norm ~1). CoGS predicts ~98% of the structure of empirical solutions across d ∈ {23, 71, 127}. For d=127, 82.96% of factorable solutions are order-4 (matching the mixed z_{F4/6} construction), and the number of order-6 solutions in the form z_{ν=i} * z_{syn,αβ} is (d−1)/2 · 2.25% ≈ 1.26, coinciding with the theoretical prediction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not benchmark against competing training methods; instead, its central empirical claim is that its algebraic constructions (CoGS) exactly reproduce what gradient descent finds. Compared to Gromov (2023), who constructed analytic Fourier solutions under an infinite-width assumption, CoGS provides a finite-width construction (order-6 per frequency) that is more concise and provably matches empirical solutions. Compared to Morwani et al. (2023), who used a max-margin framework with L_{2,3} regularization, CoGS works directly with L₂ loss and additionally characterizes the semi-ring algebraic structure of the weight space and training dynamics. The key finding is that gradient descent with weight decay strongly favors the lower-order CoGS constructions (order-4/6) over the high-order perfect memorization solution (order d²), consistent with Theorem 5 (Occam's Razor). No alternative method is shown to outperform or underperform CoGS, since the comparison is between theory and empirics rather than between competing algorithms.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of analytic solution construction for modular addition (Gromov, 2023) and group-theoretic analysis of algebraic tasks (Morwani et al., 2023) by discovering a semi-ring algebraic structure in the weight space of 2-layer networks across varying widths. Unlike Gromov's infinite-width Fourier constructions or Morwani's max-margin framework with L2,3 regularization, CoGS works with standard L2 loss and finite width, showing that the loss decomposes into sum potentials that are ring homomorphisms, enabling algebraic composition of partial solutions into global ones. The paper also departs from expressibility results (Li et al., 2024; Liu et al., 2022) by not merely constructing weights but characterizing the full algebraic structure and analyzing which solutions gradient dynamics actually selects.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The CoGS framework provides a principled, algebraic alternative to gradient descent for constructing global solutions to reasoning tasks, suggesting that decomposing the loss into sum potentials and composing low-order solutions could be more efficient and scalable than training overparameterized networks. The finding that gradient descent with weight decay preferentially selects low-order (simpler) solutions over high-order memorization solutions offers a mechanistic explanation for why networks learn Fourier features rather than memorizing. However, the framework is currently restricted to 2-layer networks with quadratic activation on Abelian group tasks with L2 loss, and extending it to deeper architectures, other activations, or non-Abelian groups remains open. The connection to grokking dynamics and the potential for algebraic training algorithms are promising but unexplored directions.
