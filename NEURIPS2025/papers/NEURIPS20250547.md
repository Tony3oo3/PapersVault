## Problem

In performative prediction, forecasts influence the outcomes they target, so the target distribution is not fixed and correct forecasts may not exist (self-defeating prophecies). Even when correct forecasts do exist, the paper proves that classical proper scoring rules (Brier, log-score, energy score) generally fail to elicit them, and parameter estimation in performative settings (Perdomo et al., 2020) lacks guarantees of stability and optimality.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a conditional forecast F(Y|A) (a mapping from discrete covariate values a to probability distributions over Y), observed (a, y) pairs drawn from P_M(A,Y|do(F)), and a causal model M specifying how F affects A and Y. It produces a scalar proper score, an unbiased estimate of the performative divergence D_pc(F,M) (requiring at least two observations per covariate value n_a ≥ 2), and a performatively stable parameter θ obtained by minimizing the decoupled performative risk. Unlike classical scoring, the reference distribution is the forecast-induced P_M(Y|A=a, do(F)) rather than a fixed target, making the score depend on the forecaster's own report.

## Method

The paper first shows that conditioning on covariates A that d-separate the forecast F from the target Y renders the target forecast-invariant (P_M(Y|A,do(F)) = P_M(Y|A)), guaranteeing a unique correct forecast (Theorem 1). It then proves a general impossibility result: no non-constant classical scoring rule is observationally proper in performative settings with full-support or deterministic action mechanisms (Theorem 2). Two solutions are provided: (i) in decision-theoretic settings, the combined score S(F,a,y) = U(a_F, y) + Δ·S'(F(Y|A=a_F), y) is observationally strictly proper and incentive-compatible when the utility gap exceeds Δ (Theorem 4); (ii) scoring with unbiased estimators of the performative divergence D_pc(F,M) = ∫[S(P_M(Y|A=a,do(F)),y) − S(F(Y|A=a),y)] P_M(da,dy|do(F)) is proper for any sample size (Theorem 5, Corollary 1), with explicit unbiased estimators given for the Brier and energy scores (Lemma 1). For parameter estimation, minimizing the decoupled performative risk R_D^d(θ, θ_t) over a strictly proper divergence yields a correct forecast that is simultaneously performatively stable and performatively optimal (Theorem 6), resolving the open issues in Perdomo et al. (2020).

## Results

The paper's main contributions are theoretical: (i) Theorem 1 establishes that forecast-invariance of Y|A is equivalent to d-separation Y ⊥_G^d F | A, guaranteeing existence of a unique correct conditional forecast; (ii) Theorem 2 proves an impossibility result showing that no non-constant proper scoring rule exists in general performative settings (even under forecast-invariance); (iii) Theorem 4 shows that adding a suitably scaled strictly proper scoring rule to the utility score yields an observationally strictly proper and incentive-compatible scoring rule; (iv) Theorem 5 proves that the performative divergence (based on unbiased estimates) is observationally strictly proper without requiring forecast-invariance; (v) Theorem 6 demonstrates that scoring-rule-based parameter estimation yields performatively stable and performatively optimal parameters, resolving the issues raised by Perdomo et al. (2020). Appendix G provides a brief empirical validation on synthetic data comparing unbiased divergence estimators against biased plugin estimators and the IPW score.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary empirical comparison (Appendix G) is against the inverse probability weighting (IPW) score of Chen et al. (2011). The divergence-based scoring method is shown to be strictly proper under weaker conditions: it does not require the principal to know or unbiasedly estimate P_M(A|do(F)), whereas the IPW score requires full knowledge of this distribution or an unbiased estimator whose existence is unclear. The paper also complements the impossibility result of Othman and Sandholm (2010) by proving a more general impossibility (Theorem 2) that applies beyond decision-theoretic settings. Relative to Oesterheld et al. (2023), this paper extends the analysis from marginal to conditional forecasts and formalises it in a structural causal model framework. Compared to Perdomo et al. (2020), the paper resolves their open issues by showing that conditional forecasts with proper scoring rules simultaneously achieve performative stability and performative optimality (Theorem 6), whereas their original framework could suffer from instability under retraining.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the performative prediction framework of Perdomo et al. (2020) and the proper scoring rules for performative forecasts of Oesterheld et al. (2023) from marginal to conditional forecasts, formalizing everything within a structural causal model (Pearl, 2009). It builds on the classical proper scoring rule literature (Brier, Gneiting & Raftery, Grünwald & Dawid) and the decision-theoretic scoring rules of Othman & Sandholm (2010), Chen et al. (2011), and Oesterheld & Conitzer (2020), but departs from them by proving a general impossibility result: even under forecast-invariance, classical proper scoring rules fail to elicit correct performative forecasts. The two proposed solutions—a utility-score-based method with a properness correction (Theorem 4) and an unbiased divergence-estimation approach (Theorem 5)—are novel contributions that go beyond the restricted decision-theoretic settings of prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The impossibility theorem (Theorem 2) is a fundamental result: it shows that the standard toolkit for evaluating forecasts breaks down in performative settings even when the problem is well-posed, which has direct implications for any system where predictions influence outcomes (traffic, economics, public health). The divergence-based scoring method (Theorem 5) is notable because it does not require forecast-invariance, only existence of a correct forecast, making it more broadly applicable. Key limitations include the requirement of a discrete action space A, the need for known causal structure to verify d-separation, and the assumption that the principal can compute unbiased divergence estimates. Open questions include extension to continuous A, point forecasts via consistent scoring functions, and practical verification of forecast-invariance in real systems.
