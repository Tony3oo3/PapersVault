## Problem

Mode collapse in time series generative models (GANs, VAEs, diffusion models) remains largely unaddressed, unlike in image generation. Existing evaluation metrics such as Predictive Score, Discriminative Score, and Context-FID are computationally expensive because they require training auxiliary models, and they provide only aggregate scores that fail to reveal which specific temporal dynamics are preserved or lost in generated sequences.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255065/cropped_page8_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes paired minibatches of real and generated multivariate time series, each of shape n×ℓ (n features, ℓ timesteps), with no requirement for pre-trained encoders or discriminators. It outputs a single scalar DMD-GEN distance (a Wasserstein-OT cost over mode subspaces) that is naturally bounded by the Grassmann geometry (principal angles in [0, π/2]), along with per-mode subspace comparisons that identify which specific dynamical modes are collapsed, frequency-shifted, or spuriously injected in the generated data.

## Method

DMD-GEN applies Dynamic Mode Decomposition to each time series to extract k dominant eigenvectors (DMD modes) that capture coherent temporal structures, then represents the subspace spanned by these eigenvectors as a point on the Grassmann manifold Gr(k,n). Pairwise dissimilarity between real and generated mode subspaces is computed via principal angles obtained from the SVD of QᵀQ̃, yielding a geodesic distance equal to the Euclidean norm of the principal-angle vector. A cost matrix of these pairwise distances is then fed into an Optimal Transport problem (solved via Sinkhorn) to compute the Wasserstein distance between the real and generated mode distributions, giving the final DMD-GEN score. The approach is entirely training-free, requires no auxiliary models, and is supported by a robustness theorem (Theorem 3.5) bounding subspace perturbation by the spectral gap, ensuring the metric is stable under small dynamical perturbations.

## Results

DMD-GEN consistently identifies the same best-performing generative model as established metrics (Predictive Score, Discriminative Score, Context-FID) across all four datasets. On the controlled mode-collapse experiment, DMD-GEN shows a stable, monotonic increase from +115% (40% collapse) to +681% (10% collapse), while Context-FID spikes to +36,796% at 10% collapse and the Discriminative/Predictive Scores fluctuate non-monotonically (e.g., Discriminative Score drops to −20.75% at 40% collapse). DMD-GEN is naturally bounded by the Grassmann geometry (principal angles in [0, π/2]), ensuring numerical stability that Context-FID lacks. The metric requires no auxiliary model training, unlike Predictive/Discriminative Scores and Context-FID.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Predictive and Discriminative Scores from Yoon et al. (TimeGAN, NeurIPS 2019), DMD-GEN produces identical model rankings on all four datasets while being training-free (those metrics require training auxiliary classifiers/forecasters). In the controlled mode-collapse experiment, the Discriminative Score fluctuates non-monotonically (ranging from +586% to −20% across collapse levels) and the Predictive Score barely moves (±1%), whereas DMD-GEN increases smoothly and monotonically with collapse severity. Against Context-FID from Jeha et al. (PSA-GAN, ICLR 2022), DMD-GEN agrees on the best model in every dataset but remains numerically bounded (principal angles ≤ π/2), while Context-FID produces extreme values (e.g., +36,796% at 10% collapse) that make cross-severity comparison difficult. Against MTopDiv from Barannikov et al. (NeurIPS 2021), DMD-GEN is far more stable: MTopDiv exhibits large standard deviations (e.g., 467.35±49.10 for TimeGAN on Energy) because it discards temporal structure by flattening sequences, whereas DMD-GEN explicitly models temporal dynamics via DMD eigenvectors.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Dynamic Mode Decomposition (DMD) [Schmid, 2010], a spectral method originally developed for fluid dynamics, repurposing it as a tool for characterizing temporal modes in time series data. It departs from existing time series evaluation metrics (Predictive/Discriminative Scores from TimeGAN, Context-FID from PSA-GAN) by replacing the need for trained auxiliary models with a training-free geometric comparison on the Grassmann manifold, combined with Optimal Transport between mode subspaces. The Koopman operator framework provides the theoretical justification for treating DMD eigenvectors as a principled basis for comparing dynamical structure across real and generated sequences.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine gap: mode collapse in time series generative models has lacked both a formal definition and an interpretable, training-free detection tool, unlike the well-studied image domain. DMD-GEN's boundedness (principal angles in [0, π/2]) and per-mode interpretability offer practical advantages over unbounded metrics like Context-FID, and the controlled-collapse experiments provide the first systematic sensitivity analysis for time series. A key limitation is that DMD provides only a linear (Koopman) approximation of potentially strongly nonlinear dynamics, so capturing richer nonlinear structure would require more modes and increased computational cost. Open questions include whether the metric generalizes to irregularly sampled or multivariate time series with varying dimensionality, and whether the OT coupling between real and generated modes remains well-defined when the number of dominant modes differs substantially between the two sets.
