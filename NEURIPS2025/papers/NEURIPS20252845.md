## Problem

Non-interactive locally private hypothesis selection previously required O(k²) samples, while interactive protocols could achieve O(k) samples. This paper closes the gap by providing a non-interactive Õ(k^{3/2})-sample algorithm, improving on both the non-interactive and interactive baselines simultaneously.

## Input / Output

The algorithm receives a set Q of k candidate distributions and n independent samples from an unknown distribution p, where each sample is held by a separate user who must apply ε-LDP before communicating. All queries are sent in a single non-interactive round (no adaptivity across users), and the output is a distribution q̂ ∈ Q satisfying ‖q̂ − p‖₁ ≤ 13·min_{q∈Q} ‖q − p‖₁ + α with high probability. The sample complexity is Õ(k^{3/2}·√log k / (α²ε²)), with Õ(k^{5/2}) expected preprocessing time to construct the query set.

## Method

The method combines three components: (1) a Relaxed Minimum Distance Estimator (RMDE) that selects the hypothesis minimizing the maximum discrepancy over a reduced query set T, requiring only that each pair of distributions be distinguishable by some T ∈ T up to a factor φ; (2) randomized response applied in parallel to estimate each query ⟨p, T⟩ under ε-LDP; and (3) the Scheffe graph, a new directed graph whose vertices are pairs {j,j′} and whose edges encode when the signed Scheffe set S_{ii′} provides sufficient ℓ₁-information about the difference δ_{jj′}. The key structural result is that the 1/6-Scheffe graph has domination number at most 4k^{3/2}√log k, proven via a triangular substructure lemma showing every triple of vertices contains at least one directed edge or bidirectional edge, which bounds the number of low-in-degree vertices and enables a random sampling argument to find a small dominating set.

## Results

The paper proves a non-interactive ε-LDP hypothesis selection algorithm requiring n₀ ≤ O(k^{3/2} √(log k) log(k/β) / (α² ε²)) samples (Theorem 1), improving upon the O(k²) bound of the straightforward Scheffé tournament privatization. The key combinatorial ingredient is showing the domination number of the 1/6-Scheffé graph is at most 4k^{3/2}√(log k) (Theorem 14), with a matching Ω̃(k^{3/2}) lower bound (Theorem 18). A multi-round corollary (Corollary 2) further reduces the exponent to k^{1+1/(3^t−1)} for t rounds, approaching the interactive O(k) lower bound as t grows.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines; comparisons are between sample-complexity bounds. The paper's Õ(k^{3/2}) non-interactive bound strictly improves upon the O(k²) non-interactive Scheffé tournament bound and the Õ(k) non-interactive bound that applies only to the simpler problem of k-wise simple hypothesis testing (where p is promised to equal some q ∈ Q). It does not match the O(k) interactive bound of GKK+20/PAA24, which requires adaptive queries. The authors show via a lower bound (Theorem 18) that their Scheffé-graph technique cannot be pushed below Ω̃(k^{3/2}) without additional structural assumptions, and they disprove a flattening conjecture from GKK+20 that would have yielded an Õ(k) non-interactive algorithm. Thus the paper closes the gap between the non-interactive O(k²) and interactive O(k) regimes by a factor of √k, while establishing that further improvement requires fundamentally new ideas.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the classical Scheffé test / minimum distance estimator of Devroye and Lugosi by introducing a relaxed version (RMDE) that works with a subset of queries rather than all C(k,2) signed Scheffé sets, and by using the new Scheffé graph to identify a small dominating set of queries. It builds directly on Gopi et al. (COLT 2020), who established the LDP hypothesis selection framework and proved an interactive O(k)-sample algorithm plus a non-interactive Õ(k)-sample algorithm for the simpler k-wise simple hypothesis testing problem. The key departure is the combinatorial insight: rather than privatizing all pairwise comparisons (yielding O(k²) samples) or requiring adaptive queries (yielding O(k) samples but interactivity), the Scheffé graph's triangular substructure guarantees a dominating set of size Õ(k^{3/2}), enabling a non-interactive protocol with intermediate sample complexity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result closes a significant gap in the LDP hypothesis selection landscape, providing the first non-interactive algorithm that beats the trivial O(k²) Scheffé tournament privatization while remaining applicable to the full (agnostic) hypothesis selection problem rather than just simple hypothesis testing. The Scheffé graph is a new combinatorial object that may find applications beyond this specific problem, and the near-matching lower bound on its domination number (Theorem 18) clarifies that the triangular substructure alone cannot yield the ideal Õ(k) bound. The counterexample to the flattening conjecture from GKK+20 is an important negative result that rules out a natural and previously-conjectured route to Õ(k) samples. The central open question is whether additional structure in the Scheffé graph (beyond triangles) can close the remaining √k gap to the known lower bound.
