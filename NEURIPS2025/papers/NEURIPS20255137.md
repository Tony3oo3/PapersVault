## Problem

Existing 1-bit post-training quantization methods for LLMs (e.g., BiLLM, ARB-LLM) suffer from severe performance degradation or complete failure on modern architectures like LLaMA3-8B, due to limited numerical expressiveness under 1-bit budgets, reliance on fixed-threshold or ℓ₁-based salient column selection, and the absence of frequency-domain awareness. Global orthogonal transforms that could improve expressiveness incur O(d²) inference overhead, making them impractical for edge deployment.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a full-precision weight matrix W ∈ R^{d×d} per linear layer and 128 C4 calibration samples (sequence length 2048) for Hessian estimation. It produces 1-bit binarized weights stored in the Haar frequency domain, organized into salient and non-salient regions, each with frequency-band-specific scaling factors (α) and group means (μ) stored in FP16, plus a binary saliency column bitmap. The average weight overhead is 1.00 bits (HBLLM-col) or 1.08 bits (HBLLM-row) per parameter, with total storage of ~2.88–3.42 bits/weight including coefficients and flags.

## Method

HBLLM integrates a single-level Haar wavelet transform into the BiLLM quantization pipeline, decomposing each weight row into low- and high-frequency subbands before group-wise sign-based binarization, which increases the cardinality of the inverse quantization set (CIQ) from 8 (BiLLM) to up to 1024. Two structure-aware grouping strategies operate jointly: (1) ℓ₂-norm-based saliency-driven column selection ranks columns by their Hessian-weighted energy to identify activation-critical directions, and (2) frequency-aware multi-parameter intra-row grouping adaptively splits coefficients within each Haar subband using data-driven percentile thresholds, doubling the number of quantization subgroups per row. For non-salient weights, a shared mean is applied across the two groups within each frequency band to reduce storage by 0.25 bits/weight. The Haar transform is implemented via two fixed 1D convolutional kernels of size 2, yielding O(d) inference cost instead of O(d²) for global transforms, and the overall quantization follows GPTQ's sequential block-wise Hessian error compensation.

## Results

On LLaMA2-13B, HBLLM-row achieves a WikiText-2 perplexity of 6.71 (vs. 4.88 FP16) at only 1.07 average weight bits, outperforming the next-best method ARB-LLM_RC (10.98) by 39%. Across all evaluated model families (OPT, LLaMA-1/2/3), HBLLM reduces perplexity by 33%–66% relative to prior 1-bit methods while retaining 73.8%–88.8% of the original model's zero-shot QA accuracy. On the modern LLaMA3-8B architecture, where BiLLM and ARB-LLM_X suffer severe degradation (Wiki2: 56.24 and 37.90 respectively), HBLLM-row maintains a Wiki2 perplexity of 16.18 with no performance collapse.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HBLLM consistently outperforms all 1-bit baselines across every model family and evaluation metric. Against BiLLM, HBLLM-row reduces WikiText-2 perplexity by 66% on LLaMA2-7B (10.52 vs. 31.38) and by 66% on LLaMA2-13B (6.71 vs. 19.57), while also improving AvgQA by 15.6 and 16.9 points respectively. Against ARB-LLM_RC (the strongest 1-bit baseline), HBLLM-row still achieves 34% lower Wiki2 perplexity on LLaMA2-7B (10.52 vs. 15.85) and 39% lower on LLaMA2-13B (6.71 vs. 10.98). Notably, HBLLM also surpasses the 2.2-bit FrameQuant method at a lower bit-width: on LLaMA2-7B, HBLLM-row achieves Wiki2 10.52 vs. FrameQuant's 13.34, and on LLaMA3-8B the gap widens to 16.18 vs. 23.36. The advantage is most dramatic on LLaMA3-8B, where BiLLM and ARB-LLM_X degrade severely (Wiki2: 56.24 and 37.90) while HBLLM-row maintains 16.18, confirming the method's robustness on modern architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

HBLLM extends the BiLLM salient-column-separation framework and GPTQ's Hessian-based error compensation by inserting a localized Haar wavelet transform into the quantization pipeline, shifting the binarization from the spatial domain to a frequency domain. It departs from global orthogonal transform approaches (FrameQuant, QuIP) that incur O(d²) inverse-transform cost, replacing them with O(d) local convolutions. The frequency-aware multi-parameter intra-row grouping and ℓ₂-norm-based saliency selection represent a structural departure from the uniform partitioning and ℓ₁-heuristic column selection used in BiLLM and ARB-LLM, while the CIQ metric provides a new theoretical lens for comparing expressiveness across 1-bit schemes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

HBLLM demonstrates that 1-bit PTQ can remain stable on modern architectures (LLaMA3-8B, DeepSeek-R1-Distill) where prior methods collapse, narrowing the perplexity gap to FP16 to a 1.2×–2.5× factor. The finding that local Haar transforms outperform global DCT transforms (which catastrophically fail on OPT-1.3B) challenges the assumption that more 'sophisticated' spectral bases are always better for structured quantization. Limitations include support only for dense models (no MoE), a 20–30% quantization-time overhead over BiLLM, and reliance on 128-sample C4 calibration; open questions include whether the approach generalizes to other wavelet families, multi-level decomposition, or hardware-native binary inference kernels.
