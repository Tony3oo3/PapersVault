## Problem

Diffusion-based image stylization suffers from two persistent gaps: existing consistency modules (e.g., ControlNet) preserve global layout but fail on fine-grained semantics and details in complex scenes, and style LoRAs degrade significantly in image-to-image pipelines compared to text-to-image generation. This paper bridges the performance gap between open-source stylization methods and proprietary systems like GPT-4o by learning a style-agnostic consistency plugin that works with arbitrary style LoRAs.

## Input / Output

The method takes an original RGB image at 1024×1024, a plug-and-play style LoRA module, and a text prompt as inputs. The original image is downsampled to 512×512 and used directly as the sole conditioning signal (no explicit geometric constraints like edges, sketches, or poses), with spatial alignment to the high-resolution output handled via conditional token mapping. The output is a 1024×1024 stylized image that preserves the input's identity, composition, and fine details while applying the target artistic style.

## Method

OmniConsistency introduces a two-stage decoupled training strategy on the Flux DiT backbone: Stage 1 independently trains 22 style-specific LoRAs to build a LoRA Bank; Stage 2 trains a lightweight Consistency LoRA applied exclusively to the condition-branch QKV projections, while a Rolling LoRA Bank Loader periodically swaps in different style LoRAs and their paired data every 50 steps to enforce style-agnosticism. Architecturally, causal attention is used so condition tokens can only attend to themselves (read-only conditioning), preventing interference with the noise/text branch and leaving all main-branch LoRA slots free for arbitrary style modules. Two efficiency mechanisms—Conditional Token Mapping (mapping 512×512 condition tokens to 1024×1024 positions) and Feature Reuse (caching condition KV projections across denoising steps)—keep overhead to just 4.6% extra GPU memory and 5.3% extra inference time. Training uses 2,600 GPT-4o-generated image pairs across 22 styles with human-in-the-loop filtering.

## Results

On the 100-image I2I benchmark with 5 unseen style LoRAs, OmniConsistency achieves the best style consistency across all five metrics: FID 39.2, CMMD 0.145, DreamSim 0.181, CLIP-I 0.875, and GPT-4o score 4.64/5. It also attains the best content-consistency DreamSim (0.278) and the highest text-image alignment CLIP-Score (0.321). Compared to the commercial GPT-4o baseline, OmniConsistency matches or exceeds it on style fidelity and text alignment while remaining competitive on content consistency (GPT-4o score 4.52 vs. 4.57).

## Baselines & Comparisons

OmniConsistency decisively outperforms both ControlNet baselines (Flux I2I+CN and Flux T2I+CN) on style consistency, where ControlNet suffers severe style degradation (FID 70.0 / 60.2 vs. 39.2; CMMD 0.736 / 0.556 vs. 0.145), confirming that rigid spatial conditioning suppresses LoRA style expression. Against the Redux-based baselines, OmniConsistency wins on every style metric (e.g., DreamSim 0.181 vs. 0.213–0.218; GPT-4o 4.64 vs. 4.33–4.49) while also improving content-consistency DreamSim (0.278 vs. 0.284–0.320), showing that stronger stylization need not sacrifice structural fidelity. The plain Flux I2I (SDEdit) baseline is dominated on all nine metrics. Compared to the commercial GPT-4o API, OmniConsistency achieves a higher GPT-4o style score (4.64 vs. not reported) and CLIP-Score (0.321 vs. 0.294), while GPT-4o retains a slight edge on content GPT-4o score (4.57 vs. 4.52) and content DreamSim (0.317 vs. 0.278 in OmniConsistency's favor), indicating near-parity with the proprietary system.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Flux Diffusion Transformer backbone and adopts the causal attention conditioning mechanism from EasyControl, but departs from both ControlNet-style spatial conditioning (which uses explicit geometric cues like edges and depth) and OminiControl's main-branch LoRA injection by isolating consistency learning entirely to the condition branch. The two-stage decoupled training with a rolling LoRA Bank loader is a novel departure from joint style-and-structure optimization, explicitly preventing the consistency module from absorbing stylistic features. Conceptually, the work positions itself as a plug-and-play augmentation layer on top of existing style LoRAs rather than a replacement, distinguishing it from end-to-end stylization pipelines like IP-Adapter or Redux.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By closing the consistency gap between open-source stylization pipelines and GPT-4o with only ~5% overhead, this work provides a practical, composable solution for the large community of style LoRA users who currently suffer from style degradation under structural constraints. The style-agnostic design (one consistency module generalizing across 22+ styles and unseen LoRAs) reduces the need for per-style consistency tuning. Open questions include whether the approach transfers beyond the Flux architecture, whether the 2,600-pair dataset is sufficient for truly long-tail style generalization, and how the method handles non-English text and fine-grained facial/hand details, which remain acknowledged failure modes.
