## Problem

Existing methods for learning linear dynamical systems either require the hidden transition matrix to be symmetric, scale polynomially with the hidden dimension, or fail under marginal stability. The paper asks what general form of sequence preconditioning—beyond classical differencing—can provably improve learnability in a system-agnostic way, and shows that convolving the target with orthogonal polynomial coefficients yields the first sublinear, hidden-dimension-independent regret bounds for marginally stable and asymmetric LDS.

## Input / Output

The method receives a sequence of input vectors u_t ∈ C^{d_in} (bounded in ℓ2 norm) and corresponding observations y_t ∈ C^{d_out} generated by an LDS, along with a fixed set of n+1 polynomial coefficients c_{0:n} (e.g., from the n-th monic Chebyshev or Legendre polynomial, with c_0 = 1). At each time step the algorithm produces a single next-step prediction ŷ_t ∈ C^{d_out} in an online fashion, where the prediction is the sum of a regression/spectral-filtering component and a correction term −Σ c_i y_{t−i} that undoes the preconditioning. The polynomial degree n is chosen to grow only logarithmically with the hidden dimension, keeping the number of learned parameters independent of d_hidden.

## Method

The key insight is that convolving the target sequence with coefficients c_{0:n} corresponds to evaluating the polynomial p_n^c(x) = Σ c_i x^{n−i} on the hidden transition matrix A, decomposing y_t into a known preconditioning term, a low-rank learnable regression term, and a residual involving p_n^c(A). Choosing Chebyshev coefficients makes the residual exponentially small (|p_n(λ)| ≤ 2^{−(n−1)} for λ ∈ [−1,1], extended to a bounded complex sector via a new lemma) while the learnable term's domain diameter grows only as n²‖c‖₁, yielding a favorable trade-off. This preconditioning is applied to two prediction algorithms: (1) convex regression over the learnable coefficients, achieving Õ(T^{−2/13}) average loss, and (2) a modified spectral filtering method using novel eigenvectors of a complex-domain integral matrix Z_T, achieving Õ(T^{−1/39}) regret. The Chebyshev choice is universal—it requires no knowledge of A's spectrum, unlike the characteristic-polynomial alternative which would demand d_hidden parameters—and the approach generalizes empirically to nonlinear systems, deep RNNs, and real-world time series (ETTh1) beyond the LDS setting.

## Results

Theoretically, USP combined with convex regression achieves average loss $	ilde{O}(T^{-2/13})$ and USP combined with spectral filtering achieves $	ilde{O}(T^{-1/39})$, both the first dimension-independent sublinear regret bounds for marginally stable, asymmetric linear dynamical systems (vs. a vacuous $O(T^{5/2})$ for vanilla regression). Empirically, on synthetic linear LDS data (d_hidden=300), Chebyshev degree-5 preconditioning reduces regression error from 0.74 to 0.15 (τ_thresh=0.01) and spectral filtering error from 5.94 to 0.69, while on nonlinear data spectral filtering error drops from 153.8 to 0.92. On the ETTh1 real-world dataset, Chebyshev/Legendre degree-5 preconditioning yields the best 10-layer LSTM performance after 1000 iterations, and jointly learned coefficients achieve the best final error at T=5000.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares USP (with Chebyshev, Legendre, or jointly learned coefficients) against no-preconditioning baselines across three algorithms (regression, spectral filtering, DNN) and three data types (linear LDS, nonlinear LDS, deep RNN). Fixed Chebyshev and Legendre coefficients yield nearly identical improvements, consistently outperforming the no-preconditioning baseline by 3–168× depending on setting (e.g., spectral filtering on nonlinear data: 153.8 → 0.92). Jointly learned coefficients further improve regression and spectral filtering (e.g., 0.27 vs. 0.15 for regression at τ=0.01) but destabilize DNN training on nonlinear and RNN-generated data, where errors increase 5–10× over baseline. Higher polynomial degrees (10, 20) degrade performance relative to degree 5 due to exponential growth in coefficient ℓ₁-norm (Lemma 3.2), confirming the theoretical tension between shrinking the residual term and controlling the learnable domain diameter. On the ETTh1 real-world benchmark, fixed Chebyshev/Legendre degree-5 preconditioning outperforms the baseline LSTM after 1000 iterations, while learned coefficients eventually surpass all fixed methods by T=5000.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalizes the classical Box-Jenkins differencing operator to arbitrary polynomial convolution coefficients, identifying a clean algebraic connection: convolving the target sequence with polynomial coefficients c₀:ₙ is equivalent to evaluating that polynomial at the hidden transition matrix A of a linear dynamical system. It builds directly on the spectral filtering framework of Hazan, Singh, and Zhang (2017) [27], extending it to handle asymmetric transition matrices with complex eigenvalues via a novel complex-domain spectral filter matrix. The regret analysis draws on online convex optimization (OGD over convex domains) and the Chebyshev polynomial's minimax property on [-1,1], extended here to a bounded-argument region of the complex plane.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper delivers the first dimension-independent sublinear regret bounds for marginally stable, asymmetric LDS, a gap that has persisted in the online control literature. The practical takeaway — a simple fixed-coefficient convolution that improves regression, spectral filtering, and even deep RNNs without requiring knowledge of the underlying system — is broadly applicable and easy to adopt. However, the theoretical guarantees require eigenvalue arguments bounded by O(1/log T) or T^{-1/3}, which is restrictive, and the authors themselves note the spectral filtering result is 'rather weak' and does not fully exploit spectral filtering's structural advantages. Open questions include whether the complex-domain spectral filter matrix (Eq. 5) admits a full eigendecay characterization, whether the Chebyshev choice is truly optimal versus Legendre or other orthogonal polynomials, and whether the preconditioning benefit persists under adversarial (non-LDS) data generation.
