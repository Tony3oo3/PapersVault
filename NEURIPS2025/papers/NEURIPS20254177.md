## Problem

World models are typically trained with surrogate objectives like maximum likelihood estimation, which misalign with the task-specific metrics that actually matter—transition prediction accuracy for language models and perceptual quality for video models. This misalignment causes known pathologies such as repetition, hallucination, and blurry predictions, and the teacher-forcing paradigm is blind to error accumulation over multi-step horizons. The paper asks whether reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR), recently successful for LLM reasoning, can be adapted to directly optimize world models for their target prediction metrics.

## Input / Output

For language world models, input is a JSON-encoded state plus a natural-language action, and output is the predicted next-state JSON (or state-difference updates), a scalar reward, and a task-completion flag. For video world models, input is a sequence of past frames at 256×320 resolution plus 13-dimensional continuous action vectors (each discretized into 256 uniform bins), and output is the predicted next frame or a sequence of future frames. In both cases, all modalities are unified into discrete token sequences: text via BPE, video via a learned VQGAN/FSQ tokenizer (320 tokens per frame for single-step, 80 tokens per frame with 1280 context tokens for multi-step), and actions via uniform binning, all fed to a decoder-only Transformer.

## Method

RLVR-World unifies world modeling across modalities into an autoregressive next-token prediction framework, then post-trains the MLE-pre-trained model using GRPO with task-specific verifiable rewards computed on decoded predictions. For language, the reward is binary accuracy or a weighted combination of property-level accuracies over predicted state changes; for video, the reward is the negative sum of L1 and LPIPS losses between decoded predicted frames and ground-truth observations. The key mechanism is that the model generates a group of candidate next-state token sequences, each is decoded back to the original modality (rule-based JSON extraction or visual decoder), and the decoded prediction is scored against ground truth—bypassing the token-level likelihood entirely. This yields substantial gains (e.g., +30.7% accuracy on text games, +9.2% relative LPIPS improvement on robot manipulation) with only hundreds of RLVR gradient steps versus hundreds of thousands for MLE pre-training, and it mitigates the repetition artifact in multi-step video prediction by reducing the repetition rate from 48.6% to 9.9%.

## Results

On text game state prediction (ByteSized32), RLVR-World with a 7B base model achieves 65.53% overall accuracy, surpassing GPT-4 (64.76%), with a +34.7% absolute gain over SFT on unchanged cases. On web page state prediction (WebArena), RLVR-World improves F1 from 49.94% (SFT) to 65.11% (+30.3% relative). On RT-1 robot manipulation, RLVR-World achieves +9.2% relative LPIPS improvement on multi-step prediction (14.8→13.4) with only hundreds of RLVR gradient steps versus hundreds of thousands for MLE pre-training. On the Granular dataset, RLVR-World achieves LPIPS 2.42, significantly outperforming DINO-WM's reported 3.5.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GPT-4 on text game state prediction, RLVR-World (7B) achieves 65.53% overall accuracy versus GPT-4's 64.76%, though GPT-4 still leads on changed cases (51.60% vs. 40.33%). Against DINO-WM on video prediction, RLVR-World matches DINO-WM on PushT (LPIPS 0.70 vs. 0.7 reported) and significantly outperforms it on the most challenging Granular dataset (LPIPS 2.42 vs. 3.5, SSIM 95.42 vs. 94.0), but underperforms on Rope (LPIPS 2.08 vs. 0.9), which the authors attribute to overfitting on the small dataset. Against the diffusion-based AVDC model, RLVR-World substantially outperforms on all three datasets (e.g., LPIPS 0.70 vs. 4.6 on PushT, 2.42 vs. 10.6 on Granular). Against SIMPLER's handcrafted simulators for policy evaluation, RLVR-World video world models yield smaller discrepancies between real and simulated success rates, suggesting a more faithful simulator for bridging the sim-to-real gap.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RLVR paradigm—established for LLM reasoning tasks via DeepSeek-R1 and GRPO—to world models across language and video modalities. It builds on iVideoGPT's autoregressive video world model architecture and compressive tokenizer, and on WMA's web-agent world model formulation, unifying both under a single sequence-modeling framework where states and actions become token sequences. The key departure is from standard MLE pre-training: rather than relying on surrogate objectives (next-token likelihood, diffusion ELBO), the paper post-trains with rule-based verifiable rewards (accuracy, F1, LPIPS) that directly target the prediction metrics practitioners actually care about.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical finding that hundreds of RLVR gradient steps can match or exceed hundreds of thousands of MLE steps offers a compelling efficiency argument for RLVR as a general post-training stage for generative models beyond LLMs. Open questions remain: whether gains persist at scale with larger, general-purpose video world models; whether RLVR improves OOD/counterfactual generalization in sequential decision-making; and how to design rewards that capture physical plausibility and temporal consistency beyond standard visual metrics. The paper also honestly notes that test-time scaling (best-of-N) eventually closes the gap, suggesting current RLVR methods have a performance ceiling.
