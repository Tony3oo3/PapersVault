## Problem

Monocular RGB-based manipulation policies struggle to disambiguate visually similar yet structurally different states, leading to inaccurate 3D action estimation. While integrating a pre-trained depth estimator provides the needed geometric cues, it introduces substantial inference-time latency and computational overhead that limits real-world deployment. The paper asks whether depth-guided 3D reasoning can be transferred to a lightweight RGB-only student without any runtime 3D module.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250181/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the student consumes a sliding window of H monocular RGB frames plus a natural-language instruction and outputs a Gaussian action distribution (mean and standard deviation) via an MLP policy head. During training, the teacher additionally ingests pseudo-depth maps generated by Depth Anything V2, which are rendered as pseudo-colored heatmaps to reduce low-level appearance mismatch before being fused with RGB through a shared ResNet-18 encoder. The language instruction is encoded by a 6-layer MiniLM into a single token that is concatenated with visual and learnable action tokens in the Transformer decoder.

## Method

MonoLift employs a tri-level knowledge distillation strategy that transfers spatial, temporal, and action-level knowledge from a depth-guided teacher to an RGB-only student, all trained end-to-end with a shared encoder, Transformer decoder, and policy head. Spatial distillation aligns the student's RGB features with the teacher's dual-path cross-modal fused features (combining multi-head self-attention and token-wise averaging over RGB and depth branches) via an L2 loss. Temporal distillation aligns central-difference gradients of the Transformer's contextualized features across the history window, teaching the student to capture 3D-structural state transitions. Action distillation minimizes the KL divergence between the teacher's and student's Gaussian action distributions, transferring geometry-aware behavioral priors. The total training objective combines the standard maximum-likelihood actor loss on expert demonstrations with these three distillation terms, while at deployment only the RGB-only student is retained, matching the inference cost of a vanilla RGB baseline (18.1 ms, 8.5 M params).

## Results

On LIBERO-90 (8 tasks, 2 scenes), MonoLift achieves 80.8±3.3% average success rate using only single-view RGB input, outperforming all baselines including those with explicit 3D inputs. On Meta-World (15 tasks), MonoLift reaches 87.8±2.3%, surpassing the RGB-D-based 3D-VLA (83.2±2.4%) by 4.6 points. On LIBERO-Long (long-horizon tasks), MonoLift attains 71.7±1.1%, nearly doubling the performance of the best single-view RGB baseline (MT-ACT at 42.0±2.2%). Deployment efficiency is preserved: MonoLift runs at 18.1 ms with 8.5M parameters, identical to the RGB-only baseline, versus 442.8 ms and 344.8M parameters for the RGB+Depth inference-time variant.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MonoLift outperforms all six baselines across every benchmark. Against single-view RGB direct-mapping methods, MonoLift (80.8%) surpasses MT-ACT (35.4%) by 45.4 points and RT-1 (47.5%) by 33.3 points on LIBERO-90, indicating that direct imitation without structural cues is fundamentally limited. Against single-view RGB methods that infer implicit 3D cues, MonoLift beats MT-R3M (49.2%) by 31.6 points and GROUND (52.5%) by 28.3 points, with notably lower variance (±3.3 vs. ±8.8 for GROUND), suggesting more stable policy learning. Most strikingly, MonoLift exceeds methods that rely on explicit 3D inputs: it outperforms 3D-VLA (68.7%, RGB-D) by 12.1 points and SPA (61.2%, multi-view RGB) by 19.6 points on LIBERO-90, and surpasses 3D-VLA (83.2%) by 4.6 points on Meta-World—all while using only monocular RGB at inference time with no additional sensors or modules.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of monocular RGB policy learning (RT-1, MT-ACT, GROUND, MT-R3M) and knowledge distillation, departing from both lines in a specific way. Prior monocular methods either map RGB directly to actions (struggling with 3D ambiguity) or use multi-stage pipelines to infer implicit 3D cues (accumulating errors), while explicit 3D input methods (3D-VLA, SPA) require costly sensors at deployment. MonoLift repurposes a pre-trained depth estimator (Depth Anything V2) as a training-only teacher, transferring spatial, temporal, and action-level knowledge to a lightweight RGB-only student via a tri-level distillation objective, thereby decoupling 3D reasoning from inference-time cost.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for resource-constrained robotic deployment: a single RGB camera suffices at inference (18.1 ms, 8.5 M params) while matching or exceeding methods that require depth sensors or multi-view cameras. The key open question the authors acknowledge is robustness to depth estimation errors in visually challenging conditions (specular surfaces, transparency, occlusion), since the framework does not model uncertainty in the pseudo-depth signal. More broadly, the pattern of using a heavy pre-trained model as a training-time teacher to 'bake in' structural knowledge into a lightweight student is a transferable recipe that could extend to other modality-limited perception tasks beyond manipulation.
