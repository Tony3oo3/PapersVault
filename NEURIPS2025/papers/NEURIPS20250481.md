## Problem

When jointly training ID-based and MLLM-based representations in recommendation systems, a convergence inconsistency arises: ID embeddings converge rapidly with strong discriminative signals while frozen MLLM features require more epochs, causing the ID branch to dominate gradient updates and suppress the multimodal branch. Existing two-stage alignment approaches (e.g., AlignRec) address pre-training alignment but do not resolve the optimization imbalance during the joint training phase itself.

## Input / Output

The system consumes user IDs, item IDs, item text descriptions, item images/videos, and user interaction histories. The MLLM (Qwen2-VL-2B) encodes text-image pairs into item embeddings via a designated [Item_cls] output token, while a User Embedding Generator (UEG) aggregates historical item multimodal embeddings into a unified user representation. The final model produces a binary click-through-rate probability by concatenating outputs from separate ID and MM backbones and passing them through a fusion layer trained with binary cross-entropy.

## Method

The framework operates in two stages. Stage 1 fine-tunes the MLLM with three alignment objectives (masked text-image reconstruction, metadata-to-description prediction, and next-item prediction from behavior sequences) and post-aligns multimodal user embeddings to frozen ID embeddings via an L2 alignment loss. Stage 2 introduces Adaptive Gradient Masking (AGM), which estimates each modality's contribution per mini-batch using a mutual-information-based score, computes softmax-normalized update ratios, and leverages the Fisher Information Matrix to score parameter importance; non-uniform adaptive sampling then generates a binary gradient mask at the sub-network level that selectively freezes or updates parameters to balance optimization between branches. An unbiased variant AGM* rescales unmasked gradients by 1/π_j to correct the bias introduced by binary masking, yielding a tighter convergence bound that removes the (1−δ²) penalty term present in AGM's theoretical guarantee.

## Results

AGM* achieves the best AUC across all four datasets: 0.6864 (Baby), 0.7310 (Electronics), 0.7145 (Sports), and 0.6992 (MicroLens), while also attaining the lowest LogLoss (0.6656, 0.5969, 0.6391, 0.5841 respectively). The unbiased variant AGM* consistently outperforms the biased AGM by 0.0012–0.0025 AUC and 0.0005–0.0027 LogLoss across all datasets. In the online A/B test on a large-scale short-video platform, AGM* delivers +0.175% watch-time and +0.124% app usage over the baseline, compared to only +0.022% and +0.008% for a simple MM addition.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AGM* outperforms all eight baselines on both AUC and LogLoss across all four datasets. The closest competitors are GUME (Sports AUC 0.7119, MicroLens AUC 0.6968) and AlignRec (Baby AUC 0.6832, Electronics AUC 0.7274), with AGM* leading by 0.0026–0.0036 AUC over the best baseline on each dataset. Among MLLM-based methods, AGM* surpasses AlignRec by +0.0032 AUC on Baby and +0.0036 on Electronics, and beats GUME by +0.0026 on Sports and +0.0024 on MicroLens. Graph-based methods (MGCN, LGMRec, FREEDOM) and self-supervised approaches (BM3) trail AGM* by 0.003–0.011 AUC. The simplest baseline, MM-Rec, underperforms AGM* by the largest margin (up to 0.029 AUC on Sports), while VBPR and BM3, which do not leverage MLLMs, show the weakest overall performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the multimodal recommendation line of work (VBPR, BM3, FREEDOM, AlignRec, GUME) by addressing a specific optimization bottleneck: the convergence inconsistency between fast-converging ID embeddings and slower-converging MLLM-based representations during joint training. While AlignRec proposed a two-stage pre-training-then-joint-training strategy to partially mitigate this gap, it does not regulate gradient flow during the joint optimization phase. The core technical contribution repurposes two established tools—non-uniform adaptive sampling (Gopal, ICML 2016) and Fisher Information Matrix-based parameter importance (Kirkpatrick et al., PNAS 2017)—into a novel sub-network-level gradient masking mechanism (AGM) that dynamically balances parameter updates between ID and MM branches, a combination not previously applied to multimodal recommendation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The convergence inconsistency identified here is a practical bottleneck for any industrial system integrating frozen MLLM features with trainable ID embeddings, a pattern that will become more common as MLLMs are increasingly deployed in production recommenders. The theoretical convergence guarantees (Theorems 1–2) and the unbiased AGM* variant provide a principled foundation beyond heuristic learning-rate scheduling. However, the framework is tied to a specific two-branch (ID + MM) architecture with concatenation fusion, and its applicability to more complex multi-modal fusion (e.g., cross-attention, gating) remains unexplored. The modest offline gains (~0.001–0.003 AUC over the best baselines) and the dependence on MLLM fine-tuning quality limit the generality of the conclusions.
