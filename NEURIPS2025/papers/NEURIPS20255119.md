## Problem

Pre-trained flow matching models (e.g., Flux, SD3.5) require many ODE steps for high-quality generation, and the shortcut-model framework that enables flexible step-skipping relies on a step-size embedding incompatible with existing architectures—making adaptation require costly retraining from scratch. The paper asks whether one can impart aggressive few-step sampling to standard pre-trained FM models without any architectural modification or explicit step-size conditioning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255119/cropped_page6_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a frozen pre-trained flow matching model (Flux.1-Dev 12B or SD3.5-Large 8B) plus a text prompt and produces a generated RGB image in as few as 3 ODE steps. Training operates on ~512×512 images from LAION-POP (600k samples) via LoRA adapters on the frozen backbone, while evaluation is conducted at 1024×1024 on COCO-30k. The distilled model retains the original CFG-conditioned interface, requiring a separate unconditional forward pass at inference time.

## Method

SCFM introduces a velocity-space distillation loss that enforces linear consistency across timesteps, implicitly encoding step-size awareness without explicit step-size embeddings. The loss combines a teacher-guided term (k samples) transferring knowledge from the pre-trained model at fine-grained timesteps with a self-distillation term (N−k samples) enforcing consistency between the student and its EMA copy at coarser timesteps, using weighted velocity interpolation as the shared target. This dual-target formulation eliminates progressive distillation stages, automatically straightening curved velocity trajectories in a single end-to-end training phase. Training uses LoRA on frozen pre-trained weights with a dual fast-slow EMA strategy (μ=0.99 and μ=0.999) for accelerated convergence, distilling a 32-step Flux into a 3-step sampler in under 24 A100 GPU hours.

## Results

On the 12B Flux model, SCFM distills a 32-step teacher into a 3-step sampler in under 24 A100 GPU hours, achieving FID 6.34 and CLIP 33.10 on COCO-30k—outperforming Flux-Schnell (FID 7.06, CLIP 33.06), Flux-Hyper-SD (FID 9.65, CLIP 31.95), and Flux-TDD (FID 8.26, CLIP 31.38) at the same 3-step budget. At 8 steps, SCFM achieves FID 2.58 and CLIP 33.76, the best among all compared methods. On SD3.5-Large (8B), SCFM at 8 steps reaches FID 2.65 and CLIP 33.91, substantially outperforming SD3.5L-Turbo (FID 8.18, CLIP 33.81). Notably, SCFM achieves these results without adversarial distillation (ADD/LADD), which all baselines employ.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SCFM consistently outperforms all five baselines on both FID and CLIP across every step count tested. At 3 steps on Flux, SCFM achieves FID 6.34 vs. Flux-Schnell's 7.06, Flux-TDD's 8.26, and Flux-Hyper-SD's 9.65, while also attaining the highest CLIP score (33.10 vs. 33.06, 31.38, 31.95 respectively). SCFM also preserves the closest fidelity to the teacher (smallest |ΔFID| = 1.01) compared to Flux-Schnell (6.58), Flux-TDD (4.46), and Flux-Hyper-SD (1.52), indicating it best retains the original model's distributional characteristics. On SD3.5-Large at 8 steps, SCFM's FID of 2.65 dramatically beats SD3.5L-Turbo's 8.18, with a ΔFID of +0.32 versus Turbo's +7.03. A key differentiator is that all baselines (Flux-Schnell, SD3.5L-Turbo, Hyper-SD, TDD) rely on adversarial distillation (ADD/LADD) for their reported performance, whereas SCFM achieves superior results without any adversarial component. The training-free TeaCache baseline (32 steps) is also outperformed by SCFM's 8-step student in both speed (3.71s vs. 4.33s) and generation quality (FID 2.58 vs. 3.56).

## Relation to Prior Work

SCFM builds directly on the shortcut model framework (Frans et al., ICLR 2025), which introduced step-size-conditional velocity prediction for unified multi-step/one-step diffusion, but departs from it by eliminating the explicit step-size embedding entirely—instead implicitly encoding step-size awareness through velocity-space self-distillation. It also reinterprets progressive distillation (Salimans & Ho, 2022) in velocity space, replacing the multi-stage curriculum with a single end-to-end dual-target objective that simultaneously transfers teacher knowledge and self-bootstraps finer-scale consistency. Unlike prior trajectory-based distillation methods (consistency models, Hyper-SD, TDD) that operate in sample space and require ODE simulation for targets, SCFM's velocity-space formulation avoids this overhead and is architecturally compatible with existing pretrained flow matching models (Flux, SD3.5) without modification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: distilling a 12B-parameter Flux model into a 3-step sampler in under 24 A100 GPU hours (vs. thousands for progressive methods) without adversarial distillation (ADD/LADD) that all competing baselines rely on makes few-step flow matching accessible to practitioners with modest compute. The few-shot result (10 images for a 12B model) opens a path toward data-efficient distillation in domains where large-scale paired data is impractical (3D, video, robotics). Open questions include whether the approach can achieve true one-step generation, how well it preserves sample diversity/creativity under different seeds, and whether velocity-space adversarial objectives (a velocity-space LADD) could further close the gap to one-step quality.
