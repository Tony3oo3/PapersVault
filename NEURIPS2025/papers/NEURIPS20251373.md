## Problem

Existing FL compression methods focus almost exclusively on deterministic FL and primarily on uplink gradient compression, leaving the downlink largely unaddressed in stochastic (Bayesian) FL. While stochastic FL inherently integrates communication constraints into training through a mirror-descent structure, the joint uplink-downlink compression problem under limited bandwidth in both directions remained unexplored. The paper asks whether bi-directional compression can achieve order-of-magnitude communication reductions in stochastic FL without accuracy loss.

## Input / Output

Rather than transmitting model parameters or gradients directly, the method transmits compact indices (log₂(n_IS) bits per block) into pre-sampled candidate sets generated from a shared prior distribution. The prior is the most recent global model estimate (a vector of Bernoulli parameters for probabilistic mask training), and the posterior is the locally trained distribution over the same parameter space. The federator reconstructs the global model by averaging MRC-decoded samples from all clients, while clients reconstruct their local copy either from relayed uplink indices (global randomness variant) or from a second MRC round (private randomness variant).

## Method

BICOMPFL employs Minimal Random Coding (MRC) for both uplink and downlink in stochastic FL, where the KL-divergence between the local posterior and the shared prior directly determines the number of importance-sampling candidates needed, making communication cost an inherent part of the mirror-descent optimization objective. Two variants are proposed: BICOMPFL-GR exploits global shared randomness so all clients sample identical candidates, allowing the federator to simply relay uplink indices to other clients (avoiding a lossy second compression round), while BICOMPFL-PR uses private shared randomness and requires an additional MRC round for downlink. The method extends to conventional FL by composing MRC with stochastic quantizers (e.g., QSGD), for which a contraction property of the resulting biased compressor is proven, enabling convergence guarantees. Theoretical analysis refines MRC bounds for Bernoulli distributions, quantifying the uplink-downlink trade-off and showing how prior accuracy creates inter-round dependency in communication cost.

## Results

BICOMPFL-GR and BICOMPFL-PR achieve 5–32× reduction in total communication cost (bits per parameter per global iteration) compared to non-stochastic baselines while maintaining competitive test accuracy across MNIST, Fashion-MNIST, and CIFAR-10. On MNIST with 4CNN (i.i.d.), the average bitrate is reduced by more than a factor of 1000 versus uncompressed FedAvg and by more than a factor of 32 versus the best bi-directional compression baselines (DoubleSqueeze, Neolithic, LIEC). On CIFAR-10 with 6CNN, BICOMPFL achieves a bitrate smaller by a factor of 5 than the best baselines. All methods reach approximately the same maximum test accuracy; the key differentiator is the communication cost at a given accuracy level.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BICOMPFL-GR and BICOMPFL-PR outperform all seven baselines on communication efficiency while matching their maximum test accuracy. Against uncompressed FedAvg, the average bitrate is reduced by more than a factor of 1000. Against the strongest conventional bi-directional compression methods—DoubleSqueeze, Neolithic, and LIEC—the bitrate is reduced by more than a factor of 32 on MNIST 4CNN (i.i.d.) and by a factor of 5 on CIFAR-10 6CNN. Mem-SGD, CSER, and M3 (with TopK) also achieve substantial compression but remain well above BICOMPFL's communication cost. BICOMPFL-GR consistently converges faster per iteration than BICOMPFL-PR due to the avoidance of an additional MRC compression round in the downlink; however, BICOMPFL-PR with model partitioning (Fixed-SplitDL) recovers much of this gap on i.i.d. data at the cost of n× lower downlink bits. On non-i.i.d. data (Dirichlet α=0.1), BICOMPFL-GR-CFL (conventional FL with stochastic SignSGD) even shows improved accuracy over baselines, attributed to implicit regularization from the Bernoulli prior.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the stochastic FL compression framework of Isik et al. (2024, KLMS), which addressed only uplink compression via Minimal Random Coding (MRC), to the full bi-directional setting by adding principled downlink compression using the same MRC mechanism with the previous global model as side information. It builds on the importance-sampling bounds of Chatterjee and Diaconis (2018) and the probabilistic mask training paradigm of Isik et al. (2023, FedPM), but departs from the dominant deterministic FL compression literature (DoubleSqueeze, Neolithic, LIEC, M3) by exploiting the mirror-descent structure of Bayesian optimization, where the KL-divergence between local posterior and global prior directly determines communication cost. The paper also bridges to conventional FL by composing MRC with stochastic quantizers (e.g., QSGD, SignSGD), proving a contraction property that was absent in prior MRC-based FL analyses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 5–32× total communication reduction while matching state-of-the-art accuracy on MNIST, Fashion-MNIST, and CIFAR-10 makes bi-directional stochastic FL practically viable in bandwidth-constrained cross-silo settings, closing a gap that prior work left open for the downlink. The refined MRC bounds (Lemma 2) and the contraction property for the biased MRC compressor (Lemma 1) are of independent interest and enable convergence guarantees that were previously unavailable for MRC-based FL. Open questions include extending the analysis beyond Bernoulli posteriors to general distribution families, quantifying the privacy-utility trade-off from sampling noise, and adapting the framework to decentralized FL without a central coordinator. The requirement for shared randomness (global or private) remains a practical constraint that limits deployment in fully asynchronous or partially-participating settings.
