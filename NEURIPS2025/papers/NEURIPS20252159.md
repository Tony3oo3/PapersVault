## Problem

Existing video temporal grounding methods are either limited to specific domains and video durations (discriminative DETR-like models) or struggle to scale to long videos due to MLLMs' constrained context windows and memory demands. No prior approach achieves universal grounding across diverse video views (egocentric/exocentric), genres (cooking, sports, movies, games), and lengths (seconds to hours) while also handling complex natural language queries.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252159/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes video frames sampled at 2 fps, a free-form text query (description or question), and their corresponding timestamps. A key design choice is that the model does not decode dense position encodings; instead, it reads out explicitly inserted textual timestamp tokens (e.g., 'timestamp: 42 seconds') from the interleaved sequence, outputting boundaries as 'From s_k seconds to e_k seconds'. For long videos exceeding a frame threshold, the input is partitioned into clips and processed hierarchically, with the final output being a refined temporal interval within the originally sampled timestamp set.

## Method

UniTime adapts a pre-trained MLLM (Qwen2-VL-7B, fine-tuned via LoRA) by interleaving textual timestamp tokens between visual tokens in the input sequence, turning temporal grounding into a retrieval problem over explicit timestamp cues. An adaptive frame scaling mechanism dynamically allocates the per-frame token budget based on video duration: short videos receive higher spatial resolution via frame resizing, while long videos use token compression (bilinear interpolation at the token level) to preserve semantic information under a fixed total token budget. For long videos, a multi-stage coarse-to-fine inference strategy first retrieves relevant segments using coarse-grained timestamps (one per segment), then performs fine-grained localization within the selected segment at higher temporal resolution. Training uses a video-centric paradigm that concatenates all query-answer pairs for a given video into a single sequence with attention masks preventing cross-query interaction, eliminating redundant video re-encoding. The model is pre-trained on a diverse collection of seven temporal grounding datasets spanning egocentric and exocentric views, multiple domains, and durations from ~30s to ~7 minutes.

## Results

On long-video temporal grounding, UniTime-Full achieves 18.41 R1@.5 and 18.80 mIoU on Ego4D-NLQ, and 55.14 R1@.5 and 50.00 mIoU on TaCoS, outperforming the previous best (UniVTG w/PT at 7.54 R1@.5 on Ego4D-NLQ and LD-DETR at 44.31 R1@.5 on TaCoS) by 6.39 and 9.10 points respectively. On short-video benchmarks, UniTime-Full reaches 75.27 R1@.5 on Charades-STA, 53.67 R1@.5 on ANet-Captions, and 76.72 R1@.5 on QVHighlights, surpassing Mr.BLIP (69.31, 53.92, 74.77) across all three. In the zero-shot setting, UniTime-Zero achieves 33.38 mIoU on TaCoS and 52.19 mIoU on Charades-STA, exceeding the best prior zero-shot methods by 15.44 and 3.79 points. As a retrieval module for VideoQA, UniTime-Full improves accuracy to 55.51% on QaEgo4D and 66.50% on MLVU over uniform sampling (49.60% and 60.53%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniTime outperforms all 24 compared baselines across the five primary temporal grounding benchmarks. On long-video Ego4D-NLQ, UniTime-Full (18.41 R1@.5) surpasses the strongest prior method RGNet (12.04) by 6.37 points and UniVTG w/PT (7.54) by 10.87 points; on TaCoS it beats LD-DETR (44.31) and SnAG (44.86) by over 10 points in R1@.5. On short-video Charades-STA, UniTime-Full (75.27 R1@.5) exceeds Mr.BLIP (69.31) and SG-DETR (70.20) by 5–6 points, while on QVHighlights UniTime-SP (77.76) leads Mr.BLIP (74.77) by 2.99 points. The universal UniTime-Full model, trained without benchmark-specific fine-tuning, matches or exceeds the dataset-specific UniTime-SP on 4 of 5 benchmarks, confirming strong cross-domain generalization. In the zero-shot setting (Table 4), UniTime-Zero achieves 50.06 R1@.3 on TaCoS versus 24.59 for Mr.BLIP and 6.48 for UniVTG, a 25-point margin. For downstream VideoQA (Table 9), UniTime-Full improves QaEgo4D accuracy from 49.60% (uniform sampling) to 55.51% and CG-Bench from 33.87% to 40.30%, outperforming UniVTG (50.00%, 34.87%) and TimeSuite (48.80%, 32.47%) as retrieval backends.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Qwen2-VL-7B as the base MLLM and extends the line of explicit timestamp-marking approaches (Mr.BLIP, TimeMarker, VideoLLaMA3) that prepend textual timestamps to video frames. It departs from prior MLLM-based grounding methods by introducing adaptive frame scaling to handle arbitrary video durations within a fixed token budget, and by adding a multi-stage coarse-to-fine inference strategy that first retrieves relevant segments before refining boundaries. Unlike DETR-based methods (Moment-DETR, SG-DETR, LD-DETR) that are dataset-specific and struggle with generalization, UniTime targets a single universal model across views, genres, and lengths. The video-centric training paradigm is a practical departure from query-centric sampling used in prior work, reducing redundant video re-encoding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Universal temporal grounding is a critical prerequisite for retrieval-then-reasoning pipelines in long-video understanding, and this paper demonstrates that a single 7B open-source model can match or exceed closed-source MLLMs (Gemini-2.5-Pro, GPT-4o, Seed1.5-VL) on both short and long video grounding. The technique of interleaving explicit timestamp tokens is simple, model-agnostic, and likely to be widely adopted as a plug-and-play component. Key limitations include reliance on fixed segment lengths for long videos, restriction to temporal grounding data (limiting reasoning ability), and the need for more diverse dense temporal annotations to unlock broader applications like dense video captioning. An open question is whether localization, reasoning, and QA can be unified into a single MLLM framework without the two-stage retrieval-then-answer pipeline.
