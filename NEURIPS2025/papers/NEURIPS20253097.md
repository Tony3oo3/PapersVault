## Problem

Existing large-scale multilingual corpora suffer from outdated Common Crawl snapshots, limited high/medium-resource language coverage, and residual noise from insufficient cleaning. Traditional data cleaning pipelines rely on manually tuned, language-specific heuristic thresholds (e.g., fixed word-count or repetition cutoffs) that fail to generalize across the vast distributional diversity of 2,000+ languages, making them both brittle and computationally expensive to adapt.

## Input / Output

The pipeline ingests raw multilingual web documents from four sources—MaLA, Fineweb, Fineweb-2, and newly extracted Common Crawl dumps (CC-MAIN-2024-22 through CC-MAIN-2024-46)—totaling 21.54TB of newly processed data. For each document, eight statistical features are extracted (word count, character/word repetition ratios, special-character ratio, stopword ratio, flagged-word ratio, GlotLID confidence, and KenLM perplexity), standardized, and fed into an anomaly detector. The output is a per-document anomaly score and a binary keep/remove label, yielding a cleaned 46.72TB corpus spanning 2,282 languages and 159 scripts, ready for direct LLM training without further cleaning.

## Method

The core contribution reframes data cleaning as an unsupervised anomaly detection problem, eliminating the need for per-language threshold tuning. Eight document-level features are extracted, z-score standardized, and scored by Isolation Forest, which assigns each document an anomaly score based on average isolation path length across random binary trees. Documents scoring below an empirically set threshold τ are retained; those above are removed. The authors benchmark four classical detectors (Isolation Forest, One-Class SVM, LOF, K-Means) and find Isolation Forest most robust and least sensitive to hyperparameters across 9 languages and 3 model sizes. The cleaned corpus is validated via LoRA-based continued pretraining on LLaMA-3.2-1B, Qwen-2.5-7B, and Aya-expanse-32B, with gains of up to +9.15 accuracy on SIB-200 and −53.23 NLL on Glot500-c for very-low-resource languages, and a manual quality audit confirming 4.4% residual noise and 5.2% false-positive rate.

## Results

On SIB-200, DCAD-2000-trained models achieve the highest accuracy across all resource levels, with the largest gains for very low-resource languages: Aya-expanse-32B reaches 26.38 (vs. 19.50 for New CC, +6.88) and Qwen-2.5-7B reaches 21.57 (vs. 15.43, +6.14). On Glot500-c (NLL, lower is better), DCAD-2000 reduces NLL by up to 53.23 points for high-resource languages with LLaMA-3.2-1B (373.14 vs. 426.37 for Fineweb-2). On FLORES-200 (BLEU), DCAD-2000 yields up to +3.23 BLEU for high-resource Eng→X translation with Qwen-2.5-7B (18.47 vs. 16.07 for New CC). Across all three benchmarks and all four resource tiers, DCAD-2000 consistently outperforms both Fineweb-2 and New CC baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the dataset baselines, DCAD-2000 consistently outperforms both Fineweb-2 and New CC across all three benchmarks (SIB-200, Glot500-c, FLORES-200) and all four resource tiers for all three model sizes. The largest relative gains appear for very low-resource languages: on SIB-200, Aya-expanse-32B improves from 19.50 (New CC) to 26.38 (+35% relative), and on Glot500-c, LLaMA-3.2-1B reduces NLL from 584.55 (Fineweb-2) to 532.86 (−8.8%). Against the anomaly detection baselines (Table 2), Isolation Forest (the chosen method) achieves the best or tied-best normalized accuracy in 7 out of 9 languages for LLaMA-3.2-1B and 8 out of 9 for Qwen-2.5-7B, while OC_SVM, LOF, and K-Means show more inconsistent performance and greater sensitivity to hyperparameter tuning. In the FineTask comparison (Figure 4), DCAD-2000 also outperforms models trained on MaLA data for French, Chinese, and Turkish, confirming that the integrated multi-source pipeline plus anomaly-detection cleaning yields superior data quality over any single source. The cost-benefit analysis (Appendix F) shows DCAD incurs only ~2 extra minutes and 6 GB more peak memory per 1M documents versus heuristic filtering, while delivering +4.7% absolute accuracy on a Global MMLU subset (48.6% vs. 43.9% for LLaMA-3.2-1B).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of multilingual corpus construction (CulturaX, MaLA, Glotcc, Fineweb-2) and data cleaning pipelines, but departs from the dominant paradigm of fixed-threshold heuristic filtering by reframing document-level quality assessment as an anomaly detection problem. It builds on the feature-extraction ideas from Roots and CulturaX (repetition ratios, stopword ratios, LID scores, perplexity) but replaces their per-language manual threshold tuning with a language-agnostic Isolation Forest scoring mechanism. The dataset itself integrates and re-cleans existing corpora (MaLA, Fineweb, Fineweb-2) alongside newly extracted Common Crawl dumps, positioning DCAD-2000 as a superset that addresses the outdated-snapshot and limited high/medium-resource coverage gaps identified in prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The release of a 46.72TB, 2,282-language, training-ready multilingual corpus with 155 high/medium-resource languages fills a practical gap for researchers who need fresh, cleaned data without re-implementing per-language threshold tuning. The anomaly-detection framing is attractive in principle for its language-agnosticism, but the evaluation is limited to four classical detectors and three model sizes, leaving open whether deep or embedding-based anomaly detection would yield further gains on this 8-dimensional feature space. The paper acknowledges that GlotLID's FastText-based LID has known limitations at this scale, and that the very low-resource tail (2,003 languages) still dominates the distribution, suggesting future work on OCR-based collection and richer semantic features.
