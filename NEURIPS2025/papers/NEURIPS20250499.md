## Problem

Conventional diffusion transformers standardize all training images to fixed, square resolutions (e.g., 256×256), discarding native resolution and aspect ratio information and severely limiting generalization to unseen sizes. This creates a fundamental gap with LLMs, which natively handle variable-length sequences and generalize zero-shot to arbitrary input lengths. The paper asks whether diffusion models can achieve analogous resolution-free flexibility by training directly on images at their original dimensions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250499/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For class-conditional generation, the model takes an ImageNet class label plus a target (height, width) pair and produces a generated image at exactly that resolution and aspect ratio—no resizing or cropping is applied at inference. For text-to-image, it takes a text prompt (encoded by Gemma3-1B) and a target resolution, producing images up to 1024×1024. The DC-AE encoder uses 32× downsampling with 32 latent channels and patch size 1, so a 1024×1024 image yields a 32×32 latent grid of 1024 tokens. Training packs multiple variable-length image latents into a single sequence up to a fixed token budget (131,072 tokens per iteration for C2I), making batch size dynamic.

## Method

NiT builds on the DiT architecture with three key innovations for native-resolution modeling. First, Dynamic Tokenization converts each image at its original resolution into a variable-length token sequence, and multiple images are packed into a single sequence up to a maximum length L, eliminating padding waste. Second, Packed Full-Attention uses FlashAttention-2 with cumulative sequence lengths (CuSeqLens) to restrict self-attention within each image instance without explicit masking, and Packed AdaLN broadcasts per-instance conditioning vectors across their respective token sub-sequences. Third, axial 2D RoPE factorizes positional encoding into independent height and width rotary components, injecting 2D structural priors that generalize across resolutions. The model is trained with flow matching (linear path, α_t=1−t, σ_t=t) on native-resolution ImageNet without any resolution-modifying augmentations, and a single 675M-parameter model simultaneously achieves SOTA FID on both 256×256 (2.03) and 512×512 (1.45) while generalizing zero-shot to 1024×1024 (FID 4.52) and extreme aspect ratios like 9:16 (FID 4.11).

## Results

On the standard ImageNet class-conditional benchmarks, a single NiT-XL model (675M params, 197B token budget) achieves FID 2.03 on 256×256 and FID 1.45 on 512×512, yielding a mean FID (mFID) of 1.74. This is the first single model to simultaneously achieve SOTA on both resolutions, outperforming EDM2-XXL (1.81 FID at 512×512) with half the model size and matching SiT-REPA's mFID (1.75) with only 37.5% of the training token budget. On zero-shot resolution generalization, NiT achieves FID 4.52 at 1024×1024 and 6.51 at 1536×1536, while all baselines (EDM2-L, FlowDCN, FiTv2, SiT-REPA) degrade severely or fail entirely beyond 1024×1024.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the 512×512 benchmark, NiT-XL (1.45 FID) decisively outperforms all baselines, beating the previous SOTA EDM2-XXL (1.81) by 0.36 FID with half the parameters, and SiT-REPA (2.08) by 0.63 FID. On 256×256, NiT-XL (2.03 FID) surpasses SiT-XL (2.06), DiT-XL (2.27), FiTv2-XL (2.26), PixNerd (2.15), and LlamaGen-3B (2.18), but trails VAR-2B (1.73) and SiT-REPA (1.42) on that specific resolution. The critical advantage is that NiT achieves both results with a single model and 197B tokens, whereas all competing diffusion baselines require two independently trained models (cumulative 1428B+ tokens for DiT/SiT, 525B for SiT-REPA). On zero-shot generalization to unseen resolutions (Table 2), NiT achieves FID 4.05/4.52/6.51 at 768/1024/1536, while EDM2-L degrades to 40.74 at 1024 and FiTv2/SiT-REPA fail entirely beyond 1024. On aspect ratio generalization (Table 3), NiT achieves FID 4.11 at 9:16 and 9.90 at 3:1, whereas EDM2-L and FlowDCN produce FIDs of 8.19/39.94 and 8.25/40.72 respectively at those extreme ratios, and SiT-REPA reaches 34.42/114.01.

## Relation to Prior Work

NiT is built directly on the DiT architecture (Peebles & Xie, ICCV 2023) and the flow-matching training objective, but departs fundamentally from the fixed-resolution training paradigm that all prior diffusion transformers (DiT, SiT, FiTv2, SiT-REPA) follow. Its key conceptual borrowing is from LLMs: just as RoPE (Su et al., 2024) enables length generalization in text, NiT adapts RoPE into an axial 2D form to inject spatial structure into variable-length image token sequences, and adopts the packed-sequence + FlashAttention-2 strategy popularized by NaViT (Deghani et al., 2023) and modern VLMs. The paper positions itself as bridging the gap between visual generative modeling and the variable-sequence processing that makes LLMs flexible, arguing that native-resolution training is the missing ingredient that prior multi-resolution strategies (bucket sampling, padding, progressive training) failed to provide.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

NiT demonstrates that a single diffusion transformer trained at native resolution can simultaneously achieve SOTA on both 256×256 and 512×512 ImageNet benchmarks while zero-shot generalizing to 1024×1024 (FID 4.52) and diverse aspect ratios (9:16, 3:1) without any retraining — a capability no prior fixed-resolution or bucket-based method achieves. The ablations convincingly isolate native-resolution training as the critical enabler, showing that capping training resolution at 256 or 512 pixels destroys generalization. However, performance degrades noticeably at 2048×2048 (FID 24.76) and extreme aspect ratios (1:3, 3:1), and the text-to-image results are more modest, leaving open whether this paradigm scales to video generation, broader data domains, and truly extreme resolutions. The 2D RoPE + packed-sequence recipe is likely to be adopted as a standard component in future diffusion transformer architectures.
