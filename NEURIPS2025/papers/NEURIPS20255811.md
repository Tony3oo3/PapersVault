## Problem

Existing monocular depth estimation models produce flying pixels at object edges when depth maps are converted to point clouds: discriminative models smooth discontinuities due to mean-prediction bias, while generative models (e.g., Marigold) rely on VAE compression that blurs edges and fine details. Performing diffusion directly in pixel space avoids VAE artifacts but is computationally expensive and difficult to optimize at high resolutions, as the model struggles to simultaneously capture global semantic structure and fine-grained spatial details.

## Input / Output

The model takes a single RGB image (512×512 or 1024×768) and produces a relative depth map at the same spatial resolution. The input image is consumed in two ways: it is concatenated with a noisy depth sample and fed into the DiT as a conditioning channel, and it is separately encoded by a frozen vision foundation model (e.g., DINOv2 ViT-L/14) to extract semantic tokens that prompt the diffusion process. The output depth map is log-scaled and normalized per-image using 2nd/98th percentiles before being fed into the diffusion pipeline.

## Method

Pixel-Perfect Depth performs Flow Matching diffusion directly in pixel space (no VAE), using a 24-block DiT (hidden dim 1024) trained from scratch. Two key designs address the difficulty of high-resolution pixel-space generation: (1) Semantics-Prompted DiT (SP-DiT) extracts L2-normalized semantic tokens from a frozen vision foundation model encoder, bilinearly interpolates them to match DiT token resolution, and fuses them into the DiT token sequence via an MLP, providing global semantic guidance that stabilizes training and preserves structural coherence; (2) Cascade DiT Design (Cas-DiT) uses a coarse-to-fine token strategy where the first 12 blocks operate at patch size 16 (fewer tokens, global structure) and then an MLP expansion + reshape increases tokens to patch size 8 for the remaining 12 SP-DiT blocks (fine details), reducing compute by ~30% while improving accuracy. The architecture is purely transformer-based with no convolutional layers, and training uses MSE velocity loss plus a gradient matching loss on synthetic depth data (Hypersim, UrbanSyn, UnrealStereo4K, VKITTI, TartanAir).

## Results

On five zero-shot relative depth benchmarks, the 1024×768 model achieves AbsRel of 4.1 (NYUv2), 7.0 (KITTI), 4.3 (ETH3D), 4.6 (ScanNet), and 6.8 (DIODE), outperforming all published generative depth models on every metric. The 512×512 variant achieves AbsRel of 4.3, 8.0, 4.5, 4.5, and 7.0 respectively, with inference time of 0.14 s on an RTX 4090. On the proposed edge-aware point cloud metric (Chamfer Distance on Hypersim), the method achieves 0.08, substantially below GT(VAE) at 0.12 and all competing models (0.14–0.18), confirming flying-pixel-free depth maps.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among generative baselines, Ours (1024) achieves the lowest AbsRel on all five benchmarks, beating the strongest generative competitor Lotus by 1.3 on NYUv2 (4.1 vs 5.4), 1.5 on KITTI (7.0 vs 8.5), 1.6 on ETH3D (4.3 vs 5.9), 1.3 on ScanNet (4.6 vs 5.9), and 3.0 on DIODE (6.8 vs 9.8). Against the top discriminative model Depth Anything v2 (62M), Ours (1024) wins on KITTI (7.0 vs 7.4 AbsRel), ETH3D (4.3 vs 13.1 AbsRel), and ScanNet (4.6 vs 6.5 AbsRel), while being marginally behind on NYUv2 δ₁ (97.7 vs 97.9) and DIODE AbsRel (6.8 vs 6.6). On the edge-aware Chamfer Distance metric, Ours (0.08) outperforms all methods including GT(VAE) (0.12), Depth Pro (0.14), GeoWizard (0.16), Marigold (0.17), and Depth Anything v2 (0.18), demonstrating that pixel-space diffusion eliminates the flying-pixel artifacts inherent to VAE-based latent diffusion. The comparison with REPA (Table 5) shows that Semantics-Prompted DiT substantially outperforms representation alignment (e.g., 4.3 vs 17.6 AbsRel on NYUv2), attributed to direct semantic integration at both training and inference time.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of fine-tuning Stable Diffusion in latent space (Marigold, GeoWizard, Lotus, DepthFM) by performing diffusion directly in pixel space with a DiT architecture, eliminating the VAE compression that the authors identify as the root cause of flying pixels in generative depth models. It builds on DiT (Peebles & Xie, ICCV 2023) and Flow Matching (Lipman et al., 2022) as its generative backbone, but introduces two key architectural innovations: Semantics-Prompted DiT, which injects normalized features from vision foundation models (DINOv2, VGGT, MAE, Depth Anything v2) into the diffusion process, and a Cascade DiT Design that progressively increases token count from coarse to fine stages. The paper also positions itself against REPA (Yu et al., 2024), showing that direct semantic prompting outperforms implicit representation alignment for this task.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete practical bottleneck—flying pixels at object edges—that limits the use of depth estimation in 3D reconstruction, robotic manipulation, and immersive content creation, and provides a clean diagnosis (VAE compression) with a working solution (pixel-space diffusion). The proposed edge-aware Chamfer Distance metric fills a gap in evaluation, since standard AbsRel/δ₁ metrics are dominated by flat regions and cannot capture edge quality. Key open questions remain: temporal consistency for video depth (the model flickers across frames), inference speed relative to discriminative models (140 ms vs. 18 ms for Depth Anything v2), and whether the SP-DiT semantic prompting strategy generalizes to other dense prediction tasks beyond depth.
