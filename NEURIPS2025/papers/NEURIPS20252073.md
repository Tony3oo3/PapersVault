## Problem

Prototype-based class incremental semantic segmentation methods assign a single fixed prototype per learned class to replay old knowledge, but this is insufficient when class feature distributions are naturally multimodal and when continual parameter updates cause anisotropic shifts in old-class features. The result is a mismatch between the stored unimodal representation and the true evolving distribution, leading to degraded performance especially in long-term incremental scenarios.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252073/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images and produces pixel-wise segmentation masks over all previously seen classes plus the current novel classes. At each incremental step, only the current step's class labels are available; all other classes (both previously learned and future) are labeled as background, making the task an overlapped incremental setting. The model operates with either a DeepLab-V3 + ResNet-101 CNN backbone or a Swin-B Transformer backbone, and the GMM-generated pseudo-features are injected as additional training signals alongside the real features to the classifier head.

## Method

After each training step, the feature extractor is frozen and a per-class Gaussian Mixture Model (K=3 components) is fitted via the EM algorithm to capture the full multimodal feature distribution of each learned class. These stored GMMs generate pseudo-features for old classes in subsequent steps, replacing the single-prototype replay. A Dynamic Adjustment strategy periodically re-fits the stored GMMs using old-class features extracted by the current (updated) model, adapting to anisotropic distribution shifts caused by parameter updates. A Gaussian-based Representation Constraint (GRC) loss pushes new-class feature centroids away from the geometric centroids of the most similar old classes (computed as weighted combinations of GMM means), maintaining discriminative separation. The total training objective combines segmentation loss, knowledge distillation loss (OCFM from STAR), and the GRC loss with balancing coefficients α=5 and β=0.05.

## Results

On Pascal VOC 2012 (ResNet-101), CoGaMiD achieves 73.8 mIoU on VOC 15-1, 59.4 on VOC 2-2 (10 steps), and 45.0 on VOC 1-1 (20 steps), outperforming the strongest prototype-based baseline STAR by 1.2, 3.8, and 8.5 mIoU respectively. On ADE20K (ResNet-101), CoGaMiD reaches 35.2 mIoU on the most challenging 100-5 scenario (11 steps), surpassing STAR by 1.5 mIoU. With a Swin-B backbone, the advantage grows substantially: on VOC 1-1, CoGaMiD achieves 54.2 mIoU versus STAR's 44.5, a 9.7 mIoU margin, and on ADE20K 100-5 it reaches 37.7 mIoU versus 36.3.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoGaMiD consistently outperforms all 17 compared baselines across every evaluated scenario on both Pascal VOC 2012 and ADE20K. The closest competitor is STAR [6], the prior prototype-replay method; CoGaMiD surpasses it by 1.2 mIoU on VOC 15-1 (73.8 vs 72.6) but the gap widens dramatically in long-term settings: +3.8 mIoU on VOC 2-2 (59.4 vs 55.6) and +8.5 mIoU on VOC 1-1 (45.0 vs 36.5) with ResNet-101. With Swin-B, the advantage over STAR on VOC 1-1 reaches 9.7 mIoU (54.2 vs 44.5). Exemplar-based methods (SSUL, MicroSeg, CoinSeg) and distillation-based methods (DKD, IDEC, PLOP, MiB) are all surpassed by a wide margin, with the largest gaps appearing in the 10- and 20-step scenarios where catastrophic forgetting is most severe. On ADE20K 100-5 (11 steps), CoGaMiD leads STAR by 1.5 mIoU (35.2 vs 33.7) and the next-best method NeST by 3.7 mIoU (35.2 vs 31.5), confirming the advantage generalizes to larger-scale benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the prototype-replay lineage in class incremental semantic segmentation (CISS), building directly on STAR [6] (which uses OCFM knowledge distillation and single-prototype replay) and the authors' own prior work [60] (adaptive prototype replay). The key conceptual departure is replacing each class's single prototype (one mean + variance) with a full Gaussian Mixture Model (K means + K covariances), motivated by the observation that real class feature distributions are often multimodal and anisotropic. A Dynamic Adjustment strategy periodically re-estimates GMM parameters using current-model features of old classes, and a Gaussian-based Representation Constraint loss pushes new-class centroids away from the geometric centroids of stored GMMs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method provides a principled, storage-efficient (0.12M vs. 10M for exemplars) alternative to both exemplar replay and single-prototype replay, with particularly strong gains in long-term incremental scenarios (e.g., +8.5 mIoU over STAR on VOC 1-1). Open questions include whether the EM-based GMM estimation scales efficiently to very high-dimensional feature spaces or hundreds of classes, whether the dynamic adjustment interval e can be made adaptive rather than fixed, and whether the approach transfers to other incremental tasks beyond segmentation (e.g., detection, panoptic segmentation). The reliance on a strong knowledge distillation baseline (OCFM) means the method's gains are partly entangled with the distillation choice.
