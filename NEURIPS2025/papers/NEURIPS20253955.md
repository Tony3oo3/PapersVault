## Problem

Current LLM-based social simulations operate under a behaviorist 'demographics in, behavior out' paradigm, producing surface-plausible stances without modeling the internal belief dynamics that drive human reasoning. This results in agents that lack causal traceability, counterfactual sensitivity, and individual-level heterogeneity, making them unreliable for policy simulation and stakeholder modeling. The paper identifies a fundamental evaluation gap: existing benchmarks measure output fluency and stance accuracy rather than the structural integrity of reasoning processes.

## Input / Output

The framework consumes semi-structured interview transcripts (natural-language Q&A eliciting causal explanations like 'why do you support X?') and parses them into directed acyclic graphs where nodes encode concepts (fairness, safety, privacy) and edges encode directional causal relations with confidence and polarity scores. Given a do-calculus intervention (e.g., do(Transparency = high)), the system produces updated posterior probabilities over downstream belief nodes and a traceable reasoning path from intervention to final stance. The RECAP benchmark additionally requires human-annotated causal motifs and belief chains as ground-truth references for structural comparison.

## Method

GenMinds is a symbolic-neural hybrid framework that models individual reasoning as a causal Bayesian network (CBN) constructed from 'cognitive motifs'—minimal reusable causal units (e.g., Transparency → Crime Rate → Public Safety) extracted from interview transcripts via LLM-guided parsing. Belief revision is performed as forward inference over the CBN using do-calculus, so that a policy intervention propagates probabilistic updates through the graph to yield revised stances with full causal traceability. The RECAP benchmark evaluates reasoning fidelity along three axes: motif alignment (structural similarity between human and model belief graphs), belief coherence (internal consistency of the reasoning trace), and counterfactual robustness (sensible belief updates under hypothetical interventions). The key architectural insight is that reasoning should be represented as persistent, revisable, compositional causal structures rather than ephemeral token-level generation, enabling agents to reuse belief motifs across novel scenarios without retraining.

## Results

This is a position paper with no empirical experiments or quantitative benchmarks. The only quantitative illustration is a toy example of belief revision via do-calculus on a causal belief network, where an intervention do(Transparency = high) shifts P(Privacy Concern) from 0.7 to 0.3 and P(Opposition to Surveillance) from 0.7 to 0.2. The paper's main contribution is conceptual: it defines reasoning fidelity (traceability, counterfactual adaptability, motif compositionality) and proposes the RECAP evaluation schema, but does not report scores on any dataset.

## Baselines & Comparisons

No empirical baselines are compared; the paper is a position paper that does not run experiments. The closest analogue to a comparison is Table 1, a qualitative paradigm contrast between the existing 'demographics in, behavior out' approach (token-level generation, static beliefs, output-fluency evaluation, averaged social representation) and the proposed GenMinds framework (structured belief graphs, revisable causal updates, reasoning-fidelity evaluation, divergent positional cognition). The paper identifies prior work on persona prompting, RLHF, chain-of-thought, and multi-agent LLM simulations as the 'existing paradigm' it critiques, but does not benchmark against them quantitatively. The RECAP benchmark is introduced as a design specification (motif alignment, belief coherence, counterfactual robustness metrics) rather than a populated dataset with reported scores.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself against the dominant behaviorist paradigm in LLM social simulation (persona prompting, RLHF, chain-of-thought generation), arguing that these methods optimize surface-level plausibility rather than internal reasoning structure. It builds conceptually on Wong et al.'s [99] dual-process framework for translating natural language into structured probabilistic reasoning, and draws on cognitive science literature on causal learning, compositional cognition, and Theory of Mind to motivate a cognitivist alternative. The proposed GenMinds framework and RECAP benchmark depart from existing social simulation evaluations (which assess stance accuracy or dialogue fluency) by introducing reasoning fidelity—traceability, counterfactual adaptability, and motif compositionality—as the core evaluation axes, a dimension the paper argues is absent from current benchmarks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As LLM-based agents are increasingly deployed in policy simulation, civic deliberation, and stakeholder modeling, the paper's critique that current systems produce 'flattened' and 'stereotyped' outputs without causal grounding carries significant practical implications for trust and fairness in high-stakes applications. However, as a position paper, it presents no empirical validation: GenMinds is a conceptual scaffold and RECAP is a proposed schema rather than an implemented benchmark, leaving open whether causal belief graphs can be reliably extracted from natural language at scale and whether the three fidelity dimensions are jointly measurable. The authors themselves acknowledge that causality alone cannot capture associative, analogical, or emotional reasoning, and that constructing CBNs from transcripts remains an unsolved NLP challenge.
