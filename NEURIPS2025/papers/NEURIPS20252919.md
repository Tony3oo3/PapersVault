## Problem

Increasing denoising steps is the standard test-time scaling axis for diffusion models, but yields rapidly diminishing returns. Optimizing the noise trajectory—the sequence of injected noise vectors at each reverse step—is a more promising but largely unexplored direction, as the search space is high-dimensional, noise-outcome interactions are complex, and full trajectory evaluations are computationally prohibitive. No prior work had addressed higher-order search over the entire noise trajectory for SDE-based diffusion models with arbitrary non-differentiable rewards.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained diffusion model (EDM at 64×64 for class-conditional generation, Stable Diffusion v1.5 or SDXL at 512×512 for text-to-image), a conditioning signal (ImageNet class label or natural language prompt), and an arbitrary black-box reward function (e.g., classifier probability, CLIP cosine similarity, brightness, JPEG compressibility, object counting). It produces a single generated image whose quality is optimized for the specified reward, operating entirely at inference time with no model retraining. The number of denoising steps T is fixed (18 for EDM, 50 for DDIM), and the initial noise x_T is drawn from the standard Gaussian prior.

## Method

The paper first formalizes T-step reverse diffusion as a finite-horizon MDP with a terminal reward, showing that Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) over the resulting denoising tree approximates exhaustive search but is impractical due to quadratic NFE scaling in T and restriction to a fixed set of N noise candidates per step. To overcome this, the MDP is relaxed into a sequence of T independent contextual bandits, where each timestep is solved greedily using Tweedie's formula to estimate the clean sample for candidate scoring. The proposed ε-greedy algorithm, at each timestep, samples N candidates from a mixture distribution: with probability ε it draws globally from N(0, σ_t²I), and with probability 1−ε it draws locally from a λ√(2d)-radius ball around the current pivot, then hill-climbs over K iterations. A key empirical finding is that the algorithm naturally explores globally at extreme timesteps (where the reward landscape is near-flat) and exploits locally at intermediate steps (where de-mixing occurs and the reward is most sensitive to perturbations), confirmed by tracking the average iteration at which random draws are selected and by estimating the Lipschitz constant of the reward as a function of σ_t. A regret bound is provided showing sublinear decay O((εNK)^(−1/d)) in the number of global exploration samples per step.

## Results

On class-conditional ImageNet 64×64 generation (EDM, 18 steps), ε-greedy noise trajectory search achieves 0.9885 classifier probability, 0.9813 brightness, and 0.7208 compressibility, outperforming MCTS (0.9666/0.7575/0.5395) with only 1440 NFEs versus MCTS's 3888. On Stable Diffusion v1.5 text-to-image (50 steps, 512×512), ε-greedy reaches 0.2973 CLIP score, 0.7110 brightness, and 0.8052 compressibility, exceeding MCTS by up to 18% on CLIP. The method delivers up to 164% improvement in sample quality over vanilla sampling (e.g., classifier reward: 0.9885 vs. 0.3778) while remaining computationally linear in the number of denoising steps.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares ε-greedy against four baselines: rejection (best-of-N) sampling, beam search, zero-order search, and MCTS. ε-greedy decisively outperforms all baselines on every reward function and both model architectures. Against MCTS (the strongest baseline, approximating exhaustive tree search), ε-greedy achieves 0.9885 vs. 0.9666 classifier probability and 0.7208 vs. 0.5395 compressibility on EDM, while using only 37% of MCTS's NFEs (1440 vs. 3888). Against zero-order search (the closest algorithmic relative, which is ε-greedy with ε=0), ε-greedy improves classifier reward from 0.6261 to 0.9885 and compressibility from 0.3751 to 0.7208, demonstrating that the global exploration component (random Normal draws with probability ε) is critical. Against beam search and best-of-4 sampling, ε-greedy leads by 0.43 and 0.44 on classifier reward respectively, confirming that step-level noise optimization far surpasses trajectory-level selection from a fixed candidate set. On Stable Diffusion, ε-greedy similarly dominates, reaching 0.2973 CLIP score vs. MCTS's 0.2885 and zero-order search's 0.2689.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the test-time scaling framework for diffusion models initiated by Ma et al. (2025), which restricted search to the initial noise in ODE-based sampling, to the full SDE setting where noise is injected at every denoising step. The key conceptual move is casting denoising as a finite-horizon MDP and then relaxing it to a sequence of independent contextual bandits, drawing on the ε-greedy strategy from classical bandit theory (Sutton & Barto, 1998). This departs from prior reward-optimization approaches that either require differentiable rewards (gradient guidance) or expensive fine-tuning, and from T-SCEND (Zhang et al., 2025), which the authors critique for not implementing true MCTS. The ε-greedy algorithm is a minimal modification of zero-order search—adding a probability ε of drawing a fresh global sample—yet this small change yields the paper's central performance gains.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first practical method for optimizing the full noise trajectory in SDE-based diffusion with arbitrary non-differentiable rewards, achieving up to 164% improvement over vanilla sampling while requiring only linear (not quadratic) NFEs in the number of timesteps. The finding that optimal search should be globally exploratory at extreme timesteps and locally exploitative at intermediate (de-mixing) steps provides a new design principle for inference-time algorithms in iterative generative models. Open questions include whether the greedy per-step selection can be improved to avoid the observed non-monotonic scaling with N, whether the approach extends to video or 3D generation where the search space is vastly larger, and whether the Tweedie-based surrogate reward can be replaced by learned value functions for further gains.
