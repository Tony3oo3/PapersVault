## Problem

Test-time noise optimization in diffusion models (e.g., ReNO) significantly improves generation quality but incurs prohibitive per-sample inference costs (10+ minutes per image). Directly fine-tuning step-distilled generators with reward signals is intractable because the KL regularization to the base model requires intractable Jacobian determinants, leading to reward hacking. The paper asks whether the benefits of test-time noise optimization can be amortized into a one-time post-training stage without modifying the frozen generator.

## Input / Output

The method takes standard Gaussian noise x_0 ~ N(0, I) in the latent space and a text prompt as input. The noise hypernetwork f_φ produces a residual perturbation Δx_0, which is added to x_0 to form the modulated initialization x̂_0 = x_0 + Δx_0. This modulated noise is then passed through the frozen step-distilled generator g_θ (1–4 NFEs) to produce the final RGB image. Notably, the same initial noise is shared between base and HyperNoise generation, enabling direct per-sample comparison, and the method generalizes across inference timesteps despite being trained only with one-step generation.

## Method

HyperNoise introduces a lightweight LoRA-based noise hypernetwork f_φ that predicts an optimized initial noise distribution p_0^φ for a frozen distilled generator g_θ, rather than modifying g_θ's parameters. The theoretical core shows that an optimal tilted noise distribution p_0* ∝ p_0 · exp(r(g_θ(x_0))) exists and that the KL divergence D_KL(p_0^φ ‖ p_0) in noise space reduces to a tractable L2 penalty ½‖f_φ(x_0)‖² under a Lipschitz condition (L < 1) on f_φ, via Stein's Lemma and a log-determinant bound. The final training objective is L_noise(φ) = E_{x_0}[½‖f_φ(x_0)‖² − r(g_θ(x_0 + f_φ(x_0)))], which requires only base noise samples, the frozen generator, and the reward function—no target data. The Data Processing Inequality provides a principled guarantee that minimizing the noise-space KL upper-bounds the data-space KL, preventing reward hacking. At inference, a single forward pass through f_φ followed by g_θ yields the reward-aligned image with negligible added latency.

## Results

On the GenEval benchmark, HyperNoise consistently improves GenEval Mean scores across all three distilled models: SD-Turbo from 0.49 to 0.57, SANA-Sprint from 0.70 to 0.75, and FLUX-Schnell from 0.68 to 0.72. These gains are achieved with minimal added inference latency (0.3s for SD-Turbo/SANA-Sprint, 0.9s for FLUX-Schnell), compared to 20–40s for ReNO, 10–15s for Best-of-N, and 95s for LLM-based prompt optimization. For SANA-Sprint, HyperNoise matches the GenEval Mean of LLM-based prompt optimization (0.75) while being 300× faster, and recovers roughly half the gap to ReNO (0.75 vs 0.81).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ReNO (test-time noise optimization), HyperNoise recovers a substantial but not complete portion of the quality gains: 0.57 vs 0.63 on SD-Turbo, 0.75 vs 0.81 on SANA-Sprint, and 0.72 vs 0.76 on FLUX-Schnell in GenEval Mean, while running 33–300× faster (0.3s vs 20–40s). Against LLM-based prompt optimization, HyperNoise matches SANA-Sprint exactly (0.75 Mean) and is within 0.02 on SD-Turbo (0.57 vs 0.59), but is 300× faster (0.3s vs 95s) and requires no external LLM calls. Against Best-of-N sampling, HyperNoise is slightly below on SD-Turbo (0.57 vs 0.60) and SANA-Sprint (0.75 vs 0.79) but again at a fraction of the inference cost. Against direct LoRA fine-tuning of the base model (Table 2), HyperNoise clearly wins: 0.75 vs 0.67 GenEval Mean for one-step SANA-Sprint, and the gap widens at more steps (0.77 vs 0.62 at four steps), demonstrating that the noise-space KL regularization prevents the reward-hacking and manifold divergence that plagues direct fine-tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends ReNO (Eyring et al., NeurIPS 2024), which performs per-sample reward-guided noise optimization at inference time for distilled generators, by amortizing that optimization into a one-time post-training stage via a lightweight LoRA-based noise hypernetwork. Theoretically, the tilted noise distribution is shown to be a special case of the stochastic optimal control framework of Uehara et al. (2024) for continuous-time diffusion models, collapsing to a simpler form when the generator is one-step. The key conceptual departure from prior reward fine-tuning work (DPO, direct reward backpropagation) is operating in noise space rather than data space, where the KL regularization becomes a tractable L2 penalty via Stein's Lemma, sidestepping the intractable Jacobian determinants that plague direct fine-tuning of distilled generators.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work makes reward-aligned generation practical for fast, step-distilled models by reducing inference overhead from minutes (ReNO) or tens of seconds (Best-of-N) to near-baseline latency, while the noise-space KL regularization provides a principled mechanism to prevent reward hacking that direct fine-tuning of distilled models is prone to. The multi-step generalization (trained on 1-step, effective at 2 and 4 steps) suggests the learned noise modulation captures a robust property of the generator's input manifold. Open questions include whether the approach extends to video or 3D generation, how sensitive it is to reward model quality (the authors note this as a key limitation), and whether the L2 approximation of the KL holds for more aggressive noise modifications or larger architectural changes.
