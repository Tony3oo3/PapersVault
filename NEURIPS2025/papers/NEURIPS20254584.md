## Problem

Prior work showed that prepending metadata like URLs to training documents can accelerate LLM pretraining, but it remained unclear which specific metadata types (URL, quality scores, topic/format domains) are actually effective and under what inference conditions benefits emerge. This paper systematically evaluates these metadata types on a real-world corpus to identify where context conditioning helps, where it is neutral, and how it can be leveraged for controllable generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254584/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During training, each document from FineWeb-Edu is optionally prepended with a metadata string (full URL, quality score 0–5, or topic/format domain label) wrapped in special <boc>/<eoc> tokens; the context tokens are excluded from the next-token loss. At inference, the model accepts a text prompt plus an optional context string and a scalar steering factor γ, producing a generated continuation. The 90/10 uniform mixture of context-prepended and context-free sequences ensures any checkpoint can perform standard context-free generation without a separate cooldown phase.

## Method

The paper introduces a context-conditioned pretraining scheme where metadata is inserted between dedicated boundary tokens before each document, with the loss computed only over the document text. A key design choice is a uniform 90/10 interleave of context-prepended and context-free documents throughout training, replacing MeCo's two-phase schedule so that intermediate checkpoints remain immediately usable. At inference, the authors adapt classifier-free guidance from diffusion models to LLM token generation: the sampling logits are computed as ctx-free logits + γ·(ctx-conditioned logits − ctx-free logits), where γ=0 recovers standard generation, γ=1 gives plain context conditioning, and γ>1 amplifies the contextual signal. The central empirical finding is that only URL metadata accelerates training (40% fewer tokens to match downstream performance), while topic/format domains, though useless for speedup, provide human-interpretable steering of generation style and content at inference time.

## Results

On 5-shot evaluation across 9 benchmarks, URL-conditioned pretraining achieves a 47.8 average score versus 46.7 for standard pretraining, with the largest gains on ARC-Easy (71.7 vs 68.6) and CSQA (46.8 vs 45.1). The headline training-efficiency result is a 40% acceleration: the URL-conditioned model matches the 100B-token standard model's downstream performance using only 60B tokens. In 0-shot evaluation, the advantage shrinks to 46.9 vs 46.7, confirming that URL conditioning benefits emerge primarily with longer prompts. Context-guided generation (γ=1.5) further improves LLM-judge scores, e.g., 9.07 vs 8.01 for health-topic continuations with DI context on the conditioned model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its uniform-mixture context-conditioned pretraining against three baselines: standard pretraining, a positional-token ablation, and the MeCo two-phase method from Gao et al. [3]. URL-conditioned pretraining outperforms standard pretraining by +1.1 points in 5-shot average (47.8 vs 46.7), with the gap concentrated on ARC-Easy (+3.1) and CSQA (+1.7), while in 0-shot the difference is negligible (46.9 vs 46.7). The MeCo baseline scores 46.7 in 5-shot, matching standard pretraining and failing to reproduce the 33% speedup reported in the original MeCo paper; the authors attribute this to differences in dataset, model, and evaluation framework (LM-Eval-Harness vs OLMES). Combining URL with quality scores or domain information actually degrades performance below standard (46.1 and 46.7 respectively), suggesting conflicting signals between the metadata types. The positional-token ablation (<boc><eoc> with empty context) confirms that the observed gains come from the metadata content itself, not from the added tokens.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the metadata-conditioned pretraining line initiated by Allen-Zhu & Li and Gao et al. (MeCo), which showed that prepending URL domains accelerates training, by systematically comparing URL, quality score, and topic/format domain metadata on real-world data (FineWeb-Edu) rather than synthetic corpora. It departs from MeCo's two-phase 90/10 cooldown schedule in favor of a uniform mixture, enabling any checkpoint to be used for context-free inference. For the inference-time steering component, the paper adapts classifier-free guidance—originally from diffusion models and previously applied to context-free LLMs by Sanchez et al. and He et al.—to the context-conditioned pretraining setting, where the 'condition' is the metadata learned during training rather than an external prompt.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The clean separation between metadata that accelerates training (URL only) and metadata that enables interpretable inference-time steering (topic/format domains) gives practitioners a concrete decision framework for what metadata to include and when to leverage it. However, all findings come from a single 1.5B-parameter model trained on 100B tokens of FineWeb-Edu, and the authors could not reproduce MeCo's reported speedup, leaving open whether the 40% acceleration and the URL-specificity generalize to larger scales or different corpora. The mechanism behind why URLs specifically help (early-layer attention to domain tokens vs. later attention sinks on shared prefixes) remains unexplained, and the interaction between metadata types producing conflicting signals is an open question for future work.
