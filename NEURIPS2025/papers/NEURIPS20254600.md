## Problem

Alaei's (2014) ex-ante relaxation framework for multi-agent revenue maximization relies on an OCRS rounding step that requires element activations to be independent, limiting it to settings without inter-buyer constraints or correlated item values. Ten years later, with a richer OCRS toolkit and modern multi-agent analysis techniques, the paper asks whether an interim relaxation combined with a more flexible rounding scheme can yield approximately optimal mechanisms for a much broader class of feasibility constraints, including matroid and knapsack constraints with both per-agent and per-item restrictions.

## Input / Output

The framework consumes a BIC-IR interim allocation/payment pair (π, q) that is feasible in expectation and α-approximate, together with a (b,c)-selectable tCRS or tOCRS for the feasibility set F, and outputs a BIC-IR mechanism (sequential for tOCRS, non-sequential for tCRS) guaranteeing α/(b(c−ε)) approximation. The key structural requirement is that the two-level stochastic process induces element activations where elements in the same row (same agent) are conditionally independent given that agent's sampled valuation, while rows are independent — a relaxation of the full independence assumed by standard OCRSs. When only black-box access to the tCRS/tOCRS is available, the mechanism is implemented via a Bernoulli division factory with O(poly(Σ|V_i|, m, 1/ε)) expected queries.

## Method

The core technical contribution is the two-level contention resolution scheme (tCRS/tOCRS), which generalizes standard CRSs/OCRSs to handle structured dependence: elements in the same row are correlated conditioned on a row-specific random variable, while rows remain independent. The framework rounds an interim relaxation (feasible in expectation, computed via a polynomial-size LP) to an ex-post feasible mechanism by sequentially or jointly activating elements per the two-level process and querying the tCRS/tOCRS, then applying a final Bernoulli coin to balance selection probabilities. For Vertical-Horizontal constraints (e.g., matroid-on-agents × matroid-on-items), a general reduction composes existing CRSs/OCRSs as black boxes to yield a (b, c·c′)-selectable tCRS/tOCRS. For knapsack and multi-choice knapsack constraints, the paper constructs (b, 1/(2+8b))-selectable and (b, 1/(2+7b))-selectable tOCRSs from scratch, with efficient implementations via approximate coin-flipping. This yields, for example, a sequential BIC-IR mechanism with a 2e/(e−1) ≈ 3.16 approximation for matroid VH constraints, improving the prior best sequential guarantee of 70.

## Results

The paper's main quantitative contributions are theoretical approximation guarantees for revenue-maximizing mechanisms. For matroid Vertical-Horizontal constraints, the framework yields a sequential BIC-IR mechanism with a 2e/(e−1) ≈ 3.16 approximation to optimal revenue, dramatically improving on the previously best-known sequential guarantee of 70 (Cai & Zhao, 2017). For Knapsack constraints, the framework gives a 10-approximation; for Multi-Choice Knapsack (each agent receives at most one item), a 9-approximation; and for arbitrary valuations under a knapsack constraint, also a 9-approximation. The constructed tOCRS for Knapsack is (b, 1/(2+8b))-selectable, implying a (1, 0.1)-selectable OCRS that improves on Feldman et al.'s 0.085-selectable OCRS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is against Alaei's [Ala14] ex-ante relaxation framework, which could not handle inter-buyer constraints or correlated item values; the new interim relaxation with tOCRS rounding subsumes and extends Alaei's approach. For the matroid VH setting, the 3.16-approximation improves over Cai and Zhao's [CZ17] 70-approximation by a factor of ~22, though Cai-Zhao's mechanism has additional simplicity properties (posted-price/two-part tariff) that this paper does not guarantee. For the Knapsack OCRS, the paper's (1, 0.1)-selectable OCRS improves on Feldman et al.'s [FSZ21] 0.085-selectable OCRS but remains below Jiang et al.'s [JMZ22] state-of-the-art 0.319-selectable OCRS (which is non-greedy and non-monotone). The paper's Stochastic Knapsack OCRS (Theorem 6) is always better than Feldman et al. and better than Jiang et al. when the maximum weight parameter k* is small, while maintaining the desirable greedy and monotone properties that Jiang et al.'s scheme lacks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Alaei's [Ala14] multi-to-single agent reduction, which used an ex-ante relaxation and an OCRS (in disguise) for rounding, but replaces the ex-ante relaxation with an interim relaxation (feasible in expectation) and introduces two-level OCRSs (tOCRSs) to handle the correlation between element activations within the same agent. The tOCRS/tCRS framework generalizes both the CRSs of Chekuri et al. [CVZ14] and the OCRSs of Feldman et al. [FSZ21] by allowing structured dependence in element arrivals, while the interim LP formulation follows the variable structure of Cai et al. [CDW12a] but relaxes the exponential ex-post feasibility constraints to polynomial-size in-expectation constraints. The paper also extends the framework to multi-parameter procurement auctions, connecting to the stochastic knapsack OCRS literature of Jiang et al. [JMZ22].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework unifies and significantly extends Alaei's reduction by handling inter-agent constraints (e.g., matroid constraints on which agents receive which items) that were previously intractable, yielding a 2e/(e-1) ≈ 3.16 sequential mechanism for matroid VH constraints versus the prior best of 70 for a special case. The two-level OCRS abstraction is a natural and reusable tool that could apply to other Bayesian online optimization problems with structured dependence. Open questions include whether the approximation gaps (e.g., 1/10 for knapsack tOCRS vs. 1/(3+e^{-2}) for standard OCRS) can be closed, whether the framework extends to non-additive valuations beyond the meta-item trick, and whether the LP (LP1) can be solved efficiently for all relevant polytopes P_F.
