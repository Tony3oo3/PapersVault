## Problem

Task vector arithmetic for VLM unlearning is unstable because individual task vectors are highly sensitive to fine-tuning configurations, producing prediction-level variance that correlates negatively with unlearning effectiveness. While ensembling multiple task vectors reduces this variance, explicitly generating and aggregating many functions is computationally prohibitive. The paper asks how to efficiently capture the full space of task-vector interpolations induced by diverse fine-tuning strategies without the cost of explicit function-level ensembling.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained VLM (e.g., CLIP ViT-B/32, B/16, or L/14) with parameters θ₀ and Q=30 task vectors obtained by fine-tuning on the forget set under different RandAugment configurations, forming a (Q−1)-simplex in an m-dimensional parameter space (m=86M for ViT-B/16). At inference, an input image x is mapped to class probabilities in [0,1]^C via the closed-form ensemble. For deployment without ensemble overhead, the method optionally distills the ensemble into a single task vector τ⁻, yielding a standard VLM with parameters θ₀ − λτ⁻.

## Method

The core idea is to model the space of Q task vectors as a (Q−1)-simplex and derive a closed-form ensemble over infinitely many functions whose parameters are uniformly sampled from this simplex. Using a second-order Taylor expansion of the VLM function around θ₀, the ensemble reduces to computing the Jacobian, class-specific Hessians, and the expected outer product E[ττᵀ] over the simplex, which has a closed form via Dirichlet distribution moments (Theorem 1). This yields an expression that interpolates between the quadratic terms of Vector Uniform Merge and Function Ensemble, controlled by the Dirichlet concentration parameter α. An advanced aggregation scheme (Theorem 2) replaces mean pooling with the probability of at least one class success across infinite trials, and variance is controlled by optimizing vertex importance weights or small perturbations within an ε-ball (Corollary 1 + Eq. 11), linking the ensemble to the bias-variance trade-off.

## Results

On the standard unlearning protocol (Table 1), the proposed task simplex method achieves average forget-set accuracy of 15.20% (ViT-B/32), 12.17% (ViT-B/16), and 9.98% (ViT-L/14), while maintaining retain-set (ImageNet) accuracy at ~95% of the pre-trained model. Compared to the strongest baseline (Function Ensemble), the method improves unlearning by 7.55%, 7.72%, and 6.34% on ViT-B/32, ViT-B/16, and ViT-L/14 respectively. The distilled single-task-vector variant achieves nearly identical unlearning (15.66% / 12.70% / 10.63%) while restoring the original VLM architecture for deployment. In the linearized (NTK) setting (Table 2), the method further reduces forget accuracy to 7.88% (ViT-B/32) and 5.70% (ViT-L/14).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed task simplex method outperforms all five baselines across all three CLIP architectures on forget-set accuracy while maintaining comparable retain-set accuracy. Against Standard Task Arithmetic [29], the method reduces forget accuracy by 9.03 points (ViT-B/32: 15.20 vs. 24.23) and 6.68 points (ViT-L/14: 9.98 vs. 16.66). Against the closest competitor, Function Ensemble [8] (vanilla averaging of Q unlearned model predictions), the closed-form simplex ensemble improves by 7.55 points on ViT-B/32 (15.20 vs. 22.75) and 6.34 points on ViT-L/14 (9.98 vs. 16.32). Among parameter-level merging baselines, Vector EMR-Merging [28] is the strongest (21.83% on ViT-B/32), still trailing the proposed method by 6.63 points. Vector TIES-Merging [59] and Vector Greedy Merge [56] from [56] are consistently outperformed by 6.9–8.7 points. The distilled variant sacrifices only ~0.5 points of forget accuracy while restoring the single-VLM architecture, making it practical for deployment without ensemble overhead.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends task vector arithmetic (Ilharco et al., ICLR 2023) from single or few task vectors to a (Q-1)-simplex whose vertices are diverse task vectors, and derives a closed-form function-level ensemble over infinite interpolated task vectors using Dirichlet distribution moments and second-order Taylor expansion. The Taylor analysis (Eq. 16) reveals the method mathematically interpolates between the quadratic terms of Vector Uniform Merge (Wortsman et al., ICML 2022) and vanilla Function Ensemble, positioning it as a principled middle ground between parameter-level merging and explicit function averaging. Unlike prior model-merging approaches (TIES, EMR, Greedy) that operate at the parameter level, this work operates at the function level, explicitly modeling prediction variance as the key driver of unlearning quality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The closed-form ensemble eliminates the need to explicitly sample and evaluate hundreds of task vectors (0.8h vs. 6.3h for ~300 samples), making high-quality unlearning practical for large VLMs without retraining. The bias-variance decomposition provides a theoretical handle on controlling model complexity during unlearning, and the distillation step ensures deployment compatibility with standard VLM architectures. Key limitations include the requirement for access to forget-set data to extract task vectors, the reliance on a second-order Taylor approximation that may degrade for large λ, and the current evaluation being limited to CLIP-based classification and a single VQA benchmark. Open questions include whether the simplex formulation generalizes to non-CLIP VLMs (e.g., LLaMA-based), whether higher-order terms matter in practice, and how the method behaves under adversarial or distribution-shifted forget requests.
