## Problem

Monitoring generalization during training traditionally requires a held-out validation set, which wastes labeled data and provides no per-sample attribution of performance. The paper asks whether interactions between training data and model weights can yield a validation-set-free metric that both tracks generalization over time and attributes performance to individual training samples.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251784/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes only the linear classifier head weights and per-sample latent representations (logits and targets) from a standard forward pass, computing per-sample gradients in closed form as g_i = -z_i · (ŷ_i - y_i)^T rather than backpropagating through the full network. It produces a per-sample alignment score (cosine similarity in [-1,1]) for each training example, aggregated into a single GWA scalar per epoch (mean alignment divided by excess kurtosis plus a constant β=1.2), which is then used to select the early-stopping epoch.

## Method

GWA quantifies the coherence between per-sample negative gradients and the model's linear head weights, motivated by the theoretical result that under perfectly classifiable data, gradients asymptotically align with weights. The key efficiency insight is restricting gradient computation to the final classification layer via a closed-form expression, reducing the cost to a single matrix multiplication per sample (~0.003 GFLOPs vs. 4.6 GFLOPs for a full ViT/S-16 forward pass). A scalable online estimator accumulates alignment scores across all mini-batches within an epoch (with drifting weights), then computes the first and fourth central moments to form GWA = M^(1) / (M^(4)/(M^(2))^2 - 3 + β). The kurtosis correction penalizes heavy-tailed alignment distributions where rare samples exert disproportionate influence, grounded in long-tail theory. Early stopping is determined by the epoch achieving maximum GWA after a 10% warm-up, and for fine-tuning, the initial minimum before the maximum is identified first.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (0% label noise) with ViT/S-16, GWA achieves 81.57% top-1 test accuracy, outperforming the 10% validation set baseline (81.10%), LabelWave (81.00%), and Gradient Disparity (79.22%). Under 17% label noise, GWA reaches 75.70% vs. 75.23% (Val 10%) and 75.02% (LabelWave). On ImageNet-1k, GWA attains 73.28% (ViT) and 71.25% (ConvNeXt), matching or exceeding the 10% validation set while substantially outperforming GD (67.22% / 71.23%). Models selected via GWA-based early stopping also show improved robustness, with average gains of +0.55% on CIFAR-C and +0.67% on ImageNet-C over the 10% validation set baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GWA consistently outperforms LabelWave (Yuan et al., ICLR 2024) across all CIFAR-10/CIFAR-10-N settings, achieving an average 0.67% higher test accuracy on ViT and 0.55% on ConvNeXt; LabelWave fails to detect overfitting under label noise, while GWA's kurtosis-corrected alignment captures the degradation. Against Gradient Disparity (Forouzesh & Thiran, ECML PKDD 2021), GWA shows a dramatic advantage on ImageNet-1k, where GD's early-stopping criteria either stop too early (5th inter-epoch increase) or never trigger (5 consecutive increases), yielding 67.22% (ViT) vs. GWA's 73.28%—a 6-point gap. Compared to the standard 10% validation set, GWA matches or slightly exceeds performance on most configurations (e.g., +0.47% on ViT CIFAR-10, +0.75% on ConvNeXt CIFAR-10 9% noise), while the 1% validation set occasionally edges out GWA on ConvNeXt ImageNet-ReaL (79.01% vs. 78.74%). On robustness benchmarks (CIFAR-C, ImageNet-C), GWA-selected models improve by +0.52 to +0.93% over the 10% validation set, indicating that GWA captures generalization-relevant dynamics beyond in-domain accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

GWA operationalizes the theoretical result that, under cross-entropy optimization on perfectly classifiable data, per-sample gradients converge in direction to the model weights (Ji & Telgarsky; Lyu & Li), by using the weights as a fixed reference vector instead of the pairwise gradient comparisons used in prior coherence/stiffness metrics (Fort et al.; Chatterjee; Sankararaman et al.). This design choice eliminates the memory bottleneck of storing all per-sample gradients and yields a per-sample signal that aggregate pairwise statistics obscure. The kurtosis correction draws on long-tail memorization theory (Feldman; Feldman & Zhang), and the early-stopping evaluation is positioned against LabelWave (prediction-change-based) and Gradient Disparity (batch-level), both of which the paper shows to be less sensitive to label noise or impractical at scale.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GWA offers a validation-set-free, per-epoch generalization proxy with negligible overhead (~2.5 s/epoch on ImageNet with ViT/S-16), which is practically important for large-scale training where holding out labeled data is costly. The per-sample alignment scores provide a free diagnostic for mislabeled or outlier samples without requiring explicit detection pipelines, and the kurtosis-aware aggregation captures heavy-tailed influence patterns that a simple mean would miss. Open questions include whether the approach extends to self-supervised or autoregressive settings, how to handle the observed bimodal-distribution failure mode, and whether the linear-head-only approximation remains valid for architectures without a clear final linear layer.
