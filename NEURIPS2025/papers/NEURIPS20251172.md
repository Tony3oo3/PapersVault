## Problem

Post-training quantization of video diffusion transformers (V-DMs) suffers from two underexplored challenges: (1) the extremely long token sequences (thousands per sample) force calibration to use only dozens of samples, making quantization performance highly sensitive to which samples are selected, and (2) existing optimization treats all tokens equally despite V-DMs exhibiting sparse attention patterns where only a small subset of tokens significantly influences the output. Prior work focused on quantizer design (granularity allocation, static-dynamic strategies) but did not address calibration data quality or token-level loss weighting.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained video diffusion transformer (CogVideoX-2B/5B or HunyuanVideo-13B) and a small calibration pool of 40 latent samples drawn from 10 text prompts across all denoising timesteps, and produces a W4A6 or W4A4 quantized model. The calibration operates on the latent token representations (n = s × t tokens, e.g., t=49 for a 6-second video at 8 FPS) rather than raw pixels, and the quantized model accepts the same text prompts as the full-precision model to generate videos. The quantized model achieves 3.9× model storage reduction and 1.3× inference speedup with negligible quality loss.

## Method

S²Q-VDiT introduces two complementary techniques within a block-wise PTQ framework (using GPTQ weight quantizer, SmoothQuant channel-wise scaling, and FlatQuant rotation matrices). First, Hessian-aware Salient Data Selection (SDS) scores each candidate calibration sample along two axes: diffusion salience C_diff = ‖x_t − x_{t−1}‖²/‖x_t‖² (measuring how much new information each timestep contributes) and quantization salience C_quant = ‖x_t^T x_t‖_2 (measuring sensitivity to quantization perturbations via Levenberg-Marquardt Hessian approximation). After min-max normalization, the final score is the product of both normalized values, leveraging the AM-GM inequality to ensure only samples that are simultaneously informative for diffusion and sensitive to quantization are selected. Second, Attention-guided Sparse Token Distillation (STD) reweights the per-token quantization loss using the full multi-head attention map: each token's weight λ_j is proportional to the sum of attention it receives across all heads and positions, normalized to [λ_min, λ_max]. This directs optimization capacity toward the ~10% of tokens that dominate the block output, enabling better convergence under the limited calibration budget.

## Results

Under W4A6 quantization on VBench, S²Q-VDiT achieves near-lossless performance across three video diffusion models (CogVideoX-2B, CogVideoX-5B, HunyuanVideo-13B). On CogVideoX-5B, it attains 60.75 Imaging Quality (vs. 61.80 FP) and 26.30 Overall Consistency (vs. 26.46 FP), even surpassing FP on Scene Consistency (46.66 vs. 45.28). On HunyuanVideo-13B, it reaches 26.91 Overall Consistency, slightly exceeding the full-precision score of 26.85. Under the more challenging W4A4 setting, S²Q-VDiT retains ~95% of FP performance while all baselines collapse (e.g., CogVideoX-2B Scene Consistency: 34.23 vs. best baseline 12.21).

## Baselines & Comparisons

S²Q-VDiT outperforms all five baselines across every model and metric under both W4A6 and W4A4 settings. The closest competitor, ViDiT-Q, still trails by a wide margin: on CogVideoX-5B W4A6, S²Q-VDiT achieves 60.75 IQ vs. ViDiT-Q's 48.87 (a 12-point gap), and 46.66 Scene Consistency vs. 27.76. QuaRot, the strongest LLM-derived baseline, reaches 52.31 IQ on HunyuanVideo but falls short of S²Q-VDiT's 58.83. Q-DiT and PTQ4DiT suffer severe degradation, with PTQ4DiT dropping to 43.54 IQ on CogVideoX-5B (vs. 61.80 FP) and SmoothQuant collapsing to 39.50 IQ. Under W4A4, the gap widens dramatically: S²Q-VDiT maintains 53.71 IQ on CogVideoX-2B while the best baseline (ViDiT-Q) reaches only 45.56, and Scene Consistency jumps from 12.21 (best baseline) to 34.23 (S²Q-VDiT), a nearly 3× improvement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the block-wise post-training quantization framework established by PTQ4DiT and the GPTQ weight quantizer, incorporating SmoothQuant-style channel-wise scaling and FlatQuant rotation matrices as standard components. It departs from prior V-DM quantization work (Q-DiT, ViDiT-Q) that focused on quantizer design—granularity allocation and static-dynamic strategies—by instead targeting two underexplored dimensions: calibration data quality and token-level optimization weighting. The Hessian-aware data selection draws on Levenberg-Marquardt Hessian approximation from GPTQ/OBC, while the sparse token distillation leverages attention sparsity observations from efficient V-DM inference literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

With video diffusion transformers scaling to 13B+ parameters and tens of thousands of tokens per sample, the 3.9× model compression and 1.3× inference acceleration demonstrated here are practically significant for deployment on consumer or edge hardware. The finding that calibration data selection introduces high performance variance under the small data budgets typical of V-DMs (dozens vs. thousands for I-DMs) challenges the common assumption that random sampling suffices for PTQ and has implications beyond video generation. The W4A4 results represent the first exploration at 4-bit activation for V-DMs, though the authors acknowledge that fully lossless performance at this extreme setting remains an open challenge. Whether the sparse attention patterns and the proposed reweighting strategy generalize to next-generation architectures with different attention mechanisms (e.g., sliding-window or factorized attention) remains to be validated.
