## Problem

Current multimodal fusion methods rely on Transformer attention to implicitly model cross-modality correlations, which fails to distinctly capture each modality's unique characteristics, lacks interpretability, and can let dominant modalities overshadow weaker ones. While normalizing flows offer an explicit, tractable alternative, their standard affine coupling layers lack the expressiveness to capture wide-range cross-modality dependencies, and scaling them to high-dimensional data incurs prohibitive computational cost and unstable training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254614/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Multimodal inputs (e.g., RGB+Depth at 256×256, image pairs from Taskonomy, or image+text from MM-IMDB) are first tokenized and projected into a lower-dimensional latent space via a perceptual compression encoder (MAE visual encoder for images, CLIP text encoder for text). The normalizing flow then operates on these latent features rather than raw high-dimensional data, and a task-specific projection head produces the final output: per-pixel segmentation maps, translated images, or genre class labels.

## Method

MANGO replaces the standard affine coupling layer in normalizing flows with an Invertible Cross-Attention (ICA) layer that uses autoregressive attention (upper-triangular mask) to ensure invertibility while capturing second-order cross-token correlations. Three partitioning strategies—Modality-to-Modality (MMCA), Inter-Modality (IMCA), and Learnable Inter-Modality (LICA, using LU-decomposed permutation matrices)—control how attention information is exchanged between modalities within each ICA layer. The bijective network G stacks 12 blocks, each containing 8 ICA layers (2 MMCA + 4 IMCA + 2 LICA) plus 1 coupling layer, and operates in a latent space produced by a perceptual compression autoencoder to enable scalability. Training jointly optimizes the negative log-likelihood of the flow (via the tractable log-determinant of the upper-triangular attention matrix) and a task-specific loss.

## Results

On RGB-D semantic segmentation, MANGO achieves 59.2% mIoU on NYUDv2 and 54.1% mIoU on SUN RGB-D, outperforming the prior best GeminiFusion by 1.5% and 0.8% respectively. On Taskonomy image-to-image translation, MANGO attains the best FID across all five settings, with a particularly large margin on Depth+Normal→RGB (FID 67.61 vs. GeminiFusion's 96.98). On MM-IMDB movie genre classification, MANGO reaches 71.7% Micro F1 and 68.2% Macro F1, surpassing the previous best (BridgeTower) by 3.5% and 4.9%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MANGO outperforms all 27 compared baselines across all three tasks. In semantic segmentation, it surpasses the strongest Transformer-based competitor GeminiFusion [20] by 1.5% mIoU on NYUDv2 (59.2 vs. 57.7) and 0.8% on SUN RGB-D (54.1 vs. 53.3), and by a wider margin over TokenFusion [65] (54.2/53.0 mIoU) and all CNN-based methods (CEN [66] at 52.5/51.1, RDFNet [39] at 50.1/47.7). In image-to-image translation on Taskonomy, MANGO achieves the lowest FID in all five settings; the gap is most dramatic on Depth+Normal→RGB where MANGO's FID of 67.61 beats GeminiFusion's 96.98 by nearly 30 points, and on RGB+Shade→Normal where MAE drops from 0.65 (GeminiFusion) to 0.52. In MM-IMDB movie genre classification, MANGO's 71.7% Micro F1 and 68.2% Macro F1 exceed BridgeTower [69] (68.2/63.3) by 3.5% and 4.9%, and also surpass vision-language foundation models BLIP [22] (67.4/62.8) and COCA [74] (67.7/62.6). The ablation in Table 4 further confirms that the ICA layer outperforms prior invertible layers (Coupling [5], Glow [21], Flow++ [15], AttnFlow [48]) by 2.7–8.4% mIoU, validating the architectural contribution over existing normalizing-flow building blocks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the normalizing flow lineage from RealNVP and Glow by replacing the standard affine coupling layer with an Invertible Cross-Attention (ICA) layer that preserves tractability and invertibility while capturing long-range cross-modality dependencies. It departs from the dominant Transformer-based implicit fusion paradigm (TokenFusion, GeminiFusion) by framing multimodal correlation as an explicit joint distribution modeled via bijective mappings, and borrows the perceptual-compression / latent-space idea from Latent Diffusion to address the scaling bottleneck of flow-based models on high-dimensional inputs. The three partitioning strategies (MMCA, IMCA, LICA) are novel architectural choices that have no direct precedent in prior flow or attention literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MANGO is one of the first studies to apply normalizing flows explicitly to multimodal fusion, offering an interpretable alternative to the black-box attention-based fusion that dominates the field. The latent-model trick makes the approach computationally practical (72.9M params, 152 GFLOPs — competitive with GeminiFusion), which is important for adoption. However, the paper acknowledges that the balance between the NLL and task-specific loss is not fully explored, and experiments are limited to standard-scale benchmarks; whether the approach generalizes to larger-scale or more than two modalities remains open. The explicit-modeling framing also raises the question of whether interpretability gains translate into practical deployment advantages over well-tuned implicit fusion.
