## Problem

Gloss-based Sign Language Translation (SLT) methods achieve strong performance by using expert-annotated gloss sequences as an intermediate representation, but such annotations are costly and largely unavailable in large-scale datasets. Gloss-free methods bypass this dependency yet suffer a significant performance gap due to the linguistic asymmetry between sign and spoken languages. The paper addresses how to recover the benefits of structured gloss supervision without requiring full expert annotation.

## Input / Output

The model takes sign language video clips (T×224×224×3 frames) as input and produces spoken language text (e.g., German or English) as output. During training, the paired spoken-language text is fed to an LLM with ~30 in-context text-gloss example pairs to generate pseudo glosses, which serve as an intermediate supervision signal. At inference, the model operates end-to-end from video to text, bypassing the pseudo gloss entirely. The S3D vision encoder extracts spatiotemporal features pooled to T/4×512, which are projected into the translator's input space.

## Method

PGG-SLT decomposes SLT into a three-stage curriculum (Sign2Gloss → Gloss2Text → Sign2Text) using LLM-generated pseudo glosses as the intermediate representation. Pseudo glosses are produced by prompting an LLM (e.g., Gemini 1.5 Pro) with a small set of text-gloss pairs via in-context learning, leveraging the LLM's ability to filter function words and normalize lemmas. A key innovation is a weakly supervised reordering step: a multi-label classifier (trained with frequency-weighted BCE loss and L1 temporal smoothing) predicts which glosses appear in each video, and a greedy algorithm reorders the LLM gloss to match the video's temporal structure, enabling effective CTC-based training. The vision encoder is S3D (pre-trained on Kinetics-400) and the translator is mBART or Gemma2-2B, with the CTC loss on pseudo glosses removed in the final training stage to avoid over-reliance on noisy supervision.

## Results

On Phoenix14T, PGG-SLT with Gemma2-2B achieves BLEU4 of 27.53 (Dev) and 27.32 (Test), outperforming the previous best gloss-free method VAP (26.62/26.02) by 0.9/1.3 BLEU4 points, and approaching gloss-based TS-SLT (28.66/28.95) while using only 30 gloss annotations out of 7,096 available. On How2Sign, PGG-SLT (Gemma2) reaches BLEU4 of 15.9 (Dev) and 13.7 (Test), surpassing VAP (14.9/12.9) and matching SSVP-SLT with large-scale pre-training (15.5 Test) without any additional pre-training data. The method also achieves competitive ROUGE scores (52.85 Dev / 52.56 Test on Phoenix14T with Gemma2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PGG-SLT outperforms all prior gloss-free methods on Phoenix14T: it beats VAP [28] by 0.5–1.3 BLEU4, Sign2GPT [53] by 4.3–4.8 BLEU4, FLa-LLM [12] by 3.8–4.2 BLEU4, SignLLM [18] by 3.4–3.9 BLEU4, and GFSLT-VLP [59] by 5.4–5.9 BLEU4. Against gloss-based methods, PGG-SLT (Gemma2) narrows the gap to TS-SLT [11] to within 1.4–1.6 BLEU4 and to MMTLB [10] to within 0.8–1.1 BLEU4, despite using only 30 gloss annotations versus the full 7,096. On How2Sign, PGG-SLT (Gemma2) surpasses VAP [28] by 1.0–1.8 BLEU4, FLa-LLM [12] by 4.0–4.2 BLEU4, and Tarrés et al. [48] by 5.7–5.9 BLEU4. It also outperforms SSVP-SLT [45] without large-scale pre-training by 6.1 BLEU4 on the test set, and slightly trails SSVP-SLT† [45] (with extra pre-training data) by 1.8 BLEU4. Against Uthus et al. [51] and Uni-Sign† [38], which leverage large-scale datasets, PGG-SLT remains competitive (13.7 vs. 12.4 and 14.9 BLEU4 on test), demonstrating that pseudo-gloss generation can substitute for data scaling.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the three-stage gloss-based SLT pipeline (Sign2Gloss → Gloss2Text → Sign2Text) established by MMTLB [10] and TS-SLT [11], but replaces the expensive expert-annotated glosses with LLM-generated pseudo glosses produced via in-context learning from a handful of text-gloss example pairs. It departs from earlier pseudo-gloss heuristics (POS-based filtering in Sign2GPT [53], CLIP-based alignment in GFSLT-VLP [59] and VAP [28]) by leveraging LLM linguistic reasoning to produce structurally richer glosses, and introduces a weakly supervised multi-label classification step (drawing from weakly supervised action segmentation) to reorder the LLM glosses into a temporally coherent sequence compatible with CTC training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: by requiring only ~30 gloss annotations (0.4% of Phoenix14T's training set) as in-context examples, the gloss-based training paradigm becomes accessible to large-scale gloss-free datasets like How2Sign and YouTube-ASL, narrowing the long-standing performance gap between gloss-based and gloss-free SLT. However, the pseudo glosses remain a lossy approximation of true sign language structure (omitting non-manual markers, classifiers, and spatial configurations), and the approach has not yet been validated on the largest ASL benchmarks where large-scale pre-training methods (SSVP-SLT, Uni-Sign) still hold a slight edge. Open questions include whether richer gloss representations (e.g., incorporating NMM spans and classifier markers as in 2M-Flores-ASL) could further close the gap, and whether the method scales effectively with dataset size and sign language diversity.
