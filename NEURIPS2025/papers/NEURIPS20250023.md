## Problem

Sparse variational GPs (SVGPs) suffer from two intertwined pathologies: they are not robust to outlier-contaminated observations, and they produce overconfident predictions in regions far from inducing points due to approximation error. Prior work addresses these challenges in isolation—RCSVGP handles outliers but remains overconfident, while CaGP corrects approximation-induced uncertainty but lacks outlier robustness. The paper argues these issues are not orthogonal: low-rank approximations can amplify the impact of outliers, so mitigating one without the other is insufficient.

## Input / Output

The model consumes input-output pairs (x, y) that may contain asymmetric outliers, along with a kernel hyperparameter vector θ and a set of action matrices S_i ∈ R^{n×i} (generalizing inducing points) that are themselves optimized during training. It outputs a predictive mean and a combined variance that explicitly includes a computational uncertainty term k_x^T Σ̃_i k_x, guaranteeing the posterior variance exceeds that of the exact RCGP. In the BO setting, the method additionally takes a set of candidate points and the current dataset D_t, and jointly outputs the next q query points, optimized action matrices, and updated hyperparameters in a single end-to-end maximization.

## Method

RCaGP applies a probabilistic linear solver to the representer weights of the robust conjugate GP (RCGP), placing a Gaussian prior on v̂ = (K + σ²J_w)^{-1}(y − m_w) and updating it via the linear Gaussian identity to obtain a posterior mean v̂_i and covariance Σ̃_i. The predictive variance is then k̂_i(x,x) = k(x,x) − k_x^T C̃_i k_x, which decomposes into the RCGP posterior variance plus a computational uncertainty term that prevents overconfidence. Outlier robustness is achieved through a weight function w(x,y) = β(1 + (y−m(x))²/c²)^{−1/2} that down-weights observations deviating from the mean prior, entering both the shrinkage term m_w and the noise matrix J_w. Hyperparameters and action matrices are optimized by maximizing a robust ELBO that replaces the Gaussian likelihood with a generalized Bayesian loss. For BO, the EULBO framework is extended so that action matrices, kernel hyperparameters, and query points are jointly optimized end-to-end, with a sparse block structure on S_i keeping the number of trainable parameters at n. The paper proves two key theoretical properties: a bounded posterior influence function (PIF) establishing formal outlier robustness, and a worst-case error bound linking the combined uncertainty to the RKHS norm of the residual, ensuring conservative predictions.

## Results

On UCI regression with 10% asymmetric outliers, RCaGP achieves the best MAE on 3 of 4 datasets (Boston 0.477, Energy 0.380, Parkinsons 0.586) and best NLL on 3 of 4 (Boston 1.272, Energy 1.162, Parkinsons 1.317), with RRP only outperforming it on Yacht (MAE 0.356, NLL 0.896). In the no-outlier setting, RCaGP remains competitive, achieving the best MAE on Boston (0.428), Yacht (0.436), and Parkinsons (0.538), while CaGP marginally wins on Energy (0.334 vs 0.393). In high-throughput BO, RCaGP clearly outperforms all baselines on Lasso-DNA 180D and ranks first or on par with CaGP on Hartmann 6D, Lunar 12D, and Rover 60D under asymmetric outlier contamination.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RCaGP consistently outperforms SVGP and Student-t SVGP across all four UCI datasets under asymmetric outliers, with MAE improvements of 30–50% over SVGP. Against CaGP, RCaGP wins on all four datasets for both MAE and NLL under contamination, confirming that robustness and computation-awareness are synergistic rather than orthogonal. Against RCSVGP, RCaGP achieves lower MAE on all four datasets (e.g., 0.477 vs 0.532 on Boston) and lower NLL on three of four, with RCSVGP only edging ahead on Yacht NLL (1.241 vs 1.253). The main exception is RRP, which outperforms RCaGP on Yacht (MAE 0.356 vs 0.435; NLL 0.896 vs 1.253) and Boston NLL (1.147 vs 1.272), though RRP is substantially slower (5–7 s vs 2.5 s clock-time). In the no-outlier regime, RCaGP remains the top performer on three of four datasets, with CaGP narrowly winning Energy MAE (0.334 vs 0.393), suggesting the weight function provides a small benefit even without explicit contamination.

## Relation to Prior Work

RCaGP unifies two recent but previously separate lines of GP research: robust conjugate GPs (RCGP/RCSVGP, Altamirano et al. 2024), which use generalized Bayesian inference to down-weight outliers, and computation-aware GPs (CaGP, Wenger et al. 2024), which quantify uncertainty from low-rank approximations via probabilistic linear solvers. The key conceptual departure is the argument that these two challenges are not orthogonal—approximations can amplify outlier effects—so addressing them in isolation is insufficient. For the BO component, the paper adapts the EULBO framework (Maus et al. 2024) to jointly optimize action matrices, hyperparameters, and query points, rather than the inducing-point/variational-parameter optimization used in standard SVGP-based EULBO.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, theoretically grounded solution to a practical problem in high-throughput BO where both sensor noise/outliers and sparse-approximation overconfidence degrade decision-making. The bounded posterior influence function and worst-case error guarantees (Propositions 4.1–4.2) give practitioners rigorous confidence in the model's behavior under contamination, which is rare in approximate GP literature. Limitations include the requirement for full batching (higher GPU memory than fully stochastic SVGPs), manual selection of the projection dimensionality, and the expert-guided mean prior's reliance on domain knowledge that may not be available. Open questions include whether the action-matrix optimization can be made fully automatic, whether the framework extends to non-Gaussian latent functions, and how it scales to truly massive datasets (n > 10^5) where even O(ni) complexity becomes prohibitive.
