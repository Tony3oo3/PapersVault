## Problem

Langevin dynamics (LD) for combinatorial optimization relies on Gaussian noise, which causes the mean escape time from local optima to grow exponentially with the energy barrier height, making it prone to getting trapped in deep, narrow local minima on rugged landscapes. This limitation also necessitates costly parallel chains for sample diversity and performs poorly under non-strict constraints, impeding the development of more advanced sampling-based and data-driven CO solvers.

## Input / Output

The method takes an undirected graph's adjacency matrix and a problem specification (MIS, Maximum Clique, or Max Cut) as input, operating over the binary solution space {0,1}^N. FLD-EG requires a closed-form energy function gradient (available for all three case studies), while FLD-IG replaces this with a 5-layer GCN (128 hidden dims) that predicts per-variable flip probabilities. The output is a binary solution vector representing the independent set, clique, or cut partition, optionally followed by a greedy decoding step to enforce hard constraints.

## Method

The core contribution replaces the Gaussian noise in the LD SDE with symmetric α-stable (SαS) Lévy noise, yielding the FLD SDE dx_t = c_α s(x_t) dt + dL_t^α, where c_α = Γ(α−1)/Γ(α/2)² and L_t^α is an SαS Lévy motion. This heavy-tailed perturbation enables Lévy flights that jump over energy barriers, and the paper proves the mean escape time scales polynomially as w^α (where w is the barrier width) versus the exponential exp(ΔH/ε) of LD. Two practical variants are provided: FLD-EG (explicit gradient, training-free, with top-d truncation on both gradient and noise terms) and FLD-IG (implicit gradient via a GCN trained with a pseudo-reinforcement-learning loop that incorporates SαS noise into the proposal distribution). A truncation strategy bounds extreme Lévy flight samples to maintain sampling stability, and α is tunable per problem—α=2 recovers standard LD for smooth landscapes while smaller α increases escape probability on rugged ones.

## Results

On the Maximum Independent Set (MIS) problem, FLD-EG achieves the best objective values across all four graph benchmarks: 20.02 on RB-[200-300], 40.25 on RB-[800-1200], 44.37 on ER-[700-800], and 377.50 on ER-[9000-11000], while FLD-IG (the data-driven variant) achieves 19.72, 39.56, 43.50, and 365.03 respectively. On Maximum Clique, FLD-EG reaches 40.63 on RB-[800-1200] (vs. 40.53 for RLSA and 40.35 for iSCO), and on Max Cut it reaches 2960.13 on BA-[800-1200] (vs. 2955.81 for RLSA and 2919.97 for iSCO). FLD-EG consistently matches or exceeds the best heuristic baselines (RLSA, iSCO) in both objective value and runtime, while FLD-IG outperforms the PRL baseline RLNN on 75% of datasets with shorter or comparable runtimes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FLD-EG (heuristic, explicit gradient) outperforms the two strongest heuristic baselines—RLSA [17] and iSCO [51]—on all six problem/dataset combinations in both objective value and runtime; for example, on MIS ER-[9000-11000] it achieves 377.50 vs. RLSA's 375.31 and iSCO's 365.37, and on MaxCut BA-[800-1200] it reaches 2960.13 vs. RLSA's 2955.81 and iSCO's 2919.97. FLD-IG (data-driven, implicit gradient) consistently beats the PRL baseline RLNN [17] on 75% of datasets (e.g., MIS RB-[200-300]: 19.72 vs. 19.52; MaxCl RB-[800-1200]: 37.40 vs. 35.23) with shorter or comparable runtimes. Against unsupervised-learning baselines (SDDS, DiffUCO, LTFT), FLD-EG achieves substantially higher objective values (e.g., MIS RB-[800-1200]: 40.25 vs. SDDS's 39.99 and DiffUCO's 38.87), while FLD-IG matches or exceeds them on most settings. OR solvers (Gurobi, KaMIS) achieve higher absolute values given sufficient runtime (hours), but the paper reports them only to illustrate the gap to the optimum rather than for direct comparison. On MaxCut BA-[200-300], FLD-EG (734.18) narrowly edges out RLSA (733.54) and iSCO (728.24), while FLD-IG (733.48) is competitive with RLSA. Overall, FLD-EG achieves SOTA among training-free/heuristic methods and FLD-IG achieves SOTA among PRL methods across all three CO problems.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the discrete Langevin dynamics sampler for combinatorial optimization (Zhang et al., ICML 2022; Sun et al., ICML 2023) by replacing Gaussian noise with symmetric α-stable Lévy noise, building directly on the continuous FLD SDE framework of Şimşekli (ICML 2017). The data-driven variant (FLD-IG) inherits its pseudo-reinforcement-learning training loop and flip-probability mechanism from Feng & Yang (2025), while the sampling-based variant (FLD-EG) generalizes the discrete LD proposal of Sun et al. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of a tunable stability parameter α that interpolates between standard LD (α=2) and heavy-tailed Lévy flights (α<2), accompanied by a truncation strategy to control outlier samples.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The polynomial-time escape bound (vs. exponential for LD) provides a principled justification for why FLD outperforms LD on rugged, multi-peak energy landscapes, and the α parameter offers a practical knob for adapting exploration to problem structure. However, the current formulation is restricted to binary-variable CO problems and three specific benchmarks (MIS, MaxCl, MaxCut); extending to integer or continuous variables, and to a broader set of CO problems, remains open. The truncation strategy, while effective empirically, lacks a formal guarantee on how much exploration capability is sacrificed, and the modest performance margins over strong baselines like RLSA on some datasets suggest the gains may be problem-dependent.
