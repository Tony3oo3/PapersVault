## Problem

Mamba models suffer significant performance degradation when evaluated on contexts longer than their pre-training length, yet prior work has attributed this failure to the vanishing accumulation of discretization time steps exp(-ΣΔt) without a fundamental theoretical justification. This paper identifies the root cause as the out-of-distribution behavior of the state transition matrix A's eigenvalue spectrum, which drives hidden-state convergence (explosion or vanishing) as sequence length grows, and shows that directly modulating A is a more principled intervention than scaling Δt.

## Input / Output

The method takes a frozen pre-trained Mamba or Mamba2 model plus a small calibration set (e.g., 20 samples at the target context length) and produces per-layer scaling factors applied to the diagonal entries of A (or equivalently log(A)) during the forward pass. For perplexity and LongBench evaluations, a single scalar factor per layer is learned via zeroth-order optimization; for passkey retrieval, one factor per inner-state dimension (Mamba) or per head (Mamba2) is trained while all other parameters remain frozen. No architectural changes or weight updates to the base model are required.

## Method

The paper first derives a theoretical bound (Theorem 4.2) showing that the expected squared norm of the limiting hidden state depends on the eigenvalue spectrum of the diagonal transition matrix Λ = diag(exp(-A)), with eigenvalues near 1 causing unbounded state growth and eigenvalues near 0 causing vanishing. Based on this, the proposed Mamba Modulation applies per-layer scaling factors to A (compressing large eigenvalues toward 0 and inflating small ones toward 1) to stabilize the state norm over longer sequences. The calibration procedure adapts the MambaExtend framework: instead of learning scaling factors for Δt, the same zeroth-order or gradient-based optimization is applied to log(A), exploiting the shared dimensionality between A and Δt so that no additional compute or memory is needed. Empirically, calibrated scaling of A outperforms scaling of Δt on language modeling perplexity (ProofPile, PG19, GovReport), passkey retrieval, and LongBench across Mamba and Mamba2 models of varying sizes, and also surpasses filtering-based methods like DeciMamba and LongMamba on PG19 perplexity.

## Results

On LongBench (mamba2-2.7b), Calibrated Scaling A achieves an average score of 14.98, outperforming MambaExtend (13.60) by ~10% relative and the base model (8.75) by 71%. On PG19 perplexity at 64K context, mamba2-1.3b drops from 1479.45 (base) to 4.72 with Calibrated Scaling A, compared to 27.50 for MambaExtend and 13.55 for LongMamba. On Passkey Retrieval, scaling A yields near-constant improvement over scaling Δt across all Mamba and Mamba2 model sizes, matching or exceeding full fine-tuning performance with far fewer trainable parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MambaExtend (Azizi et al., ICLR 2025), which calibrates per-layer scaling factors for Δt, Calibrated Scaling A consistently wins: on LongBench it achieves 14.98 vs. 13.60 average, and on PG19 at 64K it reduces perplexity from 27.50 (mamba2-1.3b) to 4.72, and from 60.00 (mamba-2.8b) to 40.00. Against DeciMamba (Ben-Kish et al., ICLR 2025), which filters tokens via decimation layers, Calibrated Scaling A wins on mamba-1.4b at 64K (19.13 vs. 28.48) but loses on mamba-2.8b at 64K (40.00 vs. 26.54), though it dominates at shorter lengths (e.g., 16K: 8.94 vs. 18.34). Against LongMamba (Ye et al., ICLR 2025), which prevents exponential decay by thresholding Δt, Calibrated Scaling A achieves substantially lower perplexity at all lengths for mamba2-1.3b (e.g., 4.72 vs. 13.55 at 64K). Overall, Calibrated Scaling A is the best or second-best method at every context length across all three models, with the largest margins at the longest sequences where MambaExtend and the base model collapse.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Mamba and Mamba2 state-space model architectures and adapts the per-layer calibration methodology of MambaExtend (Azizi et al., ICLR 2025), but redirects it from scaling the discretization time steps Δ_t to scaling the state transition matrix A. Prior length-generalization work on Mamba (MambaExtend, DeciMamba, LongMamba) has focused on Δ_t as the primary lever, motivated by the vanishing accumulation exp(−ΣΔ_t); this paper departs from that line by providing a spectral analysis showing that the eigenvalue distribution of A governs the asymptotic convergence of the hidden state norm, and that modulating A is a more well-founded intervention. The theoretical framing draws on earlier SSM spectrum analyses (Gu et al. on Hurwitz matrices, Orvieto et al. on eigenvalue-driven state decay, Grazzi et al. on negative eigenvalues) but extends them to the specific question of length extrapolation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, spectrum-based explanation for why Mamba models fail at length generalization and identifies a concrete, training-free (or parameter-efficient) fix that consistently outperforms the Δ_t-scaling baseline across perplexity, passkey retrieval, and LongBench on both Mamba and Mamba2 variants. This shifts the community's attention from the more intuitive but less effective Δ_t knob to the fixed A parameter, opening a new axis of post-hoc calibration for SSMs. Open questions include whether the spectral argument extends to non-diagonal or structured (non-scalar-identity) transition matrices, whether the approach remains effective at extreme context lengths (100K+), and whether similar spectral modulation can be applied to other recurrent or hybrid architectures beyond the Mamba family.
