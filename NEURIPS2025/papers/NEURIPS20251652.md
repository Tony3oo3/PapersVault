## Problem

Existing video customization methods are limited to single-human scenarios or struggle to establish precise correspondences between multiple subject images and their textual entities, leading to identity confusion and inconsistent interactions. Multi-subject video generation requires simultaneously preserving distinct identities, modeling text-specified interactions, and resolving semantic ambiguity among similar subjects (e.g., multiple humans), which prior approaches have not adequately addressed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251652/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts n subject images of arbitrary types (humans, animals, objects) and a free-form text prompt describing the scene, actions, and interactions. Each subject image is encoded twice: by LLaVA's CLIP-ViT encoder (yielding 24×24 visual tokens per image in text space) and by the base model's pretrained VAE (yielding spatially-structured image tokens). The text prompt is augmented with a structured identity template that explicitly links each subject noun to its corresponding image. The output is a generated video from the HunyuanVideo diffusion transformer that maintains identity consistency across all subjects while following the text-specified interactions.

## Method

PolyVivid builds on HunyuanVideo's MM-DiT architecture and introduces three key modules. First, a VLLM-based text-image fusion module uses LLaVA with a structured template to encode subject images into the text embedding space, establishing entity-image correspondences. Second, a 3D-RoPE-based identity-interaction enhancement module repurposes the base model's MM-Attention to perform bidirectional fusion between LLaVA text tokens and VAE image tokens, using 3D positional encodings that bind text and image tokens of the same subject along the temporal axis while aligning spatial indices for pixel-level interaction. Third, an attention-inherited identity injection module shares a single set of MM-Attention parameters across text-image, text-video, and identity-injection cross-attention layers, with LoRA-adapted Key/Value matrices to inject identity-enhanced image tokens into the video latent space uniformly across all frames. Training proceeds in two stages (single-subject identity learning, then multi-subject interaction) on 256 GPUs, and a MLLM-based data pipeline using Florence2 detection, SAM2 segmentation, and clique-based subject consolidation ensures high-quality multi-subject training data.

## Results

On a 100-pair multi-subject benchmark, PolyVivid achieves the best identity preservation scores: Face-sim of 0.642 (vs. 0.554 for the next-best SkyReels-A2) and DINO-sim of 0.623 (vs. 0.619 for SkyReels-A2). It also attains the lowest FVD of 959.74, indicating superior video quality and diversity compared to all baselines. On text-video alignment, PolyVivid ties Vidu-2.0 for the best CLIP-B score (0.336) and is within 0.001 on CLIP-L (0.281 vs. 0.282). Temporal consistency is slightly lower than Vidu-2.0 (0.964 vs. 0.970) and VACE-1.3B (0.966), though the authors note that some baseline videos are nearly static, inflating their scores.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PolyVivid decisively outperforms all five baselines on identity preservation: it leads Face-sim by 0.088 over SkyReels-A2 (0.642 vs. 0.554) and by 0.209 over VACE-1.3B (0.642 vs. 0.433), and leads DINO-sim by 0.024 over SkyReels-A2 (0.623 vs. 0.619). On video quality (FVD, lower is better), PolyVivid achieves 959.74, beating Pika (980.49), Kling-1.6 (1049.70), Vidu-2.0 (1083.35), VACE-1.3B (1171.42), and SkyReels-A2 (1379.65). On text-video alignment, PolyVivid ties Vidu-2.0 on CLIP-B (0.336) and trails by only 0.001 on CLIP-L (0.281 vs. 0.282), while clearly surpassing Pika (0.310/0.263) and SkyReels-A2 (0.332/0.276). The only metric where PolyVivid is not best is Temporal Consistency (0.964), where Vidu-2.0 (0.970) and VACE-1.3B (0.966) edge it out; the authors attribute this partly to those baselines producing near-static videos that artificially inflate the score. Qualitatively, the paper reports that Pika produces blurry outputs, Vidu and Kling fail to dress subjects in specified clothing, Vidu and VACE confuse animal identities (e.g., a tiger with a giraffe's shape), and SkyReels-A2 introduces frame-to-frame artifacts and incorrect relative sizing.

## Relation to Prior Work

PolyVivid builds directly on HunyuanVideo's MM-DiT architecture and its pretrained MM-Attention mechanism, repurposing the video-text attention blocks for text-image interaction and identity injection. It departs from prior multi-subject customization methods (ConceptMaster, Phantom, SkyReels-A2, VACE) that treat subject images and text as separate conditioning signals or rely on additional trained branches for cross-modal alignment; instead, PolyVivid leverages LLaVA's pretrained multimodal understanding to establish text-image correspondences natively, then uses a 3D-RoPE positional scheme to enable structured bidirectional fusion between VLLM text embeddings and VAE image embeddings. The MLLM-based data pipeline with clique-based subject consolidation also distinguishes it from prior approaches that rely on GroundingDINO+SAM or MLLM-based segmentors alone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Multi-subject video customization with consistent identity and text-specified interaction remains a key bottleneck for practical video generation in advertising, film, and content creation. PolyVivid's attention-inherited injection and 3D-RoPE interaction modules offer a reusable architectural pattern for injecting identity into MM-DiT-based video models without the temporal imbalance of token concatenation or the misalignment of adapter-based methods. However, the approach is constrained by the underlying HunyuanVideo backbone and LLaVA's capacity, may struggle with highly complex scenes involving many subjects or fine-grained physical reasoning, and the MLLM-based data pipeline remains susceptible to grounding errors under occlusion. Open questions include whether the 3D-RoPE interaction scheme generalizes to more than three subjects and whether the progressive two-stage training (identity first, then interaction) is necessary or merely a training convenience.
