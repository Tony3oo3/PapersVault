## Problem

Adapting 2D multimodal large language model (MLLM) reasoning segmentation to 3D scenes remains underexplored, primarily because existing 3D reasoning segmentation methods require prohibitively expensive <3D, text> paired annotations. A naive transfer of 2D MLLMs to 3D fails due to two critical issues: single-view hallucination (the model segments objects not visible in a given view) and cross-view prediction inconsistency, which degrades multi-view aggregation into coherent 3D masks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251839/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a set of k posed RGB images (typically 4 views) of a 3D scene, an implicit natural-language instruction, and the corresponding 3D point cloud. It outputs a per-point binary segmentation mask over the point cloud identifying the target object. All 2D components (LISA MLLM + SAM decoder) remain frozen throughout training and inference; only the 3D sparse-CNN backbone (MinkowskiNet14) and a linear projection layer are optimized, making the pipeline compatible with any frozen MLLM or mask generator.

## Method

MLLM-For3D is a label-free framework that distills 2D MLLM reasoning into a 3D segmentation network via pseudo-label supervision. A frozen LISA model generates per-view binary masks and [SEG] token embeddings; an attention-based view filtering mechanism (weighting each view by mask confidence × semantic alignment to a unified query) suppresses hallucinated or occluded views before unprojecting masks into 3D. The 3D network is trained with two losses: a token-for-Query semantic alignment loss (BCE between 3D point-feature logits and sampled 2D mask predictions, anchored to the unified [SEG] embedding) that binds consistent object identity across views, and a spatial consistency loss (negative cosine similarity between 3D point features and corresponding 2D SAM encoder features) that enforces geometric coherence. This design eliminates the need for any 3D annotations while achieving ~55% higher mIoU than prior 3D reasoning segmentation methods.

## Results

On the Instruct3D benchmark (ScanNet++), the label-free VideoLISA(MLLM-For3D) achieves 41.00 Acc@0.25, 32.10 Acc@0.50, and 28.20 mIoU, surpassing the best LLM-based baseline Reason3D (18.35 / 10.55 / 12.43) by over 22 points in Acc@0.25 and 15.8 points in mIoU. On Reason3D (ScanNet), the same model reaches 44.18 Acc@0.25 and 32.90 mIoU, slightly exceeding Reason3D (43.21 / 31.20). On Intent3D, the label-free variant attains 50.89 Acc@0.25 and 41.56 Acc@0.50, outperforming IntentNet (41.90 AP@0.25, 25.36 AP@0.50) by +9.0 and +16.2 points respectively. On VG-w/o-ON, MLLM-For3D (29.50 Acc@0.25, 24.28 mIoU) beats EDA (26.50, –) by ~3 points in accuracy and introduces a competitive mIoU score where EDA reports none.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MLLM-For3D (label-free, VideoLISA backbone) decisively outperforms Reason3D [23] on Instruct3D by +22.65 Acc@0.25 and +15.77 mIoU, while on Reason3D (ScanNet) it edges ahead by +0.97 Acc@0.25 and +1.70 mIoU. Against IntentNet [32] on Intent3D, the label-free variant wins on Acc@0.50 (41.56 vs 40.83) and mIoU (36.92 vs –) but trails on Acc@0.25 (50.89 vs 58.34); however, on the AP metrics IntentNet reports, MLLM-For3D surpasses by +9.0 (AP@0.25) and +16.2 (AP@0.50). On VG-w/o-ON, MLLM-For3D beats EDA [59] by +3.0 Acc@0.25 and +4.41 Acc@0.50, and outperforms ScanRefer [4], TGNN [24], BUTD-DETR [27], InstanceRefer [42], and M3DRef-CLIP [66] by 11–19 points in Acc@0.25. Non-LLM baselines (3D-STMN [58], TGNN [24], 3D-VisTA [69]) are consistently outperformed by 15–35 points across all benchmarks. In Appendix B, the zero-shot MLLM-For3D surpasses fine-tuned ChatScene+Mask3D [21, 50] by +21.7 Acc@0.25 on Intent3D and by +35.0 Acc@0.25 over zero-shot ChatScene+Mask3D on Reason3D, confirming that the 2D-to-3D transfer strategy outperforms direct 3D-MLLM pipelines without any 3D supervision.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LISA reasoning-segmentation paradigm (2D MLLM + SAM + [SEG] token) into the 3D domain by using a frozen 2D MLLM as a pseudo-label generator for a trainable 3D sparse-CNN backbone (MinkowskiNet14). It departs from prior 3D reasoning segmentation methods (SegPoint, Reason3D, MORE3D) that require expensive ⟨3D, text⟩ paired annotations, and from label-free 3D scene understanding approaches (OpenScene, CLIP2Scene, ConceptFusion) that handle only straightforward category-level prompts. The key conceptual departure is the token-for-Query alignment mechanism and attention-based view filtering, which address the cross-view hallucination and inconsistency problems that arise when naively unprojecting 2D masks into 3D.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The label-free paradigm removes the primary bottleneck in 3D reasoning segmentation—the prohibitive cost of generating high-quality 3D-text paired data—making the task accessible without manual annotation or GPT-4-based synthesis. The ~55% mIoU gain over prior 3D reasoning segmentation methods and the competitive performance against labeled baselines demonstrate that 2D MLLM reasoning can be effectively distilled into 3D. However, the computational overhead of repeatedly invoking a 7B/13B MLLM per training sample remains a practical limitation, and the ~10–15 point gap between label-free and labeled settings suggests room for improvement. Open questions include whether more efficient pseudo-label generation (e.g., caching, distillation) can close this gap, and whether the approach generalizes beyond indoor scenes to outdoor or dynamic environments.
