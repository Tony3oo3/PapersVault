## Problem

Most embodied AI agents operate episodically, starting with an empty internal state each episode, which is unrealistic for continuous robot operations where previously observed scene information should be exploited. The core challenge is maintaining a compact latent memory of a potentially long observation stream that can be queried in O(1) to estimate the relative pose of a previously seen location, without the quadratic cost of attending over the full history or the capacity-scaling limitations of classical recurrent networks.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a stream of 112×112 RGB images and 7-dimensional odometry deltas at each timestep, plus a goal image at query time. It maintains a distributed latent memory of N embeddings (e.g., 20 × 3072) updated recurrently, and outputs a relative pose (distance, bearing angle, and rotation matrix) between the goal image and the agent's current position. For the downstream Mem-Nav task, this pose estimate is fed alongside a binocular goal-direction signal into a GRU-based policy that emits discrete actions (move forward 0.25 m, turn ±10°, stop) over a 1000-step budget.

## Method

Kinaema decouples memory capacity from network capacity by representing state as N independent embeddings updated through a three-stage pipeline: (1) each embedding is corrected by concatenating it with encoded visual and odometry observations and projecting via a linear layer; (2) a 3-layer self-attention transformer contextualizes all embeddings jointly; (3) a weight-shared GRU gating block (update + forget gates) controls per-embedding update speed for training stability. This design allows scaling N without increasing transformer or gating parameters, unlike classical RNNs where memory and network capacity are quadratically coupled. The model is pre-trained on Mem-RPE (predicting relative pose between a query image and latent memory) combined with masked image modeling, using randomized sequence lengths (50–100) and alternative-viewpoint frames to prevent lookup-table shortcuts. For navigation, the RPE decoder is retained and its latent pose encoding is concatenated with DEBiT's binocular goal-direction signal into the agent's GRU, trained end-to-end with PPO for 300 M steps.

## Results

On the Mem-RPE task (RPE-test, T=200), Kinaema achieves 21% accuracy at the strictest threshold (1m translation, 10° rotation), 41% at (1m, 90°), and 63% at (2m, 90°), outperforming all recurrent baselines. At the harder out-of-distribution length T=800, Kinaema maintains 10%/21%/37% respectively, while GRU drops to 4%/14%/31% and xLSTM to 5%/13%/29%. On the downstream Mem-Nav navigation task, integrating Kinaema memory with a priming sequence yields 54% SPL on hard episodes (10–22m start-goal distance), a +10 point improvement over the no-primer baseline, compared to only +0 for a GRU memory variant.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Kinaema outperforms all six baselines on Mem-RPE across both sequence lengths and all three tolerance thresholds. Against the closest recurrent competitor GRU (3.1k memory), Kinaema (61.4k memory) gains +9 points at T=200 (21% vs 12% at 1m/10°) and +6 points at T=800 (10% vs 4%), showing that the distributed transformer-updated memory scales better than a single-vector GRU. Against xLSTM (2.36M memory, ~38× larger), Kinaema still wins by +13 points at T=200, indicating that memory size alone is insufficient without the right update mechanism. MooG, the architecturally closest model (recurrent + transformer update), performs worst among recurrent models (0% at 1m/10°), which the authors attribute to its patch-wise input handling overwhelming the recurrent transformer. EMA with 153.6k memory underperforms GRU despite 50× more memory, confirming that learned dynamics matter more than raw capacity. On navigation (Table 5), Kinaema with priming achieves the best SPL on hard episodes (54% vs 50% for GRU memory, 41% for h₀-injection), with the largest gains (+10 SPL) on the hardest distance bucket where previously-seen goals are most relevant.

## Relation to Prior Work

Kinaema extends the DEBiT navigation agent [9] by adding a dedicated recurrent memory module that compresses pre-episode observations into a latent state, enabling the agent to navigate to goals seen before the episode starts. Architecturally, it departs from the dominant paradigm of transformers attending over full observation history (O(N²) query cost) by proposing a distributed memory of N embeddings updated via a transformer with GRU-style gating, achieving O(1) update and query. The Mem-RPE task re-frames relative pose estimation from the classical two-image comparison (as in DUSt3R, MASt3R, DEBiT) to a single-image-versus-latent-memory formulation, and the Mem-Nav task extends ImageNav to a continuous-operation setting with a fixed priming sequence preceding each navigation episode.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap in embodied AI: most agents reset their internal state at every episode, whereas real robots operate continuously and should exploit previously observed scene information. The demonstrated generalization from T=100 training to T=1000 evaluation sequences, combined with O(1) per-step cost, suggests a viable path toward long-horizon spatial memory without the quadratic blowup of attention-based approaches. Key limitations include the absence of a confidence signal for whether a goal was actually seen, training capped at T=100, and the use of a single embedding per frame rather than patch-wise representations, which may constrain future improvements. Open questions include whether the bi-modal training stability (seeds yielding either excellent or mediocre results) can be resolved, and whether the approach scales to outdoor or multi-day operation.
