## Problem

Large Reasoning Models trained with outcome-based RL tend to overthink, producing unnecessarily long reasoning paths that waste computation and can degrade accuracy. The paper hypothesizes this inefficiency stems from the model's limited ability to dynamically select appropriate modular reasoning strategies—termed thinking patterns—at critical junctures within the reasoning process. Prior efficiency methods rely on heuristic truncation or aggregate token-length metrics, lacking the segment-level precision needed to identify which specific reasoning steps contribute meaningfully.

## Input / Output

The method takes a math problem and an LRM-generated reasoning trajectory, which is segmented into discrete thinking patterns using linguistic cues such as 'Wait' and 'Alternatively.' It produces a pruned, optimized trajectory with an appropriate termination point and final answer, validated by Monte Carlo sampling. The downstream preference optimization stage consumes pairwise datasets contrasting optimized (shorter, correct) trajectories against the longest unoptimized sampled responses, fine-tuning the LRM to reduce token usage from approximately 5,000 to 3,000 while improving accuracy.

## Method

The DTO framework segments a reasoning trajectory into thinking patterns, then identifies the optimal termination point by appending an 'exit' pattern at each candidate position and using Monte Carlo sampling (M=10 completions) to estimate the probability of producing the correct answer; the earliest position where this probability exceeds a threshold T=1.0 determines where to truncate. An auxiliary LLM (Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct) evaluates each remaining segment for redundancy, and segments are pruned only if validation decoding from the shortened trajectory still yields the correct answer. For preference optimization, the paper constructs pairwise datasets contrasting optimized trajectories against the longest unoptimized sampled responses and applies SimPO to fine-tune the LRM toward more efficient reasoning. A key empirical finding is that even originally incorrect trajectories often contain high-quality intermediate segments (high p_i values), enabling the method to transform some incorrect responses into correct ones while reducing attention FLOPs by up to 47%.

## Results

On DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B, DTO achieves 85.48% accuracy on MATH with only 1,936 tokens (efficiency 1.96×), compared to the baseline's 79.80% at 3,543 tokens. On the harder AMC2023 benchmark, DTO reaches 70.25% accuracy with 3,377 tokens versus the baseline's 58.25% at 5,339 tokens, a ~12-point accuracy gain with ~1,960 fewer tokens. On AIME2024, DTO improves accuracy from 21.44% to 28.00% while reducing tokens from 7,359 to 5,877. Across all six benchmarks, DTO achieves the highest efficiency metric (η) in 5 out of 6 cases, with the largest gains on the most difficult problems.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DTO outperforms all baselines on the efficiency metric (η) across nearly every benchmark. Against the strongest prior method FCS+Ref. [Chen et al.], DTO achieves +0.76% accuracy on MATH (85.48 vs. 84.72) while using 613 fewer tokens (1,936 vs. 2,549), and +1.33% on AMC2023 (70.25 vs. 68.92) with 790 fewer tokens. On AIME2024, DTO's 28.00% accuracy surpasses FCS+Ref.'s 24.44% by 3.56 points, a gap that widens on the hardest problems where FCS+Ref. cannot construct pairwise data when no correct response is sampled. O1-Pruner [Luo et al.] and DAST [Shen et al.] rely on aggregate token-length heuristics and underperform DTO by 1.5–3.5% accuracy on AMC/AIME while using 500–1,500 more tokens. The Instruct version (Qwen2.5-Math) uses far fewer tokens (~555 on MATH) but sacrifices 9+ points of accuracy, confirming that the reasoning overhead in LRMs is not simply removable via prompting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on SimPO [20] for preference optimization but departs from prior LRM efficiency methods (O1-Pruner, DAST, FCS+Ref.) that rely on aggregate metrics like token length or heuristic truncation points. The key conceptual departure is operating at the segment level: reasoning trajectories are decomposed into modular 'thinking patterns' (a notion borrowed from cognitive-behavior analyses [7]), and each segment's individual contribution is evaluated via Monte Carlo correctness estimation and an auxiliary LLM's redundancy judgment. The constrained optimization formulation (minimize expected cost subject to a performance lower bound) provides a principled framing that prior heuristic approaches lack.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for deployment: reducing token usage from ~5,000 to ~3,000 while simultaneously improving accuracy addresses the core cost-accuracy tradeoff that limits LRM adoption. The finding that a non-trivial fraction of originally incorrect trajectories contain high-quality intermediate reasoning (high max_i p_i) suggests a broader principle—that LRM overthinking often buries correct partial solutions under redundant verification. Key limitations include validation only on 1.5B models, the requirement for multiple sampled responses per problem, and the computational overhead of the optimization phase itself; generalization to larger models and non-mathematical domains remains an open question.
