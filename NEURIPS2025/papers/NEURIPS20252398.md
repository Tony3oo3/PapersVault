## Problem

Existing graph backdoor attacks on node classification rely on adaptive trigger generators with simple structures and limited parameters, which lack category-aware graph knowledge and degrade significantly as the number of target categories increases. This paper addresses the gap of conducting effective and unnoticeable multi-category graph backdoor attacks, where a single node can be manipulated to predict different target categories by attaching different triggers.

## Input / Output

The method takes a clean semi-supervised training graph (node features, adjacency matrix, labeled/unlabeled split) and a set of K target categories as input. It produces a Multi-Category Subgraph Triggers Pool (MC-STP) that maps each target category to a set of n_pool subgraph triggers, along with a poisoned graph where selected representative nodes have category-aware subgraph triggers attached. During inference, attaching a specific trigger from the pool to any test node causes the backdoored GNN to predict the corresponding target category, while clean nodes remain unaffected.

## Method

EUMC constructs a Multi-Category Subgraph Triggers Pool by sampling subgraphs via BFS around random central nodes in the training graph, then computing the Attachment Probability Shift (APS) of each subgraph using a clean surrogate GCN to identify influential triggers and assign them target categories via argmax of the probability shift vector. A 'select then attach' strategy ensures unnoticeability by selecting the most feature-similar subgraph trigger from the pool for each poisoned node and only attaching trigger nodes whose cosine similarity exceeds a threshold τ_a. The MC-STP is optimized via bi-level optimization: the inner loop trains a surrogate GCN on the poisoned graph, while the outer loop updates trigger parameters to maximize attack loss plus an unnoticeability loss (L_h) that penalizes dissimilar attached edges. By using subgraphs from the original graph as triggers, the method naturally preserves the feature distribution and avoids the optimization challenges of training multiple adaptive trigger generators for different categories.

## Results

EUMC achieves the highest Attack Success Rate (ASR) on 5 out of 6 datasets under no defense: 97.4% on Cora, 96.4% on Pubmed, 90.6% on Bitcoin, 91.7% on Facebook, and 83.8% on OGB-arxiv, while maintaining clean accuracy within 1–2% of the clean graph baseline. Under the strongest defense (Prune+LD), EUMC retains 93.5% ASR on Cora, 90.8% on Flickr, and 84.2% on OGB-arxiv, substantially outperforming all baselines. On Flickr, where GTA achieves 93.1% ASR without defense, EUMC (90.4%) is slightly lower but dramatically more robust under defenses (90.3–90.8% vs. GTA's 14.6–15.0%). Statistical significance testing confirms EUMC's superiority over DPGBA with p-values of 6.32×10⁻²¹ (Cora) and 2.70×10⁻⁹ (OGB-arxiv).

## Baselines & Comparisons

EUMC outperforms all four baselines on 5 of 6 datasets under no defense, with the largest margins on Cora (+10.1% over GTA, +10.1% over DPGBA) and OGB-arxiv (+15.0% over DPGBA, +15.4% over GTA). On Flickr, GTA achieves a slightly higher ASR (93.1% vs. 90.4%) without defense, but EUMC is far more robust: under Prune+LD, EUMC maintains 90.8% ASR while GTA drops to 14.6%, and under OD, EUMC retains 89.6% versus GTA's 26.4%. DPGBA, the strongest baseline overall, is consistently outperformed by EUMC by 5–15 percentage points across all datasets and defenses. UGBA and SBA are substantially weaker, with UGBA dropping below 25% ASR on Flickr under any defense and SBA remaining near random (14.3% on Cora/Flickr, 33.3% on Pubmed/Bitcoin). The paper's key advantage is robustness to defenses: EUMC's ASR degrades by less than 4 points from no-defense to Prune+LD on most datasets, whereas baselines like GTA and DPGBA suffer 50+ point drops on Flickr and OGB-arxiv.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of graph backdoor attacks on node classification established by GTA, UGBA, and DPGBA, which all rely on adaptive trigger generators to produce node-specific triggers for a single target category. The key conceptual departure is replacing the learned trigger generator with a pool of subgraphs sampled directly from the attacked graph (MC-STP), using attachment probability shifts as category-aware priors to assign each subgraph to a target category. This 'select from existing structure' paradigm sidesteps the optimization bottleneck that causes adaptive generators to degrade as the number of target categories grows, while naturally preserving the feature distribution of the original graph.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Multi-category backdoor attacks are a more realistic and dangerous threat model than single-category attacks, as they allow an adversary to manipulate predictions across an arbitrary set of labels. The paper demonstrates that adaptive trigger generators fail dramatically at scale (UGBA drops from ~99% to ~25% ASR on Flickr as categories go from 1 to 7), motivating the subgraph-pool approach. However, performance still degrades on very high-cardinality datasets (OGB-arxiv with 40 classes), and the authors acknowledge limited exploration of defensive countermeasures, leaving open the question of how to build robust defenses against category-agnostic subgraph triggers.
