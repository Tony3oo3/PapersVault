## Problem

Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) frequently generate semantically coherent but factually incorrect responses that are inconsistent with the input image, a problem rooted in the model's over-reliance on language priors during autoregressive decoding. Existing decoding-based mitigation strategies are largely empirical and lack a principled mechanism to explicitly quantify and dynamically control the mutual relevance between the visual input and the progressively generated text.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253516/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (processed into 256 or 576 visual tokens via a vision encoder and projection layer) and a free-form text instruction as input, and autoregressively generates a text response (e.g., an image caption) while simultaneously producing a binary visual token mask of size N at each decoding step. The mask dynamically determines which subset of visual tokens (controlled by a retention ratio γ, typically 80–90%) is fed to the LLM backbone, effectively shrinking the visual context as generation progresses. The purifier operates on the concatenated embeddings of image, instruction, and previously generated tokens, so the mask adapts to the evolving textual context at every step.

## Method

CMI-VLD reformulates hallucination mitigation as a conditional pointwise mutual information (C-PMI) maximization problem between the image and generated text, decomposed into a bi-level optimization with an inner text subproblem and an outer visual subproblem solved alternately at each decoding step. For the text subproblem, it constructs a calibrated token distribution (Eq. 5) that amplifies the log-probability gap between vision-conditioned and vision-free predictions, controlled by a contrastive strength λ. For the visual subproblem, a lightweight learnable purifier (a few transformer blocks and MLPs, <1% of LVLM parameters) predicts a per-token retain/discard probability, trained with Gumbel-Softmax for differentiable discrete selection and a loss combining C-PMI, layer-2 attention scores, and a Frobenius-norm regularization on the retention ratio. The purifier is trained on 2,000–4,000 image-text pairs from ShareGPT4V for 5 epochs, and at inference it filters redundant visual tokens so the LLM attends more to image regions pertinent to the ongoing response, thereby amplifying cross-modal mutual information throughout generation.

## Results

On the CHAIR metric (MSCOCO, 500 images), CMI-VLD achieves the lowest hallucination rates across all four LVLMs under both sampling and greedy decoding. Under greedy decoding, CMI-VLD reduces CHAIR_S to 29.9 on LLaVA-1.5 (vs. 45.0 for greedy baseline), 43.2 on InstructBLIP (vs. 52.2), 30.6 on Shikra (vs. 54.8), and 27.2 on LLaVA-Next (vs. 31.6). On the GPT-4o-assisted benchmark, CMI-VLD achieves a 15.89% relative improvement in Sentence-level Hallucination Ratio (SHR) over the best baseline on LLaVA-1.5. On MME and MMBench, CMI-VLD also improves general multimodal capability scores (1481.17 and 65.12 respectively) over the greedy baseline (1465.11 and 64.86).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CMI-VLD consistently outperforms all seven baselines (ICD, VCD, VTI, HALC, OPERA, SID, VASparse) on CHAIR across all four LVLMs under both sampling and greedy decoding. The largest gains are over ICD and VCD, which in some cases actually worsen hallucination relative to the default decoding (e.g., ICD increases LLaVA-1.5 CHAIR_S from 52.2 to 51.0 under sampling but 45.0→44.8 under greedy, while VCD worsens LLaVA-1.5 from 45.0 to 49.4 under greedy). Among the strongest competitors, VTI is the closest baseline on LLaVA-1.5 greedy (30.6 vs. 29.9 CHAIR_S) and LLaVA-Next greedy (30.1 vs. 27.2), while OPERA is competitive on Shikra (36.8 vs. 30.6). On POPE (Table 2), CMI-VLD achieves the best accuracy across all three splits (Random/Popular/Adversarial) for both LLaVA-Next and InstructBLIP under greedy decoding, e.g., 86.43% vs. SID's 84.97% on LLaVA-Next Random. On MME and MMBench, CMI-VLD also edges out all baselines (1481.17 MME, 65.12 MMBench), confirming that hallucination reduction does not come at the cost of general multimodal capability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies the contrastive decoding line (VCD, ICD, SID, OPERA) under a single information-theoretic objective—conditional pointwise mutual information (C-PMI)—showing that existing token-distribution calibration methods are special cases when only the text modality is optimized. It departs from prior work by extending the optimization to the visual modality as well, introducing a learnable visual token purifier (architecturally inspired by DynamicViT) that dynamically refines image tokens at each decoding step, and by incorporating the latent-space feature steering mechanism from VTI. The bi-level optimization formulation (inner: calibrated text sampling; outer: visual token selection) is the paper's core structural departure from the single-level, text-only contrastive decoding paradigm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By grounding contrastive decoding in C-PMI maximization, the paper provides a principled objective that subsumes and extends several ad-hoc logit-subtraction heuristics, offering a clearer design space for future decoding strategies. The visual token purification component is practically important because it reduces inference cost (fewer tokens to process) while improving faithfulness, and the learned purifier achieves ~4× speedup over a learning-free variant at comparable quality. Key limitations include the fixed retention ratio across all decoding steps, the need for a small training set to learn the purifier per model, and sensitivity to the contrastive strength λ; open questions include whether adaptive, step-dependent retention ratios or end-to-end joint optimization of text and visual tokens could yield further gains.
