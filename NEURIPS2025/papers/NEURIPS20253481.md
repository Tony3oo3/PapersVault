## Problem

Existing contextual dynamic pricing algorithms assume all buyers share a single unknown valuation type θ*, making them inapplicable when the buyer population is heterogeneous with types drawn from an unknown distribution. The core challenge is that the infinite continuous price space, discontinuous revenue function, and context-dependent (non-i.i.d.) stochasticity from type heterogeneity prevent direct application of regression-based or multi-dimensional binary search methods.

## Input / Output

At each round, the seller observes a d-dimensional context vector u_t on the unit sphere and posts a price p_t ∈ [0,1]; the buyer's type θ_t is sampled from an unknown distribution D* with finite support size K*, and the seller receives only binary purchase feedback y_t = 1{⟨θ_t, u_t⟩ ≥ p_t}. Contexts may be chosen adversarially, and the seller has no access to θ_t or K*. The output is a single scalar price per round, with the objective of minimizing cumulative regret against the best-response price under the true type distribution.

## Method

The main algorithm, Perturbed Optimistic Posterior Sampling (POPS), maintains a posterior over candidate type distributions and best-responds to a sampled model each round, using a loss that combines squared model-mismatch penalty with an optimism bias toward high-revenue models. The key technical insight is that the aggregate demand function under D* has at most K* jumps, so the disagreement coefficient decomposes into K*+1 non-increasing pieces, yielding dis ≤ 2(K*+1) and regret Õ(K*√(dT)). For the infinite model class, POPS adds conservative price perturbation and discretization, then couples the actual trajectory to one where D* lies in a finite Lévy-metric cover of log-cardinality Õ(dK* log T). For the non-contextual case (d=1), a separate ZoomV algorithm combines adaptive dyadic discretization with variance-aware confidence intervals, achieving the tighter Õ(√(K*T)) regret by exploiting a variance-aware zooming dimension that is zero for pricing.

## Results

The main contextual pricing algorithm (POPS) achieves regret Õ(K*√(dT)) without prior knowledge of K*, which is proven tight in d and T up to logarithmic factors via a matching lower bound of Ω(√(K*dT)) for T ≥ 8dK*³log(2d). In the non-contextual case (d=1), the ZoomV algorithm improves the dependence on K* to achieve Õ(min{√(K*T), T^(2/3)}), which is minimax optimal up to logarithmic factors. Under stronger type observability (full type vector revealed), regret improves further to Õ(√(min{K*,d}·T)).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper with no empirical baselines; comparisons are against prior regret bounds. For non-contextual pricing, Cesa-Bianchi et al. [4] achieve Õ(√(K*T)) + V(V+1) where V = max_i (θ^(K*))⁴(θ^(i)−θ^(i−1))⁻⁵ can diverge for worst-case type distributions; this paper removes the separation assumption entirely, achieving Õ(√(K*T)) uniformly. Standard zooming analysis (without variance awareness) yields only O(T^(2/3)) regardless of K*, while ZoomV's variance-aware confidence intervals unlock the √(K*T) dependence. In the large-K* regime (K* = Õ((T/d)^(1/3))), POPS no longer dominates EXP4 with naive discretization (which gives d^(2/5)T^(4/5)), and the paper notes this as an open question. The contextual lower bound of Ω(√(K*dT)) confirms optimality in d and T, leaving a √(K*) gap between upper and lower bounds in the general contextual case.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends contextual dynamic pricing from the homogeneous-buyer setting (single unknown type θ*) to a heterogeneous population where buyer types are drawn from an unknown distribution D* with finite support K*. It builds on the optimistic posterior sampling (OPS) framework of Zhang [29] and the disagreement coefficient analysis of Foster et al. [7, 8], adapting them to the pricing-specific structure of one-sided Lipschitz revenue and piecewise-constant aggregate demand. For the non-contextual refinement, it combines zooming from Lipschitz bandits [13, 24] with variance-aware confidence intervals from Audibert et al. [2], and improves upon Cesa-Bianchi et al. [4] by removing their type-separation assumption. The key departure from prior contextual pricing work (regression-based [12, 11, 6, 19] or binary-search [5, 18, 21, 17]) is that those approaches fundamentally rely on a single type and cannot handle the non-i.i.d., context-dependent stochasticity introduced by heterogeneity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to establish tight (up to log factors) regret bounds for contextual dynamic pricing with heterogeneous buyers, resolving a natural open question left by the homogeneous literature. The optimal √(K*T) dependence in the non-contextual case, achieved via a novel variance-aware zooming dimension that vanishes (ZoomDimV = 0 vs. ZoomDim = 1), demonstrates that variance adaptation is essential for worst-case guarantees. Key open questions remain: the computational cost of POPS is exponential in K* and d, the optimal K* dependence in the general contextual case is unresolved, and it is unclear whether the approach extends to broader single-bit-feedback settings beyond pricing (e.g., ε-ball or symmetric loss).
