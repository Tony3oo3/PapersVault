## Problem

Event-based structured light systems inherit the high dynamic range advantage of event cameras, yet reflective and absorptive surfaces still produce event clutter (overexposure) and event absence (underexposure) that degrade 3D reconstruction. No prior framework specifically addresses HDR scene measurement in event-based SL, leaving a gap between the sensor's theoretical dynamic range and practical reconstruction quality on real-world surfaces.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251126/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes three rectified stereo pairs at 1280×704 (synthetic) or 1280×720 (real): the left views are binary event frames generated by projecting three pairs of speckle patterns with distinct contrast levels ([32,55], [32,200], [0,255]) and mutually independent content, while the right views are the corresponding projected reference patterns. Speckle energy maps, computed from the binary pattern structure, serve as an additional input to the confidence estimation module. The network outputs a disparity map with a 256-pixel search range, which is converted to depth and 3D point clouds; the full system operates at a 200 Hz sensing rate.

## Method

The framework combines a multi-contrast HDR coding strategy with a confidence-driven stereo matching pipeline integrated into IGEV. The coding strategy projects three pairs of binary speckle patterns with varying contrast and content, enabling range-partitioned sensing so that at least one pair captures each reflectance region without per-scene tuning. The Energy-Guided Confidence Estimation (ECE) module uses deformable convolutions (DCNv4) to jointly process each frame's raw speckle data and its energy map, producing a per-frame confidence map that scores speckle quality via a residual structure over all three frames. The Confidence Propagation Volume (CPV) then expands these scalar confidences along the channel dimension to weight left features, and propagates the same weights along the disparity dimension to modulate right features, ensuring consistent confidence-aware matching that suppresses inter-frame interference from over- and under-exposed regions. The resulting confidence-weighted cost volume is fed into IGEV's iterative refinement for final disparity estimation, and the entire network is trained end-to-end on a synthetic Blender-rendered dataset of 8,550 HDR scene pairs.

## Results

On the synthetic event-based HDR SL dataset, the proposed method achieves an EPE of 0.2937 px, Bad 2.0 rate of 1.22%, and D1 of 0.62%, outperforming the strongest baseline IGEV (EPE 0.3863, Bad 2.0 1.77%, D1 0.76%) by 24%, 31%, and 18% respectively, while running slightly faster (0.408 s vs. 0.420 s per frame pair). Traditional stereo methods (SGBM-3: EPE 6.33) and monocular structured-light methods (GigaDepth: EPE 8.67; CTD: EPE 26.84) are orders of magnitude worse, confirming the difficulty of HDR structured-light reconstruction. The universality experiment further shows that plugging the confidence-driven strategy into FastACV reduces EPE by 29.9% (0.7112→0.4983) and into RAFT-Stereo by 21.5% (0.4136→0.3248).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all seven baselines across every metric on the synthetic dataset. Against the closest competitor IGEV (the backbone network into which the confidence-driven strategy is integrated), it reduces EPE from 0.3863 to 0.2937 (−24%) and Bad 2.0 from 1.77% to 1.22% (−31%), while also being marginally faster (0.408 s vs. 0.420 s). RAFT-Stereo, the iterative-optimization baseline, is beaten by 29% in EPE (0.4136→0.2937) but is 2× slower. FastACV, the attention-based cost-volume method, lags by 59% in EPE (0.7112→0.2937) despite being 6× faster. Traditional stereo (SGBM-3, EPE 6.33) and monocular structured-light methods (GigaDepth EPE 8.67, CTD EPE 26.84) are 20–90× worse in EPE, underscoring the extreme difficulty of HDR structured-light depth estimation. The universality experiment (Table 3) confirms that the confidence-driven strategy is model-agnostic: it improves FastACV EPE by 29.9% and RAFT-Stereo EPE by 21.5% without architectural changes, demonstrating broad applicability beyond the IGEV backbone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends event-based structured light (SL) systems, which have been explored for high-speed 3D scanning (Matsuda et al., Muglikar et al., Li et al.), by addressing the long-standing HDR limitation that prior event-based SL methods could not overcome. It departs from conventional HDR SL approaches (multi-exposure fusion, multi-projection fusion) that require per-scene manual tuning, instead leveraging the event camera's inherent dynamic range with a fixed set of three contrast levels. On the stereo matching side, the paper builds on IGEV's iterative geometry encoding volume but introduces a confidence propagation volume (CPV) that reweights multi-frame features by per-frame quality, a mechanism absent from prior cost-volume paradigms (GC-Net, GwcNet, FastACV, RAFT-Stereo).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to achieve robust 3D reconstruction under extreme HDR conditions (specular, absorptive, transparent surfaces) using event-based SL, and it introduces the first event-based HDR SL dataset (synthetic + real-world with GT), providing a benchmark the field lacked. The demonstrated universality of the confidence-driven strategy—improving EPE by ~30% on FastACV and ~22% on RAFT-Stereo—suggests the CPV/ECE components could be adopted in other multi-frame or multi-sensor stereo settings beyond event-based SL. Key open questions include handling occluded regions (acknowledged limitation), whether the approach generalizes to other event camera hardware, and whether the Blender-based event simulation faithfully captures real sensor noise characteristics.
