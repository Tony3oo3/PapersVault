## Problem

Two-Party Split Learning (VFL) suffers from low computational resource utilization and high training latency due to its synchronous, tightly-coupled design: workers on different parties must wait for each other to complete ID-aligned forward/backward passes, and resource or data heterogeneity between parties further exacerbates idle time. Existing Parameter Server–based VFL frameworks (e.g., FATE, PaddleFL) and asynchronous VFL variants fail to decouple the ID-alignment step from training or to holistically balance heterogeneous workloads, leaving significant computational capacity unused.

## Input / Output

The system consumes vertically partitioned tabular data: the active party holds feature vectors x^a ∈ R^{d_a} plus labels y, while the passive party holds disjoint feature vectors x^p ∈ R^{d_p} for the same sample IDs (aligned via Private Set Intersection). Beyond the standard prediction output, PubSub-VFL also produces a set of optimized hyperparameters (per-party worker counts w_a, w_p and batch size B) computed offline via a dynamic-programming planner that profiles each party's CPU cores, memory, and model complexity. The trained artifact is a split model (two bottom networks f_a, f_p and a top network g) whose forward pass requires only the exchange of DP-noised embeddings and cut-layer gradients between parties.

## Method

PubSub-VFL replaces the synchronous peer-to-peer VFL pipeline with a Publisher/Subscriber message-broker layer that carries two types of channels—embedding channels and gradient channels—each indexed by a unique batch ID, thereby decoupling ID alignment from worker computation and enabling fully asynchronous inter-party communication. Within each party, a Parameter Server coordinates multiple workers under a dynamic semi-asynchronous aggregation rule whose synchronization interval ΔT_t shrinks via a tanh-scheduled formula as the model approaches target accuracy, balancing throughput against gradient staleness. A FIFO buffer (capacity p embeddings, q gradients) and a waiting-deadline mechanism (T_ddl) guard against channel congestion and stragglers. To handle resource and data heterogeneity, the paper formulates a min-max optimization over worker counts and batch size (Eq. 14) and solves it with a dynamic-programming search over the discrete (w_a, w_p, B) space, using per-party system profiles (CPU cores, memory, model FLOPs) to equalize T_A and T_P. Gaussian Differential Privacy noise is injected into passive-party embeddings, and a convergence theorem (Theorem D.1) proves linear convergence under L-smoothness, μ-strong convexity, bounded staleness τ ≤ ΔT, and the added DP noise variance σ_dp².

## Results

On the five benchmark datasets, PubSub-VFL achieves the best accuracy on 4 out of 5: Blog RMSE 22.34 (vs. 23.12 for VFL-PS), Bank AUC 96.54 (vs. 94.54 for VFL), Credit AUC 82.34 (vs. 81.90 for VFL), and Synthetic AUC 92.87 (vs. 91.31 for VFL-PS). On Energy (RMSE, lower is better), PubSub-VFL (85.64) is slightly worse than VFL-PS (84.44). In terms of efficiency, PubSub-VFL achieves a 2–7× reduction in training time and up to 91.07% CPU utilization, with a 35% higher CPU utilization than the best baseline (AVFL-PS) on the synthetic dataset.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PubSub-VFL is compared against four baselines: Pure VFL (synchronous, no PS), VFL-PS (synchronous with Parameter Server, as in FATE/PaddleFL), AVFL (asynchronous VFL without PS), and AVFL-PS (asynchronous VFL with PS, as in Falcon). On accuracy, PubSub-VFL outperforms all four baselines on Blog (RMSE 22.34 vs. best baseline 23.12), Bank (AUC 96.54 vs. 94.54), Credit (AUC 82.34 vs. 81.90), and Synthetic (AUC 92.87 vs. 91.31), but is slightly worse on Energy (RMSE 85.64 vs. 84.44 for VFL-PS). The largest accuracy gains are on Bank (+2.0 AUC over VFL) and Synthetic (+1.56 AUC over VFL-PS). On computational efficiency, PubSub-VFL achieves a 7× reduction in running time and 35% higher CPU utilization compared to AVFL-PS (the strongest efficiency baseline), while also reducing communication cost due to its hierarchical asynchronous mechanism improving convergence speed. Under resource-heterogeneous settings (e.g., 50:14 CPU core ratio), PubSub-VFL maintains 87.42% CPU utilization versus only 42.12% for AVFL-PS, demonstrating the effectiveness of its dynamic programming-based hyperparameter optimization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on two lines of VFL system research: (1) parameter-server-based VFL frameworks (FATE, PaddleFL, Falcon) that achieve intra-party data parallelism but remain synchronously coupled across parties, and (2) asynchronous VFL methods that reduce straggler effects but still struggle with the fundamental ID-alignment coupling inherent to vertical partitioning. PubSub-VFL departs from both by introducing a Publisher/Subscriber layer that decouples the ID-alignment task from worker training, enabling true inter-party asynchrony, while retaining PS-based data parallelism within each party. The hierarchical asynchronous design (Pub/Sub asynchrony outside PS + adaptive semi-asynchronous aggregation inside PS) and the privacy-preserving dynamic-programming hyperparameter optimizer are the paper's principal architectural and algorithmic contributions beyond prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 2–7× training speedup and up to 91% CPU utilization are practically significant for industrial VFL deployments where parties have heterogeneous hardware and feature dimensions, and the dynamic-programming-based hyperparameter selection under privacy constraints offers a principled alternative to manual tuning. However, the framework is explicitly limited to two-party settings, and extending the Pub/Sub channel design and the load-balancing optimization to multi-party VFL remains an open problem. The reliance on a synchronous baseline to estimate computation/communication constants for the optimizer also limits applicability in highly dynamic or previously uncharacterized network environments.
