## Problem

Deep learning architectures are designed in silo for specific data types—CNNs for grids, GNNs for graphs, transformers for sequences—each embedding assumptions (isotropic propagation, a single shared latent space, homophily) that fail on heterogeneous, directional, or multiscale structured data. No principled framework exists to unify these paradigms or to design architectures that natively encode diverse local behaviors, directional couplings, and multi-scale information flow across arbitrary topological domains.

## Input / Output

The method consumes cell features defined on a combinatorial complex (CC), which subsumes Euclidean grids (pixels as 0-cells), graphs (nodes/edges), simplicial or cell complexes, and hypergraphs. Each cell carries its own feature vector in a potentially distinct stalk space, and the CC's neighborhood structure (adjacency, incidence, or custom) determines which cells interact. Outputs are task-specific: predicted field values for PDE simulation (heat, advection, unsteady Stokes), binary graph class labels on MUTAG, or binary complex class labels on synthetic CCs with varying triangle density.

## Method

CTNNs formulate message passing via copresheaves on combinatorial complexes: each cell x is assigned a vector space F(x) (its stalk), and each directed edge y→x in the neighborhood-induced graph carries a learnable linear transport map ρ_{y→x}: F(y)→F(x) that re-embeds y's features into x's coordinate frame before aggregation. This replaces the single-latent-space, isotropic aggregation of standard GNNs/transformers with heterogeneous, anisotropic, per-edge feature-space transformations. The framework unifies CNNs, GNNs, transformers, sheaf neural networks, and topological neural networks as special cases (e.g., SNN message passing reduces to a single copresheaf morphism on a bidirected graph; dot-product attention corresponds to ρ=I). A copresheaf Laplacian L_ρ = B_ρ^T W B_ρ provides an energy interpretation, showing that a residual CTNN layer performs an explicit gradient step on a transport-discrepancy energy, guaranteeing monotone energy decay. Concrete instantiations include copresheaf transformers (learned per-edge maps applied to value vectors in attention), copresheaf GNNs (per-edge transport maps ρ_{ij}=I+Δ_{ij} composed with GCN/SAGE/GIN aggregation), and copresheaf convolutions on grids.

## Results

On MUTAG graph classification, copresheaf-enhanced GNNs consistently outperform their standard counterparts: CopresheafSAGE achieves 73.2% accuracy vs. 68.9% for GraphSAGE, CopresheafGCN reaches 72.1% vs. 67.4% for GCN, and CopresheafGIN attains 72.4% vs. 70.0% for GIN. On physics simulation tasks, copresheaf attention reduces test MSE by over 50% on heat (9.0×10⁻⁵ vs. 2.64×10⁻⁴) and advection (1.20×10⁻⁴ vs. 3.52×10⁻⁴) compared to classical dot-product attention, and by ~15% on unsteady Stokes. On combinatorial complex classification, CT-SharedLoc achieves 97.0% accuracy versus 94.0% for the standard transformer.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares CTNN variants against four classical baselines across three task families. On MUTAG, all three copresheaf GNNs win: CopresheafSAGE (73.2%) beats GraphSAGE (68.9%) by 4.3 points, CopresheafGCN (72.1%) beats GCN (67.4%) by 4.7 points, and CopresheafGIN (72.4%) beats GIN (70.0%) by 2.4 points. On physics PDE simulation, copresheaf attention (using learned outer-product transport maps) outperforms the standard dot-product attention of Vaswani et al. [2017] by over 50% MSE reduction on heat and advection, and by ~15% with an order-of-magnitude variance reduction on unsteady Stokes. On combinatorial complex classification, CT-SharedLoc (97.0%) and CT-FC (95.5%) both outperform the classical transformer (94.0%), with the shared-local parameterization yielding the best accuracy and stability. In all comparisons, the copresheaf variants win, with the largest relative gains observed on the physics tasks where directional, anisotropic transport is most critical.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions CTNNs as a categorical unification of several previously separate deep learning paradigms: standard GNNs (GCN, GAT, GraphSAGE, GIN), sheaf neural networks (Hansen & Gebhart 2020, Bodnar et al. 2022), topological neural networks on combinatorial complexes (Hajij et al. 2023b), and transformers. The key conceptual departure from the sheaf-based line is the switch from cellular sheaves (undirected, edge-stalked, enforcing local-to-global consistency via restriction maps) to copresheaves (directed, vertex-stalked, with learnable pushforward maps), which Theorem 1 and Proposition 1 show subsume SNN message-passing and neural sheaf diffusion as special cases. The framework is grounded in combinatorial complexes (Hajij et al. 2023a,b) as the underlying domain, and the copresheaf Laplacian is shown to coincide with the quiver Laplacian (Sumray et al. 2024), linking the construction to established spectral methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unifying lens is genuinely useful for researchers working across GNN, sheaf, and topological DL communities, as it clarifies which architectural choices (neighborhood type, transport map structure, aggregation) are independent design axes rather than ad hoc decisions. However, the empirical validation is limited to small-scale settings (188-graph MUTAG, 600-sample synthetic PDEs, 250 synthetic CCs), and the per-edge parameter overhead raises scalability questions that the authors acknowledge but do not resolve. Open questions include whether the copresheaf structure provides meaningful inductive bias at scale, how to parameterize transport maps efficiently for large graphs, and whether the energy-decay guarantee (Theorem 3) translates into practical training stability advantages over standard residual updates.
