## Problem

Existing Ego-RVOS methods learn spurious correlations rather than true causal relationships, failing under two distinct confounding sources: dataset-level biases where specific object-action pairs co-occur frequently (observable confounder), and inherent egocentric visual distortions such as rapid camera motion and hand-object occlusions (unobservable confounder). No prior work applies causal inference to simultaneously address both observable and unobservable confounders in the referring video object segmentation setting, leaving models brittle on novel objects, rare actions, and visually ambiguous egocentric scenes.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a first-person video clip (RGB frames resized to 448×448) paired with a natural language query that combines an object name and an action description (e.g., 'knife used to cut carrot'), and additionally extracts geometric depth features from a frozen Depth Anything V2 encoder and temporal context from a sliding window of W=5 recent frames. It produces a per-frame binary segmentation mask (H×W) delineating the object actively involved in the described action, along with a binary activity classification (positive vs. negative object). CERES is designed as a plug-in module that wraps a pre-trained third-person RVOS backbone (ReferFormer), so the downstream mask decoder and classification heads remain unchanged.

## Method

CERES implements dual-modal causal intervention grounded in a Structural Causal Model with two adjustment modules. The Linguistic Back-door Deconfounder (LBD) blocks the spurious path T←Z→Y by constructing a fixed dictionary of object-action pair embeddings weighted by their empirical training frequencies, then adding the frequency-weighted mean confounder embedding to the text query representation (an NWGM approximation of the backdoor-adjusted score). The Visual Front-door Deconfounder (VFD) addresses the unobservable confounder U via a vision-depth mediator: Depth-guided Attention (DAttn) uses aggregated depth tokens as queries to attend over RGB feature tokens (cross-attention), yielding a mediator that is provably the MMSE estimator within the Attention-Linear Family and is less sensitive to egocentric distortions; Temporal Memory Attention (MAttn) estimates the general visual context E[X'] via softmax-weighted aggregation over a 5-frame sliding window. The final de-biased visual feature fuses the mediator and context through a gated residual MLP, and both de-biased text and visual features feed into the frozen ReferFormer backbone for mask prediction. The framework is trained end-to-end on VISOR with auxiliary segmentation losses at intermediate layers, and evaluated zero-shot on VOST and VSCOS.

## Results

On the VISOR benchmark, CERES achieves state-of-the-art performance across all three backbones. With ResNet-101, it reaches 64.0% mIoU⊕ (+4.1% over ActionVOS), 72.4% gIoU (+2.5%), and 76.3% accuracy. On the novel-object subset of VISOR (open-vocabulary evaluation), CERES attains 60.0% mIoU⊕ and 67.9% gIoU, surpassing ActionVOS by +4.7% and +2.1% respectively. On zero-shot VSCOS and VOST (trained only on VISOR), CERES achieves 55.3% mIoU⊕ and 32.0% mIoU⊕, outperforming ActionVOS by +2.8% and +1.8%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CERES consistently outperforms all three baselines across every backbone and dataset. Against ActionVOS (the strongest prior method), CERES gains +4.1% mIoU⊕ and +2.5% gIoU on VISOR with R101, +2.5% mIoU⊕ and +2.6% gIoU with VSwinB, and +0.7% mIoU⊕ and +3.1% gIoU with SwinL, while also substantially reducing mIoU⊖ (e.g., 16.9% vs. 22.8% on SwinL), indicating better rejection of non-target objects. Against ReferFormer+ (the pre-trained backbone with an auxiliary positivity head), CERES improves mIoU⊕ by +5.8% (R101) and +4.3% (VSwinB), with gIoU gains of +9.3% and +3.7% respectively. Against HOS (hand-object segmentation), CERES achieves +11.9% mIoU⊕ on SwinL while maintaining comparable mIoU⊖, showing that its causal interventions yield far better active-object segmentation than proximity-based heuristics. On the novel-object subset and zero-shot VSCOS/VOST evaluations, the margins over ActionVOS widen (+4.7% mIoU⊕ on novel VISOR; +2.8% on VSCOS), confirming that the back-door and front-door adjustments specifically improve generalization to unseen concepts and challenging visual conditions.

## Relation to Prior Work

CERES builds on the ReferFormer architecture as a pre-trained RVOS backbone and the Ego-RVOS task formulation established by ActionVOS and the VISOR benchmark. Conceptually, it departs from prior causal-inference-in-vision-language work (e.g., causal attention for VQA, deconfounded captioning) that typically applies either back-door or front-door adjustment to a single modality, by unifying both adjustments in a dual-modal framework specifically tailored to video segmentation. The front-door component introduces a novel vision-depth mediator (using a frozen Depth Anything V2 encoder) that has no direct analogue in prior RVOS or causal vision-language literature, while the back-door component repurposes the NWGM approximation from deconfounded captioning to correct object-action co-occurrence biases in referring queries.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-in causal framework offers a principled, modular way to improve robustness of any pre-trained RVOS model in the egocentric domain without retraining the backbone from scratch, which is practically valuable given the cost of egocentric video annotation. Key open questions include whether the ALF (attention-linear family) restriction on the mediator can be relaxed while preserving front-door identifiability, whether the sliding-window temporal context approximation generalizes to long-range dependencies, and whether the approach extends beyond Ego-RVOS to broader egocentric understanding tasks like action recognition. The reliance on a frozen depth estimator also means performance is bounded by the quality of monocular depth in challenging egocentric scenes (extreme motion, novel viewpoints).
