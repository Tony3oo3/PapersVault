## Problem

Professional photo retouching tools like Adobe Lightroom offer powerful but opaque manual controls that demand substantial expertise, while existing AI-based solutions (optimization, RL, diffusion-based editing) suffer from limited stylistic diversity, poor fine-grained local control, and compromised content fidelity. Instruction-guided multimodal models such as GPT-4o and Gemini-2-Flash enable natural-language editing but regenerate all pixels destructively, lack interactive region-specific adjustments, and cannot handle arbitrary-resolution inputs. The paper addresses the absence of an intelligent agent that combines professional retouching reasoning with transparent, non-destructive tool orchestration.

## Input / Output

The system accepts a source image at any resolution, a free-form natural-language instruction, and optionally spatial cues (draggable bounding boxes or free-form brushstrokes) to designate regions of interest. It produces a structured Lightroom Retouching Operation Configuration (ROC) file encoding 200+ tool invocations with precise parameter values (e.g., exposure+0.03, radial mask with center/scale/rotation), which is then executed by Lightroom via the Agent-to-Lightroom protocol to yield the final retouched image. Because editing is parameter-based rather than pixel-regenerative, the output preserves original content and supports non-destructive, iteratively refinable workflows.

## Method

JarvisArt is built on Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct and trained in two stages: first, Chain-of-Thought supervised fine-tuning on 50K MMArt samples instills a structured 'understand → reason → decide' workflow and basic Lightroom tool-selection proficiency; second, Group Relative Policy Optimization for Retouching (GRPO-R) refines decision-making using three interpretable rewards—format compliance, retouching operation accuracy (matching tool names, parameter names, and parameter values across six mask types), and perceptual quality (CIELAB color-distribution similarity plus pixel-wise fidelity). The MMArt-55K dataset is synthesized via a three-stage pipeline leveraging Qwen2.5-VL-72B for multimodal role-playing and Grounding DINO for region localization, producing source-target image pairs, diverse user instructions, and step-by-step CoT reasoning traces. An Agent-to-Lightroom (A2L) protocol provides a standardized client-server interface with dual-transport communication, ROC-to-Lua translation, and sandboxed execution to enable seamless, automated Lightroom integration with bidirectional feedback.

## Results

On MMArt-Bench (200 instances across portrait, landscape, street, and still-life scenes), JarvisArt achieves an L1×10² of 12.44, a 45.6% reduction over GPT-4o (22.84), while maintaining a competitive overall instruction-following score of O=8.52 (vs. GPT-4o's 9.18 and Gemini-2-Flash's 8.08). On the region-level (portrait mask) evaluation, JarvisArt's advantage is amplified: L1RC×10² of 7.75 versus 15.67 for GPT-4o and 16.74 for Gemini-2-Flash. On the held-out MIT-FiveK benchmark, JarvisArt generalizes well with L1×10²=12.98 and O=8.48, again outperforming GPT-4o (L1×10²=21.49, O=9.22) on content fidelity while matching it on perceptual quality (PQ=9.82 vs. 9.76).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the two non-instruction-based retouching baselines (RSFNet and 3DLUT), JarvisArt is competitive on pixel-level fidelity (L1×10²: 12.66 vs. 11.62/11.51) but uniquely supports natural-language instructions and region-specific edits. Among instruction-driven open-source methods, JarvisArt dominates across all metrics: it reduces L1×10² by 19–55% relative to InstructPix2Pix (15.62), MagicBrush (18.31), OmniGen (28.40), VARGPT-v1.1 (27.04), and Step1X-Edit (24.17), while also achieving the highest PQ scores (8.51 vs. 4.94–5.81). Against the strongest closed-source competitors, JarvisArt achieves a 45.6% lower L1×10² than GPT-4o (12.44 vs. 22.84) and a 45.2% lower L1×10² than Gemini-2-Flash (12.44 vs. 23.06), indicating substantially better content preservation; however, GPT-4o retains a slight edge on the overall instruction-following score (O=9.18 vs. 8.52), while JarvisArt surpasses Gemini-2-Flash (O=8.08). In the region-level portrait setting where content fidelity is most critical, JarvisArt's L1RC×10² of 7.75 is roughly half that of GPT-4o (15.67) and Gemini-2-Flash (16.74), and it achieves the best ORC (7.91) among all instruction-based methods, matching GPT-4o's 7.95.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the GRPO reinforcement learning framework from DeepSeekMath [48] to the photo retouching domain, replacing the single-answer reward with a multi-dimensional tool-use reward (format + operation accuracy + perceptual quality). Conceptually, it departs from the dominant diffusion-based editing paradigm (InstructPix2Pix, MagicBrush, OmniGen) and the closed-source instruction-editing models (GPT-4o, Gemini-2-Flash) by reframing retouching as a non-destructive, agent-driven orchestration of 200+ Lightroom operations rather than pixel regeneration. The MLLM-agent architecture draws on the broader LLM-agent literature (tool use, CoT reasoning, memory), but the key novelty is the domain-specific integration: a standardized Agent-to-Lightroom protocol and a two-stage SFT→GRPO-R training pipeline tailored to artistic parameter prediction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The non-destructive editing paradigm addresses a genuine limitation of generative editing models (content fidelity loss, resolution constraints, lack of interpretability), and the A2L protocol offers a practical template for integrating MLLM agents with professional creative software. However, the approach is tightly coupled to Lightroom's specific toolset, and the RL phase trains on only 5K samples, raising generalization concerns. The paper itself notes the absence of an 'aha moment' during GRPO-R training (unlike mathematical reasoning RL), pointing to an open question about how to provide step-wise visual feedback for artistic parameter chains without prohibitive compute cost. The MMArt-55K dataset and MMArt-Bench are useful resources, but the benchmark's reliance on MLLM-judged metrics (SC, PQ) introduces circularity that the authors acknowledge but do not fully resolve.
