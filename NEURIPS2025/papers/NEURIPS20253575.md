## Problem

LLM watermarking is particularly challenging when next-token distributions are near-deterministic (min-entropy ≤ 1 bit), a regime that covers over 90% of predictions in practical tasks like code generation. Existing watermarks lack a principled optimization framework for how to best exploit random side information to balance detection accuracy against text distortion, and no prior method achieves provable optimality guarantees in this low-entropy setting.

## Input / Output

The method takes as input the LLM's next-token distribution P_X (a probability vector over vocabulary of size m), a side information sample s ∈ [1:k] produced by hashing previous tokens with a secret key, a pre-specified score function f, and a tilting parameter δ ∈ (0,1). It outputs a watermarked conditional distribution P_{X|S=s} from which the next token is sampled, and at detection time the detector computes per-token scores f(x_t, s_t) and averages them over the sequence to produce a single detection statistic compared against a threshold τ. The OT cost matrix is an m×k matrix C where C_{x',s'} = -f(x',s'), and the watermarked distribution is recovered as P_{X|S=s}(x|s) = k · P_{X,S}(x,s) from the Sinkhorn solution.

## Method

The paper formulates watermark design as a minimax optimization that jointly selects the score function f and the watermarked coupling P_{X|S} to maximize the expected-score gap between watermarked and unwatermarked text, with the inner maximization constrained to couplings (preserving the marginal P_X and thus ensuring distortion-free generation). This inner problem is an optimal transport problem between P_X and a uniform P_S, solved efficiently via Sinkhorn's algorithm. For binary scores, the paper reveals an exact equivalence to distance-maximizing code design: SimplexWater uses simplex codes as its score function and is proven minimax-optimal among all binary-score watermarks, attaining the Plotkin bound. For continuous scores drawn i.i.d. from a distribution P_F, the paper proves detection power depends on the tail weight of the score-difference distribution; HeavyWater instantiates this with lognormal scores and is shown to subsume the Gumbel watermark as a special case. A tilting step (parameterized by δ) optionally upweights high-score tokens to trade a small, controllable distortion for substantially improved detection.

## Results

On the HumanEval coding benchmark, SimplexWater achieves the highest pass@5 (22.7%) and HeavyWater achieves the best pass@10 (27.8%), both close to the unwatermarked baseline (20.7% and 28.1% respectively). In detection-distortion tradeoff experiments (Figures 1 and 3a), HeavyWater achieves the strongest p-value (detection) at zero cross-entropy distortion, while SimplexWater Pareto-dominates the Red-Green watermark across all binary-score methods. When coupled with various hashing strategies, both methods yield up to 250% improvement in aggregated -log₁₀(p) over Red-Green (δ=2), and require fewer tokens to reach a target p-value of 10⁻³ (Figure 4).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Gumbel watermark [17], HeavyWater achieves superior detection at zero distortion, and the paper proves Gumbel is a special case of its heavy-tailed score framework (Theorem 3). Against the Inverse Transform watermark [13], both SimplexWater and HeavyWater outperform in detection-distortion tradeoff while preserving comparable or better code quality (pass@5: 22.7% vs 22.0%). Against the Correlated Channel watermark [16] (a binary-score method), SimplexWater Pareto-dominates it, as both use binary scores but SimplexWater's simplex-code construction attains the Plotkin bound. Against SynthID [11] (tournament sampling, K=15 layers), both proposed methods achieve higher detection p-values at lower distortion. Against Red-Green [10], SimplexWater strictly dominates in the binary-score regime, and simply replacing Red-Green's cost function with the proposed OT-based coupling yields up to 250% detection gain across multiple hashing strategies (Figure 3b).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies and extends the LLM watermarking literature by casting watermark design as a minimax optimization over score functions and optimal-transport couplings. It builds directly on the Red-Green watermark [15,10] and the Gumbel watermark [17], showing the latter is a special case of their general framework when scores follow a Gumbel distribution. The key conceptual departure is the connection to coding theory: for binary scores, the minimax-optimal watermark is equivalent to a simplex code attaining the Plotkin bound, a result that repurposes classical error-correcting code constructions for a completely different domain. The authors also extend their own prior work on the Correlated Channel watermark [16] by relaxing the binary-score restriction to heavy-tailed continuous scores.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The minimax optimality guarantee for SimplexWater and the tail-weight analysis for HeavyWater give practitioners principled, tunable watermarks that are distortion-free by construction and agnostic to side-information generation, addressing a practical pain point in deployment. The coding-theory connection opens a new design space (e.g., other code families, Q-ary codes) that could yield further improvements. Open questions include whether more powerful statistical tests beyond thresholding can close the remaining gap, how performance degrades under non-i.i.d. side information from sequential hashing, and whether the heavy-tail advantage persists under adversarial paraphrasing attacks.
