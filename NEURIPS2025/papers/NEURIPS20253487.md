## Problem

While diffusion models have advanced temporally consistent driving video generation, large-scale 3D scene generation requiring spatial coherence across expansive environments remains underexplored. Existing approaches either produce geometry without appearance (SemCity, XCube), require manually designed large-scale layouts that limit flexibility (UniScene, InfiniCube), or lack the multi-granular controllability needed for practical scene customization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253487/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system accepts either a high-level text prompt (enriched via RAG-augmented LLMs into structured scene descriptions and converted to vector-map layouts via scene-graph diffusion) or a user-provided BEV layout map with 3D bounding boxes as low-level geometric conditions. It sequentially produces 3D semantic occupancy volumes, multi-view images at up to 448×800 resolution, temporally coherent 7-frame video clips (autoregressively extended), and a final 3D Gaussian Splatting representation. The triplane VAE compresses occupancy into (100,100,16) latent planes, and the scene-graph-to-layout module outputs 7-D object boxes and N-point lane polylines as intermediate spatial specifications.

## Method

X-Scene employs a unified 3D-to-2D generation hierarchy: a triplane VAE with a novel deformable attention mechanism encodes 3D occupancy into three orthogonal latent planes, which are then synthesized via conditional diffusion using additive layout conditioning and cross-attention with box/text embeddings. The generated occupancy is converted to 3D Gaussian primitives and rendered into semantic/depth maps that, fused with object positional embeddings, provide pixel-aligned geometric conditioning for a Stable Diffusion v2.1-based image model. A temporal attention module (fine-tuned from the image U-Net) extends this to video generation using reference frames and relative ego poses. For large-scale expansion, a consistency-aware outpainting mechanism synchronizes the denoising of new triplane latents with the forward diffusion of known reference planes (guided by overlap masks) for geometry, and fine-tunes the image diffusion with explicit reference-image and camera-pose conditioning for visual coherence. The final scenes are lifted into 3DGS representations for rendering and simulation.

## Results

On nuScenes, X-Scene achieves the best multi-view image generation FID of 11.29 at 224×400 resolution, outperforming the next-best Glad (12.57) and MagicDrive (16.20). For 3D occupancy generation, X-Scene reduces FID³ᴰ by 51.2% over UniScene (258.8 vs. 529.6) and achieves F-Score of 0.778 vs. 0.396. In video generation, X-Scene attains the lowest FVD of 179.7 (vs. MagicDrive 217.9 and DreamForge 209.9) while using only 7-frame autoregressive clips. Downstream, generated data at 448×800 resolution yields 3D detection mAP of 27.65 and NDS of 34.48, approaching real-data performance (35.54 / 41.21).

## Baselines & Comparisons

X-Scene outperforms all 14 baselines across every evaluated metric. In occupancy reconstruction (Table 1), the triplane-VAE achieves 92.4 mIoU, surpassing UniScene (72.9), OccWorld (66.4), OccLLama (65.9), and OccSora (27.4) by large margins. For occupancy generation (Table 2), X-Scene dominates UniScene on all 2D and 3D metrics (e.g., FID³ᴰ 258.8 vs. 529.6, F³ᴰ 0.778 vs. 0.396) and substantially exceeds unconditioned DynamicCity (FID³ᴰ 1890). In image generation (Table 3), X-Scene beats MagicDrive by 4.91 FID points (11.29 vs. 16.20), DreamForge by 3.32 (14.61), and Glad by 1.28 (12.57), while also leading on BEV segmentation and 3D detection. For video generation (Table 4), X-Scene achieves the lowest FVD (179.7) versus MagicDrive (217.9) and DreamForge (209.9), and yields the best end-to-end planning L2 error (1.15 m avg.) and lowest collision rate (0.27% avg.). In downstream data augmentation (Table 6), X-Scene-generated data combined with real data achieves 39.9 mAP and 51.6 NDS for 3D detection, exceeding Vista (34.0/38.6), MagicDrive (35.4/39.8), UniScene (36.5/41.2), DreamForge (36.6/49.5), and Panacea (37.1/49.2).

## Relation to Prior Work

X-Scene sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) triplane-based 3D scene representation (Chan et al., CVPR 2022) and its extrapolation for scene expansion (BlockFusion, Wu et al., ACM TOG 2024); (2) scene-graph diffusion for layout generation (CommonScenes, NeurIPS 2023; EchoScene, ECCV 2024); and (3) the growing body of driving-specific generative models (MagicDrive, UniScene, DreamForge, SemCity). It departs from prior driving scene generators by eliminating the need for manually designed large-scale layouts (required by UniScene and InfiniCube), jointly producing geometry and appearance (unlike occupancy-only methods such as SemCity and XCube), and introducing LLM-augmented text-to-layout controllability as a first-class input. The triplane deformable attention mechanism is a novel architectural addition on top of the standard triplane VAE, and the consistency-aware outpainting strategy adapts BlockFusion's triplane extrapolation to the driving occupancy domain with overlap-masked denoising.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine gap: while temporal consistency in driving video generation is well-studied, spatial coherence for large-scale 3D scene expansion remains underexplored, and no prior method simultaneously delivers text-driven controllability, joint occupancy-image-video fidelity, and seamless spatial extrapolation. The practical impact is significant for autonomous driving simulation and data augmentation, as the generated 3DGS scenes support closed-loop evaluation and multi-view rendering. Key limitations include evaluation on a single dataset (nuScenes), short 7-frame video clips, and the absence of multi-agent interaction modeling; the authors acknowledge longer temporal horizons and multi-agent dynamics as future directions. The reliance on GPT-4o and Qwen2.5-VL for scene description enrichment also introduces a dependency on proprietary LLMs that may limit reproducibility and scalability of the text-to-layout pipeline.
