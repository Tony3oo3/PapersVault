## Problem

Optimizing the composition of training data for LLM pre-training currently relies on domain heuristics or deterministic extrapolation of scaling laws from small-scale experiments, both of which are brittle and fail to transfer across model scales or data domains. No existing framework jointly and adaptively selects data mixtures, model scales, and training steps under a fixed compute budget while explicitly modeling uncertainty in the performance landscape.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253300/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates over a joint decision space: data mixture proportions drawn from the 5-simplex (Wikipedia, StackExchange, GitHub, ArXiv, Book from SlimPajama), a discrete set of model scales (20M–1B parameters), and a discrete set of training step budgets (6000, 12000, 19700 steps). At each iteration it outputs a single configuration (mixture vector, model scale, training steps) to evaluate, and the final output is the best data mixture identified at the target scale. A key structural constraint is that training for z steps yields observations at all intermediate steps (hierarchical fidelity), whereas evaluating at one model scale provides no information about other scales (non-hierarchical), which the algorithm must exploit asymmetrically.

## Method

The paper formulates data mixture optimization as a Multi-Fidelity Multi-Scale (MFMS) Bayesian Optimization problem, where a Gaussian Process surrogate with a product of three RBF kernels (over the probability simplex, model scale, and training steps) plus a linear mean function maintains a posterior over the unknown performance function μ(w, m, z). The acquisition function is Expected Improvement per Unit cost (EIpu = EI / c(m,z)^α), with α decaying over iterations to shift from cost-aware exploration toward higher-fidelity exploitation. EI is optimized via L-BFGS-B over the simplex for each (m, z) tuple, and the configuration maximizing EIpu is selected for evaluation. The framework is benchmarked using an MLP predictor (R² > 0.95) trained on 472 OLMo 2 pre-training runs across 7 model scales, and achieves 2.6×–3.3× speedup over Hyperband and random search baselines in reaching the best downstream-task configuration.

## Results

The MFMS-GP method achieves a 2.6× to 3.3× speedup over both Hyperband and Random Search baselines in finding the optimal data mixture configuration, measured by evaluation units (normalized FLOPS) required to reach the best downstream task accuracy or lowest validation cross-entropy loss. The predictor used as a surrogate benchmark achieves R² > 0.95 across all 11 output metrics. The speedup is consistent across both evaluation objectives: maximizing downstream accuracy (HellaSwag, PIQA, ARC-Easy) and minimizing validation cross-entropy on held-out data (CommonCrawl, C4).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares MFMS-GP against two baselines: Hyperband (implemented via SMAC with a random forest surrogate and EI acquisition, fixing model scale at the target 1B throughout) and Random Search (uniformly sampling data proportions at the largest model size and maximum training steps). MFMS-GP outperforms both baselines by a 2.6× to 3.3× factor in the number of evaluation units needed to identify the best data mixture, as shown in convergence plots (Figures 3 and 4) across 20 random seeds. The advantage stems from MFMS-GP's ability to exploit cheap evaluations at smaller model scales (20M–700M) and fewer training steps to rapidly narrow the search space before committing compute to the target 1B scale. Hyperband, while also multi-fidelity over training steps, cannot leverage the model-scale dimension and thus wastes budget training full-size models on suboptimal mixtures. Random Search, which always trains at the target scale and full steps, is the least efficient baseline. The paper notes that even this simple GP-based implementation with standard RBF kernels and EIpu acquisition achieves these gains, suggesting further improvements are possible with better kernel design or acquisition functions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends multi-fidelity Bayesian optimization (Swersky et al. 2014; Kandasamy et al. 2017; Hutter et al. 2011), which previously varied only training steps as the fidelity dimension, by introducing model scale as a second, structurally distinct dimension—yielding the Multi-Fidelity Multi-Scale (MFMS) formulation. It departs from deterministic data mixing laws (Ye et al. 2024; Ge et al. 2025a) that fit parametric functions and extrapolate across scales, instead adopting a probabilistic GP surrogate that explicitly models uncertainty. The cost-aware acquisition function (EI per unit cost) is drawn from Lee et al. (2020), and the overall setting is positioned as a special case of multi-information source optimization (Poloczek et al. 2016), with Hyperband (Li et al. 2018) serving as the primary multi-fidelity baseline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a principled, transferable alternative to heuristic data curation and brittle deterministic extrapolation, with the 2.6×–3.3× speedup demonstrating practical efficiency gains even from a simple GP implementation. However, validation is confined to 20M–1B parameter models on five SlimPajama categories, and the benchmarking relies on an MLP predictor rather than actual training runs, leaving open whether the speedup and transferability claims hold at frontier scales (10B+) or with more heterogeneous data sources. Key open questions include kernel design exploiting the positive correlation between performance and scale/steps, batching and asynchronous evaluation strategies, and extension to fine-tuning and data filtering settings.
