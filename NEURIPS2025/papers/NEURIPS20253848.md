## Problem

Existing feedforward subject-driven video customization methods are largely limited to single-subject scenarios due to the difficulty of constructing multi-subject training data pairs, and the use of multimodal control signals (depth, mask, camera trajectory, instructive editing text) to guide and edit subjects within customized videos remains largely unexplored. Additionally, prior data pipelines fail to produce the control signal pairs needed for such conditioned generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253848/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts one or more subject images (with or without background), a text prompt that may include instructive editing instructions (e.g., 'remove the blazer'), and any combination of optional control signals: depth map sequences, segmentation mask sequences, and camera trajectories. All inputs are patchified, encoded into latent space via a 3D-VAE, and concatenated into a single 1D token sequence fed to the DiT. The output is a 64-frame video at 384×640 resolution and 24 fps, with the model able to flexibly compose any subset of conditions at inference time without retraining.

## Method

OmniVCus is a DiT-based framework fine-tuned from the 5B-parameter Wan2.1 T2V model using flow-matching loss, with two key embedding mechanisms. Lottery Embedding (LE) randomly selects K subject positions from a larger set of M during training, activating more frame position embeddings than the number of subjects actually present, thereby enabling zero-shot inference with more subjects than seen in training. Temporally Aligned Embedding (TAE) assigns identical frame embeddings to both noise tokens and temporally aligned control tokens (depth, mask) so the Transformer can extract per-frame guidance, while sparse camera signals are encoded as Plücker coordinates and added directly to noise tokens to avoid quadratic attention cost. A VideoCus-Factory pipeline constructs multi-subject training pairs from raw unlabeled videos using Kosmos-2 for captioning/detection and SAM2 for segmentation, with subject filtering and random background placement to prevent position/scale leakage. An Image-Video Transfer Mixed (IVTM) strategy bridges image instructive editing to video by aligning frame embeddings across single-subject image and video customization tasks, transferring editing capability without new video editing data.

## Results

On single-subject video customization, OmniVCus achieves CLIP-T 0.3293, CLIP-I 0.7154, DINO-I 0.5215, Temporal Consistency 0.9928, and Dynamic Degree 0.5541, surpassing the best prior method SkyReels-A2 (CLIP-T 0.2820, DINO-I 0.4612) by 0.0473 and 0.0603 respectively. On multi-subject customization, OmniVCus reaches DINO-I 0.4965 and Dynamic 0.6878, outperforming SkyReels-A2 (DINO-I 0.4107, Dynamic 0.5892) by 0.0858 and 0.0986. For camera-controlled subject customization, OmniVCus achieves DINO-I 0.5233, exceeding the best baseline CamI2V (DINO-I 0.2248) by 0.2985.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OmniVCus outperforms all nine baselines across every evaluated metric. Against single-subject customization baselines, it beats VideoBooth (DINO-I 0.3033) and DreamVideo (DINO-I 0.3792) by large margins, and surpasses the strongest single-subject baseline SkyReels-A2 (DINO-I 0.4612) by 0.0603 in identity preservation and 0.0473 in text alignment (CLIP-T). For multi-subject customization, OmniVCus exceeds SkyReels-A2 by 0.0858 in DINO-I and 0.0986 in Dynamic Degree, indicating better subject composition and motion diversity. In camera-controlled subject customization, OmniVCus dominates Motionctrl (DINO-I 0.2066), Cameractrl (DINO-I 0.1982), and CamI2V (DINO-I 0.2248) by 0.22–0.33 in DINO-I, showing substantially better identity preservation under camera trajectory control. For text-to-4D generation (Fig. 10), OmniVCus produces more 3D-consistent novel views with higher dynamic degree compared to 4D-fy and Singer et al., though no quantitative metrics are reported for this emergent capability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the feedforward subject-driven video customization line (VideoBooth, DreamVideo, SkyReels-A2, ConceptMaster) by addressing two gaps those methods leave open: multi-subject data construction and multimodal control integration. It builds on the DiT-based video generation paradigm (CogVideoX, Sora) and the flow-matching training objective, but departs from prior work by introducing a fully automated data pipeline (VideoCus-Factory) that produces multi-subject pairs and control signal pairs from raw unlabeled video, and by designing two novel embedding mechanisms (Lottery Embedding and Temporally Aligned Embedding) that have no direct counterpart in earlier subject-customization or control-condition literature. The IVTM training strategy also draws a new connection between image instructive editing (OmniEdit) and video customization, using single-subject customization as a shared bridge task.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, end-to-end recipe for multi-subject video customization with flexible multimodal control (depth, mask, camera, text editing) in a single feedforward model, which is a significant step toward production-ready video personalization. The Lottery Embedding mechanism—activating more frame positions at inference than were seen in training—is a lightweight and generalizable trick that could transfer to other token-based generation settings. Key limitations include the ~3-month data creation pipeline and dependence on the quality of Kosmos-2, SAM2, and Video-Depth-Anything for labeling; open questions include whether the approach scales beyond four subjects, whether the control signals can be made more temporally precise, and whether the IVTM transfer strategy generalizes to editing categories not covered by the image editing dataset.
