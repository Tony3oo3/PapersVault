## Problem

Transformer-based time series forecasters suffer from quadratic attention costs that limit their applicability on long sequences and resource-constrained devices, while recurrent models have historically underperformed on multivariate benchmarks. No prior work has effectively combined the expressivity of recurrent architectures with the regularization and parameter efficiency of mixing architectures for multivariate forecasting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250228/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a multivariate time series X ∈ R^{V×T} (V variates, T time steps on a uniform grid) and produces a point forecast Y ∈ R^{V×H} for H future steps, or quantile forecasts for probabilistic evaluation. The lookback window T is tunable (96–2048) and the model scales linearly in T, unlike Transformer baselines. RevIN normalization is applied per-instance and inverted at the output; the sLSTM stack strides over the variate axis, so runtime and memory scale linearly in V at a fixed number of parameters.

## Method

xLSTM-Mixer is a three-stage architecture: (1) a channel-independent NLinear forecast shared across variates provides an initial time-mixed prediction; (2) the forecast is up-projected to a higher hidden dimension D and refined by a stack of M sLSTM blocks that stride over the variate axis, jointly mixing time and variate information through exponential gating and memory mixing, with a learnable soft-prompt token η prepended to condition the initial cell state; (3) multi-view mixing computes forecasts from both the original and the reversed variate ordering (sharing all sLSTM weights) and reconciles them via a per-variate linear projection. This design is the first to combine recurrent deep learning with a mixing architecture, using weight-sharing across variates as regularization and the sLSTM's hidden-to-hidden recurrence for state tracking over long horizons. Training uses MAE loss with Adam and cosine annealing, and the multi-view setup acts as a multi-task learning objective that regularizes the sLSTM without additional parameters.

## Results

On the standard 7-dataset long-term forecasting benchmark (averaged over horizons 96/192/336/720), xLSTM-Mixer achieves the best MSE in 11/28 settings and best MAE in 16/28 settings, setting a new state-of-the-art on 6 of 7 datasets. Representative averages include MSE 0.153 / MAE 0.245 on Electricity, 0.248 / 0.307 on ETTm2, and 0.339 / 0.368 on ETTm1. On the GIFT-Eval probabilistic benchmark, xLSTM-Mixer ranks 2nd overall by CRPS (0.510) and is the top purely supervised model, trailing only TiRex (0.498). Statistical testing (Friedman + Conover post-hoc) confirms xLSTM-Mixer is significantly better than all baselines except xLSTMTime at p=0.05.

## Baselines & Comparisons

xLSTM-Mixer consistently outperforms the closest recurrent baseline xLSTMTime across nearly all datasets (e.g., Electricity MSE 0.153 vs 0.157, ETTm1 0.339 vs 0.347), with the only exception being a near-tie on Traffic (0.392 vs 0.391). Against the strongest mixer baselines, xLSTM-Mixer wins on Weather (0.219 vs 0.226 for TimeMixer++), ETTm1 (0.339 vs 0.369), and ETTm2 (0.248 vs 0.269), but loses on ETTh2 where TimeMixer achieves 0.316 MSE. Among MLP-based methods, TiDE is the strongest competitor, winning on Traffic MSE (0.356 vs 0.392) and ETTm2 MSE (0.249 vs 0.248, a near-tie), while DLinear remains competitive on Electricity (0.166 vs 0.153). The state-space model Chimera is the most challenging non-recurrent rival, matching xLSTM-Mixer on Weather MSE (0.219) and winning on ETTh2 (0.318 vs 0.340), but xLSTM-Mixer dominates on MAE across all datasets. Zero-shot pretrained models (Timer-XL, Moirai_Base) are uniformly outperformed by xLSTM-Mixer on all 6 comparable datasets, with gaps of 0.01–0.04 in MSE. On GIFT-Eval, xLSTM-Mixer (CRPS 0.510) trails only TiRex (0.498) and beats all other 38 methods, including TEMPO_ensemble (0.514) and Moirai-based models.

## Relation to Prior Work

xLSTM-Mixer sits at the intersection of two recent trends in time series forecasting: the resurgence of recurrent models (xLSTM, sLSTM) as efficient alternatives to Transformers, and the mixing-architecture paradigm popularized by MLP-Mixer and adapted to time series by TimeMixer and TSMixer. It builds directly on xLSTMTime (Alharthi & Mahmood, 2024), which first applied xLSTM to forecasting, but departs from it by replacing the trend-seasonality decomposition with a shared NLinear forecast, striding the sLSTM over variates rather than time, and introducing a multi-view mixing stage that reconciles forecasts from original and reversed variate orderings. The mixing philosophy—interleaving channel-independent and joint operations with weight-sharing for regularization—draws explicitly from MLP-Mixer and its time-series descendants, while the soft-prompt initialization of the sLSTM hidden state borrows from LLM prompt-tuning literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in achieving competitive or superior forecasting accuracy at 1–2 orders of magnitude lower memory than Transformer- and mixer-based baselines, making strong long-horizon forecasting viable on edge devices and embedded platforms. The conceptual contribution—demonstrating that recurrent models can be productively combined with mixing architectures, and that striding over variates (rather than time) is a viable and effective recurrence axis—opens a design space that prior work had not explored. Key limitations remain: the model assumes a uniform sampling grid, runtime and memory scale linearly with the number of variates (a bottleneck for very high-dimensional series), and the joint mixing of multiple views complicates interpretability. Open directions include adaptive variate grouping, continuous-time embeddings, and extension to imputation and anomaly detection.
