## Problem

Zero-shot image denoising aims to recover clean signals from a single noisy observation without any training data, clean references, or paired samples. Existing zero-shot methods (DIP, Deep Decoder, ZS-N2N, ZS-N2S, S2S) suffer from overfitting requiring early stopping, high bias on high-resolution images, or inconsistent performance across noise levels. Additionally, the theoretical foundations of compression-based denoising were limited to asymptotic results, leaving finite-sample guarantees uncharacterized.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255083/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single noisy image (grayscale or RGB, tested at 128×128 to 512×512) under either AWGN or Poisson noise, with the noise parameter (σ or α) optionally unknown and estimated from the image itself. The noisy image is decomposed into overlapping k×k patches (stride 1) for training and inference. The output is a full-resolution denoised image where each pixel is reconstructed by averaging the decoder outputs from all overlapping patches that cover that pixel, rather than relying on a single patch assignment.

## Method

ZS-NCD trains a neural compression autoencoder (3-layer CNN with GDN activations, ~0.4M params, or an MLP variant) directly on overlapping patches extracted from the single noisy image, minimizing a Lagrangian objective that combines a noise-model-specific distortion loss (MSE for AWGN, MSE or NLL for Poisson) with an entropy penalty on the discrete latent code. The built-in entropy constraint of the compression architecture acts as an implicit regularizer, preventing overfitting without early stopping or manual tuning. The Lagrangian coefficient λ is set via a heuristic (Algorithm 1) that adjusts λ until the reconstruction residual matches the expected noise variance. At inference, the trained encoder-decoder is applied to every patch, and the final denoised image is formed by averaging overlapping patch reconstructions, which yields a >2 dB gain over using only center-pixel assignments. The paper also provides finite-sample upper bounds on reconstruction error for compression-based maximum-likelihood denoisers under both AWGN and Poisson noise, showing the error decomposes into a distortion term and a rate-dependent concentration term.

## Results

Under AWGN (σ=25), ZS-NCD achieves 28.93/29.33/30.60 dB PSNR on Set11/Set13/Kodak24, outperforming the strongest zero-shot learning-based baseline ZS-N2N (27.18/28.36/29.54 dB) by 1.75–1.06 dB. Under Poisson noise (α=25), ZS-NCD reaches 27.10/27.60/28.77 dB, surpassing ZS-N2N (25.40/26.75/28.21 dB) by 1.70–0.56 dB. On fluorescence microscopy (Mouse Nuclei, σ=10), ZS-NCD attains 38.23 dB PSNR, exceeding BM3D (38.65 dB) and all learning-based zero-shot methods. ZS-NCD requires no early stopping or manual regularization, unlike DIP and DD which depend on such heuristics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ZS-NCD outperforms all learning-based zero-shot baselines (DIP, DD, S2S, ZS-N2S, ZS-N2N) on both AWGN and Poisson denoising across all three natural image datasets, with the largest margins over ZS-N2S and S2S which degrade severely on high-resolution images (e.g., ZS-N2S drops to 18.18 dB on Set13 at σ=15 vs. ZS-NCD's 31.93 dB). Against the strongest zero-shot learning baseline ZS-N2N, ZS-NCD gains 1.0–1.8 dB PSNR under AWGN and 0.6–1.7 dB under Poisson. BM3D remains competitive on grayscale Set11 under AWGN (32.22 vs. 31.35 dB at σ=15) but requires known noise variance and tends to oversmooth; ZS-NCD surpasses BM3D on all Poisson settings and on color/high-res datasets. In the appendix comparison with recent 2025 methods, ZS-NCD outperforms both DS-N2N and Pixel2Pixel under AWGN (e.g., 33.18 vs. 32.31/31.31 dB on Kodak24 at σ=15) and achieves second-best under Poisson (30.60 vs. Pixel2Pixel's 30.41 dB at α=50). Restormer, included as a supervised upper bound in Figure 2, remains above ZS-NCD, confirming the gap between supervised and zero-shot settings. On non-natural fluorescence microscopy (Mouse Nuclei), ZS-NCD achieves 38.23 dB at σ=10, outperforming all learning-based zero-shot methods and approaching BM3D (38.65 dB), while S2S collapses to 12.63 dB.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate threads: the theoretical framework of compression-based denoising (Donoho's Kolmogorov sampler, Weissman et al.'s rate-constrained source codes) and the practical machinery of learned neural compression (Balle et al.'s end-to-end optimized image compression with entropy models). The authors' own prior work (Zafari et al., 'Decompress') demonstrated that training a neural compression network on a dataset of clean images could denoise, but still required external data. ZS-NCD departs from all prior zero-shot denoisers (DIP, DD, ZS-N2N, ZS-N2S) by repurposing the entropy constraint of a compression architecture as a built-in regularizer, eliminating the need for early stopping or manual regularization that plagues untrained-network approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finite-sample theoretical bounds (Theorems 1–3) provide the first non-asymptotic characterization of how compression rate and distortion jointly determine denoising error under both Gaussian and Poisson noise, filling a gap between the asymptotic results of Weissman et al. and practical algorithm design. Practically, the entropy constraint as a natural overfitting guard makes the method robust to architecture choice (MLP outperforms CNN here) and eliminates the fragile early-stopping hyperparameter that limits DIP and DD. A key open question the authors flag is extending the theory from fixed/pre-trained compression codes to the zero-shot learned-code setting actually used in practice, which would close the loop between the guarantees and the algorithm.
