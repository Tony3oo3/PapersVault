## Problem

Existing non-asymptotic convergence results for Polyak-Ruppert averaged linear stochastic approximation (LSA) iterates are limited to i.i.d. noise, while practical applications (e.g., TD learning in reinforcement learning) involve Markovian dependence. No prior work provides explicit finite-sample Berry-Esseen rates for LSA under Markov noise, nor non-asymptotic validity guarantees for bootstrap-based confidence intervals in this dependent setting.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sequence of observations {(A(Z_k), b(Z_k))} generated by a uniformly geometrically ergodic Markov chain with known mixing time, a step-size schedule α_k = c_0/(k+k_0)^γ with γ ∈ [1/2, 1), and an initial point θ_0. It produces (i) a Kolmogorov-distance Berry-Esseen bound of order O(n^{-1/4}) for the 1-dimensional projection u^⊤√n(θ̄_n − θ*), (ii) a multiplier subsample bootstrap statistic whose distribution approximates the true sampling distribution at rate O(n^{-1/10}), and (iii) an overlapping batch means variance estimator converging at rate O(n^{-1/8}). The analysis is restricted to scalar projections u ∈ S^{d−1} rather than the full d-dimensional vector, and requires n to be known in advance to set the offset k_0.

## Method

The proof decomposes the averaged LSA error √n(θ̄_n − θ*) into a linear martingale term W and a nonlinear remainder D. The key step is a Poisson-equation decomposition of the Markov chain: the linear statistic W is rewritten as a sum of martingale-difference sequences plus boundary terms, enabling application of a Berry-Esseen bound for martingales (building on Bolthausen and Fan) with p = log n to obtain the n^{-1/4} rate. The remainder D is controlled via high-order moment bounds on products of random matrices, requiring the step-size offset k_0 to scale as (log n)^{1/γ}. For the bootstrap, the paper shows the multiplier subsample bootstrap variance estimator (an overlapping batch means estimator on the LSA iterates) equals, up to a negligible remainder, the OBM estimator applied to the unobservable noise sequence {ε(Z_ℓ)}, then applies concentration inequalities for quadratic forms of Markov chains to bound the variance estimation error, ultimately yielding the O(n^{-1/10}) bootstrap validity rate with b_n = ⌈n^{4/5}⌉ and γ = 3/5.

## Results

The paper establishes a non-asymptotic Berry-Esseen bound of O(n^{-1/4}) (up to logarithmic factors) in Kolmogorov distance for projected Polyak-Ruppert averaged LSA iterates under Markovian noise (Theorem 1, Corollary 1), achieved with step size γ=3/4. The multiplier subsample bootstrap procedure achieves a non-asymptotic coverage approximation rate of O(n^{-1/10}) (Theorem 2), with block size b_n = ⌈n^{4/5}⌉ and step size γ=3/5. As a byproduct, the overlapping batch means estimator for the asymptotic variance achieves the classical O(n^{-1/8}) rate (up to logarithmic factors) (Corollary 2), matching prior i.i.d.-noise results.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's O(n^{-1/4}) Kolmogorov-distance rate matches the recent O(log n / n^{1/4}) convex-distance bound of Wu et al. [83] for TD learning, but extends it to the general LSA setting and uses a different proof strategy (Poisson decomposition + martingale Berry-Esseen vs. d-dimensional martingale CLT). It strictly improves upon Srikant [73], whose O(√log n / n^{1/6}) Wasserstein rate implies only O(n^{-1/12}) in Kolmogorov distance — a factor of n^{1/12} slower. The O(n^{-1/10}) bootstrap validity rate (Theorem 2) is the first non-asymptotic guarantee of its kind for SA with Markov noise; prior bootstrap analyses [67, 70] achieved O(n^{-1/2}) or O(n^{-1/4}) but only under i.i.d. assumptions, and the naive extension to Markov noise was shown inconsistent in [42]. The O(n^{-1/8}) variance estimation rate (Corollary 2) recovers the classical rate from [17, 79] (i.i.d. SGD) and [63] (Markovian SGD), confirming that the Markovian dependence structure does not degrade the optimal variance estimation rate when the block size and step size are tuned jointly.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the i.i.d.-noise LSA analysis of Samsonov et al. [67] (NeurIPS 2024) to the Markovian noise setting, where the randomized concentration technique no longer applies. The proof strategy pivots to a Poisson decomposition for Markov chains combined with martingale Berry-Esseen bounds from Bolthausen [12] and Fan [28], while the bootstrap component adapts the multiplier subsample bootstrap of Ma and Zhang [43] and leverages concentration inequalities for overlapping batch means from Moulines et al. [50] (itself built on the martingale decomposition of Atchadé and Cattaneo [5]). The key conceptual departure is replacing the i.i.d. linear-statistic CLT machinery with a two-step decomposition (martingale + remainder) that handles the time-varying quadratic characteristic inherent to Markovian LSA iterates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first paper to provide non-asymptotic Berry-Esseen bounds (O(n^{-1/4}) in Kolmogorov distance) and non-asymptotic bootstrap validity (O(n^{-1/10})) for LSA under Markovian noise, closing a gap between classical asymptotic results and the i.i.d. non-asymptotic literature. A notable practical finding is the tension between step-size choices: the CLT rate is optimized at γ=3/4 while the variance estimation rate is optimized at γ→1/2, forcing a compromise (γ=3/5) in the bootstrap procedure. Open questions include extending Theorem 1 to the full d-dimensional setting (now possible via recent multivariate martingale CLTs [83]), generalizing to nonlinear SA, and whether the O(n^{-1/10}) bootstrap rate can be improved.
