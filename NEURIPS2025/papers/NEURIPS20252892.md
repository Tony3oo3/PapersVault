## Problem

Current Remote Sensing Foundation Models (RSFMs) face a dual gap: they scale up in parameter count and FLOPs without addressing the efficiency constraints critical for real-world deployment (onboard processing, rapid disaster response), and they treat multispectral data as generic image channels, ignoring physically meaningful radiometric priors such as spectral smoothness and energy conservation. The paper argues that architectural optimizations alone cannot simultaneously achieve high accuracy and deployment-level efficiency for Earth observation systems.

## Input / Output

PhySwin consumes Sentinel-2 multispectral tiles with 10 retained bands (B02–B08A, B11, B12) at resolutions of 96×96 to 256×256 pixels, with reflectance values normalized to approximately [0, 1.2]. The 10 bands are partitioned into three physically coherent groups (Visible, Red-Edge/NIR, SWIR), each embedded by a group-specific function and concatenated into a single token per spatial patch, so an N-patch input yields exactly N tokens regardless of band count. During pretraining the model outputs reconstructed reflectance patches; downstream it produces segmentation masks, binary or semantic change masks, and class labels via plug-and-play heads.

## Method

PhySwin builds on SwinV2 with three coupled innovations. First, two physics-informed regularizers are added to the reconstruction loss: a spectral-smoothness term penalizing first-order finite differences between adjacent-band reconstructions, and an energy-conservation term using ReLU to bound reflectance within [0, 1.2]. Second, the original MixMAE pretraining (which disables shifted-window attention and relies on large fixed windows) is refined by retaining SwinV2's SW-MSA and introducing SW-Masking, where the spatial mixing mask is shifted in alignment with the window configuration to promote cross-window interaction and reduce reconstruction artifacts. Third, a token-efficient grouped spectral embedding concatenates group-specific sub-embeddings into one token per spatial position, and a complementary MaskSpec mechanism randomly zeros entire spectral-group subspaces during pretraining to simulate spectral variability. Pretrained on over 1M Sentinel-2 tiles in two stages (FMoW-S2 at 96×96, then BigEarthNet-S2 at 128×128), PhySwin achieves SOTA segmentation and change-detection accuracy while reducing inference latency by up to 14.4× and FLOPs by up to 43.6× compared to ViT-based RSFMs.

## Results

PhySwin-B achieves state-of-the-art 52.32% mIoU on SegMunich (+1.32% over SpectralGPT) and 46.53% mIoU on DynamicEarthNet-S2 (+0.33% over SkySense) for semantic segmentation. For change detection, PhySwin-B reaches 61.05% F1 on OSCD (+0.80% over DynamicVis) and 18.13% SCS on Dyna.-S2 (+0.13% over SkySense). On classification, PhySwin-B attains 87.93% mAP on BigEarthNet, 63.11% top-1 on FMoW-S2, and 98.73% OA on EuroSAT, remaining within 0.5–4.2% of the best ViT-based baselines. In terms of efficiency, PhySwin-T delivers 14.4× higher inference throughput and 43.6× fewer FLOPs than SpectralGPT, while PhySwin-B achieves a 13.5× speedup over SatMAE++ with 64% less GPU memory.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PhySwin-B outperforms all ViT-based RSFMs on dense prediction tasks: it surpasses SpectralGPT by +1.32% mIoU on SegMunich and +1.81% on Dyna.-S2, and beats SatMAE++ by +1.70% and +3.66% respectively. On OSCD change detection, PhySwin-B (61.05% F1) exceeds DynamicVis (60.25%), SkySense (60.06%), HyperSIGMA (59.28%), and ChangeMamba (57.20%), though DynamicVis retains a precision advantage (79.41% vs. 67.97%) at the cost of much lower recall (48.36% vs. 55.41%). On classification benchmarks, PhySwin-B trails the strongest baselines modestly: −4.16% mAP vs. SkySense on BigEarthNet, −2.13% top-1 vs. SatMAE++ on FMoW-S2, and −0.48% OA vs. SpectralGPT on EuroSAT, which the authors attribute to SwinV2's windowed attention being less suited to global-pooling classification. The smaller PhySwin-T variant remains competitive, outperforming SatMAE and SatMAE++ on OSCD F1 (58.74% vs. 52.76%/55.31%) while using only 28.5M parameters. Across all comparisons, PhySwin achieves these accuracy gains with substantially lower computational cost—PhySwin-T runs at 1102 img/s versus 76.4 img/s for SpectralGPT and 42.8 img/s for SatMAE++.

## Relation to Prior Work

PhySwin builds directly on SwinV2's hierarchical shifted-window architecture and the MixMAE pretraining framework, refining both for multispectral EO: it retains SW-MSA (which original MixMAE disables) and introduces SW-Masking to coordinate spatial masking with window shifts. It departs from the dominant ViT-based RSFM lineage (SatMAE, SpectralGPT, SkySense) by prioritizing linear-complexity inference and by embedding radiometric physics priors—spectral smoothness and energy conservation—into the self-supervised objective, a direction previously explored only in narrow applications like crop-nitrogen retrieval. The token-efficient grouped spectral embedding with MaskSpec further distinguishes it from prior MS handling strategies (3D spatial-spectral tokens, separate feature branches) that inflate sequence length.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PhySwin addresses a practical deployment gap in RSFMs: the field has been scaling model size and token counts for accuracy, but real-world EO applications (onboard processing, rapid disaster response) demand low-latency inference. By showing that physics-informed inductive bias can partially offset the accuracy cost of efficient architectures, the paper offers a viable path to operationally deployable foundation models. Key limitations include a modest classification gap attributed to Swin's local windowed attention, sensitivity of the smoothness prior to band misregistration, and single-timestamp pretraining. Open questions include whether multi-temporal pretraining, BRDF-based constraints, and SAR-MS fusion can close the remaining accuracy gap without sacrificing the efficiency advantage.
