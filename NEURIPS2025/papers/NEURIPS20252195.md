## Problem

Transformer-based video action detection methods rely on self-attention with quadratic complexity in sequence length, making them computationally expensive for long video sequences and resource-constrained settings. While Mamba's near-linear state-space modeling offers a promising alternative, its potential for end-to-end video action detection remained largely unexplored, and prior approaches still depended on external structures such as RCNN region proposals or Transformer components like ViT.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252195/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an 8-frame RGB video clip at 224×224 resolution and performs detection on a single designated keyframe (typically the middle frame), using the remaining 7 frames purely as temporal context. It outputs 100 predicted bounding boxes (normalized coordinates) and corresponding action class logits per keyframe, with predictions aligned to ground truth via Hungarian matching. The token construction mechanism reduces the decoder input to a compact set of top-k% important non-keyframe tokens concatenated with all keyframe tokens, rather than processing the full spatiotemporal token sequence.

## Method

MOGO replaces all Transformer components with a pure Mamba-based encoder-decoder. The encoder is a 32-layer bidirectional VideoMamba backbone (pretrained on K400) augmented with a lightweight MLP-based token importance block that scores each spatiotemporal token via sigmoid-activated logits. The decoder consists of a single Mamba layer containing two novel modules: EQ-Mamba, which applies Mamba's linear state-space transformation to learnable queries alone (replacing self-attention), and QVI-Mamba, which concatenates the refined queries with encoder video features and exploits Mamba's selective copying mechanism to model cross-sequence information (replacing cross-attention), followed by an FFN. Training uses a DETR-style detection loss (classification, box regression, and GIoU with Hungarian matching) combined with a binary cross-entropy token importance loss that supervises the MLP to assign higher scores to tokens whose centers fall inside ground-truth bounding boxes, enabling data-driven token selection without explicit attention maps.

## Results

On JHMDB, MOGO achieves a frame-level mAP of 76.7 with only 82M parameters and 102–104 GFLOPs, while on UCF101-24 it reaches 78.2 mAP. Inference throughput is 256 img/s (3.9 ms/img latency), surpassing EVAD (240 img/s), WOO (176 img/s), and TubeR (64 img/s). The encoder accounts for ~97.7% of total FLOPs, while the single-layer Mamba decoder adds only 2.3 GFLOPs and 7.2M parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MOGO trails the top Transformer-based detectors in raw mAP: WOO (SF-R101) scores 80.5 and TubeR (I3D) scores 80.7 on JHMDB, versus MOGO's 76.7 — a gap of 3.8–4.0 points. On UCF101-24, TubeR (I3D) leads with 81.3 vs. MOGO's 78.2 (3.1-point gap), while MOGO actually edges out WOO (78.2 vs. 76.7). However, MOGO reduces GFLOPs by ~60% relative to WOO (104 vs. 252) and ~57% relative to TubeR (104 vs. 240), and its 256 img/s throughput exceeds all compared methods. Against all CNN-based baselines (EESSK 64.4, ACT 65.7, SMT 69.8, MOC 70.8, AVA 73.3), MOGO achieves the highest JHMDB mAP. In GPU memory, MOGO consistently uses less than EVAD: at a 40% token retention ratio EVAD needs ~1.6× more memory, and at full retention the gap widens to 2.8×.

## Relation to Prior Work

MOGO builds on three pillars: Mamba's selective state-space model (Gu & Dao, 2023) as the core sequence modeling primitive, VideoMamba (Li et al., 2024) as the pretrained video encoder, and DETR (Carion et al., 2020) as the end-to-end detection formulation with Hungarian matching and learnable queries. Its key conceptual departure from Transformer-based video action detectors (TubeR, WOO, EVAD) is a systematic 'Mamba-ization' of the attention architecture: self-attention is replaced by EQ-Mamba (intra-query linear transformation) and cross-attention by QVI-Mamba (selective copying for cross-sequence fusion), eliminating all Transformer and RCNN-like proposal modules. The token importance mechanism, supervised by a custom binary cross-entropy loss tied to ground-truth bounding boxes, is a novel learning-based substitute for the attention maps that Transformers use implicitly to weight tokens.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MOGO demonstrates that a pure Mamba encoder-decoder with a single-layer decoder can achieve competitive video action detection at roughly 60% fewer GFLOPs and significantly lower GPU memory than Transformer-based SOTA, making it relevant for resource-constrained deployment. However, the 3–4 mAP gap to the best Transformer methods (WOO, TubeR) and the notably weaker video-level mAP (42.0 vs. 80.7 on JHMDB) highlight that the current scarcity of large-scale pretrained Mamba checkpoints and the fixed-query design remain bottlenecks. Open questions include whether richer Mamba pretraining (beyond K400) closes the accuracy gap, whether the framework scales to longer sequences without the 64-frame encoder limitation, and whether the token importance mechanism generalizes beyond the specific loss formulation used here.
