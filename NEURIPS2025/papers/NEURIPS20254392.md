## Problem

Classical deep hedging strategies are highly vulnerable to small distributional shifts in the input data, yet no tractable framework exists for performing distributional adversarial attacks and training in this setting. Existing Wasserstein DRO results rely on convexity assumptions on the loss that are violated by neural-network-parameterized hedging strategies, and prior adversarial training work in ML focuses on pointwise perturbations rather than worst-case shifts of the entire data-generating distribution.

## Input / Output

The method consumes discrete-time price trajectories (and optionally variance trajectories for the Heston model) as input to per-step neural networks that output portfolio positions in r tradable assets. The adversarial component perturbs the entire training dataset of N paths simultaneously under a joint L^p budget constraint (an L^∞ distance per path), producing a worst-case perturbed dataset rather than independent per-sample perturbations. For multi-trajectory models, a weighted distance decouples price and variance perturbations, allowing them to be treated as separate input sequences scaled by a weight λ.

## Method

The paper derives a first-order sensitivity expansion of the Wasserstein DRO value function (Theorem 3.3) that yields a closed-form one-step perturbation for each path, scaled by a shared normalization constant Υ computed across the dataset. Building on this, two iterative algorithms are proposed: WPGD applies gradient-ascent steps with projection onto the Wasserstein ball, while WBPGD reparameterizes each perturbation as a per-path budget times a bounded direction, optimizing both independently for more effective budget allocation. The adversarial training loop alternates between generating worst-case perturbations (inner maximization via WBPGD) and updating network weights (outer minimization) using a combined clean-plus-adversarial loss with balance weight α. For the Heston model, a weighted distance on (S, v) pairs is shown to decouple into independent L^∞ perturbations on S and λv, enabling direct reuse of the single-trajectory algorithms.

## Results

On the Heston model with limited training data (N=5,000), adversarial training (SV-Attack) achieves a 54% lower mean hedging loss than clean training (2.86 vs. 6.21) and reduces worst-case loss by 66% (3.60 vs. 10.62). On real S&P 500 market data (Table 3), adversarial training on the FIX dataset outperforms clean Deep Hedging [2] across all five companies, e.g., improving P&L from −4.341 to −0.564 on MSFT and from −2.261 to −0.579 on AAPL. Adversarial training on the ROBUST dataset further improves P&L on 3 of 5 companies over the Robust Deep Hedging baseline [11], with the largest gain on GOOGL (0.199 vs. 0.026).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two baselines: standard Deep Hedging [2] (clean training on fixed-parameter GAD data) and Robust Deep Hedging [11] (clean training on parameter-robust GAD data). Against Deep Hedging [2], adversarial training on the FIX dataset wins decisively on all five S&P 500 companies, with P&L improvements ranging from +0.69 (GOOGL: −0.459 vs. −1.275) to +3.78 (MSFT: −0.564 vs. −4.341). Against Robust Deep Hedging [11], adversarial training on the ROBUST dataset wins on 3 of 5 companies (AAPL, GOOGL, MSFT) but slightly underperforms on AMZN (−0.860 vs. −0.849) and BRK-B (−0.294 vs. −0.281). Adversarial training on the FIX dataset also beats the ROBUST baseline on 3 of 5 companies (AAPL, AMZN, BRK-B), demonstrating that the adversarial framework can partially compensate for the lack of parameter robustness in the training data. Overall, the paper concludes that adversarial training 'consistently achieves better performance overall' compared to both baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies three research threads: deep hedging (Buehler et al. 2019), Wasserstein-based distributionally robust optimization (Kuhn, Esfahani, Gao & Kleywegt), and adversarial training (FGSM/PGD and their distributional extensions by Bai et al. and Staib & Jegelka). Prior robust deep hedging work perturbs terminal distributions [10], randomizes model parameters [11], or penalizes distributional deviations [12], but none formulates the problem as a minimax over a Wasserstein ball with a tractable adversarial training loop. The key technical departure is that existing Wasserstein DRO reformulations require convexity of the loss, which fails for neural-network-parameterized hedging; the paper instead leverages sensitivity analysis (Bartl et al. 2021) to derive a first-order perturbation that is computationally tractable without convexity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, theoretically grounded recipe for making deep hedging strategies robust to the model misspecification that is inevitable in production deployment, and validates the approach on real S&P 500 data during a period of market stress. The finding that adversarial training yields the largest gains under data scarcity (54% loss reduction at N=5,000) is practically relevant since real-world training sets are often limited. Open questions include sensitivity to the Wasserstein radius (which must be tuned per model), whether stronger multi-step attacks or alternative ambiguity geometries (e.g., f-divergence balls) would yield further gains, and whether the approach extends to multi-asset, multi-derivative portfolios with path-dependent payoffs.
