## Problem

Learning-based autonomous driving planners struggle to model interactive behaviors in complex traffic scenarios because vanilla transformer architectures lack dedicated mechanisms for capturing interdependencies among heterogeneous scene elements, and the scarcity of high-quality interactive data causes imitation learning to converge to biased distributions. Existing generative planners rely on strong structural priors (anchor trajectories, goal points) that conflict with legitimate interactive behaviors, while diffusion-based approaches are computationally expensive and still lack effective fusion designs for interaction modeling.

## Input / Output

The model consumes vectorized scene data in ego-centric front-left-up coordinates: up to 70 lanes (each a 20-point polyline with boundary vectors and traffic signals), 32 neighboring agents (21 timesteps of position, velocity, size, and type at 10 Hz), 25 navigation lanes, and static objects, all normalized via z-score. The noisy trajectory is decomposed into 20 overlapping segments (20 points each, 10-point overlap) that serve as the generative target. The output is the ego vehicle's planned trajectory over the full planning horizon, with overlapping segment predictions averaged at inference.

## Method

Flow Planner combines three coordinated innovations: (1) fine-grained trajectory tokenization that splits the noised trajectory into K=20 overlapping segments encoded by a shared MLP with sinusoidal positional encoding, balancing kinematic continuity with localized multi-modal expressiveness; (2) interaction-enhanced spatiotemporal fusion that projects heterogeneous lane, agent, and ego tokens into a shared latent space via separate adaLN modules, then applies scale-adaptive attention where learnable per-token receptive fields modulate attention scores by pairwise Euclidean distance, followed by modality-specific FFN projections to close the heterogeneity gap; (3) flow matching with classifier-free guidance that trains a single model with Bernoulli-masked neighboring-agent conditions, enabling dynamic reweighting of agent interactions at inference via a guided velocity field (ω=1.80 optimal), which amplifies scene-conditioned planning behavior beyond what pure behavior cloning achieves.

## Results

On the nuPlan Val14 benchmark (the largest and most representative evaluation set), Flow Planner achieves 90.43 (non-reactive) and 83.31 (reactive) in closed-loop scoring, becoming the first learning-based method to surpass the 90-point mark without any rule-based refinement or prior knowledge. On the challenging interPlan benchmark with 335 out-of-distribution interactive scenarios, Flow Planner scores 61.82 overall, an 8.92-point improvement over the previous state-of-the-art Diffusion Planner (52.90). With a lightweight rule-based post-processing module, Flow Planner w/ refine reaches 94.31 on Val14, competitive with rule-based PDM-Closed (92.84) and PDM-Hybrid (92.77).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary competitor Diffusion Planner (ICLR 2025), Flow Planner wins on all three nuPlan benchmarks in both non-reactive and reactive modes (e.g., 90.43 vs. 89.87 on Val14 NR; 70.42 vs. 69.22 on Test14-hard R), and achieves a substantial 8.92-point gain on interPlan (61.82 vs. 52.90), with particularly large margins on Jaywalk scenarios (43.57 vs. 26.20) and Nudge Around (72.96 vs. 60.48). Flow Planner also outperforms PLUTO (88.89 Val14 NR) by 1.54 points and PlanTF (84.27) by 6.16 points in the learning-based setting. In the hybrid setting (with rule-based refinement), Flow Planner w/ refine (94.31 Val14 NR) edges out Diffusion Planner w/ refine (94.26) and surpasses PDM-Closed (92.84) and PDM-Hybrid (92.77), though it trails slightly in reactive mode (92.38 vs. 92.90). GameFormer without refinement performs poorly (13.32 Val14 NR), and PDM-Open (53.53) is far behind, confirming that the proposed architecture and flow-matching guidance are critical for competitive learning-based planning.

## Relation to Prior Work

Flow Planner sits directly in the lineage of Diffusion Planner (Zheng et al., ICLR 2025), adopting the same MLP-Mixer scene encoder, data augmentation strategy, and overall generative-planning paradigm, but replacing the diffusion objective with flow matching for faster convergence and simpler implementation. It borrows scale-adaptive attention from SparseBEV (Liu et al., ICCV 2023) — originally designed for 3D object detection — and repurposes it to modulate inter-agent attention by spatial distance in the driving context. The classifier-free guidance mechanism (Ho & Salimans, 2022), well-established in text-to-image generation, is adapted here to dynamically reweight neighboring-agent interactions at inference time, a use case not previously explored in driving planning. The fine-grained trajectory tokenization with overlapping segments is a novel architectural choice that departs from both single-token trajectory representations (Diffusion Planner, VAD) and autoregressive timestep generation (TransFuser, DrivingGPT).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Flow Planner establishes the first learning-based planner to exceed 90 on nuPlan Val14 without relying on road-centerline priors or rule-based post-processing, and achieves an 8.92-point gain over Diffusion Planner on the interactive interPlan benchmark, suggesting that explicit architectural mechanisms for interaction modeling (scale-adaptive attention, modality-specific processing) matter more than simply scaling transformer capacity. The main open limitation is inference speed (~10 Hz on A6000), which is at the edge of industrial deployment requirements, and the method remains fundamentally imitation-learning-based, inheriting data-scarcity and distribution-shift vulnerabilities for truly novel interactive scenarios. Future work on acceleration (shortcut models) and hybrid RL for out-of-distribution interactive behaviors are natural next steps.
