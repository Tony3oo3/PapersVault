## Problem

Despite PCA's foundational role and its recent reconnection to self-attention in transformers, the classical formulations of PCA have not been systematically explored through the lens of difference-of-convex (DC) duality. Prior DC duality work on PCA was restricted to a single component and a single variance-maximization formulation, leaving open whether other PCA formulations yield novel, kernelizable, and out-of-sample-applicable dual problems, and whether the resulting DCA iterations connect to classical numerical linear algebra methods.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a data matrix X ∈ R^{N×d} (or equivalently its kernel matrix K = XX^T ∈ R^{N×N}) and produces a projection matrix W ∈ R^{d×s} with s ≤ rank(X) principal components, yielding a low-dimensional representation of each data point. For out-of-sample prediction, a new point x̃ ∈ R^d is projected using only the precomputed kernel matrix K, the dual solution H*, and the vector Xx̃, avoiding any explicit computation in the original d-dimensional space. The robust variant additionally requires per-row norms ‖X_{i,:}‖ for the l1-deviation weighting.

## Method

The paper derives three novel DC dual pairs for PCA by applying Toland duality to different classical formulations (variance maximization, reconstruction error, and Schatten-norm objectives), showing that when the primal's G term is unitarily invariant, the dual is automatically kernelizable and out-of-sample applicable. A key theoretical result establishes that DCA applied to the variance-maximization objective is equivalent to simultaneous iteration (and hence the QR algorithm) up to orthogonal transformation, providing a new optimization-theoretic interpretation of this classical eigensolver. For robust PCA, the paper formulates a kernelizable DC dual for the least-absolute-deviation reconstruction problem and shows the corresponding DCA is an instance of iteratively reweighted least squares. Empirically, simple first-order methods (proximal gradient, ZeroFPR) applied to the newly proposed formulations (n)–(o) outperform ARPACK and KrylovKit eigensolvers on large-scale problems, with the preferred formulation (primal vs. dual) depending on the aspect ratio of the data matrix.

## Results

The paper reports wall-clock timings (lower is better) to reach 10⁻³ accuracy on PCA problems with varying matrix dimensions. The best-performing methods are PG(n) and ZeroFPR(n) applied to the novel DC dual pair (n)-(o), achieving timings of 207.5 ms, 139.1 ms, and 309.1 ms on the three problem settings (4000×2000), (2000×4000), and (4500×4500) respectively. These outperform the classical ARPACK svDS baseline (808.0, 808.2, 2985.8 ms) by roughly 4–10× and KrylovKit (1130.4, 940.1, 3844.6 ms) by 3–12×. The paper also shows via Figure 3 that first-order methods consistently outperform classical eigensolvers as the number of principal components increases, provided matrix dimensions are sufficiently large.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its DC-dual-based first-order methods (PG and ZeroFPR applied to formulations (n) and (o)) against two classical eigensolver baselines: ARPACK's svDS (implicitly restarted Arnoldi) and KrylovKit (state-of-the-art Krylov subspace methods). On all three problem settings, PG(n) and ZeroFPR(n) applied to the novel (n)-(o) DC dual pair achieve the best timings, outperforming svDS by approximately 4× on the (4000,2000) setting (207.5 vs. 808.0 ms) and by nearly 10× on the (4500,4500) setting (309.1 vs. 2985.8 ms). KrylovKit is consistently slower than svDS across all settings, and both classical methods are substantially outperformed by the first-order methods on the (n)-(o) formulations. However, the paper notes that performance depends on the matrix shape: for wide matrices (N > d), the primal formulation (n) is preferred, while for tall matrices (N < d), the dual formulation (o) is competitive. The classical variance-based formulations (l) and (q) perform poorly with first-order methods, confirming that the choice of DC formulation is critical for practical performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the application of Toland (DC) duality to PCA from the single-component setting studied in [9, 36, 5] and the recent multi-component extension in [74], proposing three DC dual pairs two of which are novel. It generalizes the previously noted connection between DCA and the power method [71, Prop. 4] to show that DCA on the variance maximization objective is equivalent to simultaneous iteration, thereby linking it to the QR algorithm. The work is motivated by the recent discovery that self-attention is a projection onto kernel PCA principal components [79, 18], and builds on the least absolute deviation formulation for robust subspace recovery [45] to derive a kernelizable robust kernel PCA.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unification of PCA formulations under DC duality provides a principled framework for generating new kernelizable and out-of-sample applicable variants, which is essential for modern kernel methods and the growing connection between PCA and transformer architectures. The identification of DCA as simultaneous iteration bridges optimization theory and numerical linear algebra, potentially enabling new convergence analyses and algorithm design. Open questions include whether the first-order methods' advantage over specialized eigensolvers persists at much larger scales, whether the framework extends to structured or deep PCA variants, and how to handle the N² kernel matrix scalability in practice.
