## Problem

Existing last-iterate convergence guarantees for CFR algorithms in extensive-form games only apply to OMD-based variants, while RM-based CFR algorithms (e.g., CFR+) are empirically far superior. Moreover, the Reward Transformation framework requires solving a sequence of perturbed regularized EFGs, making parameter-free algorithms essential since fine-tuning across all sub-problems is infeasible. The paper asks whether CFR+, a classical parameter-free RM-based CFR algorithm, achieves parameter-free last-iterate convergence in perturbed regularized EFGs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253367/cropped_page8_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a two-player zero-sum EFG specified by its game tree (infosets, actions, leaf payoffs in [-1,1]) along with regularization parameters μ (regularization strength), γ (perturbation level ensuring strategies stay in a γ-perturbed simplex), and a reference strategy profile r. It outputs the last-iterate sequence-form strategy profile (a non-negative vector over the treeplex) that converges to the NE of the original EFG, eliminating the need for strategy averaging. The perturbation γ is halved every T_u iterations while r is updated to the current iterate, and the algorithm runs for N·T_u total iterations.

## Method

RTCFR+ wraps CFR+ inside the Reward Transformation framework, solving a sequence of perturbed regularized EFGs (Eq. 2) with decreasing γ and updated reference r. The key technical contribution is proving parameter-free last-iterate convergence of CFR+ for these sub-problems via a two-step argument: (i) a non-parameter-free result for η > μ/(2C₀) is established by adding a novel term η⟨-m̂*_i(I), θ_I^{t+1} - θ_I^t⟩ to the Bregman divergence inequality, which—unlike in OMD proofs—enables leveraging smoothness of instantaneous counterfactual regrets despite RM updating in the cone rather than the simplex; (ii) the parameter-free extension to all η > 0 and all initial θ_I¹ ≠ 0 exploits the linearity of the projection in CFR+ to map any (θ_I¹, η) pair to an equivalent (θ_I'¹, η') pair within the non-parameter-free regime. The perturbation is handled by mixing the non-perturbed strategy σ with the uniform vector (σ̂ = (1-α_I)σ + γ·1) while computing counterfactual regrets using the non-perturbed σ, preserving the strong monotonicity needed for convergence.

## Results

The paper evaluates exploitability (lower is better) on a logarithmic scale across nine game instances from five EFG benchmarks (Kuhn Poker, Leduc Poker, Goofspiel, Liar's Dice, Battleship) plus two HUNL subgames, running 20,000 iterations. RTCFR+ achieves the fastest empirical convergence rate among all algorithms with theoretical last-iterate convergence guarantees, and fine-tuned RTCFR+ (with per-game μ and T_u in Table 1) outperforms every tested algorithm including average-iterate convergence CFR methods. Notably, RTCFR+ is the first last-iterate convergence algorithm to match or exceed the performance of traditional average-iterate convergence CFR algorithms (CFR+, PCFR+, DCFR).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CFR+ (the base RM-based CFR algorithm), RTCFR+ achieves strictly faster convergence across all nine game instances. Against PCFR+ (predictive CFR+), no single algorithm consistently dominates; their relative performance varies by game, though fine-tuned RTCFR+ surpasses PCFR+ in most settings. Against DCFR (discounted CFR), RTCFR+ clearly outperforms. Against the two theoretical last-iterate convergence CFR baselines—R-NaD (Pérolat et al., 2021) and Reg-CFR (Liu et al., 2023)—RTCFR+ is only surpassed by Reg-CFR during the initial iterations in small-scale games (Kuhn Poker, Goofspiel(4)), and outperforms both in all other settings. Against the traditional last-iterate convergence algorithms OMWU (Wei et al., 2021) and OGDA (Lee et al., 2021), RTCFR+ is only outperformed by OGDA in the same small-scale games (Kuhn Poker, Goofspiel(4)), while dominating in all larger instances. Overall, RTCFR+ wins or ties in the vast majority of comparisons, with its only losses confined to early-stage convergence in the smallest test games.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Reward Transformation (RT) framework of Pérolat et al. (2021) and Liu et al. (2023), which reformulates Nash equilibrium learning in EFGs as solving a sequence of perturbed regularized EFGs, by establishing last-iterate convergence for the first time with RM-based CFR algorithms (specifically CFR+) rather than the OMD-based CFR algorithms used in prior work. The proof technique departs from Liu et al. (2023) by exploiting the best-response property of NEs at each infoset to introduce an auxiliary term that enables smoothness arguments despite RM+ updating in the cone of the strategy space. The parameter-free extension builds on Farina et al. (2021)'s zero-initialization invariance result, generalizing it to arbitrary initial accumulated counterfactual regrets via the linearity of the projection in CFR+.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first parameter-free last-iterate convergence guarantee for RM-based CFR algorithms in perturbed regularized EFGs, closing a gap that limited prior theoretical results to OMD-based variants with weaker empirical performance. The practical impact is significant: RTCFR+ matches or exceeds the empirical convergence of average-iterate CFR algorithms (e.g., PCFR+) while providing last-iterate guarantees, eliminating the need for strategy averaging that increases memory overhead and complicates function-approximation settings. Key limitations include the dependence on tuning μ, γ, and T_u at the RT level (preventing full parameter-freeness), and the scope is restricted to two-player zero-sum EFGs. Open questions include automated hyperparameter scheduling and whether similar results extend to multi-player or non-zero-sum settings.
