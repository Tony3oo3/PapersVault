## Problem

Existing story generation methods focus almost exclusively on character consistency while neglecting the equally critical role of scenes in visual storytelling. Two specific gaps remain: (i) scene planning, where scenes are generated solely from text prompts without ensuring scene-level narrative coherence, and (ii) scene consistency, where maintaining the same scene across multiple stories with different plots and characters is largely unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252205/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a concise user-provided text theme (e.g., 'Snowy dreams and falling stars') and optionally a manual scene image or character reference. Internally, a VLM (Qwen2-VL) decomposes the theme into a global scene description, which is rendered into a 1024×1024 global scene image via FLUX.1-dev, then partitioned into M=4 local scene crops with coordinates and N=5 story sub-prompts per scene. The final output is a set of M×N=20 scene-consistent story images generated by SDXL, along with the intermediate local scene crops and sub-prompts.

## Method

SceneDecorator is a training-free framework built on SDXL with two core techniques. First, VLM-Guided Scene Planning operates in a 'global-to-local' pipeline: Qwen2-VL conceptualizes a global scene description from the theme, FLUX.1-dev renders it into a scene image, and the VLM then partitions the image into M local scenes (via predicted bounding-box coordinates) and generates N sequential story sub-prompts per scene, ensuring narrative continuity through shared scene semantics. Second, Long-Term Scene-Sharing Attention comprises three mechanisms: (a) Mask-Guided Scene Injection, which augments IP-Adapter's decoupled cross-attention with subject-token-derived masks so scene features are injected only into background regions (via a (1−M) weighting), preserving subject style diversity; (b) Scene-Sharing Attention, which extends self-attention by concatenating masked keys and values from a paired story's latent branch (background-only via the same mask), enabling mutual scene information exchange during denoising; and (c) Extrapolable Noise Blending, which during a denoising interval [T₁, T₂] dynamically pairs N latent representations, averages their predicted noise across pairs, and normalizes, thereby fixing GPU memory to that of two stories regardless of N and enabling long-term consistency across many stories.

## Results

On 146 GPT-4o-generated themes (2,920 scene-prompt pairs), SceneDecorator achieves the best DreamSim-I (0.605, lower is better) and DINO-F (0.571) scores, outperforming the strongest baseline ConsiStory (0.723 and 0.475 respectively). In the user study (61 responses), SceneDecorator dominates all three subjective metrics: 56.5% text alignment, 76.2% scene alignment, and 57.5% image quality preference, versus ConsiStory's 21.3%, 14.1%, and 24.7%. On CLIP-T (text-image alignment), SceneDecorator (0.312) is marginally behind ConsiStory (0.320) but ahead of StoryDiffusion (0.311) and CustomDiffusion (0.306).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SceneDecorator is compared against three baselines adapted for scene-oriented story generation: CustomDiffusion (fine-tuned on local scene images), ConsiStory (modified to accept scene images via self-attention), and StoryDiffusion (used with scene images as conditioning). SceneDecorator decisively outperforms all three baselines on scene alignment (DreamSim-I: 0.605 vs. 0.723–0.752) and scene consistency (DINO-F: 0.571 vs. 0.340–0.475), with ConsiStory being the closest competitor. In the user study, SceneDecorator's scene alignment preference (76.2%) is more than 5× that of ConsiStory (14.1%) and over 12× that of CustomDiffusion (3.4%), reflecting the core contribution of the Long-Term Scene-Sharing Attention mechanism. The only metric where SceneDecorator trails is CLIP-T (0.312 vs. ConsiStory's 0.320), a marginal gap suggesting slightly weaker prompt adherence, though the user study's text alignment score (56.5% vs. 21.3%) contradicts this, likely due to CLIP-T's sensitivity to fine-grained text-image matching. Overall, SceneDecorator wins on 5 of 6 metrics and is competitive on the sixth, with the largest margins appearing in scene-specific evaluations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant character-consistency framing in story generation (ConsiStory, StoryDiffusion, CustomDiffusion) by elevating scene planning and scene consistency to first-class objectives. It repurposes IP-Adapter—originally designed for character/style injection—with cross-attention masks to achieve fine-grained scene injection while preserving subject diversity, and extends the shared self-attention mechanism from StoryDiffusion to propagate scene semantics across multiple stories. The VLM-guided 'global-to-local' planning pipeline (Qwen2-VL → FLUX.1-dev → VLM decomposition) is a novel orchestration of off-the-shelf components that has no direct precedent in prior story generation work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Scene consistency is a practically critical but underexplored dimension of visual storytelling, especially for applications like film storyboarding and game design where the same environment must persist across different narrative beats. The training-free design and constant-memory scaling (12.7 GB regardless of story count up to 25) make the approach accessible on consumer hardware. Key limitations remain: performance is bounded by the underlying foundation models (SDXL, FLUX.1-dev, Qwen2-VL), and IP-Adapter-based scene injection degrades on out-of-distribution scenes (e.g., 'an elephant in the sky'). Open questions include handling dynamic scene transitions (time-of-day, seasonal shifts) and developing scene control mechanisms beyond IP-Adapter.
