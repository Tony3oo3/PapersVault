## Problem

Memory bank-based 3D anomaly detection methods rely on feature encoders (e.g., PointMAE) that capture global semantics but fail to preserve fine-grained local geometry or maintain rotation invariance, leading to unreliable anomaly scores when registration misalignments occur. The paper argues that point-cloud registration and anomaly detection share the same core requirements—modeling local geometric structures and capturing feature similarity—yet existing pipelines treat registration as a separate preprocessing step rather than a feature-learning mechanism.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250180/cropped_page1_idx6.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a test 3D point cloud and a prototype (one training sample) as input, both processed through multi-scale voxel downsampling (8,192 points on Real3D-AD, 4,096 on Anomaly-ShapeNet). It produces a rigid registration matrix aligning the test sample to the prototype, point-level anomaly scores computed as the L2 distance from each filtered feature to its nearest coreset-sampled memory bank entry, and an object-level score obtained by max-pooling the smoothed point-level score mask. The memory bank stores concatenated normalized local features (32-dim from RIConv++) and alignment coordinates (3-dim) across all training samples.

## Method

Reg2Inv integrates registration into feature learning by training a three-component extractor—RIConv++ for rotation-invariant local features, KPConv-FPN for multi-scale point features, and a Geometric Transformer for patch-level features—under a registration objective with three complementary losses: a Sinkhorn-based negative log-likelihood loss (L_f) for local feature alignment, a point-matching loss (L_p), and an overlap-aware circle loss (L_oc) for patch matching. During inference, the model computes a Gaussian correlation matrix over patch features, selects top-k matches via dual normalization, estimates a rigid transformation via RANSAC on point-level correspondences, aligns the test cloud to the prototype, and scores anomalies by comparing normalized features against a coreset-sampled memory bank. The key distinction from prior work is that the feature extractor is shaped by registration supervision rather than a generic pre-training objective, yielding features that are simultaneously rotation-invariant, locally discriminative, and geometrically coherent for anomaly scoring.

## Results

On Real3D-AD, Reg2Inv achieves the best average O-AUROC of 78.0% and P-AUROC of 87.8%, outperforming the second-best method (ISMP) by 1.5% in O-AUROC and 4.2% in P-AUROC. On Anomaly-ShapeNet, it achieves the best O-AUROC of 86.1% (outperforming PO3AD by 2.2%) and a P-AUROC of 88.2% (second only to PO3AD's 89.8% by a 1.6% margin). The method attains the highest P-AUROC in 8 out of 12 Real3D-AD categories and the best O-AUROC across 30 of 40 Anomaly-ShapeNet categories, demonstrating strong localization and detection capabilities.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Reg2Inv outperforms all memory bank-based baselines by a wide margin: on Real3D-AD it beats ISMP (75.7/83.6) by +2.3 O-AUROC and +4.2 P-AUROC, Group3AD (75.1/73.5) by +2.9/+14.3, and Reg3D-AD (70.4/70.5) by +7.6/+17.3. On Anomaly-ShapeNet, it surpasses ISMP's P-AUROC (69.1) by 19.1 points and Reg3D-AD (57.2/66.8) by 28.9/21.4 points in O/P-AUROC respectively. Against reconstruction-based methods, Reg2Inv achieves the best O-AUROC on both datasets (78.0 vs. PO3AD's 76.5 on Real3D-AD; 86.1 vs. PO3AD's 83.9 on Anomaly-ShapeNet), while PO3AD retains a slight P-AUROC edge on Anomaly-ShapeNet (89.8 vs. 88.2). The ablation on registration strategy (Table 3) further shows that swapping FPFH+RANSAC for Reg2Inv's learned registration improves P-AUROC for all downstream methods (e.g., PatchCore(PMAE) from 64.2 to 75.6), confirming that the learned features themselves carry the rotation-invariance benefit independent of the registration module.

## Relation to Prior Work

Reg2Inv repositions point-cloud registration from a preprocessing step (as in Reg3D-AD, Group3AD, and ISMP, which rely on FPFH+RANSAC before feature extraction) to an integral feature-learning objective. It builds directly on the coarse-to-fine registration pipeline of Geometric Transformer (Qin et al., CVPR 2022), the optimal-transport matching machinery of SuperGlue (Sarlin et al., CVPR 2020), and the multi-scale point-to-node grouping of CoFiNet (Yu et al., NeurIPS 2021), but repurposes these components so that the registration loss simultaneously shapes a rotation-invariant, locally discriminative encoder. The key conceptual departure from prior memory-bank-based 3D anomaly detection is the argument that registration and anomaly detection share the same representational requirements—local geometric sensitivity and cross-sample feature consistency—making registration a natural self-supervised signal for the encoder rather than a separate alignment module.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a registration-trained encoder can outperform purpose-built anomaly encoders (e.g., PointMAE) on both object-level and point-level detection, suggesting that the field may be over-investing in task-specific pretraining when a well-chosen geometric alignment objective suffices. However, the method still requires per-class training, shows limited generalization on less discriminative categories, and depends on registration accuracy at inference—open questions that constrain its practical deployment. The rotation-invariance validation (consistent AUROC across 90°/180°/270° rotations) and the strong P-AUROC gains (up to ~20% over memory-bank baselines on Anomaly-ShapeNet) make this a useful reference for future work on geometrically robust 3D representation learning.
