## Problem

Tractable probabilistic models such as probabilistic circuits impose structural constraints (decomposability, determinism) to enable efficient inference, but prior work on expressive efficiency has focused almost exclusively on exact representations. Whether allowing a bounded approximation error (measured by f-divergences) can avoid the exponential size blow-ups observed in exact modeling remained an open question, with direct implications for PC learning from finite data and for approximate inference via approximate compilation.

## Input / Output

The hardness results take as input a (potentially unnormalized) distribution P̂—concretely a Boolean formula over n variables—and an approximation bound ε < 1/4 with a k-convex f-divergence D_f, and produce an NP-hardness certificate via reduction from SAT. The size-separation result takes a family of target distributions P_n (constructed from the Sauerhoff function over n×n Boolean matrices) and yields an unconditional lower bound of 2^Ω(n) on the size of any deterministic decomposable PC that is an ε-D_TV-approximator. The inference-guarantee results take a pair of distributions P, Q with D_f(P‖Q) < kε² and produce bounds on absolute approximation error for marginal and MAP queries, while showing no such guarantee holds for conditional queries.

## Method

The paper establishes three theoretical results. First, it proves NP-hardness of D_f-approximation for any model supporting tractable marginals by reducing from SAT: it augments a Boolean formula P̂ with an auxiliary variable Y to form P̂′ = (Y∧P̂)∨(¬Y∧X₁∧…∧Xₙ), then shows that deciding whether Q(Y=1) ≥ 1/4 for any kε²-D_f-approximator Q decides satisfiability. Second, it proves an unconditional exponential size gap between decomposable PCs and deterministic decomposable PCs for approximation by constructing a compact O(n²)-size decomposable PC from the Sauerhoff function and showing that any deterministic decomposable PC within bounded TV distance must induce a weak approximation of S_n as a d-DNNF, which requires 2^Ω(n) size via rectangle partition lower bounds. Third, it characterizes the inference guarantees: bounded k-convex f-divergence implies absolute (but not relative) approximation of marginals and MAP, while conditional marginals and conditional MAP can have arbitrarily large approximation error even under bounded TV distance.

## Results

The paper establishes two main theoretical results. First (Theorem 3.4/3.5), it proves that approximating an arbitrary (potentially unnormalized) distribution within a bounded k-convex f-divergence (e.g., kε²-D_f or ε-D_TV for 0 < ε < 1/4) is NP-hard for any model that supports tractable marginal inference, via a reduction from SAT. Second (Theorem 4.1), it proves an unconditional exponential size gap: the Sauerhoff-based distribution P_n is representable by a decomposable PC of size O(n²), yet any deterministic and decomposable PC that is an ε-D_TV-approximator for ε ≤ 1/16 − Ω(1/Poly(n²)) must have size 2^Ω(n). Additionally, the paper characterizes which inference queries (marginals, MAP) receive absolute approximation guarantees under bounded f-divergence, while showing conditionals and conditional MAP do not.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines or experimental comparisons. The central 'comparison' is between two circuit classes: decomposable PCs (smooth + decomposable) versus deterministic and decomposable PCs. For the Sauerhoff-based family P_n, decomposable PCs achieve size O(n²) while deterministic and decomposable PCs require 2^Ω(n) size even under bounded TV approximation, establishing an unconditional exponential separation that persists in the approximate setting. The paper also positions its NP-hardness result (Theorem 3.4) as strictly broader than the prior result of Martens and Medabalimi [33], which showed that a sequence of decomposable PCs converging to a specific function requires exponential size; the present result applies to any model supporting tractable marginals and allows a bounded but non-vanishing approximation error ε. For inference guarantees, the paper shows that bounded D_f implies absolute approximation of marginals (Theorem 5.1) and MAP (Theorem 5.3), but not of conditionals or conditional MAP (Theorem 5.5), delineating the boundary of what approximate modeling can guarantee.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the knowledge compilation literature on expressive efficiency separations (Bova et al. 2016 on DNNF vs. d-DNNF, De Colnet and Mengel 2021 on approximate compilation) from exact Boolean function representation to approximate probability distribution representation using probabilistic circuits. It leverages the Sauerhoff function and rectangle partition lower bounds from Sauerhoff (2003) and Bova et al. (2016), combined with the k-convex f-divergence framework of Melbourne (2020), to transfer these separations into the probabilistic setting. The key departure from prior work is the shift from exact to approximate modeling: while prior expressive-efficiency results for PCs (Choi et al. 2020, de Colnet and Mengel 2021) characterized exact representability, this paper proves that bounded f-divergence approximation does not close the exponential gap, and that the approximation task itself is NP-hard for any model supporting tractable marginals.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central negative result—that allowing a small approximation error does not alleviate exponential size separations between decomposable and deterministic+decomposable PCs, and that bounded-f-divergence approximation is NP-hard for all tractable-marginal models—places fundamental limits on what PC learning algorithms can achieve within polynomial-size hypothesis spaces. This has direct implications for the feasibility of approximate inference via compilation: one cannot hope to compile a model into a compact tractable PC with bounded divergence and then run exact inference to get guaranteed approximate answers. Open questions include whether relaxed (larger) ε values restore expressivity, whether other tractable model classes (e.g., those supporting only MAP but not marginals) escape these hardness results, and whether sufficient conditions exist for relative (rather than absolute) approximation of conditional queries.
