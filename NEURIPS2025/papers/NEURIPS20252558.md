## Problem

Model-based data selection methods for LLM pretraining—such as QuRater, DCLM, and AskLLM—have been developed almost entirely for English, leaving a critical gap in quality assessment for the non-English languages that are essential in multilingual training mixes. Existing multilingual attempts (e.g., FineWeb2-HQ) rely on benchmark-derived positive examples, risking test-set contamination, and lack a principled mechanism for transferring quality signals across languages.

## Input / Output

The MuRater model (a BGE-M3 encoder with a linear regression head) takes a single web document as input and outputs a scalar quality score; documents are then ranked and the top 10% per language are retained for pretraining. The rater itself is trained on 300K English document pairs annotated by four raters, which are translated via GPT-4o into 17 target languages to yield 150K monolingual pairs, 150K cross-lingual pairs (two different languages), and 75K parallel pairs (identical content in two languages, labeled with a neutral 0.5 preference). The scored corpus spans 1.5T English tokens plus 3T tokens across 17 languages drawn from 16 Common Crawl snapshots processed through the FineWeb-2 pipeline.

## Method

MuRating is a two-stage framework. Stage 1 consolidates four heterogeneous English raters (GPT-4o pairwise, AskLLM, FineWeb-Edu classifier, DCLM FastText) into a single unified scorer by computing majority-vote preference probabilities over 300K document pairs and fitting a Bradley-Terry model with binary cross-entropy loss. Stage 2 transfers this scorer to 17 languages by translating the scored English pairs and projecting the original preference labels onto the translated comparisons, while augmenting training with cross-lingual pairs (different-language texts sharing the English preference label) and parallel pairs (same content in two languages assigned a neutral 0.5 score to regularize cross-lingual score consistency). The final rater is a fine-tuned BGE-M3 encoder trained with a combined pairwise loss plus a parallel-regularization term (λ=0.5), achieving >93% validation accuracy. The key insight is that pairwise supervision is more robust to translation-induced variation than pointwise scoring, and that English-anchored transfer (MuRater(E)) outperforms the reverse direction (MuRater(M)) due to the richer topical and stylistic diversity of English corpora.

## Results

On the 18-language multilingual benchmark suite (11 tasks), MuRater(E) achieves an average of 50.96, outperforming QuRater-M (49.19), FineWeb-2 (46.28), and Uniform sampling (48.66). On the English-only 12-task suite, MuRater reaches 51.23 average, surpassing the strongest English baseline DCLM (50.23) by 1.0 point and Uniform+50% data (48.70) by 2.5 points. At 7B scale trained on 1T tokens, MuRater achieves 57.23 average versus QuRater-M's 56.18, confirming consistent gains across model sizes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MuRater outperforms all seven baselines across both English and multilingual settings. Against QuRater/QuRater-M, the closest multilingual competitor, MuRater(E) gains 1.77 points on the 18-language average (50.96 vs. 49.19) and 1.05 points at 7B scale (57.23 vs. 56.18), with the largest gap in World Knowledge (+2.28 points). On English, MuRater beats DCLM (51.23 vs. 50.23) and FineWeb-Edu (51.23 vs. 49.49) by 1.0 and 1.7 points respectively, while AskLLM (47.82) and QuRater (48.33) trail by 3.4 and 2.9 points. Against FineWeb2-HQ on the 13-language subset, MuRater(E) leads by 2.84 points (51.81 vs. 48.97), with the largest margin in Commonsense Reasoning (+2.76). The raw multilingual corpora HPLT-2 (45.70) and FineWeb-2 (46.28) are outperformed by 5.26 and 4.68 points respectively, confirming that model-based selection substantially improves over unfiltered web data. Notably, MuRater(E) also surpasses MuRater(M) by 0.94 points, validating the English-anchored translation direction over the reverse multilingual-to-English approach.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the English-only data quality scoring paradigm (QuRater, DCLM, AskLLM, FineWeb-Edu) to a multilingual setting by aggregating four English raters into a unified Bradley-Terry pairwise scorer and transferring judgments via translation to 17 languages. The key methodological departure from QuRater is the addition of cross-lingual and parallel text pairs as regularization signals, which enforce language-agnostic scoring behavior. The data pipeline builds directly on FineWeb-2's deduplication and heuristic-filtering infrastructure, and the rater architecture (BGE-M3 with a linear head) follows QuRater's training setup, making the contribution primarily in the multilingual transfer mechanism rather than in the underlying scoring model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Model-based data selection has been almost exclusively developed and validated for English, leaving a critical gap for multilingual LLM pretraining where 17+ languages compete for limited compute. The finding that pairwise comparisons transfer more robustly across translation than pointwise scores provides a practical design principle for any future multilingual quality-scoring system. However, the framework's dependence on GPT-4o for both annotation and translation, its restriction to 17 languages, and its limited performance on narrative/creative content (inherited from the English raters' factual bias) leave open questions about scalability to low-resource languages and culturally diverse content. Whether the English-anchored transfer strategy (MuRater(E)) generalizes to language families with very different typological properties remains untested.
