## Problem

As spiking neural networks scale to meet practical demands, even binary SNNs (BSNNs) with 1-bit weights still impose substantial storage and computational costs for edge deployment. Sub-bit compression techniques (representing weights with fewer than 1 bit via compact codebooks) have been explored for binary neural networks but remain entirely unexplored in the SNN domain, leaving a gap in further exploiting SNNs' compression and acceleration potential.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251514/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR, 224×224 for ImageNet-1K) and asynchronous event streams (DVSCIFAR-10) as input, producing class labels for classification, bounding boxes with class labels for COCO detection, and pixel-wise masks for ADE20K segmentation. During inference, the model stores only a compact codebook of 2^η binary kernels per layer plus per-kernel indices (η bits each, where η < k_w×k_h), reconstructing full binary kernels on the fly without any distance computation or outlier processing.

## Method

S²NN encodes each 3×3 convolutional kernel with an η-bit index into a layer-specific compact codebook (|P|=2^η) rather than storing all 9 bits, achieving 0.44–0.67 bits per weight. To fix the outlier-induced codeword selection bias in the baseline (where large-magnitude weights dominate L2 distance and cause suboptimal kernel assignment), OS-Quant applies IQR-based outlier detection followed by spatially-aware scaling that divides each outlier by the mean absolute difference to its 4-neighbors, preserving spatial structure while neutralizing outlier influence. For performance recovery under extreme compression, MPFD performs knowledge distillation directly at the membrane potential level using normalized Gram matrices, bypassing the surrogate gradient imprecision inherent in firing-rate-based distillation and enabling cross-architecture teacher-student pairing without matched layer dimensions.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, S²NN at 0.67-bit weights achieves 96.43% top-1 accuracy, surpassing the 1-bit Q-SNN baseline (95.54%) by 0.89% while reducing model size by 1.5×. On ImageNet-1K, S²NN (0.67-bit) reaches 68.02% accuracy, outperforming the 1-bit BESTformer (63.46%) by 4.56% and Q-Spikformer (54.54%) by 13.48%, with 2.6× smaller size and 6.8× fewer OPs. On COCO detection, S²NN achieves 40.0 mAP@50, and on ADE20K segmentation it reaches 36.4% MIoU, both substantially exceeding the best 1-bit BNN baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On CIFAR-10, S²NN at 0.67-bit weights (96.43%) outperforms the 1-bit Q-SNN (95.54%) by 0.89% and BitSNN (94.37%) by 2.06%, while reducing model size by 1.5× and OPs by 1.5×. On ImageNet-1K, S²NN (68.02%) surpasses the strongest 1-bit SNN baseline BESTformer (63.46%) by 4.56% and Q-Spikformer (54.54%) by 13.48%, with 2.6× smaller size and 6.8× fewer OPs. On CIFAR-100, S²NN matches Q-SNN exactly at 78.77% while being 1.4× smaller, and on DVSCIFAR-10 it edges out Q-SNN by 0.4% at 1.4× compression. For object detection on COCO, S²NN achieves 40.0 mAP@50, surpassing the best 1-bit BNN detector MPQ-YOLO (32.6%) by 7.4% and approaching the 32-bit SDTv3 (41.8%) at 2.08× smaller size. For semantic segmentation on ADE20K, S²NN reaches 36.4% MIoU, exceeding BiDense (18.8%) by 17.6% and the 32-bit SDTv3 (35.3% at 4 timesteps) while using 34.6× less memory.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the sub-bit neural network framework of Wang et al. (ICCV 2021), which constrains binary convolutional kernels to a compact codebook of frequently-occurring codewords, from the BNN domain to the SNN domain for the first time. It builds on the binary SNN (BSNN) paradigm established by BitSNN and Q-SNN, which already reduce weights to 1-bit, and pushes compression below that threshold. The MPFD component departs from existing SNN distillation methods (which operate at the firing-rate level via surrogate gradients) by performing distillation directly on membrane potentials, eliminating the imprecision introduced by the surrogate gradient function.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By achieving below-1-bit weight representation in SNNs, this work opens a path toward deploying large-scale spiking models on resource-constrained edge hardware where even 1-bit storage is prohibitive. The OS-Quant mechanism addresses a practical training pathology (outlier-induced codeword selection bias) that would otherwise limit the usability of sub-bit SNNs, and the MPFD result (+3.66% over baseline vs. +1.60% for firing-rate distillation) suggests that the distillation level matters more than the distillation type in highly compressed SNNs. Limitations include a focus on vision tasks (classification, detection, segmentation) with limited exploration of non-visual domains, and the method requires a well-trained teacher model, adding training overhead even though inference remains lightweight.
