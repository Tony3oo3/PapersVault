## Problem

Multimodal dataset distillation for image-text contrastive learning faces a fundamental scalability-alignment tradeoff: prior methods (MTT-VL, LoRS) freeze the text encoder to avoid the prohibitive cost of trajectory matching with large models like BERT, but this severely limits cross-modal semantic alignment and causes retrieval performance to saturate or degrade as synthetic data size grows. The paper identifies that a frozen text encoder cannot adapt to visual context, making it a bottleneck that prevents effective scaling of distilled datasets.

## Input / Output

The method takes a full image-text training set (e.g., 29K Flickr30K or 113K COCO pairs), a pretrained image encoder (NFNet, ~26M params), and a pretrained text encoder (BERT-base, ~110M params) as inputs, and produces a compact synthetic dataset of N=100–500 image-text pairs. The synthetic text is represented as optimized input embeddings fed into the text encoder's transformer layers (only the token/position embedding module is frozen), while synthetic images are optimized pixel values. This means the distilled dataset is architecture-specific to the encoder pair used during distillation, though the paper shows it generalizes to alternative encoders at evaluation time.

## Method

CovMatch derives a closed-form solution for the inner optimization of the bi-level distillation problem by fixing both encoders at each step and optimizing only the linear projection layers under a linear contrastive loss, which reduces the outer objective to aligning the Frobenius distance between real and synthetic cross-covariance matrices (Eq. 11). A feature-matching regularizer (Eq. 12) minimizes the mean discrepancy of projected features per modality to prevent trivial solutions arising from the low-rank structure of the cross-covariance. Crucially, unlike prior methods that freeze the entire text encoder, CovMatch keeps the BERT transformer layers trainable during distillation, enabling joint optimization of both encoders. An online model update step trains the full model on a real-data batch before each distillation iteration to keep alignment statistics synchronized with evolving representations, and the model is periodically reinitialized to pretrained weights to prevent overfitting to a fixed state. This design eliminates the need for expert trajectory storage entirely, reducing distillation cost from 120GB/132h (MTT multimodal) to 0GB/1.2 sec per iteration.

## Results

On Flickr30K with 500 synthetic pairs, CovMatch achieves an average retrieval accuracy of 38.4% (IR@1=14.7, TR@1=19.9), outperforming the strongest baseline LoRS (31.6%) by 6.8% absolute. On COCO with 500 pairs, CovMatch reaches 19.6% average (IR@1=5.4, TR@1=8.1), a 6.1% absolute gain over LoRS (13.5%). These gains correspond to only 1.7% and 0.4% of the full training datasets, respectively, and CovMatch maintains steady performance scaling with increasing synthetic data size, unlike baselines that saturate or degrade beyond N=1000.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CovMatch consistently outperforms all baselines across every dataset, pair count, and retrieval metric. Against the strongest distillation baseline LoRS [45], CovMatch achieves 6.8% absolute improvement in average retrieval accuracy on Flickr30K (38.4 vs. 31.6) and 6.1% on COCO (19.6 vs. 13.5) with 500 pairs; the gap widens at smaller budgets (e.g., 30.5 vs. 27.4 at N=100 on Flickr30K). Against MTT-VL [43], the advantage is even larger at 500 pairs (38.4 vs. 25.0 on Flickr30K), reflecting MTT-VL's inability to scale with synthetic data size. Both coreset selection baselines—Herding [7] and K-Center [38]—perform near-random levels (21.8 and 23.7 vs. 22.6 for Random at N=500 on Flickr30K), confirming that simple subset selection cannot capture cross-modal alignment. A key differentiator is that CovMatch jointly optimizes both image and text encoders, whereas all baselines freeze the text encoder during distillation, which the paper shows causes performance saturation beyond N=1000.

## Relation to Prior Work

CovMatch extends the closed-form inner-optimization strategy from unimodal dataset distillation (KIP, FrePo) to the multimodal contrastive learning setting, leveraging the linear contrastive loss formulation of Nakada et al. to reduce the bi-level objective to cross-covariance alignment. It departs from prior multimodal distillation methods (MTT-VL, LoRS) that rely on long-term trajectory matching and freeze the text encoder, instead enabling joint optimization of both encoders through a direct statistical matching loss. The method also connects to ClipCov's cross-covariance preservation objective but differs by optimizing synthetic data in continuous space rather than selecting subsets via heuristics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a frozen text encoder is the primary bottleneck for scaling multimodal distillation (performance saturates and degrades beyond ~1000 pairs) challenges the common practice of freezing large pretrained encoders for computational convenience. CovMatch's dramatic computational savings (zero expert trajectory storage, 15GB vs. 71GB memory) and strong cross-architecture generalization make it a practical tool for efficient vision-language model training. Key limitations include the assumption that pretrained modality-specific encoders are available and the restriction to linear projection layers, leaving open the question of distilling datasets for pretraining contrastive models from scratch.
