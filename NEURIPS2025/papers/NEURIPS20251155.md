## Problem

Motion LLMs suffer from severe out-of-domain and out-of-vocabulary issues because annotated text-motion pair datasets are limited to ~14k samples, and existing RAG approaches (e.g., ReMoDiffuse) are constrained by the small scale of 3D motion databases. This paper explores retrieving from large-scale in-the-wild video databases to supplement motion signals, but identifies two unaddressed bottlenecks: video foundation models perform poorly on human-centric retrieval, and suboptimal retrievals cause error propagation that degrades generation quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251155/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a free-form text motion description and a rank-1 video clip retrieved from a 425,988-video human-centric database (HcVD). The video is downsampled to at most 16 frames and embedded into segment-wise visual tokens via InternVideo2 + CLIP, which are concatenated with the text embedding as LLM input. The output is a 3D whole-body motion sequence (22 joints, 263-dim features including root velocities, local joint positions, and 6D rotations) decoded from discrete VQ-VAE motion tokens (codebook 512×512, temporal downsampling rate 3) produced autoregressively by a Phi-3-3.8B LLM.

## Method

VimoRAG comprises two plug-and-play modules. Gemini-MVR is a dual-channel video retriever: an action-level branch encodes per-frame 2D keypoints (from AlphaPose) through a MotionBERT-based encoder and a 4-layer temporal transformer, while an object-level branch reuses InternVideo's video encoder; a lightweight linear router adaptively weights the two cosine similarities based on the action embedding. McDPO is a two-stage training strategy: Stage 1 performs instruction tuning on text+video inputs to produce a reference model π_ref; Stage 2 applies DPO with a motion-centric dual-alignment reward that jointly penalizes feature-space distance to ground-truth motion and text-motion semantic misalignment, teaching the LLM to selectively leverage or ignore retrieved video priors and thereby mitigating error propagation from noisy retrievals.

## Results

On the in-domain HumanML3D test set, VimoRAG (Phi3-3.8B backbone) achieves a FID of 0.131, a 73% improvement over MotionGPT with the same backbone (0.501), along with +14% R-Precision Top-1 (0.452 vs 0.396), +14% Top-2 (0.655 vs 0.575), +13% Top-3 (0.764 vs 0.673), and −15% MM Dist (3.146 vs 3.724). On the out-of-distribution IDEA400 test set (zero-shot, trained only on HumanML3D), VimoRAG achieves the best FID of 2.388, substantially outperforming ReMoDiffuse (9.639) and all other motion LLMs. Performance scales steadily with retrieval corpus size, with FID and MM Dist both decreasing as the video database grows from ~50K to 425K videos.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the same-backbone baseline MotionGPT (Phi3-3.8B), VimoRAG delivers a dramatic 73% FID reduction (0.131 vs 0.501) and consistent 13–14% gains across all three R-Precision levels, while also reducing MM Dist by 15%. On the OOD IDEA400 benchmark, VimoRAG's FID of 2.388 is 4× better than ReMoDiffuse (9.639) and 2.3× better than MotionGPT (5.544), demonstrating strong generalization to unseen motion descriptions. Among motion LLMs on HumanML3D, VimoRAG achieves the best FID, outperforming MotionGPT-2 (Llama3-8B, FID 0.191) and MotionLLM (GPT4+Gemma-2B, FID 0.230). However, VimoRAG does not surpass the strongest motion specialists (e.g., BiPO FID 0.030, LAMP FID 0.032, MoMask FID 0.048), which are purpose-built for text-to-motion without the generality of an LLM. The ablation confirms both components are essential: replacing Gemini-MVR with random retrieval degrades FID by 72.8%, and removing McDPO increases FID by 43.1%.

## Relation to Prior Work

VimoRAG extends the retrieval-augmented motion generation paradigm introduced by ReMoDiffuse [6], which retrieves from small 3D motion databases (~14k samples), to a fundamentally larger and more accessible source: in-the-wild 2D video databases (425k+ clips). The generation backbone follows MotionGPT [3]'s VQ-VAE tokenization and LLM-based autoregressive framework, while the retrieval component builds on CLIP [37] and InternVideo [39] with a novel dual-channel (action-level + object-level) design. The error-propagation mitigation via McDPO adapts the DPO objective [43] from language alignment to cross-modal motion generation, a setting where prior RAG work (including ReMoDiffuse) had not addressed retrieval noise.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that 2D video priors can substantially guide 3D motion generation, offering a scalable path to overcome the ~14k-sample bottleneck in annotated motion data. The scalability experiments (performance improves monotonically with database size up to 425k videos) suggest real-world deployment with internet-scale video corpora is feasible. Key limitations include reliance on a single 3.8B LLM backbone (Phi-3), inference latency dominated by the LLM generation phase (~7s), and the 2D-to-3D gap that McDPO only partially bridges. Open questions include which LLM architectures best exploit video priors, whether unified multimodal RAG (video + 3D + image) yields further gains, and how to reduce the LLM latency bottleneck.
