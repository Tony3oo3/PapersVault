## Problem

The minimax optimality of the Median-of-Means (MoM) estimator under adversarial contamination was previously established only for Gaussian distributions, and no lower bounds on MoM's error had been derived for any other distribution class. This paper closes that gap by proving matching upper and lower bounds across finite-variance, infinite-variance, sub-exponential, sub-Gaussian, and symmetric distribution classes, revealing where MoM is optimal and where it is fundamentally sub-optimal.

## Input / Output

The estimator receives n real-valued samples of which at most αn have been adversarially modified (the adversary inspects, removes, and replaces samples), along with a tunable number of blocks k that must be chosen differently per distribution class (e.g., k ∝ αn for finite variance, k ∝ α^{2/3}n for sub-exponential). It outputs a point estimate μ̂_MoM (the median of k block means) together with a high-probability concentration inequality of the form |μ̂ − μ_p| ≤ C·σ_p(√(log(2/δ)/n) + asymptotic bias), where the asymptotic bias term depends on the distribution class and α.

## Method

The central technical tool is Theorem 3.1, which bounds MoM's error by the quantile function Q_m of the average of m i.i.d. inliers evaluated at 1/2 ± √(log(2/δ)/2k) ± αm, reducing the problem to bounding Q_m around the median for each distribution class. For finite variance, Chebyshev's inequality gives Q_m(1/2+ε) ≲ σ_p/√(m(1/2−ε)); for infinite variance, the Bahr-Esseen inequality yields a (v_r/m^r)^{1/(1+r)} rate; for sub-exponential and sub-Gaussian classes, Berry-Esseen-type normal approximation bounds on g(m) sharpen the rate. Matching lower bounds are constructed via adversarial attacks that push contaminated block means to arbitrarily large values, shifting the median by at least C·σ_p·√α (or C·σ_p·α^{2/3} for sub-exponential), with the key probabilistic ingredient being a Chernoff bound on the number of contaminated blocks below the median (Lemma B.1). For symmetric distributions satisfying a linear-growth condition on Q_m near 1/2, the quantile bound tightens to Q_m(1/2+ε) ≲ c·σ_p·ε/√m, yielding the optimal α asymptotic bias.

## Results

The paper establishes that MoM is (minimax) optimal under adversarial contamination in the class of finite-variance distributions (asymptotic bias √α) and infinite-variance distributions with finite (1+r)-th moment (asymptotic bias α^{r/(1+r)}), matching the optimal rates of the trimmed mean and Catoni's M-estimator respectively. For sub-exponential distributions, MoM achieves an asymptotic bias of α^{2/3}, which is shown to be a tight lower bound (Theorem 4.2) and is sub-optimal compared to the α√(log(1/α)) rate of the trimmed mean. For symmetric distributions (including Gaussians and Student's t), MoM attains the optimal asymptotic bias of order α, matching the best known rate. Numerical experiments on Pareto, Student's t, and half-normal distributions confirm that empirical errors align with the theoretical orders.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to the trimmed mean [24], MoM matches the optimal √α rate in the finite-variance class P₂, but is strictly sub-optimal in the sub-Gaussian class P_SG where MoM achieves α^{2/3} versus the trimmed mean's optimal α√(log(1/α)). In the symmetric distribution class P_sym^{ε₀,c}, MoM achieves the optimal α rate while the trimmed mean's performance is not characterized (and empirically appears sub-optimal in Figure 1(c)). Compared to Catoni's M-estimator [25], MoM matches the optimal rates in both P₂ (√α) and P_{1+r} (α^{r/(1+r)}), but offers a notable practical advantage: MoM's contamination tolerance is α ≤ 0.4 uniformly across all r, whereas the M-estimator's tolerance decreases with r (e.g., α ≤ 0.26 for r = 0.5 and α ≤ 0.16 for r = 0.1). In the Gaussian class, both MoM and the trimmed mean are sub-optimal relative to the α rate, but MoM achieves the optimal α while the trimmed mean only achieves α√(log(1/α)).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the analysis of the Median-of-Means estimator from the i.i.d. and additive-contamination settings to the more general adversarial contamination model introduced by Dalalyan and Minasyan (2022). Prior work by Laforgue et al. (2021) established MoM's optimality in the i.i.d. regime under additive contamination, but did not characterize the asymptotic bias that emerges as sample size grows. The paper positions MoM alongside the trimmed mean (Lugosi & Mendelson, 2021) and Catoni's M-estimator (Bhatt et al., 2022), which were already known to be minimax optimal under adversarial contamination for finite-variance and infinite-variance classes, respectively, but for which MoM's optimality was previously unknown beyond the Gaussian case.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first complete characterization of MoM's error under adversarial contamination across multiple distribution classes, establishing both upper and matching lower bounds. The key negative result—that MoM is minimax sub-optimal for sub-exponential distributions (achieving α^{2/3} instead of the optimal α·log(1/α))—reveals a fundamental limitation: the median operation cannot fully exploit light tails when distributions are asymmetric. This complements known i.i.d. limitations of MoM (large asymptotic variance) and suggests that no single fixed choice of block size k can be simultaneously optimal across all distribution classes. Open questions include whether a data-adaptive or distribution-aware choice of k could close the gap for light-tailed distributions, and whether the sub-optimality extends to multivariate settings.
