## Problem

While Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) have demonstrated broad capabilities, their practical effectiveness in ancient manuscript study—spanning palaeography, codicology, and iconography—remains entirely untested. No existing benchmark captures the full range of archaeological workflows from fine-grained character recognition to holistic cultural reasoning, nor does it systematically probe how different prompting strategies affect LMM reliability on historically grounded, previously unseen tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251169/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

Inputs are high-resolution manuscript images spanning a wide scale range (from 322×328 cropped single characters to 1098×1254 full scrolls, with multi-fragment pairs for restoration tasks) paired with text prompts in four distinct formats: open-ended Q&A, structured multiple-choice, Chain-of-Thought reasoning chains, and retrieval-augmented visual queries that include reference exemplar images alongside the query. Outputs are task-specific: transcribed text with Levenshtein edit distance, one-to-one normalized character labels, binary yes/no judgments, four-way calligraphy style labels, damage severity classifications, 0–100 probabilistic match scores, left/right/top/bottom spatial orientation predictions, relative chronological attributions, and free-form artwork captions scored via BERTScore and manual expert rubrics.

## Method

MS-Bench is a hierarchical benchmark of 5,076 images and 9,982 expert-curated questions organized into 4 vertical tiers (Textual Recognition → Textual Analysis → Materiality Study → Cultural Study) and 9 horizontal sub-tasks, co-developed over three years with 12 senior researchers across 18 panels to mirror real archaeological workflows. The evaluation protocol systematically varies four prompting strategies—direct Q&A, multiple-choice, CoT reasoning, and retrieval-augmented visual context—to disentangle LMM effectiveness, instructional robustness, and cultural knowledge grounding. Thirty-two LMMs (14 closed-source, 18 open-source, 2 advanced reasoning) are assessed with task-specific metrics (accuracy, edit distance, BERTScore) under deterministic inference (temperature=0, top-k=1), with human expert baselines provided for direct model-vs-human comparison. A key design choice is the cognitive hierarchy: tasks progress from shallow character-level perception to holistic cultural reasoning, enabling diagnosis of where LMM capability boundaries lie. The benchmark also incorporates a simulated shredding augmentation for fragment restoration and frequency-tiered allograph categorization (easy/medium/hard) to probe generalization beyond memorized glyphs.

## Results

On MS-Bench (5,076 images, 9,982 questions across 9 sub-tasks), closed-source LMMs achieve 67.49% average accuracy on Handwritten Character Recognition versus 51.57% for open-source models, with a 2.5× reduction in edit distance. Step-1o-vision-32k and SenseNova lead Allograph Normalization at 73.34% average accuracy across easy/medium/hard subsets, while GPT-4o is the most reliable model for Fragment Restoration (72.00% orientation accuracy, 59.40% binary matching). On Cultural Study tasks, closed-source LMMs reach 82.93% Icon Recognition accuracy (vs. 37.05% open-source), and Step-1o-vision-32k achieves the best Chronological Attribution at 69.57% via retrieval-augmented prompting. Retrieval-augmented visual context provides the most consistent performance boost across tasks (e.g., +14.57% on Writing Symbol Detection), while CoT has a two-sided effect: improving fine-grained symbol detection but degrading holistic style classification.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among 32 evaluated LMMs, no single model dominates all tasks, revealing task-specific specialization patterns. Step-1o-vision-32k and SenseNova lead on Allograph Normalization (73.34% avg accuracy), outperforming GPT-4o (42.35% avg) and all Qwen-series models by 15–30 points; however, GPT-4o fails entirely on Handwritten Character Recognition while SenseNova achieves the best OCR accuracy (82.88%). For Fragment Restoration, GPT-4o is the most reliable (72.00% orientation, 59.40% binary), surpassing Step-1o-vision-32k (which fails on orientation) and SenseNova (7.29% orientation), while Gemini-2.0-Flash and Qwen2.5-VL-7B show the strongest consistency across all three matching formats. On Cultural Study, SenseNova achieves the highest Icon Recognition (96.86%) with minimal drop in binary picking (93.33%), while Step-1o-vision-32k leads Chronological Attribution (69.57% retrieval-augmented) but performs poorly on Icon Recognition (83.44% classification). Open-source models lag closed-source by ~16 points on average for Textual Recognition and ~46 points on Icon Recognition, though Phi-3.5-vision (39.30%) and Qwen2-VL-2B (77.74% easy allograph) occasionally match or exceed larger closed-source models on specific sub-tasks, indicating that model size alone does not determine fine-grained recognition performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by being the first to systematically evaluate general-purpose LMMs (rather than task-specific models) on ancient manuscript analysis, bridging the gap between the LMM benchmarking literature (BenchLMM, PathMMU, OpenVLM Leaderboard) and the manuscript AI literature (OCR, fragment restoration, allograph normalization). The closest prior benchmark is OBI-Bench [15] for Oracle Bone Inscriptions, but MS-Bench is substantially broader in task scope (9 sub-tasks across 4 tiers vs. 5 domains focused on character identification) and in prompting strategy analysis (CoT, retrieval-augmented visual context). Prior LMM-for-manuscripts work (LLMCo4MR, PhiloGPT) proposed domain-specific methods; this paper instead treats LMMs as general tools and asks whether they can handle the full archaeological workflow without task-specific fine-tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark provides the first systematic evidence that current LMMs can serve as assistive tools (3× efficiency gain in symbol detection) but remain insufficient for expert-level manuscript discovery, particularly in cultural contextualization and combinatorial fragment matching. The finding that CoT has a two-sided effect—helping fine-grained visual tasks while degrading holistic perception tasks like calligraphy style classification—challenges the near-universal assumption that chain-of-thought prompting is beneficial for all multimodal tasks. Open questions include whether domain-specific fine-tuning or smaller specialized models can close the gap on cultural-knowledge-intensive tasks, and whether combinatorial solvers integrated with LMMs can handle multi-fragment restoration at scale.
