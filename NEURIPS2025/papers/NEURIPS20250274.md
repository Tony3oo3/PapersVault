## Problem

Multi-modal large language models require thousands of GPUs for training because their vision encoders (CLIP, SAM, DINOv2) are aligned with language at only a single granularity level, creating a fundamental mismatch when tasks demand multi-granularity cross-modal alignment. No existing method explicitly quantifies or adjusts the granularity level of visual representations to match the language modality's requirements, forcing reliance on massive data scaling and compute to implicitly resolve this gap.

## Input / Output

The method operates on the weight matrices within the attention layers of a frozen CLIP ViT-L/14 vision encoder, projecting them into a Poincaré ball (curvature c=0.01) and applying learnable scaling matrices via Möbius multiplication. Standard RGB images and text questions/prompts are consumed by the full MLLM pipeline (vision encoder + adapter + LLaMA/Vicuna language model), producing free-form text answers. The only new parameters are the scaling matrices (as few as 0.05M for diagonal configuration), which are adapted in the same attention layers as LoRA-style methods.

## Method

HyperET projects pre-trained vision encoder weights into hyperbolic space via exponential mapping, then adjusts their hyperbolic radius through Möbius matrix multiplication with a learnable scaling matrix W_s. The key insight is that hyperbolic radius directly encodes granularity level (small radius = pixel-level features, large radius = image-level semantics), so scaling the radius via Möbius operations provides a principled, continuous mechanism to shift visual representations to whatever granularity the language modality requires. Three parameter-efficient matrix configurations are offered—diagonal (O(n) params), block-diagonal (intra-block interactions), and banded (local off-diagonal interactions)—all degenerating to the diagonal form under specific settings. Theoretical analysis (Theorems 1 and 2) proves that Möbius scalar and matrix multiplication precisely scale the hyperbolic radius, and ablations confirm that the gains come from the hyperbolic radius adjustment mechanism itself rather than from added parameters, as equivalent Euclidean-space parameter additions yield no improvement.

## Results

On the 12-benchmark pre-training evaluation, HyperET applied to LLaVA-1.5 (Vicuna-13B) improves average performance across all benchmarks, e.g., VQAv2 from 80.0 to 82.3, GQA from 63.3 to 65.7, ScienceQA from 71.6 to 73.7, TextVQA from 61.3 to 63.9, and POPE (hallucination) from 85.9 to 88.7, using only ~50M additional parameters (<1% of the 13B language model). In the fine-tuning setting on ScienceQA, HyperET boosts MemVP (LLaMA-13B) from 93.78 to 94.72 average accuracy (+0.94) and LaVIN (LLaMA-13B) from 90.50 to 91.46 (+0.96), with only 0.05M extra trainable parameters. The method also generalizes to non-Transformer architectures, improving Cobra (Mamba-based) by 1.1–1.7 points across five benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HyperET consistently outperforms all baselines it is compared against. In the fine-tuning setting (ScienceQA), it improves LaVIN by +0.62 to +0.96 points and MemVP by +0.71 to +0.94 points in average accuracy, with only 0.05M additional parameters; notably, MemVP+HyperET (LLaMA-7B) matches the accuracy of MemVP with LLaMA-13B (93.78) while using 100,000× fewer extra parameters. In pre-training, HyperET beats LLaVA-1.5 by 1.5–2.6 points across benchmarks (e.g., +2.4 on TextVQA, +2.8 on POPE) and LLaVA-Next by 1.1–2.4 points, all with <1% extra parameters. Against the hyperbolic training method MERU, HyperET substantially outperforms it (e.g., 82.3 vs. 78.9 on VQAv2, 1584.7 vs. 1456.8 on MME), demonstrating that radius adjustment is more effective than MERU's asymmetric hierarchy approach. On the non-Transformer Cobra (Mamba) architecture, HyperET still yields consistent gains of 1.1–1.7 points, confirming architectural generality. The full fine-tuning LLaVA baseline (13B trainable) achieves 90.92 on ScienceQA, which HyperET-enhanced MemVP (5.55M trainable) surpasses at 94.72, highlighting the parameter-efficiency advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes hyperbolic geometry tools (Poincaré ball, Möbius multiplication) for a new purpose: adjusting the granularity level of vision encoder weights during MLLM training. It departs from prior hyperbolic vision-language work (e.g., MERU, hyperbolic contrastive learning) that reshapes hierarchical structures to align modalities, instead preserving the original hierarchy and using the intrinsic hyperbolic radius as a tunable knob for granularity. On the PEFT side, it is positioned as a complementary plug-in to existing methods like LaVIN and MemVP rather than a replacement, and it contrasts with LoRA-style approaches by operating in hyperbolic space with structured scaling matrices rather than low-rank Euclidean updates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a concrete and previously under-addressed source of inefficiency in MLLM training (granularity mismatch between single-level vision encoders and multi-level language understanding) and offers a principled, theoretically grounded fix with <1% parameter overhead. The ablations cleanly isolating the hyperbolic mechanism from mere parameter addition (Euclidean equivalents fail or degrade) strengthen the causal claim. Open questions include whether the fixed curvature c=0.01 is optimal across tasks, whether the approach extends to video or audio modalities, and whether the gains compound when combined with larger-scale pre-training data. The lack of error bars and the modest absolute improvements (1–3%) temper the practical urgency, but the plug-and-play nature makes it a low-risk addition to existing MLLM pipelines.
