## Problem

Existing 3D visual grounding benchmarks are confined to small indoor scenes, single-platform (typically vehicle-mounted) data, and limited scale, leaving outdoor multi-platform grounding largely unexplored. No prior dataset unifies synchronized LiDAR-RGB sensing across diverse embodied agents (vehicle, drone, quadruped) at a scale sufficient to study cross-platform generalization, range-dependent sparsity, and extreme object-size variation in real-world outdoor environments.

## Input / Output

The model F(P^β, I^β, C) → b^β consumes a LiDAR point cloud (uniformly downsampled to 16,384 points), an optional RGB image, and a natural language referring expression, producing a 7-parameter 3D bounding box (center, dimensions, yaw) per referred object. The superscript β indexes the platform (Vehicle, Drone, Quadruped), and the task explicitly accounts for platform-specific geometry via bird's-eye-view distance ρ and relative pitch θ^r. In the multi-object setting, a single expression may reference multiple targets, and the model must localize all of them jointly with Hungarian-matched one-to-one supervision.

## Method

The baseline adapts BUTD-DETR with a scale-adaptive PointNet++ backbone (1,024 visual tokens), a frozen RoBERTa language encoder, and a six-layer Transformer decoder that predicts all referenced boxes in one shot, trained with box-regression, token-alignment, and contrastive multimodal losses. Three platform-aware modules are added: (1) Cross-Platform Alignment (CPA) rotates each scan to cancel roll/pitch and applies an altitude-normalizing offset for drones, mapping all platforms into a shared gravity-aligned frame; (2) Multi-Scale Sampling (MSS) queries PointNet++ neighborhoods at radii from 0.6 m to 4.8 m so each point receives both fine local detail and broad distant context, countering range-dependent LiDAR sparsity; (3) Scale-Aware Fusion (SAF) uses a lightweight MLP to compute per-point dynamic weights over the multi-scale features, preventing wrong-scale decisions under density shifts. The dataset itself is annotated via a VLM-prompting pipeline (Qwen2-VL-72B with structured five-slot prompts) followed by human verification, and 3D boxes for drone/quadruped are produced by fusing six pretrained detectors via KDE, CTRL tracking, and Tokenize-Anything class confirmation.

## Results

Under the unified multi-platform training setting (Vehicle + Drone + Quadruped), the proposed method achieves Acc@25 of 80.86% on Vehicle, 53.45% on Drone, and 53.31% on Quadruped, with an overall Union Acc@25 of 63.84% and Acc@50 of 29.66%. This represents absolute gains of +17.45 (Vehicle), +9.25 (Drone), and +10.17 (Quadruped) in Acc@25 over the strongest baseline (EDA). In the multi-object grounding setting on Vehicle, the method achieves 32.32% Acc@25 and 56.40% mIoU, outperforming EDA (26.91% / 51.07%) and BUTD-DETR (25.40% / 47.88%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against three baselines: BUTD-DETR (ECCV 2022), EDA (CVPR 2023), and WildRefer (ECCV 2024). Under the unified multi-platform training protocol, the proposed method (with CPA, MSS, and SAF modules) outperforms all three baselines across every platform and metric. Against the strongest baseline EDA, the gains are +17.45 Acc@25 on Vehicle (80.86 vs. 65.50), +9.25 on Drone (53.45 vs. 46.00), and +10.17 on Quadruped (53.31 vs. 44.00). WildRefer, while competitive on Drone Acc@25 (50.27), collapses on Vehicle Acc@50 (10.12 vs. 50.11 for Ours), revealing its fragility under ground-level dense scenes. In the multi-object grounding setting (Table 3), the method achieves 32.32% Acc@25 and 56.40% mIoU, beating EDA by +5.41 Acc@25 and +5.33 mIoU, and BUTD-DETR by +6.92 Acc@25 and +8.52 mIoU, demonstrating stronger joint multi-object reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 3D visual grounding task from indoor RGB-D benchmarks (ScanRefer, Nr3D) and single-platform outdoor datasets (Talk2Car, STRefer, CityRefer) to a unified multi-platform outdoor setting spanning Vehicle, Drone, and Quadruped. Architecturally, it adapts BUTD-DETR (Jain et al., ECCV 2022) with three novel modules—Cross-Platform Alignment, Multi-Scale Sampling, and Scale-Aware Fusion—designed to handle the range-dependent sparsity and extreme scale variation of outdoor LiDAR. The annotation pipeline departs from purely manual labeling by leveraging Qwen2-VL-72B for structured referring-expression generation, combined with multi-detector fusion (PV-RCNN, CenterPoint, SECOND, etc.) and human-in-the-loop verification for 3D box annotation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As the first multi-platform outdoor 3D grounding benchmark at 10× the scale of prior datasets, 3EED provides a critical testbed for evaluating whether grounding models truly generalize across sensing geometries or merely overfit to a single platform's statistics. The severe cross-platform performance gaps revealed (e.g., near-zero Acc@25 when transferring Vehicle→Drone) suggest that current architectures lack genuine viewpoint invariance, and that simple gravity-alignment normalization is insufficient to close the gap. Open questions include whether platform-specific encoders or larger multi-platform pretraining can bridge the remaining drone/quadruped deficits, and whether the two-class (Vehicle, Pedestrian) focus limits the benchmark's applicability to broader open-vocabulary grounding.
