## Problem

In tracking-by-detection multi-object tracking, individual object features (appearance, motion, topology) become indistinct under occlusions and viewpoint changes, causing identity switches. Existing graph matching approaches operate at the frame level, incurring computational costs that scale cubically with object count and remaining sensitive to per-frame feature ambiguity. The paper addresses both gaps by reformulating association as matching over temporally aggregated tracklet clips rather than individual detections.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a video sequence with pre-computed detections (bounding boxes, 512-d Re-ID appearance vectors, confidence scores, and timestamps) at 800×1440 resolution. Detections are first grouped into short tracklets via strict IoU association, then segmented into fixed-length clips of N=4 frames; objects absent from a frame within a clip are represented as empty placeholder vertices. The output is a set of object trajectories with stable IDs, produced by matching tracklet clip graphs across time using a differentiable QP-based graph matching layer that yields a score matrix reshaped into a matching score map.

## Method

STAR comprises three stages: (1) CITG generates reliable initial tracklets using a dynamically adjusted IoU threshold (scaled by velocity, box size, and inter-frame interval), splitting trajectories at occlusions; (2) TCGC constructs a per-clip graph where each object is a vertex with dual feature channels—topology (normalized positions, pairwise distances, angles encoded via Kolmogorov-Arnold Networks) and appearance (Re-ID features projected from 512 to 128 dims via a 3-layer KAN)—and M rounds of vertex-edge-vertex message passing with L2-normalized updates extract high-order spatial-temporal features; (3) TCGM matches TC graphs across time using a differentiable graph matching layer that solves a Quadratic Programming problem with KKT-condition gradients, reducing the number of graphs from n×L (frame-level) to roughly 2/(N×k) of that count. Training uses a weighted binary cross-entropy loss with temperature-scaled softmax to sharpen the QP-derived score distribution, and the framework is model-agnostic, plugging into existing trackers (DeepSORT, JDE, CTracker) to improve IDF1 by 3–8%.

## Results

On MOT17 (private detection), STAR achieves HOTA 67.8, MOTA 81.9, IDF1 80.2, and 1,057 ID switches, outperforming the previous best SUSHI (HOTA 66.5) by 1.3%. On MOT20, STAR reaches HOTA 67.3 and MOTA 79.6, surpassing OccluTrack+ (HOTA 66.7) and SUSHI (HOTA 64.3) by 0.6% and 3.0% respectively. On VisDrone2021-MOT, STAR attains HOTA 45.3, IDF1 58.2, and 17.8 FPS, exceeding BoT-SORT by 2.9% HOTA and UGT by 0.4% IDF1 while running 1.6 FPS faster. On DanceTrack, STAR achieves HOTA 55.9 and MOTA 91.8, edging out SparseTrack (HOTA 55.5) and BoT-SORT (HOTA 54.7).

## Baselines & Comparisons

STAR achieves the best HOTA across all four primary benchmarks. On MOT17, it surpasses SUSHI (66.5) by 1.3% HOTA and OccluTrack+ (66.8) by 1.0%, while also reducing ID switches to 1,057 (vs. 1,149 for SUSHI and 951 for OccluTrack+). On MOT20, STAR leads OccluTrack+ (66.7) by 0.6% HOTA and SUSHI (64.3) by 3.0%, with MOTA of 79.6 vs. 77.7 for both. On VisDrone2021-MOT, STAR (HOTA 45.3) is marginally below UGT (45.5) but exceeds it in IDF1 (58.2 vs. 57.8) and FPS (17.8 vs. 16.2), while clearly outperforming BoT-SORT (42.4) by 2.9% HOTA and OCSORT (42.9) by 2.4%. On DanceTrack, STAR (55.9) edges out SparseTrack (55.5) and BoT-SORT (54.7). The ablation in Table 8 confirms STAR is model-agnostic: integrating it into DeepSORT, JDE, and CTracker improves IDF1 by 3.2%, 3.5%, and 8.0% respectively, demonstrating broad applicability beyond its native pipeline.

## Relation to Prior Work

STAR builds directly on the differentiable graph matching framework of GMTracker [32] for its TCGM module and adopts message normalization from DeeperGCN [45] for its propagation step, but departs from the frame-level graph matching paradigm (GM, GPM, SuperGlue, GMTracker) by reformulating association at the tracklet-clip level rather than the individual-detection level. The CITG component retains the IoU-based association lineage of DeepSORT/ByteTrack but adds dynamic threshold adjustment, while the TCGC component introduces KAN-based feature processing and multi-object spatial-temporal graph construction that go beyond the single-object or pairwise features used in prior graph-based trackers (GSM, GTAN, UGT). Conceptually, the paper positions itself as a bridge between the efficiency of bipartite matching and the accuracy of graph matching, arguing that operating on short trajectory segments (TCs) rather than individual frames reduces the combinatorial burden while preserving relational context.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The tracklet-clip reformulation offers a practical path to making graph-based association scalable to crowded scenes, where frame-level graph matching becomes prohibitively expensive; the demonstrated model-agnostic integration (8% IDF1 gain on CTracker) suggests the paradigm could be adopted as a drop-in module for existing trackers. However, the paper acknowledges efficiency limitations and leaves open the question of whether an end-to-end differentiable pipeline (replacing the heuristic CITG) could further close the gap. The modest margins on DanceTrack (0.4% HOTA over SparseTrack) and the reliance on a fixed TC length (N=4) raise open questions about adaptivity in highly dynamic or sparse scenes.
