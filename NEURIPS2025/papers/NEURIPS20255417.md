## Problem

Most existing OOD node detection methods in graphs rely on multi-class labels and a well-defined pretext classification task to train a supervised classifier, making them inapplicable in category-free or task-agnostic scenarios such as one-class detection, regression, or generative modeling. This paper identifies that even without gold labels, the local representation manifold of ID samples exhibits a smooth 'resonance' that can be exploited for OOD detection, and formalizes this into a practical, label-independent framework.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a graph with node features and adjacency structure, a set of known ID nodes (requiring only binary ID/OOD labels, not multi-class categories), and a small binary ID/OOD validation set used solely to identify the optimal resonance epoch. It produces a per-node resonance-based score τ (the L2 norm of the representation change in one training step), a binary OOD/ID classification per node via a trained energy-based classifier, and synthetic OOD node features generated through SGLD. Notably, the alignment target for known ID representations is an arbitrary random vector rather than class-specific targets, making the pipeline fully task-agnostic.

## Method

RSL exploits a newly identified phenomenon called Feature Resonance: when known ID node representations are optimized toward an arbitrary fixed target via MSE loss on the last linear layer, unknown ID nodes undergo significantly larger representation changes than OOD nodes, even under random or single-vector alignment. The key practical proxy is a micro-level score τ_i = ‖h_θ_{t+1}(x̃_i) − h_θ_t(x̃_i)‖₂, measuring the feature vector movement in a single training step, which captures resonance more reliably than the full trajectory in complex real-world settings. A binary validation set identifies the epoch t where resonance is most pronounced (typically mid-training), and nodes with the smallest τ values are selected as candidate OOD nodes. These candidates seed an SGLD-based synthetic OOD node generator that produces additional OOD examples aligned with real OOD patterns, which are then used to train a binary energy-based OOD classifier. The paper further provides a generalization bound (Theorem 1) showing that the OOD error rate is controlled by the distribution discrepancy ξ and the resonance threshold T, with the main error vanishing under mild conditions on the learning rate and distribution separation.

## Results

On the five primary unsupervised OOD node detection benchmarks, RSL achieves AUROC of 64.12 (Squirrel), 84.01 (WikiCS), 66.11 (YelpChi), 90.03 (Amazon), and 64.83 (Reddit), with corresponding FPR95 values of 89.90, 49.23, 80.45, 19.60, and 85.49. On the four citation/heterophilic datasets (Cora, Citeseer, Pubmed, Chameleon), RSL attains the best FPR95 of 28.76, 33.67, 35.15, and 45.81 respectively, and best AUROC of 94.14, 90.44, 89.10, and 78.04. The paper reports an average FPR95 reduction of 15.20% over the current state-of-the-art, with the raw resonance score τ alone (RSL w/o classifier) already achieving a 9.70% average FPR95 reduction over SOTA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RSL outperforms EnergyDef (the closest unsupervised competitor) on all five primary datasets, with the largest gap on WikiCS (FPR95: 49.23 vs. 83.17) and Amazon (FPR95: 19.60 vs. 32.43). Against label-dependent SOTA methods like GRASP, GNNSafe, and NodeSafe, RSL achieves superior FPR95 on heterophilic graphs (Squirrel: 89.90 vs. 90.77 for GRASP; WikiCS: 49.23 vs. 71.08; Chameleon: 45.81 vs. 66.88) while matching or slightly trailing on homophilic citation graphs (Cora FPR95: 28.76 vs. 29.70 for GRASP). The raw resonance score τ without any trained classifier already surpasses EnergyDef on Squirrel (FPR95 90.18 vs. 91.77), WikiCS (70.38 vs. 83.17), and Amazon (19.56 vs. 32.43), demonstrating the intrinsic discriminative power of feature resonance. Traditional outlier detectors (LOF-KNN, GCNAE, GAAN, DOMINANT) and transformation-based methods (GOAD, NeuTral AD) are consistently outperformed by 10–30+ points in AUROC across all datasets. On the fraud detection benchmarks (YelpChi, Amazon, Reddit), RSL achieves average improvements of 3.01%, 7.09%, and 8.95% over SOTA in AUROC, AUPR, and FPR95 respectively, with Amazon showing the most dramatic gain (AUROC 90.03 vs. 86.57 for EnergyDef).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends EnergyDef (Gong & Sun, KDD 2024), which introduced SGLD-based synthetic OOD node generation for unsupervised graph OOD detection, by replacing its energy-based candidate selection with a new 'Feature Resonance' signal derived from representation movement during arbitrary-target alignment training. Conceptually, it departs from the dominant entropy-based and classifier-output-based OOD detection paradigm (MSP, Energy, ODIN, GNNSafe, GRASP) by operating entirely in the feature/representation space rather than the label/output space, drawing an analogy to forced vibration in physics. The theoretical grounding connects to neural collapse theory to explain why ID samples exhibit coherent trajectory behavior even under random or single-target alignment, while OOD samples on distinct manifolds do not.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine practical gap: many real-world OOD detection scenarios (generative modeling, regression, one-class settings) lack multi-class labels or well-defined pretext tasks, rendering most existing graph OOD methods inapplicable. The label-independence of feature resonance—confirmed even under a single random target vector—makes RSL broadly applicable to category-free settings. However, the method still requires a small binary ID/OOD validation set to identify the resonance period, and its applicability is currently demonstrated only at the node level on static attributed graphs; scalability to dynamic or very large graphs, and extension to other modalities, remain open. The theoretical error bounds, while informative, rely on relatively strong smoothness and discrepancy assumptions that may not always hold in practice.
