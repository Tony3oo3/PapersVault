## Problem

Existing temporal point process (TPP) datasets are predominantly unimodal, capturing only temporal and categorical event attributes while ignoring the rich textual and visual context that accompanies real-world event streams. Furthermore, no benchmark exists that evaluates whether large language models can actually reason about temporal point processes through question-answering, leaving a critical gap in assessing multi-modal temporal understanding.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250910/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For DanmakuTPP-Events, each event is a tuple (timestamp, event type, Danmaku text, video frame), and classical TPP models consume only the temporal and type dimensions (multi-modal info is excluded). For DanmakuTPP-QA, LLMs receive the full TPP sequence (timestamps + Danmaku text) within a time window, while MLLMs additionally receive 3 randomly sampled video frames per window as a lightweight visual supplement. Outputs span 10 tasks: closed-ended predictions scored by ACC or RMSE (burst peak counting, next-event/burst timestamp, sentiment polarity, event type) and two open-ended reasoning tasks scored 0–1 by Qwen3-235B-A22B as an LLM judge.

## Method

The benchmark comprises two datasets built from Bilibili's Danmaku system: DanmakuTPP-Events (7,250 sequences, 10.8M events from top-100 creators) for standard TPP modeling, and DanmakuTPP-QA (2,605 videos, 10 tasks) for temporal-visual-textual reasoning. The QA dataset is constructed via a five-agent pipeline: a Task-design Agent (DeepSeek-R1) formulates 10 evaluation tasks; an Annotation Agent group (Qwen2.5 for text, Qwen2.5-VL + RAM for vision) labels events; a Quality-control Agent (Qwen3) reconciles annotations via majority voting; a Visualization Agent (Qwen2.5-Coder) auto-generates Python scripts to produce intensity timelines and sentiment plots; and a Task-solve Agent group (Qwen3, Qwen2.5-VL, Gemma-3) produces ground-truth answers with manual verification. Evaluation covers six classical TPP models (NHP, RMTPP, SAHP, THP, AttNHP, ODETPP) via the EasyTPP framework, eight LLMs/MLLMs in zero-shot and LoRA fine-tuned settings (Qwen2.5-VL-3B on a single RTX 4090), revealing that MLLMs do not consistently outperform LLMs and that task-specific fine-tuning of small models can surpass much larger pretrained counterparts on sentiment and burst-prediction tasks.

## Results

On DanmakuTPP-Events, NHP achieves the best log-likelihood (0.799) and lowest next-event RMSE (0.932) among six classical TPP models. On DanmakuTPP-QA closed-ended tasks, Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct achieves the best next-timestamp RMSE (1.11 on Task-2), Qwen3-30B-A3B leads on average sentiment RMSE (0.20 on Task-4) and event-type accuracy (23.00% on Task-7), while Qwen2.5-VL-72B achieves the highest top-2 triggering-type accuracy (47.17% on Task-8). The LoRA-finetuned Qwen2.5-VL-3B dramatically outperforms all pretrained models on sentiment prediction (RMSE 0.05/0.16/0.08 on Tasks 4–6), a 4–6× error reduction, but degrades severely on burst-peak timestamp prediction (RMSE 220.43 vs. ~121 for pretrained models).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among classical TPP models on DanmakuTPP-Events, NHP [20] dominates both log-likelihood (0.799 vs. ODETPP 0.729, SAHP 0.713, RMTPP 0.646, THP 0.619, AttNHP 0.550) and next-event RMSE (0.932 vs. THP 0.938, ODETPP 0.968), while THP [39] is competitive on RMSE but lags on log-likelihood, and SAHP [35] and RMTPP [5] trail on both metrics. On DanmakuTPP-QA, no single LLM or MLLM dominates all eight tasks: Llama-3.3-70B [18] wins Task-2 (RMSE 1.11), Qwen3-30B-A3B [33] wins Tasks 4 and 7, Qwen2.5-VL-72B [28] wins Task-8 (47.17% ACC), and Gemma3-27B [27] wins Task-6 (RMSE 0.20). Notably, MLLMs do not consistently outperform LLMs despite the multi-modal nature of the data—the authors attribute this to suboptimal visual integration at long context lengths. The LoRA-finetuned Qwen2.5-VL-3B [28] achieves 4–6× lower RMSE than the best pretrained models on sentiment tasks (Tasks 4–6) but catastrophically overfits on Task-3 (RMSE 220.43 vs. ~121 for all pretrained models). DeepSeek-V3 [18] shows strong burst-peak counting (25.00% on Task-1) and competitive sentiment scores, while the Qwen2.5 [32] family exhibits clear scaling benefits (e.g., Task-2 RMSE drops from 27.64 at 7B to 1.28 at 72B). ODETPP [4] and AttNHP [34] remain competitive mid-tier TPP baselines, with ODETPP achieving the second-best log-likelihood (0.729) and AttNHP the second-best RMSE (0.969) on DanmakuTPP-Events.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of temporal point process (TPP) modeling and multi-modal large language model evaluation. While TPP methods (NHP, RMTPP, SAHP, THP, ODETPP) and multi-modal LLMs (Qwen2.5-VL, Gemma-3, DeepSeek) are each well-studied, no prior work has systematically evaluated how well these model families handle TPPs enriched with synchronized textual and visual context. The multi-agent data construction pipeline draws on recent LLM-as-judge and multi-agent orchestration paradigms, while the QA evaluation design is inspired by time-series QA benchmarks (TSQA, ECG-QA) but is the first to target TPP-specific temporal reasoning. The paper thus repurposes existing TPP architectures and MLLM capabilities into a new multi-modal evaluation setting rather than proposing a new modeling method.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DanmakuTPPBench fills a clear gap: prior TPP datasets are unimodal (temporal + categorical), and no QA benchmark for TPPs existed before this work. The finding that MLLMs do not consistently outperform text-only LLMs on multi-modal TPP tasks challenges the assumption that adding visual context automatically improves temporal reasoning, pointing to unresolved integration challenges in current MLLM architectures. Key limitations include the dataset's restriction to Chinese-language Bilibili content and the dependence of annotation quality on the underlying LLM/MLLM capabilities. Open questions include how to better fuse visual frames with long temporal event sequences, whether the multi-modal advantage emerges at larger scales or with different frame-sampling strategies, and how to extend the benchmark beyond a single platform and language.
