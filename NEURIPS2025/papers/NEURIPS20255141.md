## Problem

Linear RNNs face a fundamental tradeoff between expressivity and efficiency dictated by their state-transition matrix structure: diagonal matrices (Mamba, GLA) are fast but cannot solve basic state-tracking tasks like addition modulo 3, while full matrices are maximally expressive but prohibitively expensive to train. DeltaNet's diagonal-plus-rank-1 structure (a single Householder) partially bridges this gap but still requires multiple layers for complex group word problems. The paper asks whether a tunable, stable mechanism exists to interpolate between diagonal and dense state-transition matrices, improving state-tracking without sacrificing training stability.

## Input / Output

For language modeling, the model consumes text token sequences and produces next-token predictions, trained on FineWeb and evaluated on CodeParrot, OpenThoughts-114kMath, and TriviaQA for length extrapolation beyond the 4096-token training context. For state-tracking, the input is a sequence of permutations from symmetric groups (S₃, S₄, A₅, S₅) and the output is the cumulative group element after composing all permutations up to that position. Internally, each token x_i generates n_h keys, n_h values, and n_h scalar betas (via learned projections with SiLU nonlinearity), which parameterize the n_h Householder factors of the state-transition matrix A(x_i) ∈ R^{n×n} and the rank-n_h update B(x_i) ∈ R^{n×d}.

## Method

DeltaProduct generalizes DeltaNet by performing n_h steps of online gradient descent per token on the associative recall loss ‖Hᵀk − v‖²/2, rather than DeltaNet's single step. Each step j uses its own key k_{i,j}, value v_{i,j}, and step size β_{i,j}, producing a state-transition matrix A(x_i) = ∏_{j=1}^{n_h}(I − β_{i,j} k_{i,j} k_{i,j}ᵀ) — a product of n_h generalized Householder transformations. This product structure guarantees ‖A(x_i)‖ ≤ 1 (stability) while allowing A(x_i) to have rank up to n_h, interpolating between DeltaNet (n_h=1) and dense matrices. By the Cartan-Dieudonné theorem, any orthogonal matrix is a product of at most n Householders, so sufficiently large n_h enables modeling rotations and arbitrary permutations. The gated variant multiplies A(x_i) by a scalar forget gate g_i ∈ [0,1] for accelerated state resetting. Theoretically, DeltaProduct solves the S_n word problem in one layer with n_h = n−1, in 3 layers with n_h > 1, or in 4 layers with n_h = 1, and Gated DeltaProduct recognizes any regular language in a finite number of layers via Krohn-Rhodes decomposition.

## Results

On state-tracking, DeltaProduct with n_h=2 solves S_3 in a single layer (vs. 3 layers for DeltaNet n_h=1), and S_5 requires n_h=4 in one layer while even 10 layers of DeltaNet remain insufficient. For language modeling, DeltaProduct_2[-1,1] with 8 heads (392M parameters) matches the parameter count of DeltaNet with 12 heads while achieving significantly better length extrapolation beyond the 4096-token training context. At n_h=3, performance degradation across sequence length is minimal. In scaling analysis, DeltaProduct achieves lower training perplexity than DeltaNet when scaling via n_h rather than head dimension, and maintains its advantage on lm-eval-harness tasks at the largest scale tested.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DeltaNet (n_h=1), DeltaProduct consistently wins: it requires fewer layers for state-tracking (e.g., 1 layer with n_h=2 for S_3 vs. 3 layers; 1 layer with n_h=4 for S_5 vs. 10+ layers insufficient), achieves significantly better length extrapolation at matched parameter counts (392M), and scales more favorably in training perplexity. Against Gated DeltaNet, Gated DeltaProduct outperforms on language modeling benchmarks, and notably DeltaProduct_3[-1,1] (without a forget gate) achieves comparable performance to Gated DeltaNet[-1,1], suggesting the additional Householder products partially compensate for the absence of explicit gating. The paper also positions DeltaProduct favorably against RWKV-7 in terms of guaranteed recurrence stability (spectral norm ≤ 1), while acknowledging RWKV-7 can recognize regular languages in 4 layers without the stability guarantee.

## Relation to Prior Work

DeltaProduct extends DeltaNet (Yang et al., NeurIPS 2024) and Gated DeltaNet (Yang et al., ICLR 2025) by replacing the single per-token gradient descent step with n_h steps, which naturally yields state-transition matrices as products of n_h generalized Householder transformations. This connects the online-learning interpretation of DeltaNet's recurrence to the Cartan-Dieudonné theorem (any orthogonal matrix decomposes into at most n reflections), providing a principled interpolation between diagonal (n_h=0) and dense matrices. The theoretical expressivity analysis builds on Grazzi et al. (ICLR 2025) for negative-eigenvalue state-tracking and on Peng et al. (RWKV-7) for multi-layer constructions, while the stability guarantee (spectral norm ≤ 1) distinguishes DeltaProduct from RWKV-7's asymmetric rank-1 updates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DeltaProduct offers a practical, tunable knob (n_h) to balance expressivity against training cost in linear RNNs, with the dramatic length-extrapolation improvement from n_h=1 to n_h=2 suggesting that current DeltaNet-based models are significantly under-parameterized in their recurrence. The open question of whether a stable parameterization can recognize any regular language in a fixed (rather than language-dependent) number of layers remains unresolved, and the linear scaling of compute with n_h limits how far this approach can be pushed. The finding that the model spontaneously discovers SO(3) rotation structure for S_4/A_5 (via two reflections composing to rotations) provides a rare empirical validation of the theoretical geometric interpretation.
