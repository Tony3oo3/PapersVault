## Problem

Multimodal large language models (MLLMs) process large numbers of visual tokens that become redundant in deeper layers, yet existing efficiency methods focus on token-wise pruning or merging without understanding *when* visual tokens stop contributing to reasoning. This paper identifies that the optimal removal layer varies across tasks and examples, making a fixed-layer or token-wise strategy suboptimal, and asks: can we dynamically and automatically determine the right moment to remove all visual tokens without degrading performance?

## Input / Output

At inference, the MLLM receives a standard RGB image and a text question, producing a text answer as usual. The DyVTE hypernetwork operates internally at each transformer layer, consuming only two scalar-level text representations: the element-wise mean of all text token hidden states and the hidden state of the last text token (which plays a critical role under causal self-attention for decoding). It outputs a binary decision—whether to remove all visual tokens from the remaining layers—while the text tokens continue through the full depth of the model unchanged.

## Method

DyVTE is built on the empirical finding that MLLM inference proceeds through three stages (early fusion, intra-modality modeling, multimodal reasoning) and that visual tokens cease to meaningfully influence text self-attention after a task-dependent layer. A lightweight two-layer MLP hypernetwork, parameterized by W₁ ∈ R^{2d×h} and W₂ ∈ R^{h×2}, takes the concatenated [mean(T₁:ₜ₋₁), Tₜ] representation at each layer and outputs a softmax binary prediction for visual-token exit. Training uses a cheap, MLLM-gradient-free supervision: at each step a random exit layer is sampled, the MLLM is run both with and without visual tokens removed at that layer, and the hypernetwork receives a positive label only if the discrete outputs match and the prediction uncertainty (scaled cross-entropy) stays below a threshold τ. This avoids backpropagating through the frozen MLLM entirely, requiring only 1% of LLaVA-665k for one epoch. Unlike dynamic early-exit methods that skip remaining layers for direct prediction, DyVTE keeps text tokens flowing through all remaining layers, preserving the model's original inference pattern while eliminating the cross-modal attention computation.

## Results

DyVTE reduces the computation overhead of LLaVA-1.5 7B by up to 45.7% TFLOPs on SEED with zero performance drop, and by 43.9% on SQA-I while maintaining 69.6% accuracy (vs. 69.4% baseline). When combined with FastV, DyVTE achieves a 51.5% average TFLOPs reduction with only 0.7% average accuracy drop across four benchmarks. On LLaVA-1.5 13B, DyVTE reduces SQA-I computation by 55.9% with only 0.8% accuracy loss. In actual inference latency, DyVTE alone achieves 32.1% speedup on SQA-I and 30.9% on MMB, while DyVTE+FastV reaches 34.9% and 34.4% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DyVTE is compared against two token-level efficiency baselines: FastV (attention-score-based token pruning) and ToMe (token merging). On LLaVA-1.5 7B, DyVTE outperforms both baselines on average accuracy (56.2% vs. 55.7% for FastV and 55.4% for ToMe) while achieving the lowest TFLOPs among single methods (5.5, tied with ToMe but with higher accuracy). Specifically, DyVTE beats FastV on SEED (58.6 vs. 58.2 accuracy, 5.0 vs. 5.8 TFLOPs) and SQA-I (69.6 vs. 69.0 accuracy, 5.5 vs. 6.2 TFLOPs), and beats ToMe on SEED (58.6 vs. 57.8), MM-Vet (31.9 vs. 30.6), and MMB (64.7 vs. 63.7). In actual inference latency (Table 3), DyVTE alone is faster than both FastV (32.1% vs. 27.7% speedup on SQA-I) and ToMe (32.1% vs. 26.2%), though with a small 0.8% accuracy drop on SQA-I. The key advantage of DyVTE is its orthogonality: when combined with FastV, the hybrid achieves 51.5% average TFLOPs reduction and 34.9% latency reduction on SQA-I with only 0.7% average accuracy drop, surpassing either method alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions DyVTE as orthogonal to token-wise visual compression methods (FastV, FitPrune, PruMerge, ToMe) that prune or merge individual visual tokens, instead targeting the overall contribution of all visual tokens as a group. It also departs from dynamic early-exit methods for LLMs (EE-LLM, DEECAP, FrameExit) that skip remaining layers for direct prediction; DyVTE keeps text tokens flowing through all remaining layers while only removing visual tokens. The empirical three-stage decomposition of MLLM inference (early fusion → intra-modality modeling → multimodal reasoning) provides a new analytical lens that unifies observations from prior attention-based studies, and the lightweight hypernetwork training regime is independent of MLLM gradient back-propagation, distinguishing it from methods that require joint optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: DyVTE reduces FLOPs by 45–56% across multiple MLLMs with negligible performance loss, and its cross-model generalization (train on LLaVA, deploy on VILA/InternVL) means a single hypernetwork can serve multiple architectures. The three-stage inference decomposition offers a reusable analytical framework for understanding MLLM behavior beyond just efficiency. Key limitations include the all-or-nothing visual-token exit (partial retention in later layers may be suboptimal) and the reliance on discrete answer-matching for training supervision, which may not capture subtle quality differences. Open questions include whether the approach extends to video MLLMs, autoregressive multi-turn settings, or cases where fine-grained visual detail is needed throughout inference.
