## Problem

Aerial-ground person re-identification (AG-ReID) must match pedestrians captured from drastically different viewpoints (top-down UAV vs. frontal ground cameras), introducing severe geometric distortions and semantic misalignment from occlusions. Prior methods learn cross-view representations but rely on implicit global alignment, overlooking the need for explicit spatial warping of features and visibility-aware filtering of occluded body regions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253137/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB pedestrian images (resized to 256×128) from either aerial or ground cameras and produces a fixed-length feature embedding via a ViT-Base backbone augmented with a view token. During training, the Dynamic Alignment Module additionally generates a channel-wise mask vector per sample that modulates class prototypes, but this mask is discarded at inference so the model outputs only the standard feature embedding for CMC/mAP/mINP retrieval evaluation.

## Method

GSAlign builds on the View-Decoupled Transformer (VDT) with a ViT-Base backbone and introduces two complementary modules. The Learnable Thin Plate Spline (LTPS) module predicts a rotation angle from patch-level features, rotates a set of learnable 2D control points, and constructs a non-rigid TPS warp that is applied to patch features before each transformer layer, enabling progressive, layer-wise geometric correction from local to global deformations. The Dynamic Alignment Module (DAM) uses a two-layer MLP with Sigmoid to generate a per-sample channel-wise mask that reweights class prototypes during training, suppressing occluded or identity-irrelevant feature dimensions while emphasizing visible, discriminative channels. Training combines ID classification loss, triplet loss, a deformation regularizer that penalizes large rotation angles, and a mask loss with alignment and entropy terms to encourage sparse, selective masking. The full model is trained end-to-end with AdamW, cosine LR schedule, and ImageNet-21K pretraining for 120 epochs at batch size 64.

## Results

On the CARGO dataset (the primary benchmark), GSAlign achieves 65.06% Rank-1, 57.95% mAP, and 44.97% mINP under the ALL protocol, and a striking 64.89% Rank-1, 61.55% mAP, and 52.81% mINP on the most challenging A↔G cross-view protocol. This represents absolute gains of +16.77% Rank-1, +18.79% mAP, and +22.86% mINP over the strongest prior method (VDT) on A↔G. On the real-world AG-ReID dataset, GSAlign reaches 83.75% Rank-1 and 75.01% mAP (A↔G), and on AG-ReID v2 it attains the highest mAP across all four protocols (81.38% on A↔G, 83.98% on A↔W).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GSAlign outperforms all 15 compared baselines on the CARGO A↔G protocol, the most challenging cross-view setting. The strongest prior method, VDT (CVPR 2024), is surpassed by +16.77% Rank-1, +18.79% mAP, and +22.86% mINP on A↔G, and by +0.96% Rank-1 and +2.75% mAP on the ALL protocol. DTST, the second-strongest baseline on CARGO (64.42% Rank-1 ALL), is outperformed by +0.64% Rank-1 and +14.36% mAP on A↔G. On the real-world AG-ReID dataset, GSAlign beats VDT by +0.84% Rank-1 and +0.57% mAP (A↔G), and on AG-ReID v2 it achieves the highest mAP across all four protocols (e.g., 81.38% vs. 80.84% for SeCap on A↔G), though SeCap and AG-ReIDv2 marginally lead on Rank-1 in some settings. Traditional CNN baselines (PCB, BoT, MGN) and earlier AG-ReID-specific methods (SBS, AGW, BAU, PAT) are all substantially outperformed, with gaps exceeding 20% Rank-1 on A↔G.

## Relation to Prior Work

GSAlign builds directly on the View-Decoupled Transformer (VDT, CVPR 2024), adopting its ViT-Base backbone with a view token as the architectural foundation. Prior AG-ReID methods (dual-stream attribute models, VDT, diffusion-based SD-ReID, text-prompt LATex) focus on learning shared embedding spaces or view-invariant features through metric/adversarial objectives, but treat geometric distortion and occlusion-induced semantic misalignment only implicitly. GSAlign departs by introducing explicit, differentiable spatial warping via a Learnable Thin Plate Spline (LTPS) module inserted progressively across ViT layers, combined with a visibility-aware channel-wise mask generator (DAM) inspired by Dynamic Prototype Masking, thereby addressing the two failure modes—geometric and semantic—that prior global-alignment strategies overlook.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The +18.8% mAP and +16.8% Rank-1 gains on the A↔G protocol represent one of the largest single-method improvements reported for AG-ReID, suggesting that explicit geometric correction is a substantially underexploited lever in cross-view ReID. The technique of embedding lightweight, learnable TPS warping inside transformer layers (at negligible FLOPs cost) is a general architectural idea that could transfer to other viewpoint-variant recognition tasks. Key limitations include reliance on the synthetic CARGO benchmark for primary evaluation, potential over-correction in low-distortion A↔A settings, and sensitivity of LTPS to control-point prediction accuracy under heavy occlusion—open questions for real-world deployment at scale.
