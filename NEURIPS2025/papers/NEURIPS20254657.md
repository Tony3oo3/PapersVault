## Problem

Existing medical LLM benchmarks are predominantly text-based and focused on Western medicine, leaving no unified, multimodal evaluation framework for Traditional Chinese Medicine (TCM) that captures the full diagnostic paradigm of inspection, auscultation, inquiry, and palpation. TCM-specific datasets that do exist are limited in scale, lack visual and auditory modalities (tongue images, herb illustrations, pulse recordings), and have not enabled systematic comparison across both general-domain and TCM-specialized large language models.

## Input / Output

The benchmark consumes five task formats: single-choice and multiple-choice text questions, fill-in-the-blank prompts, long-form diagnostic dialogues (25,163 multi-turn patient-physician exchanges), and visual comprehension tasks over 6,061 herbal medicine images and 1,394 tongue image patches, supplemented by 6,420 audio clips and 49 instructional videos. Outputs range from discrete answer selections and exact-match fill-in responses to free-form diagnostic text scored by the proposed Ladder-Score, a weighted composite (α=0.4 TermScore + β=0.6 SemanticScore) that separately measures TCM terminology accuracy and LLM-judged semantic quality including logical consistency, comprehensiveness, and fluency.

## Method

TCM-Ladder is constructed from three sources: 5,000 QA pairs hand-written by licensed TCM practitioners, questions harvested from the National Physician Qualification Examination and open-access platforms, and automatically generated visual QA pairs via a template-based pipeline that pairs a target herb or tongue image with three random distractors. All items undergo deduplication using string edit distance, TF-IDF cosine similarity, and BERT-based semantic encoding, followed by dual-physician verification, then are split 80/10/10 for training/validation/evaluation. Two models are trained on the dataset: BenCao, a ChatGPT fine-tune grounded in 700+ classical TCM texts, and Ladder-base, a Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct model enhanced with Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) over 2 epochs on two A100 GPUs to strengthen TCM-specific reasoning. The paper then runs zero-shot evaluations of nine general-domain LLMs (GPT-4o, Gemini 2.5 Pro, DeepSeek, Qwen3, Claude 3, Grok 3, Kimi k1.5, etc.) and five TCM-specific models (BenTsao, HuatuoGPT2, Zhongjing, etc.) across all five task settings, and introduces Ladder-Score as a domain-aware evaluation metric that goes beyond BLEU/ROUGE by explicitly scoring TCM terminological correctness alongside LLM-assisted semantic judgment.

## Results

On the diagnostic dialogue task, Ladder-base achieves the highest BLEU-4 (0.0249) and ROUGE-L (0.2431), while BenCao attains the best BERTScore (0.9663) and exact match accuracy on fill-in-the-blank (0.9034). Qwen3 records the highest Ladder-Score (0.861) and METEOR (0.2328) among all 14 evaluated models. On single/multiple-choice tasks, Ladder-base consistently outperforms all baselines, exceeding 0.85 exact match in Pharmacognosy, Herbal Formulas, and Pediatrics. On visual tasks, BenCao achieves over 80% accuracy on herb recognition and above 65% on tongue classification, while general-domain LLMs such as GPT-4o and Claude 3 fall below 40% on tongue classification.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Ladder-base dominates on diagnostic dialogue, achieving BLEU-4 of 0.0249 and ROUGE-L of 0.2431, surpassing the next-best Qwen3 (0.0225 / 0.1818) and Gemini 2.5 Pro (0.0180 / 0.1353). BenCao leads on fill-in-the-blank exact match (0.9034), outperforming Qwen3 (0.8786), DeepSeek (0.8740), and Ladder-base (0.8623), while also achieving the highest BERTScore (0.9663). Among general-domain LLMs, Qwen3 and Gemini 2.5 Pro are the strongest, with Qwen3 attaining the best Ladder-Score (0.861) and METEOR (0.2328), and Gemini 2.5 Pro reaching 0.7143 exact match on fill-in-the-blank. TCM-specific baselines (BenTsao, HuatuoGPT2, Zhongjing) consistently underperform, with BenTsao scoring only 0.1620 exact match and Zhongjing the lowest Ladder-Score (0.573). On visual tasks, BenCao exceeds 80% herb recognition and 65% tongue classification, while GPT-4o, Claude 3, Kimi k1.5, and Grok 3 all fall below 40% on tongue classification. Notably, human evaluators achieved 64% top-1 accuracy, approximately 4% below BenCao, suggesting the model already matches or exceeds expert-level recognition on herbal and tongue image tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of TCM-specific evaluation and multimodal benchmarking. Prior TCM QA datasets (TCMBench, TCM-3CEval, TCMD, ShenNong_TCM_Dataset) are exclusively text-based and limited in scale or task diversity, while broader medical benchmarks (CBLUE, MedBench, CMB) focus on Western medicine or lack TCM-specific diagnostic reasoning. TCM-Ladder departs from this lineage by introducing the first unified multimodal TCM QA benchmark spanning seven subdisciplines and incorporating herbal images, tongue images, audio, and video, and by proposing Ladder-Score as a domain-aware evaluation metric that combines terminology accuracy with LLM-assisted semantic scoring. The training of Ladder-base via GRPO on Qwen2.5-7B and the fine-tuning of BenCao from GPT-4 draw on standard LLM adaptation techniques but apply them specifically to TCM reasoning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This benchmark provides the TCM-AI community with a standardized, extensible, and publicly accessible platform for fair comparison across general-domain and TCM-specific LLMs, addressing a gap that has hindered progress in the field. The finding that a 7B GRPO-trained model (Ladder-base) and a fine-tuned GPT-4 model (BenCao) can match or exceed the performance of two licensed TCM physicians on image-based herb recognition (64% human vs. ~68% BenCao) raises interesting questions about the role of domain experts in multimodal TCM tasks. Key limitations remain: the dataset is derived primarily from Chinese clinical populations, pulse and olfactory modalities are underrepresented, and the overall scale (52K questions) still cannot capture the full breadth of TCM knowledge. Open questions include whether deeper multimodal fusion (beyond image classification) will improve diagnostic reasoning, and how the benchmark generalizes across ethnic and geographic diversity.
