## Problem

Optimization-based multi-task learning methods struggle to simultaneously resolve gradient conflicts and task imbalance: gradient-combination approaches (e.g., PCGrad, CAGrad) mitigate conflicts but often sacrifice balanced individual progress, while loss-reweighting approaches (e.g., GradNorm, FAMO) promote fairness but are less robust to conflicts. Prior symmetry teleportation algorithms, which could in principle find a less-conflicting point on the same loss level set, rely on bijective activations and layer-wise pseudo-inverses, making them inapplicable to modern deep networks.

## Input / Output

COST is a plug-and-play training-time augmentation that operates on the shared backbone parameters of any MTL model; it does not alter the input or output format of the underlying task. During training, LoRA adapters (rank 5) are temporarily attached to the backbone, optimized for a short teleportation phase, then merged back so that inference-time architecture and parameter count remain identical to the base model. The framework is demonstrated on RGB scene images (CityScapes, NYUv2), face attribute images (CelebA, 40 tasks), and molecular graphs (QM9, 11 regression properties).

## Method

COST triggers teleportation when a dominated conflict is detected (the mean gradient opposes the smallest-norm task gradient, or at least ⌈K/2⌉ tasks conflict with the mean). At trigger time, a LoRA module Δθ is optimized on the frozen backbone with a composite objective L_lora = L_t − γ·L_g, where L_t enforces loss invariance (minimizing per-task loss deviation from the pre-teleportation snapshot) and L_g maximizes gradient norm via a sharpness-based proxy that samples random perturbations on a sphere and weights them by inverse gradient norm to favor balanced alternatives. After LoRA is merged into the backbone, a Historical Trajectory Reuse (HTR) strategy modulates Adam's momentum and second-moment estimates by scaling them with the cosine similarity between the teleportation direction Δθ and the pre-teleportation gradient, preventing the optimizer from acting on stale trajectory information. The paper proves convergence to a Pareto stationary point under Λ-smoothness with step size η = 1/(Λ√(T−1)).

## Results

On CityScapes (2 tasks), COST achieves Δm% = 4.30±1.3 and MR = 2.00, outperforming the strongest baseline FairGrad (Δm% = 5.18, MR = 2.25). On NYUv2 (3 tasks), COST reaches Δm% = −5.39±0.5 and MR = 3.22, surpassing FairGrad (−4.66, 3.00). On the 40-task CelebA benchmark, COST attains MR = 4.80 and Δm% = 0.93±0.2, beating the next-best IMTL-G (MR = 5.53, Δm% = 0.84) on MR and FairGrad-R (MR = 6.35, Δm% = 1.15) on both metrics. On QM9 (11 regression tasks), COST achieves MR = 4.18 and Δm% = 58.3±1.2, edging out Nash-MTL (MR = 4.27, Δm% = 62.0) and FairGrad-R (MR = 4.82, Δm% = 59.9).

## Baselines & Comparisons

COST consistently outperforms all 11 baselines across all four datasets. Against the closest competitor FairGrad (its base MTL optimizer), COST improves Δm% by 0.88 on CityScapes (4.30 vs 5.18), 0.73 on NYUv2 (−5.39 vs −4.66), and 0.22 on CelebA (0.93 vs 1.15), while also achieving the best MR on CelebA (4.80 vs 6.35). Against gradient-conflict methods like PCGrad and CAGrad, the margins are substantially larger: on CityScapes, COST reduces Δm% from 18.21 (PCGrad) and 11.58 (CAGrad) to 4.30. On the 40-task CelebA benchmark, COST's MR of 4.80 beats the next-best IMTL-G (5.53) by 0.73 and Nash-MTL (5.73) by 0.93, demonstrating strong scalability to many tasks. On QM9, COST achieves the best MR (4.18) and Δm% (58.3), narrowly surpassing Nash-MTL (4.27, 62.0) and FAMO (5.18, 58.5), though the authors note the graph-based architecture limits LoRA's applicability here. The plug-and-play verification (Table 5) further confirms that COST augments CAGrad (Δm% 11.58→8.37), Nash-MTL (9.89→7.15), and FairGrad (5.18→4.30) on CityScapes, with improvements of 0.88–3.21 in Δm%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the symmetry teleportation paradigm introduced by Zhao et al. (2022), which was limited to small networks with bijective activations, by replacing the intractable group action with a LoRA-based parameter perturbation that is practical for modern deep architectures. It is built on top of FairGrad (Ban and Ji, 2024) as the primary MTL optimization backbone and borrows the sharpness estimation concept from Foret et al. (2021) as a computationally feasible proxy for gradient-norm maximization. Crucially, COST is positioned as orthogonal to existing gradient-combination MTL methods (CAGrad, PCGrad, Nash-MTL, etc.), functioning as a plug-and-play augmentation rather than a replacement, which distinguishes it from the reweighting-based approaches that dominate the optimization-based MTL literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

COST provides the first practical symmetry teleportation mechanism for large-scale MTL, opening a new axis of optimization beyond gradient reweighting. Its plug-and-play nature means it can immediately augment any existing MTL method, and the convergence guarantee to a Pareto stationary point adds theoretical grounding. However, the ~30% additional training time, the reliance on LoRA (which limits applicability to architectures supported by PEFT libraries, as seen with QM9's graph model), and the modest performance margins over strong baselines like FairGrad temper its immediate impact. Open questions include whether alternative PEFT methods or more efficient gradient estimation can reduce overhead, and whether the approach scales to even larger task counts or foundation-model-scale MTL settings.
