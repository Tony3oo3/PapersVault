## Problem

Text-to-video diffusion models frequently produce temporally incoherent videos with artifacts such as duplicated limbs, objects appearing or disappearing, and abrupt motion discontinuities. Existing remedies require retraining with temporal objectives, external motion conditioning (e.g., optical flow), or specialized architectures, limiting flexibility and generality. The paper asks whether a meaningful temporal signal can be extracted directly from a pre-trained model's own latent predictions at inference time, without any additional training, auxiliary networks, or external inputs.

## Input / Output

FlowMo is a plug-and-play inference-time module applied to pre-trained Flow Matching–based DiT video models (Wan2.1-1.3B at 480×832, CogVideoX-5B at 480×720), each generating 81 frames at 16 fps (≈5 s). It consumes only the text prompt and the model's internal velocity predictions at selected denoising timesteps (the first 12 of 50), requiring no extra conditioning signals or architectural changes. The output is the same video format as the base model but with reduced temporal artifacts; the method adds roughly 1.8× inference overhead due to gradient-based latent optimization at the targeted steps.

## Method

At chosen early-to-mid denoising timesteps, FlowMo first computes an appearance-debiased temporal representation Δu by taking the ℓ₁ distance between consecutive latent frames of the model's velocity prediction, neutralizing shared appearance content. It then measures the patch-wise temporal variance of Δu across frames for each spatial location and channel, averages over channels to obtain a spatial coherence map, and takes the maximum value as the guidance loss—targeting the single most dynamically incoherent patch. The input latent z_{t_i} is updated via one gradient-descent step (Adam, η=0.005) to minimize this loss, and the denoising step is re-executed with the refined latent. The use of max (rather than mean) is justified both intuitively (artifacts are local) and theoretically (bounded-variance lemmas showing that a single high-variance patch can push generation out-of-distribution). The optimization is restricted to the first 12 timesteps because qualitative and quantitative analyses show that coarse motion structure is established between steps 4–8, while later steps refine high-frequency details where the variance signal is less informative.

## Results

On VBench, FlowMo improves the Final Score (overall video quality) by +6.20% on Wan2.1-1.3B (75.14% → 81.34%) and +5.26% on CogVideoX-5B (65.43% → 70.69%). Motion Smoothness increases by +2.13% (Wan2.1) and +2.28% (CogVideoX), while Quality Score rises by +8.0% and +11.28% respectively. In the VideoJAM user study, FlowMo is preferred for motion coherence in 44.3% of comparisons (vs. 16.2% baseline) on Wan2.1 and 43.0% (vs. 17.6%) on CogVideoX, with similar gains in aesthetic quality and text-video alignment.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's sole direct baseline is FreeInit [59], adapted to the FM-based DiT architecture. FlowMo decisively outperforms FreeInit on every VBench dimension: Final Score 81.34% vs. 75.06% (FreeInit actually falls slightly below the 75.14% baseline), Motion Smoothness 98.56% vs. 96.54%, and Aesthetic Quality 58.03% vs. 50.99%. Critically, FreeInit degrades Dynamic Degree by −16.20% (83.21% → 67.01%), suggesting its apparent coherence comes from suppressing motion rather than improving it, whereas FlowMo preserves dynamic level (81.96%). In the head-to-head user study, FlowMo is preferred over FreeInit for motion coherence (38.7% vs. 23.4%), aesthetic quality (28.1% vs. 14.8%), and text-video alignment (16.5% vs. 5.2%). Qualitatively, FreeInit still produces disappearing limbs, rope distortions, and spontaneous front/back flips that FlowMo eliminates.

## Relation to Prior Work

FlowMo sits at the intersection of inference-time guidance (à la Attend-and-Excite [47]) and temporal coherence in video diffusion, but departs from both lines in a key way: rather than optimizing spatial attention maps or relying on external motion priors (optical flow, trajectories [6,7,8,9]), it extracts a temporal signal from the model's own velocity predictions in latent space. The closest prior work, FreeInit [59] and VideoGuide [60], targeted UNet-based DDPM/DDIM models and addressed SNR mismatches; FlowMo instead exploits the internal structure of modern FM-based DiT architectures, using an appearance-debiasing operator (ℓ₁ distance between consecutive frame latents) and patch-wise temporal variance as a self-supervised coherence metric. The method is built directly on the Flow Matching framework [44] and evaluated on Wan2.1 [1] and CogVideoX [2], positioning it as a lightweight, training-free module for the current generation of video diffusion models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FlowMo demonstrates that pre-trained video diffusion models implicitly encode exploitable temporal structure in their latent predictions, opening a path toward improving coherence without retraining, external conditioning, or architectural changes. The empirical finding that coarse spatial structure is locked in during early denoising steps (0–4) while motion emerges at intermediate steps (5–8) provides a useful inductive bias for where to apply temporal guidance. Key limitations include a 1.82× inference slowdown from gradient-based optimization and the method's inability to synthesize motion types the base model has not learned. An open question is whether the variance-based objective could be folded into training to eliminate the inference-time cost, and whether the approach generalizes to longer videos or higher resolutions where temporal artifacts compound.
