## Problem

Combinatorial Bayesian Optimization has produced several successful kernels (CASMOPOLITAN, COMBO, Bounce, BODi, BOSS), but the relationships among them remain poorly understood, making it difficult to know which properties drive their success. This paper asks whether these seemingly distinct kernels can be unified under a single theoretical framework, and whether such unification yields practical improvements in speed and robustness.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255081/cropped_page7_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on combinatorial input spaces defined as products of finite (unordered) sets, i.e., purely categorical variables with no ordinal structure. The GP surrogate is evaluated directly on these combinatorial points via a closed-form kernel expression requiring only O(n) computation per pair (no numerical eigendecomposition). The pipeline outputs the next evaluation point by maximizing an acquisition function (expected improvement) over the combinatorial space, using a genetic algorithm with a trust region for acquisition-function optimization.

## Method

The paper derives a unifying framework by viewing the combinatorial space as a Hamming graph (Cartesian product of complete graphs, one per categorical variable) and showing that heat kernels on this graph reduce to a simple closed-form product over dimensions. The central theorem proves that the CASMOPOLITAN kernel, the COMBO kernel, and the heat kernel are all equivalent, and further that they coincide with the RBF kernel applied after one-hot encoding. The authors then generalize in two directions: (i) Hamming kernels (any isotropic continuous kernel with Euclidean distance replaced by √Hamming distance) and (ii) Φ-kernels (graph kernels parameterized by a regularization function of Laplacian eigenvalues), proving the two classes coincide when all categorical variables have equal cardinality. Extensions to group invariances (via padded sorting for permutation-invariant functions) and additive decompositions (recovering CoCaBO and RDUCB kernels) connect the framework to an even wider set of existing methods. The practical pipeline is deliberately minimal: RBF kernel on one-hot-encoded inputs, a genetic algorithm for acquisition optimization, and a trust region—achieving state-of-the-art results with the third-lowest wall-clock time among all baselines.

## Results

The paper's primary empirical finding is that seven theoretically related kernels (RBF after one-hot encoding, Kondor & Lafferty heat kernel, CASMOPOLITAN, COMBO, Bounce's Matérn, graph Matérn, and RQ after one-hot encoding) achieve nearly indistinguishable performance across all five Bounce benchmarks (Pest Control, LABS, Cluster Expansion, Contamination Control, MaxSAT) when the pipeline is held fixed. The proposed simple heat-kernel pipeline (RBF + genetic algorithm + trust region) matches or outperforms all seven baselines after relocation of the optima, while achieving the third-lowest wall-clock time (closely following CoCaBO and Bounce). BODi (HED) and BOSS (SSK) show significant performance degradation when optima are relocated, whereas all heat/Hamming kernels remain unaffected.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CASMOPOLITAN and COMBO are proven theoretically equivalent to the heat kernel (Theorem 5) and confirmed empirically to produce near-identical results when compared under the same pipeline and hyperparameter settings. Bounce's Matérn kernel (after one-hot encoding) is shown to be a Hamming kernel and achieves near-identical performance to the heat kernel across all benchmarks. BODi (HED) is proven to be fundamentally different from heat kernels (Appendix A.4) and degrades substantially when optima are relocated, consistent with findings in Papenmeier et al. (2023). BOSS (SSK) is the first to be reported as degrading after optima relocation, attributed to the SSK's bias toward repeated categorical values. CoCaBO and RDUCB are connected to the heat-kernel framework via additive structure (Appendix D.2) and show similar performance to heat kernels on standard benchmarks. The proposed simple pipeline (RBF + GA + trust region) outperforms or matches all baselines after relocation while being faster than COMBO, CASMOPOLITAN, BODi, and BOSS, with only CoCaBO and Bounce being slightly faster.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the heat (diffusion) kernel framework of Kondor and Lafferty (2002) and the Φ-kernel formalism of Borovitskiy et al. (2023) to provide a unifying theoretical lens for combinatorial Bayesian optimization kernels. It positions itself against the two dominant combinatorial BO methods—CASMOPOLITAN (Hamming-distance-based) and COMBO (graph-based)—which have been treated as fundamentally different approaches in the literature, and against Bounce's (Papenmeier et al., 2023) empirical observation that certain kernels degrade under optima relocation. The key conceptual departure is proving that these apparently distinct kernel families are either identical or belong to the same generalized class, collapsing what the field has treated as separate methodological choices into a single framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unification clarifies a fragmented landscape: practitioners no longer need to choose between 'Hamming-based' and 'graph-based' kernels, as they are provably equivalent for categorical variables, and the practical consequence is a 2× speedup in COMBO's kernel evaluation (from O(Σ|Xi|³) to O(n)). The simple three-component pipeline (RBF after one-hot encoding + genetic algorithm + trust region) achieving state-of-the-art results suggests that much of the complexity in existing combinatorial BO methods may be unnecessary. Limitations include restriction to categorical (not ordinal or mixed) variables, and the framework's applicability is strongest when little prior structure is available; open questions include whether the equivalence extends to ordinal variables (where path graphs replace complete graphs but lose closed-form simplification) and to mixed continuous-categorical spaces.
