key_contribution: A training-free, Video-LLM-agnostic streaming video QA method (rLiVS) that leverages LLM attention scores to select ~6% of visual tokens per clip, recurrently feeds them into subsequent clips via a FIFO queue for temporal coherence, and performs caption-based MMR retrieval for answering, achieving state-of-the-art accuracy on streaming benchmarks with ~95% visual token compression and significantly lower memory than prior methods.
limitations: The method prioritizes efficiency over completeness by attending only to selected content, potentially overlooking fine-grained details, and the purely temporal FIFO memory buffer may not always capture semantically salient information. Being training-free and fully reliant on pre-trained backbones, it inherits their limitations in visual understanding and temporal reasoning, and the recurrent captioning process can introduce redundancy across clips that may affect retrieval performance.

surprise_factor: The most notable finding is that caption-only retrieval and answering consistently outperforms both visual-token and combined approaches (Table 5), which is counterintuitive since visual tokens theoretically carry richer information; the paper attributes this to the training-distribution mismatch of Video-LLMs on long temporal contexts. ## Problem

Video-LLMs excel at understanding short clips in-context but fail to scale to hour-long streaming videos where frames arrive continuously and questions require timely responses. Existing long-video approaches either require costly training, store large KV-caches that become memory-intensive over time, or process clips independently without temporal continuity, making entity tracking and cross-clip reasoning difficult. The paper addresses how to achieve efficient, training-free, and model-agnostic streaming video understanding with minimal memory footprint.

## Input / Output

The method consumes short video clips of 16 frames (at 0.5 FPS) encoded into 196 visual tokens per frame by a CLIP-based vision encoder, along with a text instruction and a FIFO queue of previously selected visual tokens (up to 16 past clips). During processing, it outputs a textual caption per clip; during query answering, it retrieves top-K captions via MMR and produces a free-form text answer. The entire pipeline operates within a 10K-token context window and requires no external embedding model, using only the LLM's own token space for similarity computation.

## Method

rLiVS (Recurrent LLM-informed Visual Selection) is a training-free, Video-LLM-agnostic pipeline with three components. First, after generating a caption for a short clip, cross-attention scores between the generated caption tokens and input visual tokens are extracted from a subset of LLM layers (4 of 28), averaged across heads and caption tokens, and the top 196 tokens (~6.25% of 3,136) are retained as a compressed memory trace. Second, these selected tokens are prepended to the next clip's raw tokens in a FIFO queue (max 16 clips), enabling recurrent temporal context that guides the LLM's attention toward content coherent with past events. Third, for question answering, stored caption embeddings are retrieved using Maximal Marginal Relevance (balancing query relevance and inter-caption diversity), and the LLM generates an answer from the retrieved captions plus the query—transforming the problem into a text-based long-context QA task that leverages LLMs' proven strength in extended textual reasoning.

## Results

On the primary streaming benchmark (Realtime VStream-QA), rLiVS with LLaVA-OV-7B achieves 65.3% accuracy (4.0 score) on RVS-Ego and 57.7% accuracy (3.6 score) on RVS-Movie, outperforming the previous best (ReKV) by 1.6% and 3.3% respectively, while reducing latency from 2.7s to 1.9s and peak VRAM from 36GB to 25GB. On offline long-video benchmarks, rLiVS sets new state-of-the-art on VS-Ego (61.0% acc), VS-Movie (59.3% acc), and MovieChat (78.0% acc), and achieves 33.1% on CG-Bench. When paired with Qwen2.5-VL-7B, the method reaches 68.1% accuracy on RVS-Ego, demonstrating model-agnostic plug-and-play capability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

rLiVS outperforms all nine baselines on the primary streaming benchmarks. Against the closest competitor ReKV [9] (same LLaVA-OV-7B backbone), rLiVS gains +1.6% accuracy on RVS-Ego (65.3 vs 63.7) and +3.3% on RVS-Movie (57.7 vs 54.4), while reducing latency by 0.8s (1.9s vs 2.7s) and peak VRAM by 11GB (25GB vs 36GB), and eliminating the 18.8GB/h KV-Cache growth. On offline benchmarks, rLiVS beats Flash-VStream-7B [38] by +2.0% on VS-Ego (61.0 vs 59.0) and +3.2% on VS-Movie (59.3 vs 56.1), and surpasses Goldfish [1] by +10.4% accuracy on MovieChat (78.0 vs 67.6), though Goldfish retains a slightly higher GPT-score (4.2 vs 4.0). VideoScan [20] is outperformed by +4.4% on RVS-Ego and +3.6% on RVS-Movie. On the MLVU summarization task (Appendix I), rLiVS (3.65) outperforms the training-based Video-XL [27] (3.40) despite requiring no fine-tuning. The method also beats Video-ChatGPT [22], Chat-UniVi [14], LLaMA-VID [21], and MovieChat [28] across all shared benchmarks by margins of 5–20%.

## Relation to Prior Work

rLiVS repurposes the attention-based token pruning literature (which typically drops tokens within a single forward pass for efficiency) into a cross-clip recurrent memory mechanism: attention scores from caption generation determine which visual tokens are retained and fed into subsequent clips. It departs from KV-cache-based streaming methods like ReKV by storing only a sparse set of selected tokens plus captions rather than full decoder caches, and from caption-only methods like Goldfish by introducing recurrency that lets past visual context guide current attention. The design is explicitly training-free and model-agnostic, positioning it as a plug-and-play wrapper around any short-clip Video-LLM (demonstrated on LLaVA-OneVision and Qwen2.5-VL), in contrast to training-based approaches like Flash-VStream and VideoStreaming that require task-specific fine-tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a strong, simple baseline for streaming video QA that achieves SOTA with ~95% visual token compression, no training, and significantly lower memory (11GB less peak VRAM, no KV-cache) than prior methods, making it practically deployable on consumer hardware. The finding that caption-based retrieval and answering outperforms visual-token-based approaches (Table 5) is a useful counterintuitive result that reframes long-video QA as a text-reasoning problem, leveraging LLMs' proven long-context strengths. Key open questions include whether purely temporal FIFO memory can be replaced with semantic selection, whether adaptive compression based on scene complexity helps, and how the method behaves when integrated into training pipelines rather than remaining training-free.
