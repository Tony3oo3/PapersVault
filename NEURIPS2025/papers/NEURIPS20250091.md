## Problem

Existing multi-modal test-time adaptation methods assume only one modality undergoes domain shift, but real-world deployments (e.g., autonomous driving) face simultaneous corruption across all modalities. Under multi-modal domain shifts, the pre-trained model's predictions collapse to a narrow set of classes, and confidence-based sample selection fails because high-confidence predictions are frequently false positives, causing error accumulation during adaptation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250091/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on online mini-batches of paired video-audio clips (Kinetics50, VGGSound) or tri-modal text-audio-video clips (CMU-MOSI, CMU-MOSEI, CH-SIMS) during inference, producing a single class label per sample. A critical requirement is batch-level statistics: the method computes each sample's predicted-label frequency relative to the batch mean, so it needs a sufficient batch size (or a memory queue for small batches) to estimate prediction bias. The pre-trained CAV-MAE backbone encodes each modality into tokens that are fused via attention before classification.

## Method

PTA comprises two components. First, Partition and Debiased Reweighting (PDR) computes each sample's prediction bias as the difference between its predicted-label frequency and the batch-average frequency, partitioning the batch into less-biased (reliable) and more-biased (unreliable) subsets. It then applies a quantile-based reweighting that jointly ranks bias and confidence levels, assigning positive weights to reliable samples and a fixed negative weight to unreliable ones, enabling simultaneous entropy minimization on reliable samples and entropy maximization on unreliable ones. Second, multi-modal Attention-Guided Alignment (AGA) regularizes the unreliable subset's attention maps toward those of the reliable subset via an MMD-based loss in a Gaussian-kernel RKHS, preventing the entropy-maximization objective from driving attention toward a uniform, non-discriminative distribution. The overall loss combines the weighted entropy term and the MMD alignment term with a balancing coefficient λ, updating only the attention QKV matrices in the fusion block.

## Results

On Kinetics50-MC (severity 5, multi-modal domain shifts), PTA achieves 41.47% average accuracy, surpassing the best baseline READ (35.40%) by 6.1% and the pre-trained source model (31.76%) by 9.7%. On VGGSound-MC, PTA reaches 18.29% average accuracy, outperforming READ (12.50%) by 5.8%. On real-world multi-modal domain shifts (CMU-MOSI/MOSEI/CH-SIMS), PTA achieves the best accuracy across all six domain transfer pairs, improving over the pre-trained model by 2.56% on average.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PTA outperforms all seven baselines across every corruption type on both Kinetics50-MC and VGGSound-MC. The closest competitor is READ [43] (ICLR 2024), which uses reliable fusion and robust adaptation; PTA surpasses it by 6.1% on Kinetics50-MC (41.47 vs. 35.40) and 5.8% on VGGSound-MC (18.29 vs. 12.50). ABPEM [50] (AAAI 2025), which employs attention bootstrapping, is the second-best baseline at 33.91% on Kinetics50-MC, still 7.6% below PTA. ETA [25] (ICML 2022) and SuMi [9] (ICLR 2025) achieve 33.02% and 31.44% respectively, both trailing PTA by over 8%. Tent [37] (ICLR 2021) and MMTTA [29] (CVPR 2022) degrade below the source model on Kinetics50-MC (23.72% and 27.97% vs. 31.76%), highlighting their vulnerability to multi-modal shifts. In continual test-time adaptation (Table 8), PTA (39.24%) dramatically outperforms SAR [26] (4.18%) and READ (24.85%), while most other methods collapse to near-chance levels.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends multi-modal test-time adaptation (MM-TTA) from the single-modal domain shift setting (where only one modality is corrupted) to the more realistic multi-modal domain shift setting (where all modalities are simultaneously corrupted). It builds on TENT's entropy minimization framework and MMD-based distribution alignment, but departs from prior MM-TTA methods (READ, ABPEM, SuMi) that rely on prediction confidence or entropy as the sole criterion for sample selection, demonstrating that these signals become unreliable when multiple modalities are degraded. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of prediction bias (measured via batch-level label frequency) as an additional reweighting signal, combined with quantile-based importance weighting and attention-guided alignment to prevent entropy maximization from collapsing attention maps toward uniform distributions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that high-confidence predictions have substantially degraded true positive rates under multi-modal domain shifts challenges a widely adopted assumption in TTA that confidence is a reliable proxy for sample quality. This has practical implications for deploying multi-modal systems (autonomous driving, embodied AI) where sensor degradation is simultaneous rather than isolated. Open questions include whether the batch-level bias estimation generalizes to streaming settings with very small batch sizes (partially addressed via a memory queue), whether the approach extends to more than two modalities, and whether the MMD-based attention alignment can be made more computationally efficient for real-time deployment. The paper acknowledges efficiency limitations and single-GPU evaluation as constraints for real-world adoption.
