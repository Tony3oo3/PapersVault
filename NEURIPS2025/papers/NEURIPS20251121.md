## Problem

Flow-based generative models achieve state-of-the-art sample quality but require expensive iterative ODE solving at inference time. While flow map (consistency) models can reduce this to a single network evaluation, existing training schemes either depend on a pre-trained teacher (distillation) or suffer from unstable optimization without one, and no unified mathematical framework explains how to learn the flow map directly and efficiently.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251121/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a Gaussian noise sample x₀ ~ ρ₀ and produces a generated sample x₁ in as few as one forward pass via the one-step map X_{0,1}(x₀). For multi-step inference, the two-time flow map X_{s,t} is composed over a grid 0 = t₀ < t₁ < … < t_k = 1, trading additional evaluations for higher fidelity. The parameterization X_{s,t}(x) = x + (t−s)v_{s,t}(x) is trained over the upper triangle s ≤ t of the [0,1]² time domain, with the diagonal s = t recovering the standard velocity field b_t.

## Method

The core insight is the tangent condition: the time derivative of the flow map as s→t recovers the velocity field b_t, meaning the flow map implicitly encodes a velocity model on the diagonal. Self-distillation exploits this by simultaneously training v_{t,t} on the diagonal via standard flow matching (L_b) and enforcing one of three flow map characterizations on the off-diagonal s≠t: the Lagrangian ODE (LSD), the Eulerian PDE (ESD), or the semigroup composition property (PSD). This converts any distillation scheme into a direct training objective for a single network, eliminating the need for a pre-trained teacher. LSD, which avoids both spatial derivatives and bootstrapping from smaller steps, achieves the most stable training and best FID/KL across all benchmarks. The paper further provides 2-Wasserstein error bounds showing that the loss value systematically controls the one-step generation error for both LSD and ESD.

## Results

LSD (Lagrangian Self-Distillation) achieves the best performance across all four benchmarks and all step counts. On CIFAR-10, LSD reaches FID 8.10 at 1 step and 3.34 at 4 steps, compared to 12.81 and 5.96 for PSD-M. On CelebA-64, LSD achieves FID 12.22 (1 step) down to 1.96 (16 steps), while PSD-M ranges from 19.64 to 5.09. On AFHQ-64, LSD scores FID 11.19 (1 step) and 5.61 (16 steps), versus 18.86 and 11.07 for PSD-M. On the 2D checkerboard, LSD attains KL 0.086 at 1 step and saturates near 0.070 by 8 steps, outperforming ESD (0.098 at 1 step) and both PSD variants.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three self-distillation families—LSD, ESD, and PSD (with uniform and midpoint variants)—under identical training budgets. LSD dominates on every dataset and step count: on CIFAR-10 at 1 step it achieves FID 8.10 versus 12.81 (PSD-M) and 13.61 (PSD-U), a gap of roughly 4–5 FID points. On CelebA-64 the advantage widens, with LSD at FID 12.22 (1 step) versus 19.64 for PSD-M, and LSD reaching 1.96 at 16 steps while PSD variants plateau around 5.1–5.6. ESD, which requires spatial Jacobians, suffers training instability on all image datasets (no FID reported) and is only competitive on the low-dimensional checkerboard. PSD-U generally outperforms PSD-M on image datasets, except at 16 steps on CelebA-64 and AFHQ-64 where PSD-M edges ahead slightly. The consistent LSD advantage is attributed to its avoidance of both spatial derivatives and bootstrapping from smaller steps, which the authors link to the stronger Wasserstein error guarantees in Proposition 2.4.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the flow map matching framework of Boffi et al. (2024) and the stochastic interpolant formalism of Albergo et al. (2023), converting their distillation-based training schemes into direct self-distillation objectives via the tangent condition (Lemma 2.1), which links the velocity field to the time derivative of the flow map. The key departure is eliminating the need for a pre-trained teacher: by recognizing that the diagonal v_{t,t} is itself the velocity field learnable via standard flow matching, the off-diagonal flow map can be trained simultaneously through self-consistency. The paper shows that consistency models, consistency trajectory models, shortcut models, mean flow, and align your flow all emerge as special cases of its three algorithmic families (Lagrangian, Eulerian, Progressive), providing the first unified mathematical description of this landscape.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The self-distillation framework gives practitioners a principled, plug-and-play recipe for building few-step generative models without the two-phase teacher-student pipeline, and the finding that Lagrangian self-distillation (LSD) avoids both spatial derivatives and bootstrapping from small steps—yielding the most stable training and best performance across all tested datasets—provides a clear design principle for future work. Theoretical Wasserstein bounds for LSD and ESD (but not PSD) formalize why the progressive/bootstrapping approach is fundamentally weaker. Open questions include whether the framework scales to conditional/guided generation, whether hybrid objectives combining multiple self-distillation terms help, and whether the compute-limited empirical results (no SOTA FID) close the gap with further engineering.
