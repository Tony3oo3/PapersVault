## Problem

Fishelson et al. (2025) achieved O~(T^{1/3}) pseudo swap regret for squared loss, implying the same for bounded proper losses with smooth univariate forms, but their result was limited to pseudo swap regret (measuring losses via the forecaster's conditional distribution rather than realized predictions) and did not extend to losses like log loss or Tsallis entropy with non-smooth univariate forms. This paper closes both gaps by introducing (pseudo) KL-Calibration as a stronger, unifying calibration measure that simultaneously bounds swap regret for a broader class of proper losses and holds for actual (not just pseudo) swap regret.

## Input / Output

At each round t = 1,...,T, the forecaster outputs a probabilistic prediction p_t drawn from a non-uniform discretization Z = {sin²(πi/2K)} (finer near the boundaries 0 and 1 to handle log-loss singularities), while an oblivious adversary selects a binary label y_t ∈ {0,1}. The algorithm requires knowledge of the horizon T to set K = (T/log T)^{1/3}, though a standard doubling trick removes this requirement. The primary outputs are the KL-Calibration error (equivalent to swap regret of log loss) and simultaneous swap regret bounds across loss classes L_G, L_2, and general L.

## Method

The paper introduces (pseudo) KL-Calibration, proving it is exactly equivalent to (pseudo) swap regret of the log loss via Bregman divergence, and shows it upper-bounds swap regret for all proper losses with twice-continuously-differentiable univariate forms (L_2) and G-smooth univariate forms (L_G). For the explicit algorithm, it combines the Blum-Mansour reduction with EWOO (exponentially weighted online optimization for exp-concave losses) and a novel randomized rounding procedure that weights adjacent discretization points by 1/(z(1−z)) to achieve O(1/K²) loss distortion despite the log loss being non-Lipschitz. A non-constructive existence proof for actual KL-Calibration uses von Neumann's minimax theorem to swap the adversary and forecaster, combined with the non-uniform sin² discretization and Freedman's inequality for martingale concentration (rather than Azuma-Hoeffding, which would only yield O(√T) bounds). The non-uniform discretization is essential: it satisfies the constraint max²(d_i, d_{i+1})/(z_i(1−z_i)) = O(1/K²) near the boundaries, which uniform grids fail to achieve.

## Results

The paper establishes that an explicit algorithm (Algorithm 1, based on the Blum-Mansour reduction with a novel randomized rounding scheme) achieves PKLCal = O(T^{1/3}(log T)^{2/3}), which simultaneously implies O(T^{1/3}(log T)^{2/3}) pseudo swap regret for all losses in L_2 and O(G·T^{1/3}(log T)^{2/3}) for all G-smooth losses in L_G. A non-constructive existence result (Theorem 1) shows E[KLCal] = O(T^{1/3}(log T)^{5/3}) for the actual (non-pseudo) swap regret of the log loss. In Appendix E, the paper derives the first sub-√T high-probability bound for classical ℓ₂-Calibration: Cal₂ = O(T^{1/3}(log T)^{-1/3} log(T/δ)) with probability at least 1−δ, along with a matching bound for maximum swap regret against L_G.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's explicit algorithm (Algorithm 1) recovers and extends the result of Fishelson et al. (2025), who achieved PCal₂ = Õ(T^{1/3}) via pseudo swap regret of the squared loss; here, the same Õ(T^{1/3}) rate is obtained for the broader class L_G (via Proposition 3) and additionally for L_2 (via Proposition 2), which includes losses like Tsallis entropy with α∈(1,2) that are not G-smooth. Compared to Hu and Wu (2024), who achieved O(√T log T) swap regret for all bounded proper losses simultaneously, this paper improves the rate to Õ(T^{1/3}) for the important subclasses L_2 ∪ L_G and the log loss, while retaining O(T^{2/3}) for the remaining bounded proper losses (a mild degradation from √T). Against Luo et al. (2024), who showed FTL achieves O(log T) external regret for L_2 ∪ L_G but Ω(T) for some other bounded proper losses, this paper provides a single algorithm with sublinear swap regret (a strictly stronger notion) for all bounded proper losses. The high-probability Cal₂ bound in Appendix E is the first sub-√T guarantee for classical ℓ₂-Calibration via an efficient algorithm, improving upon the in-expectation Õ(T^{1/3}) bound of Foster and Hart (2023) by providing a concentration guarantee.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the calibration-to-swap-regret equivalence framework of Fishelson et al. (2025), who showed that minimizing pseudo swap regret of the squared loss yields pseudo ℓ₂-Calibration via the Blum-Mansour reduction. The key departure is the introduction of (pseudo) KL-Calibration, which is shown to be equivalent to (pseudo) swap regret of the log loss, thereby unifying the analysis under a single Bregman-divergence framework. The non-constructive existence proof adapts the minimax-swap technique of Hu and Wu (2024) but replaces their uniform discretization with a sin²-based non-uniform scheme and substitutes Azuma-Hoeffding with Freedman's inequality to achieve the tighter T^{1/3} rate. The explicit algorithm combines the Blum-Mansour reduction with EWOO and a novel randomized rounding procedure specifically designed for the non-Lipschitz log loss.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first high-probability sub-√T bound for classical ℓ₂-Calibration via an efficient algorithm, and extends simultaneous swap regret guarantees to loss classes (Tsallis entropy, log loss) that were previously out of reach. The non-uniform discretization and log-loss-specific rounding procedure are reusable technical tools for future work on online calibration with non-smooth losses. Open questions include obtaining an explicit high-probability swap regret guarantee for the log loss, improving the T^{2/3} dependence for arbitrary bounded proper losses, and studying KL-Calibration in the offline setting.
