## Problem

Discovering symbolic PDEs from data requires balancing an expressive candidate term library against computational tractability, while accurately estimating derivatives from noisy discrete measurements. Existing methods either use compact polynomial libraries that lack expressiveness for complex structures or generate exponentially many redundant terms, and they treat the derivative calculator and term library as independent components rather than mutually reinforcing modules.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251923/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes discrete spatiotemporal triplets (t, x, û) sampled from a bounded spatial domain Ω ⊂ ℝ^d over a time horizon T, with optional 5% or 10% Gaussian noise added to simulate measurement error. It outputs a symbolic PDE expressed as a sparse linear combination of canonical-form expression terms (involving up to 10 operators) along with estimated real-valued coefficients, supporting both single PDEs and coupled PDE systems in 1D and 2D.

## Method

ABL-PDE integrates a neural network (for derivative estimation via automatic differentiation) with a First-Order Logic knowledge base (for generating canonical-form expression terms via abductive reasoning) in a bidirectional enhancement loop. The FOL knowledge base uses an abductive logic program with canonical-form grammar rules and declarative integrity constraints to prune the hypothesis space from ~10^671 terms (full space at 10 operators) to ~9,000, while retaining full expressive power (Theorem 1). A consistency optimization process alternates between Monotone Subset Selection (MSS), which combines POSS-based coarse selection with binary search on a k-level margin criterion to identify active terms, and FEMTrain, which enforces the physics-informed residual in a weighted-average sense via Galerkin projection onto FEM basis functions rather than pointwise collocation. This joint optimization of network parameters and PDE coefficients stabilizes structure identification, improves coefficient accuracy, and reduces hyperparameter sensitivity by approximately one order of magnitude compared to standard PINN-style training.

## Results

ABL-PDE achieves the best coefficient estimation (measured by sum of L2RE %) in 8 out of 9 task-noise combinations, with headline values of 2.18% (Burgers', 0% noise), 1.03% (Schrödinger, 0% noise), and 15.22% (Navier-Stokes, 5% noise). The method uses a single set of hyperparameters (initial sparsity = 10, margin threshold = 0.05) across all three tasks. Standard deviation across 5 λ values is reduced by approximately one order of magnitude compared to the no-FEM variant, e.g., 0.08 vs. 5.24 on Navier-Stokes at 0% noise.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DL-PDE++ (the strongest external synchronous baseline), ABL-PDE reduces L2RE by roughly 10× on Burgers' (2.18% vs. 22.68% at 0% noise) and by ~10× on Schrödinger (1.03% vs. 10.34%), while on Navier-Stokes it improves from 57.25† to 18.02 at 0% noise. The structure-known oracle PDERidge remains the only method that beats ABL-PDE, and only in one case (Navier-Stokes 0% noise: 17.13 vs. 18.02). The ablation ABL-PDE (w/o CO) shows that without consistency optimization, the method degenerates to PDERidge-level performance on Burgers' and Navier-Stokes but fails badly on Schrödinger (42.16† vs. 1.03), confirming that the iterative refinement loop is essential for coupled PDE systems. The FEM ablation (ABL-PDE w/o FEM) reveals that pointwise collocation is 5–10× worse in coefficient error and has ~10× larger variance across λ values, highlighting the stability benefit of the Galerkin-projected residual.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper applies the Abductive Learning (ABL) framework to PDE discovery, transforming the standard unidirectional pipeline (estimate derivatives → sparse regression over a candidate library) into a bidirectional loop where a neural network and a First-Order Logic knowledge base mutually refine each other. It builds on the synchronous PDE discovery lineage (SINDy, PDE-FIND, Weak SINDy, D-CIPHER) and asynchronous/evolutionary approaches (DL-PDE, SGA-PDE), but departs from both by using an abductive logic program with canonical-form constraints to generate candidate terms, and by replacing pointwise PINN-style collocation with a FEM-based Galerkin residual (FEMTrain) for joint coefficient and network optimization. The Monotone Subset Selection algorithm, which combines POSS for coarse selection with binary search over k-level margins, is a novel algorithmic contribution that leverages the monotonicity of the level-margin criterion (Theorem 2) to avoid the NP-hard sparse-regression subproblem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in the consistency optimization's ability to reduce coefficient L2RE by 5–10× on Burgers' equation and to cut the standard deviation across hyperparameter choices by roughly an order of magnitude, making PDE discovery far less sensitive to the notoriously difficult λ weighting. The FOL knowledge base offers a declarative, extensible mechanism for injecting domain-specific constraints (e.g., continuity-equation symmetries in Navier-Stokes), which is harder to achieve in purely data-driven or evolutionary pipelines. A key limitation acknowledged by the authors is the restriction to linear-combination coefficients, preventing discovery of unknown parameters embedded within nonlinear terms; extending the framework to parameterized term libraries is identified as future work. The approach also remains limited to relatively low-dimensional PDEs (1D and 2D) and a small number of dependent variables, leaving scalability to higher-dimensional or strongly coupled systems open.
