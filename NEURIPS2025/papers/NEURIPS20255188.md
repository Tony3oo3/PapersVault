## Problem

Existing virtual try-on methods are limited to single images or very short (5–10 s) videos, preventing users from fully experiencing garment-body interaction over time. Naively extending short-video generators to longer durations requires prohibitive compute and large-scale long-video training data, while auto-regressive segment-by-segment generation introduces two unsolved challenges: local smoothness at segment boundaries and global temporal consistency across the entire video.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255188/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Given a single user image, a target garment image, a motion reference video specifying the desired try-on motion, and an optional text prompt, VFR produces an arbitrarily long (minute-scale) virtual try-on video at 720×1152 resolution and 8 FPS (refinable to 24 FPS). The motion reference video is segmented to match each generated segment, and a 360° 'A'-pose anchor video is generated first to lock in the full-body appearance. As a by-product, the 3D-consistent anchor video can be reconstructed into a 3D human mesh via NeRFStudio.

## Method

VFR is an auto-regressive, segment-by-segment video diffusion model built on Dress&Dance (a DiT-based architecture) with two additional CondNets for pixel-unaligned video conditions. The key insight is a two-level conditioning scheme: a short 360° 'A'-pose anchor video provides a global appearance prior that enforces cross-segment temporal consistency (since consistency with a common anchor implies mutual consistency), while an f-frame prefix from the previous segment is passed as a conditioning input to ensure local smoothness at segment boundaries. Training avoids long videos by sampling two intervals from the same raw clip—one as the F-frame target and another (resampled to F_a frames) as the anchor—so the model learns to exploit anchor videos under arbitrary motions. An immediate refiner, initialized from the base model, is applied iteratively after each segment generation to remove accumulated artifacts, and the full pipeline requires only short (F to 3F frame) training clips.

## Results

On the hardest evaluation task (30–50 s arbitrary poses), VFR achieves 93.21 Subject Consistency and 95.29 Background Consistency (VBench), substantially outperforming the FramePack-based baseline (88.33 / 90.98) and the Kling-based baseline (92.71 / 94.08). Motion Smoothness is near-saturated for all methods (99.35 for VFR). On GPT-based try-on quality (GPT Overall), VFR scores 78.84, slightly below FramePack (79.33) and Kling (79.40), indicating that the consistency gains come at a marginal cost in perceived try-on fidelity. Across the full evaluation protocol (5 s anchor, 30 s, 90 s, 25 s, 30–50 s), VFR consistently leads all ablation variants on both consistency and GPT metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VFR is compared against two baselines that combine Dress&Dance image try-on with a long-video animation strategy: (1) IVT+I2V using FramePack [14] for autoregressive frame extension, and (2) RI2V using Kling Video 2.0 [86] in a repeating image-to-video loop. On the 30–50 s arbitrary-pose task, VFR wins decisively on Subject Consistency (+4.88 over FramePack, +0.50 over Kling) and Background Consistency (+4.31 over FramePack, +1.21 over Kling), confirming that the anchor-video mechanism provides a stronger global appearance prior than either history-tracking (FramePack) or repeated I2V (Kling). Motion Smoothness is also highest for VFR (99.35 vs. 98.24 / 98.92). However, on GPT Overall try-on quality, VFR (78.84) trails both baselines by a narrow margin (FramePack 79.33, Kling 79.40), suggesting that the consistency-focused conditioning slightly trades off perceived garment fidelity. The paper notes that the training-free D&D variant (no anchor, no prefix) retains most of the original try-on quality (GPT Overall 81.38 in this setting), indicating the gap is small and attributable to the additional conditioning overhead rather than a fundamental quality loss.

## Relation to Prior Work

VFR builds directly on Dress&Dance [8], a diffusion-based model that generates 5-second high-resolution virtual try-on videos, by adding two new conditioning pathways (anchor video and prefix video) to its DiT architecture. Conceptually, it departs from prior long video generation methods (FramePack, StarGen, LCT) that rely on generic memory or history-tracking mechanisms, instead introducing a task-specific anchor video—a short 360° A-pose clip—that provides a global appearance prior without requiring long video training data. The auto-regressive segment-by-segment generation with overlapping prefixes follows the standard paradigm in long video generation, but the combination of anchor conditioning for global consistency and prefix conditioning for local smoothness, together with an immediate refiner for artifact removal, is tailored specifically to the virtual try-on setting where garment and human appearance must remain stable across arbitrary motions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method to produce minute-scale, high-resolution virtual try-on videos from a single image, opening a path toward interactive, personalized garment experiences in e-commerce and virtual social platforms. The observation that temporal consistency training implicitly yields 3D-consistent outputs—enabling free-viewpoint rendering and 3D mesh reconstruction without explicit 3D supervision—is a notable conceptual finding that could inform future 4D try-on research. Key limitations include the 1–2 hour generation time for a 30-second video, the reliance on a proprietary prior work (Dress&Dance), and the acknowledged inadequacy of current evaluation metrics (VBench, GPT-based scores) to capture the smoothness and consistency differences visible to human observers. Open questions include whether the anchor video mechanism generalizes to other long video generation tasks beyond try-on, and how to accelerate inference to make the approach practical for real-time or near-real-time use.
