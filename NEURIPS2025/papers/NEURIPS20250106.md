## Problem

Existing deep hierarchical clustering methods tie leaf nodes to a fixed number of class labels, requiring retraining to change classification granularity, and treat leaf clusters as terminal representations while underutilizing the rich prototype information available at intermediate hierarchical levels. This paper addresses both gaps by learning a taxonomy directly from unlabeled data without assuming the true number of classes, treating every node in the hierarchy as a potential prototype.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250106/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes unlabeled images (28×28 grayscale for MNIST/Fashion, 32×32 RGB for CIFAR-10/100) or pre-trained DINOv2 features, with no requirement to specify the number of classes. It produces a complete binary tree of 2047 Gaussian clusters (depth 10) where every node at every level carries a prototype, along with soft assignment probabilities p(c|z) over all clusters. A post-hoc annotation step converts these into class predictions at any desired granularity (e.g., 10, 20, or 100 classes) without retraining the model.

## Method

Deep taxonomic networks replace the standard Gaussian prior in a VAE with a complete binary tree mixture-of-Gaussians prior, where each parent node's mean and variance are computed as moment-matched convex combinations of its two children, enforcing hierarchical dependency while reducing learnable parameters. The ELBO is maximized via an amortized encoder-decoder, and the paper analytically shows this objective is equivalent to maximizing categorical utility (mutual information between latent representations and the taxonomy), thereby encouraging discovery of prototypical clusters at all levels. For real-world images, the framework is jointly trained with NT-Xent contrastive losses on both image representations and cluster-level assignment distributions. Two additional regularizers—entropy on branch weights and a symmetric-KL margin between sibling leaves—stabilize training of the large 2047-cluster tree.

## Results

On Fashion-MNIST, DeepTaxonNet achieves 81.2% ACC and 72.2% NMI, outperforming TreeVAE (63.6% ACC, 64.7% NMI) by 17.6 and 7.5 percentage points respectively. On CIFAR-10 with contrastive learning, it reaches 67.97% ACC and 51.83% NMI versus TreeVAE's 52.98% and 41.44%. On CIFAR-20, the gap widens further: 40.72% ACC vs. 21.82% for TreeVAE. Notably, a frozen CIFAR-20 model evaluated on CIFAR-100 without retraining achieves 26.36% ACC, more than doubling TreeVAE's 11.98% (which was retrained for 100 classes). On MNIST, DeepTaxonNet achieves the best ACC (94.8%) and LP (96.7%) but trails TreeVAE on DP (76.6 vs. 87.9) and NMI (88.1 vs. 90.0).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against TreeVAE (the strongest baseline), DeepTaxonNet wins on all four metrics for Fashion, CIFAR-10, CIFAR-20, and CIFAR-100, with the largest ACC gains on CIFAR-20 (+18.9 pp) and CIFAR-10 (+15.0 pp). The sole exception is MNIST, where TreeVAE achieves higher DP (87.9 vs. 76.6) and NMI (90.0 vs. 88.1), which the authors attribute to MNIST's low inter-class variance causing leaf clusters to capture handwriting idiosyncrasies rather than digit-level features. Against DeepECT, DeepTaxonNet dominates across all datasets and metrics, with particularly large margins on CIFAR-10 (ACC 67.97 vs. 10.31) and CIFAR-20 (ACC 40.72 vs. 6.97). Against Cobweb and Cobweb+VAE, DeepTaxonNet wins on all metrics for Fashion and both CIFAR variants; on MNIST, raw-pixel Cobweb is competitive on DP (77.4 vs. 76.6) but DeepTaxonNet still leads on ACC (94.8 vs. 88.2) and NMI (88.1 vs. 78.1). In the DINOv2 feature setting (Table 3), DeepTaxonNet matches L2H-Turtle on LP (99.6 vs. 99.5 on CIFAR-10) and is within 1–2 pp on ACC and NMI, while trailing on DP (88.0 vs. 98.8), which the authors attribute to having ~100× more intermediate nodes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the VAE-based clustering line (VaDE's Gaussian mixture prior) by replacing the flat mixture with a complete binary tree mixture-of-Gaussians prior, where parent parameters are convex combinations of children's parameters, enforcing hierarchical dependency structurally. It draws conceptual inspiration from Cobweb's categorical utility principle and its use of intermediate prototypes, but realizes this within a modern deep generative framework with end-to-end training and contrastive learning. Unlike TreeVAE (which stacks multiple stochastic layers) or DeepECT (which recursively splits in embedding space), deep taxonomic networks use a single amortized posterior over a pre-defined tree, treating every node—not just leaves—as a potential prototype for a data point.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is that the learned hierarchy can be re-interpreted for different label granularities without retraining (a frozen CIFAR-20 model outperforms a retrained TreeVAE on CIFAR-100), which addresses a real limitation of current hierarchical clustering methods that are locked to a fixed number of classes. The theoretical link between ELBO maximization and categorical utility maximization provides a principled justification for why the learned hierarchy should capture semantically meaningful prototypes at multiple levels. Open questions include whether the fixed balanced binary tree assumption degrades on highly imbalanced datasets, and whether the generative model can produce meaningful samples at intermediate abstraction levels.
