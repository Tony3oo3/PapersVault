## Problem

Medical imaging suffers from large scale variability because radiologists routinely adjust zoom levels, causing the same anatomical structure to appear at very different sizes across images. Existing detection methods either handle multi-scale features generically (e.g., FPN, Deformable-DETR) or rely on graph-based structural priors that are fragile to noise and inconsistent zoom, leaving the zoom-induced scale information and fixed anatomical topology largely unexploited for reliable detection.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255377/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single medical image (ultrasound, CT, or MRI) uniformly resized to 800×1333 pixels and produces per-instance bounding boxes with anatomical class labels plus a predictive variance for each detection, obtained from a Gaussian Process posterior. Unlike standard DETR outputs that yield only point estimates, ZR-DETR's GP head returns both a mean and an uncertainty estimate per query, enabling calibrated confidence scores. The zoom level for each proposal is derived internally as the log-ratio of its area to the full image area, requiring no external annotation.

## Method

ZR-DETR extends Deformable-DETR's two-branch Transformer pipeline with three coupled innovations. First, a Zoom Relation Encoder computes pairwise log-scale ratios between all proposal pairs, encodes them via sinusoidal positional embeddings, and injects the resulting tensor as a multiplicative modulation into the self-attention of the matching branch, making attention scale-aware. Second, an anatomical relation consistency loss penalizes predicted bounding-box pairs whose normalized Hausdorff distance deviates from a data-estimated prior proximity, using a fixed anatomical adjacency matrix to select which pairs to constrain. Third, a Gaussian Process detection head replaces the standard MLP head with a composite kernel (RBF over appearance features + rational quadratic over zoom levels + Kronecker delta over class identity), yielding closed-form posterior mean and variance for each detection. Training combines the standard dual-branch DETR loss with the anatomy consistency term and a KL-divergence GP regularization; in the UDA setting, an additional KL term aligns the target-domain GP posterior with the source-domain kernel structure.

## Results

On the FCS (4C) dataset, ZR-DETR achieves 98.0% mAP on Site A and 94.2% mAP on Site B in single-domain detection, and 79.8% mAP in the cross-domain A→B UDA setting, outperforming all baselines. On FCS (3VT), it reaches 97.3% mAP (Site A), 48.8% mAP (Site B), and 62.4% mAP (A→B UDA). On EPV, ZR-DETR attains 94.4% mAP, and on MM-WHS it achieves 94.8% (CT) and 84.5% (MRI) single-domain mAP, with 69.4% (CT→MRI) and 77.9% (MRI→CT) cross-domain mAP, establishing new state-of-the-art across all four benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ZR-DETR consistently outperforms all 12 baselines across every evaluation scenario. In single-domain detection, it surpasses the strongest transformer baselines (Relation-DETR, MI-DETR, DINO) by 0.7–1.0% mAP on FCS 4C Site A (98.0 vs. 97.3/97.0) and by 0.8–1.0% on FCS 3VT Site A (97.3 vs. 96.6/96.6). The advantage is most pronounced under domain shift: on FCS 3VT Site B, ZR-DETR leads by 4.0% mAP over MI-DETR (48.8 vs. 44.8), and in the A→B UDA setting it beats M³-UDA by 3.0% (79.8 vs. 76.8 on 4C; 62.4 vs. 60.4 on 3VT) and DATR by 5.2% (79.8 vs. 74.6 on 4C). On the cross-modality MM-WHS benchmark, ZR-DETR-UDA achieves the best mAP in both CT→MRI (69.4 vs. DATR 68.8) and MRI→CT (77.9 vs. DATR 76.5) directions. On EPV, the margin over MI-DETR is 0.9% mAP (94.4 vs. 93.5), with ZR-DETR achieving the highest per-class scores in 6 of 9 anatomical structures. Traditional CNN-based Faster R-CNN trails by 5.9–10.3% mAP across datasets, confirming the benefit of the transformer + GP architecture.

## Relation to Prior Work

ZR-DETR extends the DETR→Deformable-DETR→Relation-DETR lineage by replacing spatial position relations with log-scale zoom-level relations, motivated by the observation that radiologists routinely adjust zoom in clinical imaging. It departs from Relation-DETR's deterministic head by introducing a Gaussian Process detection head that fuses appearance (RBF), scale (rational quadratic), and anatomical identity (Kronecker delta) kernels for uncertainty-aware predictions. The anatomical relation consistency loss, based on normalized Hausdorff distance between predicted boxes, is a novel constraint not present in prior DETR variants, drawing on graph-based morphological modeling but avoiding its sensitivity to noise.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap: scale variability induced by clinician-driven zoom adjustments is a real source of domain shift in medical imaging that prior multi-scale methods (FPN, DZIN) handle only implicitly. The GP-based uncertainty quantification is particularly relevant for clinical deployment where calibrated confidence matters, and the cross-domain results (79.8% mAP on FCS 4C A→B) demonstrate that zoom-aware priors can partially bridge site-specific imaging differences without target labels. Key open questions include whether fixed anatomical adjacency matrices generalize to atypical anatomy or rare pathologies, whether the GP head's modest ablation gain (+0.4–1.6%) justifies its O(N³) kernel computation at scale, and whether the uncertainty estimates remain reliable under the low-data, noisy conditions common in real ultrasound.
