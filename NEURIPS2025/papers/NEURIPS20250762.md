## Problem

Tubular structure analysis (e.g., vascular networks) is difficult because branch morphology varies significantly in both geometry and topology, yet standard discrete representations like voxels and point clouds incur high storage costs and lose fine-grained geometric and topological details. No prior work has constructed a topology-aware, group-equivariant network that operates directly on continuous tubular manifolds, leaving a gap between the mathematical expressiveness of spline-based representations and the inductive biases of equivariant deep learning.

## Input / Output

The pipeline accepts raw vascular centerline point clouds and surface point clouds as input, which are first fitted into compact BBSC representations (a small set of 3D control points, scalar radii, and a constrained knot vector per branch) via a DiffusionNet-based fitting module. The output is a multi-class CoW branch label (13 anatomical classes on TopCoW 2024, 22 classes on the clinical dataset). Unlike point cloud baselines that process thousands of points, the network operates on only n control parameters per branch, drastically reducing input dimensionality while preserving differentiable geometric structure.

## Method

The method defines a BBSC functional space M_p with a Sobolev H1-inspired metric that couples control points, radii, and knot vectors through tangent-space perturbations, then constructs an SE(3)-equivariant mapping φ: M_p → S by adapting the SE(3)-Transformer attention to operate on BBSC control points relative to their centroid (replacing pairwise point interactions with control-point-to-centroid interactions). Each tubular branch becomes a node in a graph whose edges encode topological connectivity, and a GCN propagates information across branches to capture inter-branch topology. The fitting stage uses DiffusionNet to embed surface points and predict BBSC parameters, with a Hausdorff-distance loss and a template-spline regularization term. The key architectural insight is that the equivariant attention kernel W^{c_o c_i}(P_i − P̄) preserves SE(3) symmetry while operating on the continuous manifold rather than a discrete point set, and the GCN layer maintains topological stability across the glued manifold structure GM.

## Results

On the TopCoW 2024 dataset (125 samples, 13 classes), SE(3)-BBSCformerGCN achieves 97.53% accuracy, 99.99% AUC-ROC, 98.44% recall, and 97.87% F1 score, with the lowest standard deviation among all methods on AUC-ROC (±0.01), recall (±0.31), and F1 (±0.41). On the larger clinical dataset (1,182 samples, 22 classes), the method achieves 96.11% accuracy and 99.38% AUC-ROC, outperforming the best baseline (PointMLP at 82.70% accuracy) by over 13 percentage points. The model also requires only 59.01M FLOPs and 2.70M parameters, compared to 9,743.76M FLOPs and 18.56M parameters for 3D DenseNet, while achieving faster inference (3.02 ms vs. 8.03 ms).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On TopCoW 2024, SE(3)-BBSCformerGCN achieves the best AUC-ROC (99.99%), recall (98.44%), and F1 (97.87%) while remaining competitive in accuracy (97.53%) with the strongest baseline RepSurf-U (97.41%). The method decisively outperforms all point cloud baselines: PointNet (94.10% acc), PointNet++ (96.61%), CurveNet (97.14%), and PointMLP (94.10%), with the largest gap over PointMLP (~3.4% accuracy). Notably, the original SE(3)-Transformer [30] performs very poorly (61.35% accuracy), suggesting that direct application of equivariant attention to raw point clouds is ill-suited for tubular structures, whereas the BBSC manifold formulation restores equivariant performance. On the clinical dataset, the gap widens dramatically: SE(3)-BBSCformerGCN reaches 96.11% accuracy versus 82.70% for the best baseline (PointMLP) and 78.22% for RepSurf-U, indicating strong generalization. The ablation rows confirm that both SE(3)-equivariance and GCN are necessary: removing GCN drops accuracy to 87.91%, and removing SE(3) equivariance (BBSCformerGCN) yields slightly higher mean accuracy (97.95%) but with larger variance (±1.22 vs. ±0.83), confirming that equivariance improves stability rather than raw peak accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SE(3)-Transformer [30] from its native point-cloud domain to a continuous Ball B-Spline Curve (BBSC) manifold, replacing pairwise point interactions with control-point-to-centroid interactions on a smooth spline. It departs from the broader tubular-structure literature (DSCNet, GraphMorph, TaG-Net, etc.) that relies on discrete voxel or point-cloud representations by introducing a compact, differentiable manifold parameterized by a small set of control points, radii, and knot vectors, and by formally defining a Sobolev H¹-type metric on the resulting functional space. The GCN component for topological connectivity follows standard graph neural network practice but is applied to a novel node representation (the equivariant BBSC feature) rather than to point-cloud or voxel-derived embeddings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The BBSC manifold representation offers a genuinely compact alternative to voxels and point clouds for tubular structures, reducing FLOPs by two orders of magnitude while preserving fine geometric detail, which is practically important for clinical deployment. The SE(3)-equivariant mapping provides a principled way to enforce rotation/translation stability without data augmentation, and the generalization story (maintaining ~96% accuracy on a 10× larger clinical dataset while baselines drop 10–20%) is compelling. A key open limitation acknowledged by the authors is that the GCN operations on the high-dimensional feature space are not themselves equivariant, leaving the full pipeline only partially equivariant; designing equivariant graph convolutions on the topological manifold space is identified as future work. The framework is stated to generalize to other tubular structures (airways, retinal vessels, traffic networks), but no experiments beyond CoW are provided.
