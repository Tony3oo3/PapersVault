## Problem

When the prior is unknown and replaced by the empirical distribution in Bayes' rule or any conditional generative model, the nonlinearity of the prior-to-posterior mapping introduces a bias that does not vanish even when averaging over training data. The paper asks whether this bias can be systematically reduced beyond the O(n^{-1/2}) or O(n^{-1}) rate of the naive plug-in approach, and whether a black-box procedure can achieve arbitrarily high-order convergence O(n^{-k}) for any prescribed k.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an observation y*, a known likelihood P_{Y|X}, n i.i.d. samples from an unknown prior, and a black-box conditional sampler f (e.g., a diffusion model or VAE) that maps a prior distribution to a posterior distribution. It outputs k posterior samples paired with (possibly negative) binomial weights w_i = binom(k, i)(-1)^{i-1}, such that the signed measure sum_i w_i P_{X^{(i)}} approximates the true posterior to order k. The output is a signed distribution rather than a proper probability measure, which can be converted to a valid sample via rejection sampling (Algorithm 2) in the discrete case.

## Method

The core mechanism is a jackknife-type debiasing built on iterated resampling. An operator B_n maps any distribution p to the expected posterior obtained by drawing n i.i.d. samples from p and applying f to the resulting empirical measure. The debiased estimator D_{n,k} = sum_{j=0}^{k-1} binom(k, j+1)(-1)^j B_n^j f(hat{pi}) is a linear combination of f applied to k recursively resampled empirical distributions, with alternating binomial weights. The key insight is that B_n approximates the identity operator, so the error operator E = I - B_n is 'small' (O(n^{-1})), and the binomial expansion of (I - B_n)^k yields an O(n^{-k}) approximation to f(pi). The procedure is fully black-box: f is never decomposed or parameterized, only queried on resampled empirical distributions, making it applicable to any conditional generative model including diffusion models and conditional VAEs.

## Results

The paper establishes that the proposed debiasing framework achieves a bias rate of O(n^{-k}) for any prescribed integer k, while maintaining a variance rate of O(n^{-1}), compared to the naive plug-in posterior which has bias O(n^{-1}) (or O(n^{-1/2}) in ∞-Rényi divergence). In the binary prior experiments (Figure 1), the debiased estimators D_{n,k} for k=1,2,3,4 exhibit log-log convergence slopes matching n^{-1}, n^{-2}, n^{-3}, and n^{-4} respectively. In the Gaussian mixture prior experiments (Figure 2), the observed bias decay for k=1 and k=2 aligns with the theoretical O(n^{-1}) and O(n^{-2}) rates, and the variance decays at O(n^{-1}) in both cases.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against external baselines; the sole comparison is between the naive plug-in posterior (k=1, which corresponds to the standard empirical Bayes approach) and the proposed debiased estimator at higher orders k. The plug-in method suffers from O(n^{-1}) bias due to the nonlinearity of Bayes' rule, while the debiased framework reduces this to O(n^{-k}) for any k without increasing the variance beyond O(n^{-1}). In the binary prior setting, the plug-in (k=1) error decays at n^{-1}, whereas k=2, 3, 4 achieve n^{-2}, n^{-3}, n^{-4} decay respectively, as confirmed by the log-log plots in Figure 1. The paper also notes an impossibility result: exact unbiasedness (zero bias for all priors) is unattainable with finite n, so the debiasing framework provides the best achievable rate rather than a perfect correction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the classical jackknife bias-reduction technique (Quenouille, 1956) and the polynomial approximation framework of Cai & Low (2011) and Wu & Yang (2020) to the problem of posterior approximation with an unknown prior. While Nowozin (2018) applied jackknife corrections to the IWAE evidence lower bound, this paper is the first to formalize a black-box debiasing operator D_{n,k} that treats an arbitrary conditional generative model (diffusion model, VAE, etc.) as a map f from prior to posterior and corrects its plug-in bias via iterated resampling. The connection to importance sampling with signed weights (Oates et al., Liu & Lee) provides an alternative interpretive lens, and the impossibility result for exact unbiasedness (Section 3.2) cleanly delimits what any finite-sample method can achieve.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a drop-in bias correction for any existing conditional generative model without retraining, which is practically attractive for diffusion-based inpainting, text-to-image generation, and offline RL. However, rigorous guarantees are established only for the Bayes-optimal sampler under continuous priors and for C^{2k}-smooth samplers under discrete priors; the extension to general black-box models (e.g., trained diffusion models) remains a conjecture supported by heuristic arguments. The curse-of-dimensionality condition kd ≪ log(n) limits applicability in high-dimensional settings, and the experiments validate only low-dimensional toy cases (binary and 1D Gaussian mixture). Open questions include whether the O(n^{-k}) rate holds for non-smooth or learned samplers, and how the framework interacts with the memorization-generalization tradeoff in practice.
