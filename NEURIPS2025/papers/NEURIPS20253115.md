## Problem

Existing face swapping methods are predominantly trained on static images and fail to address the unique challenges of video contexts, including temporal flickering, identity drift across frames, sensitivity to occlusions, and large pose variations. No prior framework specifically targets robust, temporally coherent video face swapping with a diffusion-based architecture.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253115/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single source face image and a target video clip (128 frames at 512×512) as primary inputs, augmented by a face region mask, a 3DMM reconstruction (with texture and identity coefficients zeroed to prevent leakage), and masked source images for background context. During training, static images are treated as single-frame videos to unify the pipeline, and the AIDT triplet structure (source, target, decoupling face) replaces the naive same-image assumption used by prior methods. Output is a temporally consistent swapped face video generated via 32-step DDIM sampling.

## Method

VividFace builds on a Stable Diffusion U-Net backbone extended with self-attention temporal layers (initialized from AnimateDiff) and a custom VidFaceVAE using (2+1)D convolutions that jointly encode images and video clips without temporal downsampling. A key architectural choice is the three-network face encoder (ArcFace-based identity net, DINO-based texture net, DINO-based attribute net) whose features are fused via cross-attention in a Mixer module, trained on the AIDT triplet dataset to enforce genuine identity-attribute disentanglement rather than superficial copy-paste. The framework is trained in three stages—VAE pretraining, image-only diffusion pretraining, then image-video hybrid training with temporal modules activated—alternating batches of static images and video clips to maximize diversity. 3DMM pose/expression coefficients serve as local conditioning for large pose variations, while a dynamic occlusion augmentation pipeline (hands, objects with motion trajectories) improves robustness under partial face occlusion.

## Results

On the proposed VidSwapBench (200 source images × 200 target videos, 128 frames each), VividFace achieves the best FVD₃₂ (1201.1) and FVD₁₂₈ (122.6), substantially outperforming the next-best GAN-based method SimSwap (1242.8 / 186.6) and all diffusion-based baselines. Identity retrieval reaches 78.3% Top-1 and 90.2% Top-5 accuracy, the highest among all compared methods. On the standard FFHQ image benchmark, VividFace attains the best FID (4.05) and ID retrieval (96.9% / 99.2%), while maintaining competitive pose (3.70) and expression (1.07) errors.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VividFace outperforms all seven baselines on FVD (both 32-frame and 128-frame) and identity retrieval on VidSwapBench, with the largest margins over diffusion-based methods: DiffFace (FVD₃₂ 2404.7, ID@1 1.5%) and DiffSwap (FVD₃₂ 1530.2, ID@1 14.5%) degrade severely when applied frame-by-frame to video, confirming that image-only diffusion swapping does not transfer to temporal settings. Among GAN-based methods, SimSwap is the closest competitor on pose error (5.12 vs. 5.43) but trails VividFace by 1.8 points on ID@1 and 64 FVD₁₂₈ points. REFace, the strongest diffusion-based baseline, still lags VividFace by 136 FVD₁₂₈ points and 6.4 ID@1 points. On FFHQ (image swapping), VividFace achieves the best FID (4.05 vs. REFace's 5.53) and ID@1 (96.9% vs. 95.4%), while SimSwap retains a slight edge in pose error (2.98 vs. 3.70), indicating that the 3DMM conditioning trades a small amount of pose precision for substantially better identity and temporal fidelity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the image-level diffusion-based face swapping line (DiffSwap, REFace, DiffFace) to the video domain by introducing temporal attention modules (inspired by AnimateDiff) and a custom (2+1)D VAE (VidFaceVAE) that jointly handles static and dynamic inputs. It departs from prior GAN-based video swapping methods (FSGAN, SimSwap, MegaFS) by leveraging the stability and fidelity of latent diffusion, and from 3D-based approaches (Face2Face, HifiFace) by avoiding explicit 3DMM rendering for the final output while still using 3DMM coefficients as conditioning. The AIDT triplet dataset is a conceptual departure from prior triplet-based work like ReliableSwap, shifting the focus from artifact reduction to explicit identity-attribute disentanglement during training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By introducing VidSwapBench and the AIDT dataset, the paper provides the community with the first dedicated video face swapping benchmark and a structured training resource for identity-attribute disentanglement, both of which were previously absent. The hybrid image-video training strategy is a practical recipe that addresses the data-diversity bottleneck of video-only training and could transfer to other video generation tasks. Key open questions remain around inference speed (32 DDIM steps per 8-frame clip at 512×512 limits real-time use), scalability to longer sequences and higher resolutions, and whether the 3DMM conditioning generalizes to extreme poses or multi-face scenes.
