## Problem

Fine-tuning quantized LLMs with standard LoRA adapters introduces a data-type mismatch (4-bit weights vs. 16-bit adapters) that undermines inference efficiency, and merging the 16-bit adapters back into quantized weights inevitably reintroduces quantization error. Moreover, the only existing lossless-merge method (QA-LoRA) can only adjust group-wise zero factors rather than directly modifying all quantized weights, severely limiting fine-tuning capacity. No prior method supports lossless merging of adaptation weights while adjusting every quantized weight within the quantization grid.

## Input / Output

The method takes GPTQ-quantized model weights at 2, 3, or 4-bit precision (group size 64 or 128) along with task-specific training data (Alpaca for performance recovery; GSM8K, Spider SQL, or ViGGO for task-specific tuning) and produces a fully merged model where the ternary adapter has been absorbed into the integer weight matrix and zero factor. The critical constraint is that the output model operates entirely at the original low-bit precision with no residual 16-bit components, enabling pure low-bit inference kernels (e.g., TritonV2QuantLinear) without the mixed-precision overhead that GPTQ+LoRA incurs.

## Method

LoTA-QAF replaces continuous LoRA adapters with trainable ternary adapters A_T, B_T ∈ {-1,0,1}, whose product forms an integer auxiliary matrix ΔW with elements in [-r, r]. A threshold ω (typically 0.75r) binarizes ΔW into a ternary matrix Ŵ that directly increments or decrements the quantized integer weights W_int, while a scalar offset μ derived from the residual is absorbed into the zero factor, ensuring the merged model remains exactly on the quantization grid. Training uses a novel t-SignSGD optimizer that applies sign-based unit updates only to adapter weights whose gradient magnitude exceeds a dynamically decaying percentile threshold (top 5% → 0.01%), with a fixed floor τ to filter noise. This design guarantees lossless merging because the ternary Ŵ values are integers that map one-to-one onto the quantization grid, so no additional quantization or truncation is needed at merge time, preserving both low-bit inference speed and the full learned effect of the adapter.

## Results

On the MMLU 5-shot benchmark (performance recovery), LoTA-QAF achieves up to 5.14% improvement over GPTQ+LoRA on Qwen 2.5 14B at 2-bit (47.80% vs. 42.66% average), and consistently outperforms both LoRA and QA-LoRA across all four model families at 2-bit and 3-bit quantization. For task-specific fine-tuning (GSM8K, SQL, ViGGO), 16-bit LoRA retains a lead due to higher representational capacity, but LoTA-QAF still surpasses QA-LoRA on all three tasks. After lossless merging, LoTA-QAF achieves 1.7×–2.0× inference speedup over LoRA (which requires 16-bit adapter computation at inference).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GPTQ (quantization-only baseline), LoTA-QAF recovers substantial performance at low bit-widths: e.g., on Qwen 2.5 14B 2-bit, MMLU average jumps from 31.72% (GPTQ) to 47.80% (LoTA-QAF), a 16.08-point gain. Against GPTQ+LoRA (16-bit adapters), LoTA-QAF wins on MMLU performance recovery at 2-bit across all models (e.g., 47.80% vs. 42.66% on Qwen 14B, 56.98% vs. 56.22% on Llama 70B), but loses on task-specific benchmarks where LoRA's 16-bit precision provides higher representational capacity (e.g., GSM8K: 36.25% vs. 37.23% on Qwen 14B 2-bit). Against QA-LoRA (lossless merge into zero factors only), LoTA-QAF consistently outperforms on both MMLU and task-specific tasks at 2-bit and 3-bit, because it directly adjusts all quantized weights within the grid rather than only modifying zero factors. The key trade-off is that LoTA-QAF and QA-LoRA enable pure low-bit inference after merging (1.7×–2.0× faster than LoRA), while LoRA retains a small accuracy edge on task-specific fine-tuning due to its 16-bit adapter precision.

## Relation to Prior Work

LoTA-QAF extends the quantization-aware fine-tuning (QAF) lineage initiated by QLoRA and refined by QA-LoRA. While QA-LoRA achieves lossless adapter merging by absorbing adapter effects into group-wise zero factors, it cannot directly modify quantized weights. LoTA-QAF departs from this by introducing ternary adapters whose product forms an auxiliary matrix that adjusts all quantized weights within the quantization grid, while still enabling lossless merging. The optimizer t-SignSGD draws on SignSGD's suitability for discrete parameter spaces, replacing the learning rate with a dynamic percentile-based threshold to selectively update ternary weights.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ability to losslessly merge adapters into quantized weights while adjusting all weights (not just zero factors) addresses a practical deployment bottleneck: after merging, inference runs purely at low-bit precision, yielding 1.7×–2.0× speedups over LoRA. However, the method still trails 16-bit LoRA on task-specific fine-tuning and incurs 14–25% higher training time due to the lack of native ternary dtype support in PyTorch. Open questions include whether t-SignSGD can be improved with momentum or adaptive schedules, whether the ternary range can be extended beyond {-1,0,1} for higher-bit quantization, and whether production-grade ternary kernels can close the training efficiency gap.
