## Problem

3DVG datasets such as ScanRefer contain a non-negligible fraction of toxic samples—logically paradoxical descriptions and referentially ambiguous annotations—that degrade training quality and inflate evaluation scores. Existing MLLM-based evaluators are limited to pairwise comparisons or shallow content scoring and lack the structured, scene-level multimodal reasoning needed to trace the nuanced origins of these annotation errors.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251935/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The system consumes a full 3D scene represented as a set of multi-view RGB images (both positive views containing the target object and misleading views containing same-category distractors), the natural language referring expression, and a group of alternative descriptions for the same object from the dataset. It outputs a discrete quality score on a 9-point Likert scale (scores 1–6 indicate toxic, 7–9 valid) accompanied by a structured rationale, enabling both binary toxic/valid classification and fine-grained alignment with human judgment measured via RMSE and MAE.

## Method

Refer-Judge implements a Jury-and-Judge Chain-of-Thought paradigm where four parallel jurors—Logic, Consistency, Distinguishability, and Ambiguity—each perform targeted, multi-step CoT reasoning over different modalities and perspectives (textual paradox detection, cross-description validation, existence-and-uniqueness verification, and distractor-view ambiguity probing). A key mechanism is the 'twin thought' concept, where the same existence-assessment prompt is reused on misleading views to detect ambiguity without additional prompt engineering. Magistrate Judges then apply Corroborative Refinement: they identify suspicious-assistant image pairs, horizontally stitch the aligned views to form richer visual prompts, and re-evaluate to correct errors caused by occlusion or incomplete observation—going beyond majority voting or self-consistency heuristics. A final District Judge aggregates all refined reasoning paths without revisiting raw scene inputs, mirroring judicial hierarchy and preserving interpretability. The entire pipeline operates zero-shot via in-context learning on off-the-shelf MLLMs (GPT-4o, Gemini-2.5 Pro, etc.) without task-specific fine-tuning or external perception modules.

## Results

On the ScanRefer-Justice benchmark, Refer-Judge with GPT-4o achieves 82.77% agreement and 84.33% F1 in toxic data identification, approaching human-level performance (84.87% agreement, 86.51% F1) while exceeding all other tested MLLMs (GPT-4.1-mini: 83.14% F1, Grok-3: 82.81%, Gemini-2.5 Pro: 78.53%, LLaMA-3.2-11B: 71.96%). When applied as a data purification filter on the ScanRefer training set, Refer-Judge consistently improves downstream 3DVG performance: ScanRefer gains +1.43% Overall Acc@0.5, 3DVLP gains +1.60%, and ConcreteNet gains +0.71%. On the purified validation subset, gains are even larger, reaching +3.10% (ScanRefer), +1.84% (3DVLP), and +1.21% (ConcreteNet) in Overall Acc@0.5.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Refer-Judge is evaluated as a data purification layer atop three 3DVG baselines (ScanRefer, 3DVLP, ConcreteNet), and consistently improves all of them: ScanRefer gains +1.77/+1.43 (Acc@0.25/Acc@0.5 Overall), 3DVLP gains +0.69/+1.60, and ConcreteNet gains +1.03/+0.71. The largest relative improvement is on ScanRefer (+1.43% Acc@0.5), suggesting weaker baselines benefit most from removing toxic annotations. On the purified validation subset, gains amplify to +3.10% (ScanRefer) and +1.84% (3DVLP) in Overall Acc@0.5, while models actually perform *worse* on the toxic subset (e.g., ScanRefer drops from 21.69 to 18.76 Acc@0.25), confirming toxic samples are inherently unpredictable. In the Judge System ablation, Corroborative Refinement outperforms CoT-SC [34] on RMSE (2.69 vs. 2.81) and MAE (1.71 vs. 1.91) despite a slightly lower agreement score, and beats GoT [5] on both agreement (82.77 vs. 81.55) and F1 (84.33 vs. 82.99). Among MLLM backbones, GPT-4o leads all others (84.33% F1), with GPT-4.1-mini close behind (83.14%) and LLaMA-3.2-11B trailing significantly (71.96%), indicating that strong multimodal reasoning is essential for reliable toxic data detection.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the LLM-as-a-Judge paradigm and Chain-of-Thought reasoning into a structured multi-agent deliberation framework specifically for scene-level 3DVG data quality assessment, a direction the authors identify as underexplored. It departs from prior judge approaches (JudgeLM, MLLM-as-a-Judge) that handle pairwise comparisons or isolated image-text pairs by introducing a hierarchical Jury-and-Judge architecture with four specialized jurors (Logic, Consistency, Distinguishability, Ambiguity) and a Corroborative Refinement mechanism that adaptively reorganizes visual evidence rather than relying on static consensus (majority voting, self-consistency, GoT). The 'twin thought' concept—reusing identical prompt structures on different input subsets to serve distinct evaluation purposes—represents a novel prompting strategy not seen in prior CoT or multi-agent work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that ~46% of samples in complex 3DVG scenes are toxic, and that filtering them consistently improves baseline models by 1–4% across multiple architectures, underscores a previously underappreciated data quality problem in 3D scene understanding. The counterintuitive observation that models score *higher* on toxic validation samples (due to training distribution alignment) raises important questions about the reliability of current 3DVG benchmarks and evaluation protocols. Key limitations include the high computational cost (~$10,000 in API calls for one dataset) and reliance on proprietary MLLMs, leaving open whether more efficient open-source models can match this performance at scale. Future work on reducing inference cost and extending the framework to larger, more diverse 3D datasets would be needed for broad adoption.
