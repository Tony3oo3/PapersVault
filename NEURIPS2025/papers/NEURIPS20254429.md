## Problem

Training robust MPNN-based learning-to-optimize models for LPs and QPs remains difficult in data-scarce settings, and existing graph augmentation strategies (node dropping, edge perturbation, feature masking) are not tailored to optimization problem structure and can even degrade performance. No principled, theoretically grounded augmentation framework exists that generates diverse yet optimality-preserving LP/QP instances without requiring a solver to recompute solutions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254429/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an LCQP instance (Q, A, b, c) with Q positive definite, represented as a constraint-variable bipartite graph where constraint nodes carry features from b, variable nodes carry features from c, and edges are weighted by A entries (constraint–variable) and Q entries (variable–variable). The 6-layer MPNN backbone with GraphNorm produces a pooled graph embedding that a 3-layer MLP readout maps to a scalar predicted objective value. Augmented instances are new valid LCQPs whose optimal solutions are recovered via O(n) linear-algebraic operations (e.g., diagonal rescaling, Sherman-Morrison pseudo-inverses) rather than a QP solver.

## Method

The core mechanism constructs block-diagonal affine transformations M = diag(M₁, M₂) applied to the KKT system so that the transformed system remains a valid KKT system for a new LCQP, with the new solution recovered as (Mᵀ)†[x*; λ*]. Concrete transformations include removing idle variables (x*=0), removing strictly inactive constraints, scaling variable or constraint coefficients via diagonal matrices, adding variables (using Sherman-Morrison for the pseudo-inverse), and adding constraints as non-negative convex combinations. Each transformation is classified as either efficiently recoverable (solution computable in O(n)) or solution-independent (no access to original solutions needed), enabling use in both supervised training (dynamic per-iteration augmentation with known labels) and self-supervised contrastive pretraining (NT-Xent loss over two solution-independent augmented views per instance, temperature τ=0.1). The pretrained MPNN backbone is then fine-tuned with an MLP head on the downstream objective-prediction task.

## Results

The paper evaluates mean relative objective error (lower is better) on synthetic LP and QP instances with 100 variables and 100 constraints. Under contrastive pretraining followed by supervised fine-tuning with only 10% labeled data, the proposed method achieves 3.47% error on LP and 3.79% on QP, reducing the objective gap by 59.4% and 54.1% respectively over training from scratch. In the supervised setting, combining all proposed augmentations (Combo) yields 2.30% error on LP and 1.53% on QP with 20% training data, a 62.6% improvement over no augmentation on LP. Pretrained models also transfer to OOD tasks (Set Cover, SVM, Portfolio, LASSO, etc.), consistently outperforming scratch-trained models across all data ratios.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method decisively outperforms all seven contrastive/generative SSL baselines across every data ratio and both LP and QP. GraphCL (node dropping, edge perturbation, feature masking) and GCC (random-walk subgraphs) are the closest competitors but still trail by 2.8–7.9 percentage points at 10% data; GCC in particular degrades severely on QP (11.34% vs. 3.79%). Diffusion-based methods IGSD and MVGRL perform worst, with errors of 18.1% and 20.4% on LP at 10% data—worse than no pretraining at all. Mutual-information methods InfoGraph and DGI also degrade performance relative to the no-pretraining baseline. The generative baseline GAE (edge-weight reconstruction) is the mildest underperformer but still lags the proposed method by ~5.6 points on LP at 10% data. In the supervised augmentation setting (Table 1), generic graph augmentations from You et al. (drop node, mask node, flip edge) are consistently worse than no augmentation, while every proposed optimization-aware augmentation (drop vars, drop cons, scale vars, scale cons, add vars, add cons) improves performance, with the combined strategy achieving the best results.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the MPNN-based learning-to-optimize (L2O) framework for LPs and QPs, particularly the constraint-variable bipartite graph representation extended to QPs by Chen et al. (2024a), and the broader L2O literature (Gasse et al. 2019, Khalil et al. 2022). It departs from generic graph data augmentation strategies (node dropping, edge perturbation, feature masking à la GraphCL) by deriving augmentations directly from affine transformations of the KKT system, ensuring optimality preservation by construction. The contrastive pretraining component follows the standard NT-Xent formulation but applies it to optimization-structure-preserving views rather than arbitrary graph perturbations, filling a gap the authors identify between graph SSL and L2O.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work addresses a practical bottleneck—data scarcity for L2O on QPs—by providing a model-free, theoretically grounded augmentation toolkit that works in both supervised and self-supervised regimes. The finding that generic graph augmentations can actively degrade performance on structured optimization problems, while KKT-aware augmentations yield up to ~60% relative error reduction under 10% data, suggests that domain-aware inductive biases matter more than augmentation diversity for structured tasks. Key limitations include evaluation solely on synthetic instances (no MIPLIB/QPLIB benchmarks), the convexity assumption (Q ≻ 0), and the fact that some transformations require access to the original solution, restricting their use in truly unsupervised settings. Open questions include extension to non-convex QPs, integer-constrained variants, and whether the full expressive power of the transformation framework (Eq. 7) can be exploited beyond the diagonal/structured subset studied here.
