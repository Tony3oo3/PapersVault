## Problem

Existing training-free test-time adaptation methods for vision-language models rely exclusively on entropy criteria to select visual features and update dynamic caches, ignoring generalizable factors such as shape-sensitive and style-insensitive components that are known to improve robustness under domain shift. This paper is the first to explore how such generalizable factors can be exploited in a training-free, cache-based test-time adaptation setting for CLIP-style models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255115/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single unlabeled test image, which is augmented into 63 views and further corrupted by two perturbation operations (patch-shuffle for shape, colour affine transformation with hue adjustment for style) before a shared forward pass through the pre-trained CLIP encoder. The output is a class label obtained by combining the raw zero-shot CLIP prediction with a cache-adapted prediction, where the cache stores up to 3 high-quality samples' visual features and pseudo-labels. No ground-truth labels or gradient updates are required at any point.

## Method

SSG introduces a Perturbed Prediction Difference (PPD) metric that quantifies shape-sensitive and style-insensitive factors by measuring the prediction gap between raw and perturbed images along the pseudo-label class dimension. PPD is then used in two ways: (1) it reweights the entropy-selected augmented visual features via softmax-normalized PPD values instead of simple averaging, producing a test-time feature that emphasizes generalizable factors; and (2) it augments the entropy-based cache update criterion so that samples with both low entropy and high PPD are preferentially stored. The entire procedure is training-free and plug-and-play, requiring only lightweight perturbation operations and PPD comparisons on top of the existing TDA-style positive cache, with no gradient descent or learnable parameters.

## Results

On the out-of-distribution benchmark (4 ImageNet variants), SSG with ViT-B/16 achieves 65.20% OOD average top-1 accuracy, outperforming the previous SOTA BoostAdapter (65.57%) on 3 of 4 datasets and surpassing DPE (64.43%) and TDA (63.89%). The enhanced SSG+ variant reaches 66.24% OOD average, a +0.67% gain over BoostAdapter. On the cross-domain benchmark (10 fine-grained datasets), SSG (ViT-B/16) achieves 70.24% average accuracy, beating BoostAdapter (68.68%) and DPE (69.40%). Inference time is ~15 min on ImageNet-A, comparable to TDA and 8× faster than TPT (2h36min).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SSG (ViT-B/16) outperforms all prompt-tuning baselines: TPT by +4.39% OOD average, DiffTPT by +4.68%, and PromptAlign by +1.65%, while requiring no gradient optimization. Against training-free methods, SSG beats TDA by +1.31%, DMN-ZS by +1.40%, and TPS by +1.44% on OOD average. The closest competitor is BoostAdapter (65.57%), which SSG narrowly trails on ImageNet-A (62.02 vs 64.53) but surpasses on ImageNet-R (+0.38%) and ImageNet-S (+0.85%); the SSG+ variant (combining SSG with BoostAdapter's regional bootstrapping) achieves 66.24%, a clear +0.67% win over BoostAdapter overall. On the cross-domain benchmark, SSG (ViT-B/16) leads all baselines with 70.24% average, beating DPE by +0.84% and BoostAdapter by +1.56%, with the largest gains on DTD (+10.46% over BoostAdapter) and Aircraft (+2.76%). SSG also generalizes to the larger OpenCLIP ViT-L/14 backbone, achieving 72.26% OOD average vs. TDA's 70.69% and DPE's 71.86%.

## Relation to Prior Work

SSG extends the training-free test-time adaptation line for vision-language models (TDA, BoostAdapter) by introducing generalizable factors—shape-sensitive and style-insensitive—into the feature selection and cache update pipeline, where prior methods relied exclusively on entropy criteria. The conceptual foundation draws from transfer learning literature on disentangled factors (Geirhos et al. on shape bias, MixStyle on style invariance, DeYO on factor-based analysis), but SSG is the first to operationalize these factors in a training-free, cache-based TTA setting for CLIP. Methodologically, it departs from TDA's entropy-only cache criterion by adding a Perturbed Prediction Difference (PPD) signal computed via lightweight patch-shuffle and color-affine perturbations, and from TPT/DiffTPT by avoiding any gradient-based prompt optimization entirely.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SSG offers a plug-and-play, gradient-free mechanism to improve zero-shot VLM classification under distribution shift, with inference time comparable to TDA (~15 min) and memory under 4 GB, making it practical for deployment-constrained settings. The ablation showing that shape-sensitive factors contribute more than style-insensitive ones, yet both are needed for best performance, provides useful guidance for future factor-based adaptation designs. Key limitations include the reliance on simple perturbation operations (patch shuffle, color shift) that may be insufficient for texture-dependent tasks such as medical imaging, and the modest ~1–2% gain over the strongest baseline (BoostAdapter) suggests diminishing returns from this particular factor-injection strategy. Open questions include whether more sophisticated perturbations (Fourier-based, channel-based) or learned factor decompositions could yield larger gains, and how the approach generalizes to tasks where style is the primary discriminative signal.
