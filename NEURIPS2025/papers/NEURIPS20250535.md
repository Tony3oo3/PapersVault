## Problem

Tabular foundation models pretrained on synthetic data generalize remarkably well, yet the design principles for choosing and combining synthetic data priors remain heuristic and poorly understood. This paper asks: what properties make a synthetic prior effective for TFM pretraining, and how should one construct a mixture of priors to maximize generalization on real-world tabular data?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250535/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the model receives a support set of labeled tabular rows (features plus target labels) and a query set of unlabeled tabular rows (features only), and predicts query labels in a single forward pass without parameter updates. Features can be continuous or categorical, and the model handles both classification (outputting class probabilities) and regression (outputting target values). The 2D-attention MITRA variant is pretrained on at most 16 features, yet generalizes to downstream datasets with up to 500 features.

## Method

MITRA introduces a principled framework for selecting synthetic data priors based on three quantified criteria: (1) standalone performance on real datasets (Performance Vector P), (2) diversity, measured by how hard it is for a model to overfit its own prior (diagonal of a Generalizability Matrix G), and (3) distinctiveness, measured by how poorly models trained on other priors predict data from this prior (off-diagonals of G). Using these criteria, the authors construct a mixture of structural causal models (SCM) and tree-based priors (gradient boosting, random forest, decision tree, extra tree, and a directly-sampled random forest), where SCMs provide high real-data performance and diversity, while tree-based priors add distinctiveness. The mixture is model-agnostic: it is used to pretrain both 1D row-wise attention and 2D element-wise attention transformer architectures via in-context learning, where each training step samples a synthetic dataset from the prior mixture, partitions it into support and query sets, and optimizes the likelihood over masked query labels.

## Results

On the merged classification benchmark (137 datasets from TabRepo, TabZilla, and AMLB), MITRA with ensembling and fine-tuning (+ef) achieves an Elo of 1136, AUC of 0.905, and accuracy of 0.858, outperforming all baselines. In ICL-only mode, MITRA matches TabPFNv2 (Elo 1015 vs. 1043) despite being pretrained on at most 16 features (one-tenth of TabPFNv2's 160). On the TabRepo regression benchmark, MITRA(+ef) achieves Elo 1140 and R² of 0.636, surpassing TabPFNv2(+e) (Elo 1090, R² 0.615). MITRA also demonstrates superior sample efficiency, maintaining a consistent Elo advantage over TabPFNv2 and TabICL at all down-sampling ratios from 10% to 100% of in-context examples.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MITRA(+ef) achieves the best overall Elo of 1136, edging out Attic(+ef) (1128) and TabPFNv2(+e) (1107) on the merged classification benchmark. In ICL-only mode, MITRA (Elo 1015) ties TabICL (1015) but trails TabPFNv2 (1043) by 28 Elo points; however, MITRA's ICL performance is achieved with a maximum of 16 pretraining features versus TabPFNv2's 160, highlighting the strength of its prior mixture. Against classical baselines, MITRA substantially outperforms CatBoost (988), XGBoost (926), LightGBM (917), and Random Forest (903) in Elo, with gaps of 27–133 points. On the regression benchmark, MITRA(+ef) leads TabPFNv2(+e) by 50 Elo points (1140 vs. 1090) and RealMLP by 96 points (1140 vs. 1044). In the sample-efficiency comparison, MITRA maintains a consistent 10–30 Elo advantage over TabPFNv2 and 40–70 Elo advantage over TabICL at every down-sampling ratio (10%–100%), confirming that the diverse prior mixture improves generalization from limited in-context examples.

## Relation to Prior Work

MITRA extends the tabular foundation model (TFM) lineage initiated by TabPFN (ICLR 2023), which established the in-context learning paradigm using structural causal model (SCM) priors, and the subsequent architectural advances in element-wise 2D attention from TabPFNv2 (Nature 2025) and Attic. Prior TFM works (TabForestPFN, TabICL, Attic) heuristically mixed tree-based priors with SCMs without explaining why such mixtures help. MITRA departs from this by providing the first principled, three-factor analysis (performance, diversity, distinctiveness) of what makes a synthetic prior effective, and uses this framework to construct an optimal prior mixture that is model-agnostic across both 1D row-wise and 2D element-wise attention architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The three-factor prior selection framework gives the TFM community a concrete, reproducible methodology for designing synthetic pretraining data rather than relying on ad-hoc choices, potentially accelerating future TFM development. The demonstrated model-agnosticism and improved sample efficiency (consistent gains at 10%–75% of in-context examples) have direct practical value for settings with limited labeled data. Open questions include whether the framework generalizes beyond tabular data, whether the pretraining feature limit (16 features) constrains downstream applicability to high-dimensional tables, and whether additional prior families beyond SCMs and tree-based models could further improve coverage.
