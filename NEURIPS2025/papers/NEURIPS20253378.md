## Problem

Learning disentangled visual representations from heterogeneous, non-IID datasets in federated settings remains unsolved: centralized training of object-centric models entangles cross-domain concepts and inflates computation, while per-client training leaves feature spaces incompatible. No prior work has combined self-supervised object-centric learning (Slot Attention) with federated optimization across large-scale multi-domain data, leaving a gap in how to learn a universal, compact representation that generalizes across surgical and natural domains without sharing raw data.

## Input / Output

The method consumes pre-extracted frozen features from four ViT-B/16 foundation models (DINO, SAM, MAE, CLIP) stacked into a single high-dimensional feature map per image, rather than raw pixels. It produces K object-centric slot vectors, corresponding slot attention masks for scene decomposition, and a compact adapted feature map of dimension d ≪ C_tot. The student decoder outputs a full-resolution reconstruction of the stacked foundation features, while the teacher decoder reconstructs only the low-dimensional adapted features, making the two branches asymmetric in output dimensionality.

## Method

FORLA couples a shared lightweight feature adapter (MLP, MoE, or AFM) that compresses stacked foundation features into a compact latent space with a shared Slot Attention module that extracts object-centric slots, all trained under a two-branch student-teacher objective where the student reconstructs raw foundation features and the teacher reconstructs adapted features. Federated training proceeds in two stages: first, FedAvg aggregates the student's adapter and slot encoder across clients to build a global representation; second, the teacher branch optimizes its own adapter and slot attention locally, then transfers knowledge to the student via a Local FedAvg weight-averaging step that sidesteps the permutation-matching problem inherent to Slot Attention's invariance. Progressive distillation stabilizes early training by blocking teacher gradients to the adapter and updating it via EMA from the student before enabling full two-branch optimization. This design lets FL exploit distributed domain-specific structure while the shared adapter and slot attention learn a universal, domain-invariant object representation without any supervision or data sharing.

## Results

On the stratified surgical evaluation (Table 1), FORLA with the AFM adapter achieves 80.3 CorLoc on Abdominal and 75.42 CorLoc on Thoracic, outperforming centralized training (77.97 and 65.58) and individual training (70.73 and 56.62). On natural datasets, FORLA+AFM reaches 66.87 CorLoc on YouTube-Objects and 64.84 on PASCAL, again surpassing both centralized and individual baselines. In the more challenging mixed-domain setting (Table 2), FORLA+AFM achieves an average CorLoc of 63.02 and mBO of 43.81 across all seven datasets, compared to 58.81/41.45 for Centralized+AFM and 56.82/40.33 for Individual+AFM. The paper also reports an 85.2% reduction in client-server communication versus standard fine-tuning of foundation models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FORLA consistently outperforms individual (per-dataset) training across all seven datasets, with the largest gains on Thoracic (+18.8 CorLoc) and PASCAL (+13.7 CorLoc) using the AFM adapter. Against centralized mixed training, FORLA wins on every dataset in the stratified setting; the advantage is most pronounced on Thoracic (+9.84 CorLoc) and PASCAL (+10.44 CorLoc), while on YouTube-Objects centralized training actually underperforms individual training (52.13 vs. 65.11 CorLoc), a phenomenon the authors attribute to domain dilution in unsupervised slot attention. Compared to single foundation model baselines (DINO, SAM, CLIP, MAE used as frozen feature extractors without adaptation), FORLA's adapter + slot attention pipeline yields substantial improvements—e.g., SAM alone achieves 64.30 CorLoc on Abdominal versus 80.30 for FORLA+AFM. In the zero-shot segmentation comparison (Table 7), SAM ViT-H surpasses FORLA on PASCAL (79.12 vs. 45.04 CorLoc) but fails on the proprietary Thoracic domain (23.73 vs. 75.42 CorLoc), likely because thoracic data was absent from SAM's training. FORLA's inference time is also dramatically lower (3.75 ms vs. 1.1 s per image for SAM ViT-H).

## Relation to Prior Work

FORLA sits at the intersection of three research threads: Slot Attention for object-centric scene decomposition (Locatello et al., 2020), DINOSAUR's approach of applying slot attention to frozen foundation-model features (Seitzer et al., 2023), and DINO's EMA-based teacher-student self-distillation (Caron et al., 2021). The key departure is the federated training dimension: prior object-centric work assumes centralized training on pooled data, while prior federated learning work targets classification or segmentation rather than disentangled representation learning. FORLA introduces a two-branch architecture where the teacher-student knowledge transfer is implemented via local FedAvg (treating the two branches as local clients) rather than a conventional KD loss, specifically to sidestep slot attention's permutation invariance. The feature adapter layer (MLP, MoE, or AFM) that harmonizes stacked features from multiple frozen foundation models (DINO, SAM, MAE, CLIP) into a compact latent space is also a novel component not present in prior slot attention or federated learning work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that federated object-centric learning consistently outperforms both individualized and centralized training on heterogeneous multi-domain data, which is counterintuitive since centralized training typically benefits from more data. This has direct practical implications for medical imaging (surgical video) and other privacy-sensitive domains where data cannot be pooled. Key limitations include the assumption of a fixed optimal decomposition hierarchy, reliance on foundation models for initial feature quality, and degraded performance under extreme data partitioning. Open questions include extending to dynamic slot counts, video data, weak supervision signals, and whether the federated advantage persists with stronger foundation models like DINOv3.
