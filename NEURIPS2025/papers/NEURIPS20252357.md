## Problem

Markov Persuasion Processes (MPPs) model sequential information disclosure where a sender faces a stream of myopic receivers in a Markovian environment, but prior work (Wu et al., 2022) assumed the sender knows receivers' rewards, making the model inoperable in practice. This paper addresses the fully unknown setting where the sender has no knowledge of transitions, priors, or either agent's rewards, and shows that per-episode persuasiveness is unattainable, necessitating a new objective that bounds cumulative persuasiveness violation alongside regret.

## Input / Output

The algorithm receives the MPP structure (finite sets of states X, actions A, outcomes Ω), observes state-outcome pairs and reward feedback at each step (full feedback reveals all action rewards for visited state-outcome pairs; partial feedback reveals only the played action's reward), and is parameterized by a confidence level δ and an exploration fraction α ∈ [1/2, 1]. It outputs a signaling policy φ_t at each episode (a distribution over action recommendations for each state-outcome pair) along with theoretical guarantees: Õ(√T) regret and violation under full feedback, and Õ(T^α) regret with Õ(T^{1−α/2}) violation under partial feedback, both matching tight lower bounds.

## Method

The core algorithm, Optimistic Persuasive Policy Search (OPPS), formulates the per-episode decision as a linear program over occupancy measures, where the objective uses upper confidence bounds on sender's rewards and the incentive-compatibility constraints are relaxed optimistically using upper/lower confidence bounds on receivers' rewards. Under full feedback, OPPS simply solves this optimistic LP at each episode, achieving Õ(√T) regret and violation. Under partial feedback, the algorithm splits into two phases: an initial exploration phase of N|X||Ω||A| episodes (where N = ⌈T^α⌉) that systematically visits each state-outcome-action triplet while still respecting optimistic persuasiveness constraints, followed by an exploitation phase identical to the full-feedback case. The key insight is that the exploration phase does not merely maximize visit probability but simultaneously optimistically accounts for persuasiveness, which reduces cumulative violation during exploration and achieves the optimal regret-violation trade-off for all α ∈ [1/2, 1], closing a gap left by Bernasconi et al. (2022) who only achieved optimality for α ∈ [1/2, 2/3].

## Results

For the full feedback setting, OPPS achieves Õ(√T) regret and Õ(√T) cumulative violation, with matching lower bounds (Theorem 3). For the partial feedback setting, OPPS achieves Õ(T^α) regret and Õ(T^(1−α/2)) violation for any α ∈ [1/2, 1], where α controls the exploration-exploitation trade-off (Theorems 4–5). A lower bound (Theorem 6) proves this trade-off is tight for every α ∈ [1/2, 1], and also establishes that being persuasive at every episode with high probability is impossible when the sender has no knowledge of receiver rewards.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines; comparisons are against prior theoretical results. Compared to Wu et al. [2022], who assume the sender knows all receiver rewards and obtain Õ(√T/D) regret (where D can be exponentially large), this paper removes the knowledge-of-receiver-rewards assumption entirely, at the cost of replacing per-episode persuasiveness with sublinear cumulative violation. Compared to Bernasconi et al. [2022], who study a related sequential persuasion setting with a tree structure and achieve an optimal regret–violation trade-off only for α ∈ [1/2, 2/3], this paper closes the gap by achieving trade-off optimality for the full range α ∈ [1/2, 1] via a clever exploration method that optimistically accounts for persuasiveness constraints during the exploration phase. The lower bound (Theorem 6) further shows that no algorithm can simultaneously achieve o(T^α) regret and o(T^(1−α/2)) violation, confirming the OPPS guarantees are information-theoretically tight.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Markov Persuasion Process (MPP) framework introduced by Wu et al. [2022], which modeled sequential Bayesian persuasion in an MDP but assumed the sender knows receivers' rewards. By removing this assumption entirely—so the sender knows nothing about transitions, priors, or either agent's rewards—the authors must replace the 'persuasive at every episode' guarantee with a sublinear cumulative-violation objective, drawing on techniques from online constrained MDPs (Rosenberg and Mansour [2019] for occupancy measures, Efroni et al. [2020] for optimistic constrained learning). The optimistic linear-programming formulation (Opt-Opt) and the two-phase exploration-exploitation structure for partial feedback are novel algorithmic contributions that bridge the Bayesian persuasion and online MDP literatures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that the regret-violation trade-off R_T = Õ(T^α) and V_T = Õ(T^{1-α/2}) is tight for all α ∈ [1/2,1], closing a gap left by Bernasconi et al. [2022] who achieved optimality only for α ∈ [1/2,2/3]. The impossibility result (Theorem 6) that no algorithm can be persuasive at every episode with high probability when receiver rewards are unknown is a clean negative result that reshapes how the problem must be formulated. Open questions include whether the loop-free episodic structure can be relaxed to general MDPs, whether the approach extends to farsighted receivers or multiple simultaneous receivers, and whether the LP-based per-round optimization scales to larger state spaces in practice.
