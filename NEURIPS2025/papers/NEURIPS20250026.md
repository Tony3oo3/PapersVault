## Problem

Most existing image tokenizers encode all images into a fixed number of tokens, ignoring the wide variability in image complexity. This wastes compute on simple images and loses critical detail on complex ones (e.g., text, faces). Prior adaptive methods require the input image to determine token count, making them incompatible with text-to-image generation where only a prompt is available.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250026/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

CAT takes a 512×512 RGB image and a text description (caption plus perception queries like 'are there faces or text?') as input. The LLM evaluator assigns one of three compression ratios (8×, 16×, or 32×) from the text alone, and the nested VAE produces a variable-length latent tensor of shape (c, r/f, r/f) where f is the assigned ratio. At inference for generation, only the class label or user prompt is needed to determine token count, since no image is available.

## Method

CAT combines two components: (1) a caption-based complexity evaluator that prompts an LLM to score image complexity 1–10 using a rubric covering semantic, visual, and perceptual factors, then thresholds the score into one of three compression ratios; and (2) a nested VAE architecture that routes intermediate encoder features from different downsampling depths through channel-matching ResNet blocks into a shared attention-based middle block, enabling a single model to produce latents at multiple spatial scales in one forward pass. During training, each image is processed only through the layers corresponding to its LLM-assigned ratio, providing complexity-aware supervision. The training objective jointly minimizes L1 reconstruction, KL divergence, LPIPS, MoCo v2 feature loss, and an adversarial GAN loss with a patch-based discriminator. The nested design is inspired by U-Net skip connections and Matryoshka networks, requiring no additional architectural or computational overhead beyond the standard VAE.

## Results

On reconstruction, CAT (avg 16x compression) achieves rFID of 0.65 on COCO, 0.46 on ImageNet, 1.97 on CelebA, and 5.27 on ChartQA, outperforming the fixed 16x baseline (0.66, 0.38, 2.25, 8.67) on three of four datasets while using 18% fewer tokens on ImageNet (avg 17.46x). On ImageNet-512 generation with DiT-XL, DiT+CAT achieves FID 4.56, sFID 10.55, and IS 191.09 at 0.82× the evaluation FLOPs of fixed-token baselines, beating the fixed 16x DiT (FID 4.78) and DQ-Transformer (FID 5.11). For classification, CAT attains the best linear-probing top-1 accuracy across all five diverse datasets (e.g., 71.28% on GTSRB, 66.39% on SVHN).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CAT outperforms all compared tokenizers on ImageNet-512 reconstruction (Table 2): rFID 0.46 vs. LDM 0.53, TexTok 0.73, LFQ 1.22, TiTok 1.52, and MaskGIT-VQGAN 1.97. In the fully controlled comparison (Table 3, same architecture and training data), CAT beats the fixed 16x baseline by 12% rFID on CelebA (1.97 vs. 2.25) and 39% on ChartQA (5.27 vs. 8.67), while matching or slightly exceeding it on COCO (0.65 vs. 0.66) at the cost of a small ImageNet rFID increase (0.46 vs. 0.38) offset by 18% fewer tokens. The JPEG-based adaptive baseline, which assigns 32x compression to nearly all CelebA and ChartQA images, is dramatically worse (rFID 6.57 and 10.17), confirming that pixel-level metrics misjudge perceptual complexity. For generation (Table 7), DiT+CAT (FID 4.56) beats DiT+Fixed 16x (4.78), DiT+LDM VAE (10.03), and DQ-Transformer (5.11) under the same FLOPs budget, with the advantage attributed to 23% fewer average training tokens enabling more images per step.

## Relation to Prior Work

CAT extends the standard VAE architecture (Kingma & Welling, 2014; Rombach et al., 2021) with a nested design inspired by U-Net skip connections and Matryoshka networks, enabling multiple compression ratios in a single model. It departs from prior adaptive tokenizers (ElasticTok, ALIT, DQ-VAE) that require the input image to determine token count, instead using LLM-based complexity scoring from text captions—a critical distinction for text-to-image generation where no image is available. The paper also contrasts with fixed-ratio tokenizers and traditional codecs (JPEG), showing that pixel-level metrics poorly capture perceptual complexity, motivating the shift to language-guided evaluation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CAT is the first work to combine language-guided tokenization with content-adaptive representation, offering a practical path to reducing compute in large-scale generative pipelines (23% throughput gain on ImageNet) while improving fidelity on detail-rich content (39% rFID improvement on ChartQA). Key limitations include the constraint to predefined factor-of-2 compression ratios (8x/16x/32x), the dependency on an LLM call for complexity scoring, and the absence of local (region-level) token allocation. Open questions include whether transformer backbones can enable arbitrary compression ratios, whether LLMs can automatically identify perception-critical elements without predefined queries, and how the reconstruction-generation trade-off in latent channel dimension interacts with adaptive compression.
