## Problem

Video Diffusion Transformers (DiTs) suffer from prohibitive inference costs driven by iterative denoising and quadratic spatio-temporal attention, with attention alone consuming over 70% of computation. While FP8 quantization and structured sparsity each independently accelerate inference, naively combining them in a training-free manner causes severe quality degradation (21% VBench score drop) because sparsity amplifies quantization errors in high-magnitude attention scores. No prior work jointly optimizes both techniques in a training-aware, hardware-aligned manner for video generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254633/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt as input and produces a 5-second video at 480p or 720p resolution (16 fps) using the Wan2.1 DiT backbone (1.3B or 14B parameters). Internally, the attention mechanism operates on L = T×H×W spatiotemporal tokens, where the 3D tile partitioning (e.g., 6×8×8 for hardware alignment) governs both quantization granularity and sparse window extent. The output video maintains near-lossless quality relative to the BF16 baseline while achieving 4.96× end-to-end speedup at 720p on NVIDIA H20 GPUs.

## Method

FPSAttention introduces a training-aware co-design that unifies FP8 quantization and Sliding Tile Attention (STA) sparsity at a shared 3D tile-wise granularity, so that per-tile FP8 scaling factors for Q and K, channel-wise FP8 for V, and tile-constrained sparse windows all operate at matching spatial-temporal extents aligned with GPU Tensor Core block sizes. A denoising step-aware schedule (Eq. 6) dynamically switches between coarse/sparse, fine/dense, and intermediate/medium configurations across early, mid, and late denoising steps, exploiting the observation that intermediate steps are most sensitive to approximation error. The model is trained with quantization-aware training (QAT) using FlexAttention's score_mod and mask_mod interfaces, with fused Triton kernels compiled for Hopper architecture, enabling the model to learn to compensate for joint quantization-sparsity errors and close the training-inference gap. This yields a 7.09× kernel speedup and 4.96× end-to-end speedup over BF16 without sacrificing VBench quality metrics.

## Results

On Wan2.1-14B at 720p, FPSAttention achieves a 7.09× kernel speedup for attention and a 4.96× end-to-end speedup over the BF16 baseline (1301s → 423s latency), while simultaneously attaining the best generation quality: PSNR 25.74, SSIM 0.832, LPIPS 0.076, and VBench Image Quality 0.710. On the smaller Wan2.1-1.3B at 480p, the method delivers a 2.45× E2E speedup (271s → 110s) with PSNR 21.35 and LPIPS 0.154. Notably, FPSAttention outperforms the unmodified baseline on VBench total score (0.816 vs 0.802 for 1.3B), suggesting the structured sparsity inductive bias can slightly improve generation quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FPSAttention dominates all four baselines on both quality and speed. On Wan2.1-14B at 720p, it achieves 4.96× E2E speedup versus STA (3.60×), SparseVideoGen (3.13×), SpargeAttn (2.12×), and SageAttention (1.94×), while also posting the best PSNR (25.74 vs. 24.34 for SageAttention, 22.66 for STA) and the lowest LPIPS (0.076 vs. 0.156–0.217 for all baselines). The only metric where FPSAttention trails is Subject Consistency (0.9435 vs. 0.9530 for STA and SageAttention), a marginal gap of ~1%. On the 1.3B model, FPSAttention similarly leads in PSNR (21.35), SSIM (0.808), LPIPS (0.154), and Image Quality (0.710) over all baselines, with a 2.45× speedup versus 1.78–1.91× for the others. An ablation (Table 5) further shows that naively combining FP8 quantization and sparsity without joint training causes a 21.1% VBench total-score drop, whereas FPSAttention's training-aware co-design actually improves the total score by +1.8% over the unmodified baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established acceleration lines for video diffusion: FP8/INT8 quantization (SageAttention, SageAttention2) and structured sparse attention (Sliding Tile Attention, SparseVideoGen, SpargeAttn). Prior work applies these techniques independently and in a training-free manner, and the paper explicitly demonstrates that naively combining them causes severe 21% quality degradation due to error amplification between quantization noise and sparse token selection. The key departure is a training-aware co-design that unifies both under a single 3D tile-wise granularity and a denoising step-aware schedule, leveraging FlexAttention and Triton kernels on Hopper hardware to translate algorithmic savings into wall-clock speedups.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

A 4.96× end-to-end speedup for 720p video generation is a practically transformative efficiency gain that could significantly reduce the cost and latency of deploying large video diffusion models. The finding that joint QAT with structured sparsity can actually *improve* VBench scores over the full-precision baseline (attributed to locality inductive bias) challenges the assumption that compression must trade off quality. Key limitations include dependence on FP8-capable Hopper hardware, a substantial training cost (64 nodes × 8 H20 GPUs × 7 days), and validation only on the Wan2.1 architecture; open questions include generalization to MMDiT-based models (HunyuanVideo, CogVideoX), reducing training overhead, and extending the co-design principles beyond attention to feed-forward and cross-attention components.
