## Problem

Multi-neuronal recordings in mouse visual cortex reveal high-dimensional population activity with heavy-tailed covariance eigenspectra, seemingly at odds with the hypothesis that cortical computation occurs in low-dimensional latent dynamics. The paper asks whether these two views are compatible: can a low-dimensional dynamical system in the space of pre-activations generate post-activation activity that is high-dimensional in the linear sense?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253949/cropped_page2_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The NCE method takes deconvolved calcium imaging traces from ~19,000 neurons in mouse visual cortex, randomly split into source (500) and target (1000) sets, with activity paired across different repeats of the same stimulus to discard non-stimulus variability. It outputs a low-dimensional latent vector z_t (the pre-activation dimension), a predicted target activity via a single-layer power-ReLU decoder, and a dimension estimate d sufficient to capture 95% of explainable variance. The theoretical component takes a rank-d weight matrix, a rectified-power activation function φ_{p,c}, and latent dimension d, and predicts the post-activation eigenspectrum decay exponent α = 1 + (2p+1)/(d−1).

## Method

The paper contributes three linked components. First, a solvable RNN with rank-2 cosine connectivity on a ring and Heaviside step activation, whose N-dimensional dynamics reduce exactly to a 2D latent system (Eq. 5) producing a stable limit cycle, while post-activations yield a power-law eigenspectrum with α=2 via the 0-th arc-cosine kernel. Second, a spectral theory (Conjecture 1) relating the eigenspectrum decay exponent to both the pre-activation dimension d and the activation power p, showing that high-dimensional activity is consistent with either low- or high-dimensional latents. Third, the Neural Cross-Encoder (NCE), a nonlinear generalization of Reduced Rank Regression that uses a 3-layer encoder with a bottleneck to estimate latents from source neurons and a single-layer power-ReLU decoder to predict target neurons; the constrained single-layer decoder is essential for interpreting latents as linear factors of pre-activations rather than abstract intrinsic-dimension variables. NCE is trained with SGD on cross-paired source-target activity, and when all nonlinearities are removed it reduces exactly to Reduced Rank Regression.

## Results

The solvable RNN model with 2-dimensional latent dynamics generates a post-activation covariance eigenspectrum with a power-law decay exponent α=2 (Eq. 8). Conjecture 1 predicts the general relationship α = 1 + (2p+1)/(d−1) between the eigenspectrum decay, activation power p, and pre-activation dimension d, confirmed in simulations. On mouse visual cortex recordings, NCE estimates the pre-activation dimension needed to explain 95% of explainable variance as 5.5±1.2 for drifting gratings, 7.0±1.0 for spontaneous activity, and 93.9±6.0 for natural images, demonstrating that high-dimensional activity can arise from low-dimensional latents in some conditions but not others.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is Reduced Rank Regression (RRR), the linear special case of NCE (all nonlinearities removed). For drifting grating responses, NCE requires substantially fewer pre-activation dimensions (5.5±1.2) than RRR (13.9±3.0) to explain 95% of explainable variance, confirming that the nonlinear readout captures structure invisible to linear models. For spontaneous activity, NCE and RRR give nearly identical dimension estimates (7.0±1.0 vs. 7.5±2.0), though RRR's performance degrades at higher dimensions due to overfitting while NCE remains stable. For natural image responses, NCE requires 93.9±6.0 dimensions versus RRR's 48.0±13.1; however, the paper notes this does not mean NCE 'loses'—rather, RRR explains a smaller fraction of total variance, so its 95% threshold is lower and it underestimates the true dimensionality. The key finding is that NCE's nonlinear decoder reveals low-dimensional pre-activations for gratings and spontaneous activity (where RRR also finds low dimensions but with less accuracy), while both methods agree that natural image responses are genuinely high-dimensional in pre-activation space.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) low-rank RNNs whose large-network dynamics reduce to low-dimensional latent systems (Mastrogiuseppe & Ostojic 2018, Beiran et al. 2021, Pezon et al. 2024), (2) the empirical observation that mouse visual cortex population activity has a heavy-tailed covariance eigenspectrum (Stringer et al. 2019, 2020), and (3) the spectral theory of random feature kernels (Cho & Saul 2009, Bach 2017, Bietti & Bach 2021). The key conceptual departure is the explicit separation of pre-activation and post-activation linear dimensions, which prior work on neural manifolds and intrinsic dimensionality did not distinguish. The Neural Cross-Encoder (NCE) extends Reduced Rank Regression (Izenman 1975) with a nonlinear single-layer readout, and its cross-encoding design borrows from the stimulus-pairing strategy of Stringer et al. (2019) to discard non-stimulus variability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper resolves a genuine tension in the field by showing that low-dimensional latent dynamics and high-dimensional activity are not contradictory but two views of the same system, separated by a nonlinear readout. The Conjecture 1 formula linking eigenspectrum decay exponent to pre-activation dimension and activation power provides a quantitative diagnostic, though it remains unproven. The empirical finding that natural image responses require ~94 pre-activation dimensions while gratings (~5.5) and spontaneous activity (~7) do not suggests that visual cortex genuinely encodes natural scenes in a high-dimensional pre-activation space, with implications for how cortical computation scales with stimulus complexity. Open questions include whether the NCE's failure on natural images reflects true high-dimensionality or a model/optimization limitation, and whether the pre-activations inferred by NCE correspond to measurable subthreshold membrane potentials.
