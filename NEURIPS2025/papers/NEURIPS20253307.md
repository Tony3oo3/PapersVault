## Problem

Multi-compartment Hodgkin-Huxley models require per-cell estimation of many biophysical parameters, but existing fitting methods depend on intracellular recordings (e.g., patch-clamp) that are technically difficult to obtain in vivo and typically capture voltage from only the soma. High-density extracellular probes like Neuropixel Ultra record voltage from hundreds of closely spaced sites around a neuron, yet this spatially rich signal has been used only for spike detection, discarding the biophysical information it contains. The paper addresses the gap of inferring full biophysical parameters and the neuron's 3D position relative to the probe from extracellular voltage traces alone.

## Input / Output

The method takes noisy extracellular voltage traces from M recording sites (e.g., 10 electrodes on a simulated MEA or a Neuropixel Ultra probe) sampled at 40 kHz, along with a known neuron morphology (branch connectivity, compartment lengths, and radii). The spatial position of the neuron relative to the probe may be treated as unknown, adding 6 rigid-body or up to 18 endpoint parameters. Outputs include per-compartment membrane voltages and ion-channel gating states (latent states of dimension up to 610), maximum conductances for each channel type per compartment or region (up to 24 parameters), and 3D compartment center coordinates. A key structural property is that extracellular potentials are linear functions of membrane voltages at each time step, which the method exploits for efficient inference.

## Method

The paper casts the multi-compartment HH model as a nonlinear state-space model and performs state inference with an Extended Kalman Filter (EKF) that linearizes the dynamics and emission via first-order Taylor expansions. To scale to latent state dimensions in the hundreds or thousands, two scalable EKF variants are developed: a diagonal approximation (inspired by Chang et al.) that reduces memory from O(d²) to O(d), and a block-diagonal BD(K) approximation that retains a dense K×K voltage-voltage covariance block while keeping gating variances diagonal, reducing EKF complexity from O(d³) to O(K³+d). The BD(K) family is proven closed under EKF prediction and update when the dynamics Jacobian is block-diagonal, which holds for well-behaved HH kinetics. Parameters (conductances and spatial positions) are estimated by maximizing the marginal likelihood obtained from the EKF using gradient-based optimization, with differentiable simulation via JAXLEY, state-space machinery from DYNAMAX, and sparse Jacobians from SPARSEJAC. Process noise in the dynamics provides robustness to model misspecification (omitted channels, incorrect gating kinetics), a property absent from deterministic MSE-based fitting.

## Results

On a 10-compartment single-branch neuron, EKF-based inference recovers maximum conductances (g_Na, g_K, g_L) with negligible bias and variance across 40 noisy recordings (σ_obs = 1 μV), and estimates remain close to ground truth even at 30 μV RMS noise. On a 12-compartment multi-branch neuron with unknown 3D geometry, the method recovers all 27 parameters (9 conductances + 18 spatial coordinates) accurately. On a 122-compartment retinal ganglion cell (610-dim latent state), the block-diagonal EKF converges to the true marginal log-likelihood and accurately reconstructs membrane voltages and conductances, while the diagonal EKF fails entirely. On a 60-compartment CA1 pyramidal cell (360-dim state, 30 parameters including 6 rigid-body transforms), the block-diagonal EKF recovers the neuron's spatial configuration and most maximum conductances, with residual errors attributable to known parameter degeneracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is direct MSE minimization as implemented in JAXLEY [20], which replaces the probabilistic EKF objective with deterministic dynamics and minimizes squared error between observed and predicted extracellular potentials. Under a correctly specified model with low noise (5 μM distance, 1 μV observation noise), standard EKF, block-diagonal EKF, and MSE minimization achieve nearly identical conductance estimates (Fig. 2C), confirming that the EKF's local linearization introduces negligible bias. However, under model misspecification—omitted M-type K+ and CaL channels, or perturbed gating rate constants α(V), β(V)—the EKF with process noise accurately recovers membrane voltage traces while MSE minimization produces substantially divergent estimates (Fig. 3). Among EKF variants, the diagonal approximation performs well for sparse electrode geometries (single-branch, multi-branch) but fails on the dense 10-electrode RGC setup where H^T H has strong off-diagonal structure; the block-diagonal EKF (BD(K)) resolves this and matches the full EKF in all tested settings. The block-diagonal EKF reduces computational complexity from O(d³) to O(K³ + d), making it feasible for the 610-dim RGC state space where the full EKF would be intractable.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper combines three existing toolchains—differentiable HH simulation (JAXLEY), state-space inference (DYNAMAX), and the classical Extended Kalman Filter—into a unified framework for fitting multi-compartment neuron models. The key departure from prior work is the observation modality: while HH parameter estimation has traditionally relied on intracellular patch-clamp recordings, this paper is, to the authors' knowledge, the first to fit full multi-compartment HH models to high-density extracellular voltage recordings, jointly inferring ion channel conductances and the neuron's 3D position relative to the probe. The scalable EKF variants (diagonal and block-diagonal covariance approximations) extend the diagonal-EKF idea of Chang et al. [27] to the structured sparsity of HH dynamics, and the block-diagonal construction is a new contribution that preserves the dense voltage-voltage coupling critical for high-density electrode geometries.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work opens a path toward non-invasive, in vivo biophysical characterization of individual neurons using the high-density probes (Neuropixel Ultra, MEAs) already deployed in practice, removing the need for technically challenging intracellular recordings. The demonstrated robustness to model misspecification (missing channels, incorrect gating kinetics) via process-noise-augmented EKF is a practical advantage over deterministic MSE fitting that could matter for real data where the true channel complement is unknown. Key open questions include extending the single-cell assumption to local neural populations (where overlapping extracellular signals from multiple cells become a confound), resolving the well-known parameter degeneracy in conductance-based models, and validating the approach on real in vivo recordings rather than simulated data.
