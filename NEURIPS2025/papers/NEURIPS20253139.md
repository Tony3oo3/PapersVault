## Problem

Existing neural VRP solvers are trained on a single input modality—either node coordinates or edge distances—making them inflexible for real-world scenarios where only one type of information may be available. Switching between modalities requires retraining from scratch, and single-modality training neglects the complementary structural information that the other modality provides, limiting solution quality and generalization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253139/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

UniteFormer accepts a VRP instance in one of three forms: a 2D coordinate matrix (node-only), a pairwise Euclidean distance matrix (edge-only), or both simultaneously (hybrid). During training, the input modality is randomly sampled per batch, so a single model learns to handle all three. The output is a complete tour (permutation of nodes) for TSP or a set of capacity-feasible depot-to-depot routes for CVRP, produced autoregressively via masked attention at each decoding step.

## Method

The architecture comprises a mixed encoder with two parallel sub-encoders and a parallel-attention decoder. The edge-aware sub-encoder fuses a mixed-score multi-head attention layer (which injects the edge weight matrix as an external score into standard attention) with three residual gated GCN layers that jointly propagate node and edge features, followed by a self-attention layer for global context. The node-focused sub-encoder is a standard three-layer Transformer encoder that captures spatial dependencies among node coordinates. The decoder computes attention scores in parallel over both sub-encoder outputs, then enriches the query vector via a linear query-mapping projection and a residual feed-forward layer before a masked single-head attention produces the next-node distribution. Training uses REINFORCE with POMO-style multi-start sampling (8 starts), and the input modality is randomly selected per batch to enforce cross-modal robustness in a single model.

## Results

On uniformly distributed TSP100 instances, UniteFormer-XE (hybrid input) with ×8 augmentation achieves the lowest optimality gap of 0.059% among all neural solvers, outperforming MatNet (0.410% at ×8), POMO (0.142% at ×8), and ICAM (0.22% at ×8). On CVRP100, UniteFormer-XE(×8) achieves a 0.596% gap, beating DAR (0.873%), ICAM (1.192%), and POMO (0.689%). Notably, UniteFormer-E(×8) on TSP100 (0.086%) surpasses MatNet(×128) (0.170%) with 12× fewer augmentation samples, and inference time is ~5.2 min vs. 60.1 min for MatNet(×128). On real-world TSPLib (N=1–100), UniteFormer-X(×8) achieves a 0.614% gap, and on CVRPLib (N=1–100), UniteFormer-XE(×8) achieves 1.940%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniteFormer consistently outperforms all learning-based baselines across all three input modalities. In the edge-only setting, UniteFormer-E(×8) on TSP100 (0.086% gap) beats MatNet(×128) (0.170%) by nearly 2× with 12× less compute, and dominates GREAT(×8) (0.810%) by ~9×. In the node-only setting, UniteFormer-X(×8) (0.085%) surpasses POMO(×8) (0.142%) and LEHD (0.577%) on TSP100, and on CVRP100 achieves 0.672% vs. POMO's 0.689%. In the hybrid setting, UniteFormer-XE(×8) achieves the best gap on both TSP100 (0.059%) and CVRP100 (0.596%), outperforming DAR(×8) (0.201% / 0.873%) and ICAM(×8) (0.22% / 1.192%). Against traditional solvers, Concorde and LKH3 still achieve 0% gap but require 66–135× more inference time (e.g., 66.45 min vs. 5.19 min on TSP100). On real-world TSPLib and CVRPLib benchmarks, UniteFormer generalizes well for instances up to 100 nodes, with node-only input performing best for TSP and hybrid input best for CVRP.

## Relation to Prior Work

UniteFormer sits at the intersection of three prior lines of work: edge-based matrix encoders (MatNet's mixed-score attention), node-based Transformer solvers (POMO's attention encoder and RL training loop), and residual gated GCNs (Bresson & Laurent). The key departure is that all prior neural VRP solvers are modality-specific—trained exclusively on node coordinates, edge distances, or a fixed hybrid—whereas UniteFormer trains a single model via stochastic modality sampling so that the same weights handle edge-only, node-only, and hybrid inputs at inference time. The mixed encoder (GCN + attention sub-encoders) and the parallel-attention decoder with query mapping are new architectural components, but each draws directly on established building blocks rather than introducing a fundamentally new learning paradigm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical value is clear: logistics and routing systems often have heterogeneous data availability (sometimes only coordinates, sometimes only cost matrices, sometimes both), and UniteFormer eliminates the need to maintain and retrain separate models per modality. The ablation showing catastrophic cross-modality failure (e.g., >100% gap) for single-modality-trained variants underscores that the unified training strategy is not merely convenient but necessary for robustness. Open questions include whether the heavy encoder (3 GCN layers + 3 attention layers + self-attention) can be distilled for large-scale instances (>1000 nodes), and whether the modality-unification idea extends naturally to multi-task VRP variants with different constraint structures.
