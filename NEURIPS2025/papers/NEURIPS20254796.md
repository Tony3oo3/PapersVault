## Problem

Biaffine scoring in dependency parsing, unlike Transformer self-attention, does not normalize its scores before the softmax, leading to high-variance inputs that produce sharp, unstable probability distributions. This forces models to rely on extra trainable parameters (deeper BiLSTM stacks) to implicitly reduce score variance, resulting in overparameterized parsers. The paper asks whether explicit score normalization can eliminate this need for excess parameters while maintaining or improving parsing accuracy.

## Input / Output

The model takes a tokenized sentence, encodes it with a frozen or fine-tuned BERT/DeBERTa encoder to obtain word-level representations, and optionally predicts entity tags (POS tags for syntactic parsing, named entity classes for semantic parsing) via a single-layer BiLSTM tagger. It outputs a full dependency graph: a |V|×|V| adjacency matrix of edges, a set of relation labels for each edge, and entity tags for each node. During inference, Chu-Liu/Edmonds maximum spanning tree decoding ensures the predicted graph is a well-formed arborescence with a single root and no cycles.

## Method

The core contribution is scaling biaffine scores by 1/√d (where d is the projection dimension), directly analogous to the √d_k scaling in Transformer attention, to control the variance of inputs to the softmax. Theoretically, the paper proves via singular value dynamics under gradient descent (Result 1) that deeper BiLSTM stacks reduce the effective rank of weight matrices, which monotonically decreases output variance (Claim 1) — explaining why existing models without normalization still work: depth acts as implicit regularization. Empirically, adding this single scaling factor allows matching or surpassing state-of-the-art performance with as few as one BiLSTM layer instead of three, reducing trained parameters by up to 85%. The architecture follows Bhatt et al. (2024): BERT encoder → tagger BiLSTM → N-layer parser BiLSTM → four MLP projections → biaffine edge and relation scoring → MST decoder, trained end-to-end with a joint cross-entropy loss over tags, edges, and relations.

## Results

With biaffine score normalization (a=1/√d), the proposed method achieves state-of-the-art performance across all six datasets: 0.686 F1 on ADE, 0.602 on CoNLL04, 0.320 on SciERC, 0.708 on ERFGC, 0.807 LAS on enEWT, and 0.919 LAS on SciDTB (all with N=3 BiLSTM layers). Critically, a single BiLSTM layer with normalization (N=1) matches or surpasses the 3-layer unnormalized baseline on most datasets, representing up to 85% fewer trained parameters. On the hardest dataset (SciERC), normalization without any BiLSTM layers (N=0) yields a 23% F1 improvement over the unnormalized N=0 setting, and the performance gap between normalized and raw scores is statistically significant (p<0.01) at all layer depths.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole external baseline is Bhatt et al. [3] (LREC-COLING 2024), the prior state-of-the-art for semantic dependency parsing using a 3-layer BiLSTM with unnormalized biaffine scores. With score normalization (a=1/√d) and 3 BiLSTM layers, the proposed method outperforms [3] on all six datasets: +3.3 F1 on ADE (0.686 vs 0.653), +3.6 on CoNLL04 (0.602 vs 0.566), +6.3 on SciERC (0.320 vs 0.257), +0.7 on ERFGC (0.708 vs 0.701), +0.3 LAS on enEWT (0.807 vs 0.804), and +0.4 LAS on SciDTB (0.919 vs 0.915). The most striking comparison is parameter efficiency: a single BiLSTM layer with normalization (N=1, a=1/√d) achieves 0.668/0.597/0.299/0.692 F1 on the four SemDP datasets, matching or exceeding the 3-layer unnormalized baseline (0.662/0.562/0.299/0.705) while using up to 85% fewer trained parameters. The gains are largest on SciERC, where the complex semantic dependencies and limited entity overlap between train and test splits make unnormalized scores particularly harmful.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the biaffine dependency parser of Dozat and Manning (2017) and the state-of-the-art semantic dependency parsing architecture of Bhatt et al. (2024), identifying that the biaffine scoring function lacks the 1/√d_k normalization that Transformer self-attention (Vaswani et al., 2017) has employed since its introduction. The key conceptual departure is connecting this architectural omission to implicit regularization theory (Arora et al., 2019): the paper shows that stacking BiLSTM layers reduces the effective rank of weight matrices, which implicitly reduces score variance and compensates for the missing normalization. By making this implicit effect explicit through score normalization, the authors demonstrate that the extra depth (and its associated parameters) is unnecessary.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for dependency parsing practitioners: a single BiLSTM layer with normalized scores can match or exceed three-layer unnormalized parsers, reducing trained parameters by up to 85% while also accelerating convergence. The theoretical bridge between implicit regularization (rank decay under gradient descent) and practical score variance provides a principled explanation for why deeper stacks were needed in prior work. Open questions include whether the normalization benefit persists when fully fine-tuning very large encoders (the paper shows diminishing returns at 100–400M parameters, though convergence speed still improves), whether the effect generalizes to other latent graph inference tasks beyond dependency parsing, and whether it applies to LLM-based or seq2seq approaches to the same tasks.
