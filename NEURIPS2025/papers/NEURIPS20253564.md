## Problem

In real-world settings, tables appear as images rather than clean structured formats, yet current MLLMs struggle with multi-step tabular reasoning (aggregation, comparison, arithmetic) from images alone. The paper asks whether structured tables, available only during training as privileged information, can be leveraged to bridge the modality gap and transfer reasoning capabilities to MLLMs that must operate on table images at inference time.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253564/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the model receives a table image (screenshot or scanned document) and a natural-language question, and produces a structured output consisting of a chain-of-thought reasoning trace enclosed in  tags followed by a final answer in <answer></answer> tags. During training, the model additionally receives the ground-truth structured markdown table as privileged supervision, which is discarded at test time. The base model is an 8B-parameter MLLM (Ovis2), and the training set is limited to ~9k high-quality examples drawn from five tabular QA and fact-verification datasets.

## Method

TURBO is a two-stage training framework built on the insight that reasoning chains are modality-invariant: the logical path from question to answer is identical whether the input is a structured table or its image. In the first stage, structured markdown tables paired with QA triples are fed into DeepSeek-R1 to generate structure-aware reasoning traces, which are then filtered via reject sampling (removing verbose or contradictory traces) and validated by an auxiliary LLM, yielding ~9k high-quality [question, reasoning, answer] examples for supervised fine-tuning. In the second stage, Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) is applied: for each question, 16 candidate responses are sampled, and a composite reward (format compliance + answer accuracy) is used to compute within-group relative advantages, guiding the policy toward more accurate and interpretable reasoning paths without requiring a separate critic network. The key architectural contribution is the modality-bridging data pipeline that distills LLM reasoning over structured tables into an MLLM that must perceive and reason over raw table images.

## Results

TURBO achieves a 76.42% average accuracy across seven tabular reasoning benchmarks, surpassing the strongest baseline Qwen2.5-VL (71.28%) by 7.2 percentage points. On the challenging MMMU benchmark (table-related questions), TURBO reaches 57.32%, outperforming the previous best Ovis2-CoT (50.61%) by 13% and HIPPO by 59.3% relative improvement. The model is trained on only ~9k high-quality reasoning samples, demonstrating strong data efficiency. In Appendix B.1, the 8B TURBO model is competitive with the 671B DeepSeek-R1 (87.34% avg on structured text) despite operating on raw table images, and even surpasses DeepSeek-R1 on InfoTabs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TURBO wins on all seven benchmarks against every baseline. Against the strongest open-source MLLM Qwen2.5-VL (71.28% avg), TURBO gains +7.2% on average, with the largest margin on InfoTabs (+3.98) and TAT-QA (+2.67). Against HIPPO (which uses structured tables at inference), TURBO improves by up to 21.7% on WTQ; against the fairer HIPPO w/o ST variant (image-only inference), the average TQA improvement is 26.8%. On MMMU, TURBO (57.32%) surpasses Ovis2-CoT (50.61%) by 6.71 points and HIPPO w/o ST (35.98%) by 21.34 points. In the cross-scale comparison (Appendix B.1), the 8B TURBO model (79.60% avg on image input) is competitive with the 671B DeepSeek-R1 (87.34% on structured text) and beats QvQ-72B (83.56%) on TABMWP and InfoTabs, validating the parameter efficiency of the privileged-information training strategy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the tabular reasoning line of work (HIPPO, Chain-of-Table, OPENRT) by reframing structured tables as privileged training-only information rather than inference-time input, drawing on the Learning Using Privileged Information (LUPI) paradigm from Vapnik. Methodologically, it builds on GRPO from DeepSeekMath for the RL stage and on DeepSeek-R1 as a structure-aware reasoning trace generator, combining these into a two-stage SFT+RL pipeline on an MLLM base (Ovis2). The key conceptual departure from prior multimodal table work (Table-LLaVA, TabPedia) is the explicit modality-bridging hypothesis: that reasoning chains are modality-agnostic and can transfer from structured-text supervision to image-based inference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical deployment gap—real-world tables are images, not clean Markdown—by showing that structured tables used only during training can substantially boost image-based reasoning with just 9k samples. Limitations include susceptibility to OCR errors on dense or low-resolution tables, residual failures in multi-step arithmetic, and limited testing on irregular layouts (merged cells, nested headers). Open questions include whether structured tables can also be incorporated at inference time for further gains, how the approach scales to more complex table structures, and whether the privileged-information paradigm generalizes beyond tabular reasoning to other structured-to-unstructured modality gaps.
