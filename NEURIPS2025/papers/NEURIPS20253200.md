## Problem

Existing KG-based RAG methods struggle to retrieve both accurate and diverse information for complex queries that require fusing relational structure with textual content. While Process Reward Models could provide step-wise guidance for multi-step retrieval, they require expensive process-level supervision signals that are impractical to annotate on large knowledge graphs, where only the terminal outcome reward (whether the final document supports the query) is readily available.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253200/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a complex natural-language query (e.g., a paper-searching or product-recommendation request) and a text-rich knowledge graph where each node carries an associated document (paper abstract, product description, drug annotation) and edges encode typed relations. It produces a set of K target nodes with their documents, retrieved via a multi-step traversal where the agent incrementally moves to neighboring nodes and collects documents along the path. Unlike simple relational queries with a single deterministic target, complex queries correspond to multiple valid retrieval targets, so the output is inherently a diverse set rather than a single answer.

## Method

GraphFlow formulates KG retrieval as a multi-step decision process and jointly trains a retrieval policy and a flow estimator using the GFlowNet detailed balance objective, which implicitly factorizes the terminal outcome reward into intermediate state flow values without requiring explicit process-level annotations. A local exploration strategy generates k exploratory neighbor actions at each non-terminal state, restricting the detailed balance constraint to locally reachable transitions and avoiding the computational infeasibility of enforcing it globally over the vast KG state space. The LLM (LLaMA3-8B-Instruct) serves as a shared encoder via prompt-wrapped state and state-action representations, with LoRA adapters on the frozen backbone and separate 1-layer MLP heads for the forward policy (softmax over candidate actions) and the log-flow estimator. A self-loop action allows the policy to adaptively terminate the trajectory when the current document is deemed sufficient, and boundary conditions fix the flow at initial and terminal states to ensure consistent reward propagation. This design lets the policy sample trajectories in proportion to their outcome reward, naturally promoting diverse exploration of high-reward KG regions while avoiding overfitting to a single dominant retrieval path.

## Results

On the STaRK benchmark (three text-rich KGs: Amazon, MAG, PRIME), GraphFlow with reranking achieves Hit@1 of 47.85/39.09/51.39 and MRR of 55.49/47.82/61.37 across the three datasets, outperforming the strongest baseline ToG+GPT-4o (Hit@1: 27.58/26.67/53.33; MRR: 39.08/39.65/57.78) by an average of ~10% on accuracy metrics. On retrieval diversity, GraphFlow achieves D-R@20 of 36.15/57.18/79.59, substantially exceeding ToG+GPT-4o (23.70/47.71/54.35) and PRM (18.94/36.73/45.97). GraphFlow also attains the highest Seper scores (Answer-ΔSeper: 0.219/0.145/0.206), confirming superior retrieval utility for downstream question answering.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GraphFlow outperforms all five baselines across all three STaRK datasets on both accuracy and diversity. Against the strongest agent-based baseline ToG+GPT-4o, GraphFlow gains ~20 points on Hit@1 for STaRK-AMAZON (47.85 vs. 27.58) and ~16 points on MRR for STaRK-MAG (47.82 vs. 39.65), while on STaRK-PRIME the gap narrows on Hit@1 (51.39 vs. 53.33) but GraphFlow still leads on Hit@5 (72.11 vs. 63.73) and MRR (61.37 vs. 57.78). The PRM baseline, which requires expensive process-level supervision, is clearly surpassed by GraphFlow on MRR across all datasets (e.g., 55.49 vs. 31.97 on Amazon) and on diversity (D-R@20: 36.15 vs. 18.94 on Amazon), demonstrating that flow-based credit assignment is more effective than explicit step-wise preference labels. Retrieval-based methods (DenseRetriever, G-Retriever, SubgraphRAG) are consistently the weakest, with Hit@1 scores below 10 on Amazon and MAG, confirming that embedding-based or subgraph-extraction approaches struggle with the multi-hop, text-rich nature of complex KG queries. Notably, ToG's performance is highly sensitive to the backbone LLM: ToG+LLaMA3 drops to Hit@1 of 4.21 on Amazon versus 27.58 with GPT-4o, while GraphFlow (using LLaMA3-8B) still achieves 47.85, indicating that the flow-based training objective compensates for the weaker base model.

## Relation to Prior Work

GraphFlow adapts the GFlowNet framework (Bengio et al., 2023) to the structured retrieval setting of KG-based RAG, using the detailed balance objective (Shen et al., 2023) to jointly train a retrieval policy and a flow estimator. It departs from Process Reward Models, which require expensive process-level supervision, by implicitly performing credit assignment through flow values that factorize the outcome reward across intermediate steps. The local exploration strategy is a practical departure from standard GFlowNet training, restricting the detailed balance constraint to neighborhoods of visited states to handle the vast and sparsely-reachable state space of knowledge graphs. The irreversible backward policy (P_B = 1) follows Hu et al. (2024) for amortized inference in LLMs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that GFlowNet-based credit assignment can replace costly process-level supervision in KG-based RAG, yielding a 10% average improvement over GPT-4o-based agent baselines on both accuracy and diversity metrics. The strong cross-domain generalization (retrieving from unseen KGs without retraining) suggests the learned retrieval policy captures transferable structural reasoning rather than dataset-specific patterns. Open questions include scalability to much larger KGs, sensitivity to the choice of LLM backbone beyond LLaMA3-8B, and whether the flow estimation approach extends to multi-agent or collaborative retrieval settings. The high variance in Seper scores and evaluation on a single benchmark (STaRK) leave some uncertainty about the robustness of the gains.
