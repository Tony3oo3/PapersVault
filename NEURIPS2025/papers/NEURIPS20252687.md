## Problem

Standard Mixed-Integer Constraint Learning (MICL) embeds learned surrogate models into optimization, but optimization algorithms systematically exploit small model errors, producing solutions that violate the true underlying constraints. Existing remedies like the W-MICL ensemble heuristic lack formal probabilistic guarantees and suffer from severe scalability issues as ensemble size grows. This paper asks: can we provide rigorous, distribution-free feasibility guarantees for data-driven constraints in optimization without access to the true constraint function?

## Input / Output

The method consumes a trained MIP-encodable predictive model ŷ(x), a disjoint calibration dataset of size N, and a target miscoverage level α; for regression it additionally requires a MIP-encodable uncertainty model û(x). The conformal quantile is computed offline as a single scalar, so the resulting MIP formulation size is independent of N and the method trains at most two models. It outputs optimal decision variables (x, z) certified ground-truth feasible with probability at least 1−α, along with the corresponding optimal objective value.

## Method

C-MICL replaces the pointwise learned constraint ŷ(x) ∈ Y with a conformal set containment constraint C_α(x) ⊆ Y, where C_α(x) is constructed via Mondrian conformal prediction to yield conditional coverage guarantees over both feasible and infeasible regions. In regression, conformal scores are adaptive residuals |ŷ(x_i)−y_i|/û(x_i), producing the interval [ŷ(x) ± q̂_{1−α}·û(x)] that must lie within the feasible bounds; in classification, scores are negative correct-class logits, and the constraint enforces that no undesired-class logit exceeds the quantile threshold via big-M indicator constraints. Under a conditional independence assumption linking C-MICL feasibility to conformal coverage given ground-truth feasibility, the paper proves that any feasible solution is ground-truth feasible with probability ≥ 1−α (Theorem 4.1). The key architectural distinction from W-MICL is that only a scalar quantile enters the optimization, avoiding replication of model constraints across ensemble members and yielding orders-of-magnitude speedups while preserving formal guarantees.

## Results

On the membrane reactor regression case study (α=10%), C-MICL consistently achieves the target ≥90% ground-truth feasibility rate across all four base models (ReLU NN, GBT, RF, LMDT), while no single-model MICL baseline reaches the target and W-MICL only approaches it with neural networks. The objective value penalty is modest at ~7% relative to heuristic methods, and C-MICL is two orders of magnitude faster than W-MICL(P=50). On the food basket classification case study (α=10%), C-MICL is the only method achieving the ≥90% feasibility target, with only ~1% objective value difference and solution times comparable to single-model MICL.

## Baselines & Comparisons

C-MICL is compared against two baselines: standard single-model MICL and the ensemble-based W-MICL heuristic (P=5,10,25,50) from Maragno et al. [7]. On ground-truth feasibility, C-MICL decisively wins: it consistently achieves the target ≥90% rate across all base models in regression and is the only method to do so in classification, whereas single-model MICL never reaches the target and W-MICL only empirically approaches it with neural networks. On objective value, C-MICL incurs a small penalty of ~7% (regression) and ~1% (classification) relative to the heuristics, which is a modest trade-off for formal probabilistic guarantees. On computational time, C-MICL is two orders of magnitude faster than W-MICL(P=50) in regression and significantly faster than W-MICL(P=5,10) in classification, while remaining comparable to single-model MICL. Overall, C-MICL dominates on feasibility guarantees and speed, with only a small objective-value cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of conformal prediction (Angelopoulos et al. 2024; Vovk et al. 2003) and Mixed-Integer Constraint Learning (Fajemisin et al. 2024), combining the distribution-free coverage guarantees of Mondrian conformal sets with MIP-encodable predictive models. It departs from the ensemble-based W-MICL heuristic (Maragno et al. 2025), which provides no formal guarantees and scales linearly with ensemble size, by replacing the ensemble with a single conformal quantile computed offline. The regression conformal score follows the adaptive interval construction of Lei et al. 2018, while the classification score is a novel logit-space formulation designed for MIP compatibility. Unlike prior conformal-optimization work (Lin et al. 2024 on inverse optimization, Zhao et al. 2024 on chance constraints), C-MICL conformalizes the feasible region of learned constraints rather than robustifying objectives or handling stochasticity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

C-MICL provides the first formal probabilistic feasibility guarantee for data-driven constraints embedded in optimization, closing a gap where prior methods (standard MICL, W-MICL) could produce solutions that violate the true system with no statistical bound on the violation probability. The practical impact is significant: practitioners can now tune the confidence level α and obtain implementable solutions without access to the true constraint function, while the method's independence from dataset size in the optimization formulation enables use of large training sets without computational penalty. Key limitations include the conditional independence assumption (Assumption 4.1), which may fail when the feasible region concentrates in poorly calibrated data regimes, and the degradation to infeasibility when the underlying ML model is too weak to produce informative conformal sets. Open directions include extending to multiple learned constraints, learning objective functions with guarantees, and relaxing the conditional independence assumption via finer-grained local conformal methods.
