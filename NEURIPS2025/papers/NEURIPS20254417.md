## Problem

Standard conformal prediction provides distribution-free marginal coverage but yields constant-width, non-adaptive prediction bands. While kernel sum-of-squares (SoS) formulations were recently proposed to learn adaptive score functions, they rely on semidefinite programming solvers that fail to scale beyond a few hundred samples, and lack a principled criterion for tuning kernel hyperparameters toward local coverage.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254417/cropped_page7_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates in the split conformal prediction setting: a pre-training set D_n is used to jointly learn a regression function m(·) in an RKHS and a non-negative scaling function f(·) via kernel SoS, while a separate calibration set D_m is used only to compute the adjusted score quantile. For a new observation X, the output is a symmetric adaptive interval [m(X) − q̂_α·√f(X), m(X) + q̂_α·√f(X)] whose width varies with the input, along with the point estimate m(X) and the learned variance proxy f(X).

## Method

The paper formulates adaptive band construction as a constrained optimization over a regression function m in an RKHS and a PSD operator A defining f_A(X) = φ(X)ᵀAφ(X), with an objective combining MSE, mean interval width, nuclear-norm sparsity, and Frobenius-norm ridge penalties, subject to 100% coverage on pre-training data. A general representer theorem reduces the infinite-dimensional problem to a finite SDP, and the key technical contribution is a dual formulation that reparameterizes the problem over only (n+1) non-negative variables rather than (n + n²), enabling efficient solution via accelerated gradient ascent at scales of thousands of samples. For hyperparameter tuning, the authors derive a bound on test-conditional coverage in terms of the mutual information (and equivalently HSIC) between the residual |Y − m(X)| and the learned scaling function f(X), providing a goal-oriented criterion to select the kernel lengthscale θ^f that maximizes adaptivity while preserving local coverage.

## Results

On six real-world regression datasets, kSoS (best mean width variant) achieves the lowest or near-lowest mean prediction interval width on 5 out of 6 datasets: 0.803 on Bike (vs. 0.809–1.114 for baselines), 185.83 on Diabetes (vs. 188.62–191.24), 9.15 on MPG (vs. 9.70–9.89), and 1.468 on Housing (vs. 1.453–1.816). The HSIC-optimized variant produces slightly wider bands (e.g., 9.36 vs. 9.15 on MPG) but achieves worst-set coverage closer to the 0.9 target, confirming better local adaptivity. On the Bio dataset with strong asymmetric noise, CQR outperforms all methods (1.879 vs. 2.03 for kSoS). The dual formulation scales to n=2000 training samples, whereas the SDP-based approach from prior work is limited to n≈200.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CQR (Romano et al., 2019), kSoS achieves lower mean width on 5 of 6 datasets (e.g., 9.15 vs. 9.89 on MPG, 0.803 vs. 1.114 on Bike), but loses on Bio where asymmetric noise heavily favors quantile regression (1.879 vs. 2.03). Against heteroscedastic GP (Binois et al., 2018), kSoS wins on Bike (0.803 vs. 1.000), Diabetes (185.83 vs. 191.24), and MPG (9.15 vs. 9.70), but Het GP achieves the smallest width on Concrete (0.508 vs. 0.556) at the cost of lower worst-set coverage. Against homoscedastic GP, kSoS is competitive or superior on most datasets, with the clearest advantage on MPG (9.15 vs. 9.71) and Bike (0.803 vs. 0.809). The HSIC-tuned kSoS variant trades a small increase in mean width for substantially better worst-set coverage (closer to the 0.9 target), as shown in Figure 4 on Housing and Concrete, demonstrating that the HSIC criterion targets local adaptivity rather than global width minimization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the kernel sum-of-squares (SoS) framework for conformal prediction bands introduced by Liang [2022] and Fan et al. [2024], which themselves build on the non-negative function estimation machinery of Marteau-Ferey et al. [2020]. The key departures are: (i) a general representer theorem and dual formulation that replaces intractable SDP solvers with accelerated gradient methods, enabling scaling from ~200 to 1000+ samples; (ii) a broader objective function that jointly controls mean estimation, interval width, and regularity within the standard split CP calibration; and (iii) an HSIC-based hyperparameter tuning criterion inspired by the mutual-information local coverage bound of Deutschmann et al. [2024], but reformulated for one-dimensional variables to avoid the curse of dimensionality. The paper also positions itself against the wider adaptivity literature (rescaled scores, CQR, localized CP of Hore and Barber [2024]) by arguing that goal-oriented learning of the score function via kernel SoS offers a more principled path to local coverage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dual formulation removes the most significant practical barrier to adopting kernel SoS for conformal prediction—namely the O(n³) SDP bottleneck—making the approach viable for moderate-scale regression problems where kernel methods are natural. The HSIC tuning criterion is broadly applicable beyond kernel SoS to any CP method with a learned score function, offering a principled alternative to ad-hoc bandwidth selection. However, the method remains limited to ~2000 samples due to kernel matrix operations, is less robust to outliers than CQR due to the 100% pre-training coverage constraint, and the HSIC criterion currently only applies to symmetric intervals. Open questions include extending to high-dimensional tabular data (additive kernels), generalizing the HSIC bound to non-symmetric score functions, and incorporating Nystrom or random feature approximations for larger-scale deployment.
