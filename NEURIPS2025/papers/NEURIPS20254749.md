## Problem

Current state-of-the-art protein ensemble generation models (e.g., AlphaFlow) rely on fine-tuning pre-trained folding models and evolutionary sequence information (MSA or protein language model embeddings), which limits applicability to de novo proteins where such information is absent, introduces potential biases, and makes inference orders of magnitude slower than necessary. This paper asks whether high-quality conformational ensembles can be generated from backbone geometry alone, without any evolutionary information or pre-trained folding model weights.

## Input / Output

The model takes as input the equilibrium backbone structure of a protein (represented as SE(3) frames: per-residue rotations and translations) along with the amino acid sequence as node features. The equilibrium structure is encoded geometrically as binned pairwise Cα distances (0–20 Å, 22 bins) and equivariant pairwise direction vectors for residues closer than 5 Å, serving as initial edge features. The output is a set of 250 backbone conformations (SE(3) frames) sampled from the Boltzmann distribution, emulating 300 ns of MD. Notably, no residue or chain index is used as input, which enables direct transfer to multi-chain proteins without retraining.

## Method

BBFlow formulates MD emulation as conditional SE(3) flow matching, learning a time-dependent vector field v(x, t, x_eq) that transports samples from a conditional prior to the target ensemble distribution. The architecture is an SE(3)-equivariant graph neural network with 6 Clifford Frame Attention (CFA) message-passing blocks (adapted from GAFL), operating on structural data only without an Evoformer-style sequence module. Two key innovations distinguish it from prior work: (1) a conditional prior p₀(x|x_eq) constructed by geodesic interpolation between pure noise and the equilibrium structure with hyperparameter ξ=0.2, which anchors the starting point near the fold and reduces over-stabilization; and (2) a geometric encoding of the equilibrium structure as edge features (binned distances + equivariant directions) that replaces evolutionary information entirely. The model is trained from scratch on the ATLAS dataset (1265 proteins, 3×100 ns MD trajectories) using MSE loss on the SE(3) tangent-space flow vector field plus an auxiliary frame-prediction loss, requiring only ~3 GPU days on two A100s.

## Results

On the ATLAS test set (82 proteins), BBFlow achieves RMSF MAE of 0.42 Å, pairwise RMSD MAE of 0.77 Å, DCCM Pearson r of 0.87, and PCA W₂ of 1.33 Å, while generating each conformation in 0.8 s — roughly 40× faster than AlphaFlow-T (32.6 s) at comparable accuracy. BBFlow's median RMSF (1.49 Å) closely matches the MD reference (1.48 Å), whereas AlphaFlow-T systematically over-stabilizes (1.17 Å). On de novo proteins, BBFlow maintains competitive performance (RMSF MAE 0.26 Å, Pw-RMSD MAE 0.32 Å) while AlphaFlow without templates degrades severely (RMSF MAE 4.76 Å, Pw-RMSD MAE 7.40 Å).

## Baselines & Comparisons

BBFlow outperforms AlphaFlow (without templates), ESMFlow-T, ConfDiff, and both distilled AlphaFlow variants on nearly all accuracy metrics while being 1.5–40× faster. Against the strongest baseline AlphaFlow-T, BBFlow trades a small loss in RMSF correlation (0.90 vs 0.92) and PCA W₂ (1.33 vs 1.28) for a clear win in pairwise RMSD MAE (0.77 vs 0.91) and a much more faithful median RMSF (1.49 vs 1.17, where MD=1.48), indicating AlphaFlow-T over-stabilizes. BBFlow underperforms on transient contact accuracy (29% vs 47% for AlphaFlow-T), suggesting evolutionary information still aids rare-event prediction. On de novo proteins, AlphaFlow without templates and BioEmu collapse (RMSF MAE >4 Å, Pw-RMSD MAE >7 Å) due to absent evolutionary information, while BBFlow remains competitive with template-based models (RMSF MAE 0.26 Å vs 0.25 Å for AlphaFlow-T). BBFlow is also the first ensemble model shown to generalize to multi-chain proteins without retraining, a capability none of the baselines possess.

## Relation to Prior Work

BBFlow builds on the SE(3) flow matching framework for protein backbone generation (FrameFlow, FrameDiff) and adopts the Clifford frame attention architecture from GAFL, but repurposes these tools for a fundamentally different task: MD emulation rather than de novo structure design. The key departure from the state-of-the-art AlphaFlow is the elimination of evolutionary sequence information (MSA, protein language models) and pre-trained folding model weights, replacing them with a geometric encoding of the equilibrium backbone structure and a conditional prior that interpolates between noise and the equilibrium state via geodesic paths. This decouples ensemble generation from the structure prediction task, enabling training from scratch and generalization to multi-chain and de novo proteins.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BBFlow offers a 40x speedup over AlphaFlow at comparable accuracy while removing the dependency on evolutionary information, making it the first ensemble generation model applicable to de novo proteins and multi-chain complexes—settings where MSA-based methods fail or are undefined. This opens the door to efficient dynamics screening in de novo protein design pipelines and large-scale MD emulation. Key limitations include inability to sample states far from equilibrium (e.g., alternative folding states), underperformance on rare transient contacts, and lack of sidechain or ligand modeling. Open questions include whether the geometric conditioning can be extended to longer timescales, whether sidechain ensembles can be added, and whether the conditional prior idea generalizes to other generative modeling problems.
