## Problem

Conventional BPTT-based SNN training incurs memory and compute costs that grow linearly with the number of time steps, while efficient alternatives like RateBP introduce gradient distortions that accumulate with depth. Additionally, standard self-distillation for SNNs assumes the final layer always provides the best teacher signal, but the authors empirically show this fails in over 50% of training iterations, causing unreliable teacher labels to mislead intermediate layers toward suboptimal solutions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253724/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR, 224×224 for ImageNet) or event-based images (128×128 downsampled to 48×48 for CIFAR10-DVS), which are directly encoded into temporal spike sequences at each time step. The network outputs a class label via the final classifier, while intermediate auxiliary ANN branches (depthwise separable convolutions) produce additional soft-label predictions used only during training and discarded at inference, incurring zero extra inference cost.

## Method

The framework uses a two-stage training loop: a spiking forward pass accumulates eligibility traces without constructing a computational graph, followed by a rate-based forward pass where intermediate layer firing rates are projected onto lightweight auxiliary ANN branches. The key novelty is a reliability-separated self-distillation strategy: rather than statically assigning the final layer as teacher, the method filters out samples misclassified by each individual branch and aggregates only correctly predicted outputs to form the teacher label, with a small regularization term applied to samples misclassified by all branches. Gradients are approximated via eligibility traces (∂L/∂W ≈ ∂L/∂r · E[e_ij^t]), keeping memory constant regardless of time steps, while the auxiliary branches backpropagate more accurate gradients to intermediate substructures through standard ANN pathways. The total loss combines per-branch cross-entropy with a weighted KL-divergence distillation loss (β=0.3), and the auxiliary branches are removed at inference.

## Results

On CIFAR-100 with ResNet-18 at T=2, the proposed method achieves 81.44% top-1 accuracy, outperforming RateBP (75.97%) by 5.47% and approaching BPTT-based distillation methods (SM: 79.49%, TWKD: 79.80%). On ImageNet with ResNet-34, it reaches 70.72%, surpassing RateBP (70.01%) by 0.71% and exceeding all BPTT-based direct training methods (best: 68.19% by Dspike). On CIFAR10-DVS with ResNet-19 at T=10, the one-step variant achieves 81.90%, a 1.50% gain over RateBP (80.40%). Training memory is reduced by 75.80% and time by 23.30% compared to BPTT, with memory remaining constant as timesteps increase.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method consistently outperforms RateBP [77], the closest one-step baseline, by 1.18–1.31% on CIFAR-100 (ResNet-18/19) and 0.71% on ImageNet, while matching or exceeding BPTT-based distillation methods (SM [12], TWKD [78], EAGD [72]) that require external pre-trained ANN teachers. Against BPTT direct-training baselines (Dspike [42], TET [11], SEW-ResNet [17]), the method achieves comparable or superior accuracy with 75.80% less memory and 23.30% less time. On CIFAR10-DVS, the one-step variant (81.90%) surpasses RateBP (80.40%) by 1.50% and approaches BPTT methods (TWKD: 83.80%, SM: 83.19%), while the BPTT variant of the proposed method reaches 85.90%, exceeding all prior work. The method also outperforms online training approaches (OTTT [69], OS [86]) by 1.36–10.39% on CIFAR-100, demonstrating that the reliability-separated self-distillation with auxiliary ANN branches effectively compensates for the gradient distortions inherent in efficient one-step training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RateBP framework (Yu et al., 2024), which decouples the temporal dimension from backpropagation to achieve constant memory cost, by introducing lightweight auxiliary ANN branches that project intermediate firing rates and provide more accurate gradient signals to substructures. It simultaneously builds on the self-distillation paradigm (Zhang et al., ICCV 2019), which uses the model's own final-layer predictions as teacher labels, but departs from it by empirically demonstrating that the final layer is not always the best predictor and proposing a reliability-separated aggregation strategy. The combination of rate-based one-step backpropagation with reliability-filtered self-distillation is the paper's core architectural contribution, positioning it between efficient training methods (RateBP, DSR) and distillation-based approaches (EAGD, TWKD) in the SNN training landscape.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 75.80% memory reduction and 23.30% time reduction over BPTT make high-performance SNN training feasible on resource-constrained hardware, which is critical for the neuromorphic computing ecosystem. The empirical finding that over 50% of self-distillation teacher labels are unreliable challenges a foundational assumption in the self-distillation literature and suggests that reliability-aware teacher selection could benefit ANN training as well. However, the rate-based approach remains weaker than BPTT on temporal-coding datasets (CIFAR10-DVS), and the auxiliary branches, while discarded at inference, still add a fixed training overhead. Open questions include whether the reliability-separation strategy generalizes to deeper architectures, non-ResNet topologies, and sequence-level tasks where temporal dynamics are more critical.
