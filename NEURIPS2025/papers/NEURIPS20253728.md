## Problem

DNA foundation models produce powerful sequence representations but operate as opaque black boxes that cannot generate transparent, mechanistic biological explanations, while LLMs possess strong multi-step reasoning yet lack the architecture to natively process raw genomic sequences. No prior work had trained a unified system that combines nucleotide-level representations from a DNA foundation model with LLM reasoning to perform interpretable, multi-step biological inference from variant sequences to disease phenotypes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253728/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes paired reference and variant DNA sequences (averaging ~4,000 bp, truncated to 2048 DNA tokens) alongside a natural-language query providing gene, pathway, and chromosome context. The DNA sequences are tokenized by a DNA-specific tokenizer and encoded into per-token embeddings by a frozen foundation model, which are then projected into the LLM's embedding space via a learnable linear layer and interleaved with text embeddings and special <dna_start>/<dna_end> tokens under a unified RoPE positional scheme. Output is a structured generation: a multi-step mechanistic reasoning trace enclosed in <think>... tokens (averaging ~304 words) followed by a concise final prediction (disease name or pathogenic/benign label).

## Method

BIOREASON integrates a frozen DNA foundation model (Evo2-1B or Nucleotide Transformer-500M) with a Qwen3 LLM backbone by projecting the DNA model's contextualized per-token embeddings through a learnable linear layer into the LLM's embedding dimension, then concatenating them with the tokenized text query and special delimiter tokens to form a single multimodal input sequence processed by the LLM. Training proceeds in two stages: (1) supervised fine-tuning with LoRA (rank 32) on curated reasoning traces from a novel KEGG-derived dataset (1,449 variant-to-disease entries with explicit causal paths) and two ClinVar-based VEP datasets, with loss computed only on the reasoning and answer tokens; (2) reinforcement learning via Dr. GRPO (an unbiased GRPO variant) using a composite non-differentiable reward (correctness +2.0, conciseness +0.5, format +0.5) that incentivizes both accurate predictions and adherence to the step-by-step reasoning structure. The key architectural insight is that by placing DNA embeddings directly in the LLM's input stream with shared positional encoding, the LLM can attend to and reason over genomic features as a first-class modality rather than treating sequences as opaque strings, enabling mechanistic variant-to-phenotype inference that neither component achieves alone.

## Results

On the novel KEGG-derived biological reasoning benchmark (290 test samples), the best BIOREASON configuration (Evo2+Qwen3-4B with GRPO) achieves 98.28% accuracy and 93.05% macro F1, up from 88.28%/72.43% for the standalone Evo2-1B DNA model and 90.00%/79.66% for Qwen3-4B alone. On VEP-Coding (1,230 test samples), Evo2+Qwen3-4B reaches 80.21% accuracy and 80.00% F1 for pathogenic/benign classification, and on VEP-Non-SNV (873 test samples), Evo2+Qwen3-1B leads with 88.20% accuracy and 89.91% F1. GRPO reinforcement learning provides an additional ~3% accuracy boost on the 4B backbone (95.17% → 98.28%) but yields mixed results on the 1.7B backbone (F1 actually declines from 81.46% to 75.06%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

BIOREASON outperforms all single-modality baselines across every benchmark. On the KEGG reasoning task, the Evo2+Qwen3-4B(+GRPO) hybrid (98.28% acc) beats the best DNA-only model Evo2-1B (88.28%) by 10 points and the best LLM-only model Qwen3-4B (90.00%) by 8.3 points. On VEP-Coding, Evo2+Qwen3-4B (80.21% acc, 80.00% F1) surpasses Evo2-1B alone (70.07%, 49.19% F1) by 10.1 accuracy points and Qwen3-4B alone (48.99%, 39.58% F1) by 31.2 points, indicating that LLMs treating DNA as plain text are particularly weak on variant classification. On VEP-Non-SNV, Evo2+Qwen3-1.7B (88.20% acc, 89.91% F1) leads over Evo2-1B (76.17%, 66.51%) and Qwen3-1.7B (70.67%, 76.21%). In the supplementary chromatin accessibility experiment, Enformer+Qwen3-4B (33.70% macro F1) nearly doubles standalone Enformer (17.18%) and also exceeds Qwen3-4B alone (18.96%), confirming the multimodal gain generalizes to supervised DNA encoders.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the multimodal DNA-language integration initiated by ChatNT [35] and GeneGPT [19] by being the first to jointly train a DNA foundation model (Evo2 or Nucleotide Transformer) with an LLM backbone (Qwen3) specifically for multi-step biological reasoning, rather than simple classification or retrieval. The training methodology directly builds on the GRPO/Dr. GRPO reinforcement learning framework from DeepSeekMath [36] and the R1-Zero analysis [27], adapting the group-relative advantage estimation and clipped surrogate objective to a domain-specific composite reward (correctness + conciseness + format). Architecturally, the key departure from prior DNA-LLM hybrids is the use of a learnable linear projection from DNA embedding space to LLM token space combined with unified RoPE positional encoding, enabling the LLM to attend over genomic and textual tokens as a single sequence rather than treating DNA as a separate modality block.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BIOREASON addresses a genuine gap: DNA foundation models produce powerful but opaque embeddings, while LLMs cannot natively process nucleotide sequences, and no prior system was trained to generate step-by-step mechanistic reasoning from variant to disease. The KEGG-derived reasoning benchmark (1,449 entries, 37 diseases) is a useful new evaluation resource for multi-step biological reasoning. However, several limitations temper the impact: the 2048-token DNA truncation omits distal regulatory context, the KEGG test set is small (290 points), results are from single deterministic runs without error bars, and the reliance on curated KEGG annotations introduces coverage bias toward well-characterized pathways. Open questions include whether the architecture scales to whole-genome inputs, whether the reasoning traces are genuinely mechanistic or pattern-matched, and how the approach generalizes to RNA/protein modalities or less-curated genomic regions.
