## Problem

Existing public text-to-video datasets are limited to 720p resolution with simple captions, making them inadequate for generating Ultra-High Definition (4K/8K) videos with sharp details and precise semantic control. Two core challenges remain unsolved: resolution scalability (models trained on low-res data produce artifacts and blurriness when extrapolated to UHD) and semantic granularity (datasets lack structured annotations describing camera motion, lighting, style, and atmosphere needed for fine-grained generation control).

## Input / Output

The UltraWan model takes a text prompt as input, constructed via a random caption sampling strategy that selects one of three description types (Brief, Detailed, or Summarized) plus one supplementary category from seven remaining structured fields, yielding prompts averaging 824 words. It natively outputs 1K videos at 1088×1920×81 frames or 4K videos at 2160×3840×29 frames. The UltraVideo dataset itself provides 42K short clips (3–10 s) and 17K long clips (≥10 s) at native 4K/8K resolution with up to 60 FPS, each annotated with 10 structured caption categories.

## Method

The paper contributes a four-stage automated curation pipeline for UltraVideo: (1) collecting 4K/8K clips from YouTube filtered by resolution, frame rate, and user engagement; (2) statistical filtering removing videos with excessive text (PaddleOCR), black borders, abnormal exposure, or low saturation; (3) model-based purification using Qwen2.5-VL-72B for 16 low-quality attribute checks, VTSS aesthetic scoring, RAFT motion scoring, and VideoCLIP-XL video-caption consistency; and (4) generating 9 structured captions via Qwen2.5-VL-72B plus a summarized description via Qwen3-4B. For generation, UltraWan extends Wan-T2V-1.3B (a DiT-based diffusion model) using LoRA fine-tuning (rank 64 for 1K, rank 16 for 4K) on QKV self-attention and feedforward layers, trained for one epoch with AdamW on H20 GPUs. A key design choice is the random caption sampling strategy during training, which exposes the model to diverse prompt granularities to improve text controllability without architectural modifications.

## Results

On VBench (sampled ~96 prompts), UltraWan-1K (LoRA) achieves 97.27% Subject Consistency, 62.5% Aesthetic Quality, 67.74% Imaging Quality, and 82.29% Object Class accuracy, substantially outperforming the native Wan-T2V-1.3B-1K extrapolation (95.86%, 54.82%, 55.12%, 33.33% respectively). UltraWan-4K (LoRA) attains the highest Imaging Quality at 71.61% among all tested configurations. In a 10-subject human preference study, UltraWan-1K is preferred over the official Wan-T2V-1.3B in 81.10% of cases on video quality aesthetics, 55.70% on temporal stability, and 54.50% on text consistency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares UltraWan-1K (LoRA) and UltraWan-4K (LoRA) against the native Wan-T2V-1.3B at both 480p and 1K resolutions. UltraWan-1K (LoRA) decisively outperforms the direct 1K extrapolation of Wan-T2V-1.3B on Object Class (82.29% vs. 33.33%) and Aesthetic Quality (62.50% vs. 54.82%), while also improving Subject Consistency (97.27% vs. 95.86%). Against the official 480p Wan-T2V-1.3B, UltraWan-1K (LoRA) matches or exceeds it on Subject Consistency (97.27% vs. 96.11%) and Object Class (82.29% vs. 66.66%), though it trails slightly on Aesthetic Quality (62.50% vs. 65.83%). UltraWan-4K (LoRA) achieves the best Imaging Quality (71.61%) and Spatial Relationship (100%) scores, confirming that native 4K training yields superior pixel-level fidelity, but its reduced frame count (29 vs. 81) limits temporal metrics. In the human preference study, UltraWan-1K dominates on video quality aesthetics (81.10% preference) while showing modest advantages on temporal stability (55.70%) and text consistency (54.50%), confirming that the structured captions in UltraVideo translate into perceptible generation quality gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Wan-T2V-1.3B DiT-based video generation model to native 1K/4K resolution via LoRA fine-tuning, departing from prior T2V work that operates at 720p or 1080p. On the data side, it builds on the curation philosophies of Koala-36M (VTSS scoring, structured captions) and MiraData (long-duration structured annotations) but pushes resolution to 4K/8K and caption granularity to 10 structured categories averaging 824 words, replacing closed-source GPT-4V with the open-source Qwen2.5-VL-72B for reproducibility. Conceptually, it inverts the prevailing 'quantity over quality' paradigm of million-scale 720p datasets in favor of a 42K-sample, multi-stage-filtered UHD collection, positioning data curation as the primary lever for high-resolution generation rather than architectural changes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

UltraVideo is the first public UHD-4K/8K T2V dataset, filling a clear gap for researchers targeting movie-grade or 4K short-video generation where existing 720p datasets are insufficient. The demonstration that 42K carefully curated samples with rich structured captions can meaningfully improve a small (1.3B) model's high-resolution output suggests a quality-over-quantity training recipe that may transfer to larger models. Key limitations temper the impact: experiments are confined to Wan-T2V-1.3B, the 4K model is restricted to 29 frames due to VRAM constraints, and several VBench metrics prove unreliable at high resolution. Open questions include whether the curation pipeline and LoRA strategy scale to multi-billion-parameter models, whether 8K generation is practically achievable, and how to address the noted risks of AI-generated UHD content for misinformation.
