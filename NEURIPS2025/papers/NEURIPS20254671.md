## Problem

Existing 360° video generation methods require panoramic optical flow or high-quality panoramic images as guidance, which are difficult for end users to obtain. Standard video outpainting methods fail to handle the perspective-to-360° conversion due to the large distribution gap between panoramic and perspective videos and the continuously changing video masks caused by varying camera poses across frames. This paper introduces the first framework to lift standard perspective videos into high-quality 360° equirectangular videos with rich, structured motion.

## Input / Output

The method takes a perspective video clip with narrow FOV (the anchor video) and a text prompt as input. The anchor video is projected onto a 360°×180° equirectangular canvas via P2E mapping, and the masked region is additionally encoded by SAM's visual encoder to provide fine-grained spatial guidance. The output is a full 360° equirectangular video at 512×1024 resolution (32 frames), replicating the anchor's appearance and motion while synthesizing the surrounding 360° content. The input video may have arbitrary per-frame camera rotations (yaw, pitch, roll), which are estimated at inference via MonST3R.

## Method

Imagine360 employs a dual-branch video denoising U-Net with a global panorama branch and a local perspective branch (20 views via P2E icosahedron projection), linked by cross-domain spherical attention with a novel antipodal mask that highlights reversed translational motion between opposite hemispheres. A resource-friendly fine-tuning strategy applies LoRA to spatial attention layers while fully fine-tuning only the panorama motion module, preserving the perspective generative prior. Rotation-aware designs—random Euler-angle sampling during training and MonST3R-based pose estimation at inference—handle diverse in-the-wild camera trajectories. The model is trained on the newly curated YouTube360 dataset (9,558 five-second clips at 20 fps) with structured motion, using AnimateDiff initialized from Stable Diffusion v2.1 as the backbone.

## Results

On the main benchmark (Table 1), Imagine360 achieves the best scores across all metrics: VBench IQ 0.7372 and AQ 0.5722 (vs. 0.6257/0.5211 for the next-best Follow-Your-Canvas), EPE 2.5583 (vs. 3.1767 for Follow-Your-Canvas and 3.1904 for 360DVD), and OmniFVD 204.0 (vs. 285.9 for AnimateDiff+LatentLab360 and 747.4 for 360DVD). In the user study (26 raters), Imagine360 ranks first on all three dimensions: Graphics Quality 3.68, Structure Plausibility 3.73, and Temporal Coherence 3.39, outperforming the runner-up Follow-Your-Canvas (2.77/2.13/3.04). The method also achieves the best Subject Consistency (0.9649) and matches the top Motion Smoothness (0.9866).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Imagine360 outperforms all three baselines on every reported metric. Against AnimateDiff+LatentLab360 (image-to-video), it improves IQ by +0.112, EPE by −0.98, and OmniFVD by −81.9, while also achieving substantially higher user-study rankings (GQ 3.68 vs. 1.21). Against Follow-Your-Canvas (video-to-video outpainting), the gains are IQ +0.112, EPE −0.62, and OmniFVD −111.4; notably, Follow-Your-Canvas is the closest competitor on user-study Temporal Coherence (3.04 vs. 3.39) but still trails on all other dimensions. Against 360DVD (text-to-video with GT optical flow), the advantage is largest on OmniFVD (204.0 vs. 747.4, a 3.7× reduction) and IQ (+0.187), reflecting 360DVD's tendency to produce blurred, low-detail panoramas. The paper notes that 360DVD benefits from ground-truth optical flow input, yet still lags behind Imagine360's video-conditioned generation on both motion accuracy (EPE) and overall 360° quality (OmniFVD).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the dual-branch panorama generation paradigm from image-level work (Taming Stable Diffusion for 360° [42]) into the video domain, combining it with the space-time disentangled architecture of AnimateDiff [10] and the inpainting input formulation of Follow-Your-Canvas [2]. It departs from prior video outpainting (which assumes fixed perspective masks) and text/image-guided 360° video generation (360DVD, 4K4DGen) by introducing a video-anchor-conditioned task where the mask geometry changes across frames due to camera motion. The antipodal mask in cross-domain spherical attention is a novel mechanism not present in any prior panorama or outpainting work, specifically targeting the reversed translational motion unique to spherical geometry.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Imagine360 makes 360° video creation accessible from ordinary smartphone footage or AI-generated clips, removing the need for panoramic cameras or pre-computed optical flow. The YouTube360 dataset (9.5K curated clips) addresses a real data bottleneck in 360° video research, and the rotation-aware design makes the system practical for in-the-wild inputs. Key limitations include reliance on external camera pose estimation (MonST3R), which degrades on rapid rotations, and relatively slow inference (~6 min for 32 frames). Open questions include whether the framework can be made end-to-end (integrating pose estimation), whether it scales to longer sequences, and whether the antipodal mask generalizes to non-rigid scene motion.
