## Problem

Existing time series forecasting models adopt a Uniform Distribution Modeling strategy, treating all patches as samples from a single underlying distribution. However, real-world time series exhibit heterogeneous pattern evolution across segments—seasonal variations, regime changes, and contextual shifts—causing distribution shifts between patches that degrade generalization. This paper addresses the gap by explicitly modeling patch-level distribution shifts through pattern-specific expert routing rather than forcing a single predictive function over all segments.

## Input / Output

The model takes a multivariate time series window X ∈ R^{L×C} (L=96 for most datasets, L=104 for ILI; C ranges from 7 to 862 features) and divides it into overlapping patches of length P with stride S, processed channel-independently. It produces a future series Ŷ ∈ R^{H×C} where H ∈ {96, 192, 336, 720} (or {24, 36, 48, 60} for ILI). The final prediction is formed by concatenating time-domain and frequency-domain expert outputs (yielding R^{C×N×2D}) and applying a linear projection, so the architecture inherently fuses dual-domain information at the output stage.

## Method

TFPS comprises three components operating in parallel time and frequency branches. The Dual-Domain Encoder (DDE) extracts temporal features via a standard patch-based Transformer with multi-head attention, and frequency features by replacing the attention sublayer with a 2D Fast Fourier Transform over the patch and hidden dimensions. The Pattern Identifier (PI) performs subspace clustering: it learns K subspace bases with orthogonality and differentiation constraints, computes a subspace affinity matrix measuring each patch's membership in each subspace, refines it to sharpen confident assignments, and minimizes a KL-divergence clustering loss. The Mixture of Pattern Experts (MoPE) contains K lightweight MLP experts; a gating network selects the top-k experts per patch based on the PI's cluster scores and aggregates their outputs via weighted summation. Training combines MSE forecasting loss with the PI's subspace regularization in both domains, enabling the model to simultaneously learn accurate predictions and discover interpretable pattern groupings (e.g., downward trends vs. parabolic trends) without any external labels.

## Results

TFPS achieves top-1 MSE performance in 57 out of 72 experimental configurations across 9 datasets and 4 forecasting horizons. The overall improvement is 9.5% in MSE and 6.4% in MAE over time-domain methods, 16.9% in MSE and 12.4% in MAE over frequency-domain methods, and 5.2% in MSE over time-frequency methods. The largest gains appear on datasets with severe distribution shift: ILI (39.8–43.6% MSE improvement), Traffic (15.1–21.1%), Weather (4.1–15.6%), and Electricity (0.2–14.6%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TFPS outperforms all 8 standard baselines (TSLANet, FITS, iTransformer, TFDNet-IK, PatchTST, TimesNet, DLinear, FEDformer) on the majority of 72 dataset-horizon configurations, with the most dramatic wins on ILI (e.g., 1.349 vs. 1.749 MSE for TSLANet at H=24, a 40.9% improvement) and Traffic (0.427 vs. 0.475 for TSLANet at H=96, 21.1% improvement). Against the three foundation models (AutoTimes, Moment, Timer), TFPS wins on 5 of 6 datasets in Table 3, with notable margins on ETTh2 (10.9% MSE improvement) and ETTm2 (7.3%), though it trails Timer slightly on Traffic (−3.6%). Compared to normalization-based methods (SIN, SAN, Dish-TS, RevIN applied to DLinear), TFPS consistently achieves lower MSE across all five tested datasets (1.5–5.3% improvement), confirming that pattern-specific expert modeling captures distributional heterogeneity more effectively than distributional alignment alone. TFPS's relative advantage shrinks on datasets with mild drift (Exchange H=720, ETTh1 H=96), where the IMP is negative or near zero, suggesting the pattern-specific mechanism is most beneficial when concept drift is pronounced.

## Relation to Prior Work

TFPS builds directly on the patch-based Transformer paradigm introduced by PatchTST, extending it with a dual-domain encoder that adds a Fourier sublayer (inspired by FNet) alongside the standard self-attention branch. The Mixture of Pattern Experts module adapts the MoE architecture from NLP (Shazeer et al., Switch Transformer, GLaM) to time series, but departs from standard MoE by using subspace clustering (borrowed from metric learning) to drive expert assignment rather than learned gating on raw features. The paper positions itself against the Uniform Distribution Modeling assumption prevalent in PatchTST, iTransformer, and TimesNet, and contrasts with normalization-based non-stationarity handlers (RevIN, SIN, SAN, Dish-TS) by arguing that statistical re-centering smooths away meaningful structural variation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The pattern-specific expert framework offers a principled alternative to normalization for handling concept drift, preserving intrinsic non-stationarity while adapting predictions to local regime changes. A key open question, acknowledged by the authors, is how the fixed set of K experts handles the emergence of entirely novel patterns (e.g., unforeseen epidemics) that were not represented in training clusters. The method's advantage is dataset-dependent—gains are largest on high-drift series (Weather, ILI, Electricity) but can be negative on low-drift data (Traffic, Exchange at H=720), suggesting the clustering overhead is not always justified. Future work on dynamic expert creation or online pattern discovery would address this limitation.
