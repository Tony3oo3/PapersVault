## Problem

Existing federated LoRA methods suffer from aggregation discrepancies (averaging low-rank matrices does not equal the low-rank decomposition of averaged full updates) and assume clean client data. In practice, federated clients hold noisy labels, and LoRA's constrained parameterization amplifies label-noise bias through matrix multiplication, causing cascading degradation that full fine-tuning does not exhibit. No prior method jointly addresses both the aggregation bias and the heightened noise vulnerability specific to federated PEFT.

## Input / Output

Each client receives a frozen ViT-B backbone (ImageNet-21K pretrained, 224×224 input, 16×16 patches), the current global LoRA matrices (rank r=4), and a small public proxy dataset (256 clean samples plus a symflip-noised copy) for sensitivity and importance estimation. Clients fine-tune only the LoRA matrices on their private noisy CIFAR-100 partition (non-IID, Dirichlet β=0.5) and upload a single updated low-rank matrix per round. The server aggregates and broadcasts back one matrix, halving communication versus standard FedLR. The final model outputs 100-class labels (CIFAR-100) or 3-class NLI labels (MNLI with RoBERTa-Base).

## Method

RFedLR combines two mechanisms. First, Sensitivity-Aware Robust Tuning (SRT) computes per-parameter noise sensitivity by contrasting gradients on clean versus noisy proxy data, normalizes scores layer-wise, and applies a binary mask that allows only the top-τ (20%) most sensitive parameters to receive gradient updates during local fine-tuning (forward pass remains full). Second, Adaptive Federated LoRA Aggregation (AFLA) computes a hybrid importance score I = F/(S+ε) combining empirical Fisher Information (on clean proxy data) and noise sensitivity, then selects only the higher-importance LoRA matrix (A or B) to train in each round, freezing the other. Aggregation weights each client by a convex combination of its matrix importance and data scale (λ=0.4), and the server transmits back a single updated matrix, reducing communication overhead by half while suppressing noise propagation across rounds.

## Results

On CIFAR-100 with ViT-B under 40% pairflip label noise, RFedLR achieves 67.08% average test accuracy, surpassing the best baseline RoLoRA (56.93%) by 10.15%. Under 40% symflip noise, RFedLR reaches 84.64%, outperforming SLoRA (81.51%) by 3.13%. At the milder 20% noise rate, RFedLR attains 83.12% (pairflip) and 86.97% (symflip), leading all baselines by 6.64% and 2.42% respectively. In terms of parameter efficiency, RFedLR uses only 0.1754% of the trainable parameters required by Full Fine-Tuning, the fewest among all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RFedLR outperforms all eight baseline groups across every noise setting. Against the FedPEFTuning family (FedPF, FedBF, FedAP, FedLR) [13], RFedLR leads by 10.46–17.0% under 40% pairflip noise and by 5.9–11.9% under 40% symflip noise. Among LoRA-based federated methods, RFedLR surpasses SLoRA [14] by 10.31% (pairflip, μ=0.4) and 3.13% (symflip, μ=0.4), FFA-LoRA [15] by 12.30% and 7.23%, RoLoRA [16] by 10.15% and 3.33%, and FlexLoRA [32] by 10.50% and 4.93%. Against SOTA federated noise-robust methods adapted to the PEFT setting, RFedLR outperforms FedCorr [54] by 11.26% (pairflip) and 4.84% (symflip), RHFL [53] by 9.64% and 0.08%, and FedFixer [56] by 11.47% and 6.82% at μ=0.4. The largest margin is observed under structured pairflip noise at high rates, where the sensitivity-aware selective update and adaptive aggregation mechanisms most effectively counter noise amplification.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: federated parameter-efficient fine-tuning (FedLR, SLoRA, FFA-LoRA, RoLoRA, FlexLoRA) and label-noise-robust federated learning (RHFL, FedCorr, FedFixer). It departs from existing federated LoRA methods by replacing their rigid update schedules (FFA-LoRA's permanent freezing, RoLoRA's alternating freezing) with an adaptive, importance-driven matrix selection, and departs from existing robust FL methods by operating in the constrained PEFT regime rather than full-parameter training. The key conceptual contribution is identifying that LoRA's low-rank factorization structurally amplifies label noise through the δB·δA cross-term in the product, a vulnerability absent in full fine-tuning and unaddressed by prior federated PEFT work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As large pre-trained models are increasingly deployed on resource-constrained edge devices via federated learning, the combination of parameter efficiency and noise robustness becomes essential for real-world deployments where label quality is inherently imperfect. The 10%+ accuracy gap over the best baseline under 40% pairflip noise demonstrates that the noise amplification in low-rank decomposition is a practically significant problem, not merely a theoretical concern. Key limitations include the dependence on a public proxy dataset for sensitivity estimation and the additional computational overhead of gradient-based sensitivity and Fisher information computation, both of which constrain deployment in settings where no clean reference data is available. Future work on proxy-data-free sensitivity estimation and more efficient importance measurement would broaden the method's applicability.
