## Problem

Naive finetuning of time series foundation models (TSFMs) on downstream tasks is suboptimal because it restricts learning to the original sampling scale, overlooking latent temporal patterns at coarser scales and underutilizing the multi-scale forecasting capabilities acquired during pretraining. The paper formalizes this limitation through a causal lens, showing that scale acts as a confounder between the input context and the model's activated knowledge, causing naive finetuning to learn spurious correlations rather than true causal effects.

## Input / Output

The method takes a univariate time series context window X ∈ R^{C×D} and horizon window Y ∈ R^{H×D}, which are downsampled into K+1 scales (factors 1, 2, …, 2^K) via non-overlapping average pooling before being independently patch-tokenized at each scale's native resolution. The output is a weighted sum of per-scale predictions upsampled back to the original temporal resolution; for probabilistic forecasting (MOIRAI), each scale produces mixture-distribution parameters from which point or distributional forecasts are derived. The learnable mixing weights correspond to the prior P(s) in the backdoor adjustment formula.

## Method

MSFT implements backdoor adjustment for the scale confounder by stratifying it across multiple downsampled scales and computing P(Y|do(X)) = Σ_s P(Y|X, S=s, M=g(X,s))P(s). To activate scale-specific knowledge g(X,s), it freezes all pretrained parameters and injects per-scale linear adapters on the input projection and per-scale LoRA modules on the attention layers. To eliminate cross-scale attention bias from misaligned time indices, it decouples dependency modeling into in-scale masked self-attention (tokens attend only within their own scale) and cross-scale aggregators that fuse adjacent scales bidirectionally via linear projections followed by Repeat (coarse-to-fine) or AvgPool (fine-to-coarse) operations. Finally, a softmax over learnable parameters assigns mixing weights to each scale's prediction, serving as the P(s) term and mitigating overfitting to the original scale.

## Results

On long sequence forecasting (LSF), MSFT consistently delivers the best finetuned results across all three encoder-based TSFMs (MOIRAI, MOMENT, UNITS) and all six datasets. For MOIRAI_base, MSFT achieves MSE of 0.332 (ETTm1), 0.247 (ETTm2), 0.407 (ETTh1), 0.352 (ETTh2), 0.169 (Electricity), and 0.213 (Weather), surpassing the best deep learning baseline (SimpleTM) on 5 out of 6 datasets. On probabilistic forecasting, MOIRAI_base+MSFT achieves the best CRPS on all five datasets, with a 24.4% CRPS relative reduction on Solar (0.142 vs. 0.188) and 18.3% on Istanbul Traffic (0.098 vs. 0.120) compared to full finetuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MSFT outperforms all naive and parameter-efficient finetuning baselines (Full finetuning, Linear probing, Prompt tuning, LoRA, AdaLoRA) across every TSFM and dataset. Compared to the strongest deep learning baseline SimpleTM, MOIRAI_base+MSFT achieves lower MSE on ETTm1 (0.332 vs. 0.381), ETTm2 (0.247 vs. 0.275), ETTh1 (0.407 vs. 0.422), ETTh2 (0.352 vs. 0.353), and Weather (0.213 vs. 0.243), but is slightly behind on Electricity (0.169 vs. 0.166). Against TimeMixer, MSFT on MOIRAI_base wins on all six datasets, with the largest gap on Weather (0.213 vs. 0.240) and ETTm2 (0.247 vs. 0.275). On probabilistic forecasting, MOIRAI_base+MSFT dominates all baselines including TiDE, TFT, and PatchTST, achieving the best CRPS on all five datasets (e.g., 0.046 on Electricity vs. 0.048 for TiDE, 0.142 on Solar vs. 0.420 for TiDE). The improvement over full finetuning is most pronounced on minute-level datasets (ETTm1, ETTm2, Weather), where MSFT reduces MSE by 6.3–6.8% relative to full finetuning, consistent with the richer multi-scale patterns present at finer temporal resolutions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of two underexplored threads: TSFM finetuning (where prior work has largely settled on naive strategies like full finetuning, linear probing, or standard LoRA) and multi-scale time series modeling (where prior work like Pyraformer, MICN, Scaleformer, and TimeMixer designs multi-scale architectures from scratch rather than retrofitting them onto pretrained models). MSFT departs from both by introducing a causal (backdoor adjustment) framing that identifies sampling scale as a confounder between input context and activated model knowledge, then operationalizes this through scale-specific adapters, LoRA modules, in-scale attention masking, and cross-scale aggregators plugged into frozen encoder-based TSFMs (MOIRAI, MOMENT, UNITS). The method is explicitly general and plug-and-play, unlike prior TSFM-specific finetuning approaches (UniTS prompt tuning, in-context tuning for TimesFM) that are tied to particular architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: as TSFMs become the standard for forecasting, knowing how to effectively finetune them matters as much as pretraining, yet the field has largely ignored this. The causal perspective provides a principled, reusable lens for diagnosing why naive finetuning underperforms, and the multi-scale framework is a concrete, general remedy. Limitations include scope restricted to encoder-based TSFMs (decoder-based models like TimesFM and Timer are left as future work), modest and inconsistent gains across backbones (MOMENT shows smaller improvements due to fixed context-length pretraining), and the absence of error bars across runs. Open questions include whether the backdoor-adjustment framing generalizes to other confounders (e.g., domain shift, multivariate structure) and whether the approach transfers to autoregressive TSFMs.
