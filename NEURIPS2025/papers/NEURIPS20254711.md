## Problem

Tensor Parallelism (TP) for multi-GPU transformer training requires an all-reduce communication between MHA and MLP within every block, creating a significant bottleneck that degrades efficiency as GPU count grows or interconnect bandwidth drops. The paper asks whether the direct MHA-MLP connection within each block is strictly necessary, given that the residual path already accumulates attention outputs from prior blocks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254711/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard token sequences (max length 1024) for next-token prediction in a GPT-style autoregressive language model. The architectural modification is internal to the block: rather than each MLP consuming its own block's MHA output, it consumes the layer-normalized first-block MHA output combined with the current residual, so the external I/O contract (token in, next-token logits out) remains unchanged while the internal dataflow is restructured.

## Method

FAL (First Attentions Last) replaces each block's MHA-MLP connection with a redirected path from the first block's MHA output, formulated as MLP_i(LN(X_i) + LN(MHA_1(LN(X_1)))) instead of MLP_i(LN(X_i + MHA_i(LN(X_i)))). This eliminates the per-block all-reduce in TP (halving communication) and removes the data dependency between MHA and MLP, enabling their parallel kernel execution on a single GPU. The design is motivated by two empirical findings: CKA similarity shows MLP inputs are nearly identical across blocks despite varying MHA outputs, and gradient magnitude analysis reveals the first MHA output has 5-7× higher impact on final predictions than later blocks. FAL+ augments rather than replaces the original MHA-MLP connections by adding an additional LN-gated first-attention signal, achieving lower perplexity without sacrificing training speed.

## Results

On OpenWebText, FAL reduces GPT-2 774M perplexity from 17.75 to 17.55 while cutting training time from 13.2 days to 8.6 days (35% faster); at 1.5B scale, FAL lowers perplexity from 14.72 to 14.23 with training time reduced from 24.1 to 16.1 days. FAL+ achieves the lowest perplexity (17.24 / 14.12) at both scales without increasing training time over the baseline. On SuperGLUE zero-shot, FAL matches GPT-2 at 774M (48.5 avg) and surpasses it at 1.5B (56.9 vs 53.3 avg), while FAL+ leads at both scales (49.2 and 55.4). Multi-GPU training time is reduced by up to 43.1% on PCIe and 20.1% on NVLink; single-GPU throughput improves by up to 1.18×.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GPT-2 (the primary baseline), FAL wins on both perplexity (17.55 vs 17.75 at 774M; 14.23 vs 14.72 at 1.5B) and training time (35% reduction), while matching or exceeding SuperGLUE scores (48.5 tied at 774M; 56.9 vs 53.3 at 1.5B). FAL+ further improves perplexity (17.24 / 14.12) and SuperGLUE average (49.2 / 55.4) over GPT-2 at both scales, at the cost of matching baseline training time. Against the Parallel configuration (MHA and MLP sharing the same input, from [33–35]), FAL achieves lower perplexity (17.55 vs 17.80) and substantially higher SuperGLUE average (48.5 vs 45.9) at 774M with identical training time (8.6 days), showing that reusing the first attention is a strictly better parallelization strategy than discarding MHA-MLP connections entirely. Against lossy communication-reduction methods (QSGD gradient quantization and PowerSGD low-rank approximation), FAL reduces communication time by 49.4% versus 37.8% for the compression methods, while the compression methods degrade perplexity significantly; FAL actually improves perplexity over GPT-2, demonstrating a superior speed–accuracy trade-off.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the standard Pre-LN transformer architecture (GPT-2, LLaMA) and tensor parallelism (Megatron-LM), questioning a foundational design assumption: that each block's MLP must receive the most recent MHA output within that same block. It departs from prior parallel-block configurations (GPT-J, PaliGemma, 'Simplifying transformer blocks') that execute MHA and MLP simultaneously with the same input but degrade quality, by instead reusing the first attention output as a stable substitute signal. The motivation draws on the primacy-effect literature in attention analysis and the dense skip-connection idea, while the efficiency gains are positioned against lossy communication-reduction methods (gradient quantization, low-rank approximation) that sacrifice accuracy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 44% multi-GPU training time reduction and 1.18× single-GPU throughput improvement are practically significant for anyone scaling transformer training, especially on PCIe-based clusters where communication dominates. The finding that reusing the first attention not only preserves but improves model quality (lower perplexity, higher SuperGLUE) challenges the assumption that per-block MHA-MLP connections are irreplaceable, opening a connection-level design axis for future architecture search. Limitations include a small accuracy drop for shallow models (<1B) and the fact that FAL+ (the quality-focused variant) does not reduce training time. Open questions include whether dynamic gating or NAS can adaptively decide when to inject the first-attention signal, and whether the benefits persist at the 100B+ scale where the paper's largest model is 8.3B.
