## Problem

Most existing multimodal audio datasets provide only monaural recordings, discarding spatial cues such as interaural time/level differences and room reverberation patterns that are essential for immersive VR/AR experiences. The scarcity of real-recorded spatial audio datasets with synchronized video, 3D positional geometry, and fine-grained semantic annotations (phoneme boundaries, musical scores, lyrics) has fundamentally limited progress in spatial audio generation and understanding.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254882/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The dataset provides 484 hours of synchronized binaural (48 kHz WAV) and First-Order Ambisonic audio paired with exocentric/egocentric video (1080p, 24 fps), 3D source positions (static coordinates or UWB-tracked trajectories), and task-specific annotations including word/phoneme-level timestamps, MIDI scores, and event labels. Across the five benchmark tasks, inputs range from mono audio + source coordinates (spatialization), text + 3D position (spatial TTS), lyrics + score + position (spatial SVS), symbolic score + position (spatial music), to binaural/FOA waveforms with optional visual bounding boxes (SELD); outputs are binaural spatial waveforms or joint event-class + 3D-position predictions.

## Method

MRSAudio is constructed through a four-stage pipeline: scenario planning across four subsets (MRSLife, MRSSpeech, MRSSing, MRSMusic), recording with binaural dummy heads, Zoom H3-VR for FOA, GoPro for egocentric video, and UWB for dynamic source tracking, multi-tool annotation (WhisperX for ASR/diarization, MFA + Praat for phoneme alignment, ROSVOT for MIDI transcription, basic-pitch for music transcription, with expert manual refinement), and post-processing (segmentation, multimodal synchronization, face anonymization, 15% expert audit). For benchmarking, the paper establishes five tasks using state-of-the-art methods: BinauralGrad (diffusion-based) for audio spatialization, ISDrama for end-to-end spatial speech/singing/music generation, cascaded pipelines (CosyVoice/F5-TTS + spatialization, Rmssinger + spatialization, Make-An-Audio 2 + spatialization) as baselines, and ConvNet/Transformer architectures for sound event localization and detection. Evaluation combines objective metrics (W-L2, PESQ, CER, MCD, FAD, F20°, ER20°, LECD, LRCD) with subjective MOS-Q and MOS-P scores from expert listeners.

## Results

On the audio spatialization task, BinauralGrad trained on all MRSAudio subsets (ALL) achieves W-L2 of 0.305×10⁻³, A-L2 of 0.041, P-L2 of 0.801, and STFT loss of 1.681, substantially outperforming the DSP baseline (W-L2 1.691, P-L2 1.562) on waveform and phase fidelity, while achieving MOS-Q of 3.93 and MOS-P of 3.94 (vs. 3.89/3.75 for DSP). On spatial TTS, the Mono+SP pipeline achieves the lowest CER (2.56%) and highest speaker similarity (0.98), while ISDrama attains the best angular cosine (0.48). On spatial music generation, ISDrama(music) achieves the lowest FAD (2.45) and highest ANG Cos (0.53), outperforming Make-An-Audio 2+SP (FAD 4.39, ANG Cos 0.49).

## Baselines & Comparisons

BinauralGrad trained on MRSAudio outperforms the traditional DSP (RIR+HRTF) baseline on waveform fidelity (W-L2 0.305 vs. 1.691), phase accuracy (P-L2 0.801 vs. 1.562), and subjective spatial perception (MOS-P 3.94 vs. 3.75), though DSP retains an edge on PESQ (2.830 vs. 2.352) and STFT loss (1.246 vs. 1.681). In spatial TTS, the cascaded Mono+SP pipeline achieves the best CER (2.56%) and speaker similarity (0.98), while the end-to-end ISDrama model leads on angular spatial alignment (ANG Cos 0.48 vs. 0.44 for Mono+SP). For spatial singing, Mono+SP dominates on spectral fidelity (MCD 3.19, FFE 0.17) over Rmssinger+SP (MCD 3.85, FFE 0.23), and ISDrama(sing) edges out both on MOS-Q (3.86 vs. 3.84/3.65). In spatial music generation, ISDrama(music) achieves the best FAD (2.45) and ANG Cos (0.53), clearly surpassing Make-An-Audio 2+SP (FAD 4.39, ANG Cos 0.49), indicating that general-purpose audio generators struggle with structured symbolic inputs. For SELD, the Transformer with FOA input (no visual) achieves the lowest error rate (ER₂₀° = 0.99) and highest localization recall (LR_CD = 87.32), outperforming ConvNet baselines and showing that FOA's richer spatial representation benefits over binaural audio.

## Relation to Prior Work

MRSAudio positions itself against a landscape of spatial audio datasets that are either simulated (Spatial LibriSpeech), web-crawled without spatial labels (YT-Ambigen, Sphere360), or small-scale real recordings (FAIR-Play, STARSS23). It departs from prior work by providing 484 hours of real-world recorded binaural and FOA audio spanning four distinct domains (general sound, speech, singing, music) with the richest annotation set to date—synchronized video, 3D source trajectories via UWB tracking, phoneme-level alignments, lyrics, and symbolic scores. Methodologically, the benchmarks build on ISDrama for end-to-end spatial generation and BinauralGrad for diffusion-based spatialization, while leveraging existing TTS (CosyVoice, F5-TTS), SVS (Rmssinger), and music generation (Make-An-Audio 2) models as baselines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dataset addresses a fundamental bottleneck in spatial audio research: the scarcity of large-scale, real-recorded, multimodal spatial audio with fine-grained annotations. By establishing five benchmark tasks with standardized evaluation protocols, it enables reproducible comparison across spatial generation and understanding methods. Key limitations include the relatively small FOA subset (most tasks focus on binaural), underutilized visual modalities in current benchmarks, and single-source scenarios that may not generalize to complex multi-source real-world scenes. Open questions include whether the spatial cues learned from controlled recordings transfer to uncontrolled environments, and how to scale to multi-source, dynamic scenes with interacting sound events.
