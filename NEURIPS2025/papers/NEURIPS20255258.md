## Problem

Existing 3D hand reconstruction methods (S2Hand, AMVUR, XHand, UHM) produce blurry textures and lose fine geometric details when given low-resolution multi-view inputs, while prior multi-view super-resolution approaches (NeRF-SR, DiSR-NeRF, SRGS) are restricted to static objects at fixed scales and cannot handle articulated, pose-varying hands. No prior work simultaneously super-resolves hand images and reconstructs 3D geometry while enforcing both multi-view and pose consistency.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255258/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes low-resolution multi-view RGB hand images (20 views × 20 frames), MANO template surface normal maps, and hand joint positions, and produces high-resolution hand images at arbitrary upscaling factors (×4 through ×32) along with a detailed 3D hand mesh comprising over 49,000 vertices and 98,000 faces with per-vertex albedo and spherical-harmonic lighting. Unlike fixed-scale SR methods, GIIF generates outputs at continuous resolutions via coordinate-based queries, and the 3D mesh is animatable through LBS with learned skinning weights and vertex displacements.

## Method

SRHand introduces a Geometric-aware Implicit Image Function (GIIF) that extends LIIF by concatenating RDN-encoded LR image features with hourglass-encoded MANO normal map features, then mapping continuous pixel-coordinate queries to RGB via linear layers, trained with L1, LPIPS, and GAN losses. For 3D reconstruction, it builds on XHand's subdivided MANO mesh (3× subdivision) but adds a mean texture loss that forces the delta displacement module to learn geometric appearance rather than texture-driven color, disentangling wrinkles and nails from skin tone. The key differentiator is an adaptive fine-tuning loop: every t_fine steps, the SR module is re-optimized against the current 3D reconstruction using a consistency loss (penalizing vertex-query variation across views and poses), a wavelet frequency-maintenance loss (preserving high-frequency detail that consistency loss would otherwise smooth away), and a discriminator loss, shifting the SR distribution into a 3D-consistent space without requiring dense multi-view supervision.

## Results

On InterHand2.6M, SRHand (GIIF with adaptive fine-tuning + enhanced mesh) achieves PSNR 29.88, LPIPS 0.0362, and P2P distance 2.16 mm, outperforming all other configurations. On Goliath, the full method reaches PSNR 24.31, LPIPS 0.0813, and P2P 3.50 mm. In the 3D-consistency SR comparison (Table 2), SRHand achieves the lowest LPIPS at all upscaling factors (×4: 0.0475, ×8: 0.0487, ×16: 0.0566) while matching or exceeding SRGS in PSNR at ×8 and ×16. For pure image SR at ×16, GIIF attains PSNR 31.60 and LPIPS 0.0637, surpassing LIIF* (29.85/0.0996) and IDM* (21.49/0.0970).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest 3D reconstruction baseline (XHand [10]), SRHand improves PSNR from 27.36 to 29.88 (+2.52 dB) and reduces P2P from 4.32 mm to 2.16 mm (−50%) on InterHand2.6M, while also lowering LPIPS from 0.0691 to 0.0362. UHM [39] paired with GIIF fails catastrophically on geometry (P2P = 72.55 mm), confirming that its lower-resolution mesh cannot represent fine hand details. In the 3D-consistency SR comparison, SRHand outperforms SRGS [9] on LPIPS at every scale (0.0475 vs. 0.0731 at ×4; 0.0566 vs. 0.3872 at ×16) but trails SRGS on PSNR at ×4 (29.06 vs. 32.74), indicating SRGS preserves more low-frequency structure while SRHand captures finer perceptual detail. NeRF-SR [47] and DiSR-NeRF [26] are clearly inferior, with LPIPS values of 0.1410 and 0.1303 at ×4 versus SRHand's 0.0475. For standalone image SR at ×16, GIIF (31.60 PSNR / 0.0637 LPIPS) beats LIIF (25.76/0.1848), SwinIR (25.51/0.1552), Real-ESRGAN (22.39/0.2287), and IDM (14.58/0.3603), with the normal-map conditioning contributing roughly 4 dB over vanilla LIIF.

## Relation to Prior Work

SRHand sits at the intersection of two well-developed lines of work: implicit neural image representations for super-resolution (LIIF, LIIF-GAN) and explicit mesh-based hand avatar reconstruction (XHand, UHM, MANO-HD). It departs from prior multi-view SR methods (NeRF-SR, DiSR-NeRF, SRGS, SuperNeRF) that enforce only view consistency for static objects at fixed scales, by introducing pose-aware consistency for articulated hands and supporting arbitrary upscaling factors. The key architectural departure is the Geometric-aware Implicit Image Function (GIIF), which conditions the continuous image representation on MANO-derived normal maps, and the adaptive fine-tuning loop that iteratively aligns the SR module with the 3D reconstruction to eliminate multi-view/pose inconsistencies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Hands occupy less than 1.5% of full-body captures in VR/AR pipelines, making high-fidelity reconstruction from inherently low-resolution regions a practical bottleneck that prior methods have not addressed. SRHand's joint SR + 3D mesh formulation with pose consistency is the first to tackle this for articulated deformable objects, and its arbitrary-scale capability (validated up to ×32) offers flexibility that fixed-scale NeRF/GS-based SR methods lack. Open questions include whether the approach extends to single-view or sparse-view settings, whether it generalizes to other body parts (face, feet), and the computational cost of the iterative fine-tuning loop in real-time applications. The reliance on GT MANO parameters and multi-view inputs limits immediate deployment in unconstrained capture scenarios.
