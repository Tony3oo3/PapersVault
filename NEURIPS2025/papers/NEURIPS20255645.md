## Problem

Autonomous agentic AI systems that select among candidate images via vision-language models are vulnerable to cross-modal adversarial manipulation, yet existing attacks either require visible pixel perturbations, white-box model access, or privileged environment control. This paper asks whether an attacker can, under a realistic black-box setting with no model weights or gradients, produce a visually natural image that consistently redirects an agent's selection toward a specific candidate.

## Input / Output

The method takes a target image (encoded to a 512-d CLIP ViT-B/32 embedding), a set of n−1 unmodifiable competitor images, and an attacker-chosen guidance prompt p_pos that serves as a semantic proxy for a family of likely user queries (the actual user prompt p is unknown). It outputs a single adversarial image decoded from CLIP latent space via Stable Diffusion's VAE decoder, constrained to remain within a small LPIPS perceptual budget of the original target so it passes platform-level integrity checks. The optimization variable is the CLIP image embedding itself, not pixel values, and the final image is produced by modulating the common embedding component with a spatial layout mask before diffusion decoding.

## Method

TRAP performs iterative gradient-based optimization entirely in CLIP's shared embedding space, exploiting the fact that most VLMs rank images by cosine similarity to the prompt embedding. A Siamese semantic network decomposes each embedding into a common (semantic) branch and a distinctive (identity) branch; a composite loss combines (1) cosine alignment between the adversarial embedding and the guidance prompt embedding, (2) L2 preservation of the distinctive branch to prevent identity collapse, and (3) LPIPS perceptual similarity between the decoded candidate and the original image. A layout-aware spatial mask, generated by an MLP encoder-decoder and refined with DeepLabv3 foreground segmentation, restricts semantic edits to the primary subject region. The common branch, modulated by this mask, is fed to Stable Diffusion's VAE decoder conditioned on p_pos to produce the final adversarial image. The entire pipeline is black-box with respect to the target agent: no model weights, gradients, or internal architecture are required—only the ability to query the agent and observe its selection.

## Results

TRAP achieves an Attack Success Rate (ASR) of 100% on LLaVA-1.5-34B, Gemma3-8B, and Mistral-small-3.1-24B, 99% on Mistral-small-3.2-24B, 94% on CogVLM, and 63% on GPT-4o, starting from a challenging low-preference baseline of 0–21% ASR. The best embedding-space baseline (SA_AET) reaches at most 85% ASR (LLaVA) and drops to 12% on GPT-4o, while traditional pixel-level baselines (SPSA, Bandit) peak at 36% and 6% respectively. TRAP's advantage is most dramatic on closed-source GPT-4o (63% vs. 12% for SA_AET) and on the non-contrastive CogVLM architecture (94% vs. 42% for SA_AET).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TRAP dominates all five baselines across every evaluated model. Against the strongest embedding-space competitor SA_AET (Jia et al., 2025), TRAP gains 15 points on LLaVA (100% vs. 85%), 33 points on GPT-4o (63% vs. 12%), and 52 points on CogVLM (94% vs. 42%), demonstrating that its Siamese decomposition and layout-aware masking provide substantially better semantic steering than simple text-to-image embedding projection. SSA_CWA (Chen et al., 2024), which uses ensemble-based embedding attacks, is outperformed by 35 points on LLaVA (100% vs. 65%) and 55 points on GPT-4o (63% vs. 8%). Traditional black-box pixel-level methods—SPSA (Spall, 1987) and Bandit (Ilyas et al., 2018)—are largely ineffective, with SPSA peaking at 36% ASR on LLaVA and Bandit at just 6%, confirming that pixel-space perturbations cannot reliably shift semantic-level selection preferences. Even unoptimized Stable Diffusion generation (Rombach et al., 2022) without adversarial optimization achieves only 24% ASR on LLaVA, underscoring that the iterative CLIP-space optimization loop is the critical component of TRAP's success.

## Relation to Prior Work

TRAP builds on CLIP's shared vision-language embedding space and Stable Diffusion's latent generative pipeline, but departs from prior diffusion-based adversarial attacks (Wang et al. 2023, Dai et al. 2024, Liu et al. 2023b) that require fine-tuning or guiding the diffusion model's internal parameters. Unlike earlier agentic-system attacks (Yang et al. 2024, Wu et al. 2024, Liao et al. 2025) that assume access to environment internals or model weights, TRAP operates under a strict black-box constraint where the attacker only modifies their own input image. The Siamese semantic network that decomposes CLIP embeddings into common and distinctive components is a novel architectural choice that enables targeted semantic injection while preserving image identity, distinguishing TRAP from both pixel-level attacks (FGSM, PGD) and prior embedding-space methods (SSA_CWA, SA_AET).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TRAP exposes a practical, generalized vulnerability in deployed agentic AI systems: visually natural, semantically-guided images can consistently redirect selection decisions across both open-source and closed-source models, and standard defenses (Gaussian noise, caption filters, adversarial training) fail to mitigate the attack. The 63% transfer rate to GPT-4o and 94% to non-contrastive CogVLM suggest the vulnerability is architectural rather than model-specific, raising urgent questions about embedding-level robustness criteria for multimodal agents. Open challenges include developing defenses that operate in semantic rather than pixel space, understanding whether future architectures that move beyond contrastive reasoning will close this gap, and reducing the ~520-second per-sample optimization cost to enable real-time or large-scale deployment.
