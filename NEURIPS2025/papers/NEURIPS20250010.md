## Problem

When serving a single base LLM with multiple LoRA adapters under tensor parallelism, S-LoRA's sharding strategy requires additional all-gather and all-reduce operations beyond those needed by the base model. Although theoretically small for low-rank adapters, this communication overhead is significant in practice—especially during decoding where data sizes are tiny and kernel launch latency dominates. No prior approach shards LoRA factors in a TP-aligned way that avoids all extra inter-device communication.

## Input / Output

At inference, the system consumes a text prompt (input tokens), BD-LoRA adapter weights stored as dense per-device blocks (zeros in the block-diagonal matrices are never materialized), and base model weights already sharded across N devices per the Megatron-LM strategy. The output is generated text (output tokens) produced token-by-token. A key constraint is that the TP degree N must be fixed at training time, since it determines the block-diagonal structure (each device holds blocks of size r/N × d_I/N for B1 and d_I/N × r/N for A2), and adapters cannot be converted from standard LoRA.

## Method

BD-LoRA constrains the LoRA factors B1 and A2 to be block-diagonal matrices, so that a column-sharded input X multiplied by a block-diagonal W yields a column-sharded output with no inter-device communication. Concretely, A1 is column-sharded and B1 is block-diagonal (each device holds only its diagonal block), and A2 is block-diagonal and B2 is row-sharded, allowing every device to independently compute its shard of XAB and add it to the local base-model result before the single standard Megatron all-reduce. This is equivalent to attaching independent LoRA adapters of rank r/N to each weight shard, which simplifies training: the model architecture is rewritten so each shard becomes a separate linear layer with its own standard LoRA adapter, trained with rsLoRA scaling α√N/√r. The approach eliminates all S-LoRA-specific communication (two all-gathers and one all-reduce per MLP module; three all-gathers and one all-reduce per attention module) while, for a matched number of effective (non-zero) parameters, achieving downstream performance comparable to or slightly better than standard LoRA.

## Results

BD-LoRA achieves downstream performance comparable to standard LoRA at matched parameter counts across OpenOrca (perplexity) and eight GLUE tasks (accuracy/correlation), often slightly outperforming LoRA (e.g., 76.6 avg GLUE at 289.4M params vs. 76.4 at 671.1M params for Llama-3.1-8B). In runtime, BD-LoRA delivers up to 1.79× E2E speedup over S-LoRA with 0.87× the adapter parameters for Llama-3.1-70B on 8 A100 GPUs, and up to 1.63× with 0.86× parameters for Llama-3.1-8B. At matched downstream performance, BD-LoRA achieves 1.27×–1.51× E2E speedup over S-LoRA for Llama-3.1-8B, strictly Pareto-dominating S-LoRA in performance-vs-latency trade-off plots.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against S-LoRA [28], BD-LoRA wins decisively on runtime: it eliminates all additional communication operations (all-gather and all-reduce) required by S-LoRA's tensor parallel sharding, yielding 1.23×–1.79× E2E speedup depending on model size, rank, and hardware, while using 0.86×–1.74× the adapter parameters. The speedup is largest during decoding (where communication startup latency dominates) and for medium-to-large ranks. Against standard LoRA [10], BD-LoRA matches or slightly exceeds downstream performance at similar effective parameter counts across all nine evaluation datasets (OpenOrca + 8 GLUE tasks), with confidence intervals heavily overlapping; in several settings BD-LoRA actually outperforms LoRA (e.g., 76.6 vs. 76.4 GLUE average for Llama-3.1-8B at comparable params). The block-diagonality constraint on B₁ and A₂ reduces expressiveness but is compensated by increasing rank, and the net effect is no measurable performance degradation. BD-LoRA also outperforms the NFS-LoRA baseline (replicated adapters) whenever rank is not very small, since NFS-LoRA's N× memory and compute overhead grows with rank and batch size.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LoRA parameter-efficient fine-tuning framework (Hu et al., ICLR 2022) by imposing a block-diagonal structural constraint on specific adapter factors, enabling a communication-free tensor parallel sharding strategy for multi-LoRA serving. It directly addresses a limitation of S-LoRA (Sheng et al., MLSys 2024), whose TP strategy introduces additional all-gather and all-reduce operations on top of the base model's Megatron-LM communication. The approach also adopts rsLoRA's rank-stabilized scaling (Kalajdzievski, 2023) and is conceptually related to Ladder-Residual (Zhang et al., 2025), which similarly eliminates communication through architectural modification, though BD-LoRA targets the adapter rather than the backbone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BD-LoRA offers a simple, drop-in architectural change that eliminates a real and measurable communication bottleneck in production multi-LoRA serving (up to 1.79× end-to-end speedup on 8×A100), while preserving downstream performance at matched parameter counts. The finding that the block-diagonal constraint does not degrade accuracy—and sometimes improves it—is practically important because it removes the main objection to adopting the method. Key limitations include the need to know the TP degree at training time and the inability to convert existing standard LoRA adapters to BD-LoRA without retraining, both of which the authors flag as open problems. The method's compatibility with Q-LoRA and its integration into vLLM and Huggingface PEFT suggest near-term practical adoption.
