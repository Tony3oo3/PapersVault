## Problem

The classical nonparametric maximum likelihood estimator (NPMLE) for heteroscedastic Gaussian location mixtures suffers from two fundamental limitations: Tweedie's one-step posterior mean denoising exhibits over-shrinkage bias that distorts latent structure, and the infinite-dimensional nonconvex optimization is computationally intractable in high dimensions. No existing score-based diffusion framework handles the heteroscedastic, nonparametric empirical Bayes setting with explicit recovery of the latent prior distribution.

## Input / Output

The method takes n noisy observations X_i ∈ R^d, each corrupted by an independent Gaussian noise with a known, observation-specific positive-definite covariance Σ_i (heteroscedastic across i). It produces an estimated latent prior Ĝ (parameterized as a continuous normalizing flow or discrete mixture), denoised latent signals θ̂_i obtained by integrating a reverse-time ODE from t=1 to t=0, and time-indexed score function estimates. Unlike supervised denoising, no clean ground-truth data is available; all inference is self-supervised from the noisy observations and their known noise structure alone.

## Method

The key insight is reinterpreting each noisy observation X_i as a time-1 marginal snapshot from a zero-drift forward SDE dX_i^(t) = g_i(t)Σ_i^{1/2}dB_t initialized at the unknown latent prior G*, where each observation has its own diffusion coefficient g_i(t) calibrated so that α_i(1)=1. This stochastic-process view induces a denoising score matching objective that explicitly models the prior G (G-modeling) rather than parameterizing the score with a neural network, and a multi-step reverse-time ODE denoising procedure that generalizes Tweedie's single-step formula to iterative Langevin-type updates, mitigating over-shrinkage. The prior G is learned via normalizing flows (continuous) or discrete atom mixtures, with the score matching loss computed through conditional perturbations of the noisy observations. Theoretically, the framework achieves a near-parametric score estimation rate of polylog(n)/n under compact support and well-conditioned covariance assumptions, improving upon prior diffusion-based mixture results that yield only O((log n)^{-1/p}) convergence.

## Results

On the synthetic circle and letter-A datasets (d=16, n=20,000), SDGM-C achieves Fisher divergence of 1.27 and 1.23, squared Wasserstein-2 distance of 2.07 and 1.68, and denoising MSE of 1.13 and 1.32, respectively, substantially outperforming NPMLE-N (FD 46.82/34.88, W₂² 14.43/12.41, MSE 3.57/3.04) and PEM (FD 4.71/3.78, W₂² 4.52/4.09, MSE 1.26/1.35). The hybrid variant SDGM-P further reduces FD to 0.68 (circle) and 0.49 (letter A) with W₂² of 1.07 and 1.25. On the real 19-dimensional APOGEE astronomy dataset (n=27,238 stars), SDGM-C reveals sharpened astrochemically significant manifold structures that NPMLE-based denoising fails to recover.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SDGM-C dominates both NPMLE baselines across all four metrics on both synthetic datasets. Against NPMLE-N (Lashkari & Golland, 2007), SDGM-C reduces Fisher divergence by ~37× on the circle (1.27 vs 46.82) and ~28× on the letter A (1.23 vs 34.88), and cuts denoising MSE by ~3× (1.13 vs 3.57 on circle). Against PEM (Zhang et al., 2022), SDGM-C improves FD by ~3.7× (1.27 vs 4.71) and W₂² by ~2.2× (2.07 vs 4.52) on the circle, with similar margins on the letter A. The hybrid SDGM-P variant, which uses SDGM-C to initialize support points before a convex NPMLE-N refinement, achieves the best FD and W₂² on both datasets (0.68/0.49 FD, 1.07/1.25 W₂²) but slightly worse MSE than SDGM-C on the circle (1.29 vs 1.13), suggesting a trade-off between prior estimation quality and denoising fidelity. On the 19-dimensional APOGEE data, NPMLE-based methods produce unreliable, over-shrunk estimates, while SDGM-C clearly recovers fusiform and thin-manifold structures in chemical abundance space.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of classical nonparametric empirical Bayes (NPMLE, Tweedie's formula) and modern score-based diffusion modeling. It departs from the NPMLE line (Robbins, Jiang-Zhang, Saha-Guntuboyina, Soloff et al.) by replacing the infinite-dimensional likelihood maximization with a score-matching objective that explicitly models the latent prior G, and from the diffusion-for-mixtures line (Gatmiry et al., Chen et al., Shah et al.) by retaining explicit G-modeling rather than directly parametrizing the score with neural networks. The closest prior work is Ghosh et al. (2025), which first combined score matching with G-modeling but only in the univariate homoscedastic case; this paper extends that framework to multivariate heteroscedastic Gaussian location mixtures and introduces a multi-step ODE denoising procedure that generalizes one-step Tweedie's formula.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled bridge between nonparametric EB and diffusion-based generative modeling, with near-parametric polylog(n)/n score estimation rates in the multivariate heteroscedastic setting and compelling empirical gains on 19-dimensional APOGEE astronomy data where NPMLE is computationally intractable. Key limitations include the compact-support and well-conditioned-covariance assumptions, the unfavorable (log n)^(2d+3) dimension dependence in the rates, and the reliance on heuristic optimization (normalizing flows, mini-batch score matching) without convergence guarantees for the nonconvex objective. A natural open direction is deriving rates that depend only on the intrinsic manifold dimension d₀ ≪ d, which would make the framework practical for very high-dimensional scientific data.
