## Problem

Traditional RAG rerankers face three coupled limitations: computational cost scales with document length, direct dependence on plain text blocks deployment in privacy-sensitive or IP-protected settings, and their relevance-centric scoring fails to capture whether a document is genuinely *adequate* as generation context (i.e., verifiable, comprehensive, well-evidenced, and structurally usable). No existing method assesses document utility from embedding vectors alone without ever touching the original text.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253476/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a pair of pre-computed fixed-dimension embedding vectors (query and document, typically 768–1024 dims from models such as bge-m3, gte-base, jina-v3, or KaLM) and emits a single scalar adequacy score in [0, 1]. Because the input is always a fixed-size vector regardless of source document length, inference memory stays constant at ~550 MB and latency is ~0.11 s. The score is interpretable through a six-bin semantic classification (Precise Adequacy → Irrelevance), enabling threshold-based filtering of inadequate documents before they enter the generation context.

## Method

EAReranker is a 4-layer Decoder-Only Transformer trained with supervised learning on LLM-annotated adequacy scores. Its key architectural innovation is *embedding dimension expansion*: each single embedding vector is projected through N independent linear layers to produce a sequence of N transformed vectors, which are concatenated with learnable [CLS] and [SEP] tokens to form the Transformer input, allowing self-attention to extract fine-grained semantic interactions from compressed fixed-dim vectors. Training uses a *bin-aware weighted MSE loss* (BWMSE) that up-weights samples whose predictions fall near semantic bin boundaries, sharpening decision thresholds. The annotation pipeline scores each query-document pair with seven diverse LLMs under a combinatorial cross-validation scheme (Algorithm 1), then applies within-bin score calibration that blends LLM scores with normalized multi-reranker signals to produce continuous, fine-grained targets while preserving bin boundaries.

## Results

On four public benchmarks (FEVER, NFCorpus, DuRetrieval, T2Ranking), EAReranker(bge-m3) achieves NDCG@10 scores of 94.72, 38.76, 89.57, and 87.11 respectively, remaining within 0.54%–1.30% of the best plaintext reranker (bge-reranker-v2-m3) while operating exclusively on fixed-dimension embedding vectors. For adequacy classification, the model reaches 84.28% ACC25, 86.12% LACC@25, and 92.85% LACC@10 with bge-m3 embeddings. Computationally, EAReranker maintains constant ~550 MB VRAM usage and 0.11 s inference time regardless of document length, compared to up to 8,441 MB and 5.92 s for lb-reranker-v1.0 on long inputs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EAReranker(bge-m3) trails the strongest plaintext reranker bge-reranker-v2-m3 by 0.42–1.51 points in NDCG@10/MRR across the four benchmarks, but outperforms jina-reranker-v2 by 0.36–0.98 points on every metric and matches or slightly exceeds gte-reranker-base on most benchmarks (e.g., +0.20 NDCG@10 on DuRetrieval, +0.31 MRR on T2Ranking). Against lb-reranker-v1.0, EAReranker is behind by 0.06–1.39 points, with the largest gap on NFCorpus MRR (59.32 vs. 60.71). Compared to BM25, EAReranker achieves gains of 46–70 NDCG@10 points, and over raw cosine similarity (bge-m3) it improves NDCG@10 by 13.35 points on FEVER and 5.61 on NFCorpus. The critical practical advantage is that all these near-competitive scores are obtained without accessing original text, with constant ~550 MB memory and 0.11 s latency independent of document length, whereas plaintext rerankers scale from 0.13 s to 5.92 s and up to 8.4 GB VRAM.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the established plaintext reranking paradigm (bge-reranker-v2-m3, jina-reranker-v2, lb-reranker) by operating exclusively on fixed-dimension embedding vectors rather than raw query-document text. It repurposes the Decoder-Only Transformer architecture popularized by lb-reranker for a fundamentally different input: instead of processing token sequences, it applies an embedding dimension expansion technique that projects a single vector into a multi-token sequence via independent linear layers, enabling self-attention to extract fine-grained semantic features. The multi-dimensional adequacy framework (verifiability, coverage, completeness, structure) extends the traditional relevance-only scoring criterion, and the bin-aware weighted loss is a novel training objective tailored to discrete semantic bin boundaries. The annotation methodology draws on multi-LLM cross-validation, a technique borrowed from ensemble evaluation but applied here to construct training labels.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for RAG deployments in resource-constrained or privacy-sensitive settings: constant ~550MB memory regardless of document length and 2-3× faster inference than plaintext rerankers remove key deployment barriers. The adequacy-vs-relevance distinction is conceptually important—showing that exact query repetition scores highest on similarity but lowest on adequacy validates the need for a separate assessment axis. However, the semantic bin boundaries remain heuristic, the LLM-based annotation introduces potential systematic biases, and the framework has only been validated on four benchmarks with English/Chinese content. Open questions include whether embedding-based assessment degrades for highly nuanced or domain-specific queries where token-level interaction might be essential, and whether the approach generalizes to multilingual or multimodal RAG pipelines.
