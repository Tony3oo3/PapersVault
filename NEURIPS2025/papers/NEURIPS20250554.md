## Problem

Existing LLM information-seeking methods—prompting-based pipelines and supervised fine-tuning—remain fixed by prompt rules or training corpora and are predominantly benchmarked on clean, well-structured Wikipedia sources where internal knowledge often suffices. This leaves a critical gap: no rigorous framework exists for training and evaluating LLMs to adaptively seek, verify, and reason over noisy, conflicting information in real-world open-web environments, where search intensity must scale with problem difficulty rather than following a fixed retrieval budget.

## Input / Output

The model receives a single text question and iteratively generates intermediate reasoning steps and search queries (1–5 queries per round, up to 7 rounds total). Retrieved documents (top 2 per query from Bocha or LangSearch) are concatenated into the context as external tokens, which are masked during loss computation. The final output is a structured answer accompanied by the full reasoning chain and the sequence of search queries issued across all rounds, with the model explicitly choosing between 'search' and 'answer' actions at each iteration.

## Method

DeepDiver combines a cold-start SFT phase (distilling DeepSeek-R1 responses on 2k WebPuzzle samples plus general reasoning data) with GRPO-based reinforcement learning over 5k WebPuzzle samples, where the model performs iterative RAG rollouts against a live search engine. The key mechanism is an emergent Search Intensity Scaling (SIS) behavior: rather than being explicitly rewarded for more tool calls, the model learns to escalate search frequency and depth proportionally to problem difficulty, driven by a transient extra-search reward that fires only when search-enabled rollouts succeed while search-free ones fail (trigger rate drops from 4.5% to 0.1% over training). A two-stage reward schedule—loose LLM grader (score ≥6/10) for early training, then a strict three-round majority-vote grader for later stages—stabilizes RL and breaks through mid-training plateaus. The WebPuzzle benchmark (24k training, 275 human-annotated test samples) is specifically curated to require external retrieval even for its Wikipedia subset, ensuring that all four information-seeking behaviors (evidence gathering, conflict resolution, verification/denoising, reflection/correction) are exercised rather than just simple fact lookup.

## Results

On the proposed WebPuzzle benchmark (open-web, 275 test samples), DeepDiver-Qwen2.5-7B achieves 37.6% accuracy and DeepDiver-Pangu-7B achieves 38.1%, both surpassing the 671B DeepSeek-R1 with iterative RAG (37.1%) and substantially outperforming their R1-distilled counterparts (29.8% and 30.7% respectively). On Wiki-based benchmarks, DeepSeek-R1 with iterative RAG still leads (e.g., 84.8% on C-SimpleQA-500, 65.8% on FRAMES-zh-230), while DeepDiver-Pangu-7B reaches 83.7% and 52.3% respectively. In isolation testing that removes problems solvable by internal knowledge alone, DeepDiver outperforms DeepSeek-R1 across all domains, with a 5.1-point lead on WebPuzzle, confirming that the 7B model's full-set gap stems from smaller internal knowledge rather than weaker search ability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On WebPuzzle (the paper's primary open-web benchmark), DeepDiver-Pangu-7B (38.1%) marginally outperforms DeepSeek-R1 with iterative RAG (37.1%), while DeepDiver-Qwen2.5-7B (37.6%) is essentially on par. Both DeepDiver variants substantially beat their R1-distilled counterparts by ~7–8 points, demonstrating that RL training adds value beyond distillation. On Wiki-based benchmarks (C-SimpleQA, FRAMES, BamBoogle), DeepSeek-R1 with iterative RAG still leads by 2–12 points, reflecting its superior internal knowledge from 671B parameters; however, in isolation testing that filters out internally-solvable problems, DeepDiver actually outperforms DeepSeek-R1 by 5.1 points on WebPuzzle. Compared to QwQ-32B (31.4%) and GPT-4o (27.1%) with iterative RAG on WebPuzzle, both DeepDiver variants achieve 6–11 point gains. Against Wiki-trained RL baselines R1-Searcher and DeepResearcher on English benchmarks, DeepDiver-Qwen achieves 26.1% on WebPuzzle-en versus 13.7% and 15.0% respectively, while making 14.7 average search queries versus 1.9 and 7.5, confirming that search intensity scaling is the key differentiator in noisy open-web settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends GRPO (from DeepSeekMath) with iterative RAG rollouts and loss masking for retrieved tokens, positioning itself against prior RL-based search agents (R1-Searcher, DeepResearcher, Search-R1) that train and evaluate exclusively on clean Wikipedia-based corpora. The key conceptual departure is shifting the training and evaluation environment from structured, noise-free Wiki sources to the live open web, where sources are noisy, conflicting, and unreliable. The paper also departs from SFT-based retrieval methods (Self-RAG, Auto-RAG) by arguing that distilling DeepSeek-R1's reasoning patterns overfits to a fixed inference paradigm, whereas RL training on WebPuzzle cultivates adaptive, difficulty-proportional search behavior (SIS) that generalizes beyond the training distribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

WebPuzzle provides a much-needed benchmark that tests information seeking on the live internet rather than curated Wiki snapshots, addressing a gap the authors identify in prior work. The finding that a 7B model trained with RL can match a 671B model on isolated information-seeking tasks suggests that search capability is more trainable than previously assumed, though this claim is conditional on removing problems solvable by internal knowledge. Significant open questions remain: the over-searching inefficiency on simple tasks, the reliance on DeepSeek-R1 for data curation (introducing potential bias), the Chinese-language focus of primary evaluation, and whether SIS generalizes to larger models or longer contexts. The loose-to-strict reward scheduling recipe is a practical contribution for stable RL training of search agents.
