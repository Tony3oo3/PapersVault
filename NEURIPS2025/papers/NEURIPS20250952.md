## Problem

Existing SFT-based post-training methods for MLLM personalization rely on large-scale, high-quality personal captions that are costly to curate, yet still fail to produce faithful personalized captions in real-world multi-concept scenarios (e.g., 3–4 identities in a single image). SFT's data-centric nature makes it highly sensitive to training data quality and composition, leading to poor generalization to out-of-distribution settings such as unseen concept counts. This paper asks whether reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards can replace the need for massive caption datasets while improving both visual recognition and personalized generation.

## Input / Output

At inference, the model receives a query image together with one or more reference image–text pairs (each pair containing a cropped reference image, a unique <name> token, and a short personal description). In the retrieval setting, reference images are automatically selected from a user database via YOLO-World region detection followed by CLIP cosine-similarity matching (top-2 for single/2-concept, top-4 for 4-concept). The output is a single natural-language caption that must (a) correctly reference every provided identity by name and (b) faithfully describe the visual content of the query image, with a minimum length of ~100 tokens enforced during training to prevent degenerate one-sentence outputs.

## Method

RePIC post-trains Qwen2.5-VL-7B (LoRA rank 64) using GRPO with three verifiable rewards that require no auxiliary reward model: (1) Object Consistency Tuning (OCT) gives a binary reward for correctly answering yes/no on whether a named object appears in a second image, trained on positive–negative pairs from COCO, Objects365, CelebA, and synthetic Subject200K+ images; (2) Visual Localization Tuning (VLT) rewards bounding-box predictions that achieve IoU ≥ 0.5 against ground truth, using RefCOCO+/g data to stabilize training; (3) Identity Consistency Tuning (ICT) rewards captions that include all target <name> tokens, with a single-ICT variant (one reference) and a multi-ICT variant (up to three references, scored n/m). A length-regularization gate on ICT (reward = 0 if output < 100 tokens) prevents reward hacking via trivial 'This is <name>.' responses. The entire pipeline trains on only 2K curated samples across 8 A40 GPUs, and the KL-divergence term in GRPO preserves the backbone's general captioning ability while the group-relative advantage mechanism enables stable learning from sparse binary rewards.

## Results

In the single-concept skip-retrieval setting, RePIC (2K training data) achieves F1 scores of 98.1, 97.9, and 99.0 across MyVLM, Yo'LLaVA, and DreamBooth, matching the 210K-sample RAP-Qwen baseline (99.4, 99.8, 100) with ~100× less data. In the multi-concept 2-concept setting, RePIC attains F1 of 99.4 (skip-retrieval) and 95.7 (retrieval), outperforming all SFT baselines. The most dramatic gains appear in the 4-concept out-of-distribution setting, where RePIC achieves F1 of 71.0 (skip-retrieval) and 19.2 (retrieval), compared to 34.8 and 10.0 for the zero-shot Qwen-2.5 VL and 21.3 and 4.3 for RAP-Qwen (210K).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against RAP-LLaVA (SFT, 210K samples), RePIC (2K samples) wins decisively in multi-concept settings: 99.4 vs. 96.9 F1 in 2-concept skip-retrieval, and 71.0 vs. 7.9 F1 in 4-concept skip-retrieval — a gap of over 63 points. Against RAP-Qwen (SFT, 210K), RePIC is comparable in single-concept skip-retrieval (98.1 vs. 99.4 F1 on MyVLM) but substantially outperforms it in 4-concept evaluation (71.0 vs. 21.3 F1). Against the zero-shot Qwen-2.5 VL baseline, RePIC wins in all multi-concept settings (e.g., 71.0 vs. 34.8 F1 in 4-concept skip-retrieval) while the zero-shot model is competitive in single-concept retrieval (84.1 vs. 94.4 F1 on DreamBooth, where RePIC still leads). Notably, RePIC achieves these results with only 2K training samples versus 210K for the SFT baselines, demonstrating that RL-based post-training is far more data-efficient for personalized captioning, especially in out-of-distribution multi-concept scenarios.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant SFT-based paradigm for MLLM personalization (PVIT, RAP-MLLM), which relies on large-scale, proprietary-MLLM-generated captions, and instead adopts the GRPO reinforcement learning algorithm from DeepSeekMath as its training backbone. It also draws on the IoU-based verifiable reward template from VLM-R1 for its visual localization component. Conceptually, it bridges two previously separate lines of work: MLLM personalization (which had been exclusively SFT-based) and RL-based MLLM post-training (which had focused on visual reasoning, hallucination mitigation, and alignment rather than personalization), making it the first to apply policy-based RL with verifiable rewards to personalized image captioning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that RL-based post-training with only 2K curated samples can match or exceed SFT methods trained on 210K samples, particularly in multi-concept and out-of-distribution settings where SFT degrades sharply. This has practical implications for personalization systems where collecting high-quality multi-identity captions is expensive and where new concepts continuously emerge. Key limitations include reliance on a single 7B backbone (Qwen2.5-VL), a retrieval pipeline that introduces noise, and the absence of evaluation on video/audio modalities or multi-turn conversational personalization. Open questions include whether the verifiable-reward design generalizes to other personalized tasks, how to reduce retrieval noise without additional reasoning overhead, and whether the approach scales to larger models or more complex multi-subject scenes.
