## Problem

Current LLM applications in single-cell biology either use models as thin interfaces that invoke pre-built bioinformatics tools without genuine biological reasoning, or embed expression data into opaque vector spaces that sacrifice interpretability. No existing framework enables an LLM to perform transparent, iterative, hypothesis-driven reasoning directly over raw single-cell omics data while producing auditable biological justifications for every analytical decision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250043/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system consumes a G×N single-cell expression matrix, a natural-language biological query, and contextual metadata (species, tissue, protocol), but the raw matrix is never directly fed to the LLM—instead, an algorithmic problem-to-text converter compresses it into a query-specific semantic sketch (e.g., cluster sizes, top-k marker genes, trajectory connections) that fits within a single prompt. Outputs are twofold: a numerical prediction (cell-type labels, lineage tree, or TF-gene edge scores) and a transparent reasoning trace—a sequence of (natural-language claim, omics operator) pairs that constitutes a 'verbal + computational proof' of the conclusion.

## Method

SCPILOT operationalizes a new paradigm called Omics-Native Reasoning (ONR), in which an off-the-shelf LLM iteratively proposes biological hypotheses, invokes targeted bioinformatics operators (Scanpy, Monocle 3, pySCENIC) on the raw data, interprets the structured JSON outputs, and refines its conclusions over multiple reasoning steps. The framework comprises three modules: (i) a Problem-to-Text Converter that algorithmically (not learned) compresses the expression matrix into a query-specific semantic sketch, (ii) a curated Bio-Tool Library of primitive omics operators returning machine-parsable JSON with natural-language descriptions, and (iii) an LLM Reasoner guided by a structured reasoning scaffold with domain-expert heuristics. No model fine-tuning is performed; all performance gains arise from iterative prompting strategies, contextual metadata integration, and accumulating computational evidence. The key distinction from prior tool-agent approaches is that the LLM must explicitly justify every biological claim with numerical evidence from the data, producing an auditable trace rather than merely wrapping opaque tool outputs.

## Results

On cell-type annotation, SCPILOT with o1 achieves cluster-level accuracy of 0.792 (PBMC3k), 0.518 (Liver), and 0.728 (Retina), outperforming CellTypist (0.563/0.464/0.388) and GPTCellType (0.613/0.404/0.300) across all three datasets. For trajectory inference, SCPILOT with Gemini-2.5-Pro achieves a graph-edit distance of 3.33 (×10) and spectral distance of 0.199, reducing GED by ~60% versus py-Monocle (GED=20) and by 60% versus Biomni (GED=8.33). On GRN TF-gene prediction, SCPILOT(o1) reaches AUROC 0.873 on Stomach, surpassing the best GNN baseline (GCN, 0.723) by +0.150 and LLM4GRN (0.727) by +0.146.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SCPILOT outperforms all ten baselines on their respective tasks. In cell-type annotation, SCPILOT(o1) beats CellTypist [76] by +0.229 on PBMC3k and +0.340 on Retina, and surpasses GPTCellType [28] by +0.179 and +0.428 on the same datasets; CellMarker 2.0 [29] is the only baseline that scores competitively on Retina (0.632) but is still outperformed by SCPILOT (0.728). For trajectory inference, SCPILOT (Gemini-2.5-Pro) reduces graph-edit distance to 3.33 versus py-Monocle [10] at 20 and Biomni [31] at 8.33, while also halving spectral distance (0.199 vs. 0.469 and 0.482 respectively). In GRN prediction, SCPILOT(o1) achieves AUROC 0.873 on Stomach, exceeding GCN [36] (0.723), GraphSAGE [23] (0.713), GAT [69] (0.683), LLM4GRN [2] (0.727), and BioGPT [49] (0.660) by margins of +0.150, +0.160, +0.190, +0.146, and +0.213 respectively. The one notable exception is that on the highly complex Liver dataset (28 clusters, 31 cell types), direct prompting with o1 (0.560) marginally exceeds SCPILOT(o1) (0.518), attributed to overthinking on overlapping developmental lineages.

## Relation to Prior Work

SCPILOT departs from two dominant paradigms in LLM-based single-cell analysis: (1) tool-agent systems (CellAgent, Biomni) that generate code to invoke bioinformatics packages but treat tool outputs as opaque results, and (2) single-cell foundation models (scGPT, scBERT, Cell2Sentence) that embed cells into high-dimensional vector spaces without natural-language explanations. The paper's 'omics-native reasoning' (ONR) paradigm instead requires the LLM to articulate biological hypotheses in text, invoke targeted primitive operators on raw data, interpret numerical evidence, and iteratively refine conclusions—positioning the LLM as a reasoning co-pilot rather than a code generator or embedding engine. SCBENCH further distinguishes itself from existing agent benchmarks (BixBench, ScienceAgentBench, FIRE-Bench) by demanding domain-deep omics reasoning with expert-verified ground truth across multiple single-cell workflows, rather than shallow code-orchestration tasks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SCPILOT and SCBENCH provide the first systematic, auditable framework and benchmark for evaluating whether LLMs can genuinely reason over raw single-cell data rather than merely wrapping existing tools, which is critical as the field moves toward AI-assisted biological discovery. Key limitations include heavy reliance on proprietary API models (open-source alternatives like Gemma-3-27B are 15× slower and substantially less accurate), a small benchmark (9 datasets), and the acknowledged risk that data compression may obscure rare cell populations. Open questions include scaling ONR to billion-token contexts, mitigating LLM hallucinations in biological claims, and integrating wet-lab validation to close the loop between computational prediction and experimental confirmation.
