## Problem

Existing video prompting methods are designed for ViT architectures that exploit global attention to propagate information, but when applied to state space models like VideoMamba, the sequentially compressed visual prompt tokens suffer from information decay over long token sequences. This limits the effective propagation of spatial information within frames and temporal information between frames, preventing the extraction of discriminative spatio-temporal features needed for downstream video understanding.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254794/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a video clip (T×C×H×W) where frames are resized to 224×224 and split into 14×14 patches, with 8 frames sampled for HMDB51/UCF101/SSV2 and 32 for the long-video Breakfast dataset. A CLS token is prepended to the flattened patch sequence, and the final output is an action class label produced by a linear head on the CLS token from the last Mamba layer. Only the IFG/IFS modules and classification head are trainable (~0.98M params for VideoMamba-S, ~2.41M for VideoMamba-M), while the pre-trained backbone remains frozen.

## Method

SSP introduces two complementary modules inserted after the first Mamba layer: an Intra-Frame Gathering (IFG) module that uses low-rank 2D convolution to aggregate spatial key information within each frame, producing intra-frame prompts, information entropy weights, and spatial variance measurements; and an Inter-Frame Spreading (IFS) module that samples the last token from each frame, applies low-rank attention to spread discriminative temporal information across frames, with the output gated by both entropy weights (adjusting per-frame attention) and spatial variance (controlling influence strength). The intra-frame prompts are overlaid on tokens at every Mamba layer input, while inter-frame prompts are inserted between frames in all subsequent layers. The key insight is that this gather-then-spread strategy reduces the effective information transmission path length from O(TN) to O(N) in the sequential Mamba scan, overcoming the exponential decay of the forget gate over long sequences while maintaining linear computational complexity.

## Results

On VideoMamba-M (74M params), SSP achieves top-1 accuracies of 76.66% (HMDB51), 53.72% (SSV2), 97.03% (UCF101), and 93.23% (Breakfast) using only 2.41M tunable parameters (~3.3% of full model). This represents an average improvement of 2.76% over the best existing parameter-efficient fine-tuning methods across the four benchmarks. Notably, SSP surpasses full fine-tuning (74M params) on HMDB51 (76.66% vs. 76.30%) and UCF101 (97.03% vs. 96.00%) while using only 3.3% of the parameters, though it trails on SSV2 (53.72% vs. 67.30%) and Breakfast (93.23% vs. 95.31%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SSP outperforms all eight VideoMamba-M baselines on all four datasets. Against the strongest PEFT competitor SVP (2.76M params), SSP gains +3.00% on HMDB51, +4.64% on SSV2, +0.26% on UCF101, and +3.13% on Breakfast. The gap is most dramatic on SSV2, where STOP (23.44%) and ST-Adapter (31.91%) struggle severely due to their ViT-oriented global attention design being mismatched with Mamba's sequential compression, while SSP's architecture-aware gathering and spreading modules close much of the gap to full fine-tuning (53.72% vs. 67.30%). On the long-video Breakfast dataset, SSP (93.23%) substantially outperforms STOP (71.35%) and ST-Adapter (67.70%), demonstrating the effectiveness of inter-frame spreading for long temporal contexts. SSP also exceeds full fine-tuning on HMDB51 and UCF101 despite using only 3.3% of the parameters, though it remains below full fine-tuning on SSV2 and Breakfast, suggesting room for further improvement on fine-grained and very long video tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) literature for video understanding by adapting prompt learning to the sequential state space compression of VideoMamba, departing from ViT-oriented prompting methods (DGL, STOP, MPT) that rely on global attention for information propagation. It builds directly on VideoMamba as the backbone and borrows the deep prompt overlay strategy from VPT, but introduces a complementary intra-frame gathering (low-rank convolution) and inter-frame spreading (low-rank attention) architecture specifically designed to counteract information decay in sequential SSM compression. The key conceptual departure is recognizing that Mamba's sequential token propagation makes adjacent tokens highly redundant, so naive prompt concatenation fails to aggregate discriminative spatio-temporal context across long video sequences.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As Mamba-based video models gain traction as efficient alternatives to Transformers, establishing effective PEFT strategies for them is practically important for deploying video understanding on resource-constrained settings. The paper's entropy-weighted and spatial-variance-gated prompting mechanism offers a principled way to balance local spatial focus with global temporal context, and the consistent gains across four diverse benchmarks (including the long-video Breakfast dataset) suggest the approach generalizes. Open questions include whether the IFG/IFS design transfers to other SSM backbones (e.g., Mamba2, H3), whether it extends beyond classification to dense video tasks (detection, segmentation), and how the method scales to substantially longer videos where the O(N) path-length reduction becomes most critical.
