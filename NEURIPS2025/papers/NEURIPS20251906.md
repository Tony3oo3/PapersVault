## Problem

Existing Track Any Point (TAP) methods cannot meaningfully track points once they leave the camera's field of view: 2D methods lack object permanence for out-of-frame points, and 3D methods (e.g., TAPVid-3D) require complete 4D scene models for supervision that are prohibitively expensive to acquire. This paper introduces the TAPVid-360 task, which predicts 3D directions (not absolute positions) to tracked points, enabling persistent tracking beyond the FOV while avoiding the need for dynamic 3D ground truth.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251906/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a narrow-FOV perspective video (32 frames) and a set of N query points specified as 2D pixel coordinates in the first frame, and outputs per-point, per-frame unit direction vectors in the camera coordinate system. Unlike TAPVid-3D which predicts (u,v,w) 3D positions, the output here is a normalized direction vector, abstracting away depth. Evaluation uses 256 query points across 32-frame clips; training is constrained to 32 query points and 32 frames due to memory limits on a single A40 GPU.

## Method

The key insight is to exploit 360° videos as a scalable supervision source: a 2D tracking pipeline (LangSAM → SAM2 → CoTracker3) tracks dynamic objects on the full equirectangular video, and the resulting 2D tracks are converted to 3D direction vectors via camera intrinsics. Novel narrow-FOV perspective videos are then rendered by resampling the 360° video along diverse camera trajectories (spiral, spin, simulated human, random, back-to-front), producing training pairs where tracked points frequently leave the view frustum. The baseline, CoTracker360, replaces CoTracker3's final displacement layer with a 9-output linear head whose output is reshaped to a 3×3 matrix and projected to SO(3) via Procrustes orthonormalization; this per-point rotation updates the initial direction frame-to-frame. The model is initialized from pretrained CoTracker3 offline weights and finetuned for 120 epochs with Huber loss on direction predictions (Adam, lr=1e-4, batch 8, single A40 GPU).

## Results

On TAPVid360-10k, CoTracker360 achieves the best out-of-frame angular accuracy (<δ_oof) of 0.1160, 1.3× higher than the next-best 3D method TAPIP3D (0.0850), and reduces out-of-frame angular distance (AD_oof) from 45.80° to 10.98° (a >4× reduction). Overall angular distance (AD_all) drops to 8.27°, far below TAPIP3D (36.44°), SpatialTracker (38.88°), and CoTracker3 (37.43°). In-frame accuracy (<δ_if) is competitive at 0.4060, though slightly below CoTracker3 (0.5588) and SpatialTracker (0.4893), while in-frame angular distance (AD_if) is the best at 3.95°.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoTracker360 decisively outperforms all 2D baselines (TAPNext, TAPIR, BootsTAPIR) on every metric, with angular distances roughly 5–6× lower, confirming that 2D point tracking is fundamentally inadequate for out-of-frame reasoning. Against the 3D baselines, CoTracker360 achieves the best out-of-frame accuracy (0.1160 vs. TAPIP3D's 0.0850) and dramatically lower out-of-frame angular distance (10.98° vs. TAPIP3D's 45.80° and SpatialTracker's 50.44°). On in-frame accuracy (<δ_if), CoTracker360 (0.4060) trails CoTracker3 (0.5588) and SpatialTracker (0.4893), but its in-frame angular distance (3.95°) is far superior to all baselines, indicating that when it is wrong, the error is small rather than catastrophic. Overall accuracy (<δ_all) is competitive with TAPIP3D (0.2386 vs. 0.2476) and CoTracker3 (0.2435), while the overall angular distance (8.27°) is roughly 4.5× lower than any baseline. The paper's central finding is that the directional representation and 360°-derived supervision enable robust out-of-frame tracking without sacrificing in-frame performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Track Any Point (TAP) task family—previously formulated as 2D pixel tracking (TAPVid) or 3D position tracking (TAPVid-3D)—by introducing a directional output space: predicting unit vectors in camera coordinates rather than pixel positions or full 3D locations. The baseline method directly modifies CoTracker3, replacing its displacement-prediction head with a rotation-prediction head (projected to SO(3) via Procrustes orthonormalization), and fine-tunes on data generated from 360° video. The data pipeline repurposes 360° video (previously exploited for generative tasks like novel view synthesis and panoramic outpainting) as a supervision source for tracking, using LangSAM/SAM2 segmentation and 2D point tracking on the full equirectangular panorama to derive pseudo-ground-truth directions without requiring 4D scene models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key contribution is a scalable recipe for generating out-of-frame tracking supervision from freely available 360° video, sidestepping the 4D ground-truth bottleneck that has limited TAPVid-3D. The directional formulation is a pragmatic middle ground: it enforces object permanence and allocentric reasoning without the difficulty of depth estimation, and could serve as a pretraining signal for full 3D tracking or as a conditioning signal for video generation. However, the evidence is limited to a single baseline (CoTracker360) on a fixed-FOV dataset with pseudo-ground-truth, and the in-frame accuracy actually degrades relative to the unmodified CoTracker3, raising open questions about whether the rotation-prediction formulation generalizes beyond this specific architecture and whether the approach scales to dynamic FOV, uncertainty modeling, or true 3D position recovery.
