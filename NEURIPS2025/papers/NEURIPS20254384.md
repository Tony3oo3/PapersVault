## Problem

Existing MIA-based evaluation metrics for machine unlearning are unreliable: retraining (the theoretical gold standard) is not guaranteed to yield the lowest MIA performance, results are sensitive to data composition and the specific MIA chosen, and different MIAs produce incomparable scores. This paper addresses the absence of a well-calibrated, theoretically grounded evaluation metric that can reliably measure the data removal efficacy of unlearning algorithms.

## Input / Output

The framework takes an unlearned model m = UL(LR(R∪F), F), a dataset D split into retain/forget/test sets (R, F, T) with |F| = |T| and unlearning portion α = |F|/|R∪F|, a sensitivity distribution P_D over D, and a weak MIA adversary that queries the oracle exactly once. The output is the Unlearning Quality score Q ∈ [0,1] (where Q=1 corresponds to perfect retraining-level unlearning) computed as 1 − sup_A Adv(A, UL). The practical SWAP test approximation requires only two dataset splits (s and its swap s' with F and T exchanged) rather than enumerating all splits in S_α.

## Method

The paper formalizes the unlearning sample inference game, a cryptographic game where a challenger (unlearning algorithm UL) produces an unlearned model and an adversary (MIA) attempts to distinguish whether oracle-sampled data points come from the forget set F or the unused test set T. The evaluation metric is the adversary's advantage — the absolute difference in success rates across the two scenarios, averaged over all valid dataset splits — which provably achieves zero grounding for RETRAIN (Theorem 3.3) and admits an upper bound parameterized by (ε,δ)-certified removal guarantees (Theorem 3.5). To make the metric computationally tractable, the SWAP test replaces the full split enumeration with just two swapped splits, preserving the zero-grounding symmetry. For practical deployment, the adversary is restricted to a weak form (single oracle query, matching current SOTA MIAs), and the supremum over adversaries is approximated by selecting the strongest among multiple SOTA black-box MIA attacks (shadow-model-based, correctness/confidence/entropy-based).

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with ResNet, the proposed Unlearning Quality metric Q correctly grounds RETRAIN at Q ≈ 0.993–0.999 across all DP budgets (ε = 50 to ∞), validating the zero-grounding theorem. Among approximate unlearning methods, SSD achieves the highest Q (0.888–0.996), followed by SALUN (0.689–0.979) and FISHER (0.709–0.973), while the no-unlearning baseline NONE scores 0.587–0.972. The metric exhibits the theoretically predicted negative correlation with the DP privacy budget ε, with Q decreasing monotonically as ε increases for all methods, confirming Theorem 3.5.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares the proposed Unlearning Quality (Q) against two existing attack-based metrics: the Interclass Confusion (IC) score [Goel et al., 2023] and the MIA AUC [Golatkar et al., 2021]. Under the same DP-budget sweep (ε = 50 to ∞), both IC score and MIA AUC fail to produce evaluation results that are negatively correlated with ε, whereas Q does so consistently for all methods. For example, the IC score for NEGGRAD drops from 0.925 at ε=50 to 0.180 at ε=∞, but is non-monotonic (0.953 at ε=150 > 0.946 at ε=600), and MIA AUC for NEGGRAD similarly rises from 0.476 (ε=50) to 0.482 (ε=150) before falling. In terms of ranking consistency, Q maintains a stable partial order across all ε values, while IC score and MIA AUC exhibit multiple ranking inversions (e.g., NONE vs. NEGGRAD flips between ε=50 and ε=∞ under IC; NEGGRAD vs. FISHER flips at ε=150 under MIA AUC). RETRAIN is correctly grounded at Q≈1, whereas IC score gives it only 0.962–0.976 and MIA AUC gives it 0.479–0.492, neither of which reflects the theoretically optimal status of retraining.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the cryptographic game and advantage framework from Katz and Lindell (2007) to formalize machine unlearning evaluation, departing from the ad-hoc MIA-based metrics (MIA AUC, Interclass Confusion test) that dominate the unlearning literature. It connects to the certified removal notion of Guo et al. (2020) by proving a lower bound on the proposed Unlearning Quality in terms of (ε,δ)-certified removal parameters, thereby bridging the gap between theory-based guarantees and empirical evaluation. The SWAP test approximation is a novel practical device that preserves the symmetry needed for zero grounding, in contrast to naive random-split approaches that Proposition 3.7 shows can yield spurious high advantage even for RETRAIN.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a foundational gap in machine unlearning: the lack of a well-calibrated, theoretically grounded evaluation metric. By showing that existing MIA-based metrics (IC score, MIA AUC) fail to maintain consistent relative rankings across DP budgets while the proposed Unlearning Quality does, it provides a concrete argument for adopting a new standard. Limitations include reliance on weak (single-query) MIA adversaries, restriction to i.i.d. uniform splitting, and experiments limited to CIFAR-10 with ResNet; whether the metric scales to larger datasets, non-i.i.d. unlearning settings, or white-box adversaries remains open. The practical impact hinges on community adoption of the SWAP test as a lightweight benchmarking tool.
