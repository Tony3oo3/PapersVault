## Problem

The standard SFT+RL pipeline for training small language models (SLMs) on reasoning tasks can fail completely: expert traces from larger models are too complex for SLMs to imitate via SFT (sometimes degrading performance), and when SFT initialization is weak, the subsequent GRPO phase receives no reward signal because the model cannot generate any correct trace. The paper provides a theoretical Markov-chain model proving this failure mode and asks how to bridge the SFT and RL stages so that SLMs can learn from expert reasoning even on problems that are otherwise unsolvable.

## Input / Output

During training, BREAD takes a text question Q paired with a full expert reasoning trace S (from a larger model or ground truth). The expert trace is split into K=10 episodes, and an Episode Anchor Search (EAS) binary search selects a prefix ρ of these episodes to append to Q, so the model only generates the remaining suffix. At inference, no hint is provided—the fine-tuned policy generates a complete reasoning trace from the question alone. The output is a GRPO-updated SLM policy (Qwen2.5-1.5B/3B-Instruct or LLaMA3.2-3B-Instruct) that produces chain-of-thought traces with a final boxed answer.

## Method

BREAD is a GRPO variant that adaptively injects partial expert traces as hints during RL rollouts to densify the reward signal. For each question, it first samples G rollouts; if the success rate is below a threshold, EAS performs a binary search over the expert trace's episodes to find the shortest prefix ρ such that the success rate of new rollouts conditioned on (Q, ρ) falls within a target range (neither all-correct nor all-wrong). The model then generates branched rollouts from this anchor, completing the remaining reasoning steps on its own, and the policy is updated with the standard GRPO clipped-surrogate objective plus KL regularization, conditioned on (Q, ρ). This mechanism induces a self-paced curriculum: as the model improves, EAS automatically selects shorter hints, progressively shifting from expert-guided to self-generated traces. Under a Markov-chain toy model, the paper proves that SFT+RL requires trace length Ω(K²) to succeed while BREAD succeeds with O(K²/T) length using O(T log T) total samples, and empirically BREAD matches or exceeds SFT+GRPO using ~20% of expert trace tokens while reducing training compute by ~75%.

## Results

On the NuminaMath-CoT benchmark with Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct, BREAD achieves 64.7% accuracy, outperforming vanilla GRPO (47.5%) by 17.2 points and SFT(full)+GRPO (53.7%) by 11 points, while using only ~37% of expert trace tokens. On the MATH dataset, BREAD reaches 84.3% (Qwen-3B) and 78.8% (Qwen-1.5B), matching or exceeding SFT(full)+GRPO (84.6% and 77.4% respectively). BREAD reduces total training FLOPs by approximately 75% (≈3× speedup) relative to vanilla GRPO, and on a curated set of 500 hardest questions where all baselines stall, BREAD is the only method showing sustained accuracy gains.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against vanilla GRPO, BREAD wins decisively across all model–dataset combinations: +16.2 points on MATH (Qwen-3B: 0.843 vs 0.681) and +17.2 points on NuminaMath-CoT (Qwen-3B: 0.647 vs 0.475), with the gap widening on harder problems. Against SFT(full)+GRPO, BREAD matches or exceeds performance while using only ~37% of the expert trace tokens; notably, on NuminaMath-CoT with Qwen-1.5B, SFT(full)+GRPO actually degrades below the base model (0.242 vs 0.347 for GRPO alone), whereas BREAD still improves to 0.361. Against DeepSeek-R1-Distill (trained on 800K samples from a 671B model), BREAD narrows the gap to within 3 points on MATH (0.788 vs 0.818) and 0.7 points on NuminaMath-CoT (0.361 vs 0.368) for Qwen-1.5B, without requiring the massive distillation pipeline. Against DAPO on the hardest 500-question subset, BREAD achieves 14% accuracy versus DAPO's 5%, confirming that BREAD's adaptive expert-hint mechanism is specifically effective where other RL methods fail due to sparse rewards.

## Relation to Prior Work

BREAD extends GRPO (from DeepSeekMath) by unifying the standard SFT→RL two-stage pipeline into a single adaptive procedure. Conceptually, it draws on classical RL ideas—DAgger's intermittent expert injection, GoExplore's branching from intermediate states, and Hindsight Experience Replay's reward densification—but repurposes them for LLM reasoning by branching rollouts from partial expert traces rather than from learned intermediate states. The theoretical analysis formalizes a Markov-chain model showing that SFT+RL provably fails when the expert's jump size exceeds the student's expressivity, a regime not previously characterized in the LLM post-training literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and proves a fundamental failure mode of the dominant SFT+RL paradigm for small language models: when expert traces are too complex for the student to express, SFT provides no benefit and RL receives no reward signal, making the pipeline provably unable to learn. BREAD's practical impact is significant—75% compute reduction and the ability to solve problems where SFT+GRPO stalls entirely—making it a potentially widely adopted technique for efficient SLM post-training. Key limitations include the requirement for expert traces (weakening the method when no strong teacher is available), the assumption that SLMs can follow even partial expert reasoning, and evaluation primarily on mathematical reasoning with Qwen-family models. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to non-math domains, whether simpler expert trace generation could address the 'can't follow even full solution' edge case, and how the method scales to larger student models where the SFT+RL gap narrows.
