## Problem

Modifying well-trained open-weight models for tasks like structured pruning or classwise unlearning typically requires gradients, ground-truth labels, or the original loss function—none of which are available when only synthetic or distributional data is accessible. The fundamental question of which model components are critical to predictive performance, and how to identify them without any of these resources, remains largely unaddressed, especially in a unified framework spanning both CNNs and Transformers.

## Input / Output

The method consumes only pretrained model weights and unlabeled synthetic samples (synthetically generated images for vision tasks; Alpaca or WikiText-2 calibration sequences of length 1024 for LLMs), with no access to ground-truth labels, gradients, or the original loss. For unlearning, it additionally requires samples from the specific forget class. The output is a modified model: for pruning, a sparsified network with a closed-form compensation term δ* applied to remaining weights to restore layer-output fidelity without fine-tuning; for unlearning, a model with the high-fidelity components most discriminative for the target class zeroed out, degrading forget-class accuracy while preserving retain-class performance.

## Method

The core theoretical contribution (Theorem 3.6) shows that for Lipschitz-continuous networks, the global prediction error is linearly bounded by local layer-wise reconstruction errors, establishing local component importance as a valid proxy for global influence. Building on this, the paper defines Subset Fidelity—a metric measuring how well a subset of input contributions can reconstruct a layer's output after optimal weight compensation—and proves (Theorem 3.9) that under uncorrelated features, selecting components by their singleton fidelity scores is optimal, justifying a simple greedy (NAIVE) strategy. They further show that well-trained Transformers are Lipschitz continuous (Corollary B.4), extending the theory beyond CNNs. The ModHiFi algorithm computes singleton fidelity scores via batched forward passes on synthetic data, selects top-k high-fidelity components per layer, and then either removes non-HiFi input channels (pruning, with analytical compensation) or zeros out HiFi components for the target class (unlearning), all without any gradient-based fine-tuning.

## Results

On ImageNet ResNet-50, ModHiFi-P achieves 76.70% top-1 accuracy with 2.17× FLOP reduction and 1.69× CPU speedup, outperforming all baselines at comparable sparsity; at higher sparsity (3.66× FLOP reduction) it retains 73.82% accuracy with 2.42× CPU speedup. On Llama-2-7B at 10% sparsity, ModHiFi-P-Alpaca achieves 65.96% average task accuracy (vs. 59.96% for SliceGPT) and 6.36 WikiText PPL. For classwise unlearning on CIFAR-10 ResNet-50, ModHiFi-U reduces forget accuracy to 0.2% (vs. 3.54% for Jia et al.) while retaining 92.98% accuracy on other classes, completing in 10 seconds without fine-tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On ImageNet ResNet-50, ModHiFi-P dominates all five vision pruning baselines: it achieves 76.70% accuracy (vs. 75.9% DFPC, 75.83% DepGRAPH, 74.7% OTO, 73.9% GReg-2, 71.6% ThiNet) while delivering the best CPU speedup (1.69×) at moderate sparsity, and 2.42× CPU speedup at 3.66× FLOP reduction (vs. 2.37× for DFPC at similar accuracy). On CIFAR-10, ModHiFi-P achieves 91.02% accuracy at 4.07× FLOP reduction, outperforming L2+Synthetic Tuning (90.12%) and dramatically exceeding L2 alone (15.91%). On Llama-2-7B, ModHiFi-P-Alpaca outperforms SliceGPT at 10% sparsity (65.96% vs. 59.96% avg accuracy) and 20% sparsity (60.62% vs. 51.39%), and beats ShortGPT at 20% (60.62% vs. 59.95%), though ShortGPT edges ahead at 30% (53.25% vs. 52.44%). For unlearning, ModHiFi-U achieves near-complete forgetting (0.2% forget accuracy) on ResNet-50 in 10 seconds, outperforming both Gradient Ascent (6.59%, 30s) and Jia et al. (3.54%, 363s) by over 10× in speed; on Swin-T, Jia et al. achieves lower forget accuracy (1.20% vs. 8.83%) but ModHiFi-U is 117× faster (2s vs. 235s) and closes the gap with minimal fine-tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies structured pruning and classwise unlearning under a single component-importance framework, departing from prior work that treats these tasks in isolation and typically requires gradients, ground-truth labels, or the original loss function. Theoretically, it builds on the Lipschitz-continuity literature for CNNs (Yu et al. 2018) and extends it to well-trained Transformers, contradicting the claim in Qi et al. (2023) that ViTs are not Lipschitz continuous. The Subset Fidelity metric generalizes the reconstruction-based formulation of El Halabi et al. (2022), while the practical algorithm draws on the data-free pruning line (DFPC, Narshana et al. 2023) and the LLM calibration-based pruning paradigm (SliceGPT, ShortGPT). The key conceptual departure is operating solely with distributional access via synthetic data, eliminating the need for training data or a loss function entirely.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The local-to-global error bound (Theorem 3.6) provides a principled, architecture-agnostic justification for using layer-wise reconstruction as a proxy for global predictive impact, which is practically valuable in settings where gradients and loss functions are unavailable (privacy-constrained deployment, synthetic-data-only training). The demonstration that well-trained Transformers satisfy Lipschitz continuity opens the door to applying a broader class of theoretical tools to Transformer analysis. However, the optimality guarantee (Theorem 3.9) requires uncorrelated features, which rarely holds exactly in practice, and the Swin-T unlearning results without fine-tuning underperform baselines, suggesting the method's effectiveness is architecture- and task-dependent. Open questions include whether other distributional similarity metrics (TV, Wasserstein) yield better component rankings, and whether the emergence of HiFi components is fundamentally tied to training dynamics.
