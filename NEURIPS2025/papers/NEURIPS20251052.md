## Problem

Existing generated-image detectors train binary classifiers on both natural and generated images, making their performance heavily dependent on the diversity of available generated data and limiting generalization to images from unknown generative models (e.g., Sora). The paper asks whether detection can instead rely solely on fitting the natural image distribution, eliminating the need for any generated-image training data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251052/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (256×256 or 224×224 depending on benchmark) and applies n=20 random data augmentations (horizontal flip, color jitter, Gaussian blur) to produce transformed copies. It outputs a consistency score δ(x) = |f₁(x) − f₁(h(x))| computed via cosine similarity between DINOv2 ViT-L/14 features of the original and each augmented image; the image is classified as generated if the average score exceeds a threshold. For video (Sora/OpenSora evaluation), individual frames are extracted and processed identically.

## Method

ConV exploits the geometric fact that generated images lie off the natural-image data manifold, while a well-trained self-supervised model (DINOv2) is invariant to transformations along the manifold's tangent space. It constructs two functions whose gradients occupy mutually orthogonal subspaces: f₁ is the self-supervised loss (gradient orthogonal to the tangent space) and f₂ = f₁∘h where h is a data augmentation (gradient along the tangent space). Detection is training-free: an image is flagged as generated if the loss/similarity change induced by h exceeds a threshold, since natural images on the manifold remain consistent under h while off-manifold generated images do not. F-ConV further amplifies the manifold deviation by training a RealNVP normalizing flow on DINOv2 features with a shaping loss (pushing generated features away from the natural Gaussian) and a consistency loss (widening the f₁/f₂ gap for generated images), requiring only a small amount of generated data for this auxiliary training.

## Results

On the ImageNet benchmark, the training-free ConV achieves 87.13% average AUROC and 85.15% average AP, substantially outperforming the only other training-free baseline AEROBLADE (57.87% AUROC, 57.85% AP). The flow-enhanced variant F-ConV achieves the best overall performance with 93.77% AUROC and 93.38% AP, surpassing the strongest training-based baselines FatFormer (93.68% AUROC) and DRCT (90.36% AUROC). On Sora-generated images (an unknown generative model), F-ConV reaches 90.95% average AUROC, outperforming FatFormer (89.36%) and DRCT (82.16%), while ConV (training-free) still achieves 85.29% AUROC.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among training-free methods, ConV dominates AEROBLADE by a large margin: 87.13% vs. 57.87% average AUROC on ImageNet, with ConV winning on every individual generative model (e.g., 92.83% vs. 44.36% on BigGAN, 93.02% vs. 67.05% on RQ-Transformer). Among training-based methods, F-ConV edges out FatFormer (93.77% vs. 93.68% AUROC) and DRCT (93.77% vs. 90.36%), while decisively outperforming NPR (86.00%), Ojha (85.35%), CNNspot (67.04%), and DIRE (52.70%). On the Sora benchmark—an unknown generative model not seen during training—F-ConV achieves 90.95% AUROC, beating FatFormer (89.36%), DRCT (82.16%), Ojha (72.06%), and ConV (85.29%), demonstrating strong generalization to unseen generators. Notably, ConV's training-free design means it requires no generated-image data at all, yet still surpasses most training-based baselines, highlighting the advantage of fitting only the natural image distribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant binary-classification paradigm for generated image detection (CNNspot, Ojha, DIRE, DRCT, FatFormer, NPR), which requires collecting both natural and generated images to train a detector. Instead, ConV repurposes the invariance properties of self-supervised models (DINOv2) to the detection task: since SSL models are trained to be insensitive to transformations along the data manifold, a consistency check between the model's output on an image and its augmented version reveals whether the image lies on the natural manifold. The theoretical backbone is an orthogonality principle linking the tangent space of the data manifold to the gradient of the SSL loss, and the F-ConV variant introduces a RealNVP normalizing flow to amplify the manifold deviation of generated images. The method thus carves new ground by framing detection as a distribution-fitting problem over natural images alone, rather than a two-class separation problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The generalization gap to unknown generative models is a critical practical limitation of current detectors, and ConV's training-free design (relying only on a pre-trained SSL model) offers a principled path forward, validated by strong results on Sora and OpenSora images where most baselines degrade. However, the paper acknowledges the absence of a formal generalization bound, the lack of an adversarial scenario where generators are trained to minimize the f1/f2 inconsistency, and the unexplored effect of scaling the SSL backbone. The finding that CLIP-based ConV underperforms DINOv2-based ConV significantly (72.99 vs. 87.13 average AUROC) highlights that the method's success is tightly coupled to the SSL training objective and augmentation strategy, raising open questions about which pre-trained models are suitable carriers for this consistency-verification framework.
