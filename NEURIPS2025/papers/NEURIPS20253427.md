## Problem

Existing song generation methods face a fundamental tradeoff: mixed-token approaches capture vocal-instrument harmony but suffer from limited vocabulary and lower sound quality, while dual-track approaches improve fidelity but lose harmony due to independent prediction. Additionally, the scarcity of high-quality annotated music data constrains musicality and instruction following, and no prior work has applied multi-dimensional preference alignment to song generation.

## Input / Output

LeVo takes required lyrics plus optional text descriptions and optional audio prompts as conditioning inputs, all tokenized via a BPE-based Qwen2 tokenizer and concatenated as prefix context. The output is 48kHz stereo song audio reconstructed from dual-track tokens (separate vocal and accompaniment streams) at a 25 Hz frame rate, decoded through a diffusion transformer and VAE. Audio prompts enable zero-shot style transfer, while text descriptions control genre, emotion, and instrumental arrangement.

## Method

LeVo's core architecture, LeLM, uses a 28-layer decoder-only transformer to autoregressively predict mixed tokens (capturing melody, rhythm, and tempo for vocal-instrument harmony) while a smaller 12-layer AR decoder parallelly predicts dual-track vocal and accompaniment tokens conditioned on the language model's hidden states with a delay pattern, preventing cross-token interference without the sequence-length penalty of interleaving. Training follows a three-stage paradigm: pre-training on mixed tokens only, modular extension training that unfreezes the AR decoder for dual-track prediction while freezing Stage-1 parameters, and DPO-based multi-preference alignment. The alignment stage fine-tunes three separate models on lyric-alignment, prompt-consistency, and musicality preference data (constructed semi-automatically via ASR phoneme errors, MuQ-MuLan similarity scores, and a crowdsourced reward model), then combines them via linear parameter interpolation inspired by DNI. The Music Codec pairs a MuEncoder+RVQ encoder with a DiT+VAE decoder, achieving ~7× faster inference than MuCodec while maintaining comparable reconstruction quality.

## Results

LeVo achieves the best Phoneme Error Rate (PER) of 7.2% (tied with Mureka-O1), far surpassing Suno-V4.5 (21.6%) and all open-source baselines (27.5–37.1%). It attains the highest MuQ-T (0.34, tied with Suno), highest Audiobox-Aesthetic scores for CE (7.78), CU (7.90), and PQ (8.46), and a competitive FAD of 2.68. In subjective MOS evaluation, LeVo scores 3.42 on Overall Quality and 3.38 on Lyrics Following Accuracy, outperforming all open-source systems and exceeding Suno-V4.5 on LYC (3.38 vs. 3.17).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the three industry systems, LeVo clearly outperforms Suno-V4.5 on PER (7.2% vs. 21.6%), CE (7.78 vs. 7.65), CU (7.90 vs. 7.86), PQ (8.46 vs. 8.35), and LYC MOS (3.38 vs. 3.17), but trails on FAD (2.68 vs. 2.59), MuQ-A (0.83 vs. 0.84), and subjective OVL/MEL/SSC/AQ. Versus Mureka-O1, LeVo wins on MuQ-T (0.34 vs. 0.33), CE, CU, and PQ, while Mureka retains advantages in FAD (2.50 vs. 2.68), MuQ-A (0.87 vs. 0.83), and PC (6.39 vs. 6.03). Against all four open-source academic baselines (YuE, DiffRhythm, ACE-Step, SongGen*), LeVo achieves the best or tied-best score on every objective metric and every subjective MOS dimension; the largest gaps are in PER (7.2% vs. 27.5–37.1%) and MuQ-T (0.34 vs. 0.25–0.28), confirming that the parallel mixed/dual-track token prediction and DPO-based multi-preference alignment yield substantial gains over prior song-generation approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

LeVo extends the LM-based song generation lineage from Jukebox (mixed tokens) through YuE and SongGen (dual-track tokens with interleaving) by proposing a parallel prediction architecture that simultaneously models both token types using a language model plus a lightweight AR decoder, avoiding the sequence-length penalty of interleaving while preventing cross-token interference. It also departs from prior RL/RLHF work in music (BATON, MusicRL, Tango2), which targeted a single preference dimension in non-vocal music, by introducing the first multi-preference DPO alignment for song generation, combining lyric alignment, prompt consistency, and musicality through an interpolation-based parameter blending strategy inspired by DNI from vision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LeVo demonstrates that the vocal-instrument harmony vs. sound-quality tradeoff in song generation can be addressed architecturally rather than by choosing one token representation over another, offering a practical design principle for future LM-based audio systems. The multi-preference DPO framework, with its controllable interpolation coefficients, provides a flexible post-training recipe that could transfer to other multi-dimensional generative tasks. However, the system still trails top industry models (Suno, Mureka) on audio quality and song structure clarity, and its reliance on pseudo-labeling from pre-trained models (Qwen2-Audio, Whisper, Demucs) introduces annotation noise that caps instruction-following fidelity—closing this gap with proprietary systems remains an open challenge.
