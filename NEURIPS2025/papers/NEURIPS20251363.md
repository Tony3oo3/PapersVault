## Problem

Standard MCTS for symbolic regression relies on UCB-style bandit strategies that optimize expected cumulative reward, yet SR requires identifying the single globally optimal expression (an extreme-reward objective). Additionally, the sequential, token-by-token construction of expressions limits the ability to jump to distant, promising regions of the combinatorial search space. The paper addresses both gaps by proposing an extreme-bandit allocation rule with finite-time guarantees and by embedding genetic-programming operators directly into the MCTS loop.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a set of data points (x_i, y_i) and a predefined symbol set (arithmetic operators, elementary functions, constants, and variables), and produces a symbolic expression represented as a binary tree (equivalently, a pre-order symbol sequence). Expression depth is bounded to control the state space, and any constants appearing in the candidate expression are optimized via BFGS during evaluation. The terminal reward is 1/(1+NRMSE), mapping fit quality to [0,1], and the search terminates when the computational budget (number of expression evaluations) is exhausted.

## Method

The core contribution is a two-part modification to MCTS. First, the node-selection rule is replaced by a UCB-extreme criterion that tracks the maximum observed reward per action (rather than the mean) and uses an exploration bonus of the form 2c(ln T / T_k)^γ, yielding a finite-time regret bound of O(ln T / T^{1+1/a₁}) under a polynomial-tail (beta) reward distribution assumption. Second, evolution-inspired state-jumping actions—mutation and crossover drawn from genetic programming—are triggered probabilistically at each node during selection, using a per-node priority queue of top-N high-reward trajectories. A bidirectional propagation mechanism updates both ancestor and descendant queues whenever a new high-reward expression is found, enabling efficient sharing of useful substructures across the tree. Empirically, the state-jumping actions reshape the reward landscape by reducing the effective tail-decay parameter from roughly a∈[4,6] to a∈[2,4], which accelerates convergence and makes the extreme-bandit strategy more robust to hyperparameter choices.

## Results

On the four ground-truth benchmarks (2 million evaluations, 100 runs), the proposed method achieves 93.25% average recovery on Nguyen, 100% on Nguyen C and Jin, and 71.41% on Livermore. On the SRBench Black-box benchmark (122 problems), the method ranks second in median test R² (just behind Operon) while producing substantially simpler models, and sits on the Pareto frontier of accuracy vs. model complexity alongside Operon, GP-COMEA, and DSR. The ablation study confirms that state-jumping actions are the primary driver of performance gains, with the UCB-extreme strategy providing additional improvement over standard UCB1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DSR (risk-seeking policy gradients), the proposed method wins on all four ground-truth benchmarks, with the largest margin on Livermore (71.41% vs. 30.41%) and Nguyen (93.25% vs. 83.58%). Against GEGL (GP + imitation learning), the method wins on Nguyen (93.25% vs. 86.00%), Jin (100% vs. 95.67%), and Livermore (71.41% vs. 56.36%), tying on Nguyen C. Against NGGP (neural-guided GP), the method is competitive: it slightly trails on Nguyen (93.25% vs. 92.33% — actually a narrow win), ties on Jin (both 100%), and edges ahead on Livermore (71.41% vs. 71.09%). Against PySR (genetic programming library), the method dominates across all benchmarks, with the largest gap on Nguyen C (100% vs. 65.40%). On the SRBench Black-box benchmark, the method ranks second in test accuracy behind Operon but produces substantially simpler models, placing it on the Pareto frontier alongside Operon, GP-COMEA, and DSR.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MCTS-for-symbolic-regression line (Kamienny et al. 2023, Sun et al. 2022, Shojaee et al. 2023, Xu et al. 2024) by replacing the standard UCB selection rule with an extreme-bandit allocation strategy grounded in the best-arm identification literature (Carpentier & Valko 2014), and by embedding genetic-programming mutation and crossover directly as state-jumping actions within the MCTS loop rather than alternating between separate GP and RL phases as in NGGP or GEGL. The key conceptual departure is reframing the selection problem from minimizing average regret to identifying the single maximum reward, which matches the actual SR objective, and providing finite-time regret guarantees under polynomial reward-decay assumptions that prior MCTS-SR work lacked.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first finite-time theoretical guarantees for an MCTS-based symbolic regression algorithm, and the empirical observation that state-jumping actions reshape the reward landscape (reducing the effective tail-decay parameter from ~[4,6] to ~[2,4]) offers a concrete mechanism explaining why hybrid GP+RL methods outperform pure sequential construction. Open questions include whether the polynomial-decay assumption holds broadly across SR problems, how the method scales to higher-dimensional or multi-objective settings, and whether the hyperparameter sensitivity of the UCB-extreme strategy (particularly the constant c) can be mitigated without the state-jumping crutch. The competitive-but-not-dominant performance on SRBench (second in accuracy, top on Pareto frontier) suggests the approach is a strong practical option but not a clear winner over Operon or GP-COMEA.
