## Problem

Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) fails to provide effective negative guidance in few-step diffusion models because positive and negative conditional predictions diverge dramatically after a single denoising step, causing severe artifacts rather than controlled suppression. Prior attention-space methods like NASA lack proper constraints, leading to out-of-manifold feature drift and instability, particularly in modern DiT architectures. This paper addresses the need for a stable, universal negative guidance mechanism that works across sampling regimes, architectures, and modalities without retraining.

## Input / Output

NAG operates as an inference-time plug-in that intercepts cross-attention (or MM-DiT) layers during the denoising loop, consuming the positive and negative text embeddings to compute separate attention outputs Z⁺ and Z⁻ at each step. It requires no architectural modification or retraining, and the additional computation is confined to cross-attention layers only—adding roughly 12–87% per-step latency depending on the model family, compared to CFG's ~100% overhead. The method is demonstrated on 480p images (COCO-5K) and 480p 5-second videos, spanning 1 to 25+ sampling steps across UNet and DiT backbones.

## Method

NAG applies CFG-style extrapolation directly in attention feature space (Z̃ = Z⁺ + φ(Z⁺ − Z⁻)) rather than in the output/noise space, motivated by a first-order Taylor analysis showing that layer-wise feature edits remain closer to local linearity than end-to-end output edits under aggressive step compression. Two stabilization mechanisms prevent out-of-manifold drift: (1) L1-norm-based normalization computes a point-wise ratio R[i] = ‖Z̃[i]‖₁/‖Z⁺[i]‖₁ and clips/rescales features exceeding a threshold τ, preserving directional guidance while bounding magnitude (L1 is chosen over L2 to retain low-magnitude activations that encode subtle semantics); and (2) feature refinement blends the normalized result with the positive baseline via Z^NAG = α·Ẑ + (1−α)·Z⁺, pulling extreme features back toward the stable manifold. The three hyperparameters (φ, τ, α) are set per architecture family and generalize across models without per-prompt tuning.

## Results

On COCO-5K, NAG consistently improves all four metrics across 7 models spanning 1–25 sampling steps and both DiT and UNet architectures. CLIP Score increases by +0.4 to +0.7 (e.g., 31.4→32.0 on Flux-Schnell 4-step, 31.1→31.8 on SDXL-Lightning 8-step), FID decreases by 0.18–2.93 (e.g., 25.47→24.46 on Flux-Schnell, 31.04→28.11 on Flux-Dev 25-step), PFID drops by 1.50–4.27, and ImageReward rises by +0.067 to +0.174 (e.g., 0.944→1.118 on SD3.5-Large-Turbo). The largest gains appear in few-step regimes (1–4 steps) where CFG is unavailable, confirming NAG's primary contribution of restoring negative guidance in this setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against NASA (Nguyen et al., 2024), NAG wins decisively: on UNet models (NitroSD-Realism, DMD2-SDXL, SDXL-Lightning), NAG improves CLIP by +0.2 to +0.4, FID by −1.12 to −1.88, PFID by −0.69 to −1.28, and ImageReward by +0.037 to +0.048 over NASA (Table 7). On DiT models, NASA produces severe instability and broken images even at low guidance scale, while NAG maintains stable outputs; a dedicated user study confirms +46.0% text-alignment and +56.8% visual-appeal preference for NAG over NASA. Against CFG (Ho & Salimans, 2022) as a standalone baseline in multi-step settings, NAG acts as a complementary add-on: on SD3.5-Large 25-step, CFG+NAG improves CLIP by +0.2 and ImageReward by +0.101 over CFG alone, though FID slightly worsens (+0.35). Against CFG+PAG (Xia et al., 2024), NAG further improves CLIP by +0.3 to +0.8 and ImageReward by +0.124 to +0.166, with FID improving by up to −2.72 on SDXL, demonstrating that NAG stacks effectively with existing guidance methods rather than replacing them.

## Relation to Prior Work

NAG sits at the intersection of two prior lines of work: Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG), which extrapolates in output/noise space, and NASA, which modifies cross-attention features directly but without magnitude constraints. The paper's first-order Taylor analysis (Section 4.1) provides the theoretical bridge, showing that feature-space edits remain closer to local linearity than output-space edits in few-step regimes, motivating the move of CFG-style extrapolation into attention features. The key departure from NASA is the addition of L1-norm-based clipping and alpha-blending refinement, which the authors identify as the missing constraints that prevent out-of-manifold drift—particularly in DiT architectures where feature spaces are more sensitive to perturbation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

NAG addresses a practical gap that has become increasingly urgent as few-step distilled models (1–8 steps) become the default for efficient inference: negative prompting simply does not work in this regime, limiting content safety, debiasing, and creative control. The training-free, architecture-agnostic nature of the method (a few lines of code in the cross-attention processor) makes it immediately deployable across the current diffusion ecosystem without retraining. Remaining limitations include failure to fully suppress certain concepts under extreme guidance, and the method's reliance on a fixed normalization threshold τ that may not adapt to all prompt complexities. Open questions include whether token-wise or layer-adaptive modulation could improve sensitivity to nuanced negative prompts, and whether the approach extends to other conditioning modalities beyond text.
