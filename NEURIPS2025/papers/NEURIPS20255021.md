## Problem

Large reasoning models (LRMs) are prone to overthinking, but whether this iterative chain-of-thought mechanism can be adversarially hijacked to induce perpetual, non-terminating reasoning loops remains unexplored. Existing slowdown attacks merely inflate output length via decoy content, while prior adversarial work targets accuracy or safety rather than computational resource exhaustion. This paper asks whether a single-token perturbation can fundamentally lock an LRM into an infinite reasoning cycle, exhausting its test-time compute budget.

## Input / Output

The attack takes a standard text query (e.g., a math problem) with a short trigger token sequence (e.g., "!!!!") prepended, and requires white-box access to the target LRM's embedding matrix for one-time backdoor implantation. The output is an unbounded reasoning trace that generates transitional tokens ("Wait", "But") after every punctuation mark, forcing the model to hit its maximum generation limit (4000–20000 tokens in experiments). The attack is input-agnostic: the same single adversarial embedding works universally across all queries and benchmarks without per-sample tuning.

## Method

The Deadlock Attack operates in two phases. First, a single continuous adversarial embedding vector (L=1, d-dimensional) is optimized via gradient descent to maximize the probability that the LRM generates reflective tokens (e.g., "Wait", "But") immediately after end-of-step punctuation, using a loss that averages these probabilities over all punctuation positions in reference reasoning traces (Eq. 3–4). Second, because naively projecting this continuous embedding to the nearest vocabulary tokens nullifies the attack (the continuous-to-discrete projection gap, validated via linear mode connectivity analysis), the optimized vector is instead directly implanted into the model's embedding matrix as the representation of a chosen trigger token, creating a backdoor. This backdoor mechanism preserves stealthiness—benign inputs without the trigger produce normal outputs—while the trigger activates the deadlock loop. Training requires only 20 (problem, answer) pairs sampled from MATH500 level 5, and the resulting attack achieves 100% success rate across four LRMs and three benchmarks, remaining robust against overthinking mitigation strategies like CoD, CCoT, and NoThinking.

## Results

The Deadlock Attack achieves a 100% Attack Success Rate (ASR) across all four evaluated LRMs (Phi-RM, Nemotron-Nano, R1-Qwen, R1-Llama) on all three reasoning benchmarks (GSM8K, MATH500, MMLU-Pro Math), forcing models to generate the full 4000-token limit. Baseline models without the attack show ASR between 0% and 14%, with average token counts of 525–1631 and inference times of 13.8–47.1 seconds, whereas DA-activated models consistently hit 4000 tokens with inference times of 106–119 seconds (~4× increase). In an extended evaluation with a 20,000-token limit on the challenging AIME benchmark, DA maintains 93–100% ASR versus a baseline natural exhaustion rate of only 13–17%, confirming the attack induces truly anomalous non-terminating behavior rather than amplifying existing failure modes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates DA's robustness against three test-time computing strategies designed to mitigate overthinking: Chain of Draft (CoD) [28], Concise Chain-of-Thought (CCoT) [29], and NoThinking [75]. All three strategies fail to reduce the attack's effectiveness: ASR remains 100% across all four models under every mitigation, and average inference times stay in the 96–131 second range (compared to ~106–118 s without mitigation), confirming that prompt-level brevity instructions and decoding-level tag-bypassing cannot counter the embedding-level hijacking of the reasoning loop. The paper also compares DA against the baseline (unmodified) models, where ASR ranges from 0% (Phi-RM on GSM8K, R1-Qwen on MMLU-Pro) to 14% (Phi-RM on MMLU-Pro Math), demonstrating that the attack's 100% success rate is not an artifact of natural overthinking on difficult inputs. In the extended 20,000-token evaluation on AIME 2024, the baseline natural exhaustion rate is 13.33–16.67%, while DA achieves 93.33–100%, further separating attack-induced deadlock from inherent model behavior.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the closest prior work, Kumar et al.'s slowdown attack [38], which injects decoy problems to inflate output length, by instead hijacking the model's internal CoT reasoning control flow via an optimized adversarial embedding that forces perpetual transitional tokens ("Wait", "But") after punctuation. It also repurposes the overthinking phenomenon—previously studied purely as an efficiency problem [12, 13]—as an adversarial vulnerability surface, and connects the continuous-to-discrete projection gap to Rando et al.'s [40] broader observation that gradient-based attacks in continuous proxy spaces fail when projected to discrete token inputs. The backdoor implantation mechanism borrows from the LLM backdoor literature [35–37] but applies it not to steer outputs toward specific targets, but to serve as a carrier for a continuous adversarial vector that cannot be reliably expressed as discrete tokens.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper exposes a practical AI supply-chain risk: a single modified embedding vector in a publicly released model can be remotely activated to exhaust inference resources, and existing overthinking mitigations (CoD, CCoT, NoThinking) provide no defense. The continuous-to-discrete projection gap is a broader finding with implications for all adversarial attacks on LLMs, suggesting that continuous-space optimizations may be fundamentally unrealizable as discrete prompts without model modification. Open questions include whether discrete triggers can be crafted directly via zeroth-order or query-based optimization for closed-source APIs, whether deadlock-inducing patterns transfer across model families, and what principled defenses (beyond per-step repetition detection, which is computationally expensive) can identify and remove implanted adversarial embeddings.
