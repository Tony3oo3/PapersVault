## Problem

Prior work on metric distortion in distributed voting (Anshelevich et al., 2022) established only loose bounds for deterministic mechanisms—leaving gaps such as an 11 upper bound for avg-max and a 2+√5 lower bound for max-avg—and no formal analysis existed for randomized distributed mechanisms in the metric setting. This paper closes the deterministic gaps and provides the first systematic characterization of distortion for both rand-det and rand-rand distributed mechanisms across all four cost objectives.

## Input / Output

The mechanism receives n voters partitioned into k groups, where each voter's ordinal ranking over m candidates is induced by an underlying metric distance function d. The in-group rule f_in sees only its group's size and local preference profile, while the over-group rule f_ov receives the representatives' preferences and all group sizes. Deterministic mechanisms output a single winning candidate; randomized mechanisms output a probability distribution over candidates, with expected cost evaluated under the four objectives (avg-avg, avg-max, max-avg, max-max).

## Method

For upper bounds, the paper composes simple rules: Plurality Matching (or its Pareto-efficient variant f_pm-par) as the in-group rule paired with uniform random selection as the over-group rule for rand-det, and Random Dictatorship paired with uniform random selection for rand-rand. A key insight is that Pareto efficiency of the in-group rule alone suffices to achieve distortion 3 for avg-max and max-max in rand-det, and makes the avg-max upper bound for det-det independent of the in-group distortion α (yielding 2β+3=7 instead of α+β+αβ=11). For lower bounds, the paper introduces the Bias Tournament—a complete directed graph encoding the tie-breaking bias of any deterministic rule across candidate pairs—and exploits the fact that every tournament contains a node with in-degree at least ⌈(m−1)/2⌉ to identify a 'losing' candidate that anchors hard instances on line metrics or tree graphs. The avg-avg lower bound for rand-det (5−2/k) requires a more delicate graph-based construction where the metric is defined via shortest-path distances in a star-like tree, and Euclidean lower bounds (√5−ε for rand-rand, 2+√5−ε for rand-det) are obtained by embedding instances in a hyper-simplex.

## Results

The paper establishes tight or near-tight distortion bounds for distributed voting across four cost objectives (avg-avg, avg-max, max-avg, max-max) and three mechanism classes (det-det, rand-det, rand-rand). For deterministic mechanisms, the avg-max upper bound is improved from 11 to 7, the max-avg lower bound is tightened from 2+√5 to 5 (matching the known upper bound), and the max-max upper bound is reduced from 5 to 3. For randomized mechanisms, all rand-det bounds are tight (5−2/k for avg-avg, 3 for avg-max and max-max, 5 for max-avg), and rand-rand achieves tight bounds of 3 for max-avg and max-max, with near-tight bounds of [3−2/n, 3−2/(kn*)] for avg-avg and [3−2/n, 3] for avg-max.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two prior works: Anshelevich et al. [1] (det-det bounds in general metric spaces) and Voudouris [25] (tight line-metric bounds). Against Anshelevich et al., the paper strictly improves three det-det bounds: the avg-max upper bound drops from 11 to 7 (a 36% reduction), the max-avg lower bound rises from 2+√5 ≈ 4.236 to 5 (closing the gap and proving tightness), and the max-max upper bound falls from 5 to 3 (also closing the gap). The paper's primary contribution is the first analysis of randomized distributed mechanisms, establishing that rand-det achieves tight bounds for all four objectives and rand-rand achieves tight bounds for max-avg and max-max (both at 3) with near-tight bounds for avg-avg and avg-max. A notable side result is a lower bound of 3−ε for the max objective in centralized randomized voting, matching the known deterministic upper bound of 3 up to an arbitrarily small gap.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the metric distortion framework for distributed voting established by Anshelevich et al. [1] and Voudouris [25], which focused exclusively on deterministic mechanisms. It introduces the first formal analysis of randomized distributed mechanisms (rand-det and rand-rand) in the metric setting, building on centralized randomized results from Anshelevich and Postl [15] and Kempe [37]. The novel Bias Tournament tool generalizes pairwise comparison analysis to capture tie-breaking biases in deterministic in-group rules, enabling lower-bound constructions that were previously intractable. The deterministic improvements (e.g., avg-max upper bound 11→7) build on the α-in-β-over framework of [1] but exploit Pareto efficiency to decouple the in-group rule's distortion from the overall bound.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides a near-complete characterization of distortion in distributed voting across all four cost objectives and both deterministic and randomized settings, resolving several previously open gaps (max-avg lower bound, max-max upper bound, avg-max upper bound). The finding that Pareto efficiency alone suffices for the in-group rule in the avg-max objective (making the over-group rule dominant) is a structural insight that simplifies mechanism design. Key open problems include the det-rand setting (randomized first stage, deterministic second), where the Bias Tournament tool is incompatible, and closing the remaining narrow gaps in avg-avg and avg-max for rand-rand. The Euclidean lower bounds (√5−ε and 2+√5−ε) suggest that general metric constructions may not be tight in structured spaces, pointing to a gap between general and restricted metric analyses.
