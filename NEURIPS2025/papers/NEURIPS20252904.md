## Problem

Existing causal discovery algorithms like PC become computationally prohibitive in high-dimensional settings, motivating the use of variable clusters to improve scalability. However, naively applying PC to clusters (treating each cluster as a multivariate variable) produces incorrect edge orientations because the independence structure over clusters does not map one-to-one onto collider/non-collider patterns the way it does for individual variables. No standard DAG over clusters can simultaneously encode all marginal and conditional (in)dependencies between clusters, necessitating a new graphical representation.

## Input / Output

The method takes a user-defined admissible partition of variables V into k clusters C = {C1, ..., Ck} (admissible meaning no apparent directed cycles over clusters) and a probability distribution P(C) over clusters that is faithful to the true data-generating process. It produces an α C-CPDAG, a cluster-level equivalence class graph containing directed/undirected edges, three types of independence arcs (marginally-connecting, conditionally-connecting, never-connecting), separation marks flagging unexpected independencies masked by clustering, and connection marks flagging dependencies created by conditioning on descendants of within-cluster colliders. Unlike a standard CPDAG, the α C-CPDAG explicitly encodes all cluster-level (in)dependence information, not just adjacencies and shared orientations.

## Method

The paper introduces α C-DAGs, a new graphical model that augments C-DAGs with independence arcs (annotating each triplet with its marginal and conditional (in)dependence status), separation marks (denoting paths that appear connected by arc trajectories but are actually blocked at the variable level), and connection marks (denoting triplets activated by conditioning on descendants of colliders within a cluster). A novel d-separation criterion over α C-DAGs is defined via arc trajectories and proven sound and complete. The paper then defines cluster Markov equivalence and the α C-CPDAG as its canonical representative, and proposes CLOC, a three-phase constraint-based algorithm: (1) skeleton construction plus independence arc assignment and collider identification via conditionally-connecting arcs, (2) addition of separation and connection marks via targeted independence tests over longer paths, and (3) iterative application of five orientation rules (R0–R5) that extend PC/Meek rules with cluster-specific logic, including a novel Rule 5 that orients edges based on connection marks. The algorithm is proven sound and complete for recovering the full cluster Markov equivalence class.

## Results

The paper evaluates CLOC against a PC-then-Cluster baseline on randomly generated C-DAGs (3–8 clusters) with compatible DAGs (4–256 variables) and Gaussian distributions (1,000–30,000 samples). Results are presented as comparative plots (Figure 5) rather than a numerical table. The headline finding is that PC requires exponentially more conditional-independence tests than CLOC as the number of variables grows, and CLOC also achieves improved runtime. Structural Hamming Distances (SHD) between learned and true C-DAGs differ in 'expected ways,' with CLOC operating at cluster granularity and thus not resolving all variable-level orientations that PC-then-Cluster can capture.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is a 'PC-then-Cluster' approach: run the standard PC algorithm over all individual variables, then group the resulting CPDAG edges by the predefined partition to obtain a graph over clusters. CLOC wins decisively on efficiency: the number of conditional-independence tests grows exponentially for PC-then-Cluster with the number of variables, while CLOC's test count scales with the (much smaller) number of clusters. Runtime is also improved for CLOC, and the authors note that more efficient multivariate independence tests (e.g., Mantel Test) could widen this gap further. On accuracy (SHD to the true C-DAG), the comparison is nuanced: PC-then-Cluster can orient some edges that CLOC leaves undirected because it has access to variable-level independence information, but CLOC's orientations are guaranteed sound and complete at the cluster level. The paper does not report a single aggregate SHD number; instead, it notes the differences are 'in expected ways' and defers detailed discussion to Appendix D.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the C-DAG framework (Anand et al., AAAI 2023) from a knowledge-based representation for causal effect identification to a learnable graphical model for causal discovery. It builds on the PC algorithm's constraint-based skeleton and collider search, and on Meek's orientation rules, but demonstrates that both fail when naively applied to clusters, motivating the introduction of independence arcs, separation marks, and connection marks as new graphical semantics. Unlike prior cluster-based causal discovery work that treats clusters as intermediate steps toward variable-level structure (Squires et al., Pashami et al., Yehezkel et al.), this paper takes user-defined clusters as the primary target of learning, producing a cluster-level equivalence class (α C-CPDAG) as its output.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first sound and complete algorithm for causal discovery directly over user-defined clusters in Markovian systems, addressing a scalability bottleneck in high-dimensional settings where variable-level PC becomes computationally prohibitive. The key conceptual contribution—showing that standard collider tests and orientation rules break down at the cluster level and requiring new graphical objects to restore completeness—is a foundational result for anyone working with grouped variables. Limitations include the requirement for a user-specified admissible partition, the Markovian (no latent confounding) assumption, and faithfulness, all of which restrict applicability. Open questions include extending the framework to non-Markovian settings (latent confounding), learning partitions automatically, and developing efficient multivariate independence tests that would further improve the practical runtime advantage over PC-then-Cluster.
