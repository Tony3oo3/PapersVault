## Problem

While gradient clipping has received substantial theoretical analysis under (L₀,L₁)-smoothness, the broader family of nonlinearly preconditioned gradient methods—generated by general convex reference functions φ—remains largely unexplored. In particular, no convergence guarantees existed for heavy-ball momentum variants or stochastic variants of these methods under the anisotropic descent inequality, a smoothness condition strictly less restrictive than both Lipschitz and (L₀,L₁)-smoothness.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a smooth nonconvex objective f, a (stochastic) first-order oracle, an initial point x⁰, and hyperparameters (stepsize γ, momentum β ∈ [0,1)). The reference function φ (e.g., cosh‖·‖−1) defines the nonlinear preconditioner ∇φ*, which adaptively rescales the gradient rather than clipping it to a fixed norm. The output is a sequence of iterates with convergence rates expressed in the natural stationarity measure φ(∇φ*(∇f(x))), which for the cosh reference function reduces to √(1+‖∇f(x)‖²)−1, closely related to the standard gradient norm.

## Method

The paper introduces a heavy-ball momentum scheme (Algorithm 1) where momentum is applied to the *preconditioned* gradients—i.e., mᵏ = βmᵏ⁻¹ + (1−β)∇φ*(∇f(xᵏ))—making it equivalent to standard heavy ball on the composite mapping ∇φ*∘∇f, rather than the more common approach of aggregating raw gradients first. Convergence is established under the anisotropic descent inequality via a proof technique relying solely on the convexity (and evenness) of φ, yielding an O(1/K) sublinear rate for general nonconvex f, a linear rate under a generalized PL (anisotropic gradient dominance) condition when φ is 2-subhomogeneous, and an extended momentum range β∈(0,1) under a new preconditioned Lipschitz continuity assumption. A stochastic variant applies the preconditioner directly to the noisy gradient and is analyzed under a novel noise condition—bounded φ-distance between preconditioned true and stochastic gradients—that is shown to be less restrictive than bounded variance for cosh-type reference functions. The key design insight is that the anisotropic descent inequality, a natural generalization of Euclidean smoothness via Φ-convexity, provides the correct majorization framework for these methods, and the analysis unifies across isotropic and separable reference functions.

## Results

On MNIST (two-layer MLP, cross-entropy loss), the proposed iHGD method reaches small training losses faster than SGD and Adam, with confidence intervals over five random seeds. On CIFAR-10 (ResNet-18), the proposed methods with momentum (iHGDm, β=0.9) perform comparably to SGDm and Adam, which are known to be state-of-the-art for this architecture. On the nonconvex, non-Lipschitz-smooth matrix factorization problem (MovieLens 100K, r∈{10,20,30}), iHGDm significantly outperforms GD, GDm, and AdGD-accel in both convergence speed and stability across random initializations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On MNIST, iHGD (the stochastic base method with cosh−1 preconditioner) achieves lower training loss faster than both SGD and Adam [19], indicating the adaptive sigmoid-shaped preconditioner provides a practical advantage over fixed-stepsize and per-parameter adaptive methods. On CIFAR-10 with ResNet-18, the proposed iHGDm matches the performance of SGDm and Adam [19] without outperforming them, suggesting the benefit of the nonlinear preconditioner is less pronounced for this well-conditioned deep learning task. On the matrix factorization problem (a quartic polynomial, hence not globally Lipschitz smooth), iHGDm significantly outperforms AdGD-accel [33] across all rank values (r=10, 20, 30), with notably more stable convergence across random initializations. The paper also compares against GD and GDm on matrix factorization, where iHGDm again dominates, particularly given that GDm's stepsize (1/300) was near the convergence boundary. Overall, the proposed methods show the clearest advantage on problems that violate standard Lipschitz smoothness, while matching (not exceeding) strong baselines on well-behaved neural network training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the dual space preconditioning / anisotropic gradient descent framework (Maddison et al. 2021; Laude & Patrinos 2025; Oikonomidis et al. 2025) by incorporating heavy-ball momentum applied to the preconditioned gradients rather than raw gradients, a design the authors argue is more natural for the anisotropic descent inequality. It also connects to the gradient clipping literature (Zhang et al. 2020; Zhang et al. 2020) by showing that sigmoid-shaped preconditioners (e.g., cosh-1) perform adaptive gradient rescaling rather than hard clipping. The stochastic analysis introduces a noise condition that is shown to be less restrictive than bounded variance for specific reference functions, building on the generalized smoothness framework that subsumes (L0,L1)-smoothness.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first convergence guarantees for momentum-augmented anisotropic gradient descent under a smoothness condition strictly weaker than (L0,L1)-smoothness, opening the door to analyzing a broader class of nonconvex problems (e.g., polynomial losses) that arise in practice. Key open questions include lifting the β < 0.5 restriction in the general setting, developing a stochastic momentum variant, and extending the proximal gradient framework to incorporate momentum for constrained problems. The empirical results on matrix factorization (a nonconvex, non-globally-smooth problem) suggest practical relevance, though the neural network experiments show parity rather than clear superiority over SGD/SGDm.
