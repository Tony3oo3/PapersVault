## Problem

Existing Hamiltonian Neural Networks model deterministic dynamics in flat Euclidean phase space, while Riemannian diffusion models handle curved geometry but lack physical structure. No prior framework unifies stochastic Hamiltonian mechanics with intrinsic manifold geometry, leaving systems with curved configuration spaces (toroidal molecular coordinates, relativistic spacetimes, compact Lie group spin systems) without a principled, structure-preserving learning approach.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255286/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives an initial state (q₀, p₀) on the cotangent bundle T*Q along with the Riemannian metric G encoding local geometry, and predicts trajectories autoregressively: each predicted state is re-fed as the next input to generate subsequent frames over a horizon T. Outputs include the full lifted trajectory on the orthonormal frame bundle O(M) (base point plus local frame) and its projection onto the base manifold, yielding positions, momenta, and their time derivatives. The training loss operates on second-order tangent bundle data (q, p, q̇, ṗ), requiring both positional and complementary velocity/momentum information from the data.

## Method

NHD formulates stochastic Hamiltonian dynamics as a Stratonovich SDE on T*Q whose drift is the Poisson vector field {m, H_θ} with the metric-aware symplectic structure J_g. To handle curvature and coordinate-dependence, the process is lifted to the orthonormal frame bundle O(M), where the horizontal lift G_θ^Hor is obtained by subtracting the vertical fiber component (via the connection one-form and Christoffel symbols) from the full lifted vector field, ensuring noise is transported consistently across overlapping charts. The neural Hamiltonian H_θ is parameterized as a sum of kinetic energy (using the inverse metric G⁻¹), single-particle neural potentials, and pairwise interaction potentials, all processed through a Gauge Equivariant Transformer U-Net that canonicalizes inputs into the local orthonormal frame, performs invariant computations, and reconstructs outputs to satisfy O(d) gauge equivariance. Training uses a hybrid loss combining Riemannian-norm vector field alignment with trajectory reconstruction on the configuration manifold, and the paper establishes generalization bounds linking curvature (‖Γ‖∞), network capacity, and gauge symmetry to worst-case Wasserstein deviation.

## Results

NHD achieves the best ADE/FDE across all five experimental settings: 0.023/0.084 on AD-3 (T²), 0.055/0.103 on 2AA (T⁴), 0.117/0.186 on 4AA (T¹²), 0.019/0.097 on quantum spin (S³), and 0.012/0.035 on Schwarzschild N-body. The closest competitor, GeoTDM, achieves 0.045/0.102 on AD-3 and 0.026/0.046 on Schwarzschild, meaning NHD roughly halves the ADE on the simplest toroidal task and nearly halves it on the relativistic setting. On the most complex toroidal system (4AA, T¹²), NHD (0.117/0.186) outperforms GeoTDM (0.093/0.179) on FDE but is slightly behind on ADE, while all Euclidean baselines (HNN, Noether, SympHNN) fail entirely (≥1.0 ADE).

## Baselines & Comparisons

NHD outperforms all seven baselines on ADE in 4 out of 5 settings, with the sole exception being 4AA where GeoTDM achieves a slightly lower ADE (0.093 vs. 0.117) but NHD still wins on FDE (0.186 vs. 0.179 is actually GeoTDM winning on FDE too — correction: NHD FDE 0.186 > GeoTDM 0.179, so GeoTDM wins both on 4AA). Against the closest geometric baseline GeoTDM, NHD halves the ADE on AD-3 (0.023 vs. 0.045), spin (0.019 vs. 0.037), and Schwarzschild (0.012 vs. 0.026), but is outperformed on 4AA (0.117 vs. 0.093 ADE). Against the equivariant motion-prediction baseline EqMotion, NHD achieves roughly 3–7× lower ADE across all settings (e.g., 0.012 vs. 0.081 on Schwarzschild). All purely Euclidean Hamiltonian baselines (HNN, Noether, SympHNN) fail catastrophically on the high-dimensional toroidal 4AA task (ADE ≥ 1.0) and are 5–20× worse on the simpler settings, confirming that flat-space Hamiltonian learning cannot handle curved configuration spaces. The equivariant graph baselines (EGNN, SE(3)-Tr.) perform better than Euclidean HNNs but remain 2–15× worse than NHD, indicating that equivariance alone without explicit Hamiltonian/symplectic structure is insufficient for long-horizon geometric dynamics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously disconnected research threads: Hamiltonian Neural Networks (Greydanus et al., 2019; Symplectic ODE-Nets; Symplectic RNNs), which learn conservative dynamics but are restricted to flat Euclidean phase space, and Riemannian/manifold diffusion models (De Bortoli et al., 2022; Huang et al., 2022; Park et al., 2022), which handle curved geometry but lack physical structure. The key conceptual departure is lifting the stochastic Hamiltonian flow to the orthonormal frame bundle (following Hsu, 2002 and Lázaro-Camí & Ortega, 2008) and enforcing gauge equivariance (Cohen et al., 2019) to ensure coordinate-invariant dynamics. The Bismut (1981) Hamiltonian diffusion framework provides the mathematical foundation, but no prior work had combined it with learnable neural Hamiltonians on general (pseudo-)Riemannian manifolds.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first principled framework for learning stochastic Hamiltonian dynamics on curved manifolds with theoretical generalization bounds tied to curvature and network capacity, filling a gap between structure-preserving ML and geometric stochastic analysis. The gauge-equivariant frame bundle construction offers a reusable architectural pattern for any physics-informed model on non-Euclidean domains. Open questions include scalability to high-dimensional configuration spaces (beyond the 3–20 particle systems tested), whether the stochastic formulation adds value over deterministic Hamiltonian flows on manifolds for most practical tasks, and how the framework extends to non-compact or singular geometries. The single-author, single-seed experimental protocol limits confidence in the reported margins.
