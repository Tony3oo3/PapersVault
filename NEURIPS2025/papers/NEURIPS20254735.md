## Problem

Existing visible-infrared image fusion methods are designed for clean source images and fail under real-world noise, while degradation-aware fusion methods are supervised and depend on constructed paired noisy/clean data, limiting generalization to specific noise types and fixed variances. No prior method can jointly perform denoising and fusion in an unsupervised manner that generalizes across various and variable noise conditions without requiring clean reference images.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254735/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of noisy visible (3-channel RGB) and noisy infrared (single-channel) images as input, where noise is additive Gaussian or multiplicative speckle with randomly distributed variance σ ∈ [10, 50]. Training operates on 128×128 patches from 2120 image pairs across LLVIP and M3FD datasets, with no clean reference images used. The output is a single clean fused image that simultaneously suppresses noise and integrates complementary visible details with infrared thermal prominence.

## Method

Deno-IF comprises two modules: (1) a convolutional low-rank optimization module that iteratively (30 steps, closed-form solutions) decomposes each noisy input into a clean low-rank component and a noise component via convolution nuclear norm minimization, providing unsupervised optimization guidance; and (2) I2Former, a Transformer-based joint denoising-and-fusion network with intra-modal recovery (self-attention within each modality using multi-Dconv head transposed attention) and inter-modal recovery and fusion (cross-modal attention integrating complementary information). The training loss combines data fidelity terms (intensity, gradient, chrominance) against the inferred clean components with a convolutional low-rank regularization on the fused image itself. The key insight is that denoising and fusion share the objective of extracting essential clean information, enabling a single streamlined network to replace the redundant sequential denoise-then-fuse pipeline while reducing parameter count to 1.43M.

## Results

On LLVIP with Gaussian noise, Deno-IF achieves SSIM 0.616, PSNR 17.070, FSIM 0.811, CC 0.738, and BRISQUE 16.725, outperforming the best pre-denoising+fusion pipeline (CT.+DDFM: SSIM 0.474, PSNR 16.782) and all degradation-aware fusion methods (DRMF: SSIM 0.148; Text-IF: SSIM 0.208; OmniFuse: SSIM 0.377). On M3FD with Gaussian noise, Deno-IF reaches SSIM 0.585, PSNR 15.637, and BRISQUE 24.298, again leading all competitors. Under speckle noise, Deno-IF achieves the best results on both datasets (e.g., SSIM 0.633 on LLVIP, 0.656 on M3FD) with the lowest BRISQUE scores (9.280 and 16.514 respectively), demonstrating robustness across noise types and levels.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Deno-IF outperforms all ten baselines on the majority of metrics across both datasets and both noise types. Against the strongest pre-denoising+fusion pipeline (CT.+DDFM), Deno-IF gains +0.142 SSIM and +0.288 PSNR on LLVIP (Gaussian), and +0.096 SSIM and +0.112 PSNR on M3FD, while reducing BRISQUE from 30.6 to 16.7 (LLVIP) and 35.8 to 24.3 (M3FD); the only narrow losses are FSIM on LLVIP (0.811 vs 0.815) and CC on M3FD (0.563 vs 0.587). Against the supervised degradation-aware methods, the gap is substantial: DRMF achieves only SSIM 0.148 and BRISQUE 67.9 on LLVIP (Gaussian), while Text-IF reaches SSIM 0.208 and BRISQUE 54.5, and OmniFuse reaches SSIM 0.377 and BRISQUE 41.0 — all far below Deno-IF. Under speckle noise, Deno-IF leads all methods on every metric for both datasets (e.g., SSIM 0.633 vs. 0.538 for CT.+Meta. on LLVIP; SSIM 0.656 vs. 0.649 for DRMF on M3FD), with BRISQUE scores of 9.3 and 16.5 versus 10.9–48.3 for competitors. The unsupervised nature of Deno-IF (no clean reference needed during training) contrasts with all CT.+X pipelines that rely on the supervised CTNet denoiser, and with DRMF/Text-IF/OmniFuse which require paired noisy-clean training data, yet Deno-IF still achieves superior generalization across variable noise levels (σ ∈ [10, 50]).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of degradation-aware image fusion (Text-IF, DRMF, OmniFuse) by removing the dependence on paired noisy/clean training data, replacing supervised mapping learning with an unsupervised convolutional low-rank decomposition that infers clean components from noisy inputs alone. Architecturally, it builds on Transformer-based restoration (Restormer/CTNet) but restructures the pipeline into a joint intra-modal recovery + inter-modal fusion design (I2Former) rather than treating denoising and fusion as sequential, independent tasks. The mathematical foundation draws on convolution nuclear norm minimization (Liu & Zhang, 2022), repurposing it not as a standalone denoising objective but as a physics-driven prior that guides network training and regularizes the fused output.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The elimination of paired-data dependency is practically significant: real-world noisy infrared/visible pairs with known clean ground truth are essentially unavailable, so supervised degradation-aware fusion methods are hard to deploy in new noise regimes. The 1.4M-parameter network achieving competitive or superior results against 50–600M-parameter baselines suggests that the convolutional low-rank prior encodes much of the denoising structure that large networks learn implicitly. However, all experiments use simulated Gaussian and speckle noise on two datasets; generalization to real sensor noise (e.g., non-stationary microbolometer artifacts, motion blur) remains untested. The iterative low-rank optimization module, while not needed at inference, dominates training cost and is identified by the authors as the key bottleneck for future efficiency improvements.
