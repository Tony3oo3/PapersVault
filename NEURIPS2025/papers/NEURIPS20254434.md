## Problem

Existing diffusion-based image editing methods rely on direct feature replacement from the source reconstruction branch to the target generation branch, which introduces semantic incompatibility and artifacts, especially for non-rigid edits involving pose changes or object addition/removal. Additionally, the strong inductive bias carried by the source image's features constrains the base model's generative capacity, preventing the large structural transformations that non-rigid editing demands.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254434/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a source RGB image, a source text prompt describing the original content, and a target text prompt specifying the desired edit, and produces a single edited RGB image. It is entirely tuning-free and operates in the latent space of either a UNet-based LDM (Stable Diffusion v1.5) or a DiT v3.5-Medium backbone, requiring only 50 inversion steps and a CFG scale of 7.5 during generation. The full pipeline (inversion plus generation) completes in approximately 20 seconds on a single RTX 4090 GPU with 17 GB memory.

## Method

FSI-Edit introduces three tuning-free modules applied during the early denoising steps. First, Frequency Residual Fusion (FRF) operates in self-attention layers by performing FFT-based Gaussian filtering to inject only the high-frequency components of source Query and Key features into the target, while preserving the target's low-frequency structure; Value is directly replaced from the source. Second, Stochastic Noise Injection (SNI) adds controlled Gaussian noise to the Query and Key of cross-attention layers (initialized from source) and to the frequency-fused self-attention features, expanding the latent generative space to enable structural transformations beyond the source image's constraints. Third, Inversion Trajectory Navigation (ITN) refines the DDIM or Rectified Flow inversion trajectory by blending high-frequency components of each latent x_t with low-frequency components of x_{t-1}, stabilizing source reconstruction while retaining fine detail. For rigid edits, the noise coefficients are annealed to zero to revert to deterministic attention, preserving precise structure.

## Results

On PIE-Bench (700 image-prompt pairs, 10 editing categories), FSI-Edit-DiT achieves the best Structure Distance (13.71×10⁻³) and highest CLIP similarity (25.69 whole, 22.50 edited) among all compared methods, while maintaining competitive background preservation (PSNR 26.61, SSIM 86.25). On the non-rigid editing subset (300 samples), FSI-Edit-DiT attains the highest CLIP scores (26.58 whole, 22.16 edited) with the best MSE (59.95×10⁻⁴) among DiT-based methods. The LDM variant (FSI-Edit-LDM) also outperforms all UNet-based baselines on CLIP similarity (25.46 whole) while remaining competitive on background preservation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FSI-Edit-DiT outperforms all DiT-based baselines (RF-Inv, StableFlow, RF-Edit, DCEdit) on Structure Distance (13.71 vs. 19.24–48.76) and CLIP similarity (25.69 whole vs. 24.30–25.47), while also achieving the best MSE (36.50) and SSIM (86.25) among transformer-based methods. Against UNet-based baselines, FSI-Edit-DiT surpasses PnP, MasaCtrl, FlexiEdit, and FreeDiff on all metrics except PSNR, where P2P (27.22) and FlexiEdit (25.74) retain a slight edge in background preservation. P2P remains the strongest background-preserving method (LPIPS 54.55, MSE 32.86) but lags FSI-Edit on CLIP edited-region similarity (22.10 vs. 22.50) and Structure Distance (11.65 vs. 13.71). On the non-rigid editing subset, FSI-Edit-DiT achieves the highest CLIP scores (26.58 whole, 22.16 edited) and best MSE (59.95) among all methods, with FlexiEdit performing worst (Structure Distance 76.91, LPIPS 236.02). The user study (48 responses) confirms FSI-Edit-DiT's advantage with the lowest mean rank (2.66±1.96) versus the next-best RF-Edit (3.92±2.29) and PnP (3.94±2.22).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the tuning-free diffusion editing paradigm established by P2P, PnP, and MasaCtrl, which inject features from a source reconstruction branch into a target generation branch to preserve unedited content. It departs from prior work in two key ways: (1) replacing direct feature injection with frequency-domain residual fusion at the attention-feature level (rather than the latent level used by FlexiEdit or FreeDiff), and (2) introducing controlled stochastic noise into attention queries and keys to expand the generative space for non-rigid structural changes. The inversion trajectory navigation (ITN) module further generalizes the frequency-fusion idea to the temporal inversion sequence, complementing prior reconstruction-refining methods like Null-text Inversion and Edit-Friendly Inversion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FSI-Edit addresses a practical gap in diffusion-based editing: existing feature-replacement methods degrade significantly on non-rigid tasks (pose changes, object addition/removal) due to semantic mismatch and over-constrained generation. The tuning-free, backbone-agnostic design (LDM and DiT) and single-GPU efficiency (20s/image on RTX 4090) make it accessible for practical deployment. However, the paper acknowledges limitations in color-specific edits and fine-grained localized modifications, and future work on mask-based spatial guidance and color-aware frequency strategies remains open. The broader implication is that frequency-aware feature manipulation combined with controlled stochasticity may become a standard component in diffusion editing pipelines.
