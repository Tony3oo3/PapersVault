## Problem

Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) become stale because their knowledge is frozen at training-data cutoff dates, yet no existing dataset or benchmark systematically evaluates how well models can seek or update with visual knowledge that post-dates those cutoffs. This paper addresses two gaps: (1) the absence of a large-scale, non-contaminated VQA dataset built exclusively from post-cutoff visual content, and (2) the lack of a controlled study on whether parameter-efficient fine-tuning can inject new visual knowledge without degrading general visual perception.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252513/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each LIVEVQA instance pairs a single visually distinctive image (sourced from news articles, YouTube keyframes, or arXiv figures, all dated April 2024–May 2025) with one Level-1 question (basic entity recognition: person, location, organization, event, object) and up to three Level-2 questions (multi-hop cross-modal reasoning across seven types: location, person, organization, time, event, count, reason). Questions are available in both open-ended and multiple-choice formats, each accompanied by a synthetic chain-of-thought reasoning trajectory. For the updating experiments, the model additionally outputs LoRA or DoRA adapter weights trained on the 26,988-image training split.

## Method

The paper introduces a fully automated data engine that crawls fresh visual content from CNN/BBC/Forbes news, YouTube (CC-BY-4.0, ≤10 min, English-subtitled), and arXiv (Physics/CS/Math), then applies multi-stage LLM/MLLM-in-the-loop filtering (URL validation, image-event relevance via GPT-4.1, perceptual-hash deduplication, DocLayout-YOLO text cropping, and SimHash duplicate removal) with >97% human-validated pass rate. Questions are generated at two difficulty levels: Level 1 targets visual entity recognition, while Level 2 requires multi-hop reasoning that links image details to textual metadata, with verifiability enforced by requiring GPT-4.1 to answer correctly given the full context. The empirical study benchmarks 17 MLLMs under four search configurations (no search, text search, native image search, MM-Search) and measures calibration via confidence-accuracy curves. For knowledge updating, the paper fine-tunes Qwen2.5-VL-3B/7B with LoRA and DoRA (ranks 16–64) using three data formats (QA, MCQA, QAR), revealing that MCQA format yields the fastest convergence and a 4.2-point MMMU gain, while consistently degrading MMStar visual perception scores regardless of rank or training duration.

## Results

On LIVEVQA, all 17 benchmarked MLLMs struggle significantly with live visual knowledge: the best no-search model (GPT-4.1) achieves only 16.5% average accuracy on Level 1 and 3.0% on Level 2 questions. Incorporating the MM-Search multimodal search tool more than doubles GPT-4.1's performance to 33.4% (Level 1) and 30.4% (Level 2), representing the paper's headline 327% average improvement over no-search baselines. For PEFT-based knowledge updating, MCQA-format fine-tuning with LoRA rank 64 yields a 4.2-point gain on MMMU but degrades MMStar from 65.80% to 58.16%, revealing a fundamental tension between knowledge injection and visual perception retention.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper benchmarks 17 MLLMs across four search settings. Without any search tool, all models perform poorly: GPT-4.1 leads at 16.5% (L1) / 3.0% (L2), while open-source models like Qwen-2.5-VL-72B (7.3% / 2.9%) and Gemma-3n-E4B-IT (8.5% / 1.8%) trail significantly. Text-based web search provides negligible or even negative gains (GPT-4.1 drops from 16.5% to 15.6% on L1; Gemini-2.5-Pro drops from 15.3% to 9.0%), confirming that text retrieval alone cannot resolve visual knowledge gaps. Native image search (GPT-o3, Grok-4) substantially improves L2 accuracy (15.8% and 12.6% respectively vs. ~3% without search), but still lags far behind MM-Search. The MM-Search framework applied to GPT-4.1 achieves the best overall results (33.4% L1, 30.4% L2), outperforming GPT-o3 by 10.1 points on L1 and 14.6 points on L2, demonstrating that agentic multimodal search is the most effective strategy for live visual knowledge seeking. In the PEFT updating experiments, MCQA-format data with LoRA rank 64 outperforms QA and QAR formats on the validation set, while higher rank consistently beats lower rank by an average of 5.4%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of visual world-knowledge VQA (OK-VQA, A-OKVQA, FVQA), temporal/live-knowledge benchmarks (LiveXiv, E-VQA), and parameter-efficient knowledge updating for MLLMs. It builds directly on LoRA and DoRA as the PEFT backbone for the updating experiments, and on MMSearch and WebWatcher as retrieval-augmented baselines for the seeking experiments. What it departs from is the static-knowledge assumption in prior VQA benchmarks: by sourcing all content strictly from April 2024–May 2025 (post-cutoff for every evaluated model), it reframes VQA as a problem of *live* visual knowledge rather than fixed-world knowledge, and couples seeking (retrieval) with updating (fine-tuning) in a single evaluation framework for the first time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LIVEVQA fills a concrete gap: no prior benchmark systematically evaluates MLLMs on visual content that necessarily post-dates their training, making it a natural stress-test for the temporal staleness problem that limits real-world deployment. The finding that multimodal search tools yield a 327% average improvement while text-only search does not help (and can hurt) provides actionable guidance for building live multimodal assistants. The PEFT experiments reveal a non-trivial trade-off—visual-knowledge fine-tuning improves knowledge-intensive reasoning (MMMU +4.2 pts) while degrading visual perception (MMStar −7.6 pts)—an open question for how to update models without eroding foundational capabilities. Limitations include restriction to Qwen2.5-VL for updating experiments, no full fine-tuning comparison, and potential date-category imbalance from crawling constraints.
