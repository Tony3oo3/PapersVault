## Problem

Existing color-conditional generation methods either apply post-hoc color transfer to already-generated images (producing semantically incoherent color placements) or use stylized generation approaches that entangle color with other stylistic features such as brush strokes and composition. No training-free method existed that could condition a diffusion model's sampling process solely on a reference color distribution while preserving semantic alignment with the text prompt and avoiding transfer of unwanted textures or structural features.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255489/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt and a reference image (or even a flat palette of colors) as input, and produces a generated image whose RGB color distribution matches the reference while remaining semantically faithful to the prompt. The reference image is consumed only through its pixel color distribution in 3D RGB space—no structural, textural, or semantic information is extracted from it. Experiments use 512×512 (SD-1.5) and 768×768 (SDXL) resolutions with 30 DDIM inference steps, and the method is fully training-free, requiring no fine-tuning or paired data.

## Method

SW-Guidance modifies the reverse diffusion sampling loop by introducing an auxiliary control vector u that is optimized at each timestep over M inner gradient steps to minimize the Sliced 1-Wasserstein (SW) distance between the color distribution of the predicted noiseless image and the reference palette. The SW distance is computed efficiently by projecting RGB color samples onto K random 1D directions and taking the L1 difference of empirical CDFs, leveraging a proposition that replaces the intractable inverse-CDF formulation with a direct CDF comparison. The CDF is approximated by sorting projected samples, which is differentiable, enabling gradient flow through the VAE decoder and U-Net. After M optimization steps, the shifted latent x_t + u* is passed through the standard DDIM denoising step with classifier-free guidance. Theoretically, the paper proves that convergence of the SW distance on bounded (unit-cube) distributions implies weak convergence and convergence of all moments, justifying the use of the 1-Wasserstein variant for the 3D color space.

## Results

On SDXL with 1000 ContraStyles prompts and Unsplash Lite references, SW-Guidance achieves a 2-Wasserstein distance of 0.0297±0.0005 to the reference color palette, roughly 2× lower than the best color-transfer baseline (hm-mkl-hm at 0.0543) and ~6× lower than the best stylized-generation baseline (InstantStyle at 0.1758). It also attains the highest CLIP-T prompt-adherence score (0.270) among all stylized-generation methods, while maintaining a competitive CLIP-IQA quality score of 0.285 (second only to InstantStyle's 0.332). On SD-1.5, the same pattern holds with a 2-Wasserstein distance of 0.0328±0.0003.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SW-Guidance outperforms all eight post-hoc color-transfer baselines (hm-mkl-hm, hm, PhotoWCT2, ModFlows, MKL, CT, WCT2, PhotoNAS) by a wide margin on 2-Wasserstein distance, with the closest competitor hm-mkl-hm at 0.0543 versus SW-Guidance's 0.0297 (an 82% relative reduction). Against stylized-generation methods, SW-Guidance achieves substantially better color matching than InstantStyle (0.0297 vs. 0.1758), IP-Adapter (0.0297 vs. 0.2193), and RB-Modulation (0.0297 vs. 0.3795), while also preserving higher CLIP-T prompt adherence than InstantStyle (0.270 vs. 0.238) and IP-Adapter (0.270 vs. 0.214). The one metric where SW-Guidance trails is CLIP-IQA image quality: InstantStyle scores 0.332 and RB-Modulation 0.323, both above SW-Guidance's 0.285, suggesting that the attention-based stylization methods produce slightly more visually appealing images at the cost of transferring unwanted textures and compositional features. The Colorcanny ControlNet baseline (SD-1.5 only) also loses to SW-Guidance on color distance (0.1183 vs. 0.0328). Overall, SW-Guidance is the only method that simultaneously achieves the best color fidelity, the best prompt adherence among stylized methods, and competitive image quality without transferring non-color stylistic features from the reference.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Universal Diffusion Guidance (UDG) framework, which generalized Diffusion Posterior Sampling by replacing the MSE loss with an arbitrary differentiable distance, by instantiating that distance with the Sliced 1-Wasserstein metric between color distributions. The inner-loop optimization of an auxiliary control vector is adapted from RB-Modulation, while the theoretical grounding draws on the Sliced Wasserstein literature (Rabin et al. 2012) and its convergence properties for bounded distributions. Unlike prior stylized generation methods (IP-Adapter, InstantStyle) that modify attention and entangle color with texture and composition, SW-Guidance targets the color distribution in RGB space directly, decoupling palette control from other stylistic features.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SW-Guidance provides a training-free, plug-and-play mechanism for color-conditional generation that substantially outperforms both post-hoc color transfer algorithms and attention-based stylization methods in palette fidelity while preserving prompt adherence. The practical impact is significant for creative workflows where precise color matching to a reference is needed without transferring unwanted textures or compositional elements. Key open questions include whether the requirement to differentiate through the U-net can be eliminated, how to resolve conflicts when combining SW-Guidance with other strong stylization methods, and whether the approach generalizes beyond 3D RGB to higher-dimensional color spaces or other conditioning signals.
