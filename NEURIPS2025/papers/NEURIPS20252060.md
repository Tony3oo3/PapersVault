## Problem

Existing one-shot federated learning methods for MLLMs (e.g., FuseFL, FENS) still require more than one communication round because they rely on aggregated global supervision to correct heterogeneity-induced local training bias, meaning they do not achieve true one-shot communication. This paper investigates whether implicit global supervision—i.e., the shared pre-trained weights available at initialization—can alone correct local LoRA training bias, enabling genuine single-round federated adaptation of MLLMs.

## Input / Output

Each client receives image-text pairs from its private dataset and trains only the last layer's q_proj of MiniCPM-V-2_6-int4 via LoRA (rank 8, lora_alpha 8, dropout 0.05). In the single communication round, each client uploads only its LoRA A and B matrices (not the full model), and the server aggregates them into a global LoRA that is merged with the frozen pre-trained weights. Evaluation produces task-specific scores: AUC for Hateful-Memes, F1 for CrisisMMD, and GPT-4-assessed accuracy for medical VQA datasets (VQA-RAD, SLAKE, MedAlpaca) under aligned, missing, cross, and hybrid modal scenarios.

## Method

YOCO achieves true one-shot communication through three mechanisms built on the insight that directional (sign) constraints are less disruptive to optimization than magnitude constraints. First, LoRA A and B are initialized via SVD decomposition of the pre-trained weights, giving all clients a shared prior direction. Second, during local training, a sign-based consistency regularizer (using tanh as a smooth sign approximation) aligns LoRA B's parameter signs with the prior direction to reduce cross-client conflicts, while an L1 sparsity regularizer on LoRA A encourages learning of critical client-specific features and suppresses noise. Third, at aggregation, LoRA B uses vanilla averaging (since it captures shared knowledge and exhibits low conflict), while LoRA A uses principal-component-weighted aggregation that projects client LoRA A matrices into PCA space, averages the coordinates weighted by dataset size, and maps back—preserving client-specific diversity. This design balances global consistency (via LoRA B direction alignment) with local adaptability (via LoRA A sparsity and PCA aggregation), reducing communication to ~0.03% of multi-round FL while outperforming all one-shot baselines.

## Results

On CrisisMMD (F1), YOCO with 1×1×1 communication (≈0.03% of multi-round cost) achieves 63.69/63.66/61.28 at α=5.0/1.0/0.5, surpassing the best multi-round method FedAdam (61.80/59.12/60.56) and all one-shot baselines (Mean: 62.47/60.50/60.05). On Hateful-Memes (AUC), YOCO reaches 70.14/70.76/70.65 at α=5.0/1.0/0.5, outperforming all one-shot baselines (best: Combine at 69.06/69.98/68.79) though trailing multi-round FedYogi (75.74/72.50/74.32). On medical datasets (VQA-RAD, SLAKE), YOCO consistently leads all one-shot baselines in Overall accuracy, e.g., 62.53 vs. 60.98 (FedAvgM) on VQA-RAD at I:7-T:3. Across N=10/20/30 clients, YOCO maintains top one-shot performance with up to 6.14% improvement over Local and 4.96% over other one-shot baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against multi-round baselines (FedYogi, FedAdam, FedAvgM, FedAdagrad at 25×28×4 or 40×28×4 communication), YOCO with 1×1×1 uses only ~0.03% of the communication cost. On CrisisMMD, YOCO outperforms all multi-round methods in 5 out of 6 scenarios (e.g., 63.69 vs. 61.80 F1 at α=5.0 vs. FedAdam), while on Hateful-Memes multi-round methods retain a 4–6 AUC point advantage. Among one-shot baselines (Mean, Ensemble, Combine, FedAvg, FedAdam, FedAvgM, FedAdagrad all at 1×1×1), YOCO consistently achieves the best performance across all datasets and scenarios. On Hateful-Memes, YOCO leads the best one-shot baseline (Combine) by 1.08 AUC at α=5.0 and 1.78 at α=1.0. On CrisisMMD, the margin is larger: YOCO beats Combine by 1.31 F1 at α=5.0 and 0.44 at β=50%. On medical datasets (VQA-RAD, SLAKE), YOCO surpasses the best one-shot baseline (FedAvgM) by 1.5–2.2 points in Overall accuracy across all modal scenarios.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the one-shot federated learning (OFL) line of work by replacing the aggregated 'explicit' global supervision used by adaptive ensemble methods (FuseFL, FENS) with 'implicit' global supervision derived solely from pre-trained weights, thereby achieving true single-round communication for MLLMs. The method builds directly on LoRA's low-rank decomposition (Hu et al., 2022), adopts SVD-based initialization from LoRA-Pro (Wang et al., 2025) to provide a shared directional prior, and repurposes the sign-conflict metric from TIES-Merging (Yadav et al., 2023) as a differentiable regularization objective. A key conceptual departure is the asymmetric treatment of LoRA A and B: B is sign-regularized toward the pre-trained direction to enforce global consistency, while A is sparsity-regularized to preserve client-specific adaptability, followed by PCA-weighted aggregation for A and vanilla averaging for B.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

YOCO reduces federated MLLM fine-tuning communication to ~0.03% of multi-round FL while matching or exceeding those methods on several benchmarks, which is practically significant for privacy-sensitive, bandwidth-constrained deployments (healthcare, edge devices). The finding that directional (sign) constraints correct local bias more effectively than magnitude constraints, and that constraining only LoRA B (not both A and B) is optimal, offers a reusable design principle for federated PEFT beyond this specific architecture. Open questions remain: whether implicit supervision can ever match explicit aggregated supervision in multi-round settings, whether the approach generalizes to other PEFT methods (adapters, prefix tuning), and whether last-layer-only LoRA is truly suboptimal or simply a practical choice. The method is currently limited to LoRA and a single MLLM family (MiniCPM-V), limiting immediate broad adoption.
