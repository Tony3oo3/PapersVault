## Problem

Decentralized collaborative training of large models requires participants to pool compute, but if any participant can extract the full weight set, the model's economic excludability is destroyed and contributors cannot recoup training costs. No existing decentralized training framework prevents weight materialization while preserving exact model functionality, leaving a gap between enabling open, multi-party training and protecting the trained asset from piecewise Sybil extraction attacks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250629/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on a sharded neural network split into S pipeline stages, where each participant holds only one stage's weights and processes text token sequences through their assigned layers. The unusual aspect is that stage weights are time-varying: at each transform time step (e.g., every 30 seconds), random invertible matrices are folded into boundary weights, so the same stage's parameters at time t are incompatible with those at time t'. Output during inference is standard generated text tokens with unchanged perplexity; during training, participants receive updated time-stamped shards that are only coherent within the current transform step.

## Method

UPMs decompose the model into valid subfunctions with entry and exit weight matrices, then at each pipeline boundary periodically insert a random invertible transform T by decomposing the identity as T·T⁻¹ and folding T into the upstream stage's exit matrix while folding T⁻¹ into the downstream stage's entry matrix. This preserves the end-to-end network function exactly (transforms cancel within a time step) while rendering cross-time weight assemblies incoherent, since stitching shards from different steps requires an unknown bridge matrix. Transforms are Haar orthogonal or low-condition-number matrices, are ephemeral (discarded after folding), and skip connections in transformer layers naturally achieve full incompatibility where weights depend on both boundary transforms. Training uses the Muon optimizer, which is provably equivalent under orthogonal transforms, and inference employs a high-precision disk accumulation workaround to prevent floating-point drift over 10k+ morphing steps.

## Results

On Qwen2.5-0.5B and Llama-3.2-1B, 10,000 morphing steps leave FP32 perplexity essentially unchanged (ΔPPL < 0.01, Jensen-Shannon logit drift < 4×10⁻⁵) when using the high-precision accumulation workaround. Inference overhead is 3% latency, 0.1% bandwidth, and 10% GPU memory; training overhead is 1.6% time and <1% memory. For training, orthogonal transforms produce validation losses identical to the no-transform baseline (e.g., 3.27 vs. 3.28 on GPT-2), while learning-based attacks on stitched weights require ≥60% of the compute needed to train from scratch to reach perplexity 20.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against external competing methods; instead it benchmarks UPM variants against a no-transform training baseline and evaluates attack resistance. Orthogonal (Haar) transforms yield validation losses statistically indistinguishable from the baseline across all three GPT-2 scales (e.g., 3.27 vs. 3.28 on GPT-2, 3.17 vs. 3.16 on GPT-2 XL), confirming the theoretical equivalence in Section 3.3. Low-condition-number transforms with ε=0.01 also track the baseline, while ε=0.1 introduces measurable drift (3.66 vs. 3.28 on GPT-2) and ε=0.5 causes training instability. For the learning-based attack, fine-tuning a stitched Llama-3.2-1B model (with orthogonal transforms at layer boundaries) to perplexity 20 on FineWeb requires at least 60% of the tokens needed to train the same model from scratch, and the stitched model starts at perplexity >60,000 (comparable to random initialization). The high-precision inference workaround keeps logit drift and perplexity flat over 10k morphs in FP32, whereas without it, FP16/BF16 models become unusable after a few thousand steps.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the authors' own Protocol Models / Node0 framework for communication-efficient decentralized pipeline-parallel training by adding an unextractability layer: time-varying random invertible transforms folded into stage-boundary weights. Conceptually, it departs from federated learning (which protects data but not weights) and from black-box model-extraction defenses (which assume API-level access rather than direct shard possession), positioning the threat as a piecewise Sybil attack in a trustless, model-parallel protocol. The structural reliance on pipeline parallelism (GPipe) and the algebraic identity-splitting trick are the key building blocks; no prior work addresses the specific problem of making collaboratively-held model shards mutually incompatible across time while preserving exact end-to-end function.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

UPMs provide a lightweight, non-cryptographic mechanism to make decentralized model training economically viable by guaranteeing excludability of model value, which is a prerequisite for incentive-compatible compute pooling. The finding that learning-based stitching attacks require ≥60% of from-scratch training compute is a strong security guarantee, but it is validated only on 0.5B–1B models in single-machine simulation, leaving open whether the excludability ratio holds at 70B+ scale, under real network latency, or against side-channel (timing, cache) attacks. The honest-majority assumption and the two-step joining delay are practical constraints that may be hard to enforce in truly open protocols, and the inability to inspect or disable weights raises governance and safety-evaluation concerns the authors acknowledge but do not resolve.
