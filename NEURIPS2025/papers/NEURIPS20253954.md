## Problem

Longitudinal neuroimaging data for neurodegenerative disease progression suffers from limited sample sizes, irregular temporal sampling, and substantial inter-subject variability, making continuous shape trajectory modeling difficult. Existing approaches either rely on discrete disease labels that cannot capture the gradual cognitive continuum, require multiple prior observations for prediction, or operate in high-dimensional voxel space where fine-grained subregion changes (e.g., hippocampal atrophy occupying only 1.7% of brain volume) are lost. No prior method combines continuous-time ODE dynamics with precise 3D brain shape modeling to predict disease progression from a single scan.

## Input / Output

Brain shapes are reconstructed as triangular meshes from 3D MRI scans (via SynthSeg segmentation and iterative Point2Mesh deformation), then projected onto a 150-component PCA basis to form the shape input Λ ∈ ℝ¹⁵⁰. The model also consumes a normalized age scalar t ∈ [0,1] (linearly mapped from ages 65–95) and a continuous cognitive status scalar c ∈ [0,1]. Output is a future 3D mesh at an arbitrary target time, produced by integrating the learned velocity field with RK4, plus a predicted cognitive status from a separate 3D CNN estimator. The 1-shot setting requires only a single observed shape, and the ODE solver natively handles irregular time gaps without retraining.

## Method

BrainODE parameterizes the shape deformation velocity as f_θ(Λ_t, c_t, t) = dΛ_t/dt using a self-attention network (three linear projections forming Q/K/V over the concatenated shape-age-status vector, followed by FC layers with GeLU), solved via RK4 integration between arbitrary time points. Training exploits all combinatorial forward and backward trajectory pairs from each subject's longitudinal sequence to maximize data utilization, supervised by L2 loss on PCA coefficients. The key architectural choice is PCA-based shape parameterization (150 modes, >98% variance retained) rather than raw vertices or autoencoder latents, preserving anatomical fidelity at ~375 MB training memory. A pseudo-cognitive status embedding generates intermediate shapes by interpolating between the forward trajectory (c=0) and backward trajectory (c=1) of converted subjects, producing continuous (shape, status) pairs to train a 3D CNN cognition estimator τ_θ; at inference, τ_θ provides smooth cognitive status conditions that drive progressive multi-step shape prediction with predicted cognitive decline.

## Results

BrainODE achieves the lowest Euclidean distance (mm) across all settings. On LBC1936 (regular, 3-yr intervals), BrainODE reaches 0.488 mm (hippocampus) and 1.630 mm (lateral ventricle) in 4-shot prediction, and 0.365 mm / 1.743 mm in 1-shot. On the irregular-interval dataset (ADNI+OASIS+AIBL), BrainODE achieves 0.543 mm / 1.959 mm (4-shot) and 0.492 mm / 1.673 mm (1-shot). The cognition estimator τ_θ reaches 89.1% diagnostic accuracy for NC vs. AD classification.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BrainODE outperforms all three primary baselines on every metric and setting. Against ShapeFlow [14], BrainODE reduces hippocampal error from 0.652 to 0.488 mm (4-shot, LBC1936) and lateral ventricle error from 7.112 to 1.630 mm — a 4.3× improvement on LV, where ShapeFlow's uniform-interval assumption breaks down. Against LatentODE [27], BrainODE achieves substantially lower errors (e.g., 0.488 vs. 0.880 mm on LBC1936 4-shot hippocampus), with the gap widening on LV (1.630 vs. 5.759 mm), reflecting LatentODE's autoencoder-induced information loss. Against BrLP [24], BrainODE wins on hippocampus in all settings (0.488 vs. 1.078 mm on LBC1936 4-shot) and on LV in 4-shot (1.630 vs. 2.230 mm); BrLP's voxel-based generation struggles with the small hippocampal region (1.7% of brain volume) and cannot guarantee subject identity preservation. On irregular-interval data (Table 2), BrainODE maintains its lead over BrLP (0.543 vs. 1.017 mm, 4-shot hippocampus) and RNN-Decay (0.543 vs. 0.984 mm), confirming robustness to variable acquisition gaps.

## Relation to Prior Work

BrainODE builds on the Neural ODE framework (Chen et al., 2018) for continuous-time dynamics but departs from prior medical imaging applications that operate in voxel or latent autoencoder spaces. It extends flow-based shape deformation methods (ShapeFlow, PointFlow) by handling irregular time intervals and incorporating continuous medical priors (age, cognitive status) rather than assuming uniform intervals or discrete labels. The pseudo-cognitive status embedding is a novel contribution that bridges the gap between discrete diagnostic categories and the continuous nature of neurodegenerative progression, drawing on the condition-injectivity property of the learned ODE to synthesize intermediate training data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to model longitudinal 3D brain subregion shape trajectories via neural ODEs in a compact PCA parameter space, enabling single-observation prediction of disease progression with low computational cost (~375 MB). Key limitations include the exclusion of MCI (only NC and AD are modeled), restriction to hippocampus and lateral ventricles, and the use of simple averaging for multi-observation aggregation. Open questions include whether the continuous cognitive status formulation generalizes to intermediate stages like MCI, whether the approach extends to additional brain regions, and whether more sophisticated aggregation (attention-based, time-aware) can improve multi-shot prediction.
