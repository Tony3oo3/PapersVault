## Problem

Long-CoT reasoning models achieve strong performance on complex problems but incur substantial inference overhead, and empirical analysis shows that long reasoning provides no benefit—or even degrades accuracy—on simpler problems. Existing efficiency methods (pruning, compression, token skipping) operate only within the Long-CoT distribution, never questioning whether a fundamentally shorter reasoning strategy would suffice. The paper addresses the gap of enabling a single model to adaptively select between distinct reasoning paradigms (long vs. short CoT) based on input complexity while maintaining accuracy.

## Input / Output

The model takes a math problem as plain text input and produces a single reasoning trace whose length and style are determined adaptively at inference time—either an elaborate multi-step Long-CoT derivation or a concise Short-CoT solution—followed by a boxed final answer. The output length varies per problem: on MATH the average reasoning length is reduced by 58% relative to the Long-CoT baseline, while on GSM8K it drops by 74% with improved accuracy. The model is a single merged LLM (7B or 1.5B parameters) that internally commits to a reasoning mode immediately after reading the problem, without external signals or intermediate computation.

## Method

Ada-R1 is a two-stage framework. Stage I linearly merges a Long-CoT model (DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen) and a Short-CoT model (fine-tuned from the same base with short-CoT examples) via parameter interpolation θ_H = αθ_L + (1−α)θ_S (α=0.8), producing a hybrid model capable of generating both reasoning styles. Stage II applies Bi-Level Preference Training with DPO: at the group level, K responses are sampled from each original model per problem, and the model is trained to prefer the reasoning style (long or short) whose sampled accuracy exceeds the other by a margin ε, teaching it to select the appropriate mode; at the instance level, within the preferred group, the shortest correct response is preferred over the longest responses, encouraging conciseness. Both preference signals are combined into a single DPO objective, and the merged model serves as both policy and reference, yielding a 52% average length reduction with only 0.51% accuracy loss on the 7B model.

## Results

On the 7B model, Ada-R1 achieves an average accuracy degradation of only 1.65% relative to the Long-CoT baseline while reducing average reasoning length by 50.93% across five mathematical benchmarks (AIME25, MATH500, GSM8K, Olympiad, Minerva). On the 1.5B model, the degradation is 1.21% with a 43.28% length reduction. Notably, on GSM8K Ada-R1 actually improves accuracy from 88.9% to 90.3% (7B) while cutting length from 1014 to 260 tokens, and on MATH500 it maintains 90.2% accuracy while halving the reasoning length from 3534 to 1468 tokens.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Ada-R1 outperforms DPO on both accuracy preservation (−1.65% vs. −3.56% avg. degradation) and length reduction (−50.93% vs. −33.26%) on the 7B model, making it strictly superior on both axes. Against O1-Pruner, Ada-R1 achieves a substantially larger length reduction (−50.93% vs. −34.53%) at the cost of a small accuracy drop (−1.65% vs. +2.48%), trading a marginal accuracy gain for a 16-percentage-point improvement in efficiency. CoT-Valve, while achieving the largest length reduction (−73.06%), suffers a severe accuracy collapse (−18.41%), confirming the paper's argument that broad-scope methods without adaptive selection degrade performance. On the 1.5B model, the same pattern holds: Ada-R1 (−1.21% acc., −43.28% len.) dominates DPO (−5.93%, −34.57%) and CoT-Valve (−19.61%, −67.52%), and again trades a small accuracy cost for greater length savings compared to O1-Pruner (+2.18%, −33.75%). On non-mathematical benchmarks (LogiQA, GPQA, MMLU), Ada-R1 further improves accuracy over the Long-CoT baseline by 23.63% on average while reducing length by 25.37%, outperforming DPO which gains only 7.26% accuracy with a 15.95% length reduction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant line of efficient-reasoning work (O1-Pruner, Overthinking, kimi-1.5) that operates strictly within the Long-CoT distribution to prune or compress reasoning paths, arguing instead that the Long-CoT paradigm itself is unnecessary for many problems. It also differentiates from broader-scope methods like CoT-Valve and naive model merging, which can produce shorter outputs but lack input-adaptive selection of reasoning depth. The method repurposes linear parameter interpolation (from the model-merging literature: DARE, TIES-Merging, AdaMerging) not for multi-task consolidation but to create a single model spanning both Long-CoT and Short-CoT distributions, then applies a novel two-level DPO objective (group-level style selection + instance-level conciseness) to train adaptive behavior.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The empirical finding that roughly half of math problems show no accuracy gain (or even degradation) from Long-CoT provides strong motivation for adaptive strategies, and the demonstrated 50%+ inference-length reduction with <2% accuracy loss is practically significant for deployment in latency-sensitive or resource-constrained settings. However, the evaluation is concentrated on mathematical reasoning (with limited non-math generalization checks), and the method relies on an 'early mode selection' assumption where the model must commit to a reasoning style before any intermediate computation. Open questions include whether the adaptive mechanism generalizes reliably to open-ended, multi-domain, or real-world tasks where problem complexity is harder to estimate a priori, and whether the simple linear merge remains effective as the gap between Long-CoT and Short-CoT models widens.
