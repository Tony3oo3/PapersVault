## Problem

Existing neural methods for the Time-Dependent TSP either decompose the time-dependent adjacency tensor by node or by time, failing to capture spatial and temporal structures simultaneously. Additionally, the paper identifies a critical evaluation flaw: on practical datasets, most instances retain the same optimal tour regardless of time-dependent weights, so average tour duration across all instances cannot distinguish methods that genuinely exploit temporal dynamics from strong static ATSP solvers.

## Input / Output

The model takes a time-dependent adjacency tensor A[t] of shape n×n×T, where each entry c_ij(t) is the travel time from node i to node j when departing at time t, approximated by linear interpolation over discrete samples. The output is a node permutation (tour) starting from a designated depot, with total traveling time computed by sequentially accumulating time-dependent edge costs. Because TDTSP violates cyclic invariance, the model cannot use POMO-style multi-start training and instead relies on REINFORCE with a rollout baseline.

## Method

The encoder extends MatNet's dual graph attention to the 3D tensor by treating A[t] as a bipartite vector-weighted graph where each edge carries a full temporal cost vector. The update function F+ operates in three stages: computing node-pair attention scores, concatenating those scores with A[t] along the time axis and compressing via an MLP, then performing softmax-weighted aggregation to update embeddings. The decoder constructs the tour auto-regressively, injecting the current arrival time via sinusoidal embeddings added to both query and key vectors in the attention mechanism, which exploits the fixed-norm property of sinusoidal codes to separate node-node, node-time, and constant interaction terms. At inference, a Mixture-of-Experts step selects the better of the neural tour and a MatNet ATSP tour, followed by two iterations of local search (2-opt, 3-opt, or-opt, exchange, relocate) to refine the solution.

## Results

On 10-node TDTSP instances across four cities, the improved method (Ours Imp) achieves optimality gaps of 0.11% (Lyon), 0.04% (Nairobi), and 0.04% (London), with running times of 23–40 seconds. The raw neural model alone achieves gaps of 0.28–0.87% in under 1 second, demonstrating the potential of pure neural inference. On 20-node instances, Ours (Imp) reaches gaps of 0.08–0.40%, outperforming all heuristic baselines on London-20 (0.08% vs. SA's 0.19%) and matching or exceeding SA on the other cities. The method also scales to 50-node Beijing instances with an 8.09% gap, where no other baseline except SA (8.17%) is feasible.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MatNet (ATSP) [Kwon et al., NeurIPS 2021], the proposed method achieves substantially lower optimality gaps across all tested instances: 0.11% vs. 0.78% on Lyon-10, 0.04% vs. 1.41% on Nairobi-10, 0.04% vs. 0.47% on London-10, and 0.08–0.40% vs. 1.11–2.06% on 20-node instances, confirming that directly encoding the time-dependent tensor captures temporal structure that a static ATSP encoder misses. Against Simulated Annealing (SA, following Zhang et al. [IEEE TNNLS 2021]), the improved method is within 0.03–0.07% on 10-node instances (slightly worse on Lyon and Nairobi, slightly better on London) but runs 8–100× faster (23–40 s vs. 345–5008 s). On 20-node London, Ours (Imp) actually beats SA (0.08% vs. 0.19%), and the raw neural model alone matches SA's quality on Nairobi-10 (0.28% vs. 0.02% gap) in under 1 second. The paper also notes that on instances where the TDTSP optimal tour coincides with the static ATSP optimum, MatNet slightly outperforms the raw neural model, motivating the Mixture-of-Experts inference step that selects the better of the two solutions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends MatNet (Kwon et al., NeurIPS 2021), which encodes static adjacency matrices for ATSP via dual graph attention, to the time-dependent setting by treating the adjacency tensor A[t] as a bipartite vector-weighted graph and compressing temporal patterns through an MLP-augmented attention mechanism. It departs from prior neural TDTSP methods (Guo et al., Zhang et al., Chen et al.) that either decompose the tensor by node or by time, failing to capture spatial and temporal structure simultaneously. The decoder follows the Attention Model (Kool et al., NeurIPS 2018) architecture but injects sinusoidal time embeddings into the attention computation, and the training uses REINFORCE rather than POMO due to the cyclic invariance violation inherent in TDTSP.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's most impactful contribution is an empirical finding that challenges standard TDTSP evaluation: across 12 real-world cities, roughly half of instances yield the same optimal tour regardless of time-dependent weights, and ~20% of instances account for >80% of total time savings, making average tour duration an insufficient metric for distinguishing genuinely time-aware solvers from strong static ATSP solvers. This motivates the proposed filtered-instance evaluation protocol. A key open question is how to train neural TDTSP solvers that preserve spatial structure learning without relying on cyclic invariance (which TDTSP violates), since REINFORCE currently compromises this; the MoE inference workaround is effective but not a fundamental fix. The method's scalability to 100 nodes with ~65× speedup over ACO is practically relevant for logistics applications.
