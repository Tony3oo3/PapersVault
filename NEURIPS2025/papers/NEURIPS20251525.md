## Problem

Classifier-free guidance (CFG) is the standard technique for improving image quality and prompt consistency in diffusion and flow models, but it inherently trades off diversity and can cause over-saturation at high guidance scales. Existing remedies such as AutoGuidance require a second (weaker) model, increasing memory and compute, while methods like SEG need extra forward passes. No prior approach simultaneously improves quality, diversity, and consistency at the same inference cost as CFG, and most are inapplicable to unconditional sampling.

## Input / Output

ERG operates at inference time on pre-trained diffusion transformer or flow-matching models (DiT XL/2 at 256/512 for class-conditional; MMDiT-style 1.9B model at 512 for text-to-image), taking a text prompt encoded by Llama3-8B and Flan-T5-XL, a class label, or no conditioning at all. The method modifies only the attention layers of the text encoder and denoiser during sampling—no retraining or weight changes are required—and produces a single generated image per forward pass pair, with the same two NFEs per step as standard CFG.

## Method

The core mechanism reinterprets the attention operation through the Hopfield energy lens (Ramsauer et al., 2021), where the softmax attention corresponds to a CCCP update minimizing an energy function E(ξ;X) = ½ξᵀξ − LogSumExp(Xᵀξ, β). ERG introduces two test-time hyperparameters—temperature τ scaling the softmax sharpness and weight α controlling the relative importance of the pattern-matching term—plus optional multi-step gradient descent (K steps, step size γ) to produce a deliberately weaker denoising estimate from the same model. The guidance update (Eq. 2) contrasts the standard conditional prediction D(x, φ_c, t) with this entropy-rectified prediction D^ξ(x, φ_c^τ, t; Θ_ξ), where the text encoder's self-attention is also temperature-scaled (C-ERG) and the denoiser's selected attention layers are modified (I-ERG). Because the rectified signal is derived from the conditional model itself rather than a separate unconditional branch, ERG extends naturally to unconditional sampling and requires no additional training or model checkpoints.

## Results

On COCO 512×512 text-to-image generation (1.9B MMDiT model, 50 Euler steps), ERG achieves a Density of 120.25 (+22 over CFG's 98.24), the highest VQAScore of 73.96 (+3.8 over CFG), and tied-best CLIPScore of 26.86, while requiring only 2 NFE per step (same as CFG). When combined with APG, ERG+APG achieves the best FID (11.37) and best Coverage (80.50) among all methods tested. On unconditional generation (Table 2), ERG achieves FID 36.25, Density 55.84, and Coverage 51.59, outperforming all other unconditional-compatible methods by a wide margin (e.g., +15.3 Coverage over SEG*).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ERG outperforms CFG on Density (+22.0), VQAScore (+3.8), CLIPScore (+0.4), and Coverage (+2.1), at the cost of a slightly higher FID (13.62 vs. 12.81). Against the strongest single-method baselines (APG, CADS, SAG*, PAG*), ERG consistently wins on Density by 13–19 points and on VQAScore by 0.6–1.8 points, but trails on FID by roughly 1–2 points; combining ERG with APG or CADS recovers and surpasses the best FID while retaining the Density and Coverage gains. ERG decisively outperforms SEG* and AutoGuidance on every metric (e.g., FID 13.62 vs. 16.87/16.62, Coverage 73.21 vs. 61.91/62.75), and additionally requires only 2 NFE versus SEG*'s 3. On unconditional generation, ERG achieves FID 36.25 and Coverage 51.59, beating SEG* (FID 37.75, Coverage 34.79) and AutoGuidance (FID 39.50, Coverage 34.71) by a substantial margin. On class-conditional ImageNet at 512 resolution, ERG achieves the best FID (4.56), Density (163.63), and near-best Coverage (86.13 vs. CFG's 86.70), confirming consistent gains across tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

ERG extends the contrastive guidance paradigm of CFG by replacing the unconditional branch with a 'weaker' conditional signal obtained through energy-landscape manipulation of attention layers, building directly on the Hopfield energy interpretation of attention (Ramsauer et al., 2021) and the temperature-scaling idea from SEG (Hong, 2024). Unlike AutoGuidance (Karras et al., 2024), which requires a second (weaker) model or earlier checkpoint, ERG derives both the strong and weak signals from a single model by rectifying attention entropy at inference time. The method also generalizes beyond the U-Net-specific formulations of SAG, PAG, and SEG to diffusion transformer architectures, and uniquely applies to unconditional sampling where CFG is inapplicable.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By simultaneously improving quality, diversity, and consistency—metrics previously shown to be in fundamental tension (Astolfi et al., 2024)—ERG offers a drop-in replacement for CFG that requires no retraining, no second model, and the same NFE (2 per step). Its compatibility with APG and CADS suggests it can serve as a composable component in future guidance pipelines. Open questions include whether the energy-rectification principle transfers to video or audio diffusion models, whether the multi-step gradient descent formulation (K > 1) can be made more effective, and how sensitive the gains are to architecture choices beyond DiT/MMDiT.
