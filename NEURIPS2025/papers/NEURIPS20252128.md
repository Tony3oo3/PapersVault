## Problem

Existing multimodal math benchmarks overwhelmingly focus on plane geometry, with solid geometry severely underrepresented—MathVista contains only 62 solid geometry questions (all simple object counting), and dedicated geometry benchmarks like GeoQA and Geometry3K are 100% plane geometry. Solid geometry, which demands 3D spatial reasoning over projections, unfoldings, and spatial vectors, is a core competency for spatial intelligence and AGI yet remains largely unassessed in the MLLM evaluation landscape.

## Input / Output

Each problem pairs a text question (average 77.2 words, substantially longer than MathVista's 15.6) with at least one geometry image (74.5% single image, up to 8 per question), presented bilingually in English (70.4%) and Chinese (29.6%). Questions span three formats—multiple-choice (969), open-ended single-step (1,936), and open-ended multi-step (208)—and are annotated with difficulty levels 1–3 and one of eight fine-grained solid geometry categories. Models produce a final answer (choice letter or free-form solution) scored for exact-match accuracy via a DeepSeek API judge.

## Method

SOLIDGEO is constructed via a hybrid sourcing strategy: 1,737 problems extracted from six existing benchmarks (OlympiadBench, MathVerse, MATH-Vision, GeoEval, DynaMath, CMMaTH) and 1,376 newly collected from the Zujuan K-12 platform using Mathpix OCR, yielding 3,113 unique samples after a four-stage filtering pipeline (structural completeness, OpenCV Laplacian-variance image quality threshold, DeepSeek-based semantic topic classification, and n-gram cosine-similarity cross-set deduplication). Domain experts define an 8-category taxonomy (e.g., Multi-view Projection, Planar Unfolding, 3D Coordinate and Vector Reasoning), and difficulty labels are assigned by majority voting among GPT-4o, Claude-3.7-Sonnet, and Qwen-VL-Max with human adjudication of disagreements. The evaluation protocol benchmarks 27 MLLMs (19 System-1 and 8 System-2) under zero-shot, CoT, and CoT+2-shot prompting, with a human baseline from high school students, and includes a fine-grained error taxonomy (visual perception, reasoning, hallucination, format, and other) applied to 300 sampled failures per top model.

## Results

On SOLIDGEO, OpenAI-o1 achieves the highest overall accuracy of 49.5%, followed by Gemini-2.5-pro (42.7%) and Claude-3.7-Sonnet (34.1%). Among open-source models, Llama-4-Maverick (29.6%) leads System-1 models while QvQ-72B (26.6%) leads System-2 models. All models fall substantially short of the human baseline of 77.5%, with a gap of over 28 percentage points even for the best model. Text-only baselines (DeepSeek-V3: 9.3%, GPT-4o text-only: 9.1%) confirm that visual input is essential for solid geometry reasoning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OpenAI-o1 (49.5%) outperforms all 22 other evaluated models, with the largest margin over Gemini-2.5-pro (42.7%) and Claude-3.7-Sonnet (34.1%). Among open-source System-1 models, Llama-4-Maverick (29.6%) surpasses GPT-4o (25.5%) and InternVL3-78B (26.2%), while among System-2 models, QvQ-72B (26.6%) leads over Skywork-R1V2-38B (23.0%) and Vision-R1-7B (18.1%). Notably, Gemini-2.5-pro exceeds human accuracy on 3DCV (72.9% vs. 69.2%) and SMR (75.7% vs. 70.9%), yet underperforms humans on SSI (24.8% vs. 90.2%), revealing an inverted difficulty profile. Text-only baselines (DeepSeek-V3: 9.3%, GPT-4o text-only: 9.1%) confirm that removing images causes a >16-point drop, validating the necessity of visual input. CoT and 2-shot prompting yield inconsistent gains: Qwen2.5-VL-72B improves by +4.6% with CoT, while GPT-4o degrades by −0.6%, and no model benefits consistently from 2-shot examples. All models remain 28+ points below the human baseline (77.5%), with the gap widening to over 70 points on the hardest subcategory (PUC: best model 36.1% vs. human 77.2%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by introducing the first benchmark dedicated exclusively to solid geometry mathematical reasoning, rather than extending a specific method. It departs from the dominant plane-geometry focus of prior benchmarks (GeoQA, Geometry3K, UniGeo, PGPS9K) and from multimodal math suites that include only token amounts of 3D content (MathVista's 62 counting questions, MathVision's 244, MathVerse's 119). The evaluation protocol (zero-shot, CoT, few-shot) follows standard MLLM benchmarking practice, but the fine-grained 8-category taxonomy and 3-level difficulty annotation are novel contributions that enable structured spatial-reasoning diagnostics not available in any prior dataset.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SOLIDGEO exposes a substantial and persistent gap between the best MLLM (OpenAI-o1 at 49.5%) and human performance (77.5%), confirming that 3D spatial reasoning remains a critical bottleneck for current multimodal systems. The finding that CoT and few-shot prompting do not consistently improve performance—and that System-2 models often consume more tokens on incorrect answers—challenges the assumption that longer reasoning chains reliably help on spatial tasks. Open questions include whether a formal symbolic language for solid geometry could enable programmatic verification, whether the benchmark's English/Chinese-only scope limits generalizability, and how to close the spatial-intelligence gap that the authors identify as essential for AGI.
