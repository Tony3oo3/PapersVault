## Problem

Existing instruction-following benchmarks (IFEval, FollowBench, SysBench, etc.) rely on short, synthetically generated instructions that lack the complexity of real-world agentic scenarios, where system prompts average over 1,700 words and contain tool specifications, conditional triggers, and meta-constraints. Whether LLMs can reliably follow such long, multi-constraint agentic instructions remains systematically unexplored, creating a critical gap for building dependable LLM-based agents.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251892/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

Each evaluation instance pairs an agentic instruction (a real-world system prompt of 1,723 words on average, up to 15,630, combined with a human-annotated user query) with a model's free-text response. The response is decomposed against ~11.9 annotated constraints per instruction, each verified via code, LLM judgment, or a hybrid pipeline (LLM extracts relevant segments, code checks compliance). Final scores are CSR (fraction of individual constraints satisfied) and ISR (fraction of instructions where every constraint is met), with conditional constraints excluded from scoring when their trigger condition is not met.

## Method

AGENTIF is constructed through a semi-automated pipeline over 50 real-world agentic applications (40 from GitHub, 10 industrial): GPT-4o generates ~20 user queries per agent, human annotators rewrite them, and a block-wise extraction strategy segments each long instruction into self-contained semantic blocks before GPT-4o extracts atomic constraints, followed by cross-block validation and human verification, yielding 8,415 constraints across 707 instructions. Constraints are classified along two orthogonal axes—type (formatting, semantic, tool) and presentation (vanilla, conditional, example)—and each is annotated with a specific evaluation method (deterministic Python code, GPT-4o LLM judgment, or hybrid extraction-then-code). The paper further introduces meta-constraints (constraint selection, detailing, prioritization) as a novel category governing other constraints, and validates the automated evaluator against human judgment at 94% agreement before running zero-shot evaluations across 20+ models including thinking, non-thinking, and instruction-following-specialized LLMs.

## Results

On AGENTIF, all evaluated LLMs perform poorly, with the best model (GPT-5) achieving only 60.8% Constraint Success Rate (CSR) and 30.2% Instruction Success Rate (ISR). Performance drops dramatically compared to the standard IFEval benchmark: GPT-4o falls from 87.0% to 58.5% CSR. The weakest constraint dimensions are condition constraints (33.7–44.3% across models) and tool constraints (39.8–50.9%), while example constraints are handled best (50.5–87.2%). When instruction length exceeds 6,000 words, ISR scores for all models drop to nearly 0.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AGENTIF is compared against seven prior instruction-following benchmarks (IFEval, FollowBench, InfoBench, SysBench, ComplexBench, AgentOrca, Multi-IF) along dimensions of realism, length, constraint count, and constraint type coverage. AGENTIF is the only benchmark that simultaneously covers tool constraints, conditional constraints, and example constraints, and it is the only one sourced from real-world agentic applications rather than synthetic data. Its instructions average 1,723 words (vs. 36 for IFEval, 253 for FollowBench, 521 for SysBench) and contain 11.9 constraints per instruction (vs. 1.5 for IFEval, 3.0 for FollowBench). The most striking cross-benchmark comparison is that GPT-4o's CSR drops from 87.0% on IFEval to 58.5% on AGENTIF, a 28.5-point decline, demonstrating that agentic instructions pose substantially harder challenges. Among the evaluated models, thinking models (GPT-5, o1-mini, DeepSeek-R1) consistently outperform non-thinking counterparts by 1–3 CSR points, while instruction-following-specific academic models (Crab-DPO-7B, Conifer-DPO-7B) perform worst overall (CSR 44.3–47.2), trailing the best model by over 13 points.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the instruction-following evaluation lineage (IFEval, FollowBench, InfoBench, SysBench, ComplexBench, AgentOrca, Multi-IF) by shifting from short, synthetically generated instructions to long, real-world agentic system prompts. While SysBench and AgentOrca are the closest prior work, the former uses short system prompts without tool usage and the latter focuses narrowly on function invocation without complex constraint structures. AGENTIF carves new ground by introducing tool constraints as a distinct constraint type, incorporating conditional and example-based constraint presentations, and identifying meta constraints that govern other constraints — none of which are covered in prior benchmarks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AGENTIF fills a critical evaluation gap for the rapidly growing field of LLM-based agents, providing the first systematic measurement of whether models can actually follow the long, complex system prompts that underpin real agentic deployments. The finding that even the best model perfectly follows fewer than 30% of instructions, combined with the identification of specific failure modes (conditional triggering, tool specification adherence, meta-constraint prioritization), gives practitioners concrete targets for improvement. Open questions include how to scale instruction-following training to handle 1,700+ word instructions with 12+ constraints, whether reinforcement learning can address the specification-heavy failure modes identified here, and how to handle the multilingual and multi-turn extensions the authors note as limitations.
