## Problem

Standard RL alignment methods (RLHF) optimize average reward, but as training progresses, low-reward outputs become increasingly rare in sampling and receive fewer direct gradient updates, limiting how fast their probability can be reduced. Existing approaches that explicitly target the low-reward tail (e.g., CVaR-RL, reward transformations) typically sacrifice average-case performance. The paper asks whether this tradeoff can be improved by adaptively focusing sampling on undesirable outputs without degrading expected reward.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt s_0 and a scalar reward model score r(s_0:T) as input, and produces a fine-tuned autoregressive language model p_θ that assigns lower probability to low-reward sequences. Training requires a separate reward model (e.g., Deberta-v3-large-v2 or Skywork-Reward-V2-Llama-3.2-1B) to score generated sequences, and a learned proposal LM q_ξ that is jointly optimized with p_θ. The method is agnostic to the prompt distribution, allowing a mix of adversarial and non-adversarial prompts to be used during training.

## Method

RePULSe augments the standard RL loss (REINFORCE/RLOO) with an additional loss that directly reduces p_θ's probability on samples drawn from a target distribution σ_θ that amplifies low-reward regions (e.g., σ_θ ∝ p_θ(s|s_0)·exp(−βr(s_0:T))). To efficiently sample from σ_θ as p_θ evolves, the method learns a proposal LM q_ξ via self-normalized importance sampling, trained to minimize the mass-covering KL divergence D_KL(σ_θ | q_ξ) so that q_ξ covers diverse undesirable outputs rather than collapsing to a single mode. A key technical contribution is a novel proposal-centric parameterization of the Contrastive Twist Learning loss, where twist functions ψ_ξ^t are derived from the log-ratio log q_ξ(s_t|s_0:t−1) − log p_θ(s_t|s_0:t−1), enabling end-to-end optimization of a single autoregressive LM for proposal generation while retaining the variance-reduction benefits of the CTL objective. The resulting gradient simultaneously maximizes expected reward (via the standard RL term) and suppresses the probability of undesirable sequences (via the σ_θ-weighted gradient ascent on log p_θ).

## Results

In the toy experiment (DistilGPT2, 2-token outputs), RePULSe monotonically reduces the total probability of bad outputs to a substantially lower final level than RLOO or PPO, while all methods achieve nearly identical average reward. In the more realistic Setting 1 (SmolLM-135M, T=20) and Setting 2 (Llama-3.2-1B, T=100), RePULSe improves the Pareto frontier of average return (including KL penalty) versus the probability of bad outputs (r < −5 and r < −7 respectively) at lower bad-output probabilities, despite using only half the gradient updates on p_θ. On adversarial robustness, RePULSe reduces the GCG attack success rate (measured over 10 held-out prompts, 1000 samples each) compared to all baselines in both settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares RePULSe against four baselines: PPO, RLOO (REINFORCE-LeaveOneOut), reward-transformed-REINFORCE, and a p_θ-proposal ablation (using p_θ itself instead of the learned q_ξ for σ_θ sampling). RePULSe outperforms all baselines on the probability-of-bad-outputs metric at comparable average return, improving the Pareto frontier in both model settings (Fig. 3, Fig. 4). The reward-transformed-REINFORCE baseline (red dashed line in Fig. 3/4) achieves a similar tradeoff to standard RLOO but does not match RePULSe at the low bad-output-probability end. The p_θ-proposal ablation (grey dotted line) confirms that the learned proposal q_ξ is critical: without it, the method degrades toward standard RL performance because p_θ increasingly fails to sample low-reward outputs as training progresses. On GCG adversarial robustness (Fig. 5, Fig. 6), RePULSe achieves a lower attack success rate than all baselines at comparable average return, with the advantage most pronounced at higher α values (more aggressive attack settings). Notably, RePULSe achieves these gains while performing only half as many p_θ gradient updates as the baselines, suggesting the computational cost of training q_ξ is well-spent.

## Relation to Prior Work

RePULSe builds on the probabilistic inference framework of Zhao et al. (2024), who showed that LM tasks can be cast as sampling from twisted target distributions, and on the mass-covering KL divergence / Distributional Policy Gradient objective from Parshakova et al. (2019) for learning proposals. It departs from standard RLHF (PPO, RLOO, REINFORCE) by introducing a separate learned proposal q_ξ that actively samples low-reward outputs rather than relying on p_θ to encounter them, and from CVaR-RL / worst-case RL by using a general gradient-based proposal learning method applicable to LMs rather than rejection sampling or analytically tractable settings. The method also contrasts with adversarial training, which typically uses rejection sampling on p_θ, and with reward transformations, which penalize low-reward sequences but degrade average-case performance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically critical concern: at billions of user queries, even a one-in-a-million failure mode poses significant risk, and standard RL naturally stops providing gradient signal on rare bad outputs as training progresses. RePULSe's ability to maintain a better reward-vs-tail-risk Pareto frontier while using only half the p_θ updates suggests a compute-efficient path to safer LMs, and the observed GCG robustness improvement hints at broader safety benefits. Key open questions include whether the method scales to frontier models with longer outputs, how q_ξ exploration behaves when the base model already has very low bad-output probability, and the dual-use risk of releasing a model explicitly trained to generate undesirable content.
