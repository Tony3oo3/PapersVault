## Problem

Interpreting coral reef images requires specialized marine biology expertise, limiting who can extract ecological and health information from them. While VQA powered by large vision-language models offers a user-friendly alternative, no dedicated coral reef VQA dataset exists that addresses the two key challenges of domain-specific taxonomic annotations and multidimensional ecological/health questions spanning coral condition, growth, and symbiotic relationships.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251863/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each sample pairs a real-world underwater coral reef image (average resolution 1350×1280 pixels) with a natural-language question drawn from 16 dimensions split into basic visual attributes (category, color, position, quantity, size, shape, scene type, presence) and ecological/health attributes (growth, algal presence, symbiosis, bleaching, structural integrity, susceptibility, environment, health quantity). Spatial questions reference a 3×3 grid overlay on the image; 42.1% of the 277,653 QA pairs are open-ended (free-text answers) while the remainder are closed-ended yes/no questions. The output is a short text answer (average 1.01 words on the test set).

## Method

The paper introduces a six-stage semi-automatic pipeline: (1) collecting 16,862 images from MLC, XL Catlin Seaview Survey, and CoralsofttheWorld, filtered to 12,805 via an underwater image quality metric; (2) marine-biologist-led label cleaning and genus-level re-annotation using majority voting across three experts; (3) automated extraction of basic visual attributes plus manual annotation of ecological/health attributes; (4) prompt engineering with multiple style variants for GPT-4o; (5) multimodal QA generation where GPT-4o receives both the image and extracted attributes, with a 3×3 grid for spatial grounding, temperature 0.3 for reproducibility, and a per-image cap of three questions per type; (6) three-stage human verification (manual correction by 12 marine-science students, cross-checking, and 10% expert sampling) that modified 13.4% of questions and 56.8% of answers. For benchmarking, four open-source LVLMs (Mini-Gemini, BLIP3, Qwen2.5VL, InternVL2.5) are fine-tuned on the training split and evaluated alongside zero-shot GPT-4o and Claude 3.5 Haiku on three tasks: standard test, cross-region generalization (Hawaii), and a novel bleaching-coverage estimation task scored by MAE/MASE.

## Results

On the CoralVQA test set, InternVL2.5 (fine-tuned) achieves the highest average accuracy at 71.35% on basic visual attributes and 81.42% on ecological/health-related attributes, outperforming all other models. Closed-source models GPT-4o (54.94% / 43.42%) and Claude 3.5 Haiku (45.93% / 37.61%) perform substantially worse than fine-tuned open-source models on coral-specific tasks. On the cross-region (Hawaii) test set, all models drop by over 30%, with InternVL2.5 falling to 18.75% (basic) and 28.25% (ecological). On the bleaching-coverage reasoning task, InternVL2.5 achieves MAE 0.0818 and MASE 0.8967, while Qwen2.5VL reaches MAE 0.1124 and MASE 1.2326, indicating that even the best model still exhibits large prediction errors on complex quantitative reasoning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

InternVL2.5 (fine-tuned) dominates both question groups on the standard test set, achieving 71.35% (basic visual) and 81.42% (ecological/health), surpassing Qwen2.5VL (58.60% / 72.39%) by 12.75 and 9.03 percentage points respectively. Surprisingly, both closed-source models underperform the fine-tuned open-source baselines: GPT-4o scores 54.94% / 43.42% and Claude 3.5 Haiku scores 45.93% / 37.61%, revealing that general-purpose LVLMs lack coral-specific knowledge. BLIP3 (FT) is competitive on ecological/health questions (80.32%, within 1.1 pp of InternVL2.5) but lags significantly on basic visual attributes (50.37%). Mini-Gemini (FT) is the weakest model overall (12.76% / 34.04%), struggling particularly with open-ended questions like category (8.72%) and color (2.01%). On the cross-region generalization test, all models degrade by >30%, with InternVL2.5 dropping from 71.35% to 18.75% on basic visual questions, highlighting a critical limitation in geographic transferability. On the bleaching-coverage task, InternVL2.5 outperforms Qwen2.5VL (MAE 0.0818 vs. 0.1124), while Mini-Gemini and BLIP3 fail to produce meaningful answers, indicating that complex spatial-quantitative reasoning remains largely unsolved for current LVLMs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the well-established VQA task (Antol et al., 2015) for a new domain by constructing the first large-scale coral reef VQA dataset, drawing on existing coral image datasets (MLC, XL Catlin Seaview Survey, CoralsofttheWorld) that were originally designed for classification or segmentation. It departs from prior coral datasets, which provide only taxonomic labels or segmentation masks, by introducing 16-dimensional question-answer pairs spanning both basic visual attributes and ecological/health-related assessments. The semi-automatic pipeline leverages GPT-4o's multimodal capabilities for question generation, extending the LLM-as-data-augmentation paradigm to a domain requiring marine biology expertise, with a three-stage human verification process to ensure quality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CoralVQA provides the first comprehensive benchmark for evaluating LVLMs on domain-specific ecological reasoning, revealing that even strong closed-source models (GPT-4o, Claude 3.5 Haiku) perform poorly on coral tasks and that cross-region generalization degrades by over 30%. The consistent failure across all models on spatial counting and morphological questions highlights a fundamental gap in current vision-language architectures for fine-grained ecological understanding. Open questions include whether domain-specific pre-training or knowledge injection can close the cross-region gap, and whether VQA can serve as a practical alternative to multi-step segmentation pipelines for bleaching assessment. The semi-automatic pipeline is designed to generalize to other marine domains, potentially enabling rapid dataset construction for under-studied ecosystems.
