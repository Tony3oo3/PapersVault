## Problem

CLIP and other vision-language models remain highly vulnerable to adversarial perturbations because their global matching paradigm causes image and text features to occupy separate regions of the embedding space, a misalignment that is severely amplified under attack. Existing defenses (adversarial training, prompt tuning, test-time counterattacks) either incur high computational cost or fail to address this fundamental cross-modal feature gap, leaving a near-collapse in classification accuracy under even small ℓ∞ perturbations.

## Input / Output

At test time, COLA takes a single adversarially perturbed RGB image and a set of K class names. It generates N=5 augmented image views (random crops, flips, resizes) and M=50 LLM-generated text descriptions per class, then outputs a single predicted class label. The method is entirely training-free and plug-and-play: it requires only a precomputed SVD projection matrix (top C=256 components of the text embedding matrix) and operates on top of any frozen CLIP backbone (ViT-B/32, ViT-B/16, ViT-L/14) without architectural modification or retraining.

## Method

COLA addresses adversarial misalignment at two levels within a unified optimal transport framework. First, it projects each adversarial image embedding onto a text-induced subspace (spanned by the top-256 SVD components of all class text features), which filters out non-semantic perturbations orthogonal to the semantic manifold while preserving discriminative information. Second, it models the image and each class as discrete distributions over their augmented views, with importance weights derived from prediction entropy, and computes the OT transport cost using the projected features (1 − cosine similarity between projected image views and text prototypes). Classification is performed by selecting the class with the minimum OT distance. The paper provides theoretical guarantees that the projection strictly reduces pairwise cosine similarity distortion (Δ_Π ≤ Δ) and enlarges the OT decision margin (γ(C^Π) ≥ γ(C)), implying improved generalization under adversarial conditions.

## Results

Under PGD attacks (ε=1/255), COLA achieves 50.0% robust accuracy on ImageNet (vs. 1.1% for CLIP and 40.0% for TTC) while maintaining 62.8% clean accuracy (vs. 62.1% for CLIP and 51.7% for TTC). Across the five ImageNet variants, COLA attains an average robust accuracy of 45.6% under PGD and 43.6% under CW, outperforming TTC (38.9% and 38.8%) and CLIP (26.5% and 26.6%). On the 9-dataset benchmark, COLA improves robust accuracy by up to +19.3% over TTC (e.g., 77.2% vs. 57.9% on Pets under PGD) while preserving or slightly exceeding clean accuracy across all datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

COLA dominates all baselines on both clean and robust accuracy. Against the strongest test-time defense TTC, COLA gains +10.0% robust accuracy on ImageNet under PGD (50.0% vs. 40.0%) while also recovering clean accuracy (62.8% vs. 51.7%), since TTC's counterattack mechanism degrades clean performance. Against fine-tuning-based methods (TeCoA, PMG, FARE), COLA is training-free yet achieves substantially higher robust accuracy (e.g., 50.0% vs. 19.0%/22.1%/14.0% on ImageNet PGD) without any retraining. Against other test-time defenses (TTE, HD, Anti-Adv), COLA improves robust accuracy by 20–40+ percentage points across all 9 diverse datasets. On the 9-dataset benchmark under PGD, COLA leads TTC by +19.3% on Pets, +11.3% on Flowers, +6.7% on DTD, and +9.5% on Caltech101 in robust accuracy, while matching or exceeding clean accuracy in every case.

## Relation to Prior Work

COLA sits at the intersection of two research threads: adversarial robustness for vision-language models and optimal transport for cross-modal alignment. It departs from the dominant adversarial training paradigm (TeCoA, PMG, FARE) and prompt-tuning approaches by proposing a purely test-time, training-free defense, and it extends the subspace projection idea from Project-Probe-Aggregate [65] into an adversarial setting. The OT component builds on prior work using Sinkhorn-based transport for few-shot VLM alignment (AWT [71], PLOT [8]), but those methods operate at training time or require prompt optimization; COLA is the first to embed subspace projection directly into the OT cost matrix as a test-time adversarial defense. The closest test-time baseline, TTC [59], also uses augmentation ensembling but relies on iterative counterattack optimization, whereas COLA replaces that with a single OT solve.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

COLA offers a practical, drop-in defense for deployed CLIP models: no retraining, no architectural changes, and faster inference than the strongest test-time baseline (TTC), which matters for real-world systems where retraining is infeasible. The theoretical guarantees (reduced cosine distortion, enlarged OT decision margins) provide a principled explanation for why the method works, going beyond empirical observation. However, the text-induced subspace may encode dataset-specific priors that limit generalization to unseen domains, and the paper acknowledges that stronger defenses could provoke adaptive attacks. Open questions include whether the approach extends to multimodal generation tasks, whether the SVD-based subspace can be made more adaptive per-image, and how the method performs under adaptive or distribution-shift attacks beyond the standard PGD/CW budgets tested here.
