## Problem

Existing context-aware low-rank approximation methods for LLM compression (e.g., SVD-LLM, SVD-LLM V2) rely on explicit Gram matrix computation (XX^T) and subsequent inversion, which introduces large numerical errors when input activation matrices are nearly singular or have very small singular values. These instabilities degrade approximation quality, and the approach also fails when calibration matrices exceed GPU memory or when insufficient data makes the problem ill-posed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252388/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a weight matrix W ∈ R^{m×n} and a calibration activation matrix X ∈ R^{n×k} (where k = batch size × context length, potentially reaching ~10.9 GB for LLaMA3-8B) and produces a rank-r factorization A ∈ R^{m×r}, B ∈ R^{r×n} such that W' = AB minimizes ‖(W−W')X‖_F. A key design constraint is that X need not be stored in full: it is processed in chunks via Tall-Skinny QR, and the method remains well-defined even when X is rank-deficient or when k < n (the low-data regime handled via a regularization parameter μ).

## Method

COALA replaces the Gram-matrix-plus-inversion pipeline with an inversion-free orthogonal projection: it computes the QR factorization X^T = QR (using TSQR for memory efficiency), then takes the SVD of the smaller matrix WR^T and sets W' = U_r U_r^T W, where U_r holds the first r left singular vectors. This avoids both forming XX^T and inverting any matrix, eliminating the source of numerical instability. For the low-data or overfitting-prone regime, a Tikhonov regularization term μ‖W−W'‖_F² is added, which is shown to be equivalent to augmenting X with √μ I and applying the same QR+SVD procedure. The paper proves (via the Davis-Kahan sin Θ theorem) that the regularized solution W_μ converges linearly to the unregularized solution W_0 as μ→0, with an explicit error bound depending on the singular-value gap σ_r(WX)² − σ_{r+1}(WX)². The framework also subsumes PiSSA (α=0) and CorDA (α=2) as special cases of a general weighted problem, providing a numerically robust alternative to CorDA's explicit (XX^T)^{-1} computation.

## Results

On Mistral-7B compressed to 80%, COALA achieves 78.07% on BoolQ, 77.04% on PIQA, 72.13% on ARC-E, and 43.43% on ARC-C, outperforming SVD-LLM (68.29/73.39/71.34/40.53) and SoLA (66.09/73.67/69.99/39.76) on most metrics, though trailing SoLA on MMLU (41.20 vs. 44.20). At 70% compression, COALA maintains 70.40% PIQA and 63.63% ARC-E, again leading SVD-LLM and SoLA on most tasks. In fine-tuning (Table 4), COALA with α=1 achieves 75.5% average accuracy, matching PiSSA (75.4%) and LoRA (75.0%) while substantially outperforming CorDA (60.9%). Computation time is reduced by ~28–50% compared to SVD-LLM and SVD-LLM V2 (e.g., 1811s vs. 3625s/4211s on LLaMA3-8B).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SVD-LLM [46] and SVD-LLM V2 [44], COALA wins on 6 of 8 metrics at 80% compression (BoolQ +9.8, PIQA +3.7, HSwag +3.3, ARC-E +0.8, ARC-C +2.9, OBQA +3.6) and is faster by ~50% in wall-clock time; it trails only on MMLU (41.20 vs. 41.80). Against SoLA [25], COALA leads on BoolQ (+12.0), PIQA (+3.4), HSwag (+1.7), ARC-E (+2.1), ARC-C (+3.7), and OBQA (+1.0) at 80%, but loses on MMLU (41.20 vs. 44.20) and WiNoG (68.82 vs. 68.75, essentially tied). Against FLAP [1] and SliceGPT [2], COALA dominates across all metrics by wide margins (e.g., BoolQ 78.07 vs. 62.26/37.86 at 80%). Against ASVD [50] (Table 2, 90% compression on LLaMA-3.2-1B), COALA with regularization wins on every metric (e.g., BoolQ 59.0 vs. 58.0, PIQA 62.8 vs. 52.5). In fine-tuning (Table 4), COALA α=1 matches PiSSA [33] (75.5 vs. 75.4 avg.) and LoRA [23] (75.5 vs. 75.0) while dramatically outperforming CorDA [47] (75.5 vs. 60.9), with the robustified COALA α=2 (equivalent to CorDA in exact arithmetic) recovering most of CorDA's gap (75.4 vs. 60.9).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the classical weighted low-rank approximation theory of Manton et al. (2003) and the Eckart-Young-Mirsky theorem, but departs from the SVD-LLM/ASVD/CorDA line of work by eliminating the numerically unstable Gram matrix computation and its inversion. Instead of forming S = (XX^T)^(1/2) via Cholesky or SVD and then inverting it, COALA uses an orthogonal-projection formula (W' = U_r U_r^T W) where U_r is obtained from the SVD of WR^T with R from a QR decomposition of X^T, optionally via TSQR for memory efficiency. The paper also unifies PiSSA (α=0) and CorDA (α=2) under a single Proposition 4 framework and adds a regularization term that handles the low-data regime without requiring full-rank assumptions on X.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical relevance is clear: LLM compression pipelines that rely on Gram matrix inversion can silently produce degraded approximations due to near-singular activation matrices, and this paper provides both a concrete diagnosis (Figure 1, Figure 2) and a drop-in replacement that is faster and more stable. The convergence theorem (Theorem 1) giving a linear-in-μ bound without full-rank assumptions addresses a genuine gap for low-data fine-tuning scenarios (3–5 images). Open questions include how the gap-dependent constant in the convergence bound behaves in practice for very deep networks where singular value gaps may be extremely small, and whether the regularization strategy generalizes beyond the specific μ-selection heuristic (Eq. 5) used in experiments.
