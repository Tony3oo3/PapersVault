## Problem

While reinforcement learning has been applied to stock trading and high-frequency execution, options-based volatility trading remains largely unexplored due to its dual complexity: timing long/short volatility positions and simultaneously managing path-dependent hedging decisions. Traditional approaches either rely on restrictive Black-Scholes assumptions or reduce the problem to realized volatility forecasting, failing to capture the joint optimization of position timing and adaptive hedging that determines actual PnL.

## Input / Output

The OP-Agent consumes only market features F_t (option chain data, IV surface, volume, open interest at hourly resolution) and outputs a discrete position signal in {+1, −1, 0}. The HR-Agent receives the richer state (F_t, P_t, G_t) including portfolio positions and BSM Greeks, and outputs a selection from a pool of 30 pre-trained Hedgers every 24 steps; the chosen Hedger then executes continuous underlying-asset trades at each hourly step. Notably, the two agents operate at different decision frequencies and see different state subsets, reflecting the separation of strategic timing from tactical risk management.

## Method

OPHR is a two-agent DQN framework where an OP-Agent (n-step TD, n=12) learns volatility timing and an HR-Agent (1-step TD) routes among 30 pre-trained Hedgers (Delta-threshold, Price-threshold, and Deep Hedgers with varying risk-aversion λ). Training proceeds in two phases: first, a sub-optimal Oracle policy that compares future RV against current IV with multiple thresholds generates offline trajectories to initialize the OP-Agent, reducing exploration burden; second, the agents are trained iteratively (fix one, train the other) so the OP-Agent can take more aggressive positions as the HR-Agent's hedging improves. The HR-Agent's reward is the relative PnL advantage of the selected Hedger over a rule-based baseline Hedger evaluated in a twin environment, providing a stable learning signal. This cooperative iterative design lets the two agents co-adapt: better hedging enables bolder positioning, and bolder positioning in turn demands more sophisticated hedging.

## Results

On BTC options (test period Jul 2023–Jul 2024), OPHR achieves a total return of 33.10% with an annual Sharpe ratio of 1.87, Calmar ratio of 3.35, and maximum drawdown of only 9.41%. On ETH options over the same period, OPHR delivers 44.89% total return with ASR of 1.76 and ACR of 1.58. OPHR ranks first on all eight evaluation metrics (TR, ASR, ACR, ASoR, AVOL, MDD, WR, PLR) for both assets, substantially outperforming the next-best method (OP ablation) and all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OPHR outperforms all eight baselines on every metric for both BTC and ETH. The closest competitor is DLOT (end-to-end deep learning for options PnL prediction), which achieves only 4.91% TR on BTC and 1.19% on ETH versus OPHR's 33.10% and 44.89%—a gap of roughly 28 and 44 percentage points respectively. ML-based volatility forecasters (GBDT, MLP, LSTM, DeepVol, GARCH) all produce negative total returns, with MLP being the worst (−74.55% on BTC, −70.26% on ETH), confirming that predicting RV alone does not translate into profitable path-dependent option PnL. Factor-based strategies (MR, MOM) and pure directional strategies (Long/Short) also fail to generate positive risk-adjusted returns. The ablation (OP without HR-Agent) shows that the hedger-routing component contributes an additional ~12 pp of TR on BTC and ~21 pp on ETH, highlighting the value of adaptive hedging selection.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Murray et al. (2022) for training a pool of Deep Hedgers via actor-critic methods with varying risk-aversion parameters, but departs from that work by introducing a routing agent that dynamically selects among hedgers based on live market conditions rather than deploying a single fixed hedger. It also extends the broader RL-for-trading literature (Jiang & Liang 2017, Yang et al. 2020, Qin et al. 2024), which has focused on stock or portfolio trading, to the substantially more complex setting of options-based volatility trading with path-dependent PnL and dynamic delta hedging. The closest prior work, DLOT (Tan et al. 2024), applies end-to-end deep learning to options PnL prediction but omits the hedging layer entirely; OPHR's key architectural departure is the explicit two-agent decomposition separating volatility timing (OP-Agent) from hedging strategy selection (HR-Agent), trained via a two-phase protocol combining offline Oracle distillation with iterative online co-training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first RL framework to treat volatility trading through options as a sequential decision problem with explicit hedging, filling a gap between traditional IV-RV forecasting approaches and the emerging RL-for-trading literature. The practical decomposition into a timing agent and a hedging-routing agent offers a reusable template for other derivative strategies where position selection and risk management are coupled. Key limitations include evaluation on a single test period (no error bars, acknowledged as infeasible for financial time series), restriction to ATM straddles on two crypto assets, and reliance on a sub-optimal Oracle that uses future RV for initialization. Open questions include generalization to volatility smile/skewness trading, multi-asset portfolios, and whether the multi-agent decomposition is necessary versus a single end-to-end agent with a richer action space.
