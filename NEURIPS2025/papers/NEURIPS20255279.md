## Problem

While low-precision training has become standard for large-scale neural networks, the effect of quantization on streaming PCA—specifically Oja's algorithm—remains theoretically uncharacterized. The paper asks: what is the minimum quantization resolution required to estimate the leading eigenvector to a target accuracy, and how do linear versus logarithmic quantization schemes differ in their dimension dependence? No prior work established information-theoretic lower bounds or matching upper bounds for quantized streaming PCA.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes a stream of n mean-zero vectors in R^d, a quantization grid (linear with gap δ or logarithmic with parameters ζ and δ₀), a learning rate η, and a batch count b. All intermediate quantities—the current weight vector, each per-sample gradient X_j(X_j^T w), and the accumulated batch update—are stochastically rounded to the grid, ensuring conditional unbiasedness. The output is a quantized unit vector w on the grid that approximates the true top eigenvector v₁, with error measured by sin²(w, v₁). A probability-boosting wrapper (Algorithm 2) can amplify the constant success probability to 1−θ at a Θ(log 1/θ) sample overhead.

## Method

The paper introduces a quantized batched Oja's algorithm (Algorithm 1) that applies stochastic rounding at three points per batch: the weight vector, each individual gradient term, and the summed update. The key theoretical contribution is a general convergence theorem (Theorem 1) for Oja's rule with conditionally unbiased rank-one noise, yielding sin² error bounded by a sampling term, a variance term, and a quantization term κ₁ + κ². Lower bounds (Lemmas 1–2) show the quantization error is Ω(δ²d) for linear quantization but only Ω(ζ² + δ₀²d) for logarithmic quantization; with optimal parameter allocation under a fixed β-bit budget, the latter becomes nearly dimension-free (Ṽ(4^{-β})). Batching is critical: without it, the quantization error accumulates linearly in n, whereas with b batches the error is independent of n. A SuccessBoost procedure using a quantized sin² proxy then amplifies the constant success probability to arbitrary 1−θ.

## Results

The paper establishes that under linear quantization (LQ) with β bits, the quantization error for batched Oja's algorithm is Õ(d·4^{-β}), while under logarithmic/nonlinear quantization (NLQ) it is Õ(4^{-β}) — nearly dimension-free. For the standard (non-batched) variant, the quantization error additionally scales linearly with n (i.e., dn/4^β for LQ and (β²+log²d)·n/4^β for NLQ), a dependence eliminated by batching. Empirically, on synthetic Gaussian streams (d=100, β=8), batched quantized methods require only 6–8 bits to match full-precision batched Oja's algorithm, whereas standard quantized variants need at least 10 bits. The NLQ batched method's sin²-error remains flat as dimension d grows from 100 to 500, while LQ batched error grows linearly with d.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is full-precision Oja's algorithm in both standard (b=n) and batched (b=10 or 25) forms. All quantized variants (standard_LQ, standard_NLQ, batched_LQ, batched_NLQ) closely track their full-precision counterparts in sin²-error, with the gap shrinking as β increases and converging to the full-precision curve by β=12. The key differentiator is the dimension dependence: batched_LQ error grows linearly with d (confirming the d·4^{-β} bound), while batched_NLQ error stays essentially flat, validating the nearly dimension-free Õ(4^{-β}) guarantee. A second critical comparison is standard vs. batched quantization: standard_LQ and standard_NLQ errors grow linearly with n (as predicted by Theorem 3), whereas batched_LQ and batched_NLQ errors decrease with n, matching the full-precision batched baseline. Overall, the batched NLQ scheme dominates in high-dimensional settings, achieving full-precision-level accuracy at 6–8 bits without any dimension penalty.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two well-developed threads: the extensive analysis of Oja's algorithm for streaming PCA (Jain et al. 2016, Allen-Zhu & Li 2017, Shamir 2016, and many others) and the study of low-precision/stochastic quantization effects on optimization (Alistarh et al. 2017, Li & De Sa 2019, De Sa et al. 2018). The key conceptual departure is combining these threads: prior work on quantized optimization focused on SGD for general loss functions, while prior work on streaming PCA assumed full-precision arithmetic. The paper introduces a general noisy-power-method theorem (Theorem 1) that accommodates conditionally unbiased rank-one noise, then instantiates it for both linear and logarithmic stochastic quantization of Oja's updates. The batched variant is a critical technical ingredient that changes the quantization error scaling from O(n) to O(1) in the number of samples.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical takeaway is that logarithmic (floating-point-style) quantization yields nearly dimension-free quantization error for streaming PCA, making low-precision streaming eigenvector estimation viable in high dimensions where linear quantization degrades linearly with d. The surprising n-dependence of standard (per-sample) quantized Oja's algorithm—where quantization error grows with the number of samples rather than shrinking—means that naive low-precision streaming PCA is fundamentally broken without batching, a finding with direct implications for hardware-aware algorithm design. The main limitation is that the analysis covers only the first principal component; extending to top-k via deflation is identified as future work. Experiments are limited to synthetic Gaussian data, leaving open how these guarantees hold under the heavier tails and non-stationarity common in real streaming settings.
