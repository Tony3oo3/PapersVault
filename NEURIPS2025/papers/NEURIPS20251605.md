## Problem

Existing diffusion bridge models (DDBM, DBIM, I2SB) achieve strong I2I translation quality but suffer from artificially coupled parameters, restricted bridge path formulations, and complex hyperparameter tuning, while Stochastic Interpolants offer greater design flexibility yet lack practical refinements like preconditioning and endpoint conditioning. Additionally, a previously overlooked issue is that one-to-many translation tasks (e.g., edge maps to color images) produce low conditional diversity—visually similar outputs across different sampling runs—despite injecting different noise realizations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251605/cropped_page1_idx4.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single conditioning image (edge map at 64×64, depth map at 256×256, or blurred image at 256×256) as the endpoint x_T and generates the corresponding target image (color handbag, RGB scene, or deblurred image) in 5–20 function evaluations. Unlike Stochastic Interpolants which require training two separate denoisers, ECSI uses a single network D_θ(x_t, x_T, t) that conditions on both the noisy intermediate state and the fixed endpoint. Optionally, Gaussian noise with variance b² is added to the input to form the base distribution π_T = π_cond * N(0, b²I), trading a small FID increase for substantially higher conditional diversity.

## Method

ECSI (Endpoint-Conditioned Stochastic Interpolants) unifies all existing bridge models under a single SI-based framework with decoupled parameters α_t, β_t, γ_t, showing that DDBM, DBIM, and I2SB are strict special cases. The key architectural choice is endpoint conditioning: the drift term only requires estimating E[x₀|x_t, x_T] (one network) rather than the two terms needed by vanilla SI, and EDM-style preconditioning (c_in, c_skip, c_out scaling) is incorporated for training stability. For sampling, a novel noise-control parameter ε_t = η(γ_t γ̇_t − (α̇_t/α_t)γ_t²) provides continuous interpolation between deterministic ODE (η=0) and fully stochastic SDE (η=1) sampling, with the last two steps using a closed-form update that avoids numerical instability. The paper further introduces a modified base distribution (adding controlled Gaussian noise to the input) to address the conditional diversity problem, and proposes the Average Feature Diversity (AFD) metric to quantitatively measure per-condition output variation in Inception-V3 feature space.

## Results

On Edges→Handbags (64×64), ECSI (γ_max=0.125) achieves FID 0.56, IS 4.11, and LPIPS 0.044 at 20 NFEs, substantially outperforming DDBM (FID 1.83 at 118 NFEs) and DBIM (FID 1.74 at 20 NFEs). On DIODE-Outdoor (256×256), ECSI (γ_max=0.25) reaches FID 3.27 at 20 NFEs versus DDBM's 4.43 at 118 NFEs. For ImageNet deblurring, ECSI achieves FID 0.41 (Gaussian kernel) and 1.11 (Uniform kernel) at 20 NFEs, beating the previous best (I2SB) by 3× and 3.5× respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ECSI dominates all seven baselines on both I2I benchmarks. Against the strongest prior bridge model DDBM, ECSI (γ_max=0.125) achieves FID 0.56 vs. 1.83 on Edges→Handbags while using only 20 NFEs versus DDBM's 118 NFEs — a 6× speedup with 3× lower FID. At matched NFE=20, ECSI (γ_max=0.25) beats DBIM (FID 1.74) by 20% on Edges→Handbags and DDBM's 118-NFE result by 24%. On DIODE-Outdoor, ECSI (γ_max=0.25) achieves FID 3.27 at 20 NFEs, improving over DDBM (4.43 at 118 NFEs) and DBIM (4.99 at 20 NFEs). For deblurring on ImageNet, ECSI achieves FID 0.41 (Gaussian) and 1.11 (Uniform), outperforming I2SB (3.0 and 3.9) by 3× and 3.5×, and DDNM (2.9 and 3.0) by 7× and 2.7×. The only metric where ECSI trails is MSE, which the authors attribute to higher output diversity (generated images being more distinct from references), consistent with their AFD diversity metric showing ECSI produces more varied outputs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate lines of work on diffusion bridge models: the Doob's h-transform-based approaches (DDBM, DBIM, I2SB) that offer endpoint conditioning and preconditioning but have artificially coupled parameters, and Stochastic Interpolants (SI) that provide decoupled path parameters but lack practical refinements. ECSI extends SI by adding endpoint conditioning (reducing the number of network outputs from two to one), EDM-style preconditioning, and a continuous noise-control parameter ε_t for sampling, while showing that DDBM, DBIM, I2SB, and EDM are all strict special cases of the resulting framework. The paper also departs from prior bridge models by modifying the base distribution (convolving the input with Gaussian noise) to address conditional diversity, an angle not explored in the bridge literature due to the data processing inequality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unification provides a principled design space for bridge paths and samplers, giving practitioners clear knobs (α_t, β_t, γ_t, ε_t) to tune rather than ad-hoc hyperparameter searches. The identification of the conditional diversity problem and the AFD metric fill a genuine gap: FID can be excellent while outputs for a fixed input are nearly identical, which matters for one-to-many tasks like edge-to-color. The counterintuitive finding that *destroying* information in the base distribution improves diversity opens a new design axis for bridge models. Open questions include whether optimal path shapes are task-dependent (the authors acknowledge this), whether guidance techniques can be integrated, and whether the base-distribution modification generalizes beyond the tested I2I settings.
