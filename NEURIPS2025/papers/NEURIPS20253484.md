## Problem

Existing deep RL methods for Mean Field Games are restricted to finite state/action spaces and stationary population distributions, leaving non-stationary continuous MFGs unsolved. The core difficulty is twofold: the policy must be time-dependent (departing from standard RL), and the population distribution must be modeled as a time-evolving object that supports both sampling and pointwise density evaluation for local mean-field interactions (e.g., congestion, entropy maximization). No prior RL algorithm simultaneously learns the Nash equilibrium policy and the equilibrium mean field flow in continuous, non-stationary settings.

## Input / Output

The method takes as input a continuous state x ∈ R^d and a time index t ∈ {0,…,N_T}, with the population distribution μ_t provided by the environment (or by the learned CNF during training). It produces two coupled outputs: (1) a Gaussian policy π(a|t,x) parameterized by an MLP predicting mean and standard deviation over continuous actions a ∈ R^k, and (2) a time-conditioned Conditional Normalizing Flow that both samples from and evaluates the probability density p(x|t) of the population at any (x,t) pair. The CNF output is critical because it enables direct density queries in the reward function without resorting to kernel-smoothing approximations of empirical distributions.

## Method

DEDA-FP is a Fictitious Play loop with three learned components. At each iteration, a DRL agent (SAC or PPO) computes an approximate best response against N−1 agents following the current average policy; a supervised-learning network (MLP with two 256-unit hidden layers) is trained via negative log-likelihood on a growing replay buffer to represent the running average of all past best-response policies, avoiding the intractability of averaging neural networks directly; and a time-conditioned Neural Spline Flow with autoregressive layers is trained via maximum likelihood on trajectories generated by the average policy to model the evolving population distribution. The CNF's invertible rational-quadratic spline transformations, conditioned on time t, yield exact density evaluations p(x|t) alongside efficient sampling, giving a >10× speedup over trajectory-based density estimation. The paper provides a convergence guarantee (Theorem 1) bounding the exploitability by accumulated best-response, supervised-learning, and CNF approximation errors under Lipschitz continuity assumptions on the value function and mean-field operator.

## Results

The primary quantitative result is a >10× sampling speedup: DEDA-FP generates 5000 trajectories (T=20) in 1.52±0.23 s, compared to 16.76±1.54 s for Algo.1 and 15.14±1.19 s for Algo.2. Exploitability converges to near zero across all three algorithms on all four test environments (Beach Bar, LQ, 4-rooms, Market Model), confirming that the added CNF component does not degrade equilibrium quality. In the 4-rooms case study, DEDA-FP produces a visibly smoother and more accurate mean-field flow, and can sample 10× more agents than Algo.2 within the same 10-second time budget (Figure 7).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper benchmarks DEDA-FP (Algo. 3) against two internal ablations—Algo. 1 (simple FP with behavioral buffer) and Algo. 2 (FP + supervised average policy)—as well as four prior methods (Perrin et al. 2021, Laurière et al. 2022a, Zaman et al. 2020, Angiuli et al. 2023). On exploitability, all three internal algorithms converge to near zero, so DEDA-FP does not sacrifice equilibrium quality for its distributional gains. However, DEDA-FP decisively wins on sampling efficiency (>10× faster rollout) and distribution fidelity: in the Beach Bar and 4-rooms problems, Algo. 1 and Algo. 2 produce noisy, spiky empirical distributions, while DEDA-FP yields smooth, well-concentrated flows. Critically, only DEDA-FP can directly evaluate the local density μ(x) in the reward without resorting to Gaussian-convolution approximations, which is essential for congestion-type MFGs. Among the external baselines, DEDA-FP is the first method to simultaneously handle continuous spaces, general (non-LQ) dynamics, non-stationary mean fields, local density dependence, and explicit Nash-equilibrium policy learning—a combination no single prior work achieves.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Fictitious Play framework for MFGs (Perrin et al. 2020, 2021) by combining three previously separate ingredients: DRL-based best-response computation (SAC/PPO), supervised learning for the average policy (inspired by Neural Fictitious Self-Play, Heinrich & Silver 2016, and Laurière et al. 2022a), and a time-conditioned Conditional Normalizing Flow for the population distribution. The key departure from prior work is that all existing DRL-for-MFG methods are restricted to either discrete spaces (Laurière et al. 2022a), stationary mean fields (Perrin et al. 2021, Angiuli et al. 2023), or linear-quadratic dynamics (Zaman et al. 2020); DEDA-FP is the first to handle the full combination of continuous state-action spaces, non-stationary dynamics, and general (non-LQ) rewards with local density dependence. The convergence analysis extends the error-propagation framework of Elie et al. (2020) to account for the additional approximation errors introduced by the supervised policy and the CNF distribution model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By providing the first model-free DRL solver for non-stationary continuous MFGs with general dynamics, this work removes a key barrier to applying MFGs to real-world multi-agent systems (crowd motion, finance, cloud computing) where both state spaces and population dynamics are inherently continuous and time-varying. The CNF component's ability to directly evaluate density (not just sample) is practically important for problems with local congestion or entropy terms, and the 10× sampling speedup makes finite-agent rollouts tractable. However, the theoretical analysis remains incomplete (no convergence rate for the DRL component, reliance on approximate exploitability), and the method has not been validated on truly high-dimensional or real-world problems, leaving open questions about scalability, multi-population extensions, and robustness to model misspecification.
