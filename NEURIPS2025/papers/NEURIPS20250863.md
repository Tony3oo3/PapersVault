## Problem

Linear sketching for ℓ₂ norm estimation is known to be vulnerable to adversarial inputs, but a gap existed between the quadratic upper bound on adaptive queries supported by robust estimators and the super-quadratic sizes of known attacks for general sketching matrices. This paper closes that gap by constructing a tight quadratic-size black-box attack that applies universally to any sketching matrix and any query responder, producing an adversarial input on which the optimal estimator fails.

## Input / Output

The adversary issues r = Õ(γ²α²k²log²k) nonadaptive queries of the form v = w·e_h + c·z, where w is drawn from a piecewise-linear density W, e_h is a random standard basis vector, and z is Gaussian noise supported on a random set M ⊂ [n]\{h} of size m = Ω(k²). The black-box system returns a norm gap response s ∈ {−1, 1} (indicating whether ‖v‖₂ ≤ 1 or ≥ 1+α). The output is a unit adversarial noise vector z^(adv) = Σ s^(t)z^(t) / ‖Σ s^(t)z^(t)‖₂ that causes the optimal estimator for the query distribution to deviate from the true norm by a multiplicative factor of at least γ.

## Method

The attack exploits the correlation between the responder's norm gap output and the deviation of the optimal signal estimator T_{h,M}. Each query combines a sparse signal at a random coordinate h with dense Gaussian noise on a superlinear support M, ensuring the sketch cannot exactly recover the norm (unlike k-sparse inputs). The key gain lemma shows that when the responder is accurate, E[ψ(Av)·(T_{h,M}(Av)−w)] = Ω(α⁻¹c³σ_T²(h,M)), so correct responses systematically align the signed noise accumulation toward the adversarial direction. A lower bound on σ_T²(h,M) = Ω̃(1/k) for most column choices (via a fragile-column argument) guarantees the per-step gain is nontrivial. Since the norm of the signed sum is O(√r) while the deviation grows linearly in r, r = Õ(γ²α²k²log²k) queries suffice to push the deviation past γ, yielding a tight quadratic query complexity that matches known robustness upper bounds for JL and AMS sketches.

## Results

The main theoretical result (Theorem 1.1) establishes that a universal, nonadaptive attack using r = O(γ²α²k²log²k) queries either causes a constant fraction of norm-gap responses to be incorrect or constructs an adversarial vector on which the optimal estimator fails by a multiplicative factor γ. Empirically, the attack is effective on both JL and AMS sketches with configurations (k,n) ∈ {(100,500), (250,1000), (1000,5000)} for JL and (100,500), (250,1000), (1000,2000)} for AMS, with the estimate-to-norm ratio deviating from 1 as a function of query count. The bias grows approximately as the square root of the number of queries, and the attack succeeds even with noise support as small as n/k ∈ [2,5], well below the theoretical requirement of n = Ω(k²).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not perform a head-to-head comparison against other attack methods; instead, it positions its contribution relative to prior theoretical bounds. The quadratic query complexity O(k²log²k) tightly matches the known upper bounds for robust estimators on JL and AMS sketches, closing the gap with prior attacks: Hardt and Woodruff [26] gave a polynomial-in-k attack that defeats all responders, while Cohen et al. [15] gave a quadratic attack on AMS sketches that defeats only the standard estimator. The present work achieves the same quadratic complexity but is universal (applies to any sketching matrix and any responder) and guarantees failure of the optimal estimator. Empirically, the paper compares the standard estimator (σ=0) against robustified variants (σ ∈ {0.1, 0.2, 0.4}) on the same query vectors: the standard estimator accumulates adversarial bias fastest, while higher σ slows the bias growth at the cost of reduced non-adaptive accuracy, confirming the accuracy-robustness trade-off predicted by theory.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of adversarial attacks on linear sketches initiated by Hardt and Woodruff (STOC 2013), who showed a polynomial-size attack constructing a distribution that defeats any estimator, and by Cohen et al. (AAAI 2023), who gave a quadratic universal attack specifically on AMS/countsketch matrices. The key departure is universality: the attack here applies to *any* sketching matrix A ∈ R^{k×n} and *any* query responder (including randomized, adaptive, or distribution-tailored ones), while guaranteeing failure of the optimal (MSE-minimizing) estimator rather than merely the standard one. The single-batch (nonadaptive) structure—where all queries are i.i.d. and only the final adversarial vector is constructed from responses—distinguishes it from prior adaptive attacks that require multiple rounds of information extraction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result closes the gap between the known quadratic upper bound on adaptive queries (via robust estimators with noise/subsampling) and the previously best super-quadratic lower bounds for general sketching matrices, establishing a tight Θ(k²) characterization in the batch-query model. Two open questions remain: (i) whether a quadratic-size attack can defeat *all* responders (not just the optimal one), which would require Ω̃(k) adaptive batches and is outside the single-batch setting, and (ii) whether the noise support can be reduced from the theoretical Ω(k²) to the empirically observed O(k). The paper also draws a structural parallel to adversarial examples in image classification—aggregating small aligned perturbations to fool a model—suggesting that linearity-driven accumulation may underlie vulnerability even in highly nonlinear architectures, though this connection remains speculative.
