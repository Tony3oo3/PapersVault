## Problem

Designing RNA molecules that bind specific small-molecule ligands remains an open problem: existing RNA design tools handle either inverse folding (sequence from structure) or backbone generation, but none perform ligand-conditioned de novo co-design of both RNA structure and sequence. Three compounding gaps hinder progress—RNA's conformational flexibility demands simultaneous structure-sequence modeling, no prior method explicitly conditions on ligand geometry, and high-quality RNA-ligand structural data is scarce.

## Input / Output

The model takes a target ligand specified either as a 3D atomic structure (atom types + Cartesian coordinates) or as a SMILES string (converted to 3D conformers via RDKit/MMFF), along with an RNA length (either ground-truth or sampled uniformly from [50,150] in steps of 20). It outputs a full RNA representation: per-nucleotide C4' positions and C3'–C4'–O4' rotation matrices (SE(3) frames), a discrete nucleotide sequence over {A,C,G,U}, and 8 torsion angles per nucleotide that determine the complete backbone geometry. RNA lengths are constrained to 30–200 nucleotides during training to balance diversity with GPU memory limits.

## Method

RiboFlow performs synergistic flow matching that jointly denoises continuous SE(3) backbone frames (via closed-form interpolation on R³ and SO(3)) and discrete nucleotide-type probability vectors (via linear interpolation from a uniform prior to one-hot targets) within a single SE(3)-equivariant network, so that sequence and structure are co-designed rather than generated sequentially. A shallow ResNet torsion-angle module, conditioned on the predicted structural frames, regresses the 8 per-nucleotide torsion angles to enforce local geometric validity. Ligand conditioning is injected through a two-stage distance-aware interaction module: an MLP embeds ligand atoms, then an Invariant Point Attention (IPA) layer first predicts an initial RNA frame and refines it using pairwise RNA–ligand spatial interaction features (Gaussian distance kernels). Training proceeds in two stages—co-design pre-training on 7,154 RNAsolo structures to establish structural priors, followed by fine-tuning on the newly curated RiboBind dataset (1,591 complexes / 3,012 RNA-ligand pairs from PDB, augmented via dynamic cropping around interaction sites to 4,445 pairs)—with a combined loss over translation, rotation, torsion angles, and nucleotide-type cross-entropy.

## Results

On the sequence-based evaluation set (66 RNA-ligand pairs), RiboFlow+Pre+Crop with ligand structure and sampled length achieves an AF3 score of 54.8% and GerNA-Bind score of 0.448, compared to 22.7% and 0.209 for RNA-FrameFlow — a ~2.4× improvement in binding confidence. Structural validity (scTM ≥ 0.45) reaches 25.6% for RiboFlow+Pre+Crop versus 22.7% for RNA-FrameFlow, while the unconditional generation validity of RiboFlow (34.7% at 50 steps) exceeds RNA-FrameFlow (25.0%) by ~10 percentage points. In the SMILES-only setting, RiboFlow-T achieves the highest AF3 score (46.2%) and GerNA score (0.472), demonstrating robustness even without a 3D ligand structure.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole external baseline is RNA-FrameFlow [Anand et al., 2024], a general RNA backbone generation model not conditioned on ligands. RiboFlow+Pre+Crop decisively outperforms RNA-FrameFlow on all binding-affinity metrics: AF3 score improves from 22.7% to 54.8% (~2.4×), GerNA-Bind from 0.209 to 0.448 (~2.1×), and Vina Top10 from −4.27 to −4.58 kcal/mol. On structural validity, RiboFlow+Pre+Crop (25.6%) edges out RNA-FrameFlow (22.7%), while diversity is comparable (0.589 vs. 0.545). In the unconditional generation setting (Table 1), RiboFlow achieves 34.7% validity at 50 sampling steps versus 25.0% for RNA-FrameFlow, a ~10-point gain attributed to the sequence-structure co-design loss. The paper notes that no existing method specifically targets small-molecule RNA design, so RNA-FrameFlow serves as the closest available comparison despite lacking ligand conditioning.

## Relation to Prior Work

RiboFlow extends the SE(3) flow matching framework from protein backbone generation (Yim et al., 2023) and discrete flow matching for protein co-design (Campbell et al., 2024) to the RNA domain, combining continuous SE(3) interpolation for backbone frames with a discrete probability-vector flow for nucleotide types in a single synergistic architecture. It builds directly on RNA-FrameFlow (Anand et al., 2024) for RNA parameterization and torsion angle prediction, but departs from all prior RNA design work by introducing explicit ligand conditioning via a distance-aware IPA interaction module and a co-design pre-training strategy that jointly optimizes sequence and structure. The paper also introduces RiboBind, a curated RNA-ligand dataset that substantially expands on existing resources like HairBoss and PDBBind.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first model to perform ligand-conditioned de novo RNA co-design (sequence + structure), opening a path toward computationally designed RNA therapeutics and biosensors targeting specific small molecules. The RiboBind dataset (1,591 complexes, 3,012 pairs) addresses a critical data bottleneck in the field. However, structural validity remains moderate (~35% scTM), RNA length is capped at 200 nucleotides, and all binding assessments are in-silico (Vina, GerNA, AF3) with no experimental validation. Open questions include whether the approach scales to longer functional RNAs, how well it generalizes to ligand classes underrepresented in RiboBind, and whether the predicted binding affinities translate to measurable experimental affinity.
