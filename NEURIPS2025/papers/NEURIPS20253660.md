## Problem

Tropical cyclone multi-task estimation suffers from diverse distribution shifts (covariate, label, and concept) caused by spatiotemporal heterogeneity in TC environmental fields, yet existing methods rely on data-driven multi-modal fusion without modeling the intrinsic distribution of feature representations. General invariant learning approaches fail in this domain because they lack domain-specific physical knowledge, and prior physics-informed TC methods do not explicitly address OOD generalization across all three shift types simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253660/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes two time-step multi-channel infrared satellite images (4 IR channels from Himawari-8 at 5 km spatial and 10 min temporal resolution) concatenated with tabular auxiliary data—developmental factors (previous intensity level, minutes since named storm) and correlation factors (TC fullness, concentration ratio, energy ratio, TC width)—all collected from the 12 hours preceding the estimation time. The output is a four-dimensional regression vector: TC wind speed (m/s), central pressure (hPa), inner-core size (nmi), and outer-core size (nmi), estimated jointly via shared and task-specific identity tokens.

## Method

IDOL initializes identity tokens via Gaussian sampling over encoder features (VGG13 + Self-Attention ConvLSTM backbone), then imposes physics-guided constraints on their distributions rather than on the features themselves. A Task Dependency Flow module decouples the Holland wind-field equation into a Development Encoder (producing intensity-specific identity tokens for wind speed and pressure) and a Prior-aware Approximator that uses an LSTM-inspired gated iterative algorithm to simulate the nonlinear power operation, transforming intensity tokens into size tokens (inner-core and outer-core). A Correlation-Aware Information Bridge constructs a dark knowledge graph encoding latent physical correlations among TC attributes and input factors, processed by stacked GConv layers and a self-adaptive Gaussian Mixture Model to yield a task-shared identity token bridging input and output. Four estimation heads with correlation-aware transformer attention produce final predictions, trained with MAE loss plus an identity-oriented constraint (L_idc) that aligns intra-sample correlation structures of the learned identity tokens with those of ground-truth labels.

## Results

On the PDTC dataset, IDOL achieves the best MAE across all four TC estimation tasks: 5.93 m/s (wind speed), 5.77 hPa (pressure), 6.24 nmi (inner-core size), and 17.06 nmi (outer-core size), with correspondingly lowest RMSE and STD values. These represent improvements of 32.9%, 29%, 22.9%, and 34% in MAE over the strongest baseline (DeepTCNet). The STD metric, which measures estimation stability across unseen TCs, also shows consistent gains (e.g., 4.75 vs. 7.76 for wind speed), confirming better generalization under distribution shifts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IDOL outperforms all 11 baselines on every reported metric across all four tasks. The closest competitor is DeepTCNet (physics-augmented multi-task CNN), which IDOL surpasses by 32.9% MAE on wind speed (5.93 vs. 8.84), 29% on pressure (5.77 vs. 8.13), 22.9% on inner-core (6.24 vs. 8.09), and 34% on outer-core (17.06 vs. 25.86). Among invariant learning baselines (IRM, V-Rex, SADE, DirMixE), none approach IDOL's performance; the best invariant method (IRM) still lags by 38% MAE on wind speed and 41% on outer-core, confirming that generic domain-invariance strategies without task-specific physical priors are insufficient for TC estimation. PeRCNN, which models polynomial correlations, achieves the best pressure MAE among non-IDOL methods (6.99) but is outperformed by IDOL on all other tasks. Traditional methods (ADT, MTCSWA) cover only a subset of tasks and are substantially weaker where comparable.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Holland wind field model (a classical empirical TC physics model) and repurposes it as an inductive bias for learning task-specific identity tokens, rather than using it as a post-hoc constraint or loss term. It departs from generic invariant learning methods (IRM, V-REx, MoE-based approaches) by arguing that domain-agnostic distributional invariance is insufficient for meteorological tasks, and instead grounds invariance in prior physical knowledge. The framework also extends multi-modal TC estimation methods (DeepTCNet, PeRCNN) by introducing a 'dark knowledge graph' inspired by knowledge distillation literature to model latent physical correlations as a task-shared identity bridge, combining observational and inductive physics biases in a unified identity-oriented estimation objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that encoding domain-specific physics into feature distribution constraints (via identity tokens) can substantially outperform both data-driven multi-task methods and generic invariant learning approaches for TC estimation, with 30%+ MAE reductions over the strongest baseline. This suggests a broader principle: for tasks governed by well-understood physical laws, constraining the *distribution* of learned representations with prior physics may be more effective than constraining outputs or losses. Open questions include whether the manually constructed dark knowledge graph and Holland model assumptions generalize to other basins or TC types (e.g., extratropical transitions), and whether the identity-token paradigm transfers to other physics-governed multi-task estimation problems beyond meteorology.
