## Problem

Existing missing data imputation methods either rely on sequential dimension-wise updates (MICE, HyperImpute) that scale poorly to high-dimensional data, or on GAN-based adversarial training (GAIN) that is unstable and prone to mode collapse. While prior work observed that GAIN's generator training implicitly reduces mutual information between imputed data and the missingness mask, this connection was not formalized into a principled optimization objective, and the proposed Wasserstein gradient flow correction was shown to be incorrect. The paper asks: can mutual information reduction be made into a rigorous, solvable imputation criterion that avoids adversarial training?

## Input / Output

The method takes a partially observed data vector (with NaN entries) and a binary missingness mask of the same dimension, and iteratively produces a fully imputed data vector. At each iteration, the velocity field is conditioned on the observed subvector, the mask, and a continuous time index τ∈[0,1], and only updates the missing components (gated by the (1−m) mask). The method operates on both tabular data (8–128 features across UCI benchmarks) and RGB images (32×32 CIFAR-10, 64×64 CelebA), with missingness simulated under MCAR, MAR, and MNAR mechanisms at 20%–80% rates.

## Method

MIRI is an iterative algorithm that, at each step t, minimizes the KL divergence D[P_{X^{(t)}(g),M} ‖ P_{X^{(t-1)}} ⊗ P_M], which provably decreases the mutual information I(X;M) monotonically. Proposition 2 characterizes the optimal imputer: it must sample the missing components from the conditional q(x_{1-m}|x_m) of the previous iteration's data marginal. To realize this imputer, the paper trains a rectified flow velocity field v* by minimizing a least-squares objective over linear interpolations between paired samples from the joint (reference) and product (target) measures, with the velocity conditioned on observed components and the mask. The imputation is then obtained by solving the ODE dZ_τ/dτ = (1−m) ⊙ v*(Z_τ, [0,x_m], [0,x_m], m, τ) from τ=0 to τ=1, where the (1−m) gate ensures only missing entries are updated. This avoids adversarial training entirely, uses a simple MSE loss for the velocity field, and naturally parallelizes across all missing dimensions simultaneously.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (32×32 RGB, 5000 samples), MIRI achieves FID of 6.01, 27.53, and 68.58 at 20%, 40%, and 60% missingness respectively, outperforming the strongest baseline HyperImpute (8.92, 65.01, 130.36) at all three levels. On 10 UCI regression benchmarks at 40% missingness, MIRI attains the best mean MMD rank under MCAR (1.4) and MNAR (1.3), and is competitive under MAR (2.0 vs. HyperImpute's 1.5). On a synthetic bimodal Gaussian toy example at 30% MCAR, MIRI reduces MMD to 0.036, a >60% improvement over HyperImpute (0.094), while incurring only a modest increase in point-wise error (RMSE 0.938 vs. 0.862).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MIRI consistently outperforms all generative baselines (GAIN, KnewImp, MissDiff, TabCSDI) by a wide margin on both distributional fidelity (MMD, FID) and downstream classification accuracy. Against the strongest round-robin baseline HyperImpute, MIRI wins on FID at all three CIFAR-10 missingness levels (e.g., 68.58 vs. 130.36 at 60%) and on classification accuracy at 40% and 60% (0.525 vs. 0.405; 0.364 vs. 0.212), but HyperImpute retains a slight edge on point-wise error (RMSE 0.862 vs. 0.938) and on UCI MAR ranking (1.5 vs. 2.0). On the synthetic toy example, MIRI reduces MMD by over 60% relative to HyperImpute (0.036 vs. 0.094), confirming superior distributional recovery despite a ~9% RMSE penalty. Against diffusion-based methods (MissDiff, TabCSDI) and VAE-based MIWAE, MIRI dominates on MMD across all UCI settings and missingness rates. The paper also shows MIRI is substantially faster than HyperImpute in high dimensions (1.30 h vs. 57.54 h at 5000d for 1000 samples) and on par with other flow/diffusion methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper formalizes and extends the observation from Liu et al. (ICML 2024) that GAIN's adversarial training implicitly reduces mutual information between imputed data and the missingness mask, correcting their Wasserstein Gradient Flow approach which does not correctly minimize the MI objective. It draws on rectified flow (Liu et al., ICLR 2023) to construct the optimal imputer as an ODE solution, avoiding the instability of GAN training and the architectural constraints of normalizing flows. A key conceptual contribution is showing that GAIN, MICE, HyperImpute, and other round-robin or inpainting-based methods are all approximate special cases of the unified MIRI framework, with the approximation arising from pseudo-likelihood factorization or imperfect conditional sampling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The MIRI framework provides a rigorous theoretical lens for understanding why iterative imputation works, unifying a fragmented literature under a single KL-divergence-minimization principle. Practically, the rectified-flow-based imputer offers a stable, parallelizable alternative to both adversarial training and sequential Gibbs sampling, with competitive distributional fidelity (MMD) on tabular and image benchmarks. Key open questions include extending the population-level theory to finite-sample and approximate ODE-solver settings, handling MNAR missingness where the mask depends on unobserved values, and reducing the per-iteration computational cost of training a full velocity field. The trade-off between distributional fidelity and point-wise accuracy (MIRI wins on MMD but loses on RMSE/MAE to HyperImpute) remains an important design consideration for practitioners.
