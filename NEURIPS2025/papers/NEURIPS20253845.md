## Problem

Estimating interlayer (cross-modality) edges in multilayer networks is difficult when layers have different numbers of variables and different sample sizes, as is typical in multimodal neuroimaging (e.g., MEG, fMRI, sMRI). Existing methods either require equal sample sizes across layers or identical node sets, leaving a gap for flexible, scalable estimation of the full joint dependence structure in heterogeneous multilayer systems.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253845/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes per-layer sample covariance matrices computed from i.i.d. multivariate Gaussian draws, where each layer α can have a distinct number of variables p_α and a distinct sample size n_α. It produces (i) a sparse intralayer precision matrix Ŝ_α for each layer capturing local, modality-specific edges, and (ii) a single low-rank joint latent supra-adjacency matrix L̂ of dimension p×p (where p is the total number of nodes across all layers) whose off-diagonal blocks encode the interlayer edges. Because the method operates on precision matrices rather than raw data vectors, no up- or down-sampling between modalities is required.

## Method

multiSLICE decomposes each layer's precision matrix as Σ_α^{-1} = S_α + P_Ωα(L), where S_α is a sparse, layer-specific component and P_Ωα(L) is the projection of a shared low-rank latent matrix L into the observed node set of layer α. The estimation proceeds in two stages: first, a per-layer SLICE algorithm alternates between a rank-r truncated SVD (for the latent part) and a GLASSO step (for the sparse part) to obtain Ŝ_α and L̂_α independently; second, a block-SVD matrix completion step (Bishop and Yu, 2014) reconstructs the full joint L̂ from the per-layer latent estimates by intersecting their latent subspaces and assembling all pairwise blocks L_ij = U_i Λ_i^{1/2} Λ_j^{1/2} U_j^T. This two-stage design is what allows different p_α and n_α across layers while sharing a common latent structure. The paper establishes consistency, model-selection consistency, and explicit rate bounds (‖L̂ − L*‖_∞ ≲ Σ_α C√(log p_α / n_α)) under standard irrepresentability and minimum-signal-strength conditions.

## Results

On the Wakeman & Henson (2015) multimodal neuroimaging dataset (16 subjects, 3 face stimuli), multiSLICE achieves the highest sparse-graph modularity Q(Ŝ) of 0.170–0.171 and the lowest multilayer von Neumann entropy H(L̂) of 0.626–0.660 across all three conditions, outperforming the next-best method (LRGQ) by roughly 30–50% in Q and by a factor of ~2 in H. In simulations, multiSLICE attains the best F1 score for sparse structure recovery and is the only method whose estimated latent subspace converges to the true L* (measured by sin θ of the leading eigenvector) as sample size increases. The method's performance is robust to the number of layers (l = 2–5) but more sensitive to the latent rank R(L*).

## Baselines & Comparisons

multiSLICE outperforms all four baselines that appear in the neuroimaging comparison (Table 2) on both metrics. LRGQ (Chang et al., 2022) is the closest competitor, achieving Q(Ŝ) ≈ 0.112–0.130 and H(L̂) ≈ 1.28–1.29, but multiSLICE improves Q by ~30–50% and halves H, indicating a substantially more structured latent network. CNJGL (Mohan et al., 2014) and BJEMGM (Gan et al., 2019) report only Q(Ŝ) (no latent estimate), with values of 0.104–0.107 and 0.079–0.084 respectively, both well below multiSLICE. In the simulation studies, MLGGM (Lin et al., 2016) is the second-best method when varying the number of layers (Simulation 1), while coglasso (Albanese et al., 2024) is second-best when varying the latent rank (Simulation 2); however, neither converges to the true L* as n grows, unlike multiSLICE. MLGGM, BANS, JMMLE, and coglasso are excluded from the neuroimaging table because they require equal sample sizes across layers, an assumption violated by the dataset.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate lines of work: latent variable Gaussian graphical models (Chandrasekaran et al., 2012), which decompose precision matrices into sparse and low-rank components, and the multilayer network framework (Kivelä et al., 2014), which formalizes 'networks of networks' with intra- and interlayer edges. Prior multilayer GGM methods (CNJGL, MLGGM, BJEMGM, CFR, BANS, JMMLE, LRGQ, coglasso) handle either differing sample sizes or differing node sets across layers, but not both simultaneously. multiSLICE departs from this by applying per-layer sparse+low-rank inverse covariance estimation (SLICE) followed by block-SVD matrix completion to recover a joint latent supra-adjacency matrix, explicitly accommodating heterogeneous p and n across modalities without up- or down-sampling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method addresses a practical gap in multimodal neuroimaging (M/EEG, fMRI, sMRI) where modalities inherently differ in spatial/temporal resolution and sample count, and provides the first theoretical recovery guarantees (consistency, rate bounds) for joint latent space estimation under these heterogeneous conditions. Key limitations include the assumption of independent modality measurements, i.i.d. Gaussian draws, and the untested applicability beyond neuroimaging. Open questions include extending to time-varying or longitudinal settings, non-Gaussian data, and integration into differentiable deep-learning pipelines (e.g., as a graph-construction module for GNNs).
