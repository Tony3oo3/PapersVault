## Problem

Scaling deep RL networks typically degrades performance, yet the root cause has remained poorly understood. This paper identifies that the interaction between inherent non-stationarity (policy-dependent data, bootstrapped targets) and gradient pathologies (vanishing gradients, dormant neurons, flat loss curvature) amplified by depth and width is the primary mechanism behind this failure, rather than algorithmic instability or insufficient exploration alone.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251177/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method is applied as a drop-in modification to existing RL agents, taking standard environment observations (84×84 RGB frames for Atari/ALE, or continuous state vectors for Isaac Gym and DMC) and producing the same outputs as the base algorithm (Q-values, policy probabilities, or action distributions). The interventions operate on the MLP head following a fixed convolutional encoder, so the observation preprocessing pipeline is unchanged; what changes is how gradients flow through the dense layers and how parameter updates are preconditioned.

## Method

The paper proposes two complementary gradient-stabilizing interventions. First, a multi-skip residual architecture broadcasts the flattened convolutional features directly to every subsequent MLP layer (rather than only to adjacent layers as in standard ResNet), creating unobstructed gradient paths from any depth back to the shared encoder. Second, the Kronecker-factored approximate curvature (Kron) optimizer replaces first-order optimizers like RAdam or Adam, using low-rank Kronecker products to approximate the Fisher Information Matrix and apply directionally-aware preconditioning that captures inter-parameter dependencies. The key insight is that under non-stationarity, gradient signals progressively degrade across depth, and both architectural shortcuts and curvature-aware updates are needed to preserve them; neither intervention alone is sufficient. The combined approach is validated across PQN, PPO, DQN, Rainbow, SAC, and DDPG on Atari-10, full ALE, Isaac Gym, DMC, and offline RL benchmarks, achieving a median 83.27% relative improvement over baseline PQN on the full ALE suite.

## Results

On the full ALE suite (57 games, 200M frames, 3 seeds), PQN augmented with multi-skip residuals and the Kron optimizer outperforms the baseline in 90% of environments, achieving a median relative improvement of 83.27%. On Atari-10, the combined interventions maintain high human-normalized scores (HNS) across all depths and widths, whereas the baseline collapses at greater scales. For PPO on the full ALE, the same interventions yield a median relative improvement of 31.40% and outperform the baseline in 83.64% of games. In continuous control (Isaac Gym Cartpole and Anymal), only the augmented PPO remains stable as model size increases, while the baseline degrades.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's combined method (multi-skip residuals + Kron optimizer) decisively outperforms all single alternative interventions tested on Atari-10: no alternative (Cosine LR, Cyclic LR, GELU, CReLU, L2 Norm, Weight Clip, Weight Decay, or LR scaling) approaches the proposed method's aggregate HNS of 0.58, with most alternatives scoring near zero or negative. Among second-order and adaptive optimizers specifically, Shampoo and Apollo both fail to achieve strong performance in online deep RL despite extensive hyperparameter tuning, while AdaBelief shows some robustness at shallow depths but degrades at scale. Against the offline Q-learning baselines from Park et al. (SHARSA, DSHARSA), the gradient interventions provide a complementary path to scalability: on the sparse-reward humanoidmaze-giant-navigate task, augmented methods reach near-optimal performance where their unaugmented counterparts largely fail, and performance remains consistent when generalizing from puzzle-4x5-play to the harder puzzle-4x6-play. The paper also demonstrates that the interventions generalize across algorithms (PQN, PPO, SAC, DDPG) and encoders (standard CNN, Impala CNN), with the Kron optimizer specifically reversing the performance degradation that AdamW exhibits as Simba networks scale in depth and width on DMC tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of gradient propagation theory (developed primarily for supervised learning) and the non-stationary optimization challenges unique to deep RL. It builds directly on PQN as a base algorithm, extends standard residual connections (He et al., 2016) into multi-skip residuals that broadcast encoder features to all MLP layers, and adopts Kronecker-factored curvature (Martens and Grosse, 2015) as an optimizer. Conceptually, it departs from prior scaling work (Simba, BRO, MoE-based approaches) that addressed capacity underutilization through regularization or architectural tricks, by identifying the underlying mechanism—gradient signal degradation amplified by non-stationarity—as the root cause, and targeting it directly through architectural and curvature-aware interventions without modifying the RL algorithm itself.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clear diagnostic framework (dormant neurons, SRank, Hessian trace, gradient norms) that explains *why* scaling fails in deep RL, moving the field beyond trial-and-error architectural search. The interventions are simple, composable, and validated across six algorithms and four environment suites, making them immediately actionable for practitioners attempting to scale RL networks. Key limitations include the computational overhead of K-FAC (up to ~6× slower than Adam at large scales), the restriction to MLP scaling (convolutional backbones are held fixed), and the fact that Shampoo and Apollo—state-of-the-art optimizers for large supervised models—failed in the RL setting, leaving open the question of what properties an optimizer needs to succeed under non-stationarity. Whether these findings extend to substantially larger networks (beyond ~80M parameters) or to transformer-based RL architectures remains unexplored.
