## Problem

Existing diffusion-based combinatorial optimization solvers produce opaque intermediate states during inference, offering no insight into how the final solution is progressively formed. Autoregressive solvers provide step-wise interpretability but suffer from sequential computational overhead, while non-autoregressive generative models offer efficient global modeling but lack transparency in the solution trajectory. StruDiCO bridges this gap by designing a diffusion process whose intermediate states are structurally valid partial solutions, enabling both interpretability and efficiency.

## Input / Output

The method takes a graph instance as input: for TSP, N 2-D node coordinates with a weighted adjacency matrix (sparsified to k nearest neighbors for large instances); for MIS, an undirected graph with edge probability p. The output is a binary vector x ∈ {0,1}^N representing the selected edges (TSP) or nodes (MIS), decoded via greedy insertion to enforce hard feasibility constraints (no subtours, no adjacent nodes). A key structural property is that every intermediate state x_τ during reverse inference is a valid subset of the final solution, forming a monotonically growing partial-solution trajectory rather than a noisy full-graph state.

## Method

StruDiCO replaces the standard symmetric discrete diffusion transition with a variable-absorption noising model using an asymmetric matrix Q_t = [[1,0],[β_t, 1−β_t]], which allows variables to transition only from selected (1) to unselected (0), ensuring every intermediate state is a feasible partial solution. During reverse inference, a constrained consistency sampling strategy applies a hard threshold δ to the predicted heatmap, masking low-confidence variables to reduce the effective search space and stabilize the trajectory (formally shown to reduce reverse KL divergence via Theorem 3.1). For refinement, instead of the gradient-based search used by T2T/Fast T2T, StruDiCO introduces a fully forward-pass, objective-aware scoring scheme score_i = p_i/(φ_i+ε) where φ_i encodes task-specific penalties (edge distance for TSP, neighbor conflict potential for MIS), combined with iterative structure-aware perturbation via variable-absorption noise. This eliminates all backward gradient computation, reducing time complexity from O(3BLN²d) to O(BLN²d) and memory from O(2BLN²d) to O(BLN²d) relative to Fast T2T.

## Results

On TSP-1000 with guided refinement (T_s=5, T_g=5, SL+G+2Opt), StruDiCO achieves a 0.535% drop, outperforming Fast T2T (0.603%) and T2T (1.209%) while running 2.2× faster than Fast T2T (2.614s vs 5.779s) and 5× faster than T2T (2.614s vs 13.089s). On MIS-ER (700–800 nodes) with the same refinement setup, StruDiCO reduces the drop to 6.324% under greedy decoding and 3.789% under 4× sampling, compared to 9.313%/7.378% for Fast T2T and 11.419%/7.899% for T2T, while being 2.2× faster than Fast T2T. Across all settings, StruDiCO uses 30–70% less GPU memory than Fast T2T and achieves up to 37.7% drop reduction on TSP and an average 38.1% improvement on MIS over prior diffusion-based solvers.

## Baselines & Comparisons

StruDiCO consistently outperforms the closest diffusion-based competitor Fast T2T [24] on both TSP and MIS: on TSP-1000 (SL+G+2Opt, T_s=5, T_g=5), StruDiCO achieves 0.535% drop vs Fast T2T's 0.603% while being 2.2× faster (2.614s vs 5.779s); on MIS-ER (SL+S, T_s=5, T_g=5), StruDiCO achieves 3.789% drop vs Fast T2T's 7.378% (a 49% relative reduction) at 2.2× lower runtime. Against T2T [23], StruDiCO is 5× faster on TSP-1000 (2.614s vs 13.089s) with comparable or better drop (0.535% vs 1.209%), and 11× faster on MIS-ER (1.988s vs 22.426s) with substantially better drop (3.789% vs 7.899%). Compared to DIFUSCO [22], StruDiCO reduces the TSP-1000 drop from 1.936% to 0.535% (72% relative improvement) with 2× lower runtime, and the MIS-ER drop from 18.462% to 6.324% (66% relative improvement) with 9× lower runtime. On exact/heuristic baselines (Concorde, LKH-3, KaMIS, Gurobi), StruDiCO remains within 0.5–6% of optimal, matching or approaching their solution quality at 10–100× lower inference time. On CVRP (Appendix D.1), StruDiCO outperforms COExpander [47] and Sym-NCO [16] on all three scales (CVRP-50/100/200) in both drop and runtime, e.g., 0.85% drop vs 3.90% for COExpander on CVRP-50 at 0.07s vs 0.04s.

## Relation to Prior Work

StruDiCO extends the discrete diffusion + consistency model lineage for combinatorial optimization established by DIFUSCO, T2T, and Fast T2T. Its two key departures are: (1) replacing the symmetric uniform noise transition with an asymmetric variable-absorption process that enforces monotonic variable deactivation, making every intermediate state a structurally valid partial solution; and (2) replacing T2T/Fast T2T's gradient-guided refinement with a gradient-free, objective-aware scoring scheme that integrates task penalties directly into greedy decoding. The constrained consistency sampling (threshold-based masking) is a new component that leverages the structural coherence of the absorption trajectory to stabilize reverse inference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in diffusion-based CO solvers: gradient-based refinement requires repeated forward-backward passes, inflating both time and memory. By eliminating backward computation entirely while matching or exceeding solution quality, StruDiCO makes diffusion-based CO solvers substantially more deployable (3.5× faster, 70% less GPU memory). The structural interpretability—where each intermediate state is a valid partial solution—bridges the long-standing tradeoff between autoregressive interpretability and non-autoregressive efficiency. Open questions include whether the step-wise greedy decoding variant (discussed in Appendix B) can enforce strict per-step feasibility, and whether the supervised training requirement limits applicability to domains where high-quality labels are scarce.
