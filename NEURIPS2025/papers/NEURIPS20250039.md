## Problem

Existing LLM reasoning optimization methods (PPO, DPO, and variants) treat reasoning trajectories as monolithic units, optimizing over the full sequence without identifying which intermediate steps are truly pivotal for success. Subtle errors at specific reasoning steps can cascade into complete failure, yet current fine-tuning strategies lack a mechanism to pinpoint and prioritize learning from these critical decision points within a trajectory.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250039/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a reasoning problem prompt, a multi-step reasoning trajectory segmented by newlines (where each newline-delimited segment is one 'step' rather than a single token), and a gold answer for reward computation. It produces an updated policy model and new reasoning trajectories that continue from the identified critical step. The critical step is located via Monte Carlo estimation of the per-step advantage function (4 MC samples per step in the main experiments), and the reset trajectory retains the prefix up to the critical step while regenerating all subsequent steps.

## Method

GPO identifies the most critical reasoning step in a trajectory by computing the advantage function A^π(x, y_{0:i-1}; y_i) = Q^π(x, y_{0:i-1}; y_i) − Q^π(x, y_{0:i-2}; y_{i-1}) at each step, where Q is estimated via Monte Carlo sampling of future continuations under the current policy. It then resets the policy to that critical step and generates new rollouts conditioned on the prefix up to that point, directing the learning signal specifically toward the pivotal moment. These reset trajectories feed into either an online PPO update (Procedure-I) or an offline preference optimization loss such as DPO, KTO, SimPO, or ORPO (Procedure-II), making GPO a general wrapper composable with existing optimizers. Theoretically, the advantage-weighted sampling reduces the regret bound in the online setting (Theorem 5.2) and the per-step preference formulation is shown to be equivalent to advantage-weighted RL (Theorem 5.3), providing formal justification for why focusing on critical steps yields more efficient policy improvement than uniform trajectory-level optimization.

## Results

Across 7 reasoning benchmarks (BBH, MATH, GSM8K, MMLU, MMLU-Pro, AIME-2024, AIME-2025), GPO-enhanced methods consistently outperform their unmodified baselines. The largest gains appear on MATH: GPO-PPO reaches 87.80% accuracy vs. 79.60% for standard PPO (+8.2 pp), and GPO-DPO reaches 86.80% vs. 82.40% for DPO (+4.4 pp). On the hardest benchmark AIME-2024, GPO-DPO improves from 20.00% to 26.67% (+6.67 pp) and GPO-PPO from 26.67% to 30.00% (+3.33 pp). GPO-KTO achieves the best overall MMLU score at 61.35% (vs. 59.42% for KTO) and the best GSM8K score at 90.25% (vs. 89.31% for KTO).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPO wins against all five fine-tuning baselines (PPO, DPO, KTO, SimPO, ORPO) on every one of the seven evaluated datasets, confirming its generalizability as a plug-in strategy. The most substantial gains are with PPO on MATH (+8.2 pp to 87.80%) and with DPO on AIME-2024 (+6.67 pp to 26.67%), suggesting GPO is most impactful when the base optimizer struggles with multi-step credit assignment. Against KTO, GPO-KTO leads on 6 of 7 datasets, with the largest margin on MMLU (+1.93 pp) and AIME-2025 (+6.67 pp). GPO-SimPO and GPO-ORPO show more modest but still consistent improvements (typically +1 to +3 pp), indicating that even weaker preference-based optimizers benefit from critical-step-focused data. In the ablation against Satori's random-reset strategy, GPO-PPO achieves 87.9% on MATH versus 79.9% for random reset, demonstrating that advantage-based step identification is the key driver rather than the reset mechanism alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

GPO builds directly on Satori's trajectory-reset mechanism for LLM self-improvement, but replaces Satori's random step selection with advantage-function-based identification of the single most critical reasoning step. The optimization backbones are standard PPO (online) and DPO/KTO/SimPO/ORPO (offline preference), so GPO is positioned as a general data-generation and sampling strategy that plugs into existing fine-tuning pipelines rather than a new optimizer. Conceptually, the critical-step identification draws from model-free Explainable RL (value-function-based step importance), adapted to the LLM setting where each reasoning segment (not token) constitutes an action in an MDP.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The core practical takeaway is that *where* you reset a trajectory matters as much as *that* you reset it: the ablation showing GPO outperforming Satori's random reset by ~8 points on MATH validates that advantage-guided step selection provides a meaningfully stronger training signal. The method's generality across five optimization algorithms and seven benchmarks makes it a drop-in improvement for existing reasoning fine-tuning pipelines. Key limitations include the ~1.8–1.9× computational overhead from Monte Carlo advantage estimation, and the open question of whether more sample-efficient estimators (e.g., GAE) or model-based explainability techniques could reduce this cost while preserving the benefit. Developing automated benchmarks for critical-step quality (beyond downstream accuracy) is identified as an important gap for the field.
