## Problem

Existing federated graph learning methods assume static, shared graphs across clients, while continual graph learning approaches operate on single-client static or locally evolving graphs. Neither paradigm addresses the combined challenge of preserving graph-topological integrity across incremental task transitions AND resolving parameter conflicts during server-side aggregation when clients hold heterogeneous, evolving graph views under strict storage constraints.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252363/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each client k at task t provides a local evolving graph G_k^t with node feature vectors x_i^{k,t}, an adjacency matrix A_k^t, and a task identifier; only current-task data is accessible, and a bounded replay memory (|M_k^t| ≤ B) constrains how much historical graph structure can be retained. The system produces per-node class labels via a GAT backbone and, at the server level, an updated global parameter vector φ that must simultaneously encode knowledge from all T sequential tasks without catastrophic forgetting.

## Method

MOTION couples two modules. On the client side, G-TMSC performs multi-sculpt coarsening: it computes a rich set of topological features (degree/betweenness/eigenvector centrality, PageRank, clustering coefficient, MMD, Mahalanobis distance, cosine similarity) and uses a Mixture-of-Experts sparse-activation mechanism to select top-K experts whose normalized importance scores guide which nodes to merge or prune, then fuses merged node features by arithmetic averaging and maintains dual many-to-one / one-to-one mappings for traceability. On the server side, G-EPAE constructs a per-client graph compatibility matrix M_k = Δθ_dir^k ∘ Δθ_mag^k (Hadamard product of L2-normalized update direction and relative magnitude), then applies an elastic regulation fusion that exponentially amplifies high-compatibility increments (M_k ≥ τ) and linearly decays low-compatibility ones (γ·M_k), with an adaptive L2 penalty selectively truncating gradients of poorly aligned parameters before the weighted sum updates the global model.

## Results

MOTION achieves the highest Average Accuracy (AA) on all five datasets: 62.66% (Cora), 49.08% (CiteSeer), 59.48% (PubMed), 79.09% (Amazon-Photo), and 22.26% (CoAuthor-CS), improving AA by an average of ~33.7 percentage points over the FedAvg baseline. Critically, MOTION is the only method that achieves negative Average Forgetting (AF) on every dataset (ranging from −5.32% to −24.17%), indicating that prior-task knowledge is not merely preserved but actively reinforced over the task sequence. On Amazon-Photo, MOTION outperforms the second-best baseline (FedTpp) by 49.34% in AA while reducing AF from 38.22% to −5.32%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MOTION dominates all eight baselines on both AA and AF across every dataset. Against the strongest CGL competitor, FedTpp, MOTION gains +9.03% AA on Cora (62.66 vs. 53.63), +21.41% on CiteSeer (49.08 vs. 27.67), +10.46% on PubMed (59.48 vs. 49.02), +49.34% on Amazon-Photo (79.09 vs. 29.75), and +3.11% on CoAuthor-CS (22.26 vs. 19.15), while simultaneously flipping AF from positive (19.88–58.28%) to negative (−8.34 to −11.62%). The FCL baseline FedPowde trails MOTION by 43.61% AA on Cora and 56.36% on Amazon-Photo, and exhibits severe forgetting (AF up to 71.32%). Traditional FL methods (FedAvg, FedDC, FedDyn) and FGL methods (FedSSL, FedSSP) are far behind, with AA typically below 39% and AF above 14%, confirming that neither standard federated aggregation nor graph-specific federated learning alone addresses the dual challenges of topology preservation and parameter conflict resolution in the FCGL setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by formally defining Federated Continual Graph Learning (FCGL) as the intersection of three previously separate threads: federated graph learning (FedSSL, FedSSP), continual graph learning (SEA-ER, FedTPP), and federated continual learning (FedPowde). Rather than building on a single prior method, it repurposes graph coarsening (from graph compression) and Mixture-of-Experts scoring (from NLP/ML) into a client-side topology-preserving replay mechanism (G-TMSC), and introduces a topology-sensitive compatibility matrix (G-EPAE) for server-side aggregation that departs from standard FedAvg-style weighted averaging. The paper explicitly positions prior FGL methods as assuming shared static graphs and prior CGL methods as single-client, leaving the combined decentralized-incremental setting unaddressed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FCGL is a practically motivated formulation for edge-device and privacy-constrained settings where graphs evolve over time and no single client holds the full history; formalizing it opens a new research direction. However, the experimental validation is notably constrained—only two clients, a single communication round, and Dirichlet α=5.0—making it unclear whether the reported 30%+ gains over FedAvg and negative AF values hold under realistic multi-round, many-client, or adversarial conditions. Open questions include scalability to large client populations, robustness to abrupt topological shifts, defense against malicious clients, and whether the multi-expert coarsening overhead is acceptable on resource-limited edge devices.
