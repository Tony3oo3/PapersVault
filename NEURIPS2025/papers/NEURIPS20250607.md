## Problem

Prior algorithmic stability-based high-probability excess risk bounds for strongly convex or PL-conditioned learners achieve at most O(log(n)/n) (Klochkov & Zhivotovskiy, 2021), and it was an open question whether stability-based techniques could yield bounds better than O(1/n). This paper answers affirmatively, establishing the first dimension-free high-probability excess risk bounds of rate O(log²(n)/n²) via a novel gradient-stability analysis.

## Input / Output

The framework takes a training set S of n i.i.d. samples, a learning algorithm (ERM, PGD, or SGD), and a loss function satisfying M-Lipschitz continuity, γ-smoothness, and the Polyak-Lojasiewicz condition with parameter μ. The output is a high-probability excess risk bound whose sample complexity is dimension-free (requiring only n = Ω(γ²/μ²) rather than Ω(γ²d/μ²)), and whose leading term depends on the squared gradient norm at the trained parameters E_Z[‖∇f(A(S);Z)‖²]—a quantity that vanishes as optimization converges—rather than the worst-case Lipschitz constant M. This makes the bound adaptive to the optimization quality of the algorithm.

## Method

The core technique generalizes classical uniform stability from the loss function to its gradient, termed 'uniform stability in gradients' (Definition 2), which measures the expected squared change in per-sample gradients when one training point is replaced. Under the PL condition, the population loss is upper-bounded by the squared gradient norm, so a generalization bound on the gradient gap ‖∇F(A(S)) − ∇F_S(A(S))‖₂ directly yields a bound on the excess risk. The key insight is that the gradient generalization bound (Theorem 2) contains the term E_Z[‖∇f(A(S);Z)‖²] in place of the constant M², which is small for well-optimized models, converting the usual O(M²/n) sampling term into O(1/n²). Supporting this, the paper derives optimal constants in the Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund inequality for Hilbert-space-valued random variables (via sharp Khintchine-Kahane constants and Stirling's formula) and a refined McDiarmid inequality for vector-valued functions, yielding constant-level improvements over prior moment bounds. The framework is algorithm-agnostic: stability parameters β for ERM, PGD, and SGD are computed separately and plugged into the general theorem to produce the final O(log²(n)/n²) excess risk guarantees.

## Results

The paper establishes the first dimension-free high-probability excess risk bounds of rate $\tilde{O}(1/n^2)$ for ERM, PGD, and SGD under the PL condition, smoothness, and Lipschitz continuity, improving upon the previous best algorithmic-stability-based rate of $\tilde{O}(1/n)$. For ERM and PGD, the bound is $\tilde{O}(1/n^2)$ with only $n = \Omega(\gamma^2/\mu^2)$ samples (independent of dimension $d$), while for SGD the same rate requires $T \asymp n^2$ iterations. Under the one-pass SGD regime ($T \asymp n$), the paper still achieves $\tilde{O}(1/n)$, which is the sharpest known high-probability bound under that iteration constraint. The core technical contribution is a sharper gradient generalization bound (Theorem 2) that replaces the $O(M/\sqrt{n})$ sampling term with a data-dependent $O(\sqrt{\mathbb{E}[\|\nabla f(A(S);Z)\|^2]/n})$ term, enabling the $1/n^2$ rate.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Zhang et al. [55] and Xu & Zeevi [50], which achieve the same $\tilde{O}(1/n^2)$ rate via uniform convergence, this paper matches the rate but eliminates the dimension dependence: their sample complexity requires $n = \Omega(\gamma^2 d/\mu^2)$, while this work only needs $n = \Omega(\gamma^2/\mu^2)$, making the bounds applicable in high-dimensional settings. Against Klochkov & Zhivotovskiy [20], the closest algorithmic-stability baseline, this paper strictly improves the rate from $\tilde{O}(1/n)$ to $\tilde{O}(1/n^2)$ for ERM and PGD under the same smoothness and PL assumptions, by exploiting gradient stability rather than loss stability. Against Li & Liu [29], which achieves $\tilde{O}(1/n^2)$ for SGD via uniform convergence with $T \asymp n^2$ and dimension-dependent sample size, this paper matches the rate and iteration count while being dimension-free; additionally, under the one-pass regime $T \asymp n$, this paper achieves $\tilde{O}(1/n)$ in high probability, whereas [29] requires $T \asymp n^2$ for its best rate. The paper also improves upon Fan & Lei [10] (Theorem 1) with a constant-level tightening of the gradient generalization bound via a sharper Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund inequality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the algorithmic stability framework of Klochkov & Zhivotovskiy (2021), who achieved O(log(n)/n) high-probability excess risk bounds for strongly convex learners, by shifting the stability analysis from the loss function to its gradient (uniform stability in gradients, originally introduced by Lei 2023 and Fan & Lei 2024). The key technical departure is a sharper moment bound for sums of vector-valued functions (Lemma 7) that exploits optimal Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund constants in Hilbert space, replacing the coarser real-valued concentration used in prior stability analyses. Combined with the PL condition and smoothness, this allows the gradient generalization gap to be converted into a loss-level excess risk bound at rate O(log²(n)/n²), improving upon the O(log(n)/n) ceiling of all prior stability-based results. The work also addresses an open problem from Xu & Zeevi (2024) by providing dimension-free gradient generalization bounds, in contrast to uniform convergence approaches that require n = Ω(γ²d/μ²).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The O(log²(n)/n²) rate is the first dimension-free high-probability excess risk bound achievable via algorithmic stability, matching the asymptotic minimax rate from classical statistics (Hajek-LeCam) in finite-sample form. This is practically relevant because it shows that under standard optimization assumptions (PL, smoothness, low-noise), stable algorithms like ERM, PGD, and SGD can provably achieve near-optimal generalization without dimension-dependent sample requirements. However, the results require the low-noise condition F(w*) = O(1/n) and the PL condition on the population risk, which may not hold in many deep learning settings; extending the framework to purely nonconvex objectives without PL remains open. The gradient-stability lens also suggests that prior stability-based results (e.g., for differentially private models or pairwise learning) that relied on Klochkov & Zhivotovskiy's loss-level analysis may be improvable.
