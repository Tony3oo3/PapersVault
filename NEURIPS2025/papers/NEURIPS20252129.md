## Problem

Existing test-time adaptation (TTA) methods assume each domain appears only once in the test stream, but real-world deployments involve recurring and gradually evolving domain shifts. Single-model TTA approaches suffer from catastrophic forgetting, inter-domain interference, and error accumulation when previously seen domains reappear, while persistent TTA methods that constrain parameter drift exhibit limited re-adaptation capability. The paper addresses how to maintain stable, domain-specific adaptation over prolonged, non-stationary test distributions where domains both recur and evolve incrementally.

## Input / Output

At test time, the method consumes sequential batches of unlabeled RGB images and computes style descriptors as channel-wise log-variances from the first L layers of a frozen VGG-19 network. Each batch is routed to a domain-specialized model via online clustering over these style vectors, and only the selected model receives TTA gradient updates. Final predictions are produced by ensembling the trainable parameters of all reservoir models weighted by soft-assignment scores, yielding class labels or pixel-wise segmentation masks without ever updating the ensemble parameters themselves.

## Method

ReservoirTTA maintains a pool of up to K^max domain-specialized models, each storing only trainable parameters (batch-norm affine weights, LoRA adapters, or visual prompts). A DP-Means-inspired online clustering algorithm over VGG-19 style features detects new domains and recognizes recurring ones, with centroids refined via a mutual information loss over a fixed-size Style Reservoir maintained by reservoir sampling. When a new domain is detected, a new model is initialized by cloning the reservoir model that yields the most confident and diverse predictions (minimizing mutual information of softmax outputs). Only the model with the highest soft-assignment weight for the current batch is adapted using a plug-in TTA objective (EATA, ETA, TENT, CoTTA*, etc.), while all other models remain frozen. The theoretical analysis proves that unregularized SGD-based TTA causes parameter variance to grow linearly with adaptation steps, and that weight ensembling or Fisher regularization bounds this variance geometrically—motivating the decoupled multi-model design that isolates each domain's adaptation trajectory within its own stability region.

## Results

On the primary Recurring CSC setting (20 domain recurrences), ReservoirTTA plugged into ETA reduces CIFAR-10-C error from 30.9% to 16.4% at visit 20 (vs. 43.5% source), and CIFAR-100-C error from 37.6% to 30.0%. On ImageNet-C, EATA+ReservoirTTA achieves 51.0% error at visit 20 (vs. 55.9% for EATA alone and 82.0% source), while ROID*+ReservoirTTA reaches 52.1%. For segmentation on Cityscapes→ACDC, CoTTA*+ReservoirTTA maintains 60.0% mIoU on Snow at visit 10 (vs. 59.8% for CoTTA* alone), and TENT+ReservoirTTA gains +2.6 points over TENT on Snow (54.7% vs. 52.0%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReservoirTTA consistently outperforms all 11 baselines across every dataset and setting. Against single-target TENT, the improvement is dramatic: on CIFAR-10-C (CSC), TENT degrades to 87.8% error by visit 20 while TENT+ReservoirTTA holds at 17.6%. Among continual TTA methods, ETA+ReservoirTTA reduces CIFAR-10-C error from 30.9% to 16.4% and CIFAR-100-C from 37.6% to 30.0%, while SAR+ReservoirTTA cuts CIFAR-100-C error from 60.4% to 30.5% and ImageNet-C from 67.1% to 53.1%. For persistent TTA, EATA+ReservoirTTA improves ImageNet-C from 55.9% to 51.0% and ROID*+ReservoirTTA from 55.5% to 52.1%, showing that even well-regularized single-model methods still suffer re-adaptation gaps that the reservoir resolves. On the ViT-B/16 ImageNet-C benchmark, ReservoirTTA beats both CoLA (33.8%→31.9% on CSC) and DPCore (43.1%→39.2% on CCC), with fewer trainable parameters (0.705% vs. 6.241% for CoLA). For segmentation, CoTTA*+ReservoirTTA and LoRA+ReservoirTTA both surpass BECoTTA on all four ACDC weather conditions, with the largest gains on Snow (+0.9 mIoU at visit 10).

## Relation to Prior Work

ReservoirTTA extends the persistent/robust TTA line (EATA, ROID, RDumb, PeTTA) that introduced variance-control mechanisms—Fisher regularization, weight ensembling, sample filtering—to prevent model collapse during prolonged adaptation. Its key departure is architectural: rather than forcing a single model to track all domains sequentially (as in continual TTA methods like CoTTA, RoTTA, ETA, SAR), it maintains a reservoir of domain-specialized models and routes each test batch to the appropriate specialist via online style-based clustering (inspired by DP-Means). The theoretical analysis unifies Fisher regularization and weight ensembling as equivalent variance-bounding strategies, providing a principled justification for why single-model TTA drifts under recurring shifts, and positions the multi-model reservoir as the natural remedy. The framework is explicitly plug-in, compatible with diverse TTA objectives (entropy minimization, sharpness-aware minimization, LoRA-based expert blending) and architectures (CNNs, ViTs, segmentation transformers).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically critical gap: real-world deployments (autonomous driving, surveillance) encounter recurring domain shifts (day/night cycles, seasonal weather) that single-model TTA handles poorly, and the reservoir design offers a clean, modular solution that practitioners can adopt without redesigning their TTA pipeline. The consistent 1–7% error reductions across six baselines, three corruption benchmarks, and a segmentation task demonstrate broad applicability, while the theoretical stability-region analysis gives practitioners a principled understanding of when and why their TTA method will fail. Open questions include the 30% compute overhead from maintaining multiple models, the lack of an adaptive update trigger (the method updates on every batch regardless of convergence), and whether style-based clustering generalizes to non-visual modalities or to settings with very large numbers of fine-grained domains.
