## Problem

Existing VQ-VAE-based co-speech gesture generation methods tokenize motion at a single temporal scale with a fixed frame count per token, which cannot represent the naturally varying durations of expressive patterns across different body parts (e.g., rapid facial micro-expressions vs. slower body sways). This single-scale assumption limits both the encoder's ability to capture multi-granularity motion semantics and the predictor's ability to align audio/text cues of inconsistent durations to the correct motion tokens.

## Input / Output

The method takes speech audio (resampled so that sf = sr_audio / fps_gestures samples per gesture frame) and a text transcript as input, producing a full-body 3D gesture sequence of L frames where each frame is a (55×6 + 100 + 4 + 3)-dimensional vector comprising 55 joint rotations in Rot6D, 100-dim FLAME facial expression parameters, 4 foot contact labels, and 3 global translation coordinates. The audio is decomposed into onset and amplitude features, and the transcript is encoded with pre-trained word embeddings; both are fused via element-wise attention before being split into separate face and body pathways.

## Method

PyraMotion is a two-stage framework. Stage 1 trains an Attentional Pyramidal VQ-VAE (APVQ-VAE) where n parallel TCN encoders with exponentially increasing kernel sizes produce n embedding series of lengths T/2^i, all quantized against a single shared codebook to preserve cross-scale semantic alignment; reconstruction uses Transpose TCN decoders at matching scales followed by an attentional fusion (query = mean of stacked reconstructions, key/value = stacked reconstructions) plus a residual mean connection. Stage 2 trains a Pyramidal Token Predictor that fuses audio-text features, splits them into face and body (further into upper/lower/hands) pathways, constructs two pyramidal representations per part (mean-pooled and TCN-extracted), applies temporal cross-attention between them, then performs full-body cross-attention to capture inter-part correlations, and finally predicts discrete tokens iteratively from coarse to fine granularity via cross-entropy loss. The key architectural insight is that the attentional decoder learns body-part-specific scale preferences (e.g., face favors fine-grained tokens, lower body balances coarse scales), which a fixed single-scale VQ-VAE cannot express.

## Results

On BEAT2-Standard Speaker2, PyraMotion achieves the best FGD (4.612×10⁻¹), MSE (7.176×10⁻⁸), and LVD (7.270×10⁻⁵) among all compared methods, indicating superior gesture fidelity and facial expression accuracy. However, it trails AMUSE (8.318×10⁻¹) and HoloGest (7.957×10⁻¹) on Beat Alignment, and AMUSE (14.93) and HoloGest (14.15) on Diversity. Inference time is 41±1.8s vs. 22±1.1s for single-scale EMAGE, roughly 1.9× slower.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PyraMotion outperforms all 16 baselines on FGD (4.612 vs. next-best HoloGest 5.341 and SynTalker 5.366), MSE (7.176 vs. DiffSHEG 7.665), and LVD (7.270 vs. TalkSHOW 7.771), confirming that multi-scale pyramidal tokenization improves reconstruction fidelity for both body and face. On Beat Alignment, PyraMotion (7.420) is outperformed by AMUSE (8.318), HoloGest (7.957), SynTalker (7.812), and MambaTalk (7.812), suggesting that the pyramidal structure does not fully capture rhythmic synchronization cues. On Diversity, AMUSE (14.93) and HoloGest (14.15) exceed PyraMotion (13.42), though PyraMotion still surpasses most other methods. Against the closest full-body competitors (EMage, SynTalker, HoloGest, TalkSHOW, DiffSHEG), PyraMotion consistently leads on reconstruction metrics (FGD, MSE, LVD) while trading off some synchronization and diversity. The perceptual study further confirms that participants prefer PyraMotion over CaMN and EMage in naturalness, and in a subset of cases PyraMotion even outperforms its own APVQ-VAE reconstruction, indicating the generation pipeline adds value beyond the encoder.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the VQ-VAE-based co-speech gesture generation paradigm established by EMAGE (Liu et al., 2024) and Rhythmic Gesticulator (Ao et al., 2022), which tokenize single-scale, fixed-frame-count motion into discrete codebook entries. The key architectural departure is the introduction of APVQ-VAE, which replaces the single-scale encoder with a multi-level TCN pyramid (inspired by FPN in object detection) and a Transpose TCN decoder with attentional fusion, allowing different body parts to attend to different temporal granularities. The Pyramidal Token Predictor further extends the autoregressive token prediction stage by constructing both mean-pooled and TCN-extracted multi-scale latents per body part, then fusing them via temporal cross-attention before iterative coarse-to-fine token quantization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and well-motivated limitation—fixed-frame-count tokens cannot represent motion patterns of varying durations across body parts—and provides interpretable evidence (attention maps) that different body regions genuinely prefer different temporal scales. However, evaluation is restricted to a single speaker (BEAT2-Standard Speaker2), the inference cost roughly doubles compared to EMAGE (41s vs 22s for 5 minutes of motion), and the number of pyramid layers is a fixed hyperparameter rather than adaptively determined. Open questions include whether the pyramidal tokenization generalizes across speakers and datasets, whether the ~2× inference overhead is acceptable for real-time avatar applications, and whether the body-part interference issue noted in the limitations can be resolved with adaptive fusion ratios.
