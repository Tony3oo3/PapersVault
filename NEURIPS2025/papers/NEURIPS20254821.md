## Problem

Existing black-box differential privacy auditing methods rely on fixed batch sample sizes, often requiring hundreds of thousands of mechanism evaluations to detect violations—making them impractical for expensive algorithms like DP-SGD where a single sample may require a full training step. The paper addresses this by introducing a sequential testing framework that adaptively accumulates evidence and stops as soon as a privacy violation is detected, eliminating the need to pre-specify sample size and reducing detection from ~50K samples to a few hundred.

## Input / Output

The auditor receives a stream of paired outputs (X_t ~ A(S), Y_t ~ A(S')) from a mechanism evaluated on two fixed neighboring datasets, along with claimed privacy parameters (ε, δ). In white-box settings (e.g., DP-SGD), the input is reduced to one-dimensional scalar projections of intermediate gradients onto a canary direction. The test produces an anytime-valid reject/fail-to-reject decision at each step, an empirical lower bound on the true ε (by running parallel tests at multiple candidate values), and the stopping time—the number of samples needed to detect a violation. Samples are consumed adaptively: the test halts the moment the supermartingale crosses 1/α, so no samples are wasted after detection.

## Method

The core test constructs a nonnegative supermartingale K_t = K_{t-1} × (1 + λ_t[f_t(X_t) − f_t(Y_t) − τ(ε,δ)]) where τ(ε,δ) = √2(1 − 2(1−δ)/(1+e^ε)) is a tighter MMD-to-Hockey-Stick bound (Theorem 3.1) that remains non-vacuous as ε grows, unlike prior bounds. The MMD witness function f_t is learned online via Online Gradient Ascent (OGA) in an RKHS, and the betting parameter λ_t is adapted via Online Newton Step (ONS) or, in an alternative e-process variant (Algorithm 2), via Universal Portfolio (UP) with smaller regret constants. Ville's inequality guarantees Type I error ≤ α uniformly over all stopping times under H₀, while under H₁ the process grows at rate Ω(Δ²) where Δ = MMD − τ, yielding expected stopping time O(log(1/Δ²)/Δ²). The key distinction from prior two-sided MMD betting tests is the one-sided formulation against a non-zero threshold τ, which requires constraining λ ≥ 0 to preserve the supermartingale property and non-trivial modifications to establish the existence of an optimal λ* within the restricted domain.

## Results

On additive-noise mean estimation mechanisms at ε=0.01, the sequential test detects all non-DP violations with 100% rejection rate using only 192–331 samples on average (e.g., NonDPLaplace2 in 192±18.4 samples, NonDPGaussian1 in 264±9.3 samples), while maintaining 0% false rejection on true DP mechanisms. At ε=0.1, detection still requires fewer than 1,000 samples for most mechanisms. For DP-SGD auditing, the test rejects the null hypothesis ε_canary≤0.01 in an average of just 60 gradient observations and ε_canary=0.1 in 75 observations—well under one full training run—establishing an empirical privacy lower bound of ε=0.43 after only 250 observations for a non-private implementation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is the fixed-sample-size MMD-tester from DP-Auditorium [26], which constructs a confidence interval for MMD² from a predetermined batch of samples. The sequential test dramatically outperforms this baseline: at ε=0.01, the batch MMD-tester from [26] failed to detect violations in NonDPLaplace2 and NonDPGaussian2 even with 500,000 observations, while the sequential test rejects NonDPLaplace2 in ~192 samples and NonDPGaussian2 in ~1,139 samples (85% rejection rate). An ablation in Appendix D (Table 3) decouples the sources of improvement: the sequential nature contributes the most (e.g., 'Sequential+MH' achieves 100% detection on NonDP1 vs. 0% for 'Batch+MH'), the median heuristic for bandwidth selection provides additional gains, and the tighter MMD bound from Theorem 3.1 offers modest improvement at small ε but becomes more significant at larger ε. For DP-SGD, prior methods require at least one full training run to produce any privacy estimate, whereas the sequential test identifies violations in 60–75 gradient steps, representing an orders-of-magnitude reduction in computational cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the sequential two-sample testing-by-betting framework of Shekhar and Ramdas [44]—originally designed for a two-sided MMD=0 test—into a one-sided sequential test against a non-zero threshold, a non-trivial modification requiring new analysis to preserve the supermartingale property under the restricted λ domain. It tightens the MMD-to-Hockey-Stick divergence bound from DP-Auditorium [26] (Theorem 3.1), and draws on the e-process/Universal Portfolio framework of Waudby-Smith et al. [51] to construct an alternative test (Algorithm 2) with smaller regret constants. The overall contribution is the first application of modern anytime-valid sequential inference (e-values, Ville's inequality) to black-box DP auditing, where prior work has been confined to fixed-batch confidence intervals or parametric assumptions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: detecting DP violations in a few hundred samples rather than hundreds of thousands, and identifying DP-SGD privacy failures in under one training run, makes auditing computationally feasible for expensive mechanisms. Key limitations remain—the MMD-based approach struggles with large ε (where the statistic and threshold both saturate), and the method requires a fixed pair of neighboring datasets rather than adaptively finding the worst case. Open directions include extending the sequential framework to Rényi DP and f-DP, combining with adaptive worst-case pair search, and closing the gap for high-ε regimes where the Δ² gap becomes vanishingly small.
