## Problem

Standard MLP-based Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs) frequently fail to converge on complex time-dependent PDEs, particularly hyperbolic problems like wave equations, due to spectral bias toward low frequencies, causality violations from simultaneous training over the entire time domain, and convergence to trivial equilibrium solutions. Existing architectural fixes (Fourier-feature layers, modified losses, transformer attention) address these issues only partially and do not provide structural guarantees on initial-condition satisfaction or solution uniqueness.

## Input / Output

The method takes densely sampled initial conditions (Cauchy data), PDE coefficients (e.g., spatially varying wave speed c(x)), and domain geometry as input. Initial conditions are projected onto a truncated Fourier/sine/cosine basis to yield spectral coefficient tensors of shape c₁×c₂×…×c_d×n. The output is the PDE solution u(t,x) reconstructed at arbitrary space-time points by multiplying time-evolved spectral coefficients with basis functions. Notably, time is not an input coordinate but is encoded as sequential NODE integration steps, and spatial samples must remain fixed across all time steps within a single forward pass, enabling the entire space-time loss to be evaluated in one pass rather than thousands of independent evaluations.

## Method

NeuSA reduces a PDE to a finite-dimensional ODE system via spectral decomposition (method of lines), then learns the ODE vector field with a Neural ODE. The NODE's vector field is decomposed as F̂_θ(û) = M ⊙ û + ε·F_θ(û), where M is the analytical Fourier multiplier of the linearized translation-invariant PDE and F_θ is a small neural network (MLP with dimension-wise low-rank layers) that learns the nonlinear/non-translation-invariant residual. This initialization places the network near the exact solution of the associated linear problem before any training. Causality is guaranteed by construction: the sequential NODE integration defines a flow operator, so initial conditions are exactly satisfied and solutions are unique (Theorem 1). To handle the dense inter-mode interactions inherent in spectral representations without O(m²n²) parameter blowup, the neural vector field uses dimension-wise linear layers combining Hadamard products with alternating row-wise and column-wise matrix multiplications, reducing complexity to O(mn) per layer.

## Results

NeuSA achieves the lowest rMSE across all five PDE benchmarks, with particularly dramatic gains on the 1D sine-Gordon equation (rMSE 0.001 vs. 0.020 for the best baseline QRes, a 20× improvement) and the 3D wave equation (rMSE 0.008 vs. 0.021 for QRes). Training time is also substantially shorter: NeuSA trains in 702 s on 3D Layers versus 5,990–8,775 s for baselines, and in 112 s on 2D Burgers versus 871–2,294 s. On the Marmousi reservoir benchmark, NeuSA reaches rMSE 0.171 compared to 0.412 for QRes, the next-best method, while being trained for only 2,000 steps versus 20,000 for all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NeuSA outperforms all four baselines (PINN, QRes, FLS, PINNsFormer) on every benchmark where they are all evaluated, with the largest margins on the Marmousi reservoir (rMSE 0.171 vs. 0.412 for QRes) and the 1D sine-Gordon equation (rMSE 0.001 vs. 0.020 for QRes, a 20× gap). QRes is consistently the strongest baseline, but NeuSA still achieves 1.5–20× lower rMSE depending on the problem. Training time advantages are most pronounced on the 3D wave equation (702 s vs. 5,990–8,775 s) and 2D Burgers (112 s vs. 871–2,294 s), where NeuSA trains 8–10× faster while also being more accurate. PINNsFormer performs worst overall, with rMSE exceeding 1.0 on 2D Layers and 2D Burgers, indicating failure to converge. NeuSA also uniquely demonstrates strong time-extrapolation on Burgers' equation, retaining qualitative solution structure beyond the training interval while PINN and QRes degenerate rapidly.

## Relation to Prior Work

NeuSA builds on two main threads: the physics-informed neural network (PINN) framework of Raissi et al. [1], which embeds PDE residuals into a loss function, and Neural ODEs (NODEs) of Chen et al. [35], which model continuous-time dynamics via learned vector fields. The key departure is from the standard MLP coordinate-network paradigm: instead of learning a mapping from (t,x) to u(t,x) directly, NeuSA projects the solution onto a Fourier spectral basis, reducing the PDE to a finite-dimensional ODE system (method of lines) whose dynamics are then learned by a NODE. This is conceptually distinct from Fourier-Feature layers [26] or FLS [23], which inject high-frequency information as an input encoding into an MLP; here the spectral basis is the actual representation of the solution, and the network operates in coefficient space. The Fourier-multiplier initialization scheme further distinguishes NeuSA by leveraging the algebraic structure of linear translation-invariant operators to start training near the solution of a related linear problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

NeuSA addresses two of the most persistent failure modes of PINNs—spectral bias and causality violations—by architectural construction rather than loss engineering, and provides a formal guarantee (Theorem 1) that initial conditions are satisfied and solutions are unique. The 1–2 order-of-magnitude error reductions on wave propagation benchmarks and the robust time-extrapolation behavior (Figure 6) suggest the spectral-NODE formulation captures the underlying dynamics more faithfully than coordinate networks. However, the method is currently restricted to simple (rectangular) geometries amenable to Fourier bases, relies on Runge-Kutta integration that can become unstable for stiff spectral systems (e.g., KdV), and its performance is heavily dependent on the quality of the linear initialization. Open questions include extending to complex geometries via alternative bases, handling stiff PDEs with implicit or adaptive integrators, and whether the dimension-wise layer design generalizes to higher-dimensional or anisotropic spectral representations.
