## Problem

Supervised manifold learning methods like RF-PHATE produce high-quality label-informed embeddings but lack a parametric mapping function, requiring full retraining to embed new data. Existing out-of-sample extensions (Nyström, linear reconstruction) rely on linear kernel mappings that fail to preserve complex supervised geometry, while neural network approaches (parametric UMAP, GRAE) target unsupervised structure or classification accuracy without explicitly preserving the label-informed relational structure needed for interpretable supervised visualization.

## Input / Output

The method consumes high-dimensional feature vectors (tabular, RGB image, or audio) along with a pre-trained random forest, transforming raw inputs into row-normalized RF-GAP proximity vectors of dimension N (or N* after prototype selection) that encode supervised neighborhood structure. The output is a generalizable encoder function producing 2D embedding coordinates (d=2 for visual interpretability) that can project unseen, unlabeled data points without retraining. The self-similarity in RF-GAP is specially defined by passing an identical OOB point through in-bag trees, ensuring it remains on a comparable scale to cross-similarities rather than being set to zero or one.

## Method

RF-AE is an autoencoder whose encoder and decoder operate on RF-GAP proximity vectors rather than raw features, so the bottleneck implicitly captures supervised relational structure. Training minimizes a weighted combination of a Jensen-Shannon Divergence reconstruction loss (treating RF-GAP vectors as probability distributions to avoid bias toward dominant zero entries) and a Euclidean geometric loss that aligns the bottleneck with precomputed RF-PHATE embeddings, with λ controlling the trade-off. To handle the N-dimensional input scaling with dataset size, class-wise k-medoids prototype selection on symmetrized RF-GAP dissimilarities reduces the input to N* ≪ N prototypes, also accelerating OOS projection. The key architectural insight is that reconstructing RF-GAP proximities—rather than raw features or class labels—provides a strong supervision signal tied to the relational geometry between points, enabling the learned encoder to generalize to new data while preserving both local and global supervised structure.

## Results

Across 20 datasets and 10 repetitions, RF-AE (λ=0.01, N*=0.1N_train) achieves the highest k-NN classification accuracy at 0.861±0.009, outperforming the next-best method RF-PHATE (0.816±0.010) by 4.5 percentage points. RF-AE also ranks in the top two on all four Structural Importance Alignment (SIA) metrics: local QNX 0.809±0.024, local Trust 0.822±0.022, global Spearman 0.782±0.041, and global Pearson 0.779±0.042. Ablation over λ∈{0, 0.001, 0.01, 0.1, 1} shows k-NN accuracy remains stable between 0.836 and 0.864, confirming robustness to the geometric regularization strength.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RF-AE outperforms all 13 baselines on k-NN accuracy, with the largest gap over RF-PHATE's default linear kernel extension (0.861 vs. 0.816, +4.5 pp) and over parametric supervised UMAP (0.861 vs. 0.797, +6.4 pp). On local SIA, RF-AE leads or ties with NCA (QNX 0.809 vs. 0.808) and RF-PHATE (Trust 0.822 vs. 0.825, a negligible 0.003 gap), while on global SIA it clearly surpasses RF-PHATE (Spearman 0.782 vs. 0.748, +3.4 pp) and CE (0.782 vs. 0.765). Among unsupervised methods, PACMAP, P-TSNE, P-UMAP, and PCA all trail RF-AE by 10–30 pp in accuracy and by 4–14 pp in global SIA, confirming that the absence of label-informed supervision degrades both class separability and feature-importance alignment. Notably, high-accuracy supervised baselines such as SSNP and P-SUMAP sacrifice global SIA (0.685–0.694 vs. RF-AE's 0.779–0.782), indicating they over-emphasize class separation at the cost of preserving informative feature structure. RF-AE's advantage is consistent across the λ ablation (Table 2), where even the vanilla setting (λ=1, no geometric regularization) retains 0.864 k-NN accuracy, though global SIA drops to 0.689, underscoring the value of the RF-PHATE geometric constraint.

## Relation to Prior Work

RF-AE sits at the intersection of three research threads: RF-GAP proximities (Rhodes et al., TPAMI 2023) provide the supervised kernel representation; RF-PHATE (Rhodes et al., 2024) supplies the precomputed geometric constraint embeddings; and GRAE (Duque et al., TPAMI 2022) contributes the architectural idea of regularizing an autoencoder bottleneck toward a precomputed manifold embedding. The key departure from prior work is replacing the linear kernel mapping of Nyström-style OOS extensions with a nonlinear autoencoder that reconstructs RF-GAP transition probability vectors rather than raw features, while simultaneously matching RF-PHATE coordinates. This distinguishes RF-AE from class-conditional supervised methods (CE, P-SUMAP) that distort inter-class geometry, and from unsupervised parametric extensions (P-UMAP, P-TSNE) that ignore label-informed structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RF-AE fills a practical gap: RF-PHATE produced interpretable supervised visualizations but offered no mechanism to embed new observations without retraining, a critical limitation for streaming or label-scarce settings. The introduction of Structural Importance Alignment (SIA) as an evaluation metric is a useful contribution that exposes a trade-off invisible to k-NN accuracy alone—high-accuracy methods can sacrifice feature-structure alignment. Open questions include scalability beyond the prototype-selection workaround (the full RF-GAP matrix remains O(N²)), whether the approach extends to semi-supervised hierarchical clustering, and whether alternative RF-based geometric constraints (e.g., RF-UMAP) could match or exceed RF-PHATE regularization.
