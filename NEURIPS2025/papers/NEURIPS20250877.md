## Problem

Offline RL suffers from extrapolation errors when the Bellman target selects out-of-distribution actions. Existing constraint categories each have inherent limitations: density and sample constraints are overly conservative when the behavior policy is low-quality or the dataset lacks near-optimal actions, while the support constraint—though least restrictive—requires accurate behavior policy modeling that is difficult for high-dimensional, multi-modal data. The paper asks whether one can approximate the support constraint's flexibility without the modeling burden.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a fixed offline dataset of (state, action, reward, next_state) tuples and produces a deterministic policy π_φ: S→A extracted via weighted regression toward actions refined by an auxiliary policy (not the raw dataset actions). The neighborhood radius is adapted per data point as ε·exp(−α·A(s,a)), so low-advantage samples receive larger search radii while high-advantage samples are tightly constrained, enabling pointwise conservatism without a global hyperparameter sweep.

## Method

ANQ introduces a neighborhood constraint that restricts Bellman target actions to the union of ε-neighborhoods around dataset actions, theoretically shown to approximate the support constraint (via Hausdorff distance bounds under a standardness assumption) while bounding extrapolation error (via NTK analysis) and distribution shift (via Lipschitz continuity of dynamics). The constraint is enforced through a bilevel optimization: an inner loop trains an auxiliary policy μ_ω to maximize Q within each dataset action's neighborhood (solved via a Lagrangian with a per-sample constraint threshold), and an outer loop uses expectile regression to implicitly maximize Q over all neighborhoods, yielding the V function that replaces the max in the Bellman target. Policy extraction then performs exponentiated-advantage-weighted regression toward the μ_ω-refined actions rather than the original dataset actions, allowing the learned policy to select actions superior to those observed offline.

## Results

On D4RL Gym-MuJoCo locomotion tasks, ANQ achieves the highest total normalized score of 1194.5, surpassing the next-best SPOT (1139.9) and IQL (1033.1). On the more challenging AntMaze tasks, ANQ attains a total of 441.6, outperforming IQL (392.4) and SPOT (359.6) by substantial margins, with particularly large gaps on the large-maze settings (e.g., 56.2 vs. 42.0 on antmaze-l-p). On individual locomotion tasks, ANQ leads on most, including hopper-m (100.9 vs. 87.5 for OneStep) and halfcheetah-m (61.8 vs. 58.4 for SPOT).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ANQ outperforms all nine baselines on both the locomotion total (1194.5 vs. SPOT's 1139.9, the closest competitor) and the AntMaze total (441.6 vs. IQL's 392.4). On individual locomotion tasks, ANQ leads on 12 of 15, with the largest margins on hopper-m (+13.4 over OneStep) and hopper-r (+7.7 over SPOT); it trails slightly on walker2d-m (82.9 vs. SPOT's 86.4) and halfcheetah-r (24.9 vs. SPOT's 25.4). On AntMaze, ANQ wins on 4 of 6 tasks, with the most decisive advantage on antmaze-u-d (80.2 vs. IQL's 65.6) and antmaze-l-p (56.2 vs. IQL's 42.0); IQL edges ANQ narrowly on antmaze-m-p (76.4 vs. 76.2) and SPOT on antmaze-m-d (79.1 vs. 77.2). Among density-constraint methods (CQL, TD3BC, AWAC), ANQ consistently leads by 10–50+ points on medium and medium-replay datasets. Support-constraint methods (BCQ, BEAR, SPOT) show the largest gaps on AntMaze, where BCQ and BEAR score near zero on most tasks, while SPOT remains competitive on locomotion but falls behind ANQ by 55 points in total.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves out a new constraint type—the neighborhood constraint—for offline RL that sits conceptually between the overly conservative sample constraint (IQL, OneStep) and the hard-to-enforce support constraint (BCQ, BEAR, SPOT). It builds directly on IQL's expectile regression mechanism for the outer optimization and on AWR-style exponentiated-advantage weighted regression for policy extraction, but departs from both by regressing toward optimized actions within adaptive neighborhoods rather than toward raw dataset actions. The theoretical analysis draws on geometric measure theory (Hausdorff distance approximation under standardness), NTK-based extrapolation bounds, and Lipschitz continuity of transition dynamics to characterize the constraint's properties, while the bilevel optimization framework for enforcing the constraint is a novel algorithmic contribution distinct from BCQ's perturbation model, which operates on generatively sampled actions rather than dataset actions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The neighborhood constraint offers a principled middle ground that avoids both the over-conservatism of sample/density methods and the behavior-modeling fragility of support methods, with the adaptive radius mechanism providing a practical lever for pointwise conservatism. The demonstrated robustness to noisy and limited data is practically important for real-world deployments where datasets are imperfect. However, the adaptive radius design (exponential of advantage) is acknowledged as a heuristic rather than an optimal choice, the NTK assumption restricts theoretical applicability to wide networks, and the framework is limited to deterministic policies—open questions include whether uncertainty quantification or stochastic policy extensions could further improve neighborhood construction and whether the approach generalizes to safe RL or meta-RL settings.
