## Problem

Storing multiple fine-tuned models for multi-task deployment creates severe storage overhead, and delta compression (storing only the difference between fine-tuned and pretrained weights) is a promising solution. However, existing delta compression methods—whether pruning, quantization, or hybrid—fail to simultaneously achieve ultra-high compression ratios and strong performance, and most rely on calibration or validation data. The paper identifies three specific gaps: ignoring inter-layer importance differences, disrupting intra-layer weight distributions, and lacking stability mechanisms under extreme sparsity in a data-free setting.

## Input / Output

The method takes a shared pretrained model and one or more fine-tuned models (all in FP16), computes the element-wise delta weights Δθ = θ_finetuned − θ_pre, and produces a compressed representation consisting of 4-bit quantized values with a Bernoulli-pruned sparsity mask, stored via Golomb coding of zero-run lengths. At inference, the compressed delta is dequantized, rescaled by a task-specific factor γ/(1−s), and added back to the pretrained weights to reconstruct the fine-tuned model. The pipeline is entirely data-free: no calibration, validation, or task-specific data is required at any stage.

## Method

UltraDelta is a three-stage data-free pipeline operating across inter-layer, intra-layer, and global dimensions. (1) Variance-Based Mixed Sparsity Allocation (MSA) partitions layers into three equal-parameter groups by variance and assigns lower sparsity to high-variance layers, grounded in a rate-distortion argument showing that higher-variance layers carry more information and require lower sparsity to preserve it. (2) Distribution-Aware Compression (DAC) first applies uniform 4-bit quantization to the delta weights, then groups parameters by their quantized value and performs independent Bernoulli pruning within each group, preserving the relative proportions of different values and thus the intra-layer distribution shape. (3) Trace-Norm-Guided Rescaling (TNGR) replaces the standard 1/(1−s) rescaling with γ/(1−s), where γ ∈ [0.5, 1.0] is set inversely proportional to the trace norm of each delta weight, stabilizing activation error variance (which scales as γ²s/(1−s)) under extreme sparsity. The combination achieves compression ratios of 32×–224× across LLMs, NLP, vision, and multi-modal models while matching or exceeding fine-tuned model performance.

## Results

UltraDelta achieves 32.9× compression on LLaMA-2-7B (avg 45.57) and 50.9× on LLaMA-2-13B (avg 52.05), both exceeding the uncompressed fine-tuned models (45.37 and 50.94). On T5-base, it reaches an extreme 224.6× compression with 86.74 average accuracy (vs. 86.37 for fine-tuned). On ViT-L/14, it achieves 132.5× compression with 94.4 average accuracy, matching the fine-tuned model exactly. Across all four model families (LLMs, NLP, vision, multi-modal), UltraDelta consistently outperforms every baseline at equal or higher compression ratios.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UltraDelta wins against all five baseline families across every model type tested. Against BitDelta (16×) and Delta-CoMe (16×), UltraDelta achieves 2–3× higher compression while also delivering better accuracy (e.g., 45.57 vs. 41.89/42.52 avg on LLaMA-2-7B; 86.74 vs. 84.68/85.95 on T5-base). Against DARE at matched sparsity, UltraDelta gains 6.8 points on LLaMA-2-7B (45.57 vs. 38.79) and 0.84 on T5-base (86.74 vs. 85.90); at matched compression ratio, the gap widens to 11.6 points on LLaMA-2-7B (45.57 vs. 33.97). Magnitude Pruning collapses catastrophically under high compression (e.g., 15.36 avg on LLaMA-2-13B at 50.1×, 29.5 on ViT-L/14 at 127.6×), while UltraDelta maintains near-lossless performance at even higher ratios. On ViT-L/14, UltraDelta at 132.5× matches the fine-tuned model exactly (94.4) while DARE at 127.6× drops to 89.1 and MP to 29.5. The only baseline UltraDelta does not clearly dominate is BitDelta on ViT-L/14 (94.1 vs. 94.4 at 16×), but UltraDelta still wins while compressing 8× more aggressively.

## Relation to Prior Work

UltraDelta extends the DARE framework (random Bernoulli pruning with 1/(1-s) rescaling) by introducing three orthogonal refinements: variance-based mixed sparsity allocation across layers, distribution-aware compression via uniform quantization followed by group-wise random pruning, and trace-norm-guided rescaling. It departs from prior delta compression methods (BitDelta, Delta-CoMe, DeltaZip) by being entirely data-free while targeting ultra-high compression ratios (up to 224×), whereas existing approaches either require calibration data or cannot maintain performance beyond ~16× compression. The theoretical grounding draws on rate-distortion theory to justify variance-based sparsity allocation and on activation error variance analysis to motivate the additional rescaling factor.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

For practitioners deploying multiple fine-tuned models from a shared pretrained backbone, UltraDelta offers a practical, data-free path to 30–224× storage reduction with minimal (or no) performance loss, potentially eliminating the need for multi-model serving infrastructure. The observed regularization effect—where compression sometimes *improves* over fine-tuned models—suggests an underexplored connection between sparsity and generalization in fine-tuned LLMs, though the evidence is limited to controlled experiments on two vision datasets. Open questions include whether the trace-norm heuristic generalizes to non-Transformer architectures, whether the method can be made deployment-aware (e.g., structured sparsity for hardware acceleration), and whether the regularization benefit persists in fully fine-tuned (non-zero-shot) settings.
