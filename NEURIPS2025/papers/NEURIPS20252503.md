## Problem

Existing differentiable structure learning methods for discrete data impose restrictive parametric assumptions (e.g., linear SEMs with logistic links) that may misrepresent the true data-generating process and fail to capture higher-order interactions inherent in general binary distributions. Moreover, prior approaches lack formal identifiability guarantees for the general discrete setting. This paper addresses both gaps by developing a fully general framework that captures arbitrary dependencies among binary variables without assuming a specific generative model.

## Input / Output

The method takes an n×p binary data matrix as i.i.d. samples from a multivariate Bernoulli distribution and expands each row via an extended feature map Φ to 2^p dimensions encompassing all interaction terms up to order p−1. The output is a sparsest DAG (up to Markov equivalence) encoded as a weighted adjacency matrix W(H) ∈ R^{p×p}, where each entry W(H)_ij aggregates the squared magnitudes of all interaction coefficients involving variable i in the structural equation for variable j, along with the corresponding natural parameter vectors f. Unlike linear models where a single weight W_ij determines an edge, here multiple coefficients jointly determine edge presence, making the parameter matrix H of size 2^p × p.

## Method

The core contribution is a single differentiable constrained optimization that recovers the sparsest DAG for general binary data: minimize the multivariate Bernoulli negative log-likelihood (a logistic loss on the extended feature map) plus a smooth quasi-MCP sparsity penalty on the induced adjacency matrix W(H), subject to a differentiable acyclicity constraint h(W(H))=0 and no-self-loop constraints. The key insight is that the multivariate Bernoulli distribution, expressed in exponential-family form, admits a fully general SEM where each node's conditional is a logistic function of all interaction terms among its predecessors, and the edge criterion reduces to checking whether any coefficient involving a given parent is nonzero. The paper proves non-identifiability (p! compatible graph-parameter pairs exist), characterizes the minimal equivalence class under the Sparsest Markov Representation assumption, and shows that for sufficiently small regularization parameters, every global optimum of the continuous program recovers a sparsest DAG up to Markov equivalence. For scalability beyond small graphs, BINOTEARS employs a two-stage heuristic: first learning a coarse structure via NOTEARS-MLP adapted to discrete data, then refining edges by fitting logistic regressions with first- and second-order interactions along the inferred topological order.

## Results

On the Sachs protein-signaling dataset (n=7,466, p=11), B1NOTEARS achieves a structural Hamming distance (SHD) of 13, outperforming NOTEARS-MLP (SHD=16) and NOTEARS (SHD=22). On synthetic small graphs (p=5–9) with first- and second-order interactions, BINOTEARS achieves competitive SHD relative to PC and FGES, while DAGMA—which models only first-order effects—exhibits markedly degraded recovery. When only the highest-order interaction is present (Figure 1b), BINOTEARS consistently attains near-optimal SHD across all graph types and densities.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the Sachs dataset, B1NOTEARS (SHD=13) outperforms both NOTEARS (SHD=22) and NOTEARS-MLP (SHD=16), reducing the structural Hamming distance by 41% and 19% respectively. In the synthetic small-graph experiments (p=5–9), BINOTEARS matches or slightly exceeds PC and FGES when first- and second-order interactions are present, while DAGMA degrades substantially because it can only capture first-order (linear) effects. When the data-generating process involves only the highest-order interaction (no first-order terms), BINOTEARS achieves near-zero SHD, whereas all baselines that assume linear or additive effects fail to recover the correct structure. On larger graphs (p=10–40), the two-stage B1NOTEARS approach remains competitive with FGES and PC, though all methods degrade as graph size and edge density increase. The paper also provides theoretical justification (Theorem 4) showing that prior methods like DAGMA and NOTEARS-MLP applied to discrete data are special cases of the proposed general framework under a first-order logistic assumption.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the differentiable structure learning paradigm (NOTEARS, DAGMA, NOTEARS-MLP) from continuous data to fully general binary data by parameterizing the joint distribution via the multivariate Bernoulli exponential family, which captures arbitrary higher-order interactions. It departs from prior discrete-data differentiable methods (e.g., [49], [5], [13]) that assumed a logistic-linear SEM, showing those are special cases of its general framework. The quasi-MCP penalty and consistency theory are inherited from Deng et al. [15], while the acyclicity constraint follows the NOTEARS/DAGMA lineage. The paper also provides the first formal identifiability characterization for the fully general binary setting, completing the equivalence class of all p! graph-parameter pairs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work closes a theoretical gap by showing that prior differentiable methods applied to discrete data (e.g., DAGMA on binary data) are valid only under restrictive first-order assumptions, and that higher-order interactions are necessary for exact representation of general binary distributions. The practical limitation is significant: the full formulation requires 2^p features per node, restricting it to small graphs (p ≤ 9 in experiments), while the two-stage BINOTEARS heuristic for larger graphs lacks theoretical guarantees. Open questions include scalable algorithms for thousands of nodes, finite-sample guarantees for the two-stage approach, and whether the SMR assumption is empirically verifiable in practice.
