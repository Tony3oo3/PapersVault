## Problem

Neural operators for PDE solving are severely limited by the scarcity of high-quality training data, which is expensive to collect, resolution-constrained, and often distributionally biased. This data limitation fundamentally constrains generalization, particularly in data-limited regimes where models overfit to sparse training distributions. The paper addresses this by asking whether the PDE solving task can be reformulated to implicitly augment training data without additional collection.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250427/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an input function field a_i (e.g., diffusion coefficient, velocity, or temperature field) and concatenates it along the channel dimension with an auxiliary sample a_{k_i} retrieved from the training set via cosine similarity, optionally augmented with the auxiliary solution u_{k_i} and the similarity score. The output is the full predicted solution field u_i, obtained by adding the network's predicted residual to the auxiliary solution u_{k_i} through a physical residual connection. The framework operates on both regular grid domains (e.g., 421×421 Darcy Flow, 64×64 Navier-Stokes) and irregular triangle-mesh domains (e.g., 1656-node Blood Flow), and supports cross-resolution inference via Fourier-based upsampling/downsampling.

## Method

DeltaPhi reformulates PDE solving from learning a direct operator G: A→U to learning a residual operator G^Δ: A²→ΔU that predicts the difference between two similar solution trajectories. During training, for each input a_i, the method retrieves K (default 20) most similar training samples via cosine similarity, randomly selects one as auxiliary, and trains an architecture-agnostic neural operator to predict u_i − u_{k_i} from the concatenated pair (a_i, a_{k_i}), with a physical residual connection adding u_{k_i} at the output for end-to-end optimization. During inference, the most similar training sample is greedily selected and the predicted residual is added to its known solution. The key theoretical grounding is PDE stability (Lipschitz continuity of the solution operator), which ensures that similar inputs produce proportionally similar outputs, making the residual learning task tractable and enabling implicit data augmentation: each of N training samples generates K distinct residual pairs, expanding the effective training distribution while preserving physical validity and discouraging memorization.

## Results

On regular domains with 100 training samples, DeltaPhi improves all seven base architectures on both Darcy Flow and Navier-Stokes, with the largest gains on Darcy Flow: FFNO drops from 5.22e-2 to 2.93e-2 relative L2 error (43.76% improvement) and CFNO from 4.79e-2 to 3.15e-2 (34.23%). On irregular domains, NORM(DeltaPhi) achieves 50% relative error reduction on Heat Transfer (2.70e-4 → 1.35e-4) and 41% on Pipe Turbulence (1.01e-2 → 5.96e-3) compared to NORM(Direct). In data-limited settings (100 samples), FFNO(DeltaPhi) outperforms FFNO(Direct) trained on 700 samples, and Resnet(DeltaPhi) with 700 samples beats Resnet(Direct) with 900 samples, demonstrating that residual learning effectively substitutes for additional training data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DeltaPhi is evaluated as a plug-in enhancement against the direct-learning version of each base architecture, and it wins on every single comparison across all 7 regular-domain models (FNO, FFNO, CFNO, GNOT, Galerkin, MiOnet, Resnet) and the irregular-domain NORM baseline. The gains are largest for spectral-based operators (FFNO: 43.76%, CFNO: 34.23% on Darcy Flow) and smallest for attention-based or convolutional operators (Galerkin: 3.60%, Resnet: 1.87% on Navier-Stokes), suggesting the benefit is most pronounced when the base model has fewer parameters or weaker inductive bias. On irregular domains (Table 1), NORM(DeltaPhi) outperforms all five external baselines (GraphSAGE, DeepOnet, POD-DeepOnet, FNO, NORM-Direct) across all five problems, with the most dramatic wins on Heat Transfer (50% over NORM-Direct) and Pipe Turbulence (41% over NORM-Direct). In cross-resolution zero-shot generalization (Table 4), FNO(DeltaPhi) trained at 85×85 with 900 samples (4.91e-2) surpasses FFNO(Direct) at the same resolution (4.89e-2), indicating the residual formulation can compensate for a weaker base architecture. The method also extends to Transolver and HPM on irregular domains (Appendix A.9), confirming architecture-agnostic applicability.

## Relation to Prior Work

DeltaPhi repurposes the well-established neural operator learning framework (FNO, DeepONet, GNO, NORM, etc.) by reformulating the learning objective from direct input-to-solution mapping to residual prediction between pairs of similar physical states. Conceptually, it draws on the retrieval-augmented learning paradigm from NLP (ResMem, RALM) and residual learning from super-resolution, but departs from these by grounding the auxiliary sample selection in PDE stability (Lipschitz continuity of the solution operator) rather than arbitrary memory or resolution differences. Unlike prior residual-learning approaches for PDEs that target geometric domain deformations [52] or low-to-high resolution gaps [50], DeltaPhi exploits the intrinsic trajectory similarity structure of physical systems to create implicit data augmentation without additional data collection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method addresses the central practical bottleneck in neural operator deployment—expensive, biased, or low-resolution training data—through a mechanism that requires no architectural changes and no additional data, making it a broadly applicable drop-in enhancement. The consistent gains across seven PDEs, seven+ architectures, and multiple training scales suggest the stability-based residual formulation captures a general principle about how physical systems can be learned more efficiently. Open questions include whether more sophisticated retrieval (e.g., in latent space) or physics-aware similarity metrics could unlock larger gains for highly chaotic systems, and whether the framework extends to inverse problems or multi-physics coupling where the stability assumption may be weaker.
