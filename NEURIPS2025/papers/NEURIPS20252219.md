## Problem

Retraining a model using its own predictions alongside noisy given labels is known to improve accuracy, but prior work relies on heuristic aggregation schemes (full retraining, consensus-based retraining) without addressing the fundamental question of how to optimally combine the model's predicted labels with the provided labels. This paper derives the Bayes-optimal aggregator function that minimizes prediction error when used to retrain the model, and quantifies the gains over multiple retraining rounds.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252219/cropped_page6_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each retraining round t, the method consumes the feature vector x, the noisy label ŷ ∈ {±1}, and the model's current soft prediction y^t ∈ ℝ (a logit or linear score). It produces a soft aggregated target g_t(y^t, ŷ) ∈ [-1, 1] that serves as the training label for the next round, along with updated model parameters θ^{t+1}. In the practical deployment, the soft prediction is the unnormalized logit from a pretrained ResNet-50 body, and the aggregator is applied entry-wise across the training set before retraining the linear head or full network with binary cross-entropy loss.

## Method

The paper formulates iterative retraining as an Approximate Message Passing (AMP) iteration with Onsager (memory correction) terms, enabling exact asymptotic characterization via a deterministic state evolution recursion. By applying the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality to the state evolution, it shows the optimal aggregator is the Bayes posterior 2q_t − 1, where q_t = P(Y=1 | y^t, ŷ). For the GMM setting this yields a closed-form expression involving the label-flip probability p, class priors π_±, and the signal-to-noise ratio η_t; for the GLM setting it is expressed as a ratio of one-dimensional integrals over the link function. A practical variant (BayesMix-Simple RT) replaces the noise-model-dependent term with a single tunable constant γ, making it applicable under unknown or sample-dependent noise, and is shown to outperform full and consensus-based retraining on MedMNIST Pneumonia and Food-101 under both uniform and non-uniform label noise.

## Results

On MedMNIST Pneumonia with uniform label noise (p=0.45), BayesMix RT achieves 71.42% test accuracy after 10 retraining iterations, outperforming consensus-based RT (70.03%) and full RT (68.06%). Under non-uniform noise (p=0.45, q=0.2), BayesMix RT reaches 84.39% after 10 iterations vs. 82.85% (consensus) and 79.74% (full RT). In full-network training on Food-101 (pho vs. ramen, p=0.4), BayesMix-Simple RT achieves 70.67% after 3 iterations while both baselines actually degrade below the initial 61.13% (57.67% and 58.47% respectively). On Food-101 spaghetti bolognese vs. carbonara (p=0.45, q=0.2), BayesMix-Simple RT reaches 80.30% vs. 59.70% and 56.50% for the two baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two retraining baselines from Das et al. (ICML 2025): Full RT (retrain using only the model's predicted hard labels, discarding noisy given labels) and Consensus-based RT (retrain only on samples where the predicted label agrees with the given label). In linear probing on MedMNIST Pneumonia, BayesMix RT wins at 10 iterations under both uniform (71.42% vs. 70.03% and 68.06%) and non-uniform noise (84.39% vs. 82.85% and 79.74%), though consensus-based RT edges ahead at iteration 1 under uniform noise. In full-network training on Food-101, the advantage is dramatic: BayesMix-Simple RT improves accuracy by 9.5–14.9 points over the initial model, while both Full RT and Consensus-based RT actually degrade performance by 2.7–9.0 points, indicating that naive retraining strategies can be harmful when the initial model is already reasonably good. The gap is largest under non-uniform noise, where BayesMix-Simple RT achieves 80.30% versus 59.70% (consensus) and 56.50% (full RT) on the spaghetti task.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the retraining framework of Das et al. (ICML 2025), which introduced full retraining and consensus-based retraining as heuristic strategies for mitigating label noise, and on the self-distillation literature (Dong et al. 2019, Das & Sanghavi 2023, Pareek et al. 2024) that analyzed the benefits of using model predictions as training targets. The key departure is the use of Approximate Message Passing (AMP) machinery from high-dimensional statistics (Donoho et al. 2009, Rangan 2011, Javanmard & Montanari 2013) to derive the Bayes-optimal aggregator function that provably minimizes prediction error, rather than analyzing fixed heuristic choices. While Takanami et al. (2025) performed a statistical analysis of self-distillation in the asymptotic limit, they did not address the optimal combination of predictions and given labels.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to derive a principled, Bayes-optimal rule for combining model predictions and noisy labels during retraining, providing a clear theoretical target that heuristic methods approximate. The finding that retraining can degrade performance when the initial model is already strong (Proposition 3.3) is a useful caution for practitioners who assume more retraining always helps. However, the theory is restricted to binary classification with linear models (GMM/GLM) in an asymptotic regime, and the empirical validation uses only two-class subsets of small datasets with 3 runs, leaving open whether the practical BayesMix-Simple RT generalizes to multi-class settings, non-linear architectures, and larger-scale benchmarks.
