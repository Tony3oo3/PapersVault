## Problem

Local convergence, the standard framework for analyzing sparse random graph limits, captures more structural detail than message-passing GNNs can exploit (their expressivity is bounded by color refinement). No prior work provided a complete characterization of which random graphs admit reliable MPNN generalization, nor a universal random graph model for the sparse setting analogous to graphons for dense graphs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250501/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The analysis takes a random graph sequence G_t (finite undirected multigraphs on t vertices with discrete node features) and produces a limiting PMF c_{k,∞} over refinement colors (isomorphism classes of rooted trees arising from k-round color refinement). The RCM itself is parameterized by a PMF μ over S × MultiSet(S) — a type-to-neighbor-multiset distribution — and generates a random graph by independently running configuration models within each type and bipartite configuration models between types. The key output is a complete equivalence: G_t is color convergent if and only if it satisfies probabilistic consistency of empirical risk for all k-layer MPNNs.

## Method

The paper introduces color convergence, a strict relaxation of local convergence defined via convergence of the empirical distribution of refinement colors cr_k(v) rather than full rooted-neighborhood distributions. The central theorem establishes that color convergence is equivalent to probabilistic consistency of the empirical risk for MPNN node classification, with the key insight that color refinement acts as a maximally discriminative MPNN: any non-vanishing generalization gap identifies a refinement color on which the discrepancy concentrates. The Refined Configuration Model (RCM) generalizes the configuration model by assigning each node a type s and a multiset A of neighbor types, then independently generating configuration models on same-type nodes and bipartite configuration models between distinct types. Under an involution-invariance-with-finite-degree condition on μ, the RCM converges locally to a Galton-Watson tree whose offspring distributions are explicitly determined by μ. The RCM is universal: for any sofic PMF ν on C_k, there exists an involution-invariant μ such that RCM_t(μ) has c_{k,∞} = ν, and every GWT arising as a local limit of some random graph is representable as the local limit of an RCM.

## Results

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical experiments or quantitative benchmarks. The headline results are: (1) Theorem 3.6 establishes that C_k-convergence is equivalent to probabilistic consistency of empirical risk for k-layer MPNNs, providing a complete characterization of MPNN learnability on random graphs; (2) Corollary 4.5 shows the Refined Configuration Model is universal with respect to color convergence, meaning any sofic PMF over refinement colors can be realized as the color limit of an RCM; (3) Theorem 4.7 characterizes exactly which Galton-Watson trees arise as local limits of random graphs — precisely those representable as simplified unimodular GWTs, which are in bijection with involution-invariant PMFs of finite degree. The paper also proves (Corollary 3.15) that color-convergent random graphs must be sparse, with E[|E(G_t)|] = O(t).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not perform empirical comparisons against baseline methods. Instead, it provides theoretical comparisons between notions: color convergence is shown to be a strict relaxation of local convergence (Proposition 3.9: every B_k-convergent graph is C_k-convergent, but Remark 3.10 gives a counterexample in the reverse direction). For locally tree-like graphs, however, the two notions are equivalent (Proposition 3.11). The RCM is shown to subsume the classical configuration model, bipartite configuration model, sparse Erdős-Rényi, and stochastic block models as special cases, making it a universal random graph model for the class of locally tree-like, color-convergent sequences. The paper also contrasts its framework with prior MPNN generalization analyses (VC dimension, Rademacher complexity, PAC-Bayesian), noting those works fix a specific graph distribution and are largely restricted to dense settings, whereas the present work gives a distribution-agnostic characterization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) local convergence of sparse random graphs (Benjamini & Schramm, 2011; Van Der Hofstad, 2024), which the authors relax to a coarser notion—color convergence—defined via the Weisfeiler-Leman color refinement procedure; (2) the expressivity theory of MPNNs, where the WL bound (Xu et al., 2019; Morris et al., 2019) is elevated from a per-graph discrimination statement to a limit-theoretic characterization of learnability; and (3) the configuration model (Bollobás, 1980), which is generalized into the Refined Configuration Model by allowing type-dependent neighbor multisets and independent per-type stub matching. The key conceptual departure is that local convergence, while sufficient for MPNN generalization, is not necessary—color convergence is the exact right granularity, and the RCM provides a universal generative model at that granularity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper delivers a clean, complete characterization: a random graph sequence is MPNN-learnable (in the probabilistic-consistency sense) if and only if it is color convergent, and the RCM is universal among such sequences. This resolves a gap in the sparse graph limits literature where no universal limit object was known, and it explains why dense random graphs with growing average degree are fundamentally incompatible with MPNN learning. Limitations are acknowledged: the framework is bounded by WL expressivity (higher-order GNNs would require higher-dimensional WL limits), no convergence rates or sample-complexity bounds are provided, and the RCM's practical utility as a data-generating model remains unexplored. Open questions include whether the framework extends to higher-order WL variants and whether natural learning algorithms exist for the RCM's parameters.
