## Problem

Modeling the inherent hierarchical structure of 3D scenes (e.g., parts within objects, objects within rooms) with implicit representations like NeRF remains largely unexplored. Existing hierarchical approaches either require multiple rendering passes to capture embeddings at different granularities, inflating inference cost, or depend on a fixed number of predefined discrete levels that generalize poorly to the diverse, multi-scale structures encountered in real-world scenes.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a set of multi-view RGB images with associated camera intrinsics/extrinsics, from which Semantic SAM extracts object masks and CLIP (ViT-B/16) produces language-aligned features per mask. At query time, a free-form text prompt is encoded and matched against the rendered features. The NeRF outputs per-pixel hyperbolic embeddings on the boundary of the Lorentz hyperboloid alongside color and density; these are traversed along a geodesic path toward the origin to yield a continuous set of T language-aligned features per pixel, which are aggregated into a single relevance score producing a binary segmentation mask and a localization prediction.

## Method

OpenHype embeds scene hierarchies in the Lorentz model of hyperbolic space, exploiting its exponential volume growth to represent tree-like structures with minimal distortion. A symmetric autoencoder (5-layer encoder/decoder, GELU activations) maps CLIP features and parent-child relationships into a 32-dimensional hyperbolic latent, trained with a contrastive loss on geodesic distance and exterior angle plus an MSE reconstruction term. A Nerfacto-based NeRF is then supervised to predict these hyperbolic features (extrapolated to the hyperboloid boundary for cross-view consistency) using a geodesic-distance loss with a norm-matching regularizer. At inference, a single rendering pass produces boundary features; the geodesic path from each feature to the origin is sampled at T equidistant points, decoded back to CLIP space, and scored against the text prompt. A softmax-weighted sum aggregates the T per-level similarity scores into one pixel score, enabling continuous, level-agnostic hierarchy traversal without multiple renders or a fixed number of discrete scales.

## Results

On the Search3D hierarchical benchmark (ScanNet++), OpenHype achieves 51.4 mIoU and 89.7% accuracy on object queries, and 19.9 mIoU and 50.8% accuracy on part queries, yielding an average of 35.1 mIoU and 69.5% accuracy. On the standard LERF dataset, OpenHype reaches 54.6 average mIoU across four scenes, marginally surpassing N2F2 (54.4) and LangSplat (51.4). The largest gains appear on part-level compositional queries, where baselines drop to single-digit mIoU (e.g., LERF at 6.2) while OpenHype maintains 19.9 mIoU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OpenHype outperforms all baselines on both the Search3D hierarchical benchmark and the LERF standard benchmark. Against LERF [15], the gap is most dramatic on part queries: OpenHype achieves 19.9 mIoU versus LERF's 6.2, a +13.7 mIoU improvement, while also gaining +26.0 points in localization accuracy (50.8% vs 26.6%). OpenNeRF [7] is similarly outperformed, with OpenHype reaching 19.9 vs 9.7 mIoU on parts. LangSplat [35], the strongest baseline, is beaten by +8.9 mIoU on parts and +31.5 points in part accuracy (50.8% vs 19.3%), though LangSplat retains a slight edge on object accuracy in some individual scenes. On the LERF dataset, OpenHype (54.6 avg mIoU) narrowly edges out N2F2 [1] (54.4) and LangSplat (51.4), while substantially outperforming LERF (37.4) and OpenNeRF (39.0). Among additional Gaussian-splatting baselines in the supplementary (LEGau 27.6, GOI 45.9, OpenGaussian 47.3), OpenHype leads by 3.2–27.0 mIoU on average.

## Relation to Prior Work

OpenHype extends the line of open-vocabulary NeRF segmentation (LERF, OpenNeRF) by replacing their flat or discrete multi-scale feature fields with a single continuous hyperbolic embedding. It draws on the tangent-space-at-origin formulation from Desai et al. (Hyperbolic Image-Text Representations, ICML 2023) and the pixel-wise CLIP distillation design of OpenNeRF, but departs from all prior hierarchical approaches (LERF's scale-conditioned rendering, LangSplat's three fixed SAM levels, N2F2's Matryoshka-style discrete levels) by encoding the entire hierarchy along a single geodesic path in the Lorentz model, enabling arbitrary-granularity traversal with one rendering pass.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The continuous hyperbolic hierarchy removes the need for multiple rendering passes or fixed discrete levels, making it adaptable to scenes with arbitrary depth of part decomposition. However, the approach inherits dependencies on 2D segmentation quality (Semantic SAM) and CLIP's open-vocabulary coverage, and the authors note residual noise along geodesic paths that could degrade semantic coherence. Open questions include whether the hyperbolic VAE direction they suggest can smooth latent interpolation, how the method extends to 3D Gaussian Splatting (which they note is agnostic to the radiance field representation), and whether uncertainty-aware supervision can mitigate multi-view feature inconsistencies.
