## Problem

3D inverse design requires simultaneously optimizing geometry and its physical response in an exponentially large, nonconvex space, yet existing methods either reduce the problem to 2D projections (losing volumetric detail) or require a predefined initial shape for local refinement (constraining exploration). No prior framework directly navigates the full 3D physics-geometry-coupled design space from random initialization while balancing global exploration with geometric validity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253334/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method starts from pure Gaussian noise in a learned latent space and produces a watertight 3D mesh with associated velocity and pressure fields. Internally, the PG-VAE consumes 50,000 uniformly sampled surface points (with normals) and 50,000 volumetric physical-field points per sample, encoding them into a compact triplane latent (64×64×32 per plane, upsampled to 256×256×64). The final mesh is validated via high-fidelity steady-state CFD (OpenFOAM, k-ω SST, up to 50M cells), and the FFD refinement operates on a 20×6×6 control-point grid to preserve topology.

## Method

3DID introduces a Physics-Geometry VAE (PG-VAE) that jointly encodes 3D geometry and physical fields into a unified triplane latent using parallel transformer branches with learnable tokens and cross-attention, followed by a two-stage optimization pipeline. Stage 1 performs gradient-guided diffusion sampling with a 10-block DiT model, injecting the objective gradient into the denoising score via a Bayes' rule decomposition (conditional score = data score + objective gradient), steering the trajectory toward high-performance latent regions. Stage 2 applies topology-preserving refinement via Free-Form Deformation using trivariate Bernstein basis functions on a control-point lattice, where a MeshGraphNet surrogate provides differentiable drag predictions and the loss includes smoothness and volume-preservation penalties. The key architectural insight is that the unified physics-geometry embedding carries field information through both stages, enabling physics-aware gradients during diffusion and providing the GNN surrogate with geometric context during refinement, whereas geometry-only representations (e.g., TripNet) yield weaker optimization signals.

## Results

On the DrivAerNet++ aerodynamic vehicle design task, 3DID achieves the best simulated drag coefficient (Sim-Drag) of 0.3536 and predicted drag (Pred-Drag) of 0.2607, reducing Sim-Drag by 13.6% relative to the strongest baseline (CEM with Voxel, 0.4097). It also attains the highest novelty score (1.1709), indicating the most distinct generated designs from the training set. The trade-off is a lower coverage score (0.4300 vs. 0.6950 for 3DID without topology refinement), which the authors attribute to the refinement stage pushing designs beyond the training distribution to achieve superior aerodynamic performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

3DID outperforms all eight baseline configurations (CEM/GP/Backprop × Voxel/Voxel+PCA/TripNet) on both Pred-Drag and Sim-Drag, with the largest gap over CEM+Voxel (Sim-Drag 0.3536 vs. 0.4097, a 13.7% reduction) and Backprop+Voxel+PCA (0.3536 vs. 0.4614, a 23.4% reduction). On novelty, 3DID (1.1709) surpasses every baseline, with the closest competitor being CEM+Voxel and GP+Voxel at 1.0399. The sole metric where 3DID underperforms is coverage: its full pipeline scores 0.4300, below CEM+Voxel (0.4350) and far below 3DID without topology refinement (0.6950) or CEM+TripNet (0.6050). The authors explain this coverage drop as a deliberate consequence of the topology-preserving refinement stage pushing designs beyond the training manifold to achieve lower drag. Among the three optimizer families, CEM+Voxel is the strongest single baseline on Sim-Drag (0.4097), while GP and Backprop variants cluster between 0.4146 and 0.4614, with Backprop+Voxel+PCA being the worst overall.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the diffusion posterior sampling framework (Chung et al., 2022; Bansal et al., 2023) to inject task-objective gradients into the denoising process, and on triplane latent representations (Wang et al., 2023) for compact 3D geometry encoding. It departs from prior 3D inverse design work (Tran et al., 2024; Durasov et al., 2021; Baque et al., 2018) that either relies on 2D projections or requires a pre-selected initial geometry, by generating designs directly from random noise in a unified physics-geometry latent space. The topology-preserving refinement stage repurposes classical free-form deformation (Hsu et al., 1992; Hirota et al., 1999) as a differentiable, gradient-driven optimization layer on top of the diffusion output, bridging generative exploration with local geometric refinement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for 2D proxies or seed geometries, 3DID opens a path toward fully automated 3D design in domains where expert-crafted initial shapes are a bottleneck (aerospace, biomedical implants, additive manufacturing). The unified physics-geometry latent representation is a reusable architectural contribution that could transfer to other PDE-coupled design problems. Key open questions remain: the framework is limited to static fields, single-objective optimization, and surrogate-based (rather than PDE-consistent) physics, and the coverage drop after topology-preserving refinement (0.43 vs. 0.70) suggests a tension between pushing beyond the training distribution and maintaining design validity that is not yet resolved.
