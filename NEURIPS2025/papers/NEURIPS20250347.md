## Problem

Existing zero-cost proxies (ZCPs) for training-free NAS are manually crafted through labor-intensive trial-and-error, suffer from poor correlation with final model performance, and incur high computational cost. This paper asks whether Large Language Models can automatically discover high-quality ZCPs, replacing the expert-driven design paradigm with an LLM-driven evolutionary search.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250347/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a candidate neural network architecture (from a given NAS search space such as NAS-Bench-201 or DARTS) and a small batch of 16 random input images (e.g., 32×32 CIFAR-10) as input, and produces a single deterministic scalar proxy score per architecture. Each discovered proxy is represented as a tuple of a natural-language rationale and executable PyTorch code implementing evaluate(model, inputs, targets), constrained to be training-free, input-aware, and computable as a directed acyclic graph over layer-level signals (gradients, weights, activations) with in-degree ≤ 2. The scalar scores are then used to rank all candidate architectures and select the top performer without any gradient-based training.

## Method

APD (Automatic Proxy Discovery) integrates three components into an evolutionary loop: (1) a Proxy Candidate Generator that uses an LLM with structured prompts to synthesize new ZCPs via initialization, mutation, or crossover operations over a population of candidate proxies; (2) a Fitness Evaluator that scores each proxy by its Spearman correlation with ground-truth accuracies on the NAS benchmark, penalized by a runtime cost term; and (3) an Actor-Critic RL scheduler that observes a compact state (fitness histogram, strategy history) and learns a categorical policy over evolutionary operations, receiving the mean fitness as reward. The key insight is that naive single-prompt LLM generation yields only ~60% Spearman correlation, but the RL-guided evolutionary loop—providing iterative feedback and enabling Chain-of-Thought-like reasoning over proxy structure—pushes correlation above 80% within ~30 generations on a single GPU, while the discovered proxies (e.g., combining BatchNorm L2-norm ratios with convolutional L1/L2 weight ratios) remain interpretable and require no human expertise.

## Results

On NAS-Bench-201, APD achieves 93.76% test accuracy on CIFAR-10, 72.22% on CIFAR-100, and 45.03% on ImageNet-16-120, setting new state-of-the-art among training-free NAS methods. On the DARTS search space, APD reaches 97.63% top-1 on CIFAR-10 and 76.9% top-1 on ImageNet-1k with only 0.004 GPU-days of search cost. APD also achieves the highest Spearman correlation (0.843) on CIFAR-100 (NB-201) and the fastest runtime at 16.81 ms/architecture, reducing evaluation cost by over 50% compared to prior methods like AZ-NAS (69.43 ms) and ZiCo (75.50 ms).

## Baselines & Comparisons

APD outperforms all training-free NAS baselines across the majority of benchmarks. On NAS-Bench-201 CIFAR-10, APD (93.76%) surpasses ZiCo (93.69%) by +0.07%, SynFlow (93.67%) by +0.09%, and AZ-NAS (93.49%) by +0.27%; on CIFAR-100 it leads SynFlow (71.70%) by +0.52% and ZiCo (70.63%) by +1.59%; on ImageNet-16-120 it beats AZ-NAS (44.34%) by +0.69% and SynFlow (43.39%) by +1.64%. On the DARTS search space, APD achieves 97.63% on CIFAR-10, exceeding SWAP (97.52%) and DARTS+ (97.50%), and 76.9% on ImageNet-1k, surpassing SWAP (76.0%) and DARTS+ (76.3%). On TransNAS-Bench-101-Micro, APD leads in Autoencoding SSIM (0.54 vs. ZiCo 0.48) and Scene Classification (54.0% vs. ZiCo 53.7%), but trails ZiCo on the Jigsaw task (91.2% vs. 93.2%). On OoD-ViT-NAS-Ti, APD achieves the highest Spearman correlation on ImageNet-R (0.88 vs. AutoProx 0.78, DSS 0.81) and ImageNet-1k (0.79 vs. NWOT 0.75), confirming strong generalization under distribution shift. Notably, APD's runtime of 16.81 ms/arch is roughly 4× faster than AZ-NAS and 4.5× faster than ZiCo, making it the most efficient method evaluated.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the training-free NAS paradigm established by NWOT, SynFlow, and subsequent ZCP-based methods (ZiCo, AZ-NAS, SWAP), which all rely on manually designed proxies to rank architectures at initialization. It departs from this manual-design lineage by repurposing LLMs as generative engines for ZCPs, drawing on the broader LLM-for-code and LLM-for-optimization literature, and introduces an actor-critic RL loop (inspired by CoT reasoning in GPT-4o/DeepSeek-R1) to iteratively refine proxy candidates via mutation and crossover. Conceptually, it is closest to AutoProx (Wei et al., 2024) and EZNAS (Akhauri et al., 2022), which also automate proxy discovery, but differs in using LLMs as the search operator rather than gradient-based or evolutionary heuristics over a fixed proxy space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

If LLM-driven proxy discovery proves reliable and transferable, it could substantially lower the expert-knowledge barrier in training-free NAS and offer a reusable template for LLM-guided algorithm design in other ML subfields. However, several open questions remain: the discovered proxies are validated primarily on small-scale benchmarks (NAS-Bench-201, DARTS cells), and it is unclear whether the approach scales to large supernet search spaces or maintains correlation under significant distribution shift. The method's dependence on LLM quality and API access also raises reproducibility and cost concerns that the paper does not fully address.
