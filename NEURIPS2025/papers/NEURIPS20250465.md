## Problem

Adaptive optimizers like Adam are highly sensitive to the learning rate hyperparameter, particularly when training Transformers where loss spikes necessitate costly manual tuning. The NGN step-size method demonstrated strong LR robustness and non-divergence guarantees, but it lacked momentum and coordinate-wise adaptation—both essential for modern deep learning workloads. This paper addresses how to incorporate momentum and diagonal preconditioning into the NGN step-size while preserving (and ideally enhancing) its robustness properties under minimal theoretical assumptions.

## Input / Output

At each iteration, the algorithm consumes the current model parameters x^k, a mini-batch stochastic gradient ∇f_{S_k}(x^k), and the corresponding stochastic loss value f_{S_k}(x^k) to compute an adaptive step-size γ_k that scales the update. For the diagonal variant (NGN-MD), it additionally maintains a per-coordinate RMSprop-style second-moment vector v^k to construct a diagonal preconditioner D_k. The output is the updated parameter vector x^{k+1}; the only user-facing hyperparameters are the step-size constant c, momentum coefficient β (or β_1, β_2 for the diagonal variant), and a stabilization ε—matching Adam's hyperparameter count.

## Method

The core algorithm NGN-M computes the NGN step-size γ_k = c / (1 + (c / 2f_{S_k}(x^k))‖∇f_{S_k}(x^k)‖²) first, then applies it to the gradient before feeding into a heavy-ball momentum average: m^k = βm^{k-1} + (1-β)γ_k∇f_{S_k}(x^k), x^{k+1} = x^k - m^k. This specific ordering (step-size before momentum, 'Version 1') is shown empirically and theoretically to preserve NGN's non-divergence property while achieving O(1/√K) convergence in the convex regime without interpolation or bounded-gradient assumptions. The diagonal extension NGN-MDv1 applies a shared NGN step-size scaled by an RMSprop diagonal preconditioner D_k^{-1}, while NGN-MDv2 computes a fully per-coordinate step-size γ_k^{(j)} using each parameter's own partial derivative. The theoretical analysis uses an Iterative Moving Average reformulation of momentum and a decomposition of the step-size into a fixed O(c) descent term and an O(c²) error term to balance convergence and noise.

## Results

The paper's primary metric is LR sensitivity (lower = more robust). NGN-M achieves the lowest LR sensitivity across all vision benchmarks: 12 (ResNet20/CIFAR-10), 24 (ResNet110/CIFAR-10), and 1 (ViT/CIFAR-10), compared to SGDM (26/36/28), Momo (20/31/31), and NGN (18/31/27). For the diagonal variant, NGN-MDv1 achieves LR sensitivity of 13/27/6, outperforming Adam (37/32/41) and Momo-Adam (27/32/21). Theoretically, NGN-M attains the standard O(1/√K) convergence rate in the convex regime without requiring interpolation, bounded stochastic gradients, or bounded iterates—assumptions that prior Polyak-step-size methods necessitate.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NGN-M dominates all momentum-based baselines on LR sensitivity: it reduces sensitivity by 33% vs NGN (12 vs 18 on ResNet20), by 40% vs Momo (12 vs 20), and by 54% vs SGDM (12 vs 26) on CIFAR-10. On ViT/CIFAR-10, NGN-M achieves an LR sensitivity of 1, meaning it is essentially insensitive to the LR hyperparameter, while SGDM (28), Momo (31), and NGN (27) all degrade significantly outside a narrow LR window. For the diagonal variant, NGN-MDv1 matches Adam's peak test accuracy across all model sizes (70M–1B language models) but maintains competitive performance even at LR c=10⁻², where Adam's performance drops substantially. Compared to Momo-Adam, NGN-MDv1 achieves lower LR sensitivity (6 vs 21 on ViT/CIFAR-10) while matching or exceeding peak accuracy. Theoretically, NGN-M improves upon MomSPS [63] and ALR-SMAG [93] by converging to the exact solution (not a non-vanishing neighborhood) and by not requiring the interpolation condition or bounded gradient assumptions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the NGN (Non-negative Gauss-Newton) step-size of Orvieto and Xiao [66] by incorporating heavy-ball momentum (NGN-M) and coordinate-wise diagonal preconditioning (NGN-MD), filling gaps explicitly left open in the original NGN formulation. It sits within the broader Polyak step-size lineage [54, 67, 63, 79, 93], but departs from prior Polyak+momentum combinations (e.g., Momo [79], MomSPS [63]) by leveraging the Gauss-Newton-derived NGN step-size, which provides a non-divergence guarantee for any LR hyperparameter. The diagonal variant NGN-D is positioned as the first per-parameter Polyak-type step-size achieving the standard O(1/√K) rate under smoothness and bounded variance assumptions without requiring interpolation or bounded gradients.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical relevance is significant: LR sensitivity is a major bottleneck in training Transformers and large-scale models, and NGN-MDv1 matches Adam's performance across model scales (70M–1B) while tolerating LR values 1–2 orders of magnitude larger without degradation. Key open questions include extending the convergence analysis to non-convex settings and large momentum values (β=0.9), integrating with parallelism schemes (Tensor Parallelism, ZeRO-2), and understanding the interaction with weight decay. The ~2.5× per-update overhead and the requirement to preset total iteration count K (or use a diminishing schedule) are practical limitations that may hinder immediate adoption at scale.
