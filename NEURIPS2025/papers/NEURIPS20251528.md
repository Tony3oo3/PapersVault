## Problem

Recent empirical works (HOMO, Force Matching) demonstrate that supervising on high-order trajectory derivatives (acceleration, jerk) improves generative model quality, yet no unified theoretical framework exists for flow matching with derivatives up to an arbitrary order K. In particular, the intractable K-order loss has no known tractable equivalent, the consistency constraints among velocity components are not formally characterized, and no statistical convergence rates have been established for any high-order variant.

## Input / Output

The method consumes i.i.d. data samples {x_i} from the target distribution q and draws Gaussian noise X_0 ~ N(0, I) during training, coupling each data point with noise through the affine path ψ_t(x|x_1) = μ_t x_1 + σ_t x. The output is a set of K transformer networks u^{1,θ}, …, u^{K,θ}, each mapping (x, t) → R^d, which together form the K-order velocity field f_t: R^{Kd} → R^{Kd}. At inference time, sampling proceeds via a K-th order Taylor expansion with step size h, where all K velocity components are evaluated in parallel at each step, yielding a local truncation error of O(h^{K+1}).

## Method

The paper defines a K-order velocity field f_t as the vertical concatenation of K component fields u^1, …, u^K, governed by a coupled ODE on the extended state Y_t ∈ R^{Kd} that encodes position through (K−1)-th derivative. A key technical contribution is the high-order marginalization trick (Theorem 3.3), which, under a total-derivative consistency constraint linking successive u^k components, converts the intractable marginal K-order loss into a tractable conditional regression against the k-th time derivative of the affine path ψ_t^{(k)} = μ_t^{(k)} x_1 + σ_t^{(k)} x_0. Gradient equivalence between the marginal and conditional losses is established via Bregman divergence properties, enabling standard stochastic gradient training. For statistical analysis, the paper proves approximation rates of O(N^{-2β}(log N)^{d_x/2−1}) for transformer networks, estimation rates of O(n^{-1/(10d)}(log n)^{10d_x}), and 2-Wasserstein distribution estimation rates of O(n^{-1/(18d)}(log n)^{6d_x}), showing near-minimax optimality under Hölder smoothness assumptions on the target density.

## Results

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical experiments. The key quantitative results are statistical convergence rates: the K-order velocity approximation error is O(N^{-2β} · (log N)^{d_x/2−1}) (Theorem 4.1), the velocity estimation risk is O(n^{-1/(10d)} · (log n)^{10d_x}) (Theorem 4.2), and the 2-Wasserstein distribution estimation error is O(n^{-1/(18d)} · (log n)^{6d_x}) (Theorem 4.3). The paper proves these rates are nearly minimax optimal up to logarithmic factors (Theorem 4.4), matching the established near-minimax rates of standard first-order flow matching [Jiao et al., 2024; Fukumizu et al., 2024] while extending them to arbitrary order K with transformer architectures.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper contains no empirical baselines or experimental comparisons; it is entirely a theoretical contribution. The theoretical comparison is with prior statistical analyses of flow matching: Fukumizu et al. [2024] show near-minimax-optimal W₂ rates for flow matching with ReLU networks on Besov densities, Jiao et al. [2024] derive convergence rates in latent space with transformers, and Kunkel and Trabs [2025] establish W₁ minimax rates via kernel methods. The present work extends all of these to arbitrary order K and to transformer architectures on Hölder densities in W₂, subsuming the K=1 case. The paper also provides a unifying theoretical lens for HOMO [Chen et al., 2025] (K=2 velocity + acceleration matching) and Force Matching [Cao et al., 2025] (second-order force matching), showing both are special cases of the proposed framework, though no head-to-head empirical comparison is conducted.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends standard Flow Matching (Lipman et al., 2022; Lipman et al., 2024) from first-order velocity fields to arbitrary K-order velocity fields, using the affine conditional flow structure from Rectified Flow (Liu et al., 2022) and the Bregman divergence marginalization trick from Lipman et al. (2024). It provides a unified theoretical lens under which empirically-motivated high-order methods like HOMO (Chen et al., 2025) and Force Matching (Cao et al., 2025) emerge as special cases (K=2). On the statistical side, it generalizes the near-minimax transformer rates established for first-order flow matching (Jiao et al., 2024; Fukumizu et al., 2024) to all orders K, filling a gap where high-order variants had no rigorous nonparametric guarantees.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework gives the first rigorous statistical foundation for the growing family of high-order flow matching models, and the near-minimax rates (up to log factors) in 2-Wasserstein distance confirm that transformer-based flow matching is statistically efficient across all orders. However, the paper candidly notes that increasing K does not improve the statistical rate, leaving open whether the empirical gains from high-order supervision (smoothness, stability) are purely algorithmic rather than statistical. The validity of the consistency constraint in the marginalization step (Theorem 3.3) is flagged as unresolved, and whether the K-th order Taylor expansion sampler translates into practical NFE savings remains an open empirical question.
