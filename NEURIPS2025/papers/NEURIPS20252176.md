## Problem

Estimating Expected Information Gain (EIG) in Bayesian optimal experimental design requires computing the posterior normalizing constant p(y|d) for many simulated datasets, and standard sequential Monte Carlo (SMC) estimators demand hundreds of millions of likelihood evaluations per design. The paper identifies that in BOED the variance of IG(y) across datasets dominates the within-dataset estimator variance, meaning standard SMC is 'overly precise' for individual datasets and wastes computation on unnecessary per-dataset accuracy.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a simulated joint sample (θ*, y) — where θ* is the true parameter used to generate y — along with a design parameter d and a fixed temperature sequence of N=100 levels. Because the data is simulated, θ* serves as a free posterior draw from p(θ|y,d), which is the critical input that enables the reverse trajectory. The output is a single-particle estimate of log p(y|d) via the thermodynamic integral, and the EIG for design d is computed as the average of log p(y_k|θ*_k) − log p̂(y_k|d) over K outer-loop datasets, with Monte Carlo standard errors estimated from the variance across per-temperature Simpson integrals.

## Method

RA-SMC reverses the standard SMC tempering direction: instead of evolving a population from the prior toward the posterior, it starts from the free posterior sample θ* and anneals a single particle backward through the power posterior sequence p_{t_i}(θ|y) ∝ p(y|θ)^{t_i} p(θ) from t=1 down to t=0. At each of N=100 temperature levels (spaced as t_i=(i/N)^5), the particle is rejuvenated by a random-walk Metropolis chain targeting p_{t_i}, and the log-likelihood log p(y|θ) is recorded. The log marginal likelihood is then estimated via the thermodynamic integral (Gelman & Meng, 1998) approximated with composite Simpson's rule over the recorded log-likelihoods, which directly targets log p(y) and mitigates the bias inherent in reverse-direction estimators. The key theoretical insight is that Var(IG(y)|y) ≪ Var(IG(y)) across datasets, so a single particle per temperature — which inflates the within-dataset variance by roughly an order of magnitude — still yields EIG estimates with comparable total Monte Carlo error to 20-particle forward SMC, at roughly fourfold lower computational cost. The approach is a single-particle, zero-resample modification of the bidirectional Monte Carlo framework of Grosse et al. (2015), omitting the forward pass and bounding machinery while retaining the free posterior initialization and reverse annealing.

## Results

On the source-finding problem (15 sequential steps), RA-SMC requires 8.4×10⁹ likelihood evaluations, compared to 3.7×10¹⁰ for 50-particle forward SMC and 4.8×10¹⁰ for 100-particle forward SMC, while matching the KL-divergence convergence of the 100-particle baseline. On the spring-mass and Johnson-Cook models, reducing forward SMC from 250 to 20 particles yields ~10× fewer evaluations with minimal EIG loss, and RA-SMC achieves a further ~4× reduction over 20-particle forward SMC. The inner-loop estimator variance Var(IG(y)|y) is two orders of magnitude smaller than the outer-loop variance Var(IG(y))=22.4, justifying the aggressive particle reduction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against forward SMC (Catanach & Beck, 2018), RA-SMC achieves comparable EIG accuracy at a fraction of the cost: on the spring-mass model, 20-particle forward SMC already matches 250-particle performance, and RA-SMC requires another ~4× fewer likelihood evaluations than that 20-particle baseline. On the source-finding problem, RA-SMC (8.4×10⁹ evaluations) matches the KL-divergence convergence of 100-particle forward SMC (4.8×10¹⁰) while using only ~17% of the evaluations, and substantially outperforms 50-particle forward SMC (3.7×10¹⁰) which produces erratic sensor placements. Against Nested Monte Carlo (Ryan, 2003), RA-SMC is clearly superior: NMC with the same computational budget as 20-particle forward SMC produces inaccurate EIG estimates with large standard errors and fails to locate the optimal design, because its prior-based sampling cannot adequately explore the sharply peaked posterior.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the reverse-annealing pass from Bidirectional Monte Carlo (Grosse et al., 2015), which originally 'sandwiched' the marginal likelihood between stochastic upper and lower bounds, into a practical single-particle EIG estimator for Bayesian optimal experimental design. The estimator is structured as a zero-resample, single-particle instance of the SMC tempering framework (Del Moral et al., 2006), but replaces the stepping-stone product with the thermodynamic integral (Gelman & Meng, 1998) to reduce bias. The key conceptual departure from both BDMC and standard forward SMC is the observation that in BOED, the variance of IG across simulated datasets dominates the within-dataset estimation variance, making the high-precision multi-particle estimates of standard SMC wasteful for the EIG objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RA-SMC achieves orders-of-magnitude reductions in likelihood evaluations (roughly 4× beyond what is already gained by reducing forward SMC from 250 to 20 particles), making tempering-based EIG estimation feasible for problems where hundreds of millions of evaluations were previously required. However, the method's practical utility is currently limited by ad hoc tuning of MCMC iterations, the absence of a robust diagnostic for when single-particle reverse annealing is appropriate, and the fact that all experiments are in 4–6 dimensions with synthetic data. Open questions include scalability to higher-dimensional posteriors, integration with more sophisticated MCMC kernels (HMC, NUTS), and extension to real-data settings where the 'free posterior sample' from simulation is unavailable.
