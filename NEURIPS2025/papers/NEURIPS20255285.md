## Problem

Existing minimax-optimal private samplers (Park et al., NeurIPS'24) characterize the worst-case distribution over an entire class, yielding pessimistic guarantees that rarely reflect real-world data. This paper asks whether one can obtain tighter, distribution-specific guarantees by restricting the worst-case search to a neighborhood around a known reference distribution P₀ (e.g., public data), and whether the resulting local minimax risk admits a closed-form characterization.

## Input / Output

The sampler takes a client distribution P (modeled as an empirical distribution over a sample space X), a public reference distribution P₀, a privacy parameter (ε for pure LDP or ν for Gaussian LDP), and a neighborhood radius γ ≥ 1 that defines the set N_γ(P₀) via the E_γ (hockey-stick) divergence. The output is a single sample drawn from a privatized distribution Q(P) that satisfies the chosen LDP guarantee while minimizing f-divergence from P. The neighborhood constraint Q(x) ≤ γP₀(x) and P₀(x) ≤ γQ(x) for all x makes this a strict likelihood-ratio bound, tighter than total variation.

## Method

The paper first extends the global minimax framework of Park et al. from pure LDP to the general functional LDP (g-FLDP) setting, deriving minimax-optimal samplers for continuous, discrete, and general decomposable sample spaces. The key structural result is that the local minimax risk over N_γ(P₀) equals the global minimax risk when the distribution class is restricted to that neighborhood, with the optimal sampler given by a convex mixture λ·P + (1−λ)·P₀ where λ is determined by the privacy parameter and γ. For pure LDP specifically, the paper further constructs a non-linear sampler that clips the density p(x)/r_P to the interval [(γ+1)/(γ+e^ε)·p₀(x), (γ+1)e^ε/(γ+e^ε)·p₀(x)], which is pointwise better than the linear mixture sampler because it is instance-optimal (minimizing f-divergence for each individual P) rather than using a fixed transformation. All optimal samplers are independent of the choice of f-divergence, and numerical experiments on finite and continuous (Laplace mixture) domains confirm consistent improvements over global minimax baselines.

## Results

The paper's primary contribution is theoretical: it characterizes the exact local minimax risk and derives closed-form locally optimal samplers under functional LDP, pure LDP, and ν-GLDP. Numerical validation (Section 6) confirms that the local minimax sampler consistently achieves lower worst-case f-divergence than the global minimax sampler of Park et al. [17] across all tested privacy levels (ε ∈ {0.1, 0.5, 1, 2}) and all f-divergences (KL, total variation, squared Hellinger). In the finite setting (k=20, γ=9), both risks are available in closed form and the local advantage is uniform. In the continuous setting (1-D Laplace mixtures, 100 random client distributions), the non-linear clipping sampler from Theorem 5.1 outperforms the global linear sampler from [17, Theorem 3.3] across all ε values, with the gap widening at smaller ε.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is the global minimax-optimal sampler of Park et al. [17] (NeurIPS 2024), which minimizes worst-case f-divergence over the entire distribution class P̃. The paper's local minimax sampler (Theorem 5.1, a non-linear clipping sampler) consistently and often substantially outperforms this global baseline in both the finite (k=20) and continuous (1-D Laplace mixture) settings, across all ε ∈ {0.1, 0.5, 1, 2} and all three f-divergences evaluated. The advantage is most pronounced at low privacy budgets (ε=0.1), where the global worst-case is most pessimistic relative to the local neighborhood. Additionally, the paper shows that the non-linear (clipping) local sampler is pointwise better than the linear local sampler obtained by directly instantiating the global solution (Theorem 4.1 with g=g_ε), confirming that instance-specific optimization via projection onto the mollifier M_ε yields strictly lower f-divergence for every input distribution P in the neighborhood.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the global minimax framework for locally private sampling established by Park et al. (NeurIPS'24) and Husain et al. (AISTATS'20) by introducing a local minimax formulation that restricts the worst-case analysis to a neighborhood around a fixed reference distribution P₀. It generalizes the pure-LDP setting to the broader functional LDP framework (encompassing approximate and Gaussian LDP) following Dong et al., and connects to the instance-optimality literature on private density estimation (Feldman et al., McMillan et al.) through its use of E_γ-divergence neighborhoods. The paper also distinguishes itself from Zamanlooy et al.'s concurrent work on sampling with public data by using the public distribution to define a local neighborhood rather than imposing it as a hard constraint, and by supporting both discrete and continuous domains with arbitrary (non-linear) samplers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The local minimax perspective is more realistic than the pessimistic global worst-case, since real-world data distributions are rarely adversarial, and the public-data interpretation makes the framework directly relevant to personalized federated learning where a shared (privatized) reference distribution is available. The clean theoretical result that the local minimax risk is exactly determined by the global minimax risk on a restricted class provides a principled recipe for constructing optimal samplers without re-solving the optimization from scratch. Key limitations include the computational complexity of the clipping-based non-linear sampler in high dimensions, validation only on synthetic 1-D Laplace mixtures, and restriction to single-sample release per client. Open questions include generalizing the neighborhood to arbitrary f-divergences, extending to multi-sample settings, and developing scalable implementations for high-dimensional real-world data.
