## Problem

Existing 3D relighting methods either rely on inverse rendering, which is brittle to model mismatches and requires computationally expensive per-illumination optimization, or use diffusion-based relighting that produces view-inconsistent results and demands re-optimizing a 3D representation for every new lighting condition. No prior approach enables fast, feed-forward relighting of a reconstructed 3D object under arbitrary novel environment maps without additional optimization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251443/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes N posed RGB images (512×512) of an object captured under a single unknown illumination, along with a target equirectangular environment map (512×512) specifying the desired lighting. The environment map is encoded in both HDR (logarithmic tone-mapped) and LDR (standard tone-mapped) latents and rotated to align with each camera pose before being fed to the diffusion model. The output is a relightable NeRF that renders novel views under any environment map in 0.384 seconds per frame, with the geometry remaining constant across all lighting conditions.

## Method

ROGR uses a two-stage pipeline: first, a multi-view relighting diffusion model (fine-tuned from a pretrained LDM using the CAT3D architecture) jointly denoises 64 views under a target environment map to produce view-consistent relit images, generating a dataset of N×M images across M=111 environment maps. Second, a lighting-conditioned NeRF based on NeRF-Casting is trained on this multi-illumination dataset using a dual-branch conditioning scheme: a general conditioning branch (a ViT-S8 transformer encoder mapping the full environment map to a 128-dimensional embedding) captures low-frequency diffuse lighting, while a specular conditioning branch queries the environment map at the reflection direction using multi-scale Gaussian blur kernels (σ=10, 20) to encode incident light for different surface roughnesses. This architecture enables the NeRF to generalize to unseen environment maps at inference time, achieving feed-forward relighting without per-illumination optimization or Monte Carlo light transport simulation.

## Results

On the TensoIR synthetic benchmark (4,000 renderings across 4 objects × 5 novel lightings × 200 views), ROGR achieves PSNR 30.74, SSIM 0.950, and LPIPS 0.069, outperforming all baselines on every metric. On the real-world Stanford-ORB benchmark (840 renderings across 14 objects × 3 lightings), ROGR achieves PSNR-H 26.21 and SSIM 0.980 (best), while Neural-PBIR edges ahead on PSNR-L (33.26 vs. 32.91) and LPIPS (0.023 vs. 0.027). The method renders at 0.384 seconds per frame, enabling interactive feed-forward relighting without per-illumination optimization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ROGR outperforms all 12 baselines on the TensoIR benchmark across PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS, with the closest competitor being IllumiNeRF (PSNR 29.71 vs. 30.74, a ~1 dB gap). On Stanford-ORB, ROGR wins on PSNR-H (26.21 vs. 26.01 for Neural-PBIR) and SSIM (0.980 vs. 0.979), but Neural-PBIR achieves slightly better PSNR-L (33.26 vs. 32.91) and LPIPS (0.023 vs. 0.027); the authors attribute this to Neural-PBIR's mostly-diffuse renderings being easier to match in LDR space. Among inverse-rendering methods, ROGR surpasses the strongest (Neural-PBIR, NVDiffRecMC) by 0.2–1.8 dB in PSNR-H on Stanford-ORB. Among generative/diffusion-based relighting methods, ROGR beats IllumiNeRF by ~1 dB PSNR on TensoIR and ~0.65 dB PSNR-H on Stanford-ORB, while also eliminating the need for per-illumination NeRF retraining that both IllumiNeRF and Neural Gaffer require. The Gaussian-splatting-based R3DG is competitive in speed (0.415 s/frame) but lags significantly in quality (PSNR 29.05 on TensoIR, 21.25 PSNR-H on Stanford-ORB).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two lines of work: inverse rendering for relightable 3D representations (NeRFactor, TensoIR, Neural-PBIR) and diffusion-based image relighting (Neural Gaffer, IllumiNeRF). It builds directly on NeRF-Casting for the base radiance field architecture and CAT3D for the multi-view diffusion backbone, while adapting Neural Gaffer's dual HDR/LDR environment map encoding. The key departure is that prior diffusion-based relighting methods (IllumiNeRF, Neural Gaffer) require per-illumination 3D optimization, whereas this paper distills multi-view diffusion samples into a single feed-forward NeRF with a novel dual-branch conditioning (general transformer embedding + multi-scale specular probes) that generalizes to unseen environment maps at inference without retraining.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The feed-forward relighting capability (0.384s/frame) removes the per-illumination optimization bottleneck that has limited prior generative relighting to offline use, making interactive applications in film, gaming, and AR feasible. The ablation showing that per-image appearance embeddings (NeRF-in-the-Wild style) collapse to 19.12 PSNR versus 31.88 for the learned environment map encoder underscores that generalization to novel lighting requires a structured, input-conditioned representation rather than per-sample codes. Open questions include extending to complex materials (subsurface scattering, refraction), near-field illumination, and scene-scale relighting, all of which the authors identify as limitations.
