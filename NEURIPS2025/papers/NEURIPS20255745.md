## Problem

Existing model merging methods either minimize parameter-level differences (yielding a significant gap to the individual-expert upper bound) or require costly secondary training to optimize merging weights. The paper identifies that performance degradation in merged models closely correlates with feature drift—the divergence in intermediate representations of the same sample between task-specific experts and the merged model—and that this drift amplifies progressively through network depth, motivating a layer-wise approach to explicitly minimize it.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-trained model (e.g., CLIP ViT-B/32, ViT-L/14, or BLIP), the task vectors (parameter differences between each fine-tuned expert and the pre-trained model), and a small exemplar set of 16–64 samples per task used solely for forward passes to extract per-layer input feature matrices. It outputs a single merged multi-task model whose architecture is unchanged from the pre-trained model, with no auxiliary modules, adapters, or structural modifications introduced. The entire procedure is training-free, requiring only a handful of forward passes and basic matrix operations (Moore-Penrose inverses, SVD) rather than any gradient-based optimization.

## Method

LOT Merging formulates the merging problem as a convex quadratic optimization that minimizes the sum of squared feature drift across all tasks at each layer independently, yielding closed-form solutions for three operation types: matrix multiplication (linear weights), Hadamard product (normalization scaling), and element-wise addition (biases). For linear layers, the optimal task vector is T* = (Σ_k X_k^T X_k)† Σ_k X_k^T X_k T_k, where X_k are the input features from the exemplar set; for scaling factors, a per-dimension weighted average is computed; for biases, a simple arithmetic mean suffices. A critical design choice is operating on task vectors rather than raw parameters (as in RegMean), which avoids the rank-deficient projection that would discard pre-trained knowledge when exemplar sets are small. SVD-based analysis of two extreme cases—orthogonal task feature subspaces (ideal) and fully collinear subspaces (worst)—reveals that LOT Merging adaptively projects task-specific transformations into relevant subspaces while aggregating shared patterns, explaining its robustness across diverse task configurations.

## Results

On eight vision classification tasks with ViT-B/32, LOT Merging achieves 82.7% average accuracy, outperforming the second-best training-free method (CAT Merging, 78.3%) by 4.4% and the upper bound of Individual fine-tuned models (90.5%) by only 7.8%. With ViT-L/14, LOT Merging reaches 90.5% average accuracy (vs. 89.6% for CAT Merging), winning 7 out of 8 individual tasks. On six vision-language tasks with BLIP, LOT Merging delivers the best performance on 5 of 6 tasks, including the highest CIDEr on all three captioning benchmarks and the highest accuracy on TextVQA and ScienceQA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LOT Merging substantially outperforms all training-free baselines on ViT-B/32: it beats CAT Merging (78.3%) by 4.4%, PCB Merging (75.8%) by 6.9%, AWD Merging (75.2%) by 7.5%, Ties-Merging (72.4%) by 10.3%, RegMean (71.8%) by 10.9%, Task Arithmetic (69.1%) by 13.6%, and Fisher Merging (68.3%) by 14.4% in average accuracy. On ViT-L/14, the margin narrows but LOT Merging still leads CAT Merging (89.6%) by 0.9% and wins 7 of 8 individual tasks. On vision-language tasks (BLIP), LOT Merging achieves the best CIDEr on COCO Caption (0.91), Flickr30k (0.54), and TextCaps (0.44), and the highest accuracy on TextVQA (20.82%) and ScienceQA (48.24%), while CAT Merging leads only on OKVQA (44.07% vs. 38.35%). Compared to test-time training methods (AdaMerging, Surgery), LOT Merging matches or exceeds their accuracy (e.g., 82.7% vs. 81.1% for LW AdaMerging++ on ViT-B/32) while requiring no gradient-based optimization and only 16–64 exemplars per task. The method also demonstrates superior consistency, with the lowest standard deviation of accuracy drops across tasks (4.58 for ViT-B/32 vs. 6.21 for CAT Merging and 13.78 for Fisher Merging).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the task vector framework introduced by Task Arithmetic (Ilharco et al., ICLR 2023) by formulating the merging objective as layer-wise feature drift minimization rather than parameter-space distance. Its closed-form solution for linear layers parallels RegMean (Jin et al., ICLR 2023), but operates on task vectors instead of raw parameters, thereby preserving pre-trained knowledge and avoiding the catastrophic forgetting that plagues direct parameter merging. The approach also subsumes and extends the authors' prior CAT Merging (ICML 2025), which targeted the same feature-drift objective but relied on projection/masking heuristics rather than an analytic solution, and generalizes beyond linear layers to normalization scaling and bias terms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LOT Merging offers a practically attractive middle ground: it is training-free, requires only 16–64 exemplars per task, and runs in ~44 seconds on a single GPU, making it suitable for resource-constrained deployment of multi-task models. The 4.4% average-accuracy gain over the strongest training-free baseline (CAT Merging) on ViT-B/32, combined with the theoretical SVD-based analysis showing adaptive projection behavior, provides both empirical and conceptual grounding. Open questions remain about whether the layer-wise independence assumption captures cross-layer drift interactions, whether the method extends to architectures with non-linear operations (e.g., exponential activations), and whether the small-exemplar requirement can be eliminated entirely for strictly data-free settings.
