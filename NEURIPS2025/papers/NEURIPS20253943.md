## Problem

Existing map-matched trajectory recovery methods suffer from three interrelated limitations: they require large-scale dense trajectory pairs for training (which are scarce due to cost-saving GPS settings), they treat different sampling intervals identically and must be retrained when encountering unseen intervals, and they cannot extract road conditions at missing points whose locations are unknown. These issues collectively limit both the scalability and cross-interval generalization of trajectory recovery models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253943/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a sparse trajectory (a sequence of GPS points at sampling interval μ) and a directed road network graph as input. The sparse trajectory is first normalized to a unified target interval ε (15 s) by inserting placeholder tokens for missing points, so the model always operates on a fixed-length sequence. The output is a dense map-matched trajectory where every point is a (road segment ID, moving ratio along that segment, timestamp) triple, constrained to lie on valid road segments of the input network.

## Method

PLMTrajRec fine-tunes a pre-trained BERT-small encoder with LoRA (only ~35% of parameters trainable) to recover trajectories from small-scale dense data. Two complementary prompts condition the PLM: an IF-guided explicit prompt encodes the sampling interval and movement features as natural-language text, and an AF-guided implicit prompt encodes spatiotemporal road conditions via a regional flow matrix processed with 2D spatial and 1D temporal convolutions. An interval-aware trajectory embedder unifies varying sampling intervals into a single ε-grid, encodes observed points with Learnable Fourier Features plus distance-weighted road-segment embeddings, and infers missing-point road conditions through an exponential time-decay weighted interpolation between the nearest forward and backward observed points (a road condition passing mechanism). Reference tokens, a 1D CNN, and cross-attention bridge the trajectory embedding to the PLM's input space, and a joint multi-task loss (cross-entropy for road segments, MSE for moving ratios) is optimized across multiple sampling intervals simultaneously to ensure cross-interval generalization.

## Results

On Chengdu-Small (2-min sampling interval), PLMTrajRec achieves 81.76% accuracy, 187.4 m MAE, and 366.2 m RMSE, outperforming the strongest baseline MM-STGED (78.14% Acc, 197.2 m MAE, 696.0 m RMSE). On Porto-Small, PLMTrajRec reaches 66.40% Acc, 141.9 m MAE, and 294.6 m RMSE versus MM-STGED's 65.69% Acc, 152.5 m MAE, and 400.8 m RMSE. The paper reports an average improvement of 16.51% (Chengdu-Small) and 9.35% (Porto-Small) over baselines, with RMSE reductions of 351.8 m and 119.2 m respectively. With fine-tuning (PLMTrajRec+FT), performance improves by an additional 0.13%–1.49% across the four datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PLMTrajRec outperforms all 12 baselines across every metric on all four datasets. The closest competitor is MM-STGED [38], which uses micro-macro spatial-temporal graph modeling; PLMTrajRec surpasses it by 3.62% Acc and 329.8 m RMSE on Chengdu-Small (2-min), and by 0.71% Acc and 106.2 m RMSE on Porto-Small. Among the map-matched baselines, RNTrajRec [8] (spatial-temporal transformer) and the encoder+decoder variants (T2vec, T3s, TERI, TrajBERT) cluster within 1–3% Acc of each other but all trail PLMTrajRec by 4–6% Acc and 300–500 m RMSE. Free-space methods (HMM+ShortestPath, Linear+HMM, MPR+HMM, DHTR+HMM) are substantially weaker, with Acc below 50% at the 2-min interval on Chengdu-Small, confirming that direct coordinate prediction without road-network constraints is far less effective. AttnMove+Rule [39] is the strongest free-space method but still lags PLMTrajRec by ~10% Acc. The zero-shot experiment (trained on 1-min and 4-min, tested on 2-min) further shows PLMTrajRec degrades only 2.14% Acc on Chengdu-Small versus 9.90–24.14% for baselines, highlighting its superior generalization across unseen sampling intervals.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the map-matched trajectory recovery line (MTrajRec, RNTrajRec, MM-STGED) by repurposing a pre-trained BERT encoder with LoRA fine-tuning, drawing on the broader trend of applying PLMs to non-NLP domains (Time-LLM, TEMPO, VisionLLM). Unlike prior trajectory recovery methods that rely on seq2seq, graph neural networks, or hand-crafted spatiotemporal encoders, PLMTrajRec introduces trajectory-specific prompts (interval/feature-guided explicit and area-flow-guided implicit) and an interval-aware embedder to bridge the gap between PLM token space and continuous spatiotemporal trajectory data. The use of Learnable Fourier Features for coordinate encoding and a message-passing-inspired road condition mechanism for missing points are novel design choices not present in prior trajectory recovery work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in trajectory recovery: the scarcity of dense trajectory data. Demonstrating that 20% of training data suffices to match or exceed full-data baselines, and that the model generalizes zero-shot to unseen sampling intervals with only ~2% degradation (vs. 10–18% for baselines), makes the approach attractive for real-world deployment where dense data is expensive to collect. A key limitation is that road segment embeddings and the multi-class road segment head are dataset-specific, preventing cross-city or cross-dataset transfer without retraining. Open questions include whether a universal road segment representation could enable true cross-dataset generalization, and whether the encoder-only PLM advantage over decoder-only architectures holds as PLM scale increases.
