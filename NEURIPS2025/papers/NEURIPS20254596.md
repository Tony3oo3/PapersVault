## Problem

Most trustworthy ML systems require solving a separate, often non-convex optimization problem for each desired property (fairness, robustness, privacy, etc.), which is computationally expensive and rarely transferable across objectives. While the Rashomon set of near-optimal models has been shown to offer flexibility for interpretability and fairness, it remained unclear whether this flexibility extends to other trustworthiness criteria such as adversarial robustness, privacy, stability, and machine unlearning.

## Input / Output

The method takes binary feature vectors and binary labels from tabular datasets, with continuous features binarized via GOSDT threshold guessing. TreeFARMS enumerates the full Rashomon set (all sparse trees within an ε loss tolerance of the optimal tree, with sparsity controlled by a regularization parameter λ), producing potentially millions of candidate trees. Model selection is then a lightweight post-hoc filtering step: each tree in the set is scored on seven trustworthiness metrics (adversarial accuracy, stability, MIA success rate, unlearning mismatch, and three fairness parity scores), and the user selects from the Pareto frontier or applies hard constraints without any retraining.

## Method

The paper introduces a systematic evaluation framework that leverages TreeFARMS to fully enumerate the Rashomon set of sparse decision trees, then benchmarks every tree in the set against state-of-the-art methods specifically optimized for individual trustworthiness criteria. Seven metrics are evaluated: adversarial robustness (via Kantchelian evasion attacks), stability to geometric noise perturbations, membership inference resistance (four attack types from rule-based to shadow-model), machine unlearning (data removal with a theoretical guarantee that the post-removal optimal tree remains in the Rashomon set when ε ≥ 2K/n), and three group fairness metrics (statistical parity, equal opportunity, equalized odds). The key insight is that the natural diversity within the Rashomon set—spanning different tree complexities and structures—already contains models matching or exceeding specialized baselines across most criteria, eliminating the need for per-criterion retraining. Sparsity emerges as a critical factor: the sparsest trees in the set (RSET_min) tend to be more robust and private, while fairness shows no clear sparsity correlation.

## Results

On adversarial robustness, the Rashomon-set-selected tree (RSET_kan) achieves the highest adversarial accuracy on 6 of 8 datasets, e.g., 0.938 on banknote vs. 0.665 for FPRDT and ROCT-V, and 0.894 on spambase vs. 0.653 for FPRDT. For machine unlearning, the Rashomon set guarantees 0% prediction mismatch after data removal (by Theorem 5.3), while G-DaRE and G-Boosting show 0.018–5.5% mismatch. On membership inference, RSET_min achieves attack success rates near 0.5 (random guessing), matching or exceeding differentially private trees (PRIVA, BDPT, DPLDT) that sacrifice 3–10% test accuracy. TreeFARMS computes the full Rashomon set in 139 s mean training time, versus 1526 s for ROCT-V, 1828 s for ROCT-N, and 3444 s for FOCT.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against adversarial-robustness baselines, RSET_kan outperforms FPRDT on 6/8 datasets (e.g., +0.273 on banknote, +0.124 on compas) and ROCT-V on 7/8 datasets (e.g., +0.273 on banknote, +0.344 on compas); GROOT is competitive on blood (0.762 vs. 0.739) but RSET_kan wins elsewhere. On clean test accuracy (Table 6), RSET_opt and RSET_max match or exceed CART on 7/8 datasets, while robustness-optimized baselines (FPRDT, GROOT, ROCT-V) consistently underperform by 3–15 points. For privacy, RSET_min achieves MIA success rates within 0.01 of random guessing (0.5), matching or beating PRIVA, BDPT, and DPLDT, while retaining 3–10% higher test accuracy than the DP trees. On machine unlearning, the Rashomon set's theoretical guarantee yields 0% prediction mismatch after 0.5–2% data removal, whereas G-DaRE shows 0.008–2.1% and G-Boosting shows 0.051–5.5% mismatch. For fairness, the Rashomon set contains trees matching or exceeding DPF, FOCT, and LinearPost on 4/6 datasets at depth 4 (e.g., 71% of Rashomon trees beat all baselines on bank for statistical parity), though DPF and post-processing methods retain an edge on adult and compas where the fairness-accuracy tradeoff is tighter.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on TreeFARMS (Xin et al., NeurIPS 2022), which first enabled full enumeration of the Rashomon set of sparse decision trees, and on the formal Rashomon set definition from Semenova et al. (ACM FAccT 2022). Prior work on the Rashomon set has focused almost exclusively on fairness (finding less discriminatory models within the set), while prior work on trustworthy decision trees (robust, fair, private) has treated each criterion as a separate optimization problem. This paper departs from both lines by asking whether the Rashomon set, computed once for accuracy, already contains models satisfying seven distinct trustworthiness criteria simultaneously, eliminating the need for per-criterion retraining.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical implication is significant for interpretable ML deployment: computing the Rashomon set once and then filtering for fairness, robustness, privacy, or stability is cheaper and more flexible than solving a different (often NP-hard) optimization for each criterion. However, the findings are scoped to sparse decision trees with binary features, and the privacy results are weak (all MIA success rates hover near 0.5, making differentiation difficult). Open questions include whether the effect transfers to richer model families, how to quantify the diversity that drives trustworthiness gains, and whether the selection protocol generalizes beyond the small tabular datasets tested here.
