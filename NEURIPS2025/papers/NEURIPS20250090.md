## Problem

Point Cloud Transformers tend to overemphasize a small set of salient patches while neglecting other informative regions, due to the inherent attention bias in self-attention mechanisms. This over-reliance on high-attention regions makes models sensitive to perturbations (noise, rotation, dropout) and limits feature discrimination, particularly problematic for point clouds where each region carries critical information unlike redundant 2D images or text.

## Input / Output

The method takes raw point clouds sampled to 1,024 points, partitioned into 64 patches of 32 points each via Farthest Point Sampling and KNN. During pre-training, it produces two parallel feature representations (standard and blindspot branches) that are aligned via contrastive loss; during inference, only the standard branch is used to produce class labels, part segmentation labels, or latent feature representations. The framework is plug-and-play, requiring no architectural modification to the underlying Transformer backbone (Point-MAE, PointGPT-S, or Point-BERT).

## Method

BlindFormer introduces two components: (1) Attentional Blindspot Mining (ABM), which computes per-patch significance scores from self-attention weights and value norms, then constructs a dynamic masking probability using a temperature-scaled softmax plus Gumbel noise to stochastically suppress the most attended patches, creating a 'blindspot' view that forces the model to rely on under-attended regions; (2) Blindspot-Aware Joint Optimization (BJO), a bidirectional contrastive loss that aligns global features from the masked and standard branches, combined with the original pre-training objective (reconstruction loss for Point-MAE or generation loss for PointGPT) via a phased weighted schedule. Both branches share the same Transformer weights, and training proceeds in two phases: 300 epochs on the original loss alone, followed by 300 epochs with the joint contrastive objective, ensuring task fidelity is preserved while the model learns invariant, discriminative features from previously overlooked spatial regions.

## Results

On ScanObjectNN, BlindFormer with PointGPT-S achieves 92.3% (OBJ-BG), 91.6% (OBJ-ONLY), and 87.1% (PB-T50-RS) top-1 accuracy, while with Point-MAE it reaches 90.9%, 88.8%, and 85.4% respectively. On ModelNet40, BlindFormer attains 93.7% (Point-MAE) and 93.5% (PointGPT-S) without voting. Under the augmented training protocol, PointGPT-S + BlindFormer sets new state-of-the-art at 94.5% (OBJ-BG), 93.5% (OBJ-ONLY), and 89.9% (PB-T50-RS). On ShapeNetPart, BlindFormer with Point-MAE achieves 86.4% instance mIoU and 85.2% class mIoU for part segmentation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BlindFormer consistently outperforms its integrated backbones: over Point-MAE it gains +0.9% (OBJ-BG), +0.5% (OBJ-ONLY), and +0.5% (ModelNet40); over PointGPT-S it gains +0.7%, +1.6%, and +0.2%; over Point-BERT it gains +2.1%, +0.5%, and +0.4%. Against other self-supervised methods, BlindFormer (PointGPT-S) surpasses Point-M2AE (91.2%) and GPM (90.2%) on OBJ-BG but trails PointDif (93.3%) and PointMamba (90.7%) on that split; however, under the augmented protocol it leads all methods at 94.5%. On PB-T50-RS, BlindFormer (PointGPT-S) at 87.1% beats Point-M2AE (86.4%) and GPM (84.8%) but falls short of PointNeXt (87.7%) and ADS (87.5%) among supervised-only methods. On ModelNet40, BlindFormer (Point-MAE) reaches 93.7%, exceeding Point-M2AE (93.4%) and GPM (93.3%) but slightly below PointMLP (94.1%). For part segmentation on ShapeNetPart, BlindFormer with Point-MAE achieves 86.4/85.2 mIoU (instance/class), outperforming Point-BERT (85.6/84.1) and PointGPT-S (86.2/84.1) but trailing PointMamba (86.0/84.4) on instance mIoU while leading on class mIoU.

## Relation to Prior Work

BlindFormer builds directly on the masked autoencoder (Point-MAE) and auto-regressive pre-training (PointGPT) paradigms for point cloud Transformers, adding a contrastive attention learning layer on top. It departs from standard random masking strategies by introducing Attentional Blindspot Mining, which uses the model's own self-attention scores to dynamically suppress the most salient patches during training, and a Blindspot-Aware Joint Optimization that aligns features from masked and unmasked views via a symmetric contrastive loss. Conceptually, it extends the self-supervised pre-training literature for point clouds by reframing the masking problem as an attention-redistribution problem rather than a random occlusion problem, drawing on Gumbel-Softmax for stochastic patch selection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a concrete and underexplored failure mode in point cloud Transformers—over-concentration of attention on a small subset of patches—and shows that explicitly counteracting it yields large robustness gains (up to +17.2% under Gaussian noise) while preserving or slightly improving standard classification accuracy. The plug-and-play design across Point-MAE, PointGPT-S, and Point-BERT suggests broad applicability, though validation is limited to three datasets and three backbones. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to larger-scale or multi-modal point cloud settings, whether the dual-branch training overhead is acceptable in practice, and whether the robustness gains transfer to real-world sensor degradation beyond synthetic perturbations.
