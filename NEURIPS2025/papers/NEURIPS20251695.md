## Problem

Neural network-based methods for solving operator eigenvalue problems suffer from slow convergence when the operator's eigenvalues are closely spaced, and existing approaches do not adaptively exploit the spectral information accumulated during iteration. While spectral transformations (deflation, filtering) are well-established in numerical linear algebra, they have not been integrated into learning-based eigenvalue solvers in a way that leverages the approximate eigenvalues and eigenfunctions obtained on-the-fly.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251695/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

STNet takes a differential operator L (applied via automatic differentiation), a domain Ω ⊂ R^D, a shift parameter σ, and a target number of eigenvalues L as input. It produces L approximate eigenvalues and their corresponding eigenfunctions, each represented by a separate MLP evaluated at N uniformly random mesh-free sample points. No training data or discretization grid is required; the method is entirely unsupervised, relying solely on satisfying the eigenvalue equation over the sampled domain.

## Method

STNet employs L multilayer perceptrons to approximate eigenfunctions, iteratively updated in a power-method-like loop where the inverse operator (L − σI)⁻¹ is simulated through a loss function rather than explicitly computed. The key innovation is two adaptive spectral transformation modules applied at each iteration: (1) a deflation projection D_i(L) = L − Q_{i−1}Σ_{i−1}Q_{i−1}ᵀ that removes the subspace spanned by already-converged eigenfunctions, preventing collapse into known solutions and shrinking the search space; and (2) a rational filter transform F_i(L) = ∏[(L − (λ̃_{i0}−ξ)I)(L − (λ̃_{i0}+ξ)I)] that amplifies eigenvalues within target intervals around approximate solutions while suppressing others, widening the effective spectral gap. Both modules are updated adaptively as new eigenpairs converge, and the combined transformed operator L'' = F_i(D_i(L)) is applied to the current iterate before the loss is computed and parameters are updated via gradient descent.

## Results

On the 5D Harmonic eigenvalue problem, STNet achieves relative errors of 4.66e-6, 1.60e-6, 1.05e-6, and 2.20e-5 for λ₁–λ₄, outperforming NeuralSVD (2.88e-2 to 3.56e-2) by over three orders of magnitude and NeuralEF (3.98e-1 to 1.16e+1) by four to five orders. On the 1D Schrödinger oscillator, STNet reaches a relative error of 7.24e-7, surpassing PMNN (2.34e-6) and NeuralSVD (1.25e-3). For the Fokker-Planck zero-eigenvalue problem in 2D (cᵢ=0.5), STNet achieves absolute errors of 5.26e-6 and 5.14e-2 for the two zero eigenvalues, while all baselines produce errors above 0.3.

## Baselines & Comparisons

STNet consistently outperforms all three learning-based baselines (NeuralEF, NeuralSVD, PMNN) across every tested problem. Against NeuralSVD on the 5D Harmonic problem, STNet improves relative error by 3–4 orders of magnitude (e.g., 4.66e-6 vs. 2.88e-2 for λ₁). Against NeuralEF, the gap is even wider, with NeuralEF often producing errors greater than 1 (i.e., worse than the eigenvalue itself) in 2D and 5D settings. On the Schrödinger oscillator, STNet beats PMNN (the closest competitor, also power-method-based) by roughly 3× in 1D (7.24e-7 vs. 2.34e-6) and by 12× in 2D (2.35e-6 vs. 9.07e-5), with the gap narrowing in 5D (1.29e-1 vs. 1.57e-1). Against the traditional FDM on the 5D Harmonic problem, STNet at 9⁵ grid points achieves 4.62e-5 relative error for λ₁ using 1.14 GB of memory, while FDM requires 45⁵ points (22.9 GB) to reach 3.82e-3 — a 100× accuracy improvement at 20× less memory.

## Relation to Prior Work

STNet builds directly on the Power Method Neural Network (PMNN) [45], which embeds the classical power method into an iterative neural network training loop, and on NeuralEF [7] and NeuralSVD [38], which use neural networks to approximate eigenfunctions via kernel or SVD-based formulations. The key departure is the dynamic incorporation of classical spectral transformations—deflation projection and rational filter transforms—into the neural network iteration, using approximate eigenvalues and eigenfunctions obtained during training to reshape the operator's spectrum on the fly. This bridges a gap: prior neural-network eigenvalue methods treat the operator as fixed, while STNet reformulates it adaptively, drawing on techniques from Saad's [39] and Watkins' [43] numerical linear algebra literature that had not previously been integrated into learning-based eigenvalue solvers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

STNet addresses a practical bottleneck in scientific computing: high-dimensional operator eigenvalue problems where grid-based methods suffer from the curse of dimensionality. The demonstrated 3+ orders-of-magnitude accuracy gain in 5D and the ability to resolve zero eigenvalues (Fokker-Planck) make it a strong candidate for replacing traditional solvers in physics and engineering applications. However, the method is currently restricted to linear self-adjoint operators, and the authors note that broader matrix preconditioning strategies and nonlinear eigenvalue problems remain unexplored. The longer total convergence time compared to baselines (Table 10) also raises a practical trade-off question for time-sensitive applications.
