## Problem

Existing synthetic image detectors either rely on model-specific frequency artifacts that fail to generalize across unseen generative architectures, or depend on large pre-trained backbones (e.g., CLIP-based FatFormer with 492M parameters) that are computationally expensive. There is no lightweight detector that simultaneously achieves strong cross-model generalization and low inference cost.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251402/cropped_page3_idx4.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard RGB image (224×224 during training, 256×256 at inference) and first transforms it into a Local Pixel Dependency (LPD) feature map of the same spatial dimensions before feeding it to the network. The LPD map is computed per-channel as the pixel-wise difference between the original image and a zero-masked 3×3 neighborhood-median reconstruction, effectively converting the input from raw pixel values to a local-statistical-deviation representation. The network outputs a single binary logit indicating real vs. synthetic.

## Method

The core insight is that generative models universally disrupt local pixel dependencies (rooted in Markov Random Field assumptions) through latent distribution deviations and decoding-induced smoothing, and this disruption is best captured at the 3×3 scale matching typical upsampling kernels. The LPD feature is extracted by zeroing each center pixel, computing the median over the remaining 8 neighbors, and subtracting the result from the original pixel value. FerretNet is a 1.1M-parameter CNN built around four cascaded Ferret Blocks, each using a dual-path design: a 3×3 dilated grouped convolution (dilation=2) in parallel with a 3×3 grouped convolution, fused via 1×1 convolutions to approximate a sparse 5×5 receptive field without extra parameters. The network is trained from scratch with BCEWithLogitsLoss on the 4-class ProGAN ForenSynths subset, yet generalizes to 22 generative models including modern diffusion-based systems, achieving 97.1% average accuracy at 772 FPS on an RTX 4090.

## Results

FerretNet achieves 97.1% accuracy and 99.6% average precision across 22 generative models (spanning GANs, VAEs, and LDMs) using only 1.06M parameters and 2.38 GFLOPs, running at 772.1 FPS on an RTX 4090. On the high-fidelity Synthetic-Pop benchmark, it reaches 98.3% ACC / 99.8% AP, outperforming the next-best method (SAFE) by 0.4 pp in ACC. The model is trained exclusively on the 4-class ProGAN subset of ForenSynths (72K images) and generalizes to unseen generators without fine-tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FerretNet outperforms all lightweight baselines (FreqNet, NPR, SAFE) on the overall four-test-set average, achieving 97.1% ACC vs. 96.8% (SAFE), 86.5% (NPR), and 79.2% (FreqNet). Against the heavyweight FatFormer (492.59M params), FerretNet wins by 11.0 pp in ACC and 8.6 pp in AP while using only 0.2% of the parameters; however, FatFormer retains a narrow edge on the ForenSynths GAN test set (98.4% vs. 95.9% ACC). On the high-fidelity Synthetic-Pop benchmark, FerretNet (98.3% ACC) surpasses SAFE (97.9%), CO-SPY (85.6%), NPR (77.4%), and FatFormer (70.5%), with the gap widening dramatically for transformer-based methods. On diffusion-generated images (Diffusion-6-cls), FerretNet leads with 96.9% ACC, beating FatFormer (95.0%) and SAFE (94.5%). The closest competitor is SAFE, which matches FerretNet within 0.3 pp on ForenSynths but trails by 2.4 pp on Diffusion-6-cls and 0.4 pp on Synthetic-Pop.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant trends in synthetic image detection—frequency-domain analysis (FreqNet, BiHPF, SAFE) and large pretrained-model-based approaches (FatFormer's CLIP adapter, CO-SPY's frozen encoder)—by grounding its detection signal in a simple Markov Random Field principle: local pixel dependencies disrupted by generative decoding. Rather than learning high-level semantic representations or extracting spectral signatures, FerretNet applies a zero-masked median reconstruction (LPD) as a fixed preprocessing step and feeds the residual to a 1.1M-parameter CNN. Conceptually, it is closest in spirit to NPR's neighbor-pixel-relation modeling and Ojha et al.'s observation that universal artifacts exist across generators, but it replaces learned feature extraction with a closed-form statistical operation, eliminating the need for pretrained backbones entirely.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a detector that runs at 772 FPS on a single GPU with under 1M parameters and generalizes across 22 generative models (GANs, VAEs, and modern diffusion models) offers a deployable solution where heavyweight CLIP-based methods are infeasible. The Synthetic-Pop benchmark (60K images from six current diffusion models) fills a gap in evaluation infrastructure for high-fidelity generators. However, the method's vulnerability to JPEG compression (accuracy drops to ~50% at Q=75) remains a serious open question for real-world deployment where images are routinely compressed. The ablation showing that a 20× larger FerretNet-L actually underperforms the base model also raises an open question about the optimal capacity for low-level statistical artifact detection.
