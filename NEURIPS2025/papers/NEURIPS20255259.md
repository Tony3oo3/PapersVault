## Problem

Existing lithography simulation benchmarks for ML-based surrogates are inadequate for resolution enhancement technique (RET) workflows: they cover outdated 32–45 nm nodes, use mask regions too small (≤4 μm²) to capture optical proximity effects, and omit critical process variations (source shape, dose, defocus, OPC-corrected masks) that RET optimization must handle. This gap prevents trained models from supporting differentiable OPC/SMO loops where the simulator must remain accurate as masks and sources change iteratively.

## Input / Output

The simulation takes four input modalities: a binary mask pattern at 4096×4096 pixels (1 nm/pixel, 16 μm² area), a light source distribution represented as N discrete points each with normalized intensity and 2D Cartesian coordinates in [−1,1]², a scalar exposure dose in [−12%, +12%], and a scalar defocus offset in [−80, +80] nm. The output is a 4096×4096 grayscale resist image at 1 nm/pixel resolution, produced by a rigorous lithography simulator (NA=1.35, λ=193 nm, 37-term Zernike aberrations). The dataset spans 620+ source configurations, 13 dose levels, 5 defocus levels, and four mask categories (Metal, OPC-Metal, Via, OPC-Via), yielding over 4 million input–output pairs.

## Method

LithoSim is primarily a benchmark and evaluation framework rather than a single new architecture. It generates a physically grounded dataset via a scalable pipeline (100 parallel CPUs, ~40 min per optical model, ~15 s per resist simulation) that cross-combines sources, masks, dose, and defocus, and includes a dedicated OOD test set with unseen dipole/quasar illumination and M3/via layouts from a different CPU design. For evaluation, the paper establishes six baselines—two data-driven (ED-CNN, ED-Trans) and four physics-informed (RFNO, CFNO, MFNO, SOCS)—all sharing a unified chunked litho-aware attention mechanism that embeds source coordinates via 2D positional encoding with intra-chunk and inter-chunk self-attention, and encodes dose/defocus via 1D spectral positional encoding, then fuses these conditions into mask features through chunked cross-attention. Evaluation uses both ML metrics (MSE, PA, IOU) and lithography-domain metrics (EPE_max, EPE_avg, turnaround time), with a unique OOD protocol that tests models trained on OPC-corrected masks against uncorrected counterparts and vice versa, directly mimicking the iterative mask modification in differentiable RET flows.

## Results

On the in-distribution LithoSim benchmark, no single ML model dominates all four mask categories: MFNO achieves the best OPC-Metal simulation (MSE 6.28×10⁻³, IOU 95.29%, EPE_avg 1.02 nm), ED-CNN leads on Metal (MSE 8.06×10⁻³, IOU 92.32%), and RFNO excels on both Via layers (MSE 4.77×10⁻³, IOU 83.10% for Via). All ML models achieve turnaround times of 8–11 ms per 4096×4096 pattern, compared to ~280,000 ms for the electromagnetic (EM) upper bound—a speedup of over 25,000×. On the out-of-distribution (OOD) test, SOCS achieves the best generalization with MSE 4.13×10⁻³, IOU 80.24%, and EPE_avg 0.60 nm, substantially outperforming all other baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among the six baselines, MFNO (built on the dual-band FNO from Yang et al., DAC 2022 [17] and Li et al., 2024 [39]) achieves the best in-distribution performance on OPC-Metal (IOU 95.29%, EPE_avg 1.02 nm), closely approaching the EM upper bound (97.32% IOU). RFNO (Yang et al., DAC 2022 [17]) dominates the Via layers (IOU 92.68% OPC-Via, 83.10% Via) where localized low-frequency scattering is dominant, outperforming MFNO by 1.69 and 9.58 IOU points respectively. ED-CNN (following the CNN flow of Chen et al., TCAD 2022 [28]) leads on the non-OPC Metal dataset (IOU 92.32%, EPE_avg 1.30 nm), while ED-Trans (ViT-based, Dosovitskiy et al., ICLR 2021 [29]) consistently underperforms its CNN counterpart across all categories and incurs the highest TAT (~11.3–11.6 ms). CFNO (Yang & Ren, ASPDAC 2023 [21]) is the weakest in-distribution model, with the highest MSE on OPC-Metal (20.15×10⁻³) and EPE_max exceeding 2.9 nm. SOCS (Chen et al., DAC 2023 [30]) is competitive in-distribution (second-best on OPC-Metal with EPE_avg 1.07 nm) and achieves the lowest TAT (~8 ms), but its decisive advantage emerges in OOD evaluation: SOCS achieves MSE 4.13×10⁻³ and EPE_avg 0.60 nm on unseen M3/via layouts with dipole/quasar sources, outperforming the next-best RFNO (MSE 5.34, EPE_avg 0.87 nm) by 23% in MSE and 31% in EPE, while CFNO degrades most severely (MSE 13.89, EPE_avg 1.55 nm).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of lithography simulation benchmarks (CAD13, ISPD19, N14, LithoBench) by generalizing the task from a simple mask-to-resist mapping (M→R) to a holistic multi-variable simulation M×(J,α,β)→R that incorporates source distributions, dose, and defocus at sub-28nm nodes with 16μm² masks. Rather than proposing a new architecture, it repurposes existing model families—CNN/Transformer encoder-decoders, Fourier Neural Operator variants (RFNO, CFNO, MFNO), and the physics-informed SOCS framework—as baselines to establish a unified evaluation protocol. The key conceptual departure from prior work is the introduction of out-of-distribution evaluation (OPC→non-OPC masks, novel illumination types) that directly mirrors the iterative mask modification in RET flows, a dimension entirely absent from earlier benchmarks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LithoSim fills a critical gap by providing the first benchmark where all physical variables required for differentiable RET optimization (source, mask with/without OPC, dose, defocus) are jointly varied at fab-realistic scales, enabling meaningful assessment of whether ML surrogates can actually serve as drop-in components for OPC/SMO workflows. The finding that physics-informed models (SOCS, RFNO) maintain substantially better OOD generalization than data-driven counterparts validates the need for physics constraints in production-grade RET, while the in-distribution parity suggests data diversity alone can compensate for missing inductive biases in controlled settings. Key open questions include integration into actual EDA toolchains, incorporation of dynamic resist chemistry, and validation against real fab data—none of which are addressed here.
