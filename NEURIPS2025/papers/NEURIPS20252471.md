## Problem

Autoregressive neural PDE solvers suffer from catastrophic error accumulation over long time horizons, producing unphysical collapsed or exploding solutions in chaotic, multiscale systems like turbulent flows. Existing remedies—multi-step-ahead training, frequency decompositions, or physics-informed priors—either sacrifice short-term accuracy, require additional domain knowledge, or still fail to maintain stability over extremely long rollouts. This paper reframes the problem through the lens of numerical time-stepping, identifying the instability of autoregressive models as analogous to the instability of explicit schemes (e.g., forward Euler) and proposing an implicit-scheme-inspired solution.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a 2D vorticity field snapshot on a 256×256 or 512×512 grid, augmented with L−1 hierarchical latent representations of future states obtained by fixed spatial downsampling (rates r¹=8, r²=32 for L=3), producing a 64×64 and 32×32 coarse-grained tensor respectively. The output is the next-step full-resolution vorticity field plus predicted future latent representations at each hierarchy level. During autoregressive rollout, the model bootstraps from the initial condition alone by first predicting the missing latent slots (trained via random zeroing with probability 15%), then proceeding with the full augmented state.

## Method

The core mechanism is an implicit neural emulator that conditions next-step prediction on coarse-grained future-state latents, mimicking the iterative refinement of implicit time-stepping solvers without the cost of iterative root-finding. A UNet with Fourier layers processes the current state u_n concatenated with hierarchical future latents z_{n+1}^{(1)}, z_{n+2}^{(2)}, …, z_{n+L−1}^{(L−1)}, where each latent is a fixed spatial downsample of the corresponding future frame. The network simultaneously outputs the next physical state û_{n+1} and future latent predictions, so that during autoregressive rollout the predicted latents from step n feed into step n+1, effectively distributing L refinement iterations across temporal frames. The training loss supervises both the physical state and all latent predictions with ℓ₁+ℓ₂ losses, and corner-case training (randomly zeroing latent inputs) ensures the model can initialize the hierarchy from a single snapshot. This design adds only ~10% computational overhead over the one-step baseline while achieving 93% stability over 2×10⁵ rollout steps (10× training length) on 2D Navier-Stokes at Re=10⁴.

## Results

On 2D Navier-Stokes turbulence (Re=10⁴, 256×256, with jets), the proposed L=3 hierarchical implicit emulator reduces rollout MSE by over 50% compared to the standard 1-step autoregressive baseline: 3.37e-02 vs. 8.04e-02 at 25 steps and 5.92e-01 vs. 9.38e-01 at 100 steps. For extremely long rollouts (2×10⁵ steps, 10× training length), L=3 achieves a 93% energy-based stability rate, far exceeding the next-best baseline (2-Step History at 38%). The method adds only ~10% per-iteration computational overhead (0.2204s vs. 0.2067s) and 5% more parameters (36.67M vs. 34.88M) relative to the L=1 baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the FNO baseline [22] (with 4× more parameters, 130M vs. 36.7M), the proposed L=3 method achieves lower MSE at every rollout step beyond 1-step (e.g., 3.37e-02 vs. 5.22e-02 at 25 steps; 5.92e-01 vs. 7.12e-01 at 100 steps), demonstrating that the hierarchical implicit design is more effective than simply scaling up a neural operator. Compared to the Pushforward unrolling method [18], L=3 substantially reduces 25-step MSE (3.37e-02 vs. 9.16e-02) and 50-step MSE (1.40e-01 vs. 3.19e-01) while also improving zonal mean error (0.63 vs. 1.07) and incurring lower per-iteration cost (0.2204s vs. 0.2834s); Pushforward additionally degrades 1-step accuracy (1.08e-03 vs. 5.50e-04). Versus the PDEBench multi-step history baselines [19] (2-step and 3-step History), L=3 wins at all horizons beyond 1-step, with the largest gap at 25 steps (3.37e-02 vs. 5.21e-02 and 4.28e-02) and 100 steps (5.92e-01 vs. 7.67e-01 and 6.81e-01). The ablation further shows that the temporal hierarchy (conditioning on future compressed states) is the critical design choice: spatial-only or history-only hierarchy variants yield only marginal gains over the plain L=1 baseline, while the full temporal hierarchy (L=3) delivers the dominant improvement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reframes the standard autoregressive PDE emulation paradigm through the lens of numerical implicit time-stepping: where conventional autoregressive models (analogous to forward Euler) condition only on past states, the proposed architecture conditions on hierarchically compressed representations of *future* states, mimicking the iterative refinement of backward Euler solvers. It departs from multi-step-ahead prediction methods (which forecast full-resolution future frames) by instead predicting spatially downsampled latent codes at increasing temporal horizons, and from spatial-hierarchy approaches (which use multiple resolutions of the same timestep) by introducing a *temporal* hierarchy. The base architecture is a U-Net with Fourier layers, and the hierarchical injection of latent codes follows the multigrid-inspired information flow already present in U-Net's encoder-decoder structure, but repurposed for temporal rather than purely spatial multi-scale reasoning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Long-term stability remains the central unsolved bottleneck for neural PDE emulators, and the 93% stability rate at 2×10⁵ steps (10× training length) versus 38% for the next-best baseline represents a substantial practical advance for applications like climate and geophysical forecasting where multi-decade rollouts are needed. The approach adds only ~5% more parameters and negligible inference overhead, making it a drop-in upgrade for existing autoregressive emulators. Key limitations include evaluation restricted to 2D incompressible Navier-Stokes at two Reynolds numbers, fixed (non-adaptive) downsampling rates, and a deterministic (non-probabilistic) formulation; open questions include whether the implicit-hierarchy principle transfers to 3D flows, compressible systems, or stochastic PDEs, and whether the downsampling schedule can be learned or adapted per regime.
