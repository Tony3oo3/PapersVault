## Problem

Existing safety mechanisms for diffusion models either require expensive retraining or fine-tuning (ESD, RECE) or depend on text-based negative prompts (SLD, SAFREE) that fail against adversarial prompts lacking explicit unsafe vocabulary. No training-free approach provides a formal guarantee that generated samples are drawn from a safe distribution, leaving a gap between practical safety enforcement and theoretical safety assurance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254539/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-trained diffusion model (SD-v1.4, SD-v3), a set of N unsafe reference images (e.g., 515 nudity images from I2P or 3,000 images for multi-concept inappropriate content), and optionally text prompts paired with existing safety frameworks. The unsafe images are used purely at inference time to construct a weighted-mixture approximation of the unsafe denoiser in the model's latent space, and the output is a generated image whose sampling trajectory has been steered away from the negated region. No model weights are modified; the only additional computation is evaluating Gaussian likelihoods between the current noisy latent and each reference image.

## Method

Theorem 3.2 formally derives that the safe denoiser equals the data denoiser plus a correction term β*(x_t)·(data denoiser − unsafe denoiser), where the weight β* grows as the noisy sample x_t becomes more likely to originate from unsafe data, mirroring the structure of classifier-free guidance but with an adaptive, data-driven penalty. In practice, the unsafe denoiser is approximated as a normalized weighted sum of N unsafe reference images (a finite mixture), and the intractable β* is replaced by a scalar η·β where β is the average Gaussian likelihood of x_t under the unsafe distribution, reducing the computation to a single batched distance evaluation. The safe denoiser is applied only at early timesteps (where global structure is established) to avoid degrading fine-grained details, and a threshold β_t gates its application so that already-safe samples pass through unmodified. The method is training-free, adds sub-linear wall-clock overhead (0.07 s for 3,000 reference images on an RTX 4090), and plugs into SAFREE or SLD by simply substituting the safe denoiser for the data denoiser in their guidance formulas.

## Results

On the nudity safety task (Table 2), SAFREE + Ours achieves the lowest Attack Success Rate (ASR) across all three adversarial prompt datasets: 0.127 on Ring-A-Bell, 0.207 on UnlearnDiff, and 0.469 on MMA-Diffusion, compared to 0.278, 0.353, and 0.601 for SAFREE alone. The method also attains the best FID (22.55) and competitive CLIP score (30.66) on COCO-30K, indicating minimal quality degradation. On the broader CoPro inappropriate-probability task (Table 3), SAFREE + Ours reduces average IP from 0.153 (SAFREE) to 0.144, and SLD + Ours from 0.170 to 0.151. On SD-v3 (Table 7), the method achieves a 33.2% relative ASR reduction (0.304→0.203) versus only ~9% for SAFREE alone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the untrained SD-v1.4 baseline (Rombach et al., CVPR 2022), SAFREE + Ours reduces ASR from 0.962 to 0.469 on MMA-Diffusion (a 51% relative drop) and from 0.797 to 0.127 on Ring-A-Bell (84% relative drop). Among training-based methods, RECE (Gong et al., ECCV 2024) achieves the best Ring-A-Bell ASR (0.177) but degrades sharply on MMA-Diffusion (0.651), while SAFREE + Ours (0.469) substantially outperforms it there; ESD (Gandikota et al., ICCV 2023) is consistently weaker than both. Among training-free text-based methods, SAFREE + Ours dominates SAFREE alone on every prompt set (e.g., 0.469 vs. 0.601 ASR on MMA-Diffusion) and SLD + Ours reduces SLD's MMA-Diffusion ASR from 0.881 to 0.481, a 45% relative improvement. On the class-conditional ImageNet task (Table 4), the method outperforms Sparse Repellency (Kirchhof et al., arXiv 2024) in precision (0.62 vs. 0.59) and recall (0.58 vs. 0.54) while matching its Top-1* of 0.00. On FFHQ unconditional generation (Table 6), the method achieves a lower FID (96.57 vs. 130.52) than SR while maintaining comparable female-class reduction (55.6% vs. 53.1%). On the frontier SD-v3 model (Esser et al., ICML 2024), the method achieves ASR 0.203 versus 0.278 for SAFREE alone, confirming plug-and-play compatibility with modern architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper formalizes a safe denoiser by deriving the exact relationship between safe, unsafe, and data denoisers (Theorem 3.2), showing the safe denoiser has a structure analogous to classifier-free guidance but with an adaptive weight β*(x_t) that penalizes trajectories near unsafe regions. It positions itself as a generalization of Sparse Repellency (SR), which uses a fixed-radius ReLU penalty, and as a complementary plug-in to text-based safety methods like SAFREE and Safe Latent Diffusion (SLD), which rely on negative prompts or modified token embeddings. Unlike training-based unlearning approaches (ESD, RECE) that risk catastrophic forgetting, the method is entirely training-free and operates by modifying the sampling trajectory using a small set of unsafe reference images.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first theoretical guarantee that generated samples are drawn from a safe distribution, addressing a gap where prior training-free methods (SAFREE, SLD, SR) lack formal safety bounds. Its practical impact is significant: it can be layered onto existing text-based defenses for defense-in-depth, handles adversarial prompts that evade text-based filters (e.g., MMA-Diffusion), and extends to style-level IP control and data memorization without retraining. Open questions include the need for hyperparameter tuning (η, β_t) to balance fidelity and safety, the requirement for a curated unsafe reference set, and the absence of standardized quantitative metrics for privacy-sensitive generation.
