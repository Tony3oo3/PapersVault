## Problem

Existing unaligned multi-view clustering methods rely on alignment strategies (e.g., learnable alignment matrices or Hungarian algorithms) to match sample representations across views before fusion, but the inherent heterogeneity of independently constructed view-specific representations causes these alignment strategies to produce unreliable matches, ultimately degrading cross-view fusion quality. This paper identifies that the fundamental bottleneck is not the fusion mechanism itself but the prerequisite alignment step, which is both computationally expensive (O(N²)) and inherently unreliable under representation heterogeneity.

## Input / Output

The method takes V unaligned multi-view feature matrices {X^v ∈ R^{N×D_v}} where the i-th row in one view does not necessarily correspond to the i-th row in another view, and produces a cross-view consistent representation H^core ∈ R^{N×c} (where c equals the number of clusters k) from which K-means yields final cluster assignments. The basis space dimension d is fixed at 512, and the core view is selected as the one with the largest original feature dimension to serve as the anchor for global fusion. Training operates on mini-batches of size 256 over 50 epochs, making it applicable to datasets up to ~100K samples where O(N²) alignment-based methods fail.

## Method

AF-UMC eliminates alignment entirely through a two-stage design. In the Alignment-Free Consistency Extraction stage, a single shared cross-view consistent basis space Z ∈ R^{c×d} is learned jointly across all views via multi-view reconstruction, with a Structural Clarity Regularization (SCR) that penalizes structurally equivalent basis vectors (measured by coincident neighbor relationships on a cosine-similarity graph) to ensure the basis captures matched cross-view information. View-specific autoencoders then project each view's data onto this shared Z to extract consistent representations H^v, so that consistency is inherited from the shared basis rather than imposed by post-hoc alignment. In the Global Contrastive Fusion stage, the Instance Global Contrastive Fusion (IGCF) loss pulls each instance h_i^core toward the global centers h̄^v of other views (positive pairs) while pushing it away from other instances in the same view (negative pairs), deliberately masking cross-view negative pairs to avoid penalizing same-sample pairs that are unidentifiable in unaligned data. A theoretical result (Theorem 1) establishes that minimizing the IGCF loss lower-bounds the mutual information between H^core and the global centers of all other views, providing an information-theoretic guarantee for the fusion objective.

## Results

On six small-scale unaligned multi-view datasets, AF-UMC achieves the best or second-best result on 23 out of 24 metric-dataset combinations, with the sole exception being ARI on Scene (0.2548 vs. T-UMC's 0.2856). Headline numbers include 0.8721 ACC / 0.7798 NMI on Caltech7-5, 0.9035 ACC / 0.8205 NMI on Handwritten, and 0.6950 ACC / 0.6000 NMI on Hdigit (a 19.57% ACC improvement over the second-best T-UMC). On four large-scale datasets, AF-UMC leads all baselines on every metric, reaching 0.5899 ACC / 0.4982 NMI on NoisyMNIST and 0.8453 ACC / 0.7025 NMI on CIFAR-10.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AF-UMC outperforms all six UMC-specific baselines (MVC-UM, T-UMC, UPMGC, FUMC, OpVuC, TUMCR) on 23 of 24 metric-dataset combinations in Table 2; the single loss is ARI on Scene where T-UMC scores 0.2856 vs. AF-UMC's 0.2548. The most dramatic margin is on Hdigit, where AF-UMC beats the second-best T-UMC by 19.57% in ACC (0.6950 vs. 0.4993) and 15.57% in NMI (0.6000 vs. 0.4387). On large-scale datasets (Table 3), AF-UMC also surpasses four aligned multi-view methods (LMVSC, MFLVC, GCFAgg, SCMVC) that are repurposed for the unaligned setting, achieving 0.5899 ACC on NoisyMNIST (vs. OpVuC's 0.5111) and 0.8453 ACC on CIFAR-10 (vs. OpVuC's 0.8008). OpVuC (TMM 2024) is the strongest UMC baseline overall, particularly on large-scale data, while FUMC (IJCAI 2024) is competitive on NoisyMNIST but trails AF-UMC by 7.58% in ACC. Structure-based methods (MVC-UM, T-UMC, UPMGC) and feature-based methods (FUMC, OpVuC, TUMCR) all rely on explicit alignment strategies that the paper argues degrade under cross-view heterogeneity, and AF-UMC's alignment-free design consistently avoids this failure mode.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant 'align-then-fuse' paradigm in unaligned multi-view clustering, where prior methods (MVC-UM, T-UMC, UPMGC, FUMC, OpVuC, TUMCR) construct view-specific basis spaces or self-representation structures and then apply alignment strategies (Hungarian algorithm, learnable O(N²) alignment matrices) before cross-view fusion. AF-UMC instead constructs a single shared cross-view consistent basis space and uses per-view autoencoders to project data onto it, replacing explicit alignment with implicit consistency through a shared subspace. The fusion stage draws on contrastive learning (InfoNCE-style losses) but introduces a novel instance-to-global contrastive mechanism (IGCF) that avoids instance-to-instance matching, which is infeasible under unaligned data. The Structural Clarity Regularization is a new component addressing structural equivalence in basis vectors, a problem identified but not previously formalized in the UMC literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's central claim — that alignment strategies are not merely insufficient but actively harmful due to representation heterogeneity — reframes a core design assumption in the UMC literature and offers a simpler, more scalable alternative. The elimination of O(N²) alignment cost makes the method applicable to large-scale datasets (50K–100K samples) where most existing UMC methods simply cannot run, which is a practical gap of significant importance. However, the paper does not report statistical significance (no error bars or multiple runs), the 'core view' selection heuristic (largest feature dimension) is somewhat arbitrary and unexplored, and the method still requires the number of clusters c to be known a priori. Open questions include whether the alignment-free approach extends to partial multi-view settings (missing views per sample) and whether the shared basis space degrades gracefully under high view heterogeneity.
