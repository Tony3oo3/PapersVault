## Problem

Segment matching across wide-baseline image pairs (up to 180° viewpoint rotation) remains unsolved: existing approaches relying on 2D supervision—whether SAM2's video propagator, local feature matchers, or DINOv2-based segment descriptors—degrade sharply under extreme perspective, scale, and rotation changes. No prior method enforces geometric consistency at the segment level, leaving a gap between pixel-level 3D-aware matching (e.g., MASt3R) and instance-level correspondence.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pair of RGB images along with their segmentation masks (from SAM2 at inference or ground-truth annotations at training), where each image yields up to M=100 masks (typically 20–30 in practice, zero-padded). The frozen MASt3R backbone produces patch-level features at H/16 × W/16 × 768 resolution, which the segment-feature head upsamples to full image resolution with 24-dimensional descriptors. Output is a set of one-to-one segment correspondences (or explicit non-matches via the dustbin) between the two images, represented as a discrete assignment derived from a soft doubly-stochastic matrix.

## Method

SegMASt3R repurposes the frozen MASt3R 3D foundation model—its Siamese ViT encoder and CroCo-style cross-view intertwined transformer decoder—as a geometry-aware feature extractor, then appends a lightweight MLP segment-feature head that upsamples 768-dim patch features to 24-dim pixel descriptors at full resolution and aggregates them within each mask via a single batched matrix multiplication (M_flat × P_flat^T) to yield 24-dim segment descriptors. Matching is performed by a differentiable optimal transport layer: a cosine-similarity affinity matrix is augmented with a learnable dustbin row and column (following SuperGlue), then iterated through 50 Sinkhorn normalisation steps in log-space to produce a soft bi-stochastic assignment, from which discrete correspondences are extracted by row-wise argmax over non-dustbin columns. Training uses a SuperGlue-style cross-entropy loss with explicit dustbin terms, optimised end-to-end (only the segment-feature head and dustbin parameter are trainable) on 860K ScanNet++ image pairs for 20 epochs on a single A6000 GPU. The key insight is that MASt3R's cross-view 3D-aware decoder provides geometric context that purely 2D backbones (DINOv2, CroCo alone) lack, and that aggregating these geometry-grounded pixel features at the segment level—rather than matching pixels directly—resolves perceptual instance aliasing under extreme viewpoint shifts.

## Results

On ScanNet++, SegMASt3R achieves AUPRC of 92.8% (0°–45°) down to 83.6% (135°–180°), outperforming the strongest local feature matcher (MASt3R LFM, 59.5%→45.4%) and the strongest segment matcher (SAM2, 61.9%→17.0%) by up to 38 AUPRC points in the widest-baseline bin. On the cross-dataset Replica benchmark, SegMASt3R maintains 95.0%→68.4% AUPRC versus MASt3R LFM's 78.2%→32.5%. On the outdoor MapFree dataset, the MapFree-fine-tuned model reaches 93.7% overall IoU, surpassing DINOv2 (84.4%) and vanilla MASt3R (69.2%). For downstream navigation (ImageInstanceNav), swapping RoboHop's matcher with SegMASt3R yields a 27-point absolute SPL gain under the sparsest submap setting (S_s=16, S_ρ=0.25).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SegMASt3R decisively outperforms all 11 baselines across every pose bin and dataset. Among local feature matchers, dense methods (RoMa, MASt3R LFM) beat sparse ones (SP-LG, GiM-DKM), but all remain 30–77 AUPRC points below SegMASt3R in the 135°–180° bin; notably, MASt3R's own segment-level aggregation (SegMatch variant) drops from 52.9 to 41.4 AUPRC at 90°–135°, revealing that pixel-level distinctiveness does not transfer to instance level. SAM2's video propagator, despite training on far larger data, degrades sharply beyond 45° (61.9→17.0 AUPRC), confirming its narrow-baseline bias. DINOv2 and SegVLAD achieve competitive R@5 (coarse retrieval) but lag substantially on AUPRC and R@1, reflecting their retrieval-oriented design. On the 3D instance mapping task (Replica), SegMASt3R with GT masks reaches 79.93 AP on office0 versus ConceptGraphs' 43.53 AP, and on navigation (ImageInstanceNav), SegMASt3R improves SPL from 36.34 to 63.60 over vanilla RoboHop under the sparsest submap configuration, demonstrating that the geometric matching advantage translates directly to downstream robotic performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the 3D foundation model MASt3R (itself built on CroCo cross-view completion and a ViT encoder) by appending a lightweight segment-feature head and a SuperGlue-style differentiable matching layer with Sinkhorn optimal transport and a learnable dustbin. It departs from prior segment-matching approaches (SAM2's video propagator, DINOv2/SegVLAD feature aggregation, MASA/DMESA) that rely exclusively on 2D appearance supervision, and from local feature matchers (SuperPoint, LoFTR, RoMa, MASt3R) that operate at pixel granularity. The key conceptual shift is injecting 3D geometric inductive bias—learned from metric depth and pose supervision—into segment-level descriptors, rather than treating segment matching as a purely 2D retrieval or propagation problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes wide-baseline segment matching (up to 180° viewpoint change) as a distinct and challenging sub-problem, providing a comprehensive benchmark that prior 2D-supervised methods fail on. The demonstrated downstream utility in 3D instance mapping and object-relative topological navigation (27% absolute SPL gain) makes the contribution practically relevant for robotics. Open questions include: whether the approach generalizes to outdoor/long-range settings without retraining (the MapFree results suggest partial success but still lag DINOv2 without dustbin recalibration), how it scales to very large numbers of segments, and whether the reliance on an external segmenter (SAM2/FastSAM) limits end-to-end robustness in dynamic scenes.
