## Problem

Video-based VLMs for monocular vision-and-language navigation suffer from three fundamental limitations: they cannot capture fine-grained 3D geometry and spatial semantics, they lack structured scene memory mechanisms for large-scale exploration and lifelong learning, and their frame-based representations degrade in dynamic environments where objects move. No prior approach combines a 3D-VLM with dynamically updated hierarchical scene representations for real-time monocular navigation in unseen and changing environments.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255133/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system consumes posed monocular RGB-D images from a forward-facing camera, a natural language navigation instruction, and the four most recent action tokens as history. It produces a single atomic navigation action per step (turn left/right by θ degrees, move forward d cm, or stop). The visual input to the 3D-VLM is a structured sequence of three token types: 12×48 rendered panoramic patch tokens (covering 90° vertical × 360° horizontal FOV via a generalizable feature field), 3D instance tokens, and 3D zone tokens, all sorted by Euclidean distance to the agent and augmented with relative positional embeddings (camera coordinates, distance, and horizontal angle).

## Method

Dynam3D constructs a three-level hierarchical 3D representation updated online at each observation. At the patch level, CLIP-ViT-L/14@336px features are projected into 3D world coordinates via depth maps and camera poses, with a Frustum Culling strategy that removes occluded or outdated points before adding new ones. At the instance level, FastSAM segments 2D masks, a transformer-based encoder aggregates patch features into instance representations, and a trained Merging Discriminator (binary classification over Top-K nearest 3D candidates) aligns new 2D instances with existing 3D instances to maintain multi-view geometric consistency. At the zone level, 3D space is partitioned into uniform cubic zones whose representations aggregate contained instance features for coarse-grained layout understanding. Training combines a contrastive language-alignment loss (SceneVerse, g3D-LF), a novel Subspace Contrastive Learning distillation loss that anchors instance features to a per-view semantic center rather than the CLIP origin to mitigate multi-view bias, and the Merging Discriminator classification loss. The final 3D-VLM uses LLaVA-Phi-3-mini (3.8B) as backbone, receiving the concatenated patch-instance-zone tokens plus instruction and history actions to predict the next navigation action.

## Results

On R2R-CE Val (monocular, continuous environment), Dynam3D achieves 52.9% SR and 45.7% SPL, outperforming the previous best methods g3D-LF (47.2% SR, 34.6% SPL) and Uni-NaVid (47.0% SR, 42.7% SPL) by ~5% in SR. On the more challenging REVERIE-CE benchmark, Dynam3D reaches 40.1% SR, surpassing NaVid (26.6%) by over 13% and g3D-LF (34.4%) by ~6%. On NavRAG-CE, Dynam3D achieves 24.7% SR vs. 19.4% (NaVid) and 21.4% (g3D-LF). Notably, Dynam3D uses a 3.8B-parameter LLM (LLaVA-Phi-3-mini) compared to Uni-NaVid's 7B model, while delivering superior performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Dynam3D outperforms all 8 baselines across every reported metric on R2R-CE Val. Against the closest competitors, it improves SR by 5.7 points over g3D-LF (52.9 vs. 47.2) and by 5.9 points over Uni-NaVid (52.9 vs. 47.0), while also achieving a substantially larger SPL gap (45.7 vs. 34.6 for g3D-LF; 45.7 vs. 42.7 for Uni-NaVid), indicating better path efficiency. On REVERIE-CE, the advantage is even more pronounced: Dynam3D beats NaVid by 13.5 SR points (40.1 vs. 26.6) and g3D-LF by 5.7 points (40.1 vs. 34.4). On NavRAG-CE, Dynam3D leads NaVid by 5.3 SR points (24.7 vs. 19.4) and g3D-LF by 3.3 points (24.7 vs. 21.4). The zero-shot baselines (InstructNav, AO-Planner) are significantly weaker, with SR below 31%, highlighting the benefit of task-specific training. Dynam3D also achieves the lowest navigation error (NE=5.34) among all methods, confirming more precise goal-reaching behavior.

## Relation to Prior Work

Dynam3D extends the feature-field line of work initiated by the same first author (g3D-LF, VLN-3DFF) by replacing flat 3D patch features with a hierarchical patch-instance-zone representation that is dynamically updated via Frustum Culling. It departs from the dominant video-based VLN paradigm (NaVid, Uni-NaVid, NaVILA) by arguing that temporal video frames are insufficient for capturing 3D geometry, structured scene memory, and dynamic scene changes. The 3D-VLM backbone (LLaVA-Phi-3-mini) and the Merging Discriminator for multi-view instance consistency draw on 3D-VLM literature (LLaVA-3D, SceneLLM) but reframe these components for online, incremental navigation rather than static scene QA.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first 3D-VLM system to handle monocular VLN in unseen and dynamically changing environments with real-time scene updates, addressing a gap that video-based models cannot fill. The pre-exploration and lifelong memory capabilities (>20% SR gain over NaVid) open a path toward agents that accumulate environmental knowledge over time, and the real-world Stretch 3 validation (including object relocation) demonstrates practical deployability. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to outdoor or large-scale multi-building settings, whether the 3.8B LLM can be further compressed without sacrificing the ~5% SR margin, and how to extend the action space beyond atomic turn/move/stop to support manipulation or dialogue-based task updates.
