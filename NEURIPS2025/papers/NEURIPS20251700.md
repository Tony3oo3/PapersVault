## Problem

Deterministic verification methods for neural networks become computationally intractable on the high-dimensional, pixel-level outputs of semantic segmentation networks, while existing probabilistic approaches (e.g., randomized smoothing) rely on union bounds that cause guarantees to deteriorate rapidly as output dimensionality grows. This paper addresses the gap by asking: can one provide scalable, architecture-agnostic probabilistic robustness guarantees for SSNs that avoid the excessive conservatism inherent in both deterministic and prior probabilistic methods?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251700/cropped_page9_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a multichannel image (RGB or grayscale, up to 720×960×3), a set of r adversarial noise directions with bounded coefficient vector λ ∈ [λ̲, λ̄] ⊂ R^r (where r ≪ n₀, defining a low-dimensional perturbation subspace), and a sampling distribution W over the input set. It produces a starset-shaped probabilistic reach set over the full logit tensor y ∈ R^{h×w×L}, from which each pixel is labeled robust, non-robust, or unknown, and an aggregate robustness value (RV) is computed. Unlike hyper-rectangular bounds, the starset output captures inter-pixel and inter-class correlations in the logit space.

## Method

The core technique is a conformal inference (CI)–based probabilistic reachability analysis that avoids the union-bound conservatism of randomized smoothing. A naive CI formulation yields a hyper-rectangular reachset via a max-normalized nonconformity score over logit components; to eliminate this shape conservatism, the paper trains a small surrogate ReLU network g on the perturbation subspace and computes its deterministic reach set as a starset using NNV, then inflates it with a CI-guaranteed hyperrectangle bounding the prediction error f−g (Minkowski sum). To make surrogate training tractable in the high-dimensional output space, a learning-based deflation PCA (Algorithm 1) first extracts N principal directions via guaranteed-convergence stochastic gradient ascent, reducing the problem to training a small ReLU network g₂: R^r → R^N in a low-dimensional space. The final guarantee is a double-step probabilistic bound ⟨ε,ℓ,m⟩ that decouples the choice of calibration size m, rank ℓ, and miscoverage ε, allowing the confidence level δ₂ to be driven arbitrarily close to 1 by increasing m.

## Results

On Cityscapes with HrNetV2-W48, the proposed conformal inference method achieves 0.064–0.076% uncertifiable pixels across all tested (σ, κ, r) settings, compared to 5–39% for Fischer et al. (SegCertify) and 5–28% for Anani et al. (AdaptiveCertify). The method scales to large segmentation models (up to 12.5M parameters, 720×960×12 output) on CamVid, Lung Segmentation, and OCTA-500 with average verification runtimes of 3.75–9 minutes per image. The probabilistic guarantee is a ⟨ε,ℓ,m⟩ double-step guarantee with coverage δ₁=0.999 and confidence δ₂=0.997 for the large-model experiments.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two randomized-smoothing-based probabilistic verification methods for segmentation: SegCertify (Fischer et al., ICML 2021) and AdaptiveCertify (Anani et al., 2024). On Cityscapes with HrNetV2-W48, the proposed conformal inference method reduces uncertifiable pixels by roughly two orders of magnitude (0.06–0.08% vs. 5–39% and 5–28%), a dramatic improvement across all six (σ, κ, r) configurations. However, the authors explicitly note this is not a pure superiority claim: their method assumes a prior sampling distribution over the input ball (probabilistic), whereas the baselines provide worst-case guarantees over the entire ℓ₂ ball, making the comparison a trade-off between distributional assumption and conservatism. The baselines also apply guarantees to a smoothed model rather than the base model directly. On scalability, the proposed method handles models up to 12.5M parameters with 720×960×12 outputs in under 9 minutes per image, whereas deterministic verification tools (α,β-CROWN, Marabou, PyRAT) are computationally infeasible at this scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends conformal inference (Vovk et al., 2005) from classification to the high-dimensional output space of semantic segmentation networks, a setting where prior probabilistic verification methods (randomized smoothing à la Fischer et al., 2021; Anani et al., 2024) suffer from union-bound conservatism. The key technical departure is the use of deflation PCA (Mackey, 2008) to make conformal inference tractable in high dimensions, combined with a surrogate ReLU network whose deterministic reach set (computed via NNV) is represented as a starset (Bak and Duggirala, 2017) and then inflated by a conformal error bound. This positions the work at the intersection of conformal prediction, reachability analysis, and dimensionality reduction, rather than as an incremental improvement to randomized smoothing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: deterministic verifiers (Marabou, α,β-CROWN) cannot scale to SSNs, and randomized smoothing methods produce 5–39% uncertifiable pixels on Cityscapes, whereas the proposed method reports ~0.07%. However, the authors are transparent that this gap partly reflects a distributional (prior-based) guarantee versus a worst-case one, so the comparison is not purely apples-to-apples. Open questions include whether the surrogate-based approach can verify spatial specifications across mask pixels, whether robust-CI can handle deployment distribution shift, and how the method scales when perturbation dimensionality grows large enough to force a fallback to the naive hyper-rectangular technique.
