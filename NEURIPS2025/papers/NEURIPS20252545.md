## Problem

Existing LLM prompting strategies (CoT, ToT, Best-of-N, Auto-CoT, ICL) apply fixed, general-purpose reasoning configurations that fail to achieve task-specific optimality, yet LLM performance is highly sensitive to hyperparameters like temperature, reasoning steps, and instruction format. The combinatorial search space over these factors makes manual tuning impractical, and no prior method systematically adapts the full configuration per-question in a data-efficient manner.

## Input / Output

AdaReasoner takes a text question and a target LLM as input, embedding the question via a pretrained BERT encoder (768-d) to produce a shared latent representation. It outputs a 3-dimensional discrete configuration vector: a generation temperature from {0.0, 0.1, …, 1.0}, a reasoning instruction drawn from a compositional space of 10 base cognitive strategies × 10 surface-form variations, and a number of CoT steps from {3, …, 10}. This configuration is then applied to the LLM to produce the final generated answer, making AdaReasoner a lightweight plug-in that sits between the question and the LLM call.

## Method

AdaReasoner is an RL-trained policy network that factorizes the joint configuration action into three independent heads (one per hyperparameter) sharing a common 3-layer MLP feature extractor, reducing the optimization space from multiplicative to additive. Training uses REINFORCE with a pretrained DeBERTa reward model that scores the LLM's generated answer against a ground-truth reference, providing a scalar, differentiable signal without requiring a ground-truth configuration. A Boltzmann exploration mechanism with exponentially annealed temperature (τ_t = τ_0 · α^t) balances exploration and exploitation in the few-shot regime (100 training samples), and the paper proves sublinear regret O(√(|A| ln|A| / K)) and an SGD error bound of 2(J(Θ*)−J(Θ_0))/(ηK) + Lησ². The reasoning instruction space is grounded in structure-mapping theory from cognitive psychology, composing a base cognitive strategy (e.g., creative reasoning, self-audit) with a contextual variation (e.g., challenge assumptions, validate with empirical data), enabling fine-grained adaptation across logical, creative, and subjective task types.

## Results

AdaReasoner achieves the highest average accuracy across all six backbone LLMs, ranging from 77.49% (Qwen-2.5-72B) to 86.28% (DeepSeek-R1), consistently outperforming the best fixed-configuration baseline by 1.3–5.2 percentage points. On GPT-4o, AdaReasoner reaches 80.42% average (vs. 74.42% for In-context CoT, the strongest baseline), with particularly large gains on Metaphor (71.56% vs. 62.33% for Auto-CoT) and LogiQA (82.31% vs. 80.04% for In-context CoT). On the strongest reasoning model DeepSeek-R1, AdaReasoner attains 86.28% average, surpassing ToT (82.85%) by 3.4 points. Few-shot training with only 100 samples per dataset suffices for convergence, with marginal gains beyond 100 shots.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AdaReasoner achieves the highest average accuracy on all six LLMs, beating the strongest per-model baseline by 1.3–5.2 points. Against CoT and Think Short (the simplest baselines), AdaReasoner wins decisively on every model–dataset combination, with gaps of 5–20+ points. ToT is the most competitive baseline on MMLU (e.g., 98.70% on DeepSeek-R1 vs. AdaReasoner's 96.33%, and 98.18% on GPT-o3-mini vs. 96.13%), reflecting its strength on structured mathematical reasoning, but AdaReasoner recovers the lead on the other three datasets and in overall average. Auto-CoT is the closest rival on TruthfulQA (e.g., 83.49% vs. 83.82% on Qwen) and In-context CoT edges out AdaReasoner on Metaphor for Qwen (73.39% vs. 65.19%), suggesting that in-context exemplars remain effective for certain creative/figurative tasks. Best-of-N is consistently outperformed, confirming that unguided multi-sample selection is inferior to adaptive configuration. The ablation (Table 2) further shows that adapting only the reasoning instruction a_p retains 78.62% average (vs. 80.42% full), while adapting only temperature or steps drops to ~74%, underscoring that the joint three-way adaptation is what drives the gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

AdaReasoner extends the line of work on LLM reasoning strategies (CoT, ToT, Best-of-N, Auto-CoT, ICL) by reframing the selection of reasoning hyperparameters as a multi-armed bandit problem solved via REINFORCE with Boltzmann exploration. It departs from prior prompting methods that apply fixed, task-agnostic configurations by introducing a lightweight RL-trained adapter that selects a factorized action (instruction format × temperature × step count) per query, drawing on the structure-mapping theory from cognitive psychology to compose instruction prompts. The use of a pretrained DeBERTa reward model as a proxy for answer quality borrows from the RLHF literature but is applied to a small policy network rather than the LLM itself, and the theoretical grounding in non-stochastic bandit regret bounds connects to classical exploration-exploitation theory.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in providing an LLM-agnostic, few-shot (100-sample) plugin that consistently improves reasoning across six diverse LLMs without modifying the backbone model, lowering the barrier for non-expert users to get task-tailored reasoning. Key limitations include the manually defined discrete action space, the need for per-task fine-tuning, and poor cross-model transfer (Qwen-trained policy drops ~8 points on GPT-4o), suggesting the learned policy encodes model-specific reward landscapes. Open questions include whether continuous action spaces or gradient-based prompt generation could capture finer-grained reasoning strategies, and whether the approach scales to additional hyperparameters or multi-turn settings.
