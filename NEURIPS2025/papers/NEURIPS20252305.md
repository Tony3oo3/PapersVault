## Problem

As LLM agents and RAG systems increasingly interact with semi-structured data, two challenges make traditional querying inadequate: (1) the growing diversity of domains and schema variations renders domain-customized formal methods (e.g., NL2SQL) prohibitively costly, and (2) modern NL queries combine exact field matching, fuzzy semantic requirements, numerical reasoning, and multi-field logical conditions that keyword-based search cannot handle. The paper asks whether neural dense retrievers can serve as a unified, non-formal querying solution over massive heterogeneous semi-structured collections.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252305/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The retriever takes a natural language query (containing 1+ exact matching conditions, 1+ fuzzy semantic conditions, and optional scenario descriptions, abbreviations, or alternative number/date representations) and a collection of semi-structured JSON objects (14M documents across 99 schemas in 6 domains) as input. Documents are encoded as multi-line JSON strings with 4-space indentation (shown to outperform compact JSON and YAML). The output is a ranked list of top-k data objects scored by semantic relevance, evaluated with Recall@20 and nDCG@10 under both in-schema and in-domain cross-schema retrieval settings.

## Method

SSRB is constructed via a three-stage LLM-based synthesis pipeline: (1) schema generation using a powerful LLM to produce 99 distinct schemas across 6 manually defined domains; (2) data triplet generation using a strong but cheaper LLM (Qwen2.5-32B) to produce ~70k <query, positive, negative> triplets per schema by varying five configuration slots (number of exact/semantic conditions, scenario descriptions, abbreviations, number/date representations), yielding 14M total documents; (3) relevance annotation where a committee of 8 retrievers recalls ~50 candidates per query, and two LLMs (Gemini 2.0 Flash + GPT-4o-mini) judge relevance in parallel with an OR-gate strategy, validated against human annotation (75.98% agreement vs. 86.25% inter-annotator). For evaluation, the paper benchmarks encoder-based (InstructOR, BGE, Jina-v3, Nomic-v2) and LLM-based (E5-mistral-7B, GTE-Qwen2-7B, GritLM-7B, NV-Embed-v2, Drama-1B) dense retrievers plus BM25, and demonstrates that fine-tuning Qwen2.5-0.5B/1.5B on 1M generated triplets via InfoNCE contrastive learning with in-batch negatives (batch size 104, lr 1e-5, 1 epoch on 4 A800s) achieves performance comparable to or exceeding 7B generalist models.

## Results

On the SSRB in-schema retrieval task (Marco Avg.), the best pre-trained LLM-based retriever E5-mistral-7b achieves R@20=14.66 and N@10=17.94, while the best encoder-based model Nomic-v2 reaches only R@20=6.06 and N@10=8.10. BM25 performs worst with R@20=2.01. Fine-tuning small Qwen2.5 models (0.5B/1.5B) on 1M generated triplets yields R@20=22.70/24.17 and N@10=22.03/22.09, substantially outperforming all 7B pre-trained models and demonstrating the effectiveness of the SSRB training data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates 10 retrievers as baselines on SSRB. BM25 (R@20=2.01) is the weakest, confirming that lexical matching is insufficient for complex NL queries over semi-structured data. Encoder-based models (InstructOR R@20=4.06, BGE R@20=3.99, Jina-v3 R@20=5.85, Nomic-v2 R@20=6.06) improve over BM25 by roughly 2–3× but remain far below LLM-based retrievers. Among LLM-based models, E5-mistral-7b leads with R@20=14.66 and N@10=17.94, narrowly ahead of NV-Embed-v2 (14.52/17.26) and Drama-1B (14.42/16.71), while GTE-Qwen2-7B (13.66/16.35) and GritLM-7B (13.65/16.66) trail by ~1 point. The fine-tuned Qwen2.5-1.5B (R@20=24.17) outperforms all pre-trained 7B models by 9–10 points in R@20, showing that task-specific training on SSRB data closes much of the gap to larger models. Cross-schema retrieval (Table 4) shows only a small consistent degradation (~0.5–1 R@20) across all models, indicating that schema mixing adds limited additional difficulty.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions neural dense retrieval as a unified alternative to the two dominant paradigms for querying semi-structured data: formal-language methods (NL2SQL) and keyword-based full-text search. It builds directly on the LLM-based retrieval data synthesis line (E5-mistral, DRAMA, Promptagator) and LLM-based relevance judgment (SyNDL), extending these techniques to the specific setting of heterogeneous semi-structured objects with multi-condition NL queries. The evaluation framework draws on the BEIR benchmarking tradition but introduces a fundamentally different corpus structure (JSON/YAML semi-structured objects across 99 schemas) and query complexity (combined exact and fuzzy matching with numerical/logical reasoning) that neither passage retrieval nor table QA benchmarks capture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

With LLM agents and RAG systems increasingly interacting with diverse semi-structured data sources, SSRB provides the first large-scale benchmark (14M objects, 99 schemas, 6 domains) to measure how well neural retrievers handle complex multi-condition NL queries over such data. The finding that even the best LLM-based retrievers achieve under 40% recall, while small fine-tuned models (Qwen2.5-1.5B) surpass 7B generalist models by ~10 points, highlights both the difficulty of the task and the practical value of task-specific training data. Key open questions include how to improve precise numerical and logical constraint matching, whether the LLM-generated data introduces systematic biases that limit real-world transfer, and how model scaling affects performance on this task.
