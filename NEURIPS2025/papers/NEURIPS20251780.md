## Problem

Direct-integration Vision-Language Prompting (VLP) methods that add visual regularization loss to improve novel-class generalization fail to significantly improve on higher-difficulty tasks like domain generalization and cross-dataset generalization. The paper identifies the root cause as uncoordinated learning between the cross-entropy loss (which drives few-shot fine-tuning) and the visual loss (which preserves pre-trained features), creating a natural tradeoff where one objective's improvement comes at the other's expense.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251780/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on a frozen CLIP ViT-B/16 backbone, taking a single RGB image as input and producing a class label via cosine similarity between text and visual embeddings in a shared space. It is a plug-in module compatible with existing VLP baselines (MaPLe, PromptSRC, CoPrompt), requiring no additional learnable parameters beyond the baseline's own prompts. Training uses 16-shot settings with N=60 hand-crafted text prompts (e.g., 'a photo of a', 'a drawing of a') to generate reference features, and the w-worst (w=4) of these are selected to form the regularization signal.

## Method

LePa (Levelling Paradigm) is a plug-in loss module that dynamically coordinates the vision-language alignment objective and the visual regularization objective. It computes cosine similarities between N hand-crafted prompt features and the baseline's learnable visual features, selects the w-worst-scoring prompt templates, and averages their text features to represent a 'bad case' reference. A Hand-crafted CE loss (L_HCE) is then computed between this averaged reference and the learnable visual features. The LePa loss is defined as the absolute difference |L_HCE − L_Visual|, which penalizes divergence between the two objectives and forces them to converge together rather than in opposition. The total training loss is L_CE + γ₁·L_LePa + γ₂·L_Visual (with γ₁=γ₂=5), adding zero extra learnable parameters while requiring only additional cosine similarity computations during training.

## Results

On cross-dataset generalization (ImageNet→10 target datasets), LePa+PromptSRC achieves 66.93% average accuracy, surpassing the PromptSRC baseline (65.81%) and the strongest plug-in competitor DOR (66.03%). On base-to-novel generalization across 11 datasets, LePa+PSRC reaches 81.33% harmonic mean (HM), outperforming PSRC (79.97%), CoPrompt (80.13%), and MaPLe (78.55%). On domain generalization (ImageNet→ImageNet-R/-S/-A/-V2), LePa+PSRC achieves 78.75% on ImageNet-R and 52.33% on ImageNet-A, consistently leading all baselines on the four shifted targets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LePa consistently outperforms all three VLP baselines (MaPLe, PromptSRC, CoPrompt) and both plug-in competitors (DePT, DOR) on cross-dataset generalization: LePa+PSRC reaches 66.93% average vs. PSRC alone (65.81%), DOR+PSRC (66.03%), and DePT+PSRC (66.02%). On base-to-novel generalization (11-dataset HM), LePa+PSRC achieves 81.33%, beating PSRC (79.97%), CoPrompt (80.13%), DOR+PSRC (80.48%), and DePT+PSRC (80.43). On domain generalization, LePa+PSRC leads on all four ImageNet variants (e.g., 78.75% on ImageNet-R vs. 77.80% PSRC, 77.40% CoPrompt; 52.33% on ImageNet-A vs. 50.90% PSRC). The one consistent trade-off is on the source ImageNet column, where LePa scores slightly below DePT (e.g., 71.50 vs. 71.60 for PSRC), which the authors acknowledge as a compromise between in-distribution fine-tuning and out-of-distribution generalization. LePa adds zero learnable parameters (+0K) compared to DePT's +(2+N/2)K, at the cost of ~1.7 min extra training time on SUN397 over 10 epochs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the direct-integration Vision-Language Prompt Learning (VLP) line of work (MaPLe, PromptSRC, CoPrompt) that learns joint textual and visual prompts on frozen CLIP. It departs from prior plug-in approaches (DePT, DOR) by targeting a specific diagnostic: the 'uncoordinated learning' between the cross-entropy fine-tuning loss and the visual regularization loss, which the authors argue explains why visual loss helps base-to-novel generalization but degrades cross-dataset and domain generalization. The LePa module introduces a Hand-crafted CE (HCE) loss derived from N pre-defined CLIP prompt templates and balances it against the visual loss via an absolute-difference regularizer, adding no learnable parameters.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, parameter-free plug-in that consistently (if modestly) improves generalization across 11 datasets and three task settings, offering a simple remedy for the known tradeoff between fine-tuning and regularization in VLP frameworks. However, the gains are typically 0.5–1.5 points, the method increases training time by ~10–30%, and it slightly degrades source-dataset accuracy relative to DePT. Open questions include whether the hand-crafted prompt selection (w-worst scoring) is robust across architectures and scales, and whether the approach extends to 1- or 2-shot settings where the CE loss signal is even weaker.
