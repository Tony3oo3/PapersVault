## Problem

Existing privacy-preserving face recognition methods that operate in the frequency domain still leave residual visual clues exploitable by generative reconstruction attacks (StyleGAN, U-Net, diffusion models), because the inherent sparsity of frequency-domain features preserves global structures and local consistencies. The root cause is that identity-related information is concentrated in a few dominant components scattered across low and high frequency channels, and simply filtering or shuffling channels does not adequately disrupt the spatial regularities that generative models exploit for inversion.

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard RGB face image (3×H×W), normalizes it to [0,1], upsamples by a scaling factor r, converts to YCbCr, and applies Block-based DCT on non-overlapping 8×8 blocks to produce a 192-channel localized frequency representation. After FCR prunes to 162 channels and further selects 81 identity-relevant channels, FFM remaps these into a fractal-structured token of shape [81, H/8, W/8] that is stored as an irreversible identity representation. Recognition is then performed by an IR-50 backbone trained on these 81-channel fractal tokens with ArcMargin loss, producing a standard identity verification score.

## Method

FracFace introduces two synergistic modules operating at different levels of the frequency representation. The Frequency Channel Refining (FCR) module applies a three-level Frequency Band Attenuation (FBA) strategy that selectively prunes low-frequency channels to reduce sparsity and prevent low-frequency dominance from suppressing high-frequency identity cues, then partitions the remaining 162 channels into two priority groups for granular privacy control. The Frequency Fractal Mapping (FFM) module remaps the refined 81 channels into a fractal structure space via iterative integer-based perturbations: a randomly initialized fractal kernel M₀ and lattice L₀ are expanded recursively with scaling factor β_k = 3^(2k), producing a non-invertible, nonlinear index transformation that disrupts spatial continuity between frequency channels while preserving self-similar identity structure. The nonlinearity and non-injectivity of the fractal mapping (proven via violation of homogeneity and additivity) ensure that even white-box adversaries cannot resolve original channel correspondences. The full pipeline is trained end-to-end on MS1Mv2 with an IR-50 backbone, achieving near-baseline recognition accuracy while reducing reconstruction similarity (SSIM) by 15–60% compared to prior methods under both U-Net and StyleGAN attacks.

## Results

FracFace achieves near-baseline recognition accuracy across all eight benchmarks while providing 10% frequency-channel protection: 99.69% on LFW, 95.91% on CelebA, 97.76% on AgeDB, and 94.09%/92.26% on IJB-C (1e-4/1e-5). Under U-Net reconstruction attacks, FracFace reduces SSIM to as low as 0.2045 (AgeDB) and raises LPIPS to 0.7796 (CFP-FP), while under StyleGAN attacks it achieves SSIM as low as 0.1045 (CPLFW) and LPIPS up to 0.8307 (LFW). The IDS (identity similarity) metric drops to 0.0003–0.0081 under U-Net attacks, indicating near-complete identity erasure, compared to 0.26–0.88 for all competing methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FracFace matches or exceeds all privacy-preserving baselines on recognition accuracy while using only 10% channel protection (vs. 13–82% for competitors). On LFW, FracFace (99.69%) is within 0.13% of the unprotected ArcFace baseline (99.73%) and outperforms PPFR-FD (99.39%) and PRO-Face C (99.29%). On CelebA, FracFace (95.91%) surpasses all PPFR methods except MinusFace (95.89%) by a narrow margin, while DuetFace (92.13%) and PRO-Face C (91.69%) lose 3–4% accuracy. On IJB-C (1e-4), FracFace (94.09%) trails MinusFace (95.91%) and ArcFace-FD (95.89%) but leads PartialFace (93.85%) and FaceObfuscator (94.43%). The critical differentiator is privacy: under StyleGAN attacks, FracFace achieves SSIM of 0.1045–0.2836 and LPIPS of 0.6354–0.8307, substantially outperforming FaceObfuscator (SSIM 0.1395–0.3654, LPIPS 0.5320–0.7419) and MinusFace (SSIM 0.1699–0.3421, LPIPS 0.5894–0.7313), while under U-Net attacks its IDS drops to 0.0003–0.0081 versus 0.26–0.88 for all other methods, indicating near-total identity erasure.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the frequency-domain PPFR lineage (PPFR-FD, DuetFace, PartialFace, MinusFace, FaceObfuscator) by identifying frequency-domain sparsity as the root cause of residual visual clue leakage that prior channel-selection methods fail to address. Rather than merely filtering or shuffling DCT channels, FracFace introduces a two-stage pipeline: Frequency Channel Refining (FCR) to reduce sparsity and suppress non-identity bands, followed by Frequency Fractal Mapping (FFM) that remaps the refined channels into a self-similar fractal index structure, creating a non-invertible, nonlinear transformation. The conceptual departure is the use of fractal geometry—specifically its self-similarity and local chaos—as a structured (rather than random) obfuscation mechanism, which the authors argue preserves identity-discriminative information while disrupting the spatial continuity that generative reconstruction models exploit.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work addresses a timely and practical concern: as StyleGAN and diffusion-based inversion attacks mature, frequency-domain PPFR methods that merely suppress or shuffle channels leave exploitable structural patterns. FracFace's near-zero IDS under U-Net attacks and substantially reduced SSIM/LPIPS under StyleGAN attacks demonstrate that fractal remapping can close a gap that prior methods leave open. However, the defense remains weaker against generative attacks (IDS ~0.52–0.67 under StyleGAN vs. ~0.001–0.008 under U-Net), and the approach is validated only with an IR-50 backbone on a single training set (MS1Mv2), leaving open whether the fractal structure generalizes across architectures or biometric modalities. A key open question is whether the fractal mapping can be given formal security guarantees (e.g., information-theoretic bounds on recoverable identity bits) rather than relying solely on empirical reconstruction metrics.
