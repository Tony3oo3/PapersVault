## Problem

Posterior inference in latent SDE models is typically intractable due to non-conjugate structure, and existing variational inference methods (notably VDP) suffer from slow convergence and numerical instability. No prior method provides both efficient, geometry-aware optimization of the variational posterior and a theoretical guarantee connecting the discrete-time ELBO to the true continuous-time objective.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255146/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

SING consumes conditionally independent noisy observations y(t_i) ∈ R^N at discrete timestamps t_i ∈ [0, T_max] (with N ≫ D in the low-dimensional latent regime), along with fixed or learnable model hyperparameters Θ = {θ, C, d}. It outputs a full posterior trajectory over the D-dimensional latent state as a linear time-varying SDE with block-tridiagonal precision, a drift function estimate (parametric or GP with posterior uncertainty), and learned hyperparameters. In the GP-SDE variant (SING-GP), the drift posterior is evaluated at arbitrary query points via sparse GP inducing-point approximations.

## Method

SING applies natural gradient variational inference (NGVI) to the discrete-time ELBO of a latent SDE model, exploiting the fact that the variational posterior (a linear time-varying SDE) is a minimal exponential family whose Fisher information equals the Jacobian of the natural-to-mean parameter map, yielding closed-form local updates that decompose over time steps. Two key computational tricks make the updates tractable: (i) Stein's lemma reduces intractable expectations over pairwise distributions from R^{2D} to R^D, and (ii) associative scans parallelize the natural-to-mean parameter conversion from O(D³T) to O(D³ log T) wall-clock time. A theoretical guarantee (Theorem 1) bounds the gap between the discrete and continuous-time ELBOs by C(Δt)^{1/2}. The SING-GP extension augments the variational posterior with a sparse GP over the drift, enabling nonparametric drift estimation with posterior uncertainty in a single variational EM loop.

## Results

On the linear Gaussian benchmark, SING recovers the exact posterior in a single iteration (matching Kalman smoothing), while VDP requires several iterations to converge. On the 50-dimensional embedded Lorenz attractor, SING achieves a latents RMSE of 0.6778 using a single Monte Carlo sample per expectation, with Monte Carlo matching quadrature accuracy (0.1419 vs 0.1417) at D=3. For drift estimation on the Duffing equation, SING recovers true latents and dynamics in fewer than 25 variational EM iterations, whereas VDP yields worse performance on both metrics after 50 iterations. On the neural aggression dataset, SING-GP maintains high forward-simulation R² for trajectories up to 15 seconds ahead, while VDP-GP's predictive performance degrades more rapidly and is sensitive to discretization size.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SING matches Kalman smoothing exactly in the conjugate linear-Gaussian setting, converging in one iteration as predicted by theory, while VDP [7] requires multiple iterations to reach the same solution. In the non-conjugate place cell model (Van der Pol dynamics + Poisson observations), SING outperforms VDP, Adam [52], and CMGS [54] in both convergence speed and final latent RMSE; VDP requires careful learning-rate tuning for stability and CMGS incurs error from its linear approximations. For drift estimation on the Duffing equation, SING consistently outperforms VDP across all three drift classes (GP, neural-SDE, polynomial basis) in both latent and dynamics RMSE, recovering the true dynamics in under 25 iterations versus 50 for VDP. On the neural aggression dataset, SING-GP outperforms VDP-GP [13] in final expected log-likelihood and forward-simulation R², and is notably more robust to the choice of discretization Δt. Against SDE Matching [41] on the Lorenz attractor, SING achieves better latent recovery but slightly worse drift recovery. Parallelized SING is approximately 100× faster than its sequential counterpart at T=4096 time steps.

## Relation to Prior Work

SING extends the variational inference framework for latent SDEs established by Archambeau et al. (VDP), which optimized the continuous-time ELBO via Lagrange multipliers, by replacing that optimization with natural gradient VI that exploits the exponential family structure of the Gaussian Markov variational posterior. It builds directly on Verma et al.'s (2024) proposal to apply NGVI to the discrete-time ELBO, but addresses the key gaps in that work: tractable computation of the ELBO terms via Stein's lemma, efficient natural-to-mean parameter conversion via associative scans, and the first explicit convergence rate for the discrete-to-continuous ELBO approximation. For drift estimation, SING-GP extends the GP-SDE formulation of Duncker et al. (2019) by coupling sparse GP inference with the natural gradient updates rather than the VDP Lagrange multiplier scheme.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SING provides a theoretically grounded, computationally efficient alternative to VDP for latent SDE inference, with the first explicit O(Δt^{1/2}) convergence guarantee for the discrete-time ELBO approximation and O(D³ log T) parallel scaling via associative scans. The practical impact is demonstrated on neural dynamics modeling, where SING-GP's robustness to coarse discretization enables memory-efficient inference on long recordings. Open questions include adaptive time discretization, learnable inducing point locations in SING-GP, and whether the natural gradient framework extends to the broader class of non-Gaussian (neural-SDE) variational families where the exponential family structure is lost.
