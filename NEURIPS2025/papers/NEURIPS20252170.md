## Problem

Existing DT-based offline safe RL methods (e.g., CDT) handle single-constraint, multi-budget settings but fail in multi-constraint scenarios where cost functions differ across tasks, because they cannot distinguish between diverse constraint specifications. Additionally, their reliance on heuristically specified RTG inputs creates an unresolved conflict between reward maximization and safety compliance: when RTG and CTG objectives are misaligned, the policy prioritizes reward at the expense of safety, especially under tight budgets.

## Input / Output

At deployment, the policy receives the initial safety threshold b (serving as CTG), a single expert trajectory for constraint identification (or L trajectories for unseen tasks), and the environment ID to select the appropriate state/action encoder. The CPRTG generator dynamically produces the RTG token at each step by sampling from a learned conditional distribution p(RTG|CTG, state, prompt, history) and selecting a CTG-dependent β-quantile, replacing the static RTG used in standard DT. The policy outputs a Gaussian-distributed continuous action and a deterministic cost prediction, with the cost head explicitly trained to capture constraint-specific patterns for improved task identification.

## Method

CoPDT introduces two key mechanisms atop a standard DT backbone. First, a constraint prioritized prompt encoder partitions a reference expert trajectory into safe (c=0) and unsafe (c=1) segments using the binary cost signal, encoding each segment with a dedicated sub-encoder (p_s, p_u) and averaging to produce a task representation z; this encoder is trained with joint reconstruction losses for state, reward, and cost prediction to ensure it captures constraint-specific structure. Second, a CPRTG generator models the conditional distribution p(RTG|CTG, state) as a Gaussian via a lightweight MLP, then at deployment samples X candidates and selects the β_t-quantile, where β_t decays linearly as CTG decreases (less remaining safety budget → more conservative RTG), thereby generating conflict-free RTGs that adapt to the current safety margin. Environment-specific autoencoders align heterogeneous state/action dimensions across tasks, and the final DT policy uses dual output heads (Gaussian action + deterministic cost) trained with negative log-likelihood, entropy regularization, and cost prediction loss.

## Results

On the OSRL benchmark (26 tasks), CoPDT achieves the lowest average cost across all three multi-task settings: 1.11 in Single-constraint Multi-budget, 1.66 in Multi-constraint Single-budget, and 1.45 in Multi-constraint Multi-budget. In the multi-constraint settings, CoPDT reduces average cost by over 60% compared to MTCDT (4.48/4.20) and Prompt-CDT (4.81/4.38), while maintaining competitive reward (0.36–0.38). The paper reports that CoPDT satisfies safety constraints in more than twice as many tasks as the best DT-based baseline (CDT/MTCDT/Prompt-CDT).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoPDT outperforms all baselines on safety (cost) across every setting. In Single-constraint Multi-budget, CoPDT (c=1.11) beats CDT (c=2.19) by nearly 2×, LSPC (c=1.88), and FISOR (c=1.37), while achieving comparable reward to CDT (0.40 vs 0.56). In Multi-constraint Single-budget, CoPDT (c=1.66) dramatically outperforms MTCDT (c=4.48) and Prompt-CDT (c=4.81) by over 60%, though at a reward cost (0.36 vs 0.57–0.59). The same pattern holds in Multi-constraint Multi-budget (CoPDT c=1.45 vs MTCDT 4.20, Prompt-CDT 4.38). CPQ, a single-task method, performs poorly in multi-budget settings (c=3.54) due to its inability to handle mixed safe/unsafe trajectories. FISOR achieves strong safety but cannot adapt to multiple budgets via a unified policy, and LSPC, while competitive on safety in single-task, does not support multi-budget or multi-constraint adaptation. The key differentiator is CoPDT's CPRTG mechanism resolving the RTG–CTG conflict, which CDT and its multi-constraint extensions (MTCDT, Prompt-CDT) fail to address, leading to severe safety violations under tighter budgets.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoPDT extends the Decision Transformer lineage for offline safe RL by building on CDT's CTG token formalism and MGDT's Bayes-rule-based RTG selection, while replacing Prompt-DT's simple trajectory-segment prompts with a constraint-prioritized prompt encoder that exploits sparse binary cost signals to separate safe and unsafe trajectory segments. The key departure from prior DT-based safe RL methods is the CPRTG mechanism, which dynamically generates RTG targets conditioned on the current CTG rather than relying on heuristically specified static RTG inputs, thereby resolving the reward-cost conflict that limits adaptability across varying safety budgets. The theoretical analysis in the appendix further positions CPRTG within the offline RL extrapolation-error framework (building on MBPO-style bounds), showing that generating RTGs from the offline data distribution reduces the deployment-time performance gap.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses a practically important gap: deploying a single unified safe policy across multiple tasks with different cost functions and safety budgets, a setting that arises naturally in autonomous driving and robotic control but has been poorly handled by existing DT-based methods. The constraint-prioritized prompt encoder and CPRTG mechanism together provide a modular recipe that could be adopted by other sequence-modeling safe RL approaches. However, the evaluation is confined to the 26-task OSRL benchmark, zero-shot generalization to unseen constraints still shows a notable gap versus task-specific training, and the multi-constraint baselines (MTCDT, Prompt-CDT) are relatively weak extensions of CDT, leaving open whether the claimed 'twice as many safe tasks' margin would hold against stronger multi-task baselines. Future directions include scaling to broader task distributions, integrating LLM-based zero-cost exploration, and embodied deployment.
