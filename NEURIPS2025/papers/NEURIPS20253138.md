## Problem

Existing approximate machine unlearning methods—fine-tuning (minimizing retain loss) and negative gradient (maximizing forget loss)—converge to suboptimal regions in the loss landscape, resulting in either insufficient forgetting or significant degradation on retain data. The paper identifies that these single-objective approaches fail to navigate the coupled forget/retain loss geometry and proposes a min-max optimization framework that explicitly explores high-forget-loss neighborhoods to reach better optima.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253138/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained model (ResNet-18 for CIFAR-10/100, VGG for TinyImageNet, or Zephyr-7B for LLM unlearning), a forget set D_f, and a retain set D_r, and produces updated model weights that approximate the exact retrain-from-scratch solution. Each optimization iteration requires two forward and backward passes: one to compute the forget gradient for the inner maximization perturbation, and one to compute the retain gradient at the perturbed point for the outer minimization. For LLM unlearning, the method operates on feature representations extracted from specific transformer layers (layers 5–7) rather than full model parameters.

## Method

UAM formulates unlearning as a two-stage min-max problem: an inner maximization identifies a surrogate weight ŵ = w + ρ∇L_f/‖∇L_f‖² within a local neighborhood that maximizes the forget loss, and an outer minimization reduces the retain loss evaluated at ŵ. A first-order Taylor approximation collapses the inner max to a closed-form gradient-direction step, yielding the simplified objective min_w L(w + ρ∇L_f/‖∇L_f‖², D_r). The resulting gradient update (Eq. 15) applies a projection [I − γP_f] to the retain gradient, removing its component aligned with the forget gradient; this geometrically drives the cosine similarity between retain and forget gradients negative, so that minimizing retain loss inherently increases forget loss. The Hessian term from the exact chain-rule gradient is approximated by the identity matrix (controlled by γ), reducing computational cost while preserving effective unlearning, as validated by ablation studies showing that omitting the projection entirely (γ=0) fails to reduce forget accuracy.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 class-wise forgetting, UAM achieves a ΔAcc of 0.82 with zero forget accuracy (FA = 0.00%) and 94.51% test accuracy, closely matching the Retrain oracle (ΔAcc = 0.00, FA = 0.00%, TA = 95.32%). Under random data forgetting, UAM attains ΔAcc = 2.53 (FA = 95.19%, TA = 92.74%), substantially outperforming all other methods. On the LLM unlearning benchmark (Zephyr-7B), UAM achieves the lowest WMDP-Bio score (0.2930) and WMDP-Cyber score (0.2330) while retaining 0.5644 MMLU accuracy, yielding the best overall ΔAcc of −0.5344.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UAM decisively outperforms FT and NG across all image classification settings: on CIFAR-10 class-wise forgetting, UAM's ΔAcc of 0.82 is 53× lower than FT (43.63) and 30× lower than NG (24.45), while under random data forgetting the gap widens to 1.8× over FT (4.48) and 46× over NG (115.77). Against FF, UAM wins on CIFAR-10 (0.82 vs. 10.22) and Tiny-ImageNet class-wise (1.75 vs. N/A due to memory), but FF achieves a slightly lower ΔAcc on CIFAR-100 class-wise (0.37 vs. 1.30). UAM also surpasses IU on CIFAR-10 (0.82 vs. 13.32 class-wise; 2.53 vs. 8.81 random) and Tiny-ImageNet class-wise (1.75 vs. 7.49), though ℓ₁-sparse edges out UAM on Tiny-ImageNet random data forgetting (6.12 vs. 16.23). On the LLM benchmark, UAM achieves the best ΔAcc (−0.5344), beating RMU (−0.4754), SCRUB (−0.1770), SSD (−0.0510), and LLMU (+0.0470), with the largest WMDP-Cyber reduction (0.2330 vs. RMU's 0.2763) at only a marginal 0.0016 MMLU cost over RMU.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the min-max optimization structure from Sharpness-Aware Minimization (SAM) and adversarial training to the machine unlearning domain, where the inner maximization identifies a surrogate weight with high forget loss and the outer minimization uses retain-loss gradients at that perturbed point. It departs from the standard dual-objective formulations (fine-tuning minimizes retain loss; negative gradient maximizes forget loss) and from Fisher/influence-function-based methods (FF, IU) by unifying both objectives into a single bi-level problem. For LLM unlearning, UAM is integrated into the RMU framework from the WMDP benchmark, replacing its fixed random unit vector with a unified loss formulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The geometric interpretation—showing that UAM drives the cosine similarity between retain and forget gradients negative—provides a principled explanation for why the method works and connects to recent projection-based unlearning approaches. The finding that the fixed random vector in RMU is unnecessary for effective LLM unlearning challenges a working assumption in that line of work. Open questions include a formal justification for why the identity-matrix Hessian approximation suffices, the computational overhead of two forward/backward passes per iteration, and whether the approach extends to settings where forget data are only partially accessible.
