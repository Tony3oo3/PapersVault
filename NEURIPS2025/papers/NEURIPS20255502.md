## Problem

Existing multi-agent LLM systems rely on static or handcrafted organizational topologies that lack flexibility and scalability, leading to coordination overhead, redundant computation, and degraded performance as agent diversity and task complexity grow. The paper asks whether a dynamic, learnable orchestration mechanism can simultaneously maximize collaborative effectiveness and computational efficiency without requiring extensive parameter retraining of individual agents.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255502/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a text task specification and a heterogeneous pool of LLM-based agents (each parameterized by a foundation model, a reasoning pattern such as decomposition/reflection/critique, and optional external tools like code interpreters or web search). At each step the orchestrator observes the evolving global system state and selects a single agent to activate; the process terminates when a designated Terminator agent is invoked or the step budget is exhausted, at which point an aggregation function (majority voting) combines all agent outputs into a final answer or artifact. The learned policy itself—mapping states to agent-selection distributions—is the secondary output that persists across tasks.

## Method

The core contribution is a centralized 'puppeteer' orchestrator that serializes multi-agent collaboration into a sequential decision process: at each timestep it selects one agent from the pool conditioned on the current global state and task description, satisfying the Markov property. The serialized sequence can be 'folded' back into a directed graph-of-thought, enabling cycles, cross-branch connections, and feedback loops that static topologies cannot express. The orchestrator is trained online via REINFORCE policy gradient, with a composite reward that recursively combines a terminal correctness/quality score with a per-step computational cost penalty C_t = F·log(1+t/φ), weighted by a tunable λ to trade off accuracy against token efficiency. Empirically, the evolved policy converges toward compact, cyclic reasoning structures—dense subgraphs around a small cohort of 'hub' agents with iterative verification loops—yielding higher accuracy at lower token cost than both static multi-agent baselines (MacNet, EvoAgent) and single-agent workflows (Self-Refine, AFlow) across closed- and open-domain benchmarks.

## Results

In the Titan subspace (large models), Puppeteer (multi-model) achieves an average score of 0.7731 in the evolved phase, outperforming the strongest single-agent baseline AFlow (0.6899) by 8.3 points and the best pure model GPT-4-Turbo (0.5856) by 18.8 points. In the Mimas subspace (small models), Puppeteer reaches 0.6324 average, surpassing AFlow (0.5364) by 9.6 points. The evolved phase consistently outperforms the initialized phase (e.g., 0.7731 vs. 0.6893 in Titan), demonstrating that RL-driven policy optimization yields substantial gains. Token consumption decreases over training, confirming that performance improvements do not come at the cost of increased computational overhead.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Puppeteer (multi-model, evolved) outperforms all four baselines across the Titan subspace: it beats AFlow (0.6899) by 8.3 points on average, Self-Refine (0.6157) by 15.7 points, MacNet (0.5187) by 25.4 points, and EvoAgent (0.4994) by 27.4 points. Even Puppeteer-Mono (single-model, LLaMA-3.1-405B) surpasses AFlow by 5.5 points and Self-Refine by 13.0 points, indicating that the orchestration mechanism itself—rather than model heterogeneity—drives most of the gain. On individual tasks, Puppeteer leads on GSM-Hard (0.7000 vs. AFlow's 0.5400) and MMLU-Pro (0.8300 vs. AFlow's 0.7500), while AFlow retains a slight edge on CommonGen-Hard (0.8218 vs. 0.7987). In the Mimas subspace, Puppeteer (0.6324) similarly dominates AFlow (0.5364), Self-Refine (0.4695), MacNet (0.2862), and EvoAgent (0.3981), with the gap over MacNet and EvoAgent exceeding 20 points, highlighting the fragility of static-topology multi-agent systems with weaker base models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the graph-based formalization of multi-agent systems from MacNet [45], which models collaboration as a directed graph with agents as nodes and information flow as edges. It departs from the static or handcrafted topologies used in MacNet, GPT-Swarm, and ChatDev by replacing fixed agent wiring with a centralized, RL-trained orchestrator that sequentially selects which agent to activate at each step. The approach also draws on the graph-of-thought paradigm [2] for representing reasoning structure and on classical REINFORCE policy gradients for optimization, but applies them to the novel problem of learning agent-sequencing policies in LLM-based MAS. Compared to evolutionary (EvoAgent) or search-based (AFlow, ADAS) workflow generation, Puppeteer learns a parametric policy online rather than enumerating or evolving discrete topologies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a single learned orchestrator can simultaneously improve solution quality and reduce token consumption across both small (Mimas) and large (Titan) model pools, addressing a key scalability bottleneck in multi-agent LLM systems. The observation that evolved topologies converge toward compact, cyclic structures (rather than expanding trees or chains) offers a concrete, interpretable account of why the gains occur. However, the RL training is limited to 200 optimization samples, the centralized orchestrator may become a bottleneck for very large agent pools, and the framework has not been stress-tested on tasks requiring substantially longer reasoning chains. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to open-ended, multi-turn interactive settings beyond the benchmarks tested, and whether the reward design's accuracy-efficiency trade-off transfers cleanly to production deployments with hard latency constraints.
