## Problem

During non-stationary reinforcement fine-tuning of multi-modal LLMs, the autoregressive token distributions in chain-of-thought reasoning undergo unpredictable concept drift, causing even negligible token-level probability differences (e.g., 'lung opacity' vs. 'opacity') to produce radically opposite clinical diagnoses. Existing RFT methods treat all distributional shifts uniformly, failing to distinguish beneficial domain adaptation from detrimental reasoning drift, which is especially dangerous in safety-critical medical applications.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253874/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes chest X-ray images (from MIMIC-CXR, 371,920 images across 65,079 patients) paired with text prompts as input, and produces multi-label disease classifications across 14 thoracic pathologies, free-text diagnostic radiology reports, and explicit chain-of-thought reasoning trajectories. The training pipeline uses Qwen2.5-VL (7B) with a two-stage cascade: supervised fine-tuning followed by counterfactual preference optimization, where negative samples are not random but are causally generated counterfactual CoTs derived from a structured concept graph of 12 disease entities and 53 radiographic attributes.

## Method

CPO formalizes CoT generation as a non-stationary token stream subject to concept drift (Definition 2.1), then constructs a structural causal graph identifying concept drift D as a confounder and CoT T as a mediator on the path to prediction Z. Using do-operations, it isolates the causal effect of CoT on predictions from backdoor drift propagation (Eq. 4). A hierarchical concept graph—extracted from 160,208 MIMIC-CXR reports via a medical LLM, encoding association, irrelevance, and exclusion relationships—is embedded as an expert module that generates radiologically plausible counterfactual CoTs through controlled attribute perturbation. The CPO loss (Eq. 8) is a DPO-style Bradley-Terry objective where the positive sample is the radiologist's report and the negative sample is the counterfactual CoT, jointly maximizing alignment with human preferences while minimizing similarity to adversarially generated drift paths, thereby decoupling beneficial adaptation from harmful drift during reinforced custom-tuning.

## Results

On MS-CXR-T multi-label chest disease classification, CPO achieves 81.8% average Top-1 accuracy, surpassing the strongest prior method CoCa-CXR (69.2%) by 12.6% and the DPO baseline (76.4%) by 5.4%. For diagnostic report generation on MIMIC-CXR, CPO attains BLEU-4 of 0.155, ROUGE-L of 0.321, METEOR of 0.236, and CIDEr of 0.375, outperforming the previous best (MambaXray) by 16.5%, 10.3%, 34.8%, and 55.6% respectively. In zero-shot classification across six external benchmarks, CPO achieves AUC scores of 84.4 (Open-I), 82.0 (PadChest), 85.1 (PadChest20), 81.7 (ChestX-ray14), 92.5 (CheXpert), and 80.1 (ChestXDet10), exceeding the second-best CARZero on all six.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CPO decisively outperforms DPO (76.4% vs. 81.8% avg) with identical training data, confirming that counterfactual negatives are not functionally equivalent to random dispreferred responses. Against the strongest external baseline CoCa-CXR (69.2%), CPO gains 12.6% on average, with particularly large margins on Consolidation (+7.3%) and Pneumonia (+26.0%), the two clinically correlated diseases most affected by concept drift. The sole per-class loss is on Edema, where TempA-VLP (77.1%) edges out CPO (75.3%) by 1.8%, which the authors attribute to TempA-VLP's use of additional Chest ImaGenome annotations. In report generation, CPO surpasses all 11 prior methods on every metric, with the largest gap over MambaXray on METEOR (0.236 vs. 0.167, +34.8%) and CIDEr (0.375 vs. 0.241, +55.6%). In zero-shot transfer, CPO beats CARZero on all six external benchmarks by 0.4–1.5 AUC points, demonstrating that the counterfactual training generalizes beyond the MIMIC-CXR training distribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) by replacing its randomly sampled negative responses with counterfactual negatives generated through a domain-specific concept graph, drawing on Pearl's structural causal models to formalize the intervention. It also imports concept drift theory from the streaming data literature (Lu et al., Yang et al.) and repurposes it to characterize autoregressive CoT token generation as a non-stationary distributional process, a framing not previously applied to MLLM fine-tuning. The knowledge-graph construction pipeline builds on prior chest-radiology VLP work (Zhang et al., KAD/MedKLIP), but the key departure is using the graph not for representation learning but as a causal expert that synthesizes radiologically plausible counterfactual reasoning trajectories for preference optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The CCF dataset (320K counterfactual CoT pairs across 14 thoracic pathologies) is a tangible resource for the medical VLP community studying reasoning robustness. The formalization of CoT as a concept drift process, while mathematically light (essentially a definition plus a do-calculus decomposition), provides a useful vocabulary for diagnosing why RFT can destabilize in long-tailed medical settings. However, the evaluation is confined to a single 7B model (Qwen2.5-VL), a single training corpus (MIMIC-CXR), and one epoch, leaving open whether the CPO advantage over DPO persists at scale, across architectures, or in other clinical domains. The paper also does not ablate the concept graph's contribution independently of the counterfactual generation pipeline, making it unclear how much of the gain comes from structured medical knowledge versus the preference-optimization objective itself.
