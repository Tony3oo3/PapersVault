## Problem

Existing learned priors for imaging inverse problems either ignore the null-space structure of the sensing matrix entirely (sparsity, smoothness, PnP denoisers) or apply the full null-space projector, which is high-dimensional and algorithm-specific. This paper asks whether a compact, task-adaptive subspace of the null-space can be learned as a plug-in regularizer that is both interpretable (tied to the geometry of H) and flexible (compatible with diverse solvers).

## Input / Output

The method takes undersampled measurements y ∈ R^m and a known sensing matrix H ∈ R^{m×n} (m ≪ n) as input, and produces a reconstructed image x̂ ∈ R^n. The NPN network G* internally maps y to a low-dimensional null-space coefficient vector Sx* ∈ R^p (p ≤ n−m), which is then used as a regularizer rather than as the final output. The projection matrix S is designed per-task: QR-based for dense CS matrices, frequency-complement rows for MRI (DFT) and CT (Radon), and complementary filter coefficients for Toeplitz deblurring/SR operators.

## Method

NPN introduces a regularizer φ(x̃) = ‖G*(y) − Sx̃‖² into the reconstruction objective, where S ∈ R^{p×n} has rows in Null(H) and G*: R^m → R^p is a neural network (U-Net or ConvNeXt) trained to predict the null-space projection Sx* from measurements. The key design choice is restricting the learned prior to a compact p-dimensional subspace of the full (n−m)-dimensional null-space, concentrating regularization on the most informative unobserved directions. S and G* can be jointly optimized with orthogonality and full-rank penalties on the combined matrix A = [H; S], allowing the subspace to adapt to task-specific signal statistics. The paper provides two theoretical results: (i) linear convergence of PnP-NPN within a Convergence Improvement Zone (CIZ) defined by the network's estimation error, and (ii) an upper bound on the regularization value that vanishes to a constant determined by the Lipschitz constant of the network error as iterations converge.

## Results

On the main comparison (Table 1), NPN consistently outperforms all baselines across three inverse problems. For compressed sensing (m/n=0.1), NPN-PnP achieves 21.12 dB PSNR, surpassing the best null-space baseline DDN-I (20.70 dB) by 0.42 dB and standard PnP (20.04 dB) by 1.08 dB. For MRI at AF=4, NPN-Sparsity reaches 38.16 dB, beating DNSN (35.20 dB) by nearly 3 dB. For deblurring (σ=2), NPN-RED achieves 33.67 dB, exceeding DNSN (33.07 dB) and DDN-C (33.03 dB). In diffusion-based solvers, NPN regularization adds up to 1.85 dB (DPS) and 0.61 dB (DiffPIR) over their unregularized counterparts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NPN wins against all seven baseline families across every task. Against standard variational and PnP baselines, NPN-PnP improves over PnP by 1.08 dB (CS), 2.09 dB (MRI), and 0.64 dB (Deblurring); NPN-RED improves over RED by 0.08, 2.07, and 0.83 dB respectively. Against the closest null-space competitors, NPN-PnP beats DDN-I (20.70 dB) by 0.42 dB and DNSN (20.10 dB) by 1.02 dB on CS, while on MRI NPN-Sparsity (38.16 dB) surpasses DNSN (35.20 dB) by 2.96 dB and DDN-C (33.70 dB) by 4.46 dB. On deblurring, NPN-RED (33.67 dB) edges out DNSN (33.07 dB) by 0.60 dB. In diffusion solvers, NPN-DPS improves over the DPS baseline from 28.22 to 30.07 dB (+1.85 dB), and NPN-DiffPIR improves from 31.30 to 31.91 dB (+0.61 dB). The gains are most pronounced in MRI, where the structured Fourier null-space allows NPN to exploit complementary frequency components effectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the null-space learning lineage initiated by Schwab et al. (NSN, 2019) and Chen & Davies (DDN, 2020), which decompose signals into range-space and null-space components for reconstruction. The key departure is that NPN does not apply the full null-space projector; instead, it identifies a compact p-dimensional subspace of Null(H) via a projection matrix S and trains a lightweight network to predict coefficients in that subspace, turning the null-space structure into a plug-in regularizer rather than a standalone reconstruction architecture. This modular design contrasts with NSN/DDN, which are algorithm-specific and directly produce reconstructions, and positions NPN as complementary to existing image-domain priors (PnP denoisers, RED, sparsity) rather than a replacement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The CIZ (Convergence Improvement Zone) framework provides a principled, quantitative characterization of *when* the null-space regularizer helps versus when it becomes counterproductive due to network estimation error—a nuance absent from prior null-space methods. The method's modularity (validated across PnP-FISTA, unrolling, DIP, DPS, and DiffPIR) makes it a practical drop-in for existing reconstruction pipelines. However, the requirement to train a dedicated network per sensing configuration (H, S pair) and the two-stage training for unrolling limit immediate adoption, and the paper acknowledges that joint end-to-end training and extension to non-linear forward operators remain open. Performance gains are consistent but modest (1–2 dB typical, up to ~5.6 dB for in-distribution unrolling), suggesting the method is best viewed as a complementary refinement rather than a paradigm shift.
