## Problem

Despite diversity being a well-established principle in traditional reinforcement learning for improving exploration and policy quality, its role in RL-based training for LLM reasoning remains largely unexplored. This paper asks whether promoting diversity during RL training is essential for LLM reasoning and, if so, how to do so without degrading solution quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253169/cropped_page3_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a math question as a text prompt (max 256 tokens) and generates a chain-of-thought reasoning solution (max 756 tokens) ending with a final answer enclosed in \boxed{} format. During training, 6 responses are sampled per question at temperature 0.9, and a rule-based reward assigns accuracy (correct final answer) and format (proper \boxed{} usage) scores. Evaluation uses greedy decoding for Pass@1 and temperature 0.5 with 8 samples for Avg@8 across four mathematical benchmarks.

## Method

The paper introduces a token-level entropy-based diversity objective that avoids the length bias of sequence-level entropy by normalizing per-token, then reformulates it via importance sampling into a practical form amenable to backpropagation through the policy. The key design choice is to apply this diversity term exclusively to positive (correct) samples, motivated by gradient analysis showing that the diversity gradient pushes up low-probability tokens—beneficial for enriching correct solutions but harmful when applied to incorrect ones. This selective diversity objective is added with a small weight (λ=0.01) to the standard GRPO loss in the R1-zero training framework, yielding R1-zero-Div. The empirical motivation comes from a novel Potential@k metric that quantifies an LLM's reasoning headroom, which shows a strong positive correlation (R²=0.81) with solution diversity among high-performing models.

## Results

On four mathematical reasoning benchmarks (GSM8K, MATH500, Olympiad Bench, College Math), R1-zero-Div achieves an average Pass@1 accuracy of 64.4%, a 3.5% improvement over standard R1-zero (61.0%). The method also outperforms SimpleRL-Zoo (64.1%) and Eurus-2-7B-PRIME (62.1%) on average. On the Avg@8 metric, R1-zero-Div reaches 63.56% versus 60.06% for R1-zero and 63.31% for SimpleRL-Zoo. Additionally, R1-zero-Div preserves the highest solution diversity among all RL-finetuned methods (Div-Equ: 79.29, Div-N-gram: 29.60, Div-Self-BLEU: 58.89).

## Baselines & Comparisons

R1-zero-Div consistently outperforms the standard R1-zero baseline across all four benchmarks, with the largest gains on College Math (+4.3%) and MATH500 (+3.6%). Against SimpleRL-Zoo, R1-zero-Div wins on GSM8K (91.7 vs. 90.2), Olympiad Bench (40.1 vs. 39.0), and College Math (47.6 vs. 47.2), but trails on MATH500 (78.2 vs. 80.0), resulting in a narrow 0.3% average advantage. Compared to Eurus-2-7B-PRIME, R1-zero-Div leads on every individual benchmark (GSM8K +3.7, MATH500 +3.8, Olympiad +0.7, College Math +1.0) for a 2.3% average margin. Notably, R1-zero-Div achieves these gains while also producing the most diverse solutions (Div-Equ 79.29 vs. 75.02 for R1-zero and 74.89 for SimpleRL-Zoo), suggesting the diversity objective does not come at the cost of solution variety.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the R1-zero training framework (GRPO + rule-based rewards) from DeepSeek-R1 by adding a token-level entropy diversity term applied selectively to positive samples. It draws on the well-established role of diversity in traditional RL (population-based methods, entropy regularization) but departs from standard entropy regularization in two ways: using per-token entropy to avoid length bias, and restricting diversity promotion to correct answers to mitigate the quality-diversity trade-off. The Potential@k metric and the empirical correlation study (12 LLMs on MATH) are novel contributions that bridge the gap between RL exploration theory and LLM reasoning practice.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The R²=0.81 correlation between solution diversity and reasoning potential among high-performing models provides empirical grounding for diversity-aware training, and the method is trivially integrable into existing GRPO pipelines (one additional term, λ=0.01). However, the 3.5% improvement is modest, experiments are limited to 1.5B/7B models on a single training dataset (GSM8K) with 8×A6000 GPUs, and the diversity metric is purely statistical (entropy) rather than semantic. Open questions include whether the approach scales to 70B+ models, whether it transfers to non-mathematical reasoning, and how to incorporate user-defined semantic diversity (e.g., requiring algebraic vs. arithmetic solutions) rather than relying on implicit entropy maximization.
