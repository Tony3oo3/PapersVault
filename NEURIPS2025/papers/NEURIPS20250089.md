## Problem

In Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) architectures, achieving meaningful expert specialization remains challenging in single-domain settings where data does not naturally partition into distinct subtasks or distributions. Existing methods rely on explicit task boundaries or separate datasets per expert, leaving open how joint training of gating and expert modules can dynamically allocate domain-specific expertise under implicit data heterogeneity, particularly in decentralized environments where clients hold non-i.i.d. data.

## Input / Output

The method consumes raw images (32×32 CIFAR-10/100, EMNIST character images) or text (Yahoo! Answers question titles and content, with answer text excluded) as input. A frozen pretrained common expert (ResNet-34 for images, TinyBERT for text) first converts each sample into an embedding, which feeds the gating function; the selected experts then operate on the raw input to produce a class label. At test time, only the top-K experts (K=2 in most experiments) are dispatched to unseen clients, and the single highest-scoring expert among them produces the final prediction per sample rather than a weighted average.

## Method

DDOME (Dynamically Decentralized Orchestration of MoEs) maintains a pool of M independent experts and a small two-layer MLP gating function on the server, while each client holds a frozen pretrained common expert used solely as a feature extractor. The gating function maps common-expert embeddings to relevance scores over all M experts, and a top-K rule selects the subset dispatched to each active client for joint local training of both expert weights and gating parameters. A key architectural element is the anchor-client mechanism: M clients are pre-assigned one-to-one to M experts and activated more frequently with an independent loss, acting as regularizers that stabilize early specialization. The paper proves theoretically (Theorem 1) that decoupled training—training experts first with frozen random gating, then gating with frozen experts—incurs a constant lower bound on test loss, establishing that joint gating-expert training is necessary for meaningful specialization. At test time, DDOME achieves just-in-time personalization on unseen clients by sending only the gating function and K selected experts, reducing communication cost relative to methods like FedMix that transmit all M experts to every client.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, DDOME achieves 95.7% zero-shot personalization accuracy (with a 93% common expert), outperforming FedAvg (58.4%), FedProx (71.4%), and FedMix (42.9%). On CIFAR-100, DDOME reaches 78.6% (with a 73% common expert) versus 74.0% for FedAvg and FedProx. On Yahoo! Answers text classification, DDOME attains 74.90% versus 69.36% for Average Ensembles and 60.18% for FedAvg. On EMNIST, DDOME achieves 80.5% versus 74.4% for Average Ensembles and 72.1% for FedAvg, representing a 4–24% improvement over the strongest baselines across datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DDOME consistently outperforms all baselines across all four datasets. On CIFAR-10, DDOME (95.7%) surpasses the strongest baseline FedProx (71.4%) by 24.3 points and FedAvg (58.4%) by 37.3 points; FedMix (42.9%) and Average Ensembles (53.7%) perform even worse due to sending all experts to each client without selective routing. On CIFAR-100, DDOME (78.6%) leads FedAvg and FedProx (both 74.0%) by ~4.6 points, while FedMix (48.3%) collapses. On Yahoo! Answers, DDOME (74.90%) beats Average Ensembles (69.36%) by 5.5 points and FedAvg (60.18%) by 14.7 points. On EMNIST, DDOME (80.5%) outperforms Average Ensembles (74.4%) by 6.1 points and FedAvg (72.1%) by 8.4 points, while FedMix (15.9%) fails catastrophically. Scaffold is excluded from main tables due to training instability (achieving only ~10% on CIFAR-10 and <5% on CIFAR-100). Domain generalization methods FedSR (28.24%) and FedADG (41.83%) are also substantially outperformed by DDOME (87.86%) on CIFAR-10 with 100 clients.

## Relation to Prior Work

DDOME repurposes the Mixture-of-Experts paradigm for federated learning by combining three previously separate ideas: sparse top-K expert routing (from MoE literature), a frozen pretrained common expert as a feature extractor for the gating function (from transfer learning), and persistent client-expert anchoring to bootstrap specialization (a novel mechanism). It departs from prior MoE-for-FL work like FedMix, which transmits all experts to every client (M=K), by showing that sparse selection (K≪M) driven by a jointly-trained gating function both reduces communication and improves accuracy. The theoretical contribution (Theorem 1) extends the MoE training literature by formally proving that sequential (decoupled) training of router and experts provably fails to achieve specialization, a gap not previously addressed in the FL context.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for decentralized ML deployments: DDOME achieves 4–24% accuracy gains over standard FL baselines while communicating only K of M experts per client, and it enables zero-shot personalization to unseen clients without any fine-tuning data. Key limitations include a hard dependence on the quality of the pretrained common expert (a 'phase transition' around ~66% accuracy on CIFAR100), moderate-scale testing (100 clients), and the assumption of a single shared task domain. Open questions include whether the approach extends to architecturally heterogeneous experts, larger client pools, and multi-domain settings where the single-task assumption breaks down.
