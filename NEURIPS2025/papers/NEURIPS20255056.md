## Problem

Existing test-time adaptation methods for vision-language models, such as test-time prompt tuning, require backpropagation through large frozen text encoders or modification of internal model architecture, incurring substantial computational overhead and memory costs during inference. This limits their practicality for real-world deployment on resource-constrained devices or proprietary models where internal access is restricted. The paper asks whether effective per-sample adaptation can be achieved by operating entirely in the latent embedding space with a minimal number of learnable parameters.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single unlabeled test image (processed into 64 views via random resized crops and horizontal flips) and a pre-computed set of C text class prototypes (D-dimensional vectors from a frozen CLIP text encoder using a prompt template). It outputs a predicted class label and a set of adapted text prototypes. The only learned quantity per sample is a shared vector of k_t coefficients (typically far fewer than D), where k_t is determined by Gavish-Donoho optimal hard thresholding on the SVD of the prototype matrix. The steering vector is applied uniformly to all class prototypes, preserving their relative geometric structure.

## Method

STS pre-computes a low-dimensional spectral subspace by performing SVD on the initial text prototype matrix and retaining the top k_t right singular vectors as an orthonormal basis B_T. At test time, a single shared coefficient vector γ ∈ R^{k_t} is learned to define a steering vector Δz_T = B_T γ, which is added to every class prototype and re-normalized. The coefficients are optimized for one step (AdamW, lr=5e-3) by minimizing the Shannon entropy of the marginal prediction distribution over the top-10% most confident augmented views, plus an L2 regularization on the shift magnitude. Because the adaptation is confined to the principal SVD subspace and only k_t scalar coefficients are updated, the method avoids any backpropagation through the frozen encoders, achieving 8× faster inference and 12× smaller memory than conventional test-time prompt tuning while treating the VLM as a fully black-box feature extractor.

## Results

On ImageNet OOD variants (A, V2, R, Sketch), STS achieves an average OOD accuracy of 62.64%, surpassing TPT (60.71%) by 1.93 points and zero-shot CLIP (57.2%) by 5.44 points. The STS Ensemble variant (using 7 generic CLIP templates) further improves to 64.96% OOD average, setting a new state-of-the-art among all compared methods. On fine-grained classification across 10 datasets, STS Ensemble achieves 65.06% average accuracy, outperforming TPT (64.78%) and all other TTA baselines. STS runs 8× faster than TPT (0.09s vs. 0.75s per sample) with a 12× smaller memory footprint (1.4 GB vs. 17.6 GB).

## Baselines & Comparisons

STS outperforms all TTA baselines on the OOD average: it beats TPT by 1.93 points (62.64 vs. 60.71), DiffTPT by 2.12 points, TPS by 2.30 points, and C-TPT by 3.56 points. The largest individual gain is on ImageNet-A, where STS reaches 61.23% versus TPT's 54.39% (+6.84 points) and TPS's 57.46%. The STS Ensemble variant extends these advantages, achieving 64.96% OOD average and surpassing even CoOp (59.28%) and the 7-prompt CLIP Ensemble (59.42%). On fine-grained classification, STS Ensemble (65.06% avg) edges out TPT (64.78%) and clearly outperforms C-TPT (63.58%), TTL (63.59%), and TPS (63.49%). In terms of efficiency, STS requires only 0.09s and 1.4 GB per sample compared to TPT's 0.75s and 17.6 GB, while simultaneously delivering higher accuracy—a combination no other baseline achieves.

## Relation to Prior Work

STS sits at the intersection of two TTA lineages for VLMs: Test-Time Prompt Tuning (TPT, Shu et al. 2022), which optimizes learnable prompt tokens by backpropagating through the text encoder to minimize marginal entropy over augmented views, and Test-Time Prototype Shifting (TPS, Sui et al. 2024), which learns unconstrained per-class shift vectors directly in the embedding space. STS departs from TPT by eliminating encoder backpropagation entirely and from TPS by constraining the shift to a low-dimensional SVD subspace of the text prototypes, learned via a single shared coefficient vector rather than per-class vectors. The Gavish-Donoho optimal hard thresholding criterion for selecting the subspace rank is borrowed from random matrix theory and applied here for the first time to VLM text-embedding adaptation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 8× speedup and 12× memory reduction over TPT while matching or exceeding accuracy (e.g., +6.8 pts on ImageNet-A) makes high-quality TTA practical for resource-constrained and real-time deployment, a regime where prior prompt-tuning methods were prohibitive. The black-box, architecture-agnostic design broadens applicability to proprietary or frozen VLMs. Open questions include whether linear steering within the SVD subspace suffices for highly non-linear domain shifts, whether the same mechanism transfers to visual embeddings, and how to eliminate the linear scaling cost of repeated visual encoder forward passes over augmented views.
