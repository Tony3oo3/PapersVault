## Problem

Medical time-series diagnosis suffers from two intertwined limitations: labeled data is scarce and single-center, causing overfitting, and conventional contrastive learning relies on handcrafted positive-negative pairings that introduce human bias and constrain representation quality. No existing framework simultaneously leverages external normal data to mitigate overfitting and eliminates manual pairing in contrastive learning for hierarchical medical signals.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255551/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multivariate medical time-series (EEG or ECG) segmented into fixed-length windows (1 s or 5 s, 256–1250 timestamps per window, 12–33 channels), organized in a four-level hierarchy (subject → trial → epoch → temporal). An external dataset of healthy recordings from a different institution is used solely to pretrain the Discrepancy Estimator. The output is a binary disease label (e.g., AD vs. healthy, MI vs. healthy) plus a scalar sequence-level reconstruction error that is concatenated to the raw signal as an extra feature channel before contrastive encoding.

## Method

DAAC operates in three stages. First, a GAN-enhanced autoencoder (Discrepancy Estimator) is pretrained on external healthy data; its sequence-level MSE reconstruction error on target samples serves as an abnormality indicator and is appended to the input, increasing the feature dimension by one. Second, an Adaptive Contrastive Learner—comprising a dilated convolutional backbone h(x) for series-level features and a multi-head attention module g(x) for view-level features—is trained with five InfoNCE losses (subject-wise, trial-wise, epoch-wise, temporal-wise, and a combined inter/intra-view loss) that contrast embeddings across hierarchical granularities and across MHA attention heads, eliminating handcrafted pair design. Third, the encoder is fine-tuned with a logistic classifier under binary cross-entropy. The key architectural insight is that MHA heads act as learnable views: the inter-view loss pushes different heads apart while the intra-view loss separates different subjects within the same head, so the model discovers its own contrastive structure without external pairing heuristics.

## Results

On the AD dataset with 100% labeled data (full fine-tuning), DAAC achieves 93.23% accuracy and 98.03% AUROC, outperforming the strongest baseline COMET (84.50% acc, 94.44% AUROC) by ~8.7 points in accuracy. With only 10% labeled data, DAAC reaches 94.67% accuracy and 98.10% AUROC on AD, 95.60% accuracy on TDBrain, and 83.66% accuracy on PTB, consistently surpassing all baselines. The partial fine-tuning results on AD show DAAC achieving 80.48% accuracy and 85.96% AUROC with a frozen encoder, confirming the quality of the learned representations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DAAC outperforms all six baselines across every dataset and labeled-data fraction. Against COMET (the strongest single-method baseline), DAAC gains ~8.7 points in accuracy on AD (100%) and ~3.2 points at 10% labeled data. The ablation COMET+ACL (adding only the Adaptive Contrastive Learner) is the closest competitor, trailing DAAC by ~2.1 points in accuracy on AD (100%) and ~1.9 points at 10%, confirming that the Discrepancy Estimator provides additional benefit beyond contrastive learning alone. CLOCS, which also leverages subject-level structure, remains 14–18 points behind DAAC in accuracy. SimCLR and TF-C perform near-random or poorly (54–76% accuracy), indicating that generic contrastive or time-frequency strategies are insufficient for hierarchical medical time series. Notably, at 10% labeled data, DAAC's advantage over COMET widens (e.g., 94.67% vs. 91.43% on AD), demonstrating that the discrepancy-augmented features and adaptive multi-view contrastive learning are particularly effective in low-resource regimes.

## Relation to Prior Work

DAAC builds directly on COMET (Wang et al., NeurIPS 2023), which established hierarchical contrastive learning across subject, trial, epoch, and temporal levels for medical time series. It departs from COMET in two key ways: (1) introducing a GAN-enhanced autoencoder (Discrepancy Estimator) trained on external normal data to produce a reconstruction-error feature that augments the input, and (2) replacing handcrafted positive-negative pairing with a Multi-Head Attention-based Adaptive Contrastive Learner that learns inter-view and intra-view contrastive relationships adaptively. The broader contrastive time-series literature (TS2Vec, CLOCS, TS-TCC, TF-C) informs the design but is treated as insufficient for the hierarchical, data-scarce medical setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical relevance is strong: achieving competitive or better performance with only 10% labeled data (e.g., 94.67% accuracy on AD vs. 93.23% at 100%) addresses a critical bottleneck in clinical deployment where annotation is expensive. However, the paper itself acknowledges that the discrepancy signal remains coarse and that domain shift between external and target data can cause negative transfer (COMET+DE AUROC drops from 94.44 to 93.97 in their own ablation), suggesting the external-data integration is not yet fully robust. Open questions include whether the MHA-based view discovery generalizes beyond EEG/ECG to other hierarchical modalities, whether joint optimization of the five loss weights would yield further gains, and whether the discrepancy estimator can be made more adaptive to distributional differences across centers.
