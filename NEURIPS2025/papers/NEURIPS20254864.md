## Problem

Multimodal physiological emotion recognition faces two unresolved challenges: (1) existing backbones (CNNs, Transformers, RNNs) cannot simultaneously capture intra-modal long-range temporal dependencies and high-order inter-modal correlations, and (2) missing multimodal data amplifies distribution shifts between training and test sets, degrading model performance. Prior TTA methods either adapt indiscriminately to all corrupted samples or only handle unimodal corruption, leaving the multimodal missing-data case unaddressed.

## Input / Output

The method consumes three concurrent physiological signal streams per subject (EEG + EOG + EMG on DEAP at 128 Hz, or EEG + ECG + GSR on MAHNOB-HCI at 256 Hz), each represented as a C_i × L matrix of acquisition channels by time steps, segmented into 4-second non-overlapping windows. Missing data is simulated via random channel masking at ratios 0.2–0.8. The output is a binary valence/arousal label (high vs. low, thresholded at 5 on the 1–9 self-report scale) produced by a softmax classifier over the fused multimodal representation.

## Method

BiM-TTA combines a multimodal BiMamba backbone with a multimodal test-time adaptation procedure. The backbone first applies an intra-modal BiMamba to each signal independently—using a selective SSM in both forward and backward directions with a SiLU gating mechanism to model long-range temporal dependencies—then concatenates the per-modality features along the channel axis, transposes time and channel dimensions, and feeds the result through an inter-modal BiMamba that performs bidirectional state-space modeling along the channel dimension to capture cross-modal correlations. An auxiliary unimodal classifier per modality regularizes training. For TTA, a two-level entropy-based filter iteratively selects samples with low multimodal entropy (reliable predictions) and high unimodal entropy (rich multimodal information) using a gradually relaxing threshold, and a mutual-information-sharing loss minimizes KL divergence between each modality's prediction and a blend of its complementary probability and the multimodal prediction, aligning informative modalities with corrupted ones. Only the first convolutional layer, first fully-connected layer of the inter-modal module, and all batch-normalization layers are surgically fine-tuned at test time.

## Results

On DEAP, BiM-TTA achieves 67.3% valence and 64.1% arousal accuracy, outperforming the best baselines (VSGT at 63.1% valence, ACRNN at 63.8% arousal) by 4.2 and 0.3 percentage points respectively. On MAHNOB-HCI, it reaches 65.0% valence and 63.5% arousal accuracy, surpassing the strongest competitors (TSception at 63.3% valence, MambaFormer at 61.9% arousal) by 1.7 and 1.6 points. Under missing-data conditions (mask ratios 0.2–0.8), BiM-TTA's TTA component yields average improvements of 1.17% (DEAP valence), 1.31% (DEAP arousal), 1.09% (MAHNOB-HCI valence), and 0.51% (MAHNOB-HCI arousal) over the No Adapt baseline, consistently exceeding all TTA baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BiM-TTA outperforms all 10 emotion recognition baselines on both datasets and both dimensions (valence and arousal). The largest margin is on DEAP valence, where BiM-TTA (67.3%) exceeds VSGT (63.1%) by 4.2 points; on DEAP arousal the gap is smaller (64.1% vs. ACRNN's 63.8%). On MAHNOB-HCI, BiM-TTA leads TSception by 1.7 points on valence and MambaFormer by 1.6 points on arousal. Among TTA baselines under missing data, BiM-TTA's average improvement (1.17% DEAP valence, 1.31% DEAP arousal) surpasses 2LTTA (0.86%, 0.43%) and READ (0.78%, 0.37%), while Tent and EATA show near-zero or negative average gains. On MAHNOB-HCI with missing data, BiM-TTA (1.09% valence, 0.51% arousal) substantially outperforms EATA (0.17%, 0.22%) and 2LTTA (0.12%, 0.02%), with READ and Tent degrading performance at higher mask ratios.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Mamba's selective state-space model from unimodal sequence modeling to a multimodal physiological signal setting, building directly on EEG-Mamba's bidirectional SSM design for temporal dependencies. The key architectural departure is repurposing the SSM's explicit global state variables to model inter-modal correlations along the channel dimension (via a transpose-and-BiMamba operation), rather than relying on the pairwise attention mechanisms used by prior multimodal fusion methods (e.g., cross-attention in Koorathota et al. or graph-based fusion in GJFusion). On the adaptation side, the multimodal TTA synthesizes ideas from Tent's entropy minimization, EATA's sample reweighting, READ's inter-modal reliability adjustment, and 2LTTA's two-level objective into a unified two-level entropy filtering plus mutual information sharing framework, with surgical fine-tuning limiting parameter updates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The combination of a BiMamba backbone with a missing-data-aware TTA addresses a practical gap in physiological emotion recognition: real-world sensor data is frequently incomplete, and prior methods either ignore this or require retraining. The mutual information sharing mechanism for aligning modalities under asymmetric corruption is a reasonable step toward robust multimodal deployment. However, the evaluation is limited to two small datasets (DEAP, MAHNOB-HCI) with only three modalities each, and the TTA improvements over 'No Adapt' are modest (1–2% average), leaving open whether the approach generalizes to larger-scale or more diverse physiological tasks. The authors themselves note that real-time latency, multi-task applicability, and validation on additional datasets remain unaddressed.
