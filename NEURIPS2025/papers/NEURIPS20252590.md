## Problem

Selecting k nodes in a social network whose internal opinions should be set to 1 to maximize the overall equilibrium opinion (OPINIONMAX) is computationally intractable at scale: the exact solution requires inverting an n×n matrix in O(n³) time, which is infeasible even for networks with tens of thousands of nodes. The paper addresses this gap by developing scalable algorithms that either approximate or exactly solve the problem on networks with tens of millions of nodes.

## Input / Output

The method takes an unweighted directed or undirected graph G=(V,E), a per-node internal opinion vector s with entries in [0,1], a diagonal resistance matrix R with entries α_i ∈ (0,1] (where smaller α_i means greater susceptibility to persuasion), and a target set size k ≪ n. It outputs the optimal node set T of size k and the potential influence vector Δ where Δ(i) = ρ_i(1−s(i)), with ρ_i being the structural centrality derived from the fundamental matrix M = (I−(I−R)P)⁻¹R. The problem reduces to selecting the top-k nodes by Δ, since the overall opinion gain from modifying node i is exactly ρ_i(1−s_i).

## Method

The paper proposes three algorithms of increasing precision. RWB estimates structural centrality via absorbing random walks (each node absorbs with probability α_i), requiring O(n/ε² · log n) walks. FOREST leverages the matrix-forest theorem to connect structural centrality to spanning converging forests, sampling them via an extension of Wilson's loop-erased random walk algorithm. The main contribution, MIS (MAXINFLUENCESELECTOR), is a deterministic two-phase asynchronous algorithm: Phase 1 (GLOBALINFAPPROX) propagates a residual vector r_a asynchronously—each active node distributes (1−α_i)/d_i⁺ · r_a(i) to out-neighbors and accumulates (1−s_i)α_i·r_a(i) into its influence estimate—terminating locally when residuals fall below ε, yielding a (1−ε)-relative-error guarantee. Phase 2 (TARGETEDNODEREFINE) refines only the small boundary candidate set C identified by Lemma 8, running a second asynchronous pass with in-neighbor propagation to obtain absolute error bounds, while iteratively halving ε until the gap between the k-th and (k+1)-th largest Δ values is resolved, guaranteeing exact identification of the optimal set in O(d_max⁺·n/α_min · log(1/ε) + m/(k_gap·α_min)) time.

## Results

The proposed MIS (asynchronous) algorithm achieves perfect precision (exactly 1.0) and NDCG scores approaching 1.0 across all eight benchmark networks, while RWB and FOREST also consistently outperform all five centrality-based baselines on overall opinion, precision, and NDCG. In terms of efficiency, MIS is orders of magnitude faster than both sampling methods: on the 58.7M-node SinaWeibo network, MIS completes in 156.83–1712.25 s versus 10,538–11,743 s for FOREST, and RWB fails to finish within the 6-hour limit on large networks under strict resistance distributions. The direct matrix-inversion EXACT baseline fails to complete even on the smallest DBLP network (317K nodes) within 6 hours.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against five standard centrality-based baselines: TOPRANDOM, TOPDEGREE, TOPCLOSENESS, TOPBETWEENNESS, and TOPPAGERANK. All three proposed methods (RWB, FOREST, MIS) consistently outperform every baseline across all eight networks and all three evaluation metrics (overall opinion, precision, NDCG) under uniform, normal, and exponential resistance coefficient distributions. MIS achieves perfect precision (1.0) and near-perfect NDCG, meaning it exactly identifies the optimal k-node set, while the sampling methods (RWB, FOREST) show small but measurable precision fluctuations that do not substantially degrade overall opinion scores. In terms of runtime, MIS is roughly 200–400× faster than RWB and 10–70× faster than FOREST on medium-scale networks, and the only method that completes on the 41M-node Twitter and 58M-node SinaWeibo graphs within practical time limits (RWB times out entirely on these). The TOPPAGERANK baseline [49], which is the strongest centrality heuristic, is consistently the best-performing baseline but still falls well short of all three proposed methods in both accuracy and opinion maximization quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Friedkin-Johnson opinion dynamics model and the established line of work on maximizing overall opinion through various intervention strategies (modifying resistance coefficients, expressed opinions, or network topology). The specific task of modifying internal opinions of key nodes has been explored in prior work on leader selection and initial opinion shifting, but those approaches relied on O(n³) matrix inversion. The paper's methodological contribution draws on two distinct theoretical threads: the absorbing random walk interpretation of the Neumann series (connecting to Wilson's algorithm and matrix-forest theorems for the FOREST sampler) and a novel deterministic asynchronous update framework that avoids both matrix inversion and stochastic sampling entirely.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is that the MIS algorithm provides exact (not approximate) identification of the optimal k-node set while running orders of magnitude faster than both the sampling-based approximations and the infeasible matrix inversion baseline, scaling to networks with ~58 million nodes. This makes opinion-maximization optimization tractable for real-world social network analysis at a scale previously inaccessible. Open questions include extension to dynamic network topologies, multi-topic opinion optimization, and whether the sensitivity to small resistance coefficients (which inflates runtime) can be mitigated. The paper acknowledges that boundary-node ties in exact optimization can cause termination issues, though this is deemed practically negligible.
