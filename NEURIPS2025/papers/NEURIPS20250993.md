## Problem

Existing video retrieval benchmarks are limited to trimmed videos, one-to-one retrieval, text-only queries, or narrow categories like news and film, failing to reflect the practical reality of long-video platforms where users search untrimmed videos with multi-modal queries across diverse content types. No prior benchmark provides a unified matching criterion that spans low-level (frame copy) to high-level (event) visual correspondence, nor does it evaluate the reranking capability of MLLMs on video retrieval tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250993/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each query is a video-centric multi-modal tuple: an untrimmed 2-minute video clip (6 fps, 336 px long edge) serving as the primary visual query, a ~20-word text description specifying key retrieval content, optional tag prompts (positive/negative attributes like 'animation' or 'first-person view') for hierarchical filtering, and SAM2-generated mask prompts for fine-grained spatial attention. The system produces a ranked list over 53,462 untrimmed videos (one-to-many retrieval) scored via max-pooled frame-level similarity; MUVR-QA additionally outputs a binary Yes/No relevance judgment per query-target pair, aggregated into a Reranking Score that penalizes false removals more heavily than false retentions.

## Method

MUVR organizes 53K Bilibili videos into five partitions (news, region, instance, dance, others) based on a proposed six-level visual correspondence taxonomy (copy, scene, instance, action, event, others), where each partition emphasizes a different correspondence level matching user retrieval interests. Three benchmark versions are constructed: MUVR-Base (1,050 queries, 84K positive matches), MUVR-Filter (9,979 tag-prompted queries with 74K multi-labeled tags enabling combinatorial filtering), and MUVR-QA (200 hard discrimination questions derived from EVA-CLIP failures). A novel Reranking Score metric is introduced that assigns asymmetric penalties (+1/0/−1/−2) to four possible MLLM outcomes on true/false positive pairs, measuring whether a model can refine initial retrieval rankings. Evaluation spans 3 video retrieval models (InternVideo2, S2VS, CoVR), 6 image-based VLMs (CLIP variants, EVA-CLIP, BLIP, BLIP2), and 10 MLLMs (InternVL, MiniCPM-V, LLaVA variants, VideoRefer, GPT-4o, Gemini-2.0-Flash) under both text-query-only and multi-image comparison prompting strategies.

## Results

On MUVR-Base (multimodal query), the best model EVA-CLIP achieves 58.0% mAP and 44.6% uAP, while the weakest (CLIP RN50x4) reaches only 42.9% mAP. Performance drops sharply on MUVR-Filter (with tag prompts), where EVA-CLIP falls to 34.0% mAP, revealing the difficulty of fine-grained tag-based filtering. On MUVR-QA, GPT-4o attains the highest accuracy (65.0%) but a Reranking Score of only 0.19, while most open-source MLLMs (InternVL2.5, MiniCPM-V, LLaVA variants) produce negative Reranking Scores (−0.84 to −0.10), indicating they are unreliable for reranking untrimmed video retrieval.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EVA-CLIP dominates across all partitions and metrics on MUVR-Base, outperforming the next-best OpenCLIP (ViT-H-14) by 4.0 mAP points (58.0 vs. 54.0) and BLIP-2 by 7.0 points, confirming that larger-scale image-text pretraining transfers well to untrimmed video retrieval. Video-specific models InternVideo2 and S2VS achieve the best uAP (37.4% and 36.6% respectively) when using pure video queries, showing their temporal modeling advantage, but trail EVA-CLIP in mAP under multimodal queries. CoVR, designed for composed video retrieval, performs poorly (43.3% mAP) because it only supports single-frame query input, making it ill-suited for untrimmed videos. On MUVR-Filter, adding tag prompts yields only marginal gains (e.g., EVA-CLIP: 32.9→34.0% mAP), and small models like BLIP show almost no improvement (25.5→25.6%), indicating limited tag comprehension. For MLLM reranking on MUVR-QA, GPT-4o (65.0% accuracy, Reranking Score 0.19) and Gemini-2.0-Flash (63.5%, 0.07) are the only models with positive Reranking Scores, while all open-source MLLMs (InternVL2.5, MiniCPM-V, LLaVA variants, VideoRefer) score at or below zero, revealing a clear gap between proprietary and open-source models in multi-video relevance judgment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by unifying elements from three distinct retrieval paradigms—text-to-video retrieval, composed video retrieval, and fine-grained video retrieval (FVR)—into a single task formulation. Unlike FVR benchmarks (FIVR, EVVE) that are restricted to news/film categories and event-level matching, MUVR introduces multi-level visual correspondence (six levels: copy, scene, instance, action, event, others) across five diverse video partitions, and replaces the single video query with a video-centric multi-modal query (video + text description + tag prompt + mask prompt). The Reranking Score metric for MLLM evaluation extends the two-stage retrieval workflow explored in image retrieval (INQUIRE, M-BEIR) to the more challenging video domain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals that even the strongest model (EVA-CLIP) achieves only 58% mAP on MUVR-Base and 34% on MUVR-Filter, exposing a substantial gap between current retrieval capabilities and the demands of real-world untrimmed video platforms. The finding that MLLMs achieve >60% discrimination accuracy yet produce negative or near-zero Reranking Scores challenges the assumption that accuracy translates to useful reranking, pointing to a critical open problem in multi-video understanding. Key open questions include how to effectively fuse multi-modal query signals (video, text, tags, masks), how to improve temporal modeling for dynamic content like dance, and how to make MLLMs efficient enough for practical reranking at scale.
