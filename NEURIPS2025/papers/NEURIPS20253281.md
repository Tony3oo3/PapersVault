## Problem

Prior deterministic certified-training methods (FullCert, Sosnin et al.) propagate interval or polyhedral bounds layer-by-layer, causing compounding over-approximations that make parameter bounds grow monotonically and training diverge. These methods also cannot express complex threat models involving categorical features, conditional constraints, or structured perturbations beyond simple ℓp-norms. The paper asks whether a more precise, optimization-based formulation can yield stable, tight bounds while supporting expressive perturbation models.

## Input / Output

During training, the method consumes a dataset with a user-specified perturbation precondition (e.g., ℓ∞ radius, sparsity constraints, categorical value restrictions) and initial model parameters, producing per-parameter interval bounds [θ̲, θ̄] after each iteration. At inference, it takes a single test data point and the final parameter bounds, encoding only the forward pass to check whether one logit is provably dominant across the entire parameter box; it returns either a certified class label or abstain. The method operates on tabular data with both continuous and categorical features, using small MLPs (2 layers, 20 neurons) with ReLU activations and hinge loss.

## Method

MIBP-Cert frames each training step as a mixed-integer bilinear program that exactly encodes the forward pass, loss, backward pass, and parameter update as constraints over all variables (inputs, parameters, activations, gradients). The key design choice is to relax only the parameter space between iterations: within a single step, the MIBP is solved exactly (preserving operand dependencies so bounds can shrink), while between steps, only the resulting parameter intervals are carried forward. Bilinear terms arise from multiplying parameter variables by input/activation variables in linear layers and gradient computations; mixed-integer terms arise from ReLU and max-based loss functions encoded via Big-M or SOS constraints. For each of the m parameters, two MIBPs (min and max) are solved to obtain its lower and upper bound, using Gurobi's native bilinear and piecewise-linear constraint support. This per-step exactness avoids the monotonic bound growth that causes divergence in interval-based methods, and the constraint syntax generalizes to arbitrary linear, bilinear, logical, and integer perturbation models.

## Results

On Two Moons, MIBP-Cert achieves 81.4%±0.06 certified accuracy at ε=0.01, substantially outperforming FullCert (71.5%±11.20) and Sosnin et al. (69.0%±0.10), while also exhibiting far lower variance across seeds. On the Iris 2-class dataset, MIBP-Cert reaches 100% certified accuracy at ε=0.1 versus 88% for FullCert, and on Breast Cancer Wisconsin it achieves 94% at ε=0.001 versus 83% for FullCert. For complex structured perturbations on the NPHA health dataset, MIBP-Cert certifies 91.5% of predictions under a mental-health over-reporting model with 50.7% certified accuracy, a threat model no prior method can handle.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MIBP-Cert is compared against two deterministic certified-training baselines: FullCert [20] (interval-based) and Sosnin et al. [28] (interval + CROWN polyhedral). At small perturbation radii (ε ≤ 0.001), all three methods achieve similar certified accuracy (~82–84%) on Two Moons, but MIBP-Cert shows dramatically lower variance (±0.05 vs. ±3.60–4.40 for FullCert), reflecting more stable training. At ε=0.01, MIBP-Cert pulls ahead decisively: 81.4% vs. 71.5% (FullCert) and 69.0% (Sosnin et al.), a gap of 10–12 percentage points. The advantage widens further on UCI datasets—on Iris 2-class at ε=0.1, MIBP-Cert certifies 100% of test points versus 88% for FullCert, and on Breast Cancer at ε=0.01 it reaches 51% versus 38%. Beyond accuracy, MIBP-Cert uniquely supports complex structured perturbations (e.g., categorical missing values, reporting bias) that neither baseline can express, as demonstrated on the NPHA dataset where it certifies 91.5% of predictions under a mental-health over-reporting model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the deterministic certified-training line initiated by FullCert (Lorenz et al., 2024) and Sosnin et al. (2024), which propagate interval or polyhedral over-approximations layer-by-layer through forward and backward passes. The key departure is replacing these propagation-based relaxations with an exact mixed-integer bilinear program (MIBP) per training step, solved via Gurobi, which preserves operand dependencies and avoids the compounding over-approximation that causes prior methods to diverge. The formulation also generalizes the threat model from simple ℓp-norm balls to arbitrary linear, bilinear, logical, and integer constraints over inputs, labels, and auxiliary variables, enabling certification of categorical features and structured perturbations that no prior bound-based method could express.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a fundamental practical failure mode of prior certified training—monotonically growing parameter bounds that cause training to diverge—and shows that exact per-step MIBP solving eliminates this instability, yielding both higher certified accuracy and dramatically lower variance under larger perturbations. The NPHA health-data experiment demonstrates a practically important capability: certifying predictions under realistic reporting-bias and missing-data models on tabular data, a setting where prior methods are simply inapplicable. The main open question is scalability: at 19–156 s per epoch versus ~0.01 s for interval methods, the approach is currently limited to small MLPs on tabular data, and the authors identify hybrid solvers, sparsity exploitation, and parallelization as necessary directions before the method can impact larger-scale or image-based settings.
