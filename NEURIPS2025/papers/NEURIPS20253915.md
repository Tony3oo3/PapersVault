## Problem

Existing graph coarsening methods prioritize structural fidelity (spectral preservation, connectivity) while largely ignoring node features, and even the recent Featured Graph Coarsening (FGC) approach preserves only individual feature characteristics rather than the mutual relationships between features. This paper asks whether preserving the inner products between graph signals (node features treated as functions on the graph) during coarsening can simultaneously retain both structural and feature-relational information, and whether this objective can be formalized and optimized in a principled geometric framework.

## Input / Output

The method takes a graph Laplacian L ∈ R^{n×n}, a node features matrix X ∈ R^{n×p} (where each column is a graph signal), and a target super-node count k ≪ n. It produces a coarsening matrix C ∈ R^{n×k} with orthogonal non-negative columns (a point on the Grassmann manifold Gr(n,k)), from which the coarsened Laplacian L_c = C^T L C ∈ R^{k×k} and coarsened features X_c = C^† X ∈ R^{k×p} are derived. The inner product between signals is defined with respect to the graph Laplacian as ⟨x,y⟩_L = x^T L y, and the method additionally requires the k leading eigenvectors of L as a reference subspace for the Grassmann similarity term.

## Method

The core contribution is a functorial coarsening objective that minimizes the Inner Product Error (IPE) = ‖X^T L X − X_c^T L_c X_c‖_F^2 while maximizing the Grassmann similarity tr(U^(k)(U^(k))^T CC^T) between the coarsening matrix C and the k leading Laplacian eigenvectors U^(k), plus l_{1,2} and log-det regularization for valid partitioning and connectivity. The Grassmann similarity term is the key differentiator: Proposition 3.4 proves that any C spanning the same subspace as U^(k) achieves zero IPE for all k-smooth signals, so this term generalizes IPE minimization to unseen smooth features beyond the observed X. Two algorithms are provided: INGC optimizes the full objective via projected gradient descent with periodic Hardmax(C) projections onto the Grassmann manifold, while SINGC drops the IPE term entirely, reducing to a simpler gradient descent on the Grassmann similarity plus regularization with a single final projection. Proposition 3.5 further shows that as the Grassmann similarity gap ε shrinks, the bounds on Relative Eigen Error and Dirichlet Energy Error tighten, linking the proposed objective to standard coarsening metrics.

## Results

On coarsening metrics (Table 1), INGC achieves the best or second-best score in 39 out of 60 settings across REE, RE, HE, DEE, and IPE on four datasets, with particularly strong DEE values (e.g., 0.01 on Karate Club at r=0.7) and RE values (e.g., 6.27 vs. 8.70 for FGC on Karate Club at r=0.7). On node classification (Table 2), INGC achieves 87.55% on Cora (r=0.3), 76.89% on Citeseer (r=0.3), 94.29% on Co-Physics (r=0.05), 83.59% on Pubmed (r=0.05), and 90.84% on Co-CS (r=0.05), outperforming all baselines in 7 of 15 settings. SINGC, the lighter variant that omits the direct IPE term, remains competitive, achieving best or second-best in 11 of 15 node classification settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the spectral coarsening baselines LVN and LVE (Loukas & Vandergheynst, ICML 2018), INGC and SINGC consistently outperform on all feature-aware metrics (RE, HE, DEE, IPE) while remaining competitive on REE; for example, on Karate Club at r=0.7, INGC achieves RE=6.27 vs. 9.71 (LVN) and 9.39 (LVE), and DEE=0.01 vs. 36.2 and 17.3 respectively. Against FGC (Kumar et al., ICML 2023), the closest structural competitor that also incorporates node features, both INGC and SINGC achieve lower IPE, DEE, and RE across all four coarsening-metric datasets; on Cora at r=0.5, INGC records DEE=0.40 vs. FGC's 0.41 and IPE=0.43 vs. 1.01. On node classification, INGC surpasses FGC by 1.76 pp on Cora (r=0.3: 87.55 vs. 85.79) and by 2.25 pp on Citeseer (r=0.3: 76.89 vs. 74.64). Against graph condensation (GCOND, Jin et al. 2021) and SCAL (Huang et al., KDD 2021), both of which focus primarily on structural preservation without explicit feature handling, INGC and SINGC lead by 2–6 pp on most settings, with the gap widening at aggressive coarsening ratios (e.g., SCAL drops to 14.42% on Co-CS at r=0.01 while SINGC maintains 83.39%). Against MGC (Halder et al., WWW 2025), INGC achieves higher accuracy on Pubmed (83.59 vs. 81.89 at r=0.05) and Co-CS (90.84 vs. not reported at r=0.05), while MGC edges out INGC slightly on Co-Physics at r=0.05 (94.52 vs. 94.29).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the graph coarsening literature by shifting the preservation objective from structural fidelity (spectral metrics like REE/RE in LVN/LVE) or individual feature reconstruction (FGC) to preserving inner products between node features as graph signals. It draws on Grassmann manifold geometry—previously used for functorial comparison of graph signals (Cohen & Talmon, 2024)—to formulate a coarsening operator that aligns with the leading Laplacian eigenvectors, thereby generalizing Inner Product Error minimization to unseen smooth signals. The optimization framework (projected gradient descent with Hardmax projection onto the Grassmann manifold) builds directly on the multi-block optimization structure of FGC (Kumar et al., 2023), but replaces the feature-reconstruction term with an IPE term and adds a Grassmann similarity regularizer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key contribution is a clean theoretical bridge: maximizing Grassmann similarity between the coarsening operator and leading eigenvectors simultaneously minimizes IPE for all k-smooth signals and tightens bounds on both REE and DEE, unifying feature and structural preservation under one geometric objective. This matters for graph learning pipelines where coarsening precedes GNN training, as the method consistently outperforms SOTA on both structural and feature-based metrics across multiple datasets. Limitations include scalability to very large graphs (optimization-based, O(n²k) eigendecomposition overhead) and reliance on a homophily/smoothness assumption that restricts applicability to heterophilic graphs. Open questions include adapting the frequency-band alignment for heterophilic settings and integrating the coarsening into graph pooling architectures.
