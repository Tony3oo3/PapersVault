## Problem

Quantum-inspired tensor networks (TNs) have been restricted to simple benchmarks like MNIST due to low computational efficiency and the absence of feature self-interaction, while polynomial and multilinear networks that do scale to ImageNet lose their polynomial unfolding structure when layer normalization is applied. The paper addresses this by establishing a theoretical equivalence between TNs and polynomial networks under specific conditions, and by designing an architecture that captures exponential-order multiplicative interactions without activation functions while remaining scalable to large-scale benchmarks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251560/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For the primary image classification task, DTTN takes RGB images (224×224 for ImageNet-1K, 32×32 for CIFAR-10, 64×64 for Tiny ImageNet) and produces class labels via average pooling and a fully-connected head. The input is consumed through a vanilla linear patch embedding (no BN) that maps local patches to d-dimensional vectors, after which the network operates purely on multilinear operations—no activation functions or attention mechanisms are used anywhere. In secondary applications, AIM is plugged into CTR models (taking feature vectors, outputting AUC scores) and replaces linear layers in PINNs (taking spatial-temporal coordinates, outputting PDE solution fields).

## Method

DTTN is constructed by sequentially stacking L Antisymmetric Interaction Modules (AIMs), each of which applies two branches with reversed ordering of depthwise convolution and linear projection to the input feature map, then combines the branch outputs via a Hadamard product to capture second-order multiplicative interactions. The antisymmetric design reduces the parameter and FLOP ratio between branches to ~1/r_exp, making the module hardware-efficient. Without layer normalization, each AIM is provably equivalent to a binary tree node in a tensor network contraction, so the full network unfolds into a tree-topology TN with 2^L leaf nodes, and the output is a homogeneous polynomial of degree 2^L over the patch-embedded features. Theorem 1 further establishes that any polynomial network with this expansion form can be transformed into a quantum-inspired TN with finite bond dimension, bridging the two paradigms. Three variants (Tiny/Small/Large) vary block count (34–56) and hidden sizes (64–384) across four stages, with an optional LN inside AIM that trades strict polynomial form for ~0.4–1.8% accuracy gain and faster convergence.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K, DTTN†-L achieves 82.4% Top-1 accuracy with 35.9M parameters and 12.3B FLOPs over 300 epochs, outperforming DeiT-S/16 (81.2%), Swin-T/16 (81.3%), and Vim-S (80.5%). The smaller DTTN†-T reaches 77.9% Top-1 with only 7.1M parameters and 2.3B FLOPs, surpassing MONet-T (77.0%) by 0.9% while using ~30% fewer parameters. On smaller benchmarks, DTTN†-S achieves 95.0% on CIFAR-10, 63.8% on Tiny ImageNet, and 87.7% on ImageNet-100, setting new state-of-the-art among polynomial and multilinear networks. The unfoldable DTTN-S (without LN) reaches 77.2% on ImageNet-1K, a 12% improvement over Π-Nets (65.2%) at the same parameter budget.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest multilinear competitor MONet, DTTN†-T outperforms MONet-T by 0.9% (77.9% vs 77.0%) with ~30% fewer parameters (7.1M vs 10.3M) and 20% fewer FLOPs; however, DTTN†-S (79.4%) trails MONet-S (81.3%) by 1.9% at the larger scale. Against polynomial networks, DTTN-S achieves a dramatic 12% improvement over Π-Nets (77.2% vs 65.2%) and 7% over R-PolyNets (77.2% vs 70.2%) on ImageNet-1K, while also surpassing Hybrid Π-Nets (70.7%) and D-PolyNets (70.0%) by 6.5% and 7.2% respectively. Among modern architectures, DTTN†-L (82.4%) outperforms DeiT-S/16 (81.2%), Swin-T/16 (81.3%), Vim-S (80.5%), and ViP-Small/14 (80.5%) without using attention or activation functions, though it requires more FLOPs (12.3B vs 4.5–6.5B). On smaller benchmarks (CIFAR-10, Tiny ImageNet, ImageNet-100), DTTN†-S achieves the best results among all polynomial/multilinear networks, beating MONet-T by 0.2%, 2.3%, and 0.5% respectively. Against quantum-inspired TNs on MNIST and Fashion-MNIST, DTTN-S (0.9930 / 0.9236) outperforms aResMPS (0.9907 / 0.9142) and all other TN baselines (MPS, PEPS, LoTeNet), with the gap attributed to higher effective bond dimension from parameter sharing.

## Relation to Prior Work

DTTN sits at the intersection of two research threads: quantum-inspired tensor networks (MPS, TTN, MERA) that map inputs into exponential-dimensional Hilbert spaces, and polynomial/multilinear networks (Π-Nets, R-PolyNets, MONet) that capture high-order feature interactions through multiplicative operations. The paper's key conceptual move is proving a formal equivalence (Theorem 1) between these two families under a specific local mapping, thereby unifying them. The architectural departure from MONet is the antisymmetric two-branch AIM design (depthwise-conv→linear vs. linear→depthwise-conv) combined with a single shortcut, which preserves the polynomial unfolding property that LN-preceded Hadamard products in MONet and R-PolyNets destroy, while reducing parameter count by ~1/r_exp in one branch.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that a tensor-network-based architecture can reach competitive accuracy on ImageNet-1K (82.4% Top-1 for DTTN†-L), a benchmark where prior TNs were confined to MNIST-scale tasks. The theoretical equivalence between quantum-inspired TNs and polynomial networks provides a principled bridge between the physics-inspired interpretability community and the modern architecture design community, potentially enabling quantum-hardware-compatible interpretable models at scale. Open questions include whether the approach scales to pre-training on JFT-300M, whether multilinear transformer variants can match attention-based LLMs, and whether the polynomial structure offers genuine interpretability advantages in practice beyond the algebraic form. The absence of statistical significance testing and robustness evaluation limits the strength of the empirical claims.
