## Problem

Existing Federated Graph Learning methods rely on statistical aggregation of shared knowledge (prototypes, spectral features) to handle non-IID graph distributions, but this imposes overly strict assumptions about correlations between structural features and the global objective, leading to spurious correlations that fail to generalize. The paper identifies two unexplored questions: how to discover invariant subgraph patterns shared across heterogeneous client distributions, and how to extract them in a privacy-preserving manner without uploading raw structural knowledge to the server.

## Input / Output

Each client provides local graphs represented by adjacency matrices and node feature matrices, which are decomposed via a learned mask into an invariant subgraph (top-t% edges selected by an edge-wise MLP scorer) and a variant subgraph (remaining edges). The global model fuses invariant and variant branch representations for downstream graph-level classification or clustering, while a separate local model encodes the variant subgraph with a private encoder that never participates in federated aggregation, ensuring client-specific structural information stays local.

## Method

FedIGL decomposes each client graph into client-agnostic and client-specific subgraphs using a Federated Subgraph Generator (FSG) that applies a GNN followed by an edge-wise MLP to produce a binary mask, selecting the top-t% edges as the invariant subgraph. Two parallel GNN branches encode the invariant and variant subgraphs separately, with the invariant branch shared across all clients via FedAvg aggregation and the variant branch remaining client-specific. The key training innovation is a Bi-Gradient Regularization strategy: consistency regularization penalizes deviation of the invariant encoder's gradient from the previous round's global mean (encouraging cross-client alignment), while diversity regularization penalizes the variant encoder's gradient when it falls within a margin ε of the global mean (enforcing cross-client repulsion). A separate local model trained only on the variant subgraph representation (with the invariant branch frozen) captures client-specific patterns without contaminating the global model. Theoretical analysis proves O(1/T) convergence for the invariant branch and guaranteed pairwise gradient separation ≥ ε for the variant branch under the bi-gradient objective.

## Results

On graph-level classification across six non-IID settings (single-domain, double-domain, and multi-domain), FedIGL achieves the best accuracy in 5 out of 6 settings, with a headline result of 83.07% on the SM setting (vs. 79.62% for the strongest baseline FedSSP, a ~4.3% relative gain). On the SM-CV setting, FedSSP narrowly edges out FedIGL (84.29% vs. 83.14%), but FedIGL recovers its lead in all three multi-domain settings (e.g., 78.50% vs. 75.07% on BIO-SN-CV). For graph-level clustering, FedIGL achieves the best or near-best F1 scores across all five non-IID configurations, e.g., 69.4 F1 on SM and 67.5 F1 on SM-BIO-SY, consistently outperforming FedGCN and FedSSP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedIGL outperforms all seven baselines on the majority of evaluation settings. Against the strongest federated graph learning baseline FedSSP [32], FedIGL wins 5 of 6 classification settings (e.g., 83.07% vs. 79.62% on SM, 78.50% vs. 75.07% on BIO-SN-CV), with FedSSP narrowly winning only on SM-CV (84.29% vs. 83.14%). FedStar [31] and GCFL [48] are consistently outperformed by 3–8 percentage points across classification and clustering tasks. The classical federated baselines FedAvg [27] and FedProx [21] show the largest gaps, with FedIGL exceeding them by 8–14 points on classification (e.g., 83.07% vs. 74.12% for FedAvg on SM). FedSage [52] is also clearly surpassed, particularly in multi-domain settings where it degrades to 67.91% on BIO-SN-CV versus FedIGL's 78.50%. For clustering, FedGCN [22] is the closest competitor, achieving competitive NMI on SN (30.4 vs. 23.1) but FedIGL leads on ACC, ARI, and F1 across most configurations (e.g., 77.0 vs. 75.9 ACC on SM; 36.3 vs. 31.1 ARI on SM).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends invariant graph learning (IGL), which decomposes graphs into invariant and variant subgraphs for out-of-distribution generalization (Li et al., NeurIPS 2022; Wu et al., ICLR 2022), into the federated learning setting where each client with a distinct distribution is treated as a separate environment. It departs from prior federated graph learning methods (FedSSP, FedStar, FedGCN, GCFL) that rely on sharing generic knowledge such as consensus prototypes, spectral features, or structural encoder parameters, arguing that these impose overly strict assumptions about the correlation between shared structural features and the global objective. The key methodological contribution is the Bi-Gradient Regularization strategy, which enforces gradient consistency on the invariant branch and gradient diversity on the variant branch using previous-round aggregated gradients as references, enabling disentangled subgraph learning without sharing raw structural knowledge.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FedIGL provides the first principled framework for applying invariant learning in federated graph learning, offering a privacy-preserving alternative to prototype- or spectral-knowledge-sharing approaches that risk data leakage. The theoretical guarantees (convergence of the invariant encoder, representation separation for the variant encoder) give confidence in the optimization dynamics under non-IID conditions. However, the privacy argument remains somewhat limited—the method avoids sharing prototypes but still exchanges model parameters, and the authors acknowledge that 'stringent measures for model privacy preservation' remain future work. Open questions include scalability to large-scale graph datasets beyond TU-Datasets, whether the invariant/variant decomposition captures truly causal structure or merely statistical regularity, and how the method performs under more extreme distribution shifts or adversarial client behavior.
