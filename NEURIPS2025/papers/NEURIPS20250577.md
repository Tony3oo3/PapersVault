## Problem

Deep graph clustering (DGC) lacks a unified benchmark: existing studies use fragmented datasets with inconsistent scales and metrics, evaluate almost exclusively on clustering accuracy while neglecting robustness, stability, scalability, and discriminability, and follow non-standardized experimental protocols that impede reproducibility and fair comparison across methods.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250577/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark consumes attributed graphs (adjacency matrix A ∈ R^{n×n} plus feature matrix X ∈ R^{n×d}) or non-graph data where a kNN graph (k=3) is constructed on the fly. Beyond standard clustering labels, it reports both final-epoch and best-intermediate metrics (ACC, NMI, ARI, F1), efficiency metrics (speed, wall-clock time, GPU memory, parameter count), and discriminability indicators (HOM, COM, silhouette coefficient), alongside t-SNE and UMAP visualizations. Robustness is assessed by injecting structural perturbations (edge drop/addition, feature drop at 0.2/0.5/0.8 probability) and Gaussian noise (σ = 0.1/1/10) into node features.

## Method

DGCBench is not a new clustering algorithm but a standardized evaluation framework built around the PyDGC open-source library (compatible with PyG and OGB). It refactors 12 state-of-the-art DGC methods spanning three paradigms—reconstructive (GAE, GAE_S, DAEGC, SDCN, DFCN), predictive (DGCluster), and contrastive (AGC-DRR, DCRN, HSAN, CCGC, MAGI, NS4GC)—into a unified pipeline of initialization, data loading with optional augmentation, feature learning, and multi-dimensional evaluation. The benchmark spans 12 datasets covering small-to-large scales (2,405 to 169,343 nodes), homogeneous/heterogeneous/kNN graph types, and low- to high-dimensional feature spaces. Systematic experiments across six dimensions (effectiveness, robustness, stability, scalability, efficiency, discriminability) reveal that DGC performance is upper-bounded by dataset homophily, that fixed-epoch termination yields suboptimal results relative to best intermediate checkpoints, that the classical GAE remains the most efficient baseline, and that learned embeddings exhibit strong intra-cluster cohesion but weak inter-cluster separability.

## Results

Across 12 homogeneous and heterogeneous datasets, no single DGC method dominates all settings. On high-homophily citation graphs (Cora, ACM, Citeseer), contrastive methods lead: DCRN achieves the best final NMI on ACM (0.7166) and Citeseer (0.4411), while NS4GC attains the best final NMI on Cora (0.5683). On heterogeneous graphs (Blog, Flickr), AGC-DRR and DCRN substantially outperform reconstructive methods (e.g., AGC-DRR NMI 0.6548 vs. SDCN 0.0757 on Blog). A critical finding is the homophily bottleneck: best NMI values on high-homophily datasets (Cora 0.81, ACM 0.82, DBLP 0.80) rarely exceed the dataset's inherent homophily ratio, suggesting a structural performance ceiling. Additionally, the gap between best intermediate and final results is large for many methods (e.g., MAGI on Cora: best NMI 0.5619 vs. final 0.5575; DFCN on Citeseer: best 0.4250 vs. final 0.3630), indicating that fixed-epoch training is suboptimal.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The benchmark evaluates 12 methods across three paradigms (reconstructive, predictive, contrastive) on 12 datasets. On homogeneous citation graphs, contrastive methods (DCRN, AGC-DRR, NS4GC) consistently outperform reconstructive baselines: DCRN achieves the highest final NMI on ACM (0.7166) and Citeseer (0.4411), while NS4GC leads on Cora (0.5683) and PubMed (0.3128). On heterogeneous graphs (Blog, Flickr, Roman), the gap widens dramatically—AGC-DRR reaches NMI 0.6548 on Blog versus 0.0757 for SDCN, and DCRN's best NMI on Blog (0.6683) far exceeds all reconstructive methods. On non-graph kNN data (USPS, HHAR), reconstructive methods reverse the trend: SDCN (NMI 0.7486) and DFCN (NMI 0.7506) outperform all contrastive methods on USPS. The predictive method DGClu is consistently mid-tier, excelling on discriminability (best silhouette coefficient 0.424 on Cora) but trailing on raw clustering accuracy. GAE, the simplest baseline, achieves the best average efficiency score (3.53) with minimal GPU memory (169 MB on Cora), while HSAN and CCGC consume the most memory (up to 13.2 GB on Citeseer). Only MAGI successfully processes the large-scale ARXIV dataset (169K nodes), achieving NMI 0.4684, while all other methods either overflow GPU memory or are not evaluated at that scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper does not propose a new DGC algorithm but instead carves new ground by providing the first unified benchmark (DGCBench) and open-source toolkit (PyDGC) for systematically evaluating the three established DGC paradigms—reconstructive (GAE, DAEGC, SDCN, DFCN), predictive (DGCluster), and contrastive (AGC-DRR, DCRN, HSAN, CCGC, MAGI, NS4GC). It departs from prior DGC studies, which typically report results on a handful of datasets with inconsistent protocols and narrow metrics (mostly ACC/NMI), by standardizing the entire pipeline (initialization, data loading, feature learning, evaluation) and expanding assessment to six dimensions including robustness, stability, scalability, efficiency, and discriminability. The benchmark is built on top of PyG and OGB infrastructure, and the 12 methods are refactored into a common codebase to ensure fair comparison.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The most consequential finding is the homophily bottleneck: across high-homophily datasets, no DGC method's best clustering result significantly exceeds the dataset's inherent homophily ratio, suggesting a fundamental ceiling that current GNN-based approaches cannot break without rethinking the homophily assumption. Additionally, the efficiency plateau—where the 2016 vanilla GAE matches or outperforms 2022–2024 methods on comprehensive efficiency—challenges the field's implicit assumption that architectural complexity translates to practical gains. Open questions include how to design methods that operate beyond the homophily ceiling, how to achieve stable convergence without fixed-epoch termination, and how to scale DGC to industrial-sized graphs without GPU memory overflow. The PyDGC toolkit and DGCBench are likely to become the de facto standard for DGC evaluation, lowering the barrier for fair comparison and accelerating future research.
