## Problem

Existing visual generative models are not amenable to reinforcement learning post-training: diffusion models suffer from intractable inversion that prevents reliable trajectory rewarding, and autoregressive models using spatial visual tokens violate the Bellman equation due to anti-causal dependencies between tokens, breaking the policy improvement theorem. This paper identifies these two fundamental obstacles and shows that neither current paradigm supports the step-by-step policy optimization that makes RL effective for LLMs.

## Input / Output

The model takes a free-form text prompt as input and produces a 256×256 RGB image as output. Internally, the image is represented as a 1D sequence of discrete visual tokens drawn from a 32,768-entry codebook, which are decoded into pixels by a one-step renderer (a modified MMDiT with learnable canvas tokens). The VLM backbone is Llama3-8B with its vocabulary expanded to jointly embed textual and visual tokens in a unified space, so generation proceeds as next-token prediction over a single discrete action space.

## Method

The core contribution is Selftok, a self-consistency tokenizer that encodes an image into a sequential 1D stream of discrete tokens by aligning the AR probability factorization with the recursive structure of the reverse diffusion ODE: each token corresponds to a segment of the denoising trajectory rather than a spatial patch. This AR structure guarantees that the Bellman equation holds, because a future token cannot anti-causally affect an earlier one, enabling valid greedy policy improvement. The VLM is first pre-trained with standard language modeling on interleaved text-visual tokens (530M pairs), then post-trained with a simplified GRPO (no importance sampling, KL-regularized) using two reward types: program-based (MM-Detection for counting, position, color) and QA-based (InternVL/mPLUG VQA for complex prompts). No pairwise text-image supervision is used during the RL stage; the model is incentivized purely by task-specific rewards on completed generations.

## Results

On GenEval, Selftok-Zero achieves a state-of-the-art overall score of 92, surpassing GPT-4o (85), HiDream-I1 (83), and SD3-Medium (74) by 7, 9, and 18 points respectively. On DPG-Bench, Selftok-Zero scores 85.57 overall, outperforming SD3-Medium (84.08) and Janus-Pro-7B (84.19), though slightly trailing HiDream-I1 (85.89). Visual RL provides substantial gains over the supervised baseline: +18 on GenEval (74→92) and +3.77 on DPG-Bench (81.80→85.57), with the largest improvements in Position (45→96) and Counting (66→88) on GenEval.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Selftok-Zero (92) outperforms all 18 baselines on GenEval, with the largest margins over diffusion models: +18 over SD3-Medium (74), +9 over HiDream-I1 (83), and +7 over GPT-4o (85). Among pure dAR models, it surpasses Janus-Pro-7B (80) by 12 points and ILLUME+ (72) by 20 points. On DPG-Bench, Selftok-Zero (85.57) beats SD3-Medium (84.08) and Janus-Pro-7B (84.19) but is marginally below HiDream-I1 (85.89) by 0.32 points. A key finding is that AR Selftok tokens yield substantially larger RL gains than spatial tokens: Selftok-Zero improves +18 over its SFT baseline on GenEval, while Janus-Pro-7B-Zero (spatial tokens) gains only +6 under the same RL procedure. The paper also shows that program-based rewards (GenEval) produce stronger RL gains (+18) than QA-based rewards (DPG-Bench, +3.77), suggesting structured detection signals are more effective training targets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Selftok tokenizer [70], which encodes images into 1D autoregressive token sequences derived from the reverse diffusion process, and adapts the GRPO policy gradient algorithm [58] from LLM RLHF to visual generation. It departs fundamentally from two prior lines: (1) diffusion-based RL (DPOK, DDPO, Diffusion-DPO), which suffers from intractable diffusion inversion and behavior-target policy mismatch, and (2) spatial-token AR models (Janus, Emu3, VAR), which the paper proves violate the Bellman equation due to anti-causal links among grid-arranged tokens. The key conceptual departure is showing that the long-standing spatial prior in visual tokenization is not merely suboptimal but theoretically incompatible with RL-based policy improvement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to demonstrate that RL post-training can substantially enhance open-vocabulary AR visual generation, achieving SOTA on GenEval (92) and DPG-Bench (85.57) without any pairwise supervision. The theoretical result—that only truly AR tokens satisfy the Bellman equation and thus support valid policy improvement—provides a principled design criterion for future visual tokenizers in RL settings. Key limitations include the 256×256 resolution, reliance on relatively simple reward models (detectors + VQA), and the single-model-family validation; open questions include whether the AR-token advantage persists at higher resolutions, whether richer reward signals (e.g., physics-aware) further close the gap, and how to accelerate sequential token generation for practical deployment.
