## Problem

Existing mean-based gossip algorithms are vulnerable to corrupted or outlier-contaminated nodes, yet robust statistics such as trimmed means have not been explored in the gossip literature. The core difficulty is that ranks and trimmed means are inherently global statistics—unlike averages, minima, or maxima that rely on local conservation—making them substantially harder to estimate in a fully decentralized, pairwise-communication setting over arbitrary graphs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250894/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

Each node k holds a single real-valued observation X_k on a connected, non-bipartite graph G=(V,E); at every synchronous iteration one edge is drawn uniformly at random and only its two endpoints communicate. The trimming level α∈(0,1/2) and the contamination fraction ε<1/2 (Huber model) are the only additional parameters. GORANK outputs a per-node rank estimate R_k(t)∈[1,n]; GOTRIM outputs a per-node trimmed-mean estimate Z_k(t) that converges to the α-trimmed mean x̄_α, formulated as a weighted average (1/n)Σ w_{n,α}(r_k)X_k where the weight function is an indicator over the inclusion interval [⌊αn⌋+1, n−⌊αn⌋].

## Method

GORANK exploits the identity r_k = 1 + n·(1/n)Σ I{X_k > X_l}, i.e., a rank is a U-statistic of degree 2 computable from pairwise comparisons. Each node maintains an auxiliary observation Y_k that performs a random walk on the graph via random edge swaps; at iteration t the node updates its comparison indicator as a running average (1−1/t)R'_k + (1/t)·I{X_k > Y_k}, yielding an O(1/ct) convergence rate where c = λ₂/|E| is the graph's algebraic connectivity. GOTRIM pairs with any ranking algorithm (including GORANK) and dynamically adjusts a weighted gossip average: at each step it injects the rank-change correction (W_k(t)−W_k(t−1))·X_k into the standard gossip averaging matrix W₂ = I − (1/2|E|)L, so that past rank errors are compensated online. This yields an O(1/c²t) convergence rate for the trimmed mean and a τ-breakdown point that recovers the exact α-trimmed-mean breakdown point as t→∞.

## Results

GORANK achieves an O(1/ct) convergence rate for expected rank estimation and O(1/√(ct)) for expected absolute error, where c = λ₂/|E| is the graph's algebraic connectivity. GOTRIM achieves an O(1/c²t) convergence rate for trimmed mean estimation, with a τ-breakdown point bounded below by (⌊⌊αn⌋ + 1/2 − K(δ)/√(t−T)⌋)/n and above by ⌊αn⌋/n, recovering the exact α-trimmed mean breakdown point as t→∞. Empirically, on n=500 nodes over 8×10⁴ iterations, GORANK consistently outperforms the Chiuso et al. baseline on poorly connected graphs (2D grid, c=1.65×10⁻⁵), and GOTRIM with ε=0.1 corruption (scaling s=10, shifting s=100) quickly surpasses the naive corrupted mean and converges to the true trimmed mean (α=0.2) on both synthetic and the Basel Luftklima dataset (n=105 sensors, c=4.7×10⁻⁴).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Chiuso et al. baseline [9] (the only prior decentralized ranking algorithm), GORANK provides faster rank approximations on large, poorly connected graphs such as the 2D grid, where the baseline's convergence is bottlenecked by the random-walk return time. The authors' improved variant Baseline++ ultimately achieves lower final error than GORANK by directly minimizing the Kendall τ loss, but requires an initial ranking and lacks convergence rate guarantees on arbitrary graphs. Against ClippedGossip [17] (He et al.), GOTRIM clearly wins under Huber contamination: ClippedGossip with a fixed clipping radius ultimately converges to the corrupted mean and fails to reduce outlier impact, whereas GOTRIM converges to the true α-trimmed mean. On the Basel Luftklima dataset (n=105, shift corruption s=100, α=0.2), GOTRIM quickly improves over the naive corrupted mean (black dashed line in Fig. 3c) and tracks the trimmed mean, while ClippedGossip remains stuck at the biased value. Overall, GORANK and GOTRIM are the only methods in the comparison that are simultaneously fully decentralized, unbiased, and equipped with O(1/t) convergence rates on arbitrary (not just complete) communication graphs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the GOSTA framework (Colin et al., NeurIPS 2015), originally designed for distributed U-statistic estimation, to the novel problem of gossip-based rank estimation by exploiting the decomposition of ranks into pairwise comparison indicators. It departs from the only prior decentralized ranking algorithm (Chiuso et al., 2011), which guarantees almost-sure finite-time convergence but provides no rate bounds and performs poorly on poorly connected graphs. The trimmed mean estimation (GOTRIM) is positioned as the first gossip algorithm for this statistic, contrasting with federated learning approaches that rely on a central server for robust aggregation and with ClippedGossip (He et al., 2022), which the authors show ultimately converges to the corrupted mean rather than a robust estimate.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work fills a notable gap in the gossip literature by providing the first convergence rate guarantees (O(1/t)) for both distributed ranking and trimmed mean estimation on arbitrary communication graphs, along with a breakdown point analysis that quantifies robustness at finite iterations. The practical relevance is significant for sensor networks, IoT, and edge computing where centralized aggregation is infeasible and data contamination is common. Key open questions include extending the analysis to the Byzantine model (where nodes actively send incorrect updates), completing the asynchronous convergence theory, and determining whether the c² dependence in the GOTRIM bound is tight or an artifact of the analysis.
