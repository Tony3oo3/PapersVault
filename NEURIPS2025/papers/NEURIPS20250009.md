## Problem

Diffusion-based antibody design methods are bottlenecked by the scarcity of experimentally resolved antibody-antigen complex data, while AlphaFold3-like protein folding models have acquired broad biomolecular interaction knowledge from large PDB corpora but lack any sequence generation capability. This paper asks whether the general protein-interaction knowledge embedded in these folding foundation models can be transferred to the specialized task of antibody CDR sequence-structure co-design.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250009/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a full antibody-antigen co-crystal complex as context: all non-CDR regions (antibody framework sequences and structures, antigen sequences and structures, and the binding pose) are fixed, while CDR positions are replaced with <X> tokens. The model jointly outputs CDR amino acid sequences (one of 20 standard residues per position) and backbone atom coordinates; side-chain packing and structure relaxation are then performed post-hoc with PyRosetta. Replacement sampling is used at inference so that the generated CDRs are conditioned on the given co-crystal structure, enabling in-situ redesign rather than de novo generation.

## Method

The core contribution is augmenting Boltz-1's (an open-source AF3 reimplementation) existing EDM-based structure diffusion module with a discrete sequence diffusion head using D3PM-absorbing, where <X> serves as the natural absorbing state analogous to BERT's <MASK>. A key alignment trick restricts structure diffusion training to sample noise levels only from the same 200 predefined levels used at inference, creating a discrete timestep schedule that matches the 200-step sequence diffusion, thereby enabling joint training without a theoretical bridge between continuous and discrete diffusion. The TokenDenoiser (a small MLP with GELU activations) directly reuses the token-level representations {a_i} already produced by the structure diffusion's DiffusionTransformer, avoiding any additional backbone. The entire Boltz-1 network is fine-tuned over four stages with progressive token cropping (256→384→512 tokens) and a final full-complex stage that mixes antibody-local and global sampling to preserve general structure prediction. A continuous-diffusion variant (MFDesign-C) using EDM for sequence is also implemented and shown to excel on RMSD while the discrete variant (MFDesign-D) leads on AAR.

## Results

On typical antibodies with all CDRs designed simultaneously, MFDesign-D achieves 65.04% AAR on CDR-H3 (the most challenging loop), outperforming the best baseline dyMEAN (34.69%) by 87.5%. On the key Loop-AAR metric (middle loop of CDR-H3, the primary antigen-binding region), MFDesign-D reaches 63.38% versus 20.80% for dyMEAN and 15.86% for DiffAb — a 204.7% improvement over the best baseline. For binding energy improvement (IMP), MFDesign-C achieves 65.37%, well above DiffAb (53.35%) and dyMEAN (5.60%). On nanobodies, MFDesign-D maintains 63.51% CDR-H3 AAR while DiffAb drops to only 15.39%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two representative baselines: DiffAb (Luo et al., 2022), a diffusion-based generative method, and dyMEAN (Kong et al., 2023a), a 3D equivariant graph translation (discriminative) method. MFDesign dominates both baselines across nearly all metrics. On CDR-H3 AAR, MFDesign-D (65.04%) surpasses dyMEAN (34.69%) by 87.5% and DiffAb (22.26%) by 192%. On Loop-AAR, the improvement is even more dramatic: MFDesign-D (63.38%) vs. dyMEAN (20.80%) and DiffAb (15.86%). For structural accuracy (RMSD), MFDesign-C achieves the best values across all six CDRs (e.g., 1.26 Å on CDR-H2 vs. 1.44–1.47 Å for baselines). The one exception is CDR-H2 AAR, where dyMEAN (66.38%) slightly edges out MFDesign-C (65.59%), though MFDesign-D (67.54%) still leads. On nanobodies, where data is scarcer, MFDesign-D maintains 63.51% CDR-H3 AAR while DiffAb collapses to 15.39%, highlighting the advantage of leveraging pre-trained protein interaction knowledge.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the structure diffusion module of AlphaFold3-like folding models (specifically Boltz-1, an open-source AF3 reimplementation) by augmenting it with a discrete sequence diffusion head (D3PM-absorbing) to enable simultaneous CDR sequence and structure generation. Conceptually, it bridges two previously separate lines of work: protein folding models that learn general biomolecular interaction from large PDB corpora, and antibody-specific design methods (DiffAb, dyMEAN, AbDiffuser, AbX) that are constrained by the scarcity of labeled antibody-antigen complex data. The key architectural insight is that AF3's existing token-level representations from its structure denoising pipeline can be directly reused as input to a lightweight TokenDenoiser MLP, requiring minimal modification to the pre-trained network.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By transferring general protein interaction knowledge from a foundation model to the antibody design task, this work addresses the fundamental data bottleneck that limits purpose-built antibody design methods, achieving a 65% CDR-H3 recovery rate (87% improvement over the best baseline) while retaining structure prediction accuracy comparable to the original folding model. The approach is notably simple—adding a single MLP head and aligning diffusion timesteps—yet yields substantial gains, suggesting that foundation model pre-training may be underexploited for specialized biomolecular design tasks. Open questions include whether the slight degradation in structure prediction when CDRs are provided (Table 4) can be mitigated, whether the approach extends to other biomolecular co-design problems (e.g., enzyme design, protein-protein interface design), and how performance scales with larger antibody-antigen training sets as data becomes available.
