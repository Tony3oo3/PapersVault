## Problem

Temperature scaling, the standard technique for increasing LLM response diversity, fails under mode collapse where next-token probabilities are severely miscalibrated (e.g., GPT-4 assigns 87% of logit weight to 'California' when asked to name a US state). Raising temperature only marginally softens this skew while degrading generation quality, which is especially problematic for syntax-sensitive tasks like code generation. The paper addresses how to achieve diverse, high-quality LLM responses without relying on temperature scaling, even when the model's own probability distribution is heavily biased.

## Input / Output

The method takes a user prompt (e.g., 'Name a US State') and an LLM (proprietary or open-source) as input, and produces diverse responses by first constructing a stratified prompt — the original prompt augmented with a randomly sampled stratum constraint (e.g., 'east of the Mississippi River') — then sampling from the LLM at the user's chosen temperature. The entire pipeline is training-free and operates at inference time; the auto-stratification and heuristic estimation outputs can be cached per prompt to avoid recomputation. The probabilistic prompt distribution is human-readable, allowing practitioners to inspect and manually adjust stratum proportions to correct unwanted biases.

## Method

SimpleStrat is a three-stage, training-free sampling approach: (1) auto-stratification prompts the LLM using a 20-questions framing to identify balanced partition dimensions of the solution space (e.g., East/West of the Mississippi River and North/South of the Missouri Compromise Line for US states); (2) heuristic estimation uses LLM forecasting (inspired by Halawi et al., 2024) to estimate the marginal proportion of valid solutions in each stratum, then computes joint stratum probabilities under an independence assumption; (3) probabilistic prompting samples a stratum from the joint distribution, augments the original prompt with the stratum's constraints, and samples a final answer from the LLM. The key insight is that even a model incapable of generating diverse answers can be prompted to identify semantically meaningful partitions of the solution space. Diversity arises from both the random stratum selection and the LLM's decoding temperature, making SimpleStrat orthogonal to and composable with temperature scaling.

## Results

On CoverageQA, SimpleStrat applied to GPT-4o achieves up to 3.7× higher recall than standard GPT-4o at temperature 0 (0.2423 vs. 0.0646) and 1.7× at temperature 1.5 (0.4634 vs. 0.2676). On Llama 3 models, SimpleStrat reduces average KL divergence from the uniform target distribution by 0.36 on CoverageQA-Wikipedia and by 1.14 on CoverageQA-Curated (e.g., Llama-3.1-70B drops from 2.70 to 1.54 on the curated set). On WritingPrompts creative writing, SimpleStrat at temperature 0 achieves the same pairwise embedding diversity as standard GPT-4o at temperature 1, without degrading format adherence or spelling quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SimpleStrat consistently outperforms standard temperature-scaled GPT-4o across all temperatures on CoverageQA recall, with the largest relative gain at low temperatures (3.7× at T=0, narrowing to 1.7× at T=1.5), confirming that stratification and temperature scaling provide orthogonal diversity benefits. Against AttrPrompt (Yu et al., 2023), which directly asks the model for partitions without the 20-questions framing, SimpleStrat achieves substantially higher recall at every temperature (e.g., 0.2423 vs. 0.1215 at T=0; 0.4634 vs. 0.3304 at T=1.5), demonstrating that the 20-questions auto-stratification produces better-balanced and more semantically meaningful partitions. On the distributional diversity metric (KL divergence), SimpleStrat on Llama 3.1-70B reduces divergence from 2.70 to 1.54 on CoverageQA-Curated and from 2.78 to 2.38 on CoverageQA-Wikipedia, while the single-prompt ablation (which collapses all instructions into one prompt) performs worse than the unmodified GPT-4o baseline, confirming that the multi-stage probabilistic prompting is essential rather than mere prompt length. Precision is maintained with only a minor ~10% reduction, indicating the diversity gains do not come at a significant quality cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

SimpleStrat repurposes classical stratified sampling from statistics (Simpson, 1951) into the LLM generation context, departing from the dominant temperature-scaling paradigm and from search-based methods like Diverse Beam Search that operate over token sequences. It also distinguishes itself from MAP-Elites-style quality-diversity approaches (Samvelyan et al., 2024; Bradley et al., 2023) by eliminating the need for manually specified diversity dimensions and seed solutions, instead using the LLM itself in a '20 questions' framing to auto-identify balanced partition functions. The closest prior method is AttrPrompt (Yu et al., 2023), which also asks the model for partitions, but SimpleStrat's multi-stage probabilistic prompting (with heuristic probability estimation and random stratum selection) introduces controlled randomness that the ablations show is essential to the diversity gains.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical and well-motivated gap: temperature scaling fails under mode collapse (e.g., GPT-4 assigning 87% logit weight to 'California'), and the resulting diversity loss is particularly harmful in low-temperature settings like code generation and multi-step reasoning where quality must be preserved. CoverageQA fills a genuine benchmark gap by providing underspecified questions with exhaustively enumerable valid answers, enabling quantitative diversity measurement that prior ambiguous-QA datasets (ClariQ, AmbigQA) do not support. Open questions include how the method scales to long-form or multi-step generation, how sensitive it is to the quality of the LLM's probability estimates in heuristic estimation, and whether the independence assumption across partition functions holds in practice for complex solution spaces.
