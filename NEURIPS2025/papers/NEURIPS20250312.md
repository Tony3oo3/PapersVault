## Problem

Preference-based Pure Exploration (PrePEx) requires identifying the exact Pareto optimal arm set in a multi-objective bandit under an arbitrary preference cone, and a matching information-theoretic lower bound exists (Shukla and Basu, 2024). However, no computationally efficient algorithm can track this lower bound: the existing PreTS algorithm is intractable due to optimization over a non-convex Alt-set, and prior efficient methods are restricted to the positive orthant cone or Gaussian rewards. This paper fills the gap by making the lower-bound optimization tractable for arbitrary polyhedral cones and general exponential family distributions.

## Input / Output

The algorithm receives a bandit instance with K arms each yielding L-dimensional reward vectors from an L-parameter exponential family, a polyhedral preference cone C specified by its half-space matrix W (full row-rank, normalized), and a confidence parameter δ. At each step it consumes one L-dimensional reward vector from the pulled arm and outputs a recommended Pareto optimal arm set P_t with probability at least 1−δ of correctness. Internally it maintains an allocation vector ω over the simplex Δ_K, updated via Frank-Wolfe, and a per-arm pull count N_{k,t} used in the C-tracking sampling rule and the Chernoff-type stopping threshold c(t,δ).

## Method

FraPPE reduces the intractable sup-inf-inf-inf optimization in the PrePEx lower bound through three structural observations: (1) the Pareto optimal policy set Π^P is spanned by p pure policies, so the inf over policies reduces to a finite min over O(K min{K,L}) pairs of Pareto arms and their Carathéodory neighbours; (2) the Alt-set decomposes into a union of convex sets Λ_ij(M), eliminating the need for the expensive convex-hull construction used by PreTS; (3) each alternative instance is parameterized by an L-dimensional vector on the boundary of the polar cone C° rather than a full K×L matrix. The outer maximization over the allocation ω is then solved with a Frank-Wolfe method adapted via an r-sub-differential set H_M(ω,r) to handle the non-smooth min over neighbours, achieving O(1/tol) per-step cost instead of O(K/tol). A relaxed Chernoff-type stopping rule (Eq. 8) replaces the intractable convex-hull stopping criterion, and the overall per-step complexity is O(KL min{K,L}), yielding asymptotic optimality as δ→0 for arbitrary polyhedral cones and exponential family rewards.

## Results

On the COV-BOOST trial dataset (K=20 arms, L=3 objectives, δ=0.1), FraPPE achieves an average sample complexity of 3,523 over 100 independent runs, compared to 20,456 for PSIPS and 17,909 for TnS — a 5–6× reduction. Computationally, FraPPE runs in O(KL·min{K,L}) time per step, versus O(K^{L+1}L³) for the prior gradient-based method (Crepon et al., 2024) and an intractable convex-hull construction for PreTS (Shukla and Basu, 2024). On a synthetic correlated-Gaussian instance (5 arms, 2 objectives), FraPPE uniformly outperforms PSIPS in sample complexity across all correlation coefficients from −1 to 1, with narrower standard deviations indicating greater stability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FraPPE dominates all three baselines on the primary metric of sample complexity. Against PSIPS (Kone et al., 2024), FraPPE reduces average sample complexity from 20,456 to 3,523 on COV-BOOST (δ=0.1) and also exhibits lower variance across runs, making it a more stable strategy. Against TnS (Crepon et al., 2024), FraPPE achieves roughly 5× lower sample complexity (3,523 vs. 17,909) while also reducing per-step computational cost from O(K^{L+1}L³) to O(KL·min{K,L}). Against PreTS (Shukla and Basu, 2024), FraPPE provides the first tractable implementation of the same lower bound, replacing an intractable convex-hull construction with a union-of-convex-sets reduction solvable in O(K·min{K,L}) oracle calls. On the correlated-objectives experiment, FraPPE uniformly beats PSIPS across all 21 correlation values tested (−1 to 1 in steps of 0.1), with the gap widening at extreme correlations. FraPPE also reduces the error probability 1(P̂_t ≠ P*) faster than both PSIPS and a uniform sampler on COV-BOOST.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the PrePEx problem formulation and information-theoretic lower bound of Shukla and Basu (2024), whose PreTS algorithm was computationally intractable due to a convex hull construction over the Alt-set. The key departure is three structural observations (Pareto policy basis, Alt-set as union of convex sets, polar cone representation) that eliminate the convex hull and reduce the intractable sup-inf-inf-inf problem to a tractable max-min-min-min problem. The Frank-Wolfe acceleration for the outer maximisation follows Wang et al. (2021) for scalar BAI, extended here to vector-valued rewards under arbitrary preference cones and exponential family distributions, going beyond the Gaussian-only and positive-orthant-only restrictions of Crepon et al. (2024) and Kone et al. (2024).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FraPPE is the first algorithm that is simultaneously computationally efficient (polynomial in K and L), asymptotically optimal, and applicable to arbitrary preference cones with general exponential family rewards, resolving the main open problem posed by Crepon et al. (2024). The 5–6× reduction in sample complexity on COV-BOOST is practically meaningful for costly multi-objective experiments (clinical trials, materials discovery). Open questions include learning the preference cone simultaneously with the Pareto set, scaling to structured bandits (linear, contextual), and applying the framework to RLHF where reward modelling is inherently multi-objective and preference-dependent.
