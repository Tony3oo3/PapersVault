## Problem

Existing protein function prediction methods rely on predefined ontologies (e.g., GO terms) or homology-based retrieval (e.g., BLAST), limiting their expressiveness and generalization to novel sequences. Deep learning approaches that generate free-text descriptions have not been rigorously evaluated under low-homology conditions, leaving open whether they truly learn functional semantics or merely exploit sequence similarity to training examples.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250883/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an amino acid sequence (truncated to 1,024 residues for ESM-3B compatibility) as primary input, with optional protein name and taxonomy fields that are dropped with 80% probability during training to ensure robustness to missing metadata. It generates a free-form natural language description of protein function (up to 512 tokens at inference) via autoregressive decoding. Evaluation is conducted under a strict low-homology protocol where CD-HIT clustering enforces ≤40% sequence identity between training and test sets, making the setting far more challenging than standard random splits.

## Method

Prot2Text-V2 combines a frozen ESM-3B protein encoder with a LLaMA-3.1-8B-Instruct decoder through a two-layer nonlinear modality projector (linear → GELU → linear → LayerNorm) that maps protein embeddings into the decoder's hidden space; these projected embeddings replace placeholder tokens in a structured chat template via soft-prompt injection. The key innovation is H-SCALE (Hybrid Sequence-level Contrastive Alignment Learning), a pretraining stage that aligns both mean-pooled and standard-deviation-pooled protein embeddings with intermediate (16th-block) text representations from the frozen decoder using a combined InfoNCE loss (λ₁=0.7 for mean, λ₂=0.3 for std), effectively matching the first two moments of the cross-modal embedding distributions. This is followed by LoRA-based instruction fine-tuning (rank 32, α=64) with cross-entropy loss computed only over caption tokens, enabling the decoder to generate function descriptions conditioned on the aligned protein features. Unlike ProtT3's Q-Former with learnable queries and a redundant text encoder, H-SCALE is parameter-free at the alignment level, preserving the full expressivity of the pretrained protein encoder while reducing latency and memory overhead.

## Results

On the low-homology SwissProt test set (≤40% sequence identity between train and test), Prot2Text-V2 with H-SCALE achieves BLEU-2 F1 of 46.67, BLEU-4 F1 of 43.34, ROUGE-L F1 of 55.39, RoBERTa BERTScore of 91.95, BioBERT BERTScore of 86.81, and a GPT-4o LLM-judge score of 64.58. In the low-similarity regime (BLAST bitscore 0–50), Prot2Text-V2 substantially outperforms both Prot2Text and BLAST, with BLEU-4 F1 of 11.89–16.05 versus 6.19–13.10 for ProtT3 and 6.58–12.58 for Prot2Text. On the ProtT3 data split (random partitioning), Prot2Text-V2 reaches BLEU-4 F1 of 57.29 and ROUGE-L F1 of 65.61, surpassing ProtT3 (51.47 and 62.16) by 6+ points on both metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Prot2Text-V2 (w/ H-SCALE) outperforms all baselines across every reported metric. Against the closest prior protein-to-text model, Prot2Text, it gains +10.3 BLEU-2, +10.9 BLEU-4, +6.9 ROUGE-L, +1.4 RoBERTa, +2.6 BioBERT, and +7.6 GPT-judge points. Against ProtT3 (on the ProtT3 random-split protocol), it leads by +5.1 BLEU-2, +5.8 BLEU-4, +3.5 ROUGE-L, and +2.4 ROUGE-2. General-purpose LLMs (GPT-4o-mini, Claude-3.5-Sonnet, LLaMA-3.1) are far behind, scoring below 10 on BLEU-2 and below 84 on RoBERTa, confirming that domain-specific alignment is critical. In the low-homology regime (BLAST bitscore 0–50), Prot2Text-V2 achieves BLEU-4 F1 of 11.89–16.05, roughly doubling Prot2Text (6.58–12.58) and ProtT3 (6.19–13.10), while BLAST degrades sharply below 40% identity. The ablation without H-SCALE (Prot2Text-V2 w/o CL) trails the full model by ~4 BLEU-2 and ~3.7 GPT-judge points, confirming the contrastive alignment stage's contribution. In human expert evaluation (1–5 scale), Prot2Text-V2 scores 4.0–5.0 on well-characterized proteins, outperforming GPT-4o (2.9–3.8), Grok-3 (2.6–2.6), and Claude-3.7 (1.9–3.4), though Evolla-10B (trained on ~200× more data) edges it slightly at 4.5–5.0.

## Relation to Prior Work

Prot2Text-V2 extends the protein-to-text generation line opened by Prot2Text (Abdine et al., 2024) and departs from ProtT3 (Liu et al., 2024) in its cross-modal alignment strategy. Where ProtT3 relies on a Q-Former with learnable queries and a redundant text encoder, this paper replaces that learned bottleneck with a two-layer nonlinear modality projector and a parameter-free contrastive objective (H-SCALE) that aligns mean- and std-pooled protein embeddings to intermediate LLaMA decoder representations. The architecture is otherwise a standard ESM-3B encoder + LLaMA-3.1-8B decoder-only LLM with LoRA instruction tuning, making the novelty concentrated in the alignment phase rather than the generative backbone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's emphasis on low-homology evaluation (≤40% sequence identity via CD-HIT) addresses a real gap: most prior protein-function models are never stress-tested on remote homologs where sequence copying fails. The finding that a model trained on ~250K curated SwissProt entries is within one expert point of Eolla-10B (trained on ~50M proteins) suggests efficient data curation and contrastive pretraining can partially substitute for raw scale. Key open questions include whether the approach transfers to wet-lab-validated biological accuracy (the paper explicitly lacks this) and whether H-SCALE's gains persist at much larger dataset scales where the alignment phase may become less critical.
