## Problem

LLM-as-Benchmark-Generator methods are widely used for scalable evaluation, but the self-bias phenomenon—where LLMs achieve overestimated performance on benchmarks they generate themselves—remains systematically unexplored. This bias inflates evaluation results and undermines the reliability of LLM-generated benchmarks, yet prior work on benchmark generation (e.g., BenchMaker, AttrPrompt) does not account for the generator's own bias, leaving a gap between generated benchmark quality and true model capability assessment.

## Input / Output

SILENCER takes a task description, seed samples, and a pool of T > 2 LLM generators as input, and produces a self-bias-silenced benchmark of configurable size N (default 50 per sub-task) along with per-generator ensemble weights. Each output sample is a (question, label) pair that has been processed through cross-generator attribute integration, cross-paraphrase, and label calibration. The final benchmark is a weighted union of the T processed sub-benchmarks, with weights determined iteratively by the Bias-Neutralizing Ensemble Algorithm rather than set uniformly.

## Method

SILENCER operates at two levels. At the sample level, it mitigates three formally defined sub-biases: Attribute Integration (each generator consolidates attribute sets from all T generators to produce domain-unbiased question topics, reducing B^q), Cross Paraphrase (each generator paraphrases another generator's questions to break stylistic monotony, reducing B^s), and Label Calibration (each generator revises its own labels using predictions and rationales from all other generators, reducing B^l, which is the dominant sub-bias at ~67–80% of total self-bias). At the benchmark level, the Bias-Neutralizing Ensemble Algorithm iteratively computes each generator's evaluation consistency (Pearson correlation) with the current ensembled benchmark, then updates ensemble weights via ReLU(correlation) + ε, converging to a fixed point where weights are inversely proportional to estimated self-bias. The paper proves convergence via contraction mapping and shows that the Evaluation Consistency strategy outperforms naive Self-Bias and Accuracy-based weighting (correlation with true self-bias: 0.861 vs. 0.530 vs. 0.343).

## Results

SILENCER reduces average self-bias from 0.113 to 0.009 and improves average evaluation effectiveness (Pearson correlation with human-annotated benchmarks) from 0.655 to 0.833 across three tasks (MATH, MMLU-Pro, HellaSwag) and seven LLM generators. On MATH reasoning with BenchMaker, self-bias drops from 0.1205 to 0.0140 while r_p rises from 0.7863 to 0.9386. On MMLU-Pro, self-bias is nearly eliminated (0.0802 → 0.0011) with r_p reaching 0.8340. The framework also improves label accuracy from 0.877 to 0.892 and increases benchmark diversity (entropy from 9.749 to 10.078).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SILENCER is evaluated as a plug-in framework on top of two existing LLM-as-Benchmark-Generator methods: BenchMaker (Yuan et al., 2025) and AttrPrompt (Yu et al., 2023). Against BenchMaker, SILENCER reduces self-bias by 88–99% (e.g., 0.1205→0.0140 on MATH, 0.0802→0.0011 on MMLU-Pro) and boosts Pearson correlation with human-annotated benchmarks by 15–25 percentage points (e.g., 0.7863→0.9386 on MATH). Against AttrPrompt, the gains are even larger in relative terms: self-bias drops from 0.1427 to 0.0187 on MATH (−87%) and r_p jumps from 0.6179 to 0.8641 (+24.6 pp). SILENCER wins on every metric across all three tasks for both baselines, with the largest absolute r_p improvement observed on MMLU-Pro (from 0.6816 to 0.8340 with BenchMaker, from 0.5843 to 0.7725 with AttrPrompt). The ablation further shows that Label Calibration contributes the most to self-bias reduction at the sample level, consistent with wrong-label bias being the dominant sub-bias (67–80% of total self-bias).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the self-bias concept previously studied in the LLM-as-a-Judge setting (Panickssery et al., 2024; Xu et al., 2024) to the LLM-as-Benchmark-Generator paradigm, where the bias manifests not as content-based self-preference but through a combination of domain, style, and label misalignment. It builds on existing benchmark generation methods (BenchMaker, AttrPrompt) as baselines but introduces a multi-level bias-neutralization framework that repurposes ensemble weighting, cross-paraphrasing, and label calibration in a unified pipeline. The Bias-Neutralizing Ensemble Algorithm draws on the general idea of weighted aggregation but applies an iterative consistency-based weighting scheme specifically designed to counteract generator-specific self-bias without requiring a human-annotated reference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As LLM-generated benchmarks become increasingly common to address benchmark saturation and data contamination, unmitigated self-bias systematically inflates model performance estimates, undermining the reliability of the entire evaluation pipeline. The finding that wrong-label bias accounts for 67–80% of self-bias (far more than domain or style effects) redirects mitigation efforts toward label quality rather than question diversity alone. The ~30% additional token cost is a practical trade-off, and open questions remain about how the framework behaves with highly homogeneous generator pools, with very large benchmark sizes, or in settings where no reference models are available for the consistency-based weighting.
