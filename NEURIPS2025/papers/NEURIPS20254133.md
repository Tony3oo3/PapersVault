## Problem

Conventional model merging methods apply uniform scaling coefficients across all layers, ignoring the functional heterogeneity of neural components (e.g., lm_head governs generation length while intermediate layers encode reasoning). Existing activation-based merging either relies solely on pre-trained model activations, overlooking fine-tuned task characteristics, or requires complex backpropagation to compute sensitivity. This paper addresses both limitations by deriving layer-specific coefficients from the mutual information between pre-trained and fine-tuned model activations, without any gradient computation.

## Input / Output

The method consumes the weight matrices of a pre-trained backbone and one or more fine-tuned models, along with a small calibration corpus (~100 samples drawn via K-means clustering from the s1K dataset, with 8K/10K max sequence lengths for fast/slow-thinking models). It produces a single merged model whose parameters are computed layer-by-layer as θ₀ᵏ + (1/N)Σλᵢᵏ·δᵢᵏ, where each λᵢᵏ is a layer-specific coefficient in [0,1] derived from sigmoid-normalized mutual information. The resulting coefficients are plug-and-play: they can be dropped into any existing task-vector merging framework (Task Arithmetic, TIES-Merging, DARE) without modifying the underlying arithmetic.

## Method

ACM first establishes a theoretical link between activation patterns and weight salience via Taylor expansion of the activation function, showing that parameter importance emerges from gradient-perturbation interactions. It then computes layer-wise activations for both the pre-trained and fine-tuned models on a shared calibration set, and measures the mutual information I(A₀ᵏ, Aᵢᵏ) between corresponding activations at each layer k. These MI values are normalized through a sigmoid transform λᵢᵏ = 1 − σ(t·Iᵢᵏ), yielding coefficients that are inversely proportional to similarity: layers with high MI (shared generative capability) receive lower weights to suppress redundancy, while layers with low MI (task-specific reflective capability) receive higher weights to preserve fine-tuned knowledge. The entire procedure is training-free, requires only a forward pass over ~100 calibration samples, and runs in tens of seconds to a couple of minutes depending on model size.

## Results

On Qwen-7B L2S merging, ACM-TIES achieves 55.8% average accuracy across six math benchmarks (GSM8K, MATH500, Minerva Math, OlympiadBench, College Math, AIME24), a +1.3 point improvement over the best baseline (TIES-Merging at 54.8%), while reducing average response length by 55.3% relative to DeepSeek-R1-7B. On the smaller Qwen-1.5B model, ACM-TIES reaches 43.3% average accuracy (+2.3 over TIES-Merging's 39.1%) with a 73.5% length reduction. On general merging (LLaMA-7B, Table 3), ACM-TIES improves average accuracy from 33.62 (TIES) to 33.70, and on Qwen-14B (Table 8), ACM-TA achieves 73.3% average accuracy (+0.3 over Task Arithmetic's 69.1%) with comparable efficiency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ACM consistently outperforms all task-vector-based merging baselines on accuracy: ACM-TIES (55.8%) beats TIES-Merging (54.8%), Task Arithmetic (53.5%), DARE (47.3%), and Average Merging (48.6%) on Qwen-7B, with the gap widening on Qwen-1.5B where ACM-TIES (43.3%) surpasses TIES-Merging (39.1%) by 4.2 points. Against activation-based baselines, ACM-TIES edges out AIM (55.0%) and Sens-Merging (55.0%) by 0.8 points on 7B while achieving the shortest response length (538.3 tokens on GSM8K vs. 604.2 for Sens-Merging). Compared to the training-based DPO baseline (56.7%), ACM-TIES sacrifices only 0.9 points in accuracy but achieves a 55.3% length reduction versus DPO's 15.0%, without any training cost. On the general LLaMA-7B merging task, ACM-TIES improves over TIES by +0.08 average (33.70 vs. 33.62) and ACM-TA over TA by +0.1 (33.18 vs. 33.08), with the largest gains on HellaSwag (+0.18 and +0.22 respectively). On Qwen-14B, ACM-TA (73.3%) substantially outperforms Task Arithmetic (69.1%) and TIES-Merging (66.9%) by 4.2 and 6.4 points respectively, while on Qwen-32B, ACM-TA (77.4%) improves over Task Arithmetic (76.9%) and TIES-Merging (75.5%). The DeepSeek-R1 reference model achieves 54.5% average accuracy but with much longer responses (e.g., 8675 tokens on AIME24 vs. 3076 for ACM-TIES), confirming ACM's core advantage of matching or exceeding System 2 accuracy at System 1-level brevity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the task-vector model merging lineage (Task Arithmetic, TIES-Merging, DARE) by replacing their uniform layer-wise scaling with activation-informed coefficients. It specifically departs from prior activation-based merging methods (AIM, which only uses pre-trained model activations, and Sens-Merging, which requires backpropagation) by computing mutual information between corresponding PT and FT layer activations on a shared calibration set, then mapping these MI scores to layer-specific coefficients via a sigmoid transform. The theoretical grounding connects activation patterns to weight salience through a Taylor-expansion argument, positioning MI as a principled redundancy measure that avoids the asymmetry of KL divergence and the interpretability issues of cosine similarity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ACM offers a training-free, plug-and-play mechanism to combine System 1 and System 2 LLMs, which is timely given the rapid proliferation of reasoning models (o1, R1, QwQ) that are accurate but verbose. The observation that embed and lm_head layers exhibit substantially higher MI than intermediate layers provides a concrete explanation for where fast- and slow-thinking models truly diverge, informing future merging and distillation strategies. Key limitations include the difficulty of achieving meaningful length reduction in larger models (14B/32B) due to parameter redundancy, and the method's dependence on a calibration dataset whose composition (clustering, sampling rate) still requires some tuning. Open questions include whether MI-based coefficients generalize to merging models from different architectures or to multi-task settings beyond the two-model PT/FT setup.
