## Problem

Super-resolution for PBR texture maps (albedo, roughness, metallic, normal) is fundamentally harder than natural image SR because no large-scale paired LR-HR PBR datasets exist for supervised training, and off-the-shelf image SR models ignore the spatial coherence and distinct material properties required for physically based rendering. Applying 2D SR directly to PBR channels or to rendered images introduces view- and lighting-dependent inconsistencies that break material fidelity and prevent downstream tasks like relighting.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D mesh with UV coordinates and its corresponding low-resolution PBR texture set (albedo, roughness, metallic, normal maps) at 4× lower resolution (e.g., 1024×1024 or 512×512) and produces high-resolution PBR texture maps at 4× scale (e.g., 4096×4096 or 2048×2048). The albedo map is the only channel fed through the diffusion SR model for initialization; roughness, metallic, and normal maps are bicubically upsampled. The resulting HR textures are fully relightable under arbitrary environment maps and camera viewpoints, unlike pixel-level SR outputs.

## Method

PBR-SR is a zero-shot, training-free pipeline that combines a pretrained diffusion-based SR model (DiffBIR) with differentiable Cook-Torrance PBR rendering. It initializes HR textures (albedo via DiffBIR, other channels via bicubic), then iteratively refines them over 2000 Adam steps by rendering the mesh from 4 randomly sampled viewpoints per batch, upscaling those renderings with DiffBIR to create pseudo-GT images, and minimizing a robust pixel-wise loss between differentiable renderings and pseudo-GTs. A key design is a learnable per-image 64×64 weighting map that adaptively downweights unreliable pixels (specular highlights, shadows, view-inconsistent regions) while a regularization term keeps weights near 1. PBR consistency constraints (L1 + SSIM between average-pooled HR textures and the LR input) and total variation regularization enforce material fidelity and spatial smoothness, ensuring the upscaled textures remain faithful to the original low-resolution input while gaining high-frequency detail from the 2D image prior.

## Results

On the Artist-designed Dataset (16 meshes, 4× SR), PBR-SR achieves the highest PSNR across all five metrics: 29.731 (Albedo), 31.602 (Roughness), 31.889 (Metallic), 29.088 (Normal), and 28.001 (Renderings). On the AI-generated Dataset (32 meshes, 512→2048), PBR-SR scores 33.841, 35.059, 35.352, 34.564, and 31.208 respectively, again leading all baselines. The method is zero-shot (no training data required) and uses DiffBIR as the underlying image SR prior with differentiable PBR rendering for iterative texture optimization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PBR-SR outperforms all eight baselines on every metric in both evaluation sets. Against the strongest supervised baseline CAMixerSR-FT (fine-tuned on 24K PBR pairs), PBR-SR gains +1.93 dB on Albedo (29.731 vs 27.800), +3.43 dB on Normal (29.088 vs 25.655), and +0.07 dB on Renderings (28.001 vs 27.928) on the Artist-designed set; on the AI-generated set the Normal gap widens to +3.51 dB (34.564 vs 31.054). Against the optimization-based Paint-it SR, PBR-SR leads by +4.04 dB on Renderings (28.001 vs 23.959) and +0.85 dB on Normal (29.088 vs 28.237), indicating that view-consistent PBR optimization with robust weighting is more effective than naive render-loss optimization. Among direct image SR methods, DiffBIR applied naively to texture maps scores 24.842 on Albedo, while PBR-SR (which uses DiffBIR as a rendering-space prior) reaches 29.731 — a +4.89 dB improvement — demonstrating the value of the differentiable rendering loop. Transformer-based methods (SwinIR, HAT, CAMixerSR) show competitive scores on individual channels (e.g., HAT reaches 30.559 on Roughness) but lack consistency across all PBR maps and underperform PBR-SR on Renderings by 1.8–4.2 dB.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the direct-application paradigm of running off-the-shelf image SR models (SwinIR, HAT, DiffBIR, etc.) on PBR texture channels, instead framing PBR texture SR as a differentiable-rendering optimization problem that distills 2D image priors into physically consistent material maps. It builds directly on DiffBIR's two-stage diffusion architecture as the SR prior and on NRD-style differentiable rasterization for the rendering loop, but the key conceptual departure is the iterative multi-view optimization with a robust per-pixel weighting mechanism and PBR-domain consistency constraints. In the mesh texture generation literature (Paint-it, Text2Tex, Decorate3D), SR appears only as a post-hoc upsampling step on generated textures; here, SR is the primary task, and the only prior mesh-texture-SR work [21] operates on baked appearance rather than separable PBR channels.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PBR-SR provides the first practical zero-shot pipeline for upgrading legacy or AI-generated 3D assets to high-resolution PBR textures without requiring paired training data or re-creating assets, directly enabling relighting and material-aware editing workflows in gaming and VFX. The main limitation is optimization speed (~30 min per mesh at 2K→8K on an A6000), which precludes real-time use, and the method degrades on severely corrupted inputs where natural image priors lack sufficient structural cues. Open questions include whether PBR-specific generative priors (rather than general image diffusion models) could further improve fidelity, whether the optimization can be accelerated via learned initialization or distillation, and how to integrate multimodal cues (text, sketches) for cases where LR textures are too degraded for pure image-prior recovery.
