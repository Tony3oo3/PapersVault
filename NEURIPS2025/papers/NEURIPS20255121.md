## Problem

Multimodal large language models (M-LLMs) have not been explored for image manipulation detection (IMD), and when applied directly they suffer from hallucinations and overthinking that make them unreliable for precise tampered-region localization. M-LLMs excel at recognizing high-level semantic anomalies but lack the ability to capture low-level statistical features (noise, texture, color inconsistencies) that are critical for IMD, and no existing dataset provides structured analysis instructions for supervised fine-tuning in this domain.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a free-form text prompt query as input. It produces three outputs simultaneously: a binary pixel-level segmentation mask of tampered regions (generated by a vision decoder), an autoregressive textual forgery analysis organized in a Chain-of-Clues structure (high-level semantic anomalies → middle-level visual defects → low-level pixel statistics), and a real/fake classification embedded in the text via special <REAL>/<FAKE> tokens. The segmentation mask is not binarized in qualitative visualizations to reveal confidence gradients, and the text output includes the [SEG] token that triggers the dense prediction head.

## Method

ForgerySleuth integrates LLaVA-7B-v1-1 as the M-LLM backbone with an independent trace encoder (ViT-B with constrained convolutions and residual connections for noise enhancement) and a frozen SAM ViT-H vision backbone. The M-LLM detects high-level semantic anomalies and emits a [SEG] token whose hidden embedding, after MLP projection, guides a three-layer fusion attention decoder that cross-attends between LLM tokens, trace embeddings, and image content embeddings to produce the segmentation mask. Only 5.47% of parameters are trainable via LoRA on the M-LLM, while the trace encoder and vision decoder are fully trained. Training follows a two-phase strategy: pre-training on the 50k-sample ForgeryAnalysis-PT dataset (auto-generated by a fine-tuned LLaVA data engine) followed by supervised fine-tuning on the 1,752 expert-revised ForgeryAnalysis-SFT samples, using a combined autoregressive cross-entropy loss for text and a BCE+DICE loss for the mask.

## Results

ForgerySleuth achieves the best pixel-level F1 across all five localization benchmarks under both optimal and fixed (0.5) thresholds, with the largest gains on the challenging NIST16 dataset (0.518 fixed F1 vs. 0.413 for UnionFormer, a +0.105 margin) and Coverage (0.684 vs. 0.592). On the threshold-independent AUC metric, ForgerySleuth reaches 0.992 on Columbia, 0.962 on Coverage, and 0.911 on IMD20, consistently outperforming UnionFormer. In the forgery analysis evaluation (GPT-4 scored, 1–10 scale), ForgerySleuth attains an overall score of 9.45 with 92.2% recall, surpassing GPT-4o (6.96, 68.8% recall) by 35.8% and the concurrent FakeShield (5.55, 52.8% recall) by 70.3%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest traditional IMD baseline UnionFormer, ForgerySleuth wins on every dataset under both threshold settings, with the most substantial margins on NIST16 (+0.105 fixed F1) and Coverage (+0.092 fixed F1), and a +0.051 AUC gain on IMD20. The concurrent M-LLM work FakeShield performs poorly on localization (e.g., 0.052 fixed F1 on Coverage vs. 0.684 for ForgerySleuth) due to its two-stage pipeline that misses many manipulations, and also trails in forgery analysis (overall 5.55 vs. 9.45 on GPT-4 scoring). Among general-purpose M-LLMs used as analysis baselines, GPT-4o achieves the highest STS (0.725 with mpnet-base-v2) and GPT-4 overall score (6.96) without fine-tuning, but ForgerySleuth surpasses it by a wide margin (STS 0.961, overall 9.45) after SFT on ForgeryAnalysis. LISA, even after LoRA fine-tuning on the same SFT data, remains far behind ForgerySleuth on both STS (0.401 vs. 0.961) and GPT-4 scoring (overall 1.92 pre-SFT), confirming the benefit of the dedicated trace encoder and fusion mechanism. On robustness (Table 3), ForgerySleuth maintains the highest AUC across all distortion types (resize, Gaussian blur, Gaussian noise, JPEG), with the gap over UnionFormer widening under stronger distortions (e.g., +0.008 AUC under GSN σ=15 vs. +0.017 under no distortion), indicating that high-level semantic reasoning provides resilience when low-level traces are degraded.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on LISA's [SEG]-token-to-vision-decoder architecture for dense prediction from M-LLMs, but departs from it by adding a dedicated trace encoder (with constrained convolutions) to capture low-level forensic features that the M-LLM's high-level semantic representations miss. The Chain-of-Clues prompting structure adapts Chain-of-Thought reasoning to the hierarchical nature of forensic evidence (high-level semantic anomalies → mid-level visual defects → low-level pixel statistics), drawing on the established IMD literature (Mantra-Net, TruFor, UnionFormer) for what constitutes reliable tampering evidence. The data engine approach of fine-tuning a smaller M-LLM to replicate GPT-4o's analysis capability for scalable dataset construction follows the broader trend of using LLMs as annotation engines, but is specifically tailored to the forensic domain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work demonstrates that M-LLMs can be made reliable for IMD by grounding their high-level reasoning in low-level trace features, addressing the hallucination and overthinking problems that make raw M-LLMs unsuitable for precise manipulation detection. The ForgeryAnalysis dataset (2,370 expert-verified samples + 50k auto-generated) fills a genuine gap: no prior IMD dataset included structured reasoning instructions for SFT. Key open questions remain: whether the 7B LLM + ViT-H SAM pipeline's computational cost is justified over a lighter trace-encoder-only approach for deployment, whether the GPT-4-based evaluation introduces circularity bias, and whether the method generalizes to video or 3D manipulation detection. The paper also acknowledges that small-region manipulations in complex scenes remain a significant failure mode.
