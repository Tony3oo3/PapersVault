## Problem

Existing competitive programming benchmarks (APPS, CodeContests, LiveCodeBench) target short-duration, pass/fail exact-solution contests where frontier LLMs are approaching saturation. The other major category—long-duration, score-based heuristic contests such as the AtCoder Heuristic Contests, where problems are NP-hard, admit no known exact solution, and require iterative refinement over hours to weeks—has no dedicated AI benchmark. ALE-Bench fills this gap as the first benchmark for evaluating AI systems on algorithmic score-based optimization tasks with open-ended, continuously improvable scores.

## Input / Output

The agent receives a Markdown problem statement (with optional images) describing a combinatorial optimization task and can interactively invoke test runs (receiving per-case scores, compilation/runtime errors, execution time, memory usage) and visualization tools (static Rust-generated images or an interactive web-based visualizer) within a timed session that mirrors the original contest duration. The output is a single final solution code submission in C++20, Python3, or Rust that undergoes one private evaluation on hidden test cases, yielding a score, a rank against the original human leaderboard, and an Elo-like performance metric (typically 0–3500+) that is directly comparable to human contestant ratings. Unlike pass/fail benchmarks, scoring is continuous and open-ended with no ceiling, so AI systems can keep improving beyond the best human scores.

## Method

ALE-Bench is a Python library wrapping 40 past AtCoder Heuristic Contest problems (10 in a 'lite' subset) with a Docker-based code sandbox replicating AtCoder's execution environment, a Session object enforcing contest time limits, and interfaces for public test runs, custom input generation, visualization, and a single final private-evaluation submission. For agent evaluation, the paper introduces ALE-Agent, a specialized scaffold combining two techniques: (1) domain-knowledge prompting that injects expert guidance on simulated annealing, beam search, neighborhood design, and evaluation-function crafting, and (2) a diversity-oriented best-first search with beam width 30 that generates multiple candidate refinements in parallel from each promising node, prioritized by a composite score of acceptance ratio and problem-specific score. All experiments run on standardized AWS C6i instances matching AtCoder's Intel Xeon Platinum 8375C CPU, enabling fair, reproducible comparison against the thousands of human contestants in the original contests.

## Results

In the one-shot setting, o3-high is the only model to surpass an average performance of 1000 (1044 overall), while the human average is 1260; no model reaches 2000 performance on any problem. In the iterative-refinement setting (4 hours of feedback-driven refinement), o4-mini-high achieves the best average performance of 1520 and a rating of 2104 (top 11.8% of humans), with all four tested models improving by over 400 points over one-shot. The full ALE-Agent (with domain-knowledge prompting and beam-search-based diversity) on the lite subset reaches an average performance of 1879 (short: 2285, long: 1474) and a rating of 2222 (top 8.6%), substantially outperforming both sequential refinement and OpenHands.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary method, ALE-Agent (with domain-knowledge prompting + beam-search diversity, beam width 30), achieves an average performance of 1879 on the lite subset, decisively outperforming OpenHands CodeActAgent (859–903 depending on model) and sequential Self-Refine-style refinement (1016–1411). Specifically, ALE-Agent with Gemini 2.5 Pro reaches 1879 vs. OpenHands with the same model at 903 (a ~1076-point gap) and vs. sequential refinement at 1198 (a ~681-point gap). Against human baselines, the best AI configuration (ALE-Agent, 1879) exceeds the human average performance of 1260, but the paper shows this is misleading: o4-mini-high's rating of 2104 (top 11.8%) overstates its consistency, as its ≥1600 performance rate (32.5%) is closer to humans rated 1600–1700 (33.9%) rather than its own rating band (56.2%). A controlled experiment comparing iterative refinement against 150 independent one-shot trials confirms that the performance gains come from genuine long-horizon refinement rather than brute-force parallel search, with a consistent ~300-point deficit for the parallel-search approach.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the competitive programming benchmark lineage (APPS, CodeContests, LiveCodeBench) from short-duration, pass/fail exact-solution tasks to long-horizon, score-based heuristic optimization contests drawn from the AtCoder Heuristic Contests. Conceptually, ALE-Agent builds on Self-Refine's iterative refinement loop but departs by injecting domain-specific algorithmic knowledge (simulated annealing, beam search) and a diversity-oriented best-first/beam search over LLM-generated candidates, moving beyond single-path sequential refinement. It is positioned as distinct from MLE-Bench (data-centric ML, GPU-dependent) and from ML-for-CO approaches (GNNs, RL) that train fixed models on predefined tasks rather than generating novel code from natural-language problem descriptions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ALE-Bench is the first standardized benchmark for evaluating AI on long-horizon, score-based algorithm engineering, a domain directly relevant to industrial optimization (routing, scheduling, production planning). The key open question it surfaces is whether frontier LLMs can achieve genuine long-horizon reasoning and continuous solution improvement comparable to human experts, or whether their apparent progress is an artifact of massive parallel search over mediocre ideas. The finding that iterative refinement outperforms 150 independent one-shot trials by 300+ points suggests the former matters, but the significant performance-distribution asymmetry (AI excels at simulated-annealing-suitable problems but lags on others) indicates substantial headroom. Limitations include the small problem set (40 contests) and the absence of real-time leaderboard information available to human contestants.
