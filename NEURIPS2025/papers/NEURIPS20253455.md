## Problem

SNNs consistently underperform ANNs, a gap traditionally attributed to information loss from binary spike activations. This paper challenges that assumption by revealing a previously overlooked frequency bias: spiking neurons inherently suppress high-frequency components and preferentially propagate low-frequency information, causing rapid dissipation of fine-grained local details that are critical for effective feature representation.

## Input / Output

The method accepts either static RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR, 224×224 for ImageNet) or asynchronous event streams from DVS sensors (128×128). Static images are converted to spike sequences by repeating frames over T timesteps and encoding pixel intensities through spiking neurons; event streams are binned into temporal frames. The network processes all information through discrete binary spikes over time and outputs a single class label (10, 100, or 1000 classes).

## Method

The paper first proves theoretically that the LIF neuron's charging process V[n] = βV[n-1] + (1-β)I[n] yields a first-order IIR low-pass transfer function H(z) = (1-β)/(1-βz⁻¹), and that cascading L layers amplifies this as ((1-β)/(1-βz⁻¹))^L, making spiking neurons network-level low-pass filters. Building on this, Max-Former is a 3-stage hierarchical Spiking Transformer that restores high-frequency signals through two lightweight operators: (1) extra Max-Pool in patch embedding (Embed-Max/Embed-Max+) to inject high-frequency content at the input, and (2) Depth-Wise Convolution (DWC-3/5/7) replacing self-attention in early stages to preserve local high-frequency details, while the final stage retains Spiking Self-Attention for global context. The architecture uses Membrane Shortcut for residual connections to maintain spike-driven compatibility. The same high-frequency restoration principle is extended to convolutional architectures via Max-ResNet, which adds only two max-pooling operations to MS-ResNet, achieving state-of-the-art CIFAR-10/100 accuracy with identical model size.

## Results

On ImageNet, Max-Former-10-768 (T=4) achieves 82.39% top-1 accuracy with 63.99M parameters and 14.87 mJ energy, surpassing Spikformer-8-768 (74.81%, 66.34M, 21.48 mJ) by +7.58% while using 30% less energy. On CIFAR-10/100, Max-Former (T=4, 6.57M params) reaches 97.04% and 82.65%, outperforming all spiking baselines. Max-ResNet-18 (T=4, 12.50M) further sets state-of-the-art among convolutional SNNs at 97.17% (CIFAR-10) and 83.06% (CIFAR-100), improving over MS-ResNet-18 by +2.25% and +6.65% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Max-Former decisively outperforms Spikformer [8] on ImageNet by +7.58% (82.39% vs. 74.81%) with fewer parameters (63.99M vs. 66.34M) and 30% lower energy (14.87 vs. 21.48 mJ). Against S-Transformer [41], Max-Former-10-768 gains +6.09% (82.39% vs. 76.30%) at comparable parameter count. On CIFAR-10/100, Max-Former beats S-Transformer by +1.44%/+4.25% and Spikformer by +1.53%/+4.44%. Compared to QKFormer [24] (pre-spike shortcut, T=1), Max-Former-10-768 (T=4) achieves +0.70% on ImageNet (82.39% vs. 81.69%); against the membrane-shortcut variant MS-QKFormer (T=1), the gap widens to +4.61%. On the smaller Max-10-384 model, Max-Former outperforms MS-QKFormer-10-384 by +1.34% (77.82% vs. 76.48%) with lower energy (4.89 vs. 5.52 mJ). Max-Former also surpasses SWformer [19] by +6.96% on ImageNet and Meta-Spikformer [45] by +5.19%. For convolutional architectures, Max-ResNet-18 outperforms MS-ResNet-18 [46] by +2.25%/+6.65% on CIFAR-10/100 and KDSNN-ResNet-18 [47] by +1.45%/+4.60%, establishing new SOTA among directly-trained convolutional SNNs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Spikformer's Spiking Self-Attention (SSA) mechanism and QKFormer's hierarchical 3-stage architecture, but departs fundamentally from the prevailing 'representation error' narrative that attributes SNN performance gaps to binary activation precision. Instead, it introduces a frequency-domain lens, proving that LIF neurons act as first-order IIR low-pass filters at the network level, and repurposes the MetaFormer token-mixing paradigm (Avg-Pool vs. Max-Pool) by showing SNNs exhibit the *opposite* preference to ANNs. The Max-ResNet extension further generalizes the insight beyond transformers to convolutional SNNs, building on MS-ResNet's membrane shortcut design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The low-pass filter characterization provides a unifying theoretical explanation for why multiple prior SNN architectures (Spikformer v2, S-Transformer) empirically required max-pooling or convolutional layers without understanding why, potentially redirecting future SNN design toward principled frequency management rather than ad-hoc architectural tweaks. The practical recipe (extra Max-Pool in patch embedding + DWC in early stages) is extremely simple yet yields +7.58% on ImageNet with 30% lower energy, making it immediately adoptable. Key limitations include the need for manual frequency-component balancing across stages and the lack of an automated or learned frequency restoration mechanism; the authors note that Fourier- or wavelet-based spike representations would be a natural next step but remain unsolved.
