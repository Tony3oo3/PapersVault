## Problem

Long reasoning models (LRMs) such as DeepSeek-R1 and QwQ scale test-time compute to produce extended chains of thought, yet they frequently abandon promising reasoning paths prematurely in favor of switching to alternative strategies. This 'underthinking' behavior causes incorrect responses to consume 225% more tokens than correct ones through 418% more frequent thought-switching, without improving accuracy on challenging problems. The paper identifies and quantifies this underexplored failure mode, showing that over 70% of incorrect responses contain at least one correct thought that was never pursued to completion.

## Input / Output

The method operates at the decoding level on text-based reasoning problems (mathematics, graduate-level science). Given a problem statement, the model generates a chain-of-thought reasoning sequence followed by a final answer; TIP modifies the per-token probability distribution during generation by subtracting a penalty α from the logits of designated thought-switching tokens (e.g., 'alternatively', 'messy') when they appear within β positions of a thought boundary. No changes to the model weights, prompt format, or output structure are required—the intervention is purely a logit adjustment applied during autoregressive sampling.

## Method

The paper introduces two complementary contributions. First, an underthinking metric ξ_UT that measures token efficiency in incorrect responses by computing the fraction of tokens generated after the first correct thought (identified via LLM-based assessment) that fail to contribute to a correct answer. Second, a decoding strategy called Thought Switching Penalty (TIP) that selectively penalizes thought-switching tokens only when they appear within a recent context window (β positions from a thought start), distinguishing unproductive rapid switching from deliberate strategic shifts like backtracking. The penalty modifies logits as ẑ_{t,v} = z_{t,v} − α for v ∈ V̂ when t < Ψ + β, reducing the probability of premature transitions and encouraging deeper exploration of each reasoning path. TIP requires no model fine-tuning and is tuned with a single (α, β) pair (α=3, β=600) that generalizes across models and benchmarks, yielding consistent accuracy gains (e.g., +5.8% Pass@1 on AIME2024 for QwQ-32B) and synergizing with best-of-N strategies like self-consistency and laconic decoding.

## Results

On AIME2024, TIP improves Pass@1 accuracy by 5.8% on QwQ-32B-Preview (38.3→44.1), 2.7% on R1-Distill-Qwen-32B (61.4→64.1), and 1.0% on DeepSeek-R1 (73.8→74.8), while reducing the underthinking (UT) score across all models. On MATH500-Hard, Pass@1 gains are smaller (83.1→83.7 for QwQ, 88.3→89.4 for R1-Distill-Qwen) but thought-switching tokens drop dramatically (e.g., 12.6→5.7 for QwQ). On GPQA Diamond, QwQ-32B-Preview improves from 57.6→59.1 Pass@1. The method requires no fine-tuning and uses fixed hyperparameters (α=3, β=600) tuned once on AIME 2022-23.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares TIP against two best-of-N sampling baselines: Self-Consistency (Wang et al., 2023) and Laconic Decoding (Raoof & Dimakis, 2025). TIP is not a replacement but a complement to both: when combined with Self-Consistency on AIME2024 (4 samples), QwQ-32B-Preview jumps from 43.7% to 51.4% accuracy (+7.7%), R1-Distill-Qwen from 67.0% to 69.9% (+2.9%), and DeepSeek-R1 from 79.3% to 81.3% (+2.0%). Similarly, combining TIP with Laconic Decoding yields gains of +3.3% (QwQ, 47.0→50.3), +4.3% (R1-Distill-Qwen, 71.1→75.4), and +1.7% (R1, 81.4→83.1) at 4 samples. The largest absolute gains appear on the hardest benchmark (AIME2024) and for weaker models (QwQ-32B-Preview), suggesting TIP is most beneficial when underthinking is most severe. UT scores also drop consistently in all TIP+baseline combinations, confirming that the accuracy gains come from reduced thought-switching rather than longer responses.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by identifying 'underthinking' as a distinct failure mode in long reasoning models, complementary to the recently studied 'overthinking' phenomenon (Chen et al., 2024). The proposed Thought Switching Penalty (TIP) decoding strategy repurposes the coverage penalty mechanism from neural machine translation (Tu et al., 2016; Wu et al., 2016) to penalize premature reasoning-path transitions, adapting a well-established NMT technique to the novel context of chain-of-thought reasoning. The paper positions itself against existing best-of-N sampling strategies (Self-Consistency, Laconic Decoding) not as a replacement but as a complementary decoding-level intervention that operates before answer selection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that over 70% of incorrect LRM responses contain at least one correct reasoning thought reframes the problem: models often understand the problem but fail to commit to a promising path, suggesting that reasoning depth (not breadth) is the bottleneck on hard problems. The TIP method's practicality—requiring no fine-tuning and only two hyperparameters—makes it immediately deployable, and its consistent gains across model families (QwQ, R1-Distill, DeepSeek-R1) and datasets suggest broad applicability. Open questions include whether underthinking is an artifact of RL training objectives that reward exploration, whether adaptive or learned penalties could replace the fixed-window approach, and whether the phenomenon persists as models scale further or as reasoning architectures evolve beyond linear CoT.
