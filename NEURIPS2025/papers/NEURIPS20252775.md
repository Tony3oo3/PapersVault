## Problem

Existing CAD reverse engineering methods either produce fully parametric outputs that lose fine-grained geometric detail (top-down) or preserve local geometry without full parametric editability (bottom-up). Critically, no prior approach recovers sketch-level geometric constraints (e.g., tangent, perpendicular, parallel), which are essential for making reconstructed CAD models behave correctly under designer modifications.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252775/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D mesh (from scanning or synthetic data) and samples N equally-spaced slicing planes along each axis, converting each cross-section into a 128×128 binary raster image. The output is a sequence of sketch-extrude steps: each step contains a constrained 2D parametric sketch (lines, arcs, circles with up to 12 constraint types) and an extrusion defined by direction, length, and type (new/cut), assembled into a fully parametric CAD model directly compatible with standard CAD software such as FreeCAD.

## Method

MiCADangelo mirrors the human reverse-engineering workflow in three stages. First, a ResNet34 encoder with a multi-head transformer encoder (4 layers, 4 heads, d=256) identifies key sketch planes from all sampled cross-sections, using contextual embeddings that encode slice index, axis, and normalization parameters to preserve spatial relationships. Second, each closed loop in a key slice is rasterized and passed through a shared CNN encoder and a transformer encoder-decoder that jointly predicts quantized parametric primitives and geometric constraints via separate heads, trained on SketchGraphs and fine-tuned on noise-augmented synthetic loops. Third, a differentiable extrusion optimization samples anchor points along each loop boundary, extrudes them along the plane normal with a shared learnable length per loop, and minimizes a point-to-vector Chamfer loss against mesh points plus an L2 regularization on extrusion lengths via 200 steps of AdamW gradient descent. The key novelty is the explicit prediction of sketch constraints from 2D loop images, which reduces the solution space and enables the reconstructed model to propagate edits correctly through geometric relationships.

## Results

On the DeepCAD test set, MiCADangelo achieves a median Chamfer Distance of 0.20, IoU of 80.6%, and Edge Chamfer Distance of 0.46, outperforming the closest competitor CAD-SIGNet (0.28 CD, 77.6% IoU, 0.74 ECD). On Fusion360, it reaches 0.48 CD, 68.7% IoU, and 2.66 ECD, again surpassing CAD-SIGNet (0.56 CD, 65.6% IoU, 4.14 ECD). On complex models with ≥4 loops, MiCADangelo achieves 0.37 CD and 68.3% IoU versus CAD-SIGNet's 1.34 CD and 49.2% IoU, demonstrating substantially better preservation of fine-grained geometric detail. For sketch parameterization on single-extrusion models, MiCADangelo reduces average Sketch Chamfer Distance to 0.283 compared to Davinci's 0.827.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MiCADangelo outperforms CAD-SIGNet [17], the strongest baseline, on median Chamfer Distance, IoU, and Edge Chamfer Distance across both DeepCAD and Fusion360, with the largest gains on ECD (0.46 vs. 0.74 on DeepCAD; 2.66 vs. 4.14 on Fusion360). However, CAD-SIGNet reports a lower Invalidity Ratio (0.9 vs. 2.6 on DeepCAD; 1.6 vs. 3.2 on Fusion360), though the authors note this is enabled by test-time sampling; without it, CAD-SIGNet's IR rises to 4.4 and 9.3. Against the bottom-up method Point2Cyl [8], MiCADangelo improves IoU by ~7 points on DeepCAD (80.6 vs. 73.8) and reduces CD by over 20×. CAD-Diffuser [9] and the original DeepCAD [6] are substantially outperformed on all shared metrics. For 2D sketch parameterization, MiCADangelo's fine-tuned network reduces average Sketch Chamfer Distance to 0.283 versus Davinci [37]'s 0.827, a ~3× improvement attributed to fine-tuning on synthetically generated noisy closed-loop images.

## Relation to Prior Work

MiCADangelo departs from both dominant paradigms in automated CAD reverse engineering—top-down sequence prediction (CAD-SIGNet, CAD-Diffuser) and bottom-up primitive fitting (Point2Cyl, ExtrudeNet)—by instead mimicking the human reverse-engineering workflow of slicing a scan into 2D cross-sections, parameterizing each loop as a constrained sketch, and optimizing extrusions. The constrained sketch parameterization network is architecturally inherited from the authors' prior DaVinci work [37], which established a single-stage CNN-Transformer pipeline for 2D sketch inference; here it is repurposed and fine-tuned on synthetic cross-section loops. The differentiable extrusion optimization, which jointly fits all extrusion lengths via point-to-vector distance, is a novel component not present in prior sketch-extrude reconstruction methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first CAD reverse engineering method to recover sketch-level constraints (coincident, tangent, perpendicular, etc.) alongside geometry, which is critical for parametric editability: the constraint-propagation experiment shows that without explicit constraints, small sketch edits cause large geometric distortions (ECD 20.4 vs 1.3), whereas constrained models preserve structural consistency. However, the method is limited to extrusion-only operations with axis-aligned directions and does not support B-splines or other CAD operations (revolution, fillet, cut), restricting its applicability to a subset of real CAD models. Open questions include whether the cross-section paradigm generalizes to non-axis-aligned extrusions, whether constraint learning can be extended to 3D-level constraints, and how the approach scales to models with many sequential operations.
