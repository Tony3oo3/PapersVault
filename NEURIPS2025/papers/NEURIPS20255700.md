## Problem

Existing activity regularization methods for spiking neural networks face a fundamental dilemma: directly penalizing spike activity conflicts with the task loss (which requires non-zero representations) and causes gradient vanishing that traps training at saddle points where all spikes are zero. This limits such methods to shallow networks or prevents them from achieving highly sparse models without severe accuracy degradation.

## Input / Output

The method accepts standard RGB images (32×32 CIFAR-10, 224×224 ImageNet/ImageNet-100) or asynchronous event streams (DVS-CIFAR10), which are encoded into binary spike trains over T timesteps (4–10 depending on dataset). The network outputs a class label, but the key design goal is to produce highly sparse spike trains with average firing rates as low as 0.02–0.06, where the AT-LIF neuron emits 0-θ scaled outputs rather than standard 0-1 spikes, enabling the threshold scaling to be absorbed into subsequent layer weights at inference for hardware compatibility.

## Method

The paper proposes the AT-LIF (Adaptive Threshold LIF) neuron, which combines two mechanisms: (1) a current-based output that scales the binary spike by the neuron's threshold θ (output ∈ {0, θ} instead of {0, 1}), preserving average output magnitude while reducing firing frequency; and (2) a learnable adaptive threshold that is regularized to minimize firing rate. Crucially, the training decouples optimization: synaptic weights are updated solely on the task loss, while thresholds are updated on both task loss and an activity regularization term, resolving the inherent conflict between representation learning and sparsity. A triangle-shaped surrogate gradient (bounded at 1) handles the non-differentiable Heaviside firing function. The paper proves that the saddle-point problem of vanilla activity regularization no longer applies under threshold adaptation, and empirically demonstrates that gradient norms remain healthy across layers, enabling stable training of highly sparse SNNs (e.g., 0.06 average firing rate, 2.22M parameters on ImageNet).

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (6 Conv, 2 FC), the composite AT-LIF method achieves 90.20% accuracy with an average firing rate of 0.020 and 16.88M synaptic operations (SOPs), reducing firing rate by ~91% over the unpruned baseline. On ImageNet (SEW-ResNet18), AT-LIF reaches 59.61% top-1 accuracy with 0.063 average firing rate, 2.22M parameters, and 310.53M SOPs. The method also demonstrates strong performance on ImageNet-100 (82.40% at 0.038 firing rate) and DVS-CIFAR10 (82.20% at 0.043 firing rate), consistently outperforming direct activation regularization at comparable sparsity levels.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against STDS (Chen et al., ICML 2022), AT-LIF achieves higher accuracy on both CIFAR-10 (90.20% vs. 89.40%) and ImageNet (59.61% vs. 58.90%) while dramatically reducing the average firing rate (0.020 vs. 0.059 on CIFAR-10; 0.063 vs. 0.189 on ImageNet). However, STDS retains fewer parameters (0.07M vs. 0.09M on CIFAR-10; 1.84M vs. 2.22M on ImageNet), reflecting its focus on weight sparsity. Against Unstru. (Shi et al., ICLR 2024), AT-LIF achieves substantially lower firing rates (0.020 vs. 0.049 on CIFAR-10; 0.063 vs. 0.106 on ImageNet) and higher ImageNet accuracy (59.61% vs. 59.23%), but Unstru. achieves lower SOPs on CIFAR-10 (8.49M vs. 16.88M) and fewer parameters (6.93M vs. 0.09M — though this reflects different pruning granularities). Overall, AT-LIF is not optimal on every individual metric but delivers the best balance between spike sparsity (lowest firing rate in all settings) and competitive accuracy, making it complementary to weight-pruning approaches rather than a strict replacement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the established SNN training paradigm of BPTT with surrogate gradients and the LIF neuron model, but departs from prior activity-pruning work (Neil et al., 2016; Yan et al., 2022; Deng et al., 2021) that directly regularizes spike output in the loss function. Rather than adding a penalty term that conflicts with the task objective, the authors modify the neuron model itself—introducing the AT-LIF neuron with adaptive thresholds and current-based (0-θ) output—to decouple weight learning (task loss) from sparsity control (threshold regularization). This positions the work as a neuron-level intervention complementary to, rather than competing with, existing weight-pruning frameworks like STDS (Chen et al., 2022) and unstructured pruning (Shi et al., 2024).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Activity sparsity is the primary efficiency lever for neuromorphic hardware deployment, where computation is event-driven and proportional to spike count; this paper provides a practical mechanism to push firing rates very low (0.020–0.063) while preserving accuracy, and demonstrates composability with weight pruning to reach 2.22M parameters on ImageNet. The theoretical identification of zero-spike saddle points (Theorem 1) clarifies why naive regularization fails, giving practitioners a principled reason to prefer neuron-model modifications over loss-level penalties. The main limitation acknowledged is that the work remains at the algorithmic level—no hardware implementation or energy measurements are provided—leaving open whether the theoretical SOP reductions translate into real energy savings on devices like Loihi or Tianjic.
