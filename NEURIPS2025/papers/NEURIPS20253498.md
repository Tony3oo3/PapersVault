## Problem

Existing SMC-based inference-time reward alignment methods for score-based generative models initialize particles from the standard Gaussian prior, which is agnostic to the reward function and fails to cover high-reward regions efficiently. As the diffusion coefficient vanishes toward t=0, the reward signal in the SMC proposal weakens, making the initial particle placement critically important. The paper addresses the challenge of sampling from the reward-aware posterior distribution in extremely high-dimensional latent spaces (e.g., 65,536 for FLUX), where conventional MCMC methods like MALA suffer from vanishing acceptance rates.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt and a differentiable reward function (mIoU against target bounding boxes for layout generation, negative smooth L1 loss against a target count for quantity-aware generation, or a predicted aesthetic score) and produces a single reward-aligned image. The reward is evaluated at intermediate denoising steps via Tweedie's formula, which provides a clean-data estimate from any noisy latent. The method operates in the latent space of FLUX (65,536 dimensions) or SANA-Sprint, with 25 denoising steps for SMC-based methods, and the total NFE budget is split equally between initial particle sampling and the SMC propagation stage.

## Method

Ψ-Sampler is a two-stage pipeline: (1) pCNL-based initial particle sampling, and (2) SMC-based reward-guided denoising. The key innovation is the use of the Preconditioned Crank-Nicolson Langevin (pCNL) algorithm to sample from the reward-aware posterior at t=1, defined as p₁*(x₁) ∝ p₁(x₁) exp(r(x_{0|1})/α). The pCNL update x' = ρx + √(1−ρ²)(z + √ε/2 · ∇r(x_{0|1})/α) uses a semi-implicit Crank-Nicolson discretization that inherits dimension-robustness from the pCN algorithm (designed for infinite-dimensional Hilbert spaces) while incorporating gradient-informed Langevin dynamics, enabling a fixed step size (0.5) regardless of dimension—unlike MALA, which requires step sizes scaling as O(d^{-1/3}). An MH correction guarantees convergence to the correct target. The resulting particles (selected via burn-in and thinning) then serve as initial particles for the TDS SMC framework, where particles are propagated through the denoising process with reward-guided Gaussian proposals and importance-weighted resampling.

## Results

Across three reward-alignment tasks (layout-to-image, quantity-aware, and aesthetic-preference generation), Ψ-Sampler consistently achieves the best performance on the primary reward metric: mIoU 0.467 (layout), negative smooth L1 loss 0.850 (quantity), and aesthetic score 7.012 (aesthetic). It also generalizes well to held-out reward models, e.g., improving held-out mIoU from 0.402 (TDS) to 0.471 and held-out counting MAE from 5.3 (TDS) to 2.925. On the general quality metric ImageReward, Ψ-Sampler leads in layout (1.035) and quantity (0.796) tasks, and is competitive in aesthetic (1.171 vs. DAS 1.166).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Ψ-Sampler outperforms the base SMC method TDS [14] on every primary reward metric: mIoU improves from 0.417 to 0.467 (+12%), quantity loss drops from 1.804 to 0.850 (−53%), and aesthetic score rises from 6.853 to 7.012. Against DAS [12], which adds a tempering strategy to SMC, Ψ-Sampler wins on all three primary rewards (0.467 vs. 0.363 mIoU; 0.850 vs. 1.151 count loss; 7.012 vs. 6.935 aesthetic), with the largest gap in layout-to-image. Among posterior-based initialization baselines, Ψ-Sampler (pCNL) clearly beats ULA and MALA, which suffer from near-zero acceptance rates or biased sampling in the 65,536-dimensional FLUX latent space; for example, ULA degrades mIoU to 0.370 and count loss to 3.035, while MALA achieves 0.401 and 1.601 respectively. The simpler Top-K-of-N baseline is the closest competitor, reaching 0.425 mIoU and 1.077 count loss, but Ψ-Sampler still leads by a meaningful margin (0.467 and 0.850). Single-particle methods DPS [39] and FreeDoM [40] are consistently the weakest, with mIoU around 0.17 and count loss above 14, confirming the advantage of multi-particle SMC over gradient-ascent on a single trajectory.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the SMC-based inference-time reward alignment framework of TDS [14] and DAS [12], which couple sequential Monte Carlo with the denoising process of score-based generative models, and on the stochastic optimal control formulation of Uehara et al. [20] that provides closed-form expressions for the optimal initial distribution and transition kernel. The key departure is that all prior SMC-based methods initialize particles from the standard Gaussian prior, which is agnostic to the reward function; this paper is the first to sample initial particles from the reward-aware posterior (Eq. 4) and the first to apply the preconditioned Crank-Nicolson Langevin (pCNL) algorithm—originally developed for infinite-dimensional Bayesian inverse problems [36, 37]—in the context of generative modeling. The pCNL choice is motivated by the extreme dimensionality of modern latent spaces (e.g., 65,536 for FLUX), where standard MALA's acceptance probability degenerates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a concrete bottleneck in SMC-based reward alignment: the vanishing diffusion coefficient g(t)² → 0 as t → 0 weakens the reward signal in the proposal, making initial particle quality the dominant factor rather than later-stage exploration. This reframing has practical implications for compute allocation in inference-time alignment pipelines. Limitations include the requirement for differentiable reward models and dependence on accurate Tweedie approximations at early denoising steps; the pCNL update is also restricted to t=1 where the prior is Gaussian, limiting its applicability to intermediate timesteps. Open questions include whether the approach extends to non-differentiable rewards, whether pCNL can be adapted for non-Gaussian priors at intermediate times, and whether the gains persist as base models become even higher-dimensional.
