## Problem

Existing tensorized incomplete multi-view clustering (TIMVC) methods rely on fixed-norm noise assumptions (ℓ₁, ℓ₂,₁, Frobenius) that cannot capture complex real-world noise patterns, model only global Euclidean structure while neglecting local manifold geometry, and use the biased tensor nuclear norm as a rank surrogate. These limitations collectively degrade tensor representation quality and clustering accuracy under high missing rates.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252420/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes incomplete multi-view feature matrices {X^v} where missing samples are encoded via a diagonal indicator matrix W^v, along with a user-specified cluster number c and anchor count t (typically 2c–3c). It produces final cluster assignments via k-means on the left singular vectors of the stacked coefficient matrices (1/√m)[(Z¹)ᵀ,…,(Zᵐ)ᵀ], alongside learned per-view affinity matrices and a low-rank tensor representation. The anchor-based formulation restricts the dictionary to t representative samples rather than all n, reducing computational cost while preserving expressiveness.

## Method

GUITAR formulates incomplete multi-view clustering as a tensor optimization problem with three novel regularizers solved via ADMM. First, a Gaussian Regression Norm (GRN) models reconstruction error as the negative log-likelihood under a view-specific multivariate Gaussian with learnable covariance Σ^v, replacing fixed-norm assumptions with adaptive noise characterization. Second, a Dual Manifold Regularization (DMR) imposes two complementary Laplacian constraints: DMR-1 enforces cross-view manifold alignment by regularizing each view's coefficient matrix against other views' Laplacians, while DMR-2 fuses all views into a consensus Laplacian for global manifold consistency. Third, a High-Preservation ℓ_δ-norm (HP ℓ_δ) replaces the standard ℓ_δ-norm by applying tanh to singular values, causing the penalty to saturate at π/(2δ+π) < 1 for large singular values while retaining near-ℓ_δ behavior for small ones, thus preserving dominant tensor components. The non-convex problem is solved with an ADMM scheme featuring closed-form updates for all variables (including a DC-programming-based proximal operator for HP ℓ_δ), with provable convergence to KKT stationary points.

## Results

At the standard missing rate of 0.5, GUITAR achieves ACC of 57.33% (Yale3), 80.00% (MSRC_v1), 35.75% (EYaleB10), 74.51% (COIL20MV), 82.31% (Mfeat), and 58.00% (Scene), outperforming the best baseline (SCSL) by 16.89%, 12.86%, 5.35%, 33.51%, 24.12%, and 15.98% respectively. The method also maintains strong performance at lower missing rates (0.1 and 0.3), e.g., reaching 77.14% ACC on MSRC_v1 and 74.11% on COIL20MV at 10% missing. Ablation studies confirm that the Gaussian Regression Norm (GRN) contributes the largest single gain, with dual manifold regularization (DMR-1 + DMR-2) providing further improvements on top.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GUITAR outperforms all five baselines across every dataset and missing rate on ACC and PUR. The strongest baseline is SCSL (Liu et al., AAAI 2024), a sample-level cross-view similarity learning method; GUITAR exceeds it by 12.86% ACC on MSRC_v1 (80.00 vs. 67.14) and by 33.51% on COIL20MV (74.51 vs. 40.69). The matrix-based methods IMVC-CBG (Wang et al., CVPR 2022) and PSIMVC-PG (Li et al., TNNLS 2022) degrade severely at 50% missing (e.g., 19.05% and 18.29% ACC on MSRC_v1), highlighting the advantage of tensor-based high-order modeling. The older baselines BSV (Wen et al., TCYB 2018) and PVC (Li et al., AAAI 2014) are consistently outperformed by 20–60% ACC across datasets. Notably, on the Scene dataset, SCSL achieves an anomalously high NMI (66.50) but very low ACC (19.57), suggesting a degenerate clustering; GUITAR provides a more balanced result (58.00 ACC, 30.02 NMI). The gap between GUITAR and the best baseline is largest on COIL20MV and Mfeat, where tensor high-order correlations are most informative.

## Relation to Prior Work

GUITAR extends the anchor-based tensorized multi-view clustering framework of Ji and Feng (TPAMI 2025) to the incomplete-data setting, and modifies the ℓ_δ-norm non-convex rank approximation of Kang et al. (ICDM 2015) into a High-Preservation variant. It departs from prior TIMVC methods (e.g., Wen et al. 2021, Zhang et al. 2023, EDISON 2024) in three targeted ways: replacing fixed-norm noise models with a learnable Gaussian regression norm, augmenting single consensus Laplacian regularization with a dual (view-specific + consensus) manifold constraint, and applying differentiated singular-value penalties via the HP ℓ_δ-norm. The overall architecture—anchor-based reconstruction, tensor stacking, ADMM optimization, and spectral clustering—follows the established TIMVC paradigm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Gaussian regression norm offers a more principled and adaptive noise model than fixed ℓ1/ℓ2,1/Frobenius norms, which is particularly relevant when missing data introduces heterogeneous reconstruction errors. The dual manifold regularization is a natural but useful refinement over single-consensus Laplacian approaches, and the HP ℓ_δ-norm provides a mild but consistent improvement over the standard ℓ_δ-norm in preserving dominant singular values. However, the cubic scaling with sample size (due to matrix inversion in the Z^v update) limits practical applicability to large-scale datasets, and the Gaussian noise assumption may not hold for all real-world corruption patterns. Open questions include whether the framework can be made sub-cubic (e.g., via sketching or randomized SVD) and whether the learnable covariance Σ^v generalizes beyond i.i.d. per-view noise.
