## Problem

Sparse autoencoders (SAEs) extract interpretable features from LLM activations but treat each feature in isolation, ignoring temporal dependencies between concepts, instantaneous structural constraints (e.g., mutual exclusivity), and offering no identifiability guarantees for the recovered features. Existing causal representation learning (CRL) methods provide such guarantees but rely on Jacobian estimation that scales poorly, limiting them to dozens or hundreds of latent variables—far below the thousands of concept features needed for LLM interpretability.

## Input / Output

The method consumes sequences of LLM layer activations (e.g., from Pythia-160m at 768, 3072, or 6144 dimensions) in their original temporal order, explicitly disabling the random shuffling used in standard SAE training pipelines to preserve token-to-token structure. It outputs a set of latent concept variables per token, a set of time-delayed causal relation matrices B_τ (for τ = 1 to 20, aggregated via max-pooling into a single matrix), and a strictly lower-triangular instantaneous causal relation matrix M, all of which are sparse and interpretable.

## Method

The framework models LLM activations as a linear mixing of latent variables governed by a vector autoregression with both time-delayed effects (B_τ) and instantaneous effects (M), trained end-to-end with three loss terms: reconstruction (linear autoencoder), independent noise estimation (L1 penalty enforcing Laplacian noise), and sparsity regularization (L1 on B_τ and M). The key scalability insight is that under linearity, the Jacobians required by prior CRL methods reduce to the parameter matrices themselves, eliminating the superlinear computational bottleneck. Identifiability is established via Yule-Walker-type recursions on autocovariance matrices combined with non-Gaussianity of the innovations, yielding recovery of latent variables up to signed permutation and scaling under sparsity constraints on M. The strictly lower-triangular constraint on M resolves the permutation indeterminacy, and the entire pipeline trains in linear time with dimensionality, scaling to 1024+ dimensions where prior CRL methods exhaust GPU memory.

## Results

On SAEBench (3072-dim features, Pythia-160m), the proposed method achieves a reconstruction loss of 0.0108, sparse probability of 0.6736, absorption of 0.0139, and autointerpretability of 0.6883, performing on par with standard ReLU and TopK SAEs. On the semi-synthetic relation recovery task, the method dramatically outperforms SAE+regression, achieving scores of 19.95 (Legal), 8.63 (XML), and 2.66 (Email) versus 0.54, 0.94, and 0.74 respectively. In scalability experiments, the linear model maintains a Mean Correlation Coefficient (MCC) of approximately 0.9 at dimensions up to 1024, while IDOL exhausts memory beyond 200 dimensions and iCITRIS fails beyond 16 dimensions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ReLU SAE, the proposed method achieves a marginally higher reconstruction loss (0.0108 vs. 0.0110) but improves absorption (0.0139 vs. 0.0141) and autointerpretability (0.6883 vs. 0.6791), indicating slightly better feature quality. Against TopK SAE, the proposed method has a slightly worse reconstruction loss (0.0108 vs. 0.0097) and lower sparse probability (0.6736 vs. 0.7141), but substantially outperforms on absorption (0.0139 vs. 0.0280) and autointerpretability (0.6883 vs. 0.6822). On the unique relation recovery task (Table 1), the proposed method achieves scores 37×, 9×, and 3.6× higher than SAE+regression on Legal, XML, and Email respectively, demonstrating that standard SAEs with a post-hoc regression layer cannot capture the temporal and instantaneous causal structure that the joint framework recovers. Overall, the method matches or slightly exceeds standard SAEs on conventional reconstruction metrics while uniquely providing interpretable causal relations between features.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of sparse autoencoder (SAE) interpretability for LLMs (Cunningham et al. 2023; Templeton et al. 2024) and causal representation learning (CRL) for sequential data (Li et al. 2025/IDOL; Lippe et al. 2023/iCITRIS). It departs from SAEs by adding explicit temporal and instantaneous causal structure with identifiability guarantees, and departs from prior CRL by replacing neural-network-based Jacobian estimation with a linear vector autoregression formulation, which is the key scalability enabler. The identifiability theory extends Zhang & Hyvärinen (2011) to accommodate an instantaneous mixing matrix M, yielding new indeterminacy characterizations (Theorem 1) and sparsity-based corollaries for component-wise and subspace identifiability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first theoretically-grounded, scalable framework for recovering causal structure among LLM concept features, addressing a gap where SAEs lack guarantees and CRL methods cannot handle thousands of dimensions. However, all real-model experiments use only Pythia-160m (160M parameters), leaving open whether the linear assumption and identifiability conditions hold for larger, more nonlinear models. The linear approximation is a significant simplification that may miss important nonlinear interactions in attention and feed-forward layers. Future work on cross-layer concept transformations, larger models, and leveraging the recovered causal graphs for targeted interventions would substantially strengthen the practical impact.
