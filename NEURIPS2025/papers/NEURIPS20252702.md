## Problem

Existing endoscopic datasets are fundamentally incomplete for quantitative polyp measurement: 2D segmentation datasets (Kvasir-SEG, CVC-ColonDB) lack depth and real-world scale information, while 3D reconstruction datasets (SimCol3D, EndoMapper) lack instance-level polyp size annotations. No public resource integrates synchronized appearance, 3D structure, camera geometry, and verified millimeter-scale size labels needed to develop and benchmark depth-aware polyp sizing algorithms.

## Input / Output

The benchmark pipeline takes RGB endoscopic frames at varying resolutions (320×320 virtual, 640×480 physical, 1157×1006 clinical) along with calibrated camera intrinsics as input. It produces three coupled outputs: a binary polyp segmentation mask, a depth estimate (dense per-pixel metric depth for virtual data, sparse forceps-derived depth points for clinical data), and a final polyp size in millimeters computed by applying the local scale factor from depth to the mask's major axis. The dataset itself ships with all modalities pre-aligned per frame, including 6DoF camera poses for virtual sequences and forceps-annotated reference frames for clinical sequences.

## Method

PolypSense3D is constructed from three complementary sources: (1) a Unity-based virtual colon environment built on VR-Caps with 30 procedurally textured polyps (1.79–20.52 mm) embedded in CT-derived anatomy, yielding ~32k frames with pixel-perfect masks and dense 16-bit depth maps; (2) a 50 cm 3D-printed transverse colon phantom with 13 embedded polyps (4.00–14.89 mm) imaged via a calibrated veterinary endoscope; and (3) ~11k clinical frames from 127 patients annotated using a novel biopsy-forceps-assisted protocol. The clinical annotation protocol has two components: proximal comparison (fully opened 5 mm forceps placed adjacent to the polyp to derive a local mm/pixel scale applied to the SAM-assisted segmentation mask) and extended-forceps contact (forceps with 5 mm gradation markings extended to touch the polyp apex, with a pre-computed universal homography rectifying perspective distortion and a 2nd-order polynomial regression mapping rectified pixel coordinates to metric depth). Benchmarking evaluates SOTA segmentation models (MSNet, PraNet, SAM, MobileSAM, VM-UNet, ASPS, SAM2-UNet) and depth estimators (DAM-V1/V2, ZoeDepth) trained on virtual data and tested across all three domains, with end-to-end size MAE computed by combining predicted segmentation and depth outputs.

## Results

On the virtual (Unity) test set, Sam2-unet achieves the best segmentation (mDice 0.9399, mIoU 0.9010), while DAM V2-mini achieves the best depth estimation (RMSE 0.185e-3, δ₁ 0.997). On the clinical test set, SAM leads segmentation (mDice 0.7992) and DAM V2 leads depth estimation (RMSE 8.089, δ₁ 0.463). The headline end-to-end result is that the best fully automated size estimation pipeline achieves 0.95 mm MAE on clinical data (Seg(Pred)+Depth(Pred)), outperforming physician visual estimates (1.84 mm MAE), while the virtual-domain pipeline achieves 4.34 mm MAE with GT segmentation and predicted depth.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a dataset/benchmark paper, so the 'method' is the PolypSense3D benchmark itself, and the comparisons are among the 10 benchmarked models. For segmentation, Sam2-unet dominates on the virtual (mDice 0.9399) and physical (0.8983) subsets, but SAM overtakes it on the clinical subset (0.7992 vs. 0.7346), indicating that the general-purpose foundation model transfers better to real in-vivo imagery than the polyp-specific architectures. Lightweight models (MobileSAM, VM-UNet) collapse on clinical data (mDice 0.2728 and 0.0634 respectively), while MSNet and PraNet degrade more gracefully. For depth estimation, DAM V2-mini is best on the virtual set (RMSE 0.185e-3) but DAM V2 (full) is best on the clinical set (RMSE 8.089, δ₁ 0.463), showing that the larger model handles real-world noise better. The critical finding is that the fully automated size estimation pipeline (0.95 mm MAE on clinical) outperforms physician visual estimation (1.84 mm MAE), demonstrating the dataset's value in quantifying where automated methods already surpass human baselines and where domain gaps remain the primary bottleneck.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of two established but disconnected research threads: 2D polyp segmentation datasets (Kvasir-SEG, CVC-ColonDB, SUN-SEG) that provide pixel-level masks but no depth or physical scale, and 3D endoscopic perception datasets (SimCol3D, EndoMapper, EndoSLAM, VR-Caps) that offer geometric structure but lack instance-level polyp size annotations. The virtual simulation component is built directly on the VR-Caps Unity platform, extending it with calibrated polyp models and synchronized multi-modal capture. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of a biopsy-forceps-assisted in-vivo annotation protocol that yields verifiable millimeter-scale size labels and sparse metric depth from standard clinical video — a methodological contribution absent from all prior endoscopic datasets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Polyp size is a clinically critical metric (polyps >20mm carry ~15% malignancy risk vs. <2% for <10mm), yet no prior public resource enabled systematic benchmarking of depth-aware size estimation. The finding that the best automated pipeline (MAE 0.95mm) outperforms physician visual estimation (1.84mm) on clinical data is encouraging for clinical deployment, though the evidence is limited to a single-center cohort of 53 polyps. Key open questions include: whether the forceps-based sparse depth protocol generalizes across operators and institutions, how to close the pronounced virtual-to-clinical domain gap, and whether end-to-end architectures can bypass the error propagation observed in the cascaded segmentation→depth→size pipeline. The dataset's single-center origin and small clinical subset (100% polyps <10mm) limit immediate generalizability.
