## Problem

Current LLMs are not explicitly trained to express calibrated uncertainty, leading to overconfidence where incorrect answers are reported with high confidence. Existing calibration approaches either rely on prompt engineering with limited effectiveness or fine-tune on heuristically generated proxy confidence scores (e.g., group-level accuracy, multi-sample consistency, or model judgment), each introducing strong assumptions, computational overhead, or model bias. The paper asks whether LLMs can be naturally calibrated during training without any ground-truth confidence labels or proxy estimates.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251783/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a reasoning question as input and produces both an answer and a verbalized confidence score (e.g., 'Confidence: 80%') from a predefined discrete token set T_N = {0, 1, ..., N} representing uniformly spaced probabilities. During training, the key intermediate quantity is the model's probability distribution over these confidence tokens, extracted by selecting the relevant logits from the full vocabulary logit vector and applying softmax. The method supports both fine-grained (0–100%) and coarse (0–9) confidence scales, as well as linguistic expressions (high/medium/low) and implicit natural-language uncertainty at inference time.

## Method

ConfTuner introduces the tokenized Brier score ℓ(q, y) = Σᵢ qᵢ(y − i/N)², which penalizes the model's confidence token distribution q against the binary correctness indicator y, and proves it is a proper scoring rule for verbalized confidence (Theorem 1), meaning the risk-minimizing distribution places all mass on the token closest to the true conditional correctness probability. The training procedure has two steps: (1) extract the model's probability distribution over confidence tokens from its pre-softmax logits, and (2) fine-tune via LoRA (rank 8) to minimize the tokenized Brier score against ground-truth answer correctness. This requires only 2,000 training samples and ~4 minutes of training on 4 A40 GPUs, with no repeated sampling, no proxy confidence targets, and no additional architectural components—making it a drop-in replacement for standard fine-tuning that naturally incentivizes accurate verbalized confidence.

## Results

ConfTuner achieves up to 54.7% improvement in ECE and 14.4% improvement in AUROC over the best baseline (LACIE) across three base LLMs (LLaMA-3.1-8B, Qwen2.5-7B, Ministral-8B) and five reasoning datasets. For LLaMA, ConfTuner reduces average ECE from 0.2387 (LACIE) to 0.1082 and raises average AUROC from 0.6229 to 0.6740. The method also generalizes to black-box models, where ConfTuner (based on LLaMA) calibrates GPT-4o's responses with ECE 0.0970 vs. GPT-4o's self-assessed 0.1640, and requires only 2,000 training samples and 4 minutes of training time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ConfTuner outperforms all training-based baselines (LACIE and SaySelf) on both ECE and AUROC across all three base models and all five datasets; for LLaMA, it reduces average ECE by 54.7% (0.1082 vs. 0.2387 for LACIE) and improves average AUROC by 14.4% (0.6740 vs. 0.6229). Against the prompt-based Ensemble baseline (Xiong et al., ICLR 2024), ConfTuner achieves substantially lower ECE (e.g., 0.1082 vs. 0.2756 for LLaMA) while matching or exceeding its AUROC, and does so with a single forward pass rather than three sampled responses. For black-box calibration of GPT-4o (OpenAI, 2024), ConfTuner (trained on LLaMA) achieves ECE 0.0970 and AUROC 0.6275, beating GPT-4o's self-assessed confidence (ECE 0.1640, AUROC 0.5945) and the Ensemble method applied to GPT-4o (ECE 0.1500, AUROC 0.6410). Against the logit-based P(True) method (Kadavath et al., 2022), ConfTuner achieves dramatically lower ECE (0.1082 vs. 0.3427) with a marginal AUROC gain (0.6740 vs. 0.6690), confirming that verbalized confidence calibrated via proper scoring rules surpasses internal logit-based estimates.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the classical proper scoring rule framework (Brier 1950; Blasiok et al. NeurIPS 2023) from numeric classifier outputs to verbalized confidence tokens in LLMs, introducing the 'tokenized Brier score' as a loss that provably incentivizes correct confidence expression. It departs from prior verbalized-confidence work (Xiong et al. ICLR 2024, Lin et al. 2022, SaySelf EMNLP 2024, LACIE 2024) by eliminating the need for heuristically generated proxy confidence targets—group-level accuracy, multi-sample consistency, or model-judged labels—replacing them with a single-sample, ground-truth-correctness-only objective. The method is implemented via lightweight LoRA fine-tuning, positioning it as a drop-in replacement within existing fine-tuning pipelines rather than a new training paradigm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The clean theoretical guarantee (Theorem 1) combined with minimal practical overhead (4 minutes, 2,000 samples vs. 120 minutes and 9 million samples for SaySelf) makes principled verbalized calibration accessible to practitioners who previously had to rely on expensive sampling or biased proxy labels. The demonstrated downstream benefits—improved self-correction and more cost-effective model cascading—suggest calibrated confidence is not merely a diagnostic metric but a functional component of reliable LLM systems. Open questions remain: how far the fixed-token-set formulation generalizes to open-ended conversational uncertainty, whether the theoretical guarantee translates to well-calibrated behavior under distribution shift (the Qwen results are notably weaker than LLaMA/Ministral), and how data quality and optimization dynamics interact with the proper-scoring-rule objective in practice.
