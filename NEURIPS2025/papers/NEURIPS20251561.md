## Problem

In the online Pandora's Box problem with semi-bandit feedback, a gap exists between the Ω(√(nT)) lower bound and the best known Õ(Un√T) upper bound (where U can be as large as n). Additionally, in contextual settings where reward distributions shift over time, existing approaches achieve only Õ(nT^{5/6}) regret with poor dependence on the horizon T. This paper closes both gaps by achieving near-optimal regret in the non-contextual case and a √T-type bound in the contextual linear case.

## Input / Output

The learner receives n boxes with known costs c_i ∈ [0,1] and unknown reward distributions supported on [0,1]; in the contextual variant, each box also has a known d-dimensional context vector x_{t,i} with ‖x_{t,i}‖₂ ≤ 1 and an unknown parameter θ_i with ‖θ_i‖₂ ≤ 1. The algorithm outputs a threshold vector σ_t ∈ [0,1]^n that induces both an inspection order (descending threshold) and a stopping rule (halt when max observed reward exceeds the next box's threshold). Feedback is semi-bandit: only rewards of opened boxes are revealed, and the learner must adapt the threshold vector each round based on accumulated samples.

## Method

The core algorithm constructs optimistic distributions by adaptively shifting CDF mass using Bernstein-type confidence intervals (data-dependent, unlike the fixed-mass shifting of Agarwal et al. 2024), then computes thresholds from these optimistic distributions to ensure stochastic dominance. The key analytical insight decomposes the per-round regret into an integral of (F_{D_i}(z) − F_{Ê_{t,i}}(z)) · ∂R̃_i(σ_t;z)/∂z, then bounds it by splitting at σ_{t,i+1}: for large z, the Bernstein factor √(F(1−F)) is small; for small z, a sharp derivative bound (∂R̃_i/∂z ≤ ∏_{j<i} F_{D_j}(z)/Q_{t,i}) yields tighter control, eliminating the linear U factor. In the contextual linear setting, the algorithm estimates θ_i via ridge regression, constructs value-optimistic empirical distributions by debiasing past rewards with LCB and adding UCB for the current context, and bounds the mean-estimation error using the 1-Lipschitz property of R̃_i combined with the elliptical potential lemma, achieving Õ(nd√T) regret.

## Results

For the non-contextual Pandora's Box problem, the proposed algorithm achieves O~(√(nT)) regret, matching the known Ω(√(nT)) lower bound of Gatmiry et al. [2024] up to logarithmic factors. For the contextual linear Pandora's Box problem, the algorithm achieves O~(nd√T) regret, which improves upon the O~(nT^{5/6}) bound of Atsidakou et al. [2024] whenever d = o(T^{1/3}). The same techniques extend to the Prophet Inequality problem, yielding O~(√(nT)) regret in the non-contextual setting and O~(nd√T) in the contextual linear setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is against Agarwal et al. [2024], whose O~(Un√T) bound for non-contextual Pandora's Box carries a factor U (the utility magnitude, which can be as large as n), effectively giving O~(n^{3/2}√T) in the worst case. This paper removes the U factor entirely, achieving O~(√(nT)) by replacing the fixed-mass CDF shifting with an adaptive Bernstein-type confidence interval construction and a sharper derivative bound on the utility function. For the contextual linear setting, the comparison is against Atsidakou et al. [2024], whose reduction to linear-quadratic online regression yields O~(nT^{5/6}) regret; this paper's O~(nd√T) bound is strictly better whenever d = o(T^{1/3}), which holds for any fixed dimension as T grows. The non-contextual result matches the Ω(√(nT)) lower bound of Gatmiry et al. [2024] up to logarithmic factors, closing the gap that existed in prior work. For Prophet Inequality, the O~(√(nT)) bound improves upon Agarwal et al.'s O~(n√T), though a √n gap remains relative to the Ω(√T) lower bound.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the optimism-based framework of Agarwal et al. [2024] for semi-bandit Pandora's Box and Prophet Inequality, but replaces their fixed-mass CDF shifting with an adaptive, Bernstein-type confidence interval construction (itself inspired by Guo et al. [2019, 2021]) that yields a data-dependent optimistic distribution. The key analytical departure is avoiding the total-variation-distance bound (which incurs a linear U factor) in favor of a refined integral decomposition that exploits a sharp, problem-dependent bound on the derivative of the expected utility with respect to a single box's value. For the contextual linear setting, the paper departs from Atsidakou et al. [2024], who reduce the problem to linear-quadratic online regression and obtain Õ(nT^{5/6}) regret, by instead maintaining per-box ridge-regression estimates and constructing value-optimistic empirical distributions that preserve the non-contextual analysis structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The non-contextual result closes the gap to the Ω(√(nT)) lower bound of Gatmiry et al. [2024] up to logarithmic factors, settling the minimax regret for online Pandora's Box under semi-bandit feedback. The contextual linear result improves the time dependence from T^{5/6} to √T, a meaningful gain whenever d = o(T^{1/3}). Open questions include whether the √n gap for Prophet Inequality (upper Õ(√(nT)) vs. lower Ω(√T)) can be closed, whether the linear n-dependence in the contextual case is truly necessary, and whether the d-dependence is improvable. The paper is purely theoretical with no experiments, so its impact is on the analytical toolkit for sequential decision-making under partial feedback rather than on deployed systems.
