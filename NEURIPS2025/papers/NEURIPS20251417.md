## Problem

Existing methods for responsible text-to-image generation (fairness and safety) typically improve one objective at the expense of semantic fidelity and image quality, producing outputs that are either biased or degraded. The paper addresses the gap of simultaneously enforcing responsible concepts (e.g., gender/race fairness, content safety) while preserving the original diffusion model's generative trajectory and visual quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251417/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a neutral text prompt (e.g., 'a person' for fairness categories, 'a scene' for safety) and a frozen pre-trained diffusion model (SD v1.4 or SDXL) as input, and learns lightweight constant-function transformations added to the UNet's bottleneck (h-space) activations. At inference, it produces images steered toward a target concept (e.g., 'a woman', 'a black person') or free of unsafe content, while remaining semantically aligned with the neutral prompt. Training requires only 5000 iterations (fairness) or 1500 iterations (safety) with batch size 1 on a single GPU, with no profession-specific or domain-specific data.

## Method

RespoDiff introduces a dual-module transformation on the intermediate bottleneck representations of the diffusion UNet: a Responsible Concept Alignment Module (RAM) that steers latents toward a target concept, and a Semantic Alignment Module (SAM) that preserves alignment with the original model's trajectory. Both modules are trained via a novel score-matching objective: RAM minimizes the MSE between the neutral latent's UNet prediction (under the RAM-transformed model) and the target concept's UNet prediction, while SAM minimizes the MSE between the full dual-transformed model's prediction and the original model's prediction on the same neutral latent. The neutral denoised latent, obtained by reverse diffusion through the current model, serves as a stable reference point that provides explicit directional guidance rather than relying on implicit noise reconstruction. Training alternates between updating RAM (minimizing L_resp) and SAM (minimizing λ·L_sem), and at inference, fairness is achieved by randomly sampling a concept transformation while safety is achieved by summing all anti-concept transformations.

## Results

On the Winobias benchmark with SD v1.4, RespoDiff achieves a deviation ratio (DevRat) of 0.14 for gender and 0.16 for race, compared to 0.68 and 0.56 for the unmodified SD baseline, representing a ~20% improvement in fairness. For safety on the I2P benchmark, RespoDiff reduces the inappropriateness score from 0.27 (SD) to 0.16, outperforming the dedicated safety method SLD (0.20). Image quality is preserved: FID remains at 14.91 (gender) and 12.82 (race) versus SD's 14.09, and CLIP scores stay within 0.7 points of the baseline. On SDXL, RespoDiff reduces gender DevRat from 0.72 to 0.26 and race DevRat from 0.57 to 0.23 while maintaining FID within ~1 point of the SDXL baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RespoDiff achieves the lowest DevRat across all fairness baselines: 0.14 vs. SDisc (0.17), FDF (0.40), BAct (0.57), and FD (0.31) for gender, and 0.16 vs. SDisc (0.23), FDF (0.32), BAct (0.45), and FD (0.50) for race. Against SDisc, the closest bottleneck-space competitor, RespoDiff improves FID dramatically (14.91 vs. 23.59 for gender; 12.82 vs. 17.47 for race) while also matching or exceeding WinoAlign and CLIP scores, indicating SDisc's noise-reconstruction approach degrades image quality. For safety, RespoDiff (I2P 0.16) outperforms SLD (0.20) and ESD (0.32) while maintaining CLIP alignment (31.10) above SLD (29.75), though ESD retains a lower FID (13.68 vs. 17.89). Compared to the unmodified SD/SDXL baselines, RespoDiff trades a small FID increase (~0.8–1.0) and a minor CLIP drop (~0.3–0.7) for a 4–5× reduction in bias, a favorable trade-off. On SDXL, RespoDiff reduces gender DevRat from 0.72 to 0.26 and race DevRat from 0.57 to 0.23, confirming scalability to larger models without architecture-specific modifications.

## Relation to Prior Work

RespoDiff extends the line of work on bottleneck-space interventions in diffusion models initiated by Kwon et al. (2023) and Li et al. (2024, SDisc). While SDisc learns concept directions via indirect noise reconstruction from pre-generated target images, RespoDiff replaces this with an explicit score-matching objective that directly aligns the neutral and target diffusion trajectories using the neutral denoised latent as a stable reference. The dual-module decomposition (Responsible Concept Alignment + Semantic Alignment) departs from prior single-transformation approaches by separating the steering objective from the fidelity-preserving objective, enabling independent optimization of each. The score-matching formulation draws on the SDE-based score function framework of Song et al. (2021b) to provide a principled gradient signal for trajectory alignment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RespoDiff offers a practical, lightweight path to fair and safe T2I generation that generalizes to unseen professions without profession-specific training data, a key limitation of fine-tuning-based methods like FDF. Its seamless integration into SDXL demonstrates scalability to production-grade models, making it relevant for real-world deployment. However, the reliance on binary gender/race categories and a fixed set of predefined concepts limits its applicability to intersectional or emergent identities, and the composition of multiple concept modules shows measurable interference that requires manual scaling. Open questions include automatic discovery of new bias categories, dynamic composition of multi-attribute transformations, and whether the neutral-prompt assumption generalizes beyond the tested alternatives.
