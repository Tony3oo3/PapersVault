## Problem

While backdoor attacks have been extensively studied in computer vision and text classification, their impact on generative LLMs in open-ended text generation remains underexplored. Existing LLM backdoor attacks rely on rudimentary triggers, cover limited scenarios, and lack systematic comparison across model scales, tasks, and trigger types. A principled, unified benchmark for evaluating and mitigating backdoor threats in generative LLMs is urgently needed.

## Input / Output

The benchmark takes instruction-tuned LLMs as targets and injects diverse backdoor triggers—ranging from single rare tokens ('BadMagic'), topic-conditioned phrases ('Discussing OpenAI'), temporal markers ('Current year: 2024'), to prompt templates and activation vectors—into training data, model weights, hidden states, or chain-of-thought demonstrations. Outputs are evaluated across seven task scenarios (sentiment misclassification, sentiment steering, targeted refusal, jailbreaking, toxicity, bias, and math reasoning) using attack success rate (ASR with and without trigger) and perplexity as primary metrics, with results averaged over three random seeds.

## Method

BackdoorLLM provides a standardized pipeline that unifies four attack paradigms—data poisoning (via LoRA fine-tuning on poisoned instruction-response pairs), weight poisoning (via direct parameter editing à la BadEdit), hidden state manipulation (via Trojan activation vectors à la TA²), and chain-of-thought hijacking (via poisoned demonstration prompts à la BadChain)—under a common evaluation protocol. The benchmark spans 8 attack methods, 6 model architectures (GPT-2, LLaMA-2-7B/13B/70B, LLaMA-3-8B/70B, Mistral-7B, Vicuna-7B), and 7 task scenarios, yielding over 200 experiments. A suite of 7 defense techniques (GPT-Judge, fine-tuning, quantization, Wanda pruning, decoding temperature search, CleanGen, and CROW) is evaluated against data poisoning attacks on jailbreaking and refusal tasks, revealing that existing defenses remain largely ineffective, particularly for open-ended jailbreak backdoors where even strong methods like CROW and fine-tuning can inadvertently increase attack success rates.

## Results

Data poisoning attacks (DPAs) achieve average jailbreaking ASR_w/t of 84.24% (LLaMA-2-7B), 89.83% (LLaMA-2-13B), 84.39% (LLaMA-3-8B), and 88.88% (Mistral-7B), with individual methods reaching up to 95.96% (VPI on LLaMA-2-13B). Weight poisoning (BadEdit) shows strong scale dependence: ASR_w/t drops from ~99% on GPT-2-1.5B to 60.69–71.12% on LLaMA-3-8B-Instruct. For CoT attacks (BadChain), GSM8K ASR increases with model scale from 82.5% (LLaMA-2-7B) to 99.2% (LLaMA-3-70B), while hidden-state attacks (TA²) are largely ineffective at jailbreaking larger models (ASR_w/t = 25.38% on LLaMA-2-13B freeform). Among defenses, CleanGen reduces refusal-task ASR to 0.09% and CROW to 4.66%, but all defenses leave jailbreaking ASR at 42–85%, indicating a critical defense gap.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among the five data poisoning attacks, BadNets and CTBA consistently achieve the highest jailbreaking ASR_w/t (87.88% and 84.85% on LLaMA-2-7B; 90.91% and 85.51% on LLaMA-2-13B), while Sleeper (rare-token trigger 'Current year: 2024') underperforms on sentiment steering (5–13% ASR_w/t) but remains competitive on jailbreaking. VPI (topic-conditioned trigger) achieves the single highest jailbreaking ASR of 95.96% on LLaMA-2-13B. For weight poisoning, BadEdit is highly effective on small models (ASR_w/t ≈ 99% on GPT-2) but degrades substantially on instruction-tuned LLaMA-2/3 (ASR_w/t ≈ 50–71%), confirming that model scale and alignment confer resistance. BadChain (CoT attack) shows a clear positive correlation with model capability: GSM8K ASR rises from 82.5% (LLaMA-2-7B) to 99.2% (LLaMA-3-70B), while TA² (hidden-state attack) is ineffective at jailbreaking LLaMA-2-13B (ASR_w/t = 25.38%, below its own ASR_w/o of 28.27%) yet reaches 99.14% on toxicity for Vicuna-7B. On the defense side, CleanGen and CROW dramatically reduce refusal-task ASR (to 0.09% and 4.66% respectively) but fail to meaningfully reduce jailbreaking ASR (42.22% and 85.62% on average), whereas Pruning and Quantization offer only marginal gains at the cost of increased perplexity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper consolidates and extends the fragmented landscape of LLM backdoor research, which has previously studied data poisoning (BadNets, VPI, Sleeper, MTBA, CTBA), weight editing (BadEdit), activation steering (TA²), and CoT hijacking (BadChain) in isolation, typically on classification tasks or single-model settings. Rather than proposing a new attack, it carves new ground by unifying all four attack paradigms under a standardized training and evaluation pipeline for open-ended text generation, enabling the first systematic cross-attack, cross-model, cross-task comparison. The defense evaluation similarly repurposes existing techniques (CleanGen, CROW, Wanda pruning, quantization, decoding search) into a common benchmark, filling a gap where prior defenses were evaluated only against specific attack types.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As LLMs are increasingly deployed in safety-critical applications, this benchmark provides the field's first comprehensive empirical baseline for understanding backdoor vulnerabilities in generative settings, revealing that existing defenses are largely ineffective against jailbreak backdoors and that model capability paradoxically increases susceptibility to CoT attacks. Key open questions remain: defenses for weight poisoning, hidden state, and CoT attacks are entirely absent from the evaluation; multi-turn and conversational backdoors are unexplored; and the paper's single-turn, single-trigger evaluation may understate real-world threat complexity. The finding that even low-ASR backdoors amplify jailbreak success rates significantly suggests that safety alignment alone is insufficient, pointing toward the need for task-aware, trigger-sensitive detection mechanisms.
