## Problem

While spectral bias in the *sampling* process of trained diffusion models has been documented, the complementary question—how the generated distribution evolves *during training* and which eigenmodes of the data covariance are mastered first—remained analytically uncharacterized. No closed-form description existed for how architectural choices (depth, convolution, weight sharing) reshape the order and speed at which different frequency components of the data distribution are learned.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253207/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The theoretical framework takes a data distribution specified entirely by its mean μ and covariance Σ (via the Gaussian-equivalence principle for linear denoisers) and produces closed-form trajectories for both the denoiser weights and the generated covariance at every training time τ. Empirically, the method consumes 32×32 or 64×64 RGB images (FFHQ, AFHQ, MNIST, CIFAR-10) or synthetic high-dimensional Gaussians, trains a diffusion model via full-batch or stochastic DSM, and outputs the variance of generated samples projected onto the data's covariance eigenbasis at regular training checkpoints, from which emergence times and power-law exponents are extracted.

## Method

The paper establishes that full-batch DSM training of a linear denoiser at noise scale σ is exactly ridge regression with strength σ², then solves the resulting gradient-flow ODE in closed form on the covariance eigenbasis, yielding exponential (one-layer) or sigmoidal (two-layer) convergence of each mode with rate proportional to (σ² + λ_k). By integrating the probability-flow ODE under a commutativity condition (satisfied by aligned initialization or circulant convolution), the generated distribution remains Gaussian with per-mode variance given by an explicit formula involving the exponential-integral function. This exposes a universal inverse-variance spectral law: the first-passage time for mode k to reach its target variance scales as τ_k* ∝ λ_k^(−α) with α ≈ 1, so high-variance (coarse) structure converges orders of magnitude faster than fine detail. Extending to deep linear networks preserves the law with sigmoidal weight dynamics, while full-width circular convolution redirects the bias to Fourier space and multiplies all rates by N via weight sharing; local (patch) convolution instead couples Fourier modes through the patch covariance, qualitatively altering the bias. Empirical validation on MLP-based UNets confirms the spectral law on Gaussian and natural-image data, whereas convolutional UNets show near-simultaneous emergence of all eigenmodes (effective α ≈ 0), implicating local convolution and network width as the key factors that reshape learning dynamics.

## Results

The paper's central quantitative finding is the inverse-variance spectral law: the first-passage (emergence) time τ* for each eigenmode to reach its target variance scales as τ* ∝ λ_k^(−α) with α ≈ 1. In MLP-UNet experiments on Gaussian data, the fitted exponent is α = 1.08 (d = 256) and α_incr = 1.05 / α_decr = 1.13 (d = 512), closely matching the linear-theory prediction. On FFHQ32 with an MLP-UNet, the power-law exponents are −0.48 (R² = 0.97, N = 57) for increasing-variance modes and −0.35 (R² = 0.92, N = 2,914) for decreasing-variance modes, confirming the spectral ordering persists in nonlinear, deep, practical training. In stark contrast, a CNN-UNet on the same FFHQ32 dataset yields an effective exponent α ≈ 0, meaning all eigenmodes emerge near-simultaneously and spectral bias is essentially absent.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper, so there are no external method baselines in the traditional sense; the key comparison is between the paper's own closed-form predictions and empirical observations in practical (nonlinear, stochastic, finite-batch) training. The linear-theory prediction of α ≈ 1 is confirmed quantitatively in MLP-UNet on Gaussian data (α = 1.05–1.13) and qualitatively on natural images (exponents −0.35 to −0.48, attenuated but robust across MNIST, CIFAR-10, FFHQ32, AFHQ32). The most striking contrast is architectural: the same FFHQ32 dataset trained with an MLP-UNet exhibits clear spectral ordering (coarse structure first, fine detail last), while a CNN-UNet shows near-simultaneous emergence of all eigenmodes (α ≈ 0). Ablations on CNN channel width (ch = 4 vs. 128) reveal that narrower networks recover a stronger spectral ordering consistent with the patch-convolution theory, whereas wide networks (ch = 128) learn all modes almost instantaneously, suggesting the ratio of network channels to input channels governs the deviation from the linear-theory prediction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the spectral-bias literature from regression and kernel learning (Rahaman et al. 2019; Bordelon et al. 2020) and from deep linear network dynamics (Saxe et al. 2013; Gunasekar et al. 2018) into the diffusion-model training setting, where the key novelty is tracking the full generated distribution (not just weights) through the nested gradient-flow and probability-flow ODE dynamics. It builds on the observation that learned diffusion scores are well-approximated by a Gaussian linear score (Wang & Vastola 2023; Li et al. 2024), which justifies the linear-denoiser analysis, and departs from prior diffusion-learning theory (Shah et al. 2023; Kamb & Ganguli 2024) by targeting anisotropic covariance reconstruction rather than component-mean recovery or constrained optimal scores. The ridge-regression equivalence of the DSM objective (Sec. 4.1) is the conceptual bridge that lets classical results on exponential/sigmoidal mode convergence be imported directly.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The inverse-variance spectral law (τ* ∝ λ_k^{-1}) provides a mechanistic, quantitative explanation for the familiar 'wrong detail' artefacts in early-stopped diffusion samples, and the fact that it survives in deep nonlinear MLPs with shared noise-level parameters makes it a robust design principle. The more striking open question is why local convolution qualitatively destroys this ordering (α ≈ 0, near-simultaneous mode emergence), a phenomenon the paper attributes to frequency-coupling by small kernels but leaves without a complete analytic treatment of multi-channel convolutional U-Nets. A rigorous theory of how channel width, kernel size, and depth jointly reshape the learning spectrum in practical architectures remains the key gap, and the paper's ablation suggesting that high-channel inputs (e.g., 64–128-channel autoencoders) may restore the spectral law points toward a concrete direction for future work.
