## Problem

Automated theorem proving faces an exponentially growing search space where many candidate tactics are semantically equivalent or lead to execution errors (75–85% in their experiments), wasting computational resources. Existing search approaches do not filter tactics by their semantic effect on the proving environment, and no prior work learns how tactics transform the environment to enable diversity-aware pruning before execution.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252169/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Given a formal goal and N=64 candidate tactics (with their policy logits) from an underlying tactic generator (ReProver or InternLM2.5-Step-Prover), 3D-Prover produces a filtered subset of K tactics (K ∈ {8, 16, 32}) that are semantically diverse and high quality. The filtering operates purely on learned embeddings and predicted outcomes before any tactic is executed in the Lean environment, so the output is a pruned candidate set that feeds into the existing proof tree search (Best First Search or Critic Guided).

## Method

The approach has two components. First, a transition model (Encoder–Predictor–Decoder) is trained in a supervised fashion on ~500K synthetic transitions from a single proof attempt, learning unit-norm tactic embeddings that encode the predicted environment response (success/failure status, execution time, and output text). The Encoder is an encoder-only Transformer initialized from the base proving LLM, the Predictor is a small MLP estimating error probability and time, and the Decoder is an encoder-decoder Transformer reconstructing the environment output. Second, a k-Determinantal Point Process is constructed with these embeddings as diversity features and a composite quality score (combining tactic policy logits, predicted success probability, and predicted execution time, weighted by hyperparameters λ_s, λ_τ, and temperature θ). The k-DPP samples a fixed-size subset of K tactics that maximizes the volume spanned by their quality-scaled embeddings, thereby selecting tactics that are simultaneously high quality and semantically diverse. The entire filtering step adds only ~0.9% time overhead and ~7% memory overhead relative to the base tactic generator.

## Results

On miniF2F-test with ReProver, 3D-Prover achieves 24.4% Pass@1 at K=8 (vs. 22.4% Top-K and 19.0% Random), a +8.9% relative gain, and 28.2% at K=32 (+1.4%). On miniF2F-valid, the improvement is larger: 25.0% at K=8 (+15.2% over Top-K) and 29.1% at K=16 (+9.4%). With InternLM2.5-Step-Prover on miniF2F-test, 3D-Prover reaches 45.7% Pass@1 (K=8) vs. 44.3% Top-K (+3.2%), and 53.1% Pass@6 under Critic Guided search vs. 49.0% No Filtering (+8.4%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ReProver (Yang et al., 2023) as the No Filtering baseline (27.8% on miniF2F-test, 27.9% on miniF2F-valid), 3D-Prover achieves 28.2% and 28.7% respectively at K=32, and substantially outperforms it at K=8 (24.4% vs. 27.8% on test is actually below No Filtering, but the Top-K baseline at K=8 is only 22.4%, showing the filtering is most impactful when aggressive pruning is applied). The key comparison is against Top-K, which 3D-Prover consistently beats: +8.9% at K=8 on miniF2F-test and +15.2% at K=8 on miniF2F-valid. Against InternLM2.5-Step-Prover (Wu et al., 2024), 3D-Prover improves Pass@1 from 44.3% (Top-K) to 45.7% under BFS, and from 43.7% (No Filtering) to 45.7% under Critic Guided search. The gains grow with more attempts: Pass@6 under Critic Guided improves from 49.0% to 53.1% (+8.4%), indicating that the diversity introduced by 3D-Prover enables discovery of proofs that are missed in single-attempt settings. On the larger LeanDojo Novel Premises benchmark (2000 proofs), 3D-Prover finds 646 proofs vs. 626 (Top-K) and 622 (No Filtering), a +3.2% and +3.9% relative improvement respectively, and uniquely finds the two longest proofs (length 7).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the DPP framework (Kulesza 2012; Kulesza & Taskar 2011) for diverse subset selection, repurposing it from domains like video summarization and image retrieval to the problem of tactic selection in interactive theorem proving. It departs from prior search-augmentation work (Lample et al. 2022; Wang et al. 2023) that uses proof attempts to improve search but ignores node diversity, and from Yang et al. (2025) which selects diverse subgoals via simple text embeddings, by instead learning transition-aware tactic representations that capture predicted environment dynamics (error likelihood, execution time, output) and filtering tactics *before* execution. The approach is explicitly modular, designed to augment any underlying tactic generator (demonstrated with ReProver and InternLM2.5-Step-Prover) rather than replacing the generator itself.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that 75–85% of generated tactics result in execution errors, combined with the demonstration that semantically diverse (not merely syntactically diverse) tactic selection yields consistent proof-rate improvements, provides a practical and lightweight mechanism (~0.9% time overhead) to make proof search more efficient. The modular design means the technique can be dropped into any existing proving pipeline without retraining the base model. Key limitations include the restricted experimental scale (single RTX 4090 for training, limited Pass@k evaluations), evaluation only on Lean-based benchmarks, and the fact that improvements are modest (3–15% relative) and diminish as filtering becomes less aggressive. Open questions include whether the transition-model approach generalizes to other proof assistants, whether the DPP quality-diversity tradeoff can be further exploited for very deep proofs, and whether the imperfect error predictions (Table 1) could be leveraged more deliberately for exploration.
