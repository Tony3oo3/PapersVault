## Problem

Message Passing Neural Networks (MPNNs) suffer from oversmoothing (node representations become indistinguishable with depth) and oversquashing (exponential decay of information from distant nodes), which fundamentally limit their use in graph foundation models. Existing solutions adopt global approaches—such as graph rewriting guided by Ricci curvature or spectral gap manipulation—that may benefit some graph regions while harming others. The paper proves that increasing the spectral gap, the central quantity targeted by these global methods, actually causes gradient vanishing with respect to input features and accelerates oversmoothing, motivating a local alternative.

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard undirected graph with a binary adjacency matrix and a node feature matrix X ∈ R^{N×d}, and produces node representations for supervised classification. A key structural detail is that nodes are partitioned into interior and boundary sets for each subgraph, and the boundary coefficients (α, β, γ) are not fixed hyperparameters but are computed as 2-layer MLPs of each node's current state, making the message-passing behavior adaptive per node and per layer. The layer-wise computational complexity is O(|V|+|E|), matching standard MPNNs.

## Method

GBN (Graph Boundary conditioned message passing Neural network) interprets MPNNs as neural solvers of the heat equation on a Riemannian manifold and introduces a nonhomogeneous Robin boundary condition on submanifolds to locally adjust message passing. The Robin condition α_i·x_i + β_i·Σ(x_i − x_j) = γ_i on boundary nodes controls how much message flows through topological bottlenecks (mitigating oversquashing), while the nonhomogeneous term γ_i acts as an external source that prevents Dirichlet energy from decaying to zero (mitigating oversmoothing). The coupled system of interior and boundary node equations is solved via the Jacobi method, yielding a unified update rule X^{(k+1)} = σ(D^{-1}(V+U)φ^{(k+1)}(X^{(k)})) + D^{-1}Γ where an indicator function distinguishes boundary from interior nodes. Theoretically, the Jacobian ∂x_i^{(K)}/∂x_j is shown to be independent of hop distance K in the limit, guaranteeing no oversquashing, and the Dirichlet energy decays polynomially rather than exponentially, enabling networks deeper than 256 layers without performance degradation.

## Results

GBN achieves the best node classification accuracy on 6 out of 7 benchmark datasets, with headline results of 86.21% on WikiCS, 95.78% on CS, 89.83% on RomanEmpire, and 53.51% on Ratings. The only loss is on Computer (91.33% vs. BORF's 91.76%). Notably, GBN does not exhibit performance degradation even when the network depth exceeds 256 layers, and it consistently achieves the best mean squared error on long-range graph transfer tasks (line, ring, crossed-ring topologies) at hop distances up to 50.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GBN outperforms all 17 baselines on 6 of 7 datasets, with the sole exception being Computer where BORF (graph rewriting via Ricci curvature) edges ahead by 0.43 points (91.76 vs. 91.33). On heterophilic graphs, GBN leads by substantial margins: +10.18 over GCN on Texas (85.01 vs. 55.83), +1.44 over G²-GCN on Wisconsin (86.78 vs. 86.12), and +1.47 over Graph-Mamba on RomanEmpire (89.83 vs. 88.36). Among graph transformers, GBN surpasses the strongest (Spexphormer) by +1.68 on WikiCS and +2.29 on RomanEmpire, while VCR-Graphormer is competitive on Ratings (53.31) but trails GBN by 0.20. On homophilic graphs, GBN beats the best spectral method UniFilter by +0.53 on CS (95.78 vs. 95.25) and the best curvature-based method BORF by +0.32 on WikiCS (86.21 vs. 85.89). The ablation confirms that removing the nonhomogeneous boundary term γ or the Robin coefficient β degrades performance by 3–23 points depending on the dataset, validating that both the oversquashing and oversmoothing mechanisms are essential.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on GCN as the base message-passing architecture and departs from the dominant global-approach paradigm (graph rewriting via Ricci curvature, spectral gap manipulation, edge addition/deletion) by introducing a local approach grounded in Riemannian geometry. The key conceptual departure is the first introduction of boundary conditions (specifically the Robin condition) into MPNNs, reframing message passing as a neural solver of the heat equation on submanifolds with nonhomogeneous boundary constraints. This positions GBN as a structural-preserving alternative to methods like BORF and ProxyGap that modify the graph topology, and as a more principled generalization of virtual-node methods (which the paper identifies as a special case of homogeneous Neumann conditions).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical finding that increasing the spectral gap—the standard global remedy for oversquashing—provably causes gradient vanishing challenges a core assumption in the graph rewriting literature and motivates a fundamentally different design philosophy. Practically, GBN's O(|V|+|E|) per-layer complexity and demonstrated stability beyond 256 layers make it a viable candidate for deep graph foundation models, a direction the authors explicitly target. Open questions include how the boundary-node identification scales to very large or dynamic graphs, whether the 2-layer MLP parameterization of α, β, γ is sufficient for complex topologies, and whether the Riemannian boundary-condition framework extends naturally to heterogeneous or attributed-edge graphs.
