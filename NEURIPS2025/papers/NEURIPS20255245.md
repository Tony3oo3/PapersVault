## Problem

While unimodal foundation models are known to learn convergent representations, the specific form and ultimate target of this convergence remain unexplored. Existing approaches treat model output embeddings as final representations, suffering from structural myopia (ignoring inter-sample relations), limited expressiveness in cross-modal matching, and dimensional incompatibility across models. The paper asks: what is the concrete structure that these representations converge to, and can it be made explicit to enable training-free multimodal alignment?

## Input / Output

The method consumes frozen embeddings from pretrained unimodal encoders (e.g., ViT/ConvNeXt/DINOv2 for images, BERT/RoBERTa for text, wav2vec/WavLM/HuBERT for audio) and produces an n-dimensional relational distance vector per sample, where n is the dataset size. Unlike standard fixed-dimensional embeddings, the Indra representation's dimensionality equals the number of samples in the reference set, and it encodes each sample's angular distance to every other sample rather than its intrinsic features. For cross-modal retrieval, Indra vectors from two modalities are compared directly via CLIPScore or CLAPScore, yielding Top-k rankings without any learned projection.

## Method

The paper formalizes the Indra representation as the V-enriched Yoneda embedding of a sample within a Lawvere metric space (a category enriched over the cost category [0,∞] with ≥, 0, +). Concretely, each sample X_i is mapped to its covariant Hom-functor C(X_i, ·), which is the vector of angular distances d(X_i, X_j) = arccos(f(X_i)ᵀf(X_j)/(‖f(X_i)‖‖f(X_j)‖)) to all other samples. Theoretical guarantees establish that this representation is unique (under T0 separation), complete (via the Yoneda Lemma, it determines all admissible distance assignments), and structure-preserving (relational structure among samples is isomorphic to structure among their Indra representations). For cross-modal alignment, Indra representations from independently pretrained vision and language (or audio) models are computed over the same paired dataset and matched directly, requiring no alignment loss, fusion module, or retraining—only the pairwise angular distance matrix.

## Results

On CIFAR-100 under heavy Gaussian noise (σ=7.0), Indra representations with DINOv2 achieve 58.67% accuracy versus 40.16% for the original DINOv2 embeddings, a 18.5-point gain. On MS-COCO vision-language retrieval, Indra representations with ViT+RoBERTa improve Top-5 text-to-image CLIPScore from 0.486 to 1.048 (2.2×), and Top-50 from 4.853 to 9.702 (2.0×) over original embeddings. On TIMIT speech-language retrieval, Indra with wav2vec-base+RoBERTa boosts Top-5 audio-to-text CLAPScore from 0.072 to 0.578 (8×). Across all settings, stronger backbones (DINOv2, large audio models) yield larger absolute gains, and improvements are consistent across architectures.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares Indra representations against two fully aligned multimodal baselines: CLIP (Radford et al., ICML 2021) for vision-language and CLAP (Wu et al., ICASSP 2023) for speech-language. On MS-COCO, the best Indra configuration (ViT+RoBERTa) reaches Top-5 T→I CLIPScore of 1.048, a 2.2× improvement over the original embeddings (0.486), but still trails CLIP's 1.420 by roughly 26%. On TIMIT, the best Indra configuration (wav2vec-base+RoBERTa) achieves Top-5 A→T CLAPScore of 0.578 versus 0.072 for original embeddings (8× gain), yet remains well below CLAP's 1.836. In single-modality robustness experiments (CIFAR-10/100, Office-Home), Indra consistently outperforms the original embeddings of the same backbone under all noise levels (σ=3, 5, 7), with the largest gains on CIFAR-100 (e.g., +18.5 pts at σ=7 with DINOv2). The paper acknowledges the remaining gap to fully aligned models, attributing it to the training-free nature of the approach and the limited capacity of the unimodal encoders used.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the representation convergence literature (Huh et al., Khosla et al., Hosseini et al.) by asking not just *that* unimodal models converge, but *what* they converge to, formalizing the answer as a relational profile via the V-enriched Yoneda embedding over a Lawvere metric space. It also carves new ground relative to cross-modal alignment methods (CLIP, BLIP) by proposing a training-free, parameter-free construction that replaces learned alignment losses with a purely geometric distance matrix. The connection to Moschella et al.'s relative representations is acknowledged but the category-theoretic framing and the T₀-separation/uniqueness guarantees are novel contributions that distinguish this work from prior distance-based or graph-based approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a principled, training-free mechanism for bridging unimodal foundation models, which is practically attractive given the cost of cross-modal retraining. However, the O(n²) memory and compute cost limits direct applicability to large-scale settings, and the gap to fully aligned models (CLIP, CLAP) remains substantial, suggesting the relational profile captures only part of the alignment signal. Open questions include whether the shared relational structure is truly modality-agnostic or an artifact of overlapping training data, and whether Kan-extension-based extrapolation from partial distances can preserve the theoretical guarantees at scale.
