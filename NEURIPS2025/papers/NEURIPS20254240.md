## Problem

Existing neural combinatorial optimization (NCO) methods for vehicle routing are trained on instances with a fixed constraint value (e.g., vehicle capacity C=50) and exhibit severe performance degradation—up to 9× gap expansion—when applied to instances with tighter or looser constraints. No prior work systematically analyzed how constraint tightness affects NCO generalization or proposed a mechanism to handle the full spectrum from extremely tight to extremely loose constraints.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254240/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes VRP instances defined on graphs with node coordinates in a unit square, per-customer demands (uniformly drawn from {1,…,9}), and a vehicle capacity C that varies per instance during both training and inference. For CVRPTW, time windows [e_i, l_i] and service times s_i are additionally provided, with tightness controlled by a coefficient α. The output is a complete routing tour—a sequence of sub-tours each starting and ending at the depot—constructed node-by-node via greedy decoding. Unusually, the capacity value C is explicitly fed as a conditioning signal into the multi-expert gate mechanism, not merely used as a feasibility check.

## Method

The paper proposes two complementary modifications to existing NCO backbones (LEHD for CVRP, POMO for CVRPTW). First, Varying Constraint Tightness Training (VCT) samples each training instance's capacity independently and uniformly from [C_min, C_max] (e.g., [10, 500]), forcing the model to learn adaptive strategies across the full tightness spectrum rather than overfitting to a single value. Second, a Multi-Expert Module (MEM) is inserted after the decoder's stacked attention layers, consisting of a discrete one-hot gate that routes instances to one of m specialized expert layers based on which capacity interval their C falls into; each expert contains its own m_e stacked attention layers followed by a softmax probability head. The key insight motivating this design is that CVRP with very tight capacity structurally resembles OVRP (many short sub-tours), while CVRP with very loose capacity approaches TSP (one long tour), so fundamentally different routing strategies are required and a single monolithic policy cannot serve both regimes effectively.

## Results

On CVRP100 with varying capacities (C=10 to C=500), the proposed method (LEHD + VCT + MEM) achieves an average optimality gap of 1.86%, substantially outperforming the best existing NCO method's average of 5.13%. The method maintains gaps below 1% for all loose-constraint settings (C=200 to C=500), while the best baselines (LEHD, BQ) degrade to 4.18%–6.82% in the same range. On CVRPTW100 with varying time-window tightness (α=0.2 to 3.0), the method reduces the average gap from 19.58% (POMO) to 11.58% (POMO+VCT+MEM).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method (LEHD backbone + VCT + MEM) is compared against 10 baselines: HGS, AM, POMO, MDAM, BQ, LEHD, ELG, INViT, POMO-MTL, and MVMoE. It decisively wins on tight constraints (C=10: 1.54% vs. MDAM's 7.47%, a 4.8× improvement) and all loose constraints (C=200–500: 0.75%–1.49% vs. LEHD's 4.18%–6.82%, a 3–7× improvement). It slightly loses on the in-domain settings C=50 (3.82% vs. BQ's 3.25%) and C=100 (3.67% vs. BQ's 3.48%), where BQ was specifically trained. The average gap across all seven capacity settings drops from 5.13% (best existing) to 1.86%, a 64% relative reduction. On CVRPTW100, POMO+VCT+MEM outperforms standard POMO by 41% in average gap (11.58% vs. 19.58%), with the largest gains at extreme tightness (α=0.2: 9.13% vs. 14.95%) and extreme looseness (α=3.0: 10.37% vs. 33.56%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the dominant line of NCO methods for VRPs (POMO, LEHD, BQ, INViT, ELG, MVMoE) that all train and evaluate under a single fixed constraint value (e.g., C=50 for CVRP100). It departs from prior work on capacity variation (Manchanda et al. using meta-learning on C∈{10,30,40}; Zhou et al. on instance-level adaptation) by systematically studying the full tightness spectrum from extremely tight (C=10) to extremely loose (C=500) and revealing that the overfitting problem is far more severe than previously acknowledged. The multi-expert module draws conceptually on MVMoE's mixture-of-experts architecture but replaces the learnable soft gating with a simpler discrete switching mechanism based on capacity ranges, which empirically outperforms the Top-K MoE variant.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that all major NCO methods suffer 5–9× gap expansion when constraint tightness shifts even moderately from the training value is a fundamental diagnostic for the field, suggesting that many reported SOTA results are artifacts of in-domain evaluation. The proposed fix (uniform capacity sampling during training + discrete multi-expert routing) is simple and broadly applicable, demonstrated on CVRP, CVRPTW, and complex variants (VRPBTW, VRPBLTW). Open questions include whether adaptive (non-uniform) sampling could further improve performance, whether continuous generalization policies can replace the discrete switching, and whether the framework extends to other COPs like scheduling and bin packing. The paper is limited to 100-node instances and a single GPU, leaving scalability to larger problem sizes untested.
