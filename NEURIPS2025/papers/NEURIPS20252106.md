## Problem

Classifier-free guidance (CFG) is the backbone of state-of-the-art image generation, yet its underlying mechanism remains largely unknown. Prior theoretical analyses relied on oversimplified data assumptions (isotropic Gaussians, 1-D mixtures) that neglect the covariance structures critical to natural images, leaving it unclear how CFG actually reshapes the generation process. This paper asks two concrete questions: what is the specific failure mode of naive conditional sampling, and how does CFG correct it?

## Input / Output

The analysis operates on diffusion models (EDM-1 at 64×64 pixel space, EDM-2 at 512×512 latent space) that consume a noisy image x_t and a class condition c to produce a denoised estimate, with CFG applied as an additional guidance term during reverse sampling. The linear models under analysis are constructed from empirical means and covariances of CIFAR-10 (5,000 images per class) and ImageNet (50,000 generated samples per class for more accurate covariance estimation). The key intermediate quantities are the denoiser Jacobians (proportional to posterior covariances) and their eigendecompositions, which yield the contrastive principal components used to interpret the guidance.

## Method

The paper analyzes CFG within an optimal linear diffusion model, where the guidance term decomposes into three interpretable components: a mean-shift term that pushes samples toward the class-mean direction, a positive contrastive principal component (CPC) term that amplifies class-specific features (directions where conditional variance exceeds unconditional variance), and a negative CPC term that suppresses generic features prevalent in the unconditional data. Under a Common Principal Components assumption, a closed-form solution to the CFG-guided ODE is derived, showing that each principal component is scaled by a factor h(λ_c, λ_uc)^(γ/2) that enhances positive CPCs and suppresses negative ones. The authors then validate these linear-regime insights on nonlinear EDM models, demonstrating that at high-to-moderate noise levels linear CFG closely matches nonlinear CFG, while at low noise levels the CPC interpretation extends naturally through the denoiser's input-dependent Jacobian (posterior covariance), where adaptive CPCs selectively amplify class-specific details.

## Results

The paper's primary quantitative finding is the inter-class FID gap: naive conditional sampling yields an average inter-class FID of ~216 (across 10 ImageNet classes), which is *lower* than the training data's FID of 226.6, confirming that generated images are less distinguishable across classes than real images. Applying CFG (γ=4, 20-step Euler) raises the inter-class FID to 258.9, a ~20% increase that restores and exceeds the ground-truth class separation. The paper further shows that linear CFG closely matches nonlinear CFG at high-to-moderate noise levels (σ(t) ∈ (4, 80]), with the two diverging only in the low-noise nonlinear regime where CFG primarily refines local details rather than global structure.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is an analysis paper rather than a method paper, so it does not propose a new generator to benchmark against existing ones. Instead, it compares three internal settings: (i) naive conditional sampling vs. CFG-guided sampling, (ii) linear CFG vs. nonlinear (EDM) CFG, and (iii) individual CFG components (mean-shift, positive CPC, negative CPC) applied in isolation. Naive conditional sampling consistently underperforms the training data in inter-class FID (216 vs. 226.6), while CFG closes and exceeds this gap (258.9). In the linear regime, linear CFG produces visually near-identical results to nonlinear CFG, and the mean-shift component alone replicates most of CFG's effect on FD_DINOv2 scores; however, mean-shift degrades diversity at high γ, whereas CPC guidance remains effective over a broader γ range and can mitigate color-oversaturation artifacts. In the nonlinear (low-noise) regime, linear CFG diverges from actual CFG, but a positive-CPC-inspired guidance (Eq. 17) applied to the denoiser output qualitatively matches CFG's detail-sharpening effect, while a non-selective PC-enhancement baseline (Eq. 18) fails and produces oversaturated images.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the CFG framework (Ho & Salimans, 2021) and the EDM formulation (Karras et al., 2022), but its key analytical tool is the recently established result that deep diffusion denoisers are well-approximated by optimal linear denoisers over a broad noise range (Li et al., 2024; Wang & Vastola, 2024). It departs from prior theoretical analyses of CFG (Wu et al., 2024; Chidambaram et al., 2024), which relied on oversimplified data assumptions (isotropic Gaussians, 1-D compactly supported mixtures), by instead leveraging the covariance structure of real image data and the Contrastive PCA framework (Abid et al., 2018) to decompose the guidance into three interpretable components. The paper also connects to the posterior covariance interpretation of denoisers (Manor & Michaeli, 2024) to bridge the linear and nonlinear regimes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first mechanistic analysis of CFG that accounts for the covariance structure of natural images, revealing that the 'lack of class-specificity' in naive conditional sampling stems from non-distinct class covariances and that CFG corrects this via a clean three-term decomposition (mean-shift, positive CPC amplification, negative CPC suppression). The finding that mean-shift guidance dominates CFG in the linear regime is practically useful for understanding why high guidance strengths reduce diversity. Open questions include extending the analysis to Gaussian mixtures (only initial attempts in the appendix), fully characterizing the nonlinear regime where the locally-linear Jacobian approximation breaks down, and whether the identified class-specificity gap can be addressed through modified training objectives rather than post-hoc guidance.
