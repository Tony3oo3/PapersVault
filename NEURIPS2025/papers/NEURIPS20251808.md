## Problem

Neural networks learn entangled latent representations that mix task-relevant signals with redundant, task-irrelevant information, making them highly sensitive to perturbations on irrelevant input regions (e.g., image backgrounds). Existing approaches either rely on post-hoc interpretability, require external supervision or dedicated datasets for contrastive learning, or apply uniform compression without explicitly separating salient from non-salient features. H-SPLID addresses this gap by performing supervised, end-to-end decomposition of the latent space into salient and non-salient subspaces without external annotations or adversarial training.

## Input / Output

The method accepts standard images (RGB 3×224×224 for COCO, ISIC-2017, and ImageNet-derived datasets; grayscale 1×64×64 for the synthetic C-MNIST benchmark) and produces a k-class prediction via a linear head applied exclusively to the salient subspace. The encoder's m-dimensional latent vector is partitioned by a learnable diagonal mask into a low-dimensional salient component z_s (used for classification) and a non-salient component z_n (regularized to be label-independent). The mask is continuous during training (values in [0,1]) and can be thresholded to binary after convergence.

## Method

H-SPLID introduces a learnable diagonal mask M_s that partitions the encoder's latent space into salient and non-salient subspaces, with classification performed only on the salient part. Two complementary regularization mechanisms enforce the separation: (1) masked clustering losses that pull same-class representations together in the salient space while collapsing all representations to a global mean in the non-salient space, and (2) HSIC penalties that reduce statistical dependence between inputs and salient features (removing redundant information) and between labels and non-salient features (ensuring no class signal leaks into the non-salient space). Training uses alternating optimization: SGD updates network parameters with a fixed mask, then a closed-form solution updates the mask given fixed representations. The paper proves that the expected prediction deviation under bounded input perturbations is upper-bounded by the product of the salient subspace dimension and the HSIC between inputs and salient representations, formally linking robustness to representation compression.

## Results

On the COCO benchmark, H-SPLID achieves 57.5% accuracy under background-only PGD attacks (ε=3/255), outperforming the best competitor (43.0%) by 14.5 points, and 58.9% under full-image PGD (ε=2/255) versus 34.2% for the vanilla baseline. On ISIC-2017, H-SPLID attains the highest robustness across brightness (70.0%), defocus (68.4%), and occlusion (69.5%) corruptions applied to non-salient regions. On transfer benchmarks, H-SPLID reaches 76.7% on ImageNet-9, 64.5% on the Only-FG variant, 59.5% on MixedRand, and 60.6% on CounterAnimal-Counter, all leading or matching the best baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

H-SPLID outperforms all four baseline families across nearly every evaluation setting. Against Weight Decay (Krogh & Hertz, 1991), H-SPLID gains 4.1 points on IN-9 (76.7 vs. 72.6) and 6.2 points on CA-Counter (60.6 vs. 54.4). Against L1 regularization variants (Tibshirani, 1996), H-SPLID leads by 1.9 points on IN-9 over L1 Sparse Activations (76.7 vs. 74.8) and by 2.5 points on CA-Counter (60.6 vs. 58.1). Against Group-Lasso (Yuan & Lin, 2006), H-SPLID surpasses Group Lasso Activations by 1.4 points on IN-9 (76.7 vs. 75.3) and by 2.3 points on CA-Counter (60.6 vs. 58.3). Against HBaR (Wang et al., 2021), the closest competitor, H-SPLID wins by 3.1 points on MixedRand (59.5 vs. 53.8), 2.1 points on CA-Counter (60.6 vs. 58.5), and 1.2 points on IN-9 (76.7 vs. 73.6); the only near-tie is on CA-Common where L1 Sparse Activations edges out H-SPLID by 0.4 points (80.7 vs. 80.3). On the COCO adversarial benchmark (Table 2), H-SPLID's advantage over HBaR is most pronounced under background-targeted attacks, reaching 57.5% vs. 39.9% under PGD at ε=3/255.

## Relation to Prior Work

H-SPLID extends the HSIC information bottleneck framework (Ma et al., AAAI 2020; Wang et al., NeurIPS 2021) by decomposing the latent space into salient and non-salient subspaces with separate HSIC penalties, rather than applying a single bottleneck to the full representation. The mask-based latent partitioning draws on Miklautz et al. (IJCAI 2021), which introduced similar decomposition for clustering, but H-SPLID embeds this split directly into a supervised classifier's training loop. The paper explicitly departs from contrastive analysis methods that require external target/background datasets, and from adversarial training approaches, positioning itself as a purely supervised, end-to-end feature selection method that requires no saliency annotations or attack-specific tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

H-SPLID offers a practical, training-time mechanism for learning saliency-preserving representations without adversarial training or external supervision, which is valuable for settings where attack knowledge is unavailable or where robustness to diverse real-world corruptions (not just adversarial perturbations) is desired. The theoretical bound linking prediction sensitivity to salient subspace dimensionality and HSIC provides a formal justification for the decomposition, though the bound's tightness in practice remains unverified. Key limitations include the assumption that irrelevant information is present and separable, restriction to image data, and dependence on dataset diversity for effective salient/non-salient separation. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to non-visual modalities (graphs, text) and whether it can be integrated with self-supervised pretraining frameworks like I-JEPA.
