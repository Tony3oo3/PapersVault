## Problem

Multi-modal spatio-temporal prediction suffers from three intertwined gaps: prior multi-modal approaches either treat modalities as homogeneous (e.g., taxi vs. bicycle flows) or focus solely on temporal dynamics while neglecting spatial structure; causal relations in spatio-temporal systems are confounded by latent environmental variables that existing single-modal causal methods cannot disentangle; and Transformer-based predictors incur quadratic O(T²·N²) complexity that makes city-scale forecasting prohibitively slow. No existing framework jointly addresses heterogeneous cross-modal fusion, multi-modal causal confounding, and computational efficiency in a unified architecture.

## Input / Output

The model consumes three input streams: a spatio-temporal tensor X_st ∈ R^{T×N×d} (e.g., 12-step wind speed or traffic velocity over N grid/road nodes), event-related text aligned to the nearest temporal step via regex-parsed timestamps and duplicated N_st times to match spatial dimensionality, and geo/satellite images aligned jointly in time and space via 2D coordinate proximity. Text is encoded by a frozen BERT and images by a CNN before entering the fusion pipeline. The output is a future S-step spatio-temporal sequence Ŷ ∈ R^{S×N_st×d} (12-step prediction horizon), produced by an MLP decoder on top of the STED encoder.

## Method

E²-CSTP combines three modules. First, a cross-modal fusion stage projects spatio-temporal, BERT-encoded text, and CNN-encoded image features into a shared dimension, applies two Cross-Modal Attention blocks (spatio-temporal as query, text/image as key-value), and gates the concatenated result with a learned Fusion Gate to weight each modality's contribution. Second, a dual-branch causal inference design routes the raw spatio-temporal sequence through a main branch and the fused multi-modal features through an auxiliary branch; a DeepSHAP-derived adjacency matrix (exponentially smoothed every 5 epochs) defines spatial causal structure, and a backdoor-adjustment intervention (Eq. 11) subtracts the confounder-driven component from the auxiliary input so that ∂x̂/∂S → 0, blocking the backdoor path X_st ← S → Y_st. Third, the STED prediction module replaces quadratic self-attention with a hybrid of multi-layer GCN (spatial message passing, O(T·N²·d)) and Mamba selective state-space blocks (temporal modeling, O(T·N·d)), stacked in three alternating spatial-temporal layers with residual connections and LayerNorm, yielding overall O(B·T·N²·d) complexity and 17–56% runtime reduction over Transformer variants while improving MAE by up to 9.66%.

## Results

E²-CSTP achieves the best MAE across all four datasets: 2.43 (Terra), 3.56 (BjTT), 0.13 (GreenEarthNet), and 2.99 (BikeNYC), outperforming the strongest baseline (UniST) by 1.61%–9.66% in MAE. On GreenEarthNet, the MAPE improvement is particularly large (57.09% vs. 68.37% for CaPaint, a 16.5% relative reduction). In terms of efficiency, replacing the Transformer-based prediction module with the proposed GCN-Mamba STED module reduces per-epoch runtime by 17.37%–56.11% while simultaneously improving accuracy by 1.78%–5.45% on Terra.

## Baselines & Comparisons

E²-CSTP outperforms all nine baselines on every dataset and metric. The strongest competitor is UniST (a prompt-empowered foundation model), which E²-CSTP beats by 1.61% MAE on Terra (2.43 vs. 2.47), 1.66% on BjTT (3.56 vs. 3.62), 7.14% on GreenEarthNet (0.13 vs. 0.14), and 9.66% on BikeNYC (2.99 vs. 3.31). Among causality-based methods, CaPaint and NuwaDynamics trail by 2–5% MAE on most datasets, with the largest gap on GreenEarthNet MAPE (57.09% vs. 68.37% and 73.26%, respectively). Multi-modal baselines T3 and FNF are evaluated only on Terra and BjTT (where text is available); E²-CSTP surpasses them by 3.95% MAE on Terra and 2.20% on BjTT. Single-modal baselines (D2STGNN, ST-SSL, HimNet) are consistently the weakest, with MAE gaps of 5–25% depending on the dataset. On efficiency, E²-CSTP's GCN-Mamba STED module reduces per-epoch runtime by 17.37%–56.11% compared to Transformer alternatives (Informer, Autoformer, FEDformer, iTransformer) while also improving accuracy by 1.78%–5.45%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies three previously separate research threads: multi-modal spatio-temporal fusion (prior work like T3, FNF, and GPT4MTS handles either homogeneous modalities or temporal-only patterns), causal inference for spatio-temporal prediction (NuwaDynamics, CaPaint, and CauSTG address confounding but exclusively in single-modal settings), and efficient sequence modeling (Mamba's linear-complexity state-space approach has not been combined with GCN for joint spatio-temporal encoding). E2-CSTP carves new ground by being the first framework to simultaneously integrate heterogeneous modalities (spatio-temporal sequences, text, images) through cross-modal attention, apply backdoor-adjustment-style causal interventions to mitigate confounding bias, and replace quadratic Transformer encoding with a GCN-Mamba hybrid. The causal mechanism draws on Pearl's do-calculus and the backdoor criterion, while the efficiency design repurposes Mamba's selective state-space kernel—previously applied to 1D sequences—for per-node temporal modeling alongside graph-based spatial encoding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in demonstrating that multi-modal spatio-temporal prediction can be both more accurate and substantially cheaper than Transformer-based alternatives, which is critical for city-scale deployment where training times of several hours are prohibitive. However, the causal intervention mechanism (DeepSHAP-based adjacency updates every 5 epochs plus gradient-based confounder cancellation) remains somewhat heuristic and its contribution is difficult to fully isolate from the multi-modal fusion gains. The framework is limited to text and image as auxiliary modalities, leaving open whether the causal-disentanglement approach generalizes to richer sensor suites (LiDAR, audio, social signals). Additionally, the acknowledged slower per-epoch runtime on multi-modal data compared to the strongest single-modal baseline tempers the 'efficient' claim in the most relevant deployment scenario.
