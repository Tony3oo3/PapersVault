## Problem

GNN-based motion planners suffer from two key limitations: inaccurate graph construction (neural collision checkers incorrectly retain or remove edges, disrupting heuristic estimation) and limited structural reasoning (local neighborhood message passing cannot capture global topological information, degrading performance in weakly connected graphs). The paper addresses these by drawing on the human brain's two-stage Perception-Decision model, where visuospatial perception constructs safe spatial representations and semantic-episodic synergy supports decision-making under uncertainty.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a random geometric graph (k-NN graph on free configuration space) with a variable number of obstacles encoded as 2n-dimensional configurations, plus start and goal node indices. During training, N_S receives the full RGG and obstacle set to output per-edge collision probabilities (binary classification), while N_H receives the pruned safe graph G' and goal state to output per-node heuristic values. At inference, the pipeline produces a collision-free path as a sequence of edges, with selective sampling (bias probability β) controlling the RGG construction and shortcut retrieval (O(T²) window) post-processing the A* result to remove detours.

## Method

NeuroMP is a two-stage brain-inspired framework. Stage 1 (Perceptive Segment Selector) iteratively encodes obstacle information into node and edge features via cross-attention with GLU gating, then predicts edge collision probabilities through a three-layer MLP trained with BCE loss, producing a safer pruned graph G'. Stage 2 (Global Alignment Heuristic) enriches node representations by concatenating spatial features with spectral features derived from K-order propagation through the graph Laplacian (Graph Fourier Transform), followed by multi-scale max-pooling message passing. The key training innovation is Alignment Dual-Flow Learning (A2FL): a dual-channel strategy where the complete graph G provides global semantic context to the weakly connected G' while sparse connections in G' provide structural feedback back to G, enforced by a symmetric contrastive alignment loss on latent embeddings. N_H is jointly trained end-to-end with a differentiable A* module using L1 loss on the closed-list vector against an oracle Dijkstra path.

## Results

NeuroMP achieves success rates of 0.98–1.00 across five of six environments (dropping to 0.64 on the hardest Kuka3Arms task, matching BrainyMP). It reduces collision checks to 57–71% of GraphMP's across all environments (e.g., 392.33 vs. 645.51 on Kuka13; 1266.69 vs. 1875.29 on Kuka3Arms) and attains the lowest path cost on Kuka13 (9.32) and Kuka2Arms (10.33). Planning time is reduced by up to 2.88× over RRT* (Link8: 818.73 s vs. 1254.93 s) and by 10–25% over BrainyMP in most settings. With the GNN-Smoother post-processor, NeuroMP achieves the lowest path cost from Kuka13 through Kuka3Arms (8.57, 9.15, 10.98 respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the classical planners, NeuroMP dominates on reliability and efficiency: RRT* achieves only 0.41 SR on Link8 and 0.39 on Ur5, while BIT* and LazySP, though competitive on SR, require 2–3× more collision checks (e.g., BIT* uses 22,992 CK on Link8 vs. NeuroMP's 8,040). The CNN-based NEXT planner degrades sharply in high dimensions (SR 0.41 on Link8, 0.37 on Ur5) and incurs the highest planning times (19,495 s on Link8). Among GNN-based methods, NeuroMP consistently outperforms GNN-Explorer on all four metrics (e.g., CK 5,328 vs. 8,806 on Stick3; PT 248.7 s vs. 688.0 s). Compared to GraphMP, NeuroMP reduces collision checks by 29–43% across environments while matching or exceeding SR (e.g., 0.93 vs. 0.89 on Link8). Against the SOTA BrainyMP, NeuroMP achieves lower CK (392 vs. 407 on Kuka13; 1,267 vs. 1,575 on Kuka3Arms), lower PC (9.32 vs. 9.40 on Kuka13; 10.33 vs. 11.43 on Kuka2Arms), and faster planning (69.5 s vs. 74.8 s on Kuka13; 314.6 s vs. 406.9 s on Kuka3Arms), while matching SR in all environments.

## Relation to Prior Work

NeuroMP extends the GNN-on-RGG + differentiable A* framework established by GraphMP (NeurIPS 2023) and the differentiable A* formulation of Yonetani et al. (ICML 2021), replacing GraphMP's Neural Collision Checker with a Perceptive Segment Selector that uses cross-attention over obstacle features for more accurate edge filtering. Its key architectural departure is the Global Alignment Heuristic, which injects spectral features (via graph Fourier transform of the Laplacian) into node embeddings to capture global topological structure that local message passing in prior GNN planners (GNN-Explorer, BrainyMP) cannot represent. The Alignment Dual-Flow Learning strategy further distinguishes NeuroMP by jointly training on both a complete graph and a collision-filtered weak graph with a contrastive alignment loss, addressing the sparse-connectivity problem that degrades heuristic quality in pruned graphs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that incorporating global spectral structure and dual-channel training meaningfully improves planning efficiency (30–40% fewer collision checks) and path quality over the current SOTA (BrainyMP) across environments up to 21 DoF, suggesting that the structural-reasoning bottleneck in GNN-based planners is partially addressable without architectural overhauls. However, the method remains limited to static, offline planning with fixed obstacle configurations, and the absence of released code, error bars, and dynamic-environment evaluation limits its immediate practical impact. Open questions include whether spectral features scale efficiently to very large graphs, whether the A2FL strategy generalizes to online or multi-agent settings, and whether the brain-inspired framing yields further testable design principles beyond the two modules presented here.
