## Problem

Efficient PAC-learning of high-dimensional Bayesian network distributions has been limited to tree-structured models (via Chow-Liu) and polytrees in the realizable setting. No polynomial-time algorithm existed for agnostically learning Bayes nets on non-tree skeletons such as chordal graphs, despite their broad applicability in causal modeling and their status as a natural generalization of trees. This paper bridges the gap by connecting distribution learning to the well-studied problem of efficient counting and sampling of graph structures.

## Input / Output

The algorithm receives i.i.d. samples from an unknown distribution P* on [k]^n, a known undirected chordal graph G on n vertices, and parameters k (alphabet size), d (maximum indegree), and ε (error tolerance). It outputs either a single Bayes net (proper learner) or an efficiently-samplable mixture of Bayes nets (improper learner), all sharing skeleton G with indegree at most d. The skeleton is given but edge orientations are not; the algorithm must discover a valid acyclic orientation. The improper output is a mixture over exponentially many DAGs that can nevertheless be sampled in polynomial time.

## Method

The core technique embeds PAC-learning within an online learning framework (EWA or RWM) where each expert corresponds to a discretized Bayes net associated with an acyclic orientation of the skeleton. Conditional distributions at each node are pre-learned via add-one estimators on a separate sample batch, reducing the problem to a purely combinatorial weighted counting/sampling task over DAG structures. The key technical contribution is an efficient dynamic programming algorithm on the clique tree decomposition of the chordal graph that computes the RWM/EWA normalization constant (a weighted sum over all acyclic orientations) in polynomial time, exploiting the property that chordal graphs allow independent acyclicity checks per subtree. For tree-structured distributions, the matrix-tree theorem (Tutte's theorem) provides an alternative polynomial-time sampling mechanism, yielding a new algorithm that improves the sample complexity dependence on k from k^3 (Chow-Liu) to k^2, which is information-theoretically tight.

## Results

For agnostic learning of Bayes nets with a known chordal skeleton and bounded indegree d, the paper achieves sample complexity Õ(n⁴/ε⁴ + nk^{d+1}/ε) for improper learning and Õ(n³/(ε²δ²) + nk^{d+1}/ε) for proper learning, both with poly(n,k,1/ε,1/δ) running time. For tree-structured distributions with unknown skeleton, the paper improves the realizable sample complexity from Õ(nk³/ε) (Chow-Liu) to Õ(nk²/ε), which is sample-optimal up to polylog factors. For agnostic polytree learning with known skeleton, the paper achieves Õ(n⁴/ε⁴ + nk^{d+1}/ε) samples, answering an open question from prior work that only handled the realizable setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Chow-Liu algorithm [12], this paper improves the realizable sample complexity for tree-structured distributions from Õ(nk³/ε) to Õ(nk²/ε), achieving a quadratic improvement in the alphabet size k that matches the known Ω(nk²) lower bound. Against Choo et al. [24] for polytrees with known skeleton, this paper extends the guarantee from the realizable setting to the agnostic setting, resolving an open question left by that work. Against Abbeel et al. [1] for bounded-total-degree Bayes nets, this paper handles the more general case of bounded indegree with unbounded total degree (e.g., chordal graphs), and additionally provides agnostic learning guarantees and proper learning algorithms that [1] lacks. Against Brustle et al. [14] for bounded in-degree Bayes nets, this paper provides computationally efficient algorithms (poly(n,k,1/ε) time) where [14]'s approach is computationally infeasible due to exponential search over DAG structures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Chow-Liu line of work on tree-structured distribution learning [12] and the realizable polytree learner of Choo et al. [24] to the significantly broader class of chordal-structured Bayes nets with bounded indegree, in the agnostic setting. The key conceptual departure is the use of an online learning framework (EWA/RWM) where the exponentially many experts correspond to DAG structures, and the computational bottleneck is resolved by importing efficient counting and sampling algorithms from the graph theory literature (matrix-tree theorem for trees, dynamic programming over clique-tree decompositions for chordal graphs). This repurposing of graph-structure sampling as a subroutine within online learning is the paper's central methodological novelty, distinguishing it from prior score-based or constraint-based structure learning approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first efficient algorithm for agnostic PAC-learning of Bayes nets on non-tree skeletons without distributional assumptions, closing a gap that has persisted since Chow-Liu (1968) and the realizable polytree results of [24]. The sample complexity is near-optimal (nk^{d+1}/ε up to polylog factors), and the framework naturally yields a KL-contamination robustness interpretation. Key limitations include the requirement of a known skeleton and bounded indegree; open questions include extending to bounded-treewidth graphs (where the Tutte polynomial connection is suggestive but unresolved), searching over Markov equivalence classes rather than individual DAGs, and whether approximate sampling via Markov chains can replace the exact counting subroutines.
