## Problem

EMG decomposition into individual motor unit spike trains is foundational for motor neuroscience and neuroprosthetics, yet unlike spike sorting or EEG source localization, it lacks open, standardized benchmarks spanning diverse muscles, movement contexts, and noise conditions. Existing methods are evaluated on private data with heterogeneous preprocessing pipelines and inconsistent metrics, making it difficult to gauge real progress or identify failure modes across the growing ecosystem of decomposition algorithms.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255020/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The simulation stack accepts a user-defined JSON config specifying anatomical parameters (muscle type, electrode grid geometry), movement profiles (trapezoidal, triangular, sinusoid, ballistic), effort levels (5–80% MVC), and SNR (10–30 dB), and emits raw HDsEMG signals, ground-truth binary spike trains, kinematic variables, and a full provenance record. The decomposition pipelines consume raw HDsEMG (typically 64-electrode grids or 10×32 bracelets sampled at 10,240 Hz) and produce estimated MU spike trains alongside process metadata (runtime, hyperparameters). Evaluation outputs a report card with spike-matching metrics (F1, precision, recall, rate-of-agreement) when ground truth is available, and signal-based metrics (FVE, silhouette score, pulse-to-noise ratio) universally.

## Method

MUniverse is a modular, containerized suite with four components: (1) a simulation stack wrapping NeuroMotion that combines OpenSim-based muscle kinematics, Fuglevand-type motor neuron pool models, and conditional generative MUAP models, with Latin hypercube sampling over the parameter space to generate 10,000+ diverse recordings; (2) a uniform API to four decomposition algorithms—CBSS (FastICA with a tunable sparse contrast function and quasi-Newton fixed-point solver), SCD (CBSS backbone with particle-swarm-optimized higher-order cumulants and peel-off), a deep autoencoder (orthogonal encoder + tanh-shrink decoder trained with reconstruction and sparsity losses), and a linear upper bound computed from ground-truth MUAPs; (3) a standardized evaluation framework using cross-correlation-based spike matching (±1 ms tolerance, 30% common-spike threshold, linear sum assignment) and recursive peel-off FVE; and (4) BIDS-EMG data standardization with Croissant metadata for FAIR compliance. All external dependencies (NeuroMotion, SCD) are containerized to isolate environments and ensure cross-platform reproducibility.

## Results

Across 1,230 recordings on six datasets, all three decomposition pipelines (CBSS, SCD, AE) achieve median F1 scores above 0.9 when ground truth is available, confirming reliable spike train reconstruction. On the NeuroMotion Test dataset, CBSS yields the most units (11,198) while SCD achieves the highest median F1 (1.00) and best matched-source fraction (90.1%); the linear upper bound (UB) detects 18,040 sources but only matches 75.8%, revealing a substantial gap to theoretical optimum. On experimental data (Avrillon et al. 2024), SCD leads in 90th-percentile unit yield (69.5 vs. 52.7 for CBSS) and FVE (0.67 vs. 0.61), while CBSS dominates on simulated datasets. The deep autoencoder (AE) consistently underperforms, achieving only 1.0 median units on NeuroMotion Test versus 8.0 for CBSS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CBSS (FastICA-based, Negro et al. 2016) is the most robust general-purpose algorithm, achieving the highest unit yield on both simulated datasets (11,198 on NeuroMotion Test; 1,420 on Hybrid Tibialis) and the best median FVE on simulated data. SCD (Grison et al. 2025) outperforms CBSS in spike train accuracy (median F1 of 1.00 vs. 0.96 on NeuroMotion Test; 0.99 vs. 0.92 on Hybrid Tibialis), detects more low-amplitude units, and leads in 90th-percentile unit yield on all three experimental datasets (e.g., 69.5 vs. 52.7 on Avrillon et al. 2024). The deep autoencoder baseline (Mayer et al. 2023) is substantially outperformed by both CBSS and SCD across every metric and dataset, yielding only 1.0 median units on NeuroMotion Test versus 8.0 (CBSS) and 3.0 (SCD), and achieving the lowest FVE (0.04 median vs. 0.27 and 0.12). The linear upper bound (Klotz & Rohlén 2025) demonstrates that even with perfect knowledge of MUAP waveforms, only 75.8% of ground-truth units can be recovered on NeuroMotion Test, establishing a clear performance ceiling that no current method approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

MUniverse unifies the convolutive BSS decomposition framework established by Farina & Holobar (2016) and Negro et al. (2016) with the NeuroMotion simulation platform (Ma et al., 2024) into a single, reproducible benchmarking suite. Prior EMG decomposition work has been fragmented across private datasets, heterogeneous preprocessing pipelines, and inconsistent metrics, in stark contrast to the standardized benchmarking culture that drove progress in spike sorting (SpikeForest) and EEG source localization (BCI Competitions). The paper departs from this fragmented landscape by providing the first open, FAIR-compliant, BIDS-structured benchmark that spans synthetic, hybrid, and experimental HDsEMG recordings with a uniform API over multiple decomposition algorithms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals a considerable and persistent gap between state-of-the-art decomposition methods and the theoretical linear upper bound, even for isometric contractions, indicating substantial room for algorithmic improvement. The finding that no single algorithm dominates across all conditions (CBSS is most robust general-purpose; SCD excels in top-level and automated settings) motivates ensemble approaches and highlights that the field lacks a clear 'best' method. Key limitations include the absence of per-recording hyperparameter optimization, no post-processing of spike trains, single-muscle simulation only, and the exclusion of widely used packages like MUEdit and Demuse. The significantly weaker performance of the unsupervised deep autoencoder baseline compared to classical CBSS/SCD methods underscores that neural approaches have yet to close the gap in this domain, leaving open the question of whether self-supervised or non-linear ICA frameworks can eventually surpass classical pipelines.
