## Problem

In X-ray prohibited item detection, overlapping objects cause foreground-background feature coupling that degrades general detectors. In Deformable DETR-based models, content queries progressively lose their classification semantic information as positional encoding is integrated through decoder layers, and existing contrastive learning approaches (e.g., MMCL) lack category-specific prior knowledge, leading to indiscriminate inter-class repulsion and misalignment with the true feature manifold of each category.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254738/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes standard X-ray baggage images (320×320 or 512×512) and outputs bounding boxes with prohibited item class labels, identical to the base detector. CSPCL is a training-time-only mechanism that operates on the internal content queries within the decoder; it adds no extra parameters, FLOPs, or inference latency, so the deployed model's input/output interface and computational cost remain unchanged from the underlying Deformable DETR variant.

## Method

CSPCL uses the classifier weight matrix as category prototypes and aligns them with content queries in selected decoder layers via a novel CSP loss. The CSP loss comprises two components: (1) an Intra-Class Truncated Attraction (ITA) loss that pulls same-class queries toward their prototype using cosine similarity, but applies a gradient truncation function that zeroes the gradient once similarity exceeds a threshold (1−γ), preserving intra-class diversity; and (2) an Inter-Class Adaptive Repulsion (IAR) loss that pushes inter-class queries away from other-class prototypes, with a repulsion factor R(k₁,k₂) = exp(1−τ·(1−sim(pᵢᵏ¹, pⱼᵏ²))) that scales repulsion strength by prototype-prototype similarity, so similar categories receive stronger separation. The mechanism is plug-and-play: it is inserted into a configurable set of decoder layers (default: all layers) and added to the standard detection loss, requiring no architectural modification and no change to the inference pipeline.

## Results

On the PIDray dataset, C-AO-DETR (AO-DETR + CSPCL) achieves 70.8% overall AP, outperforming the AO-DETR baseline (70.0%) and the previous best DETR-based method DN-DETR (67.8%) by 0.8% and 3.0% respectively. On PIXray, CSPCL improves DINO from 64.3% to 67.5% AP (+3.2%) and Deformable DETR from 36.6% to 41.8% AP (+5.2%), without increasing model parameters, FLOPs, or inference speed. On CLCXray, C-AO-DETR achieves 63.1% AP, surpassing the previous best ForkNet (R101&R18) at 62.0% AP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

C-AO-DETR outperforms all 20 compared baselines on PIDray. Against general CNN detectors, it surpasses Mask R-CNN (67.0% overall AP) by 3.8%, ATSS (63.6%) by 7.2%, and FCOS (62.5%) by 8.3%. Among DETR-based methods, it beats DN-DETR (67.8%) by 3.0% and Deformable DETR (61.3%) by 9.5%. Against prohibited-item-specific detectors, it improves over ForkNet (66.8%) by 4.0% and SDANet (61.6%) by 9.2%. On CLCXray, C-AO-DETR (63.1% AP) outperforms ForkNet R101&R18 (62.0%), LAcls (59.3%), and PIXDet (60.4%). The plug-and-play nature is confirmed on PIXray where CSPCL adds +5.2% AP to Deformable DETR, +3.2% to DINO, +0.8% to AO-DETR, and +0.4% to RT-DETR, with no change in parameters, FLOPs, or FPS.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of DETR-based prohibited item detection for X-ray images, building directly on Deformable DETR's decoder architecture and DINO's denoising training framework. It departs from the prior contrastive learning approach MMCL (same group, 2024) by introducing classifier weights as explicit class prototypes and designing a tailored CSP loss with gradient-truncated intra-class attraction and similarity-scaled inter-class repulsion, rather than MMCL's indiscriminate repulsion. The work also positions itself against AO-DETR's category-specific query assignment (CSA), offering a more portable plug-and-play alternative that does not require modifying the query initialization or assignment strategy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play nature of CSPCL—requiring no architectural changes, no additional parameters, and no inference overhead—makes it an attractive drop-in improvement for any Deformable DETR variant in the X-ray security domain. However, the paper's own limitation section reveals only ~0.1% AP gain on COCO, confirming the mechanism is tightly coupled to the X-ray overlapping phenomenon and unlikely to transfer to general object detection. An open question is whether the class-prototype alignment idea can be generalized to other domains where queries suffer from semantic drift (e.g., open-vocabulary detection or few-shot settings), and whether the modest gains on already-strong baselines like AO-DETR (+0.8%) justify adoption in production pipelines.
