## Problem

Existing GNNs rely on a binary assumption that homophilic graphs need low-pass filters and heterophilic graphs need high-pass filters, implying a monotonic relationship between heterophily degree and optimal spectral response. The paper demonstrates through both theory and experiment that this relationship is non-monotonic and graph-dependent, meaning fixed filter types are fundamentally insufficient for graphs with varying or mixed heterophily patterns.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252162/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a graph's adjacency matrix and node feature matrix, performing eigen decomposition of the normalized adjacency matrix to obtain the spectral basis. For each node, it selects a patch of top-p spectrally relevant neighbors (not just topological neighbors) and produces a final node-level representation after multi-layer mixing, which is passed to a classification head. The Fast-HEROFILTER variant replaces eigen decomposition with a truncated Neumann series (K-step diffusion), enabling scalability to graphs with millions of nodes without explicit spectral computation.

## Method

HEROFILTER consists of two modules: a Patcher that learns an adaptive polynomial filter g(Λ) = Σ σ(w_k ⊙ Λ^k) to compute a node-node spectral relevance matrix R = U g(Λ) U^⊤, then selects the top-p most spectrally aligned nodes per node to form patches; and a Mixer that applies alternating patch-mixing (MLP across the patch dimension) and feature-mixing (MLP across the feature dimension) layers, inspired by MLP-Mixer, to aggregate and transform patch representations. The theoretical contribution establishes that the average filter response and spectral-domain heterophily are non-monotonic (Proposition 1), that adaptive polynomial filters can align with arbitrary label patterns (Proposition 2), and derives a generalization error bound coupling filter design with heterophily (Theorem 1). Fast-HEROFILTER approximates the patcher via a personalized PageRank-style objective minimized with a truncated Neumann series, avoiding costly eigen decomposition while preserving spectral filtering semantics.

## Results

On heterophilic graphs, HEROFILTER achieves state-of-the-art accuracy across all 8 benchmarks, with the largest gains on Snap-Patents (64.2%, +9.2% over GOAT) and Squirrel (47.7%, +6.2% over PolyFormer). On homophilic graphs, HEROFILTER is competitive, matching or exceeding the best baselines on Cora (86.8%) and OGBN-Arxiv (70.5%), while trailing by ≤0.4% on CiteSeer and PubMed. Notably, HEROFILTER avoids out-of-memory failures on large-scale graphs (Snap-Patents with ~3M nodes, OGBN-Arxiv with ~170K nodes) where several spectral and transformer baselines fail entirely.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HEROFILTER wins on all 8 heterophilic benchmarks, with the most dramatic margins on Snap-Patents (+9.2% over GOAT, the next-best method) and Squirrel (+6.2% over PolyFormer). Against spectral methods (BernNet, GPRGNN, ChebNetII), HEROFILTER consistently outperforms on heterophilic graphs while remaining competitive on homophilic ones (within 0.4% on CiteSeer/PubMed). Against graph transformers (GOAT, NAGphormer, Exphormer, PolyFormer, VCR-Graphormer), HEROFILTER achieves superior accuracy on heterophilic datasets and avoids the OOM failures that plague most transformers on large-scale graphs like Snap-Patents. Against heterophily-aware GNNs (H₂GCN, BM-GCN, G²-GCN), HEROFILTER gains 2–9% on heterophilic benchmarks, confirming that adaptive spectral filtering outperforms fixed multi-hop or block-modeling strategies. On homophilic graphs, HEROFILTER is essentially tied with the best baselines (e.g., 86.8% vs. APPNP's 86.7% on Cora; 70.5% vs. PolyFormer's 70.4% on OGBN-Arxiv), showing no degradation in the regime where low-pass filters are already effective.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of spectral GNNs (ChebNet, GPRGNN, BernNet), heterophily-aware message-passing models (H2GCN, FAGCN, G2-GCN), and the MLP-Mixer architecture from vision. It departs from the prevailing binary assumption that homophilic graphs require low-pass filters and heterophilic graphs require high-pass filters, instead proposing an adaptive polynomial filter whose per-frequency response is learned end-to-end. The patch-based neighborhood construction repurposes personalized PageRank-style diffusion (in Fast-HEROFILTER) to avoid costly eigen decomposition, while the aggregation backbone borrows the dual-axis mixing paradigm from vision transformers rather than relying on traditional message passing or global attention.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The formal demonstration that filter response and heterophily degree are non-monotonic challenges a foundational design heuristic in the spectral GNN literature and provides theoretical justification for adaptive filtering. Practically, HEROFILTER's 9.2% improvement on large-scale heterophilic graphs (Snap-Patents) and its ability to avoid OOM where transformer baselines fail make it a strong candidate for real-world deployment. Open questions include whether the theoretical error bound is tight in practice, whether the adaptive filter generalizes to graph structures unseen during training, and whether the approach extends naturally to other tasks such as link prediction or graph-level classification.
