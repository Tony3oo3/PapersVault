## Problem

Existing model fusion methods for LLMs focus primarily on the supervised fine-tuning phase, leaving preference alignment—a critical step in RLHF pipelines—largely unexplored. The few approaches that address fusion during preference alignment, such as WRPO, discard the probabilistic outputs of source models and rely solely on response-level supervision, missing contrastive signals from dispreferred responses. The paper tackles how to systematically fuse multi-source model knowledge during preference optimization while preserving full sequence-level probability information and avoiding the vocabulary alignment challenges inherent in token-level fusion.

## Input / Output

The method consumes preference pairs (prompt x, preferred response y_w, dispreferred response y_l) along with pre-computed sequence-level log probabilities from N source models (7B–24B parameters) and the pivot model, all computed offline via vLLM before training begins. Because fusion operates at the sequence level, only a few scalar log-probability values per sequence are needed during optimization rather than full 100k-vocabulary distributions, keeping memory overhead minimal even as source model count grows. The output is an updated 14B pivot model (Phi-4) that has absorbed complementary capabilities—mathematical reasoning, code generation, general instruction following—from heterogeneous source models while retaining its own architecture and tokenizer.

## Method

InfiFPO replaces the reference model in DPO with a fused source model M_fu^s(y|x) = ∏(M_i^s(y|x))^γ_i, a weighted geometric mean of source model sequence probabilities, derived by relaxing a sequence-KL constrained RLHF objective (FuseRLHF) into a fully offline loss that avoids online sampling and reward model training. This sequence-level formulation sidesteps the vocabulary alignment problem that forces token-level fusion methods into complex heuristic matching, while preserving each source model's full probability signal over the entire response. Three stability-enhancing strategies are introduced: length normalization (dividing log-probabilities by tokenized sequence length to correct for tokenizer-induced length bias), probability clipping (bounding source model probabilities by the initial pivot model's values to suppress noisy gradients from underperforming sources), and max-margin fusion (selecting the single source model with the largest absolute probability deviation from the pivot as the reference, a winner-takes-all strategy that prioritizes the most complementary knowledge). Gradient analysis reveals that the preference disparity coefficient adaptively up-weights samples where source models exhibit stronger preference distinctions than the pivot model, enabling efficient and targeted transfer of preference knowledge without additional training cost.

## Results

Using Phi-4 (14B) as the pivot model, InfiFPO improves the average score across 11 benchmarks from 79.95 to 83.33 (+3.38). With a fair 3-source-model comparison (InfiFPO*), the method achieves 82.74 average, outperforming the best model fusion baseline InfiFusion* (82.38) by +0.36 while using only 34% of the GPU hours (55 vs. 160). Against the strongest preference optimization baseline SFT-WRPO (82.80), InfiFPO gains +0.53 in average score at comparable compute cost (58 vs. 57 GPU hours). Gains are most pronounced on IFEval (+4.4 over InfiFusion*) and HumanEval (+2.4 over InfiFusion*).

## Baselines & Comparisons

InfiFPO outperforms all three token-level model fusion baselines (FuseLLM, FuseChat, InfiFusion) on average score, with the largest margin over InfiFusion* (+0.36 avg) achieved at only 34% of the GPU hours. Against preference optimization baselines, InfiFPO beats SFT-WRPO by +0.53 average (83.33 vs. 82.80) at nearly identical compute (58 vs. 57 GPU hours), and outperforms SFT-DPO (+0.66) and SFT-IPO (+0.95). The advantage is most visible on code generation (HumanEval: 87.80 vs. 86.59 for WRPO) and instruction following (IFEval: 82.25 vs. 81.18 for WRPO), while math scores (GSM8K, MATH) remain competitive across all methods. In the fair 3-source comparison (InfiFPO*), the method still edges out InfiFusion* by +0.36 and SFT-WRPO by −0.06, confirming that the gains are not solely due to additional source models. Supplementary experiments on UltraFeedback and with Qwen2.5-14B as pivot model confirm consistent wins over the same baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two previously separate research threads: token-level model fusion (FuseLLM, FuseChat, InfiFusion) and offline preference optimization (DPO, IPO). The closest prior work, WRPO, already attempted fusion during preference alignment but discarded source-model probability information and used only response-level supervision for preferred outputs. InfiFPO departs from both lines by replacing the DPO reference model with a sequence-level geometric-mean fusion of multiple source models, derived from a constrained RLHF objective (FuseRLHF) with sequence-level KL constraints, thereby sidestepping the vocabulary-alignment bottleneck that has limited token-level fusion while preserving full probability signals for both preferred and dispreferred responses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By showing that the reference-model slot in DPO can be repurposed as a fusion interface, the paper offers a drop-in mechanism to combine heterogeneous LLMs during alignment without the engineering overhead of token-level vocabulary matching, and it does so at roughly one-third the GPU cost of the best token-level fusion baseline. However, the absolute gains over strong baselines are modest (≈0.4–0.5 points on average), the method still inherits DPO's known limitations (sensitivity to β, no explicit reward model), and experiments are confined to 7B–24B open-source models, leaving open whether the approach scales to frontier-scale models or benefits from more sophisticated fusion weighting than the winner-takes-all max-margin rule.
