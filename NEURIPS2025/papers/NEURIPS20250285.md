## Problem

Existing activation steering methods either intervene indiscriminately on every generated token or decide intervention based solely on the input question, making it impossible to assign appropriate intervention strength to each generation. This is particularly problematic for truthfulness tasks where the same question can elicit correct or deviated responses, and where question-only probing yields poorly separated probability distributions that hinder fine-grained decisions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250285/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a text prompt and, during autoregressive generation, inspects the activations of selected attention heads at each newly generated token position. It produces steered text output where, upon detecting deviation, the last s tokens are discarded and regenerated with an adaptive steering offset added to the outputs of top-k attention heads. The intervention strength is a scalar proportional to the classifier's deviation probability, and the binary mask c^{ℓ,H} restricts modification to only the identified heads rather than the full residual stream.

## Method

FASB operates in two phases. First, a linear probe is trained on concatenated question-answer pairs to identify the top-k attention heads most discriminative for the target behavior; the probe's weight vector serves simultaneously as both the classifier for state tracking and the steering vector. Second, during generation, after each token the classifier computes a deviation probability by averaging (1 − p) across the selected heads. If this probability exceeds a threshold β, the method backtracks s tokens, then applies an adaptive steering offset r = I(p > β) · p · α to the outputs of the selected heads for all subsequent tokens, regenerating the backtracked span and continuing. This design ensures intervention is applied only when needed, scaled to the severity of deviation, and applied early enough (via backtracking) to correct tokens that have already strayed from the desired behavior.

## Results

On TruthfulQA open-ended generation, the Probe method achieves 80.56% True*Info, outperforming the baseline (66.50%) and the strongest baseline ITI (76.11%). On TruthfulQA multiple-choice, it reaches 48.71% MC1, 66.58% MC2, and 41.95% MC3, surpassing all baselines by large margins (e.g., +10.4% MC1 over ITI, +11.9% MC2 over SADI-HEAD). Across six multiple-choice tasks (COPA, StoryCloze, NLI, MMLU, SST2, Winogrande), the average accuracy is 78.8%, compared to 65.1% for the baseline and 66.8% for the best baseline (CAA).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The Probe method outperforms all six baselines on every reported metric. Against ITI (the closest competitor on True*Info at 76.11%), Probe gains +4.45% True*Info, +10.4% MC1, and +9.43% MC2, attributed to its adaptive intervention strength versus ITI's fixed, indiscriminate steering. Against SADI-HEAD (the strongest on MC2 at 54.65%), Probe leads by +11.93% MC2 and +4.45% True*Info. Against CAA, ORTHO, CAST, and ACT, the margins are even larger (e.g., +14.5% MC1 over CAA, +12.5% MC2 over ORTHO, +15.5% MC1 over ACT). On the six multiple-choice tasks, Probe's 78.8% average exceeds the best baseline (CAA at 66.8%) by 12 percentage points, with particularly large gains on COPA (+25.6%) and StoryCloze (+30.0%). The ablation confirms that both the adaptive strength and the backtracking mechanism are essential: removing backtracking drops True*Info to 62.11% (below baseline), and using fixed strength reduces MC1 from 48.71% to 38.31%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the activation steering / representation engineering line of work (ITI, CAA, ORTHO, SADI, ACT, CAST) by addressing a key limitation: prior methods either intervene indiscriminately on all generated tokens or decide intervention based solely on the input question. FASB departs from this by tracking the LLM's internal activations token-by-token during generation to dynamically determine both whether and how strongly to intervene, and introduces a backtracking mechanism that regenerates recently deviated tokens rather than only affecting future ones. The method also connects probing and steering by using the trained probe parameters directly as steering vectors, building on ITI's head-selection strategy but applying it in a fundamentally different (adaptive, generation-aware) manner.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The backtracking mechanism addresses a practical gap in activation steering: intervening only after a deviation is detected is often too late, and the ablation showing that intervention without backtracking actually degrades performance below baseline (62.11 vs. 66.50 True*Info) underscores this. The method's generality across LLMs (LLaMA2, LLaMA3.1, Qwen2.5), datasets (TruthfulQA, six MC benchmarks, Natural Questions, TriviaQA, WikiHop), and even toxic content generation suggests a broadly applicable inference-time control technique. However, the approach remains hyperparameter-sensitive (threshold β, strength α, backtracking depth s), is evaluated primarily on English QA tasks, and relies on LLM-as-judge evaluation for open-ended generation. The dual-use demonstration (steering toward toxic content) raises important safety questions about flexible steering methods more broadly.
