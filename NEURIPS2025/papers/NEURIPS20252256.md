## Problem

Existing differentially private (DP) text generation methods, such as Amin et al.'s exponential mechanism over LLM logits, require 100× or more computational overhead over non-private generation to produce non-degenerate text, and rely on high-temperature sampling from the full vocabulary that yields low-quality output. This makes DP long-form generation intractable at scale for practical applications like RAG over confidential knowledge bases or synthetic medical/legal text generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252256/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a non-sensitive text query, a batch of B sensitive reference texts, and white-box access to an LLM's next-token logit function, requiring (B+1) LLM inference calls per generated token (B private + 1 public). It outputs autoregressive synthetic text sequences of up to T tokens satisfying a ρ-zCDP guarantee under replace-by-null adjacency at the example level. The batch size B directly fixes the per-token computation budget, and the method gracefully degrades toward public-model generation when B or the privacy budget is small.

## Method

INVISIBLEINK interprets each token sample as an exponential mechanism over the LLM's next-token logits, with two key innovations. First, DCClip clips only the difference between private and public logits (φ_i − φ_pub) to [−C,C] rather than the full private logits, isolating the sensitive signal and reducing the required clip norm from C≈8 to C≈1, yielding an ~8× reduction in the batch size B needed to maintain a usable sampling temperature. Second, Top-k+ sampling constructs a tight superset V_k+ of the union of top-k private tokens using only public logits (by expanding the public top-k threshold by 2C/B), enabling truncated decoding that suppresses degenerate tail tokens at zero additional privacy cost. The per-token zCDP loss is C²/(2B²τ²), and adaptive composition over T tokens gives the sequence-level guarantee ρ_seq = TC²/(2B²τ²), with the clip norm set as C = Bτ√(2ρ_seq/T) for a target privacy budget.

## Results

On the MIMIC-IV-Note dataset (1000 synthetic generations, TinyLLaMA), INVISIBLEINK with Top-k+ sampling (k=100) achieves MAUVE scores of 68.4–75.0% across ε=1–10 with only B=3–31 inference calls per token, while Amin et al. [13] requires B=63–127 calls to reach 68.9–70.1% MAUVE at ε=10 — an 8× reduction in compute for higher quality. INVISIBLEINK also produces more medical named entities and longer generations than all baselines. Under tight budgets (ε=1, B≤31), Amin et al. becomes infeasible (INF) and AdaPMixED exhausts its privacy budget after ~10 tokens, whereas INVISIBLEINK degrades gracefully to near-public-model quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Amin et al. [13] (the prior white-box SoTA), INVISIBLEINK achieves higher MAUVE at 8–16× fewer LLM inference calls per token: at ε=10, INVISIBLEINK (k=100, B=31) scores 75.0% versus Amin et al. (B=127) at 70.1%, while Amin et al. becomes entirely infeasible (INF) for B≤31 at ε≤5. Against AdaPMixED [19], INVISIBLEINK wins on both quality (75.0% vs. 58.9% MAUVE at ε=10, B=31) and reliability — AdaPMixED's data-dependent privacy budget is exhausted after as few as 10 tokens at small B, and it exceeds 36-hour wall-clock limits (TLE) at B=127, ε=10. Against the API-access method AugPE [41], INVISIBLEINK outperforms across all privacy budgets and metrics on MIMIC (Fig. 5), though AugPE shows competitive performance on Yelp where the reference distribution is well-represented in pretraining data. The paper notes it gives Amin et al. and AugPE sensitivity advantages (using their best-case bounds under a more favorable adjacency), yet INVISIBLEINK still wins decisively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the exponential-mechanism-based DP text generation framework of Amin et al. (2024), which interprets next-token sampling as the canonical exponential mechanism over LLM logits, by introducing two targeted modifications: DCClip, which clips only the private-over-public logit difference rather than raw logits (reducing sensitivity ~8×), and Top-k+ sampling, which adapts standard truncated decoding to the DP setting via a privacy-free superset construction. The approach also draws on the broader DP synthetic data literature (exponential mechanism, public-data priors) and the NLP decoding literature (top-k, temperature), but its core contribution is making the exponential mechanism practical for long-form generation under strict compute budgets. It departs from prior work by providing a data-independent, ex-ante zCDP guarantee with straightforward non-adaptive privacy accounting, in contrast to the data-dependent or adaptive guarantees of AdaPMixED and the SVT-based calibration of Amin et al.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

INVISIBLEINK makes DP long-form text generation practical for the first time, reducing compute from ~100× non-private cost (Amin et al.) to 4–8×, which is critical for deploying private RAG and synthetic data pipelines in regulated domains (healthcare, finance). The graceful degradation property—falling back toward the public model when privacy or compute budgets are tight—addresses a practical failure mode of prior methods that produce gibberish under tight budgets. Open questions include whether the linear dependence on token budget T in Theorem 2 can be improved (e.g., via regularization), whether the white-box logit access requirement can be relaxed to API-only settings, and how the method behaves under adaptive or multi-turn generation settings. The open-source package (invink) lowers the barrier for practitioners to adopt DP text generation.
