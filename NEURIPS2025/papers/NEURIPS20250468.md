## Problem

Contrastively trained vision-language models like CLIP struggle with compositional reasoning because the text encoder learns to align individual words with visual objects rather than capturing syntactic and relational structure between words. Prior remedies either introduce hard negatives into the contrastive objective (risking shortcut learning) or add auxiliary losses targeting both encoders or only the image encoder, leaving the text encoder—the identified primary bottleneck—largely unaddressed by dedicated auxiliary objectives.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250468/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes an RGB image paired with a text caption (sampled from MS-COCO's multi-caption sets) and produces a cosine-similarity score between the image and text embeddings in a shared space. During training, each image-text pair is additionally augmented with M=3 rule-based hard negative captions, K=1 alternative caption from the same image's caption set (for reconstruction), and one LLM-generated paraphrase (for alignment). The frozen T5-Large decoder receives a projected version of the text embedding (via a learnable linear projector W) and autoregressively predicts tokens of the alternative caption.

## Method

READ augments the standard CLIP contrastive loss (modified to include hard negatives in the image-to-text denominator) with two auxiliary objectives that target the text encoder. First, a token-level reconstruction loss trains the encoder so that a frozen T5-Large decoder, conditioned on the projected text embedding, can reconstruct an *alternative* caption (not the original), encouraging the encoder to encode inter-word relationships necessary for generating semantically equivalent but differently worded text. Second, a sentence-level alignment loss applies a contrastive objective in the text embedding space to pull together embeddings of an original caption and its GPT-4o-mini-generated paraphrase while pushing apart paraphrases from other images in the batch. The final training loss is L_READ = L_Contrastive + 0.1·L_TokenReconstruction + 0.5·L_SentenceAlignment, and the method is applied as a fine-tuning procedure on top of pre-trained CLIP (ViT-B/32) using 100K MS-COCO samples for 5 epochs.

## Results

READ-CLIP achieves a state-of-the-art average accuracy of 64.1% across five compositional reasoning benchmarks (WhatsUp, VALSE, CREPE, SugarCrepe, SugarCrepe++), outperforming the pre-trained CLIP ViT-B/32 by 12.1% and the strongest baseline FSC-CLIP by 1.7%. It ranks first on four of five benchmarks and second on CREPE (41.5% vs. FSC-CLIP's 42.5%). The method also provides consistent gains when applied on top of existing fine-tuning approaches: +1.2% over naive CLIP, +1.0% over NegCLIP, and +0.8% over FSC-CLIP in average accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

READ-CLIP outperforms all six baselines in average accuracy, with the largest margin over the pre-trained CLIP (+12.1%) and the smallest over FSC-CLIP (+1.7%). Against NegCLIP (the most widely cited hard-negative baseline), READ-CLIP gains 4.5% on average, winning on WhatsUp (43.9 vs. 42.4), VALSE (76.2 vs. 73.7), SugarCrepe (87.0 vs. 83.6), and both SugarCrepe++ subtasks. Against FSC-CLIP, READ-CLIP wins on WhatsUp (+4.1), VALSE (+1.8), SugarCrepe (+1.8), SugarCrepe++ ITT (+1.9), and SugarCrepe++ TOT (+1.8), but trails by 1.0% on CREPE (41.5 vs. 42.5). Against CE-CLIP, the gap is 5.7% on average, with READ-CLIP leading on every benchmark. The ablation (Table 2) confirms that the token-level reconstruction loss primarily drives gains on WhatsUp, VALSE, and CREPE, while the sentence-level alignment loss is critical for SugarCrepe++ where paraphrase recognition is the core challenge.

## Relation to Prior Work

READ builds on the contrastive learning framework of CLIP and the line of work introducing hard negatives to improve compositional reasoning (NegCLIP, CE-CLIP, FSC-CLIP, Triplet-CLIP). It departs from prior auxiliary-objective approaches (SF-CLIP, IL-CLIP, SDS-CLIP, CLIP-SGVL) by specifically targeting the text encoder rather than both encoders jointly or the image encoder alone. The token-level reconstruction loss repurposes the encoder-decoder reconstruction paradigm from NLP (MASS, RetroMAE, Skip-Thought Vectors) into VLM fine-tuning, while the sentence-level alignment loss draws on paraphrase-based contrastive objectives. The key conceptual contribution is combining these two objectives as complementary: one captures intra-sentence word relationships, the other captures inter-sentence semantic consistency.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

READ provides a modular, drop-in fine-tuning recipe that consistently improves compositional reasoning across five benchmarks and can be stacked on top of existing CLIP variants (NegCLIP, FSC-CLIP) without re-deriving their objectives, making it practically useful for practitioners. The finding that reconstructing an *alternative* caption rather than the original mitigates overfitting to exact wording is a useful design insight for future reconstruction-based objectives. Limitations include reliance on multi-caption datasets or LLM-generated paraphrases, use of only a T5 decoder without exploring alternatives, and evaluation limited to ViT-B/32 on 100K COCO samples. Open questions include whether fine-tuning the decoder jointly would further improve performance, how the method scales to larger architectures, and whether the gains persist on more diverse or domain-specific data.
