## Problem

General-purpose neural networks (MLPs, Transformers, KANs) exhibit notable shortcomings in modeling and reasoning about periodic phenomena, achieving only marginal in-domain performance and failing to extrapolate to out-of-distribution scenarios. Existing Fourier-based networks possess periodicity modeling ability but cannot scale to deeper architectures and are typically restricted to specific tasks, limiting their applicability to general-purpose modeling such as language modeling.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252225/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The FAN layer accepts a vector x ∈ R^{d_x} and produces a concatenated output [cos(W_p x) || sin(W_p x) || σ(B_p̄ + W_p̄ x)] ∈ R^{2d_p + d_p̄}, where the periodic (sin/cos) and non-periodic (activated linear) components are computed in parallel rather than sequentially. In practice, FAN layers replace MLP layers within larger architectures (Transformer, CNN, FNO), so the end-to-end input/output depends on the host model: scalar inputs for periodicity fitting, 96-step time series for forecasting, token sequences for sentiment classification, or 28×28 images for digit recognition. The hyperparameter d_p defaults to d_output/4, yielding a ~25% reduction in parameters and FLOPs relative to an equivalent MLP layer.

## Method

FAN is built on the Fourier Series principle, where each layer simultaneously computes learnable sinusoidal projections cos(W_p x) and sin(W_p x) alongside a standard nonlinear projection σ(B_p̄ + W_p̄ x), concatenating all three into the layer output. This parallel (rather than sequential) design satisfies two core principles: (1) the capacity to represent Fourier coefficients grows with network depth, and (2) every hidden layer's output retains the structure needed for subsequent layers to perform periodicity modeling. When W_p converges to zero, FAN degenerates to a standard MLP, preserving universal approximation and general-purpose capability. The full FAN stacks L FAN layers followed by a linear output layer, and a gated variant (FAN-Gated) introduces a learnable scalar g to balance the periodic and non-periodic branches. The architecture is validated across periodicity fitting, symbolic formula representation, time series forecasting, language modeling, image recognition, and SciML (Burgers' equation via FNO), consistently outperforming MLP, KAN, Transformer, LSTM, Mamba, and prior Fourier-based networks while using fewer parameters.

## Results

On time series forecasting (averaged over Weather, Exchange, Traffic, ETTh), Transformer with FAN achieves 0.839 MSE and 0.631 MAE, improving 15.6% and 8.4% over standard Transformer while using 1.06M fewer parameters. On language modeling (SST-2 test), FAN reaches 81.54% accuracy and 0.4094 loss, outperforming Transformer (81.19%, 0.4297) and achieving up to 14.65% loss reduction and 8.50% accuracy gain on OOD datasets (Amazon Reviews: 77.63% vs. 71.55%). On periodicity modeling, FAN dramatically outperforms MLP, KAN, and Transformer in both in-domain and OOD extrapolation, while using fewer parameters and FLOPs than MLP by a factor of d_p/d_output (default 1/4).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FAN consistently outperforms all baselines across tasks. Against MLP and KAN on periodicity modeling and symbolic formula representation, FAN achieves significantly lower test loss, especially in OOD extrapolation where baselines diverge sharply; KAN shows a U-shaped degradation at larger parameter counts while FAN continues improving. Against Transformer on time series forecasting, FAN reduces average MSE by 15.6% (0.839 vs. 0.994) and MAE by 8.4% (0.631 vs. 0.689) with fewer parameters. Against LSTM and Mamba on both time series and language modeling, FAN achieves the best or near-best results on all four sentiment datasets, with the largest OOD gains on Amazon Reviews (77.63% vs. 71.55% for Transformer, 71.52% for LSTM). Against Fourier-based networks (FNN/SIREN, FSNN, FTNN/FNO), FAN is the only architecture that excels on both periodicity modeling and general-purpose language modeling; FNN and FSNN fail entirely on language modeling, while FTNN (300M params) underperforms FAN (95M params) on both tasks. Against CNN on image recognition, FAN yields modest but consistent gains (e.g., 99.67% vs. 99.63% on MNIST, 51.88% vs. 49.82% OOD on Fashion-MNIST). Against FEDformer on time series, FAN improves MSE across nearly all settings (e.g., 0.577 vs. 0.587 on Traffic-96, 1.159 vs. 1.195 on Exchange-720).

## Relation to Prior Work

FAN builds directly on the Fourier Series mathematical framework, but departs fundamentally from prior Fourier-based networks (FNN/SIREN, FSNN, FTNN/FNO) that either use periodic functions as activations or apply Fourier Transform as an intermediate processing step. The key conceptual departure is that FAN embeds the Fourier Series structure *within* each layer's output (concatenating cos, sin, and standard nonlinear components), ensuring that periodic modeling capacity scales with depth—a property the authors prove is absent in naively stacked Fourier Series networks (Eq. 5). This positions FAN as a drop-in MLP replacement rather than a task-specific architecture, unlike prior Fourier networks that were limited to shallow models or narrow applications.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a fundamental and previously underappreciated weakness of standard architectures: their inability to learn and extrapolate periodic structure, demonstrated systematically across MLP, KAN, and Transformer. The practical impact is significant—FAN replaces MLP layers with ~25% fewer parameters and FLOPs while improving performance on time series, language modeling, and image tasks, making it a potentially attractive component for large-scale model design. Open questions include whether the d_p hyperparameter (default 1/4 of hidden dim) can be adapted per-task, whether the benefit persists at much larger scales beyond the 1B LLM experiment, and whether the periodicity inductive bias could ever be harmful for tasks with no latent periodic structure.
