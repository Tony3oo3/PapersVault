## Problem

Adafactor is a widely adopted memory-efficient optimizer (used in PaLM, T5) that approximates Adam's second-moment matrix via rank-1 factorization and removes first-order momentum, yet its convergence behavior for non-convex smooth optimization has remained entirely unexplored. The matrix factorization and update clipping mechanisms create analytical challenges that render standard proxy step-size techniques from Adam/AdaGrad inapplicable, leaving it unknown whether Adafactor can guarantee finding stationary points and at what rate.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes n×m stochastic gradient matrices G_k = g(X_k, Z_k) at each iteration along with hyperparameters: a step-size sequence ρ_k, a decay rate β_{2,k} ∈ [0,1), a regularization constant ε_1, and a (time-varying) clipping threshold d_k. It produces updated parameter matrices X_{k+1} ∈ ℝ^{n×m} and, theoretically, a convergence rate guarantee of Õ(1/√T) for the minimum gradient norm over T iterations under sub-Gaussian noise. The key structural constraint is that the second-moment matrix W_k is never stored explicitly—only its row sums R_k ∈ ℝ^m and column sums C_k ∈ ℝ^n are maintained, reducing memory from O(mn) to O(m+n).

## Method

The core analytical technique is viewing Adafactor as an approximation of Adam and designing a new proxy step-size matrix A_k that replaces the stochastic gradient-dependent terms in W_k with deterministic upper bounds (Σ_k², 𝒢_k), making A_k conditionally independent of the k-th stochastic gradient so that martingale concentration inequalities apply. The proxy must remain close to W_k, which is controlled by showing the relative distance |w_ij − a_ij|/√a_ij = O(D_k√(1−β_{2,k})), vanishing as β_{2,k}→1. An induction argument then bounds the gradient magnitude D_t ≤ H uniformly over the trajectory, enabling the descent lemma to yield O(1/T) (full-batch) and Õ(1/√T) (stochastic) convergence rates. For the update clipping variant, an additional error term from the RMS-based clipping is bounded using sub-Gaussian tail estimates, showing clipping does not degrade the rate under light-tailed noise.

## Results

The primary results are theoretical convergence guarantees: full-batch Adafactor achieves an O(1/T) rate for finding stationary points, matching the lower bound for deterministic first-order methods up to constants. In the stochastic setting (sub-Gaussian noise), Adafactor attains the nearly optimal Õ(1/√T) rate both with and without update clipping, matching the lower bound for stochastic non-convex smooth optimization. Supplementary experiments on BERT-Large (GLUE/MNLI) and GPT-2 (BookCorpus) confirm that the theoretically optimal hyperparameter setting (c=1, i.e., β₂,k=1−1/k, ρ_k=ρ₀/√k) yields training loss and test accuracy comparable to the default Adafactor configuration and to Adam.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's main comparison is theoretical: Adafactor's convergence rate matches that of Adam and RMSProp (Õ(1/√T) in the stochastic case, O(1/T) in the full-batch case), confirming that the rank-1 matrix factorization of the second-moment matrix does not fundamentally hinder convergence. In the supplementary experiments (Section 9, Appendix D), the authors compare their theoretically motivated hyperparameter configuration (c=1) against the default Adafactor setting from Shazeer & Stern [38] and against Adam on BERT-Base/Large (GLUE/MNLI) and GPT-2 (BookCorpus). All three configurations achieve comparable test accuracy and training loss, with the paper noting that performance improves as the decay exponent c increases from 0.6 to 1.0, consistent with the theoretical prediction. When update clipping is included, the theoretically prescribed increasing threshold d_k = k^(c/(2(α−1))) performs comparably to the standard fixed threshold d_k=1 and to Adam.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper provides the first convergence analysis of Adafactor (Shazeer & Stern, ICML 2018), a memory-efficient optimizer that approximates Adam's second-moment matrix via rank-1 factorization and removes first-order momentum. The analysis is positioned within the well-established line of convergence proofs for adaptive gradient methods (AdaGrad, Adam, RMSProp), borrowing the proxy step-size technique from prior Adam/AdaGrad analyses but requiring a novel construction to handle Adafactor's non-linear matrix factorization update rule. The paper explicitly frames Adafactor as an approximation of Adam, and its convergence rates match those already established for Adam and RMSProp, confirming that the memory-saving rank-1 approximation does not degrade the theoretical guarantees.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Adafactor is a widely adopted optimizer in large-scale LLM training (PaLM, T5, and numerous recent memory-efficient optimizer papers use it as a benchmark), yet it lacked any convergence guarantee despite being proposed years ago. This work closes that gap, showing that the rank-1 second-moment approximation and update clipping do not fundamentally hinder convergence under sub-Gaussian noise, which provides theoretical justification for its practical use. Open questions remain: convergence under heavy-tailed or affine-variance noise, analysis with constant (rather than increasing) clipping thresholds, extension to more general smoothness conditions like (L₀, L₁)-smoothness, and empirical validation on large language models.
