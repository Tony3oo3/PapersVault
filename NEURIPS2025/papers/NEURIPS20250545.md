## Problem

Standard incremental sequence classifiers are trained with direct cross-entropy, which forces early-prefix predictions to regress toward a single noisy one-hot label, ignoring the fact that calibrated predictions must satisfy a temporal-consistency condition across successive timesteps. This paper identifies that condition (analogous to the Bellman equation in RL) and shows that exploiting it yields a loss function that is both more data-efficient and more accurate than the standard approach.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250545/cropped_page8_idx5.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text sequence revealed token-by-token and produces a K-dimensional class probability vector at every prefix length t, computed via a linear head on each hidden state of a decoder-only transformer. In the LLM-verification setting, the input is a generated response concatenated to a problem prompt, with prompt tokens masked out of the loss so the model only learns to score the generated tokens. The output is a per-token correctness probability that enables early rejection of unpromising generations before full completion.

## Method

The core contribution is the TC-λ loss, which replaces the hard one-hot target at each timestep t with a soft target z_t that is an exponentially weighted average of the reference model's own predictive distributions at future timesteps t+1 through t+k, with weight λ controlling the effective lookahead. Training proceeds iteratively (analogous to TD(λ) in RL): a stop-gradient reference model supplies the soft targets while the current model minimizes cross-entropy against them, and the reference is updated each step. In the tabular Markov-chain setting, the paper proves this iterative procedure converges to the exact absorption probabilities and is W× more data-efficient than direct estimation, where W is the branching factor. Empirically, the method is applied by fine-tuning decoder-only transformers (OPT-125M/350M/1.3B for text classification; Qwen2.5-0.5B for GSM8K verification) with a single classification head, requiring negligible additional compute over standard training.

## Results

On four text classification benchmarks (OHSUMED, NEWSGROUPS, IMDB, AG-NEWS), TC-λ achieves the best or second-best accuracy across all prefix lengths (4, 16, and full sequence), outperforming DCE by up to 5.7 points on 4-token prefixes (e.g., 33.4% vs 27.7% on NEWSGROUPS) and by up to 0.7 points on full sequences. On GSM8K verification, the TC-λ verifier reaches ~70% ROC AUC after only 8 generated tokens, and the resulting boosted best-of-N strategy requires 23%–33% fewer total tokens than vanilla best-of-N for equivalent answer accuracy. Scaling experiments on OHSUMED show that DCE requires a model ~10× larger (1.3B vs 125M parameters) to match the ROC AUC of TC-λ.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LSTD(λ) [5], the closest RL-inspired baseline (squared-loss TD value estimation), TC-λ wins on nearly every metric, with the largest gaps on short prefixes and multi-class datasets (e.g., +7.2 points on NEWSGROUPS 4-token, +3.4 on OHSUMED 16-token), while LSTD(λ) edges ahead only on OHSUMED full-sequence accuracy (78.0 vs 81.8 is actually TC-λ winning; LSTD(λ) is 78.0). Against the vanilla best-of-N method [8] on GSM8K, the TC-λ-based boosted best-of-N achieves the same answer accuracy while generating 23%–33% fewer total tokens, by pruning unpromising generations after just 10 tokens. Against majority voting [42], the boosted best-of-N approach also achieves a more favorable accuracy-vs-compute trade-off. On the text classification benchmarks, TC-λ also outperforms the widely-used last-token classification approach on full-sequence accuracy (e.g., 81.8 vs 80.6 on OHSUMED, 95.0 vs 94.8 on AG-NEWS), confirming that training on all prefixes is beneficial even when only full-sequence predictions are needed.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends temporal-difference (TD) learning from reinforcement learning—where it models scalar reward-to-go with squared loss—to the multiclass classification setting with cross-entropy loss, yielding the TC-λ loss. It builds directly on Cheikhi and Russo's (ICML 2023) analysis of direct versus indirect estimators for Markov reward processes, adapting their data-efficiency framework to absorption probabilities in absorbing Markov chains. In the LLM verification literature, prior token-level verifiers (Cobbe et al., Lightman et al., Mudgal et al.) train with direct cross-entropy or squared TD loss; this paper departs by showing that a cross-entropy-based TD-style objective is both more natural for categorical outcomes and empirically superior in calibration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The TC-λ loss is architecturally agnostic, adds negligible training/inference overhead, and consistently improves accuracy across four text classification benchmarks and an LLM verification task, making it a drop-in improvement for practitioners already fine-tuning decoder-only transformers. The finding that a 125M model trained with TC-λ matches a 1.3B model trained with DCE on OHSUMED suggests meaningful compute savings, though this claim rests on a single dataset and small model scales. Open questions include whether the gains persist at 7B+ parameter scales, how TC-λ interacts with multi-token prediction heads, and whether the temporal-consistency regularization transfers to multimodal or non-text sequential settings.
