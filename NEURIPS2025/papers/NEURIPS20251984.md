## Problem

Existing SSL algorithms (FixMatch, FlexMatch, SoftMatch) were designed for training neural networks from scratch, leaving open whether they remain effective when vision foundation models (VFMs) like CLIP and DINOv2 serve as backbones. This paper reveals that under fair hyperparameter tuning, labeled-only PEFT often matches or surpasses these SSL methods, exposing a fundamental mismatch between scratch-oriented SSL and the VFM era. The work also identifies that standard SSL benchmarks (CIFAR, Food101) are too easy for frozen VFMs to meaningfully evaluate SSL's added value.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images in two sets: a small labeled set (1–80 samples per class depending on dataset) and a large unlabeled set, both drawn from six diverse domains (texture, scene, remote sensing, medical, synthetic, autonomous driving). Output is a single class label per image, evaluated via Top-1 accuracy. A key operational detail is that pseudo-labels are generated as one-hot vectors from each VFM-PEFT pair and averaged into soft targets, rather than using raw logits or probabilities, to compensate for the inconsistent confidence scales across differently-pretrained backbones.

## Method

V-PET (VFM-PEFT Ensemble Training) is a single-round self-training pipeline with four phases: (a) fine-tune N PEFT methods (e.g., LoRA, AdaptFormer) on M VFM backbones (e.g., CLIP, DINOv2) using only labeled data; (b) generate one-hot pseudo-labels for all unlabeled samples from each of the N×M fine-tuned models; (c) ensemble pseudo-labels via 'Mean Labels'—averaging one-hot vectors across all models to produce soft targets, which handles the calibration gaps that make logit- or probability-averaging unreliable; (d) fine-tune one selected VFM-PEFT pair on the ensembled pseudo-labels. The core insight is that different PEFT methods and VFMs produce complementary high-confidence predictions even at similar overall accuracy, so their ensemble yields more robust pseudo-labels without any confidence thresholding, eliminating the need for iterative self-training rounds.

## Results

Across 12 evaluation settings (6 datasets × 2 shot counts), V-PET achieves the highest average top-1 accuracy: 60.5% (CLIP + LoRA), 61.0% (CLIP + AdaptFormer), 61.2% (DINOv2 + LoRA), and 61.5% (DINOv2 + AdaptFormer). The best traditional SSL baseline (SoftMatch) reaches only 59.7%, 56.3%, 50.2%, and 48.5% respectively, while labeled-only PEFT fine-tuning achieves 55.7%, 55.6%, 56.0%, and 51.6%. V-PET's advantage over the strongest SSL competitor (SoftMatch) ranges from +0.8 points (CLIP + LoRA) to +13.0 points (DINOv2 + AdaptFormer), with the gap widening substantially for DINOv2 where consistency-based SSL methods degrade severely.

## Baselines & Comparisons

V-PET outperforms all four SSL baselines across every VFM × PEFT configuration. Against FixMatch, V-PET gains +6.8 to +13.8 points; against FlexMatch, +4.3 to +13.7 points; against SoftMatch (the strongest traditional SSL method), +0.8 to +13.0 points; and against FineSSL (the most directly comparable VFM-based SSL method), +6.6 to +14.7 points. The advantage is most pronounced with DINOv2, where consistency-based methods (FixMatch, FlexMatch) drop to ~47–49% average accuracy—below even labeled-only fine-tuning—while V-PET maintains 61.2–61.5%. Notably, V-PET also surpasses labeled-only PEFT by 4.5–9.9 points, confirming that the ensembled pseudo-labels from diverse VFM-PEFT pairs provide genuine additional signal beyond what labeled data alone can offer. The progressive improvement ST → PET → V-PET (e.g., 52.9 → 56.7 → 61.2 for DINOv2 + LoRA) demonstrates that diversity in both PEFT methods and VFM backbones is the key driver of the gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the self-training/pseudo-labeling paradigm (Lee et al., 2013) and repositions it within the foundation model era, where VFMs like CLIP and DINOv2 serve as pre-trained backbones. It departs from traditional SSL methods (FixMatch, FlexMatch, SoftMatch) that were designed for training from scratch, showing they offer limited benefit when paired with VFMs. The most direct prior work is FineSSL [25], which also explores CLIP-based SSL but on small-scale datasets where frozen VFMs already perform well; this paper extends the scope to diverse, challenging benchmarks and introduces ensembling across multiple PEFT methods and VFM backbones as the key mechanism for robust pseudo-label generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that labeled-only PEFT matches or surpasses standard SSL methods with VFMs is practically significant: it suggests practitioners may not need complex SSL pipelines when strong pre-trained backbones are available, and redirects SSL research toward exploiting the complementary behaviors of diverse foundation models. The proposed V-PET baseline is simple (single-round self-training with mean-label ensembling, ~1.16× runtime overhead) and provides a competitive reference point for future VFM-era SSL work. Limitations include restriction to image classification, a small set of VFMs and PEFT methods, and the open question of whether these insights transfer to dense prediction tasks like segmentation and detection.
