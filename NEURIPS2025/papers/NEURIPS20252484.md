## Problem

The prevailing trend in automated theorem proving relies on RL-based large-scale training of LLMs (e.g., DeepSeek-Prover-V2, Kimina-Prover), while the earlier inference-only Draft-Sketch-Prove (DSP) framework was significantly outperformed. This paper asks whether careful neuro-symbolic coordination of existing off-the-shelf reasoning models and tactic step provers can revive DSP to competitive accuracy without any model training or finetuning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252484/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single formal Lean 4 theorem statement as input, requiring no human informal proof, and produces a fully verified Lean 4 proof checked by the Lean 4 compiler (v4.17.0-rc1) with Mathlib4. Intermediate artifacts include a concise natural-language proof draft and a formal sketch structured as a hierarchy of subgoals, each annotated with explicit hypothesis hints via a prove_with placeholder. The workflow is executed multiple times (pass@k) to exploit LLM output randomness, and an ensemble setting chains different model configurations until a proof is found or a timeout occurs.

## Method

DSP+ enhances the three-phase Draft-Sketch-Prove framework with fine-grained, cross-phase neuro-symbolic coordination, unlike the original DSP's coarse-grained, phase-independent design. In the draft phase, a reasoning model (QwQ-32B or DeepSeek-R1) generates concise natural-language subgoals from the formal statement, with thinking tokens filtered to prevent overloading downstream phases. In the sketch phase, an LLM (DeepSeek-V3) autoformalizes the draft into Lean 4 subgoals with explicit hypothesis annotations, and syntactically invalid lines are automatically masked by parsing the code into an indentation-based tree and pruning error subtrees. In the proving phase, Aesop's symbolic tree search is tightly integrated with a step prover (BFS-Prover) via the Lean Copilot framework, so each node in the tactic tree can be generated by either the symbolic engine or the neural step prover, with the search budget split between hypothesis-guided and full-context strategies. The entire pipeline is inference-only, and an ensemble setting with multiple draft/sketch model combinations further boosts accumulative accuracy.

## Results

DSP+ (inference-only, no training) achieves 80.7% on miniF2F-test with DeepSeek-R1-671B as draft model at 1024 workflow attempts, matching Kimina-Prover-Preview-72B (80.7% at 8192 passes) while using fewer total inference tokens. The ensemble setting accumulates to 83.6% miniF2F-test, 33.9% ProofNet-test, 25/644 PutnamBench, and 45% miniF2F/IMO. Notably, DSP+ proves imo_2019_p1, an IMO problem unsolved by any prior method, and discovers 8 wrongly formalized statements in miniF2F-test.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DSP+ (R1-V3-BFS) at 1024 passes achieves 80.7% miniF2F-test, exactly matching Kimina-Prover-Preview-72B at 8192 passes (80.7%) but with substantially fewer inference tokens (3.6k+0.8k vs. 10k per pass). Against DeepSeek-Prover-V2-671B (86.6% at 1024, 82.0% at 8192), DSP+ trails by 5.9–11.9 points on miniF2F-test, though the ensemble setting (83.6%) narrows the gap to 3.0 points at the 8192-equivalent budget. On ProofNet, DSP+ ensemble (33.9%) is competitive with DeepSeek-Prover-V2-671B at 128 passes (33.6%) but below its 1024-pass result (37.1%). On PutnamBench, DSP+ ensemble (25/644) is far below DeepSeek-Prover-V2-671B (49/658 at 1024) but exceeds all other methods except DeepSeek-Prover-V2. Against tree-search baselines, DSP+ outperforms BFS-Prover-7B (70.8%), HunyuanProver-7B (68.4%), and InternLM2.5-StepProver-7B (65.9%) by 10–15 points on miniF2F-test. The original DSP (38.9%) is surpassed by over 40 points, demonstrating the impact of the neuro-symbolic enhancements.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper revives the original Draft, Sketch, and Prove (DSP) framework (Jiang et al., ICLR 2023), which had been significantly outperformed by the wave of RL-based large-scale training approaches (DeepSeek-Prover-V2, Kimina-Prover, BFS-Prover, etc.). The key conceptual departure is that DSP+ replaces the coarse-grained, phase-independent neural-symbolic workflow of the original DSP with fine-grained, integrated neuro-symbolic enhancements: reasoning models with filtered thinking tokens for drafting, explicit hypothesis specification and error-line masking for sketching, and tight built-in integration of step provers (BFS-Prover) with Aesop tree search for proving. It positions itself explicitly as a counter-trend to the prevailing RL-training paradigm, demonstrating that careful coordination of off-the-shelf models can substitute for expensive training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical implication is significant: DSP+ achieves 80.7% on miniF2F-test without any model training or finetuning, using fewer inference tokens than Kimina-Prover-Preview-72B, offering a low-cost, modular alternative for theorem proving that can be easily adapted as new reasoning models emerge. The discovery of eight wrongly formalized problems in miniF2F and the proof of imo_2019_p1 (unsolved by all prior work) demonstrate the framework's value as a diagnostic and exploratory tool. Open questions include whether the underexplored design space (optimal model combinations, ensemble strategies) can close the remaining gap to DeepSeek-Prover-V2-671B (86.6%), and whether the approach generalizes beyond Lean 4 and Mathlib to other proof assistants and domains.
