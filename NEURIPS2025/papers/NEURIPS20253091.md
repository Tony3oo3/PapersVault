## Problem

Existing global image geo-localization methods rely on data-driven cross-modal alignment between images and GPS coordinates, neglecting the logical relationships among fine-grained geographical indicators within images. They also lack interpretable reasoning mechanisms and are constrained by closed-domain assumptions (candidate databases or fixed grids), limiting open-ended coordinate prediction. Furthermore, prior benchmarks evaluate only terminal prediction accuracy while ignoring the quality of intermediate reasoning chains.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253091/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single ground-view RGB image as input and produces a structured output consisting of a chain-of-thought reasoning block (enclosed in think/answer tags) followed by predicted geographic coordinates in (latitude, longitude) format. The CoT is expected to progressively identify explicit indicators (landmarks, text, symbols) and implicit indicators (architectural style, vegetation, climate) before narrowing down the location. Evaluation uses a threshold metric reporting the percentage of predictions within 1 km, 25 km, 200 km, 750 km, and 2500 km of ground truth, plus a CoT-quality score combining recall, RefCLIPScore, and BertScore.

## Method

GRE builds on Qwen2.5-VL-7B and applies a three-stage post-training pipeline: (1) cold-start supervised fine-tuning on GRE30K-CoT (20k GPT-o3-generated CoT samples filtered by regex matching and manual annotation) to establish structured reasoning patterns; (2) RL Stage I using GRPO on GRE30K-Judge (10k yes/no judgment tasks) with a binary accuracy reward plus a format reward enforcing think/answer tags; (3) RL Stage II using GRPO on 170k seed images with a continuous geodesic-distance-based reward R_geo = 2/(1+exp(d/θ)) that smoothly scales with prediction error. The GRPO objective clips the probability ratio to [1−ε, 1+ε] and adds KL regularization against the reference policy, while the threshold θ is adaptively refined during training to push the model toward finer-grained accuracy.

## Results

On Im2GPS3k, GRE achieves 11.3% (1 km), 35.3% (25 km), 51.7% (200 km), 69.3% (750 km), and 85.7% (2500 km) threshold accuracy, surpassing the previous SOTA GeoCLIP (10.8/31.1/48.7/67.6/83.2) by +0.5/+4.2/+3.0/+1.7/+2.5 percentage points respectively. On the more challenging GWS15k benchmark, GRE reaches 0.9/4.1/18.9/54.8/78.3%, outperforming GeoCLIP (0.6/3.1/16.9/45.7/74.1) with gains up to +4.2 pp at the 2500 km threshold. On GREval-Bench, GRE attains the highest CoT-quality score (59.54) among all evaluated models, including the much larger Qwen2.5VL-32B (55.56). Notably, GRE achieves these results using only 5% of the MP-16 training data, whereas prior methods use the full dataset.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GRE outperforms all 14 baselines across every distance threshold on both Im2GPS3k and GWS15k. Against the strongest prior alignment-based method GeoCLIP [57], GRE gains +0.5 pp at 1 km and +2.5 pp at 2500 km on Im2GPS3k, and +4.2 pp at 2500 km on GWS15k. Among reasoning-based VLM baselines, GeoReasoner [27] (9.9/33.8/46.1/65.3/80.3) and SeekWorld [55] (4.3/29.8/44.9/59.1/67.3) are both clearly surpassed, with SeekWorld trailing by up to 18.4 pp at the 2500 km threshold. General-purpose MLLMs without geo-specific training (Qwen2.5-VL-7B, LLaVA-v1.5-7B, Molmo-D-7B) show substantially weaker performance, with Qwen2.5-VL-7B—the base model—improving from 3.2% to 11.3% at 1 km after GRE's training pipeline. On GREval-Bench, GRE also beats the larger Qwen2.5VL-32B (5.45/23.12/37.41/54.33/65.00) and InternVL3-8B (1.32/7.50/14.34/25.90/35.38) on all spatial thresholds while achieving the highest CoT-quality score (59.54 vs. 55.56 and 36.48 respectively). Traditional classification/retrieval methods (PlaNet, CPLaNet, ISNs, Translocator, GeoDecoder, kNN) are all dominated, with the best among them (CPlaNet at 10.2% 1 km) still trailing GRE by 1.1 pp at the finest granularity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper transplants the DeepSeek-R1/GRPO training paradigm (cold-start SFT followed by rule-based RL with verifiable rewards) from the LLM reasoning literature into the multimodal geo-localization setting, a domain previously dominated by cross-modal alignment methods (GeoCLIP, PlaNet, Translocator) and retrieval-based approaches. It departs from prior VLM geo-localization work (GeoReasoner, SeekWorld) by introducing a structured two-stage RL pipeline with geo-specific reward functions (geodesic-distance-based and format-based) rather than relying on end-to-end alignment or single-stage prompting. The dataset construction leverages GPT-o3 for CoT generation, positioning the work at the intersection of synthetic data generation and RL-based post-training for vision-language models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The GRE Suite provides the first integrated framework (dataset + model + benchmark) specifically targeting reasoning quality in geo-localization, with GREval-Bench offering a CoT-quality metric that prior benchmarks lacked. The finding that a 7B model trained on only 5% of MP-16 outperforms alignment-based methods trained on the full dataset suggests reasoning-based approaches may be more data-efficient for geo-localization, though the absolute improvements over GeoCLIP remain modest (1–4 percentage points). Key limitations include high compute costs (8×H20 GPUs, ~24K GFLOPs for the language model alone), dependence on GPT-o3 API for data generation, and the ablation revealing that RL Stage I (judgment tasks) can actually degrade performance when the reward type mismatches the test task — an open question for future work on reward design in multimodal RL.
