## Problem

Existing multi-agent imitation learning methods assume access to large volumes of high-quality expert demonstrations, which are impractical to obtain due to the combinatorial explosion of joint state-action spaces. This paper tackles the first instance of offline multi-agent IL from entirely unlabeled, mixed-quality trajectories containing both expert and suboptimal behaviors, where no prior quality annotations are available.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255471/cropped_page7_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single unlabeled dataset of shuffled trajectories (e.g., 200 expert + 1000 poor, combined and randomized) from cooperative multi-agent environments, along with LLM-generated pairwise preference labels over sampled trajectory pairs. It produces decentralized local policies for each agent, which are provably factorizable into the globally optimal joint policy. The input is consumed in two sequential stages: first for preference-based reward recovery and trajectory ranking, then for DICE-based occupancy-matching IL.

## Method

MisoDICE is a two-stage framework. Phase 1 uses GPT-4o to generate initial trajectory-pair preferences, trains O-MAPL to learn a soft Q-function from those preferences, recovers per-state-action rewards via R ≈ Q − γV, and ranks trajectories to partition the unlabeled data into expert and suboptimal subsets. Phase 2 formulates a DICE-style occupancy-matching objective (KL to expert + α·KL to union distribution) over the joint policy, then decomposes the global value function into local value functions via a linear mixing network under CTDE. The linear mixer is critical: it preserves convexity of the training objective in both mixing parameters and local values, and enables a closed-form policy extraction where each local policy is a softmax over local Q-functions weighted by the behavior policy. A discriminator-based occupancy ratio estimator (also decomposed via a linear mixer) replaces the intractable joint-state ratio, and a global-local consistency theorem guarantees that independently optimized local policies compose to the globally optimal joint policy.

## Results

On SMACv2, MisoDICE achieves the highest final average return across nearly all 19 tasks tested. Headline results include 16.4±1.3 on 2c_vs_64zg (vs. 14.6±1.0 for the best baseline MARL-SL), 5.8±0.8 on corridor (vs. 4.7±0.6 for VDN), and 13.5±0.5 on Protoss 20_vs_20 (vs. 13.1±0.4 for MARL-SL). The advantage is most pronounced on tasks with fewer agents or more complex dynamics, where MisoDICE gains 1.5–3.7 points over the strongest baseline. Ablation on expert dataset size (k) shows peak performance around k=200 trajectories, confirming the labeling pipeline's effectiveness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MisoDICE outperforms all three primary baselines across every SMACv2 task. Against BC (all three β variants), MisoDICE gains 2.8–4.3 points on the hardest tasks (e.g., 16.4 vs. 12.6 on 2c_vs_64zg; 5.8 vs. 4.3 on corridor), confirming that simple re-weighting of expert vs. mixed data is insufficient. Against OMAPL (end-to-end preference-based MARL), MisoDICE improves by 1.5–3.6 points (e.g., 16.4 vs. 12.2 on 2c_vs_64zg; 13.5 vs. 11.4 on Protoss 20v20), demonstrating the benefit of the separate DICE-based IL phase over direct preference optimization. Against INDD (independent per-agent DemoDICE), MisoDICE consistently leads by 0.5–2.4 points (e.g., 16.4 vs. 14.6 on 2c_vs_64zg; 10.2 vs. 9.7 on Zerg 10v10), highlighting the necessity of coordinated value decomposition over independent learning. The stronger MARL-SL and VDN baselines (which incorporate MARL optimization and value decomposition, respectively) remain below MisoDICE by 0.4–1.8 points, isolating the contribution of the learnable mixing architecture and the mixed-quality DICE formulation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DICE occupancy-matching framework (Kostrikov et al., 2019) and its mixed-quality extension DemoDICE (Kim et al., 2021) from single-agent to cooperative multi-agent settings, introducing a value-decomposition and mixing architecture under CTDE to handle the exponential joint state-action space. It also repurposes O-MAPL (Bui et al., 2025), a preference-based MARL algorithm, not as a final policy learner but as a reward-recovery and trajectory-ranking tool in a two-stage pipeline with LLM-generated initial preferences. The key departure from prior multi-agent IL methods (MIFQ, multi-agent GAIL/AIRL) is the explicit handling of unlabeled, heterogeneous-quality demonstrations rather than assuming access to curated expert data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap: acquiring large volumes of expert demonstrations in multi-agent environments is infeasible due to combinatorial state-action explosion, yet prior multi-agent IL methods all assume high-quality expert data. The convexity guarantee from linear mixing and the global-local policy consistency result provide theoretical grounding that is absent in most CTDE-based IL work. Key limitations include restriction to cooperative settings, dependence on LLMs for labeling (which fails in domains like MaMuJoCo lacking semantic context), and the expressiveness trade-off of requiring linear mixing for convexity. Extending to competitive environments and finding labeling strategies for non-semantic domains remain open.
