## Problem

Multivariate time-series anomaly detection faces three intertwined challenges: severe class imbalance from rare real-world anomalies, the inability of static graph structures to capture dynamically shifting inter-variable correlations under anomalous conditions, and the 'anomaly reconstruction' problem where reconstruction-based methods overfit to normal patterns and assign low errors to subtle anomalies.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252841/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multivariate time-series windows of shape d×T (e.g., 51×100 for SWaT, 25×100 for SMAP, 55×100 for MSL), where d is the number of sensor channels and T is the sliding-window length. It produces a single anomaly probability score in [0,1] per window via a binary classifier. Notably, during training the model simultaneously processes both the original normal window and its self-perturbed reconstruction through dual graph structures, stacking their spatio-temporal features before the final prediction head.

## Method

SPAGD is an end-to-end framework with three coupled components. First, a Transformer-based autoencoder reconstructs normal time series, and the reconstruction deviations (which shrink progressively during training) serve as adaptive pseudo-anomalous samples that alleviate class imbalance without external data. Second, an anomaly-aware graph construction module builds an initial top-K cosine-similarity graph for normal data, then dynamically boosts edge weights involving variables whose reconstruction residuals exceed a threshold, creating a second 'anomalous-candidate' graph that reflects disrupted inter-variable correlations. Third, a spatio-temporal detection module applies GAT layers on both graphs in parallel (dual-graph message propagation), partitions the resulting spatial features into C chunks for temporal convolution, and feeds the concatenated representations to a binary classifier trained with cross-entropy loss. The entire pipeline is jointly optimized with a weighted sum of the reconstruction loss and the classification loss, so that the quality of pseudo-anomalous samples and the detector's decision boundary co-evolve during training.

## Results

SPAGD achieves the best average score (52.11%) across all three datasets and three metrics (AUC, AUPRC, F1), outperforming the strongest baselines. On SWaT, SPAGD reaches 86.30% AUC and 78.77% F1; on SMAP, 62.38% AUC and 18.15% AUPRC; and on MSL, 66.50% AUC and 30.89% F1. The method outperforms the runner-up baselines (COUTA, Deep IF, USAD) by 3.35%, 1.49%, and 4.83% AUC on their respective best datasets, and surpasses all 16 baselines on the average metric across all three datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPAGD outperforms all 16 baselines on the average score across the three datasets. On SWaT, SPAGD (86.30% AUC) beats the closest competitor COUTA (82.95%) by 3.35 points, and also surpasses Deep-SVDD (82.55%) and TcnED (82.38%). On SMAP, SPAGD (62.38% AUC) edges out Deep IF (60.89%) by 1.49 points and GDN (58.09%) by 4.29 points. On MSL, SPAGD (66.50% AUC) leads USAD (61.67%) by 4.83 points and COPOD (60.56%) by 5.94 points. Notably, SPAGD also dominates on AUPRC across all three datasets (77.20%, 18.15%, 21.45%), indicating stronger precision in identifying true anomalies. Reconstruction-based methods like TranAD and AnomalyTrans are consistently outperformed, which the authors attribute to SPAGD's classifier-based approach avoiding the 'anomaly reconstruction' problem. Static graph-based methods such as GDN and TcnED are also surpassed, highlighting the benefit of SPAGD's dynamic anomaly-aware graph construction.

## Relation to Prior Work

SPAGD synthesizes ideas from three distinct threads in multivariate TSAD: reconstruction-based methods (USAD, TranAD, AnomalyTrans), graph-based methods (GDN, MST-GAT), and temporal convolution approaches (TimesNet, TcnED). Its key conceptual departure is repurposing the reconstruction process itself as a data generator: rather than using reconstruction error as an anomaly score (the standard paradigm), SPAGD treats the reconstruction residuals as pseudo-anomalous training signals for a separate classifier. The anomaly-aware graph construction extends static graph strategies (GDN, GRELEN) by making the adjacency matrix a function of evolving reconstruction residuals, and the dual-graph GAT message propagation is a natural but effective unification of spatial and temporal modeling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The self-perturbation mechanism offers a principled way to address the class-imbalance problem without external anomalous data or hand-crafted augmentation, and the ablation confirming that a purely reconstruction-based scoring (SP w/ Recon) degrades significantly validates the 'anomaly reconstruction' problem as a real limitation of the dominant paradigm. However, the method is evaluated on only three datasets (all from NASA/industrial telemetry), assumes a homogeneous sensing landscape, and the mutual-improvement claim of end-to-end training would benefit from more diverse evaluation. Open questions include whether the self-perturbation strategy generalizes to highly heterogeneous or streaming settings, and whether the dynamic graph construction adds meaningful value over simpler adaptive alternatives at scale.
