## Problem

Existing multi-modal object ReID methods assume that query and gallery images are captured under strictly aligned modality conditions (e.g., all cameras simultaneously provide RGB, NIR, and TIR). In real-world deployments, heterogeneous sensor availability means queries and galleries often come from different modalities, a modality-mismatched scenario that current methods cannot handle. This paper tackles the any-to-any ReID problem, where retrieval must work across arbitrary combinations of available modalities.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251418/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts any subset of RGB, NIR, and TIR images (resized to 256×128 or 128×256 depending on dataset) for both query and gallery samples, with missing modalities represented as zero vectors in a fixed 6-component feature vector accompanied by a binary availability mask. The output is a scalar similarity score computed as the average of a modality-specific component (dot products between same-modality specific features, masked for joint availability) and a modality-shared component (all pairwise dot products among available shared features), which drives the retrieval ranking.

## Method

MDReID replaces the standard single [CLS] token in a CLIP-Base ViT backbone with two learnable tokens per modality—a modality-specific token and a modality-shared token—so that multi-layer self-attention naturally disentangles each modality's representation into a transferable shared component and a modality-unique specific component. A Modality-aware Metric Learning (MML) strategy further refines this decoupling through two losses: a Representation Orthogonality Loss (ROL) that minimizes squared error between the computed 6×6 pairwise similarity matrix and a target matrix enforcing orthogonality between specific components and mutual alignment among shared components, and a Knowledge Discrepancy Loss (KDL) that uses a triplet-style formulation to ensure the combined shared+specific representation is more discriminative than either component alone. The total training objective combines label-smoothed cross-entropy, triplet loss, and the MML loss (weighted by w₁=1.5 and w₂=5.25). At inference, the fixed-size zero-padded feature vector and availability mask enable a unified similarity computation that gracefully handles any modality presence pattern without requiring modality prediction or reconstruction.

## Results

On the modality-matched (RNT-to-RNT) setting, MDReID achieves 82.1% mAP / 85.2% R-1 on RGBNT201, 85.3% mAP / 95.6% R-1 on RGBNT100, and 51.0% mAP / 68.9% R-1 on MSVR310, outperforming the strongest prior method (TOP-ReID) by 9.8%, 4.1%, and 11.5% mAP respectively. In modality-mismatched scenarios (e.g., R-to-N, RT-to-NT), MDReID gains an average of 3.4%, 11.8%, and 10.9% mAP over TOP-ReID on the three datasets. The model also requires only 57.2M parameters and 22.3G FLOPs, substantially fewer than TOP-ReID (278.2M / 34.5G) and EDITOR (117.5M / 38.6G).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MDReID outperforms all 27 compared baselines across all three datasets in the modality-matched setting. The closest competitor, TOP-ReID [12], is surpassed by 9.8% mAP on RGBNT201 (82.1 vs 72.3), 4.1% on RGBNT100 (85.3 vs 81.2), and 15.1% on MSVR310 (51.0 vs 35.9). EDITOR [11], designed exclusively for modality-matched retrieval, collapses in mismatched scenarios (e.g., 2.8% mAP on R-to-N for RGBNT201), while MDReID maintains 16.6% mAP in the same setting. In modality-mismatched evaluation (Table 3), MDReID improves average mAP over TOP-ReID by 3.4% (RGBNT201), 11.8% (RGBNT100), and 10.9% (MSVR310), with the largest single-scenario gain being +17.7% mAP on RGBNT100 RT-to-N (39.6 vs 21.9). Even against a dedicated cross-modality model (DEEN [50]) trained specifically for R-to-N, MDReID achieves 3.5% higher mAP (16.6 vs 11.5) while remaining a single unified model. In terms of efficiency, MDReID uses only 57.2M parameters and 22.3G FLOPs versus 278.2M/34.5G for TOP-ReID, and extracts features ~22× faster (3.27s vs 71.78s on RGBNT201 RNT-to-RNT).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the multi-spectral object ReID line of work (TOP-ReID, EDITOR, HTT) by departing from the assumption that query and gallery share identical modality availability. Rather than following TOP-ReID's reconstruction-based strategy for missing modalities, MDReID draws on the ill-posedness argument from RLE to motivate an explicit decoupling of modality-shared and modality-specific features via dual learnable tokens in a ViT backbone (CLIP-Base). The modality-aware metric learning (orthogonality + knowledge discrepancy losses) is a novel training objective not present in prior multi-modal ReID methods, which typically rely on standard triplet or cross-entropy losses alone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to handle arbitrary query-gallery modality combinations in object ReID, addressing a practical gap where real deployments rarely guarantee matched sensor availability. The authors acknowledge that current accuracy remains insufficient for production use and that existing datasets are limited in scale and modality diversity, leaving open whether the approach generalizes to larger, more heterogeneous settings. The significant efficiency advantage (57.2M params, 22.3G FLOPs vs. 278.2M/34.5G for TOP-ReID) while outperforming on accuracy suggests the decoupling paradigm may be more broadly applicable than reconstruction-based approaches for cross-modal retrieval tasks.
