## Problem

Learned Image Compression (LIC) models trained on clean natural images suffer substantial rate-distortion degradation when compressing degraded inputs (rain, haze, noise) because the latent distributions deviate from the clean-image priors assumed by the entropy model. The restoration-then-compression paradigm partially addresses this but still yields suboptimal compression because restored images retain distributional divergence from natural images, and deploying separate restoration models is impractical under resource constraints.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250120/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single degraded RGB image (rain, haze, or Gaussian noise at varying intensities) as input and produces a compressed bitstream plus a reconstructed image, operating on 256×256 patches. The IB-LRU module is a lightweight plug-in (4.6M parameters) inserted at the encoder side of a frozen LIC backbone (Bmshj2018, TIC, or MLIC++), requiring no modification to the backbone's pre-trained parameters. At inference, the probabilistic prompt is sampled from the PPG's posterior and passed through the DAGM module to modulate intermediate encoder features before the standard quantization and entropy coding pipeline.

## Method

The core contribution is the IB-LRU scheme, which unifies compression and restoration by aligning degraded-image latent distributions with clean-image priors through prompt-guided encoder modulation. A VAE-based Probabilistic Prompt Generator (PPG) encodes each degraded image into a multivariate Gaussian distribution (mean μ_p and per-dimension variance σ_p²) over a compact prompt vector, replacing the factorized prompt vectors used in prior work like PromptIR. The Degradation-Adaptive Gating Modulation (DAGM) module uses μ_p to produce basis scale/shift maps and σ_p² to compute a gating signal that controls the strength of the affine modulation applied to frozen backbone features. An Information Bottleneck constraint, derived via variational upper and lower bounds, regularizes the prompt to retain only degradation-relevant information while suppressing redundant image context, with the final loss combining the standard rate-distortion objective and a KL divergence term between the approximate posterior and a standard Gaussian prior.

## Results

IB-LRU achieves average bpp reductions of 27.1% (light rain), 51.6% (heavy rain), 18.2% (noise σ=15), 34.6% (noise σ=25), and 64.9% (noise σ=50) across three LIC backbones (Bmshj2018, TIC, MLIC++) compared to the unmodified backbones. In terms of efficiency, IB-LRU adds only 4.6M parameters and runs at 109.5 ms inference, versus 8.93–35.59M parameters and 349–483 ms for restoration-then-compression baselines. In the supplementary comparison against the joint denoising-compression method JDC-CN, IB-LRU achieves 28.23/28.07/26.53 dB PSNR (noise 15/25/50) versus 24.71/24.20/23.01 dB, a consistent ~3.5 dB gain.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IB-LRU outperforms all four restoration-then-compression baselines (AirNet, Restormer, PromptIR, MOCE-IR) on both parameter count and inference speed, requiring only 4.6M extra parameters and 109.5 ms per image versus 8.93–35.59M parameters and 349–483 ms for the cascaded approaches, while achieving competitive or superior perceptual quality (PSNR) at lower bpp. Against the joint denoising-compression method JDC-CN [47], IB-LRU achieves a consistent ~3.5 dB PSNR advantage across all three noise levels (e.g., 28.23 vs. 24.71 dB at σ=15), demonstrating that the probabilistic prompt + IB constraint generalizes better even in a single-degradation setting. The paper also shows that the probabilistic prompt design surpasses the factorized prompt from PromptIR [6] in both PSNR (e.g., 28.01 vs. 27.95 dB on Rain_L) and generalization to unseen mixed degradations (24.09 vs. 19.24 dB on Haze+Rain), while using 4.6M vs. 16.0M parameters.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: learned image compression (LIC) with hyperprior-based entropy models (Balle et al., TIC, MLIC++), prompt learning adapted from NLP to vision (CoOp, PromptIR), and the Information Bottleneck principle (Tishby; Alemi et al.'s VIB). The key conceptual departure is from the 'restoration-then-compression' paradigm, which cascades a multi-task restoration model before a frozen LIC codec. Instead, IB-LRU introduces a 'compressor-as-restoration' paradigm where a lightweight VAE-based Probabilistic Prompt Generator and a Degradation-Adaptive Gating Modulation module steer the frozen LIC encoder's latent representations toward clean-image distributions, with the IB constraint ensuring prompts encode only degradation characteristics rather than redundant image content. Unlike PromptIR's factorized prompt vectors, the probabilistic design models each degradation as a distribution, yielding more compact and discriminative representations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical value is clear: a 4.6M-parameter, 109 ms plug-in that improves compression efficiency under rain, noise, and haze without modifying any LIC backbone parameters, compared to 25–35M-parameter restoration cascades. The finding that restoration-then-compression underperforms because restored images retain distributional divergence from natural images (defeating the entropy model's prior) is a useful caution for practitioners who might naively cascade a restorer. Open questions include generalization beyond the three tested degradation types, the observed bpp increase under haze (where smoothing makes degraded images already well-matched to the LIC prior), and whether the IB-constrained prompt formulation extends to more complex or compound degradations. The method's reliance on a frozen backbone also limits its applicability to architectures whose encoder features are amenable to affine modulation.
