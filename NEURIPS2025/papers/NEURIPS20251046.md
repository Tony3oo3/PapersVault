## Problem

Existing fine-tuning-based jailbreak attacks rely on explicitly or implicitly harmful QA pairs, making them detectable by moderation models, while the closest stealthy alternative (AOA) proves fragile—shuffling its 10 training samples drops ASR from 78% to 8%. The paper asks whether LLMs can be jailbroken using only completely benign QA pairs, and if so, what mechanism allows benign data to substitute for harmful data in compromising safety alignment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251046/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The attacker uploads exactly 10 benign QA pairs through a Fine-tuning-as-a-Service (FaaS) interface (e.g., OpenAI's fine-tuning API or dashboard), requiring no white-box access to model parameters. The same 10 benign questions are reused in both stages: in Stage 1 their answers are replaced with a single identical refusal sentence, and in Stage 2 they are restored to their original standard answers. The output is a fine-tuned model that produces compliant, detailed answers to harmful questions (evaluated on AdvBench and five additional benchmarks), while the fine-tuning dataset itself scores a Harmful Score of 1—indistinguishable from genuinely benign data by moderation models.

## Method

The attack is a two-stage supervised fine-tuning procedure that exploits overfitting to trigger catastrophic forgetting of safety alignment. In Stage 1, the LLM is fine-tuned on 10 benign QA pairs whose answers are all the same refusal sentence, deliberately driving the model into a sharp, narrow minimum in the loss landscape where it rigidly outputs the refusal regardless of input. In Stage 2, the same 10 questions are fine-tuned with their original standard benign answers; because the overfitted model sits in a sharp valley, even these benign updates produce large parameter gradients that push the model away from the refusal behavior, effectively erasing the association between harmful questions and refusal. The paper formalizes this via loss-landscape visualization and gradient cosine-similarity analysis, showing that for highly overfitted models the gradient direction on benign data closely aligns with that on harmful data, making benign fine-tuning a functional substitute for malicious fine-tuning. The approach achieves ~95% average ASR across ten LLMs (comparable to malicious fine-tuning at ~97%) while maintaining maximum stealth, and it bypasses a token-wise loss defense mechanism.

## Results

Across ten LLMs (Llama2-7b through GPT-4.1), the proposed two-stage benign fine-tuning attack achieves an average Attack Success Rate (ASR) of 94.84% and Harmful Score (HS) of 4.48 on AdvBench, closely matching malicious fine-tuning (97.25% ASR, 4.87 HS) while using entirely benign data. The attack's fine-tuning dataset receives a Harmful Score of 1 from a GPT-4.1-mini moderation model, identical to genuinely benign data, confirming maximum stealth. Ablation shows that removing Stage-1 (overfitting) drops ASR to near-zero (e.g., Llama2-7b: 92.57% → 4.42%), confirming overfitting is the critical mechanism.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the AOA attack (Qi et al., ICLR 2024), the proposed method achieves 94.84% vs. 32.23% average ASR — a 62.6-point gap — and the paper further shows that shuffling the AOA dataset alone drops its ASR from 78% to 8%, exposing AOA's fragility. Against Encryption fine-tuning (Halawi et al., ICML 2024), the proposed method dominates with 94.84% vs. 14.66% ASR; encryption fails on weaker models because they cannot learn the cipher, and even on GPT-4.1 it reaches only 72.12%. Against Malicious fine-tuning (Yi et al., ACL Findings 2024), the proposed method is slightly lower in raw ASR (94.84% vs. 97.25%) and HS (4.48 vs. 4.87), but critically uses a dataset with Harmful Score 1 (indistinguishable from benign) versus explicitly harmful data, making it undetectable by moderation models. Against Indirect malicious fine-tuning (Ganguli et al., 2022), the proposed method more than doubles the ASR (94.84% vs. 40.75%). Against Irrelevant/Alpaca fine-tuning (Taori et al., 2023), the proposed method achieves 94.84% vs. 38.68% ASR, showing that the overfitting mechanism is far more effective than simply fine-tuning on large benign corpora.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the fine-tuning attack lineage (AOA, encryption-based, and malicious fine-tuning) by identifying overfitting dynamics rather than dataset harmfulness as the operative mechanism for breaking safety alignment. It repurposes the continual-learning concept of 'spurious forgetting' and loss-landscape sharpness to explain why benign data, when applied to an overfitted model, induces the same catastrophic forgetting of refusal behavior as harmful data. The AOA attack's fragility under shuffling (78%→8% ASR) is used as motivation to argue that prior explanations (identity shifting) are incomplete, redirecting attention to answer similarity and overfitting as the true driver.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that content-based moderation of fine-tuning data is fundamentally insufficient—because the training process itself (sharp loss minima from overfitting) can be weaponized with entirely benign inputs—shifts the defense burden from data inspection to monitoring training dynamics (e.g., loss dropping to near-zero). Practical limitations remain: hyperparameters must be tuned per model, the jailbroken model produces repetitive follow-up answers, and the attack requires access to FaaS. An open question the paper raises is whether monitoring the characteristic sharp loss collapse in Stage-1 can serve as a reliable detector without false-positiving on legitimate small-dataset fine-tuning.
