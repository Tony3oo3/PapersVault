## Problem

While LoRA matches full fine-tuning performance on many downstream tasks, it remains unclear whether the two methods learn structurally equivalent solutions or fundamentally different ones. No prior work has compared the spectral properties of weight matrices between LoRA and full fine-tuning on tasks where they achieve matched performance, leaving the mechanism by which LoRA achieves comparable results—and the behavioral differences that do emerge—unexplained.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255808/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The analysis takes a pre-trained weight matrix W₀ and its fine-tuned counterpart W_tuned (from either LoRA or full fine-tuning), computes the SVD of each, and compares the top-k singular vectors (typically k=10) via maximum cosine similarity against all pre-trained singular vectors using a tunable threshold ε. The primary outputs are the count of intruder dimensions per weight matrix (summed across the model), forgetting scores measured as pre-training distribution loss or OOD benchmark accuracy, and spectral similarity matrices visualizing the mapping between pre-trained and fine-tuned singular vectors.

## Method

The paper introduces the concept of intruder dimensions: high-ranking singular vectors in LoRA-fine-tuned weight matrices whose maximum cosine similarity to every pre-trained singular vector falls below a threshold ε, indicating they are spectrally novel directions absent from the pre-trained model. Full fine-tuning, by contrast, retains the pre-trained spectral structure with near one-to-one singular vector mapping and no intruder dimensions. To establish causality, the authors intervene post-fine-tuning by scaling the singular value of the top intruder dimension in each weight matrix (W = W₀ + ΔW + (λ−1)uᵢσᵢvᵢᵀ), showing that reducing λ from 1 to 0.3–0.7 yields 25–50% reductions in forgetting with <1% test accuracy loss, while scaling non-intruder neighbors produces the opposite effect. They further demonstrate that intruder dimensions accumulate across sequentially fine-tuned tasks in a continual learning setting, causing LoRA to degrade faster than full fine-tuning despite initially forgetting less on a single task.

## Results

LoRA-fine-tuned models contain high-ranking intruder dimensions (singular vectors with near-zero cosine similarity to all pre-trained singular vectors) in their top 10 SVD components, while full fine-tuning almost never does (even at ε=0.6–0.9). The number of intruder dimensions correlates extremely strongly with forgetting: Spearman ρ=0.971 (p≪0.001) for RoBERTa-base and ρ=0.59 (p=0.0006) for LLaMA2-7B. Causally scaling down the top intruder dimension (λ=0.3) on LLaMA2-7B fine-tuned on MetaMath (r=256) yields only a 0.1% drop in test accuracy but a 33.3% reduction in forgetting; on RoBERTa QQP (r=8, λ=0.7) it yields 0.0% test accuracy change and a 33.2% forgetting reduction. During continual learning on six sequential tasks, LoRA degrades substantially more than full fine-tuning due to accumulating intruder dimensions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is between LoRA and full fine-tuning: both achieve equivalent downstream task accuracy (e.g., RoBERTa MNLI: LoRA r=8 at 87.04% vs. full fine-tuning at 87.45%), but LoRA introduces intruder dimensions while full fine-tuning does not, and LoRA forgets less on single tasks (extending Biderman et al. 2024 to the equal-fit setting). However, during continual learning on six sequential tasks, LoRA degrades more rapidly than full fine-tuning because intruder dimensions accumulate across tasks. Among LoRA variants tested (AdaLoRA, LoRA+, PiSSA, VeRA), all exhibit intruder dimensions with similar characteristics to vanilla LoRA, confirming the phenomenon is not unique to the original parameterization. Setting α=2r (vs. α=8) significantly reduces intruder dimensions and improves generalization, particularly at higher ranks. Higher learning rates increase intruder dimensions and forgetting, with LoRA at large learning rates actually forgetting more than full fine-tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the intrinsic dimension hypothesis (Li et al., 2018; Aghajanyan et al., 2021) that motivated LoRA's low-rank parameterization, and on Biderman et al. (2024), who showed LoRA forgets less than full fine-tuning on math and code tasks. It departs from the implicit assumption that LoRA recovers an approximately equivalent solution to full fine-tuning by adopting SVD as an analytical lens (inspired by Sharma et al., 2024's rank-reduction work) to reveal that the two methods produce structurally distinct weight matrices. The paper also extends Biderman et al.'s forgetting finding to the equal-performance regime and provides a causal mechanism—'intruder dimensions'—that prior behavioral comparisons had not identified.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of intruder dimensions as the causal driver of forgetting offers a data-free model selection criterion (fewer intruders = better generalization) and a post-hoc remedy (scaling down intruder singular values reduces forgetting by ~33% with negligible task-performance loss). The continual learning result—that LoRA, which forgets less on a single task, degrades faster than full fine-tuning over sequential tasks due to intruder accumulation—has direct practical implications for multi-task adapter pipelines. Open questions include whether findings hold at larger model scales (the authors lack compute for >7B), whether the low-rank constraint is fundamentally responsible or whether architectural modifications could eliminate intruder dimensions while retaining efficiency, and how to optimally mitigate intruder accumulation in production continual-learning settings.
