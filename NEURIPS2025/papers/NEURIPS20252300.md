## Problem

In few-shot, large-batch Bayesian Optimization, the initialization batch critically shapes surrogate quality, yet practitioners rely on model-agnostic space-filling designs (Sobol, LHS) that neither reduce predictive uncertainty in optimization-relevant regions nor efficiently learn GP hyperparameters such as lengthscales. No principled criterion existed that simultaneously balances predictive uncertainty reduction with hyperparameter informativeness during initialization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252300/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

HIPE requires no observed data: it consumes only the GP model structure (kernel, ARD lengthscales, noise/signal variance), their hyperpriors, the search space, and a test distribution p* (typically uniform). It outputs a jointly optimized batch of q candidate points in the qD-dimensional input space, where the batch is selected to maximize the combined EPIG+BALD objective. The method is designed for the cold-start, few-shot regime (often B=2 total batches) where there is little opportunity to correct a poor initial design.

## Method

HIPE defines an acquisition function that linearly combines the EPIG objective (expected reduction in predictive entropy over the test distribution) with the BALD objective (mutual information between observed batch values and hyperparameters), weighted by β = EIG(y(x*); θ|D), the pre-computable mutual information between hyperparameters and test-point values. This choice of β is hyperparameter-free and balances the two terms according to their downstream effect on predictive uncertainty. Proposition 1 proves that for β=1, HIPE is exactly equivalent to maximizing the expected joint information gain over both test function values and hyperparameters. The practical implementation uses a parallel Monte Carlo estimator with M hyperparameter samples, T test locations, and N predictive posterior samples, yielding a deterministic, auto-differentiable objective amenable to joint batch optimization via multi-start L-BFGS-B in qD-dimensional space.

## Results

In batch active learning (4 batches, q=16, 100 seeds), HIPE is the only method that consistently ranks in the top two on both RMSE and NLL across all synthetic and LCBench surrogate tasks. In the two-shot BO setting (B=2, q=24) on noisy synthetic functions, HIPE achieves the best or second-best out-of-sample inference performance across all benchmarks including high-dimensional Hartmann variants with dummy variables. On five LCBench HPO tasks (200 seeds each), HIPE attains the highest final performance on four of five tasks (Fashion-MNIST, MiniBooNE, Higgs, Car) and remains competitive on Segment. On the 40D SVM task, HIPE infers lengthscales for the two relevant dimensions nearly half an order of magnitude smaller than Sobol initialization, demonstrating superior hyperparameter identification.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against BALD, HIPE matches or exceeds performance on NLL (model calibration) while substantially outperforming on RMSE, since BALD's axis-aligned query clustering sacrifices space-filling coverage. Against NIPV, HIPE is competitive on RMSE but clearly outperforms on NLL and hyperparameter identification; NIPV's performance degrades specifically on tasks where accurate lengthscale inference is critical (e.g., high-dimensional Hartmann with dummy variables). Against Sobol (scrambled quasi-random), HIPE substantially outperforms across all metrics and tasks, confirming that uniform space-filling does not equate to model informativeness. Against LHS-Beta, which tailors pairwise distances to a Beta distribution for isotropic-kernel lengthscale learning, HIPE achieves better overall performance by jointly addressing both predictive uncertainty and multi-dimensional hyperparameter learning. All structured initialization methods (HIPE, BALD, NIPV, Sobol, LHS-Beta) substantially outperform pure random search, with HIPE consistently achieving the highest relative ranking across the full suite of experiments.

## Relation to Prior Work

HIPE unifies two previously separate active learning criteria—Expected Predictive Information Gain (EPIG, Bickford Smith et al. 2023) for reducing predictive uncertainty and BALD (Houlsby et al. 2011) for resolving hyperparameter uncertainty—into a single batch acquisition function with a theoretically grounded weighting. The paper departs from the dominant practice of model-agnostic space-filling initialization (scrambled Sobol, LHS) and from LHS-Beta (Zhang et al. 2019), which targets lengthscale learning through pairwise distance distributions, by explicitly optimizing a joint information-theoretic objective that accounts for both test-set prediction quality and hyperparameter identifiability. Proposition 1 provides a clean equivalence showing that the unweighted (β=1) combination recovers joint information gain over test values and hyperparameters, anchoring the method in Bayesian experimental design theory.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The few-shot, large-batch regime (e.g., parallel A/B tests, batch lab experiments) is increasingly common in practice, yet initialization has received little principled attention; this paper fills that gap with a hyperparameter-free method that is already integrated into BoTorch. Key limitations include computational cost from nested Monte Carlo estimation (making it less suitable for high-throughput BO) and GP-specificity, requiring re-derivation for other surrogate families. Open directions flagged by the authors include transfer-learning extensions via multi-task GPs, incorporation of expert priors through non-uniform test distributions, and multi-objective settings with heterogeneous surrogates.
