## Problem

Normalizing flows require invertible linear layers that balance expressiveness with efficient Jacobian determinant and matrix inversion computation. Existing designs (dense 1×1 convolutions, Woodbury transformations, butterfly layers) either retain O(n²) parameter complexity or incur O(n³) cost for determinant/inverse operations, creating a bottleneck for both training and sampling. This paper asks whether a structured matrix decomposition can simultaneously reduce parameter count and accelerate both critical operations.

## Input / Output

The model consumes RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10 and ImageNet-32, variable-size galaxy images) and produces per-dimension log-likelihood in bits-per-dimension (BPD) for density estimation, or generated images by inverting the flow from Gaussian noise. The linear layer operates on channel dimension n (e.g., 96 channels), decomposing the n×n weight matrix into m diagonal vectors and m−1 circulant vectors (default m=2), so only O(mn) scalars are stored rather than n² weights. For the supplementary Flow Matching experiments, the same CD-Convolution layer replaces standard linear layers in a 5-layer MLP on 2D toy data (Chessboard, Moons, Circles).

## Method

CDFlow replaces the dense 1×1 convolution in standard flow modules with a CD-Convolution layer whose weight matrix is an alternating product of m diagonal matrices and m−1 circulant matrices, reducing parameter complexity from O(n²) to O(mn). The key mechanism exploits the fact that circulant matrices are diagonalized by the DFT: the log-determinant is computed as a sum of log-magnitudes of precomputed FFT eigenvalues in O(mn), and matrix inversion reduces to O(mn log n) via forward/inverse FFT with element-wise reciprocal in the frequency domain. The full CDFlow architecture stacks these CD-Convolution layers with ActNorm and affine coupling layers inside a multi-scale squeeze/split framework (3 blocks, 32 flow steps for standard images). Training uses maximum likelihood with spectral normalization applied to the structured weights and a channel-aware learning rate to stabilize updates to the circulant/diagonal parameters.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, CDFlow achieves 3.31±0.004 BPD with 44.2M parameters, outperforming Glow (3.36), Emerging (3.34), Woodbury (3.42), and ButterflyFlow (3.33). On ImageNet 32×32, CDFlow reaches 4.04 BPD, beating Glow, Emerging, Woodbury, and ButterflyFlow (all at 4.09). On the Galaxy dataset with periodic structure, CDFlow achieves 1.92 BPD, the best among all compared methods, with a 4.95% improvement over Glow. In terms of computational efficiency, CDFlow speeds up matrix inverse and log-determinant computations by 1.17× and 4.31× respectively compared to a general dense matrix at 96 channels.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CDFlow outperforms all same-architecture baselines (Glow, Emerging, Woodbury, ButterflyFlow) on all three datasets, achieving the best BPD among them on CIFAR-10 (3.31 vs. 3.33–3.42), ImageNet 32×32 (4.04 vs. 4.05–4.09), and Galaxy (1.92 vs. 1.95–2.02). On CIFAR-10 and ImageNet, CDFlow is slightly behind i-DenseNet (3.25 / 3.98 BPD) and Residual Flows (3.28 / 4.01 BPD), which use different architectural designs rather than the same multi-scale coupling framework. On the Galaxy dataset, CDFlow achieves the best BPD (1.92) by a clear margin over the next-best ButterflyFlow (1.95), with a 4.95% improvement over Glow, demonstrating its particular strength on data with inherent periodicity. In the Flow Matching experiments on 2D toy datasets (Chessboard, Moons, Circles), CDMLP with only 8.45K parameters achieves the best NLL on Chessboard (7.04) and Circles (15.49), and the best MSE and FID on Moons, while using 93–99% fewer parameters than the standard MLP baseline. In runtime benchmarks, CDFlow consistently outperforms all baselines in both inverse computation and log-determinant evaluation, with the advantage growing at larger channel sizes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper applies the circulant-diagonal matrix factorization (Huhtanen & Perämäki, 2015) to the specific problem of invertible linear layers in normalizing flows, a setting where prior work on this factorization (Moczulski et al., 2015; Araujo et al., 2019; Ding et al., 2025) focused solely on parameter efficiency in standard feedforward networks without addressing reversibility. It positions itself against the established family of flow-based linear layer designs—Glow's LU-decomposed 1×1 convolutions, Emerging's periodic convolutions, Woodbury's low-rank updates, and ButterflyFlow's butterfly matrices—by exploiting the FFT diagonalization of circulant matrices to simultaneously achieve O(mn) parameter count, O(mn) log-determinant, and O(mn log n) inverse, whereas prior methods typically optimize only one of these three costs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The clean separation of training cost (logdet) from sampling cost (inverse) via FFT-based circulant inversion offers a practical efficiency path for flow-based models where both operations are invoked repeatedly. However, the current restriction to 1×1 convolutions limits expressiveness compared to d×d alternatives, and the performance gains over strong baselines (ButterflyFlow, Emerging) are modest—3.31 vs. 3.33 BPD on CIFAR-10—making the method's primary appeal its computational efficiency rather than a quality leap. Open questions include whether the circulant-diagonal structure can be extended to spatial convolutions without losing the FFT-based inversion advantage, and whether the periodicity bias (evidenced by the Galaxy result) is a genuine modeling strength or an artifact of the small dataset.
