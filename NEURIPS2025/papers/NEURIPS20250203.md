## Problem

Existing temporal 3D Semantic Scene Completion methods either rely on sparse single-frame observations or naively stack multiframe features aligned by estimated camera poses, ignoring scene motion dynamics and failing to achieve temporal consistency. The paper asks how to accurately identify the correlation between historical and current frames to guide temporal SSC modeling, capturing motion, deformation, and occlusion cues that static alignment strategies miss.

## Input / Output

The method takes a current RGB frame plus 2 historical frames (resized to 1280×384 for SemanticKITTI or 1408×384 for SSCBench-KITTI-360) as input, with bidirectional optical flow pre-computed by a frozen GMFlow network. It outputs a dense 3D semantic voxel grid of size 256×256×32 covering a 51.2m × 51.2m × 64m scene, where each voxel receives an occupancy flag and one of 20 (SemanticKITTI) or 19 (SSCBench-KITTI-360) semantic class labels.

## Method

FlowScene introduces two key modules built on a RepViT+FPN backbone. The Flow-Guided Temporal Aggregation (FGTA) module warps historical 2D features into the reference frame using backward optical flow, assigns per-pixel weights via cosine similarity between warped and current features, and aggregates them into a motion-aware feature map; it then applies neighborhood cross-attention masked by a forward-backward consistency occlusion mask to selectively fuse non-occluded historical texture into the current frame. The Occlusion-Guided Voxel Refinement (OGVR) module projects the current, aggregated, and mask features into 3D voxel space via the Lift-Splat-Shoot paradigm with depth bins, then fuses them per-voxel: non-occluded voxels adopt the multi-frame aggregated features while occluded voxels retain the current-frame features, with a normalization term ensuring smooth boundaries. The overall loss combines scene-class affinity loss for geometry and semantics, class-frequency-weighted cross-entropy, and a LiDAR-projected depth distribution loss, trained end-to-end with a frozen optical flow estimator.

## Results

On the SemanticKITTI hidden test set, FlowScene achieves an mIoU of 17.70 and IoU of 45.20, setting new state-of-the-art among both single-frame and temporal methods while using only 2 historical frames (vs. 5 for most temporal baselines). On SSCBench-KITTI-360, it reaches mIoU 20.81 and IoU 46.95, with particularly strong gains on dynamic objects (e.g., car: 32.48, truck: 14.86, other-vehicle: 9.62). The model uses only 52.4M parameters and 0.301s inference time, substantially fewer parameters than HTCL (181.4M) while outperforming it by 1.00% mIoU on the validation set.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FlowScene outperforms the strongest temporal baseline HTCL (ECCV'24) by +0.61% mIoU (17.70 vs. 17.09) and +0.97% IoU (45.20 vs. 44.23) on SemanticKITTI, while using only 2 historical frames versus HTCL's 3. Against the best single-frame method CGFormer (NeurIPS'24), it gains +1.07% mIoU and +0.79% IoU. On SSCBench-KITTI-360, FlowScene leads CGFormer by +0.76% mIoU (20.81 vs. 20.05) and shows especially large margins on dynamic classes: car (+2.63), truck (+8.07), and other-vehicle (+2.99). Compared to Symphonize (CVPR'24), the improvement is +5.18% mIoU on SSCBench-KITTI-360. In the static/dynamic split analysis (Table 5), FlowScene achieves the highest dynamic-object mIoU on both datasets (7.50 on SemanticKITTI, 14.87 on SSCBench-KITTI-360), confirming that optical-flow-guided temporal alignment is most beneficial for moving objects. The method also uses far fewer parameters (52.4M) than HTCL (181.4M) and BRGScene (161.4M) while matching or exceeding their accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

FlowScene departs from the dominant temporal SSC paradigm of stacking historical frame features or aligning them via estimated camera poses (VoxFormer-T, H2GFormer, HTCL) by instead using pre-computed optical flow (GMFlow) to warp and weight historical features in 2D image space before 3D projection. The flow-guided warping and similarity-weighted aggregation draw on FGFA's flow-guided feature aggregation for video object detection, while the occlusion-aware neighborhood cross-attention and LSS-based voxel projection are adapted from prior SSC and BEV-depth literature. The key conceptual shift is treating temporal alignment as a motion-estimation problem rather than a pose-estimation or feature-stacking problem, and explicitly modeling occlusion in both 2D and 3D spaces.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FlowScene demonstrates that optical flow guidance can outperform multi-frame stacking with fewer historical inputs (2 vs. 3–5 frames) and substantially fewer parameters (52.4M vs. 181.4M for HTCL), while showing particularly strong gains on dynamic objects (7.50% vs. 5.48% mIoU on SemanticKITTI). The finding that adding more temporal frames degrades performance due to flow quality decay is a practical caution for temporal SSC design. Limitations include dependence on a frozen pretrained flow estimator (vulnerable in degraded visual conditions), single-camera scope, and inference time that still lags the fastest baselines; extending to multi-camera settings and improving robustness to flow estimation errors are natural next steps.
