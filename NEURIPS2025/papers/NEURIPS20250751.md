## Problem

MCMC algorithms generate samples sequentially, so runtime scales linearly with chain length, making it difficult to leverage modern parallel hardware like GPUs. Prior work on parallelizing MCMC has focused on running many independent chains simultaneously, but the per-chain cost remains linear. This paper asks whether the sequence length itself can be parallelized to achieve sublinear complexity in the number of samples.

## Input / Output

The method takes a target distribution (via its log-density and gradient), a fixed initial state, pre-sampled input randomness (Gaussian noise vectors and uniform random variables shared across parallel and sequential runs), and sampler hyperparameters (step size, leapfrog step count). It outputs the entire MCMC chain of length T as a single batch, numerically equivalent to sequential evaluation within a tolerance δ. A key structural detail is that the parallel Newton iterations produce intermediate sample sequences at each iteration, enabling early-stopped approximate sampling before full convergence.

## Method

The core mechanism reformulates an MCMC chain as a nonlinear recursion s_t = f_t(s_{t-1}) and applies a parallel-in-time Newton's method (DEER): at each iteration, the transition functions are linearized around a current state guess, yielding a linear dynamical system with time-varying Jacobians that is solved in O(log T) parallel time via associative scan. The paper adapts this to Gibbs sampling (reparameterized coordinate updates), MALA (using a stop-gradient trick to make the accept-reject step differentiable for the Jacobian while preserving exact forward-pass behavior), and HMC (both parallelizing across sampling steps and parallelizing the inner leapfrog integration). Two new quasi-Newton variants reduce memory from O(TD²) to O(TD): a stochastic quasi-DEER that estimates the Jacobian diagonal via Hutchinson's method in a single forward pass, and a block quasi-DEER that retains the diagonal of each 2×2 block of the leapfrog Jacobian to capture position-momentum coupling at linear cost.

## Results

Parallel MALA on the German Credit Dataset (Bayesian logistic regression) achieves wall-clock speedups of up to 20–30× over sequential MALA for smaller batch sizes (B ≤ 16), typically converging in only tens of Newton iterations even for chains of 64K samples. Parallel Gibbs sampling on the Eight Schools problem achieves ~2× speedup for batches of 32–64 chains, with quasi-DEER requiring only 100–150 iterations for chains up to 1M samples. For the 768-dimensional IMDB sentiment classification task, parallel MALA with a sliding window of size 256 achieves over 3× faster wall-clock time than sequential MALA. The memory-efficient stochastic quasi-DEER variant is over 10× faster than an automatic-differentiation-based quasi-DEER implementation, and early-stopping at 8 Newton iterations yields near-comparable MMD quality to full convergence with ~10× faster runtime.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against sequential Gibbs sampling (Geman & Geman, 1984), parallel quasi-DEER wins on wall-clock time for batches of 32–64 chains, achieving approximately 2× speedup while requiring only 100–150 Newton iterations for chains up to 1M samples; for smaller batch sizes the advantage narrows. Against sequential MALA (Besag, 1994), parallel MALA achieves dramatic speedups of up to 20–30× for batch sizes B ≤ 16, with virtually identical sample quality as measured by MMD against NUTS ground truth; however, at B=32 sequential MALA is slightly faster due to GPU resource saturation, and the paper shows that splitting into multiple smaller parallel runs (e.g., four B=8 runs instead of one B=32 run) recovers the advantage. Against sequential HMC (Duane et al., 1987), the results are setting-dependent: for the BLR model where the optimal number of leapfrog steps is small, parallelizing HMC across the sequence length achieves the highest peak ESS/s, while parallelizing the leapfrog integration within each step provides substantial gains only when the number of leapfrog steps is large; block quasi-DEER reduces the number of parallel iterations needed for leapfrog convergence by ~2× compared to standard quasi-DEER.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper applies the parallel Newton framework for nonlinear recursions (DEER/DeepPCR by Lim et al. and Danieli et al., and quasi-DEER by Gonzalez et al.) to a new domain: MCMC sampling. While prior work parallelized MCMC by running independent chains simultaneously, this paper parallelizes across the chain length itself, treating the MCMC trajectory as a fixed-point problem solvable via parallel-in-time Newton iterations. The paper also introduces two algorithmic contributions of independent interest: a block quasi-DEER variant exploiting the 2×2 block structure of leapfrog Jacobians, and a stochastic quasi-DEER using Hutchinson-style diagonal estimation that reduces D forward passes to one. The stop-gradient relaxation of the Metropolis accept-reject step, combined with the convergence guarantee from Gonzalez et al., is a key technical bridge that makes the Newton framework applicable to discrete MCMC kernels.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MCMC is a workhorse of Bayesian inference, and the demonstrated order-of-magnitude wall-clock speedups (up to 20–30× for parallel MALA at moderate batch sizes) could make previously intractable sampling budgets feasible on a single GPU. The paper also reveals a new resource-allocation tradeoff—splitting parallel capacity between batch size and sequence length—that practitioners can exploit. Key limitations include reliance on unimodal targets, fixed step sizes (incompatible with NUTS), and increased memory overhead that can negate gains for high-dimensional or memory-constrained problems. Open questions include a theory of when MCMC trajectories are efficiently parallelizable, extension to adaptive samplers, and whether the approach scales to highly multimodal posteriors where local Jacobians may be unstable.
