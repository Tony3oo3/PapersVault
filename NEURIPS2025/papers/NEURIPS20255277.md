## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting produces millions of redundant Gaussian primitives during densification, but existing compaction methods rely on per-Gaussian heuristic importance scores (opacity, gradient, ray contribution) that discard primitives independently without any global fidelity guarantee. This leads to loss of critical structural details and geometric distortion, particularly in regions with fine-scale features. The paper asks how to design a compaction method that preserves 3D spatial and structural geometry through a principled global optimization rather than local pruning heuristics.

## Input / Output

The method takes a fully trained 3DGS model (a set of n Gaussian primitives each with position μ∈ℝ³, covariance Σ∈ℝ³ˣ³, opacity α∈[0,1), and spherical-harmonics color coefficients) along with a user-specified retention ratio ρ (e.g., 10% or 20%). It outputs a compacted 3DGS model with m = ρn Gaussians whose positions and covariances are re-optimized (not merely subset-selected from the original set) and whose color and opacity attributes are fine-tuned via standard 3DGS rendering. The method is post-hoc and plug-and-play: it can be inserted at any training iteration of vanilla 3DGS or any variant (Mini-Splatting, AtomGS, etc.) without modifying the backbone's densification logic.

## Method

The key insight is to interpret the 3DGS geometric representation (positions, covariances, opacities) as a Gaussian Mixture Model and cast compaction as Gaussian Mixture Reduction (GMR) via Composite Transportation Divergence (CTD), an optimal-transport-based divergence between mixtures. The GMR is solved with a k-means-like iterative algorithm where a novel cost function c(φ,φ′) = ‖μ−μ′‖²₂ + ‖Σ−Σ′‖²_F replaces the KL divergence from prior GMR work, yielding closed-form weighted-average updates that avoid expensive covariance inversions. For scalability, the scene is spatially partitioned into blocks via a KD-tree (depth d = ⌊log₂(n/s)⌋), and GMR is applied independently within each block, reducing per-iteration cost from O(ρn²) to O(ρs²log n). The pipeline is two-stage: Stage 1 performs geometric compaction (re-optimizing μ and Σ only), and Stage 2 freezes geometry while fine-tuning color and opacity through T additional steps of standard 3DGS backpropagation, decoupling geometric and appearance optimization.

## Results

At 10% Gaussian retention, 3DGS+GHAP achieves PSNR 23.312 / SSIM 0.818 / LPIPS 0.242 on Tanks & Temples (157k Gaussians), outperforming all post-training compaction baselines (LightGaussian: 22.113, PUP-3DGS: 21.519, Trimming the Fat: 21.535, MesonGS: 20.714 PSNR). With the Mini-Splatting-D backbone, MiniSplatting+GHAP reaches PSNR 23.232 / SSIM 0.835 / LPIPS 0.198 using only 79k Gaussians, surpassing the original MiniSplatting (22.661 PSNR, 78k) and matching or exceeding vanilla 3DGS quality. On Mip-NeRF 360 and Deep Blending, the same pattern holds: GHAP consistently leads in SSIM and PSNR among methods with comparable primitive counts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among post-training compaction baselines at ~10% retention, 3DGS+GHAP wins on SSIM and PSNR across all three datasets: it beats LightGaussian by +1.2 dB PSNR and +0.062 SSIM on Tanks & Temples, PUP-3DGS by +1.8 dB PSNR, and MesonGS by +2.6 dB PSNR (though MesonGS achieves the best LPIPS at 0.208 vs. GHAP's 0.242). Against the end-to-end 3DGS-MCMC baseline, GHAP leads by +1.2 dB PSNR and +0.039 SSIM on Tanks & Temples. With the Mini-Splatting-D backbone, MiniSplatting+GHAP outperforms the original MiniSplatting by +0.6 dB PSNR and +0.036 SSIM while using a comparable number of Gaussians (79k vs. 78k), and also surpasses LocoGS (which uses ~7× more primitives) on SSIM. The only metric where GHAP trails is LPIPS, where MesonGS achieves slightly lower values (0.208 vs. 0.242 on T&T), suggesting a minor perceptual-quality gap that the authors attribute to the L1/SSIM loss weighting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reframes 3DGS compaction as Gaussian Mixture Reduction (GMR) via composite transportation divergence, building directly on Zhang et al.'s (2023) OT-based GMR framework and adapting it to the 3DGS setting with a novel L2+Frobenius cost function and blockwise KD-tree partitioning for scalability. It departs from the dominant paradigm of per-Gaussian heuristic pruning (LightGaussian, PUP-3DGS, Trimming the Fat, MesonGS) by treating the scene geometry as a global probabilistic object rather than a collection of independently scored primitives. The closest prior work, 3DGS-MCMC (Kheradmand et al., 2024), also takes a probabilistic view but relies on pairwise Langevin dynamics without convergence guarantees under a principled divergence, whereas GHAP provides a globally optimal transport solution with finite-step convergence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As 3DGS becomes the dominant real-time rendering paradigm for AR/VR and autonomous driving, efficient compaction is critical for deployment on resource-constrained devices; GHAP's plug-and-play design (applicable to vanilla 3DGS, Mini-Splatting, AtomGS, and MCMC variants at any training stage) makes it broadly adoptable with minimal engineering. The finding that GMR-based compaction can occasionally *improve* rendering quality over the original model (by regularizing over-saturated Gaussian distributions) suggests a useful side-benefit of principled reduction. Open questions include extending to dynamic 3DGS, multi-scale and overlap-aware partitioning, and closing the small LPIPS gap relative to some baselines, which the authors attribute to a trade-off between perceptual and distortion metrics.
