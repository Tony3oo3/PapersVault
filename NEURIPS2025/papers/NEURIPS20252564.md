## Problem

KV cache memory overhead is the primary bottleneck for long-context LLM inference, and prior pruning approaches have been restricted to structured patterns (e.g., channel-wise removal) that cap achievable sparsity at around 30–50% before severe accuracy degradation. The paper asks whether removing all structural constraints on the sparsity pattern—i.e., adopting fully unstructured, element-level pruning—can push KV cache sparsity to 70% or higher while preserving model accuracy, and how to make the resulting irregular sparsity computationally efficient during attention.

## Input / Output

During prefill, the full KV cache (Key and Value matrices of shape [tokens × head_dim] per layer and head) is generated via standard FlashAttention. At the start of decode, each token's Key and Value vectors are pruned per-token by magnitude (keeping the top-(1−s) fraction of elements) and compressed into a bitmap-based sparse tile format (64-element tiles with a 64-bit mask and offset). The most recent 32 tokens form a local dense window that bypasses pruning. During each decode step, the single Query vector is multiplied against both the compressed sparse KV cache (via SpMV) and the dense local window (via standard MV), and the two partial outputs are combined after a joint softmax to produce the next token's attention output.

## Method

Mustafar combines a training-free per-token magnitude-based pruning strategy with a custom sparse attention kernel. For pruning, the Key cache benefits from prominent channel-wise outliers that per-token magnitude selection naturally captures, while the Value cache—despite its uniform magnitude distribution—also responds well to per-token magnitude pruning because the attention formulation (AttentionScore × Value) makes each element's contribution proportional to its magnitude, rendering per-token magnitude pruning inherently output-aware. For efficient computation, the pruned KV cache is stored in a bitmap-based sparse format (extending Coruscant) where each 1×64 tile carries a 64-bit non-zero mask and a tile offset, enabling maximal compression of arbitrary sparsity patterns. A custom CUDA kernel performs batch SpMV directly on this compressed representation using a load-as-compressed, compute-as-dense pipeline: compressed tiles are loaded from global memory into registers, decompressed into shared memory, and fed to tensor cores for dense tile-wise computation. The decode attention is split into a sparse SpMV over the compressed cache and a dense MV over the 32-token local window, with the SpMV speedup (81% of dense time at 50% sparsity, 62% at 70%) more than compensating for the ~8% overhead of runtime pruning and compression.

## Results

On LongBench with Llama-3-8B-Instruct, Mustafar's per-token magnitude-based unstructured pruning achieves 42.65 average score at 50% KV sparsity and 40.96 at 70% KV sparsity, compared to 43.19 for the dense model. The KV cache is compressed to as low as 45% of its dense size at 70% sparsity, and the custom sparse attention kernel delivers up to 2.23× tokens/sec throughput improvement over dense inference (Llama-3, batch size 8). On the RULER benchmark (65K context), Mustafar at 70% joint Key-Value sparsity maintains near-dense accuracy (e.g., 1.000 on Needle-Single, 0.995 on Needle-MultiKey1) while ThinK at the same sparsity collapses to near-zero (0.000–0.073).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole structured-pruning baseline is ThinK [44], which applies query-driven per-channel structured pruning to the Key cache only. At 50% Key sparsity, Mustafar's unstructured per-token magnitude pruning (K0.5 V0.0) scores 42.84 on LongBench average versus ThinK's 38.53, a 4.3-point gap. At 70% Key sparsity the gap widens dramatically: Mustafar K0.7 V0.0 achieves 41.55 while ThinK 0.7 drops to 26.55 (a 15-point gap). Most strikingly, Mustafar at 70% sparsity on *both* Key and Value caches (40.96) still outperforms ThinK at only 50% Key-cache sparsity (38.53), meaning Mustafar achieves higher total compression with better accuracy. On the RULER benchmark at 65K context length, the contrast is even sharper: at 70% joint KV sparsity, ThinK's accuracy collapses to 0.000–0.073 across most tasks, while Mustafar retains 0.969–1.000, matching the dense model. The paper also validates compatibility with orthogonal methods (H2O token eviction, KIVI quantization), showing that 50% Mustafar sparsity combined with these techniques preserves near-dense accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the KV cache compression literature by replacing the structured (channel-wise) pruning paradigm of ThinK [44] and KVPruner [31] with unstructured, element-wise sparsity, arguing that removing structural constraints enables substantially higher sparsity at equivalent accuracy. The computational backbone is inherited from Coruscant [20] (bitmap-based sparse format) and FlashLLM [43] (load-as-compressed, compute-as-dense SpMV pipeline), which previously targeted weight sparsity in projection layers; Mustafar repurposes these for the attention-specific KV cache. The pruning strategy itself draws on KIVI's [30] observation of Key-cache channel outliers to motivate per-token direction, while the Value-cache design is novel in showing that the attention multiply-accumulate structure makes per-token magnitude pruning inherently output-aware.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

KV cache memory is the dominant bottleneck for long-context LLM serving, and this paper demonstrates that 70% unstructured sparsity on both Key and Value caches is achievable with minimal accuracy loss (40.96 vs. 43.19 LongBench average on Llama-3-8B), translating to 45% memory compression and up to 2.23× throughput. The practical impact is significant: the technique is tuning-free, compatible with FlashAttention prefill, and composes with orthogonal methods (H2O eviction, KIVI quantization). Key limitations include degraded throughput at batch size 1 due to GPU underutilization, lack of low-bit precision support in the current kernel, and the use of uniform sparsity across all heads and layers rather than per-head/per-layer optimal allocation, which the authors identify as the most promising direction for further gains.
