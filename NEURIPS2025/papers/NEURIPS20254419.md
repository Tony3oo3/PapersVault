## Problem

Existing virtual try-on methods are largely restricted to clothing and require explicit wearing-area masks or bounding boxes, making them impractical for diverse wearable objects like jewelry, accessories, and shoes where the interaction with the body is complex. Moreover, paired training data (object image + corresponding try-on result) is scarce for most non-clothing object types, limiting the scalability of standard VTON training pipelines.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254419/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a person image and a wearable object image (which may have a white background, natural background, or be a try-on photo) as input, with no mask, bounding box, or pose annotation required. The output is a photorealistic try-on result image where the object is naturally placed on the person. All images are resized to a maximum of 1 million pixels while preserving original aspect ratios, and the model handles varying aspect ratios within a batch via token padding.

## Method

OmniTry uses a two-stage training pipeline on a FLUX.1 Fill inpainting DiT backbone. Stage 1 trains mask-free localization on large-scale unpaired portraits: objects are detected (GroundingDINO + SAM), erased via inpainting, and the model learns to re-paint them from text descriptions alone; the inpainting model is repurposed for mask-free generation by simply setting the mask input to zero, which causes the model to trivially copy the person image and focus on locating the modification area. A 'traceless erasing' strategy (applying image-to-image re-painting after erasure and blending with the original) prevents the model from exploiting invisible inpainting artifacts as shortcuts. Stage 2 fine-tunes on paired data for object identity preservation by concatenating object image tokens into the DiT sequence with a masked full-attention mechanism (blocking information flow from the generated try-on to the object image) and two-stream LoRA adapters that separately process person and object tokens.

## Results

On the full OmniTry-Bench (12 object types, 360 pairs), OmniTry achieves 0.6160 M-DINO and 0.8327 M-CLIP-I for object consistency, 0.0542 LPIPS and 0.9333 SSIM for person preservation, and 0.9972 G-Accuracy for localization. On the clothes subset, OmniTry reaches 0.6995 M-DINO, 0.8560 M-CLIP-I, 0.1021 LPIPS, and 0.9105 SSIM, outperforming both mask-based VTON methods (e.g., FitDiT at 0.6733 M-DINO) and the only other mask-free VTON (Any2AnyTryon at 0.6747 M-DINO). The ablation on few-shot training shows that the two-stage pipeline enables satisfying performance with as few as one paired sample per class, versus a clear degradation for from-scratch training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the full 12-class benchmark, OmniTry dominates all mask-free general-purpose methods: it surpasses OmniGen (0.5435) and VisualCloze (0.5292) by 7–13 points in M-DINO, and reduces LPIPS from 0.67/0.45 to 0.054, indicating far superior person preservation. Against mask-based editors (Paint-by-Example, MimicBrush, ACE++), OmniTry also leads on object consistency (0.6160 vs. 0.45–0.47 M-DINO) while matching or exceeding their person-preservation scores. On the clothes subset, OmniTry outperforms the strongest mask-based VTON (FitDiT: 0.6733 M-DINO, 0.1618 LPIPS) and the only other mask-free VTON (Any2AnyTryon: 0.6747 M-DINO, 0.2089 LPIPS), achieving 0.6995 M-DINO and 0.1021 LPIPS. The sole metric where OmniTry trails slightly is CLIP-T on the clothes subset (0.2799 vs. 0.2832 for Any2AnyTryon), a negligible 0.003 gap. In the human evaluation (Table 4), OmniTry achieves the best average rank of 0.62, ahead of Any2AnyTryon (0.77) and FitDiT (2.28).

## Relation to Prior Work

OmniTry extends the virtual try-on (VTON) literature, which has been dominated by garment-specific methods (VITON, OOTDiffusion, FitDiT, CatVTON), into a unified framework for any wearable object. Architecturally, it builds on the FLUX diffusion transformer and the full-attention image concatenation paradigm from Ominicontrol and In-context LoRA, but departs from prior mask-free VTON work (Any2AnyTryon, Boow-VTON) by repurposing an inpainting model for mask-free generation and introducing a two-stage training pipeline that decouples localization from identity preservation. The traceless erasing strategy and masked full-attention with two-stream LoRA adapters are novel design choices not present in prior controllable generation or VTON systems.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first unified benchmark (OmniTry-Bench) and framework covering 12 wearable object classes in a mask-free setting, filling a gap where prior work addressed only clothing or required manual masks. The two-stage training dramatically reduces data requirements—achieving reasonable performance with as few as one paired sample per class—which is practically important for long-tail object categories where paired data is scarce. Key open questions include generalization to object types absent from the unpaired pre-training set, handling objects with large geometric transformations (e.g., bags), and whether the inpainting-repurposing trick transfers to other editing tasks beyond try-on.
