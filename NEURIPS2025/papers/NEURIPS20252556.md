## Problem

Posterior sampling from p(x|y) given a noisy linear measurement y = Ax + ξ is computationally intractable in general, and existing heuristics (DPS, particle filtering, etc.) lack convergence guarantees. Standard Langevin dynamics, which naturally handles the posterior score via Bayes' rule, requires an MGF (sub-exponential tail) bound on score-estimation error, far stronger than the L² bound sufficient for unconditional diffusion sampling. The paper asks whether provably efficient and robust posterior sampling is possible for log-concave priors with only an L⁴ score-accuracy guarantee.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes a noisy measurement y ∈ ℝ^m, a known matrix A ∈ ℝ^{m×d}, L⁴-accurate score estimates ŝ_{σ²}(x) of the smoothed prior, and optionally a warm-start estimate x₀ (e.g., from LASSO). It outputs a sample whose total-variation distance from the true posterior p(x|y) or p(x|x₀, y) is at most ε, with the number of iterations polynomial in d, m, ‖A‖/(η√α), and 1/ε. For the local log-concavity setting, the warm start x₀ must satisfy ‖x₀ − x‖ ≤ R with high probability, and the prior must be nearly log-concave within a ball of radius R·poly(d, m, ‖A‖R/η, 1/δ) around x.

## Method

Algorithm 1 initializes with a sample from p(x) via the diffusion SDE (which only needs L² score accuracy), then runs an annealed Langevin dynamics with a decreasing noise schedule η₁ > η₂ > … > η_N = η. At each annealing step i, a short Langevin SDE run of duration T_i transitions the process from p(x|y_i) to p(x|y_{i+1}) using the estimated posterior score ŝ_{y_{i+1}}(x) = ŝ(x) + Aᵀ(y_{i+1} − Ax)/η_{i+1}². The key insight is that by keeping consecutive posteriors close (via the admissible schedule), each short Langevin step keeps the process distribution on the prior manifold, so the L⁴ score error bound suffices to control per-step error—avoiding the off-manifold drift that would require MGF-level accuracy in a single long run. For local log-concavity (Theorem 1.2 / Corollary 1.3), a Gaussian perturbation of the warm start x₀ is treated as a spherical Gaussian measurement, allowing the diffusion model to first sample from p(x|x₀) and then the annealed Langevin steps to refine toward p(x|x₀, y).

## Results

On FFHQ-256 (1k validation images), the paper evaluates three inverse problems—30%–70% inpainting, 4× super-resolution, and Gaussian deblurring (61×61 kernel, std 3.0)—reporting per-image L² distance to ground truth and FID of the sample distribution. Across all three tasks, increasing the annealed Langevin time decreases L² distance but increases FID, reflecting a reconstruction-fidelity tradeoff. In the inpainting setting, when the discretization step size is sufficiently small, the proposed annealed Langevin method surpasses DPS on both L² distance and FID. All experiments run on 4× NVIDIA A100 GPUs in roughly two hours per task.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole quantitative baseline is DPS (Diffusion Posterior Sampling, [DS24]), used both as the initial reconstruction (warm start) and as the comparison method in Figure 4. In the inpainting task, the proposed annealed Langevin refinement surpasses DPS on both L² distance and FID when the step size is small enough, as shown by the solid curves dropping below the dashed DPS baselines in Figure 4. For super-resolution and Gaussian deblurring, the paper reports the same L²-vs-FID tradeoff curve but does not claim a clear win on both metrics simultaneously; the advantage is most pronounced in inpainting. Qualitatively (Figures 5–6), the proposed method better preserves ground-truth attributes (e.g., facial identity in inpainting, fine texture in super-resolution) compared to DPS, which tends to produce smoother, less faithful reconstructions. The paper positions DPS as the standard heuristic baseline that lacks convergence guarantees, whereas the annealed Langevin approach provides the first provably correct posterior sampling under local log-concavity with only L⁴ score accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two sampling paradigms: diffusion models, which achieve unconditional sampling with only L² score accuracy (Chen et al. 2022), and Langevin dynamics, which converges rapidly under strong log-concavity but demands MGF-level score accuracy (Dalalyan 2017). It departs from existing posterior sampling heuristics (DPS, particle filtering, DiffPIR) that lack convergence guarantees, and from the computational lower bound of Gupta et al. 2024 showing posterior sampling is intractable in general, by showing that a warm-start observation plus local log-concavity makes the problem tractable. The core methodological novelty is an annealed Langevin schedule that keeps the process on the prior manifold, reducing the required score accuracy from MGF to L⁴.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first provably efficient and robust posterior sampling algorithm under log-concavity with only L⁴ score accuracy, and the first competitive compressed sensing guarantee of this kind. The conceptual decomposition—that the hardness of posterior sampling is essentially a localization problem, not a sampling problem—reframes how the field should think about inverse problems with generative priors. Key limitations include the log-concavity requirement (global or local), the need for a warm start in the local case, and polynomial but potentially large iteration counts. Open questions include whether the L⁴ requirement can be relaxed to L², whether the log-concavity assumption can be replaced by weaker geometric conditions, and whether the approach extends to non-Gaussian or nonlinear measurement models.
