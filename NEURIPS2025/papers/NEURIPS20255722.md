## Problem

Linear Mode Connectivity (LMC) has been well-studied for standard feedforward and convolutional networks, where permutation symmetries are fully characterized. However, Mixture-of-Experts architectures introduce additional structural symmetries from their gating mechanism—permutation of experts combined with translation invariance of softmax—that had not been formally analyzed. This paper asks whether these MoE-specific symmetries are sufficient to guarantee LMC and how to algorithmically discover the correct alignment between independently trained MoE models.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes two independently fine-tuned MoE models, each parameterized by n experts with gating vectors (W_i ∈ R^d, b_i ∈ R) and expert MLP weights (A_i, u_i, B_i, v_i) for a single hidden layer of size h. It outputs an expert-level permutation τ ∈ S_n and per-expert hidden-unit permutations {P_i} ∈ S_h, which together define the aligned parameter space for linear interpolation. The entire procedure is data-independent, operating purely in parameter space with O(n³ + nh³) complexity via the Hungarian algorithm, and produces LMC loss barrier curves evaluated at 25 interpolation points on a held-out test set.

## Method

The paper first establishes a group action G(n) = R^d × R × S_n on the MoE weight space and proves (Theorems 4.1–4.2) that this action fully characterizes functional equivalence for both dense and sparse gating, using linear independence of exponential functions and the piecewise-affine structure of ReLU networks. A key observation is that the translation component of G(n) does not affect the loss barrier, so only the permutation τ matters for LMC. The proposed Weight Matching algorithm is two-stage: (1) expert-order alignment via a Linear Assignment Problem using either centered gating parameters or permutation-invariant Gram-matrix representations of expert weights, and (2) internal neuron alignment within each matched expert pair using the Git Re-Basin Weight Matching procedure. Empirically, the method is validated by replacing FFN layers in ViT and GPT-2 Transformers with dense MoE, sparse Top-2 MoE, or DeepSeekMoE, fine-tuning three independent models per configuration, and demonstrating near-zero loss barriers across MNIST, CIFAR-10/100, ImageNet, WikiText103, and One Billion Word.

## Results

The paper confirms the existence of Linear Mode Connectivity (LMC) across all three MoE variants (dense, sparse top-2, and DeepSeekMoE with shared expert) on six datasets spanning vision (MNIST, CIFAR-10/100, ImageNet) and language (WikiText103, One Billion Word). For expert order matching, both proposed methods (Gate Weight Matching and Expert Weight Matching) achieve near-optimal rankings among all 24 permutations of 4 experts, with average ranks of 2.1–4.6 and scaled loss barrier L̂ as low as 0.07 (Gate Weight Matching, CIFAR-100, layer 12). LMC is most pronounced when the MoE replaces the first Transformer layer, with loss barrier ratios (Algorithm 1 vs. naive interpolation) dropping to ~4–5% for 6-layer models on CIFAR-10.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not benchmark against external LMC alignment methods (e.g., OT-based alignment, Sinkhorn re-basin, or activation matching); instead, it compares its two proposed expert-ordering strategies—Gate Weight Matching and Expert Weight Matching—against each other and against naive (unaligned) interpolation. Both methods perform comparably, with Gate Weight Matching achieving slightly lower L̂ on CIFAR-100 (0.07 vs. 0.07 at layer 12) and Expert Weight Matching achieving slightly lower L̂ on CIFAR-10 (0.13 vs. 0.09 at layer 12), indicating no clear winner. Against the naive interpolation baseline, both methods reduce the loss barrier by over 90% in most configurations (L̂ < 1 means the method's barrier is within 1% of the best possible permutation). On the large-scale One Billion Word dataset, the full Total Weight Matching (expert order + internal alignment) consistently yields lower loss barriers than the Skipping-Expert-Order Matching baseline, confirming that correct expert ordering is essential for revealing LMC.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Linear Mode Connectivity (LMC) framework from standard feedforward and convolutional networks to Mixture-of-Experts architectures, building directly on Entezari et al.'s [24] demonstration that permutation invariance suffices for LMC and Ainsworth et al.'s [3] Git Re-Basin weight-matching algorithm. The key conceptual departure is the characterization of a richer symmetry group G(n) = ℝ^d × ℝ × S_n that captures both gating translations and expert permutations simultaneously, going beyond the single-level neuron permutations studied in prior LMC work. The proposed two-stage alignment algorithm (expert ordering via LAP, then internal neuron alignment) generalizes Git Re-Basin to the nested permutation structure inherent to MoE models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As MoE architectures (Switch Transformer, DeepSeek, GLaM) become the dominant paradigm for scaling language and vision models, understanding their loss geometry has direct implications for model merging, weight-space ensembling, and federated learning. The clean theoretical characterization of functional equivalence (Theorems 4.1–4.2) provides a foundation for future work on MoE-specific optimization and generalization. Key limitations include the absence of theoretical bounds on the loss barrier, the treatment of experts as black-box functions (ignoring internal ReLU-network symmetries), and the deferral of the k=1 sparse gating case due to additional scaling invariances. Open questions include whether LMC persists under more complex gating (e.g., learned routing with capacity constraints) and whether the results extend to State Space Models or other emerging architectures.
