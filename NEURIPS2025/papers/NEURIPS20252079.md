## Problem

Prior no-regret autobidding algorithms either require truthful auction mechanisms (e.g., second-price) or, for non-truthful mechanisms like first-price auctions, benchmark only against a weaker Lipschitz-continuous strategy rather than the unconditionally optimal one. This paper resolves the open question of whether a no-regret online bidding algorithm can be designed against the unconditionally optimal (possibly randomized) strategy in non-truthful first-price auctions with ROI constraints.

## Input / Output

At each of T rounds, the algorithm observes a value v_t drawn i.i.d. from an unknown distribution H and must immediately submit a bid b_t ∈ [0,1] before seeing the competing bid d_t (also i.i.d. from an unknown distribution F). Feedback is either full (the realized competing bid d_t is revealed) or bandit (only a binary win/loss indicator is revealed). The output is a bid sequence {b_t} that approximately maximizes total realized value subject to the constraint that total reward is at least ρ times total payment (ROI constraint), with regret measured against the optimal randomized strategy in hindsight.

## Method

The key structural insight is that optimal randomized bidding strategies in first-price auctions can be reduced to deterministic strategies via a concave envelope of the allocation-payment curve G(b·F(b)) = F(b), yielding an equivalent distribution F_conv under which deterministic bidding suffices (Theorem 3.1). Building on this, a primal-dual mirror-descent algorithm (Algorithm 1) maintains a Lagrangian multiplier λ updated via negative entropy to balance reward against the ROI penalty, achieving Õ(√T) regret when F is known. For the unknown-F full-feedback setting, a stage-based bootstrapping algorithm (Algorithm 2) periodically restarts the mirror-descent primitive with an increasingly accurate optimistic empirical CDF, pacing restarts to achieve Õ(√T) regret. For bandit feedback, an explore-then-exploit algorithm (Algorithm 3) first discretizes [0,1] into K = Θ(T^{1/4}) grid points and bids each for M = Θ(√T) rounds to estimate F, then runs a conservative variant of Algorithm 1 with shifted bids (adding 1/K to each bid) to handle the lack of uniform estimation error, yielding Õ(T^{3/4}) regret.

## Results

In the full feedback setting (where the competing bid is observed), the proposed algorithm achieves a near-optimal $\widetilde{O}(\sqrt{T})$ regret bound against the unconditionally optimal (possibly randomized) strategy in hindsight, matching the $\Omega(\sqrt{T})$ lower bound inherited from stochastic bandits. In the bandit feedback setting (where only win/loss is observed), the algorithm attains a $\widetilde{O}(T^{3/4})$ regret bound. The expected ROI violation is bounded by $\widetilde{O}(\sqrt{T})$ in the full feedback setting and $\widetilde{O}(T^{3/4})$ in the bandit feedback setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper improves upon two key prior works. Compared to Feng et al. [21], which achieves $\widetilde{O}(\sqrt{T})$ regret but only for truthful auctions (where optimal strategies reduce to uniform bid scaling), this paper extends the same near-optimal $\widetilde{O}(\sqrt{T})$ guarantee to first-price auctions (a non-truthful mechanism) while benchmarking against the unconditionally optimal possibly randomized strategy. Compared to Aggarwal et al. [3], which handles non-truthful auctions but only guarantees no-regret against the weaker Lipschitz-continuous benchmark, this paper achieves the same $\widetilde{O}(T^{3/4})$ bandit-feedback regret bound against the stronger unconditionally optimal benchmark. The key technical contribution is showing that optimal randomized bidding strategies in non-truthful auctions are essentially parameterized by a single number (the marginal ROI), enabling a mirror-descent-based approach that was previously only applicable to truthful auctions. The $\widetilde{O}(T^{3/4})$ bandit-feedback bound matches the best known guarantee for the weaker benchmark, while the ideal $O(\sqrt{T})$ bound is ruled out by an $\Omega(T^{2/3})$ lower bound from Aggarwal et al. [3].

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the primal-dual mirror descent framework of Balseiro et al. [6] and Feng et al. [21] from truthful auctions to nontruthful first-price auctions, while simultaneously strengthening the benchmark from the optimal Lipschitz-continuous strategy (Aggarwal et al. [3]) to the unconditionally optimal (possibly randomized) strategy. The key technical departure is the allocation-payment curve and its concave envelope, which provides a constructive reduction showing that randomized bidding strategies in first-price auctions can be equivalently represented as deterministic strategies under a modified distribution F_conv. This structural observation—optimal strategies are parametrized by a single marginal-ROI parameter—restores the 'simple strategy class' condition needed for mirror descent, which was previously believed to fail in nontruthful settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

First-price auctions dominate real-world ad markets, so achieving near-optimal √T regret against the true optimal strategy (rather than a weaker Lipschitz proxy) has direct practical implications for advertiser ROI. The bandit feedback result (T^{3/4}) matches the rate Aggarwal et al. achieved against their weaker benchmark, but now against the stronger unconditionally optimal benchmark, closing a meaningful gap. The open question of whether the T^{3/4} rate can be improved toward the Ω(T^{2/3}) lower bound in the bandit setting remains, and the paper's reliance on i.i.d. value and competing-bid distributions limits applicability to non-stationary or adversarial environments.
