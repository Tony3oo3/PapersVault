## Problem

Glorot initialization, the standard baseline for linear recurrent networks, is assumed to ensure stable signal propagation because the circular law places the bulk spectrum within the unit disk. However, the spectral radius of Glorot-initialized matrices converges to one from above by O(1/√n), and this small positive deviation is exponentially amplified when the same weight matrix is applied repeatedly over long sequences. No prior theoretical framework had characterized this instability in the double-scaling regime where both width and sequence length grow, leaving a gap between the infinite-width/fixed-depth assumptions underlying Glorot and the unbounded-depth reality of recurrent processing.

## Input / Output

The method operates on sequential inputs of varying lengths: flattened image pixels (3K for Sequential CIFAR-10), text tokens (up to 8K for IMDB), and nested arithmetic expressions (up to 2K for ListOps). Each input token x_t ∈ ℝ^n is consumed by a linear recurrent layer that maintains a hidden state h_t = W h_{t-1} + x_t, and the final hidden state is mapped to a classification label. The key structural constraint is that the same n×n weight matrix W is applied at every time step, making the spectral properties of W the sole determinant of long-horizon stability.

## Method

The paper proves that under complex Glorot initialization, the expected hidden state norm grows as exp(k²/2n) for sequence length k, showing that sequences of length Θ(√n) suffice to induce explosion in the double-scaling limit. To correct this, they propose a dimension-aware rescaling of the Glorot variance by the factor (1 + √(ρ_n/4n) + a_p/√(4ρ_n·n))^{-2}, where ρ_n = log(n/(2π(log n)²)) and a_p is a Gumbel quantile chosen so that the spectral radius lies below one with probability p ≈ 0.86 (one standard deviation above the Gumbel mean). This shifts the expected spectral radius slightly below one, preventing exponential growth while preserving slow signal decay favorable for memory retention. The rescaled initialization is validated both theoretically (via a variance lower bound derived from the Ginibre eigenvalue density) and empirically on Long Range Arena benchmarks, where standard Glorot fails to train while the rescaled variant achieves the best accuracy in 4 of 6 settings across dense and diagonal linear RNN architectures.

## Results

On the Long Range Arena benchmarks, standard Glorot initialization fails to train (exploding hidden states) on all three tasks. The proposed rescaled initialization achieves 81.54% on Sequential CIFAR-10, 87.55% on IMDB, and 47.51% on ListOps in the dense setting, outperforming the naive Glorot/2 baseline (76.4%, 88.74%, 46.21%) on CIFAR-10 and ListOps. In the diagonal setting, Diag.Rescaled reaches 87.31% on IMDB and 49.49% on ListOps, the best among all diagonal variants. Overall, the rescaled initialization attains the highest accuracy in 4 out of 6 dense/diagonal settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against standard Glorot initialization, the proposed rescaled method is strictly superior: Glorot fails to train on all three LRA tasks due to exploding hidden states, while the rescaled variant trains stably and achieves competitive accuracy. Compared to the LRU circular-law baseline (Diag. Uniform, which samples diagonal entries uniformly from the complex unit circle), the rescaled initialization wins on IMDB (87.31% vs. 86.34%) and ListOps (49.49% vs. 48.93%) in the diagonal setting, but trails slightly on CIFAR-10 (79.53% vs. 81.47%). Against the naive Glorot/2 baseline, the rescaled method achieves a 5.1-point gain on CIFAR-10 (81.54% vs. 76.4%) and a 1.3-point gain on ListOps (47.51% vs. 46.21%), though it lags by 1.2 points on IMDB (87.55% vs. 88.74%). The paper also shows that adding Layer Normalization to Glorot recovers trainability but at ~29% higher per-epoch cost and still below the rescaled method without normalization (86.82% vs. 87.55% on IMDB dense).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Glorot initialization (Glorot and Bengio, 2010) and the circular law from non-Hermitian random matrix theory, which together form the theoretical justification for using Gaussian weight matrices in recurrent settings. It departs from the LRU architecture (Orvieto et al., 2023), which explicitly designs its diagonal initialization to replicate Glorot's spectral properties, by showing that this replication is insufficient for stability under repeated application. The analysis also connects to the emerging double-scaling limit literature (Hanin and Nica, 2020; Seleznova and Kutyniok, 2022), where both width and depth grow, but is the first to characterize this regime specifically for recurrent weight sharing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper challenges a widely-held assumption in the RNN/SSM community that Glorot-style initialization guarantees stable signal propagation, revealing that the spectral radius converges to one from above and that sequences of length O(√n) suffice to trigger exponential explosion. The proposed dimension-aware rescaling is simple and theoretically grounded, but its practical impact is somewhat limited: modern SSMs already employ structured initializations with predefined spectra, and the empirical gains on LRA benchmarks are modest (1–5 percentage points). Key open questions include extending the analysis to temporally correlated inputs, refining the lower bound to an exact characterization, and studying optimization dynamics beyond initialization in the infinite-length regime.
