## Problem

Interpreting 1D NMR spectra to deduce molecular structure remains a manual, expertise-intensive process that bottlenecks natural product discovery and drug development. Prior ML approaches either predict only substructures, require richer multimodal inputs beyond 1D NMR, or are limited to simple molecules (≤101 atoms), leaving the de novo elucidation of complex natural products (up to 274 atoms) from raw 1D NMR spectra and chemical formula unsolved.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253868/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes 1D ¹H and ¹³C NMR spectra (interpolated to 10,000-dimensional vectors over defined ppm ranges, or 80-bin binary vectors for ¹³C in some benchmarks) along with the chemical formula encoded as one-hot atom types. It outputs 3D atomic coordinates (N×3) for all atoms including hydrogens, from which canonical non-stereo SMILES are reconstructed via RDKit for evaluation. The spectra are normalized by peak intensity, and the model supports flexible conditioning on either or both spectra via classifier-free guidance.

## Method

CHEFNMR combines two components: (1) NMR-ConvFormer, a hybrid encoder that processes each spectrum through a convolutional tokenizer (two 1D conv layers with ReLU and max-pooling) to extract local peak features, then a 4-block transformer encoder to model intra- and inter-spectral dependencies, and finally multihead attention pooling to produce a fixed-size spectral embedding; and (2) a non-equivariant Diffusion Transformer (DiT) that generates 3D atomic coordinates via the EDM diffusion framework, conditioned on atom types and the spectral embedding through adaLN-Zero. Training uses a combined MSE + smooth LDDT loss to enforce both global alignment and local geometric validity (bond lengths), with random SE(3) coordinate augmentation for invariance and classifier-free guidance for flexible spectral conditioning. The key architectural choice is a non-equivariant transformer over equivariant GNNs, motivated by superior scalability, paired with a large synthetic dataset (SpectraNP, 111k natural products up to 274 atoms) to cover the chemical complexity of natural products.

## Results

CHEFNMR-L achieves 72.04% top-1 accuracy on SpectraBase, 81.57% on USPTO, and 40.15% on the challenging SpectraNP dataset (natural products up to 274 atoms), with top-10 accuracy reaching 88.20%, 93.01%, and 65.74% respectively. On experimental spectra (zero-shot, trained only on synthetic USPTO data), CHEFNMR attains 56% top-1 accuracy on SpecTeach and 21% on NMRShiftDB2. The advantage is most pronounced on SpectraNP, where CHEFNMR outperforms the strongest baseline (Hu et al.) by over 20 percentage points in top-1 accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CHEFNMR-L decisively outperforms Hu et al. [22] (multitask transformer predicting SMILES) across all three synthetic benchmarks: 72.04% vs. 45.24% top-1 on SpectraBase, 81.57% vs. 38.02% on USPTO, and 40.15% vs. 19.26% on SpectraNP. Against Alberts et al. [4] (transformer predicting stereo SMILES from peak lists), CHEFNMR-L wins on USPTO with 81.57% vs. 73.38% top-1 accuracy and 93.01% vs. 89.98% top-10, though Alberts et al. only reported results on USPTO and SpecTeach. The newly introduced graph-based baseline NMR-DiGress (discrete graph diffusion with NMR-ConvFormer conditioning) is substantially weaker, particularly on complex natural products where it achieves only 2.12% top-1 on SpectraNP versus CHEFNMR's 40.15%. On zero-shot experimental spectra, CHEFNMR (56% top-1 on SpecTeach, 21% on NMRShiftDB2) significantly outperforms both Hu et al. and NMR-DiGress, which generalize poorly to real-world noise and solvent effects.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant SMILES-based and graph-based formulations of NMR structure elucidation (Hu et al. 2024, Alberts et al. 2024) by recasting the problem as conditional 3D atomic coordinate generation via a Diffusion Transformer (DiT) built on the EDM framework. The spectral encoder (NMR-ConvFormer) is a novel hybrid convolutional-transformer design that replaces the 1D-CNN-only or simple patching approaches used in prior spectral encoders. The smooth LDDT loss is adapted from AlphaFold3 to enforce local geometric validity in small molecules, and classifier-free guidance enables flexible conditioning on either or both ¹H and ¹³C spectra. The key conceptual shift is from sequence/graph generation to continuous 3D diffusion, motivated by the observation that non-equivariant transformers scale better than equivariant GNNs for molecular generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Automating NMR structure elucidation is a long-standing grand challenge in natural product chemistry, where over half of FDA-approved small-molecule drugs originate. The SpectraNP dataset (111k natural products up to 274 atoms) is a significant resource that substantially expands the chemical complexity beyond prior benchmarks (≤101 atoms). Key limitations include reliance on synthetic spectra for training (zero-shot transfer to experimental data shows degradation, especially for certain solvents), absence of stereochemistry prediction, and no incorporation of 2D NMR data. Open questions include whether scaling SpectraNP with experimental spectra and 2D NMR inputs can close the remaining accuracy gap, and whether a confidence module can make predictions actionable for chemists without manual verification.
