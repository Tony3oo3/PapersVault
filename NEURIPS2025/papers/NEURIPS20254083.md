## Problem

Existing concept-based explanation methods for neural networks either provide unfaithful post-hoc attributions, make restrictive assumptions (class-specificity, fixed spatial extent, pre-defined concept sets), or rely on approximate measures to estimate concept importance. No prior approach offers a model-inherent decomposition where every logit is an exact sum of concept contributions and every concept is faithfully visualized at input-level, while remaining shared across classes and free of spatial-size constraints.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254083/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

FaCT takes standard RGB images (ImageNet resolution) and produces class logits, but additionally outputs two faithful explanation channels: (1) an exact per-concept contribution score to each logit (Eq. 9), and (2) a pixel-level attribution map for each concept at input resolution (Eq. 12). The SAE operates on intermediate feature patches via 1×1 convolutions, and concepts are shared across all classes rather than being class-specific. The C²-score evaluation metric consumes DINOv2-Small features upsampled with LoftUP to assess concept consistency in a class-agnostic manner.

## Method

FaCT combines B-cos transforms (which guarantee dynamic-linearity, enabling exact input-to-output attribution) with bias-free TopK-SAEs inserted at intermediate layers to decompose features into sparse, interpretable concepts. Because B-cos layers can be collapsed into a single dynamic-linear transform, the logit for any class is an exact sum of concept contributions (Eq. 9), and because the SAE encoder is bias-free, each concept activation is a strict linear combination of intermediate features that can be traced back to input pixels (Eq. 12). The model is built on pre-trained B-cos checkpoints (ResNet-50, DenseNet-121, ViTc-S) with SAEs trained on importance-sampled feature patches from ImageNet using Adam with cosine scheduling. The paper also introduces the C²-score, a class-agnostic concept-consistency metric that leverages DINOv2 features and a random-attribution baseline to evaluate both shared and class-specific concept sets without requiring human part annotations.

## Results

On ImageNet, FaCT maintains competitive accuracy with a drop of less than 3% compared to the original B-cos baseline across all architectures (ResNet-50, DenseNet-121, ViTc-S) and layers. The key interpretability metric, C²-score, improves dramatically: from 0.09 for B-cos channels to 0.37 for FaCT (ResNet-50 Block 4/4), and from 0.11 to 0.39 for DenseNet Block 3/4. In concept-deletion experiments, FaCT's inherent concept contributions (Eq. 9) produce significantly sharper logit and accuracy drops than Saliency, Sobol indices, or CRAFT-based importance measures, confirming their faithfulness. A user study with 38 participants found FaCT concepts substantially more interpretable than B-cos channels, with faithful input attributions providing an additional ~0.5/5 interpretability boost for early-layer concepts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FaCT is compared against four categories of baselines. Against B-cos channels (the base model without SAE), FaCT achieves a 4× improvement in C²-score (0.37 vs. 0.09) while sacrificing less than 3% ImageNet accuracy, and user studies confirm significantly higher interpretability. Against CRAFT [18], FaCT outperforms on both C²-score (Fig. 4-left shows FaCT's concept distribution shifted toward higher consistency) and concept-deletion faithfulness (Fig. 7 shows steeper logit/accuracy drops), because CRAFT's concepts are class-specific and not directly used in the forward pass. Against CRP [1] (Concept Relevance Propagation), FaCT again achieves higher C²-scores, benefiting from its shared, class-agnostic concept basis versus CRP's channel-level attributions. Against Saliency and Sobol index-based importance measures used in prior concept-extraction work [22, 33], FaCT's Eq. (9) contributions produce significantly sharper drops in both top-1 logit and overall accuracy during iterative concept deletion, demonstrating that the model-inherent contributions are a more faithful signal of concept importance than any post-hoc approximation.

## Relation to Prior Work

FaCT sits at the intersection of two threads: B-cos networks, which provide faithful pixel-level attributions by design through dynamic-linear transforms, and sparse autoencoders (SAEs), which have recently become a standard tool for decomposing activations into interpretable features in both LLMs and vision models. The paper departs from prior concept-based explanation methods (CRAFT, VCC, ProtoPNet, CBMs) in two key ways: (1) concepts are part of the model's forward pass rather than extracted post-hoc, and (2) no restrictive assumptions are made about class-specificity, spatial extent, or pre-defined concept sets. The C²-score metric further departs from annotation-based evaluation (e.g., IoU with PartImageNet masks) by leveraging DINOv2 features for class-agnostic consistency measurement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FaCT addresses a genuine gap: prior concept-based explanations are either unfaithful (post-hoc methods) or rely on restrictive assumptions that limit what concepts can be discovered. The faithfulness-by-design guarantee (logit = sum of concept contributions, concept activation = sum of pixel contributions) provides a principled foundation that post-hoc methods cannot match. The C²-score offers a scalable, annotation-free evaluation framework that could become a standard for comparing concept extraction methods. Limitations include a small accuracy drop (<3%), the need for SAE training per layer, and evaluation primarily on ImageNet; open questions include whether the approach scales to very large models and whether the shared concept basis generalizes to fine-grained or multi-label settings.
