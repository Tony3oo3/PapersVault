## Problem

Existing video highlight detection methods rely on offline, full-context processing (bidirectional attention over the entire video), rendering them unsuitable for real-time streaming where decisions must be made frame-by-frame without future access. Streaming Video-LLMs that handle continuous video either modify standard HD benchmarks, use post-hoc smoothing that implicitly leaks future information, or achieve suboptimal highlight detection performance. This leaves a critical gap: no robust method exists for accurate, task-conditioned, strictly online highlight detection on standard benchmarks.

## Input / Output

AHA consumes a continuous video stream sampled at 1 fps (384×384 frames, 49 tokens per frame after spatial pooling) alongside a fixed natural language task description and optional system prompt provided once at stream start. At each timestep it emits a single scalar highlight score ŷ_t for the current frame, computed by fusing three decoupled head outputs: a relevance score (Smooth L1-regressed against engagement signals), a binary informativeness score, and a clamped log-variance uncertainty score. The model operates under strict causal constraints—only past and present frames are visible—enabling real-time, low-latency operation with constant memory via the Dynamic SinkCache (~45 task tokens + 2048 recent visual tokens).

## Method

AHA is a lightweight autoregressive framework built on a frozen SigLIP visual encoder, a single linear multimodal projector, and a Qwen2-7B decoder-only transformer fine-tuned via LoRA. The key architectural contribution is the Dynamic SinkCache, which modifies the SinkCache mechanism by populating the attention sink exclusively with the natural language task objective tokens (rather than generic initial tokens) while dedicating a 2048-token sliding window to recent visual context, yielding constant memory across arbitrarily long streams. Training uses a weighted multi-task objective: Smooth L1 + total variation loss for relevance, BCE for informativeness, Gaussian NLL with a variance-diversity penalty for uncertainty, and an auxiliary LM cross-entropy head, all supervised on the newly constructed HIHD dataset (~23k videos with YouTube replay-based engagement scores and synthetic task prompts) plus Shot2Story and COIN for informativeness and captioning signals. At inference, the final highlight score is a piecewise-linear, uncertainty-aware fusion of the three heads that applies a linear penalty when predicted uncertainty exceeds a threshold, implementing a risk-averse scoring policy.

## Results

On TVSum, AHA achieves a new SOTA of 91.6 top-5 mAP in a truly zero-shot setting (no TVSum-specific tuning), surpassing the previous best tuned model TR-DETR (87.1 mAP) by +4.5 points, and further reaches 93.0 mAP with lightweight grid-search adaptation. AHA also sets SOTA on rank correlations: Kendall's τ = 0.304 and Spearman's ρ = 0.433. On the large-scale Mr.HiSum benchmark, AHA achieves 64.19 mAP@50 and 32.66 mAP@15, improving over the prior best (PGL-SUM, 55.89 mAP@50) by +8.3 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On TVSum, AHA (Zero-Shot) outperforms all prior methods: it beats the strongest tuned baseline TR-DETR (87.1 mAP) by +4.5 mAP, QD-DETR (86.6) by +5.0, UniVTG (84.6) by +7.0, and PGL-SUM (57.1) by +34.5, while also achieving the best rank correlations (Kendall τ 0.304 vs. LLMVS's 0.211; Spearman ρ 0.433 vs. LLMVS's 0.275). On Mr.HiSum, AHA's relevance head achieves 64.19 mAP@50, surpassing PGL-SUM (55.89) by +8.3, SL-module (55.31) by +8.9, DSNet (50.78) by +13.4, and iPTNet (50.53) by +13.7. On streaming moment retrieval (Charades-STA), AHA with smoothing window w=8 reaches 50.7% R@0.5, exceeding MMDuet (w=8) at 42.4% and VTG-LLM at 33.8%. On auxiliary tasks, AHA is competitive but trails specialized methods: on YouCook2 dense captioning it achieves 15.1% F1 (vs. MMDuet's 19.2% and VTG-LLM's 17.5%), and on MAGQA it scores 2.42 in-span (vs. MMDuet's 2.93), reflecting that these tasks were not primary training objectives.

## Relation to Prior Work

AHA extends the streaming Video-LLM line of work (MMDuet, VideoLLM-online) by repurposing their informativeness and language-modeling heads for a dedicated highlight detection objective, while departing from their dialogue-centric, multi-turn interaction paradigm in favor of unidirectional frame-wise scoring. Its core memory mechanism, Dynamic SinkCache, is a targeted modification of the attention-sink approach from StreamingLLM [21], replacing generic initial tokens with the natural language task objective to create a constant-footprint, task-aware long-term memory. The paper explicitly positions itself against the dominant offline, full-context HD literature (TR-DETR, QD-DETR, PGL-SUM) and against streaming Video-LLMs that treat HD as an auxiliary capability with modified evaluation criteria, arguing that neither is suitable for strict causal, task-conditioned streaming.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AHA demonstrates that strict online causality need not sacrifice highlight detection quality, achieving SOTA on TVSum and Mr.HiSum while using constant memory across arbitrarily long streams—a property critical for deployment on drones, robots, and edge devices where full-video access is infeasible. Key open questions remain: the uncertainty head lacks ground-truth supervision, the 1 FPS throughput means the system cannot keep pace with live video without frame-skipping logic, and the engagement-based labels in HIHD may misalign with expert judgment in safety-critical domains. The paper also leaves training with persistent cross-segment memory and adaptive (non-static) scoring weights as important future directions.
