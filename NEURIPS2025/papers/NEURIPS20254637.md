## Problem

Discrete diffusion models lack a rigorous finite-sample theory for score estimation error, leaving the fundamental question of how precisely the true distribution can be reconstructed from limited training data unresolved. A naive analysis yields an error bound of Õ(M/n) where M is the state space cardinality, which is intractable when M is exponentially large in the ambient dimension (e.g., M = 2^D for hypercubes). This paper closes the gap by deriving state-size independent bounds that depend only polylogarithmically on M.

## Input / Output

The method takes n i.i.d. samples from the true distribution p_0 on a finite discrete state space X (e.g., {0,1}^D) and a symmetric transition rate matrix Q defining the forward Markov process. It produces a deep ReLU score network ŝ_t(x,y) trained via denoising score entropy (a Bregman divergence loss with K(x)=x−log x), along with a high-probability upper bound on KL(p_δ ‖ q̂_{T−δ}) that scales as Õ(n^{−2β̃(s−1)/((1+2β̃)(s−1)+β̃)}) for s≥1, independent of M. The score network is constrained to output values in [1/R, R] and the generated sample is produced via the τ-leaping algorithm using the estimated intensity function.

## Method

The key insight is to embed the discrete state space X into R^D and approximate the orthonormal eigenvectors of the graph Laplacian L=−Q by smooth functions in an anisotropic Besov space B_{p,q}^β, enabling the use of deep ReLU approximation theory for smooth Euclidean functions rather than treating each of the M states independently. The score s°_t(x,y)=p_t(y)/p_t(x) is approximated by composing networks that separately approximate Mp_t(x) (via truncated spectral expansion with exponential decay e^{−λ_j t}) and its reciprocal 1/(Mp_t(x)), then multiplying them. Generalization error is controlled through local Rademacher complexity with a peeling device, exploiting the self-bounding property Pg²≲TRD̄·Pg to achieve an O(1/n) rate rather than the standard O(1/√n). Under a polynomial decay condition on spectral coefficients |c_j|≲j^{−s} and bounded Besov norms of the approximating functions, the M-dependence reduces from linear to polylogarithmic, nearly matching the optimal rate conjectured by Ren et al. (2025).

## Results

The paper's main theoretical contribution is a state-size independent error bound for discrete diffusion score estimation. The naive analysis (Theorem 1) yields a KL divergence bound of Õ(Mt/n), linear in the number of states M. By embedding the discrete space into a continuous Euclidean space and exploiting Besov smoothness of eigenvectors, the improved bound (Theorem 3, Corollary 1) reduces the M-dependence to polylogarithmic: for smoothness s ≥ 1 on the hypercube {0,1}^D, the rate becomes Õ(n^{-(2(s-1))/(2s-1)}), independent of M = 2^D. A small numerical experiment on D = 6, 8, 10 hypercubes confirms the predicted mild (logarithmic) growth of denoising score entropy with M, with error bars of approximately 0.4–0.7 × 10⁻⁴ over 10 trials.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no direct method-to-method benchmark comparisons; the `compares_to` list is empty. The primary 'comparison' is internal: the naive estimator (Theorem 1, bound Õ(M/n)) versus the embedding-based estimator (Theorem 3 / Corollary 1, bound Õ(n^{-(2(s-1))/(2s-1)}) for s ≥ 1). The improved bound removes the linear M-dependence entirely, matching the optimal rate conjectured by Ren et al. (2025) up to logarithmic factors. For the less smooth regime s < 1, the bound degrades back to Õ(M/n), matching the naive rate, because approximating O(M) basis functions dominates. The numerical toy experiment (one-hidden-layer ReLU network, 256 units, 20 epochs, ADAM lr=10⁻³) validates the theory qualitatively: DSE increases mildly from D=6 to D=10 (consistent with log M growth) and is higher for high-frequency coefficient mixtures (consistent with the smoothness parameter s in the bound), without any hyperparameter tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the score estimation error analysis of Oko et al. (2023) from continuous-state to discrete-state diffusion models, adapting the Bregman divergence loss (replacing L² score matching) and controlling estimation error via the Hellinger distance. It fills the missing ε_sc term in the error decomposition of Ren et al. (2025), who provided the stochastic integral framework and τ-leaping algorithm but left the score estimation rate uncharacterized. The key methodological departure is embedding the discrete state space into a continuous Euclidean space and approximating graph Laplacian eigenvectors via anisotropic Besov functions (Suzuki and Nitanda, 2021), which converts an intractable O(M/n) bound into a polylogarithmic-in-M rate. The denoising score entropy loss from Lou et al. (2024) and the continuous-time discrete diffusion framework from Campbell et al. (2022) provide the modeling substrate.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first rigorous score estimation error bound for discrete diffusion models, closing a gap that has limited theoretical understanding of how many samples are needed to reconstruct a target distribution in settings like text generation and molecular design where the state space is exponentially large. The state-size independent bound (polylogarithmic in M for s ≥ 1) is practically significant because real discrete spaces (e.g., {0,1}^D for D-dimensional binary data) have M = 2^D, making the naive O(M/n) rate useless. Key limitations include the bounded score assumption (Assumption 3), the requirement that eigenvectors be Besov-approximable, and the very small-scale empirical validation (D ≤ 10, single hidden layer, 20 epochs). Open questions include relaxing the score boundedness condition (analogous to Zhang et al. 2024 for continuous diffusion) and extending to non-hypercube state spaces where the embedding structure is less clear.
