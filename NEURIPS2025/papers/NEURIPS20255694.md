## Problem

Existing transfer-based adversarial attacks on vision-language pre-trained (VLP) models only reduce the similarity of positive image-text pairs, inadvertently also diminishing the similarity of negative pairs and leaving VLP models biased toward retrieving the correct match. Query-based alternatives avoid this but require costly iterative cross-search over the victim model. The paper addresses how to generate high-quality adversarial examples that simultaneously push positive pairs apart and pull negative pairs together, without any queries to the victim model.

## Input / Output

The method consumes clean image-text pairs (e.g., 1000 images with 5000 captions on Flickr30K) and a frozen surrogate VLP model (ALBEF, TCL, or CLIP). It outputs an adversarial image constrained to L∞ perturbation ε_v = 2/255 (optimized via PGD with step size 0.5/255 over 10 iterations) and an adversarial text constrained to a single word substitution (ε_t = 1). The adversarial pair is then transferred to a victim model of the same or different architecture to evaluate attack success rate on retrieval, captioning, and grounding tasks.

## Method

HQA-VLAttack is a two-stage transfer-based attack. For text perturbation, it builds substitute word sets using counter-fitting word vectors (selecting synonyms with cosine similarity > τ = 0.4 to the original word vector, falling back to BERT-Attack MLM for uncovered words), then selects the single-word substitution that minimizes cosine similarity between the candidate text embedding and the original image embedding on the surrogate model. For image perturbation, it first computes per-layer importance weights as the cosine similarity between each layer's [CLS] token and the top-layer [CLS] token, then initializes the adversarial image by minimizing a weighted sum of per-layer feature similarities via PGD. A second optimization stage applies contrastive learning: a loss simultaneously minimizes the cosine similarity of positive (matched) image-text pairs (scaled by penalty factor λ = −10) and maximizes the cosine similarity of negative (unmatched) pairs within a batch of size 16, with scale transformations S = {0.50, 0.75, 1.00, 1.25, 1.50} applied to the adversarial image for robustness. This dual objective forces the victim model to retrieve negative examples rather than the correct match.

## Results

On Flickr30K, HQA-VLAttack achieves 73.02% TR R@1 and 77.60% IR R@1 when transferring from ALBEF to TCL, surpassing the strongest baseline DRA (49.74% / 58.83%) by 23.28 and 18.77 percentage points respectively. On MSCOCO, the same surrogate-to-victim pair yields 85.69% TR and 87.81% IR, a 25.00 and 20.35 point gain over DRA. In white-box settings, HQA-VLAttack reaches nearly 100% ASR (e.g., 99.79% TR, 99.98% IR for ALBEF→ALBEF on Flickr30K; 100.00% for CLIPvit→CLIPvit on both datasets). Cross-task transfer to Image Captioning improves CIDEr by up to 11.3% and BLEU-4 by up to 3.0% over the second-best method.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HQA-VLAttack consistently outperforms all five baselines in transfer (black-box) settings across both datasets and all surrogate-victim pairs. Against the strongest transfer baselines SGA and DRA, HQA-VLAttack gains 13–29 percentage points in TR R@1 and 13–27 points in IR R@1 on Flickr30K, and 15–25 points on MSCOCO. For example, on Flickr30K with CLIPvit→CLIPCNN, HQA-VLAttack reaches 74.07% TR / 77.19% IR versus DRA's 45.47% / 50.74% and SGA's 38.76% / 47.79%. In white-box settings, HQA-VLAttack also leads, reaching 100% ASR on CLIPvit→CLIPvit and 99.79–99.98% on ALBEF→ALBEF, compared to SGA's 97.24–99.08% and DRA's 96.14–99.00%. The weaker baselines (PGD, BERT-Attack, Sep-Attack, Co-Attack) trail by 20–60+ points in transfer scenarios, confirming that joint multimodal perturbation with contrastive optimization is critical for high-quality attacks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the transfer-based black-box adversarial attack line on VLP models initiated by SGA (set-level guidance) and DRA (trajectory diversification), both of which focus exclusively on reducing positive image-text pair similarity. The key departure is the introduction of a contrastive learning objective that simultaneously decreases positive-pair similarity and increases negative-pair similarity, addressing what the authors identify as a blind spot in prior methods. For text perturbation, the method repurposes counter-fitting word vectors (Mrksic et al., 2016) as a semantically grounded alternative to the MLM-based substitution used in BERT-Attack, and for image initialization it introduces a layer-importance weighting scheme that departs from the uniform layer treatment in prior PGD-based approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The contrastive-learning formulation provides a principled way to make VLP retrieval models prefer negative examples, which is a practical concern for any deployment relying on image-text retrieval (search, recommendation, accessibility). The demonstrated transferability to closed-source MLLMs (GPT-4o, Claude-3.7 Sonnet) hints at broader implications for multimodal LLM safety, though the evidence there is limited to qualitative examples. Open questions include whether the single-word text perturbation constraint generalizes to longer captions, whether the layer-importance weighting transfers across architectures with different depths, and whether the contrastive objective can be extended to multi-step or iterative settings without the query cost of cross-search methods.
