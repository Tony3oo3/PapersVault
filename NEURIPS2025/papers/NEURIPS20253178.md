## Problem

Deep neural networks underperform on tabular data in low-label regimes due to their spectral bias toward smooth, low-frequency functions and sensitivity to nuisance features, while standard self-supervised augmentations (noise injection, feature swapping) distort critical feature relationships in heterogeneous tabular inputs. The paper asks whether a hybrid architecture combining complementary inductive biases—neural smoothness and tree-based sharpness—can learn effective tabular representations without relying on handcrafted data augmentations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253178/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a tabular feature vector (mixed categorical and numerical columns, one-hot encoded and min-max normalized to [0,1]) and produces either a class label or a continuous regression prediction via a lightweight single-layer MLP head. During pretraining, each input is first transformed by sample-specific stochastic gating networks that output soft binary masks in [0,1]^D, creating per-sample masked views; at inference, only the neural encoder and its gating network are deployed, keeping the inference path lean. Evaluation is conducted in a low-label regime with 50–1000 labeled samples per dataset, pretrained on 2,000 unlabeled samples per class.

## Method

TANDEM (Tree-And-Neural Dual Encoder Model) is a hybrid masking autoencoder that jointly trains a 4-layer MLP neural encoder and an ensemble of oblivious soft decision trees (OSDTs) through a shared decoder, with each encoder fed by its own sample-specific stochastic gating network. The OSDT encoder uses depth-wise consistent projection vectors and temperature-scaled sigmoid splits to produce a soft distribution over 2^L leaves per tree, averaged across T trees to form the latent code. Training combines three losses: per-encoder reconstruction (MSE to the original input), cross-encoder alignment (MSE between the two reconstructions), and latent representation similarity (cosine distance between the two latent vectors). The tree encoder's strong high-frequency inductive bias acts as model-based augmentation during training, nudging the neural encoder's representations toward tabular-relevant sharp patterns, while the gating networks serve as learnable, sample-specific feature filters that suppress nuisance variation. At inference, only the neural encoder and a frozen gating network are used, preserving compatibility with downstream tasks.

## Results

On 19 classification datasets at 400 labeled samples, TANDEM achieves a mean accuracy of 0.7124 and mean rank of 1.58, outperforming the closest competitor TabPFN (0.7012, rank 2.56) and all tree-based baselines (XGBoost 0.6706, CatBoost 0.6731). On 13 regression datasets at the same budget, TANDEM attains the lowest mean MSE of 0.3234 and best mean rank of 3.38, edging out CatBoost (0.3318) and XGBoost (0.3405). Across the full low-label regime (50–1000 labels), TANDEM remains the top or near-top method throughout, with statistically significant wins over most baselines on the majority of datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TANDEM wins on 17 of 19 classification datasets, losing only to TabPFN on EL (0.6940 vs 0.7723) and HI (0.6459 vs 0.6499), and is statistically significant over CatBoost (19/19 datasets), DeepTLF (19/19), and MLogReg (19/19). Against the strongest tree-based baselines, TANDEM outperforms XGBoost on 18/19 datasets (mean 0.7124 vs 0.6706) and CatBoost on 19/19 (0.7124 vs 0.6731). Among SSL baselines, TANDEM beats VIME on 16/19, SCARF on 17/19, and SubTab on 16/19 classification datasets. For regression, TANDEM achieves the best mean MSE (0.3234) and beats CatBoost on 12/13 datasets, XGBoost on 10/13, and all SSL baselines (VIME, SCARF, SubTab) on 13/13, though it trails CatBoost on the BF dataset (1.0057 vs 0.6565). TabPFN, limited to classification, is the closest overall competitor at mean accuracy 0.7012, with TANDEM winning on 13 of 18 comparable datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

TANDEM builds directly on the NODE architecture (Popov et al., 2019) for its oblivious soft decision tree encoder and on stochastic gating networks (Yamada et al., 2020; Jana et al., 2023) for its sample-specific feature masking, but repurposes both in a novel hybrid autoencoder framework. The key conceptual departure from prior SSL-for-tabular work (VIME, SCARF, SubTab) is shifting from data-level augmentation (noise, masking, corruption) to model-based augmentation: a structurally different encoder (OSDT) is used only during training to shape the neural encoder's representations, then discarded at inference. This positions TANDEM between the tree-based methods that dominate tabular benchmarks (XGBoost, CatBoost) and the deep-learning approaches that struggle with spectral bias, offering a unified training objective that exploits complementary inductive biases without requiring handcrafted augmentation schemes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a persistent and practically important gap: deep neural networks consistently underperform tree-based ensembles on tabular data, especially in low-label regimes common in healthcare and finance. By demonstrating that a training-only tree encoder can nudge neural representations toward tabular-relevant, high-frequency patterns, TANDEM offers a principled path toward closing this gap while preserving the flexibility of neural encoders for downstream tasks. Limitations include evaluation on relatively small OpenML datasets (≤1000 labels), a focus on low-label settings that may not reflect large-scale tabular deployments, and the modest margin over TabPFN (~1% mean accuracy). An open question is whether the hybrid-encoder + gating principle transfers to transformer-based tabular foundation models, as the authors themselves suggest for future work.
