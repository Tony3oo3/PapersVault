## Problem

Non-clairvoyant scheduling, where job processing times are unknown until completion, is limited to a competitive ratio of 2, while clairvoyant scheduling achieves optimality via Shortest Processing Time First. Existing learning-augmented approaches rely on static a priori predictions (job sizes or orderings) that cannot improve as the scheduler interacts with the system. This paper asks whether a dynamic feedback model—progress bars revealing estimated completion fractions over time—can interpolate between these two extremes and enable algorithms to refine their decisions during execution.

## Input / Output

The scheduler receives n jobs at time 0 with unknown processing times p_j, and observes for each job a progress bar φ_j: [0,1]→[0,1], a non-decreasing step function with granularity g that maps actual progress x to a displayed progress estimate. In the adversarial setting, the jump points β_j^(h) can be arbitrary; in the stochastic setting, they follow a Poisson point process of rate g. The algorithm produces a preemptive schedule (a sequence of job allocations over continuous time) and the objective is the sum of completion times ΣC_j. The progress bar feedback is the key distinguishing input: it provides a prediction π_j = e_j/X_j(e_j) on remaining job length that refines as more computation is allocated.

## Method

The paper develops three algorithmic contributions across adversarial and stochastic progress bar models. For single-signal (granularity-1) adversarial progress bars, Algorithm 1 alternates between SETF exploration and preferential execution of signaled jobs for a duration (1/(ρα)−1)·e_j(t), achieving (1+α)-consistency, (1+1/ρα)-robustness, and a smoothness bound governed by parameter ρ. A general combining algorithm (Algorithm 2) samples m = O(n^{2/3}(log g)^{1/3}) job pairs, runs them to completion, computes empirical mutual delays for each candidate algorithm, and schedules remaining jobs with the best-performing one—extending the permutation-prediction combination of Elias et al. to arbitrary algorithms with computable delays. For stochastic progress bars modeled as Poisson point processes, a Repeated Explore-Then-Commit algorithm (Algorithm 3) runs Round-Robin until a job's displayed progress reaches threshold k/(g+1) with k = ⌈(g/2)^{2/3}⌉+1, then commits to that job, achieving a competitive ratio of 1+O(g^{−1/3}) which is proven asymptotically optimal via a matching lower bound.

## Results

For truthful single-signal progress bars (granularity 1), the paper proves a (1+α)-competitive algorithm that is optimal even for randomized algorithms (Theorem 2.1). For untrusted signals, Algorithm 1 achieves (1+α)-consistency, (1+1/ρα)-robustness, and a smoothness bound of (2n/(ρ(1−ρ)α²))·Σ|βᵢ−α|pᵢ (Theorem 2.4), with a matching lower bound of 1+Ω(√(1/α)) on robustness for any (1+α)-consistent algorithm (Theorem 2.5). In the stochastic Poisson progress bar model, the Repeated Explore-Then-Commit algorithm achieves a competitive ratio of 1+(12/g)^(1/3), which is asymptotically optimal given a matching lower bound of 1+(1/36)g^(−1/3) (Theorems 4.2–4.3). The general combining algorithm (Theorem 3.1) achieves E[ALG] ≤ min_h A^(h) + (9/4)n^(5/3)(log g)^(1/3)·max pᵢ, yielding consistency 1+o(1) and robustness 2+o(1) when max pᵢ = o(n^(−5/3)·OPT).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the time-sharing baseline of Purohit et al. [PSK18], which achieves a consistency-robustness tradeoff of (1/λ, 2/(1−λ)), the paper's delayed-predictions approach (Section 2.3) improves consistency to 2/(1+λ) at the same robustness level 2/(1−λ), and the combining algorithm (Section 3.3) further achieves consistency 1+o(1) with robustness 2+o(1) on instances where max pᵢ = o(n^(−5/3)·OPT)—the first result to simultaneously approach both the consistency lower bound of 1 and the robustness lower bound of 2. In the empirical comparison (Figure 2b), the combining algorithm outperforms both time sharing and delayed predictions in robustness and consistency while requiring no hyperparameter tuning, with the gap widening for larger n. Against Round-Robin (the classical 2-competitive non-clairvoyant baseline of Motwani et al. [MPT94]), the paper's algorithms strictly improve whenever progress bar information is available: the truthful single-signal algorithm achieves 1+α < 2 for any α < 1, and the stochastic algorithm approaches competitive ratio 1 as granularity g grows. The combining framework also generalizes and improves upon the permutation-prediction combining strategy of Elias et al. [EKMM24] by extending it to arbitrary scheduling algorithms with computable delays, not just permutation-based ones.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the classical non-clairvoyant scheduling problem (MPT94) by introducing a progress bar feedback model that interpolates between the zero-information non-clairvoyant setting and the full-information clairvoyant setting. It builds directly on the learning-augmented scheduling framework (PSK18, EKMM24), extending the time-sharing robustification technique and the permutation-prediction combining strategy to a general algorithm-combining framework with computable delays. The stochastic progress bar analysis draws on multi-armed bandit and mortal bandit literature (LS20, CKRU08), adapting explore-then-commit strategies to the continuous-time scheduling context.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The progress bar model provides a realistic and natural information framework for practical scheduling (operating systems, HPC, ML training) where information improves over time, addressing a key limitation of static prediction models. The general combining algorithm (Theorem 3.1) is a reusable tool that achieves simultaneous near-perfect consistency and robustness (1+o(1), 2+o(1)) on a broad class of instances, substantially narrowing the gap to known lower bounds. Open questions include closing the Θ(α^{-1/2}) gap between upper and lower bounds on robustness for the single-signal model, and whether even a single uniformly distributed signal suffices to break the competitive ratio of 2 in the stochastic setting.
