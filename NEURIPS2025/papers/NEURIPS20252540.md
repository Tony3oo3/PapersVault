## Problem

Existing multimodal benchmarks either target general domains or rely on expert-curated agricultural questions, failing to capture the authentic distribution of real-world farmer and gardener challenges. No comprehensive benchmark evaluates VLMs on knowledge-intensive agricultural tasks that demand both fine-grained visual perception and domain expertise, with questions derived from actual user-expert interactions rather than recruited annotators.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252540/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each question pairs one or more user-uploaded agricultural images (varying in resolution, aspect ratio, and quality, unlike curated datasets such as iNaturalist) with a text prompt in either 4-option multiple-choice or open-ended format. For OEQs, identification tasks (species, disease, pest) expect short-phrase answers, while management and symptom tasks require multi-statement responses averaging 2.7 and 1.8 factual statements respectively. The benchmark spans 10,164 species, 5,071 pests, and 4,545 diseases across five knowledge types, with multi-image understanding being a common requirement.

## Method

AGMMU is constructed through a four-stage pipeline from 116,231 real-world Cooperative Extension conversations: (1) LLaMA-70B categorizes each dialogue into agricultural subdomains, (2) LLaMA-405B extracts structured knowledge (species, disease, symptom, management, pest) using domain-specific prompts with manually annotated in-context examples, (3) GPT-4o formats extracted facts into MCQs and OEQs, and (4) human annotators verify faithfulness, expert certainty, image quality, and MCQ feasibility, yielding 746 high-quality questions from 1,742 candidates. A complementary development set, AGBASE (57,079 multimodal facts), is built from non-overlapping conversations for fine-tuning. OEQ evaluation uses GPT-4.1 as judge with a four-level grading scheme (correct, partially correct, incorrect, not attempted) and harmonic-mean scoring adapted from SimpleQA, while MCQs use standard letter-matching accuracy. Fine-tuning experiments employ LoRA adapters on LLaVA-1.5-7B and Qwen2.5-VL, combining AGBASE with instruction-tuning data to preserve general capabilities.

## Results

On AGMMU, even the strongest proprietary model (Gemini 2.5 Pro) achieves only 47.7% average on open-ended questions (OEQs) and 88.9% on multiple-choice questions (MCQs), confirming the benchmark's difficulty. The best open-source model, LLaMA-3.2-11B, scores 27.0% on OEQs and 78.3% on MCQs, trailing proprietary models by 20+ points on OEQs. Management instruction and symptom description are the hardest subcategories across all models (e.g., GPT-4o scores only 24.2% and 25.7% respectively on OEQs). Fine-tuning LLaVA-1.5-7B on AGBASE boosts OEQ performance by up to 11.6% on average, narrowing the open-source gap.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among proprietary models, Gemini 2.5 Pro leads on OEQs (47.7% avg) and MCQs (88.9% avg), excelling in species identification (77.1% OEQ) and insect/pest identification (61.1% OEQ), while Gemini 1.5 Pro uniquely leads in management instructions (40.1% OEQ) and GPT-4o leads in symptom description (25.7% OEQ). Among open-source models, LLaMA-3.2-11B is the strongest overall (27.0% OEQ, 78.3% MCQ), outperforming LLaVA-1.5-7B (16.7% OEQ) by over 10 points and VILA1.5-13B (23.6% OEQ) by 3.4 points. The open-source-to-proprietary gap is stark on OEQs (27.0% vs. 47.7% best) but narrows on MCQs (78.3% vs. 88.9%), suggesting open-source models can recognize correct answers among options but struggle to recall them freely. Fine-tuning LLaVA-1.5-7B on AGBASE yields up to 11.6% average OEQ improvement, with the 10k SFT mixture (AGBASE + 10k LLaVA-Instruct) slightly outperforming the 57k SFT mixture, indicating that smaller curated domain data mixed with general instruction data is more effective than larger generic mixes. Error analysis reveals that knowledge errors dominate (48.6–51.1% of errors across Gemini, LLaMA, and GPT-o4), followed by perceptual errors (22.2–37.5%), confirming that domain knowledge rather than visual perception is the primary bottleneck.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper combines the MCQ evaluation format from MMMU with the open-ended, factuality-focused OEQ format from SimpleQA, applying both to the agricultural domain. It departs from prior agricultural benchmarks (CROP, CDDM, iNaturalist) by sourcing questions and images from real user-expert conversations via USDA Cooperative Extension rather than from expert-curated or textbook materials, and by pairing the evaluation set with a large-scale training corpus (AGBASE). The fine-tuning experiments follow the standard LLaVA instruction-tuning recipe, using LoRA adapters to adapt open-source VLMs to agricultural knowledge.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AGMMU is the first agricultural VLM benchmark grounded in authentic farmer questions, filling a gap where prior datasets could not capture the distribution and complexity of real-world growing problems. The finding that even top proprietary models score below 50% on OEQs, and that simple fine-tuning on AGBASE narrows the open-to-closed-source gap by up to 11.6%, suggests the primary bottleneck is domain knowledge rather than architectural capability. Open questions include whether more sophisticated knowledge integration (e.g., multimodal RAG over AGBASE) can outperform plain fine-tuning, how to handle the multi-statement nature of management and symptom questions, and whether the LLM-as-judge protocol generalizes reliably to other agricultural subdomains.
