## Problem

Verified unlearning frameworks like SISA achieve efficient data removal for single models via sharding and checkpointing, but they break down in knowledge distillation pipelines: information from the full teacher ensemble propagates into every student constituent model during initial training, so any teacher-side unlearning forces costly complete student retraining. No prior method provides efficient, verified unlearning specifically for teacher-side updates within a KD setting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252392/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework operates on a pre-trained SISA teacher ensemble (M constituent models) and a student dataset partitioned into N shards, with each shard further divided into c_k chunks and r slices. At unlearning time, the input is a single data point to remove from either the teacher's or student's training set; the output is an updated student constituent model (reverted to a prior checkpoint and partially retrained) plus regenerated soft labels for affected chunks. At inference, the final student prediction is a non-trainable aggregation (e.g., averaging) over all N student constituent models, producing a class label for image or text inputs.

## Method

PURGE introduces a constituent mapping that partitions M teacher constituent models into N disjoint ensembles, each assigned to one student constituent model, so that unlearning in teacher T_{k,i} only affects student S_k. Within each student shard, data is further chunked into c_k ordered segments, and soft labels for chunk l are generated by an incrementally growing teacher subensemble T_{k,l} = ∪_{i≤l} T_{k,i}, which smooths supervisory signal transitions while confining each teacher's influence to chunks l ≥ i. Each chunk is then sliced into r sub-slices following standard SISA checkpointing, and the student trains incrementally over all slices with cumulative data, storing intermediate states. When a teacher unlearns, the affected student reverts to the checkpoint just before the first chunk that used that teacher and retrains only the remaining chunks with regenerated soft labels, yielding a theoretical speedup of at least N× over naive SISA (where N is the number of student constituent models).

## Results

On MNIST with M=32 teacher and N=32 student constituent models, PURGE achieves 97.16% accuracy (vs. 97.08% for the SISA baseline) and reduces per-request student retraining time from 737.14 s to 23.17 s, yielding a ~32× speed-up over naive SISA for teacher-side unlearning. On SVHN, PURGE reaches 83.09% accuracy (SISA: 83.44%). On the more challenging ARC reasoning task with Qwen2.5-32B→Qwen2.5-3B distillation, PURGE requires only 2.36 GPU-hours per teacher-side unlearning request versus 18.9 GPU-hours for naive SISA, while achieving 26.09% accuracy (SISA: 26.42%; teacher: 27.76%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole external baseline is naive SISA applied independently to both teacher and student ensembles, where any teacher-side unlearning forces full retraining of all N student constituent models. PURGE decisively wins on unlearning speed: on MNIST it achieves a ~32× reduction in per-request student retraining time (23.17 s vs 737.14 s), and on ARC a ~8× reduction in GPU-hours (2.36 vs 18.9). On predictive accuracy, PURGE essentially ties the SISA baseline: it is marginally better on MNIST (+0.08 pp) and marginally worse on SVHN (−0.35 pp) and ARC (−0.33 pp), while matching SISA on CIFAR-100 and SST5. The internal single-teacher soft-label ablation confirms the design choice: with N=1 student and c=32 chunks, the ablation loses 1.30 pp on MNIST and 7.35 pp on SVHN relative to SISA, whereas PURGE loses only 0.14 pp and 0.18 pp respectively, validating the incremental multi-teacher strategy for training stability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SISA verified-unlearning framework (Bourtoule et al., 2021) into the knowledge distillation paradigm, where prior work had not addressed teacher-side unlearning. While approximate KD-unlearning methods (SCRUB, RKLD) and SISA adaptations for other paradigms (federated learning, GNNs, NLP adapters) exist, none provide exact unlearning guarantees for the teacher model within a distillation pipeline. PURGE departs from the naive SISA-to-KD application by introducing constituent mapping and incremental multi-teacher distillation, which structurally preserves data isolation during the soft-label generation phase—the key mechanism that breaks isolation in standard distillation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As distilled models become the standard deployment vehicle for large foundation models, the inability to efficiently unlearn from the teacher side creates a practical compliance gap under GDPR/CCPA that this work directly addresses. The at-least-N× speedup guarantee is clean and empirically validated, but the framework inherits SISA's storage overhead (multiple checkpoints per constituent model) and shows accuracy limitations on complex tasks (CIFAR-100, ARC reasoning), where sharding constrains per-model data. Open questions include whether more sophisticated multi-teacher aggregation can close the remaining accuracy gap while preserving isolation, and how the student/teacher-side unloading trade-off (parameter r) should be tuned in production settings with mixed request patterns.
