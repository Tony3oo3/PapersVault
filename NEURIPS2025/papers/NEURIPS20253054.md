## Problem

State-of-the-art weather and climate models operate at kilometer scale and cannot resolve individual shallow cumulus clouds, which span less than a kilometer yet cover up to 40% of Earth's surface. Existing observation systems face a fundamental trade-off: satellites provide wide spatial coverage but revisit only every days, while cloud-profiling radars offer high temporal resolution but sample a single narrow vertical column. No prior method reconstructs full 3D cloud physical properties (liquid water content, heights, thickness) at both high spatial and temporal resolution from ground-based imagery.

## Input / Output

The method consumes N synchronized skyward camera images (six cameras in the deployment, baselines 190–350 m) along with their calibrated poses and intrinsics, sampled every 5 seconds. It produces a full 3D liquid water content volume on a 200×200×160 grid (25 m voxels over 5 km × 5 km × 4 km), plus 2.5D maps of liquid water path, cloud base height, and cloud top height. At inference, tracking the 3D LWC field across time yields a height-resolved horizontal wind profile, effectively turning a static reconstruction pipeline into a 4D (3D + time) cloud state estimator.

## Method

The core insight is that cloud layers are vertically thin relative to their horizontal extent, so a homography mapping each image to a plane at a given altitude reduces the 3D reconstruction to a 2D-to-2D task. DINOv2 features (upsampled via LoftUp) are lifted into a multi-height feature volume using this cloud homography, then flattened and processed by a 2D CNN (EDM-based) conditioned on height via adaLN to predict LWP, CBH, and geometric thickness. These 2.5D predictions are lifted into an initial 3D LWC field (Eq. 2, with a linear profile toward cloud tops), and a sparse transformer (TRELLIS-based, 12 blocks, 12 heads) refines only the non-empty voxels, preserving the LWP constraint via softmax normalization along the height axis. Training is two-stage supervised learning on MicroHH large-eddy simulations (ARM, Cabauw, RICO scenarios rendered via Monte Carlo path tracing in Blender Cycles), with pre-training on Terragen volumes. Wind retrieval is decoupled from the reconstruction: CoTracker3 tracks points on height-sliced LWC over 20-frame sequences to estimate horizontal displacement, converted to wind speed using the 25 m pixel size.

## Results

On the 17-hour real-world dataset (12 cumulus days, 5 km × 5 km area, 25 m voxels, 5 s cadence), Cloud4D achieves a cloud occupancy F1 of 0.70, LWC MAE of 0.03 g m⁻³, LWP MAE of 0.06 kg m⁻², CBH MAE of 189.6 m, and CTH MAE of 295.8 m against collocated radar ground truth. This corresponds to a single-digit (<10%) relative error on LWC and LWP. The system operates at 25 m spatial and 5 s temporal resolution, an order-of-magnitude improvement in temporal resolution over Sentinel-2 (~5-day revisit) and MODIS (~1-day revisit).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole numerical baseline is VIP-CT (Ronen et al., 2022), a satellite-based cloud tomography method that implicitly learns multi-view geometry from orthographic satellite views. Cloud4D outperforms VIP-CT on every reported metric: occupancy F1 improves from 0.40 to 0.70, LWC MAE drops from 0.13 to 0.03 g m⁻³ (4.3×), LWP MAE drops from 0.39 to 0.06 kg m⁻² (6.5×), CBH MAE drops from 791 m to 190 m (4.2×), and CTH MAE drops from 1021 m to 296 m (3.5×). The authors note that VIP-CT's failure on ground-based cameras is expected because it relies on unobstructed orthographic views and implicitly learned geometry, whereas ground-based cameras have highly variable viewing directions. Beyond the numerical baseline, Cloud4D is qualitatively compared against ERA5 reanalysis (km-scale, hourly), collocated radar (30 m vertical resolution, 30 s cadence, single beam), a radar wind profiler (70 m height resolution, 5-min cadence), and Sentinel-2/MODIS satellite imagery; in all cases Cloud4D matches or exceeds the reference in spatial and temporal fidelity while covering a 5 km × 5 km area.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges three research threads: multi-view stereo cost-volume approaches (MVSNet, Cascade Cost Volume), satellite-based cloud tomography (VIP-CT, 3DeepCT), and ground-based stereophotogrammetry of clouds (Romps & Oktem 2018, Oktem et al. 2014). It departs from satellite-based methods by explicitly modeling camera geometry via a cloud-layer homography rather than implicitly learning it, and from prior ground-based work by recovering physical properties (LWC) rather than only geometric ones (CBH). The architecture repurposes vision foundation models (DINOv2 + LoftUp) as feature extractors and a sparse 3D transformer (TRELLIS-style) for volumetric refinement, combining these in a pipeline not previously applied to atmospheric observation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Cloud4D closes a long-standing observational gap: no existing instrument captures individual shallow cumulus microphysics at both the spatial (~25 m) and temporal (5 s) scales needed to validate or train sub-kilometer weather models. The practical implication is that a cheap, scalable camera array can substitute for expensive radar/satellite infrastructure for high-frequency cloud monitoring. Key limitations remain: the system is validated only on shallow cumulus (single layer, <2000 m), is susceptible to weather occlusion (rain, fog), and the external baseline comparison is limited to one satellite method (VIP-CT) that was not designed for ground-based geometry. Extending to multi-layer and optically thick clouds, embedding radiative-transfer constraints, and coupling with differentiable simulators are the natural next steps.
