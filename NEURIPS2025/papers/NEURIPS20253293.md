## Problem

Existing automotive CFD datasets (DrivAerNet++, DrivAerML) suffer from inadequate mesh resolution, missing engine compartments and cooling systems, and wind tunnel validation errors exceeding 5%, making them unsuitable for training production-grade surrogate models in industrial vehicle development workflows. This paper addresses that gap by introducing DrivAerStar, a 12,000-sample dataset generated with the industry-standard STAR-CCM+ solver that achieves 1.04% mean drag coefficient error against wind tunnel measurements—a five-fold improvement over prior datasets.

## Input / Output

Input consists of 3D vehicle geometry as STL meshes (≥4 million triangles) and complementary point clouds, parameterized by 20 CAD parameters varied via Free Form Deformation across three DrivAer rear-body configurations (Fastback, Estateback, Notchback) sampled with Latin Hypercube Sampling. Output includes surface pressure and wall shear stress distributions, integrated drag/lift coefficients, volumetric velocity and turbulent kinetic energy fields, 3D streamlines, and iso-surfaces, stored as VTK surface files and EnSight flow field files totaling ~20 TB. Each simulation domain contains ~12 million hexahedral-dominant cells with 6-layer prismatic boundary layers and wall y+ values constrained to [30, 200].

## Method

The dataset is generated through a three-stage pipeline: (1) FFD-based parametric morphing of three DrivAer reference bodies varying 20 local and global CAD parameters; (2) automated STAR-CCM+ mesh generation with sub-regional surface adaptation, 10% relative refinement on critical features (drivetrain, grille), and strict y+ control to overcome OpenFOAM's wall shear stress deficiencies; (3) 3D steady RANS simulation using SST k-ω turbulence with hybrid all-y+ wall treatment, SIMPLE pressure-velocity coupling, and AMG linear solvers at 40 m/s freestream (Re ≈ 9.5×10⁶–1.5×10⁷). Unlike prior datasets, DrivAerStar includes complete engine bay assemblies with porous-media cooling models (0.6 porosity) capturing internal airflow channels. For benchmarking, the paper trains Transolver, GNOT, and PointNet architectures in a supervised setting to predict surface pressure, wall shear stress, and drag coefficients from geometry, evaluating both single-configuration and multi-configuration generalization with systematic data-scaling experiments from 400 to 12,000 training samples.

## Results

DrivAerStar achieves a wind tunnel validation error of 1.04% mean relative deviation in drag coefficient (C_D), a five-fold improvement over DrivAerNet++ (≤5.0%) and DrivAerML (≤6.5%). The dataset contains 12,000 samples with an average C_D precision of 0.005 and wall y+ range of [30, 200], compared to 8,000 samples (precision 0.012, y+ [50, 300]) for DrivAerNet++ and 500 samples (precision 0.010) for DrivAerML. In benchmark experiments, Transolver trained on the full 12,000-sample multi-configuration dataset achieves a C_D validation loss of 0.0266, a 29.07% improvement over the 400-sample baseline (0.0375), demonstrating effective data scaling.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DrivAerStar outperforms both existing automotive CFD datasets across every reported metric. Compared to DrivAerNet++ (Elrefaie et al., 2024), DrivAerStar provides 50% more samples (12,000 vs. 8,000), a tighter wall y+ range ([30, 200] vs. [50, 300]) indicating superior boundary layer resolution, a 2.4× improvement in average C_D precision (0.005 vs. 0.012), and a wind tunnel error of 1.04% versus ≤5.0%—roughly a five-fold reduction. Against DrivAerML (Ashton et al., 2024c), the advantage is even more pronounced: 24× more samples (12,000 vs. 500), 2× better C_D precision (0.005 vs. 0.010), and a wind tunnel error of 1.04% versus ≤6.5%. Additionally, DrivAerStar is the only dataset among the three to include PIV flow-field validation, complete engine compartment geometry, and cooling system components, addressing critical gaps that prevent industrial adoption of the prior datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DrivAer reference vehicle platform (Heft et al., 2012) and the existing automotive CFD dataset lineage (DrivAerNet++, DrivAerML, AeroSUV) by replacing the OpenFOAM-based simulation pipeline with the industry-standard STAR-CCM+ solver and adding complete engine compartment and cooling system geometry that prior datasets omitted. The key conceptual departure is treating mesh resolution and wall y+ control as first-class dataset design parameters rather than implementation details, arguing that prior datasets' 5–6.5% wind tunnel errors stem from solver-level deficiencies in OpenFOAM's wall shear stress calculation rather than merely insufficient sample counts. The ML benchmarking (Transolver, GNOT, PointNet) follows the established paradigm of neural operator and point-cloud architectures for geometry-to-flow prediction, without introducing new model architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DrivAerStar is the first automotive CFD dataset validated to sub-1% wind tunnel accuracy with complete internal vehicle geometry, making it the most credible training ground for industrial surrogate models that could compress design iteration from weeks to minutes. The 5× validation improvement over prior datasets reveals that the academic-industrial gap was partly a solver-fidelity problem, not just a data-volume problem, which reframes how the community should evaluate CFD datasets. Open questions include whether the 20-parameter FFD space adequately captures the full industrial design space, whether the single Reynolds number range (9.46×10⁶–1.48×10⁷) generalizes to other operating conditions, and how the dataset extends to multidisciplinary coupling (thermal management, structural dynamics) that the authors flag as future work.
