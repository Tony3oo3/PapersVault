## Problem

Current multi-modal LLMs are limited to turn-based Q&A or offline video narration and cannot react asynchronously to a live video stream to provide interactive step-by-step task guidance. Specifically, no existing model can simultaneously deliver the next instruction, detect its successful completion, and flag mistakes in real time, and no dedicated benchmark with densely annotated, timestamped instructions and mistake feedback exists to evaluate this capability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250755/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a streaming egocentric video at 2 fps and 448×448 resolution alongside a text-based step-by-step recipe plan, processing frames sequentially and deciding autonomously when to emit a response via special <vision> and <response> tokens. Outputs are timed text messages: the next instruction (issued at step start) and feedback (success acknowledgment at step completion or a mistake alert at the exact timestamp the error occurs visually). In the advanced planning set, a divergent action triggers an external re-planner (Qwen3-32B) that updates the remaining instruction sequence before the next instruction is issued.

## Method

LIVEMAMBA pairs an InternViT-300M vision encoder with a Q-Former adapter (reducing 1025 per-frame tokens to 32 via VisionZip and four cross-attention layers) and a lightweight Mamba-130M language backbone for efficient streaming inference. A 'when-to-say' mechanism using <vision>/<response> tokens lets the model request the next frame or emit feedback at the appropriate timestep without external prompting. Training is two-stage: pre-training aligns the Q-Former on grounding (LVIS, EPIC-KITCHENS VISOR) and action-recognition (SSv2, Ego4D) data, then fine-tuning on the Qualcomm Interactive Cooking dataset with three augmentations—temporal jittering (±30 s) to absorb autoregressive drift, instruction-completion augmentation (converting EPIC-KITCHENS/Ego4D actions into instruction-feedback pairs), and counterfactual mistake augmentation (substituting nouns/verbs via Qwen2.5-32B to synthesize plausible errors). For divergent mistakes in the advanced planning set, an external Qwen3-32B re-planner extracts the performed action and decides whether to repeat, remove, or reorder remaining steps, keeping the lightweight backbone focused on timely feedback.

## Results

On the Qualcomm Interactive Cooking benchmark (Main Set, zero-shot streaming), Gemini-2.5-Flash achieves the best instruction completion accuracy at 23.1%, while all models struggle severely with mistake detection (best F1 = 0.05 by Qwen2-VL-7B). After fine-tuning, LIVEMAMBA reaches 31.5% IC-Acc and 0.13 mistake F1 on the Main Set, substantially outperforming the fine-tuned VideoLLM-Online baseline (7.6% IC-Acc, 0.01 F1). In the turn-based evaluation, LIVEMAMBA achieves 51.0% IC-Acc and 0.19 mistake F1, surpassing the best zero-shot model (Qwen2.5-VL-7B at 38.9% IC-Acc, 0.06 F1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

In the zero-shot streaming setting (Table 3), all nine SOTA multi-modal LLMs perform poorly: Gemini-2.5-Flash leads with 23.1% IC-Acc but only 0.02 mistake F1, while Qwen2-VL-7B achieves the best mistake F1 (0.05) at the cost of very low IC-Acc (6.3%). Models like LLaVA-NeXT, Video-ChatGPT, VideoChat2, Video-LLaVA, and VideoLLaMA3-7B essentially fail at both tasks (IC-Acc ≤ 2.0, F1 = 0.00), likely because their Q&A-style training causes them to answer 'yes' too early. VideoLLM-Online, designed for narration, produces overly verbose narrations that trigger false-positive mistake alerts (Rec = 0.98 but Prec = 0.02). After fine-tuning on Qualcomm Interactive Cooking, LIVEMAMBA outperforms the fine-tuned VideoLLM-Online by 4.1× on IC-Acc (31.5% vs. 7.6%) and 13× on mistake F1 (0.13 vs. 0.01) on the Main Set. In the turn-based evaluation (Table 5), LIVEMAMBA (51.0% IC-Acc, 0.19 F1) beats all zero-shot baselines, with the closest competitor being Qwen2.5-VL-7B (38.9% IC-Acc, 0.06 F1), demonstrating that fine-tuning with the proposed data augmentation scheme is critical for mistake recognition.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the streaming video LLM line of work (VideoLLM-Online, StreamChat, TimeChat-Online) from narration and VQA to a fundamentally different interactive paradigm: the model must autonomously decide when to speak, detect instruction completion, and flag mistakes in real time without user prompting. The 'when-to-say' mechanism borrows special-token design from VideoLLM-Online [8] and the live fitness coaching framework [45], but the paper departs sharply by introducing a goal-directed, mistake-aware guidance loop with iterative re-planning via an external LLM (Qwen3-32B). Architecturally, it replaces the standard transformer language backbone with a Mamba-130M state-space model to enable edge-deployable inference over long video streams, paired with an InternViT + Q-Former vision encoder and VisionZip token compression.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first dedicated benchmark for live, situated step-by-step coaching with timestamped mistake feedback, filling a clear gap left by existing procedural-activity datasets that lack user errors or timed interactive feedback. The strikingly low absolute performance of all evaluated models (best streaming IC-Acc of 31.5% for LIVEMAMBA, mistake F1 of 0.13) reveals that current multi-modal LLMs are far from capable of real-time task guidance, a finding that reframes expectations for AI assistant deployment. Key open questions include generalizing beyond cooking, handling subtle measurement errors (teaspoon vs. tablespoon), reducing the 6.1-second re-planning latency, and whether the non-reactive simulation setup adequately approximates truly interactive scenarios.
