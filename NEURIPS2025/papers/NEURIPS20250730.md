## Problem

While diffusion-based generative models can produce seemingly endless variations of training data, there is no principled information-theoretic measure of how much information the network actually stores about the data distribution. The paper asks: how many bytes of data is the learned distribution p_d worth, and how does this depend on the specific diffusive process used to erase and reinstate that information?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250730/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During training, the model consumes finite samples from p_d (28×28 grayscale MNIST, 32×32 RGB CIFAR-10, or D-dimensional Gaussian mixtures) and learns to reverse their diffusion to noise. At generation time, a random Gaussian vector is the sole input, and the output is a sample from the reconstructed distribution. The paper additionally computes the neural entropy S_NN by simulating the forward SDE and taking Monte Carlo averages of the network output e_θ along the diffusion path, yielding a scalar information measure in nats (or bytes when divided by log 2).

## Method

The paper introduces neural entropy S_NN, defined as the time-integral of (σ²/2)·E[‖e_θ‖²] along the forward diffusion trajectory, which approximates the total entropy S_tot produced by diffusing p_d to equilibrium. The training objective is an 'entropy matching' loss (Eq. 17) that upper-bounds the KL divergence between p_d and the generated distribution, making minimization equivalent to tightening the information gap. The key theoretical result (Eq. 15) shows S_tot equals the KL divergence between p_d and the quasi-invariant equilibrium state, linking the measure to the Jarzynski equality and the thermodynamic uncertainty relation. Empirically, the paper demonstrates that S_NN scales logarithmically with the number of training samples N for U-Net image models (reflecting efficient ensemble compression), while MLP models on unstructured Gaussian mixtures show no such scaling and degrade at low N due to overfitting.

## Results

On MNIST and CIFAR-10 image diffusion models (U-Net), neural entropy S_NN scales approximately logarithmically with the number of training samples N, meaning the marginal information gained per sample decreases as ~1/N. On Gaussian mixture transport experiments (D=6, 5 components, N=8192), the standard VP process produces less total entropy and achieves more accurate reconstruction of p_d than both the VPx (κ=σ₀=0.1) and Straight Line (SL) processes, which subdue randomness in the diffusion and thereby increase the information load on the network. The SL process exhibits a singularity in entropy production rate at T=1, manifesting as a divergence in the S_NN curve during early training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline comparison is between the Straight Line Diffusion (SL) process from Ni et al. [31] and the Variance-Preserving (VP/VPx) processes on Gaussian mixture data. The SL process, which uses a strong drift-to-noise ratio to produce nearly straight trajectories, generates a narrower quasi-invariant distribution p_eq^(t) ∝ exp(−y²/σ₀²), increasing the KL divergence to p_d and thereby raising S_tot. Empirically, the VP model (with p_eq = N(0, I_D)) trains substantially faster and produces a more faithful reconstruction of p_d than either VPx or SL (Fig. 10), because the 'distance' from p_d to its equilibrium state is smaller. On image datasets (MNIST, CIFAR-10), the comparison is instead across sample sizes N: neural entropy grows as ~log N rather than linearly, demonstrating that diffusion models compress ensemble statistics far more efficiently than storing individual images. This logarithmic scaling is absent in the MLP-based Gaussian mixture experiments, where weak inductive biases cause overfitting at low N and S_NN can even exceed S_tot.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the SDE-based diffusion model framework (Sohl-Dickstein et al., Song et al.) and the maximum-likelihood training bound of Song et al. (2021), reframing the training objective as an 'entropy matching' loss that is nearly identical to flow matching (Lipman et al., 2022). Conceptually, it draws on Schrödinger's 1931 thought experiment on time-reversal, the Jarzynski equality, and the thermodynamic uncertainty relation to define a new quantity—neural entropy—that quantifies the information a diffusion model must store to reverse its forward process. The paper departs from prior information-theoretic analyses of generative models by tying the stored information not to the Shannon entropy of the data distribution but to the total entropy produced by the specific diffusive process, making it process-dependent rather than distribution-dependent.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, thermodynamically grounded measure of information content in diffusion models and reveals a non-obvious practical consequence: straighter/faster forward trajectories (e.g., straight-line diffusion) impose a higher information load on the network, creating a thermodynamic speed-accuracy tradeoff. The empirical finding that neural entropy scales logarithmically with the number of training samples for image models (but not for Gaussian mixtures on MLPs) suggests diffusion models compress ensemble statistics far more efficiently than per-sample storage, though the paper offers no mechanistic explanation for this scaling. Open questions include whether the log-N trend persists in diffusion transformers, its connection to known scaling laws, and how to extend the framework to discrete diffusion (e.g., LLMs) and score-matching models where the entropy interpretation is less clean.
