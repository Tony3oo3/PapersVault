## Problem

Current 3D generators treat objects as monolithic shells, but many applications (animation, interactive design, manufacturing) require objects to be decomposed into semantically meaningful, editable parts. Existing part-aware methods either rely on expensive multi-view image generators (PartGen) or require pre-segmented 3D masks as input (HoloPart), and none can automatically discover the number and identity of parts while ensuring they fit together cohesively without 3D annotations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255132/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts three input modalities: a full 3D mesh (object-to-parts), a single RGB image (image-to-parts), or an RGB image paired with K 2D binary part masks (masks-to-parts). The output is a variable-length sequence of K complete 3D parts represented as SDF zero-level-set meshes, where K is determined autoregressively by the model emitting a zero-valued [EoT] latent. In the object-to-parts case, the input mesh may be incomplete (a 'shell'), and the model simultaneously completes the parts to recover the full object.

## Method

The key insight is that 3DShape2VecSet's VecSet latent space is inherently compositional: concatenating latent codes of two parts decodes to the union of their surfaces without retraining. AutoPartGen exploits this by generating a sequence of part latents z^(1),...,z^(K) autoregressively using a DiT-based flow-matching diffusion model, where each part is conditioned on the overall object latent z̃, a re-encoded representation of all previously generated parts, and scenario-specific evidence (image features from DINOv2, masked images, or 3D geometry tokens). A single model handles all three scenarios via random condition dropping during training, and classifier-free guidance with separate weights (w_img, w_geom) allows users to trade off image adherence versus geometric coherence at inference. The re-encoding strategy decodes previously generated parts back to point clouds and re-encodes them into a fixed-length latent, which ablations show slightly outperforms direct concatenation or a learned latent fuser.

## Results

On the PartObjaverse-Tiny benchmark for 3D part completion, AutoPartGen achieves a part-level IoU of 0.665, F-score of 0.861, and Chamfer Distance of 0.047, while the assembled whole object reaches IoU 0.832, F-score 0.967, and CD 0.012. These are the best numbers across all three metrics for both individual parts and the merged object, surpassing both HoloPart and PartGen. The evaluation uses 64³ voxel grids for IoU and a 0.02 distance threshold for F-score.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AutoPartGen outperforms both baselines on every reported metric. Against HoloPart [60], which has access to a partial 3D object plus 3D part segmentations, AutoPartGen improves part IoU from 0.658 to 0.665, part F-score from 0.836 to 0.861, and part CD from 0.065 to 0.047, while also requiring no 3D mask input. Against PartGen [5], which uses multi-view diffusion without 3D masks, the gains are larger: part IoU improves from 0.614 to 0.665 (+5.1 pts), part CD drops from 0.121 to 0.047 (−61%), and whole-object F-score rises from 0.921 to 0.967. The paper attributes PartGen's lower CD to its lack of explicit joint-distribution modeling over parts, which causes overlapping and intersecting parts, whereas AutoPartGen's autoregressive conditioning on previously generated parts ensures geometric coherence. HoloPart's advantage over PartGen is explained by its access to richer input (partial 3D geometry + 3D masks), yet AutoPartGen still surpasses it using only 2D masks and a partial 3D shell.

## Relation to Prior Work

AutoPartGen builds on the 3DShape2VecSet latent representation (Zhang et al., 2023) and introduces the observation that its latents are inherently compositional—concatenating two latent codes decodes to the union of the corresponding surfaces. For generation, it adopts a DiT-based flow-matching diffusion model conditioned via DINOv2 image features and classifier-free guidance, unifying three input scenarios (3D object, image, 2D masks) in a single autoregressive model. This departs from PartGen, which relies on multi-view diffusion models for segmentation and completion, and from HoloPart, which requires pre-segmented 3D masks; AutoPartGen instead generates parts sequentially, letting the model itself decide part count and ordering without external segmentation or multi-view infrastructure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The compositional property of VecSet latents is a reusable insight that could benefit other part-based or scene-level 3D generation tasks beyond this paper's scope. The autoregressive formulation elegantly handles the variable and unknown number of parts, and the single-model design simplifies the pipeline relative to multi-view approaches. Key limitations include the restriction to bounded scenes (inherited from the VAE latent space), the lack of explicit granularity control outside the masks-to-parts setting, and a failure mode with repeated/identical parts. Open questions include extending to unbounded world generation, incorporating high-level semantic controls over decomposition granularity, and whether the VecSet compositionality property generalizes to other latent 3D representations.
