## Problem

Scaling LLM reasoning beyond math and coding is bottlenecked by the scarcity of diverse, high-quality, and genuinely challenging reasoning questions. Existing datasets are either confined to narrow domains with short, rule-verifiable answers or rely on heavy human curation and rule-based filtering, leaving the vast landscape of open-ended, multi-domain reasoning underrepresented in training data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252246/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes raw pretraining documents from DCLM-baseline and FineMath corpora and produces 2.8M self-contained reasoning questions (averaging 55 words) paired with reference answers derivable from the source document (available for 81.68% of questions). Questions span STEM, economics, social sciences, law, and other domains, with answer lengths ranging from single words (10.7%) to long free-form analyses (50.9%), and each question is additionally accompanied by a Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct response serving as a distillation teacher signal.

## Method

The approach backtranslates reasoning questions from pretraining documents using LLMs at every stage, eliminating human annotation and rule-based filtering. First, an LLM scores each document on four axes (Problem Completeness, Complexity/Technical Depth, Technical Correctness, and Thinking/Reasoning); only documents scoring high on complexity and reasoning proceed. Second, an LLM synthesizes a novel, self-contained question grounded in the document's content and verifies whether a correct reference answer can be extracted. Third, near-duplicates are removed via word-level MinHash (similarity threshold 0.55) and benchmark contamination is filtered using 13-gram matching. The resulting dataset is then validated through three training paradigms: supervised distillation from DeepSeek-R1 (showing steeper scaling than curated datasets like LIMO and s1K-1.1), unsupervised self-training via RFT/DPO with self-rewarding (self-score-filtered DPO achieves the best average of 43.67), and preliminary RLVR with GRPO and a General Verifier.

## Results

In the long-CoT distillation setting (Table 2), 1,000 randomly sampled NATURALREASONING questions achieve an average of 75.9 across GPQA-Diamond (63.5), MMLU-Pro (78.0), and MATH-500 (86.2) when distilling DeepSeek-R1 responses into Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct, matching or exceeding carefully curated datasets LIMO (73.3 avg, 817 questions) and s1K-1.1 (75.6 avg, 1,000 questions). Scaling to 100K NATURALREASONING questions yields 78.9 avg, approaching DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-70B (79.4 avg, 800K questions) while outperforming it on GPQA-Diamond (67.3 vs 65.2) and MMLU-Pro (79.5 vs 78.5). In the SFT scaling experiments, 1.5M NATURALREASONING examples surpass Llama3.1-8B-Instruct on average, while competing datasets (OpenMathInstruct-2, WebInstruct) fail to do so even at 2.8M examples.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the curated reasoning datasets LIMO (817 questions) and s1K-1.1 (1,000 questions), a random 1K sample from NATURALREASONING outperforms both on GPQA-Diamond (63.5 vs 56.5 and 62.7) and MMLU-Pro (78.0 vs 76.8 and 77.4), while tying on MATH-500 (~86.2–86.6). At 100K scale, NATURALREASONING surpasses DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-70B (trained on 800K examples) on GPQA-Diamond (67.3 vs 65.2) and MMLU-Pro (79.5 vs 78.5) but trails on MATH-500 (89.8 vs 94.5), reflecting the math-specific strength of the latter. In the SFT scaling experiments, NATURALREASONING is the only dataset whose 1.5M subset surpasses Llama3.1-8B-Instruct on average; OpenMathInstruct-2 excels on MATH (59.25 at 2.8M) but plateaus on GPQA (~26–27) and MMLU-Pro (~32–34), while WebInstruct shows inconsistent scaling with GPQA dropping from 29.02 (500K) to 25.37 (1.5M). Against OpenThoughts-114k (100K sample), NATURALREASONING wins on GPQA-Diamond (43.1 vs 37.8), MMLU-Pro (61.2 vs 59.0), and SuperGPQA (32.2 vs 30.0) but loses on MATH-500 (69.0 vs 82.2), confirming complementary domain coverage.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the instruction backtranslation paradigm (Li et al., 2023) from general instruction-following to reasoning question generation, using LLMs to annotate pretraining documents for reasoning content and then synthesize novel, self-contained questions rather than extracting existing ones. It departs from math-centric synthetic datasets (MetaMathQA, NuminaMath, OpenMathInstruct-2) by grounding questions in diverse web corpora (DCLM-baseline, FineMath) to cover physics, CS, economics, and social sciences. For the self-training component, the paper builds on the self-rewarding framework of Yuan et al. (2024), applying it to open-ended reasoning questions where exact-match verification is infeasible, and contrasts it with external reward models (Qwen2.5-Math-RM-72B, INF-ORM).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

NATURALREASONING provides a 2.8M-question, multi-domain reasoning resource that is publicly released and demonstrated to be more sample-efficient than existing datasets for post-training, making it a practical drop-in for teams building reasoning models beyond math. The finding that random 1K subsets match heavily curated datasets (LIMO, s1K) suggests that large-scale diversity may substitute for labor-intensive curation, potentially changing how reasoning training data is sourced. Key open questions include whether the preliminary RLVR results (50 GRPO steps) scale meaningfully, how the dataset performs under online RL with more sophisticated reward signals, and whether the LLM-annotated quality scores reliably proxy for genuine reasoning difficulty across all domains.
