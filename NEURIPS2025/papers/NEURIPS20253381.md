## Problem

Estimating the accuracy of LLM ensemble judges requires knowing the judgment distribution, but the standard Binomial assumption is demonstrably wrong—it predicts monotonically decreasing majority-voting error as ensemble size grows, whereas real LLM ensembles show a stable error floor due to stratification between easy and hard questions. Additionally, there is no principled, distribution-free criterion for determining how many labeled samples are sufficient for accurate estimation, making the annotation cost unpredictable.

## Input / Output

The method consumes binary correct/incorrect judgment outcomes from k LLMs (odd integer, e.g., k=11) across r samples, where r is determined dynamically by the adaptive stopping rule rather than fixed in advance. For the prior transfer component, it additionally requires text inputs from the target dataset and one or more source datasets, which are embedded via NV-Embed-V2 to compute cosine-similarity-based transfer weights. The output is a set of five mixture parameters (α₁, β₁, α₂, β₂, w) defining the two-component Beta-Binomial model, from which the majority-voting error rate is computed analytically via the Beta function, along with a theoretical error bound under the stopping threshold ξ.

## Method

BetaConform replaces the single Binomial model with a mixture of two Beta-Binomial distributions—one capturing 'easy' questions (high accuracy) and one 'hard' questions (low accuracy)—fitted via a distribution-tailored EM algorithm that assigns per-sample responsibilities to each component. To determine when sampling is sufficient, the framework monitors the (1−ε)-quantile of conformal prediction nonconformity scores (|Sᵢ − E[S]|) across iterative batches and stops when the quantile change falls below a threshold ξ, yielding a theoretical sample-size bound r ≥ 56 for ξ=0.03, τ=25. When only ~10 target samples are available, a text-similarity-based prior transfer mechanism computes per-source-dataset weights λᵢ = log(rᵢ)·σ(ρ₁·(CosSim(E̅₀, E̅ᵢ) − ρ₂)) and aggregates source distribution parameters into the target estimate, with the sigmoid gate suppressing dissimilar sources to avoid degradation. The full pipeline is distribution-free (no parametric assumption on the underlying data), requires no calibrated questions, and provides a closed-form error-rate bound (1 ± min(ξ, τ/√r))·P̃_BB under adaptive stopping.

## Results

Across 11 datasets and 5 LLM ensembles (Llama-3.3-70B, Qwen-2.5-72B, InternLM-2.5-20B, GPT-3.5, GPT-4), BetaConform's mixture of Beta-Binomial distributions achieves average error margins of 6.38%–8.63%, compared to 9.78%–14.98% for the Binomial baseline and 8.03%–13.12% for a single Beta-Binomial, representing 32.4%–54.1% improvements over Binomial. The conformal prediction-based adaptive stopping requires an average of 43–53 samples (theoretical bound: 56), confirming distribution-free behavior. With only 10 target samples plus prior transfer from source datasets, the error margin drops to as low as 3.37% (Llama on TruthfulQA) and averages 10.84% across models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three distribution assumptions for modeling LLM ensemble judgment: the standard Binomial distribution, a single Beta-Binomial, and the proposed mixture of two Beta-Binomial distributions. BetaConform (mixture BB) consistently outperforms both baselines across all 5 models and 11 datasets, achieving 32.4%–54.1% lower average error margin than Binomial (e.g., 8.63% vs. 12.76% on Llama-3.3-70B; 6.38% vs. 10.91% on GPT-4). It also beats the single Beta-Binomial in most settings, with the largest gap on Qwen-2.5-72B (6.48% vs. 9.20%) and GPT-4 (6.38% vs. 10.11%). The single BB occasionally matches or slightly underperforms Binomial on specific datasets (e.g., PRM800K with Llama: 16.45% vs. 10.11%), highlighting that a single component cannot capture the bimodal 'easy/hard' structure. For the prior transfer setting (10 samples), BetaConform with transfer reduces average error margin by 5.0%–25.0% over no-transfer estimation, with the most dramatic improvement on TruthfulQA (15.30% → 3.37% for Llama).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the standard Binomial distribution assumption for LLM ensemble judgment by proposing a mixture of Beta-Binomial distributions fitted via EM, motivated by the observed two-peak structure (easy vs. hard questions) in real judgment data. It adapts conformal prediction (Shafer & Vovk, 2008) to design an adaptive stopping criterion for iterative sampling, and introduces a text-embedding-based prior transfer mechanism (using NV-Embed-V2) to leverage distributions estimated on open-source datasets. The work positions itself as pioneering in the specific formulation of LLM ensemble judgment distribution estimation, distinguishing itself from IRT (which requires calibrated questions) and prior conformal prediction work on LLMs (which addresses factuality or abstention rather than ensemble accuracy estimation).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By showing that the Binomial assumption leads to an always-decreasing majority voting error rate (contradicting observed stable error), the paper corrects a potentially widespread modeling error in LLM ensemble evaluation and provides a theoretically grounded alternative with explicit error bounds. The practical impact is significant: reducing annotation from hundreds of samples to ~56 (or as few as 10 with prior transfer) could substantially lower the cost of evaluating LLM judges at scale. Open questions include whether the two-component mixture is sufficient for more complex judgment distributions, whether textual similarity reliably proxies for distributional similarity in prior transfer, and how the framework extends to non-binary or non-odd-ensemble settings.
