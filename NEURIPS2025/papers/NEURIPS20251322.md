## Problem

While gradient-based optimizers for overparameterized models all reach zero training loss, they converge to different solutions with distinct generalization properties. The implicit bias of spectral descent and Muon in multiclass linear classification with cross-entropy loss had not been characterized, and prior implicit bias results were largely limited to binary classification or L2-geometry gradient descent. This paper provides the first complete non-asymptotic characterization of margin convergence for a broad family of norm-constrained steepest descent and momentum algorithms in the multiclass setting.

## Input / Output

The method operates on multiclass separable data with n samples in R^d across k classes, a weight matrix W ∈ R^{k×d}, and the cross-entropy loss. The key structural detail is that the specific norm (entry-wise p-norm or Schatten p-norm) used to define the steepest descent direction determines which geometric margin the iterates maximize: SignGD converges to max-norm margin, NGD to Frobenius margin, and Spectral-GD/Muon to spectral-norm margin. The output is an explicit margin-gap bound of O((log t + n)/√t) for NSD and O((d log t + dn)/√t) for NMD, with the data margin γ computed via convex optimization (CVXPY).

## Method

The core technical contribution is a unified analysis framework built around a proxy function G(W) = (1/n)Σ_i(1 − S_{y_i}(Wh_i)) that simultaneously lower-bounds the dual gradient norm (via ‖∇L‖_* ≥ γ·G(W)) and upper-bounds the second-order Taylor term of the CE loss. The key insight enabling generality across norms is an ordering property: for any entry-wise or Schatten p-norm, ‖A‖_max ≤ ‖A‖ ≤ ‖A‖_sum, which allows all norm-specific analyses to reduce to the max-norm/sum-norm pair. For NMD algorithms (including Muon), the analysis extends by decomposing G(W) per-class to bound the sum-norm of the momentum-gradient difference Ω_t = M_t − ∇_t, avoiding an extra k-factor. The framework further extends to Adam (without the stability constant), showing it converges to the max-norm margin solution at rate O((d log t + nd)/t^{1/3}).

## Results

The paper establishes the first non-asymptotic margin convergence rates for normalized steepest descent (NSD) and normalized momentum steepest descent (NMD) on multiclass separable data with cross-entropy loss. For NSD (including Spectral-GD), the margin gap converges at rate O((log t + n)/t^{1/2}); for NMD (including Muon), the rate is O((d log t + dn)/t^{1/2}). For Adam (without stability constant), a slower O((d log t + nd)/t^{1/3}) max-norm margin rate is proven. Empirically, on synthetic multiclass data and MNIST two-layer networks, each algorithm's iterates correlate strongly with the max-margin separator defined by its own norm geometry (e.g., Spectral-GD and Muon align with the spectral-norm max-margin solution, while SignGD aligns with the max-norm solution).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's central comparison is across norm geometries: each algorithm provably converges to the max-margin solution defined by its own norm, and experiments confirm this. Spectral-GD and Muon (spectral norm) favor the spectral-norm margin over both the 2-norm and max-norm margins, with Muon's behavior closely mirroring Spectral-GD (as expected since Muon reduces to Spectral-GD when β₁=0). SignGD and Signum (max-norm) favor the max-norm margin, exhibiting the opposite preference to NGD and NMD-GD (2-norm), which favor the 2-norm margin. In the nonlinear two-layer MNIST experiments, the unnormalized spectral-norm margin of Spectral-GD and Muon grows faster than that of SignGD, NGD, Signum, and NMD-GD, confirming the linear-setting norm-preference trend extends to nonlinear models. Adam (without stability constant) is shown to converge to the max-norm margin solution, consistent with prior binary-case results by Zhang et al. [77] but now extended to the multiclass matrix setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the implicit bias literature from binary classification (Soudry et al., Nacson et al.) and entry-wise p-norm steepest descent (Gunasekar et al., Sun et al.) to the multiclass setting with cross-entropy loss and arbitrary Schatten p-norms. The key technical departure is a unified analysis framework that reduces all entry-wise and Schatten norm analyses to the max-norm case via a natural ordering property (max-norm ≤ p-norm ≤ sum-norm), combined with a novel proxy function G(W) that simultaneously bounds the gradient dual-norm and the second-order Taylor term. This framework then extends naturally to momentum variants (NMD, Muon) and Adam, providing the first non-asymptotic implicit bias characterization for Spectral Descent and Muon on multiclass separable data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

With Muon emerging as a serious alternative to AdamW for large-scale LLM training, understanding its implicit regularization was a critical gap; this paper closes it by showing Muon converges to the spectral-norm max-margin solution at rate O((d log t + dn)/√t). The unified framework means a single analysis covers an entire family of optimizers (SignGD, NGD, Spectral-GD, Muon, Adam, etc.), giving practitioners a principled way to predict which geometric margin each optimizer will implicitly maximize. Open questions include removing the factor-d penalty in the momentum rate, extending to non-separable and non-linear settings, and characterizing Shampoo with non-diagonal preconditioners. The practical limitation is that all results are for linear models on separable data, leaving the connection to deep network training largely empirical.
