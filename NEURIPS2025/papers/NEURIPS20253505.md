## Problem

Single-source domain generalized object detection (SDGOD) trains on one source domain (e.g., Day-Clear Cityscapes) and must generalize to unseen weather/lighting conditions without any target-domain data. Existing approaches rely on data augmentation or domain-invariant constraints but lack a unified theoretical explanation for why models fail: they over-rely on spurious correlations (e.g., white color → truck, central position → vehicle) rather than causal features like object geometry, causing severe OOD degradation. This paper connects the failure mode to causal inference and provides a principled mechanism to suppress spurious features.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253505/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB driving scene images (Cityscapes resolution) from a single source domain and produces bounding boxes with class labels for 7 categories (Bus, Bike, Car, Motor, Person, Rider, Truck). Only ~1% of parameters are trainable (visual prompts and adapter modules) while the DINOv2-Large backbone remains fully frozen, making it a parameter-efficient fine-tuning setup. Evaluation spans four zero-shot target domains (Day-Foggy, Dusk-Rainy, Night-Rainy, Night-Clear) and two corruption benchmarks (Cityscapes-C, BDD100K-C) with 15 synthetic corruption types at 5 severity levels.

## Method

Cauvis integrates two components into each backbone layer: (1) Cross-Attention Prompts (CAP), which link learnable prompt tokens to image features via cross-attention, with a theoretical proof that the SVD of the attention matrix partitions into causal (top-k singular values) and spurious (residual) subspaces, making the mechanism equivalent to Pearl's back-door adjustment that blocks confounding paths; and (2) a Dual-Branch Adapter where a Causal Branch (MLP with sigmoid) projects cross-attended prompt activations into causal feature space, while an Auxiliary Branch applies a 2D Fourier transform followed by a high-pass mask to extract domain-invariant high-frequency structure (contours, edges) and filter out low-frequency domain-specific artifacts (color casts, background patterns). The system uses DINOv2-Large as a frozen backbone (first application in SDGOD) with DINO or Faster R-CNN as the detection head, trained for 12 epochs with AdamW at lr=1e-4, updating only the prompt and adapter parameters.

## Results

On the SDGOD benchmark (trained on Day-Clear, tested on four unseen weather domains), Cauvis achieves 56.5/64.6/47.6/61.2 mAP on Day-Foggy/Dusk-Rainy/Night-Rainy/Night-Clear (allm), outperforming the strongest prior SDGOD method UFR by +16.9 to +31.4 mAP. On Cityscapes-C (level-5), Cauvis reaches 35.6 mPC, a 13.8% improvement over the best prior method (OA-DG, 21.8 mPC). In the PEFT comparison across three vision foundation models (EVA02, SAM, DINOv2), Cauvis achieves 52.0/53.8/60.7 average mAP, surpassing the best PEFT baseline (Rein) by 1.5/1.6/1.7 mAP respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Cauvis decisively outperforms all prior SDGOD methods: it surpasses UFR (the strongest prior SDGOD baseline) by +16.9 mAP on Day-Foggy and +31.4 mAP on Dusk-Rainy (allm), and leads G-NAS, PDDOC, SRCD, ClipGap, and CDSD by 15–31 mAP across all four target domains. Against traditional domain-generalization baselines (IterNorm, SW, Faster R-CNN), the margins are even larger (20–50 mAP), reflecting the advantage of the DINOv2 backbone combined with causal prompting. In the PEFT comparison (Table 7), Cauvis beats VPT-Deep, EVP, SPT-Deep, AdaptFormer, and Rein by 1.5–4.6 mAP on average across EVA02, SAM, and DINOv2 backbones, confirming that the cross-attention + Fourier design is superior to token-insertion or adapter-style prompting. On Cityscapes-C robustness, Cauvis achieves 35.6 mPC versus 21.8 for OA-DG (+13.8) and 29.3 for FR+DINOv2 (+6.3), with the largest single-corruption gain being +23.7 mPC on Snow. The ablation (w/o Cauvis row) shows that removing the full Cauvis module from FR+DINOv2 drops allm by 1.7–3.2 points, confirming the module's contribution beyond the backbone alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

Cauvis sits at the intersection of three research threads: visual prompting for parameter-efficient fine-tuning (VPT, EVP, SPT, Rein), causal representation learning (Pearl's back-door adjustment, CFA), and Fourier-domain domain adaptation (FDA). It departs from prior visual prompt methods by replacing simple concatenation or token insertion with a cross-attention mechanism that the authors formally link to back-door adjustment via SVD-based subspace decomposition, and by adding a Fourier high-pass auxiliary branch inspired by FDA. The paper is also the first to adopt DINOv2 as a frozen backbone in the SDGOD setting, building on the broader trend of leveraging vision foundation models for detection. Unlike data-augmentation-heavy SDGOD methods (CDSD, SRCD, UFR), Cauvis operates entirely in the parameter space of a frozen backbone, positioning itself as a PEFT alternative with explicit causal motivation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical takeaway for practitioners is that freezing DINOv2 and adding lightweight Cauvis adapters yields strong SDGOD performance with minimal training cost, and the cross-attention + Fourier dual-branch design is a reusable recipe for other PEFT-on-frozen-backbone tasks. However, the causal-invariance proof relies on strong saddle-point and Hessian assumptions, and the SVD-based partition of attention into 'causal' (top-k) and 'spurious' (remaining) subspaces is a heuristic that is not empirically validated beyond the specific DINOv2+DINO setup. Open questions include whether the back-door-adjustment interpretation generalizes to other architectures or tasks, whether the Fourier branch's modest ~0.9% ablation gain justifies its complexity, and whether the large headline improvements (15–31%) would persist if the backbone were replaced with a less powerful VFM. The absence of error bars and multi-seed runs limits confidence in the finer-grained comparisons.
