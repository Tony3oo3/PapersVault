## Problem

Existing antibody sequence-structure co-design methods face two critical gaps: (1) 3D antibody-antigen structural datasets are orders of magnitude smaller than sequence-only corpora, limiting supervised learning, and (2) training objectives (AAR, RMSD) ignore biophysical energy functions, producing designs with high steric clashes and poor binding affinity. The paper addresses both by leveraging large-scale sequence pre-training and introducing energy-based preference alignment that explicitly optimizes for low repulsion and high attraction at the binding site.

## Input / Output

The model receives the full antibody-antigen framework (all residues outside the target CDR-H3 loop) as context, represented by residue types, Cα coordinates, and SO(3) side-chain orientations, with 128 nearest neighboring residues providing local structural context. It generates the CDR-H3 region at atomic resolution: a categorical distribution over 20 amino acid types per position, 3D Cα coordinates, and rotation matrices for side-chain orientations. During alignment, the model is queried to produce 1,280 candidate designs per complex, which are scored by Rosetta-derived energy measurements (CDR-Ag E_att and E_rep) at the residue level to form preference pairs.

## Method

AlignAb follows a three-stage pipeline: (1) pre-training a 12-layer BERT encoder on 50M heavy-chain sequences via masked language modeling over CDR residues, (2) transferring the frozen BERT representations into an SE(3)-equivariant diffusion model (DiffAb architecture) trained on 2,340 SAbDab clusters to jointly denoise sequence, coordinates, and orientations, and (3) aligning the diffusion model via Pareto-Optimal Energy Alignment (POEA). POEA extends DPO by incorporating the ground-truth reward margin Δr̂ into the sigmoid loss (Eq. 4.5), enabling the model to exploit known energy values rather than relying solely on binary preference labels. To handle conflicting objectives (maximizing attraction vs. minimizing repulsion), POEA constructs a Pareto front of models parameterized by a weighting vector w over the two reward functions, with preference pairs formed by comparing collective rewards r̂ = w_att·r_att + w_rep·r_rep. An iterative online variant (Algorithm 1) augments the dataset each round by sampling from the current policy, and a decaying temperature schedule (T=1.5) during inference mitigates mode collapse, closing the entropy gap between generated and natural CDR-H3 sequences.

## Results

On the RAbD benchmark (42 antigen-antibody complexes), AlignAb achieves the smallest energy gap relative to reference antibodies: 17.91 kcal/mol (top-1) and 39.00 kcal/mol (average over 1,280 samples), outperforming all six baselines. The average CDR E_total is 30.45 kcal/mol and CDR-Ag ΔG is 25.16 kcal/mol, compared to reference values of −19.33 and −16.00 respectively. AlignAb also shows the smallest top-to-average gap (17.91 vs. 39.00), indicating high consistency in generated antibody quality across samples.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AlignAb outperforms all six baselines on the overall energy gap metric, achieving 17.91 (top-1) and 39.00 (average) versus DiffAb's 19.74/263.44 and AbX's 37.75/63.43. The most competitive baseline is DiffAb, which benefits from diffusion-model diversity to produce a strong top-1 design (gap 19.74) but suffers from high variance across samples (average gap 263.44); AlignAb narrows this top-to-average spread dramatically. AbX, which incorporates evolutionary and geometric constraints, is the second-closest competitor on average gap (63.43) but still trails AlignAb by ~39%. MEAN achieves a deceptively low CDR-Ag ΔG (−19.94 top) but has a severely elevated CDR E_total (46.27 top, 186.05 avg), indicating structural clashes that undermine its binding score. HERN and dyMEAN perform catastrophically, with average gaps exceeding 2,400 and 6,100 kcal/mol respectively, reflecting severe steric repulsion. ABGNN sits in the middle (gap 354.38/778.54), confirming that pre-training alone without energy-based alignment is insufficient for producing physically rational designs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the three-stage LLM training paradigm (pre-training, transfer, alignment) to antibody sequence-structure co-design, building on DiffAb's diffusion-based CDR generation and AbGNN's pre-trained antibody language model. Its core methodological contribution extends DPO for diffusion models (Wallace et al., 2023) and AbDPO (Zhou et al., 2024b) in two directions: incorporating ground-truth energy reward margins into the preference loss, and generalizing to multi-objective Pareto-optimality for conflicting biophysical objectives (attraction vs. repulsion). The iterative online alignment with decaying temperature scaling draws an explicit analogy to online RL and ε-greedy exploration, distinguishing it from the offline single-pass alignment in prior antibody DPO work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By shifting evaluation and alignment from gameable metrics (AAR, RMSD) to physics-based energy measurements (Lennard-Jones, electrostatics, hydrogen bonding), the paper narrows the gap between in silico design and in vitro functionality, which is a persistent pain point in computational antibody engineering. The Pareto-front formulation gives domain experts a tunable knob (weight vector w) to balance competing objectives per complex, a practical advantage over single-scalar reward approaches. Key limitations include the small test set (42 complexes), only 3 alignment iterations, fixed reward weights across all antigens due to compute constraints, and CDR-H3-only scope; extending to full CDR sets, larger datasets, and other biomolecular targets (small molecules, TCRs) remains open.
