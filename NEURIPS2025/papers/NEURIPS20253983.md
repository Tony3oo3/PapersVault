## Problem

Most existing VRP benchmarks assume static travel times, fixed customer availability, and deterministic constraints, failing to capture the stochastic dynamics of real urban logistics such as peak-hour congestion, random accidents, and heterogeneous delivery time windows. This gap leads to overly optimistic performance assessments and misdirects algorithmic development toward unrealistic assumptions, leaving it unclear how well solvers generalize under genuine uncertainty.

## Input / Output

Each instance specifies customer locations (sampled via K-Means clustering with 2D Gaussian scatter around city centers), discrete demands, stochastic time windows (bimodal Gaussian for residential, single-mode for commercial), vehicle capacities, depot positions, and a full time-dependent travel time matrix T(a,b,t) incorporating congestion, log-normal noise, and Poisson accident delays. Solvers produce vehicle routes that are then scored on total cost (summing sampled stochastic travel times), feasibility rate, constraint violation rate, wall-clock runtime, and robustness (variance of cost across N stochastic realizations of the same instance). Instances span 10–1000 customers across single/multi-depot and single/multi-vehicle configurations.

## Method

SVRPBench's core contribution is a four-stage stochastic modeling pipeline: (1) time-dependent congestion modeled as a product of a Gaussian-mixture time factor (peaks at 8 AM and 5 PM) and an exponential distance-decay term; (2) multiplicative log-normal travel-time noise whose mean and variance are themselves time-varying functions of the same congestion peaks; (3) accident disruptions drawn from a time-inhomogeneous Poisson process (peaking at 9 PM) with uniform delay durations; and (4) customer time windows sampled from empirically grounded residential (bimodal) and commercial (unimodal) distributions. The instance generator uses K-Means clustering to produce spatially realistic city layouts validated against satellite imagery, and supports multi-depot, multi-vehicle, and capacity-constrained configurations. The evaluation suite benchmarks classical heuristics (NN+2opt), metaheuristics (Tabu Search, ACO, HGS, PyVRP), an industrial solver (OR-Tools), and RL methods (Attention Model, POMO, LEHD, MVMoE, and a purpose-built SVRP solver) under identical stochastic protocols, revealing that RL solvers degrade by over 20% in cost under distributional shift while classical and metaheuristic methods maintain feasibility and cost stability.

## Results

On the aggregate SVRPBench evaluation (Table 2), OR-Tools achieves the lowest total cost (40,259.3) with 98.4% feasibility, followed by ACO (40,566.5) and POMO (40,650.4), while NN+2opt delivers the fastest runtime (0.697 s) at 98.4% feasibility. Introducing time windows (TWCVRP) inflates total cost by 536–648% across all solvers, with OR-Tools incurring the largest relative penalty (+647.6%) and the Attention Model the smallest (+536.2%). Under the enhanced baseline suite (Table 6), the purpose-built SVRP Solver attains the best RL cost (9,223 CVRP / 70,689 TWCVRP) at 94.5% feasibility and 1.34 s runtime, while metaheuristics HGS and PyVRP maintain >98% feasibility but at 12–45 s runtimes. The feasibility drop from deterministic to stochastic settings is 5.5 points for the Attention Model versus only 1.6 points for OR-Tools, confirming the benchmark's sensitivity to uncertainty.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OR-Tools dominates on cost (40,259) and ties NN+2opt on feasibility (98.4%), but is ~2.8× slower than NN+2opt (1.94 s vs 0.70 s); ACO achieves the second-best cost (40,566) yet suffers the worst feasibility (69.0%) and longest runtime (11.4 s) among the core six. POMO offers the best cost-to-speed trade-off among RL methods (40,650 cost, 1.42 s), outperforming the Attention Model on cost (−708) and CVR (1.7% vs 1.9%) while matching its runtime. In the extended comparison (Table 6), PyVRP (9,156 CVRP cost, 98.5% feasibility, 12.1 s) and HGS (9,234, 98.7%, 45.2 s) set the metaheuristic ceiling, but both are 9–34× slower than the purpose-built SVRP Solver (9,223, 94.5%, 1.34 s), which is the only RL method to approach metaheuristic cost. LEHD (9,834, 92.5%) and MVMoE (9,723, 92.8%) trail PyVRP by ~6–7% in cost with ~6-point feasibility gaps, while the LLM-based Self-Guiding Exploration (9,891, 69.5%, 312 s) is both the least feasible and slowest by an order of magnitude. The key takeaway is that no RL method matches the feasibility of OR-Tools/HGS/PyVRP under stochastic conditions, and the SVRP-specific solver narrows but does not close the gap, highlighting the benchmark's difficulty.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper does not extend a single prior method but rather carves new ground by unifying multiple stochastic elements—time-dependent congestion (Gaussian mixtures), log-normal travel-time delays, Poisson-distributed accidents, and empirically grounded heterogeneous time windows—into a single open benchmark for the Stochastic Vehicle Routing Problem. While SVRP formulations have existed since Gendreau et al. (1996) and Laporte et al. (1992), prior public benchmarks (CVRPLIB, SINTEF, TSPLIB, RL4CO) largely assume deterministic travel times and fixed customer availability. The evaluation harness repurposes well-known solver architectures (AM, POMO, ACO, Tabu Search, OR-Tools, HGS, PyVRP, LEHD, MVMoE) under a shared stochastic protocol, positioning the contribution as a diagnostic platform rather than a new algorithm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark exposes a concrete 'reality gap': RL solvers trained on deterministic instances degrade by over 20% in cost and lose 5–20 feasibility points under stochastic conditions, while classical solvers like OR-Tools and NN+2opt remain nearly stable, challenging the assumption that learned policies are inherently more adaptive. Open questions include how to design RL or hybrid solvers that generalize under distributional shift, whether incorporating real road-network topology (OSM/GIS) and multi-day rolling-horizon dynamics changes the ranking, and whether the current Gaussian/log-normal delay models are sufficient or whether network-level congestion cascades are needed. The released dataset (500+ instances up to 1000 customers) and standardized evaluation suite lower the barrier for reproducible stochastic-routing research.
