## Problem

Existing machine learning force fields model molecular interactions at the atomic level (distances, angles, torsions) but overlook electron density (ED), the fundamental quantity that uniquely determines all ground-state properties per the Hohenberg-Kohn theorem. Two practical barriers prevent ED-based modeling: the absence of large-scale, high-quality ED datasets (DFT computation is prohibitively expensive) and the lack of a systematic ED-centric benchmark to evaluate whether models can actually learn from density representations.

## Input / Output

For prediction and retrieval tasks, the ED is consumed as a point cloud of (x, y, z, ρ) tuples extracted from CUBE files (0.4 Bohr grid, 4.0 Bohr padding, 0.85 isosurface fraction), with a density threshold ρ_τ (e.g., 0.05–0.2) discarding negligible-density points to keep vector lengths tractable. Molecular structures are standard (atomic numbers + 3D coordinates). Outputs span regression targets (6 energy components, 7 frontier orbital energies, 4 multipole moments), a binary open/closed-shell label, a ranked retrieval list over 11 candidates, and a full predicted ED grid for the generation task.

## Method

EDBench is built by running B3LYP/6-31G**/+G** DFT via Psi4 on 3,359,472 drug-like molecules from PCQM4Mv2 (>205,000 core-hours), producing CUBE-format ED plus energy components, orbital energies, and multipole moments. Six benchmark tasks (ED5-EC, ED5-OE, ED5-MM, ED5-OCS, ED5-MER, ED5-EDP) are constructed with a structure- and label-balanced k-means sampling strategy (k=100 on concatenated ECFP4+USR fingerprints and on label vectors) and scaffold split (80/10/10). For the ED generation task, the authors extend EGNN to a heterogeneous graph (HGEGNN) where atoms and ED grid points are two node types connected by k-NN edges (k=9, same-type and cross-type), and a prediction head reconstructs masked density values via L2 loss. Prediction tasks use ED point-cloud encoders (PointVector, X-3D) with task-specific heads; retrieval uses paired MS and ED encoders trained with InfoNCE contrastive loss (τ=0.07) over 10 hard/easy negatives per anchor.

## Results

On the ED5-EC prediction task, X-3D achieves a total energy (E6) MAE of 190.77±1.98, outperforming PointVector (243.49±74.73) by ~22%. On ED5-MM, X-3D reaches 0.6820 MAE on dipole magnitude versus PointVector's 0.7397. For the ED5-EDP generation task, HGEGNN achieves 99.2% Pearson correlation at ρτ=0.2 with a runtime of 0.013 s/mol, compared to DFT's 245.8 s/mol—a roughly 19,000× speedup while maintaining high fidelity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

X-3D consistently outperforms PointVector across the prediction benchmarks: on ED5-EC it reduces total-energy MAE from 243.49 to 190.77 (with far tighter variance), on ED5-MM it improves dipole-magnitude MAE from 0.7397 to 0.6820, and on ED5-OCS it achieves higher accuracy (57.65% vs 55.57%) and ROC-AUC (60.48% vs 55.97%). PointVector edges out X-3D slightly on ED5-OE orbital energies (e.g., HOMO-0: 1.92 vs 1.98 MAE×100) and on ED5-OCS F1-score (66.96 vs 61.41), suggesting PointVector's MLP architecture captures local orbital patterns marginally better. For the retrieval task (ED5-MER), EquiformerV2 as the molecular-structure encoder consistently outperforms GeoFormer when paired with either ED encoder, with E+X achieving the best top-1 retrieval accuracy. On the generation task, HGEGNN (built on EGNN) produces ED with 99.2% Pearson correlation at ρτ=0.2 in 0.013 s/mol, versus DFT's 245.8 s/mol, demonstrating that learned ED prediction can approximate DFT-quality densities at orders-of-magnitude lower cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the geometric deep learning paradigm for molecular force fields from atomic-level many-body interactions (1-body through 5-body) to electronic-level modeling using electron density as the primary input representation. It builds directly on EGNN [5], extending it to a heterogeneous graph (HGEGNN) that jointly encodes atoms and ED points, and repurposes generic point-cloud architectures (PointVector, X-3D) as ED encoders. The dataset construction follows the DFT pipeline established by PubChemQC/PCQM4Mv2 but upgrades the basis set (6-31G**/+G** vs. 6-31G*) and adds CUBE-format density grids absent from prior drug-like molecule resources such as QMugs and ∇²DFT, which only provide density matrices.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

EDBench is the first large-scale (3.36M molecules) electron-density benchmark for drug-like molecules, filling a critical gap that has limited ED-driven MLFF research to small or materials-focused datasets. The demonstrated ~10,000× speedup in ED generation (0.013 s/mol vs. 245.8 s/mol for DFT) at 99.2% Pearson correlation makes quantum-aware modeling practically scalable. Key open questions include whether higher-rung functionals (beyond B3LYP) would yield meaningfully different ED patterns, whether ED-specific architectures (voxels, images, point clouds) outperform generic point-cloud encoders, and how the benchmark extends to periodic systems and larger molecules beyond 20 heavy atoms.
