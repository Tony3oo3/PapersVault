## Problem

Standard LLM training applies a single uniform weight decay rate to all modules, ignoring the substantial spectral heterogeneity between attention and MLP components. This uniform treatment fails to account for the fact that attention modules (especially Q and K) exhibit much heavier-tailed singular value spectra than MLP modules, meaning they are more sensitive to regularization. The paper asks whether a module-wise weight decay schedule guided by spectral properties can improve LLM pre-training performance.

## Input / Output

The method consumes the weight matrices of each transformer module (att.q, att.k, att.v, att.o, mlp.gate, mlp.up, mlp.down) at periodic intervals during training, computing eigenvalues of each module's correlation matrix W^T W to obtain its empirical spectral distribution. It produces a per-module weight decay coefficient bounded within [s1·η, s2·η] (where η is the base decay and s1, s2 are scaling ratios), which is applied to the optimizer for the next update interval. The final output is a trained LLM with improved validation perplexity and downstream task accuracy.

## Method

AlphaDecay leverages Heavy-Tailed Self-Regularization (HT-SR) theory to quantify each module's spectral tail heaviness via the PL_Alpha_Hill metric, computed with the Hill estimator over the largest half of eigenvalues from each module's correlation matrix. Modules with lower PL_Alpha_Hill (more heavy-tailed, indicating stronger feature concentration, e.g., att.q/k) are assigned proportionally lower weight decay, while modules with higher PL_Alpha_Hill (lighter-tailed, e.g., MLP components) receive higher decay. The assignment uses linear interpolation: f_t(i) = η · ((α_t^i − α_t^min)/(α_t^max − α_t^min) · (s2 − s1) + s1), ensuring all decay values stay within [s1·η, s2·η]. This schedule is recomputed every 500 training steps, dynamically rebalancing module-wise spectral properties throughout training. Ablations confirm that the linear assignment function, the Hill estimator, and the Median PL-fitting method are the optimal choices among alternatives tested.

## Results

On C4 pre-training, AlphaDecay achieves the lowest validation perplexity across all four LLaMa scales (60M–1B) and all three weight decay settings (1e-5, 5e-6, 1e-6). At the best setting (WD=5e-6), AlphaDecay reaches 31.58 (60M), 22.55 (135M), 16.66 (350M), and 14.55 (1B) perplexity, improving over Uniform by 0.98, 0.59, 0.08, and 0.11 respectively. On downstream tasks, AlphaDecay-trained LLaMa-1B scores 43.35 average on seven zero-shot commonsense benchmarks (vs. 41.50 for Uniform) and 85.12 average on GLUE fine-tuning (vs. 83.83). Cross-architecture validation on ViT-tiny/ImageNet-1K yields 67.73% Top-1 accuracy, outperforming all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AlphaDecay consistently outperforms both adaptive baselines across all model sizes and weight decay settings. Against AWD [12] (Ghiasi et al., NeurIPS 2023), AlphaDecay achieves substantially lower perplexity — e.g., 22.55 vs. 24.45 on LLaMa-135M (WD=5e-6) and 14.55 vs. 16.22 on LLaMa-1B — a gap of roughly 1.9 and 1.7 PPL respectively, indicating AWD's gradient-norm-based adaptation is poorly suited to transformer architectures. Against AdaDecay [30] (Nakamura & Hong, IEEE Access 2019), AlphaDecay also wins on every configuration, with the largest margin on LLaMa-1B (14.55 vs. 18.14 at WD=5e-6, a 3.59 PPL gap) and a more modest but consistent 0.5–0.6 PPL advantage on smaller models. The paper attributes these gaps to the baselines' lack of awareness for the distinct spectral characteristics of attention versus MLP modules in transformers. On downstream evaluation, AlphaDecay's pre-training gains transfer: it leads on 6/7 zero-shot commonsense tasks (avg 43.35 vs. 41.68 for AdaDecay) and 6/8 GLUE tasks (avg 85.12 vs. 84.66 for AWD), and achieves the highest Top-1 on ViT-tiny/ImageNet-1K (67.73% vs. 66.41% Uniform, 64.98% AWD, 66.26% AdaDecay).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes Heavy-Tailed Self-Regularization (HT-SR) theory—previously applied to model selection, pruning, and layer-wise quality estimation—as a guide for weight decay scheduling, a connection no prior work had explored. It departs from existing adaptive weight decay methods (AWD, AdaDecay), which were designed for CNNs and modulate decay uniformly across all layers at each timestep, by introducing the first module-wise, spectral-guided decay schedule for transformer LLMs. While [3; 19] demonstrated that hand-tuned, distinct decay values for attention vs. MLP modules help LLM training, AlphaDecay replaces grid search with an automated, principled assignment driven by the Hill-estimator power-law exponent of each module's empirical spectral distribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that module-wise spectral imbalance (heavier tails in attention, lighter in MLP) is a core driver of suboptimal training, and that a simple linear rescaling of weight decay can correct it, offers a drop-in improvement for any LLM training pipeline without architectural changes. The observation that CNN-designed adaptive decay methods (AWD, AdaDecay) actually underperform uniform decay on LLMs highlights a gap in transferability of optimization heuristics across architectures. Open questions include whether the approach extends to Mixture-of-Experts models, how it interacts with other regularization or optimizer techniques (e.g., GaLore, LoRA), and whether the 500-step update interval remains optimal at much larger scales (7B+).
