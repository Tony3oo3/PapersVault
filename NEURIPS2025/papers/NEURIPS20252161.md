## Problem

Estimating epistemic uncertainty in regression ensembles with probabilistic outputs has relied on Monte Carlo sampling (e.g., BALD), which becomes prohibitively expensive as output dimensionality grows because the number of samples needed for accurate entropy estimation scales poorly. No sample-free method existed for computing the mutual information I(y,θ|x) in high-dimensional continuous-output regression settings, leaving a gap for applications like robotic locomotion where data acquisition is costly and output spaces can exceed 200 dimensions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252161/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Given a query point x and an ensemble of M probabilistic models (Nflows Base or PNEs), the method computes pairwise distributional distances between the Gaussian base distributions of each ensemble component at x, yielding a scalar epistemic uncertainty score per candidate. In the active learning loop, 10⁴ candidate inputs are scored and the top-B (B=10) are selected per acquisition batch. The output dimensionality ranges from 1 (1D sinusoidal) to 257 (Humanoid), and the method requires no samples from the mixture distribution—only the parameters (mean, covariance) of each component's base Gaussian.

## Method

PairEpEsts replace MC sampling with closed-form pairwise distances between ensemble components in the base distribution space. For Nflows Base, the ensemble is constructed over Gaussian base distributions shared through a single bijective normalizing flow, so the Bhattacharyya or KL distance between any pair of components has a closed-form expression. The epistemic uncertainty estimator is I_ρ(y,θ) = −Σᵢ πᵢ ln Σⱼ πⱼ exp(−D_ρ(pᵢ‖pⱼ)), which provably bounds the true mutual information (Bhattacharyya gives a lower bound, KL gives an upper bound). A key theoretical result shows PairEpEst-KL yields a strictly tighter upper bound on MI than the Expected Pairwise KL (EPKL) baseline. Because no sampling is needed, the method achieves 1–2 orders of magnitude speedup over MC and scales to 257-dimensional outputs where MC requires 5000+ samples per candidate.

## Results

On the 100th acquisition batch of Nflows Base, PairEpEst-KL and PairEpEst-Bhatt achieve the lowest RMSE in all high-dimensional environments: 3.4±0.48 and 3.37±0.44 on Humanoid (257D), 0.9±0.07 and 0.93±0.08 on Ant (32D), and 0.3±0.03 and 0.29±0.03 on Hopper (11D). The advantage over the MC(BALD) baseline grows sharply with dimensionality, from a marginal 0.02 RMSE gap on Hopper to a 7.3 RMSE gap on Humanoid (3.4 vs. 10.71). In 1D settings (hetero, bimodal), all methods converge to similar performance, with PairEpEsts matching or slightly trailing the best baseline. PairEpEsts also provide a 1–2 order-of-magnitude runtime reduction over MC estimators, enabling evaluation of 10⁴ candidates per acquisition step even in the 257D Humanoid environment.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MC(BALD), the closest and most directly comparable baseline, PairEpEsts achieve statistically significant RMSE reductions in all multi-dimensional environments: 0.30 vs. 0.32 on Hopper, 0.90 vs. 1.01 on Ant, and 3.40 vs. 10.71 on Humanoid, while matching MC(BALD) in the 1D settings. Against BatchBALD, PairEpEsts win decisively in high dimensions (3.40 vs. 5.33 on Humanoid) and are competitive in lower dimensions, though BatchBALD edges ahead on Pendulum (0.13 vs. 0.05). Against BADGE, which excels on the 1D hetero task (1.44 vs. 1.47), PairEpEsts dominate in all multi-dimensional settings, with the gap widening from 0.81 RMSE on Hopper (0.30 vs. 1.11) to 3.91 on Humanoid (3.40 vs. 7.31). Against BAIT, designed for 1D regression, PairEpEsts outperform across every environment, with the most dramatic margin on Humanoid (3.40 vs. 11.01). Overall, the paper's key finding is that the advantage of PairEpEsts over all baselines scales with output dimensionality, confirming that sample-free pairwise-distance estimation is particularly valuable in the high-dimensional regime where MC methods suffer from the curse of dimensionality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Pairwise-Distance Estimators (PaiDEs) from Kolchinsky and Tracey [35], originally developed for estimating mixture entropy in unsupervised settings, to the supervised regression context for epistemic uncertainty estimation. It builds directly on Berry and Meger's Nflows Base architecture [4], which established that epistemic uncertainty can be estimated in the base distribution space of conditional normalizing flows, and on the BALD acquisition framework of Houlsby et al. [27]. The key departure from prior work is the elimination of Monte Carlo sampling entirely: by leveraging closed-form pairwise distances (Bhattacharyya, KL) between Gaussian base distributions, the authors obtain a deterministic, sample-free estimator of mutual information that was previously only accessible via expensive sampling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 1–2 order-of-magnitude speedup over MC estimators, combined with superior RMSE in high-dimensional settings (up to 257 dims in Humanoid), makes principled epistemic uncertainty estimation practical for robotic dynamics modeling where data acquisition is costly. The quadratic cost in ensemble size and the requirement for closed-form pairwise distances limit generality, and the small bias (though ranking-preserving) means the estimator is not a drop-in replacement for MC in all contexts. Open questions include extending to non-Gaussian or heavy-tailed base distributions, scaling to very large ensembles (e.g., weather forecasting), and whether the framework applies to architectures beyond NFs and PNEs.
