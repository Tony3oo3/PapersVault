## Problem

Max-min multi-objective reinforcement learning (MORL) is a natural fairness criterion for resource allocation problems, but the non-differentiability of the min operator prevents direct application of standard RL methods. The prior model-free algorithm of Park et al. (ICML 2024) solves the exact max-min problem via a convex primal-dual formulation but requires 20 copies of the Q-network per weight update, incurs ~3× longer training time, and guarantees only average-iterate convergence. This paper seeks a single-loop algorithm with last-iterate convergence and drastically reduced memory and compute.

## Input / Output

The method operates on a multi-objective MDP with K vector-valued rewards r(s,a) ∈ ℝ^K, a transition kernel P, and a discount factor γ. The learner maintains a policy parameter θ (softmax in the tabular case, neural network in the deep case) while the adversary maintains a weight vector w ∈ Δ^K (the (K−1)-simplex). The output is a stochastic policy π* that maximizes min_k V_k^π, together with the equilibrium weight w* that identifies which objective dimensions are binding. Entropy regularization τ on the policy and τ_w on the adversary's weight are required to resolve the known indeterminacy of max-min MORL (where only stochastic optimal policies may exist).

## Method

The key insight is a max-min = min-max equivalence (Theorem 3.1): because the value function is linear in the occupancy measure, the entropy-regularized max-min MORL problem admits a saddle point, so finding a Nash equilibrium of the two-player zero-sum game with payoff ⟨w, V_τ^π⟩ directly yields the max-min optimal policy. The learner updates its policy via natural policy gradient (NPG) with KL-divergence Bregman divergence, which in the tabular softmax case gives a closed-form multiplicative update and in the deep case reduces to PPO. The adversary updates w via a modified mirror descent step with negative Shannon entropy regularization −τ_w H(w) on the simplex, yielding the closed-form softmax update w_{t+1} = softmax(−(1−β)/τ_w · V^{π_t} + β log w_t) with β = 1/(λτ_w + 1) ∈ (0,1) guaranteed for any hyperparameters. This single-loop primal-dual structure (ERAM) achieves global last-iterate convergence at rate O(1/ε² log 1/ε_acc) iterations, and an adaptive variant (ARAM) replaces the uniform reference in the adversary's regularizer with a correlation-based vector c_t that emphasizes poorly-performing objectives, further improving practical performance.

## Results

On the SUMO-RL traffic signal control benchmark, ARAM achieves the best max-min performance across all three scenarios: −1160 (Base-4), −2696 (Asym-4), and −15043 (Asym-16), where higher (less negative) values indicate lower total waiting time. ERAM achieves comparable results (−1387, −2732, −17334) while maintaining architectural simplicity. In terms of efficiency, ERAM reduces training wall-time by approximately 68% and memory usage by ~95% compared to Park et al. [37], while also guaranteeing last-iterate convergence versus only average-iterate convergence.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Park et al. [37] (the prior state-of-the-art for exact max-min MORL), ARAM improves max-min performance by 31% (Base-4: −1160 vs. −1681), 23% (Asym-4: −2696 vs. −3510), and 36% (Asym-16: −15043 vs. −23663), while reducing training time by ~68% and memory by ~95%. Against GGF-PPO [50], which optimizes only the single worst objective dimension per batch, ARAM achieves substantially better max-min values (e.g., −1160 vs. −1731 on Base-4), and the paper shows that GGF-PPO is a special case of ERAM with τ_w=0 and no Bregman divergence. GGF-DQN [50] performs worse than GGF-PPO in most settings because it optimizes a Jensen's-inequality surrogate rather than the true max-min objective. ERAM matches ARAM closely (within ~15% on Base-4, within ~1% on Asym-4) at essentially the same computational cost, confirming that the adaptive regularization in ARAM provides a modest but consistent gain. Both methods also outperform the simple Avg-DQN baseline by large margins (e.g., 58% improvement on Base-4), demonstrating the importance of the max-min criterion over sum-based scalarization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Park et al. (ICML 2024), who established the equivalence between max-min MORL and a convex minimization over the weight simplex and proposed a double-loop model-free algorithm using Gaussian-smoothed zeroth-order gradients. The key departure is a game-theoretic reformulation: by proving the max-min = min-max value equality (non-trivial since value functions are non-concave in policy), the authors recast the problem as a two-player zero-sum game solvable by a single-loop mirror descent algorithm with closed-form softmax updates for the adversary. The method also generalizes GGF-PPO (Siddique et al., ICML 2020), which optimizes only the worst objective dimension per batch; here, entropy regularization on the adversary's weight vector spreads attention across multiple objectives simultaneously, enabling last-iterate convergence rather than the average-iterate guarantee of one-hot switching.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper delivers the first single-loop algorithm for exact max-min MORL with provable last-iterate convergence, reducing memory by ~95% and training time by ~65–68% compared to the prior state-of-the-art (Park et al.), while integrating seamlessly with PPO for the learner. This makes max-min fairness optimization practical for real-world resource allocation (traffic control, cloud scheduling) where the previous double-loop approach was prohibitively expensive. Open questions include extending the theoretical guarantees beyond the tabular setting, providing convergence analysis for the adaptive ARAM variant, and understanding how the method scales to very large state-action spaces or continuous action spaces where the closed-form NPG update is unavailable.
