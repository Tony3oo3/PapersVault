## Problem

Existing FDR-controlled feature selection methods based on mirror statistics (data splitting and Gaussian mirror) implicitly assume unit variance for the paired coefficient estimates β_j^+ and β_j^-, an assumption that empirical evidence shows is violated in practice. This unit-variance constraint limits the achievable statistical power of the resulting test statistics, and the Gaussian mirror paper provides no formal power analysis. The paper asks whether incorporating the actual (non-unit) variance of these coefficients can yield a uniformly more powerful mirror test statistic while preserving FDR guarantees.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a normalized design matrix X ∈ R^{n×p} (columns zero-mean, unit-norm scaled to n), a response vector y, a nominal FDR level q, and a hyperparameter k (number of modes for k-means clustering of coefficient magnitudes). It outputs a set of selected feature indices Ŝ_1 = {j : w_j ≥ τ_q} along with per-feature mirror test statistics w_j. A key practical detail is that the true coefficient magnitude δ for non-null features is unavailable, so the estimation algorithm (Algorithm 2) uses k-means on the values v_j = |β_j^+ + β_j^-|/2 to cluster and assign each feature to its nearest non-zero mode, effectively performing a generalized likelihood ratio test.

## Method

G²M generalizes the Gaussian mirror perturbation to x_j^+ = x_j + c_j·z_j and x_j^- = x_j - d_j·q_j, then derives (via Proposition 3.1) that the OLS coefficients decompose as β_j^+ = α·β_j + γ^T·ε and β_j^- = ζ·β_j + η^T·ε, making their variances ‖γ‖² and ‖η‖² explicitly computable from the design matrix. The core theoretical result (Lemma 3.5) applies the Neyman-Pearson lemma under a variance-generalized normal model to obtain a closed-form entry-wise UMP test statistic f(a,b) = U[P_+ exp(-S_-) + P_- exp(-S_+)] where S_± and P_± depend on the variances σ_a², σ_b² and the coefficient magnitude δ. Theorem 3.6 then proves this statistic dominates both the Gaussian mirror statistic |a+b|-|a-b| and the data splitting statistic sign(ab)(|a|+|b|) in power at the same FDR level, by showing that per-feature UMP optimality combined with index-dependent null distributions suffices for overall FDR control. In practice, Algorithm 2 estimates δ via k-means clustering (with k chosen by silhouette score) over the p coefficient-magnitude values, excluding the smallest cluster (corresponding to null features) to obtain k-1 candidate modes for assignment.

## Results

On Gaussian synthetic data (n=200, p=100, FDR level 0.1), G²M† achieves the highest power among all FDR-controlled methods across all fitting methods (OLS, Ridge, LASSO) and correlation levels (ρ=0.6, 0.7), e.g., 0.84 power with 0.06 FDR under LASSO at ρ=0.6 vs. 0.76 for Gaussian Mirror† and 0.70 for Data Splitting†. On non-Gaussian synthetic data (Copula, Gaussian Mixture), G²M† achieves the highest power with controlled FDR in 4 out of 5 settings, reaching 0.95 power (Clayton&Gamma) and 0.92 (Gaussian Mixture), outperforming all deep-learning-based knockoff methods. On the semi-synthetic IBD metabolomics dataset, G²M† achieves 0.61 power at 0.10 FDR, the best among all methods that maintain FDR control, while on the real IBD dataset it identifies 18/22 literature-referenced metabolites, on par with DeepDRK (19/23).

## Baselines & Comparisons

G²M consistently outperforms its two direct theoretical predecessors—Gaussian Mirror [45] and Data Splitting [9]—across all synthetic settings, confirming the theoretical UMP guarantee (Theorem 3.6); for example, on Gaussian Mixture data G²M† achieves 0.92 power vs. 0.83 for Gaussian Mirror† and 0.76 for Data Splitting†. Against deep-learning-based knockoff methods (Deep Knockoff, DDLK, KnockoffGAN, sRMMD), G²M† achieves comparable or higher power while maintaining FDR ≤ 0.1, whereas most deep methods severely violate FDR control (e.g., Deep Knockoff reaches 0.74 FDR on Gaussian Mixture). DeepDRK [34], the strongest deep-learning baseline, is the only method that slightly outperforms G²M† on the semi-synthetic RNA dataset (0.73 vs. 0.66 power), but G²M† wins on the semi-synthetic IBD dataset (0.61 vs. 0.25 power) and is on par on the real IBD case study (18/22 vs. 19/23 referenced metabolites). CRT [8] and HRT [40] are consistently the weakest baselines, with power often below 0.25, while Distilled-CRT [19] frequently fails FDR control (e.g., 0.53–0.58 FDR on Gaussian data). Powerful Knockoff [37] shows moderate performance but is dominated by G²M in nearly all settings, particularly under high correlation (ρ=0.7) where its power drops to 0.55–0.63 vs. G²M†'s 0.67–0.70.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalizes the mirror statistics framework introduced by Dai et al. (data splitting, JASA 2023) and Xing et al. (Gaussian mirror, JASA 2023), which both rely on a unit-variance assumption for the paired coefficient estimates β_j⁺ and β_j⁻. The authors show empirically (Appendix A) that this assumption is violated in practice, then derive a variance-aware UMP test statistic via the Neyman-Pearson lemma that subsumes both prior statistics as special cases. The perturbation mechanism (Gaussian noise added to the design matrix) is inherited directly from the Gaussian mirror, while the FDR control machinery (symmetry-about-zero + threshold selection) follows the data splitting framework. The method is positioned against the broader FDR-controlled feature selection landscape including Model-X knockoffs, CRT, and deep-learning-based knockoff methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of the unit-variance assumption as a hidden power limitation in the mirror statistics framework is a clean theoretical insight that explains why prior methods underperform, and the UMP result provides a principled path to improvement. The method's practical appeal is strong: it requires only OLS fits plus a lightweight k-means step, yet outperforms computationally expensive deep-learning-based knockoff methods in most experimental settings. A key limitation is the normality assumption on the fitting coefficients, which the authors acknowledge restricts the theoretical guarantees; extending beyond this (e.g., to generalized linear models) is flagged as future work. The k-means estimation of δ, while practical, introduces a hyperparameter (k) and an ad-hoc exclusion of the null mode that could be more principled.
