## Problem

Entropy minimization, the dominant objective for gradient-based test-time adaptation, is fundamentally misaligned with the contrastive image-text pre-training of vision-language models like CLIP. This objective mismatch causes pseudo-label drift and class collapse, particularly when initial accuracy is low or batches contain out-of-distribution samples. The paper asks how to design an adaptation loss that is naturally suited to the contrastive training paradigm of VLMs.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (resized to 224×224) in batches of 128, paired with class-specific text prompts of the form 'A photo of a <class>' for each of C classes. It produces a class label via softmax over image-text cosine similarities (with a fixed temperature τ), and in the open-set setting additionally outputs an ID/OOD score computed as the maximum predicted probability (MCM score) with a learnable sigmoid threshold. Adaptation updates only the affine parameters of the visual encoder's normalization layers, leaving the text encoder frozen in the main experiments.

## Method

CLIPTTA replaces entropy minimization with a soft contrastive loss that mirrors CLIP's pre-training objective but operates on the full distribution over pseudo-captions within a batch rather than hard pseudo-labels. The loss computes the entropy of the image-to-text probability matrix (row-wise) and optionally the text-to-image matrix (column-wise), where probabilities are normalized over the N batch samples rather than C classes. Gradient analysis shows the update for each sample aggregates contributions from all batch pseudo-captions weighted by β coefficients, enabling cross-sample error correction, and that the gradient magnitude vanishes as one class dominates the batch—providing a built-in collapse-prevention mechanism absent in entropy-based objectives. A class-wise confident memory (CCM) and a negative marginal entropy regularizer are added to the final loss. For open-set adaptation, an Outlier Contrastive Exposure (OCE) loss is introduced that maximizes the squared gap between the mean MCM scores of ID and OOD samples, with a jointly learned threshold α, improving both OOD detection (AUC, FPR95) and ID accuracy.

## Results

CLIPTTA achieves 58.1% average top-1 accuracy on corruption benchmarks (CIFAR-10-C, CIFAR-100-C, ImageNet-C), a +17.8 pt gain over zero-shot CLIP (40.3%) and +8.3 pt over the best competing method (CLIPArTT, 49.8%). On domain-shift benchmarks (VisDA-C, PACS, OfficeHome, ImageNet-Domains), CLIPTTA reaches 83.7% average, outperforming all baselines. In the open-set setting on ImageNet with Places-365 as OOD, CLIPTTA+OCE achieves 97.7% AUC and 9.7% FPR95, surpassing the best open-set baseline UniEnt (95.4% AUC, 17.1% FPR95) while maintaining 67.6% classification accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CLIPTTA outperforms all four TENT-style entropy-minimization baselines (TENT, ETA, SAR, RoTTA) on every corruption and domain-shift dataset, with the largest gaps on corruptions where entropy-based methods suffer from class collapse (e.g., +20.5 pts over TENT on CIFAR-10-C, +17.4 pts on CIFAR-100-C). Against CLIP-specific TTA methods, CLIPTTA beats CLIPArTT by +8.3 pts on corruption average and WATT by +14.6 pts, while also leading on domain shifts by +2.9 and +1.6 pts respectively. Compared to the two most recent CLIP TTA methods, CLIPTTA improves over TPT (prompt tuning) by +19.4 pts on corruptions and over TDA (gradient-free) by +15.6 pts; TDA is the sole exception, edging CLIPTTA by 1.6 pts on ImageNet-Domains (65.0 vs 63.4) due to per-dataset hyperparameter tuning. In the open-set setting, CLIPTTA+OCE surpasses UniEnt by +2.3 AUC and +7.4 FPR95, and SoTTA by +8.4 AUC, while all closed-set entropy-based methods (TENT, CLIPArTT, SAR) degrade severely below zero-shot CLIP when OOD samples are present.

## Relation to Prior Work

CLIPTTA builds directly on CLIP's image-text contrastive pre-training objective (Radford et al., 2021) and TENT's test-time adaptation framework (Wang et al., 2021), but departs from the dominant entropy-minimization paradigm by replacing it with a soft contrastive loss that normalizes over batch pseudo-captions rather than over all classes. It extends prior CLIP-specific TTA methods (CLIPArTT, WATT) that attempted to leverage multimodal structure but remained heuristically driven, and extends open-set TTA (OSTTA, SoTTA, UniEnt) with a discriminative OCE loss that learns the ID/OOD threshold end-to-end. The key conceptual departure is making the adaptation loss batch-aware so that gradients from other samples in the batch can correct individual pseudo-label errors, rather than treating each sample independently as entropy-based methods do.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clear, theoretically grounded answer to a practical question: why do standard TTA methods (TENT and variants) degrade CLIP's performance under corruptions, and what loss should be used instead. The gradient analysis showing that the soft contrastive loss's magnitude vanishes under class imbalance offers a mechanistic explanation for collapse resistance that goes beyond empirical observation. Limitations include reliance on global image-text interactions (potentially limiting fine-grained or localized shifts), restriction to classification tasks, and the assumption that additional inference-time computation is available. Open directions include exploiting cross-modal consistency more thoroughly, extending to real-time/embodied settings, and generalizing beyond classification.
