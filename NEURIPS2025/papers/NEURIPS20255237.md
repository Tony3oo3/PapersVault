## Problem

Discrete diffusion models for text generation rely on a Markovian assumption that restricts each denoising step to condition only on the current latent state, causing small decoding errors to accumulate irreversibly across timesteps. This limitation makes discrete diffusion models lag behind causal language models in generation quality, despite offering more flexible bidirectional and partially-specified generation. The paper asks whether lifting the Markov constraint—allowing each step to access the full generative trajectory—can close this gap while preserving the architectural simplicity of decoder-only transformers.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a 2D input structure: a sequence of corrupted token blocks arranged along both the token-position axis (length L) and the diffusion-timestep axis (T steps), forming a non-Markovian forward trajectory (x_T, x_{T-1}, ..., x_0). At inference, the model iteratively predicts a clean estimate x̃_0 from the trajectory x_{t:T}, then re-applies the corruption kernel to produce x_{t-1}, looping until the final sample is recovered. For conditional generation, a label prompt (e.g., positive/negative) is prepended and certain token positions are held fixed, enabling infilling from arbitrary locations rather than only left-to-right.

## Method

CaDDi unifies sequential (left-to-right token order) and temporal (diffusion timestep) dimensions in a single decoder-only transformer by arranging the non-Markovian trajectory as a block-wise causal sequence and applying a 2D rotary positional encoding that rotates query/key vectors along disjoint subspaces for token position and timestep. The reverse process is non-Markovian: p_θ(x_{t-1} | x_{t:T}) = q(x_{t-1} | x_0 = μ_θ(x_{t:T}, t)), so the denoiser conditions on the entire future trajectory rather than a single state. The token-level variant CaDDi-AR further factorizes over token positions, reducing training to a standard next-token prediction loss and making T=1 a strict special case of a causal language model—enabling direct fine-tuning of pretrained LLMs with no architectural changes. A semi-speculative decoding strategy reuses the previous timestep's prediction as a draft, verifying tokens in parallel to cut inference cost from O(L×T) sequential steps. For absorbing kernels, the ELBO simplifies to a weighted cross-entropy between the model's clean-data prediction and the ground truth, with time-dependent weights α_{t-1} reflecting corruption level.

## Results

On LM1B, CaDDi achieves a generative perplexity of 23.81 (Llama-2-7B oracle, T=0.5), substantially outperforming the best discrete diffusion baseline SEDD (46.91) and DFM (61.91). On Text8 at 64 discretization steps, CaDDi reaches ≤1.41 BPD, beating MDLM and SEDD (both ≤1.46) and UDLM (≤1.60), though trailing autoregressive baselines like Discrete Flow (1.23 BPD). When fine-tuned from QWen2-1.5B, CaDDi-AR achieves 34.2% on ARC-Challenge and 66.3% on LAMBADA, improving over the base model by +1.9% and +2.4% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CaDDi consistently outperforms all discrete diffusion baselines (D3PM, SEDD, MDLM, UDLM, DFM) on LM1B generative perplexity across all three LLM oracles and temperature settings, with the largest margin over SEDD (23.81 vs. 46.91 PPL under Llama-2, T=0.5). On Text8 at 64 steps, CaDDi (≤1.41 BPD) beats MDLM and SEDD (≤1.46) and UDLM (≤1.60), but trails autoregressive baselines: Discrete Flow and standard Autoregressive both reach 1.23 BPD, and Argmax Flow achieves 1.39 BPD. Among any-order autoregressive models, CaDDi is competitive with MAC (≤1.40) and ARDM (≤1.43) but slightly behind both. On reasoning benchmarks, CaDDi-AR (fine-tuned from QWen2-1.5B) surpasses the base QWen2 model on ARC-Challenge (34.2% vs. 33.7%) and LAMBADA (66.3% vs. 63.9%), and outperforms the from-scratch diffusion model MDM (1.1B) on most tasks (e.g., ARC-Easy 67.8% vs. 48.7%, LAMBADA 66.3% vs. 52.7%) while requiring less training effort. On conditional generation (Amazon Polarity), CaDDi-CFG with γ=1.25 achieves 85.92% negative sentiment accuracy, exceeding GPT-2's 75.18%, while matching GPT-2 on positive (73.61% vs. 73.07%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the non-Markovian diffusion framework of DART [23]—originally formulated for continuous data with smooth kernels—to the discrete token setting, where the independent per-timestep corruption process yields a fundamentally different trajectory structure than the standard Markov chain of D3PM [2]. The reverse process is shown to be inherently autoregressive over the latent trajectory, which the authors exploit by embedding both the sequential (token) and temporal (diffusion step) dimensions into a single decoder-only transformer with a 2D rotary positional encoding extending RoPE [53]. A key positioning claim is that standard causal language models emerge as the T=1 special case of CaDDi-AR, enabling direct fine-tuning of pretrained LLMs without architectural modification, and the inference procedure borrows the verify-then-correct structure of speculative decoding [34] to reduce the O(L×T) sampling cost.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical implication that a pretrained causal LLM can be fine-tuned into a discrete diffusion model with no architectural changes (only a 2D RoPE extension and a modified training objective) is a low-friction path to gaining bidirectional/infilling generation capabilities while retaining large-scale pretraining knowledge. However, the empirical gains over strong discrete diffusion baselines (MDLM, UDLM, SEDD) are consistent but moderate, and the gap to large autoregressive transformers remains substantial, particularly on LM1B generative perplexity. Open questions include whether the full-trajectory conditioning is necessary at scale or whether truncated/recomposed trajectories (mentioned in Appendix C) suffice, how the method behaves at much larger model sizes (experiments are limited to ~1.5B), and whether the non-Markovian formulation's error-correction advantage persists as sequence length and diffusion steps grow.
