## Problem

Diffusion Classifiers (DC) suffer from noise instability: different randomly sampled Gaussian noises lead to significantly different classification predictions for the same image. Existing work mitigates this by ensembling hundreds of noise samples, which linearly increases inference cost (e.g., 18 seconds per image on an RTX 3090). The paper asks whether there exist 'good noises' that inherently stabilize DC predictions, and proposes principles and an optimization method to find them.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254393/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a clean RGB image x_0 (in the latent space of a pretrained text-to-image diffusion model) and K category text prompts as input, and outputs a single class label. Unlike standard DC that samples noise from a standard Gaussian at inference, NoOp replaces the random noise with a deterministic optimized noise ε* = ε + ε', where ε is a fixed dataset-specific parameter and ε' is predicted per-image by a lightweight U-Net Meta-Network. The timestep is fixed (e.g., t=500), and classification is performed by selecting the category whose denoising prediction is closest to ε* in L2 distance.

## Method

NoOp (Noise Optimization) learns a matching noise through two complementary mechanisms grounded in two principles. For Frequency Matching, a single dataset-specific noise ε is randomly initialized from a Gaussian and directly optimized via cross-entropy classification loss, learning to destroy the frequency-band signals most relevant to the target dataset's categories. For Spatial Matching, a lightweight U-Net Meta-Network (≈7M params, <3G FLOPs) takes the input image as conditioning and outputs an image-specific noise offset ε', adapting the destruction pattern to each image's spatial layout. The final noise ε* = ε + ε' replaces random sampling in the DC forward process. A key training detail is Z-score normalization across the K category logits before cross-entropy, which amplifies the small inter-category distance differences that would otherwise yield near-zero gradients. The denoising network remains frozen throughout; only ε and the Meta-Network are trained on few-shot data, making NoOp orthogonal to and composable with prompt optimization.

## Results

On ISIC-2019, NoOp achieves 23.72% top-1 accuracy at 4-shot, outperforming zero-shot DC (13.25%) and TiF Learner (19.76%). On FGVCAircraft, NoOp reaches 14.61% at 16-shot, while the NoOp+TiF combination achieves 25.74%. Across eight few-shot datasets, NoOp with only 2 shots already beats the 5-noise ensemble baseline on average. In cross-dataset transfer (4-shot ImageNet → 8 targets), NoOp gains +2.86% average accuracy over the 5× ensemble, and reduces total compute from 153.0 to 37.0 GPU-hours (≈24%) while improving accuracy by 3.19%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the zero-shot DC and 5-noise ensemble baselines from Clark et al. [6] and Li et al. [7], NoOp with only 2 shots already surpasses the 5× ensemble on average across eight datasets, while requiring roughly 24% of the total compute (37.0 vs. 153.0 GPU-hours on ImageNet 16-shot). Against TiF Learner (prompt-optimization DC) from Yue et al. [10], NoOp wins clearly on ISIC-2019 (e.g., 20.80% vs. 13.89% at 2-shot; 23.72% vs. 19.76% at 4-shot) but loses on FGVCAircraft (e.g., 14.61% vs. 21.03% at 16-shot), indicating the two methods target complementary aspects of the classification pipeline. The NoOp+TiF combination achieves the best results on both datasets (29.29% at 8-shot on ISIC-2019; 25.74% at 16-shot on FGVCAircraft), confirming that noise optimization and prompt optimization are orthogonal few-shot learners. In cross-dataset transfer, NoOp outperforms the 5× ensemble on 6 of 8 target datasets, with the largest gains on CIFAR-100 (+11.40%) and Flowers102 (+12.01%), though it underperforms on CIFAR-10 (−6.65%) and STL-10 (−3.78%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Diffusion Classifier (DC) framework introduced by Clark & Jaini (NeurIPS 2023) and Li et al. (ICCV 2023), which repurpose pretrained text-to-image diffusion models for zero-shot/few-shot classification by comparing denoising errors under different category prompts. While prior few-shot DC work (e.g., TiF Learner, Yue et al. CVPR 2024) optimizes text prompts to bridge the distribution gap, and all existing DCs rely on expensive multi-noise ensembling to mitigate noise instability, this paper identifies the noise itself as the optimization target. It draws on the Frequency Bias Theory to motivate a dataset-specific frequency-matching noise and introduces a lightweight U-Net Meta-Network for image-specific spatial matching, positioning noise optimization as a new, orthogonal axis of adaptation for DC.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

NoOp addresses a practical bottleneck in DC-based classification: the 5× (or more) inference cost of noise ensembling, reducing it to a single forward pass per category while matching or exceeding ensemble accuracy. The demonstrated orthogonality to prompt optimization is practically significant, as it enables stacking independent gains without interference. However, the method remains within the DC framework, inheriting its O(K) forward-pass cost per image, and cross-dataset transfer is inconsistent (negative on CIFAR-10 and STL-10). Open questions include whether the optimized noise generalizes to open-vocabulary settings, whether it composes with flow-matching or unified autoregressive-diffusion architectures, and whether the frequency/spatial matching principles hold for video or 3D classification.
