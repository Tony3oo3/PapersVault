## Problem

Text-to-image diffusion models use fixed text embeddings from a frozen text encoder across all diffusion timesteps, limiting their ability to adapt to the evolving generative process. Since different timesteps influence generation in distinct ways, static embeddings fail to capture evolving semantics, resulting in suboptimal text-image alignment and human preference satisfaction. The paper asks whether dynamically adapting text embeddings during sampling can improve alignment without retraining the model.

## Input / Output

DATE takes a text prompt (or a source image plus target prompt for editing) and produces a generated or edited RGB image. It operates as a test-time plug-in on top of any pre-trained text-to-image diffusion model (SD v1.5, PixArt-α, SD3, FLUX, SDXL) and sampler (DDIM, DDPM, DPM-Solver), requiring no architectural changes or retraining. The user selects an evaluation function h (e.g., CLIP score, ImageReward, PickScore, or a weighted combination) and a scale hyperparameter ρ that controls the magnitude of embedding updates; updates are applied at a configurable subset of sampling steps to balance quality and runtime.

## Method

DATE formulates a sequential optimization problem that maximizes a text-conditioned evaluation function h on the mean predicted image x̄₀ at each diffusion timestep, subject to a bounded deviation ρ from the original embedding. Using Tweedie's formula, x̄₀ is computed via a single score-network evaluation, and a first-order Taylor approximation reduces the objective to a closed-form update: c_t = c_org + ρ · ∇_c h_t / ||∇_c h_t||₂, which is a single normalized gradient-ascent step on the evaluation function. Theoretically, this update is shown to introduce a guidance term into the score function that balances semantic alignment with the underlying model distribution, preserving generative quality. The method is entirely training-free, model-agnostic, and sampler-agnostic, and can be combined with multi-metric objectives (e.g., weighted CS + IR) to simultaneously improve multiple quality dimensions.

## Results

On COCO validation with SD v1.5 (DDIM, 50 steps), DATE with 10% CLIP-score updates achieves FID 17.90, CLIP score 0.3237, and ImageReward 0.2364, outperforming the fixed embedding baseline (FID 18.66, CS 0.3204, IR 0.2132) while matching its sampling time. With ImageReward as the evaluation function, DATE boosts IR to 0.4792 (10% updates) or 1.2972 (all updates) while maintaining competitive FID. DATE generalizes across backbones: on SDXL it improves IR from 0.7284 to 0.9096, and on FLUX it improves FID from 29.59 to 29.41 and CS from 0.3257 to 0.3283.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DATE outperforms both baselines across all three metrics (FID, CLIP score, ImageReward) in the primary SD v1.5 / DDIM 50-step setting. Against EBCA [40], DATE is substantially better: FID 17.90 vs. 25.85, CLIP score 0.3237 vs. 0.2877, and ImageReward 0.2364 vs. −0.3128, indicating that EBCA's per-layer energy-based updates degrade both generation quality and semantic alignment. Against Universal Guidance [2], DATE achieves a lower FID (17.90 vs. 18.56), higher CLIP score (0.3237 vs. 0.3216), and higher ImageReward (0.2364 vs. 0.2221), showing that embedding-space optimization yields better alignment than injecting guidance directly into the data space. The paper also demonstrates that DATE's gains are not limited to SD v1.5: it consistently improves over fixed embeddings on PixArt-α, SD3, FLUX, and SDXL, confirming model-agnostic applicability.

## Relation to Prior Work

DATE sits at the intersection of three prior lines of work on improving text-to-image diffusion: (1) parameter fine-tuning and data-space guidance (Classifier/Universal Guidance, reward fine-tuning), (2) prompt-level optimization via RL or search, and (3) text-embedding manipulation (EBCA's per-layer energy-based updates, P2L's inverse-problem embedding optimization, and special-token tuning like Textual Inversion). The key departure is that DATE treats the full text embedding as a time- and instance-dependent variable optimized at test time via a single normalized gradient step on a differentiable evaluation function, using Tweedie's formula to compute the mean predicted image in one forward pass. This makes it orthogonal to and composable with existing guidance and fine-tuning methods, rather than competing with them.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DATE provides a simple, training-free, architecture-agnostic mechanism (one gradient step on the embedding) that consistently improves text-image alignment across SD v1.5, PixArt-α, SD3, FLUX, and SDXL, with a theoretical guarantee (Proposition 1) that adaptive embeddings weakly dominate fixed ones. Theorem 2 further clarifies the mechanism: the embedding update acts as an implicit guidance term on the score function, balancing alignment with the model's learned distribution. Open questions include how the method behaves with non-differentiable or discrete evaluation signals, whether the Taylor approximation degrades for very long or compositional prompts, and how DATE interacts with other test-time guidance (e.g., classifier-free guidance scaling). The computational overhead, while mitigated by sparse updates and half-precision, remains a practical consideration for real-time applications.
