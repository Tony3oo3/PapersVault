## Problem

Existing PPI-based semi-supervised learning methods provide only asymptotic guarantees at the population optimum with no finite-time convergence bounds, and require offline tuning of the interpolation parameter λ that depends on unknown quantities (labeled data variance and teacher accuracy). This paper addresses both gaps by deriving the first gradient variance bounds for PPI-based training and replacing offline λ selection with an online adaptive procedure.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253632/cropped_page7_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes n labeled samples, N unlabeled samples (with N >> n, e.g., 20 labeled vs. 1,000 unlabeled in synthetic experiments), and a fixed teacher model f that produces pseudo-labels for the unlabeled data. The teacher is frozen throughout training (teacher-student paradigm, not self-training), and the student model is updated via first-order optimization. The output is a trained model (linear regressor or ResNet-50-based classifier) that produces regression predictions or class labels, evaluated on both the full test set and per-subgroup to expose teacher-quality-dependent performance.

## Method

PP-SSL constructs an unbiased prediction-powered gradient g_PP^λ = g^n + λ(ḡ^{N,f} − g^{n,f}) that interpolates between standard SGD (λ=0) and full PPI (λ=1), where the correction term cancels teacher bias in expectation. The paper derives a variance bound showing the optimal λ* = (1/(1+r))·σ²/(σ²+σ_e²) depends on the unknown labeled-data variance σ² and teacher-error variance σ_e², making offline tuning infeasible. To resolve this, λ is co-optimized with model parameters w using a one-dimensional AdaGrad update on the per-step gradient second moment h_t(λ) = ‖g_t + λd_t^f‖², while w is updated with an AdaGrad-Norm stepsize that implicitly adapts to the unknown variance scale. The resulting convergence rate is O(√(V*/T) + Mβ/T + √(Mβ)G/T), matching the optimal-λ rate up to a lower-order term, with V* reflecting the variance reduction from high-quality pseudo-labels and abundant unlabeled data.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with Brightness corruption (severity 1), PP-SSL achieves 60.55% accuracy on the corrupted Group B, outperforming PPI++ (56.93%), SSL (4.92%), and Only Labeled (3.72%), while maintaining competitive overall accuracy (81.23% vs. 81.48% for SSL). On the UTKFace age estimation task, PP-SSL consistently achieves the lowest MAE across all N_B/N_A ratios, with the largest gains on Group B (ages 0–29) where the teacher is weakest. On synthetic regression data, PP-SSL achieves the best Group B MSE across all noise bias levels μ ∈ {0.1, 1, 3, 5, 7}, with the advantage growing as bias increases. The adaptive λ tuning matches or exceeds the best fixed-λ PPI++ baseline across all tested λ values.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against PPI++ (offline-tuned λ from [24]), PP-SSL wins on every dataset and metric, with the largest gaps on the subgroup where the teacher is weakest: on CIFAR-10 Brightness corruption, PP-SSL reaches 60.55% on Group B versus 56.93% for PPI++ (+3.6 pp), and on synthetic data PP-SSL achieves lower Group B MSE at all bias levels μ, with the gap widening as μ grows. Against standard SSL pseudo-labeling [4], PP-SSL is decisively superior on Group B: the SSL baseline often performs *worse* than the Only Labeled model on the corrupted/biased subgroup (e.g., 4.92% vs. 3.72% on CIFAR-10 Brightness Group B), while PP-SSL maintains 60.55%, demonstrating that the debiased gradient with adaptive λ prevents confirmation bias. On Group A (where the teacher is accurate), all methods perform comparably, confirming that PP-SSL's advantage is specifically in handling unreliable pseudo-labels. The adaptive λ tuning also matches the best fixed-λ PPI++ variant on synthetic data, validating the online learning approach without requiring knowledge of σ² or σ_e².

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Prediction-Powered Inference (PPI) framework from Angelopoulos et al. (Science 2023) and its PPI++ variant from the offline inference setting (confidence interval construction) to online semi-supervised model training. It builds on Doubly-Robust Self-Training (Zhu et al., NeurIPS 2024), which first applied a PPI-like debiased loss for training but only provided asymptotic guarantees and used a fixed step function for the interpolation weight. The key departure is replacing offline λ tuning (which requires impractical knowledge of data variance and teacher accuracy) with an online AdaGrad-based update, yielding the first finite-time convergence guarantees for PPI-based gradient estimators in a training context.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, bias-free mechanism for leveraging abundant unlabeled data in SSL that automatically down-weights unreliable pseudo-labels, addressing a well-known failure mode where standard pseudo-labeling amplifies teacher errors on underrepresented subgroups. The online tuning result—matching the convergence rate of the optimal but unknown λ*—removes a critical hyperparameter from practice. Key limitations include the assumption of a fixed teacher (ruling out self-training with evolving pseudo-labels) and the requirement that labeled and unlabeled data share the same feature distribution, both of which are natural but important constraints for real-world deployment. Extending the theory to non-stationary self-training and covariate shift are identified as open directions.
