## Problem

Standard alignment methods like DPO and RLHF cannot be directly applied to compound AI systems (e.g., an LLM paired with a diffusion model, or multi-LLM pipelines) because non-differentiable interactions between components block end-to-end gradient optimization, and system-level preferences are not decomposable into independent component-level preferences. This paper asks: how can we jointly align all components of a compound system to satisfy holistic, system-level human preferences?

## Input / Output

For the LLM+diffusion application, the system takes a text prompt (e.g., 'generate three images of a cat being progressively angrier') and produces a sequence of n images whose attributes should follow a coherent progression; the LLM generates intermediate captions that condition the diffusion model. For the LLM collaboration application, the system takes a user question and produces a refined text answer via a two-stage pipeline (first LLM drafts, second LLM refines). Preference data consists of (input, preferred_output, rejected_output) triples; SysDPO-Direct additionally requires observed intermediate outputs in the dataset, while SysDPO-Sampling works with only input–final-output pairs.

## Method

SysDPO models a compound AI system as a Directed Acyclic Graph (DAG) whose nodes are input, intermediate, and final outputs, with edges encoding data flow between components. This DAG structure enables factorizing the joint generation probability into a product of per-component conditional probabilities, which is then plugged into the DPO loss to yield an end-to-end optimizable objective. Two variants handle different data availability: SysDPO-Direct uses the exact factorization when intermediate outputs are observed in the preference dataset, while SysDPO-Sampling approximates the intractable marginalization over unobserved intermediates by summing over a small set of diverse, high-probability candidates generated via Diverse Beam Search. For diffusion-model components where the likelihood is intractable, the paper derives a tractable upper bound on the loss by converting log-probabilities into denoising losses (extending Wallace et al.). A theoretical analysis proves that both variants achieve β-perfect alignment with the preference oracle in the population setting under a uniform reference model.

## Results

On the LLM + diffusion model task, SysDPO-Direct achieves a Preference Score of 0.25 and an Order Consistency Ratio of 73%, compared to 32% for the unaligned system and 65% for training only the language model. On the two-LLM collaboration system (Intel/orca-dpo-pairs), SysDPO-Sampling achieves a 19.8% win rate against human-preferred responses (WR-Chosen) and a 66.4% win rate against the prompted baseline (WR-Prompted), a 55% relative improvement over the unaligned prompted system (12.8% WR-Chosen). The method also generalizes to a three-LLM system, achieving a 58% win rate against its unaligned counterpart.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the LLM + diffusion task, SysDPO-Direct outperforms all four baselines: it beats Best-of-N Sampling (73% vs. 67% OC Ratio) and Only Train Language Model (73% vs. 65% OC Ratio), while the unaligned system and Only Train Diffusion Model perform poorly (32% and 38% OC Ratio respectively). On the LLM collaboration task, SysDPO-Sampling (19.8% WR-Chosen, 66.4% WR-Prompted) outperforms Separate-DPO (16.6% WR-Chosen, 57.3% WR-Prompted) by 3.2 and 9.1 percentage points, demonstrating that joint system-level alignment surpasses independently aligning each component. It also beats both stage-wise ablations (SysDPO-ψ1: 16.0%/60.4%; SysDPO-ψ2: 18.1%/63.9%), confirming that jointly optimizing both components yields the best collaboration. The unaligned Prompted System performs worst at 12.8% WR-Chosen, underscoring that prompt engineering alone is insufficient for effective multi-component coordination.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) from single differentiable models to compound AI systems by formulating them as Directed Acyclic Graphs, enabling decomposition of the joint generation probability into per-component terms. It builds directly on the Bradley-Terry preference model and the DPO loss, and adapts the diffusion-DPO technique of Wallace et al. (CVPR 2024) to handle the intractable likelihood of diffusion models within the compound setting. The key departure from prior work is the identification of three structural bottlenecks—non-differentiable inter-component interactions, non-decomposable system-level preferences, and absence of component-level benchmarks—that render standard RLHF/DPO inapplicable, and the proposal of two variants (SysDPO-Direct and SysDPO-Sampling) that address different data-availability regimes. The paper positions itself against prompting-based composition, separate per-component DPO, and concurrent work (OptiMAS) that relies on local reward models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As compound AI systems (multi-agent LLM pipelines, LLM+diffusion workflows, RAG systems) become the dominant deployment paradigm, a principled alignment framework that optimizes system-level preferences end-to-end is a practical necessity that prior single-model alignment cannot address. The β-perfect alignment guarantee generalizes the DPO theoretical foundation to multi-component settings, providing a clean target for future work. Key limitations include the restriction to static DAGs (no feedback loops or dynamic routing), the computational cost of SysDPO-Direct when intermediate outputs are high-dimensional, and the modest experimental scale (≤8B parameters, small preference datasets). Open questions include extending to non-static architectures, improving sampling efficiency for SysDPO-Sampling, and validating at larger scale where the gap between joint and separate alignment may widen or narrow.
