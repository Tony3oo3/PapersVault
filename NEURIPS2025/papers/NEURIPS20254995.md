## Problem

One-shot NAS for semantic segmentation suffers from inefficient supernet training because all candidate paths are optimized uniformly, causing gradient conflicts and weight interference between promising and suboptimal paths. Existing selective-training methods rely solely on validation loss for path filtering, which is noisy in early training and fails to capture multiple dimensions of path quality (convergence, expressiveness, generalization) that matter at different training phases.

## Input / Output

The method operates on RGB images at resolutions up to 1024×2048 (Cityscapes), 720×960 (CamVid), and 720×1280 (BDD100K), producing pixel-level semantic segmentation masks with 11–19 classes. The search space encompasses multi-branch CNN architectures with configurable downsample rates (8, 16, 32), kernel sizes, group counts, expansion ratios, and dilation ratios, yielding a final architecture specification (e.g., 22-block SqueezeNAS-style or 6–7 cell FasterSeg-style design) alongside the segmentation output.

## Method

DPS is a stage-wise selective training strategy for one-shot supernet NAS that dynamically shifts the path-selection criterion across three phases: Stage I uses an angle score (cosine distance between initial and trained weights, with a sampling-frequency penalty) to prioritize convergent paths; Stage II activates a random-forest performance predictor trained on 1000 sampled paths to rank by expressiveness; Stage III combines the predictor with a multi-scale Information Bottleneck (MS-IB) objective that estimates I(Z;Y) via Donsker-Varadhan and I(X;Z) via vCLUB across multiple feature resolutions to select for generalization. At each selection step, m candidate paths are scored on the active proxies plus FLOPs, and the top-k non-dominated paths (Pareto front) are retained for training, with a diversity-driven exploration mechanism replacing the worst path if a structurally distant candidate exceeds a Hamming-distance threshold. A dynamic stage scheduler triggers transitions when the average improvement of the current metric over a sliding window drops below a variance-scaled threshold, and an evolutionary search with an elite population of 1,500 paths is applied post-training to extract the final architecture.

## Results

On the SqueezeNAS search space, DPS achieves 74.5% mIoU on Cityscapes validation and 73.8% on CamVid test with 10.96 GFLOPs, outperforming the original gradient-based SqueezeNAS (72.4%/73.2%) by 2.1% and 0.6% respectively at nearly identical computational cost. On the FasterSeg search space, DPS reaches 73.2% mIoU on Cityscapes and 72.1% on CamVid with 29.59 GFLOPs, surpassing FasterSeg (73.1%/71.1%) without relying on knowledge distillation. On the Cityscapes test set, DPS achieves 73.5% mIoU, the highest among all compared methods, while maintaining a compact 10.96 GFLOPs budget.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DPS outperforms all nine baselines on both search spaces. Against the gradient-based SqueezeNAS [21], DPS gains 2.1% mIoU on Cityscapes (74.5% vs 72.4%) and 0.6% on CamVid (73.8% vs 73.2%) at nearly the same FLOPs (10.96 vs 10.86). Against FasterSeg [8], DPS achieves 0.1% higher Cityscapes mIoU (73.2% vs 73.1%) and 1.0% higher CamVid mIoU (72.1% vs 71.1%) without the knowledge distillation that FasterSeg employs. The sampling-based GreedyNAS (Loss Based) [13] is outperformed by 1.8% on Cityscapes and 0.7% on CamVid (SqueezeNAS space), while Uniform Sampling [6] trails by 2.9% and 2.0% respectively. The training-free SasWOT [39] shows a large gap, particularly on CamVid (72.1% vs 64.3% on FasterSeg space). On the Cityscapes test set (Table 6), DPS achieves 73.5% mIoU, surpassing HR-NAS-A [41] (74.2% val but only 1.91 GFLOPs), FasterSeg (71.5% test), CAS [22] (70.5% test), and GAS [42] (71.8% test), confirming consistent gains across evaluation splits.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the one-shot NAS paradigm for semantic segmentation (DARTS, SqueezeNAS, FasterSeg) by departing from the common practice of using validation loss as the sole path-filtering criterion in multi-path sampling methods (GreedyNAS, GreedyNASv2, AttentiveNAS). It repurposes the angle score from Hu et al. (ECCV 2020) as a convergence proxy, introduces a random-forest performance predictor for expressiveness, and adapts Information Bottleneck theory (Tishby 2015) with MINE (Belghazi 2018) and vCLUB (Cheng 2020) estimators into a multi-scale generalization metric tailored to segmentation. The key conceptual departure is the stage-wise activation of these proxies, motivated by empirical observations that each metric's reliability evolves across training phases, combined with Pareto-based selection and a dynamic stage scheduler.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The stage-wise proxy strategy offers a practical and well-motivated solution to the gradient-conflict and weight-sharing problems that plague one-shot supernet training in large multi-branch segmentation search spaces. The MS-IB extension provides a principled, training-free generalization signal that improves robustness to corruptions (2.5% average mIoU gain), though its mixed results on cross-dataset transfer (slightly worse than single-scale IB on CamVid) suggest that not all feature scales contribute equally to generalization. Open questions include whether the stage-wise scheduling generalizes beyond segmentation to other dense prediction or classification tasks, and whether the IB-based proxy can be made more adaptive to scene complexity. The absence of error bars and the reliance on a single search space per experiment limit the strength of the empirical claims.
