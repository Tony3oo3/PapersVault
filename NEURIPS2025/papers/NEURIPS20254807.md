## Problem

Standard fine-tuning methods like GRPO assume high-quality reward signals or clear ground-truth answers, but complex long-horizon decision-making tasks (e.g., combinatorial optimization) inherently lack a single optimal action, and any finite-budget evaluation (e.g., MCTS Q-values) is a noisy approximation. Directly applying GRPO to such ambiguous, imperfect signals causes training instability and reward hacking, leaving a gap in how to robustly train LLMs from the noisy exploration data that is the only realistic supervision available in these domains.

## Input / Output

At each decision step, the LLM receives a structured text prompt encoding the static problem instance (node count, distance statistics, vehicle/capacity parameters for CVRP), the dynamic solution state (visited/unvisited counts, current total cost, edge cost statistics, solution validity flag), and a short history of the last few heuristic selections with their cost deltas. The output is a structured response containing qualitative state assessments (problem type, state type, cost level relative to a greedy baseline), a reasoning block, and a selected heuristic action drawn from a predefined set of ~15 named algorithms (e.g., 2opt, simulated_annealing, GRASP) or a termination signal.

## Method

NaDRO replaces GRPO's single top-1 outcome reward with a dual-reward architecture. The Preference-based Outcome Reward (POR) partitions the ranked action space into a positive set (top k%) and negative set, assigning linearly scaled rewards within each set to reduce training variance at the cost of a controlled bias; the optimal cutoff k is derived by maximizing a value-separability objective J(k) that measures the expected Q-value gap between the two sets. The Context Perception Reward (CPR) adds a dense, outcome-independent process reward that checks whether the LLM correctly identifies qualitative state features (e.g., whether current cost is high/low/normal relative to a greedy reference) before committing to an action, using rule-based ground-truth labels. Training proceeds offline via GRPO with LoRA fine-tuning on MCTS-generated state-action-value pairs (1000 MCTS iterations per state), and the two rewards are combined with standard format and language-consistency auxiliary rewards. The key insight is that relative preference rankings from noisy MCTS evaluations are far more robust than absolute Q-values, and that coupling a variance-reducing outcome signal with an immediate process signal prevents both overfitting to noise and reward hacking.

## Results

On 10 TSPLIB TSP instances, NaDRO-Qwen 7B achieves an average optimality gap of 1.57% (range 0.08%–5.31%), while NaDRO-Llama 3.1-8B achieves 2.25% (range 0.31%–6.22%). The most dramatic improvement is over standard GRPO fine-tuning on the same base models: on gr202, the optimality gap drops from 14.68% (GRPO-Qwen) to 2.29% (NaDRO-Qwen), and from 11.33% (GRPO-Llama) to 2.41% (NaDRO-Llama). On the CVRP Golden dataset, NaDRO-Qwen 7B achieves gaps of 7.49%–24.82%, substantially outperforming GPT-4o (12.65%–34.42%) and its own GRPO baseline (27.08%–48.94%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

NaDRO-Qwen 7B outperforms GPT-4o on 9 out of 10 TSP instances (e.g., gr202: 2.29% vs 4.23%; a280: 5.31% vs 7.11%) and GPT-o3 on 7 out of 10 (losing only on a280: 5.31% vs 3.71% and pr152: 0.22% vs 0.28% where GPT-o3 is marginally better). Against DeepSeek-R1, NaDRO-Qwen wins on 8 of 10 instances, with the largest margin on a280 (5.31% vs 14.10%). The most striking comparison is against standard GRPO fine-tuning on the identical base models: NaDRO reduces the gr202 gap from 14.68% to 2.29% (Qwen) and from 11.33% to 2.41% (Llama), a 6–8× improvement attributable solely to the dual-reward design. Against metaheuristics, NaDRO-Qwen beats EoH+GLS and ReEvo+GLS on most instances but remains behind the specialized LKH solver (0.00–0.01% gap) and is competitive with OR-Tools. On CVRP, NaDRO-Qwen 7B outperforms all LLM baselines (GPT-4o, GPT-o3, DeepSeek-R1) and its own GRPO baseline by 10–30 percentage points on average, and even surpasses ReEvo+ACO on several large instances where the latter fails to complete.

## Relation to Prior Work

NaDRO extends the GRPO training paradigm (from DeepSeekMath) by redesigning its reward structure for noisy, ambiguous supervision signals. It draws on the preference-learning literature (RLHF, DPO) for the POR component's bias-variance tradeoff, and on process-reward shaping (reward shaping, auxiliary tasks) for the CPR mechanism. The paper departs from prior LLM-as-meta-controller work on combinatorial optimization (EoH, ReEvo) by addressing a specific failure mode: standard GRPO's reliance on high-quality, unambiguous reward signals that are unavailable in long-horizon decision tasks where MCTS-generated Q-values are inherently noisy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

NaDRO demonstrates that moderately-sized LLMs (7B–8B) can be trained on noisy MCTS data to outperform much larger general-purpose models (GPT-4o, DeepSeek-R1) on combinatorial optimization, reducing reliance on expensive expert labeling. The dual-reward design (preference-based outcome + process-based context perception) offers a general recipe for stabilizing RL fine-tuning under imperfect supervision, with potential transfer to other domains facing noisy or ambiguous rewards. A key limitation is that CPR's qualitative features are manually defined and rule-annotated, constraining automatic generalization to new domains; future work on learning these features automatically would broaden applicability.
