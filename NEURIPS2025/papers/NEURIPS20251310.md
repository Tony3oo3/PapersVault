## Problem

Existing offline RL methods for LLM conversation optimization (STaR-GATE, StepDPO) only use reward signals indirectly—selecting high-reward trajectories for SFT or constructing preference pairs for DPO—rather than directly optimizing for reward. Meanwhile, standard RL algorithms like PPO and GRPO rely on token-level propensity score ratios that cause numerical instability and require additional hyperparameters. The paper asks whether offline RL for LLMs can be recast as a simple weighted fine-tuning problem that directly optimizes rewards without propensity score ratios.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a logged dataset of 500 multi-turn conversations per benchmark, where each entry consists of a context string x (the question or task), a trajectory τ_n of n=3 agent actions and environment observations, and a scalar reward r ∈ [0,1] computed by a GPT-4o judge. The output is a fine-tuned Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct policy that generates multi-turn conversation responses (reasoning steps or clarifying questions) conditioned on the conversation history. In Swift, the reward is further standardized per context using m=3 logged trajectories sampled at different temperatures, yielding a standardized reward r̃ that can be negative.

## Method

The core insight (Lemma 1) is that the expected policy value V(θ) is lower-bounded by E[r(x,τ_n) log π(τ_n|x;θ)], which decomposes into a sum of per-action log-probabilities each weighted by the full trajectory reward—making the offline RL objective equivalent to reward-weighted fine-tuning (Refit). This avoids token-level propensity score ratios entirely, unlike PPO/GRPO, and can be implemented as a minor modification to standard SFT in TRL. Swift extends Refit by standardizing rewards per context (subtracting the mean and dividing by the standard deviation across m logged trajectories), which reduces gradient variance and is justified by Lemma 2 showing that the standardized objective shares the same maximizer under a joint-optimality assumption. The gradient update for a single trajectory is algebraically equivalent to fine-tuning on n SFT data points weighted by the same reward, enabling a single-pass training procedure over the logged dataset.

## Results

On the ARC benchmark (thinking mode), Swift achieves 79.9% accuracy and 7.19/10 overall conversation reward, outperforming Refit (78.9%, 7.12), STaR-GATE (69.9%, 6.67), DPO (64.7%, 5.72), and the base policy (37.7%, 6.47). Across all 12 reasoning experiments (6 datasets × 2 modes), Swift wins on accuracy in 7/12 and is among the top-2 in 10/12; Refit is among the top-2 in 9/12. On the hardest dataset (MathDial), Swift reaches 19.3% accuracy vs. 0% for the base policy, while on the easiest (SciQA) it reaches 92.5%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against STaR-GATE [Andukuri et al., 2024], which fine-tunes on the most rewarding trajectories, Swift and Refit achieve substantially higher accuracy (79.9%/78.9% vs. 69.9%) and overall reward (7.19/7.12 vs. 6.67) on ARC thinking mode, with the gap widening on harder datasets like MMLU (70.3%/71.0% vs. 60.0%). Against DPO [Rafailov et al., 2023], which uses final winning/losing responses without conversation, Swift and Refit lead by 15+ points in accuracy and ~1.5 points in overall reward. Against StepDPO [Chen et al., 2024], which generates alternative responses per step and applies DPO, the advantage is even larger: Swift outperforms StepDPO by ~16 points in accuracy and ~1.7 points in overall reward on ARC. The distillation variant STaR-GATE-D improves accuracy over STaR-GATE but degrades conversation quality metrics (R Overall drops from 6.67 to 5.47), while Refit and Swift improve both simultaneously. The base (untrained) policy is consistently the worst on accuracy, confirming the value of offline RL learning, though it sometimes scores comparably on reward metrics, suggesting the base model already produces fluent but less accurate conversations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the offline RL framework (Levine et al., Lange et al.) to LLM-based conversation policies by deriving a lower bound on the online policy-gradient objective that reduces to reward-weighted fine-tuning, avoiding the token-level propensity score ratios required by PPO and GRPO. It departs from the closest prior work in conversation optimization—STaR-GATE (SFT on top-reward trajectories) and StepDPO (DPO on step-level preferences)—by directly optimizing the reward signal rather than converting it into a binary SFT or preference problem. The theoretical foundation builds on the log-inequality lower bound from Ma et al. and Liang and Vlassis, and the standardized-reward variant (Swift) is motivated by GRPO's use of group-normalized advantages.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that Refit and Swift can be implemented as a minor modification to standard SFT pipelines (e.g., TRL), making offline RL accessible without the hyperparameter tuning and numerical instability associated with PPO/GRPO clipping. Key limitations include the computational cost of generating logged trajectories, reliance on a single LLM judge (GPT-4o) for reward computation, and the absence of human evaluation. Open questions include whether the lower-bound gap (which is loose when the learned policy diverges from the logging policy) degrades performance in practice, whether the approach generalizes to longer-horizon or multi-agent conversations, and how sensitive results are to the quality and diversity of the logged dataset.
