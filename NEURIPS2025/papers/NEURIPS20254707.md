## Problem

Graph Condensation (GC) synthesizes small yet informative graphs for efficient GNN training, but its performance degrades significantly when the original graph is corrupted by adversarial attacks on features, structure, or labels. Existing robust GNN techniques (e.g., MedianGCN, GNNGuard) fail to improve GC robustness, and no universal defense covering all three attack types has been proposed. The paper identifies that GC inherently reduces classification complexity (intrinsic dimension, boundary complexity, class ambiguity) and that attacks specifically disrupt this property, motivating a geometry-based defense.

## Input / Output

The method takes a poisoned graph Ĝ = {X̂, Â, Ŷ} with n nodes where features, adjacency, and labels may all be corrupted, and produces a compact condensed graph G' = {X', A', Y'} with n' ≪ n nodes. The condensed graph is initialized by randomly selecting non-outlier nodes from the original graph (outliers identified via Euclidean distance thresholds), and the adjacency/label initialization follows the GCond protocol. The output condensed graph is then used to train a GNN for node classification on test nodes of the original graph, with the objective of minimizing expected task loss over a distribution of GNN parameterizations.

## Method

MRGC is a plug-and-play framework that adds three graph data manifold learning modules to any existing GC backbone (GCond used in experiments). (1) Intrinsic Dimension Manifold Regularization estimates the dimension of the manifold on which the condensed graph lies via a Laplacian approximation of the coordinate-gradient integral, using node representations Z' = (A')²X' after two rounds of message passing, and regularizes the product of the manifold volume (via det(Σ_Z' + I)) and the graph Laplacian operator. (2) Curvature-Aware Manifold Smoothing computes the Gaussian curvature at each node by fitting a quadratic hypersurface in the tangent space (derived from k-NN eigendecomposition) with targets given by normal-vector projections, then weights each node's |K(i)| by its Ricci curvature (Ollivier definition) to emphasize bridge nodes at community boundaries. (3) Class-Wise Manifold Decoupling minimizes the squared difference between the sum of individual class manifold volumes and the total data manifold volume to reduce class overlap. The total loss is L_GC + αL_dim + βL_cur + γL_sep, with PCA applied before computing regularization terms to keep d' manageable.

## Results

MRGC achieves the best node classification accuracy across nearly all datasets and condensation ratios under simultaneous structure (1%), feature (10%), and label (20%) attacks. On Cora at 1.30% ratio, MRGC reaches 77.43% accuracy, outperforming GCond (70.69%) and RobGC (71.60%) by 6.7 and 5.8 points respectively. On PubMed at 0.08% ratio, MRGC achieves 74.85% vs. GCond's 70.81% and RobGC's 66.27%. The only exception is Ogbn-arxiv at 0.25% and 0.50% ratios, where GEOM (66.49% and 67.56%) slightly edges out MRGC (61.79% and 63.32%) due to trajectory-matching methods' inherent advantage on that dataset.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MRGC consistently outperforms all five standard GC baselines (GCond, SGDD, SFGC, GEOM, GCDM) across all four smaller datasets (Cora, CiteSeer, PubMed, DBLP) under all attack types and budgets. Against RobGC, the first dedicated robust GC method, MRGC gains 3.8–8.6 points on Cora and CiteSeer, and 2.2–8.6 points on PubMed and DBLP, with the largest margins under feature and label attacks where RobGC degrades significantly. The three denoising-preprocessing variants (GCond+S, GCond+J, GCond+K) provide only marginal improvements over vanilla GCond (≤1–2 points), confirming that preprocessing alone cannot address feature and label corruption. On Ogbn-arxiv at higher condensation ratios (0.25%, 0.50%), GEOM surpasses MRGC by ~5 points, attributed to trajectory-matching methods' inherent advantage on that large-scale dataset in clean settings. Under varying attack budgets (Table 2), MRGC maintains stable superiority: e.g., on CiteSeer at 40% label perturbation, MRGC achieves 59.20% vs. GCond's 49.25% and RobGC's 48.83%, a ~10-point gap.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the graph condensation literature (GCond, SFGC, GEOM, GCDM, SGDD) and the robust graph learning line (RobGC, MedianGCN, GNNGuard), but departs from both by reframing the problem through classification complexity theory. Rather than applying existing robust GNN architectures as condensation backbones—which the authors demonstrate empirpirically fails—it introduces three manifold-constrained regularization terms (intrinsic dimension, Gaussian curvature weighted by Ricci curvature, and class manifold overlap) directly into the GC training loop. The theoretical contribution that GC is inherently an intrinsic-dimension-reducing process, and that adversarial perturbations counteract this reduction, is absent from all prior GC work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first study to address GC robustness under simultaneous feature, structure, and label attacks, filling a gap left by RobGC (structure-only defense) and benchmark analyses that identified vulnerability without proposing a universal fix. The classification-complexity lens provides a principled design rationale—preserve the dimension-reducing, boundary-smoothing, and class-separating properties of GC—rather than ad hoc denoising. Key limitations include the exclusiveclusiE focus on node classification (graph classification is explicitly deferred), the reliance on GCond as the sole experimental backbone despite the plug-and-play claim, and the open question of whether manifold-constrained regularization transfers to trajectory- or distribution-matching GC methods.
