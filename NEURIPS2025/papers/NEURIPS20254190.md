## Problem

Existing convergence rate analyses for kernel ridge regression in RKHSs rely on the Bernstein condition (subexponential noise) to apply concentration inequalities within the integral operator framework. This paper asks whether the same minimax optimal rates can be achieved under the significantly weaker assumption of only finitely many higher conditional moments (heavy-tailed noise), and demonstrates that they can, with the same regularization schedules.

## Input / Output

The method takes n i.i.d. sample pairs (X_i, Y_i) from a joint distribution on a topological space X × R, a bounded symmetric positive semidefinite kernel k (with k(X,X) ≤ κ² a.s.), and a regularization parameter α > 0. It produces the ridge regression estimate f̂_α in the RKHS along with excess risk bounds that exhibit two distinct confidence regimes: a subgaussian regime (logarithmic in δ) for large sample sizes and a polynomial regime (polynomial in δ) for small sample sizes, with the transition governed by an effective sample size n₀ that depends on the moment order q and the ratio Q/σ².

## Method

The core technical contribution is a sharpened Hilbert-space version of the Fuk-Nagaev inequality (Proposition 5.1) providing tail bounds for sums of Hilbert-space-valued random variables with both subgaussian and polynomial components, fixing an inconsistency in the original proof by Yurinsky. Within the integral operator framework, the effective dimension N(α) is used not only as a variance proxy (as in the Bernstein inequality approach) but also as a proxy for the q-th absolute moment appearing in the Fuk-Nagaev bound. This dual use of the effective dimension, combined with polynomial eigenvalue decay assumptions, ensures the polynomial term is asymptotically dominated by the subgaussian term, recovering minimax optimal rates with the same regularization schedule as the light-tailed setting. The analysis covers both capacity-independent and capacity-dependent settings, with the latter yielding the sharper optimal rates matching known minimax bounds.

## Results

The paper establishes that kernel ridge regression under heavy-tailed noise (finite q-th conditional moment, q ≥ 3) achieves the same minimax optimal convergence rate (log(8c₁/δ)/n)^(min{ν,1}/(2min{ν,1}+p)) as under the standard subexponential (Bernstein) noise assumption, with the same regularization schedule α(n,δ) = (log(8c₁/δ)/n)^(1/(2min{ν,1}+p)) that is independent of q. In the capacity-independent setting, the rate is (log(6c₁/δ)/n)^(min{ν,1}/(2min{ν,1}+1)). A numerical experiment (Appendix A) with n=20 and a t(3) noise model confirms that the excess risk quantiles transition from subgaussian to polynomial behavior beyond confidence 1−δ ≈ 0.95, requiring stronger regularization than the Gaussian noise case.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not run head-to-head method comparisons; its contribution is theoretical. The central comparison is against the prior literature under the Bernstein condition (3), which requires all higher moments to grow factorially (subexponential tails). The authors show that the minimax optimal rates of Caponnetto & De Vito [3] and Steinwart et al. [35]—previously derived only under subexponential noise via the Bernstein inequality—hold under the strictly weaker finite-moment assumption (MOM) with the same regularization schedule. In the numerical experiment (Appendix A), the heavy-tailed t(3) noise model produces excess risk quantiles of the same order as the Gaussian noise model at moderate confidence levels, but diverges sharply at high confidence (1−δ > 0.95) for small α, confirming the predicted polynomial confidence regime and the need for stronger regularization. The Fuk-Nagaev tail bound (Proposition 5.1) is shown to be asymptotically sharp via a t-distribution example, matching the rate of the real-valued Fuk-Nagaev inequality [25].

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the integral operator framework for kernel ridge regression (Cucker & Smale, Caponnetto & De Vito, Smale & Zhou, Bauer et al.) by replacing the two-decade-standard Bernstein (subexponential) noise condition with a finite q-th moment assumption. The key technical ingredient is a sharpened Hilbert-space Fuk-Nagaev inequality, modifying Yurinsky's vector-valued result, which yields excess risk bounds with both subgaussian and polynomial tail components. The effective dimension, previously used solely as a variance proxy in Bernstein-type inequalities, is repurposed here as a proxy for higher moments, directly generalizing the concentration mechanism from the light-tailed setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result establishes that kernel ridge regression is asymptotically robust to heavy-tailed noise in a way that was not previously proven: minimax optimal rates are recovered under a finite moment condition with the same regularization schedule, removing a significant theoretical barrier for applying kernel methods in domains like finance and insurance where heavy tails are prevalent. Limitations include the restriction to integer q ≥ 3 (though extension to real q > 2 is expected), the well-specified setting (ν ≥ 1/2), and bounded kernels. Open questions include extending the analysis to misspecified models, unbounded kernels, and developing confidence-level-dependent regularization parameter choice rules that account for the polynomial confidence regime.
