## Problem

Loss weighting for last layer retraining (LLR) is known to be optimal in the population regime (δ→0) but ineffective in the separable overparameterized regime (δ>1), leaving the intermediate proportional regime δ∈(0,1) — where the number of retraining samples is on the same order as the latent dimension — theoretically uncharacterized. This gap is critical in practice, where LLR typically operates with limited data and a proportionately sized final layer, and the classical ratio-of-priors weighting is known to be suboptimal.

## Input / Output

The method consumes latent feature vectors from a pre-trained backbone (e.g., ResNet34 with 128–512 dimensional output), the class priors (π+, π−), and the overparameterization ratio δ = d_eff/n, where d_eff is the effective latent dimension estimated via PCA (number of principal components capturing 99% of variance) and n is the number of retraining samples. It outputs a single scalar weight ρ̃ applied to the minority class in a weighted square-loss objective, which is then used to retrain only the final linear layer, yielding a binary classifier with balanced per-class error.

## Method

Using the Convex Gaussian Minimax Theorem (CGMT), the paper reduces the (d+1)-dimensional weighted ERM problem to a system of four scalar equations in (α, γ, λ, b) that characterizes the asymptotic solution as d,n→∞ at fixed δ. For square loss, the Moreau envelope admits a closed form, collapsing the system to explicit algebraic equations with a unique solution. The key result (Theorem 2) derives the optimal minority-class weight ρ̃ = (π−/π+) + (π−/π+ − 1)·δ/(2π+ − δ), which equalizes per-class errors and differs from the classical ratio of priors by an overparameterization-dependent offset. The paper further proves that wERM with ρ̃ strictly outperforms downsampling in WCE for δ>0, and validates the prescription on CelebA and CIFAR-10 by substituting the PCA effective dimension for the full latent dimension, showing ρ̃ tracks the empirically optimal weight across sample sizes and imbalance ratios.

## Results

The paper derives a closed-form optimal loss weight ρ̃ = (π₋/π₊) + (π₋/π₊ − 1)·δ/(2π₊ − δ) for binary classification under square loss in the underparameterized regime δ ∈ (0,1), which reduces to the classical ratio of priors only as δ→0. The resulting worst-class error is Q(s²√(1−Δ)/√(Δ+s²)) where Δ = δ/(4π₊) + δ/(4π₋). Empirically, on CelebA (Straight Hair, 21% minority) with n=20 retraining samples, ρ̃ computed from the effective latent dimension (3) achieves substantially lower WCE than the ratio of priors. On CIFAR-10 (planes vs. trucks, 9% minority), ρ̃ with effective dimension 2 closely predicts the empirically optimal weight at both n=90 and n=20.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline comparison is against downsampling (Chaudhuri et al. [4]), where the majority class is downsampled to match the minority class size. Figure 5 shows that wERM with the optimal weight ρ̃ achieves significantly lower per-class error than downsampling at any fixed δ > 0, and the gap widens as overparameterization increases. This is because downsampling effectively increases δ to δ/(2π₊), which grows as 1/π₊ for highly imbalanced data, amplifying the overparameterization penalty. The paper also compares against the classical ratio of priors (π₋/π₊), which is the population-optimal weight (δ→0). Figures 2, 6, and 7 consistently show that the ratio of priors is suboptimal for any δ > 0: on CelebA with n=20, ρ̃ is distinctly larger than π₋/π₊ and yields much better WCE, while on CIFAR-10 with n=20 (only 1–2 minority examples), ρ̃ recovers a more balanced classifier where the ratio of priors fails. The theoretical restriction δ < 2π₊ is also validated: when this condition is violated (e.g., deer vs. horses on CIFAR-10 with effective dimension 9 and n=20), the per-class risks never intersect and the optimal strategy is ρ→∞, which is confirmed empirically.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper fills a critical gap between two well-studied extremes in cost-sensitive learning: the population regime (δ→0) where Welfert et al. [3] showed wERM and downsampling are equivalent and optimal, and the separable overparameterized regime (δ>1) where Byrd & Lipton [11] and Xu et al. [16] demonstrated that loss weighting has no effect on the learned model. The authors use the convex Gaussian minimax theorem (CGMT) [22] to derive a four-equation scalar system for the weighted ERM solution in the underparameterized proportional regime δ∈(0,1), which is the regime most relevant to practical last layer retraining where the number of retraining samples is on the same order as the latent dimension. This positions the work as a theoretical bridge, showing that wERM remains effective in the intermediate regime but requires weights that deviate from the classical ratio of priors in a δ-dependent way.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a concrete, theoretically grounded prescription (ρ̃) for loss weighting in last layer retraining that outperforms the widely-used ratio of priors, particularly when retraining data is limited. However, practical application depends on estimating the 'effective' latent dimension via PCA, which the authors acknowledge is heuristic and a major limitation. Open questions include extending the analysis to multiclass settings, non-Gaussian latent spaces, and developing a principled (non-PCA-based) method for estimating the effective dimension. The finding that overparameterization fundamentally changes the optimal weight — and that the classical ratio of priors is only recovered as δ→0 — has direct implications for practitioners fine-tuning large pretrained models on small, imbalanced validation sets.
