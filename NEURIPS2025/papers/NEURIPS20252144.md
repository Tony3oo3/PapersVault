## Problem

Current LLM-based data synthesis methods (temperature sampling, Evol-Instruct, Persona Hub) start from local distributions—model biases, seed data, or low-variation prompts—without a global view of the target data space, causing repetition, homogeneity, and space collapse as data scales grow. The paper asks whether an automatic solution exists that starts from a global perspective to fully and evenly cover a domain-specific data space, ensuring both diversity and comprehensiveness without seed data.

## Input / Output

The method requires only a free-text description of the target data space (e.g., 'GSM8K-style mathematical questions'), a generation LLM, and hyperparameters (tree depth d, pivot sample count l, max attribute values N, samples per leaf N_Leaf). It produces a spatial partitioning tree whose leaf nodes define mutually exclusive and exhaustive atomic subspaces, plus a training dataset where each sample is generated within a specific leaf subspace. For the data-balancing variant, an existing dataset is additionally routed through the tree to identify over- and under-represented subspaces, yielding a rebalanced dataset with a per-subspace sample threshold N_Sub.

## Method

TREESYNTH operates in two stages. In Data Space Partitioning, a spatial partitioning tree is built via breadth-first search: at each node, Criterion Determination uses an LLM to select the single most discriminative criterion from diverse pivot samples, and Subspace Coverage expands the resulting attribute values to guarantee exhaustive (and exclusive) coverage of the parent space, recursing until maximum depth d. In Subspace Data Synthesis, each leaf node's description is formed by intersecting the attribute values along its root-to-leaf path, and an LLM generates N_Leaf samples constrained to that subspace; collecting all leaf samples yields the final dataset. The mutual exclusivity of leaf nodes intrinsically prevents cross-subspace repetition, while their exhaustiveness prevents space collapse—properties that prior local-bias methods lack. A TREESYNTH-Guided Data Balance extension routes existing samples through the tree, downsamples over-represented subspaces, and synthesizes additional samples in under-represented ones to achieve uniform per-subspace coverage.

## Results

With GPT-4o-powered synthesis, TREESYNTH achieves an average score of 74.06 on Qwen2.5-7B (vs. 61.82 for vanilla data, 72.12 for Persona Hub, 68.49 for Evol-Instruct, and 70.88 for Temperature Sampling) across GSM8K, MATH, MBPP, and HumanEval, representing a ~12% absolute gain over human-curated data. On LLaMA3.1-8B, TREESYNTH reaches 49.46 average (vs. 41.93 vanilla, 45.36 Persona Hub). TREESYNTH also consistently achieves the lowest cosine-similarity diversity metric (e.g., 0.12 on MATH, 0.19 on code) across all generation models, confirming superior data diversity. Scalability experiments show near-linear performance growth with data volume (1k→100k), while baselines saturate or degrade.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TREESYNTH outperforms all three synthetic baselines across every benchmark and both foundation models. Against Temperature Sampling [Wang et al., 2020], TREESYNTH gains +8.58 avg on Qwen2.5-7B (74.06 vs. 65.75 with LLaMA3.3-70B; 74.06 vs. 70.88 with GPT-4o) and also achieves substantially lower cosine similarity (0.12–0.19 vs. 0.29–0.45), indicating far greater diversity. Against Evol-Instruct [Xu et al., 2023], TREESYNTH leads by +5.57 avg on Qwen2.5-7B (74.06 vs. 68.49) and +4.41 on LLaMA3.1-8B (49.46 vs. 45.05), with the gap widening on MATH (66.84 vs. 61.10) and code tasks. Against Persona Hub [Ge et al., 2024], TREESYNTH edges ahead by +1.94 avg on Qwen2.5-7B (74.06 vs. 72.12) and +4.10 on LLaMA3.1-8B (49.46 vs. 45.36), while matching or exceeding diversity on all benchmarks. Against the three human-curated vanilla datasets (GSM8K [Cobbe et al., 2021], MATH [Lightman et al., 2023], Code Alpaca [Chaudhary, 2023]), TREESYNTH achieves +12.24 avg on Qwen2.5-7B and +7.53 on LLaMA3.1-8B, with the largest single-benchmark gain on MATH (+19.16 over vanilla on Qwen2.5-7B). Notably, Evol-Instruct and Persona Hub underperform vanilla data on HumanEval in several settings, whereas TREESYNTH never falls below vanilla on any benchmark.

## Relation to Prior Work

TREESYNTH repurposes the structural properties of decision trees—mutual exclusivity and exhaustiveness of leaf nodes—as a principled mechanism for LLM-based data synthesis, departing from prior methods that operate from local distributions (model biases in Temperature Sampling, seed data in Evol-Instruct, or fixed attribute dimensions in Persona Hub). While attribute-driven approaches like Persona Hub already recognized the value of structured diversity, they rely on flat, manually curated attribute combinations; TREESYNTH generalizes this to a recursive, hierarchically constructed spatial partitioning tree that dynamically discovers criteria at each level. The BFS-based tree construction and the 'infinite node' handling for non-enumerable criteria are practical extensions that make the decision-tree analogy operational for open-ended text spaces.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a pressing practical bottleneck—diverse, high-quality training data for LLM customization—by offering a seed-free, globally-guided synthesis pipeline that scales linearly with data volume while baselines saturate, a property with direct implications for cost-effective large-scale data generation. The TREESYNTH-guided data balancing capability (rebalancing existing datasets via subspace allocation) is a useful secondary tool that could be adopted independently. Open questions include how the method behaves when LLMs produce ambiguous or overlapping criteria during partitioning, whether the empirically chosen tree depth (3–4) is optimal for all domains, and whether the approach extends naturally to multimodal or continuous-output spaces where 'mutually exclusive' partitioning is less well-defined.
