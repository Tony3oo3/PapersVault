## Problem

Grokking—the abrupt generalization of neural networks long after near-perfect training accuracy—lacks a mechanistic explanation. Rubin et al. proposed it is a first-order phase transition requiring crossing a free-energy barrier between memorization and generalization states, but this has not been verified by directly measuring the entropy landscape. This paper asks whether grokking involves a thermodynamic barrier or is instead a kinetic relaxation process, and whether the statistical-mechanics entropy framework can resolve the question.

## Input / Output

The method operates on a one-layer transformer (4 attention heads, width 32, MLP width 512) trained on modular arithmetic tasks with modulus p=67 and a 50% train/test split. WLMD sampling produces a 2D binned entropy landscape S(ln(L_train), A_test) where the x-axis is the logarithm of cross-entropy training loss and the y-axis is a sigmoid-smoothed test accuracy (slope s=7); absolute entropy values are arbitrary, only bin-to-bin differences are meaningful. Parameter space is bounded either by layer-specific reflective bounds or by a fixed weight-norm shell (norm=30) to keep the volume finite. The WanD optimizer outputs a trained transformer that generalizes, with weight norms reaching ~175.

## Method

The paper applies Wang-Landau Molecular Dynamics (WLMD) to sample the Boltzmann entropy of a transformer's parameter space as a function of log training loss and smoothed test accuracy, using an entropy-gradient-driven equation of motion with Langevin thermostatting to achieve uniform sampling across all regions. The key finding is that the entropy landscape shows no barrier between the memorization (low test accuracy, low train loss) and generalization (high test accuracy, low train loss) states, and the equilibrium curve is continuous—disproving the first-order phase transition hypothesis. The authors identify a large 'high-entropy advantage under grokking': the AdamW training trajectory sits far below the max-entropy equilibrium curve, indicating the network is trapped in a non-equilibrium glassy state. Inspired by this, they design WanD, a toy optimizer based on 1D WLMD that adds an entropy bias to drive exploration toward high-entropy (generalizing) states at low training loss, eliminating grokking without weight-norm constraints and finding high-norm generalizing solutions.

## Results

On the modular addition task (x+y mod 67), WanD achieves generalization after ~2,000 optimization steps with comparable performance to AdamW, while running faster (~50 s per 1K steps vs. ~72 s for AdamW on a single RTX 4090). Critically, WanD finds generalizing solutions at a weight norm of ~175 without any explicit regularization, whereas AdamW with weight decay=1 converges to lower-norm solutions. Entropy landscape sampling (60M–80M WLMD epochs) reveals no entropy barrier between the memorization and generalization states, and a large high-entropy advantage where the equilibrium curve significantly outperforms the AdamW training curve in test accuracy at matched training loss.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is AdamW, the standard adaptive optimizer used in the original grokking literature. WanD matches AdamW in generalization speed (~2,000 steps) while being ~30% faster per step and eliminating grokking without any weight-norm constraint or output scaling. Against the first-order phase transition hypothesis of Rubin et al. [1], the paper's entropy landscape sampling directly refutes it by showing a continuous equilibrium curve with no entropy or energy barrier between memorization and generalization. Against Liu et al. [3]'s Goldilocks-zone theory, WanD produces strictly-defined counterexamples: generalizing solutions at weight norm ~175, far above the norm-30 constraint that eliminates grokking in their protocol. The high-entropy advantage observed here is substantially larger than what Yang et al. [2] reported for non-grokking tasks, and persists (albeit weakened) even when grokking is suppressed via fixed weight norm, confirming the entropy–generalization link is not an artifact of glassy dynamics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Boltzmann entropy framework for neural networks introduced by Yang et al. [2] to the specific phenomenon of grokking, applying Wang-Landau Molecular Dynamics (WLMD) [30] to sample the entropy landscape of one-layer transformers on modular arithmetic tasks. It directly challenges Rubin et al. [1], who interpreted grokking as a first-order phase transition requiring a free-energy barrier, and Liu et al. [3], who attributed grokking to weight-norm evolution toward a Goldilocks zone. The glass relaxation analogy draws on Winter and Janssen [28], who previously noted structural similarities between deep networks and structural glasses, and the WanD optimizer repurposes the WLMD algorithm from statistical physics as a training procedure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that grokking lacks an entropy barrier and instead resembles slow glass relaxation offers a unifying physical picture that is consistent with multiple prior observations (representation learning shifts, sparse subnetworks, slingshot dynamics) while contradicting the phase-transition and weight-norm narratives. However, the evidence is confined to one-layer transformers on modular arithmetic with p=67, a single data split, and the WanD optimizer is explicitly a hyperparameter-sensitive toy that has not been validated beyond this narrow setting. Key open questions include whether the glass-relaxation picture holds for deeper architectures, other grokking benchmarks, and how it interacts with standard regularization methods, as well as whether entropy-guided optimization can be made into a practical general-purpose optimizer.
