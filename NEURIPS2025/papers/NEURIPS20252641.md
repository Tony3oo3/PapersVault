## Problem

Existing human action segmentation methods are designed for single-person scenarios with predefined action protocols, leaving multi-person untrimmed videos with unstructured, spontaneous action sequences unaddressed. No prior work combines textual reference guidance with action segmentation in such complex settings, making it impossible to segment the actions of a specific individual identified by a natural language description in a crowded video.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252641/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an untrimmed multi-person video (3,000-frame samples, ~3.3 minutes each) and a textual reference describing the target person (e.g., 'the girl wearing a pink dress') without revealing their actions. It produces frame-level multi-label action predictions over 137 fine-grained atomic action classes, with N/A labels for frames where the referred person is absent. The pipeline is two-stage: VLM features (BLIP-2 or CLIP) are pre-extracted from both the full video and a GroundingDINO-cropped person stream, and the diffusion model operates on these 768-dim (BLIP-2) or 512-dim (CLIP) feature sequences.

## Method

HopaDIFF is a dual-branch diffusion model where a holistic branch processes the full video and a partial branch processes a GroundingDINO-cropped person stream, both conditioned on the textual reference via a shared VLM feature extractor. The key architectural contribution is HP-xLSTM, which performs bidirectional cross-input gate attention (BCA) at the input gates of mLSTM blocks, allowing each branch to selectively incorporate temporal cues from the other for long-range holistic-partial reasoning. A novel frequency-domain conditioning mechanism applies DFT along the temporal axis to the HP-xLSTM outputs, injecting frequency-based control signals into the diffusion denoising process to preserve temporal coherence across both low- and high-frequency motion patterns. Two structurally identical decoders (ASFormer-based) denoise the action label sequence in each branch, supervised by binary cross-entropy and temporal boundary losses, with final predictions obtained by averaging the two branch outputs at inference.

## Results

On RHAS133 with BLIP-2 features under random partition, HopaDIFF achieves 62.58% ACC, 7.75 EDIT, and 87.96/85.50/79.39 F1@{10,25,50} on the test set, outperforming the best baseline ActDiff (41.85% ACC, 63.29% F1@50) by over 20 percentage points in ACC and ~16 points in F1@50. Under the more challenging cross-movie evaluation, HopaDIFF maintains 59.63% ACC and 89.26% F1@50, while ActDiff collapses to 2.36% ACC, and LTContent (the best baseline in this setting) reaches only 52.52% ACC and 42.55% F1@50. With CLIP features, HopaDIFF still leads all baselines by large margins (46.62% ACC vs. 23.95% for FACT).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HopaDIFF outperforms all six baselines across every metric in both random-partition and cross-movie settings. ActDiff [34] is the strongest baseline under random partition (41.85% ACC, 63.29% F1@50) thanks to its diffusion-based iterative denoising, but HopaDIFF surpasses it by ~21% ACC and ~16% F1@50; under cross-movie evaluation, ActDiff's learned action priors fail to transfer (ACC drops to 2.36%), while HopaDIFF remains stable at 59.63% ACC. LTContent [2] is the best cross-movie baseline (52.52% ACC, 42.55% F1@50) due to its sparse long-range transformer, yet still trails HopaDIFF by ~7% ACC and ~47% F1@50. RefAtomNet [42], designed for trimmed referring action recognition, achieves the second-best ACC (38.01%) but has the worst F1 scores (27.62% F1@50) because it lacks temporal boundary modeling. FACT [40] and ASQuery [18] are consistently the weakest, with F1@50 below 50% and 30% respectively, confirming that dual-branch cross-attention and query-based approaches struggle with multi-person, unstructured action sequences. The consistent gap across BLIP-2 and CLIP backbones further confirms that HopaDIFF's gains stem from its holistic-partial fusion and Fourier conditioning rather than feature extractor quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends diffusion-based action segmentation (ActDiff) by introducing a dual-branch architecture that fuses holistic video context with partial, person-cropped features via a novel cross-input gate attentional xLSTM (HP-xLSTM), and adds DFT-based frequency-domain conditioning to the diffusion denoising process. It departs from prior action segmentation methods (FACT, ASQuery, LTContent), which assume single-person, fixed-protocol videos, and from referring action recognition (RefAtomNet), which operates on trimmed clips, by formulating the problem over untrimmed multi-person movies with free-form action sequences. The HP-xLSTM module repurposes the mLSTM architecture from the xLSTM line of work, inserting bidirectional cross-attention at the input gate to enable structured information exchange between the two branches rather than simple concatenation or cross-attention fusion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By introducing the first dataset and benchmark for referring human action segmentation (RHAS133), this paper opens a new research direction at the intersection of language-guided video understanding and temporal action segmentation in realistic multi-person settings. The large performance gaps over adapted baselines—particularly in EDIT scores and cross-movie robustness—suggest that target-aware partial reasoning and frequency-domain conditioning are critical for fine-grained temporal boundary detection in unconstrained scenarios. Key limitations include reliance on a two-stage pipeline with pre-extracted VLM features (limiting real-time applicability), evaluation on a single self-constructed dataset, and acknowledged long-tail class issues. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond movie-domain data, whether end-to-end training without pre-extracted features is feasible, and how the method scales to even longer videos or larger action vocabularies.
