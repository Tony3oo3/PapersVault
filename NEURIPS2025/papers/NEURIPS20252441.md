## Problem

Existing NLOS perception systems can only detect or track hidden objects but cannot reconstruct the full 3D geometry of occluded scenes around corners. Visual sensors fail at NLOS because their short wavelengths scatter diffusely off common materials, while RF signals with millimeter-scale wavelengths produce predictable specular reflections that encode rich geometric information about hidden spaces. No prior system combines radar multi-bounce reflections with full 3D scene reconstruction in a practical, deployable form.

## Input / Output

The input is a 3D mmWave radar heatmap of shape 64×512×256 (elevation × azimuth × range bins), generated by beamforming over a mechanically rotated single-board radar operating at 77–81 GHz with 3.75 cm range resolution; only amplitude is retained (no phase). The system produces three dense range images (one per bounce up to 3), a semantically labeled 3D point cloud of the full scene (empty space, structures, humans) in a 20 m × 20 m × 3.5 m volume discretized into 256×256×32 voxels, and per-human centroid positions with a 50 cm matching threshold for detection evaluation.

## Method

HoloRadar uses a two-stage pipeline that decouples signal interpretation from spatial reasoning. Stage 1 (multi-return RF imaging) is a UNet with a shared 7-layer encoder and three separate 7-layer decoders that predict per-bounce range images from the noisy heatmap, trained with L1 range loss plus LPIPS perceptual loss against ray-traced ground truth from LiDAR SLAM meshes. Stage 2 (reflection-aware scene reconstruction) cascades three hybrid ray-tracing blocks, each combining a physics-guided signal-processing branch (specular reflection equations and closed-form surface-normal estimation from range-image derivatives) with a residual CNN branch to correct noise-sensitive derivatives; the blocks iteratively map mirrored measurement-space points back to world coordinates. A final sparse 3D UNet refinement module (Minkowski Engine) fuses all bounce predictions into a volumetric representation, performing multi-class voxel classification (empty/human/structure) and sub-voxel offset regression, supervised with cross-entropy, L1, and Euclidean Distance Transform losses. End-to-end inference runs in 298 ms, within the radar's 0.5 s rotation period.

## Results

On the HoloRadar dataset (32 corners, 28k radar scans), the full two-stage pipeline achieves a LOS F-score of 85.7% and NLOS F-score of 54.6%, with Chamfer distances of 12.2 cm (LOS) and 40.0 cm (NLOS). For hidden human detection, the system reaches 93.48% F-score with a mean localization error of 13.77 cm. The multi-return RF imaging stage (first stage) achieves a mean range error of 7.03 cm on the 1st bounce and 19.03 cm on the 2nd bounce, outperforming both transformer baselines across all three bounces.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two end-to-end transformer baselines: ViT (Dosovitskiy et al., ICLR 2020) and DPT (Ranftl et al., ICCV 2021), both adapted to directly predict 3D point locations from the radar heatmap. HoloRadar outperforms both baselines on every metric in both LOS and NLOS regions. On the NLOS F-score, HoloRadar achieves 54.6% versus 40.4% (ViT) and 37.0% (DPT), a 14.2 and 17.6 percentage-point improvement respectively. On LOS F-score, the gap is 13.3 points over ViT (85.7% vs 72.4%) and 15.9 points over DPT (85.7% vs 69.8%). Even without the scene refinement module, the hybrid ray-tracing stage alone (50.3% NLOS F-score) already surpasses the best baseline (40.4%), demonstrating that the physics-guided decomposition is the primary source of the performance gain rather than the refinement step.

## Relation to Prior Work

HoloRadar builds directly on the PanoRadar [20] radar platform and beamforming pipeline, repurposing the 3D RF heatmap as input to a learned reconstruction system. It departs sharply from prior radar-based NLOS work (CornerRadar, RFlect, Mosaic), which either requires known scene geometry, retro-reflectors, or time-consuming mechanical scanning, and which is limited to localizing or imaging hidden objects rather than reconstructing full 3D geometry. The two-stage decomposition—UNet-based multi-return range estimation followed by a physics-guided ray-tracing module with hybrid signal-processing/neural estimation blocks—represents a new architectural lineage that explicitly models specular reflection geometry rather than learning the mapping end-to-end.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first practical system to reconstruct both LOS and NLOS 3D scenes from a single mmWave radar on a mobile robot, opening a path toward robots that can plan collision-free paths around corners and detect hidden humans without visual line of sight. Key limitations include the indoor-only scope, the absence of an explicit human-shape prior in the refinement stage, and the still-moderate NLOS F-score (54.6%), suggesting substantial room for improvement. Open questions include extending to outdoor environments with longer propagation paths and dynamic interference, scaling beyond three bounces, and whether the physics-guided ray-tracing inductive bias generalizes to non-specular or partially absorbing surfaces.
