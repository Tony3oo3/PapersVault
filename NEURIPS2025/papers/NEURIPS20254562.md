## Problem

EAGLE and its variants perform autoregression at the feature level, requiring the draft model to predict the target model's top-layer features before converting them to tokens. This feature prediction constraint limits the draft model's expressiveness and prevents it from benefiting from increased training data, while top-layer features—being uniquely tied to next-token logits—are poorly suited for multi-step draft generation. The paper asks whether removing this constraint and redesigning the draft model's input can unlock a scaling law for inference acceleration.

## Input / Output

At each drafting step, the method concatenates the target model's low-, mid-, and high-level features (each k-dimensional) into a 3k-vector, projects it to k-dimensions via a fully connected layer, and concatenates with the embedding of the previously sampled token. For subsequent autoregressive steps where the target model's features are unavailable, the draft model's own output vector (a) replaces the fused feature, creating a self-referential input loop. The output is a sequence of draft tokens (up to 8 steps deep in the draft tree) that are verified in parallel by the target model using standard speculative sampling acceptance criteria.

## Method

EAGLE-3 removes the feature prediction loss entirely, training the draft model to directly predict tokens via the target model's LM head rather than regressing toward top-layer features. The key technique, training-time test, simulates multi-step autoregressive drafting during training by feeding the draft model's own predicted outputs back as inputs for subsequent positions, with a modified attention mask that enforces tree-like causal dependencies matching the inference-time draft structure. Instead of reusing only the top-layer features, EAGLE-3 fuses low-, mid-, and high-level features from the target model through a projection layer, capturing richer multi-scale semantic information. The draft model remains a single Transformer decoder layer, but this architectural freedom—no longer constrained to output top-layer features—enables a clear scaling law: increasing training data from ~68K to ~464K entries yields proportional speedup gains, a behavior never observed in EAGLE or HASS.

## Results

EAGLE-3 achieves a maximum speedup ratio of 6.5x (HumanEval, Vicuna-13B, temperature=0) with an average acceptance length τ of 7.54, compared to 4.96x/5.41 for EAGLE-2 and 3.58x/4.39 for EAGLE. Across all five tasks at temperature=0, EAGLE-3's mean speedup on Vicuna-13B is 5.51x (τ=6.62), representing a ~1.4x improvement over EAGLE-2 (4.22x, τ=4.83). In the SGLang production framework, EAGLE-3 maintains a 1.38x throughput improvement at batch size 64, whereas EAGLE degrades below 1.0x (0.99x) at the same batch size.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EAGLE-3 outperforms all eight baselines across every target model and task. Against the closest prior method EAGLE-2, EAGLE-3 improves mean speedup by 20–40% (e.g., 5.51x vs. 4.22x on Vicuna-13B at temp=0; 4.44x vs. 3.23x on LLaMA-3.1 8B). Against EAGLE, the improvement is roughly 1.8x in speedup (5.51x vs. 3.05x on Vicuna-13B). HASS, the most similar concurrent work, achieves only 3.42x mean speedup on LLaMA-3.1 8B versus EAGLE-3's 4.44x, and HASS fails to benefit from data scaling while EAGLE-3 shows a clear scaling curve. Among earlier speculative methods, Hydra (2.80x) and Medusa (2.12x) on Vicuna-13B are substantially outperformed, as are PLD (1.74x), Lookahead (1.62x), and standard speculative sampling (1.92x). Notably, in the SGLang framework at batch size 64, EAGLE-3 sustains 1.38x throughput while EAGLE drops to 0.99x, highlighting EAGLE-3's robustness at larger batch sizes.

## Relation to Prior Work

EAGLE-3 is a direct architectural successor to EAGLE (ICML 2024) and EAGLE-2 (EMNLP 2024), retaining the single-layer Transformer draft model and the context-aware dynamic draft tree from EAGLE-2. Its key departure is the removal of the feature prediction loss (l_fea) that constrained EAGLE's draft model to approximate top-layer features, replacing it with direct token prediction via a 'training-time test' technique that simulates multi-step autoregressive generation during training. This is conceptually related to HASS, which also simulates multi-step drafting during training, but EAGLE-3 goes further by eliminating the feature prediction constraint entirely and substituting top-layer features with a fusion of low-, mid-, and high-level target model features. The training-time test mechanism draws on the scheduled sampling idea from the RNN era, repurposed here to resolve the train-test distribution mismatch in speculative decoding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The most significant contribution is the discovery of a scaling law for inference acceleration: removing the feature prediction constraint allows the draft model to benefit proportionally from more training data, a behavior never observed in EAGLE or EAGLE-2. This means speculative decoding is no longer a fixed-overhead technique with a hard ceiling but can continue improving as data scales, aligning with the broader LLM community's data-scaling paradigm. Practically, the 6.5x speedup and 38% throughput gain at batch size 64 in SGLang make this immediately deployable. Open questions include how far the scaling law extends beyond the ~8x data range tested, whether it holds for models beyond 70B (untested due to GPU constraints), and whether the multi-layer feature fusion strategy generalizes to non-Transformer architectures.
