## Problem

Machine learning methods for high-energy physics have been evaluated on disparate ad-hoc datasets, making it impossible to compare their ability to handle systematic (epistemic) uncertainties. This paper introduces a 280M-event benchmark dataset with six parameterised nuisance parameters and a competition metric that requires participants to report confidence intervals for the Higgs signal strength μ rather than point estimates, providing the first common ground for uncertainty-aware ML in particle physics.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252767/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives an unlabelled pseudo-experiment of ~1000 tabular events (28 features: 16 primary kinematic quantities and 12 derived observables per event) generated from the primary 280M-event dataset with six nuisance parameters (TES/JES calibrations, soft MET noise, and three background normalisation scales) and a hidden signal strength μ ∈ [0.1, 3]. The output is a 68.27% confidence interval (μ₁₆, μ₈₄) for μ, evaluated by a quantile score that penalises both wide intervals and coverage deviating from 68.27%, with an asymmetric penalty that is more forgiving of over-coverage than under-coverage, reflecting HEP convention.

## Method

The two co-winning approaches represent distinct paradigms for incorporating systematics. HEPHY's GOLLUM builds an unbinned surrogate of the extended likelihood: a multiclass classifier (calibrated via iterative isotonic regression) identifies the four physics processes, while separate neural networks parameterise the relative variation of differential cross-sections under calibration-type nuisances via an exponential ansatz; nuisance parameters are then profiled with MINUIT to extract the CI. Ibrahim's CNF method trains contrastive normalizing flows with a modified loss (adding c·log p_θ^(s)(x_b)) to learn class-conditional densities that concentrate in signal-rich regions, transforms these into monotonic discrimination features, feeds them to a two-headed DNN trained on 1000 shifted mixtures, and constructs the CI via a Neyman confidence belt inverted at evaluation time. Both treat the task as regressing μ and the six nuisance parameters from unlabelled pseudo-experiment data, but differ fundamentally in how they model the likelihood surface and propagate systematic variations.

## Results

On the final evaluation (1000 trials × 100 pseudo-experiments), the top two methods achieve nearly identical quantile scores: HEPHY (GOLLUM) scores −0.582 and IBRAHIME (CNF) scores −0.576, with a bootstrap analysis confirming they cannot be reliably ranked (1st tie). The third-place method, HZUME (decision-tree aggregated features), scores −2.16, substantially lower. The HEPHY unbinned surrogate improves the expected 1σ confidence interval on signal strength by ~20% relative to a traditional binned classifier-template analysis, and reduces the impact of calibration-related nuisance parameters (ν_tes, ν_jes) by up to 65% compared to the binned case.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's main comparison is among three finalist methods on the FAIR Universe HiggsML Uncertainty benchmark. HEPHY's GOLLUM method (simulation-based inference with a calibrated multiclassifier and parametric regressors for systematics) and IBRAHIME's Contrastive Normalizing Flows approach achieve statistically indistinguishable final quantile scores (−0.582 vs. −0.576), with the paper explicitly noting their results are 'not very correlated, which implies the optimum has not been reached yet.' Both substantially outperform HZUME's decision-tree-based hybrid (−2.16), which uses a much simpler two-stage aggregation-and-estimation pipeline. The two winning methods differ fundamentally in approach: GOLLUM constructs a full unbinned extended likelihood surrogate profiled with MINUIT, while the CNF method learns class-conditional densities via contrastive normalizing flows and inverts a Neyman confidence belt. HEPHY additionally reports a ~20% CI-width improvement over a standard binned template analysis and up to 65% reduction in calibration nuisance parameter impact, establishing a concrete advantage of the unbinned, ML-augmented likelihood over traditional HEP analysis workflows.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 2014 HiggsML challenge (which focused on binary classification of Higgs events) by shifting the task from point estimation to confidence-interval estimation under parameterized systematic biases, and by scaling the dataset from under 1 million to 280 million events. It builds on a broad literature of uncertainty-aware ML in HEP—including nuisance-parameter-aware training, adversarial decorrelation, Bayesian neural networks, and simulation-based inference—but provides the first common benchmark with a unified metric for comparing these approaches. The winning methods draw on distinct lineages: GOLLUM (HEPHY) extends simulation-based inference with calibrated multiclassifiers and parametric systematics regressors, while the CNF approach (ibrahime) repurposes contrastive normalizing flows from anomaly detection as nuisance-robust discrimination functions feeding a maximum-likelihood estimator.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The permanent 280M-event dataset with a biasing script and a well-defined quantile-score metric gives the HEP-ML community a long-term benchmark for uncertainty quantification that was previously fragmented across ad-hoc analyses. The paper explicitly acknowledges the 'Known Unknowns' limitation (biases are exactly parameterized) and the simplifications relative to full LHC analyses (Pythia/Delphes vs. MadGraph/Geant4), leaving open the question of whether these methods transfer to truly unknown systematics. The observation that the two top-performing approaches (SBI-based and CNF-based) achieve nearly identical scores yet produce uncorrelated results suggests the optimum has not been reached, motivating further method development. Future directions include more complex (nonlinear, non-parametric) biases, morphing/optimal-transport parameterizations, and few-data regimes.
