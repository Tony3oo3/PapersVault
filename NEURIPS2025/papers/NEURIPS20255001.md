## Problem

Transformer-based LLMs require storing the full KV cache during inference, creating an O(L) memory bottleneck that limits long-context deployment. Existing KV cache reduction methods (H2O, Streaming LLM, SnapKV, etc.) rely on inflexible predefined rules or heuristics based on past attention scores to decide which tokens to evict, rather than learning token importance end-to-end from the task objective.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255001/cropped_page4_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a standard text token sequence and, at each attention layer and head, a lightweight linear Attention Score Layer (only ~13 MB for Llama-3.1-8B) maps the input embedding to a 3-way categorical distribution over token roles. The output is a per-head, per-layer learnable sparse attention mask that determines which tokens remain in the KV cache, alongside the standard next-token prediction. Token roles are assigned independently per head and per layer, so the same token can be Global in one head and Sliding Window in another.

## Method

NAtS defines a search space of three token types—Global (preserved indefinitely), Local (survives until the next Global Token), and Sliding Window (survives a fixed W steps)—and uses the Gumbel-Softmax trick to make the discrete role assignment differentiable. A multiplicative attention mask is constructed from the sampled roles, and the mask gradients are backpropagated through the Attention Score Layer in a single forward/backward pass, jointly optimizing token roles and model weights without bilevel optimization. A sparsity regularization λ applied to Global and Local token gradients encourages the model to prefer Sliding Window tokens, controlling the KV cache budget. The approach is integrated into FlashAttention to avoid explicit mask materialization, and can be applied both when training a transformer from scratch and when fine-tuning an existing LLM (where only the Attention Score Layer is trained via a distillation loss against full-attention outputs).

## Results

On RULER with Llama-3.1-8B, NAtS achieves near-lossless performance at 50% KV budget (e.g., 95.32 vs. 95.06 full attention at 4k; 84.09 vs. 84.01 at 64k) while using only 41–51% of the KV cache. At the more aggressive 25% budget, NAtS still scores 93.93 at 4k and 64.26 at 128k, whereas H2O collapses to 0.00 and MoA drops to 13.40. On LongBench (25% budget), NAtS achieves the best or near-best score on 15 of 21 tasks, e.g., 61.18 on MultiFieldQA-zh (vs. 60.01 full) and 91.61 on TriviaQA (vs. 91.15 full). In the from-scratch PG-19 experiment, NAtS with the most aggressive sparsity (≈3% KV cache) outperforms H2O at 12.5% KV budget.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NAtS outperforms all nine baselines across every context length on RULER. Against the closest competitor DuoAttention, NAtS leads by 1.1 points at 4k (50% budget) but the gap widens dramatically at 25% budget: 93.93 vs. 73.18 at 4k and 64.26 vs. 53.05 at 128k. H2O, which relies on top-k attention scores, collapses to 0.00 at 128k (both budgets) and 0.07 at 64k, while NAtS retains 74.77 and 84.09 respectively. MoA, which searches at the head level rather than token level, degrades severely at longer contexts (11.05 at 128k, 50% budget) compared to NAtS's 74.77. Among the one-time eviction methods (SnapKV, AdaKV, ChunkKV, CriticalKV, PyramidKV), CriticalKV is the strongest baseline but still trails NAtS by 12–30 points at 25% budget. On LongBench (25% budget), NAtS achieves the best score on 15 of 21 tasks, including exceeding full-attention performance on MultiFieldQA-zh (61.18 vs. 60.01), TriviaQA (91.61 vs. 91.15), and SAMSum (44.21 vs. 43.72), while using only 13–38% of the KV cache.

## Relation to Prior Work

NAtS draws its core mechanism from GDAS's Gumbel-Softmax relaxation of discrete architecture choices, repurposing it to select per-token roles (Global, Local, Sliding Window) rather than layer-level operations. The multiplicative attention mask formulation is adapted from DynamicViT's token sparsification. Conceptually, the paper departs from the dominant KV-cache-eviction paradigm (H2O, SnapKV, PyramidKV, etc.) that relies on post-hoc heuristics over past attention scores, and from head-level sparse-attention search (MoA, DuoAttention) by pushing the decision granularity down to individual tokens and jointly optimizing those decisions with model weights in a single forward-backward pass, analogous to one-shot NAS.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: NAtS achieves near-lossless performance at 25% KV budget on RULER and LongBench while enabling 3.5× longer context (700k tokens) on a single H100, and the learned Attention Score Layer adds only ~13MB of parameters, making it trivially deployable as a plug-in for existing LLMs. The main limitation acknowledged by the authors is that the mask construction is column-wise (vertical sparsity only), excluding block-sparse and slash patterns that capture Q-dependent structure; extending the search space to more structured or hierarchical patterns is identified as key future work. An open question is whether the token-level NAS framing generalizes to multimodal or non-causal settings where the Global/Local/Sliding-Window taxonomy may not suffice.
