## Problem

While feedforward neural networks have been analyzed for nonparametric quantile regression, recurrent neural networks—which are naturally suited to sequential and dependent data—lack theoretical guarantees in this setting. No prior work established approximation error bounds for RNNs or sparse RNNs under compositional function classes, nor convergence rates for RNN-based quantile regression estimators under weakly dependent (β-mixing) observations.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sequence of N covariate vectors (x_{t-N+1}, …, x_t) ∈ R^{d_x × N} and a target quantile level τ ∈ (0,1), and outputs the conditional τ-th quantile estimate f̂(x_{t-N+1}, …, x_t) ∈ R. The RNN processes the sequence sequentially through L recurrent layers with width W, and the prediction is the final element of the output sequence (a many-to-one architecture). Training data consists of n stationary β-mixing observations, from which overlapping subsequences of length N are formed for empirical risk minimization.

## Method

The paper constructs RNN and SRNN estimators via empirical risk minimization of the check loss over overlapping subsequences, and proves they achieve the minimax-optimal rate n^{-2γ*/(2γ*+1)} (up to log factors) under stationary exponential β-mixing, where γ* is the intrinsic smoothness of the hierarchical interaction model. The key technical contributions are: (i) novel approximation error bounds for RNNs and SRNNs on hierarchical interaction model functions, leveraging the equivalence between sparse feedforward and sparse recurrent architectures; (ii) oracle inequalities for QR under dependence, established through a donut-shaped decomposition of the error probability, a refined blocking technique that retains all observations (unlike prior squared-loss analyses), and a sharp empirical process inequality built on Bousquet's entropy method; and (iii) a slower but still near-optimal rate under algebraic β-mixing. The SRNN variant additionally controls overparameterization via a sparsity budget s on the total number of nonzero parameters across all recurrent, input, and output layers.

## Results

Theoretically, RNN- and SRNN-based ERM estimators achieve the minimax-optimal convergence rate of n^{-2γ*/(2γ*+1)} under the squared L₂ norm (up to a logarithmic factor) for stationary exponentially β-mixing data, where γ* is the intrinsic smoothness of the hierarchical interaction model. On the DJIA 30-day-ahead forecasting task, the SRNN achieves the lowest mean empirical check loss at all five quantile levels (e.g., 0.305 at τ=0.9 vs. 0.365 for QRF and 0.404 for FNN), while the RNN is nearly identical (0.306 at τ=0.9). In simulation studies on two nonlinear AR models with both Gaussian and heavy-tailed (t_{2.25}) noise, RNN and SRNN estimators show significantly lower MSE variance and superior overall performance compared to QRF and FNN baselines across τ=0.1 and τ=0.5.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares RNN and SRNN estimators against two baselines: Quantile Regression Forests (QRF, Meinshausen 2006) and a 2-hidden-layer ReLU FNN (Padilla et al. 2022, widths W₁=W₂=200). On the DJIA task, both RNN and SRNN consistently outperform QRF and FNN at every quantile level; for example, at τ=0.9 the SRNN achieves a check loss of 0.305 versus 0.365 (QRF) and 0.404 (FNN), a 16–25% relative improvement. At the median (τ=0.5), the SRNN records 0.759 compared to 0.817 (QRF) and 0.810 (FNN). In the simulation experiments (Model 1 and Model 2, both noise types), the RNN and SRNN box plots show markedly lower MSE variance and lower medians than both QRF and FNN, with the advantage being most pronounced under heavy-tailed t_{2.25} noise. The SRNN matches the RNN's accuracy while using only 60% of the parameters (40% pruned), confirming that sparsity does not degrade predictive performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RNN approximation theory of Jiao et al. [2024] from nonparametric regression (squared loss) to nonparametric quantile regression (check loss), and from Hölder smoothness assumptions to the hierarchical interaction model of Kohler and Langer [2021]. The FNN-to-RNN equivalence (Lemma 2) and the SFNN approximation machinery (adapted from Schmidt-Hieber [2020] and Fan et al. [2024]) are directly reused, but the QR-specific analysis—requiring a novel donut-shaped decomposition, a refined blocking technique that retains all observations, and a sharper empirical process inequality—is entirely new. The paper thus occupies the intersection of two previously separate threads: RNN theory under dependent data and nonparametric QR with neural networks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to establish minimax-optimal convergence rates (up to logarithmic factors) for RNN- and SRNN-based quantile regression estimators under stationary β-mixing dependence, filling a gap between the FNN-based QR literature (which assumes independent covariates) and the RNN regression literature (which uses squared loss). The hierarchical interaction model assumption is crucial for avoiding the curse of dimensionality in high-dimensional sequential settings, making the results practically relevant for time series applications like financial forecasting. Open questions include extending to the full quantile process (all τ simultaneously), handling nonstationary data, and investigating whether the logarithmic factors can be removed.
