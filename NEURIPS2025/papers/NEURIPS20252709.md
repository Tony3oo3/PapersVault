## Problem

Deep RL evaluation still relies primarily on reward curves and aggregate performance, offering limited insight into how agents actually solve structured, partially observable tasks. Mature behavioral analysis tools from neuroscience and ethology—GLMs, path analysis, neural decoding—remain largely unused in DRL, leaving agent strategies, memory, and planning invisible. This paper asks whether model-free RNN agents can exhibit structured, planning-like behavior purely through emergent recurrent dynamics, without explicit world models or symbolic memory.

## Input / Output

At each timestep the agent receives a 9×11 egocentric grid view centered on its position within a 96×96 procedurally generated arena, plus a scalar inventory vector (health, food, drink, energy); the 512-dim GRU hidden state is logged alongside all behavioral and environmental variables for downstream analysis. The agent outputs discrete actions (move, eat, drink, sleep, craft, attack), while the analysis pipeline produces decoded allocentric positions at past/future offsets (via ridge regression on hidden states), GLM coefficients quantifying multi-objective revisitation decisions, unsupervised movement-state segmentations, and spatial entropy metrics. Reward is survival-based—positive only when all four physiological variables exceed half their maximum—rather than task-completion, and arenas are regenerated per episode to enforce generalization.

## Method

The core architecture is PPO with a single-layer 512-unit GRU recurrent core (~6.5M parameters), trained for 3 billion timesteps across 1024 parallel ForageWorld environments. An optional auxiliary head shares the GRU hidden state to predict the agent's allocentric position via L2 regression (weighted by W_aux=0.025), and magnitude-based pruning to 90% sparsity is applied mid-training via JaxPruner to approximate biological connectivity. The key finding is that these model-free agents develop allocentric position encoding decodable up to 50–100 timesteps into the past and future, multi-objective patch revisitation (integrating recency, predator history, cow count, and position-prediction uncertainty), and phase-structured exploration-to-revisitation transitions—all without explicit world models. The analysis framework combines GLMs for decision decomposition, ridge-regression decoders for spatial representation, unsupervised clustering (bayesmove) for behavioral phase segmentation, and spatial entropy metrics, forming a reusable neuroethology-inspired toolkit for probing memory, planning, and internal representations in DRL agents.

## Results

Model-free PPO-GRU agents in ForageWorld exhibit structured, planning-like behavior: allocentric position is decoded from RNN hidden states up to 50–100 timesteps into both the past and future (above chance baseline), with ~100/512 neurons showing position sensitivity at the single-neuron level. GLM analysis of patch revisitation decisions reveals a multi-objective strategy: agents strongly prefer recently visited patches (Recency, coef = −0.598, p < 0.001) and patches with more observed cows (CowCount, coef = +0.531, p < 0.001), while avoiding patches with more predator encounters (PredRate, coef = −0.093, p < 0.001) and patches with higher prior position-prediction uncertainty (Uncertainty, coef = +0.095, p < 0.001). Expert agents forage at a mean eat rate of 0.011 (~once per 95 steps), drink rate of 0.034, and sleep rate of 0.262, reflecting emergent multi-objective pacing rather than rapid resource depletion.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary architectural comparison is PPO-GRU (the paper's baseline) versus PQN-LSTM (an off-policy parallelized Q-network). Both achieve comparable survival performance and similar learning histories and pathing, but they converge to different predator strategies: PPO-GRU develops a predator-fighting strategy (crafting swords, reducing predator HP to 0), while PQN-LSTM converges to predator evasion with much lower rates of predator killing. Ablations confirm that recurrence is essential: replacing the 512-unit GRU with a feedforward network or downsizing to 64 units significantly impairs survival, and memoryless agents become trapped in local loops. Removing the auxiliary path-integration loss degrades performance in large arenas and eliminates above-chance allocentric decoding, while 90% magnitude pruning leaves performance unchanged but improves spatial decoding accuracy. Front-facing FOV agents explore farther earlier in training but show no final performance difference from the baseline 360° view.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Craftax open-ended RL benchmark with foraging-specific ecological structure (depleting resource patches, intermittent predators, survival-based rewards) to create ForageWorld, while its primary conceptual contribution is importing neuroethology and neuroscience analysis tools—GLMs, behavioral phase segmentation, path analysis, and recurrent state decoding—into DRL evaluation, a domain that has historically relied almost exclusively on reward curves. It positions itself against the growing body of work on implicit planning in model-free RNNs (Ni et al., Chung et al., Bush et al.) by providing the first systematic behavioral-neural analysis of such agents in a complex, naturalistic, partially observable environment rather than a simplified POMDP. The analytical framework (Table 1) draws on methods long established in animal neuroscience (Krakauer et al., Niv) but previously underused in ML interpretability, which has focused predominantly on representational similarity metrics rather than joint behavioral-neural analysis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a reusable, neuroethology-inspired diagnostic framework for probing memory, planning, exploration, and internal representations in DRL agents, addressing a genuine gap in how the field evaluates and interprets agent behavior beyond aggregate performance. The finding that model-free GRU agents develop allocentric spatial encoding and multi-objective revisitation strategies without explicit world models challenges a common assumption in both RL and neuroscience, though the evidence is somewhat indirect (linear decoding from hidden states rather than explicit planning demonstrations). Key limitations include the 96×96 grid ceiling, focus on a single architecture family (PPO-GRU with one PQN-LSTM comparison), and the absence of multi-agent or language-based settings. Open questions include whether these findings scale to larger arenas, whether the allocentric encoding is truly 'planning' or merely path integration, and how the framework generalizes to transformer-based or LLM agents.
