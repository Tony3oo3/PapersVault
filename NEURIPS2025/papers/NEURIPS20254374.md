## Problem

Existing signed network embedding methods rely on node-centric GNN frameworks with balance theory, which either limit representational power by only capturing pairwise node relationships or incur severe computational costs from neural network training. In particular, balance theory cannot distinguish edges that are isomorphic at the node level but differ in higher-order simplicial structures (e.g., an edge in two triangles vs. one triangle), and integrating simplicial complexes into GNNs demands massive parameter training. The paper addresses this by asking whether higher-order signed network structure can be captured efficiently without any learning process.

## Input / Output

The method takes a signed network g=(V, E⁺∪E⁻) with positive and negative edges, an embedding dimension D (default 300), and a number of iterations R (default 1), and produces D-dimensional binary vectors for each edge. Unlike node embedding methods that derive edge representations by concatenating endpoint vectors, EdgeSketch+ directly sketches each edge as a binary vector. The binary output can be expanded to 2D dimensions via negation-and-concatenation (e.g., [1,0,1,0,0] → [1,0,1,0,0,0,1,0,1,1]/√5), enabling direct use with linear classifiers like logistic regression and avoiding the need to store large precomputed kernel matrices for SVM.

## Method

EdgeSketch+ extracts all 0-, 1-, and 2-simplexes from the signed network, constructs the boundary adjacency matrices B₁ (node-edge) and B₂ (edge-triangle), and the 1-dimensional Hodge Laplacian L₁, then iteratively applies convolution operations on these matrices to propagate information across the edge surrounding (the edge itself plus its adjacent edges, nodes, and triangles). At each iteration, the edge surrounding is projected into D random hyperplanes via LSH: the algorithm aggregates signed projections from all four neighborhood types (self via L₁, boundary-adjacent nodes via B₁, co-boundary-adjacent triangles via B₂, and L₁-adjacent edges) and thresholds the sum to produce a binary bit. The random vectors are generated offline once and shared globally, so no gradient-based training is needed. Theoretically, the paper proves EdgeSketch+ belongs to an LSH family, produces a positive-definite hash kernel, and satisfies a Hoeffding concentration bound on the kernel-similarity gap, with total time complexity O(N₁² + ν̄·N₁·D²·R).

## Results

EdgeSketch+ achieves the best Binary-F1, Accuracy, and Macro-F1 across all four signed network datasets (e.g., 0.9780 Binary-F1 and 0.9581 Accuracy on Bitcoin-α; 0.9659 Binary-F1 and 0.9405 Accuracy on Epinions), while its AUC trails the top GNN baselines by an average of only 0.0253. The method is dramatically faster than all competitors, completing Bitcoin-α in 4.03 s versus 2204.68 s for SGCL (a 546.07× speedup), and is the only method that avoids OOM/OOT on the large Slashdot and Epinions datasets. On Epinions, EdgeSketch+ runs in 273.24 s while SiGAT requires 30,651.82 s and SGCL times out entirely.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EdgeSketch+ outperforms all six signed-network GNN baselines (RSGNN, SDGNN, SNEA, SGCL, SiGAT, GSGNN+SGA) on Binary-F1, Accuracy, and Macro-F1 across every dataset, while SGCL retains a slight AUC edge on Bitcoin-α (0.9101 vs. 0.8829) and Bitcoin-OTC (0.9152 vs. 0.8695), and SiGAT edges out on Epinions AUC (0.9248 vs. 0.9217). Against the three edge-embedding baselines, EdgeSketch+ dominates: EdgeRWSE achieves the worst AUC (0.5314 on both Bitcoin datasets, essentially random), TER and edge2vec trail by 0.05–0.30 in AUC, and all three are 10–500× slower. The closest LSH-based competitor, MPSketch, is consistently outperformed by EdgeSketch+ on every metric (e.g., 0.9780 vs. 0.9705 Binary-F1 on Bitcoin-α) and is 30× slower on Bitcoin-α (121.23 s vs. 4.03 s). Critically, EdgeSketch+ is the only method that completes within memory and time limits on both Slashdot and Epinions, where most GNN baselines hit OOM or OOT, underscoring its practical advantage at scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

EdgeSketch+ sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) signed network embedding via GNNs and balance theory (RSGNN, SDGNN, SGCL, SiGAT, GSGNN+SGA), which are node-centric and require expensive parameter training; (2) simplicial-complex-augmented GNNs (MPSN, SaNN, SCN, EdgeRWSE), which capture higher-order interactions but inherit the computational cost of neural training; and (3) LSH-based graph hashing (MPSketch, SCHash), which avoids learning but has been limited to node-level or whole-graph representations. The paper's key departure is making the first edge-centric, no-learning sketching method that explicitly leverages the Hodge Laplacian and boundary/co-boundary matrices of a simplicial complex, thereby combining the representational power of higher-order topology with the efficiency of randomized hashing in a signed-network setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 546× speedup over GNN-based signed network embedders while matching their accuracy makes large-scale signed network analysis (e.g., Slashdot, Epinions) tractable on commodity hardware without GPUs, which is practically significant for the era of big data. The theoretical guarantees (LSH sensitivity, positive-definite hash kernel, Hoeffding concentration bound) provide a rigorous foundation that purely empirical GNN approaches lack. Two limitations remain open: the framework currently caps at 2-simplices (triangles), potentially missing higher-order interactions, and the unsupervised sketching still depends on a downstream classifier requiring labeled edges, limiting applicability in low-label regimes. Extending to k-simplices for k > 2 and exploring fully unsupervised sign prediction are natural next steps.
