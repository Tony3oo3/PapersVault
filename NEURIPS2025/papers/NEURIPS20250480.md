## Problem

Existing instance-wise occlusion and depth order prediction methods (e.g., PCNet-M, OrderNet, InstaOrderNet) require binary segmentation masks as input and perform pairwise edge-level inference, necessitating a quadratic number of forward passes to recover the full ordering graph. This makes them impractical for real-world deployment where masks are unavailable and scenes contain many instances. The paper asks whether the full geometric ordering can be predicted holistically—i.e., the entire adjacency matrix in a single forward pass—from a bare RGB image alone.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (resized to 1024×1024 for training, 800×1333 for evaluation) with no auxiliary inputs such as masks, category labels, or text prompts. It outputs the full n×n occlusion order matrix (binary: occludes/does-not-occlude) and n×n depth order matrix (3-class: in front, behind, overlapping depth) for all detected instances simultaneously, along with the instance segmentation masks themselves. The number of instances n is determined at inference time by a confidence threshold (0.8) on segmentation queries, and the inference cost remains constant regardless of n.

## Method

InstaFormer couples a frozen Mask2Former segmentation backbone with a trainable transformer-based geometrical ordering module. The backbone produces mask embeddings Q, per-pixel embeddings P, and binary masks M; the ordering module then constructs per-instance latent mask descriptors D by masking P with M, applying a single transformer layer with Masked Self-Attention (restricting each instance's attention to its own region), and max-pooling. The core insight is that Q (carrying geometric/shape knowledge) and D (carrying spatially-grounded information) represent the same objects with complementary information; they are concatenated and passed through L transformer layers to create cross-type interactions. The final ordering matrix is computed as a dot product between MLP-projected Q and D, followed by task-specific MLP heads for occlusion (BCE loss) and depth (CE loss). Adapters are attached to the FFNs of the frozen backbone to enable parameter-efficient adaptation, and the entire system is trained end-to-end on INSTAORDER.

## Results

On INSTAORDER, InstaFormer^o achieves the best occlusion F1 of 81.89, surpassing the strongest pairwise baseline InstaOrderNet^{o,d} (F1=81.86) while requiring only an RGB image instead of ground-truth binary masks. For depth order, InstaFormer^{o,d} achieves the best WHDR (All) of 13.30, substantially outperforming InstaOrderNet^{o,d} (15.99) and all foundation-model pipelines (22.36–29.85). The joint InstaFormer^{o,d} model performs competitively on both tasks simultaneously (F1=81.37, WHDR All=13.30) in a single forward pass, whereas pairwise methods require O(n²) forward passes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

InstaFormer^o (F1=81.89) outperforms all pairwise occlusion baselines that require ground-truth masks, including InstaOrderNet^o (80.65), OrderNet M+I (77.51), and PCNet-M (63.02), while operating solely from an RGB image. For depth order, InstaFormer^{o,d} (WHRD All=13.30) beats InstaOrderNet^{o,d} (15.99) by 2.7 points, MiDaS (20.92) by 7.6 points, and the best foundation-model pipeline (22.36) by 9.1 points. Against the VLM baseline LLaVA, InstaFormer dominates by a wide margin: zero-shot LLaVA scores F1=34.10 and WHDR All=39.52, and even finetuned LLaVA† (F1=60.41, WHDR All=25.98) trails InstaFormer by over 20 F1 points and 12 WHDR points. The Mask2Order baseline (which concatenates Mask2Former with a pairwise order head) achieves F1=77.17 and WHDR All=17.09, confirming that self-generated masks degrade pairwise prediction quality relative to InstaFormer's holistic single-pass approach. Notably, InstaFormer^{o,d} slightly underperforms InstaFormer^o on occlusion F1 (81.37 vs 81.89), suggesting joint optimization is marginally harder, yet it still matches or exceeds all pairwise methods on both tasks simultaneously.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the pairwise paradigm established by PCNet-M, OrderNet, and InstaOrderNet, which predict occlusion/depth order one edge at a time and require binary masks as input. It reformulates the problem as a single adjacency-matrix-level prediction ("holistic order prediction") and builds on Mask2Former's object-query architecture, introducing an interaction between object queries and latent mask descriptors via masked self-attention to produce the full order matrix in one forward pass. The approach also positions itself against foundation-model pipelines (Grounded SAM + Depth Anything V2) and VLMs (LLaVA), showing that a compact 34M-parameter specialist can outperform both on this task.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By reducing inference from O(n²) forward passes to a single pass and eliminating the need for input binary masks, InstaFormer makes instance-wise geometric reasoning practical for real-time and in-the-wild settings (e.g., autonomous driving, robot perception). The finding that depth order prediction is largely insensitive to segmentation backbone quality—plateauing around 13.7 WHDR regardless of Swin size—suggests the task may be easier than previously assumed and could be tackled with lighter models. Key limitations include evaluation on a single dataset capped at 10 instances per image, and the authors note that extending to VLMs and layer-aware generative modeling remains open.
