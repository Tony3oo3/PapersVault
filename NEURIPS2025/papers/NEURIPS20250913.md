## Problem

Prior probing studies have shown that large language models develop a low-rank linear subspace separating true from false statements, and that interventions along this direction can steer outputs toward factual completions. However, the mechanism by which such a truth subspace emerges during training, and how it is computed at inference time, remained unexplained. This paper asks: what minimal data-generating conditions force an LM to represent truth, and what circuit computes the linear signal?

## Input / Output

The model consumes 4-token sequences of the form x y x' y' (two subject-attribute pairs) where the truthfulness of the first pair is correlated with the second via a latent Bernoulli variable T. The key output is a norm-based linear separator in the post-layer-norm hidden state at the x' position: true sequences produce smaller pre-norm norms (due to cancellation of key-value terms), which layer norm converts into a linearly separable signal. Additionally, the model produces sharpened (spiked) softmax distributions on true sequences and flattened distributions on false sequences, with the probability of the correct attribute on the second pair declining when the first pair is false.

## Method

The paper introduces a one-layer transformer with one-hot embeddings, uniform causal attention, a single value matrix W, and layer normalization (no MLP, no learned keys/queries). The core insight is the Truth Co-occurrence Hypothesis: when true statements statistically co-occur, inferring a latent truth bit T reduces language-modeling loss by H₂(ρ) bits. The mechanism works in two phases: first, gradient descent rapidly builds a key-value memory (W maps e_x → u_{g(x)}), then—over a longer horizon—the contrast between the memorized prediction and the observed attribute creates a norm difference (‖ζ(x,g(x))‖ < ‖ζ(x,y)‖ for y≠g(x)) that layer norm amplifies into a linearly separable truth direction. The paper proves (Theorem 1) that this structure sharpens predictions on true sequences via inverse-norm scaling in the softmax, proves (Theorem 2) that layer norm is necessary for linear separability, and provides a gradient analysis (Theorem 3) showing the block structure in W emerges from just two steps on L₁ followed by one on L₃. Experiments on synthetic data, natural-language CounterFact data, and LLAMA3-8B confirm the two-phase dynamics and the sensitivity of predictions to preceding false context.

## Results

On the MAVEN-FACT corpus (71,274 certain-labeled mentions across 2,913 articles), the pairwise certain-false co-occurrence rate is 0.0009, roughly 2× the independence baseline (p² = 0.00044), with a clustering ratio of 1.23 and χ² = 4.17×10³ (p ≈ 9×10⁻⁴⁹), confirming the Truth Co-occurrence Hypothesis. In the synthetic 1-layer model (|S|=|A|=512, d=256, ρ=0.99), memorization completes within ~1,000 batches while linear truth encoding emerges abruptly after ~7,500 batches (~1M examples). On LLAMA3-8B (CounterFact, SPEAKSLANGUAGE, 128 n-tuples), preceding a sentence with two false statements reduces the probability of the correct attribute by 4.55× (ΔNLL = 1.52) relative to a true context, and steering along the truth subspace (layer 11, α=3.0) reverses this effect, with a linear classifier reaching >95% accuracy on middle and last layers.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a mechanistic interpretability paper without traditional method-vs-baseline comparisons; the 'compares_to' set is empty. The key empirical contrasts are: (i) in the synthetic setting, the model's P[g(x′)] on false sequences drops sharply once the linear truth encoding emerges (~7,500 batches), while it remains near 1 during the memorization phase, showing the truth signal is distinct from mere key-value recall; (ii) on LLAMA3-8B, the no-intervention condition with false context (FF) yields a 4.55× lower probability of the correct attribute than the true-context condition (TT), and adding the steering vector α(μ_T − μ_F) at layer 11 (α=3.0) restores the probability, confirming the truth subspace is causally relevant; (iii) the 1-layer attention-only model exhibits epoch-wise double descent in truth-classification AUC (rises, dips, rises again), a pattern not seen in deeper models, suggesting the single-layer architecture forces a specific two-phase learning trajectory. The paper also contrasts its lexicon-independent mechanism against prior persona-based accounts (Joshi et al., 2024; Li et al., 2023a), which rely on surface lexical cues correlating with truthfulness, whereas the TCH requires no such cues.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the key-value associative memory framework of Geva et al. (2021) and the persona-based accounts of factual inconsistency (Li et al., 2023a; Joshi et al., 2024) into a unified mechanistic explanation for the linear truth subspaces identified by probing studies (Azaria and Mitchell, 2023; Burns et al., 2022; Marks and Tegmark, 2024; Bürger et al., 2025). While prior work established that truth-encoding directions exist and are behaviorally relevant, the mechanism of their emergence remained unexplained. The paper's Truth Co-occurrence Hypothesis quantifies the persona hypothesis in a provable form, and the toy model departs from Joshi et al. (2024) by requiring no lexical or stylistic cues to distinguish true from false assertions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first minimal, end-to-end mechanistic account of why and how linear truth subspaces emerge during training, with a clean two-phase dynamic (rapid key-value memorization followed by slower truth-encoding emergence) and a provable role for layer normalization in creating the separating signal. The TCH is validated on real news data (MAVEN-FACT) and its predictions are confirmed in LLAMA3-8B, lending credibility beyond the toy setting. Key limitations include the single-relation, no-MLP, uniform-attention architecture, and the assumption of uniform corruption for false variants; extending to multi-relation settings with logical dependencies (transitivity, mutual exclusivity) and to learned attention patterns remains open. The finding that truth encoding can emerge even when ρ=1 (all-true training) suggests a form of out-of-distribution generalization that may not hold in more expressive models where attention can collapse to self-attention.
