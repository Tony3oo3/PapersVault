## Problem

Most hierarchical representation learning methods treat only one data mode (samples or features) at a time, yet real-world datasets such as word-document matrices and single-cell RNA-seq data exhibit hierarchical structure in both modes simultaneously. This paper asks whether the hierarchy in one mode can be leveraged to improve the discovery of hierarchy in the other, and proposes a joint, unsupervised framework to do so.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255781/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a non-negative data matrix X ∈ R^{n×m} along with initial pairwise distance matrices M_r (n×n) and M_c (m×m) for the sample and feature modes, plus regularization parameters γ_r, γ_c > 0. Rows and columns are L1-normalized into discrete histograms before TWD computation, and a snowflake-penalty regularizer is added to each pairwise distance. The outputs are a sample tree T(W_r) with n leaves, a feature tree T(W_c) with m leaves, and the converged TWD matrices W_r and W_c, which can be directly used for kNN classification, sparse Haar approximation, or as a preprocessing step to initialize hyperbolic graph convolutional networks.

## Method

The core mechanism is an alternating coordinate-descent scheme: at each iteration, the current feature TWD matrix is used to construct a new feature tree via hyperbolic diffusion geometry (ref. [37]), which then serves as the hierarchical ground metric to compute the updated sample TWD, and vice versa. This departs from standard TWD usage where trees are built for computational efficiency in approximating Euclidean ground metrics; here, trees are learned to reflect the intrinsic hierarchical structure of the data. An additional Haar wavelet filtering step (Alg. 2) is applied at every iteration: each sample is treated as a signal on the feature tree (and each feature as a signal on the sample tree), and only the top-magnitude Haar coefficients (selected by cumulative L1 norm threshold) are retained to suppress noise before the next tree update. Theoretical guarantees (Theorems 1 and 2) establish that both the plain and wavelet-filtered iterative schemes converge to a fixed point under γ_r, γ_c > 0, with per-iteration cost O(n^{1.2} + m^{1.2}).

## Results

On document and single-cell classification (Table 2), Alg. 2 achieves 97.3% on BBCSPORT, 76.7% on TWITTER, 97.6% on CLASSIC, 94.2% on AMAZON, 94.0% on ZEISEL, and 93.3% on CBMC, outperforming the best baseline (Co-SWQTWD or Co-Flowtree) by 1.1–10.6 percentage points across all six datasets. For sparse approximation (Table 1), Alg. 2 achieves the lowest L1 norm of Haar coefficients on all datasets (e.g., 10.1/4.8 on BBCSPORT vs. 22.5/22.1 for the best baseline Co-UltraFit-TWD). When integrated into HGCNs (Table 3), HGCN-Alg.2 achieves 98.4 ROC AUC on DISEASE link prediction and 92.1% accuracy on AIRPORT node classification, surpassing H2H-GCN (97.0 / 89.3) and standard HGCNs (90.8 / 90.6).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against 18 baselines spanning TWD variants (Quadtree, Flowtree, TSWD, UltraTree, WCTWD/WQTWD, sliced variants, MST), hyperbolic clustering methods (HHC, gHHC, UltraFit, TR), co-manifold learning (QUE), and unsupervised ground metric learning (WSV, Tree-WSV), all adapted to the iterative cross-mode framework with a 'Co-' prefix. Alg. 2 wins on all six classification datasets by a substantial margin: the largest gaps are on ZEISEL (+10.6 pp over Co-UltraTree, the best scRNA-seq baseline) and CBMC (+9.3 pp over Co-Flowtree), while on document data the margins are 1.1–4.3 pp over the strongest baselines (Co-SWQTWD, Co-Flowtree, Co-Quadtree). For sparse approximation (Table 1), Alg. 2 achieves the lowest L1 Haar norm on every dataset, with particularly large improvements on BBCSPORT (10.1 vs. 22.5 for Co-UltraFit-TWD) and TWITTER (22.4 vs. 51.5 for Co-HHC-TWD). In the HGCN integration (Table 3), HGCN-Alg.2 outperforms H2H-GCN on DISEASE LP (98.4 vs. 97.0), AIRPORT NC (92.1 vs. 89.3), PUBMED NC (83.6 vs. 79.9), and CORA LP (96.9 vs. 95.0), while H2H-GCN retains a slight edge on AIRPORT LP (96.4 vs. 97.2) and CORA NC (82.8 vs. 83.9 is actually won by HGCN-Alg.2). The wavelet filtering step (Alg. 2 vs. Alg. 1) consistently adds 0.5–4 pp in classification and 1–3 pp in L1 norm reduction, confirming its contribution to hierarchical representation quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes Tree-Wasserstein Distance (TWD) from its standard role as a computationally efficient Wasserstein approximation into a mechanism for jointly learning hierarchical representations across both data modes (samples and features). It builds on the tree construction method of Lin et al. [37], which derives trees from hyperbolic diffusion embeddings, and on adaptive Haar wavelets on trees [43], but departs from prior TWD work by using the tree of one mode as the ground metric for the other mode in an alternating coordinate-descent scheme. The closest prior work—WSV [59] and Tree-WSV [60]—also alternates between modes but does not explicitly learn hierarchical tree representations, while co-manifold learning [52–57] handles both modes but relies on Riemannian geometry with non-negative curvature, ill-suited to the negative-curvature structure of hierarchical data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first principled framework for jointly discovering hierarchies in both rows and columns of a data matrix, with convergence guarantees (Theorems 1–2) and a practical integration into hyperbolic GNNs that improves link prediction and node classification. The finding that Haar wavelet filtering improves results only when applied to trees from their specific construction (not to trees from other TWD methods) validates that their trees capture genuine hierarchical structure rather than merely approximating a Wasserstein ground metric. Key limitations include the assumption of underlying tree-like (negative-curvature) geometry, which may not hold for data on spherical or mixed-curvature manifolds, and the non-differentiable nature of the current TWD computation, which prevents end-to-end integration into neural architectures. Open directions include differentiable TWD variants, multi-way (tensor) data, and incorporating supervision.
