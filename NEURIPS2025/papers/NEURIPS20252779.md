## Problem

While MLLMs have achieved strong OCR accuracy on static images, their performance degrades significantly on video due to motion blur, temporal variation, and visual effects. Existing video OCR benchmarks cover only a narrow range of task types (primarily text recognition), lack diversity in video content, and neglect deeper text-based understanding and reasoning, leaving a critical gap in how we evaluate and guide the development of practical video OCR capabilities in MLLMs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252779/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each evaluation sample pairs a video clip (varying in resolution, aspect ratio, and duration across 44 scenario types) with a text question. The expected output format depends on the task: plain-text recognition (checked via containment match), a single multiple-choice letter (A–D), or a free-form translation (scored by GPT-4o for semantic equivalence). The benchmark comprises 1,464 videos and 2,000 manually annotated QA pairs, with a deliberate inclusion of vertically formatted videos to counteract the horizontal-video bias in most MLLM training data.

## Method

MME-VideoOCR is a benchmark rather than a model, so its core contribution is the construction pipeline and task taxonomy. Videos are sourced through three channels: reconstruction from existing video QA datasets (filtered via PaddleOCR text extraction and GPT-4o quality checks), manual collection from public platforms (YouTube, Bilibili, Kuaishou) to cover underrepresented scenarios, and AI-generated videos (Wan text-to-video) for controlled challenges like occluded text revealed over time. Annotation follows a two-stage manual process (3–4 QA pairs per video by annotators, 1–2 retained by a second expert) plus expert verification and a dedicated debiasing test using Qwen2.5-VL-7B with no visual input to suppress language-prior and knowledge-leakage biases. The 25 tasks across 10 categories span perception (text recognition, grounding, attribute recognition), understanding (visual text QA, special text parsing, cross-frame text understanding), and reasoning (text-based reasoning, multi-hop needle-in-a-haystack, robust video testing with AIGC/long/adversarial videos). Evaluation of 18 MLLMs reveals that even the best model (Gemini-2.5 Pro) reaches only 73.7% overall, with near-zero accuracy on trajectory recognition and strong language-prior bias causing models to 'correct' visually present misspellings into semantically plausible words.

## Results

On MME-VideoOCR (2,000 QA pairs across 25 tasks and 44 scenarios), the best-performing model Gemini-2.5 Pro achieves only 73.7% overall accuracy, while the worst (LLaVA-OneVision 7B) scores 46.0%, demonstrating strong discriminative power. Cross-Frame Text Understanding is the most challenging category, with all top-5 models scoring below 25% (Trajectory Recognition: 0% for all top-5 models; Scrambled Recognition: 4–76%). Token-compression architectures (VideoChat-Flash, Slow-fast MLLM) underperform sparse-sampling models despite strong scores on general video benchmarks, suggesting critical OCR information is lost during token merging.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among the 18 evaluated MLLMs, Gemini-2.5 Pro leads at 73.7% overall, outperforming GPT-4o (66.4%) and Gemini-1.5 Pro (64.9%) by 7.3 and 8.8 points respectively, with the largest margin on Visual Text QA (91.6% vs. 81.6% and 77.6%). Among open-source models, Qwen2.5-VL 72B (69.0%) and InternVL3 78B (67.2%) are the strongest, yet both trail Gemini-2.5 Pro by 4.7 and 6.5 points. A clear scaling trend is visible within the Qwen2.5-VL (59.1→61.0→69.0%), InternVL3 (59.8→66.1→67.2%), and Oryx-1.5 (49.6→55.2%) families. Token-compression methods (VideoChat-Flash 47.8%, Slow-fast MLLM 47.8%) underperform sparse-sampling counterparts of similar size (e.g., LLaVA-Video 52.8%, VideoLLaMA 3 53.5%) despite competitive scores on general video benchmarks like Video-MME and MLVU. The most striking gap across all models is in Cross-Frame Text Understanding: even Gemini-2.5 Pro reaches only 48.7%, while all top-5 models score 0% on Trajectory Recognition and below 35% on Scrambled Recognition (except Gemini-2.5 Pro at 76%), exposing a fundamental limitation in temporal text integration.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of OCR benchmarking for MLLMs from static images (TextVQA, OCRBench, OCRBench v2) to video, and further departs from the two preliminary video OCR benchmarks (OCR Benchmark [23], FG Bench [24]) that focused almost exclusively on text recognition. It also broadens the scope beyond scene-text video QA datasets (EgoTextVQA, RoadTextVQA, M4-ViteVQA) by incorporating understanding, reasoning, cross-frame temporal integration, and robustness testing (AIGC, adversarial, long videos) into a unified evaluation framework. The benchmark construction leverages existing video QA datasets for efficiency but supplements them with manually collected and AI-generated videos to cover underrepresented scenarios.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals a critical and consistent failure mode across all 18 evaluated MLLMs: models that perform well on single-frame or few-frame text tasks (e.g., 86–96% on Subtitle-Based Video Understanding) collapse to near-chance levels on tasks requiring cross-frame integration (e.g., 0% on Trajectory Recognition, <25% on Cross-Frame Text Understanding overall). This sharp contrast suggests that current MLLMs rely on a small number of salient frames rather than genuinely aggregating information over time, pointing to a fundamental architectural limitation in temporal reasoning. Open questions include whether longer context windows or dedicated temporal attention mechanisms can close this gap, how to mitigate the strong language prior bias that causes models to 'correct' visually present misspellings, and whether token compression strategies can be redesigned to preserve fine-grained textual detail without sacrificing efficiency.
