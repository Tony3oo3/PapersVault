## Problem

Existing hallucination mitigation methods for LVLMs intervene along a single causal path (e.g., image-to-output-text or text-to-text) and perform inconsistently across benchmarks with different question formats. No prior work has identified that LVLMs rely on distinct causal pathways depending on the alignment format (binary, multiple-choice, open-ended), nor has a unified framework been proposed to jointly intervene on all relevant paths.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254757/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a text prompt (binary question, multiple-choice question, or open-ended captioning instruction) and produces a hallucination-mitigated text response at inference time. Head identification is performed on a separate dataset that shares only the question format with the test set but uses entirely different images, ensuring the identified heads generalize. The intervention modifies attention head outputs during a single forward pass, requiring no additional model parameters or training.

## Method

AllPath proposes a multi-path intervention framework aligned with the transformer's causal architecture, identifying three causal pathways: image-to-input-text, image-to-output-text, and text-to-text. Two lightweight scoring methods identify critical heads per pathway: the T2T Score (a refined Log Probability Increase measuring each head's differential contribution to hallucinated vs. non-hallucinated token logits) and the I2T Score (the ratio of attention to semantically aligned image regions versus total image attention). A key empirical finding is that T2T heads are strongly correlated within the same alignment format but nearly uncorrelated across formats, and that image-to-input-text and image-to-output-text heads are functionally distinct. At inference, the method scales up the top-ranked I2T and T2T heads (Z+) by γ⁺=2.0 and zeros out the bottom-ranked T2T heads (Z⁻) by γ⁻=0.0 in the multi-head attention output, adaptively selecting which pathways to intervene on based on question type.

## Results

On LLaVA-v1.5-7B, AllPath achieves the best performance across all three benchmark formats simultaneously: POPE (COCO) Adversarial F1 of 82.1 (vs. 80.0 vanilla, +2.1), MCQ-POPE (COCO) Adversarial F1 of 69.7 (vs. 64.5 vanilla, +5.2), and CHAIR with Cs of 26.6 and Ci of 7.2 (vs. 52.2/14.6 vanilla, roughly halving both hallucination rates). On the MME hallucination subset, AllPath reaches a total score of 600.0 (±8.66) versus 540.0 (±39.69) for vanilla, using the same heads identified from POPE without re-extraction. The method is training-free and requires only a single forward pass for head identification, with inference-time intervention via simple per-head scaling factors (γ⁺=2.0, γ⁻=0.0).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AllPath outperforms all four baselines on every benchmark simultaneously, which no single baseline achieves. VCD [27] improves POPE F1 by up to 1.5 points but degrades CHAIR (Cs worsens from 52.2 to 58.2), confirming its text-coherence bias. ICD [61] provides only marginal gains on POPE (F1 79.5 on Adversarial, below vanilla's 80.0) and is essentially neutral on CHAIR. PAI [39] excels on CHAIR (Cs 28.8, Ci 7.9) by reinforcing image attention but offers only modest POPE improvements (F1 81.4) and does not generalize well to MCQ-POPE. AD-HH [72] similarly targets CHAIR (Ci 7.5) but leaves POPE at vanilla level (F1 79.9 on Adversarial), reflecting its text-dominant head suppression strategy. AllPath's multi-path design captures the complementary strengths: it matches or exceeds PAI/AD-HH on CHAIR while also delivering the largest gains on POPE and MCQ-POPE, demonstrating that no single causal pathway suffices for comprehensive hallucination mitigation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the intervention-based line of hallucination mitigation (PAI, AD-HH) by moving from single-path to multi-path attention head intervention, and refines the Log Probability Increase (LPI) scoring from Yu & Ananiadou (2024) for head identification. It departs from contrastive decoding methods (VCD, ICD) that calibrate output distributions, instead directly manipulating internal attention heads along three distinct causal pathways (image-to-input-text, image-to-output-text, text-to-text). The key conceptual departure is the observation that prior methods intervene along only one causal path, whereas this work shows that LVLMs route information through different pathway combinations depending on the question-answer alignment format, necessitating a unified multi-path framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that LVLMs adaptively select different causal pathways based on question format (e.g., POPE relies heavily on image-to-input-text while CHAIR relies on image-to-output-text) provides a mechanistic explanation for why single-path interventions fail across benchmarks, and offers a principled basis for format-aware hallucination mitigation. The method is training-free, requires only a single forward pass, and generalizes across model families (LLaVA, Qwen-VL, Qwen2.5-VL) and benchmarks. Limitations include the need for a separate head-identification dataset per format, the format-specific nature of the selected heads (requiring format awareness at inference), and evaluation primarily on 7B-scale models. Open questions include whether automatic pathway selection without explicit format labels is feasible, and whether the framework extends to video or longer-context settings.
