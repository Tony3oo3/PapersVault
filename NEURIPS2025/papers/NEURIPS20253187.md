## Problem

Existing recursive reasoning methods for LLMs apply uniform supervision across all iterations and often require costly pretraining, ignoring that each recursive step has different representational capacity and functional role. This misalignment over-regularizes early iterations and under-trains later ones, preventing the model from developing progressively deeper reasoning. The paper asks: can we equip pretrained LLMs with structured, depth-aware recursive reasoning through fine-tuning alone, without modifying pretraining or requiring annotated intermediate steps?

## Input / Output

The method takes a discrete text prompt, which is encoded once by the head block into an initial latent state z^(0). This state is then iteratively refined through T=3 recursive passes, each producing a new latent state z^(t) that is decoded and supervised by a capacity-matched teacher. The final output is a discrete text prediction (answer or reasoning trace) decoded from z^(T) by the tail block. The student model is Qwen2.5-0.5B with a 151,936-token vocabulary, while teachers range from Qwen2.5-1.5B to 7B (152,064 tokens), requiring logit truncation and renormalization during distillation.

## Method

SCOUT decomposes a pretrained LLM into a head block (embedding + first half of layers), a recursive block (remaining half), and a tail block (output projection), then introduces two key mechanisms. First, progressive distillation assigns each recursive step a teacher of matching capacity (1.5B→3B→7B for steps 1→2→3), optimizing per-step KL divergence losses so that early steps receive gentler guidance while later steps are pushed by stronger teachers. Second, a cross-attention retrospective module lets each step (t≥2) perform self-attention over the original latent state z^(0) while cross-attending to the previous step's output z^(t-1) as external memory, preserving the pretrained attention flow without architectural rewiring. Training is standard fine-tuning over a composite instruction dataset with the total loss as a weighted sum of per-step distillation objectives, requiring no pretraining modifications or annotated intermediate reasoning traces.

## Results

SCOUT (T=3) achieves an average accuracy of 39.03 across eight benchmarks, yielding a +1.81% improvement over standard SFT (37.21) and showing monotonic gains across iterations (+0.23 → +1.05 → +1.81). Peak individual scores include 35.45 on GSM8K, 52.35 on GLUE, and 30.20 on ARC-Challenge. The progressive distillation strategy is critical: reversing the teacher order (R-SCOUT) causes performance to collapse from +1.47 at iteration 1 to −0.42 at iteration 3, while uniform 7B supervision (R-Distill-EQ) degrades from +1.23 to +0.15.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DSFT (Wu et al., 2024), which applies AKL soft-target distillation from Qwen2.5-7B without any recursive computation, SCOUT at T=3 achieves 39.03 vs. 38.06 average (+0.97), demonstrating that recursion with progressive teacher alignment adds meaningful gains beyond single-pass distillation alone. Against R-SFT (Saunshi et al., 2025), which applies uniform hard-label supervision at each recursive step, SCOUT at T=3 reaches 39.03 vs. 37.49 (+1.54); critically, R-SFT stagnates after iteration 2 (37.73 → 37.49), while SCOUT continues to improve monotonically. The R-Distill-EQ baseline (uniform 7B teacher across all iterations) initially outperforms SCOUT at iteration 1 (+1.23 vs. +0.23) but collapses by iteration 2 (−0.04), confirming that capacity-matched progressive supervision is essential for sustained refinement. The reversed-teacher control (R-SCOUT) further validates the design: starting with the strongest teacher (7B) and weakening to 1.5B causes a sharp drop from +1.47 to −0.42, showing that over-regularizing early iterations with too-strong targets distorts latent states and impairs downstream reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the recursive/looped transformer line of work (Saunshi et al. 2025; Geiping et al. 2025) by replacing their uniform hard-label supervision across all iterations with a progressive distillation strategy that assigns capacity-matched teachers to each recursive step. It also departs from prior recursive methods' simple feed-back or concatenation mechanisms by introducing a cross-attention retrospective module that treats prior latent states as external memory while preserving the pretrained model's original attention flow. The broader framing draws on knowledge distillation (Hinton et al.; Wu et al. 2024) and Chain-of-Thought reasoning (Wei et al. 2022), but repositions recursive inference as a structured cognitive trajectory (Flow CoT) rather than black-box repetition, and achieves this through fine-tuning alone without the costly pretraining that prior recursive LLMs require.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SCOUT demonstrates that depth-aware recursive reasoning can be instilled in pretrained LLMs via lightweight fine-tuning, lowering the barrier to multi-iteration inference that previously required architecture-level pretraining. The progressive distillation principle—matching teacher strength to iteration depth—is a clean, generalizable insight that could inform other iterative training settings. However, the empirical gains are modest (≤1.8% over SFT), validated only on the Qwen2.5-0.5B model across a fixed T=3 schedule, and the manually selected teacher hierarchy and fixed iteration count limit practical adaptability. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger students, whether dynamic/adaptive iteration control can replace the fixed schedule, and whether the cross-attention retrospective module generalizes beyond the Qwen2.5 architecture.
