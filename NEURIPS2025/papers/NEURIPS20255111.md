## Problem

Existing inference-time alignment methods (Best-of-N, ARGS, CBS) operate by searching over a discrete set of candidate responses generated by the base policy, making their performance fundamentally bounded by the base model's capability and the candidate set size. When the base policy is weak or N is small, the selected response is likely far from the reward model's optimal region, and the required N grows exponentially as base model quality degrades. No prior method performs continuous optimization over the response space to escape these discrete search limitations.

## Input / Output

SEA takes a text prompt, a frozen reference LLM (e.g., LLaMA-3.2-1B/3B/8B-Base or 1B-Instruct), and a reward model (GRM-LLaMA-3.2-3B) as inputs, and produces a single aligned text response. The key unusual aspect is that optimization operates on continuous logits (soft outputs) of the reference model rather than discrete tokens; the straight-through estimator bridges the forward pass (argmax for discrete tokens) and backward pass (softmax for continuous gradients). After N Langevin steps, the final continuous logits are decoded into discrete text, with a self-revision prompt used to handle incomplete or redundant sequences arising from non-autoregressive optimization.

## Method

SEA formulates the optimal RLHF policy as an energy-based model with energy E(x,y) = log π_ref(y|x) + α·r(x,y), then samples from it via Langevin MCMC in the continuous logit space. At each iteration, the logits are updated by subtracting the gradient of the energy (computed through both the reference model and reward model) and adding Gaussian noise, effectively performing gradient-based optimization toward high-reward regions without being constrained to the base model's discrete support. The method uses multi-initialization (four concurrent Langevin chains from different base-model samples) to improve reward-space exploration, and a Top-k mask to narrow both the optimization and discretization spaces. Unlike discrete search methods that select among N candidates, SEA iteratively refines a single continuous representation, enabling it to reach reward regions unreachable by any finite candidate set from a weak base policy.

## Results

On AdvBench (safety), SEA reduces the harmful rate from 65.96% (SFT) to 5.58% with LLaMA-3.2-1B-Base, a 91.54% relative improvement, and to 3.85% with LLaMA-3-8B-Base, outperforming BoN-64 (43.85% / 8.85%) and CBS (24.81% / 6.35%). On MATH (reasoning), SEA achieves 32.00% accuracy versus 16.00% for BoN-64 and 27.50% for SFT, with a 74.96% relative reward increase. On TruthfulQA, SEA reaches 78–89% truthful rate while maintaining 99–100% informative rate and the highest diversity (0.87–0.91) across all four base models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SEA outperforms all four search-based baselines across every model and task. Against BoN (Nakano et al., 2021), SEA reduces harmful rate from 43.85% to 5.58% (LLaMA-3.2-1B-Base) and from 8.85% to 3.85% (LLaMA-3-8B-Base) on AdvBench, and improves MATH accuracy from 16.00% to 32.00%. Against Rejection Sampling (Liu et al., 2023), SEA achieves substantially lower harmful rates (5.58% vs. 50.00% on 1B-Base) and higher MATH accuracy (32% vs. 13%). Against ARGS (Khanov et al., 2024), SEA reduces harmful rate from 25.96% to 5.58% and boosts MATH accuracy from 7.00% to 32.00%, while also avoiding ARGS's severe truthful–informative trade-off on TruthfulQA (ARGS drops IR to 87% on 8B-Base vs. SEA's 99%). Against CBS (Zhou et al., 2024), SEA achieves lower harmful rates (5.58% vs. 24.81% on 1B-Base) and higher MATH accuracy (32% vs. 0.50%), demonstrating that continuous gradient-based optimization consistently surpasses discrete search at every granularity (sentence, token, and chunk level).

## Relation to Prior Work

SEA departs from the dominant 'search within a discrete space' paradigm of inference-time alignment (BoN, ARGS, CBS, Rejection Sampling) by reframing the problem as continuous optimization over logits using Langevin MCMC. Conceptually, it builds on the closed-form optimal RLHF policy (an energy-based model combining reference log-probability and reward) and borrows the gradient-based sampling machinery from COLD/COLD-Attack, which applied Langevin dynamics to vocabulary-space logits for constrained generation. The key novelty is repurposing this EBM+Langevin framework for alignment rather than constraint satisfaction, and using the straight-through estimator to make the discrete-to-continuous logit optimization end-to-end differentiable.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that continuous gradient-based optimization at inference time can substantially outperform discrete search, particularly when base models are weak or candidate budgets are small, and it naturally achieves 'deep' safety alignment (KL budget spread across all token positions) rather than the shallow prefix-focused alignment of token-by-token methods. This robustness to prefilling attacks (0% ASR for the instruct model) is practically significant for deployment. However, the evidence is limited to 1B–8B models, the evaluation uses the same reward model as the optimization signal (circular), and the straight-through estimator introduces an approximation whose error is not formally bounded. Open questions include scaling to 70B+ models, multi-turn settings, and whether the method remains effective when the reward model is significantly misaligned with true human preferences.
