## Problem

While no-regret learning algorithms for monotone games guarantee time-average convergence to a Nash equilibrium, the last-iterate trajectories may still exhibit cycling, undermining practical utility. It remained open whether a no-regret algorithm could achieve last-iterate convergence to a Nash equilibrium in general monotone and smooth games under bandit feedback and strongly uncoupled dynamics, where each player only observes its own cost at its chosen action and has no knowledge of other players' cost functions or action spaces.

## Input / Output

Each player receives only a scalar bandit feedback value c_i(x_i, x_{-i}) at its chosen action and operates on a compact convex action set X_i ⊆ R^d with bounded radius B. The algorithm requires two game-dependent regularizers: a ν-self-concordant barrier h on the interior of X_i and a convex function p with bounded Hessian eigenvalues (μI ≼ ∇²p ≼ ζI) such that c_i − κp remains convex. The output is a sequence of individual actions per iteration, with convergence measured by the Bregman divergence D_p to the Nash equilibrium set rather than the ℓ₂ norm or gap function used in prior work.

## Method

The algorithm is a doubly regularized online mirror descent that combines a self-concordant barrier regularizer h (which shapes an ellipsoidal gradient estimator to handle bandit feedback) with a second convex regularizer p (which accommodates monotone but not strongly monotone games). The gradient estimator uses the matrix A_i^t = (∇²h(x_i^t) + η_t(t+1)∇²p(x_i^t))^{-1/2} to define an ellipsoidal exploration direction, and the update minimizes η_t⟨x_i, ĝ_i^t⟩ + η_tκ(t+1)D_p(x_i, x_i^t) + D_h(x_i, x_i^t). The key analytical insight is that G = ∇c_i − ∇p is itself a monotone operator, enabling a recursive Bregman-divergence inequality that yields O(T^{-1/4}) last-iterate convergence for general monotone games and O(T^{-1/2}) for strongly monotone games (where p reduces to the Euclidean norm). The choice of p is game-dependent and determines the convergence metric, and for linear cost functions a modified regularization with τ = T^{-1/6} recovers O(T^{-1/6}) convergence matching the zero-sum matrix game rate.

## Results

For general monotone and smooth games under bandit feedback and strongly uncoupled dynamics, the proposed doubly regularized mirror descent algorithm achieves a last-iterate convergence rate of O(T^{-1/4}) in Bregman divergence to the Nash equilibrium set — the first non-asymptotic last-iterate guarantee for this class. In the strongly monotone case, the rate improves to O(T^{-1/2}), matching the best known rates (Drusvyatskiy et al., 2022; Lin et al., 2021). Individual regret is O(T^{3/4}) for monotone games and O(T^{1/2}) for strongly monotone games. For time-varying games with O(T^φ) equilibrium variation, the algorithm achieves O(max{T^{2φ/3−2/3}, T^{(4φ+5)²/72−9/8}}), improving upon prior state-of-the-art.

## Baselines & Comparisons

For strongly monotone games under bandit feedback, the proposed algorithm matches the O(T^{-1/2}) rate of Drusvyatskiy et al. (2022) and Lin et al. (2021) while additionally providing a high-probability guarantee (first of its kind) and extending to noisy bandit feedback. It improves upon Bravo et al. (2018), which achieved only O(T^{-1/3}). For general monotone (not strongly monotone) games under bandit feedback, the O(T^{-1/4}) rate is the first non-asymptotic last-iterate convergence result, whereas prior works (Mertikopoulos & Zhou 2019; Tatarenko & Kamgarpour 2019) provided only asymptotic guarantees. For linear games, the O(T^{-1/6}) rate matches Cai et al. (2023) but extends from two-player zero-sum to general multi-player linear games with convex compact action sets. In time-varying settings, the algorithm outperforms Duvocelle et al. (2023) (O(T^{φ/5−1/5})) and Yan et al. (2023) (O(T^{φ/3−2/3})) for equilibrium tracking, and provides the first non-asymptotic rate O(T^{-1/4+α}) for converging games where prior work only gave asymptotic convergence.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the two-regularizer mirror descent framework of Lin et al. (2021) from strongly monotone games to general monotone games under bandit feedback, replacing their fixed Euclidean norm regularizer with a game-dependent convex regularizer p whose curvature is matched to the cost functions. The ellipsoidal gradient estimator, originally from Abernethy et al. (2008) for linear bandit optimization and extended by Hazan and Levy (2014) to strongly convex costs, is adapted here with a carefully tuned perturbation parameter δ_t to handle the non-strongly-convex case. The key departure from prior work (Bravo et al. 2018; Tatarenko and Kamgarpour 2019) is that those results were either limited to strongly monotone games or provided only asymptotic convergence, whereas this paper delivers the first non-asymptotic last-iterate convergence guarantee for general monotone games under bandit feedback and strongly uncoupled dynamics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first finite-time last-iterate convergence result for general monotone games under bandit feedback, resolving an open question explicitly posed in the literature. The no-regret property (O(T^{3/4}) in monotone, O(T^{1/2}) in strongly monotone games) is practically important because it guarantees individual player performance even if other players deviate from the protocol. Open questions remain: the linear cost function case requires a modified algorithm with a slower O(T^{-1/6}) rate, the Bregman divergence convergence metric does not directly imply gap-function convergence in general, and it is unclear whether the O(T^{-1/4}) rate can be improved. The time-varying extensions provide the first non-asymptotic results for converging monotone games and improve equilibrium tracking rates over prior work.
