## Problem

Inverse folding models achieve strong zero-shot correlation with experimental protein stability changes, yet the formal link between their amino acid probabilities and thermodynamic free energy remains incompletely understood. The standard practice of computing log-likelihood ratios over a single structure works empirically, but it is unclear why conditioning on one structure suffices for an ensemble property, or whether the unfolded state should be explicitly modeled. This paper derives the missing theoretical connection and uses it to identify concrete improvements to the prediction protocol.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a wild-type protein backbone structure (typically a single PDB entry), the wild-type and variant amino acid sequences, and optionally a structural ensemble (20 ns MD trajectories or 20 BioEmu-generated conformations) plus IDP amino acid frequency statistics from MobiDB. The output is a scalar ΔΔG value (change in folding free energy in units of β⁻¹) or, equivalently, a rank ordering of variants by stability change. Unlike the standard single-structure log-ratio, the improved estimators require evaluating the inverse folding model on multiple sampled structures and incorporating a sequence-frequency correction term.

## Method

The paper derives a formal expression for ΔΔG as a difference of importance-sampling expectations over folded and unfolded structural ensembles (eq. 20), showing that the standard log-likelihood ratio is a single-sample Monte Carlo estimate under the assumptions of unfolded-state invariance and a single native structure. The key technical contribution is expressing the pseudo-free-energy change for each state via Bayes' theorem and the inverse folding model, yielding a tractable estimator that separates into a folded-state term (computable from structural ensembles) and an unfolded-state term (computable from sequence models or short fragments). Empirically, the best zero-shot performance is achieved by combining multi-sample folded ensembles (from MD or BioEmu) with IDP amino acid frequency statistics as a proxy for the unfolded state, plus a position-independent p_θ(a) correction, improving Pearson correlations from ~0.66 to ~0.72 on Protein G and from ~0.51 to ~0.54 on VAMP-seq over the single-structure baseline.

## Results

The standard single-sample log-odds baseline (the current zero-shot practice) achieves Pearson correlations of 0.63 (Guerois), 0.66 (Protein G), and 0.51 (VAMP-seq). The best proposed strategy—averaging over an MD structural ensemble for the folded state combined with IDP amino-acid frequency statistics for the unfolded state—improves these to 0.62, 0.72, and 0.54 respectively, with the largest gain on Protein G (+0.06). Adding the p_θ(a) marginal sequence correction provides a small but consistent boost (e.g., 0.70→0.71 on Protein G with MD). On the mega-scale dataset (42,253 variants, 40 proteins), replacing MD with BioEmu-generated ensembles (20 samples) retains the improvement over the single-structure baseline at a fraction of the computational cost.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not benchmark against external predictor baselines (e.g., FoldX, ESM-1b, or ProteinGym entries); instead, it compares its derived free-energy estimators against the standard single-sample log-odds expression that constitutes the current zero-shot practice. The two most impactful improvements over this baseline are: (1) replacing the single native structure with an MD ensemble average for the folded state, which consistently raises correlation by 0.02–0.04 on Protein G and VAMP-seq, and (2) adding an explicit unfolded-state contribution, where IDP amino-acid frequency statistics outperform both Monte Carlo unfolded simulations and minimal-context inverse-folding fragments. The Monte Carlo unfolded approach actually degrades performance below the baseline (e.g., 0.59 vs. 0.63 on Guerois), likely because ESM-IF was trained on AlphaFold-predicted structures and misinterprets the MC-sampled unfolded conformations. An ablation with ProteinMPNN confirms that the relative gains from the improved strategies are model-agnostic, though absolute correlations remain lower than with ESM-IF (0.58 vs. 0.72 best on Protein G). The BioEmu generative ensemble nearly matches MD (0.72 vs. 0.72 on Protein G), suggesting a practical path to scaling the ensemble-based approach without expensive simulations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper provides the missing theoretical bridge between inverse folding model likelihoods (ESM-IF, ProteinMPNN) and thermodynamic stability, building on the free-energy perturbation framework of Zwanzig (1954) and the empirical observation that log-odds ratios of inverse folding probabilities correlate with ΔΔG (Boomsma & Frellsen 2017; Meier et al. 2021; Dutton et al. 2024). Prior work used these ratios as zero-shot predictors without a formal derivation; here the authors show the standard practice is a single-sample Monte Carlo estimate under specific approximations, and that a previously ad hoc p(a) correction term follows naturally from the derivation. For structural ensembles, the paper leverages BioEmu (Lewis et al., 2025) as a computationally efficient proxy for MD simulations, extending the generative-ensemble paradigm to stability prediction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The formal derivation clarifies why inverse folding models work for stability prediction and identifies concrete, implementable improvements (IDP frequency statistics for the unfolded state, BioEmu ensembles for the folded state) that yield consistent but modest gains over the single-structure baseline. The framework generalizes directly to binding affinity prediction and provides a template for combining structure-based and sequence-based models. Open questions include why inverse folding likelihoods also correlate with absolute stability (not just ΔΔG), whether the IDP-statistics shortcut holds across diverse protein classes, and whether the approach scales to multi-site mutations where the small-substitution assumptions break down.
