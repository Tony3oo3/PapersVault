## Problem

Modern LM training is divided into multiple sequential stages (pre-training, continued pre-training, SFT, RL), yet it remains difficult to isolate how design choices at each stage affect downstream reasoning performance. Existing studies rely on opaque checkpoints with incomplete learning-rate decay or off-the-shelf base models with uncontrolled variables, confounding fair comparisons. This paper addresses the gap by training over 100 models from scratch with full control over every hyperparameter and data budget across all four stages.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252778/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

Models consume text prompts in a Human/Assistant template format covering math word problems, code function snippets with inputs, logical reasoning puzzles, and commonsense questions, and produce full step-by-step solution text. Evaluation employs four accuracy metrics (Pass@1, Maj@16, RM@16, Pass@16) computed over 16 sampled responses at temperature 1, plus an ORM score from an 8B reward model and a correct-ratio metric. Upstream evaluation uses 0-shot cloze accuracy on six standard benchmarks, while downstream evaluation spans in-domain (GSM8K-Platinum, MATH) and out-of-domain (CRUXEval, BoardgameQA, TabMWP, StrategyQA) generative tasks.

## Method

EvoLM trains 100+ decoder-only LLaMA-2 models (0.5B, 1B, 4B parameters) from scratch through four sequential stages with systematically varied compute budgets: pre-training on FineWeb-Edu (10B–320B tokens), continued pre-training on FineMath with a replay strategy (0–50B tokens, ~5% general-domain replay to mitigate catastrophic forgetting), SFT on filtered math QA pairs (50K–400K examples, 1–32 epochs), and RL via PPO with binary verifiable rewards (0–400K examples, 0–32 epochs). All models use complete warmup-cosine-decay learning-rate schedules, and only final checkpoints are evaluated. The key experimental design is a factorial sweep across model size, pre-training tokens, CPT budget, SFT scale, and RL scale, enabling the authors to identify saturation points (e.g., pre-training gains plateau around 80–160× model size, SFT OOD performance peaks at 2–4 epochs, RL saturates at 4–8 epochs) and to demonstrate that intermediate checkpoints from longer runs underperform dedicated shorter runs, that ORM scores correlate strongly (r ≈ 0.62–0.84) with downstream accuracy while validation perplexity does not, and that SFT/RL data allocation under a fixed budget trades off in-domain versus out-of-domain generalization.

## Results

EvoLM's 1B and 4B models, pretrained on only 320B tokens from FineWeb-Edu, achieve 52.49% and 56.94% average upstream accuracy respectively, performing competitively with TinyLlama-1B (53.23%, trained on 2T tokens) and Qwen1.5-4B (58.86%, trained on 3T tokens) despite using 6.25× and 9.38× less pre-training data. Downstream, the 4B-160BT SFT+RL model reaches 34.8% ID Maj@16 and 33.2% OOD Maj@16, while the 1B-320BT SFT+RL model achieves 25.0% ID Maj@16 and 29.9% OOD Maj@16. Pre-training performance saturates around 80×–160× model size, with negligible gains beyond 160B tokens for 1B models and 320B tokens for 4B models. CPT with 8B replay + 42B FineMath tokens yields the best downstream GSM8K-Platinum Pass@1 (21.01%) versus 19.27% for pure FineMath CPT.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EvoLM's 1B-320BT model (52.49% avg) trails TinyLlama-1B (53.23%, 2T tokens) by only 0.74 points despite 6.25× less pre-training data, and is within 2.8 points of Llama 3.2 1B (55.31%, 9T tokens). The 4B-320BT model (56.94% avg) is within 1.9 points of Qwen1.5-4B (58.86%, 3T tokens) and 6.7 points of Llama 3.2 3B (63.62%, 9T tokens), confirming strong data efficiency. On individual benchmarks, EvoLM 1B-320BT actually exceeds TinyLlama-1B on ARC-Easy (62.29 vs. 55.47) and OBQA (37.20 vs. 36.80), while losing on HellaSwag (53.86 vs. 61.47) and Winogrande (53.51 vs. 59.43). The 4B-320BT model surpasses Qwen1.5-4B on OBQA (41.80 vs. 39.60) and ARC-Easy (67.55 vs. 61.41) but lags on HellaSwag (61.38 vs. 71.45) and Winogrande (57.46 vs. 64.09). Overall, EvoLM demonstrates that 320B tokens of high-quality FineWeb-Edu pre-training captures most of the upstream capability that would require an order of magnitude more compute, validating the diminishing-returns finding central to the paper.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of scaling-law research (Chinchilla, Kaplan et al.), catastrophic-forgetting studies during continued pre-training (Ibrahim et al., Que et al.), and post-training analyses of SFT vs. RL (Raghavendra et al., Yue et al., Zhao et al.). Rather than introducing a new method, it carves new ground by being the first to study all four training stages—pre-training, CPT, SFT, and RL—end-to-end with fully transparent, from-scratch models under controlled variables, addressing the confounding factors (opaque checkpoints, incomplete LR decay, uncontrolled model-size/data interactions) that plague prior stage-by-stage analyses. It extends Springer et al.'s 'overtrained LMs are harder to fine-tune' finding from upstream loss to downstream generative reasoning and RL, and extends Yue et al.'s RL-confidence observation with precise saturation thresholds across both epochs and dataset size.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides practitioners with concrete, quantitative guidance on compute allocation across the full LM training pipeline: pre-training saturates around 80×–160× model size, CPT benefits from ~5% replay, SFT peaks at 2–4 epochs for OOD generalization, and RL saturates at 8–16 epochs or 100–200K examples. Its open-sourcing of 100+ models, all training data, and the full pipeline makes it a reference resource for the community. Key limitations include scope restricted to ≤4B parameters, PPO-only RL, and math-centric post-training, leaving open whether the observed saturation thresholds and the RL-confidence-sharpening behavior generalize to larger models, alternative RL algorithms (e.g., GRPO, DPO), and non-reasoning objectives like safety alignment or tool-use.
