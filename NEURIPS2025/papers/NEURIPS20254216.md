## Problem

Standard diffusion models operate in continuous intensity spaces with independent per-pixel noise, making them fundamentally unsuited for discrete quantities (particle counts, material phases) that must obey strict conservation laws such as mass conservation. Existing discrete-state diffusion models treat each pixel as an independent stochastic process, so they cannot preserve total image intensity exactly per sample. No prior diffusion framework could generate discrete-state images while exactly conserving global quantities like porosity or phase volume fractions.

## Input / Output

The method takes a target total intensity (particle count) per color channel as a hard constraint, optionally augmented by a class label or a spatial mask for conditional and inpainting tasks. It produces discrete-intensity images on a W×H×C lattice where each pixel holds a non-negative integer, and the sum over all pixels in each channel exactly equals the specified target. In scientific settings, this corresponds to binary or multi-phase microstructure images (e.g., 256×256 or 1000×1000) with exact porosity or phase volume fractions, enabling generation far outside the training distribution's intensity range.

## Method

DSD replaces independent per-pixel noise with a spatially correlated corruption: each unit of intensity is treated as a particle performing a continuous-time random walk on the pixel lattice, hopping to nearest neighbors at rate r while never changing its color channel, so total intensity per channel is conserved by construction in both forward and reverse processes. The reverse-time transition rates are derived analytically from the forward Master Equation solution and depend on the initial particle configuration; a neural network (NCSN++ with 4×C output channels and SoftPlus activation) learns to predict these rates from the corrupted image and time. Training uses either L1 rate-matching or a process-likelihood loss, with a noise schedule designed to equalize SSIM degradation across timesteps. Sampling employs an adaptive binomial τ-leaping integrator whose step size is governed by a fixed Courant–Friedrichs–Lewy number, ensuring numerical stability without negative particle populations. With periodic boundaries, the forward transition probabilities are computed efficiently via a 2D FFT diagonalizing the discrete Laplacian.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, DSD achieves a best FID of 18.1 and sFID of 15.7 (with CFL ε=0.01, bilateral filtering, and classifier thresholding at p≥0.99). On CelebA 64×64, the best configuration yields FID 28.9 and sFID 25.7. For subsurface rock microstructures, DSD achieves an FID of 0.9 on carbonate samples, substantially lower than the 18.1 reported by Lee and Yun [46] on similar lithology. The headline differentiator is exact per-sample intensity (porosity) preservation, which no prior diffusion method guarantees.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is against a Gaussian diffusion (NCSN++) baseline with explicit total-intensity conditioning. On MNIST, the Gaussian baseline produces small negative pixel values and fails to respect the intensity constraint at distribution tails, while DSD guarantees exact per-sample intensity preservation by construction (Fig. 14). On Savonnières carbonate microstructures, the Gaussian baseline incurs large relative porosity errors (>20%) when extrapolating beyond the training range, whereas DSD samples lie exactly on the identity line for target porosity even at μ±7σ (Figs. 15–17). For rock microstructures, DSD achieves an FID of 0.9 versus 18.1 reported by Lee and Yun [46] on similar carbonate lithology. A heuristic MSE guidance penalty applied to Gaussian diffusion also fails to enforce intensity constraints reliably at the tails. For lithium-ion NMC cathodes, DSD enables precise tuning of phase volume fractions, a capability absent in prior GAN-based approaches [29].

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the discrete-state diffusion lineage established by Santos et al. (Blackout Diffusion, ICML 2023) and Campbell et al. (NeurIPS 2022), which formulated reverse-time dynamics for discrete-state Markov chains, by introducing spatially correlated noise through a particle random walk on a lattice. It departs from prior discrete-state models (Austin et al., Hoogeboom et al., Sun et al.) that treat each pixel as an independent stochastic process, and from spatially-correlated continuous models (IHDM, Blurring Diffusion) that only preserve intensity in expectation. The noise schedule design follows the Ornstein-Uhlenbeck correspondence framework of Santos and Lin (NeurIPS 2023 Workshop), adapted to the discrete spatial diffusion process. The key conceptual departure is that the forward corruption is a discrete-state spatial random walk rather than independent pixel transitions or continuous blurring, making exact per-sample conservation a structural property of the process rather than an imposed constraint.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DSD provides the first diffusion framework that exactly preserves total intensity (mass, porosity, phase fractions) per sample in both forward and reverse processes, which is essential for scientifically grounded generative modeling of materials microstructures, battery electrodes, and porous media where conservation laws are non-negotiable. The demonstration on rock microstructures and NMC cathodes with quantitative transport/electrochemical metrics validates the approach in settings where approximate conditioning (projections, guidance, classifier-based methods) is insufficient. Open questions include scalability to 3D volumetric data, performance on high-bit-depth images where particle counts become large, and whether the framework can incorporate additional conservation laws (e.g., momentum, charge) or symmetries beyond total intensity. The FID scores on standard benchmarks (18.1 CIFAR-10, 28.9 CelebA) remain well below SOTA, suggesting the discrete-state constraint imposes a quality gap that may narrow with larger architectures or better training strategies.
