## Problem

Existing unified multimodal foundation models (e.g., Show-o, Transfusion, Janus) predominantly focus on architectural design and pretraining strategies, leaving a critical gap in post-training methodologies for non-autoregressive (diffusion) settings. Specifically, there is no systematic framework for applying chain-of-thought fine-tuning and reinforcement learning to diffusion-based models that jointly handle textual reasoning, multimodal understanding, and image generation under a single probabilistic objective.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254636/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts text prompts (for image generation and textual reasoning) or image-plus-text prompts (for multimodal understanding). Images are encoded into 32×32 = 1024 discrete tokens via a MAGVIT-v2 quantizer (downsampling factor 16, codebook size 8192), while text uses the LLaDA tokenizer. Outputs follow a unified CoT format: a reasoning trace enclosed in special tokens followed by the final result (text answer or 1024-token image sequence decoded to 512×512). Text generation uses semi-autoregressive block-wise denoising (block size 64, 512 steps), while image generation uses non-autoregressive cosine remasking (50 steps, CFG scale 3.5).

## Method

MMaDA replaces all modality-specific objectives with a single discrete masked-token-prediction loss (L_Diff-disc) applied uniformly to both text and image tokens, eliminating the need for separate AR and diffusion branches. Post-training proceeds in two stages: (1) Mixed Long-CoT fine-tuning, where a unified task-agnostic CoT format (reasoning trace + result) is jointly masked and reconstructed, enabling cross-modal reasoning transfer and providing a cold-start for RL; (2) UniGRPO, a diffusion-adapted GRPO that addresses three challenges unique to non-autoregressive generation—local masking dependency, mask ratio sensitivity, and the absence of a chain rule for sequence-level likelihoods—by sampling a random mask ratio per response, approximating log-likelihoods as the average over masked tokens, and applying a clipped surrogate objective with KL regularization. Diversified rewards (correctness, format, CLIP alignment, ImageReward) are instantiated per task branch within a single unified optimization objective.

## Results

On text-to-image generation, MMaDA-8B achieves a WISE Natural score of 0.67 (vs. 0.43 for SDXL and 0.16 for Janus), CLIP Score of 32.46, ImageReward of 1.15, and GenEval Overall of 0.63, setting new state-of-the-art among both generation-only and unified models. On multimodal understanding, MMaDA scores 86.1 on POPE, 67.6 on Flickr30k, 68.5 on MMB, and 64.2 on SEED, outperforming unified baselines like Show-o (80.0 POPE, 62.5 Flickr30k) and SEED-X (84.2 POPE, 52.3 Flickr30k). On textual reasoning, MMaDA reaches 73.4 on GSM8K and 36.0 on MATH, surpassing LLaDA-8B (70.7 GSM8K, 27.3 MATH) and LLaMA3-8B (53.1 GSM8K, 15.1 MATH), though trailing Qwen2-7B (80.2 GSM8K, 43.5 MATH).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On image generation, MMaDA decisively outperforms all unified baselines: it leads SDXL (the strongest generation-only model) by +0.24 on WISE Natural (0.67 vs. 0.43), +0.02 on ImageReward (1.15 vs. 1.13), and +0.34 on CLIP Score (32.46 vs. 32.12), while also surpassing Janus (0.61 GenEval Overall) by +0.02. Among unified models, the gap is even larger—MMaDA exceeds Show-o by +0.39 on WISE and +3.52 on CLIP Score, and SEED-X by +0.41 on WISE. On multimodal understanding, MMaDA beats all unified baselines on POPE (86.1 vs. 84.2 SEED-X, 80.0 Show-o), Flickr30k (67.6 vs. 62.5 Show-o, 52.3 SEED-X), MMB (68.5), and SEED (64.2), though it trails dedicated understanding-only models like LLaVA-v1.5 on MME (1410.7 vs. 1510.7), VQAv2 (76.7 vs. 78.5), and MMMU (30.2 vs. 35.4). On textual reasoning, MMaDA surpasses LLaDA-8B on all six benchmarks (e.g., GSM8K 73.4 vs. 70.7, MATH 36.0 vs. 27.3) and LLaMA3-8B on MMLU (68.4 vs. 64.5), GSM8K (73.4 vs. 53.1), and MATH (36.0 vs. 15.1), but remains behind Qwen2-7B on most metrics (GSM8K 73.4 vs. 80.2, MATH 36.0 vs. 43.5, TruthfulQA 43.1 vs. 54.2), reflecting its training on limited open-source text data.

## Relation to Prior Work

MMaDA departs from the dominant AR+Diffusion hybrid paradigm (Show-o, Transfusion, Janus) by adopting a fully unified discrete diffusion (masked token prediction) objective for both text and vision, building on LLaDA's text tokenizer and Show-o's MAGVIT-v2 image quantizer. Its most significant conceptual departure is the systematic treatment of post-training: while prior unified multimodal models focus almost exclusively on pretraining architecture and data, MMaDA introduces Mixed Long-CoT finetuning and UniGRPO—a diffusion-adapted GRPO that addresses the specific challenges of non-autoregressive likelihood estimation (local masking dependency, mask-ratio sensitivity, absence of a chain rule). This positions the paper as the first to close the pretraining-to-post-training loop for unified diffusion-based multimodal foundation models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a single 8B diffusion model can be competitive with specialized AR models across text reasoning, multimodal understanding, and image generation simultaneously, validating diffusion as a viable general-purpose multimodal paradigm rather than a generation-only tool. The sampling efficiency advantage (maintaining quality at 15–50 denoising steps versus 1024) and the natural inpainting/extrapolation capability are practically significant for deployment. Open questions include whether the approach scales beyond 8B, whether diffusion-centric RL methods (e.g., TraceRL) can close the remaining gap to strong AR baselines on math benchmarks (GSM8K: 73.4 vs. Qwen2-7B's 80.2), and whether the unified CoT format generalizes to additional modalities such as video or audio.
