## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting achieves real-time, high-quality novel view synthesis but requires 59 parameters per Gaussian, leading to large model sizes. While encoding Gaussian properties in neural fields reduces storage, the fixed resolution of grid-based representations cannot capture high-frequency spatial variations in opacity, scale, and view-dependent color, causing loss of fine geometric and photometric details. The paper addresses how to combine the compactness of neural fields with the high-frequency fidelity of explicit Gaussians without sacrificing rendering speed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250617/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes multi-view RGB images with known camera poses and a COLMAP-derived 3D point cloud as initial Gaussian anchor positions. It produces a compact scene representation consisting of a multi-resolution hash-encoded neural field (16 levels, feature size 2) plus a sparse set of explicit Gaussians storing only 8 parameters each (3D position, 3D diffuse color, isotropic scale, opacity), reducing per-Gaussian storage from 59 to 8 parameters. At render time, the representation synthesizes novel views at real-time frame rates (100+ FPS) while also generating a learned background map on a sphere of radius 100 for distant scene elements.

## Method

HyRF decomposes the scene into a sparse set of explicit compact Gaussians (storing position, diffuse color, isotropic scale, and opacity) and a decoupled pair of grid-based neural fields: a geometry field predicting anisotropic scale, opacity, and rotation, and a separate radiance field predicting view-dependent color conditioned on view direction via positional encoding. The neural field predictions are aggregated with the explicit Gaussian parameters through element-wise addition followed by sigmoid or L2-normalization activations, allowing the explicit residuals to recover high-frequency details that the grid cannot represent. A hybrid rendering pipeline first applies frustum pre-culling to avoid unnecessary neural field queries, then performs standard 3DGS alpha-blending on visible Gaussians, and finally composites a background color sampled from the radiance field at the ray-sphere intersection point using the accumulated transmittance. Training uses the standard 3DGS L1+SSIM loss with periodic opacity reset and low-opacity pruning, converging to 24–45% fewer Gaussians than vanilla 3DGS while achieving over 20× model size reduction.

## Results

On the three standard real-world benchmarks (MipNeRF360, Tanks & Temples, Deep Blending), HyRF achieves the best rendering quality across all metrics: PSNR of 27.78 / 24.02 / 30.37, SSIM of 0.816 / 0.844 / 0.910, and LPIPS of 0.211 / 0.176 / 0.241, while maintaining real-time rendering at 102–114 FPS. Model sizes are 34–49 MB, representing a 10–20× reduction over 3DGS (411–734 MB) and 2–5× over Scaffold-GS (66–244 MB). On urban-scale datasets (Mill19, Urbanscene3D), HyRF achieves PSNR 23.52 and 24.68 at 75–77 FPS with 202–215 MB, outperforming 3DGS by 1.1–3.3 dB PSNR while being 4–7× smaller.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HyRF outperforms 3DGS on all three standard datasets in PSNR (+0.07 to +0.96 dB), SSIM, and LPIPS while reducing model size by 10–20× (e.g., 49 MB vs. 734 MB on MipNeRF360) at comparable FPS (102 vs. 117). Against Scaffold-GS, HyRF achieves higher PSNR (+0.39 to +0.16 dB) and LPIPS while being 2–5× smaller (49 vs. 244 MB on MipNeRF360). Versus MLP-based NeRF methods (Mip-NeRF360, Instant-NGP, Plenoxels), HyRF matches or exceeds rendering quality at 1000–1700× faster rendering speed (102 FPS vs. 0.06–9.43 FPS). On urban-scale datasets, HyRF surpasses MegaNeRF and SwitchNeRF by 0.6–3.3 dB PSNR while being 7000× faster (<0.01 FPS for NeRF baselines). In the compression comparison (Table 4), HyRF's compressed version (18.04 MB, PSNR 27.66) beats all five 3DGS compression baselines (Niedermayr, Lee, Girish, Papantonakis, Chen) on both size and quality. On the Spec-GS anisotropic synthetic dataset, HyRF achieves +1.58 dB PSNR over 3DGS (35.41 vs. 33.83) with 82% less memory, demonstrating the advantage of MLP-based view-dependent modeling over spherical harmonics.

## Relation to Prior Work

HyRF sits at the intersection of two major lines of novel view synthesis research: explicit 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) and grid-based neural radiance fields (Instant-NGP). It departs from prior compressed-3DGS work like Scaffold-GS (which uses local anchor points with neural features) and LocoGS (which stores Gaussian properties in neural fields) by predicting all Gaussian properties globally through decoupled multi-resolution hash fields while retaining a sparse set of explicit Gaussians that store only 8 parameters (position, diffuse color, isotropic scale, opacity) to preserve high-frequency details. The key conceptual departure is the frequency-based decomposition—neural fields handle low-frequency spatial variation while explicit residuals capture high-frequency structure—and the decoupling of geometry and appearance into separate neural fields, which the authors show is necessary for adequate representational capacity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 20× model size reduction over 3DGS while matching or exceeding rendering quality and maintaining real-time speeds makes this practically significant for deployment scenarios where storage and bandwidth are constrained (mobile, web, large-scale scene archives). The method's compatibility with post-hoc vector quantization and Huffman encoding further extends its compression envelope, and the natural convergence to 24–45% fewer Gaussians suggests the hybrid representation is inherently more parameter-efficient. Open questions include whether the approach addresses aliasing (acknowledged as unresolved), whether it scales to web platforms or integrated graphics without high-end GPUs, and whether the decoupled-field insight generalizes to other hybrid explicit-implicit representations beyond Gaussian splatting.
