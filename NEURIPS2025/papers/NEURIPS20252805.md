## Problem

Traditional in-context learning (ICL) methods select demonstrations based on surface-level input similarity (e.g., k-nearest neighbors in embedding space), which breaks down under out-of-distribution (OOD) scenarios where the query and candidate pool originate from different domains or languages. The paper identifies that superficially similar examples can correspond to fundamentally different underlying problems, causing the LLM to latch onto spurious patterns rather than the causal structure of the task.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text query (x*, t*) along with an in-distribution training pool of (x, y, t, e) tuples, where e encodes observable domain attributes (language, dataset origin). A pre-trained VAE maps text embeddings (from models like text-embedding-3-small) into two latent vectors: a task-relevant causal representation c and a domain-specific source representation s. The output is a K-shot demonstration set selected by c-similarity, which is then concatenated into the LLM prompt to produce a final prediction. Unlike standard ICL that retrieves in raw embedding space, CCL retrieves in the learned causal latent space, decoupling problem identity from surface form.

## Method

CCL introduces a VAE-based causal representation learning module whose generative model encodes the causal graph t→c→{x,y} and s→{x,e}, with c capturing invariant task structure and s capturing domain-specific variation. The key architectural choice is factorizing the inference model as q_φ(c,s,y|x,t,e) = q_φ(c,s|x,t,e)·p_θ(y|c), which injects the causal mechanism c→y directly into inference and analytically marginalizes out the unobserved y at test time, yielding a modified ELBO (Eq. 2) that depends only on q_φ(c,s|x,t,e). Training uses MMD regularization to enforce c–s independence and an iVAE-style conditional prior p_θ(c|t) to condition the causal latent on the observed task variable. At inference, the trained encoder φ_c produces c* for the query, and demonstrations are selected via KNN or K-means clustering in c-space rather than x-space. The paper further provides theoretical guarantees (Theorems 3.3–3.4) showing that c-similarity selection yields strictly tighter parameter-estimation and test-prediction error bounds than x-similarity selection under a linear-causal data-generating assumption.

## Results

On the primary OOD generalization benchmark (Table 3), CCL achieves the best average accuracy across all three language models: 75.74% for Llama-3.2-3B-IT (vs. 64.75% ZS, 60.12% LLM-R, 65.70% ICL-K-means), 81.44% for Phi-4-mini-IT, and 94.01% for GPT-4o. The improvement is most pronounced for the smaller Llama-3.2-3B-IT model, where CCL outperforms the next-best baseline (ICL-K-means) by 10.04 points on average. On MGSM (5-shot), CCL reaches 94.55% total accuracy and 91.80% OOD accuracy, surpassing ICL(KNN) (94.07% / 91.00%) and ICL(Fixed) (91.20% / 91.10%). On advanced tasks (Table 6), CCL consistently leads: 90.05% on Yelp sentiment reversal, 87.70% on Amazon, 61.52% on MMLU, and 84.43% on HotpotQA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary external baseline is LLM-R (Wang et al., EACL 2024), a learned-retriever approach for selecting in-context examples. CCL decisively outperforms LLM-R on the smaller Llama-3.2-3B-IT model, achieving 75.74% average OOD accuracy versus LLM-R's 60.12% (a 15.6-point gap), with particularly large margins on QNLI (75.18 vs. 29.93) and Yelp (95.44 vs. 79.48). For the larger Phi-4-mini-IT and GPT-4o models, CCL edges out LLM-R by 1.04 and 0.14 points on average, respectively, indicating that the advantage of causal-aware retrieval diminishes as model capacity increases. CCL also consistently beats the vanilla ICL(K-means) baseline, which uses the same K-means clustering strategy but retrieves based on raw input embeddings rather than causal embeddings; the gap is largest for Llama-3.2-3B-IT (75.74 vs. 65.70) and smallest for GPT-4o (94.01 vs. 91.76). On MGSM, CCL surpasses both ICL(KNN) and ICL(Fixed) in OOD accuracy (91.80% vs. 91.00% and 91.10%), confirming that causal-embedding-based retrieval is more effective than surface-similarity retrieval under language shift.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate research threads: causal representation learning (CRL) for OOD generalization and in-context learning (ICL) demonstration selection. It builds directly on Liu et al. (NeurIPS 2021) for the VAE-based causal semantic representation framework and on the iVAE (Khemakhem et al., AISTATS 2020) for conditional priors, repurposing these tools not for direct prediction but for selecting ICL demonstrations. The key departure from prior ICL work (KNN-based retrieval, LLM-R learned retrievers) is the replacement of surface-level input similarity with causal-feature similarity, and the key departure from prior CRL work is the application of learned invariant representations to prompt construction rather than to a downstream classifier.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first theoretical guarantee (Theorems 3.3–3.4) that causal-similarity demonstration selection yields strictly tighter parameter and test error bounds than input-similarity selection, giving a principled justification for the approach beyond empirical observation. Practically, the method is a lightweight plug-in (train a VAE on the ID pool, then retrieve on causal embeddings) that consistently improves OOD ICL accuracy across multiple LLMs and tasks, with the largest gains for smaller models like Llama-3.2-3B-IT. Open questions include whether the VAE-based latent space can capture the full richness of natural language (acknowledged limitation), whether the linear-causal theoretical assumptions hold for real LLM attention mechanisms, and how the approach scales to multi-step reasoning or compositional OOD shifts beyond single-domain language changes.
