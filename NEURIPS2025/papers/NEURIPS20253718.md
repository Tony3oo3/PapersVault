## Problem

Estimating a causal effect in a target domain is obstructed when an unobserved confounder U affects both treatment and outcome, and the causal effect shifts across domains due to changes in U's distribution. Prior proxy-based domain adaptation methods (e.g., Tsai et al.) target the conditional mean E[Y|X=x] rather than the interventional distribution, and require observing both X and W in the target domain. This paper asks whether the interventional distribution q(y|do(x)) is identifiable and estimable when only the proxy W is observed in the target domain.

## Input / Output

The method consumes two data streams: n_src i.i.d. source-domain tuples (E, W, X, Y) from k_E source domains, and n_tgt i.i.d. target-domain observations of W alone (no X or Y). All variables are assumed discrete with full support, though extensions to continuous X and Y are discussed. The output is the target-domain interventional probability q(y|do(x)) for each (x,y) pair, optionally accompanied by asymptotic confidence intervals at a chosen level (1−α). Estimates are clipped to [0,1] since the plug-in formula can produce values outside this range.

## Method

The key identifiability result (Thm. 1) shows that under a rank condition on P(W|E,x) (Asm. 1, requiring the proxy to be sufficiently informative across domains), the target interventional distribution equals P(y|E,x)·P(W|E,x)†·Q(W), a product of three quantities estimable from the available data. Two consistent estimators are proposed: a causal parametrisation that performs MLE over the full SCM parameters (P(U|E), Q(U), P(W|U), P(X|U), P(y|U,W,x)) and applies a plug-in formula, and a reduced parametrisation that directly estimates only the components appearing in the identification formula via empirical frequencies. The reduced estimator is shown to be asymptotically normal via the delta method, yielding closed-form confidence intervals with valid coverage. The approach relies on the latent shift assumption (domain E affects only U) and the invariance of the structural equations P(W|U) and P(y|U,W,x) across domains.

## Results

On the Expedia Hotel Searches dataset, the reduced parametrisation estimator achieves an average absolute error of 0.044 in estimating q(Y=1|do(X=1)) across 18 target domains, outperforming NoAdj (0.051), NoAdj* (0.080), WAdj (0.053), and WAdj* (0.075). The reduced estimator also yields shorter confidence intervals (median length 0.14 vs. 0.17 for NoAdj). In simulations with n=20,000, the causal and reduced estimators achieve average absolute errors of 0.040 and 0.058 respectively, both substantially below the no-adjustment and W-adjustment baselines, with the oracle (using unavailable interventional data) achieving the lowest error.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its two proposed estimators (causal and reduced parametrisation) against four baselines: no-adjustment (NoAdj, NoAdj*) and W-adjustment (WAdj, WAdj*), where the starred variants use target-domain (X,Y) data unavailable to the other methods. On the real-world Expedia dataset, the reduced estimator achieves the lowest average absolute error (0.044) among all non-oracle methods, beating NoAdj by 0.007, WAdj by 0.009, NoAdj* by 0.036, and WAdj* by 0.031. In simulations, both proposed estimators show similar performance (avg. error 0.040 vs. 0.058) and clearly outperform all baselines, whose error distributions are shifted toward larger values. The oracle, which uses data from the actual interventional distribution, achieves the lowest error overall, as expected. The reduced estimator is also faster to compute than the causal estimator, and its confidence intervals have valid empirical coverage with decreasing length as sample size grows.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of proximal causal inference (Kuroki & Pearl 2014; Miao et al. 2018) and proxy-based domain adaptation (Tsai et al. 2024). It departs from Miao et al. by working in a multi-domain setting where the causal effect of X on Y can shift across domains due to latent confounder distribution changes, and from Tsai et al. by targeting the interventional distribution q(y|do(x)) rather than the conditional mean, and by requiring only the proxy W (not X or Y) in the target domain. The identifiability proof adapts the matrix adjustment framework of Kuroki and Pearl to the multi-domain latent-shift structure, with the key technical ingredient being the invertibility of P(W|E,x) across sufficiently diverse source domains.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean identifiability result showing that a causal effect in a completely unseen domain can be recovered from source-domain data plus only the proxy distribution in the target domain, with two consistent estimators and valid confidence intervals. This is practically relevant for settings like A/B testing generalization, where one wants to predict treatment effects in new populations with minimal data collection. Key limitations include the discrete-variable assumption (though continuous extensions are sketched), the latent shift assumption, and the requirement that source domains be sufficiently diverse (Asm. 1). Open questions include extending to continuous unobserved confounders, relaxing the latent shift assumption further, and developing more robust estimation when the condition number of P(W|E,x) is large.
