## Problem

Existing test-time adaptation methods for OOD detection either assume a closed-set setting or rely on batch processing, making them ill-suited for real-time open-set scenarios where individual samples arrive sequentially and may belong to unknown categories. The paper tackles the gap of performing single-image test-time adaptation that simultaneously handles both distributional and semantic shifts in an open-set environment, where the ID-OOD boundary becomes ambiguous and must be refined on the fly without labeled data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255033/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single RGB image (processed by a frozen-text-encoder VLM such as CLIP or MaPLe with ViT-B/16 or ViT-B/32 vision encoders) together with per-class positive prompts ("a photo of a [CLS]") and negative prompts ("a photo of no [CLS]"). It produces an ID/OOD binary decision, a class prediction for ID samples, and updated vision-encoder weights. Operationally, the method maintains a sliding-window score bank and feature bank (each of size 128, updated FIFO) and a dynamic confidence threshold computed as the mean of the most recent 512 confidence scores, all operating at batch size 1 with an SGD learning rate of 1.5e-3.

## Method

Open-IRT comprises two modules. The Polarity-aware Prompt-based OOD Filter (PPF) computes a per-sample score S(f) that jointly captures absolute semantic alignment (cosine similarity to the best-matching positive prompt) and relative semantic polarity (the gap between positive and negative prompt similarities), then classifies the sample as ID or OOD via a two-component GMM fitted with EM over a sliding-window score bank. The Intermediate Domain-based Test-time Adaptation (IDT) module constructs a middle-domain feature f_m by style-transferring the normalized sample feature using either ID or OOD bank statistics (depending on the PPF decision and a dynamic confidence threshold), then applies an InfoNCE-based contrastive loss that pulls the sample toward its same-domain nearest neighbors while repelling it from both the opposite-domain neighbors and the intermediate feature, thereby indirectly enlarging the ID-OOD gap. A bidirectional pseudo-label loss complements this: high-confidence samples are aligned to their predicted class via cross-entropy, while low-confidence samples are pushed away from their prediction via negated cross-entropy, suppressing intra-class noise. The total test-time loss L_TTA = L_psd + L_mid is backpropagated through the vision encoder for each incoming sample in an update-then-evaluate loop.

## Results

On the CIFAR-10C→SVHN benchmark with CLIP, Open-IRT achieves 96.52% AUROC, 18.34% FPR95, and 80.62% AccHM, outperforming the strongest baseline ROSITA (94.94% AUROC, 31.22% FPR95, 79.12% AccHM) by +1.58% AUROC, −12.88% FPR95, and +1.50% AccHM. On CIFAR-10C→MNIST with CLIP, Open-IRT reaches 99.73% AUROC and 1.28% FPR95, surpassing ROSITA (99.43%, 3.25%). The paper reports that on the more challenging CIFAR-100C→SVHN setting (detailed in appendix), gains reach +11.04% AUROC, −10.80% FPR95, and +8.45% AccHM over prior SOTA. Across ImageNet-C and VisDA benchmarks, Open-IRT consistently leads, e.g., +0.47% AUROC and −7.49% FPR95 on ImageNet-C→MNIST with CLIP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Open-IRT outperforms all seven baselines on the majority of settings. Against ROSITA (the strongest prior method), Open-IRT wins on AUROC and FPR95 across nearly all CIFAR-10C→{MNIST, SVHN, Tiny-ImageNet} tasks with both CLIP and MaPLe, e.g., 99.73% vs 99.43% AUROC and 1.28% vs 3.25% FPR95 on MNIST (CLIP). The one notable exception is CIFAR-10C→CIFAR-100C (small distribution shift), where TDA edges out Open-IRT on AUROC (83.54 vs 82.65) and AccHM (70.13 vs 69.20) with CLIP, and ROSITA leads on FPR95 for CIFAR-100C→SVHN with MaPLe (11.45 vs 15.92). Open-IRT substantially dominates TPT/TPT-C, UniEnt, and DPE, which show near-zero-shot or degraded performance (e.g., DPE drops to 46.97% AUROC on CIFAR-10C→MNIST). Against OWTTT, Open-IRT achieves consistent gains of 1–16% in AUROC and 5–60% in FPR95 reduction. Against PAlign/PAlign-C (MaPLe-only baselines), Open-IRT improves AccHM by 1–4% across all OOD targets, confirming that the intermediate-domain strategy generalizes beyond CLIP's single-modal prompting to multi-modal prompt learning frameworks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the VLM-based OOD detection line (CLIPN's negative prompts, NegPrompt, ROSITA's test-time alignment) and the single-image test-time adaptation line (TPT, TDA, MEMO) by unifying them under an open-set, single-sample streaming setting. The key conceptual departure is the Intermediate Domain Hypothesis: rather than directly maximizing ID-OOD distance (as ROSITA does via feature alignment) or relying on batch-level entropy minimization (as UniEnt does), Open-IRT constructs a middle feature domain and enforces bidirectional repulsion from it, indirectly decomposing the ID-OOD gap. The PPF module's polarity-aware scoring (positive + negative prompt contrast) builds on CLIPN's 'teach CLIP to say no' idea but formalizes it with a Semantic Contrast Hypothesis and a GMM-based adaptive threshold.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap: real-time, single-image OOD detection in open-set environments (autonomous driving, industrial inspection) where batch processing is infeasible and unknown categories appear unpredictably. The intermediate-domain strategy offers a reusable conceptual tool for any ID-OOD separation task where direct OOD distribution knowledge is limited. However, the method's reliance on a 128-sample sliding-window memory bank partially contradicts its 'single-image' framing, and the authors acknowledge that extending to detection/segmentation and reconciling the memory requirement with true single-image inference remain open. The approach is also validated only on classification benchmarks with relatively simple OOD sources (MNIST, SVHN), leaving open how it performs under more semantically overlapping OOD distributions.
