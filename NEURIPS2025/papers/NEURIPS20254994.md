## Problem

Offline-to-Online RL (O2O RL) methods are designed for clean simulator data, yet real-world offline datasets and online transitions are frequently corrupted by noise or adversarial attacks on states, actions, rewards, and dynamics. Prior robust RL work addresses offline-only corruption, while O2O work targets exploration efficiency but ignores corruption entirely. The paper identifies that data corruption induces heavy-tailedness in the learned policy itself (not just Q-targets), which silently degrades online exploration efficiency and has been overlooked by both lines of work.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a corrupted offline replay buffer (with random or adversarial noise injected into a fraction c₁ of transitions across states, actions, rewards, and next states) and online environment transitions that are similarly corrupted with probability c₂ during storage. It takes as starting point a frozen offline-pretrained policy π_β and value functions Q, V (trained via IQL or RIQL on the corrupted data), and produces a refined online policy π_θ for continuous-state control tasks (MuJoCo locomotion, AntMaze). The IPW mechanism requires access to exact action probabilities from both the offline and online policies, which constrains compatibility with probability-agnostic models like diffusion policies.

## Method

RPEX modifies PEX's softmax-based action selection (Eq. 5) by adding an Inverse Probability Weighting (IPW) term to the numerator and denominator, yielding Eq. (6): P_w[i] ∝ exp(Q(s,a_i)/α) + κ·clip((Q−V)/π_i(a_i|s), MIN, MAX). For low-quality actions (Q−V<0) that the corrupted policy assigns non-negligible probability, the IPW weight strongly penalizes their selection during online exploration; for high-quality actions, the clipped IPW saturates and the standard Q-based softmax dominates. The theoretical analysis (Proposition 6.1) shows Eq. (6) is the closed-form solution to a regularized maximum-entropy objective where the regularization term acts as a rectification mechanism that suppresses corruption-induced heavy-tailed policy mass on inferior actions. In practice, the offline policy is frozen, a new online policy is initialized independently, and the composite policy [π_β, π_θ] with K=2 is used for exploration while the online policy is trained via IQL/RIQL on a buffer mixing offline and online data. The method also incorporates state normalization, AlignIQL policy extraction under state attacks, and a high UTD ratio specifically for action corruption.

## Results

On D4RL Medium-Replay tasks (Halfcheetah, Walker2d, Hopper) under random data corruption (c₁=0.3, c₂=0.5, ε=1), RPEX achieves an average O2O score of 75.16, a 28.7% improvement over its offline score of 58.4. This outperforms RIQL-PEX (66.6, +14.1%), IQL-RPEX (59.1, +32.7%), and vanilla RIQL (54.9, −6.1%). On the more challenging AntMaze-Large tasks under dynamics and observation corruption, RPEX improves from 26.6 to 34.1 (+30.7%), while RIQL-PEX degrades from 26.6 to 26.1 (−1.9%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RPEX (RIQL+IPW) is the strongest method across 11 of 12 corruption settings, achieving an average O2O score of 75.16 versus 66.6 for RIQL-PEX (the closest baseline, +12.8% relative gain) and 54.9 for vanilla RIQL (+36.9%). The largest margins appear under action and reward corruption: on Hopper-MR action attack, RPEX reaches 112.6 vs. 93.2 for RIQL-PEX; on Walker2d-MR reward attack, RPEX scores 100.5 vs. 91.9. IQL-based variants (IQL, IQL-PEX, IQL-RPEX) consistently underperform their RIQL counterparts because IQL's offline scores are lower (44.5 vs. 58.4), and the IPW modification alone (IQL-RPEX, 59.1) cannot close the gap to RIQL+RPEX. On AntMaze-Large tasks, RPEX improves from 26.6 to 34.1 while RIQL-PEX degrades to 26.1, a ~30.7% relative advantage. The only setting where RPEX does not clearly lead is Halfcheetah-MR dynamics, where all methods show minimal O2O gains (44.4 vs. 44.7 for RIQL-PEX), consistent with the known difficulty of dynamics corruption for value-based methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two previously separate research threads: robust offline RL under data corruption (RIQL [50], which uses Huber loss and quantile estimators to handle heavy-tailed Q-targets) and offline-to-online RL (PEX [53], which uses a composite policy set to bridge the offline-to-online transition). The key conceptual departure is identifying that data corruption induces heavy-tailedness in the *policy itself*—not just in Q-targets—and that this policy heavy-tailedness is the primary bottleneck for online exploration efficiency in O2O settings. The method repurposes Inverse Probability Weighting (IPW) from causal inference and long-tailed classification [42] as a regularization term within PEX's action-selection mechanism, yielding a single modified probability formula (Eq. 6).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Robust O2O RL is critical for real-world deployment where sensor noise, human annotation errors, and adversarial perturbations are unavoidable, yet prior work treated offline robustness and O2O efficiency as separate problems. The finding that RIQL's Q-target fix inadvertently worsens policy heavy-tailedness (Fig. 1b) challenges the assumption that addressing one corruption-induced pathology is sufficient. A key limitation is that IPW requires access to exact action probabilities, precluding integration with probability-agnostic policy representations like diffusion models. Open questions include whether the approach extends to high-dimensional continuous control beyond MuJoCo, to language-conditioned or VLA policies, and whether the policy heavy-tailedness diagnosis generalizes to model-based O2O settings.
