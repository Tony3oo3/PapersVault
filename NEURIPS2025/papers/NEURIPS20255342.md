## Problem

Video LLMs frequently fail at temporal reasoning (e.g., understanding continuous actions or tracking object transformations) because they are misled by language priors from LLM pre-training and by 'image' priors—static visual features in individual frames that dilute temporal perception. Existing remedies require costly retraining or architectural changes, and findings from one architecture often do not generalize. The paper asks whether a model-agnostic, training-free inference-time method can explicitly steer decoding away from these temporally insensitive failure modes.

## Input / Output

The method takes a multi-frame video (8 frames for Video-LLaVA, 32 for LLaVA-Video-7B-Qwen2) encoded into frame embeddings, plus a text query, and produces a standard text response. During decoding, the video is consumed twice: once in its original form and once after attention-guided temporal distortion is applied to the frame embeddings. No additional training data, model weights, or architectural modifications are required—the method is a plug-in decoding strategy applied on top of any existing Video LLM.

## Method

The core contribution is Video Temporal Distortion (VTD) integrated into Contrastive Decoding. On the first forward pass, attention maps from intermediate LLM layers are aggregated with momentum to compute per-frame and per-token importance scores. The top-w_fdr most important frames are replaced by their mean-pooled embeddings plus Gaussian noise (removing temporal order while retaining confounding static visual context), and the remaining frames undergo fine-grained token masking plus a block-level moving-content disruption that smooths dynamic regions identified via cosine similarity within sliding windows. A second forward pass with the distorted video yields logits that are contrastively subtracted from the original logits via softmax[(1+α)·logit_orig − α·logit_distorted], with an adaptive plausibility constraint that restricts contrastive decoding to high-probability tokens. The key design principle is that the distortion must be calibrated: too little fails to induce errors, while too much randomizes the response and destroys the contrastive signal.

## Results

On TempCompass, the proposed method (applied to LLaVA-Video-7B-Qwen2) achieves 67.5% overall accuracy, outperforming VCD (65.7%), SID (65.8%), and TCD (65.7%). On EventHallusion, it reaches 72.1% overall (vs. 66.5% for VCD and 67.2% for TCD), with the largest gain on the Interleave task (75.6% vs. 66.8% for TCD). On Video-MME, Temporal Perception improves dramatically from 61.1% to 84.1%, surpassing even VILA1.5-40B (60.0%), while overall accuracy rises from 59.8% to 61.6%. On MLVU, overall accuracy improves from 67.4% to 68.5%, with notable gains on temporal-related sub-tasks (Action Count: 41.8→44.1, Action Order: 57.9→62.6).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method consistently outperforms all three contrastive decoding baselines (VCD, SID, TCD) across every benchmark: on TempCompass (LLaVA-Video), it achieves 67.5% vs. 65.7–65.8%; on EventHallusion, 72.1% vs. 66.4–67.2%; on Video-MME, 61.6% vs. 59.8–60.0%; and on MLVU, 68.5% vs. 67.3–67.4%. Against other Video LLMs, the method applied to LLaVA-Video-7B-Qwen2 surpasses all 7B/8B models (Video-LLaVA 39.9%, VILA1.5-8B 45.9%, LongVA 52.6% on Video-MME) and approaches the much larger VILA1.5-40B (62.3%), while on MLVU it exceeds LongVA-7B (56.4%) and VILA1.5-40B (46.2%) by a wide margin. On EventHallusion, the method (72.1%) beats VILA (65.0%), LLaMA-VID (66.0%), and ShareGPT4-Video (49.1%), with the largest relative gain on the Interleave task (+9.8 points over TCD). On TempCompass, the method also outperforms Intern-VL2 (52.1%), LLaVA-OneVision (47.6%), and LongVA (44.7%) in the Video-LLaVA backbone setting. The only notable trade-off is a slight drop on spatial-oriented MLVU sub-tasks (Anomaly Recognition, Topic Reasoning), suggesting a mild temporal–spatial tension under aggressive distortion.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Contrastive Decoding (CD) from Li et al. (ACL 2023) from text generation to video understanding, building on the line of work that applied CD to mitigate hallucinations in image MLLMs (VCD, SID). The closest prior work, TCD (AAAI 2025), also uses frame-level distortion for video event hallucination but relies on random frame dropping. The key departure is the identification of 'image prior'—a distinct failure mode where static visual content in individual frames misleads temporal reasoning—and the design of an attention-guided, adaptive temporal distortion that preserves misleading static context while selectively removing temporal cues, rather than applying uniform or random corruption.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, model-agnostic nature of the method makes it immediately applicable to any Video LLM without retraining, which is practically valuable given the rapid proliferation of new architectures. The identification of 'image prior' as a separate failure mode from language prior provides a useful diagnostic lens for understanding why Video LLMs fail on temporal tasks. Open questions include whether the attention-based importance estimation can be made more precise, whether the approach extends to multi-modal inputs (e.g., video + subtitles), and whether the observed trade-off between temporal and spatial understanding (performance drops on anomaly recognition and topic reasoning) reflects a fundamental limitation or can be mitigated with more refined distortion strategies.
