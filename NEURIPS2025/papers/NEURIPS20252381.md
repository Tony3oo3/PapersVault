## Problem

Existing algorithms for overlapping and robust edge-colored clustering of hypergraphs face a fundamental trade-off: greedy combinatorial algorithms run fast but sacrifice solution quality, while LP-rounding algorithms produce near-optimal solutions at prohibitive computational cost. Two open questions from Crane et al. [19] also remained unresolved—whether an O(1)-approximation exists for LOCAL ECC, and whether bicriteria O(1),O(1)-approximation is achievable for GLOBAL ECC.

## Input / Output

The method takes an edge-colored hypergraph H=(V,E) with a color set C, edge colors c_e, non-negative edge weights w_e, and a problem-specific budget: a per-vertex color budget b_v (LOCAL ECC), a node-removal budget b_robust (ROBUST ECC), or a global extra-color budget b_global (GLOBAL ECC). It produces a vertex coloring σ: V→2^C (or σ: (V\V_R)→C for ROBUST ECC, where V_R is the removed set) that minimizes the total weight of 'mistakes'—edges where some incident vertex's assigned color set does not include the edge's color—subject to the respective budget constraints.

## Method

The paper designs a family of combinatorial algorithms via the primal-dual method, constructing solutions by continuously increasing dual variables while maintaining feasibility, guided by complementary slackness to decide which edges become mistakes. For LOCAL ECC, the algorithm processes vertices sequentially, increasing dual variables for all incident colors until the (b_v+1)-th tightest edge constraint is met, yielding a (b_local+1)-approximation in linear time. For ROBUST and GLOBAL ECC, the algorithm simultaneously increases dual variables for a set R of 'conflicting' vertices (those with ≥2 incident loose edges of distinct colors) until the budget is exhausted, producing true 2(b+1)-approximations. A key technical contribution is a modified LP relaxation for ROBUST ECC that adds the constraint z_v + Σ_c x_{v,c} ≤ 1, reducing the integrality gap from ∞ (in Crane et al.'s formulation) to Θ(b_robust), enabling a true rather than bicriteria approximation. The approach is complemented by UGC-hardness inapproximability results for LOCAL ECC and integrality gap lower bounds matching the achieved ratios.

## Results

For LOCAL ECC, the proposed combinatorial algorithm achieves an average relative error estimate of 0.141 (vs. 0.297 for the greedy baseline) while running in 0.121 s on average—over 1,200× faster than the LP-rounding baseline (146.470 s) and more than 2× faster than the greedy algorithm on most datasets. For ROBUST ECC, the proposed algorithm's average relative error is 0.042, six times better than the greedy baseline (0.272). For GLOBAL ECC, the proposed algorithm achieves an average relative error of 0.039 on nontrivial instances, more than 23× better than the greedy baseline (0.912). Theoretically, the algorithms achieve (b_local+1)-, 2(b_robust+1)-, and 2(b_global+1)-approximation ratios for LOCAL, ROBUST, and GLOBAL ECC respectively, with the LOCAL ECC ratio matching the integrality gap and being UGC-hard to improve.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two baselines from Crane et al. (WSDM 2024): a greedy combinatorial r-approximation algorithm and an LP-rounding algorithm. Against the greedy baseline, the proposed algorithm wins on solution quality across all three problems—achieving 0.141 vs. 0.297 average relative error for LOCAL ECC, 0.042 vs. 0.272 for ROBUST ECC, and 0.039 vs. 0.912 for GLOBAL ECC—while also being faster for LOCAL ECC (e.g., 11× faster on Trivago) but slower for ROBUST and GLOBAL ECC. Against the LP-rounding baseline, the proposed algorithm is dramatically faster (e.g., 2,100× on Trivago for LOCAL ECC) while the LP-rounding method produces near-optimal solutions at the cost of solving LPs with Gurobi. A key distinction is that for ROBUST and GLOBAL ECC, the proposed algorithm provides a true (non-bicriteria) approximation that strictly satisfies the budget constraint, whereas Crane et al.'s LP-rounding is only bicriteria and empirically violated the budget in most ROBUST ECC instances. The proposed algorithm thus occupies a sweet spot: combinatorial speed with LP-quality guarantees, answering two open questions from Crane et al. regarding O(1) bicriteria approximation for GLOBAL ECC and the inapproximability of LOCAL ECC.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the primal-dual framework of Goemans and Williamson [29, 30] to the edge-colored hypergraph clustering setting, building directly on Crane et al. [19] (WSDM 2024), who introduced LOCAL, GLOBAL, and ROBUST ECC along with greedy and LP-rounding algorithms. The key conceptual departure is a modified LP constraint for ROBUST ECC (z_v + Σx_{v,c} ≤ 1) that reduces the integrality gap from infinity to O(b_robust), enabling a true (non-bicriteria) approximation where none was previously possible. The inapproximability results draw on UGC-hardness of hypergraph vertex cover (Bansal & Khot [10], Dinur et al. [23]), connecting ECC hardness to a well-studied problem family.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper resolves two open questions from Crane et al. [19]: it proves UGC-hardness of approximating LOCAL ECC within b_local + 1 − ε (ruling out O(1)-approximation), and it affirmatively shows bicriteria (O(1),O(1)) approximation is achievable for GLOBAL ECC. The integrality gap lower bounds (Θ(b_robust) and Θ(b_global)) suggest the proposed ratios are essentially tight for the given relaxations, limiting room for further improvement without new formulations. Open questions remain: whether linear-time algorithms can be achieved for ROBUST/GLOBAL ECC, whether an O(1)-approximation exists for ROBUST ECC (analogous to the 2-approximation for k-PARTIAL VERTEX COVER), and whether ECC techniques transfer to correlation clustering variants.
