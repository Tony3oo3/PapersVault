## Problem

In adaptive data analysis with correlated samples, the concentrated queries framework of Bassily and Freund [2016] limits the number of accurately answerable queries to O(n), a stark gap from the O(n²) achievable in the i.i.d. setting. It was unclear whether this linear barrier is inherent to the problem or merely a limitation of existing algorithms. This paper proves the barrier is inherent for all known efficient mechanisms (noise-addition and subsampling) and provides a simplified positive result that matches the lower bound.

## Input / Output

The mechanism receives a dataset sample S of size n drawn from a potentially correlated distribution D over X^n, and a sequence of k adaptively chosen statistical queries q: X → [0,1] that must be (ε,γ)-concentrated (i.e., the empirical mean on a fresh sample deviates from the true mean by more than ε with probability at most γ). It outputs a real-valued answer per query: either the empirical mean plus independent Laplace noise (noise-addition) or the empirical mean on a random subsample (subsampling). Outputs are assumed to lie in a bounded range ([-1,2] for noise-addition, [0,1] for subsampling) with finite precision.

## Method

The negative result constructs a distribution where the domain is partitioned into r = 1/ε subsets and each sample contains one element from each subset, repeated εn times, so a single shared index determines the entire sample. The attacker issues k adaptive queries that assign i.i.d. Bernoulli values within one targeted subset, accumulating scores that isolate the true index via a positive-expectation signal (1/6r per round for the correct element, 0 otherwise), then issues a final query identifying the recovered sample. The positive result introduces a three-mechanism thought experiment (real-world, oracle, hybrid) and extends the advanced composition theorem from differential privacy to show the hybrid and oracle transcript distributions remain close over k rounds. Since the real-world and hybrid mechanisms are identical on ε-good sample-transcript pairs, and the oracle mechanism is accurate with high probability by concentration, the real-world mechanism inherits statistical accuracy with k = O(min{1/γ, 1/(ε²[ln(1/ε)]²)}).

## Results

The paper establishes matching upper and lower bounds (up to logarithmic factors) on the number of adaptively chosen concentrated queries that can be accurately answered when data correlations are allowed. The negative result (Theorem 3.2) shows that no Noise or Subsampling (NS) mechanism can answer more than k = Ω(min{1/γ, (1/ε²)·ln(1/(ε·β·γ))}) queries without failing, while the positive result (Theorem 4.10) shows a Laplace noise-addition mechanism achieves (α,β)-accuracy for k = O(min{1/γ, 1/(ε²[ln(1/ε)]²)}) queries. Under Hoeffding-style concentration (γ = 2exp(−2nε²)), both bounds yield k = O(n), in sharp contrast to the O(n²) queries achievable in the i.i.d. setting. The gap between the upper and lower bounds is only a logarithmic factor in 1/ε.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines, so comparisons are between theoretical bounds. The paper's negative result matches the O(n) query barrier established by Bassily and Freund [2016] (via typical stability) and Kontorovich et al. [2022] (via compression), but proves this barrier is inherent for all NS mechanisms rather than merely a limitation of specific algorithms. The positive result improves upon Bassily and Freund's analysis by saving logarithmic factors in the bound on k and by using differential privacy (Laplace mechanism + advanced composition) instead of the more complex typical stability framework. Compared to the i.i.d. setting where O(n²) adaptive queries are answerable (e.g., via DP mechanisms of Dwork et al. [2015b], Steinke and Ullman [2015]), the correlated-data setting with concentrated queries is fundamentally limited to O(n) queries — a quadratic gap that the paper proves is unavoidable under the current framework. The attack technique builds on the adaptive attack of Nissim et al. [2018] for the i.i.d. setting, adapted to exploit the correlation structure in the constructed distribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the concentrated queries framework introduced by Bassily and Freund (2016), who used typical stability to show that O(n) adaptive queries can be answered under data correlations. The positive result departs from their approach by replacing typical stability with a differential-privacy-based analysis (Laplace mechanism plus advanced composition from Dwork et al. 2010), yielding a simpler proof that saves logarithmic factors. The negative result adapts the adaptive attack structure of Nissim et al. (2018) from the i.i.d. setting to correlated data, constructing a distribution where every query is concentrated yet the attacker can still recover the sample. Together, these results close the gap between the best-known upper and lower bounds for NS mechanisms in this framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The O(n) impossibility for noise-addition and subsampling mechanisms is the first negative result for adaptively chosen concentrated queries, establishing that the utility gap versus the i.i.d. setting (O(n²) queries) is inherent under current algorithmic paradigms. This means breaking the linear barrier requires either new structural assumptions on the data distribution or fundamentally new algorithmic techniques beyond independent noise and resampling. The simplified positive result also makes the Bassily-Freund algorithm more accessible and tightens the achievable query count by log factors. A key open question is whether any mechanism (not restricted to NS) can support super-linear queries under Hoeffding-style concentration with correlated data.
