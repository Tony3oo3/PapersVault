## Problem

VLMs for egocentric understanding struggle with fine-grained temporal reasoning about human intent and attention, producing coarse or hallucinated descriptions of current and future activities. Prior gaze-augmented approaches either require gaze as a model input at inference time or use it only as an auxiliary feature, limiting practical deployment. This paper asks whether human gaze can serve purely as a training-time regularization signal to align VLM attention with human visual focus, improving egocentric prediction without any gaze dependency at test time.

## Input / Output

At inference, the model consumes only standard RGB egocentric video frames (downsampled to 1 fps) in sequences spanning a 3-second observation window for activity understanding or a 5-second window for future prediction (with a 2-second or 5-second anticipation horizon). Gaze pixel coordinates, optical flow, and heatmaps are used exclusively during training and are entirely absent at test time. The output is a fine-grained natural-language caption (typically 15–20 words) describing the current activity or predicting the upcoming action, including specific objects, spatial relationships, and movement cues.

## Method

The core contribution is a modular gaze-regularized attention block inserted between the visual encoder and language decoder of transformer-based VLMs. Raw gaze coordinates are first converted to spatial heatmaps via Gaussian smoothing, then temporally aggregated over a ~200 ms window using bidirectional optical-flow consistency (RAFT) to discard occluded or unreliable gaze points, yielding a robust aggregated heatmap. This pixel-level heatmap is pooled into patch-wise distributions matching the ViT patch grid, and a KL-divergence loss (weighted by λ) is added to the standard cross-entropy training objective to pull the model's attention weights toward the human gaze distribution. Because the regularization acts only on attention during training, the resulting model requires no gaze hardware or optical-flow computation at inference, and the same block can be plugged into OpenFlamingo, LaViLa, InternVL, and OpenLLaVA with minimal architectural changes.

## Results

On Ego4D, the gaze-regularized framework improves semantic similarity scores by 8.9–10.5% for future event prediction and 4.9–6.9% for current activity understanding across five VLM architectures (OpenFlamingo, Modified OpenFlamingo, LaViLa Narrator, InternVL, OpenLLaVA), all using RGB-only inputs at inference. The largest future-prediction gain is +10.5% (Modified OpenFlamingo: 0.6155 → 0.7200) and the largest activity-understanding gain is +6.9% (LaViLa Narrator: 0.6576 → 0.7268). On the out-of-distribution EGTEA+ Gaze dataset, gains of 7–9% (future prediction) and 5–6% (activity understanding) are maintained without fine-tuning. A human evaluation shows the hallucination rate (C₁ score) drops from 0.205 to 0.140, and top-10 attention–gaze overlap improves from 42% to 68%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is between each VLM architecture trained with and without gaze regularization (same data, same architecture, RGB-only inference). The gaze-regularized model wins on every architecture for both tasks: future prediction gains range from +8.9% (LaViLa Narrator) to +10.5% (Modified OpenFlamingo), and activity understanding gains range from +4.9% (OpenLLaVA) to +6.9% (LaViLa Narrator). In a secondary sanity-check (Appendix Table 8), the trained gaze-regularized OpenFlamingo (0.7505) substantially outperforms zero-shot pretrained VLMs on the same future-prediction task: InternVL-2.5-1B scores 0.1596 and OpenFlamingo-4B scores 0.1878, indicating that task-specific training with gaze supervision is critical rather than relying on pretraining alone. On the out-of-distribution EGTEA+ Gaze benchmark, the gaze-regularized models retain 7–9% future-prediction and 5–6% activity-understanding advantages over their base counterparts, confirming that the learned attention alignment generalizes beyond the Ego4D training domain.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work using gaze as a training-time supervisory signal for egocentric understanding, most directly building on Min and Corso (2020), who integrated gaze into attention via a VAE for activity recognition. It departs from that work by (1) targeting large-scale VLMs for fine-grained text generation rather than classification, (2) replacing the VAE-based feature modulation with a simple KL-divergence regularization on patch-level attention weights, and (3) confining optical flow (adapted from Hur and Roth's MirrorFlow consistency check) to pre-processing for occlusion-aware gaze aggregation rather than requiring it at inference. Unlike Voila-A, which aligns cross-attention with gaze at both training and test time, this method eliminates all gaze dependency at inference, making it deployable with standard RGB inputs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The modular, training-only design makes gaze regularization practically deployable in assistive robotics and wearable AI where eye-tracking hardware is unavailable at test time, and the consistent 5–11% gains across five heterogeneous VLM architectures suggest the technique is broadly applicable. The observed reduction in visual hallucinations (C₁ from 0.205 to 0.140) hints that gaze-grounded attention can serve as a lightweight anti-hallucination mechanism, though the single-evaluator, 200-sample protocol limits confidence. Open questions include optimal λ calibration across tasks and scales, whether the approach benefits from larger gaze-annotated corpora (e.g., VDE's ~240 hours), and whether joint gaze-action modeling could further close the gap between observation and prediction horizons.
