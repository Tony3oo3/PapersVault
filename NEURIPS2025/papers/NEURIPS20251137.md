## Problem

Search-augmented LLMs struggle with complex reasoning tasks that require multi-hop retrieval and integration of multiple evidence pieces beyond simple span extraction. Existing approaches either rely on single-turn retrieval, depend on expensive closed-source LLMs for iterative prompting, or use memory-intensive online RL that is impractical for deployment. The paper addresses the lack of an efficient, data-centric training recipe that jointly enhances both retrieval decomposition and reasoning without requiring intermediate annotations or proprietary frontier models.

## Input / Output

Given a complex question q and a corpus (e.g., Wikipedia), the system first decomposes q into a sequence of n subquestion templates, retrieves k=10 passages per subquestion (capped at N=15 total documents), and then generates intermediate answers stepwise before producing a final answer. The output is a structured trajectory: subquestion sequence → per-subquestion intermediate answers → final answer, with the solver producing a full rationale only for the final answer to keep input contexts manageable. Evaluation uses exact match for QA/fact verification and accuracy via official scripts for document-level reasoning.

## Method

AceSearcher trains a single LLM to alternate between two roles—a decomposer ρ that breaks complex questions into subquestions and a solver π that integrates retrieved contexts to produce intermediate and final answers—via cooperative self-play. Training is two-stage: (1) SFT on 180K examples mixing context-rich QA, question decomposition (GSM8K, ConvFinQA, StrategyQA), and chain-of-thought/program-of-thought data; (2) preference-based reinforcement fine-tuning (RFT) using iterative multi-turn DPO, where preference pairs are constructed from m×m' rollout trajectories scored solely by final-answer exact match plus a format reward, eliminating the need for intermediate supervision. The key insight is that better decompositions yield more accurate solver outputs, so the decomposer is optimized to maximize the solver's expected reward while the solver is reinforced to produce correct answers given any decomposition. The KL-regularized joint objective admits a closed-form optimal policy, and the iterative DPO algorithm is proven to converge to the true optimum under regularity conditions.

## Results

On multi-hop QA and fact verification (Table 1), AceSearcher-32B achieves the highest overall average EM of 60.7, outperforming the strongest open-source baseline (CORAG 8B Inference Scaling, 53.5 EM) by 7.2 points and proprietary IRCOT few-shot (50.8 EM) by 9.9 points. On DocMath-Eval (Table 2), AceSearcher-32B reaches 66.1 average accuracy, nearly matching DeepSeek-V3 685B (66.4) while using less than 5% of its parameters. At smaller scales, AceSearcher-8B (59.6 Avg.All EM) surpasses 70B-parameter baselines such as R1-Distill 70B (43.8) and Llama-4 Maverick (41.2), and AceSearcher-1.5B (49.4) matches or exceeds 8B baselines like Llama-3.1-it 8B (13.3) and R1-Distill 8B (23.3).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AceSearcher-32B (60.7 Avg.All EM) outperforms all open-source baselines by a wide margin: it beats CORAG 8B Inference Scaling (53.5) by 7.2 points, Search-o1 32B (44.0) by 16.7 points, and Plan-RAG 32B (45.2) by 15.5 points. Against proprietary systems, it surpasses GPT-4o RAG (34.4) by 26.3 points and IRCOT few-shot with GPT-4o (50.8) by 9.9 points. At the 8B scale, AceSearcher-8B (59.6) exceeds R1-Distill 70B (43.8) by 15.8 points and Llama-4 Maverick 17B×128 (41.2) by 18.4 points, demonstrating that the self-play training recipe compensates for a 9× parameter gap. On DocMath-Eval, AceSearcher-32B (66.1) nearly matches DeepSeek-V3 685B (66.4) with under 5% of the parameters, and AceSearcher-14B (62.4) surpasses 72B models including AceMath 72B (60.9) and Qwen-2.5-Math 72B (60.4). The 1.5B variant (49.4) matches or exceeds all 8B baselines in Table 1, with the notable exception of CORAG 8B Inference Scaling which benefits from test-time compute scaling. Ablations confirm that both SFT and RFT stages are necessary, and that iterative DPO outperforms RAFT, RESTEM, offline DPO, and SimPO alternatives.

## Relation to Prior Work

AceSearcher extends the DPO framework (Rafailov et al., 2023) into a multi-turn, iterative preference optimization scheme that jointly trains a decomposer and solver within a single LLM, building on the KL-regularized RL objective from RLHF (Ouyang et al., 2022) to derive closed-form optimal policies for both roles. It departs from concurrent RL-based search LLMs (Search-R1, R1-Searcher, DeepResearcher) that rely on memory-intensive online RL and QA-only supervision, instead adopting a data-centric two-stage recipe (SFT on mixed reasoning/search data + iterative DPO with final-answer rewards) that avoids intermediate annotations. The cooperative self-play framing also distinguishes it from prior self-play work focused on alignment or theorem proving, tailoring the paradigm specifically to reasoning-intensive RAG.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The parameter-efficiency results are practically significant: a 32B model matching DeepSeek-V3 (685B) on document-level financial reasoning, and a 1.5B model rivaling 10×-larger baselines on multi-hop QA, suggest that task-structured training can substitute for raw scale in search-augmented settings. Open questions include whether joint optimization of the retriever and reasoner (currently fixed) yields further gains, whether the decomposition pipeline generalizes to open-ended generation or real-time tool use, and whether the iterative DPO approach can be replaced by more expressive RL formulations without the associated memory overhead. The inference latency cost of multi-step decomposition remains a practical constraint for latency-sensitive deployments.
