## Problem

Offline robust two-player zero-sum Markov games (RTZMGs) require finding Nash equilibria that remain stable under worst-case environmental perturbations, but existing algorithms like P²M²PO suffer from a sample complexity of Õ(C_r H⁵ S² AB/ε²) that scales quadratically in states and actions and ignores the influence of uncertainty levels. The paper asks whether an algorithm can achieve near-optimal sample complexity in both state and action spaces while explicitly accounting for distributional uncertainty under partial state-action coverage.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes an offline dataset of K independent trajectories generated by an unknown behavior policy (μⁿ, νⁿ) in a nominal MDP, along with user-specified TV-distance uncertainty radii σ⁺ and σ⁻ for the max- and min-players. It outputs an ε-robust Nash equilibrium policy pair (μ̂, ν̂) where μ̂ is the max-player's policy and ν̂ is the min-player's policy, together with estimated robust value and Q-functions at every state-action pair and time step. The method operates under a robust unilateral clipped concentrability assumption (C_r*) that permits partial coverage, meaning the dataset need not visit every state-action pair with uniform frequency.

## Method

RTZ-VI-LCB is a model-based algorithm that performs backward value iteration with lower confidence bounds, where each step computes an optimistic robust Q-value by combining the empirical reward, a worst-case transition expectation over the TV uncertainty set (solved via a one-dimensional dual over a clipping level α), and a data-driven Bernstein-style penalty that captures the variance structure of the next-step value. A two-stage subsampling procedure first splits the dataset into two halves, then trims and resamples transitions to produce a distributionally equivalent set of independent samples, decoupling the statistical dependencies inherent in trajectory data. The key theoretical contribution is the robust unilateral clipped concentrability coefficient C_r*, which measures distribution shift between the behavior policy and the single optimal robust policies under uncertainty, yielding a tighter bound than the C_r used in P²M²PO. This achieves a sample complexity of Õ(C_r* H⁴ S(A+B)/ε² · f(σ⁺,σ⁻,H)), which is provably optimal in S and {A,B} and matches an information-theoretic lower bound, and extends to multi-player general-sum games via Multi-RTZ-VI-LCB.

## Results

Theoretically, RTZ-VI-LCB achieves a sample complexity of Õ(C_r* H⁴ S(A+B)/ε² · f(σ⁺,σ⁻,H)) for finding an ε-optimal robust Nash equilibrium, improving upon P²M²PO's Õ(C_r H⁵ S² AB/ε²) in all key dimensions: H⁴ vs H⁵, linear S vs S², and (A+B) vs AB. Numerically, on a tabular TZMG with S=50, A=B=2, H=100, RTZ-VI-LCB achieves value gaps of 3.17, 2.15, 0.23, and 0.08 at log(KH)=6,7,8,9 respectively, consistently outperforming P²M²PO (3.60, 2.25, 0.24, 0.09). A log-log regression on the value gap vs. sample size yields a slope of −0.4877, closely matching the theoretical O(1/√K) prediction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against P²M²PO [5], the prior state-of-the-art for offline robust TZMGs. RTZ-VI-LCB strictly dominates P²M²PO in sample complexity: it reduces the horizon dependence from H⁵ to H⁴, the state dependence from S² to S, and the action dependence from AB to (A+B), while additionally incorporating the uncertainty level f(σ⁺,σ⁻,H) that P²M²PO ignores. Numerically, RTZ-VI-LCB achieves lower value gaps than P²M²PO at every dataset size tested (e.g., 0.0819 vs. 0.0890 at log(KH)=9), confirming the theoretical advantage in practice. The paper also compares against a non-optimistic baseline RTZ-VI (standard robust value iteration without confidence bounds), which RTZ-VI-LCB consistently outperforms across all states and all sample sizes (Figure 1a). The information-theoretic lower bound confirms that RTZ-VI-LCB is optimal in S and {A,B}, making it the first provably optimal algorithm for offline RTZMGs under partial coverage.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the offline robust two-player zero-sum Markov game (RTZMG) framework established by P²M²PO [Blanchet et al., NeurIPS 2024], which achieved sample complexity Õ(C_r H⁵ S² AB/ε²) but suffered from the curse of multiagency (quadratic in S, product in A·B). The algorithmic design draws on the Bernstein-style penalty and two-stage subsampling from single-agent model-based offline RL [Li et al., Annals of Statistics 2024], adapted here with a novel robust unilateral clipped concentrability coefficient C_r* that is strictly tighter than the C_r used in P²M²PO. The dual formulation for the TV-distance uncertainty set follows Iyengar's robust dynamic programming [2005], and the optimism/pessimism principle for offline RL is inherited from Jin et al. [ICML 2021].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first algorithm for offline RTZMGs with sample complexity optimal in both state space S and action spaces {A,B}, closing a gap that prior work (P²M²PO) left open, and the matching information-theoretic lower bound certifies that no algorithm can do better. The extension to multi-player general-sum games breaking the curse of multiagency (linear in ΣA_i rather than product) is a notable conceptual advance. However, the results are confined to tabular settings with TV-distance uncertainty sets; extending to function approximation, other divergences (KL, Wasserstein), or non-rectangular uncertainty sets remains open. The empirical validation is limited to a single small-scale tabular instance (S=50, A=B=2, H=100), leaving practical scalability questions unanswered.
