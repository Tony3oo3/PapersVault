## Problem

The non-Euclidean geometry of graph structures fundamentally impedes cross-graph knowledge transfer, and existing approaches lack (i) benchmarks for aligning structural semantics across domains, (ii) a principled mechanism to avoid negative transfer from geometric heterogeneity, and (iii) parameter-efficient transfer that avoids multi-task auxiliary overhead. The paper posits that statistically recurrent connectivity patterns (topological primitives/motifs) form domain-agnostic structural 'textures' analogous to transferable texture units in visual style transfer, but no systematic framework existed to exploit this for universal graph structure learning.

## Input / Output

The method serializes each graph into paired sequences: a topological primitive distribution vector (7 motif scores computed over 1-hop and 2-hop neighborhoods, including triangle/quadrilateral/pentagon frequencies and homogeneous cycle probability) and an LLM-generated textual annotation (STA) of ~126 tokens per node. These sequences are vectorized via a shared bag-of-words model (length 85 for text, 10 for motif scores) so that all 18 datasets share a common vocabulary and representation space. Outputs are task-specific: node classification accuracy, link prediction (Hits@100, Hits@30, MRR), graph classification (F1, AP, ROCAUC), or graph regression (MAE), produced by a lightweight task decoder on top of fused structural-node representations.

## Method

G²SN-Transfer comprises three components. TopoGraph-Mapping bijectively maps 7 topological primitive distributions to LLM-generated textual descriptors via a dictionary, converting non-Euclidean graphs into transferable text-motif sequence pairs (the STA-18 benchmark). G²SN is a dual-stream Mamba-based SSM where the ODE parameters (A, B, C, Δ) are data-adaptive functions of both the textual annotation sequence and the motif score sequence, enabling structure-controlled selection that jointly governs information flow; a closed-form ZOH solution provides convergence guarantees (Theorem 4.1). AdaCross-Transfer injects pre-trained structural knowledge into downstream GNNs via a cross-attention module whose weights are computed from per-node motif distribution scores (Eq. 10), dynamically reweighting structural versus node-feature contributions; the transfer encoder shares n−1 layers from pre-trained G²SN with the first layer of the target GNN, adding fewer than 9% parameters and supporting both full fine-tuning and frozen transfer without gradient computation on the backbone.

## Results

On the LRGB structural learning benchmark, G²SN achieves state-of-the-art performance on 4 of 5 tasks: Peptides-Func AP 0.6824, Peptides-Struct MAE 0.2462, PascalVOC-SP F1 0.4199, and COCO-SP F1 0.4022, with 2.5%–4.8% improvements over the strongest sparse-attention baselines. On the 13-dataset structural knowledge transfer evaluation, AdaCross-Transfer yields a 5.2% average relative gain, with peak improvements of +14.02% Hits@100 on ogbl-ppa and +12.37% Accuracy on ogbn-arxiv. The frozen (zero-training) transfer mode also delivers consistent gains, confirming that static topological primitive integration alone provides useful inductive bias.

## Baselines & Comparisons

G²SN outperforms all traditional GNN baselines (GCN, GIN, GatedGCN) by large margins across every LRGB task, with the gap most pronounced on PascalVOC-SP F1 (0.4199 vs. 0.2873 for GatedGCN) and COCO-SP F1 (0.4022 vs. 0.2641). Against the GraphGPS family, G²SN beats GPS+Transformer on 4/5 tasks (the 5th being OOM for Transformer) and surpasses GPS+Performer and GPS+BigBird on all five, with the largest margin over BigBird on PascalVOC-SP F1 (+14.4 points). The closest competitor is Exphormer, which G²SN wins on 4/5 tasks but narrowly loses on MALNET-TINY Accuracy (0.9391 vs. 0.9422, a 0.31-point gap). In the transfer experiments (Table 2), G²SN's AdaCross-Transfer consistently improves every baseline GNN it is paired with—GCN, GIN, GraphSAGE, SIGN, and GraphSAINT—across all 13 downstream datasets, with the frozen mode alone providing gains of 0.33–5.61 points depending on dataset and task.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper draws a conceptual analogy between texture transfer in neural style transfer and structural knowledge transfer across graphs, repurposing motif/graphlet distribution profiles (from gl2vec, HONE, MPool) as 'topological primitives' that can be serialized into text via LLM-generated annotations. Architecturally, it adapts the Mamba state-space model into a dual-stream sequence network (G²SN) that jointly processes textual annotations and motif score sequences, departing from prior graphlet methods that treat subgraph patterns as isolated features rather than transferable sequential units. The AdaCross-Transfer mechanism extends parameter-efficient transfer learning (LoRA-style adapters) to the graph domain by introducing motif-aware cross-attention weighting between structural and node-feature streams, building on but generalizing the frozen/fine-tuned transfer paradigm from vision-language models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The STA-18 benchmark (18 graph datasets with aligned topological-textual pairs) fills a genuine gap in cross-domain structural semantics evaluation, and the density-correlation finding (ρ=0.82) provides actionable guidance for when structural transfer is most effective. However, the framework is constrained to two-hop neighborhoods (acknowledged limitation), heterogeneous graphs show reduced transfer efficiency, and the 5.2% average improvement, while consistent, is modest relative to the substantial architectural complexity. Open questions include whether higher-order topological primitives would close the heterogeneity gap, and whether the LLM-annotation bottleneck (214 hours for UniKG-STA) limits practical adoption at scale.
