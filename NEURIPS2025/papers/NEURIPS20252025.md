## Problem

The dominant temporal forecast (TF) paradigm encodes and decodes time series directly in the time domain, where entangled step-wise dependencies hinder forecasting performance. Existing transformed-domain alternatives (Fourier, wavelet) rely on fixed, dataset-independent bases that fail to exploit dataset-specific temporal correlation structure. Additionally, the necessity of multi-head self-attention for modeling multivariate dependencies in time series has not been rigorously challenged.

## Input / Output

The model takes a multivariate time series X ∈ R^{N×T} (N variates, T lookback steps) and produces predictions for τ future steps in R^{N×τ}. Two orthogonal matrices Q_i ∈ R^{T×T} and Q_o ∈ R^{τ×τ} are pre-computed from the training set's temporal Pearson correlation matrix via eigenvalue decomposition and remain fixed throughout training and inference. A dimension extension step computes the outer product with a learnable vector φ_d ∈ R^{16} to enhance expressiveness before the orthogonal projection.

## Method

OLinear operates in an orthogonally transformed domain: the input series is projected onto the eigenvectors of the temporal Pearson correlation matrix (OrthoTrans), yielding a decorrelated feature representation where linear encoding becomes more effective. For multivariate dependency modeling, the Cross-Series Learner (CSL) employs a NormLin layer—whose weight matrix is made positive via Softplus and row-wise L1-normalized—replacing self-attention while halving FLOPs. The Intra-Series Learner (ISL) uses a two-layer MLP with GELU to capture sequential dynamics. The full pipeline is: RevIN → dimension extension → OrthoTrans → Linear Encode → L stacked (CSL+ISL) blocks → Linear Decode → inverse OrthoTrans → RevIN DeNorm. Both OrthoTrans and NormLin function as plug-in modules that consistently improve existing Transformer-based forecasters (e.g., 5.1% MSE reduction for iTransformer, 10.3% for PatchTST) and scale to large decoder-only models like Timer.

## Results

On long-term forecasting (Table 2, averaged over 4 prediction lengths), OLinear achieves the best MSE on 5 out of 12 datasets and the best MAE on 9 out of 12, including dominant margins on all four PEMS traffic datasets (e.g., PEMS07: MSE 0.077 vs. next-best 0.084) and ECL (MSE 0.159, MAE 0.248). On short-term forecasting (Table 3), OLinear wins 4 MSE and 10 MAE out of 12 datasets, with particularly large gains on ILI (MSE 1.429 vs. 1.715 for iTransformer) and Unemp (MSE 0.729 vs. 0.565 for DLinear, though DLinear wins there). As a plug-in, NormLin reduces MSE by 6.7% in iTransformer and 10.3% in PatchTST, while OrthoTrans improves iTransformer by 5.1% and PatchTST by 10.1% average MSE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OLinear achieves the best MSE on all four PEMS traffic datasets (e.g., PEMS07: 0.077 vs. Leddam 0.084, iTransformer 0.101) and on ECL (0.159 vs. Leddam 0.169, iTransformer 0.178), while TimeMixer++ retains the best MSE on ETT (0.349 vs. 0.359), Weather (0.226 vs. 0.237), Solar (0.203 vs. 0.215), and Traffic (0.416 vs. 0.451). However, OLinear wins the MAE metric on 9 of 12 long-term datasets, including large margins on PEMS03 (0.199 vs. 0.210) and Solar (0.217 vs. 0.258). Among linear-based baselines, OLinear substantially outperforms DLinear (e.g., PEMS07 MSE 0.077 vs. 0.329) and TimeMixer (PEMS03 MSE 0.095 vs. 0.167). As a plug-in module, NormLin consistently beats multi-head self-attention when swapped into iTransformer (6.7% MSE reduction) and PatchTST (10.3% MSE reduction), and also outperforms Reformer, Flowformer, FLatten, and Mamba attention variants in Table 8. OrthoTrans as a plug-in improves iTransformer by 5.1% and PatchTST by 10.1% average MSE (Table 5), confirming its dataset-adaptive advantage over fixed Fourier or wavelet bases (Table 4 shows 5.5% MSE reduction over Fourier on PEMS03).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the transformed-domain forecasting line (FilterNet, Fredformer) by replacing fixed, dataset-agnostic bases (Fourier, wavelet) with a data-adaptive orthogonal transformation (OrthoTrans) derived from the temporal Pearson correlation matrix. For representation learning, it builds on the linear-model tradition (DLinear, RLinear) and the dimension-extension trick from FilterNet, but introduces NormLin—a linear layer whose weights are constrained to be positive and row-stochastic via Softplus + L1 normalization—inspired by the structural properties of self-attention matrices. The key departure is from the Transformer-based forecasting paradigm (iTransformer, PatchTST, CARD), where NormLin is shown to replace multi-head self-attention with equal or better accuracy at roughly half the FLOPs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a simple normalized linear layer can match or exceed multi-head self-attention across multiple Transformer architectures and even large-scale pre-training (Timer) offers a practical, efficient alternative for time series model design. OrthoTrans and NormLin both function as plug-in modules that improve existing forecasters without architectural overhaul, lowering the barrier to adoption. Open questions include whether the pre-computed orthogonal matrices remain valid under distribution shift, whether the data-adaptive basis is truly necessary versus simpler fixed transforms, and whether the NormLin advantage persists in non-forecasting time series tasks (classification, anomaly detection).
