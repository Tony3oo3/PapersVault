## Problem

Policy gradient methods for reinforcement learning with general utilities (RLGU), where the objective is a nonlinear function of the state-action occupancy measure, lack global optimality guarantees beyond the tabular setting. Existing tabular results rely on hidden convexity that does not connect to the gradient domination framework developed for standard expected-return RL, and the few works extending beyond tabular settings only establish first-order stationarity rather than global convergence. This paper closes both gaps by proving a gradient domination inequality for RLGU objectives and providing global optimality guarantees with sample complexity independent of the state-action space size.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes finite-length trajectories sampled from an MDP under the current policy, a known general utility function F (with accessible gradient ∇_λ F with respect to the occupancy measure), and i.i.d. state samples drawn from the occupancy distribution via a geometric-horizon sampler. It outputs the final policy parameter vector θ_T that maximizes F(λ^{π_θ}). A key structural detail is that the pseudo-reward r_t = ∇_λ F(λ̂_t) is computed only at visited state-action pairs along sampled trajectories, not over the full state-action space, making the procedure scalable. The occupancy measure is approximated by a parametric probability distribution (e.g., softmax over neural-network features) rather than a full table.

## Method

The core insight is that the RLGU policy gradient ∇_θ F(λ(θ)) equals the standard expected-return policy gradient evaluated at the pseudo-reward ∇_λ F(λ(θ)), which enables a direct extension of the gradient domination inequality from linear RL to general concave utilities (Theorem 1). For large state-action spaces, the proposed PG-OMA algorithm alternates between (i) estimating the occupancy measure via maximum likelihood estimation within a parametric function class of dimension m, and (ii) performing stochastic policy gradient ascent using the REINFORCE estimator with the MLE-based pseudo-reward. The MLE step yields a total-variation error bound of Õ(√(m/n)) that is independent of the state-action space cardinality, in contrast to mean-square-error approaches that incur an extra |S| factor. Under concavity of F and a policy overparametrization assumption, the algorithm achieves a last-iterate global optimality guarantee with total sample complexity Õ(mε⁻⁴), while for nonconcave utilities it provides a first-order stationarity guarantee at Õ(mε⁻²).

## Results

The paper establishes a gradient domination inequality (Theorem 1) for the RLGU objective in the tabular setting, extending the standard expected-return result to general concave utilities. For the large state-action space setting, the proposed PG-OMA algorithm achieves a first-order stationarity sample complexity of $\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(m\epsilon^{-2})$ (Corollary 1) and a global optimality sample complexity of $\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(m\epsilon^{-4})$ (Corollary 2) for concave utilities, where $m$ is the dimension of the occupancy measure function approximation class. Crucially, both bounds depend only on $m$ and not on the state-action space size $|\mathcal{S}|\cdot|\mathcal{A}|$, making the algorithm scalable. The MLE-based occupancy estimation (Proposition 1) requires $\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(m\epsilon^{-2})$ samples to achieve $\epsilon$-accuracy in $l_1$-norm.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper is the first to simultaneously achieve (i) global optimality guarantees, (ii) operation beyond the tabular setting, and (iii) no dependence on state-action space size in the sample complexity bound. Compared to Barakat et al. (2023, §5), which is the closest prior work addressing large state-action spaces, this paper upgrades the guarantee from first-order stationarity to last-iterate global optimality and replaces MSE-based occupancy estimation with MLE, eliminating the hidden $1/\rho_{\min}$ (i.e., $|\mathcal{S}|$) dependence present in the MSE approach. Against Huang and Jiang (2024), which also uses MLE but focuses on finite-horizon expected return, this paper targets infinite-horizon discounted RLGU and provides a last-iterate global convergence rate rather than a best-iterate rate. Compared to the tabular-setting works (Hazan et al. 2019, Zhang et al. 2020/2021, Zahavy et al. 2021), the proposed method removes the count-based Monte Carlo occupancy estimation bottleneck. Against Mutti et al. (2023), which addresses a different finite-trial formulation with a model-based algorithm requiring a planning oracle, this paper provides a model-free PG algorithm with optimization guarantees and no feature-vector access requirement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the gradient domination inequality established for standard expected-return RL (Agarwal et al., 2021; Bhandari and Russo, 2024) to the RLGU setting, providing a new structural property that connects RLGU policy gradients to the broader PG analysis literature. It departs from the 'hidden convexity' approach of Zhang et al. (2021) for tabular RLGU by showing that gradient domination is the right lens, and from Barakat et al. (2023) for large-scale RLGU by replacing MSE occupancy estimation with MLE, arguing the latter is fundamentally necessary for scalability. The MLE statistical guarantee builds on Huang et al. (2023) for low-rank MDPs, adapted here to the infinite-horizon discounted setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The gradient domination result (Theorem 1) opens a path to analyzing non-tabular policy parameterizations (e.g., softmax) for RLGU, which the hidden convexity approach could not handle. The MLE-based algorithm (PG-OMA) provides the first global optimality guarantees for RLGU beyond the tabular setting with sample complexity depending only on the function approximation dimension m rather than the state-action space size. Key limitations include the realizability assumption (true occupancy must lie in the approximation class), the restrictive policy overparametrization assumption (Assumption 5) needed for global optimality, and the absence of experimental validation. Open questions include extension to continuous state spaces, average reward settings, and relaxing the overparametrization condition.
