## Problem

Existing vision-language pretraining methods for embodied agents (e.g., LIV, DecisionNCE) rely on a rigid goal-reaching assumption that aligns language instructions with the final or future frames of a video. This assumption is easily violated in noisy egocentric human action videos where actions may terminate early or include irrelevant trailing content, leading to erroneous vision-language associations. The paper asks whether one can learn ordered and continuous vision-language representations that capture the intrinsic temporal structure of actions without imposing explicit goal-frame constraints.

## Input / Output

During pretraining, the method consumes 10 uniformly sampled frames per video clip from EPIC-KITCHENS-100 along with their coarse-grained text annotations, processed by a CLIP-initialized ResNet-50 visual encoder and CLIP transformer text encoder into shared d-dimensional embeddings. For downstream robot control, the frozen encoders take a single RGB camera image, a language instruction, and proprioceptive state (joint positions/velocities) as input, and a lightweight MLP policy maps the concatenated embeddings to a 6-DoF end-effector displacement plus gripper action at 20 Hz. The same embeddings can also be repurposed to produce a dense per-frame visual reward via cosine similarity between the current visual embedding and the language goal embedding.

## Method

AcTOL introduces two complementary losses that jointly enforce temporal ordering and continuity without goal-frame assumptions. The Vision-Language Ordering (VLO) loss reformulates the tNCE contrastive objective by defining a semantic alignment score as the negative L2 distance between the cosine similarities of two frames to the instruction, then constructing negatives as frames temporally more distant from the anchor than the positive, so that closer frames are pushed to have smaller VL-similarity gaps. The Brownian Bridge (BB) loss models the sequence of visual embeddings within a sampled interval as a Brownian bridge process, penalizing each intermediate frame's deviation from the linear interpolation between the interval endpoints with a time-dependent variance that peaks mid-interval, thereby enforcing smooth local transitions. The total objective is L_VLO + λ·L_BB (λ=0.1), and the paper proves three theorems: a tight lower bound on L_VLO that guarantees the VLO ordering property, a continuity bound showing small embedding changes yield bounded alignment-score changes under Lipschitz similarity, and a robustness bound showing alignment scores shift by at most 2Cδ_l under language perturbations of magnitude δ_l.

## Results

On Franka Kitchen, AcTOL achieves 42.60% success rate with only 5 demonstrations, outperforming the strongest baseline DecisionNCE (25.33%) by 48.95% relative gain. With 15 and 25 demos, AcTOL reaches 61.80% and 64.60% respectively, maintaining gains of 43.06% and 24.40% over DecisionNCE. On Metaworld, AcTOL achieves 53.81%, 74.13%, and 81.13% success rates at 5/15/25 demos, consistently surpassing all baselines by 14–16% relative improvement. On the real Unitree D1 robot arm, AcTOL reaches 50%, 80%, and 90% success rates on Pick Cup, Open [X] Drawer, and Close [X] Drawer tasks, roughly doubling the performance of the next-best method (DecisionNCE at 20%, 40%, 60%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AcTOL outperforms all four baselines across every evaluated setting. Against CLIP (vanilla frozen embeddings), AcTOL gains 31–42 percentage points in success rate, confirming that temporal coherence pre-training is essential beyond static image-text alignment. Against R3M (pretrained on the much larger Ego4D dataset), AcTOL still leads by 14–36 points, showing that temporal structure matters more than raw data scale. Against LIV (which aligns language with the final frame), AcTOL improves by 19–42 points, particularly in low-data regimes where LIV's rigid goal-reaching assumption is most vulnerable to noisy video endings. Against DecisionNCE (the strongest baseline, aligning language with the initial-to-final transition), AcTOL achieves 14–49% relative gains, with the largest improvements under data scarcity (5 demos) in Franka Kitchen, where DecisionNCE's goal-reaching heuristic is most likely to be misled by irrelevant trailing frames.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reformulates the time-contrastive learning (tNCE) objective from Sermanet et al. (ICRA 2018) into a Vision-Language Ordering (VLO) loss that contrasts frames by relative temporal distance rather than enforcing alignment with a designated goal frame. It departs from the goal-reaching assumption shared by R3M, LIV, and DecisionNCE, which align language with the final or future frames under the rigid premise that actions monotonically approach their target. The Brownian bridge continuity constraint is borrowed from stochastic process theory and applied here for the first time to regularize intermediate visual embeddings in video pre-training, while the theoretical proof of the ordering property adapts the rank-n-contrast analysis from Zha et al. (NeurIPS 2023) to a triplet formulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By removing the goal-reaching assumption, AcTOL addresses a practical failure mode in noisy egocentric human videos where actions terminate early or include irrelevant trailing content, making it a more robust pre-training recipe for embodied agents. The theoretical guarantees on ordering, continuity, and bounded sensitivity to language perturbations provide a principled foundation that prior heuristic methods lack. However, the authors acknowledge that the coherent-progression assumption may break down for repetitive or cyclic behaviors, and the method has only been validated on manipulation tasks with a single pre-training dataset (EPIC-KITCHENS-100), leaving open questions about scalability to longer-horizon or multi-embodiment settings.
