## Problem

Using vision-language models to extract reward signals for RL in visual environments remains challenging: embedding-based methods (e.g., CLIP) produce noisy rewards due to domain mismatch, while preference-based methods (e.g., RL-VLM-F) still suffer from VLM comparison errors and require many queries to train a well-shaped reward function. GoalLadder targets two specific gaps: robustness to noisy VLM feedback and query efficiency, without relying on environment code or non-visual state representations.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single natural language instruction (e.g., 'the robotic arm is to open the green drawer') and RGB image observations from the environment as input. The VLM (Gemini 2.0 Flash) is queried with pairs of images plus the instruction, returning a ternary comparison (better/worse/equivalent). The output is a trained SAC policy producing continuous actions; the reward at each step is the negative Euclidean distance between the current observation's VAE embedding and the top-rated goal's embedding, with the VAE trained unsupervised on the agent's own collected observations. No environment modifications (e.g., removing the robot from images) are applied.

## Method

GoalLadder incrementally discovers and ranks candidate goal states using a VLM, then trains an RL agent to approach the top-ranked goal in a learned embedding space. A candidate goal buffer (capped at 10 entries) stores image–rating pairs; new observations are admitted only if the VLM judges them closer to the language-specified goal than the current top-rated candidate. Ratings are updated via an ELO-based system (C=400, T=32) over pairwise VLM comparisons, which absorbs noisy feedback by weighting updates according to expected outcomes rather than trusting any single comparison. The reward is defined as the negative L2 distance in a small convolutional VAE latent space (16-dim bottleneck, β=0.1) trained on unlabelled agent observations, so the reward generalises to unseen states without additional VLM queries. SAC is used as the RL backbone with periodic relabeling of the replay buffer (every 5000 steps) to handle the non-stationary reward as the target goal shifts.

## Results

GoalLadder achieves an average final success rate of ~95% across seven continuous-control tasks (CartPole, MountainCar, and five Metaworld manipulation tasks), compared to ~45% for the best competitor (RL-VLM-F). The method nearly matches the oracle agent with ground-truth reward and convincingly surpasses it on the Drawer Open task. In terms of VLM query efficiency, GoalLadder solves all Metaworld tasks after only ~4,500 queries, whereas PEBBLE (a preference-based method using ground-truth preferences) requires ~15,000 labels for comparable performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GoalLadder decisively outperforms all three baselines. Against RL-VLM-F [11] (the strongest competitor, also using Gemini 2.0 Flash for pairwise image comparisons), GoalLadder achieves ~95% average success versus ~45%; RL-VLM-F solves only the simpler tasks (CartPole, MountainCar, Drawer Close) but fails on the more complex Metaworld tasks due to its sensitivity to noisy preference labels and higher feedback requirements. Against VLM-RM [16] (CLIP-based embedding similarity as reward), GoalLadder wins across all tasks, as VLM-RM's noisy reward signal from the CLIP training-data mismatch prevents effective learning in nearly every environment. Against RoboCLIP [15] (video-language model similarity), GoalLadder similarly dominates, with RoboCLIP failing to learn in most tasks. Notably, GoalLadder also matches or exceeds the oracle (ground-truth reward) agent, surpassing it on Drawer Open where the oracle agent learns to reach the drawer but not to pull the handle.

## Relation to Prior Work

GoalLadder sits at the intersection of two lines of work on VLM-guided RL: embedding-based reward definition (VLM-RM, RoboCLIP) and preference-based reward learning (RL-VLM-F). It departs from both by (i) using an ELO rating system to robustly aggregate noisy pairwise VLM comparisons into a persistent goal ranking rather than directly training a reward function from preference labels, and (ii) defining rewards as distances in a small, self-trained VAE embedding space rather than in a pretrained VLM's embedding space. The relabeling strategy for non-stationary rewards follows PEBBLE, and the RL backbone is SAC, both carried over from prior work to ensure fair comparison.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~50-point gap over the best VLM-based competitor (~95% vs ~45%) and near-parity with ground-truth reward suggest that careful handling of noisy VLM feedback and efficient reward generalisation can close much of the gap between language-specified and hand-crafted rewards. However, the method is limited to static-goal tasks identifiable from a single image, relies on visual similarity as a proxy for task progress, and is validated on only seven environments with a single VLM (Gemini 2.0 Flash). Open questions include whether the approach extends to video-based or temporally extended goals, whether more advanced self-supervised encoders (beyond the small VAE) would help or hurt, and whether the ELO-based ranking generalises to settings with more than a handful of candidate goals.
