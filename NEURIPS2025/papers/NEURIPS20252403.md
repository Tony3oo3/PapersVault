## Problem

Existing comparisons of SAA, ETO, and IEO in data-driven stochastic optimization are confined to the dichotomous cases of well-specified or fully misspecified models, with different asymptotic scalings in each regime and no smooth transition between them. This paper asks how the relative performance of these three methods changes as the degree of model misspecification varies continuously, and how the direction of misspecification affects decision quality.

## Input / Output

The framework takes n i.i.d. samples from an unknown distribution Q, a parametric family {P_θ}, and a non-linear cost c(w,z), and characterizes asymptotic decision error under a local misspecification parameterization where Q deviates from P_{θ₀} in direction u(z) at rate t = n^{-α}. The output is a three-regime classification (mild, balanced, severe based on α relative to 1/2) with explicit closed-form expressions for decision bias, variance, and regret for each method, plus sufficient conditions under which a given misspecification direction is approximately impactless on the final decision.

## Method

The core technique applies contiguity theory from asymptotic statistics—specifically Le Cam's third lemma—to tilted distributions Q_t that locally perturb P_{θ₀} in direction u(z). By setting the perturbation magnitude to t = n^{-α} and analyzing the three regimes where α is above, equal to, or below 1/2, the paper derives asymptotic normality results that decompose decision error into a bias term b^□ = E_{θ₀}[u(z)(IF^□(z) − IF^{SAA}(z))] and a variance term governed by the influence-function covariance. A key finding is the non-regularity of ETO and IEO estimators, whose asymptotic distributions depend on the misspecification direction u, a phenomenon rarely seen in standard statistical literature. The paper further proves that if u(z) lies in the linear span of the score function s_{θ₀}(z), the bias vanishes for all three methods, yielding a transparent sufficient condition for approximately impactless misspecification that depends only on the parametric model, not the downstream optimization.

## Results

The paper's main results are theoretical, establishing that under balanced misspecification (α=1/2), a bias-variance tradeoff emerges: SAA achieves zero bias but worst variance, ETO achieves best variance but worst bias, and IEO is intermediate on both metrics, with no universal regret ordering. Under severe misspecification (0<α<1/2), SAA strictly dominates IEO which dominates ETO on both bias and regret. Under mild misspecification (α>1/2), all biases vanish and ETO stochastically dominates IEO which dominates SAA on regret. Numerical experiments on the multi-product newsvendor problem with two misspecification directions confirm these orderings in finite samples.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three data-driven optimization methods: SAA (Shapiro et al., 2021), ETO via MLE (Elmachtoub and Grigas, 2022), and IEO (Donti et al., 2017). No single method universally dominates: under mild misspecification, ETO achieves the lowest regret (stochastic dominance), while under severe misspecification, SAA achieves zero regret and dominates both model-based methods. In the balanced regime, IEO can potentially achieve lower overall regret than either SAA or ETO by balancing its intermediate bias and variance. The key insight is that the bias for ETO and IEO is an inner product between the misspecification direction u(z) and the difference of influence functions relative to SAA, meaning misspecification directions in the linear span of the score function yield zero bias for all methods (Theorem 5). Numerical experiments on the newsvendor problem with two perturbation directions confirm the theoretical orderings across all three regimes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the comparison of SAA, ETO, and IEO in data-driven stochastic optimization from the binary well-specified/misspecified dichotomy (Elmachtoub et al., 2023) to a smooth local misspecification framework using contiguity theory from asymptotic statistics (Le Cam and Yang, 2000; van der Vaart, 2000). By parameterizing the misspecification magnitude as t = O(n^{-α}), the authors derive a unified asymptotic analysis that captures the transition between regimes, something prior work could not do. The influence-function-based bias and variance decompositions build on classical M-estimation theory but are applied here to the non-regular estimators arising from the estimation-optimization pipeline, a setting rarely encountered in standard statistical literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to characterize how the degree and direction of model misspecification affect the relative performance of SAA, ETO, and IEO in data-driven optimization, providing practitioners with a principled framework for choosing among these methods. The balanced-regime finding that no universal ordering exists (SAA best on bias, ETO best on variance, IEO in between) has direct practical implications for model-based decision-making under realistic near-misspecification. Key limitations include the restriction to unconstrained, non-contextual problems and the purely asymptotic nature of the results; extending the framework to contextual or constrained settings, and deriving finite-sample guarantees, are natural open questions.
