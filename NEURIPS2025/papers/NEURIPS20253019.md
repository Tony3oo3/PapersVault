## Problem

In the proportional asymptotic regime (p/n → c ∈ (0,∞)), no consistent estimator of the nuisance functions exists, so the standard Double Machine Learning framework for estimating doubly robust functionals breaks down. For the Expected Conditional Covariance (ECC), it is unclear how the tuning of ridge-regularized nuisance estimators interacts with the choice of sample-splitting strategy and estimator type to determine the asymptotic variance of the final functional estimate. This paper provides the first complete asymptotic characterization of this interplay under linear nuisance models.

## Input / Output

The method consumes n i.i.d. triples (X_i, Y_i, A_i) where X ∈ R^p follows a linear model with bivariate normal errors (correlation ρ) and p/n → c, along with ridge tuning parameters λ₁, λ₂. The output is structured as six debiased estimators (three estimator types × two splitting strategies), each accompanied by an explicit asymptotic variance formula expressed as integrals over the Marchenko-Pastur distribution. The bias-correction constants are deterministic functions of (λ₁, λ₂, c) that can be computed via Monte Carlo, and the optimal tuning parameters are obtained by minimizing the derived variance expressions rather than prediction error.

## Method

The paper analyzes three ECC estimators—the integral-based plug-in, the Newey-Robins, and the doubly robust (influence-function) estimator—under two sample-splitting strategies (two-split and three-split). Using random matrix theory, it shows that all six resulting estimators are asymptotically biased in the proportional regime, with the bias characterized by deterministic integrals over the Marchenko-Pastur distribution (e.g., g(λ₁,λ₂) = ∫ x dF_MP(x)/(x+λ)). The key debiasing mechanism is an inversion trick: since the bias is a known scalar multiple of the target parameter (or a linear combination of θ₀ and α₀ᵀβ₀), dividing by the appropriate deterministic function recovers an unbiased, √n-consistent estimator. The central finding is that the tuning parameters minimizing the asymptotic variance of the debiased ECC estimator differ substantially from those minimizing the prediction error of the nuisance functions, demonstrating that standard DML wisdom of prediction-optimal nuisance estimation does not carry over to the inconsistency regime.

## Results

The paper establishes that all six debiased ECC estimators (three estimator types × two splitting strategies) achieve √n-consistency under proportional asymptotics (p/n → c ∈ (0,∞)), as formalized in Theorems 3.1–3.3. Theorem 3.4 provides explicit asymptotic variance expressions V_v^A(λ₁, λ₂) for each estimator, revealing that the variance depends non-trivially on the ridge tuning parameters through Marchenko-Pastur integrals. Simulations (10,000 replications, split size 500, c = 0.25 and c = 2) confirm that the inference-optimal λ minimizing the asymptotic variance of the ECC differs substantially from the prediction-optimal λ that minimizes nuisance function estimation error, with the gap widening as c increases. In the two-split setting, the bias-correction constant diverges near λ ≈ 1.48, causing the variance to blow up, but the minimum-variance λ remains well-defined and distinct from the prediction-optimal value.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against external baseline methods but instead compares three internal estimator families (Integral-based, Newey-Robins, and Doubly Robust) under two sample-splitting strategies (two-split and three-split), yielding six debiased estimators. The central empirical finding is that the prediction-optimal ridge tuning parameter (minimizing nuisance function estimation error) does not coincide with the inference-optimal tuning parameter (minimizing the asymptotic variance of the ECC estimator), and the gap between the two is non-negligible across both c = 0.25 and c = 2 regimes. Among the six estimators, the three-split versions generally have simpler bias-correction structures (single division by a deterministic function) compared to the two-split versions, which require a two-step inversion due to correlation between the estimation errors of α₀ and β₀. The two-split DR estimator exhibits the most complex variance structure, with the bias-correction constant diverging near λ ≈ 1.48, effectively restricting the usable tuning range. The paper's practical recommendation is to directly minimize the asymptotic variance V_v^A(λ₁,λ₂) (via parametric bootstrap or the closed-form expressions) rather than relying on prediction-optimal cross-validation for the nuisance functions, which is the standard DML practice in fixed-dimensional settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Double Machine Learning (DML) framework of Chernozhukov et al. (2018) and the Newey-Robins estimator (2018) to the proportional asymptotic regime (p/n → c ∈ (0,∞)), where no consistent nuisance function estimator exists. It builds directly on Celentano and Wainwright's (2023) characterization of the 'inconsistency regime' and on McGrath and Mukherjee's (2022) analysis of nuisance function tuning and sample splitting for doubly robust estimation, but shifts the setting from nonparametric models (where consistent estimation is possible) to a linear model with ridge regression under proportional asymptotics, where random matrix theory (Marchenko-Pastur distribution) becomes the key analytical tool. The paper departs from the standard DML philosophy that nuisance functions should be tuned for prediction optimality, showing instead that a different tuning criterion is needed when the goal is inference on the functional.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to provide a complete asymptotic characterization of three doubly robust estimators (integral-based, Newey-Robins, and influence-function-based) under proportional asymptotics with two sample splitting strategies, revealing that prediction-optimal ridge tuning parameters do not minimize the asymptotic variance of the ECC estimator. The result challenges a core assumption of the DML framework and provides a template for understanding how sample splitting, estimator choice, and tuning interact in the over-parameterized regime. Key limitations include the assumption of Gaussian covariates for variance calculations, restriction to linear nuisance functions and ridge regression, and the absence of an established efficiency bound in this regime. Open questions include extending to non-Gaussian settings via universality, incorporating other regularizers (e.g., Lasso), and determining whether the class of optimally tuned estimators studied here can illuminate eventual minimax or semiparametric efficiency bounds for doubly robust functionals under proportional asymptotics.
