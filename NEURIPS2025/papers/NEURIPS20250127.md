## Problem

Existing work on neural representational geometry has focused on static, time-independent settings, while interpretability of dynamical systems has relied on fixed-point manifolds and local linearisations that miss transient and nonlinear dynamics. The link between computation-through-dynamics and the geometry of neural representations in systems driven by time-varying inputs remains poorly understood, leaving open how RNNs transform input manifolds into activity manifolds to perform task computations.

## Input / Output

The framework takes a trained RNN (weight matrix W, input matrix B, nonlinearity φ) and a family of time-varying inputs parametrised by coordinates κ on an m-dimensional input manifold (e.g., two evidence levels u₁,u₂ for decision-making, or two angles θ₁,θ₂ for working memory). It produces the (m+1)×(m+1) pullback Riemannian metric tensor on the state manifold at each time point, computed either via the adjoint ODE ȧᵢ = J_f aᵢ + h(x,t,κ) or via autodifferentiation through the ODE solver. Derived quantities include Gaussian curvature, geodesic distances along gridlines, and the tangent-space basis vectors ∂_t x and ∂_{κᵢ} x, which together characterise how the manifold is stretched or compressed in each task-relevant direction.

## Method

The paper proves that if inputs lie on an m-dimensional manifold, the RNN state is constrained to an (m+1)-dimensional manifold (m input dimensions plus time), and derives the pullback Riemannian metric on this manifold as G = UUᵀ where U stacks the dynamics vector f and adjoint variables aᵢ that solve a controlled linear ODE driven by the input Jacobian. For RNNs specifically, the metric entries decompose into ‖f‖², fᵀA, and AᵀA, with Ȧ = (Wφ′(x) − I)A + B(du_κ/dκ), enabling efficient computation via autodifferentiation. Applied to contextual evidence integration, the metric reveals that the manifold warps over time to compress the irrelevant input direction while stretching near the decision boundary along the relevant direction, and causal experiments confirm warping is both necessary and sufficient for task performance. Applied to sequential working memory, the framework shows the state lies on a hyper-torus whose intrinsic geometry (Gaussian curvature) and extrinsic embedding (decoder alignment, PC subspace) dynamically reorient during retrieval to selectively compress non-retrieved memories.

## Results

The paper proves that an m-dimensional input manifold constrains the RNN state to an (m+1)-dimensional manifold (Theorem 3.1), and derives the pullback Riemannian metric via an adjoint ODE. In the contextual evidence-integration task, the metric's eigenvalue along the irrelevant input direction decays to near zero by the decision time, while the relevant-input eigenvalue remains large, confirming geometric compression of irrelevant information. In the causal experiment (S8.3), constraining the network to prevent warping degrades test MSE from 0.001±0.002 to 0.239±0.257, while training only the recurrent weights to warp the representation (without task labels) achieves 0.001±0.001 MSE—demonstrating warping is both necessary and sufficient. For sequential working memory, the RNN state lies on a hyper-torus whose Gaussian curvature is non-uniform and whose metric selectively compresses non-retrieved stimuli during the recall phase.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not benchmark against competing interpretability methods in the traditional sense; instead, it positions its Riemannian geometric framework against prior approaches to RNN interpretability. Compared to the linearised fixed-point/attractor analysis of Mante et al. [39] and Sussillo & Barak [21], the proposed framework captures fully nonlinear, time-varying geometry without requiring the system to settle into a fixed point, and reveals context-dependent warping that is invisible to local linearisation. The causal experiment in S8.3 serves as the key internal comparison: preventing warping (equal-magnitude metric diagonal constraint) degrades performance to near-untrained levels (0.239 vs. 0.001 MSE), while training solely for warping recovers full task performance, establishing warping as a necessary and sufficient computational mechanism. For working memory, the framework extends beyond the orthogonal-subspace and discrete-attractor accounts [56,58] by showing that a single continuous hyper-torus with dynamically evolving intrinsic and extrinsic geometry encodes and retrieves multiple stimuli.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Riemannian geometry of neural representations (Hauser & Ray 2017; Benfenati & Marta 2023) from static feedforward networks to dynamical systems receiving time-varying inputs, and extends attractor-manifold analysis of RNNs (Sussillo & Barak 2013; Mante et al. 2013) from local linearised dynamics around fixed points to the full nonlinear geometry of the activity manifold. The pullback metric is derived via an adjoint differential equation, providing an exact mathematical characterisation rather than a dimensionality-reduction approximation. It is complementary to low-rank RNN work (Mastrogiuseppe & Ostojic 2018; Valente et al. 2022), which constrains the linear embedding dimensionality, whereas this framework constrains the intrinsic dimensionality of the nonlinear state manifold.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a general, mathematically rigorous tool for interpreting RNN computations beyond the fixed-point paradigm, applicable to any dynamical system with low-dimensional inputs. The causal experiment showing that warping is both necessary (preventing it degrades performance) and sufficient (training only to warp recovers near-optimal performance) elevates the geometric analysis from correlational to mechanistic. Open questions include the distribution of manifolds under stochastic noise (the paper treats noise pathwise), the degeneracy between geometry and connectivity (same metric from different architectures), and whether the framework can be applied to RNNs fit directly to large-scale biological neural recordings.
