## Problem

Objective gait assessment in Parkinson's Disease is hindered by the absence of large, diverse, multi-site motion datasets with clinical annotations, causing existing ML models to overfit to single-site protocols and fail under real-world variability. No publicly available collection unifies heterogeneous capture modalities (RGB video and optical motion capture) into a common 3D representation with UPDRS-gait labels across multiple clinical centers.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254391/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Input consists of variable-length 3D SMPL mesh sequences at 30 FPS (22 joints, 6890-vertex textureless meshes), derived from either monocular RGB video via WHAM or optical MoCap via SparseFusion fitting. For encoder-specific consumption, sequences are windowed into non-overlapping fixed-length clips of N frames and converted to each encoder's native format (e.g., 2D perspective-projected joints for MixSTE/PoseFormerV2, 3D Human3.6M joints for POTR). Output is a discrete UPDRS-gait severity score (0–3) obtained by majority voting across clip-level predictions from a frozen encoder plus a lightweight probe; for pretext tasks, output is 3D joint positions evaluated via MPJPE, PA-MPJPE, and acceleration error.

## Method

CARE-PD introduces a harmonized preprocessing pipeline that converts nine heterogeneous cohorts (RGB at 25–60 FPS, MoCap at 100–150 FPS) into unified 30 Hz SMPL mesh sequences, including a Kabsch-algorithm-based slope correction for ceiling-mounted camera artifacts and privacy-preserving anonymization (textureless meshes only, no shape parameters). The benchmarking framework evaluates seven frozen SOTA motion encoders (spanning transformers, VQ-VAE, and masked-modeling paradigms) with linear and k-NN probes under four generalization protocols—within-dataset LOSO, cross-dataset, leave-one-dataset-out, and multi-dataset in-domain adaptation—alongside a Random Forest baseline on handcrafted spatiotemporal and stability gait features. For pretext tasks, MotionAGFormer (2D-to-3D lifting) and MoMask (3D reconstruction) are fine-tuned or trained from scratch on CARE-PD to quantify whether exposure to ~19 hours of pathological gait improves both 3D pose accuracy (MPJPE drops from 60.8 mm to 7.5 mm) and downstream UPDRS-gait F1 (up 17 percentage points) relative to healthy-gait or generic-motion pretraining.

## Results

For the 2D-to-3D lifting pretext task, fine-tuning MotionAGFormer on CARE-PD reduces MPJPE from 60.7 mm (zero-shot) to 7.5 mm and boosts downstream UPDRS-gait macro-F1 from 48.1 to 65.1. For 3D reconstruction, MoMask's MPJPE drops from 22.5 mm to 8.7 mm with CARE-PD fine-tuning, and F1 rises from 41.4 to 62.7. In clinical score estimation, frozen encoders achieve within-dataset macro-F1 up to 0.73 (PD-GaM) and 0.68 (BMClab), but cross-dataset F1 drops by 0.2–0.4; under the MIDA protocol, MixSTE, MotionAGFormer, MotionBERT, and PoseFormerV2 reach 0.74–0.78 on BMClab, halving the LODO error.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Seven frozen motion encoders (POTR, MixSTE, PoseFormerV2, MotionBERT, MotionAGFormer, MotionCLIP, MoMask) are compared against a Random Forest baseline on handcrafted gait features. Encoders consistently outperform the handcrafted-feature baseline in cross-dataset settings, where the Random Forest deteriorates sharply on PD-GaM and 3DGait while remaining competitive only on T-SDU-PD (F1 ≈ 0.49 in the 3-class setup). Among encoders, MoMask (a VQ-VAE) is the most robust cross-site, maintaining average cross-dataset F1 above 0.40, while MotionBERT is the most sensitive to target-site choice (peaking at 0.49 on PD-GaM but dropping to 0.25 on T-SDU-PD in LODO). Under MIDA, MixSTE, MotionAGFormer, MotionBERT, and PoseFormerV2 all reach 0.74–0.78 macro-F1 on BMClab, substantially outperforming the handcrafted baseline. For pretext tasks, CARE-PD fine-tuning dramatically outperforms healthy-gait fine-tuning (e.g., MotionAGFormer MPJPE: 7.5 mm vs. 29.8 mm), confirming that pathological kinematics—not merely more walking data—drive the improvement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by unifying nine heterogeneous gait datasets (RGB video and optical MoCap) from eight clinical sites into a single anonymized SMPL mesh representation, addressing the fragmentation that has limited prior PD gait research to small, single-site collections. Rather than proposing a new architecture, it repurposes seven existing state-of-the-art motion encoders (POTR, MixSTE, PoseFormerV2, MotionBERT, MotionAGFormer, MotionCLIP, MoMask) as frozen backbones with lightweight probes, and introduces a harmonized preprocessing pipeline (WHAM for video, SparseFusion for MoCap, Kabsch-based slope correction) that bridges modality-specific conventions. The benchmarking framework—four generalization protocols (within-dataset LOSO, cross-dataset, LODO, MIDA) plus two pretext tasks—extends the representation-learning evaluation paradigm from generic motion benchmarks (Human3.6M, AMASS) into the clinical domain where pathological gait patterns and multi-site variability are the central challenges.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CARE-PD is the largest publicly available 3D mesh gait dataset for Parkinson's disease and the first multi-site collection of its kind, providing a critical resource for developing generalizable clinical motion models. The finding that fine-tuning on ~19 hours of pathological gait reduces MPJPE by 8× (60.7→7.5 mm) and boosts severity F1 by 17 points—while healthy-gait fine-tuning yields far smaller gains—confirms that exposure to clinically relevant kinematics, not merely more walking, is what enables effective adaptation. Open questions remain around handling the rare severe class (UPDRS score 3), bridging persistent domain gaps that even LODO+MIDA cannot fully close (e.g., 3DGait stays ≤0.18), and whether test-time adaptation or domain-aware training can further narrow the cross-site performance drop of 0.2–0.4 F1. The dataset's non-commercial license and the absence of raw video or shape parameters limit some downstream applications but ensure privacy compliance.
