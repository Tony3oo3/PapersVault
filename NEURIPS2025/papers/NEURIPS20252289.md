## Problem

REINFORCE is fundamentally an on-policy algorithm: its negative-reward term is unbounded, causing model collapse when training data is sampled from a distribution that drifts away from the current policy. Existing remedies—KL regularization, PPO's tight clipping window, or discarding negatives via SFT—either slow learning, cap achievable improvement, or waste inference on negative examples. The paper asks whether a stable, efficient off-policy policy gradient for LLMs can fully exploit both positive and negative trajectories without any explicit KL penalty.

## Input / Output

TOPR consumes a fixed batch of prompt–response trajectories generated once by a reference policy μ (typically the base model at the start of an iteration) together with a binary reward signal (+1 correct, −1 incorrect). It operates in a fully offline regime: trajectories are never resampled or updated during training, so the reference distribution μ is static throughout. The output is the updated parameters of an autoregressive LLM (e.g., Llama 3 8B or DeepSeek-R1 8B), evaluated on reasoning benchmarks via Pass@1 and Maj@K metrics.

## Method

TOPR applies an asymmetric, tapered importance-sampling rule to the REINFORCE gradient: positive examples receive a pure SFT-style update (no importance ratio, guaranteeing a minimum learning rate even when the trajectory is unlikely under the current policy), while negative examples receive a truncated-importance-sampling update with the ratio π(τ)/μ(τ) clipped to [0,1]. This asymmetry makes the surrogate objective bounded above—eliminating the unbounded destructive force that causes naive REINFORCE to collapse—while preserving fast learning on positives and allowing the model to gracefully reduce the probability of bad trajectories. The paper proves that the REINFORCE baseline parameter, traditionally a variance-reduction device, actually modulates the effective positive/negative balance of the dataset in the off-policy regime, and that an effective positive proportion of roughly 10–20% yields optimal performance. No KL regularization is required, and the algorithm reduces to a simple modification of any existing REINFORCE implementation.

## Results

On GSM8K, TOPR fine-tuned Llama 3 8B achieves 79.6% Pass@1 with Anna Karenina sampling (vs. 75.4% with uniform sampling), and the multi-iteration TOPR model surpasses Llama 3 70B in Maj@16 accuracy. On MATH, TOPR nearly doubles Pass@1 accuracy over the base model. For the generative verifier task on MATH, the TOPR-trained 8B verifier reaches 70.9% verifier accuracy (vs. 32.6% for the untrained base model) and improves weighted self-consistency from 55.5% to 61.5%. In the learning-to-search task, TOPR improves intersection-over-union from 35.03% to 35.83% while reducing complete misses.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TOPR outperforms all three primary baselines in the off-policy regime on GSM8K (Figure 1, left). Naive REINFORCE initially tracks TOPR but collapses as the policy diverges from the data distribution, producing overwhelmingly degenerate generations. PPO makes little progress because its clipped objective zeroes out gradients once the importance ratio leaves the [1−ε, 1+ε] window, effectively stalling after a handful of updates. DPO, which handles negatives through a preference-based formulation, is more stable off-policy than PPO or naive REINFORCE but still falls well short of TOPR's final Pass@1. Against truncated importance sampling (TIS), TOPR's positive-example acceleration (a⁺=1) provides substantially greater robustness when the effective proportion of positive examples is low (below ~20%), while TIS reaches a marginally higher peak Pass@1 in the best-case composition. Compared to positive-only SFT, TOPR's use of negative examples yields higher test accuracy and better self-consistency efficiency, requiring fewer test-time samples to reach the same performance level.

## Relation to Prior Work

TOPR is a principled recombination of two well-studied ideas: supervised fine-tuning (SFT) for positive trajectories and truncated importance sampling (TIS) for negative trajectories, unified under a single asymmetric policy gradient. It departs from the standard off-policy REINFORCE formulation (Ahmadian et al., 2024) by removing the importance ratio entirely for positive examples, and from PPO by applying the ratio to the gradient rather than the reward, avoiding PPO's gradient vanishing outside the [1−ε, 1+ε] window. The theoretical analysis builds on the log-ratio lower-bound framework of Le Roux (2016, 2017) and Munos et al. (2016), extending it to the asymmetric positive/negative case and providing a boundedness guarantee (Proposition 3.2) that no prior method in the REINFORCE family achieves without explicit KL regularization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TOPR offers a drop-in replacement for PPO or naive REINFORCE in LLM fine-tuning pipelines that is simpler to implement (no KL penalty, no ε hyperparameter) and more data-efficient (all generated trajectories are used, including negatives). The finding that REINFORCE's baseline parameter — traditionally viewed as a variance-reduction device — actually controls the effective positive/negative ratio in the training set is a conceptual shift that may reshape how practitioners curate RL datasets for LLMs. Open questions include whether TOPR extends cleanly to fully offline settings with a fixed expert dataset, whether the 10–20% optimal effective positive proportion generalizes beyond math reasoning, and whether the method transfers to non-LLM RL domains such as robotics or game-playing.
