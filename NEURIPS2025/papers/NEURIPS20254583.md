## Problem

Conventional PINNs embed boundary conditions and PDE residuals into a single loss function, causing gradient conflicts that prevent simultaneous minimization—especially when boundaries are geometrically complex (segmented inlets, internal obstructions). Existing hard-constraint and adaptive-weighting variants (hPINN, XPINN, SA-PINN) still produce significant errors in such scenarios, leaving a gap in accurately solving the Navier-Stokes equations with irregular boundary geometries.

## Input / Output

The method consumes space-time coordinates (x, y, t) and outputs velocity components (u, v) and pressure p as a composite field q(x,t) = P_q(x,t) + D_q(x,t)·H_q(x,t), where each term is produced by a separate DNN. The distance function D_q is supervised by a power-law transformation of the signed distance to the nearest boundary, ensuring it evaluates to 0 on boundaries and rapidly approaches 1 in the interior. For transient cases, the input dimension increases to 3 (x, y, t) while the output structure remains unchanged.

## Method

HB-PINN decomposes the solution into three pre-trained subnetworks: a Particular Solution Network (N_P) trained with heavily weighted boundary/initial condition losses (λ_BC, λ_IC = 1000 vs. λ_PDE = 1) to enforce boundary compliance without strict PDE constraints; a Distance Metric Network (N_D) supervised by a power-law function f(D̂_q) = 1 − (1 − D̂_q/max(D̂_q))^α that encodes spatial proximity to boundaries; and a Primary Network (N_H) trained exclusively on PDE residuals after N_P and N_D are frozen. The power-law exponent α controls the transition steepness of the distance function, making the boundary layer thinner for larger α. This decoupling eliminates the multi-loss gradient conflicts inherent to conventional PINNs, allowing the primary network to focus entirely on governing equation compliance while boundary conditions are structurally guaranteed by the composite formulation.

## Results

HB-PINN achieves the lowest error across all three benchmark cases (2D cylinder wake, steady-state obstructed cavity, and transient obstructed cavity) at Re=100. On Case 2 (the most geometrically complex steady-state problem), HB-PINN attains MSE 0.00088, MAE 0.01888, and Relative L2 0.06655, outperforming the next-best method PirateNet (MSE 0.00186) by a factor of ~2.1 in MSE. On the transient Case 3, HB-PINN reduces MSE to 0.00825 versus PirateNet's 0.03202 (≈3.9× improvement) and sPINN's 0.12400 (≈15× improvement). The method also maintains its advantage at higher Reynolds numbers (Re=500–2000), achieving an order-of-magnitude MSE reduction over conventional PINN variants at Re=500 and Re=1000.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HB-PINN outperforms all six baselines (sPINN, hPINN, MFN-PINN, XPINN, SA-PINN, and PirateNet) on every metric across all three cases. The closest competitor is PirateNet [26], which HB-PINN surpasses by 43% in MSE on Case 1 (0.00433 vs. 0.00763), 53% on Case 2 (0.00088 vs. 0.00186), and 74% on Case 3 (0.00825 vs. 0.03202). SA-PINN [22] and XPINN [23] show moderate improvements over the original sPINN [4] and hPINN [21] but still lag HB-PINN by 1–2 orders of magnitude in MSE on the complex-boundary cases (e.g., SA-PINN MSE 0.00542 vs. HB-PINN 0.00088 on Case 2). The hard-constrained hPINN [21] performs poorly on complex boundaries, requiring boundary relaxation in specific subdomains to converge, and yields the worst or near-worst results in Cases 1 and 3. MFN-PINN [18,24] and XPINN [23] struggle most with the segmented-inlet geometry (Case 2), where their MSE values (0.11626 and 0.02149) are 130× and 24× higher than HB-PINN's, respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

HB-PINN extends the hard-constraint PINN framework of Lu et al. (2021), which uses an analytical distance-informed ansatz to enforce boundary conditions, by replacing both the particular solution and distance functions with learned DNNs. This departure is motivated by the infeasibility of constructing closed-form distance functions for geometrically complex boundaries (e.g., segmented inlets with internal obstructions). The method also draws on the soft-constraint PINN paradigm of Raissi et al. (2019) for the pre-training phase of the particular solution network, but decouples boundary enforcement from PDE residual minimization through a three-stage training protocol, addressing the loss-conflict problem identified by SA-PINN and XPINN.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that decoupling boundary-condition satisfaction from PDE residual minimization via pre-trained subnetworks can yield order-of-magnitude MSE reductions over both soft- and hard-constrained PINNs on complex-boundary Navier-Stokes problems, a practical gap that has limited PINN adoption in industrial CFD. However, the method is validated only on 2D incompressible flows at Re ≤ 2000, and critical hyperparameters (the power-law exponent α, N_P training epochs) remain empirically tuned without a principled selection criterion. Open questions include whether the three-network architecture scales to 3D flows, higher Reynolds numbers with turbulence, or coupled multi-physics systems, and whether the pre-training cost (300k+ epochs for N_D) is prohibitive for time-sensitive applications.
