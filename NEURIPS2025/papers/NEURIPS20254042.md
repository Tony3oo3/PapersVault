## Problem

Existing language-grounded visual concept learning methods rely on a small set of manually predefined primitive axes (e.g., color, shape) and are evaluated primarily on synthetic datasets, making them infeasible for real-world scenes where relevant concept axes are diverse, image-specific, and unknown in advance. Additionally, prior approaches either require a separate specialized encoder per axis (scaling poorly) or force concept embeddings to match image-agnostic text descriptors, sacrificing instance-specific visual details.

## Input / Output

The framework takes 256×256 RGB images and, for editing tasks, a source-target image pair. A pretrained VLM (InternVL) is queried once per image with a universal prompt to produce a dictionary of discovered concept axes and their textual descriptions (e.g., {'fur_color': 'brown', 'ear_shape': 'floppy', ...}), where the number of axes K varies per image. The universal concept encoder then maps DINOv2 visual features to K continuous concept embeddings, which are fed into a frozen Stable Diffusion decoder (10 DDIM steps) to produce the edited 256×256 output image.

## Method

The framework has three key components. First, a universal prompt (general task description + a single output exemplar) guides a pretrained VLM to adaptively discover diverse image-related concept axes in a single query, generalizing to unseen domains without prior knowledge. Second, a Universal Concept Encoder (UCE) adapts the Q-Former by replacing its learnable queries with text embeddings of the discovered axes, so a single transformer architecture dynamically binds visual features to any number of arbitrary axes without per-concept parameters. Third, a compositional anchoring objective enforces disentanglement by randomly swapping concept representations across batch images that share the same axis, re-encoding the resulting composite image with a frozen copy of the encoder, and using a shared lightweight regression network g_φ to verify that each axis's predicted description matches the composed text — thereby ensuring each axis is independently manipulable without forcing z_i = v_i and preserving instance-specific details. Training combines the diffusion reconstruction loss, the compositional anchoring loss, and a regression loss on non-swapped representations, with the T2I decoder and vision encoder kept frozen.

## Results

On the visual concept editing task, the proposed method achieves the highest CLIP-Score and BLIP-Score across all four datasets. On ImageNet-S20, it reaches 0.251 CLIP and 0.474 BLIP, outperforming the strongest text-based baseline DDPM-Inversion (0.243/0.467). On CelebA-HQ, it scores 0.239 CLIP and 0.496 BLIP, surpassing LIVCL (0.226/0.469) and DDPM-Inversion (0.220/0.483). On AFHQ-Dog and AFHQ-Cat, it achieves 0.272/0.535 and 0.271/0.514 respectively, consistently leading all baselines by a clear margin on both metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all five baselines on every dataset and both metrics (CLIP-Score and BLIP-Score). Against the closest text-based competitor, DDPM-Inversion, the gains are consistent but moderate: +0.008 CLIP on ImageNet-S20, +0.019 on CelebA-HQ, +0.006 on AFHQ-Dog, and +0.005 on AFHQ-Cat. Against LIVCL (the prior language-informed visual concept learning method), the advantage is larger on CelebA-HQ (+0.013 CLIP, +0.027 BLIP) and AFHQ-Cat (+0.003 CLIP, +0.034 BLIP), reflecting LIVCL's inability to capture image-specific visual nuances due to its direct alignment with text embeddings. The text-based editing methods (SDEdit, InstructPix2Pix, NullText Inversion) lag substantially behind, with CLIP scores 0.02–0.06 lower, because they lack any mechanism to extract and transfer visual concepts from a source image. On the multi-axis composition task (Table 3), the performance gap widens as the number of simultaneously edited axes increases from 2 to 4, where baselines degrade noticeably while the proposed method shows only marginal decrease, confirming superior disentanglement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends LIVCL (Lee et al., ICLR 2024), which grounded visual concepts along a few predefined primitive axes (color, shape, style) on synthetic datasets, by replacing fixed axes with VLM-discovered adaptive axes and moving to real-world scenes. Architecturally, it adapts the Q-Former from BLIP-2 into a universal concept encoder that binds visual features to arbitrary axes via text-embedding queries, eliminating per-axis specialized encoders. The key methodological departure is the compositional anchoring loss, which enforces disentanglement by verifying axis independence in the generated output space rather than forcing direct alignment between concept representations and image-agnostic text embeddings as LIVCL does.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a practical gap: real-world images contain far more diverse and image-specific concept axes than any fixed set can cover, and the compositional anchoring objective resolves the disentanglement-vs-detail trade-off that limits prior approaches. Limitations include dependence on VLM quality for axis discovery, 256×256 resolution, and the inability to guarantee capture of all underlying factors of variation. Open questions include whether the approach scales to higher resolutions, more complex multi-object scenes, and whether the VLM bottleneck can be further reduced as models improve.
