## Problem

Single-step retrosynthesis—predicting which reactants produce a given product molecule—remains challenging because the combinatorial reactant space is vast and existing template-free generative models struggle to simultaneously achieve high accuracy and diverse, chemically feasible predictions. No prior work has applied discrete flow matching to this task, and the choice of source distribution for the generative process has not been explored as a means to inject structural priors.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251846/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a product molecule as an attributed graph (16 atom types + dummy, 4 bond types + no-bond) and, for the RSF variant, additionally takes synthon graphs derived by severing predicted reaction-center bonds. Ten dummy nodes are appended to the source graph to guarantee node-count alignment with the reactant graph. Output is a set of N=100 reactant molecule graphs (each a potentially disconnected graph), generated over T=50 discrete flow-matching steps, with optional FK-steering via SMC resampling (K=4 particles) to bias toward round-trip-feasible candidates.

## Method

RETRO SYNFLOW formulates retrosynthesis as discrete flow matching over molecular graph tokens, learning a continuous-time Markov chain that transports a source distribution to the reactant distribution. The key architectural choice is using synthons—intermediate fragments obtained by a pretrained reaction-center predictor—as the source distribution rather than the raw product, reducing the generation task to synthon completion and encoding valuable structural priors. The denoiser is a graph transformer with PNA aggregation and FiLM conditioning that jointly predicts clean node and edge probabilities, trained with a weighted cross-entropy objective over uniform time samples. At inference, Feynman-Kac steering with Sequential Monte Carlo resampling guides particles toward high-reward trajectories, where the reward is defined by a forward-synthesis model (Molecular Transformer) checking round-trip consistency, yielding a 19% gain in top-5 round-trip accuracy over prior template-free SOTA.

## Results

On the USPTO-50k benchmark, RETRO SYNFLOW achieves 60.0% top-1 exact-match accuracy, outperforming the previous best template-free method (DualTFaug, 53.6%) by 6.4 points and the best template-based method (GraphRetro, 53.7%) by 6.3 points. The reward-steered variant RETRO PRODFLOW-RS achieves 80.1% top-5 round-trip accuracy, surpassing the prior template-free SOTA (RetroBridge, 67.8%) by 12.3 points and the best template-based method (GLN, 66.2%) by 13.9 points. RETRO SYNFLOW also improves top-3 and top-5 exact-match accuracy to 77.9% and 82.7% respectively, while remaining competitive with the Chimera ensemble (59.6% top-1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RETRO SYNFLOW (60.0% top-1) outperforms all individual template-free baselines, with the largest margin over DualTFaug (53.6%, +6.4 pts) and RetroBridge (50.8%, +9.2 pts), and also surpasses all template-based methods including GraphRetro (53.7%) and LocalRetro (52.6%). It slightly edges out the Chimera ensemble (59.6%) on top-1 while being a single model rather than an ensemble of multiple architectures. On round-trip accuracy (Table 2), RETRO PRODFLOW-RS dominates: it achieves 80.1% top-5 round-trip accuracy versus 67.8% for RetroBridge (the best prior template-free method) and 66.2% for GLN (the best template-based method), a gain of 12.3 and 13.9 points respectively. RETRO PRODFLOW-RS also leads on round-trip coverage at all k values (98.7% at k=5 vs. 97.1% for RetroBridge). The ablation in Table 4 confirms that the FK-steering with SMC resampling (RPF-RS, 80.1% top-5 round-trip) substantially outperforms both greedy sampling (73.0%) and simply scaling the number of reactants from 100 to 400 (68.5%), demonstrating that inference-time steering is the key driver of the diversity gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper applies discrete flow matching (Gat et al. 2024; Campbell et al. 2024) to retrosynthesis for the first time, building directly on RetroBridge's (Igashov et al. 2023) Markov bridge formulation between product and reactant graphs. The key conceptual departure is replacing the product distribution as the flow's source with a synthon distribution derived from reaction center prediction (Shi et al. 2020), which reduces the generation task to a simpler conditional mapping. Additionally, the paper introduces Feynman-Kac steering with SMC resampling (Singhal et al. 2025) as an inference-time mechanism for retrosynthesis, using a forward-synthesis model (Molecular Transformer) as a reward oracle to enforce round-trip consistency—something prior template-free methods did not exploit at generation time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 60% top-1 accuracy (a ~20% gain over prior template-free SOTA) and the 19% improvement in top-5 round-trip accuracy from FK steering demonstrate that both the synthon inductive bias and inference-time reward steering are effective levers for retrosynthesis quality and diversity. However, the method is limited to single-step retrosynthesis on a single benchmark (USPTO-50k), depends on an external reaction center predictor and a forward-synthesis model whose own errors propagate (25% misscoring of valid reactants), and has not been evaluated in multi-step planning pipelines where it would ultimately need to operate. Open questions include whether the synthon-source formulation generalizes to multi-step or template-constrained settings, and whether the FK steering overhead is acceptable in iterative planning loops.
