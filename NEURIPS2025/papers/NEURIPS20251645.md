## Problem

Most state-of-the-art LLMs overfit on the 25 verifiable constraint templates in IFEval and fail to generalize to unseen output constraints, with leading models scoring below 50% on new constraint types. This paper identifies and quantifies this generalization gap, then investigates what training data and algorithms enable models to follow diverse, previously unseen verifiable instructions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251645/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark (IFBENCH) pairs unseen WildChat prompts with 1–2 hand-crafted verifiable constraints spanning 7 categories (count, ratio, words, sentence, format, custom, copy), producing 300 evaluation instances in single-turn and multi-turn formats. Training data (IFTRAIN) combines TULU-3-SFT instructions with up to 6 constraints per prompt, using variable ranges deliberately widened or shifted relative to test ranges. The output is a text response scored by Python verification functions that check each constraint independently, yielding strict and loose prompt-level accuracy.

## Method

The core training procedure, IF-RLVR, applies GRPO (Group Region Policy Optimization) with verifiable outcome rewards: each generated response is scored by a Python function per constraint, and the instance reward is the weighted sum of individual constraint rewards. Key design choices that drive generalization include (1) interleaving multiple constraints per training prompt (up to 6, with a conflict dictionary to prevent contradictions), (2) using wider or disjoint variable ranges for training constraints relative to test ranges, and (3) combining 29 new IFTRAIN constraints with the 25 IFEval constraints. The paper shows GRPO consistently outperforms DPO on the same preference data, that training from base models with a reasoning chat template (think/answer tags) improves out-of-domain generalization, and that multi-turn RLVR training specifically improves multi-turn constraint following. A notable side effect is that RLVR-trained models over-prioritize constraints over task quality, motivating the addition of a preference reward signal to balance the two.

## Results

IF-RLVR training with GRPO substantially improves precise instruction following: the TULU-3-8B-DPO model improves from 81.1 to 92.2 on IFEval and from 25.5 to 44.6 on IFBENCH (loose accuracy). Training from the Qwen2.5-7B base model with a reasoning chat template yields 87.8 IFEval and 53.7 IFBENCH, while the Llama-3.1-8B base reaches 88.2 IFEval and 54.1 IFBENCH. On downstream benchmarks, IF-RLVR TULU-DPO 8B retains competitive GSM8K (83.2 vs. 84.3) and BBH (68.9 vs. 68.7) scores but drops on AlpacaEval 2 (21.3 vs. 33.5), indicating a trade-off between constraint adherence and general response quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the TULU-3 family (Lambert et al., 2024), IF-RLVR TULU-DPO 8B wins decisively on both instruction-following benchmarks: 92.2 vs. 81.1 on IFEval (+11.1) and 44.6 vs. 25.5 on IFBENCH (+19.1), but loses on AlpacaEval 2 (21.3 vs. 33.5) and slightly on GSM8K (83.2 vs. 84.3), confirming a constraint-prioritization trade-off. For Qwen2.5 (Qwen Team, 2024), IF-RLVR from the 7B base model achieves 87.8 IFEval and 53.7 IFBENCH, outperforming the Qwen2.5-7B-instruct baseline (74.7 IFEval, 31.3 IFBENCH) by +13.1 and +22.4 respectively. For OLMo2 (Team OLMo, 2024), IF-RLVR from base reaches 70.4 IFEval and 46.6 IFBENCH, improving over the OLMo2-instruct baseline (61.7, 16.7) by +8.7 and +29.9. The paper also notes that frontier models like Qwen3-32B (Yang et al., 2025) and Claude 4 Sonnet score below 50% on IFBENCH out-of-the-box, positioning IF-RLVR-trained 7–8B models as competitive with much larger systems on this specific task.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper applies the RLVR training paradigm from TULU 3 (Lambert et al., 2024) and the GRPO policy optimization algorithm from DeepSeekMath (Shao et al., 2024) to the task of precise instruction following, a domain previously addressed primarily through targeted synthetic SFT data or DPO. It departs from the IFEval benchmark (Zhou et al., 2023) by introducing IFBENCH with 58 unseen verifiable constraints to expose overfitting, and from prior IF benchmarks like FollowBench and InFoBench by maintaining fully automatic verifiability rather than relying on LLM-as-judge scoring. The paper also positions itself against VFF (Wang et al., 2025), the only other work generating verifiable IF training data, by adding RLVR training and a systematic generalization analysis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that frontier models (GPT-4.1, Claude 3.7 Sonnet, Qwen3-32B) score below 50% on IFBENCH while exceeding 80% on IFEval reveals a critical evaluation gap: the field's primary IF benchmark has saturated and no longer measures genuine constraint-following ability. IFBENCH and IFTRAIN provide the community with tools to measure and improve generalization, while the IF-RLVR recipe offers a practical, well-ablated training method applicable across model families. Key open questions include how to balance constraint adherence with task quality (the reward hacking / over-optimization problem), whether the approach extends to non-verifiable constraints that dominate real-world usage, and how to integrate IF-RLVR with other post-training objectives without degrading downstream performance.
