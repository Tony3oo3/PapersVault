## Problem

GUI automation agents operating in online interactive environments are highly vulnerable to single-step errors that can cascade into irreversible outcomes (e.g., file deletion, erroneous payments). Existing reflection and critic mechanisms operate post-operatively—after the action has already been executed—making them too late to prevent dangerous mistakes. This paper introduces a pre-operative critic that diagnoses the correctness of a proposed action before it is executed, filling a gap in both the mechanism and the training data needed for such a model.

## Input / Output

The model receives a GUI screenshot, the user's natural-language instruction, the textual action history, and the agent's proposed next action (e.g., click, type, scroll). It produces a structured output in a progressive chain-of-thought format: a <thinking> block containing three sub-components (Observation of the interface, Possible Result of the action, and Critique of correctness), followed by a <score> field with a binary correctness label, and a <suggestion> field with a natural-language corrective action. The model is a 7B-parameter MLLM (Qwen2.5-VL-7B backbone) designed for real-time, plug-and-play integration into existing GUI agent frameworks.

## Method

GUI-Critic-R1 is trained in two stages. First, an RFT cold-start performs supervised fine-tuning on a 6K-sample GUI-Critic-Train dataset constructed via a reasoning-bootstrapping pipeline: negative operations are sampled from MLLM predictions and filtered by rule-based criteria plus GPT-4o verification, while chain-of-thought critiques are generated without conditioning on ground-truth labels to avoid annotation bias, then selected only when the generated score and suggestion match the annotation. Second, a Suggestion-aware Group Relative Policy Optimization (S-GRPO) stage extends standard GRPO with a novel suggestion reward that uses an LLM-based similarity judge to verify whether the model's corrective suggestion semantically matches the ground-truth action, alongside format and accuracy rewards. This suggestion reward specifically incentivizes the model to produce actionable, correct fix suggestions rather than merely flagging errors, and the group-relative advantage normalization across sampled outputs drives iterative self-improvement of the reasoning process.

## Results

On the static GUI-Critic-Test benchmark, GUI-Critic-R1 achieves 69.20% critic accuracy and 52.43% suggestion accuracy on GUI-I (mobile instruction generalization), surpassing the base Qwen2.5-VL-7B by +14.32 and +9.29 points respectively. On the dynamic AndroidWorld benchmark, GUI-Critic-R1 as a pre-critic improves the success rate from 22.4% (baseline) to 27.6% and achieves a 31.8% Efficiency Advantage Rate (EAR), outperforming GPT-4o as pre-critic (22.4% SR, 26.1% EAR). The model also generalizes to web scenarios (GUI-W) with 63.08% critic accuracy, a +3.97 gain over the base model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GPT-4o, GUI-Critic-R1 wins on GUI-I critic accuracy (69.20 vs. 66.01) and suggestion accuracy (52.43 vs. 40.54), but loses on GUI-W critic accuracy (63.08 vs. 68.45), indicating GPT-4o retains an edge in web-domain generalization. Against the base model Qwen2.5-VL-7B, GUI-Critic-R1 achieves substantial gains across all three settings, with the largest improvement on GUI-I (+14.32 critic accuracy) and GUI-S suggestion accuracy (+9.65). Compared to the larger Qwen2.5-VL-72B, GUI-Critic-R1 surpasses it on GUI-I (69.20 vs. 56.40 critic accuracy) and GUI-S suggestion accuracy (47.37 vs. 38.79), demonstrating that the specialized S-GRPO training is more effective than simply scaling model size. Against open-source baselines DeepSeek-VL2-7B, InternVL2.5-8B, and Qwen2.0-VL-7B/72B, GUI-Critic-R1 dominates on all metrics, with particularly large margins over DeepSeek-VL2-7B which scores 0% suggestion accuracy. On the dynamic AndroidWorld evaluation, GUI-Critic-R1 as pre-critic (27.6% SR, 31.8% EAR) outperforms both GPT-4o pre-critic (22.4% SR, 26.1% EAR) and Qwen2.5-VL-72B pre-critic (23.2% SR, 24.4% EAR), confirming its superiority in real-time error diagnosis.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GRPO reinforcement learning framework from DeepSeek-R1 by introducing a suggestion-aware reward specifically designed for GUI operation critique, forming S-GRPO. It departs from prior critic models for LLMs/VLMs (Critic-V, LLaVA-Critic) that operate on offline tasks by targeting the online, interactive setting of GUI automation where errors can be irreversible. The pre-operative critic mechanism also contrasts with post-operative reflection modules in multi-agent GUI frameworks (e.g., Mobile-Agent-v2), which only detect errors after execution and require additional corrective steps. The reasoning-bootstrapping data pipeline is a novel contribution that addresses the absence of dedicated GUI critic training data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The pre-operative critic addresses a practical and underexplored failure mode in GUI automation: irreversible errors (file deletion, erroneous payments) that post-hoc reflection cannot remedy. The finding that a 7B specialized model outperforms GPT-4o as a pre-critic suggests that domain-specific GUI common-sense, rather than raw model scale, is the critical factor for anticipating interaction outcomes. Limitations include reliance on single-step visual information (no trajectory-level critique), evaluation limited to Android and web platforms, and the modest absolute success rate improvement (22.4% → 27.6%) on AndroidWorld, leaving substantial room for improvement in overall GUI agent reliability.
