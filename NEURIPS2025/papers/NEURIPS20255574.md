## Problem

Boltzmann Generators based on continuous normalizing flows require expensive Jacobian trace integrals along the ODE path to compute sample likelihoods for reweighting, making them impractical for large molecular systems. This paper asks whether the likelihood of a generative model can be efficiently amortized by a learned surrogate, decoupling likelihood estimation from the sampling vector field and enabling unbiased Boltzmann statistics at scale.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255574/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes molecular conformers represented as 3D Cartesian coordinates with atom types (featurized as fully connected graphs with amino acid identity and positional information for transferable models), along with a standard Gaussian prior. The EBM takes a conformer and a continuous time point t as joint input, outputting a scalar energy that serves as the log-likelihood surrogate up to a normalization constant. The final output is a set of importance-weighted conformers from which free energy profiles, energy histograms, and arbitrary Boltzmann-averaged observables can be estimated via self-normalized importance sampling.

## Method

BoltzNCE operates in two stages: first, a Boltzmann Emulator (GVP or ECNF) is trained via stochastic interpolant flow matching to generate conformers; second, an EBM (Graphormer) is trained on those generated samples using a hybrid loss that combines denoising score matching with an InfoNCE objective defined over a second stochastic interpolant from the Gaussian prior to the generated distribution. The InfoNCE loss is reformulated as a multi-class time-discrimination problem: given an interpolant sample at time t, the model must distinguish the true t from negative times sampled from a narrow Gaussian centered at t, which mitigates the density-chasm problem by ensuring informative contrast. The EBM directly parameterizes U_t(x) − log Z_t, absorbing the intractable normalization constant as a time-dependent bias, so that evaluating log ρ̂₁(x) reduces to a single forward pass at t=1. Both objectives are simulation-free after the initial generation step, and the combined loss is shown to outperform either objective alone, with InfoNCE acting as a regularizer that improves mode weighting relative to score matching in isolation.

## Results

On alanine dipeptide, BoltzNCE-VF achieves the lowest free energy error (0.02±0.13 kBT) and lowest reweighted energy W2 distance (0.27±0.02) among all methods, while completing inference on 10⁶ conformers in 0.09 h—approximately 100× faster than ECNF (9.37 h) and 200× faster than GVP-VF (18.4 h). On the 7-dipeptide generalizability benchmark, BoltzNCE runs in 4.0 h versus 24.0 h for MD and 123.1 h for TBG-ECNF, a ~6× speedup over MD, though at the cost of higher free energy error (0.43±0.21 vs. 0.18±0.22 for MD). On 2D synthetic systems, combining score matching and InfoNCE reduces KL-divergence to 0.0150 (8-mode Gaussian) and 0.1987 (checkerboard), far below either objective alone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is the Transferable Boltzmann Generator (TBG-ECNF) from Klein & Noé [8], which uses an equivariant continuous normalizing flow with exact Jacobian-trace likelihoods. On alanine dipeptide, BoltzNCE-VF matches or slightly exceeds ECNF's free energy accuracy (ΔF error 0.02 vs. 0.03 kBT) and achieves a lower reweighted E-W2 (0.27 vs. 0.37), while running ~100× faster (0.09 h vs. 9.37 h for 10⁶ conformers). The GVP-based emulators (both vector-field and endpoint variants) outperform ECNF as pure emulators but degrade significantly as generators due to unstable Jacobian-trace integration; BoltzNCE resolves this by decoupling likelihood estimation into a separate EBM forward pass. On the 7-dipeptide generalizability test, BoltzNCE is ~6× faster than 1 µs MD (4.0 h vs. 24.0 h) and ~30× faster than TBG-ECNF (4.0 h vs. 123.1 h), though it trails both in free energy error (0.43 vs. 0.18 for MD, 0.13 for TBG-ECNF) and E-W2 (1.08 vs. 0.17 for MD), reflecting the approximate nature of EBM likelihoods on unseen systems.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of Boltzmann Generators (Noé et al. 2019; Klein & Noé 2024), which reweight flow-matching samples to recover exact Boltzmann statistics, and energy-based models trained via noise contrastive estimation (Gutmann & Hyvärinen 2010; van den Oord et al. 2018). The key departure is decoupling the sampling model (a Boltzmann Emulator built on stochastic interpolants/flow matching) from the likelihood model (a Graphormer-based EBM), replacing the expensive Jacobian trace integral with a single EBM forward pass. The training objective is novel in combining denoising score matching over a stochastic interpolant with an InfoNCE loss that performs multi-class time discrimination, using the interpolant's annealing structure to mitigate the density-chasm problem that plagues standard NCE.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By amortizing likelihoods into a single EBM evaluation, BoltzNCE makes Boltzmann reweighting practical for systems where Jacobian trace integration is intractable, achieving 100× faster inference on alanine dipeptide while matching or exceeding the accuracy of exact likelihoods. The transfer learning result (6× speedup over MD on unseen dipeptides with minimal fine-tuning) suggests a path toward scalable equilibrium sampling for larger biomolecular systems. However, the method is validated only on dipeptides, and the authors acknowledge that performance degrades in the generalization setting; scaling to full proteins or larger systems remains an open question that will require further architectural and hyperparameter exploration.
