## Problem

LiDAR-based place recognition traditionally depends on costly, time-consuming prior 3D maps, and existing alternatives (OSM, street-view, pseudo-point-cloud) either restrict retrieval to known routes or small areas. This paper tackles the unexplored challenge of cross-view (ground vs. aerial) and cross-modal (LiDAR vs. remote sensing) place recognition over large-scale urban scenes exceeding 100 km², using only a LiDAR and a magnetometer as onboard sensors.

## Input / Output

The method consumes short LiDAR scan sequences (registered into 60 m × 60 m point cloud submaps via FastGICP), a magnetometer heading (±10° accuracy), and a pre-built database of ~110,000 semantic remote sensing submaps at 0.5 m resolution. Point cloud submaps are semantically segmented (roads, sidewalks, vegetation, buildings) and projected into north-aligned bird's-eye-view images; remote sensing submaps are similarly segmented and tiled at 20 m intervals. The output is the UTM center coordinate of the most probable remote sensing submap, with correctness defined as Euclidean distance < 30 m from the true query center.

## Method

L2RSI bridges the cross-modal gap by operating entirely in a shared semantic domain: a dual-branch network with fully shared weights (MAE-B encoder → GeM pooling → FCN) encodes both BEV semantic images and remote sensing semantic submaps into a common descriptor space, trained with symmetric InfoNCE contrastive loss. The key architectural insight is that while raw LiDAR and satellite imagery differ drastically in appearance, their high-level semantic layouts (road topology, building footprints, vegetation) are strongly correlated, making semantic alignment feasible. At inference, a novel Spatial-Temporal Particle Estimation (STPE) module refines retrieval by propagating top-K candidate particles from a sliding window of L=50 sequential queries through a motion model, fitting a Gaussian Mixture Model to the aggregated particle distribution, and re-ranking database submaps by integrated probability density—replacing the fragile particle filtering approach that suffers from particle decay under cross-modal ambiguity.

## Results

On the LiRSI-XA dataset, L2RSI (with STPE) achieves Recall@1 of 88.93% on the 4 km² test set and maintains 83.27% Recall@1 even at the full 100 km² retrieval range (Test Set-G), with only a 5.66% degradation from the smallest to the largest database. On the cross-scene LiRSI-Oxford benchmark (no fine-tuning), L2RSI achieves Recall@1 of 42.29% and 43.77% on two trajectories, while all baselines drop below 8%. The total inference time is 37.5 ms (5.8 ms for global descriptor extraction + 31.7 ms for STPE), enabling real-time operation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

L2RSI (w. STPE) decisively outperforms all four baselines across every test set. On the most challenging 100 km² retrieval (Test Set-G), L2RSI achieves 83.27% Recall@1, surpassing the strongest baseline Sample4Geo (22.95%) by over 60 percentage points, and FRGeo (15.54%), LIP-Loc (11.02%), and GeoDTR (4.39%) by even wider margins. The cross-scene generalization gap is even more dramatic: on LiRSI-Oxford, L2RSI reaches 42–44% Recall@1 while the best baseline (Sample4Geo) manages only 7–8%, and LIP-Loc, GeoDTR, and FRGeo all fall below 2%. Among the baselines, Sample4Geo consistently ranks first (leveraging ConvNeXt-B and dynamic similarity sampling), followed by FRGeo, LIP-Loc, and GeoDTR, but none approach L2RSI's performance. The paper also shows that adding STPE to any baseline (Table 11) yields substantial gains, confirming that the spatio-temporal aggregation is the key differentiator beyond the semantic contrastive learning backbone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of LiDAR-based place recognition (which traditionally requires costly prior 3D maps) and cross-view geolocalization (which typically uses street-level images as queries). It repurposes the semantic contrastive learning paradigm from CLIP-like cross-modal alignment to bridge the LiDAR-to-remote-sensing gap, and introduces Spatial-Temporal Particle Estimation (STPE) as a deliberate departure from standard particle filtering, replacing particle decay/re-initialization with Gaussian mixture aggregation. Prior work by Tang et al. achieved LiDAR-to-overhead retrieval but only along known routes, and Cho et al. used OSM in small areas; L2RSI extends the retrieval range to 100 km² with no route assumption, while the semantic-domain alignment strategy draws on the observation that LiDAR and RSI share high-level semantics despite large appearance gaps.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating 83.27% Recall@1 over a 100 km² urban area using only LiDAR and a magnetometer against a remote sensing database, this work shows that large-scale LiDAR place recognition can be decoupled from expensive 3D map construction, with practical implications for autonomous driving and robotic navigation in GPS-denied environments. Key limitations include the dependence on an external magnetometer for orientation, the three-year temporal gap between LiDAR and RSI data, and the urban-scene focus (rural areas with sparse semantics may degrade performance). Open questions include whether the magnetometer requirement can be eliminated, how the method scales to non-urban environments, and whether the STPE aggregation principle generalizes to other cross-modal sequential estimation tasks beyond place recognition.
