## Problem

In multi-camera, multi-time-step autonomous driving reconstruction, photometric inconsistencies (varying illumination, camera settings, ISP effects) cause geometric artifacts such as floaters that degrade 3D accuracy. Existing remedies—appearance codes (a single global affine transform per image) and bilateral grids (pixel-wise transforms)—each cover only part of the variation spectrum: appearance codes cannot model local lighting shifts, while bilateral grids are notoriously difficult to optimize and constrain in large scenes. The paper asks whether these two paradigms can be unified into one trainable framework that handles both global and local photometric discrepancies.

## Input / Output

The method consumes synchronized multi-view RGB images (5–7 cameras per ego-vehicle) and LiDAR depth maps from driving sequences, with bottom crops applied to rear cameras to remove ego-vehicle occlusion. It produces a hybrid 3D Gaussian scene graph—sky environment map, static background Gaussians with spherical-harmonics appearance, and dynamic objects in canonical space with SE(3) pose sequences (plus optional deformation networks for pedestrians)—together with per-view, per-level bilateral grid tensors. At inference the scene renders novel-view RGB and depth maps, with the bilateral grid applying a learned per-pixel 3×4 affine color correction driven by a luminance guidance map of the rendered image.

## Method

The core contribution is a three-level bilateral grid pyramid (2×2×1, 4×4×2, 8×8×4 spatial×intensity bins, each storing a flattened 3×4 affine color transform) that hierarchically composes photometric corrections from coarse to fine. A luminance-based guidance map derived from the rendered image drives a slice operation at each level, retrieving localized affine coefficients via trilinear interpolation; the resulting level-specific transformations are then sequentially composed (coarse→fine) to yield the final enhanced image. At the coarsest scale the grid degenerates to a global appearance code, while at the finest it approximates a pixel-wise bilateral grid, so the framework naturally interpolates between the two paradigms depending on scale. Training uses level-dependent learning rates (coarse grids update faster), an adaptive total-variation regularizer whose weight scales with grid resolution, and a circle-regularization loss that applies the inverse affine transform to ground-truth images to prevent over-correction. The full scene is optimized jointly with a composite loss (L1 + SSIM + depth + opacity) on a single NVIDIA L40 GPU, requiring only ~4 M grid parameters versus ~28 M for a single large bilateral grid.

## Results

The proposed multi-scale bilateral grid achieves state-of-the-art results across all four driving datasets. On Waymo, it reduces Chamfer Distance to 0.989 (a 28.2% improvement over the best baseline OmniRe w/AC at 1.378) while achieving the highest reconstruction PSNR of 29.23 and NVS PSNR of 26.55. On NuScenes, the method reaches a reconstruction PSNR of 27.69 (vs. 26.37 for OmniRe) and CD of 1.161 (vs. 1.458 for OmniRe). The single bilateral grid baseline (w/BG) consistently degrades performance (e.g., NVS PSNR drops to 21.51 on Waymo), confirming the optimization challenges that the multi-scale hierarchy resolves.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against OmniRe (the base Gaussian Splatting framework), the proposed method wins on every metric across all four datasets, with the largest gains in geometric accuracy (e.g., CD drops from 1.482 to 0.989 on Waymo, a 33% reduction). Compared to OmniRe with appearance codes (w/AC), the multi-scale bilateral grid consistently outperforms, most notably in CD (0.989 vs. 1.378 on Waymo; 1.161 vs. 1.437 on NuScenes) and reconstruction PSNR (+0.28 dB on Waymo, +1.31 dB on NuScenes). The single bilateral grid baseline (w/BG) is the weakest performer, often degrading NVS PSNR by 4–5 dB (e.g., 21.51 vs. 26.40 on Waymo), demonstrating that a single high-resolution grid fails to converge without the coarse-to-fine initialization. When integrated into ChatSim, the method improves reconstruction PSNR from 25.10 to 27.04 and CD from 1.557 to 1.236 on NuScenes. Similarly, integrating into StreetGS boosts PSNR from 25.74 to 27.90 and reduces CD from 1.604 to 1.272, confirming the module's generalizability beyond the OmniRe architecture.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the OmniRe/NeuRAD driving scene reconstruction framework (Gaussian Splatting with hybrid scene graphs for sky, background, and dynamic objects) and the bilateral grid lineage from Chen et al. (2007) and Gharbi et al. (2017). It departs from prior work by unifying two previously separate photometric correction paradigms—global appearance codes and pixel-wise bilateral grids—into a single three-level hierarchical grid that naturally interpolates between them at extreme scales. The coarse-to-fine composition strategy and the use of downsampled guidance maps for patch-wise slicing are the key architectural departures from both the single-grid and single-code approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes a concrete link between photometric inconsistency and geometric degradation (floaters) in driving scene reconstruction, showing that better appearance modeling directly improves 3D geometry—a finding important for safety-critical autonomous driving applications. The method's generalizability (substantial gains when plugged into ChatSim and StreetGS) suggests the multi-scale bilateral grid is a broadly useful module rather than a bespoke fix. Open questions include whether the approach scales to extremely fast-moving or highly non-rigid objects where LiDAR-camera misalignment is severe, and whether the ~9% training overhead over appearance codes is acceptable in resource-constrained deployment settings.
