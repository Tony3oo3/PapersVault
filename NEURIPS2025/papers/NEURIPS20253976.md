## Problem

Existing MLLM reasoning methods perform visual perception only once before entering the reasoning phase, so scaling test-time compute (e.g., Best-of-N sampling, longer chain-of-thought) cannot remedy poor initial perception. The paper asks whether MLLMs can dynamically refine their visual focus during inference—mimicking human hierarchical attention—to improve reasoning without additional training data or model parameters.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253976/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts video clips (4–768 frames sampled at 2 FPS, resolution capped at 768×2 pixels) or RGB images alongside a text question. During iterative perception, the model outputs spatio-temporal coordinates (bounding boxes for images, time intervals for video) that drive re-sampling: for video, dense frame extraction within identified temporal segments with sparse sampling elsewhere; for images, concurrent processing of the full frame and cropped ROI regions. The final output is a text answer accompanied by chain-of-thought reasoning and the predicted region-of-interest coordinates, with a default of 3 perception iterations at inference.

## Method

VTTS extends the standard autoregressive MLLM objective to model visual dependencies across iterations: at each step k, the model predicts a textual answer and spatio-temporal ROI, then a sampling function δ generates a new focused visual V_{k+1} from V_k conditioned on that prediction. Training uses GRPO-based reinforcement fine-tuning with a composite reward combining IoU-based spatio-temporal alignment, answer accuracy, and format compliance—replacing SFT's discretized cross-entropy with a continuous, metric-aware penalty that preserves sub-second and sub-pixel precision. The VTTS-80K dataset (15K temporal clues, 30K spatial clues, 80K CoT annotations, 50K QA pairs) provides the supervision signal, and the approach is applied to Qwen2.5-VL-3B/7B backbones, yielding 5–6% average gains across 15+ benchmarks without additional long-video training data.

## Results

VideoChat-R1.5-7B (with 3 iterations of iterative perception) achieves 70.6% on MVBench, 72.3% on PerceptionTest, 62.6% on LongVideoBench, 51.4% on VideoMMMU, 67.1% on VideoMME, and 48.4% on LVBench, improving over the Qwen2.5-VL-7B baseline by 2.2%, 1.8%, 6.6%, 4.7%, 2.0%, and 3.1% respectively. On grounded video QA (NextGQA), the 7B model reaches 79.9% Acc@IoP@0.5 and 74.9% mIoP, while on ReXTime it achieves 74.8% accuracy and 45.8% mIoU, surpassing GPT-4o (73.7% Acc, 36.3% mIoU). On temporal grounding (Charades-STA), the 7B model attains 60.6% mIoU and 71.6% R@0.5, outperforming the specialized InternVideo2-6B model. The 3B variant shows even larger relative gains (up to +5.4% on LongVideoBench), demonstrating the method's effectiveness at smaller scales.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary baseline Qwen2.5-VL (3B/7B), VideoChat-R1.5 achieves consistent gains of 1.4–6.6% across all seven video QA benchmarks, with the largest improvements on long-video tasks (LongVideoBench +6.6%, LVBench +3.1%) and video knowledge (VideoMMMU +4.7%). On ReXTime, VideoChat-R1.5-7B (74.8% Acc, 45.8% mIoU) surpasses GPT-4o (73.7% Acc, 36.3% mIoU) and Claude-3-Opus (68.7% Acc, 28.4% mIoU) on all grounding metrics. On temporal grounding (Charades-STA), the 7B model (60.6% mIoU, 71.6% R@0.5) outperforms the specialized InternVideo2-6B (70.0% R@0.5) and TimeSuite (44.8% mIoU on QVHighlight), while in zero-shot settings it improves over Qwen2.5-VL-7B by 22.1% mIoU on QVHighlight. On spatial grounding (RefCOCO), VideoChat-R1.5-7B (91.1/93.6/86.7) approaches Grounding DINO-L (90.6/93.2/88.3) despite training on only 16K in-domain samples, and on GOT-10k tracking it achieves 52.2% AO versus 12.6% for Qwen2.5-VL-7B. The method does not surpass Gemini-1.5-Pro on VideoMME (67.1% vs. 75.0%) or GPT-4o on LongVideoBench (62.6% vs. 66.7%), indicating that proprietary models retain an edge on some long-context video understanding tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends VideoChat-R1's reinforcement fine-tuning for spatio-temporal perception by introducing Visual Test-Time Scaling (VTTS), which adds an Iterative Perception (ITP) mechanism at inference time. It departs from prior test-time scaling methods (Best-of-N, beam search, MCTS) that perform a single perception pass before extended reasoning, instead dynamically reallocating visual compute across multiple perception cycles guided by evolving textual predictions. The RL training leverages GRPO from DeepSeek-R1 with a custom IoU-based spatio-temporal reward, and the approach is conceptually inspired by V* and Visual-CoT's top-down visual search, but generalizes from one-shot to multi-step iterative refinement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes a practical 'perception scaling law'—consistently gaining 1–6% across 15+ benchmarks by simply increasing the number of perception iterations at test time—offering a compute-for-accuracy trade-off that practitioners can tune. The ablation showing that SFT actively degrades spatio-temporal grounding (e.g., OCRBench drops from 856 to 815) while RL improves it is a useful negative result for anyone fine-tuning MLLMs on continuous targets. Key limitations include validation only on the Qwen2.5-VL backbone, unoptimized iterative inference (no caching of shared visual tokens), and restriction to the visual modality, leaving open whether the scaling law holds with more iterations or across architectures.
