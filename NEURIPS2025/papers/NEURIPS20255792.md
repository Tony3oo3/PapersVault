## Problem

Most causal discovery algorithms assume both acyclicity and causal sufficiency (no unmeasured confounders), yet real-world systems—especially biological networks—exhibit feedback loops and hidden confounders simultaneously. Existing methods that handle confounders are restricted to linear SEMs or acyclic graphs, and no unified framework existed for learning nonlinear cyclic causal structures with unmeasured confounders from interventional data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255792/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes tabular data from multiple experimental conditions: one observational dataset plus single-node interventional datasets (one per node), with known interventional target sets specifying which variables were surgically set. It outputs a directed mixed graph (DMG) where directed edges encode causal dependencies and bidirectional edges encode confounding, along with the full covariance matrix Σ_Z of the Gaussian exogenous noise (whose off-diagonal entries identify confounded variable pairs) and the neural network parameters defining the causal mechanisms.

## Method

DCCD-CONF models each causal mechanism as an implicit flow with contractive neural networks (enforced via spectral normalization), guaranteeing a unique fixed point for the cyclic SEM. Confounders are captured by allowing the Gaussian exogenous noise vector Z to have a non-diagonal covariance Σ_Z, so that correlated noise terms induce bidirectional edges in the DMG. The graph structure is learned by relaxing the binary adjacency matrix with Gumbel-Softmax sampling and optimizing a regularized log-likelihood score over all interventional settings, with an L1 sparsity penalty. Training alternates between (i) backpropagating through the implicit flow to update network weights and graph parameters, and (ii) updating Σ_Z via a column-wise graphical lasso (sparse inverse covariance estimation) to maintain positive definiteness. The log-determinant of the Jacobian required for the change-of-variables density is computed efficiently using a stochastic power-series estimator combined with the Hutchinson trace estimator, avoiding the O(d²) gradient calls of exact computation.

## Results

On the Perturb-CITE-seq gene perturbation dataset (61 genes, 218K cells), DCCD-CONF achieves the lowest negative log-likelihood (NLL) across all three cell conditions: 1.375 (Control), 1.245 (Co-Culture), and 1.235 (IFN-γ), outperforming NODAGS-Flow (1.465/1.406/1.504), LLC (1.385/1.325/1.430), and DCDI (1.523/1.367/1.517). On synthetic experiments with d=10 nodes, DCCD-CONF consistently achieves the lowest normalized structural Hamming distance (SHD) and highest F1 score for confounder identification across varying confounder counts (0.2–0.8 ratio), cycle counts (0–8), nonlinearity levels (β=0 to 1), and intervention counts (0–10). Scaling to d=80 nodes, DCCD-CONF maintains its advantage over all baselines in both directed edge recovery and confounder identification.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DCCD-CONF outperforms all four main baselines (NODAGS-Flow, LLC, DAGMA, ADMG) on synthetic nonlinear SEMs across all tested dimensions of confounders, cycles, nonlinearity, and interventions, achieving the lowest SHD and highest F1 for confounder identification. On the real-world Perturb-CITE-seq dataset, DCCD-CONF achieves the best NLL in all three conditions, with the largest margin over NODAGS-Flow (~0.09–0.27 NLL) and DCDI (~0.15–0.29 NLL), and a smaller but consistent margin over LLC (~0.01–0.19 NLL). In the linear regime (β near 0), LLC slightly outperforms DCCD-CONF, with both performing comparably around β=0.25; however, as nonlinearity increases, DCCD-CONF pulls ahead decisively. DCCD-CONF also demonstrates superior scalability, maintaining its performance advantage as the number of nodes grows from 10 to 80, whereas baselines degrade more sharply. Notably, DCCD-CONF is the only method that simultaneously handles nonlinearity, directed cycles, unmeasured confounders, and interventional data, whereas each baseline is limited in at least one of these dimensions.

## Relation to Prior Work

DCCD-CONF directly extends NODAGS-Flow (Sethuraman et al., AISTATS 2023), which learned nonlinear cyclic causal graphs via implicit flows but assumed no unmeasured confounders, by modeling confounding through correlated Gaussian exogenous noise and jointly estimating the confounder covariance. The implicit flow architecture with contractive functions is inherited from Lu et al. (ICLR 2021), while the Gumbel-Softmax graph parameterization and regularized log-likelihood score function follow Brouillard et al. (NeurIPS 2020). The key conceptual departure is unifying four challenges—directed cycles, nonlinearity, latent confounders, and interventional data—into a single differentiable optimization framework, whereas prior methods (LLC, DAGMA, ADMG, LiNGAM-MMI) each handle only a subset of these simultaneously.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to jointly handle nonlinearity, cycles, unmeasured confounders, and interventions in a differentiable setting, with a consistency guarantee (Theorem 2) showing recovery of the interventional Markov equivalence class. The Gaussian exogenous noise assumption is a notable limitation, and the paper explicitly identifies non-Gaussian noise, missing data, soft interventions, and unknown interventional targets as open directions. For biological network inference (e.g., gene regulatory networks with feedback loops and hidden regulators), this fills a practical gap where existing constraint-based methods (FCI, JCI-FCI) lack scalability and score-based methods lack confounder modeling. The open question of necessary and sufficient conditions on interventional targets for perfect recovery in general DMGs with cycles and confounders remains unresolved.
