## Problem

Outcome-based RL fine-tuning significantly increases hallucinations in reasoning models: models become more fluent and confident in their chain-of-thought while introducing more factually incorrect intermediate claims. The paper identifies three theoretical causes—high-variance policy gradients under sparse binary rewards, entropy-induced randomness needed for exploration, and spurious local optima where the model confidently outputs wrong answers with zero gradient signal—but prior work has not addressed how to mitigate these dynamics during RL training.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a text question paired with supporting Wikipedia evidence segments (from HotpotQA/2WikiMultiHopQA) or a math problem (from SimpleRL), along with a base LLM (Qwen2.5-7B or Llama-3.1-8B). During training, the model generates a full reasoning chain followed by a final answer; each generated sentence is then checked by the HHEM-2.1 verifier against the evidence to produce a step-wise factuality score. The output is a fine-tuned model that produces reasoning chains with higher factual fidelity and improved answer accuracy on both knowledge-intensive QA and mathematical benchmarks.

## Method

FSPO extends GRPO by introducing a step-wise factuality reward computed via an automated entailment verifier (HHEM-2.1) that labels each reasoning sentence as entailed (+1), neutral (0), or contradicted (−1) by the given evidence. The final reward combines the binary answer-correctness signal with the mean factuality score across all reasoning steps, yielding a denser reward that reduces gradient variance. The key mechanism is a factuality-aware advantage adjustment: for each token, if it belongs to a factually contradicted sentence but the group-level advantage is positive, the advantage is flipped to penalize that token, and vice versa for entailed sentences with negative advantage. This ensures the policy gradient is non-zero even when the final answer is wrong, directly escaping the spurious local optima identified in the theoretical analysis while incentivizing factual correctness at every reasoning step.

## Results

FSPO(Qwen2.5-7B-Base) achieves 89.5% on GSM8K, 75.5% on MATH-500, and 16.7%/13.3% on AIME 2024/2025 (Pass@1), substantially outperforming the base model (65.2%/35.7%/3.3%/3.3%) and other open-source baselines. On hallucination benchmarks, FSPO(Qwen-Base) reaches 58.4% on TruthfulQA, 83.0% on HaluEval-QA, and 52.0% on HalluQA, surpassing all open-source and distilled reasoning models and even exceeding GPT-o1 (62.4/70.2/56.0) on TruthfulQA and HaluEval-QA. The method also generalizes across backbones: FSPO(Llama-Instruct) improves from 77.5% to 86.2% on GSM8K and from 36.7% to 67.1% on HaluEval-QA over the unmodified Llama3.1-8B-Instruct.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against open-source baselines, FSPO(Qwen-Base) improves GSM8K by +16.3 pp over Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct (89.5 vs 73.2) and +12.0 pp over Llama3.1-8B-Instruct (89.5 vs 77.5), while HaluEval-QA accuracy jumps from 38.4% to 83.0% (+44.6 pp) over Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct. Against distilled reasoning models (R1-Distill-Qwen-7B, R1-Distill-Llama-8B), FSPO dominates on all three hallucination benchmarks (e.g., 83.0 vs 11.6 on HaluEval-QA) while remaining competitive on GSM8K (89.5 vs 84.3/82.1). Against API-based models, FSPO(Qwen-Base) surpasses GPT-o1 on HaluEval-QA (83.0 vs 70.2) and TruthfulQA (58.4 vs 62.4 is a loss, but 58.4 vs 53.0 beats DeepSeek-V3), yet trails DeepSeek-R1 and GPT-o1 on mathematical reasoning (MATH-500: 75.5 vs 97.3/96.4; AIME 2024: 16.7 vs 79.8/79.2). The ablation (GRPO vs GRPO+factuality vs FSPO) confirms that the step-wise advantage adjustment in Eq. 11, rather than merely adding a factuality reward to the scalar, is what drives the hallucination reduction. FSPO also generalizes to Reinforce++, showing comparable gains over the unmodified algorithm on both MATH-500 and HaluEval-QA.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on GRPO (from DeepSeek-Math) as its base RL algorithm and on the broader RL-for-reasoning paradigm established by DeepSeek-R1, QwQ, and Kimi k1.5. It departs from prior hallucination mitigation work—which has focused on pre-training data curation, SFT, RLHF, or post-hoc verification—by identifying hallucination as an emergent *training-time* side effect of outcome-based RL and addressing it within the policy optimization loop itself. The key conceptual departure is the step-wise factuality-aware advantage adjustment (Eq. 11), which re-weights token-level advantages based on per-sentence entailment against evidence, rather than applying a single scalar reward to the entire response as in standard GRPO or PPO.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that RL fine-tuning systematically increases hallucination rates (e.g., R1-Distill-Qwen-7B drops from 38.2 to 6.9 on TruthfulQA) is a timely warning for a field heavily investing in RL-based reasoning, and the three-factor theoretical explanation (high-variance gradient, entropy pressure, spurious local optima) provides a mechanistic account that guides future reward design. FSPO is a practical, plug-and-play modification to GRPO that requires only an external factuality verifier, making it accessible to existing RL pipelines. Open questions include whether the approach scales to 14B+ models, how sensitive results are to verifier quality (HHEM-2.1 is itself an LLM), and whether the method generalizes to tasks without clear textual evidence (e.g., pure mathematical derivation).
