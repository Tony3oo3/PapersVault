## Problem

Both post-hoc feature attribution methods and intrinsically interpretable additive models (GAMs, NAMs, EBMs) systematically assign spurious importance to non-informative suppressor variables, because their shape functions reflect how features must be transformed for prediction rather than their statistical association with the target. While the activation pattern correction was established for linear models, no principled generalisation existed for the broad family of non-linear additive models, leaving their conventional shape-function interpretations theoretically unjustified in the presence of correlated or suppressor features.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a trained additive model (NAM, EBM, or QLR) together with the training features and labels, from which it extracts the model's univariate and bivariate shape functions as a feature vector z. It produces corrected global explanations in the form of PatternGAM/PatternQLR shape functions (linear rescalings of the original shape functions fitted to true labels), scalar importance metrics (PAT, SD, DISCR, PROD) for each univariate and bivariate term, and aggregated input-space importance scores via a max-based combination rule that prevents bivariate importance from leaking into individual feature scores.

## Method

PatternGAM extends the linear activation pattern to additive models by collecting the fitted GAM shape functions into a feature vector z^GAM, re-estimating their weights via logistic regression, standardising, and then computing the Pattern as Cov[z^GAM, g(ŷ^GAM)]·Var[g(ŷ^GAM)]^{-1}, which provably satisfies the Statistical Association Property (SAP) for univariate features. PatternQLR applies the same principle to a degree-2 polynomial expansion of the input, making quadratic logistic regression interpretable. For scale-invariant interpretation, the method additionally fits D univariate logistic regression models of the form f_i^P(x) = b_i·f_i^GAM(x) + d_i against true labels, yielding shape functions whose coefficients and standard deviations are comparable across features. The paper also introduces FNI-EMDA, a false-negative-invariant variant of earth mover's distance accuracy that sets transport costs between ground-truth features to zero, avoiding unfair penalisation of subset-style explanations while preserving higher score resolution than vanilla EMD.

## Results

On the XAI-TRIS benchmark (12 scenarios spanning linear, multiplicative, and XOR tasks with white, correlated, and distractor noise), the proposed PROD(f^GAM) metric achieves near-perfect FNI-EMDA scores of 0.99–1.00 across virtually all settings. SD(f^PQLR) similarly reaches 0.94–1.00, while DISCR(f^GAM) attains 0.88–0.97. In contrast, EBM drops to 0.59–0.68 on XOR scenarios and Kernel Pattern falls to 0.62–0.68 on MULT scenarios, demonstrating systematic false-positive attribution to suppressor variables. On real-world COMPAS and MIMIC-IV datasets, PatternGAM correctly nullifies suppressor features (e.g., race, sex, Hematocrit) that conventional GAM shape functions misleadingly highlight.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares PatternGAM/PatternQLR against six baselines: EBM, Kernel Pattern, PatternNet, PatternAttribution, SHAP, and Integrated Gradients. PROD(f^GAM) achieves near-perfect FNI-EMDA (0.99–1.00) across all 12 XAI-TRIS scenarios, decisively outperforming EBM (which collapses to 0.59–0.68 on XOR) and Kernel Pattern (which drops to 0.62–0.68 on MULT). SHAP (0.79–0.91) and Integrated Gradients (0.82–0.99) show moderate performance but still trail the Pattern-based metrics by 4–15 points in correlated-noise and distractor settings. PatternNet and PatternAttribution degrade most severely under correlated noise (e.g., PatternNet drops to 0.69–0.70 on MULT-CORR), confirming their inability to handle suppressor variables. On the real-world COMPAS dataset, PatternGAM correctly identifies race, sex, and charge degree as suppressors (near-zero PROD) that the NAM does not meaningfully use, whereas conventional GAM shape functions would misleadingly suggest their importance. On MIMIC-IV, PatternGAM reveals a negative suppression effect for Dias BP mean (entering the model with opposite polarity to its true association with mortality) and nullifies Hematocrit and insurance as classical suppressors.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the activation pattern (Pattern) framework of Haufe et al. (2014) from linear models to the broad family of additive models (GAMs, NAMs, EBM), building directly on Wilming et al.'s (2022, 2023) demonstrations that standard XAI methods systematically misattribute importance to suppressor variables. It departs from prior non-linear Pattern approaches (PatternNet, PatternAttribution, Kernel Pattern) by exploiting the decomposable structure of additive models to compute univariate generative surrogate models per shape function, rather than tracing signal flow through arbitrary network architectures. The XAI-TRIS benchmark from Clark et al. (2024b) is extended with explicit distractor patterns to create a more controlled suppressor setting, and the FAST algorithm from Lou et al. (2013) is used for bivariate term selection in the NAM architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean theoretical result (Theorem 2) showing that standard GAM shape function interpretation is unjustified in the presence of suppressors, which challenges a widely-held assumption in the XAI community about the interpretability of additive models. The real-world case studies (COMPAS, MIMIC-IV) demonstrate concrete misinterpretations that could lead to harmful downstream decisions, such as incorrectly attributing predictive power to sensitive attributes or misreading the direction of clinical feature effects. Open questions include formalizing data-driven notions of higher-order feature interactions, extending SAP guarantees to bivariate attributions beyond the IMP_AGG aggregation rule, and developing appropriate metrics to disentangle different types of suppression from true interactions within additive models.
