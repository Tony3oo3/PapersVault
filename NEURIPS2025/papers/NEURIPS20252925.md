## Problem

Existing counterfactual inference methods for language models operate at the token level, always increasing the probability of the observed token regardless of whether its meaning persists in the counterfactual context. This is inadequate for LM agents whose actions are open-ended strings or context-dependent choices, where identical tokens can encode entirely different semantics across situations (e.g., 'choice 2' meaning 'hide' in one scene but 'attack' in another). The paper addresses this by shifting counterfactual reasoning from individual tokens to high-level semantic abstractions that remain stable across contexts.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a factual state (prompt plus model parameters), an observed action (a sampled token sequence or a single choice token), a counterfactual state (a modified prompt or latent-space-transformed model), and an abstraction distribution P_Y(Y|s,a) that maps state-action pairs to semantic categories. It outputs a counterfactual action distribution over the action space in the new state, from which counterfactual actions can be sampled. Crucially, the LLM is treated as a black box—only autoregressive sampling is required, with no access to internal logits or weights needed for the abduction step.

## Method

The paper introduces an abstraction variable Y_t = f_Y(A_t, S_t, U_t^Y) into the LM agent's structural causal model, where Y captures the high-level semantics of action A in state S and remains consistent across contexts. Counterfactual inference proceeds in three steps: (1) abduction over the combined mechanism f_Y ∘ f_A conditioned only on the observed abstraction value y (not the raw action a), yielding a posterior over the abstraction's exogenous noise; (2) forward propagation of this posterior through the counterfactual state s' to obtain a distribution over Y'; and (3) mapping Y' back into the action space via Bayes' rule, weighting each candidate action by its probability of producing Y'. This design bypasses the token-level Gumbel-Max mechanism during abduction, so the LLM need only be sampled, not inspected. The abstraction can be supervised (a fine-tuned DistilBERT classifier over expert-defined categories like professions or emotions) or unsupervised (a two-stage GPT-4o-mini pipeline for topic discovery and assignment). The paper proves interventional consistency: averaging ACF counterfactual distributions over the prior of U_Y recovers the interventional distribution.

## Results

On the GoEmotions token-level intervention task, ACF reduces the Abstraction Change Rate (ACR) from 0.32–0.67 (TLCF) to 0.02–0.41 across GPT-2-XL and Llama-3.2-1B with supervised and unsupervised abstractions, while raising the Counterfactual Probability Increase Rate (CPIR) from 0.47–0.68 to 0.75–0.97. On the Bios latent-space gender-steering task (GPT-2-XL), ACF achieves an ACR of 0.04 (supervised) and 0.12 (unsupervised) versus 0.40 and 0.38 for TLCF, with CPIR of 0.98 in both cases versus 0.59 and 0.73. Semantic Tightness win rates for ACF over TLCF range from 0.72 to 0.82 across all settings, with paired t-statistics of 8.4–13.6 (all p < 0.001).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is the token-level counterfactual (TLCF) method of Ravfogel et al. [27], which performs Gumbel-Max SCM abduction directly on the observed action tokens. ACF wins on every metric across all four model/abstraction configurations in both the GoEmotions and Bios experiments. The most dramatic gap appears in ACR: on GoEmotions with GPT-2-XL and supervised emotion abstraction, ACF drops the abstraction change rate from 0.32 to 0.02 (a 16× reduction), and on Bios with supervised profession abstraction from 0.40 to 0.04 (a 10× reduction). CPIR improvements are also substantial, e.g., 0.96 vs. 0.68 (GPT-2-XL supervised) and 0.87 vs. 0.48 (GPT-2-XL unsupervised) on GoEmotions. Semantic Tightness win rates consistently favour ACF (0.72–0.82), with the largest advantage in the unsupervised GoEmotions setting (0.82 for GPT-2-XL, 0.80 for Llama-3.2-1B). The MACHIAVELLI case study provides qualitative confirmation: ACF correctly shifts probability mass to actions sharing the observed ethical annotation (e.g., 'physical harm:1'), whereas TLCF merely boosts the probability of the same action token regardless of its changed meaning in the counterfactual context.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the structural causal model (SCM) framework for LLM counterfactuals established by Ravfogel et al. (2025) and Chatzi et al. (2024), which model autoregressive token generation via Gumbel-Max SCMs and perform counterfactual inference at the token level. The key departure is the introduction of an abstraction variable Y that mediates between the token-level action mechanism and the counterfactual inference, shifting the abduction step from the semantics-agnostic token generator to a context-aware semantic proxy. Unlike prior token-level approaches that require white-box access to the LM's internal logits and Gumbel noise, ACF operates with only black-box sampling access, making it applicable to any LM agent regardless of architecture. The abstraction can be constructed either through supervised classifiers (expert-defined categories) or unsupervised concept discovery via an auxiliary LLM, broadening the method's applicability beyond settings where expert annotations are available.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As LM agents are deployed in high-stakes domains (medicine, law, diplomacy), the ability to generate semantically consistent counterfactuals is critical for accountability, blame assignment, and policy improvement. The paper demonstrates that token-level counterfactuals systematically fail in choice-based and open-text environments because identical tokens carry different meanings across contexts—a failure mode that is structural rather than a tuning issue. Key limitations include the computational cost of repeated black-box sampling and the non-trivial task of defining an appropriate abstraction granularity (too coarse loses nuance, too fine loses interpretability). Open questions include whether unsupervised abstraction discovery can reliably identify the right semantic level in complex multi-agent settings, and how the method scales when the action space is truly open-ended (free-form text) rather than constrained to a finite set of choices.
