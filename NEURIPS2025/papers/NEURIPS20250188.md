## Problem

Concept bottleneck models (CBMs) rely on human-annotated concept labels for training, yet the impact of noisy annotations on CBMs has been largely overlooked. This paper provides the first systematic study showing that even moderate concept noise (10–40%) simultaneously degrades prediction accuracy, interpretability, and the effectiveness of post-hoc concept interventions, with a small subset of 'susceptible' concepts driving most of the performance loss.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250188/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes 224×224 RGB images as input and produces a two-stage output: first a k-dimensional binary concept vector (112 concepts for CUB, 85 for AwA2) from a fine-tuned InceptionV3 concept predictor, then a target class label from a single linear layer consuming those concepts. At inference, the concept vector is editable by a human expert (concept-level intervention), making the intermediate representation a practical interface for correction rather than a fixed internal state.

## Method

The paper proposes a two-stage mitigation framework targeting the identified susceptible concept set (~23% of concepts with above-average accuracy drop). At training time, sharpness-aware minimization (SAM) is applied to the concept predictor, which the authors theoretically show introduces adaptive ℓ₂-regularization on both intermediate activations and final-layer weights, selectively stabilizing noise-sensitive concepts (gains of +3.85–4.07% on susceptible concepts at 20–40% noise). At inference time, where clean labels are unavailable, concepts are ranked by predictive entropy (Shannon uncertainty) and only the top-n most uncertain concepts are corrected, using uncertainty as a practical proxy for susceptibility. A theoretical analysis (Appendix B) proves that under a linear target predictor and bounded loss gradient, uncertainty-based top-s selection asymptotically recovers the true susceptibility-ranked set as noise variance vanishes, validated empirically by a positive Pearson correlation between entropy and accuracy drop within the susceptible subset.

## Results

On CUB, SAM training improves task accuracy from 74.31% to 78.96% under clean supervision and from 3.99% to 4.95% under 40% concept noise; on AwA2, the gain at 40% noise is larger (41.85% → 46.53%, +4.68%). Concept accuracy gains are modest (e.g., +0.91% on CUB at 20% noise) but translate into substantial downstream task improvements. The combined SAM + uncertainty-guided intervention (UCP) recovers 75.2% task accuracy on CUB at 20% noise with only 5 targeted corrections, matching the clean-model baseline of 74.31%, whereas BASE + Random reaches only 56.2% under the same conditions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the intervention baselines from Shin et al. [43], the paper's uncertainty-guided (UCP) strategy consistently outperforms both Random and CCTP selection, with the margin widening at higher noise: on CUB at 20% noise with 5 interventions, UCP reaches 71.2% (BASE) or 75.2% (SAM) versus 56.2% (BASE+Random) and 59.9% (SAM+Random). At 40% noise, even exhaustive Random intervention on a clean-trained model caps at 53.1%, well below the 74.3% clean baseline, while SAM+UCP with just 5 corrections reaches 8.0% (vs. 4.8% for BASE+Random). In the noise-mitigation comparison (Appendix D.5), SAM outperforms both Label Smoothing Regularization (LSR, Pereyra et al. [31]) and Symmetric Cross-Entropy (SL, Wang et al. [56]) across noise levels, confirming that sharpness-aware optimization is more effective than these dedicated label-noise defenses for the CBM setting. The susceptible-concept analysis (Table 2) further shows SAM selectively recovers the noise-sensitive subset by +3.85% (20% noise) and +4.07% (40% noise) in concept accuracy, while non-susceptible concepts remain near-saturated (~99%), indicating the gains are targeted rather than uniform.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the original CBM framework of Koh et al. [19] by systematically studying how noisy concept annotations degrade its three core properties (prediction, interpretability, intervention). It builds on Baek et al.'s [2] theoretical explanation of why SAM is robust to label noise, extending the ℓ₂-regularization argument to the two-layer CBM architecture, and leverages the intervention taxonomy of Shin et al. [43] (UCP, CCTP, Random) as the basis for its inference-time correction strategy. The key departure from prior robust-learning work is the identification of a 'susceptible concept set' whose non-uniform degradation drives most performance loss, a mechanism specific to the bottleneck structure of CBMs rather than a generic label-noise phenomenon.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As CBMs gain traction in high-stakes interpretable-AI deployments (medicine, wildlife monitoring, finance), the finding that even 10% concept noise can halve task accuracy on fine-grained benchmarks is a practical warning that annotation quality directly governs model reliability. The two-stage mitigation (SAM at training, entropy-guided intervention at inference) offers a low-cost, architecture-agnostic recipe that recovers most lost accuracy with only five targeted corrections. Open questions include whether the susceptible-set mechanism persists under correlated or instance-dependent noise, whether it generalizes to hierarchical or continuous concept spaces, and how LLM-generated concept labels (an emerging annotation source) interact with these vulnerabilities.
