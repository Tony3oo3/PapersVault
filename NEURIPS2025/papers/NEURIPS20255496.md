## Problem

Fine-tuning foundation models on multiple reward signals is challenging because existing approaches face a fundamental trade-off: combining rewards into a single scalar (MORLHF) prevents learning objective-specific skills and yields high performance variance across objectives, while training separate experts and merging them once (Rewarded Soups) can produce a suboptimal unified policy when experts diverge significantly due to conflicting objectives. No prior method provides a principled mechanism to balance objective-specific specialization against controlled divergence among expert policies.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained foundation model (GPT-2 for SMILES/DNA generation, Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct for text summarization), N reward functions with known preference weights summing to 1, and a set of training prompts. It produces a single fine-tuned policy parameter vector obtained by merging expert policies after T optimization steps. A key hyperparameter is the merge frequency m (every m steps experts are synchronized), and a subset M ≤ N of objectives is randomly sampled at each merging step to reduce computational cost. Rewards are normalized to [0,1] using training-data statistics, and evaluation uses Pareto-front extraction over generated samples.

## Method

IterativeRS alternates between objective-specific PPO fine-tuning and periodic merging of expert policies. Every m steps, the N expert parameter vectors are combined via a weighted linear combination (weights proportional to objective preferences) to produce a shared parameter vector ρ_t, which is then assigned to all experts before resuming individual optimization. This merge-and-fine-tune loop controls divergence among experts while preserving objective-specific skill acquisition, and the method generalizes both MORLHF (m=1, merging every step) and Rewarded Soups (m=T, merging only once at the end). A theoretical convergence analysis under L-smoothness and μ-strong convexity assumptions provides a performance gap bound that depends on the merge frequency m, the number of selected objectives M, and the total steps T, revealing a trade-off between tighter bounds and computational cost.

## Results

On small molecule generation (QM9), IterativeRS achieves an average Pareto-front reward of 1.4017, outperforming MORLHF (1.2910), Rewarded Soups (1.3062), and RiC (0.6765). On DNA sequence generation (MPRA), IterativeRS reaches 0.5927 average reward with an ICV of 3.8310, surpassing RiC (0.5842, ICV 2.4672) and both RL baselines. On text summarization (Reddit Summary), IterativeRS attains the highest average score (0.4849) and ICV (4.9134) among all four methods, with per-objective scores of 0.6927 (faithful), 0.5854 (summary), and 0.4398 (deberta).

## Baselines & Comparisons

IterativeRS consistently outperforms MORLHF (reward-combining) and Rewarded Soups (expert-merging) on average reward across all three tasks: 1.4017 vs. 1.2910/1.3062 on molecules, 0.5927 vs. 0.5667/0.5666 on DNA, and 0.4849 vs. 0.4525/0.4732 on summarization. Against RiC (supervised fine-tuning), IterativeRS also leads on average reward in every domain, with the largest gap on molecules (1.4017 vs. 0.6765) where RL-based exploration over pre-trained data provides a clear advantage. On the ICV consistency metric, IterativeRS wins on DNA (3.8310 vs. 3.1356/3.8235/2.4672) and text summarization (4.9134 vs. 4.5500/4.5870/3.9579), but trails MORLHF and RS on molecules (3.5854 vs. 4.1883/4.2674), suggesting the iterative merging strategy trades some per-sample balance for higher peak rewards in that domain. The paper also shows that combining IterativeRS with the MolMoE merging strategy further improves ICV by 11% on molecules without sacrificing average reward.

## Relation to Prior Work

IterativeRS unifies two previously separate paradigms for multi-objective RL fine-tuning: reward-combining (MORLHF, which optimizes a weighted sum of all rewards simultaneously) and expert-merging (Rewarded Soups, which trains independent expert policies and linearly interpolates their weights). By introducing a tunable merge frequency m, the algorithm recovers MORLHF at m=1 and Rewarded Soups at m=T, positioning itself as a strict generalization that interpolates between the two. The convergence analysis draws directly on the FedAvg convergence framework of Li et al. (ICLR 2020), repurposing federated-learning synchronization arguments to bound the performance gap of the iterative merge-and-fine-tune loop.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, theoretically grounded alternative to ad-hoc reward weighting or one-shot expert merging, with the merge frequency m offering a practical knob to trade off objective-specific specialization against cross-objective consistency. However, the convergence guarantee relies on strong convexity and L-smoothness assumptions that are unlikely to hold for transformer policies, limiting its direct practical guidance. Empirical gains over Rewarded Soups are modest (roughly 5–10% in average reward) and the ICV advantage is inconsistent across tasks, leaving open whether the iterative merging strategy yields meaningful benefits at larger scales or with more than three objectives. The finding that selective merging (choosing the best merge weights per step) does not outperform fixed uniform merging is a useful negative result that tempers expectations for more complex merging heuristics.
