## Problem

Data-driven engineering design research is hampered by the absence of standardized, publicly available simulation environments and diverse cross-domain datasets. Most existing datasets are private, problem-specific, and use inconsistent conventions, while generating new ones requires weeks of configuring complex solvers (CFD, FEA, circuit simulation) with significant domain expertise. This barrier prevents fair, reproducible comparison of ML and optimization algorithms across engineering domains.

## Input / Output

Each problem accepts design variables in heterogeneous formats—192-point airfoil coordinate tuples with a scalar angle of attack, 2D density images (e.g., 100×50 for Beams2D, 101×101 for HeatConduction2D), 3D tensors (51×51×51), or mixed-type circuit parameter vectors—paired with environmental conditions such as Mach number, volume fraction, or force distance. Outputs are validated through physics-based simulation engines (MachAero, Dolfin-adjoint, NgSpice, ceviche), producing objective values, constraint violation reports, and four domain-specific benchmark metrics (COG, MMD, RVC, DPP) that go beyond standard statistical similarity. Datasets are distributed as HuggingFace Dataset objects with automatic column extraction via the API.

## Method

EngiBench provides a unified, versioned Python API that abstracts simulation complexity behind a standardized interface: each problem exposes design_space, objectives, conditions, a HuggingFace dataset, constraint checking, simulation, and adjoint-based optimization through a single object, so switching problems requires only changing an import line. The companion EngiOpt library supplies CleanRL-style single-file implementations of GAN2D, CGAN2D, conditional diffusion, BezierGAN (airfoil-specific Bézier parameterization), and MLP+NSGA-II surrogate optimization with Bayesian hyperparameter search and implicit deep ensembles. A key design insight is that engineering design manifolds are highly sensitive and constrained (e.g., airfoil spline continuity, volume fraction bounds), making them far harder for generative models than unconstrained image tasks—unconditional GANs can outperform conditional variants and diffusion models on engineering-relevant metrics like COG, while all methods struggle with feasibility (RVC) and simulation failure rates. The framework supports distributed dataset generation via Slurm, containerized simulation (Docker/Podman/Apptainer), and integration with PyTorch, BoTorch, Pymoo, and HuggingFace Diffusers.

## Results

Across three topology-optimization problems (Beams2D, HeatConduction2D, Photonics2D), the unconditional GAN2D achieves the best COG (1.56e+08 on B2D, 2.56e-03 on HC2D) and highest DPP diversity (8.08e-07 on B2D, 7.73e-01 on HC2D), while CGAN2D attains the lowest RVC (1.25e-01 on B2D, 3.68e-01 on HC2D). The conditional diffusion model (CDiffusion2D) performs worst on COG and MMD for B2D and HC2D but excels on DPP for Photonics2D (8.23e-01 vs. 5.11e-07 for GAN2D). On the Airfoil problem, the domain-specific BezierGAN achieves the lowest ratio of failed simulations (RF = 0.014 ± 0.027) compared to Diffusion (0.034 ± 0.038) and standard GAN (0.304 ± 0.167). For PowerElectronics, all MLP+NSGA-II surrogate Pareto fronts diverge sharply from the NgSpice simulator, with all squared MMD tests rejecting distributional equality across 10 runs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper benchmarks five algorithm families across multiple engineering design problems. On Beams2D and HeatConduction2D, the unconditional DCGAN (Radford et al., 2016) outperforms both the conditional GAN (Mirza & Osindero, 2014) and the conditional diffusion model (Ho et al., 2020) on COG and DPP, suggesting that conditioning does not necessarily help for topology-optimization inverse design. The CGAN achieves the best RVC on B2D (1.25e-01 vs. 1.07e-01 for GAN2D) and HC2D (3.68e-01 vs. 8.43e-02 for GAN2D), indicating it better respects volume-fraction constraints. The diffusion model performs worst on COG and MMD for both B2D and HC2D but uniquely dominates DPP on Photonics2D (8.23e-01 vs. 5.11e-07 for GAN2D), showing that the 'best' generative model is problem-dependent. On the Airfoil problem, the domain-specific BezierGAN (Chen et al., 2020) achieves the lowest simulation failure rate (RF = 0.014 ± 0.027), substantially outperforming the standard GAN (Goodfellow et al., 2014; RF = 0.304 ± 0.167) and the diffusion model (RF = 0.034 ± 0.038), confirming that geometric priors are critical for meshing-sensitive shape optimization. For PowerElectronics, the MLP + NSGA-II surrogate stack (using Bayesian hyperparameter search and implicit deep ensembles) fails to reproduce the NgSpice Pareto front, with all 10 runs showing significant divergence, underscoring the difficulty of surrogate modeling for stiff, mixed-timescale circuit dynamics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the standardized-benchmark paradigm established by Gymnasium (for RL) and PDEBench/LagrangeBench (for PDE flows), but extends it to the broader, multi-physics domain of engineering design optimization. It departs from prior domain-specific datasets (airfoils, beams, car bodies) by providing a unified API across seven physics domains (aerodynamics, heat conduction, elasticity, photonics, power electronics) with integrated simulators, constraint checking, and adjoint optimization. The companion EngiOpt library follows the CleanRL philosophy of single-file, dependency-light algorithm implementations, positioning it as a pedagogical and extensible baseline suite rather than a state-of-the-art method collection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

EngiBench addresses a bottleneck the authors identify as having 'significantly slowed' progress in data-driven engineering design: the absence of shared, cross-domain benchmarks with standardized interfaces and physics-backed validation. By lowering the barrier from weeks of simulator configuration to a pip install, it opens the field to ML researchers without engineering backgrounds and enables the first systematic cross-domain comparisons of generative and surrogate methods. Key open questions remain: whether the observed difficulty of constrained design manifolds (high RVC, simulation failures) can be addressed by better inductive biases, whether dynamic/multi-part problems will reveal fundamentally different challenges, and whether a 'foundation design model' transferable across physics domains is feasible. The framework's current limitations—static simulations only, no unstructured meshes, no assembly-level tasks—delimit the scope of conclusions that can be drawn.
