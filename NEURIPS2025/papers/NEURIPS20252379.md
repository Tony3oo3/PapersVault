## Problem

Most convergence guarantees for the extragradient (EG) method rely on the standard L-Lipschitz assumption on the operator F, which is overly restrictive for many modern problems (e.g., F(x)=x², cubic min-max objectives, and operators arising from LSTMs or transformers where the Jacobian norm grows with the operator norm). This paper addresses the gap by establishing convergence guarantees under the more general α-symmetric (L₀, L₁)-Lipschitz condition, where the Lipschitz-like quantity scales as L₀ + L₁‖F(x)‖^α rather than being a fixed constant.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an operator F: R^d → R^d, an initial point x₀, and problem-dependent constants (L₀, L₁, α) that characterize the α-symmetric (L₀, L₁)-Lipschitz structure. It produces a root x* satisfying F(x*) = 0 along with explicit iteration complexity bounds. A key practical detail is that the step sizes are fully self-adaptive—computed from the current iterate's operator norm ‖F(x_k)‖—so no global Lipschitz constant needs to be known or tuned in advance, though the constants c₀, c₁ in the step size formula depend on L₀, L₁, and α.

## Method

The core contribution is a novel adaptive step size strategy for EG of the form γ_k = 1/(c₀ + c₁‖F(x_k)‖^α), paired with a convergence analysis under the α-symmetric (L₀, L₁)-Lipschitz assumption for three operator classes. For strongly monotone operators, the method achieves linear convergence; for monotone operators, sublinear O(1/K) convergence; and for weak Minty operators, local sublinear convergence (requiring x₀ sufficiently close to x*). The analysis splits into two regimes: α=1, where a key intermediate inequality (Proposition 3.1) introduces an exp(L₁‖x-y‖) factor that is subsequently eliminated via a refined proof technique for the strongly monotone case (Corollary 3.3), and α∈(0,1), where the bound involves auxiliary constants K₀, K₁, K₂ derived from L₀, L₁, and α. The step size design ensures that when L₁=0 (recovering the standard L-Lipschitz case), the adaptive scheme reduces to a constant step size matching known optimal rates.

## Results

The paper establishes linear convergence for strongly monotone α-symmetric (L₀,L₁)-Lipschitz operators with iteration complexity K = O((L₀/μ + (K₁K₂^α + K₂)‖x₀−x*‖^{α/(1−α)}/μ) · log(1/ε)), and sublinear O(1/K) convergence for monotone operators. For the strongly monotone experiment (L₀ = 1+2√2, L₁ = 2√2), the proposed adaptive step size reaches larger magnitudes (>0.032) compared to Vankov et al.'s constant step size (~0.02), yielding faster relative error reduction. On the GlobalForsaken weak Minty problem (ρ ≈ 0.119732), the proposed method converges to the equilibrium (0,0) significantly faster than both AdaptiveEG+ and EG+ with fixed step size γ = 0.1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Vankov et al. [2024] on a strongly monotone problem (L₀ = 1+2√2, L₁ = 2√2), the proposed adaptive step size γ_k = ν/(L₀+L₁‖F(x_k)‖) outperforms their min-based step size: the proposed step size grows to >0.032 while Vankov et al.'s remains fixed at ~0.02, resulting in faster relative error reduction (Figure 3). On the monotone min-max problem (20), the adaptive (L₀,L₁) step size with (c₀,c₁) = (10,10) outperforms the best constant step size (c = 10⁵) in relative error, and most of the 9 adaptive grid-search combinations beat the constant step size baseline (Figure 4). On the GlobalForsaken weak Minty problem from Pethick et al. [2022], the proposed method with (c₀,c₁) = (1,1) converges to the equilibrium (0,0) significantly faster than both AdaptiveEG+ and EG+ (which use fixed γ = 0.1), as shown by trajectory plots (Figure 5). The paper also demonstrates that Corollary 3.3 eliminates the exponential dependence on ‖x₀−x*‖ present in Theorem 3.2, providing a tighter iteration bound for strongly monotone problems compared to Vankov et al.'s guarantees.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the extragradient (EG) method of Korpelevich (1977) from the standard L-Lipschitz setting to the more general α-symmetric (L₀, L₁)-Lipschitz framework, which was introduced for minimization by Zhang et al. (2020b) and for variational inequalities by Vankov et al. (2024). The key analytical tool—a bound on ‖F(x)−F(y)‖ that eliminates the path maximum—is adapted from Chen et al. (2023) for the (L₀, L₁)-smooth minimization case. The paper's main departure from prior work is twofold: it provides the first convergence analysis of EG for monotone and weak Minty operators under the relaxed assumption, and it introduces a novel adaptive step size of the form γₖ = 1/(c₀ + c₁‖F(xₖ)‖^α) that tightens the strongly monotone rate by removing an exponential dependence on the initial distance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The L-Lipschitz assumption is known to be overly restrictive for operators arising in modern machine learning (e.g., LSTMs, transformers, and polynomial min-max objectives), so establishing convergence under the (L₀, L₁)-Lipschitz condition broadens the theoretical guarantees available to practitioners. The adaptive step size is practical and empirically outperforms the fixed-step-size baseline of Vankov et al. (2024) on strongly monotone problems. Key limitations include the restriction to unconstrained root-finding (no projection step), the need to know L₀, L₁, and α a priori, and the local-only convergence for weak Minty operators. Open directions flagged by the authors include extensions to constrained and stochastic settings, analysis of optimistic gradient methods under the same assumption, and fully adaptive estimation of the unknown constants.
