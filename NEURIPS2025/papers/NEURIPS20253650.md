## Problem

Geometry problem solving faces two underexplored challenges: the multi-step reasoning gap between initial conditions and the goal creates an enormous search space for solution exploration, and obtaining multiple interpretable and shorter solutions remains an open problem. Existing neural-based methods cannot prevent redundant or incorrect steps, while symbolic-based methods rely on step-wise neural prediction with limited pruning gains, and no prior work simultaneously guarantees interpretability, shorter paths, and multiple solutions.

## Input / Output

The method takes a geometric diagram (parsed via a diagram parser) and problem text (parsed via a text parser) as input, converting them into a formalized set of geometric conditions in structural clause format. The output is the numerical answer to the problem goal plus multiple interpretable solution paths, each an ordered sequence of theorem rule applications that is executed by an external symbolic engine. Solutions are sorted in length-increasing order and verified for feasibility, with a user-configurable constraint λ on the number of candidate solutions per target node.

## Method

Causal-R introduces Causal Graph Reasoning (CGR), which constructs a hypergraph per theorem rule from a predefined rule base, where primitive nodes represent geometric attributes with positive/negative reachability status, causal edges encode deduction paths from head to tail nodes, and prerequisite edges gate the application of each causal edge. The key technical contribution is Forward Matrix Deduction (FMD), which transforms the sequential causal graph deduction into iterative matrix operations: a status vector is updated each iteration by combining a condition matrix, a prerequisite matrix, and a causal matrix via element-wise max/min operations, enabling GPU-accelerated reasoning with O(α) complexity for an α-step problem. This compresses the search space simultaneously from the known conditions (beginning), the target nodes (end), and intermediate deduction constraints. Solutions are generated by backtracing from each target node at the earliest iteration it first becomes reachable, yielding globally shorter and multiple interpretable paths without redundant steps.

## Results

On Geometry3K with ground-truth parsing, Causal-R achieves 91.2% accuracy and an average solution length of 1.89 theorem steps (1.68 unique theorems), surpassing the best prior method E-GPS (89.8% accuracy, 2.18 steps) by 1.4% in accuracy and 0.29 steps in solution length. Causal-R is the only method that achieves a solution length below 2 steps, compared to 2.18 (E-GPS), 2.34 (GeoDRL), 3.99 (E-GPS with 17 theorems), 4.12 (Pi-GPS), and 7.10 (Inter-GPS). With the model's own parsing (no GT), Causal-R still leads among symbolic methods at 69.2% accuracy with 1.98-step solutions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Causal-R outperforms all symbolic baselines on both accuracy and solution length under the same 24-theorem rule base. Against E-GPS (the closest competitor), Causal-R gains 1.4% accuracy (91.2 vs. 89.8) and reduces solution length from 2.18 to 1.89 steps, making it the only method below the 2-step threshold. Against GeoDRL, the gap is larger: 91.2% vs. 89.4% accuracy and 1.89 vs. 2.34 steps. Against Inter-GPS (17 theorems, GT), Causal-R improves accuracy by 12.9 points and cuts solution length from 7.10 to 1.89 steps. Against Pi-GPS, Causal-R leads by 13.4% in accuracy (91.2 vs. 77.8) and 2.23 steps in solution length (1.89 vs. 4.12). Among neural-based methods (PGPSNet 77.9%, LANS 82.3%, GeoGen-SFT-7B 58.4%), Causal-R achieves the highest accuracy while also providing interpretable, multi-solution outputs that these methods cannot guarantee.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the symbolic-based geometry problem solving lineage (Inter-GPS, GeoDRL, E-GPS, Pi-GPS) by introducing a purely symbolic causal-reasoning stage that precedes actual problem solving. While prior symbolic methods rely on neural models for step-wise theorem prediction to prune search space, Causal-R replaces this with a unified causal graph deduction framework that compresses the solution space simultaneously from the initial conditions, the problem goal, and intermediate reasoning paths. The Forward Matrix Deduction is a novel implementation that transforms sequential causal graph application into parallelizable matrix operations, departing from the iterative theorem-prediction paradigm of GeoDRL and the top-down decomposition of E-GPS.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses two practical gaps that impede real-world deployment of geometry solvers: guaranteeing interpretable, redundancy-free solutions and obtaining multiple shorter solution paths. The causal graph framework is presented as potentially generalizable to any domain where reasoning can be approximated as causal deduction, though current limitations (fixed theorem base, no support for construction rules, growing matrix storage for complex problems) constrain immediate applicability. The open question of whether this symbolic-reasoning-first paradigm can be extended to richer theorem bases or combined with neural parsing to close the remaining accuracy gap (69.2% without GT parsing vs. 91.2% with) remains important for broader adoption.
