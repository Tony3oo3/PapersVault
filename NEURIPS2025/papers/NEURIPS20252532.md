## Problem

Existing personalized Bayesian federated learning (PBFL) methods suffer from two coupled limitations: client-side posterior inference relies on restrictive parametric assumptions (typically Gaussian variational families) that mismatch complex true posteriors, and server-side aggregation naively averages variational parameters in Euclidean space, ignoring that probability distributions reside on a manifold where geometric averaging differs from coordinate-wise averaging. This paper addresses both gaps simultaneously.

## Input / Output

Each of K clients holds a local non-i.i.d. image dataset (28×28 grayscale for MNIST/FMNIST, 32×32 RGB for CIFAR-10/100) and a lightweight model (single-hidden-layer MLP or LeNet-style CNN). The method consumes N=10 particles per distribution as the unit of communication: clients upload their local posterior particles and receive the global prior particles each round. Outputs are per-class predictions with calibrated uncertainty (quantified via ECE), plus the full posterior distribution over model parameters represented as a particle set, enabling both point predictions and uncertainty-aware decision-making.

## Method

FedWBA replaces both the local inference and global aggregation stages of PBFL. Locally, each client approximates its posterior via Stein Variational Gradient Descent (SVGD), representing the variational distribution as N particles updated by an attraction-repulsion rule with an RBF kernel, eliminating any parametric family assumption. Globally, the server computes the 2-Wasserstein barycenter of the K uploaded posterior particle sets: it first solves a linear-programming optimal transport problem to obtain the transport plan T*_k between the current global prior particles and each client's posterior particles, then updates the global prior particles via the closed-form barycenter formula Θ* = (1/K)Σ_k T*_k Θ_k diag(N^{-1}). A kernel density estimate (Gaussian kernel, bandwidth 0.55) reconstructs a continuous global prior from the barycenter particles so that clients can compute the log-prior gradient in the next SVGD round. Theoretically, the paper proves a per-iteration ELBO increase lower bound (local convergence) and shows the Wasserstein barycenter converges to the true parameter at rate O_p(√(log^{2/α}s / s^{1/α})) as per-client data size s→∞ (global convergence).

## Results

FedWBA achieves the best or near-best test accuracy across all four datasets and client counts. On MNIST (50 clients), it reaches 97.99% accuracy, edging out pFedVEM (97.90%) and pFedGP (97.69%). On CIFAR-100 (50 clients), it achieves 64.21%, substantially outperforming the next-best pFedGP (63.30%) and pFedBayes (61.45%). On FMNIST (50 clients), it scores 92.50%, slightly ahead of DSVGD (92.41%). DSVGD fails to produce results within 10 hours on CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100, highlighting FedWBA's computational advantage.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedWBA outperforms all Bayesian FL baselines (pFedBayes, pFedVEM, pFedGP) on MNIST, FMNIST, and CIFAR-100 across all client counts, with the largest margin on CIFAR-100 where it leads pFedGP by ~0.9% and pFedBayes by ~2.8% at 50 clients. On CIFAR-10 (50 clients), FedPer narrowly edges FedWBA (73.53% vs. 73.40%), but FedWBA surpasses FedPer at 100 and 200 clients (72.68% vs. 68.95%; 69.83% vs. 65.41%). Among non-Bayesian PFL methods, FedWBA consistently leads on CIFAR-100 by 11+ points over FedPer and 17+ points over Scaffold. DSVGD, the closest particle-based competitor, is computationally infeasible on CIFAR-10/100 (no results within 10 hours), while FedWBA completes all experiments. On FMNIST, FedWBA marginally beats DSVGD (92.50% vs. 92.41% at 50 clients) and all other methods by at least 0.7%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the particle-based Bayesian federated learning line initiated by DSVGD (Kassab & Simeone, 2022) by replacing its multi-round SVGD updates with a single-round particle transport and, crucially, by substituting the naive parameter-averaging aggregation used in pFedBayes and pFedVEM with a Wasserstein barycenter computed via optimal transport. At the local level, it inherits the nonparametric flexibility of SVGD (Liu & Wang, 2016) to avoid the Gaussian variational family assumption that constrains most existing PBFL methods. The geometric motivation for barycenter aggregation draws on information geometry and the theory of Wasserstein barycenters (Agueh & Carlier, 2011), positioning the method as a principled alternative to Euclidean averaging on the space of probability measures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses two practical pain points in PBFL—parametric mismatch in local posteriors and geometrically ill-defined server aggregation—with a unified particle-based solution backed by convergence guarantees at both levels. However, the method's practical adoption is limited by the computational cost of solving the linear program for each client's transport plan and the small particle count (N=10) needed to keep communication feasible; DSVGD's inability to finish CIFAR-10/100 within 10 hours underscores the scalability challenge. Open questions include whether the Wasserstein barycenter advantage persists at larger particle counts or with more clients, how the method behaves under extreme data heterogeneity, and whether the KDE step for reconstructing continuous priors introduces additional approximation error that degrades the theoretical guarantees in practice.
