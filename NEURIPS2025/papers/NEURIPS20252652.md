## Problem

State-of-the-art MLLM serving stacks (vLLM, TGI) time-multiplex modality encoders and the shared text decoder on the GPU, sequentially idling SMs or HBM in turn. This wastes the pronounced resource complementarity between compute-intensive, memory-light encoders and memory-hungry, compute-light decoders, inflating time-per-output-token by up to 28.9× under concurrent multimodal load.

## Input / Output

SpaceServe ingests multimodal requests containing images (224×224 to 2048×2048, arbitrary aspect ratios), video clips, text prompts, and audio waveforms, and produces autoregressively generated text tokens. Input resolution is dynamic and critically shapes the resource footprint: quadrupling image resolution inflates encoder arithmetic intensity from ~338 to ~8827 FLOPs/byte while decoder AI stays flat at ~0.89, so the scheduler must adapt per-request rather than use a fixed partition. The primary latency metrics are TTFT (first-token delay) and TPOT (steady-state per-token delay).

## Method

SpaceServe decouples all modality-specific encoders from the shared text decoder and co-locates their kernels on the same GPU via fine-grained SM partitioning (libsmctrl), so encoder and decoder execute concurrently on disjoint SM slices. A hierarchical operator-level profiling workflow builds Resource-Utility Curves mapping SM allocation to latency for each encoder/decoder variant under varying input sizes; at runtime, the Space-Inference Scheduler (SIS) uses these curves to solve for the SM split that minimises max(encoder latency, decoder latency) per arriving request. A Time-Windowed Shortest-Remaining-First (TWSRFT) scheduler batches encoder requests within fixed windows by ascending patch count, keeping batch size within the GPU's compute capacity while preventing starvation of large requests. The LLM side employs chunked prefill with a vision-cache check that defers prefill chunks until the corresponding encoder output is available, preserving decode-priority scheduling.

## Results

SpaceServe reduces Time Per Output Token (TPOT) by 4.81× on average and up to 28.9× compared to vLLM-v1 at 10 RPS across four Qwen2-VL model sizes (2B–72B) on NVIDIA A100 GPUs. At 10 RPS, TPOT drops from 365 ms (vLLM) to 12.62 ms (SpaceServe) on Qwen2-VL-2B, and from 489 ms to 40 ms on Qwen2-VL-7B. On MoE-based models, SpaceServe achieves 4.08× TPOT speedup on DeepSeek-VL2 and 9.84× on Kimi-VL. The advantage scales with input resolution, reaching 12.39× at 2K×2K, while Time To First Token (TTFT) remains virtually unchanged between the two systems.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole external baseline is vLLM-v1 (the latest vLLM architecture with zero-overhead scheduling and MLLM-specific optimizations). SpaceServe decisively outperforms vLLM-v1 on TPOT across every model size, GPU configuration, and input resolution tested, with speedups ranging from 3.14× (Qwen2-VL-72B on 8×A100) to 28.9× (Qwen2-VL-2B on 1×A100). The gap widens with higher request rates and higher input resolutions, peaking at 12.39× at 2K×2K where the compute-heavy encoder would otherwise block the decoder for the entire generation. On TTFT, the two systems are essentially tied (within ~1–2 s at 10 RPS), confirming that SpaceServe's gains come from steady-state token throughput rather than first-token latency. Ablations against NVIDIA MPS (coarse-grained SM sharing) and vLLM's default encoder scheduler show that the fine-grained SM partitioning contributes up to 3.3× additional TPOT improvement over MPS, and the TWSRFT encoder scheduler prevents the TPOT degradation that vLLM's unbatched encoder path causes at higher concurrency.

## Relation to Prior Work

SpaceServe builds directly on vLLM's PagedAttention serving stack and adapts Sarathi-Serve's chunked-prefill mechanism with a vision-cache-aware scheduling extension (Algorithm 3). Conceptually, it departs from the time-multiplexing paradigm that all current MLLM serving systems (vLLM, TGI, SGLang) employ, and from the prefill/decode disaggregation line (SplitWise, DistServe, PD-Serve) which addresses a different axis of resource contention. The key novelty is repurposing fine-grained GPU SM-partitioning primitives (libsmctrl, AMD CU masks) — previously used for real-time or multi-tenant isolation — as a mechanism to co-locate compute-bound encoder kernels with memory-bound decoder kernels on the same device, guided by an offline cost model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As MLLMs move into production with increasingly high-resolution and variable-length inputs, the encoder-decoder resource asymmetry becomes a dominant latency bottleneck; SpaceServe's 4.81× average TPOT reduction (up to 28.9×) addresses this directly and the technique is likely to be adopted by serving frameworks. A clear limitation is that TTFT remains essentially unchanged, since the design targets steady-state throughput rather than initial-token latency, and the authors acknowledge that reducing single-encoder-pass latency is an open problem. Open questions include whether the cost model generalizes across GPU architectures (evaluated only on A100), how the approach behaves with audio/video encoders of very different compute profiles, and whether the SM-partitioning overhead becomes significant on next-generation hardware with different SM microarchitectures.
