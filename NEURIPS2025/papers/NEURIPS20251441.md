## Problem

Prior work on critical point computation for non-convex functions established optimal rates under either a gradient-only oracle (O(L₁^{1/2}L₂^{1/4}Δε^{-7/4}) queries) or a full Hessian oracle (O(L₂^{1/2}Δε^{-3/2}) queries), but the intermediate trade-off space between the number of gradient and Hessian queries remained uncharacterized. This paper asks what new trade-offs are achievable when one is allowed a tunable number n_H of (possibly approximate) Hessian queries alongside gradient queries, and whether the L₁-Lipschitz gradient assumption can be removed entirely.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes an initial point x^(0) with Δ-bounded sub-optimality, a target accuracy ε, and access to two oracles: a gradient oracle returning ∇f(x) and a δ-approximate Hessian oracle returning a symmetric matrix H_x with ‖H_x − ∇²f(x)‖ ≤ δ. The number of Hessian queries n_H is a free parameter that the user sets, and the algorithm guarantees at most n_H such queries. The output is a point x with ‖∇f(x)‖ ≤ ε. Notably, the result extends to functions with only an L₂-Lipschitz Hessian (no L₁ bound) via a one-shot reduction that adds a single extra Hessian query.

## Method

The core subroutine (Critical-or-Progress) performs accelerated gradient descent in a norm induced by a carefully constructed matrix Ĥ = φ(H), where φ is a piecewise function that rescales the eigenvalues of the approximate Hessian to introduce controlled spectral gaps. This norm choice, combined with Davis-Kahan-type perturbation bounds, allows the algorithm to either certify an ε-critical point or guarantee a function value decrease of Ω̃(ε^{3/2}/L₂^{1/2}) per call. The outer loop (Algorithm 2) alternates between negative-curvature descent (when the Hessian estimate has a sufficiently negative eigenvalue) and the Critical-or-Progress subroutine, with lazy Hessian updates triggered when iterates drift beyond a threshold R. A final reduction (Algorithm 3) removes the L₁-Lipschitz gradient assumption by restricting optimization to a low-curvature subspace and performing a single Newton step in the complementary high-curvature subspace, adding only one extra Hessian query.

## Results

The main result (Theorem 1) provides a new trade-off: for a function with L₂-Lipschitz Hessian, the algorithm outputs an ε-critical point using n_H queries to a δ-approximate Hessian oracle and Õ(Δ L₂^{1/4} n_H^{-1/2} ε^{-9/4}) gradient queries. In the bounded-dimension setting (Corollary 1), this yields Õ(d^{1/3} L₂^{1/2} Δ ε^{-3/2} + d) gradient queries, improving the d-dependence from √d (Doikov et al.) to d^{1/3}. With a single Hessian query, the gradient complexity is Õ(Δ^{3/2} L₂^{3/4} ε^{-9/4}), improving upon prior work by a factor of O(Δ^{1/2} L₂^{1/4} ε^{-3/4}). Setting δ = L₁ (no Hessian queries) recovers the known O(L₁^{1/2} L₂^{1/4} Δ ε^{-7/4}) gradient-only bound.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's Corollary 1 improves upon Doikov et al. [18] by reducing the dimension dependence from √d to d^{1/3} (i.e., from O(√(dL₂) Δ ε^{-3/2} + d) to Õ(d^{1/3} L₂^{1/2} Δ ε^{-3/2} + d)) for all ε in the standard range. It also improves upon Li & Lin [29] for sufficiently small ε (specifically ε ≤ O(L₁^{3/2} L₂^{-1} d^{-4/3})), where the d^{1/3} L₂^{1/2} Δ ε^{-3/2} term dominates the L₁^{1/2} L₂^{1/4} Δ ε^{-7/4} term. Compared to Nesterov & Polyak [34], the improvement is from a linear d factor to d^{1/3}. The paper further shows (Appendix B, Lemma 2) that Jiang et al. [25]'s bound of O(d^{1/4} L₁^{1/4} L₂^{3/8} Δ ε^{-13/8}) is always at least the minimum of the Doikov et al. and Li & Lin bounds, meaning it does not improve upon either. For the unbounded-L₁ setting with a single Hessian query, the paper improves upon Doikov et al. by a factor of O(Δ^{1/2} L₂^{1/4} ε^{-3/4}) up to polylogarithmic factors.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the restarted accelerated gradient descent framework of Li and Lin [29], which achieved O(L₁^{1/2} L₂^{1/4} Δε^{-7/4}) gradient-only query complexity for ε-critical point computation. The key departure is working in a norm induced by a carefully constructed function φ of the approximate Hessian's eigenvalues, combined with intermittent Hessian recomputation and negative curvature descent, yielding a new trade-off curve between gradient and Hessian query counts. The paper also introduces a reduction (Algorithm 3) that removes the L₁-Lipschitz gradient assumption at the cost of one additional Hessian query, extending applicability to functions with only L₂-Lipschitz Hessians.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that a single Hessian query suffices to achieve Õ(Δ^{3/2} L₂^{3/4} ε^{-9/4}) gradient queries (improving prior bounds by a factor of O(Δ^{1/2} L₂^{1/4} ε^{-3/4})) and the d^{1/3} dimension dependence (versus √d or d in prior work) provide concrete new trade-offs for practitioners who can afford occasional second-order information. However, the work is purely theoretical with no experiments, incurs multiple polylogarithmic factors, and the asymptotic optimality of the d^{1/3} dependence remains open. The dimension-dependent lower bounds for this problem are still lacking, making it difficult to assess how close these upper bounds are to optimal.
