## Problem

ML-based architecture-level power models have shown promise over classical analytical models like McPAT, but no open-source dataset exists to train or benchmark them. Existing private datasets suffer from critical realism gaps: some exclude SRAM (which consumes >50% of CPU power), some omit clock-gating during synthesis, and most cover only a single CPU architecture, preventing generalization evaluation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252295/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each of the 200 samples provides a 101-element feature vector (14 hardware parameters defining the CPU configuration plus 87 event statistics from gem5's O3CPU simulator) and a 60-element power label vector. The power labels decompose into 12 entities (whole CPU + 11 components: BP, IFU, ICache, RNU, ROB, ISU, Regfile, FU Pool, LSU, DCache, Other Logic) × 5 values (total power plus four fine-grained groups: combinational logic, sequential logic, memory, and clock power). Component masks (14-bit hardware + 87-bit event) are provided to extract per-component feature subsets from the full 101-dim vector.

## Method

The core contribution is a realistic dataset generation pipeline rather than a new model. The authors configure 25 CPU designs (15 BOOM + 10 XiangShan) across varying scales, generate RTL via Chipyard and OpenXiangShan, integrate realistic SRAM macros from a 40nm Memory Compiler, and perform logic synthesis with clock-gating enabled using Synopsys Design Compiler. Ground-truth power labels are collected by running RTL simulation in VCS followed by post-synthesis power analysis in PrimePower, while event features are extracted from gem5. The dataset is paired with a training-testing framework offering three scenarios (Balance, Small, Large) that reflect realistic few-shot and out-of-distribution evaluation settings. Six models are benchmarked: two analytical (McPAT, McPAT-Plus) and four ML-based (McPAT-Calib with XGBoost, its per-component and per-power-group variants, and PANDA with resource functions), revealing that ML models achieve ~6–10% MAPE in-distribution but degrade sharply (30–90% MAPE) when test configurations fall outside the training distribution.

## Results

On ArchPower, PANDA achieves the best average total-power MAPE of 15.5% (BOOM) and 23.2% (XiangShan) with correlation R = 0.96 and 0.86, respectively. Under the standard Balance training scenario, McPAT-Calib-Component reaches 6.2% MAPE (R = 0.98) on BOOM, while PANDA achieves 6.8% MAPE (R = 0.97). Analytical baselines perform poorly: McPAT has MAPE >100% and McPAT-Plus ~25.8% on average. In out-of-distribution scenarios (Small/Large), all ML models degrade sharply, but PANDA remains most robust (10.4% MAPE on BOOM in the Large scenario vs. 48.9% for McPAT-Calib-Component).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper benchmarks six models: two analytical (McPAT, McPAT-Plus) and four ML-based (McPAT-Calib, McPAT-Calib-Component, McPAT-Calib-CompGroup, PANDA). McPAT [14] is the weakest, with MAPE exceeding 100% across all scenarios, confirming that classical analytical models are unreliable for modern CPUs. McPAT-Plus improves MAPE to ~26% by fitting a scaling factor but still lags far behind ML approaches. McPAT-Calib [20] (XGBoost-based) achieves 8.2% MAPE on BOOM under the Balance scenario but degrades to 50.6% in the Large (out-of-distribution) scenario. PANDA [24] is the strongest overall, achieving the best average MAPE (15.5% BOOM, 23.2% XiangShan) and the most robust generalization, maintaining 10.4% MAPE on BOOM even when training only on the largest configurations and testing on smaller ones. The key takeaway is that while ML models substantially outperform analytical baselines, their generalization across configuration scales remains a significant open challenge.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as a data infrastructure contribution for the ML-based architecture-level power modeling task, which has been explored by prior works (McPAT-Calib, PANDA, FirePower) but always on private, in-house datasets. It departs from prior datasets by addressing three specific gaps: integrating realistic SRAM macros (absent in McPAT-Calib and ANN-based models), enabling clock-gating during logic synthesis (absent in PANDA and FirePower), and spanning two distinct CPU architectures (BOOM and XiangShan) rather than a single one. The evaluation framework reuses the analytical baselines (McPAT, McPAT-Plus) and ML baselines (McPAT-Calib, PANDA) from prior literature, but applies them under a unified, reproducible protocol with multiple training scenarios.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By releasing the first open-source dataset with ground-truth power labels from a full VLSI flow (RTL synthesis with clock-gating, SRAM macros, PrimePower simulation), ArchPower removes the primary barrier preventing the broader ML community from contributing to architecture-level power modeling. The evaluation reveals that current ML power models generalize poorly across CPU scales (MAPE jumps from ~6% in Balance to ~50–90% in Small/Large scenarios), suggesting that existing approaches learn configuration-specific correlations rather than transferable physical principles. Key limitations include only two architectures, 25 configurations, 8 single-thread workloads, and a 40nm technology node; the authors plan to expand to FinFET libraries and more workloads, but the current scope constrains conclusions about cross-architecture generalization.
