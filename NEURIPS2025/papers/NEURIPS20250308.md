## Problem

Fine-tuning LLMs to inject medical knowledge causes catastrophic forgetting of previously acquired knowledge, but the specific pattern of what gets forgotten and whether existing mitigations (LoRA, replay, knowledge editing) are sufficient in the medical domain remain unexplored. This paper reveals that forgetting is proximity-dependent—knowledge semantically close to the injected content degrades far more than distant knowledge—and shows that current mitigation methods fail to adequately preserve this closely-related knowledge.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250308/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes structured medical knowledge triples (head entity, relation, tail entity) sampled from PrimeKG (~20k triples across 21 relation types) as the injection target, and additionally queries the target LLM itself to extract related internal knowledge. The output is a fine-tuned LLM trained on referencing-style multiple-choice samples (10 options, 20 diverse samples per triple) where the correct answers include both the injected tail entity and internally-probed related tail entities, so the model learns to retain prior knowledge alongside acquiring new knowledge.

## Method

InternAL (Internal Knowledge Augmentation Learning) mitigates proximity-dependent forgetting by leveraging the LLM's own internal knowledge as a training signal. First, a probing module generates questions from the head-relation pairs of injected triples using relation-specific templates, queries the target LLM K=5 times (temperature 0.6), and extracts related tail entities to form an internal knowledge set. Second, the injection sample generation is augmented: for each triple, n (randomly 0–3) internally-probed tail entities sharing the same head and relation are included as additional correct answers among m=10 candidates, so the model is trained to recognize both new and previously-known facts simultaneously. This differs from replay-based methods (which mix in external data) and PEFT (which restricts parameter updates) by using the model's own recalled knowledge as the augmentation source, directly targeting the proximal knowledge that is most vulnerable to forgetting.

## Results

On Llama3-8B, InternAL+GenFT achieves the best overall performance (D_total=71.2%) while reducing relative forgetting on D_eval from 34.0% (RefInject) to 15.4%, and on MedQA from 32.6% to 11.1%. On Qwen3-8B, InternAL+GenFT reaches D_total=73.7% with only 8.0% relative forgetting on D_eval (vs. 21.0% for RefInject). The method consistently reduces relative forgetting by 5–14 percentage points across all medical benchmarks and all four backbone models (Llama3-8B, Qwen3-1.7B/8B/32B) compared to the base RefInject method, while maintaining injection accuracy within 2–6 points of RefInject on D_inject.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to the base RefInject method, InternAL+GenFT reduces relative forgetting on D_eval by 6.5 points (15.4% vs. 21.9%) and on MedQA by 6.5 points (11.1% vs. 17.6%) for Llama3-8B, while maintaining comparable injection accuracy (71.4% vs. 73.4% on D_inject). Against knowledge editing baselines (MEMIT, AlphaEdit), InternAL achieves far superior injection effectiveness (71.4% vs. 36.9%/32.7% on D_inject) while also better preserving original medical knowledge (MedQA 45.1% vs. 48.0%/44.7% for MEMIT/AlphaEdit, but D_eval 77.4% vs. 75.9%/77.1%). Compared to LoRA-based PEFT, InternAL+GenFT achieves higher D_total (71.2% vs. 66.9%) and lower forgetting on D_eval (15.4% vs. 28.7% relative drop). On Qwen3-8B, InternAL alone (without GenFT) already outperforms RefInject+GenFT on D_eval (82.0% vs. 76.6%) and D_total (73.2% vs. 70.0%), demonstrating that the internal knowledge augmentation itself provides substantial forgetting mitigation independent of general-domain replay.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: fine-tuning-based knowledge injection into LLMs, catastrophic forgetting mitigation (replay, PEFT, knowledge editing), and internal knowledge awakening via prompting. It builds on the diverse-sample generation strategy from Zhang et al. (NeurIPS 2024) to prevent entity co-occurrence shortcuts, and draws on the knowledge-locality hypothesis from ROME/MEMIT to motivate its proximity-dependent forgetting analysis. The key departure is repurposing internal knowledge probing—previously used to improve inference-time performance—as a data augmentation mechanism during fine-tuning, specifically targeting the semantically proximal knowledge that existing methods (MEMIT, AlphaEdit, LoRA, GenFT) fail to preserve.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The proximity-dependent forgetting pattern provides a concrete, actionable characterization of what gets lost during domain fine-tuning, which should inform how practitioners design injection datasets and evaluate post-injection models. InternAL is a lightweight, training-time-only intervention that requires no external data or architectural changes, making it easy to adopt in existing fine-tuning pipelines. However, the paper acknowledges it does not fully resolve catastrophic forgetting, and generalizability beyond the medical domain and structured triplets is only preliminarily tested. Open questions include whether richer internal knowledge extraction (beyond single-relation probing) or multi-step augmentation could close the remaining gap, and whether the proximity effect persists in larger or differently-architected models.
