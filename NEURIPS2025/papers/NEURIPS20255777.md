## Problem

Active testing provides label-efficient model evaluation by strategically selecting which test inputs to label, but its standard implementation involves repeated gradient-based surrogate training, expensive surrogate predictions, and target-model predictions over the entire pool — costs that become prohibitive for large language models. This paper identifies these three computational bottlenecks and shows how to eliminate them while preserving evaluation accuracy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255777/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pool of N unlabelled text inputs, a target LLM (e.g., Llama 2 70B), a smaller surrogate LLM configured with 50 in-context examples (or 5 for MMLU), a logarithmic loss, and a label budget M ≪ N. It outputs a risk estimate via the LURE unbiased estimator, the sequence of M acquired indices, and a bootstrap 95% confidence interval on the estimation error. Notably, the surrogate is fixed after a single in-context-learning step (no gradient updates), and target-model predictions can be entirely avoided during acquisition by substituting the surrogate's predictive entropy for the cross-entropy acquisition score.

## Method

The paper modifies standard sampling-based active testing (LURE) with three cost-reducing changes. First, the surrogate model is constructed via a single step of in-context learning on a small random subset of test data and then held fixed, eliminating repeated gradient-based training within the acquisition loop. Second, a smaller surrogate (e.g., 7B or 4B) can guide acquisition for a much larger target (e.g., 70B), and because the surrogate is fixed, only one forward pass per pool input is needed rather than one per acquisition step. Third, target-model predictions during acquisition are replaced by the surrogate's predictive entropy (for log loss), reducing target-model calls from N to M. The authors show sampling-based LURE is preferable to interpolation-based ASE under a fixed surrogate, since ASE's risk estimate directly depends on surrogate quality. They additionally derive a bootstrap estimator of the LURE's mean squared error from a single run, providing a practical on-the-fly diagnostic of estimation accuracy.

## Results

The paper's primary metric is the median squared risk-estimation error (×10⁴) across random seeds, comparing active testing (LURE) against uniform-random testing. Across four text-classification datasets (SST-2, FPB, HS, Subj) and four Llama 2 target models, LURE reduces risk-estimation error by 32% on average (median over all experimental configurations and label budgets of 1–400), with reductions of 25–50% typical and up to 80% in some settings. A key finding is that a 7B surrogate model (or even Gemma3-4B) can guide data acquisition for a 10× larger 70B target model with comparable accuracy to a same-size 70B surrogate. On the harder MMLU dataset, active testing still outperforms uniform-random in most surrogate–target combinations, with the sole failure occurring when both surrogate and target are 70B-few.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is uniform-random testing, the standard practice for LLM evaluation. LURE (sampling-based active testing with a fixed in-context-learned surrogate) outperforms uniform-random in the vast majority of configurations, achieving a 32% median reduction in risk-estimation error across all datasets, target models, and label budgets. The one notable failure is LURE-70B evaluating the 70B-few target on SST-2, where the 70B surrogate's high confidence on mislabeled examples (75% of examples with NLL > 5 are mislabelled) causes it to acquire uninformative labels; in this case the smaller LURE-7B surrogate actually performs better. The secondary comparison is against ASE (interpolation-based active testing, Kossen et al. 2022), which uses the surrogate model both for data acquisition and to simulate labels in the risk estimate. LURE substantially outperforms ASE in nearly all settings; ASE only wins on the HS dataset with the 70B-few target, and its risk estimator is constant (not improving with more labels) when the surrogate is fixed, making it fundamentally less suitable for the cheap-surrogate regime the paper targets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the sampling-based active testing framework of Kossen et al. (2021) and the LURE unbiased risk estimator of Farquhar et al. (2021) to the LLM scale by identifying and eliminating three computational bottlenecks: repeated gradient-based surrogate training, large surrogate models, and target-model predictions during acquisition. The key methodological departure is replacing iterative surrogate training with a single step of in-context learning (Brown et al., 2020), fixing the surrogate, and using a smaller surrogate than the target. The paper also contrasts with the interpolation-based ASE approach of Kossen et al. (2022), showing that sampling-based active testing is more robust when the surrogate is deliberately kept crude.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work makes principled active testing practically feasible for LLMs, reducing risk-estimation error by 25–80% over uniform-random sampling with minimal computational overhead — a significant efficiency gain for a task that is increasingly critical as frontier models proliferate. The bootstrap error estimator provides a useful on-the-fly diagnostic for practitioners who cannot run repeated experiments. Open questions include the method's sensitivity to noisy labels (demonstrated on SST-2), whether the bootstrap estimator's empirical accuracy holds without theoretical guarantees, and how well the approach transfers to more complex, open-ended generation tasks beyond classification.
