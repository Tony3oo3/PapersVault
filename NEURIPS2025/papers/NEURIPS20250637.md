## Problem

Existing neural Granger causality methods (cMLP, cLSTM) struggle to capture long-range temporal dependencies and non-linear interactions in multivariate time series, often requiring careful feature engineering or assuming linearity. Prior sparsity-inducing regularizers (e.g., group lasso) only shrink weights toward zero without guaranteeing strict sparsity, necessitating an arbitrary post-hoc threshold to extract the causal graph. The paper addresses how to robustly recover Granger causal relations in complex, non-stationary data with long-range dependencies.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a multivariate time series S ∈ R^{V×T} (V variates, T regularly spaced steps) and uses a sliding context window of C past steps (typically C=10) to predict the next value of each variate. The primary output is a directed Granger causal graph (V, E) extracted from the learned sparse projection matrices: an edge (w→v) exists if the corresponding column of W_v has non-zero norm after training. A secondary output is the next-step forecast for each variate, used during training to drive the sparsity optimization. The method requires only one hyperparameter λ (sparsity strength) tuned per dataset, with all other hyperparameters held fixed across experiments.

## Method

GC-xLSTM trains V separate sLSTM forecasters (one per variate), each preceded by a shared sparse linear projection W_v ∈ R^{D×V} that maps all V input variates into a D-dimensional embedding. The key novelty is a joint optimization procedure that alternates between (i) standard gradient descent on the projection weights φ, xLSTM parameters θ, and learned reduction coefficients α_v (parameterized as softmax(β_v) to ensure non-negativity and unit sum), and (ii) a proximal gradient descent compression step that shrinks each column of W_v proportionally to its corresponding α_v^{w} followed by soft-thresholding. The reduction loss uses a log-group-lasso form λ·log(Σ_w α_v^w ‖sg(W_v^w)‖₂) with a stop-gradient on W, so α learns which columns to suppress while the proximal step performs the actual compression. This adaptive mechanism enforces strict sparsity without requiring a post-hoc binarization threshold, and the logarithm in the penalty equalizes gradient magnitudes across columns, improving robustness to noise and λ sensitivity.

## Results

On the chaotic Lorenz-96 system (V=20), GC-xLSTM achieves 99.1% accuracy and 98.5% balanced accuracy at F=10, and 96.3% accuracy and 96.6% balanced accuracy at the more challenging F=40 setting, outperforming all baselines on both accuracy metrics. On the noisy fMRI brain-connectivity dataset, GC-xLSTM reaches 73.3% balanced accuracy, surpassing the next-best method (TCDF at 72.8%) and substantially exceeding VAR (51.3%) and cLSTM (65.5%). The AUROC from sweeping λ∈{5,…,15} remains competitive (99.3 at F=10, 88.0 at F=40), confirming robustness to the sparsity hyperparameter.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GC-xLSTM outperforms all five baseline families on accuracy and balanced accuracy across both Lorenz-96 forcing levels. Against GVAR (Marcinkevičs & Vogt, 2021), the closest competitor, GC-xLSTM gains +0.9 Acc and +0.3 BA at F=10, and a substantial +1.8 Acc and +8.1 BA at F=40; however, GVAR retains a higher AUROC at F=40 (97.0 vs. 88.0), suggesting its ranking-based score is more discriminative in the most chaotic regime. Against cMLP and cLSTM (Tank et al., 2022), GC-xLSTM improves F=40 balanced accuracy by +16.1 and +31.0 points respectively, highlighting the advantage of xLSTM's long-range memory over shallow MLPs or standard LSTMs. GC-KAN (Lin et al., 2024) trails GC-xLSTM by 7.2 AUROC points at F=10 (92.1 vs. 99.3) and by 0.9 at F=40 (87.1 vs. 88.0). TCDF (Nauta et al., 2019) and eSRU (Khanna & Tan, 2020) are consistently outperformed by 10–25 points on balanced accuracy across both settings, with the gap widening sharply at F=40 where non-linear dynamics dominate.

## Relation to Prior Work

GC-xLSTM sits at the intersection of two recent research threads: the Neural Granger Causality framework of Tank et al. (2022), which trains component-wise forecasting models with sparsity regularization on the input projection, and the xLSTM architecture of Beck et al. (2024), which revived recurrent models with exponential gating and multi-head memory mixing. The key departure from prior neural GC methods (cMLP, cLSTM) is the replacement of a fixed L2/group-lasso penalty plus post-hoc thresholding with a novel joint optimization that learns per-variate reduction coefficients via a log-sum penalty and enforces strict sparsity through proximal gradient descent with soft-thresholding. This eliminates the need for a user-defined sparsity threshold while adapting the compression strength dynamically during training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Granger causality detection underpins applications in neuroscience (brain connectivity), finance (inter-company dependencies), and climate science, where long-range and non-linear temporal dependencies are the norm. GC-xLSTM's use of sLSTM blocks addresses a known weakness of prior neural GC methods (cMLP, cLSTM) in capturing extended temporal contexts, and the ablation confirms that both the architecture and the joint optimization are necessary for strong performance. However, the paper acknowledges the absence of formal convergence guarantees, the limited scale of evaluation (dozens of variates), and the lack of rigorous theoretical analysis of sLSTM blocks, leaving open whether the approach scales to hundreds or thousands of variates as encountered in genomics or high-resolution fMRI.
