## Problem

Prompt tuning and in-context learning are among the most widely used ways to adapt pretrained models, yet method development has been largely empirically driven without a unifying conceptual framework. This paper asks a fundamental question: under what conditions can a prompt make a pretrained predictor (near-)Bayes-optimal on a target task, and what are the inherent limitations that can only be overcome by weight adaptation?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255565/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained neural sequential predictor (LSTM or Transformer) and prepends a tunable prefix of length L=6 to input sequences before the forward pass. The prefix can be hard tokens (2-dim one-hot), simplex vectors (probability distributions over the 2-symbol alphabet), real-valued vectors in input space, or 128-dimensional embedding-space vectors (Soft Prompting). The output is a next-token probability distribution over the binary coin-flip alphabet, evaluated as cumulative regret (excess log loss) against the ground-truth data-generating distribution over sequences of length 200.

## Method

The paper establishes that memory-based meta-learning over a distribution of tasks drives a neural predictor to approximate a Bayesian mixture predictor, whose hallmark is rapid in-context adaptation. Prompting is then formally characterized as conditioning this Bayesian predictor: a prefix steers the posterior over tasks, and optimal prompting is provably possible iff the target is a single task with support under the pretraining distribution (Eq. 8). Two fundamental limitations are identified: (i) multimodal target distributions cannot be optimally prompted because posteriors collapse to unimodal deltas, and (ii) novel atomic tasks absent from pretraining cannot be reached. Empirically, the theory is validated on coin-flip sequences using LSTMs and Transformers, comparing four prefix-tuning methods against five weight-tuning methods. A key mechanistic finding is that soft prefixes (off-distribution embedding-space vectors) substantially outperform even the best hard-token prefixes of equal length by exploiting neural circuitry to inject more information into internal states, and can even cause untrained Transformers to behave as competent sequential predictors.

## Results

The paper evaluates prefix-tuning and weight-tuning methods on coin-flip sequence prediction, measuring performance via excess log loss (regret, Eq. 10; lower is better). On the Single Coin target task (bias 0.2), Soft Prompting (SoftPT) achieves Bayes-optimal performance (matching the 'TargetBayes' Laplace predictor) for both Transformer and LSTM, while all other prefix-tuning methods (HardPT, SimplexPT, RealPT) fail to reach optimality. On the Two-Coin Mixture target, no prefix-tuning method achieves Bayes-optimality, confirming the theoretical limitation; however, FullWT and LoRAWT (rank-4) do reach 'TargetBayes' performance. Soft prefixes of length 6 outperform the best hard-token prefixes of the same length, and the advantage is largely attributable to the higher embedding dimensionality (128 vs. 2 input dimensions).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares four prefix-tuning methods (HardPT, SimplexPT, RealPT, SoftPT from Lester et al. 2021) against five weight-tuning methods (EmbedWT, UnembedWT, Un+EmbedWT, FullWT, LoRAWT from Hu et al. 2021) on two target tasks. SoftPT (Lester et al.) is the strongest prefix-tuning method, achieving Bayes-optimality on the Single Coin task where HardPT, SimplexPT, and RealPT all fall short, demonstrating that off-distribution soft inputs inject substantially more task information than any hard-token sequence of equal length. LoRA (Hu et al.) is the most effective parameter-efficient weight-tuning method, reaching 'TargetBayes' on both the Single Coin and Two-Coin Mixture tasks, whereas no prefix-tuning method can achieve optimality on the mixture. On the Two-Coin Mixture, SoftPT provides meaningful gains over the untuned pretrained predictor but remains bounded away from 'TargetBayes', while FullWT and LoRAWT close the gap entirely. For untrained Transformers, SoftPT is the only effective prefix-tuning method, nearly reaching Bayes-optimality on both target tasks, while LoRA is the strongest weight-tuning method in that setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper formalizes the connection between memory-based meta-learning, Bayesian sequential prediction, and prompt optimization, building on the meta-learning-to-Bayes-optimal-predictor line (Ortega et al. 2019, Mikulik et al. 2020, Genewein et al. 2023) and the Bayesian explanation of in-context learning (Xie et al. 2021, Chan et al. 2022a). It positions existing prompt-tuning methods (Soft Prompting from Lester et al. 2021, prefix-tuning from Li & Liang 2021) and weight-tuning methods (LoRA from Hu et al. 2021) within a unified Bayesian conditioning framework, providing the first formal characterization of when optimal prompting is and is not possible. The paper departs from prior empirical-driven prompt optimization work by grounding method design in a conceptual theory rather than treating prompting as a black-box search problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical identification of two fundamental limitations of prefix-tuning (multimodal target distributions and novel atomic tasks) gives practitioners a principled criterion for when to use prompting versus weight-tuning, which was previously determined only empirically. The finding that soft prefixes can induce Bayes-optimal behavior in untrained Transformers suggests that in-context algorithms are latent in random initializations, with implications for understanding what pretraining actually adds. However, the authors are explicit that their educational experiments on small networks and coin-flip sequences cannot be straightforwardly extrapolated to frontier model scale, and key questions remain open: what constitutes a 'task' for an LLM, whether soft prefixes can truly reprogram models to arbitrary distributions, and whether tuned prefixes transfer across architectures.
