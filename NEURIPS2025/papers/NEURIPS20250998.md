## Problem

Concave and monotone polynomial games enjoy strong equilibrium guarantees (existence, uniqueness, convergence of decentralized dynamics), yet verifying whether a given game possesses these properties is NP-hard, even for quartic utilities over boxes. This paper asks whether tractable sufficient certificates for concavity and monotonicity exist, and how to compute the closest game that does admit such a certificate.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250998/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an n-player polynomial game of degree d specified by coefficient vectors of utility functions and a compact convex basic semialgebraic strategy set defined by polynomial equality/inequality constraints, together with a hierarchy level ℓ. It produces (i) a sequence of SDP objective values SOS_ℓ(G) that upper-bound max_x λ_max(SJ(x)), certifying monotonicity when the value is ≤ 0 and strict monotonicity when < 0, and (ii) for the projection problem, the modified utility functions of the closest ℓ-SOS-monotone (or ℓ-SOS-concave) game under the coefficient-vector ∞-norm, solvable as a single SDP.

## Method

The key reduction is that for polynomial games, monotonicity is equivalent to the symmetrized Jacobian of the pseudo-gradient being negative semidefinite everywhere, which via the Rayleigh-Ritz theorem becomes a polynomial nonnegativity problem y^T SJ(x) y ≤ 0 over X × B. The paper constructs a Putinar-type SOS hierarchy where at level ℓ one checks whether λ − y^T SJ(x) y admits a degree-ℓ SOS decomposition in the quadratic module Q_ℓ(X × B); each level is a semidefinite program solvable in polynomial time. Under the Archimedean condition, Putinar's Positivstellensatz guarantees convergence of the hierarchy to the true maximum eigenvalue, and a measure-theoretic argument shows that almost all monotone polynomial games are strictly monotone and hence certified at some finite level. The paper further proves that ℓ-SOS-monotone games are dense in monotone games and that projecting any game onto this class reduces to a single SDP, enabling the computation of the closest certifiable game while preserving structural constraints such as zero-sumness or monomial basis.

## Results

The paper establishes that verifying concavity/monotonicity in polynomial games is strongly NP-hard (Theorem 3.1), then constructs SOS hierarchies (Theorem 3.2) whose objective values converge to max_x λ_max(SJ(x)) and certify strict monotonicity at a finite level for almost all monotone games (Theorem 3.3). Experimentally, the degree-4 strictly monotone game is certified at hierarchy level 4 with objective value −1, the degree-5 zero-sum game's closest SOS-monotone approximation is found at distance 49, and the degree-8 general-sum game (168 monomial basis) is solved in ≈37.53 seconds. The closest SOS-monotone game to the no-NE zero-sum game in Figure 2 is found at distance 10, and the resulting modified game provably admits a Nash equilibrium.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper that introduces new certification methods rather than comparing against existing baselines; no prior method exists for certifying concavity/monotonicity of polynomial games via SOS hierarchies. The experiments are illustrative rather than comparative, demonstrating that the proposed SDP hierarchies successfully certify monotonicity for small-to-medium EFGs with imperfect recall and compute the closest SOS-monotone approximation to games lacking Nash equilibria. The scalability discussion notes that the degree-8 game (168 monomial basis) takes ≈37.53 s, highlighting that SOS-based approaches are currently limited to a handful of variables, consistent with known limitations of SDP solvers for high-degree polynomial optimization. The paper positions its contribution as complementary to existing equilibrium computation methods (e.g., [20, 38, 9]) by providing a prerequisite verification step: confirming that the game structure guarantees equilibrium existence and convergence of decentralized dynamics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SOS-based polynomial optimization framework of Lasserre and Parrilo to the game-theoretic setting, building directly on Rosen's (1965) characterization of concave and monotone games via Hessian and symmetrized Jacobian negative semidefiniteness. The NP-hardness result (Theorem 3.1) is a direct corollary of Ahmadi et al.'s (2013) and Ahmadi & Hall's (2020) hardness results for polynomial convexity verification, repurposed to the multi-player game structure. The connection to extensive-form games with imperfect recall (Piccione & Rubinstein, 1997; Wichardt, 2008) is novel in that it positions these games as polynomial games over the simplex amenable to SOS certification, bridging game theory and semialgebraic optimization in a way not previously formalized.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first systematic, computationally tractable framework for certifying structural game properties (concavity, monotonicity) that underpin equilibrium existence, uniqueness, and convergence guarantees in continuous games. The density result (Theorem 4.3) is practically significant: it means that for any game lacking a Nash equilibrium in behavioral strategies (e.g., the absent-minded taxi driver), one can compute a nearby game that does have one, preserving the information structure. However, scalability remains a serious limitation—SDP solve times grow rapidly with polynomial degree and number of variables (37 seconds for a degree-8, 168-monomial game), and the required SOS level ℓ for certification is unbounded in the worst case. Open questions include whether sparsity-aware or hierarchical SDP solvers can push the practical frontier, and whether the framework extends to non-polynomial or non-convex strategy sets.
