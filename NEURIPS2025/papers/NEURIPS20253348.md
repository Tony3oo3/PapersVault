## Problem

Applying GRPO to LVLMs causes intra-group reward convergence as training progresses: the model memorizes visual-textual training data, so different responses to the same sample receive similar rewards, eliminating the gradient signal needed for further reasoning improvement. This manifests as premature saturation on easy samples (uniformly high rewards) and uninformative failure on hard samples (uniformly low rewards), causing chain-of-thought reasoning to stagnate or collapse. Existing remedies—distilling from stronger teacher models or manually collecting harder datasets—either cap the model's ceiling at the teacher's level or incur prohibitive human annotation costs without adapting to the model's evolving capabilities.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a base multi-modal sample (an RGB image paired with a text question and answer) and progressively transforms it through k rounds of image editing (via Flux.Kontext diffusion) and text editing (appending reasoning-requiring operations to the question/answer), producing an increasingly complex multi-modal problem. The trained answer model outputs a structured chain-of-thought reasoning process enclosed in think tags followed by a final answer in \boxed{} format, with a format reward encouraging this structure. The question generator outputs editing instructions (image or text) that are applied to synthesize new training samples on the fly, with a judge model verifying semantic consistency between edited and original images.

## Method

EvolvedGRPO alternates GRPO training between two models initialized from the same LVLM: a question generator Q that produces multi-modal editing instructions, and an answer model A that solves the resulting problems via chain-of-thought reasoning. Q is trained adversarially to maximize the difficulty imposed on A (minimizing A's answer probability) while maintaining answer confidence through image similarity and intermediate-step probability terms, with a lower bound via max(π_A(a_k|q_k,v_k), 1/test) to prevent variance collapse. A is trained with a correctness-plus-format reward using rule-based MathRuler verification. The number of editing steps k increases on a curriculum schedule (k = ⌊t/c⌋), progressively guiding the model from k-step to k+1-step reasoning chains. Ground truth answers for evolved samples are computed via TTRL majority voting over the model's own outputs, reducing complexity from O(N^k) to O(kN). Training uses a GAN-inspired ratio of multiple A-steps per Q-step to keep data difficulty matched to A's current capability.

## Results

On multi-modal reasoning benchmarks, EvolvedGRPO achieves an average score of 63.0 across MathVista-mini (74.0), MathVision-full (30.8), MathVerse-mini (51.8), GSM8K (85.1), and MATH500 (73.2), approaching GPT-4o's 63.9 average. On general LVLM benchmarks, it scores 53.0 on MMMU, 62.7 on MMStar, and 83.3 on AI2D. The method improves over the Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct base model by +5.4 points on average across reasoning benchmarks, with the largest gain on MathVision (+6.1) and MATH500 (+5.8).

## Baselines & Comparisons

EvolvedGRPO outperforms all seven open-source baselines on the average reasoning score (63.0 vs. 52.0–60.4). Among distillation-based methods, it beats MindGYM (60.0) by +3.0, Virgo (54.4) by +8.6, and Mulberry (52.0) by +11.0, with the largest margins on MathVision where distilled CoT data provides limited benefit. Among collection-based methods, it surpasses NoisyRollout (60.4) by +2.6, MM-EUREKA (59.9) by +3.1, and OpenVLThinker (57.9) by +5.1, indicating that self-evolved instruction augmentation is more effective than static data collection. The only model that outperforms EvolvedGRPO is GPT-4o (63.9 vs. 63.0), and the gap narrows to just 0.9 points on average, with EvolvedGRPO actually exceeding GPT-4o on MathVista-mini (74.0 vs. 63.8) and GSM8K (85.1 vs. 94.2 is the exception where GPT-4o leads). On general benchmarks, EvolvedGRPO also leads all open-source baselines on MMMU (53.0 vs. 52.3 for MM-EUREKA) and AI2D (83.3 vs. 82.7 for OpenVLThinker), though it remains well below GPT-4o on MMMU (53.0 vs. 83.4).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on GRPO (from DeepSeekMath) as the core RL training algorithm and on TTRL for computing ground-truth answers during instruction evolution. It departs from the two dominant paradigms for enhancing LVLM reasoning—distilling chain-of-thought from stronger external models (Mulberry, Virgo, MindGym) and collecting progressively harder perceptual datasets (OpenVLThinker, NoisyRollout, MM-EUREKA)—by instead having the model itself generate increasingly difficult training data through adversarial instruction editing. The key conceptual departure is framing data augmentation as a self-evolving, curriculum-driven process where a question generator and answer model are trained in an adversarial loop, eliminating dependence on external teacher models or manual data curation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and well-motivated failure mode of GRPO in multimodal settings—reward convergence due to the model memorizing training data—which has been a practical bottleneck for the community. The self-evolving instruction framework is attractive because it requires no external teacher and only ~10% additional compute over standard GRPO training. However, the evaluation is limited to a single 7B base model (Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct) and primarily mathematical reasoning; the large gap on general benchmarks like MMMU (53.0 vs. GPT-4o's 83.4) suggests the method has not yet unlocked fine-grained perceptual reasoning. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger models, generalizes beyond math to broader visual reasoning, and how sensitive it is to the quality of the external image-editing tool (Flux.Kontext).
