## Problem

FTPL policies have not matched FTRL's Best-of-Both-Worlds (BOBW) guarantees because existing analyses require the ratio f/F to be monotonically decreasing over the entire support, a condition easily violated when perturbations are unbounded on both sides of the real line. Additionally, the conjectured equivalence between FTRL with 1/2-Tsallis entropy and FTPL with symmetric Fréchet-type perturbations was only established for two-armed bandits, leaving it unclear whether this connection extends to general K-armed settings.

## Input / Output

At each round, the agent observes only the scalar loss of the single played arm (partial feedback) and must construct an unbiased loss estimator; since the arm-selection probability φ_i has no closed form for general perturbations, a geometric resampling estimator of 1/w_{t,i} is used in place of explicit importance weighting. The output is an arm selection decision at each round together with a provable regret bound: O(√KT) adversarial regret and O(Σ log T / Δ_i) stochastic regret under the stated tail-index conditions.

## Method

The core technical contribution is a refined bound on −φ'_i/φ_i that explicitly accounts for the negative-perturbation term −f'(x) for x < 0, thereby relaxing the previously required global monotonicity of f/F. This yields BOBW guarantees for a broad family of asymmetric unbounded Fréchet-type perturbations U_{α,β} where the left tail index β is at least α+2 (right tail index α), including hybrid Laplace-Pareto distributions combining Gumbel-type and Fréchet-type tails. For the symmetric case, numerical inverse-Fourier-transform analysis of the characteristic function associated with 1/2-Tsallis entropy in two-armed bandits suggests a symmetric Fréchet-type perturbation with index 2, and the paper proves BOBW for symmetric Pareto perturbations when K=2. However, a counterexample for K≥3 shows that symmetric Fréchet-type perturbations violate the key condition −φ'_i/φ_i = O(1/λ_i) needed for logarithmic stochastic regret, revealing a fundamental limitation of extending the two-armed Tsallis–Fréchet equivalence to the general multi-armed setting.

## Results

The paper establishes Best-of-Both-Worlds (BOBW) guarantees for FTPL with asymmetric unbounded Fréchet-type perturbations: O(√KT) adversarial regret for all tail indices α > 1, and O(Σ_{i≠i*} log T/Δ_i) stochastic regret when α = 2 (Corollary 4.2). For the concrete Laplace-Pareto distribution, the explicit adversarial regret bound is Reg(T) ≤ 49.25√(KT) + O(√K + K log T) at optimal learning rate m = 0.23 (Theorem 4.3). In the two-armed case, FTPL with symmetric Pareto (shape 2) perturbations also achieves BOBW: O(√KT) adversarial and O(log T/Δ) stochastic regret (Corollary 5.4). However, for K ≥ 3, symmetric Fréchet-type perturbations violate the key condition −φ′/φ^{3/2} = O(1), with a counterexample showing −φ′/φ^{3/2} ≥ Õ((c+2)/(K√K)) (Proposition 5.5).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's main contribution is extending BOBW guarantees from nonnegative Fréchet-type perturbations (Honda et al. 2023; Lee et al. 2024) to asymmetric unbounded perturbations supported on all of ℝ, relaxing the previously required monotonicity of f/F (Assumption 2.3) which is violated when the distribution has support on both positive and negative reals. Compared to the FTRL side, the asymmetric Laplace-Pareto perturbation is analogous to hybrid regularizers combining Shannon and Tsallis entropies (as used in Zimmert et al. 2019, Tsuchiya et al. 2024), but the paper does not identify the exact corresponding regularizer. Against the near-optimal O(√KT log K) bound from Abernethy et al. 2015 (bounded hazard analysis), the paper achieves the optimal O(√KT) rate by exploiting the tail-index structure. The negative result for symmetric perturbations at K ≥ 3 contrasts with the positive K=2 result and with the FTRL Tsallis-INF algorithm (Zimmert and Seldin 2021), which achieves BOBW for all K — highlighting that no FTPL counterpart exists for Tsallis entropy when K ≥ 4 (Kim and Tewari 2019), and that the symmetric Fréchet-type perturbation, while matching Tsallis entropy behavior in K=2, does not straightforwardly generalize.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the BOBW analysis of FTPL from nonnegative (semi-infinite) Fréchet-type perturbations [Honda et al. 2023, Lee et al. 2024] to fully unbounded asymmetric perturbations, where the left tail is lighter than the right tail by at least two indices (β ≥ α+2). It builds on the classical FTPL–FTRL duality from discrete choice theory [Hofbauer & Sandholm 2002, Feng et al. 2017] to revisit the connection between Tsallis entropy regularizers and Fréchet-type perturbations originally conjectured by Kim and Tewari [2019], and on the hazard-function analysis of Abernethy et al. [2015] which previously yielded only near-optimal O(√KT log K) regret. The key departure is relaxing the monotonicity assumption on f/F that was required for semi-infinite supports, by explicitly accounting for the negative-perturbation contribution to the derivative bound.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled FTPL analogue to hybrid regularizers (Shannon + Tsallis) widely used in FTRL, offering a computationally simpler alternative for bandit and semi-bandit settings. The negative result—that symmetric Fréchet-type perturbations violate the key derivative-ratio condition for K≥3 even when suboptimal arms have identical losses—reveals a genuine structural limitation of the two-armed intuition and cautions against straightforward generalization. Open questions include whether alternative (nonstandard) analyses can recover BOBW for symmetric perturbations in general K, and whether the conjectured i.i.d. perturbation reproducing 1/2-Tsallis entropy actually exists. The work is primarily of interest to researchers in online learning and bandit algorithms rather than to the broader ML community.
