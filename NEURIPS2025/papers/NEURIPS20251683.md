## Problem

Existing code-switching datasets are limited to bilingual mixing within homogeneous ethnic groups, lack the scale and linguistic diversity needed for robust multilingual ASR research, and are often produced via machine translation that yields unnatural, grammatically inconsistent speech. Additionally, standard ASR metrics like WER and CER are overly rigid for code-switching scenarios, penalizing semantically equivalent transcriptions (e.g., native script vs. phonetic transliteration) and failing to capture true model performance.

## Input / Output

The LinguaMaster framework takes a topic query, persona metadata (age, gender, education, ethnicity, first/second language), a target language pair with a desired code-switching ratio, and a CS type (inter-sentential, intra-sentential, extra-sentential) as input, producing grammatically valid code-switched text that is then recorded by 174 bilingual speakers into 80+ hours of audio across 12 languages and 63 ethnic groups. For evaluation, SAER takes a predicted transcription and a reference transcription, routing to CER for logographic languages (Chinese, Japanese) or WER for alphabetic languages via an indicator function, then blending the form error with a LaBSE-based semantic cosine distance weighted by a tunable parameter α.

## Method

LinguaMaster is a nine-agent, generate-evaluate-refine pipeline where a GenerationAgent produces candidates through a four-step Structure & Switch routine: parsing the L1 sentence into a dependency tree, translating to L2 with token alignment, identifying permissible switch points that satisfy Poplack's Free-Morpheme and Equivalence constraints, and splicing the L2 fragment back into the L1 skeleton. Four parallel evaluator agents (Fluency, Naturalness, CSRatio, SocialCulture) score each candidate, and a SummarizeAgent aggregates scores to route to either AcceptanceAgent or RefinerAgent for iterative improvement (averaging 1.3±0.4 refinement loops). A Model Context Protocol (MCP) layer injects domain-relevant snippets from external sources (news APIs, social media, custom hooks) into the generation prompt, with the same syntactic constraint pipeline applied to ensure linguistic validity. The SAER metric (Eq. 3) combines a LaBSE semantic error term with a language-specific form error term via a weighted inner product, providing a more semantically sensitive evaluation than WER/CER alone.

## Results

On the dataset quality benchmark (Table 2), SwitchLingua achieves an overall score of 85.4 (human) / 80.0 (LLM), outperforming the second-best dataset SEAME (63.2/60.0) by 25.5%. The largest margins are in Language & Racial Diversity (+31.0%), Realism (+28.1%), and Linguistic Richness (+25.9%) over the runner-up. On the ASR benchmark (Table 3), Whisper-Large-v3 achieves the best SAER in 8 of 10 language pairs, with the lowest SAER of 0.0707 (Spanish–English) and 0.0950 (Russian–English), while Qwen2-Audio-7B-Instruct shows the weakest performance due to systematic language-switching errors (e.g., overproducing Chinese text).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On dataset quality (Table 2), SwitchLingua outperforms all eight existing code-switching corpora on every evaluation dimension. The closest competitors are SEAME (63.2/60.0) and MCE (62.7/56.0) in overall score; SwitchLingua leads SEAME by 22.2 points (human) and 20.0 points (LLM). The largest relative gains are in Language & Racial Diversity (+31.0% over BangorTalk's 12.4/14.3) and Linguistic Richness (+25.9% over SEAME's 12.1/13.0), reflecting SwitchLingua's 12-language, 63-ethnic-group coverage versus the 2-language scope of most prior datasets. On the ASR benchmark (Table 3), Whisper-Large-v3 achieves the best SAER in 8 of 10 language pairs (e.g., 0.0707 Spanish–English, 0.0950 Russian–English, 0.0992 Mandarin–English), followed by Seamless-M4T-Large-v2 (best in Mandarin–English at 0.0404 SAER). Qwen2-Audio-7B-Instruct performs worst overall due to language-detection interference that causes it to overproduce Chinese text regardless of target language. SenseVoice-Small is competitive on raw CER/WER for Cantonese–English (0.2440 CER) but lags on SAER, highlighting the gap between surface-form accuracy and semantic fidelity in code-switching.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the code-switching ASR (CSASR) literature, which has so far been confined to bilingual pairs (Cantonese-English, Arabic-English, Hindi-English) within relatively homogeneous speaker populations, by scaling to 12 languages and 63 ethnic groups. Methodologically, it departs from prior MT-based [8] and single-LLM [9] synthesis approaches by introducing a multi-agent collaboration framework (LinguaMaster) that enforces Poplack's syntactic constraints (Free-Morpheme and Equivalence) at generation time and integrates external tools via Model Context Protocols for topical enrichment. For evaluation, it builds on LaBSE [42] as a semantic embedding backbone but departs from WER/CER/MER/PolyWER by proposing SAER, which fuses cosine-distance semantic error with language-appropriate form error in a single weighted score.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SwitchLingua fills a genuine gap as the first large-scale, multi-ethnic CS benchmark, and the SAER metric addresses a well-recognized blind spot in ASR evaluation where transliteration and script differences inflate error rates without reflecting comprehension loss. Benchmark results reveal that even the strongest model (Whisper-Large-v3) achieves SAER as high as 0.86 on Korean-English, underscoring that CS remains far from solved. Open questions include whether LLM-synthesized text truly captures the spontaneity and pragmatic triggers of natural code-switching, how the SAER α parameter should be tuned per language pair, and whether the dataset's synthetic provenance introduces systematic biases that human-recorded naturalistic corpora would not.
