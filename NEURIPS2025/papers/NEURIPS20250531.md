## Problem

Diffusion-based samplers for Boltzmann distributions face a fundamental trade-off: matching-based objectives that are scalable require the memoryless condition, which restricts the source distribution to a Dirac delta and precludes useful priors like Gaussians or domain-specific harmonic oscillators. No prior method combines scalable matching objectives with arbitrary (non-memoryless) source distributions, leaving a gap between theoretical flexibility and practical efficiency.

## Input / Output

The method consumes only an unnormalized, differentiable energy function E(x) and a sample-able source distribution (Gaussian for generic potentials, or a harmonic prior μ_harmonic(x) ∝ exp(−α/2 Σ‖x_i − x_j‖²) for molecular systems), with no explicit target samples required during training. For amortized conformer generation, a molecular topology g (SMILES string) conditions the energy E(x|g) predicted by the eSEN foundation model. The output is a learned drift u_t^θ that transports source samples to the Boltzmann distribution ν(x) ∝ exp(−E(x)) at t=1, yielding particle configurations or 3D molecular conformers.

## Method

ASBS recasts diffusion sampler learning as a Schrödinger Bridge problem—minimizing KL divergence between the controlled and base path distributions subject to fixed marginals at t=0 and t=1. The key theoretical result (Theorem 3.1) shows the kinetic-optimal SB drift solves an SOC problem with terminal cost log(φ̂₁/ν), where φ̂₁ is the backward SB potential that debiases non-memoryless processes. Computationally, ASBS alternates between two matching objectives: Adjoint Matching (AM) updates the drift u by regressing against σ_t(∇E + h)(X₁) using on-policy samples, and Corrector Matching (CM) updates the corrector h by regressing against ∇_{x₁}log p^base(X₁|X₀). This alternating scheme is proven equivalent to Iterative Proportional Fitting, guaranteeing global convergence to the SB solution (Theorem 3.2). The approach uses Equivariant GNNs for molecular systems and fully-connected networks otherwise, with a degenerate base drift f_t=0 and geometric noise schedule.

## Results

On synthetic energy functions, ASBS achieves the best Wasserstein-2 distance across all four benchmarks: 0.43 on DW-4 (vs. 0.62 for AS), 1.59 on LJ-13 (vs. 1.67 for AS), and 4.00 on LJ-55 (vs. 4.04 for AS), while also attaining the lowest energy-space W2 of 1.99 on LJ-13 and 28.10 on LJ-55. On alanine dipeptide, ASBS achieves the lowest KL divergence on all five torsion angle marginals (0.01–0.03) and the lowest joint W2 of 0.25, compared to 0.65 for AS and 0.68 for DDS. On amortized conformer generation, ASBS with a harmonic prior reaches 74.05% coverage on SPICE without any warm-start, surpassing AS (56.75%) and RDKit ETKDG (56.94%), and with RDKit warm-start achieves 77.84% coverage and 0.79 AMR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ASBS outperforms all eight baselines across every benchmark where they are jointly evaluated. Against the closest competitor, Adjoint Sampling (AS; Havens et al., 2025), ASBS improves W₂ by 31% on DW-4 (0.43 vs. 0.62), by 5% on LJ-13 (1.59 vs. 1.67), and by 1% on LJ-55 (4.00 vs. 4.04), while reducing energy-space W₂ by 17% on LJ-13 (1.99 vs. 2.40) and by 9% on LJ-55 (28.10 vs. 30.83). On alanine dipeptide, ASBS reduces joint W₂ from 0.65 (AS) and 0.68 (DDS) to 0.25, and cuts KL divergence on the γ₂ torsion from 0.56 (AS) and 0.03 (DDS) to 0.02. Among matching-based methods, iDEM (Akhound-Sadegh et al., 2024) and PDDS (Phillips et al., 2024) are substantially outperformed on LJ-13 (W₂ 1.61 and 4.66 vs. 1.59) and LJ-55 (W₂ 4.69 and – vs. 4.00). On conformer generation, ASBS with a harmonic prior (74.05% SPICE coverage) surpasses both AS (56.75%) and the domain-specific RDKit ETKDG baseline (56.94%) without any warm-start, and with RDKit warm-start achieves the best coverage (77.84%) and lowest AMR (0.79) on SPICE.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Adjoint Sampling (Havens et al., 2025), which introduced scalable Adjoint Matching objectives for diffusion samplers but required the restrictive memoryless condition (Dirac delta prior). ASBS removes this condition entirely by recasting the problem as a general Schrödinger Bridge and introducing a Corrector Matching objective that debiases non-memoryless processes, thereby generalizing AS to arbitrary source distributions including domain-specific priors like harmonic oscillators. The theoretical framework draws on the SOC reinterpretation of SB problems (Theorem 3.1) and connects the alternating optimization to Iterative Proportional Fitting, providing a convergence guarantee absent in prior matching-based samplers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Removing the memoryless condition opens the design space for diffusion samplers to incorporate physically motivated priors, which the paper shows can replace data-dependent warm-starts (e.g., harmonic prior ≈ RDKit conformer warmup in conformer generation). The mode-collapse limitation inherent in SOC-based samplers remains an open challenge, and the authors note that importance-weighted corrections could mitigate this. Broader applicability to amortized Boltzmann distributions and higher-dimensional molecular systems represents a natural next step.
