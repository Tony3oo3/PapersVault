## Problem

Pre-trained text-to-image flow models (e.g., Flux.1.0-dev) are confined to their training resolution (~1024×1024) and suffer severe quality degradation and structural breakdown at higher resolutions. Existing training-free high-resolution methods either manipulate architecture-specific internal features (limiting cross-model transferability) or fuse a single upsampled low-resolution endpoint into the denoising target, which introduces artifacts from distribution discrepancy and neglects detail-oriented guidance throughout the sampling trajectory.

## Input / Output

HiFlow takes a standard text prompt and produces high-resolution RGB images up to 4096×4096 via a cascade pipeline (1K→2K→3K→4K). At each cascade stage, the method first synthesizes a low-resolution image, then uses the full step-wise sampling trajectory (not just the final output) of that low-resolution generation to guide the next higher-resolution pass. The method is model-agnostic and works with both DiT-based (Flux) and U-Net-based (SDXL) backbones, as well as with LoRA, ControlNet, and 4-bit quantized variants.

## Method

HiFlow constructs a virtual reference flow in the high-resolution space by upsampling the step-wise predicted clean samples X_{0←t}^{low} from the low-resolution sampling trajectory, creating a time-dependent guidance signal rather than a single anchor. Three flow-aligned guidance mechanisms then operate during high-resolution denoising: (1) initialization alignment starts the high-res sampling from a noisy variant of the reference flow's clean sample at time τ, skipping early steps and preserving low-frequency consistency; (2) direction alignment replaces the low-frequency component of the high-res predicted clean sample with that of the reference flow at the same timestep t, using 2D FFT with a Butterworth low-pass filter to suppress structural drift; (3) acceleration alignment modifies the flow vector by aligning the second-order derivative (acceleration) of the high-res trajectory with the reference flow, synchronizing the model's content synthesis order and rhythm for high-fidelity detail generation. The guidance weights α_t and β_t are set to t/τ for gradually weakening control as denoising progresses.

## Results

On the Flux.1.0-dev backbone evaluated against 10K real high-resolution images from LAION-High-Resolution, HiFlow achieves the best FID (55.39 at 2K, 52.55 at 4K), best FID_patch (47.70 at 2K, 45.01 at 4K), and best IS_patch (13.86 at 2K, 9.73 at 4K) among all training-free baselines. At 4K, HiFlow also leads in overall IS (24.62 vs. 22.21 for I-Max) and CLIP score (35.40). HiFlow is additionally the fastest method, with a 4K latency of 379 s versus 972 s for DemoFusion and 735 s for I-Max.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HiFlow outperforms all four baselines on FID and FID_patch at both 2K and 4K resolutions, with the largest margin over DiffuseHigh (FID 55.39 vs. 61.62 at 2K; 52.55 vs. 62.01 at 4K). Against the closest competitor I-Max, HiFlow improves FID by 2.18 (2K) and 0.72 (4K), and FID_patch by 6.86 (2K) and 7.92 (4K), indicating substantially better local detail quality. At 4K, HiFlow also leads in overall IS (24.62 vs. 22.21 for I-Max) and IS_patch (9.73 vs. 8.87 for BSRGAN), confirming superior perceptual quality. The super-resolution baseline Flux+BSRGAN achieves a marginally higher CLIP score at 2K (35.34 vs. 35.32) but trails HiFlow on all image-quality metrics. HiFlow is also the fastest method, completing 4K generation in 379 s compared to 972 s (DemoFusion), 533 s (DiffuseHigh), and 735 s (I-Max).

## Relation to Prior Work

HiFlow extends the line of training-free high-resolution generation methods (DemoFusion, DiffuseHigh, I-Max, FreeScale) that modify inference-time sampling to exceed a model's training resolution. It departs from prior approaches in two key ways: (1) instead of using a single upsampled endpoint as a static guidance anchor, it constructs a virtual reference flow from the entire step-wise low-resolution sampling trajectory, enabling time-matched guidance that avoids distribution discrepancy; and (2) it introduces acceleration alignment (the second derivative of the flow) as a novel guidance signal for detail fidelity, going beyond the structural/low-frequency guidance that prior methods provide. The framework is model-agnostic, operating on the flow vector rather than internal features, which distinguishes it from architecture-specific methods like HiDiffusion or ScaleCrafter.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

HiFlow offers a practical, zero-training pathway to 4K generation from 1K-trained Rectified Flow models, with the added benefit of compatibility with LoRA, ControlNet, and quantized variants—making it broadly deployable without retraining. A key limitation acknowledged by the authors is that structural errors in the low-resolution reference flow propagate to the high-resolution output (e.g., a duplicated tiger tail), capping the method's ceiling at the quality of the base model's low-resolution generation. Open questions include whether the acceleration alignment generalizes to video generation or non-rectified flow formulations, and whether the cascade paradigm (1K→2K→3K→4K) can be extended to even higher resolutions without compounding artifacts.
