## Problem

Federated learning with differential privacy (DP) has not been successfully applied to large transformer-based end-to-end ASR models, as deep architectures exhibit severe gradient heterogeneity across layers that standard optimization techniques cannot handle, even without DP. No prior work established a competitive, practical training recipe or benchmark for FL with DP in ASR, leaving a gap between the privacy guarantees needed for speech data and the optimization challenges of 250M+ parameter models.

## Input / Output

The model consumes 80-coefficient log-mel filterbank features (25 ms window, 10 ms stride, per-sequence zero-mean/unit-variance normalization) extracted from raw speech audio and produces text transcriptions via CTC decoding. In the FL setting, each speaker's audio forms a separate client with its own data distribution; client model deltas are clipped to an L2 norm bound C and perturbed with Gaussian noise scaled by C²σ²_DP·q/Σω²_k before server-side aggregation, making the effective input to the central optimizer a noisy, bounded pseudo-gradient rather than a raw gradient.

## Method

The core contribution is a training recipe combining per-layer clipping with the LAMB layer-wise adaptive optimizer for the central update. Per-layer clipping redistributes the total clipping budget C across H layers (either uniformly as C_h = C/√H or dimension-proportionally as C_h = C·√(d_h/Σd_i)), which mitigates the observed imbalance where attention parameters have an order-of-magnitude smaller delta norms than LayerNorms. LAMB's trust ratio (weight norm / gradient norm per layer) then dynamically rescales the effective learning rate per layer, jointly reducing clipping bias and inter-layer gradient heterogeneity. The paper derives a finite-time convergence bound (Corollary 1) that explicitly captures the coupling among per-layer clipping constants C_h, LAMB trust ratios R_h, DP noise σ_DP, and intra/inter-client update variances, showing that per-layer intervention tightens the bound when gradient distributions are heterogeneous across layers. Empirically, this combination achieves user-level (7.2, 10⁻⁹)-DP with only 1.3% absolute WER degradation (extrapolated to ~70M users) and (4.5, 10⁻⁹)-DP with 4.6% degradation (~7M users), using a 250M-parameter pre-LayerNorm transformer with CTC loss, SGD locally, and LAMB centrally.

## Results

The paper establishes the first practical benchmark for FL with DP in end-to-end ASR using a 250M-parameter transformer with CTC loss. With per-layer clipping and σ_DP=3·10⁻⁶, the model achieves 17.9%/21.2% dev/test WER (vs. 15.9%/19.1% without DP), corresponding to (7.2, 10⁻⁹)-DP when extrapolated to ~70M users — a 1.3% absolute WER degradation. Increasing noise to σ_DP=10⁻⁵ yields 21.3%/25.0% WER with (4.5, 10⁻⁹)-DP at ~7M users (4.6% degradation). Central training achieves 14.7%/17.8% WER, and FL without DP reaches 15.7%/18.9%, showing FL models are at worst ~0.3–1.4% WER behind central models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is between per-layer clipping and global (whole-model) clipping under identical DP noise. Per-layer clipping dramatically outperforms global clipping: at σ_DP=3·10⁻⁶, per-layer achieves 17.9% dev WER versus 27.0% for global (a 9.1-point gap), and at σ_DP=10⁻⁵ the gap widens to 21.3% vs. 30.7%. Against the no-DP FL baseline (15.9%/19.1%), per-layer clipping incurs only 2.0/2.1 points of WER degradation at σ_DP=3·10⁻⁶, while global clipping degrades by 11.1/12.0 points. Compared to central training (14.7%/17.8%), FL without DP is within 1.0–1.3% WER, and FL with DP (per-layer, σ_DP=3·10⁻⁶) is within 3.2/3.4% WER. FedProx was evaluated as an additional baseline and marginally improved FL performance in some settings (Appendix G.7) but did not close the gap to central training. The LS-100 seed model alone (54.7%/61.2% WER) is substantially outperformed by FL with DP, demonstrating that even under strong privacy constraints, federated fine-tuning recovers most of the lost accuracy from domain shift.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper revives per-layer clipping for differential privacy, a technique proposed by McMahan et al. (ICLR 2018) but deemed ineffective in their LSTM language model setting, and shows it becomes critical when combined with the LAMB optimizer (You et al., ICLR 2020) for large transformer models. It extends the FL-for-ASR line of work (which previously used smaller LSTM or conformer models and lacked DP) to a 250M-parameter vanilla transformer with user-level DP, and provides the first convergence bound that jointly accounts for per-layer clipping, LAMB's trust ratio, and DP noise. The paper also builds on the divergence accumulation phenomenon identified in recent FL theory, showing it is the root cause of the inter-layer gradient imbalance that per-layer clipping addresses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work establishes that FL with DP is practically viable for large transformer ASR models, achieving (7.2, 10⁻⁹)-DP with only a 1.3% WER drop, but the strongest guarantees require extrapolation to populations of ~70M users, which limits near-term deployment. The core insight—that per-layer clipping redistributes the clipping budget to counteract gradient heterogeneity amplified by depth, and that LAMB's trust ratio dynamically re-normalizes the resulting per-layer signal-to-noise ratio—generalizes beyond ASR to any deep model trained under FL with DP. Open questions include whether the recipe extends to conformer or seq2seq architectures, multi-lingual settings, and whether smaller models (more practical for on-device deployment) can match these results with appropriate training recipes.
