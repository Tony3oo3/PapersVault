## Problem

Most SLAM systems assume static, distant illumination and optimize a photometric bundle adjustment loss that compares rendered and captured images directly. In settings where the only light source is co-located with the camera (endoscopy, subterranean robotics, search-and-rescue), the illumination at each surface point changes with camera pose, introducing strong view-dependent shading that standard photometric BA cannot explain, leading to large tracking and mapping errors. No prior work has explicitly modeled near-field lighting geometry within the bundle adjustment loss of a neural-rendering SLAM pipeline.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB video frames (384×384 for endoscopy, 256×192 for indoor scenes), per-frame depth maps (ground-truth or estimated by PPSNet / DA-Hybrid), and known camera intrinsics, and outputs camera poses, a 3D scene representation (Gaussian splat or neural implicit field), and re-rendered images. A notable implementation detail is that the near-field shading term PPS is computed in linear radiance space while stored frames are sRGB, so an inverse gamma correction (γ=2.2) is applied to inputs or a forward correction (γ=1/2.2) to rendered outputs before the photometric loss is evaluated. The loss is designed as a strict drop-in replacement: only the rendering equation inside the bundle adjustment step changes, leaving all other SLAM components (tracking, keyframe selection, regularization) untouched.

## Method

NFL-BA reinterprets the per-primitive color in the volumetric rendering equation as the product of a view-independent albedo ρ(x) and a per-pixel shading term PPS(x_p) = L^a(x_p)·(L^d(x_p)ᵀ n(x_p)), where L^a is an inverse-square attenuation 1/‖x_p − x_L‖² and L^d is the unit vector from the co-located light to the surface point. This decomposes dynamic illumination from intrinsic surface color, so the rendered image Ĉ_pps(p) = Σ ρ(x_i)·PPS(x_i)·α_i·Π(1−α_j) naturally accounts for how shading shifts as the camera-light rig moves. Surface normals are obtained from occupancy gradients in neural fields or from the shortest axis of each Gaussian in 3DGS, and are flipped to face the camera for stability. The angular attenuation coefficient β is set to zero (pure inverse-square law), justified empirically on both endoscopic and indoor data. The resulting NFL-BA loss (Eq. 8) replaces the standard photometric rendering loss in the bundle adjustment objective and is validated as a plug-in for MonoGS, EndoGSLAM, and NICE-SLAM, yielding 37–49% reductions in translational tracking error on C3VD and ~35% on self-captured indoor scenes.

## Results

On the C3VD colonoscopy dataset with PPSNet-predicted depth, replacing Photometric BA with NFL-BA reduces MonoGS translational tracking error (ATE_t) from 3.48 mm to 2.18 mm (37% improvement) and mapping error (Chamfer) from 1.59 mm to 0.99 mm (38% improvement). With DA-Hybrid depth, the ATE_t improvement is even larger at 49% (4.63→2.35 mm). On four self-captured indoor scenes with a co-located flashlight, NFL-BA reduces MonoGS tracking error by ~35% on average (e.g., 0.30→0.18 m on Guitars, 0.36→0.20 m on Stairs), while LPIPS rendering quality remains comparable.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares NFL-BA against the standard Photometric BA loss within three SLAM frameworks: MonoGS (CVPR'24), EndoGSLAM (MICCAI'24), and NICE-SLAM (CVPR'22). With PPSNet depth on C3VD, NFL-BA consistently outperforms Photo-BA on MonoGS across all four metrics (ATE_t, ATE_r, Chamfer, LPIPS), achieving the best scores in the table. For EndoGSLAM, NFL-BA improves ATE_t (3.03→2.62 mm) and ATE_r (1.73→1.24°) with PPSNet but shows a slight Chamfer regression (1.23→1.25 mm); with DA-Hybrid depth, the ATE_t gain is larger (6.67→3.91 mm) but Chamfer worsens (2.12→2.39 mm). On NICE-SLAM with oracle depth, NFL-BA reduces ATE_t from 4.16 to 2.88 mm and Chamfer from 1.95 to 1.70 mm, though ATE_r slightly increases (2.68→2.81°). On indoor scenes, NFL-BA beats Photo-BA on ATE_t in all four scenes (27–44% reduction) while LPIPS is roughly comparable, confirming the benefit generalizes beyond endoscopy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the near-field lighting image formation model (inverse-square law attenuation + Lambertian shading) previously used in single-view endoscopic depth estimation (PPSNet, LightDepth) to a multi-view 3D neural rendering context, and integrates it directly into the bundle adjustment loss of SLAM. It departs from standard Photometric Bundle Adjustment, which assumes static illumination and models scene appearance as fixed RGB color, by decomposing appearance into albedo and a per-pixel shading term computed from scene geometry and camera pose. The authors explicitly note that near-field lighting models had never been applied to SLAM or combined with neural rendering methods prior to this work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play nature of NFL-BA (replacing only the photometric rendering loss) makes it immediately applicable to any existing neural rendering SLAM system, which is practically important for endoscopy navigation, search-and-rescue robotics, and subterranean inspection where co-located lighting is the sole illumination source. Key limitations include the inability to handle specular reflections, sub-surface scattering, and inter-reflections, which remain open challenges. Future work on learning the angular attenuation coefficient β per-scene and incorporating specular models could further close the gap on real clinical data with strong specular highlights (as seen on Colon10K).
