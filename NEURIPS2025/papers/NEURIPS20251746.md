## Problem

Existing token pruning methods for Transformers rely on attention scores to estimate token importance, which the authors show does not accurately reflect each token's true contribution to model predictions. Additionally, most pruning strategies apply fixed or globally learned retention ratios, ignoring substantial variation in sequence length and complexity within and across datasets, leading to under- or over-pruning.

## Input / Output

The method takes a token sequence (up to 512 tokens for SQuAD v2.0, shorter for GLUE tasks) and adaptively prunes it to a variable number of tokens at the input embedding layer before feeding it into the Transformer backbone. The output is task-specific: a class label for GLUE classification tasks (CoLA, RTE, QQP, MRPC, SST-2, MNLI, QNLI, STS-B) or an answer span for SQuAD v2.0. The pruning ratio is determined per-sequence at inference time by a lightweight retainer module, so the effective input length varies dynamically.

## Method

AD-TP replaces attention-based importance scoring with Integrated Gradients (IG), which integrates gradients along a path from a [PAD] baseline to the actual input to quantify each token's marginal contribution to the prediction. A lightweight Attribution-Aware Adaptive Token Retainer then dynamically selects how many tokens to keep: an Adaptive Retention Ratio Predictor (ARP) maps the [CLS] embedding through a linear layer and argmax over a candidate ratio list, while a Token Saliency Predictor (TSP) scores each token via a two-layer MLP, and the top-ρ% tokens are retained. Training combines teacher supervision (logits distillation plus an attribution distillation loss that aligns normalized IG scores between teacher and student) with self-supervised learning, where a scheduled weight k gradually shifts reliance from teacher to student attribution scores. A dual normalization strategy (L2 followed by sequence-level) ensures attribution scores are comparable across models with different token counts after pruning.

## Results

On the GLUE benchmark, AD-TP12 achieves an average 7.37× FLOPs reduction while maintaining or improving accuracy over the unpruned BERT-base teacher (e.g., SST-2: 94.7% vs. 93.7%; MRPC F1: 90.8 vs. 91.4; CoLA Matthews: 56.9 vs. 60.3). AD-TP6 achieves a 7.02× average FLOPs reduction with a 6-layer student. On long-sequence tasks, AD-TP6 surpasses BERT-base on 20News (87.3% vs. 86.7% accuracy) while reducing FLOPs by 8.74×, and achieves 76.2 F1 on SQuAD v2.0 with 5.07× FLOPs reduction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AD-TP12 outperforms the strongest token-pruning baseline ToP on 6 of 8 GLUE tasks in accuracy (e.g., SST-2: 94.7 vs. 93.5; MRPC F1: 90.8 vs. 89.2; STS-B: 88.7 vs. 87.6) and achieves higher FLOPs reduction on 7 of 8 tasks, with the largest gap on SST-2 (8.45× vs. 3.82×). Against LTP, AD-TP12 wins on accuracy across all 8 tasks while also delivering greater FLOPs savings (e.g., CoLA: 56.9 vs. 52.3 at 13.89× vs. 8.66×). Compared to PoWER-BERT, AD-TP12 improves accuracy on every task (e.g., SST-2: 94.7 vs. 92.1) with substantially higher compression (8.45× vs. 2.40× on SST-2). On long-sequence tasks, AD-TP6 surpasses Transkimmer on SQuAD v2.0 (76.2 vs. 75.7 F1) and 20News (87.3 vs. 86.1 accuracy) while achieving higher FLOPs reduction (5.07× vs. 4.67×; 8.74× vs. 8.11×). In the distillation/structured-pruning comparison (Table 3), AD-TP6 achieves 8.74× FLOPs reduction on 20News versus DistilBERT's 2.00× and CoFi's 5.90×, while maintaining higher accuracy (87.3 vs. 85.8 and 85.9). The only task where a baseline edges out AD-TP12 in accuracy is CoLA, where ToP reaches 60.5 vs. AD-TP12's 56.9, though AD-TP still provides greater FLOPs savings (13.89× vs. 9.62×).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant attention-based token importance scoring used in PoWER-BERT, LTP, ToP, and SpAtten by substituting Integrated Gradients (IG) as the attribution signal, motivated by the observation that accumulated attention weights do not reliably identify tokens critical to model predictions. It extends the adaptive-pruning line initiated by LAT and LTP by replacing fixed or globally-learned thresholds with a per-sequence retention ratio predicted by a lightweight module (ARP + TSP), making pruning decisions instance-level rather than dataset-level. The training framework combines teacher-student knowledge distillation with a novel dual-normalization attribution alignment loss and a curriculum that gradually shifts supervision from teacher to self-supervision, a combination not present in prior token pruning or distillation work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AD-TP demonstrates that attribution-driven, sequence-adaptive token pruning can achieve 7–14× FLOPs reduction on GLUE while matching or slightly exceeding the unpruned student's accuracy, offering a practical path to efficient BERT inference on edge devices. However, the method currently prunes only at the input layer, leaving open the question of whether adaptive pruning can be extended to intermediate Transformer layers without compounding information loss. The reliance on a teacher model and IG during training (adding ~20–30% training time) and the modest accuracy gains over strong baselines like ToP suggest that the approach's main value is in its principled importance estimation and adaptiveness rather than in a large performance leap. Generalization to decoder-only architectures, longer contexts, and non-BERT backbones remains untested.
