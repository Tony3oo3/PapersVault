## Problem

Prior discrete diffusion language models use a decoder-only architecture that must invoke the full network at every denoising step, incurring high computational cost. The paper identifies that diffusion models perform two fundamentally different computations—representing clean tokens and denoising corrupted tokens—but prior work conflates both within a single set of layers, preventing efficient allocation of compute between the two tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250528/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a clean text prompt (P tokens) and a noised token sequence containing [MASK] tokens as input. Under the block diffusion parameterization, the sequence is partitioned into blocks of size S; the encoder processes the prompt plus all previously decoded clean blocks, while the decoder processes only the currently active noised block. Output is a fully denoised text sequence generated block-by-block, with the encoder invoked once per block to update its running representation.

## Method

E2D2 separates the two computations into an N_Enc-layer encoder that represents clean tokens and a lightweight N_Dec-layer decoder (N_Dec < N_Enc) that iteratively denoises noised tokens via cross-attention to encoder features. The key efficiency gain is that the decoder can be invoked T times per block without re-calling the encoder, amortizing the encoder's cost over multiple lightweight decoding steps. Two encoder-decoder coupling variants are explored: a 'last hidden state' design (T5-style, concatenating the final encoder output to each decoder layer) and a 'shared KV cache' design (reusing per-layer encoder KVs to keep decoder cross-attention aligned with the base model, improving fine-tuning stability). A fused attention kernel jointly attends to both encoder outputs and the decoder's own hidden states in a single operation. For block diffusion training, custom attention masks (block-causal for the encoder, block-diagonal + offset block-causal cross-attention for the decoder) enable a single vectorized forward pass over all B blocks, halving training FLOPs compared to decoder-only BD3LM which must process 2L tokens through every layer.

## Results

On CNN/DailyMail summarization, E2D2 (20 enc / 8 dec layers) achieves ROUGE-1 of 36.0, ROUGE-2 of 14.1, and ROUGE-L of 23.9 at 155.8 tokens/sec, outperforming the 28-layer MDLM (30.6/12.5/22.7 at 49.3 t/s) and the 12-layer BD3LM (35.8/13.7/23.7 at 135.1 t/s). On WMT14 de-en translation, E2D2 (28/4) reaches BLEU 24.8 at 162.0 t/s, surpassing the 16-layer BD3LM (24.0 at 102.4 t/s). On GSM8K mathematical reasoning, E2D2 (28/14) achieves 47.9% zero-shot pass@1 at 102.8 t/s, substantially outperforming BD3LM (33.2% at 86.6 t/s) and MDLM (14.0% at 31.9 t/s). For language modeling on OpenWebText, E2D2 attains a validation PPL of 21.73 (comparable to BD3LM's 20.73) while training 40% faster (8.4×10⁴ vs 5.9×10⁴ tokens/sec).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MDLM (28 layers), E2D2 wins decisively on all three downstream tasks: ROUGE-1 36.0 vs 30.6 on summarization, BLEU 24.8 vs 18.4 on translation, and pass@1 47.9% vs 14.0% on GSM8K, while also being ~3× faster in inference throughput. Against BD3LM, E2D2 consistently achieves higher quality at higher throughput: on GSM8K, E2D2 (28/14) reaches 47.9% pass@1 versus 33.2% for the 21-layer BD3LM, and on translation E2D2 (28/4) scores BLEU 24.8 versus 24.0 for the 16-layer BD3LM at 162 vs 102.4 t/s. Against the same-size AR baseline (28 layers), E2D2 outperforms on summarization (ROUGE-1 36.0 vs 31.7) with ~75% higher throughput, but trails on GSM8K (47.9% vs 66.6%) and is marginally below on translation (24.8 vs 25.2 BLEU). On language modeling perplexity, E2D2 (21.73 OWT PPL) is slightly worse than BD3LM (20.73) but substantially better than SEDD (24.10) and MDLM (22.98), while training 40% faster than BD3LM. The paper also maps the Pareto frontier by varying decoder depth, showing E2D2 dominates BD3LM at every matched throughput level on GSM8K.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the block diffusion language modeling framework (BD3LM) and the masked diffusion formulation (MDLM/D3PM), which prior work has shown to outperform full-sequence diffusion in quality. The key architectural departure is from the decoder-only paradigm used by all prior diffusion language models (LLaDA, Seed Diffusion, MMaDA, Dream7B), which must invoke the full network at every denoising step. By splitting the two distinct computational roles—representing clean tokens and denoising corrupted tokens—into separate encoder and decoder modules, E2D2 borrows the encoder-decoder pattern from T5-style sequence-to-sequence models but applies it to iterative diffusion denoising rather than autoregressive decoding. The closest prior work is ENAT for discrete image generation, which similarly decouples clean-token and masked-token computation, but E2D2 extends this to variable-length text with KV caching support.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 2× training FLOPs reduction for block diffusion and the ability to amortize encoder cost over multiple lightweight decoder steps address the central practical bottleneck of diffusion language models: high per-step inference cost. Mapping the Pareto frontier by varying decoder depth provides practitioners with a clear knob for trading quality against throughput. However, experiments are limited to 80M–1.7B parameter models on domain-specific tasks, leaving open whether the encoder-decoder advantage persists at the 7–8B scale where current diffusion LMs compete with AR models. The paper also acknowledges that further innovation is needed to close both the performance and throughput gaps relative to autoregressive models, and the optimal N_Enc/N_Dec split ratio remains task-dependent.
