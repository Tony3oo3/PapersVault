## Problem

Reconstructing 4D dynamic scenes from monocular videos has been limited to either per-scene optimization (requiring hours per video) or feed-forward models trained on synthetic object-level data that fail to generalize to complex real-world dynamics. No prior method combines a transformer-based feed-forward architecture with 4D Gaussian Splatting trained exclusively on large-scale real-world monocular posed videos, leaving a gap between scalability and reconstruction quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251419/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes 64 consecutive posed monocular frames at 504×504 resolution with 6DoF camera poses (encoded as Plücker coordinates) and timestamps, processed in a rolling-window fashion for longer sequences. It outputs approximately 1 million pixel-aligned 2D Gaussians per window, each carrying spatial parameters (center, 2D scale, quaternion orientation, opacity) and temporal parameters (lifespan, 3D velocity, angular velocity), which are rasterized in real-time to produce novel view renderings, metric depth maps, surface normals, optical flow, and motion segmentation masks.

## Method

4DGT uses a 12-layer ViT fusion network that takes timestamp-aware Plücker rays concatenated with DINOv2 features and RGB patches as input, decoding pixel-aligned 2DGS with explicit temporal motion attributes (lifespan, velocity, angular velocity) that unify static and dynamic content in a single representation. A two-stage density control strategy first trains at coarse resolution, then prunes 80% of Gaussians via opacity-histogram thresholding and densifies with 16× higher space-time sampling (R_s=2, R_t=4, S=10 active channels per 14×14 patch). A multi-level spatiotemporal attention mechanism (L=3 levels, M=4 temporal trunks) alternates spatial resolution and temporal range to reduce self-attention cost by ~2×. Training is self-supervised via rendering losses (MSE, LPIPS, SSIM) over 128-frame segments, augmented with regularization toward static/long-lifespan Gaussians and pseudo-supervision from DepthAnythingV2 depth and StableNormal normals, all trained on 1000+ hours of real-world monocular posed video from Project Aria, Epic-Fields, Cop3D, and ARKitTrack.

## Results

On the ADT benchmark, 4DGT achieves 28.31 dB PSNR and 0.934 depth RMSE, substantially outperforming the closest feed-forward baseline L4GM (7.35 dB PSNR, 2.606 RMSE) by over 20 dB in appearance quality. Against the optimization-based Shape of Motion (SoM), 4DGT matches PSNR on TUM (21.02 vs. 21.03) and DyCheck (16.12 vs. 16.49) while achieving 4.4× lower depth RMSE (0.934 vs. 4.158) and 1.35× lower normal angle error (25.92° vs. 35.05°). Inference runs at 25 ms/frame, making it 2,400× faster than SoM (60,000 ms/frame) and 8× faster than L4GM (200 ms/frame).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against L4GM (the closest prior feed-forward 4D Gaussian model trained on synthetic data), 4DGT wins decisively on every metric: PSNR improves from 7.35 to 28.31 dB on ADT (+21 dB), depth RMSE drops from 2.606 to 0.934, and normal angle error falls from 63.51° to 25.92°, demonstrating that real-world monocular training generalizes far better than synthetic object-level training. Against the optimization-based Shape of Motion (SoM), 4DGT achieves on-par PSNR on TUM (21.02 vs. 21.03) and DyCheck (16.12 vs. 16.49) and even exceeds SoM on Aria (27.36 vs. 26.69), while dramatically outperforming it on geometry (depth RMSE 0.934 vs. 4.158; normal error 25.92° vs. 35.05°) and being 2,400× faster (25 ms vs. 60,000 ms per frame). Against expert monocular models (DepthAnythingV2 + StableNormal), 4DGT surpasses them on PSNR (28.31 vs. 23.32 on ADT) and depth RMSE (0.934 vs. 2.931), showing that the learned 4D representation produces more metrically consistent geometry than the very experts used as training supervision. Against MonST3R (dynamic point-based representation), 4DGT wins on all appearance and geometry metrics except LPIPS on TUM, confirming the advantage of explicit 4D Gaussian motion modeling over per-frame point clouds. The static LRM baseline (trained on the same data) achieves 19.29 dB PSNR on ADT versus 28.31 for 4DGT, confirming that the dynamic Gaussian representation is essential for handling motion.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two recent lines of work: feed-forward Large Reconstruction Models (LRMs) for static 3D scenes (GS-LRM, Long-LRM) and 4D Gaussian Splatting for dynamic scenes (FreeTimeGS, 4DGS). It departs from prior feed-forward 4D methods like L4GM, which rely on synthetic object-level training data and multi-view diffusion priors, by training exclusively on real-world posed monocular videos. It also extends the 2DGS representation (which improves geometry over 3DGS) with explicit temporal attributes (lifespan, velocity, angular velocity) to unify static and dynamic content in a single feed-forward pipeline, and introduces a density-control strategy (histogram-based pruning plus multi-level spatiotemporal attention) borrowed conceptually from 3DGS densification and ViT multi-stage training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 3-orders-of-magnitude speedup over per-scene optimization (25 ms vs. 60 s per frame) makes long-video 4D reconstruction practically feasible for the first time in a feed-forward setting, with direct implications for AR/VR content pipelines and robotics. The finding that real-world monocular training alone—without synthetic data or multi-view supervision—can match optimization-based quality on cross-domain videos challenges the prevailing assumption that such supervision is necessary. Key open questions include generalization to unseen device types (the model degrades with inaccurate metric calibration), artifacts at extreme viewpoints inherent to monocular reconstruction, and whether incorporating flow or tracking experts (e.g., TAPIR) could further improve temporal consistency without sacrificing the feed-forward speed advantage.
