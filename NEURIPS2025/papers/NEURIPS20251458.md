## Problem

LLM unlearning research is fragmented: different benchmarks (TOFU, MUSE, WMDP) use incompatible evaluation metrics, unlearning methods are not consistently implemented across setups, and there is no consensus on whether existing metrics reliably detect true forgetting. This fragmentation prevents rigorous cross-method comparison and reproducibility, and the reliability of the evaluation metrics themselves has gone unexamined.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251458/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework consumes LLM model weights across 8 architectures (LLaMA-2/3.1/3.2, Phi-1.5/3.5, Gemma, Zephyr, Qwen-2.5), forget and retain datasets from 3 benchmarks, and YAML-specified unlearning method configurations, producing unlearned model checkpoints, per-metric scores across memorization/privacy/utility dimensions, and composite benchmark rankings. Uniquely, it also outputs meta-evaluation scores (faithfulness AUC-ROC and robustness under quantization/relearning) computed over a test-bed of 450+ publicly released checkpoints with known ground-truth knowledge states, enabling objective stress-testing of any evaluation metric.

## Method

OpenUnlearning is a modular, Hydra-configured pipeline that unifies 13 unlearning algorithms, 16 evaluation metrics, and 3 benchmarks (TOFU, MUSE, WMDP) under a single extensible architecture where new methods, metrics, and datasets are added via a two-step handler-registration + YAML-config procedure. The key technical contribution is a meta-evaluation protocol that assesses each metric on two desiderata: faithfulness (AUC-ROC separating 30 models trained with target knowledge from 30 trained without, across diverse formats) and robustness (stability under benign interventions like 4-bit quantization and non-benign interventions like relearning on the forget set, measured via relative recovery rates against a retain model). These are aggregated via harmonic mean to produce an overall reliability score per metric. The framework further benchmarks 8 unlearning methods (SimNPO, RMU, UNDIAL, AltPO, NPO, IdkNLL, IdkDPO, GradDiff) using 10 metrics with 27 hyperparameter tuning trials per method, following the SemEval 2025 ranking procedure, and releases all evaluated checkpoints to support community-driven metric and method development.

## Results

On the TOFU benchmark, SimNPO achieves the best aggregate unlearning score of 0.53 (Mem. 0.32, Priv. 0.63, Utility 1.00), approaching the Retain model's gold-standard score of 0.58. RMU follows closely at 0.52 aggregate but suffers a significant utility drop (0.61 vs. 0.99 for Retain). In the meta-evaluation of 12 unlearning metrics, Extraction Strength (ES) emerges as the most reliable overall metric (Agg. 0.85, Faithfulness 0.92, Robustness 0.79), while MIA-based metrics show high faithfulness (0.81–0.93) but poor robustness (0.48–0.57).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SimNPO outperforms all other methods on the aggregate score (0.53 vs. 0.52 for RMU and 0.42 for UNDIAL), primarily because it fully preserves utility (1.00) while achieving competitive privacy (0.63) and memorization (0.32) scores close to the Retain model. RMU achieves the best memorization score (0.47) but at the cost of a severe utility drop to 0.61, reflecting aggressive over-unlearning. UNDIAL (0.42) offers a middle ground with moderate scores across all three dimensions. The three methods AltPO, IdkNLL, and NPO all tie at 0.15 aggregate but for very different reasons: AltPO and NPO over-unlearn (high Mem. 0.63/0.52, near-zero Priv. 0.06) while IdkNLL under-unlearns (low Mem. 0.08, low Priv. 0.17). GradDiff, the original TOFU baseline, performs worst (0.009 aggregate) due to extreme over-unlearning that destroys both privacy and utility. The authors note that different ranking/aggregation schemes can produce substantially different method orderings, highlighting sensitivity in the evaluation protocol.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies the fragmented LLM unlearning landscape by consolidating three major benchmarks (TOFU, MUSE, WMDP), 13 unlearning methods spanning preference optimization (SimNPO, NPO, AltPO), gradient-based approaches (GradDiff, IdkDPO, IdkNLL), and self-distillation (UNDIAL, RMU) under a single extensible framework. Its most distinctive conceptual departure is the meta-evaluation of unlearning metrics themselves—assessing faithfulness (AUC-ROC between models with/without target knowledge) and robustness (stability under quantization and relearning)—a layer of scrutiny that prior benchmark papers (TOFU, MUSE, WMDP) did not provide. The framework also addresses practical fragmentation by cross-porting metrics between benchmarks (e.g., MUSE's PrivLeak into TOFU) and integrating LM Eval Harness for general utility assessment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By releasing 450+ model checkpoints with known ground-truth states and a standardized pipeline, OpenUnlearning gives the community a shared testbed for developing and stress-testing both unlearning methods and their evaluation metrics—addressing a critical reproducibility gap in a field where prior work has been criticized for unreliable metrics. The meta-evaluation finding that MIA-based privacy metrics, while faithful, are highly vulnerable to benign interventions (quantization) and non-benign ones (relearning) challenges the common practice of relying on MIA scores as evidence of successful unlearning. Key open questions include whether the faithfulness/robustness desiderata generalize beyond the TOFU forget10 synthetic setup, whether the ranking sensitivities observed (different aggregation schemes yielding different method orderings) can be resolved, and how the framework scales to open-ended unlearning tasks without a defined forget corpus.
