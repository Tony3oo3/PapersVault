## Problem

AlphaFold-predicted structures in the AFDB, while highly accurate at the fold level, exhibit a systematic geometric bias—narrower dihedral angle distributions, tighter bond length variances, and reduced conformational variability—compared to experimentally determined PDB structures. This bias causes inverse folding models trained on AFDB to suffer severe performance degradation (e.g., PiFold drops from 43.76% to 17.16% recovery rate) despite the structures being within ~0.2 Å RMSD of their PDB counterparts. No prior work had systematically quantified this distributional shift or proposed a principled debiasing framework to restore structural realism for downstream learning.

## Input / Output

The method consumes backbone coordinates (N, Cα, C, O per residue) from AFDB predictions and produces debiased backbone coordinates that better match the PDB conformational distribution. During training, PDB structures are corrupted by randomly selecting 10% of residues and replacing one backbone atom's position with the centroid of the remaining three atoms in that residue, forcing the model to infer local geometric integrity from context. The debiased output preserves global fold (RMSD to PDB remains ~0.2 Å) while restoring the broader local geometric variability characteristic of experimental structures.

## Method

DeSAE is a compact (5.9M parameter) SE(3)-equivariant autoencoder trained with a denoising objective on PDB structures. The encoder uses 8 layers of Frame Aggregation—projecting node/edge features into virtual atom point clouds, computing relative frame geometry, and capturing inter-node interactions via aligned-frame dot products—while the decoder adds 6 layers of Frame Updating that iteratively refine per-residue rotation (via attention-weighted quaternion aggregation) and translation. Training supervision uses a 5-term structure consistency loss (global, fragment, pair, neighbor, distance) inspired by Chroma, computed at every decoder layer. At inference, the trained DeSAE is applied to AFDB structures to project them onto the learned experimental structural manifold, producing a 'Debiased AFDB' dataset that, when used to train inverse folding models, consistently closes the generalization gap toward PDB-trained performance across five architectures and four benchmarks.

## Results

On the CATH 4.3 test set, DeSAE-debiased AFDB structures consistently improve inverse folding recovery rates over raw AFDB across all five evaluated models, with gains ranging from +3.57% (GraphTrans) to +17.64% (PiFold). The largest absolute improvement is for PiFold, which jumps from 17.74% (AFDB) to 35.38% (Debiased AFDB), closing most of the gap to its PDB-trained performance of 43.74%. ProteinMPNN also benefits substantially, improving from 26.48% to 35.22%. Larger-scale DeSAE pretraining (Debiased AFDB-XL) yields only marginal additional gains of +0.33% to +0.58% in recovery rate but more meaningful perplexity reductions (e.g., PiFold perplexity drops from 10.39 to 8.43).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates five inverse folding baselines—StructGNN/GraphTrans [7], GVP [8], ProteinMPNN [48], and PiFold [9]—each trained independently on PDB, AFDB, and Debiased AFDB structures, then tested on CATH 4.2, CATH 4.3, TS50, and TS500. Debiased AFDB consistently outperforms raw AFDB across all five methods and all four benchmarks, with the most dramatic gain for PiFold (+17.64% on CATH 4.3) and ProteinMPNN (+8.74%), confirming that higher-capacity models suffer most from AFDB's geometric idealization. However, PDB-trained models still lead in absolute recovery rate (e.g., PiFold: 43.74% PDB vs. 35.38% Debiased AFDB), indicating that debiasing narrows but does not fully close the distributional gap. On the complementary AFDB-version CATH 4.2 test set (Table 2), raw AFDB-trained models achieve the highest scores (e.g., ProteinMPNN: 76.83%), while Debiased AFDB-trained models rank second (45.34%), validating that the debiasing shifts the learned manifold toward PDB-like geometry. The perplexity metric (Table 3) further shows that Debiased AFDB-XL refines probability distributions beyond what recovery rate captures, with PiFold perplexity dropping from 13.83 (AFDB) to 8.43 (Debiased AFDB-XL), approaching the PDB-trained value of 6.42.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the SE(3) equivariant geometric deep learning framework for proteins (frame aggregation and frame updating layers) and adopts the multi-scale structure consistency loss from Chroma [79] to supervise a denoising autoencoder. Conceptually, it departs from the inverse folding literature (StructGNN, GraphTrans, GVP, PiFold, ProteinMPNN) by identifying that the structural training data itself—specifically AFDB—carries a systematic geometric bias that prior work has not addressed. The corruption strategy (replacing a backbone atom with the centroid of its three neighbors) is a novel denoising objective tailored to protein backbone geometry, distinct from standard Gaussian noise or coordinate perturbation approaches used in molecular denoising.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AFDB is the dominant structural resource for data-driven protein design, and this paper demonstrates that its systematic geometric idealization (tighter dihedral distributions, reduced bond-length variance) silently degrades downstream model generalization—particularly for high-capacity architectures. The finding that stronger models are *more* susceptible to AFDB bias (PiFold drops 61% relative) challenges the assumption that model capacity alone guarantees robustness to data distribution shifts. A key limitation is the backbone-only scope; extending debiasing to sidechain orientations and full-atom representations remains open. The lightweight nature of DeSAE (5.9M params, ~3 min for 20k structures on one A100) makes it practically deployable as a preprocessing step, but whether the debiased manifold generalizes to tasks beyond inverse folding (e.g., binding site prediction, PPI interfaces) warrants further investigation.
