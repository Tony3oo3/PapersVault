## Problem

Existing data selection methods for visual instruction tuning suffer from two critical drawbacks: they require the target MLLM to process the entire candidate dataset (incurring computational costs comparable to full fine-tuning), and they treat importance and diversity as decoupled objectives optimized in separate stages, leading to suboptimal trade-offs. This paper asks whether both objectives can be jointly optimized while training on only a small random fraction of the data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255569/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes image-text pairs from a visual instruction dataset (e.g., LLaVA-665K) and pre-extracts four feature types per sample: CLIP multimodal embeddings (dimensionality-reduced), a CLIP alignment score, an ImageReward image-quality score, and an LLM-based text-quality score (using DeepSeek-V3). These concatenated features are fed into a 4-layer MLP scorer that outputs a single scalar CoIDO score per sample. At selection time, the trained scorer is applied to the full dataset, and the top-γ fraction of samples per downstream task (lowest scores = most important and diverse) is retained for fine-tuning the target MLLM.

## Method

CoIDO introduces a lightweight plug-in scorer (4-layer MLP) co-trained with the target MLLM on only p% (default 20%) of randomly sampled data, eliminating the need to process the full dataset. The key mechanism is a coupled optimization of two losses: an importance loss that re-weights the cross-entropy loss by learnable per-sample scores (lower score = more important, driven by backpropagation), and a diversity loss that minimizes the variance of mean scores across spectral clusters (M=20) to prevent cluster dominance. These two objectives are balanced automatically via a homoscedastic uncertainty formulation (Kendall et al.), where learnable parameters σ_I and σ_D act as task-specific noise scales, yielding the total loss L_total = (1/σ_I²)L_I + (1/2σ_D²)L_D + log σ_I + log σ_D. Spectral clustering on the extracted features provides the cluster assignments used in the diversity loss, and the entire pipeline requires no specialized selection algorithm at inference—samples are simply ranked by their CoIDO scores.

## Results

CoIDO achieves a relative performance (Rel.) of 98.2% on LLaVA-1.5-7B across ten multimodal benchmarks while training the scorer on only 20% of the LLaVA-665K dataset, requiring just 4.2 ExaFLOPs in total — the lowest among all compared methods. This surpasses the best model-involved baseline COINCIDE (97.4% Rel., 4.9E FLOPs) and ICONS (97.1% Rel., 12.6E FLOPs), as well as the best model-free baseline D2-PRUNING (94.8% Rel.). On individual benchmarks, CoIDO attains 77.2 on VQAv2, 60.4 on GQA, 55.6 on TextVQA, and 70.1 on LLaVA-Bench, matching or exceeding full-data fine-tuning on several tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against model-free baselines, CoIDO (98.2% Rel.) substantially outperforms all seven methods, with the closest competitor D2-Pruning reaching only 94.8% and CLIP-Score the lowest at 91.2%. Among model-involved methods, CoIDO surpasses COINCIDE (97.4%) by 0.8% in Rel. while using only 20% of the training data versus COINCIDE's 100%, and reduces total FLOPs from 4.9E to 4.2E. Compared to ICONS (97.1%, 12.6E FLOPs, requiring 100%+5%+2.2% data), CoIDO gains 1.1% Rel. at roughly one-third the computational cost and without needing benchmark validation sets. TIVE (96.7%, 11.7E FLOPs) and Self-Filter (90.9%, 31.2E FLOPs) are further behind in both accuracy and efficiency. Notably, CoIDO is the only method that achieves >97% Rel. while keeping MLLM Training Data Cost at just 20%, making it the most efficient high-performing selection strategy in the comparison.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the data selection literature for visual instruction tuning (TIVE, ICONS, SELF-FILTER, COINCIDE) by addressing two limitations of prior work: the decoupled treatment of importance and diversity, and the requirement to process the entire dataset with the target MLLM. The core optimization mechanism is adapted from Kendall et al.'s homoscedastic uncertainty framework for multi-task learning, repurposed here to jointly balance an importance loss (weighted cross-entropy) and a diversity loss (inter-cluster weight variance) without manual hyperparameter tuning. The lightweight MLP scorer trained on a 20% random subset departs from the gradient-based or clustering-based full-dataset evaluation strategies used by all direct competitors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 80% reduction in MLLM training data cost (20% vs. 100%) while retaining 98.2% of full-data performance makes high-quality visual instruction tuning accessible to groups without large-scale GPU clusters, a practical gap the paper explicitly targets. The transferability result—where a scorer trained on the larger LLaVA-665K outperforms one trained in-domain on Vision-Flan—suggests that data selection scorers may benefit from training on more diverse corpora, an open question worth further investigation. Limitations include the absence of error bars (attributed to compute cost), evaluation on a single model family (LLaVA), and reliance on pre-computed features (CLIP, ImageReward, LLM scores) that add a non-trivial preprocessing pipeline. Future directions noted by the authors include dynamic selection during training, proxy-model co-training, and extension to video and audio modalities.
