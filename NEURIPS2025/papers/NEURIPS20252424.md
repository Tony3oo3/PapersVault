## Problem

Existing hidden state differential privacy analyses are restricted to convex optimization problems, limiting their practical application to logistic regression for classification. This paper asks whether convex approximations of two-layer ReLU networks can be constructed that preserve model performance while enabling the use of privacy amplification by iteration for tighter DP guarantees on non-linear models.

## Input / Output

The method takes flattened image vectors (28×28 grayscale for MNIST/FashionMNIST, 32×32×3 color for CIFAR-10) and produces 10-class labels. At inference, the model evaluates P randomly sampled hyperplane activation patterns against the input, computing a weighted sum of activated feature projections. For multi-class outputs, K independent linear models run in parallel, each predicting one output dimension, yielding a total parameter dimensionality of d×P×K.

## Method

The core contribution is a stochastic strongly convex approximation of the two-layer ReLU minimization problem. Starting from the convex dual formulation of Pilanci and Ergen (2020), the paper replaces the group ℓ2-ℓ1 regularization with L2 regularization and removes the data-dependent cone constraints, yielding a loss that is both λ-strongly convex and β-smooth. This formulation is equivalent to a generalized linear model whose features are constructed from P randomly sampled hyperplane activation patterns (boolean diagonal matrices). The strong convexity enables the privacy amplification by iteration analysis of Bok et al. (2024) to be applied to NoisyCGD with fixed disjoint mini-batches, avoiding the Poisson subsampling and variable batch sizes required by DP-SGD. Empirically, NoisyCGD on this convex model achieves privacy-utility trade-offs on par with DP-SGD applied directly to 2-layer ReLU networks on MNIST, FashionMNIST, and CIFAR-10.

## Results

On MNIST, NoisyCGD applied to the stochastic convex approximation achieves 92.4% test accuracy at ε=1.33 and 94.4% at ε=4.76, matching or exceeding DP-SGD on a 2-layer ReLU network (91.7% and 94.3% respectively). On CIFAR-10, the convex approximation reaches 41.0% (ε=1.33) and 45.4% (ε=4.76) with NoisyCGD, slightly below DP-SGD+ReLU (42.5% and 47.0%). All iterative methods substantially outperform the one-shot Sufficient Statistics Perturbation baselines, which peak at 64.1% (MNIST) and 28.5% (CIFAR-10) under the same privacy budgets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to DP-SGD on a 2-layer ReLU network (Abadi et al., 2016), the convex approximation is competitive: on MNIST, DP-SGD+ConvexApprox actually exceeds DP-SGD+ReLU by 1.4 points at ε=1.33 (93.1% vs 91.7%), while NoisyCGD+ConvexApprox is within 0.3 points. On CIFAR-10, DP-SGD+ReLU retains a small 1–1.5 point edge over both convex-approximation variants. Against the Sufficient Statistics Perturbation one-shot baselines (Amin et al., 2023), all iterative methods win decisively—by roughly 28–30 points on MNIST and 13–17 points on CIFAR-10 at ε=1.33—confirming that iterative training of non-linear models is essential for high-utility hidden-state DP. The key practical finding is that NoisyCGD with fixed disjoint mini-batches achieves privacy-utility trade-offs on par with DP-SGD while offering simpler implementation and higher throughput (no Poisson sampling, no straggler batches).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate research threads: convex reformulations of two-layer ReLU networks (Pilanci & Ergen 2020; Kim & Pilanci 2024; Wang et al. 2022) and privacy amplification by iteration for hidden-state differential privacy (Feldman et al. 2018; Bok et al. 2024). Prior hidden-state DP analyses were restricted to convex problems like logistic regression, while the convex dual formulations of ReLU networks had not been explored for their privacy implications. The paper's key conceptual move is showing that a stochastic, strongly convex approximation of the ReLU dual problem satisfies the conditions of Bok et al.'s GDP analysis for NoisyCGD, thereby extending hidden-state DP guarantees from linear models to neural-network-level performance. It also positions NoisyCGD with fixed disjoint mini-batches as a practical alternative to DP-SGD's Poisson subsampling, building on the computational-motivation work of Chua et al. (2024a).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work demonstrates that the hidden-state DP threat model, previously limited to logistic regression, can achieve privacy-utility trade-offs comparable to DP-SGD on 2-layer ReLU networks for image classification — a meaningful step toward making hidden-state analyses practically relevant for deep learning. NoisyCGD's fixed batch sizes offer a simpler, higher-throughput implementation path compared to DP-SGD's variable-size Poisson sampling. Key limitations include restriction to 2-layer ReLU architectures, small benchmark datasets (MNIST, FashionMNIST, CIFAR-10), and the need for L2 regularization that slightly degrades utility. Open questions include extending the convex reformulation to deeper networks, convolutional layers, and other activation functions, as well as analyzing the n/d < 1 regime in the utility bound.
