## Problem

Adversarial examples crafted on a surrogate model often fail to transfer to unseen target models in black-box settings, due to two underexplored root causes: decision-boundary variation across models and representation drift in feature space. Existing transfer-based attacks inject diversity heuristically but do not explicitly or jointly address both factors, leading to sharp performance drops when target architecture diverges from the surrogate.

## Input / Output

CORTA takes a clean RGB image (32×32 for CIFAR-100, 224×224 for ImageNet) and a single pretrained surrogate model (ResNet-18 for CNN surrogates, ViT-Tiny for ViT surrogates), and produces an L∞-bounded perturbation δ (ε=16/255) over T=100 iterations. The output is the adversarial example x+δ, which is evaluated by its transfer success rate on unseen target models. Unlike ensemble-based methods that require four surrogate architectures, CORTA operates with only one surrogate, and its stochastic feature blending is applied to all layers for CNNs or all linear layers for ViTs.

## Method

CORTA frames untargeted transferability as a consensus-robust optimization: maximize the worst-case loss over an uncertainty set defined by bounded weight perturbations (parameter channel) and stochastic feature blending (representation channel). The parameter channel regularizes the squared Frobenius norm of the logits' Jacobian with respect to surrogate weights, reducing sensitivity to decision-boundary shifts; the representation channel performs Monte Carlo sampling of blending coefficients λ∈[λ_min,1] at selected layers to mix clean and adversarial activations, emulating feature drift. The practical objective jointly maximizes expected cross-entropy loss under random blends while penalizing parameter sensitivity, optimized via any first-order gradient method (I-FGSM, MI-FGSM, DIM-FGSM). A theoretical analysis provides a certified lower bound on the robust target objective in terms of the expected blended loss and the Jacobian norm, giving principled guarantees for the approximations.

## Results

On CIFAR-100 with ResNet-18 as surrogate, CORTA achieves average Transfer Success Rates (TSR) of 97.6% on CNN targets and 96.3% on ViT targets, outperforming the strongest baselines (Checkpoints at 93.5% and Ens at 82.1%) by 4.1 and 14.2 percentage points, respectively. On ImageNet with the same surrogate, CORTA reaches 95.5% (CNN) and 57.4% (ViT) average TSR. The headline result is a 19.1 pp gain over the ensemble-based Ens method when transferring from ResNet-18 to Swin-B on CIFAR-100 (97.9% vs. 78.8%), while using only a single surrogate model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CORTA outperforms all seven baselines on every individual target model across both datasets. Against the ensemble-based methods (Ens, AdaEA, Checkpoints), CORTA's advantage is most dramatic on ViT targets: on CIFAR-100 it surpasses Ens by 14.2 pp (96.3% vs. 82.1%) and Checkpoints by 32.9 pp (96.3% vs. 63.4%), despite using a single CNN surrogate versus their multi-architecture ensembles. On ImageNet ViT targets, CORTA leads Checkpoints by 9.2 pp (57.4% vs. 48.2%) and Ens by 15.1 pp (57.4% vs. 42.3%). Among single-surrogate baselines, CORTA beats the strongest feature-level method BFA by 1.3 pp on ImageNet CNN targets (95.5% vs. 94.2%) and by 7.1 pp on ImageNet ViT targets (57.4% vs. 50.3%). The gradient-based ANDA and input-transformation Admix baselines trail CORTA by 10–55 pp depending on target architecture, with the largest gaps on ViT targets where their transferability degrades most severely. CORTA also maintains its lead under adversarial training defenses (69.8% avg TSR vs. 67.9% for Checkpoints) and input-transformation defenses (68.4% vs. 63.7%), confirming robustness beyond standard settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigms in transfer-based adversarial attacks—gradient refinements, input transformations, model ensembles, and feature-level objectives—by reframing transferability as a consensus-robust (set-robust) optimization problem over an uncertainty set of plausible targets. It is conceptually closest to feature-level methods like DHF on the representation-drift axis, but introduces a principled dual-channel formulation that simultaneously models decision-boundary variation (via parameter perturbations) and representation drift (via stochastic feature blending), whereas prior methods address at most one of these factors. The Frobenius-norm-of-logits-Jacobian regularizer and the Monte Carlo blending procedure are new constructs, and the certified lower-bound certificate linking the practical surrogate to the worst-case robust objective is, to our knowledge, the first such guarantee in the transfer-attack literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CORTA's ability to outperform 4-model ensemble attacks by up to 19.1 percentage points using only a single surrogate model is practically significant: it removes the need to train or maintain multiple diverse surrogates while achieving superior cross-architecture transferability, including the notoriously difficult CNN-to-ViT direction. The theoretical lower-bound certificate provides a principled justification for the two regularization terms, moving the field beyond heuristic feature-mixing. Key limitations include the reduced surrogate generation success rate (69.9% vs. ~100% for baselines) and the per-sample second-order derivative computation that limits batch parallelization. The authors identify extension to targeted attacks as the most important open direction, since steering predictions to a specific class under model variability is substantially harder than the untargeted setting studied here.
