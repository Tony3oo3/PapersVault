## Problem

Large multimodal models (LMMs) cannot reliably identify or ground object-specific parts in images, a fundamental capability for fine-grained compositional reasoning, robotic manipulation, and explainable recognition. Existing segmenting LMMs (LISA, GLaMM, PixelLM) suffer from two architectural flaws: they introduce special [SEG] tokens unseen during pretraining (causing distribution shift that degrades general VQA performance by up to 97%), and they discard prior mask predictions during sequential decoding, losing visual context that could guide subsequent segmentations. No comprehensive benchmark existed to evaluate part-level understanding across identification, comparison, and part-whole reasoning.

## Input / Output

The model receives a 1024×1024 RGB image, a natural-language question about an object's parts (e.g., 'What visible parts does the agricultural plane have?'), and five multiple-choice answer strings. It outputs a selected answer choice (chosen by minimizing sequence-level entropy over the five options) and pixel-level segmentation masks for each part named in the chosen answer, generated by a SAM ViT-H decoder. For Part-Whole Reasoning questions, the model performs two sequential selections—first the part set, then the object label—conditioning the second on the first. Text is truncated to 512 tokens; masks are evaluated via micro/macro gIoU against ground-truth part annotations.

## Method

PLUM replaces dedicated segmentation tokens with a bidirectional BIO span extractor: a two-layer MLP followed by a bidirectional Transformer encoder block tags each output token as Beginning, Inside, or Outside a segmentable span, and contiguous B→I chains are greedily merged into text spans projected into mask queries. A Gaussian KL-divergence penalty (λ_KL=0.1) anchors span hidden states to a frozen teacher snapshot of the LLaVA backbone, preventing the segmentation head from drifting the VLM's textual representations. A mask feedback loop injects FiLM conditioning layers into the SAM mask encoder, encoding each previously predicted mask into text-enhanced feature maps that are patch-wise attention-pooled and fed back into the decoder, so every new mask is conditioned on all prior visual predictions. Training uses a Focal-Tversky loss (α=0.7, β=0.3) biased toward recall for sharper fine-grained masks, combined with per-pixel BCE and standard LM cross-entropy, in a two-stage schedule: 25 epochs on a mixed corpus (PACO, Pascal Parts, PartImageNet, COCO-Stuff, ADE20K, RefCOCO, LLaVA-150K, ReasonSeg) followed by 4 epochs on PARTONOMY training splits, with LoRA (r=8) on the LLaMA decoder and a frozen CLIP ViT-L/14 vision tower.

## Results

On PARTONOMY-Core, zero-shot PLUM-13B achieves 14.5/27.4 micro/macro gIoU on Part Identification, substantially outperforming LISA-13B (5.9/7.0), PixelLM-13B (6.8/8.4), and GLaMM (5.3/5.9). After fine-tuning on PARTONOMY training splits, PLUM-13B(ft) reaches 36.2/41.6 micro/macro gIoU on Identification and 42.1/45.9 on Intersection, matching or exceeding GLaMM(ft) and LISA-13B(ft) while using no extra segmentation data. On the ReasonSeg benchmark, PLUM-13B(ft) achieves 57.3 gIoU, surpassing LISA-13B(ft) (56.2). Critically, PLUM preserves general VQA ability (30.11 TextVQA, +31.8% over LLaVA-13B backbone), whereas LISA-13B collapses to 1.58 (−93.1%) and PixelLM-13B to 8.37 (−63.4%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the three primary segmenting LMMs, PLUM-13B (zero-shot) outperforms LISA-13B by 8.6 micro / 20.4 macro gIoU on Identification, PixelLM-13B by 7.7 / 19.0, and GLaMM by 9.2 / 21.5, without using any extra segmentation data during pretraining. After fine-tuning on PARTONOMY, PLUM-13B(ft) leads on macro-gIoU across all five question types (e.g., 41.6 vs. 38.8 for GLaMM(ft) on Identification; 45.9 vs. 42.1 on Intersection), while remaining within 0.6 micro-gIoU of the best micro scores. On the ReasonSeg benchmark, PLUM-13B(ft) (57.3 gIoU) surpasses LISA-13B(ft) (56.2) and all open-vocabulary segmentation models (X-Decoder 22.6, SEEM 25.5, Grounded SAM 2 26.0, OVSeg 28.5). The most striking comparison is on general VQA: LISA-13B loses 93.1% of its TextVQA accuracy and 97.4% of GQA accuracy after segmentation finetuning, while PLUM-13B actually improves TextVQA by 31.8% over the LLaVA-13B backbone and POPE by 8.9%, demonstrating that span-based segmentation without special [SEG] tokens preserves the LLM's pretrained reasoning. SegLLM (LLaVA-1.5 + HIPIE) remains the strongest single competitor on PARTONOMY-Core (29.6–33.8 gIoU zero-shot), but PLUM's finetuned version exceeds it by 6–12 gIoU on most tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of segmentation-enabled LMMs (LISA, GLaMM, PixelLM) by identifying and addressing two architectural deficiencies: reliance on special [SEG] tokens that induce distribution shift from pretraining, and the discarding of prior mask predictions during sequential decoding. PLUM replaces [SEG] tokens with a bidirectional BIO span extractor and introduces a FiLM-based mask feedback loop, while the PARTONOMY benchmark extends existing part segmentation datasets (PACO, PartImageNet, Pascal-Part) into a unified Explanatory Part Segmentation task that adds part comparison and part-whole reasoning on top of simple part identification. The base architecture follows the LLaVA + SAM decoder paradigm common to prior segmenting LMMs, with LoRA adapters on the LLM and a Focal-Tversky loss for mask supervision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that LISA-13B achieves only 5.9% gIoU on part grounding despite being trained on part segmentation data exposes a critical gap between training data coverage and actual part-level understanding in current LMMs, with implications for safety-critical applications like robotic manipulation and automated inspection. The distribution-shift diagnosis (LISA losing 93–97% of VQA/hallucination performance after segmentation finetuning) provides a concrete, actionable insight for the segmenting-LMM community. Open questions include whether the span-tagging + feedback-loop paradigm scales to higher-resolution imagery, whether it generalizes beyond the LLaVA family, and whether the PARTONOMY benchmark's 1K-image Core split is sufficient to capture the full diversity of real-world part understanding.
