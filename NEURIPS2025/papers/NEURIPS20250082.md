## Problem

Vision Language Models lack the explicit multi-step reasoning needed for complex vision-grounded decision-making, and existing approaches to instill such reasoning rely on large-scale, expensive paired image-text datasets that are impractical to curate for diverse real-world scenarios. The paper investigates whether foundational decision-making reasoning can be disentangled from visual perception and learned primarily through language-based representations, then transferred to visually grounded inference.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250082/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During training, the model consumes GPT-4o-generated textual situation descriptions (replacing images) paired with multiple-choice action-selection questions; during inference, it processes actual RGB images or video frames (for EgoNormia, frames sampled at 1 fps are concatenated left-to-right into a single composite image) alongside the same question format. The output is a structured response with an explicit reasoning chain enclosed in <think> tags followed by a final multiple-choice answer in <answer> tags, parsed via rule-based matching with GPT-4o fallback.

## Method

Praxis-VLM trains a Qwen2.5-VL backbone (3B/7B) using a two-stage GRPO (Group Relative Policy Optimization) procedure on text-only data, updating only the language model components while keeping the vision encoder frozen. Stage 1 performs a cold-start on geometry3k to establish the think/answer output format and foundational logical structuring; Stage 2 trains on a 10K-sample GPT-4o-synthesized decision-making corpus using an adaptive R1 reward that combines format adherence, answer accuracy, and a length bonus (capped at 250 words) to encourage thorough situational analysis. The key mechanism is that reasoning skills acquired purely from textual scenarios transfer to multimodal inference: at test time the full VLM architecture processes image-text pairs, and the textually-learned policy generates explicit reasoning before selecting an action. This avoids the SFT-then-RL paradigm by directly applying GRPO to an instruction-tuned VLM, and the adaptive reward shifts emphasis from structural compliance to decision accuracy across stages.

## Results

Praxis-VLM-7B achieves 84.03% accuracy on VIVA, 60.25% on PCA-Bench, and 54.33% on EgoNormia, outperforming the vanilla Qwen2.5-VL-7B baseline (80.97%, 46.37%, 46.19%) by +3.06, +13.88, and +8.14 points respectively. The 3B variant similarly improves over its baseline (79.03% vs 76.61% on VIVA, 50.79% vs 48.58% on PCA-Bench, 54.27% vs 51.92% on EgoNormia). Notably, SFT baselines degrade sharply on the out-of-domain EgoNormia benchmark (e.g., 34.83% for 7B SFT vs 54.33% for Praxis-VLM-7B), while Praxis-VLM maintains strong generalization. The multi-stage cold-start further boosts EgoNormia accuracy from 49.57% (one-stage) to 54.33% (two-stage) for the 7B model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Praxis-VLM is compared against the vanilla Qwen2.5-VL backbone (3B and 7B) and two SFT variants (direct answer prediction and reasoning-chain-then-answer). Praxis-VLM-7B wins on all three benchmarks: +3.06% over Qwen2.5-VL-7B on VIVA, +13.88% on PCA-Bench, and +8.14% on EgoNormia. The SFT baselines, while competitive on in-domain VIVA, suffer severe degradation on out-of-domain EgoNormia (dropping to ~34–35% for 7B), whereas Praxis-VLM-7B reaches 54.33%, a gap of roughly 20 points. The largest absolute gain is on PCA-Bench (embodied robotics/driving/games), where the text-learned reasoning transfers most effectively. The multi-stage cold-start (geometry3k) provides an additional ~4.8% boost on EgoNormia over the one-stage variant, confirming that foundational logical reasoning aids generalization to novel video-based tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on GRPO (from DeepSeekMath) as its core RL training algorithm but departs fundamentally from prior VLM reasoning work (R1-OneVision, Vision-R1, OpenVLThinker) that requires large-scale paired image-text data for RL. The key conceptual departure is the preliminary finding that textual descriptions of visual situations yield comparable or even superior decision-making performance to actual images, motivating a text-only RL training paradigm that transfers to multimodal inference. The authors also show that the commonly adopted SFT-then-RL pipeline can be circumvented by directly applying GRPO to an instruction-tuned VLM with adaptive rewards, simplifying the training recipe relative to prior approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for data efficiency: by eliminating the need for expensive paired image-text decision-making annotations, this approach opens a scalable pathway to improve VLM decision-making across diverse real-world scenarios. The strong out-of-domain generalization on EgoNormia (video, egocentric) compared to SFT baselines suggests the learned reasoning is genuinely transferable rather than overfit to training patterns. However, error analysis reveals persistent failures in situational misinterpretation, safety prioritization, and normative alignment, and the method has only been validated on Qwen2.5-VL at 3B/7B scale, leaving open questions about scaling dynamics and whether tighter visual grounding could further improve the text-to-vision transfer.
