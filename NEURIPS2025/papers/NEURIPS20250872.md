## Problem

Despite VLMs being increasingly deployed as user-facing assistants in smart homes, healthcare, and robotics, no existing benchmark systematically evaluates their ability to adapt to individual users' visual concepts and preferences. Prior personalization datasets (MyVLM, Yo'LLaVA, MC-LLaVA) are small-scale, lack preference-grounded reasoning, omit multi-turn interaction, and do not account for the cold-start setting where models must personalize with minimal prior context.

## Input / Output

Each evaluation instance follows a three-stage protocol: (1) a personalizable concept is injected via either 2 reference images or a moderate-level textual description (with 4 granularity levels available for ablation), (2) the model engages in a 0- or 10-turn general-topic dialogue sampled from LMSYS-Chat-1M to test concept retention, and (3) a query image paired with a multiple-choice text question is presented. The model outputs a single MCQ answer (yes/no for Awareness/Appropriateness, 4-option for Coherency). All concept names are anonymized as <sks> to prevent pretraining leakage, and queries are filtered to exclude those solvable by text alone or image alone.

## Method

MMPB is a 10,017-sample benchmark spanning 111 personalizable concepts (50 humans, 20 animals, 25 objects, 16 characters) organized into three task types—Awareness (detecting concept presence), Appropriateness (suppressing concept when absent), and Coherency (context-appropriate reasoning about the concept)—yielding 15 evaluation tasks when combined with recognition and preference-grounded variants. The dataset is constructed via a three-step human-model collaborative pipeline: Ovis2-34B drafts descriptions and candidate queries, human annotators refine them, and a multi-model quality evaluation stage (Ovis2-34B, InternVL2.5-78B, Qwen2.5-VL-72B) flags trivial, concept-only, or image-only solvable queries for human review. The paper evaluates 23 open- and closed-source VLMs under cold-start conditions and identifies four key failure modes: (i) inability to perform abductive reasoning required by preference-grounded tasks, (ii) safety-induced refusal behaviors in closed-source models on human-centric queries, (iii) a systematic under-personalization bias (models reject valid personalization more than they affirm invalid ones), and (iv) lost-in-the-middle degradation where mid-conversation concepts are forgotten in long-context settings.

## Results

Across 23 VLMs evaluated on MMPB (10,017 image-query pairs, 111 concepts), the human upper bound is 92.1% accuracy. Text-based concept injection consistently outperforms image-based injection, achieving 63.8% vs. 57.8% in the 0-turn setting and 57.9% vs. 52.6% in the 10-turn setting. Averaged across all settings, open-source models outperform closed-source models (59.9% vs. 51.4%), and all models suffer substantial multi-turn degradation. Preference-grounded tasks are markedly harder than recognition tasks, with closed-source models showing up to 2,237 evasive/refusal responses out of 7,501 human-category queries.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Open-source VLMs (InternVL2.5, Ovis2, LLaVA, Qwen-VL, DeepSeek-VL2, Llama-3.2) collectively outperform closed-source models (GPT-4o, Claude-Sonnet, Gemini-Flash) by ~8.5 points on average (59.9% vs. 51.4%), despite the latter's strong general VQA rankings. Closed-source models are particularly weak on human-centric tasks: GPT-4o and Claude-Sonnet produce frequent refusal responses (e.g., 'I cannot identify or name specific features of the person'), with up to 2,237 evasive answers out of 7,501 human queries, while Gemini-Flash is the only closed model without systematic refusals. InternVL2.5-78B, the top-ranked open model on general VQA benchmarks, still shows weak personalization, likely due to additional safety filters in its web-based deployment. On preference-grounded tasks specifically, all models struggle far more than on recognition, with open models at 52.8% and closed models at only 37.0% (0-turn), highlighting a gap in abductive reasoning. Personalization fine-tuning (LoRA) on LLaVA-1.5-13B improves overall accuracy from 51.5% to 67.1% at 30 epochs, confirming MMPB's sensitivity to personalization techniques beyond general VLM capability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by formalizing VLM personalization as a systematic evaluation problem, departing from prior personalization methods (MyVLM, Yo'LLaVA, MC-LLaVA, PLVM, RAP) that focused on training-time adaptation and overlooked cold-start, in-context personalization. While those works demonstrated that models *can* be personalized via fine-tuning or external modules, none provided a unified benchmark to measure whether off-the-shelf VLMs can maintain user-specific concepts through multi-turn dialogue, handle preference-grounded reasoning, or discriminate fine-grained visual identities. MMPB repurposes the standard VQA format but introduces a new evaluation axis—user-centric context injection and retention—alongside a formal four-criteria framework (Awareness, Appropriateness, Coherency, Persistency) that prior personalization work did not operationalize.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MMPB is likely to become the standard diagnostic tool for VLM personalization, given its scale (10K questions, 111 concepts, 23 models) and its coverage of both recognition and preference-grounded tasks under cold-start and multi-turn conditions. The finding that safety alignment in closed-source models actively degrades personalization (via refusal behaviors on human-centric queries) raises a concrete, unresolved tension between safety and utility that the field has not yet addressed. Key open questions include how to evaluate dynamically evolving user traits (changing appearances, shifting preferences), how to extend evaluation to application-driven tasks like personalized captioning or robot action, and whether the limited utility of visual injection (three keywords ≈ one image) reflects a fundamental architectural limitation or a prompting gap.
