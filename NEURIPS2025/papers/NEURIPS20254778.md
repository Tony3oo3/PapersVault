## Problem

Existing symmetry exploitation in deep reinforcement learning for robotics focuses almost exclusively on spatial symmetries (reflection, rotation, translation), while temporal symmetries—particularly time-reversal symmetry common in manipulation tasks like door opening/closing or peg insertion/removal—remain largely underexplored. The paper addresses how to systematically leverage time-reversal symmetry to improve sample efficiency, including the harder case where only part of the state (e.g., object position) is reversible while the full transition is not.

## Input / Output

The method operates on state-vector inputs comprising end-effector position/orientation and object positions, with an additional task embedding in multi-task settings. A key structural requirement is that the state must be decomposable into an object-related component (reversible) and an agent-related component (potentially irreversible), enabling the partial time reversal symmetry mechanism. The agent outputs end-effector actions (position/orientation commands), and the framework requires training on a pair of temporally reversible tasks simultaneously, with transitions from one task augmenting the other.

## Method

TR-DRL introduces two complementary techniques for exploiting time-reversal symmetry in off-policy DRL (SAC). For fully reversible transitions, it learns an inverse dynamics model to recover reversed actions and a forward dynamics model as a consistency filter: a reversed transition is only accepted if the forward model's prediction error falls below a threshold β, preventing infeasible augmentations from corrupting training. For partially reversible transitions (where only the object state component is reversible), it trains a potential model on object states extracted from successful trajectories of the reversed task, then applies potential-based reward shaping (γΦ(s')−Φ(s)) to guide the agent toward following reversed successful trajectories. The framework also introduces the novel formal notion of partial time reversal symmetry (PTR), relaxing the global involution assumption of prior work to handle contact-rich scenarios like pushing a door without grasping the handle.

## Results

On Robosuite (10 environments, 5 reversible task pairs), TR-SAC achieves an IQM success rate of 0.92 at 100k transitions and 1.00 at 150k, compared to 0.62 and 0.93 for Single-Task SAC. In the multi-task setting, Multi-Head TR-SAC reaches 0.54 at 50k transitions (vs. 0.23 for Multi-Head SAC) and 1.00 at 200k. On MetaWorld MT50 (12 reversible task pairs), Lang-Cond TR-SAC achieves 0.81 IQM at 100k transitions, outperforming Lang-Cond SAC (0.72) and Task-Cond SAC (0.65). The method also reduces the average number of training episodes to reach 100% success from 239 (Single-Task SAC) to 205 (Single-Task TR-SAC) on the 12 reversible pairs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TR-DRL consistently outperforms all baselines across both Robosuite and MetaWorld. Against Single-Task SAC, TR-SAC achieves a 0.30 IQM gap at 100k transitions (0.92 vs. 0.62) and reaches 1.00 success rate 50k transitions earlier. Against Task-Cond SAC, the multi-task TR variant (Task-Cond TR-SAC) improves from 0.71 to 0.93 at 100k and from 0.90 to 0.99 at 150k. Against Multi-Head SAC, Multi-Head TR-SAC shows the largest early-training gain (0.54 vs. 0.23 at 50k) and reaches 1.00 at 200k versus 0.97. On MetaWorld MT50, Lang-Cond TR-SAC (0.81 at 100k) beats Lang-Cond SAC (0.72) by 9 points, while Task-Cond TR-SAC (0.73) surpasses Task-Cond SAC (0.65) by 8 points. The ablation confirms that both trajectory reversal augmentation and reward shaping contribute independently, with their combination yielding the strongest results.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the time reversal symmetry framework of Barkley et al. (2023), which assumed global symmetry and a known action involution, by introducing the novel notion of partial time reversal (PTR) symmetry and a learned inverse dynamics model to recover reversed actions without assuming a simple negation. It combines this with potential-based reward shaping (Ng et al., 1999) applied in a new context—guiding learning via reversed successful trajectories—and integrates both techniques into SAC (Haarnoja et al., 2018) and multi-task variants (Yu et al., 2020; Hendawy et al., 2024). Unlike prior spatial-symmetry augmentation work (Lin et al., 2020; Yarats et al., 2022) or goal-state-only reversal methods (TRASS, Nair et al., 2020), TR-DRL applies temporal symmetry to every transition in a trajectory and handles the practical challenge of invalid reversed transitions through a forward-dynamics consistency filter.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper fills a clear gap in the symmetry-exploitation literature by showing that temporal symmetries, long neglected relative to spatial ones, can yield meaningful sample-efficiency gains in robotic manipulation. The dynamics-aware filtering mechanism is a practical contribution: without it, naively reversed transitions degrade performance on partially reversible tasks, a failure mode that practitioners would likely encounter. Key limitations include the absence of real-robot validation and the requirement of prior knowledge about which task pairs exhibit time reversal symmetry; the authors note that automatic identification of such pairs is an open direction. The method's orthogonality to multi-task learning frameworks (task-conditioned, multi-head, language-conditioned) makes it a composable module rather than a standalone system.
