## Problem

Image stitching in scenes with significant depth variation produces ghosting and structural misalignment because the planar homography assumption breaks down across multiple depth layers. Existing deep learning stitching methods still struggle with cross-domain generalization and maintaining structural consistency under large parallax, leaving a gap between accurate multi-depth alignment and artifact-free fusion.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254339/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of RGB images (reference and target) captured from different viewpoints, with depth maps derived on-the-fly via Depth Anything (relative, not absolute depth). Features are extracted at 1/16 and 1/8 resolutions through a shared ResNet50 backbone. The output is a single stitched wide-field-of-view image with pixel-level adaptive blending in overlapping regions; efficiency is benchmarked at 512×512 input resolution.

## Method

The pipeline has two stages. First, a depth-aware transformation estimation module uses FCA-based correspondence at coarse (1/16) and fine (1/8) scales, predicting a coarse homography via DLT followed by mesh-based RBF residual deformation, all supervised by depth-consistency losses from Depth Anything alongside alignment, mesh-shape, and mesh-angle losses. Second, a soft-seam fusion module built on a dilated-convolution UNet generates pixel-wise adaptive blending masks from the difference between aligned views, trained with terminal, cost, smoothness, and depth-regularization losses to suppress ghosting. A Reparameterization Block Adaption (RBA) strategy dynamically evaluates the contribution of 1×1 vs 3×3 branches in the regression head and prunes the 1×1 branch when its weight falls below a threshold, reducing inference cost while preserving alignment accuracy.

## Results

On the UDIS-D test set, the proposed method achieves PSNR 25.467, SSIM 0.839, SIQE 43.732, and LPIPS 0.462, outperforming the closest competitor UDIS++ (PSNR 25.426, SSIM 0.837, SIQE 43.184, LPIPS 0.469) on all four metrics. On the IVSD dataset, the method reaches PSNR 26.778, SSIM 0.820, SIQE 46.568, and LPIPS 0.436, again leading all baselines. In terms of efficiency, the proposed method processes a 512×512 image pair in 67.04 ms, faster than UDIS++ (79.73 ms), SRS (83.17 ms), and TRIS (107.98 ms), and orders of magnitude faster than traditional methods like APAP (6683 ms) and LPC (13435 ms).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all eight baselines on every metric across both UDIS-D and IVSD datasets. The closest competitor is UDIS++ [21], which the proposed method edges out by small margins (e.g., +0.041 PSNR and +0.548 SIQE on UDIS-D; +0.129 PSNR and +0.185 SIQE on IVSD), while also being ~16% faster in inference (67.04 ms vs. 79.73 ms). Against traditional feature-based methods (APAP, ELA, LPC, SPW), the gains are substantial—up to +4.86 PSNR over SPW on IVSD—and the speed advantage is dramatic (67 ms vs. 6.7–13.4 s). Among other deep learning baselines, the method surpasses TRIS by +0.99 PSNR on UDIS-D and +2.59 PSNR on IVSD, and outperforms SRS by +0.64 PSNR on UDIS-D and +2.54 PSNR on IVSD. The UDIS baseline [32] is notably weaker on UDIS-D (PSNR 21.171) but competitive on IVSD (PSNR 23.535), suggesting the proposed depth-supervised approach provides the most consistent improvement across scene complexity levels.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of deep learning-based image stitching (UDIS, UDIS++, TRIS, SRS) and single-image depth estimation (Depth Anything), repurposing monocular depth maps as a supervisory signal for transformation estimation rather than as a geometric prior for warping. It extends the two-stage coarse-to-fine alignment paradigm (global homography + mesh refinement) inherited from APAP and ELA, but replaces handcrafted energy minimization for seam selection with a learned soft-seam diffusion module built on a dilated UNet. The reparameterization strategy (RBA) adapts the RepVGG training-time/inference-time separation to the shift regression head, a direct but non-trivial transplant from object detection (YOLOv7) into the stitching pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The depth-consistency supervision offers a principled way to regularize alignment in large-parallax scenes without requiring multi-view geometry or synthetic training data, which is practically valuable given the scarcity of annotated real-world stitching pairs. However, the method is explicitly limited to two-image stitching and cannot handle loop closure or global error propagation in multi-image panoramas, a significant gap for its stated applications (panoramic photography, remote sensing). The reliance on monocular depth estimation also introduces vulnerability to dynamic objects and textureless regions, which the authors acknowledge but do not resolve. Open questions include whether the soft-seam formulation generalizes to N-image fusion and whether the depth supervision signal remains informative under severe viewpoint changes.
