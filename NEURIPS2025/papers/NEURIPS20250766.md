## Problem

Diffusion Transformers (DiTs) degrade severely when generating images at resolutions beyond their training resolution, primarily because explicit positional encodings (e.g., RoPE) must be extrapolated to unseen positions, causing out-of-distribution failures. While recent LLM work suggests that removing positional encodings (NoPE) can aid length extrapolation, it remains unclear whether DiTs can similarly benefit from NoPE for resolution extrapolation, and prior attempts (e.g., LookHere) applied to image generation produce severe object duplication.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250766/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

LEDiT is trained at a fixed resolution (256×256 on ImageNet or COCO) but generates images at arbitrary higher resolutions—up to 4× scaling (e.g., 256×256 → 512×512, or 512×512 → 1024×1024)—and arbitrary aspect ratios (e.g., 512×384, 384×512) without any multi-aspect-ratio training. For conditional generation, the input is a class label plus random noise; for text-to-image, it is a CLIP-encoded text prompt plus noise. The output is a full-resolution RGB image, with the model requiring no resolution-specific positional indices at inference time.

## Method

LEDiT removes all explicit positional encodings and instead relies on two complementary mechanisms: (1) causal attention, which the paper proves (Theorem 3.1) implicitly encodes positional ordering because the variance of the attention output at position i scales as 1/(i+1), giving tokens a learnable global position signal that generalizes to unseen lengths; and (2) a negligible-cost multi-dilation 3×3 convolution applied after patchification, which captures fine-grained local spatial relationships that causal attention alone cannot resolve at large positions. The LEDiT block alternates between standard self-attention and multi-head causal attention layers, and during training a multi-dilation strategy randomly switches the convolution's dilation rate between 1 and 2 (with probability p=0.1) to mitigate object-duplication artifacts from fixed receptive fields. The model can be trained from scratch or fine-tuned from a pretrained DiT in just 100K steps, and supports both ImageNet class-conditional generation (LEDiT-XL/2) and COCO text-to-image generation (LEMMDiT based on MMDiT).

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256-trained models extrapolated to higher resolutions, LEDiT achieves FID of 9.34 at 384×384, 17.62 at 448×448, and 33.25 at 512×512, substantially outperforming the previous best (DiT-VisionYaRN: 13.51, 28.23, 49.86 respectively). On the text-to-image task (COCO 256×256 → 512×512), LEMMDiT achieves FID 29.89 and sFID 39.98, beating the best RoPE-based baseline MMDiT-RIFLEx (FID 34.58, sFID 52.58). LEDiT also supports arbitrary aspect ratios (e.g., 512×384, 384×512) without multi-aspect-ratio training, achieving FID 20.29 and 18.82 respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LEDiT consistently outperforms all RoPE-based extrapolation methods. Against DiT-Sin/Cos PE (Peebles, ICCV 2023), LEDiT reduces FID from 213.77 to 33.25 at 512×512 — a 6.4× improvement. Against DiT-VisionYaRN (FiT, Lu, ICML 2024), the strongest RoPE variant, LEDiT achieves FID 33.25 vs. 49.86 at 512×512 and FID 9.34 vs. 13.51 at 384×384. Against DiT-RIFLEx (Zhao, ICML 2025), LEDiT wins by a large margin (FID 33.25 vs. 251.92 at 512×512), as RIFLEx's frequency-reduction strategy fails to prevent structural collapse. Against FiTv2 (Wang, arXiv 2024), LEDiT achieves FID 33.25 vs. 155.80 (YaRN) and 257.63 (NTK) at 512×512. Against LookHere (Fuller, NeurIPS 2024), LEDiT achieves lower FID (35.86 vs. 66.93 fine-tuned; 36.79 vs. 41.97 from scratch) but LookHere achieves slightly lower sFID in the from-scratch setting (71.42 vs. 76.98), indicating LookHere preserves finer local details at the cost of severe object duplication. For text-to-image, LEMMDiT outperforms vanilla MMDiT (Esser, ICML 2024) by a wide margin (FID 29.89 vs. 160.72) and beats MMDiT-RIFLEx (FID 29.89 vs. 34.58). Against DiT-RoPE (Su, Neurocomputing 2024) and DiT-Learnable PE (ViT, Dosovitskiy, ICLR 2021), LEDiT matches in-distribution quality (FID 2.38 vs. 2.33 and 2.38) but dominates on extrapolation (FID 33.25 vs. 213.77 and 208.45 at 512×512).

## Relation to Prior Work

LEDiT builds directly on the DiT architecture (Peebles & Xie, ICCV 2023) and MMDiT (Esser et al., ICML 2024), but departs fundamentally from the RoPE-based length extrapolation line (FiT, RIFLEx, VisionNTK, VisionYaRN) by eliminating explicit positional encoding entirely. It draws on the NoPE observations from LLMs (Kazemnejad et al., Haviv et al., Chi et al.) and the causal-attention design of LookHere (Fuller et al., NeurIPS 2024), but provides a theoretical proof (Theorem 3.1) that causal attention implicitly encodes positional information via output variance, and adds a multi-dilation convolution to address the local-detail gap that LookHere's approach leaves unaddressed in generation tasks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LEDiT offers a principled alternative to the dominant RoPE paradigm for resolution extrapolation in diffusion transformers, showing that 4× scaling (256→512, 512→1024) and arbitrary aspect ratios are achievable without any multi-resolution training tricks, and that fine-tuning from a pretrained DiT for only 100K steps suffices. The theoretical characterization of implicit positional encoding via causal attention variance provides a mechanistic explanation that could inform future architecture design beyond diffusion. Key limitations include evaluation only on ImageNet and COCO (not LAION-scale data), modest in-distribution FID degradation (~0.1), and the fact that 16× extrapolation (256→1024) still yields high FID (~213), leaving open whether the approach scales to the extreme resolutions needed for production systems.
