## Problem

Existing LLM scaling strategies have two inherent limitations: they cannot retain previously trained scales and always require training from scratch, and they activate a fixed parameter count regardless of the task's complexity. This paper asks whether a single architecture can natively encode multiple model scales with causal dependencies, enabling progressive scaling and elastic inference without retraining.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252672/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes tokenized text (LLaMA-2 tokenizer, vocab size 32,000, sequence length up to 4096 for training) and produces next-token predictions. Uniquely, the hidden representation at every layer is structured as a Chain-of-Representation: a concatenation of n sub-representations (chains) where scale i is obtained by activating only the first i chains. This means a single forward pass yields outputs at multiple granularities, and the embedding layer is simply sliced to the first i chains' dimensions for each scale. In CoLM-Air, all attention keys and values are computed exclusively within the first chain and broadcast to subsequent chains, so switching between scales requires no KV recomputation.

## Method

The paper introduces Chain-of-Layer (CoL), a reformulation of any neural layer where output chain i is conditioned only on input chains 1 through i, satisfying three properties: generality (n=1 recovers the standard layer), causality (scale i requires computing only the first i sub-weights), and compositionality (stacking CoL layers preserves the property). CoLM applies CoL to every Transformer component: Linear layers become block-triangular Chain-of-Linear layers, attention heads are partitioned so each chain owns a dedicated set of Q/K/V heads, FFN and RMSNorm operate chain-wise, and the embedding is sliced per scale. CoLM-Air further restricts all key and value computation to the first chain (analogous to GQA replication), enabling prefilling speedup and seamless switching between sub-model scales. Training uses a multi-chain cross-entropy loss inspired by Matryoshka Representation Learning, with a two-stage strategy: pre-train the full backbone with standard CE on the largest scale, then freeze the backbone and fine-tune per-scale classification heads. The architecture also supports Chain Expansion (adding new chains to a pretrained model while freezing existing ones) and Chain Tuning (fine-tuning only later chains to reduce cost and avoid catastrophic forgetting).

## Results

On commonsense reasoning benchmarks (7 tasks, zero-shot), CoLM with C={16,16} and D=2560 (1.11B params) achieves an average accuracy of 45.27, marginally surpassing the standard LLaMA-3.2-1B baseline (45.11) with comparable parameter count. CoLM-Air (with KV sharing) achieves 44.80 under the same configuration, a slight trade-off for enabling prefilling acceleration and seamless model switching. For prefilling, CoLM-Air delivers up to 1.6× and 3.0× speedup at 1M context length (for C={16,16} and C={8,8,8,8} respectively), reaching up to 27× speedup when combined with MInference. Chain Expansion on TinyLLaMA-v1.1 improves average accuracy from 57.43 to 58.35 (+0.92) using only ~8B additional tokens of training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the standard LLaMA-3.2-1B baseline (C={32}, 1.10B params), CoLM with C={16,16} and D=2560 (1.11B params) achieves a marginal improvement in average accuracy (45.27 vs. 45.11), while CoLM-Air under the same configuration scores 44.80, reflecting the small cost of KV sharing. For Chain Expansion, the method improves TinyLLaMA-v1.1 by +0.92 average (57.43→58.35) and LLaMA-3.2-1B by +0.14 (60.39→60.53) after only 20K steps (~8B tokens) of additional training with the first chain frozen. On GLUE fine-tuning, Chain Tuning (freezing the first chain, tuning the second) achieves 67.79 average accuracy, outperforming LoRA (66.12) by +1.67 points while fine-tuning only ~42% of parameters. In prefilling benchmarks, CoLM-Air consistently outperforms LLaMA-3.2-1B and LLaMA-3.1-8B at equivalent parameter counts, with the speedup growing with sequence length (e.g., 2,600ms→1,189ms at 64K for 1B; 1,920s→927s at 1M for 8B with C={16,16}).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Progressive Neural Networks (PNN) for continual learning and Matryoshka Representation Learning (MRL) for multi-scale representations, but unifies them into a single architectural principle: Chain-of-Representation (CoR), which decomposes hidden states into causally-ordered sub-representations (chains). Unlike elastic inference methods (Once-for-All, MatFormer) that require sampling policies or nested blocks, CoLM enforces causal dependencies at the hidden-dimension level within standard Transformer layers, making multi-scale inference a property of the architecture rather than a training strategy. The KV-sharing variant (CoLM-Air) draws on GQA's shared-head mechanism but applies it across chains rather than within a single model, enabling seamless scale switching without KV recomputation. The authors explicitly position CoM as orthogonal to both the base architecture (Transformer, Mamba, RWKV) and MoE, framing it as a 'scaling architecture' layer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The core practical contribution is that a single CoLM model can serve as multiple sub-models at different scales (elastic inference), be incrementally expanded via chain addition without retraining from scratch, and accelerate prefilling by only computing KV in the first chain (up to 3× speedup, 27× with MInference). However, all evidence is at ~1B parameter scale with only 200B tokens of training, so whether the causal-chain structure preserves quality at 7B+ scale remains unproven. Key open questions include: optimal first-chain configuration (since it determines understanding quality for all subsequent chains), Tensor Parallelism compatibility for large-scale training, and whether the width-only scaling approach can be extended to depth. The architecture's practical adoption will depend on resolving the TP infrastructure gap and demonstrating that the modest performance trade-off of KV sharing is acceptable at production scale.
