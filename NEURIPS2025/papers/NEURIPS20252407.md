## Problem

Conditional Sequence Modeling (CSM) methods like Decision Transformer treat offline RL as goal-conditioned behavior cloning, which optimistically assumes incidental performance gains in offline data are generalizable. This leads to poor performance in stochastic environments or with suboptimal data, and existing CSM architectures lack a unified design that works well in both pure offline and offline-to-online settings. The paper addresses how to deeply integrate value functions into the CSM framework to enable robust trajectory stitching and bridge the offline-to-online gap.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a K-step historical trajectory window of (RTG, state, action) tuples as input tokens, where RTG is the cumulative return from the current step to episode end. During sampling, it generates m parallel candidate actions under m different predefined RTGs, autoregressively predicts E-step future trajectories for each candidate, and selects the single optimal action via Q-value evaluation over the horizon. In the online tuning phase, RTG tokens are relabeled with actually achieved returns to correct the mismatch between predefined and realized returns.

## Method

VDT augments the standard DT training loss with two value-guided components: an advantage-weighted regression term (exp(η·(min Q̂(s,a) − V(s)))·‖π_DT(τ)−a‖²) that up-weights high-return trajectories, and a Q-value regularization term (−λ·min Q̂(s, π_DT(τ))) that penalizes the policy's own predicted actions if they have low estimated value. The Q-functions are trained via expectile regression (asymmetric L2 loss with threshold ε) and n-step Bellman targets with double Q-networks, inspired by IQL, to estimate an upper bound of the action-value within the dataset support. For online tuning, VDT introduces a trajectory-level replay buffer (FIFO, sampled proportionally to trajectory length) and RTG alignment to correct return mismatches. At inference, m parallel RTG-conditioned trajectories are evaluated simultaneously over an E-step horizon using the Q-function, and the action with the highest cumulative Q-value is selected—effectively a single-step population-based search that integrates guidance from all RTGs while maintaining constant inference latency via GPU batching.

## Results

On the D4RL Gym benchmark (9 tasks), VDT achieves an average normalized reward of 84.1, outperforming the best CSM baseline CGDT (82.4) and value-based IQL (76.5). Across all five D4RL domains, VDT leads on Adroit (57.6 vs. GDT 38.9), Kitchen (71.0 vs. DD 61.0), Maze2D (74.2 vs. COMBO 72.5), and AntMaze (76.7 vs. IQL 73.8). In the offline-to-online setting, VDT reaches an average of 94.38 after tuning, surpassing TD3+BC (79.0), CQL (81.6), and ODT (75.13). On Atari, VDT attains 127.4 average score versus DC (111.3) and CQL (87.7).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VDT outperforms all 21 baselines on average across the five D4RL domains. Among CSM methods, VDT (84.1 avg on Gym) beats CGDT (82.4), GDT (79.1), and DT (76.2); the gap is most dramatic on Adroit where VDT (57.6) nearly doubles GDT (38.9) and triples DT (32.2), and on Maze2D where VDT (74.2) surpasses MPPI (21.7) and DT (19.6) by large margins. Among value-based methods, VDT exceeds IQL (76.5 Gym avg), CQL (77.6), and BCQ (53.8) on Gym, and dominates on AntMaze (76.7 vs. IQL 73.8, CQL 70.6). In the offline-to-online setting (Table 2), VDT (94.38) leads TD3+BC (79.0), CQL (81.6), PDT (80.51), ODT (75.13), and AWAC (59.4), with the largest gains on hopper-medium-replay (96.0→119.2) and antmaze-medium-diverse (20.0→75.0). On Atari, VDT (127.4) outperforms DC (111.3), CQL (87.7), and DT (81.0), though the margin over DC is modest on Qbert (69.4 vs. 67.0). VDT slightly trails CGDT on halfcheetah-medium-replay (39.4 vs. 40.4) and IQL on halfcheetah-medium (43.9 vs. 47.4), but these are the only Gym tasks where a single baseline exceeds VDT.

## Relation to Prior Work

VDT builds directly on the Decision Transformer (DT) architecture for conditional sequence modeling in offline RL, augmenting it with value functions trained via IQL-style expectile regression and n-step Bellman updates. The training loss combines advantage-weighted regression (from Mitchell et al.) with a Q-value regularization term, a combination not previously applied within the CSM framework. For the offline-to-online transition, VDT extends the ODT pipeline by introducing a trajectory-level replay buffer and RTG alignment, while the sampling process borrows the multi-candidate evaluation idea from CEM but grounds it in learned Q-values rather than model-based rollouts. The paper positions itself as the first CSM architecture to achieve competitive performance in both pure offline and offline-to-online settings without task-specific modifications.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that a single value-guided CSM architecture can handle diverse data qualities (expert, medium, suboptimal, sparse-reward) across six environment families including visual Atari, VDT provides a practical template for deploying transformer-based policies in settings where online interaction is limited. The theoretical guarantees (policy value improvement over behavior policy, bounded suboptimality gap) add rigor that prior CSM work largely lacked. Key limitations include the additional computational cost of parallel trajectory evaluation during sampling and the reliance on a well-tuned expectile threshold ε for sparse-reward tasks. Open questions include whether the value-guided approach scales to multi-agent or hierarchical settings, and whether the n-step Bellman horizon and evaluation horizon E can be adapted online rather than fixed.
