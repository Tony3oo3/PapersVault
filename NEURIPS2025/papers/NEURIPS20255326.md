## Problem

Multi-domain task incremental learning (MTIL) requires a model to sequentially acquire knowledge from heterogeneous domains while preserving zero-shot generalization to unseen domains, yet existing methods either focus on single-domain incremental learning or rely on task-sequential replay constrained by task order and stored data. The paper identifies two unresolved challenges: how to establish effective incremental transfer between previously learned adapters and the current adapter, and how to replay prior knowledge flexibly without being bound by task-learning sequence or domain-specific feature storage.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images paired with text class-name prompts, processed through a CLIP ViT-B/16 backbone with LoRA-based adapters for both image and text encoders. During training, a Stable Diffusion V1.4 generator synthesizes replay samples conditioned on previously encountered semantic concept sets (class names from prior tasks), eliminating the need to store raw historical data. The model outputs a class label via element-wise product of image and text feature vectors, evaluated across 11 non-overlapping domain tasks totaling 1,201 classes.

## Method

LEBA introduces two complementary modules. The Continuous-Domain Bridge Adaptation (CBA) module formulates knowledge transfer between the current adapter and historical adapters as a Schrödinger Bridge problem: it models the feature distribution transition as a pair of forward/reverse SDEs derived from the Fokker-Planck equation, then trains a 4-layer MLP to approximate the score function that drives the reverse diffusion from the current adapter's distribution back to an ensemble of historical adapters' distributions. The Progressive Knowledge Ensemble (PKE) module maintains a compact adapter pool (K=2) selected via KL-divergence similarity, generates replay samples from a diffusion model conditioned on historical class names (bypassing task-order constraints), and computes learnable per-sample weights that adaptively weight each historical adapter's contribution to the bridge target distribution. The total loss combines supervised cross-entropy with the CBA bridge-matching loss and a PKE cosine-alignment loss, enabling the model to simultaneously adapt to new domains and preserve prior knowledge without storing raw samples.

## Results

On the MTIL benchmark (Order-I, 11 domains, 1,201 classes), LEBA achieves 69.2% Transfer, 77.9% Average, 87.3% Last, and 84.5% Preserve, outperforming the strongest baseline MoE-Adapters (68.9/76.7/85.0/82.4) by +0.3/+1.2/+2.3/+2.1 points respectively. Under a randomized task order (Order-II), LEBA maintains its lead with 65.1% Transfer, 75.6% Average, 86.4% Last, and 85.8% Preserve. The gains are most pronounced on the 'Last' and 'Preserve' metrics, indicating stronger knowledge retention across the full task sequence.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LEBA outperforms all five baselines across every metric on both task orders. Against the closest competitor MoE-Adapters [17], LEBA gains +0.3 on Transfer, +1.2 on Average, +2.3 on Last, and +2.1 on Preserve (Order-I), with similar margins under Order-II (+0.8/+0.9/+2.3/+2.9). ZSCL [16] trails LEBA by a wider margin (−1.1 Transfer, −2.5 Average, −3.7 Last, −4.5 Preserve on Order-I), confirming that parameter-regularization-based approaches are less effective at preserving zero-shot transfer than LEBA's distribution-alignment strategy. The older continual-learning baselines—LwF [9], iCaRL [57], and WiSE-FT [58]—lag substantially behind, with Average scores of 64.7, 65.7, and 60.7 respectively, roughly 12–17 points below LEBA, highlighting the difficulty of adapting single-domain incremental methods to the multi-domain setting. LEBA's advantage is most visible on the 'Preserve' metric (84.5 vs. 82.4 for MoE-Adapters), suggesting that the Schrödinger-bridge-based distribution alignment and the diffusion-model replay specifically reduce cumulative forgetting across the 11-task sequence.

## Relation to Prior Work

LEBA extends the multi-domain task incremental learning (MTIL) line initiated by ZSCL and MoE-Adapters, which established the CLIP+adapter paradigm for sequential domain learning with zero-shot transfer. Its key conceptual departure is importing Schrödinger Bridge (SB) theory from score-based generative modeling to formulate inter-adapter knowledge transfer as a distribution-matching problem via forward-backward SDEs, rather than relying on parameter regularization or MoE routing. The progressive knowledge ensemble component repurposes Stable Diffusion as a sample-free replay mechanism, replacing the task-ordered feature storage used by prior methods like iCaRL and LwF.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The SB-based adapter alignment offers a principled, continuous-domain formulation for cross-task knowledge transfer that could generalize beyond vision-language MTIL to other sequential adaptation settings. However, the practical gains over the strongest baseline (MoE-Adapters) are modest (+0.3 to +2.9 points across metrics), and evaluation is confined to a single 11-task benchmark, leaving open whether the SB machinery is necessary versus simpler distribution-matching losses. The reliance on Stable Diffusion for replay introduces a quality bottleneck, and the compact adapter pool (K=2) raises questions about scalability to longer task sequences or more heterogeneous domain shifts.
