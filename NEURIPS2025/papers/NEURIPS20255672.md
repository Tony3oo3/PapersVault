## Problem

Existing PU learning methods are often complicated by integrating multiple specific tricks, leaving their basic characteristics—such as which pseudo-labeling techniques, thresholding strategies, and generic tricks are actually practical—largely unclear. This paper conducts a fine-grained empirical study to disentangle these components and identify what genuinely drives performance in both disambiguation-free empirical risks and pseudo-labeling methods.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a small set of positive-labeled instances (n_p = 1,000) and a large unlabeled set from image datasets (Fashion-MNIST 28×28 grayscale, CIFAR-10 3×32×32 RGB, STL-10 3×96×96 RGB), together with the positive-class prior π. The unlabeled data is partitioned into subsets of size S (typically 256) for set-aware supervision. The output is a binary classifier g: R^d → R that assigns +1 or −1 labels to unseen instances, evaluated by classification accuracy.

## Method

The paper proposes SAPU, a risk-consistent set-aware empirical risk that treats the unlabeled data as sets with an approximate positive-label proportion π, applying cross-entropy loss between the mean prediction within each subset and π. Building on this, the GPU framework integrates SAPU as a regularization term with a pseudo-labeling objective (soft labeling with class-adaptive thresholding and mixup augmentation), preceded by a warm-up stage that trains solely on SAPU for 20 epochs to stabilize early pseudo-label quality. The paper further provides a systematic empirical comparison of six loss functions (hinge, logistic, sigmoid, squared, ramp, double-hinge), subset sizes, hard vs. soft pseudo-labeling, three thresholding strategies, and generic tricks (mixup, moving average), establishing that smooth differentiable losses pair best with the set-aware architecture while mixup consistently helps but moving average tends to degrade performance due to systematic bias in PU pseudo-labels.

## Results

The proposed GPU framework achieves an overall rank of 3.2 across six binary PU learning benchmarks, with classification accuracies of 96.4% (F-MNIST-1), 96.1% (F-MNIST-2), 88.4% (CIFAR-10-1), 87.9% (CIFAR-10-2), 82.7% (STL-10-1), and 84.9% (STL-10-2). GPU is tied with HolisticPU (rank 3.2) and trails only PUL-CPBF (rank 1.2) and LaGAM (rank 2.8) among all 16 compared methods. The supervised upper bound (PN learning) reaches 97.7% on F-MNIST and 91.9% on CIFAR-10, indicating a remaining gap of roughly 1–3% for the best PU methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPU outperforms all four disambiguation-free empirical risk baselines (uPU, nnPU, absPU, Dist-PU) on every dataset, with the largest margins on STL-10 (e.g., 82.7% vs. 77.1% for nnPU on STL-10-1) and F-MNIST (96.4% vs. 94.8% for nnPU). Against pseudo-labeling baselines, GPU beats P³Mix-C on 4 of 6 datasets (e.g., 96.4% vs. 92.8% on F-MNIST-1) but is slightly behind on CIFAR-10-1 (88.4% vs. 88.7%). GPU surpasses HolisticPU on F-MNIST and STL-10 (e.g., 84.9% vs. 84.0% on STL-10-2) but trails it on CIFAR-10 (88.4% vs. 91.0% on CIFAR-10-1). GPU wins on F-MNIST against LaGAM (96.4% vs. 94.9%) but loses on CIFAR-10 and STL-10 (e.g., 82.7% vs. 85.3% on STL-10-1). Overall, GPU is consistently behind the top method PUL-CPBF on all six datasets (e.g., 88.4% vs. 91.4% on CIFAR-10-1), with the gap attributed to PUL-CPBF's use of ensemble-based controlled probability boundary fencing rather than a general-purpose framework.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as a systematic empirical study of the two established families of PU learning—disambiguation-free empirical risks (uPU, nnPU, absPU, Dist-PU) and pseudo-labeling methods—rather than proposing a single novel architecture. Its key conceptual departure is importing the aggregate-supervision / label-proportion learning perspective (Busa-Fekete et al., 2023) into PU learning, formulating the unlabeled set as a bag with a known positive-class prior and optimizing a set-level cross-entropy (SAPU). The GPU framework then integrates this set-aware risk as a regularizer into a pseudo-labeling pipeline, drawing thresholding strategies from semi-supervised learning (FixMatch, FlexMatch) and generic tricks (mixup, moving average) that have been applied ad hoc in prior PU work but never systematically ablated.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides practitioners with the first fine-grained ablation of loss functions, pseudo-labeling techniques, thresholding strategies, and generic tricks under a unified PU learning protocol, yielding actionable guidance (e.g., soft labeling + class-adaptive thresholding + mixup as a near-universal recipe). The counterintuitive finding that moving average degrades performance in PU learning—contrary to its success in semi-supervised settings—challenges a commonly adopted practice. Open questions remain around pseudo-label quality when positive samples are extremely scarce, scalability beyond the three binary-split image benchmarks used here, and whether the set-aware formulation generalizes to multi-class or non-SCAR settings.
