## Problem

Few-shot keypoint estimation still requires non-negligible annotations per novel class, making it impractical at scale, while unsupervised methods only discover keypoints for a single class with no guarantee of semantic desirability. This paper introduces weak-shot keypoint estimation, where multiple novel classes are learned entirely from unlabeled images with the support of a small set of labeled base classes, and identifies the key question of what to transfer to bridge this gap.

## Input / Output

During training, the model consumes paired RGB images (x_i, x_r) from the same class—labeled pairs from base classes (3 images per class by default) and unlabeled pairs from novel classes (30 images per class). At inference, a single RGB image from any novel class is fed through the pretrained latent diffusion model, producing N=200 keypoint heatmaps (H×W×N) from which keypoint coordinates and visibility scores [P;V] ∈ R^{N×(2+1)} are extracted via soft argmax. The 200 prompts are shared across all classes, and keypoints are padded to a unified count K with visibility flags to handle variable keypoint numbers (8–68) across MP-100 classes.

## Method

The framework builds on a pretrained latent diffusion model, using N=200 learnable keypoint prompts as cross-attention keys to extract heatmaps from U-Net transformer layers, and aggregates multi-layer, multi-timestep features into hyperfeatures for downstream estimation. The core insight is transferring two class-agnostic comparisons: (1) keyness, a 2D vector field computed as the spatial derivative of GT keypoint Gaussian maps that indicates the direction toward the most key pixel in a local region, and (2) correspondence, bipartite pairwise cosine similarity between hyperfeatures of two paired images that encodes semantic part matching. For base classes, these are learned with supervised losses (matching-based heatmap loss, keyness L2 loss, symmetric cross-entropy correspondence loss); for novel classes, the learned keyness provides an offset guiding loss that nudges estimated keypoints toward local maxima, and the learned correspondence provides a consistency loss that regularizes heatmaps to be mutually consistent across paired images. An additional equivariance and localization loss from the unsupervised setting complements the transferred supervision, and all components are trained end-to-end with a single unified loss that switches terms based on whether the image pair belongs to base or novel classes.

## Results

On the MP-100 benchmark (100 object classes, 5 random splits), the proposed method achieves an average mAP of 62.6 and L2 error of 22.0, substantially outperforming the strongest baseline MetaPoint (mAP 32.3, L2 30.6) by over 30 mAP points. The method reaches approximately 75% of the Oracle upper bound (mAP 76.6), which is trained with full annotations on both base and novel classes. Results are consistent across all five splits, with mAP ranging from 58.9 to 70.4 and L2 from 18.4 to 26.2.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method decisively outperforms all eight baselines on both mAP and L2 across all five MP-100 splits. Against the strongest baseline MetaPoint [9] (a few-shot method requiring annotated novel-class support images), the improvement is +30.3 mAP (62.6 vs. 32.3) and −8.6 L2 (22.0 vs. 30.6), with p-values of 0.0 in significance tests. The unsupervised diffusion-based method Hedlin et al. [18] achieves only 23.8 mAP because it cannot adaptively learn transferable keypoint prompts across multiple classes. Sim.Base. [68], GroupPose [37], and He et al. [16] (all adapted from fully-supervised or single-class settings) score between 13.6 and 18.1 mAP, confirming that direct adaptation without transfer mechanisms is insufficient. In the few-shot comparison (Table 2), methods like PoseAnything [19] and SCAPE [34] reach ~42 mAP but require extra annotated novel-class images, whereas the proposed method achieves 70.4 mAP on Split1 without any novel-class annotations. In the unsupervised comparison (Table 3), Hedlin et al. [18] drops from 32.6 mAP on a single-class 'Novel Bird' split to 24.9 on multi-class MP-100 Split1, while the proposed method maintains 70.4 mAP by leveraging transferred keyness and correspondence.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: unsupervised keypoint discovery from pretrained diffusion models (Hedlin et al., 2024), the weak-shot learning paradigm of transferring class-agnostic relational knowledge from labeled base classes to unlabeled novel classes (Chen et al., 2021, 2022), and diffusion hyperfeatures for semantic correspondence (Luo et al., 2023). It departs from prior unsupervised keypoint work by extending the single-class setting to multiple novel classes, and from few-shot keypoint estimation by eliminating the need for any novel-class annotations. The key conceptual move is reframing keypoint localization as two types of pixel-level comparisons—keyness (intra-image, via 2D Gaussian derivatives) and correspondence (inter-image, via bipartite matching)—both of which are class-agnostic and thus transferable.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By defining weak-shot keypoint estimation as a distinct task, this paper opens a practical middle ground between the annotation-heavy few-shot setting and the single-class-only unsupervised setting, which is particularly relevant for category-agnostic pose estimation across many object types. The 75%-of-oracle mAP with only 3 labeled base-class images per class suggests that relational transfer (keyness + correspondence) is a strong inductive bias for keypoint discovery. Key open questions include scalability beyond MP-100's 100 classes, handling of very dense or irregular keypoint configurations (acknowledged as a limitation), and whether the diffusion-model dependency can be relaxed. The reliance on a single benchmark and adapted (rather than purpose-built) baselines leaves room for stronger competitive comparisons.
