## Problem

Despite the success of multimodal chain-of-thought (MCoT) in enhancing Large Vision-Language Models, the mechanisms driving improvements remain unexplained, and the debate between Textual-MCoT (text-only rationales) and Interleaved-MCoT (image-text interleaved rationales) is unresolved. No unified framework exists to explain why different MCoT paradigms work, when each is optimal, or what generalizable principles govern their effectiveness across tasks.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB input image and a text query (question with multiple-choice options) as input. Depending on the MCoT variant, the intermediate visual thought is produced as either a natural language caption, a JSON-formatted scene graph, an edited image (via Grounding DINO, SAM 2, or Depth Anything), or a DALL-E 3–generated image. The final output is a step-by-step reasoning path followed by a selected answer, where the visual thought is consumed as additional context in a two-stage prompting framework rather than being part of a single autoregressive generation.

## Method

The paper introduces the concept of visual thoughts: intermediate, logic-driven reasoning steps that distill instruction-relevant visual information from the input image into a cache-like representation, which subsequent reasoning steps query instead of reprocessing the full image. Four expression strategies are defined and systematically evaluated—Natural Language (N-LANG), Structured Language (S-LANG), Edited Image (E-IMG), and Generative Image (G-IMG)—each implemented via a two-stage prompt where Stage 1 generates the visual thought and Stage 2 reasons from it. Internal analysis via attention distribution and information-flow perturbation in LLaVA and Qwen2-VL reveals that visual thoughts redirect attention away from the raw image and into deeper transformer layers (beyond layer 12), where direct image attention otherwise collapses to near zero. The paper further shows that effectiveness correlates strongly with expression clarity (ρ > 0.8) and conciseness, but not with fidelity to the original image, confirming the cache rather than replica interpretation. External noise from image-editing or generation tools degrades performance, establishing a quality threshold for visual thought inputs.

## Results

Across four LVLMs (LLaVA-1.5-7B, Qwen2-VL-7B, GPT-4o-mini, GPT-4o) and nine benchmark sub-tasks, all four visual thought (VT) expressions consistently outperform the no-VT baseline. Average accuracy gains range from +4.2% (Qwen2-VL-7B, S-LANG: 56.11→60.41) to +9.8% (GPT-4o-mini, E-IMG: 48.55→58.33). The largest single-task improvement is on CoMT-Selection (GPT-4o, E-IMG: 26.50→34.00, +7.5 pts). Image-form VT (E-IMG, G-IMG) consistently surpasses text-form VT (N-LANG, S-LANG) on fine-grained and operation-heavy tasks, while text-form VT leads on coarse-grained perception (e.g., MMVP).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is between the no-visual-thought (w/o VT) baseline and four VT expressions (N-LANG, S-LANG, E-IMG, G-IMG) across four LVLMs. All four VT strategies beat w/o VT on average for every model, with gains of 4.2–9.8 percentage points. A secondary comparison shows image-form VT (E-IMG/G-IMG) outperforms text-form VT (N-LANG/S-LANG) on fine-grained and operation-heavy tasks (e.g., V*Bench-attributes, CoMT), while text-form VT leads on coarse-grained perception (e.g., MMVP, where N-LANG reaches 85.33 vs. E-IMG's 83.00 on GPT-4o). A third comparison against caption-only baselines reveals that VT provides >7% additional accuracy over simple captions on complex scenes, confirming VT functions as a distilled visual cache rather than a faithful image replica. Attention and information-flow analyses further show that VT tokens receive higher attention than the raw image in deeper transformer layers (beyond layer 12), and blocking query→VT information flow degrades accuracy more than blocking query→image flow, establishing VT as the critical intermediary for visual reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously debated MCoT paradigms—Textual-MCoT (T-MCoT, e.g., DDCoT, scene-graph prompting) and Interleaved-MCoT (I-MCoT, e.g., Visual Sketchpad, Chain of Images)—under a single mechanistic concept: 'visual thoughts,' intermediate reasoning steps that cache instruction-relevant visual information for downstream reasoning. It extends the MCoT framework of Zhang et al. [54] by providing the first systematic account of *why* both paradigms improve LVLMs, rather than treating them as competing approaches. The four expression forms (N-LANG, S-LANG, E-IMG, G-IMG) draw on existing techniques (captioning, scene graphs, Grounding DINO/SAM editing, DALL-E 3 generation) but reframe them as instances of a unified visual-thought mechanism, and the internal attention/information-flow analysis builds on saliency-based interpretability methods applied to multimodal transformers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 'visual thoughts as cache' framing offers a practical design principle for future MCoT systems: clarity and conciseness of the visual intermediate matter more than fidelity to the original image, and the choice between textual and visual expression should be matched to task granularity. Key limitations include the single-sample attention and information-flow analyses (one ScienceQA instance), evaluation on only four models, and the exclusion of multi-round visual-thought interactions. Open questions include whether the cache analogy holds for natively interleaved generation (without external tools), how visual thoughts interact with long-context or video settings, and whether the architecture-over-scale finding generalizes beyond LLaVA and Qwen families.
