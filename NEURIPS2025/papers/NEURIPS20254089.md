## Problem

While performative prediction has been extensively studied for prediction accuracy and convergence rates, statistical inference under performativity—constructing confidence intervals and conducting hypothesis tests for policy parameters—remains largely unexplored. The only prior CLT result [7] assumed all structural knowledge (including the covariance) was given a priori and provided no data-driven estimation procedure, leaving no complete end-to-end inference framework for practitioners in policy-making contexts.

## Input / Output

The method consumes labeled data {z_{t,i}} drawn from a distribution D(θ̂_t) that shifts with the current policy estimate, abundant unlabeled data {x_{t,i}^u} from the same marginal, a black-box annotating model f, and a strongly convex loss ℓ. A key unusual requirement is that, to estimate the score function ∇_θ log p(z,θ), the method additionally samples under perturbed policies {θ̂_t + ηe_i} (e.g., slightly different price strategies in local markets). Outputs include asymptotically valid confidence regions for both the intermediate iterate θ_t and the performative stable point θ_PS (via bias-aware inflation), along with plug-in variance estimates V̂_t.

## Method

The paper establishes a CLT for the repeated risk minimization (RRM) iterates under performativity, where the asymptotic variance V_t is a sum over all previous iterations weighted by products of ∇G(θ_k), capturing how estimation errors propagate through the dynamic trajectory. To make inference data-driven, the authors introduce a gradient-free score matching estimator that approximates the density p(z,θ) via a parametric or DNN model M(z,θ;ψ), using policy perturbation (finite differences over θ) to estimate the otherwise intractable score ∇_θ log p(z,θ) without requiring explicit density knowledge. Bias-aware inference then inflates the confidence region for θ_t by a ball of radius 2B(εβ/γ)^t to cover θ_PS, with the gap vanishing exponentially in t. Finally, the framework extends to prediction-powered inference under performativity, combining labeled and unlabeled data through a PPI++-style objective with a greedy sequential selection of λ_t that minimizes a user-specified scalarization of the asymptotic variance, yielding tighter confidence regions while remaining valid.

## Results

In the semi-synthetic performative linear regression experiment (d=2, ε≈0.02, γ=2, N=2000), all three PPI strategies (λ=0, λ=1, and optimized λ̂_t) achieve approximately 0.9 confidence-region coverage as labeled sample size n grows, across inference steps t∈{2,3,4}. The optimized λ̂_t (Eq. 3) consistently produces the narrowest confidence-region width compared to both λ=0 (labeled-only) and λ=1 (full unlabeled weight), confirming the theoretical variance reduction. The bias-adjusted confidence regions for θ_PS upper-bound those for θ_t, with the gap vanishing as t increases, validating Corollary 3.7. Score matching models (both Gaussian parametric and DNN-based) achieve training loss J(ψ)<0.05, with variance-estimation error ‖V̂_t − V_t‖ decreasing as n grows.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against external methods but rather evaluates three internal strategies for the PPI weight parameter λ in the performative setting: λ=0 (standard RRM using only labeled data), λ=1 (naive full weight on unlabeled data), and the data-driven optimized λ̂_t from Eq. 3. The optimized λ̂_t achieves the narrowest confidence-region width across all inference steps t∈{2,3,4} and all labeled sample sizes, while maintaining correct 0.9 coverage. The λ=0 baseline (equivalent to the non-PPI RRM in Section 3) provides valid but wider intervals, and λ=1 can be suboptimal when the annotating model f is noisy (here f introduces a −0.2 mean shift). The bias-aware confidence region for θ_PS (dashed curves) is slightly wider than that for θ_t (solid curves), with the gap shrinking exponentially as t grows, confirming the theoretical bound involving (εβ/γ)^t. The CLT is validated via density maps and multivariate Q-Q plots showing tight alignment with N(0, I_d), and the score matching estimator achieves negligible variance-estimation error for both parametric and DNN-based models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the performative prediction framework of Perdomo et al. (ICML 2020), which established the repeated risk minimization (RRM) algorithm and the performative stable point as a tractable proxy for the performative optimum. It also extends the PPI++ framework of Angelopoulos et al. (2023) to the dynamic performative setting. The key departure from prior work is that while [25] and subsequent papers focused on prediction accuracy and convergence rates, and Cutler et al. [7] established a CLT for single-sample stochastic gradient updates without data-driven covariance estimation, this paper provides the first complete end-to-end inference framework (CLT + variance estimation + confidence regions) for the batch RRM algorithm, introducing a novel gradient-free score matching method with policy perturbation to estimate the Jacobian ∇G(θ).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work fills a critical gap: in policy-making contexts where θ represents a concrete policy (tax rate, credit cutoff), knowing convergence to θ_PS is insufficient without confidence intervals and hypothesis tests. The PPI extension also addresses data scarcity in policy feedback collection (e.g., survey non-response). Open questions include obtaining direct (non-bias-aware) inference for θ_PS, extending to non-convex losses or multiple performative stable points, and validating the framework on real-world performative datasets beyond semi-synthetic simulations. The exponential vanishing of the bias gap in Corollary 3.7 suggests the bias-aware approach is practically tight for moderate t, but a direct CLT for θ_PS would be cleaner.
