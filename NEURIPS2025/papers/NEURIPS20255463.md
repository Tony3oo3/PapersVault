## Problem

Existing relational database (RDB) generation methods impose a fixed table ordering and model tables autoregressively, which prevents parallel sampling, restricts downstream tasks like missing-value imputation, and compounds errors through conditional independence assumptions. This paper introduces the first non-autoregressive generative model for RDBs that jointly models all tables without any predefined ordering.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255463/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a real RDB's schema (table definitions and primary-foreign key relationships) and learned per-edge-type indegree distributions as input, and produces a complete synthetic RDB with all tables, rows, and attributes. Generation proceeds in two stages: first a degree-preserving random graph is sampled to define the PK-FK structure, then all row attributes are jointly generated via diffusion. Dimension tables (e.g., COUNTRIES) are treated as fixed metadata rather than being generated, and categorical variables are label-encoded into continuous space so that a single Gaussian diffusion process handles both numerical and categorical features.

## Method

GRDM represents an RDB as a heterogeneous directed graph where rows are nodes and PK-FK links are edges, then factorizes the joint distribution as p(V,E)·p(X|V,E). The structure p(V,E) is sampled via a simple degree-preserving random graph algorithm, while the features p(X|V,E) are generated by a graph-conditional diffusion model whose denoising network is a heterogeneous GraphSAGE GNN. At each diffusion step, every node's denoising is conditioned on its K-hop neighborhood subgraph, and because the receptive field grows by K hops per diffusion step, the model can capture dependencies up to O(TK) hops away even with K=1. Training uses the standard DDPM noise-prediction objective (L_simple) with a randomly sampled node and its neighborhood, and sampling denoises all nodes in parallel at each timestep, enabling efficient scaling to very large databases.

## Results

GRDM achieves state-of-the-art inter-table correlation fidelity across all six databases, with the largest gains on multi-hop dependencies: on Berka (3-hop schema), it surpasses the best baseline (ClavaDDPM) by 14.14% on 3-hop and 11.30% on 2-hop Inter-Table Trends; on Instacart 05, it improves 2-hop from 16.22 (ClavaDDPM) to 92.95 (+473%). On 1-hop Inter-Table Trends, GRDM gains an average of 8.15% over the best baseline. On single-table metrics (Column Shapes, Intra-Table Trends), GRDM is competitive, matching or exceeding baselines on most databases (e.g., 98.23 vs. 90.53 on Berka Intra-Table Trends).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GRDM is compared against four baselines: SDV (Gaussian Copula, statistical), SingleTable (independent per-table DDPM), Denorm (join-then-generate DDPM), and ClavaDDPM (cluster-guided autoregressive diffusion). GRDM decisively wins on Inter-Table Trends across all six databases, with the most dramatic improvement on Instacart 05's 2-hop metric (92.95 vs. 14.40–16.22 for all baselines, a >5× gain) and Berka's 3-hop metric (92.43 vs. 80.98 for ClavaDDPM, +14.14%). On 1-hop Inter-Table Trends, GRDM leads by 8.15% on average, with the largest single gain on CCS (+17.52% over ClavaDDPM). On single-table metrics (Column Shapes, Intra-Table Trends), GRDM is competitive but occasionally trails: it loses slightly on Instacart 05 Column Shapes (97.59 vs. 99.70 for SingleTable) and MovieLens Column Shapes (97.22 vs. 99.19 for SingleTable), while winning on most other single-table metrics. SDV is excluded from databases with >5 tables, limiting its comparison to CCS and California only, where it is consistently the weakest method.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the DDPM diffusion framework (Ho et al., 2020) and the graph representation of relational databases from Relational Deep Learning (Fey et al., 2023), combining them into a novel graph-conditional diffusion model. It departs fundamentally from all prior RDB generation methods (SDV, ClavaDDPM, PrivLava, Xu et al.), which impose a fixed table ordering and factorize the joint distribution autoregressively, by instead modeling all tables simultaneously without any ordering constraint. The graph structure generation component draws on classical degree-preserving random graph construction (Molloy & Reed, 1995), while the feature generation uses heterogeneous GraphSAGE message passing within a diffusion denoising loop.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By removing the autoregressive table ordering, GRDM enables parallel sampling, supports missing-value imputation across tables, and avoids compounding errors from sequential generation—practical advantages that prior methods cannot offer. The empirical results show substantial gains in multi-hop inter-table correlation (up to 14% on 3-hop for Berka), confirming that the iterative diffusion process effectively extends the receptive field beyond the local K-hop neighborhood. Open questions include whether the simple first-order degree-preserving graph generator suffices for more complex schemas, how to incorporate differential privacy guarantees, and whether the approach extends to conditional or task-specific generation.
