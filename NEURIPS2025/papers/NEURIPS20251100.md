## Problem

Existing model watermarking schemes for diffusion models are highly vulnerable to fine-tuning: even a few hundred fine-tuning steps can completely remove the embedded watermark, undermining their practical utility in real-world deployment where downstream users routinely fine-tune models. The paper identifies the root cause via Linear Mode Connectivity (LMC) analysis—existing watermarks converge to sharp minima in the loss landscape, so small parameter perturbations during fine-tuning easily erase the watermark signal.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained text-to-image latent diffusion model (Stable Diffusion v1.4), a rare trigger prompt (e.g., "[V]"), and a 512×512 watermark image (QR code) as inputs, and produces watermarked model parameters. Detection is performed via black-box access: the model provider queries the deployed model with the trigger prompt, generates 100 images through a 20-step DPM-Solver++ denoising process, and verifies the watermark using LPIPS, SSIM, MSE, and a composite SCORE metric, or by scanning the output with a standard QR code reader. The watermark is embedded by fine-tuning the full model for ~1 GPU hour on 4 A6000 GPUs with a learning rate of 1×10⁻⁶.

## Method

RoMa decomposes watermark embedding into two complementary components: Embedding Functionality (EF), which standardly fine-tunes the model to map the trigger prompt to the watermark image, and Path-specific Smoothness (PS), which adds an auxiliary gradient computed at a linearly interpolated point along the watermark-connected path between the current watermarked weights and the original pre-trained weights. At each training step, the path-aware step size r controls how far along this LMC path the auxiliary gradient is evaluated, and a balance coefficient α (default 0.40) weights the combined gradient g = (1−α)g₁ + αg₂. The key insight is that this path-specific smoothness objective steers the watermarked model into a flatter region specifically along the direction back toward the pre-trained model, making it difficult for fine-tuning to revert the parameters and erase the watermark—unlike SAM, which optimizes adversarial smoothness in all directions and does not prevent reversion along the LMC path. Ablations confirm that removing either EF or PS degrades performance, and sensitivity analyses show stable results across a wide range of r and α values.

## Results

On MS-COCO-2017, RoMa maintains a SCORE of 0.713 after 6,000 fine-tuning steps, compared to 0.510 for WatermarkDM and 0.517 for SAM, representing a 42.5% lower LPIPS and 72.1% higher SSIM over WatermarkDM. RoMa preserves device-recognizable QR codes beyond 4,000 fine-tuning steps (3/100 detected), while WatermarkDM loses all verifiability after ~1,000 steps. For generation quality, RoMa achieves FID 16.36 and CLIP 31.83 after 3k steps on MS-COCO-2017, closely matching the unwatermarked SD 1.4 baseline (FID 15.86, CLIP 31.87). Watermark verification achieves AUC=1 with no Type I or Type II errors.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RoMa decisively outperforms WatermarkDM [72] on all robustness metrics: at 6,000 fine-tuning steps on MS-COCO-2017, RoMa achieves SCORE 0.713 vs. 0.510, LPIPS 0.261 vs. 0.454, and SSIM 0.590 vs. 0.343. Against SAM [16], RoMa also wins consistently (SCORE 0.713 vs. 0.517 at 6k steps), with the paper attributing SAM's inferiority to its focus on adversarial smoothness rather than path-specific smoothness along the watermark-connected path. For generation quality, RoMa (FID 16.36, CLIP 31.83 at 3k) is comparable to the unwatermarked SD 1.4 reference (FID 15.86, CLIP 31.87), confirming that watermark embedding does not significantly degrade general generation. In the binary-bit watermarking evaluation (Section 7), both Stable Signature [13] and AquaLora [12] show severe vulnerability to fine-tuning (bit accuracy degrades within 1,000 and 40 steps respectively), further highlighting the robustness gap that RoMa addresses. Against adaptive unlearning attacks, RoMa preserves QR structural features until ~4,925 steps versus ~150 steps for WatermarkDM, a 30× improvement in attack resistance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the trigger-based model watermarking paradigm established by WatermarkDM [72], which fine-tunes a pre-trained diffusion model to associate a rare trigger prompt with a specific watermark image. Its key conceptual departure is borrowing Linear Mode Connectivity (LMC) from the mode-connectivity literature [18, 15, 37] as a diagnostic proxy for fine-tuning dynamics, revealing that existing watermarks converge to sharp minima along the watermark-connected path. Unlike SAM [14], which imposes general adversarial smoothness, RoMa introduces path-specific smoothness — an optimization objective that explicitly smooths the loss landscape along the interpolation path between the watermarked and pre-trained models. This positions RoMa alongside AIAO [28] and SleeperMark [58] in addressing fine-tuning robustness, but with a simpler black-box-detectable design and a unified geometric explanation for why prior schemes fail.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As fine-tuning becomes the standard deployment paradigm for diffusion models, the vulnerability of existing watermarks to even a few fine-tuning steps undermines their practical value for IP protection; RoMa's 4× improvement in fine-tuning tolerance (4,000+ vs ~1,000 steps) and resistance to adaptive unlearning attacks (4,925 vs ~150 steps) makes trigger-based watermarking substantially more deployable. The LMC-based analysis provides a reusable diagnostic tool for understanding why any watermarking scheme is fragile, potentially guiding future robustness improvements beyond diffusion models. Limitations include evaluation on a single architecture (SD 1.4), a single watermark type (QR code), and full-parameter fine-tuning only; open questions include whether path-specific smoothness transfers to LoRA-based fine-tuning, larger models (SDXL, video diffusion), and whether the approach generalizes to binary-bit watermarking schemes like Stable Signature and AquaLora, which the paper shows are also highly vulnerable.
