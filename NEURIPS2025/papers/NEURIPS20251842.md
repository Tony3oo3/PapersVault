## Problem

Designing effective vaccination strategies (node removal) to control epidemic spread on contact networks requires detailed knowledge of the contact structure, which is often sensitive. While non-private heuristics based on reducing the maximum degree or spectral radius of the residual graph are well-studied, no differentially private algorithms existed for these problems under the edge-DP model. This paper introduces the first (ε,δ)-differentially private algorithms for PRIVMAXDEG and PRIVMINSR, along with matching lower bounds showing that explicit solutions must incur Ω(log n) additive error.

## Input / Output

The method takes a contact network G=(V,E) where V is public and E is private (edge-DP model), a target threshold D (for max degree) or τ (for spectral radius), and privacy parameters (ε,δ). The output comes in two forms: an implicit solution, which is a differentially private permutation π over all nodes such that each node can locally determine whether it belongs to the vaccination set, and an explicit solution, which is a concrete subset {π(1),...,π(k)} determined by an AboveThreshold stopping rule. The explicit solution may leave some nodes with degree exceeding D by O(log n/ε'), reflecting the fundamental impossibility of exact explicit covers under DP.

## Method

The core technique reduces both epidemic control problems to a novel multi-set multi-cover (MULSET) problem: each vertex v maps to a star set S_v containing v (infinite multiplicity) and its neighbors, with covering requirements r_v = max(deg(v)−D, 0) for PRIVMAXDEG, or r_v = max(Σ_{u~v} d(u) − D, 0) for the spectral-radius variant. A differentially private iterative exponential mechanism (Algorithm 5) samples sets in decreasing order of utility, producing an implicit permutation with an O((ln m/ε + ln q)·|OPT|) approximation. For PRIVMINSR, a second approach reduces the number of length-4 walks via a partial set cover formulation, achieving ρ(G) ≤ O(n^{1/4}·Δ^{1/2}). The explicit solution is constructed by applying the AboveThreshold (sparse vector) technique to the permutation, selecting the first k nodes where the noisy marginal utility drops below a noisy threshold, incurring an additional 4ε₁ privacy cost.

## Results

On BTER graphs (n=1000, target D=20, ε=4.0), implicit solutions achieve the target maximum degree of 20 and spectral radius of ~18.4, but require a vaccination budget of 430–507 nodes. Explicit solutions dramatically reduce the budget to 66–84 nodes but incur a maximum degree violation of ~92.8 and spectral radius of ~72–78. On Montgomery County subnets (n=10,000, D=45), the implicit solution cost is within a factor of ~10 of the non-private greedy baseline, consistent with the theoretical O((1+1/ε′)ln|V|) approximation guarantee. The explicit solution cost counter-intuitively increases with ε because the AboveThreshold mechanism allows more sets to be selected before halting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against other private epidemic-control methods (none exist prior to this work), but evaluates against two internal baselines. First, the non-private greedy algorithm for multi-set multi-cover (with H_Δ-approximation) serves as the cost baseline: the private implicit solution is within a factor of ~10 of this non-private optimum on Montgomery County networks. Second, the paper compares implicit versus explicit solutions: implicit solutions meet the target max degree (20) and achieve spectral radius ~18.4 but at 5–8× the vaccination budget of explicit solutions (430–507 vs. 66–84 nodes). Explicit solutions trade off a large max degree violation (~93 vs. target 20) and higher spectral radius (~72–78 vs. ~18) for a substantially lower budget. The paper notes that the explicit solution's cost increases with ε (opposite to the implicit case), highlighting that privacy parameter selection requires careful trade-off analysis depending on whether an explicit or implicit solution is needed.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the iterative exponential mechanism for private set cover from Gupta et al. [19] and the private partial set cover framework of Li et al. [30], but introduces a new multi-set multi-cover (PRIVATEMULSET) formulation that had not been previously studied under differential privacy. It also adapts the greedy walk-hitting algorithm of Saha et al. [44] for spectral radius reduction, wrapping it in a private mechanism. A key departure from prior private covering work is the need for explicit solutions (actual node sets to vaccinate) rather than the implicit/blackboard model used in most earlier results, which the authors address via the sparse vector technique.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to formalize and solve epidemic control problems (max-degree and spectral-radius minimization) under edge differential privacy, filling a gap between the rich non-private epidemic control literature and the growing body of private graph algorithms. The lower bounds showing that explicit solutions incur an Ω(log n) additive error are a meaningful negative result with practical implications for deployment. Open questions include whether tighter privacy-utility tradeoffs are possible, how these methods scale to temporal or adaptive networks, and whether the counter-intuitive behavior of explicit solutions (cost increasing with ε) can be mitigated.
