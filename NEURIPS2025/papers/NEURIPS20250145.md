## Problem

Rapidly evolving face forgery techniques outstrip existing detectors, which perform well on known manipulation types but fail on novel ones. Retraining from scratch each time a new forgery type emerges is impractical, and prior continual learning approaches for forgery detection suffer severe catastrophic forgetting. The paper frames face forgery detection as a continual learning problem, asking how a model can adapt to emerging forgery types with limited data and computation while preserving knowledge of previously learned types.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250145/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes face images or video frames (20 frames sampled per video, 100 videos per task) and produces a binary real/fake classification score. The ViT-B/16 backbone is frozen; only LoRA adapters on FFN layers are trainable, keeping trainable parameters to roughly 1/10 of compared methods. At inference, the router aggregates outputs from all LoRA experts (one Real-LoRA plus t Fake-LoRAs) to produce a joint decision, so the model grows additively with each new forgery type rather than being retrained.

## Method

DevFD employs a Developmental Mixture of Experts where LoRA modules serve as individual experts on the FFN layers of a frozen ViT backbone. A single shared Real-LoRA continuously refines common real-face knowledge, while a growing orthogonal sequence of Fake-LoRAs captures forgery-type-specific cues, with a new branch added per task. The key theoretical insight is that subspace orthogonality alone is insufficient to prevent forgetting because early training stages violate the orthogonality condition; the paper therefore integrates an orthogonal gradient constraint (estimated via SVD of the input matrix) into the orthogonal loss, forming an integrated orthogonal loss that constrains both the LoRA subspace and the gradient direction. A label-guided localized balancing strategy uses ground-truth labels to softly route real samples toward the Real-LoRA and fake samples toward the appropriate Fake-LoRA, preventing the shared expert from disrupting the orthogonal structure while encouraging collaboration among all experts at inference.

## Results

Under the dataset incremental protocol [FF++, DFDC-P, DFD, CDF2], DevFD achieves the highest average accuracy of 93.48% after DFDC-P and maintains 89.82% average accuracy after all four tasks with the lowest average forgetting rate of 4.03%. Under the manipulation types incremental protocol [Hybrid, FR, FS, EFS], DevFD achieves the highest final average AUC of 94.90% with the lowest average forgetting rate of 2.44%, surpassing the next-best method SUR-LID by 0.57% in AUC while reducing forgetting by 0.55%. In the Task10 long-sequence experiment on DF40, DevFD again achieves the highest average AUC and lowest forgetting rate among all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DevFD outperforms all ten baselines on both protocols. In the dataset incremental protocol, the closest competitor DMP [56] achieves 89.99% average accuracy with 4.08% AF, while DevFD reaches 89.82% with 4.03% AF — a near-tie on accuracy but with lower forgetting; however, DevFD leads by 2.26% on FF++ (98.41 vs. 95.96) and 1.95% on DFD (93.14 vs. 91.19). General continual learning methods (LwF, ER, SI, iCaRL, DER) suffer severe catastrophic forgetting, with AF values ranging from 14.67% to 52.34% in the dataset protocol and up to 40.29% in the manipulation protocol. Among specialized forgery detection baselines, DFIL [40] shows a 40.72% AUC drop on previous tasks during FR-type training, and HDP [55] achieves only 78.26% average AUC with 22.65% AF after EFS. The strongest competitor in the manipulation protocol, SUR-LID [7], achieves 94.33% average AUC with 2.99% AF, but DevFD surpasses it by 0.57% in AUC and 0.55% in AF, while also maintaining more stable per-task scores (e.g., 93.88% on FR vs. SUR-LID's 87.66%). Notably, DevFD achieves these results without any replay set, whereas DFIL and SUR-LID rely on replay mechanisms.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the orthogonal LoRA subspace framework (O-LoRA, InfLoRA) into face forgery detection by identifying a theoretical gap: subspace orthogonality alone is insufficient because the orthogonality condition is violated during early optimization steps, allowing gradient interference with established subspaces. It departs from prior continual forgery detection methods (DFIL, HDP, SUR-LID) that rely on replay or distillation by proposing a strictly data-isolated developmental MoE architecture where a shared Real-LoRA captures stable real-face knowledge while an orthogonal sequence of Fake-LoRAs captures incremental forgery cues. The label-guided localized balancing strategy is a novel routing mechanism that soft-assigns experts by label without hard selection, distinguishing it from standard MoE routers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical need to adapt face forgery detectors to emerging manipulation techniques without full retraining is increasingly urgent as generative models evolve rapidly. The theoretical clarification of why orthogonal subspaces still forget (early-stage gradient interference) and the proposed integrated orthogonal gradient loss offer a reusable principle for any LoRA-based continual learning setting beyond face forgery. Key limitations include linear parameter growth with task count and dependence on a strong pre-trained backbone; open questions include whether the approach scales to very long sequences (beyond 10 tasks) and whether the Real-LoRA/Fake-LoRA split generalizes to other binary detection tasks with stable-positive/evolving-negative asymmetry.
