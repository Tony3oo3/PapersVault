## Problem

Designing RL tasks that effectively unleash LLM reasoning capability remains an open problem because existing tasks (math, programming, logic puzzles) fail to simultaneously satisfy three critical criteria: scalability of training data without human annotation or expensive LLM synthesis, unambiguous rule-based verifiability of outputs, and fine-grained controllable difficulty to enable progressive curriculum learning. No prior RL task framework meets all three requirements.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250933/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a Boolean Satisfiability problem in conjunctive normal form (CNF) parameterized by (n, k, l) — n literals per clause, k total variables, and l total clauses — and produces a full satisfying Boolean assignment enclosed in a \boxed{} wrapper, preceded by a free-form reasoning trajectory. Difficulty is controlled by adjusting the (n, k, l) tuple, with a composite difficulty score D(n,k,l) = log₂(k) + 2log₂(l) − n + k/n used to order instances from easy to hard. All generated instances are guaranteed satisfiable by construction, and the verifier checks the assignment against all clauses in linear time.

## Method

SATURN is a multi-stage curriculum learning RL framework built around two alternating loops: a Curriculum Estimation Loop that generates validation SAT instances at the current difficulty, evaluates the model's pass@1, and advances to a harder (n,k,l) configuration only when performance exceeds a threshold ε; and an LLMs Training Loop that applies GRPO (Group Relative Policy Optimization) on freshly generated training instances at the current difficulty until the threshold is met. SAT instances are constructed programmatically via SAT_Construction(n,k,l,m), which samples satisfiable CNF formulas by first generating a random Boolean solution and then selecting clauses consistent with it, ensuring all instances are solvable. The reward function is a simple three-tier scheme: r=−1 for malformed output (missing \boxed{}), r=0 for well-formatted but incorrect assignments, and r=1 for correct satisfying assignments. Applied to DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B and 7B with two curriculum iterations, SATURN achieves +14.0 and +28.1 average pass@3 gains on unseen harder SAT problems and transfers to math and programming benchmarks (AIME, LiveCodeBench, etc.), outperforming prior constructing-RL-task approaches like Logic-RL by +8.8%.

## Results

On the SATURN-2.6k unseen harder test set, SATURN-1.5B and SATURN-7B achieve average pass@3 improvements of +14.0 (10.1→24.2) and +28.1 (36.1→64.2) over their respective base models. On math and programming benchmarks, SATURN-1.5B reaches an average of 48.2 (AIME 28.3, AMC 73.5, Math500 84.6, GPQA-D 37.4, LiveCodeBench 17.4), improving +4.9 over DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B. SATURN-7B achieves an average of 63.4 (AIME 48.3, AMC 85.5, Math500 95.0, GPQA-D 50.5, LiveCodeBench 37.7), improving +1.8 over its base. On the SAT-(3,5,5) seen difficulty, SATURN-1.5B raises pass@1 from 36.7 to 70.3 (+33.6) across two curriculum iterations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SATURN-1.5B (avg 48.2) outperforms all 1.5B baselines: Still-3-1.5B-Preview (46.9), the SFT-only counterpart (43.4), and s1.1-1.5B (20.1), and notably beats the 7B models z1-7B (35.4) and s1.1-7B (44.0) on AIME by +8.3 and +21.7 respectively. SATURN-7B (avg 63.4) surpasses its base DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B (61.7) on AMC (+4.8), Math500 (+1.8), GPQA-D (+1.5), and LiveCodeBench (+2.3), though it slightly drops on AIME (48.3 vs 50.0). Against prior constructing-RL-task approaches, SATURN with only 1k training examples achieves +8.8% relative improvement over Logic-RL (5k examples) on Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct-1M (36.2 vs 33.3 avg), and outperforms ScaleQuest (33.3) and SPGA (7.1). On DeepScaleR-1.5B-Preview (already RL-trained on 40k math/programming examples), SATURN still adds +2.4 average points (49.9→52.3), confirming complementarity with existing math/programming RL. The SFT baseline shows an alignment tax on LiveCodeBench (16.4→14.6), while SATURN improves it to 17.4, demonstrating broader generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the RL-for-reasoning paradigm established by DeepSeek-R1 (GRPO) and OpenAI-o1, but departs from the dominant practice of using math or programming problems as RL tasks by proposing Boolean Satisfiability (SAT) as the training domain. It directly addresses limitations of prior constructing-RL-task approaches (Logic-RL, ScaleQuest, SPGA) that rely on LLM synthesis or human annotation for task generation, offering instead a programmatically scalable, linear-time verifiable, and difficulty-controllable alternative. The difficulty estimation formula also departs from prior SAT-evaluation work (Hazra et al., Pan et al.) that anchored difficulty to the phase-transition point of heuristic solvers, arguing instead that a composite sparsity-plus-structure metric better captures LLM-specific reasoning difficulty.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SATURN provides the first RL task that simultaneously satisfies scalability, verifiability, and controllable difficulty, filling a gap identified across all prior constructing-RL-task approaches. The SATURN-2.6k benchmark with 10 graded difficulty levels offers a reusable tool for tracking how reasoning degrades with complexity, and the finding that self-verification behaviors learned on formal SAT transfer to math and code suggests a path toward domain-agnostic reasoning training. However, the paper honestly reports a plateau in cross-domain gains after two curriculum iterations, and acknowledges that SAT cannot inject domain-specific knowledge, leaving open whether this approach can sustain improvements at scale or on frontier models.
