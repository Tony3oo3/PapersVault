## Problem

Standard MLLM training with next-token prediction causes models to prioritize language comprehension over fine-grained visual perception (depth, spatial reasoning), which is critical for embodied AI and robotics. Existing remedies scale the number of visual encoders, but this demands large training data, high compute, and increases inference latency. The paper asks whether visual perception knowledge can be infused directly into the LLM's internal representations without requiring multiple encoders at inference time.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252743/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the model takes a single RGB image and a text query, producing a text answer using only one base vision encoder (e.g., CLIP-ViT-L or CLIP-ConvNeXT-XXL). During pre-training, the input sequence is augmented with special tokens ⟨g⟩, ⟨d⟩, ⟨s⟩ (8 tokens each, derived from embedding predictor queries) appended after the image tokens, and features from three frozen expert encoders—DINOv2-L (depth, 576×1024), Swin-L/OneFormer (segmentation, 576×1536), and CLIP-ViT-L/unCLIP (generation, 1×1024)—serve as distillation targets. The output is a standard text response evaluated on VQA benchmarks.

## Method

VisPer-LM distills visual perception knowledge from three expert encoders into the LLM's intermediate hidden states during pre-training via a predictive embedding objective. At selected middle layers (e.g., layers 8 and 20 for depth, 10 and 18 for segmentation, 12 and 20 for generation), a single-layer Perceiver Resampler predictor maps the LLM's token sequence to a prediction that is matched against the corresponding target encoder's features using a combined Smooth-L1 and InfoNCE contrastive loss. The total pre-training loss is the sum of the standard next-token prediction loss and weighted embedding losses (λ=0.5 per task). Special tokens ⟨t⟩, initialized from the predictor's learnable queries, are appended to the image tokens to foster an implicit visual chain-of-thought; these tokens are frozen during instruction fine-tuning to preserve their task-specific information. At inference, only the single base vision encoder is used, yielding throughput comparable to the single-encoder baseline while outperforming multi-encoder approaches on average.

## Results

VisPer-LM achieves an average score of 57.6 on CV-Bench (Llama3-8b + CLIP-ViT-L), outperforming the single-encoder LLaVA-1.5 baseline (55.1) by 2.5 points and the multi-encoder feature-concatenation baseline (56.8) by 0.8 points. The most notable gains are on perception-critical tasks: +8.7% on Depth3D (71.5 vs. 62.8 with CLIP-ConvNeXT-XXL) and +5.6% on Distance3D (54.3 vs. 48.7 with CLIP-ViT-L). On general reasoning benchmarks (MMStar, RWQA, OK-VQA), VisPer-LM matches or slightly exceeds LLaVA-1.5, confirming no degradation in general capabilities. Inference throughput is 9.86 samples/sec, nearly identical to the single-encoder baseline (9.92) and roughly 1.9× faster than the multi-encoder baseline (5.32).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the single-encoder LLaVA-1.5 baseline, VisPer-LM wins on average across all encoder–LLM combinations (e.g., 57.6 vs. 55.1 with CLIP-ViT-L + Llama3-8b; 58.9 vs. 57.4 with CLIP-ConvNeXT-XXL + Phi3-4k-mini), with the largest margins on Depth3D (+8.7%) and Distance3D (+5.6%). Against the multi-encoder baselines (feature concatenation and token concatenation of four encoders), VisPer-LM achieves a higher average (57.6 vs. 56.8/56.7) while using only a single encoder at inference, though the multi-encoder variants retain a slight edge on Depth3D (75.5/75.7 vs. 74.2). Compared to Cambrian-1 (four-encoder, trained on millions of samples), VisPer-LM trained on only LLaVA-558K + 665K outperforms it on every reported CV-Bench sub-task and on MMStar/RWQA. Against RADIO-ViT-L (which distills expert knowledge into a single student encoder before MLLM training), VisPer-LM achieves 57.6 vs. 55.1 average, confirming that distilling directly into LLM representations is more effective than encoder-level distillation. On the BLINK perception benchmark, VisPer-LM scores 58.8 average (75.5 on Spatial Relation), surpassing Cambrian-1 (56.9) and RADIO-based LLaVA-1.5 (55.8).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts IJEPA's joint-embedding predictive architecture (Assran et al., ICCV 2023) to the MLLM training pipeline, specifically distilling expert visual encoder features into intermediate LLM layers during pre-training rather than into a separate student encoder as in RADIO (Ranzinger et al., CVPR 2024). It departs from the dominant multi-encoder paradigm (Cambrian-1, VCoder) that simply concatenates or token-concatenates features from multiple vision encoders, instead using a single base encoder at inference while injecting perception knowledge through embedding losses at selected LLM layers. The base architecture is LLaVA-1.5 (Liu et al., CVPR 2024), and the probing methodology draws on prior work analyzing internal representations of sequence models (OthelloGPT) and vision foundation models, but is the first to systematically probe LLM layers in MLLMs against expert perception features.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes a practical, low-overhead recipe for improving MLLM visual perception (depth, spatial reasoning) without the inference latency cost of multi-encoder architectures, which is directly relevant to embodied AI and robotics applications. The 0.98 correlation between depth probing quality and CV-Bench accuracy provides a useful diagnostic tool for the field to monitor visual representation quality during training. Key limitations include restriction to image modality, testing only up to 8B-parameter LLMs, and modest average gains (~2.5%) that may not justify the added training complexity for all practitioners. Open questions include whether the approach scales to 70B+ LLMs, extends to video/motion, and benefits from general-purpose (rather than task-specific) teacher encoders.
