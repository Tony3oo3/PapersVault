## Problem

Generating large-scale 3D outdoor semantic scenes remains underexplored due to the absence of publicly available, well-annotated benchmarks and the difficulty of capturing complex spatial structures with existing BEV-based or multi-scale hierarchical approaches. Additionally, sketch-based 3D generation has been confined to isolated objects or simple indoor scenes, leaving the extension to outdoor urban environments wide open. Cartesian voxel scanning orders further distort local neighborhood relationships in scenes with limited spatial coherence.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252710/cropped_page7_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes two 256×256 2D conditions — a binary freehand-style sketch (generated via Canny edge detection on BEV projections of 3D ground truth) and a single-channel pseudo-labeled satellite image annotation (produced by aligning CLIP text embeddings with SAM mask embeddings from geo-referenced satellite imagery) — plus a list of semantic label keywords. The output is a 256×256×32 volumetric voxel grid where each voxel is assigned one of 19–20 semantic class labels (class 0 = empty space). The 2D conditions are projected into the 3D voxel space and jointly encoded into a latent representation that conditions the diffusion process.

## Method

CymbaDiff operates in the latent space of a VAE (4× spatial downsampling, trained with cross-entropy + Lovász-Softmax loss) and uses a Mamba-based denoiser whose core innovation is the Cylinder Mamba Block. This block fuses a Triple Mamba module (forward, backward, and random inter-slice scans in Cartesian order, preserving precise geometric distances) with a proposed Cylinder Mamba layer that reorders features by angular, radial, and vertical indices (θ, r, z), explicitly encoding cylindrical continuity and vertical hierarchy before mapping outputs back to Cartesian ordering for fusion. A Scene Structure Estimation Network (SSEN) with multi-scale DDR blocks provides a coarse structural prior to accelerate convergence, while Cross-Scale Contextual Blocks and Dilated Decomposed Convolution Blocks capture local-to-global context from the conditioning inputs. The denoiser is trained with standard noise-prediction diffusion loss (100 steps) and the full pipeline is trained on a single RTX 4090 with batch sizes of 2–4.

## Results

On the Sketch-based SemanticKITTI validation set, CymbaDiff achieves a 3D FID of 40.67 and MMD of 0.04, substantially outperforming the 3D DiT baseline (FID 138.86, MMD 0.08) and 3D Latent Diffusion (FID 165.65, MMD 0.09). On the cross-dataset Sketch-based KITTI-360 evaluation (trained only on SemanticKITTI, no fine-tuning), CymbaDiff still achieves FID 107.53 and MMD 0.08, compared to 272.83/0.11 for 3D DiT and 330.86/0.12 for 3D Latent Diffusion. For 3D semantic scene completion on the SemanticKITTI validation set, CymbaDiff reaches 43.2% IoU and 14.6% mIoU using only sketch + PSA inputs, surpassing the best stereo-based method (H2GFormer-S, 13.7% mIoU) by 0.9% mIoU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CymbaDiff decisively outperforms both 3D generative baselines (3D Latent Diffusion and 3D DiT) on both SketchSem3D subsets, reducing FID by roughly 3.4× on SemanticKITTI (40.67 vs. 138.86 for DiT) and 2.5× on KITTI-360 (107.53 vs. 272.83 for DiT). Against the prior scene-generation methods SSD and Semcity, which report only 2D FID without MMD, CymbaDiff's 3D FID of 40.67 is ~16 points lower than Semcity's 56.55, though the metrics are not directly comparable (2D vs. 3D FID). In the cross-domain comparison (Table 7), CymbaDiff's FID of 40.67 on SemanticKITTI is over 23× lower than 3D SemCity (987.91) and 3D CityDreamer (950.16), indicating that their denoisers (simple conv/linear or stacked transformer layers) are poorly suited to large-scale 3D voxel generation. For scene completion (Table 4), CymbaDiff achieves 14.6% mIoU with only sketch+PSA inputs, beating the best monocular method (OccFormer, 13.5% mIoU) by 1.1% and the best stereo method (H2GFormer-S, 13.7% mIoU) by 0.9%, despite using far simpler input modalities than RGB images. The ablation (Table 3) confirms that removing the cylinder Mamba block degrades FID from 40.67 to 74.09, validating its critical role in spatial coherence.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) latent diffusion models for 3D generation (LDM, 3D DiT), (2) state-space models for structured spatial reasoning (Mamba, Segmamba, Triple Mamba from Vivim), and (3) sketch-based 3D generation, which has so far been limited to isolated objects and indoor scenes. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of a cylindrical coordinate ordering (θ, r, z) alongside Cartesian scanning in the Mamba denoiser, motivated by the observation that adjacent voxels in a Cartesian sequence can misrepresent spatial proximity in vehicle-centric outdoor scenes. The conditioning paradigm also departs from prior outdoor scene generation work (UrbanDiff's BEV maps, P-DiscreteDif's multi-scale hierarchies) by using freehand-like sketches and pseudo-labeled satellite annotations, which are cheaper to obtain at test time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduction of SketchSem3D as the first public large-scale benchmark for sketch-conditioned outdoor 3D scene generation addresses a critical gap that has prevented fair comparison in this area. The cylinder mamba block offers a potentially reusable architectural primitive for any 3D voxel task where radial/angular structure matters (e.g., autonomous driving perception). However, several open questions remain: the 'sketches' are Canny edges derived from ground truth rather than authentic freehand drawings, the PSA annotations are pseudo-labeled and noisy, and evaluation is confined to KITTI-family datasets. Scaling to truly diverse urban environments, incorporating genuine human-drawn sketches, and validating the cylindrical ordering on non-vehicle-centric scenes are natural next steps.
