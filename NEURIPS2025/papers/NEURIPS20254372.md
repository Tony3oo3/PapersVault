## Problem

Existing object removal methods face a fundamental trade-off: strictly mask-aligned approaches only edit the masked region and leave causal visual artifacts (shadows, reflections) behind, while loosely mask-aligned approaches can remove these artifacts but lack controllability and risk over-erasing nearby objects. The paper identifies that this stems from ignoring the causal link between an object's geometric presence and its illumination effects, and proposes to exploit this relationship explicitly.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254372/cropped_page1_idx7.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 1024×1024 RGB image and a binary object mask that only needs to cover the target object (not its shadows or reflections) and produces a photorealistic RGB image where both the object and its causal visual artifacts are removed. Internally, the pipeline operates on estimated depth maps (via Depth Anything) as an intermediate geometric representation, and the rendering stage consumes a horizontally concatenated tensor of the masked RGB image with two colorized depth maps (pre- and post-editing geometry) as a single 3-channel composite input.

## Method

GeoRemover decouples object removal into two stages: (1) geometry removal, where a FLUX.1-Fill diffusion model (LoRA rank 64) edits the depth map under strictly mask-aligned supervision, augmented with a DPO-style Bradley-Terry preference loss that rewards smooth depth flow (low spatial gradient) inside the mask to suppress hallucinated structure insertion; and (2) appearance rendering, where a second diffusion model takes the masked RGB image concatenated with both the original and edited depth maps and synthesizes the final RGB output, trained bidirectionally (removal and insertion) for structural coherence. The key insight is that causal artifacts like shadows and reflections are consequences of the object's geometric presence, so removing the object from the depth domain implicitly eliminates these effects during geometry-conditioned rendering without requiring explicit artifact masks or loose mask alignment.

## Results

On RemovalBench, GeoRemover achieves FID 29.88, CMMD 0.089, LPIPS 0.124, and PSNR 25.52, outperforming the closest competitor OmniEraser (FID 39.52, CMMD 0.208, LPIPS 0.133, PSNR 21.11) by substantial margins. On RORD-Val, the method records FID 31.15, CMMD 0.182, LPIPS 0.103, and PSNR 23.70, again leading all baselines. On the CausRem benchmark for causal artifact removal, GeoRemover reaches 73.76% IoU versus OmniEraser's 68.29%. The only metric where GeoRemover trails is Aesthetic Score (AS), where OmniEraser scores 4.66 and 4.99 on RemovalBench and RORD-Val respectively, compared to GeoRemover's 4.54 and 4.69.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GeoRemover outperforms all 12 baselines on FID, CMMD, LPIPS, and PSNR across both RemovalBench and RORD-Val. The closest competitor is OmniEraser (the only open-source loosely mask-aligned method handling causal artifacts), which GeoRemover beats by 9.64 FID points (29.88 vs. 39.52) and 4.41 PSNR points (25.52 vs. 21.11) on RemovalBench, and by 12.56 FID points (31.15 vs. 43.71) and 1.57 PSNR points (23.70 vs. 22.13) on RORD-Val. Among strictly mask-aligned methods, the best performer is Attentive-Eraser (FID 55.49 on RemovalBench), still trailing GeoRemover by 25.6 FID points. On the CausRem benchmark specifically designed for shadow and reflection removal, GeoRemover achieves 73.76% IoU versus OmniEraser's 68.29%, confirming superior causal artifact handling. The sole metric where GeoRemover underperforms is Aesthetic Score, where OmniEraser leads by 0.12 (RemovalBench) and 0.30 (RORD-Val), suggesting slightly less visually appealing inpainting despite better structural fidelity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of three research threads: (1) object removal/inpainting, where it departs from both strictly mask-aligned methods (ClipAway, LDM, RePaint) that ignore causal artifacts and loosely mask-aligned methods (OmniEraser, ObjectDrop) that sacrifice controllability; (2) geometry-aware generation, where it goes beyond using depth as an auxiliary conditioning signal by fully decoupling geometry editing from appearance rendering into separate stages; and (3) diffusion preference optimization (DPO), where it adapts the Bradley-Terry preference framework with a custom depth-flow-based reward to suppress structural hallucinations in the geometry removal stage. The core conceptual departure is the causal reasoning insight: since shadows and reflections are geometric consequences, removing the object from the depth domain implicitly eliminates its visual artifacts without requiring explicit artifact masks or loose supervision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap in image editing: users who mask only the object (not its shadow/reflection) get incomplete removal from standard inpainting, while loosely mask-aligned methods risk over-erasing nearby content. The two-stage geometry-then-rendering design offers a principled, controllable solution that achieves clear SOTA on both RemovalBench and RORD-Val. Limitations include the doubled computational cost of the two-stage pipeline, failure under motion blur or transparent objects where depth contrast is weak, and reliance on a single depth estimator (Depth Anything) whose errors propagate to Stage 2. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to 3D scene editing, whether richer geometric representations (normal maps, multi-view depth) would further improve causal artifact handling, and whether the DPO-style preference signal can be extended beyond depth-flow smoothness to capture more nuanced structural priors.
