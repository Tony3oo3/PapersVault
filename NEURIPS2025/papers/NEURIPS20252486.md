## Problem

Variational Learning (VL) consistently finds flatter minima and generalizes better than standard gradient descent, yet the implicit regularization mechanism responsible remains unexplained. Existing theories (PAC-Bayes, MDL, marginal likelihood) justify why flat solutions generalize but do not characterize how VL's optimization dynamics actively drive trajectories toward flatter regions. This paper is the first to analyze the Edge of Stability (EoS) dynamics of VL, filling the gap between the known EoS result for GD and the empirical flatness advantage of variational methods.

## Input / Output

The method operates on a parameterized model with a twice-differentiable loss, a fixed learning rate ρ, an isotropic posterior variance σ², and a Monte-Carlo sample count N_s. Unlike GD's constant stability threshold of 2/ρ, the VGD stability threshold is dynamic: it depends on the Variational Factor VF(N_s/σ² · c_{i,t}), where c_{i,t} = (λ_i m_t^T v_i)² is a loss-dependent constant evaluated at the current iterate, causing the threshold to fluctuate across training steps. The output is the updated posterior mean m_{t+1} along with a characterization of sharpness dynamics that track the predicted VF.

## Method

The paper derives an exact stability threshold for Variational GD on a quadratic loss by analyzing the expected loss change under the stochastic update m_{t+1} = m_t − ρ(1/N_s)Σ∇ℓ(m_t + ε_i). The key result (Theorem 3.1) shows the threshold is (2/ρ)·VF(z) where VF(z) = ρ√(z/3)·sinh((1/3)arcsinh((3/ρ)√(3/z))) is strictly less than 1, making the VGD threshold always smaller than GD's 2/ρ. Two interacting mechanisms lower the threshold: a perturbation effect from the posterior covariance (larger σ² amplifies gradient noise along sharp directions) and a smoothing effect from finite Monte-Carlo averaging (smaller N_s increases estimator variance). The quadratic result is extended to deep networks via a local quadratic approximation, and empirically validated on MLPs, ResNet-20, and ViTs trained on CIFAR-10, SVHN, and FashionMNIST, where normalized sharpness closely tracks the predicted VF. Additional analyses cover heavy-tailed Student-t perturbations (which further reduce sharpness) and the Adaptive Edge of Stability for IVON, showing preconditioned sharpness stays below 2/ρ.

## Results

The paper's central theoretical result (Theorem 3.1) shows that Variational GD (VGD) has a stability threshold strictly below GD's 2/ρ, governed by the Variational Factor VF(N_s/σ² · c_{i,t}), which is always less than 1. Empirically, VGD with isotropic Gaussian noise consistently achieves lower Hessian sharpness and higher test accuracy than full-batch GD across MLP, ResNet-20, and ViT on CIFAR-10 (Figure 4). The normalized sharpness ‖∇²ℓ(m_t)‖₂/(2/ρ) closely tracks the predicted VF across learning rates ρ ∈ {0.01, 0.02, 0.05} and posterior variances (Figure 5). Increasing posterior variance σ² or decreasing Monte-Carlo samples N_s both lower the stability threshold and final sharpness (Figure 6), while heavy-tailed Student-t perturbations with smaller degrees of freedom α yield even lower sharpness and better test accuracy (Figure 7).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is full-batch Gradient Descent (GD) with constant learning rate ρ, whose EoS threshold is 2/ρ. VGD wins on both sharpness and test accuracy across all three architectures (MLP, ResNet-20, ViT) on CIFAR-10: the normalized sharpness of VGD hovers around the VF (which is < 1), meaning VGD operates at a strictly lower sharpness level than GD's 2/ρ threshold, and this translates to consistently higher test accuracy (Figure 4). For the adaptive EoS comparison, IVON (a large-scale VON implementation) achieves lower preconditioned sharpness than Adam across temperatures τ, with smaller τ (wider posterior) yielding even lower preconditioned sharpness (Figure 8). The paper also shows that VGD's advantage over GD is controllable: larger posterior variance and fewer Monte-Carlo samples both widen the gap in sharpness reduction, while in the limit N_s → ∞, VGD recovers GD's dynamics. On the NLP task (SST-2 with frozen BERT-mini), VGD again achieves lower sharpness than GD throughout training (Figure 21).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Edge of Stability (EoS) framework, originally developed for gradient descent (Cohen et al., 2021) and later adapted to adaptive optimizers (Cohen et al., 2022) and SAM (Long & Bartlett, 2024), to Variational Learning (VL). The VGD update rule is drawn from the Bayesian learning rule of Khan and Rue (2023), and the adaptive EoS analysis builds on the IVON implementation of Shen et al. (2024). Conceptually, the paper departs from PAC-Bayes and minimum-description-length explanations of VL's generalization advantage by instead characterizing the *optimization dynamics*—specifically, how posterior variance and Monte-Carlo sample count lower the stability threshold below the GD value of 2/ρ through a closed-form Variational Factor (VF).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to provide a mechanistic, dynamics-based explanation for why VL finds flatter minima, complementing (rather than replacing) PAC-Bayes and MDL accounts. The practical takeaway—that practitioners can steer sharpness by tuning σ² and N_s—gives concrete knobs for VL training that were previously only intuited. Open questions include deriving the exact stability threshold for IVON (which requires joint analysis of coupled mean-covariance dynamics), extending the theory to heavy-tailed posteriors, and validating the results at the scale of ImageNet or LLM pretraining where VL has shown the largest empirical gains.
