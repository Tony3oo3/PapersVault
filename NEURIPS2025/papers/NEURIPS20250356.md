## Problem

ML models deployed in high-stakes settings (lending, hiring, social media) can cause unintended real-world repercussions, but existing methods for managing these effects require analytical models describing how predictions influence downstream outcomes—models that are often infeasible to construct. THEIA addresses the open problem of providing high-confidence guarantees on a classifier's repercussions using only empirical data from a previously deployed model, without any analytical relationship between predictions and their effects.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250356/cropped_page6_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

THEIA consumes a dataset of n i.i.d. tuples (X_i, Y_i, Ŷ_i^β, R_i^β) where the predictions Ŷ^β and observed repercussions R^β were generated by a known stochastic behavior model β, along with user-specified confidence level δ, tolerance thresholds τ, and Boolean conditionals c(X,Y) defining the constraint scope. It outputs either a new stochastic classifier π_θ whose repercussions are guaranteed to satisfy all constraints with probability ≥ 1−δ, or a 'No Solution Found' (NSF) signal when data is insufficient to certify any model—proactively refusing to return an untrusted solution rather than silently violating constraints.

## Method

THEIA's core mechanism is an importance-sampling estimator R̂^{π_θ} = [π_θ(X, Ŷ^β)/β(X, Ŷ^β)]·R^β that reweights observed repercussions from the behavior model to produce unbiased estimates of what a candidate model's repercussions would be, without ever deploying the candidate. The algorithm splits data into a candidate set D_c and a final set D_f: on D_c, a cost function combines sample loss with a high-confidence upper bound U⁺ on the repercussion objective (via Hoeffding's inequality or Student's t-test) to select a candidate θ_c; on D_f, an independent upper bound U is computed to verify θ_c passes all constraints before returning it. This two-stage design—optimizing under pessimistic bounds during selection, then validating on held-out data—yields the Seldonian guarantee Pr(g(a(D))≤0)≥1−δ and a consistency guarantee that THEIA finds a solution with probability →1 as n→∞ when one exists.

## Results

THEIA achieves a 0% failure rate on both repercussion constraints across all experiments, while all competing methods (Fairlearn, Fairness Constraints, QSA, and logistic regression) consistently fail to satisfy both constraints simultaneously. In EXP-1 (foster care employment prediction), THEIA reaches ~90% accuracy under low-data regimes and achieves parity with unconstrained baselines as training data increases, while maintaining its constraint guarantees. THEIA begins returning valid solutions with only 3.1% of available data and achieves 90% probability of returning a solution with just 12.5% of data. Under varying prediction-repercussion dependency (α from 0 to 1), THEIA never violates constraints, though accuracy drops from 95% to 90% as α approaches zero.

## Baselines & Comparisons

THEIA is the only method that satisfies both repercussion constraints with high probability (0% observed failure rate across 500 trials), while Fairlearn [2], Fairness Constraints [46], QSA [42], and logistic regression all consistently fail to satisfy both constraints simultaneously. On accuracy, the unconstrained baselines (Fairlearn, Fairness Constraints, LR) achieve slightly higher accuracy (~90%+) in low-data regimes, but THEIA closes this gap as training data increases while maintaining its constraint guarantees. QSA [42] returns candidate models with less training data than THEIA, but those models also fail to satisfy both constraints simultaneously. The key trade-off is that THEIA sacrifices a small amount of accuracy (bounded by a 75% minimum constraint) in exchange for provable high-confidence guarantees on repercussions—something no baseline provides. In EXP-2 (lending with distribution shift), THEIA maintains its constraint guarantees even when the relationship between predictions and repercussions changes between the behavior model's deployment and THEIA's search, a robustness property not tested or guaranteed by the baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

THEIA extends the Seldonian algorithm framework (Thomas et al., Science 2019) from contextual bandits to supervised classification, and adapts the proof strategy of Metevier et al. (NeurIPS 2019) to this setting. Its core technical mechanism borrows importance sampling from off-policy evaluation in reinforcement learning (Precup et al., ICML 2001) to estimate the repercussions of a new model using only data logged under a previously deployed classifier. The key departure from prior work on managing ML side effects (Liu et al. 2018, Zhu et al. 2021, Carroll et al. 2022) is that those methods all require an analytical model of how predictions causally influence downstream outcomes, whereas THEIA operates in a fully model-free setting using only empirically observed repercussions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method to provide provable high-confidence guarantees on a classifier's real-world repercussions without requiring an analytical model of the prediction-to-repercussion relationship—a relationship that is often infeasible to characterize due to complex social and economic factors. The consistency guarantee (Theorem 3) ensures that as data accumulates, THEIA will find a solution if one exists, and its proactive 'No Solution Found' behavior prevents deployment of untrusted models. Limitations include the requirement for a stochastic behavior model, the assumption that repercussions depend only on the prediction (not the model that produced it), and sensitivity to distribution shift between data collection and deployment. The authors identify extending THEIA to settings where the prediction-repercussion relationship evolves over time as a key open direction.
