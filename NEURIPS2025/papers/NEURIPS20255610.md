## Problem

CLIP and similar multimodal contrastive learning models are highly vulnerable to backdoor attacks, yet existing defenses either require fine-tuning the entire model or access to poisoned pre-training data, making them resource-intensive or impractical. The paper identifies the root cause as CLIP's low Perturbation Resistivity (PR): its visual encoder captures many off-dataset, non-predictive features that make encoded representations easily distorted by small structured perturbations such as backdoor triggers. The gap this paper fills is an efficient, data-light post-training defense that works with only few-shot clean downstream samples and a tiny fraction of tunable parameters.

## Input / Output

The defender provides a frozen backdoored CLIP model (ViT visual encoder) and a small set of clean labeled downstream samples (e.g., 16 images per class on ImageNet, Caltech101, or OxfordPets). RVPT outputs a defended model whose only tunable parameters are visual prompts (0.27% of total CLIP parameters); at inference, the defended model takes an RGB image and a text prompt (e.g., 'a photo of a <CLS>.') and produces a class label, a generated caption (for image captioning), or a ranked image list (for text-to-image retrieval). Notably, the defender never needs access to the original pre-training data or any poisoned samples.

## Method

RVPT injects learnable visual prompts of length b into the feature stream of the frozen ViT encoder starting from a specified depth D, concatenating them with class and patch embeddings at each subsequent layer. Training jointly optimizes two objectives: (1) a multi-layer feature-repelling (FR) loss that minimizes the mean cosine similarity between the prompted class embeddings and the corresponding original (unprompted) CLIP features across all prompted layers, and (2) a standard cross-entropy (CE) loss on the downstream classification task. The FR loss acts as an adversarial pressure that forces the prompts to discard non-predictive features (noise, trigger patterns) and retain only those that contribute to CE optimization, effectively raising the model's Perturbation Resistivity. The overall loss is L = L_CE + α·L_FR with a balancing factor α (default 2). This 'natural selection' mechanism—first scramble features, then pick the predictive ones—contrasts with adversarial training's 'pick bad features then unlearn' strategy, and requires tuning only ~0.34M parameters versus 126M for full fine-tuning.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K, RVPT reduces the Attack Success Rate (ASR) to near-zero across five of six attacks (0.01%–0.11%) and to 2.76% against the most advanced multimodal attack BadCLIP, while maintaining Clean Accuracy (CA) of 61.81% (vs. 61.33% for the undefended model). The method tunes only 0.27% of CLIP's parameters (0.34M out of 126M) using just 16 clean samples per class. On Caltech101 and OxfordPets, RVPT similarly drives ASR below 1.6% for all evaluated attacks while preserving or improving CA over the undefended model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RVPT decisively outperforms CleanCLIP [3] on every attack: against BadCLIP, RVPT achieves 2.76% ASR versus CleanCLIP's 89.70%, and against BadNet, 0.05% versus 23.79%. RVPT also preserves substantially higher clean accuracy (e.g., 61.81% vs. 57.55% on BadCLIP), whereas CleanCLIP degrades CA by ~4 points due to full-model fine-tuning on 250K CC3M pairs. Against Linear Probe [5], RVPT wins on all comparable attacks (e.g., 2.76% vs. 99.70% on BadCLIP; 0.05% vs. 3.05% on BadNet) and is the only method that can defend against TrojVQA, which targets the multimodal VQA task and is incompatible with a vision-only linear probe. In the downstream image-captioning and retrieval evaluation (Table 7), RVPT reduces ASR to 0% on both tasks, while plain VPT [26] still yields 81.21% ASR on captioning and 22.67% on retrieval, confirming that the feature-repelling loss is essential beyond standard visual prompt tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Visual Prompt Tuning (VPT) [26] by introducing a feature-repelling (FR) loss that adversarially pushes prompted features away from the original CLIP representations at deeper layers, while a cross-entropy loss preserves task accuracy. It departs from existing multimodal backdoor defenses (CleanCLIP, full fine-tuning, pruning) that either require access to poisoned pre-training data or tune the entire model, instead operating in a few-shot, parameter-efficient regime with only 0.27% of CLIP's parameters. The introduction of the Perturbation Resistivity (PR) metric provides a new diagnostic lens, reframing CLIP's backdoor vulnerability as a consequence of its encoder capturing non-predictive, off-dataset features that amplify sensitivity to structured perturbations like triggers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RVPT offers a practically deployable defense: it requires only 16 clean samples per class, tunes a tiny fraction of parameters, and generalizes across datasets (ImageNet → Caltech101, OxfordPets) and domains (ImageNet-V2/A/R/S), making it suitable for real-world settings where pre-training data is inaccessible. The PR metric gives practitioners a layer-wise diagnostic to quantify and monitor a model's susceptibility to input perturbations beyond just backdoor triggers. Open questions include whether the approach extends to text-side or dual-modality triggers (TrojVQA results are limited), whether it scales to larger VLMs (e.g., LLaVA-13B) without architectural changes, and how it performs against adaptive attacks designed specifically to evade feature-repelling defenses.
