## Problem

While prior work (REPA, RCG) empirically demonstrated that aligning diffusion model internals with pretrained representations improves generation quality, there was no systematic theoretical framework explaining when and how representations should be injected into the denoising process. This gap limited the design space for representation-enhanced diffusion training and prevented principled extensions beyond single-modality, single-latent settings.

## Input / Output

For image generation, the model takes a class label and produces a 256×256 image in Stable Diffusion VAE latent space, with auxiliary text captions synthesized by Qwen2-VL 2B and joint image-text embeddings extracted from Qwen2-VL 7B's 15th layer. For protein inverse folding, a 3D backbone structure (≤256 residues) is the conditioning input, and the output is an amino acid sequence; auxiliary 3D structures are generated by AlphaFold3 to provide structure, single-token, and pair embeddings. For molecule generation, the model is unconditional and outputs 3D atom coordinates with bond connectivity, using 2D graph features extracted via RDKit as an auxiliary modality alongside UniMol graph-level invariant representations.

## Method

REED derives a variational bound on the negative log-likelihood of a diffusion model augmented with an auxiliary latent z, decomposing the joint distribution p(x_{0:T}, z) along a timestep-weighted schedule {α_t} that produces a hybrid reverse step interpolating between representation-conditioned and unconditional denoising. From this framework, two strategies emerge: (1) a multi-latent alignment loss that simultaneously matches different diffusion transformer layers to representations from multiple pretrained encoders (e.g., DINOv2 patch features at shallow layers and Qwen2-VL pooled embeddings at deeper layers), using synthetic cross-modal data to avoid shortcut learning; and (2) a phase-in curriculum where the representation alignment loss is active from the start while the diffusion loss coefficient α(n) ramps linearly from zero over the first 50K iterations, ensuring the model learns to extract meaningful representations before fully committing to denoising. The framework unifies REPA (linear schedule, single latent) and RCG (constant schedule, decoupled stages) as special cases, and extends naturally to discrete diffusion (protein sequences) and equivariant flow matching (3D molecules) by aligning with domain-appropriate pretrained encoders.

## Results

On class-conditional ImageNet 256×256 (no CFG), REED achieves FID=8.2 on SiT-XL/2 at 300K iterations, surpassing vanilla SiT-XL/2 at 7M iterations (FID=8.3) for a 23.3× training speedup, and reaches FID=4.7 at 1M iterations versus REPA's FID=5.9 at 4M. With classifier-free guidance, REED matches REPA's 800-epoch FID of 1.80 at only 200 epochs (4× speedup). For protein inverse folding, REED at 50 epochs achieves 41.06% sequence recovery and pLDDT 82.74, exceeding DiscreteDiff at the same budget and approaching ProteinMPNN's 200-epoch performance. On Geom-Drug molecule generation, SemlaFlow+REED improves molecule stability to 98.1% and reduces strain energy to 65.33 kcal/mol over the SemlaFlow baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

REED consistently outperforms both vanilla SiT and REPA at every model scale (B/2, L/2, XL/2) and training iteration on ImageNet 256×256 without CFG, with the largest gap at the SiT-B/2 scale (FID 19.5 vs. 24.4 for REPA and 33.0 for vanilla SiT at 400K iterations). Against REPA specifically, REED achieves a 4× training speedup with CFG, matching REPA's 800-epoch FID of 1.80 at just 200 epochs, and at 1M iterations without CFG reaches FID 4.7 versus REPA's 5.9 at 4M iterations. For protein inverse folding, REED at 50 epochs (41.06% seq. recovery, pLDDT 82.74) surpasses DiscreteDiff at the same budget (38.29%, 81.52) and approaches ProteinMPNN's 200-epoch results (41.73%, 82.68), while at 150 epochs REED exceeds ProteinMPNN on pLDDT (83.17 vs. 82.68) and %pLDDT>80 (69.07% vs. 65.66%). On Geom-Drug molecule generation, SemlaFlow+REED improves over the SemlaFlow baseline on every metric (molecule stability 98.1% vs. 97.4%, energy 105.63 vs. 114.44, strain 65.33 vs. 74.18) and outperforms EQGAT-diff on energy and strain while using only 100 NFE versus 500. The ablation (Table 3) confirms both the multimodal representation and the curriculum schedule contribute: removing either degrades FID from 24.6 to 27.7 and 29.4 respectively on SiT-B/2 at 200K iterations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper provides the first systematic theoretical framework for representation-enhanced diffusion training, deriving a variational bound with a decomposed probabilistic structure that recovers both REPA (linear weight schedule, single latent) and RCG (constant weight schedule, two-stage) as special cases. Building on the DDPM variational framework and the SiT/flow-matching architecture, it extends the single-latent REPA setting to multi-latent representations from diverse modalities (e.g., DINOv2 image features + Qwen2-VL text-image embeddings) and introduces a curriculum that phase-ins the diffusion loss while keeping the representation alignment loss active from the start. The practical instantiations draw on AlphaFold3 for protein structure representations and UniMol for molecular graph representations, applying representation alignment to discrete diffusion and equivariant flow matching for the first time in those domains.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 4× training speedup over REPA (matching its 800-epoch FID at 200 epochs) and 23.3× speedup over vanilla SiT-XL make representation-guided diffusion training substantially more accessible, particularly for compute-constrained settings and iterative research cycles. The theoretical unification gives practitioners a principled design space (weight schedule, latent hierarchy, alignment depth) rather than ad-hoc heuristics, and the finding that representation learning is most critical early in training (and can be decayed later) offers a simple, broadly applicable training protocol. Open questions include whether adaptive (non-linear) weight schedules can further improve performance, how the framework extends to video or audio generation, and whether the synthetic-pairing strategy generalizes to domains where no suitable cross-modal generator exists.
