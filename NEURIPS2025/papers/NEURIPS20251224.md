## Problem

Private all-pairs shortest distance (APSD) computation achieves polylogarithmic error on trees and paths via the binary tree mechanism, but degrades to Õ(√n/ε) on general graphs. A significant gap exists for intermediate graph classes—particularly planar graphs and K_h-minor-free graphs—that arise naturally in applications like road networks and metro maps, where the topology is publicly known but edge weights (e.g., traffic) are sensitive. The paper asks whether the structural properties of such graphs can be exploited to substantially improve the privacy-utility tradeoff.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes a publicly known weighted undirected graph G = ([n], E, w) whose topology is public but whose edge weights are the sensitive data, along with privacy parameters (ε, δ). Two graphs are neighbors under weight-level privacy if they share the same edge set and differ in exactly one edge weight by at most 1. The output is a set of estimated all-pair shortest distances {d̂_G(s,t)}_{s,t∈[n]} with worst-case additive error bounded by Õ(h·(nW)^{1/3}/ε^{2/3}) for K_h-minor-free graphs (where W is the maximum edge weight), or Õ(n^{1/4}√W/ε^{1/2}) for grid graphs. Internally, the algorithm constructs a labeled binary tree of subgraph decompositions and a synthetic graph with noisy shortcut edges that serve as the private intermediate representation.

## Method

The core contribution is identifying recursive separability as the structural property that generalizes the binary tree mechanism beyond paths and trees. The algorithm recursively decomposes the graph using balanced separators (of size at most p) to build a binary tree of O(log n) levels, then adds two types of noisy shortcuts: complete graphs within each separator and complete bipartite graphs between adjacent-level separators. A key pruning trick—routing distances through the parent separator rather than accumulating errors across all leaves at the same level—prevents error from compounding within a recursion level. For K_h-minor-free graphs, the algorithm further reduces compositions by finding k-covering sets within each separator (using a contraction argument with the Alon-Seymour-Thomas separator theorem) and only adding shortcuts among covering-set vertices, yielding the Õ(h·(nW)^{1/3}/ε^{2/3}) bound. For grid graphs, the connectedness of separators enables n^{1/4}-sized covering sets, tightening the error to Õ(n^{1/4}√W/ε^{1/2}).

## Results

For K_h-minor-free graphs (which includes planar graphs as K_5-minor-free), the paper achieves an additive error of Õ_δ(h·(nW)^{1/3}/ε^{2/3}) for private APSD, where W is the maximum edge weight. For grid graphs specifically, the error is further reduced to Õ_δ(n^{1/4}√W/ε^{1/2}). The general framework for (p,q,q')-recursively separable graphs yields an error of O(p·log²(n/δ)/ε), recovering the polylog(n) bound for trees and improving bounded tree-width results from Õ(p²/ε) to Õ(p/ε).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper improves upon both prior baselines on both planar and grid graphs. Compared to Sealfon [42], which achieves Õ_δ(√(nW/ε)) for planar graphs, the new method reduces the error to Õ_δ((nW)^{1/3}/ε^{2/3}), a significant improvement in the dependence on both n and W. For grid graphs, Sealfon's bound of Õ_δ(n^{1/3}/ε + W·n^{1/3}) is improved to Õ_δ(n^{1/4}√W/ε^{1/2}), which is strictly better for large n and moderate W. Compared to Chen et al. [12], whose planar graph bound involves the complex expression Õ_δ(W^α·n^{α+o(1)}/ε^{1−α} + n^{α+o(1)}/ε) with α≈0.414, the new Õ_δ((nW)^{1/3}/ε^{2/3}) bound is cleaner and tighter. The paper also improves the bounded tree-width result from Ebrahimi et al. [23], reducing the error from Õ(p²/ε) to Õ(p/ε) for graphs of tree-width p.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalizes the binary tree mechanism for private range queries (equivalently, APSD on paths) and Sealfon's extension to trees, by identifying recursive separability as the unifying structural property that enables the divide-and-conquer privacy analysis. It departs from the sub-sampling-based approaches of Chen et al. [12] and Fan et al. [26], which achieve O~(√n/ε) on general graphs but are oblivious to graph topology, by instead leveraging separator theorems (Lipton-Tarjan for planar graphs, Alon-Seymour-Thomas for K_h-minor-free graphs) to construct topology-aware shortcuts. The covering-set trick within separators builds on Sealfon's grid graph analysis but is embedded in a recursive framework that composes across separator levels.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper closes a natural gap between the polylog(n) error achievable on trees and the O~(√n/ε) error on general graphs, providing the first sub-√n bounds for planar and K_h-minor-free graphs under weight-level privacy. The recursive separability abstraction opens a pathway to import the rich literature on graph separator theorems (genus-bounded, string graphs, induced-minor-free) into private graph algorithm design. Key limitations include the dependence on a bounded maximum edge weight W, the additive (not multiplicative) error guarantee, and the fact that the lower bound for general graphs (Ω(n^{1/4}/ε) by Bodwin et al.) remains far from the upper bound, leaving the optimal rate for general graphs open.
