## Problem

Existing RL policy exploration methods (ε-greedy, Gaussian noise, OU processes) rely on preset stochastic processes applied uniformly across all tasks, ignoring environment-specific characteristics that influence exploration. Moreover, their evolution during training is rigid—typically only a variance decay—failing to adapt to the agent's real-time learning status. This paper asks whether LLMs' reasoning capabilities can be leveraged to generate task-specific, dynamically adjusted exploration strategies.

## Input / Output

The method consumes purely textual inputs: a structured task description (action space, reward details, end conditions, goal), M=100 actions sampled uniformly from the latest episode, and the total episode reward. State information is deliberately excluded to maintain text-only compatibility, which is critical for image-based environments like Atari. For discrete action spaces, the output is a full probability distribution over all actions; for continuous spaces, it is a per-dimension bias vector added to the symmetric Gaussian noise, both updated every K episodes.

## Method

LLM-Explorer employs a two-stage LLM pipeline: a first LLM analyzes the sampled action-reward trajectory alongside the task description to summarize the agent's current learning status and recommend exploration strategies; a second LLM then converts this analysis into a concrete probability distribution (discrete) or bias vector (continuous) for the next K episodes. The generated distribution replaces the preset stochastic process in the base RL algorithm (DQN, DDPG, TD3, Rainbow, CURL, etc.) as a drop-in plug-in requiring no architectural changes. The distribution is periodically refreshed, yielding a task-specialized stochastic process that evolves with the agent's learning progress. The design uses GPT-4o mini with temperature 1.0, and the paper further validates compatibility across LLMs (GPT-4o, GPT-3.5, Llama-3.1, Qwen2.5) and introduces KL-divergence-based safeguards against over-skewed or hallucinated distributions.

## Results

On the Atari benchmark (15 tasks), LLM-Explorer plugged into DQN achieves a 37.27% mean and 13.84% median improvement in human-normalized game score over raw DQN, with gains in 13 out of 15 environments. The largest individual improvements are on Freeway (+292.73%), Hero (+304.35%), and Alien (+126.92%). On MuJoCo (5 tasks), LLM-Explorer plugged into DDPG consistently improves performance across all environments. The method also generalizes to DQN variants (Double-Dueling DQN, Rainbow, CURL) and TD3, showing consistent improvements in all 12 tested algorithm-environment combinations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the base DQN algorithm, LLM-Explorer improves human-normalized scores in 13 of 15 Atari tasks (losing only on Qbert by -2.80% and Seaquest by -4.09%), with a 37.27% mean improvement. Against NoisyNet and RND (two non-LLM exploration baselines), LLM-Explorer consistently outperforms both across Alien, Freeway, and MsPacman; under a fixed wall-clock time budget (Appendix F), LLM-Explorer still wins even when NoisyNet is allowed ~10% more training steps (e.g., Freeway: 29.16 vs. 10.39). Against RL algorithm variants (Double-Dueling DQN, Rainbow, CURL, TD3), LLM-Explorer plugged in consistently improves performance over the original algorithm in all 12 tested combinations (Figure 3), confirming plug-in compatibility. On MuJoCo, LLM-Explorer improves DDPG across all 5 tasks (HalfCheetah, Hopper, Humanoid, HumanoidStandup, Walker2d). The two tasks where LLM-Explorer underperforms (Qbert, Seaquest) are attributed to overly skewed LLM-generated distributions in highly dynamic environments, which a KL-divergence clipping safeguard partially mitigates (Qbert: 301.97→316.92, Seaquest: 196.15→221.81).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two research threads: (1) policy exploration in RL, where prior work relies on preset stochastic processes (ε-greedy, Gaussian/OU noise, NoisyNet, RND, entropy bonuses) that are uniform across tasks and rigidly decayed over time; and (2) LLM-enhanced RL, where existing studies use LLMs for reward shaping, state representation, or high-level task decomposition but not for exploration. LLM-Explorer departs from both by using LLMs specifically to generate a task-specific, dynamically updated exploration distribution, positioned as a drop-in replacement for the stochastic process in any existing RL algorithm (DQN, DDPG, TD3, Rainbow, CURL) without architectural modification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-in design lowers the barrier to adoption: any practitioner using DQN-family or actor-critic algorithms can integrate LLM-Explorer by swapping one probability distribution, potentially improving exploration in complex or non-stationary environments where fixed schedules underperform. However, the approach carries non-trivial computational overhead (~$1.3 and ~10 hours per 500k-step run), requires a capable LLM (≥7B parameters), and fails on some highly dynamic tasks (Qbert, Seaquest) where the LLM produces overly skewed distributions. Open questions include whether fine-tuning or distilling a specialized small LLM can reduce the minimum model size, whether more sophisticated adaptive update triggers can better balance cost and performance, and whether the KL-divergence-based safeguard can be replaced by a more principled mechanism for detecting harmful exploration distributions.
