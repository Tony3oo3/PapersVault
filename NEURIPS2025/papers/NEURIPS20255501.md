## Problem

Current Large Speech-Language Models (LSLMs) are largely constrained to processing short speech segments (under 30 seconds), as long-speech processing faces two critical barriers: a severe scarcity of long-speech training data and the high computational cost of long speech sequences, which are typically more than four times longer than their text equivalents. This paper tackles how to extend LSLM capabilities to efficient long-speech understanding without requiring dedicated long-speech training data.

## Input / Output

The method takes a raw speech waveform sampled at 16 kHz and a text instruction as input. The audio encoder (Whisper-based) converts the waveform into speech representations at 25 Hz frame rate, which are then compressed by the iterative fusion strategy into condensed representations of a configurable target length L (e.g., 750, 400, 200, 100, 50, 25, or 12 frames). For long-speech inference, the input is split into 30-second clips processed sequentially before being concatenated. The LLM produces a free-form text response conditioned on the condensed speech representations and the instruction.

## Method

FastLongSpeech introduces a speech extractor module on top of Qwen2-Audio's audio encoder that employs an iterative fusion strategy: at each iteration, it identifies the most similar adjacent frame pairs via cosine similarity, groups consecutive frames into spans, and merges each span into a single frame using content density (derived from a CTC decoder's non-blank token probabilities) as fusion weights, halving the sequence length per iteration until it fits within the LLM's speech window. Training proceeds in two stages: first, a CTC decoder is trained on ASR data to learn content density estimation; second, a dynamic compression training method uniformly samples the target length L from a set of hyperparameters during fine-tuning on short-speech QA data, enabling the LLM to generalize across compression ratios and transfer short-speech capabilities to long-speech tasks without any long-speech training examples. The approach uses LoRA fine-tuning on the LLM and achieves a 70% runtime reduction and 60% FLOPs decrease compared to NTK-RoPE on long-speech tasks while maintaining superior generation quality.

## Results

On the LongSpeech-Eval benchmark (long-speech spoken QA), FastLongSpeech achieves a score of 3.55, outperforming NTK-RoPE (3.44), AvgPool (3.10), and Random (2.54). In terms of inference efficiency on the same long-speech task, FastLongSpeech requires only 26.44 TFLOPs and 1.47 s average runtime, compared to 61.21 TFLOPs and 4.80 s for NTK-RoPE — a 60% reduction in compute and 70% reduction in latency. On short-speech tasks (LibriTTS/OpenASQA), FastLongSpeech at L=200 achieves a score of 3.87 with 5.64 TFLOPs, matching or exceeding the vanilla Qwen2-Audio baseline (3.73, 9.79 TFLOPs) while using roughly half the computation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against NTK-RoPE, the primary long-context baseline, FastLongSpeech wins on both quality (3.55 vs. 3.44) and efficiency (26.44 vs. 61.21 TFLOPs; 1.47 s vs. 4.80 s runtime), demonstrating that iterative fusion with dynamic compression training is more effective than simply extending the RoPE window. Against the cascaded Whisper+LLM pipeline, FastLongSpeech trades a small quality gap (3.55 vs. 3.75) for an 11.7× speedup (1.47 s vs. 18.61 s total). On the MELD emotion recognition task at a matched 50% inference-cost reduction, FastLongSpeech (52.95% accuracy) outperforms FastAdaSP (52.14%). On the SPIRAL-H long-speech information retrieval benchmark, FastLongSpeech achieves 76.81% accuracy at a 65.88% prune rate, substantially surpassing SpeechPrune's 63.77% at a 60% prune rate. The method also generalizes to the stronger Qwen2.5-Omni backbone, where it maintains competitive scores across compression ratios (3.82 at L=750, 3.75 at L=200) without requiring the dynamic compression training stage.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Qwen2-Audio as its foundational LSLM, extending its speech window from ~30 seconds to multi-minute inputs without requiring long-speech training data. Conceptually, it sits at the intersection of two prior lines of work: (1) speech token pruning/compression methods like SpeechPrune and FastAdaSP, which reduce inference cost by selecting or merging speech representations, and (2) long-context extension techniques like NTK-RoPE, which expand the model's positional encoding. The key departure is the iterative fusion strategy guided by CTC-based content density, which selectively merges frames based on both redundancy (cosine similarity) and informativeness (non-blank probability), combined with a dynamic compression training paradigm that transfers short-speech capabilities to long-speech settings purely through varying compression ratios during fine-tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine gap: LSLMs are largely confined to short-speech inputs (<30s), and the two main barriers—scarcity of long-speech training data and quadratic computational cost—remain unresolved. The finding that dynamic compression training on short-speech data alone can effectively transfer to long-speech understanding is practically significant, as it removes the need for expensive long-speech dataset construction. However, the LongSpeech-Eval benchmark is small (164 samples, all synthesized speech), the cascaded Whisper+LLM baseline actually outperforms FastLongSpeech on long-speech quality (3.75 vs 3.55), and optimal compression ratio is task-dependent, limiting straightforward deployment. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to real (non-synthesized) long speech, whether it scales to hour-length inputs, and whether the iterative fusion can be integrated with in-LLM token pruning methods like FastAdaSP for further efficiency gains.
