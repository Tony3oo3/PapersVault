## Problem

Decomposing mutual information between neural responses and stimuli into stimulus-specific contributions is fundamentally ill-posed, with infinitely many valid decompositions. Existing measures (I_SSI, I_sp, I_surp, I_CiSSI) violate key interpretability axioms such as locality and positivity, while Fisher information, though local, only approximates mutual information in the low-noise limit and fails completeness. The paper addresses this by formalizing five axioms and deriving a decomposition that satisfies all of them while remaining computationally tractable for high-dimensional stimuli.

## Input / Output

The method takes a high-dimensional image stimulus (MNIST digits at 32×32 or natural images at 64×64), a neural population response vector (from a linear-nonlinear-Poisson model or recorded retinal ganglion cells), and two trained diffusion models (an unconditional prior and a response-conditioned posterior). It produces a scalar stimulus-wise information attribution I_local(x) and, more usefully, a pixel-wise map I_i(x) of the same spatial resolution as the input image, showing which individual pixels contribute most to the total encoded information. The pixel-wise output is structured as a sum over diagonal Fisher information elements, making each pixel's contribution independently interpretable.

## Method

The core insight is an exact identity (via de Bruijn's identity) expressing mutual information as an integral of the Fisher information matrix evaluated at noise-corrupted stimuli X_γ = x + √γ Z, integrated over all noise levels γ ∈ [0, ∞). The stimulus-specific decomposition I_local(x) is obtained by conditioning the Fisher information expectation on a fixed stimulus x rather than averaging over the full stimulus distribution, which by construction guarantees completeness and, because the Gaussian kernel p(X_γ|x) has finite support, locality. The feature-wise decomposition follows from taking individual diagonal elements of the Fisher matrix, with the insensitivity-to-irrelevant-features axiom forcing the weights to be the identity matrix. Practically, the Fisher information is estimated via Tweedie's formula as the squared difference between conditional means E[X|x_γ, r] and E[X|x_γ], which are approximated by the outputs of an unconditional and a conditional DDPM (UNet-2D architecture) trained on the stimulus prior and posterior respectively, with the integral over γ discretized over 24 diffusion steps.

## Results

The paper's central result is that the proposed $I_{\text{local}}(x)$ is the only stimulus-specific decomposition satisfying all four core axioms (completeness, locality, positivity, additivity), as summarized in Table 1. All five previously proposed decompositions ($I_{sp}$, $I_{SSI}$, $I_{surp}$, $I_{CiSSI}$, and Fisher $J(x)$) violate at least one axiom. Empirically, on MNIST with a 49-neuron LNP model, $I_{\text{local}}$ reveals information concentrated along object edges (where posterior sensitivity is highest), whereas the Fisher information diagonal produces blob-like patterns centered on individual receptive fields. The method scales to 32×32 pixel stimuli with a UNet-based DDPM, requiring ~1 minute per image on an RTX 4080 GPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares $I_{\text{local}}$ against five prior decompositions and the Fisher information. $I_{sp}$ and $I_{SSI}$ both fail locality and positivity: they depend on global terms (marginal $p(r)$ or posterior $p(x|r)$) and can yield negative attributions. $I_{surp}$ and $I_{CiSSI}$ satisfy positivity but fail locality, since their values at any $x$ shift when the likelihood or prior changes at a distant $x'$. The Fisher information $J(x)$ is inherently local and positive but fails completeness—it does not integrate to the total mutual information except in the low-noise limit. The two decompositions from Kong et al. (2024), when averaged over $p(R|x)$, reduce to $I_{surp}$ (non-local, can be negative) and a non-additive measure, respectively, as demonstrated in Appendix B and Figure 5. $I_{\text{local}}$ is the unique decomposition satisfying all four axioms simultaneously, and its locality is empirically confirmed: perturbing a tuning curve near $x=-3$ leaves $I_{\text{local}}(x)$ unchanged for $|x|>6$, while all other decompositions show global shifts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the classical Brunel & Nadal (1998) result, which approximated mutual information via Fisher information in the low-noise limit, by deriving an exact integral identity (Eq. 17) that holds for arbitrary noise levels and stimulus distributions. It departs from prior stimulus-specific information decompositions (I_SSI, I_sp, I_surp, I_CiSSI) by showing they all violate a locality axiom, and from Kong et al. (2024) by demonstrating that their diffusion-based decompositions, when averaged over responses, fail additivity or locality. The axiomatic framework draws conceptual parallels to integrated gradients (Sundararajan et al. 2017), but is tailored to noisy neural responses and does not require an arbitrary baseline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first stimulus- and feature-wise information decomposition that simultaneously satisfies completeness, locality, positivity, additivity, and insensitivity to irrelevant features, making it a principled tool for interpreting what specific stimuli or pixels a neural population encodes. Its connection to diffusion models makes it scalable to high-dimensional naturalistic stimuli, a regime where prior methods are intractable. Key limitations include testing only on simple LNP models and MNIST/Tiny ImageNet, an unproven uniqueness claim, and a sampling approximation that may introduce bias. Open questions include application to fitted biological neural models, natural image datasets at scale, and whether zero-shot posterior sampling can eliminate the need for a trained conditional diffusion model.
