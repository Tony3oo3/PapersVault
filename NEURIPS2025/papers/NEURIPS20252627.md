## Problem

Despite the proven superiority of attention mechanisms in modeling capability, YOLO architectures have remained CNN-dominated because attention-based models suffer from quadratic computational complexity and inefficient memory access, making them roughly 3× slower than CNNs under similar compute budgets. This paper addresses the gap by designing an attention-centric YOLO framework that matches the inference speed of prior CNN-based YOLOs while capturing the accuracy benefits of attention.

## Input / Output

The model takes RGB images at 640×640 (or arbitrary rectangular resolutions such as 672×608) and outputs bounding boxes with class labels in the standard YOLO detection format. A key distinction from prior window-attention-based detectors (e.g., Swin, CSwin) is that Area Attention imposes no input-size divisibility constraints beyond the total token count being divisible by the number of areas, enabling native distortion-free rectangular inference without resizing. Five model scales (N/S/M/L/X) are provided, ranging from 2.6M to 59.5M parameters.

## Method

YOLOv12 introduces three core components: (1) Area Attention (A2), which flattens an H×W feature map into a 1D sequence, splits it into l contiguous segments, and applies local attention within each segment using only flatten and reshape operations—preserving a large receptive field while reducing complexity and supporting arbitrary input shapes; (2) Residual Efficient Layer Aggregation Networks (R-ELAN), which add a block-level residual shortcut with a small scaling factor (0.01) to stabilize training of deep attention stacks and replace ELAN's split-concat aggregation with a bottleneck structure that reduces FLOPs and parameters; and (3) architectural refinements for YOLO integration, including FlashAttention to eliminate memory-access bottlenecks, a decoupled projection strategy that computes v separately from q/k to avoid redundant feature reorganization (~10% speedup), removal of positional encoding, a 7×7 separable convolution 'position perceiver' on the attention value, and a reduced MLP ratio of 1.5 (vs. the standard 4.0) to shift compute toward the attention mechanism. The backbone retains YOLO's hierarchical multi-scale structure with grouped convolutions for downsampling, and the neck/head follow YOLOv11's design with C3K2 blocks replaced by R-ELAN.

## Results

On COCO val (640×640 input), YOLOv12 achieves state-of-the-art mAP across all five model scales: YOLOv12-N reaches 40.5% mAP at 1.62 ms latency (6.0G FLOPs, 2.6M params), YOLOv12-S reaches 47.8% at 2.44 ms (19.5G, 9.1M), YOLOv12-M reaches 52.5% at 4.30 ms (59.9G, 19.7M), YOLOv12-L reaches 53.8% at 5.89 ms (82.6G, 26.6M), and YOLOv12-X reaches 55.4% at 10.47 ms (184.9G, 59.5M). All models are trained from scratch for 600 epochs with SGD on MSCOCO 2017, without pre-training or additional training tricks. Latency is measured on a T4 GPU using TensorRT FP16.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the YOLO series, YOLOv12 wins at every scale: YOLOv12-N beats YOLOv10-N by 2.0% mAP and YOLO11-N by 1.1% mAP at comparable speed; YOLOv12-S beats YOLO11-S by 0.9% mAP with fewer FLOPs (19.5G vs 21.5G); YOLOv12-M beats YOLO11-M by 1.0% mAP with 12% fewer FLOPs; YOLOv12-L beats YOLO11-L by 0.5% mAP with 5% fewer FLOPs; and YOLOv12-X beats YOLO11-X by 0.8% mAP with 5% fewer FLOPs. Against RT-DETRv2/v3, YOLOv12-S matches RT-DETRv2-R18 in mAP (47.8 vs 47.9) while being 47% faster (2.44 vs 4.58 ms) with only 33% of the FLOPs; YOLOv12-L beats RT-DETRv2/v3-R50 by 0.4% mAP while being 15% faster with 61% of the FLOPs; and YOLOv12-X beats RT-DETRv2/v3-R101 by 0.8–1.1% mAP while being 22% faster with 29% fewer FLOPs. The only losses are at the L and X scales against D-FINE (54.0% and 55.8% mAP respectively), though YOLOv12-L and X still run substantially faster (5.89 ms vs 8.07 ms; 10.47 ms vs 12.89 ms) with fewer parameters. Against DAMO-YOLO and DEYO (Table 10), YOLOv12 dominates at every scale with higher mAP, lower FLOPs, and faster inference.

## Relation to Prior Work

YOLOv12 breaks from the CNN-dominated YOLO lineage (YOLOv8 through YOLO11) by replacing convolutional feature extractors with an attention-centric backbone, while retaining the hierarchical multi-scale structure and training protocol established by YOLO11. Its R-ELAN module is a direct evolution of ELAN from YOLOv7, adding residual shortcuts with layer scaling to address convergence issues that arise when attention blocks replace convolutions. The Area Attention mechanism draws inspiration from local attention in Swin and CSwin Transformers but deliberately simplifies them to a flatten-and-reshape operation, and incorporates FlashAttention to address the memory-access bottleneck that has historically made attention slower than CNNs in practice.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first YOLO variant to make attention the core backbone while matching CNN inference speed, challenging the long-held assumption that attention is inherently too slow for real-time detection. The practical impact is significant given YOLO's dominance in deployed real-time systems, and the code is open-sourced. Key limitations include the hard dependency on FlashAttention (restricting deployment to Turing/Ampere/Ada/Hopper GPUs) and evaluation on a single dataset (COCO). Open questions include whether the attention-centric design generalizes to other detection tasks (segmentation, tracking), whether the 600-epoch training requirement (vs. ~500 for prior YOLOs) is a fundamental cost of attention-based optimization, and whether further architectural refinements can close the remaining gap to the strongest DETR-based detectors like D-FINE-X.
