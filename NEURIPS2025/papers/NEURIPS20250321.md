## Problem

Single-image depth foundation models produce accurate shapes but suffer from scale ambiguity and visual illusions in real scenes, while traditional depth-from-focus methods require at least 4–5 frames at low temporal resolution, making data acquisition slow and limiting focus-sweeping fidelity. Event cameras record focus sweeping at microsecond resolution but existing event-based DFF methods only yield sparse depth, and no public dataset jointly provides AIF images, focal stacks, events, and ground-truth depth for unified evaluation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250321/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes two modalities captured by a co-aligned hybrid camera: an all-in-focus RGB image (e.g., 720×1280) and asynchronous focus-sweeping events from a DVS sensor (e.g., Prophesee EVK4). Events are binned into 32 temporal slices with polarity signs before network processing, and the AIF image is fed to a frozen Depth Anything V2 backbone. The output is a dense metric depth map at the input image resolution, supervised against ground-truth depth in meters.

## Method

The key insight is that events triggered at intensity boundaries during focus sweeping undergo a polarity reversal at the optimal focus timestamp, encoding differential in-focus information analogous to DFV but at far higher temporal resolution. The authors build a multi-scale EDFV via a convolutional encoder over 32 event bins, then apply a Spatial Attention Mask Extraction (SAME) strategy—combining image gradients and event density—to suppress noise in textureless regions and produce a sparse metric depth map. A prompting network (EDPN) fuses this sparse depth with the IFM's initial dense depth through two U-Net feature extractors (SparseNet with max-pooling for densification, DenseNet with large-kernel convolutions for high-level features) and a Multi-scale Cross-attention-guided Prompting Module (MCPM) that uses deformable convolutions to generate keys/values from sparse event features, handling sparsity and misalignment. Training uses a composite loss: pixel-wise MSE on the final dense map, masked MSE on the sparse map, and a multi-scale patch-level edge-guided scale-shift invariant loss at four MCPM levels.

## Results

On the in-domain Sintel-Dr. Bokeh dataset, the proposed method achieves RMSE 0.095, outperforming the best DFF baseline DFF-DFV (0.134) by 29.1% and the best monocular baseline Metric3D V2 (0.170) by 44.1%. On Blender-Syn, it achieves RMSE 0.068 and AbsRel 0.077, surpassing all DFF methods and matching or exceeding DA V2 (RMSE 0.063) on most other metrics. In zero-shot evaluation, the method reduces RMSE from 0.294 (Metric3D V2) to 0.148 on Blender-Syn and from 0.267 (DFF-FV) to 0.233 on Sintel-Dr. Bokeh, demonstrating strong generalization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DFF baselines, the proposed method dominates across both datasets: on Sintel-Dr. Bokeh it beats DFF-DFV (the strongest DFF method) by 29.1% in RMSE (0.095 vs. 0.134) and by 75.3% in AbsRel (0.141 vs. 0.569), while on Blender-Syn it outperforms HybridDepth (the best DFF method there) by 23.6% in RMSE (0.068 vs. 0.089). Against monocular IFM baselines, the method achieves the best RMSE on Sintel-Dr. Bokeh (0.095 vs. 0.170 for Metric3D V2 and 0.297 for DA V2), confirming that event-based focus cues effectively resolve the scale ambiguity that plagues single-image methods. On Blender-Syn, DA V2 achieves a marginally lower RMSE (0.063 vs. 0.068), but the proposed method still wins on AbsRel (0.077 vs. 0.089) and log10 (0.028 vs. 0.035), and substantially outperforms Metric3D V2 and DAC. In zero-shot settings (Table 2), the advantage widens dramatically: on Blender-Syn the method achieves RMSE 0.148 versus 0.294 for Metric3D V2 (53% improvement), and on Sintel-Dr. Bokeh it reaches 0.233 versus 0.267 for DFF-FV, while all monocular baselines degrade severely (DA V2: 0.337, DAC: 0.515). The method also achieves the best results on the semi-real 4DLFD-Semi-Real dataset (RMSE 1.549 vs. 1.573 for HybridDepth and 2.915–2.997 for monocular methods), validating its robustness to real-world event noise.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends frame-based Differential Focus Volume (DFV) [Yang et al., CVPR 2022] to the event domain by exploiting the polarity-reversal property of events triggered during focus sweeping, yielding the Event-based Differential Focus Volume (EDFV). The event stacking and multi-scale encoding design draws on NEST [Teng et al., ECCV 2022] for event-based neural representations. On the prompting side, the paper adapts the LiDAR-prompting paradigm for depth completion [Park et al., CVPR 2024; Lin et al., CVPR 2025] to the event modality, introducing deformable convolutions in the cross-attention module to handle the sparsity and edge-biased nature of event-derived sparse depth. The overall pipeline positions events as a high-temporal-resolution substitute for focal stack frames, decoupling DFF from the multi-frame acquisition bottleneck.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work demonstrates that a single event stream from a focus sweep can replace 32-frame focal stacks with ~8.7× fewer MACs while maintaining or improving accuracy, making DFF practical on resource-constrained devices. The four released datasets (two synthetic, one semi-real, one real) fill a gap in the literature where no public benchmark jointly provides AIF images, focal stacks, events, and GT depth. Key open questions include performance in low-light or textureless scenes where event density drops, sensor synchronization in high-speed capture, and whether scaling training data to IFM-level scale would further close the zero-shot gap. The reliance on a physical focus-sweeping process and a co-aligned event-RGB hybrid camera limits immediate deployment but opens a path toward liquid-lens-based systems for faster acquisition.
