## Problem

Nonlinear dynamical systems deployed in the wild face distribution shifts—changing road surfaces, payloads, or mechanical degradation—that invalidate a single fixed dynamics model. Existing Koopman-based predictors are trained offline and remain static at test time, while meta-learning approaches that do adapt typically rely on gradient-based inner loops too slow for real-time control. The paper asks whether one can combine the structured linearity of Koopman operators with the data efficiency of meta-learning to obtain closed-form, uncertainty-aware online adaptation.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a window of q recent state-action pairs (7-D states: longitudinal/lateral velocities, accelerations, yaw, articulation angle, slip angle; 3-D controls: throttle, brake, steering) plus a horizon of future control inputs. It produces a full posterior predictive distribution over future latent states (a multivariate Student-t, approximated as Gaussian), a posterior MNIW over the Koopman operator, and—via the variational action encoder—thousands of dynamically feasible trajectories scored by a cost function for sampling-based planning. The key structural detail is that the context encoder operates on a *sequence* of delayed inputs (Takens-style time-delay embedding) rather than a single state-action pair, and the output uncertainty decomposes into aleatoric (process noise Σ) and epistemic (operator uncertainty via V̂) components.

## Method

MetaKoopman places a Matrix Normal-Inverse Wishart (MNIW) prior over the finite-dimensional Koopman operator K and process noise covariance Σ, and meta-learns the prior parameters {M̃, Ṽ, ν̃, Ψ̃} plus a scalar tempering factor β across a distribution of trajectory-segment tasks by minimizing the negative log-likelihood of held-out query data under the posterior predictive. Because MNIW is conjugate to the Gaussian linear-regression likelihood, test-time adaptation is a single closed-form update (Eq. 11) from a short recent trajectory—no gradient inner loop. A meta-learned tempering scalar β rescales prior precision (Ṽ′ = β⁻¹Ṽ, Ψ̃′ = β⁻¹Ψ̃) to balance responsiveness against overfitting. Multi-step forecasting propagates first two moments via a moment-matched Gaussian recursion (Eq. 14), and a variational action encoder (CLAS) regularizes future-action embeddings to N(0,I), letting the planner sample latent actions directly without re-running the encoder, accelerating trajectory rollouts by an order of magnitude.

## Results

MetaKoopman achieves the lowest validation MSE across all three primary environments: 0.0369 on Hopper-Gravity, 0.0596 on the real-world Truck Dataset, and 0.7490 on HalfCheetah-Slope. The closest competitor, Bayesian MAML, trails by 22% on Hopper (0.0475), 33% on the Truck Dataset (0.0888), and 15% on HalfCheetah-Slope (0.8808). Non-adaptive baselines (DKO, EMLP, NeuralODE) are substantially worse, with validation losses 2–5× higher than MetaKoopman, particularly on the Truck Dataset where DKO reaches 0.2042 and EMLP 0.2589.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MetaKoopman outperforms all five baselines on every environment. Bayesian MAML [45], the closest competitor (also a Bayesian meta-learner but relying on optimization-based inner-loop updates), is beaten by 22% on Hopper, 33% on the Truck Dataset, and 15% on HalfCheetah-Slope, confirming that closed-form MNIW updates are more sample-efficient than gradient-based adaptation. GrBAL [24], a gradient-based adaptive learner, is 2.5× worse on Hopper and 1.5× worse on the Truck Dataset, and lacks calibrated uncertainty. DKO [33], a non-adaptive deep Koopman operator, suffers the largest degradation under distribution shift—its Truck Dataset loss (0.2042) is 3.4× MetaKoopman's, and its high variance (±0.1674) signals instability. EMLP [18] (deep ensemble) and Neural ODE [5] are the weakest baselines overall, with losses 4–5× higher on the Truck Dataset, reflecting their inability to adapt to changing dynamics at test time.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of Koopman operator-based dynamics modeling (DMD, Deep Koopman, KNF) and Bayesian meta-learning (Bayesian MAML, PLATIPUS, PEARL). Prior Koopman methods learn a fixed operator offline and cannot adapt at test time, while prior meta-learning approaches for dynamics (GrBAL, Bayesian MAML) rely on gradient-based inner loops that introduce latency. MetaKoopman departs from both by placing a conjugate Matrix Normal-Inverse Wishart prior over the Koopman operator and meta-learning that prior, yielding closed-form posterior updates from short trajectory segments—no inner-loop optimization required. The variational action encoder (CLAS) further distinguishes it from prior Koopman planners by enabling order-of-magnitude faster trajectory sampling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The closed-form Bayesian adaptation makes principled uncertainty-aware dynamics modeling viable in real-time safety-critical control, as demonstrated by the 37.5-ton truck deployment in winter conditions where non-adaptive models fail. A key limitation is that the encoder remains deterministic, so representation-level uncertainty (e.g., in sparse or ambiguous latent regions) is not captured; the authors acknowledge this as a future direction. The truck dataset and planning code are not publicly released, limiting full reproducibility of the real-world results. Open questions include whether the MNIW conjugate structure generalizes to multi-agent or partially observable settings, and how the method interacts with reinforcement learning for uncertainty-aware exploration.
