## Problem

Vanilla scaled-dot product attention (VSA) has quadratic complexity in sequence length, making it a major bottleneck for both training and inference. Existing computation-acceleration methods—particularly single-stage sparse attention—suffer from significant performance degradation because they capture only local or only global information, and most cannot replace VSA without retraining. The paper addresses the gap of designing a sparse attention mechanism that simultaneously preserves local inductive biases and long-range dependencies with low approximation error.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251372/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

DuSA is a drop-in replacement for the attention layer in any transformer: it consumes the standard query, key, and value matrices (Q, K, V ∈ R^{m×c}) produced by linear projections of the input embedding, and outputs the updated embedding matrix H ∈ R^{m×c}. The sequence is partitioned into blocks of size b (chosen per architecture level), and the blockify strategy (fixed or rolling) determines how interblock attention scores are aggregated. Because both stages operate on sub-quadratic sub-matrices, the mechanism is directly compatible with memory-efficient kernels such as FlashAttention-2 or SpargeAttn for further speedup.

## Method

DuSA decomposes attention into two sequential sparse stages. In Stage 1 (intrablock sparse attention), a block-diagonal mask Z₁ restricts softmax to within-block dot products, yielding H₁ = A₁V that captures local inductive biases at O(m·b·c) cost. In Stage 2 (interblock sparse attention), the method aggregates information across blocks: Strategy 1 uses a fixed partition Z₂ so each token attends to one token per block, while Strategy 2 (rolling indices) sums b shifted attention-score matrices A_k″ to let each token see more cross-block context, at O(m²/b·c) cost. The final output is H₂ = A₂(A₁V), a composition of the two sparse attention maps. The paper derives Frobenius-norm upper bounds on ‖H₂ − H‖_F showing the dual-stage design approximates VSA more tightly than single-stage sparse attention in practice (despite a looser theoretical bound), and demonstrates that both stages can be further accelerated by memory-efficient attention kernels, yielding 2–8× speedups over VSA while matching or exceeding its accuracy across image classification, segmentation, detection, video generation, and long-context tasks.

## Results

On Long Range Arena, DuSA achieves 60.2% average accuracy, outperforming VSA (58.8%), Nyströmformer (59.0%), and all other efficient attention baselines, while delivering 2.4–8.6× speedups over VSA and 1.1–2.1× over FlashAttention-2. On ImageNet-1K, CSWin-T-DuSA reaches 83.2% top-1 accuracy (vs. 83.1% VSA, 83.1% ELFATT) and Swin-T-DuSA reaches 82.7% (vs. 82.4% VSA), with 2.2–4.3× inference speedups over VSA. On ADE20K semantic segmentation, DuSA achieves 49.6/48.0 mIoU (CSWin-T/Swin-T) and on MS COCO object detection 46.9/46.0 AP^b (1× schedule), both matching or exceeding VSA while running 5.3–8.3× faster. For text-to-video generation with CogVideoX-2B, DuSA achieves 258 TOPS (vs. 103 for FA2, 198 for SpargeAttn) with comparable or better CLIPSIM (0.2024 vs. 0.2022/0.2015).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DuSA outperforms VSA on 4 of 5 LRA tasks (e.g., ListOps 38.1 vs. 37.1, Retrieval 81.4 vs. 79.4) and on ImageNet-1K for both CSWin-T (83.2% vs. 83.1%) and Swin-T (82.7% vs. 82.4%), while being 2.4–8.6× faster; the only loss is Pathfinder (73.3 vs. 74.2). Against ELFATT, DuSA matches or slightly exceeds on ImageNet-1K (83.2% vs. 83.1% CSWin-T) and ADE20K mIoU (49.6 vs. 49.6 CSWin-T, 48.0 vs. 47.7 Swin-T), and is faster (32 vs. 32 FPS CSWin-T, 37 vs. 38 FPS Swin-T). DuSA clearly beats FLatten, EFFATT, and Agent on both accuracy and speed across all vision benchmarks (e.g., ADE20K mIoU 49.6 vs. 49.3/48.8/48.5 for CSWin-T). On text-to-video generation, DuSA surpasses SpargeAttn on all quality metrics (CLIPSIM 0.2024 vs. 0.2015, Flow-score 6.05 vs. 5.76) while being 30% faster (258 vs. 198 TOPS), and matches FA2 quality at 2.5× the speed. On LongBench with Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, DuSA achieves 41.18 average score vs. 40.68 for XAttention and 40.92 for FlashInfer's FlashAttention, confirming its effectiveness for LLM prefilling acceleration.

## Relation to Prior Work

DuSA builds directly on basic single-stage sparse attention (Child et al., 2019) by introducing a sequential two-stage design: intrablock sparse attention for local inductive biases followed by interblock sparse attention for long-range dependencies. This is conceptually distinct from hierarchical sparse attention (NSA, Big Bird) that combines different attention types in parallel, and from hybrid head architectures (ELFATT) that mix sparse and linear attention across heads. The key departure is that both stages operate sequentially on the same token sequence with low computational complexity, and the interblock stage uses attention-score-based aggregation (via rolling indices) rather than fixed-step or randomly selected tokens, making it compatible with memory-acceleration kernels like FlashAttention.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DuSA offers a practical, hardware-agnostic sparse attention that accelerates both training and inference across vision and language tasks, with the notable property that it can match or exceed VSA accuracy after training while providing 2–8× speedups. The counterintuitive finding that the dual-stage design (with a higher theoretical upper bound) achieves lower empirical approximation error than single-stage sparse attention suggests the theoretical analysis is too coarse and motivates tighter bounds as future work. Limitations include the static sparsity design (which cannot perfectly preserve VSA accuracy in plug-and-play settings without training) and the relatively rough worst-case error bounds. The dynamic sparse variant for LLM prefilling (building on XAttention's antidiagonal scoring) hints at broader applicability but remains preliminary.
