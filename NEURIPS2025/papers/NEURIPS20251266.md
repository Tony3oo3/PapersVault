## Problem

Adversarial IRL methods suffer from training instability due to their minimax game structure, while recent non-adversarial IRL approaches improve empirical stability but lack principled control over reward updates and formal guarantees of monotonic progress. No existing IRL framework provides a theoretical stability guarantee analogous to TRPO's monotonic policy improvement guarantee in forward RL.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251266/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a finite set of expert demonstration trajectories and access to the MDP environment for on-policy rollouts, operating in continuous state-action spaces (MuJoCo locomotion, Gym Robotics). It outputs a parameterized reward function r_θ (which can be restricted to state-only rewards to mitigate reward-shaping ambiguity) and a corresponding policy π_old optimized via Soft Actor-Critic. The reward update is constrained by an adaptive L2-norm penalty on reward differences, implicitly regularizing reward magnitude similar to SQIL's sparsity regularization.

## Method

TRRO formulates reward learning as a Minorization-Maximization process: at each step, it constructs a surrogate ℓ_{θ_old}(θ) that matches the expert-likelihood objective to first order at the current parameter, then maximizes a lower bound ℓ_{θ_old}(θ) − C·ε(θ) to guarantee monotonic improvement in the likelihood of expert demonstrations (equivalently, monotonic reduction in the imitation gap). The practical algorithm PIRO approximates this guarantee by replacing the non-differentiable max-norm ε with a differentiable L2 norm estimated on expert batches and policy rollouts, and introduces an adaptive coefficient μ that scales the trust-region penalty based on whether the current reward deviation exceeds a target threshold. Policy optimization is performed via k rounds of SAC-based soft policy iteration under the current reward, and the reward is updated via n stochastic gradient steps on the surrogate objective. The entire procedure is model-free, requires only first-order optimization, and degrades to standard non-adversarial IRL (Algorithm 2) when k=n=1 and μ=0.

## Results

On MuJoCo locomotion tasks, PIRO achieves rewards of 5967.2 (Ant-v4), 5954.9 (Humanoid-v4), 5643.7 (Walker2d-v4), 3362.0 (Hopper-v4), and 12587.4 (HalfCheetah-v4), matching or exceeding expert-level performance on 4 of 5 tasks. On Gym Robotics, PIRO scores 25.7 (AntMaze-UMazeDense), 9.4 (AntMaze-MediumDense), 8.8 (AntMaze-LargeDense), and 254.0 (AdroitHandPen), substantially outperforming all baselines in the harder maze and dexterous manipulation domains. The average gain over the best baseline is +381.5 on MuJoCo and +83.8 on Robotics, with the largest single-task improvement of +849.0 on Walker2d-v4 over f-IRL.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PIRO outperforms all 12 baselines on 8 of 9 tasks, with the only loss being Hopper-v4 where ML-IRL (3424.5) edges out PIRO (3362.0) by 62.5 points. Among adversarial IRL methods, PIRO dominates: FILTER and HyPE (the strongest adversarial online baselines) score below 1000 on most MuJoCo tasks, while PIRO reaches 5600–12600. Among non-adversarial methods, f-IRL is the closest competitor on MuJoCo (e.g., 5382.5 on Ant-v4 vs. PIRO's 5967.2), but PIRO pulls ahead by 584.7–849.0 points. On the harder Gym Robotics tasks, the gap widens dramatically: on AdroitHandPen, PIRO scores 254.0 while the best baseline (f-IRL) is still negative at -251.2, and on AntMaze-UMazeDense, PIRO (25.7) nearly doubles the best baseline (DAC at 11.9). Implicit-reward methods (IQ, CSIL, P²IL) consistently underperform explicit-reward methods, confirming the paper's argument that decoupling reward from dynamics improves transferability. PIRO's runtime per iteration (96–178s) is higher than most baselines but comparable to P²IL, and the authors argue the improved stability leads to faster overall convergence.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper constructs the inverse-RL counterpart to Trust Region Policy Optimization (TRPO), transplanting the Minorization-Maximization framework that guarantees monotonic policy improvement in forward RL into the IRL setting to guarantee monotonic improvement in expert-behavior likelihood. It builds directly on the maximum-likelihood IRL formulation of Zeng et al. (ML-IRL, 2022), which showed that non-adversarial IRL methods (SQIL, IQ-Learn, f-IRL, ML-IRL) can be unified under a single likelihood objective, and on Soft Actor-Critic as the policy optimization backbone. The key departure from prior non-adversarial IRL is the introduction of a principled trust-region constraint on reward updates (via a surrogate minorization), replacing the heuristic decaying step sizes used in ML-IRL with a provable monotonic-improvement guarantee.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is, to the authors' knowledge, the first formal stability guarantee for IRL, closing a gap that has persisted since the transition from adversarial to non-adversarial formulations. The practical PIRO algorithm demonstrates that the theoretical trust-region constraint translates into competitive (often superior) empirical performance without sacrificing sample efficiency, validated across MuJoCo locomotion, Gym Robotics, and a real-world meerkat behavior task. Open questions include whether the on-policy sampling requirement limits scalability to sample-expensive domains, whether the guarantee extends to approximate (non-exact) policy optimization, and whether the framework generalizes to alternative policy-alignment divergences beyond KL. The authors also highlight LLM reward modeling as a promising application direction.
