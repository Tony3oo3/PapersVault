## Problem

Existing 3D motion-text retrieval methods align global motion-text concepts directly, but a single motion sequence typically couples multiple fine-grained body-part movements, making global-only alignment ambiguous and prone to incorrect concept correspondence (e.g., matching arm swinging to 'walk' instead of 'wave hand'). The paper identifies that the difficulty lies not only in cross-modal alignment but also in the decomposition of semantic concepts within the same modality, and that prior vision-bridged approaches ignore the inherent structural (body-part) properties of human motion.

## Input / Output

The motion input is a SMPL skeleton sequence (T×J×3 joint positions) patchified into T×PN×3 where P=5 body parts (left/right arm, left/right leg, torso) and N=224 frames, fed into a ViT-B/16 encoder. Text inputs include the global description and LLM-generated part descriptions (one per body part) processed by Distill-BERT. The model outputs 256-dimensional aligned embeddings for both modalities at both global and part levels; at test time, an optional augmentation combines global and part similarity scores (weighted by α=0.1) to produce the final retrieval ranking.

## Method

SGAR introduces a Generation-Augmented Retrieval (GAR) paradigm where LLaMA3-70B generates part-level motion descriptions from global text, providing structural linguistic priors for fine-grained alignment. The motion encoder registers additional [cls-p_j] tokens per body part to extract part-level embeddings alongside the global [cls] embedding. Training combines four objectives: (1) standard contrastive alignment for full-body motion-text pairs, (2) soft contrastive part alignment that replaces hard labels with intra-modality text similarity scores to handle the low diversity of part motions, (3) part-mixture decorrelation learning that CutMix-style swaps 2–3 body-part patches between two motions and trains on the mixed samples with linearly blended text targets to prevent the model from shortcutting part embeddings via global motion co-occurrence, and (4) a directional relation alignment loss that enforces cosine consistency between the difference vectors (full-body minus part) in motion and text embedding spaces, regularizing the feature manifold. The total loss is L_full + L_part + λ_mix(L_full^mix + L_part^mix) + λ_r·L_r with λ_mix=0.5 and λ_r=0.1.

## Results

On the All-HML3D benchmark, SGAR achieves R@1 of 12.86 (text-to-motion) and 13.82 (motion-to-text), surpassing the previous best MoPatch (10.80/11.25) by approximately 2.1 and 2.6 points respectively, with MedR reduced from 19.00 to 15.00. On All-KIT, SGAR reaches R@1 of 16.75 (T2M) and 14.46 (M2T), outperforming MoPatch (14.02/13.61) by 2.7 and 0.85 points. The optional testing-augmented variant SGAR++ further boosts All-HML3D T2M R@1 to 14.07 and All-KIT M2T R@1 to 16.14. In transfer learning on BABEL, SGAR pretrained on Motion-X with full fine-tuning achieves 46.21% Top-1, exceeding the supervised 2s-AGCN baseline (41.14%) by over 5 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SGAR outperforms all retrieval baselines on both HML3D and KIT. Against MoPatch [45] (the strongest prior method using ViT patches), SGAR gains +2.06 R@1 on All-HML3D T2M and +2.73 R@1 on All-KIT T2M, with MedR improvements of 4 and 1.5 ranks respectively. Against TMR [25], the original motion-text retrieval framework, SGAR improves R@1 by +3.94 (All-HML3D T2M) and +6.70 (All-KIT T2M). ChronRet [6] and TriModal [44] are consistently outperformed across all protocols. In transfer learning on BABEL, SGAR (Motion-X pretrained, full fine-tune) reaches 46.21% Top-1, beating the supervised 2s-AGCN [34] (41.14%) and MotionCLIP [35] (40.90%) by 5+ points. For zero-shot action recognition on NTU 60/120, SGAR's text encoder (83.92/45.66 on 55/5 and 48/12 splits) surpasses CLIP-B [28] (83.28/39.72), Sentence-BERT [30] (82.37/41.38), and Distill-BERT [32] (83.78/39.72). In motion generation, SGAR's text encoder yields FID of 0.221 with MDM [36] (vs. 0.528 for TMR [25] and 0.544 for CLIP [28]) and 0.081 with T2M-GPT [47] (vs. 0.103 for TMR and 0.074 for ChronRet [6]), while Lex [19] achieves 0.524 FID with MDM but requires 4 training stages versus SGAR's single-stage training (~8× faster).

## Relation to Prior Work

SGAR builds directly on the contrastive motion-language retrieval framework established by TMR and the patch-based ViT motion representation from MoPatch, but departs from both by introducing a Generation-Augmented Retrieval (GAR) paradigm that inverts the more common RAG direction: instead of retrieving to augment generation, it uses LLM-generated part-level motion descriptions to augment retrieval. The part-mixture decorrelation learning adapts CutMix to the skeleton domain, and the directional relation alignment draws an analogy to inter-word vector differences in language model spaces. Unlike prior vision-bridged methods (MotionCLIP, TriModal) that render motions into images to leverage CLIP, SGAR exploits the inherent structural properties of the human body (five body parts) for fine-grained alignment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The GAR paradigm offers a practical and extensible recipe for leveraging increasingly capable LLMs to address data scarcity in motion-text retrieval, with the added benefit of an optional testing-time augmentation (SGAR++) that requires no retraining. The structural decomposition into body parts addresses a genuine limitation of global-only alignment, and the demonstrated transfer to action recognition and motion generation suggests the learned representations are broadly useful. Key open questions include whether temporal grounding of part motions (currently absent) can be incorporated, how the approach scales to more granular body part decompositions, and whether the reliance on LLM-generated part texts remains a bottleneck as motions grow more complex.
