## Problem

Existing moment retrieval methods and datasets operate under a Single-Moment Retrieval (SMR) assumption, where each query maps to exactly one relevant temporal segment. In real-world video understanding, a single query often aligns with multiple non-overlapping moments (e.g., 'cutting vegetables' appearing several times in a cooking video), yet no standardized dataset, evaluation metrics, or dedicated model architecture existed for this Multi-Moment Retrieval (MMR) setting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255715/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a 5–30 minute video (segmented at 0.5 FPS into clips encoded by frozen SlowFast and CLIP) and a natural language query (encoded by CLIP), both projected into a shared d-dimensional space. Rather than outputting a single (start, end) pair, FlashMMR produces a fixed set of three temporal spans (s_i, e_i, c_i), each with a start timestamp, end timestamp, and confidence score, enabling retrieval of up to three relevant moments per query. The QV-M² dataset provides ground-truth annotations averaging 2.9 moments per query across 1,341 videos.

## Method

FlashMMR builds on the FlashVTG backbone (Adaptive Cross Attention with learnable dummy tokens, a Transformer Encoder, and a multi-scale temporal feature pyramid via strided 1D convolutions) and adds a Post-Verification Module as its key contribution. This module first applies constrained temporal adjustment (clipping, rounding, min/max length enforcement) to initial boundary predictions, then extracts per-interval multi-modal features from the fused video embeddings. A GRU-based verification network re-evaluates these interval representations to produce refined confidence scores, supervised by tIoU against ground-truth moments plus a contrastive representation loss that enforces semantic coherence among temporally related frames. Training combines Focal Loss, L1 Loss, Clip-Aware Score Loss, and the post-verification loss (MSE + Cross-Entropy), with the verification loss weighted at 9 and the representation loss at 7. The design ensures that low-confidence or semantically inconsistent proposals are pruned while preserving recall for genuinely relevant multi-moment instances.

## Results

On the newly introduced QV-M² test set, FlashMMR achieves 35.14 G-mAP, 52.59 mAP@1_tgt, and 22.89 mAP@3+tgt, surpassing the previous SOTA (FlashVTG) by +3.00% G-mAP, +2.70% mAP@3+tgt, and +2.56% mR@3. On QVHighlights validation, FlashMMR reaches 48.07 G-mAP and 15.15 mAP@3+tgt, maintaining a lead over FlashVTG (48.02 G-mAP, 13.85 mAP@3+tgt) and CG-DETR (43.69 G-mAP, 10.02 mAP@3+tgt). The ablation confirms the Post-Verification module contributes +3.00% G-mAP and +3.04% mAP@2_tgt on QV-M².

## Baselines & Comparisons

FlashMMR outperforms all seven baselines across every metric on both QV-M² and QVHighlights. On QV-M², the largest gains are over the closest competitor FlashVTG (+3.00% G-mAP, +5.43% mAP@1_tgt, +2.70% mAP@3+tgt, +2.56% mR@3), confirming the Post-Verification module's benefit in multi-moment scenarios. Against earlier methods, FlashMMR leads TR-DETR by +3.91% G-mAP and +3.25% mR@3, and CG-DETR by +6.27% G-mAP and +2.11% mR@3 on QV-M². On QVHighlights, FlashMMR edges out FlashVTG by +0.05% G-mAP but gains more substantially on multi-target metrics (+1.30% mAP@3+tgt, +1.65% mR@2, +1.51% mR@3), while leading CG-DETR by +4.38% G-mAP and +1.22% mR@3. Notably, all SMR-designed baselines show a significant performance drop when evaluated on QV-M² (e.g., FlashVTG drops from 48.02 to 32.14 G-mAP), underscoring the difficulty of the multi-moment setting and the advantage of FlashMMR's dedicated architecture.

## Relation to Prior Work

FlashMMR extends the FlashVTG architecture (Cao et al., WACV 2025) by adding a Post-Verification module (GRU-based semantic consistency re-evaluation) on top of the existing feature extraction, multi-scale temporal processing, and adaptive score refinement pipeline. The cross-attention mechanism with learnable dummy tokens is inherited from CG-DETR (Moon et al., 2024). Conceptually, the paper departs from the dominant Single-Moment Retrieval paradigm—where each query maps to exactly one segment—by reframing moment retrieval as a one-to-many problem, and it positions itself against prior MMR efforts (NExT-VMR, SFABD) by providing the first fully human-annotated MMR dataset with dedicated evaluation metrics rather than adapting existing SMR benchmarks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduction of QV-M² as the first fully human-annotated multi-moment retrieval dataset, paired with new MMR-specific metrics (G-mAP, mIoU@k, mR@k), gives the video temporal grounding community a standardized benchmark that was previously absent. The observed performance drop across all SMR-trained models when evaluated under MMR settings (e.g., FlashVTG G-mAP drops from 48.02 to 40.28) concretely demonstrates the gap between current methods and real-world multi-moment scenarios. Key limitations include the relatively small dataset scale (2,212 queries / 1,341 videos) and the early-stage nature of the verification module, leaving open questions about whether more sophisticated strategies (reinforcement learning, contrastive learning) or larger-scale annotations are needed to push MMR performance further.
