## Problem

Integrating high-fidelity image generation into multimodal LLMs without degrading their reasoning capabilities remains challenging. Single-architecture approaches are computationally expensive and risk undermining the LLM's language abilities, while existing bridging methods that use 1D text descriptions or continuous query tokens are either too sparse to capture fine-grained spatial detail or require large connectors and extensive end-to-end training to align representations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252345/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the MLLM receives a text prompt alongside a fully masked 2D grid of CLIP image embeddings (e.g., 14×14 = 256 tokens for 256×256 images) and autoregressively predicts the unmasked patch-level CLIP latents in a randomly sampled order. These predicted 2D CLIP latents are downsampled by a factor of 2 via a lightweight 2D convolution, reshaped into spatial form, and fed to a latent ControlNet that conditions the FLUX.1-dev diffusion model (12B parameters) to render the final image. The frozen MLLM backbone simultaneously handles standard multimodal understanding tasks (VQA, captioning) producing text responses, so the system outputs both generated images and text depending on the task.

## Method

BIFROST-1 bridges a pretrained MLLM (Qwen2.5-VL) and a pretrained diffusion model (FLUX.1-dev) using 2D patch-level CLIP image embeddings as the communicative medium, exploiting the fact that these latents are natively aligned with the MLLM's own CLIP visual encoder. A visual generation branch—initialized as a trainable copy of the MLLM's QKV, MLP, and normalization layers with only a single linear vision head randomly initialized—predicts masked CLIP patch embeddings via masked autoregressive training (MSE loss, mask ratio ~1.0±0.25). On the diffusion side, a latent ControlNet (4 MM-DiT + 1 Single-DiT blocks, trained with flow-matching loss) replaces the standard ControlNet's image input with a linear projection from MLLM embedding dimension plus a 2D downsampling convolution, injecting spatial structure directly onto the noisy DiT latents. Training is decoupled: the MLLM vision branch and latent ControlNet are trained separately to allocate compute efficiently and reduce memory pressure from the 12B diffusion backbone. Because the MLLM backbone parameters remain entirely frozen, the model fully preserves the original multimodal reasoning capabilities while adding generation at a fraction of the training cost of single-architecture or 1D-token bridging alternatives.

## Results

On multimodal understanding benchmarks (Table 2), BIFROST-1 (7B, Qwen2.5-VL backbone) perfectly matches BLIP3-o 8B with MME-P 1685.2, MMB 83.5, SEED 76.9, MMMU 58.6, and MM-Vet 66.6, since the MLLM backbone is fully frozen. On generation benchmarks (Table 3), BIFROST-1 7B achieves GenEval 0.81 and DPG-Bench 77.67, while BIFROST-1 3B achieves GenEval 0.61 and DPG-Bench 76.41, trained on only 62M and 9M images respectively. On ImageNet 256×256 (Table 1), BIFROST-1 achieves FID 25.77, sFID 53.67, and IS 98.57, substantially outperforming all ablation variants (best alternative: cross-attention at FID 76.32). The paper notes that FID scores are heavily influenced by the diffusion backbone choice (FLUX.1-dev vs. SD1.5/SANA).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On multimodal understanding, BIFROST-1 7B exactly matches BLIP3-o 8B (same Qwen2.5-VL 7B backbone) across all five benchmarks, confirming that the frozen backbone fully preserves reasoning capabilities—unlike JanusPro-7B (MMMU 41.0) or MetaMorph (no MMMU reported) which fine-tune the LLM and degrade understanding. On generation, BIFROST-1 7B matches MetaQuery-XL on GenEval (0.81 vs. 0.80) and outperforms TokenFlow-XL on DPG-Bench (77.67 vs. 73.38), but trails JanusPro-7B (DPG-Bench 84.19) and MetaQuery-XL (82.05) on DPG-Bench. The key differentiator is training efficiency: BIFROST-1 7B is trained on only 62M images versus 200M for MetaQuery-XL and 194M for JanusPro-7B, while BIFROST-1 3B uses just 9M images. On the ImageNet ablation (Table 1), patch-level CLIP latents with Latent ControlNet (FID 25.77) dramatically outperform 2D learnable query tokens (FID 118.69), VAE latents (FID 284.51), SigLIP encoder (FID 274.16), and cross-attention conditioning (FID 76.32), validating each design choice.

## Relation to Prior Work

BIFROST-1 sits at the intersection of two lines of work: bridging LLMs with diffusion models (MetaMorph, MetaQuery, TokenFlow) and masked autoregressive image generation (MAR, unCLIP). Its key departure is using the MLLM's own CLIP visual encoder to produce patch-level 2D latents as the bridge signal, rather than learning new 1D query tokens or relying on text descriptions. The visual generation branch follows LMFusion's parameter-sharing strategy, and the diffusion-side conditioning adapts ControlNet into a 'latent ControlNet' that accepts CLIP embeddings instead of pixel-space control images, extending unCLIP's pooled-embedding paradigm to the patch level.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central practical contribution is a large training-efficiency gain: by leveraging natively aligned CLIP latents, BIFROST-1 avoids the costly realignment that single-architecture or 1D-bridging methods require, and the frozen MLLM backbone preserves reasoning capabilities without degradation. However, at current compute (62M images for the 7B model), generation quality on COCO/MJHQ FID still lags behind higher-compute baselines like MetaQuery-XL and JanusPro-7B, so the efficiency-quality tradeoff remains an open question. Future work on scaling data, exploring other MLLM/diffusion backbones, and extending to video generation would be needed to determine whether the CLIP-latent bottleneck ultimately limits ceiling quality.
