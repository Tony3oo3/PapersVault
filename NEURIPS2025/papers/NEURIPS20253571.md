## Problem

In complex digital ecosystems, multiple actors (authors, distributors, generative model developers) need to embed different watermarks in the same image for provenance, IP tracking, and content attribution, yet whether deep learning-based watermarks can coexist without overwriting each other had never been studied. The prevailing assumption was that a second watermark would destroy the first, making multi-watermark scenarios and super-watermark-based decoder identification infeasible.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253571/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The ensembling procedure takes a clean RGB image, two binary secret messages (one per watermarking method), and two pre-trained watermarking models, producing a single watermarked image from which both secrets can be independently decoded. Two application modes are supported: series (second watermark applied to the already-watermarked image, order matters) and parallel (both residuals computed from the original and averaged, order-invariant). Post-hoc PSNR-based strength clipping and binary linear error-correcting codes can further adjust the output without retraining any model.

## Method

The paper performs the first systematic empirical study of watermark coexistence by applying all ordered pairs of eight open-source methods (DwtDct, DwtDctSvd, HiDDeN, RivaGAN, RoSteALS, SSL, TrustMark B, TrustMark Q) to 1020 Adobe Stock images and measuring full-secret decoding accuracy, robustness under five augmentation suites, and PSNR. The key finding is that, contrary to expectation, most cross-method pairs preserve high accuracy for both watermarks, suggesting methods encode in quasi-orthogonal perturbation subspaces rather than fully occupying the image's information channel. Building on this, the authors introduce watermark ensembling as a post-training modification tool: combining two coexisting watermarks effectively doubles capacity, and when paired with PSNR strength clipping and binary linear error-correcting codes (e.g., LC[148,102,14]), it opens new accuracy–quality–robustness–capacity trade-off points without any retraining. A geometric intuition in the appendix explains coexistence via sparsity induced by robustness constraints, showing that different methods select non-overlapping valid perturbation sets within the quality ball.

## Results

The paper's central finding is that 8 open-source image watermarking methods can coexist in the same image with only minor accuracy degradation. In Table 1, TrustMark B (96% standalone) retains 96% accuracy when DwtDct is applied second, and 94% when TrustMark Q is applied second; similarly, DwtDct (51% standalone) actually *improves* to 76% when SSL is applied after it. Ensembling with error-correcting codes and strength clipping can boost weaker methods: RoSteALS + SSL (LC[130,100,10], 0.2 clip) maintains 100-bit capacity while improving accuracy by ~15% and quality by 2 dB over RoSteALS alone, and HiDDeN + RoSteALS (LC[148,102,14]) increases capacity from 48 to 102 bits with small gains in accuracy, robustness, and quality. However, no ensemble dominates all base models across every metric simultaneously.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates 8 watermarking methods (DwtDct, DwtDctSvd, HiDDeN, RivaGAN, RoSteALS, SSL, TrustMark B, TrustMark Q) in all pairwise coexistence configurations. TrustMark B and TrustMark Q are the strongest coexistence partners: TrustMark B retains 94–96% accuracy regardless of which second watermark is applied (except RoSteALS, which drops it to 15%), and TrustMark Q similarly holds 88–94%. DwtDct and DwtDctSvd show mutual coexistence well (51%→59% and 85%→78% respectively), and DwtDct even improves when SSL is applied after it (51%→76%). HiDDeN is the most fragile: its 15% standalone accuracy drops to 3–15% when any other method is applied second, and it is nearly always overwritten. RoSteALS is the most destructive to prior watermarks, reducing TrustMark Q's accuracy from 94% to 0% and SSL's from 94% to 49%. No method can coexist with itself (0% on the diagonal), confirming that same-family watermarks overwrite each other.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new empirical ground by being the first systematic study of coexistence among deep learning-based image watermarks, extending the classical multiple-watermarking literature (Sheppard et al. 2001; Wong et al. 2003; Zear et al. 2018) that was limited to frequency-domain transforms like DWT-DCT. It repurposes the well-known post-training tools of strength clipping and error-correcting codes alongside the newly observed coexistence phenomenon to form a three-tool framework for modifying watermark trade-offs without retraining. The work is positioned within the C2PA content-provenance ecosystem, where the practical need for super-watermarks and multi-actor provenance chains motivates the study, but it does not build on any single prior method's architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that heterogeneous deep watermarks largely coexist without mutual destruction is practically critical for the emerging C2PA and multi-actor provenance infrastructure, where a single image may carry watermarks from an author, a distributor, and a generative-model developer simultaneously. The ensembling framework offers a no-retraining lever to increase capacity and shift the capacity-accuracy-robustness-quality Pareto front, though the paper honestly notes it is not a universal improvement—sometimes ECC plus clipping on a single strong model dominates. Key open questions remain: why quasi-orthogonal encoding subspaces emerge (the geometric intuition in App. A is suggestive but not formalized), whether coexistence can be designed for or against, and whether the findings extend to video, audio, and text watermarking.
