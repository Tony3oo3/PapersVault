## Problem

While the logical expressiveness of static GNNs has been well characterised using modal and first-order logics, no connection between temporal GNNs (TGNNs) and logical languages had been established prior to this work. TGNNs process both spatial (graph-structure) and temporal (evolution over time) dimensions simultaneously, making their expressive power particularly challenging to analyse. This paper initiates the logical characterisation of TGNNs by relating them to two-dimensional product logics that combine temporal and modal operators.

## Input / Output

The input is a temporal graph—a finite sequence of static, undirected, node-labelled graphs sharing the same node set with strictly increasing timestamps—along with a pointed temporal graph specifying a particular node and timestamp at which to evaluate. Node labels are vectors in R^k (or binary colour vectors in {0,1}^k). The output is a binary value in {0,1} produced by a single-layer FNN with truncated-ReLU activation applied to the final node embedding at the last timestamp. The logical expressiveness notion used is 'uniform expressiveness': a TGNN class captures a logic if for every formula there exists a single TGNN that correctly classifies all pointed temporal graphs with respect to that formula.

## Method

The paper uses two-dimensional product logics—specifically PTL_{P,Y} × K, combining past propositional temporal logic (with operators P and Y) and basic modal logic K (with diamond operator)—as the analytical framework, with the key insight that TGNN computations naturally decompose into spatial and temporal components. For recursive TGNNs, the construction builds an MPNN whose hidden state is partitioned into three blocks (current, previous, and past satisfaction of subformulas), with temporal operators Y and P handled by dimension shifts and modal diamond handled by message-passing aggregation; this yields the full product logic PTL_{P,Y} × K. In contrast, time-and-graph TGNNs (which use two separate MPNNs plus a cell function) and global TGNNs (which use temporal message-passing with time functions) cannot express the full product logic because their architectures syntactically restrict how temporal and spatial operators can interact, and the paper identifies the precise restricted fragments L1 and L2 that each class can capture.

## Results

This is a purely theoretical paper with no experimental benchmarks. The key results are expressiveness theorems: recursive TGNNs (T_rec[Ĥ]) can express all properties in the full product logic PTL_{P,Y} × K (Theorem 6), while both time-and-graph TGNNs (Theorem 8) and global TGNNs (Theorem 10) strictly cannot. Each of the latter two architectures captures only a syntactically restricted fragment (L₁ and L₂ respectively) of PTL_{P,Y} × K. Corollary 12 establishes that recursive TGNNs are strictly more expressive than both other classes, and that time-and-graph and global TGNNs are incomparable in expressiveness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three TGNN architectures theoretically rather than empirically. Recursive TGNNs (T_rec) are shown to be strictly more expressive than both time-and-graph TGNNs and global TGNNs: the formula ◇(c₁ ∧ Y◇c₂) is expressible in PTL_{P,Y} × K (hence by T_rec) but not by any time-and-graph TGNN (Theorem 8), while Yc₁ is expressible by T_rec but not by any global TGNN (Theorem 10). Time-and-graph and global TGNNs are incomparable: each captures a different syntactic fragment (L₁ vs L₂) that the other cannot express. Theorem 13 further shows that every time-and-graph TGNN can be simulated by a recursive TGNN, confirming the strict hierarchy. The paper also extends the main result to stronger logics (K# and L-MP²) with correspondingly more expressive MPNN classes, showing the product-logic framework generalises beyond the base case.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the well-established programme of logical characterisation of static GNNs (Barceló et al. 2020, Grohe 2021, Nunn et al. 2024, Benedikt et al. 2024) to the temporal setting by introducing two-dimensional product logics (PTL × K) as the analytical framework. Prior work on TGNN expressiveness relied on Weisfeiler-Leman variants or direct architectural comparisons (Gao & Ribeiro 2022, Walega & Rawson 2025, Souza et al. 2022), but no logical characterisation existed. The paper also draws on the rich theory of multi-dimensional modal logics (Kurucz et al. 2003, Bennett et al. 2002) to formalise the interaction between spatial and temporal operators, and leverages inductive-capture results for K# and L-MP² to obtain analogous theorems for stronger static logics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to connect TGNNs with logical languages, providing a principled vocabulary for reasoning about what temporal graph properties different architectures can and cannot express. The finding that global TGNNs cannot even express the trivially simple property 'yesterday I had colour c₁' (Theorem 10) is a striking limitation that has practical implications for architecture selection. The paper opens clear future directions: tight expressive bounds, extension to more TGNN architectures, and transfer of product-logic complexity and finite-model-theory results to the neural setting. A key limitation is that the results are purely formal (expressiveness, not learnability) and cover only three snapshot-based TGNN classes.
