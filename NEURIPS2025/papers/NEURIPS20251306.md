## Problem

Existing medical LVLM benchmarks and instruction datasets focus on general-purpose clinical scenarios and overlook oral radiology entirely, leaving panoramic X-ray interpretation—a modality characterized by dense anatomical structures and fine-grained pathological cues—without tailored evaluation or training resources. This gap hampers the adaptation of large vision-language models to dentistry-specific tasks such as tooth-level diagnosis, treatment history recognition, and jawbone assessment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251306/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes panoramic X-ray images (20,563 images sourced from TED3 and the Hoang Viet Do dataset, spanning at least six countries) as visual input, paired with text questions or instruction prompts. Outputs are structured across four sub-datasets: grounding captions with per-structure coordinates, categories, and confidence scores; three-section medical reports (Teeth-Specific Observations, Jaw-Specific Observations, Clinical Summary & Recommendations); closed-ended multiple-choice and open-ended QA pairs mapped to five diagnostic dimensions (Teeth, Patho, HisT, Jaw, SumRec); and multi-turn patient-friendly dialogues. The benchmark (MMOral-Bench) evaluates on 100 curated images with 491 closed-ended and 578 open-ended questions, scored by accuracy and GPT-4-turbo few-shot rubric (0–1 scale) respectively.

## Method

The curation pipeline proceeds in four stages: (1) ten DINO/MaskDINO-based visual specialist models (average mAP50 of 81.25%) detect 49 anatomical structure categories with overlapping category spaces for cross-validation; (2) an eight-step post-processing pipeline deduplicates detections, assigns pathological findings to specific teeth via spatial relationships, and injects domain knowledge rules (e.g., recommending extraction of maxillary third molars lacking mandibular counterparts); (3) a two-stage LLM generation scheme produces medical reports—DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-70B generates initial reports from grounding captions, then GPT-4-turbo corrects them using 8 manually identified error rules, with 95.45% of reports successfully revised; (4) instruction data is generated via template-based perturbations for closed-ended QA and GPT-4-turbo prompting for open-ended QA and multi-turn chat. For evaluation, the benchmark is integrated into VLMEvalKit, and SFT experiments on Qwen2.5-VL-7B and LLaVA-Next-13B (via LLaMA-Factory, single epoch) demonstrate a 24.73% average score improvement on MMOral-Bench.

## Results

On MMOral-Bench (100 panoramic X-ray images, 491 closed-ended + 578 open-ended questions across 5 diagnostic dimensions), the best-performing model GPT-4o achieves only 43.31% average accuracy, with Ovis2-34B (41.24%) and Yi-Vision (39.96%) as the top open-source models. Among medical-specific LVLMs, HealthGPT-XL32 leads at 39.67%, still trailing general-purpose models. Supervised fine-tuning Qwen2.5-VL-7B on the full MMOral instruction data (Report + VQA + Chat) for one epoch yields a 24.73% absolute improvement on MMOral-Bench (from 21.46% to 46.19%), surpassing even GPT-4o's zero-shot score.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPT-4o leads all 64 evaluated models at 43.31% average, but the margin over the second-best (Gemini-2.0-Flash at 42.40%) is narrow, and all models remain far below clinical usability. Among open-source models, Ovis2-34B (41.24%) and Yi-Vision (39.96%) outperform all medical-specific LVLMs, with HealthGPT-XL32 (39.67%) and HuatuoGPT-V34B (38.99%) trailing by 1.6–2.3 points, confirming that current medical LVLMs offer no clear advantage over general-purpose models for dental tasks. LLaVA-Med performs worst among medical models (15.21%), with near-zero open-ended scores (4.76%), indicating severe domain mismatch. A consistent pattern across nearly all models is a substantial drop from closed-ended to open-ended performance (e.g., GPT-4o: 39.92% → 46.70% is an exception; most models drop 5–20 points), with 50.9% of open-source models scoring below 25% on open-ended VQA. The SFT experiments show that MMOral instruction data is particularly effective for open-ended generation: adding MMOral-Chat to the SFT mix boosts open-ended scores from 42.85% to 52.77% while slightly reducing closed-ended accuracy (46.20% → 39.60%), suggesting the dialogue data trades some discriminative precision for richer free-form generation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by introducing the first multimodal instruction dataset and benchmark specifically for panoramic X-ray interpretation, a domain entirely absent from existing medical VLM benchmarks (GMAI-MMBench, RadBench, OmniMedVQA) and specialized benchmarks (PathMMU, LMOD, CXPMRG-Bench). Methodologically, it repurposes established object detection and instance segmentation frameworks (DINO, MaskDINO) as 'visual specialist' models for anatomical structure extraction, and chains LLM-based generation (DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-70B for drafting, GPT-4-turbo for correction) to produce structured medical reports and instruction data. The evaluation and fine-tuning infrastructure follows standard tooling (VLMEvalKit, LLaMA-Factory), positioning the contribution squarely as a domain-specific resource rather than a methodological advance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Panoramic X-rays are among the most widely used dental imaging modalities, yet no prior VLM benchmark or instruction dataset addressed them, leaving a significant gap in medical AI evaluation. The finding that medical-specific LVLMs (LLaVA-Med, HuatuoGPT, HealthGPT) show no advantage over general-purpose models for dentistry challenges the assumption that domain-specific pretraining automatically transfers to new imaging subdomains. Key limitations include coverage of only one imaging modality (no periapical, intraoral, or CBCT images), reliance on LLM-generated instruction data that may propagate systematic biases, and a benchmark of only 100 images. Open questions include whether the SFT gains generalize to other dental modalities and whether the LLM-generated ground truth introduces errors that ceiling the achievable performance.
