## Problem

Agnostic learning of Boolean halfspaces is NP-hard in the worst case, and while smoothed analysis (Chandrasekaran et al., 2024) bypasses this hardness in continuous domains via Gaussian perturbations, no analogous framework existed for discrete domains like the Boolean hypercube where additive Gaussian noise is ill-defined. Prior positive results on the hypercube required strong structural assumptions (uniform, product, or permutation-invariant distributions), leaving a gap for general distributions with only mild tail bounds.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes i.i.d. labeled examples (x, y) with x ∈ {±1}^n drawn from a distribution whose x-marginal is (α, λ)-strictly sub-exponential, along with a smoothing parameter σ ∈ (0, 1/2) controlling the per-coordinate bit-flip probability and a target error ε. It outputs a hypothesis h: {±1}^n → {±1} that achieves error at most opt_σ + ε with high probability, where opt_σ is the best halfspace error after each input is independently bit-flipped with probability σ. The algorithm is improper (the hypothesis need not be a halfspace) and requires N = n^{poly((λ/(σε))^{(1+1/α)^3})} log(1/δ) samples.

## Method

The approach reduces smoothed agnostic learning to L1 polynomial regression by constructing, for each noise vector z, a low-degree polynomial p_z(x) approximating the smoothed halfspace T_{1-ρ}f_x(z) (the Bonami-Beckner noise operator applied to the target). A key rerandomization trick decomposes each biased hypercube coordinate into a mixture of the original bit and a uniform Radmacher variable, enabling conditional application of the Berry-Esseen theorem. A critical index decomposition then splits the weight vector into a 'head' of few dominant coordinates and a 'tail' that is α-regular: in the head-dominated case the function depends on O(K) coordinates and is exactly a low-degree polynomial, while in the regular-tail case the Boolean tail sum is approximated by a Gaussian, reducing to the continuous density-ratio method of Chandrasekaran et al. (2024). The resulting polynomial family has degree poly((λ/(σε))^{(1+1/α)^3}), and L1 regression over all monomials of that degree yields the final hypothesis.

## Results

The main result (Theorem 4.8) establishes that under a (α, λ)-strictly sub-exponential distribution on {±1}^n, there exists an algorithm with sample complexity N = n^{poly((λ/(σε))^{(1+1/α)^3})} log(1/δ) and runtime poly(n, N) that achieves error opt_σ + ε with probability at least 1−δ. This matches the complexity of the continuous-domain result of Chandrasekaran et al. (2024) but is the first such guarantee for the Boolean hypercube. The polynomial degree of the approximator is O((Cσ^{-1/2} λ log(1/ε)/ε)^{6(1+1/α)^3}), and the algorithm is improper (outputs a thresholded polynomial rather than a halfspace).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is with Chandrasekaran et al. (2024), which achieves the identical complexity n^{poly((λ/(σε))(1+1/α)^3)} but only for the continuous domain R^n; this paper extends that guarantee to the Boolean hypercube {±1}^n, a setting where additive Gaussian perturbations are ill-defined. Against prior Boolean-hypercube results (Kalai et al. 2008, Wimmer 2010), this paper relaxes the distributional assumption from uniform/product/permutation-invariant to strictly sub-exponential, a substantial generalization, while also switching from the worst-case benchmark opt to the smoothed benchmark opt_σ. Compared to Kane et al. (2013), which handles sub-exponential distributions on R^n with input-noise smoothing, this paper achieves a dramatically better complexity (polynomial exponent vs. exponential-in-log-log) and uses concept-noise smoothing instead. The key trade-off is that the smoothed benchmark opt_σ is weaker than opt (the learner only needs to match the best halfspace under bit-flip perturbations), which is what circumvents the NP-hardness of worst-case agnostic learning on the hypercube.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the smoothed agnostic learning framework of Chandrasekaran et al. (2024), which operates in continuous domains with additive Gaussian perturbations, to the Boolean hypercube where such perturbations are ill-defined. The key technical departure is replacing Gaussian smoothing with bit-flip noise and the Bonami-Beckner noise operator, then bridging the discrete-to-continuous gap via a rerandomization trick that exposes uniform Radmacher variables amenable to Berry-Esseen approximation. The critical index decomposition (from Servedio 2006, Diakonikolas et al. 2010) handles irregular weight vectors, while the L1 polynomial regression reduction (Kalai et al. 2008) and density ratio method (Chandrasekaran et al. 2024) provide the final polynomial approximators. Prior discrete results required strong structural assumptions (uniform, product, or permutation-invariant distributions); this work relaxes those to strictly sub-exponential marginals.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first computationally efficient guarantee for smoothed agnostic learning of halfspaces on the Boolean hypercube, closing a gap between worst-case NP-hardness and practical learnability in discrete settings. The strictly sub-exponential assumption is notably weak, suggesting the smoothed framework may extend to even heavier-tailed distributions. Key open questions include whether the approach generalizes to intersections of multiple halfspaces (where different weight vectors induce incompatible coordinate orderings), whether comparable guarantees hold under weaker tail conditions, and how the framework connects to smoothed input-distribution learning in the Boolean setting. The polynomial degree and runtime degrade as the smoothing parameter σ → 0, limiting applicability to very small noise regimes.
