## Problem

Diffusion models exhibit a widely observed but poorly understood phenomenon: the quality of their learned representations follows a unimodal curve across noise levels, peaking at an intermediate timestep. No prior work provides a theoretical explanation for why this unimodal pattern emerges or connects it to the model's ability to capture the underlying data distribution versus merely memorizing training samples.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-trained diffusion model (e.g., EDM or DDPM U-Net) and evaluates its internal feature extractor h_θ(x_t, t) at multiple noise levels t, using both noisy inputs x_t and clean inputs x_0. The primary output is a Signal-to-Noise Ratio (SNR) curve across timesteps, where the numerator captures energy aligned with the true class subspace and the denominator captures residual energy in irrelevant directions. Downstream, this SNR curve is compared against linear probing accuracy to characterize whether the dynamics are unimodal (indicating generalization) or monotonically decreasing (indicating memorization).

## Method

The paper introduces a theoretical framework built on a K-class noisy Mixture of Low-Rank Gaussians (MoLRG) data model, where each class occupies a low-dimensional subspace perturbed by class-irrelevant noise. A simplified U-Net-like network with a blockwise mixture-of-experts mechanism is parameterized to be analytically tractable, and representation quality is quantified via an SNR metric within the target class subspace. The key theoretical result (Theorem 1) shows that the unimodal SNR curve arises from a trade-off: as noise σ_t increases, the denoising rate σ_t²/δ² grows monotonically while the positive class confidence rate h(ŵ_t⁺, δ) decays, producing a peak at an intermediate timestep where class-irrelevant components are maximally suppressed while class-relevant features remain. Empirically, the paper demonstrates that this unimodal pattern reliably signals generalization: it emerges when the model captures the low-dimensional data distribution and progressively vanishes into a monotonic curve as the model transitions to memorizing training data, validated across dataset sizes, model capacities, and training iterations on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, TinyImageNet, and Oxford-IIIT Pet.

## Results

The paper's primary result is theoretical: under a noisy MoLRG data model, the SNR of the optimal denoising autoencoder is provably unimodal across noise levels (Theorem 1), with the peak arising from a trade-off between denoising rate σ_t²/δ² and class confidence rate h(ŵ_t⁺, δ). Empirically, this unimodal SNR pattern aligns closely with linear probing accuracy on CIFAR-10 and TinyImageNet (Figures 3, 5), and the unimodal-to-monotonic transition in representation dynamics reliably tracks the generalization-to-memorization phase transition as training data size decreases (Figure 6) or training progresses (Figure 8). In the supplementary ensemble experiments (Table 1), DiT(Ensemble) achieves 78.4% clean MiniImageNet accuracy, outperforming MAE (73.7%), EDM (67.2%), and DiT (77.6%), with the gap widening under 80% label noise (56.3% vs. 51.5% for MAE).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three pre-trained representation learners—MAE [95], EDM [49], and DiT [37]—alongside their multi-timestep ensemble variants, all evaluated on MiniImageNet under increasing label noise. DiT(Ensemble) dominates across all noise levels, achieving 78.4% clean accuracy and 56.3% at 80% label noise, outperforming DiT alone by 0.8 and 9.0 points respectively. MAE, a non-diffusion self-supervised baseline, achieves 73.7% clean accuracy but degrades more sharply under noise (51.5% at 80%), trailing DiT(Ensemble) by 6.8 points. Vanilla EDM lags behind both MAE and DiT in clean accuracy (67.2%), but its ensemble variant recovers to 72.0%, closing most of the gap with MAE. The consistent advantage of ensemble (weight-sharing across timesteps) over single-timestep DAEs supports the paper's hypothesis that diffusion models' cross-noise-level parameter sharing creates an implicit ensemble effect that enhances representation robustness.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the MoLRG data model from Wang et al. (2024) and the score-based SDE framework of Song et al. (2021) to provide the first theoretical explanation for the unimodal representation dynamics observed empirically in diffusion-based representation learning [19, 20, 23]. It departs from prior theoretical work by Han et al. (2024), which analyzed optimization dynamics of a two-layer CNN under diffusion loss but did not characterize how representation quality evolves across timesteps, and from Yue et al. (2024), which studied timestep effects for attribute classification without a formal unimodality explanation. The network parameterization draws on U-Net's encoder-decoder structure and mixture-of-experts mechanisms, while the DAE training objective follows Xiang et al. (2023).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a formal SNR-based metric and a clean theoretical account (via the interplay of denoising rate and class confidence) for a phenomenon that was widely observed but previously unexplained, giving practitioners a principled tool for selecting optimal timesteps in diffusion-based representation learning. The empirical finding that unimodal dynamics reliably signal generalization while monotonic dynamics signal memorization offers a practical early-stopping criterion that does not require external reference models. Key limitations include the reliance on the simplified MoLRG data model and a shallow U-Net-like architecture; extending the framework to realistic high-dimensional distributions and deep architectures, and establishing a rigorous link between representation geometry and the generalization-memorization phase transition, remain important open directions.
