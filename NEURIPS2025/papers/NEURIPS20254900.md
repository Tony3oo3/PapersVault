## Problem

Consistency models can distill diffusion and flow-based generators into efficient one-step samplers, but they inherently degrade in quality as the number of sampling steps increases—a limitation the paper proves analytically via error accumulation in multi-step denoising. Flow maps, which connect any two noise levels in a single step, remain effective across all step counts, yet prior flow map formulations have not achieved high-performance image generation. The paper addresses how to scale continuous-time flow map distillation to state-of-the-art few-step generation quality.

## Input / Output

The model takes Gaussian noise (optionally conditioned on a class label for ImageNet or a text prompt for text-to-image) and produces a generated image in 1–8 sampling steps. For ImageNet 512×512, generation occurs in latent space using a 280M-parameter EDM2-S backbone; for text-to-image, a FLUX.1 [dev] base model is adapted via LoRA. The flow map f_θ(x_t, t, s) is parameterized with c_skip=1 and c_out=(s−t), aligning with an Euler ODE solver, and at inference uses a γ-sampling algorithm for stochastic multi-step traversal between arbitrary noise levels.

## Method

AYF introduces two continuous-time distillation objectives for flow maps: AYF-EMD (Eulerian), which enforces consistency of the output as (x_t, t) moves along the teacher's PF-ODE for a fixed target s, and AYF-LMD (Lagrangian), which enforces consistency at the target timestep s. The EMD loss generalizes both the continuous-time consistency loss (when s=0) and the standard flow matching loss (as s→t). To sharpen the distilled distribution, the paper is the first to distill an autoguided teacher—combining a strong model with a weaker checkpoint via a guidance scale λ—rather than relying on adversarial objectives during main training. Stabilization techniques include tangent normalization, a modified linear time embedding (c_noise=t) to avoid log-singularities in the flow matching parameterization, and a clamped tangent warmup (r_max=0.99) that retains a residual flow-matching regularization term. An optional short adversarial finetuning stage on top of the pretrained AYF model further boosts 1-step quality with minimal diversity loss.

## Results

On ImageNet 64×64, AYF (non-adversarial) achieves FID 1.25 at 2 steps and FID 1.12 at 8 steps, outperforming the best prior non-adversarial distillation method sCD (FID 1.66 at 2 steps). With a short adversarial finetuning stage, AYF+adv reaches FID 1.10 at 4 steps, surpassing the best single-step adversarial baseline SiDA (FID 1.11 at 1 step). On ImageNet 512×512, the small AYF-S model (280M params) achieves FID 1.87 at 2 steps, matching the 1.5B-parameter sCD-XXL (FID 1.88 at 2 steps) while using only 18% of the compute. For text-to-image, AYF distilled from FLUX.1 via LoRA in ~32 GPU hours outperforms LCM and TCD in a user study and matches the commercially distilled FLUX.1[schnell] at 4 steps.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among non-adversarial distillation methods, AYF decisively outperforms sCD (FID 1.25 vs 1.66 at 2 steps) and MultiStep-CD (FID 1.25 vs 1.90 at 2 steps) on ImageNet 64×64, and on ImageNet 512×512 the small AYF-S model (280M) matches the 5.4× larger sCD-XXL (1.5B) at 2 steps (FID 1.87 vs 1.88) while being over 2× faster in wall-clock time. AYF's 1-step FID (2.98) is slightly worse than sCD's (2.44), reflecting the harder flow-map learning task, but this gap closes rapidly with additional steps. With adversarial finetuning, AYF+adv surpasses all single-step adversarial baselines at 4 steps: FID 1.10 beats SiDA (1.11), GDD-I (1.16), and DMD2 (1.28), while maintaining higher recall (0.65 vs 0.60–0.62). Against the teacher EDM2+Autoguidance (FID 1.01 at 63 NFE), AYF+adv at 8 steps (FID 1.07) approaches teacher quality with ~8× fewer function evaluations. For text-to-image, AYF (FLUX.1-based, 4 steps) is preferred over LCM and TCD (SDXL-based) in a user study and matches the adversarially trained FLUX.1[schnell] in perceived quality without any adversarial loss during distillation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions flow maps as a strict generalization of both consistency models (s=0) and flow matching (s→t), building directly on the CTM/Flow Map Matching formulation of Kim et al. [38] and Boffi et al. [5]. Its key conceptual departure is proving that consistency models are inherently incompatible with multi-step sampling (Theorem 3.1), thereby motivating the flow map framework as the correct abstraction. The training objectives (AYF-EMD and AYF-LMD) are novel continuous-time losses that subsume both the sCM consistency loss and the standard flow matching loss, while the stabilization techniques (tangent normalization, clamped warmup, modified time embeddings) are adapted from Lu & Song [49]. A distinctive practical contribution is the first use of autoguidance [35] as a distillation teacher signal, replacing the more common CFG-based distillation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The proof that consistency models degrade with more steps is a fundamental result that reframes the few-step generation landscape: flow maps are the only formulation that remains effective across all step counts, making them the natural target for distillation. Practically, AYF achieves SOTA few-step FID on ImageNet 64/512 with small networks (280M params), where 4-step sampling is as fast as prior 1-step methods, and extends to text-to-image via efficient LoRA distillation of FLUX.1. Open questions include closing the remaining gap to the multi-step teacher (likely requiring more model capacity), whether direct flow map training without a teacher is viable, and extension to video or other modalities.
