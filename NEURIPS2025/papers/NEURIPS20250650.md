## Problem

Current Medical Large Multimodal Models for chest X-ray interpretation operate at the image level with single-step predictions, lacking the ability to focus on specific anatomical regions and perform multi-step clinical reasoning. This leads to missed subtle findings, hallucinations, and poor interpretability, since radiologists naturally scan globally then examine specific regions with structured reasoning. The paper addresses both gaps by introducing anatomy-centric, region-level multi-step reasoning into MLMMs.

## Input / Output

The model accepts a 336×336 chest X-ray image, a textual question or instruction, and optionally a visual ROI crop specified by normalized bounding box coordinates. During VQA, the model generates chain-of-thought answers in a triplet format per region (region name, coordinates, ROI visual feature), dynamically activating the region encoder mid-generation once coordinate tokens are emitted. For report generation, it outputs either a full-image radiology report or a single sentence strictly aligned to a user-specified anatomical region.

## Method

AOR comprises a CLIP-ViT-L/14 image encoder, a region encoder that builds a hierarchical feature pyramid from four encoder layers and applies RoIAlign to extract 14×14 multi-scale region features, and a LLaVA-1.5 LLM that jointly models projected image, region, and text tokens. Training proceeds in three stages: (1) region recognition aligning region features with anatomical names, (2) region grounding for bidirectional name-to-coordinate and coordinate-to-name mapping, and (3) instruction tuning on the AOR-Instruction dataset (290k VQA + 532k report pairs). The key data contribution is a clinically grounded CoT construction pipeline using three anatomical ontologies—hierarchical object relationships (38 regions), causal attribute relationships (68 attributes), and restrictive object-attribute constraints—yielding 2,812 CoT templates reviewed by three expert physicians, which guide the model to reason from whole to parts, link findings to diseases, and eliminate anatomically impossible scenarios.

## Results

On MIMIC-CXR-VQA, AOR-t achieves 80.48% (verify), 71.96% (choose), and 65.05% F1 (query), outperforming the second-best medical LMM (LLaVA-Med) by an average of 6.98%. On zero-shot transfer to CheXpert, AOR-t reaches 71.58% closed and 53.85% open accuracy, surpassing all baselines. For full-image report generation on MIMIC-CXR, AOR-r/t achieves 25.38 ROUGE-L, 83.95 BERTScore, and 48.28 F1-CheXbert; for region report generation, it reaches 35.62 ROUGE-L and 84.76 BERTScore, substantially outperforming prior methods (e.g., LLaVA-Med at 9.47 ROUGE-L for region reports).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AOR-t dominates all medical-domain baselines on MIMIC-CXR-VQA: it surpasses LLaVA-Med (75.71/58.31/60.37) by 4.77/13.65/4.68 points on verify/choose/query, and outperforms CheXagent (75.02/33.49/48.49) by 5.46/38.47/16.56 points, with the largest gap on the complex 'choose' type. On zero-shot CheXpert, AOR-t achieves 71.58% closed accuracy, beating the next-best CheXagent (62.28%) by 9.3 points and XrayGPT (60.59%) by 11.0 points. On VQA-RAD, CheXagent leads in closed accuracy (68.49 vs. 63.01 for AOR-t), but AOR-t still wins on open-ended questions (28.19 vs. 24.94). For report generation (Table 2), AOR-r/t improves F1-CheXbert from 46.20 (LLaVA-Rad) to 48.28 on full-image reports and from 33.02 (CheXagent) to 36.89 on region reports, while ROUGE-L jumps from 20.79 to 35.62 for region-level generation. General-domain LMMs (LLaVA, LLaVA-1.5, GPT4RoI, VoCoT) are consistently outperformed across all three VQA benchmarks, confirming the advantage of anatomy-centric multi-step reasoning over image-level single-step prediction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) medical LMMs like LLaVA-Med and CheXagent that perform image-level VQA and report generation but lack explicit region-level perception; (2) region-aware LMMs such as GPT4RoI and RegionGPT that incorporate ROI features but operate in general domains without medical grounding; and (3) CoT-augmented medical VLMs (MedCoT, MedVLM-R1, X-Reasoner) that introduce reasoning steps but rely on generic or RL-generated chains without domain-specific structure. AOR departs from all three by encoding three types of anatomical ontologies (hierarchical, causal, restrictive) directly into the CoT construction pipeline and by activating a region encoder mid-generation to inject multi-scale ROI visual features, creating a tightly coupled text-visual reasoning loop specific to CXR anatomy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The AOR-Instruction dataset (290k VQA + 532k report pairs with expert-validated CoT) addresses a genuine gap in high-quality, clinically grounded multimodal reasoning data for medical VLMs, and the region report generation sub-task (generating a sentence for a user-specified anatomical region) has no direct prior equivalent. However, the approach is tightly coupled to CXR and to the Chest ImaGenome bounding box annotations; generalizing the ontology construction to other modalities (CT, MRI) or to settings without pre-annotated anatomical boxes remains open. The manual, expert-driven ontology design (38 objects, 68 attributes, three relationship types) is a scalability bottleneck that limits immediate transfer to new anatomical domains.
