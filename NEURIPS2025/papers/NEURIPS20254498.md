## Problem

Contrastive self-supervised learning has achieved strong performance but its geometric foundations remain largely underexplored. Existing methods treat embeddings as isolated points with pairwise losses, ignoring the structured geometry of augmentation sub-manifolds and their interactions in representation space. This paper asks whether recasting representation learning as a manifold packing problem—drawing on physics of short-range repulsive particle systems—can yield a simpler, more interpretable loss that naturally enforces both positive-pair similarity and negative-pair separability.

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10, 224×224 for ImageNet) and produces m augmented views per image via asymmetric augmentation pipelines (random crop, color jitter, Gaussian blur, etc.). The backbone encoder outputs a representation vector per view, which is projected by an MLP head onto a unit hypersphere; the projection head is discarded after pretraining, leaving only the frozen backbone features for downstream linear probing, semi-supervised fine-tuning, or object detection. Each image's m augmented embeddings collectively form an ellipsoidal sub-manifold whose centroid, covariance, and effective radius are computed per batch.

## Method

CLAMP approximates each image's augmentation sub-manifold as a high-dimensional ellipsoid defined by the mean and covariance of its m projected embeddings, with an effective radius r_i = r_s·√(Tr(Λ_i)/m). The loss is a single-term, short-range repulsive potential: for every pair of sub-manifolds whose centroids are closer than the sum of their radii, the loss accumulates (1 − ‖Z_i−Z_j‖/(r_i+r_j))², and is zero otherwise; the log of this overlap sum is minimized. This design simultaneously shrinks sub-manifold sizes (pulling positive views together) and increases inter-centroid distances (pushing different images apart) without requiring separate alignment and uniformity terms or global all-pairs interactions. The architecture uses a ResNet backbone with an MLP projection head, trained with the LARS optimizer and cosine-decay schedule, and the short-range nature of the potential means each sub-manifold interacts only with its local neighbors in embedding space, reducing computational cost relative to global repulsion schemes.

## Results

Under the standard linear evaluation protocol (frozen backbone + linear probe), CLAMP with 4 views achieves 85.12% top-1 accuracy on ImageNet-100, setting a new state of the art among the compared SSL methods, and 69.50% on ImageNet-1K (70.04% with 8 views). In semi-supervised fine-tuning on ImageNet-1K, CLAMP reaches 47.87% (1% labels) and 65.96% (10% labels) with 8 views. For object detection on VOC2007, CLAMP attains 55.7 mAP, outperforming all SSL baselines including BYOL (55.6) and SimCLR (54.4).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On ImageNet-100 linear evaluation, CLAMP (85.12%) decisively outperforms all baselines, with the closest competitor MMCR at 82.88% and SimSiam at 81.6%, a margin of over 2 points. On ImageNet-1K, CLAMP (70.04% with 8 views) trails MMCR (71.5%) by ~1.5 points but edges out BYOL (69.3%), CorInfoMax (69.08%), and Barlow Twins (68.7%). In semi-supervised learning, CLAMP is competitive but does not lead: at 1% labels it reaches 47.87% versus BYOL/SwAV at 49.8% and MMCR at 49.4%; at 10% labels it achieves 65.96%, nearly matching MMCR (66.0%) and BYOL (65.0%) but below SwAV (66.9%). For object detection on VOC2007, CLAMP achieves the best mAP (55.7) and AP75 (62.4), narrowly beating BYOL (55.6 mAP, 62.0 AP75) and clearly surpassing SimCLR (54.4), MoCo-V2 (54.7), and MMCR (54.6). On Brain-Score, CLAMP attains the highest scores in V2 (0.336) and IT (0.570) among all compared methods, but trails Barlow Twins in V1 (0.258 vs 0.276) and BYOL in V4 (0.558 vs 0.585). On CIFAR-10, CLAMP (90.21%) is competitive but below BYOL (92.58%) and Barlow Twins (92.10%), while exceeding SwAV (89.17%) and DeepCluster V2 (88.85%).

## Relation to Prior Work

CLAMP departs from the dominant point-wise contrastive learning paradigm (SimCLR, BYOL, MoCo, SwAV) by treating each image's augmented views as a geometric sub-manifold rather than a single embedding vector, and by replacing the InfoNCE or alignment-uniformity loss with a single short-range repulsive potential inspired by jammed-packing physics. It is most directly comparable to Maximum Manifold Capacity Representations (MMCR), which also operates on augmentation sub-manifolds, but CLAMP uses local (short-range) interactions rather than global SVD-based capacity maximization, and avoids the need for a momentum encoder. The framework also draws on the neuroscience literature on neural manifolds (Cunningham & Yu, Stringer et al.) to motivate the geometric interpretation, and on random-organization and hyperuniformity physics (Corte, Wilken, Hexner & Levine) to justify the repulsive-particle loss design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a unifying geometric lens—manifold packing—that simultaneously explains alignment, uniformity, and collapse avoidance through a single physics-grounded term, and it demonstrates that the learned eigenspectrum (λ ∝ n^{-1.013}) closely matches the power-law decay observed in mouse V1 (α ≈ 1.04), lending biological plausibility to the self-organization story. Open questions include whether hierarchical class manifolds emerge during training, whether local Hebbian-style update rules can replicate the packing dynamics without backpropagation, and whether incorporating sub-manifold orientation could enable denser packing and further accuracy gains. The weak dependence on batch size (unlike SimCLR) is a practical advantage, though the overall performance gains over strong baselines are modest, and the Brain-Score comparison relies on linear readout that may understate non-linear correspondences with cortex.
