## Problem

Physiological emotion recognition from EDA and PPG signals lacks a standardized, large-scale evaluation framework: the 19 publicly available datasets differ in experimental protocol, sensor hardware, and labeling strategy, creating domain shifts that prevent models trained on one dataset from generalizing to others. No prior work has systematically benchmarked multiple modeling paradigms across this heterogeneous collection or quantified how specific dimensions of heterogeneity (setting, device, labeling) affect cross-dataset transfer.

## Input / Output

Input consists of per-participant, per-task signal segments (or hourly segments for real-world data) of EDA and PPG, each z-score normalized within the participant; handcrafted statistical features are extracted separately per modality, min-max normalized, and concatenated for the combined modality. For CLSP fine-tuning, the model additionally consumes enriched emotion-specific textual descriptions processed through a text encoder, with input-conditional context tokens generated by lightweight Meta-Net modulation networks. Output is a binary arousal label (high/low), binary valence label (positive/negative), or a four-class circumplex label (HAPV, HANV, LAPV, LANV) assigned via a unified binning strategy that maps stimulus metadata, self-reports, or expert annotations onto the arousal-valence plane.

## Method

FEEL benchmarks 16 architectures across four paradigms—traditional ML (RF, LDA), deep learning on handcrafted features (MLP, ResNet, LSTM, Attention), deep learning on raw signal windows (ResNet, LSTM+MLP, CNN+Transformer), and pretrained CLSP models—using LOSO-CV for within-dataset evaluation and leave-one-dataset-out for cross-dataset transfer. The key technical novelty is a CoCoOp-based fine-tuning strategy for CLSP that conditions textual prompts on physiological signal embeddings via two MetaNet-inspired modulation networks (linear or 1D-convolutional variants), enabling adaptation to target datasets without requiring ground-truth text annotations. Cross-dataset generalization is then dissected along three harmonization axes—experimental setting (lab/constraint/real), device type (E4/custom/lab-based), and labeling method (stimulus/self-report/expert)—with supplementary demographic transfer experiments on gender and age. The framework further introduces a unified four-class circumplex binning and a data-quality meta-analysis to attribute performance variability to specific collection-pipeline factors.

## Results

Across 19 datasets and 3 modalities (EDA, PPG, EDA+PPG), fine-tuned CLSP models achieve the highest F1 in 71 of 114 binary classification instances, while RF (17), LDA (8), and HC+MLP (11) remain competitive, collectively accounting for 36 top performers. Models using handcrafted features dominate 107/114 best-performing cases over raw-signal deep learning models (only 3 top instances). Binary F1 ranges from 0.30 (CASE, ADARP) to 0.98 (WESAD EDA+PPG), with EDA alone achieving top performance in 12/19 datasets for arousal and 13/19 for valence. Cross-dataset transfer is strong: real-life-trained models reach F1=0.79 on lab data and F1=0.78 on constraint data; lab-device-trained models transfer to custom wearables at F1=0.81 and to Empatica E4 at F1=0.73.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper benchmarks four modeling paradigms against each other across 19 datasets. CLSP pretrained models (zero-shot and fine-tuned at 5%/25%/50%) win 71/114 top-performing slots, with CLSP+CNN contributing 37 and CLSP+MLP contributing 22; zero-shot CLSP alone tops 14 datasets without any fine-tuning. Classical ML (RF: 17, LDA: 8) and handcrafted-feature DL (HC+MLP: 11, HC+Attention: 2) collectively capture 36 top slots, showing that simpler models remain highly competitive, especially on well-structured lab datasets (e.g., RF wins ForDigitStress at F1=0.99, Unobtrusive at F1=0.88). Raw-signal deep learning models (ResNet, LSTM, CNN+Transformer) are the weakest paradigm, securing only 3 top instances (WESAD EDA, MAUS EDA, CASE EDA), confirming that handcrafted features (107/114 wins) substantially outperform end-to-end signal learning in this low-resource, noisy setting. Within CLSP variants, fine-tuning with a CNN Meta-Net consistently outperforms the MLP Meta-Net, and 21 instances achieve competitive results with only 5% fine-tuning data, demonstrating strong few-shot transferability of the pretrained representations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the CLSP contrastive language-signal pretraining framework (Singh et al., 2024) and adapts CoCoOp conditional prompt learning (Zhou et al., 2022) to physiological signals, proposing a novel fine-tuning strategy that enables CLSP models to operate on datasets lacking textual annotations. Conceptually, it departs from prior single-dataset evaluations (Han et al., 2024; Zhang et al., 2024) by unifying 19 heterogeneous datasets under a common binning and preprocessing protocol, then systematically analyzing cross-dataset transferability along three harmonization dimensions (experimental setting, device type, labeling method). The paper positions itself as the first large-scale, multi-dataset benchmark for physiological emotion recognition, filling a gap where prior work treated each dataset in isolation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FEEL provides the field's first unified evaluation framework spanning 19 datasets, 16 architectures, and four modeling paradigms, offering practitioners concrete guidance on model selection under data scarcity and heterogeneity. The finding that handcrafted features outperform raw-signal deep learning in 107/114 cases challenges the prevailing assumption that end-to-end deep learning is always superior in physiological signal settings. Key open questions remain: whether the harmonization strategy (binary binning of arousal/valence) adequately captures emotional nuance, how findings extend beyond EDA/PPG to other modalities, and whether the cross-dataset transferability patterns hold as datasets grow in scale and diversity. The paper also acknowledges gaps in accounting for cultural context, health status, and more advanced domain-specific architectures.
