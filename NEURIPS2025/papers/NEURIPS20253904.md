## Problem

Existing RLVR (Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards) methods for training large reasoning models are inherently on-policy, meaning the model can only learn from its own self-generated outputs through iterative trial-and-error. This fundamentally bounds learning to amplifying behaviors the base model already possesses, preventing acquisition of genuinely novel reasoning capabilities. The paper asks: how can we empower LLMs to acquire reasoning behaviors that surpass their initial cognitive boundaries?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253904/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a math problem as a text prompt and produces a structured chain-of-thought reasoning trace followed by a final answer enclosed in \boxed{}. During training, each prompt is paired with 1 off-policy reasoning trace (generated by DeepSeek-R1) and 7 on-policy rollouts from the model itself, with a verifiable reward (binary correct/incorrect) computed via Math-Verify on the extracted answer. The off-policy traces are filtered to those under 8192 tokens and verified correct, yielding 45k training prompts from OpenR1-Math-220k.

## Method

LUFFY extends GRPO to Mixed-Policy GRPO by directly incorporating off-policy reasoning traces into the group of on-policy rollouts for advantage computation, so that high-reward off-policy traces naturally receive higher advantage when the model's own rollouts fail, creating a dynamic imitation-exploration balance. An importance sampling ratio π_θ/π_φ calibrates the off-policy gradient, with π_φ set to 1 for practical efficiency (avoiding tokenization mismatches and enabling use of off-the-shelf datasets). To counteract entropy collapse caused by rapid convergence toward high-probability off-policy tokens, LUFFY introduces policy shaping via a regularized importance sampling function f(x) = x/(x+γ) with γ=0.1, which re-weights gradients to emphasize low-probability yet crucial actions. The paper provides a convergence guarantee of O(1/√K) for the importance-weighted policy gradient estimator under bounded importance weights, and empirically demonstrates that LUFFY trains weak models (e.g., LLaMA-3.1-8B) on hard problems where on-policy RLVR completely fails.

## Results

On six in-distribution math benchmarks (AIME 24/25, AMC, MATH-500, Minerva, OlympiadBench), LUFFY achieves an average score of 50.1, outperforming the best prior RLVR method (Oat-Zero, 43.7) by +6.4 points and the authors' own On-Policy RL replication (45.5) by +4.6 points. On three out-of-distribution benchmarks (ARC-c, GPQA-diamond, MMLU-Pro), LUFFY reaches 57.8 average, surpassing the best RLVR baseline (OpenReasoner-Zero, 51.6) by +6.2 points. With extended training (LUFFY†), the OOD average further improves to 61.8. LUFFY also successfully trains LLaMA-3.1-8B on hard math problems where on-policy RLVR completely fails (reward collapses to zero).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LUFFY outperforms all four prior RLVR baselines across every benchmark. Against the strongest RLVR method, Oat-Zero (43.7 in-distribution, 45.2 OOD), LUFFY gains +6.4 and +12.6 points respectively. Against OpenReasoner-Zero (41.0, 51.6), the gains are +9.1 in-distribution and +6.2 OOD. Against PRIME-Zero (40.7, 41.4), LUFFY leads by +9.4 and +16.4 points. Against SimpleRL-Zero (37.4, 29.3), the margins are the largest at +12.7 and +28.5 points. LUFFY also beats the authors' own On-Policy RL replication (45.5, 57.3) by +4.6 in-distribution and +0.5 OOD, and surpasses the strongest off-policy alternative SFT+RL (48.2, 44.8) by +1.9 in-distribution and +13.0 OOD while using only 59% of the GPU hours.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends GRPO (DeepSeekMath) into a Mixed-Policy GRPO framework by incorporating off-policy reasoning traces from a stronger model (DeepSeek-R1) into the advantage computation and policy gradient, using importance sampling to calibrate cross-distribution gradients. It departs from the standard RLVR paradigm—which is inherently on-policy and thus bounded by the base model's existing capabilities—by treating off-policy demonstrations as a form of cognitive scaffolding rather than mere SFT targets. The policy shaping via regularized importance sampling (f(x) = x/(x+γ)) is a novel regularization that addresses entropy collapse specific to mixed-policy training, drawing on variance-reduction ideas from off-policy RL (DQN, SAC) but applied in a new context for LLM policy optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The most practically significant finding is that LUFFY successfully trains weak models (LLaMA-3.1-8B) on hard problems where on-policy RLVR completely fails (rewards collapse to zero), suggesting off-policy guidance can break through fundamental capability ceilings that self-play RL cannot. This opens a pathway for bootstrapping reasoning in models that lack the foundational cognitive behaviors needed for on-policy exploration. Key limitations constrain broader impact: the method is validated only on math reasoning with verifiable rewards, on models ≤8B, and with a single off-policy trajectory per question. Open questions include scaling to larger models, extending to non-verifiable-reward tasks, and whether multiple teachers or trajectories yield further gains.
