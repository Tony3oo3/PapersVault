## Problem

Benign overfitting—where a model perfectly fits noisy training data yet generalizes well—has been extensively characterized for linear models, CNNs, and fully-connected networks, but the overfitting behavior of the attention mechanism, the fundamental building block of Transformers, remained unexplored. This paper asks: under what conditions does a single-head softmax attention model exhibit benign overfitting in a classification setting with label-flipping noise, and what is the precise role of the signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) in determining whether overfitting is benign or harmful?

## Input / Output

Each input is a sequence of T tokens in R^d, where one token is a fixed signal vector (μ₁ or μ₂, orthogonal, norm ρ) and the remaining T−1 tokens are i.i.d. Gaussian noise vectors orthogonal to the signal. The model appends a fixed query token q = e₁ to the sequence and produces a scalar logit via f(X; v, p) = vᵀXᵀS(Xp), whose sign gives the binary prediction. The critical parameter is the SNR = ρ/√d, which the paper shows must scale as Ω(1/√n) for benign overfitting, where n is the training set size.

## Method

The paper analyzes the single-head attention model f(X; v, p) = vᵀXᵀS(Xp) (following Ataee Tarzanagh et al.), where v is the attention head and p is the attention-weight vector, trained with logistic loss via gradient descent from zero initialization. The key result (Theorem 3.3) proves that after just two GD iterations, the model interpolates all training data while achieving test error approaching the Bayes rate η, provided SNR = Ω(1/√n). The mechanism is a token-selection effect: after two steps, the softmax assigns high probability to the signal token for clean examples (enabling generalization) and to noise tokens for label-flipped examples (enabling interpolation). The paper further shows that min-norm/max-margin interpolators (Theorems 4.2, 4.4) achieve the same benign behavior at the same SNR threshold, and proves the threshold is tight (Theorem 4.6): below SNR = O(1/√n), the min-norm interpolator exhibits harmful overfitting with constant test error.

## Results

The paper proves that a single-head softmax attention model trained with gradient descent and logistic loss achieves benign overfitting after just two iterations when the signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) is Ω(1/√n), where n is the number of training samples. The min-norm (max-margin) interpolator also exhibits benign overfitting under the same SNR threshold, with test error approaching the Bayes error η as d→∞. Crucially, Theorem 4.6 establishes that this SNR bound is tight: when SNR ≤ O(1/√n), the min-norm interpolator suffers harmful overfitting with test error at least 1/16. Empirically, a heatmap (Figure 1) across SNR and sample sizes validates the critical threshold SNR² ≈ 2.1/n, confirming the theoretical prediction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper that does not benchmark against competing methods in the standard sense; instead, it establishes necessary and sufficient conditions for benign overfitting in a specific attention architecture. The closest prior work is Sakamoto and Sato [26], who also study benign overfitting in token selection but require a restrictive assumption that the angle between a fixed attention head and the signal is bounded below by a constant — a condition that fails with exponentially high probability for Gaussian-initialized heads in high dimensions. In contrast, this paper optimizes both the attention head v and the softmax weights p, showing that they learn distinct patterns (signal tokens for clean examples, noise tokens for noisy examples) without such assumptions. The paper also differs from Jiang et al. [25], who study a simplified transformer but do not allow label-flipping noise, which is essential for understanding whether interpolation is compatible with generalization. The tightness result (Theorem 4.6) provides a clear separation: below the Ω(1/√n) SNR threshold, no min-norm interpolator can generalize, making this a fundamental limit rather than an artifact of the optimization procedure.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the benign overfitting literature—which has been thoroughly studied for linear models, convolutional networks, and fully-connected networks—to the single-head softmax attention mechanism, the fundamental building block of Transformers. The model formulation and parameterization (v, p) are directly inherited from Ataee Tarzanagh et al. [6], and the min-norm/max-margin learning rule in Section 4 builds on the SVM analysis framework of Ataee Tarzanagh et al. [5]. The paper departs from concurrent work by Sakamoto and Sato [26] by optimizing both the attention head and softmax weights (rather than fixing one), avoiding a restrictive angle assumption, and providing a tight asymptotic SNR characterization. Unlike Jiang et al. [25], it explicitly incorporates label-flipping noise, which is essential for understanding whether interpolation is compatible with generalization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Transformers dominate modern deep learning, yet the overfitting behavior of their core attention mechanism was previously uncharacterized. The tight SNR threshold of Ω(1/√n)—proven both necessary and sufficient—gives a clean, interpretable condition for when attention-based interpolation is benign versus harmful. Key limitations include the single-head, constant-token, high-dimensional (d ≫ n²) regime and synthetic data, leaving open whether these results extend to multi-head attention, deeper stacks, or realistic data distributions. The finding that benign overfitting emerges after just two GD iterations provides a concrete mechanistic picture (signal tokens for clean examples, noise tokens for noisy ones) that could inform initialization and regularization strategies in practice.
