## Problem

Existing concept erasure methods for text-to-image diffusion models face a fundamental trade-off: parameter-modifying approaches (fine-tuning, model editing) achieve erasure but cause catastrophic forgetting, establish static defenses vulnerable to paraphrasing, and are costly per new concept, while inference-time methods that intervene at the token level during diffusion are insufficient because self-attention in the text encoder spreads target semantics across the entire embedding sequence. The paper addresses how to achieve robust, complete erasure with high locality using a zero-shot, training-free strategy that operates at the global semantic level before the diffusion process begins.

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural-language text prompt and a user-specified set of target concept names (e.g., "nude", "gull", "Van Gogh") as input. It produces a modified CLIP text embedding (a single vector in R^k) by subtracting a calibrated, scaled direction vector, which is then fed into the frozen Stable Diffusion v1.4 pipeline (U-Net + VAE decoder) to generate a 512×512 RGB image. Optionally, a vision detector (OWL-ViT or NudeNet) inspects the first-pass image and, if a target concept persists visually, triggers a second, stronger surgery pass on the original embedding before final generation.

## Method

Semantic Surgery performs calibrated vector subtraction directly on the global CLIP text embedding, leveraging the linear structure of the embedding space (analogous to word2vec analogies and LLM activation engineering). A Semantic Biopsy step estimates each target concept's presence intensity ρ by computing the cosine similarity between the input embedding and the concept direction (φ(concept) − φ(∅)), then mapping it through a sigmoid calibrated via a separability assumption on the α_c distribution. For multi-concept erasure, Co-Occurrence Encoding constructs a single composite prompt from all actively present concepts and computes one unified removal direction, avoiding the over-subtraction artifacts of naive per-concept vector summation. The final surgery is e′ = e_input − ρ̂_joint · Δe_co, where ρ̂_joint is the maximum estimated presence score among active concepts. An optional Visual Feedback Loop addresses Latent Concept Persistence (U-Net priors causing concept resurgence) by detecting visually present concepts in a first-pass image and applying a second, stronger surgery to the original embedding.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 object erasure, Semantic Surgery achieves a harmonic mean H-score of 93.58, outperforming the best parameter-modifying baseline Receler (88.74) by +4.84 points, with an average robustness error of just 2.00% (vs. 10.00% for Receler and 13.80% for MACE). On the I2P explicit content benchmark, the method reduces detected nudity instances to 1 (vs. 82 for SAFREE, 123 for MACE, 159 for Receler) while improving FID to 12.2 (below the original SD v1.4's 14.04). For artistic style erasure, it attains H_a = 8.09 (vs. MACE's 5.99) with zero MS-COCO FID/CLIP degradation. Against adversarial attacks, it achieves 1.05% ASR on RAB (vs. 3.95% for MACE) and 0.0% on the white-box UnlearnDiffAtk.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Semantic Surgery decisively outperforms all seven baselines on the primary H-score metric for object erasure (93.58 vs. Receler 88.74, MACE 87.13, UCE 81.90, ESD-u 73.72, AC 70.00, ESD-x 56.03), with its largest margin over Receler (+4.84) and MACE (+6.45). The most striking advantage is in robustness against paraphrased prompts: Acc_R of 2.00% is roughly 5× better than Receler (10.00%) and 7× better than MACE (13.80%), while MACE retains a marginal edge on simple-prompt efficacy (Acc_E 0.40 vs. 1.50). On the I2P explicit content task, Semantic Surgery reduces nudity to 1 instance, beating SAFREE (82), MACE (123), SLD (149), Receler (159), and ESD-u (55) by wide margins, and simultaneously improves FID to 12.2. In adversarial robustness, it achieves 1.05% ASR on RAB (statistically significant over MACE's 3.95%, p=0.0089) and 0.0% on white-box UnlearnDiffAtk, whereas SLD and SAFREE remain highly vulnerable (78.68% and 55.80% ASR respectively). For multi-celebrity erasure at scale (100 concepts), Semantic Surgery reaches H_c ≈ 0.965 versus MACE's 0.892, UCE's 0.554, and Receler's 0.441, while maintaining FID-30K ≈ 14.45 and CLIP-30K ≈ 31.34 comparable to the unmodified model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the linear-structure principle from CLIP embedding spaces and LLM activation engineering (contrastive activation addition, function vectors) for a new application: targeted concept erasure in text-to-image diffusion models. It departs from the dominant parameter-modifying paradigm (ESD, UCE, AC, Receler, MACE) by operating entirely at inference time on the global text embedding via calibrated vector subtraction, and from prior inference-time methods (SLD, SAFREE) by intervening pre-diffusion at the whole-embedding level rather than token-wise or mid-diffusion. The Co-Occurrence Encoding mechanism and the Visual Feedback loop for Latent Concept Persistence are novel components not present in prior erasure work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

A training-free, zero-shot erasure method that matches or exceeds parameter-modifying SOTA while preserving (and in one case apparently improving) general image quality is practically attractive for deployment, since it avoids costly retraining per concept and doesn't permanently alter the base model. The identification and mitigation of Latent Concept Persistence—where U-Net priors resurrect erased concepts via correlated semantics—is a previously underexplored failure mode that this paper formalizes. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond SD v1.4 to newer architectures (SDXL, DiT-based models), how it scales to hundreds of interacting concepts, and whether the reliance on external vision detectors for the LCP loop introduces a single point of failure in adversarial settings.
