## Problem

Solving noisy linear inverse problems (super-resolution, inpainting, deblurring) with pretrained diffusion models requires alternating denoising steps with projection steps to enforce measurement consistency. Existing methods like DPS use many small projection steps—each requiring a full network evaluation—making constrained inference 10–50× slower than unconditional sampling. The paper asks: can we drastically reduce the number of projection steps while still guaranteeing exact constraint satisfaction in the noiseless case and robust performance under noise?

## Input / Output

The method takes a partial or noisy linear measurement y ∈ ℝ^d (e.g., a downsampled, masked, or blurred 256×256 RGB image scaled to [0,1]), a known linear operator A ∈ ℝ^{d×n}, and a pretrained DDPM/DDIM model. It produces a full-resolution reconstruction x̂ that satisfies Ax̂ = y exactly in the noiseless case, or stays within the statistically plausible residual region under Gaussian or Poisson measurement noise. The linear operator A can be any sparse or dense matrix (binary masks for inpainting, downsampling kernels for super-resolution, blur kernels for deblurring), and trace quantities involving A are estimated via Hutchinson's method when a full matrix representation is unavailable.

## Method

CDIM replaces the fixed small projection steps of DPS with an adaptive strategy guided by the analytically tractable χ² distribution of the proxy residual R_t = ‖Ax_t − y‖² under the forward diffusion process. At each DDIM denoising step, the algorithm computes the mean μ_t(y) and variance σ_t²(y) of R_t in closed form, then performs gradient descent on the Tweedie-based measurement residual L_t = ‖A x̂₀(x_t) − y‖² with a step size η chosen by a 1D search to land R_t at the plausible-region boundary ρ_t = μ_t + c·σ_t, stopping once R_t ≤ ρ_t. Because R_t is computable without a network forward pass, the step-size search is nearly free, and the number of projection steps adapts automatically: few at high noise (where the plausible region is wide) and more at low noise (where it constricts to zero). As t→0, the Tweedie estimate converges to the identity at rate O(√β_t), making the constraint set feasible and the objective locally convex, which guarantees exact recovery of noiseless measurements.

## Results

On FFHQ 256×256, CDIM with T'=25 denoising steps achieves FID of 33.87 (Super Res), 27.51 (Box Inpainting), 34.18 (Gaussian Deblur), and 29.67 (Random Inpainting), with LPIPS of 0.276, 0.187, 0.276, and 0.243 respectively, all in just 2.4 seconds. With T'=50 steps, CDIM improves to FID 29.68 on Gaussian Deblur (best among all methods) and 28.52 on Random Inpainting in 6.4 seconds. The headline speedup is 10–50×: CDIM runs in 2.4–6.4 s versus 70–117 s for DPS and FPS-SMC, while matching or exceeding their reconstruction quality on most tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CDIM (T'=50) achieves the best FID on Gaussian Deblur (29.68) and Random Inpainting (28.52), outperforming FPS-SMC (29.97, 33.10) and DPS (44.05, 21.19) on FID for those tasks, while being 18–49× faster. FPS-SMC retains the best FID on Super Resolution (26.62 vs. 31.54) and Box Inpainting (26.51 vs. 26.09) and the best LPIPS on Box Inpainting (0.150 vs. 0.196), but at 116.9 s runtime versus CDIM's 6.4 s. DPS wins on LPIPS for Super Resolution (0.214 vs. 0.269) and Random Inpainting (0.212 vs. 0.240) but is 11× slower and has substantially worse FID on three of four tasks. Against DDRM, MCG, PnP-ADMM, Score-SDE, and ADMM-TV, CDIM dominates on both FID and LPIPS across all four tasks. In the head-to-head with DAPs-50 (50 NFEs), CDIM (T'=25) achieves lower LPIPS on every task (e.g., 0.187 vs. 0.244 on Box Inpainting, 0.271 vs. 0.306 on Random Inpainting) with fewer visible artifacts. Against Diff-PIR (100 NFEs), CDIM (T'=50) achieves markedly better FID (31.54 vs. 65.77 on Super Res; 29.68 vs. 59.65 on Deblur) though Diff-PIR edges out on Deblur LPIPS (0.236 vs. 0.252).

## Relation to Prior Work

CDIM builds directly on the DDIM accelerated sampling framework (Song et al., 2021) and the DDPM forward/reverse process formulation (Ho et al., 2020), but departs from the dominant DPS-style paradigm of alternating many small fixed projection steps with unconditional denoising. The key conceptual departure is using the analytically tractable chi-squared distribution of the proxy residual R_t = ||Ax_t − y||² under the forward noising process as an adaptive stopping criterion and step-size guide, rather than relying on a fixed number of gradient steps per timestep. This positions CDIM as a principled projection strategy layered on top of DDIM, distinct from methods like DSG that use soft constraints or MCG that use MCMC-based guidance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 10–50× reduction in projection steps (and the resulting ~30× wall-clock speedup, from ~70s to ~2.5s) makes constrained diffusion inference practical for near-real-time applications, while the exact noiseless constraint satisfaction (even for out-of-distribution inputs) addresses a correctness gap that DPS cannot close. The method is limited to linear constraints and pixel-space diffusion; extending the chi-squared alignment principle to nonlinear observations or latent diffusion models remains open. The finding that simply combining DPS with DDIM's accelerated schedule produces blurry or divergent results (Figure 7) underscores that the projection strategy, not the sampling schedule, is the bottleneck for constrained inference.
