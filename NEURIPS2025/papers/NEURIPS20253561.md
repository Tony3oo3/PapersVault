## Problem

Neural collapse and loss-landscape flatness both emerge late in training and correlate with generalization, but their causal roles remain ambiguous: are they prerequisites for generalization or mere by-products of training dynamics? The paper tackles this by exploiting grokking—where memorization precedes generalization—as a temporal probe to disentangle necessity from correlation for each geometric property.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253561/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The analysis spans three task families: modular arithmetic over abstract tokens (input: token pair, output: result token), image classification on CIFAR-10 (32×32 RGB) and ImageNet-100 (input: RGB image, output: class label), and sentiment classification on SST-5 (input: text sequence, output: sentiment label). All measurements—neural collapse clustering (NCC) and relative flatness—are computed in the penultimate layer of standard architectures (ResNet-18/10, ViT-tiny, TinyBERT, DistilGPT2), with relative flatness made tractable via a closed-form Hessian-trace upper bound valid under cross-entropy loss.

## Method

The paper uses two complementary perturbations to isolate causal roles. First, it suppresses neural collapse by adding a negative NCC regularizer (L_CE − λ·NCC) to the training loss, which actively discourages intra-class clustering; models still generalize at full accuracy while NCC rises, proving NC is not necessary. Second, it induces delayed generalization (artificial grokking) by adding a negative relative-flatness regularizer (L_CE − λ·κ_Tr^φ(w)), which pushes the network into sharp minima; removing the regularizer mid-training triggers a sudden jump in validation accuracy, demonstrating that flatness is functionally required. Theoretically, the paper proves that under classical NC assumptions (features collapse to class means forming an equiangular tight frame, weights align with means), relative flatness is bounded above by a quantity that decays exponentially in the weight scale λ, explaining why NC and flatness co-occur empirically while showing NC is a sufficient but non-exclusive path to flatness.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (ResNet-18), suppressing neural collapse via NCC regularization leaves validation accuracy unchanged at ~78% and relative flatness stable, demonstrating NC is not necessary for generalization. In contrast, regularizing against relative flatness (encouraging sharpness) drops validation accuracy to ~60% on CIFAR-10 (ResNet-10), ~38% on ImageNet-100 (ViT), ~38% on SST-5 (TinyBERT), and ~37% on SST-5 (DistilGPT2), compared to baselines of ~78%, ~43%, ~44%, and ~45% respectively. Relative flatness values under the sharpness regularizer remain orders of magnitude higher (e.g., 2209.94 vs. 1.91 on CIFAR-10; 22212.75 vs. 313.22 on ImageNet-100) until the regularizer is removed, at which point generalization recovers to baseline levels.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not compare against external methods but instead contrasts three training regimes to isolate causal roles. The NCC-regularized regime (suppressing neural collapse) matches the standard baseline exactly on CIFAR-10 validation accuracy (~78%) and relative flatness, proving NC is not a prerequisite for generalization. The relative-flatness-regularized regime (encouraging sharpness) consistently degrades validation accuracy by 10–18 percentage points across all four dataset/architecture combinations (CIFAR-10, ImageNet-100, SST-5 with two LMs), and this degradation is directly correlated with elevated relative flatness values (e.g., 2209.94 vs. 1.91 on CIFAR-10). Upon removing the sharpness regularizer ('unplugging'), validation accuracy recovers to within 1% of the standard baseline in every setting, confirming that the performance gap is caused by the absence of flatness rather than by the training procedure itself. The grokking experiments on modular arithmetic further show that relative flatness drops sharply only at the onset of generalization, while NCC decreases earlier during memorization, providing temporal evidence that flatness tracks generalization more tightly than collapse does.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two major threads in generalization theory: the neural collapse literature (Papyan et al., Mixon et al., Zhou et al.) and the relative flatness framework of Petzka et al. [2021], which the authors extend using the closed-form computation from Walter et al. [2024]. It departs from the prevailing NC narrative by using grokking (Power et al., 2022) as a causal probe to temporally separate memorization from generalization, and by adopting the NCC clustering measure from Galanti et al. [2022] to quantify collapse. The key conceptual departure is from the unconstrained-features-model results (Mixon et al., Zhou et al., Sükenik et al.) that establish NC as the global optimum, by showing empirically and theoretically that NC is a sufficient but non-exclusive path to the flatness that actually drives generalization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that generalization proceeds without neural collapse and that suppressing flatness reliably delays it across ResNets, ViTs, and pretrained LMs, the paper reframes flatness as the more fundamental geometric prerequisite and NC as an inductive bias toward it. The theoretical result (NC implies relative flatness under classical assumptions) unifies the empirical co-occurrence of the two phenomena under a single geometric framework. Open questions include whether relative flatness is also sufficient, whether the locally-constant-label and representativeness assumptions hold in structured prediction or high-noise regression, and whether findings extend to contrastive pretraining or large-scale language models. The ability to actively induce grokking-like behavior in non-grokking settings also opens a new experimental lever for probing training dynamics.
