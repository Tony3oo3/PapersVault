## Problem

Projection-free first-order methods for constrained optimization over general compact convex sets remain largely open: Frank-Wolfe methods require expensive linear optimization oracles that are intractable for complex constraints (e.g., SDP), while penalty-based approaches suffer from poor feasibility and ill-conditioning. The paper asks whether one can design a projection-free approach that simultaneously achieves optimal convergence rates and low per-iteration cost without any optimization oracle.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252352/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a smooth (possibly non-convex) objective f, a compact convex constraint set K defined by inequalities, and a single interior point x° of K (obtained once via a convex feasibility problem). At each iteration it requires only a membership oracle (feasibility check, O(n²) for common sets) and function/gradient evaluations of f, with the gauge mapping computed via bisection (O(log 1/ε) membership calls) or closed-form for standard constraint types. The output is the optimal solution x* = ψ(z*) mapped back from the ball-constrained space, or an ε-approximate stationary point with per-iteration cost Õ(n²) and no LOO or projection subproblem.

## Method

Hom-PGD constructs an explicit gauge mapping ψ(z) = (‖z‖/γ_K(z, x°))z + x° that homeomorphically maps the unit ball B to the constraint set K, transforming the original problem into an equivalent ball-constrained problem H with objective h = f∘ψ. Although h is non-convex due to the nonlinear mapping, the ball constraint admits a closed-form projection, so standard PGD can be applied directly without any optimization oracle. The key theoretical insight is that the gauge mapping is bi-Lipschitz (with constants controlled by the inner/outer radii around x°), and under LICQ, stationary points of H correspond to global optima of P, preserving the convex structure. This yields optimal convergence rates—O(1/ε) for convex, O(log 1/ε) for strongly convex, and O(1/ε²) for non-convex objectives—with constant step-size and Õ(n²) per-iteration complexity. A log-sum-exp smoothing technique handles the non-differentiability of the gauge mapping at boundary points (e.g., low-rank SDP solutions) at an O(η) optimality gap.

## Results

Hom-PGD achieves optimal convergence rates matching standard PGD: O(1/ε) for convex objectives, O(log(1/ε)) for strongly convex objectives, and O(1/ε²) for non-convex objectives, all with a constant step-size. The per-iteration complexity is O(n²) using only a membership oracle, avoiding expensive linear optimization oracles (LOO) or projection operations. In experiments on SOCP (1000-dim), Hom-PGD reaches a 10⁻³ objective optimality gap in under 600 seconds, while the commercial MOSEK solver requires 5424 seconds. Per-iteration runtime is up to 3–5 orders of magnitude lower than projection-based or Frank-Wolfe baselines, and gauge mapping computation takes less than 0.01 seconds up to 3000-dimensional constraints.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Hom-PGD is compared against four baselines: PGD, Frank-Wolfe (FW), Augmented Lagrangian Method (ALM), and Radial-Dual (RD). Against PGD, Hom-PGD achieves comparable convergence rates but with dramatically lower per-iteration cost (up to 3–5 orders of magnitude faster) because it replaces expensive projection over the general convex set with a closed-form projection onto the unit ball. Against FW, Hom-PGD avoids the linear optimization oracle entirely, which is critical for complex constraint sets (e.g., SDP where LOO itself requires solving an SDP); Hom-PGD also avoids FW's characteristic oscillatory behavior near the solution. Against ALM, Hom-PGD outperforms in per-iteration complexity since ALM incurs high cost for each inner minimization and suffers constraint violations on additional equality constraints (e.g., in the Burer-Monteiro SDP formulation). Against RD, Hom-PGD achieves similar convergence rates with a simpler constant step-size (vs. implicit or vanishing step-sizes in RD) and lower per-iteration overhead. On the 1000-dim SOCP instance, Hom-PGD reaches 10⁻³ optimality gap in under 600 seconds versus 5424 seconds for MOSEK (interior-point), and on 50²-variable max-cut SDP, Hom-PGD maintains competitive total running time despite slower per-iteration convergence on linear objectives.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalizes the Hadamard parameterization idea (Li et al. 2023), which transforms simplex-constrained optimization to sphere-constrained optimization, to arbitrary compact convex sets via the gauge (Minkowski) mapping introduced by Tabas & Zhang (2022) and Li et al. (2025). It departs from Frank-Wolfe methods (which require a linear optimization oracle) and radial duality frameworks (Grimmer 2024, which require quadratic optimization oracles) by replacing the expensive oracle with a simple membership oracle and a bisection-based gauge computation. The point-to-boundary distance function from Tordesillas et al. (2023) is leveraged for efficient gauge mapping evaluation and central interior point selection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Hom-PGD is the first projection-free first-order method that handles general compact convex sets without expensive optimization oracles while achieving optimal convergence rates (O(1/ε) convex, O(log 1/ε) strongly convex, O(1/ε²) non-convex) with O(n²) per-iteration cost. The 3–5 orders of magnitude per-iteration speedup over projection-based and Frank-Wolfe baselines makes it practically competitive with commercial solvers (e.g., beating MOSEK on 1000-dim SOCP). Open questions include whether Nesterov-style acceleration can be incorporated given the non-convexity of the transformed problem, whether alternative homeomorphisms could yield better conditioning, and how to extend guarantees to general non-convex constraint sets beyond star-shaped ones.
