## Problem

Deep reinforcement learning policies trained on a single environment context overfit to specific parameter values (e.g., friction, gravity) and fail to generalize zero-shot to unseen contexts. Existing remedies—domain randomization, meta-RL, or problem-specific architectures—require either many training contexts or substantial domain engineering, making them impractical when environment construction is expensive. The paper asks whether the known smooth structure of the dynamics can be exploited to improve generalization without ever sampling from unseen contexts.

## Input / Output

The agent receives a state vector concatenated with the fully-observable context parameter vector (e.g., gravitational acceleration, mass, goal direction) as input to a feed-forward SAC network, and outputs a continuous action. The key additional input is access to first-order partial derivatives of the transition and reward functions with respect to context parameters, which are assumed available (e.g., from known physics equations). CSE produces synthetic (reward, next-state) pairs for perturbed contexts by applying these gradients to observed transitions, effectively converting one real sample into multiple approximate samples across a neighborhood of the training context.

## Method

The paper introduces the Context-Enhanced Bellman Equation (CEBE), which replaces the true transition and reward functions of a CMDP with their first-order Taylor linearizations about a base training context c₀, yielding a Q-function provably within O(‖c−c₀‖²) of the true Bellman solution (Theorems 2–3). From CEBE, the authors derive Context Sample Enhancement (CSE), a data-augmentation rule that takes an observed transition (s, a, r, s′) and a context perturbation Δc, and returns a synthetic sample (r̄, s̄′) by adding the context-gradient corrections to both the reward and the next state (Eq. 11). In practice, CSE is plugged into standard off-policy SAC training: each minibatch sample is augmented with perturbed-context copies before the Bellman update, so the policy is implicitly trained on a neighborhood of contexts without ever interacting with them. The theoretical analysis rests on a general (T,R)-stability theorem (Theorem 1) bounding Q-function differences in terms of Wasserstein distance between transition kernels and sup-norm reward gaps, combined with Lipschitz regularity assumptions on the policy and dynamics.

## Results

The paper validates CEBE's first-order accuracy on tabular Cliffwalking, showing the approximation error ||Q_CE^c - Q_BE^c||_∞ scales as O(||c-c₀||²) with a fitted log-log slope ≈ 2 for two different reward functions. In continuous control environments (SimpleDirection, PendulumGoal, CheetahVelocity, AntDirection), CSE consistently outperforms vanilla SAC baseline across all swept context parameters. CSE achieves performance comparable to Local Domain Randomization (LDR)—an idealized benchmark with access to exact data from perturbed contexts—while requiring only a single training context plus gradient information. In PendulumGoal, CSE notably outperforms LDR when the goal torque exceeds 0.6, and in CheetahVelocity, CSE outperforms LDR when goal velocity exceeds 2.6.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares CSE against two baselines: vanilla SAC (baseline training on a single context) and Local Domain Randomization (LDR, which samples exact trajectories from perturbed contexts as an idealized upper bound). CSE consistently outperforms the vanilla SAC baseline across all five environments (Cliffwalking, SimpleDirection, PendulumGoal, CheetahVelocity, AntDirection) for all swept context parameters. Against LDR, CSE performs comparably in most settings, confirming that first-order Taylor-based data augmentation can approximate the benefits of exact multi-context training. CSE achieves strictly better performance than LDR in specific regimes: in PendulumGoal when goal torque τ > 0.6, and in CheetahVelocity when goal velocity > 2.6. The one notable exception is AntDirection, where CSE achieves slightly lower returns than LDR in the context range [3.5, 5]. Overall, CSE matches or exceeds LDR while requiring only gradient information from a single training context rather than full environment access at multiple contexts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of contextual MDP theory and practical RL data augmentation. It departs from domain randomization (Tobin et al., 2017; Sadeghi and Levine, 2016), which requires sampling from perturbed contexts, by instead using first-order Taylor linearization of transition and reward functions in context space to synthesize approximate samples from unseen contexts. The theoretical framework builds on the CMDP formalism of Beukman et al. (2023) and Hallak et al. (2015), but unlike prior PAC-bound work (Modi et al., 2018) that relies on zeroth-order covering arguments, this paper exploits first-order gradient information to achieve tighter approximation guarantees. The CSE procedure is conceptually analogous to action-space sample enhancement (Qiao et al., 2021) but applied to the context variable, and it can be viewed as a structured alternative to importance sampling for OOD generalization (Tirinzoni et al., 2018).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CSE offers a practical, easy-to-implement data augmentation method that requires only gradient access to the environment dynamics at a single training context, making it attractive for settings where constructing multiple training environments is expensive (e.g., robotics). The clean O(||c-c0||²) approximation guarantee provides theoretical justification for why this linearization-based approach should work in a neighborhood of the training context. Key limitations include the requirement for deterministic transitions (CSE), fully observable context, and access to context gradients, which restricts applicability. Open questions include the sample complexity of CSE versus domain randomization in high-dimensional context spaces, extension to stochastic transitions via transport map gradients, and applicability to offline RL where new samples are impractical.
