## Problem

RL-based job scheduling methods train a single policy that suffers from mode collapse in the exponentially large JSP search space, limiting exploration and solution quality. Additionally, designing problem-specific reward functions for the many JSP variants (differing in machine environments, constraints, and objectives) remains a labor-intensive bottleneck. The paper asks whether a single model can learn multiple diverse, specialized policies that collectively explore the solution space more effectively, without requiring handcrafted reward signals.

## Input / Output

The method takes a JSP instance (jobs, machines, operations with processing times, eligibility sets, and problem-specific constraints) and k latent variables sampled from a fixed prior distribution Z. At each of |O| sequential decision steps, the conditioned policy outputs a discrete action (which operation to schedule next), producing k full candidate schedules. At inference, the best of the k candidates is selected by objective value; the latent variables are sampled once per rollout and remain fixed throughout solution construction, so the same network weights generate all k policies.

## Method

MP-ASIL represents k policies as latent conditioned policies: a single DNN (GNN or Transformer backbone) is conditioned on k distinct latent variables z^1,...,z^k, each defining a separate decision-maker without additional parameters. Training follows a self-evolutionary loop: (Step A) sample k latent variables and roll out k candidate solutions; (Step B) select the best solution τ* as a pseudo-label; (Step C) update the model via cross-entropy to imitate τ* at each step, but only for the winning latent variable z*. The key innovation is an adaptive imitation weight — the normalized advantage |f(τ*,s)−mean(s)|/std(s) — that scales the loss so high-quality pseudo-labels are strongly imitated while near-average ones are weakly imitated, preventing over-exploitation of suboptimal self-teachers. This reformulates policy optimization from sparse-return RL into dense step-level classification, eliminating the need for reward functions while the population-level min objective is reflected through selective updates on the best policy.

## Results

On JSSP (Taillard benchmark), MP-ASIL achieves an average gap of 7.4% across eight problem sizes, outperforming the best prior neural constructive heuristic SLIM (7.8%) and all other NCH/NIH baselines. On SMSP, MP-ASIL trained only on |J|=50 instances achieves 0.00% gap at sizes 50 and 100 and 0.16% at size 500, demonstrating strong cross-size generalization. On PFSP, MP-ASIL attains a 1.68% average gap, surpassing the best classical heuristic NEH (2.28%) and all prior neural solvers. On TSP-100, MP-ASIL finds practically optimal solutions (0.000% gap vs. LKH3) in under one minute, matching the exact solver while being orders of magnitude faster.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MP-ASIL consistently outperforms all 28 baselines across six JSP variants and two routing problems. On JSSP, it beats the closest prior NCO method SLIM (same backbone, different training) by ~5.1% relative improvement in average gap (7.4% vs. 7.8%), and substantially outperforms L2D (20.5%), SIGD (8.4%), and all neural improvement heuristics (10.7–12.2%). On UPMSP, MP-ASIL with the same architecture as Cho et al. reduces the gap from 20.16% to 9.57% at 100×6, and the Large variant achieves 0.00% gap. On PFSP, MP-ASIL (1.68% avg. gap) surpasses the best classical heuristic NEH (2.28%) and all prior neural solvers (10.03–18.38%). On FFSP, it improves over PolyNet (1.59%→1.32%) and MatNet (2.02%→1.32%) with the same model. On TSP-100, MP-ASIL achieves 0.000% gap (matching LKH3) in ~1 min vs. Poppy's 0.07% and POMO's 0.146%; on CVRP-100 it reaches 0.28% vs. Poppy's 0.51% and POMO's 0.76%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper synthesizes two previously separate threads in neural combinatorial optimization: latent conditioned policies (from COMPASS [55] and PolyNet [56], which represent multiple policies within a single network via conditioning on sampled latent variables) and self-imitation learning (from SLIM [20] and SIGD [45], which eliminate reward design by generating pseudo-labels from the model's own rollouts). The key departure is the combination of these mechanisms with an adaptive advantage-weighted cross-entropy loss that modulates imitation intensity based on pseudo-label quality, and the reframing of policy optimization as a classification task rather than return maximization. Unlike multi-decoder approaches (MDAM, Poppy) that scale parameters linearly with the number of policies, MP-ASIL uses a fixed-parameter latent space, and unlike prior SIL methods that discard all but the best candidate, it leverages the full distribution of k rollouts for the quality signal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses two persistent practical barriers in deploying RL for scheduling: the need for problem-specific reward engineering and the mode-collapse problem that limits exploration in single-policy models. Its demonstrated cross-size generalization (trained on |J|=50, achieving 0.00% gap at |J|=100 and 0.16% at |J|=500 for SMSP) and model-agnosticism (plug-and-play across GNN and Transformer backbones, six JSP variants, and routing problems) make it a practical recipe for practitioners who lack domain expertise in reward design. Open questions include whether the k-rollout inference cost is acceptable in real-time settings, how the method behaves when the objective function is noisy or stochastic (beyond the FJSSP experiments), and whether the latent space can be further structured to enable targeted policy search at test time.
