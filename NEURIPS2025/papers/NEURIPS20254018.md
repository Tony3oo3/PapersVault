## Problem

Existing online free-viewpoint video (FVV) reconstruction methods based on 3D Gaussian Splatting require prohibitive storage (>20 MB/s) because they update Gaussian attributes point-wise across all frames, redundantly modeling static regions and repeatedly encoding similar motions. This makes real-time streaming transmission impractical despite the high rendering fidelity these methods achieve.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view video sequences with known camera poses (18–21 views at 2704×2028 for N3DV, 13 views at 1280×720 for MeetRoom, both at 30 FPS, downsampled by 2× for training). It produces a compact per-frame stream of keypoint motion parameters (14 values per keypoint) plus sparse error-correction residuals, compressed via 8/16-bit quantization and Huffman entropy coding, requiring only ~0.05 MB per frame for the small variant—enabling real-time transmission at 91–147 FPS rendering speed.

## Method

ComGS replaces point-wise Gaussian updates with a keypoint-driven motion representation that exploits two properties of dynamic scenes: motion locality and motion consistency. First, a viewspace gradient difference strategy selects ~200 motion-sensitive keypoints by computing inter-frame rendering loss gradient changes, ensuring keypoints fall in dynamic regions. Second, each keypoint carries a learnable spatial influence field (parameterized by a quaternion and scaling vector) that defines a Gaussian-shaped region of control, allowing neighboring points to share the keypoint's translation and rotation offset weighted by their influence score—replacing rigid KNN neighborhoods with adaptive, scale-aware propagation. Third, an error-aware corrector at periodic key frames uses a learnable soft mask (binarized via straight-through estimator) to selectively update only Gaussians with detected reconstruction errors, mitigating drift from non-rigid motion and novel object emergence without redundant full-scene updates.

## Results

On the N3DV dataset, ComGS-s achieves a PSNR of 31.87 dB with only 0.049 MB storage, representing a 159× reduction over 3DGStream (7.80 MB) and a 14× reduction over QUEEN-s (0.68 MB), while ComGS-l reaches 32.12 dB PSNR at 0.106 MB. On MeetRoom, ComGS-s outperforms 3DGStream by +0.7 dB PSNR (31.49 vs. 30.79) with a 146× smaller storage footprint (0.028 MB vs. 4.1 MB). ComGS also achieves the best LPIPS among online methods on N3DV (0.132 for ComGS-s, 0.129 for ComGS-l), indicating superior perceptual quality. Rendering speed is competitive at 91–147 FPS, though lower than 3DGStream (261 FPS) and QUEEN (248–345 FPS).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against 3DGStream, ComGS achieves a dramatic 159× storage reduction on N3DV (0.049 MB vs. 7.80 MB) and 146× on MeetRoom (0.028 MB vs. 4.1 MB), while also improving PSNR by +0.7 dB on MeetRoom (31.49 vs. 30.79) and matching or slightly exceeding it on N3DV (31.87 vs. 31.67 for ComGS-s). Against QUEEN, ComGS-s trades a marginal 0.02 dB PSNR loss (31.87 vs. 31.89) for a 14× storage advantage (0.049 vs. 0.68 MB), and ComGS-l similarly sacrifices 0.07 dB (32.12 vs. 32.19) for a 7× reduction (0.106 vs. 0.75 MB); however, ComGS achieves better LPIPS in both variants. Against NeRF-based online methods (StreamRF, TeTriRF), ComGS substantially outperforms on all quality metrics (e.g., +1.2 dB PSNR over StreamRF on N3DV) while using far less storage. The main trade-off is rendering speed: ComGS operates at 91–147 FPS versus 261–350 FPS for 3DGStream and QUEEN, and training time is higher (37–43 s per frame vs. 4.65–8.5 s), reflecting the cost of per-frame keypoint optimization. On the long-video Flame Salmon sequence (1200 frames), ComGS-s achieves 29.56 dB PSNR with 0.053 MB storage, slightly outperforming TGH (29.44 dB, 0.075 MB) in both quality and compactness.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the online FVV streaming paradigm established by 3DGStream (which uses per-frame hash-based MLPs for Gaussian transformations) and QUEEN (which adds learned quantization-sparsity for Gaussian residuals) by replacing point-wise motion updates with a keypoint-driven representation. Conceptually, it draws from offline sparse control point methods (SC-GS, SP-GS) that use KNN to propagate motion from a few control points, but departs from them by making keypoint selection motion-sensitive (via viewspace gradient difference) and by replacing rigid KNN neighborhoods with learnable anisotropic spatial influence fields. The error-aware correction strategy is a novel addition that selectively updates only high-error Gaussians at keyframes, contrasting with the full-attribute updates used by prior online methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 159× storage reduction over 3DGStream and 14× over QUEEN makes real-time FVV streaming feasible on bandwidth-constrained connections, which is a practical bottleneck for VR/AR and volumetric video conferencing applications. However, the method still requires dense multi-view inputs (18–21 cameras), depends heavily on first-frame initialization quality, and has not been optimized for encoding-stage training time, limiting immediate deployment. Open questions include whether the keypoint-driven approach generalizes to sparse-view or monocular settings, and whether the fixed k=200 keypoint budget scales appropriately to scenes with more complex or distributed motion.
