## Problem

Robust policy evaluation for non-rectangular uncertainty sets in MDPs is strongly NP-hard even in approximation, and existing methods suffer from either exponential iteration complexity or large accuracy gaps. While rectangular relaxations are tractable, they introduce exponentially many spurious environments (the volume ratio scales as O(2^{-n})), leading to overly conservative solutions. The paper asks whether useful classes of non-rectangular kernel uncertainty sets can circumvent this hardness barrier.

## Input / Output

The method takes a nominal transition kernel P̂, a stationary policy π, a global L_p uncertainty radius β (assumed small enough to keep all perturbed kernels valid), a reward function R, and an initial state distribution μ. It outputs the robust return J_U^π and the worst-case kernel P_U^π = P̂ − bk^T, where b ∈ R^{S×A}_+ with ||b||_p ≤ β and k ∈ R^S with ||k||_p ≤ 1 and 1^T k = 0. The key structural constraint is that the uncertainty set is defined by a single global norm constraint rather than per-state-action constraints, which is what distinguishes it from the NP-hard polyhedral cases.

## Method

The paper decomposes the non-rectangular L_p uncertainty set into an infinite union of sa-rectangular L_p sets (Proposition 3.2), then exploits the fact that the worst-case kernel for each sa-rectangular component is a rank-one perturbation of the nominal kernel. Applying the Sherman-Morrison formula to the occupancy matrix (I − γ(P̂^π − b^π k^T))^{-1} yields a novel dual formulation expressing the robust return as J^π minus a penalty term involving only nominal quantities (d^π, v_R^π, v_b^π). For the L_1 case, the bisection function F(λ) = max_{b,k} k^T E_λ^π b admits a closed-form solution (Proposition 3.5), enabling a binary search algorithm with O(log ε^{-1}) iteration complexity and linear convergence. This is the first polynomial-time robust policy evaluation algorithm for L_1-normed non-rectangular robust MDPs, and the dual formulation also reveals the adversary's two-fold strategy of maximizing trajectory uncertainty and optimizing perturbation direction.

## Results

The proposed binary search algorithm (Algorithm 1) achieves O(log ε⁻¹) iteration complexity for robust policy evaluation with L₁-normed non-rectangular uncertainty sets, with a proven linear convergence rate of Jₙ − J_{U_p}^π ≤ O(2⁻ⁿ). In experiments (S=12, A=8, γ=0.9, tolerance 10⁻⁴), Algorithm 1 converges in only a few iterations across uncertainty radii β ∈ {0.005, 0.01, 0.05}, while the CPI baseline from [21] converges very slowly and settles far from the true robust return. The method scales favorably to state spaces up to S=190, whereas CPI's computational demands grow substantially with larger state spaces.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its binary search algorithm (Algorithm 1) against the CPI-style Algorithm 3.2 from Li et al. [21], the only prior method for general non-rectangular kernel robust policy evaluation. Algorithm 1 decisively outperforms CPI on both iteration count and wall-clock time: it converges in a handful of iterations (logarithmic in 1/ε) while CPI requires O(ε⁻²) iterations and still converges to a value far from the true robust return due to the highly non-rectangular structure of the L₁ uncertainty set. Additionally, Algorithm 1 does not require the irreducibility assumption (Assumption 1 of [21]) that CPI needs for its mismatch coefficient δ_d to exist, and it avoids the potentially exponential δ_d = O(2^S) and large non-rectangularity constant δ_P = O(S√A) that render CPI's accuracy bound meaningless for many practical MDPs. A brute-force benchmark (500 million random kernel samples) confirms that Algorithm 1's output closely matches the empirical minimum, while CPI's estimate remains significantly above it.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the rectangular robust MDP framework of Kumar et al. (NeurIPS 2023, ICML 2024), which established that worst-case kernels for L_p-bounded sa-rectangular and s-rectangular sets are rank-one perturbations of the nominal kernel, to the non-rectangular setting. The key departure is showing that the NP-hardness barrier proven by Wiesemann et al. (2013) for general polyhedral uncertainty sets does not apply to L_p-bounded non-rectangular sets, since the hardness reduction relies on many linear constraints while L_p balls impose a single global norm constraint. The decomposition of the non-rectangular set into an infinite union of sa-rectangular sets (Proposition 3.2) and the resulting dual formulation are novel, building on the Sherman-Morrison-based reformulation from [18] and the GSTD framework from [20]. The method directly improves upon the CPI-style algorithm of Li et al. (2024), which required irreducibility assumptions and suffered from exponentially large accuracy gaps.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first polynomial-time robust policy evaluation algorithm for non-rectangular L_1-normed uncertainty sets, with O(log ε^{-1}) iteration complexity and exact ε-accuracy, closing a gap left by prior methods that were either exponential or had large accuracy gaps. The dual formulation provides the first structural characterization of the adversary in non-rectangular robust MDPs, revealing a two-stage strategy (maximize trajectory uncertainty, then optimize perturbation direction) that could inform the design of more resilient policies. Key limitations include the closed-form solution being available only for p=1 (with a spectral heuristic for p=2), the small-radius assumption ensuring kernel validity, and the restriction to stationary policies. Open questions include extending to KL-divergence uncertainty sets (where the worst kernel has an exponentiated rather than rank-one form), non-stationary policies, and whether the NP-hardness is truly confined to polyhedral sets with many constraints.
