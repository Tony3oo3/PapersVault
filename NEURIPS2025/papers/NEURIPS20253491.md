## Problem

Generating images with multiple humans while preserving each person's facial identity, producing the correct number of people, and rendering text-described actions remains a significant challenge for current diffusion models. A major obstacle is the absence of a dedicated, standardized benchmark with well-defined metrics for multi-human generation; existing benchmarks focus on single-subject fidelity, general text-to-image quality, or multi-object composition but none address the added complexity of multiple distinct human identities simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253491/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark provides 1,800 test samples, each pairing 1–5 reference face images (drawn from 5,550 unique IDs sampled uniformly across age, ethnicity, and gender from FFHQ, SFHQ, and CelebA-HQ) with a text prompt describing the scene and an optional human-curated pose conditioning image. Evaluation produces four complementary scores per sample: Count Accuracy (face detection), Hungarian ID Similarity (ArcFace cosine similarity with optimal assignment), HPSv2 prompt alignment, and MLLM-based Action QA (simple and complex), combined into a unified geometric-mean metric that penalizes weakness in either identity or alignment.

## Method

The paper's core technical contribution is a pair of training-free, plug-and-play techniques for unified multimodal diffusion models. Unified Regional Isolation modifies the self-attention mask so that tokens from each reference image can only attend to a designated region-of-interest (ROI) in the latent space, preventing identity blending across subjects. Implicit Region Assignment automatically determines these ROIs without explicit pose or box priors by probing intermediate self-attention maps at a chosen timestep, segmenting the decoded intermediate latent with SAM2, and applying Hungarian matching to assign each reference image to the latent region with maximum attention overlap. Applied to OmniGen (yielding MH-OmniGen) and IR-Diffusion (yielding MH-IR-Diffusion), these techniques improve Multi-ID similarity by 5+ points and reduce subject-leaking artifacts while requiring no additional training.

## Results

On the primary Task 1 (Reference-based Multi-Human Generation in the Wild), the proposed MH-OmniGen achieves the best Unified score of 61.6, outperforming OmniGen (59.2) and GPT-Image-1 (54.3). MH-OmniGen leads in Multi-ID similarity (54.5), Action-S (91.6), and HPS (26.3), while GPT-Image-1 dominates Count accuracy (87.9) and Action-C (91.1). Across all 30 evaluated methods, no model consistently achieves high scores on all metrics simultaneously; the best Count accuracy among open-source methods is only 60.5 (OmniGen), and the best Multi-ID among non-unified architectures is 44.2 (UniPortrait).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MH-OmniGen (the paper's proposed training-free enhancement) consistently outperforms its base model OmniGen across four of five metrics: Multi-ID (54.5 vs. 49.4, +5.1), Action-S (91.6 vs. 87.5, +4.1), Action-C (72.9 vs. 71.3), and Unified (61.6 vs. 59.2). Against the proprietary GPT-Image-1, MH-OmniGen wins decisively on Multi-ID (54.5 vs. 28.8, +25.7) and Unified score (61.6 vs. 54.3), but loses on Count accuracy (60.3 vs. 87.9) and Action-C (72.9 vs. 91.1), as GPT-Image-1 hallucinates facial features rather than preserving reference identities. Among open-source multi-human tuning-free methods, UniPortrait (RV1.5 backbone) is the strongest non-unified competitor at 51.7 Unified, but trails MH-OmniGen by 9.9 points. Tuning-based approaches (LoRA, MuDI on SDXL) and multi-object tuning-free methods (IP-Adapter, MIP-Adapter, Lambda-Eclipse) all score below 43 Unified, confirming that general-purpose multi-object personalization methods are poorly suited for multi-human identity preservation. In Task 2 (with regional priors), UniPortrait and OmniGen tie for the best Unified score (62.5), while Regional-PuLID achieves the highest Multi-ID (50.7) among Flux-based methods, and RectifID achieves the highest Count (90.1) among SD1.5-based methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends two complementary lines of work: (1) unified multimodal architectures like OmniGen and Show-O that process text and reference images in a shared token space, and (2) regional isolation techniques from IR-Diffusion and InstantFamily that use attention masking to prevent identity leakage between multiple subjects in T2I models. The key departure is adapting regional isolation to unified architectures via a modified attention mask (Unified Regional Isolation) and replacing explicit regional priors with an implicit Hungarian-matching strategy guided by intermediate attention scores and SAM segmentation. As a benchmark, it carves new ground by being the first to systematically evaluate multi-human ID-consistent generation across four distinct task formulations with a multi-metric suite, filling a gap the authors identify in existing benchmarks that focus on single-subject fidelity or general T2I quality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals that no current method—proprietary or open-source—consistently achieves high scores across all dimensions (count, ID, action, quality) simultaneously, with even GPT-Image-1 showing notably weak ID retention (28.8) despite strong action and count scores. The training-free plug-and-play enhancements (regional isolation + implicit Hungarian assignment) offer a broadly applicable recipe for improving any unified multi-modal generator without retraining. Open questions include whether base models can be improved to generate correct human counts natively (best is only 63.9%), how ID preservation scales beyond five people, and how to eliminate the demographic biases (age, ethnicity, gender) the benchmark exposes across all evaluated models.
