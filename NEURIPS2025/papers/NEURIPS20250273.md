## Problem

Offline RL agents trained on stationary, fully observable data fail when deployed in environments where the observation function shifts by unknown, per-episode additive offsets that evolve according to arbitrary real-world time-series patterns. Existing robust offline RL methods handle only synthetic or Gaussian test-time perturbations, and prior non-stationary RL work either requires online interaction, assumes full observability, or presupposes specific non-stationarity patterns during training. FORL targets the underexplored setting of passive, non-Markovian, episodic observation offsets that render the environment a sequence of POMDPs, with no access to ground-truth offsets during evaluation.

## Input / Output

At test time, the method consumes three streams: (i) offset-shifted observations o_t = s_t + b^j received from the environment, (ii) a limited history of past per-episode offsets (b^{j-C}, …, b^{j-1}) revealed only after each episode terminates, and (iii) the in-episode sequence of action vectors and observation deltas (Δo_t, a_{t-1}) accumulated in a sliding window of size w. The output is a per-timestep state estimate ŝ_t (a point in the original state space) that feeds an off-the-shelf offline RL policy to produce the action a_t. The diffusion model is trained exclusively on a standard stationary offline RL dataset (b^j = 0), and the forecasting model operates zero-shot, so no non-stationary training data or retraining is ever required.

## Method

FORL combines two independently trained modules fused at test time. First, a conditional denoising diffusion model (FORL-DM) is trained offline with a noise-prediction objective to model p(s_t | τ_{(t,w)}), where τ_{(t,w)} is a window of (Δo, a) pairs; at inference it generates k multimodal candidate states via the reverse diffusion chain, capturing the fact that a given action-effect history is consistent with multiple possible true positions. Second, a zero-shot probabilistic time-series foundation model (Lag-Llama) forecasts l samples of the next P per-episode offsets from C past offsets, producing l candidate states per timestep via ŝ_t = o_t − b̂. The two candidate sets are fused by Dimension-wise Closest Match (DCM): for each state dimension d, DCM selects the forecast sample whose value is nearest to any diffusion candidate in that dimension, yielding a single state estimate without hyperparameters. For the first w timesteps of each episode (before the diffusion window fills), only the forecaster's mean is used. The entire pipeline is policy-agnostic and requires no environment interaction or policy updates at test time.

## Results

FORL achieves average normalized scores of 74.1 (maze2d-medium), 38.8 (maze2d-large), 75.1 (antmaze-umaze-diverse), 48.7 (antmaze-medium-diverse), 34.6 (antmaze-large-diverse), and 21.3 (kitchen-complete), consistently outperforming all baselines across five real-world time-series non-stationarity domains. On the OGBench tasks, FORL-F reaches 45.7 on cube-single-play and 42.2 on antmaze-large-navigate, versus 5.2 and 21.2 for the best baseline (FQL+LAG-s̄). FORL also achieves the lowest ℓ₂ prediction error in recovering true agent states, with a maximum error of 2.40 compared to 9.33 for the next-best fusion strategy (FORL-MAX).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FORL outperforms DQL [14] by large margins across all environments (e.g., 74.1 vs. 10.0 on maze2d-medium, 38.8 vs. 3.5 on maze2d-large), confirming that a standard diffusion offline RL policy without any state-estimation mechanism fails under non-stationary offsets. Against DMBP+LAG [3], which integrates forecasted states into a noise-robust predictor, FORL still wins consistently (e.g., 74.1 vs. 34.6 on maze2d-medium, 75.1 vs. 51.4 on antmaze-umaze-diverse), demonstrating that the conditional diffusion model's multimodal candidate generation plus DCM fusion surpasses a single-stage robust prediction approach. On the policy-agnostic evaluation (Table 2), FORL paired with RORL [25] achieves 71.9 average on maze2d-large versus 2.8 for RORL alone and 21.6 for RORL+LAG-s̄, while FORL with TD3+BC [2] reaches 34.4 versus 3.0 for TD3+BC alone. On OGBench tasks, FORL-F [23] dominates FQL (45.7 vs. 0.1 on cube-single-play; 42.2 vs. 12.0 on antmaze-large-navigate), showing the framework generalizes beyond diffusion-based policies. The only setting where FORL's advantage narrows is kitchen-complete (21.3 vs. 18.1 for DQL+LAG-s̄), where the manipulation task's lower-dimensional state space reduces the benefit of multimodal state estimation.

## Relation to Prior Work

FORL extends the robust offline RL line (DMBP, RORL) that handles test-time observation perturbations by replacing their assumption of known or stationary perturbation patterns with arbitrary, non-Markovian, time-evolving additive offsets drawn from real-world time-series data. It also departs from non-stationary RL approaches like ProST and Prognosticator, which assume full state observability and online interaction during training, by operating entirely offline with partial observability. The conditional diffusion model for multimodal state estimation repurposes the architecture of diffusion policies (DQL) for a different role—belief refinement from action-effect sequences rather than policy generation—while the zero-shot forecasting component (Lag-Llama) is borrowed from the time-series foundation model literature and applied here for the first time in an RL state-estimation context.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and addresses a concrete gap: offline RL agents deployed in environments where sensor calibrations, regulatory data aggregations, or hardware drift introduce unknown per-episode offsets that violate stationarity assumptions. The modular, policy-agnostic design (validated across DQL, FQL, TD3+BC, RORL, IQL) makes it a practical plug-in for existing offline RL pipelines. Key limitations include the restriction to additive (not multiplicative or general) observation transformations, the reliance on a univariate forecaster applied per-dimension, and the assumption that offsets remain constant within an episode. Open questions include whether the framework extends to general observation-space transformations, how it scales to high-dimensional continuous state spaces beyond the 2D maze/antmaze benchmarks, and whether the DCM fusion strategy remains effective when the diffusion model's multimodal candidates are poorly calibrated.
