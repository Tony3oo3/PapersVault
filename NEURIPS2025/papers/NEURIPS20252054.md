## Problem

The Duality-based Cost Function Market Maker (DCFMM) is the dominant framework for automated prediction markets, but its pricing mechanism is tightly coupled to the Follow-The-Regularized-Leader (FTRL) learning algorithm. The paper asks whether other optimization algorithms—specifically general steepest gradient descent—could inspire a new market maker design that preserves the DCFMM's axiom guarantees (no arbitrage, bounded loss, information incorporation, expressiveness) while achieving a strictly better worst-case monetary loss for the market maker.

## Input / Output

At each trading round the market maker receives a trade bundle vector r_t ∈ ℝ^d and the current market state q_t ∈ ℝ^d, and returns a scalar payment Pay_L(q_t, r_t) = ⟨∇C(q_t), r_t⟩ + (L/2)‖r_t‖², after which the state updates to q_{t+1} = q_t + r_t. The instantaneous price distribution is read off as ∇C(q_t) ∈ Δ_d. The choice of norm ‖·‖ (ℓ_1, ℓ_2, ℓ_∞, or general) determines both the quadratic fee magnitude and the geometric path along which the market state converges to a trader's belief, while the CIIP cost function C (e.g., softmax or sparsemax) governs the price mapping.

## Method

The Smooth Quadratic Prediction Market (SQPM) decomposes the DCFMM payment C(q_t + r_t) − C(q_t) into a linear price term ⟨∇C(q_t), r_t⟩ plus a Bregman divergence fee, then replaces the Bregman fee with the simpler upper quadratic bound (L/2)‖r_t‖² derived from the L-smoothness of C. This substitution yields a strictly higher collected revenue (hence better worst-case loss) while preserving Axioms 1–4. Although the SQPM does not satisfy full incentive compatibility in a single trade, the paper proves a form of incremental incentive compatibility: a sequence of agents sharing belief μ drives ∇C(q_t) → μ at rate 1/t, and the per-round optimal trade is exactly a gradient descent step (for ℓ_2) or a general steepest descent step (for ℓ_p) on the modified objective C̄(q) = C(q) − ⟨μ, q⟩. The paper further analyzes constrained variants (budget-bounded traders, buy-only markets) and sketches an adaptive-liquidity extension via a volume-parameterized smoothness schedule.

## Results

The Smooth Quadratic Prediction Market (SQPM) provably achieves a strictly better worst-case monetary loss than the DCFMM (Theorem 4), since its quadratic fee (L/2‖r‖²) upper-bounds the Bregman divergence fee charged by the DCFMM. The SQPM satisfies Axioms 1–4 (Existence of Instantaneous Price, Information Incorporation, No Arbitrage, Expressiveness) but does not satisfy the full Incentive Compatibility axiom (Axiom 5). Instead, it satisfies a weaker Incremental Incentive Compatibility (Axiom 6): a sequence of agents with the same belief μ drives ∇C(q_t) → μ at a 1/t rate, with the update rule corresponding exactly to gradient descent (ℓ₂ case) or general steepest descent (ℓₚ case) on C(q) − ⟨μ, q⟩.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is against the DCFMM (Abernethy et al., 2013), the standard cost-function market maker. The SQPM strictly dominates the DCFMM in worst-case monetary loss: for any fixed trade history, the total revenue collected by the SQPM is at least as large as that of the DCFMM (Theorem 4), because the quadratic upper bound L/2‖r‖² ≥ D_C(q_{t+1}, q_t) holds pointwise by L-smoothness. The DCFMM retains an advantage in that it satisfies full single-trade Incentive Compatibility (Axiom 5), whereas the SQPM only guarantees convergence to the agent's belief over a sequence of trades (Axiom 6). Both markets satisfy the core axioms of no arbitrage, information incorporation, expressiveness, and existence of instantaneous price. Under realistic constraints (budget-bounded traders, buy-only markets), the SQPM still converges to the agents' belief distribution regardless of the norm used (ℓ₁, ℓ₂, ℓ∞), as shown both analytically (buy-only) and experimentally (budget-bounded). No other market maker baselines are compared numerically; the contribution is a structural decomposition showing the SQPM replaces the Bregman fee with a simpler quadratic fee while preserving guarantees.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Duality-based Cost Function Market Maker (DCFMM) of Abernethy et al. (2013), which uses Follow-The-Regularized-Leader (FTRL) to set prices, and the Volume-Parameterized Market Maker (VPM) of Abernethy et al. (2014) for adaptive liquidity. The key conceptual departure is replacing the Bregman divergence 'curvature fee' in the DCFMM payment with a simpler quadratic fee derived from the L-smoothness upper bound, which shifts the collective learning algorithm implemented by traders from FTRL to general steepest gradient descent. The paper also formally establishes (in Appendix C) a tight equivalence between Continuous FTRL regret and DCFMM worst-case loss, a connection previously only hinted at in the literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The SQPM demonstrates that the price update rule and the fee structure in a prediction market can be decoupled while preserving core axioms (no arbitrage, bounded loss, expressiveness, information incorporation), opening a design space beyond the DCFMM's Bregman-divergence-based fees. However, the market does not satisfy full incentive compatibility—only an incremental version where a sequence of traders with the same belief converges to that belief—and the adaptive liquidity extension is explicitly left incomplete, with bounded worst-case loss and incentive compatibility under volume-dependent smoothness remaining open. Future directions include generalizing beyond AD securities, analyzing heterogeneous beliefs (reducing to stochastic gradient descent), and applying the framework to asset exchanges via the CFMM equivalence.
