## Problem

Protein language models are widely assumed to capture evolutionary information through large-scale sequence pretraining, yet it remains unclear whether they can actually perform evolutionary reasoning—inferring phylogenetic relationships among a set of sequences. This paper demonstrates that under standard usage (frozen or fine-tuned embeddings with distance-based comparison), existing PLMs consistently fail to recover phylogenetic structure, performing no better than a naive Hamming distance baseline, revealing a fundamental gap between sequence-level distributional modeling and inter-sequence relational inference.

## Input / Output

PHYLA takes a batch of unaligned protein sequences (tokenized over a 23-token alphabet: 20 amino acids + CLS, mask, pad) concatenated into a single input with a [CLS] token prepended to each sequence, and processes them jointly in one forward pass. The output is a per-sequence embedding vector (hidden dimension 256) whose Euclidean pairwise distances are designed to approximate true evolutionary (patristic) distances; these distances feed into neighbor-joining to reconstruct phylogenetic trees, k-means for taxonomic clustering, or TreeCluster-based label averaging for functional prediction. The model is alignment-free and guide-tree-free at inference, operating directly on raw unaligned sequences.

## Method

PHYLA is a 24M-parameter hybrid architecture that alternates between inter-sequence reasoning blocks (16 BiMamba layers with gated linear recurrence for detecting long-range conserved motifs across sequences) and intra-sequence reasoning blocks (a single sparsified attention layer that contextualizes motifs within each sequence via a binary mask restricting attention to tokens within the same sequence). The key training innovation is a quartet loss that replaces masked language modeling: for randomly sampled quartets of sequences, the model is penalized when the predicted pairwise distance sums do not preserve the correct quartet topology from ground-truth MSA-derived distances, teaching the embedding geometry to reflect evolutionary branching structure. This is the sole pretraining objective—no MLM is used—and the model is trained on 3,321 curated MSAs from OpenProteinSet with adaptive batch sizing that dynamically selects subtree sizes to fit GPU memory. The combination of joint multi-sequence processing, the sparsified attention mechanism, and phylogenetic supervision is what enables evolutionary reasoning, as ablations show that replacing the quartet loss with MLM or removing sparsified attention causes substantial degradation across all tasks.

## Results

PHYLA (24M parameters) achieves a normalized Robinson-Foulds (normRF) distance of 0.73 on TreeBase and 0.58 on TreeFam, outperforming all tested PLMs (best: ESM2-650M at 0.78/0.67) by 6.4% and 13.4% respectively. On taxonomic clustering (GTDB), PHYLA achieves species-level homogeneity of 0.98, a 23.2% improvement over the strongest PLM baseline (0.75). On ProteinGym functional prediction, PHYLA reaches a Spearman correlation of 0.64, competitive with models 27× larger. The traditional MAFFT+FastTree pipeline remains the accuracy upper bound (normRF 0.65/0.32) but requires 2–66 hours versus PHYLA's under 1 hour on a single H100 GPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PHYLA outperforms every PLM baseline on both tree reconstruction benchmarks: it beats ESM2-650M (the strongest PLM) by 6.4% normRF on TreeBase (0.73 vs 0.78) and 13.4% on TreeFam (0.58 vs 0.67), and surpasses ESM3-1.4B, ESM C, ProGen2-XLarge, and Evo 2 by even wider margins. On taxonomic clustering, PHYLA dominates at every level, with species-level homogeneity of 0.98 versus 0.75 for ESM2-650M and ProGen2-Large (a 23.2% relative gain). Notably, all PLMs—including 6.4B-parameter ProGen2-XLarge—underperform the naive Hamming distance baseline (0.75) on TreeBase, underscoring that standard PLM embeddings do not capture phylogenetic structure. The traditional MAFFT+FastTree pipeline still achieves lower normRF (0.65/0.32 vs PHYLA's 0.73/0.58), but requires 2 hours (TreeBase) to 66 hours (TreeFam) on multiple CPU nodes versus under 1 hour for PHYLA on a single H100 GPU. On ProteinGym functional prediction, PHYLA (Spearman 0.64) trails ESM2 (0.78) but exceeds ESM3 (1.4B) and Tranception, demonstrating that its tree-based representations retain meaningful functional signal despite 27× fewer parameters.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant PLM paradigm (ESM2, ESM3, ProGen2, ESM C) in two fundamental ways: it replaces single-sequence masked language modeling with multi-sequence joint processing, and it substitutes the MLM objective with a quartet-based tree loss that directly supervises phylogenetic topology. Architecturally, PHYLA draws on the BiMamba state-space module (Schiff et al., 2024) for inter-sequence motif detection and on hybrid state-space/transformer patterns (Poli et al., 2023b) for the overall design, but introduces a sparsified attention mask that constrains attention within individual sequences while allowing state-space layers to operate across the full batch. Conceptually, the paper reframes the relationship between 'evolutionary modeling' (capturing residue distributions) and 'evolutionary reasoning' (inferring tree structure), arguing that the former does not imply the latter—a distinction prior PLM work has not explicitly drawn.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central finding—that PLMs trained on hundreds of millions of sequences fail to recover phylogenetic structure and underperform naive Hamming distance on TreeBase—challenges a widely held assumption in the protein ML community and motivates a new class of 'evolutionary foundation models.' PHYLA's 24M-parameter model outperforming 6.4B-parameter PLMs on tree reconstruction while matching them on functional prediction suggests that task-aligned architecture and objective matter more than scale for relational inference. However, classical alignment-based pipelines (MAFFT + FastTree) still achieve substantially better accuracy, and PHYLA's reliance on Euclidean-space embeddings and neighbor-joining for tree construction limits its expressiveness for deep hierarchical structure. Open questions include whether the quartet loss generalizes to non-binary or reticulate phylogenies, whether the approach scales to genome-scale inputs without the chunking heuristic used for TB, and whether richer tree-based objectives (e.g., differentiable topology losses) could close the remaining gap to classical methods.
