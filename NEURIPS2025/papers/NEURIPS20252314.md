## Problem

Existing DNA foundation models face a three-way trade-off: autoregressive models are training-efficient but unidirectional, masked language models are bidirectional but waste ~85% of tokens per step, and k-mer tokenization sacrifices single-nucleotide resolution needed for SNP-level analysis. No prior model simultaneously achieves bidirectional understanding, single-nucleotide resolution over ultra-long contexts (>100k bp), and high training efficiency.

## Input / Output

The model consumes raw DNA sequences at single-nucleotide resolution (no k-mer grouping), processing both the forward strand and its reverse complement in parallel through identical model weights. It supports context lengths up to 1 million base pairs on a single 80GB GPU. The output is a per-position bidirectional embedding (fused from forward and backward representations, then RC-pooled) that serves as a fixed-length representation for downstream classification heads or eQTL scoring.

## Method

JanusDNA uses two independent stacks of Mamba + FFN/MoE layers (8 layers each, MoE replacing 50% of FFNs with 16 experts) to encode the sequence left-to-right and right-to-left separately, preventing premature information leakage. A FlexAttention fusion layer with a custom 4-case attention mask (Eq. 6) then integrates the two representations so that each token's prediction depends on all other tokens but not itself. The key training innovation—Janus modeling—computes cross-entropy loss over every token conditioned on its full bidirectional context, combining the per-token efficiency of autoregressive training with the bidirectional comprehension of masked modeling. Reverse complement is handled post-hoc by running the identical model on both strands and averaging embeddings, adding minimal inference cost.

## Results

On the Nucleotide Transformer Benchmark (18 tasks), JanusDNA-MLP (2.009M activated params) achieves state-of-the-art performance on 12 out of 18 tasks, surpassing models with up to 250× more activated parameters (e.g., NT-v2 at 500M). On the DNALongBench eQTL prediction task (450k bp sequences), JanusDNA (7.7M params) outperforms the expert model Enformer (252M params) on 8 out of 9 tissue datasets, with AUROC gains of up to +0.23 (e.g., Nerve Tibial: 0.914 vs. 0.683). On the Genomic Benchmark (8 regulatory element classification tasks), JanusDNA achieves SOTA on 3 out of 8 tasks, with the remaining tasks within ~1% of the best model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against same-scale SSM baselines, JanusDNA (2.0M) consistently outperforms HyenaDNA (1.6M), Caduceus-PH (1.9M), and ConvNova (1.7M) on 12/18 Nucleotide Transformer tasks, with the largest margins on histone markers (e.g., H3k4ME3: 0.675 vs. 0.549/0.544/0.566; H3k14AC: 0.718 vs. 0.612/0.631/0.644). Against the much larger Enformer (252M) and NT-v2 (500M), JanusDNA still wins on 12/18 tasks despite having ~125–250× fewer activated parameters, though it trails on splice site and some promoter subtasks where data scale appears more critical. On the DNALongBench eQTL task (450k bp), JanusDNA (7.7M) decisively beats both Caduceus-PH (7.7M) and the expert model Enformer (252M) on 8/9 tissues, with AUROC improvements of +0.05 to +0.23 over Enformer (e.g., Nerve Tibial 0.914 vs. 0.683; Skin Sun Exposed 0.846 vs. 0.700). On the Genomic Benchmark, JanusDNA matches or leads on 3/8 tasks (Human NonTATA Promoters: 0.954 vs. 0.951 ConvNova; Human vs. Worm: 0.971 vs. 0.973 Caduceus-PH; Human Regulatory: 0.877 vs. 0.873 ConvNova) but trails ConvNova on Coding vs. Intergenic (0.911 vs. 0.943) and Mouse Enhancers (0.769 vs. 0.784), which the authors attribute to the benchmark being near-saturated.

## Relation to Prior Work

JanusDNA sits at the intersection of three prior lines of work: SSM-based DNA models (HyenaDNA, Caduceus, Evo/Evo2) that achieve long-range modeling but are either unidirectional or rely on inefficient masked training; attention-based DNA models (DNABERT, Nucleotide Transformer) that offer bidirectional understanding but are limited to ~12k bp via k-mer tokenization; and hybrid architectures (Enformer, HybriDNA, Jamba) that combine multiple encoding mechanisms. The key departure is the Janus modeling paradigm, which predicts every token using its full bidirectional context—achieving autoregressive-level training efficiency (100% of tokens in the loss) while preserving the bidirectional comprehension that masked modeling provides. Architecturally, the Mamba-MoE-Attention hybrid draws on Jamba's interleaved design and Switch Transformer's auxiliary load-balancing loss, but is the first to combine all three for bidirectional DNA at single-nucleotide resolution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 250x activated-parameter efficiency (JanusDNA ~2M vs. Enformer 252M) and the ability to process 1 Mbp at single-nucleotide resolution on a single 80GB GPU make high-fidelity genomic modeling accessible to labs without large compute clusters, potentially accelerating eQTL discovery and variant-effect prediction. The Janus modeling paradigm is a general-purpose training objective that could transfer to other sequential domains where bidirectionality matters but masked training is wasteful. Key limitations include training on only the HG38 reference genome (no population diversity or non-human species), absence of multimodal integration (chromatin accessibility, single-cell transcriptomics), and no error bars on the DNALongBench eQTL results due to compute constraints—open questions that the authors explicitly flag for future work.
