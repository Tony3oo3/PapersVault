## Problem

In contextual bandits with large action spaces, standard Thompson sampling methods (LinTS, LinUCB) treat action parameters as independent, missing correlations that could dramatically improve exploration efficiency. While diffusion models excel at capturing complex high-dimensional dependencies, no practical algorithm existed for efficiently computing and sampling posteriors under a diffusion-model prior in an online bandit setting. This paper bridges that gap by developing diffusion Thompson sampling (dTS) with tractable posterior approximations that are exact in the linear-Gaussian limit.

## Input / Output

At each round, the agent receives a context vector X_t ∈ R^d and selects from K actions, with the reward drawn from a GLM P(·|X_t; θ_{A_t}). The diffusion prior is specified by L link functions f_ℓ (which can be neural networks) and L+1 covariance matrices Σ_ℓ, pre-trained offline on estimates of action parameters. The algorithm outputs a sampled action A_t and, internally, hierarchical posterior samples: a top-layer latent ψ_{t,L}, intermediate latents ψ_{t,ℓ}, and per-action parameters θ_{t,a}. The key structural constraint is that action parameters are conditionally independent given ψ_1, so the posterior is sampled top-down through the latent chain before drawing all K action parameters in parallel.

## Method

dTS decomposes the intractable posterior into a hierarchical chain of conditional Gaussians and approximates each layer with a closed-form Gaussian whose mean and covariance are data-dependent. Two approximation layers are applied: (i) a likelihood approximation that replaces the GLM reward likelihood with a Gaussian centered at the MLE with precision equal to the Hessian of the negative log-likelihood, and (ii) a diffusion approximation that generalizes the exact linear-case posteriors by substituting the non-linear link f_ℓ(ψ_ℓ) for the linear term W_ℓψ_ℓ. The resulting approximate action posterior has precision Σ_1^{-1} + Ĝ_{t,a} and mean as a precision-weighted combination of the prior mean f_1(ψ_1) and the MLE, while latent posteriors are computed via a recursive backward pass that propagates information gain from the action level up through all L latent layers. In the linear-Gaussian special case the approximation is exact, yielding a Bayes regret bound of Õ(√(n(dKσ_1² + Σ_ℓ d_ℓ σ_{ℓ+1}² σ_MAX^{2ℓ}))) that improves over LinTS by replacing multiplicative dependence on L with additive dependence when mixing matrices are sparse.

## Results

dTS consistently achieves the lowest cumulative regret across all four combinations of linear/non-linear diffusion priors and linear/non-linear (Gaussian/Bernoulli) rewards, outperforming all baselines in both small (K=10²) and large (K=10⁴) action spaces. The performance gap between dTS and LinTS widens monotonically as K increases from 10 to 5×10⁴, confirming the theoretical prediction that dTS's regret scales with Kσ₁² rather than K·Σ_ℓσ_ℓ². On the Swiss roll dataset (where the true prior is not a diffusion model), dTS outperforms LinTS by a factor of 1.5× with as few as 50 pre-training samples, and on MovieLens dTS also achieves lower regret than LinTS. Empirical regret scaling with K, d, and L matches the derived Bayes regret bound in the linear-Gaussian setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

dTS outperforms all five baselines across every experimental setting. Against LinTS [3] (the strongest baseline in most settings), dTS achieves substantially lower regret, with the gap widening as the number of actions K grows (Fig. 2a); on Swiss roll data the final-round regret ratio LinTS/dTS reaches ~1.5× with 50 pre-training samples. Against HierTS [33] (which marginalizes all latent parameters except ψ_L), dTS wins in both linear and non-linear diffusion settings, with the advantage most pronounced at large K where HierTS's two-level approximation loses information. Against LinUCB [1], dTS dominates in all linear-reward configurations, benefiting from Thompson sampling's natural incorporation of the informative diffusion prior. In non-linear (Bernoulli) reward settings, dTS outperforms both UCB-GLM [50] and GLM-TS [18], and notably, even a dTS variant with a misspecified linear-Gaussian reward (but correct diffusion prior) beats GLM-TS and UCB-GLM that use the correct Bernoulli reward but ignore the latent structure—highlighting that the diffusion prior captures more signal than the reward model alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the author's earlier linear diffusion bandit work [7] to the non-linear case and generalizes hierarchical Bayesian bandits [33] (which use simple 2-level Gaussian hierarchies) to arbitrary-depth, non-linear diffusion model priors. It builds on the Thompson sampling framework of Russo & Van Roy [59] and the diffusion model formulation of Ho et al. [30], but departs from prior diffusion-in-bandits work (Hsieh et al. [34], Kveton et al. [44]) by targeting the broader contextual bandit setting with large action spaces and providing an efficient closed-form posterior approximation that avoids MCMC. The key methodological departure from LinTS and HierTS is maintaining the full hierarchical latent structure rather than marginalizing it, which preserves action correlations at a fraction of the computational cost.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in large-scale recommendation and decision-making: standard bandit algorithms become statistically inefficient when actions are correlated but treated independently. The efficient posterior approximation (O((L+K)d³) vs O(K³d³) for joint posteriors) is a broadly useful technique for Bayesian inference under diffusion priors, applicable beyond bandits to RL and inverse problems. However, the Bayes regret guarantee holds only in the linear-Gaussian case where the approximation is exact; formal guarantees for the non-linear regime remain open, and the method's reliance on a pre-trained diffusion prior introduces a dependency on offline data quality that is only empirically (not theoretically) characterized.
