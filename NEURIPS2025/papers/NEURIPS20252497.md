## Problem

LLM serving systems treat each user prompt as an atomic unit, optimizing only through inter-query batching or token-level decoding tricks, while ignoring the latent semantic parallelism embedded within individual prompts. Many real-world prompts contain decomposable structures—repeated generations, multi-item extraction, parallel comprehension questions—that could be executed concurrently to reduce latency, but no prior benchmark systematically captures or evaluates this intra-query parallelism from natural user interactions.

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes raw first-user-message prompts from LMSYS-Chat-1M and WildChat-1M (over 2 million candidates) and produces a five-field structured schema per validated prompt: a task template with placeholders, shared context, either an itemized data list or an integer generation count (enforced as mutually exclusive), and a category label. Downstream, the execution suite takes these schemas and produces parallel subtask outputs alongside latency, normalized speedup, and LLM-judged semantic fidelity scores, with the normalized speedup explicitly correcting for the 'length budgeting' truncation bias that serial multi-item prompts exhibit.

## Method

The curation pipeline uses Claude 3.5 to generate full decomposition schemas rather than binary parallelizable/not labels, a design choice that surfaces both the structural decomposition and its reliability simultaneously. A three-tier validation framework (high-confidence with explicit structural markers, medium-confidence with softer linguistic cues, and rejected candidates) applies rule-based constraints including mutual exclusivity between data and n fields, template-placeholder compatibility, and minimum parallelism thresholds, prioritizing precision over recall. The resulting 37,070 validated prompts span seven canonical categories and 400+ novel categories across 11 languages. For evaluation, a category-agnostic C++ execution backend with fine-grained thread control and exponential backoff runs both serial and parallel strategies via GPT-4-1106-preview, measuring raw and normalized speedup alongside independent LLM-judge quality scores. The approach is distinguished from prior decomposition methods (SoT, ToP, LLMCompiler) by targeting naturally occurring unstructured prompts where parallelizable structure must be discovered rather than assumed from predefined schemas.

## Results

On the PARALLELPROMPT benchmark, parallel execution yields substantial latency reductions across all evaluated task categories: Reading Comprehension achieves the highest normalized speedup of 5.72× (raw 2.94×), Repeated Generation reaches 4.39× normalized (2.50× raw), and Keyword Extraction achieves 2.54× normalized (1.36× raw). Even including schema extraction overhead end-to-end, Repeated Generation still delivers a 3.41× normalized speedup. Quality preservation varies by task type: Reading Comprehension maintains 92% semantic fidelity, Keyword Extraction and NER retain 82–85%, while creative generation tasks drop to 76% preservation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its schema-based decomposition approach against two established task-decomposition methods: Skeleton-of-Thought (SoT, ICLR 2024) and Tree-of-Problems (ToP, EMNLP 2024). Both baselines fail on a significant fraction of the real-world prompts in PARALLELPROMPT. SoT's two-phase outline-then-expand architecture makes strong assumptions about task structure that do not generalize to extraction or analytical tasks (e.g., Named Entity Recognition, Keyword Extraction), where subtasks are flat and independent rather than hierarchical. ToP's recursive, dependency-focused decomposition adds unnecessary overhead for prompts with no inter-subtask dependencies, and its compositional structure is rarely present in everyday user prompts. In contrast, the paper's category-agnostic schema approach—requiring only a template, optional context, and either a data list or iteration count—achieves consistent 1.36×–5.72× normalized speedups across all seven canonical categories without task-specific logic, demonstrating broader generalization to the diverse parallelization patterns found in natural user prompts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves out a new problem axis—latent intra-query parallelism in natural user prompts—that sits orthogonal to both inter-query batching (vLLM, TensorRT-LLM) and token-level decoding accelerations (speculative decoding, Medusa, Lookahead Decoding). Prior prompt-decomposition methods (Skeleton-of-Thought, Tree-of-Problems, LLMCompiler) target synthetic tasks or require predefined schemas and symbolic APIs, and the authors demonstrate they fail on many real-world prompts in their benchmark. Rather than building on a specific prior decomposition framework, the paper repurposes LLM-assisted schema extraction (Claude 3.5) with rule-based multilingual validation to discover parallelizable structure in unstructured, naturally occurring prompts from LMSYS-Chat-1M and WildChat-1M, positioning this as the first systematic treatment of the problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that ~10% of real user prompts contain exploitable parallel structure quantifies a previously unmeasured optimization opportunity in LLM serving, with demonstrated 3–5× latency reductions on structured tasks. However, dependency blindness affects ~25% of structurally valid prompts, creative tasks suffer meaningful quality degradation (76% preservation), and East Asian languages show substantially lower validation rates (28–34% vs. 55–63% for European languages), all of which limit straightforward deployment. Open questions include developing task-adaptive strategies that detect when parallelism preserves semantic integrity, language-aware extraction methods, and whether the 10% prevalence rate generalizes beyond the specific extraction pipeline used. The benchmark provides a standardized testbed, but the reliance on a single LLM for both schema extraction and quality judgment introduces methodological circularity that future work should address.
