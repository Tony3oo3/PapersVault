## Problem

Existing early-exit DNNs rely on a static confidence threshold computed on a validation set, which fails under two conditions: models can be overconfident on wrong classes at shallow layers, and the threshold does not adapt to distribution shifts encountered during deployment. No prior method jointly addresses both the reliability of the confidence signal and the need for online, unsupervised threshold adaptation at inference time.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255684/cropped_page9_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts standard inputs per task (text sentences for classification, image+text pairs for vision-language tasks, text prompts for generation) and produces the corresponding prediction (class label, caption, summary, translation) along with an adaptively selected exit threshold that determines which intermediate layer the sample exits from. The threshold is drawn from a discrete set of 10 equally spaced values in [0.5, 1.0] and is updated online per-sample via a bandit policy, requiring no ground-truth labels at inference. A single global threshold is shared across all exit classifiers, and inference is performed per-instance (batch size 1).

## Method

UAT (UCB-based Adaptive Thresholds) operates in two stages. In the offline stage, a reliability function g (a single linear layer with shared parameters across exits) is trained with a modified loss L_i = L_CE · (1 + g + Φ(c − φ(g))) that couples cross-entropy to a reliability score, where the regularizer Φ enforces that g assigns high values to at least a c-fraction of samples, preventing trivial collapse. At inference, each candidate threshold τ in a finite set Ω is treated as an arm in a Multi-Armed Bandit; the per-sample reward is r(τ) = C_τ^i · (1 − C_g^i) − λ·i, where C_τ^i is the exit confidence, (1 − C_g^i) approximates the correctness probability p(y=ŷ|x,ŷ), and λ·i penalizes deeper exits. The UCB index Q(τ) + γ√(ln t / N(τ)) selects the threshold each round, and the sample exits at the first layer where the product C_τ^i(1−C_g^i) exceeds the selected threshold. Theorem 4.1 provides a high-probability bound showing that with λ ≤ ε/L, the empirical risk of UAT is at most ε + ε* (where ε* is the full-model risk) with probability (1−δ₁)(1−δ').

## Results

On BERT-large (GLUE + SQuAD), UAT achieves 95.8% on SST-2, 86.6% on MNLI, 68.3% on RTE, 92.4% on QNLI, 73.0% on QQP, and 83.2 F1 on SQuAD at 1.85× speedup—exceeding the full model on every task while accelerating inference. On T5-large (summarization, QA, MT), UAT attains 48.09 ROUGE-L on SamSum, 90.59 F1 on SQuAD, and 38.62 BLEU-4 on IWSLT at 2.10× speedup, with less than 1% drop from the full model. On BLIP-2 vision-language tasks, UAT reaches 43.3 BLEU-4, 142.9 CIDEr, 81.1 VQA accuracy, and 42.9 MRR on VisDial at 1.72× speedup. Across all settings, the speedup range is 1.70–2.10× with performance drops below 2% relative to the full model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UAT outperforms all eight baselines on both accuracy and speedup simultaneously across every task. Among early-exit methods, BEEM (ensemble-based) is the closest competitor, achieving 95.4% on SST-2 at 1.79× speedup, but UAT surpasses it by 0.4 points on SST-2 and 0.8 points on SQuAD while also delivering higher speedup (1.85× vs 1.79×). Among risk-based methods, CALM achieves the best accuracy (95.5% SST-2) but at only 1.39× speedup, whereas UAT gains 0.3 points on SST-2 and 1.0 point on SQuAD while nearly doubling CALM's speedup. On T5-large generation tasks, JEI-DNN achieves the highest speedup among EE baselines (2.05×) but suffers a 4.4 ROUGE-L drop on SamSum (44.45 vs full model 48.82), while UAT maintains only 0.73 ROUGE-L drop at 2.10× speedup. On BLIP-2 vision-language tasks, EERS (risk-based) achieves 42.5 BLEU-4 at 1.38× speedup, while UAT reaches 43.3 BLEU-4 at 1.72× speedup, outperforming it on both axes. Notably, UAT exceeds the full model's accuracy on all six BERT-large tasks (e.g., 95.8 vs 95.2 on SST-2), attributed to mitigating the 'overthinking' phenomenon in deeper layers.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the early-exit DNN literature (BranchyNet, PABEE, ZTW, BEEM) and the risk-control line (CALM, MIE-LAP, EERC) by reframing the exit-threshold selection as an online Multi-Armed Bandit problem, building on the UCB framework of Auer et al. and the distribution-free risk-control formulation of Angelopoulos et al. The key departure is the introduction of a learned reliability score g that calibrates raw confidence, combined with a reward function that jointly penalizes unreliable exits and unnecessary depth. Unlike prior risk-based methods that still rely on a static threshold tuned on a validation set, UAT adapts the threshold online and unsupervised, making it robust to test-time distribution shift.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is a consistent 1.70–2.10× speedup with under 2% performance degradation across text classification, summarization, QA, and vision-language tasks, while the theoretical risk bound (Theorem 4.1) provides a principled guarantee absent in prior EE methods. The reliability-aware confidence metric directly addresses the overconfidence problem that causes premature wrong-class exits, a known but under-addressed failure mode. Open questions include whether per-layer adaptive thresholds (rather than the current uniform threshold) yield further gains, and how the method scales when the number of candidate thresholds or exit layers grows substantially.
