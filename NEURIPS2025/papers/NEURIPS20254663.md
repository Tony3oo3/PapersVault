## Problem

Current MAS LLM literature appropriates multi-agent systems terminology without engaging with foundational MAS principles established since the 1980s, creating critical discrepancies in how agency, environments, coordination, and emergence are conceptualised. This position paper argues that the field risks reinventing the wheel and losing traction by overlooking decades of MAS research that has already addressed these problems.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254663/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The analysis surveys approximately 110 MAS LLM benchmark and evaluation papers (2023–2025) for environment characteristics, 60+ papers for emergent behaviour measurement methods, and 22 papers for asynchronicity implementations. The output is a structured critique organised around four MAS-theoretic dimensions, each paired with concrete research directions, rather than a new benchmark, dataset, or system implementation.

## Method

The paper systematically maps current MAS LLM implementations against four foundational MAS criteria: (1) social intelligence, showing LLMs lack native cooperative/competitive pre-training and fail Theory of Mind benchmarks; (2) environment design, demonstrating that LLM-centric textual environments inherit non-determinism, hallucination, and memory-persistency limitations; (3) coordination and communication, identifying the near-absence of asynchronicity and over-reliance on natural language over structured protocols like KQML and FIPA-ACL; and (4) emergent behaviour quantification, finding that the vast majority of surveyed papers use only qualitative descriptions without formal, falsifiable metrics. For each identified gap, the authors propose specific research directions including multi-agent pre-training with interactive feedback, multi-modal structured environments with formal planners, natively asynchronous frameworks inspired by Petri nets, and measurable definitions of emergence aligned with weak/strong emergence distinctions in computer science.

## Results

This is a position paper without a primary experimental results table. Key quantitative findings from its surveys include: 37% of MAS LLM failure cases are attributed to inter-agent misalignment or coordination issues; only 22 works (out of the surveyed 2022–2025 literature) explicitly model or discuss asynchronous agent interactions; and out of 60+ papers on emergent behaviours in MAS LLMs, only a few define clear metrics to measure emergence, with the majority relying on qualitative, observational evaluation. The paper also categorises ~110 MAS LLM benchmark papers (2023–2025), finding most operate in partially observable, deterministic, temporally dynamic, discrete environments with textual representations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

As a position paper, this work does not propose a new method to benchmark against baselines. Instead, it critically compares current MAS LLM frameworks (AutoGen, CAMEL, MetaGPT, MetaAgent, OpenAI Agents, Google's agentic framework) against established MAS theory and identifies systematic gaps. The paper highlights that multi-agent reinforcement learning agents outperform LLMs at planning tasks, underscoring the role of pre-training; that LLMs fail on Theory of Mind benchmarks (e.g., Ullman 2023, Shapira et al. 2023); and that CAMEL and MetaAgent exhibit role-swapping, hallucination, and infinite-loop failures when agents are specialised to subtasks. The authors argue that many MAS LLMs effectively reduce to aggregation mechanisms (e.g., majority vote) rather than genuine multi-agent coordination, and that natural-language-only communication is both costly and ambiguous compared to structured protocols like KQML or FIPA-ACL.

## Relation to Prior Work

This position paper carves new ground by systematically juxtaposing four decades of established MAS theory (Wooldridge & Jennings 1995, BDI architectures, KQML/FIPA-ACL protocols, Petri-net-based concurrency) against the 2023–2025 wave of LLM-based multi-agent frameworks (AutoGen, CAMEL, MetaGPT, ReAct, Generative Agents, Project Sid). Rather than proposing a new method, it repurposes classical MAS concepts—social agency, environment characterisation, performative communication, and formal emergence—to diagnose specific architectural gaps in current MAS LLM implementations. The paper departs from the prevailing MAS LLM literature, which largely treats multi-agent coordination as a prompting or orchestration problem, by arguing that the field is reinventing solutions that the MAS community developed in the 1990s.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper warns that the rapidly growing MAS LLM field risks stalling by ignoring foundational MAS principles, and its systematic surveys (only 22 of surveyed papers address asynchronicity; very few of 60+ emergence papers define quantifiable metrics) make the gap concrete. Open questions it raises—native social pre-training for LLMs, non-LLM-centric multi-modal environments, natively asynchronous agent frameworks, and falsifiable definitions of emergence—are substantial and would require new training paradigms and infrastructure rather than incremental prompting improvements. As a position paper, its main limitation is the absence of empirical validation for its prescriptions; the argument's strength rests on the authority of the cited MAS literature and the breadth of the survey rather than on controlled experiments.
