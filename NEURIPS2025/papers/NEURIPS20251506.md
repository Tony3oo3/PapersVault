## Problem

Existing robustness benchmarks for multimodal models evaluate single-modality or unimodal corruptions in isolation, leaving the critical question of how audio-visual recognition models behave under simultaneous, correlated distributional shifts affecting both modalities entirely unexplored. Real-world deployment scenarios (e.g., adverse weather for autonomous vehicles) naturally produce such co-occurring bimodal corruptions, yet no prior work systematically assesses state-of-the-art AV models under these conditions at test-time.

## Input / Output

The benchmark consumes 10-second audio-visual pairs (video frames + corresponding audio waveforms) from four source datasets and applies one of 15 corruption types at a chosen severity level (1–5) simultaneously to both modalities, yielding 75 distinct corruption tasks. For recognition, models output single-label class predictions (VGGSound: 309 classes, Kinetics: 32, Epic-Kitchens: 97 nouns + 300 verbs) or multi-label predictions (AudioSet: 527 classes, evaluated via mAP). For the TTA component, the adapted output is updated QKV attention weights in CAV-MAE's joint encoder, enabling on-the-fly cross-modal fusion without access to ground-truth labels.

## Method

AVROBUSTBENCH introduces 15 bimodal corruptions (Gaussian, Impulse, Shot, Speckle, Compression, Snow, Frost, Spatter, Wind, Rain, Underwater, Concert, Smoke, Crowd, Interference) at 5 severity levels, where each corruption simultaneously and correlatedly degrades both the visual and audio channels (e.g., Rain adds bluish raindrops to frames while overlaying hard rainfall audio). The paper evaluates six SotA supervised/self-supervised AV models and six online TTA methods, revealing that norm-based TTA overfits under bimodal noise and that READ's QKV adaptation develops a growing modality bias over time steps. To address this, the authors propose AV2C, which adapts QKV attention weights in CAV-MAE's joint encoder by minimizing a weighted Shannon entropy that penalizes high-entropy samples, while selecting diverse samples for entropy minimization based on similarity between current predictions and an exponential moving average of past predictions—achieving 5.32% and 8.5% mean accuracy gains over READ and SuMi on VGGSOUND-2C.

## Results

At severity 5, state-of-the-art supervised models show large performance drops: CAV-MAE drops from 65.50% to 35.54% mAcc on VGGSOUND-2C (ρ=0.54), and EquiAV from 86.02% to 63.73% on KINETICS-2C (ρ=0.74). Self-supervised models fare worse, with Wav2CLIP dropping to 4.99% mAcc on VGGSOUND-2C (ρ=0.21) and ImageBind to 10.25% (ρ=0.36). For TTA, the proposed AV2C achieves 44.06% mean accuracy on VGGSOUND-2C, outperforming READ (38.74%) by 5.32% and SuMi (35.56%) by 8.5%, while matching READ on KINETICS-2C (62.33% vs. 62.30%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On VGGSOUND-2C, AV2C (44.06% mean) outperforms all TTA baselines: it beats READ by 5.32%, SuMi by 8.5%, EATA by 1.08%, and dramatically outperforms TENT (19.09%), RPL (22.28%), and SAR (26.07%), which suffer from norm-parameter overfitting under bimodal corruptions. On KINETICS-2C, AV2C (62.33%) is essentially tied with READ (62.30%) and slightly ahead of EATA (61.16%) and SuMi (60.94%), while TENT (58.30%) and SAR (43.57%) degrade. Among supervised models evaluated on the benchmark, EquiAV achieves the highest relative robustness (ρ=0.74 on KINETICS-2C), while CAV-MAE leads on VGGSOUND-2C (ρ=0.54); all self-supervised models (AudioCLIP, ImageBind, Wav2CLIP) show ρ below 0.55, confirming that contrastive pre-training does not confer robustness to correlated bimodal shifts. READ's advantage under unimodal corruption (Tables 7–8) disappears under the proposed bimodal setting due to growing modality bias in its QKV attention, which AV2C mitigates by penalizing high-entropy samples during cross-modal fusion.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the robustness benchmarking lineage from ImageNet-C (vision-only) and READ (unimodal AV corruptions) to a genuinely bimodal setting where correlated corruptions hit both audio and visual streams simultaneously. For the TTA component, AV2C combines EATA's entropy-weighted sample selection with READ's QKV attention adaptation in CAV-MAE's joint encoder, but departs from both by restricting updates to low-entropy reliable samples and using EMA-based diversity selection. The benchmark itself is positioned against prior multimodal robustness efforts (MULTIBENCH, Hong et al.) by being the first to enforce co-occurring, correlated shifts across both modalities rather than treating them as independent perturbations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AVROBUSTBENCH fills a clear gap: no prior benchmark systematically evaluates AV recognition models under realistic, correlated bimodal distribution shifts, which is the regime that matters for safety-critical deployments (autonomous vehicles, wearables). The finding that existing TTA methods—including the purpose-built READ—degrade or fail under bimodal corruptions, while a simpler entropy-weighted QKV update (AV2C) recovers meaningful accuracy, reframes the TTA design space for multimodal models. Open questions include whether the modality-bias mechanism identified in READ generalizes to other fusion architectures, whether the benchmark's 15 corruption types adequately capture the diversity of real-world shifts, and whether TTA can ever close the large gap to clean-test performance under severe bimodal noise.
