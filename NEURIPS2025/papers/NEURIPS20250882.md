## Problem

Existing object-centric embeddings in 3D scene graphs are learned for specific downstream tasks (localization, alignment, retrieval) and cannot be decoded into explicit geometry or appearance, forcing systems to retain storage-heavy raw data (images, point clouds) alongside the embeddings. Conversely, high-fidelity 3D representations like 3D Gaussian Splatting produce monolithic scene-level models that lack object-level modularity and cannot serve as compact descriptors for semantic tasks. No prior method unifies a single compact embedding that is simultaneously decodable into high-fidelity 3D geometry and discriminative enough for diverse downstream tasks.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D scene reconstruction with object segmentation as input; for each object, it consumes a canonical point cloud or mesh (voxelized into a 64³ grid), multi-view posed images with instance masks (processed by a frozen DINOv2 encoder), and optionally text descriptions or scene-graph attributes. The output is a single compact U-3DGS embedding of 16³×8 = 4,096 parameters per object (~0.14 MB), which is decodable into a full set of 3D Gaussian splat parameters (position, scale, rotation, opacity, color) for rendering and mesh extraction, while also serving directly as a descriptor for localization, alignment, and retrieval without task-specific retraining.

## Method

Object-X operates in three stages: (1) multi-view DINOv2 features are projected into a 64³ voxel grid via multi-view projection to form a Structured Latent (SLat) representation, a sparse set of (feature, voxel-position) pairs; (2) a 3D U-Net compresses the SLat into a fixed-size 16³×8 unstructured embedding (U-3DGS), supervised by a masked MSE loss that ensures the embedding can be decompressed back to the original SLat; (3) a deterministic autoencoder maps the decompressed SLat to 3D Gaussian parameters, trained end-to-end with photometric losses (L1, SSIM, LPIPS), where Gaussian centers are constrained near voxel centers via tanh offsets for geometric consistency. For downstream tasks, auxiliary encoders (e.g., CLIP for text, scene-graph relationship encoders) produce feature vectors concatenated with the U-3DGS embedding, trained in two stages—first with the core frozen, then jointly fine-tuned with a combined reconstruction + task-specific loss—preserving decodability while enhancing discriminative power.

## Results

On 3RScan, Object-X achieves F1@0.05 of 77.80% for geometric accuracy, substantially outperforming all baselines (best baseline: 44.35% for 3DGS-12V) while maintaining competitive novel-view synthesis quality (SSIM 0.953, PSNR 30.98, LPIPS 0.065). On ScanNet (zero-shot, no training), Object-X reaches F1@0.05 of 89.09% and SSIM 0.966, again leading all baselines in geometry. Critically, Object-X requires only 0.14 MB per object (vs. 6.71–55.26 MB for image-based baselines) and reconstructs in 33–51 ms (vs. 58–129 s for optimization-based methods), representing 3–4 orders of magnitude storage reduction and 3–4 orders of magnitude speedup.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against optimization-based 3DGS and 2DGS baselines, Object-X achieves comparable or slightly lower photometric quality (SSIM 0.953 vs. 0.944 for 3DGS-12V on 3RScan) but dramatically superior geometric accuracy (F1 77.80% vs. 44.35%, a 33-point gap), while running 3–4 orders of magnitude faster (51 ms vs. 58 s) and using 48× less storage (0.14 MB vs. 6.71 MB). DepthSplat, a feed-forward baseline, fails to produce usable mesh geometry and lags Object-X on all photometric metrics (SSIM 0.619 vs. 0.953). For coarse visual localization on 3RScan, Object-X (using U-3DGS + auxiliary modalities) achieves R@1 of 56.6% and R@3 of 82.2%, outperforming SceneGraphLoc (53.6%, 81.9%) and CrossOver (46.0%, 77.9%). On 3D scene alignment, Object-X (zero-shot, no task-specific training) reaches MRR 0.910 and Hits@1 0.864, competitive with SGAligner trained specifically for alignment (MRR 0.884 with point cloud only; 0.950 with all modalities) and EVA (MRR 0.867). For single-image 3D reconstruction, Object-X (RGB-D) achieves F1@0.05 of 63.22%, outperforming the generative MIDI baseline (34.52%) by nearly 29 points, and also surpasses 3DGS optimized from a single view on all photometric and geometric metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

Object-X sits at the intersection of two previously separate research threads: (1) object-centric 3D scene graph embeddings (SceneGraphLoc, SGAligner, CrossOver), which learn compact descriptors for tasks like localization and alignment but cannot be decoded into explicit geometry, and (2) 3D Gaussian Splatting-based reconstruction (3DGS, 2DGS, DepthSplat), which produces high-fidelity geometry but as a monolithic, scene-level representation with no object modularity. The paper borrows the Structured Latent (SLat) voxel encoding from Trellis [31] as its intermediate representation and the 3DGS parameterization as its output, but departs from both by learning a single compressed, unstructured embedding (U-3DGS) that is simultaneously decodable into per-object Gaussians and discriminative enough for cross-modal retrieval and alignment tasks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 3–4 orders of magnitude storage reduction (0.14 MB per object vs. 6–270 MB for image/point-cloud baselines) while maintaining comparable photometric quality and substantially better geometric accuracy makes object-centric scene representation practically viable for AR and robotics where storage and bandwidth are constrained. The key open question is whether this unification holds at scale: the paper evaluates only on indoor scenes (3RScan, ScanNet) with relatively simple object geometries, and the authors acknowledge that high compression loses fine detail on large or complex objects. Integrating monocular 3D priors and extending to dynamic or outdoor scenes are natural next steps that the paper identifies but does not address.
