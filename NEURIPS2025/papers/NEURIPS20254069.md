## Problem

Existing brain foundation models capture either structural morphology or functional dynamics in isolation, and fMRI-based models are restricted to homogeneous repetition times (TRs), preventing integration of multi-site datasets. Moreover, prior work ignores the well-established neuroscience principle that cortical geometry constrains functional dynamics, leaving a gap in how structural information is leveraged to inform functional representation learning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254069/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes T1-weighted MRI volumes (167×212×160 voxels, intensity-normalized to [0,1]) and resting-state fMRI time series parcellated into 400 ROIs (Schaefer-400) with arbitrary TR values ranging from sub-second to several seconds. It produces a fixed set of 128 continuous-valued 1D brain hub tokens (each 768-dimensional) that form a compact, modality-agnostic latent representation; for downstream tasks, these tokens are average-pooled and passed through a linear projection head to yield disorder class labels or cognition scores.

## Method

BrainHarmonix uses a two-stage self-supervised pretraining pipeline. In the unimodal encoding stage, a 3D MAE (ViT-B backbone) learns structural representations from T1 volumes, while a JEPA encoder learns functional representations from fMRI with two key innovations: (1) geometric harmonics derived from the Laplace-Beltrami operator on a population-level cortical surface mesh serve as positional embeddings, pre-aligning functional ROIs with cortical geometry; and (2) Temporal Adaptive Patch Embedding (TAPE) dynamically adjusts patch size per TR to maintain a fixed temporal duration τ per token, resizing embedding weights via pseudoinverse resize (PI-resize) so the model handles heterogeneous TRs natively. In the multimodal fusion stage, 128 learnable 1D brain hub tokens are concatenated with frozen structural and functional latents and processed by a Harmonizer transformer; lightweight decoders then reconstruct each modality's latent from the hub tokens, training the tokens as a representational bottleneck that captures shared structure-function information. A novel data augmentation strategy further enriches fMRI pretraining by downsampling high-resolution scans to multiple hierarchical TR levels.

## Results

BrainHarmonix achieves state-of-the-art performance across all six benchmark datasets. On neurodevelopmental disorder diagnosis, it reaches 66.67% accuracy (F1 74.88%) on ABIDE-II and 70.09% accuracy (F1 66.72%) on ADHD-200, outperforming the strongest function-based foundation model BrainMass by 7.3 and 4.1 accuracy points respectively. On neurodegenerative and cognitive tasks, it attains 64.34% accuracy on PPMI (4-class), 64.65% accuracy (F1 68.75%) on ADNI, and a Spearman correlation of 0.42 on HCP-A executive function prediction, surpassing Brain-JEPA (ρ=0.26) by 0.16. Even the function-only ablation BrainHarmonix-F outperforms all prior fMRI foundation models, and linear probing with only 0.0015M trainable parameters already matches or exceeds prior state-of-the-art.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BrainHarmonix consistently outperforms all seven baselines across both neurodevelopmental and neurodegenerative benchmarks. Against the strongest function-based foundation model BrainMass, it gains +7.32 ACC on ABIDE-II (66.67 vs 59.35), +4.10 ACC on ADHD-200 (70.09 vs 65.99), +4.57 ACC on PPMI (64.34 vs 59.77), and +5.05 ACC on ADNI (64.65 vs 59.60). Against Brain-JEPA (the prior best fMRI dynamics model), it improves PPMI accuracy by +3.98 (64.34 vs 60.36) and HCP-A cognition prediction correlation by +0.16 (ρ=0.42 vs 0.26). Against the structure-based BrainMVP, it gains +10.96 ACC on ABIDE-II (66.67 vs 55.71) and +7.24 ACC on ADNI (64.65 vs 57.41). The only notable exception is ADHD-200 F1, where the full multimodal model (66.72) slightly trails its own function-only ablation BrainHarmonix-F (68.75), which the authors attribute to motion artifacts degrading T1 quality in ADHD patients. Task-specific models (BrainNetCNN, BrainGNN, BrainNetTF) are uniformly outperformed by 5–10+ accuracy points on most datasets, confirming the advantage of large-scale self-supervised pretraining over task-specific training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the authors' own Brain-JEPA (NeurIPS 2024) and the broader line of brain foundation models (BrainLM, BrainMass, BrainMVP) by being the first to jointly encode structural morphology (T1 MRI) and functional dynamics (fMRI) into a unified representation. It departs from prior fMRI foundation models by introducing TAPE to handle heterogeneous repetition times—a practical limitation that rendered BrainLM and Brain-JEPA infeasible on multi-site datasets—and by incorporating geometric harmonics (from Pang et al., Nature 2023) as a physics-informed positional encoding that pre-aligns functional tokens with cortical geometry. The multimodal fusion via learnable 1D brain hub tokens draws on the Perceiver-style bottleneck architecture but is novel in its application to neuroimaging, where prior work treated structure and function as separate modeling problems.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BrainHarmonix addresses two practical bottlenecks that have limited real-world deployment of brain foundation models: the inability to handle heterogeneous TRs across scanners and the lack of joint structural-functional representations. The finding that frozen 1D token representations with a simple linear probe already surpass prior SOTA suggests the learned latent space is genuinely informative, not just a large-capacity memorizer. Key open questions include whether the model generalizes to the full age spectrum (current pretraining covers 8–11 and 44–83, missing young adulthood), whether jointly optimizing unimodal encoders with the fusion module yields further gains, and whether the 1D token bottleneck can serve as a basis for a clinically deployable 'brain digital twin' as the authors envision.
