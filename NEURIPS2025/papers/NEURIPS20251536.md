## Problem

Despite rapid growth in protein sequence datasets and model sizes, three critical questions remain under-explored: what is the optimal training data distribution for large PLMs, how does model scale influence the diversity and viability of generated proteins in the wet lab, and how does model alignment with experimental data interact with scale. No prior work has systematically studied these questions or validated scaling effects experimentally.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251536/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

ProGen3 accepts amino acid sequences up to 8192 tokens and supports three generation modes: unconditional N-to-C or C-to-N terminal generation, and span infilling where target residues are replaced by sentinel tokens and appended to the sequence end with position-preserving fuzzy encoding. For alignment, the model consumes DMS assay fitness scores or Megascale folding free energy (ΔG) measurements as reward signals. Outputs include novel full-length protein sequences, infilled spans, per-sequence fitness/stability scores, and Rosetta energy estimates for in silico stability characterization.

## Method

ProGen3 is a family of autoregressive transformers with a sparse mixture-of-experts architecture (8 experts, top-2 routing, 27% active parameters per forward pass) spanning 112M to 46B parameters. Pre-training uses the newly curated PPA-1 dataset (3.4B full-length proteins, 1.1T tokens) sampled from an optimized Inverse Log distribution over 50% ID clusters, which the authors show improves out-of-distribution generalization over uniform or unmodified sampling. Compute-optimal scaling laws are derived for sparse PLMs (N_opt(D) ∝ D^1.479), guiding the 46B model trained on 1.5T tokens. The training objective combines causal language modeling with a generalized language modeling (GLM) infilling objective at a 1:2 ratio, using a truncated mixture of Gaussians for span lengths up to 400 residues. Post-training alignment uses Iterative Reasoning Preference Optimization (IRPO), a generalization of DPO that adds an NLL regularization term on highest-reward completions, to align model likelihoods with experimental fitness or stability measurements.

## Results

ProGen3-46B generates 49% valid protein sequences (vs. 40% for 3B, 30% for 339M, and 4% for ProGen2-XL) and covers a significantly wider diversity of 30% ID clusters. On ProteinGym fitness prediction, the stability-aligned ProGen3-46B achieves Spearman ρ=0.673, outperforming KERMUT (ρ=0.628) and matching ConFit (ρ=0.679). On Megascale stability prediction, aligned ProGen3-46B achieves ρ=0.737 overall (vs. ProteinDPO's ρ=0.72) and ρ=0.820 on multi-substitution variants (vs. ProteinDPO's ρ=0.468). The derived scaling law N_opt(D) = (2.462×10⁻⁷)D^1.479 predicts compute-optimal configurations, and the 46B model achieves a validation loss of 1.345, below the predicted frontier.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ProGen2-XL (the prior-generation dense 6.4B model), ProGen3-46B dramatically outperforms on generation validity (49% vs. 4%) and cluster diversity, and ProGen3 consistently outperforms ProGen2 on all alignment tasks at matched parameter counts. On ProteinGym fitness prediction, aligned ProGen3-46B (ρ=0.673) beats KERMUT (ρ=0.628) by 4.5 points and slightly trails ConFit (ρ=0.679) by 0.6 points, while being a far more flexible generative model that also handles insertions and deletions. On Megascale stability prediction, aligned ProGen3-46B marginally outperforms ProteinDPO overall (ρ=0.737 vs. 0.72) but substantially outperforms it on multi-substitution variants (ρ=0.820 vs. 0.468), despite ProteinDPO leveraging structural information and ProGen3 being sequence-only. The paper also shows that zero-shot fitness prediction actually degrades beyond ~3B parameters (Figure 4a), meaning the advantage of larger models is unlocked specifically through alignment with laboratory data.

## Relation to Prior Work

ProGen3 extends the ProGen2 line of autoregressive protein language models by replacing the dense architecture with a sparse mixture-of-experts transformer, deriving compute-optimal scaling laws specifically for sparse PLMs (complementing Cheng et al.'s dense-PLM scaling laws), and introducing a systematically curated 3.4B-protein dataset (PPA-1) with an optimized Inverse Log sampling distribution. The alignment framework builds on DPO and IRPO from NLP, extending them to protein fitness and stability prediction, while the wet-lab evaluation protocol (split-GFP expression assay) follows prior benchmarks (Ingraham et al., 2023) but is applied here for the first time to isolate the effect of model scale on generated protein viability. The paper departs from prior PLM work by treating data distribution optimization and model-scale effects on generation as first-class research questions rather than implementation details.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic wet-lab demonstration that scaling PLMs unlocks viable protein generation across a much broader diversity of protein families, with the 46B model producing valid sequences at 49% rate versus 4% for the prior-generation ProGen2-XL. The finding that models beyond ~3B parameters actually degrade in zero-shot fitness prediction but benefit disproportionately from alignment with laboratory data reframes the value proposition of large PLMs: their power lies not in out-of-the-box prediction but in their capacity to absorb supervised signal. Open questions include whether the scaling-law exponent (D^1.479 for sparse vs. D^1.370 for dense) holds at even larger scales, whether alignment can be extended to more diverse functional attributes beyond stability and activity, and whether the Inverse Log distribution remains optimal as datasets grow further.
