## Problem

Current conversational recommender systems present identical item images to all users, ignoring individual visual preferences expressed during multi-turn dialogues. No prior work has systematically addressed how to generate personalized visual content (e.g., movie posters) that simultaneously reflects a user's stated and implicit likes/dislikes from conversation history while preserving the identity and semantic content of the target item.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251160/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes three inputs: (1) a multi-turn conversation transcript processed by Qwen3-8B to extract weighted preference/dislike words, (2) the target item's original image serving as the subject condition, and (3) a set of historically liked item images that are spatially concatenated row-wise or column-wise into a single wide canvas (H × N·W × C) to serve as the background condition. The output is a single generated image (e.g., a movie poster) that blends the target item's identity with the user's preferred visual style and thematic elements, evaluated at 224×224 for CLIP/LPIPS metrics and at native resolution for FID/DIS.

## Method

PCG introduces a two-stage LoRA training pipeline on FLUX.1-dev with the EasyControl plugin architecture. First, a Global (style) LoRA is trained with flow-matching loss to capture task-specific visual style (e.g., movie poster aesthetics) without any visual conditioning. Second, a Local (background) LoRA is fine-tuned with the frozen Global LoRA, conditioning on the concatenated historical images and personalized prompts via the EasyControl subject/background branch separation. The Inclinations Analyzer converts LLM-extracted preference scores into weighted prompt tokens using γ(w_i) = 1 + t_i, which provides non-zero preservation of low-preference attributes and compresses extreme score differences. A Sequence Invariance strategy randomly permutes the order of concatenated historical images during training (via random permutation matrices P ∈ S_N), making the model's output independent of input ordering and interpretable as a flow-matching regularization toward the symmetric group invariant subspace. At inference, the personalized prompt attends to both subject and background branches through the local LoRA, while subject and background tokens remain isolated from each other, with fusion weights λ₁=1.0 and λ₂=0.85 balancing fidelity against personalization.

## Results

On E-ReDial, PCG achieves a CLIP-T of 26.52 (historical liked) and 30.52 (target item), with MS-SSIM of 0.1589 and 0.1938 respectively, and an FID of 21.88. On Inspired, PCG scores CLIP-T 26.23 (historical) and 29.56 (target), FID 22.41. In GPT-4o evaluation on the full E-ReDial test set, PCG attains the highest scores across all four dimensions: Visual Aesthetics 4.34, Integration 4.01, Thematic Coherence 4.17, and Detail Representation 4.38, outperforming the next-best baseline (EC-SUBJ) by 0.20–0.35 points per dimension.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PCG is compared against four baselines: SD3 and FLUX.1-dev (text-to-image), and EasyControl-SUBJ and EasyControl-BG (image-to-image). Against EasyControl-SUBJ, which achieves the best target-item fidelity (DIS 87.94, CLIP-I 77.92, LPIPS 0.5561, FID 20.20), PCG trades a small fidelity gap (e.g., DIS 85.76 vs. 87.94) for substantially stronger personalization (CLIP-T historical 26.52 vs. 25.97, MS-SSIM historical 0.1589 vs. 0.1437). Against EasyControl-BG, PCG dominates on nearly every metric, improving target CLIP-T by 3.56 points (30.52 vs. 26.96) and FID by 3.13 (21.88 vs. 25.01). Compared to FLUX.1-dev, PCG improves target CLIP-T by 3.03 points and FID by 4.63, while FLUX.1-dev edges out PCG only on historical MS-SSIM (0.1741 vs. 0.1589). In GPT-4o subjective evaluation, PCG leads all baselines on every dimension, with the largest margin over EC-SUBJ in Integration (4.01 vs. 3.82) and Detail (4.38 vs. 4.03), confirming that the dual-LoRA + visual-conditioning design yields a more balanced and coherent personalized output than any single-conditioning baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on two key threads: the EasyControl framework for dual-branch (subject/background) LoRA conditioning in diffusion transformers, and the In-Context LoRA approach that demonstrates DiTs can process concatenated images as unified visual context. It departs from prior personalized image generation work (DreamBooth, Textual Inversion, FLUX-based customization) by introducing an LLM-powered Inclinations Analyzer that extracts weighted user preferences from multi-turn conversation history, and by combining this textual signal with a visual content condition (spatially concatenated liked-item images) under a dual-stage LoRA training scheme. The Sequence Invariance strategy—randomly permuting concatenated images during training—is a novel regularization not present in either parent line of work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is one of the first systematic attempts to close the loop between conversational preference elicitation and visual content generation, a gap the authors identify as largely unexplored. Practical impact is clear for e-commerce, entertainment, and digital-assistant scenarios where identical item images are currently shown to all users. Key limitations include dependence on high-resolution source images, the quality of the LLM's preference extraction, and slow generation speed that hinders real-time deployment. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond movie posters to other visual domains, how it handles human faces and fine-grained identity preservation, and whether the concatenation-based conditioning scales to long conversation histories with many liked items.
