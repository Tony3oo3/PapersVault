## Problem

Existing graph condensation methods are simultaneously architecture-bound (relying on a fixed GNN encoder that limits generalization to other models) and task-constrained (requiring known labels during condensation, forcing re-condensation whenever the downstream task changes). This paper derives a generalized GC optimization objective from GNN optimization consistency, showing that all prior methods are special cases governed by a reconstruction term and a fitting term, and proposes a pre-trained framework that decouples condensation from both architecture and task dependencies.

## Input / Output

The method takes a large-scale original graph (symmetrically normalized adjacency matrix A ∈ R^{N×N} and node feature matrix X ∈ R^{N×f}) and produces a condensed graph with M ≪ N nodes, a discrete semantic assignment matrix M ∈ R^{N×M} that maps each source node to its condensed counterpart, and the optimal transport plan π*_Z encoding transfer probabilities. Notably, no task labels are required during pre-training; labels are transferred at test time via the traceable semantic harmonizer, which applies the assignment matrix to the original training labels to synthesize condensed labels. The diffusion intervals are sampled from a uniform distribution bounded above by 2/λ_max(L) to ensure numerical stability of the explicit Euler solver.

## Method

PreGC operates in two phases. In pre-training, a hybrid-interval graph diffusion augmentation solves the graph heat equation with randomly sampled diffusion intervals δt_τ ~ U(δt_min, 2/λ_max(L)), generating diverse spectral states that break architecture-specific bias (Proposition 4.1 guarantees full spectral coverage as the number of samples grows). For task-agnostic semantic alignment, the method computes a fused Gromov-Wasserstein transport plan π*_D over the graph space (jointly encoding feature and structural costs) and a Wasserstein transport plan π*_Z over the diffused representation space, then minimizes the Frobenius divergence between the two plans (L_plan) alongside the Wasserstein transport cost (L_cost). At test time, the optimal representation transport plan is discretized into a hard assignment matrix via argmax, which serves as a traceable semantic harmonizer to map original labels onto condensed nodes; an optional fine-tuning step (PreGC_ft) further refines this assignment using task-specific GNN predictions with a decay-rate interpolation.

## Results

On node classification (NC), PreGC achieves 81.1% on Cora (1.3% ratio) and 79.9% on Pubmed (0.08% ratio), matching or slightly exceeding the best baselines (GDEM at 80.8% and 78.7% respectively). On link prediction (LP), PreGC shows the most dramatic gains: 75.3% on Cora (1.3%) and 82.9% on Pubmed (0.08%), outperforming the next-best GDEM by 8.4 and 39.4 points respectively. On OGB-Arxiv (1.25% ratio), PreGC reaches 60.55% accuracy on the topic task, surpassing the best baseline SFGC (59.64%) by ~1 point, and is the only method capable of handling node regression on H&M (34.39% R² on price prediction). Across nine GNN architectures on Pubmed, PreGC achieves 79.8% average accuracy, exceeding the full-graph baseline (78.1%) and all other condensation methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PreGC consistently outperforms all six baselines on link prediction (LP), with the largest margins over GDEM (e.g., 75.3% vs. 66.9% on Cora 1.3%, 82.9% vs. 43.5% on Pubmed 0.08%) and GCDM (75.3% vs. 56.8% on Cora). On node classification (NC), PreGC is competitive with or slightly ahead of the strongest baselines: it edges out GDEM by 0.3 points on Cora (81.1% vs. 80.8%) and by 1.2 points on Pubmed (79.9% vs. 78.7%), while matching GDEM on Citeseer (70.6% vs. 70.6%). SFGC is the only baseline that cannot perform LP at all (marked N/A) due to its structure-free design, and it also lags PreGC on NC→NClu transfer tasks. On OGB-Arxiv, PreGC surpasses the best baseline SFGC by ~1 point on the topic task (60.55% vs. 59.64%) and by 6.3 points on the cross-task Y→T setting (59.62% vs. 53.32% for CGC). PreGC is the only method that can handle node regression (NR) on H&M, achieving 34.39% R² where all baselines report N/A, and the fine-tuning variant PreGC_ft further improves to 34.75%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the graph optimal transport framework (Chen et al., ICML 2020; Vayer et al., ICML 2019) and repurposes it as the core alignment mechanism for graph condensation, replacing the gradient-matching and distribution-matching objectives used by GCond, GCDM, SFGC, SGDD, GDEM, and CGC. Conceptually, it departs from the entire GC lineage by reframing condensation as a pre-training problem: rather than co-optimizing the condensed graph with a fixed GNN and task labels (as in all prior methods), PreGC uses a parameter-free graph heat equation for representation and a fused Gromov-Wasserstein + Wasserstein transport plan matching for semantic alignment, eliminating both architecture and task dependencies. The theoretical grounding draws on the distribution-matching unification from CGC (Gao et al., WWW 2025) and the eigenbasis-matching analysis from GDEM (Liu et al., ICML 2024), but generalizes their reconstruction/fitting decomposition into a task-free optimization objective (Definition 3.1).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PreGC is the first GC method that is simultaneously task-agnostic and architecture-agnostic, which addresses a practical bottleneck: in real-world settings (social networks, federated learning, continual learning), tasks and labels change frequently, and re-condensation is costly. The transport plan also provides a free interpretability byproduct (node significance scores) that prior GC methods lack. Open questions include scalability: the fused Gromov-Wasserstein computation is expensive (quartic in node count in the worst case), and all experiments are on graphs up to ~100K nodes (OGB-Arxiv); whether the approach remains tractable and effective on million-node graphs is untested. Additionally, the fine-tuning step (PreGC_ft) still requires a specific GNN and task, so the 'architecture-agnostic' claim applies only to the pre-trained condensed graph, not to the final adaptation.
