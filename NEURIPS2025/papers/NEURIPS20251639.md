## Problem

Classical spectral sparsification preserves the full Laplacian spectrum by sampling edges proportional to their effective resistances, but estimating those resistances requires solving large Laplacian linear systems, negating the computational gains of sparsification. This paper asks whether uniform edge sampling—a structure-agnostic strategy requiring no preprocessing—can suffice to preserve the spectral subspace needed for clustering, and identifies the structural conditions (a large structure ratio Υ(k)) under which it does.

## Input / Output

The method takes an unweighted graph G=(V,E) with n vertices and a target number of clusters k, and produces a reweighted subgraph with O(κ²·n log n / ε²) edges, where κ = λ_n/λ_{k+1} is the rank-(n-k) condition number. The guarantee is not full spectral approximation but a subspace-specific one: the top (n-k) eigenspace of the sparsified Laplacian remains approximately orthogonal to the k-dimensional cluster indicator subspace, with squared Frobenius norm bounded by k(1/Υ(k) + εκ/(1-ε)). This is weaker than a (1±ε) spectral sparsifier but sufficient to preserve the bottom-k eigenvectors used by spectral clustering.

## Method

The core insight is that in well-clustered graphs (large Υ(k)), the leverage-score distribution over edges is provably close to uniform, so uniform sampling implicitly approximates importance sampling. The paper introduces a rank-(n-k) effective resistance (Definition 4.4) that measures edge influence only on the dominant eigenspace, then proves that intra-cluster edges have uniformly bounded rank-(n-k) resistance (Lemma 4.5) and that inter-cluster edges constitute a small fraction of the edge set (Lemma 4.6). These bounds yield a relative comparison between the leverage-score distribution and the uniform distribution (Lemma 4.7), enabling a matrix Chernoff concentration argument restricted to the top-(n-k) eigenspace (Theorem 4.8) rather than the full space. The final guarantee (Theorem 4.3) follows by combining this subspace spectral approximation with a Davis-Kahan-type eigenspace perturbation bound to control the alignment between the sparsified bottom-k eigenvectors and the original cluster indicators.

## Results

The main theoretical guarantee (Theorem 4.3) shows that uniformly sampling O(κ²/((1−k/Υ(k))²(1−ρ_G(k))²) · n log n / ε²) edges yields a sparsifier whose top (n−k) eigenspace satisfies ‖Ṽ_{n−k}Ṽ_{n−k}^T C‖_F² ≤ k(1/Υ(k) + ε/(1−ε)·κ), where κ = λ_n/λ_{k+1} is the rank-(n−k) condition number. Empirically, on well-clustered Stochastic Block Model graphs (k=4, 200 nodes/cluster), uniform sampling achieves a slightly lower principal-angle error than effective resistance sampling across all tested γ values. On weakly-clustered graphs and LFR benchmark networks (800 nodes), uniform sampling tracks the error trajectory of effective resistance sampling closely, with both methods degrading as cluster separation weakens (larger γ or larger mixing parameter μ).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is Effective Resistance Sampling (Spielman–Srivastava [19]), which samples edges with probability proportional to their effective resistance. On well-clustered Stochastic Block Model graphs, uniform sampling actually achieves a slightly smaller principal-angle error than effective resistance sampling across all tested condition-number values γ; the authors hypothesize this is because uniform sampling implicitly undersamples inter-cluster edges, strengthening subspace alignment with cluster indicators. On weakly-clustered graphs (small intra/inter-edge probability ratio), the two methods produce nearly identical error curves, with uniform sampling neither clearly winning nor losing. On the hierarchical Stochastic Block Model (16 sub-clusters under 4 top clusters) and on LFR benchmark graphs (800 nodes, varying mixing parameter μ), uniform sampling again matches or marginally outperforms effective resistance sampling in the strong-structure regime, while the gap narrows as structure weakens. Overall, the paper demonstrates that for well-clustered graphs, the simpler uniform sampler is at least as good as the more expensive resistance-based sampler for preserving the spectral subspace needed for clustering.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two well-developed lines of work: spectral graph sparsification (Spielman & Srivastava, Batson et al.) and structure-aware spectral clustering (Peng et al., Macgregor & Sun). Classical spectral sparsification requires importance sampling proportional to effective resistances, which is computationally expensive; this paper shows that under a large structure ratio Υ(k), the simpler uniform sampling strategy provably preserves the bottom-k eigenspace needed for clustering. The conceptual parallel to the clustering coreset literature (Braverman et al., Huang & Vishnoi), where uniform sampling suffices for well-structured data, is explicitly drawn, but the technical machinery is distinct: the paper introduces a rank-(n-k) effective resistance formulation and a matrix Chernoff bound restricted to the dominant eigenspace, neither of which appears in the coreset or classical sparsification literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result provides the first provable guarantee that uniform edge sampling suffices for structure-preserving spectral sparsification, eliminating the expensive resistance-estimation preprocessing step that has been a practical bottleneck for scaling spectral clustering to large graphs. However, the guarantee requires a large structure ratio Υ(k) and a bounded rank-(n-k) condition number κ, and the sampling rate scales as κ², which may be restrictive for real-world graphs with heterogeneous cluster sizes or weak spectral gaps. Open questions include extending the analysis to weighted graphs, overlapping clusters, and tightening the dependence on κ; the experiments are limited to small synthetic graphs (≤800 nodes), leaving scalability to truly large networks untested.
