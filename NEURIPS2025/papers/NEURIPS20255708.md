## Problem

Pareto Set Learning (PSL) has emerged as a powerful paradigm for multi-objective optimization, but applying it to black-box objectives—where neither analytic expressions nor derivatives are available—remains largely unsolved. Traditional evolution strategies, which excel at capturing dimensional dependencies, face prohibitive computational complexity from full covariance matrices and cannot leverage their sophisticated adaptation strategies across the many decomposed subproblems inherent to PSL, making direct integration ineffective.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255708/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a preference vector α uniformly sampled from the (M−1)-dimensional simplex and a black-box objective F(x) that provides only function values (no gradients or analytic form). The set model φ_θ outputs, for each α, a distribution over solutions parameterized by a mean, step-size, and direction vector per segment; a solution is then generated sequentially by sampling p segments of length d conditioned on all previously sampled segments. The trained model can produce a tailored Pareto optimal solution for any valid preference without re-optimization, recovering the full Pareto set in both solution and objective spaces.

## Method

Neural-ES partitions the n-dimensional decision variable into p segments of length d and factorizes the search distribution as a chain of conditional Gaussians, where a neural network parameterizes each segment's mean, step-size, and rank-one covariance direction (σ²(I + vvᵀ)) conditioned on all preceding segments. This autoregressive structure with rank-one covariances reduces space and time complexity to O(n), avoiding the Θ(n²)–Θ(n⁴) cost of full covariance matrices. Training uses a novel gradient estimator derived from the natural gradient: the Fisher information matrix is absorbed into the update rule, and rank-dependent log-weights replace raw objective values to prevent degenerate search distributions. The PSL formulation employs a reparameterization trick with nested expectations, so the ES acts as the back-end of the set model, sampling solutions and back-propagating estimated gradients through the chain rule to update the network parameters.

## Results

On the BBO-PSL suite (8 instances, dimensions 32–1024), Neural-ES achieves the lowest testing IGD across all instances, outperforming EPSL, EPSL-R1, LMO-CSO, and IM-MOEA/D by a large margin (Figure 4). On the MO-UAV navigation problems, Neural-ES attains HV values of 3.40±0.28 (easy, n=30), 3.44±0.45 (moderate, n=60), and 2.44±1.14 (hard, n=120), substantially exceeding EPSL (2.47, 2.40, 0.72) and EPSL-R1 (1.58, 1.52, 0.84). On the 100-dimensional trajectory planning task, Neural-ES achieves an average HV of 8.314±0.215 versus 5.144±0.622 for EPSL-R1 and 4.186±0.709 for EPSL, with 299 out of 300 EPSL solutions dominated by Neural-ES solutions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Neural-ES decisively outperforms all four baselines on every BBO-PSL instance (F1–F8) across dimensions 32 to 1024 in testing IGD. Against EPSL [15], the gap is the largest: EPSL's finite-difference approach fails to learn dimensional dependencies, producing scattered solutions poorly aligned with preference vectors, while Neural-ES's neural-network-parameterized conditional Gaussians capture these dependencies effectively. EPSL-R1 (p=1, the simplest ES-in-PSL integration) performs only marginally better than EPSL on a few instances and its advantage dissipates as dimensionality grows, confirming that naively plugging an ES into an adaptation-free PSL setting is insufficient. The two MOEA baselines—LMO-CSO [35] and IM-MOEA/D [36]—struggle to scale when users require numerous trade-offs due to their heavy evaluation budgets and limited generalization, whereas the PSL paradigm (and Neural-ES in particular) generalizes to arbitrary new preferences without re-optimization. On the real-world MO-UAV problems, Neural-ES achieves roughly 38–40% higher HV than EPSL on easy/moderate cases and over 3× higher HV on the hard case (2.44 vs. 0.72), while on trajectory planning it dominates 299/300 EPSL solutions and 194/300 EPSL-R1 solutions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of Pareto Set Learning (PSL) and Evolution Strategies (ES). PSL, introduced by Lin et al. [9, 15], learns a neural network mapping preferences to Pareto-optimal solutions, but prior work was limited to expensive (Gaussian-process-based) or smooth MOPs, with derivative-free variants (EPSL) validated only on low-dimensional toy problems. On the ES side, CMA-ES and NES handle dimensional dependencies via adaptive covariance matrices, but their Θ(n²) space and Θ(n⁴) time complexity, plus reliance on evolution-path adaptation strategies, make them intractable within PSL's many-subproblem structure. Neural-ES departs from both lines by replacing the explicit covariance matrix with a neural network that conditions each variable segment on all preceding segments, and by deriving a natural-gradient-based update rule that avoids the instability of vanilla score-function gradients when the covariance shrinks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to scale the PSL paradigm to over 1,000 dimensions in a black-box setting, and the introduced BBO-PSL benchmark (8 instances, 32–1024D, non-separable, with known ground-truth PS/PF) fills a gap in evaluation infrastructure for black-box multi-objective learning. The finding that naively plugging a rank-one ES into PSL (EPSL-R1) yields only marginal gains over finite-difference EPSL is a useful negative result: it shows that the adaptation-free, many-subproblem structure of PSL fundamentally changes what makes an ES effective. Open questions include extending to constrained MOPs and understanding why the reparameterized objective in (4) may only guarantee approximate Pareto stationary points rather than exact ones, as noted by Ye et al. [7].
