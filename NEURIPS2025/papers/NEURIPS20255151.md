## Problem

Despite a surge of federated prompt learning (FPL) algorithms for adapting vision-language models, existing evaluations suffer from inconsistent experimental protocols, limited scenario coverage, and the absence of adapted centralized prompt learning baselines, obscuring the true relative strengths of each method. No standardized, comprehensive benchmark exists to reliably compare FPL algorithms across diverse federated settings such as data heterogeneity, few-shot learning, novel-class generalization, and cross-domain shifts.

## Input / Output

Each FPL method takes an RGB image and a text prompt (learned soft prompt vectors concatenated with the class name embedding) as input. The frozen CLIP image encoder (ResNet-50 or ViT-B/16) and text encoder map both modalities into a shared embedding space, and classification is performed via temperature-scaled cosine similarity between the image feature and each class's text feature, yielding a probability distribution over classes. In the federated setting, only the small prompt parameters (not full model weights) are communicated between clients and server, drastically reducing per-round communication cost.

## Method

FLIP is a unified, modular, open-source benchmark that faithfully implements 13 FPL baselines—six native federated methods (PromptFL, FedOTP, FedTPG, FedPGP, PromptFolio, DP-FPL), six centralized prompt learning methods adapted to federation via FedAvg (f-CoCoOp, f-PLOT, f-ProDA, f-ProGrad, f-SRC, f-KgCoOp), and a zero-shot CLIP reference—under three FL protocols (standard learning with 10 clients, partial participation with 100 clients at 10% sampling, and personalized learning) across six evaluation scenarios on 12 datasets. All methods share a common CLIP backbone with frozen encoders and learn only text-side soft prompt vectors of length 4, with Dirichlet(α=0.1) non-IID data partitioning by default. The benchmark introduces a superiority indicator (count of datasets where a method beats the PromptFL baseline) alongside standard accuracy metrics, and includes sensitivity analyses of prompt count and length to characterize cost-performance trade-offs. Key empirical findings include: PromptFL (CoOp + FedAvg) is a surprisingly strong baseline; regularization-based methods (f-SRC, f-KgCoOp) reduce client drift for global and cross-domain tasks; FedOTP's optimal-transport-based global-local prompt cooperation excels in personalized and few-shot settings; and no single method dominates all scenarios.

## Results

FLIP benchmarks 13 FPL methods across 8 image classification datasets under 6 evaluation scenarios. On global shared FPL (Table 1), f-KgCoOp achieves the best average accuracy of 69.7%, with f-ProDA and PromptFL close behind at 69.6%, while the zero-shot CLIP baseline scores 61.3%. On personalized FPL (Table 2), FedOTP leads with 76.5% average accuracy, outperforming f-PLOT (75.5%) and f-ProDA (75.4%). On few-shot learning (k=1 to 16 on Tiny-ImageNet, Table 4), FedOTP achieves the best average of 45.8%, and on cross-domain generalization (Table 8), f-CoCoOp and f-KgCoOp lead at 39.9% and 39.8% average respectively. No single method dominates all scenarios, confirming the absence of a silver bullet for FPL.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PromptFL (CoOp + FedAvg) emerges as a surprisingly strong baseline, matching or exceeding most FPL methods on global shared accuracy (69.6% avg) and even winning on fine-grained datasets like Oxford-Pets (89.4%) and Flowers (84.0%). f-KgCoOp and f-ProDA are the top global performers (69.7% and 69.6% avg), with f-KgCoOp winning on Aircraft (23.0%) and UCF (70.4%), while f-ProDA leads on Aircraft (23.1%) and UCF (70.2%). FedOTP dominates personalized FPL (76.5% avg vs. 75.2% for PromptFL) and few-shot learning (45.8% avg at k=1–16), attributed to its global-local prompt cooperation with optimal transport. f-CoCoOp consistently underperforms PromptFL on global accuracy (67.2% vs. 69.6%), likely due to its image feature aggregation module being sensitive to non-i.i.d. data heterogeneity. On cross-domain generalization, f-CoCoOp (39.9%) and f-KgCoOp (39.8%) lead, benefiting from test-time image feature injection and self-regularization. Regularization-based methods (f-SRC, f-KgCoOp) show particular advantages under client sub-sampling (62.6% avg) and base-to-novel class generalization, where PromptFL is less effective.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two active research threads: prompt learning for vision-language models (CoOp, CoCoOp, PLOT, ProDA, ProGrad, SRC, KgCoOp) and federated learning (FedAvg and its variants). Rather than proposing a new FPL algorithm, FLIP carves new ground as the first unified, standardized benchmark specifically for FPL on vision-language models, addressing three gaps the authors identify in prior FPL papers: inconsistent experimental protocols, limited evaluation scenarios, and the absence of properly adapted centralized PL baselines. It is conceptually distinct from general FL benchmarks (LEAF, TFF, FedML) and the related PROFIT benchmark, which targets LLM prompt tuning rather than VLM adaptation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FLIP provides the FPL community with a reproducible, extensible evaluation framework that will likely become the default reference for future FPL papers, reducing the risk of overclaiming from inconsistent protocols. The finding that no single method dominates across all six scenarios (global, personalized, few-shot, novel-class, cross-domain, cost-performance) and that the simple PromptFL baseline is surprisingly competitive redirects attention toward scenario-specific algorithm design rather than universal solutions. Open questions remain around safety implications of PL in federated settings, robustness against adversarial clients, and whether the observed regularizations' diminishing returns in personalized settings reflect a fundamental tension between global and local objectives.
