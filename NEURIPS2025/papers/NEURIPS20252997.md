## Problem

Diffusion Language Models (DLMs) underperform autoregressive models in both likelihood modeling and generated text quality. The paper identifies that this gap arises when important tokens (low-frequency or semantically key words) are masked early in the forward process, depriving the model of the contextual information needed for accurate reconstruction of the remaining sequence.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252997/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The diffusion model takes a partially masked text sequence (tokens replaced by a special mask symbol) and produces a fully unmasked sequence through iterative denoising. Internally, the anchor network outputs a sequence of soft mixture distributions over the vocabulary—skewed toward important tokens for masked positions and peaked at true values for already-unmasked positions—which the denoiser then consumes as conditioning. For the AR variant (ACoT), the input is a question with [ANT] tokens inserted before the reasoning trace, and the output is a reasoning trace plus final answer, with anchor embeddings processed in latent space without explicit decoding.

## Method

ADLM splits the standard one-step denoising into a two-stage anchored process: an Anchor Transformer (φ) predicts mixture distributions over important tokens from the masked input, and a Denoising Transformer (ψ) conditions on these soft anchor predictions to unmask remaining tokens. The reverse transition is reparameterized as p_θ(z_s|z_t) = q(z_s|z_t, x_ψ(y_φ(z_t))), where the anchor network introduces intermediate latent reasoning over key tokens before the denoiser resolves the full sequence. Training uses the Anchored Negative ELBO (ANELBO), which jointly optimizes both networks end-to-end with a weighted anchor loss imposed only on important tokens, reducing the denoiser's burden of modeling high-entropy distributions over missing key words. Theoretically, anchoring reduces sample complexity from O(K^L) to O(LK^{d+1}) in directed graphical models by restricting each token's conditioning set to a bounded-size anchor set. Anchor selection is task-specific: relative frequency (TF-IDF-inspired) for generative modeling, digit extraction for math reasoning, and verb/noun extraction for logical reasoning.

## Results

On LM1B, ADLM achieves a test PPL of 26.40 at 33B tokens (vs. MDLM's 27.04) and 24.46 at 65B tokens, narrowing the gap with AR models (21.55). On OpenWebText at 524B tokens, ADLM reaches PPL 20.14, outperforming MDLM (22.98) by 12.3% and matching the hybrid BD3LM (20.73). With the remasking sampler, ADLM achieves a MAUVE score of 0.791 at T=4096, surpassing the AR baseline (0.760) for the first time among diffusion models. For reasoning, ACoT reaches 45.2% on GSM8K (vs. CoT 42.9%, COCONUT 34.1%) and 97.3% on ProsQA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ADLM outperforms all prior diffusion baselines on both LM1B and OWT: it beats MDLM by 9.5% (LM1B, 33B) and 12.3% (OWT, 524B), SEDD by 25.4% (OWT, 262B), and GIDD by 8.1% (OWT, 262B). Against the hybrid BD3LM (L′=4), ADLM matches its OWT PPL (20.14 vs. 20.73) while using a purely diffusion architecture. In zero-shot evaluation (Table 4), ADLM surpasses the AR baseline on Lambada (44.32 vs. 51.28), PubMed (37.56 vs. 49.01), and ArXiv (33.69 vs. 41.73), and outperforms MDLM on 6 of 7 benchmarks. With the remasking sampler, ADLM's MAUVE score (0.791 at T=4096) exceeds AR (0.760) and ReMDM (0.656), while also achieving lower GPT-2 Large PPL (15.9 vs. 17.6 for ReMDM). For reasoning, ACoT outperforms CoT (45.2% vs. 42.9% on GSM8K), COCONUT (34.1%), iCoT (30.0%), and BoLT (33.6%), and achieves 100% on ProntoQA and 97.3% on ProsQA, surpassing all prior latent-reasoning methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

ADLM builds directly on the absorbing discrete diffusion framework of D3PM (Austin et al., 2021) and the masked diffusion language model parameterization of MDLM (Sahoo et al., 2024), which it generalizes by inserting an intermediate anchor-prediction stage before the standard denoising step. The key conceptual departure is the two-stage anchored denoising: rather than a single network predicting all masked tokens simultaneously, an Anchor Transformer first produces soft predictions skewed toward important (low-frequency) tokens, and a Denoising Transformer then conditions on these to resolve the remaining positions. The theoretical contribution extends the standard NELBO to an Anchored NELBO (ANELBO), and the anchoring idea is further transplanted into autoregressive models as Anchored Chain-of-Thought (ACoT), connecting to the latent-reasoning line of work (COCONUT, BoLT) but with explicit token-level supervision rather than purely continuous latent variables.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ADLM marks the first time a diffusion language model surpasses an AR model in MAUVE score (human-like text quality), and narrows the perplexity gap with AR baselines by up to 25% over prior DLMs at GPT-2 scale. The anchoring mechanism is lightweight (a half-depth denoiser suffices when guided by anchors) and generalizes beyond diffusion to improve AR reasoning on math and logic tasks, suggesting a broadly applicable principle: resolving high-information tokens early reduces the effective entropy the model must model. A key limitation acknowledged by the authors is that token importance is defined heuristically (frequency-based for LM, digit/verb-noun extraction for reasoning), and whether adaptive or LLM-guided anchoring can replace these hand-crafted criteria remains open. Scaling beyond GPT-2 to larger models and more diverse domains is the natural next step to validate that the anchoring benefit persists at scale.
