## Problem

Existing expressivity results for RNNs (e.g., Sarrof et al. 2024, Grazzi et al. 2025) rely on incompatible finite-precision frameworks with different assumptions, making cross-architecture comparisons difficult and results hard to verify. There is no single principled theory that unifies the analysis of diverse RNN architectures—vanilla RNNs, xLSTM, SSMs—under a common formal language, while simultaneously addressing both idealized and real-world finite-precision settings.

## Input / Output

The method takes a continuous dynamical system (state space X ⊆ ℝ^d or ℂ^d, input space U, continuous dynamics function f) representing an RNN architecture, along with a formal language over a finite alphabet. It produces a canonical finite automaton capturing the system's expressivity, an expressivity classification (e.g., star-free vs. non-star-free), and a robustness certificate (ε-robustness) guaranteeing that floating-point implementations with sufficient precision preserve the system's language-recognition behavior. The η-finiteness condition (finite union of compact, path-connected components) replaces the discrete finiteness of classical automata, allowing continuous state spaces to be analyzed with algebraic automata theory tools.

## Method

Metric Automata Theory (MAT) generalizes classical automata theory to continuous dynamical systems through four key constructs: (1) η-finiteness, which partitions a continuous state space into compact path-connected components that behave as discrete states; (2) a canonical automaton mapping that translates any η-finite system into a finite automaton, enabling direct application of Algebraic Automata Theory (Krohn-Rhodes decomposition, group-free/aperiodic characterizations); (3) ε-robustness, a geometric condition on the dynamics ensuring that small numerical perturbations (as in floating-point arithmetic) cannot change the system's η-state transitions, thereby bridging idealized theory to real-world implementations; and (4) geometrically-constrained systems (GCS), which relax η-finiteness to allow unbounded state spaces with piecewise-linearly separable output regions, capturing empirical length-generalization phenomena. Using this framework, the paper proves that xLSTM cascades robustly recognize all star-free languages, that SSMs with non-negative eigenvalue gates are aperiodic and thus restricted to star-free languages, and that Mamba as a GCS can approximate star-free languages with length-generalization but cannot implement alternating functions like Parity.

## Results

The paper's main contributions are theoretical expressivity results rather than benchmark scores. The headline findings are: (1) xLSTM cascades can robustly recognize all star-free regular languages (Theorem 5), while SSMs with non-negative eigenvalue gates (including Mamba) cannot robustly implement FLIP-FLOP (Theorem 7); (2) as Geometrically-Constrained Systems, Mamba can recognize all star-free languages (Theorem 8) but cannot implement alternating functions like Parity or modulo-M counting (Theorem 9). Empirically, 1-layer Mamba achieves 100% validation accuracy on the FLIP-FLOP task but exhibits state-space collapse after ~1000 iterated 'ignore' inputs, confirming the theoretical prediction. On the Bhattamishra et al. (2023) benchmark, Mamba achieves perfect accuracy on all 11 star-free tasks with length-generalisation, while struggling on non-star-free (modulo counting) tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not benchmark a single proposed method against baselines; instead it provides a unified theoretical framework comparing the expressivity of multiple RNN architectures. The key architectural contrast is between xLSTM (non-linear recurrence) and SSMs (linear recurrence): xLSTM admits strongly robust realisations of FLIP-FLOP and hence all star-free languages, while SSMs with non-negative eigenvalue gates are provably restricted to star-free languages and cannot robustly implement FLIP-FLOP. Within SSMs, Mamba's parametrisation makes it a contracting dynamics that cannot implement FLIP-FLOP in the η-finite (finite-precision) setting, yet as a Geometrically-Constrained System it can approximate star-free languages with length-generalisation—explaining its strong empirical performance on star-free tasks. On the Chomsky Hierarchy benchmark, DeltaNet with negative eigenvalues can model Modular Arithmetic w/o Brackets but falls short of perfect accuracy across all sequence lengths, whereas xLSTM (sLSTM) reportedly achieves perfect accuracy on this task, supporting the paper's argument that non-linear recurrences offer a robustness advantage over linear ones.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the dynamical systems formalism of Knorozova and Ronca (2024a,b) for RNN expressivity, and imports the algebraic machinery of Krohn-Rhodes decomposition and Mealy machine realisation from Hartmanis and Stearns (1966) into the continuous setting. It departs from the fragmented finite-precision analyses of Sarrof et al. (2024) and Grazzi et al. (2025), which used incompatible frameworks and assumptions, by unifying them under a single theory (Metric Automata Theory) with η-finiteness as a general finite-precision notion. The introduction of robustness and geometrically-constrained systems (GCS) as new analytical tools goes beyond what any prior work provided, enabling results that bridge the gap between strict finite-precision expressivity and observed length-generalisation behaviour.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified framework resolves a key methodological gap in the RNN expressivity literature: prior results on SSMs and LSTMs were hard to compare because they relied on different finite-precision assumptions. The robustness notion has practical implications for architecture design and training stability, while the GCS framework explains why Mamba empirically generalises on star-free tasks despite being theoretically restricted in the η-finite setting. Open questions include how robustness interacts with optimisation dynamics, whether tokenisation granularity affects state-tracking capability, and whether GCS can characterise counter languages and other beyond-regular phenomena. The dynamics-dependent state space requirement remains a technical obstacle for deriving learning-theoretic guarantees.
