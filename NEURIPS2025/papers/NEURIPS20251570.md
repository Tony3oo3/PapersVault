## Problem

Most test-time adaptation (TTA) methods rely on entropy minimization to produce confident predictions under distribution shift, but this paper shows that entropy minimization alone neither reduces the model's energy (a proxy for marginal likelihood) nor drives predictions to the zero-entropy regime without explicit discriminative guidance. The paper formalizes the energy-entropy conjugate duality and identifies that test data are trapped in discrete 'bands' of the energy-entropy landscape, requiring both likelihood maximization and class-targeted supervision to escape.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB test images (e.g., 224×224 ImageNet-C corruptions at severity 5, ImageNet-R renders, ImageNetV2 re-samples, ImageNet-S sketches) together with a pre-trained classifier (ResNet-50 with BN/GN or ViT-Base with LN). It performs one-shot adaptation: each test sample is observed once, and only the affine parameters (scale and shift) of normalization layers are updated. The output is a class label prediction plus the adapted normalization parameters, with no access to ground-truth labels or training data at test time.

## Method

ReTTA unifies three losses: (1) standard entropy minimization (EM), (2) a Sliced Score Matching (SSM) loss that approximates the Fisher divergence between the test and model distributions via a one-step Langevin update projected onto random directions, avoiding both MCMC sampling and full Hessian computation, and (3) a Targeted Class Convergence (TCC) loss that applies cross-entropy toward the argmax class to push logits into the k=1 zero-entropy band. The SSM and EM terms are balanced by a self-adjusting coefficient λ₁ derived from multi-objective gradient optimization (Sener & Koltun), which adaptively weights energy reduction relative to entropy reduction per mini-batch without manual tuning. The theoretical foundation rests on the Fenchel conjugate relationship between energy and entropy (Lemma 1–2) and a closed-form energy-entropy band equation (Theorem 1) that characterizes how conditioned logits constrain the joint distribution of these two quantities.

## Results

On ImageNet-C (severity 5, mild scenario), ReTTA achieves 49.2% average top-1 accuracy with ResNet-50 (BN), surpassing the strongest baseline DeYO (48.6%) by 0.6%. The largest gains appear in noise corruptions (Gaussian +1.7%, Shot +1.8%, Impulse +1.8%) and digital corruptions (Contrast +1.5%). Under the more challenging online label-shift setting (imbalance ratio ∞), ReTTA widens its lead to 47.5% vs. 43.9% on ResNet-50 (GN) and 64.8% vs. 62.3% on ViT-Base (LN), demonstrating robustness to severe distribution shifts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReTTA outperforms all seven baselines on ImageNet-C (severity 5). The closest competitor is DeYO (48.6% avg), which ReTTA surpasses by 0.6% overall, with the largest margins in noise corruptions (Gaussian +1.7%, Shot +1.8%, Impulse +1.8%) and Contrast (+1.5%). EATA (47.8%) and SAR (43.9%) trail by 1.4% and 5.3% respectively, while Tent (43.1%) and MEMO (23.9%) are substantially weaker. The energy-based baselines TEA (32.6%) and AEA (39.5%) underperform all entropy-based methods, confirming that energy minimization alone is insufficient without discriminative guidance. Under online label shifts (Table 2), ReTTA's advantage grows: it beats DeYO by 3.6% on ResNet-50 (GN) and 2.5% on ViT-Base (LN), with particularly large gains on Zoom Blur (+21.2% over DeYO on ResNet) and Fog (+18.8%). Ablations (Table 4) further show that adding SSM and TCC to EATA, SAR, and DeYO each yields consistent improvements, validating the complementary roles of the energy and discriminative objectives.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of two TTA lineages: entropy minimization (Tent, EATA, SAR, DeYO) and energy-based adaptation (TEA, AEA). It departs from the dominant EM-only paradigm by proving via convex conjugacy that minimizing entropy does not provide a gradient signal to reduce the model's energy (i.e., marginal likelihood), and it departs from prior energy-based TTA by showing that energy reduction alone sacrifices the discriminability that EM provides. The method repurposes Sliced Score Matching (Song et al., UAI 2020) as a sampling-free Fisher-divergence surrogate for the energy objective, and borrows the multi-objective gradient balancing trick from Sener & Koltun (NeurIPS 2018) to derive a self-adjusting coefficient that modulates the energy loss relative to EM without manual tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The energy-entropy duality analysis and the 'band' structure (Theorem 1) offer a concrete geometric explanation for why EM plateaus at non-zero entropy under distribution shift, which could inform future TTA objective design beyond this specific method. However, the practical gains are modest (0.6–3.6% over the strongest EM baseline DeYO), the method still restricts adaptation to normalization-layer affine parameters, and the one-shot setting limits applicability to streaming scenarios where multiple passes are possible. Open questions include whether the SSM objective remains stable when more parameters (e.g., classifier weights) are adapted, whether the self-adjusting coefficient generalizes to more than two competing objectives, and whether the band structure persists under more severe or compound shifts.
