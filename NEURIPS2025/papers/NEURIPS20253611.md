## Problem

Existing Label Distribution Learning (LDL) algorithms systematically underperform on low-entropy label distributions—precisely the samples most valuable for risk-sensitive decision-making—while conventional evaluation metrics are biased by the numerical dominance of high-entropy samples. The paper identifies excessive angular cohesion among output-layer anchor vectors as the root cause of this entropy bias, both empirically and through a formal lower-bound theorem on prediction entropy.

## Input / Output

The model takes a feature vector x and produces a full conditional label distribution y ∈ R^M (a probability simplex, summing to 1) via a softmax over M anchor-vector inner products. Unlike standard classification, the supervision signal is the complete ground-truth label distribution rather than a single label or mean, and the datasets span modalities including facial expression images (Jaffe, BU-3DFE), audio/sentiment features (Music Mood, Emotion6, Art Painting), scene descriptors (Natural Scene), and rating vectors (Movie, M2B).

## Method

The core contribution is an Inter-Anchor Angular Regularization (IAR) term added to the standard KL-divergence loss: it penalizes pairwise cosine similarity between anchor vectors, thereby enforcing wider angular separation and expanding the representable entropy range. Theorem 3.1 proves that when all inter-anchor angles are bounded above by τ, the prediction entropy is lower-bounded by a function of τ that increases as τ decreases, formally establishing that cohesive anchors cannot represent low-entropy samples. A second contribution is an Entropy-Calibrated Aggregation (ECA) evaluation strategy that partitions the test set into low- and high-entropy subsets via a threshold, computes per-subset performance, and reports the expected average over a uniform threshold distribution (approximated by Monte Carlo with T=10 samples), eliminating the numerical imbalance that biases conventional arithmetic-mean evaluation.

## Results

On the four high-entropy datasets, IAR achieves the best ECA KL divergence across all four (e.g., 0.041 on Jaffe, 0.054 on BU-3DFE, 0.259 on Movie, 0.136 on Music Mood) and significantly outperforms all baselines under LEA evaluation. On the four low-entropy datasets, IAR consistently delivers the best LEA KL divergence (e.g., 0.892 on Natural Scene vs. 1.901 for LDM; 0.906 on M2B vs. 1.252 for DPA), confirming its advantage on low-entropy samples. Under HEA evaluation on low-entropy datasets, LRR occasionally matches or slightly edges IAR (e.g., 0.612 vs. 0.655 on Natural Scene), reflecting a trade-off where IAR prioritizes low-entropy accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IAR significantly outperforms LDM [30] on all eight datasets under both ECA and LEA; the gap is largest on low-entropy data (e.g., Natural Scene LEA: 0.892 vs. 1.901 KL). Against DPA [6], IAR wins on most datasets but the margin narrows on high-entropy data (BU-3DFE ECA: 0.054 vs. 0.057), while the advantage is substantial on low-entropy samples (M2B LEA: 0.906 vs. 1.252). FCC [31] is consistently outperformed by IAR across all datasets and both entropy regimes. LRR [7] is the closest competitor: on high-entropy datasets IAR wins under ECA/LEA but LRR occasionally edges IAR under HEA (Movie HEA: 0.090 vs. 0.097); on low-entropy datasets IAR dominates LEA but LRR sometimes achieves lower HEA KL (Natural Scene HEA: 0.612 vs. 0.655; Emotion6 HEA: 0.472 vs. 0.490), indicating IAR trades a small amount of high-entropy accuracy for substantially better low-entropy predictions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper operates within the well-established Label Distribution Learning (LDL) framework (Geng 2016) and its rich lineage of loss-function-engineering methods (LDM, DPA, FCC, LRR, etc.). It builds on the preliminary observation from Lu et al. (2023, [19]) that LDL algorithms exhibit a propensity for over-uniform predictions, but goes further by providing a systematic geometric diagnosis: excessive cohesion (small inter-anchor angles) among the softmax output vectors is the root cause of the entropy bias. Rather than extending a specific prior algorithm, the paper introduces a general-purpose regularization term (IAR) applicable to any anchor-based LDL model, alongside a new evaluation protocol (ECA) that addresses a methodological gap shared by all existing LDL benchmarks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The entropy bias is practically significant because low-entropy label distributions carry the most actionable information for risk-sensitive decision-making, yet current algorithms and evaluation metrics systematically underweight them. The ECA evaluation strategy is likely to influence how the LDL community reports and compares results going forward. Key limitations include the incompatibility of IAR with tree-based LDL methods and the restriction of Theorem 3.1 to non-negative-gradient output layers (e.g., softmax). Open questions include whether the angular regularization generalizes to deeper or non-linear LDL architectures, and whether task-specific entropy thresholds in ECA would yield more informative evaluations than the uniform distribution assumed here.
