## Problem

Visual Autoregressive (VAR) models generate images via next-scale prediction, but this coarse-to-fine strategy causes exponential KV cache growth across scales—consuming ~85 GB for a single 1024×1024 image at batch size 8 with Infinity-8B. Existing KV cache compression techniques (quantization, eviction, merging) are designed for single-sequence LLM/VLM inference and fail to exploit VAR's multi-scale structure or the distinct attention patterns that emerge across layers and scales.

## Input / Output

ScaleKV operates as a post-training compression on pre-trained VAR text-to-image models (Infinity-2B and Infinity-8B), taking a text prompt and producing a generated image at resolutions up to 1024×1024 (with potential for 4K). Before inference, a lightweight calibration step uses only 10 prompts to identify which transformer layers act as drafters versus refiners at each scale. The method then enforces a global KV cache budget (4%, 10%, or 20% of the original) while preserving pixel-level fidelity, measured by FID, LPIPS, and PSNR against full-cache outputs.

## Method

ScaleKV introduces the Attention Selectivity Index (ASI), which combines the proportion of attention directed to the current token map with the Top-K concentration of attention over history tokens, to classify each layer-scale pair as either a drafter (dispersed cross-scale attention, high cache demand) or a refiner (localized attention on the current map, low cache demand). ASI values are Z-score normalized within each scale, and the lowest-scoring pairs are assigned as drafters. A scale-aware budget allocation then linearly decays refiner cache budgets as scale increases (B_r(k) = B_r(0) − δ·k), reallocating saved capacity to drafters so that B_d(k) ≫ B_r(k). For token-level selection within each budget, ScaleKV partitions each token map into patches, uses centroid tokens as a compact observation window (~16 tokens), and retains the top-scoring KV states by cumulative attention from that window—adapting SnapKV's mechanism to the multi-scale VAR setting.

## Results

On MS-COCO 2017, ScaleKV consistently outperforms all compression baselines across 4%, 10%, and 20% KV cache budgets. At the most constrained 4% budget on Infinity-8B, ScaleKV achieves FID 3.37, LPIPS 0.12, and PSNR 21.41, reducing FID by 48.5% over the next-best baseline (PyramidKV, FID 6.55). On perceptual quality benchmarks, ScaleKV-compressed Infinity-8B at 10% budget retains GenEval 0.790 (vs. 0.792 full) and DPG 86.49 (vs. 86.61 full), while reducing KV cache memory from 85 GB to 8.5 GB (10× reduction).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ScaleKV dominates all four KV cache compression baselines (Sliding Window, StreamingLLM, SnapKV, PyramidKV) across every budget level and both model sizes. At 4% budget on Infinity-8B, ScaleKV achieves FID 3.37 versus PyramidKV's 6.55 and SnapKV's 6.68, a roughly 48–50% FID reduction; Sliding Window degrades catastrophically to FID 19.23. At 10% budget, the gap narrows but ScaleKV still leads by a wide margin (FID 2.12 vs. 4.68 for SnapKV). On GenEval and DPG, ScaleKV-compressed Infinity-8B (0.790 / 86.49) is nearly indistinguishable from the full model (0.792 / 86.61) and still outperforms DALL-E 3 (0.67 / 83.50) and Emu3-8.5B (0.66 / 81.60). The paper also notes that PyramidKV's fixed pyramidal allocation sometimes underperforms SnapKV (e.g., FID 5.51 vs. 5.10 at 4% on Infinity-2B), confirming that static allocation strategies fail to capture VAR's scale-dependent attention dynamics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends KV cache compression from the LLM/VLM setting (where methods like SnapKV, PyramidKV, and StreamingLLM operate on a single growing sequence) to the multi-scale structure of Visual Autoregressive models, where cache demands vary along two axes: transformer depth and generation scale. The token selection mechanism borrows directly from SnapKV's observation-window importance scoring, but the key conceptual departure is the scale-aware, layer-differentiated budget allocation driven by the Attention Selectivity Index, which identifies 'drafter' vs. 'refiner' layers per scale. Unlike prior VAR efficiency work (LiteVAR, FastVAR, CoDe, Hack) that targets speed or requires auxiliary models, ScaleKV is a training-free, plug-and-play memory reduction method.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 10× KV cache reduction (85 GB → 8.5 GB for Infinity-8B) with near-zero quality degradation is a practically significant win that unblocks batch-size-16 inference and ultra-high-resolution (4K) generation on a single GPU, areas previously infeasible. The drafter/refiner taxonomy provides a reusable conceptual lens for understanding attention in hierarchical generative models beyond VAR. Key limitations include evaluation restricted to the Infinity family (2B/8B) due to the scarcity of larger open VAR models, and the method's dependence on base-model quality as a post-training compression. Open questions include whether the ASI-based layer categorization transfers to other next-scale architectures (e.g., audio or 3D VAR) and whether the approach composes with quantization or speculative decoding for further gains.
