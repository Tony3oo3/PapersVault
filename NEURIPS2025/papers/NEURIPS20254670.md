## Problem

LLMs exhibit a puzzling duality after fine-tuning with new factual knowledge: they generalize remarkably from new facts yet simultaneously hallucinate incorrect information, and the mechanism connecting these two behaviors has remained poorly understood. This paper proposes that both phenomena stem from a single underlying mechanism—out-of-context reasoning (OCR), where the model associates concepts based on co-occurrence regardless of whether the association is causally valid—and provides the first theoretical characterization of how and why this occurs in transformers.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a token sequence of the form [subject, relation, EOS] and produces a next-token logit vector over a structured answer space partitioned into facts A₁ and implications A₂. Training data consists of fact-implication triples where a subset of subjects (m_train per pair) appear in both their fact (s, r₁, bᵢ) and implication (s, r₂, cᵢ), while test subjects (m_test per pair) appear only in facts. The critical evaluation is whether the model can predict the unseen implication cᵢ for a test subject given only its fact bᵢ during training, with performance measured by mean-rank of the ground-truth implication among all candidates.

## Method

The core contribution is demonstrating that a one-layer single-head linear attention transformer with factorized output-value matrices (W_O, W_V) can learn OCR, while a reparameterized model with combined W_OV = W_O W_Vᵀ cannot, despite having identical expressivity (Proposition 1). The theoretical analysis shows that gradient flow on the factorized model implicitly minimizes the nuclear norm of the combined output-value matrix, whereas the non-factorized model minimizes the Frobenius norm (Theorem 1). This distinction is decisive: nuclear norm minimization preserves non-zero structure in the 'test-implication' block of the weight matrix, enabling generalization to unseen subject-implication pairs, while Frobenius norm minimization zeros out these entries entirely, yielding zero margin on test implications (Theorem 2). The closed-form SVM solution reveals that the test margin depends only on the ratio m_train/m_test, explaining why even a single training example per fact-implication pair suffices for OCR. Empirically, this mechanism is verified across five LLMs (Gemma-2-9B, OLMo-7B, Qwen-2-7B, Mistral-7B, Llama-3-8B), confirming that OCR drives both generalization when fact-implication pairs are causally related and hallucination when they are not.

## Results

On the synthetic OCR task, all five LLMs (Gemma-2-9B, OLMo-7B, Qwen-2-7B, Mistral-7B-v0.3, Llama-3-8B) achieve near-perfect generalization on causally related associations (City-Language mean-rank 0.00–0.13) while also exhibiting strong hallucination on fictitious associations (mean-rank 0.15–4.55), confirming that OCR drives both behaviors. In the theoretical setting, a one-layer single-head factorized attention model (W_O, W_V) achieves zero test loss on the symbolic OCR task, while the non-factorized model (W_OV) fails to generalize, with test loss bounded below by log|A_2| > 0. The theoretical margin guarantee for the factorized model is h ≥ min{√(m_train/m_test), 1}, depending only on the ratio of training to test subjects per partition.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The central comparison is between the factorized parameterization (W_O, W_V) and the non-factorized parameterization (W_OV = W_O W_V^T) of a one-layer single-head linear attention model. Both models achieve zero training loss, but only the factorized model generalizes to unseen test implications (zero test loss), while the non-factorized model's test loss is provably bounded below by log|A_2| > 0 (Theorem 3), meaning it assigns uniform probability across all implication answers. The theoretical explanation is that gradient flow on the factorized model implicitly minimizes the nuclear norm of W_OV^F, which preserves non-zero weights in the test-implication block, whereas the non-factorized model minimizes the Frobenius norm, which zeros out all test-implication entries. In the LLM experiments, generalization performance (causally related City-Language) is consistently stronger than hallucination performance (fictitious associations), with mean-rank gaps of 0.15–4.55 across models, likely because causal knowledge aligns with pretrained representations. The factorized model's OCR capability is sample-efficient: the margin guarantee depends only on the ratio m_train/m_test, not on the absolute number of examples, explaining why as few as 4 training subjects per partition suffice.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the factual recall task formalization of Nichani et al. [2024b] and the empirical OCR observations of Feng et al. [2024], extending them with a rigorous theoretical analysis of why a one-layer linear attention transformer can or cannot perform out-of-context reasoning. The key conceptual departure is from prior work on transformer training dynamics (Tian et al. 2023a, Tarzanagh et al. 2023a, Sheen et al. 2024), which studied the (W_K, W_Q) vs W_KQ reparameterization for optimization; here the authors shift the lens to the (W_O, W_V) vs W_OV reparameterization and connect the resulting implicit bias (nuclear norm vs Frobenius norm) directly to generalization behavior. The SVM characterization of gradient flow limit points follows Lyu and Li [2019], but the closed-form solution analysis and its interpretation in terms of OCR capability are new.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a unified theoretical explanation for the 'double-edged sword' of LLM fine-tuning: the same associative mechanism that enables strong generalization from a few examples also causes hallucination when the learned association is spurious. The finding that the commonly-used reparameterization W_OV = W_O W_V^T in theoretical transformer analysis fundamentally alters generalization behavior is a caution for the field, suggesting that such simplifications may mask important dynamics. Open questions include extending the analysis to multi-layer transformers, to factorized models with trainable W_KQ, and to practical mitigation strategies for hallucination during knowledge injection.
