## Problem

Aligning and interpreting continuous wearable sensor data with natural language remains challenging because large-scale, high-quality paired sensor-text corpora are essentially absent, and standard LLMs cannot process the high-dimensional, temporally extensive nature of minute-level multimodal recordings (a single day expands to over 200,000 tokens). Existing sensor-text datasets are orders of magnitude smaller and restricted to narrow question-answering formats, insufficient for training generalizable foundation models.

## Input / Output

The model consumes one-day windows of multimodal wearable sensor data at minutely resolution, formatted as a [26 features × 1440 minutes] matrix derived from PPG, 3-axis accelerometer, altimeter, skin temperature, and EDA sensors, which is patchified into 1872 tokens by a ViT-2D encoder with patch size (2,10). Outputs include normalized sensor and text embeddings for zero-shot classification and cross-modal retrieval, as well as autoregressively generated natural language captions from the multimodal decoder. The text side accepts diverse prompt templates for classification or free-form captions for retrieval and generation tasks.

## Method

SensorLM introduces a hierarchical caption generation pipeline that produces three levels of paired text—statistical (quantitative summaries), structural (temporal trends and spikes), and semantic (activity labels, sleep periods, mood logs)—enabling curation of the largest sensor-language dataset to date (59.7M hours from 103,000 people). The architecture comprises a ViT-2D sensor encoder, a text encoder, and a causally masked multimodal text decoder that attends to sensor features via cross-attention. Pretraining uses a composite loss combining a symmetric contrastive objective (CLIP-style) and an autoregressive captioning objective, with the combined formulation recovering CLIP, Cap, and CoCa as specific weight configurations (λ_con, λ_cap). This unified framework allows the model to simultaneously learn aligned cross-modal representations for retrieval/classification and generative captioning, with the CoCa variant (both objectives active) consistently outperforming single-objective alternatives.

## Results

On zero-shot 20-class activity recognition, SensorLM achieves an AUROC of 0.84, F1 of 0.29, and BAcc of 0.31, substantially outperforming Gemma-3-27B (AUROC 0.50) and Gemini 2.0 (AUROC 0.51), which perform near random. In zero-shot cross-modal retrieval, SensorLM achieves 100% Recall@1 on a 100-sample benchmark and 98.2% Recall@1 at 5,000 samples (Sensor→Text), while LLM baselines are largely infeasible due to context limits. For few-shot learning, SensorLM reaches AUROC 0.88 with only 50 labels per class on 20-class AR, and in linear probing it attains AUROC 0.94 on Activity (vs. 0.72 for MSN and 0.68 for SimCLR).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SensorLM decisively outperforms both LLM baselines (Gemma-3-27B and Gemini 2.0) across all zero-shot classification tasks, achieving AUROC 0.84 vs. ~0.50 (near random) on 20-class activity recognition, with gains of +33% AUROC and +23% F1. Even Gemini 2.0 with supervised fine-tuning (SFT) remains far below SensorLM (F1 0.06 vs. 0.29), indicating that tabular sensor input to LLMs is fundamentally insufficient. In cross-modal retrieval, SensorLM achieves 100% R@1 on 100 samples and 98.2% at 5k scale, while both LLMs are infeasible at 5k+ due to context length limits and score near-chance (R@1 = 1.0–5.0) at 100 samples. Against SSL baselines (SimCLR, DINO, MSN) in linear probing, SensorLM achieves AUROC 0.94 on Activity (vs. 0.72 MSN, 0.68 SimCLR, 0.66 DINO) and 0.60 on Hypertension (vs. 0.58 MSN), though MSN slightly edges SensorLM on Anxiety (0.66 vs. 0.65). In few-shot settings, SensorLM consistently surpasses all SSL baselines, reaching AUROC 0.88 at 50 labels/class on Activity where the best SSL method (MSN) remains substantially lower.

## Relation to Prior Work

SensorLM directly extends the vision-language pretraining lineage (CLIP, Cap, CoCa) to the wearable sensor domain, recovering each as a specific configuration within a unified contrastive-plus-generative framework. The key departure from prior sensor-text work (Xing et al., Moon et al., Yu et al., Li et al.) is the shift from small-scale, QA-format, or manually-annotated pairs to an automated hierarchical captioning pipeline (statistical → structural → semantic) that generates paired text at scale from uncurated wearable data. The sensor encoder adapts a ViT-2D backbone to 26-feature × 1440-minute input matrices, while the text side follows standard transformer encoders/decoders, making the architecture a straightforward but effective transplant of VLP components into the time-series sensor setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 59.7M-hour, 103K-person sensor-language dataset and the hierarchical captioning pipeline address the fundamental data bottleneck that has limited sensor-language research to toy-scale experiments, making this a potential reference resource for the wearable-AI community. The demonstrated zero-shot generalization to unseen activities and the strong cross-modal retrieval (96%+ R@1 at 40k scale) suggest a path toward natural-language interfaces for health wearables. Key open questions include generalizability beyond Fitbit/Pixel Watch devices, clinical validation for diagnostic use, and whether the scaling trends observed (saturation beyond ~12M hours) will hold as data and model sizes grow further.
