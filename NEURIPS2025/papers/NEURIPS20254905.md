## Problem

Existing spatio-temporal forecasting models overlook dynamic deviations between current observations and their historical patterns, which carry critical signals from events like policy changes or incidents. Prior approaches either ignore these deviations entirely or treat them as binary threshold events, failing to capture their continuous, context-dependent nature. The core challenge is how to quantify such deviations in a high-dimensional latent space and leverage them to improve forecasting without external labels.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254905/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a current spatio-temporal sequence X^c ∈ R^{T×N×C} (T=12 steps at 5-minute resolution) and its timestamp-aligned historical anchor X^a, computed as the pointwise average of the same timesteps across all weeks in the training set. Both sequences are enriched with node and time-of-day embeddings before encoding. The output is a forecasted sequence X̂ ∈ R^{T'×N×C} (T'=12 steps) for all N sensors, with C=1 on all benchmarks. The sensor graph topology is used for Chebyshev graph convolutions within the GCRU backbone.

## Method

ST-SSDL builds on a GCRU encoder-decoder backbone and introduces Self-Supervised Deviation Learning (SSDL) as a core component. Historical weekly averages serve as self-supervised anchors; both current and anchor sequences are encoded in parallel through the shared GCRU encoder. A set of M=20 learnable prototypes (dimension d=64) discretizes the latent space, and query-prototype cross-attention maps each encoded representation to its nearest prototype as a proxy. Two auxiliary losses guide training: a triplet-style contrastive loss that enforces inter-prototype separability, and a deviation loss that minimizes the gap between the physical-space distance D (between current and anchor queries, stop-gradiented) and the latent-space distance D̃ (between their assigned prototypes), thereby enforcing relative distance consistency. The stop-gradient operator prevents lazy-mode collapse where all queries map to a single prototype. The decoder receives an adaptive adjacency matrix computed from the concatenated [H^c | V^c | H^a | V^a] representation, enabling context-conditioned spatial interaction modeling. The overall objective jointly optimizes MAE forecasting loss with weighted contrastive and deviation terms.

## Results

ST-SSDL achieves state-of-the-art performance across all six spatio-temporal forecasting benchmarks (METRLA, PEMSBAY, PEMSD7(M), PEMS04, PEMS07, PEMS08) on all three metrics (MAE, RMSE, MAPE) at every prediction horizon. On METRLA (15-min horizon), it reaches MAE 2.60, RMSE 5.02, and MAPE 6.62%, outperforming the next-best STDN (2.62/5.04/6.68%). On the larger PEMS07 dataset (883 sensors), ST-SSDL achieves MAE 19.19, RMSE 32.51, and MAPE 8.11%, surpassing the second-best STID (19.61/32.79/8.30%). Notably, these gains are achieved with a simple 1-layer GCRU encoder-decoder backbone (100K–498K parameters), demonstrating that the self-supervised deviation learning component, not architectural complexity, drives the improvement.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ST-SSDL outperforms all 13 baselines on every metric across all six datasets, with no losses or ties. The closest competitors are STDN (trend-seasonality decomposition) and STID (spatial-temporal identity), which are the strongest baselines on most settings; for example, on METRLA 15-min MAE, ST-SSDL (2.60) edges out STDN (2.62) by 0.02, while on PEMS07 average MAE the gap widens to 0.42 (19.19 vs. 19.61 for STID). Against the classic GNN-based methods (STGCN, DCRNN, GWNet, MTGNN), ST-SSDL achieves substantially larger margins—e.g., on PEMSBAY 60-min RMSE, ST-SSDL (4.34) improves over GWNet (4.60) by 5.7%. Notably, ST-SSDL uses a simple 1-layer GCRU backbone with only 100K–498K parameters, yet it surpasses heavier Transformer-based (GTS, ST-WA) and meta-learning (MegaCRN) approaches, indicating that the self-supervised deviation learning mechanism, rather than architectural scale, is the primary source of the gains. The statistical baseline HI and univariate GRU are far behind, confirming that spatio-temporal modeling plus deviation awareness is essential for competitive performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

ST-SSDL builds on the GCRU-based spatio-temporal forecasting lineage (DCRNN, Graph WaveNet, AGCRN) as its backbone, while introducing a self-supervised deviation learning module that departs from prior work in two key ways. First, it repurposes learnable prototypes—previously used to capture typical channel behaviors (Chen et al., NeurIPS 2024; Li et al., ICML 2023)—as a mechanism to discretize the continuous latent space, enabling structured comparison between current and historical representations. Second, it moves beyond fixed temporal offsets (same time previous day/week) used by methods like STID and STNorm toward modeling dynamic, continuous deviations via a 'relative distance consistency' principle enforced through a novel deviation loss, filling a gap the authors identify in both supervised and self-supervised spatio-temporal learning literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important but underexplored aspect of spatio-temporal forecasting: how to quantify and leverage the degree to which current observations deviate from historical norms, without requiring explicit event labels or threshold heuristics. The self-supervised formulation (historical averages as anchors + prototype discretization) is clean and potentially transferable to other periodic time-series domains beyond traffic. However, the GCRU backbone introduces a significant inference-time cost (e.g., 217.8s on PEMS07 vs. 5.1s for MTGNN), and all experiments are confined to California traffic data, leaving open questions about scalability to larger sensor networks, non-traffic domains, and whether the deviation-learning principle generalizes to non-periodic or irregularly sampled series. The authors note hierarchical prototype structures as a future direction.
