## Problem

Mars science lacks a standardized benchmark for systematically evaluating foundation models, unlike Earth Observation, medical imaging, or NLP where benchmarks like Geo-Bench and MIMIC-IV have accelerated progress. Existing Mars ML datasets are fragmented, inconsistently formatted, and not interoperable, preventing fair comparison of models across diverse Martian tasks and limiting the ability to assess generalization or robustness.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254098/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Inputs are Mars orbital grayscale patches (100×100 to 299×299 px from HiRISE, CTX, THEMIS) and surface RGB images (256×256 to 1024×1024 px from Mastcam, Pancam, MAHLI), with a special case of temporal before/after pairs encoded as composite RGB (blue=before, green=after, red=0). Outputs are class labels (binary, 8–36 class multi-class, or 25-class multi-label), single-channel pixel-level segmentation masks (class 0 = background), or bounding boxes provided in COCO, Pascal VOC, and YOLO formats. Several datasets are extremely small (tens of samples), making them stress tests for low-data regimes.

## Method

Mars-Bench curates, corrects, and standardizes 20 existing Mars science datasets into a unified ML-ready format with consistent train/val/test splits, multiple annotation formats, and few-shot (1–20 shot) and partitioned (1%–50%) training subsets. Expert planetary scientists validated segmentation masks and revised classification labels, and geographically disjoint splits were created for crater datasets to prevent spatial leakage. For baseline evaluation, the authors benchmark five classification architectures (ResNet101, SqueezeNet1.1, InceptionV3, SwinV2-B, ViT-L/16), four segmentation architectures (U-Net, DeepLabV3+, SegFormer, DPT), and four detection architectures (YOLO11, SSD, RetinaNet, Faster R-CNN) under three training strategies—scratch, frozen feature extraction, and full fine-tuning—using ImageNet or COCO pre-training. They further evaluate EO foundation models (SatMAE, CROMA, Prithvi) and proprietary VLMs (Gemini 2.0 Flash, GPT-4o Mini) to assess cross-domain transfer, finding that Mars-specific pre-training is likely necessary for robust performance.

## Results

Across 20 Mars science datasets spanning classification, segmentation, and object detection, the paper establishes baselines using ImageNet/COCO-pretrained models under feature extraction, fine-tuning, and from-scratch training. For classification, ViT-L/16 and SwinV2-B achieve the highest normalized F1-scores, while SqueezeNet1.1 consistently underperforms due to its small parameter count. For segmentation, U-Net achieves the highest overall normalized IoU despite wide confidence intervals on some datasets, outperforming transformer-based models (SegFormer, DPT). For object detection, YOLO11 achieves the best normalized mAP across all three datasets (boulder, cone, dust devil). Proprietary VLMs (Gemini 2.0 Flash, GPT-4o Mini) show inconsistent performance, with F1-scores ranging from 0.41 (mb-crater_multi_seg, Gemini) to 0.84 (mb-mars_seg_msl, Gemini), and EO-pretrained models (SatMAE, CROMA, Prithvi) underperform an ImageNet-pretrained ViT-L/16.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In classification, ViT-L/16 and SwinV2-B (transformer architectures) consistently outperform ResNet101, InceptionV3, and SqueezeNet1.1 across six diverse Mars datasets under feature extraction, with SqueezeNet1.1 showing the weakest performance due to its limited capacity. For segmentation, U-Net dominates over DeepLabV3+, SegFormer, and DPT on nearly all six datasets; DPT in particular exhibits highly unstable results with large confidence intervals, making it the least reliable baseline. For object detection, YOLO11 achieves the best normalized mAP on all three datasets (mb-boulder_det, mb-conequest_det, mb-dust_devil_det), outperforming SSD, RetinaNet, and Faster R-CNN. When comparing EO-pretrained foundation models (SatMAE, CROMA, Prithvi) against an ImageNet-pretrained ViT-L/16, the ImageNet model wins across all classification datasets, likely due to its larger pre-training corpus (14M vs. ≤1M images) and greater data diversity. Among VLMs, Gemini 2.0 Flash and GPT-4o Mini perform well on terrain segmentation (mb-mars_seg_msl: F1 0.84 and 0.70 respectively) but drop sharply on fine-grained geologic tasks like mb-crater_multi_seg (F1 0.41, 0.51) and mb-domars16k (F1 0.32, 0.30), indicating insufficient domain-specific knowledge for Mars science.

## Relation to Prior Work

Mars-Bench follows the established paradigm of domain-specific evaluation benchmarks (Geo-Bench for Earth Observation, BigBio for biomedical NLP, MIMIC-IV for clinical data) and applies it to planetary science, a domain that previously lacked any standardized evaluation framework. Rather than introducing new model architectures, the paper curates, corrects, and standardizes 20 existing Mars science datasets into a unified ML-ready format, then benchmarks a broad suite of well-established architectures (CNNs, ViTs, U-Net, YOLO) under multiple training regimes. The conceptual departure from prior Mars ML work is the shift from isolated, task-specific models with non-interoperable datasets toward a common evaluation protocol that enables systematic comparison of foundation models across diverse Martian tasks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Mars-Bench fills a critical infrastructure gap: without it, Mars ML research has been limited to evaluating models on 1–2 ad-hoc tasks, making it impossible to assess generalization or compare approaches fairly. The finding that Earth-observation-pretrained foundation models (SatMAE, CROMA, Prithvi) underperform a simple ImageNet-pretrained ViT on Mars data is a useful negative result that motivates Mars-specific pretraining rather than cross-planet transfer. Key limitations include the absence of georeferencing for most datasets (preventing spatial analysis), the small size of several datasets (reflecting the cost of expert annotation), and the reliance on grayscale orbital imagery that limits visual cues. Open questions include whether a Mars-specific foundation model pre-trained on the full MRO/rover archive would close the gap, and how models generalize across sensors, missions, and Martian regions.
