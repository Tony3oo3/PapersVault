## Problem

CLIP's contrastive learning objective optimizes only global image-text similarity, causing the model to underutilize fine-grained semantic signals encoded in individual words (object status, spatial relations, actions). This limitation persists even when captions are enriched via re-captioning with MLLMs, where CLIP's performance actually degrades. The paper addresses how to recover this overlooked token-level textual supervision without additional annotated data or substantial computational overhead.

## Input / Output

The method takes 224×224 RGB images and text captions tokenized with CLIP's subword tokenizer (vocabulary size V) as input. Beyond the standard image and text embedding vectors used for contrastive alignment, the model additionally produces V-dimensional classification logits from a linear head applied to the vision encoder's averaged image features. The text tokens are converted into IDF-weighted K-hot vectors that serve as soft classification targets, with frequent stopwords downweighted to emphasize discriminative terms.

## Method

SuperCLIP augments the standard CLIP contrastive loss with a lightweight classification loss that treats raw text tokens (prior to the text encoder) as direct classification labels for the vision encoder. Each caption is tokenized into a K-hot vector over the full vocabulary, reweighted by inverse document frequency to suppress generic terms, and normalized into a soft target distribution. A single linear layer maps the vision encoder's pooled features to V-dimensional logits, and the classification loss is the cross-entropy between these logits and the weighted token distribution. The total training objective is simply L_CLIP + L_Class, requiring only 0.077% additional FLOPs and no extra annotated data. Because the classification loss is computed per-sample rather than across the batch, it is inherently insensitive to batch size, which mitigates CLIP's well-known performance degradation under small-batch training. The approach generalizes directly to other CLIP-style frameworks such as SigLIP and FLIP with consistent gains.

## Results

SuperCLIP consistently improves over CLIP across all model scales. At the L-512M setting (ViT-L/14 pretrained on 512M DataComp-1B samples), SuperCLIP achieves 70.1% zero-shot ImageNet-1K val accuracy (+4.0% over CLIP's 66.1%), 62.5% on ImageNet-v2 (+5.1%), and 62.4% Recall@1 on Flickr30K image retrieval (+5.4%). At the largest scale (L-12.8B), gains narrow but remain positive: 80.0% ImageNet val (+1.0%) and 45.5% COCO image retrieval (+1.6%). The additional FLOPs from the linear classification head are only 0.077% of total computation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CLIP (Radford et al., ICML 2021), SuperCLIP wins on every benchmark and model size tested, with the largest absolute gains at L-512M: +4.0% ImageNet val, +5.1% ImageNet-v2, and +5.4% Flickr30K image retrieval. At L-12.8B the margins shrink to +0.6–1.6%, suggesting diminishing returns at very large scale but no degradation. Against SigLIP (Zhai et al., ICCV 2023), SuperSigLIP improves ImageNet val by +3.7% (64.1 vs. 60.4) and image/text retrieval by +2.7–2.9% on both COCO and Flickr30K. Against FLIP (Li et al., CVPR 2023), SuperFLIP gains +3.2–3.4% on classification and up to +5.3% on Flickr30K text retrieval (72.0 vs. 66.7), indicating the classification supervision is particularly effective for text-side retrieval. In all three comparisons, SuperCLIP variants strictly dominate their respective baselines with no task where performance drops.

## Relation to Prior Work

SuperCLIP extends CLIP's contrastive learning paradigm by incorporating a classification-based supervision signal derived from raw text tokens, building directly on the token-as-label strategy from Huang et al. (2024). It departs from prior fine-grained alignment efforts (RegionCLIP, Long-CLIP, UniCL, SigLIP 2) that either require additional annotated data, introduce substantial computational overhead, or compromise other capabilities. The key conceptual positioning is that the contrastive objective alone cannot exploit fine-grained textual semantics—even when explicitly available in rich re-captioned data—whereas a lightweight classification head on the vision encoder can recover this signal with negligible cost (0.077% FLOPs).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that enriching captions via LLM re-captioning actually degrades standard CLIP performance, yet SuperCLIP recovers and surpasses the original, reveals a fundamental mismatch between contrastive objectives and dense textual supervision that the field has not fully addressed. The method's simplicity (one linear layer, no extra data) and batch-size independence make it immediately adoptable in existing CLIP/SigLIP/FLIP training pipelines, lowering the barrier to fine-grained alignment. An open question the authors raise is whether a symmetric approach—using image features to supervise the text encoder—could yield complementary gains, suggesting the current solution only addresses one direction of the alignment problem.
