## Problem

Vision-language models (VLMs) frequently exhibit degraded factual recall compared to their text-only LLM backbones, yet the mechanistic reason for this gap remains unexplained. The paper identifies the root cause as a two-hop problem: VLMs must first construct entity representations from visual inputs before accessing the LLM's preexisting factual knowledge circuit, but in LLaVA-style models this entity resolution occurs too late in the forward pass to engage the early-layer MLPs that encode factual associations.

## Input / Output

The benchmark feeds each model an image of a Wikipedia entity (sampled from the WIT dataset) paired with a GPT-4.1-generated factual question about that entity (e.g., 'In which country is the entity located?'). Evaluation is two-stage: the VLM must first correctly identify the entity (misidentified samples are discarded), then answer the factual question, with 1000 valid samples collected per VLM/LLM pair from a 15,000-question pool. The mechanistic analyses produce per-sublayer attribution scores (Grad×Delta across MLP and Attention branches), layer-wise linear probe accuracies on ImageNet-100 entity prediction, and patching recovery rates measured on originally-incorrect examples.

## Method

The paper combines three mechanistic interpretability techniques to diagnose and causally verify the two-hop failure. Attribution patching (Grad×Delta with targeted Gaussian corruption of entity/image embeddings) reveals that LLaVA-style VLMs activate only mid-to-late layer sublayers for factual recall, whereas their LLM backbones and well-performing VLMs (Gemma-3, Qwen2.5-VL) also engage early-layer MLPs at entity token positions. Heuristic activation patching then provides causal evidence: by backpropagating a logit-difference objective to select optimal token positions and patching early-layer MLP outputs from the LLM backbone into the VLM, the authors recover 35% of the factual recall gap (vs. 13% for back-patching and 16% for random patching). Layer-wise linear probes on residual-stream representations confirm that LLaVA models do not encode reliable entity information until middle-to-late layers, while Gemma-3 and Qwen2.5-VL maintain high probe accuracy from early layers onward. Finally, chain-of-thought prompting is tested as an inference-time mitigation, closing the full performance gap for several models by forcing the VLM to verbalize the entity before answering.

## Results

Across 14 VLMs (7B–124B parameters, Adapter/Native/Cross-Attention architectures), 11 of 14 exhibit factual-recall degradation relative to their LLM backbones, with Adapter-based models suffering the steepest drops (LLaVA-MORE-8B: 41%→23%, ↓43.9%; LLaVA-1.5-7B: 30%→19%, ↓36.7%). Heuristic activation patching of early-layer MLP outputs from the LLM backbone into degraded VLMs recovers 35% of the factual-recall accuracy gap, substantially outperforming back-patching (13%) and random patching (16%). Chain-of-thought prompting closes the full performance gap for Pixtral-12B and Pixtral-Large-124B, and recovers over half the gap for LLaVA-1.5-13B (28%→37%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary methodological comparison is between its proposed heuristic patching and two existing back-patching baselines. Heuristic patching (which uses a Grad×Delta attribution objective to select the optimal token position for injecting LLM backbone MLP outputs) recovers 35% of the VLM–LLM factual-recall gap on average across three LLaVA models, compared to only 13% for the back-patching procedure from Biran et al. (2024) and Nikankin et al. (2025), which copies entire layer outputs from later to earlier layers across image tokens. Random patching (same procedure but with uniformly random start positions) recovers 16%, confirming that the position selection in heuristic patching is meaningful. The large gap between heuristic (35%) and back-patching (13%) demonstrates that the specific early-layer MLP outputs at entity-relevant positions are the critical missing component, not merely a general depth-shift of representations. Additionally, the paper compares VLM performance against their own LLM backbones as the natural baseline, showing that 11/14 VLMs underperform, with the degradation concentrated in Adapter-based architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper synthesizes two previously separate threads of mechanistic interpretability research: (1) the finding that factual recall in LLMs is implemented in early-layer MLPs (Meng et al. 2022, Chughtai et al. 2024), and (2) the observation that visual projections in LLaVA-style VLMs are misaligned with the LLM's token space in early layers (Masry et al. 2025, Venhoff et al. 2025). By connecting these, the paper provides a mechanistic explanation for the factual recall degradation previously reported by Cohen et al. 2024, framing it as a 'two-hop problem' analogous to the multi-hop reasoning limitations identified by Biran et al. 2024 in text-only LLMs. The attribution patching methodology follows Nanda's framework, and the back-patching baseline is drawn from both Biran et al. 2024 and Nikankin et al. 2025.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first mechanistic, causally-grounded explanation for why LLaVA-style VLMs lose factual knowledge relative to their LLM backbones, shifting the design question from 'can the model see the entity?' to 'does the model resolve the entity early enough to engage the recall circuit?' This has direct implications for adapter architecture: the finding that massive multimodal fine-tuning (Qwen2.5-VL) or native pretraining (Gemma-3) resolves the issue suggests that alignment depth, not just alignment existence, is the critical factor. Open questions include whether the two-hop bottleneck affects other knowledge-intensive tasks (multi-hop QA, temporal reasoning), whether lighter-weight adapter redesigns can force earlier entity resolution without requiring trillions of tokens, and whether the CoT mitigation generalizes to models without strong reasoning capabilities. The lack of statistical significance testing and the focus on a single task (factual recall) limit the generality of the conclusions.
