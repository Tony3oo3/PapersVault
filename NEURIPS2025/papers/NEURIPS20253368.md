## Problem

Existing distributed MoE implementations suffer from low GPU utilization (as low as 9–14%), high latency, and poor overlap efficiency because they rely on CPU-managed scheduling, synchronous AlltoAll collectives with straggler-induced barriers, and hundreds of short-lived kernel launches per layer. No prior system fuses the entire distributed MoE operator—gate, dispatch, expert FFN, and combine—into a single GPU kernel with device-initiated communication, leaving a fundamental gap between hardware capability and software orchestration.

## Input / Output

FlashMoE consumes a token embedding matrix A ∈ R^{S×H} (S up to 16K, H=2048), a 3-D expert weight tensor X ∈ R^{E×H×D} (E up to 128 local experts, D=2048), and internally computes a routing table T_φ via a fused gate. The output is the combined expert activation O ∈ R^{S×H}, where each token's output is a weighted sum of its top-k expert results. Internally, tokens are partitioned into (128,64) tiles, and inter-GPU staging uses a symmetric tensor layout L ∈ R^{P×R×B×E×C×H} with 4× temporal overprovisioning to guarantee write-write conflict-free one-sided accesses without synchronization.

## Method

FlashMoE implements the entire distributed MoE forward pass as a single persistent GPU kernel using an actor-based concurrency model with three specialized roles: N−1 thread blocks act as Processors executing CUTLASS-based GEMM and element-wise operations on tiles, while a dedicated OS block contains a Scheduler warp (work-conserving task dispatch) and three Subscriber warps (decoding remote tile packets into task descriptors). Communication is fully device-initiated via NVSHMEM one-sided (R)DMA transfers, replacing synchronous AlltoAll collectives with asynchronous, payload-efficient tile-level sends that skip null-padded tokens. A provably write-write conflict-free symmetric tensor layout with temporal buffering (2 rounds × 2 staging slots) eliminates the need for inter-GPU synchronization during data movement. The unified task abstraction F_t(A,B,C,D) := C ← φ(A ⋆_t B + D) expresses both FFN GEMMs and the expert-combine Hadamard product under a single interface, enabling the scheduler to treat all compute uniformly and pipeline dispatch, computation, and combine at tile granularity within the kernel's lifetime.

## Results

On an 8-H100 GPU node with MoE models up to 128 experts and 16K token sequences, FlashMoE achieves 93.17% average SM utilization (vs. 9.67% for FasterMoE, 13.55% for DeepEP+Megatron-LM, 42.31% for COMET, and 59.11% for Megatron-TE), 17.7 MTokens/s throughput, and up to 6.4× lower forward latency compared to baselines. Notably, FlashMoE operates entirely in FP32 while all baselines use FP16, meaning its communication volume and computational workload are doubled relative to baselines. The single-kernel design reduces GPU operations per MoE layer from 33–550 (baselines) to exactly 1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FlashMoE outperforms all five baselines across every evaluated metric. Against FasterMoE [14], FlashMoE achieves 9.6× higher SM utilization (93.17% vs. 9.67%), 5.7× higher throughput, and 2.6× lower latency at 16K tokens on 4 GPUs. Against DeepEP+Megatron-LM [1], FlashMoE delivers 6.8× higher SM utilization (93.17% vs. 13.55%) and up to 6.4× lower latency at 8 GPUs, where DeepEP degrades steeply due to increasing communication costs. Against COMET [12], FlashMoE achieves 2.2× higher SM utilization (93.17% vs. 42.31%) and up to 6.6× lower latency at 128 experts on 8 H100s. Against Megatron-TE [16], FlashMoE provides 4× higher SM utilization (93.17% vs. 59.11%), 4.9× higher throughput, and 4.6× lower latency at 16K tokens on 4 GPUs. Against DeepSpeed-MoE [11], FlashMoE eliminates 549 of 550 per-layer GPU operations, removing the kernel launch overhead that causes non-deterministic start times and synchronization stalls. All comparisons favor FlashMoE, and the gap widens with increasing GPU count, sequence length, and number of experts.

## Relation to Prior Work

FlashMoE departs fundamentally from the dominant paradigm in distributed MoE systems (DeepSpeed-MoE, Megatron-LM, FasterMoE, COMET) that rely on CPU-orchestrated, multi-kernel execution with bulk-synchronous AlltoAll collectives. While prior work like COMET and Lancet introduced computation-communication overlap, they still launch 33–550 kernels per layer and remain subject to straggler-induced idle time from synchronous barriers. FlashMoE instead applies the actor model of concurrent computation to GPU thread blocks, fusing gate, dispatch, expert GEMM, and combine into a single persistent kernel that uses one-sided, device-initiated (R)DMA via NVSHMEM and a provably conflict-free symmetric tensor layout to eliminate all host-driven synchronization. It builds on CUTLASS for in-kernel GEMM and on PGAS programming concepts from HPC, but is the first to unify the entire distributed MoE operator under a single kernel launch.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result demonstrates that CPU orchestration is not a necessary bottleneck for distributed MoE inference: a fully GPU-resident, actor-based megakernel can achieve 93% SM utilization versus 10–14% for state-of-the-art baselines, with 6× lower latency and 5.7× higher throughput even at FP32. This opens a path toward GPU-native autonomous pipelines that eliminate straggler effects inherent in synchronous collectives, with projected further gains in multi-node RDMA/InfiniBand settings. Key open questions include extending the fusion to training (backward pass + gradient communication), resolving the suboptimal FP16 path, and reducing the engineering barrier (6,820 lines of hand-tuned CUDA) via compiler or DSL abstractions. The single-node, single-layer, inference-only scope limits immediate applicability but establishes a strong architectural template.
