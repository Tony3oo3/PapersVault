## Problem

Existing autoregressive world models suffer from three interrelated limitations: raster-scan flattening disrupts spatial locality and causes structural degradation (object disappearances, blurred manipulators), sequential next-token prediction is slow and accumulates errors over long rollouts, and the lack of explicit motion modeling produces physically implausible dynamics in interactive scenes. These issues collectively prevent autoregressive architectures from matching the fidelity and efficiency needed for real-time action-conditioned video prediction and model-based control.

## Input / Output

SAMPO takes a short sequence of observation frames (1–2 context frames at 64×64 or 256×256 resolution), a corresponding action sequence, and trajectory-aware motion prompts (overlaid point-tracking trajectories from CoTracker3 on a G×G grid, filtered by confidence and displacement thresholds) as input. It produces future frames as hierarchical multi-scale discrete token maps (coarse-to-fine, e.g., 6 scales at 64×64 or 8 scales at 256×256), which are decoded back to pixels via the VQ tokenizer. A key asymmetry: observed frames are tokenized densely across all spatial scales, while future frames use only a sparse subset of coarser scales with cross-attention to observed frames, reducing token count and focusing capacity on dynamic regions. An optional linear head on the last token's hidden state yields a scalar reward prediction for RL applications.

## Method

SAMPO introduces a hybrid autoregressive formulation that is causal across frames (temporal) but generates each frame's tokens in a coarse-to-fine, parallel manner within each spatial scale (spatial), replacing raster-scan order with bidirectional spatial attention. This is paired with an asymmetric multi-scale VQ tokenizer (built on VQGAN, codebook size 8192) where observed frames receive full-resolution tokenization and future frames receive sparser, coarser-scale tokenization augmented with cross-attention to observed frames, disentangling static background from dynamic foreground. A trajectory-aware motion prompt module extracts point trajectories via CoTracker3, filters out static and low-confidence tracks, and overlays the remaining dynamic trajectories onto observations as a dual-branch input (concatenated with a special separator token), providing explicit spatiotemporal priors that focus attention on robot arms and manipulated objects. Training uses a multi-scale cross-entropy loss computed only on future frames with scale-specific weights proportional to token count, and inference leverages KV-caching with block-wise causal masks to achieve 4.4× faster decoding than standard autoregressive baselines while improving FVD and SSIM.

## Results

On action-conditioned video prediction, SAMPO achieves FVD 55.5 (BAIR 64×64), 57.1 (RoboNet 64×64), 175.3 (RoboNet 256×256, SAMPO-L), and 227.1 (1X 256×256), consistently outperforming iVideoGPT by 5–25 FVD points across all settings. On the VP² visual planning benchmark, SAMPO reaches 72.2% average success rate, surpassing iVideoGPT (70.1%) and matching SVG (72.5%). In model-based RL on Meta-World, SAMPO accelerates policy convergence and improves the success-rate upper bound over iVideoGPT while outperforming DreamerV3 in both sample efficiency and final performance. Inference is 4.4× faster than the raster-scan AR baseline (2.06 s/vid vs. 9.05 s/vid on BAIR).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAMPO outperforms all nine baselines on the primary video prediction metrics. Against the closest competitor iVideoGPT [60], SAMPO reduces FVD by 5.3 (BAIR 64×64), 6.1 (RoboNet 64×64), 22.6 (RoboNet 256×256), and 24.7 (1X 256×256), while improving PSNR by 1.5–2.2 dB and SSIM by 3.5–4.5 points. On VP² visual planning, SAMPO (72.2% avg.) edges out iVideoGPT (70.1%) by 2.1% and is within 0.3% of SVG (72.5%), while substantially surpassing MaskViT (52.1%), FitVid (65.6%), MCVD (64.3%), and Struct-VRNN (44.2%). In model-based RL on Meta-World, SAMPO significantly outperforms DreamerV3 [22] in both sample efficiency and final success rate, and accelerates policy convergence relative to iVideoGPT. The speed advantage is notable: SAMPO-S with 6 spatial scales runs in 2.06 s/vid versus 9.05 s/vid for the 138M-parameter AR baseline, a 4.4× speedup, while simultaneously improving FVD from 60.8 to 55.5 on BAIR.

## Relation to Prior Work

SAMPO sits at the intersection of two recent lines of work: VAR's next-scale spatial prediction (Tian et al., NeurIPS 2024), which replaces raster-scan token ordering with coarse-to-fine multi-scale generation, and iVideoGPT's (Wu et al., NeurIPS 2024) causal autoregressive formulation of interactive world models over VQ-tokenized video. The key architectural departure is the hybrid decoding scheme that applies VAR's bidirectional spatial attention *within* each frame while maintaining causal autoregression *across* frames—a combination neither prior work explores. The trajectory-aware motion prompt module draws on TraceVLA's (Zheng et al., ICLR 2025) visual trace prompting idea but adapts it to an online, interactive world-model setting using CoTracker3 point tracking, and the asymmetric multi-scale tokenizer (dense for observed frames, sparse for future frames) is a novel design motivated by the static-background/sparse-motion structure of robot-centric video.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SAMPO addresses a practical bottleneck in autoregressive world models: raster-scan flattening destroys spatial locality and forces sequential decoding, making rollouts slow and structurally incoherent. The 4.4× inference speedup with simultaneous quality gains (FVD, SSIM, LPIPS) makes interactive rollouts more feasible for real-time planning and MBRL, and the zero-shot transfer from Open X-Embodiment pretraining to the 1X World Model dataset suggests the architecture scales to diverse embodiments. However, the paper acknowledges that long-horizon error accumulation remains unresolved, and the motion prompt module cannot operate on single-frame inputs—both are open problems for deploying such models in closed-loop control. The asymmetric tokenizer's assumption of a stable camera viewpoint also limits applicability to egomotion-heavy settings.
