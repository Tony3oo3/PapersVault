## Problem

Collaborative perception research has been dominated by vehicle-to-vehicle (V2V) and vehicle-to-infrastructure (V2I) frameworks, with aerial UAV perspectives largely unexplored due to the absence of high-quality real-world datasets. Existing UAV-related datasets are either simulated, focus on UAV-to-UAV cooperation, or (in the case of CoPeD) provide only 2D annotations outside driving scenarios. No prior dataset combines real-world driving conditions, LiDAR-equipped UAVs, and full 3D bounding box annotations for aerial-ground cooperative perception.

## Input / Output

Each agent provides synchronized LiDAR point clouds at 10 Hz (128-beam RoboSense Ruby Plus on vehicles, 32-beam RoboSense Helios on the UAV) and multi-view camera images (5 cameras per vehicle with dual focal lengths for front views, 1 forward-facing camera on the UAV). The perception range is set to ±140.8 m along both X and Y axes in a unified BEV representation. Outputs are 9-DoF 3D bounding boxes (center, dimensions, roll/pitch/yaw) for 13 object categories, each annotated with one of three occlusion levels (visible, partially occluded, heavily occluded), plus projected 2D polygons and minimum enclosing rectangles on camera images.

## Method

AGC-Drive is a dataset and benchmark contribution rather than a new detection architecture. The collection pipeline uses two instrumented vehicles and one modified DJI M350 RTK UAV, with GPS-disciplined clocks for unified time sources and ICP-based point cloud registration to align all agents into a common ego-vehicle frame. Annotation was performed by 100 annotators over two review rounds using a customized Xtreme1 platform, yielding over 720K 3D boxes across 350 scenes (14 scenario types, 8:2 day-to-night ratio, ~17% dynamic interaction events). For benchmarking, six cooperative perception frameworks (Late/Early Fusion, V2VNet, Cobevt, Where2comm, V2X-ViT) are adapted to the dataset with PointPillars as a shared backbone, trained for 60 epochs on 8×Nvidia L40 GPUs. A dedicated Δ_UAV metric quantifies the marginal performance gain from incorporating the aerial agent relative to the V2V-only baseline.

## Results

On AGC-V2V, Cobevt achieves the best intermediate-fusion performance with 46.1% mAP@0.5 and 41.7% mAP@0.7, substantially outperforming V2VNet (18.4%/5.7%) and V2X-ViT (44.1%/36.6%). On AGC-VUC, incorporating the UAV improves all frameworks: V2VNet gains the most (Δ_UAV = +11.5, reaching 40.1%/27.9%), while V2X-ViT gains +4.8 (42.6%/33.9%), Where2comm gains +1.5 (44.2%/32.0%), and Cobevt gains only +0.6 (42.9%/37.5%). The late-fusion baseline (PointPillars) scores 17.7%/13.5% on V2V, and early fusion marginally improves to 19.6%/14.1%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper benchmarks four intermediate-fusion cooperative perception frameworks (V2VNet, Cobevt, Where2comm, V2X-ViT) on both AGC-V2V and AGC-VUC. On the V2V task, Cobevt leads with 46.1% mAP@0.5, followed by V2X-ViT (44.1%), Where2comm (39.3%), and V2VNet (18.4%), with V2VNet's poor mAP@0.7 (5.7%) indicating severe localization errors. When the UAV is added (AGC-VUC), all four methods improve, but the magnitude of gain is inversely correlated with baseline strength: V2VNet benefits most (+11.5 Δ_UAV) because its weak ground-only perception leaves large occluded regions that the aerial view fills, while Cobevt, already the strongest V2V method, gains only +0.6. V2X-ViT shows a balanced improvement (+4.8), and Where2comm gains +1.5. Overall, no single framework dominates both V2V and V2U settings, suggesting that the UAV's top-down perspective is most valuable for methods that struggle with occlusion and long-range detection in ground-only configurations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the collaborative 3D perception dataset lineage (OPV2V → V2V4Real → DAIR-V2X → V2X-Real for ground agents; CoPerception-UAV, UAV3D, Griffin for aerial agents) by introducing the first real-world, driving-scenario dataset that unifies two ground vehicles and one LiDAR-equipped UAV under a shared 3D annotation framework. Unlike prior V2U efforts (V2U-COO, Griffin) which are simulated, or CoPeD which is real-world but non-driving and 2D-only, AGC-Drive repurposes existing cooperative detection backbones (V2VNet, Cobevt, Where2comm, V2X-ViT) as benchmarks while contributing the novel data modality of airborne LiDAR point clouds in a driving context. The spatiotemporal alignment pipeline (GPS/IMU + ICP registration) follows standard multi-agent localization practice but is applied here for the first time to a vehicle-UAV pair in open-road driving.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AGC-Drive fills a concrete gap: no prior dataset offered real-world, driving-scenario aerial-ground 3D perception with UAV LiDAR, making it the natural benchmark for the emerging V2U cooperative perception subfield. However, the benchmark results reveal that the current 32-beam UAV LiDAR provides only marginal gains (+0.6 to +4.8 mAP) for already-competitive ground methods, with the largest improvement (+11.5) reserved for the weakest baseline (V2VNet), suggesting that sparse aerial point clouds are insufficient for fine-grained object-level assistance. Open questions include whether higher-density UAV LiDAR or multi-UAV configurations can close this gap, how performance degrades under adverse weather, and whether the retained time delays and pose errors (intentionally preserved for realism) can be mitigated without sacrificing the dataset's ecological validity.
