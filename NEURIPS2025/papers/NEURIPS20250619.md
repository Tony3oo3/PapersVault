## Problem

Current document retrieval-augmented generation (DocRAG) systems are text-centric, frequently overlooking critical visual evidence such as charts, tables, and figures in lengthy multimodal documents. Moreover, no existing benchmark evaluates a model's ability to select relevant multimodal evidence from noisy retrieved quotes or to coherently integrate visual content with text in generated answers, leaving a gap in assessing complex multimodal RAG scenarios.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250619/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a user question and a fixed candidate set of 15 or 20 quotes (10/12 text + 5/8 image quotes), where only ~18%/13.5% are gold and the remainder are hard negatives with high textual or visual similarity to the question or answer. The output is an interleaved text-image answer with explicit traceable citations (text quotes as [i], image quotes as ![](image_j)), evaluated via per-modality quote selection F1 and LLM-as-judge scores on five dimensions (fluency, citation quality, text-image coherence, reasoning logic, factuality), each scaled 0–5.

## Method

MMDocRAG is built from 222 long documents (avg. 67 pages, 33k words) across 10 domains, parsed with MinerU/LayoutLMv3 to extract content-aware quotes at multiple granularities (page-level screenshots down to fine-grained layout segments). Two novel evaluation features are introduced: (1) a quote selection metric that mixes hard text and image negatives with gold quotes to test evidence discrimination under noise, and (2) a multimodal output paradigm where answers interleave document figures, infographics, charts, and tables within text rather than generating visuals from scratch. The paper benchmarks 60 VLM/LLM models (33 VLMs handling interleaved inputs, 27 text-only LLMs) and 14 retrievers (6 text, 4 visual, 4 hybrid) in both fixed-quote and end-to-end RAG settings, and fine-tunes 9 open-source models (Qwen2.5 3–72B, Qwen2.5-VL 3/7B, InternVL3 8/9B) on the 2,055-question dev-set. Key findings: GPT-4.1 achieves the best quote-selection F1 of 70.2% and 4.14 average answer quality; proprietary VLMs show modest gains from multimodal over text-only inputs while open-source VLMs degrade significantly; and targeted fine-tuning yields substantial improvements (e.g., Qwen2.5-72B F1 rises from 59.1 to 64.9).

## Results

On MMDocRAG (20-quote setting), GPT-4.1 achieves the highest quote selection F1 of 70.2% (multimodal input) and 68.3% (pure-text input), with an average answer quality score of 4.14/5. Other leading proprietary models (Gemini-2.5-Pro, Claude-3.5-Sonnet, GPT-4o) achieve F1 scores of 57–65% and answer quality of 3.6–3.9. Open-source models lag substantially: the best open-source LLMs (Qwen2.5-72B, DeepSeek-V3) reach F1 ≈ 59–61%, while smaller VLMs (InternVL3-8B, Qwen2.5-VL-7B) drop to F1 ≈ 37–51% in the multimodal setting. Fine-tuning on the MMDocRAG dev-set yields large gains, e.g., Qwen2.5-72B improves from F1 59.1→64.9 and answer quality 3.75→3.97.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPT-4.1 leads all 60 evaluated models on both quote selection (F1 70.2%) and answer quality (4.14/5), outperforming the next-best proprietary models (Gemini-2.5-Pro at F1 65.4, Claude-3.5-Sonnet at 62.5) by 5–8 F1 points. Among open-source LLMs, DeepSeek-V3 (F1 61.1) and Qwen2.5-72B (F1 59.1) are the strongest, but both trail GPT-4.1 by ~10 F1 points; fine-tuning Qwen2.5-72B narrows this gap to ~5 points (F1 64.9). A striking finding is that smaller open-source VLMs (InternVL3-8B, Qwen2.5-VL-7B) suffer severe degradation with multimodal inputs—InternVL3-8B drops from F1 48.1 (text) to 37.0 (multimodal), a 23% relative loss—while proprietary VLMs (GPT-4.1, Gemini, Claude) show modest or no degradation. For LLMs processing image quotes as text, VLM-generated descriptions consistently outperform OCR-extracted text by 6–10 F1 points on average (e.g., Qwen2.5-7B: 59.8 vs 49.6). In end-to-end RAG (Table 7), replacing perfect retrieval with a single BGE retriever drops GPT-4.1's F1 from 70.2 to 54.4 (−22.5%) and answer quality from 4.14 to 3.53 (−14.7%), while multi-retriever ensembles partially recover performance (F1 59.9).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on MMDocIR [19] for its document corpus and a subset of QA pairs, extending that retrieval-focused benchmark into the generation side of the RAG pipeline. It departs from prior DocVQA/DocRAG benchmarks (MP-DocVQA, DUDE, MMLongBench-Doc, M3DocVQA, M-Longdoc) that evaluate only text-only answers, and from multimodal RAG work (MuRAR, M²RAG) that is limited to webpage domains without document-level evidence chains. The key conceptual departure is introducing explicit quote selection from noisy multimodal candidates and interleaved text-image answer generation as first-class evaluation targets, rather than treating visual content as an afterthought.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MMDocRAG fills a clear gap: no prior benchmark evaluates whether a model can distinguish relevant multimodal evidence from hard negatives and produce traceable, interleaved answers over long documents. The finding that even GPT-4.1 plateaus at 70.2% F1 on quote selection, and that open-source VLMs degrade significantly when given multimodal (vs. pure-text) inputs, highlights a substantial and underexplored weakness in current architectures. Open questions include how to make smaller VLMs handle long interleaved contexts without the token overhead that currently makes them underperform their LLM counterparts, and whether the fine-tuning gains observed here transfer to truly open-ended document corpora beyond the 222-document test set.
