## Problem

Despite LLMs' strong multilingual translation performance, the internal computational mechanisms driving translation remain unexplained, even at the word level. Prior interpretability work has been observational (neuron activation patterns, intermediate representations) rather than causal, leaving it unclear which specific components are indispensable for translation, what functional roles they play, and whether selectively modifying them can improve translation without degrading general capabilities.

## Input / Output

The method consumes contrastive prompt pairs—word-level translation prompts (e.g., 'English: cloud - 中文:') paired with counterfactual variants that preserve grammar but remove translation logic (e.g., 'English: cloud - Nothing:')—along with the full set of component activations extracted at the final token position. It outputs per-component importance scores (relative logit changes), a sparse set of crucial attention heads (<5%) classified into source/indicator/positional roles, crucial MLPs with English-centric latent representations, and a targeted fine-tuned model that modifies fewer than 5% of parameters while matching full-parameter fine-tuning on BLEU/COMET/BLEURT.

## Method

The core contribution is subspace-intervened path patching, which refines standard path patching by first identifying a low-dimensional 'translation-steering' subspace within each component's activation space via SVD-based common-specific decomposition of contrastive activation differences, then confining causal interventions to this subspace rather than the full activation vector to isolate translation-specific effects from polysemantic confounds. This enables precise detection of a sparse set of crucial attention heads (concentrated in middle and final layers, with >70% overlap across language pairs sharing a source or target) and crucial MLPs. Behavioral analysis reveals three specialized head roles—source heads attending to source-language tokens, indicator heads tracking translation-task signals, and positional heads maintaining sequential coherence—while MLP analysis via cosine similarity with unembedding vectors shows iterative integration of source features into English-centric latent representations before target generation. Knockout validation confirms component importance, and targeted fine-tuning of just 64 heads achieves translation performance parity with full-parameter SFT while preserving general capabilities (MMLU, ARC, SIQA) and halving training time.

## Results

Targeted SFT, fine-tuning only 0.27B parameters (<5% of the 6.7B LLaMA2-7B model), achieves translation performance at or above full-parameter fine-tuning: 21.3/79.1/64.3 (BLEU/COMET/BLEURT) on Zh⇒En vs. 20.4/78.7/63.9 for Full SFT, and 37.1/83.7/71.4 on De⇒En vs. 35.4/83.4/70.7. Crucially, Targeted SFT preserves general capabilities (MMLU 46.0, Commonsense 55.7) while Full SFT degrades them (MMLU 42.6, Commonsense 50.2). Training speed is 33 samples/sec for Targeted SFT versus 17 for Full SFT. Ablation shows that fine-tuning just 64 crucial attention heads (0.134B params) reaches 21.3/79.1/64.3 on Zh⇒En, matching the full Targeted SFT result.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Full SFT (6.7B params), Targeted SFT (0.27B params) matches or slightly exceeds translation quality across all four directions (e.g., +0.9 BLEU on Zh⇒En, +1.7 BLEU on De⇒En) while preserving general capabilities that Full SFT degrades by 3.3 points on MMLU and 5.1 points on Commonsense. Against Random SFT (same 0.27B parameter budget but randomly selected components), Targeted SFT substantially outperforms on translation (e.g., 21.3 vs. 16.9 BLEU on Zh⇒En, +4.4 BLEU) and maintains general capabilities (MMLU 46.0 vs. 45.9), confirming that the identified crucial components are genuinely translation-specific rather than a generic parameter-count effect. Against standard path patching (Wang et al., 2023), the proposed subspace-intervened path patching identifies more critical components: in Table 9, it yields larger average logit changes (e.g., −6.81% vs. −3.12% on En⇒Sw), larger accuracy drops upon knockout of top-5 heads (−42% vs. −28%), and better targeted SFT performance (3.91/55.1/43.7 vs. 1.83/51.5/40.9 BLEU/COMET/BLEURT on En⇒Sw), demonstrating that subspace-confined intervention isolates translation-specific causal effects more precisely than full-vector patching.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the mechanistic interpretability toolkit—specifically path patching (Wang et al., 2023; Goldowsky-Dill et al., 2023)—to the domain of multilingual translation in decoder-only LLMs, a setting where prior NMT interpretability work (Voita et al., 2019; Bau et al., 2019) was confined to encoder-decoder seq2seq architectures. The key methodological departure is the proposed subspace-intervened path patching, which refines standard full-vector activation patching by confining interventions to a translation-specific subspace identified via common-specific SVD decomposition (Xie et al., 2022; Piratla et al., 2020), thereby isolating causal translation effects from polysemantic confounds. The behavioral characterization of attention heads (source, indicator, positional roles) follows Voita et al. (2019), while the MLP analysis via unembedding projection builds on Geva et al. (2022), and the English-centric latent representation finding corroborates Wendler et al. (2024).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic causal analysis of translation mechanisms in decoder-only LLMs, providing a component-level circuit description that moves beyond the observational neuron-activation studies that dominated prior multilingual LLM interpretability. The practical implication—that fine-tuning only 64 heads (<5% of parameters) matches full-parameter SFT on translation while preserving general capabilities—offers a concrete, efficient pathway for improving multilingual LLMs without catastrophic forgetting. Key limitations include the focus on word-level translation (though sentence-level extension is shown), restriction to open-source LLaMA-2/Mistral architectures, and the unresolved question of whether the English-centric pivot is an inherent design constraint or an artifact of training data distribution. Open questions include whether these sparse circuits persist at document-level translation, how they scale to larger models, and whether the English pivot can be deliberately reconfigured for non-English-dominant multilingual systems.
