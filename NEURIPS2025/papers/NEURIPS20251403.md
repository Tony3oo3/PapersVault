## Problem

Existing zero-shot monocular metric depth estimation (MMDE) methods suffer from significant performance degradation on out-of-distribution scenes due to inherent depth-scale ambiguity and texture-depth coupling. While relative depth estimation (MDE) generalizes well, its outputs lack the metric scale needed for downstream 3D tasks. The paper asks whether classical defocus blur cues from a variable-aperture camera can be injected at inference time into a pretrained relative depth diffusion model to recover metric depth without any retraining.

## Input / Output

The method requires two raw (linear, unprocessed) images captured from the same viewpoint with a variable-aperture DSLR: an all-in-focus image at high F-stop (N=22) and a blurred image at low F-stop (N=8), along with camera parameters (focal length f, focus distance F, F-stop N) read from EXIF/lens metadata and a priori scene depth bounds (s_min, s_max). The AIF image is encoded by the Stable Diffusion v2 VAE and fed to Marigold-LCM for relative depth prediction, while the blurred image serves as the physics-based supervision signal. The output is a per-pixel metric depth map in meters, recovered by affine-transforming the [0,1]-normalized relative depth with optimized scale and offset parameters.

## Method

The paper formulates MMDE as an inverse problem under a defocus blur forward model, using Marigold-LCM as a differentiable monocular depth prior. A single inference step of Marigold-LCM maps a learnable noise latent z_T^(d) to a relative depth map, which is then affine-transformed with learnable metric scale α and offset β (parameterized via sigmoid for boundedness) to yield metric depth. The core optimization minimizes the L2 loss between the captured blurred image and a synthesized blurred image produced by convolving the AIF image with a depth-dependent disc PSF (whose radius is determined by the circle-of-confusion equation), updating both the noise latent and the affine parameters via Adam over 200 iterations. A key design choice is using a disc PSF with linear boundary falloff rather than a Gaussian, which better matches the real camera's point spread function and yields substantially lower error. The noise latent is rescaled to maintain its L2 norm after each gradient step to stay on the Gaussian manifold, and the entire procedure is training-free, requiring only a variable-aperture camera and known scene depth bounds.

## Results

On the self-collected DiffusionCam dataset (7 real-world indoor scenes), the proposed method (Ours-Disc) achieves RMSE 0.273, REL 0.125, log10 0.052, and δ₁ 0.879, outperforming all zero-shot MMDE baselines across every metric. The closest competitor, Metric3D, achieves RMSE 0.349 and δ₁ 0.611, while MLPro and UniDepth trail further behind (RMSE 0.468 and 0.574, respectively). The method also outperforms fine-tuning-based baselines (DepthAnythingv2 FT on NYUv2: RMSE 0.407; Marigold FT on NYUv2: RMSE 0.475), validating the zero-shot inference-time optimization approach. On the NYUv2 test set, the method is on par with MMDE baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all three zero-shot MMDE baselines on every metric. Against Metric3D (the strongest baseline), it reduces RMSE from 0.349 to 0.273 (22% improvement) and improves δ₁ from 0.611 to 0.879. Against MLPro, RMSE drops from 0.468 to 0.273 and δ₁ jumps from 0.597 to 0.879. UniDepth is the weakest baseline (RMSE 0.574, δ₁ 0.263), with the proposed method achieving roughly 2× lower RMSE. Compared to fine-tuning-based methods (DepthAnythingv2 FT on NYUv2: RMSE 0.407; Marigold FT on NYUv2: RMSE 0.475), the zero-shot inference-time optimization approach achieves substantially better results without any dataset-specific training. The paper also reports outperforming Srinivasan et al. [56], a self-supervised multi-aperture depth method, though detailed numbers are deferred to the supplement. Notably, replacing the physically-motivated Disc PSF with a Gaussian PSF causes severe degradation (RMSE 0.528 vs. 0.273), underscoring the importance of a physically consistent forward model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Marigold (Ke et al., CVPR 2024), a diffusion-based monocular relative depth estimator, by repurposing it as a metric depth predictor through inference-time optimization of its noise latents. Conceptually, it bridges two previously separate lines of work: classical depth-from-defocus (DfD) methods that exploit the circle-of-confusion relationship between aperture and depth, and modern diffusion-model-based inverse problem solvers that incorporate forward-model constraints during sampling. The key departure from prior diffusion-based inverse problem approaches (e.g., DPS, DDPM-based solvers) is that rather than injecting the forward model into the denoising chain, the paper optimizes the initial noise latent against a physics-based defocus blur loss, keeping the generative model frozen. It also extends the inference-time noise optimization paradigm (DITTO, TiNO-Edit) from learned guidance losses to a differentiable physical imaging model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a training-free, zero-shot approach combining a pretrained relative depth prior with a simple physics-based forward model can outperform fine-tuned metric depth models on real data, suggesting that physical cues can substitute for task-specific training in scale-sensitive estimation problems. However, the method is constrained by slow inference (~4 min per scene), a requirement for a variable-aperture camera with known parameters, and a limited depth range where defocus cues remain discriminative. Open questions include whether the approach scales to outdoor scenes with large depth ranges, how it handles occlusion boundaries where the forward model breaks down, and whether the technique generalizes to other depth-dependent imaging processes (hyperspectral, endoscopy, microscopy) as the authors speculate.
