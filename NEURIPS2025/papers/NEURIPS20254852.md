## Problem

Existing safe and stable control methods face a fundamental tension: QP-based certificate methods require control-affine dynamics and step-wise greedy optimization that produces globally inconsistent policies, while MPC suffers from computational infeasibility and local minima. The paper asks whether diffusion sampling can replace gradient-based solvers entirely, yielding trajectory-level policies that guarantee both safety and stability without slack variables or affine-dynamics assumptions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254852/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Given a current state x, a known differentiable dynamics model f(x,u) (which need not be control-affine), a learned CLBF V parameterized as a neural network, and optionally a nominal policy, the method produces a full control trajectory u_{1:T} over a fixed horizon T via guided diffusion sampling. The CLBF is iteratively refined using the sampled trajectories, so the system outputs both the control sequence and an improved certificate function. Unlike QP-based methods that produce a single-step action, the output is a complete trajectory-level policy, and the CLBF's non-quadratic neural parameterization allows it to capture non-convex safe sets.

## Method

S²Diff reformulates the constrained control problem as a Gibbs measure over trajectory sequences, where safety (V(x_t) ≤ c), stability (Lie derivative dissipation), and cost are encoded as trajectory-level scoring functions. A Monte Carlo score ascent diffusion process then samples trajectories guided by the CLBF, avoiding both slack variables and control-affine assumptions. The CLBF is updated iteratively via a loss that enforces all five CLBF conditions (equilibrium, positivity, safe/unsafe set boundaries, and uniform dissipation) using both automatic-differentiation and discretized Lie derivative terms. The key theoretical contribution links this probabilistic formulation to Almost Lyapunov theory: the CLBF need not decrease at every point, only almost everywhere, with violations confined to a small-volume region Ω, yielding an almost-sure exponential decay bound V(x_t) ≤ exp(−λ₁t)V(x₀) + O(ε^{1/n}). This end-to-end loop—diffusion sampling generates trajectories, trajectories refine the CLBF, and the refined CLBF guides subsequent sampling—progressively shrinks the violation region and tightens the stability guarantee.

## Results

Across 8 nonlinear control tasks, S²Diff achieves a 98.75% average safety rate and 0.226 average terminal tracking error (‖x−x⋆‖), substantially outperforming rCLBF-QP (78.75% safety, 0.384 error), MPC (66.25% safety, 0.501 error), and MBD (73.75% safety, 0.954 error). Inference time averages 45.64 ms, faster than MPC (249.53 ms) and MBD (122.73 ms) but slower than rCLBF-QP (10.06 ms). On the 16-dimensional non-control-affine F-16 task, S²Diff achieves 100% safety with 47.61 tracking error in 257.2 ms, outperforming MBD (68.34 error, 611.3 ms) while rCLBF-QP and MPC are inapplicable.

## Baselines & Comparisons

S²Diff dominates all three baselines on the primary safety and stability metrics: it achieves 98.75% average safety rate versus 78.75% (rCLBF-QP), 66.25% (MPC), and 73.75% (MBD), and reduces average terminal tracking error to 0.226 versus 0.384, 0.501, and 0.954 respectively. Against rCLBF-QP, S²Diff wins decisively on safety and tracking but is ~4.5× slower in inference (45.64 ms vs 10.06 ms), reflecting the cost of trajectory-level diffusion sampling versus step-wise QP. Against MPC, S²Diff wins on all three metrics—safety, tracking, and speed (45.64 ms vs 249.53 ms)—demonstrating that diffusion sampling avoids the computational bottleneck of non-convex receding-horizon optimization. Against MBD (the closest diffusion-based baseline), S²Diff improves safety by 25 percentage points and tracking error by 4×, attributed to the CLBF-guided sampling that shapes a global energy landscape rather than relying on local penalty-based repulsion. On the non-control-affine F-16 task, S²Diff is the only method besides MBD that can operate at all (rCLBF-QP and MPC require control-affine dynamics), and it reduces tracking error from 68.34 to 47.61 while cutting inference time nearly in half (257 ms vs 611 ms).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: model-based diffusion planning (Pan et al., NeurIPS 2024), control Lyapunov-barrier functions (CLBFs, Romdlony & Jayawardhana, Automatica 2016), and Almost Lyapunov theory (Liu et al., Automatica 2020). It departs from the dominant QP-based certificate approach (Dawson et al., CoRL 2022) by replacing step-wise quadratic programming with CLBF-guided diffusion sampling, thereby eliminating the need for control-affine dynamics, slack variables, and per-step greedy optimization. The key conceptual move is reframing the CLBF dissipation constraint as a soft probabilistic term in a Gibbs measure, which the authors show is a natural realization of Almost Lyapunov theory rather than a mere relaxation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework removes two major practical barriers in safe control: the control-affine assumption (demonstrated on the 16-state F-16) and the globally inconsistent behavior introduced by slack variables in QP formulations. The theoretical link between diffusion sampling and Almost Lyapunov stability provides a principled justification for why soft constraints suffice, with the violation region empirically kept below 2.5% across all tasks. A key limitation is inference speed (~46 ms average vs. ~10 ms for QP), which the authors acknowledge could be addressed via policy distillation; whether this distillation preserves the safety guarantees remains an open question. Broader implications include whether the CLBF-guided diffusion paradigm can extend to multi-agent or partially-observed settings where the current single-agent, full-state-observation assumption breaks down.
