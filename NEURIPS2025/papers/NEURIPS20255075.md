## Problem

Existing multimodal alignment methods require tens of millions of paired samples to learn a shared embedding space, making them infeasible in domains like healthcare and biology where paired data is expensive to collect. This paper asks whether pretrained unimodal foundation models can be effectively aligned into a shared representation space using only tens of thousands of paired samples—less than 1% of what prior work requires.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255075/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes frozen pretrained unimodal encoder outputs (e.g., DINOv2 ViT-G patch embeddings for images, RoBERTa sentence embeddings for text, or BEATs for audio) and learns lightweight alignment functions (linear projections, MLPs, or CSA decompositions) that map each modality's latent space into a shared k-dimensional embedding space. Training uses as few as 80K paired samples from MS COCO, and the resulting shared space supports zero-shot classification (via text prompt embeddings) and cross-modal retrieval (image-to-text and text-to-image). The encoders remain frozen throughout; only the alignment functions are optimized.

## Method

The paper introduces two plug-and-play components that can be added to any existing alignment method. First, STRUCTURE is a regularization term that preserves the multi-scale neighborhood geometry of each modality's pretrained latent space: it computes temperature-scaled similarity matrices, applies row-wise softmax to obtain transition distributions, raises them to powers l=1,...,L to capture l-hop relational structure, and penalizes the Jensen-Shannon divergence between the original and aligned distributions at each level, weighted by 1/l. Second, instead of defaulting to the final encoder layers, the method selects the layer pair with the highest representational similarity (measured by mutual kNN on ~5K paired samples) for alignment, since higher similarity correlates with better downstream performance. The combined loss is L_A + λ(R_S(X₁, f₁(X₁)) + R_S(X₂, f₂(X₂))), where L_A is any standard contrastive objective. A generalization bound (O(1/√N)) is proven via McDiarmid's inequality, showing the empirical regularizer concentrates around its population expectation.

## Results

Using only 80K MS COCO pairs (0.02% of CLIP's 400M), the full STRUCTURE framework (most-similar layers + regularization) achieves 96.3% on CIFAR-10, 25.1% on ImageNet, and 65.9% R@1 on Flickr30 I2T retrieval with MLP alignment. Average relative improvements over the corresponding last-layer baselines are 51.6% in zero-shot classification and 91.8% in cross-modal retrieval across 24 benchmarks. On CIFAR-10, the method even outperforms CLIP by ~2% while using 5000× less training data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Linear+Last [10], the full method (Linear+Similar+R_S) wins on every reported dataset, with the largest gaps on ImageNet (24.7 vs 9.9) and Flickr30 I2T retrieval (65.8 vs 32.5 R@1). Against MLP+Last [9], the full method (MLP+Similar+R_S) similarly dominates, e.g., CIFAR-10 96.3 vs 79.2 and ImageNet 25.1 vs 10.6. Against CSA+Last [29], the full method (CSA+Similar+R_S) wins on most datasets (CIFAR-10: 97.2 vs 78.5; ImageNet: 26.8 vs 23.2) but slightly trails on Food-101 (28.6 vs 29.3), indicating CSA's matrix-decomposition objective already captures some structure that STRUCTURE partially overlaps. Compared to CLIP [7] trained on 400M pairs, the method matches or exceeds CLIP on CIFAR-10 (96.3 vs ~94%) using only 0.02% of the data, and with just 3 in-domain samples per class on Flowers it reaches >95% vs CLIP's 93%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the parameter-frozen modality alignment line of work (Khan & Fu 2023, Maniparambil et al. 2024, Li et al. 2025/CSA), which aligns pretrained unimodal encoders via lightweight projection layers trained with contrastive objectives. The key departure is addressing the low-data regime: prior methods require tens of millions of paired samples, while this paper introduces a STRUCTURE regularizer (multi-scale Jensen-Shannon divergence on neighborhood distributions) and a mutual-kNN-based layer selection strategy that enable effective alignment with as few as tens of thousands of pairs. Conceptually, the work draws on the Platonic representation hypothesis and geometric priors in representation alignment, but applies them as an explicit training-time constraint rather than a matching criterion, and challenges the default assumption of aligning last layers by showing intermediate layers with higher cross-modal similarity are superior.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for domains like healthcare and biology where collecting millions of paired multimodal samples is infeasible; the demonstration that a handful of in-domain samples per class can match or exceed CLIP (trained on 400M pairs) on fine-grained benchmarks is a strong signal for resource-constrained applications. The plug-and-play nature of the regularization means it can be dropped into any existing alignment pipeline without architectural changes. Open questions include extending to three or more modalities, understanding which data types most effectively drive alignment quality in low-data settings, and closing the remaining gap on the most challenging tasks (e.g., Pets at ~15% without in-domain data). The regularization's diminishing returns with more data suggest it is specifically a low-data tool rather than a universal improvement.
