## Problem

Chemical foundation models are constrained by the absence of diverse, large-scale, high-quality datasets that capture the multifaceted nature of chemistry. Existing resources are fragmented across single modalities (e.g., SMILES-only), narrowly scoped to one subdomain, and rarely curated with machine learning in mind, leading to inconsistent coverage, data leakage, and poor generalization. No prior open dataset reaches the scale or content-type diversity needed for meaningful foundation-model training in the chemical sciences.

## Input / Output

The pipeline ingests heterogeneous raw sources—EuroPMC full-text articles (filtered via a BERT multilabel classifier), LibreTexts HTML, MIT OCW transcripts, YouTube CC-licensed lectures, US Olympiad PDFs, StarCoder/CodeParrot code (regex-filtered), Stack Exchange Q&A, and structured chemical tables—and converts them into a unified Parquet format on HuggingFace. Each molecular entry is expanded into multiple representations (SMILES, SELFIES, IUPAC via OPSIN, InChI, and RanDepict-generated images), and tabular data is rendered through 1,636 manually curated language-interfaced templates supporting completion, instruction, and multiple-choice formats. The final output is 77 billion tokens (250 GB compressed) across eight subsets with scaffold-split (RDKit Murcko) train/validation/test partitions that prevent molecular leakage across all tabular datasets simultaneously.

## Method

ChemPile is constructed through a multi-stage curation pipeline: (1) source-specific collection and filtering (BERT classifier for papers, regex for code, HTML parsing for education, Nougat for PDF extraction); (2) a custom sampling engine that fills 1,636 expert-curated templates with synonym sampling, permutation of enumeration symbols, and conditional formatting to generate diverse language-interfaced text from tabular data; (3) systematic computation of alternative molecular representations and images for every entry; and (4) expert review via GitHub Pull Requests with at least one domain-expert reviewer per change. For empirical validation, the authors sample 100M tokens per subset, train separate LoRA adapters, and evaluate two novel combination strategies—LoRA-Ensemble (perplexity-based generation selection) and LoRA-Merge (linear adapter merging)—on ChemBench, achieving a 9% overall improvement over the Qwen-2.5-7B-Instruct base model.

## Results

On the ChemBench benchmark, the best ChemPile-trained configurations (LoRA-Ensemble and LoRA-Merge, each using ~315–395M tokens from four subsets) achieve an overall score of 0.47, outperforming the base Qwen-2.5-7B-Instruct model (0.43) by approximately 9%. Individual subset LoRA fine-tunes (100M tokens each) yield modest gains: ChemPile-Paper reaches 0.45, ChemPile-Education 0.44, and ChemPile-LIFT 0.43. The combined LoRA Mix + SFT approach (415M tokens) achieves 0.46, confirming that multi-subset training consistently improves performance across chemistry subfields including Analytical Chemistry (0.34 vs. 0.24 base) and Organic Chemistry (0.52 vs. 0.50 base).

## Baselines & Comparisons

In terms of dataset scale, ChemPile (77B tokens, 257 GB) is more than 50% larger than ChemDFM's training corpus (34B tokens, unreleased) and orders of magnitude larger than LlaSMol (instruction-tuning dataset) and ChemDual (4.4M reaction instructions). Compared to domain-specific foundation models like BioGPT (~4.5B estimated tokens) and BatGPT (chemical engineering), ChemPile provides roughly 17× more tokens, making it the only open chemical dataset at a scale meaningful for foundation model pre-training. On the ChemBench evaluation, the ChemPile-trained LoRA-Ensemble and LoRA-Merge models (0.47 overall) outperform the base Qwen-2.5-7B-Instruct (0.43) and also surpass the larger Mixtral 8x7B (0.42), demonstrating that targeted chemical data curation can yield gains even with a 7B-parameter model. The LoRA-Merge variant shows particular strength in Inorganic Chemistry (0.60 vs. 0.57 base) and General Chemistry (0.51 vs. 0.44 base), while LoRA-Ensemble leads in Analytical Chemistry (0.34 vs. 0.24 base), suggesting complementary knowledge captured by different subsets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LIFT (Language-Interfaced Fine-Tuning) paradigm [25] to chemistry by systematically converting structured chemical datasets into diverse natural-language templates, while also drawing on the broader trend of constructing large, diverse corpora for foundation model pretraining (The Pile, Dolma, FineWeb). In the chemical domain specifically, it departs from prior resources like Mol-instructions, MoMu, PubChemSTM, and ChemDFM, which each cover a single modality or subdomain, by unifying seven distinct content types (education, papers, structured data, code, reasoning, captions, instructions) and multiple molecular representations (SMILES, SELFIES, IUPAC, InChI) under one consistent interface. The scaffold-based splitting strategy and the inclusion of both completion and instruction-format templates for ablation studies represent methodological extensions beyond what prior chemical datasets offered.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ChemPile addresses the core bottleneck in chemical AI — the absence of a large, diverse, curated dataset that mirrors the full spectrum of chemical expertise — and at 77B tokens it is the largest open chemical corpus available, making it a likely standard resource for the next generation of chemical foundation models. However, the empirical validation is limited to 100M-token LoRA finetuning on a single 7B base model evaluated on one benchmark (ChemBench), so the dataset's true value for full-scale pretraining and its optimal mixing ratios remain untested. Open questions include whether the LLM-generated instruction data (ChemPile-Instruction) introduces systematic biases, whether the heterogeneous licensing across subsets will limit adoption, and whether the IUPAC-name correlation finding generalizes to full pretraining rather than just embedding similarity.
