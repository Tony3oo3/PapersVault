## Problem

Directly minimizing the balanced classification loss (which assigns equal importance to all classes regardless of frequency) is intractable, making effective surrogate losses essential for imbalanced multi-class learning. Existing surrogates such as class-weighted cross-entropy fail to shift decision boundaries in separable regimes, and Logit-Adjusted losses are not Bayes-consistent for τ≠1 and admit H-consistency bounds that scale as 1/p_min with the minimum class probability. This paper introduces two new surrogate loss families with stronger theoretical guarantees and better empirical performance.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 64×64 for Tiny ImageNet) paired with class labels during training, and outputs a predicted class label at inference. The loss functions operate on the per-class logits produced by a ResNet-32 backbone, incorporating the known class prior p(y) and, for GCA, class-specific confidence margin parameters ρ_y (defaulted to m_k^{1/3} where m_k is the class sample count). Evaluation is via balanced error (average per-class error) on imbalanced test sets with imbalance ratios ρ=100 and ρ=1000.

## Method

The paper proposes two surrogate loss families derived from the general cross-entropy (GCE) framework. GLA losses generalize Logit-Adjusted losses by adding a class-prior bias term log(p(y))/(1−q) to each logit before applying the GCE function Ψ^q, unifying the LA loss (q=0, τ=1) with robust GCE variants (q∈(0,1)). GCA losses generalize class-weighted cross-entropy by (i) inversely weighting the GCE loss by 1/p(y), (ii) scaling logits by class-dependent confidence margins ρ_y to modulate pairwise score differences, and (iii) extending to the full GCE family. Theoretically, GLA is Bayes-consistent for q∈[0,1) but H-consistent only for complete (unbounded) hypothesis sets with bounds scaling as 1/p_min, while GCA is H-consistent for any bounded or complete hypothesis set with bounds scaling as 1/√p_min, offering substantially stronger guarantees in highly imbalanced settings. Empirically, both losses outperform WCE, LA, FOCAL, LDAM, CB, and EQUAL baselines on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and Tiny ImageNet under long-tailed and step imbalances, with GLA slightly ahead at moderate imbalance and GCA leading at extreme imbalance (ρ=1000).

## Results

The paper reports balanced error (lower is better) for ResNet-32 on long-tailed and step-imbalanced CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and Tiny ImageNet at imbalance ratios ρ=1000 and ρ=100. On long-tailed CIFAR-100 at ρ=1000, GCA achieves 33.17±0.57 and GLA achieves 33.99±0.52 balanced error, substantially outperforming LA(τ=1) (35.92±0.47) and standard CE (38.45±0.37). At the more moderate ρ=100 on long-tailed CIFAR-100, GLA edges out GCA (53.68±0.76 vs. 54.02±0.38), while both still clearly beat LA(τ=1) (56.23±0.21) and CE (61.53±0.29). On Tiny ImageNet (long-tailed, ρ=1000), GCA reaches 64.88±0.66 and GLA 65.57±0.27, compared to LA(τ=1) at 67.17±0.49 and CE at 70.23±0.38.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Both GCA and GLA consistently outperform all six baselines (CE, WCE, LA, EQUAL, CB, FOCAL, LDAM) across every dataset and imbalance setting. On long-tailed CIFAR-100 at ρ=1000, GCA (33.17) and GLA (33.99) reduce balanced error by roughly 2.7–5.3 points over the strongest baseline LA(τ=1) (35.92), and by 4.5–8.3 points over CE (38.45). At the moderate ρ=100 setting, GLA slightly edges GCA on CIFAR-100 (53.68 vs. 54.02) and CIFAR-10 (2.07 vs. 2.19), while GCA takes a small lead on Tiny ImageNet (101.34 vs. 100.70 is actually GLA winning; GCA wins on CIFAR-100 at ρ=1000). The gap over class-weighted CE (WCE) is particularly large on Tiny ImageNet at ρ=1000, where GCA (64.88) and GLA (65.57) improve over WCE (75.89) by over 10 points. CB (Class-Balanced) and FOCAL perform comparably to or worse than plain CE in most settings, while LDAM and EQUAL offer modest improvements over CE but remain 2–5 points behind GCA/GLA on CIFAR-100.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalizes two established surrogate loss families for imbalanced classification: Logit-Adjusted (LA) losses (Menon et al., 2021) and class-weighted cross-entropy (Xie and Manski, 1989), by embedding both within the Generalized Cross-Entropy (GCE) framework (Mao et al., 2023f). GLA extends LA's logit shifting to the full GCE family, while GCA augments class-weighting with class-dependent confidence margins. The key theoretical departure is establishing H-consistency bounds with respect to the balanced loss (rather than the standard 0-1 loss), revealing that LA is not Bayes-consistent for τ≠1 and that GLA's H-consistency requires complete (unbounded) hypothesis sets, whereas GCA works for both bounded and complete sets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first H-consistency guarantees for surrogate losses targeting the balanced loss, filling a gap where most existing imbalanced-learning methods (WCE, LA, LDAM, FOCAL) lack such theoretical grounding. The 1/√p_min scaling of GCA versus 1/p_min for GLA offers a principled criterion for method selection under varying degrees of imbalance. Open questions include extending these loss families to structured prediction and multi-label settings, and refining consistency bounds under realistic (non-complete, non-bounded) hypothesis classes. Empirically, both methods consistently outperform strong baselines, with GCA showing its theoretical advantage most clearly at extreme imbalance ratios (ρ=1000).
