## Problem

CORRELATION CLUSTERING partitions nodes into disjoint clusters, making hard binary decisions that cannot capture the nuanced, overlapping nature of real-world opinion structures where ideologically neighboring groups may agree on some issues while disagreeing on others. This paper asks whether the conflicts in a signed network reveal a small, interpretable set of prevalent opinion ranges (intervals) that explain interactions more accurately than disjoint clustering.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255784/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a signed graph G = (V, E+ ∪ E−) as input, where edge signs encode positive (amicable) or negative (conflicting) interactions between node pairs. For the constrained version, k intervals (e.g., a chain of 8 overlapping intervals) are specified as input, and each vertex must be assigned to exactly one of them. The output is an interval I_v ⊂ R per vertex such that positive edges correspond to overlapping intervals and negative edges to disjoint intervals, yielding a visually interpretable opinion spectrum.

## Method

The paper introduces BEST INTERVAL APPROXIMATION, which maximizes the number of edges consistent with an interval assignment (positive edges → overlap, negative edges → disjoint). Theoretically, the problem is shown NP-hard via a reduction from ACYCLIC DIGRAPH PARTITION that works even when positive edges form a single cycle, and a PTAS is provided for complete graphs with fixed k by generalizing the Giotis–Guruswami framework to handle overlapping clusters. Practically, two heuristics are proposed: GAIA performs iterative batch reassignment where vertices are greedily assigned to the interval maximizing their local agreement, and VENUS extends this with simulated annealing using a temperature-scaled softmax over agreement values to escape local optima. The key structural insight is that overlapping intervals are strictly more expressive than disjoint clusters, enabling representation of non-transitive relationships that CORRELATION CLUSTERING cannot capture without error.

## Results

On 8 real-world signed graph datasets, GAIA and VENUS (using only 8 overlapping intervals) achieve 20%–91% fewer violated edges than the best CORRELATION CLUSTERING baseline, with an average improvement of 38%. The largest gain is on the Bundestag co-voting dataset (0.25% disagreement vs. 2.95% for the best baseline, a 91.5% reduction), while the smallest is on WikiElec (11.24% vs. 14.13%, a 20.5% reduction). VENUS (simulated annealing variant) slightly outperforms GAIA on larger graphs (e.g., Slashdot: 8.94% vs. 9.05%) and exhibits lower variance across runs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against four state-of-the-art CORRELATION CLUSTERING algorithms: GAEC and GAECKLj (Keuper et al., ICCV 2015), SCMLEvo (Hausberger et al., ALENEX 2025), and RAMA (Abbas & Swoboda, CVPR 2021). GAIA and VENUS outperform all four baselines on every one of the 8 datasets, with the best baseline varying by dataset (SCMLEvo is typically strongest on larger graphs, while GAEC/GAECKLj are competitive on smaller ones). The improvement is most dramatic on the Bundestag dataset (91.5% fewer disagreements), where the overlapping interval structure captures the continuous political spectrum that disjoint clustering cannot. Notably, the baselines are allowed an unrestricted number of clusters while the proposed methods are limited to just 8 intervals, making the comparison particularly favorable to the interval approach. RAMA consistently performs worst among the baselines, especially on large graphs (e.g., 17.17% on Slashdot vs. 8.94% for VENUS), while SCMLEvo is the strongest baseline overall.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper introduces BEST INTERVAL APPROXIMATION, a new problem that generalizes CORRELATION CLUSTERING by replacing disjoint cluster assignments with potentially-overlapping opinion intervals, and extends the SITTING ARRANGEMENT problem of Kermarrec and Thraves by allowing general (non-unit) intervals rather than unit-length ones. The PTAS for complete graphs with fixed k directly generalizes the fixed-cluster PTAS of Giotis and Guruswami, while the NP-hardness reduction builds on and significantly extends the cycle-based reduction of Cygan et al. for the sitting arrangement problem. The paper also connects to Interval Editing for the complete-graph case, showing that the problem is FPT there but not for incomplete graphs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The sharp hardness boundary—NP-hard when positive edges form a cycle but trivially solvable when they form a path—provides a clean structural characterization that rules out several natural algorithmic approaches and has implications for detecting forbidden induced subgraphs in signed graphs. The 38% average reduction in disagreements over unrestricted-cluster CORRELATION CLUSTERING baselines using only 8 intervals demonstrates that overlapping structure captures real-world opinion dynamics that partition-based methods fundamentally cannot. Open questions include whether a 3/4-approximation always exists for the agreement version, whether a PTAS exists for the disagreement version with fixed k, and whether higher-dimensional or temporal interval models yield further gains.
