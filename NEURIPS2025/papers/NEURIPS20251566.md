## Problem

Adversarial training in kernel methods requires solving a min-max problem where the inner maximization over input-space perturbations is non-concave and generally intractable for nonlinear functions, limiting practical use. Moreover, standard kernel ridge regression requires tuning the regularization parameter with knowledge of the signal-to-noise ratio to achieve optimal rates. This paper asks whether adversarial training can be reformulated to be both computationally tractable and automatically adaptive to noise levels and function smoothness.

## Input / Output

The method takes tabular feature vectors x ∈ R^p with scalar labels y and an adversarial radius δ bounding perturbations in the RKHS norm. It outputs a regression function f in a (potentially infinite-dimensional) RKHS, evaluated at test points via the kernel trick. A key practical detail is that δ can be set without knowledge of the noise magnitude σ: for clean-data performance, δ ∝ 1/√n is recommended, while for adversarial robustness a fixed δ matching the test-time attack budget is used. The method also supports multiple kernel learning, where the perturbation set is the intersection of balls across D kernel spaces.

## Method

The core reformulation shifts adversarial perturbations from the input space to the RKHS feature space, replacing max_{Δx∈Ω_X} with max_{d∈Ω_H} where Ω_H = {d : ||d||_H ≤ δ}. This yields a closed-form inner maximization: (|y − f(x)| + δ||f||_H)², making the overall objective convex and amenable to efficient optimization. The resulting problem is solved via an iterative kernel ridge regression algorithm (Algorithm 1) that uses the η-trick variational reformulation to alternate between solving a reweighted kernel ridge regression and updating per-sample weights and a global regularization parameter. The paper proves that the feature-perturbed objective upper-bounds the input-perturbed one under kernel-distance constraints (Proposition 1), and derives generalization bounds showing that δ can be chosen without knowledge of σ/R, yielding near-optimal rates comparable to kernel ridge regression with a cross-validated parameter. The framework extends naturally to multiple kernel learning, where the perturbation set becomes the intersection of RKHS balls across D kernels.

## Results

On the Abalone dataset, feature-perturbed adversarial kernel training (Adv Kern) with δ ∝ n^{-1/2} achieves R² = 0.57 on clean data, matching cross-validated kernel ridge regression. Under adversarial attacks, the method substantially outperforms both baselines: at ℓ₂ ≤ 0.1 it achieves R² = 0.39 vs. 0.26 (KRR) and 0.27 (input-space adversarial training), and at ℓ∞ ≤ 0.1 it achieves R² = 0.13 vs. −0.16 (KRR) and −0.14 (input-space adversarial training). With a fixed feature-space radius ‖d‖_H ≤ 0.1, the method further improves to R² = 0.30 under ℓ∞ ≤ 0.1 attacks, demonstrating a clear robustness-accuracy trade-off. On clean-data benchmarks (Figure 2), adversarial kernel training matches or slightly exceeds KRR with cross-validation across multiple datasets while requiring only one hyperparameter (γ) instead of two.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two baselines: (1) kernel ridge regression with cross-validated regularization (Schölkopf & Smola, 2002), and (2) input-space adversarial training via PGD (Madry et al., 2018). On clean data, all three methods achieve nearly identical R² ≈ 0.57 on Abalone, and adversarial kernel training matches KRR-CV across multiple benchmark datasets (Figure 2) while requiring only one hyperparameter (kernel bandwidth γ) versus two (γ and λ). Under adversarial attacks, the feature-perturbed method clearly wins: at ℓ₂ ≤ 0.1 it achieves R² = 0.39, outperforming KRR (0.26) by 13 points and input-space adversarial training (0.27) by 12 points. The gap widens further at ℓ∞ ≤ 0.1, where the proposed method (R² = 0.13) avoids the negative R² scores of both baselines (−0.16 and −0.14). Notably, even though the proposed method is trained with feature-space perturbations rather than input-space perturbations, it outperforms the input-space adversarial training baseline under the same test-time threat model, suggesting the feature-space formulation offers more favorable optimization properties.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on adversarial training in linear models (Ribeiro et al., 2023; Xie & Huo, 2024) to the non-parametric RKHS setting, and adapts the iterative reweighted least-squares solver from Ribeiro et al. (2025) to kernel regression. The key conceptual departure is shifting perturbations from input space to feature space, which transforms the intractable inner maximization into a closed-form expression and reveals a direct structural link to kernel ridge regression. The multiple kernel learning extension draws on the SimpleMKL framework (Rakotomamonjy et al., 2008) but reformulates it under adversarial feature-space perturbations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The feature-space formulation provides a principled, hyperparameter-light alternative to kernel ridge regression that automatically adapts to noise level and function smoothness, which is practically valuable for kernel methods where cross-validation is expensive. The generalization bounds in the fixed-design setting are clean but the random-design and mispecified cases remain open, as does the extension to classification and infinitely wide networks (NTK). Empirical validation is limited to tabular regression benchmarks, leaving open whether the approach transfers to high-dimensional or image-based settings where adversarial training is most commonly needed.
