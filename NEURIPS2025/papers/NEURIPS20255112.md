## Problem

Fine-tuning discrete diffusion models with reinforcement learning is challenging because sampling from categorical distributions is non-differentiable, making standard gradient-based reward optimization inapplicable. Existing approaches either require differentiable rewards (Gumbel-Softmax), suffer from scalability issues (classifier guidance), or are restricted to specific model architectures (masked diffusion only, conditional generation only). The paper addresses the gap of providing a unified, theoretically grounded policy gradient framework for discrete diffusion that works with arbitrary non-differentiable rewards in both conditional and unconditional settings.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained discrete diffusion model parameterized by a concrete score network s_θ over a finite state space X = {α_1,…,α_m}^n (e.g., 200bp DNA sequences over 4 bases, or text token sequences), a reward function R: X → R that need not be differentiable, and optionally a conditioning dataset D. The concrete score s_θ(x,t)_y is only evaluated for states y at Hamming distance one from x, yielding O(mn) ratios rather than O(m^{2n}). The output is a fine-tuned score network whose denoising process q_t^θ generates sequences that maximize the reward while staying close to the pretrained distribution, evaluated via metrics like predicted enhancer activity (DNA) or LLM-judged response quality (text).

## Method

SEPO derives a discrete REINFORCE estimator (Theorem 3.1) that expresses the policy gradient ∇_θ ℓ_t(θ) entirely in terms of the concrete score s_θ, bypassing the need for differentiable rewards. The key practical challenge is estimating the marginal q_t^θ(y) appearing in the inner sum; SEPO uses self-normalized importance sampling with a Bayes-optimal proposal q_t^θ(·|y), yielding a closed-form harmonic-mean estimator with O(1/M) variance. The outer expectation is reweighted via importance sampling from an old policy q_t^{θ_old}, and the ratio q_t^θ(x)/q_t^{θ_old}(x) is expressed through concrete score ratios, enabling a clipped-ratio loss (PPO or GRPO variant) that enforces a trust region. An optional gradient-flow sampling step (corrector CTMC with rate Q_t + Q̄_t^θ) improves sample quality by reaching the target distribution in infinite-time horizon. The paper proves an O(1/√S) convergence bound on the average gradient norm under boundedness and KL-Lipschitz assumptions.

## Results

On the DNA sequence modeling task (Gosai dataset, 200 bp regulatory sequences), SEPO achieves a median Pred-Activity of 7.55 and 99.5% ATAC-Acc, while SEPO with gradient flow (GF) reaches 7.64 Pred-Activity and 99.9% ATAC-Acc — the highest values across all methods. SEPO also exhibits the lowest run-to-run variance (±0.01 on Pred-Activity, ±0.2 on ATAC-Acc over 3 seeds). On the language modeling task, SEDD-SEPO-1024 achieves 75.8% favorable judgments from a GPT-3.5 Turbo judge at 512 denoising steps, outperforming the pretrained SEDD Vanilla model (74.5% at 128 steps).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SEPO outperforms all RL-based fine-tuning baselines on the two primary functional metrics: it beats DRAKES (Wang et al., 2025) by +1.94 on Pred-Activity (7.55 vs. 5.61) and +7.0 pp on ATAC-Acc (99.5% vs. 92.5%), and beats GLID²E (Cao et al., 2025) by +0.20 on Pred-Activity (7.55 vs. 7.35) and +8.9 pp on ATAC-Acc (99.5% vs. 90.6%). Against inference-time guidance methods, SEPO dominates: it surpasses CFG (Ho & Salimans, 2021) by +2.51 on Pred-Activity and +7.4 pp on ATAC-Acc, and far exceeds SMC/TDS (Wu et al., 2023) by +3.40 on Pred-Activity and +59.6 pp on ATAC-Acc. Classifier guidance (CG, Nisonoff et al., 2025) performs worst among fine-tuned methods (Pred-Activity 3.30, ATAC-Acc 0.0%), confirming its limitations for discrete diffusion. The one metric where SEPO trails is 3-mer Corr (0.500 vs. 0.887 for DRAKES and 0.876 for CFG), which the authors attribute to 3-mer frequency capturing low-level distributional similarity rather than functional relevance; SEPO with GF narrows this gap to 0.638. On language modeling, both SEPO variants outperform the pretrained SEDD Vanilla model across all denoising step counts, with SEDD-SEPO-1024 achieving the best overall score of 75.8% favorable judgments.

## Relation to Prior Work

SEPO sits at the intersection of two active research threads: discrete diffusion modeling (concrete score / score entropy framework of Lou et al. 2024, CTMC-based denoising of Campbell et al. 2022) and policy gradient RL fine-tuning (PPO, GRPO, TRPO). For continuous diffusion, RL-based fine-tuning is well-studied (Black et al. 2024, Marion et al. 2024), but extending these methods to discrete state spaces is non-trivial because categorical sampling is non-differentiable. Prior discrete-diffusion fine-tuning work either requires differentiable rewards (DRAKES via Gumbel-Softmax, Wang et al. 2025), is restricted to masked diffusion architectures (d1, Zhao et al. 2025), or relies on reward shaping (GLIDE, Cao et al. 2025). SEPO departs from all of these by providing a unified policy gradient formulation in the concrete score framework that handles arbitrary non-differentiable rewards and supports both conditional and unconditional generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first theoretically grounded policy gradient algorithm for discrete diffusion that does not require differentiable rewards, which is important because many practical reward signals (e.g., biological activity scores, human preference judgments) are inherently non-differentiable. The self-normalized importance sampling estimator for marginal probabilities and the gradient flow interpretation of CTMC sampling are reusable technical contributions. However, the method's inner SNIS step adds per-sample computational overhead, and experiments are limited to relatively small models (SEDD Medium, 200 bp DNA sequences), leaving open whether the approach scales to large language models or long sequences. The lower 3-mer correlation compared to guidance-based baselines also raises questions about distributional fidelity under aggressive reward optimization.
