## Problem

Existing vision-language models exhibit significant positional bias in interleaved multi-image reasoning: simply permuting the order of input images can flip correct answers to incorrect ones, because models rely on superficial positional cues rather than semantic content. While recent RL-based approaches (e.g., GRPO variants) have improved single-image spatial reasoning, they remain limited to single-image contexts and fail to generalize to multi-image positional reasoning where cross-image relationships are critical.

## Input / Output

The model takes interleaved sequences of RGB images (represented via <image> placeholder tokens) and a text query as input, and produces a chain-of-thought reasoning process enclosed in think tags followed by a final answer in \boxed{} format. For multi-image inputs, the method generates permuted variants of the image sequence at training time, with GPT-4o verifying semantic invariance and reindexing answer options (e.g., swapping 'A' and 'B') when the permutation changes which image occupies which position. Training uses 22K multi-image examples from Mantis-Instruct and 36K single-image math examples from K12, with 12 rollouts per input (6 per permutation order).

## Method

PeRL extends GRPO by augmenting each training sample with randomly permuted image sequences (controlled by a linearly decaying probability α_t) and correspondingly rephrased text/answers, then merging all permutation groups into a single advantage computation so that the baseline and variance are estimated across the full set of permuted rollouts. A multi-stage data preprocessing pipeline (rule-based filtering, GPT-4o format rephrasing, rollout-based difficulty filtering, and semantic variation checking) curates the training set to balance difficulty distribution, reducing the mean accuracy score from 0.78 to 0.38. Unlike NoisyRollout, which conditions the policy update solely on the original images, PeRL computes the policy loss for each rollout group on its augmented permuted input, preventing model collapse. A theoretical analysis proves by induction that PeRL's success probabilities are less sensitive to input permutations than naive GRPO's, formalizing the reduction in positional bias.

## Results

Qwen-PeRL (7B) achieves state-of-the-art performance on multi-image benchmarks, scoring 76.39 on Mantis-Eval, 58.53 on BLINK, 54.23 on MMIU, 43.38 on Remi, and 25.68 on MV-MATH, surpassing the strongest prior methods by 3–7 points. On single-image math reasoning, it scores 73.00 on MathVista, 49.56 on MathVerse, and 28.26 on MathVision, remaining competitive with dedicated RL math models. The overall average across all eight benchmarks is 51.13, a +3.23 improvement over the Qwen2.5-VL-7B base model (47.90). Notably, the model was trained only on multi-image natural scene understanding and single-image math data, yet generalizes to out-of-distribution multi-image math reasoning (Remi, MV-MATH).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest RL baselines, Qwen-PeRL wins decisively on all five multi-image benchmarks: it outperforms Noisy-K12 by +3.24 on Mantis-Eval (76.39 vs 73.15), +4.30 on BLINK (58.53 vs 54.23), +2.10 on MMIU (54.23 vs 52.13), +2.11 on Remi (43.38 vs 41.27), and +0.69 on MV-MATH (25.68 vs 24.99). On single-image math, PeRL ties MM-Eureka on MathVista (73.00) but trails Noisy-K12 on MathVerse (49.56 vs 52.80) and MathVision (28.26 vs 28.90), indicating a slight trade-off where permutation augmentation prioritizes spatial/positional generalization over pure math reasoning. Compared to instruction-tuned multi-image models like Migician-630K and InternVL2.5, PeRL leads by 7–15 points on multi-image benchmarks, demonstrating that RL with permutation augmentation substantially outperforms supervised fine-tuning for cross-image reasoning. Against R1-OneVision, PeRL gains +4.63 on Mantis-Eval and a striking +26.15 on BLINK, while R1-OneVision retains a small edge on MathVision (29.90 vs 28.26). Overall, PeRL achieves the best average (51.13) among all 7B models, confirming that the permutation-enhanced GRPO strategy yields the most balanced multi-image and single-image performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GRPO-based reinforcement learning framework (originally from DeepSeekMath) to the interleaved multi-image setting, building on the recent wave of multimodal RL work (MM-Eureka, NoisyRollout, R1-VL, R1-OneVision) that has so far been confined to single-image reasoning. It departs from prior interleaved VLM approaches (Mantis, LLaVA-Interleave, Migician) that rely on supervised instruction tuning, and from the training-free positional bias mitigation of SoFA [45], by instead addressing positional coreference through permutation-augmented RL training with a corresponding theoretical guarantee. The rollout filtering mechanism for difficulty balancing is a practical addition that complements the permutation strategy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Multi-image positional reasoning remains a clear weakness of current VLMs, and this paper provides a simple, plug-and-play RL recipe (permutation + difficulty filtering) that closes a meaningful gap on multi-image benchmarks while preserving single-image performance. The theoretical analysis showing that permutation GRPO provably reduces the spread of success probabilities across image orderings gives the method a principled grounding beyond empirical observation. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger models or more than two images, whether adaptive permutation schedules (the authors mention this as future work) can further improve efficiency, and whether the gains hold under different base architectures. The lack of error bars and the single 7B-scale evaluation limit confidence in the generality of the results.
