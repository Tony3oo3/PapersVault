## Problem

3D registration in serial section EM must correct nonlinear distortions introduced by mechanical sectioning without erasing natural biological morphological deformations between slices. Existing methods either prioritize pixel-level similarity (destroying biologically meaningful changes) or operate with local receptive fields that accumulate errors over long sequences. The paper reframes registration as a continuous manifold trajectory optimization problem, exploiting the observation that nonlinear distortion manifests as abrupt curvature mutations while natural tissue evolution is smooth.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254263/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a stack of N 2D EM slices of size H×W (1184×1184 for synthetic OpenOrganelle datasets, 1024×1024 for real FemFlyBrain data) and produces per-slice 2D deformation fields (N×H×W×2) that warp each slice to correct axial misalignments. Training is fully unsupervised, requiring no ground-truth correspondences; for long real-world sequences, a sliding-window strategy with ~100-slice receptive fields is used to handle volumes of 700+ slices. The trajectory tracking network outputs subpixel displacement vectors between adjacent slices, which are accumulated into continuous 2D trajectories per pixel before being inverted back to the discrete pixel grid via bilinear splatting.

## Method

NeuroTrajReg decomposes registration into three stages: (1) a 2D U-Net with residual multi-kernel fusion estimates inter-slice displacement fields, which are propagated into per-pixel trajectories via a transport equation with bilinear interpolation at subpixel positions; (2) trajectory smoothing is solved by an adaptive blend of Gauss-Seidel iteration (fast convergence on the linear Laplacian-smoothing system) and a Neural ODE with a 3D U-Net parameterizing the velocity field f_θ (capturing residual nonlinear deviations), where the mixing weight α evolves via a sigmoid of the Gauss-Seidel convergence progress, inspired by Universal Differential Equations; (3) the optimized continuous trajectories are inverted to pixel-grid displacements through bilinear splatting. The training loss combines NCC data fidelity, a Laplacian smoothness term penalizing acceleration discontinuities (filtering biologically implausible curvature mutations), and a spatial consistency term preserving local topology between neighboring pixel trajectories.

## Results

On six synthetic OpenOrganelle datasets (mouse heart, kidney, liver, pancreas, skin), NeuroTrajReg achieves the highest SSIM across all tissues (0.84–0.87) and the highest MI (1.47–1.88), outperforming the closest competitor GaussReg by 0.03–0.07 in SSIM. NCC is essentially saturated at 0.99 for the top methods. On the real-world FemFlyBrain dataset, NeuroTrajReg corrects nonlinear distortions while better preserving local structural textures compared to GaussReg, and maintains stable SSIM over ~1000 slices without the error accumulation seen in SEMLeSS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NeuroTrajReg outperforms all five baselines on SSIM across all six tissue types, with the largest margins over EMReg (0.32–0.44 SSIM gap) and SEMLeSS (0.25–0.35 gap). The closest competitor is GaussReg (the authors' prior supervised method), which NeuroTrajReg surpasses by 0.03–0.07 in SSIM (e.g., 0.87 vs. 0.83 on Heart, 0.85 vs. 0.78 on Kidney) and by 0.04–0.12 in MI, while matching its NCC of 0.99. TrakEM2 and EFSR achieve competitive NCC (~0.97–0.98) but lag substantially in SSIM (0.57–0.67) and MI, indicating they over-smooth or fail to correct nonlinear distortions. On the real-world FemFlyBrain dataset, GaussReg reduces distortions but erroneously removes structural textures, whereas NeuroTrajReg preserves local details. In the long-sequence error accumulation test (~1000 slices), NeuroTrajReg maintains stable SSIM while SEMLeSS degrades significantly and GaussReg shows higher variance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the ssEM 3D registration lineage (TrakEM2, SEAMLeSS, and the authors' own GaussReg) by reframing the problem through a trajectory-optimization lens borrowed from autonomous driving reference-line smoothing. The key conceptual departure is treating each pixel as a particle whose axial displacement forms a continuous trajectory, then applying Laplacian-based curvature smoothing (inspired by path planning) combined with Neural ODEs for nonlinear correction. The hybrid Gauss-Seidel + Neural ODE solver draws on Universal Differential Equations to blend explicit iterative convergence with implicit dynamic modeling, a combination not previously applied to image registration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The trajectory-optimization framing provides physical interpretability for distinguishing biologically meaningful deformation from technical distortion—a long-standing ambiguity in ssEM registration that purely pixel-similarity methods struggle with. However, the performance gains over the authors' own GaussReg baseline are modest (SSIM ~0.80→0.87), and the method is validated on a limited set of tissue types with synthetic distortions. Open questions include whether the biophysical prior (Laplacian smoothness) generalizes to tissues with genuinely abrupt structural changes, how the method scales to the TB-scale datasets mentioned in the introduction, and whether the adaptive Gauss-Seidel/Neural ODE blending can be replaced by a more principled formulation.
