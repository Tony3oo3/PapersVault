## Problem

Existing meta-learning-based few-shot relational learning methods suffer from two gaps: they learn relation meta-knowledge in isolation per task, ignoring shared relational patterns across semantically related tasks, and they rely on a single shared initialization for adaptation, lacking the flexibility to capture task-specific local contexts from only a few support triplets. MoEMeta addresses how to disentangle globally shared relational knowledge from task-specific adaptation to achieve both effective generalization and rapid adaptation under few-shot settings.

## Input / Output

Given a novel relation r with K support triplets (typically K=1,3,5) and query triplets with head entities, the method consumes entity embeddings (dimension 100 for Nell-One/FB15K-One, 50 for Wiki-One) initialized from TransE-pretrained weights, along with one-hop neighborhood tuples (capped at 50 neighbors) for aggregation. It outputs a plausibility score for each candidate tail entity in the provided candidate set, from which a ranking is derived; the intermediate relation-meta embedding (dimension d) serves as the task-adapted relational representation used in the scoring function.

## Method

MoEMeta combines three components under a MAML-style two-stage training loop. First, an attentive neighbor aggregator enriches head and tail entity embeddings by gating one-hop neighbor tuple embeddings through a sigmoid attention mechanism. Second, a sparsely-gated mixture-of-experts (M=32 experts, top-N=5 selected per triplet) computes relation representations from augmented entity pairs, which are averaged over the support set to form the relation-meta; the gating network dynamically routes each triplet to specialized experts, enabling the model to learn globally shared relational prototypes across tasks. Third, a task-tailored local adaptation mechanism introduces three randomly initialized projection vectors (p_h, p_r, p_t) that modulate entity and relation-meta embeddings via scalar alignment scores, inspired by TransD's per-entity projections but applied at the task level to avoid overparametrization. During meta-training, the inner loop updates the local projection vectors and relation-meta on the support set, while the outer loop backpropagates the query loss to update all global parameters (neighbor aggregation weights, expert networks, and gating network).

## Results

On Nell-One (1-shot), MoEMeta achieves MRR 0.322, Hits@1 0.228, Hits@5 0.429, and Hits@10 0.501, surpassing the second-best method HiRe by 11.8% MRR and 23.9% Hits@1. On Wiki-One (5-shot), MoEMeta reaches MRR 0.377 and Hits@1 0.324. On FB15K-One (3-shot), MoEMeta attains MRR 0.423, Hits@1 0.302, and Hits@10 0.651, outperforming the runner-up RelAdapter by 4.4% MRR and 13.2% Hits@5. All improvements over the second-best method are statistically significant at the 0.05 level (paired t-test).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MoEMeta outperforms all 12 baselines across every dataset and shot setting. Against conventional KG embedding methods (TransE, TransD, DistMult, ComplEx), MoEMeta leads by large margins—e.g., on Nell-One 1-shot, MoEMeta's MRR of 0.322 exceeds TransE (0.105) by over 3× and TransD (0.159) by 102%. Among metric-learning FSRL baselines, MoEMeta surpasses GMatching (0.185 MRR), FSKGC (0.184), and FAAN (0.279) on Nell-One 1-shot/5-shot. Among meta-learning competitors, MoEMeta beats MetaR-I (0.250), MetaR-P (0.164), MetaP (0.232), GANA (0.236), and HiRe (0.288) on Nell-One 1-shot MRR, with the largest gap over HiRe at +11.8% MRR and +23.9% Hits@1. On FB15K-One 3-shot, MoEMeta (MRR 0.423) edges out RelAdapter (0.405) by 4.4% MRR and GANA (0.388) by 9.0% MRR, while achieving a notable 13.2% gain in Hits@5 over RelAdapter (0.651 vs. 0.575). On Wiki-One, MoEMeta matches or slightly exceeds HiRe (0.325 vs. 0.322 MRR 1-shot; 0.377 vs. 0.371 MRR 5-shot) and clearly surpasses RelAdapter (0.325 vs. 0.247 MRR 1-shot). All reported improvements over the second-best method are statistically significant at the 0.05 level.

## Relation to Prior Work

MoEMeta extends the MAML-based meta-learning framework for few-shot relational learning (FSRL) established by MetaR, GANA, HiRe, and RelAdapter, but departs from them in two key ways. First, it introduces a sparsely-gated mixture-of-experts model to learn globally shared relational prototypes across tasks, addressing the i.i.d. task assumption that prior methods implicitly rely on. Second, it adds a lightweight task-tailored adaptation mechanism using three projection vectors (inspired by TransD's per-entity/per-relation projections) to capture local, task-specific contexts at the embedding level, rather than relying solely on a shared initialization for adaptation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and addresses two concrete limitations in meta-learning-based FSRL—neglecting cross-task relational commonalities and insufficient task-specific adaptation—that are well-motivated by the heterogeneous, non-i.i.d. nature of KG relations. The MoE-as-meta-learner design is a novel architectural choice for this domain and could inform future work on structured meta-learning beyond KGs. However, the method is evaluated on only three relatively small benchmarks (67–183 tasks), and the added MoE overhead (~15% more training time) may become more significant at scale. Open questions include whether the relational-prototype concept generalizes to larger, industrial-scale KGs and whether the sparsity level (top-N of M experts) can be learned adaptively per task.
