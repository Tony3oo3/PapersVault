## Problem

Existing 360° monocular depth estimation methods divide equirectangular images into perspective patches and process them independently, causing depth scale drift and visible seams across patches. Training-based approaches require expensive 360° depth annotations and have limited zero-shot generalization, while training-free methods still rely on costly post-processing or additional 360° depth models. The paper asks whether the temporal consistency inherent in video depth estimation (VDE) models can be repurposed to enforce spatial consistency across perspective patches without any 360°-specific training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251633/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 360° equirectangular projection (ERP) image as input, supporting resolutions up to 4K (2048×4096). Internally, the ERP image is decomposed into a sequence of square perspective frames (e.g., 252×252 to 756×756) with a configurable field-of-view (default 90°), which are fed to a frozen VDE model as a video. The output is a complete 360° depth map in ERP format, produced by re-projecting per-frame depth predictions back onto the spherical surface and blending overlapping regions via mean or Poisson blending.

## Method

ST²360D is a training-free pipeline that represents a 360° image as a sequence of perspective frames mimicking VR viewpoint adjustments, then exploits the temporal consistency of a VDE model (e.g., Video Depth Anything) to ensure spatial consistency across patches. Two tailored strategies align the frame sequence with VDE models: (1) Spherical-Uniform Sampling (SUS) distributes viewpoints using vertices of a subdivided icosahedron to avoid oversampling structurally sparse polar regions; (2) Latitude-Guided Scanning (LGS) organizes frames starting from equatorial viewpoints with rich structural detail and progressively traverses toward higher latitudes, selecting the next viewpoint from spherical neighbors to maintain coherent transitions. The pipeline re-projects the resulting video depth maps onto the ERP plane and merges them using mean blending (or optionally Poisson blending for smoother seams), requiring no 360°-specific training data or additional panoramic depth models.

## Results

On the high-resolution Replica360-2K and Replica360-4K datasets, ST²360D with VDA-Large and Poisson blending achieves RMSE of 0.0144 and AbsRel of 0.0503/0.0492 respectively, outperforming all baselines including PanDA-Large (RMSE 0.0187/0.0183) and 360MonoDepth (RMSE 0.0706/0.0611). On Matterport3D-2K, ST²360D (VDA-Large, Poisson) achieves RMSE 0.4460 and AbsRel 0.1264, competitive with data-specific methods like SliceNet (RMSE 0.4463) but slightly behind PanDA-Large (RMSE 0.4658) and Peng et al. (RMSE 0.4791). On the lower-resolution Matterport3D and Stanford2D3D benchmarks, ST²360D (VDA-Large) achieves AbsRel of 0.1153 and 0.1005 respectively, matching or exceeding PanDA-Large (0.1122/0.1026) without any 360° depth training annotations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ST²360D (VDA-Large, Poisson) decisively outperforms 360MonoDepth across all three high-resolution datasets, reducing RMSE by 42–76% (e.g., 0.0144 vs. 0.0706 on Replica360-2K), and also surpasses the DAv2-upgraded variant of 360MonoDepth. Against PanDA-Large, which is fine-tuned on synthetic 360° depth data, ST²360D wins on Replica360-2K (RMSE 0.0144 vs. 0.0187) and Replica360-4K (0.0144 vs. 0.0183) but trails slightly on Matterport3D-2K (0.4460 vs. 0.4658), where PanDA benefits from dataset-specific training. Compared to Peng et al., which relies on a pre-trained 360° depth estimator as reference, ST²360D achieves substantially better results on Replica360-2K (RMSE 0.0144 vs. 0.0272) and Replica360-4K (0.0144 vs. 0.0332) but is slightly behind on Matterport3D-2K (0.4460 vs. 0.4791). On the lower-resolution Matterport3D and Stanford2D3D benchmarks, ST²360D (VDA-Large) matches or exceeds PanDA-Large on most metrics (AbsRel 0.1153 vs. 0.1122 on Matterport3D; 0.1005 vs. 0.1026 on Stanford2D3D) while being entirely training-free. The method also outperforms perspective-only baselines DAv2 and Marigold by large margins (e.g., AbsRel 0.1153 vs. 0.1962/0.2103 on Matterport3D), confirming that the temporal-to-spatial consistency transfer is the key differentiator.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Video Depth Anything (VDA) and the broader VDE literature, but introduces a conceptual reframing: it treats a single 360° ERP image as a sequence of perspective video frames, thereby converting the spatial consistency problem across patches into a temporal consistency problem that VDE models are natively designed to solve. This departs from prior training-free 360° depth methods (360MonoDepth, Peng et al.) that process perspective patches independently and rely on post-hoc alignment, and from training-based methods (PanDA, HRDFuse) that require 360° depth annotations. The two tailored strategies—spherical-uniform sampling via icosahedron vertices and latitude-guided scanning—are novel contributions specifically designed to make the synthetic video sequence compatible with VDE models' reliance on informative initial frames and coherent temporal transitions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free pipeline achieves competitive or superior results to methods requiring 360° depth annotations, while supporting resolutions up to 4K (2048×4096), which is practically important for VR and high-fidelity scene understanding. The ablation showing 14–34% AbsRel improvement from temporal consistency alone (vs. per-frame processing) validates the spatial-to-temporal reframing as a general principle. The pipeline is model-agnostic (demonstrated with VDA, ChronoDepth, DepthCrafter, DepthAnyVideo), suggesting the approach could extend to other 360° tasks beyond depth estimation (e.g., semantic segmentation) as the authors note. A key limitation is that minor seams persist in texture-less regions, and the method inherits the scale ambiguity inherent in monocular depth estimation.
