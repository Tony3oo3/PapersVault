## Problem

Practical RL deployment spans online, offline, and offline-to-online settings, yet existing algorithms are specialized to one setting each: online methods (PPO, SAC) fail on offline data due to distribution shift, offline methods (CQL, IVR) are overly conservative with online access, and offline-to-online methods (Cal-QL, AWAC) require ad-hoc design choices. The paper asks whether a single model-free framework can unify all three settings without changing the learning objective while maintaining sample efficiency and scalability.

## Input / Output

The method consumes state observations and a replay buffer (static for offline, growing for online/offline-to-online) and produces a policy parameterized as either a Gaussian or a diffusion/flow-matching model, along with Q and V value functions. A key structural detail is that all value learning uses only actions sampled from a reference policy μ (the dataset behavior policy in offline, a soft-updated target policy in online), making the scheme fully in-sample and eliminating the need to evaluate Q at out-of-distribution actions. The reference policy μ is the single knob that switches the algorithm between settings: π_D for offline, a periodically updated π̄ for online, and a transition from π_D to π̄ for offline-to-online.

## Method

Uni-RL generalizes Implicit Value Regularization (IVR) by replacing the fixed dataset behavior constraint with a constraint relative to a reference policy μ, yielding a unified f-divergence-regularized value learning objective solvable in-sample via duality. In the online setting, an iteratively soft-updated reference policy π̄ (π̄ ← λπ + (1−λ)π̄) provides an implicit trust-region update through the value function while keeping the algorithm off-policy, solving the over-conservatism of directly applying IVR to a growing replay buffer. For policy extraction, the paper introduces In-sample Policy Gradient (InPG), which learns new BC weights w* by solving a constrained QP that projects the MaxQ policy-gradient direction onto the weighted-BC gradient direction, using only in-sample actions from μ. This yields a weighted-BC-style loss that is mode-seeking (unlike standard weighted BC) while avoiding the out-of-distribution Q queries of MaxQ+BC. Three theorems provide guarantees: the f-divergence between the extracted policy and μ is bounded by the learned weight range, there exists a weight range ensuring J(π_InPG*) ≥ J(π_IVR*), and direct policy gradient has no such improvement guarantee over the reference policy.

## Results

On the D4RL offline RL benchmark (15 datasets), Uni-RL with a Diffusion policy achieves the best or near-best score on 13/15 tasks, including hopper-medium (101.3), walker2d-medium (92.3), antmaze-large-diverse (69.0), and antmaze-medium-play (88.5). The Gaussian-policy variant already matches or outperforms most baselines, with the diffusion variant providing additional gains of 5–10 points on multi-modal datasets (e.g., antmaze-large-play: 68.6 vs. 59.9 for Gaussian). In online RL across 8 continuous control environments, Uni-RL achieves the highest sample efficiency, surpassing both off-policy methods (TD3, SAC) and trust-region on-policy methods (PPO, MPO), particularly on challenging DMControl tasks like finger-turn-hard and quadruped-walk.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Uni-RL (Diffusion) outperforms all baselines on 12/15 D4RL datasets, with the largest margins over conservative methods: CQL (e.g., antmaze-large-play: 68.6 vs. 38.4) and IQL (e.g., hopper-medium: 101.3 vs. 66.3). Against the closest diffusion-based competitor Diffusion-QL, Uni-RL wins on 13/15 tasks (e.g., antmaze-large-play: 68.6 vs. 46.4; antmaze-medium-play: 88.5 vs. 76.6), losing only on walker2d-medium-replay (90.8 vs. 95.5). Compared to its direct predecessor IVR, Uni-RL (Gaussian) already improves on 12/15 datasets (e.g., hopper-medium: 99.5 vs. 75.5; antmaze-large-diverse: 60.2 vs. 52.3), with the diffusion variant extending this to 14/15. In online RL, Uni-RL surpasses PPO and MPO (trust-region on-policy) in sample efficiency while also outperforming TD3 and SAC (off-policy without trust-region), confirming that the implicit trust-region update via value regularization provides the best of both worlds. In offline-to-online RL, Uni-RL outperforms constraint-based methods (AWAC, IQL) that suffer from over-conservatism, and also beats Cal-QL and RLPD on difficult tasks (Adroit, AntMaze-large) where offline data is highly suboptimal.

## Relation to Prior Work

Uni-RL directly extends the Implicit Value Regularization (IVR) framework (Xu et al., 2023), which solved offline RL via in-sample value learning under a dataset behavior constraint, by generalizing that constraint to an arbitrary reference policy μ. This single generalization unifies offline RL (μ = behavior policy), online RL (μ = iteratively updated target policy), and offline-to-online RL (μ transitions from behavior to target policy) under one objective. The policy extraction step introduces In-sample Policy Gradient (InPG), which bridges the stability of weighted behavior cloning (mode-covering) with the mode-seeking quality of MaxQ+BC by learning BC weights that project the MaxQ gradient into the BC gradient direction. The implicit trust-region update imposed through value regularization is a conceptual departure from explicit trust-region methods (TRPO, PPO) that are inherently on-policy, enabling off-policy learning with trust-region-like stability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that a single algorithm and codebase can handle all three major RL deployment settings without architectural changes, reducing the engineering burden of maintaining separate pipelines for online, offline, and hybrid scenarios. The scalability to diffusion and flow-matching policies is particularly relevant as generative policies become standard for complex manipulation tasks. Limitations include the model-free restriction (no world model), evaluation limited to continuous control benchmarks (MuJoCo, AntMaze, Adroit, DMControl), and the two hyperparameters (α, λ) still requiring per-environment tuning. Open directions flagged by the authors include model-based extensions and adaptation to LLM+RL settings, where the in-sample learning property could be especially valuable given the cost of environment interaction.
