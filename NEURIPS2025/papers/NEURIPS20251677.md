## Problem

Existing masked autoencoder (MAE) frameworks for point cloud pre-training inject explicit positional embeddings (e.g., patch center coordinates) into the encoder, which leaks geometric information and prevents the model from learning spatial structure purely from data-driven cues. The paper asks how to design a diffusion-based pre-training framework that eliminates this positional shortcut while simultaneously enhancing the modeling of local geometric structures for faithful reconstruction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251677/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D point cloud of 1,024 points, partitions it into 64 patches of 32 points each via FPS and KNN, normalizes each patch by subtracting its center to remove global location bias, and applies a 60% random+block masking ratio. During pre-training, the encoder outputs predicted clean patch centers (3D coordinates) and the decoder outputs reconstructed masked patch coordinates; after pre-training, only the 12-layer transformer encoder is retained and fine-tuned with a task-specific head (classification MLP, segmentation head, or 3DETR detection backbone) to produce class labels, per-point part labels, or 3D bounding boxes.

## Method

Point-MaDi introduces a dual-diffusion pretext task within an MAE encoder-decoder architecture. In the encoder, a center diffusion process adds Gaussian noise to both visible and masked patch centers over 2,000 steps (linear β schedule from 1e-4 to 2e-2), and a 12-layer transformer with shared-parameter self-attention (visible tokens) and cross-attention (masked tokens querying visible context) predicts the clean centers, replacing explicit positional embeddings entirely. In the decoder, a patch diffusion process noises the masked patches and progressively denoises them using a 4-layer transformer conditioned on the encoder's visible latent tokens and the predicted (stop-gradient) masked centers, with reconstruction supervised by L2 Chamfer Distance. The total loss combines MSE for center prediction (weighted γ=0.1) and Chamfer Distance for patch reconstruction, and the stop-gradient on predicted centers prevents the decoder from backpropagating gradients that would disrupt the encoder's center-denoising objective. This design forces the encoder to infer global spatial relationships from partial observations rather than reading them off provided coordinates, while the decoder focuses on fine-grained local geometry recovery.

## Results

On ScanObjectNN, Point-MaDi achieves 95.52% (OBJ-BG), 93.46% (OBJ-ONLY), and 89.52% (PB-T50-RS) overall accuracy, surpassing Point-MAE by 5.50%, 5.17%, and 4.34% respectively. On ModelNet40, it reaches 93.8% (w/o vote) and 94.1% (w/ vote). For part segmentation on ShapeNetPart, Point-MaDi attains 84.8% category mIoU and 86.3% instance mIoU; on S3DIS Area 5 it achieves 71.0% mAcc and 61.2% mIoU; and on ScanNet 3D detection it reaches 43.7% AP50.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary single-modal baseline Point-MAE [31], Point-MaDi wins on all three ScanObjectNN variants by 4.34–5.50% and on ModelNet40 by 0.3–0.6%. Against the closest diffusion-based competitor PointDif [70], Point-MaDi leads by 3.61% on OBJ-BG, 0.17% on OBJ-ONLY, and 1.91% on PB-T50-RS, while matching PointDif's 43.7% AP50 on ScanNet detection. Against the strongest single-modal baseline PCP-MAE [68], Point-MaDi ties on OBJ-BG (95.52%) but trails by 0.86% on OBJ-ONLY and 0.83% on PB-T50-RS. Among cross-modal methods, Point-MaDi outperforms I2P-MAE [67] on OBJ-BG (+1.38%) and OBJ-ONLY (+1.89%) but falls short on PB-T50-RS (−0.59%), and it edges out ReCon [36] on OBJ-BG (+0.34%) while losing on OBJ-ONLY (−0.17%) and PB-T50-RS (−1.11%). On part segmentation (ShapeNetPart) and scene segmentation (S3DIS Area 5), Point-MaDi achieves the best single-modal results, surpassing Point-MAE by +0.6/+0.2 mIoU and +1.1/+0.4 mAcc/mIoU respectively, and outperforming PointDif by +0.4/+0.5 mIoU and +1.5/+1.0 mAcc/mIoU.

## Relation to Prior Work

Point-MaDi sits at the intersection of two recent lines of work: masked autoencoders for point clouds (Point-MAE, Point-BERT) and diffusion-based pre-training (PointDif, DiffPMAE). It departs from Point-MAE by replacing explicit patch-center positional embeddings with a center diffusion process in the encoder, forcing the model to infer spatial layout from data rather than being given it. Compared to PointDif's holistic point-to-point denoising with a separate aggregator, Point-MaDi unifies center prediction and patch reconstruction in a single bi-jective encoder-decoder, applying diffusion to both global centers (encoder) and local patches (decoder) as a coupled dual-diffusion pretext task.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and previously underexploited weakness in MAE-based point cloud pre-training: positional embedding leakage that shortcuts geometric learning. The dual-diffusion design is a clean, well-motivated solution that achieves consistent gains across classification, segmentation, and detection without requiring cross-modal data or additional supervision. Open questions include whether the 2000-step diffusion schedule is necessary at inference time, how the approach scales to much larger or more complex scenes (e.g., outdoor LiDAR), and whether the center-diffusion mechanism generalizes to other irregular-structure modalities beyond point clouds.
