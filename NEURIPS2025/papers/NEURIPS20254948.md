## Problem

Predicting Chern numbers of multiband topological insulators (N > 1 filled bands) with neural networks has been intractable because the relevant U(N) gauge symmetry acts independently at each lattice site, exponentially enlarging the symmetry group beyond what standard equivariant or non-equivariant architectures can handle. While gauge equivariant networks (LGE-CNNs) respect this symmetry by construction, their bilinear layers cause severe training instabilities—exploding or vanishing gradients—that make deep training impossible for larger gauge groups.

## Input / Output

The model consumes Wilson loops W_{i,j} ∈ U(N) (N×N complex unitary matrices) defined on a 2D rectangular lattice with periodic boundary conditions, and optionally link variables U^x, U^y on lattice edges for the convolutional variant. The output is a single scalar: the discrete Chern number C̃ = Σ_{(i,j)} Im Tr log W_{i,j}, an integer topological invariant. For the 4D extension, each site carries C(4,2)=6 Wilson loops aligned with different plane orientations, and the output is a non-integer higher-order Chern number evaluated via MAE rather than rounding accuracy.

## Method

The primary architecture, GEBLNet, is a purely local gauge equivariant network stacking blocks of three layers: GEBL (bilinear mixing of Wilson loops per site, capturing higher-order flux terms), GEAct (equivariant nonlinearity σ(Tr W)·W), and a novel TrNorm layer that normalizes each channel by |mean_γ{Tr W_k^γ}|—a gauge-invariant scalar—thereby preventing the variance explosion across stacked bilinear layers that previously made deep training unstable. Features remain equivariant (U(N)-valued tensors) through all local layers, are rendered gauge invariant only at the final Trace layer, then passed through a dense layer and summed over sites to yield the scalar prediction. Training combines a global L1 loss on the Chern number with a standard-deviation loss that prevents the network from collapsing to uniform zero outputs, which is critical when training exclusively on topologically trivial (C=0) samples. The paper proves a universal approximation theorem showing GEBLNet is dense in L²_class(U(N)), and demonstrates that a model trained only on trivial samples generalizes to non-trivial Chern numbers with ~94% accuracy at N=4 bands, extending to N=7 bands and 4D grids.

## Results

GEBLNet achieves 95.9% accuracy on a 5×5 grid with 4 filled bands, maintaining high accuracy up to 7 bands (91.7%) before degrading at 8 bands (52.5%). Training exclusively on topologically trivial samples (Chern number = 0) still yields ~94.1% accuracy on non-trivial test data, demonstrating strong generalization. For 4D second-order Chern numbers on a 3⁴ grid with 3 bands, the model achieves an MAE of approximately 0.25, well within rounding error. The model generalizes to unseen larger grid sizes with only a mild linear decrease in accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares GEBLNet against three baselines: GEConvNet (gauge equivariant convolutional network), TrMLP (a non-equivariant MLP baseline), and DeepSpec (a gauge-invariant spectral model). GEBLNet outperforms all baselines in both accuracy and robustness across multiple random seeds. DeepSpec, which restricts inputs to gauge-invariant spectral features, drops to 44.7% accuracy at N=2 bands and 7.8% at N=4, confirming that operating solely on invariants is insufficient for the multi-band regime. GEConvNet with positive kernel sizes struggles to learn correct results, likely due to redundant information from the convolutional interactions, while its degenerate local variant (kernel size 0) performs better but remains less robust than GEBLNet. TrMLP is so unstable that only 3 out of many runs converge successfully. Previous approaches in the literature [13, 14, 35] could only handle the U(1) case (N=1 band), whereas GEBLNet extends this to N≥7 filled bands.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the Lattice Gauge Equivariant Convolutional Neural Networks (LGE-CNNs) of Favoni et al. [15], originally developed for lattice QCD, to the problem of predicting topological invariants of multiband insulators. Prior work on learning Chern numbers [13, 14, 35] was restricted to the U(1) single-band case, while the DeepSpec baseline [24] attempted multi-band prediction via gauge-invariant spectral features but failed as band number increased. The key departure here is the introduction of a novel gauge-equivariant normalization layer (TrNorm) that resolves the training instabilities plaguing LGE-CNNs, combined with a universal approximation theorem establishing the expressivity of the resulting architecture for U(N) class functions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that gauge equivariant networks can learn multi-band (U(N), N≥7) Chern numbers, and the first to extend to 4D topological invariants, opening a path toward ML-based classification of richer topological phases beyond the single-band regime. The TrNorm layer is a broadly useful technique for stabilizing deep gauge-equivariant architectures, and the UAT provides theoretical justification for the model's expressivity. Limitations include reliance on synthetic data, restriction to Chern numbers (not other invariants like Z2 indices), and a sharp accuracy drop at 8 bands, suggesting the approach may not scale indefinitely without further architectural changes.
