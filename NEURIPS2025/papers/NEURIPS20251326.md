## Problem

Existing vision state space models (Vim, VMamba, PlainMamba, LocalMamba) rely on manually designed, data-independent scanning patterns (sweeping, continuous, or local) to flatten 2D images into 1D sequences for Mamba processing. These fixed strategies either disrupt spatial adjacency of nearby patches or sacrifice global modeling capacity, and cannot adapt their scanning focus to the content of each input image. The paper asks whether a data-driven, input-adaptive scanning mechanism can simultaneously preserve local spatial structure and maintain global receptive field with linear complexity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251326/cropped_page1_idx4.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes standard RGB images (224×224 for classification, 1280×800 for detection, 512×2048 for segmentation) and produces task-specific outputs: 1000-way class labels, bounding boxes with instance masks, or pixel-level semantic masks. A key architectural detail is that the input image is first converted to a 2D feature map via a stem of four overlapping 3×3 convolutions (reducing resolution by 4×), and the Dynamic Adaptive Scan operates on this 2D feature map rather than on a pre-flattened 1D sequence, with the scanning order determined per-image by learned offsets.

## Method

The core contribution is Dynamic Adaptive Scan (DAS), which replaces fixed scanning patterns with a data-driven mechanism. An Offset Prediction Network (OPN) — a depthwise convolution followed by LayerNorm, GELU, and a linear projection — predicts a 2D coordinate offset for every patch from the input feature map. These offsets are added to the original patch positions, and bilinear interpolation samples features at the predicted locations, creating a differentiable link that allows the offsets to be optimized end-to-end. The sampled patches are then ordered by their original top-to-bottom, left-to-right positions to form a content-adaptive 1D sequence fed into the Mamba S6 selective state space block. The full DAMamba backbone follows VMamba's 4-stage hierarchical design (resolutions H/8 through H/32) with convolutional positional encoding and ConvFFN for local feature extraction, available in four scales (F/T/S/B) from 6M to 86M parameters.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K (224×224), DAMamba achieves top-1 accuracies of 79.1% (F, 6M), 83.8% (T, 26M), 84.8% (S, 45M), and 85.2% (B, 86M), consistently outperforming the strongest SSM baseline VMamba by 1.2–1.3% at every scale. On COCO 2017 with Mask R-CNN (1× schedule), DAMamba-T/S/B reach 48.5/49.8/50.6 AP^b and 43.4/44.5/44.9 AP^m, surpassing VMamba by 1.1–1.4 AP^b. On ADE20K with UperNet, DAMamba-T/S/B achieve 50.3/51.2/51.9 mIoU (single-scale), exceeding VMamba by 2.3–0.9 mIoU respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DAMamba consistently outperforms all compared SSM baselines (VMamba, Vim, PlainMamba, LocalVMamba, FractalMamba, EfficientVMamba) across every model scale and task. At the Tiny scale, DAMamba-T (83.8%) surpasses VMamba-T (82.6%) by 1.2%, Swin-T (81.3%) by 2.5%, and even the strong CNN InternImage-T (83.5%) by 0.3%. At the Small scale, DAMamba-S (84.8%) edges out TransNeXt-Small (84.7%) by 0.1% and InternImage-S (84.2%) by 0.6%, while beating VMamba-S (83.6%) by a clear 1.2%. At the Base scale, DAMamba-B (85.2%) leads VMamba-B (83.9%) by 1.3%, InternImage-B (84.9%) by 0.3%, and TransNeXt-Base (84.8%) by 0.4%. On COCO detection (1×), DAMamba-T gains 1.2 AP^b over VMamba-T and 5.8 AP^b over Swin-T; on ADE20K segmentation, DAMamba-T leads VMamba-T by 2.3 mIoU. The only notable near-tie is DAMamba-S vs. TransNeXt-Small on ImageNet (84.8% vs. 84.7%), but DAMamba-S uses fewer parameters (45M vs. 50M) and fewer FLOPs (10.3G vs. 10.3G), and decisively wins on all dense prediction tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

DAMamba builds directly on the Mamba S6 selective state space block and the hierarchical 4-stage architecture introduced by VMamba, while also drawing on Vim's bidirectional scanning formulation. The key conceptual departure is replacing all prior manually-designed scanning strategies (sweeping, continuous, local) with a data-driven Dynamic Adaptive Scan that uses an Offset Prediction Network and bilinear interpolation to learn per-patch sampling positions — an idea analogous to deformable convolutions and deformable attention, but applied to the sequence ordering in SSMs. The architecture further integrates convolutional positional encoding and ConvFFN for local feature extraction, following patterns established in PVTv2 and other hybrid CNN-Transformer designs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The scanning strategy is arguably the most critical design decision in vision SSMs, and this paper demonstrates that making it input-adaptive yields consistent gains across classification, detection, and segmentation without sacrificing linear complexity. The approach opens a natural question of whether other fixed structural choices in SSMs (e.g., the number of scan directions, the state dimension) could similarly be made data-driven. A notable gap is the absence of any discussion of limitations or failure modes, and the modest 0.6% ablation gain from DAS over sweeping scan (on the smallest model) suggests the benefit may be more pronounced at larger scales or on more complex scenes, which remains to be fully characterized.
