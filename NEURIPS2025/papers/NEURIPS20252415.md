## Problem

Deploying CNNs on microcontrollers with as little as 16–128 kB of RAM is severely constrained by peak memory usage, and prior patch-based fusion approaches (MCUNetV2, StreamNet) were limited to a single fusion block, were hardware- and model-specific, and used brute-force or heuristic search. No prior work systematically explored multiple fusion blocks across a CNN or formulated the fusion configuration search as a tractable graph optimization problem.

## Input / Output

The optimizer takes a CNN architecture (e.g., MobileNetV2-w0.35, MCUNetV2 variants) together with a user-specified constraint—either a maximum compute overhead factor F_max or a peak RAM budget P_max—and outputs a set of fusion settings (which layers to fuse and at what depth) plus generated C code deployable via microTVM on ARM Cortex-M, RISC-V, or Xtensa MCUs. At inference time, the fused model consumes standard RGB images (144×144×3, 80×80×3, or 176×176×3) or audio sequences and produces class labels, with peak RAM reduced by up to 90% relative to the unfused model while preserving identical accuracy.

## Method

msf-CNN models a CNN as a directed acyclic graph (DAG) where nodes are intermediate tensors and edges represent either single layers or multi-layer fusion blocks, each annotated with peak RAM usage and MAC count. The dual optimization problems (minimize peak RAM subject to a compute budget, or minimize compute subject to a RAM budget) are cast as constrained shortest-path problems on this DAG. A key pruning strategy iteratively removes edges with the highest RAM usage and re-solves the shortest path, reducing worst-case complexity from O(2^(V−2)) to O(V³). The approach supports multiple fusion blocks simultaneously (unlike prior single-block methods), uses an H-cache scheme to balance recompute cost against buffer size, and further squeezes RAM by rewriting global pooling and dense layers as iterative, element-wise computations that require only a fraction of the original tensor in memory.

## Results

Under minimal peak RAM settings (unconstrained compute overhead), msf-CNN reduces peak RAM to 8.56 kB (MBV2-w0.35), 15.37 kB (MN2-vww5), and 51.16 kB (MN2-320K), achieving 65–87% reduction over prior art. The trade-off is a 2×–5× increase in inference latency relative to vanilla (non-fused) CNNs. Model accuracy is fully preserved: top-1 accuracy on ImageNet and VWW is identical to the unfused baseline (e.g., 48.94% for MBV2-w0.35, 88.89% for MN2-vww5, 61.76% for MN2-320K). Under constrained settings (e.g., F_max=1.3), msf-CNN still achieves 21.29 kB / 17.76 kB / 186.03 kB peak RAM with only 1.3× compute overhead.

## Baselines & Comparisons

msf-CNN decisively outperforms both MCUNetV2 and StreamNet on peak RAM across all three models: 8.56 kB vs. 63.00 kB (MCUNetV2) and 66.00 kB (StreamNet) for MBV2-w0.35; 15.37 kB vs. 45.00 kB and 44.00 kB for MN2-vww5; and 51.16 kB vs. 215.00 kB and 208.00 kB for MN2-320K — a 65–87% reduction. On latency, however, msf-CNN is slower than StreamNet because StreamNet leverages ARM CMSIS intrinsics and a 2-D cache, whereas the base msf-CNN implementation uses a 1-D H-cache without hardware-specific acceleration; adding an optional CMSIS backend narrows the gap considerably. Against MCUNetV2, msf-CNN wins on both RAM and (in most constrained settings) latency, since MCUNetV2's heuristic of fusing only early layers yields suboptimal configurations. The paper also notes that msf-CNN's optimizer provides a continuous trade-off curve (via F_max or P_max constraints), whereas MCUNetV2 and StreamNet offer only a single fixed fusion configuration, giving system designers substantially more flexibility.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends patch-based layer fusion from its FPGA origins (Alwani et al., MICRO 2016) to microcontroller deployment, going beyond the single-fusion-block heuristics of MCUNetV2 (NeurIPS 2021) and the brute-force two-dimensional cache search of StreamNet (NeurIPS 2024). The key conceptual departure is formulating multi-stage fusion as a constrained shortest-path problem on a directed acyclic graph, where edges encode both RAM and MAC costs, enabling polynomial-time optimization over an exponentially large fusion configuration space. Implementation builds on microTVM and RIOT-ML, with iterative rewrites of global pooling and dense layers as an additional memory-saving mechanism orthogonal to the fusion search itself.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By reducing peak RAM by 50% over prior art and enabling deployment on 16 kB-RAM boards, msf-CNN opens the door to running non-trivial CNNs on the most constrained IoT devices (RFC 7228 class). The graph-based formulation is generalizable to other architectures and hardware targets, and the dual optimization (min RAM s.t. compute, min compute s.t. RAM) gives system designers a principled trade-off knob. Open limitations include restriction to CNNs, a single H-cache paradigm, fixed single-element-per-iteration output, and the absence of 2-D caching that would close the remaining gap to StreamNet on ARM platforms.
