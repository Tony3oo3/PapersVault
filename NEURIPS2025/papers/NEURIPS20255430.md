## Problem

Existing multimodal RoPE variants (M-RoPE, VideoRoPE) allocate frequencies to temporal and spatial dimensions based on heuristics without theoretical justification, and the paper proves that no fixed frequency allocation can reliably preserve semantic similarity over sufficiently long contexts. Additionally, applying a single fixed temporal scaling factor to all videos is suboptimal given the wide variance in real-world video speeds and information densities.

## Input / Output

The model consumes video frames (sampled at 2 fps, up to 128 frames during training, minimum 144 tokens per frame at evaluation) encoded by a ViT vision encoder into patch-level tokens, alongside text prompts. Visual tokens receive 3D positional indices (t, x, y) with a dynamic scaling factor γ applied to the temporal component, while text tokens use standard 1D indices. Outputs are free-form text answers for understanding tasks (multiple-choice, open-ended) or identification of a specific 'needle' frame within a 1-hour haystack video for retrieval.

## Method

HoPE introduces two mechanisms: (1) a Hybrid Frequency Allocation (HFA) strategy that assigns high frequencies to spatial dimensions (x, y) in an interleaved manner for local semantic capture, while setting the lowest 32 frequencies to zero (equivalent to NoPE) for the temporal dimension, which the paper proves via Lemma 4.1 and Theorem 4.1 to dominate any non-zero temporal frequency choice in preserving the semantic preference property; (2) a Dynamic Temporal Scaling (DTS) mechanism that randomly samples a scaling factor γ from {0.5, 0.75, 1, 1.25, 1.5} during training to learn multi-scale temporal relationships, and allows flexible γ selection at inference (γ=0.75 for retrieval, γ=1.5 for understanding). The approach is implemented on Qwen2-2B/7B backbones with Qwen2-VL vision encoders, trained via instruction tuning on ~300k video-text pairs from LLaVA-Video-178k at 8k context length.

## Results

On long video understanding (Qwen2-7B-Video, 32k context), HoPE achieves 63.85 on MLVU, 55.34 on LongVideoBench, and 59.44 on Video-MME, outperforming all baselines across every context length from 8k to 64k. On the V-NIAH long video retrieval benchmark, HoPE surpasses the best baseline (VideoRoPE) by a margin of 22.23%. The paper reports average improvements of 8.35% on long video understanding and 22.23% on long video retrieval over existing RoPE variants. Performance gains scale with backbone size, with the LongVideoBench (32k) advantage over vanilla RoPE growing from 0.66 (2B) to 4.05 (7B).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HoPE is compared against three RoPE variants: vanilla RoPE (1D flattening), M-RoPE (highest frequencies for temporal), and VideoRoPE (lowest frequencies for temporal with fixed scaling). At the 7B scale and 32k context, HoPE outperforms vanilla RoPE by 2.82 (MLVU), 4.05 (LongVideoBench), and 1.45 (Video-MME); it beats M-RoPE by 1.39, 1.85, and 1.07 respectively; and it edges out VideoRoPE by 1.34, 1.52, and 0.31. The advantage is most pronounced at 64k context, where HoPE retains 50.01 on MLVU while vanilla RoPE drops to 34.38 and VideoRoPE to 45.31. On V-NIAH (1-hour haystack video), HoPE surpasses the best baseline by 22.23%, confirming its superiority in extreme-length retrieval. The 2B model shows smaller absolute gaps (e.g., 0.66 on LongVideoBench 32k vs. vanilla RoPE), attributed to the limited capacity of the smaller backbone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the multimodal RoPE line initiated by M-RoPE (Qwen2-VL) and VideoRoPE, which partition rotary dimensions across temporal and spatial axes but rely on heuristic frequency allocation. HoPE departs from both by providing the first theoretical analysis showing that all existing allocation strategies (highest or lowest frequencies for temporal) provably fail to maintain semantic preference beyond a critical context length, and by borrowing the NoPE insight of zeroing frequencies to give temporal dimensions an identity rotation. The dynamic temporal scaling mechanism generalizes VideoRoPE's fixed unidirectional scaling to a bidirectional, randomly-sampled set of factors during training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical result that no fixed frequency allocation can guarantee semantic preference at arbitrary context lengths reframes how the community should think about multimodal position encoding, shifting the design principle from 'which frequencies to assign' to 'how many dimensions to zero out.' Practically, the 22% gain on V-NIAH and consistent improvements across understanding benchmarks make HoPE a drop-in replacement for M-RoPE/VideoRoPE in existing VLM pipelines. However, the evaluation is limited to 2B and 7B backbones trained on ~300k pairs, and all methods still degrade sharply at 64k context, leaving open whether the approach scales to larger models or benefits from longer training contexts.
