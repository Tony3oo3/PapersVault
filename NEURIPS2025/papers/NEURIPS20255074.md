## Problem

Existing feed-forward 3D reconstruction models are limited to static scenes, while dynamic scene reconstruction from monocular video still relies on expensive per-scene optimization (hours to days). No prior feed-forward approach can generalize to real-world dynamic scenes, as the only existing one (L4GM) is restricted to synthetic object-centric data with fixed multi-view supervision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255074/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a subset of monocular video frames (up to 12 context views at 256×256 or 512×512 resolution) along with their camera poses (SE(3)) and timestamps, plus a target 'bullet' timestamp specifying when the output scene should be frozen. It outputs a complete pixel-aligned 3D Gaussian Splatting scene at that timestamp, which can be rendered in real-time from arbitrary novel viewpoints. Inference takes 150 ms for 12 views at 256² on a single A100 GPU, and the resulting 3DGS supports real-time rendering at 115+ FPS.

## Method

BTimer introduces a bullet-time formulation where a sinusoidal positional encoding of the target timestamp is added to every context frame's token, instructing the ViT backbone (24 self-attention blocks with Plücker camera embeddings) to aggregate information across all context frames and predict a 3DGS scene at that specific time. Each 8×8 patch outputs 12 parameters (RGB, scale, quaternion rotation, opacity, ray distance) with 3D position recovered via pixel-aligned unprojection. A separate 3D-free Novel Time Enhancer (NTE) module, sharing the same ViT architecture but using KV-Cache for efficient inference, synthesizes intermediate frames at unobserved timestamps to handle fast motion. Training follows a three-stage curriculum: (1) static pretraining on 390K samples from Objaverse, RE10K, MVImgNet, and DL3DV at increasing resolutions, (2) dynamic co-training on Kubric, PointOdyssey, DynamicReplica, Spring, and 40K annotated PANDA-70M clips, and (3) long-context fine-tuning from 4 to 12 views. Supervision uses only photometric losses (MSE + LPIPS) with both in-context and interpolation supervision strategies, eliminating the need for 3D ground truth.

## Results

On the NVIDIA Dynamic Scene benchmark, BTimer achieves 25.82 dB PSNR and 0.086 LPIPS with a reconstruction time of 0.78 s and 115 FPS rendering, compared to 26.10 dB / 0.082 LPIPS for DynNeRF (74 h, 0.05 FPS) and 25.89 dB / 0.065 LPIPS for RoDynRF (28 h, 0.42 FPS). On the DyCheck iPhone benchmark, BTimer reaches 16.52 dB PSNR, 0.570 SSIM, and 0.338 LPIPS in 0.98 s, ranking 2nd in both SSIM and LPIPS among all baselines. On the static RE10K benchmark, the BTimer static variant achieves 0.070 LPIPS, outperforming GS-LRM (0.114), MVSplat (0.128), and PixelSplat (0.142). Inference with 12 context frames at 256×256 resolution takes only 150 ms on a single A100 GPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the NVIDIA Dynamic Scene benchmark, BTimer (25.82 dB PSNR) is within 0.3 dB of the best optimization-based methods DynNeRF (26.10 dB) and RoDynRF (25.89 dB), while being 3–4 orders of magnitude faster in reconstruction (0.78 s vs. 28–74 h) and rendering 115 FPS vs. 0.05–0.42 FPS. BTimer clearly outperforms the other 3DGS-based dynamic methods: 4D-GS (21.45 dB, +5 dB gap) and Casual-FVS (24.57 dB, +1.25 dB gap). On DyCheck, BTimer (16.52 dB) trails T-NeRF (16.96 dB) and HyperNeRF (16.81 dB) by ~0.3–0.4 dB in PSNR but matches or beats them in SSIM (0.570 vs. 0.577/0.569) and LPIPS (0.338 vs. 0.379/0.332), all without per-scene optimization. On the static RE10K benchmark, BTimer-Static achieves the best LPIPS (0.070) among all feed-forward baselines including GS-LRM (0.114) and MVSplat (0.128). On the NSFF benchmark under DynIBaR's protocol, BTimer (26.31 dB) underperforms DynIBaR (30.86 dB) by ~4.5 dB, but DynIBaR requires ~300 h of per-scene optimization and additional optical-flow/depth supervision. Overall, BTimer trades a small quality gap (≤0.5 dB PSNR) against the top optimization-based methods for a 1000×+ speedup in reconstruction and real-time rendering.

## Relation to Prior Work

BTimer extends the pixel-aligned 3D Gaussian Splatting prediction paradigm established by GS-LRM [5] from static to dynamic scenes by introducing a bullet-time timestamp embedding that conditions the ViT backbone on a target reconstruction time. The Novel Time Enhancer module is directly inspired by the decoder-only LVSM [16] architecture, repurposing it for frame interpolation at novel timestamps. Conceptually, the paper departs from the dominant per-scene optimization paradigm for dynamic NVS (HyperNeRF, DynNeRF, 4D-GS, MoSca) by showing that a single feed-forward model, trained with a curriculum across static and dynamic datasets, can match or exceed these methods without any per-scene fitting, and it unifies static and dynamic reconstruction under one formulation by simply equalizing timestamps for static scenes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The bullet-time formulation provides a clean, scalable path to real-time dynamic scene reconstruction from casual monocular video (150 ms inference), which is orders of magnitude faster than existing optimization-based pipelines and opens the door to interactive applications in AR/VR and content creation. Key limitations remain: the pixel-aligned 3DGS representation does not explicitly model temporal deformations, recovered geometry/depth is imprecise, and the model struggles with very fast or complex motions without the NTE module. Open questions include whether explicit motion fields can be extracted post-hoc, whether the formulation scales to much longer videos, and whether the implicit scene-flow learning observed in the supplementary material can be made explicit and controllable.
