## Problem

Most asymptotic optimality results for weakly-coupled MDPs (WCMDPs) under the average-reward criterion are restricted to homogeneous or typed-heterogeneous settings, where arms can be grouped into a constant number of types. The fully heterogeneous setting—where all N arms have distinct model parameters—leads to an exponentially growing state space and had no known efficiently computable policy with a vanishing optimality gap. This paper establishes the first O(1/√N) optimality gap for fully heterogeneous average-reward WCMDPs.

## Input / Output

The method takes as input an N-armed WCMDP instance in which each arm i has its own finite MDP (state space S, action space A, transition kernel P_i, reward r_i, and K cost functions c_{k,i}), together with K budget constraints of the form Σ_i c_{k,i}(s_i,a_i) ≤ α_k N. The output is a stationary Markov policy that assigns an action to each arm at every time step, computed in polynomial time in N via a one-time LP solve (with O(N) variables) followed by a simple greedy execution loop. The guarantee is that the per-arm average reward under this policy is within O(1/√N) of the optimal per-arm average reward as N → ∞.

## Method

The proposed ID policy with reassignment has two phases: a preprocessing phase that solves an LP to obtain per-arm optimal single-armed policies π̄_i* and then reassigns arm IDs so that cumulative cost over any contiguous ID block is approximately proportional to block length (eliminating cost plateaus), and a real-time phase that iterates arms by reassigned ID, letting each arm follow its ideal action until a budget is exhausted, after which remaining arms take the zero-cost action. The key technical innovation is a projection-based Lyapunov function: rather than aggregating arm states into an empirical distribution (which requires symmetry), the function projects each arm's one-hot state vector onto a set of feature vectors encoding instantaneous reward, instantaneous cost, and their ℓ-step-ahead expectations under the per-arm transition matrices P_i, then measures the supremum deviation from the optimal state distributions μ_i* across all such features. A geometric discount factor γ^ℓ = exp(−ℓ/(2τ)) is applied to the ℓ-step features to induce a negative drift, and a focus-set construction converts the per-subset functions h(X_t, D) into a single Lyapunov function V(X_t) satisfying the standard drift condition E[V(X_{t+1})|X_t] − V(X_t) ≤ −C₂V(X_t) + O(√N), which yields the O(1/√N) reward gap.

## Results

The main theoretical result (Theorem 1) establishes that the ID policy with reassignment achieves an O(1/√N) optimality gap for fully heterogeneous average-reward WCMDPs, with the constant C_ID = O(K⁵ max{r_max, c_max}⁷ τ⁴ / α_min⁶). This is the first asymptotic optimality result for fully heterogeneous average-reward WCMDPs. Experimentally, the optimality ratio (achieved reward / LP relaxation upper bound) approaches 1 as N increases from 100 to 3200, confirming the theoretical prediction. A finite-time bound (Proposition 1) further shows the gap decomposes as C_ID/√N + C_finite/T.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares the ID policy with reassignment against the ERC policy from Xiong et al. [46] (NeurIPS 2022), which is the most general prior method for heterogeneous WCMDPs. However, the comparison is restricted to a special case (single budget constraint, state-independent costs, typed heterogeneity with 10 types) to make a fair comparison, since ERC does not handle fully heterogeneous or multi-constraint settings. In this restricted setting, the ID policy shows a slight improvement over ERC in some instances (illustrated in Figure 2) and comparable performance in others. The key practical advantage is generality: the ID policy applies to fully heterogeneous WCMDPs with multiple actions, multiple budget constraints, and state-dependent cost functions, whereas ERC is limited to typed heterogeneity with a single constraint and state-independent costs. No prior method achieves an O(1/√N) gap for fully heterogeneous average-reward WCMDPs, making this a direct comparison against the state of the art in the most general setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the ID policy from Hong et al. [27], originally designed for homogeneous restless bandits, to the fully heterogeneous setting of general weakly-coupled MDPs with multiple actions and multiple budget constraints. The key departure is technical: in the homogeneous or typed-heterogeneous setting, Lyapunov analysis relies on aggregating arm states into empirical distributions, which is impossible when all N arms are distinct. The authors introduce a projection-based Lyapunov function that projects arm states onto feature vectors encoding instantaneous and future-expected rewards and costs, circumventing the need for symmetry. The LP relaxation framework and the overall drift-method structure follow the standard approach in the WCMDP literature [15, 22, 24, 27, 39, 42, 46], but the construction of the Lyapunov function is novel.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first result establishing an O(1/√N) optimality gap for fully heterogeneous average-reward WCMDPs, closing a gap where prior work was limited to homogeneous or typed-heterogeneous settings with o(1) guarantees. The projection-based Lyapunov technique is potentially transferable to other heterogeneous large-scale stochastic systems where state aggregation is infeasible, offering a more direct steady-state analysis framework than existing mean-field or interchange-of-limits approaches. Limitations include the planning setting (all parameters known), the uniform mixing-time bound assumption, and the fact that the constant in the gap bound has not been optimized. Open questions include extending the result to the learning setting with unknown parameters and whether the projection technique generalizes to systems with exogenous coupling.
