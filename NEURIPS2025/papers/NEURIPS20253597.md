## Problem

Self-supervised representation learning, particularly contrastive learning, is prone to over-relying on spurious features—simple, easily-learnable signals that are spuriously correlated with labels but fail to generalize. The paper identifies a spectral signature of this failure (dominance of top singular modes in the feature covariance matrix) and asks whether a fully self-supervised, label-free regularizer can flatten this spectrum to promote more diverse and robust representations.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/SpurCIFAR-10/C-MNIST, 64×64 for CelebA/MetaShift/Waterbirds) and produces d-dimensional L2-normalized feature vectors (512-dim with ResNet-18, 2048-dim with ResNet-50). During pretraining, the regularizer operates on the centered mini-batch feature Gram matrix (|B|×d), and at evaluation time the frozen encoder's features are fed to a linear probe that outputs binary or multi-class labels. No group annotations or labels are required at any stage of pretraining.

## Method

The core contribution is a spectrum-flattening regularizer R_spec(G) = Σᵢ pᵢ² where pᵢ = λᵢ/Σⱼλⱼ are the normalized eigenvalues of the centered mini-batch feature covariance matrix G = F̃F̃ᵀ, added to the standard contrastive loss (SimCLR or SimSiam) with a small weight α. The theoretical justification (Theorem 4) shows that under a fixed-trace constraint, the expected downstream generalization error over random linear tasks is minimized when FFᵀ has a uniform eigenspectrum, i.e., all non-zero eigenvalues are equal. The regularizer is architecture-agnostic, requires no labels or group annotations, and is computed per mini-batch on centered features, making it seamlessly integrable into any SSRL pipeline. Empirically, even without truncating small singular values, simply flattening the full spectrum improves worst-group accuracy from ~30% to ~40% on SpurCIFAR-10, and the full method achieves state-of-the-art worst-group accuracy across five spurious-correlation benchmarks.

## Results

On the five spurious-correlation benchmarks, the spectral regularizer (applied to SimCLR) achieves the best worst-group accuracy on 3 of 5 datasets: 95.10% on C-MNIST (vs. 83.8% for SimCLR-LateTVG), 64.65% on SpurCIFAR-10 (vs. 61.4% for SimSiam-LateTVG), and 83.96% on CelebA (vs. 83.1% for SimSiam-LateTVG). For average accuracy, the method leads on 4 of 5 datasets, reaching 98.08% on C-MNIST, 82.85% on SpurCIFAR-10, 76.57% on MetaShift, and 58.32% on Waterbirds. The method trails SimSiam-LateTVG on worst-group accuracy for MetaShift (61.14% vs. 79.6%) and Waterbirds (50.25% vs. 56.3%), though it uses 512-dim embeddings versus LateTVG's 2048-dim representations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SimCLR and SimSiam baselines, the spectral regularizer yields large worst-group accuracy gains on all five datasets (e.g., +58.15 pts on SpurCIFAR-10, +49.64 pts on MetaShift). Compared to the strongest baseline SimSiam-LateTVG (2048-dim), the method wins on C-MNIST (+12.0 pts), SpurCIFAR-10 (+3.25 pts), and CelebA (+0.86 pts) for worst-group accuracy, but loses on MetaShift (−18.5 pts) and Waterbirds (−6.05 pts). For average accuracy, the method surpasses SimSiam-LateTVG on C-MNIST (+17.5 pts), SpurCIFAR-10 (+6.75 pts), MetaShift (+6.5 pts), and Waterbirds (+3.5 pts), while trailing slightly on CelebA (−0.5 pts). The paper notes that LateTVG does not report standard deviations or its pruning hyperparameter, making direct variance-aware comparison difficult. Additional baselines (Barlow Twins, DirectDLR, IFM, BYOL, DINO) are reported in Appendix H but are not the primary comparison focus.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of spurious-correlation robustness (Sagawa et al., Geirhos et al.) and self-supervised representation learning (SimCLR, SimSiam), building on the observation that contrastive methods inherit the simplicity bias that favors easy-to-learn features. It departs from prior diversity-promoting regularizers—Barlow Twins' redundancy reduction, VICReg's covariance penalty, and IFM's adversarial feature perturbation—by targeting the eigenspectrum of the feature covariance matrix directly, motivated by a new theoretical result (Theorem 4) showing that a uniform spectrum minimizes expected downstream generalization error under a trace constraint. The most direct competitor is LateTVG (Hamidieh et al., 2024), which also targets spurious correlations in SSRL but relies on stop-gradient architectures and feature-space augmentation rather than spectral regularization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean theoretical justification (Theorem 4) for why spectral diversity matters in SSRL, connecting the effective rank of the feature matrix to downstream generalization via a pseudoinverse-based bound. The regularizer is architecture-agnostic, label-free, and adds negligible overhead, making it broadly applicable. However, the sensitivity to the regularization weight α (performance drops ~10 points from α=0.001 to α=0.010 on MetaShift) and the idealized random-task model in the theory limit practical confidence. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger backbones and datasets (e.g., ImageNet), how it interacts with other regularizers like Barlow Twins, and whether the uniform-spectrum optimum holds under non-uniform task distributions.
