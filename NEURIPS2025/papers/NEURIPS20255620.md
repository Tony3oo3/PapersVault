## Problem

Existing RAG rerankers select a fixed number of documents (k) for all queries and score relevance using only the LLM's internal knowledge, ignoring the natural supervisory signal that generation quality itself provides about how many documents are actually needed. This static threshold either omits critical information for complex queries or introduces noise for simple ones, degrading both accuracy and efficiency.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255620/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The reranker receives a text query plus Top-N retrieved passages (up to 45 for LLaMA3-8B, 20 for LLaMA2-7B, from Contriever-MS MARCO over 2018 English Wikipedia) and outputs a comma-separated list of document identifiers (or 'None') representing a reordered subset of size k ≤ 15. The generator then consumes the query plus the selected Top-k documents to produce a final answer (short phrase for open-domain QA, paragraph for ELI5, or SUPPORTS/REFUTES/NEI for FEVER). At inference, the full pipeline requires only two LLM calls: one for reranking and one for generation.

## Method

DynamicRAG models the reranker as an RL agent that jointly determines document order and count via list-wise ranking in a single forward pass. Training proceeds in two stages: first, behavioral cloning with MonoT5-generated expert trajectories via SFT to establish basic selection capability; second, DPO-based reinforcement learning where the generator acts as an interactive environment, and the reranker is optimized using trajectory pairs (best vs. worst reward) sampled across multiple generations. The reward is a weighted combination of Exact Match, BERTScore, ROUGE, a length penalty (1/(1+len)), and an LLM-based evaluation score, directly tying reranking decisions to downstream generation quality. The reranker and generator can share parameters, and the framework is backbone-agnostic, validated on LLaMA2-7B, LLaMA2-13B, and LLaMA3-8B.

## Results

DynamicRAG (LLaMA3-8B, ~150k training data) achieves NQ EM 48.4, HotpotQA EM 36.7, ASQA EM 56.3, FEVER Acc 91.4, and ELI5 ROUGE-L 24.6, outperforming GPT-4o (no retrieval) on NQ (48.4 vs. 40.0) and HotpotQA (36.7 vs. 36.1). On the reranker recall benchmark, DynamicRAG achieves an average R@5/R@10/R@20 of 73.7 using only 20k training samples, surpassing RankRAG (72.5 with 50k samples) and GPT-4 (69.8). The method also demonstrates strong efficiency, requiring only 2 LLM calls at inference and ~17× higher throughput than RankRAG's sequential scoring approach.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DynamicRAG (LLaMA3-8B) outperforms GPT-4o (no retrieval) on NQ (48.4 vs. 40.0 EM) and HotpotQA (36.7 vs. 36.1 EM), and beats ChatQA-1.5 on NQ (48.4 vs. 42.4), HotpotQA (36.7 vs. 33.4), 2WikiMultihopQA (34.2 vs. 26.8), and FEVER (91.4 vs. 90.9 Acc) while using roughly 3× less training data (~150k vs. ~442k). Against the strongest same-size baseline RankRAG (~470k data), DynamicRAG wins on HotpotQA (36.7 vs. 35.3) and 2WikiMultihopQA (34.2 vs. 31.4) but trails on NQ (48.4 vs. 50.6), TriviaQA EM (78.3 vs. 82.9), and FEVER (91.4 vs. 93.8). On the dedicated reranker recall benchmark (Table 2), DynamicRAG achieves an average of 73.7 with only 20k training samples, exceeding RankRAG (72.5 with 50k), GPT-4 (69.8), and all open-source rerankers (monoT5 70.0, RankLLaMA 71.8). The method also substantially outperforms its own SFT-only variant (LLaMA3-8B-SFT) across all benchmarks, with the largest gap on ASQA (56.3 vs. 46.8 EM), confirming the contribution of the RL/DPO training stage. Ablations show that removing retrieval drops average EM from 47.1 to 22.1, removing reranking drops it to 34.5, and removing RL drops it to 39.9, validating each component's necessity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RAG reranking literature by reframing the reranker as an RL agent that interacts with the generator as an environment, building directly on DPO for preference-based policy optimization. Prior LLM-based rerankers (RankRAG, ChatQA-1.5, Self-RAG) treat document scoring as a static, fixed-k problem, while DynamicRAG departs by jointly optimizing document order and count using downstream generation quality as the reward signal. The two-stage training (behavior cloning with MonoT5 expert trajectories, then DPO with generator feedback) draws on imitation learning and agent-tuning paradigms, but the key conceptual departure is treating the generator's output quality—rather than isolated relevance scores—as the supervisory signal for reranking decisions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Dynamic k selection addresses a practical pain point in RAG deployments where fixed document counts either miss critical information or introduce noise, and the 17× throughput advantage over point-wise reranking (RankRAG) makes the approach attractive for latency-sensitive applications. However, the work is limited to list-wise ranking, uses only DPO (leaving PPO/GRPO unexplored), and constrains k≤15, which may limit applicability to long-context or multi-hop settings. Open questions include whether step-wise or progressive reranking could further improve efficiency, whether the approach generalizes to non-Wikipedia corpora, and whether on-policy RL methods would yield meaningfully different reranking policies.
