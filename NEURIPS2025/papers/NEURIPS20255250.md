## Problem

Current sequence models (transformers, RNNs, SSMs) are fundamentally perceptron-based and do not capture the oscillatory dynamics characteristic of biological neural circuits. While recent oscillatory SSMs like LinOSS model rhythmic temporal dynamics, they operate in an abstract latent space without a structured topographic substrate, limiting their ability to express interpretable, wave-like spatio-temporal coordination patterns analogous to those observed in cortical tissue.

## Input / Output

The model consumes multivariate time series (21–862 channels, 405–52,696 timesteps) by mapping each input dimension onto a local patch of 7–8 cells on a 2D spatial grid (10×10, 15×15, or 20×20). It produces either a class label (UEA classification, 2–5 classes) or a scalar regression prediction (heart rate, traffic volume, etc.). In the audio visualization experiment, normalized logits from a tagging backbone serve as point-wise inputs to the grid, and the evolving pressure-field (p) values are recorded as the output signal.

## Method

BioOSS organizes two coupled state types on a 2D grid: p units (membrane-potential-like signal carriers) and o units (velocity-like latent states controlling phase, time scale, and damping), governed by a discretized pressure–velocity PDE pair with per-location trainable wave speed c and damping coefficients k^p, k^o. An explicit two-step discretization (velocity update followed by damping correction) yields a sparse state-transition matrix A = M^{Damp^{-1}} M^{Velocity}. To enable efficient long-sequence processing, the paper applies eigendecomposition in a periodic-grid wavenumber basis, deriving a closed-form complex-conjugate eigenvalue pair per location that parameterizes a scan-friendly diagonal recurrence (O(log N) parallel scan). A CFL-like sufficient stability constraint clips c relative to Δt, Δx, and damping factors. The full model stacks a single BioOSS block with a gated residual update (sigmoid gate × tanh + residual), GELU activation, and a GLU readout, trained end-to-end with Adam under supervised loss.

## Results

On the UEA multivariate time-series classification benchmark (6 datasets), BioOSS achieves the highest average accuracy of 66.25%, outperforming LinOSS (65.12%), S5, and LRU. BioOSS attains state-of-the-art results on three datasets: EigenWorms (92.8%), EthanolConcentration (33.4%), and Heartbeat (74.8%). On the PPG-DaLiA heart-rate regression task, BioOSS achieves MSE ×10⁻² of 7.7±0.2 (second to LinOSS at 6.4±0.23), while on four long-term forecasting benchmarks (Electricity, Solar-Energy, Traffic, Weather), BioOSS outperforms LinOSS on all four, with the largest margin on Traffic (62.28 vs 68.83 MSE ×10⁻²).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LRU, BioOSS wins on 4 of 6 classification datasets (Worms, Ethanol, Heartbeat, Motor) and on all four forecasting benchmarks, while LRU edges out on SCP1 (86.8% vs 85.6%). Against S5, BioOSS is superior on 5 of 6 classification tasks and all forecasting tasks; S5 only matches or leads on SCP1. Against the closest competitor LinOSS, BioOSS achieves a higher average classification accuracy (66.25% vs 65.12%), winning on Worms (+0.6%), Ethanol (+3.5%), and Heartbeat (+2.9%), but losing on SCP2 (55.1% vs 60.4%) and Motor (55.8% vs 60.4%). On PPG-DaLiA regression, LinOSS retains a narrow lead (MSE 6.4 vs 7.7 ×10⁻²), though both substantially outperform LRU (15.64) and S5 (12.79). On the four long-term forecasting benchmarks, BioOSS beats LinOSS on every dataset, with the most pronounced gain on Traffic (62.28 vs 68.83 MSE ×10⁻², a ~9.5% relative improvement) and Electricity (18.70 vs 21.88, ~14.5% relative improvement).

## Relation to Prior Work

BioOSS extends the oscillatory state-space model lineage initiated by LinOSS (Rusch and Rus, 2025) and the broader SSM family (S5, LRU) by organizing states on a 2D topographic grid with heterogeneous per-location damping and wave-speed parameters, rather than operating in an abstract 1D latent space. It draws conceptual inspiration from cortical columnar organization and PFC circuitry (Watakabe et al., 2023; Buonomano and Maass, 2009), but explicitly positions itself as a scan-friendly diagonal modal parameterization rather than a full stencil-based PDE wave solver. The eigendecomposition-based diagonal recurrence for efficient parallel scanning follows the associativity-preserving framework established in the SSM literature (Orvieto et al., 2023; Rusch and Rus, 2025), while the separation of pressure-like (p) and velocity-like (o) components introduces a structural inductive bias absent in prior oscillatory SSMs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BioOSS offers a principled, interpretable substrate for studying how spatially structured oscillatory dynamics can support task-relevant spatio-temporal pattern formation, with the eigenvalue-to-frequency mapping providing a mechanistic account of emergent frequency selectivity. However, the practical gains over LinOSS are modest (1–3% on classification, a few MSE points on forecasting), the model requires only a single block across all tasks, and the linear encoder limits expressiveness of intrinsic oscillatory behavior. Open questions include whether the 2D grid structure scales to higher-dimensional or irregular topologies, whether the slow-variation assumption on c and k holds under strong spatial heterogeneity, and whether the interpretability benefits translate to downstream applications beyond benchmark accuracy.
