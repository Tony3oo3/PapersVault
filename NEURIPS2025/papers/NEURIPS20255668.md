## Problem

Generative models inevitably incur learning errors in their score or drift vector fields, yet empirically the generated samples shift along the data manifold rather than into zero-probability regions. Prior theoretical work focused on convergence guarantees as errors vanish, but did not characterize when a model with nonzero errors still captures the high-density support. This paper asks: what dynamical mechanism explains the robustness of the predicted support, and when can we expect it?

## Input / Output

The analysis takes a learned vector field (score or drift) parameterizing a generative process, a source density, and a perturbation vector field representing learning errors, and produces finite-time Lyapunov vectors (principal deformation directions at the final time), the tangent bundle of the data manifold, and a binary alignment/robustness characterization. Unusually, the method operates simultaneously on probability space (via Frobenius-Perron/transfer operators) and on the tangent bundle (via linearized sample-path dynamics), so the same Lyapunov vectors that quantify error sensitivity also serve as a computable estimate of the data manifold's tangent spaces without requiring explicit manifold fitting.

## Method

The paper treats the generative process as a non-autonomous (random) dynamical system and performs two complementary perturbation analyses. On probability space, it derives an explicit expression (Eq. 2) showing that the statistical response of the predicted density to an infinitesimal perturbation is the target density multiplied by a scalar field, hence supported only where the target is nonzero. On the tangent bundle, it tracks how infinitesimal perturbations evolve along sample paths via the time-dependent Jacobian, identifying the most sensitive directions as finite-time Lyapunov vectors computed by QR decomposition of the tangent dynamics. The key result (Theorem 4.3) proves that if the vector field acts as a uniform attracting force toward the support with negligible cross-derivatives in the final phase of generation, then the top d Lyapunov subspaces align with the tangent space of the data manifold (orthogonal to the target score at the boundary). This alignment property is shown to be sufficient for robustness of support (Proposition 4.1) and is itself stable under small perturbations of the dynamics (Lemma 4.4), meaning approximate generative models retain the alignment and can be used for manifold learning.

## Results

This is a primarily theoretical paper with qualitative experiments rather than quantitative benchmark comparisons. The key theoretical result (Theorem 4.3) provides sufficient conditions under which the top d-dimensional Lyapunov subspace aligns with the data manifold tangent space, ensuring robustness of support. Empirically, on MNIST, perturbing a generated digit along the 1st (most sensitive) Lyapunov vector yields another recognizable digit, while perturbing along the 100th LV produces artifacts that leave the data manifold. A small gap in the finite-time Lyapunov exponents appears at an index near 20, consistent with prior estimates of the intrinsic dimension of MNIST. On CIFAR-10, large score perturbations shift the generated density primarily tangent to the data manifold without moving samples to zero-probability regions. In contrast, flow matching with an 8-Gaussian source density does not exhibit the robustness property.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not perform head-to-head method comparisons in the traditional sense, as it is a theoretical analysis of robustness properties rather than a new generative model. The closest comparison is between model classes: score-based generative models (SGMs) satisfy the alignment/robustness conditions (Theorem 4.3) and empirically show support robustness on MNIST and CIFAR-10, while conditional flow matching with an 8-Gaussian source density does not exhibit robustness (Appendix G.5). The paper positions its contribution relative to prior convergence guarantees (e.g., [15, 12, 34, 35]) by showing that robustness of support is a distinct property from distributional convergence, and relative to manifold-learning results ([47, 54]) by demonstrating that aligned GMs recover the full tangent bundle (not just the intrinsic dimension) of the data manifold. The alignment property is shown to be regular (Lemma 4.4), meaning approximate GMs retain robustness, which is stronger than prior results that only guarantee dimension recovery.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant line of work establishing convergence guarantees for SGMs and flow matching (e.g., [15, 12, 34, 35, 10]) by asking a different question: when the score/drift is learned with errors, does the predicted density remain supported on the data manifold? It builds on the manifold hypothesis literature [47, 13] which empirically observed support robustness but did not identify the dynamical mechanism, and repurposes finite-time Lyapunov vector analysis from fluid dynamics [50, 21, 33] and random dynamical systems theory [5, 28] to provide that mechanism. The Frobenius-Perron/transfer operator framework and the tangent dynamics formalism are standard tools from dynamical systems, but their application to characterizing generative model robustness is new.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a computable criterion (alignment of top Lyapunov vectors with the data manifold tangent space) for verifying robustness of a given generative model, and shows that aligned models can be used to extract the tangent bundle of the data manifold at the same sample complexity as generation. The alignment property is itself robust under small perturbations (Lemma 4.4), meaning approximate models retain the geometric information. Key limitations include that only sufficient (not necessary) conditions are provided, empirical validation is limited to 2D densities and MNIST, and the connection to memorization is not addressed. Open questions include the prevalence of alignment across model architectures and whether the framework extends to class-conditional or guided generation.
