## Problem

Semi-supervised histopathology segmentation suffers from topological errors (false merges and splits) that severely impact clinical reliability, yet existing topology-aware SSL methods like TopoSemiSeg rely on fixed, hand-picked persistence thresholds to identify meaningful structures. This is not data-driven and can exclude biologically relevant structures or retain noise. The paper asks how to adaptively and robustly identify reliable topological structures directly from perturbed model predictions without ground truth.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253528/cropped_page1_idx5.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 256×256 H&E-stained histopathology patches with only 10% or 20% pixel-wise annotations plus abundant unlabeled images, and produces binary segmentation masks. Unusually, the training pipeline generates multiple perturbed likelihood maps per input (4 MC dropout realizations per iteration and 4 temporal training snapshots), from which 0-D persistent homology features are extracted via super-level set filtration. The dual-level topological consistency losses are computed on these multi-facet predictions and directly optimize the student model, while the teacher (EMA of student) provides only the standard pixel-wise consistency target.

## Method

The core contribution is a dual-level topological consistency framework built on two novel matching algorithms. MATCH-Pair solves a Hungarian assignment problem between two persistence diagrams using a combined similarity metric of spatial IoU, normalized persistence weights, and Euclidean proximity of birth critical points, avoiding the spatial blindness of Wasserstein matching and the ground-truth dependence of Betti matching. MATCH-Global extends this to T facets by sequentially matching adjacent pairs and linking homologous components into globally consistent tracks via connected-component analysis on the resulting match graph. The dual-level consistency then classifies structures as matched (stable across facets) or unmatched (transient): matched structures are penalized for deviating from optimal probability values at their birth and death critical points, while unmatched structures are driven toward vanishing topological lifespan. This is applied at two levels—across MC dropout predictions within a single iteration (intra) and across consecutive training snapshots (temporal)—and integrated into a Mean Teacher SSL framework with UNet backbone.

## Results

On CRAG (20% labeled), MATCH achieves Dice_obj 0.909, Betti Error 0.188, BME 7.425, and DIU 40.250, outperforming the closest topology-aware baseline TopoSemiSeg (BE 0.226, BME 8.575, DIU 43.712) while matching its pixel-wise accuracy. On GlaS (20%), MATCH reduces BE to 0.392 and DIU to 26.175 versus TopoSemiSeg's 0.510 and 30.462. On MoNuSeg (20%), MATCH achieves BE 4.930 and DIU 982.286, again surpassing TopoSemiSeg (BE 5.150, DIU 1105.946). Across all three datasets and both 10% and 20% label ratios, MATCH consistently achieves the best topology-wise metrics with comparable or slightly lower pixel-wise Dice_obj.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MATCH outperforms all seven standard SSL baselines (MT, EM, UA-MT, URPC, XNet, PMT, TopoSemiSeg) on topology metrics across all three datasets. Against the closest competitor TopoSemiSeg [69], MATCH reduces Betti Error by 17% on CRAG (0.188 vs 0.226), 23% on GlaS (0.392 vs 0.510), and 4% on MoNuSeg (4.930 vs 5.150), while achieving comparable or slightly lower Dice_obj. The non-topology-aware baselines (MT, EM, UA-MT, URPC) are substantially worse on topology metrics (e.g., BE >1.8 on CRAG vs 0.188 for MATCH), confirming that explicit topological constraints are necessary. XNet and PMT, the strongest non-topology baselines, still trail MATCH by 2–3× on BE and BME. In ablation comparisons, MATCH-Pair surpasses both Wasserstein Matching [21] (BE 0.423) and Betti Matching [56] (BE 0.237) on CRAG. Against foundation models finetuned with LoRA on 20% CRAG data, MATCH (BE 0.188, BME 7.425) outperforms both LoRA-SAM (BE 0.440, BME 27.300) and LoRA-MedSAM (BE 0.268, BME 11.275), demonstrating that explicit topological modeling remains essential even with powerful pretrained backbones.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends TopoSemiSeg [69], which first introduced persistent-homology-based topological constraints into semi-supervised segmentation but relied on a fixed, hand-picked persistence threshold to identify meaningful structures. The key departure is replacing that threshold with an adaptive, data-driven matching strategy (MATCH-Pair and MATCH-Global) that identifies stable topological features by their consistency across multiple perturbed predictions (MC dropout realizations and temporal training snapshots), framed as a contrastive learning problem between stable and transient structures. The overall SSL architecture follows the Mean Teacher paradigm [57] with EMA-updated teacher, but the dual-level topological consistency losses (intra and temporal) directly optimize the student model rather than relying on the teacher's predictions for topological supervision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Topological errors (false merges and splits) in histopathology segmentation have direct clinical consequences for diagnosis and quantification, yet most SSL methods ignore them entirely. By eliminating the need for domain experts to select persistence thresholds, this work makes topology-aware SSL more practical and generalizable across tissue types and imaging conditions. Limitations include a primary focus on 0-D features (connected components), testing restricted to 2D histopathology, and a ~65% increase in per-iteration compute over TopoSemiSeg. Open questions include whether the adaptive matching approach scales to 3D volumetric data, whether it generalizes to non-histopathology domains with different topological error profiles, and whether the dual-level consistency can be made more computationally efficient for clinical deployment.
