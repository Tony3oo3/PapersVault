## Problem

Current LLM-based persona generation relies on ad hoc, heuristic techniques without methodological rigor or systematic evaluation of whether generated personas faithfully represent target populations. This paper reveals that these generation-stage biases—particularly a systematic leftward drift—can produce significant deviations from real-world outcomes in downstream simulations such as election forecasting and opinion surveys, yet no prior work has rigorously quantified this bias at the persona-generation step rather than the simulation step.

## Input / Output

The pipeline begins with census-derived joint distributions (age, sex, race, state) sampled at 1,000 personas per U.S. state, then progressively augments them with LLM-generated attributes across three tiers (objective tabular, subjective tabular, descriptive). Each persona is paired with a multiple-choice survey question and fed to an LLM in a single prompt, producing a forced-choice letter response; aggregating responses across ~1M personas yields population-level opinion distributions evaluated against ground truth via Wasserstein distance alignment scores.

## Method

The paper systematizes persona generation into four tiers—Meta (pure census sampling with no LLM), Objective Tabular (LLM fills predefined Census categories), Subjective Tabular (LLM generates open-ended attributes like political affiliation), and Descriptive (freeform LLM narrative)—and evaluates each across six open-source LLMs (Llama 3.1 8B/70B, Mistral 8x7B, Qwen 2.5B, Athene 70B, Nemetron 70B) on 2016/2020/2024 election simulations and 500 OpinionQA questions. Cross-model simulation, where personas generated by one LLM are simulated by another, confirms the bias is universal rather than model-specific. Sentiment analysis via TextBlob and word-cloud inspection of descriptive personas reveal that increased LLM generative freedom systematically introduces positive sentiment and optimistic framing, mechanistically explaining the observed leftward drift in simulated opinions. The authors open-source approximately one million generated personas as a community benchmark resource.

## Results

The paper's central quantitative finding is that alignment scores (1 − Wasserstein distance between simulated and real opinion distributions) decrease monotonically as the amount of LLM-generated content in personas increases, from Meta → Objective Tabular → Subjective Tabular → Descriptive. In the 2024 U.S. presidential election simulation, Meta Personas produce state-level voting distributions closest to reality, while Descriptive Personas yield a complete Democratic sweep of all 50 states — a dramatic leftward drift. This pattern replicates across the 2016 and 2020 elections and across all six LLMs tested (Llama 3.1 8B/70B, Mistral 8x7B, Qwen 2.5B, Nemetron 70B, Athene 70B), confirming the bias is universal rather than model-specific. On the 500-question OpinionQA benchmark, alignment variance among persona types is largest on controversial topics (race, gender, misinformation), and sentiment analysis of generated personas shows increasing positivity and subjectivity with more LLM-generated detail.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against external baseline methods; instead, it ablates the degree of LLM involvement in persona generation as the primary axis of comparison. Meta Personas (pure census sampling, no LLM) consistently achieve the highest alignment with ground-truth election results and OpinionQA responses, serving as the de facto best baseline. Objective Tabular Personas (LLM fills structured fields from predefined categories) show moderate degradation, while Subjective Tabular Personas (LLM adds open-ended fields like ideology, Big Five scores, quirks) and Descriptive Personas (fully free-form LLM narrative) show progressively worse alignment. The cross-model simulation (each persona set simulated by all six LLMs) demonstrates that no simulation model rescues the bias introduced at the persona-generation stage — the leftward drift persists regardless of which LLM performs the simulation. The one notable exception is the Yi-34B chat model, which exhibits a rightward rather than leftward bias, reinforcing that the direction of bias is model-dependent but the magnitude of deviation from ground truth is not.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the 'silicon sample' paradigm established by Argyle et al. (2023) and the digital-twin simulation framework of Park et al. (2024), as well as the structured persona generation approach of Castricato et al. (2024). However, it deliberately shifts the analytical lens from LLM simulation bias (well-studied in prior work) to bias introduced by the persona *generation* step itself, which prior work has largely taken for granted. The paper systematizes existing generation methods into a four-tier taxonomy (Meta → Objective Tabular → Subjective Tabular → Descriptive) and, for the first time, conducts a controlled ablation across these tiers using ~1M personas and six open-source LLMs to isolate the effect of LLM-generated content on downstream opinion fidelity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that increasing LLM-generated persona content systematically worsens representativeness (culminating in a full Democratic sweep in 2024 election simulations) directly undermines the validity of current 'silicon sample' deployments in social science, marketing, and policy research. The paper identifies several open questions: what attributes are truly essential for realistic simulation, how to calibrate generated personas to real joint distributions (beyond marginal census data), and how to build a large-scale open benchmark analogous to ImageNet for persona generation. The open-sourcing of ~1M personas provides an immediate practical resource, but the core methodological gap—principled calibration of LLM-generated personas to target populations—remains unsolved.
