## Problem

Existing uncertainty quantification methods for LLMs fall into two families—information-based (token probabilities) and consistency-based (semantic agreement among samples)—but prior attempts to combine them lack a principled theoretical foundation and often fail to consistently outperform simpler baselines. The paper asks whether a unified, theoretically grounded framework can merge both signals into a single, more reliable uncertainty measure.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253546/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The full CoCoA method consumes a text prompt, the LLM's token-level probability distribution for a greedy (or best-sample) output, and M stochastically sampled outputs (temperature 1.0, top-k 50) from which pairwise semantic similarities are computed via a RoBERTa-large cross-encoder. The CoCoA Light variant requires only the single greedy output and its middle-layer hidden-state embedding (16th layer for LLaMA/Mistral, 14th for Falcon), eliminating repeated sampling. In both cases the output is a single scalar uncertainty score per input, evaluated via Prediction Rejection Ratio (PRR) against task-specific quality metrics (Accuracy, AlignScore, COMET).

## Method

CoCoA defines a Minimum Bayes Risk function r(y, y′|x) = u(y|x)·(1 − s(y, y′)), where u is an information-based confidence measure (Sequence Probability, Perplexity, or Mean Token Entropy) and s is a semantic similarity score, yielding an uncertainty estimate that is the product of model confidence and average semantic inconsistency with M sampled outputs. The multiplicative form is derived from the MBR framework and is shown in ablations to outperform additive or pairwise-aggregated alternatives. CoCoA Light replaces the sampling-based consistency term with a lightweight MLP trained in a self-supervised fashion on unlabeled held-out inputs to map middle-layer LLM embeddings to consistency scores, matching the full method's quality while requiring only a single greedy generation at inference. The framework is evaluated across QA, summarization, and translation tasks on LLaMA 3.1 8B, Mistral 7B, and Falcon 3 7B, consistently outperforming Semantic Entropy, SAR, and other state-of-the-art UQ baselines.

## Results

Using Prediction Rejection Ratio (PRR, higher is better) under greedy decoding, CoCoA methods achieve the best scores across all three task families and all three models (Llama 3.1 8B, Mistral 7B, Falcon 3 7B). For example, CoCoA_PPL reaches 0.454 PRR on QA and 0.387 on summarization for Llama 8B, while CoCoA_MTE Light attains 0.533 on NMT for Falcon 7B. The CoCoA Light variants (which replace repeated sampling with a learned MLP on middle-layer embeddings) retain most of the gain, e.g., CoCoA_PPL Light scores 0.528 on NMT for Falcon 7B versus 0.508 for the full CoCoA_PPL. Improvements over the corresponding base information measures (SP, PPL, MTE) are consistent: every CoCoA variant outperforms its un-augmented counterpart by 0.02–0.13 PRR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoCoA methods dominate all five baseline families across every task and model. Against Semantic Entropy (Kuhn et al., 2023), CoCoA_PPL gains +0.098 PRR on Llama QA (0.454 vs. 0.356) and +0.100 on Falcon NMT (0.508 vs. 0.348). Against SAR (Duan et al., 2024), the advantage is +0.064 on Llama NMT (0.519 vs. 0.455) and +0.076 on Falcon NMT (0.533 vs. 0.458). Against the consistency-based DegMat and EigValLaplacian (Lin et al., 2024), CoCoA wins by 0.05–0.13 PRR on most cells, with the largest gap on summarization (e.g., CoCoA_PPL 0.387 vs. DegMat 0.081 on Llama SUM). Against the information-based baselines from Fomicheva et al. (2020) (SP, PPL, MTE), each CoCoA variant improves its own base by 0.02–0.13 PRR, confirming that the consistency component adds value beyond raw token probabilities. The verbalized-confidence baseline P(True) (Kadavath et al., 2022) performs worst, often yielding negative PRR (e.g., −0.064 on Llama QA), while CoCoA methods remain positive and strong across all settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the recent line of work connecting Minimum Bayes Risk (MBR) decoding to uncertainty quantification in NLP (Wang & Holmes 2025; Daheim et al. 2025), which established that MBR-based risk functions yield well-grounded uncertainty measures. Prior hybrid methods such as Semantic Entropy (Kuhn et al. 2023), SAR (Duan et al. 2024), and BSDetector (Chen & Mueller 2024) attempted to merge information-based confidence with output consistency, but the authors argue these lack a principled derivation from a risk function. CoCoA departs by deriving its multiplicative combination u(y|x)·(1−s(y,y′)) directly as an MBR risk, and introduces CoCoA Light, which approximates the consistency component via a learned function from internal embeddings—technique inspired by Kossen et al. (2024) but applied to the consistency term rather than full semantic entropy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CoCoA provides a theoretically grounded, plug-and-play framework for combining confidence and consistency that consistently outperforms both individual families and prior hybrids across QA, summarization, and translation. The CoCoA Light variant is practically significant because it eliminates the need for costly repeated sampling at inference, making principled UQ feasible in API-only or latency-sensitive settings. Open questions remain around task/domain sensitivity of the similarity function, the limited sample sizes used for consistency estimation, and whether the MBR-derived multiplicative form generalizes to settings where model probabilities are poorly calibrated (e.g., instruction-tuned or RLHF'd models).
