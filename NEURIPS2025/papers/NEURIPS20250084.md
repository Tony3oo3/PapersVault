## Problem

Existing temporal graph learning methods rely on memory and aggregation modules to encode interaction history, yet they struggle to generalize to unseen edges in real-world scenarios. The paper identifies two overlooked architectural gaps: the absence of unique node identifiers (limiting expressiveness when node/edge features are missing) and the lack of target-aware matching that directly assesses how well a candidate destination aligns with the source's recent interaction patterns.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250084/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Given a source node u, its k most recent neighbors (looked up via binary search over the temporal edge sequence), a set of q candidate destination nodes, and the prediction timestamp t, CRAFT produces a scalar likelihood score per destination for ranking. The source's neighbor embeddings (with learnable positional encoding) serve as keys and values in cross-attention, while candidate destination embeddings serve as queries; the final score is computed by an MLP over the concatenated edge representation and a linearly projected elapsed-time-since-last-activity vector for each destination.

## Method

CRAFT discards both memory and aggregation modules, replacing them with two components: (1) learnable node embeddings that act as unique identifiers, encoding contextual information from interaction history even in the absence of explicit features, and (2) a multi-layer cross-attention mechanism where each candidate destination attends to the source's recent neighbor sequence, enabling direct target-aware matching of destination compatibility with source context. Positional encoding is added to neighbor embeddings to capture temporal order, and elapsed-time encoding of destinations captures their current temporal status. For seen-dominant datasets, a repeat-time encoding is additionally concatenated before the prediction MLP. The model is trained end-to-end with BPR ranking loss, and its simpler architecture (only source neighbors needed, no per-destination neighbor retrieval) yields substantially lower time complexity than baselines that aggregate neighborhoods for both source and destination.

## Results

CRAFT achieves the best MRR on 14 out of 17 datasets, with relative improvements over the second-best baseline exceeding 10% on 10 of 17 datasets. On unseen-dominant datasets (Table 2), CRAFT leads with MRR of 35.91 on ML-20M, 70.68 on Taobao, 72.69 on Yelp, and 91.72 on tgbl-comment (a 20.4% relative gain over GraphMixer). On seen-dominant datasets (Table 3), CRAFT-R achieves 62.32 on mooc, 55.21 on lastfm, 88.47 on tgbl-coin, and 91.39 on tgbl-flights, with only minor underperformance (<2%) versus DyGFormer on three datasets. CRAFT also demonstrates superior efficiency, completing inference in under half the time of the fastest baseline (JODIE) on large datasets like Yelp and WikiLink.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CRAFT outperforms all seven temporal graph baselines (JODIE, DyRep, TGAT, TGN, CAWN, GraphMixer, DyGFormer) on the majority of unseen-dominant datasets, with the largest gains over TGN and GraphMixer (e.g., +11.9 MRR on ML-20M over SGNN-HN, +15.6 over GraphMixer on tgbl-comment). Against the recommendation baselines, CRAFT beats SGNN-HN on 7 of 9 unseen-dominant datasets (losing narrowly on GoogleLocal by 0.53 and YouTube by 0.72) and SASRec on all bipartite datasets tested in the appendix. On seen-dominant datasets, CRAFT-R dominates with large margins (e.g., +20.1 MRR over SGNN-HN on mooc, +12.9 over SGNN-HN on tgbl-coin), but DyGFormer edges out CRAFT-R by <2% on wikipedia (88.69 vs. 88.25), uci (76.61 vs. 75.11), and Flights (84.13 vs. 83.16), likely due to DyGFormer's neighbor co-occurrence frequency heuristic. The ablation in Section 5.2 further shows that adding learnable embeddings to TGAT and DyGFormer (TGAT-LE, DyGFormer-LE) yields up to 5× gains on Yelp, yet both still underperform CRAFT, confirming that cross-attention-based target-aware matching is the critical differentiator beyond node identifiers alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

CRAFT deliberately departs from the dominant memory-aggregation architecture that underpins virtually all prior temporal graph learning methods (TGN, JODIE, DyRep, TGAT, GraphMixer, DyGFormer). Rather than compressing interaction history into a recurrent memory state and aggregating neighborhood information via graph attention, CRAFT replaces both with two simpler components: learnable node embeddings as unique identifiers and a cross-attention mechanism between the candidate destination and the source's recent neighbors. This design draws on the Transformer cross-attention paradigm from NLP and connects to GNN expressiveness results (e.g., Abboud et al. showing that random node embeddings outperform structure-only models), repurposing these ideas for the temporal link prediction setting where node features are typically absent.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper challenges a foundational assumption in temporal graph learning—that memory and aggregation modules are necessary—by showing that a minimal architecture with learnable embeddings and target-aware cross-attention outperforms complex baselines across 17 datasets while being dramatically more efficient (e.g., 0.6h vs. >24h per epoch on WikiLink). This has direct practical implications for large-scale deployment where fast inference over millions of candidate edges is critical. A key limitation is that CRAFT is primarily transductive: it relies on historical interactions to learn meaningful node embeddings, leaving the cold-start/inductive setting (new nodes with no history) unaddressed. Open questions include whether the cross-attention design generalizes to heterogeneous temporal graphs with rich node/edge features, and whether the efficiency advantage persists under stricter inductive evaluation protocols.
