## Problem

The literature on ε-global differentially private stochastic bandits has a significant gap between the best-known regret lower and upper bounds: they match only in order but differ in constants, with the lower bound involving a min{KL, εΔ} term and upper bounds involving min{Δ², εΔ}. The paper asks whether matching bounds up to the same constant are achievable, and whether the 'reward-forgetting' design technique (used by all prior DP bandit algorithms) is fundamentally necessary for optimal regret.

## Input / Output

The algorithms take as input a privacy budget ε > 0, number of arms K, horizon T, and a geometric batch-size sequence {B_m} with ratio α > 1. At each step the policy observes a single Bernoulli reward (0 or 1) for the pulled arm. The output is a sequence of arm actions (a_1, …, a_T) satisfying ε-global DP, with performance measured by cumulative regret. A key structural detail is that the algorithm maintains cumulative noisy reward sums across all phases (no forgetting), so the number of Laplace noise terms grows with the number of phases for each arm, and the batch sizes grow geometrically to keep this noise sublinear in T.

## Method

The core technical contribution is a new information-theoretic quantity d_ε(x,y) = inf_{z∈[x∧y, x∨y]} {ε|z−x| + kl(z,y)} that smoothly interpolates between KL divergence (low-privacy regime) and TV distance (high-privacy regime), yielding a tighter regret lower bound via a 'double change of environment' proof technique. A new concentration inequality (a DP version of the Chernoff bound) shows that for sums of n Bernoullis plus m Laplace(1/ε) noises with m/n = o(1), the tail probability is governed by d_ε in the exponent, with the effect of m noises comparable to a single noise. Building on this, DP-KLUCB and DP-IMED run in arm-dependent phases with geometrically increasing batch sizes (ratio α), add one Laplace(1/ε) per phase to the cumulative reward sum (without forgetting prior rewards), and use d_ε-based exploration indexes. This eliminates the need for reward-forgetting while achieving regret upper bounds that match the lower bound up to the constant α, which can be set arbitrarily close to 1.

## Results

The paper proves matching asymptotic regret upper and lower bounds for ε-global DP Bernoulli bandits, both expressed in terms of a new information-theoretic quantity d_ε that interpolates between KL divergence and Total Variation distance. The upper bound for DP-KLUCB and DP-IMED is α·Σ Δ_a log(T)/d_ε(μ_a, μ*), where α > 1 is the geometric batch ratio and can be set arbitrarily close to 1, matching the lower bound Σ Δ_a log(T)/d_ε(μ_a, μ*) up to this constant. Experimentally, on 5-arm Bernoulli environments with ε = 0.25 and T = 10^6, DP-KLUCB and DP-IMED achieve up to 10× lower regret than existing ε-global DP algorithms (DP-SE, AdaP-KLUCB, Lazy-DP-TS) on average across 100 runs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares DP-KLUCB and DP-IMED against three existing ε-global DP bandit algorithms and one non-private benchmark. Against DP-SE (Sajed & Sheffet, 2019), AdaP-KLUCB (Azize & Basu, 2022), and Lazy-DP-TS (Hu & Hegde, 2022), both proposed algorithms achieve strictly lower regret across all four 5-arm Bernoulli environments tested, with improvements of up to 10× on average at ε = 0.25. The key advantage comes from (a) not forgetting rewards between phases (all prior algorithms discard past samples) and (b) using tighter d_ε-based exploration indexes instead of the looser min{Δ_a², εΔ_a} or min{kl(μ_a,μ*), 6εΔ_a} terms. Against the non-private IMED benchmark (Honda & Takemura, 2015), the DP versions incur additional regret due to Laplace noise but remain asymptotically optimal. Theoretically, the new lower bound (Theorem 1) strictly improves upon Azize & Basu [2022] since d_ε(μ_a, μ*) ≤ min{kl(μ_a,μ*), εΔ_a} ≤ min{kl(μ_a,μ*), 6εΔ_a}, closing the gap between upper and lower bounds that was previously only matched in order.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the line of DP bandit algorithms starting from DP-UCB (Mishra and Thakurta, 2015) through DP-SE (Sajed and Sheffet, 2019), AdaP-KLUCB (Azize and Basu, 2022), and Lazy-DP-TS (Hu and Hegde, 2022), all of which share a common blueprint of arm-dependent phases, reward-forgetting, and Laplace noise. The key departure is the introduction of d_ε, a new information-theoretic quantity that smoothly interpolates between KL divergence and Total Variation distance, which simultaneously tightens the regret lower bound (via a novel 'double change of environment' proof technique) and enables new private indexes for DP-KLUCB and DP-IMED. Crucially, the paper refutes the conjecture of Hu et al. (2021) that forgetting is necessary for optimal DP bandit algorithms, by showing that a coupled concentration inequality for Bernoulli sums plus Laplace noise (Proposition 1) allows all rewards to be retained across phases while still achieving matching upper and lower bounds.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper closes a significant gap in the DP bandits literature by providing matching regret upper and lower bounds up to a constant arbitrarily close to 1, resolving an open problem posed by Azize and Basu (2022). The finding that forgetting is not a fundamental design requirement challenges a working assumption in the field and opens the door to simpler algorithm designs. However, the results are currently restricted to Bernoulli bandits; extending the concentration inequality and matching bounds to general distribution families (sub-Gaussian, exponential families) remains an open direction. The d_ε quantity and the coupled noise-signal concentration technique are broadly useful tools for the DP literature beyond bandits.
