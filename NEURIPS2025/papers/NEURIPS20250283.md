## Problem

Causal discovery in multivariate time series that exhibit multiple unknown regimes (non-stationarity) and complex noise distributions (non-Gaussian or heteroscedastic) remains unsolved. Existing multi-regime methods like CASTOR assume Gaussian noise, stationary methods like Rhino and DYNOTEARS assume homoscedasticity, and no prior approach can simultaneously infer the number of regimes, their boundaries, and per-regime causal DAGs under either non-Gaussian or heteroscedastic noise.

## Input / Output

FANTOM takes a single multivariate time series of d components and length |T|, a maximum lag L, and a minimum regime duration ζ as input; it does not require the number of regimes K a priori. It outputs the number of regimes K, the regime index sets (which time points belong to which regime), a temporal causal DAG per regime containing both instantaneous and time-lagged directed edges, and per-time-step regime membership probabilities. The initialization divides the series into N_w > K equal windows (each longer than ζ), which are then iteratively merged or pruned until convergence.

## Method

FANTOM formulates a general SEM where each regime r has its own mixing function f^{i,r}, a strictly positive heteroscedastic scaling g^{i,r}, and an arbitrary noise density ε^{i,r}, then optimizes the ELBO via a Bayesian EM loop. The E-step updates regime membership probabilities β_t^r using a time-varying softmax prior π_t(ω^r) and the per-regime likelihood computed by conditional spline flows (CNFs) that transform heteroscedastic noise to a fixed standard normal. The M-step performs Bayesian structure learning over each regime's DAG using Gumbel-Softmax reparameterization of Bernoulli edge variables, a Temporal Graph Neural Network (TGNN) with weight-shared embeddings for the mixing function, and an augmented Lagrangian for the acyclicity constraint. A key initialization trick seeds N_w > K equal windows (creating both pure and impure regimes), after which the E-step progressively reassigns boundary samples and prunes regimes with fewer than ζ samples until N_w converges to K. The paper proves identifiability of the temporal heteroscedastic model in the stationary case (Theorem 4.2) and of the full multi-regime mixture in the non-stationary case (Theorem 4.3), up to permutation and translation.

## Results

On synthetic MTS with d=10 nodes and K=3 regimes, FANTOM achieves near-perfect regime detection (99.4% accuracy under non-Gaussian noise, 97.1% under heteroscedastic noise) and 99.5% F1 on instantaneous links in the non-Gaussian setting. Under heteroscedastic noise, FANTOM reaches 93.3% F1 on instantaneous links and 90.9% on lagged links, a 60% improvement over the second-best method on instantaneous edges. On the real-world Wind Tunnel (Causal Chambers) dataset, FANTOM attains 99.9% regime accuracy and 38.5% F1 on the summary causal graph, outperforming all baselines even when they are given ground-truth regime partitions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FANTOM decisively outperforms all six baselines in the heteroscedastic noise setting, where most competitors collapse: it achieves 93.3% F1 on instantaneous links versus 5.1% for Rhino, 31.5% for DYNOTEARS, 23.4% for CASTOR, and 11.1% for PCMCI+. In the homoscedastic non-Gaussian setting, FANTOM leads on instantaneous links (99.5% F1) and regime detection (99.4% accuracy vs. 51.6% for CASTOR with ground-truth splits), though Rhino—given ground-truth regime labels—slightly edges it on lagged links (95.2% vs. 89.1% F1). CD-NOD, which outputs only a summary graph, is far behind FANTOM's summary-graph equivalent (31.8% vs. 95.6% F1 in non-Gaussian; 6.2% vs. 96.5% in heteroscedastic). RPCMCI fails to converge in the non-Gaussian setting and cannot handle heteroscedasticity. On the real-world Wind Tunnel data, FANTOM (38.5% F1, 99.9% regime accuracy) surpasses Rhino (32.0% F1), PCMCI+ (22.9%), CD-NOD (20.0%), and CASTOR (19.5% even with ground-truth splits), confirming its advantage on practical non-stationary data.

## Relation to Prior Work

FANTOM extends the multi-regime causal discovery line initiated by CASTOR (the authors' own prior work), CD-NOD, and RPCMCI by introducing a general SEM that accommodates both non-Gaussian and heteroscedastic noise, which none of these methods handle. It builds on the Bayesian structure learning framework of Rhino and SCOTCH for stationary MTS, and on conditional spline flows (Durkan et al.) to model complex noise distributions, but departs from them by coupling these flows with a Bayesian EM procedure that simultaneously infers regime count, boundaries, and per-regime DAGs. The identifiability proofs extend the temporal heteroscedastic noise model results of Immer et al. and Strobl et al. to the multi-regime setting with time-varying mixture weights.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Real-world time series in neuroscience, finance, and climate science routinely exhibit both regime shifts and heteroscedastic or non-Gaussian noise, yet no prior method could jointly address all three challenges; FANTOM closes this gap with provable identifiability. Key limitations include the non-convex BEM objective (no convergence rates or finite-sample bounds), sensitivity to initialization window size (over-pruning can destroy performance), and degraded accuracy when regimes contain very few samples. Open questions include whether the identifiability guarantees extend to more complex regime dynamics (e.g., overlapping or gradual transitions) and whether the approach scales to hundreds of variables without prohibitive compute.
