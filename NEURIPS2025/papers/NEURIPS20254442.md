## Problem

Parallel diffusion inference across multiple accelerators is bottlenecked by inter-device communication of large activation tensors, which can consume over 45% of inference time on PCIe interconnects. Prior methods (DistriFusion, PipeFusion) mitigate this via displaced parallelism—reusing stale activations to overlap communication with computation—but this introduces quality degradation, leaves the actual data volume unchanged, and requires nontrivial pipeline rework. The paper asks whether temporal redundancy in diffusion activations can be exploited more fundamentally by eliminating redundant data from the communication path entirely rather than merely hiding its transfer cost.

## Input / Output

The system operates as a drop-in compression layer over standard communication primitives (send/recv, AllGather) in existing parallel diffusion frameworks (xDiT, distrifuser). It intercepts activation tensors (K/V shards in attention layers) during sequence-parallel execution of FLUX.1-dev (1024×1024, 28 denoising steps) or CogVideoX-2b (50 steps), compresses the step-wise residual before transmission, and decompresses on the receiving device. A single warmup step transmits the full uncompressed activation to initialize the local base tensor; all subsequent steps transmit only compressed residuals, reducing payload from ~60 GB per image per GPU to under 1% in the low-rank variant.

## Method

CompactFusion transmits compressed activation residuals (differences between consecutive denoising steps) instead of full activations, exploiting the observation that adjacent-step activations are highly similar and residuals have much smaller magnitude. A lightweight error feedback mechanism stores the per-step compression error locally and adds it to the next residual before compression, preventing long-term drift; a theoretical steady-state error bound (Proposition 3.1) shows the residual+feedback error is lower than naive compression by a factor of σ_Δ²/σ_a² under L-smoothness. Three interchangeable compression backends are provided: element-wise quantization (1-bit/2-bit with rank-1 scale estimation), low-rank approximation via subspace iteration with INT4 quantization (enabling >100× compression by trading per-step precision for broader directional coverage), and N:M block sparsity. The design is structurally decoupled from the parallel pipeline—wrapping standard communication primitives with fewer than 20 lines of core code changed—making it portable across parallel strategies (Patch Parallel, Ring Attention, Ulysses) and hardware (NVLink, PCIe, Ethernet).

## Results

On 4×L20 (PCIe) with FLUX.1-dev at 1024×1024, Compact-2bit achieves a 3.06× speedup over single-device inference (7.57s vs 23.16s) while delivering FID of 3.26 and LPIPS of 0.114 (vs. ground-truth COCO images), substantially outperforming DistriFusion (FID 9.91, LPIPS 0.310) and PipeFusion (FID 6.72, LPIPS 0.250). Compact-1bit reaches 3.10× speedup (7.46s) with FID 7.08. Under simulated Ethernet bandwidth on 4×A40, CompactFusion uniquely enables sequence parallelism with up to 6.7× speedup over DistriFusion, where all other baselines fail to achieve any speedup.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DistriFusion (displaced patch parallel), Compact-2bit improves FID from 9.91 to 3.26 (3× lower), LPIPS from 0.310 to 0.114, and PSNR from 21.63 to 29.54, while also reducing latency from 8.05s to 7.57s on L20; Compact-1bit similarly beats DistriFusion on FID (7.08 vs 9.91) and latency (7.46s vs 8.05s). Against PipeFusion (displaced TeraPipe), Compact-2bit wins on every quality metric (FID 3.26 vs 6.72, LPIPS 0.114 vs 0.250, PSNR 29.54 vs 23.42) and cuts latency by 20% (7.57s vs 9.49s on L20). Against the non-displaced baselines (Ring Attention, Patch Parallel, DeepSpeed-Ulysses), Compact-2bit achieves the lowest latency (7.57s vs 10.89s/10.90s/9.13s on L20) while being the only method that reports quality metrics, since the others transmit full activations without compression. On the extreme-bandwidth Ethernet setting (4×A40), CompactFusion is the only method that achieves a speedup over single-device, reaching 6.7× over DistriFusion, whereas all other baselines fail to beat single-device latency. The Compact-Lowrank variant (100.05× compression) trades speed for extreme bandwidth reduction, achieving lower speedup (2.18× on L20) but still outperforming DistriFusion on FID (8.68 vs 9.91) while transmitting less than 1% of original activation data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the displaced-parallelism paradigm established by DistriFusion and PipeFusion, which exploit temporal redundancy by reusing stale activations to overlap communication with computation. Instead of hiding the cost of transmitting redundant data, CompactFusion eliminates it at the source by transmitting only compressed step-wise residuals, borrowing the error-feedback mechanism from gradient compression (Karimireddy et al., Lin et al.) and adapting subspace iteration from PowerSGD for extreme-ratio low-rank compression. The method is applied within the sequence-parallelism framework (Ring Attention, Patch Parallel, DeepSpeed-Ulysses) that dominates current parallel diffusion inference, and integrates as a drop-in wrapper over standard communication primitives in xDiT and distrifuser.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CompactFusion addresses a growing practical bottleneck: as diffusion models scale (FLUX at 12B+ parameters) and compute outpaces interconnect bandwidth, communication increasingly dominates parallel inference cost. The demonstration that >100× compression (transmitting <1% of original data) maintains quality superior to state-of-the-art displaced methods, and that sequence parallelism becomes viable on Ethernet-level bandwidth (6.7× speedup over DistriFusion), opens deployment on cost-constrained hardware. Limitations include compression overhead that erodes gains on fast interconnects (NVLink) and the slower subspace iteration in the low-rank variant. Open questions include whether the L-smoothness assumption (L<1) holds universally across architectures and whether the approach extends to other parallel dimensions (pipeline, tensor parallelism) or to training-time communication.
