## Problem

Unified Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) consistently exhibit stronger understanding capabilities than generation capabilities, yet no prior work has explicitly identified or addressed this gap. While recent studies (Liquid, MetaMorph) demonstrated that understanding and generation are synergistic at the data level, they failed to consider the capability imbalance between the two, leaving it unclear whether and how to bridge this disparity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252082/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework operates on homologous (image, caption/prompt) pairs, where the same pair serves dual roles: the image is the input for understanding preference curation (producing n candidate captions scored by BERT similarity against the ground-truth caption), and the caption/prompt is the input for generation preference curation (producing n candidate images scored by self-VQA accuracy using TIFA-generated questions). The final training signal is a six-tuple (x, y, x_w, x_l, y_w, y_l) pairing the winning and losing samples from both modalities under the same semantic context, with no external supervision beyond the model's own outputs.

## Method

HermesFlow introduces Pair-DPO, a preference optimization objective that jointly optimizes understanding and generation by taking the product of the two DPO log-likelihood ratios (Δ_Und · Δ_Gen) inside a single sigmoid, rather than summing two independent DPO losses. This coupling ensures that the model only receives a strong gradient signal when both modalities simultaneously improve, effectively enforcing mutual alignment. Preference data is curated entirely from the model's own outputs: understanding pairs are selected via BERT similarity between generated captions and the reference caption, while generation pairs are selected via a self-critic VQA scoring scheme where the MLLM answers TIFA-generated questions about its own generated images. A self-play iterative optimization loop then updates preference pairs across rounds: if the model's new best sample exceeds the previous round's winner, the bar is raised; otherwise, the new best replaces the loser to provide a simpler learning gradient, enabling progressive self-improvement without any external high-quality data.

## Results

On visual generation, HermesFlow (1.3B) achieves a GenEval Overall score of 0.69 and DPG-Bench average of 70.22, surpassing its backbone Show-o (0.64 / 67.48) and the diffusion model SD 2.1 (0.49 / 68.09). On multimodal understanding, it improves over Show-o across all six benchmarks (e.g., POPE 81.4 vs 80.0, MME 1249.7 vs 1232.9, MMMU 28.3 vs 27.4). Most notably, the understanding–generation gap (measured via a homologous VQA pipeline) shrinks from 0.087 (Show-o) to 0.036, while both capabilities improve simultaneously. On MME-Unify, HermesFlow scores 14.01 overall, outperforming Show-o (12.74) and the 8B Emu3 (13.79).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HermesFlow outperforms its backbone Show-o on every reported metric: GenEval Overall 0.69 vs 0.64, DPG-Bench 70.22 vs 67.48, FID 9.07 vs 9.24, CLIP-Score 31.08 vs 30.63, and all six understanding benchmarks (e.g., POPE 81.4 vs 80.0, MMMU 28.3 vs 27.4). Against Janus (1.3B), HermesFlow wins on GenEval Overall (0.69 vs 0.61) and DPG-Bench (70.22 vs not reported), while Janus retains a higher Position sub-score (0.46 vs 0.32). Compared to the 7B VILA-U, HermesFlow achieves a much smaller understanding–generation gap (0.036 vs 0.169) but a lower absolute understanding score (0.533 vs 0.646), reflecting VILA-U's larger parameter budget. Against diffusion baselines, HermesFlow surpasses SD 2.1 (GenEval 0.69 vs 0.49, DPG-Bench 70.22 vs 68.09) and DALL·E 2 (0.69 vs 0.52) despite using far fewer parameters. On MME-Unify, HermesFlow (1.3B) beats the 8B Emu3 on understanding average (34.27 vs 33.19) and overall MME-U score (14.01 vs 13.79), though Emu3 retains a higher generation TIG score (49.08 vs 46.48). The ablation in Table 5 further shows that a single round of Pair-DPO already outperforms single-modality DPO on both understanding and generation, confirming the synergy between the two tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Show-o as its backbone MLLM and on DPO as its optimization primitive, but departs from prior DPO-based multimodal alignment work (e.g., CSR for understanding, Emu3 for generation) that optimizes only one modality at a time. It extends the empirical synergy findings of Liquid and MetaMorph—which showed that understanding and generation data mutually benefit each other—by explicitly formulating the asymmetry between the two as a 'gap' to be closed, and introduces Pair-DPO (a product of two DPO terms over homologous preference pairs) plus self-play iterative optimization as the mechanism. The conceptual lineage is: unified autoregressive MLLMs → single-modality DPO → joint paired-preference DPO with iterative self-improvement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The systematic observation that MLLMs consistently exhibit stronger understanding than generation, quantified via a GPT-4o-judged VQA pipeline, provides a useful diagnostic for the field and motivates balanced post-training. However, the practical impact is currently limited: experiments are confined to a single 1.3B backbone (Show-o) with only 5,000 homologous pairs, and the absolute gains (e.g., GenEval 0.64→0.69, DPG-Bench 67.5→70.2) are modest. Open questions include whether Pair-DPO generalizes to larger or diffusion-based backbones (the authors mention MMaDA as a future target), whether the gap-closing effect persists at scale, and whether the GPT-4o-based gap metric is a reliable proxy for true capability balance.
