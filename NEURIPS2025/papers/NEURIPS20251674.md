## Problem

Existing keyframe interpolation methods that leverage pre-trained video diffusion priors condition solely on input keyframes without intermediate geometric guidance, causing them to produce implausible or distorted results for complex articulated human motions involving large occlusions and non-rigid joint movements. While 2D skeleton-based control (e.g., FCVG) offers some structural cues, it cannot convey full body shape and 3D geometry, limiting both fidelity and controllability of the synthesized dynamics.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251674/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of human-centric keyframes (start and end) along with per-frame 2D DWPose keypoints and 3D SMPL-X parameters (pose θ, shape β, expression ψ) as control signals, and produces the intermediate video frames between them. During training, clips are cropped around the largest human bounding box and resized to 512×320; at inference the system supports up to 1024×576 resolution over 25-frame sequences. The SMPL-X model is first forwarded to obtain 3D vertex and joint coordinates, which are then projected to 2D to establish pixel-level correspondences with the video latent space.

## Method

PoseFuse3D-KI integrates a novel 3D-informed control model, PoseFuse3D, into a pre-trained Wan2.1 video diffusion model (14B DiT with causal 3D-VAE) via cross-normalization after the first denoising block, following a ControlNeXt-inspired injection strategy. PoseFuse3D comprises three jointly trained modules: (1) a visual encoding module with two parallel convolutional encoders processing DWPose visualizations and SMPL-X colored-surface renderings; (2) a SMPL-X encoder that operates directly in 3D space—extracting vertex and joint coordinates via the SMPL-X forward pass, encoding them with MLPs and temporal residual blocks, then aggregating 3D features onto the 2D image plane through separate joint and vertex attention mechanisms; and (3) a coarse-to-fine condition fusion module using two attention blocks (self-attention on 3D features, cross-attention with shifted window-partition for 2D–3D spatial alignment, and temporal self-attention). The entire framework is fine-tuned end-to-end with AdamW (lr 8×10⁻⁵) and LoRA (rank 32) on the VDM's input patch embeddings and attention projections, trained for 70k iterations on the newly constructed CHKI-Video dataset of 2,614 clips with full 2D pose and SMPL-X annotations.

## Results

On the CHKI-Video benchmark, PoseFuse3D-KI achieves 22.27 dB PSNR and 0.1304 LPIPS on whole-image metrics, outperforming the strongest baseline FCVG by 1.85 dB in PSNR and 0.0796 in LPIPS (a 9% PSNR improvement and 38% LPIPS reduction). On human-centric metrics, it reaches 19.49 dB PSNR_bbox, 18.24 dB PSNR_mask, 0.0450 LPIPS_mask, and 0.9189 HA score. The method also maintains strong performance across temporal gaps, achieving 23.86 dB PSNR at 24 frames and 19.44 dB PSNR even at 96 frames.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against FCVG (CVPR 2025), the closest competitor that also uses intermediate control signals, PoseFuse3D-KI wins on every metric: +1.85 dB PSNR, +1.44 dB PSNR_bbox, +1.33 dB PSNR_mask, and −0.0796 LPIPS (whole image), with the gap widening on human-centric LPIPS_mask (0.0450 vs. 0.0606, a 26% reduction). Against GIMM-VFI (NeurIPS 2024), a traditional flow-based interpolation method, PoseFuse3D-KI improves PSNR by 1.98 dB and LPIPS by 0.0650, while also achieving a higher HA score (0.9189 vs. 0.9146). Against GI (ICLR 2025), a control-free keyframe interpolation method, the margin is largest: +6.46 dB PSNR and −0.2060 LPIPS, reflecting GI's difficulty with complex articulated human motion without intermediate guidance. In the in-the-wild setting (no ground-truth controls, using linearly interpolated SMPL-X joints), PoseFuse3D-KI still outperforms FCVG by 4.5% in PSNR and 22.1% in LPIPS. On the easier FCVG-Test-HC benchmark, PoseFuse3D-KI achieves a 7.6% PSNR_mask improvement and 31% LPIPS_mask reduction over FCVG, though traditional methods like GIMM-VFI score higher on HA due to their reliance on input keyframe textures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of keyframe interpolation (GI, ViBiDSampler, FCVG) and pose-guided human animation (Champ, Animate Anyone, RealisDance), bringing 3D-informed control into the interpolation setting. It departs from Champ's approach of rendering SMPL-X into 2D proxies (normals, depth, colored surfaces) and fusing them via simple summation; instead, PoseFuse3D encodes 3D geometry directly in 3D space and projects features onto the 2D plane through attention, preserving occluded structural information. The control injection mechanism follows ControlNeXt's cross-normalization strategy, and the base interpolation engine adapts Wan2.1's flow-matching DiT with LoRA fine-tuning, extending the single-forward-path design from prior I2V-based interpolators.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The CHKI-Video dataset (2,614 clips, 180K+ frames with 2D poses, SMPL-X parameters, masks, and bounding boxes) fills a clear gap: no prior interpolation benchmark provides the 3D human annotations needed to evaluate controllable human-centric interpolation. The 38% LPIPS reduction over FCVG demonstrates that explicit 3D geometric guidance is substantially more informative than 2D skeleton or rendered-proxy controls for articulated motion. Key limitations remain: the method inherits the accuracy ceiling of SMPL-X estimators, does not model human-object interactions, and the in-the-wild pipeline relies on linear joint interpolation that cannot capture acceleration or multi-step dynamics. Open questions include whether text-to-motion models can replace linear interpolation for realistic in-the-wild control, and whether the 3D-informed control paradigm extends to multi-person or human-scene interaction scenarios.
