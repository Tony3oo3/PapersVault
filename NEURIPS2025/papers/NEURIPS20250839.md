## Problem

Safe RL methods deployed in real-world control face multiple correlated uncertainty sources (sensor noise, actuator delays, parameter drift) whose joint effects are often super-additive, yet existing robust approaches either assume independent perturbations, reduce to overly conservative worst-case min-max optimization, or require careful per-source parameter tuning. No prior work has leveraged fuzzy measure theory to model non-additive uncertainty interactions within the constrained MDP (CMDP) framework for safe RL.

## Input / Output

The framework consumes state vectors from control environments (e.g., cart position/velocity, pole angle, quadrotor pose) and applies stratified Gaussian perturbations at K discrete uncertainty levels (typically K=10–15) to generate perturbed state copies for value estimation. It outputs a safe action policy via a primal-dual Lagrangian update, along with Choquet-integrated robust reward and cost value estimates that replace standard Bellman backups. The fuzzy density parameters g_k are learned by a small MLP (two hidden layers) with softmax output, and the interaction parameter λ is solved per-state via a bisection-Newton root-finding step, making the uncertainty model state-dependent rather than fixed.

## Method

Fuz-RL introduces a Fuzzy Bellman Operator that replaces the standard expectation over next states with a Choquet integral of the value function with respect to a convex λ-fuzzy measure over K partitioned uncertainty levels. The key theoretical result (Theorem 4.4) proves that this CMDP-form problem is exactly equivalent to a distributionally robust CMDP: the Choquet integral over a convex fuzzy measure equals the minimum over its core (probability measures dominating the fuzzy measure), so the min-max robust objective is recovered without explicit adversarial optimization. Practically, the Choquet integral is approximated by sorting K perturbed value estimates and weighting them by differences of the λ-fuzzy measure over tail sets (descending for rewards, ascending for costs via the dual measure m′). The framework is model-free and modular: it plugs into any existing safe RL algorithm (PPOL, CUP, CPPO) by swapping the value-network TD targets with Choquet-integrated ones, while the fuzzy network is updated at a lower frequency against Monte Carlo return targets to stabilize the uncertainty structure.

## Results

Across 36 Fuz-RL-based experiments on Safe-Control-Gym (4 tasks × 3 uncertainty types), Fuz-RL achieves superior safety (lower AvgRisk) in 94.9% of cases and robust performance (higher AvgRet) in 88.9% of tasks compared to the corresponding base safe RL algorithms. Against the SOTA robust safe RL method RAMU, Fuz-RL attains higher AvgRet in 83.3% of tasks and lower AvgRisk in 52.8%. As a concrete example, Fuz-CUP on CartPole-Stab under observation uncertainty achieves 40±19 AvgRet (vs. 32±26 for CUP, a 61.4% improvement) and 0.26±0.09 AvgRisk (vs. 0.29±0.11, a 16.7% reduction). Fuz-RL also reduces variance in AvgRet by 20.7%, 9.9%, and 8.6% (observation, action, dynamics) and in AvgRisk by 13.2%, 7.1%, and 22.6% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to PPOL, Fuz-PPOL consistently improves AvgRet across all four tasks under observation uncertainty (e.g., +21 on CartPole-Track, +25 on Quadrotor-Stab) while reducing AvgRisk in most settings (e.g., 0.28 vs. 0.48 on Quadrotor-Track). Against CUP, the gains are most dramatic: Fuz-CUP achieves 61.4% higher AvgRet and 16.7% lower AvgRisk on CartPole-Stab, and 45-point AvgRet improvements on CartPole-Track and Quadrotor-Track under action uncertainty. Versus CPPO, Fuz-CPPO delivers substantial AvgRisk reductions (e.g., 0.32 vs. 0.47 on CartPole-Stab, 0.47 vs. 0.77 on Quadrotor-Track) while matching or exceeding AvgRet. Against the SOTA robust method RAMU, Fuz-RL wins on AvgRet in 83.3% of all 36 task-uncertainty combinations and on AvgRisk in 52.8%; notably, Fuz-RL dominates RAMU in every single Action Uncertainty task, where RAMU's conservative Wang-transform approach sacrifices reward to reduce risk. The paper also validates on Safety-Gymnasium (4 tasks) and an IEEE 39-bus power system, where Fuz-PPOL achieves 11.6% higher returns and 17.6% lower risk under observation noise compared to PPOL.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate research threads: distributionally robust safe RL (which handles uncertainty via min-max optimization over Wasserstein or KL ambiguity sets) and fuzzy measure theory (which models non-additive, super-additive interactions among uncertainty sources). The key conceptual departure is showing that a λ-fuzzy measure with Choquet-integrated value functions is mathematically equivalent to a distributionally robust CMDP, thereby replacing an intractable min-max problem with a tractable CMDP solved via standard value iteration. The framework is designed as a plug-in layer over existing safe RL algorithms (PPO-Lagrangian, CUP, CPPO), building on the m_λ measure from Guo & Wang (2018) and the Choquet integral representation of Gilboa & Schmeidler (1994), while competing with the recent RAMU approach (Queeney & Benosman, NeurIPS 2024) that uses Wang transforms for risk-averse robustness.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The equivalence theorem provides a principled, interpretable alternative to worst-case robust optimization that avoids the conservatism of min-max while still guaranteeing robustness, which is practically valuable for safety-critical control where overly conservative policies sacrifice performance. The plug-in design means practitioners can adopt the fuzzy Bellman operator without rewriting their safe RL pipeline. However, the acknowledged scalability limitation in high-dimensional state spaces (the K-level perturbation sampling and fuzzy network add per-step overhead) and the mixed results against RAMU on risk metrics (winning only 52.8% of risk comparisons) suggest the approach is best suited to moderate-dimensional control tasks. Open questions include handling non-stationary uncertainty distributions and whether the learned fuzzy densities generalize across tasks or require retraining.
