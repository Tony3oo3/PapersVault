## Problem

Existing test-time adaptation (TTA) research for vision-language models suffers from inconsistent experimental settings, reliance on unreproduced baseline numbers, and an almost exclusive focus on classification accuracy, making fair comparisons between methods impossible and obscuring trade-offs in model trustworthiness. No prior work has systematically evaluated TTA methods across diverse VLM architectures (beyond CLIP), training-time fine-tuned models, and multiple quality dimensions such as calibration, OOD detection, and adversarial robustness.

## Input / Output

For episodic TTA, each test image is augmented into 64 views (1 weakly augmented view for prediction plus 63 AugMix views for adaptation), while online TTA methods process a fixed-order stream of test batches with batch size 1. The benchmark evaluates on 15 datasets (10 fine-grained classification sets and 5 ImageNet variants) using CLIP (ResNet-50, ViT-B/16, ViT-B/32) and SigLIP (ViT-B/16) as base models, with a fixed text template 'a photo of a [CLASS]'. Beyond top-1 accuracy, the framework reports Expected Calibration Error (20-bin ECE), OOD detection AUC (with 50% of categories held out as OOD), adversarial robustness under PGD attacks, and stability under mixed OOD/adversarial streams.

## Method

TTA-VLM is a unified, reproducible benchmark that implements 8 episodic TTA methods (TPT, C-TPT, RLCF, MTA, ZERO, TTL, TPS, R-TPT) and 7 online TTA methods (TDA, DMN, OnZeta, BoostAdapter, DPE, ECALP, DynaPrompt) under standardized protocols: fixed augmentation schedules, consistent text templates, and original hyperparameters. The benchmark extends evaluation to SigLIP (sigmoid-loss VLM) and three training-time fine-tuned models (CoOp, MaPLe, TeCoA) to test generality and compatibility. Key findings from the comprehensive evaluation are: (1) most recent TTA methods yield only marginal gains over the pioneering TPT baseline under fair conditions; (2) TTA methods show poor collaboration with training-time fine-tuning, with episodic methods often causing negative transfer on robust TeCoA models; and (3) accuracy improvements consistently come at the cost of degraded calibration (up to 47% ECE increase), weakened OOD detection (1–4% AUC drop), and limited adversarial robustness.

## Results

On 10 fine-grained datasets with CLIP-ResNet50, the best episodic TTA method (TPT) achieves 57.80% average accuracy, while the best online method (ECALP) reaches 59.38%, compared to the CLIP zero-shot baseline of 55.84%. On ImageNet-X benchmarks, BoostAdapter leads at 48.07% average (vs. CLIP's 44.19%), with DMN close behind at 47.69%. A critical finding is that TPT (2022) remains highly competitive, with most subsequent methods yielding only marginal gains (≤1.5%) under fair evaluation. However, these accuracy gains come at a significant trustworthiness cost: calibration error (ECE) increases from 6.07% (CLIP) to 16.44% (TPT) and up to 52.95% (ECALP), and OOD detection AUC drops by 1–4% for most episodic methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The benchmark reveals that TPT (2022), the pioneering episodic method, remains the strongest single-sample baseline at 57.80% average on fine-grained datasets, with most subsequent episodic methods (C-TPT 57.37%, RLCF 57.07%, MTA 56.82%, ZERO 55.76%) failing to significantly surpass it. Among online methods, ECALP (59.38%) and BoostAdapter (58.60%) lead on fine-grained tasks, while on ImageNet-X benchmarks BoostAdapter (48.07%) and DMN (47.69%) outperform all episodic methods, with TPT at 47.21%. On SigLIP, TTA gains are minimal (TPT: 69.87% vs. SigLIP baseline 69.01%), and most methods fail to improve on fine-grained tasks, exposing poor generalizability beyond CLIP. When combined with training-time fine-tuning, TTA collaboration is disappointing: on CoOp, only RLCF gains 2.14%; on MaPLe, four episodic methods cause degradation; on TeCoA, nearly all episodic methods degrade performance while ECALP achieves the best online gain of 4.96%. Across all methods, calibration degrades substantially (ECE rises from 6.07% to 12–53%), OOD detection AUC drops by 1–4%, and adversarial robustness remains near-zero for most episodic methods on CLIP-ViT, indicating a fundamental accuracy–trustworthiness tradeoff.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper does not propose a new TTA algorithm but instead carves new ground as a critical re-evaluation benchmark (TTA-VLM) that unifies 15 existing TTA methods (8 episodic, 7 online) under a single, reproducible framework. It departs from prior TTA papers in three key ways: (1) all baselines are reproduced under identical settings rather than relying on reported numbers, (2) evaluation extends beyond CLIP to SigLIP and training-time tuned models (CoOp, MaPLe, TeCoA), and (3) metrics go beyond accuracy to include calibration, OOD detection, adversarial robustness, and streaming stability. Conceptually, it positions itself as a corrective to what the authors identify as a field-wide problem of inconsistent experimental protocols and narrow evaluation that has obscured the true state of TTA progress.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark's central finding—that the 2022 pioneering method TPT remains competitive or superior to most 2024–2025 TTA methods under fair comparison—challenges the field's narrative of rapid progress and suggests that much of the reported improvement was an artifact of inconsistent evaluation. The discovery that TTA gains in accuracy systematically degrade calibration, OOD detection, and adversarial robustness raises a critical open question: can TTA be designed to improve accuracy without sacrificing trustworthiness? Practical limitations include coverage of classification only (no segmentation, VQA, or captioning), exclusion of TTA methods relying on LLMs or generative models, and absence of error bars across multiple seeds. The poor collaboration between TTA and training-time fine-tuning (especially the near-universal degradation on TeCoA) opens an important question about how to compose test-time and training-time adaptation in a complementary rather than conflicting manner.
