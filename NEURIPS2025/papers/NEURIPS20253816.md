## Problem

While pre-trained Stable Diffusion models have been successfully leveraged as feature extractors for visual correspondence, Diffusion Transformers (DiTs) — which offer superior scalability and generation quality — produce features with 'massive activations': a few fixed dimensions carrying values over 100× the median across all spatial tokens, rendering features uninformative for correspondence. This paper investigates the origin of these activations and proposes a training-free mechanism to extract semantically discriminative features from DiTs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253816/cropped_page0_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 960×960 RGB image, a diffusion timestep t (selected via grid search, e.g., t=260 for Flux), and optionally a text embedding c, and produces dense feature maps from a specific intermediate DiT block (e.g., block 28 of 57 for Flux). Features are extracted at the pre-AdaLN stage of the chosen block, then modulated by AdaLN's channel-wise scaling and shifting parameters regressed from (t, c). The resulting feature maps are used for nearest-neighbor matching to establish dense correspondences; when combined with DINOv2 features, PCA is applied to the concatenated DiT features to reduce dimensionality to 1280.

## Method

DiTF is a training-free framework that exploits the built-in AdaLN mechanism in DiTs to suppress massive activations. The key insight is that massive activations concentrate in a small set of fixed dimensions that align exactly with the residual scaling factor α_k produced by AdaLN-zero, indicating AdaLN already localizes these problematic channels. DiTF extracts the pre-AdaLN intermediate feature z_t^k from a DiT block, then applies the AdaLN operation (1+γ)·LayerNorm(z_t^k)+β to perform channel-wise modulation that adaptively normalizes the concentrated activations. A channel discard strategy further zeros out dimensions that still exhibit weak residual massive activations after AdaLN, as these carry negligible local spatial information. The approach requires no additional training or fine-tuning and is applied to four pre-trained DiTs (Pixart-alpha, SD3, SD3-5, Flux), achieving state-of-the-art correspondence performance.

## Results

On SPair-71k, DiTF_flux achieves 64.0% PCK@0.10 (per point), outperforming DINOv2+NN (53.9%) and DIFT (52.9%) by over 10 points. With DINOv2 integration (DiTF_sd3-5†), the method reaches 67.4% PCK@0.10 on SPair-71k and 71.3% on AP-10K-I.S., establishing new state-of-the-art. On the challenging cross-species AP-10K-C.S. benchmark, DiTF_sd3-5† achieves 69.4% PCK@0.10, surpassing the previous best GeoAware-SC† (64.6%) by 4.8%. On PF-Pascal, DiTF_flux reaches 97.6% PCK@0.15, and on ADE20K semantic segmentation, DiTF_flux achieves 54.8% mIoU (multi-scale), outperforming DINOv2 (53.1%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiTF_flux (64.0% PCK@0.10) substantially outperforms both DINOv2+NN (53.9%) and DIFT/SD-based (52.9%) on SPair-71k, demonstrating that DiTs with AdaLN modulation yield far more discriminative features than either self-supervised ViTs or U-Net-based Stable Diffusion. Against the strongest prior unsupervised method GeoAware-SC† (65.4%), DiTF_sd3-5† achieves 67.4% on SPair-71k and a notable 4.8% gain on the cross-species AP-10K-C.S. (69.4% vs. 64.6%), where DINOv2 and DIFT degrade significantly. On AP-10K-I.S., DiTF_sd3-5† reaches 71.3%, surpassing GeoAware-SC† (68.7%) and SD+DINO† (62.9%). For geometric correspondence on HPatches, DiTF_flux (41.9% at ε=1) is competitive with DIFT (45.6%) but slightly below, while on temporal correspondence (DAVIS-2017), DiTF_flux (72.2% J&F_m) outperforms DIFT (70.0%) by 2.2%. On ADE20K segmentation, DiTF_flux (54.8% mIoU_ms) edges out DINOv2 (53.1%) and matches VPD (54.4%), confirming generalizability beyond correspondence.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work using pre-trained generative models as feature extractors for visual correspondence, which has progressed from DINOv2-based methods to Stable Diffusion (DIFT, SD+DINO, GeoAware-SC). It is the first to systematically investigate Diffusion Transformers (DiTs) for correspondence, building directly on the DiT architecture (Peebles & Xie, ICCV 2023) and its AdaLN-zero mechanism. The analysis of massive activations draws on the LLM literature (Sun et al., 2024) and ViT outlier studies, but identifies a distinct DiT-specific phenomenon where activations concentrate in fixed dimensions across all spatial tokens, linked to the residual scaling factors produced by AdaLN. The key departure from prior SD-based work is that DiTs require no architectural modification—AdaLN already present in the model suffices to produce discriminative features when applied in the correct order.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work establishes that DiTs, despite their generative superiority over SD, suffer from a representation pathology (massive activations) that degrades their utility as feature extractors, and that the fix is already embedded in the architecture. The training-free nature of DiTF makes it immediately applicable to any DiT-based model without retraining, lowering the barrier to using newer, larger diffusion backbones for perception tasks. Open questions include the role of massive activations in the generative process itself (acknowledged as a limitation), whether the channel discard strategy generalizes to video or 3D DiTs, and whether the AdaLN modulation insight transfers to other representation-learning tasks beyond correspondence. The finding that Flux features align closely with DINOv2 (causing redundancy when combined) while SD3-5 features are complementary raises interesting questions about how training scale and data affect representation geometry.
