## Problem

Recent Bilinear RNNs (Gated-DeltaNet, RWKV-7, TTT) improve upon Linear RNNs by introducing supervised memory management via the Delta learning rule, but they only apply first-stage state feedback and lack a principled control-theoretic framework for the output pathway. Additionally, their state transition structures (IPLR or DPLR) introduce extra parameters or impair model capacity. The paper asks: can a more structured, closed-loop control perspective yield a simpler and more effective Bilinear RNN that maintains chunk-wise parallelism?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253583/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For language modeling, the model consumes text token sequences (pretrained on SlimPajama at 2K context) and produces next-token predictions with a fixed-size recurrent state (dk×dv per head) enabling O(1) inference. For vision tasks, it processes 224×224 image patches (ImageNet-1K) or video frame sequences (GOT10k, LaSOT) to output class labels or object bounding boxes. During training, sequences are partitioned into chunks of size C, and the chunk-wise parallel formulation computes inter-chunk state recurrence and intra-chunk parallel attention simultaneously.

## Method

Comba reformulates Bilinear RNNs through closed-loop control theory, introducing a two-stage feedback strategy: (1) state feedback corrects the input value via v_t^new = v_t − P_t(S_{t−1}), and (2) output feedback refines the query as o_t = S_t(q_t − d·k_t), which is equivalent to adding a similarity optimization objective ⟨q_t, d·k_t⟩. The state transition adopts a Scalar-Plus-Low-Rank (SPLR) form S_t = S_{t−1}(α_t − β̃_t k_t k_t^T) + β_t v_t k_t^T, where the forget gate α_t is constrained near 1 (Mamba2-style), the input gate β_t ∈ (0,1), and the state feedback strength β̃_t = b⊙β_t < β_t ensures feedback is weaker than incremental information. For hardware-efficient training, the paper derives a WY representation with a novel mathematical induction that reduces the number of inverse matrix computations (one vs. two in Gated-DeltaNet), combined with a UT transform to minimize non-matmul FLOPs, all implemented in Triton. This yields a 40% forward-pass speedup over Gated-DeltaNet and 2× pretraining acceleration over RWKV-7 at half the memory usage.

## Results

At 1.3B scale, Comba-splr achieves a Lambada perplexity of 13.39 and WikiText perplexity of 16.19, substantially outperforming Gated-DeltaNet (18.80/17.14), GLA (19.66/17.61), and Transformer++ (19.29/17.61). On commonsense reasoning, Comba-splr reaches 51.78% average accuracy (vs. 49.58% for Gated-DeltaNet and 49.93% for Transformer++), and on recall-intensive tasks it achieves 36.37% average accuracy (vs. 32.29% for Gated-DeltaNet and 37.31% for Transformer++). On ImageNet-1K, Comba-T achieves 76.3% Top-1 accuracy with only 1.1G FLOPs, surpassing DeiT-T (72.2%), Agent-T (74.9%), and Vim-T (76.1%). On LongBench (10K context), Comba-splr attains 19.16 average score, leading all recurrent baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Comba-splr decisively outperforms all recurrent baselines on perplexity at both scales: at 1.3B, it achieves 13.39 Lambada PPL vs. 18.80 (Gated-DeltaNet), 19.66 (GLA), 19.01 (Mamba), and 21.97 (RetNet). On commonsense reasoning, Comba-splr leads all recurrent models (51.78% avg acc.) and even surpasses Transformer++ (49.93%), while on recall-intensive tasks it nearly matches Transformer++ (36.37% vs. 37.31%) and substantially outperforms Gated-DeltaNet (32.29%), GLA (31.93%), and Mamba (27.70%). The SPLR variant consistently outperforms the IPLR variant on recall tasks (36.37% vs. 32.67% at 1.3B) while the IPLR variant achieves slightly lower perplexity, suggesting SPLR is more robust against overfitting. On ImageNet-1K, Comba-T (76.3%) beats DeiT-T (72.2%), Agent-T (74.9%), and Vim-T (76.1%) with fewer FLOPs (1.1G vs. 1.2G/1.5G), and Comba-S (80.5%) ties Agent-S while using 4.3% fewer FLOPs. On object tracking (GOT10k), Comba-iplr achieves the best AO (0.718) and SR0.75 (0.688), exceeding Softmax Attention (0.704/0.675) by 1.4% and 1.3% respectively. On LongBench, Comba-splr (19.16 avg) leads Gated-DeltaNet (18.61) and GLA (18.75) in QA and few-shot tasks but trails Gated-DeltaNet on summarization and code tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

Comba sits at the intersection of three recent threads in efficient sequence modeling: (1) the Delta-rule family (DeltaNet, Gated-DeltaNet) that introduced supervised memory correction via Householder transforms, (2) RWKV-7's diagonal-plus-low-rank (DPLR) state transition, and (3) Mamba2's near-1 forget gate design. The paper's key conceptual departure is reframing Bilinear RNNs through closed-loop control theory, adding an output-stage feedback term (q_t − d·k_t) that prior models (DeltaNet, RWKV-7, TTT) lack, and replacing the DPLR/IPLR state transition with a simpler scalar-plus-low-rank (SPLR) form. The chunk-wise parallel kernel builds on DeltaNet's WY representation but derives a more concise formulation that requires only one matrix inverse instead of two.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The SPLR-over-DPLR finding is practically significant: it yields a 2× pretraining speedup over RWKV-7 with half the memory, while the one-line output correction (q_t − d·k_t) is applicable to nearly all existing RNN variants, making it a low-cost drop-in improvement. However, the paper is limited to 340M/1.3B scale, cannot compare against Titans, Lattice, or MIRAS (not open-sourced), and Comba still lags Gated-DeltaNet on summarization and code tasks in LongBench. Open questions include whether the closed-loop control framing generalizes to Nonlinear RNNs, how to achieve chunk-wise parallelism for higher-order optimization models, and whether the SPLR advantage persists at larger scales (2.7B+).
