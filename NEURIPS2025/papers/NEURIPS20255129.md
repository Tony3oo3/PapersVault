## Problem

Existing deep-learning methods for structural topology optimization are confined to fixed square grids, a handful of predefined boundary-condition configurations (at most 42), and single forces at domain borders, making them unable to generalize to arbitrary shapes, resolutions, or load setups. This generalizability gap prevents the emergence of a foundation model for TO that can handle the full range of practical design problems in a single architecture.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255129/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a fully specified TO problem as a fixed-size embedding built from five components: boundary conditions and applied forces encoded as order-invariant point clouds via BPOM (with binary directional features and 2D vector load features, respectively), plus scalars for target volume fraction, cell/pixel size, and domain aspect ratio, each passed through small MLPs. The output is a continuous material density field ρ(x) ∈ [0,1] rendered at any requested resolution (64×64 through 256×256) and aspect ratio (up to 10:1) via the neural-field decoder, with no retraining or architectural change needed between configurations.

## Method

OAT is a 730M-parameter conditional latent diffusion model built on a resolution- and shape-agnostic autoencoder. The autoencoder's encoder maps variable-resolution topologies (padded to 256×256) to a fixed-dimension latent z; a decoder produces a feature tensor φ; and a convolutional neural-field renderer (extending CLIF from INFD) reconstructs the topology at arbitrary resolution by sampling connected patches with local coordinates and cell sizes, enabling scalable training on random patches and tiled high-resolution inference. The TO problem is encoded into a fixed-size conditioning vector P by concatenating BPOM embeddings of the boundary-condition and force point clouds with MLP embeddings of volume fraction, cell size, and aspect ratio. The diffusion model uses a velocity-parameterized objective trained with 50% unconditional dropout for classifier-free guidance, and samples with 20 DDIM steps at guidance scale 2.0. The entire framework is trained on OpenTO, a 2.2M-sample corpus of SIMP-optimized structures with fully randomized boundary conditions (including interior fixtures), forces (up to 4000 loads), shapes, and resolutions, making it the first dataset to cover the general TO problem.

## Results

On the standard 64×64 benchmark, OAT achieves 1.74% mean compliance error (CE) and 0.25% volume fraction error (VFE) without any post-generation optimization, outperforming the best prior model (TopoDiff w/G at 2.59% CE) by 32%. On the 256×256 benchmark, OAT reaches 1.51% CE versus 9.18% for NITO and 16.62% for TopoDiff. On the fully general OpenTO benchmark with random boundary conditions and resolutions, OAT attains 8.17% CE and 39.2% failure rate, compared to 13.14% CE and 60.0% failure for NITO. Inference time is 0.51 s at both 64×64 and 256×256 on a single RTX 4090 GPU, scaling nearly constant with resolution.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OAT outperforms all four baselines on every benchmark tested. Against TopologyGAN [33] (48.51% CE) and cDDPM/DOM [14] (60.79%/4.44% CE), OAT's 1.74% CE represents a 28×–35× reduction in compliance error on the 64×64 set. Against TopoDiff [30] (3.23% CE without guidance, 2.59% with guidance), OAT achieves a 32–46% relative reduction in CE while also halving the VFE (0.25% vs 1.14–1.18%). Against NITO [34] (8.13% CE), OAT without optimization already delivers a 4.7× lower CE; with 10 steps of SIMP refinement, OAT+10 (0.050% CE) beats NITO+10 (0.17% CE) by 3.4×. On the general OpenTO benchmark, OAT reduces the failure rate from 60.0% (NITO) to 39.2% and lowers CE from 13.14% to 8.17% without optimization, and after 10 refinement steps both methods converge to similar failure rates (~16%) but OAT maintains a lower VFE (0.57% vs 0.83%).

## Relation to Prior Work

OAT extends the Latent Diffusion Model paradigm (Rombach et al., 2022) to the topology optimization domain by coupling it with a resolution- and shape-agnostic autoencoder inspired by INFD's Convolutional Local Image Functions (Chen et al., 2024), and adopts the Boundary Point Order-invariant MLP (BPOM) from NITO (Nobari et al., 2024) for encoding arbitrary boundary conditions and forces as point clouds. The key departure from prior TO deep-learning work is the simultaneous handling of arbitrary aspect ratios, resolutions, and boundary-condition configurations within a single model, whereas previous approaches (TopoDiff, cDDPM, TopologyGAN) were confined to fixed square grids and a handful of predefined setups. The OpenTO dataset (2.2M samples) is introduced to provide the training scale necessary for this generalization, addressing what the authors identify as the core 'generalizability challenge' in the field.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

OAT and OpenTO together establish the first practical foundation-model framework for minimum-compliance topology optimization, enabling sub-second inference across resolutions from 64×64 to 256×256 and aspect ratios up to 10:1, which opens the door to interactive, real-time design exploration that was previously infeasible. The 2.2M-sample OpenTO corpus is a significant community resource that removes the data bottleneck that has constrained prior TO deep-learning research. However, the 39% failure rate on fully random benchmarks (reduced to ~16% with 10 SIMP refinement steps) and the restriction to 2D minimum-compliance linear elasticity mean the framework is not yet deployment-ready for complex engineering applications; open questions include whether reinforcement-learning-based guidance can eliminate failures, whether the approach extends to 3D and multi-physics objectives, and whether the near-constant inference-time scaling holds at even higher resolutions.
