## Problem

Autoregressive text-to-image models require thousands of sequential forward passes to generate a single image, and while Speculative Jacobi Decoding (SJD) accelerates this via parallel token verification, its unconstrained token refinement makes convergence slow and speedup ratios low. This paper addresses the gap by integrating the structured denoising trajectory from diffusion models into Jacobi decoding, providing a controlled path for token refinement that stabilizes convergence.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252663/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt as conditioning and initializes a fixed-length sliding Jacobi window (96 tokens for Lumina-mGPT, 128 for Emu3) with pure Gaussian noise in the normalized embedding space. During each iteration, noisy token embeddings are concatenated with sinusoidal timestep encodings indicating their noise level, then processed through the transformer with a causal attention mask to predict clean token probabilities at each position. The output is a sequence of discrete tokens (decoded to an RGB image) progressively accepted from the window as they pass a probabilistic verification criterion.

## Method

The core contribution is a next-clean-token prediction paradigm in which a pre-trained autoregressive model is fine-tuned to accept noise-perturbed normalized token embeddings and predict the clean token at a one-position offset, trained with the standard cross-entropy loss. During fine-tuning, input sequences are randomly segmented and each segment receives Gaussian noise at a specific level (flow matching: α_t = 1−t, σ_t = t), with noise levels non-strictly monotonically increasing across segments; embedding normalization before noise addition and denormalization before the transformer are shown to be critical. At inference, the Jacobi window is initialized with pure Gaussian noise, and each iteration performs a single parallel forward pass to predict clean token probabilities, applies a probabilistic acceptance criterion to verify a prefix of tokens, and refines unaccepted noisy tokens via one denoising step—a linear combination of the current noisy embedding and the predicted clean embedding following the continuous-time denoising formula. This denoising trajectory replaces SJD's unconstrained resampling, stabilizing token convergence and reducing the number of iterations needed by roughly 4–5×.

## Results

On MSCOCO validation, SJD2 reduces model forward passes by ~4× on Lumina-mGPT (2357→592 steps, 4.02× compression) and ~5.6× on Emu3 (8193→1461 steps, 5.62× compression) while maintaining or slightly improving FID and CLIP-Score. On the GenEval benchmark, SJD2 achieves an overall score of 0.51 with Lumina-mGPT, nearly matching the tuned AR baseline (0.52). In practical latency on a single A100 GPU, SJD2 reduces inference time from 88.55s to 33.64s on Lumina-mGPT (>2× speedup) and from 375.29s to 147.65s on Emu3 (>2.5× speedup).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SJD2 substantially outperforms SJD [6] in step compression (4.02× vs 2.23× on Lumina-mGPT; 5.62× vs 2.32× on Emu3) while maintaining comparable FID and CLIP-Score, confirming that the denoising trajectory stabilizes token refinement. Against the autoregressive baselines Lumina-mGPT [11] and Emu3 [4], SJD2 reduces forward passes by 4× and 5.6× respectively with only marginal FID degradation (≤0.6). In the broader comparison (Table 7, appendix), SJD2 achieves superior acceleration over Jacobi Decoding [5], Eagle [46], Lantern [47], and ZipAR [53] while preserving visual quality. On the smaller Janus-Pro-1B model [72], SJD2 cuts latency from 9.1s to 2.5s (3.6×), outperforming SDXL [39] in speed (2.5s vs 4.3s) though still trailing SD3-Medium [70] (1.7s). The method incurs ~3GB additional GPU memory over standard AR decoding due to denoising variables (timestep tokens), a trade-off the authors acknowledge.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Speculative Jacobi Decoding (SJD) by importing the structured denoising trajectory from continuous diffusion models into the otherwise unconstrained token refinement of Jacobi iterations. The key conceptual bridge is the 'next-clean-token prediction' paradigm, which reinterprets the autoregressive model's output as a denoising prediction over noisy normalized token embeddings, drawing the denoising step formula directly from continuous-time consistency models [61]. Unlike prior parallel decoding methods (Medusa, speculative decoding, blockwise parallel) that rely on auxiliary heads or smaller draft models, SJD2 requires no additional modules—only a short fine-tuning of the pre-trained autoregressive backbone to accept noise-perturbed inputs. The approach also departs from embedding-diffusion language models (Diffusion-LM, AR-diffusion) by preserving the discrete next-token prediction objective and categorical probability outputs rather than regressing continuous embeddings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SJD2 achieves roughly 4× step compression on Lumina-mGPT and 5.6× on Emu3 while preserving FID and CLIP-Score, and the fine-tuning cost is modest (6 epochs, ~14×8 A100 hours), making it practical for existing autoregressive image models. A notable practical finding is that the denoising trajectory alone (without Jacobi iterations) can already produce coherent images, suggesting the structured noise schedule is the primary driver of convergence rather than the iterative verification loop. Open questions include whether the latency speedup remains consistent across models with very different KV-cache sizes (the paper acknowledges inconsistency), whether the approach extends to video or other long-sequence autoregressive tasks, and whether the ~12× Flops overhead per token (Table 4) becomes prohibitive at higher resolutions or with larger Jacobi windows.
