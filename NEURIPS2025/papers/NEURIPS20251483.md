## Problem

Existing OOD detection methods are vulnerable to spurious correlations in training data, where models rely on statistically correlated but non-causal features (e.g., background) rather than core object features. This is especially problematic for spurious OOD (SP-OOD) samples that share spurious attributes with ID data but lack core features, making them nearly indistinguishable from ID samples under biased decision boundaries. Prior post-hoc approaches either require retraining, additional OOD data, or are evaluated only on limited synthetic benchmarks, leaving a gap in robust, broadly applicable SP-OOD detection.

## Input / Output

SPROD takes a frozen pretrained feature extractor (ResNet-50/18, ViT, CLIP, etc.) and ID training samples with class labels as input; no OOD data, group annotations, or hyperparameter tuning are required. It outputs a scalar OOD score per test sample computed as the Euclidean distance from the sample's L2-normalized embedding to its nearest refined group prototype, which is then thresholded for an ID/OOD binary decision. The method is fully post-hoc and operates on any backbone without fine-tuning on the target dataset.

## Method

SPROD is a three-stage post-hoc prototype refinement that explicitly mitigates spurious bias without group labels. Stage 1 computes initial per-class prototypes as the mean of L2-normalized embeddings. Stage 2 classifies all training samples using these prototypes, then computes separate prototypes for correctly classified (majority) and each misclassified subgroup (minority) within every class, expanding from one prototype per class to up to C prototypes. Stage 3 performs a K-means-style reassignment of samples to their nearest group prototype and recomputes refined prototypes, further purifying subgroup representations. The key insight is that misclassification patterns reveal spurious subgroups, and by scoring each query against all group-specific prototypes (rather than a single biased class centroid), the method eliminates the spurious bias term in ID distances while preserving the core-feature gap that separates SP-OOD samples. The OOD score uses a generative distance-based formulation (negative minimum distance) rather than softmax-based discriminative scoring, which the paper shows is more robust under spurious correlation.

## Results

On the five SP-OOD benchmarks (Waterbirds, CelebA, UrbanCars, Animals MetaCoCo, Sp-ImageNet100) using a ResNet-50 backbone, SPROD achieves an average AUROC of 85.1% and FPR@95 of 49.0%, outperforming the second-best method (KNN) by 4.8% AUROC and 9.3% FPR@95. SPROD attains near-perfect AUROC on Waterbirds (98.8%) and UrbanCars (97.4%), and leads on all five datasets. On conventional OOD benchmarks (OpenOOD protocol), SPROD achieves the highest average AUROC of 85.15%, surpassing RMDS (84.74%), KNN (84.58%), and VIM (83.27%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPROD outperforms all 19 post-hoc baselines on average across the five SP-OOD datasets, with the largest margins over output-based methods (MSP, Energy, MLS, GEN) which average only ~61–62% AUROC versus SPROD's 85.1%. The closest competitor is KNN (80.3% avg AUROC, 58.4% avg FPR@95), which SPROD surpasses by 4.8% AUROC and 9.3% FPR@95; KNN is particularly strong on Waterbirds (98.6 AUROC) but degrades on CelebA (54.5) and Sp-ImageNet100 (77.4). Feature-based methods like MDS (72.2% avg AUROC) and Relation (78.7%) show strong performance on individual datasets (e.g., MDS at 91.8 on UrbanCars, Relation at 96.0 on UrbanCars) but lack consistency across settings. On conventional OOD benchmarks (Table 3), SPROD achieves the best average AUROC of 85.15%, edging out RMDS (84.74%) and KNN (84.58%), while also matching or exceeding VIM and ASH on far-OOD settings. Against CLIP-based zero-shot methods on Waterbirds (Table 4), SPROD with a frozen CLIP ViT-B/16 encoder achieves 99.01 AUROC / 2.94 FPR@95, matching CMA (99.01/3.22) and outperforming MCM (98.36/5.87) and CoVer (90.52/33.17) without requiring text prompts.

## Relation to Prior Work

SPROD adapts the prototypical networks framework [Snell et al., NeurIPS 2017] from few-shot classification to OOD detection, but departs fundamentally from prior prototype-based OOD methods (MDS, PALM, CIDER) by being fully post-hoc and explicitly designed to counter unknown spurious correlations without group annotations. Its three-stage refinement is inspired by the 'Just Train Twice' subgroup identification strategy [Liu et al., 2021] and the reassignment step in K-means, but applied to OOD scoring rather than classification. Unlike training-time SP-OOD methods (BackMix, RW) that require retraining or auxiliary data, and unlike zero-shot CLIP-based approaches (MCM, CMA) that depend on text modality, SPROD operates purely on visual features from any pretrained backbone with no hyperparameters.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the most comprehensive SP-OOD benchmark to date (five datasets spanning binary to 24-class, synthetic to real-world, single to multi-spurious attributes) and introduces Animals MetaCoCo, a realistic multi-class benchmark that fills a notable gap in the literature. A counterintuitive finding—that fine-tuning the backbone on ID data degrades SP-OOD detection, particularly for feature-based methods—challenges common deployment assumptions and has practical implications for how practitioners should configure their pipelines. Open questions include whether the generative (distance-based) scoring advantage over softmax-based scoring can be formalized theoretically, whether SPROD's reliance on class labels limits its applicability to semi-supervised or label-free settings, and whether the prototype refinement strategy extends to more expressive class-conditional distributions beyond simple means.
