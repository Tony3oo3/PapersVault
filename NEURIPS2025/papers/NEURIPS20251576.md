## Problem

Large-scale VQE suffers from two intertwined bottlenecks: barren plateaus that exponentially suppress gradients, and a linear growth in circuit evaluations with the number of tunable parameters per gradient step (due to the no-cloning theorem and parameter-shift rule). While barren-plateau mitigation has received extensive attention, the equally fundamental measurement overhead from redundant parameters has been underexplored. This paper identifies a previously unquantified 'frozen-parameter' phenomenon—where a substantial subset of ansatz parameters consistently exhibits negligible gradient influence—and asks whether these inactive parameters can be predicted a priori to reduce optimization cost without sacrificing accuracy.

## Input / Output

The input is a structured tensor of shape (3+K) × L × (D×N), where the first three channels encode normalized spatial coordinates (layer index, gate index within layer, qubit index) and K additional channels broadcast problem-specific descriptor scalars (e.g., coupling constants, symmetry tags) uniformly over the parameter grid. The output is a freeze-intensity tensor of shape L × (D×N) predicting how frequently each parameter is frozen across a full training trajectory; thresholding this tensor at a user-chosen value τ yields a binary mask that determines which parameters are held fixed during VQE optimization. The architecture is dimension-agnostic, so the same trained weights process any (L, N, D) combination without architectural modification.

## Method

TITAN comprises three tightly coupled components. First, the Adaptive Parameter Freezing and Activation (APFA) mechanism runs alongside VQE training, maintaining per-parameter exponential moving averages of gradient magnitudes, adaptive freeze/activate thresholds modulated by a global gradient-decay ratio, and patience counters that produce a full time-resolved binary mask trajectory; the cumulative sum of this trajectory forms the training label. Second, a theoretical result (Theorem 1) refines the Gaussian initialization of Zhang et al. (2022) to guarantee a Θ(1/L) lower bound on gradient variance for local Hamiltonians, ensuring that dataset-construction runs avoid barren plateaus and yield informative freezing trajectories. Third, the CFCSA predictor—a ResNet-18 backbone with stride-1 convolutions followed by multi-head self-attention over flattened spatial tokens—maps the coordinate-and-descriptor input tensor to the freeze-intensity tensor in a fully convolutional, dimension-agnostic manner, enabling a single model to generalize across ansatz depths, qubit counts, and Hamiltonian families without retraining.

## Results

On the HEA isotropic Heisenberg benchmark (L∈{5,…,10}, N∈{5,…,15}), TITAN achieves ΔE ≤ 0 (matching or beating the unfrozen baseline) in over 90% of all layer–qubit configurations, with ~50% of cells also freezing ≥5 parameters. For molecular systems (H₂, HF, LiH, BeH₂, H₂O, N₂, CO), TITAN freezes 30–66% of parameters a priori (e.g., 77/117 = 65.8% for CO) while maintaining ΔE within ±0.02 Ha of the baseline under Gaussian initialization. In the TFIM convergence study, TITAN reaches lower energy than the baseline in fewer than 15 iterations for N ≤ 15 qubits, with the energy gap widening to ~1.5–2.0 units at N = 20, and exhibits markedly narrower run-to-run variance (±σ envelopes).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares TITAN against two primary baselines: (1) the unfrozen VQE baseline (vanilla gradient descent with no parameter freezing) and (2) a Random Freeze control that freezes the same number of parameters chosen uniformly at random. Against the unfrozen baseline, TITAN matches or improves the final energy (ΔE ≤ 0) in over 90% of HEA isotropic configurations and in the majority of anisotropic (a,b,c) coefficient settings, while simultaneously reducing the number of active parameters by 40–66%. Against Random Freeze, TITAN consistently wins: Random Freeze never outperforms the unfrozen baseline and typically incurs energy penalties of 0.3–1.2 Ha on SU2/SEL ansätze, whereas TITAN maintains or improves energy. In the TFIM convergence study, TITAN's gradient ℓ₂-norm decays within ~10 iterations versus 20–40 for both the baseline and Random Freeze, and the final-energy gap at N=20 reaches ~1.5–2.0 units in TITAN's favor. For molecules, Gaussian initialization under TITAN yields ΔE ≤ 0.02 Ha across all seven systems, while Uniform initialization under Random Freeze produces deviations up to +2.5 Ha (N₂), confirming that the benefit is specific to TITAN's learned mask rather than mere parameter reduction.

## Relation to Prior Work

TITAN extends the Gaussian initialization framework of Zhang et al. (NeurIPS 2022) by proving a Θ(1/L) gradient lower bound under milder observable conditions, making it applicable to general many-body Hamiltonians rather than Pauli-matrix observables. Conceptually, it departs from prior adaptive-freezing strategies (e.g., Grant et al. 2019, Cho et al. 2025) that reactively freeze parameters during training based on instantaneous gradient magnitudes; TITAN instead predicts the freeze mask a priori via a learned ResNet-18 + self-attention model, shifting from reactive to proactive parameter management. The CFCSA encoding is positioned as complementary to existing measurement-grouping, architecture-search, and optimizer techniques, forming what the authors call a 'fourth axis' of VQE efficiency.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 40–60% reduction in circuit evaluations while matching baseline accuracy is practically significant for near-term quantum chemistry workflows where measurement cost is the dominant bottleneck. The empirical 'frozen-parameter' phenomenon—that a predictable subset of angles is consistently inactive across Hamiltonian families—opens a new research direction on the geometry of VQE parameter landscapes. However, all experiments are confined to classical simulators with ≤30 qubits and specific Hamiltonian families (Ising, Heisenberg, small molecules), leaving open whether the learned masks transfer to noisy hardware, deeper circuits, or chemically complex systems with hundreds of qubits.
