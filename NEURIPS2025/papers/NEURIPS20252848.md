## Problem

Classical CLTs for constant learning-rate SGD require the iterate sequence to be stationary (i.e., n→∞ before γ→0), which is unrealistic since practitioners fix an arbitrary initialization θ₀ yielding a non-stationary sequence. Moreover, non-asymptotic Gaussian approximation rates (Berry-Esseen bounds) and sharp concentration inequalities for constant learning-rate SGD were entirely absent from the literature, leaving uncertainty quantification for the last-iterate estimator theoretically ungrounded in finite samples.

## Input / Output

The analysis takes i.i.d. data samples Xᵢ ~ Π from an unknown distribution, a μ-strongly convex objective G with L_p-Lipschitz stochastic gradients, a fixed learning rate γ > 0, and an arbitrary initial parameter θ₀ ∈ ℝᵈ. The key outputs are: (1) a quenched CLT with the limiting covariance Γ defined as the unique solution to the Lyapunov equation AΓ + ΓA = S where A = ∇²G(θ*) and S = E[∇g(θ*,X)∇g(θ*,X)ᵀ]; (2) a Berry-Esseen bound over all convex sets in ℝᵈ with rate dominated by √γ; (3) Nagaev-type tail bounds of the form C₁γ^(p-1)/εᵖ + 2exp(-C₂ε²/γ) + initialization-dependent terms; and (4) explicit Lᵖ moment convergence rates showing exponential forgetting of initialization at rate (1-α₀γ)ⁿ.

## Method

The core technique is a refined linear approximation of the SGD recursion: the estimation error Δₙ is decomposed as (I_d - γA)ⁿΔ₀ - Lₙ + I₁,ₙ + I₂,ₙ, where Lₙ = γΣ(I_d - γA)^(n-k)∇g(θ*,X_k) is a linear combination of i.i.d. gradient noise and the remainder terms I₁,ₙ, I₂,ₙ are shown to be higher-order under local Hessian smoothness. The temporal dependence structure is quantified via the functional dependence measure ψ(n,t,p) = ‖θₙ - θₙ⁽ᵗ⁾‖_p from Wu [2005], which decays exponentially as (1-α₀γ)^((n-t)/2). For the quenched CLT, the condition nγ ≥ ν log n ensures the linear approximation error vanishes, and the multivariate Lindeberg-Feller CLT applies to the standardized linear sequence. The Berry-Esseen bound is derived by applying Shao and Zhang [2022] with the dependence measure, while the Nagaev-type tail inequality uses a separate non-linear autoregressive approximation βₙ = βₙ₋₁ - γ∇G(βₙ₋₁) - γ∇g(θ*,Xₙ) combined with moment-generating function and truncation arguments following Nagaev [1979].

## Results

The paper establishes four main theoretical results for constant learning-rate SGD: (1) an L^p moment convergence bound ‖θ_n − θ*‖_p² ≤ (1−α₀γ)^n ρ_p² + 3(p−1)M_p²α₀⁻¹γ showing exponential forgetting of initialization; (2) a quenched CLT requiring only nγ ≥ ν log n for any initialization θ₀; (3) the first Berry-Esseen bound with rate C[√γ + (1−λγ)^{(n−1)/2} + √γ·n(1−λγ)^{n−1} + γ + (1−λγ)^{2n}], which under optimal γ ∝ log n/n yields O(√log n / √n); and (4) a Nagaev-type tail bound P(|⟨v,Δ_n⟩| > ε) ≤ C₁γ^{p−1}/ε^p + 2exp{−C₂ε²/γ} + initialization term. Numerical experiments on logistic regression with t(3)-distributed features (n=500,000, γ ∈ {0.005, 0.001, 0.0002}) confirm the Nagaev bound is substantially tighter than the Markov inequality and closely tracks the empirical tail probability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is between the proposed Nagaev-type tail bound (Theorem 4.2) and the naive Markov-inequality bound derived from the L^p convergence (equation 7). In the numerical study (Figure 1), the Markov bound is excessively conservative across all three learning rates, while the Nagaev bound closely matches the empirical probability, with the polynomial term γ^{p−1}/ε^p dominating at large ε and the sub-Gaussian term exp{−C₂ε²/γ} dominating at small ε. For the Gaussian approximation rate, the paper's O(√log n / √n) rate under constant learning rate is sharper than the O(n^{−1/4}) rate achieved by ASGD with decaying learning rate schedules (Shao and Zhang 2022; Samsonov et al. 2024). The quenched CLT also improves upon classical results (Pflug 1986; Dieuleveut et al. 2020) by removing the requirement that the initialization follow the stationary distribution, instead holding for any arbitrary θ₀ under the milder condition nγ ≳ log n.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the classical CLT lineage for constant-step-size SGD (Pflug 1986, Dieuleveut et al. 2020, Chen et al. 2022) by eliminating the requirement that iterations go to infinity before the learning rate shrinks, instead proving a quenched CLT valid for any fixed initialization under the concurrent regime nγ ≥ ν log n. Methodologically, it departs from the Markov-chain/stationary-distribution framework by introducing a linear approximation via recursive random functions, and it leverages Wu's (2005) functional dependence measure together with Shao and Zhang's (2022) Berry-Esseen machinery and Nagaev's (1979) large-deviation technique to obtain non-asymptotic guarantees that were previously unavailable for constant learning rates. The autoregressive approximation in Section 4 further connects to the nonlinear time-series literature (Li et al. 2024) while remaining distinct from the linear-model concentration results of Durmus et al. (2021) and Zhu et al. (2022).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper fills a concrete gap: before this work, no explicit Gaussian approximation rate or sharp tail bound existed for constant learning-rate SGD, leaving practitioners without finite-sample confidence intervals for the last iterate. The finding that the optimal Berry-Esseen rate √(log n)/√n under constant learning rate is sharper than the O(n^{-1/4}) rate for Polyak-Ruppert averaged SGD with decaying schedules is a useful theoretical benchmark. Limitations include the strong-convexity and smoothness assumptions, the restriction to i.i.d. data, and the absence of high-dimensional or non-convex extensions; open questions include whether the quenched CLT and Berry-Esseen bounds extend to mini-batched or distributed SGD, and whether practical variance estimators can achieve the stated rates without knowledge of the Hessian.
