## Problem

Existing LLM planning methods rely on long-horizon (sequential, deep) thinking that decomposes problems into linear sub-tasks, which fails for real-world planning where multifaceted constraints are deeply interconnected and must be considered simultaneously. Additionally, existing multi-constraint planning benchmarks evaluate constraints in isolation, ignoring causal dependencies within a journey (e.g., a delayed train cascading into subsequent itinerary failures), rendering static evaluations inadequate for assessing real-world feasibility.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a heterogeneous long context combining structured spatial data (POI clusters, shortest routes, inter-cluster distances from map APIs), unstructured travel blog posts, traveler profiles with detailed preferences and stamina parameters, and a user planning instruction. The output is a detailed multi-day travel itinerary in a strict markdown format with specific timestamps, bracketed location names, and per-POI detailed guides, preceded by an intermediate aspect-aware planning blueprint from the strategist. The planner operates in multi-turn dialogue, processing one aspect per turn before synthesizing the final plan in the last turn.

## Method

MAoP introduces a two-model architecture where a strategist performs pre-planning in two stages—decomposing the request into parallel aspects with concise guidance, then routing and aggregating them into a coherent planning blueprint that captures inter-aspect associations—while a planner conducts focused aspect-aware thinking in sequential multi-turn dialogue following that blueprint. Training uses rejection sampling finetuning (RFT) for the strategist and GRPO-based reinforcement learning for the planner, with a multi-dimensional reward combining personalization scores and format compliance to mitigate reward hacking. The paper also introduces Travel-Sim, an agent-based evaluation framework where an LLM traveler agent with a rule-based stamina engine executes plans in an event-driven sandbox integrated with live map APIs and real travel blog content, providing causal, multi-granularity (per-POI, per-day, per-trip) evaluation across five dimensions. A distillation procedure compresses the multi-turn MAoP process into single-step wide-horizon thinking for smaller models, enabling 3B-parameter models to outperform larger MAoP combinations.

## Results

On Travel-Sim, MAoP with R1-Distill-7B as both strategist and planner achieves FEA=60.7 and PER=81.4, substantially outperforming the Qwen2.5-7B(s.)+Qwen2.5-32B(p.) combination (FEA=35.2, PER=56.3). The distilled one-step wide-horizon model (R1-DistillQwen-7B) further improves to FEA=73.7 and PER=84.2, surpassing the multi-turn MAoP combination of the same base model. Even the 3B distilled models (Llama-3.2-3B: FEA=52.9, PER=65.7; Qwen2.5-3B: FEA=53.1, PER=66.9) outperform the larger MAoP combination of Qwen2.5-7B+32B, demonstrating that distillation from a strong teacher (Gemini-2.5-Pro) yields substantial gains.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Plan-and-Solve long-horizon baseline [Wang et al., 2023], MAoP achieves a 5–40% improvement across all planners on FEA and PER, with the gap widening for stronger models (e.g., DeepSeek-R1: Plan&Solve FEA=57.4 vs. Wide FEA=58.9 in preliminary experiments, and MAoP further boosting beyond that). Compared to zero-shot DeepSeek-R1 [Guo et al., 2025] as a planner, adding the MAoP strategist yields significant gains in both FEA and PER, confirming that the pre-planning blueprint adds value even for strong reasoning models. Similarly, zero-shot Gemini-2.5-Pro [Google, 2025] benefits substantially from the strategist's routing, with the R1-Distill-7B strategist providing the best scalability as more aspects are considered. The key finding is that MAoP's inference-time scaling (more aspects → better plans) is only effective with a sufficiently strong strategist; Qwen2.5-7B as strategist shows limited scalability beyond 3–5 aspects, while R1-Distill-7B continues to improve. The distilled one-step model (R1-DistillQwen-7B, PER=84.2) ultimately surpasses both the multi-turn MAoP combination of the same model (PER=81.4) and the larger Qwen2.5-7B+32B MAoP (PER=56.3), showing that distillation from Gemini-2.5-Pro as teacher compresses the multi-turn process without quality loss.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant long-horizon thinking paradigm (CoT, Plan-and-Solve, Tree of Thoughts, DeepSeek-R1) that treats planning as a deep, sequential deductive process, instead proposing 'wide-horizon thinking' that integrates multifaceted constraints in parallel across multiple aspects. Methodologically, MAoP repurposes the strategist-planner decomposition from multi-agent systems and combines it with GRPO-based RL training and rejection sampling finetuning, while the Travel-Sim benchmark adapts agent-based simulation (inspired by Generative Agents and robotics simulation evaluation) to the novel purpose of causally evaluating plan feasibility. The paper carves new ground by framing the wide-vs-long distinction as a fundamental cognitive mismatch for real-world planning, and by introducing a stamina engine and event-driven sandbox that go beyond the static constraint-pass-rate metrics used by prior travel benchmarks (TravelPlanner, ITINERA, ChinaTravel).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Travel-Sim benchmark addresses a genuine gap: existing travel planning evaluations ignore causal dependencies (e.g., fatigue cascading through a multi-day itinerary), and this simulation-based approach offers a more faithful proxy for real-world feasibility. The wide-horizon thinking framework provides a useful conceptual lens that could generalize beyond travel to other multi-constraint planning domains (logistics, event coordination). Key limitations include the method's reliance on two coordinated models (mitigated by distillation), the LLM-based evaluation introducing potential bias (though 92% human consistency is reported), and the evaluation being restricted to Chinese cities and a single agent model. Open questions include whether the wide-horizon advantage persists in domains with fewer parallel constraints, and whether the simulation approach can be made cheaper and more generalizable.
