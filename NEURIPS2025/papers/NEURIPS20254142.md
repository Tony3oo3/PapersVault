## Problem

Accurate in-vivo localization of microelectrode array channels to anatomical brain regions remains a critical bottleneck for systems neuroscience and clinical neurostimulation, yet current approaches rely on external anatomical references (atlas coordinates, MRI, post-hoc histology) that are imprecise, non-longitudinal, and incompatible with chronic or clinical recordings. No prior work has applied self-supervised learning directly to raw local field potential (LFP) signals to learn anatomically meaningful representations that generalize across labs, probe geometries, and species.

## Input / Output

The model consumes raw multi-channel LFP voltage traces segmented into 3-second trials, preprocessed via the IBL destriping pipeline (2 Hz high-pass filtering, bad-channel interpolation, common-mode subtraction) and normalized to standardize across Neuropixels and Neuronexus SiNAPs probes and across mouse and macaque recordings. It produces per-channel brain region labels (5 classes: e.g., CA1, CA2, CA3, DG, cortex), 768-dimensional anatomical embedding vectors obtained by mean-pooling Transformer outputs across time, and session-level disease classification labels. A lightweight post-processing step applies temporal window averaging with class priors and spatial majority voting over 5 nearest-neighbor channels to enforce laminar continuity without retraining.

## Method

Lfp2vec adapts the wav2vec 2.0 architecture to electrophysiology: a 7-layer 1D CNN encoder (kernels 10,3,3,3,3,2,2; strides 5,2,2,2,2,2,2) converts each channel's raw voltage trace into 512-dimensional tokens, which are discretized via Gumbel-softmax product quantization (2×320 codebooks) and fed into a 12-layer Transformer (12 heads, 768 hidden, 3072 FFN) trained with a contrastive masked-prediction objective that pulls contextual embeddings toward the correct quantized target while repelling batch-sampled distractors. The key architectural insight is cross-domain transfer: rather than training from scratch, the model is initialized with audio-pretrained wav2vec 2.0 weights and then continued with self-supervised training on large-scale unlabeled LFP data, after which a two-layer MLP head is fine-tuned end-to-end on anatomical labels. Ablations confirm that this out-of-domain initialization plus in-domain SSL substantially outperforms random initialization, with an audio-initialized model using only 6k LFP trials matching a randomly-initialized model trained on over 400k trials. The framework is evaluated under strict across-session and across-lab protocols, demonstrating zero-shot generalization from mouse to macaque and across Neuropixels to Neuronexus SiNAPs probe geometries.

## Results

On the Allen dataset, Lfp2vec achieves a Silhouette score of 0.576±0.026 for anatomical region clustering (vs. −0.054 for spectrogram, −0.062 for SimCLR, and 0.146 for BrainBERT) and a linear probing accuracy of 0.921±0.004, confirming that its embeddings are dominated by anatomical identity rather than session or probe artifacts. Lfp2vec attains the highest balanced accuracy and macro-F1 across all three rodent datasets (Neuronexus, Allen, IBL) and the macaque dataset, with the largest gains on Neuronexus and Allen. In the ablation, an audio-initialized model with only 6k LFP pretraining trials matches a randomly-initialized model trained on over 400k trials on Neuronexus, demonstrating the value of cross-domain initialization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Lfp2vec consistently outperforms both SimCLR and BrainBERT on balanced accuracy and macro-F1 across all three rodent datasets (Neuronexus, Allen, IBL), with the largest margins on Neuronexus and Allen where hippocampal subregion boundaries (CA2, CA3) are hardest to resolve. Against the spectrogram baseline, Lfp2vec shows the clearest separation in confusion matrices and PCA projections, particularly for underrepresented subregions. In cross-lab zero-shot transfer, Lfp2vec generalizes strongly between IBL and Allen (shared Neuropixels probes and visual tasks) but shows weaker transfer from Neuronexus due to its distinct 1024-channel SiNAPs geometry; a single one-shot session from the target lab largely closes this gap. On the macaque Neuropixels-NHP dataset, Lfp2vec again outperforms all baselines without any species-specific adaptation, with the fewest misclassifications between closely related motor regions (SMA vs. M1). For the Alzheimer's disease classification task, Lfp2vec embeddings yield higher accuracy and F1 than both SimCLR and BrainBERT, and PCA reveals clear separation between AD-model and healthy-control animals.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the wav2vec 2.0 self-supervised framework (CNN encoder, Gumbel-softmax quantizer, Transformer context network, contrastive masked-prediction loss) from the audio domain to raw local field potential (LFP) signals, introducing a cross-domain transfer strategy where audio-pretrained weights initialize the model before continued SSL on electrophysiology data. It departs from prior SSL work in neural data (POYO, NEMO, NDT2, NEDS), which operates on spike-based features and requires spike sorting, and from BrainBERT/Brant, which use broadband intracranial signals for speech or cognitive-state decoding rather than anatomical localization. The paper also moves beyond traditional brain-region decoding that relies on hand-crafted biomarkers (sharp wave ripples, gamma coherence, firing statistics) by learning representations directly from raw multi-channel voltage traces without any anatomical labels or probe geometry during pretraining.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ability to localize microelectrode arrays in vivo from raw LFP alone—without histology, MRI, or atlas-based planning—addresses a practical bottleneck in chronic recordings, flexible probes, and clinical deep brain stimulation where tissue cannot be sacrificed. Cross-lab and cross-species generalization (mouse to macaque, Neuropixels to Neuronexus SiNAPs) is critical for reproducibility in a field plagued by inter-laboratory variability, and the finding that LFP signals carry rich anatomical information challenges the assumption that spikes are necessary for fine-grained localization. Key open questions include whether performance extends to deep brain structures, whether the approach works in real-time streaming settings, and whether the limited disease-classification results (7 mice) can be validated at clinical scale. Sensitivity to the IBL destriping preprocessing pipeline also raises questions about robustness across different acquisition hardware.
