## Problem

Existing methods for model-based clustering address variable selection and missing data handling in isolation: the SRUW variable-role framework handles complete data, while MNARz clustering methods do not perform variable selection. No unified framework jointly performs adaptive variable selection and models missingness that depends on latent class membership, and prior penalized clustering approaches (e.g., Celeux et al.) lack formal guarantees that their ranking recovers the true signal-redundant-uninformative partition.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an incomplete data matrix Y ∈ ℝ^{N×D} together with a binary missingness mask C, a model grid over (K, m) specifying the number of components and covariance structure, and regularization grids G_λ × G_ρ. It outputs a variable role partition (Ŝ, R̂, Ũ, Ŵ), cluster assignments, GMM parameter estimates, and class-specific MNARz missingness probabilities. Notably, Stage A (ranking) operates on a fast single imputation of Y solely to enable the penalized GMM path, while Stage B (role assignment) fits unpenalized SRUW-MNARz directly on the original incomplete data, so the final model never depends on the imputation quality.

## Method

The framework extends the SRUW model by integrating the MNARz mechanism, where each variable's missingness probability depends only on its latent class membership, so the missingness factor f_c(c_n; ψ_k) separates from the data likelihood and the observed-data log-likelihood retains a tractable mixture form amenable to EM. For the ranking stage, the paper replaces the inverse-partial-correlation penalty weights with a spectral-distance-based adaptive scheme: the symmetrically normalized Laplacian of a thresholded initial precision graph yields a scale-invariant shrinkage target, producing cluster-specific penalty matrices P_k that drive a LASSO-like penalized GMM along a geometric λ-path. The two-stage procedure then traverses the ranked variable list once, fitting unpenalized SRUW-MNARz at each step and assigning each new variable to S, U, or W via a BIC criterion. The paper proves identifiability of the full SRUW-MNARz model, BIC consistency for the role partition, and selection consistency of the two-stage procedure under an RSC-type curvature condition on the GMM log-likelihood, establishing the first formal guarantees for a LASSO-ranked SRUW selection in the MNAR setting.

## Results

At the most challenging setting (50% MNAR missingness), SelvarMNARz achieves a mean ARI of 0.511 (std 0.052), significantly outperforming all three baselines (Clustvarsel 0.363, Selvar 0.348, VarSelLCM 0.344) with p < 0.001 across 20 replications. The method also delivers the lowest WNRMSE across every missingness level (5%–50%) under both MAR and MNAR mechanisms. Performance degrades only modestly as missingness increases, whereas impute-then-cluster baselines deteriorate sharply beyond 30% missingness under MNAR. On the Arabidopsis thaliana transcriptome (1,267 genes), the framework identifies 18 clusters with P1–P4 as core clustering drivers and reclassifies P6–P7 as redundant (U-block), a departure from prior analyses that treated them as informative.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against three baselines, all of which use a pre-imputation (Random Forest via missRanger) + clustering pipeline except VarSelLCM which uses a model-based approach. SelvarMNARz wins decisively on both ARI and WNRMSE across all missingness levels and both MAR/MNAR mechanisms. Clustvarsel [37] (Maugis et al., 2009) achieves the highest baseline ARI (0.363) but still trails SelvarMNARz by 0.148 ARI at 50% MNAR; its variable-role recovery degrades noticeably on Dataset 1 as missingness exceeds 20%. Selvar [13] (Celeux et al., 2019) performs slightly worse than Clustvarsel (0.348 ARI) and also suffers sharp ARI drops under high MNAR missingness. VarSelLCM [35] (Marbac & Sedki, 2017) records the lowest baseline ARI (0.344) and, like the other imputation-based methods, misidentifies relevant variables once missingness exceeds 20%. All three baselines show statistically significant inferiority (p < 0.001, Bonferroni-corrected) to SelvarMNARz at the 50% MNAR level, confirming that explicitly modeling the missingness–latent-class dependency within the clustering algorithm yields a robust advantage over post-hoc imputation strategies.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies three previously separate research threads: the SRUW variable-role framework for model-based clustering (Maugis et al., 2009), the LASSO-based two-stage variable ranking procedure (Celeux et al., 2019; Zhou et al., 2009), and the MNARz missingness mechanism where missingness depends on latent class membership (Sportisse et al., 2024). Prior work addressed variable selection under MAR (Maugis-Rabusseau et al., 2012) or MNAR clustering without variable selection (Sportisse et al., 2024) in isolation; this paper is the first to jointly handle both within a single SRUW framework and to provide identifiability and selection-consistency guarantees for the combined procedure. The adaptive penalty weights are computed via spectral distance on a graph Laplacian rather than the inverse partial correlations used in the original penalized GMM, drawing on ideas from the graphical lasso literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides the first formal consistency proof for a two-stage (ranking + BIC role assignment) variable selection procedure in model-based clustering under MNAR missingness, filling a theoretical gap that limited prior methods to heuristic guarantees. Practically, it shows that ignoring MNAR effects can misidentify variable roles — in the Arabidopsis transcriptome, two previously reported clustering drivers (P6, P7) are reclassified as redundant once MNAR is modeled, altering biological interpretation. Key limitations include restriction to continuous Gaussian data, the restrictive MNARz assumption (missingness depends only on class, not on unobserved values), and computational cost of the EM-based SRUW fitting in Stage B. Extending to categorical/mixed-type data and to more general MNAR mechanisms remains open.
