## Problem

Existing referring expression segmentation (RES) methods are limited to object-level targets with explicit semantic labels, failing on part-level references (e.g., 'mesh backrest') and implicit expressions that describe properties like function, material, style, or logos. Prior reasoning-based approaches (LISA, GLaMM) require task-specific fine-tuning, limiting scalability and generalization to unseen expression types.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252243/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a free-form text expression as input, where the expression can range from explicit semantic labels to implicit property descriptions (material, function, design) or graphical references (logos, packaging labels). It outputs a binary segmentation mask for the referenced region, or a reasoned text explanation when the target is invisible or irrelevant to the image. The entire pipeline is training-free, relying on off-the-shelf SAM ViT-H for proposals and Pixtral 12B (or Claude 3.5 Sonnet / Qwen2-VL) as the MLLM backbone, with CLIP-ViT-B-32 for auxiliary scoring.

## Method

RESAnything introduces attribute prompting as a Chain-of-Thought mechanism for zero-shot arbitrary RES. In the first stage, SAM generates segmentation proposals (filtered for size and duplicates), and an MLLM produces a reference text describing the target's visual attributes (shape, color, location) from the input expression, plus candidate texts describing each proposal using dual visual prompts (bounding box for spatial context + mask-cropped for visual detail). In the second stage, a multi-metric selection algorithm combines MLLM binary yes/no decisions and CLIP scalar scores across both text-to-text (reference vs. candidate) and text-to-image (reference vs. masked region) comparisons, with a grouping procedure that unions masks when multiple proposals jointly satisfy the expression. The key insight is that converting the image-text alignment problem into text-text comparison via attribute descriptions circumvents MLLMs' weakness in directly scoring image-region similarity, while the dual-model (MLLM + CLIP) design compensates for each component's limitations.

## Results

On standard RES benchmarks, RESAnything (zero-shot) achieves 68.5/60.7/60.1 cIoU on RefCOCO/+/g val sets, more than doubling the best prior zero-shot method (CaR: 33.6/34.2/36.7) and approaching early supervised methods like VLT (67.5/56.3/55.0). On the reasoning segmentation benchmark ReasonSeg, RESAnything achieves 74.6 gIoU / 72.5 cIoU, surpassing LISA-13B (57.7/60.3) by ~17% and SAM4MLLM (58.4/60.4) by ~16% without any task-specific training. On the newly introduced ABO-Image-ARES benchmark targeting implicit part-level queries, RESAnything reaches 78.2 gIoU / 72.4 cIoU, outperforming GLaMM (46.2/38.7) by over 30 points in gIoU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among zero-shot methods, RESAnything dramatically outperforms both GLCLIP (26.2 cIoU on RefCOCO val) and CaR (33.6 cIoU), more than doubling their performance across all three RefCOCO variants. Against supervised methods, RESAnything (68.5 cIoU on RefCOCO val) is competitive with VLT (67.5) but trails the strongest pre-trained methods like SAM4MLLM (79.8) and GLaMM (79.5) by roughly 10–12 points, which the authors attribute to SAM's smaller mask proposals struggling with large complete objects. On ReasonSeg, RESAnything (74.6 gIoU) decisively beats all pre-trained competitors: LISA-13B (57.7), SAM4MLLM (58.4), and GLaMM (47.4), demonstrating that attribute prompting enables superior reasoning without fine-tuning. On ABO-Image-ARES, the gap widens further—RESAnything (78.2 gIoU) exceeds GLaMM (46.2) and LISA-13B (43.3) by 30+ points, highlighting its advantage on implicit part-level queries. On COCO-Tasks (affordance-based RES), RESAnything (51.2 mIoU zero-shot) surpasses supervised TOIST (44.1) and TaskCLIP (50.3) but remains below CoTDet (56.9), with prompt optimization closing the gap to 54.6.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MLLM-based RES line (LISA, GLaMM, SAM4MLLM) by replacing fine-tuning with a training-free 'attribute prompting' strategy that leverages off-the-shelf MLLMs (Pixtral, Claude, Qwen2-VL) for Chain-of-Thought reasoning about visual attributes. It builds on SAM for mask proposal generation and CLIP for scalar similarity scoring, but departs from prior zero-shot RES methods (GLCLIP, CaR) by introducing an intermediate text-to-text comparison stage that circumvents MLLMs' known weakness in direct image-text alignment. The conceptual lineage is: SAM proposals → MLLM attribute descriptions → multi-metric (MLLM binary + CLIP scalar) selection, where the key novelty is the systematic prompting that forces the MLLM to reason about shape, color, and location before making a selection decision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that careful prompt engineering can match or exceed fine-tuned MLLM methods on reasoning segmentation without any task-specific training, which has significant practical implications for scalability and deployment cost. The ABO-Image-ARES benchmark fills a gap by providing part-level, implicit-query RES instances (materials, functions, logos) that prior benchmarks lack. Key limitations include dependence on SAM's proposal quality (failure when SAM misses the target region), inference cost from generating candidate texts for all proposals, and sensitivity to the specific MLLM backbone. Open questions include whether attribute prompting can be automated or generalized beyond the hand-crafted attribute set (color, shape, location), and whether the approach extends to video or 3D segmentation.
