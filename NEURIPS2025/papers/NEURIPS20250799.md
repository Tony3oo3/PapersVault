## Problem

Existing metrics for sequential data (EvoRate, ForeCA) quantify intrinsic signal properties but lack a principled link to achievable model performance, and prior generalization bounds focus on the gap between empirical and true risk rather than the absolute floor. This paper asks two questions that remain open: what is the minimal achievable prediction risk on a given sequential dataset, and can we distinguish whether a model's poor performance reflects its own limitations or the inherent unpredictability of the data?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250799/cropped_page7_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework consumes a stationary stochastic process (typically a d-dimensional time series) along with a collection of pre-trained autoregressive models {Q_k} at multiple regression orders k = 1, …, M. It produces the predictive information I_pred(k, k′), the universal learning curve Λ(k), a model-dependent estimate of the minimal achievable risk R̂^∞(Q*), the optimal regression order k*, and an estimate of the parameter-space dimension dim(Θ). The MI estimation itself relies on variational neural estimators (SMILE, NWJ, InfoNCE, TUBA, DV) trained with contrastive objectives over past–future window pairs, with critic architectures ranging from separable encoders to LSTM-based sequential encoders.

## Method

The paper generalizes EvoRate to the predictive information I_pred(k, k′) = I(X_{t−k+1}^t; X_{t+1}^{t+k′}) and establishes its connection to the universal learning curve Λ(k) = ℓ(k) − ℓ₀, showing that the discrete increment I_pred(k+1, k′) − I_pred(k, k′) converges to Λ(k) as k′ → ∞. For Markov processes of order m, Λ(k) vanishes identically for k ≥ m, providing a theoretical certificate for memory-length identification; for finite-dimensional parametric families, I_pred grows as (p/2) ln k, linking the learning-curve decay rate p/(2k) to the intrinsic parameter dimension. The central practical result is the bound R^∞(Q*) ≤ R̂^k(Q) − Λ(k) + 2R̂_n(F_k) + 3 ln(1/δ)/n, which, combined with a collection of models at different orders, yields the oracle estimator R̂^∞(Q*) = min_k {R̂^k(Q_k) − Λ(k)} and a lower bound on the optimal regression order. This enables a direct diagnostic: if a model's empirical risk approaches the oracle estimate, further capacity gains are futile; a persistent gap signals unexploited temporal structure.

## Results

On Ising spin sequences (Table 1), the proposed oracle estimator R̂^∞(Q*) yields values of 0.3724 (LSTM) and 0.3719 (MLP) for M=10,000, consistently lower than the best empirical model losses of 0.4853 and 0.4872, revealing a ~30% gap attributable to non-stationarity. As block size M grows to 10,000,000, the oracle risk drops to 0.0697/0.0867 and the ratio R̂^k(Q)/R̂^∞(Q*) approaches 1, confirming model adequacy. On AR processes (Figure 2), the estimated learning curve Λ̂(k) correctly identifies the true Markov order (k=p for p∈{5,10}) with a sharp transition, and the parameter dimension estimator recovers dim(Θ)=1 with p̂=0.9580 at k=10 for M=10,000.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against EvoRate [55], the proposed oracle estimator R̂^∞(Q*) provides a quantitative lower bound on achievable risk, whereas EvoRate only signals the presence of learnable structure without a numerical target; for M=10,000 the ratio R̂^k(Q)/R̂^∞(Q*)≈1.3 quantifies suboptimal model performance, a diagnostic EvoRate cannot offer. Among the five variational MI estimators compared in Figure 1 and Table 4 (SMILE [52], NWJ [39], InfoNCE [40], TUBA [43], DV/MINE [5]), all perform reasonably in low dimensions (d≤20), but SMILE and NWJ exhibit consistent underestimation in structured high-dimensional regimes (e.g., Periodic and RQ kernels), while the Concatenated critic architecture generally outperforms the Separable and Sequential variants. The paper does not claim superiority over any single MI estimator; rather, it uses them as interchangeable tools to compute I_pred, noting that refining estimation is beyond scope. The key differentiator is the theoretical framework linking I_pred to minimal achievable risk via the universal learning curve, which none of the compared baselines provide.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalizes EvoRate (Zeng et al., NeurIPS 2025) from a single-step mutual information metric to the multi-step predictive information I_pred(k, k'), and connects it to the universal learning curve Λ(k) from Bialek and Tishby (1999) and Crutchfield and Feldman (2003). The key conceptual departure is bridging these information-theoretic quantities to learning theory: the paper derives Rademacher-complexity-based bounds linking Λ(k) to the minimal achievable risk R^∞(Q*), yielding a practical oracle estimator that prior pattern-detection methods (ForeCA, EvoRate) did not provide. Unlike classical generalization bounds that measure the gap between empirical and true risk, this framework targets the irreducible risk floor imposed by the data's intrinsic structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a principled diagnostic for distinguishing whether poor sequential-model performance stems from model inadequacy or from the data's inherent unpredictability—a distinction that is practically critical but previously lacked a quantitative tool. However, the validation is limited to synthetic data (Gaussian processes, AR processes, Ising spin chains), and the MI estimation in high dimensions remains unstable (negative Λ(k) estimates, significant underestimation by SMILE/NWJ at d≥20). Open questions include whether the oracle estimator's model-dependence (it relies on the hypothesis class H_k) limits its usefulness in practice, and whether the framework scales to real-world non-stationary, high-dimensional sequential data where the stationarity assumption (H_0) is violated.
