## Problem

Traditional rule-based agent-based models (ABMs) for financial markets oversimplify human decision-making by assuming homogeneous agents with static behavioral rules, failing to capture the irrational factors (herding, overconfidence, loss aversion) emphasized in behavioral economics. Existing LLM-based agent systems either rely on fixed prompts, lack multi-agent social interactions, or do not model how individual cognitive processes aggregate into systemic phenomena. TwinMarket addresses the gap of how micro-level individual behaviors, shaped by cognitive biases and social influence, propagate through a network to produce macro-level emergent outcomes such as financial bubbles, crashes, and volatility clustering.

## Input / Output

The simulation is initialized with real-world data: 639 user profiles and 11,965 transaction records from Xueqiu, 83,246 stock recommendation transactions from Guba, SSE 50 fundamental stock data (Jan–Dec 2023) from CSMAR, and news articles plus company announcements from Sina, 10jqka, and CNINFO. Each agent operates in a daily loop, consuming personalized news feeds and social media posts (ranked by a hot-score formula balancing upvotes and recency decay over ~2 weeks) and producing trading orders, sentiment scores, and social media posts. The macro-level outputs include simulated stock index prices, order flow statistics, and market-level stylized-fact metrics (kurtosis, GARCH parameters, volume-return correlation) comparable to real SSE 50 data.

## Method

TwinMarket structures each LLM agent (GPT-4o) around a Belief-Desire-Intention (BDI) cognitive loop: beliefs are encoded as individualized system prompts derived from real user profiles and dynamically updated daily based on environmental feedback; desires drive proactive information retrieval via generated queries; intentions commit the agent to concrete trading decisions and social media actions. At the macro level, a dynamic social network is constructed as a weighted graph where edge weights are computed from time-decayed weighted Jaccard similarity over industry-level trading patterns, and an information aggregation mechanism restricts each agent's perception field to top-k posts from high-similarity neighbors ranked by a hot score h(p) = log₁₀(u_p − d_p + 1) / (T_p + 1)^1.8. An order-driven trading system settles all daily orders and updates the stock index, closing the feedback loop between individual decisions and market prices. The framework is grounded in established behavioral theories (prospect theory, behavioral portfolio theory) and calibrated against real transaction data, enabling it to reproduce four key stylized facts (fat tails, leverage effect, volume-return correlation, volatility clustering) more faithfully than rule-based ABM baselines (HPM, BH models).

## Results

TwinMarket most closely replicates real-world market stylized facts across all four benchmarks: Kurtosis of 5.24 (vs. real 7.26), Leverage Effect of 0.11 (vs. real 0.14), significant Volume-Return correlation (p<0.01), and GARCH persistence of 0.89 (vs. real 0.95). In the ablation study, the full model achieves RMSE 0.02, MAE 0.02, and correlation 0.77 against real index prices, while removing the BDI framework degrades correlation to 0.34 and removing agent heterogeneity collapses it to −0.61. At scale, TwinMarket with 1,000 agents maintains behavioral realism, and increasing agent activation from 10% to 80% monotonically reduces predictive error.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TwinMarket is compared against two rule-based ABM baselines: the HPM model (Franke & Westerhoff, 2012), which captures herding and strategy adaptation, and the BH model (Chen & Gostoli, 2014), which incorporates hyperbolic discounting and stochastic volatility. TwinMarket outperforms both baselines on three of four stylized facts. On Kurtosis (SF I), TwinMarket (5.24) is closer to the real value (7.26) than HPM (4.47) and BH (4.99). On the Leverage Effect (SF II), TwinMarket (0.11) most closely matches the real value (0.14), while HPM underestimates (0.05) and BH overestimates (0.19). On GARCH persistence (SF IV), TwinMarket (0.89) again tracks the real value (0.95) more closely than HPM (0.82) or BH (0.72). All three methods achieve the same significant Volume-Return correlation (p<0.01) on SF III, making that a tie. Overall, TwinMarket's LLM-driven BDI agents produce market statistics that are consistently closer to empirical benchmarks than either rule-based ABM.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the agent-based financial market simulation lineage (LeBaron, Tesfatsion, Franke & Westerhoff) by replacing hand-crafted behavioral rules with LLM-driven agents structured by the Belief-Desire-Intention (BDI) cognitive architecture. It departs from recent LLM-agent financial simulations (CompeteAI, EconAgent, ASFM) that either used fixed prompts, lacked multi-agent interaction, or modeled only two strategies; TwinMarket introduces a dynamic social network with weighted Jaccard similarity edges, a hot-score-based information aggregation mechanism, and scales to 1,000+ agents. The key conceptual departure is treating the social media layer as a first-class component that shapes belief propagation and information cascades, rather than a peripheral add-on.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework demonstrates that LLM agents with BDI structure and social interaction can reproduce all four classical stylized facts (fat tails, leverage effect, volume-return correlation, volatility clustering) without explicit programming of those properties, offering a potential testbed for behavioral finance hypotheses at scale. However, the current scope is limited to the China A-share market with a single daily call auction and a zero-sum environment, and the reproducibility analysis reveals substantial run-to-run variance (31–63% RSD on GARCH parameters), tempering confidence in precise quantitative claims. Open questions include whether the framework generalizes to continuous-trading markets, whether the emergent phenomena are artifacts of GPT-4o's training distribution, and how agent trust and institutional heterogeneity would alter the macro-level dynamics.
