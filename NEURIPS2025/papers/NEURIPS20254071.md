## Problem

Existing benchmarks for interleaved image-text generation rely on unimodal metrics (FID, BLEU) that ignore cross-modal integration, or on MLLM-based scoring that introduces model-specific biases and evaluation instability. No comprehensive benchmark exists for evaluating RAG-based interleaved generation in open-domain question answering, where models must select, arrange, and contextualize retrieved images alongside generated text.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254071/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each sample pairs a user query with up to 3 retrieved documents and their associated images (averaging 12.96 retrieved images per query, max 51), all constrained to 540px width. The MLLM produces a markdown-formatted response in which images are referenced by index placeholders (e.g., ![description](IMG#k)) that are subsequently mapped to actual image URLs, yielding an interleaved text-image sequence averaging 2.26 images per answer out of the larger candidate pool.

## Method

The RAG-IG framework integrates MLLMs with a retrieval mechanism: the model processes the query alongside retrieved multimodal content and generates markdown with image index placeholders, which are then resolved to actual images. The evaluation benchmark introduces rule-based metrics across three dimensions—ROUGE-1 for text quality, Edit Distance and Kendall Score for image selection accuracy and ordering, and CLIP Score plus a novel Alignment Score (using Conan-embedding to compare the textual context surrounding correctly selected images between generated and ground-truth answers) for image-text consistency. Data construction follows a three-stage pipeline: MLLM generation guided by CoT/ICL-inspired prompts that first categorize queries into four types (what-is, how-to, yes-or-no, head-to-head), expert human annotation (κ>0.85) with manual image refinement, and multi-dimensional quality filtering yielding 6,057 final samples. The paper further demonstrates that these metrics function as effective reward signals for GRPO training, enabling reinforcement learning in open-domain multimodal settings where rule-based evaluation was previously infeasible.

## Results

On RAG-IGBench, GPT-4o achieves the highest overall mean score of 46.01 across all five evaluation dimensions (ROUGE-1, Edit Distance, Kendall Score, Alignment Score, CLIP Score), followed by QwenVL-Max (40.95) and Gemini-1.5-pro (38.61) among proprietary models. Among open-source models, Qwen2.5VL-72B leads with a mean of 37.79, closely followed by InternVL2.5 78B (36.71) and Qwen2VL-72B (35.34). The largest performance gap between proprietary and open-source models appears in image quality metrics (Edit Distance: 51.28 vs. 40.38; Kendall Score: 46.50 vs. 30.12), while the CLIP Score gap is relatively small (36.04 vs. 40.03), suggesting open-source models have reached comparable proficiency in direct image-text semantic alignment but lag in image selection and ordering.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPT-4o dominates across all five metrics, achieving the best ROUGE-1 (57.42), Edit Distance (51.28), and Kendall Score (46.50), outperforming the next-best proprietary model QwenVL-Max by 8.18, 6.62, and 8.48 points respectively on these dimensions. Among open-source models, Qwen2.5VL-72B achieves the highest mean (37.79) and the best CLIP Score (40.03) among all models tested, surpassing even GPT-4o (36.04) on that specific metric, while InternVL2.5-78B leads in ROUGE-1 (50.71) and Edit Distance (36.86) among open-source models. Claude-3.5-Sonnet underperforms notably (mean 30.81), primarily due to incorrect image selections, trailing GPT-4o by 15.2 points in overall score. NVLM-D-72B performs worst overall (mean 17.33), with particularly poor image quality scores (Edit Distance: 13.97, Kendall: 11.87) and the highest hallucination rate (39 instances). The paper also validates its metrics against human evaluation, showing RAG-IGBench metrics achieve Pearson correlations of 0.256 (image quality) and 0.172 (image-text consistency), substantially outperforming FID (−0.223), IS (−0.174), and GPT-4o-based scoring (0.097/0.151) on the same dimensions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RAG framework (Lewis et al., 2020) to the interleaved image-text generation task, departing from prior approaches that rely on unified transformer architectures (Chameleon, Show-o) or diffusion models (OpenLeaf) for joint image-text generation. Rather than generating images from scratch, RAG-IG retrieves images from external documents and uses MLLMs to compose them into coherent markdown responses, reducing computational cost while maintaining semantic consistency. For evaluation, the paper deliberately moves away from the MLLM-as-judge paradigm used by INTERLEAVEDBENCH (GPT-4o scoring) and MMIE (fine-tuned VLM scoring), instead proposing rule-based metrics (Edit Distance, Kendall Score, CLIP Score, Alignment Score) that are reproducible and free from model-specific bias.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RAG-IGBench fills a gap by providing the first dedicated benchmark for RAG-based interleaved generation with multi-dimensional, reference-based evaluation, and its fine-tuning experiments demonstrate cross-benchmark transfer (e.g., +8.49 on Mantis-Eval), validating dataset quality. However, the modest Pearson correlations with human evaluation (0.14–0.26) raise questions about whether rule-based metrics can fully capture the subjective quality of interleaved content, and the single-platform (Xiaohongshu) origin with 6,057 samples limits generalizability. Open questions include whether these metrics transfer to non-RAG interleaved generation settings, whether the benchmark can scale beyond its current size, and whether the GRPO reward functions derived from these metrics can drive meaningful improvements in open-domain multimodal generation.
