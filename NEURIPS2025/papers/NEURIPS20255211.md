## Problem

Existing retrieval-augmented reasoning methods follow a 'search-during-think' paradigm where LLMs reason directly over raw retrieved documents without an explicit distillation step, making them vulnerable to noise and irrelevant details—especially in multi-hop settings where early distractions derail the entire chain. Additionally, these methods rely solely on outcome-based rewards (final answer correctness), providing no direct supervision signal for improving the quality of retrieval or evidence extraction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255211/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Given a text question, the model autonomously issues up to 5 search queries against an external engine (E5-base-v2 over a December 2018 Wikipedia dump, returning top-3 documents per query, truncated to 512 tokens). The output is a structured reasoning trajectory of interleaved <think>, <search>, <documents>, <refine>, and <answer> blocks, where each <refine> step compresses ~600+ token retrieved documents into ~100–200 tokens of distilled evidence, culminating in a final answer within <answer> tags. The number of search-refine cycles is not fixed but dynamically determined by the model based on question complexity.

## Method

AutoRefine introduces a 'search-and-refine-during-think' paradigm that inserts an explicit <refine> step after each retrieval call, forcing the model to distill and organize key evidence before planning the next query. Training uses GRPO with a dual reward: an answer reward (F1 between predicted and ground-truth answer) and a retrieval-specific reward (binary indicator that all ground-truth answer components appear in the concatenated <refine> blocks). The overall reward is non-linear—full credit for a correct answer, a partial 0.1 credit if the answer is wrong but the refinement captured relevant evidence, and zero otherwise—prioritizing final correctness while still incentivizing faithful extraction. Retrieved document tokens are masked out during loss computation, and the model is trained for 250 steps on a combined NQ + HotpotQA set using Qwen2.5-3B/7B base and instruct variants.

## Results

AutoRefine achieves an average exact-match accuracy of 0.405 (Base) and 0.396 (Instruct) across seven QA benchmarks, surpassing the strongest baseline (Search-R1) by 0.093 and 0.060 respectively. Gains are most pronounced on multi-hop benchmarks: AutoRefine-Base improves 2Wiki by +0.119 (0.393 vs. 0.274) and Musique by +0.091 (0.157 vs. 0.066) over Search-R1-Base. The model also demonstrates robustness across retrieval depths (k=1 to 7), maintaining consistent 0.04–0.10 accuracy gains over baselines at every depth.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AutoRefine-Base (avg 0.405) outperforms all six baselines, with the largest margins over Search-R1-Base (+0.093) and ReSearch-Base (+0.086). On multi-hop benchmarks specifically, AutoRefine-Base leads Search-R1-Base by +0.108 on HotpotQA, +0.119 on 2Wiki, and +0.091 on Musique, while also surpassing ReSearch-Base by +0.100 on HotpotQA and +0.121 on 2Wiki. The Instruct variant similarly dominates: AutoRefine-Instruct (0.396) beats Search-R1-Instruct (0.336) by +0.060 and ReSearch-Instruct (0.331) by +0.065. Against non-RL baselines, AutoRefine-Base exceeds Naive RAG (0.270) by +0.135, Search-o1 (0.255) by +0.150, and IRCoT (0.181) by +0.224. Statistical T-tests confirm significance (p < 10⁻⁵) over both Search-R1 and ReSearch across all seven benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

AutoRefine extends the RL-based retrieval-augmented reasoning line initiated by Search-R1, ReSearch, and Search-o1, which train LLMs to interleave search calls with reasoning using outcome-only rewards under GRPO. The key departure is twofold: (1) an explicit <refine> action is inserted between successive search calls, creating a 'search-and-refine-during-think' loop where the model must distill evidence before planning the next query, and (2) a retrieval-specific reward computed on the refined content provides direct supervision for the refinement step, complementing the answer-correctness signal. The underlying policy optimization (GRPO), model backbone (Qwen2.5), and retrieval infrastructure (E5 over Wikipedia) are shared with the baselines, isolating the paradigm and reward changes as the source of improvement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that explicit, RL-trained knowledge refinement between search hops yields substantial multi-hop gains (up to ~27% relative on Musique) that are not achievable by simply bolting on an external summarizer, suggesting that refinement must be tightly coupled with the reasoning and query-planning process. The non-linear reward combination (full credit for correct answer, partial credit for good refinement) is a practical design choice that balances final-answer accuracy against intermediate information extraction. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger models or live web search, whether the refinement step generalizes beyond factoid QA to open-ended or long-form answers, and how the method behaves when the retrieval corpus is dynamic or noisy in ways beyond Wikipedia snapshots.
