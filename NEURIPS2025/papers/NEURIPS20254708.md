## Problem

Constructing valid prediction intervals for infinite-horizon RL returns is fundamentally harder than in finite-horizon or i.i.d. settings: the true return is unobservable, transitions are temporally dependent (violating exchangeability), and state distributions shift over time or across policies. Existing distributional RL methods provide pointwise quantile estimates without formal coverage guarantees, and prior conformal RL approaches either restrict to finite horizons or suffer from the curse of horizon in off-policy importance weighting.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single test state S_test (not a full trajectory), a trained DRL return-distribution model (e.g., QTD quantile particles), and a calibration buffer of transition tuples extended to k-step segments. It outputs a conformal predictive region for the infinite-horizon return G^π(S_test) at a user-specified level 1−α, constructed by aggregating B independently subsampled intervals and retaining the set of returns covered by at least a (1−ξ) fraction of them. The k-step pseudo-return at each calibration point combines observed rewards with a single bootstrapped sample from the learned tail distribution, so no full trajectory rollout is required.

## Method

The framework has three coupled components. First, k-step pseudo-returns (Eq. 1) approximate the infinite-horizon return by summing observed rewards over k steps and bootstrapping the tail with a sample from the DRL-estimated return distribution, creating a natural bias-variance trade-off controlled by k. Second, experience replay with random subsampling from the calibration buffer decorrelates temporally linked transitions, restoring approximate exchangeability needed for conformal validity. Third, weighted subsampling using estimated importance weights (density-ratio for on-policy time drift; product of state-visit and action-selection ratios for off-policy) reweights the calibration set to match the test-time distribution, avoiding the multiplicative weight explosion of trajectory-level importance sampling. The nonconformity score is the absolute deviation of the pseudo-return from the DRL value function, and the theoretical coverage bound (Theorems 1–2) decomposes the gap from nominal 1−α into an importance-weight estimation error and a Wasserstein-distance model-bias term that decays as γ^k.

## Results

Across both synthetic examples (two-state MDP and continuous-state MDP) and the Mountain Car benchmark, the proposed conformal framework consistently achieves near-nominal 90% coverage for k-step pseudo-returns with k = 1, …, 5, in both on-policy and off-policy settings. The DRL-QR baseline (raw quantile intervals from the QTD model) exhibits systematic undercoverage due to model bias in the estimated return distribution. Average prediction interval length increases monotonically with k, reflecting the higher variance introduced by longer truncation horizons, while the conformal correction maintains valid coverage regardless of k. In the Mountain Car off-policy setting, the method similarly corrects KDE model bias and recovers near-nominal 90% coverage.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is DRL-QR, which constructs prediction intervals directly from the quantiles of the QTD-learned return distribution without any conformal correction. The proposed method clearly outperforms DRL-QR on coverage: while DRL-QR systematically undercovers (falling below the nominal 90% level) in both on-policy and off-policy settings across all k values, the conformal framework restores coverage to near-nominal levels. The trade-off is that conformal intervals are somewhat wider than DRL-QR intervals, particularly at larger k, but this is the price of valid statistical guarantees under model misspecification. A secondary comparison with Foffano et al. [12] (weighted conformal off-policy evaluation) is provided in the supplementary material on the two-state MDP; the paper notes that [12] suffers from the curse of horizon due to multiplicative importance weights, whereas the proposed stepwise pseudo-return approach avoids this issue. For the Mountain Car benchmark, the KDE-QR baseline (kernel density estimation on Monte Carlo rollouts) also undercovers, and the conformal method corrects this bias to achieve near-nominal 90% coverage.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies three previously separate threads: distributional RL (QTD/C51) for learning return distributions, split conformal prediction for distribution-free uncertainty quantification, and experience replay from deep RL for decorrelating sequential data. It departs from prior conformal RL work (Foffano et al. 2023, Dietterich & Hostetler 2022, COPP 2023) by replacing trajectory-level importance weighting—which suffers from the curse of horizon—with a modular k-step pseudo-return construction and a simpler weighted subsampling scheme. The theoretical analysis builds on a recent Wasserstein-regularized conformal prediction result [33] to obtain tighter coverage-gap bounds than the total-variation-based bounds used in earlier work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to provide distribution-free prediction intervals for infinite-horizon returns in general RL with arbitrary state and action spaces, filling a gap left by prior methods limited to finite horizons or discrete settings. The pseudo-return construction offers a practical bias-variance knob (k) that avoids full trajectory rollouts, making the approach scalable to long-horizon problems. Open questions include how to optimally choose or aggregate across k values, whether the Wasserstein-based guarantees tighten in practice, and how the framework extends to policy optimization rather than pure evaluation. Experimental validation remains limited to small synthetic MDPs and Mountain Car, leaving scalability to high-dimensional continuous control untested.
