## Problem

Simultaneous speech translation requires deciding when enough input has been received to produce the next output token, but existing read/write policies either impose artificial supervision signals or require architectural changes that degrade quality. In multilingual many-to-many settings, divergent optimal policies across language pairs further complicate learning a unified strategy, and current systems like Seamless suffer 7–17% BLEU degradation at practical latencies.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251423/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The S2TT system consumes streaming speech audio in fixed-size chunks processed by a hybrid encoder (20 chunk-autoregressive blocks with cached KV + 4 non-autoregressive blocks), with a learnable end-of-stream binary flag prepended before the NAR blocks to signal stream termination. The TTS variant consumes one text token at a time from a decoder-only backbone (CosyVoice2). Outputs are incremental: translated text tokens or speech units are emitted as soon as the learned policy deems the available prefix sufficient, with a threshold λ controlling the quality-latency trade-off at inference time.

## Method

SimulMEGA converts an offline autoregressive model into a simultaneous system by adding a global routing gate (a 2-layer MLP with sigmoid head over the decoder's final hidden state) and a Mixture-of-Experts refiner module active only during training. Each refiner block contains a prefix expert (cross-attention over truncated encoder hidden states) and a global expert (MLP over a mean-pooled global embedding), whose mixture weights are set by the gate's scalar output p, implicitly encoding the read/write decision. To prevent global information leakage through self-attention, the refiner replaces self-attention with a previous-output attention module that only attends to the decoder's prior outputs. Training proceeds in two stages: offline pre-training with LoRA on the encoder, then simultaneous fine-tuning with a combined loss (offline + refiner + prefix) where the prefix loss is restricted to positions where the gate is confident (p < λ). Score normalization (aligning mean gate output to prefix-length ratio) and pre-sigmoid Gaussian noise (σ=1) stabilize the gate's output distribution across language pairs, enabling a single threshold to generalize without per-task tuning.

## Results

On CoVoST2 and FLEURS, SimulMEGA-S2T (561M params) achieves near-offline quality in simultaneous mode: only 0.3–3.1% BLEU degradation relative to its offline base, compared to 6.0–8.3% for the 2.0B Seamless-S2T. At 2 s average lagging, SimulMEGA shows 3–5% BLEU degradation across CoVoST2 X-EN, EN-X, and FLEURS X-X, while Seamless degrades by 9–17%. For streaming TTS, SimulMEGA-TTS achieves 1.2/0.5 token-unit AL on LibriSpeech/Seed-TTS (vs. 8.4–23.1 for CosyVoice2 streaming) with comparable WER and speaker similarity, and in cross-lingual S2ST it surpasses Seamless by 10 SIM points with >40% lower WER.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary baseline Seamless (2.0B), SimulMEGA (561M) wins in 4 of 5 evaluation scenarios (CoVoST2 X-EN, EN-X, FLEURS X-EN, X-X) with substantially lower BLEU degradation (≤3.1% vs. 6.0–8.3%), and even at 2 s AL the gap widens to 3–5% vs. 9–17% degradation. Among policy-based baselines, EDATT is the most stable but still incurs >0.5 s additional LAAL at matched quality; DiG-SST and AlignATT exhibit instability and fail on specific language pairs (notably all X→ZH pairs, likely due to byte-level BPE tokenization of CJK characters). Wait-K, the fixed-ratio baseline, shows the worst latency-quality trade-off across all settings. For streaming TTS, SimulMEGA-TTS reduces AL from 8.4–23.1 (CosyVoice2-S) to 0.5–1.2 token units while maintaining equivalent WER and speaker similarity. In end-to-end S2ST, SimulMEGA-S2S outperforms both NAST-S2S and StreamSpeech in ASR-BLEU and adds <200 ms AL over its S2TT component, whereas Seamless suffers 10–20% BLEU reduction from S2TT to S2ST.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in simultaneous speech translation (SimulST) where read/write policies are learned through explicit signals—wait-k schedules, divergence-based decisions, attention-score thresholds, or dedicated policy heads—and instead repurposes Mixture-of-Experts routing as an implicit, unsupervised policy mechanism. The streaming speech encoder builds on the chunk-autoregressive + NAR hybrid design, while the TTS component extends CosyVoice2's decoder-only backbone with the same MoE refiner. Conceptually, the work unifies two previously separate research threads (SimulST policy learning and streaming TTS) under a single prefix-based training framework, requiring only minimal architectural modifications to standard transformers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The near-zero BLEU degradation (0.3–3%) at practical latencies, achieved by a 561M model that outperforms the 2.0B Seamless system, suggests that explicit policy supervision may be less critical than previously assumed for simultaneous translation quality. The zero-inference-overhead design (MoE refiner active only during training) makes the approach directly deployable. Key limitations remain: the cascaded S2ST pipeline introduces token-space mismatch and ~100 ms extra latency, the TTS component supports only two languages, and the 30-second input cap necessitates external VAD segmentation. Open questions include whether end-to-end S2ST with unified token spaces, longer-context handling via sliding windows, and broader language coverage can close these gaps.
