## Problem

Langevin dynamics sampling in generative models (EBMs, SGMs) is extremely slow, requiring hundreds to thousands of neural function evaluations because particles become trapped at near-zero-gradient regions (local minima, unstable equilibria) and must rely on random noise to escape. Reducing steps or increasing step sizes naively introduces larger stochastic fluctuations that degrade sample quality, and existing acceleration methods either require retraining (CLD) or dataset-specific statistics (preconditioned diffusion). The paper asks whether control-theoretic feedback mechanisms can accelerate Langevin sampling in a training-free, dataset-agnostic manner while preserving stability.

## Input / Output

The method requires only a pretrained score or energy model (NCSNv2, IGEBM, IRED), a random initial point, and three PID gains (k_p, k_i, k_d) plus a decay rate γ — no additional data, retraining, or prior information. It produces 32×32 CIFAR-10 or 64×64 CelebA images, or discrete reasoning solutions (Sudoku grids, graph connectivity paths). A key implementation detail is that within annealed Langevin dynamics, the accumulated integral state I_t and the previous gradient for the derivative term are carried over across noise scales, ensuring continuous use of historical information throughout the multi-scale sampling trajectory.

## Method

PIDLD reinterprets the Langevin update as a feedback control loop where the energy gradient ∇_x U_θ(x) serves as the error signal. The proportional term provides standard gradient guidance; the integral term accumulates gradient history (normalized by 1/t and exponentially decayed as k_i(t)=γ^t·k_i) to create a momentum-like force that helps particles traverse energy barriers and escape unstable equilibria; the derivative term captures the gradient change rate to dampen overshoot and accelerate descent toward potential wells. The full update is x_{t+1} = x_t + ε(k_p∇U(x_t) + (k_i/t)Σ_{s=0}^t ∇U(x_s) + k_d(∇U(x_t)−∇U(x_{t-1}))) + √(2ε)ξ_t. A stability proposition proves that under local m-strong convexity, the derivative term preserves asymptotic stability of standard Langevin dynamics provided ε < 1/((1+2k_d)m), and the exponential decay of k_i guarantees the dynamics asymptotically reduce to vanilla Langevin, ensuring convergence to the correct stationary distribution. The method is a drop-in replacement for the Langevin step within any existing ALD pipeline, achieving at least 10× NFE reduction on SGM image generation while improving FID.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with SGM (NCSNv2), PIDLD achieves FID 12.1 at just 100 NFEs, surpassing the vanilla ALD best of 12.5 at 1160 NFEs (232×5) and MILD's 13.0 at the same budget — a >10× speedup. On CelebA with SGM, PIDLD reaches FID 5.7 at 250 NFEs versus vanilla ALD's best of 9.5 at 2500 NFEs. For EBM (IGEBM), PIDLD improves FID from 35.3 to 33.2 on CIFAR-10 (40 NFEs) and from 35.4 to 30.0 on CelebA (30 NFEs). On reasoning tasks, PIDLD achieves 50.54% Sudoku accuracy at 5 NFEs (vs. 51.00% for vanilla at 10 NFEs) and 91.32% Connectivity accuracy at 2 NFEs (vs. 90.33% for vanilla at 10 NFEs).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PIDLD consistently outperforms both vanilla ALD [33,8] and MILD [31] across all evaluated settings. Against vanilla ALD, the gains are most dramatic under SGM: on CelebA, PIDLD at 250 NFEs (FID 5.7) beats vanilla ALD's best at 2500 NFEs (FID 9.5) by 40%, and on CIFAR-10, PIDLD at 100 NFEs (FID 12.1) beats vanilla at 1160 NFEs (FID 12.5). Against MILD [31], PIDLD achieves lower FID at every comparable NFE budget (e.g., 11.4 vs. 13.0 on CIFAR-10 SGM at 232×5; 5.6 vs. 11.0 on CelebA SGM at 500×5), with the gap widening at lower NFEs where MILD is unavailable. On reasoning tasks, PIDLD surpasses the IRED vanilla baseline [7] on both Sudoku (50.54% at 5 NFEs vs. 51.00% at 10 NFEs) and Connectivity (91.32% at 2 NFEs vs. 90.33% at 10 NFEs), demonstrating a 5× efficiency gain on Connectivity. The paper attributes PIDLD's consistent advantage over MILD to the derivative term's adaptive damping, which MILD's fixed momentum lacks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reinterprets Langevin dynamics sampling through the lens of feedback control theory, treating energy gradients as error signals in a PID controller. It builds directly on the annealed Langevin dynamics framework of Song & Ermon (NCSN/NCSNv2) and the energy-based sampling of IGEBM, while drawing on the classical PID control law (Astrom, 1995). Conceptually, it departs from prior acceleration methods—momentum-based (MILD, adaptive momentum), preconditioning-based (PDS), and Hamiltonian-based (CLD)—by framing the problem as a control stability issue rather than an optimization or statistical mechanics one. The derivative term's role as an adaptive damper distinguishes PIDLD from purely momentum-driven approaches, which lack an explicit mechanism to counteract overshoot.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PIDLD offers a training-free, dataset-agnostic, drop-in accelerator for any pre-trained model relying on Langevin sampling, with a claimed 10× speedup for SGMs that could make these models practical for latency-sensitive applications. The control-theoretic framing provides interpretability (bias mitigation via I-term, oscillation reduction via D-term) that purely empirical acceleration methods lack. Open questions include: adaptive or automated tuning of (k_p, k_i, k_d, γ) across models and NFE budgets, convergence guarantees beyond local strong convexity for the full PID controller in nonconvex landscapes, and whether the PID principles extend to ODE/SDE solvers used in modern diffusion models. The per-setting hyperparameter tuning (Table 4) remains a practical friction point for true plug-and-play deployment.
