## Problem

Current medical LLM benchmarks assess knowledge recall through single-turn questions where complete clinical information is provided upfront, failing to capture the sequential, hypothesis-driven reasoning process central to real clinical diagnosis. This paper addresses the gap by introducing VivaBench, a multi-turn benchmark that simulates viva voce examinations, requiring LLM agents to actively probe for findings, select investigations, and refine diagnoses across multiple interaction steps under diagnostic uncertainty.

## Input / Output

The agent receives a limited initial clinical stem and interacts through a constrained multi-turn dialogue (max 20 steps, with category-specific limits of 10 history, 5 examination, 3 imaging, and 3 lab requests), issuing natural-language queries that are mapped to structured keys in the underlying case. The agent must produce a provisional diagnosis (with up to 5 ICD-10-coded conditions and confidence scores) after the Review phase, then a final diagnosis after the Investigation phase. Evaluation outputs include top-k exact/approximate accuracy, a confidence-weighted score S_conf ranging from -1 to 1, information-seeking precision and recall per category, and LLM-classified failure mode labels (inappropriate hypothesis, premature closure, inadequate investigations).

## Method

VivaBench transforms free-text PubMed case reports into a formalized Clinical Case schema (History, Physical Examination, Imaging, Labs, Diagnosis) mapped to SNOMED-CT, LOINC, and ICD-10 codes, with 990 physician-validated cases spanning nine specialty groups. The evaluation framework centers on a two-phase interactive protocol (Review → Investigation) where a Mapper module translates the agent's free-text queries into structured information keys—offering both a deterministic variant (PubMedBERT embeddings + SNOMED-CT mediated similarity + LOINC synonym dictionaries) and an LLM-based variant (GPT-4.1 with few-shot prompting, validated at >0.99 IoU determinism)—and a Parser module that converts retrieved key-value pairs into natural-language responses while preventing information leakage. Six frontier LLMs are evaluated at temperature 0, with a Full Information control condition to isolate sequential reasoning from knowledge recall. Failure modes are quantified via GPT-4.1-based classification of 738 failed traces, revealing that 86% of errors stem from reasoning deficits (anchoring bias, satisfaction-of-search) rather than knowledge gaps.

## Results

On VivaBench (934 cases), Gemini 2.5 Pro achieves the highest top-1 final diagnosis accuracy at 35% and top-1 full-information accuracy at 69%, while o4-mini attains the highest confidence-weighted score (S_conf = 0.44) for final diagnoses. All six models show a substantial performance gap between full-information and interactive settings, with final diagnosis top-1 accuracy ranging from 16% (Grok 3 Mini Beta) to 35% (Gemini 2.5 Pro) versus full-information top-1 accuracy of 47–69%. The progression from top-1 to top-5 accuracy shows diminishing returns, with most gains occurring between top-1 and top-3, indicating that correct diagnoses are often present in models' alternative lists even when the primary prediction is wrong.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Gemini 2.5 Pro leads on top-1 final diagnosis accuracy (35%) and full-information accuracy (69%), outperforming o4-mini (32% and 63%) and DeepSeek-R1 (23% and 61%) by 3 and 12 percentage points respectively on the interactive task. o4-mini achieves the highest confidence-weighted score (S_conf = 0.44) for final diagnoses, surpassing Gemini 2.5 Pro (0.37) and DeepSeek-R1 (0.35), suggesting better calibration between stated confidence and actual correctness. Grok 3 Mini Beta and Qwen 3 trail significantly, with top-1 final accuracy of only 16% and 21% respectively, and the lowest full-information scores (60% and 47%), indicating both weaker knowledge retrieval and weaker sequential reasoning. Llama-4 Maverick shows the lowest provisional top-1 accuracy (8%) but recovers to 23% at the final stage, suggesting it benefits more from investigation data than other models. Across all models, the gap between full-information and interactive top-1 accuracy is at least 12 percentage points, confirming that the primary bottleneck is not medical knowledge but the ability to strategically gather and synthesize information under uncertainty.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by repurposing the viva voce examination format from medical education into a structured LLM evaluation framework, departing from both static QA benchmarks (PubMedQA, MedQA) that assess knowledge recall and prior multi-turn medical benchmarks (AI Hospital, AgentClinic) that rely on non-deterministic LLM-as-examiner or human-in-the-loop evaluation. The key conceptual departure is the formalization of clinical diagnosis as a partially observable MDP with deterministic information retrieval, enabling reproducible evaluation of sequential hypothesis refinement. It also draws on the broader multi-turn LLM evaluation literature (MT-Eval, MultiChallenge) that documents performance degradation in interactive settings, but applies this specifically to the high-stakes clinical domain with physician-curated structured cases.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VivaBench provides the first open-source, deterministic multi-turn clinical reasoning benchmark, filling a practical gap for evaluating conversational medical AI intended for real-world decision support. The finding that models possess sufficient medical knowledge (69–86% top-5 full-information accuracy) yet achieve only 35–48% top-1 in the interactive setting, with failure modes mirroring human cognitive biases (anchoring, satisfaction-of-search), suggests that the bottleneck is sequential reasoning under uncertainty rather than knowledge acquisition. Key limitations include the modest dataset scale (990 cases from a single source type), single evaluation runs, and the simplified approximation of real clinical encounters. Open questions include whether scaling or targeted training on sequential information-gathering can close the interactive-to-full-information gap, and how to handle the inherent non-determinism in LLM-based information retrieval without sacrificing ecological validity.
