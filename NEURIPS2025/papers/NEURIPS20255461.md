## Problem

Conventional video outpainting methods maintain coherent textures and scene structure but fail in dynamic scenes where objects move or the camera shifts, producing temporally inconsistent and flickering results. The core gap is the absence of instance-aware modeling that can track individual object motions and preserve identity across frames, particularly when an instance is temporarily outside the visible frame but its dynamic shadow remains as a cue for its position and motion.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255461/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a video clip and a user-defined target aspect ratio, padding the original frames with blank borders to define the region to be generated. It produces an outpainted video extending beyond the original field of view, along with intermediate outputs: per-instance object and shadow mask predictions for the invisible regions and completed optical flow fields. The visible region mask (binary, same spatial size as the original frame) delineates what is given versus what must be synthesized, and the method handles up to 10 shadow-object pairs per frame.

## Method

The framework operates in four stages. First, a shadow-aware instance prediction module treats each shadow-object pair as a token in a spatiotemporal transformer, performing inter-frame fusion followed by inter-pair spatial fusion to predict object and shadow masks in the padded (invisible) regions. Second, an instance-aware optical flow completion module uses a pseudo-3D U-Net with cross-attention conditioned on the predicted instance features to fill in optical flow in the invisible areas, preserving sharp motion boundaries where instances are present. Third, the outpainting stage injects both spatial (instance mask logits + memory embedding) and temporal (completed flow) conditioning into a Stable Diffusion v1.5 backbone augmented with temporal convolution modules, using two separate cross-attention adapters at different U-Net blocks. Finally, a video-aware shadow alignment discriminator with 3D convolutions enforces spatiotemporal coherence between the generated semantics and their corresponding dynamic shadows during adversarial fine-tuning.

## Results

On DAVIS, the proposed method achieves PSNR 20.81, SSIM 0.7254, LPIPS 0.1842, and FVD 234.7, outperforming all baselines on PSNR, SSIM, and FVD. On YouTube-VOS, it reaches PSNR 20.32, SSIM 0.7719, LPIPS 0.1793, and FVD 40.78, again leading on PSNR, SSIM, and FVD. The FVD improvement is particularly notable: 234.7 vs. 286.3 (MOTIA) on DAVIS and 40.78 vs. 58.99 (MOTIA) on YouTube-VOS, indicating substantially better temporal coherence. The only metric where the method trails is LPIPS, where MOTIA (which uses per-sample fine-tuning) achieves lower values of 0.1595 and 0.1727 respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against four baselines: Dehan (GAN-based), SDM (latent diffusion without temporal modeling), M3DDM (coarse-to-fine diffusion), and MOTIA (per-sample fine-tuning). The proposed method wins on PSNR, SSIM, and FVD across both DAVIS and YouTube-VOS, with the largest FVD gains over MOTIA (234.7 vs. 286.3 on DAVIS; 40.78 vs. 58.99 on YouTube-VOS), reflecting superior temporal consistency. On LPIPS, MOTIA achieves the best scores (0.1595 on DAVIS, 0.1727 on YouTube-VOS) because it fine-tunes on each test sample, whereas the proposed method operates zero-shot. Against M3DDM, the proposed method improves FVD by 21.6% on DAVIS and 38.7% on YouTube-VOS, while also gaining 0.55 dB PSNR on DAVIS. The user study further confirms the advantage: 56.4% preference for visual quality and 62.0% for temporal consistency versus M3DDM (18.0%/22.3%) and MOTIA (25.6%/15.7%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the authors' prior work on shadow-enlightened image outpainting (Yu et al., CVPR 2024) to the video domain, adding temporal modeling via a spatiotemporal transformer and instance-aware optical flow completion. It builds on the diffusion-based video outpainting line (M3DDM, MOTIA) and the Stable Diffusion / IP-Adapter conditioning architecture, but departs from these methods by introducing dynamic shadow-object pairs as an explicit spatiotemporal guidance signal rather than relying solely on texture-level consistency. The shadow tracking component leverages ViShadow for paired detection, while the flow completion module borrows the pseudo-3D U-Net structure from video inpainting literature, conditioned via cross-attention on predicted instance masks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to explicitly model shadow-instance associations for video outpainting, addressing a concrete failure mode (temporal inconsistency and action misalignment when objects leave the frame) that prior methods handle only implicitly. The key limitation is that the approach depends on visible shadows carrying sufficient semantic information; in scenes with diffuse lighting or no cast shadows, it degrades to standard instance-aware outpainting. Open questions include how the method scales to scenes with multiple dynamic light sources, very low shadow contrast, or instances that are invisible for extended periods beyond the temporal context window of the transformer.
