## Problem

Jointly modeling fMRI and EEG brain imaging remains underexplored because simultaneously recorded datasets are too small for reliable training, and the fundamental signal mismatch between fMRI's slow hemodynamic responses (seconds) and EEG's rapid neural oscillations (milliseconds) creates a highly complex data distribution gap. Additionally, existing graph-based methods assume one-to-one ROI correspondences across modalities, whereas fMRI-EEG relationships are inherently many-to-many, a structural property that standard graphs cannot represent.

## Input / Output

The method consumes asynchronously acquired resting-state fMRI BOLD signal matrices (per-ROI time series) and 62-channel EEG recordings from the same subjects, without requiring temporal alignment or resampling. The two modalities arrive at fundamentally different sampling rates and time scales, which the model handles natively through its ODE-based dynamics rather than through manual synchronization. The output is a binary class label distinguishing high- versus low-scoring groups on four cognitive assessments (sustained attention, cognitive flexibility, verbal intelligence, and logical intelligence), evaluated via 5-fold cross-validation.

## Method

FE-NET comprises two modules. The FEH module is a GAN-based hypergraph generator: an Optimal fMRI-EEG Isomorph (OFEI) algorithm computes a consensus hypergraph maximizing Jaccard-similarity isomorphism across all subjects' initial hypergraphs, and cascaded Interactive Hyperedge Neuron (IHEN) layers perform vertex-hyperedge-vertex message passing to produce a multimodal connectivity tensor; a discriminator trained on random-walk trajectory probabilities over the functional connectivity matrix drives the generator to match the empirical fMRI-EEG joint distribution. The FED module embeds the generated hypergraphs via a Neural ODE whose velocity function is decomposed by Lie-Trotter splitting into a control step (a Dual-Stream Control Function with LSTM and attention blocks capturing modality-specific temporal dynamics) and a diffusion step (a structured matrix A with teleportation parameters α_v and α_e that propagates information across the hypergraph's vertex-hyperedge incidence structure). This control-diffusion ODE formulation naturally accommodates the mismatched sampling rates of fMRI and EEG without explicit temporal alignment, and the hypergraph topology encodes the non-one-to-many ROI correspondences that pairwise graphs cannot represent.

## Results

On the LEMON dataset (227 participants), FE-NET achieves the highest accuracy across all four cognitive classification tasks: 85.29±2.57% (TAP-WST, sustained attention), 79.18±3.47% (TMT-LPS, cognitive flexibility), 82.19±2.84% (verbal intelligence), and 77.38±3.47% (logical intelligence). These results outperform the strongest baseline MMP-GCN by 5.87, 12.89, 4.90, and 10.54 percentage points respectively, while also exhibiting notably lower standard deviations (e.g., ±2.57 vs. ±5.19 on TAP-WST), indicating more consistent performance across 5-fold cross-validation. Generalization to the synchronized CN-EPFL dataset (20 subjects) further confirms FE-NET's advantage over all baselines in both cross-dataset and in-dataset settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FE-NET outperforms all eight baselines on every task and metric. Among single-modal methods (Group A), BrainGNN, M-GAT-BC, and SGP-SL trail FE-NET by 10–30 percentage points in accuracy, confirming the benefit of fusing fMRI and EEG. Among multimodal methods (Group B), MMP-GCN is the closest competitor, reaching 79.42% on TAP-WST and 77.29% on verbal intelligence, yet FE-NET still leads by 5.87 and 4.90 points respectively. Cross-GNN (74.83% on TAP-WST) and TAN (73.12%) are the next strongest multimodal baselines, both surpassed by FE-NET by over 10 points; the paper attributes their inferiority to reliance on static graph representations that cannot capture the non-one-to-one ROI correspondence between modalities. RH-BrainFS and MCRLN show the weakest multimodal performance, with RH-BrainFS dropping to 61.29% on TMT-LPS and MCRLN to 61.29% on logical intelligence, far below FE-NET's 79.18% and 77.38%. Ablation variants (FE-NET without FEH or FED) confirm that both the GAN-based hypergraph generation and the Neural ODE embedding contribute meaningfully, with removal of either module degrading accuracy by 4–10 points.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: hypergraph neural networks (HGNN, Hyper-Atten, HyperGCN), Neural ODEs for continuous-time dynamics, and GAN-based distribution alignment. It departs from prior hypergraph learning, which is predominantly defined on static structures, by formulating hypergraph representation evolution as an ODE system with Lie-Trotter splitting that separates modality-specific control dynamics from cross-modal diffusion. The multimodal brain imaging fusion line (Cross-GNN, TAN, RH-BrainFS, MCRLN, MMP-GCN) has focused on simultaneously recorded data with pairwise or static graph structures; FE-NET extends this to asynchronous recordings and non-pairwise (hyperedge) relationships, motivated by the finding that fMRI-EEG ROI correspondences are not one-to-one.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical motivation is strong: asynchronous fMRI-EEG acquisition is far cheaper and more widely available than simultaneous recording, yet prior methods have been constrained to small synchronized datasets. If the approach generalizes, it could unlock multimodal brain imaging research using existing single-modality archives. However, the paper lacks a dedicated limitations discussion, does not report compute resources, and the evidence base rests on a single primary dataset (LEMON, N=227) with one small synchronized comparison set (CN-EPFL, N=20), leaving open questions about scalability, sensitivity to the GAN training dynamics, and whether the Neural ODE component is truly necessary versus a well-tuned RNN (the ablation with RNN/LSTM/GRU is deferred to supplementary material).
