## Problem

Graph-based learned simulators for solid mechanics struggle with two coupled limitations: local message passing restricts the receptive field, making it difficult to capture global phenomena like beam bending or long-range stress propagation, and direct next-step prediction with MSE loss causes error accumulation over long rollouts by overlooking low-amplitude high-frequency components. Additionally, diffusion-based refinement, while promising for multi-frequency accuracy, incurs K model evaluations per physical time step, making it impractically slow for long simulations.

## Input / Output

The model consumes an unstructured mesh graph (triangles in 2D, tetrahedrons in 3D) with node positions, a noisy displacement residual at a given diffusion step k, the previous fully denoised state, and task-specific node features (applied forces, density, Young's modulus, or actuator displacement residuals). It outputs a per-node denoising velocity that, combined with the DDPM scheduler, iteratively refines the displacement residual and von Mises stress. The model operates autoregressively, conditioning each step only on the immediately preceding reconstructed state, and predicts residuals (Δu) rather than absolute states to improve long-horizon fidelity.

## Method

ROBIN combines two key innovations. First, Rolling Diffusion-Batched Inference (ROBI) parallelizes denoising across a rolling temporal window: after m denoising steps at time t, it initializes and begins denoising time t+1 in the same batch, reducing model calls from O(KT) to O(T) for stride m=1 while preserving time-shift equivariance. Second, the Algebraic-hierarchical Message Passing Network (AMPN) builds a V-cycle over a mesh hierarchy constructed via root-node AMG coarsening, with shared Pre-Processing, Downsampling, Solving, Upsampling, and Post-Processing modules that propagate information across L+1 resolution levels. The model is trained with a v-prediction DDPM objective (MSE on the denoising velocity) using an exponential noise schedule, where early steps capture global low-frequency structure and later steps refine local high-frequency details. The hierarchical architecture with shared layers enables efficient transfer to meshes an order of magnitude larger without architectural changes.

## Results

ROBIN achieves state-of-the-art displacement RMSE on all three solid mechanics benchmarks: 29.00×10⁻³ on BENDINGBEAM (vs. 121.53 for HCMT, 189.52 for MGN, 141.98 for BSMS), 12.33×10⁻³ on IMPACTPLATE (vs. 19.57, 54.07, 63.52), and 4.98×10⁻³ on DEFORMINGPLATE (vs. 8.04, 8.76, 13.59). The default ROBI inference setting (stride m=1, full K=20 denoising) runs in 15.03 s on BENDINGBEAM, roughly 10× faster than conventional autoregressive diffusion (162.43 s) while matching its accuracy (29.00 vs. 28.90 RMSE). On the 12× larger BENDINGBEAMLARGE meshes, fine-tuning a pre-trained ROBIN yields 77.59×10⁻³ RMSE versus 215.50×10⁻³ for a from-scratch model, demonstrating efficient transfer to substantially larger geometries.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ROBIN outperforms all three baselines (HCMT, MGN, BSMS) on every dataset in terms of displacement RMSE. The margin is largest on BENDINGBEAM, where ROBIN's 29.00×10⁻³ RMSE is roughly 4× lower than HCMT (121.53), 6.5× lower than MGN (189.52), and 4.9× lower than BSMS (141.98), reflecting the difficulty of propagating local boundary conditions across high-aspect-ratio beams. On IMPACTPLATE, ROBIN (12.33) beats HCMT (19.57) by ~37% and MGN (54.07) by ~4.4×, while on DEFORMINGPLATE the gap is more modest: ROBIN (4.98) vs. HCMT (8.04) and MGN (8.76). In terms of inference speed, ROBIN is slower than the fastest baselines (BSMS at 4.70 s, MGN at 2.93 s on BENDINGBEAM) but substantially faster than HCMT (25.82 s); its key speed advantage is over conventional diffusion inference, where ROBI reduces runtime from 162.43 s to 15.03 s (~10×) on BENDINGBEAM without accuracy loss. The paper also shows that even the fastest ROBIN variant (1/1, single denoising step) still outperforms all baselines on BENDINGBEAM (64.23 vs. 121.53–189.52), indicating that the diffusion formulation itself, not just the multi-step refinement, contributes to the accuracy gain.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two lines of work: diffusion-based iterative refinement for PDE solvers (PDE-Refiner, Lippe et al. 2023) and hierarchical graph neural networks for mesh-based simulation (MeshGraphNets, Pfaff et al. 2020; HCMT, Yu et al. 2023; BSMS, Cao et al. 2023). It departs from prior mesh simulators by replacing direct next-step MSE prediction with a DDPM-based denoising process, and from prior diffusion simulators (which operate on grids or isolated frames) by grounding the denoiser in an Algebraic-hierarchical Message Passing Network (AMPN) built on algebraic multigrid coarsening. The key conceptual departure is ROBI, a rolling inference schedule that overlaps denoising steps across consecutive physical time steps, reducing inference from O(KT) to O(T) model evaluations while preserving time-shift equivariance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ROBIN addresses a fundamental limitation of local message-passing simulators: their inability to capture global structural phenomena (bending, long-range stiffness coupling) without prohibitively deep networks. The ROBI inference scheme is broadly applicable to any autoregressive diffusion simulator and offers a practical order-of-magnitude speedup that makes diffusion-based simulation competitive with one-step models in wall-clock time. The BENDINGBEAM dataset provides a useful diagnostic benchmark that exposes the global-receptive-field bottleneck in existing simulators. Open questions include whether the approach extends to fluid dynamics, whether SO(3) equivariance can be incorporated, and whether alternative diffusion formulations (score-based, flow-matching) could further improve efficiency or accuracy.
