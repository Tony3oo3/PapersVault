## Problem

Existing failure detectors for robot manipulation policies are trained and evaluated on one or a few specific tasks, making them impractical for generalist vision-language-action (VLA) models that must handle diverse, unseen tasks at deployment. This paper introduces the multitask failure detection problem, where a single detector must generalize to novel tasks and environments without collecting additional rollouts or fine-tuning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251337/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

SAFE consumes the last-layer hidden state vectors of a VLA model (aggregated from multiple token positions or diffusion steps into a single fixed-dimensional embedding per timestep) and produces a scalar failure score at each timestep. When this score exceeds a time-varying threshold calibrated via functional conformal prediction, a binary failure flag is raised, triggering task abort or human takeover. The VLA itself ingests RGB images, a natural language instruction, and proprioceptive robot state, but SAFE only requires access to the VLA's internal features, not the raw observations.

## Method

SAFE is built on the key observation that VLA internal features occupy a distinct 'failure zone' in latent space that is consistent across different tasks, objects, and environments. The detector is a lightweight 1–2 layer MLP or LSTM that regresses the sequence of VLA embeddings up to timestep t to a scalar failure score: the MLP variant applies a per-timestep sigmoid projection and accumulates scores (trained with L1 loss), while the LSTM variant sequentially processes the feature stream and outputs a sigmoid-normalized score (trained with binary cross-entropy). To set the detection threshold in a principled, distribution-free way, SAFE adopts functional conformal prediction, calibrating a one-sided time-varying prediction band on successful rollouts from seen tasks so that the false-positive rate on any new successful rollout is bounded by a user-specified α. Training uses both successful and failed rollouts from multiple tasks with class-frequency-weighted losses and L2 regularization, and the detector is then evaluated zero-shot on held-out unseen tasks.

## Results

On simulation benchmarks (LIBERO-10 and SimplerEnv), SAFE-MLP achieves the highest average ROC-AUC of 78.00% on unseen tasks and 81.43% on seen tasks, outperforming the best baseline (Cosine k-NN at 73.93% unseen) by ~4%. On real-world experiments, SAFE-MLP reaches 88.42% ROC-AUC on OpenVLA+WidowX unseen tasks and 64.16% on π₀-FAST+Franka unseen tasks, again the best among all methods. SAFE introduces less than 1% computational overhead (0.73 ms for the 2.3M-parameter LSTM) compared to the VLA's own inference time (e.g., 149 ms for π₀).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAFE outperforms all baselines on average across simulation benchmarks, with SAFE-MLP achieving 78.00% unseen ROC-AUC versus 73.93% for the best embedding-distance baseline (Cosine k-NN) and 73.65% for STAC-Single [18]. Token uncertainty methods adapted from the LLM literature [22] (max/avg probability, max/avg entropy) perform near chance level (50–62% ROC-AUC), confirming that token-level confidence is a poor proxy for robotic task failure. Sample consistency methods [22, 23] (action/translation/rotation/gripper variance, cluster entropy) achieve 62–71% unseen ROC-AUC but require 10+ action samples per timestep, making them impractical for real-time VLA control. Learned OOD detectors LogpZO [8] and RND [39] reach 70.97% and 65.95% unseen ROC-AUC respectively, with LogpZO overfitting to training tasks in the multitask setting. On real-world Franka and WidowX experiments, SAFE-MLP achieves the best unseen-task ROC-AUC (64.16% and 88.42%), beating the strongest embedding-distance baselines (Euclidean k-NN at 60.27% and Mahalanobis at 70.00%) by 4 and 18 points respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on runtime failure detection for robot policies (Xu et al. 2025, Agia et al. 2025, Liu et al. 2024) by reframing it as a multitask problem where a single detector must generalize to unseen tasks without collecting new rollouts. It draws on the functional conformal prediction framework (Diquigiovanni et al. 2024) and the one-sided time-varying CP band formulation from Xu et al. (2025) for principled threshold calibration. Conceptually, the method is inspired by LLM hallucination detection via internal feature probing (Azaria & Mitchell 2023, Li et al. 2023), repurposing that idea for robotic manipulation: the key departure is the empirical finding that VLA latent features already encode task-generic success/failure structure, making a lightweight probe sufficient rather than requiring multi-sample consistency scores or VLM queries.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As VLAs move toward real-world deployment with 30–60% out-of-the-box success rates on unseen tasks, a safe and efficient failure detector is a prerequisite for practical use; SAFE provides this with <1% computational overhead and a distribution-free false-positive guarantee via conformal prediction. The multitask formulation is critical because generalist policies will inevitably encounter novel instructions and environments where per-task detector training is infeasible. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes across embodiments, sim-to-real gaps, and action-less video data, and how to leverage multi-layer features rather than only the last layer. The requirement to collect hundreds of labeled rollouts across multiple tasks before deployment remains a practical barrier for truly zero-shot settings.
