## Problem

Local SGD (Federated Averaging) consistently outperforms mini-batch SGD in practice, yet existing convergence theory under data heterogeneity either fails to demonstrate this advantage or relies on overly restrictive assumptions that preclude meaningful variation across machines. Patel et al. (2024) conjectured that a second-order heterogeneity condition—bounding Hessian differences across clients—combined with standard first-order conditions could explain the gap, but matching upper and lower bounds under these broader conditions remained open.

## Input / Output

The analysis takes M machines with local data distributions D_m, a loss function f(x; z), and hyperparameters (step-size η, local steps K, communication rounds R) as input, and produces convergence bounds on function-value suboptimality and communication complexity as output. The bounds are parameterized by second-order heterogeneity τ, first-order heterogeneity constants ζ* and φ*, and third-order smoothness Q, and they interpolate smoothly between the homogeneous regime (τ = 0, where constant communication rounds suffice) and the heterogeneous regime. A key structural feature is that the communication complexity scales with τ rather than the more conservative smoothness constant H, which is what makes Local SGD communication-efficient when client tasks are aligned.

## Method

The core technical contribution is a new coupled recursion for the consensus error (Lemma 2) that bounds the deviation between local and global iterates using only the distance-between-optima, distance-to-average-optimum, and bounded-Hessian-differences assumptions, avoiding the restrictive uniform gradient similarity assumption (Assumption 7) used in prior work. This bound improves with lower τ and incorporates the expected iterate error at the most recent communication round, which decreases over time, enabling sharper convergence guarantees. Combining this with a canonical one-step progress lemma yields upper bounds for strongly convex objectives (Theorems 2–3) showing Local SGD provably dominates mini-batch SGD when τ is small. For third-order smooth functions, the authors derive fourth-moment bounds on the consensus error and handle four coupled recursions via matrix algebra (Theorem 6), while a new lower bound (Theorem 1) decouples τ from H by introducing an additional dimension to the hard instance, confirming that communication complexity is governed by τ rather than H.

## Results

The paper establishes that Local SGD's communication complexity for target accuracy ε scales as Õ(Hτ/μ² + τHφ⋆/(μ²√ε) + H²ζ⋆/(μ²√ε)) in the strongly convex setting, where τ is the second-order heterogeneity constant. For quadratic objectives (Q=0), this improves to Õ(τ²/μ² + τ²φ⋆/(μ²√ε) + τHζ⋆/(μ²√ε)), which becomes a constant number of rounds when τ=0. In contrast, mini-batch SGD requires Õ(κ) rounds and cannot improve with lower heterogeneity. The matching lower bound (Theorem 1) confirms that Ω(τB²/R) communication rounds are necessary, validating that τ—not the more conservative smoothness constant H—governs Local SGD's communication complexity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Koloskova et al. [18], this paper's upper bounds (Theorem 3) replace the H³φ⋆²/(μ²R²) term with τ²Hφ⋆²/(μ²R²), showing that the dependence on the smoothness constant H is reduced by a factor of H²/τ² when second-order heterogeneity is low. Against Woodworth et al. [20], the key improvement is removing the need for Assumption 7 (uniform bounded first-order heterogeneity), which was so restrictive it only allowed variation in linear terms; instead, the new consensus error bound (Lemma 2) depends on the milder Assumptions 4–6. Against Patel et al. [25], this paper removes the same restrictive Assumption 7 while also providing a matching lower bound that decays gracefully with τ (their lower bound degenerates when τ=0). Most importantly, the paper proves the first-ever domination of Local SGD over mini-batch SGD under reasonable heterogeneity: Local SGD's communication complexity Õ(Hτ/μ² + τHφ⋆/(μ²√ε) + H²ζ⋆/(μ²√ε)) improves with lower τ, ζ⋆, and φ⋆, while mini-batch SGD's Õ(κ) is invariant to data heterogeneity. For quadratic objectives, the gap widens further: Local SGD needs only Õ(τ²/μ²) rounds (constant when τ=0), while mini-batch SGD still requires Õ(κ) rounds.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Patel et al. (COLT 2024), who conjectured that a second-order heterogeneity condition (bounded Hessian similarity τ) combined with standard first-order assumptions explains Local SGD's empirical advantage over mini-batch SGD. It also extends the consensus error analysis framework of Woodworth et al. (NeurIPS 2020), replacing their restrictive uniform gradient-boundedness assumption (Assumption 7) with a coupled recursion that depends only on the distance between optima (ζ*), Hessian similarity (τ), and fixed-point discrepancy (φ*). The lower bound construction extends Patel et al.'s hard instance by adding a dimension to decouple τ from the smoothness constant H, and the upper bounds incorporate third-order smoothness (Q) to interpolate between general convex and quadratic regimes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first result to prove that Local SGD provably dominates mini-batch SGD under reasonable heterogeneity assumptions (depending only on τ, ζ*, φ* rather than the conservative ζ), resolving a long-standing gap between theory and practice that Wang et al. termed the 'unreasonable effectiveness' of Local SGD. The practical implication for federated learning is that communication efficiency is governed by Hessian similarity (covariate shift) rather than gradient dissimilarity, suggesting that large local steps K remain beneficial even when optimal predictors differ across clients. Open questions include extending the upper bounds to general convex (μ=0) objectives without stringent constraints, decoupling ζ* and φ* in lower bounds, and whether the fourth-moment analysis in Theorem 6 can be tightened to remove extraneous terms.
