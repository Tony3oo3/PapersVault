## Problem

Offline safe RL must learn high-reward policies from a fixed dataset while respecting cost constraints, but faces two coupled challenges: distribution shift that causes critic overestimation and out-of-distribution actions, and a fundamental tension between reward maximization and cost minimization that existing Lagrangian or penalty-based methods handle poorly without on-policy data. No prior method simultaneously provides a principled behavioral regularizer and a gradient-level mechanism to resolve reward-cost conflicts in a purely offline setting.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a static offline dataset of (state, action, reward, next_state, cost) tuples collected by an unknown behavioral policy, along with a cost budget l. At inference, it takes only a state vector and outputs a single action sampled from a Gaussian policy π_θ(a|s) = N(a; m_θ(s), Σ_θ(s)), requiring no iterative denoising steps. The diffusion model is used exclusively during training as a score-function regularizer, so the deployed policy is a lightweight MLP with constant-variance Gaussian output, achieving inference speeds comparable to standard actor-critic baselines.

## Method

DRCORL first pretrains a conditional diffusion model on the offline dataset to approximate the behavioral policy π_b, then extracts a simple Gaussian policy by optimizing reward and cost critic values regularized by the reverse-KL divergence to the diffusion policy. The key mechanism is that the diffusion model's learned denoising function ε_ψ provides a tractable surrogate for the score ∇_a log μ_ψ(a|s), enabling closed-form gradient computation of the reverse-KL term without any diffusion sampling at inference. For the reward-cost trade-off, the method applies gradient manipulation: when the estimated cost (via a UCB ensemble of pessimistic cost critics) is safely below the budget, reward gradients are used alone; when above, cost gradients dominate; and in a transition band, conflicting gradients (angle > 90°) are resolved by projecting each onto the other's null space before averaging. Theoretical guarantees include a cost upper bound (Proposition 3.1) showing the learned policy's cost is bounded by the behavioral policy's cost plus a term scaling with √ε_dist, and a convergence theorem (Theorem 3.1) establishing O(1/√T) optimality and constraint-violation gaps under tabular NPG updates.

## Results

On the DSRL benchmark (13 tasks across Safety-Gymnasium and BulletSafety-Gym), DRCORL is the only method that satisfies the safety constraint (normalized cost ≤ 1.0) on all 13 tasks. Among safe policies, DRCORL achieves the highest normalized reward on 8 of 13 tasks, with standout results on CarGoal1 (0.91 reward, 0.00 cost), PointGoal1 (0.88, 0.00), CarRun (0.99, 0.30), and BallCircle (0.78, 0.00). Inference speed is competitive with lightweight MLP-based methods (BCQ, CPQ) while far exceeding diffusion-based baselines (TREBI, FISOR), as shown in the efficiency-vs-performance trade-off plot.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DRCORL is the only method that remains safe (normalized cost ≤ 1.0) across all 13 tasks; every other baseline violates the cost constraint on at least one task (e.g., BEARL exceeds cost by 100×+ on HalfCheetahVel and HopperVel; BCQ-Lag and CPQ frequently exceed the budget on Point* and Car* tasks). Among safe policies, DRCORL achieves the highest reward on 8/13 tasks, with the largest margins on CarGoal1 (0.91 vs. 0.38 for BC-Safe), PointGoal1 (0.88 vs. 0.53 for BC-Safe), and BallCircle (0.78 vs. 0.55 for BC-Safe). On the remaining 5 tasks, DRCORL is slightly outperformed by specific baselines: CDT edges it on AntVel (0.91 vs. 0.88 reward), BC-Safe and CDT lead on HalfCheetahVel (0.94/0.96 vs. 0.86), CAPS wins on HopperVel (0.83 vs. 0.68), and BCQ-Lag/CAPS lead on Walker2dVel (0.80/0.78 vs. 0.74). The paper also benchmarks inference speed: DRCORL's extracted Gaussian policy matches the speed of MLP-based baselines (BCQ, CPQ) while being orders of magnitude faster than diffusion-based methods TREBI and FISOR, which require ≥10 denoising steps per action.

## Relation to Prior Work

DRCORL unifies two previously orthogonal threads: diffusion-based behavioral policy modeling (Chen et al. 2023, Wang et al. 2022) and gradient-manipulation-based safe RL (Gu et al. 2024a). Rather than using the diffusion model at inference (as in Diffusion Policy or IDQL), it trains the diffusion model solely to estimate the behavioral policy's score function, which then serves as a tractable reverse-KL regularizer for a simple Gaussian policy. The gradient manipulation mechanism is adapted from the online safe RL setting to the offline regime, where on-policy data is unavailable, and is combined with a UCB-based cost critic ensemble to guard against off-policy cost underestimation. The reward critic is pretrained with IQL, while the cost critic uses pessimistic TD learning, drawing on the conservative offline RL literature (Kostrikov et al. 2021, Xu et al. 2022).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical recipe for offline safe RL that avoids the slow inference of diffusion-based policies while retaining their expressiveness as a training-time regularizer, and it backs this with a cost upper bound (Proposition 3.1) and convergence guarantees (Theorem 3.1) in the tabular setting. Key limitations include the additional pretraining overhead (diffusion model + two critic families), the theoretical proof dropping the KL term for tractability, and dependence on dataset coverage—sparse or biased data may cause the diffusion model to under-represent safe modes. Open questions include whether alternative generative priors (VAEs, normalizing flows) can replace the diffusion estimator under the same framework, and how to tighten safety guarantees when dataset quality is imperfect.
