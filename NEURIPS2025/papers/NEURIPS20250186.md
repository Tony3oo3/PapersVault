## Problem

Applying machine learning to agricultural water management is hindered by the absence of fine-grained, large-scale datasets that fuse the diverse modalities—satellite imagery, soil properties, hydrology, and climate—needed to model water availability. Existing irrigation mapping products either lack irrigation-type granularity, operate at coarse resolutions, or do not integrate the invisible physical factors (soil texture, drainage, groundwater) that drive irrigation decisions. The paper addresses this by constructing a unified, ML-ready multimodal dataset and a reproducible pipeline for fusing heterogeneous geospatial sources.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250186/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each sample is a 224×224 pixel-aligned patch extracted from 10-band Sentinel-2 imagery (10 m native, 20 m bands upsampled) at 10 m resolution, paired with a rule-based natural-language text prompt encoding dominant soil components (texture, drainage class, hydrologic group, slope, geomorphic description), plus tabular environmental features (monthly ET, groundwater depth, surface water elevation, precipitation) and categorical crop/land-use labels. The primary output is a per-pixel irrigation-type segmentation mask over three classes (Flood, Sprinkler, Drip); secondary outputs include crop classification labels and continuous environmental variable predictions. The dataset comprises 1,484,301 patches across 20 contiguous US states, with 293,890 labeled examples from six states and 1,190,206 unlabeled examples.

## Method

The core technical contribution is a fully automated geospatial fusion pipeline that harmonizes 6.8 TB of raster, vector, and point-based data from 11 public sources onto a common 10 m Albers Equal Area Conic (EPSG:5070) grid using spatial joins, nearest-neighbor reprojection for categorical layers, bilinear sampling for continuous rasters, and centroid-based lookup for station-level hydrology. A domain-informed, rule-based prompt generator converts structured soil attributes (e.g., 'Tulare, sandy loam, well drained, moderate runoff, 3.2% slope') into coherent natural-language descriptions, enabling alignment with vision-language models without one-hot or numerical encoding. To prevent spatial leakage, train/validate/test splits are performed at the satellite-tile level rather than the individual-patch level, and a leave-one-state-out protocol evaluates cross-regional generalization. Benchmarking spans nine architectures—CNNs (ResNet, FPN, DeepLabV3+), transformers (ViT, SegFormer, FarSeg), and vision-language models (CLIP, RemoteCLIP, KIIM)—demonstrating that structured multimodal inputs (RGB + crop + land use, or RGB + text) substantially outperform RGB-only baselines, with KIIM achieving the highest Dice scores (93.6% Flood, 95.8% Sprinkler, 94.6% Drip).

## Results

On the IRRISIGHT dataset, KIIM (RGB + Crop + Land) achieves the highest Dice scores across all three irrigation types: 93.6% (Flood), 95.8% (Sprinkler), and 94.6% (Drip), with corresponding IoU of 88.0%, 91.9%, and 89.7%. Vision-language models with text prompts also outperform RGB-only baselines: RemoteCLIP reaches 92.3% Dice on Drip (vs. 88.5% for ResNet RGB-only), and CLIP achieves 90.7% Dice on Drip. Among RGB-only architectures, FarSeg leads with 89.2% Dice on Drip, while ResNet performs worst on Flood (35.2% Dice). Cross-state generalization (leave-one-state-out) reveals significant degradation for RGB-only models, particularly for Drip irrigation in CO and UT where even KIIM scores below 15% Dice.

## Baselines & Comparisons

KIIM (RGB + Crop + Land) outperforms all baselines across every irrigation type, achieving 94.6% Dice on Drip versus 89.2% for the best RGB-only model (FarSeg) and 92.3% for the best vision-language model (RemoteCLIP). The gap between multimodal and RGB-only approaches is most pronounced on Flood irrigation, where KIIM scores 93.6% Dice compared to 88.2% for FarSeg and only 35.2% for ResNet. RemoteCLIP and CLIP, which incorporate structured text prompts, consistently surpass all six RGB-only architectures (ResNet, ViT, FPN, SegFormer, DeepLabV3+, FarSeg) by 2–5 Dice points on average, confirming that language-grounded supervision adds value beyond architecture alone. In the leave-one-state-out cross-state setting, KIIM maintains the strongest generalization, while CLIP and RemoteCLIP degrade in states with less distinctive irrigation patterns (e.g., WA, FL), and RGB-only models suffer the largest drops. Drip irrigation remains the hardest class for all models, with cross-state Dice falling below 15% for KIIM in CO and UT, highlighting the challenge of fine-grained, low-contrast irrigation footprints.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the authors' own line of work on irrigation type mapping (IrrNet, KIIM, IrrMap) by scaling from 4–6 states to 20 states and from primarily visual inputs to a fully multimodal representation that fuses Sentinel-2 imagery with soil surveys (SSURGO), hydrological station data (NWIS), evapotranspiration rasters (FEWS), and crop/land-cover layers. The key conceptual departure from prior irrigation mapping efforts (AIM-HPA, LANID, IrrMapper, MIrAD) is the introduction of domain-informed structured text prompts derived from soil attributes as a first-class modality for vision-language model alignment, rather than treating non-visual features as auxiliary numerical channels. The scalable geospatial fusion pipeline (raster, vector, and point data onto a unified 10 m grid) generalizes the ad-hoc preprocessing in earlier works into a reproducible, extensible system.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dataset fills a critical gap: no prior large-scale, fine-grained, multimodal resource existed for agricultural water availability at national scale, and the 1.4 M aligned patches with a spatially disjoint split provide a much-needed benchmark for cross-state generalization. A notable open challenge highlighted by the results is the consistently poor cross-state performance on drip irrigation (Dice < 15 % in CO and UT even for the best model), suggesting that fine-grained, low-contrast irrigation patterns remain difficult without target-region labels. The modular pipeline and text-prompt generation strategy are directly reusable for related tasks (flash drought detection, fallowing prediction, irrigation suitability mapping), but the reliance on only six labeled states and the acknowledged risk of raster reprojection artifacts limit immediate deployment confidence in unlabeled regions.
