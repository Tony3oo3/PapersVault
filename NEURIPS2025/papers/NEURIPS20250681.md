## Problem

Existing warm-start bandit methods assume offline and online data share the same feedback structure (both absolute or both relative) and the same underlying distribution. In practice, offline data often originates from different sources with heterogeneous feedback types and distributional shifts, yet no prior work simultaneously addresses both heterogeneity and bias in the MAB framework. This paper tackles the fundamental question of how to effectively leverage heterogeneous offline data with potential bias to accelerate online bandit learning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250681/cropped_page9_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework operates in two complementary settings: (1) offline absolute rewards (1-subgaussian) inform online dueling bandits with pairwise Bernoulli feedback under the Bradley-Terry model, and (2) offline pairwise preferences inform online stochastic bandits with 1-subgaussian rewards. A critical input is the valid bias bound V_{i,j} ≥ |p_{i,j}^{off} − p_{i,j}|, which quantifies the distributional shift and controls how much offline data is trusted; when V_{i,j} ≥ 1, the algorithm gracefully degrades to pure online learning. The output is a per-round arm (or arm pair) selection policy with provable instance-dependent and instance-independent regret guarantees.

## Method

The core technical contribution is a Minimum Variance Unbiased Estimator (MVUE) that optimally combines heterogeneous offline and online data by weighting each source inversely proportional to its variance, with the Bradley-Terry model used to transform absolute data into relative form (or vice versa). Two UCBs are maintained per arm pair—a pure online UCB and a hybrid UCB that incorporates the offline estimate plus a pessimistic bias term proportional to V_{i,j}—and the candidate set is their intersection, ensuring the algorithm never performs worse than pure online learning. For HybUCB-AR, the algorithm selects the arm pair maximizing min{UCB, UCB^hyb} within the candidate set, which improves upon RUCB by replacing the denominator min{Δ_i², Δ_j²} with max{Δ_i², Δ_j²} in the regret bound. The regret analysis reveals a 'Saving' term proportional to the harmonic mean of offline sample sizes and identifies an 'intermediate phase' where the benefit of offline data is jointly determined by sample size and distributional shift magnitude.

## Results

The primary quantitative result is that HybUCB-AR outperforms RUCB by 15% to 40% in cumulative regret, even without any offline data, purely by maximizing informative pairs within the candidate set. When heterogeneous offline data is incorporated, both HybUCB-AR and HybElimUCB-RA further reduce regret compared to their respective online-only baselines. The theoretical contribution provides instance-dependent regret upper bounds of O(Σ(Δ_i+Δ_j)log T / max{Δ_i²,Δ_j²} − Saving) for HybUCB-AR and O(Σ log T / Δ_i − Saving) for HybElimUCB-RA, where the 'Saving' term quantifies the benefit from offline data. Experiments are conducted on synthetic data and the MovieLens-20M dataset (with Yelp in the appendix), using K=8 to 32 arms over 50,000 rounds (synthetic) and 10,000 rounds (real-world).

## Baselines & Comparisons

For the online dueling (preference-based) setting, HybUCB-AR is compared against IF (Yue et al., 2012) and RUCB (Zoghi et al., 2014). HybUCB-AR outperforms RUCB by 15–40% in cumulative regret even in the absence of offline data, due to its improved candidate-set exploration strategy that replaces the min{Δ_i²,Δ_j²} denominator with max{Δ_i²,Δ_j²}. When offline absolute feedback is added, the gap widens further. For the online stochastic (reward-based) setting, HybElimUCB-RA is compared against ETC, vanilla UCB (Lattimore and Szepesvári, 2020), and Thompson Sampling (Agrawal and Goyal, 2013). HybElimUCB-RA surpasses all three baselines when offline preference data is available, with the regret reduction scaling with the number of offline comparisons involving the optimal arm. In the worst case where offline data is uninformative (large bias V_{i,j}), both algorithms gracefully degrade to match the performance of their pure-online counterparts (RUCB and vanilla elimination UCB, respectively), ensuring no performance penalty from incorporating biased offline data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends two established lines of bandit research: (1) dueling bandits with the RUCB algorithm (Zoghi et al., 2014), where the authors improve the regret bound by replacing the denominator min{Δ_i², Δ_j²} with max{Δ_i², Δ_j²} through a more informative pair selection strategy, and (2) warm-start bandits with biased offline data (Cheung and Lyu, 2024), where the valid bias bound V_{i,j} framework is adopted and refined. The key conceptual departure is that all prior warm-start and dueling bandit work assumes homogeneous feedback structure between offline and online data, whereas this paper introduces a hybrid estimator (MVUE-based) that unifies absolute and relative feedback under the Bradley-Terry model, explicitly handling the offline-to-online distributional shift. The paper positions itself as the first to simultaneously address heterogeneous feedback types and distributional bias in the MAB framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical motivation is strong: recommendation systems, RLHF, and robotics routinely face scenarios where offline data (e.g., explicit ratings or preference rankings) differs in format from online feedback (e.g., pairwise comparisons or scalar rewards), and the 'Saving' term provides a clean, interpretable quantification of when and how much offline data helps. However, the framework requires a valid bias bound V_{i,j} as input, which is often unavailable in practice, and the BT model assumption limits generality. Open questions include extending to linear bandits, adversarial environments, and settings where heterogeneity exists within (rather than between) offline and online data. The HybElimUCB-RA algorithm also does not perfectly match vanilla UCB performance when offline data is uninformative, leaving a gap in the worst-case guarantee.
