## Problem

Existing equivariant GNNs for physical dynamics simulation assume stationarity, yet real-world trajectories (molecular, protein, human motion) exhibit non-stationary statistical properties where mean, variance, and covariance shift over time. Prior non-stationary time-series methods rely on normalization that breaks the E(3) symmetry essential for physical dynamics, creating a fundamental tension between handling distributional drift and preserving geometric equivariance.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a historical trajectory of T timesteps represented as a graph with 3D node coordinates, scalar node features (e.g., atom types), and edge features, where only the position vectors evolve over time. It outputs a multi-step future trajectory of Δt timesteps (e.g., 10 steps with Δt=5 for MD17), predicting the full 3D coordinate sequence rather than a single next frame. The PFT module segments the input trajectory into overlapping windows of configurable length, and the NS-Pooling layer aggregates first- and second-order differences to produce the final prediction.

## Method

NS-EGNN introduces two key modules around an EGNN backbone: (1) a Patch Fourier Transform (PFT) that applies windowed (Hamming) DFT to overlapping segments of the trajectory at multiple scales, extracting time-varying frequency features that are provably E(n)-invariant, thus encoding non-stationary variance without breaking symmetry; and (2) an equivariant non-stationary temporal pooling (NS-Pooling) layer that computes first- and second-order differences of the EGNN output coordinates to remove linear and quadratic trends, then learns a weighted combination with the final position for multi-step prediction. The EGNN backbone performs standard equivariant message passing using relative distances, augmented with the invariant frequency features as additional node attributes. The entire pipeline is proven E(3)-equivariant (Theorem 3.2), and training uses MSE loss on predicted versus ground-truth positions, with no additional temporal attention modules needed since PFT already captures temporal structure at O(N log N) cost.

## Results

On the MD17 molecular dynamics benchmark (100-frame input, 10-step prediction), NS-EGNN achieves the best Average Displacement Error (ADE) across all 8 molecules, with headline values of 0.421×10⁻³ (Aspirin), 0.050×10⁻³ (Benzene), and 0.275×10⁻³ (Naphthalene), yielding a 29.77% relative ADE improvement and 33.33% FDE improvement over the prior SOTA (ESTAG). On CMU Motion Capture, NS-EGNN delivers an 18.41% overall MSE reduction (21.52% on Walk, 15.29% on Run) across time gaps of 5–20 timesteps. On the AdK protein dynamics dataset, NS-EGNN achieves the lowest MSE of 1.738, narrowly surpassing ST-GMN (1.743) and ESTAG (1.758).

## Baselines & Comparisons

NS-EGNN outperforms all 11 baselines on every MD17 molecule in both ADE and FDE. The closest competitor, ESTAG (NeurIPS 2024), is surpassed by 29.77% on average ADE and 33.33% on FDE, with the largest gap on Aspirin (0.421 vs 0.740, a 42.74% relative gain). Non-equivariant baselines STGCN and AGL-STAN fail catastrophically on several molecules (e.g., AGL-STAN reaches 587.048 ADE on Aspirin; STGCN reaches 214.904 on Ethanol), confirming that symmetry constraints are essential for physical dynamics. Among equivariant baselines, ST-EGNN (the backbone without PFT/NS-Pooling) is 7.7× worse on Aspirin (3.257 vs 0.421), and ST-SE(3)TR, ST-TFN, and EqMotion all trail NS-EGNN by 2–9×. On CMU Motion Capture, NS-EGNN wins in 7 of 8 time-gap settings; AGL-STAN edges out NS-EGNN only on Run at 20 timesteps (0.831 vs 1.226 MSE). On AdK protein dynamics, NS-EGNN (1.738 MSE) narrowly beats ST-GMN (1.743) and ESTAG (1.758), consistent with the dataset's weaker non-stationarity (48.7% non-stationary ratio vs >94% for MD17 molecules).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on EGNN (Satorras et al., ICML 2021) as its equivariant message-passing backbone, extending the line of equivariant GNNs for physical dynamics (ST-TFN, ST-SE(3)TR, EqMotion, ESTAG) by explicitly addressing non-stationarity. The key conceptual departure is from prior non-stationary time-series methods (RevIN, DAIN, FAN, Non-stationary Transformer) that rely on normalization to stationarize data — an operation that breaks E(3) symmetry. Instead, NS-EGNN repurposes two classical signal-processing tools that are naturally compatible with equivariance: Patch Fourier Transform (invariant, inspired by short-time FT) to capture time-varying frequency content, and first/second-order differencing (equivariant, inspired by ARIMA) to remove linear and quadratic trends before pooling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and formalizes a gap that prior equivariant dynamics models overlooked: physical trajectories are demonstrably non-stationary (confirmed via ADF tests), and the standard remedy (normalization) is fundamentally incompatible with equivariance. The plug-and-play PFT + NS-Pooling modules can in principle be attached to other equivariant backbones (GMN, DEGNN), broadening applicability. Limitations include modest gains on more stationary data (AdK protein), reliance on a single backbone (EGNN), and the observation that gains diminish when the data are already near-stationary. Open questions include whether wavelet or other spectral decompositions can be made equivariant, and whether the approach extends to constrained or bounded physical systems.
