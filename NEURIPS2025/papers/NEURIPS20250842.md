## Problem

Open-World Object Detection (OWOD) suffers from two persistent failures: semantic confusion between known and unknown classes (e.g., truck vs. car headlamps) and catastrophic forgetting of previously learned classes during incremental adaptation. Existing pseudo-labeling and objectness-based strategies cannot simultaneously discover representative unknown instances and preserve discriminative known-class representations, resulting in low unknown recall and degraded known-class accuracy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250842/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images processed through a Faster R-CNN (ResNet-50 backbone) producing 500 RoI proposals per image, alongside a small replay buffer of previously known class examples. CROWD-D selects k=10 pseudo-labeled unknown RoIs per image from the RPN pool using a frozen model, while CROWD-L fine-tunes the detector on both known and mined unknowns. The output is an updated detector h^{t+1} that produces bounding boxes with class labels for known objects, flags unknown proposals with a placeholder label (0), and retains a replay buffer for the next incremental task.

## Method

CROWD reformulates OWOD as an interleaved combinatorial data-discovery and representation learning problem. CROWD-Discover (CROWD-D) identifies unknown RoIs by greedily maximizing Submodular Conditional Gain (SCG) between candidate subsets and the union of known and background RoIs, selecting instances that are dissimilar to both known classes and background under a cardinality budget. CROWD-Learn (CROWD-L) then fine-tunes the detector with a novel combinatorial loss L_CROWD that jointly minimizes Submodular Total Information over each known class (enforcing intra-class compactness to prevent forgetting) and maximizes SCG between known and unknown embeddings (enforcing inter-class separation to reduce confusion). The loss is instantiated with Facility-Location, Graph-Cut, or Log-Determinant submodular functions, with Facility-Location emerging as most robust to class imbalance. The entire pipeline operates on a Faster R-CNN with ResNet-50 backbone trained incrementally across four tasks using a 256-dimensional RoI feature space.

## Results

On the M-OWOD benchmark, CROWD achieves 61.7% mAP (Task 1, current classes) and 38.5% mAP (Task 4, all classes), surpassing the strongest baseline OrthogonalDet by 2.8% and 0.6% respectively. On S-OWOD, CROWD reaches 73.5% mAP (Task 1) and 46.4% mAP (Task 4), outperforming OrthogonalDet by 1.9% and 0.2%. The headline gain is in unknown recall: CROWD achieves 57.9% U-Recall on M-OWOD Task 1 versus 24.6% for OrthogonalDet (~2.4×), and 69.6% on Task 3 versus 29.1%. On PASCAL VOC IOD (10+10 split), CROWD attains 72.0% overall mAP, exceeding OrthogonalDet (67.0%) by 5.0%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CROWD outperforms all 11 baselines on both U-Recall and known-class mAP across M-OWOD and S-OWOD. Against the strongest OWOD baseline OrthogonalDet [61], CROWD improves U-Recall by 2.4× (57.9% vs. 24.6% on M-OWOD Task 1) while also gaining 0.4–2.8% mAP on known classes, indicating reduced forgetting. Compared to other pseudo-labeling-based methods (PROB [77], CAT [44], ALLOW [45]), CROWD's combinatorial data-discovery strategy yields 8.4% higher U-Recall on M-OWOD, attributed to more representative unknown selection via SCG maximization. On the IOD setting (PASCAL VOC 10+10), CROWD achieves 72.0% overall mAP, beating OrthogonalDet (67.0%), PROB (66.5%), and ILOD (63.2%) by 5.0%, 5.5%, and 8.8% respectively, with the largest gains on previously seen classes (73.5% vs. 69.4% for OrthogonalDet), confirming that the combinatorial self-loss effectively mitigates catastrophic forgetting.

## Relation to Prior Work

CROWD reformulates Open-World Object Detection as a combinatorial (set-based) problem, drawing on two distinct research threads: submodular data discovery (Kothawade et al.) for selecting representative unknown RoIs via Submodular Conditional Gain maximization, and submodular combinatorial representation learning (Majee et al., SCoRe) for designing loss functions that enforce known-unknown separation while preserving intra-class compactness. The architecture builds directly on OrthogonalDet's Faster R-CNN backbone, but departs from the pseudo-labeling and feature-orthogonalization strategies used by most prior OWOD methods (ORE, OW-DETR, CAT, PROB) by instead framing unknown identification as a targeted combinatorial selection problem with theoretical approximation guarantees. This positions CROWD at the intersection of combinatorial optimization and open-world detection, a connection not previously explored in the OWOD literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~2.4× improvement in unknown recall over the strongest baseline (OrthogonalDet) is substantial and addresses a core weakness of existing OWOD methods, while the 2–3% gains in known-class mAP indicate reduced catastrophic forgetting. The combinatorial framing provides theoretical grounding (greedy (1−e⁻¹) approximation) that pseudo-labeling heuristics lack, and the modular design (CROWD-D for discovery, CROWD-L for learning) allows each component to be swapped independently. A key limitation acknowledged by the authors is that the fixed selection budget in CROWD-D injects spurious exemplars in images lacking true unknowns, and the approach has only been validated on a single architecture (Faster R-CNN with ResNet-50), leaving open whether the combinatorial formulation transfers to transformer-based detectors or scales to larger class vocabularies.
