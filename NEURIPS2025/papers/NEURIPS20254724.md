## Problem

Transform-based tensor models built on the t-product (e.g., t-SVD, Tensor Robust PCA) have achieved strong empirical success, yet their underlying geometric principles remain poorly understood. Classical differential geometry, formulated over real-valued function spaces, cannot capture the algebraic and spectral structure induced by treating tubes as indivisible t-scalar units under a transform-based multiplication rule. No unified framework existed for defining smooth structures, Riemannian metrics, geodesics, or learning-theoretic guarantees in a transform-consistent manner over the t-scalar ring.

## Input / Output

The framework operates on t-vectors in K_c^d (discrete) or K_∞^d (continuous), where each coordinate is a t-scalar — a 1×1×c tensor (e.g., an image row or RGB channel triple) or a smooth function — treated as an indivisible algebraic unit under the t-product. All geometric and learning operations are performed slice-wise in the transform domain (e.g., DCT), so the effective input is a collection of frequency-indexed real-valued slices. Outputs include t-manifold structure estimates (hypothesis test decisions, fitted manifolds with Hausdorff-distance guarantees), cluster assignments, denoised video frames, and learned K_c-valued smooth function values on the manifold.

## Method

The paper introduces t-manifolds: C^β-smooth topological spaces locally modeled on free t-modules K^p over the commutative t-scalar ring K ∈ {K_c, K_∞}, with transition maps required to be C^β-smooth and frequency-wise independent in the transform domain. A sheaf-theoretic construction defines the structure sheaf O_M of K-smooth functions, tangent spaces as K-linear derivations, and 1-forms via Kähler differentials, enabling transform-consistent Riemannian metrics (K-valued, positive-definite slice-wise), gradients, Laplacians, Levi-Civita connections, and geodesics. For learning theory, the paper extends the manifold hypothesis to the t-product setting: Theorem 1 provides a statistical test distinguishing near vs. far t-manifold structures using spectral volume and reach; Theorem 2 gives nonasymptotic Hausdorff-distance bounds for fitting a C² t-manifold from noisy samples; Theorem 3 establishes that tensor neural networks with transform-domain ReLU activations achieve L² error rates of O(n^{-2β/(p_eff+2β)}) where p_eff = O(p log(cd)). A Bidirectional Tensor Representation (BTR) example applies dual t-module low-rank constraints (row-wise and column-wise) to image clustering and video denoising, demonstrating how the geometric framework translates into practical modeling constraints.

## Results

On five benchmark clustering datasets (FRDUE, FRDUE-100, Olivetti, PIE-10, USPS1000), the proposed Bidirectional Tensor Representation (BTR) with DCT achieves the best accuracy, NMI, and purity across all datasets. For example, on Olivetti, BTR reaches 66.7% ACC, 80.8% NMI, and 70.1% PUR, outperforming the strongest baseline R-TLRR (DFT) by 2.2%, 1.8%, and 2.5% respectively. On PIE-10, BTR (DCT) attains 61.1% ACC versus 59.0% for R-TLRR (DFT), and on USPS1000 it achieves 58.0% ACC versus 51.9% for R-TLRR (DCT). The DCT variant of BTR consistently dominates the DFT variant, confirming the benefit of the transform-domain bidirectional low-rank constraint.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BTR (DCT) outperforms all six baseline families across every dataset and metric. Against the Tensor Robust PCA family (R-TPCA [41,40] and OR-TPCA [82]), BTR gains 1.5–21.5% in ACC, with the largest margin on Olivetti (66.7% vs. 39.7%) and PIE-10 (61.1% vs. 54.0%), indicating that the bidirectional low-rank constraint captures structure that single-direction PCA-based methods miss. Against the Tensor Low-Rank Representation family (R-TLRR [83,73] and OR-TLRR [68]), BTR still leads by 1.5–6.1% in ACC; the gap is most pronounced on USPS1000 (58.0% vs. 51.9%) and Olivetti (66.7% vs. 64.5%). Notably, OR-TLRR [68], which already handles outliers, is consistently weaker than R-TLRR on most datasets, while BTR surpasses both, suggesting that the geometric coupling from dual-module constraints provides a more principled form of robustness than explicit outlier handling. The DFT variant of BTR is noticeably weaker than its DCT counterpart (e.g., 19.8% vs. 61.1% ACC on PIE-10), highlighting the importance of the transform choice in the t-product algebra.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the t-SVD algebraic framework (Kilmer et al.) by constructing a full differential geometric structure—tangent spaces, Riemannian metrics, Laplacians, geodesics—over the commutative t-scalar ring, using a sheaf-theoretic formulation rooted in commutative algebra. It adapts the classical manifold hypothesis testing and fitting results of Fefferman et al. to the t-product setting via spectral volume and reach, and leverages the transform-domain TNN architecture of Malik et al. for function approximation. Prior work on t-product geometry (e.g., Grassmannian and Stiefel manifolds over t-scalars) was narrowly scoped; this paper provides the first unified framework that simultaneously handles discrete and continuous t-scalars and defines transform-consistent smooth structures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a principled algebraic-geometric foundation for geometry-aware tensor learning, potentially unifying discrete-time (video) and continuous-time (functional) tensor representations under one smooth manifold theory. However, the authors acknowledge the work is preliminary: key assumptions (frequency-wise independence, existence of a unit element, transform-domain smoothness) are idealized, and the empirical validation is limited to a single clustering table and a denoising figure. Open questions include relaxing the independence assumption to capture cross-frequency coupling, developing scalable algorithms for optimization over t-manifolds, and extending the framework to generative modeling, graph networks, and temporal dynamics.
