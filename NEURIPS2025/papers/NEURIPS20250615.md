## Problem

Fully incomplete multi-view clustering (FIMC) requires learning a unified partition from views where every instance carries data from only one view and no cross-view correspondences exist. Existing partially incomplete methods fundamentally depend on at least some paired instances to guide imputation or alignment, making them inapplicable when views are entirely disjoint. This paper tackles the gap by asking how to establish cross-view cluster structure without any instance-level anchors.

## Input / Output

The method consumes pre-extracted feature matrices (e.g., DECAF/VGG19 for images, GoogLeNet/doc2vec for CUB, GIST/HOG/HIST for YouTubeFaces) where each of N instances is present in exactly one of V views, along with a binary view-indicator matrix W and a user-specified cluster count C. It produces a single unified set of cluster labels spanning all views, shared-space latent representations, and a set of cross-view-aligned prototypes. Notably, no instance-level pairing or correspondence information is ever provided, and the autoencoders are view-specific with no weight sharing.

## Method

CPMN operates in three stages. First, view-specific autoencoders are trained with a correspondence-free graph contrastive loss that uses mutual k-nearest neighbors (MNN) in the latent space to define positive pairs both within each view and across views, establishing initial representations and K-means prototypes without any paired data. Second, a bipartite graph matching step formulates prototype alignment as a Linear Assignment Problem solved by the Hungarian algorithm, producing an optimal one-to-one mapping between the C prototypes of each view pair based on cosine-dissimilarity costs. Third, a prototype contrastive learning stage replaces K-means with a learnable clustering layer (Student's t-distribution soft assignments) and contrasts each instance against both its own-view prototype and the cross-view matched prototype, while a KL-divergence clustering loss refines assignments. The key distinction from prior work is operating at the prototype (category) level rather than the instance level, which is robust to the absence of any cross-view correspondences.

## Results

On the fully incomplete (100% unaligned) setting, CPMN achieves the best ACC across all five datasets: 57.0% on Caltech-101, 72.5% on CUB, 84.8% on NoisyMNIST, 88.0% on MNIST-USPS, and 70.0% on YouTubeFaces. The most notable margin is on Caltech-101 ARI, where CPMN reaches 68.4 versus 33.0 for SMILE (the strongest baseline), a 35.4-point gap. Under the 100% missing-view scenario, CPMN similarly leads on ACC for Caltech-101 (38.7 vs. 30.5 for SMILE) and NoisyMNIST (71.1 vs. 69.0), while SMILE retains a slight NMI edge on MNIST-USPS (69.6 vs. 69.2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CPMN outperforms the strongest baseline SMILE on ACC for all five datasets in the 100% unaligned setting, with the largest gaps on Caltech-101 (+9.4 ACC, +35.4 ARI) and YouTubeFaces (+17.5 ACC, +14.9 ARI). Against UPMGC-SM, CPMN leads on ACC for every dataset (e.g., +6.0 on Caltech-101, +15.2 on YouTubeFaces) and on ARI for all but CUB where UPMGC-SM edges ahead (59.7 vs. 55.2). Conventional methods MVC-UM and GWMAC are substantially outperformed; GWMAC in particular collapses to near-random ACC (<16%) on most datasets. In the 100% missing-view scenario, CPMN beats SMILE on ACC for Caltech-101 (+8.2), CUB (+6.8), and NoisyMNIST (+2.1), though SMILE retains a marginal NMI advantage on MNIST-USPS (69.6 vs. 69.2) and ARI on YouTubeFaces (18.5 vs. 16.2). Against DM2C (designed for fully missing data), CPMN achieves substantially higher ACC across all datasets (e.g., 38.7 vs. 28.2 on Caltech-101, 71.1 vs. 23.2 on NoisyMNIST).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself against the partially incomplete multi-view clustering (PIMC) and partially aligned multi-view clustering (PAMC) literature, arguing that both rely on at least some cross-view correspondences that are unavailable in the fully incomplete setting. The most direct predecessor is SMILE, which tackles FIMC by enforcing semantic invariance across views at the instance level; CPMN departs from this by shifting the alignment abstraction from individual instances to cluster prototypes, using a bipartite graph matching (Hungarian algorithm) to establish prototype correspondences. The correspondence-free graph contrastive learning stage borrows the MNN-based positive-pair construction from the contrastive learning literature, repurposing it for a setting where no paired instances exist.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FIMC is a practically critical scenario (e.g., clinical diagnostics where patients undergo only one test type) that prior methods cannot handle, and the prototype-level alignment paradigm offers a more robust alternative to instance-level or distribution-invariance approaches that degrade under noise or class imbalance. Key limitations include the assumption of a predefined cluster count C and the dependence on initial K-means prototype quality, both of which the authors flag as open directions. Extending the framework to a non-parametric setting where C is inferred from data, and analyzing convergence properties of the joint optimization, remain important open questions for the field.
