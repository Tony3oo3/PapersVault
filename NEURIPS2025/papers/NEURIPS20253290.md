## Problem

Standard empirical risk minimization on observational (X, Y) data yields biased causal effect estimates when hidden confounders exist, and instrumental variables—while theoretically sound—are rarely available in practice (e.g., computer vision, NLP). Data augmentation is a ubiquitous regularizer for i.i.d. generalization, but its potential for reducing confounding bias in causal effect estimation had not been studied. This paper asks whether outcome-invariant DA can be repurposed as a soft intervention on the treatment mechanism to mitigate unobserved confounding without requiring explicit IVs.

## Input / Output

The method consumes only observational (X, Y) pairs plus a set of outcome-invariant DA transformations parameterized by G (e.g., rotations, translations, color perturbations), with the critical constraint that f(gx) = f(x) for all g ∈ G. Unlike conventional DA, the augmented dataset is NOT grown—the sample size is held fixed to isolate the causal-estimation benefit from variance reduction. The output is a predictor h (linear or neural) that simultaneously provides an ATE estimate and a robust predictor minimizing worst-case risk over a perturbation set of treatment interventions defined by the DA group.

## Method

The key insight (Observation 1) is that when f is invariant to DA, the joint distribution of (GX, Y, G, C) is identical to the interventional distribution under do(τ := Gτ), meaning DA on X is equivalent to a soft intervention on the treatment mechanism. A second observation (Observation 2) shows that in this intervened SEM, the DA parameters G satisfy the first three IV properties (treatment relevance, exclusion restriction, unconfoundedness) but may fail outcome relevance, making them IV-like (IVL) variables. The paper introduces IVL regression with risk R_IVL_α(h) = R_IV(h) + α·R_ERM(h), which regularizes the under-determined IV problem by anchoring solutions near the ERM optimum while encouraging search in the subspace containing f. Composing DA with IVL regression (DA+IVL) then simulates a worst-case adversarial application of DA over a bounded transform set G_α, yielding a min-max formulation that strictly reduces confounding bias beyond simple DA+ERM. Theoretical results in a linear Gaussian setting prove that DA+ERM dominates ERM (never worse on CER) and DA+IVL dominates DA+ERM, with strict improvement iff the DA perturbs X along spurious (confounded) features.

## Results

The primary metric is normalized Causal Excess Risk (nCER), where lower values indicate better causal effect estimation (0 = perfect, 1 = pure confounding). On the optical device dataset (12 datasets, 'all' DA), DA+IVL_α^cc achieves the best nCER of 0.509±0.078, closely followed by DA+ERM at 0.513±0.075, both significantly outperforming ERM (0.823±0.083) and all domain generalization baselines (IRM 0.953, DRO 0.952, V-REx 0.925, MM-REx 1.000). On the colored MNIST benchmark, DA+IVL_α^CV achieves the best overall performance among all methods tested. In the linear simulation, DA+IVL consistently reduces confounding bias as κ increases, while DA+IV (unregularized) is unstable and under-determined.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DA+IVL_α (with confounder correction) achieves the lowest nCER (0.509) on the optical device dataset, outperforming DA+ERM (0.513) by a small margin and Kania & Wit's causal regularization (0.543) by ~3.4 nCER points. All domain generalization baselines—IRM (0.953), DRO (0.952), RICE (0.959), V-REx (0.925), and MM-REx (1.000)—perform near the worst-case (nCER ≈ 1), indicating they fail to mitigate confounding when only DA parameters are available as proxy environments. The ℓ1/ℓ2 causal regularization of Janzing (0.817/0.828) barely improves over ERM (0.823), confirming that standard norm-based regularizers do not address confounding bias. On colored MNIST, DA+IVL_α^CV achieves the best overall performance, competitive with the strongest DG baselines, while ERM underperforms substantially. The unregularized DA+IV (2SLS) baseline is unstable and under-determined, performing poorly (0.640 on optical device, 0.973 on random-permutation DA), which motivates the IVL regularization proposed in this work.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the causal regularization paradigm—previously applied to ℓ1/ℓ2 penalties (Janzing, 2019) and the min-norm interpolator (Vankadara et al., 2022)—to data augmentation, arguing that outcome-invariant DA can be reinterpreted as a soft intervention on the treatment mechanism. It builds on the group-theoretic DA framework (Chen et al., 2020) and IV regression (Muandet et al., 2020), introducing IV-like (IVL) variables that relax the full IV assumptions (particularly outcome relevance) while retaining enough structure to reduce confounding bias via a new regularized objective combining IV risk with ERM. The DA+IVL composition, which simulates a worst-case adversarial application of DA, connects to robust optimization and domain generalization methods (IRM, DRO, REx) but requires only observational (X,Y) data plus symmetry knowledge, unlike those baselines which need multiple environments or explicit confounders.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, theoretically grounded justification for using DA beyond its conventional i.i.d. regularization role, offering a practical alternative when instrumental variables are unavailable (e.g., in vision or NLP). Key limitations include the requirement for outcome invariance, the need for domain knowledge to target spurious features (otherwise DA only matches ERM), and the non-trivial selection of the IVL regularization parameter α in finite samples. Open questions include whether the framework extends to approximate or violated invariance, non-linear settings beyond the linear Gaussian analysis, and whether the 'carpet bombing' strategy of exhausting all available invariant DA can reliably capture spurious features without explicit domain knowledge.
