## Problem

Prior evaluations of ensemble-based black-box transfer attacks have been systematically biased, testing methods only under overly optimistic conditions: homogeneous surrogate ensembles architecturally similar to the target, non-robust target models, and no proper ablation isolating the contribution of query-based refinement. This paper asks whether these attacks actually generalize to realistic threat models with diverse surrogates, robust defenses, and whether querying the target truly improves over simple query-free transfer.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254665/cropped_page7_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

Each evaluation scenario provides an input image (32×32 CIFAR-10 or 299×299 ImageNet), a target label, a pool of surrogate models with full white-box access, a black-box target model exposed only as a callable (no gradient or parameter access), and a perturbation budget of ε=16/255 under the l∞ norm. The attack produces a single adversarial example per input, and the benchmark reports attack success rate (ASR) and average queries per success (q̄) over 1000-sample subsets. The target is queried at most Q=50 times, and attacks are evaluated in batches of at least 20 samples to exploit GPU memory efficiently.

## Method

TransferBench is a plug-and-play Python/PyTorch library that defines 17 evaluation scenarios across three types: Homogeneous (HoS, surrogates share the target's architecture family), Heterogeneous (HeS, surrogates differ architecturally), and Robust-Homogeneous (HoS+R, robustly trained targets from RobustBench). The framework unifies all ensemble-based attacks under a two-stage formulation—Surrogate-Based Attack Optimization (SBA) solving a differentiable local loss over the surrogate ensemble, and Query-Based Refinement (QBR) using target feedback to update ensemble weights or reinitialize the attack. To isolate the value of query-based refinement, the benchmark includes two naive baselines (NaiveAvg10 and NaiveAvg100) that use a fixed uniform-weight ensemble with projected gradient descent and no adaptive weight updates, alongside query-free transfer attacks (ENS, CWA, SASD_WS, etc.) imported from the TransferAttack library. Nine state-of-the-art ensemble attacks (BASES, DSWEA, GAA, GFCS, SIMBAODS, HYBRID, DSA, SUBSPACE, and others) are re-implemented in efficient batched form, and a CLI tool (trbench) integrated with Weights&Biases orchestrates large-scale runs. The key finding is that under heterogeneous and robust scenarios, query-based methods fail to outperform simple fixed-weight baselines, and many achieve near-zero ASR against robust targets, contradicting prior claims of near-perfect performance.

## Results

On ImageNet, the query-free SASD_WS baseline achieves the highest ASR across most scenarios (96.4% on ResNeXt-101 HoS, 69.2% on ResNeXt-101 HeS, 59.8% on Pub-RN-50), outperforming all query-based ensemble attacks including BASES (86.3%/29.0%/54.6%) and DSWEA (83.5%/17.7%/46.8%). Against truly robust targets (Mim-Sw-L and Amini-Sw-L), every evaluated attack—including all 9 query-based methods—achieves 0% ASR, demonstrating complete failure. On CIFAR-10, most attacks reach near-perfect ASR (~99–100%) in homogeneous scenarios, but drop to 1.5–2.5% against robust targets (Peng-RWRN-70, Barto-WRN-94). The simple NAIVEAVG100 baseline (no weight updates, 100 PGD iterations) matches or exceeds all complex query-based refinement methods, indicating that target feedback provides negligible benefit.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates 13 attacks (7 query-based ensemble methods + 6 query-free baselines) across 17 scenarios. The headline finding is that the query-free SASD_WS [36] consistently outperforms all query-based methods: on ResNeXt-101 HeS it achieves 69.2% ASR vs. 29.0% for BASES [8] and 17.7% for DSWEA [16], and on Pub-RN-50 it reaches 59.8% vs. 54.6% for BASES. The simple NAIVEAVG100 baseline (fixed uniform weights, 100 PGD steps, no adaptive re-weighting) matches or exceeds every complex query-based refinement method—e.g., 89.6% vs. 86.3% (BASES) and 83.5% (DSWEA) on ResNeXt-101 HoS—contradicting the claimed benefit of target feedback. GAA [38], GFCS [23], SIMBAODS [33], and HYBRID [31] all perform poorly, with ASR below 40% even in the most favorable homogeneous setting. Against robust targets (Mim-Sw-L, Amini-Sw-L), all 13 attacks fail completely (0% ASR), while Pub-RN-50 remains vulnerable to transfer (54–60% ASR), likely due to gradient obfuscation. On CIFAR-10, the gap between query-based and query-free methods narrows in homogeneous scenarios (all reach ~99%+), but the same pattern holds: SASD_WS and NAIVEAVG100 match or beat BASES and DSWEA, and all methods collapse to ~2% ASR on robust targets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by re-evaluating the family of ensemble-based black-box transfer attacks (BASES, DSWEA, GAA, GFCS, SIMBAODS, HYBRID, etc.) under conditions that prior work systematically avoided: heterogeneous surrogate pools, robust target defenses, and explicit query-free baselines. It departs from existing benchmarks (TransferAttack, TransferAttackEval, BlackBoxBench) which either supported only query-free attacks or used fixed, architecture-matched surrogate sets. Conceptually, the paper unifies the SBA/QBR decomposition of these attacks into a single optimization formulation (Problem 1), then uses that formalism to isolate and test the actual contribution of each component.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central finding—that sophisticated query-based refinement mechanisms (adaptive re-weighting, score-based surrogate selection) provide little to no benefit over a fixed-weight average of surrogates—challenges the core value proposition of an entire family of recent methods and redirects the field's attention toward the harder problem of improving surrogate-based optimization itself. The benchmark also exposes that all evaluated attacks fail against state-of-the-art robust targets, undermining claims of near-perfect black-box attack performance. Open questions include whether the null result on query-based refinement holds under larger query budgets, different perturbation norms, or more diverse surrogate pools, and whether truly query-efficient attacks can be built without relying on architectural similarity to the target.
