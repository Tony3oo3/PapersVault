## Problem

Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) for text-to-image diffusion models requires a costly and rigid pipeline: offline generation of large candidate image sets followed by extensive human pairwise ranking. This process is expensive, inflexible to new prompts or domains, and the fixed dataset limits the diversity of preference learning signals, potentially hurting generalization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250083/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

During training, the method consumes a text prompt paired with a high-quality image from a curated dataset (treated as the 'winner') and a randomly sampled unrelated image from the same dataset (serving as the 'loser' via text-image misalignment). At inference, it takes a natural language prompt and produces a single generated image at 512×512 (SD1.5) or 1024×1024 (SDXL) resolution. The key distinction from standard DPO is that no pre-generated paired images or human annotations are needed—only the same text-image pairs used for supervised fine-tuning.

## Method

Self-DPO constructs preference pairs on the fly at every training iteration: each curated image is treated as a winner x^w, and a loser x^sl is obtained by randomly sampling a different image from the training set, creating a text-image misalignment signal. The Self-DPO loss (Eq. 8) is a modified DPO objective that compares the noise-prediction MSE of the winning versus losing samples under both the policy and a frozen reference model, scaled by a factor C (set to −2500). The total training loss combines the standard diffusion MSE loss and the Self-DPO loss with equal weights (λ₁ = λ₂ = 0.5). The key insight is that the continuous stream of diverse, randomly sampled 'losing' images provides richer and more varied preference supervision than a fixed set of human-annotated pairs, enabling the model to learn broader alignment signals without any additional data collection or annotation overhead.

## Results

On Pick-a-Pic V2 with SD1.5, Self-DPO achieves a CLIP score of 34.79 (+2.21 over the base model) and an ImageReward score of 71.00, far exceeding DPO (4.13) and SFT (45.03). For SDXL on the same dataset, Self-DPO reaches an ImageReward of 103.96 versus 81.14 for DPO and 30.89 for SFT. The ImageReward win rate for Self-DPO reaches 85.20% on SD1.5 (vs. 61.00% for DPO), and consistent gains are observed on PartiPrompts and HPDv2 as well.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Self-DPO is compared against three baselines: the unmodified base models (SD1.5/SDXL), supervised fine-tuning (SFT), and Diffusion-DPO [Wallace et al., CVPR 2024]. Against DPO, Self-DPO achieves substantially higher ImageReward scores (71.00 vs. 4.13 on SD1.5; 103.96 vs. 81.14 on SDXL) and higher CLIP scores (34.79 vs. 33.19 on SD1.5), while matching or slightly trailing DPO on PickScore and Aesthetics. Against SFT, Self-DPO wins on CLIP and ImageReward across both architectures; notably, SFT actually degrades SDXL performance (ImageReward drops from 50.62 to 30.89), whereas Self-DPO improves it to 103.96. The ImageReward win rate for Self-DPO reaches 85.20% on SD1.5 versus 61.00% for DPO, confirming a clear preference advantage. On the real-world LAION-COCO subset, Self-DPO again outperforms SFT (ImageReward 17.11 vs. −23.95) while no DPO baseline is available due to the absence of paired annotations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Diffusion-DPO (Wallace et al., CVPR 2024), which adapted the DPO objective from LLMs (Rafailov et al., NeurIPS 2023) to text-to-image diffusion models, by replacing the requirement for pre-generated, human-ranked image pairs with on-the-fly self-supervised pair construction. The key conceptual departure is that the 'losing' sample is obtained by randomly sampling an unrelated image from the training set rather than through a costly offline generation-and-annotation pipeline. The method retains the Bradley-Terry-based DPO loss structure and the reference-model KL regularization, but reframes the preference signal as a text-image alignment problem solvable within a standard SFT data loop.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for extra preference images and human ranking, Self-DPO makes preference-based alignment of diffusion models dramatically cheaper and more accessible, reducing the data requirements to those of standard SFT. The finding that a crude random-image negative outperforms carefully curated DPO pairs suggests that text-image misalignment, rather than fine-grained visual quality differences, is the dominant preference signal the model needs to learn. Open questions include whether this approach generalizes to smaller or domain-specific datasets where random negatives may be uninformative, and whether more content-aware degradation strategies (adversarial perturbations, structured corruptions) could further close the gap to full DPO on SDXL.
