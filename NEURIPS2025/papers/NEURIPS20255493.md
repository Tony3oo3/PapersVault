## Problem

In cluttered environments, the order in which objects are manipulated critically affects task success, yet existing approaches either apply brittle heuristic post-processing after detection or rely on slow VLM-based two-stage pipelines that cannot meet real-time robotic deployment requirements. No unified framework directly learns spatial-aware manipulation ordering from visual input while capturing the geometric and physical dependencies that constrain feasible action sequences.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255493/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a single RGB-D image at 1408×1024 resolution from a top-down camera together with the current end-effector pose in SE(3), and produces per-object 3D bounding box representations (center, size, orientation, category) along with continuous priority scores that are sorted into a manipulation order sequence. The system replans after each pick action, and the suction-based end-effector constraint is encoded into the spatial features via relative object-to-end-effector transformations, making the output order physically grounded in the robot's current reachability.

## Method

OrderMind is a unified architecture built on a modified Objects-as-Points backbone that jointly performs spatial perception and ordering prediction in a single forward pass. A Spatial Context Understanding module constructs a k-NN graph over object center points, aggregates neighborhood features via PointNet-style max pooling to capture local geometric structure, and fuses object-manipulator relative poses to encode accessibility. A Temporal Priority Structuring module then refines object tokens through self-attention (inter-object interaction) and cross-attention (querying global scene context and visual features) before decoding continuous priority scores. Training uses a pairwise ordering loss with logarithmic rank-difference weighting, aligned via Hungarian matching, and supervision is generated by a VLM (Qwen2.5-VL) guided by two spatial priors—an independence prior (horizontal separation threshold) and a local optimality prior (vertical occlusion check)—enabling label-free distillation without manual annotation.

## Results

On the Manipulation Ordering Benchmark (163,222 samples), OrderMind achieves 96.5% success rate (SR) in easy mode, 95.3% in moderate, and 95.4% in hard, with only 0.4, 1.0, and 3.3 residual objects (RC) respectively. The lightweight OrderMind-Mini variant (35.2M params) reaches 94.2%/89.6%/90.4% SR at 21.3 FPS, while the full model (41.8M params) runs at 5.6 FPS. In real-world closed-loop testing, OrderMind achieves 93.3% SR (easy), 78.5% (moderate), and 76.6% (hard) with RC of 0.2, 2.0, and 3.0 respectively. The method also outperforms privileged VLMs (which have access to ground-truth object poses) on all difficulty levels.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OrderMind outperforms all baselines across every difficulty level and metric. Against privileged VLMs (which receive ground-truth object poses), OrderMind achieves 96.5% vs. 92.5% SR in easy mode and 95.4% vs. 78.5% in hard mode, surpassing the best VLM (Gemini-2.5) by 16.9 points in hard while running 56× faster (5.6 vs. 0.1 FPS). Heuristic pipelines (YOLOv11+SPH) reach at most 79.8% SR in moderate mode, trailing OrderMind by 15.5 points, and suffer higher object disturbance (3.9 vs. 1.4 OD). Two-stage frameworks combining UniDet3D detection with VLM ordering perform worst overall, with SR dropping to 24.9–46.4% and fail counts exceeding 60–140 attempts per episode due to false-positive detections. The lightweight OrderMind-Mini (35.2M params, 21.3 FPS) still exceeds all baselines in SR while being 1.8× faster than the YOLOv11 pipeline (11.9 FPS) and 213× faster than VLM-based approaches. In real-world testing, OrderMind maintains 76.6–93.3% SR across difficulty levels, confirming that the simulation-to-reality gap is manageable.

## Relation to Prior Work

OrderMind builds directly on the Objects as Points [11] backbone for object-centric visual encoding, extending it with a k-NN spatial graph (inspired by PointNet-style neighborhood aggregation) to capture object-object and object-manipulator geometric relations. It departs from two dominant paradigms in cluttered-scene manipulation: heuristic post-processing pipelines (e.g., YOLO + spatial priors) that lack generalization, and two-stage VLM frameworks that suffer from prohibitive inference latency (~0.1 FPS). The key conceptual departure is unifying spatial perception and manipulation ordering into a single end-to-end inference pass, with VLM-generated labels (guided by independence and local-optimality spatial priors) serving as a distillation signal rather than a runtime dependency.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper introduces the first dedicated large-scale benchmark (163,222 samples) for manipulation ordering, filling a gap where prior evaluation was ad hoc or embedded within broader manipulation tasks. The finding that a 41.8M-parameter model outperforms 72B+ VLMs (even with privileged ground-truth access) by 15–20 points in success rate on hard scenes suggests that spatial reasoning for manipulation sequencing requires specialized inductive biases that general-purpose VLMs do not provide. Limitations include the assumption of a static scene during execution, reliance on accurate 3D attribute estimation under occlusion, and evaluation primarily with a suction end-effector on rigid objects, leaving open questions about dynamic environments, deformable objects, and generalization to gripper-based manipulation.
