## Problem

Vision-language models achieve strong zero-shot detection on common objects but fail catastrophically on out-of-distribution classes, tasks, and imaging modalities absent from internet-scale pre-training. Existing grounding benchmarks (RefCOCO, ODinW) evaluate on classes already well-represented in pre-training data, artificially inflating performance and masking this generalization gap. The paper asks whether VLMs can be aligned to novel concepts using a few visual examples and rich textual annotator instructions, rather than simply scaling pre-training data.

## Input / Output

Each model receives an RGB image (resized to 640×1333 for GroundingDINO) along with a text prompt containing either bare class names or full multi-modal annotator instructions, and optionally 10 few-shot reference images per class. Outputs are axis-aligned bounding boxes with class labels and confidence scores; MLLMs (Qwen2.5-VL, Gemini 2.5 Pro) do not natively report per-box confidence, so scores are set to 1.0. Evaluation uses pycocotools with maxDets=500 (rather than the standard 100) because many RF100-VL images contain dense object counts, and all methods are scored on the same fully-annotated test split regardless of training regime.

## Method

The core contribution is a 100-dataset, 564-class, 164K-image benchmark (RF100-VL) sourced from Roboflow Universe and curated to exclude concepts common in internet pre-training, instead emphasizing scientific names, acronyms, context-dependent labels, material properties, and non-RGB modalities (X-ray, thermal, aerial). For each dataset, the authors generate multi-modal annotator instructions via GPT-4o (with manual verification) that pair rich per-class textual descriptions with few-shot visual examples, mirroring how human annotators are aligned to labeling policies. Evaluation spans four data regimes—zero-shot (class-name prompting), 10-shot few-shot (visual prompting, multi-modal prompting, and federated fine-tuning), semi-supervised (10% labeled), and fully-supervised—using both specialist open-vocabulary detectors (GroundingDINO, Detic, OWLv2, MQ-GLIP) and generalist MLLMs (Qwen2.5-VL 72B, Gemini 2.5 Pro). A key empirical finding is that specialist detectors consistently outperform MLLMs by 5–25 mAP, and that multi-modal annotator instructions yield inconsistent or even negative gains for MLLMs due to their rigid instruction-tuned prompt structures, while gradient-based fine-tuning of GroundingDINO achieves the best few-shot performance (33.6 mAP overall).

## Results

On the full Roboflow100-VL benchmark (100 datasets, 564 classes, 164K images), GroundingDINO achieves the best zero-shot performance at 15.7 mAP and the best few-shot (10-shot) performance at 33.6 mAP, significantly outperforming all other baselines. MLLMs struggle dramatically: Qwen2.5-VL (72B) scores only 5.6 mAP zero-shot and 7.6 mAP few-shot, while Gemini 2.5 Pro scores 11.6 mAP zero-shot and 8.8 mAP few-shot. In the CVPR 2025 Foundational FSOD Challenge (20-dataset subset), the winning team BEATON achieves 50.4 mAP, surpassing the best paper baseline (GroundingDINO at 33.4 mAP) by 17 mAP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GroundingDINO [86] is the strongest baseline in both zero-shot (15.7 mAP) and few-shot (33.6 mAP) settings, outperforming all other specialist detectors and MLLMs by a wide margin. Among specialist open-vocabulary detectors, OWLv2 [97] (13.6 mAP zero-shot) and MQ-GLIP [152] (12.2 mAP zero-shot) trail GroundingDINO by 2–3.5 mAP, while Detic [161] scores 9.5 mAP zero-shot but improves to 22.8 mAP with federated fine-tuning [91]. MLLMs underperform dramatically: Qwen2.5-VL [28] achieves only 5.6 mAP zero-shot and 7.6 mAP few-shot, and Gemini 2.5 Pro [47] scores 11.6 mAP zero-shot but degrades to 8.8 mAP few-shot when given annotator instructions and images. Notably, Gemini 2.5 Pro's performance drops from 11.6 to 4.5 mAP when prompted with instructions instead of class names, suggesting MLLMs cannot effectively leverage rich textual context. Among YOLO variants, YOLOv11 [74] does not improve over YOLOv8 [71] on RF100-VL (55.3 vs. 54.9 mAP fully-supervised for nano; 56.5 vs. 56.4 for medium) despite superior COCO scores, indicating COCO overfitting. The federated loss from Madan et al. [91] provides modest gains for YOLO (e.g., YOLOv8s: 20.7→23.6 mAP) and Detic (9.5→22.8 mAP) in the few-shot regime, but still falls well short of fine-tuned GroundingDINO.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the few-shot object detection line (Wang et al. 2020; Madan et al. 2024) by introducing multi-modal annotator instructions—combining few-shot visual examples with rich per-class textual descriptions—as the mechanism for concept alignment, drawing on the annotation-guidelines literature (Chang et al. 2023). It departs from prior open-vocabulary detection benchmarks (ODinW, RefCOCO) by deliberately curating datasets where class names alone are insufficient (scientific names, acronyms, material properties, context-dependent labels), forcing models to leverage multi-modal context rather than relying on pre-trained semantic priors. The evaluation spans both specialist open-vocabulary detectors (GroundingDINO, Detic, OWLv2, MQ-GLIP) and generalist MLLMs (Qwen2.5-VL, Gemini 2.5 Pro), bridging two communities that have been evaluated separately.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RF100-VL exposes a stark gap between benchmark performance and real-world OOD generalization: models scoring 40+ mAP on ODinW drop to under 16 mAP here, and MLLMs achieve less than 2% on medical imaging. The finding that annotator instructions can *degrade* MLLM performance (Gemini drops from 11.6 to 4.5 mAP) challenges the assumption that richer context always helps, pointing to a fundamental mismatch between instruction-tuned prompting and open-vocabulary detection. Open questions include how to effectively fuse MLLM versatility with specialist detector precision, how to generate better automatic annotator instructions that incorporate visual evidence, and whether the 17 mAP gap between the best baseline and the CVPR 2025 challenge winner signals a tractable path to substantially better few-shot concept alignment.
