## Problem

While diffusion models can generate high-quality images, how the generated output responds to perturbations in the initial noise remains under-explored, limiting controllability of the sampling process. Prior controllable generation work focuses on per-sample constraints (e.g., plug-and-play projections or score modifications), with little exploration of how to regulate the overall distribution of generated samples to match statistical targets such as a specified mean with a controlled MSE level.

## Input / Output

The method takes a target image (256×256 for pixel diffusion on FFHQ/CIFAR-10, or 512×512 upscaled for Stable Diffusion on CelebA-HQ), a user-specified perturbation scale C0 or target rMSE level, and optionally a text prompt with CFG for conditional latent diffusion. It first computes the DDIM inversion of the target to obtain initial noise x_T, then produces a batch of generated images whose sample mean approximates the target while the average L2 distance (rMSE) to the target matches the user-specified diversity level. A binary-search controller (Algorithm 2) iteratively adjusts C0 until the achieved rMSE falls within a tolerance of the target.

## Method

The key theoretical contribution is proving that under DDIM sampling, a small perturbation λΔx to the initial noise x_T produces an approximately linear change in the output x_0, quantified by a matrix-valued Jacobian γ_0 (Proposition 1 for discrete DDIM, Proposition 2 for the continuous ODE via Grönwall's inequality). Building on this, the CCS algorithm perturbs x_T via spherical linear interpolation (slerp) with a random Gaussian vector ε at angle C0, which preserves the norm ‖x_T'‖₂ ≈ ‖x_T‖₂ (keeping the input on the Gaussian hypersphere for quality) while E[ε]=0 ensures the sample mean remains centered on the target. For conditional latent diffusion models, P-CCS performs partial inversion at an intermediate timestep t₀ (e.g., t₀=45 out of T=50) to avoid degrading CFG semantics, then applies the same spherical interpolation on the residual noise component. The approach is training-free and requires only a pretrained diffusion model plus DDIM inversion.

## Results

On FFHQ (pixel diffusion, target rMSE=0.12), CCS achieves the best PSNR of 25.13 dB (vs. 23.52 for the strongest baseline CCDF-C), the highest diversity (SD=0.104), and the best perceptual quality (MUSIQ=66.79, CLIP-IQA=0.750). On CelebA-HQ (Stable Diffusion 1.5, target rMSE=0.07), CCS reaches 30.29 dB PSNR and 49.66 MUSIQ, outperforming CCDF-C (27.66 dB, 49.29 MUSIQ) and LDPS-C (24.56 dB, 29.07 MUSIQ). The linearity of the noise-to-output mapping is validated with R² values of 0.995 (FFHQ) and 0.988 (CIFAR-10) for pixel diffusion, and 0.959 (CelebA-HQ) and 0.947 (fMoW) for latent diffusion.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CCS outperforms all four baselines on every metric in both the FFHQ (pixel) and CelebA-HQ (latent) experiments. The largest gains are in PSNR (sample-mean centering): CCS achieves 25.13 dB on FFHQ versus 23.52 for CCDF-C (SDEdit), 21.02 for DPS, 20.04 for ILVR, and 18.88 for Gaussian Perturbation — a 1.6 dB improvement over the strongest baseline. On CelebA-HQ, CCS reaches 30.29 dB PSNR versus 27.66 for CCDF-C and 24.56 for LDPS-C, while LDPS-C suffers severe quality degradation (MUSIQ drops to 29.07 vs. 49.66 for CCS). CCS also achieves the highest diversity (SD) across all settings, indicating that the spherical interpolation mechanism preserves sample spread better than additive noise or posterior-sampling approaches. The only near-tie is CLIP-IQA on CelebA-HQ, where CCDF-C edges out CCS by 0.003 (0.735 vs. 0.732), but CCS still leads on all other metrics including MUSIQ and PSNR.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on DDIM sampling (Song et al., 2021) as its sampling backbone and repurposes spherical linear interpolation (Shoemake, 1985) — originally from quaternion animation — as the noise perturbation mechanism. It departs from prior controllable generation work (DPS, ILVR, CCDF, plug-and-play methods) that constrains individual samples, by instead controlling the distribution-level statistics (sample mean and MSE) of a batch of generated images. It also extends earlier observations of local linearity in the denoiser/score function at specific timesteps (Chen et al., Wang et al.) by proving that this linearity composes through the full DDIM sampling process to yield a linear input-output (initial noise → generated image) relationship, with a much weaker assumption (second-order smoothness of the score) than prior analytical results requiring Gaussian mixture data distributions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The discovered linearity provides a principled, training-free mechanism for precise image editing and controllable generation, with a user-adjustable 'diversity slider' that has direct practical value in photo editing applications. The CCS method consistently outperforms baselines in centering samples around a target mean at a fixed MSE while maintaining superior perceptual quality, though the margins are moderate. Key limitations include: only mean and MSE are controlled (other statistical properties are unaddressed), DDIM inversion may produce non-Gaussian noise that slightly degrades quality, and linearity degrades significantly for out-of-distribution data in non-foundation models. Open questions include whether the linearity property can guide better latent space design for large-scale diffusion models and whether the approach extends to controlling higher-order statistics or multi-modal targets.
