## Problem

Existing reverse-engineering tools for multistable RNNs—fixed-point finding and local linearization—cannot characterize separatrices, the manifolds separating basins of attraction that determine how perturbations shift the system between stable states. In high-dimensional settings, directly locating these boundaries via grid search or repeated ODE simulation is computationally intractable, leaving a critical gap in the dynamical-systems analysis toolkit for neuroscience and ecology.

## Input / Output

The method requires only the vector field f(x) as a black-box callable (no trajectory data or analytical solutions), a user-chosen sampling distribution p(x) that must be approximately bisected by the separatrix and forward-invariant, and a scalar eigenvalue λ controlling the timescale of the eigenfunction. It outputs a smooth scalar field ψ(x) whose zero level set is the separatrix, from which minimal-norm perturbations Δ* can be extracted via gradient-based optimization to push the system across basin boundaries with the smallest possible input amplitude.

## Method

The key insight is that Koopman eigenfunctions with positive real eigenvalues satisfy the PDE ∇ψ·f = λψ and vanish precisely on separatrices, reducing separatrix identification to a mesh-free PDE solve. The authors parameterize ψ with a ResNet (tanh activations, residual connections) and train it by minimizing a ratio loss: the Koopman PDE residual divided by a shuffle-normalized version that breaks the trivial ψ≡0 solution. Two degeneracies are addressed: a balance regularization term (squared mean divided by variance) prevents the KEF from collapsing to zero within individual basins, and for higher-dimensional systems, an ensemble of KEFs trained on local Gaussian distributions centered at separatrix points (found via binary search between attractors) captures different separatrix directions. The entire procedure is unsupervised and requires only function evaluations of f(x) and automatic differentiation through the network, scaling to 668-dimensional RNNs in minutes.

## Results

In the 668D RNN fit to mouse neural activity (Finkelstein et al. 2021), the learned KEF shows strong agreement with ground-truth separatrix positions along Hermite curves (Figure 8C), and the KEF-guided minimal perturbation Δ* is smaller in norm than perturbations aimed directly at the target fixed point or at random separatrix points (Figure 8D). Scaling experiments on 1-bit flip-flop RNNs of increasing size yield R² > 0.99 for separatrix prediction at dimensions 32, 64, 256, and 512, with wall-clock training times of 527–718 s. The method also recovers separatrices in an 11D Lotka-Volterra ecological model and a limit-cycle system with no fixed points, demonstrating generality across system types.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not include a formal head-to-head quantitative comparison against other separatrix-finding methods (e.g., FTLE ridges, grid search, or bisection with ODE simulation). Instead, the primary implicit comparison is in the perturbation-design task (Figure 8D): the KEF-guided minimal-norm perturbation Δ* is consistently smaller than (i) a perturbation aimed directly at the target fixed point and (ii) perturbations aimed at randomly sampled separatrix points, confirming that the learned KEF provides a more efficient decision boundary. The authors argue qualitatively that FTLE-based methods may be less suitable in high dimensions due to sharp gradients near separatrices and the computational cost of differentiating through the dynamics, but no numerical FTLE baseline is reported. The curve-based R² validation (Table 2) serves as the main quantitative performance metric, with the 128D case showing a notable dip (R² = 0.867) while all other dimensions exceed 0.99.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper combines Koopman operator theory (Budišić et al. 2012) with the mesh-free neural PDE-solving paradigm of the Deep Galerkin/Deep Ritz Methods (Sirignano & Spiliopoulos 2018; E & Yu 2018) to approximate Koopman eigenfunctions as separatrix indicators. It departs from prior Koopman-based work, which typically targets negative eigenvalues (λ<0) for within-basin prediction or uses dynamic mode decomposition for system comparison, by specifically exploiting positive eigenvalues whose zeros coincide with separatrices. In the RNN reverse-engineering literature (Sussillo & Barak 2013), where fixed-point localization and local linearization are standard, this paper fills a gap by providing a global, gradient-based tool for basin-boundary identification that complements rather than replaces local analysis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method offers the RNN reverse-engineering and computational neuroscience communities a practical way to characterize global dynamical structure and design minimal perturbations (e.g., for optogenetic stimulation) that cross basin boundaries, demonstrated up to 668 dimensions. Key open questions include the absence of theoretical guarantees linking KEF approximation error to separatrix localization accuracy, the requirement of knowing the vector field over the entire phase space (limiting direct application to trajectory-only data), and the extension to stochastic or uncertain dynamics. The identified degeneracies (basin collapse, directional ambiguity) and their regularization solutions are important practical contributions that will guide future applications.
