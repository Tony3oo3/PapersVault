## Problem

In incomplete multi-view semi-supervised learning, missing views for certain samples create 'vacuum regions' that fragment complete class clusters into disconnected sub-clusters, violating the smoothness assumption underlying label propagation. Existing methods (e.g., AMSC, SLIM) simply disregard missing samples when constructing view-specific graphs, which distorts local structure and misleads both graph fusion and label prediction. This paper identifies this phenomenon as the Sub-Cluster Problem (SCP) and is the first to propose a framework that explicitly mitigates its impact on graph-based multi-view semi-supervised learning.

## Input / Output

The method takes incomplete multi-view feature matrices {X_v ∈ R^{n×d_v}} where each sample may be missing 1 to V−1 views, along with a small set of labeled samples (label annotation ratio as low as 1%). Bipartite graphs Z_v ∈ R^{n×m} are pre-constructed per view using m anchors (m ≪ n) selected via BKHK, with missing-sample rows initialized as uniform probability vectors. The output is a consensus bipartite graph P ∈ R^{n×m}, soft label matrices F (for samples) and Q (for anchors), and final hard class predictions for all unlabeled samples.

## Method

AGF-TI combines three mechanisms: (1) an Adversarial Graph Fusion operator formulated as min_α max_P over the simplex, where view weights α are minimized while the consensus graph P is maximized, yielding a fused graph robust to sub-cluster distortions; permutation matrices T_v align anchors across views to resolve the anchor-unaligned problem. (2) A tensorial imputation module that stacks all view-specific bipartite graphs into a third-order tensor Z ∈ R^{m×V×n} and applies the t-SVD-based Tensor Nuclear Norm to recover missing sample–anchor similarities from cross-view high-order consistency. (3) An anchor-based strategy that reduces complexity from O(n³) to O(nm²) via Woodbury matrix identity. The resulting max-min-max objective is solved by an ADMM-based alternating algorithm with a reduced gradient descent step for the α-subproblem (using the envelope theorem to compute the gradient of the value function), tensor tubal-shrinkage for the TNN subproblem, and SVD-based updates for the permutation matrices, with proven convergence to a stationary point.

## Results

Under the standard setting of VMR=50% and LAR=5%, AGF-TI achieves the highest accuracy across all six datasets: 80.23% on CUB, 95.23% on UCI-Digit, 79.99% on Caltech101-20, 95.58% on MNIST-USPS, and competitive results on OutScene and AwA. The method maintains robustness as view missing increases, with ACC declining less than 6% on OutScene when VMR rises from 30% to 70%, compared to ~13% drops for SLIM and AMSC. At the extreme of only 1% labeled data (LAR=1%, VMR=60%), AGF-TI's performance is on par with most baselines operating at higher label ratios, demonstrating superior label utilization efficiency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AGF-TI consistently outperforms all 11 baselines on CUB, UCI-Digit, Caltech101-20, and MNIST-USPS across all tested VMRs (30%, 50%, 70%). The largest margins appear on CUB (80.23% vs. 69.19% for CFMSC, the strongest early-fusion baseline, at VMR=50%) and MNIST-USPS (95.58% vs. 89.61% for AMGL), where the Sub-Cluster Problem is most severe. Among the two dedicated IMvSSL baselines, AMSC (91.21% on UCI-Digit) and SLIM (81.41%) are the closest competitors on UCI-Digit, but AGF-TI still leads by 4.02 and 13.82 points respectively, attributed to the tensorial imputation recovering missing sample relationships. On AwA (10,158 samples, 50 classes), AGF-TI obtains two sub-optimal results at VMR=30% but shows dominant superiority as VMR increases, suggesting the method's advantage grows with data incompleteness. The ablation confirms that both the adversarial min-max fusion (removing α_v drops average ACC by ~5%) and tensorial imputation (removing TI drops average ACC by ~6.2% at VMR=30%, up to 17.8% at VMR=70%) are essential components.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the incomplete multi-view semi-supervised learning (IMvSSL) line initiated by AMSC [19] and SLIM [46], which handle missing views by simply ignoring missing samples during graph construction. The key departure is the identification of the Sub-Cluster Problem (SCP): missing samples create 'vacuum regions' that fragment clusters and violate the smoothness assumption underlying label propagation. To address SCP, the paper combines two previously separate ideas—min-max adversarial graph fusion (adapted from [35]) and tensor nuclear norm-based imputation (from [33, 36])—within a unified anchor-accelerated framework [11, 25], rather than treating missingness as a purely data-level issue to be imputed before graph construction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The SCP identification provides a concrete mechanistic explanation for why existing IMvSSL methods underperform, shifting the focus from 'how to impute missing features' to 'how to preserve local manifold structure despite missingness.' The empirical finding that general GMvSSL methods with simple feature imputation (DMF) can outperform dedicated IMvSSL methods challenges the assumption that explicit missingness-aware graph construction is necessary. Limitations include the reliance on pre-constructed bipartite graphs (anchor selection is not jointly optimized), evaluation on relatively small datasets (≤10K samples), and the assumption that missingness is random rather than informative. Future work on joint anchor-graph optimization and scalability to very large or non-randomly-missing settings remains open.
