## Problem

In scientific experiments with complex measurements (e.g., videos of animal behavior), outcome annotations are expensive and slow, motivating the use of predictive models to impute missing labels for causal inference. However, when the model must be trained on a similar but different experiment (different treatments, recording platform, or effect modifiers) and transferred to a target experiment, standard Empirical Risk Minimization—even with invariance constraints like IRM or vREx—fails to guarantee valid causal conclusions due to spurious shortcuts in anticausal prediction. This paper formalizes Prediction-Powered Causal Inference (PPCI) and identifies the conditions under which a predictor yields valid treatment effect estimates in this out-of-distribution generalization setting.

## Input / Output

The method consumes video frames (700×700 RGB, analyzed at 2 fps from 30 fps recordings) as complex measurements X, alongside observed treatment assignments T and low-dimensional discrete experimental setting covariates W (e.g., batch, position, time of day). The model outputs a predicted outcome Y (a binary grooming label per frame), which is aggregated to the video level and fed into an AIPW estimator to produce an average treatment effect point estimate with 95% confidence intervals. Critically, the target experiment provides no human annotations at all—the outcome is entirely imputed by the fine-tuned model, making the prediction-powered sample the sole basis for causal estimation.

## Method

The core theoretical contribution is the Causal Lifting constraint, a conditional independence condition I(φ(X), Z | Y) = 0 on the encoder representation that prevents the model from encoding experimental settings information (treatment, effect modifiers) beyond what is mediated by the outcome. The paper proves that this constraint, combined with representation transferability and a Bayes-optimal head, is sufficient to transfer causal validity from a training experiment to a similar target experiment (Theorem 3.1). For practical enforcement, the authors propose Deconfounded Empirical Risk Minimization (DERM), which reweights the training distribution so that the conditional outcome distribution becomes uniform across experimental settings and observations are weighted inversely to their conditional outcome variance, effectively removing the signal for the encoder to learn spurious setting-outcome correlations. In practice, DERM is applied as a one-time per-sample weight computed before fine-tuning a pre-trained vision transformer (ViT, CLIP, DINOv2) with a small MLP head on a similar annotated experiment, after which the model is applied zero-shot to the target experiment for AIPW-based ATE estimation.

## Results

On CausalMNIST, DERM achieves ATE estimation bias of 0.14±0.14 under OoD linear-effect soft shift, compared to 0.86±0.14 (ERM), 0.83±0.15 (vREx), and 0.76±0.18 (IRM). Under OoD non-linear-effect soft shift, DERM reaches 0.08±0.27 bias versus 0.64±0.18 (ERM), 0.55±0.17 (vREx), and 0.45±0.18 (IRM). On the real-world ISTAnt experiment, DERM is the only method that consistently produces valid 95% confidence intervals (overlapping the ground-truth ATE) across all five pre-trained encoders, without ever estimating the effect with the wrong sign, whereas ERM, vREx, and IRM all produce at least one invalid interval.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares DERM against three baselines: vanilla ERM, vREx (Variance Risk Extrapolation, Krueger et al. 2021), and IRM (Invariance Risk Minimization, Arjovsky et al. 2019). On CausalMNIST, DERM dominates all baselines in every OoD setting: under linear-effect soft shift it reduces ATE bias from 0.76–0.86 (ERM/vREx/IRM) to 0.14, and under non-linear-effect soft shift from 0.45–0.64 to 0.08. Under hard shifts (where the full-support assumption is violated), DERM still leads (0.75 vs. 1.02–1.05 for linear; 0.45 vs. 0.77–0.85 for non-linear), though the gap narrows. On the real-world ISTAnt experiment, DERM is the only method that yields valid ATE confidence intervals across all five pre-trained backbones (ViT-B, ViT-L, CLIP-ViT-B, CLIP-ViT-L, DINOv2), while ERM consistently underestimates the effect and vREx/IRM remain occasionally invalid. The ablation in Table 3 further confirms that the prediction bias from ERM propagates identically across AIPW, X-Learner, BART, and Causal Forest estimators, whereas DERM's lower bias is preserved regardless of the downstream causal estimator.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the factual outcome estimation problem for causal inference from complex observations introduced by Cadei et al. (2024) into a formal cross-experiment generalization setting (PPCI), departing from classic Prediction-Powered Inference which targets in-distribution efficiency. The DERM implementation adapts the resampling/reweighting paradigm of Kirichenko et al. (2022) and Li & Vasconcelos (2019) to enforce a new Causal Lifting constraint, positioning itself as a simpler alternative to invariance regularizers like IRM and vREx. Conceptually, the paper shifts the causal representation learning agenda from full block-identifiability of latent variables toward task-specific representations that make a particular causal estimand estimable, aligning with the 'representation learning' framing of Bengio et al. (2013).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration of valid causal inference on a real scientific experiment (ISTAnt) with complex video measurements and no human annotations, by fine-tuning a pretrained model on a similar annotated experiment — a practical milestone for accelerating ecological and biomedical research. Key open questions include: whether the Causal Lifting constraint and conditional calibration can be verified without target labels, how DERM behaves when the full-support assumption (Eq. 8) is violated (as shown by its degradation on 'hard shift' CausalMNIST instances), and whether the approach extends to non-discrete or high-dimensional experimental settings. The strong reliance on idealized assumptions (Bayes-optimal head, representation transfer, identifiability) limits immediate applicability but provides a clean theoretical scaffold for future work.
