## Problem

Most theoretical analyses of Adam focus on its full-batch version, yet stochastic Adam used in practice behaves fundamentally differently from its full-batch counterpart even at vanishingly small learning rates. This paper addresses the open question of how batch size and weight decay jointly govern Adam's and AdamW's generalization in nonconvex, over-parameterized settings, and why Adam demands far more sensitive weight decay tuning than AdamW.

## Input / Output

The theoretical analysis operates on a feature-noise patch data model where each input is a 2d-dimensional vector composed of two d-dimensional patches: a 1-sparse signal patch (y·v) and an s-sparse Gaussian noise patch (ξ), producing a binary label y ∈ {−1,+1}. The two-layer CNN with width m = polylog(n) and activation σ(x) = [x]_+^q (q ≥ 3) maps these inputs to two logits for binary classification. Empirical validation uses standard RGB image classification: VGG16 and ResNet18 on CIFAR-10 (32×32) and ResNet-50 on ImageNet-1K (224×224), with batch sizes varied from 16 to full dataset size.

## Method

The paper provides the first theoretical characterization of how batch size affects Adam/AdamW generalization on two-layer over-parameterized CNNs, using a two-stage analysis (pattern learning followed by regularization) and proving that stochastic Adam can be rigorously approximated by SignSGD when gradient magnitudes dominate optimization noise. The key mechanism is that mini-batch training creates an asymmetry: shared feature vectors are learned robustly across all samples, while data-specific sparse noise is suppressed because Adam's coordinate-wise normalization maintains consistent learning rates for shared features but substantially slows noise memorization, and weight decay then erases residual noise components. For large-batch training (n/B = Θ(1)), both Adam and AdamW converge to solutions with test error ≥ 1/2 − o(1) despite zero training error, as the model overfits noise before regularization takes effect. The paper further derives that Adam's admissible weight decay is bounded by λ_Adam ~ σ_0^(q−2), strictly smaller than AdamW's λ_AdamW ~ B²/n ∧ 1, because Adam's adaptive normalization amplifies the effective impact of weight decay and can destabilize updates when λ is too large.

## Results

The paper's main contributions are theoretical: Theorems 4.1/4.4 prove that large-batch Adam and AdamW (n/B = Θ(1)) achieve test error ≥ 1/2 − o(1) despite zero training error, while Theorems 4.2/4.5 prove that mini-batch variants (n/B ≥ Θ(log ε⁻¹)) achieve near-zero test error. Empirically, on CIFAR-10 with VGG16 and ResNet18, both optimizers show a sharp test-error degradation once batch size exceeds a critical threshold (Figure 1). For weight decay sensitivity at batch size 16 (Figure 2), Adam's test error spikes for λ > 0.05, whereas AdamW remains robust up to λ = 0.5, confirming the theoretical bound that Adam's admissible λ is O(σ₀^{q−2}) while AdamW's is Ω̃(B²/n ∧ 1) up to Õ(1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not benchmark against external baselines; instead it compares Adam vs. AdamW and large-batch vs. mini-batch regimes. Theoretically, both Adam and AdamW fail to generalize in the large-batch regime (test error ≈ 50%), but mini-batch training restores near-zero test error for both. The key differentiator is weight decay tolerance: Corollary 4.6 establishes that Adam's effective λ is bounded by σ₀^{q−2} (extremely small, initialization-dependent), while AdamW tolerates λ up to Õ(1) (constant-order, initialization-independent). Empirically, Adam with λ = 5×10⁻⁴ degrades catastrophically above λ ≈ 0.05, while AdamW with λ = 1×10⁻² remains stable up to λ = 0.5 on both VGG16 and ResNet18. This explains the well-known practical observation that Adam requires delicate weight-decay tuning while AdamW is far more robust.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the full-batch Adam generalization analysis of Zou et al. (2023b) to the stochastic (mini-batch) regime, which is the setting actually used in practice. It adopts the two-stage (pattern learning + regularization) proof framework from Cao et al. (2022) and leverages the SignSGD approximation of Adam (Bernstein et al., 2018) as the key analytical tool, extending that correspondence from full-batch to stochastic settings. The paper also extends the analysis from Adam alone to AdamW, providing the first theoretical comparison of their effective weight decay bounds in nonconvex feature-learning settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean theoretical explanation for two well-known but previously unexplained empirical phenomena: (1) why Adam's generalization degrades sharply with large batch sizes, and (2) why Adam requires much smaller weight decay than AdamW. The key insight—that stochastic gradient noise itself acts as an implicit regularizer that selectively suppresses noise memorization while preserving feature learning—offers a mechanistic understanding beyond the standard 'SGD noise helps generalization' narrative. Limitations include restriction to two-layer CNNs and a simplified single-feature/single-noise-patch data model; extending to deeper architectures, NLP, and accounting for modern techniques (learning rate schedules, gradient clipping, normalization layers) that allow some architectures to succeed with large batches remain important open questions.
