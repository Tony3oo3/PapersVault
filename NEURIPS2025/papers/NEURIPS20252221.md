## Problem

Existing regret analyses for policy gradient methods in infinite-horizon average-reward MDPs rely on strong ergodicity assumptions (exponential mixing, aperiodicity, no transient states) that are difficult to verify in practice. No prior work provides order-optimal regret guarantees for general parameterized policy gradient methods under the weaker unichain assumption, which permits both transient states and periodicity yet still supports the classic policy gradient theorem.

## Input / Output

The algorithm operates entirely online on a single trajectory of length T = 2KHB, requiring no simulator access or ability to reset to arbitrary initial distributions. It takes as input the MDP's state and action spaces, transition dynamics, and reward function, along with a parameterized policy class Π_Θ ⊂ ℝ^d and a linear feature map φ: S → ℝ^m for the critic. The output is a sequence of policy parameters θ_k achieving Õ(√T) expected regret, with the bound depending on problem-specific constants C_hit (worst-case expected time to enter the recurrent class) and C_tar (expected hitting time of a stationary-distributed state).

## Method

NAC-B (Natural Actor-Critic with Batching) runs K outer iterations, each collecting 2BH contiguous samples under the current policy: the first BH samples drive H critic updates (estimating average reward η_k and linear value function ζ_k^⊤φ(·) via a joint TD-style recursion), and the next BH samples drive H natural policy gradient updates using the resulting advantage estimates. The key technical insight is that large-batch averaging (B = Θ(√T)) replaces the exponential mixing used in ergodic analyses: the time-averaged distribution converges to stationarity at O(1/t) rate (Lemma 2), reducing both bias and variance of gradient estimates by a factor of 1/B without requiring aperiodicity. Transient states are handled by proving the chain enters its recurrent class within B steps with probability ≥ 1 − 2^{−⌊B/2C_hit⌋} (Lemma 10), after which the Strong Markov Property allows restricting all analysis to the irreducible recurrent class. A weaker critic regularity condition (Assumption 2) replaces strict positive definiteness, since the kernel of the critic matrix A_v(θ) depends on the set of transient states and thus varies across policies in the unichain setting.

## Results

The main theoretical result (Theorem 1) establishes that NAC-B achieves an expected regret bound of $\tilde{O}(T(\sqrt{\epsilon_{\text{bias}}} + \sqrt{\epsilon_{\text{app}}}) + \sqrt{T}(C^2 C_{\text{tar}} + C_{\text{tar}}))$ under the unichain assumption, with $K = \Theta(\sqrt{T}/\log T)$ outer iterations, $B = \Theta(\sqrt{T})$ batch size, and $H = \Theta(\log T)$ inner iterations. This yields the order-optimal $\tilde{O}(\sqrt{T})$ regret when approximation errors vanish. The bound depends on the problem-specific constants $C_{\text{hit}}$ (worst-case expected time to enter the recurrent class) and $C_{\text{tar}}$ (expected hitting time to a stationary-distributed state), which replace the mixing time used in ergodic analyses.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NAC-B is the only algorithm in Table 1 that simultaneously achieves $\tilde{O}(\sqrt{T})$ regret, operates without ergodicity (requiring only the unichain condition), and uses a general parameterized policy. Compared to value-based methods (MDP-OOMD, MDP-EXP2, UCB-AVG, Optimistic Q-learning, γ-DC-LSCVI-UCB), NAC-B avoids the need to maintain Q-values for all state-action pairs and the computational cost of maximizing over the full action set at each iteration. Against policy-gradient baselines PPG ($\tilde{O}(T^{3/4})$) and PHAPG ($\tilde{O}(\sqrt{T})$ but requiring ergodicity), NAC-B matches or improves the regret rate while relaxing the structural assumption from ergodicity to unichain. The discounted-to-average reduction approach (Appendix A) would yield at best $\mathcal{O}(T^{4/5})$ regret using existing discounted PG guarantees, which is strictly worse than NAC-B's $\tilde{O}(\sqrt{T})$. The unichain assumption here is strictly weaker than the conditions in [3] (which require a state recurrent under all policies) and [23] (which additionally requires aperiodicity), making NAC-B applicable to a broader class of environments.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the actor-critic line of work initiated by the MLMC-NAC algorithm [16], which achieved order-optimal convergence for average-reward MDPs but required ergodicity (exponential mixing). The key departure is replacing the ergodicity assumption with the classical unichain condition—permitting transient states and periodicity—while using large-batch averaging in place of multi-level Monte Carlo to control bias and variance. It also contrasts with value-based methods (optimistic Q-learning, UCB-AVG) that achieve similar regret but require tabular or linear-MDP structure, and with direct PG methods (PPG, PHAPG) that assume ergodicity. The foundational Policy Gradient theorem of Sutton et al. [33] is the theoretical anchor, and the paper explicitly rejects the discounted-to-average reduction as impractical due to its simulator requirement and poor dependence on (1−γ)⁻¹.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first regret guarantee for general parameterized policy gradient methods under the unichain assumption, which is the weakest condition under which the average-reward policy gradient theorem is valid. The practical implication is that PG methods now have theoretical backing for environments with periodic dynamics or transient states (e.g., real-time network traffic, robotics) where ergodicity is hard to verify. Open questions include extending the analysis to constrained MDPs, neural critics, and potentially relaxing the unichain assumption further, though the authors note this last step is likely very challenging. The constants C_hit and C_tar provide a useful problem-specific complexity measure that practitioners can estimate for their environments.
