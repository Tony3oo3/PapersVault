## Problem

Prior work on constrained best-arm identification either assumes independence between reward and cost dimensions or restricts to sub-Gaussian distributions, leaving the general case of arbitrarily dependent bivariate reward-cost distributions unaddressed. This paper tackles the gap by deriving instance-optimal sample complexity bounds and matching algorithms for three models (Gaussian with fixed covariance, Gaussian with unknown covariance, and non-parametric on [0,1]²) where reward and cost can be arbitrarily coupled.

## Input / Output

At each round the algorithm pulls one of K arms and observes a bivariate sample (reward, cost) drawn from that arm's joint distribution. The answer space is [K] ∪ {None}, where None is returned when no arm's mean cost falls below the threshold γ. The stopping time is adaptive: the algorithm halts as soon as the Generalized Likelihood Ratio statistic exceeds a confidence-dependent threshold, and the recommended answer is the empirical best feasible arm at that time. A key structural detail is that the 'None' answer requires sampling the currently-best arm to accumulate evidence of its infeasibility, creating a non-trivial type conversion between arms and answers.

## Method

The core technical contribution is a unified lower-bound framework parameterized by two model-specific 'transportation cost' functions: c₁ (cost to make one arm both feasible and better than another) and c₂ (cost to flip an arm's feasibility status). The characteristic time T*(ν) and oracle sampling weights w*(ν) are computed from these functions via two nested binary searches, yielding a generic recipe that applies across all three arm models. The proposed algorithm (TaS) combines Track-and-Stop sampling with plug-in oracle weights (C-tracking) and a Generalized Likelihood Ratio stopping rule whose threshold accounts for the Pure Exploration Rank of two (at most two arms deviate simultaneously). For the Gaussian fixed-covariance and bounded non-parametric cases, δ-correctness follows from existing mixture-martingale concentration results; for the unknown-covariance Gaussian case, the paper proves a new concentration bound via the Hotelling t² distribution. The dependence between reward and cost manifests as diagonal (non-axis-aligned) optimal transports in the c₁ computation, which the algorithm must account for to achieve optimal sampling proportions.

## Results

The paper evaluates average sample complexity (runtime) across four bandit instances (Easy, Hard, All feasible, None feasible) under two models: Gaussian with unknown covariance and bounded non-parametric on [0,1]². In the Gaussian unknown covariance setting (Table 1), the proposed TaS achieves runtimes of 76.3 (Easy), 3218.7 (Hard), 190.4 (All feasible), and 222.6 (None feasible), while the adapted TopTwo-TCI baseline is faster on three of four instances (68.8, 2859.9, 174.6) but slower on None feasible (241.9). In the bounded setting (Table 2), GA-TaS achieves 98.8, 539.7, 241.0, and 69.6, while TopTwo-TCI is consistently faster (96.4, 457.3, 191.1, 47.5). All algorithms maintain empirical error rates below δ = 0.01.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper adapts two BAI sampling strategies to the constrained setting: TopTwo-TCI (from Jourdan et al., 2022) and GA-TaS (from Ménard, 2019). In the Gaussian unknown covariance model (Table 1), TopTwo-TCI outperforms the proposed TaS on three of four instances (Easy: 68.8 vs 76.3; Hard: 2859.9 vs 3218.7; All feasible: 174.6 vs 190.4), with TaS winning only on the None feasible case (222.6 vs 241.9). In the bounded non-parametric model (Table 2), TopTwo-TCI dominates GA-TaS across all four instances, with the largest gap on the Hard instance (457.3 vs 539.7, a 15% reduction) and the All feasible instance (191.1 vs 241.0, a 21% reduction). Notably, the TaS-1d baseline (which ignores the cost dimension entirely) performs catastrophically on the None feasible instance (3293.4 samples vs ~220 for other methods), highlighting the importance of properly handling the feasibility constraint. The empirical results suggest that while TaS is proven asymptotically optimal, the adapted TopTwo-TCI heuristic achieves lower finite-sample runtimes in these moderate-confidence (δ=0.01) experiments.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the classical fixed-confidence best-arm identification (BAI) framework of Garivier and Kaufmann [2016] and Degenne and Koolen [2019] to a constrained setting where each arm carries a joint reward-cost distribution with arbitrary dependence. It unifies the exponential-family and non-parametric BAI frameworks (Agrawal et al. [2020]) through a transportation-cost abstraction (two functions c₁ and c₂), and imports concentration results from Kaufmann and Koolen [2021] and Agrawal et al. [2021] for the stopping thresholds. Unlike prior constrained bandit work (Wang et al. [2022], Hu and Hu [2024], Katz-Samuels and Scott [2019]), it explicitly handles dependence between reward and cost and covers non-parametric models, filling a gap the authors identify in the literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first information-theoretic lower bounds and asymptotically optimal algorithms for constrained BAI with dependent reward-cost pairs across three distributional models, giving practitioners a principled tool for budget-constrained policy selection (e.g., promotions under ROI constraints). The transportation-cost interface is a clean abstraction that could extend to other constrained identification problems. Open questions include handling multiple simultaneous constraints (which becomes combinatorially harder in the unknown-covariance case) and tightening the stylized ln ln n threshold for the unknown-covariance and bounded models to match the proven ln n thresholds.
