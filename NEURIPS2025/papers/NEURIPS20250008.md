## Problem

Multi-modal LLMs suffer from three distinct hallucination types in multi-image settings—context omission, context conflation, and detail misinterpretation—rooted in weak cross-modal alignment and deficient internal captioning ability. Existing DPO-based methods like MIA-DPO anchor optimization to a single image reference via explicit query pointers, fundamentally limiting holistic cross-image context modeling and autonomous fine-grained perception.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250008/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sequence of 2–5 RGB images (up to 24K tokens in MM-NIAH evaluation) alongside a text instruction, and produces structured per-image captions in the fixed format 'For Image 1: <caption 1>, For Image 2: <caption 2>, …' or region-specific descriptions 'For the marked area of Image X: <caption rX>'. For needle-level optimization, visual prompts (bounding boxes, keypoints, or segmentation masks) are overlaid directly onto input images to define target regions, and the model must generate descriptions aligned with those marked areas rather than the full image content.

## Method

CcDPO is a two-level hierarchical DPO framework trained in three sequential stages. Stage 1 (Context-Level DPO) reformulates multi-image captioning as a structured per-image task and constructs preference pairs via two perturbation strategies—sequence truncation (simulating context omission) and content swapping (simulating context conflation)—optimizing the standard DPO loss to prefer coherent full-sequence descriptions over disrupted ones. Stage 2 (Needle-Level TDPO) overlays region-specific visual prompts (bounding boxes or keypoints) onto images and trains the model via DPO to prefer descriptions of the prompted region over descriptions of non-overlapping regions within the same image. Stage 3 (Needle-Level VDPO) introduces a vision-contrastive objective with two components: a Focus loss that rewards generating a description conditioned on the correctly aligned image over the image-free baseline, and a Reject loss that penalizes assigning high probability to that description when conditioned on a visually contradictory image. All stages are supported by MultiScope-42k, a 41.8k-sample dataset with automatically generated preference pairs sourced from LLaVA-23K, MDVP, and MVC, eliminating the need for manual multi-image annotation.

## Results

On the seven multi-image benchmarks, CcDPO applied to LLaVA-OV-7B achieves an average score of 67.1, surpassing the GPT-4o reference (67.6) on most individual tasks and outperforming MIA-DPO (61.9) by +5.2 points and SFT (64.1) by +3.0 points. On Qwen2-VL-7B, CcDPO reaches 66.6 average, a +2.1 gain over both SFT and MIA-DPO (64.5). On the ultra-long-context MM-NIAH retrieval task, CcDPO achieves 84.5 average across 1K–24K token contexts, a +12.8-point improvement over the LLaVA-OV baseline (71.7) and +3.4 over MIA-DPO (81.1). On single-image benchmarks, CcDPO also yields consistent gains (e.g., +1.2 avg for LLaVA-OV, +0.3 for Qwen2-VL), with the largest improvement on HallBench (+5.5 points).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CcDPO on LLaVA-OV-7B (67.1 avg) substantially outperforms MIA-DPO (61.9), the closest prior multi-image DPO method, by +5.2 points, with the largest gap on MUIRBench (+7.2) and MIBench (+7.4), confirming that MIA-DPO's reliance on predefined image references limits holistic context modeling. CcDPO also beats the SFT baseline (64.1) by +3.0, demonstrating that preference optimization with structured negative samples is more effective than supervised fine-tuning alone. Against the GPT-4o reference (67.6), CcDPO-LLaVA-OV is competitive on average but trails by 0.5 points, while CcDPO-Qwen2-VL (66.6) falls slightly behind. On single-image benchmarks (Table 3), CcDPO improves over both SFT and MIA-DPO for both base models (e.g., LLaVA-OV: 69.7 vs 69.4/69.0; Qwen2-VL: 74.1 vs 73.3/73.3), with the largest single-image gain on HallBench (+5.5 for LLaVA-OV), indicating that multi-image preference training does not degrade—and often enhances—single-image perception. On the MM-NIAH long-context retrieval task, CcDPO achieves 84.5 average, outperforming MIA-DPO (81.1) by +3.4 and the raw baseline (71.7) by +12.8, with the advantage growing at longer contexts (up to +19.7 at 24K tokens).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) from single-image to multi-image settings by proposing a hierarchical two-level framework (CcDPO) that departs from MIA-DPO's single-level, reference-anchored approach. While MIA-DPO conditions responses on explicit image references (e.g., 'In Image 1, what is...?'), CcDPO introduces structured per-image captioning preferences with targeted perturbations (truncation, swapping) at the context level, and region-specific visual prompts with vision-contrastive supervision at the needle level. The method also draws on vision-contrastive DPO ideas (V-DPO, CHIP) and visual prompting literature (SAM, CPT), but uniquely integrates these into a sequential multi-image preference pipeline rather than applying them to isolated single-image tasks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Multi-image understanding is increasingly critical for document processing, multi-view reasoning, and temporal sequence tasks, yet MLLMs exhibit severe hallucination degradation as image count grows. The finding that multi-image DPO training *improves* single-image performance (especially on hallucination-sensitive benchmarks like HallBench, +5.5) challenges the prior assumption that multi-image training degrades single-image capabilities. Open questions include whether the strict context-before-needle ordering generalizes beyond 7B models, whether the approach extends naturally to video (the paper shows preliminary VideoMME results but acknowledges temporal modeling is not explicit), and whether the automatic perturbation-based data generation scales to more complex inter-image relationships beyond caption-level swaps.
