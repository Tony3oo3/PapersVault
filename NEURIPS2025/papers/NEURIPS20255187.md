## Problem

Existing text-to-motion diffusion models generate high-quality single-semantic motion clips but produce awkward transitions and abrupt artifacts when applied to composite sequences containing multiple semantically distinct segments. Prior long-term generation methods model transitions in raw motion space, overlooking the intrinsic kinematics of each segment and causing over-smoothing or sudden stops at boundaries.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255187/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework takes text prompts (encoded via CLIP-ViT-B/32) for each motion segment and, for inbetweening tasks, the preceding and succeeding motion sequences in HumanML3D format (269 parameters/frame including 6D root rotation). It produces composite motion sequences with smooth transitions, inbetweening segments of user-specified length (45–250 frames), or long-term continuous sequences spanning hundreds of segments. A key structural detail is that all denoising occurs in a fixed-size phase latent space (frequency, amplitude, offset, phase shift vectors of dimension Q), decoupling the representation from variable segment lengths.

## Method

The framework comprises three components operating in a unified phase latent space: (1) ACT-PAE, a transformer-based periodic autoencoder that encodes variable-length motion into four phase parameters (F, A, B, S) and reconstructs motion via a sinusoidal parameterization Q = A·sin(F·(T−S)) + B, replacing DeepPhase's fixed-window convolution with a length-agnostic transformer; (2) SPDM, a self-attention ε-diffusion model that denoises phase latents conditioned on CLIP text embeddings, using both param-level and frame-level tokens to preserve semantic alignment; and (3) TPDM, a cross-attention ε-diffusion model that denoises phase latents conditioned on the clean phase latents of temporally adjacent segments, ensuring transition-aware dynamics. Predictions from SPDM and TPDM are combined via a phase mixing equation with a time-varying weight r=(k/K)³ for semantic segments (prioritizing transition compatibility early, then enriching semantics) and r=1 for unconditional transition segments. The pipeline is scalable: for long-term generation, the [TPDM_f, SPDM, TPDM_b] triplet is unrolled across all segments and denoised in parallel, with bidirectional phase propagation ensuring global coherence independent of sequence length.

## Results

On the BABEL-TEACH test set for compositional motion pair generation, the proposed Compositional Phase Diffusion achieves the best Overall FID (0.782) and Overall MMD (4.711), outperforming all baselines. It also attains the best Semantic FID (0.736) and Transition FID (1.807), demonstrating superior motion realism in both semantic and transitional segments. For long-term motion generation (302,298 frames / 168 minutes), the method achieves Overall FID 0.847 and Overall MMD 4.849, again leading all compared methods. In unconditional motion inbetweening, the framework achieves the lowest L2-Vel (0.0101 at 60 frames), L2-Rot6D (0.2124), NPSS (0.3651), and RMS-Jerk (0.0963) across all transition lengths tested.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all five long-term/compositional baselines (TEACH, PCMDM, priorMDM, MDM-30, MLD-30) on Overall FID and Overall MMD in compositional motion pair generation. Against TEACH, which achieves the best Semantic MMD (3.185 vs. 3.509), the proposed method wins on every FID metric and on Transition MMD (6.545 vs. 7.479), indicating TEACH sacrifices motion quality for text alignment. Compared to PCMDM (Overall FID 0.837) and priorMDM (Overall FID 0.839), the proposed method achieves a lower Overall FID (0.782) while also substantially improving Semantic FID (0.736 vs. 1.056/1.148) and Transition MMD (6.545 vs. 7.323/7.399). In motion inbetweening (Table 3), the method dominates all six baselines (RMIB, RSMT, CMB, MDM, priorMDM) across every metric and transition length, with particularly large margins on RMS-Jerk (0.0963 vs. 0.4058 for priorMDM at 60 frames) and L2-Vel (0.0101 vs. 0.0151 for priorMDM). MLD-30 performs worst overall (Overall FID 14.25), confirming that latent-space diffusion without phase modeling is poorly suited to compositional tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the periodic autoencoder (PAE) line of work from DeepPhase, replacing its fixed-length convolution scheme with a transformer-based ACT-PAE that handles variable-length motion natively. It departs from prior long-term motion generation methods (TEACH, PCMDM, priorMDM) that model transitions in raw motion space by instead performing diffusion in a unified phase latent manifold, where SPDM and TPDM modules iteratively denoise phase parameters conditioned on text and adjacent-segment dynamics. The compositional parallel denoising of multiple segments with bidirectional phase propagation is a new architectural arrangement, though the underlying task and evaluation protocol follow established conventions from the BABEL-TEACH benchmark.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The phase-space diffusion formulation offers a principled alternative to raw-motion-space transition modeling, potentially reducing artifacts like over-smoothing and abrupt stops that plague blending-based approaches. The framework's scalability to arbitrary segment counts and its natural extension to motion inbetweening (with notably strong RMS-Jerk results) suggest a reusable template for multi-segment motion synthesis. However, evaluation is limited to a single dataset (BABEL-TEACH), no error bars are reported, and the phase mixing strategy is a simple linear interpolation that the authors acknowledge could be improved with score-based or learnable mixing. Open questions include whether the phase-space advantage persists at much longer sequences or with more diverse motion vocabularies beyond the BABEL action set.
