## Problem

Current knowledge graph embedding (KGE) methods are primarily optimized for link prediction via local contrastive learning, yet strong link-prediction performance does not correlate with downstream task utility. Additionally, applying these methods to the largest knowledge graphs (tens to hundreds of millions of entities) requires distributed multi-GPU systems with significant engineering overhead, making them impractical on commodity hardware.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255655/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

SEPAL takes a large knowledge graph as entity-relation triples (h, r, t) and produces fixed-dimensional node embeddings (typically d=100) suitable as features for downstream tabular regression or classification. The method is model-agnostic: it wraps any KGE whose scoring function follows the form f(h,r,t) = −sim(φ(θ_h, w_r), θ_t), including DistMult, TransE, RotatE, ComplEx, TuckER, MuRE, and QuatE. The output embeddings are explicitly designed for feature enrichment of external tables rather than for link prediction, and the entire pipeline is constrained to fit within a single GPU's memory (demonstrated on a 32 GB V100 for a 91M-entity graph).

## Method

SEPAL replaces full-graph contrastive optimization with a three-stage pipeline: (1) BLOCS partitions the scale-free graph into a dense core subgraph (selected by entity degree and relation coverage) plus overlapping outer subgraphs of bounded size that fit on one GPU; (2) a standard KGE model (e.g., DistMult) is trained only on the core to obtain core-entity and relation embeddings; (3) these embeddings are propagated to all outer entities via T steps of relation-aware message passing that applies the KGE's own relational operator φ, with L2-normalized updates and no negative sampling. The key insight is that this propagation is provably equivalent to projected gradient descent on a global alignment energy over positive triples, yielding globally consistent embeddings without the local-contrastive bias that hinders downstream transfer. BLOCS specifically addresses the scale-free topology of large KGs by switching between diffusion and dilation regimes to handle both high-degree hubs and long peripheral chains.

## Results

Across 46 downstream tasks (4 real-world regression + 42 WikiDBs classification/regression) and 7 knowledge graphs (from Mini YAGO3 to WikiKG90Mv2 with 91M entities), SEPAL achieves the best average normalized downstream score (R² for regression, weighted F1 for classification) for every source knowledge graph, as confirmed by critical difference diagrams with statistical significance tests. SEPAL embeds WikiKG90Mv2 (91M entities, 601M triples) on a single 32GB V100 GPU, and speeds up DistMult training by over 20× for a given configuration. On the performance-cost Pareto frontier, SEPAL dominates for larger graphs while FastRP remains Pareto-optimal at very low computation budgets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SEPAL consistently outperforms both PyTorch-BigGraph (PBG) and DGL-KE on downstream feature-enrichment tasks across all 7 knowledge graphs, as shown by critical difference diagrams where SEPAL holds the best average rank. Against FastRP (sparse random projection), SEPAL wins on larger knowledge graphs where relational structure matters, but FastRP remains competitive at very low compute budgets and is Pareto-optimal for small embedding times. When used as a wrapper, SEPAL improves the downstream performance of its base model: SEPAL+DistMult, SEPAL+RotatE, and SEPAL+TransE all outperform their respective standalone counterparts on the 4 real-world regression tasks. On knowledge graph completion (link prediction), SEPAL sometimes underperforms PBG and DGL-KE, though no method is consistently superior and statistical tests show no significant differences, confirming that SEPAL's design trade-off (global alignment over local contrast) favors downstream tasks over link prediction.

## Relation to Prior Work

SEPAL builds on the standard KGE framework (DistMult, TransE, RotatE, etc.) but fundamentally restructures the optimization: rather than applying local contrastive learning (negative sampling) across the entire graph as in PyTorch-BigGraph or DGL-KE, it trains only on a dense core subgraph and then propagates embeddings to the periphery via relation-aware message passing. This departs from prior embedding propagation work (CompGCN, REP), which uses propagation as a post-processing smoothing step on already-computed full-graph embeddings, and from distributed-training systems (Marius, SMORE, GE2) that scale by parallelizing the same stochastic optimization. The BLOCS partitioning algorithm is introduced because existing graph partitioning methods (METIS, spectral, propagation-based) fail on the scale-free, high-degree-node structure of large knowledge graphs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that global embedding alignment (maximizing scores on positive triples without negative sampling) is more beneficial than local contrastive discrimination for downstream feature enrichment, challenging the field's default assumption that negative sampling is essential. Practically, SEPAL embeds a 91M-entity, 601M-triple knowledge graph on a single 32GB GPU with a 20× speedup over standard DistMult training, removing the need for distributed systems. Limitations include degraded link-prediction performance (expected, given the absence of negative sampling) and the fact that a simple random-projection baseline (FastRP) remains Pareto-optimal at low compute budgets, suggesting the relational structure captured by KGE models may be less critical than previously assumed for tabular feature enrichment. Open questions include adaptation to continual learning as knowledge graphs are updated, and whether the core-plus-propagation paradigm extends to other graph-structured data beyond knowledge graphs.
