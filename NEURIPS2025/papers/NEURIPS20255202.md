## Problem

Production LLM systems must route each user prompt to the right model from a diverse pool, balancing accuracy against inference cost. Existing routers either ignore prompt-specific context, require expensive profiling or full retraining when the expert pool changes, or rely on cost-agnostic objectives that collapse toward cheap models. No prior work simultaneously embeds prompts and arbitrary experts (open-weight or black-box) into a shared metric space, bakes cost awareness directly into the training loss, and generalizes to unseen models without retraining.

## Input / Output

At inference, the router takes a single text prompt, encodes it with a frozen all-MiniLM-L6-v2 sentence transformer plus a lightweight 2-layer MLP head, and performs a k-NN lookup (k=4) in a pre-built FAISS index over expert descriptors. The expert descriptors are 256-dimensional vectors computed offline: logit footprints (averaged top-256 token probabilities over 10 generation steps on 192 probes) for open-weight models, or perplexity fingerprints (GPT-2 cross-entropy over model outputs on the same probes) for black-box APIs. The output is a single routing decision—the expert maximizing cosine similarity minus a cost penalty λ·c(h)—delivered in microseconds with no retraining when the pool changes.

## Method

CSCR maps both prompts and experts into a shared unit-sphere embedding space and trains a contrastive encoder with a novel Cost-Spectrum InfoNCE loss. The loss partitions experts into K=5 percentile cost bands, retains all correct experts as positives within each band (preventing cost-collapse toward cheap models), applies band-specific temperatures τ_k = τ_min + α·c̄_k to smooth gradients in sparse high-cost bands, and subtracts γ·c_m in the denominator to penalize expensive wrong experts more heavily. At inference, routing reduces to a single FAISS k-NN query followed by a cost-adjusted argmax, requiring no retraining when experts are added or removed. The paper provides an excess-risk bound showing the cost-spectrum k-NN rule converges to the Bayes-optimal router at the standard k-NN rate, and a directional-alignment lemma proving the InfoNCE optimum ranks cheaper correct experts ahead of expensive or incorrect ones.

## Results

CSCR achieves the highest Area Under the Deferral Curve (AUDC) across all three benchmarks: 0.541 on EmbedLLM, 0.051 on MixInstruct, and 0.711 on RouterBench, outperforming the strongest baselines (UMR at 0.515/0.049/0.568 and Parametric at 0.506/0.039/0.691). It also attains the lowest Query-Normalized Cost (QNC) among competitive routers on EmbedLLM (43.28 vs. UMR's 58.61) and MixInstruct (9.32 vs. UMR's 10.35), indicating more cost-efficient routing. Peak accuracy is competitive or superior: 0.578 on EmbedLLM (matching Soft-MoE's 0.577) and 0.794 on RouterBench (matching Thompson and Soft-MoE). The paper reports up to 25% improvement in accuracy-cost efficiency over baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CSCR outperforms UMR [40] on AUDC across all three benchmarks (0.541 vs. 0.515 on EmbedLLM, 0.051 vs. 0.049 on MixInstruct, 0.711 vs. 0.568 on RouterBench) while also achieving substantially lower QNC on EmbedLLM (43.28 vs. 58.61) and MixInstruct (9.32 vs. 10.35). Against Thompson Sampling [48], CSCR wins on AUDC by a wide margin on EmbedLLM (0.541 vs. 0.472) and RouterBench (0.711 vs. 0.622), though Thompson achieves slightly lower QNC on RouterBench (1.634 vs. 1.660). CSCR surpasses Soft-MoE [64] on AUDC across all benchmarks (e.g., 0.541 vs. 0.404 on EmbedLLM) and matches its peak accuracy on RouterBench (0.794). The Parametric softmax router [62] is the closest competitor on RouterBench (AUDC 0.691 vs. 0.711), but CSCR still leads and achieves comparable peak accuracy. On the generalization tests (Tables 2 and 3), CSCR maintains its lead over UMR and Thompson when tested on unseen LLMs and out-of-distribution prompts, with AUDC of 0.4848 (new LLMs) and 0.5146 (OOD) versus the next-best baselines at 0.4766 and 0.4863 respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

CSCR extends the contrastive-learning formulation of InfoNCE (van den Oord et al., 2019) by injecting cost-awareness directly into the training objective through banded positives, band-specific temperatures, and a negative cost penalty, rather than deferring cost to a post-hoc scoring function as in UMR or LLM-Bandit. It also departs from parametric softmax-gate routers (Switch Transformer, SoftMoE) by reducing inference to a single FAISS k-NN lookup, eliminating retraining when the expert pool changes. The two lightweight descriptors—logit footprints for open-weight models and perplexity fingerprints for black-box APIs—draw on the observation that a model's first-token distribution and its cross-entropy under a shared scorer are sufficient, task-independent capability signatures, a framing not previously exploited for routing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: microsecond routing latency, no retraining on pool changes, and a unified descriptor space that mixes open and closed models make CSCR a drop-in component for production multi-LLM systems. The ablation revealing that vanilla InfoNCE suffers 'cost-collapse' (AUDC 0.342 → 0.495 with cost-aware training) is a useful diagnostic for anyone building contrastive routers. Open questions include whether the approach scales to pools of hundreds of models, whether the 256-token logit basis is near-optimal, and how to handle experts whose capabilities shift over time (e.g., via continued fine-tuning). The authors note they could not test very large pools due to resource limits, leaving the scaling behavior of the FAISS index and the contrastive objective under stress unverified.
