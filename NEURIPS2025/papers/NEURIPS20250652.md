## Problem

Maximal Update Parametrization (μP) enables stable hyperparameter transfer from small to large vanilla Transformers, dramatically reducing tuning costs at scale. However, diffusion Transformers differ fundamentally in both architecture (adaLN blocks, cross-attention, timestep conditioning) and training objective (iterative denoising vs. autoregressive prediction), leaving it unclear whether μP's theoretical guarantees and practical HP transferability extend to this family. Without a principled answer, scaling diffusion Transformers to billions of parameters remains bottlenecked by prohibitively expensive manual HP tuning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250652/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

For text-to-image generation, the model consumes a text prompt (or class label for ImageNet) and Gaussian noise, producing a 256×256 RGB image through iterative denoising. The μP framework itself operates in hyperparameter space: it takes a small proxy model (e.g., 0.04B parameters, 5 epochs) and a target model (e.g., 0.61B or 18B parameters), searches optimal base HPs (learning rate, gradient clip, loss weights, warm-up steps) on the proxy, and transfers them zero-shot to the target. Evaluation uses FID, CLIP Score, GenEval, and human alignment scoring on 256×256 generated samples, with or without classifier-free guidance depending on the architecture.

## Method

The paper proves (Theorem 3.1) that the forward passes of DiT, U-ViT, PixArt-α, and MMDiT are expressible as NE⊗OR⊤ Programs, meaning their μP scaling rules are identical to those of vanilla Transformers (Table 1: input/hidden/output weight abc-exponents). Practically, μP is implemented by fixing the attention head dimension and scaling the number of heads, then adjusting each weight's multiplier, initialization variance, and learning rate by powers of the width ratio n/n_base. The μ Transfer algorithm searches optimal base HPs on a small proxy model (e.g., 0.04B, 5 epochs, small batch) and applies them directly to a large target model (e.g., 18B, 200K steps, batch 4096). This yields 2.9× faster convergence for DiT-XL-2, consistent outperformance for PixArt-α at 5.5% tuning FLOPs, and superior MMDiT-18B results at only 3% of the FLOPs consumed by human expert tuning.

## Results

DiT-XL-2-μP with the transferred base learning rate (2⁻¹⁰) achieves 2.9× faster convergence than the original DiT-XL-2, reaching the target FID-50K in 2.4M steps versus 7M steps. On text-to-image generation, PixArt-α-μP (0.61B) consistently outperforms PixArt-α across GenEval, FID-30K, and CLIP Score at all training checkpoints (e.g., at epoch 30: GenEval 0.26 vs 0.15, FID-30K MJHQ 29.96 vs 42.71), requiring only 5.5% FLOPs of one full pretraining run for HP tuning. At the largest scale, MMDiT-μP-18B surpasses the human-expert-tuned MMDiT-18B on GenEval (0.8218 vs 0.8154) and human alignment accuracy (0.715 vs 0.703) while consuming only 3% of the FLOPs typically spent on manual HP tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the original DiT-XL-2 [52], DiT-XL-2-μP with the zero-shot transferred learning rate converges 2.9× faster (2.4M vs 7M steps to match or exceed the original's best FID-50K), demonstrating that μP eliminates the need for expensive large-scale HP search. Against PixArt-α [8], the μP variant wins on every reported metric at every checkpoint: at epoch 30 it improves GenEval from 0.15 to 0.26, FID-30K (MJHQ) from 42.71 to 29.96, and FID-30K (MS-COCO) from 37.61 to 25.84, while the baseline degrades after epoch 20 due to overfitting. Against the human-expert-tuned MMDiT-18B [16], MMDiT-μP-18B achieves a higher overall GenEval score (0.8218 vs 0.8154), better performance on the Two-object (94.44 vs 93.69) and Position (62.25 vs 57.5) sub-tasks, and a higher human alignment accuracy (0.715 vs 0.703 across 22,500 tests), all while using only 3% of the FLOPs that human experts typically spend on HP tuning (≈5× full pretraining cost).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Maximal Update Parametrization (μP) from vanilla Transformers to diffusion Transformers, building directly on the Tensor Programs V framework [Yang et al., 2022] for the μ Transfer algorithm and the NE⊗OR⊤ Program formalism [Yang & Littwin, 2023] for the representability proof. It departs from the empirical-only application of μP to MMDiT in AuraFlow v0.1 by providing a rigorous theoretical guarantee (Theorem 3.1) that DiT, U-ViT, PixArt-α, and MMDiT all share the standard μP scaling rules, and by systematically validating HP transferability across widths, batch sizes, and training steps. The work also connects to the broader line of scaling studies for diffusion Transformers (DiT, SD3, DiT-MoE, HourglassDiT), all of which use standard parameterization and thus suffer from non-transferable HPs at scale.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By reducing HP tuning cost to 3–5.5% of a full pretraining run and achieving 2.9× faster convergence for DiT-XL-2, this work provides a principled, low-cost pathway to scaling diffusion Transformers that is immediately actionable for practitioners. The observation that optimal base HPs (e.g., learning rate 2⁻¹⁰) appear consistent across different architectures (DiT vs. PixArt-α) and datasets (ImageNet vs. SA-1B) hints at a deeper universality that remains unexplained. Open questions include determining the optimal proxy task size, extending μP to more exotic architectures (linear attention, MoE), and developing a learning-theoretic account of why μP improves generalization in diffusion settings. A key limitation is the absence of error bars and the non-public internal dataset used for the 18B MMDiT experiments.
