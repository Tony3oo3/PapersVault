## Problem

Current methods for interpreting VLM representations either focus on a single modality or apply separate sparse autoencoders to vision and language representations, causing a concept mismatch where neurons at the same index correspond to different semantics across modalities. This prevents meaningful comparison of vision and language semantics in a unified framework, leaving the vision-language alignment mechanism uninterpretable and hindering the diagnosis of misalignment failures such as hallucinations.

## Input / Output

The method consumes frozen vision-language representations extracted from pre-trained VLMs: for CVLMs (OpenCLIP ViT-B/32 through ViT-H/14), the outputs of the vision and text encoders are used directly; for LVLMs (LLaVA1.5, Qwen-VL), token-level representations from specific LLM hidden layers (29th and 26th respectively) are averaged across the token axis. The primary output is a unified concept set of ~32,700 neurons, each defined by its maximally activating images and texts. For downstream enhancement, the method produces either an enhanced similarity score combining representation-level and concept-level cosine similarities (for zero-shot classification) or a refined language representation that aligns its concept activations with the vision side (for hallucination elimination via contrastive decoding).

## Method

VL-SAE is a sparse autoencoder with three key architectural choices that ensure activation consistency across modalities. First, for LVLMs with implicit alignment, an auxiliary autoencoder trained with InfoNCE contrastive loss converts the alignment into an explicit cosine-similarity form. Second, the shared encoder activates neurons based on the Euclidean distance between L2-normalized inputs and learnable weight vectors (g = √(2−2cos)), which satisfies the triangle inequality—bounding the activation discrepancy between semantically similar representations by their mutual distance, unlike raw cosine similarity. Third, two modality-specific decoders reconstruct vision and language representations separately, preventing distributional information from leaking into the shared hidden activations. Training is self-supervised via reconstruction loss with Top-K sparsification (k=256, hidden ratio 8), requiring only a single RTX 4090 GPU and under 10 minutes for CVLMs.

## Results

On zero-shot image classification across 14 datasets, VL-SAE consistently improves mean accuracy over the base OpenCLIP models by 0.7–0.9 points (e.g., ViT-H/14: 76.9% → 77.8%, ViT-L/14: 72.2% → 72.9%). On the POPE hallucination benchmark, VL-SAE achieves 87.07% accuracy under the Random setting with LLaVA1.5, surpassing both VCD (85.53%) and regular decoding (82.93%). On the CHAIR open-ended captioning benchmark, VL-SAE reduces the sentence-level hallucination rate (CHAIR_S) from 53.4 to 47.8 on LLaVA1.5 and from 44.6 to 39.6 on Qwen-VL, while also improving recall.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary external baseline is VCD (Visual Contrastive Decoding) [Leng et al., CVPR 2024], a method specifically designed for hallucination elimination. VL-SAE outperforms VCD on all three POPE settings (Random, Popular, Adversarial) for both LLaVA1.5 and Qwen-VL, with the largest gains in precision (e.g., 97.28% vs. 93.68% on LLaVA1.5 Random) and accuracy (87.07% vs. 85.53%). On the CHAIR open-ended benchmark, VL-SAE also surpasses VCD, reducing CHAIR_S from 55.0 to 47.8 (LLaVA1.5) and from 42.6 to 39.6 (Qwen-VL), while achieving higher recall (76.3% vs. 76.0% and 63.3% vs. 60.0%). Notably, VL-SAE is a representation-interpretation method rather than a purpose-built hallucination-elimination technique, yet it matches or exceeds the dedicated VCD approach, demonstrating that concept-level alignment provides a more effective mechanism for mitigating hallucinations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the sparse autoencoder (SAE) framework for representation interpretation—originally developed for single-modal settings (language models by Cunningham et al., vision by Lim et al.)—to the multi-modal vision-language domain. The key departure is addressing the 'concept mismatch' problem: applying separate SAEs to vision and language representations yields incompatible concept sets, while a naively shared SAE still suffers from modality-specific distributional effects. VL-SAE resolves this with a distance-based encoder (exploiting the triangle inequality of Euclidean distance on normalized vectors, unlike raw cosine similarity) and modality-specific decoders, plus an auxiliary InfoNCE-trained autoencoder to make implicit LVLM alignment explicit.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified concept set enables both interpretation (visualizing which concepts are co-activated by image and text representations) and intervention (improving zero-shot classification and reducing hallucinations by aligning representations at the concept level). The finding that a general-purpose interpretation tool outperforms the purpose-built VCD hallucination elimination method on POPE suggests that improving alignment through concept-level understanding is a viable and potentially underexploited strategy. Limitations include dead neurons, high-frequency concepts that dilute interpretation, and a simple linear blending strategy for enhancement that likely underutilizes the learned concept structure; scaling to more VLM architectures and incorporating relational modeling among concepts are natural next steps.
