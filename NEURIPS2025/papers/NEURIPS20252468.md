## Problem

Existing state-space models for brain dynamics operate entirely in Euclidean space, treating functional connectivity matrices as flat vectors and ignoring their intrinsic geometry as symmetric positive definite (SPD) matrices on a curved Riemannian manifold. This Euclidean treatment fails to capture the holistic, self-organized nature of brain network dynamics and cannot properly model how coordinated connectivity patterns evolve over time. The paper asks: can we formulate a principled SSM whose states, transitions, and observations all respect the SPD manifold structure?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252468/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For brain data, the input is a sequence of N×N SPD correlation matrices (N=360 for HCP, 116 for AAL parcellation, 160 for Destrieux) constructed via sliding windows over BOLD time courses; for action recognition, inputs are SPD covariance matrices computed from 3D joint displacements relative to a fixed root joint. The final SPD output state is mapped to the tangent space at the identity via the matrix logarithm before being vectorized and passed through a softmax classifier. The model also produces interpretable attention maps (δ) that highlight disease-relevant brain connections or joint interactions.

## Method

GeoDynamics replaces the Euclidean linear operators of classical SSMs with Riemannian geometric operators on the SPD manifold equipped with the Stein metric. State aggregation uses weighted Fréchet means (intrinsic geodesic barycenters) instead of linear combinations, and state transitions use orthogonal group actions (isometric translations gXgᵀ) instead of additive updates, guaranteeing all intermediate states remain SPD. Temporal discretization employs a matrix exponential scheme (Ã = exp(ΔA)) for stable integration, and a global convolutional reformulation enables parallel training. An SPD-preserving attention (SPA) module applies elementwise multiplicative modulation via exp(·) on the manifold, preserving positive definiteness while adaptively amplifying task- or disease-relevant connectivity patterns. Theoretical proofs establish isometry of the translation operation, SPD preservation under convolution, and positive definiteness of the exponential and padding operations.

## Results

On the HCP-WM task-based fMRI dataset (360 brain regions), GeoDynamics achieves 98.29% ± 0.26 accuracy with 14.60M parameters, outperforming the best Mamba configuration at a matched parameter budget (14.07M params, 95.92% ± 0.50) by approximately 2.4 percentage points. On the ADNI multi-class classification task (5 cognitive stages), GeoDynamics reaches 56.00% ± 3.36 accuracy, surpassing the best spatial model (GSN, 52.80%) and best sequential model (Transformer, 52.00%). Across all five brain datasets (HCP-WM, ADNI, OASIS, PPMI, ABIDE), GeoDynamics consistently achieves the highest accuracy, with statistically significant improvements over the second-best method on neurodegenerative disease classification (p < 0.05).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GeoDynamics outperforms all 29 baselines across the eight evaluation datasets. On HCP-WM, it beats Mamba (the strongest SSM baseline) by 2.4% accuracy at a matched ~14M parameter budget (98.29% vs. 95.92%), and by 1.1% even against Mamba's largest 132M-parameter configuration (98.29% vs. 97.22%). On ADNI multi-class, GeoDynamics (56.00% Acc) surpasses the best spatial GNN (GSN, 52.80%) and best sequential model (Transformer, 52.00%), with Mamba trailing at 47.20%. On the ABIDE autism dataset, SPDNet (the prior manifold-based method) consistently ranks second, confirming that Riemannian geometry is the key differentiator. On neurodegenerative disease classification (OASIS, PPMI), GeoDynamics achieves statistically significant gains over the second-best method (p < 0.05), while on task-based HCP-WM, sequential models collectively outperform spatial models by up to 30% (p < 10⁻⁴), with GeoDynamics leading both groups. On the HAR benchmarks (Florence, HDM05, UTKinect), GeoDynamics remains competitive with dedicated action-recognition methods like DMT-Net and GR-GCN, validating cross-domain generalizability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate research threads: (1) manifold-based deep learning for functional connectivity (SPDNet, geometric-attention networks by Dan et al. 2022), which captured spatial structure of FC matrices on the SPD manifold but lacked a principled temporal dynamics model, and (2) state-space models (Mamba, S4) that model temporal evolution through latent states but operate entirely in Euclidean space. The key architectural departure is replacing the linear algebra of classical SSMs (matrix multiplication for state transitions, linear combinations for aggregation) with Riemannian geometric operations: weighted Fréchet means for temporal aggregation and orthogonal group actions for state propagation, all constrained to the SPD manifold. Prior SSM applications to neuroimaging (Suk et al. 2016, Tu et al. 2019) used Euclidean formulations with separate encoder/decoder components, while prior manifold brain models lacked the dual state/observation equation structure that gives SSMs their interpretability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The geometric SSM formulation provides a principled way to model brain dynamics that inherently respects the positive-definite structure of connectivity matrices, avoiding the distortions introduced by Euclidean projections. The SPD-preserving attention mechanism adds a layer of interpretability that highlights disease-relevant subnetworks (e.g., default mode network in AD, sensorimotor regions in PD), potentially aiding biomarker discovery. However, the empirical gains over strong Euclidean SSM baselines (Mamba) at matched parameter budgets are modest (~1% on HCP-WM), raising the open question of whether the geometric formulation is strictly necessary or whether a well-tuned Euclidean SSM with appropriate input encoding could close the gap. Scalability to very fine parcellations (beyond 360 regions) and the computational cost of iterative Fréchet mean computation remain practical concerns for large-scale deployment.
