## Problem

Integrating vision and speech in a single MLLM is challenging because the two modalities have fundamentally different feature structures (spatial vs. temporal), causing training conflicts that degrade performance on both. Additionally, existing speech-to-speech systems rely on cascaded ASR and TTS modules, introducing latency and losing paralinguistic information, while open-source models lack the combined vision-and-speech interaction demonstrated by proprietary systems like GPT-4o.

## Input / Output

The model accepts RGB images at 448×448 (with dynamic patching for high-resolution detail), video clips with duration-adaptive frame sampling (4 frames for <4s, 1 fps for 4–16s, 16 frames for >16s), speech audio encoded as Mel-filter bank features (25 ms window, 10 ms shift), and text prompts. Outputs are text responses and, for speech, a continuous 24 kHz waveform generated end-to-end via a single-codebook TiCodec (1024 codes at 40 Hz) that decodes discrete speech tokens produced by the LLM's NAR+AR decoder stack—no external TTS is invoked.

## Method

VITA-1.5 couples an InternViT-300M visual encoder and a 350 M-parameter audio encoder (4× downsampling convolutions + 24 Transformer blocks, 12.5 Hz output) through lightweight adapters into a Qwen2-7B LLM, with an end-to-end speech output head comprising a TiCodec and a 120 M-parameter NAR+AR decoder pair (4 LLaMA layers each, hidden size 896). The core contribution is a three-stage training strategy that progressively introduces modalities to relieve inter-modality conflicts: Stage 1 trains vision alignment, caption understanding, and visual QA; Stage 2 adds a CTC-trained speech encoder and adapter, then fine-tunes with mixed speech/text QA while keeping vision modules trainable; Stage 3 trains the codec and NAR+AR speech decoders on 3,000 hours of text-speech pairs with the LLM frozen, preserving all prior multimodal capabilities. The NAR decoder first produces a global semantic distribution over speech tokens, which the AR decoder then refines step-by-step, and the final token sequence is decoded into a waveform by the codec's decoder.

## Results

On image understanding benchmarks, VITA-1.5 (Stage 1) achieves an average score of 67.1 across 10 benchmarks (MMBench, MMStar, MMMU, MathVista, HallusionBench, AI2D, OCRBench, MMVet, MME), and retains 66.8 after the full three-stage training including audio output. On ASR, VITA-1.5 achieves 2.2% CER on AISHELL-1 and 3.3% WER on LibriSpeech dev-clean, outperforming all speech baselines including Freeze-Omni (2.8% CER, 4.2% WER) and VITA-1.0 (12.2% CER, 7.6% WER). On video understanding, VITA-1.5 scores 56.1 on Video-MME (w/o subtitles) and 66.7 on TempCompass, competitive with open-source models but trailing GPT-4o (71.9, 73.8) and Gemini-1.5-Pro (75.0, 67.1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On image understanding, VITA-1.5 (Stage 3, avg 66.8) trails the top open-source MiniCPM-V 2.6 (68.5) and InternVL2 (67.3) by 1.7 and 0.5 points respectively, and lags behind GPT-4o and Claude 3.5 Sonnet (both 69.3) by ~2.5 points, but surpasses GPT-4o-mini (66.3) and GPT-4V (58.5). On video understanding, VITA-1.5 (56.1 Video-MME w/o sub, 66.7 TempCompass) is competitive with LLaVA-OneVision (58.2, 64.2) and MiniCPM-V-2.6 (60.9, 66.3) but clearly behind GPT-4o (71.9, 73.8) and Gemini-1.5-Pro (75.0, 67.1). On ASR, VITA-1.5 decisively outperforms all speech baselines: it achieves 2.2% CER on AISHELL-1 vs. Freeze-Omni's 2.8% and 3.3% WER on LibriSpeech dev-clean vs. Freeze-Omni's 4.2% and Mini-Omni2's 4.8%, while VITA-1.0 scores 12.2% CER and 7.6% WER on the same sets. A key finding is that the three-stage training preserves visual capabilities: VITA-1.5 drops only 0.3 points in average image score (67.1→66.8) and 0.7 points on Video-MME (56.8→56.1) after adding audio input and output modules, confirming the modality-conflict mitigation strategy works.

## Relation to Prior Work

VITA-1.5 extends its predecessor VITA-1.0 by replacing the external TTS module with an end-to-end speech generation pipeline (NAR + AR decoders over a TiCodec single-codebook model), drawing the decoder training strategy directly from Freeze-Omni. The audio encoder architecture (downsampling convolutions + 24 transformer blocks at 12.5 Hz) also follows Freeze-Omni's design. The key methodological departure is the three-stage progressive training strategy—vision-language first, then audio input, then audio output—which is explicitly motivated by the modality conflict observed when speech and vision data are trained simultaneously, a problem not addressed in prior omni-models like Mini-Omni2 or LLaMA-Omni that handle speech but lack visual capabilities.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VITA-1.5 is among the first open-source models to achieve near real-time, end-to-end vision-and-speech interaction without cascaded ASR/TTS, narrowing the gap with proprietary systems like GPT-4o. The demonstrated preservation of vision-language performance after audio training stages validates the staged-training approach as a practical recipe for multi-modality integration. However, the model still lags proprietary systems on video understanding, lacks personalization and long-term memory, and the speech evaluation is limited to ASR benchmarks rather than subjective speech quality or full duplex dialogue, leaving open questions about real-world interactive quality at scale.
