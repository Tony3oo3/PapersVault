## Problem

Existing image compression methods are optimized in isolation for either human visual perception or specific machine vision tasks, creating a fundamental trade-off: fidelity-oriented codecs degrade semantic features needed for downstream tasks, while task-specific learned codecs lack generalization across diverse tasks and human viewing. The paper identifies that both objectives share two critical requirements—semantic integrity and perceptual realism—and asks whether a single generative codec can simultaneously satisfy both without task-specific adaptation.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 512×512 RGB image as input and produces a compressed bitstream containing quantized latent variables in the Stable Diffusion VAE latent space (8×8 spatial downsampling, not pixel space) plus losslessly encoded word-level tag IDs (~113 bits average overhead from Recognize Anything). At decode time, the bitstream is reconstructed into a full-resolution image via iterative diffusion denoising (DDIM, 50 steps), yielding a perceptually realistic reconstruction that supports downstream tasks without any task-specific fine-tuning.

## Method

Diff-ICMH builds a generative compression pipeline on a frozen pre-trained Stable Diffusion model, where a learned codec (encoder-decoder with hyperprior) compresses the image into the VAE latent space and a modified ControlNet-style control module conditions the diffusion model on the decoded latent for generative reconstruction. Two key innovations ensure semantic fidelity: (1) a Semantic Consistency loss that projects both the ground-truth and decoded latents through the frozen diffusion model's forward pass and maximizes cosine similarity of the resulting features, and (2) a Tag Guidance Module that extracts word-level semantic tags via Recognize Anything, encodes them with fixed-length coding, and injects them as text conditioning with Classifier-Free Guidance (scale 5.0) to activate relevant generative priors. Training is two-stage (200K iterations at high bitrate, then fine-tuning across all bitrates) with only the codec and control module parameters learnable while the diffusion model and tag extractor remain frozen. The composite loss combines rate, latent-space MSE distortion, diffusion noise prediction, and the SC loss, with the distortion computed in VAE latent space rather than pixel space to prioritize semantically salient information.

## Results

Diff-ICMH achieves state-of-the-art or highly competitive performance across 10 diverse downstream intelligent tasks (object detection, instance/panoptic segmentation, pose estimation on COCO 2017; image-text retrieval on Flickr30K; referring expression on RefGTA; open-set segmentation on ADE20K) without any task-specific adaptation. At very low bitrates (0.01–0.05 bpp on Flickr30K, 0.02–0.1 bpp on ADE20K), Diff-ICMH demonstrates significant advantages over all compared methods, attributed to its SC loss and TGM. For perceptual quality, Diff-ICMH achieves SOTA FID and DISTS scores among all compared methods, significantly surpassing both fidelity-focused codecs (BPG, VTM-18.2) and other perception-oriented methods (HiFiC, PerCo, MS-ILLM), while its PSNR/MS-SSIM trails behind traditional fidelity-optimized codecs as expected for a generative approach. The ablation confirms that combining SC loss and TGM yields approximately 4 mAP gain over the baseline at ~0.025 bpp on COCO object detection.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against traditional and fidelity-oriented codecs (VTM-18.2/VVC, ELIC, FTIC), Diff-ICMH consistently outperforms on all intelligent task metrics and perceptual quality (LPIPS, FID, DISTS), while trailing on PSNR/MS-SSIM as expected for a generative method. Among perception-oriented generative codecs, Diff-ICMH surpasses HiFiC, PerCo, and MS-ILLM across LPIPS, FID, and DISTS, with particularly strong gains at extremely low bitrates. DiffEIC is the closest competitor: the two methods are comparable on COCO pose estimation and RefGTA referring expression, but Diff-ICMH generally leads or matches on the remaining tasks and achieves better FID/DISTS. Against task-specific optimized codecs TransTIC and Adapter-ICMH, Diff-ICMH demonstrates superior generalizability, matching or exceeding their performance across all 10 tasks without requiring any task-specific fine-tuning, whereas those methods are optimized for specific tasks and show limited cross-task transfer.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of image compression for machines (ICM) and generative image compression. It builds directly on Stable Diffusion's VAE latent space and pre-trained diffusion model as the generative backbone, and adapts ControlNet's architecture into a bilateral control module for conditioning on compressed latents. Conceptually, it departs from prior ICM work (which either tunes traditional codecs for specific tasks, trains task-specific end-to-end codecs, or compresses intermediate features) by reframing the problem around two universal information losses—semantic integrity and perceptual realism—rather than task-specific objectives. The Semantic Consistency loss, which uses the diffusion model's own forward pass as a semantic feature extractor, is a novel repurposing of generative models for compression training signals, distinct from prior GAN-based perceptual losses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Diff-ICMH provides the first single codec evaluated across 10 diverse downstream tasks (detection, segmentation, retrieval, MLLM-based understanding) without any task-specific adaptation, demonstrating that a generative prior plus semantic alignment can generalize broadly. The key open question is computational cost: diffusion-based decoding takes 5–13 seconds per image, far exceeding practical real-time requirements, and the authors acknowledge that efficient samplers and distillation are needed before deployment. The finding that fidelity-oriented codecs suffer increasing feature divergence at deeper network layers (while generative codecs do not) provides a useful diagnostic for understanding why compressed images fail on semantic tasks, and may inform future codec design beyond this specific framework.
