## Problem

Zero-shot VLN-CE agents often suffer from execution drift, correctly understanding an instruction yet deviating from the intended path over long horizons, because existing methods operate in an open-loop fashion without structured spatial constraints or feedback on prior actions. The gap is a lack of a decision space that is simultaneously shaped by the environment's spatial layout and continuously regulated by task-progress signals.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250899/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each timestep the agent receives a 360-degree panoramic observation of 12 RGB-D views (RGB resized to 244x244x3, depth at 256x256) sampled at 30-degree intervals, plus a natural-language instruction. The output is a single selected waypoint from a sparse set of skeleton-derived candidates, each paired with a VLM-generated textual description of the corresponding visual direction; the selected waypoint is then converted into a continuous movement action. The decision space is thus a small set of spatially grounded, semantically annotated options rather than a free-form action vector.

## Method

STRIDER optimizes the agent's decision space through two coupled modules. The Structured Waypoint Generator reconstructs a local point cloud from multi-view depth, projects ground-level points into a 2D top-down plane, applies morphological thinning to extract a topological skeleton, and retains only degree-1 endpoint nodes as candidate waypoints, which are then annotated with VLM-generated directional descriptions to form a layout-constrained action space. The Task-Alignment Regulator closes the loop by feeding consecutive observation pairs and the current subtask into a VLM to produce a textual progress signal, which is concatenated with the instruction and the next structured decision space as input to an LLM that selects the next waypoint. This decision-feedback cycle iterates over T steps, allowing the agent to correct drift while remaining within spatially coherent paths, all without any task-specific training.

## Results

On R2R-CE, STRIDER achieves SR 35%, SPL 30.30, NDTW 51.8, OSR 39, and NE 6.91, outperforming the previous zero-shot SOTA (SmartWay: SR 29%, SPL 22.46) by 20.7% relative in SR and 34.9% in SPL. On RxR-CE, STRIDER reaches SR 21.2%, SPL 9.6, and NDTW 30.1, improving over CA-Nav (SR 19.0%, SPL 6.0, NDTW 13.5) by 11.5% in SR and 122.9% in NDTW. Notably, STRIDER's NE (6.91) is slightly higher than Open-Nav-GPT4 (6.70), reflecting a design trade-off favoring instruction fidelity over exact endpoint proximity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

STRIDER outperforms all zero-shot baselines on R2R-CE in SR, SPL, and NDTW: it beats SmartWay (SR 29→35, SPL 22.46→30.30) and Open-Nav-GPT4 (SR 19→35, SPL 16.10→30.30, NDTW 45.79→51.8) by substantial margins. On RxR-CE, STRIDER surpasses CA-Nav (SR 19.0→21.2, SPL 6.0→9.6, NDTW 13.5→30.1) and A2Nav (SR 16.8→21.2, SPL 6.3→9.6), with the largest relative gain in NDTW (+122.9%). The only metric where STRIDER trails a zero-shot baseline is NE on R2R-CE (6.91 vs. Open-Nav-GPT4's 6.70), which the authors attribute to a deliberate preference for semantically coherent stopping points over minimal endpoint distance. Against supervised methods (e.g., HNR: SR 61, SPL 51 on R2R-CE), STRIDER remains competitive despite operating entirely zero-shot without scene-specific training. The ablation in Table 6 further shows that applying STRIDER's Structured Waypoint Generator to the fine-tuned BEVBert model improves SR from 59 to 61 and SPL from 50 to 53, confirming the module's transferability.

## Relation to Prior Work

STRIDER positions itself within the zero-shot VLN-CE line initiated by Open-Nav [42] and extended by SmartWay [46] and CA-Nav [5], but departs from their reliance on learned waypoint predictors or unconstrained LLM action outputs. It repurposes classical morphological skeletonization from image processing and robotics path planning to construct a layout-constrained action space, and introduces a VLM-generated textual feedback loop (Task-Alignment Regulator) that has no direct analogue in prior zero-shot VLN-CE methods. The 'decision space optimization' framing explicitly borrows from robotics task-and-motion planning literature, distinguishing STRIDER from the sequence-to-sequence policy paradigm dominant in the field.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The structured waypoint generator's model-agnostic design (validated on BEVBert and across VLMs from 7B to GPT-4o) makes it a practical plug-in for existing navigation stacks, and the closed-loop feedback mechanism addresses the well-documented execution drift problem in long-horizon zero-shot navigation. However, absolute performance remains far below supervised methods (35% SR vs. 60%+ on R2R-CE), and evaluation on only 100 episodes with API-based models limits confidence in generalization. Open questions include whether skeleton-based priors scale to outdoor or highly cluttered environments, and whether the feedback loop's reliance on VLM textual descriptions introduces latency or error accumulation in real-time deployment.
