## Problem

Native multimodal LLMs like GPT-4o have demonstrated strong joint text-image understanding and generation, but their capabilities remain confined to 2D modalities. No existing model can natively process and generate 3D assets alongside text and images within a single autoregressive framework, leaving a critical gap for applications in 3D content creation, robotics, and immersive environments.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252983/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts interleaved sequences of text tokens, continuous image features (512×512 RGB), and 1024 discrete 3D tokens (from a 64³ voxel grid compressed via VQVAE into an 8192-entry codebook). It produces 3D meshes (decoded from 1024 tokens through a Rectified Flow-based voxel-to-mesh pipeline), text captions, edited 3D meshes, and conversational text responses. The 3D editing task takes a 3D mesh plus a natural-language instruction and outputs a modified mesh while preserving identity.

## Method

ShapeLLM-Omni extends Qwen-2.5-VL-Instruct-7B by appending an 8192-entry 3D VQVAE codebook, enabling a fully next-token prediction paradigm over mixed text/image/3D token sequences. The 3D VQVAE encodes 64³ voxel grids into 16³ latent grids, serializes them to 4096 tokens, then concatenates every four neighboring tokens along the channel dimension to yield 1024 tokens with 32 channels—matching the token budget of image representations in Chameleon. Training uses a 3.46-billion-token 3D-Alpaca dataset (2.56M samples) spanning text-to-3D, image-to-3D, 3D-to-caption, and 3D editing, where the editing subset (420k pairs) is constructed via an image-mediated pipeline: ChatGPT-4o edits rendered frontal views, and Trellis reconstructs the edited images back into 3D meshes. The visual encoder is frozen to preserve image understanding, while the full language model and 3D codebook are fine-tuned over 15 epochs on 48 H100 GPUs.

## Results

On image-to-3D generation (Toys4K test set), ShapeLLM-Omni achieves a CLIP score of 84.5, FD_incept of 12.2, and KD_incept of 0.09, outperforming CRM (76.1/14.7/0.12), 3DTopia-XL (76.5/49.5/1.63), and SAR3D (84.7/20.6/0.17), while remaining close to the Trellis upper bound (85.0/8.31/0.07). On text-to-3D, it scores CLIP 26.7, FD 25.9, KD 0.25, surpassing SAR3D (23.9/27.2/0.28) but trailing Trellis (30.8/18.3/0.19). For 3D captioning on Objaverse, ShapeLLM-Omni achieves B-1 of 18.51, R-L of 21.37, and METEOR of 19.89, outperforming ShapeLLM and 3D-LLM while remaining competitive with the single-task PointLLM-13B. Language capabilities are largely preserved: MMLU drops from 66.9 to 63.9, while GSM8K actually improves from 42.9 to 55.1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ShapeLLM-Omni outperforms all autoregressive and reconstruction-based baselines on image-to-3D: it beats CRM by 8.4 CLIP points (84.5 vs 76.1) and 3DTopia-XL by 8.0 CLIP points with a dramatically lower FD (12.2 vs 49.5). Against SAR3D, it wins on FD (12.2 vs 20.6) and KD (0.09 vs 0.17) for image-to-3D, and on all three metrics for text-to-3D (CLIP 26.7 vs 23.9, FD 25.9 vs 27.2, KD 0.25 vs 0.28). The only method it consistently trails is Trellis (its own base model for the VQVAE and reconstruction pipeline), which benefits from task-specific diffusion training; the authors show that after task-specific fine-tuning, ShapeLLM-Omni narrows the gap to Trellis (CLIP 30.1 vs 30.8). On 3D captioning, ShapeLLM-Omni surpasses ShapeLLM (B-1 18.51 vs 17.88, METEOR 19.89 vs 17.96) and 3D-LLM (B-1 18.51 vs 16.91), and matches or exceeds PointLLM-13B* on B-1 and R-L while being slightly behind on S-BERT (49.34 vs 50.15). For language benchmarks, the model retains most of Qwen2.5-VL's capabilities (MMLU 63.9 vs 66.9, PIQA 78.6 vs 81.0) and even improves GSM8K (55.1 vs 42.9), confirming that 3D fine-tuning does not catastrophically degrade general reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the autoregressive 3D generation line (MeshGPT, LLaMA-Mesh, SAR3D) by unifying 3D generation, understanding, and editing within a single next-token-prediction model, rather than treating them as separate pipelines. It builds directly on Qwen-2.5-VL-7B as the multimodal backbone and adopts Trellis's 3D U-Net VQVAE architecture and Rectified Flow mesh decoder, while borrowing Chameleon's token-compression strategy (concatenating neighboring tokens to reduce sequence length from 4096 to 1024). Conceptually, it departs from LLaMA-Mesh, which processes raw OBJ text and ignores 3D topology, by using VQVAE-discretized voxel tokens that preserve spatial structure, and it extends the ChatGPT-4o unified text+image paradigm to include 3D as a first-class generative modality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 3D-Alpaca dataset (3.46B tokens, 2.56M samples spanning generation, understanding, and editing) is a substantial community resource that addresses a previously scarce area of 3D instruction-tuning data. The unified autoregressive framework demonstrates that a single 7B model can handle six tasks (text-to-3D, image-to-3D, 3D captioning, 3D editing, image understanding, text reasoning) with only modest degradation in language benchmarks, suggesting a viable path toward 3D-native conversational AI. However, generation quality remains below the dedicated diffusion-based Trellis, the editing dataset is limited to 70k pairs, and the 7B scale constrains capability—leaving open whether autoregressive 3D generation can close the gap with flow/diffusion models at scale, and whether the unified paradigm's optimization trade-offs persist as model size grows.
