## Problem

Existing large-scale dataset distillation methods rely on matching global distributional statistics (mean, variance) or cosine-similarity-based diversity guidance, which overlooks instance-level characteristics and intra-class variations, causing local mode collapse and distributional mismatch in the distilled set. The paper addresses this by reformulating dataset distillation as an Optimal Transport (Wasserstein distance) minimization problem, enabling fine-grained distributional geometry alignment at both global and instance levels throughout the entire pipeline.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254048/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a full real dataset (e.g., ImageNet-1K, 1.28M images at 224×224), a pretrained latent DiT diffusion model, a VAE encoder/decoder from Stable Diffusion, and an IPC-dependent ensemble of pretrained teacher models (ResNet-18, MobileNet, EfficientNet, ShuffleNet). It produces a compact distilled dataset of IPC=10 or 50 synthetic images per class with region-level soft labels (averaged teacher outputs), which is then used to train new student models from scratch. The distilled images are generated in latent space via guided diffusion and decoded to pixel space, while the number of contributing teachers scales with IPC to match label complexity to image distribution capacity.

## Method

The paper decomposes the total Wasserstein distance W(μ_true, ν_new) via the triangle inequality into a dataset discrepancy term (modulated by a multiplicative soft-label contraction factor α) and a logit matching error term, then minimizes each through three components. First, OT-guided diffusion sampling computes the entropy-regularized Sinkhorn distance between accumulated synthetic latents and a random batch of real image latents at every diffusion step, using the resulting gradient to steer the reverse sampling trajectory alongside inherited trajectory and diversity guidance. Second, Label-Image-Aligned Soft Relabeling selects an IPC-dependent subset of teacher models so that the soft label distribution's complexity matches the distilled image distribution's representational capacity, directly reducing the contraction factor α. Third, OT-based logit matching minimizes a batch-wise Sinkhorn distance (with ℓ₁-norm cost) between student model outputs and soft labels, capturing inter-sample distributional structure that per-sample divergences miss. All OT computations use iterative Sinkhorn normalization (20 iterations), and the additional runtime overhead is consistently under 1%.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K with ResNet-18, the method achieves 52.9% top-1 accuracy at IPC=10 (300 epochs) and 58.6% at IPC=10 (1000 epochs), outperforming the strongest generative baseline DiT-IGD (45.5%) by 7.4 points and the strongest model-inversion baseline EDC (48.6%) by 4.3 points. At IPC=50, it reaches 61.9% (300 epochs) and 64.2% (1000 epochs), surpassing DiT-IGD (59.8%) and EDC (58.0%). Across all six architectures (ResNet, MobileNet-V2, EfficientNet-B0, Swin Transformer, ConvNet, ConvNeXt), the method achieves at least a 4% accuracy improvement over the best prior method at IPC=10 on ImageNet-1K, with gains up to 10.1% on ConvNeXt (61.2% vs. 51.1% for EDC).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all 17 baselines across every dataset, architecture, and IPC setting evaluated. Among generative-model-based methods, DiT-IGD (ICLR 2025) is the strongest competitor, achieving 45.5% at IPC=10 on ImageNet-1K/ResNet-18; the proposed OT framework surpasses it by 7.4 points (52.9%) at 300 epochs and by 13.1 points (58.6%) at 1000 epochs. Among model-inversion-based methods, EDC (NeurIPS 2024) is the closest baseline at 48.6% (IPC=10) and 58.0% (IPC=50), still trailing the proposed method by 4.3 and 3.9 points respectively at 300 epochs. On ImageNette (10 classes), the method reaches 79.0% (ResNet-18, IPC=10, soft labels) versus 74.8% for DiT-IGD* and 61.4% for RDED. On ImageNet-100, it achieves 47.7% at IPC=10 (ResNet-18), exceeding RDED (36.0%) by 11.7 points and DELT (28.2%) by 19.5 points. On CIFAR-100 with ConvNet-3, the method attains 58.7% at IPC=100, outperforming all traditional and large-scale baselines. The consistent margin across architectures (MobileNet-V2, EfficientNet-B0, Swin Transformer, ConvNeXt) confirms that the OT-based distributional alignment generalizes beyond the condensation model used during distillation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the generative-model-based dataset distillation paradigm initiated by IGD (Chen et al., ICLR 2025), which introduced influence-guided diffusion sampling to generate training-optimal synthetic data. The key departure is a principled reformulation: rather than matching global gradient statistics or using cosine-similarity-based diversity, the authors recast the entire distillation pipeline as a Wasserstein distance minimization problem, decomposed into three tractable sub-objectives (OT-guided latent sampling, IPC-aware soft relabeling, and batch-wise OT logit matching). The Sinkhorn algorithm (Cuturi, 2013) provides the computational backbone for both the sampling and matching stages, while the student training protocol follows EDC (Shao et al., NeurIPS 2024). Conceptually, the paper bridges optimal transport theory and diffusion-based generation in a way that prior dataset distillation work had not systematically pursued.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The OT-based decomposition offers a theoretically grounded and practically efficient framework for large-scale dataset distillation, with the contraction factor α providing an interpretable measure of how well soft labels bridge the image-label distributional gap. The consistent 4%+ gains at IPC=10 on ImageNet-1K across six architectures, with less than 1% runtime overhead, make this a practically attractive option for reducing training costs in federated, continual, or resource-constrained settings. Open questions include whether the OT formulation extends naturally to other modalities (video, audio, 3D), how it behaves under extreme compression (IPC=1 or 2), and whether the class-wise OT decomposition in Eq. 7 remains valid under long-tailed or open-set label distributions.
