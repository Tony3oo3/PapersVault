## Problem

Existing heterogeneous graph datasets are either small-scale and domain-specific (e.g., IMDB with 21K nodes, ACM with 11K nodes) or large but severely limited in entity and relation diversity (e.g., MAG240M has only 3 node/edge types). This gap prevents comprehensive representation learning across the full breadth of real-world knowledge and makes it impossible to benchmark methods on truly universal, multi-domain heterogeneous graphs. Additionally, existing aggregation-based and meta-path-based GNNs suffer from propagation degradation and exponential meta-path explosion when faced with thousands of relation types.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a heterogeneous graph with 77.31M nodes (each represented by a 128-dim CLIP text embedding derived from reorganized entity descriptions in the format '<Label> be <Description>. <Relation1>. <Relation2>...') and 564M directed edges typed across 2,082 relation types, stored as a 37.52GB dense feature tensor. The primary output is a 256-dim learned node embedding used for multi-label classification over 2,000 consolidated cluster labels (downsampled from 74,666 original Wikidata annotations via CLIP-based clustering and GPT-4-assisted mapping). A secondary output is the pre-trained node embedding itself, which serves as a universal knowledge signal injected into downstream recommendation and link-prediction models via embedding retrieval.

## Method

HGD (Heterogeneous Graph Decoupling) decouples the expensive graph convolution into two stages: a train-free Anisotropic Feature Propagation (AFP) module and a lightweight graph-free feature mapping. AFP computes K-hop anisotropic propagation features on CPU by applying a Dimension Adaptive Tensor Product between a relation-aware adjacency tensor (N×N×d) and an expanded feature tensor, followed by row-wise softmax and L2-norm along the edge dimension—no trainable parameters are involved, and the process is executed once before training. The graph-free mapping then adaptively aggregates the K multi-hop representations using diagonal attention matrices (one per hop) plus a residual skip connection, trained end-to-end with binary cross-entropy loss. This decoupling reduces time complexity from O(K|E| + KNd²) to O(KNd²) and space from O(|E| + KNd) to O(KNd), enabling 22.1× speedup over HGT while achieving 96.09% F1 on the full 77M-node graph. The paper also provides a convergence proof showing the AFP iteration contracts to a unique fixed point under a spectral radius condition on the adjacency tensor.

## Results

On the full UniHG dataset (77.3M nodes, 564M edges, 2,082 relation types), HGD achieves 93.16% Subset Accuracy, 93.83% Recall, and 96.09% F1-score for multi-label node classification, outperforming the strongest baseline SIGN (69.04% Acc, 81.48% F1) by 24.12% in accuracy. On the 1M-node subset, HGD reaches 75.41% accuracy vs. SIGN's 56.73%, and on the 10M-node subset, 89.03% vs. 73.58%. HGD also runs 22.1× faster than HGT (182 min vs. 4,033 min per epoch on UniHG-full) while using only 1.51M parameters versus HGT's 411M.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HGD decisively outperforms all seven baselines across all three UniHG scales. Among convolution-based methods, HGT (411M params) is the strongest at 55.36% accuracy on UniHG-full but is 22.1× slower than HGD; GCN and GAT perform poorly (26.72% and 31.02% accuracy) due to feature distribution biases on heterogeneous subgraphs. Among decoupling-based methods, SIGN is the closest competitor (69.04% Acc, 81.48% F1 on full), yet HGD still leads by 24.12% accuracy and 14.61% F1. MTMP (ICML 2024) achieves 65.67% accuracy on full, trailing HGD by 27.49%. In the knowledge transfer experiments (Table 4), injecting UniHG pre-trained embeddings into recommendation models (LightGCN, NGCF, CSCF, PSCF, JSCF) yields 1–28% relative improvements in Precision/Recall/NDCG@20, with UniHG achieving higher average NDCG gains (11.48% on Amazon-book, 11.71% on Yelp2018) than the domain-specific KGAT baseline (10.05% and 5.54% respectively). For link prediction under domain transfer (Table 11), UniHG-augmented methods consistently improve over their baselines (e.g., BUDDY: 84.50→85.39 on Cora, 69.56→72.65 on PubMed), while ELPH suffers OOM on ogbl-collab regardless of UniHG augmentation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the heterogeneous graph representation learning literature by introducing a universal (cross-domain) graph constructed from Wikidata, departing from the domain-specific datasets (ACM, IMDB, DBLP, MAG240M) that have dominated prior work. Methodologically, it builds on graph decoupling (SGC, SIGN, GAMLP) by replacing isotropic propagation with an anisotropic tensor-product operation (from Gerstner & Griebel's dimension-adaptive quadrature) that natively handles 2,082 relation types without meta-path enumeration, and on adaptive graph diffusion (Sun et al., 2020) for the hop-wise attention aggregation. The key conceptual departure from aggregation-based methods (HGT, GAT) is that all multi-hop structural information is pre-computed train-free on CPU, reducing the GPU workload to a simple MLP over pre-aggregated features.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

UniHG is the first encyclopedic, cross-domain heterogeneous graph benchmark (77M nodes, 564M edges, 2,082 relation types), filling a gap where prior datasets were either small-scale or confined to a single domain. The practical finding that meta-path-based methods require ~75 TB of memory on UniHG versus ~270 GB for HGD establishes a clear feasibility boundary for the field. However, the dataset relies solely on Wikidata, the cross-domain transfer gains (11.48% NDCG) are moderate and compared against a single domain-specific baseline (KGAT), and the universal knowledge transfer mechanism (retrieving pre-trained embeddings as additional self-supervised signals) remains under-theorized. Open questions include whether the anisotropic propagation advantage persists as relation types grow further, and whether the 2,000-cluster label consolidation preserves sufficient discriminative signal for fine-grained downstream tasks.
