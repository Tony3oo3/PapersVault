## Problem

LLM benchmarks are the primary tool for assessing model capabilities, yet there is no systematic evidence that they actually measure the abstract phenomena they claim to measure (e.g., 'reasoning', 'safety', 'alignment'). The paper identifies that weak links between operationalised tasks and target phenomena lead to poorly supported scientific claims, misdirected research, and ungrounded policy implications, but no prior work has quantified how prevalent these construct validity failures are across the benchmarking literature.

## Input / Output

The review draws on 46,114 articles from ICML, ICLR, NeurIPS (2018–2024) and ACL, NAACL, EMNLP (2020–2024), narrowed by keyword screening to 2,189 candidates and then to 445 included benchmarks. Each included article is annotated against a 21-item codebook covering four dimensions (phenomenon definition, task design, scoring metric, and validity claims), producing a structured dataset of categorical and free-text responses. The final outputs are a coded annotation dataset, an eight-point construct validity checklist with actionable sub-items, and a worked example applying the checklist to GSM8K.

## Method

The paper conducts a systematic review in which 29 domain experts code 445 benchmark articles using a deductively derived codebook grounded in psychometric construct validity theory (face, content, ecological, predictive, convergent, and discriminant validity). Initial screening of 46,114 articles is performed by GPT-4o mini against three inclusion criteria (validated at 84% F1 on a 50-article human-labelled sample), followed by manual filtering with a fourth criterion (novelty of the benchmark). Each article is coded by a primary reviewer, with a second reviewer mapping responses to a simplified option set; inter-rater agreement is quantified via Brennan-Prediger Kappa (.524 mean across 30 categorical questions on a 46-paper double-coded sample). Recommendations are then synthesised through inductive open coding by the first author over a 50-article subset, followed by iterative collaborative refinement across five multi-author meetings, yielding eight prioritised recommendations each with a concrete checklist.

## Results

Across 445 reviewed LLM benchmarks, 78.2% provided a definition of their target phenomenon, but 47.8% of those definitions addressed contested or poorly defined concepts. Only 16.0% of benchmarks used uncertainty estimates or statistical tests to compare model results, and 53.4% presented any evidence for the construct validity of their benchmark. Task construction was dominated by handcrafted items (43.3%) and reuse of existing benchmarks (42.6%), with 27.0% incorporating convenience sampling. Exact matching was the most common scoring metric (used at least partially by 81.3%), while LLM-as-a-judge appeared in only 17.1% of benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a systematic review rather than a method comparison, so there are no baseline methods to benchmark against. Instead, the paper identifies gaps between current practice and recommended best practices across 445 benchmarks. Key contrasts include: only 17.1% used random sampling versus 27.0% relying on convenience sampling; 55.2% used at least partially targeted sampling; and less than 10% employed complete real-world tasks. For construct validity justification, 35.2% compared to other benchmarks of similar phenomena, 32.4% included a human baseline, and 31.2% tested in a more realistic setting. The inter-rater reliability across 29 expert reviewers yielded a mean Brennan-Prediger Kappa of .524 over 30 categorical questions, and the GPT-4o-mini pre-screening achieved an F1 of 84% against human labels on a 50-article sample.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper applies the psychometric construct validity framework (Cronbach & Meehl, 1955) to LLM benchmarking, building on Raji et al.'s (2021) decomposition of benchmarks into phenomenon–task–metric triples. While Bowman & Dahl (2021) and Reuel et al. (2024) have raised concerns about benchmark quality and aggregated best practices, no prior work has conducted a large-scale systematic review specifically through the lens of construct validity. The paper departs from these earlier efforts by operationalising validity subtypes (face, content, ecological, convergent, discriminant) into a 21-item codebook and applying it across 445 benchmarks with 29 expert annotators, yielding quantitative prevalence estimates for each validity failure mode.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that nearly all 445 reviewed benchmarks have at least one construct validity weakness, and that only 16% employ any statistical testing, quantifies a gap that the community has discussed anecdotally but never measured at scale. The eight recommendations and operational checklist provide a concrete, actionable framework that benchmark designers can adopt during the design phase, potentially shifting norms in how LLM evaluations are constructed and reported. Open questions include whether the checklist generalises to non-English or non-text modalities, how to enforce adoption without a centralised review body, and whether the GPT-4o-mini screening step (84% F1) introduced systematic biases that skew the prevalence estimates. The paper's focus on peer-reviewed conference proceedings also means industry-released benchmarks remain unassessed.
