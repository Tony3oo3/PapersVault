## Problem

Existing PEFT-based continual learning methods (e.g., InfLoRA, SD-LoRA) constrain parameter updates to a single fixed low-rank subspace, which inherently limits the model's learning capacity and leads to inferior performance. While this rigid constraint reduces task interference, it prevents the model from adapting its optimization directions as the gradient landscape evolves during training. The paper asks whether one can retain the memory efficiency of low-rank optimization while allowing the model to learn in a richer, dynamically evolving set of subspaces.

## Input / Output

The method takes 224×224 RGB images as input and produces class labels in a class-incremental setting where task identities are unavailable at inference time and no historical data is accessible. The backbone is a pre-trained ViT-B/16 (ImageNet-1K or DINO self-supervised), and CoSO only optimizes the output projection layers within multi-head attention modules rather than the full QKV transformations. Training uses minimal augmentation (random resized crop and horizontal flip) with a cross-entropy loss at temperature 3.

## Method

CoSO replaces the fixed low-rank subspace of LoRA-based methods with a sequence of dynamically determined subspaces derived from SVD of the gradients at each step. To prevent catastrophic forgetting, each gradient is first projected onto the orthogonal complement of a historical task subspace (maintained as an orthogonal basis matrix), ensuring new updates cannot interfere with previously learned directions. The projected gradient is then decomposed via truncated SVD to obtain a projection matrix, the gradient is projected into a compact low-rank space where Adam is applied, and the result is projected back for the parameter update. To consolidate task knowledge, CoSO uses Frequent Directions to incrementally maintain a compact sketch matrix across all training steps, capturing the dominant gradient covariance of the current task. Upon task completion, the principal directions of this sketch are extracted and appended to the historical subspace, progressively expanding the orthogonal constraint for subsequent tasks.

## Results

On ImageNet-R, CoSO achieves 82.10% final accuracy and 86.38% average accuracy on 5 tasks, 81.10% and 85.56% on 10 tasks, and 78.19% and 83.69% on 20 tasks, consistently outperforming all baselines. On CIFAR100 (10 tasks), CoSO reaches 88.77% final accuracy and 92.99% average accuracy. On DomainNet (5 tasks), CoSO attains 74.27% final accuracy and 80.05% average accuracy, surpassing the best baseline by 1.75% and 1.37% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoSO outperforms all six baselines across every dataset and task setting. Against the strongest baseline VPT-NSP², CoSO gains +2.38% final accuracy on ImageNet-R 5 tasks, +3.23% on 10 tasks, and +2.77% on 20 tasks, with corresponding average accuracy improvements of +2.05%, +2.47%, and +2.37%. The LoRA-based baselines InfLoRA and SD-LoRA are the closest competitors, with CoSO leading by 4.57–7.89% (InfLoRA) and 2.93–3.76% (SD-LoRA) in final accuracy on ImageNet-R. Prompt-based methods (L2P, DualPrompt, CODA-P) trail substantially, with CoSO exceeding CODA-P by 8.45–11.03% and L2P by 7.07–19.55% in final accuracy. On CIFAR100, CoSO leads VPT-NSP² by 0.68% final accuracy; on DomainNet, it leads SD-LoRA by 1.07% final accuracy and 1.02% average accuracy. The advantage grows with task sequence length, confirming CoSO's superior resistance to catastrophic forgetting in long-stream settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoSO adapts the dynamic gradient-subspace optimization loop from GaLore (project → Adam → project back) to the continual learning setting, where prior PEFT-based methods like InfLoRA and SD-LoRA confine updates to a single fixed low-rank subspace. The key departure is enforcing orthogonality between the current task's optimization subspace and a cumulative historical task subspace, drawing on the gradient-projection-memory idea from OGD/GPM. For consolidating task-specific gradient directions into the historical subspace, CoSO repurposes the Frequent Directions matrix sketching algorithm from streaming linear algebra, which had not previously been applied to continual learning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method offers a practical, replay-free alternative to LoRA-based continual learning that avoids the capacity bottleneck of a fixed subspace while remaining memory-efficient (comparable GFLOPs and memory to InfLoRA). The consistent 2–3% gains over strong baselines across three datasets and up to 20 tasks suggest the dynamic-subspace + orthogonality combination is a robust recipe for PEFT-based continual learning. Limitations include evaluation only on ViT-B/16 backbones, class-incremental image classification, and a single threshold hyperparameter (ε_th = 0.98) that may not transfer to other architectures or task structures. The authors identify extension to multimodal task settings as a natural next step.
