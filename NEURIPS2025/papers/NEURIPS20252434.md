## Problem

Diffusion models have surpassed GANs as the leading image generators, yet no existing dataset distillation method can perform end-to-end gradient-based optimization through a pre-trained diffusion model. The root cause is vanishing gradients: backpropagating through the long T-step denoising chain causes gradient norms to decay nearly tenfold as T grows from 10 to 90, making latent optimization infeasible. Prior diffusion-based distillation attempts therefore resort to sampling or selecting fixed representations, forfeiting the fine-grained gradient-matching that underpins privacy, robustness, and cross-architecture generalization benefits.

## Input / Output

The method takes a real image classification dataset (e.g., 10-class ImageNet subsets or CIFAR-10) with class labels and a frozen pre-trained latent diffusion model (LDM) with its 2× compression autoencoder. It initializes learnable latent codes Z by encoding a small random subset of real images through the LDM's encoder, and conditioning codes c via the pre-trained class embedding network. The output is a compact synthetic image set S = D(z₀) with M = C × IPC images at 128×128 or 256×256 resolution, optimized to minimize a standard distillation loss (DC, DM, or MTT) against the original dataset.

## Method

LD3M's core innovation is a modified reverse diffusion step that injects a linearly decaying residual connection from the initial noisy state z_T into every denoising step: the predicted mean μ_θ is replaced by a weighted average (1 − t/T)·μ_θ + (t/T)·z_T, so the weight of z_T decreases linearly from 1 at t=T to 0 at t=0. This creates a direct gradient pathway from the distillation loss back to z_T and hence to the learnable Z, bypassing the long product of Jacobians that causes vanishing gradients in standard sampling. Both the latent codes Z and conditioning codes c are optimized end-to-end through the frozen LDM using gradient checkpointing for memory efficiency, with no fine-tuning of diffusion weights. The modification intentionally sacrifices exact distribution fidelity of the sampler in favor of tractable gradient flow, which is well-suited to distillation where downstream utility (feature/gradient matching) matters more than photorealism. The approach is compatible with any standard distillation objective and any LDM architecture, and requires only simple autoencoder-based initialization rather than costly GAN inversion.

## Results

On 10 ImageNet subsets at 128×128 with 1 IPC, LD3M achieves the best overall cross-architecture average accuracy in 9/10 subsets, reaching 30.9% (DC), 30.4% (MTT), and 25.9% (DM) compared to GLaD's 29.8%, 29.0%, and 22.4% respectively. At 10 IPC, LD3M pushes average accuracy above 47% (DC: 47.1%, DM: 47.3%), surpassing GLaD by +2.5% and +3.5% respectively. At 256×256 resolution (DC, 1 IPC), LD3M reaches 36.3–36.5% average across different generator initializations, outperforming GLaD (34.0–34.5%) by roughly +2 percentage points and pixel-space distillation (29.5%) by +7 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary baseline GLaD (StyleGAN-XL prior), LD3M wins across all distillation algorithms and resolutions: at 128×128 IPC=1, LD3M improves DC by +1.1 pp (30.9% vs 29.8%), MTT by +1.4 pp (30.4% vs 29.0%), and DM by +3.5 pp (25.9% vs 22.4%); at 256×256 the gap widens to ~+2 pp on average. Compared to pixel-space distillation (DC, DM, MTT), LD3M gains +3.0 pp (DC), +6.7 pp (DM), and +5.0 pp (MTT) at 128×128 IPC=1, with the largest relative improvement for DM (+16.3% over pixel-space DM). On CIFAR-10 at IPC=50, LD3M surpasses both the sampling-based D4M (which delivered ~10% accuracy for all tested models) and the decoupled SRe2L method. At IPC=10 (128×128), LD3M extends its lead over GLaD to +2.5 pp (DC: 47.1% vs 45.9%) and +3.5 pp (DM: 47.3% vs 45.8%), confirming consistent gains at higher sample budgets. Ablation confirms that the diffusion process itself (vs. autoencoder-only) contributes +1.2 pp, and the proposed residual gradient enhancement is the critical component enabling end-to-end optimization through the denoising chain.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the generative-prior dataset distillation line initiated by GLaD (Cazenavette et al., CVPR 2023), which optimized latent codes through a pre-trained StyleGAN-XL. It departs from prior diffusion-based distillation attempts (D4M, Minimax Diffusion) that circumvented the vanishing-gradient barrier by sampling or selecting fixed representations, and from Duan et al. who used only the LDM autoencoder without the denoising chain. The core methodological contribution is a linearly decaying residual connection injected from the initial noisy state into every reverse diffusion step, drawing on the ResNet/LSTM intuition for gradient flow but specifically structured for the multi-step denoising chain in the context of distillation optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By enabling true end-to-end gradient-based optimization through a pre-trained diffusion model, LD3M opens the door to leveraging the current state-of-the-art generative architecture for dataset distillation, a task previously locked to GANs or autoencoders. The practical benefit is significant: simpler initialization (no GAN inversion), faster distillation, and up to 4.8-point accuracy gains over GLaD. Key open questions include whether the linear decay schedule generalizes to very large T or to fast samplers like DPM-Solver, whether inter-class-aware distillation objectives can further exploit the expressive latent trajectories, and whether the approach scales to full ImageNet-1K. The paper also acknowledges that the modified sampling process sacrifices distributional fidelity, which may limit applicability to tasks requiring photorealistic outputs.
