## Problem

Existing text-to-image benchmarks emphasize instruction following, compositionality, and aesthetics, but largely overlook the reasoning capability of generation models. The paper introduces knowledge image generation—a task where a model must autonomously infer pertinent concepts and relationships from a vague prompt and render them as a coherent explanatory visual (diagram, chart, infographic)—and provides the first large-scale benchmark and structured metric to evaluate this reasoning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253193/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a concise, question-like text prompt (e.g., 'Illustrate the structure of a neuron and the process of action potential propagation') rather than a descriptive scene instruction, and must produce a knowledge image at 1024×1024 resolution that faithfully visualizes structured domain knowledge. Evaluation is grounded in a knowledge graph (entities + typed relationships) extracted from both the prompt and the generated image, making the output assessment format-agnostic across charts, diagrams, mind maps, and infographics spanning 10 disciplines and 6 educational tiers.

## Method

The paper contributes three components: (1) the MMMG benchmark of 4,456 expert-validated prompt-image pairs, each annotated with a knowledge graph using domain-agnostic predicates (Defines, Entails, Causes, Contains, Requires, TemporalOrder); (2) MMMG-Score, a multiplicative metric combining knowledge fidelity (1 − normalized graph edit distance between the grounded subgraph extracted by OpenAI-o3 and the reference graph) and visual readability (a piecewise-linear score over the number of coherent segments from SAM-2.1 + PaddleOCR, with thresholds n_min=70, n_max=160); and (3) FLUX-Reason, a cascaded baseline where a reasoning LLM (DeepSeek-R1 or OpenAI-o3) generates chain-of-thought traces conditioned on the knowledge graph, which are then fed through an extended T5 encoder (2048-token context) into FLUX.1-[dev] fine-tuned with LoRA over 10K steps on 16,000 curated knowledge-image pairs.

## Results

On the MMMG benchmark (4,456 prompt-image pairs across 10 disciplines and 6 educational tiers), GPT-4o achieves the highest MMMG-Score of 50.20, while the best open-source model, HiDream-I1-Full, reaches only 28.04. The proposed FLUX-Reason (R1) baseline scores 34.45, outperforming all other open-source and diffusion-based models but trailing GPT-4o by ~16 points. Performance degrades sharply with educational level: most models drop from 20–30 at pre-school to below 10 at PhD level, exposing severe limitations in abstract reasoning and compositional visual planning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FLUX-Reason (R1) at 34.45 outperforms all 19 baseline T2I models except GPT-4o (50.20) and Gemini Nano Banana (45.73), establishing it as the strongest open-source baseline. It surpasses the next-best open-source model HiDream-I1-Full (28.04) by 6.4 points and FLUX.1-[pro] (27.14) by 7.3 points, and beats Gemini 2.0 Flash (29.31) by 5.1 points. Among autoregressive models, FLUX-Reason (R1) leads by a wide margin over Infinity (19.18), SimpleAR (9.81), and LlamaGen (3.02). A key ablation shows that structured reasoning traces (FLUX-Reason R1: 34.45) substantially outperform simple recaptioning with OpenAI-o3 (FLUX.1-[dev] recaption: 18.10), confirming that explicit chain-of-thought planning—not merely longer prompts—is critical for knowledge-grounded generation. The gap between GPT-4o and all other models (14.5+ points) underscores that even the best current systems struggle with the multi-entity, multi-relation reasoning demanded by MMMG.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by defining 'knowledge image generation' as a distinct task, departing from prior T2I benchmarks (GenEval, T2I-CompBench++, DPG-Bench, Commonsense-T2I, WISE) that focus on compositionality, prompt adherence, or implicit world knowledge in photorealistic scenes. The closest prior work, WISE, evaluates world knowledge but through LLM-judged photorealism rather than explicit structured visualization; MMMG instead requires models to autonomously infer entities and relations from a vague prompt and render them as diagrams, charts, or infographics. Methodologically, the evaluation draws on knowledge graph extraction (via OpenAI-o3), graph edit distance, and segmentation-based readability scoring (SAM-2), while the FLUX-Reason baseline repurposes the cascaded LLM+diffusion paradigm from recent reasoning-enhanced generation work (T2I-R1, MINT, ImageGen-CoT) but grounds it in structured KG supervision rather than free-form captions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark exposes a fundamental gap: even GPT-4o scores only 50.20/100, and the best open-source model (HiDream-I1-Full) reaches just 28.04, revealing that current T2I systems lack the structured multimodal reasoning needed for knowledge visualization. The finding that FID correlates negatively (r=-0.774) with human judgment for this task challenges the field's reliance on perceptual metrics for reasoning-oriented evaluation. Open questions remain about reliable KG grounding in generated images (OpenAI-o3 still struggles with dozens of entities), scalable collection of high-quality knowledge images from fragmented textbook sources, and closing the performance gap to proprietary systems through multimodal pretraining and architectural improvements.
