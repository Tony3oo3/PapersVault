## Problem

The dominant two-stage paradigm in computational pathology (pre-trained encoder + MIL aggregator) suffers from disjoint optimization, where the encoder is never adapted to downstream tasks. While slide-level supervised end-to-end (E2E) learning is the natural remedy, it has been underexplored due to prohibitive costs and suboptimal results. The paper identifies the overlooked root cause: sparse-attention MIL disrupts E2E optimization by causing the encoder to overfit on limited discriminative regions while being vulnerable to redundant ones, creating a deteriorating feedback loop that prior E2E work failed to address.

## Input / Output

The method consumes gigapixel whole slide images (WSIs) by first cropping them into non-overlapping 256×256 patches at multiple magnifications (5×, 10×, 20×, and 40× depending on the dataset), then applying multi-scale random sampling to select a fixed-size subset (e.g., 512 patches) that is resized to a unified resolution before encoding. Outputs are slide-level predictions: ISUP cancer grades (0–5) for PANDA, binary or multi-class sub-type labels (e.g., LUAD vs. LUSC, IDC vs. ILC) for TCGA datasets, and survival time estimates evaluated by C-index for prognosis tasks. All training uses only slide-level ground truth labels, with no patch-level annotations required.

## Method

The core contribution is ABMILX, a sparse-attention MIL designed specifically to stabilize E2E optimization. It introduces (1) Multi-head Local Attention (MHLA), which splits instance features into m subspaces and computes independent sparse attention per head via a shared MLP, so that no single head can excessively focus on redundant patches while collectively covering discriminative regions; and (2) a Global Attention Plus (A+) module that builds a patch similarity matrix and propagates attention from high-attention discriminative instances to their morphologically similar neighbors, refining local attention with a learnable scaling factor α that adaptively balances sparsity. A theoretical analysis formalizes 'optimization risk' as the maximum attention assigned to noisy instances and proves that both MHLA and A+ reduce this risk. The full pipeline pairs ABMILX with multi-scale random instance sampling (MRIS) to capture multi-granularity context at low cost, and is trained end-to-end with a ResNet encoder using only slide-level supervision, achieving foundation-model-level accuracy in under 10 RTX 3090 GPU hours.

## Results

On PANDA (ISUP grading, top-1 accuracy), ABMILX with ResNet-50 E2E training achieves 78.83%, surpassing the best foundation model (UNI + 2DMamba at 76.37%) by +2.46% and the best two-stage R50 method (WIKG at 62.72%) by +16.11%. On TCGA-BRCA sub-typing (AUC), ABMILX-R50 reaches 95.17%, exceeding the best FM (GIGAP + RRTMIL at 94.82%) and the best R50 two-stage (RRTMIL at 89.35%). On survival analysis (C-index), ABMILX-R18 achieves 67.78 on BRCA, outperforming all two-stage baselines. Training cost is under 10 RTX3090 GPU hours (9h for R18), with inference at 1.7s/slide versus 83s for GIGAP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ABMILX with E2E training decisively outperforms all two-stage ResNet-50 baselines: on PANDA it gains +16.1% over WIKG (78.83 vs 62.72), +13.9% over RRTMIL (78.83 vs 61.97), and +17.3% over TransMIL (78.83 vs 56.42). Against the three SOTA pathology foundation models, ABMILX-R50 surpasses UNI+2DMamba on PANDA (78.83 vs 76.37, +2.46%) and GIGAP+2DMamba (78.83 vs 75.72, +3.11%), while matching or slightly trailing UNI+RRTMIL on NSCLC sub-typing (97.06 vs 97.88). On BRCA sub-typing, ABMILX-R50 (95.17 AUC) exceeds the best FM (GIGAP+RRTMIL at 94.82) and the best R50 two-stage (RRTMIL at 89.35) by +0.35% and +5.82% respectively. Among prior E2E methods, ABMILX dramatically outperforms C2C (78.34 vs 62.91 on PANDA, +15.4%) and FT (78.34 vs 66.06, +12.3%) while training 5–9× faster (9h vs 84h/45h). On external CPTAC validation, ABMILX-R50 achieves 85.19 AUC on CPTAC-NSCLC, matching UNI+TransMIL (85.24) and exceeding UNI+ABMIL (83.73), while on CPTAC-LUAD it reaches 54.00 C-index vs 53.59 for UNI+ABMIL.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends ABMIL (Ilse et al., ICML 2018) into ABMILX by adding multi-head local attention and a global correlation-based attention refinement module, specifically to address optimization challenges in end-to-end training. It departs from the dominant two-stage paradigm (offline feature extraction followed by MIL aggregation) and from prior E2E CPath methods (C2C, FT) that focused on complex sampling strategies, instead identifying that the MIL architecture itself is the bottleneck for E2E optimization. The work also positions against pathology foundation models (UNI, CHIEF, GigaPath) that achieve strong two-stage performance through massive pre-training, arguing that their disjoint optimization with MIL limits downstream task adaptation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a small E2E-trained ResNet (26M params, ImageNet pre-trained) with ABMILX can surpass foundation models requiring 100K+ slides and 307M–1134M parameters, at a fraction of the computational cost (<10 GPU hours vs. thousands). This challenges the prevailing assumption in CPath that massive-scale pre-training is necessary for competitive performance and opens the door to task-specific, data-efficient training pipelines. Limitations include reliance on patch sampling (not truly processing all patches), somewhat inconsistent survival analysis results across model sizes, and the method's generalization to ViT-based encoders or larger backbones remaining untested. Open questions include whether E2E learning can further benefit from increased model capacity and whether the identified optimization dynamics generalize beyond the ResNet family.
