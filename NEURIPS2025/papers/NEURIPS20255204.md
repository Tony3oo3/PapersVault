## Problem

Traditional retrieval-augmented generation relies on a single static retrieval step before inference, which fails for complex tasks where information needs are ambiguous, multi-hop, or evolve during reasoning. Existing search-enhanced reasoning methods (e.g., Search-R1) address this by enabling iterative retrieval but optimize only on final-answer correctness, leaving the quality of intermediate retrieval steps unsupervised and making training sparse and sample-inefficient.

## Input / Output

The model takes a natural-language query and an external knowledge corpus (Wikipedia dumps for standard QA; cached Google Search results and scraped web pages for real-time tasks) as input. During generation, the model emits structured blocks—<think> for reasoning, <search> for subqueries, <info> for retrieved passages, and <answer> for the final response—interleaving up to 6 retrieval steps. The output is thus a full reasoning trajectory with embedded retrieval actions, not just a final answer string.

## Method

InForage frames search-enhanced reasoning as an information foraging process and trains a small LLM (Qwen-2.5-3B/7B) via a two-stage pipeline: supervised fine-tuning on GPT-4o-generated trajectories derived from human web-browsing annotations, followed by PPO reinforcement learning with a composite reward. The reward decomposes into three components: an Outcome Reward for final-answer correctness, an Information Gain Reward measuring cumulative coverage of retrieved patches against golden evidence URLs, and an Efficiency Penalty (exponential decay β^(T−2)) that discourages unnecessarily long trajectories. This design explicitly supervises intermediate retrieval quality rather than relying solely on sparse outcome signals. The training dataset comprises 20,000 multi-hop QA examples built from open-ended human browsing sessions, each recording the full sequence of search queries, selected web pages, and extracted bridge claims, with at least three non-redundant information hops per example.

## Results

InForage achieves an average Exact Match (EM) of 41.0 across eight QA benchmarks, outperforming the strongest baseline Search-R1-GRPO (35.6) by 5.4 points. Gains are most pronounced on multi-hop and self-constructed tasks: +6.4 EM on HotpotQA, +5.9 on 2WikiMultihopQA, and +7.4 on the Self dataset (44.1 vs. 36.7 for Search-o1). On standard open-domain QA (NQ, TriviaQA, PopQA), InForage leads by 1.2–4.7 EM over Search-R1-GRPO, confirming consistent improvements across task complexity levels.

## Baselines & Comparisons

InForage outperforms all seven baselines on every dataset. Against the closest competitor Search-R1-GRPO (35.6 avg), InForage leads by 5.4 EM on average, with the largest margins on HotpotQA (+6.4) and 2WikiMultihopQA (+5.9), indicating that the information-gain reward is particularly effective for multi-hop decomposition. Compared to Search-R1-PPO (31.8), the gap widens to 9.2 EM, suggesting that the structured three-component reward (outcome + information gain + efficiency) provides denser supervision than outcome-only RL. Against static RAG (Lewis et al., 26.5 avg), InForage gains 14.5 EM, confirming that dynamic, reasoning-integrated retrieval substantially outperforms one-shot retrieval. On the self-constructed real-time web QA set, InForage (44.1) surpasses Search-o1 (36.7) by 7.4 EM and RQRAG (28.2) by 15.9 EM, demonstrating superior handling of ambiguous, multi-layered information needs. IRCoT (15.9) and Self-RAG (19.8) underperform even the non-retrieval Reasoning baseline (19.9) on several datasets, highlighting the fragility of manually designed retrieval-reasoning interleaving without learned optimization.

## Relation to Prior Work

InForage extends the search-augmented reasoning line initiated by Search-R1 (Jin et al., 2025a) and Search-o1 (Li et al., 2025), which already moved retrieval from a static pre-inference step into the inference loop via RL. The key conceptual departure is borrowing Information Foraging Theory (Pirolli & Card, 1999) from cognitive science to motivate a multi-component reward structure—outcome correctness, intermediate information-gain coverage, and efficiency decay—whereas prior methods like Search-R1 rely primarily on final-answer correctness. The paper also departs from standard QA datasets by constructing a human-guided, iterative web-browsing trajectory dataset with golden evidence URLs, enabling dense supervision over intermediate retrieval steps that existing benchmarks lack.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The information-gain reward directly addresses the sparse-reward bottleneck in RL for multi-step reasoning, and the ablation confirms it is the most critical component. However, the framework is currently limited to short-form QA with verifiable answers (for rule-based reward assignment), and experiments are restricted to Qwen-2.5-3B/7B, leaving open whether the IFT-inspired reward design transfers to long-form synthesis, multi-tool agents, or larger model families. The self-constructed real-time web QA benchmark (500 samples) is a useful but small evaluation set, and the ~5-point average improvement over Search-R1-GRPO, while consistent, does not yet establish a paradigm shift—rather, it validates that richer intermediate supervision meaningfully helps search-augmented reasoning.
