## Problem

Existing robust GNN methods rely on the independent feature noise (IFN) assumption, which posits that noise in node features does not affect graph structure or node labels. In real-world applications, however, noisy features (e.g., fake profiles) can propagate to create spurious edges and misassigned labels, forming a chain of noise dependencies that no prior method explicitly models. This paper introduces the dependency-aware noise on graphs (DANG) scenario and a corresponding robust GNN that captures these causal dependencies.

## Input / Output

DA-GNN consumes a noisy graph with node features X ∈ R^{N×F}, an adjacency matrix A ∈ R^{N×N}, and partially observed node labels Y (semi-supervised setting with labeled set V^L). Beyond standard task outputs (predicted node labels for classification and edge probabilities for link prediction), the model explicitly infers three latent variables: a clean graph structure Z_A, clean node labels Z_Y, and noise-incurring variables ε (decomposed into ε_X for feature noise and ε_A for structure noise). The structure inference operates on a pre-computed proxy graph of O(|E_k^γ ∪ E|) edges rather than the full N² edge set, making it tractable for large graphs.

## Method

DA-GNN is a deep generative model trained via variational inference to maximize the ELBO of P(X, A, Y) under a causal graphical model where X ← (ε, Z_Y), A ← (Z_A, X, ε), and Y ← (Z_Y, X, A). Three inference encoders approximate the posteriors: a GCN with γ-hop subgraph similarity regularization infers Z_A, a small-loss-based estimator with EMA smoothing infers ε_A, and a GCN classifier with homophily regularization infers Z_Y. Three generative decoders model the likelihoods: an edge reconstruction loss with label smoothing and feature-similarity regularization for P(A|X,ε,Z_A), an MLP feature reconstruction for P(X|ε,Z_Y), and a GCN classifier for P(Y|X,A,Z_Y). The key distinction from prior robust GNNs is that DA-GNN does not assume any data source is noise-free; instead, it jointly infers all latent clean variables while explicitly modeling how feature noise causes structural noise and label noise through the causal edges in the DGP.

## Results

Under synthetic DANG noise, DA-GNN consistently achieves the highest node classification accuracy across all five datasets (Cora, Citeseer, Photo, Comp, Arxiv) at every noise level. At the most challenging DANG-50% setting, DA-GNN reaches 69.7% on Cora, 59.0% on Citeseer, 87.6% on Photo, 82.2% on Comp, and 44.0% on Arxiv, outperforming the best baseline by 4.2–5.6 percentage points. On the real-world DANG benchmarks (Auto and Garden), DA-GNN also leads on both node classification (79.3% / 88.7% accuracy) and link prediction (92.6 / 92.4 ROC-AUC), confirming its advantage under noise dependencies that are plausible in e-commerce systems.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DA-GNN outperforms all ten baselines under DANG noise at every level tested. The closest competitor is SG-GSR, which also models structure and feature noise jointly but assumes clean labels; DA-GNN beats it by 2.1–3.6 pp on Cora (DANG-30/50%) and by 3.6–4.4 pp on Comp (DANG-30/50%). Among structure-learning baselines, RSGNN and STABLE are competitive at moderate noise (e.g., RSGNN 74.3% vs DA-GNN 78.2% on Cora DANG-30%) but degrade sharply at 50% noise. Feature-noise-robust methods (AirGNN, ProGNN) and label-noise-robust methods (NRGNN, RTGNN) suffer the largest drops under DANG because they assume at least one data source is clean. Generative baselines WSGNN and GraphGNN, which also use variational inference but assume noise-free graphs, are particularly vulnerable: WSGNN drops to 55.9% on Cora DANG-50% (vs. DA-GNN's 69.7%), and GraphGLOW struggles on Cora and Citeseer while performing better on Photo and Comp. On the large Arxiv graph, most baselines run out of memory, leaving only AirGNN, EvenNet, and RTGNN as feasible comparisons, all of which DA-GNN surpasses by 5–6 pp at DANG-50%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the variational inference framework for instance-dependent label noise under structural causal models (Yao et al., NeurIPS 2021) to the graph domain, introducing additional latent variables (clean structure Z_A, clean labels Z_Y, noise variable ε) and causal edges that prior graph generative models (WSGNN, GraphGLOW) did not capture. It departs fundamentally from the entire noise-robust GNN literature (AirGNN, RSGNN, STABLE, NRGNN, RTGNN, SG-GSR), which all operate under the independent feature noise (IFN) assumption that at least one data source—features, structure, or labels—is noise-free. DA-GNN instead models a dependency-aware noise scenario (DANG) where feature noise causally propagates to both graph structure and node labels, a formulation absent from prior robust GNN work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: real-world graphs (social networks, e-commerce, web) routinely exhibit noise dependencies that the IFN assumption cannot capture, and existing robust GNNs degrade significantly under such conditions. The introduction of DANG benchmark datasets (Auto, Garden) built on Amazon review data provides the community with a more realistic evaluation protocol. However, the DANG model still excludes the reverse dependency A→X (structure noise causing feature noise), and the authors acknowledge it does not apply to domains with fixed graph topology (e.g., molecular graphs). Open questions include whether the variational inference approach scales to very large graphs and whether the DANG assumption can be further generalized to capture bidirectional noise dependencies.
