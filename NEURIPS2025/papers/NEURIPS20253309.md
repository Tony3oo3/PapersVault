## Problem

Existing plug-and-play modules for low-light vision (e.g., YOLA, FeatEnHancer) rely on the classical Lambertian model and spatial-domain channel ratios, which fail to account for localized light sources and non-uniform highlights prevalent in real-world low-light scenes. The nonlinear highlight residual disrupts clean illumination-reflectance separation in the spatial domain, and fixed-receptive-field convolutions limit global spectral perception.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253309/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

FRBNet accepts a standard low-light RGB image (resized to task-specific resolutions: 608×608 for ExDark detection, 1500×1000 for DarkFace with TOOD, 2048×1024 for ACDC-Night segmentation, 1330×800 for LIS instance segmentation) and produces a 3-channel illumination-invariant feature map that is concatenated with the original spatial features before being fed into a downstream detector or segmenter. The module adds only 9K parameters and 53.1 GFLOPs, operating as a frontend without modifying the backbone or loss function.

## Method

The paper extends the Lambertian image formation model with a spatially varying highlight term (inspired by Phong's additive decomposition) to capture localized light sources, then derives a Frequency-domain Channel Ratio (FCR) by applying the Fourier transform to log-differences between RGB channel pairs, showing that illumination and reflectance occupy separable frequency bands while the highlight residual becomes a phase-modulated component. A Learnable Frequency-domain Filter (LFF) is designed with two components: a zero-DC Gaussian window that suppresses low-frequency illumination offsets, and an improved radial basis filter combining K=10 learnable Gaussian RBFs (for spectral magnitude selectivity) with sinusoidal angular harmonics (for directional selectivity against oriented highlight residuals). The filtered spectral features are inverse-Fourier-transformed, concatenated across all three channel pairs, and fused with original spatial features via a lightweight convolution block, yielding a 9K-parameter plug-and-play module that requires no loss-function modification.

## Results

On ExDark, FRBNet achieves 74.9% mAP with YOLOv3 and 75.4% mAP with TOOD, surpassing the previous best (YOLA) by 2.2 and 0.2 mAP respectively. On DarkFace, it reaches 65.1% mAP with TOOD, a +1.9 mAP improvement over YOLA. For nighttime semantic segmentation on ACDC-Night, FRBNet attains 61.6% mIoU (+2.9 over YOLA), and for instance segmentation on LIS it achieves 30.2% mAP (+1.1 over FeatEnHancer). The module adds only 9K parameters and runs at 89.5 FPS, maintaining efficiency comparable to the baseline detector.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FRBNet outperforms all 23 compared baselines across every task and detector configuration. The closest competitor is YOLA [5] (NeurIPS 2024), which also uses channel-ratio-based illumination-invariant features but in the spatial domain; FRBNet exceeds it by +2.2 mAP on ExDark (YOLOv3), +1.9 mAP on DarkFace (TOOD), +2.9 mIoU on ACDC-Night, and +1.1 mAP on LIS. Among plug-and-play methods, FeatEnHancer [21] (ICCV 2023) is the second-closest baseline, trailing FRBNet by 3.7 mAP on ExDark and 5.6 mIoU on ACDC-Night, while also being significantly slower (33.1 vs. 89.5 FPS). Enhancement-based methods (SMG, NeRCo, LightDiff, DarkIR, ZeroDCE, KinD, etc.) consistently underperform FRBNet by 2–6 mAP on detection, confirming that pixel-level enhancement does not reliably translate to feature-level gains. Synthetic-data approaches (DAINet, WARLearn) and multi-task methods (MAET, IAT) also fall short, with DAINet reaching only 73.4 mAP on ExDark versus FRBNet's 74.9. On instance segmentation (LIS), FRBNet leads FeatEnHancer by 1.1 mAP and YOLA by 5.3 mAP, demonstrating that frequency-domain filtering is particularly effective for fine-grained mask prediction under low illumination.

## Relation to Prior Work

FRBNet extends the spatial-domain channel ratio paradigm introduced by YOLA (NeurIPS 2024) into the frequency domain, motivated by the observation that illumination and reflectance components occupy distinct frequency bands. The theoretical foundation builds on the classical Lambertian image formation model, extended here using the Phong model's additive decomposition to account for localized light sources (streetlights, headlights) that violate the pure diffuse reflection assumption. The frequency correlation coefficient used in the filter design follows the complex correlation derivation from Tong et al. (NeurIPS 2024). Unlike prior plug-and-play modules (DENet, FeatEnHancer, YOLA) that operate via spatial convolutions with fixed receptive fields, FRBNet performs global spectral filtering through learnable radial basis functions with angular modulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a theoretically grounded, lightweight (9K parameters) plug-and-play module that consistently improves detection and segmentation under low-light conditions without modifying loss functions, making it broadly applicable to existing architectures. The extension of the Lambertian model to capture non-uniform highlights is a practical step toward more realistic low-light imaging, though the first-order Taylor approximation (H_C ∈ [0,1]) may break down under extreme highlight conditions. Open questions include whether the frequency-domain channel ratio generalizes beyond RGB to other color spaces or modalities, and whether the angular modulation component adds meaningful value in scenes without strong directional lighting. The method's reliance on Fourier transforms introduces a global operation that may be less suitable for very high-resolution or streaming applications.
