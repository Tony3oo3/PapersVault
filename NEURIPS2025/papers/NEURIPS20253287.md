## Problem

LLMs frequently fail to enforce instruction hierarchy when system and user prompts contain conflicting directives, a failure that is especially acute in reasoning models where errors propagate across intermediate steps. Existing approaches address this by fine-tuning on oracle completions from frontier models (GPT-4o, Claude), but these lack programmatically verifiable reward signals and chain-of-thought traces, making them inapplicable to reasoning model alignment or black-box prompt optimization.

## Input / Output

Each training instance is a persona-grounded prompt scaffold (system segment + user segment) with a compliance directive injected at the system level and a mutually exclusive conflicting directive at the user level, paired with a deterministic Python verifier function restricted to syntactic operations (word replacement, insertion, deletion, structural reformatting). The verifier returns a binary pass/fail signal that serves directly as the GRPO reward, eliminating the need for oracle labels or token-level reasoning traces. The full dataset comprises 22,922 verified instances drawn from 99,361 synthesized candidates after unit-test-based filtering.

## Method

The paper introduces a four-stage LLM synthesis pipeline (persona sampling → compliance directive → conflicting directive + verifier → prompt scaffold) that generates diverse instruction-conflict instances, followed by a unit-test-based validation and repair procedure using three test types (positive for i⁺, positive for i⁻, negative for i⁻) with N sampled outputs each and up to R repair attempts. A probabilistic model provides closed-form expressions for false retention and false rejection rates, guiding the choice of N and R for reliable cleaning. Models are trained via GRPO using the verifier's binary output as the answer reward (plus a format reward for reasoning models), with structural augmentation varying directive placement across prompt levels to promote generalization. The same verifier-defined supervision extends to black-box prompt optimization via OPRO-style discrete search, improving IHEval scores without gradient access or oracle completions.

## Results

On IHEval (the primary instruction-hierarchy benchmark), verifier-supervised fine-tuning improves DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-8B conflict safety from 16.7 to 36.5 and aligned safety from 64.0 to 91.6, while preserving MATH-500 Pass@1 (88.2→81.6) and AIME (50.4→50.4). For instruction-following models, the method matches or exceeds oracle-supervised DPO (RealGuardrail) on IHEval conflict task execution (47.6 vs. 46.2 on Qwen2.5-7B; 59.4 vs. 51.2 on Llama3.1-8B) and safety (37.6 vs. 20.7 on Qwen), while improving StrongReject jailbreak robustness to 99.7 (Qwen) and 97.8 (Llama). Safety robustness generalizes without adversarial training data: PurpleLlama scores rise from 44.4→50.0 (Qwen-7B reasoning) and 47.4→57.2 (Llama-8B reasoning).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole external baseline is RealGuardrail [23], which uses oracle completions from GPT-4o/Claude-3.7 for SFT and DPO. On Qwen2.5-7B, the proposed method wins on IHEval conflict safety (37.6 vs. 20.7, +16.9), aligned rule-following (72.7 vs. 59.9, +12.8), aligned task execution (68.3 vs. 56.9, +11.4), and PurpleLlama (91.3 vs. 79.1, +12.2), while matching on conflict rule-following (53.5 vs. 53.3) and slightly trailing on aligned safety (66.0 vs. 73.7). On Llama3.1-8B, the comparison is more mixed: the method wins on conflict task execution (59.4 vs. 51.2, +8.2) and StrongReject (97.8 vs. 93.4, +4.4), but loses on conflict safety (60.6 vs. 85.5, −24.9) and conflict rule-following (54.9 vs. 64.9, −10.0). The authors attribute the Llama safety gap to their training data containing non-adversarial directives, in contrast to RealGuardrail's specialized adversarial oracle data, and frame adversarial syntax robustness as orthogonal to instruction-hierarchy logic. MMLU generalization is preserved in both cases (73.4 vs. 73.7 on Qwen; 66.7 vs. 66.2 on Llama), confirming no broad capability regression.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant oracle-supervised paradigm for instruction hierarchy alignment (Wallace et al., Guan et al., RealGuardrail), which fine-tunes on GPT-4o or Claude-generated completions, by replacing oracle labels with programmatically verifiable checker functions embedded in synthesized instruction-conflict instances. It extends the programmatic verification paradigm—previously confined to code and math final-answer checking (HumanEval, GSM8K, PAL, RLEF)—to instruction-level behavioral compliance under hierarchical prompt constraints, and borrows the discrete search structure of OPRO for its black-box prompt optimization extension. The reward design follows the format-plus-answer decomposition introduced in DeepSeek-R1, but applies it to directive adherence rather than mathematical correctness.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a scalable, deterministic supervision signal for instruction hierarchy that works for both reasoning models (where SFT/DPO cannot supervise intermediate steps) and instruction-tuned models, without requiring oracle completions, architectural modifications, or gradient access. The notable transfer from syntactic single-turn directive training to semantic multi-turn safety robustness (e.g., +37.8 on RuLES Redteam Harmless) suggests that hierarchy logic generalizes beyond the training distribution, though the authors acknowledge the method is restricted to syntactically verifiable directives and does not address adversarial obfuscation. Open questions include extending to semantic/nuanced instructions, multi-turn synthesis, and combining hierarchy alignment with dedicated adversarial robustness training.
