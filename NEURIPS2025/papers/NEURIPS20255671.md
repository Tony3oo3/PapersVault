## Problem

While numerous concept erasure methods have been proposed to suppress NSFW content generation in text-to-image diffusion models, no comprehensive benchmark exists to systematically compare their effectiveness across diverse scenarios. Existing safety benchmarks either evaluate base models without erasure interventions or target object/style suppression rather than the specific challenges of NSFW content, and the weak correlation between prompt toxicity and generated image safety makes simple word-filtering insufficient. This paper fills that gap by providing the first full-pipeline evaluation framework and systematic empirical study of 13 state-of-the-art NSFW concept erasure methods.

## Input / Output

The benchmark consumes text prompts from four NSFW-themed datasets (I2P, 4chan, Lexica, Template; 5,637 prompts total) and a general COCO-10K set, along with theme-specific keyword sets (detailed or concise) and, for Mode-2 methods, 20/200/1000 training images per theme. All methods operate on Stable Diffusion v1.4 with a fixed 40-step diffusion process, producing one image per prompt (I2P), three (4chan, Lexica), or twenty (Template). Generated images are scored by a VQA-based classifier for theme-specific NSFW detection, and the full output includes Erasure Proportion, Genital Ratio Difference, FID, LPIPS, CLIPScore, and ImageReward across all four evaluation perspectives.

## Method

The paper introduces a full-pipeline toolkit organized around four evaluation perspectives: overall erasure effectiveness, sensitivity to training data scale and theme, robustness against adversarial toxic prompts (via RAB red-teaming), and preservation of unrelated concepts. A key design choice is the taxonomy that categorizes 13 methods by intervention stage (dataset cleaning, parameter fine-tuning, post-hoc correction), required data type (text-only vs. text+image), and trained component (UNet, text encoder, or none). For automated detection, a VQA model is selected over CLIP, MHSC, and NudeNet based on its superior recall alignment with human annotations across nudity, violence, and horror themes. The evaluation introduces a new metric, Genital Ratio Difference (GRD), to quantify whether nudity erasure specifically targets genital regions rather than over-suppressing all body parts. Experiments reveal that post-hoc methods (SLD) offer the best efficiency-effectiveness trade-off for resource-constrained settings, adversarial fine-tuning (AU) provides the strongest robustness against adversarial prompts, and increasing training data scale yields diminishing returns while degrading image quality.

## Results

Across four NSFW datasets (I2P, 4chan, Lexica, Template), post-hoc methods dominate erasure effectiveness: SLD-Max achieves 91.81% overall Erasure Proportion (EP), SLD-Str 80.22%, and SD-NP 71.65%, while the best fine-tuning method AU reaches 70.58% EP. Among fine-tuning baselines, ESD-u (5.44%), SPM (5.54%), and ESD-x (7.88%) perform poorly, indicating that concept-specific hyperparameters limit generalization. For adversarial robustness (RAB dataset), SLD-Max achieves 96.09% overall EP and AU 93.96%, while MACE reaches 84.99%—the best among image-data-based fine-tuning methods. On generative quality (COCO-10K), ESD-u attains the best FID (17.77) and SPM the best CLIPScore (26.29), whereas MACE degrades most severely (FID 51.24, ImageReward −1.88).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Post-hoc methods (SLD, SD-NP) consistently outperform all fine-tuning-based methods in erasure effectiveness, with SLD-Max achieving 91.81% overall EP versus AU's 70.58%—a gap of over 21 percentage points—while requiring zero training time. Among fine-tuning methods, AU (adversarial training on the text encoder) leads at 70.58% EP and also achieves the best robustness (93.96% on RAB), but at the cost of the longest training time (604 min). ESD-u and SPM, despite being popular baselines, achieve only 5–8% EP, indicating that their concept-specific hyperparameters and semantic-distance mechanisms severely limit generalization across NSFW themes. MACE stands out for robustness (84.99% on RAB, second only to AU and SLD-Max) by operating on image-level perception via masked attention, but it degrades generative quality the most (FID 51.24, IR −1.88 on COCO-10K). SalUn shows strong EP on 4chan and Template (77.95%, 78.88%) but the worst GRD (−6.38%), meaning its unlearning process over-erases non-genital body parts. SPM achieves the best semantic alignment (CLIPScore 26.29) and competitive FID (19.40), making it suitable for quality-priority scenarios despite its low erasure effectiveness.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as the first systematic benchmark specifically for NSFW concept erasure in diffusion models, carving out a gap left by prior safety benchmarks (UnsafeD, UCANVAS, HUB) that either evaluate base model safety without erasure methods or focus on object/style suppression rather than NSFW content. It repurposes the existing landscape of 13+ concept erasure techniques (ESD, SPM, UCE, AU, AC, SelfD, SalUn, MACE, SLD, SD-NP) into a unified evaluation pipeline with a novel taxonomy organized by intervention stage, data requirements, and trained components. The evaluation infrastructure draws on established metrics (FID, LPIPS, CLIPScore, ImageReward) and detection tools (VQA, NudeNet, CLIP) but introduces the Genital Ratio Difference (GRD) metric and fine-grained thematic annotation to enable multi-dimensional NSFW-specific comparisons.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This benchmark provides the first practical decision framework for deploying concept erasure in real-world scenarios, with clear recommendations: post-hoc methods (SLD) for resource-constrained settings, adversarial training (AU) for high-security environments, and text-only methods for data-scarce conditions. The finding that improved erasure effectiveness consistently degrades generation quality (e.g., MACE's FID of 51.24 vs. ESD-u's 17.77) highlights a fundamental trade-off that practitioners must navigate. Limitations include coverage of only three NSFW themes (nudity, violence, horror), exclusion of politically sensitive content, evaluation on a single model architecture (SD v1.4), and reliance on a VQA classifier whose boundaries may not fully capture human judgment. Open questions include whether findings generalize to newer architectures (SDXL, Flux), whether the erasure-quality trade-off can be broken, and how to handle context-dependent NSFW themes that lack distinctive visual signatures.
