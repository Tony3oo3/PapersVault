## Problem

Existing LLM input saliency methods lack a rigorous, model-agnostic evaluation protocol for long-context scenarios. The paper's needle-in-a-haystack stress test reveals a systemic flaw: all seven popular attribution techniques assign non-trivial importance to irrelevant distractor tokens, with misattribution exceeding 90% at 10K-token inputs, making current interpretability tools actively misleading in realistic long-context settings.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255164/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

ABO takes a frozen LLM's input token sequence and a single target output token, and produces a per-token saliency score vector (normalized to sum to one) derived from the optimized attention bias magnitudes. Each token receives a single scalar bias shared across all attention heads and layers, yielding a compact L-dimensional output. The method operates entirely at inference time without modifying model weights, requiring only forward and backward passes through the attention mechanism.

## Method

ABO reframes token attribution as a differentiable optimization over attention logits: a learnable scalar bias α_i is injected into each input token's attention score (L_ij ← L_ij + α_j), shared across all heads and layers. The biases are optimized to maximize the biased model's probability of generating the target token, regularized by a sigmoid penalty (λΣσ(α_i)) that promotes sparsity and suppresses saliency on irrelevant tokens. The resulting bias magnitudes serve as causal saliency scores by construction, representing the minimal attention perturbation needed to influence the target output. Unlike discrete masking or gradient-based linear approximations, ABO performs soft continuous reweighting that preserves full context while directly coupling token importance to the model's internal attention dynamics, yielding stable attributions even at 10K-token lengths.

## Results

On the single-token NIAH stress test, ABO achieves a Hit Ratio of 41.75% at 1K tokens on LLaMA-2-7B, roughly 2× the best baseline (AT2 at 21.15%), and maintains 15.43–15.44% at 10K tokens on Mistral-7B and DeepSeek-R1 while the strongest baselines drop below 6%. In the long-context token pruning experiment on LongBench-2wikimqa_e, ABO retains 51.2% QA accuracy at 95% pruning on QWen3-14B versus 36.2% for the best baseline (AT2). For zero-shot toxicity suppression on REALTOXICPROMPTS, ABO reduces overall toxicity from 0.53 (original) to 0.24, outperforming AT2 (0.28) and TOKENSHAP (0.41).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ABO outperforms all six baselines (NAIVE, GRADNORM, INPUT × GRAD, TOKENSHAP, TDD, ATMAN, AT2) across every model and context length tested. The two strongest baselines are AT2 and TOKENSHAP, which lead among prior methods at short contexts (e.g., AT2 at 21.15% vs. TOKENSHAP at 17.73% on LLaMA2-7B at 1K), but both collapse rapidly as context grows—dropping below 7% by 4K tokens and below 6% by 10K tokens. ABO's advantage widens with length: at 1K tokens it leads AT2 by roughly 20 percentage points, while at 10K tokens on Mistral-7B it leads by nearly 10 points (15.43% vs. 5.59%), a relative improvement of over 175%. Gradient-based methods (GRADNORM, INPUT × GRAD) perform worst at long contexts, falling to 1–2% Hit Ratio, barely above the random NAIVE baseline. In the token pruning experiment, ABO also dominates: at 95% pruning on QWen3-14B it achieves 51.2% QA accuracy versus AT2's 36.2% and ATMAN's 34.1%, confirming that its saliency scores more faithfully identify causally essential tokens.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: LLM input saliency (gradient-based, attention-derived, Shapley-based, and perturbation-based methods) and the needle-in-a-haystack (NIAH) evaluation paradigm originally designed for long-context retrieval. It departs from prior saliency work by repurposing NIAH as a controlled stress test with explicit ground-truth saliency distributions, exposing a systematic failure mode (misattribution to distractors) that short-context evaluations had masked. Methodologically, ABO breaks from the gradient/perturbation/attention-weight lineage by reframing attribution as a differentiable optimization over per-token attention bias scalars, a formulation motivated by the empirical observation that small attention-logit shifts can deterministically flip model outputs among contradictory messages.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that all six widely-used saliency methods misallocate over 90% of their mass to irrelevant tokens at 10K context length is a significant credibility problem for the interpretability toolkit that practitioners currently rely on. ABO offers a more causally grounded alternative with practical downstream utility (detoxification, sentiment steering, reasoning-error correction), but its absolute Hit Ratio (15–45%) and per-example optimization cost leave substantial room for improvement. Open questions include whether the NIAH stress test generalizes to multi-step reasoning and multimodal settings, whether bias inference can be amortized via meta-learning or distillation, and whether the shared-bias-across-layers design is truly optimal or merely a pragmatic compromise.
