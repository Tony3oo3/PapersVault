## Problem

Standard RL pretraining algorithms (e.g., TD, MC) empirically produce Transformer parameters that enable in-context reinforcement learning, but it remains unclear why these parameters emerge. Wang et al. [2025] showed the converged parameters form an invariant set of the pretraining dynamics, but their analysis was restricted to single-layer Transformers, the invariant set was larger than the actual converged parameters, and convergence to that set was not guaranteed. This paper asks whether the ICRL-capable parameters are actually global minimizers of the pretraining loss, providing a stronger and more complete explanation for their emergence.

## Input / Output

The Transformer takes a structured prompt Z_0 ∈ R^{(2n+1)×(m+1)} whose first m columns encode the context (one-hot state features, discounted expected next-state features, and scalar rewards) and whose last column encodes a single query state. For training, the context columns enumerate all n states with their expected transitions; for evaluation, the context is a sampled trajectory of length m from a novel MRP. The output is a single scalar: the estimated value v(s_q) of the query state, extracted as the negative of the bottom-right element of the final layer embedding.

## Method

The paper proves that the θ_L^TD parameterization—which makes each layer of a looped linear Transformer execute one step of batched TD—converges to the true value function as depth L→∞ (Theorem 1) and is a global minimizer of the Norm of Expected Update (NEU) loss for both multi-task TD and multi-task MC pretraining (Theorem 2, Corollary 1). The proof rests on three lemmas: the expected TD error decays as γ^L, the evolving embedding norms remain uniformly bounded, and the gradient norm grows only polynomially in L, so their product vanishes. The parameter space is restricted by a sparsity constraint (Θ^Looped) that fixes the P matrix structure and allows only the A block of Q to vary, making the optimization tractable. Empirically, pretraining a 30-layer looped linear Transformer on 20,000 randomly generated Boyan's chain MRPs with either multi-task TD or MC recovers the θ^TD weights and yields decreasing MSVE with context length on held-out tasks.

## Results

The paper's primary contributions are theoretical: Theorem 1 proves that the L-layer linear Transformer parameterized by θ_L^TD converges to the true value function v(s_q) as L→∞, and Theorem 2 (with Corollary 1) proves that θ_L^TD is a global minimizer of the NEU loss for both multi-task TD and multi-task MC pretraining in the infinite-depth limit. Empirically, on Boyan's chain with L=30 looped Transformer layers and 20,000 training tasks, both multi-task TD and MC produce Transformers whose MSVE decreases monotonically with context length (Figure 1), confirming in-context policy evaluation capability. The converged P and Q matrices from both pretraining schemes visually match the theoretical θ_L^TD parameterization (Figure 2), with similar noise patterns across the two methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not benchmark against competing ICRL methods; its empirical section is a proof-of-concept validation of theoretical predictions rather than a comparative study. The only internal comparison is between multi-task TD and multi-task MC pretraining, both of which converge to the same θ_L^TD parameter configuration and exhibit similar MSVE-vs-context-length curves, suggesting the Transformer learns to implement in-context TD regardless of whether the pretraining target is a TD bootstrap or a Monte Carlo return. The paper positions itself as extending Wang et al. [2025] (ICLR 2025), which established the single-layer invariant-set result, by (1) proving inference-time convergence for multi-layer Transformers, (2) upgrading the invariant-set result to a global-minimizer result, and (3) extending the analysis from TD to MC pretraining. No quantitative head-to-head comparison with other ICRL approaches (e.g., Amago, RL², or algorithm distillation) is provided.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Wang et al. (ICLR 2025), who proved that a linear Transformer's layer-by-layer forward pass is equivalent to iterative TD updates under a specific parameterization θ_L^TD, and who showed these parameters belong to an invariant set of the multi-task TD update. The current work extends that analysis in three ways: proving convergence of the value estimate as depth grows, proving θ_L^TD is a global minimizer of the NEU loss (not merely an invariant-set member), and extending the global-minimizer result to Monte Carlo pretraining. It deliberately departs from the supervised-pretraining / algorithm-distillation line (Laskin et al. 2023, Lin et al. 2024) by studying reinforcement pretraining, where the pretraining dynamics are fundamentally different and the emergence of ICRL is non-trivial.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that θ_L^TD is a global minimizer for both TD and MC pretraining provides a principled explanation for why standard RL algorithms produce ICRL-capable parameters, addressing a central open question in the ICRL literature. The finding that MC pretraining (which uses no bootstrapping) still yields a Transformer that implements TD in its forward pass suggests the network discovers an optimal inference-time learning strategy rather than merely imitating the training algorithm. However, the analysis is restricted to linear attention, one-hot features over enumerated states, and Boyan's chain, leaving open whether these conclusions extend to non-linear activations, continuous state spaces, or control tasks.
