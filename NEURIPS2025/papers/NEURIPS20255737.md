## Problem

Existing GNN-based protein learning methods operate at a single residue-level scale, missing the hierarchical organization of secondary structure motifs (α-helices, β-sheets, loops) that are critical for distinguishing biologically distinct protein conformations sharing identical primary sequences. Prior multiscale approaches that use large radial cutoffs or surface modeling to capture broader context incur substantial computational overhead and lack explicit biological grounding, making them impractical for long sequences.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 3D α-carbon coordinates, DSSP secondary structure token assignments per residue, and amino acid type labels as input. The DSSP annotations drive a segmentation step that partitions the residue sequence into contiguous subsequences sharing the same structural token, which then define the nodes of the coarse-grained graph. The framework outputs a global protein embedding vector that is fed to task-specific heads for enzyme reaction classification (EC number) or ligand binding affinity regression (pKd), with the same architecture producing both outputs depending on the downstream task.

## Method

The core contribution is a two-level hierarchical geometric graph construction: (1) a collection of fine-grained intra-structural graphs, one per secondary structure motif, built via the SCHull algorithm over α-carbon coordinates to capture local residue-level interactions, and (2) a single coarse-grained inter-structural graph whose nodes are motif geometric centers, with edges determined by SCHull and augmented with relative orientation features g_i^T g_j computed from equivariant local frames. A two-stage GNN then operates on this hierarchy: the first-stage GNN performs message passing independently within each motif subgraph to produce compressed motif embeddings, and the second-stage GNN performs message passing on the inter-structural graph to model long-range dependencies and produce the final global representation. The framework is modular (any off-the-shelf GNN can be plugged into either stage), provably sparse with total edges bounded by 3N, and theoretically shown to preserve maximal expressiveness under injective update/aggregation/readout functions, guaranteeing it can distinguish any two non-identical protein structures up to rigid motion.

## Results

On the React enzyme reaction classification benchmark, Mamba+SSHG achieves 88.4% test accuracy (vs. 85.9% for Mamba alone), while ProNet+SSHG reaches 87.2% (vs. 86.4% for ProNet-Backbone) with 33% less training time per epoch (140s vs. 210s). On the PDBbind ligand binding affinity task, Mamba+SSHG achieves the best RMSE of 1.399, Pearson correlation of 0.614, and Spearman correlation of 0.610, outperforming all baselines. Across both tasks, SSHG consistently reduces training time by 25–40% and memory usage by up to 90% compared to equivalent dense radial-graph constructions, while improving accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SSHG consistently improves every backbone it is integrated with: GCN gains +4.7 pp (66.5→71.2%), GVP-GNN gains +5.1 pp (68.5→73.6%), ProNet-Backbone gains +0.8 pp (86.4→87.2%), and Mamba gains +2.5 pp (85.9→88.4%) on React. On PDBbind, the same pattern holds with Mamba+SSHG reducing RMSE from 1.457 to 1.399 and ProNet+SSHG from 1.458 to 1.435. ProNet+SSHG matches IEConv's 87.2% accuracy on React while using only 1.3M parameters versus IEConv's 9.8M, and Mamba+SSHG (RMSE 1.399) surpasses IEConv (1.554) and HoloProt-Full Surface (1.464) on LBA. SSHG also outperforms DWNN (76.7%) and GearNet (79.4%) by 10.5 and 7.8 pp respectively on React. On the LBA task, Mamba+SSHG beats TAPE (RMSE 1.890) by a wide margin (1.399 vs. 1.890) and exceeds HoloProt-Full Surface on all three regression metrics (Pearson 0.614 vs. 0.509, Spearman 0.610 vs. 0.500). The efficiency gains are substantial: compared to ProNet with a 16 Å radial cutoff (14,881 edges, 247 s/epoch, 17,768 MiB), ProNet+SSHG uses only 1,593 edges, 140 s/epoch, and 1,818 MiB while achieving higher accuracy (87.2% vs. 87.0%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the SCHull sparse graph construction from the same group's prior work [41] and the equivariant local frame formalism of [8, 9], combining them with DSSP-based secondary structure segmentation to create a two-level hierarchical graph for proteins. It departs from prior multiscale protein GNNs (HoloProt [33], IEConv [14]) by replacing data-driven clustering or large-radius radial graphs with biologically grounded secondary structure motifs as the natural hierarchical unit, yielding a provable sparsity bound of |E| < 3N. The two-stage message-passing architecture is modular, allowing any off-the-shelf GNN (GVP-GNN, ProNet, Mamba) to be plugged into either stage, and the theoretical expressiveness guarantee (Theorem 4.2) extends the SCHull distinguishability result to the hierarchical setting via local frame orientation encoding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practical path to scaling protein GNNs to longer sequences by decoupling local and global interactions into sparse subgraphs, with the 90% memory reduction and 2× speedup being directly relevant to resource-constrained settings. However, the approach is limited to tasks where 3D structure is available (DSSP requires coordinates), has only been validated on two benchmarks (EC classification and LBA), and the injectivity assumption underlying the expressiveness theorem is not met by standard MLPs with ReLU. Open questions include whether the framework generalizes to fold classification, protein-protein interaction, and whether integration with pretrained protein language models can further close the gap to larger architectures.
