## Problem

Classical momentum-based optimizers (heavy ball, Adam) applied naively to low-rank weight factorizations do not respect the geometry of the rank-r matrix manifold, and can fail to converge to valid low-rank optima. While dynamical low-rank training (DLRT) corrects this for SGD by projecting gradient flows onto the tangent space, it has not been extended to momentum-based optimizers, leaving a significant gap since state-of-the-art models rely on Adam and its variants for stable training.

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard neural network inputs (RGB images at 32×32 to 256×256 pixels, text sequences up to 640 tokens) and produces standard outputs (class labels, next-token predictions, similarity scores). Internally, each weight matrix W is factorized as W = USV⊤ with orthonormal bases U, V and a coefficient matrix S, and the optimizer jointly updates the weight coefficients, first-moment coefficients, and (for Adam) second-moment coefficients while adaptively adjusting the rank r via basis augmentation and SVD-based truncation.

## Method

The paper first proves that naive heavy-ball updates on individual low-rank factors produce a coupled dynamics (Eq. 5) whose stationary points need not satisfy the manifold optimality condition P(W)∇W L = 0 (Theorem 4). It then derives the correct projected gradient flow (Eq. 7) and constructs a low-rank heavy ball method (Algorithm 1) that avoids stiff S_V^{-1} terms by evolving augmented basis matrices K_V and L_V via forward Euler, sharing orthonormal bases between weights and momentum, and projecting momentum coefficients onto the updated bases before the coefficient update. The low-rank Adam method (Algorithm 2) extends this by projecting both the first and second exponential moving averages onto the augmented bases (with a square-root projection for the second moment to preserve element-wise positivity), applying standard Adam bias correction, and performing an adaptive coefficient update. Rank adaptivity is achieved through a basis-augmentation–truncation cycle that doubles the basis rank each step and truncates back via a relative SVD threshold, enabling the method to discover the appropriate rank during training.

## Results

On the GLUE benchmark (DeBERTaV3-base), Algorithm 2 achieves a mean score of 88.75 with only 0.89M trainable parameters, surpassing full fine-tuning (87.63 with 184M params) and all competing PEFT methods. On Llama-2 7B finetuning, Algorithm 2 reaches 84.09% accuracy on BoolQ and 76.77% on PIQA, dramatically outperforming LoRA (62.17% and 52.18% respectively) at comparable compression rates (~99.7%). For GPT-2 pretraining from scratch on OpenWebText, Algorithm 2 achieves a validation loss of 3.4642 at 60.61% compression, compared to 7.0242 for LoRA pretraining at a similar 60.79% compression.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Algorithm 2 outperforms all eight compared baselines on the GLUE mean score (88.75), using fewer trainable parameters than every fixed-rank method. Against the closest competitor GeoLoRA (88.19, 0.88M params), Algorithm 2 gains +0.56 mean points with a comparable parameter budget, winning on 5 of 7 tasks (SST-2, CoLA, QQP, RTE, MRPC). Against AdaLoRA (88.00, 1.27M), the gain is +0.75 with 30% fewer parameters. On Llama-2 7B, Algorithm 2 achieves a massive 21.9-point improvement over LoRA(r=6) on BoolQ (84.09% vs 62.17%) and 24.6 points on PIQA (76.77% vs 52.18%) at nearly identical compression rates (~99.7–99.8%). In transfer learning (Table 1), Algorithm 2 matches or exceeds full-rank baseline accuracy at 86–95% compression, while DLRT without momentum projection drops 5–13% and LoRA pretraining also degrades significantly. For GPT-2 pretraining, Algorithm 2's validation loss of 3.4642 is 3.56 points lower than LoRA pretraining (7.0242) at the same ~60% compression, approaching the full-rank baseline (3.2313).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the dynamical low-rank training (DLRT) framework [28] and its geometric LoRA variant GeoLoRA [27] from SGD-only to momentum-based optimizers (heavy ball and Adam), which is the critical practical gap since nearly all modern deep learning training relies on Adam/AdamW. The construction draws on the robust rank-adaptive integrator of Ceruti et al. [2] to handle the stiffness introduced by the manifold projection, and on the tangent-space geometry of the rank-r matrix manifold from Koch & Lubich [18]. The key conceptual departure from LoRA-style methods [15] is that the paper shows naively applying momentum to low-rank factors does not correspond to a valid projected gradient flow and can stall at non-optimal stationary points (Theorem 4), motivating the shared-basis, projected-momentum design of Algorithms 1–2.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By providing the first principled momentum-based optimizer that provably converges to low-rank optima while jointly compressing weights, gradients, and optimizer states, this work removes the main practical barrier to adopting DLRT in real training pipelines where Adam is standard. The empirical gaps (5–13% accuracy drops for unprojected DLRT, and a 3.5-point validation-loss gap for LoRA pretraining on GPT-2) are substantial and consistent across VGG, ViT, DeBERTa, and Llama-2 architectures. Open questions include whether the approach scales to 100B+ parameter models, how it interacts with other PEFT techniques (e.g., quantization, sparsity), and whether the rank-adaptive truncation criterion can be made fully automatic without per-dataset tuning of τ.
