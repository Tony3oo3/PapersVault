## Problem

Conventional representation learning methods learn universal embeddings that predominantly capture a single dominant semantic (typically 'shape' or 'category'), making them suboptimal for customized tasks that prioritize alternative criteria such as color, texture, count, or scene. Adapting to a new criterion via supervised fine-tuning incurs high annotation and computational costs, and no efficient, general-purpose mechanism exists to reorient an existing representation toward an arbitrary user-specified criterion without retraining.

## Input / Output

The method takes a batch of RGB images and a short user-specified criterion string (e.g., 'color', 'texture', 'count', 'scene') as input. It outputs a conditional representation vector per image whose dimensions correspond to the LLM-generated descriptive texts, giving each axis an explicit semantic meaning. Downstream, these vectors feed directly into logistic regression (few-shot classification), k-means (clustering), or cosine-similarity ranking (retrieval) without further architectural changes; for the fashion retrieval task, a two-layer MLP head is optionally appended and trained on top of the CRL embeddings.

## Method

CRL formulates conditional representation learning as a basis transformation in the VLM's shared image-text feature space. Given a criterion C, an LLM is prompted to generate a set of descriptive texts W (e.g., 'red', 'crimson', 'scarlet' for 'color'), which are then encoded by a VLM text encoder into a text basis matrix T. The images are encoded by the VLM image encoder into universal representations I, and the conditional representation is computed as R = I·Tᵀ, projecting each image vector onto the text basis so that each coordinate of R measures the image's alignment with one descriptive text. This projection is training-free and exploits the modality alignment already learned by the VLM; the resulting representation is more expressive under the specified criterion while remaining interpretable, since each dimension maps to a concrete textual descriptor. The approach is backbone-agnostic (validated with CLIP, ALIGN, MetaCLIP, and BLIP-2) and requires no labeled data or gradient updates to switch criteria.

## Results

On the Clevr4-10k few-shot classification task, CRL achieves a mean accuracy of 64.96–67.76% across backbones, compared to 52.01–55.83% for the strongest baseline (BLIP2), with the most dramatic gain on the 'color' criterion (1-shot: 26.85% → 65.28%, a +38.4 pp improvement). On customized clustering, CRL boosts CLIP's color-criterion ACC from 12.23% to 88.05%. On the GeneCIS similarity retrieval benchmark, CLIP+CRL achieves a mean recall of 26.8 (vs. 17.0 for CLIP_image), and with fine-tuned weights reaches 32.6, surpassing the fine-tuned Combiner baseline (28.9) by 3.7 pp. On DeepFashion fashion retrieval, CRL attains a mean MAP of 11.33 after training, outperforming the best prior method RPF (10.22) by 10% relatively, and gains 30% relatively over CLIP in a training-free setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CRL consistently outperforms all four VLM baselines (CLIP, ALIGN, MetaCLIP, BLIP2) across all four downstream tasks. On few-shot classification, CRL yields a mean accuracy gain of ~10 pp over the best baseline, with the largest margin on non-dominant criteria like 'color' (+38 pp at 1-shot) and 'count' (+6 pp). On clustering, CRL surpasses traditional methods CC and SCAN on all criteria except 'shape', where CC/SCAN retain a slight edge due to their specialized design; CRL also outperforms the CLIP-based Multi-Map by 18–27 pp in mean NMI/ACC/ARI. On GeneCIS similarity retrieval, CLIP+CRL (26.8 mean recall) beats all five composed-retrieval baselines (Pic2Word, SEARLE, LinCIR, CIG, and the three CLIP-only variants) by 6.9–10.8 pp, and with fine-tuned weights (CLIP+CRL*) it surpasses the fine-tuned Combiner by 3.7 pp. On DeepFashion fashion retrieval, CRL in training-free mode (7.93 MAP) already exceeds the supervised CSN (8.01) and ASEN (8.74) baselines, and after appending a two-layer MLP it reaches 11.33 MAP, beating the strongest prior method RPF (10.22) by 10% relatively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on CLIP's aligned image-text feature space, repurposing it as the substrate for a basis transformation that re-weights semantic emphasis. It departs from conventional representation learning (CLIP, ALIGN, MetaCLIP, BLIP2), which the authors argue inherently biases toward a single dominant criterion like 'shape' or 'category,' and from task-specific conditional similarity methods (CSN, ASEN, RPF) that are delicately designed for particular domains. The key conceptual departure is framing conditional representation as a linear algebra basis change—where LLM-generated descriptive texts span a customized feature space—rather than as supervised fine-tuning or per-task architecture design, a perspective not explored in prior task-conditioned work like Taskonomy or Task2Vec.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CRL offers a simple, largely training-free mechanism (a single matrix multiplication R = I·Tᵀ) to redirect a frozen VLM's representation toward arbitrary user-specified criteria, potentially reducing the annotation and compute costs of supervised fine-tuning for domain-adapted features. The paper acknowledges two limitations: it underperforms specialized clustering methods on the dominant 'shape' criterion, and the LLM-generated text basis is only a rough approximation with no effective filtering strategy yet. Open questions include whether better basis construction (e.g., iterative refinement, learned text selection) could close the gap on dominant criteria, and whether the approach extends to non-visual modalities or more abstract criteria beyond attribute-level descriptors.
