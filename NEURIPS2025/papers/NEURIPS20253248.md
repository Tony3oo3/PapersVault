## Problem

While RL fine-tuning has been applied to VLA models, there is no systematic understanding of what specific generalization benefits it confers over supervised fine-tuning (SFT), nor which RL algorithms from the LLM/VLM literature transfer effectively to the POMDP structure of robotic tasks. This paper addresses both gaps by introducing a comprehensive generalization benchmark and directly comparing PPO, GRPO, and DPO for VLA fine-tuning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253248/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each timestep the policy receives a single 640×480 RGB frame (history length H=1) and a natural-language instruction, and outputs a 7-DoF Cartesian end-effector delta plus a binary gripper signal. Following the RT-2 discretisation recipe, each continuous action scalar is mapped to one of 256 bins (split between the 1st and 99th percentiles of the training set), and the resulting action tokens overwrite the 256 least-used tokens in Llama-2's vocabulary so the causal decoder emits them directly via next-token cross-entropy.

## Method

The paper identifies PPO as the preferred RL algorithm for VLA fine-tuning over GRPO and DPO, and distills an efficient recipe built on three design choices: a shared actor-critic backbone (the OpenVLA policy serves as the actor while a 3-layer MLP value head regresses the hidden state h⁰ at the first action-token position to a scalar value), a VLA warm-up phase using 140 motion-planner trajectories to stabilise early training, and a single PPO epoch per batch to avoid overfitting to rollouts. All fine-tuning uses LoRA (rank 32) on the Llama-2 7B backbone, and the full PPO training converges in ~42 hours on a single A100 GPU. The evaluation benchmark probes generalisation along three axes—Vision (unseen tables, dynamic textures/noise at weak and strong intensities), Semantics (unseen objects, receptacles, 16 re-phrased instructions, multi-object and distractive-receptacle tasks), and Execution (unseen object/receptacle positions, randomised robot initial poses, and mid-episode object repositioning)—revealing that RL substantially improves semantic grounding and execution robustness while matching SFT on visual perturbation resilience.

## Results

On the in-distribution pick-and-place task, RL (PPO) achieves 93.8% task success rate vs. 78.1% for SFT-16k. On out-of-distribution evaluation, RL outperforms SFT by 42.6% on unseen objects and tables. The largest gains appear in Execution (e.g., Robot Pose: 79.7% vs. 33.9%; Mid-Episode Object Reposition: 74.5% vs. 28.6%) and Semantics (e.g., Unseen Objects: 71.4% vs. 45.3%; Multi-Object OOD: 57.8% vs. 29.7%), while Vision tasks show comparable performance between the two methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is between PPO-based RL fine-tuning and SFT (behavioral cloning) on the same OpenVLA backbone. RL substantially outperforms SFT on Semantics (e.g., Unseen Objects: 71.4% vs. 45.3% success; Multi-Object OOD: 57.8% vs. 29.7%) and Execution (e.g., Robot Pose: 79.7% vs. 33.9%; Mid-Episode Reposition: 74.5% vs. 28.6%), while matching SFT on Vision tasks. Among RL algorithms, PPO consistently outperforms both GRPO (from DeepSeekMath) and DPO/TPO (from GRAPE) on the pick-and-place task; the authors attribute GRPO's underperformance to non-stationary POMDP dynamics destabilizing group-relative advantage estimates, and DPO's to sparse rewards making trajectory-level preference signals unreliable. In a preliminary sim-to-real transfer test on a Franka Panda, RL achieves 43% grasp success and 27% pick-and-place success versus 10% and 0% for SFT, confirming the generalization advantage transfers to physical hardware.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on OpenVLA (Kim et al., 2024) as the base VLA architecture and adapts PPO (Schulman et al., 2017) from the LLM/VLM RL fine-tuning literature to the POMDP setting of robotic manipulation. It departs from prior RL-for-VLA efforts (FLaRe, GRAPE, Guo et al. 2025b) by showing that online PPO can be applied directly without multi-stage imitation-RL pipelines, and by systematically testing whether LLM-native RL algorithms (GRPO, DPO) transfer to sequential robotic tasks. The key conceptual departure is framing VLA generalization as a three-axis problem (Vision, Semantics, Execution) and using this decomposition to isolate *where* RL provides gains over SFT, rather than reporting a single aggregate success rate.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first systematic, multi-dimensional comparison of RL vs. SFT fine-tuning for VLAs, yielding actionable guidance: RL substantially improves semantic grounding and execution robustness under distribution shift but offers no visual-robustness advantage, and PPO is the preferred algorithm over GRPO/DPO for POMDP robotic tasks. The efficient recipe (shared actor-critic backbone, VLA warm-up, single PPO epoch, ~42h on one A100) lowers the barrier to RL fine-tuning of large VLAs. Key limitations include confinement to a single pick-and-place task, simulation-only evaluation (with only a preliminary 30-trial sim-to-real check), and reliance on motion-planner-generated demonstrations rather than human-collected data. Open questions include whether the findings generalize to multi-task, long-horizon, or dexterous manipulation settings, and whether the PPO-over-GRPO/DPO gap persists with richer reward structures or larger-scale RL training.
