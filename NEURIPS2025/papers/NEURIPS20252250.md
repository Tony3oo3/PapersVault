## Problem

Classifier-free guidance (CFG) is the de facto method for improving diffusion model output quality and prompt alignment, but it requires a specific training procedure (randomly dropping conditions) and is inherently restricted to conditional generation. Existing training-free alternatives such as PAG and SEG manipulate attention maps but produce over-smoothed early-step results and offer only marginal gains in prompt alignment and FID, particularly for unconditional generation. The paper seeks a training-free, condition-agnostic guidance mechanism that delivers CFG-like improvements across both conditional and unconditional settings.

## Input / Output

TPG is a plug-and-play inference-time module applied to pre-trained latent diffusion models (SDXL, SD 2.1, SD3) without any architectural modification or retraining. For conditional generation it consumes a text prompt alongside the standard random noise input; for unconditional generation it requires only the noise. At each denoising step the method performs two forward passes through the denoiser (one clean, one with token shuffling applied at selected layers), so the sampling cost is comparable to CFG. The output is a single generated image, evaluated at 30k samples on MS-COCO 2014 validation prompts.

## Method

TPG defines the negative score s⁻ in the standard guidance formula s̃ = s⁺ + γ(s⁺ − s⁻) by applying a random permutation (shuffling) matrix S_{k,t} ∈ R^{N×N} to the intermediate token representations H ∈ R^{B×N×C} at selected denoiser layers k and time steps t. The shuffling is orthonormal (SᵀS = I), so it preserves token ℓ₂-norms and avoids internal covariate shift, while disrupting local spatial correlations and maintaining global statistics. The key insight is that this norm-preserving, structure-disrupting perturbation produces a guidance vector that is nearly orthogonal to the ground-truth noise and injects strong low-frequency energy in early denoising steps—behavior closely mirroring CFG—whereas attention-based methods (PAG, SEG) exhibit negative alignment at mid-steps and substantially lower guidance magnitudes. Ablations show shuffling outperforms other norm-preserving alternatives (Hadamard, Haar, sign flip) because it selectively breaks local patterns without globally mixing all token information, and that applying the perturbation only to downsampling layers of the U-Net yields the best results.

## Results

On SDXL unconditional generation, TPG achieves a FID of 69.31, nearly 2× better than the vanilla SDXL baseline (124.04) and substantially outperforming PAG (98.83) and SEG (82.64). In conditional generation, TPG reaches a FID of 17.77 and CLIP Score of 30.15, closely matching CFG (FID 12.79, CLIP 32.03) while significantly outperforming vanilla SDXL (FID 48.97), PAG (FID 20.49), and SEG (FID 23.94). On Stable Diffusion 2.1 unconditional generation, TPG further reduces FID from 25.24 (vanilla) to 16.69, with the highest Inception Score of 36.28.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against vanilla SDXL, TPG wins decisively on every metric in both unconditional (FID 69.31 vs. 124.04) and conditional (FID 17.77 vs. 48.97) settings. Compared to the other training-free methods, TPG outperforms PAG (FID 69.31 vs. 98.83 unconditional; 17.77 vs. 20.49 conditional) and SEG (FID 69.31 vs. 82.64 unconditional; 17.77 vs. 23.94 conditional) across all reported metrics. Against CFG, TPG is the only method applicable to unconditional generation (where CFG is not defined), and in conditional generation TPG trails CFG by roughly 5 FID points (17.77 vs. 12.79) and ~2 CLIP points (30.15 vs. 32.03), but remains competitive on sFID and Aesthetic Score. On Stable Diffusion 2.1, TPG similarly dominates vanilla SD (FID 16.69 vs. 25.24), PAG (21.30), and SEG (20.98) in unconditional generation. The paper also shows TPG generalizes to the ViT-based SD3 architecture, where PAG actually degrades performance (FID 138.08 vs. vanilla 113.86) while TPG improves it (FID 83.01).

## Relation to Prior Work

TPG directly adopts the classifier-free guidance formula (s̃ = s⁺ + γ(s⁺ − s⁻)) from Ho & Salimans [17] but replaces the unconditional negative score with a token-shuffled perturbed score, eliminating the need for conditional training. It departs from the attention-based perturbation line (PAG [19], SEG [18], SAG [20]) by operating on intermediate token representations rather than attention maps, and from autoguidance [16] by requiring no auxiliary 'bad' model. The key conceptual move is showing that a simple norm-preserving permutation along the token axis produces a guidance signal whose direction, frequency content, and norm profile closely mirror CFG's, bridging the gap between training-free methods and CFG.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TPG provides a genuinely plug-and-play, training-free guidance mechanism that works for both conditional and unconditional generation across U-Net and ViT-based architectures, addressing a practical gap where CFG is unavailable (unconditional models) or requires specific training. The ~2× FID improvement for unconditional SDXL generation and near-CFG performance in conditional settings make it immediately useful for practitioners. Open questions include whether the two-forward-pass cost (inherited from the CFG formula) can be reduced, and whether the method extends to video, audio, or 3D diffusion models where token structure differs. The finding that simple shuffling outperforms more structured orthogonal transforms (Hadamard, Haar) suggests the guidance signal benefits from local-structure disruption rather than global mixing, a design principle worth exploring further.
