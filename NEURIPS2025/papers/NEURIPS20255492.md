## Problem

While it is well established that wide neural networks converge to Gaussian processes in the infinite-width limit, both at initialization and during NTK training, prior quantitative results providing explicit finite-width error bounds were confined to the initialization regime. No rigorous upper bounds on the Wasserstein distance between a trained network's output and its Gaussian process counterpart existed at arbitrary positive training times, leaving a gap between qualitative NTK theory and practical finite-width deployment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255492/cropped_page8_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single-hidden-layer network of width n1 and input dimension n0 with i.i.d. Gaussian-initialized parameters, a finite training set with scalar labels, a test point x ∈ R^{n0}, and a training time t ≥ 0. It produces an explicit upper bound on the squared quadratic Wasserstein distance W2²(f(x;θ_t), G_t(x)) that scales as O(log n1 / n1) with a polynomial-in-t correction term, along with the closed-form mean μ_t(x) and covariance Σ_t(x,x') of the limiting Gaussian process, both expressed via the limiting NTK k_∞ and the matrix operator I_t(B) = (1 − e^{−Bt})B^{−1}.

## Method

The proof decomposes W2(f_t, G_t) via triangle inequality into a linearization error W2(f_t, f_lin) and a Gaussian approximation error W2(f_lin, G_t). The first term is controlled by partitioning parameter space into a high-probability 'good' event (where quenched estimates à la Bartlett et al. apply, yielding O(1/n1) bounds) and a 'bad' tail event handled via concentration inequalities over countably many discs, with the t⁸ time dependence arising from the operator I_t(k_0) ≤ t when no lower eigenvalue bound is available. The second term leverages the Basteri–Trevisan initialization-time Wasserstein bound combined with Grönwall's inequality on the gradient flow ODEs, reducing the problem to L^p estimates of the empirical NTK minus its limit. The key technical contribution is showing these two error sources remain simultaneously controlled at arbitrary training time t, yielding the final rate O(log n1 / n1) that holds for t growing polynomially in n1.

## Results

The main theoretical result (Theorem 3.4) establishes that for a trained shallow neural network with width n₁, the squared quadratic Wasserstein distance to its Gaussian process approximation satisfies W₂²(f(x;θ_t), G_t(x)) ≤ r(a₁ log n₁ / (λ_min^∞)³ n₁ n₀ + a₂ n₀ / (λ_min^∞)^r n₁^(r/4) (1+t⁸)), yielding an O(log n₁/n₁) decay rate for any training time t growing polynomially in n₁. Numerically, Experiment 2 (Figure 1, right) confirms this by computing the empirical W₂ distance between trained networks and G_t for widths n₁ ∈ {2, 4, ..., 256} with sigmoid activation, showing a clear power-law decay consistent with the theoretical prediction. The Gaussian process approximation (mean μ_t and covariance Σ_t) closely tracks individual trained networks of width 700 and 1000 over 20,000 epochs (Experiment 1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not compare against competing methods in a head-to-head empirical sense; rather, it extends prior theoretical bounds from initialization to arbitrary training times. The closest prior results are Basteri & Trevisan (2024), Trevisan (2023), and Favaro et al. (2025), which established quantitative W_p or total-variation convergence rates at initialization (t=0) for randomly initialized networks. This work generalizes those to t > 0, showing the O(log n₁/n₁) rate persists throughout training (up to polynomial time in n₁). The linearization error bound (Proposition 3.6) adapts Theorem 5.1 from Bartlett et al. (2021) and Theorem 2.2 from Chizat et al. (2019) to the setting of both inner and outer weights with unscaled MSE loss, whereas those prior results either freeze one weight matrix or use a different loss normalization. The Gaussian-process-side bound (Proposition 3.7) builds directly on the initialization-time W₂ estimates of Basteri & Trevisan (2024) and extends them via Grönwall-type arguments to finite training time.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the quantitative Gaussian process convergence results at initialization (Trevisan 2023, Basteri & Trevisan 2024, Favaro et al. 2025) from t=0 to arbitrary positive training times, filling a gap left by the qualitative NTK linearization results of Lee et al. 2020 and Jacot et al. 2018. The proof strategy adapts the quenched linearization error bounds of Bartlett et al. 2021 and Chizat et al. 2019 to a setting with both inner and outer weights and unscaled MSE loss, then combines them with the initialization-time Wasserstein estimates via a triangle inequality decomposition. The limiting kernel k_∞ and the operator I_t(B) formalize the connection between the finite-width NTK dynamics and the infinite-width Gaussian process G_t.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The explicit O(log n₁/n₁) bound on W₂² provides practitioners with a concrete criterion for when the NTK/Gaussian process approximation is quantitatively reliable during training, enabling uncertainty quantification and safe deployment of kernel-based theoretical insights to finite-width architectures. Key limitations include the t⁸ time-dependence (restricting the result to polynomially growing training times), the requirement of bounded Lipschitz activations (excluding ReLU), and the restriction to shallow networks. Open questions include whether the t⁸ scaling is sharp, whether the result extends to deep or convolutional architectures, and whether the rate can be improved to n₁⁻² (matching the initialization-time rate of Trevisan 2023) with sharper concentration arguments.
