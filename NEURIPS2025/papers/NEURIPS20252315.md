## Problem

Existing composed image retrieval (CIR) benchmarks rely on automated image-pair selection that produces ambiguous, semantic-level positives with high false-negative rates and text-only shortcuts, while training-based CIR methods require large volumes of high-quality triplets that are impractical to obtain at scale. The field lacks both an instance-level evaluation dataset with explicit hard negatives and a strong training-free baseline that leverages pre-trained VLMs without additional supervision.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an RGB image query depicting a specific object instance (e.g., Temple of Poseidon) and a short text query describing a modification (e.g., 'at sunset'), and produces a ranked list of database images scored by a fused cross-modal similarity. The database is instance-specific: each object instance has its own pool of ~3.7K images (positives and hard negatives), rather than a single shared database across all queries. Features are extracted with CLIP ViT-L/14, and the projection matrix and centering means are computed on the query side only, so stored database embeddings remain unaltered CLIP vectors.

## Method

BASIC is a training-free pipeline that treats composed retrieval as a logical AND over two modalities. It first centers image and text features by subtracting modality-specific means, then projects image features onto a low-dimensional semantic subspace (top-k eigenvectors of a contrastive covariance matrix built from an object-name corpus C+ and a style/condition corpus C−) to emphasize object identity while suppressing style variation. Text queries are contextualized by embedding caption-like expansions (query terms combined with random subject terms) to bridge the distribution gap between short modification phrases and CLIP's training distribution. Per-modality dot-product similarities are min-normalized and fused multiplicatively with a Harris-corner-inspired regularizer that penalizes items where only one modality is highly activated. An optional query-expansion step further refines the image query feature using top-ranked database neighbors, and the entire pipeline operates without any gradient updates or database re-embedding.

## Results

BASIC achieves state-of-the-art mAP across all five evaluated benchmarks: 32.13% on ImageNet-R, 31.65% on NICO++, 39.58% on MiniDomainNet, 41.38% on LTLL, and 31.64% on the newly introduced i-CIR (macro-mAP over 202 instances). On i-CIR, BASIC outperforms the strongest competitor CoVR-2 by 3.14 mAP points (31.64 vs. 28.50) and the next-best training-free method FreeDom by 14.4 points. Notably, BASIC is entirely training-free, requiring no fine-tuning of CLIP ViT-L/14 and no database re-embedding, yet it surpasses both training-based and training-free CIR methods on every dataset.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BASIC wins on all five datasets against all nine SOTA competitors. On i-CIR, the closest rival is CoVR-2 (28.50 mAP), a pseudo-triplet method that generates synthetic training data; BASIC leads by 3.14 points. FreeDom, the strongest training-free competitor on the domain-conversion benchmarks (ImageNet-R 29.91, MiniDN 37.27, LTLL 33.24), is outperformed by BASIC by 2.2–8.1 mAP points depending on the dataset, but collapses on i-CIR to 17.24, suggesting its domain-conversion focus does not transfer to instance-level retrieval. MagicLens (27.35 on i-CIR) and SEARLE (19.90) trail further behind, while textual-inversion methods like Pic2Word (19.36) and WeiCom (18.03) struggle with the instance-level granularity. The simple Text×Image baseline (17.48 on i-CIR) already captures much of the compositional signal, but BASIC's centering, semantic projection, min-normalization, and Harris fusion collectively add ~14 mAP points, confirming that each component contributes meaningfully.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of two lines of work: composed image retrieval (CIR) benchmarks and training-free VLM-based retrieval. It departs from the dominant CIR dataset construction paradigm (CIRR, FashionIQ, CIRCO), which automatically selects image pairs and generates difference descriptions, by instead defining an instance-level class notion and curating explicit hard negatives through a semi-automated LAION retrieval pipeline. The BASIC method draws on classical ideas—Harris corner detection for multiplicative fusion, query expansion from information retrieval, and contrastive covariance subspaces from [37]—but repurposes them in a training-free CLIP-based pipeline, distinguishing it from both textual-inversion methods (Pic2Word, SEARLE) and pseudo-triplet approaches (CompoDiff, CoVR-2) that require synthetic data generation or fine-tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

i-CIR fills a concrete gap identified by prior work: existing CIR benchmarks operate at the semantic-class level and exhibit text-only shortcuts, false negatives, and weak negative mining, making them poor proxies for genuinely compositional retrieval. The finding that i-CIR yields a +490% composition gain over uni-modal baselines while CIRR yields only +11% provides a clear diagnostic that the field's standard benchmarks may not be measuring what they claim. Open questions include whether the logical-AND fusion assumption generalizes to highly entangled image-text pairs, whether the single-salient-object design limits applicability to multi-object scenes, and whether the instance-level formulation scales to open-world settings beyond the 202 curated instances.
