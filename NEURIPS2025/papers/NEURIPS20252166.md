## Problem

Neural routing problem solvers (e.g., for TSP and CVRP) struggle to generalize across different instance scales and distributions because they overfit to patterns tied to specific training settings. Existing approaches either restrict the decision space or employ divide-and-conquer strategies, which inherently sacrifice solution optimality, and none leverage universal structural principles of routing problems to guide learning. This paper identifies a missing piece: a fundamental structural law governing optimal solutions that can serve as instance-independent guidance during training.

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of 2D vertex coordinates scaled to the unit square [0,1] as input, representing a routing problem instance (TSP or CVRP). During training, it additionally computes the purity order for every edge (the count of vertices lying inside the circle with that edge as diameter) and the purity availability of the unvisited vertex set at each decision step. At inference, only the standard autoregressive policy is used to produce a tour (vertex sequence) and its total Euclidean cost, with no extra computation beyond the base solver.

## Method

The paper first uncovers the Purity Law: in optimal routing solutions, the proportion of edges with a given purity order k follows a negative exponential decay (log y(k) = −βk + log α), with parameters statistically invariant across scales and distributions. Building on this, PUPO modifies the REINFORCE policy gradient by introducing a purity weighting W_t at each decision step, defined as 1 plus a discounted sum of purity costs (the purity order of the newly formed edge plus the change in purity availability of the remaining unvisited set). This weighting acts as a multiplicative factor on the log-probability gradient at each step, encouraging the policy to favor actions that maintain low-purity structure both in the current partial tour and in the future unvisited set. The supermodularity of purity availability ensures that the marginal purity signal becomes more significant at later decision stages, where myopic choices are most damaging for generalization. PUPO requires no architectural changes and adds no inference overhead, integrating seamlessly with existing solvers like INViT, ELG, POMO, and Pointerformer.

## Results

On randomly generated TSP instances, PUPO-trained INViT-100 achieves the largest relative gap reductions on large-scale instances: 18.05% on I-10000 (8.27%→6.78%), 16.74% on U-10000 (7.51%→6.25%), and 15.45% on U-1000 (7.41%→6.26%). On CVRP, PUPO-trained ELG-50 shows dramatic improvements of 68.58% on E-5000 (27.10%→8.52%) and 63.73% on I-5000 (19.43%→7.05%), while INViT-100 achieves 41.07% on C-5000 (7.66%→4.51%). On real-world TSPLIB, PUPO-trained ELG-50 reduces the gap from 22.88% to 20.24% (11.55% relative) on 1001–5000 instances, and on CVRPLIB 501–1000 from 16.25% to 11.12% (31.6% relative). PUPO introduces no additional inference overhead, with solving time deviations mostly within 10%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PUPO is applied as a training-time modification to four existing solvers without altering their architectures. Against INViT (Fang et al., ICML 2024), PUPO consistently improves generalization on large-scale instances (5–18% relative gap reduction across scales 1000–10000) while causing only minor degradation (≤2%) at the training scale of 100. Against ELG (Gao et al., IJCAI 2024), PUPO yields the most dramatic gains on CVRP large-scale instances (up to 68.58% on E-5000) but shows slight degradation on small-scale TSP (e.g., U-100: 6.60%→7.10%). Against POMO (Kwon et al., NeurIPS 2020) and Pointerformer (Jin et al., AAAI 2023), PUPO improves large-scale TSP gaps by 3–15% (e.g., POMO-100 on U-5000: 80.64%→68.42%; PF-100 on I-5000: 120.67%→103.74%), though absolute gaps remain higher than the more advanced ELG/INViT solvers. The consistent pattern across all four baselines is that PUPO sacrifices a small amount of accuracy at the training scale in exchange for substantially better cross-scale and cross-distribution generalization, attributed to its implicit regularization effect (reducing parameter Frobenius norms by ~1–4%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the REINFORCE policy gradient framework and POMO's multiple-optima baseline, but departs from prior generalization-focused work (INViT, ELG, HTSP, LEHD) that typically modifies network architecture or restricts the action space. Instead, PUPO identifies a universal structural principle (Purity Law) in optimal routing solutions and encodes it directly into the policy gradient as a training-time weighting, leaving the underlying architecture untouched. This positions the work as a training-paradigm contribution rather than an architectural one, complementing rather than competing with prior structural priors like k-nearest-neighbor constraints.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Purity Law provides the first empirically and theoretically validated universal structural regularity in routing optima that is invariant across scales and distributions, offering a principled inductive bias for neural solvers. PUPO's plug-and-play nature (no architecture changes, no inference overhead) makes it immediately applicable to a wide range of existing constructive solvers, with particularly large gains on out-of-distribution and large-scale instances. However, the observed trade-off—slightly degraded performance at the training scale—suggests the purity weighting acts as an implicit regularizer that may over-constrain small instances, and the authors acknowledge that integrating Purity Law into network design itself remains an open direction.
