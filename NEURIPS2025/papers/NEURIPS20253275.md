## Problem

Existing offline RL methods for medical treatment, such as CQL, suppress out-of-distribution actions but leave OOD states unaddressed, allowing learned policies to drift into clinically invalid or unsafe state trajectories during rollout. This action-only regularization imitates short-term clinician behavior without enabling safe exploration of improved long-term treatment strategies, and provides no mechanism to enforce explicit physiological safety boundaries on intermediate states.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an offline clinical dataset of state-action trajectories (e.g., 18,923 ICU stays from MIMIC-III with 13-dimensional physiological state vectors and 2-dimensional continuous actions for IV fluid rate and vasopressor dosage at 4-hour intervals, or 18-dimensional states at hourly resolution for hypotension). It produces a treatment policy mapping patient states to continuous action recommendations, with the reward defined as 1/SOFA score and explicit safety constraints on physiological thresholds (SpO₂ ≥ 92%, urine output ≥ 0.5 mL/kg/hour). The policy is guaranteed to remain within the dataset support with high probability over a finite horizon, rather than merely suppressing individual OOD actions.

## Method

OGSRL introduces a dual-constraint mechanism within a model-based offline safe RL framework. First, an OOD guardian classifier—formulated as a polynomial sum-of-squares (PSoS) sublevel set learned from the dataset—jointly constrains both states and actions to remain within the in-distribution support, encoded as an OOD cost constraint in the constrained MDP. Second, explicit vector-valued safety cost constraints encode physiological safety boundaries (e.g., minimum SpO₂, minimum urine output) that must be satisfied regardless of data coverage. The guarded E-CMDP is solved via constrained policy optimization (CPO), yielding a policy with theoretical guarantees: the guardian classifier is contained in the true in-distribution region with probability bounded by exp(−2N(α_c−α)²), and the resulting policy is both safe and near-optimal with error terms that vanish as dataset size grows. Unlike CQL which only penalizes OOD actions, this approach restricts the feasible policy space during optimization so that entire state-action trajectories stay within clinically supported regions, enabling safe improvement beyond observed clinician behavior.

## Results

On the MIMIC-III sepsis dataset (18,923 ICU stays), GMB-CPO achieves a mortality estimate (ME) of 1.38×10⁻², representing a 78% reduction compared to the standard of care (6.32×10⁻²), while simultaneously improving cumulative reward by 51% over SOC. GMB-CPO also attains the best action smoothness (ACP:MVD = 4.34, matching SOC exactly) and the highest Appropriate Intensification Rate (AIR = 44.8×10⁻²) among all guardian-augmented methods. On the cross-disease Acute Hypotension dataset, GMB-CPO achieves the highest mean reward (14.88 vs. 10.37 for SOC) and the best AIR (0.482), with MCR improving from near-zero (0.060) for MB-CPO to 0.700.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CQL (Kumar et al., NeurIPS 2020), GMB-CPO achieves a 72% lower mortality estimate (1.38 vs. 4.86 ×10⁻²) and a 3.4× higher AIR (44.8 vs. 13 ×10⁻²), though CQL retains a higher MCR (789 vs. 549 ×10⁻³), reflecting CQL's stronger imitation of individual clinician actions. Against CCQL (Nambiar et al., KDD 2023), GMB-CPO dominates on ME (1.38 vs. 4.81) and AIR (44.8 vs. 3.93), with CCQL again showing higher MCR (827 vs. 549). Against the model-based baselines MB-TRPO (Luo et al., ICLR 2019) and MB-CPO (Achiam et al., ICML 2017), GMB-CPO shows dramatic improvements: MCR jumps from near-zero (0.04) to 549, ACP:MVD drops from 48–50 to 4.34 (matching SOC), and ACP:IFA drops from 492–1670 to 6.47, indicating that without the guardian, model-based methods produce wildly off-distribution action sequences. The only metric where a baseline slightly edges GMB-CPO is AIR for MB-CPO (49.6 vs. 44.8), but MB-CPO's action smoothness is 75× worse than GMB-CPO's, making its recommendations clinically impractical.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the offline safe RL line initiated by CQL (which suppresses OOD actions via value penalization) by introducing a joint state-action OOD guardian based on polynomial sublevel sets, addressing the previously unhandled problem of OOD state trajectories. It builds on constrained policy optimization (CPO) as the policy solver and on chance-constrained MDP formulations (Shen et al., 2024) for the OOD cost constraint, while departing from shielding methods that operate online by restricting the feasible policy space during offline optimization. The sepsis treatment formulation extends prior work (Komorowski et al., 2018) by replacing terminal mortality rewards with SOFA-based instantaneous rewards and adding explicit physiological safety constraints (SpO₂, urine output) alongside the distribution-aware guardian.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical guarantees linking dataset size, OOD threshold, and policy safety/near-optimality provide a rare verifiable safety argument for offline RL in clinical deployment, where unverifiable extrapolation is unacceptable. The cross-disease validation (sepsis + acute hypotension) with consistent guardian benefits strengthens the case for a generalizable framework, though the practical instantiation relies on approximations (k-NN for dynamics, kernel methods for the PSoS classifier) that may not preserve all theoretical properties at scale. Open questions include whether the framework extends to higher-dimensional continuous action spaces (e.g., multi-drug regimens), how the OOD threshold should be calibrated in practice without access to the true in-distribution boundary, and whether the guarantees hold under more complex dynamics models (e.g., neural networks) rather than KDE.
