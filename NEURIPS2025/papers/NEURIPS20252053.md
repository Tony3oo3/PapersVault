## Problem

Existing uncertainty estimation methods for graph neural networks assume that spatially close nodes share similar uncertainty (a homophily-based prior), which breaks down under low label informativeness where neighboring nodes exhibit different label distributions. No prior work provides a principled mechanism to explicitly model and control the spatial correlation structure between node uncertainties, limiting the ability to capture long-range uncertainty dependencies on heterophilic or low-LI graphs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252053/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a graph with node feature vectors and adjacency structure, processed through a semi-supervised node classification setup where a subset of nodes have labels. It produces node class predictions accompanied by both aleatoric uncertainty (via the predictive distribution entropy) and epistemic uncertainty (via the expected entropy over the stochastic trajectory), which together yield an OOD detection score. The key design parameter ν of the Matérn kernel controls the spatial range of uncertainty correlation: low ν yields rough, long-range dependencies suited to low-LI graphs, while high ν yields smooth, short-range dependencies for high-LI graphs.

## Method

The paper proposes a Structure Informed SPDE (SISPDE) that formulates GNN message passing as a stochastic PDE driven by a Φ-Wiener process whose spatial covariance is a Matérn kernel on the graph Laplacian spectrum, rather than the raw Laplacian used in GNSD. This allows explicit control over the smoothness of the noise covariance via the parameter ν, enabling the model to capture both short-range and long-range uncertainty dependencies. The practical implementation is a Graph Neural ODE where at each time step, spatially correlated Gaussian noise ξ_t ~ N(0, t(2ν/κ² + Δ)^{-ν}) is injected through MLP functions F and G, with the Matérn kernel approximated via Chebyshev polynomials for O(m|E|) efficiency. Theoretical contributions include proving the existence and uniqueness of the mild solution to the SPDE, establishing conditions under which the Φ-Wiener process is a valid Q-Wiener process (ν > d), and deriving an error bound for the Chebyshev approximation. Training uses a distributional uncertainty loss based on expected Shannon entropy over the stochastic trajectory of the final node embeddings.

## Results

SISPDE achieves the best or second-best result in 71 out of 72 experimental settings (98.6%), winning outright in 50 cases (69.4%). On the high-LI Cora dataset (Label OOD), SISPDE reaches AUC 96.85 and FPR95 10.87, outperforming GNSD (AUC 95.75, FPR95 19.76) and GNNSafe (AUC 90.74, FPR95 46.86). On the low-LI Minesweeper dataset, SISPDE achieves AUC 64.77 versus GNSD's 57.46, demonstrating a 7.3-point AUC gain where spatial correlation modeling is most critical. Across all 8 datasets and 3 OOD shift types (label, structure, feature), SISPDE outperforms GNSD in 26/27 high-LI cases and 45/45 low-LI cases.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SISPDE consistently outperforms GNSD [26], the closest stochastic message-passing baseline, in 26/27 high-LI cases and all 45/45 low-LI cases, with the largest margins on heterophilic datasets (e.g., +7.3 AUC on Minesweeper, +13.1 AUC on Tolokers). Against GCN [21], SISPDE gains 12.9 AUC on Cora and 10.8 AUC on Minesweeper for Label OOD, confirming that stochastic spatially-correlated noise substantially improves uncertainty calibration over deterministic message passing. Compared to GNNSafe [55] (energy-based OOD), SISPDE achieves higher AUC on all tested datasets (e.g., 96.85 vs. 90.74 on Cora) while also reducing FPR95 by ~36 points. Against non-graph OOD methods (ODIN [25], MSP [17], Mahalanobis [23], OE [18]), SISPDE generally dominates on graph-structured tasks, though on low-LI datasets like Tolokers, non-graph methods (ODIN: AUC 60.76) can be competitive with weaker graph baselines (GCN: 44.43) when the graph structure is uninformative—precisely the regime where SISPDE's Matérn kernel design provides its largest advantage. SISPDE also outperforms GOLD [52] and GEBM [12] on most metrics, with the exception of Pubmed Label OOD where GNSD retains a narrow AUC edge (82.41 vs. 69.49), suggesting the Matérn smoothness parameter ν may require dataset-specific tuning for certain shift types.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the stochastic message passing framework of GNSD [26], which models GNN layers as a stochastic PDE driven by a Q-Wiener process with the graph Laplacian as its covariance operator, by generalizing the noise to a Φ-Wiener process parameterized by the Matérn kernel on graphs [4]. The key conceptual departure is that GNSD's fixed Laplacian-based covariance cannot control spatial correlation smoothness, whereas the Matérn parameter ν explicitly tunes the range of uncertainty dependencies between nodes. The PDE-based message passing lineage traces back to GRAND [5], and the practical implementation follows the Wong-Zakai approximation to convert the SPDE into a randomly forced Graph Neural ODE. The paper's novel positioning is linking the kernel smoothness parameter to label informativeness, arguing that low-LI graphs require rougher (lower ν) covariance structures to capture long-range uncertainty correlations that the Laplacian alone cannot express.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a gap explicitly flagged as future work by multiple prior studies [55, 26, 12]: uncertainty estimation on graphs with low label informativeness, where neighboring nodes' labels provide little information about the center node. The graph rewiring experiment (Section 4.3) offers a useful conceptual insight—that the Matérn covariance kernel implicitly restructures the graph to increase LI by up to 2.94×—suggesting that spatial correlation priors can substitute for missing structural signal. Open questions include scalability beyond the Chebyshev approximation for very large graphs, extending to nonlinear uncertainty dependencies (e.g., mutual information), and whether the framework generalizes to substructure-level or temporal covariance. The theoretical results (existence/uniqueness of mild solutions, Chebyshev error bounds) provide a rigorous foundation but require ν > d for the Q-Wiener property, which constrains the smoothness regime.
