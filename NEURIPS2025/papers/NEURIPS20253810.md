## Problem

Vision-language models become significantly more vulnerable to jailbreak attacks when harmful content is delivered through images rather than text, yet the underlying mechanism remains unclear. Prior work identified distribution shifts in activation space caused by the visual modality but did not explain how these shifts specifically distort safety perception or how to rectify them without degrading visual reasoning utility.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253810/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard vision-language inputs (an RGB image paired with a text prompt) and produces a text response, identical to the base VLM's I/O. During inference, it additionally generates a caption for the image to construct a text-only counterpart, then computes per-layer activations for both the original and caption-based inputs. A small pre-computed safety direction (derived from ~160 safe/unsafe text-only pairs) is stored and used at inference time, requiring no training or fine-tuning.

## Method

ShiftDC is a training-free, inference-time intervention that disentangles the modality-induced activation shift into a safety-relevant component and a safety-irrelevant component. For each layer, it computes the per-sample shift between the vision-language activation and its text-only counterpart, then projects this shift onto a pre-computed safety direction (the difference-in-mean vector between safe and unsafe text-only activations). Only the projected (safety-relevant) component is subtracted from the activation, while the orthogonal residual (safety-irrelevant shift carrying visual semantics) is preserved. This selectively restores the LLM backbone's inherent safety alignment without imposing additional screening or losing fine-grained visual information, distinguishing it from prior activation-calibration methods that apply fixed-scale adjustments or discard visual details entirely.

## Results

On MM-SafetyBench (lower ASR = better defense), ShiftDC reduces attack success rate across all five VLMs and all three attack types (SD, OCR, SD+OCR). For example, on LLaVA-1.5-7B, ShiftDC achieves 38.0% ASR on SD attacks (vs. 45.4% Direct, 40.3% ECSO, 42.6% AdaShield) and 39.7% on OCR (vs. 69.3% Direct). On FigStep, ShiftDC achieves 13.2% ASR on LLaVA-1.5-7B and 8.5% on LLaVA-1.6-34B, compared to 52.4% and 47.6% for the unmodified models. Utility is preserved: on MME, ShiftDC scores 1863.6 vs. 1863.1 (Direct) for LLaVA-1.5-7B, while ECSO drops to 1838.1 and AdaShield to 1854.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ShiftDC outperforms both ECSO and AdaShield on ASR across nearly all model–attack combinations. Against ECSO (image-to-text captioning), ShiftDC achieves lower ASR on 4 of 5 models for SD attacks (e.g., 38.0 vs. 40.3 on LLaVA-1.5-7B) and on all 5 models for OCR attacks (e.g., 39.7 vs. 43.0); on FigStep, ShiftDC wins on 4 of 5 models (e.g., 9.2 vs. 17.8 on ShareGPT4V-7B, 8.5 vs. 11.7 on LLaVA-1.6-34B). Against AdaShield (defensive prompting), ShiftDC is consistently better on SD and OCR attacks for all 5 models and on FigStep for 4 of 5 models (e.g., 13.2 vs. 13.6 on LLaVA-1.5-7B). On utility, ShiftDC matches or marginally exceeds the unmodified model (MME 1863.6 vs. 1863.1; MMBench 64.3 vs. 64.5; MM-Vet 30.4 vs. 30.5 on LLaVA-1.5-7B), whereas ECSO degrades MME by up to 82 points and MM-Vet by up to 11.2 points, and AdaShield degrades MMBench by up to 6.4 points—confirming that ShiftDC's selective removal of only the safety-relevant shift preserves visual reasoning while the baselines sacrifice utility for safety.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the activation/representation engineering line of work for LLM safety (Arditi et al., NeurIPS 2024, who showed refusal is mediated by a single linear direction) to the VLM setting, and builds directly on CMRM (Liu et al., Findings of ACL 2025), which first identified that multimodal inputs shift activations away from text-only distributions. The key departure is a principled disentanglement: rather than subtracting the full modality-induced distribution difference (CMRM) or applying a fixed-magnitude steering vector (InferAligner), ShiftDC projects the per-input modality shift onto the pre-computed safety direction and removes only that component, preserving safety-irrelevant visual semantics. This positions the work as a mechanistic refinement of prior activation-calibration defenses, grounded in a new diagnostic framework (safety perception distortion) that prior methods did not explicitly model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first clear mechanistic account of *why* VLMs are more jailbreakable than their LLM backbones—visual inputs induce a shift that systematically pushes activations toward the 'safe' side of the text-only safety boundary—and shows that even blank images produce this effect, implicating the modality itself rather than image content. Practically, ShiftDC is training-free, requires only ~160 calibration samples and one extra forward pass for captioning, and preserves utility scores (MME, MMBench, MM-Vet) while reducing ASR across five VLMs and three attack benchmarks. Open questions include whether the single safety direction generalizes to larger or differently-aligned backbones, how the method behaves under multi-image or video inputs, and whether the small calibration set (160 samples) is sufficient for diverse safety taxonomies beyond the eight scenarios tested.
