## Problem

Prior work established that a three-layer, single-head transformer can represent the conditional k-gram model for any Markov order k, while a one-layer transformer cannot do so without exponential width. The natural question of whether a two-layer, single-head transformer suffices for arbitrary-order Markov processes remained open, leaving a gap in the tightest known depth characterization for in-context learning of induction heads.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251259/cropped_page3_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a token sequence x_{0:T} drawn from a k-th order Markov chain over a finite vocabulary S and produces the conditional k-gram estimate: the empirical next-token probability distribution given the last k tokens. The embedding dimension is 6S+3, and the architecture uses relative positional encodings, softmax causal attention, and MLP blocks with ReLU activations and layer normalization. The output logits are computed via a linear projection followed by ReLU (rather than softmax), and the construction requires Ω(log T + k) bits per parameter to achieve O(1/T) additive approximation error.

## Method

The key insight is that the MLP between the two attention layers—specifically its ReLU nonlinearity and layer normalization—can isolate the one-hot embedding e_{x_{n-k}} from the first layer's attention output v_n, eliminating the need for a second attention head. The first layer's single head computes v_n (a weighted sum over positions n-1 through n-k using a triadic/3-ary positional encoding), and the MLP then reconstructs u_n (the current k-token context) from v_n, e_{x_n}, and the extracted e_{x_{n-k}} via a linear combination. The second layer acts as a k-th order induction head, computing attention scores proportional to the cosine similarity ⟨u_T, v_n⟩, which equals 1 if and only if the preceding k tokens at position n match the final k tokens. For learning dynamics, the paper proves that a two-stage gradient descent procedure (first optimizing positional scalars, then the attention temperature) on a simplified first-order variant converges to near-optimal loss, demonstrating that the in-context representation is not only expressible but also learnable.

## Results

The paper proves that a two-layer, single-head transformer with embedding dimension 6S+3 can represent the conditional k-gram model for any Markov order k ≥ 1 (Theorem 3), improving upon the prior best of three layers (Rajaraman et al.). For first-order Markov chains, a two-stage gradient descent algorithm on a simplified variant of this architecture achieves near-optimal cross-entropy loss, with the gap to optimal scaling as O(log(T+1)/(T+1)^(cγ)) (Theorem 4). In terms of parameter efficiency, the construction uses 9(6S+3)² + (6S+3)(2T+2S+9) parameters, which is the most compact among all known constructions for arbitrary-order Markov-ICL.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's main contribution is reducing the depth from three layers (Rajaraman et al.) to two layers while maintaining a single attention head per layer, at the cost of a slightly larger MLP. Compared to Rajaraman et al. [26] (3-layer, 1-head), the proposed construction uses 9(6S+3)² vs. 15(6S+3)² quadratic parameters and (2T+2S+9) vs. (3T+2S+10) linear terms, yielding fewer total parameters. Against Nichani et al. [20] (2-layer, k-head), the proposed method requires only one head per layer rather than k heads in the first layer, making it strictly more parameter-efficient as k grows. For first-order processes, Bietti et al. [5] and Edelman et al. [10] also use 2-layer, 1-head architectures, but they do not extend to higher-order Markov chains; the proposed construction subsumes their setting as the k=1 special case. The paper also reveals a depth-width tradeoff: the 3-layer/1-head construction of Rajaraman et al. is equivalent to a 2-layer/2-head architecture (Theorem 2), and the single-head result (Theorem 3) further eliminates the need for multiple heads by leveraging MLP non-linearities (ReLU + LayerNorm) to isolate individual symbols.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of the Markov-ICL line of work (Bietti et al., Edelman et al., Nichani et al., Rajaraman et al.), which studies how transformers learn conditional k-gram models from Markovian sequences. It directly improves upon Rajaraman et al.'s 3-layer, 1-head construction by showing that the same representational power can be achieved with only 2 layers and 1 head, by leveraging MLP non-linearities (ReLU + LayerNorm) to isolate individual symbols from weighted context representations—something prior constructions achieved only through additional attention heads or layers. The gradient descent analysis for first-order chains follows the two-stage reparameterization strategy of Nichani et al., adapted to an architecture with relative positional encodings and softmax attention.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result provides the tightest known depth characterization for in-context learning of conditional k-grams, closing the gap between the known impossibility of 1-layer models and the sufficiency of 3-layer models. The finding that MLP non-linearities are not merely auxiliary but essential for expressing higher-order inductive structures reframes how we think about the division of labor between attention and feed-forward components in transformers. However, the representation result requires infinite attention temperature (κ→∞) and the gradient analysis covers only first-order chains, leaving the learning dynamics for higher-order processes as an important open question. Broader implications include guidance for designing more parameter-efficient transformer architectures for sequence modeling tasks.
