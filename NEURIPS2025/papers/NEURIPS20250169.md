## Problem

Post-trained LLMs develop a narrow generation distribution that external reward models, trained on general data, fail to recognize, causing test-time scaling methods like best-of-N and beam search to offer minimal gains over simple majority voting. This distribution shift between the specialized generator and general verifier remains an unsolved barrier to synergizing post-training improvements with inference-time compute scaling.

## Input / Output

The model takes a math problem as input and generates a chain-of-thought solution ending in a boxed answer. For verification, it receives the problem paired with a candidate solution and produces a step-by-step verification rationale concluding with a binary Yes/No judgment. At inference time, N candidate solutions are sampled, each scored by the probability of the 'Yes' token in the verification prompt, and the final answer is selected via a weighted aggregation combining majority vote count with verification confidence (controlled by hyperparameter α).

## Method

The framework unifies answer generation and verification within a single GRPO reinforcement learning loop, eliminating the need for a separate external verifier. Two key mechanisms stabilize joint training: (1) a policy-aligned buffer that retains only solutions generated within the last T_b training steps, ensuring the verifier learns from outputs representative of the current policy rather than stale early-training generations; and (2) a dynamic verification reward that uses GRPO group statistics (counts of correct vs. incorrect generations) to upweight verification of difficult cases, maintaining expected reward at 1 while focusing learning on challenging verification instances. At inference, the single trained model serves both roles: it generates N solutions and then scores each via the likelihood of the 'Yes' token in a verification prompt, aggregating answers as argmax over candidate answers of the sum of indicator(answer match) × (1 + α·verification_score), which interpolates between majority voting (α=0) and pure verifier selection (α→∞).

## Results

On test-time scaling with a 16-sample budget, Self-Verification-R1-1.5B achieves 93.60% on MATH500, 57.67% on AIME24, 37.67% on AIME25, 92.00% on AMC23, and 50.96% on OlympiadBench, outperforming all baselines on every benchmark. Self-Verification-Qwen-7B reaches 87.20% on MATH500 and 26.67% on AIME24, surpassing self-consistency (86.00%, 23.67%) and all external-verifier methods. In post-training (greedy decoding), Self-Verification-R1-1.5B scores 87.00% on MATH500 and 43.00% on AIME24, exceeding GRPO-only training (87.00%, 37.67%) and DeepScaleR-1.5B-Preview (83.00%, 37.00%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against self-consistency (majority voting), Self-Verification wins on 4 of 5 benchmarks for Qwen-7B (e.g., +1.20 on MATH500, +3.00 on AIME24) and all 5 for R1-1.5B (e.g., +2.60 on MATH500, +5.67 on AIME24, +4.50 on AMC23), with the sole exception being AIME25 where self-consistency edges ahead (21.67 vs. 19.00 for Qwen-7B). External-verifier methods (Best-of-N, Beam Search, DVTS) consistently underperform self-consistency, confirming the distribution-shift hypothesis: for R1-1.5B, Best-of-N trails Self-Verification by 7.60 points on MATH500 and 11.34 on AIME24, while Beam Search lags by 5.20 and 15.00 respectively. On verification accuracy (Table 2), Self-Verification-Qwen-7B achieves 87.20% accuracy and 92.83 F1 on MATH500, surpassing GPT-4o (85.20%, 91.57) and approaching Claude-3.7-Sonnet (90.20%, 94.46) and DeepSeek-v3 (89.00%, 93.73). In post-training, Self-Verification-R1-1.5B outperforms GRPO-only by 5.33 points on AIME24 (43.00 vs. 37.67) and 4.56 on OlympiadBench (44.30 vs. 40.74), and beats DeepScaleR-1.5B-Preview on MATH500 (87.00 vs. 83.00) and AIME24 (43.00 vs. 37.00). The EM 1-shot baseline (Appendix G) is substantially weaker, scoring only 68.8% on MATH500 versus 83.6% for Self-Verification-Qwen-7B.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends GRPO-based RL post-training (from DeepSeekMath/DeepSeek-R1) by jointly training a model for both problem-solving and generative verification within a single RL loop, departing from prior generative verifier work (Zhang et al., 2024) that relied on SFT. The key conceptual departure is identifying a distribution shift between post-trained generators and externally-trained reward models, which motivates replacing external verifiers with a self-verification capability learned in-distribution. The policy-aligned buffer and dynamic verification reward are novel training mechanisms designed to keep the verifier on-policy and focus on difficult cases.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that external reward models actively underperform simple majority voting on post-trained models is a practical warning for the test-time scaling community, suggesting that the common recipe of 'generate with model A, verify with model B' breaks down after RL fine-tuning. The unified single-model approach also offers a clear efficiency advantage (one deployment vs. generator + RM), making it attractive for production inference. However, the framework is currently limited to mathematical reasoning with verifiable answers, and the performance gains over self-consistency are modest (1–3 points), leaving open whether the approach generalizes to open-ended tasks, code generation, or agentic settings where ground-truth verification is unavailable.
