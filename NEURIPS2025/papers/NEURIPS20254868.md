## Problem

Single-cell perturbation prediction models can achieve high average accuracy yet produce unreliable individual predictions, particularly for unseen or functionally distant genes. Existing uncertainty estimates (e.g., GEARS' Monte Carlo dropout variance) are poorly calibrated and fail to jointly capture both model uncertainty (unfamiliarity with the input) and data uncertainty (inherent biological stochasticity), leaving no principled way to flag or filter untrustworthy predictions.

## Input / Output

The model takes a genetic perturbation (single gene or gene combination, encoded via pre-trained scGPT gene embeddings) and the pre-perturbation control cell expression profile as input. Gene expression is projected to a 10-component PCA space. The output is a full Normal-Inverse-Wishart posterior over the post-perturbation expression (yielding a mean vector and covariance matrix) plus a scalar pseudo E-distance uncertainty score that unifies epistemic and aleatoric uncertainty. For out-of-distribution perturbations, the predictive distribution reverts toward the control (null) state, providing a safe fallback.

## Method

PRESCRIBE is a multivariate extension of the Natural Posterior Network built on a Normal-Inverse-Wishart conjugate prior. Its architecture comprises three modules: an encoder that maps the perturbation and control state to a latent embedding, a normalizing flow that estimates the local data density (evidence) in that latent space, and a linear decoder that produces sufficient statistics for the NIW posterior. The key metric, pseudo E-distance, is defined as 2×(normalized posterior evidence) − (normalized predictive entropy), where the evidence term captures epistemic uncertainty (driven to zero for OOD inputs by the flow) and the entropy term captures aleatoric uncertainty. Training uses a composite loss combining expected log-likelihood, entropy regularization, a ListMLE-based E-distance ranking loss to supervise uncertainty ordering, and an uncertainty-regularization term that prevents vanishing gradients in low-evidence regions. The paper proves that under fixed prior conditions, the pseudo E-distance preserves the ordinal ranking of the true E-distance, providing a theoretical guarantee for its use as a unified uncertainty surrogate.

## Results

On the Norman dataset, PRESCRIBE-10% (filtering the 10% least confident predictions) achieves a Pearson correlation of 64.32% and directional accuracy of 64.73%, outperforming scGPT (61.48% / 61.96%) and scFoundation (60.79% / 35.66%). On Replogle2022_Rep1, PRESCRIBE-10% reaches 60.28% r_pred,truth and 67.89% ACC, surpassing scGPT (50.32% / 61.72%) by over 10 points in correlation. On K562, PRESCRIBE-10% achieves 38.58% r_pred,truth and 61.04% ACC, again leading scGPT (32.72% / 57.44%). The uncertainty-guided filtering yields consistent >3% accuracy gains over the best baselines across all three datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PRESCRIBE-10% outperforms all baselines across all three datasets on both r_pred,truth and ACC_pred,truth. Against the strongest baseline scGPT, PRESCRIBE-10% gains +2.84 (Norman), +9.96 (Rep1), and +5.86 (K562) in Pearson correlation. Against scFoundation, the margins are even larger: +3.53, +12.68, and +13.43 respectively. Among uncertainty-aware baselines, GEARS-Ens-10% (the best GEARS variant with 10% dropout) achieves 50.91/48.05/31.29, while PRESCRIBE-10% leads by +13.41, +12.23, and +7.29 in correlation. Notably, PRESCRIBE's unfiltered version (58.38/59.18/36.20) already matches or exceeds scGPT on Rep1 and K562, and the uncertainty-guided filtering provides the decisive edge on Norman. The ablation in Table 3 confirms that random filtering of 10% does not reproduce these gains (58.28 vs. 64.32 on Norman), validating that the improvement comes from the quality of the pseudo E-distance confidence scores rather than mere data removal.

## Relation to Prior Work

PRESCRIBE extends the Natural Posterior Network (NatPN) framework from univariate exponential-family regression to a multivariate Normal-Inverse-Wishart setting, building on the multivariate evidential regression formulation of Meinert et al. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of 'pseudo E-distance' as a unified uncertainty surrogate that simultaneously captures epistemic uncertainty (via latent-space density estimated by a normalizing flow) and aleatoric uncertainty (via predictive distribution entropy), inspired by the energy distance metric from scPerturb. Unlike prior perturbation prediction methods (GEARS, scGPT, CellOracle) that either ignore uncertainty or use poorly calibrated variance estimates (e.g., Monte Carlo dropout), PRESCRIBE provides a single forward-pass confidence score with a provable rank-ordering guarantee relative to the true E-distance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Uncertainty quantification for single-cell perturbation prediction has been largely neglected, yet it is critical for identifying unreliable predictions on unseen or functionally distant genes. PRESCRIBE's practical utility—filtering the 5–10% least confident predictions yields consistent >3% accuracy gains across three datasets—demonstrates that well-calibrated uncertainty can directly improve downstream decision-making in perturbation biology. Limitations include reliance on foundation-model gene embeddings (scGPT), a continuous Gaussian assumption for inherently discrete count data, and an indirect (density-based) proxy for epistemic uncertainty. Open questions include how the choice of reference/null state affects predictions, whether the framework extends to chemical perturbations requiring richer embeddings, and whether more direct epistemic uncertainty representations can replace the normalizing-flow density estimate.
