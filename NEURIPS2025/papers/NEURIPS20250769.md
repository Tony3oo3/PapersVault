## Problem

Existing RAW-based high-level vision systems require full fine-tuning of both ISP modules and backbone networks for each downstream task, leading to catastrophic cross-task degradation and high computational cost. Additionally, data inconsistency arises because different datasets are captured with distinct camera sensors and under varying illumination, making it difficult for a single model to generalize across tasks and lighting conditions without task-specific retraining.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250769/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes minimally processed camera RAW images (Bayer-pattern sensor responses at native bit depth) as input, bypassing the full ISP pipeline. A lightweight pre-processing stage (sensor/illumination mapping plus re-mosaicing) normalizes the RAW before feeding it into a frozen Swin-T backbone. Outputs are task-specific: bounding boxes with class labels for object detection, per-pixel class maps for semantic segmentation, instance-level masks for instance segmentation, and 14-body-joint keypoint positions for pose estimation, all evaluated under normal, over-exposed, and dark lighting conditions.

## Method

Dr. RAW combines two lightweight pre-processing steps with parameter-efficient backbone adaptation. First, sensor and illumination mapping estimates a 3×3 sensor matrix and a 1×3 illumination matrix from the RGB-uv histogram of the demosaiced RAW, followed by a U-shaped re-mosaicing network with channel-splitting gating to reduce sensor- and scene-dependent artifacts. Second, task-specific conditioning is injected via Sensor Prior Prompts (SPP), which are learnable d-dimensional embeddings generated by an FFN from the concatenated mapping matrices and prepended to the image patch embeddings in each transformer layer's self-attention, and via LoRA low-rank matrices (rank r ≪ d) inserted into Q, K, V, and FFN weights. The Swin-T backbone is pretrained on ADE20K RAW and then frozen; only the SPP prompts, LoRA matrices, and task head are updated, reducing tunable parameters to ~29–43% of the total while achieving SOTA across four tasks and nine datasets.

## Results

On object detection (PASCAL RAW), Dr. RAW with adapter tuning achieves 90.4/90.3/89.7 mAP under normal/over-exposed/dark lighting, outperforming fully-tuned RAW-Adapter (89.7/89.0/83.6) and Dirty-Pixel (89.6/89.8/78.5) while tuning only 29% of parameters. On the LOD dataset, Dr. RAW reaches 72.1 mAP, surpassing the strongest fully-tuned baseline (Direct RAW, 67.2) by +4.9 mAP. For semantic segmentation on ADE20K RAW, Dr. RAW attains 46.29/45.28/38.46 mIoU (normal/over-exp/dark) with only 42.9% tunable parameters, beating RAW-Adapter (46.57/44.19/37.62, 100% tunable). On pose estimation (ExLPose) and instance segmentation (LIS), Dr. RAW achieves 37.0 mAP (LL-N) and 41.2 mAP respectively, both SOTA within the unpaired RAW-dark training category.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Dr. RAW (adapter) outperforms all fully-tuned baselines on PASCAL RAW dark lighting by a wide margin: +6.1 mAP over RAW-Adapter (89.7 vs 83.6), +11.2 over Dirty-Pixel (89.7 vs 78.5), and +17.4 over InvISP (89.7 vs 72.3), while tuning only 29% of parameters versus 100% for all competitors. On LOD, Dr. RAW achieves 72.1 mAP, beating Direct RAW fully-tuned (67.2) by +4.9 and Karaimer et al. fully-tuned (62.5) by +9.6. For semantic segmentation, Dr. RAW leads in over-exposed (45.28 vs 44.19 RAW-Adapter) and dark (38.46 vs 37.62) conditions while matching RAW-Adapter closely in normal lighting (46.29 vs 46.57). On pose estimation, Dr. RAW (37.0/30.9/19.6/30.4 mAP) surpasses Direct RAW fully-tuned (36.1/26.9/18.5/29.9) and Karaimer et al. fully-tuned (35.4/29.2/19.1/30.1) across all four low-light testsets. For instance segmentation, Dr. RAW (41.2 mAP) exceeds Direct RAW fully-tuned (40.2) and Karaimer et al. fully-tuned (34.6), and approaches the paired-supervision method Chen et al. (42.7) despite using only unpaired dark RAW training data.

## Relation to Prior Work

Dr. RAW sits at the intersection of parameter-efficient fine-tuning (LoRA, visual prompt tuning) and RAW-based computer vision. Prior RAW perception methods (RAW-Adapter, Dirty-Pixel, ReconfigISP, AdaptiveISP) treat ISP optimization and backbone tuning as a coupled, task-specific problem requiring full end-to-end training. Dr. RAW decouples these concerns: lightweight sensor/illumination mapping and re-mosaicing handle data inconsistency at the input stage, while Sensor Prior Prompts (adapted from visual prompt tuning) and LoRA (adapted from NLP) inject task-specific knowledge into a frozen, RAW-pretrained Swin Transformer backbone. The sensor mapping and illumination estimation design directly follows Afifi & Brown's two-stage white-balance formulation, repurposed as a learnable pre-processing block rather than a fixed ISP stage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is that a single frozen RAW-pretrained backbone can be adapted to multiple high-level tasks (detection, segmentation, pose) by swapping lightweight adapter modules (~29% of parameters), eliminating the need to retrain full networks per task. The zero-shot generalization to unseen camera sensors (only ~2 mAP drop) suggests the pre-processing + SPP design genuinely captures sensor-invariant structure rather than overfitting to a specific sensor. Open questions include whether this approach scales to truly diverse sensor types (e.g., automotive vs. smartphone), whether a single multi-task RAW foundation model is feasible (the authors flag this as future work), and whether the RAW pretraining requirement can be further relaxed. The modest accuracy gains over RAW-Adapter (1–3 mAP) mean the primary value is efficiency and flexibility rather than raw performance.
