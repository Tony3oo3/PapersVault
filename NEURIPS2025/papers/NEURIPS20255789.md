## Problem

Existing Masked Graph Autoencoders for molecular pretraining use fixed mask ratios and hard-label reconstruction objectives that are predetermined and lack dynamic adaptability during training. Moreover, these proxy tasks often violate chemical priors: a uniform mask ratio ignores per-molecule structural redundancy, masking does not prioritize chemically important atoms, and forcing a unique reconstruction target for masked atoms produces conflicting self-supervised signals when multiple valid reconstructions exist.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255789/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 2D molecular graphs (atoms as nodes with 124-dim features, bonds as edges with 12-dim features) from ZINC15 (2M molecules) during pretraining. GIBMS outputs a per-node importance score p_i ∈ [0,1] that determines a molecule-specific mask ratio and which atoms to mask, while SLG outputs a probability distribution over n=128 learnable prototypes rather than a single hard token label. The final pretrained encoder produces node embeddings and a pooled graph-level representation used for downstream molecular property prediction, drug-target affinity, and quantum chemistry tasks.

## Method

DyCC comprises two model-agnostic modules. GIBMS extends the supervised Graph Information Bottleneck to the unsupervised pretraining setting by replacing the unavailable downstream label with a self-supervised contrastive signal (InfoNCE over two Bernoulli-sampled IB-graph views), justified via a multi-view mutual information lower bound. It computes per-node retention probabilities via an MLP on GNN embeddings, applies Gumbel-Sigmoid for differentiable Bernoulli sampling, and injects Gaussian noise into low-importance nodes to form a compressed IB-graph, yielding an adaptive, molecule-specific mask ratio that preferentially masks key atoms. SLG replaces hard-label reconstruction with prototype-based soft label assignment: both ground-truth token embeddings and encoder predictions are mapped to probability distributions over n learnable prototypes via temperature-scaled softmax of cosine similarities, and the training loss minimizes cross-entropy between these two distributions plus a mean-entropy-maximization regularizer. Because prototypes are updated during training, the effective reconstruction difficulty shifts dynamically—converging toward one-hot (easy) or uniform (hard) distributions—eliminating the need for a fixed tokenizer and reducing reliance on specific tokenization strategies.

## Results

On the eight MoleculeNet classification datasets (scaffold split), SimSGT-DyCC achieves the best average ROC-AUC of 74.1%, surpassing the no-pretrain baseline by 10.4% and the strongest non-DyCC baseline (GraphMVP, 72.6%) by 1.5 points. Integrating DyCC into AttrMask improves its average from 70.8% to 73.7% (+2.9), Mole-BERT from 72.2% to 73.8% (+1.6), and SimSGT from 73.0% to 74.1% (+1.1). On regression tasks (Table 5), SimSGT-G-DyCC achieves 0.944 average RMSE on molecular property prediction and 0.198 average MSE on drug-target affinity, both outperforming the original SimSGT-G (0.965 and 0.204).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SimSGT-DyCC (74.1% avg ROC-AUC) outperforms all 14 non-DyCC baselines, with the largest margins over GraphMVP (+1.5), GraphMAE (+1.8), and MGSSL (+1.8), and a particularly large gap over GPT-GNN (+5.6) and G-Contextual (+4.8). Among the three MGAEs that DyCC is integrated into, the improvement is most pronounced for AttrMask (+2.9 points), which originally used the simplest atom-level tokenizer, while SimSGT (already using K-hop subgraph tokens) gains a more modest +1.1. Notably, DyCC narrows the performance gap between the three MGAEs: without DyCC, AttrMask trails SimSGT by 2.2 points, but with DyCC the gap shrinks to only 0.4 points, indicating reduced reliance on tokenizer design. On regression tasks (Table 5), SimSGT-G-DyCC beats GraphMVP (0.944 vs. 1.022 avg RMSE) and Mole-BERT (0.944 vs. 1.024) on molecular property prediction, and also leads on drug-target affinity (0.198 vs. 0.225 MSE for GraphMVP). On quantum chemistry (Table 7), SimSGT-DyCC achieves the best MAE on QM8 (0.0180) and QM9 (5.60), though it slightly trails GraphMAE on QM7 (77.6 vs. 78.4).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Graph Information Bottleneck (GIB) framework from Yu et al. [52, 51]—originally designed for supervised subgraph recognition—into the unsupervised pretraining setting by replacing downstream labels with self-supervised contrastive signals under a multi-view assumption. It also adapts the prototype-based soft label assignment mechanism from DINO [7] and its successors [4, 2] to the molecular graph reconstruction context, replacing hard token labels with learnable prototype distributions. The resulting DyCC framework is positioned as a model-agnostic plug-in for existing MGAEs (AttrMask, MoleBert, SimSGT), departing from their fixed masking ratios and predetermined tokenizers by introducing per-molecule adaptive masking (GIBMS) and dynamic soft-label reconstruction (SLG).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The model-agnostic design means DyCC can be integrated into any MGAE without architectural changes, and the consistent improvements across AttrMask, MoleBert, and SimSGT suggest broad applicability. A notable practical finding is that DyCC substantially narrows the performance gap between different tokenizers, reducing the need for careful tokenizer selection—a common pain point in molecular pretraining. The key limitation is the reliance on the multi-view assumption (ε_info negligible), which the authors acknowledge may not always hold; whether the GIBMS module's contrastive prediction term is the optimal unsupervised proxy for task-relevant information remains an open question. Extension to 3D molecular representations and larger-scale pretraining corpora would further validate the approach.
