## Problem

Optimizing decision tree policies in MDPs is NP-hard, and the existing exact method (OMDT) encodes the entire problem as a single monolithic MILP that becomes intractable beyond ~10^4 states. The paper addresses the scalability gap by asking whether one can retain global optimality guarantees at each iteration while dramatically reducing the computational cost of finding the optimal tree policy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253906/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a discrete MDP (state set, action set, transition probabilities, rewards, discount factor), per-state feature vectors in [0,1]^F, a tree depth D, and a sequence of thresholds {φ_l} controlling which tree parameters are tunable per iteration. It outputs a deterministic decision tree policy mapping states to actions (with at most 2^D leaves) and the corresponding value function. A warm-start variant (SPOT+WS) additionally accepts a partial OMDT solution as initialization, and the algorithm retains the best policy across all iterations by expected return.

## Method

SPOT replaces OMDT's single monolithic MILP with a policy-iteration loop: at each step, the value function V^old is fixed, and the tree-policy optimization is reformulated as a two-stage stochastic program where first-stage variables (a, b, c, d) encode the shared tree structure and second-stage variables (z, μ) are state-specific allocations. This decoupling makes the problem decomposable across states, enabling parallel solving. A reduced-space branch-and-bound (RSBB) algorithm branches exclusively on tree-structure variables rather than all integer variables, and a closed-form decomposable upper bound—obtained by relaxing the non-anticipativity constraint so each state can choose its own tree—provides tight bounds in O(|T_B|+|T_L|) per state. Each iteration performs an exact greedy policy improvement (Proposition 1), and under the assumption that the tree class can represent the optimal policy with strictly positive occupancy weights, SPOT converges to the global optimum (Proposition 2).

## Results

On 9 benchmark MDPs with tree depth D=3, SPOT+WS (SPOT with OMDT warm start) achieves the highest normalized return on 7 of 9 instances, with dramatic gains on challenging MDPs: +1201.7% on firewire (0.049 → 0.638) and +505.95% on csma_2_2 (0.00047 → 0.00287). Vanilla SPOT (without warm start) is more mixed, underperforming OMDT on csma_2_2 (−49.5%) and firewire (−120.5%) but matching or exceeding it on the remaining instances. On simpler MDPs (sys_ad_1, wlan0, wlan1), all three methods achieve comparable returns within ~1%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is OMDT (Vos & Verwer, IJCAI 2023), which formulates the optimal decision-tree policy problem as a single monolithic MILP solved with a standard solver (Gurobi). SPOT+WS decisively outperforms OMDT on the most challenging MDPs: firewire (+1201.7%), csma_2_2 (+505.95%), and csma_2_4 (+100.99%), where OMDT's single-MILP approach struggles to find good solutions within the 60-minute budget. On moderate MDPs (sys_ad_2, tiger_vs_ant, wlan1), SPOT+WS matches or slightly exceeds OMDT. However, SPOT+WS underperforms on tiger_vs_ant (−19.7%) and is marginally worse on sys_ad_1 (−0.22%), suggesting the warm-start strategy can occasionally lock in a suboptimal tree structure. Vanilla SPOT without warm start is inconsistent: it beats OMDT on 5 of 9 instances but falls significantly short on csma_2_2 and firewire, highlighting that the iterative decomposition alone is insufficient without a good initial solution for harder problems.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the exact-optimization line of work on decision tree policies in MDPs initiated by OMDT (Vos & Verwer, IJCAI 2023), which formulated the problem as a single monolithic MIP. The key departure is a policy-iteration decomposition that replaces one large MILP solve with a sequence of smaller, independently solvable subproblems, each addressed via a reduced-space branch-and-bound (RSBB) framework inherited from Hua et al. (NeurIPS 2022) and Cao & Zavala (2019). This structural decoupling of MDP dynamics from tree-structure constraints enables parallel search across states, a capability absent in OMDT's coupled formulation. The paper also positions itself against relaxation-based (Gupta et al., Topin et al.) and imitation-based (VIPER, DAgger) approaches, arguing that direct tree optimization avoids the imitation gap when the optimal policy is tree-representable.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for safety-critical domains where interpretable policies are mandatory: SPOT achieves an order-of-magnitude runtime speedup over OMDT (e.g., 11,528s → 16s on firewire) and scales to MDPs with ~8,000 states where the monolithic MIP approach fails within reasonable time. However, the warm-start strategy (SPOT+WS) shows highly inconsistent gains—dramatic improvements on some benchmarks (1201% on firewire) but regressions on others (−19.7% on tiger_vs_ant at D=3)—suggesting the approach's effectiveness is problem-dependent. Open questions include whether solver-independent acceleration or approximate formulations can extend applicability to very large-scale MDPs, and whether the policy-iteration decomposition can be made more robust to the inconsistent warm-start behavior observed across environments.
