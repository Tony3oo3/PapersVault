## Problem

Enumerating the Rashomon set of sparse decision trees—near-optimal trees within a tolerance of the best—is essential for variable importance analysis, fairness evaluation, and stakeholder-driven model selection, but the state-of-the-art (TreeFARMS) requires a pre-specified tolerance, produces trees in arbitrary order, and is restricted to classification. Without ordered enumeration, users cannot obtain the highest-quality trees early (anytime behavior), must guess the tolerance a priori, and cannot stop after collecting a fixed number of top-ranked models.

## Input / Output

SORTD takes a binary-feature tabular dataset, a maximum depth budget d, a complexity penalty λ, and either a Rashomon multiplier ε or a maximum tree count n^T (specifying either suffices). It produces a sorted list of sparse decision trees in non-decreasing order of the regularized objective value, so the best trees appear first. For post-evaluation, the user supplies a leaf cost function and a combining operator (e.g., for equality-of-opportunity), and SORTD returns per-tree tuples that can be filtered to a Pareto front without re-enumeration.

## Method

SORTD operates in two phases: Phase 1 computes the globally optimal tree via the STreeD dynamic-programming framework and caches optimal subproblem solutions; Phase 2 constructs a search tree where each node maintains a sorted solution list and a pointer to its next unprocessed solution. The core mechanism is a best-first, lazy enumeration: a branching node computes its next solution by performing an iterative sorted Cartesian sum over the solution lists of its left and right child nodes, incrementing indices and using a min-heap of candidate index pairs to discover the next-best combination. A specialized depth-two subroutine exploits precomputed feature-pair frequency counts to enumerate all remaining-depth-two trees in bulk, dramatically reducing runtime for deeper budgets. Upper bounds propagated from the Rashomon bound prune helper nodes that can no longer contribute valid solutions, and the framework generalizes to any separable, totally ordered objective for enumeration and any separable, partially ordered objective for post-hoc evaluation.

## Results

SORTD achieves geometric mean runtime speed-ups of 86.95× (depth 4) and 24.30× (depth 5) over TreeFARMS at λ=0.001, with individual speed-ups reaching two orders of magnitude. SORTD uses up to one order of magnitude less memory (most instances below 1 GB vs. substantially higher for TreeFARMS). For variable importance via LOFO, 26 of 29 datasets achieve Jaccard index ≥ 0.8 between top-100 and top-10,000 trees, and Kendall's τ ≥ 0.7 holds for 17 datasets, indicating stable importance estimates from small Rashomon subsets. In the regression experiment, SORTD finds orders of magnitude more trees within the 10% Rashomon bound than both CART and STreeD within a one-minute time limit.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against TreeFARMS [30] (the state-of-the-art for Rashomon set enumeration of sparse decision trees), SORTD wins decisively on runtime: at depth 4 with λ=0.001, TreeFARMS times out (>300 s) on 6 of 30 datasets while SORTD completes all in under 5 s, yielding a geometric mean speed-up of 86.95×; at depth 5 the gap widens further (24.30× geometric mean) as TreeFARMS hits the time limit on nearly all high-dimensional datasets. SORTD also uses up to one order of magnitude less memory. In the regression experiment (Fig. 7), SORTD finds orders of magnitude more trees within the 10% Rashomon bound than both the heuristic CART [2] and the optimal single-tree method STreeD [40], which each find only a handful of trees in the same one-minute window. For the fairness post-evaluation (equality of opportunity), SORTD is the only method that can enumerate and score the full Rashomon set, whereas STreeD can only produce a single optimal tree per fairness constraint. Overall, SORTD dominates all three baselines on the primary metric (runtime to enumerate the full Rashomon set) and uniquely provides anytime, in-order enumeration.

## Relation to Prior Work

SORTD builds directly on the STreeD dynamic programming framework [40] for computing optimal sparse decision trees and adapts the depth-two frequency-count subroutine and caching mechanism from Murtree [16]. Its primary departure is from TreeFARMS [30], the prior state-of-the-art for Rashomon set enumeration, which uses depth-first search and requires a pre-specified tolerance without preserving solution order. SORTD replaces this with a best-first search over a tree of sorted solution lists (using iterative Cartesian-sum merging at branching nodes) and a specialized depth-two solver, yielding in-order enumeration with anytime termination. The framework also generalizes beyond classification to any separable, totally ordered objective and supports post-hoc evaluation with partially ordered objectives such as fairness metrics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The anytime property—guaranteeing the best trees are produced first—removes the need to guess a Rashomon multiplier and enables practical early stopping, which is critical given that even modest feature counts and depth budgets can yield sets exceeding 10^12 trees. The two-orders-of-magnitude speedup and reduced memory usage make Rashomon set exploration feasible on high-dimensional datasets where TreeFARMS times out. Open questions include theoretical guidelines for the minimum number of trees needed for stable variable-importance estimates, extension beyond binary features, and whether the in-order enumeration advantage persists for non-additive or non-separable objectives. The NP-hardness of even single-tree optimization remains a fundamental ceiling on scalability for large depth budgets or dataset sizes.
