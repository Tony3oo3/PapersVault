## Problem

Current multimodal large language models for visual reasoning operate on static, fully observable images, making them ineffective in real-world physical environments where occlusion, containment, or limited field of view leave critical information hidden. Existing embodied QA and exploration methods either rely on pre-captured passive observations or optimize for task completion rather than targeted information acquisition, failing to close the perception-reasoning-action loop that humans use to resolve uncertainty through strategic interaction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250136/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each interaction step, the model receives a growing sequence of RGB observations (384×384 resolution, processed with a max-pooling layer that reduces visual tokens by 3× to handle multi-image inputs efficiently), the question text, and 5–8 candidate action options (e.g., Pick, Move Viewer, Rotate Viewer, Move Object) that include both informative and distractor choices. The output is a structured Chain-of-Thought trace covering goal analysis, visual reasoning over the observation history, uncertainty hypothesis, information gain prediction, and planning pseudocode, culminating in either an action tag (<action>E</action>) or a final answer tag (<answer>A/B/C/D/E/F...</answer>).

## Method

PhysVLM-AVR builds on the LLaVA architecture (Qwen2.5-3B LLM + SigLIP-400M visual encoder) with a max-pooling modification for efficient multi-image processing, trained via a four-stage curriculum: connector alignment, single-image understanding, comprehensive visual understanding, and finally general reasoning plus active visual reasoning. The key technical contribution is the AVR-152k dataset, whose core subset AVR-Core (2k samples from 640 real-world tabletop settings collected via UMI devices) models AVR as a higher-order Markov Decision Process with expert-annotated CoT traces that explicitly supervise three sub-skills: identifying information uncertainty, predicting action-conditioned information gain, and selecting information-maximizing actions. This MDP formulation, combined with the CoT supervision, teaches the model to iteratively assess whether current observations suffice, forecast which candidate action would most reduce uncertainty, and commit to either an action or a final answer—capabilities that ablations show are indispensable (removing AVR-Core drops ACC_ISJ from 90.5% to 16.4% and ACC_FA from 39.7% to 2.3%).

## Results

On the CLEVR-AVR benchmark, PhysVLM-AVR-3B achieves 90.5% Information Sufficiency Judgment Accuracy (ACC_ISJ), surpassing GPT-4o (88.4%), and 39.7% Final Answer Accuracy (ACC_FA), the best among open-source models. The fine-tuned AVR-Qwen2.5-VL-7B reaches 89.3% ACC_ISJ and 38.1% ACC_FA. All existing open-source MLLMs and embodied MLLMs score near-zero on these metrics (e.g., LLaVA-OV-7B: 0%, Embodied-Reasoner-7B: 20.2% ACC_ISJ but only 1.6% ACC_FA), confirming that passive visual reasoning does not transfer to active, interactive settings. On GeoMath and Geometry3K, PhysVLM-AVR-3B also achieves the highest accuracy among all compared models, outperforming GPT-4o and specialized reasoning models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PhysVLM-AVR-3B decisively outperforms all open-source MLLM baselines: LLaVA-OV-7B and Reason-RFT-7B score near-zero across all metrics, while Qwen2.5-VL-7B and R1-Onevision-7B reach only ~4–5% ACC_ISJ and <3% ACC_FA. Among embodied MLLMs, Embodied-Reasoner-7B is the strongest baseline (20.2% ACC_ISJ) but collapses to 1.6% ACC_FA, revealing that detecting information incompleteness does not translate into effective action; RoboBrain-7B is even weaker (3.6% ACC_ISJ, 1.6% ACC_FA). Against API-based models, PhysVLM-AVR-3B surpasses GPT-4o on ACC_ISJ (90.5% vs. 88.4%) but trails on IGR (29.9% vs. 50.8%) and ACC_FA (39.7% vs. 45.7%), indicating that while our model excels at recognizing when information is insufficient, GPT-4o still selects more informative actions and integrates multi-step observations more effectively. Gemini-2.0-flash is substantially weaker than GPT-4o on all three metrics (53.2/31.3/30.2 vs. 88.4/50.8/45.7). The ablation further confirms that removing AVR-Core drops ACC_ISJ from 90.5% to 16.4% and ACC_FA from 39.7% to 2.3%, while removing only the CoT annotations halves ACC_ISJ to 47.6%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the LLaVA architecture (with a max-pooling modification for multi-image efficiency) but departs fundamentally from the static visual reasoning paradigm by formulating Active Visual Reasoning (AVR) as a higher-order MDP where reasoning drives sequential, information-maximizing actions. It positions itself against three existing lines of work: static visual reasoning (CLEVR, CLEVR-Math) that assumes full observability; embodied QA (OpenEQA, RoboVQA) that reasons over pre-captured sequences without closed-loop information seeking; and embodied exploration methods that optimize task completion rather than targeted information gain. The key conceptual departure is the explicit coupling of uncertainty assessment, action-conditioned information gain prediction, and strategic action selection within a single iterative reasoning trace, formalized through the information gain objective in Equation 2.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's central empirical finding—that current embodied MLLMs can detect information incompleteness (20.2% ACC_ISJ for Embodied-Reasoner-7B) yet almost never translate this into correct answers (1.6% ACC_FA)—reveals a specific and previously unarticulated gap in embodied reasoning: the ability to strategically act on recognized uncertainty. The CLEVR-AVR benchmark and AVR-152k dataset (particularly the 2k-sample AVR-Core with expert CoT annotations in a higher-order MDP) provide the first dedicated infrastructure for studying this gap. However, the absolute performance remains modest (39.7% vs. GPT-4o's 45.7% on final accuracy), the benchmark is simulation-based and limited to tabletop CLEVR-style scenes, and the large ACC_ISJ-to-ACC_FA gap (90.5% → 39.7%) indicates that optimal multi-step action selection and temporal integration remain largely unsolved. Open questions include whether these capabilities transfer to open-world, non-simulated environments and whether the CoT supervision approach scales beyond the 2k expert-annotated samples.
