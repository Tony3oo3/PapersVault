## Problem

SAM2's Memory Attention mechanism provides strong dense feature matching and mask generation, but its representations are entangled with instance-level tracking cues, causing severe performance drops on few-shot segmentation tasks with high semantic shift (e.g., cruise ship vs. rowboat). The paper investigates whether SAM2's class-agnostic pretraining has implicitly learned semantic-aware representations that can be disentangled through lightweight transformations, enabling a unified model for both video object segmentation and few-shot segmentation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253969/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes K reference images each paired with a flexible annotation (pixel mask, point, box, or scribble) and one unannotated target image, which are concatenated into a pseudo-video processed sequentially through SAM2's streaming pipeline. The output is a per-pixel segmentation mask for the target image. A key design choice is that reference frames are encoded into the Memory Bank without undergoing cross-attention with each other, ensuring target predictions are invariant to reference ordering, and the model natively supports variable k-shots without architectural modification.

## Method

SANSA repurposes SAM2's prompt-and-propagate framework by reinterpreting the temporal video dimension as a collection of semantically related images, shifting Memory Attention from instance-level visual similarity to semantic-level concept tracking. To make SAM2's latent semantic structure explicit, AdaptFormer bottleneck adapters (down-project → ReLU → up-project) are inserted into the last two layers of the frozen Hiera-Large Image Encoder, with only ~10M parameters trained while the entire SAM2 backbone and decoder remain frozen. A novel episodic training objective inverts the standard k-shot setup: a single labeled reference propagates to J unlabeled targets, and each intermediate prediction is encoded back into the Memory Bank as a pseudo-reference for subsequent frames, encouraging robust semantic correspondences. Supervision uses binary cross-entropy plus Dice loss on all target predictions. The ablations show that low-capacity bottleneck adapters outperform both full fine-tuning and higher-capacity adapters (e.g., MONA), confirming that constrained transformations best expose SAM2's latent semantics without overfitting.

## Results

In the strict few-shot segmentation setting (1-shot), SANSA achieves 48.8 mIoU on LVIS-92i, 60.2 mIoU on COCO-20i, and 91.4 mIoU on FSS-1000, outperforming the best trained competitor SegIC by +8.3%, +6.3%, and +1.2% respectively. In the 5-shot setting, SANSA reaches 53.9, 64.3, and 92.1 mIoU on the same benchmarks, surpassing DiffewS by +10.2% on LVIS-92i. SANSA uses only 234M parameters (10M trainable) and runs 3× faster than GF-SAM, while also achieving +13.6% mIoU over GF-SAM on the challenging LVIS-92i benchmark.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SANSA consistently outperforms all 19 compared baselines on the primary strict FSS benchmarks. Against the strongest trained competitor SegIC (1.2B params), SANSA gains +8.3% on LVIS-92i and +6.3% on COCO-20i (1-shot) while using 5× fewer parameters. Versus the best training-free pipeline GF-SAM, SANSA achieves +13.6% on LVIS-92i and +3.4% on FSS-1000, though it trails by −2.5% on COCO-20i 5-shot. Against DiffewS (the best single-model trained method), SANSA leads by +14.9% on LVIS-92i and +8.0% on COCO-20i (1-shot). Specialist methods (AMFormer, HMNet) are outperformed by +9.2% and +8.1% on COCO-20i respectively. In the generalist in-context setting (Table 2), SANSA with LVIS+PACO training reaches 49.1 mIoU on Pascal-Part and 43.0 on PACO-Part, surpassing GF-SAM by +4.6% and +6.7% respectively, and outperforming all trained generalist models (SegIC, DiffewS, SINE, SegGPT, Painter) by large margins on part segmentation. The only notable loss is the −2.5% gap to GF-SAM on COCO-20i 5-shot, where the training-free method's DINOv2-based matching retains an edge on coarse-grained categories with abundant training data.

## Relation to Prior Work

SANSA repurposes SAM2's Memory Attention mechanism—originally designed for tracking a single object across temporally contiguous video frames—by reinterpreting the temporal axis as a collection of semantically related images, thereby converting an object-tracking pipeline into a semantic-tracking one for few-shot segmentation. This departs from the dominant modular paradigm (DINOv2 for matching + SAM for decoding, as in Matcher, GF-SAM, VRP-SAM) and from single-model alternatives that rely on diffusion features (DiffewS) or DINOv2 with a separate decoder (SegIC), by exploiting the fact that SAM2 already unifies dense feature matching and high-quality mask generation in one architecture. The conceptual bridge to self-supervised learning—where instance-level invariance across views elicits semantic structure—motivates the use of minimal AdaptFormer bottleneck adapters to disentangle latent semantics from tracking-specific cues, rather than the heavier fine-tuning or multi-model pipelines typical of prior FSS work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a model trained purely for class-agnostic video tracking already encodes hierarchical semantic structure (from coarse categories to fine-grained part-level distinctions) that can be made explicit with only ~10M trainable parameters, while preserving SAM2's state-of-the-art VOS and promptable segmentation capabilities. This has practical implications for annotation pipelines: SANSA is 3× faster and 4–5× smaller than competing FSS methods, supports diverse prompts (points, scribbles, boxes) to avoid costly pixel-level masks, and generalizes across domain/style shifts inherited from frozen SAM2. Open questions include whether cross-instance contextual relationships can be exploited for multi-class FSS, whether the semantic disentanglement transfers to other foundation models beyond the SAM2 family, and how the approach scales to very large vocabulary or open-vocabulary settings.
