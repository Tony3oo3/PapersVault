## Problem

In fair representation learning, the three standard fairness criteria—independence (demographic parity), separation (equalized odds), and calibration—are provably incompatible whenever the target variable Y depends on the sensitive attribute S, yet existing approaches either attempt to approximate all three simultaneously (contradicting impossibility results) or require heuristic selection among mutually exclusive constraints. This leaves practitioners without a principled, quantitative framework to measure how much predictive accuracy is sacrificed when enforcing each criterion, or to determine which criterion is appropriate given the underlying data bias.

## Input / Output

The method operates on tabular data with a feature vector X, a binary sensitive attribute S ∈ {0,1}, and a binary target Y ∈ {0,1}. The encoder f maps X to a representation Z in a kernel space, and a task head h: Z → [0,1] produces the prediction Ŷ ~ Bernoulli(h(Z)). The key intermediate object is the Y-reweighted mixture distribution Z̄^(s) = p_{0|0}·Z_s^0 + p_{1|0}·Z_s^1 for each sensitive group s, from which the EO_k score is computed as an MMD between the two group-specific mixtures; the empirical estimator requires stratified bootstrap resampling to obtain i.i.d. samples from these mixtures.

## Method

The core contribution is EO_k, defined as the MMD between Y-reweighted mixture distributions across sensitive groups, where the supremum is taken over an affine image of the unit ball in a reproducing kernel Hilbert space (RKHS) that forms the feasible set of admissible predictors h: Z → [0,1]. This construction yields a unified, context-adaptive characterization: in the unbiased regime (Y ⊥ S), minimizing EO_k simultaneously enforces both demographic parity and equalized odds; in the biased regime (Y ⊥̸ S), EO_k preserves the Bayes-optimal predictor while providing explicit lower bounds on the unavoidable DP and calibration violations, with the bound scaling with the dataset bias |p_{0|0} − p_{0|1}| and the representation's predictive power β. The empirical estimator EO_k̂ is a closed-form kernel statistic computable in O(n²) time (or O(n) with linear-time approximations), and a uniform concentration inequality guarantees convergence to the population EO_k at rate O(n^{-1/2}) for feed-forward neural network encoders. In practice, EO_k̂² is used as a penalty term in the FRL objective argmin_{h,f} L_sup(h∘f) + λ·EO_k̂², enabling gradient-based optimization with hyperparameter-free convergence of the fairness constraint.

## Results

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical experiments or datasets. The key quantitative results are theoretical bounds: (1) In the unbiased setting (Y⊥S), EO_k exactly equals the supremum of Demographic Parity over the RKHS-feasible predictor class (Theorem 2.5, Eq. 11); (2) In the biased setting (Y⊄S), DP is lower bounded by |p_{0|0}−p_{0|1}|·β − EO_k (Theorem 2.5, Eq. 12), where β quantifies optimal balanced accuracy; (3) The uniform concentration inequality (Theorem 2.10) shows the empirical estimator converges at rate O(√(log(d)+log(δ⁻¹))/(n₀+n₁)) for feed-forward neural network encoders, with a logarithmic dependence on input dimension d.

## Baselines & Comparisons

No empirical baselines are compared, as the paper contains no experiments. Theoretically, the paper positions EO_k against three existing fairness criteria: Demographic Parity (DP, Dwork et al. 2012), Equalized Odds (EO, Hardt et al. 2016), and Calibration (DC, Shen et al. 2022). The key finding is that EO_k simultaneously recovers both DP and EO in the unbiased regime (Y⊥S), while in the biased regime (Y⊄S) it uniquely preserves the Bayes-optimal predictor (achieving Pr(Ŷ*=Y)=1) at the cost of a quantifiable, irreducible DP violation of at least |p_{0|0}−p_{0|1}|·β. In contrast, enforcing DP or DC in the biased setting necessarily destroys predictive accuracy. The paper also contrasts with Shen et al. (2022) and Jang et al. (2024), arguing that their composite metrics cannot be minimized to zero under distributional bias due to the fundamental incompatibility of the TVD terms they combine.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the MMD framework (Gretton et al., 2012) and the concentration inequality results of Ni and Huo (2024) to construct a kernel-based quantification of the Equalized Odds constraint originally proposed by Hardt et al. (2016). It departs from prior attempts (Shen et al., 2022; Jang et al., 2024) to simultaneously approximate multiple group fairness notions by arguing that such joint optimization conflicts with the impossibility results of Kleinberg (2018) and Hardt et al. (2016). Instead, the paper positions EO_k as a context-adaptive metric that automatically recovers Demographic Parity in unbiased settings (Y⊥S) while preserving the Bayes-optimal predictor in biased settings (Y⊥̸S), thereby acknowledging rather than resolving the fundamental incompatibility among DP, EO, and calibration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, theoretically grounded answer to the long-standing question of which fairness constraint to enforce when they are mutually incompatible, showing that a single kernel-based statistic can navigate the trade-off without heuristic selection. However, the work is entirely theoretical with no empirical validation, leaving open whether the O(n²) (or O(n) approximate) estimator performs well in practice on real-world datasets with high-dimensional or imbalanced features. A key open question is whether the RKHS unit-ball feasible set is expressive enough for deep representation learning in practice, and whether the concentration bounds are tight enough to be useful as fairness certificates at realistic sample sizes.
