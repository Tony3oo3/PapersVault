## Problem

Convex smooth surrogate losses (e.g., logistic, squared) are preferred for efficient optimization and estimation, but they have been believed to inherently suffer from suboptimal square-root surrogate regret bounds, while losses achieving the optimal linear regret rate (e.g., hinge, polyhedral) lack smoothness or convexity. This paper overcomes this apparent impossibility for arbitrary discrete target losses by constructing a family of convex smooth surrogates that retain a linear regret rate.

## Input / Output

The loss takes a continuous score vector θ ∈ R^d (where d can be far smaller than the number of classes K via a (ρ, ℓ^ρ)-decomposition) and a discrete label y, producing a scalar loss value. The associated prediction link is a π-argmax: it solves an infimal-convolution optimization over the probability simplex Δ^N to obtain a weight vector π, then selects the prediction t with the largest π_t. As a by-product, ∇Ω*(θ* + L^ρ π) at the surrogate risk minimizer yields a Fisher-consistent estimate of the linear property E[ρ(y)], recovering class probabilities under the trivial one-hot encoding.

## Method

The construction starts from a strongly convex base negentropy Ω (e.g., Shannon negentropy) and augments it with the negative Bayes risk T(p) = −min_t ⟨p, ℓ^ρ(t)⟩ to form the convolutional negentropy Ω_T(p) = Ω(p) + T(p). The induced Fenchel-Young loss has conjugate Ω_T*(θ) = inf_{π∈Δ^N} Ω*(θ + L^ρ π), an infimal convolution that encodes the target loss structure into the score space. The key mechanism for the linear regret bound is an additive decomposition: the surrogate regret splits into a non-negative Fenchel-Young risk plus a convex combination of target regrets, so the target regret is bounded by N times the surrogate regret. The constant N is then tightened to affdim(L^ρ)+1 via a Carathéodory-type argument exploiting the low-rank geometry of the loss matrix. Smoothness follows from strong convexity of Ω, and the gradient is computed via an envelope theorem that reduces to evaluating ∇Ω*(θ + L^ρ π) for any minimizer π.

## Results

The paper's primary contribution is theoretical: Theorem 15 establishes that for any discrete target loss with a (ρ, ℓ^ρ)-decomposition, there exists a convex smooth surrogate loss (the convolutional Fenchel-Young loss) with a linear surrogate regret bound ψ(r) = Nr, overcoming the previously believed impossibility of combining convexity, smoothness, and linear regret. Corollary 16 tightens the constant from N to affdim(ℒ^ρ)+1, which is logarithmically smaller in structured prediction (e.g., d = log₂N for multilabel Hamming loss). The multiclass classification instantiation (Section 4) yields an explicit loss with an O(K ln K) solver (Algorithm 1) and a Fisher-consistent probability estimator (Theorem 17). Empirical validation on ImageNet and CIFAR-10 (Appendix F) confirms competitive accuracy with standard cross-entropy baselines while providing the additional theoretical guarantees.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper is primarily theoretical, so its 'comparisons' are against prior impossibility and lower-bound results rather than empirical baselines. Frongillo and Waggoner [39] proved that locally smooth and strongly convex losses must suffer at least a square-root regret rate; the convolutional Fenchel-Young loss circumvents this by being smooth but not locally strongly convex (since T is non-differentiable). Mahdavi et al. [50] showed a trade-off between generalization and optimization for interpolated hinge-logistic losses; the present construction avoids this by encoding the target loss structure directly into the negentropy via infimal convolution. Ramaswamy et al. [76] argued that convex smooth losses redundantly model continuous distributions; the convolutional Fenchel-Young loss addresses this by restricting the prediction link to the discrete space via the π-argmax. Empirically, the paper reports competitive top-1 accuracy on ImageNet and CIFAR-10 relative to standard cross-entropy training, with a 5% t-test confirming statistical significance, though the specific numerical gaps are detailed in Appendix F.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Fenchel-Young loss framework (Blondel et al., 2020) and the (ρ, ℓ^ρ)-decomposition from structured prediction (Blondel, 2019; Ciliberto et al., 2016), but departs from prior work by introducing an infimal convolution between a base negentropy and the negative Bayes risk of the target loss. This 'convolutional negentropy' construction is the key novelty: it encodes target-loss structure additively into the negentropy, which translates into an infimal convolution in the conjugate, yielding a loss that is convex and smooth yet evades the square-root regret lower bound that applies to locally strongly convex losses (Frongillo & Waggoner, 2021). The π-argmax prediction link, derived from the minimizer of this infimal convolution, replaces the standard argmax and is essential for achieving the linear regret rate.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper resolves a long-standing open problem by demonstrating that convexity, smoothness, and linear surrogate regret bounds are simultaneously achievable for arbitrary discrete prediction problems—overcoming impossibility results and tradeoff arguments from Mahdavi et al. (2014), Frongillo & Waggoner (2021), and Ramaswamy et al. (2018). The construction is systematic and general, and the smoothness enables efficient first-order optimization while the linear regret ensures optimal transfer of surrogate error to target error. Open questions include whether fast O(1/n) estimation rates can be recovered under margin or low-noise conditions, how to extend the analysis to H-consistency with restricted hypothesis classes, and whether the practical optimization benefits of smoothness outweigh the additional computational cost of solving the infimal convolution at each gradient step.
