## Problem

Safety-critical collision scenarios are scarce in real-world driving datasets due to the high cost and risk of collecting such data, leaving autonomous driving systems undertrained on dangerous corner cases. Existing generative approaches (STRIVE, CAT, SEAL) that modify existing neighboring agents to create collisions produce insufficiently diverse adversarial behaviors and achieve low collision generation rates, limiting their utility for robust AD testing and training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251849/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework takes a real-world driving scenario from WOMD (downsampled to 2 Hz, 19 prediction steps, up to 128 agents with vector map and traffic-light context) and a randomly sampled collision state (position, heading, velocity, and time step) for a newly inserted adversarial agent. It outputs a complete safety-critical traffic scenario in which the adversarial agent's full trajectory is reconstructed in reverse chronological order from the collision state back to the initial frame, optionally with forward-refined reactive trajectories for surrounding traffic agents. A rule-based rejection filter discards candidates whose trajectories violate a maximum curvature threshold (κ > 0.8) or exhibit implausibly short driving distances.

## Method

The core contribution is the Bidirectional Motion Transformer (BMT), a GPT-style autoregressive model that tokenizes continuous agent motion into a shared discrete vocabulary of 33×33 acceleration–yaw-rate bins derived from a simplified bicycle model. A temporally reversible tokenization scheme allows the same token space to encode both forward (future) and reverse (history) motion: reverse decoding inverts the bicycle dynamics with Δt → −Δt, enabling reconstruction of past trajectories from a known future state. The architecture pairs a scene encoder (map polylines, traffic lights, agent states with Fourier-encoded relational features) with a multi-cross-attention motion decoder (agent-to-agent, agent-to-time, agent-to-scene) that predicts one token per agent per step via nucleus sampling. BMT is trained in two stages—1 M steps of forward-only pretraining followed by 1.5 M steps of bidirectional fine-tuning—using cross-entropy on token predictions, requiring no collision data. The Adv-BMT pipeline then (1) samples diverse collision initializations for a new adversarial agent, (2) reverse-predicts its trajectory with BMT (in teacher-forced, closed-loop, or forward-refinement modes), and (3) applies rule-based rejection sampling to ensure physical plausibility.

## Results

Adv-BMT achieves a 100% adversarial attack success rate (collision rate between adversary and ego), compared to only 38–55% for the three baselines (STRIVE, CAT, SEAL). In terms of diversity, Adv-BMT with filtering (BMT_TF+Filter) yields a Final Displacement Diversity (FDD) of 2.32 and Average Displacement Diversity (ADD) of 1.00, while all baselines produce near-zero diversity (FDD ≤ 0.01, ADD = 0.00). On realism, Adv-BMT attains the lowest Jensen-Shannon Divergence values (JSD_Vel = 0.08, JSD_Acc = 0.12, JSD_TTC = 0.09) and the lowest ADV-to-traffic collision rate (0.12), indicating more physically plausible interactions. In downstream RL training, agents trained on Adv-BMT scenarios reduce episode collision rates by 20% and episode cost by 17% relative to the best baseline on safety-critical test environments.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Adv-BMT decisively outperforms all three baselines on both diversity and realism metrics. On diversity, the baselines (CAT, SEAL, STRIVE) produce essentially identical adversarial trajectories for the same input scene (FDD ≤ 0.01, ADD = 0.00), whereas Adv-BMT generates substantially varied collision behaviors (FDD up to 2.32, ADD up to 1.00). On attack success rate, Adv-BMT achieves 100% collision generation, while STRIVE reaches only 38%, CAT 48%, and SEAL 55%. On realism, Adv-BMT with filtering attains the lowest JSD values across velocity (0.08), acceleration (0.12), and TTC (0.09), and the lowest ADV-to-traffic collision rate (0.12 vs. 0.30–0.32 for baselines), indicating that the generated adversarial agents interact more naturally with surrounding traffic. In closed-loop RL (Table 5), Adv-BMT-trained agents achieve a 38% reduction in episode cost compared to the strongest closed-loop baseline (CAT), with lower collision rates (0.09 vs. 0.10) and higher reward (33.13 vs. 32.15) on Waymo validation environments.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the autoregressive transformer paradigm from forward motion prediction (Trajeglish, MotionLM, SMART, BehaviorGPT) by introducing a temporally reversible tokenization scheme that enables a single model to predict both future and past trajectories. For safety-critical scenario generation, it departs from the dominant 'select-and-modify' paradigm (STRIVE, CAT, SEAL) that perturbs existing neighboring agents, instead inserting entirely new adversarial agents and reconstructing their histories via reverse prediction. The rule-based rejection sampling and multi-mode generation (teacher-forced, closed-loop, forward-refined) are practical additions that distinguish Adv-BMT from prior diffusion-based or RL-based generators (SafeSim, AdvSim, GOOSE).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a critical bottleneck in AD validation: the near-absence of collision data in real-world driving logs. By generating diverse, physically plausible collision scenarios without requiring any collision data during training, Adv-BMT offers a scalable data augmentation pipeline for safety testing and adversarial RL. Key limitations include the quality gap between forward and reverse prediction (WOSAC realism 0.753 vs 0.554), reliance on long token sequences that demand significant compute, and evaluation restricted to a single dataset (WOMD) and a single downstream task (TD3 in MetaDrive). Open questions include whether the reverse-prediction quality gap can be closed with more data or architectural changes, and whether the approach generalizes to other driving datasets or downstream tasks beyond RL.
