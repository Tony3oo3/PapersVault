## Problem

In Federated Class-Incremental Learning, exemplar-based data replay suffers from two forms of class imbalance: within the replay buffer (due to clients lacking global awareness of cross-client data distributions) and between replayed old classes and newly arrived classes. This imbalance causes the model to overfit to majority classes and progressively forget minority or earlier-task classes, degrading overall accuracy as tasks accumulate.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254863/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 64×64 for TinyImageNet) and outputs a class label over the full set of classes seen so far across all tasks. In the federated setting, each client holds a disjoint subset of classes per task and maintains a fixed-budget replay buffer (M samples per task) of real exemplars from prior tasks; the global model's classifier head is dynamically expanded as new classes arrive, and the final prediction spans all accumulated classes.

## Method

FedCBDR comprises two modules. The Global-perspective Data Replay (GDR) module applies Inverse SVD (X′ = P_k X Q) on each client's local feature matrix to produce privacy-preserving pseudo-features, which the server concatenates and decomposes via SVD; leverage scores computed from the left singular vectors quantify each sample's contribution to the global structure, and i.i.d. sampling with inverse-probability weight adjustment yields a class-balanced replay set whose indices are sent back to clients. The Task-aware Temperature Scaling (TTS) module then modulates the training loss by applying a lower temperature τ_old to old-class logits (sharpening confidence in retained knowledge) and a higher temperature τ_new to new-class logits (reducing overconfidence), combined with per-sample re-weighting (ω_old > 1, ω_new < 1) in a concatenated cross-entropy objective that balances the optimization signal between majority and minority classes across tasks.

## Results

FedCBDR achieves the highest Top-1 accuracy in 7 out of 8 settings across CIFAR-10 (3/5 tasks), CIFAR-100 (5/10 tasks), and Tiny-ImageNet (10/20 tasks) under varying Dirichlet heterogeneity levels (β=0.1, 0.5, 1.0). On CIFAR-10 with 3 tasks, FedCBDR reaches 64.11% (β=0.5) and 65.20% (β=1.0), surpassing the strongest baseline LANDER by over 10 percentage points. On Tiny-ImageNet with 10 tasks, FedCBDR attains 18.37–18.86%, outperforming Re-Fed (15.73–15.93%) and LANDER (13.80–16.36%) by 2.5–5.1 points. The only suboptimal result is on CIFAR-100 with 5 tasks at β=1.0, where FedCBDR (52.06%) trails LANDER (52.77%) by 0.71 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedCBDR outperforms all five baselines in 7 of 8 evaluation settings, with the sole exception being CIFAR-100 (5 tasks, β=1.0) where LANDER edges ahead by 0.71 points. Against FedEWC [46], FedCBDR gains 24–26 points on CIFAR-10 and 32–33 points on CIFAR-100, reflecting the severe limitations of parameter-regularization-only approaches under non-IID federated settings. FedLwF [37] performs competitively on CIFAR-10 (56–58%) but degrades sharply on CIFAR-100 and Tiny-ImageNet, where FedCBDR leads by 12–27 points. TARGET [27] (generative replay via distillation) is consistently outperformed by FedCBDR by 12–20 points across all datasets, confirming the fidelity limitations of synthesized samples. LANDER [19] (text-anchored generative replay) is the strongest baseline, reaching 60.79% on CIFAR-10 (β=1.0) and 52.77% on CIFAR-100 (β=1.0), yet FedCBDR still leads by 4.4–10.2 points in most settings while requiring no generative model training. Re-Fed [30] (exemplar-based replay with personalized selection) is the closest competitor on Tiny-ImageNet (15.73–16.05% vs. FedCBDR's 18.37–18.86%), but its class-imbalanced local sampling strategy results in a consistent 2.5–5.1 point gap, validating the benefit of global-perspective balanced replay.

## Relation to Prior Work

FedCBDR sits at the intersection of exemplar-based replay methods (Re-Fed, GLFC) and privacy-preserving federated aggregation techniques. It departs from prior replay strategies that operate purely on local data distributions by introducing a global coordination mechanism: client features are encrypted via inverse SVD (building on Chai et al.'s decentralized SVD framework), aggregated server-side, and decomposed to compute leverage scores that drive class-balanced sampling. The task-aware temperature scaling module complements this by addressing the orthogonal problem of inter-task sample imbalance, combining it with the sampling strategy in a unified training objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and underexplored failure mode in FCIL: local replay buffers become class-imbalanced under non-IID data, silently degrading retention of minority classes. The privacy-preserving global sampling mechanism is a practical contribution that could be adapted to other federated settings requiring cross-client data awareness without raw data sharing. However, the method's sensitivity to temperature and weight hyperparameters (Section 5.6), the computational overhead of SVD at scale, and evaluation limited to three small-scale image datasets with a single backbone (ResNet-18) leave open questions about robustness and generalizability to larger, more diverse settings.
