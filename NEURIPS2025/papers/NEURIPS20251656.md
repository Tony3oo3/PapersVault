## Problem

Off-policy evaluation in confounded POMDPs—where both partial observability and unmeasured confounding are present—lacks a comprehensive finite-sample error analysis. Existing estimators either suffer from exponential-in-horizon variance (importance sampling) or achieve only O(T²/√n) rates (unconfounded POMDPs), leaving open whether the sharp linear O(T/√n) rate known for tabular MDPs can be recovered under unobserved confounding, and how the cardinality of the observation-action space explicitly enters the error bound for history-dependent policies.

## Input / Output

The method consumes n i.i.d. offline episodes collected under a behavior policy that depends on the unobserved state S_t (not merely the current observation), together with a target policy π that may be fully history-dependent (π_t: O × H_{t-1} → Δ(A)) or memoryless (π_t: O → Δ(A)). It outputs the scalar estimated policy value V̂(π) computed from the first-step proxy value function b̂_{V,1}, along with the full sequence of estimated value functions {b̂_{V,t}}_{t=1}^T. A key practical constraint is that each history triple (o_0, h_{t-1}, a_t) must be visited sufficiently often in the data for the conditional probability matrices to be well-conditioned, requiring n to scale at least as polylog(|O|^T, |A|^T, T) / min p_t^{π^b}(o_0, h_{t-1}, a_t).

## Method

The paper proposes a two-step model-based estimator grounded in a Bellman-type recursion over observable variables. First, under rank conditions on the action-conditioned emission matrices P(O_t | A_t=a_t, S_t) and P(O_t | A_t=a_t, O_0, H_{t-1}), the policy value is identified entirely in terms of observable quantities via Moore-Penrose inverses, using the full history {O_0, H_{t-1}} as a proxy for the hidden state. Second, rather than directly inverting multiple estimated matrices (which is numerically unstable), the estimator solves a system of linear equations P_{a_t} B_t = Σ P_{a_t,r_t,o_{t+1}}[r_t π_t + Σ b_{V,t+1} ⊙ π_t] iteratively backward from t=T to t=1, where P_{a_t} is a |O||H_{t-1}| × |O| conditional probability matrix estimated entry-wise from the data. The key theoretical insight is a variance decomposition (Lemma 1) showing that the sum of conditional variances across time steps is bounded by the total reward variance, which—combined with bounded concentrability coefficients ρ_t—yields the first-order rate O(T/√n · (|O||A|)^{T/2}) for history-dependent policies and O(T/√n · √(|O||A|)) for memoryless policies, matching the minimax-optimal tabular MDP rate. Without the concentrability assumption, the rate degrades to O(T^{1.5}/√n · (|O||A|)^{T/2}).

## Results

The paper establishes finite-sample error bounds for OPE in confounded POMDPs. For a fully history-dependent policy, the first-order estimation error achieves O(T^{1.5}/√n · (|O||A|)^{T/2}) under basic invertibility and coverage assumptions (Theorem 2). Under an additional bounded concentrability coefficient assumption (Assumption 3), the rate improves to O(T/√n · (|O||A|)^{T/2}) for history-dependent policies (Theorem 3a). For memoryless policies, the bound further reduces to O(T/√n · √(|O||A|)), matching the minimax-optimal rate known for tabular MDPs (Theorem 3b, Corollary 1). Higher-order terms exhibit stronger T^3 and (|O||A|)^{3T/2} or (|O||A|)^{5T/2} dependence but converge faster in n.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no direct empirical method comparisons; the 'baselines' are prior theoretical bounds. The paper improves upon the O(T²/√n) rate established for unconfounded POMDPs by Zhang and Jiang (2025a,b) and for confounded POMDPs by Bennett and Kallus (2024), achieving a sharper T^{1.5} (or T with concentrability) dependence on horizon. For memoryless policies, the O(T/√n · √(|O||A|)) rate matches the minimax-optimal bound of Yin and Wang (2020) for tabular MDPs, demonstrating that the confounded POMDP setting does not incur additional horizon cost in this case. The exponential (|O||A|)^{T/2} dependence for history-dependent policies is shown to be inherent to using full history as a proxy for hidden states, and no prior work had characterized this dependence explicitly. The simulation (Section 6) empirically validates the linear-in-T error scaling for memoryless policies and the exponential-in-T (log-linear) scaling for history-dependent policies, consistent with the theory.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the identification framework of Tennenholtz et al. (2020) and Shi et al. (2022) for OPE in confounded POMDPs by replacing the one-step observation proxy with the full observable history {O₀, H_{t-1}}, and by providing the first comprehensive finite-sample error bounds in the tabular setting. The variance decomposition technique is adapted from Yin and Wang (2020) for MDPs, and the proximal causal inference machinery of Tchetgen et al. (2020) underpins the state-elimination step in the identification proof. Unlike prior work that achieves O(T²/√n) rates (Uehara et al., 2023; Zhang and Jiang, 2025a,b) or relies on function approximation (Shi et al., 2022; Bennett and Kallus, 2024), this paper works in a fully tabular, model-based framework and explicitly characterizes how history-dependent policies introduce exponential dependence on the observation-action space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to provide a complete finite-sample analysis of OPE in confounded POMDPs, resolving the open question of whether the minimax-optimal T/√n rate known for MDPs can be recovered in the presence of unobserved confounding. The result that memoryless policies achieve O(T/√n · √(|O||A|))—matching the tabular MDP rate—suggests that confounding does not fundamentally worsen the horizon dependence when the policy is simple. However, the exponential (|O||A|)^{T/2} dependence for fully history-dependent policies highlights a fundamental statistical barrier, and the minimax optimality of the derived rates remains open. Extending the framework to continuous state/observation spaces and to non-tabular settings is identified as key future work.
