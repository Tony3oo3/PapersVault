## Problem

Existing scaling laws for compressed models are specialized to a single compression type (sparsity or quantization) and a single format, making them unable to predict performance for hybrid representations (e.g., sparse-quantized weights) or composite compression (e.g., quantized weights and activations simultaneously). No unified framework connects parameter efficiency to intrinsic, representation-level properties that transfer across compression types and model families.

## Input / Output

The framework takes the model parameter count N, dataset size D, and a compressed representation R fully characterized by its Gaussian MSE (GMSE), which is computed via fast Monte Carlo estimation over random Gaussian inputs for any format (scalar INT, FP, sparse, VQ, group-wise, outlier-aware). It outputs a predicted loss curve, a scalar representation capacity ρ(R) ∈ (0,1], and a Pareto ranking of formats at a given bit-width. A key structural property is that ρ factorizes multiplicatively across independent compression dimensions (e.g., ρ(R_{q,s}) = ρ(R_q)·ρ(R_s)), enabling low-cost prediction of arbitrary composite configurations without additional training runs.

## Method

The paper proposes a unified scaling law Loss(N,D) = A/(N·ρ̃(GMSE(R)))^α + B/D^β + E, where the capacity function takes the specific form ρ̃(GMSE) = L·tanh(F·log_{1/4}(GMSE))^C with only three representation-specific scalars (L, F, C) that are fit once per compression type and then reused across all instances (e.g., all bit-widths of INT quantization share the same L, F, C). Theoretically, Theorem 1 proves that Adam with STE over compressed iterates converges at a rate governed by N·E[‖θ̂_t − θ_t‖₂], directly linking parameter efficiency to the average compression RMSE and justifying the GMSE-based capacity metric. Empirically, the law is validated on Llama and OLMo2 decoder-only Transformers (30M–200M params) trained on C4 and ClimbMix with QuEST, LSQ, HIGGS VQ, and magnitude pruning, showing fit errors comparable to or better than prior representation-specific laws. As a practical application, the paper derives RMSE-Banded Backward Masking (RBBM), which uses the Gaussian RMSE structure to design a banded gradient mask for sparse training that consistently improves capacity by >20% over magnitude-pruning baselines at the same sparsity.

## Results

The unified scaling law (Eq. 1) with capacity ρ(R) = L·tanh(F·log_{1/4}(GMSE(R)))^C achieves a sparsity fit error of 4.7×10⁻⁴ and a quantization fit error of 2.1×10⁻³, matching or exceeding prior representation-specific laws. The capacity factorizes multiplicatively across compression types (e.g., sparse-quantized weights, weights+activations) with element-wise errors of order 10⁻³–10⁻². The proposed RBBM backward masking heuristic improves sparse training capacity by over 20% in some sparsity regimes (≥40%) compared to standard magnitude pruning. GMSE-based format ranking (e.g., INT4 vs. FP4) correlates directly with actual C4 validation loss, enabling zero-cost format comparison.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Kumar et al. [19] (scaling laws for precision), the proposed GMSE-based formulation nearly halves the quantization fit error (2.1×10⁻³ vs. 4.5×10⁻³), with the gap most pronounced at higher bit-widths where Kumar's exponential form ρ(P_w)=1−e^{−P_w/γ_w} struggles with curvature near GMSE=1. Against Frantar et al. [10] (decoupled compression scaling law), the proposed method is within 10–15% on fit error (4.7×10⁻⁴ vs. 4.2×10⁻⁴ for sparsity; 2.1×10⁻³ vs. 1.9×10⁻³ for quantization), but critically eliminates the need to independently fit eff_C for every compression instance—instead, a single parametric form ρ̃(GMSE) transfers across all instances of the same representation type. Against Frantar et al. [9] (sparsity scaling law), the proposed method has a slightly higher sparsity fit error (4.7×10⁻⁴ vs. 5.7×10⁻⁴) but extends the same formulation to quantization and hybrid sparse-quantized formats, which [9] cannot address. Against GMP (Zhu et al. [37]) as a sparse training baseline, the RBBM heuristic achieves consistently higher representation capacity at the same sparsity level, with the advantage growing at higher sparsities (≥40%), while RigL variants underperform even the naive magnitude-pruning baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies the separate scaling-law formulations for sparsity (Frantar et al., ICLR 2024) and quantization (Kumar et al., 2024) into a single framework where the parameter-efficiency term ρ(R) is a parametric function of the representation's Gaussian MSE, building on the preliminary single-parameter law of Frantar et al. (2025). The key departure is that prior laws required independent fitting for each compression instance, whereas here the same parametric form transfers across compression types (sparse, quantized, hybrid, vector-quantized) and is grounded in an intrinsic, representation-only property. A theoretical convergence analysis for Adam with straight-through estimation (Theorem 1) provides the first formal link between the N·RMSE compression-error product and the scaling-law capacity term.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The GMSE-based capacity metric enables practitioners to rank and compare compressed numerical formats (INT4 vs. FP4, group-wise vs. per-tensor, outlier-aware vs. not) via fast Monte Carlo estimation without retraining, and the factorization property allows predicting combined compression effects (e.g., 4-bit + 2:4 sparse) from individual measurements. The RBBM heuristic demonstrates a >20% capacity gain in some sparsity regimes, showing the framework has immediate algorithmic utility. Limitations include validation only on 30M–200M Llama/OLMo2 models on C4/ClimbMix in the data-rich regime, and potential breakdown at ultra-low precision (≤2-bit) or very small VQ codebooks; scaling to billion-parameter models and diverse architectures remains open.
