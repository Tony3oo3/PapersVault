## Problem

For many non-convex losses in ML (e.g., LSTMs, transformers), the gradient and Hessian are not Lipschitz, yet all existing non-asymptotic guarantees for finding Second Order Stationary Points (SOSPs) via first-order methods require Lipschitz smoothness. This paper establishes the first convergence guarantees for first-order methods to SOSPs under generalizations of smoothness, specifically a self-bounding regularity condition where the Hessian norm is controlled by the function value.

## Input / Output

The method takes a twice-differentiable non-convex function F satisfying self-bounding regularity (‖∇²F(w)‖_op ≤ ρ₁(F(w))), an initialization w_0, and a tolerance ε. It outputs a sequence of candidate vectors (one per iterate or per block of iterates for SGD), at least one of which is a FOSP (‖∇F(w)‖ ≤ ε) or SOSP (‖∇F(w)‖ ≤ ε and ∇²F(w) ≥ −√ε I). The step size η is fixed a priori based on F(w_0) and the self-bounding functions, not adapted per-iterate, and the algorithm runs for a bounded number of oracle calls determined by F(w_0)/ε² (for FOSPs) or polylog(d)/ε² (for SOSPs).

## Method

The core contribution is a systematic 'decrease procedure' framework: an algorithm is certified as a decrease procedure if, for every point in the F(w_0)-sublevel set, it either decreases the function value by a threshold Δ or outputs a candidate in the target set S. The key insight is that under self-bounding regularity, GD/SGD iterates remain 'local' (bounded displacement) within the sublevel set, so the Hessian can be uniformly bounded along segments between consecutive iterates by L₁(F(w₀)). This enables chaining per-iterate decrease: if ‖∇F‖ ≥ ε at each step, F decreases by at least ε²/(2L₁(F(w₀))) per iterate, and since F is lower-bounded, a FOSP must be reached. For randomized algorithms (SGD, Perturbed GD, Restarted SGD), the framework groups iterates into blocks of length t_thresh and shows high-probability decrease per block via concentration inequalities, yielding the first first-order SOSP guarantees under generalized smoothness with complexity matching the smooth setting.

## Results

The paper establishes non-asymptotic convergence rates for first-order methods under self-bounding regularity (Assumptions 1.1 and 1.2), which generalize (L₀,L₁)-smoothness. For finding SOSPs, Perturbed GD achieves O(ε⁻² log⁴(d/(εδ))) oracle calls (Theorem 3.4) and Restarted SGD achieves Õ(ε⁻³·⁵) oracle calls (Theorem 3.5), both with polylogarithmic dependence on dimension d. For FOSPs, GD requires 2F(w₀)L₁(w₀)/ε² calls (Theorem 3.1) and SGD requires Õ(ε⁻⁴ polylog(1/ε, 1/δ)) calls (Theorem 3.3). The paper notes these ε and d dependencies match known lower bounds for smooth functions, making them optimal in this setting as well.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper that does not run head-to-head empirical comparisons against competing methods; instead, its contribution is extending convergence guarantees from the smooth (Lipschitz gradient/Hessian) setting to the generalized smoothness setting. The key 'comparison' is that the derived rates for Perturbed GD (O(ε⁻² log⁴(d/(εδ)))) and Restarted SGD (Õ(ε⁻³·⁵)) match the corresponding rates in the smooth setting established by Jin et al. (2017) and Fang et al. (2019), respectively, now under the weaker self-bounding regularity assumptions. The paper also shows its Assumption 1.1 subsumes (L₀,L₁)-smoothness from Zhang et al. (2019) and Li et al. (2023a), and Assumption 1.2 subsumes the Hessian-Lipschitz-in-neighborhood assumption of Xie et al. (2024), which is the only prior result on finding SOSPs under generalized smoothness but requires full Hessian access (second-order). The practical simulations in Section G validate that the theoretical prediction—that larger F(w₀) shrinks the working step-size window—matches observed behavior for F(w)=‖Aw‖ᵖ with p=2,3,4,5,6.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the line of work studying non-convex optimization under generalized smoothness, initiated by Zhang et al. (2019) with (L0,L1)-smoothness and gradient clipping, and extended by Li et al. (2023a) to fixed-step-size GD/SGD. It departs from Xie et al. (2024), the only prior result on finding SOSPs under generalized smoothness, by replacing their second-order trust-region method (requiring the full Hessian) with first-order algorithms (Perturbed GD, Restarted SGD), which are the only tractable option in high-dimensional ML. The key methodological departure is the 'decrease procedure' framework, which systematically verifies that standard first-order algorithms decrease function value within sublevel sets, enabling convergence proofs without a global Lipschitz constant.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first result establishing non-asymptotic convergence of first-order methods to SOSPs under generalized smoothness, closing a gap that previously forced practitioners to either assume Lipschitz Hessian (often violated in practice, as shown empirically for LSTMs and transformers) or resort to intractable second-order methods. The practical implication that step size should be tuned based on initialization loss rather than a global smoothness constant is actionable for training non-smooth losses. Open questions include whether the framework extends to higher-order stationary points, adaptive methods like Adam under these assumptions, and whether the self-bounding regularity assumption can be further relaxed or verified automatically from model architecture.
