## Problem

Existing visual token pruning methods for LVLMs rely on attention scores from a single layer and a single criterion to identify redundant tokens, but the paper demonstrates that redundancy emerges progressively and dynamically across layers, and that single-layer, single-criterion scoring can produce contradictory importance rankings. The paper asks whether such a simple criterion is rigorous enough to support pruning decisions, and shows it is not.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253174/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard LVLM pipelines: images (576 tokens for LLaVA-1.5, up to 2880 for LLaVA-NeXT, dynamic for Qwen2-VL) or video frames (8×256 tokens for Video-LLaVA) paired with text prompts. Pruning is applied at the vision encoder's penultimate layer for smaller models, or spans from the vision encoder through the second LLM layer for larger token counts, progressively reducing to the target budget. The output is a reduced set of visual tokens fed to the LLM for autoregressive text generation, and the entire procedure is training-free and compatible with FlashAttention since it only requires 1×N CLS-token attention values rather than the full N×N map.

## Method

FlowCut is an information-flow-aware pruning framework built on three components. First, an attention-entropy-driven adaptive prune ratio uses the CLS token's attention entropy at each layer to determine how many tokens to remove: high entropy (broad attention) triggers conservative pruning, while low entropy (concentrated attention) permits aggressive pruning, with the formula P = ((N−T)/√L)·(1−r_H²). Second, a multi-criteria evaluator fuses three signals per token—CLS attention strength, semantic similarity to the CLS value vector, and L1-norm information density of the value vector—into a composite importance score S = (I^a/ΣI^a + I^s/ΣI^s) × I^d. Third, a cumulative importance tracker aggregates scores across layers via an exponential moving average (0.5 current + 0.5 historical), pruning every two layers so that noisy single-layer fluctuations are smoothed out. The CLS token serves as a global information relay whose attention pattern approximates the full token-to-token flow, enabling this simplified yet representative proxy for redundancy detection.

## Results

On LLaVA-1.5-7B with 88.9% token reduction (64 tokens retained), FlowCut achieves 96.0% average accuracy relative to the unpruned upper bound, outperforming the previous best (VisionZip, 94.4%) by 1.6%. On LLaVA-NeXT-7B with 94.4% reduction (160 tokens), FlowCut reaches 91.9% average vs. VisionZip's 87.6%, a 4.3% margin. In the prefilling stage, FlowCut delivers a 3.2× speedup on LLaVA-NeXT-7B (63.8 ms vs. 206 ms vanilla) and 1.6× on LLaVA-1.5-7B (62.6 ms vs. 102 ms), while reducing TFLOPs from 31.03 to 4.29 on LLaVA-NeXT.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FlowCut consistently outperforms all 10 compared baselines across every token reduction setting. At the most aggressive 88.9% reduction on LLaVA-1.5-7B, FlowCut (96.0% avg) beats VisionZip (94.4%) by 1.6%, PDrop (86.1%) by 9.9%, SparseVLM (84.6%) by 11.4%, FastV (77.5%) by 18.5%, and ToMe (71.4%) by 24.6%. On LLaVA-NeXT-7B at 94.4% reduction, the gap widens: FlowCut (91.9%) surpasses VisionZip (87.6%) by 4.3%, PDrop (86.2%) by 5.7%, and SparseVLM (79.8%) by 12.1%. In the appendix comparison (Table 9, ~64 tokens, 1.95 TFLOPs), FlowCut leads FasterVLM (55.2→55.6 TextVQA, 76.1→80.2 POPE), RCom (55.5→55.6 TextVQA, 72.0→80.2 POPE), and ST3 (85.2→86.1 POPE, 63.8→64.3 MMB) at matched FLOPs. On video understanding (Video-LLaVA, 256 tokens total), FlowCut achieves 98.6% average accuracy vs. VisionZip's 94.4%, a 4.2% gain. The only setting where FlowCut does not lead is the 66.7% reduction on LLaVA-1.5, where VisionZip (98.5%) and FlowCut (100.2%) are very close, with FlowCut still ahead by 1.7%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in visual token pruning (FastV, SparseVLM, VisionZip, PDrop) that identifies redundancy via single-layer attention scores, reframing the problem through an information-flow lens that models how information propagates between tokens across ViT layers. Conceptually, it builds on the CLS token's role as a global information relay (building on prior CLS-token analyses in ViT) and incorporates value-vector norms and semantic similarity as complementary importance signals drawn from KV-cache compression literature. The key methodological departure is the combination of adaptive layer-wise pruning ratios driven by attention entropy, multi-criteria scoring, and cumulative importance tracking across layers, rather than a fixed single-layer decision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that single-layer attention scores can produce contradictory redundancy identifications challenges a widely adopted assumption in the LVLM efficiency community, and the information-flow perspective offers a principled alternative that aligns pruning with the model's own dynamic behavior. The observation that aggressive pruning (192 tokens) can slightly exceed the unpruned baseline's accuracy hints at a potential hallucination-mitigation mechanism worth further investigation. Open questions include whether the information-flow analysis generalizes to non-ViT encoders (e.g., convolutional or hybrid backbones), how the method behaves under extreme compression (below 32 tokens), and whether the CLS-token relay assumption holds for architectures without an explicit CLS token.
