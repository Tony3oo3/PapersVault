## Problem

Existing Gaussianity regularizations for latent noise optimization in text-to-image models either capture only marginal spatial statistics (KL, kurtosis, norm-based) or enforce full covariance matching at quadratic cost (PRNO). No prior method simultaneously enforces both spatial and spectral properties of the standard Gaussian at near-linear complexity, leaving optimized latents with residual structural artifacts that degrade image quality and enable reward hacking.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250614/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt, an initial D-dimensional Gaussian latent vector (D=65,536 for FLUX), and a differentiable reward function (e.g., aesthetic score or PickScore), and produces an optimized latent that, when decoded by the one-step FLUX model, yields a generated image. Optimization runs for 500 iterations at test time using Nesterov momentum (coefficient 0.9) with gradient clipping at 0.01; the regularization gradient is normalized to match the reward gradient magnitude. The latent is randomly permuted before each loss evaluation to enforce permutation invariance, and the spectral loss operates on frequency blocks of size 16.

## Method

The paper proposes a unified Gaussianity regularization loss combining spatial-domain moment matching (enforcing first and second moments to 0 and 1) with spectral-domain power spectrum matching (aligning batched DFT magnitude means to the expected value of χ₂/√₂, set to 0.875). The moment loss is shown to subsume KL divergence, kurtosis, and norm-based regularization as special cases, while the power spectrum loss achieves the same identity-covariance objective as PRNO but via FFT in O(D log D) rather than O(D²). A key design choice is applying the spectral loss to the mean of 16 randomly sampled frequency coefficients rather than individual coefficients, preserving the natural variance of the χ₂/√₂ distribution and preventing spectral collapse. The final loss is L = L₁ + L₂ + 25.0 · L_power, applied to randomly permuted latents to ensure permutation invariance. Theoretical results (Theorem 1, Lemma 1) establish that matching all moments uniquely characterizes the standard Gaussian, and that DFT magnitudes of i.i.d. Gaussian vectors follow scaled chi distributions, providing the analytical foundation for both loss components.

## Results

The paper evaluates its Gaussianity regularization on two reward-alignment tasks using FLUX as the one-step text-to-image model: aesthetic image generation (given reward: aesthetic score; held-out: HPSv2, ImageReward) and text-aligned image generation (given reward: PickScore; held-out: HPSv2, ImageReward) on 60 prompts from T2I-CompBench++. Across both tasks, the proposed method achieves the highest scores on all metrics at every 100-iteration checkpoint (up to 500 iterations), outperforming all baselines while preventing reward hacking. In a toy experiment from a checkerboard initialization, the method produces clean outputs in approximately 50× less time than PRNO. The method uses K={1,2} moments with λ_power=25.0, batch size 16 for spectral averaging, and a regularization coefficient of 2.0.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against five regularization baselines: KL divergence [18], Kurtosis [7], Norm/ReNO [12, 27], and PRNO [33], plus a no-regularization reference. Spatial-only methods (KL, Kurtosis, Norm/ReNO) all fail to capture spectral properties of the Gaussian prior, leaving visible checkerboard artifacts in the toy experiment and producing cartoon-like degradation in aesthetic generation; they plateau quickly in text-aligned generation. PRNO [33] captures both spatial and spectral properties via covariance matching but incurs O(Dk) time/memory complexity and still exhibits visible latent structure after 10K iterations. The proposed method outperforms all baselines on every metric (aesthetic score, HPSv2, ImageReward, PickScore) at every optimization iteration, prevents the reward-hacking degradation seen in the No Reg. baseline (where held-out rewards steadily decline), and converges faster. In the toy experiment, it requires roughly 50× less optimization time than PRNO while producing cleaner outputs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies several existing Gaussianity regularizations (KL divergence, kurtosis, norm-based) under a single moment-matching framework in the spatial domain, then extends the approach to the spectral domain via a power-spectrum loss that achieves the same covariance-matching objective as PRNO [33] at O(D log D) complexity instead of O(D²). It builds directly on ReNO [12] for the test-time reward-alignment setup with one-step text-to-image models, replacing ReNO's norm-only regularization with the proposed dual-domain loss. The key conceptual departure is recognizing that spatial-domain moment matching alone is insufficient to enforce i.i.d. Gaussianity and that the spectral domain captures the inter-component dependency structure that prior methods miss.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is a drop-in regularization that prevents reward hacking and accelerates convergence in test-time latent optimization for text-to-image models, with a ~50× speedup over the closest competitor (PRNO). The unification provides a clean theoretical lens for understanding why prior regularizations work (or fail) and suggests principled extensions to higher-order moments. However, the evaluation is limited to a single base model (FLUX) and two reward types, and the authors acknowledge that the loss value itself is not a reliable Gaussianity metric, leaving open the question of how to directly measure and certify Gaussianity of optimized latents.
