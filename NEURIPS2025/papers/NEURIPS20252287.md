## Problem

Crowdsourcing datasets intended to capture human judgment are increasingly contaminated by workers using LLMs to generate responses, undermining the diversity and authenticity of the collected data. Existing LLM-detection methods rely on high-dimensional textual features and are unsuitable for structured annotation tasks (e.g., multiple-choice labeling), while standard peer prediction mechanisms fail when workers can coordinate on highly correlated cheap signals such as LLM outputs. The paper asks: can we design a training-free scoring mechanism that rewards genuine human effort and penalizes LLM-reliant responses without access to ground truth?

## Input / Output

The method takes as input an n×m discrete response matrix (each entry drawn from a finite label space Σ, e.g., binary or 5-point scale) and the principal's own LLM-generated labels Z on a subset of questions (typically 20–50%). It produces a single scalar reliability score per agent, where higher scores indicate responses that carry information beyond what the LLM provides. The mechanism is training-free and operates purely on the frequency statistics of the response matrix conditioned on Z, requiring no model training or access to ground-truth labels.

## Method

The paper proposes the Conditioned Correlated Agreement (CA_Z) mechanism, which extends the standard CA peer-prediction mechanism by conditioning all agreement/penalty comparisons on the principal's LLM-generated signal Z. In practice, the method first estimates a scoring function T_Δ̃ from the empirical conditioned joint distribution of worker report pairs (computing the delta tensor as joint minus product of marginals, given Z), then scores each agent by rewarding agreement with a peer on the same question and penalizing agreement on two distinct questions, both restricted to questions where Z takes the same value. The key insight is that if a worker merely copies LLM output, the bonus and penalty terms cancel in expectation, yielding a near-zero score, while genuine human effort produces a score proportional to the conditional TVD mutual information I_TVD(X_i; X_j | Z). The paper provides theoretical guarantees: ε-information monotonicity for arbitrary strategies under strong assumptions that Z blocks all residual correlations between cheap signals, and exact information monotonicity for the more practical class of lazy-reporting strategies under the weaker condition that human-human correlation strictly exceeds both human-LLM and LLM-LLM correlations given Z.

## Results

The proposed conditioned CA mechanism (CA_Z) achieves the highest average AUC for detecting low-effort agents across all four datasets: 0.85 on Hatefulness, 0.94 on Offensiveness, 0.91 on Toxicity, and 0.85 on Preference Alignment. The advantage is most pronounced on Preference Alignment, where CA_Z reaches 0.85 AUC compared to 0.70 for CA and 0.68 for EM. CA_Z also attains the highest bottom-10% quantile (worst-case robustness) on every dataset, ranging from 0.77 to 0.91, indicating consistent performance even in the most challenging crowd compositions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares CA_Z against four baselines: Output Agreement (OA), Correlated Agreement (CA, Shnayder et al. 2016), Conditioned Agreement (OA_Z), and EM under the Dawid-Skene model (Dawid & Skene 1979). CA_Z outperforms all baselines on every dataset in both average AUC and worst-case (bottom-10%) AUC. Against CA, the gap is largest on Preference Alignment (0.85 vs. 0.70, a 15-point improvement) and moderate on Hatefulness (0.85 vs. 0.84) and Toxicity (0.91 vs. 0.89). Against EM, CA_Z leads by 4 points on Hatefulness (0.85 vs. 0.81), 5 points on Offensiveness (0.94 vs. 0.89), 6 points on Toxicity (0.91 vs. 0.85), and 17 points on Preference Alignment (0.85 vs. 0.68). The authors note that EM can outperform CA_Z when the crowd contains only random or biased agents (no LLM-reliant workers), but CA_Z provides the most robust performance across the full range of crowd compositions, which is the practically relevant setting where the designer does not know the crowd's makeup in advance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the correlated agreement (CA) mechanism from peer prediction (Shnayder et al., 2016) by conditioning the scoring function on an external signal Z that captures LLM-generated cheap signals, yielding the 'conditioned CA' mechanism. It draws conceptual inspiration from Kong and Schoenebeck (2018), who used conditioned mutual information to elicit hierarchical information, but inverts the setup: rather than eliciting low-effort signals first to condition on, the principal already possesses samples of the cheap signal. The paper also positions itself against the LLM-content-detection literature (watermarking, statistical detection, deep-learning classifiers), arguing that those training-based approaches are unsuitable for structured, non-textual annotation tasks where discrete labels lack rich textual features.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a timely and practically important gap: as LLMs become widely accessible to crowdsourcing workers, datasets intended to capture human judgment are increasingly contaminated, yet existing detection tools target open-ended text rather than structured labels. The training-free, theory-grounded approach offers a scalable solution for annotation platforms, and the empirical finding that human correlation patterns are distinguishable from LLM patterns (under the right conditioning model) provides actionable guidance for platform designers. Key open questions include distinguishing harmful LLM substitution from productive human-AI collaboration, extending the method to open-ended textual responses, and understanding how the approach behaves as LLMs improve to match or exceed human performance on subjective tasks.
