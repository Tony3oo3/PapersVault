## Problem

MPE inference in PGMs is NP-hard, and conditioning on variable subsets is a key strategy for reducing search space, but the choice of which variables to fix, to which values, and in what order is critical—poor decisions can irrevocably eliminate the optimal solution. Existing approaches rely on handcrafted heuristics or structure-based metrics that lack generalization across instances. The paper asks whether a data-driven scoring function can be learned to identify conditioning assignments that are simultaneously safe (aligned with optimal solutions) and useful (significantly reduce solver effort).

## Input / Output

The model takes a partially observed PGM instance—the current evidence assignment and the set of remaining query variables—and produces two scores per variable-value pair: an optimality score (probability the assignment appears in the MPE solution) and a simplification score (expected reduction in solver effort). At inference, these scores drive either a greedy or beam-search conditioning loop that progressively fixes variables before invoking an exact solver (SCIP or AOBB), or branching and node-selection policies within a branch-and-bound solver. The model is trained per-PGM, with supervision derived from oracle MPE solutions and re-solving statistics collected after individually fixing variables to their optimal values.

## Method

The architecture is an attention-based network where each variable-value pair is represented by a learned embedding that queries a multi-head attention module using evidence variable embeddings as keys and values, contextualizing each candidate assignment relative to the observed evidence. A shared encoder with residual connections and dropout processes the contextualized representations, feeding two prediction heads: a sigmoid MLP for optimality (trained with binary cross-entropy against oracle solutions) and a ranking MLP for simplification (trained with list-ranking cross-entropy against solver statistics such as runtime and node counts). Training supervision comes from a scalable data generation pipeline that solves each MPE query once with an oracle, then re-solves after fixing individual variables to their optimal values, recording solver statistics as ranking targets. A key design insight is that solution ambiguity modulates conditioning risk: the model implicitly learns to prefer conditioning on variables whose values are more evenly distributed across optimal solutions, where misestimation is less costly. At inference, the combined scores guide greedy or beam-search conditioning to progressively simplify the problem, or serve as branching and node-selection heuristics within SCIP or AOBB solvers.

## Results

Across 14 high-treewidth PGMs (90–1444 variables), L2C-RANK enables the SCIP oracle to achieve a higher log-likelihood than the unconditioned query in 12/12 configurations on 12 of 14 networks, while L2C-OPT achieves 10–12/12. In the NN-guided branch-and-bound setting, L2C-RANK produces negative average LL gaps (i.e., better solutions than SCIP's default heuristics) on 11 of 14 networks at 30s, with the largest improvements on BN 53 (−12.3%) and BN 59 (−11.6%). Per-decision inference time for L2C methods is 0.001–0.025 s, roughly 100–1000× faster than full strong branching (1.3–20.9 s), which times out entirely on the two largest networks (BN 30, BN 32).

## Baselines & Comparisons

L2C-RANK dominates both baselines in the greedy conditioning setting: it wins in 12/12 configurations on 12 of 14 networks, whereas the graph-based max-degree heuristic [Siddiqi & Huang, UAI 2008] wins in 0–12/12 (highly inconsistent, and 0/12 on 8 of 14 networks) and full strong branching [Applegate et al., 1995] wins in at most 1/12 configurations across all networks. In the NN-guided branch-and-bound comparison against SCIP's default branching/rounding heuristics [Achterberg et al., 2012], L2C-RANK achieves negative LL gaps (better solutions) on 11 of 14 networks at the 30s budget, with the largest gains on BN 53 (−12.3%) and BN 59 (−11.6%); the only notable exception is BN 32 at 30s (+22.1%), where the method degrades. On per-decision latency, L2C methods (0.001–0.025 s) are two to three orders of magnitude faster than strong branching (1.3–20.9 s), which fails to return within the 30 s timeout on BN 30 and BN 32, while the graph heuristic (0.001–0.107 s) is competitive in speed but far less effective in solution quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper synthesizes ideas from classical PGM conditioning strategies (cutset conditioning, recursive conditioning), decimation in statistical physics, and strong branching in ILP solvers, but departs from all of them by replacing handcrafted or structure-based variable-selection heuristics with a learned attention-based scoring function. It is most closely related to recent neural approaches to MPE inference (Arya et al., 2024–2025) that optimize relaxed likelihood objectives, but L2C instead learns from solver search traces to produce dual scores (optimality preservation and simplification utility) that guide conditioning decisions. The data-generation pipeline—extracting supervision from a single oracle solve plus targeted re-solves under individual variable fixings—is a practical contribution that avoids the infeasible enumeration of all optimal solutions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

L2C provides a solver-agnostic, instance-adaptive conditioning strategy that consistently improves solution quality and reduces search effort across two structurally different solver backends (SCIP and AOBB) on 14 high-treewidth PGMs, with decision times that remain stable (≤25 ms) as network size grows—unlike strong branching, which times out on the largest models. Key limitations include dependence on solver-derived supervision (costly to generate at scale), restriction to a fixed PGM structure, and binary variables only. Open questions include extending to other solver families (MAXSAT, local search), incorporating richer solver signals (branch-and-cut statistics), and scaling to models with millions of variables via bootstrapped data collection.
