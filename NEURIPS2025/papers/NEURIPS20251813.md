## Problem

The query complexity of computing envy-free cake allocations remains wide open, with an O(n^(n^(n^n))) upper bound and only an Ω(n²) lower bound. This paper introduces two new fairness hierarchies—Harmonically Coalition-Resistant (HCR-k) and Linearly Coalition-Resistant (LCR-k)—that occupy the unexplored middle ground between proportionality and super envy-freeness, revealing that envy-freeness sits between a computationally tractable notion (HCR-n) and a computationally impossible one (LCR-⌈n/2⌉).

## Input / Output

The algorithms operate in the Robertson-Webb query model, where the only allowed operations are EVAL_i(I) (returning agent i's value for interval I) and CUT_i(x,v) (returning the point x' such that V_i([x,x'])=v). The HCR-n algorithm (Algorithm 1) takes n agents with normalized, additive, non-atomic valuation functions over [0,1] and outputs a complete allocation where every agent values their own piece at least 1/n and every other agent's piece at least 1/(2n). The δ-LCR-n algorithm (Algorithm 2) additionally takes a precision parameter ε and outputs a complete, proportional, ε-perfect allocation, with query complexity O(n⁵/ε · ln(1/ε)/ln(n)).

## Method

The HCR-n algorithm (Algorithm 1) introduces n/3 phantom agents with arbitrary valuations, computes a 1/(4n)-perfect allocation for 4n/3 agents via Brânzei-Miltersen, combines the phantom agents' pieces into a residue R, then runs a Last Diminisher procedure where agents sequentially claim a piece from the B-set plus a cut from R. The key insight is that B-pieces need not be pre-assigned to specific agents, bypassing the impossibility of weighted proportional division. For δ-LCR-n (Algorithm 2), the residue is recursively split into (n+1)-piece ε'-perfect allocations over d = Θ(ln(1/ε)/ln(n)) iterations, with pieces combined across iterations so that each final piece has value in [1/n − ε/n, 1/n] for every agent. Lower bounds are established via an active-intervals adversary argument (yielding Ω(n²) for HCR-2) and a reduction from the exact division problem (yielding unbounded complexity for LCR-2, and hence for super envy-freeness).

## Results

The paper establishes that HCR-n allocations (a strict superset of envy-free allocations) can be computed in O(n⁴) Robertson-Webb queries (Theorem 1), while HCR-2 allocations require Ω(n²) queries (Theorem 6). On the other end, LCR-2 allocations (and hence all LCR-k for k ≥ 2) cannot be computed with any bounded protocol (Theorem 7), which also implies super envy-freeness has unbounded query complexity. For the relaxation δ-LCR-n, the paper gives an O(n⁶/δ · ln(1/δ)/ln(n)) query algorithm (Theorem 2), built on a subroutine for complete, ε-perfect, proportional allocations in O(n⁵/ε · ln(1/ε)/ln(n)) queries.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines; comparisons are between query-complexity bounds for different fairness notions. The key positioning result is that HCR-n (computable in O(n⁴)) is a strict superset of envy-freeness, while LCR-⌈n/2⌉ (unbounded) is a strict subset, placing envy-freeness in a 'curious middle ground' between a tractable and an intractable notion. The Ω(n²) lower bound for HCR-2 matches the best known lower bound for envy-freeness [Procaccia 2009], showing that even the weakest non-trivial coalition-resistance condition is as hard as EF at the lower-bound level. The unboundedness of LCR-2 (Theorem 7) is a new result that also resolves the previously open question of whether super envy-freeness has bounded query complexity. The O(n⁴) upper bound for HCR-n improves upon the O(n^(n^(n^n))) bound for envy-freeness by exploiting the weaker coalition-level constraints, while the O(n⁵/ε · ln(1/ε)/ln(n)) algorithm for ε-perfect proportional allocations is stated to be of independent interest, improving upon the O(n³/ε) bound for ε-perfect allocations alone [Brânzei & Miltersen 2015] by adding the proportionality guarantee.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself in the well-studied Robertson-Webb query complexity landscape for cake cutting, where proportionality is solvable in O(n log n) queries and envy-freeness has an astronomical O(n^{n^{n^n}}) upper bound with only an Ω(n²) lower bound. It introduces two new fairness hierarchies (HCR-k and LCR-k) that interpolate between proportionality and super envy-freeness, building algorithmically on the Brânzei-Miltersen ε-perfect allocation protocol and the Dubins-Spanier Last Diminisher method, while leveraging Procaccia's active-intervals adversary framework and the Robertson-Webb exact division problem for lower bounds. Conceptually, the work extends the line of group-fairness studies by Segal-Halevi and Nitzan and Berliant et al., but with a key distinction: LCR-k constrains all possible groupings of size at most k rather than pre-determined groups, making it strictly more restrictive.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central insight is a sharp computational cliff around envy-freeness: the minimally weaker notion (HCR-n) is computable in O(n⁴) queries, while the minimally stronger notion (LCR-⌈n/2⌉) requires unbounded queries, implying that any super-polynomial lower bound for envy-freeness must arise from a subproblem strictly harder than HCR-n. The paper also resolves an open problem by showing super envy-freeness has unbounded query complexity. The δ-LCR-n approximation (O(n⁶/δ · ln(1/δ)/ln(n)) queries) provides a practical middle ground, but the fundamental gap between the O(n⁴) upper bound for HCR-n and the Ω(n²) lower bound for HCR-2 remains wide open, as does the question of whether envy-freeness itself admits a polynomial-time protocol.
