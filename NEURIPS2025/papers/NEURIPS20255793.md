## Problem

Most LLM-based multi-agent frameworks rely on static, pre-defined workflows where agent roles and execution order are fixed, making them unable to revise plans when sub-tasks fail or produce flawed intermediate outputs. Even automated workflow generators (AFlow, ADAS, MaAS) perform one-shot planning and remain fixed during execution, lacking mechanisms to adapt subgoal decomposition and operator selection based on real-time feedback. DyFlow addresses this gap by enabling dynamic, feedback-driven restructuring of reasoning procedures at the subgoal level during execution.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255793/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a text reasoning task specification as input and maintains a progressively updated state s_t that accumulates the original task, all previously generated stage subgraphs, intermediate operator outputs (partial answers, review verdicts), and encountered errors. At each step, the full state is condensed via a summarization function (GPT-4o-mini) before being fed to the designer. The output is a final answer plus a complete execution trajectory τ = (s_0, G_0, s_1, G_1, …, s_T), where each G_t is a directed stage subgraph of operator instances with context-specific instructions and memory-buffer input references, rather than a single linear reasoning chain.

## Method

DyFlow employs a hierarchical designer–executor architecture: a lightweight designer policy π_θ (Phi-4 trained with LoRA) generates a stage subgraph G_t = (V_t, E_t, v_start, C_end) at each step, selecting and configuring operator instances from a fixed template set (e.g., DECOMPOSE_PROBLEM, GENERATE_ANSWER, REVIEW_SOLUTION, REFINE_ANSWER), while a fixed executor LLM runs those instances in topological order using a global key-value memory buffer. Crucially, all control flow—branching, looping, backtracking, termination—is delegated to the designer's per-step decisions rather than hardcoded into graph topology, so dynamic workflows emerge from context-aware replanning. The designer is trained in two phases: (1) supervised fine-tuning via knowledge distillation from GPT-4.1-generated successful trajectories, and (2) offline KTO preference optimization on self-play trajectories where subgraphs are labeled preferred or discarded based on full trajectory success, enabling the compact model to match or exceed proprietary designers (GPT-4.1, Claude-3.7-Sonnet) at a fraction of the inference cost.

## Results

DyFlow achieves the highest average accuracy of 61.45% across five reasoning domains (SocialMaze, PubMedQA, MATH, LiveBench, HumanEval), outperforming the strongest baseline MaAS (57.74%) by 3.71 points. Notably, DyFlow is trained only on MATH, PubMedQA, and LiveBench yet generalizes to the held-out HumanEval (92.07% pass@1) and SocialMaze (17.18% accuracy) benchmarks, where it surpasses all baselines by 3.7 and 3.8 points respectively. Under Pass@k evaluation, DyFlow reaches 0.9817 on HumanEval at k=5, demonstrating a strong reasoning upper bound.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DyFlow outperforms all seven baselines across every domain. Against prompting-based methods, DyFlow gains +7.02 over CoT (61.45 vs 54.43 avg), +5.52 over Self-Consistency, +5.65 over LLM-Debate, and +8.09 over Self-Refine, with the largest margins on SocialMaze (+11.07 over CoT) and LiveBench (+9.34 over CoT). Among automated agent frameworks, DyFlow surpasses AFlow (57.50) by 3.95 points and MaAS (57.74) by 3.71 points on average; the gap is most pronounced on SocialMaze where DyFlow scores 17.18 versus MaAS's 13.36 and AFlow's 11.45. ADAS performs worst among agent frameworks (52.99 avg), essentially matching the Vanilla baseline, confirming that offline workflow search without runtime feedback provides limited benefit. DyFlow's advantage is consistent across both in-domain (MATH, PubMedQA, LiveBench) and zero-shot (HumanEval, SocialMaze) settings, with the largest zero-shot gain on SocialMaze (+3.82 over MaAS) and HumanEval (+3.66 over AFlow).

## Relation to Prior Work

DyFlow extends the line of automated agentic workflow generation (AFlow, ADAS, MaAS) by shifting from one-shot, pre-execution planning to a feedback-driven, subgoal-level replanning loop. It departs from static multi-agent frameworks (MetaGPT, AutoGen, CAMEL) and query-specific configurators (DyLAN, MaAS) by delegating all execution control—including conditional branching and iteration—to a learned designer that observes intermediate outputs at every step. The training pipeline repurposes KTO preference optimization in a self-play setting, where the designer generates its own trajectories and learns from trajectory-level success/failure signals, combining this with supervised distillation from a proprietary model to initialize a lightweight planner.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstration that a 14B open-weight model (Phi-4) trained with SFT + KTO self-play can match or exceed GPT-4.1 and Claude-3.7-Sonnet as a structured planner is practically significant for resource-constrained deployments, and the cross-domain generalization (trained on 3 of 5 domains, tested zero-shot on 2) suggests the learned planning policy captures transferable reasoning structure rather than domain-specific heuristics. Key limitations include the absence of external tool/API integration (operators are symbolic and textual only), the lack of error bars across runs, and the relatively small training sets (~50–87 examples per domain). Open questions include whether the framework scales to tool-using, embodied, or multimodal agents, and whether the KTO-based self-play loop can be made online to adapt to novel task distributions without retraining.
