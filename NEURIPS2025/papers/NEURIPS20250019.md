## Problem

Controlling the spatial and semantic structure of diffusion-generated images remains difficult: training-based methods like ControlNet require handcrafted condition maps and per-condition retraining, while inversion-based test-time approaches (DDIM inversion, TRAC) incur high inference cost from dual-path denoising. The paper asks whether strong structural guidance can be extracted from a raw reference image in a single forward step—without inversion, retraining, or multi-step attention accumulation—and whether the quality of that guidance can be independently tuned.

## Input / Output

The method takes a raw RGB reference image (512×512 or 1024×1024) and a text prompt as input, with no preprocessing such as Canny edge or depth map extraction. The reference image may be a compositional assembly of objects cropped from multiple sources, optionally Gaussian-blurred to reduce copy-paste seams. Output is a single structure-conditioned image generated by FLUX.1-dev (DiT backbone, FlowMatchEulerDiscrete scheduler, 25 denoising steps, timestep range 1000–400, guidance scale 6.5) that aligns with both the reference's spatial layout and the prompt's semantic content.

## Method

FreeControl performs one-step attention extraction: it forward-simulates a noised latent from the reference image at a single key timestep t*=661, runs one denoising step, and extracts self-attention query matrices Q at each transformer layer. During generation, these fixed Q matrices replace the model's dynamically computed queries at every denoising step, while keys and values remain dynamic to preserve responsiveness to the evolving latent. A layer-aware injection strategy restricts structural Q to the last 25 transformer layers, avoiding disruption of low-level appearance features (color, texture) handled by early layers. Latent-Condition Decoupling (LCD) further separates the latent construction from the timestep conditioning: instead of adding Gaussian noise, a noise-free scaled latent x̃=(1−σ)·x₀ is used, where σ (typically 0.25–0.5) controls stability, while the key timestep independently governs structural granularity from coarse layout to fine contour detail. The entire pipeline is training-free, adds only ~5% inference cost over the vanilla FLUX pipeline, and is compatible with fine-tuned or LoRA-augmented model variants.

## Results

On the COCO 2017 validation set (5,000 images, 512×512), FreeControl (One-Step Extraction) achieves the best FID of 15.64, SSIM of 0.7564, and PSNR of 17.49, substantially outperforming all ControlNet-style baselines on structural similarity metrics. For depth-conditioned structural fidelity, FreeControl attains the lowest MSE of 21.18, compared to 27.79–47.04 for baselines. CLIP-Text similarity is competitive at 0.3087, within ~0.007 of the best baseline (UniControlNet Depth at 0.3156). Inference time is 26.11 s on an RTX A6000, only ~5% above the vanilla FLUX pipeline (24.89 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FreeControl decisively outperforms all five ControlNet-family baselines (ControlNet, ControlNet++, UniControl, UniControlNet, FLUX ControlNet) on FID (15.64 vs. 17.97–28.24), SSIM (0.7564 vs. 0.1968–0.3714), and PSNR (17.49 vs. 9.71–11.66), reflecting its use of the full raw image as structural input rather than lossy edge or depth maps. On Canny F1, FreeControl (0.28) trails UniControl (0.35) and ControlNet++ (0.30), indicating that handcrafted edge maps still capture some edge-level detail that attention-based guidance does not fully replicate. On depth MSE, FreeControl (21.18) is the best performer, beating ControlNet++ (27.79) and FLUX ControlNet Depth (47.04). CLIP-Text similarity is competitive but not dominant: FreeControl (0.3087) is slightly below UniControlNet Depth (0.3156) and UniControl Canny (0.3103), suggesting a minor trade-off between structural rigidity and prompt semantic alignment. Against the two image-editing baselines (In-Context Edit and Taming Rectified Flow, reported in Appendix D), FreeControl matches or exceeds Taming Rectified Flow on most metrics while In-Context Edit achieves higher PSNR (21.34) by preserving unedited regions but at the cost of lower CLIP-T (0.3024) and limited generative flexibility.

## Relation to Prior Work

FreeControl departs from two dominant paradigms in structure-conditioned generation: training-based conditioning (ControlNet, T2I-Adapter, UniControl) that requires per-condition retraining and handcrafted maps, and test-time inversion-based methods (DDIM inversion, Prompt-to-Prompt, TRAC) that extract attention across multiple denoising steps. Its core conceptual move is collapsing multi-step attention extraction into a single forward-simulated step at one key timestep, combined with Latent-Condition Decoupling to separate the noised latent from the conditioning timestep. This repurposes the forward diffusion process (rather than reverse inversion) as a lightweight structural probe, and the layer-aware injection strategy builds on the observation that deeper transformer layers encode spatial-semantic structure while early layers encode appearance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~5% inference overhead and training-free nature make FreeControl immediately deployable alongside fine-tuned or LoRA-augmented models where ControlNet-style branches break down—a practical gap the paper demonstrates clearly. The compositional reference-image interface lowers the barrier for non-expert users to specify spatial layout without segmentation tools or prompt engineering. Key limitations include the inability to accept sparse condition maps (edges, segmentation) and the empirical (rather than theoretical) justification for the one-step assumption; whether the single-timestep extraction generalizes to video diffusion or substantially different architectures remains open.
