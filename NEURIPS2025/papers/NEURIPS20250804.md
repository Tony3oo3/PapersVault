## Problem

Estimating heterogeneous treatment effects (HTEs) for continuous-valued (dosage-dependent) interventions on time-to-event (TTE) outcomes remains largely unexplored in the ML literature. While HTE methods for continuous treatments and survival models for TTE outcomes have been developed separately, no existing approach jointly handles dosage-dependent treatments, non-dosage treatment options, and censored survival data in a unified framework.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250804/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes tabular patient covariates, a categorical treatment identifier (from M mutually exclusive options), a continuous dosage value normalized to [0,1], and a discrete time index, operating on data in person-period (long) format where each patient contributes one row per time interval they remain at-risk. Outputs are per-time-step conditional hazard probabilities for each treatment-dosage pair, from which treatment-specific survival curves, patient-specific dose-response curves (RMST as a function of dosage), and HTE estimates between any two interventions are derived. The architecture natively supports mixed settings where some treatment arms are continuous-valued and others are non-dosage (binary).

## Method

DoseSurv is a discrete-time survival network with a shared representation Φ(x) and treatment-specific hazard estimator heads that perform multi-output binary classification across t_m time intervals. The core innovation is the RBF hazard estimator: each layer's weights and biases are parameterized as linear combinations of Gaussian radial basis functions with learnable centers, bandwidths, and amplitude weights, making network parameters continuous functions of dosage q and preserving dose-response continuity without discretizing dosage into bins. To address covariate shifts from treatment selection, dosage selection, censoring, and event occurrence, DoseSurv applies an IPM regularization term that minimizes the Wasserstein distance (computed via Sinkhorn iterations) between the representation distributions of each treatment × dosage-bin × time-bin subpopulation and the overall at-risk population. The total loss combines the standard binary cross-entropy over at-risk rows with this IPM penalty weighted by a hyperparameter γ, and the model is trained end-to-end with Adam optimization.

## Results

On the Twins dataset (continuous treatment), DoseSurv achieves the lowest √MSE of 12.501 ± 0.063, outperforming the best baseline DeepHit (13.272 ± 0.203) by 6.2%. On the binary Twins setting, DoseSurv records √MSE of 15.601 ± 0.531, beating the closest competitor RSF (15.923 ± 0.022) by 2.1%. Across all three synthetic scenarios (S1–S3) and five levels of dosage-selection bias (η = 0–4), DoseSurv or its no-IPM variant consistently achieves the best MISE and DPE, with IPM regularization providing the largest gains in the two-continuous-treatment scenario S2. On the external GBSG breast-cancer validation set, DoseSurv attains the best C^td (0.688), IBS (0.098), and INBLL (0.338) among all methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DoseSurv outperforms all six baselines on both Twins settings. In the continuous-treatment scenario, DeepHit is the strongest baseline (√MSE 13.272), yet DoseSurv still leads by 6.2%; DeepSurv, RSF, and NSC trail by 13.8–15.7%. In the binary-treatment scenario, RSF is the closest competitor (√MSE 15.923 vs. DoseSurv's 15.601, a 2.1% gap), while SurvITE performs worst at 19.599 (25.6% worse than DoseSurv). On the synthetic benchmarks (S1–S3), DoseSurv dominates across all dosage-bias levels; the gap over baselines widens with increasing confounding (η), and IPM regularization yields the most pronounced improvement in S2 (two continuous treatments), where it reduces MISE substantially. Among baselines, RSF and RSF-T tend to achieve the lowest DPE on synthetic data, while DeepSurv(-T), DeepHit(-T), and NSC-T show broadly similar performance across metrics. The GBSG external validation (Table C.1) confirms DoseSurv's advantage in calibration (IBS 0.098 vs. next-best NSC 0.108) and discrimination (C^td 0.688 vs. RSF 0.685), with IPM regularization contributing measurable gains over the no-IPM variant.

## Relation to Prior Work

DoseSurv sits at the intersection of two previously separate research threads: varying-coefficient networks for continuous-valued treatments (VCNet, Nie et al. 2021) and discrete-time survival models for HTE estimation (SurvITE, Curth et al. 2021). The paper departs from VCNet by replacing its truncated polynomial basis with Gaussian RBFs featuring learnable centers and bandwidths, and from SurvITE by extending the IPM-based representation balancing from binary treatments to continuous dosages via discretized dosage and time bins. The IPM regularization itself traces back to Shalit et al. (2017), adapted here to handle the additional covariate shifts introduced by censoring and event occurrence in TTE settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The combination of continuous-valued (dosage-dependent) treatments and time-to-event outcomes is highly relevant in clinical practice—chemotherapy dosing, insulin regimens, and cardiovascular treatments all involve dosage-dependent interventions with survival endpoints—yet had no dedicated ML framework before this work. The reinterpretation of the Twins dataset as a continuous-treatment benchmark provides a reusable real-world evaluation point for future methods. Key open questions include the sensitivity of the IPM regularization strength γ to covariate shift magnitude, scalability to very high-dimensional covariates or many treatment arms, and whether the discrete-time formulation loses information relative to continuous-time alternatives in long-horizon settings.
