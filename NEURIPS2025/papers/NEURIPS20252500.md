## Problem

Existing imitation learning methods treat observation features as static during action sequence generation, extracting them from a single time-point and holding them fixed while the policy denoises actions. This decoupling overlooks the causal reciprocity between perception and action that humans naturally exploit, where intermediate action feedback continuously refines environmental understanding. The result is abrupt or discontinuous motions, particularly under dynamic or partially observable conditions.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (encoded via ResNet-18), 2D keypoints (via fully connected networks), and proprioceptive state (via MLP), which are jointly encoded into a Gaussian posterior over latent features. Unlike standard DP where this latent is frozen across all K=100 denoising steps, DP-AG evolves the latent at every diffusion step. The output is a short-horizon action sequence (6D rotation or 3D axis-angle per step) generated by iterative denoising, with the latent observation dynamically updated in lockstep with each denoising step.

## Method

DP-AG extends Diffusion Policy by replacing the static observation conditioning with an action-guided SDE over latent features, where the Vector-Jacobian Product of the diffusion noise predictor with respect to the latent serves as a structured stochastic drift at each denoising step. Observation features are first encoded into a Gaussian posterior via variational inference, then reparameterized with VJP-guided noise (scaled by a hyperparameter γ) so that latents evolve phase-aligned with action diffusion. A cycle-consistent InfoNCE contrastive loss enforces agreement between noise predictions conditioned on the static latent versus the VJP-evolved latent, pulling matched pairs together while pushing apart mismatched ones within the batch. The total training objective combines the standard DP noise-matching loss, the contrastive loss, and a KL divergence between the variational posterior and a Gaussian prior centered at the static latent. Theoretically, the paper derives a variational lower bound for the action-guided SDE and proves that minimizing the contrastive loss upper-bounds the latent drift, guaranteeing mutual continuity in both latent and action trajectories.

## Results

On Push-T (image), DP-AG achieves 0.93 target coverage, outperforming DP (0.87) and AdaFlow (0.87) by 6%. On the novel Dynamic Push-T benchmark, DP-AG reaches 0.80 coverage versus 0.65 for DP and 0.67 for AdaFlow, a 13% absolute gain. On real-world UR5 tasks, DP-AG delivers at least 23% higher manipulation success (e.g., 85% vs. 0% on Peg-in-Hole) and approximately 60% smoother actions (average jerk reduced from 0.096 to 0.036) compared to DP. On Robomimic and Franka Kitchen, DP-AG achieves near-perfect success rates (1.00 on most subtasks), matching or marginally exceeding DP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DP-AG consistently outperforms all six baselines across every benchmark. Against the closest diffusion-based competitor DP [Chi, 2023], DP-AG gains 6% on Push-T (img), 4% on Push-T (kp), and 15% on Dynamic Push-T, with the largest real-world gap on Peg-in-Hole (85% vs. 0% success). Against the strongest flow-matching baseline AdaFlow [Hu, 2024], DP-AG leads by 6% on Push-T (img) and 13% on Dynamic Push-T, indicating that the VJP-guided latent evolution provides benefits beyond what deterministic flow matching alone can achieve. Against FlowPolicy [Zhang, 2025], the gap is even wider (8% on Push-T img, 27% on Dynamic Push-T). Among direct-mapping methods, DP-AG surpasses BET [Shafiullah, 2022] by 13% on Push-T (img) and 22% on Dynamic Push-T, IBC [Florence, 2022] by 18% and 28% respectively, and LSTM-GMM [Mandlekar, 2022] by 24% and 46%. On Robomimic and Franka Kitchen (Table 2), DP-AG matches DP's near-perfect scores on most subtasks while improving Transport (mh) from 0.89 to 0.94, confirming that the perception-action interplay is most beneficial under dynamic or partially observable conditions.

## Relation to Prior Work

DP-AG extends Diffusion Policy (Chi et al., 2023) by replacing its static observation conditioning with a dynamic latent evolution governed by an action-guided SDE, where the VJP of the diffusion noise predictor serves as a structured drift term. This draws on variational inference (PlaNet, Dreamer, Embed to Control) for the Gaussian posterior over observations, and on stochastic adjoint sensitivity methods (Li et al., 2019) for efficient VJP-based gradient propagation through SDEs. The key departure from all prior IL methods (BC, DP, flow-matching, VLA) is the explicit closure of the perception-action loop: rather than freezing observation features during a K-step denoising sequence, DP-AG refines them at every diffusion step via a cycle-consistent InfoNCE loss that enforces coherence between static and evolving latents.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a fundamental gap in diffusion-based policy learning—the assumption that perception is static during action generation—and provides both a principled variational lower bound and a continuity theorem linking contrastive alignment to smooth latent-action trajectories. The real-world UR5 results, particularly the jump from 0% to 85% success on Peg-in-Hole, suggest that dynamic latent reinterpretation can unlock capabilities (e.g., inferring 3D geometry from 2D cues under occlusion) that static-feature policies fundamentally lack. Open questions include whether the VJP overhead scales to high-dimensional or long-horizon tasks, whether the approach generalizes beyond manipulation to locomotion or navigation, and how it interacts with the rapidly growing VLA paradigm where perception is already partially dynamic via language grounding.
