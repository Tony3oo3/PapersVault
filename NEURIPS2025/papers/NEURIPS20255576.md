## Problem

Autoregressive video diffusion models trained with Teacher Forcing or Diffusion Forcing suffer from exposure bias: they are conditioned on ground-truth or noise-corrupted context frames during training but must condition on their own imperfect outputs at inference time. This train-test distribution mismatch causes progressive error accumulation, manifesting as oversaturation and quality degradation over longer sequences. Prior attempts to mitigate this (e.g., injecting noise into context at inference) sacrifice temporal consistency and do not fundamentally resolve the distributional gap.

## Input / Output

The model takes a text prompt (drawn from a filtered, LLM-extended VidProM set of ~250k prompts) and generates a 5-second video at 16 FPS and 832×480 resolution in a compressed latent space encoded by a causal 3D VAE. Generation proceeds autoregressively either frame-by-frame or in chunks of 3 latent frames, with the chunk-wise variant achieving 17 FPS throughput and 0.69 s first-frame latency, while the frame-wise variant achieves 8.9 FPS with 0.45 s latency on a single H100 GPU. The rolling KV cache mechanism extends generation to arbitrarily long videos without recomputation.

## Method

Self Forcing performs autoregressive self-rollout during training: each frame is denoised conditioned on previously self-generated outputs via KV caching, exactly mirroring the inference procedure. To keep this sequential process tractable, the authors use a 4-step diffusion backbone and a stochastic gradient truncation strategy that backpropagates only through the final denoising step of each frame (randomly sampled per iteration), while detaching gradients across frames through the KV cache. Instead of frame-wise denoising MSE, the training objective is a holistic video-level distribution-matching loss (DMD, SiD, or R3GAN) applied to the complete generated sequence, aligning the full-sequence output distribution with real video data. A rolling KV cache of fixed size L enables O(TL) long-video extrapolation, with a local-attention training trick (preventing the model from attending to the first chunk when denoising the last) to avoid flickering artifacts when the initial image latent scrolls out of the cache.

## Results

Self Forcing (chunk-wise) achieves the highest VBench total score of 84.31 (Quality 85.07, Semantic 81.28) among all compared models while delivering real-time throughput of 17.0 FPS with 0.69 s first-frame latency on a single H100 GPU. The frame-wise variant achieves a near-identical total score of 84.26 with the lowest latency of 0.45 s. In a user preference study using 1,003 MovieGenBench prompts, Self Forcing is consistently preferred over all baselines, including the many-step Wan2.1 model it is initialized from.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CausVid (the closest prior method, also 1.3B and 17 FPS), Self Forcing chunk-wise improves VBench total from 81.20 to 84.31 (+3.11), quality from 84.05 to 85.07, and semantic alignment from 69.80 to 81.28 (+11.48), demonstrating that matching the true inference-time distribution via self-rollout substantially reduces error accumulation. Compared to the non-causal Wan2.1 base model (84.26 total, 103 s latency), Self Forcing matches or slightly exceeds quality (84.31 total, 81.28 semantic vs. 80.09) while being ~150× faster in latency. Among chunk-wise AR baselines, Self Forcing outperforms SkyReels-V2 (82.67) and MAGI-1 (79.18) on all VBench dimensions. The frame-wise variant (84.26 total, 0.45 s latency) beats NOVA (80.12) and Pyramid Flow (81.72) on total and quality scores while offering the lowest latency of any compared model. In the user preference study, Self Forcing is preferred over every baseline, including Wan2.1, confirming that the VBench gains translate to perceptual quality improvements.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly builds on CausVid (Yin et al., CVPR 2025), which trains few-step autoregressive video diffusion models via Diffusion Forcing (DF) and Distribution Matching Distillation (DMD). The key conceptual departure is identifying that CausVid's DF training outputs do not originate from the model's actual inference-time distribution, meaning DMD optimizes against the wrong target; Self Forcing corrects this by performing autoregressive self-rollout with KV caching during training so that the holistic distribution-matching loss (DMD, SiD, or GAN) aligns the true model distribution. The approach also draws on GAN principles for distribution matching, few-step diffusion backbones, and the rolling KV cache mechanism from LLM streaming literature (attention sinks), while positioning itself within the broader AR-diffusion hybrid paradigm that includes Teacher Forcing, Diffusion Forcing, and rolling diffusion variants.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By bridging the train-test distribution gap, Self Forcing enables real-time streaming video generation (17 FPS, sub-second latency on a single H100) at quality matching or exceeding much slower bidirectional diffusion models, opening the door to genuinely interactive applications like live streaming, gaming, and world simulation. The counterintuitive finding that sequential self-rollout training is more wall-clock efficient than parallel TF/DF training challenges the assumption that parallelizable training is always superior, and the authors advocate a broader paradigm of parallel pre-training followed by sequential post-training. Open questions remain: quality still degrades beyond the training context length, gradient truncation may limit long-range dependency learning, and whether the approach generalizes to substantially longer sequences or other continuous-valued sequence domains (e.g., audio, 3D) is untested.
