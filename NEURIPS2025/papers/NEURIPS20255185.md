## Problem

Existing federated learning backdoor defenses rely on empirical statistical measures (update norm, OOD detection, consistency) that are loosely coupled with backdoor attacks, allowing SOTA attacks like 3DFed, CerP, and DarkFed to mimic benign update statistics and bypass detection. The paper identifies this fundamental decoupling as the root cause of defense failures and seeks a measure that directly reflects the inherent maliciousness of a model rather than relying on indirect statistical proxies.

## Input / Output

MARS operates at the central server and consumes only the model parameters (weights) of all local models uploaded in each FL round—no clean data, trigger knowledge, or auxiliary datasets are required. It produces two outputs: a binary classification of each client as benign or malicious (via cluster assignment), and the aggregated global model computed as the weighted average of only the trusted clients' parameters. The method is agnostic to the number of attackers and works even when malicious clients constitute a majority (up to 95%).

## Method

MARS introduces Backdoor Energy (BE), defined as the Lipschitz constant of each neuron's sub-network, derived as a data-free approximation of a theoretical upper bound on the expected activation difference between clean and triggered inputs (Theorem 4.1). To amplify malignity, the top-κ% BE values per layer are extracted and concatenated into a Concentrated Backdoor Energy (CBE) vector per model. A novel K-WMeans clustering algorithm uses Wasserstein distance (instead of Euclidean or cosine distance) as the metric, making it insensitive to element ordering—a critical property since backdoor neurons may appear at different positions across clients. Cluster selection uses the L1 norm of the cluster center (smaller norm = benign) rather than majority voting, with a Wasserstein-distance threshold ε to detect the all-benign scenario and avoid unnecessary model exclusion.

## Results

MARS achieves a Comprehensive Ability of Defense (CAD) of 93.80–97.40 across MNIST, CIFAR-10, and CIFAR-100 under all three SOTA attacks (MRA, CerP, 3DFed), consistently outperforming all eight baselines. Against the hardest attack (3DFed), MARS maintains 100% True Positive Rate and 0% False Positive Rate while keeping Attack Success Rate at 9.72–9.86% (near the clean-model baseline of ~9.3–9.7%), whereas most defenses see ASR remain above 60–100%. On CIFAR-10 under 3DFed, MARS achieves 85.07% clean accuracy with 9.86% ASR (CAD 93.80), compared to FedAvg's 84.37% ACC with 96.76% ASR (CAD 46.90) and FedCLP's 69.25% ACC with 7.55% ASR (CAD 80.85).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MARS dominates all nine baselines on the comprehensive CAD metric across every dataset–attack combination. Against the most challenging 3DFed attack, norm-constraint and OOD-based defenses (Multi-Krum, RFLBAT, FLAME, FLDetector) collapse to CAD of 33–57%, while consistency-based FoolsGold and DeepSight also fail (CAD 45–66%). FedCLP (CLP-based pruning) is the closest competitor, achieving CAD of 80.85–88.59 on 3DFed, but at the cost of 1.64–15.82% lower clean accuracy than MARS (e.g., 69.25% vs. 85.07% on CIFAR-10). The most recent defense, BackdoorIndicator (USENIX Sec. 2024), handles MRA well (CAD 91–93) but degrades sharply against CerP (CAD 62–65, ASR ~72%) and 3DFed (CAD 33–42, ASR ~97%), confirming that heuristic OOD-based detection cannot handle constraint-aware attacks. MARS's advantage stems from its malignity-aware backdoor energy, which is tightly coupled to backdoor intent rather than relying on loosely coupled empirical statistics that SOTA attacks can mimic.

## Relation to Prior Work

MARS departs from the three dominant families of FL backdoor defenses—norm-constraint, OOD-detection, and consistency-detection—by arguing that all rely on empirical statistical measures that are 'loosely coupled' with backdoor intent and can be mimicked by sophisticated attacks (3DFed, CerP, DarkFed). Conceptually, it repurposes the Lipschitz-constant idea from CLP [44], but instead of using it to prune neurons (which is imprecise and degrades accuracy), MARS treats the per-neuron Lipschitz constant as a malignity feature for downstream Wasserstein-distance-based clustering. The theoretical upper bound on backdoor energy (Theorem 4.1) provides a rigorous justification for this approximation that CLP's empirical correlation lacks, and the K-WMeans clustering with Wasserstein distance is a novel metric choice that addresses the order-sensitivity problem inherent in Euclidean and cosine distances for CBE vectors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a critical and timely gap: SOTA adaptive backdoor attacks have rendered all previously published FL defenses ineffective, yet no principled alternative existed. MARS's consistent 100% TPR / 0% FPR across attacker ratios from 0% to 95% is practically significant for real-world FL deployments where the attacker fraction is unknown. However, the defense is explicitly scoped to backdoor attacks and does not extend to free-rider or privacy-stealing threats, and the adaptive-attack results (Table 3) reveal that at high regularization (λ ≥ 0.05), the original cluster-selection strategy inverts, requiring the MARS* majority-based variant—a vulnerability that a sufficiently informed adversary could exploit. Open questions include whether the BE-based approach generalizes to non-visual modalities (only NLP is briefly tested in the appendix) and to very large-scale models where per-neuron SVD computation may become prohibitive.
