## Problem

Visual generative models (VQVAE) and understanding models (CLIP) rely on fundamentally different tokenizers, making it difficult to unify them in a single multimodal framework. Prior attempts to jointly train a tokenizer with both reconstruction and contrastive objectives suffered severe performance degradation, commonly attributed to loss conflicts between pixel-level and semantic supervision. This paper shows the true bottleneck is the limited representational capacity of the discrete token space, not an inherent conflict between the two learning objectives.

## Input / Output

The tokenizer takes 256×256 RGB images and paired text captions as input, producing a grid of discrete visual tokens (H×W×K, where K=8 sub-codebook indices per spatial position) at a 16× downsampling ratio. For MLLM integration, every K consecutive sub-codebook indices are merged into a single visual token for autoregressive prediction, and a depth transformer head predicts the next K codes jointly. The unified MLLM additionally accepts free-form text prompts and image-question pairs, outputting generated images or text responses.

## Method

UniTok introduces multi-codebook quantization (MCQ), which splits each latent vector into n chunks and quantizes each chunk independently against a small sub-codebook, exponentially scaling the effective vocabulary (e.g., 8 sub-codebooks of 4096 entries yield 2^88 combinations) while avoiding the dead-code problem of large monolithic codebooks. For token factorization, the paper replaces conventional linear projections with adapted multi-head attention modules that use average pooling instead of concatenation to compress channels, preserving semantics during dimensionality reduction. The tokenizer is trained with a combined VQVAE reconstruction loss (pixel, LPIPS perceptual, GAN discriminator, and VQ commitment terms) plus a CLIP-style image-text contrastive loss, using a ViTamin-L/16 hybrid architecture. Ablations confirm that MCQ outperforms residual quantization by 15–45× in quantization loss at 64-d latent dimension, and that reconstruction and contrastive objectives do not inherently conflict once the discrete space is sufficiently expressive.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256, UniTok achieves a reconstruction FID of 0.38 and 78.6% zero-shot classification accuracy (with CLIP weight initialization), surpassing the continuous SD-VAE (0.61 rFID) and all prior discrete tokenizers. In the unified MLLM setting, UniTok scores 76.8 on VQAv2, 61.1 on GQA, 51.6 on TextVQA, and 1448 on MME, outperforming the Liquid baseline by 5.5%, 2.7%, 9.2%, and 329 points respectively. For class-conditional generation under LlamaGen, UniTok reduces CFG-free gFID from 14.65 (VQGAN) to 2.51, a 12.1-point improvement. On GenEval, UniTok achieves an overall score of 0.59 with only 30M training images, competitive with diffusion models trained on 2B images.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniTok decisively outperforms all prior discrete tokenizers on reconstruction: 0.38 rFID vs. VQ-GAN (4.98), RQ-VAE (1.30), VAR (0.90), TokenFlow (1.37), and VILA-U (1.80), and even surpasses the continuous SD-VAE (0.61). On zero-shot classification, UniTok† (78.6%) beats CLIP (76.2%) and VILA-U† (73.3%) but trails SigLIP (80.5%) and ViTamin (81.2%), which are dedicated understanding models. In the unified MLLM setting, UniTok outperforms Liquid [59] by 5.5% on VQAv2, 9.2% on TextVQA, and 329 points on MME, and beats VILA-U [61] by 1.5% on VQAv2 and 112 points on MME-Perception. On GenEval, UniTok (0.59) surpasses Liquid (0.55) and LWM (0.47) but remains below Show-o (0.68, trained on 2B images) and DALL-E 3 (0.67). On MJHQ-30K at 256×256, UniTok (FID 7.46) beats VILA-U (12.81), Show-o (15.18), and LWM (17.77) but trails Liquid (5.47 at 512×512) and diffusion models at 1024×1024. For CFG-free class-conditional generation, UniTok (gFID 2.51) dramatically outperforms VQGAN (14.65) under the same LlamaGen generator, demonstrating that semantic supervision yields a more structured latent space for autoregressive modeling.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the VQVAE/VQGAN lineage for discrete visual tokenization and the CLIP contrastive framework for semantic alignment, integrating both into a single unified tokenizer. It departs from prior unified-tokenizer work (VILA-U, TokenFlow) that attributed performance degradation to irreconcilable conflicts between reconstruction and semantic losses; instead, this paper identifies the true bottleneck as the limited representational capacity of the discrete token space (specifically, token factorization and discretization). The proposed multi-codebook quantization (MCQ) is a divide-and-conquer alternative to residual quantization, and the attention-based projection for factorization replaces the standard linear layers used in ViT-VQGAN.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that reconstruction and semantic supervision do not inherently conflict—only the discrete capacity bottleneck does—reframes how the community should approach unified tokenizer design, shifting focus from loss balancing to representational capacity. Practically, MCQ offers a drop-in replacement for single-codebook or RQ quantization that scales vocabulary exponentially without the dead-code problem, making it broadly applicable to any VQ-based generative or understanding pipeline. Key limitations include training for only one epoch (insufficient for full CLIP convergence) and evaluation primarily at 256×256 resolution; whether the capacity-bottleneck diagnosis holds at higher resolutions or with different backbone architectures remains open.
