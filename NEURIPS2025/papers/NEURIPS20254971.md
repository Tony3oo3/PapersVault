## Problem

Current text-to-image safety evaluations rely on homogeneous rater pools or policy-driven annotations, failing to capture how harm perception varies across demographic groups and lived experiences. This leads to systems misaligned with significant user populations, raising the question: whose safety are we actually designing for? The paper argues that pluralistic alignment requires both a dataset covering diverse viewpoints and techniques to evaluate and steer models toward those perspectives.

## Input / Output

The dataset consists of 1000 adversarial prompt-image pairs (text prompt + generated RGB image) sourced from Adversarial Nibbler, each annotated by 20–30 raters from distinct demographic trisections. Each rater provides two 5-point Likert ratings (harm to self, harm to others), a violation-type label (Bias/Explicit/Violent), an uncertainty option, and optional free-text explanations. Downstream analyses produce group cohesion metrics (GAI), false-negative rates for safety classifiers, and Kendall's Tau correlations between LLM-rater outputs and human ratings per demographic group.

## Method

The core contribution is the DIVE dataset, built by recruiting 637 raters via Prolific with uniform representation across 30 intersectional demographic trisections (2 gender × 3 age × 5 ethnicity), each rating ~50 PI pairs. PI pairs were selected to maximize perceptual subjectivity by prioritizing those with split opinions among policy raters (U=3 > 2 > 4 > 1 > 5 > 6). Analysis employs the Group Association Index (GAI) to quantify whether intersectional groups show higher internal cohesion than single-demographic groups, and simulations that vary rater-pool demographics to identify content flagged as unsafe by one group but safe by others. For LLM-rater steerability, Gemma 4B and Gemini 1.5 Flash are prompted in-context with demographic group labels (zero-shot and few-shot) to simulate specific perspectives, with alignment measured via Kendall's Tau against the corresponding human rater subset.

## Results

The DIVE dataset (1000 prompt-image pairs, 35,164 harm evaluations from 637 raters across 30 demographic intersections) reveals that demographic attributes significantly shape harm perception: Women rate harm to self higher than Men (probability 0.55, p<0.01), and Black raters rate higher than White raters (probability 0.57, p<0.01). Intersectional groups show higher cohesion than single demographics, with GenZ-Black (GAI=1.38) and Millennial-Black (GAI=1.29) exhibiting the strongest group association. In safety classifier comparisons, all three baselines (policy raters, LlavaGuard, ShieldGemma) show high false negative rates on 'Bias' content relative to diverse raters, and agree on only 9% of pairs as unsafe. For LLM steerability, adding demographic information to prompts increases Kendall's Tau correlation with diverse raters from ~0.024 (no demographic) to ~0.23 (with demographic), with Gemini 1.5 Flash + examples reaching up to 0.302.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three safety evaluation baselines (policy raters from AdvNib, LlavaGuard, ShieldGemma) against the diverse rater pool from DIVE. Policy raters flag 24% of pairs as unsafe, LlavaGuard 34%, and ShieldGemma 25%, but all three agree on only 88 pairs (9%) as unsafe, revealing substantial blind spots. All classifiers show notably high false negative rates on 'Bias' violations compared to diverse raters, while their false negative rates on 'Explicit' and 'Violent' content are lower for policy raters and LlavaGuard. For LLM-based raters, Gemma (4B) without demographic information achieves near-zero correlation (Kendall's Tau ≈ 0.024) with diverse raters, while adding demographic context raises it to ~0.23. Gemini 1.5 Flash with demographic information and in-context examples from DIVE achieves the highest correlations (up to 0.302 for S-Asian and White groups), outperforming all Gemma configurations, though absolute correlations remain modest, indicating significant room for improvement in LLM-based pluralistic safety evaluation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DICES dataset (Aroyo et al., NeurIPS 2024) from text-only conversational AI safety to the multimodal text-to-image domain, while introducing finer-grained demographic trisections (30 intersectional groups of gender × age × ethnicity) rather than broader single-axis demographics. It sources prompt-image pairs from Adversarial Nibbler (Quaye et al., FAccT 2024) and builds on the GRASP disagreement-analysis framework (Prabhakaran et al., NAACL 2024) for quantifying group cohesion. The LLM steerability experiments connect to the pluralistic alignment literature (Sorensen et al., ICML 2024; Value Kaleidoscope, AAAI 2024) by providing large-scale human grounding data to test whether prompted LLMs can simulate intersectional perspectives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DIVE is the first large-scale multimodal dataset (35,164 evaluations from 637 raters across 30 demographic intersections) designed explicitly for pluralistic alignment, and its finding that all current safety classifiers—both policy raters and automated models like LlavaGuard and ShieldGemma—show substantially higher false-negative rates on bias-related content compared to diverse raters suggests existing T2I safety pipelines have systematic blind spots. The modest LLM steerability results (Kendall's Tau ≈ 0.23–0.30 even with demographic prompting and in-context examples) indicate that current LLMs cannot reliably simulate diverse human perspectives, pointing to a gap that training-based approaches must fill. Open questions include whether individual value profiles (beyond demographics) better predict harm perception, how to disentangle prompt-level from image-level safety effects, and how to scale pluralistic alignment beyond Likert-scale ratings to other modalities and tasks.
