## Problem

Existing multimodal time series forecasting methods primarily align text instructions with numerical sequences, leaving the rich visual data (e.g., satellite imagery) in forecasting datasets underexplored. Even when vision-language models are applied, their projection layers focus solely on pixel-level semantics and overlook the physical metadata—timestamps and geospatial coordinates—embedded in each image, which is critical for tasks like typhoon track and intensity prediction where pixel values must be correlated with time-specific geophysical attributes.

## Input / Output

The model consumes 12 hours of historical satellite infrared images (512×512 px, 5 km resolution, resized to 224×224) alongside the corresponding hourly time series of latitude, longitude, and central pressure, plus a text task instruction. Images are split into 28×28 non-overlapping patches and encoded by a frozen Qwen-2.5-VL vision encoder, while the time series is tokenized and concatenated with the instruction for the LLM. The output is the forecasted hourly latitude, longitude, and pressure for the next 12 hours, evaluated on the Digital Typhoon dataset (1978–2023, Western North Pacific, 1,116 typhoon sequences, 192,956 images) with a 0.7/0.15/0.15 train/val/test split.

## Method

PIPE embeds physical knowledge into a VLM (Qwen-2.5-VL) through two mechanisms applied to vision token positional encodings. First, physics-informed positional indexing replaces the standard 3D (temporal, height, width) indexing with real-world physical values: temporal IDs are computed as day-of-year × 24 + hour (range 0–8784), and spatial IDs use the actual latitude (0–180) and longitude (0–360) of each image patch center; these vision IDs are mapped to negative values to avoid range conflicts with sequential text token IDs, after which RoPE is applied to encode intra-instance positional relationships. Second, a variant-frequency sinusoidal encoding modifies the standard sinusoidal positional encoding by scaling each dimension with a physics-specific wavelength (p_day=366, p_hour=24, p_latitude=180, p_longitude=360), splitting the embedding dimensions into temporal (first half) and spatial (latter half) components so that different physical variables occupy distinct frequency domains. The vision encoder is frozen while the LLM is fine-tuned via LLaMA-Factory (AdamW, lr=1e-5, cosine schedule, batch size 1, 1 epoch) on 4×NVIDIA H800 GPUs, with the 32B variant using LoRA (rank 8) for efficiency.

## Results

On the Digital Typhoon dataset (6-hour lead time), PIPE-3B achieves an intensity MAE of 1.515 hPa, a 12% improvement over the best vision-free baseline (TiDE, 1.724 hPa), and a track distance error of 16.275 km, outperforming the best domain model GenCast (20.331 km) and ECMWF-HRES (27.181 km). For latitude and longitude, PIPE-3B achieves MAE of 0.084° and 0.095° respectively, the best among all compared methods. The sole exception is intensity RMSE, where TiDE (2.819) and PatchTST (2.867) marginally outperform PIPE-3B (2.981).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against vision-free AI baselines, PIPE-3B achieves the lowest intensity MAE (1.515 vs. TiDE's 1.724, a 12% gain) and the lowest track distance error (16.275 km vs. iTransformer's 31.248 km), though TiDE and PatchTST retain a slight edge on intensity RMSE (2.819/2.867 vs. 2.981). Against the original Qwen-2.5-VL-3B (same backbone without PIPE), the physics-informed encoding yields consistent gains across all metrics: intensity MAE drops from 1.617 to 1.515, and distance error from 17.129 to 16.275 km. Against the zero-shot Gemini-2.5-flash, PIPE-3B substantially outperforms on every metric (e.g., intensity MAE 1.515 vs. 1.924, distance 16.275 vs. 36.006 km). Against domain-specific NWP and ML weather models, PIPE-3B's 16.275 km track error beats GenCast (20.331 km), ECMWF-HRES (27.181 km), and PanGu (32.892 km), though TIFS reports a lower intensity RMSE (7.292 hPa) as it is a dedicated intensity-only scheme. The generalizability experiment on the Australian basin confirms that PIPE-3B (zero-shot, 19.090 km) already outperforms the tuned Qwen-2.5-VL-3B (19.718 km), and after tuning achieves 18.835 km.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Qwen-2.5-VL's 3D positional encoding architecture and RoPE, but departs fundamentally from prior multimodal time series forecasting work (TimeLLM, UniTime, AutoTimes) that aligns only text instructions with numerical sequences. Where existing VLMs (GPT-4o, Gemini, Qwen-VL) rely on projection layers (CLIP, Q-Former, MLP) that capture only pixel-level semantics, PIPE instead encodes global physical metadata—timestamps and geospatial coordinates—directly into positional IDs and variant-frequency sinusoidal encodings, requiring no additional architectural modules. The conceptual lineage runs from standard transformer positional encoding → 3D indexing for video → physics-informed indexing that preserves cross-instance spatiotemporal relationships.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete gap in climate science: satellite imagery carries rich geophysical context (latitude, longitude, time) that existing VLMs discard, and this omission limits high-stakes forecasting like typhoon track prediction. The lightweight design (modifying positional encoding rather than adding modules) makes the approach broadly applicable to other geospatial time series tasks beyond typhoons. Open questions include computational cost for longer sequences and higher-resolution images, whether explicit physical laws (not just metadata) can be further integrated, and whether the approach generalizes to non-cyclonic weather phenomena. The zero-shot transfer to the Australian basin suggests the physical encoding captures transferable structure, but validation on more diverse domains remains to be done.
