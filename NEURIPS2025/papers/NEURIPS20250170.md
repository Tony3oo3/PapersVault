## Problem

Existing human motion datasets capture short, decontextualized actions in static lab environments, focusing on kinematic 'what' rather than the semantic 'why' behind movement. This limits the development of embodied AI systems that must reason about human goals, adapt to dynamic scenes, and handle multi-person social interactions. The paper introduces I2M to provide intention-grounded, long-duration, two-person motion data in realistic, evolving home environments.

## Input / Output

The benchmark task predicts the next 5 seconds (150 frames at 30 fps) of 3D SMPL joint positions given the past 3 seconds (90 frames), with a 60-frame sliding window stride yielding 25,000 training and 606 test samples. Conditioning modalities are each embedded into 512-dimensional vectors: intention text via CLIP, multi-view RGB via ResNet-50, root trajectory via an MLP, and past motion semantics via MotionCLIP. The dataset itself provides 10.1 hours of 240 fps optical mocap (17.5M frames), 4-camera 30 fps RGB-D video (436K frames), instance-labeled 3D point clouds with mesh models, and 1,388 timestamped intention annotations across 215 sequences.

## Method

The paper's primary contribution is the I2M dataset, but the empirical benchmark adapts the AffordMotion diffusion model for conditional inpainting: a full 240-frame sequence is denoised from random noise with the first 90 frames replaced by ground-truth past motion, conditioned on scene point cloud and one of eight semantic input configurations (Self/Pair × Intention Text, RGB, Trajectory, MotionCLIP, or Multimodal combinations). The pretrained AffordMotion is fine-tuned for 20,000 steps on a single A100 GPU with batch size 256 and learning rate 1e-4, repeated 5 times per configuration. A key finding is that trajectory-only conditioning achieves the lowest positional errors (ADE@20 ≈ 0.49 for Pair Trajectory), while semantically rich inputs like intention text and RGB images produce higher diversity (APD) but worse multimodal coverage (MMADE/MMFDE), revealing that current diffusion architectures cannot effectively ground high-level semantic goals into physically and socially coherent motion.

## Results

On the I2M benchmark, Pair Trajectory conditioning achieves the best geometric accuracy with ADE@1 of 1.013 m, FDE@1 of 1.099 m, and MPJPE@20 of 0.678 m. Pair Multimodal (combining intention text, RGB, trajectory, and MotionCLIP) achieves the best multimodal coverage with MMADE@20 of 0.853 m and MMFDE@20 of 0.835 m, but trails trajectory-only on all single-prediction geometric metrics. Notably, Self Intention Text alone yields the worst ADE@1 (1.573 m) yet the highest diversity (APD@20 of 5.530 m), revealing that current models struggle to ground high-level semantic goals into physically coherent motion.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a dataset paper, so the comparison is between conditioning configurations for the same AffordMotion diffusion model rather than between external methods. Pair Trajectory dominates all single-prediction geometric metrics (ADE@1: 1.013 vs. 1.220 for Pair Multimodal; FDE@1: 1.099 vs. 1.260), confirming that low-level kinematic cues remain the strongest signal for positional accuracy. However, Pair Multimodal achieves the best multimodal coverage (MMADE@20: 0.853 vs. 0.709 for Pair Trajectory) and the lowest APD (3.992 vs. 4.281), suggesting it produces a tighter, more intention-consistent distribution of plausible futures. Adding the partner's information consistently improves over self-only conditions across all metrics (e.g., ADE@1 drops from 1.175 to 1.013 for trajectory; from 1.336 to 1.220 for multimodal). The central finding is that semantically rich inputs (intention text, RGB) increase diversity (APD up to 7.461 for RGB) without improving geometric accuracy, exposing a fundamental gap in current architectures for translating high-level goals into physically plausible motion.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the AffordMotion diffusion-based motion generation framework (Wang et al., CVPR 2024) as its experimental backbone, adapting it from generation to a conditional inpainting formulation for prediction. Conceptually, it departs from the dominant lineage of human motion datasets (Human3.6M, AMASS, CIRCLE, etc.) that annotate kinematics or action categories, instead introducing explicit intention text as the primary semantic supervision. It also resolves a long-standing methodological trade-off in the field—optical mocap accuracy versus natural-clothing visual realism—by affixing unobtrusive markers to everyday garments, a practical bridge between the two camps that prior datasets could not achieve.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central empirical finding—that trajectory-only conditioning outperforms all semantically rich inputs (intention text, RGB, MotionCLIP, multimodal) on every quantitative metric—exposes a fundamental architectural gap between high-level goal representation and low-level kinematic generation in current diffusion models. This reframes the bottleneck in embodied AI from a data problem to a model-design problem, motivating new architectures that can ground abstract intentions into physically and socially coherent motion over time. Open questions include the development of intention-aware evaluation metrics that measure goal fulfillment rather than geometric proximity, and whether the observed gap persists across architectures or is specific to the AffordMotion/CLIP/ResNet pipeline tested here. Limitations include the absence of hand/finger articulation, egocentric vision, gaze tracking, and the restriction to two-person indoor scenarios.
