## Problem

Existing few-shot WSI classification methods using prompt learning rely on slide-level prompts (e.g., 'a WSI of [CLASS]') that fail to capture the subtype-specific phenotypic variations of histological entities such as nuclei, cytoplasm, and glands across multiple magnification scales. These fine-grained, multi-scale entity attributes are what pathologists actually use for differential diagnosis, yet prior approaches treat instances only as implicit aggregation units rather than explicit diagnostic targets.

## Input / Output

The method consumes WSIs tiled into 256×256 patches at two magnifications (10× high-resolution and 5× low-resolution), embedded by a frozen PLIP vision encoder, along with a small number of slide-level labels (4, 8, or 16 per class). GPT-4 is queried offline to generate entity-level prompts (generic visual descriptions plus subtype-specific morphological attributes) and slide-level prompts for each cancer subtype at each scale. The output is a single cancer subtype class label derived from a weighted combination of entity-level logits (per-entity cosine similarity to subtype-specific attribute embeddings) and slide-level logits (aggregated entity features aligned with slide-level prompt embeddings), computed independently at both scales and then averaged.

## Method

MAPLE introduces a hierarchical, two-level prediction framework built on a frozen vision-language backbone (PLIP). First, GPT-4 generates multi-scale entity-level prompts identifying histological entities and their subtype-specific attributes, plus slide-level prompts conditioned on those entity descriptions. A language-guided instance selection step uses region prompts to retain only the top-r% most tumor-relevant patches at each scale. An entity-guided cross-attention module then aggregates selected patch features into per-entity visual representations, which are aligned with subtype-specific attribute embeddings for entity-level classification. A cross-scale entity graph (built via top-k cosine similarity) is processed by a GAT to propagate semantic information within and across scales, enriching entity representations before gated-attention aggregation into a slide-level vector. The final prediction is a weighted sum (controlled by λ) of entity-level and slide-level logits across both scales, optimized end-to-end with cross-entropy loss while keeping the vision and text encoders frozen.

## Results

On the three TCGA cancer cohorts (BRCA, RCC, NSCLC), MAPLE achieves the best AUC across all few-shot settings (4/8/16-shot). In the 16-shot setting, MAPLE reaches AUC of 80.1% (BRCA), 96.9% (RCC), and 90.3% (NSCLC), with corresponding ACC of 73.5%, 86.7%, and 81.0%. Even in the challenging 4-shot regime, MAPLE attains AUC of 72.2%, 90.9%, and 74.0% on the three datasets, consistently outperforming the strongest baseline FOCUS by 1.9–4.1 AUC points. The method uses only 1.86M trainable parameters with ~46 ms/slide inference time, comparable to other prompt-based methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MAPLE outperforms all 11 baselines across every dataset and few-shot setting. The strongest competitor is FOCUS, which MAPLE surpasses by 1.9 AUC points on TCGA-BRCA (80.1 vs. 74.5), 1.8 on TCGA-RCC (96.9 vs. 95.1), and 4.1 on TCGA-NSCLC (90.3 vs. 86.2) in the 16-shot setting; in the 4-shot setting the gap widens to 1.9, 2.9, and 2.7 AUC points respectively. Among prompt-based methods, ViLa-MIL and MSCPT are the next closest, with MAPLE leading by 1.2–4.3 AUC points depending on dataset and setting. Traditional MIL baselines (ABMIL, TransMIL, GTMIL, WiKG) trail by 3–10 AUC points, reflecting the advantage of vision-language prompt alignment over purely visual aggregation. MAPLE also beats multi-scale MIL methods (DTFD-MIL, Dual-Stream-MIL, Cross-Scale MIL) in supplementary comparisons, confirming that the gain stems from the combination of multi-scale modeling with language-guided entity-level prompt supervision rather than multi-scale integration alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

MAPLE extends the prompt-learning-for-WSI line initiated by TOP (Qu et al., NeurIPS 2023) and ViLa-MIL (Shi et al., CVPR 2024), both of which align aggregated slide-level visual features with learnable text prompts. The key conceptual departure is that prior methods treat histological instances implicitly through pooling, whereas MAPLE introduces explicit entity-level prediction: LLM-generated prompts identify multi-scale histological entities (nuclei, glands, stroma) and their subtype-specific phenotypic attributes, which are then aligned with entity features extracted via cross-attention. A cross-scale entity graph (GAT) further refines these representations by capturing semantic correlations within and across resolution levels before slide-level aggregation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By modeling fine-grained entity-level diagnostic cues that pathologists actually use (e.g., nucleolus size distinguishing LUAD from LUSC), MAPLE offers both improved accuracy and clinical interpretability in data-scarce pathology settings. The finding that entity-level predictions alone can rival slide-level predictions challenges the field's default assumption that slide-level aggregation is the necessary bottleneck. Open questions include whether the LLM-generated entity prompts generalize beyond the three TCGA cohorts tested, how sensitive performance is to the quality of GPT-4-generated descriptions, and whether the approach extends to other pathology tasks (e.g., grading, survival prediction) or to settings where the relevant entities are not well-characterized in the literature.
