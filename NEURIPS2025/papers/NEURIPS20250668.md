## Problem

Existing doubly robust (DR) debiasing methods for recommendation assume exact matching between training and test sets, i.e., the same users and items appear in both. In practice, training and test sets often have limited or no unit overlap, and the paper shows that under this more realistic super-population scenario (where only the joint distribution P(x,r) is shared), the standard DR estimator is biased even when both the imputed errors and learned propensities are perfectly correct, due to an additional covariance term that previous analyses miss.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 32-dimensional matrix-factorization embeddings as user-item features, binary or continuous observed ratings, and binary observation indicators, with the training set being a random b% sample of users and items from the full population (default b=50%). It outputs predicted ratings for all user-item pairs and an unbiased estimate of the expected ideal loss over the super-population. Unlike prior DR methods that require identical unit sets, SuperDR only requires that training and test sets are drawn from the same joint distribution P(x,r), making it applicable to industrial settings where offline training data and online test data differ in which specific users and items are present.

## Method

The key insight is that under super-population, the DR bias decomposes into the standard propensity/imputation accuracy term plus an additional covariance term Cov((p̂−o)/p̂, e−ê) that does not vanish even with perfect models. To control this term, the paper introduces a corrected imputation model ẽ_{u,i} = ê_{u,i} + ε(o_{u,i} − p̂_{u,i}) with a single learnable scalar ε, trained by minimizing a new loss L_e^Sup = (1/|D|) Σ o_{u,i}(e_{u,i} − ẽ_{u,i})²/p̂_{u,i}. Minimizing this loss drives the empirical covariance to zero on observed pairs (via the gradient with respect to ε) and bounds it on missing pairs via Rademacher complexity and concentration inequalities. The resulting SuperDR estimator replaces ê with ẽ in the standard DR formula, and is trained via an alternating joint learning algorithm that updates the imputation parameters (φ, ε) and prediction parameters (θ) in separate inner-loop steps while keeping the propensity model fixed. The paper further proves that the correction has a no-harm property (ε*→0 when the original imputation already has zero covariance), reduces both bias and variance of the imputed errors, and yields a tighter generalization bound than prior DR methods.

## Results

SuperDR achieves the best debiasing performance across all three datasets and all evaluation metrics. On Coat, it reaches AUC 0.757, NDCG@5 0.667, and Recall@5 0.637; on Yahoo! R3, AUC 0.686, NDCG@5 0.667, and Recall@5 0.463; and on the large-scale industrial KuaiRec dataset, AUC 0.828, NDCG@50 0.640, and Recall@50 0.680. Most improvements over the strongest baselines (D-DR, DCE-TDR, AKBDR) are statistically significant (p ≤ 0.05), with the largest gains appearing when the overlap between training and test users/items is minimal (sample ratio 5–10%), where previous DR methods degrade noticeably while SuperDR maintains robust performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SuperDR outperforms all 15 baselines on every metric across all three datasets. The closest competitors are the recent DR variants: on Coat, D-DR achieves AUC 0.750 vs. SuperDR's 0.757 (+0.007) and NDCG@5 0.654 vs. 0.667 (+0.013); on Yahoo! R3, DCE-TDR leads among baselines with AUC 0.679 vs. SuperDR's 0.686 (+0.007) and NDCG@5 0.662 vs. 0.667 (+0.005); on KuaiRec, AKBDR and DCE-TDR tie at AUC 0.824 vs. SuperDR's 0.828, but SuperDR gains a larger margin on NDCG@50 (0.640 vs. 0.629/0.632) and Recall@50 (0.680 vs. 0.667/0.671). The advantage widens substantially under low-overlap conditions (5–10% sample ratio), where DCE-TDR and D-DR degrade noticeably while SuperDR remains stable, confirming that the corrected imputation model effectively controls the additional covariance term introduced by sampling. IPS-based methods (IPS, SNIPS, ASIPS, IPS-V2) consistently underperform DR-based methods, and the naive MLP baseline is outperformed by all debiasing approaches, validating the necessity of bias correction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the doubly robust (DR) debiasing framework introduced by Wang et al. (ICML 2019) from the 'exact matching' scenario—where training and test sets share identical user-item units—to a more general 'super-population' scenario where only the joint distribution P(x,r) is shared. The correction mechanism draws on targeted maximum likelihood estimation (TMLE) as used in TDR-CL (Li et al., ICLR 2023), but repurposes it specifically to control an additional covariance term that emerges under super-population. The paper also builds on the broader DR literature (MRDR, StableDR, DCE-TDR, D-DR) as baselines, positioning SuperDR as a principled fix for a previously unaddressed source of bias rather than a competing estimator design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that the DR estimator is biased even when both the imputation and propensity models are correct under super-population challenges a core assumption in the debiasing literature and has direct practical implications for industrial recommender systems where train/test user-item overlap is limited. The proposed correction is lightweight (one scalar parameter ε) and provably reduces both bias and variance, but the theoretical guarantees rely on boundedness assumptions (K_ψ, K_φ) and Rademacher complexity terms that are difficult to estimate in practice. Open questions include developing tighter covariance bounds, extending the analysis to multi-task or sequential settings, and understanding how the method behaves when the super-population assumption itself is violated (e.g., distribution shift beyond sampling).
