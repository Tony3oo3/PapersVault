## Problem

Standard Chain-of-Thought reasoning forces LLMs to commit to a single discrete token at each reasoning step, collapsing the full probability distribution and restricting the model to one reasoning trajectory. This discrete, sequential constraint limits the model's ability to represent abstract concepts and explore multiple plausible paths simultaneously, often leading to suboptimal answers and wasted tokens on incorrect branches. The paper asks whether LLMs can reason in a continuous concept space that preserves the full distributional information at each step, analogous to how human cognition operates on fluid, non-verbal concepts rather than discrete words.

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard text prompt (math problem or coding task) as input and produces a final discrete text answer. The key structural difference lies in the intermediate reasoning phase: instead of generating discrete token IDs, the model outputs full probability distributions over the vocabulary (concept tokens), which are converted to continuous embeddings via probability-weighted interpolation over the top-n tokens. A Cold Stop mechanism monitors the entropy of each concept token and inserts an end-of-thinking marker when the model remains overconfident (low entropy) for k consecutive steps, after which the model switches to standard discrete decoding for the final answer.

## Method

Soft Thinking is a training-free method that replaces discrete token sampling in CoT with probability-weighted concept tokens in a continuous concept space. At each intermediate reasoning step, the full next-token probability distribution p is retained as a concept token, and the next input embedding is computed as the convex combination Σ p[k]·e(k) over all vocabulary embeddings, rather than selecting a single token. This preserves the entire distributional information, allowing the model to implicitly explore multiple reasoning paths in parallel within a single forward pass. A Cold Stop mechanism computes the entropy of each concept token and terminates reasoning early when entropy stays below a threshold τ for k consecutive steps, mitigating out-of-distribution collapse caused by feeding continuous embeddings the model has never seen during training. The paper provides a theoretical analysis showing that this procedure approximates the exact exponential path-summation of CoT through iterative first-order linearization, collapsing the combinatorial sum into a single forward pass under a sequence of concept tokens.

## Results

Soft Thinking improves pass@1 accuracy by up to 2.48 points on mathematical benchmarks (QwQ-32B: 83.84% → 86.32% average) and up to 0.90 points on coding benchmarks (DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-32B: 83.23% → 84.13% average), while simultaneously reducing generation length by up to 22.4% (DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-32B math: 4995 → 3875 tokens) and up to 19.1% on coding tasks. On the challenging AIME 2024 dataset, QwQ-32B gains 6.45 points (76.88% → 83.33%). All improvements are achieved without any training, using only the model's existing embedding matrix.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares Soft Thinking against two baselines: Standard CoT Thinking (sampling with temperature 0.6, 16 samples per problem) and Standard Greedy CoT Thinking (temperature 0, single sample). Soft Thinking outperforms both baselines on average accuracy across all three models and both math and coding domains. Against Standard CoT, Soft Thinking gains 1.11–2.48 points in average pass@1 on math and 0.48–0.90 points on coding, while reducing generation length by 11.6–22.4% (math) and 16.1–19.1% (coding). Against Greedy CoT, Soft Thinking is more consistent: greedy decoding reduces token usage but often degrades accuracy substantially (e.g., DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-32B drops 10.53 points on coding average), whereas Soft Thinking improves both accuracy and efficiency simultaneously. In the ablation (Table 3), the training-free COCONUT baseline [27] fails entirely (0.0% accuracy, always hitting max generation length of 32,768), and a simple average-embedding strategy yields only 6.66–7.49% accuracy, confirming that probability-weighted concept tokens are essential.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends standard Chain-of-Thought reasoning by replacing discrete token sampling with probability-weighted 'concept tokens' in a continuous embedding space, drawing on the broader line of continuous-space reasoning work (Yang et al. 2024; Biran et al. 2024; Shalev et al. 2024). Its key departure from COCONUT (Hao et al. 2024) is being entirely training-free: rather than feeding raw hidden states as next-step inputs (which fails for models >7B where embedding and LM-head layers are decoupled), Soft Thinking uses the vocabulary probability distribution as a bridge to construct embeddings in the input space. The Cold Stop mechanism, an entropy-based early-termination strategy, is a novel practical addition not present in prior continuous-reasoning methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The simultaneous gain in accuracy (+2.48 pts) and token efficiency (−22.4%) breaks the typical performance-vs-efficiency trade-off in CoT reasoning, and the training-free, plug-and-play nature makes it immediately applicable to any LLM without retraining. However, the fundamental OOD limitation remains: models have never seen concept tokens during training, so long reasoning chains risk collapse, and Cold Stop only mitigates rather than resolves this. Open questions include whether training-based adaptation to concept tokens could close the gap, whether the linearization assumptions in the theoretical analysis hold at scale, and whether the approach generalizes beyond math and code to broader reasoning domains.
