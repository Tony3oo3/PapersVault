## Problem

Traditional evolutionary algorithms for graph-structured combinatorial problems rely on abstract encodings (binary, permutation) that discard the topological context of solutions, causing crossover and mutation to operate without structural awareness. While image-based encoding with MLLMs can preserve this context, large-scale networks produce visually cluttered plots that degrade MLLM reasoning, and any single sparsified view or layout choice introduces systematic bias into the optimization process.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250296/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a network graph G=(V,E) and a seed size k, sparsifies it into multiple subgraphs of 50 nodes and 100 edges via degree-based and community-based strategies, then renders each as a 1200×1200px image under three layout algorithms (Kamada-Kawai, Fruchterman-Reingold, GraphOpt) using plotly. During crossover, only seed nodes are highlighted in the image to reduce inference difficulty, whereas mutation visualizes all nodes with distinct coloring. The output is an optimized seed set S⊆V of exactly k nodes, whose fitness is evaluated by the expected diffusion value (EDV) surrogate computed on the original full network rather than the sparsified subgraph.

## Method

The framework integrates three mechanisms: (1) graph sparsification via degree-based and community-based (FastGreedy partitioning + betweenness centrality selection) strategies to produce multiple simplified views that preserve complementary structural properties; (2) a cooperative master-worker evolutionary optimization where each subprocessor evolves a population on a different sparsified network, with a global elite pool enabling cross-domain knowledge transfer through projection (sparsified→original) and injection (original→sparsified) mapping functions, including a heuristic filling step to handle size mismatches when injecting elites into domains with different node sets; (3) an ensemble strategy that feeds each graph image under L different layouts into GPT-4o for initialization, crossover, and mutation, then aggregates outputs via consensus voting where nodes accumulate frequency-based votes across layouts and are selected by threshold before greedy completion by uncovered-neighbor count. The MLLM serves as the evolutionary operator, interpreting visual structure to propose candidate solutions, while the EDV surrogate on the original network provides fitness feedback to guide selection.

## Results

On the influence maximization task (measured by Expected Diffusion Value, EDV), the proposed Co-MLLM(KK) method achieves the best average ranking of 1.38 across eight real-world networks, outperforming all single-domain baselines. Headline values include 524.9±16.76 on WikiVote, 1146.7±18.53 on MSU24, and 2522.9±111.28 on Texas84. The method wins on 6 of 8 networks, with the two exceptions (USAir, Polblogs) where the divide-and-conquer CoeCo baseline edges ahead. The ensemble MLLM variant (Table 4) further improves robustness, achieving an average ranking of 1.13 over single-layout MLLM operators.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three external baselines (SAEP, CoeCo, SSR) that operate on the degree-based sparsified network, alongside two internal single-domain variants (Spars-D-EO, Spars-C-EO) and a cooperative non-MLLM variant (Co-EO). Co-MLLM(KK) outperforms all baselines on 6 of 8 networks: it beats CoeCo by 0.8 on Texas84 (2522.9 vs 2515.7), by 23.9 on Rutgers89 (765.9 vs 742.0), and by 20.7 on WikiVote (524.9 vs 504.2 vs SSR's 504.2). The two losses are to CoeCo on USAir (45.3 vs 48.9) and Polblogs (207.4 vs 223.5), where the divide-and-conquer decomposition strategy has a structural advantage. SSR [39] is consistently outperformed by Co-MLLM(KK) on all eight networks, with the largest gap on Texas84 (2522.9 vs 2198.2, +14.8%). SAEP [22] is the weakest external baseline, losing to Co-MLLM(KK) on all networks except USAir where it is marginally behind. The average ranking confirms Co-MLLM(KK) as the overall best method (1.38 vs 2.63 for CoeCo, 3.38 for SSR, 4.38 for SAEP).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the authors' prior work on visual evolutionary optimization with MLLMs [10], which first demonstrated that image-based encoding of graph structures enables MLLMs to serve as structure-aware evolutionary operators. It departs from that single-domain setting by introducing a cooperative multi-domain framework where multiple sparsified views of the same network are optimized in parallel via a master-worker architecture with cross-domain knowledge transfer, drawing on distributed cooperative EA literature [14, 15]. The ensemble strategy with consensus voting across multiple layout algorithms (KK, FR, GraphOpt) addresses the layout sensitivity documented in [16], and the sparsification techniques (degree-based, community-based) connect to the broader graph reduction literature [12, 13].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical recipe for scaling MLLM-driven evolutionary optimization to networks far larger than what can be visualized in a single image, by combining sparsification, cooperative search, and layout ensembling. A key open question is scalability: the ~200-node visualization ceiling and the inapplicability of sparsification to permutation problems (e.g., TSP) limit the framework's reach, and the authors propose divide-and-conquer strategies as future work. The consistent but modest improvements over single-domain baselines across eight networks suggest the framework is robust but not transformative, and the reliance on a single backbone MLLM (GPT-4o) leaves open how results transfer to other models or as MLLM capabilities evolve.
