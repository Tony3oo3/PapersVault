## Problem

Existing online fair allocation literature either assumes divisible (fractional) resources or restricts agents to a single group label, leaving a gap for integral allocation with multi-labeled agents arriving sequentially. This paper addresses the Online Multi-Class Selection (OMcS) problem where a decision-maker must irrevocably accept or reject each arriving agent while simultaneously satisfying group fairness constraints across overlapping classes, a setting that is both practically relevant (e.g., cloud scheduling, social housing) and algorithmically challenging due to the interdependence of fairness constraints when one agent serves multiple groups.

## Input / Output

The algorithm receives agents one at a time, each carrying a valuation v_t bounded in [1, θ_j] for their class j and a label set J_t ⊆ [K] indicating all groups they belong to; the inventory size B, class-specific fluctuation ratios {θ_j}, and (for GFQ) fairness quotas {m_j} are known a priori, while the total number of arrivals T and future valuations/labels are hidden. The output is an immediate binary decision x_t ∈ {0,1} per agent, with the guarantee that the expected competitive ratio and group fairness metric (GFQ or β-PF) hold in expectation over the algorithm's internal randomness. For the learning-augmented variant, an additional untrusted binary advice sequence ADV is consumed, and the algorithm outputs a randomized mixture of its robust decision and the advised decision.

## Method

The core technical contribution is a lossless online rounding scheme (Algorithm 2) that converts any fractional online solution into an integral one with zero expected performance loss, using a single Bernoulli random seed per round whose success probability is calibrated to the fractional utilization level z_t. For GFQ, the algorithm (R-SETASIDE-GFQ) first reserves m_j units per class via a set-aside mechanism, then applies an optimal fractional threshold policy (FRAC-GFQ) to the remaining budget, and finally rounds via the lossless scheme to achieve the same competitive ratio as the fractional optimum. For β-PF, the relaxation phase designs K + 1 + C(K,2) threshold functions—local per-class, pairwise per-class-pair, and a global efficiency threshold—parameterized by b ∈ [0,1] to smoothly trade off fairness and efficiency, with the same rounding scheme preserving the β-PF guarantee exactly in the integral setting. The learning-augmented variant (LiLA) blends the robust Algorithm 3 decision with a black-box predicted fair allocation via a combination probability ρ(ε), yielding a (1+ε)-consistent and (1+ε)(β−1)/ε-robust proportional fairness guarantee.

## Results

The paper's main contributions are theoretical guarantees: R-SETASIDE-GFQ achieves the optimal competitive ratio α* among all algorithms for OMcS with GFQ (Theorems 3.1–3.2), and R-SETASIDE-PF achieves α(b)-competitiveness and β(b)-proportional fairness simultaneously (Theorems 4.1, 4.3), with the lossless rounding scheme preserving fractional performance exactly. In the special case K=1, m₁=0, the algorithm recovers the tight competitive ratio 1+ln(θ₁). The learning-augmented LiLA algorithm achieves (1+ε)-consistent and ((1+ε)(β−1)/ε)-robust proportional fairness (Theorem 5.1). Empirically on Google Cluster Data (B=100, θ₁=5, θ₂=10, θ₃=15, m_j=5), β-PF allocates resources more equitably across classes while maintaining high total utility, and LiLA improves the empirical fairness guarantee when prediction quality is high (ξ→0).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its algorithms against several baselines. R-SETASIDE-GFQ (randomized) significantly outperforms D-SETASIDE-GFQ (deterministic) in competitive ratio for small inventory sizes B, with the gap narrowing as B grows (Figure 1). Against d-DYNAMIC (the threshold algorithm from Tan et al. 2025 without fairness constraints), D-SETASIDE-GFQ exhibits lower admission thresholds due to the set-aside reservation for fairness, reflecting the efficiency cost of enforcing GFQ (Figure 5). In the β-PF setting, the proposed R-SETASIDE-PF allocates resources more equitably across the three classes compared to the no-fairness baseline (α-CR), while maintaining comparable total utility (Figure 2). The learning-augmented LiLA improves the empirical β-PF guarantee over the non-predictive algorithm when the adversarial probability ξ is low (high-quality advice), but degrades toward the robust baseline as ξ increases, illustrating the consistency-robustness trade-off (Figure 4). The CDF of competitive ratios (Figure 3) confirms that predictions tighten the distribution of empirical performance, and that r-GFQ dominates d-GFQ across the full range of competitive ratios.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the online selection (k-search) problem to a multi-class setting with group fairness, building directly on the fractional, single-labeled group fairness framework of Zargari et al. (ZJST25) and the threshold-based optimal algorithms of Tan et al. (TYBLG25). The core technical contribution—a lossless online rounding scheme—is inspired by the correlated k-rental rounding of Nekouyan et al. (NSBT25) and the online correlated selection (OCS) framework of Huang et al. (HZZ20, FHTZ22), but is adapted to non-reusable, non-renewable resources via per-round independent random seeds. The set-aside mechanism for enforcing fairness constraints and the relax-and-round architecture follow the broader online primal-dual and randomized rounding tradition (Buchbinder & Naor 2009, Ma 2024), while the learning-augmented variant draws on the consistency-robustness framework of Wei & Zhang (2020) and Kumar et al. (2018).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper closes a notable gap in online fair allocation: prior work either assumed offline agents with online resources or restricted to fractional, single-labeled settings, whereas many real-world applications (cloud scheduling, social housing) involve sequential multi-labeled agents competing for indivisible resources. The lossless rounding scheme is of independent interest and could tighten integrality gaps across a range of online selection and revenue-management problems. Key open questions include extending the results to stochastic or random-order arrival models, multi-resource (combinatorial) settings, and understanding how the fairness-efficiency trade-off parameter b interacts with adversarial prediction quality in the learning-augmented variant. The empirical validation is limited to a single dataset (Google Cluster Data) with randomly assigned valuations, leaving the practical robustness of the theoretical guarantees under realistic, correlated arrival patterns untested.
