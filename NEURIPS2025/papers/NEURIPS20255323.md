## Problem

Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) generate extended thinking traces for every query, incurring substantial token and latency overhead even for trivial inputs like a single greeting word. No existing model can adaptively decide whether to engage in extended thinking based on query complexity, creating a gap between the strong reasoning capability of LRMs and the efficiency expected in real-world deployment.

## Input / Output

The model receives a single text query and autonomously selects one of two output modes: a Thinking mode that produces a -wrapped reasoning trace followed by the final answer, or a No-Thinking mode that produces a <no_think>-wrapped direct response. The mode selection is made implicitly by the model based on the semantic and contextual characteristics of the query, with no external classifier or routing mechanism. The paper introduces Hybrid Accuracy (H_acc) as a new evaluation metric that measures how often the model's chosen mode matches the ground-truth preferred mode determined by a reward model.

## Method

The paper proposes a two-stage training pipeline. Stage I (Hybrid Fine-Tuning) performs supervised fine-tuning on 1.7M examples mixing thinking-style data (math, code, science with  tags) and no-thinking-style data (simple conversational queries with <no_think> tags) to establish a stable cold start supporting both modes without collapse. Stage II (Hybrid Group Policy Optimization, HGPO) is an online RL algorithm that, for each query, samples N/2 responses under each mode, then assigns two types of binary rewards: inter-group rewards that compare average quality across modes (teaching the model when to think) and intra-group rewards that compare quality within each mode (teaching response quality). A margin hyperparameter δ in the inter-group reward controls the bias toward No-Thinking, and the advantage is computed via GRPO applied separately to each reward type, with the inter-group advantage applied only to the mode-selection tokens ( or <no_think>). The policy is updated with a standard PPO-style clipped objective plus KL regularization against a reference model.

## Results

LHRMs-7B achieves an average score of 63.1 across all benchmarks, outperforming the strongest LRM baseline DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B (56.8) by 11.1% and the best hybrid baseline HFT-DPO-7B (58.9) by 7.1%. On the hardest math benchmark AIME24, LHRMs-7B reaches 66.7, surpassing DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B (55.5) by 20.2% and HFT-DPO-7B (58.7) by 13.6%. On general-capability benchmarks, LHRMs-7B scores 35.0 on AlpacaEval 2.0 and 26.0 on Arena-Hard, exceeding DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B (19.1 / 17.9) by 83.2% and 45.8% respectively. The proposed Hybrid Accuracy metric reaches 71.9% for LHRMs-7B, nearly double that of HFT-DPO-7B (37.1%), confirming effective adaptive mode selection.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest LRM baseline DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B, LHRMs-7B wins on every benchmark: AIME24 (66.7 vs 55.5), MATH500 (93.8 vs 92.8), LiveCodeBench (38.8 vs 37.6), MBPP (81.5 vs 74.3), AlpacaEval (35.0 vs 19.1), and Arena-Hard (26.0 vs 17.9). Against the best hybrid baseline HFT-DPO-7B, LHRMs-7B leads on AIME24 (66.7 vs 58.7, +13.6%), AlpacaEval (35.0 vs 23.3, +50.2%), Arena-Hard (26.0 vs 13.0, +93.4%), and Hybrid Accuracy (71.9 vs 37.1, +93.8%), while matching on MATH500 and LiveCodeBench. Against HFT-RFT-7B, LHRMs-7B wins on MATH500 (93.8 vs 87.8), AIME24 (66.7 vs 55.3), and Arena-Hard (26.0 vs 14.0), though HFT-RFT slightly edges it on MBPP (81.0 vs 81.5 is a near-tie). At the 1.5B scale, LHRMs-1.5B outperforms DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B on all benchmarks and beats HFT-DPO-1.5B on AIME24 (35.3 vs 32.6), MBPP (61.1 vs 50.5), and Hybrid Accuracy (54.4 vs 45.8). Notably, LHRMs also generalizes to the code domain (not used in RL training), gaining +11.1% on MBPP over HFT-1.5B, whereas HFT-DPO and HFT-RFT show drops of 7.8% and 9.3% respectively on the same task.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Large Reasoning Model (LRM) paradigm established by DeepSeek-R1 and OpenAI o1/o3, which generate extended thinking traces for all queries, by introducing an adaptive mechanism that selects between Thinking and No-Thinking modes per query. The training pipeline builds directly on GRPO (from DeepSeekMath) and REINFORCE++ for the RL stage, but augments them with a novel two-level reward structure (inter-group and intra-group) in HGPO to explicitly teach mode selection. The cold-start stage (HFT) draws on the same hybrid data construction ideas used in distilled LRM training, while the evaluation leverages existing benchmarks (MATH500, AIME24, LiveCodeBench, AlpacaEval, Arena-Hard) alongside a newly proposed Hybrid Accuracy metric.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The overthinking problem in LRMs is a practical bottleneck for real-world deployment, and this paper provides the first systematic training recipe (HFT + HGPO) to address it, with the margin δ offering a tunable knob between latency and reasoning depth. The cross-domain transfer finding—RL on math data improving code performance via better mode selection—is a useful signal that hybrid thinking patterns generalize beyond their training domain. However, the work is limited to Qwen-2.5 at 1.5B and 7B scales, lacks error bars or multiple-seed runs, and the 'first' claim rests on the absence of competing adaptive-thinking systems rather than a rigorous comparison. Open questions include whether the approach scales to 70B+ models, whether a learned router could replace the implicit mode selection, and how the method interacts with multi-turn conversations where difficulty shifts mid-dialogue.
