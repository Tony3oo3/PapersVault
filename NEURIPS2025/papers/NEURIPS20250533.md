## Problem

Offline multi-objective optimization requires identifying Pareto-optimal designs from a fixed dataset without further evaluations, but existing generative (inverse) methods either scalarize objectives via surrogate models or fail to ensure diversity across the Pareto front. This paper addresses both gaps by introducing a preference-based guidance mechanism that directly encodes Pareto dominance and diversity without training per-objective surrogates.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250533/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a dataset of N continuous design-objective pairs (x ∈ R^d, f(x) ∈ R^m) with d ranging from 2 to 32 and m from 2 to 8 objectives, and generates 256 new designs that approximate the Pareto-optimal set. The preference model consumes pairs of d-dimensional design vectors (2×d input) and outputs a binary dominance logit, while the diffusion model operates on individual d-dimensional design vectors. Unlike forward methods that require a well-defined search space, the generative output can extrapolate beyond the training distribution toward the true Pareto front.

## Method

PGD-MOO combines an unconditional DDPM denoising model with a time-conditioned Bradley-Terry preference classifier that predicts whether one design Pareto-dominates another. The preference model is an MLP trained on pairs from the dataset, where dominance labels are assigned via non-dominated sorting, and ties within the same front are broken using crowding distance to encourage diversity. During sampling, the gradient of the preference score (∇_{x̃_t} p_φ(x̃_t ≺ r | x̃_t, r, t)) is added to the denoising mean at each timestep, steering generation toward more dominant regions; the reference design r is initialized as the most dominant point in the dataset and updated to the current denoised sample at each step. This eliminates the need for per-objective surrogate models and scalarization, while the crowding-distance-based labeling ensures generated solutions are well-distributed across the objective space.

## Results

On the synthetic benchmark (DTLZ + ZDT), PGD-MOO achieves an average hypervolume ranking of 4.42 across 12 tasks, outperforming the primary generative baseline ParetoFlow (7.58) and all forward surrogate-based methods. On the MO-NAS neural architecture search tasks, PGD-MOO achieves the best average ranking of 1.86, well ahead of ParetoFlow (4.67) and the best forward method MultipleModels-COM (2.72). On the RE engineering suite, PGD-MOO is competitive (ranking 7.67) with the best forward methods (e.g., MultipleModels-IOM at 4.6) but slightly trails them. On the Δ-spread diversity metric, PGD-MOO achieves the best average ranking of 2.83 (synthetic) and 4.28 (RE), substantially outperforming ParetoFlow (10.0 and 9.0 respectively) and all forward baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ParetoFlow (the primary generative/inverse baseline using flow-matching with per-objective surrogate classifiers), PGD-MOO wins decisively on synthetic tasks (average hypervolume ranking 4.42 vs. 7.58) and MO-NAS tasks (1.86 vs. 4.67), but is slightly behind on the RE engineering suite (7.67 vs. 6.17). Against forward surrogate-based methods (MultiHead, MultipleModels with COM/IOM/ICT/RoMA/TriMentoring, and their multi-task variants), PGD-MOO achieves the best or near-best hypervolume on synthetic and MO-NAS benchmarks, while forward methods like MultipleModels-IOM and MultipleModels-COM remain competitive on RE tasks. On the Δ-spread diversity metric, PGD-MOO dominates all baselines by a wide margin: it achieves an average ranking of 2.83 on synthetic and 4.28 on RE, compared to 10.0 and 9.0 for ParetoFlow and 4.6–9.25 for forward methods, confirming that the crowding-distance-based preference training effectively enforces Pareto front coverage. The data-pruning variant (PGD-MOO+DataPruning, using only top-30% dominant points) performs comparably on hypervolume but slightly worse on RE, suggesting the full dataset is beneficial for real-world engineering tasks. Overall, PGD-MOO is the strongest inverse method across all three task families and remains competitive with the best forward methods while offering the advantage of generative exploration without per-objective surrogate training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the DDPM framework (Ho et al., 2020) and the classifier-guidance mechanism (Dhariwal & Nichol, 2021), but departs from both the standard label-conditioned guidance and the surrogate-model-based forward approaches for offline optimization. The key conceptual departure is replacing per-objective surrogate classifiers with a single preference model trained on Pareto dominance pairs via a Bradley-Terry formulation, which avoids scalarization and the need for m separate objective models. It also extends the offline MOO benchmarking framework of Xue et al. (2024) by introducing the first generative/inverse approach to this setting and by elevating Pareto front diversity (via crowding distance) to a first-class training signal, a concern absent in prior offline MOO methods including the concurrent ParetoFlow.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Offline MOO is a practically important but underexplored setting where sequential evaluation is infeasible (e.g., batch drug screening), and this paper provides the first principled generative approach that simultaneously addresses convergence and diversity. The main limitation is that the preference model relies solely on dominance relations, precluding fine-grained control over specific objective trade-offs—a gap the authors flag for future work. An open question is whether combining the generative exploration of PGD-MOO with iterative surrogate refinement (a forward-inverse hybrid) could close the remaining hypervolume gap with the strongest forward methods on high-dimensional or many-objective tasks.
