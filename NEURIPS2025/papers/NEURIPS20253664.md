## Problem

Existing multi-teacher knowledge distillation methods are inherently task-specific: they require task labels, task-specific loss functions, or prior knowledge about the downstream objective, forcing a new distillation run for every new task. The paper asks how to distill knowledge from multiple diverse embedding models into a single student representation that retains maximal utility across an arbitrary range of unseen downstream tasks, without any task-specific supervision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253664/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The student embedder accepts raw inputs (text sentences, RGB images, or molecules represented as SMILES strings, 2D graphs, or 3D point clouds) and outputs a single fixed-dimensional embedding vector. During training, the student does not see raw inputs directly for the distillation loss: teacher embeddings are pre-computed and stored (requiring ~100 GB for the largest text teacher), and the student is trained on batches of these pre-computed vectors. At inference, the frozen student embedding is paired with a small feedforward head trained on the target task, making the representation itself the transferable artifact.

## Method

The core insight is a theoretical bound (Proposition 3.1) showing that the probability of a student's Bayes classifier disagreeing with a teacher's Bayes classifier on any task is upper-bounded by 1 − exp(−h(T_k(X)|S(X))), the conditional differential entropy of the teacher embedding given the student embedding. Since this bound is task-independent, minimizing the average conditional entropy across all K teachers yields a task-agnostic distillation objective. Practically, the conditional distribution p(T_k(X)|S(X)) is modeled with a teacher-specific Gaussian kernel (mean μ_k(S(X)) and covariance Σ_k(S(X)) parameterized by small MLPs), and the student is trained end-to-end by minimizing the negative log-likelihood of the pre-computed teacher embeddings under these Gaussians. This is equivalent to maximizing mutual information I(T_k(X); S(X)) since h(T_k(X)) is constant with respect to the student. The approach replaces unstable point-estimation losses (MSE, cosine similarity) with a probabilistic interval-estimation objective, and the Gaussian kernels are discarded after training, leaving only the student embedder.

## Results

On the MTEB text classification benchmark (12 tasks), the NLL-distilled 109M-parameter student achieves 76.7 average accuracy, outperforming all models three times its size (e.g., bge-base 75.5, GIST 76.0) and even the 335M NLL student (76.5). At the 23M scale, the NLL student reaches 74.0 average, surpassing GIST (72.7) by 1.3 points. In molecular modeling (32 TDC ADMET/HTS tasks), the 8-teacher student-2M achieves the best average rank of 3.72, beating the strongest single teacher ChemBERTaMTR-77M (rank 4.79) and all distillation baselines (MSE: 6.15, Cosine: 5.13). In vision, the 3.7M-parameter PVTv2 student distilled from ~87M-parameter ViT teachers consistently sits on the Pareto frontier, matching performance of models 20× larger.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GIST (Solatorio, 2024), the NLL student wins at 23M (74.0 vs 72.7), 109M (76.7 vs 76.0), and 335M (76.5 vs 76.0), but loses slightly at 33M (75.4 vs 76.1). Against MSE distillation (SimReg, Navaneet et al., 2022), NLL consistently wins across all text sizes (e.g., 76.7 vs 76.0 at 109M; 76.5 vs 74.4 at 335M) and in molecular modeling (avg rank 3.72 vs 6.15). In the molecular domain, the 8-teacher student (rank 3.72) outperforms every individual teacher, including the strongest ChemBERTaMTR-77M (rank 4.79), GraphMVP (7.89), and 3D-infomax (6.91), as well as Cosine distillation (5.13) and CompRess (Abbasi Koohpayegani et al., 2020). The paper also shows that multi-teacher training (8 teachers) consistently beats single-teacher distillation in molecular tasks (e.g., 2-teacher student avg R² 0.449 vs 1-teacher 0.392–0.405), confirming the benefit of teacher diversity. In vision, the PVTv2 student (3.7M) matches or exceeds CNN baselines (ResNet, MobileNetV2, ShuffleNetV2) and approaches the accuracy of ~87M-parameter ViT teachers (Swin, DINOv2, BEiT) on fine-grained datasets like CUB and FGVCAircraft.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the task-oriented single-task distillation approaches that dominate the prior literature, and introduces a task-agnostic framework based on a majority-vote objective that is bounded by the conditional entropy of the teachers given the student output. The authors leverage a recent differentiable Gaussian-kernel-based estimator of the differential conditional entropy to formulate an information-theoretic loss that maximizes the mutual information between the student and all the teachers, eliminating the dependence on task-specific labels or prior knowledge about the downstream task.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

For practitioners working on high-dimensional representation learning, this method provides a way to compress the knowledge of multiple large embedders into a smaller student model without task-specific labels, potentially reaching higher accuracy on a wide range of downstream tasks. However, the quality of the student embeddings critically depends on the relevance of the chosen teachers to the actual downstream task, and because the objective does not explicitly impose structure on the embedding space, performance in tasks like clustering or STS may be more modest than in classification where a small feed-forward network is trained on top of the embeddings.
