## Problem

Automatic factuality metrics for summarization have been validated against human benchmarks, but it remains unclear whether they genuinely measure source-summary factual consistency or merely exploit shallow surface cues. This paper stress-tests six SOTA metrics (QuestEval, SummaC-Conv, UniEval, AlignScore, MiniCheck, ChatGPT-DA) to determine whether their scores reflect true factual reasoning or are vulnerable to benign edits, adversarial manipulation, and reliance on parametric knowledge rather than the provided reference.

## Input / Output

Each metric under evaluation takes a source document and a generated summary as input and produces a continuous factual consistency score (or binary label). The paper's own shallow MLP probe consumes surface-level features—ROUGE-2 F1, entity overlap, BERT cosine similarity, word/sentence novelty ratios, and a conciseness ratio—and outputs a binary factuality prediction whose confidence is used to stratify examples into easy, medium, and hard difficulty tiers. The gaming experiments modify summaries by appending content-free phrases (e.g., 'the document discusses') or assertion sentences, while the parametric-knowledge test swaps in counterfactual reference documents from ConflictBank that contradict the evaluator LLM's internal world knowledge.

## Method

The paper introduces a four-part stress-testing framework rather than proposing a new metric. First, a two-hidden-layer MLP (100→50 units, lr=0.001) trained on shallow surface features stratifies benchmark examples by prediction confidence, revealing that all metrics suffer substantial AUC drops from easy to hard cases. Second, using GenAudit's annotated inconsistent/corrected summary pairs plus GPT-4-generated benign rewrites (paraphrase, negation, simplification, etc.), the authors measure whether score deltas track factual corrections or benign edits. Third, TF-IDF analysis of top-scoring summaries identifies gaming phrases ('the document discusses', metric-specific assertion sentences) that, when appended to summaries, inflate NLI and specialized metric scores by >0.2 points—often exceeding gains from genuine model improvements. Fourth, the ConflictBank dataset provides counterfactual Wikipedia references to test whether ChatGPT-DA grounds judgments in the source or defaults to parametric knowledge, showing a 15× increase in mis-scoring (0.2%→3.1%) when references contradict the model's internal beliefs.

## Results

All six factuality metrics show substantial AUC drops when moving from 'easy' to 'hard' examples (categorized by a shallow MLP's prediction confidence), with older metrics like QuestEval and SummaC-Conv showing the sharpest declines. Gaming experiments reveal that appending innocuous phrases (e.g., 'the document discusses') inflates scores by >0.2 absolute points for SummaC-Conv, UniEval, AlignScore, and MiniCheck, while appending assertion phrases to summaries adds 0.1–0.15 points—gains comparable to or exceeding those from genuine model improvements. ChatGPT-DA shows minimal sensitivity to gaming but exhibits a parametric-knowledge bias: the score gap between supported and unsupported summaries narrows significantly (p<0.001) when references are counterfactual, and unsupported factual summaries are rated higher than supported ones in 3.1% of cases versus a 0.2% baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among the six metrics stress-tested, ChatGPT-DA (LLM prompting) emerges as the most robust: it consistently ranks faithful revisions higher than inconsistent originals, remains stable under benign paraphrasing and negation-preserving rewrites, and shows minimal score inflation from gaming phrases. In contrast, QuestEval (QA-based) shows no meaningful score improvement in response to factual corrections and is the most vulnerable to gaming. SummaC-Conv (NLI-based) exhibits the sharpest AUC drop on hard examples and receives a >0.2 absolute boost from a single constant phrase, yet shows no score increase for genuinely corrected summaries. UniEval and AlignScore (specialized models) are overly sensitive to benign edits and meaning-preserving negation, with some metrics showing greater score shifts from superficial changes than from actual factual improvements. MiniCheck (specialized) is highly sensitive to negation and shows the most dramatic gaming effect (0.005 → 0.49 with a single phrase). The key caveat for ChatGPT-DA is its reliance on parametric knowledge: when reference documents contradict GPT's internal knowledge (counterfactual Wikipedia articles from ConflictBank), its discriminative ability degrades significantly, with unsupported factual summaries incorrectly rated higher in 3.1% of cases versus 0.2% under factual references.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as a critical meta-evaluation of existing automatic factuality metrics, building on prior meta-evaluation work (Gabriel et al. 2021, Chen et al. 2021a, Kamoi et al. 2023b, Goyal and Durrett 2021) that examined sensitivity to specific error types or domains. It departs from that lineage by introducing a unified stress-testing framework combining four distinct probes: a shallow MLP-based difficulty stratification, controlled sensitivity analysis (factual corrections vs. benign edits), adversarial gaming via innocuous phrase insertion, and parametric-knowledge grounding tests using counterfactual references. Rather than proposing a new metric, the paper repurposes existing evaluation infrastructure (TF-IDF analysis, paired t-tests, AUC across difficulty strata) to interrogate what these metrics actually capture versus what they purport to measure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The findings challenge a working assumption in the summarization and fact-checking communities that current SOTA metrics reliably measure factual consistency, with practical consequences for leaderboard rankings, model selection, and deployment in high-stakes domains like medicine and law. The gameability result—where appending content-free phrases produces score gains comparable to or exceeding genuine model improvements—suggests that metric-based comparisons may be partially illusory. Open questions include how to design metrics that are simultaneously robust to superficial manipulation and grounded in source content rather than parametric priors, and whether the LLM-based evaluation paradigm (ChatGPT-DA) can be made more reliably source-grounded. The paper's recommendations for saliency-aware scoring and graded-severity benchmarks point toward concrete directions but leave the core problem of building a truly robust, gameable-resistant factuality metric unresolved.
