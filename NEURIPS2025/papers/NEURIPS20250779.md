## Problem

Variance-dependent regret bounds for contextual bandits have been established almost exclusively for UCB-based algorithms, while Thompson sampling variants remain understudied. The only prior TS-based variance-aware algorithm (LinVDTS) is restricted to linear rewards and achieves suboptimal regret in the model dimension d (scaling as d^{1.5} rather than d). This paper asks whether a Feel-Good Thompson Sampling algorithm can simultaneously achieve variance-dependent regret and optimal scaling in d for general reward function classes.

## Input / Output

At each step t, the algorithm receives a context x_t, a context-dependent action set A_t, and the noise variance σ_t² (revealed by the environment under a weak-adversary assumption). It samples a reward function estimate from a posterior distribution and selects the action a_t that maximizes the sampled function. The theoretical guarantee is a regret bound of Õ(√(dc·log|F|)·√(Σσ_t²) + dc), where dc is the generalized decoupling coefficient; for linear bandits this reduces to Õ(d√Λ + d). Notably, the algorithm does not require knowledge of the horizon T at initialization.

## Method

FGTS-VA constructs a posterior distribution that weights each historical log-likelihood by a variance-dependent coefficient η_s = σ̄_s^{-2} (where σ̄_s = max{σ_s, α}) and augments it with a Type-B feel-good exploration term λ_t·max_a f(x_t,a) that depends only on the current step. The exploration coefficient λ_t = c√Λ_t/σ̄_t² uses the partial variance sum Λ_t rather than the total Λ, eliminating the need to know T. The analysis introduces a generalized decoupling coefficient with free parameters β_t, which upper-bounds the Bellman error by relating current-step error to historical squared errors weighted by β_s/β_t. Two key proof techniques avoid the obstacle that blocks Type-A FGTS: (1) taking expectation over reward noise first (exploiting subgaussianity) before posterior sampling, and (2) applying Donsker-Varadhan duality twice to convert log-expectation terms into simple expectations plus KL divergences that cancel. For linear bandits, dc = Õ(d) via the elliptical potential lemma, yielding the near-optimal Õ(d√Λ + d) regret matching UCB-based methods.

## Results

The main theoretical result (Theorem 5.4) establishes that FGTS-VA achieves expected regret of $\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{(1+\Lambda)\cdot dc\cdot\log|\mathcal{F}|} + dc)$, where $\Lambda = \sum_{t=1}^T \sigma_t^2$ is the total variance and $dc$ is the generalized decoupling coefficient. For linear contextual bandits, this specializes to the nearly optimal $\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}(d\sqrt{\Lambda} + d)$, matching the best UCB-based algorithms (Weighted OFUL+, SAVE). In the deterministic case ($\Lambda=0$), the regret reduces to $\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}(d)$, matching the known lower bound. In synthetic experiments with $d=5$ linear bandits under sparse and dense noise, FGTS-VA outperforms all baselines by a large margin across 100 runs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FGTS-VA matches the regret bound of Weighted OFUL+ (Zhou & Gu, 2022) and SAVE (Zhao et al., 2023) at $\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}(d\sqrt{\Lambda}+d)$, but is the first TS-based algorithm to achieve this rate, and unlike Weighted OFUL+ it does not require knowledge of the horizon $T$. Against LinVDTS (Xu et al., 2023), the only prior TS-based variance-aware algorithm, FGTS-VA improves the dependence on dimension from $d^{1.5}$ to $d$, making it optimal in $d$. Compared to standard FGTS (Zhang, 2022), which has regret $\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}(d\sqrt{T})$ independent of noise structure, FGTS-VA achieves the strictly better $\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}(d\sqrt{\Lambda}+d)$ whenever $\Lambda < T$, and in the deterministic case improves from $\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}(d\sqrt{T})$ to $\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}(d)$. In the empirical comparison (Figure 1), FGTS-VA with $c=0.003$ outperforms all four baselines by a large margin in both sparse and dense noise settings, confirming the theoretical advantage in practice.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Feel-Good Thompson Sampling (FGTS) from Zhang (2022) and Dann et al. (2021) to the variance-dependent contextual bandit setting, where prior TS-based work (LinVDTS, Xu et al. 2023) was restricted to linear rewards and suboptimal in dimension d. The key algorithmic departure is adopting the Type B posterior (originally from model-free RL) rather than the Type A posterior used in standard FGTS for contextual bandits, combined with variance-dependent weights η_s = σ̄_s^{-2}. Analytically, the paper introduces a generalized decoupling coefficient with inhomogeneous parameters β_t, extending the standard decoupling coefficient from Dann et al. (2021), and proves it is bounded by the generalized Eluder dimension for general function classes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FGTS-VA is the first Thompson-sampling-based algorithm to achieve the nearly optimal variance-dependent regret Õ(d√Λ + d) for linear contextual bandits, matching the best UCB-based results (Zhou and Gu 2022; Zhao et al. 2023) and resolving a gap where TS methods were suboptimal in d. The generalized decoupling coefficient provides a reusable analytical tool for future FGTS-based algorithms with inhomogeneous parameters. A key limitation is the requirement that σ_t² is revealed to the agent (weak adversary setting); extending to unknown or adversarial variances, and to reinforcement learning, are identified as important open directions.
