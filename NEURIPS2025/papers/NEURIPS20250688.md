## Problem

Rotary Positional Encodings (RoPE) are widely attributed their success to shift-equivariance (relativity), and this assumption drives the design of all multi-dimensional extensions (Axial RoPE, Mixed RoPE, LieRE). However, this creates a circular argument: equivariant embeddings are assumed to work because they are relative, and new embeddings must be relative because relative ones work. The paper asks whether strict equivariance is actually necessary for RoPE's performance in vision, where context lengths are short and patch content is highly informative.

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-100, 224×224 for ImageNet-1k) processed by a ViT-S backbone, producing class labels for classification and per-pixel segmentation masks (via bilinear interpolation of patch logits) for the auxiliary Pascal VOC task. Token positions are parameterized as 2D coordinates (p_x, p_y) in [−π, π], and the positional encoding modifies query and key vectors within the attention mechanism rather than being added to patch embeddings. For the resolution-generalization experiment, test images are upscaled and patch positions are extrapolated proportionally.

## Method

The paper first proves that 1D-LieRE is equivalent to RoPE with learned frequencies (by absorbing the eigenvector matrix into W_q and W_k), and that M-D LieRE with commutative generators reduces exactly to Mixed RoPE, establishing Mixed RoPE as the most general equivariant rotary embedding. It then proposes Spherical RoPE, which replaces 2D rotation pairs with 3D triplets rotated by a yaw matrix (for p_x) followed by a roll matrix (for p_y), making the generators strictly non-commutative and thus breaking equivariance while retaining oblique-direction expressivity and fixed-frequency efficiency. A second ablation, Uniform RoPE, uses Axial RoPE with a single shared frequency to isolate the effect of frequency diversity. Empirically, Spherical RoPE matches or exceeds Mixed RoPE on CIFAR-100 and ImageNet-1k, and generalizes best to higher resolutions, while Uniform RoPE outperforms learned APE but underperforms diverse-frequency RoPE variants—concluding that equivariance is a minor contributor to RoPE's advantage over additive positional encodings in vision.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K, Spherical RoPE (non-equivariant) achieves 76.4% top-1 accuracy with fixed frequencies and 77.4% with learned frequencies, matching Mixed RoPE's 77.4% while outperforming Axial RoPE (75.6%) and Uniform RoPE (74.9%). On CIFAR-100, Mixed RoPE leads at 74.7%, with Learned Spherical RoPE close behind at 74.1%, while Learned APE trails all RoPE variants by 3–12 points. Spherical RoPE also extrapolates best to higher image resolutions (Figure 3), further suggesting equivariance is not the driver of RoPE's generalization advantage.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Learned APE (ViT baseline [17]), all RoPE variants win by large margins: Spherical RoPE gains +3.7 points on ImageNet (76.4 vs 72.7) and +9.0 on CIFAR-100 (73.2 vs 64.2), confirming that rotary encodings broadly outperform additive positional embeddings. Against Axial RoPE [68,15], Spherical RoPE consistently wins on both datasets (76.4 vs 75.6 on ImageNet; 73.2 vs 72.1 on CIFAR-100), indicating that oblique frequency directions matter more than strict axial separation. Against Mixed RoPE [26], Learned Spherical RoPE ties exactly on ImageNet (77.4 vs 77.4) and trails by only 0.6 points on CIFAR-100 (74.1 vs 74.7), despite lacking the commutativity constraint that guarantees equivariance. Against LieRE [52], Spherical RoPE is roughly 2× faster in wall-clock time (6.7 s vs 13.6 s per forward pass with torch.autocast) while achieving comparable or better accuracy, making it a more practical non-equivariant alternative. The key takeaway is that removing equivariance (Spherical RoPE) costs nothing in performance, while removing frequency diversity (Uniform RoPE) costs 1.5–2.5 points, suggesting frequency diversity—not relative position—is the critical property.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies the landscape of rotary positional encodings through a Lie algebra lens, showing that 1D LieRE is equivalent to RoPE with learned frequencies, and that M-D LieRE with commutative generators reduces exactly to Mixed RoPE (Heo et al., 2024), while Axial RoPE (Su, 2021) corresponds to a stricter mutual-exclusivity constraint. It then departs from the prevailing assumption that equivariance is the critical property of RoPE by proposing Spherical RoPE, a non-commutative (hence non-equivariant) variant that rotates 3D triplets via Euler angles, and Uniform RoPE, a single-frequency ablation. The paper positions itself against the 'circular argument' in the literature where equivariance is assumed necessary because relative embeddings work well, and new methods must be relative because relative embeddings work well.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a non-equivariant positional encoding (Spherical RoPE) matches or exceeds its equivariant counterparts on classification and outperforms them on resolution extrapolation challenges a widely-held assumption in the positional encoding literature, potentially freeing future vision PE designs from the equivariance constraint for simpler, faster implementations. However, the evidence is limited to ViT-S on CIFAR-100 and ImageNet-1K with short context lengths (~256 patches), and the authors explicitly note that the result may not transfer to NLP where context windows reach 128K tokens and numerical stability of non-skew-symmetric generators becomes critical. Open questions include whether non-equivariant encodings scale favorably at larger model sizes, whether the small performance gaps observed here widen or narrow at scale, and whether the frequency-diversity effect (all RoPE variants beating Uniform RoPE) is the true driver of RoPE's advantage over APE.
