## Problem

Clustering multi-dimensional functional data (vector-valued random functions) remains challenging because existing approaches rely on linear representations (e.g., multivariate FPCA) that cannot capture complex nonlinear interdependencies among component functions, or are restricted to supervised settings. Moreover, prior functional autoencoders lack nonlinear decoders with universal approximation guarantees, and their clustering results are sensitive to phase variation (time warping) in the functions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255402/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes p-dimensional functional data as discrete, noise-contaminated evaluations on a compact interval T, which must first be smoothed (e.g., via cubic B-splines) and standardized before being fed to the network. It produces cluster assignments for each sample function along with their learned s-dimensional latent representations. The framework handles both Euclidean-valued and Riemannian manifold-valued functional data, with the latter requiring a readout map to project decoder outputs back onto the manifold.

## Method

FAEclust is a functional autoencoder whose encoder uses continuous functional weights (modeled as basis-function expansions) to map p-dimensional functions into a finite-dimensional latent space, while the decoder employs additional nonlinear hidden layers with functional weights and biases (Eqs. 2–4), for which the paper proves a universal approximation theorem (Theorem 1) in both Euclidean and compact Riemannian manifold settings. Training is joint: a Calinski-Harabasz-inspired clustering loss is added to the penalized reconstruction objective, and cluster assignments are updated in the forward pass via convex clustering (Eq. 5) solved by an O(n log n) path-following homotopy algorithm. Shape-informed clustering is achieved by computing pairwise similarities through the SRV/Fisher-Rao distance (or fast DTW approximations), making the clustering objective invariant to phase variation. Regularization includes an ℓ₂ orthogonality penalty on encoder functional weights and a second-order roughness penalty on decoder functional weights and biases, all optimized via mini-batch gradient descent with momentum over the basis-expansion coefficients.

## Results

On 17 Euclidean functional datasets from the UEA/UCR Time Series Repository, FAEclust achieves the best Adjusted Mutual Information (AMI) on 12 of 17 datasets and ranks top-2 on 15 of 17, with standout scores of 0.925 (Fungi), 0.907 (Plane), and 0.887 (DiatomSizeReduction). On five manifold-valued datasets (Hypersphere, Hyperbolic, Swiss roll, Lorenz, Pendulum), FAEclust attains the highest mean AMI in all scenarios, reaching 0.986 on Pendulum and 0.798 on Hyperbolic. Under random time warping (phase variation), FAEclust's mean AMI/ARI shifts by only 0.013 on average across 12 simulation scenarios, demonstrating strong shape-invariance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FAEclust outperforms all seven baselines on 12 of 17 Euclidean datasets, with the largest margins over funHDDC and funclust (which frequently score near 0, e.g., 0.001 on Symbols, 0.013 on Plane). The closest competitors are VANO (Seidman et al., 2023) and FAE (Hsieh et al., 2021): VANO narrowly edges FAEclust on CBF (0.743 vs. 0.724) and JapaneseVowels (0.899 vs. 0.893), while FAE wins on BasicMotions (0.676 vs. 0.539) and ERing (0.743 vs. 0.664). On the five manifold-valued datasets, FAEclust dominates every scenario, with particularly large gaps on Hyperbolic (0.798 vs. 0.410 for VANO, the next best) and Swiss roll (0.432 vs. 0.382 for FADP1). Under random time warping, FAEclust attains the highest AMI in 11 of 12 scenarios, improving over the best baseline by +0.10 to +0.50 AMI depending on the scenario, while the only exception (scenario L) is a near-tie where FNN scores 0.728 vs. FAEclust's 0.699 AMI but FAEclust still leads on ARI (0.709 vs. 0.667). The FNN baseline (Heinrichs et al., 2023) is competitive on a few datasets (e.g., Plane 0.841, Symbols 0.748) but is consistently outperformed by FAEclust on the majority of both Euclidean and manifold-valued benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the functional autoencoder line of work (Hsieh et al. 2021, Seidman et al. 2023) by replacing integral-kernel or grid-based parameterizations with continuous functional weights expressed as basis-function expansions, enabling both computational efficiency and discretization invariance. It departs from prior FD clustering methods that rely on linear representations (multivariate FPCA) by learning nonlinear latent embeddings through a deep MLP backbone, and from prior functional neural networks (Rossi et al. 2002, Heinrichs et al. 2023) by operating in an unsupervised, joint training-and-clustering framework. The shape-informed clustering objective, which incorporates SRV-based Fisher-Rao distance or fast DTW into a convex clustering formulation, is a novel integration of elastic shape analysis with the path-following homotopy algorithm of Hoefling (2010).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FAEclust provides the first deep-learning framework for multi-dimensional FD clustering with provable universal approximation for the decoder, robustness to phase variation, and support for manifold-valued outputs, filling a gap identified in the Zhang and Parnell (2023) review where existing methods are limited to linear representations. The near-perfect phase-invariance (mean AMI/ARI shift of only 0.013 under random time warping) is practically significant for applications like gait analysis and medical imaging where temporal misalignment is common. Open questions include scalability to very large datasets (DTW pairwise distances remain O(n²) even with fast approximations), handling of irregularly sampled or missing data, and whether the basis-function parameterization of functional weights generalizes to higher-dimensional function spaces (e.g., images as 2D functions).
