## Problem

Energy-Based Models rely on MCMC sampling (e.g., Langevin dynamics) that becomes computationally expensive and biased in high-dimensional, multi-modal energy landscapes. Existing mirror Langevin algorithms use fixed, predefined mirror maps that cannot adapt to the intrinsic geometry of complex data distributions, and no convergence analysis exists for mirror Langevin with learnable deep-network mirror maps. This paper addresses both gaps by introducing a data-driven mirror map and providing the first non-asymptotic convergence guarantee in this setting.

## Input / Output

Sampling starts from Gaussian noise N(0,I) in the dual space and iteratively refines it through T mirror Langevin steps, alternating between dual-space gradient/noise updates and primal-space mapping via the convex conjugate, ultimately producing 32×32 RGB images. For inpainting, a partially observed (masked) image serves as input, and the EBM energy is minimized subject to the constraint that unmasked pixels remain fixed. The cooperative training loop consumes both real data images and CPMLA-synthesized images at each iteration to update the EBM and CP-Flow parameters jointly.

## Method

CPMLA replaces the fixed mirror map in Mirror Langevin Dynamics with a Convex Potential Flow (CP-Flow) built from Input-Convex Neural Networks, whose gradient ∇G_θ is guaranteed to be a bijective mirror map by adding a quadratic strong-convexity term. The sampling uses the Alternative Forward Discretization (MLA_AFD) scheme: at each step, the sample is mapped to dual space, updated by a deterministic EBM gradient step and a stochastic step with geometry-aware noise scaled by ∇²G_θ, then mapped back to primal space via the convex conjugate. Training is cooperative: CP-Flow is trained by maximum likelihood (solving the Monge optimal-transport problem with a matrix-free log-determinant via Hutchinson's estimator and conjugate gradient), while the EBM is trained by contrastive divergence between real and CPMLA-synthesized samples, with synthesized samples fed back into CP-Flow training. The key theoretical contribution is a convergence proof (Theorem 4.5) showing exponential convergence with vanishing bias under a relaxed mirror log-Sobolev inequality, explicitly accounting for approximation errors from both the EBM and CP-Flow neural networks.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, CPMLA achieves an FID of 20.85 with only 20 Langevin Monte Carlo iterations, outperforming the strongest EBM baseline CoopFlow (FID 21.16) which requires 30 iterations. CPMLA also reduces wall-clock inference time to 15.92 s per 1k images (vs. 16.84 s for CoopFlow) and uses only 0.26 M parameters for the flow component (0.9% of CoopFlow's 28.78 M). Total training time on CIFAR-10 drops from ~38 hours (CoopFlow) to 10.5 hours (CPMLA) under 24 GB VRAM constraints. On the Eight Gaussians toy benchmark, CPMLA matches CP-Flow's 10-iteration result in just 3 iterations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest EBM-family baseline CoopFlow [58], CPMLA wins on every axis: lower FID (20.85 vs. 21.16), fewer LMC iterations (20 vs. 30), less inference time (15.92 s vs. 16.84 s per 1k images), and dramatically fewer flow parameters (0.26 M vs. 28.78 M, i.e. 0.9%). Compared to other Flow+EBM methods, CPMLA substantially outperforms EBM-FCE (FID 37.30) and NT-EBM (FID 78.12), and also beats the EBM-only baselines CoopNets (33.61), CoopVAEBM (36.20), EBM-IG (38.20), and EBM-SR (44.50). Against standalone normalizing flows (Glow 45.99, Residual Flow 46.37), CPMLA achieves a markedly lower FID, demonstrating that the cooperative EBM+CP-Flow training is more effective than flow-only density estimation. The paper notes that score-based diffusion models (NCSN++ FID 2.20) and GANs (StyleGAN2-ADA FID 2.92) still achieve lower FIDs, but these belong to different model families with different architectural requirements; CPMLA's contribution is closing the gap within the EBM family while preserving EBM flexibility. On the toy Eight Gaussians benchmark, CPMLA converges in 3 iterations to match CP-Flow's 10-iteration result [20], confirming faster sampling convergence in a controlled setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Mirror Langevin Dynamics (Hsieh et al., 2018; Jiang, 2021; Ahn & Chewi, 2021), which previously relied on a fixed, predefined mirror map, by replacing it with a learnable Convex Potential Flow (Huang et al., 2020) that is jointly trained with the EBM via a cooperative learning strategy. The key conceptual departure is making the sampling geometry data-driven and adaptive rather than static, grounded in Brenier's optimal transport theorem to guarantee the mirror map is the gradient of a convex potential. The convergence analysis builds on Jiang's (2021) isoperimetric framework but is the first to incorporate approximation errors from deep neural network parameterizations of both the mirror map and the energy function.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

EBMs have long suffered from slow, biased MCMC sampling in high-dimensional spaces; CPMLA addresses this by adapting the sampling geometry to the data manifold, achieving competitive FID scores with dramatically fewer flow parameters (0.9% of CoopFlow's) and fewer inference iterations. The theoretical contribution—exponential convergence with vanishing bias under relaxed log-concavity for DNN-parameterized mirror maps—fills a gap in the mirror Langevin literature. However, the mirror LSI assumption (Assumption 4.1) remains difficult to verify for complex EBM targets, and the Hessian diagonal approximation introduces bias, leaving open whether the convergence guarantees hold in practice for highly multi-modal distributions. Future work on connecting sampling to optimization (adaptive step sizes, higher-order discretizations) could further close the gap between theory and practice.
