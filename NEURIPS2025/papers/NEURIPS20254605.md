## Problem

Existing multi-scale time series anomaly detection methods either model each scale independently or fuse features with simple strategies, overlooking the dynamic cross-scale associations that are disrupted during anomalies. Additionally, fixed sliding windows prevent these methods from capturing global multi-scale context beyond individual window boundaries, weakening detection performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254605/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a multivariate time series window (window sizes range from 96 to 192 depending on the dataset) using a channel-independent formulation, where each dimension is processed separately. It produces a per-time-point anomaly score by interpolating and averaging reconstruction errors across all m scales to a unified length, then applies the SPOT extreme-value thresholding to yield binary anomaly labels.

## Method

CrossAD introduces two coupled mechanisms. First, cross-scale reconstruction generates m multi-scale series via average pooling with different kernel sizes, encodes each scale independently through a Transformer encoder with a scale-independent block-diagonal mask, and then decodes finer-grained series from coarser ones using a cross-scale lower-triangular mask so that each scale can only attend to its coarser counterparts; the reconstruction error at each scale serves as the anomaly signal. Second, cross-window modeling builds a learnable query library of n sub-series queries, uses a period-aware router (top-k Fourier components fed into an MLP with Gumbel-Softmax gating) to select relevant queries per window, extracts cross-scale sub-series representations via cross-attention, and maintains a global multi-scale context of K prototypes updated by exponential moving average; this context is injected into the decoder's next-scale generation step, allowing the model to share multi-scale information across windows and transcend the fixed window-size limitation.

## Results

CrossAD achieves the best VUS-PR and VUS-ROC scores across all seven real-world datasets (SMD, MSL, SMAP, SWaT, PSM, GECCO, SWAN). Headline results include VUS-PR of 0.6211 on GECCO, 0.5596 on PSM, 0.4767 on SWaT, and 0.3144 on MSL, consistently outperforming the strongest multi-scale baselines (TimesNet, TimeMixer) by 10–30+ percentage points on VUS-PR. On the multi-metric evaluation (Table 2), CrossAD also leads on Accuracy, Standard-F1, Affiliation-F1, AUC-ROC, AUC-PR, Range-AUC-ROC, and Range-AUC-PR across SMD, PSM, and GECCO.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CrossAD outperforms all 18 baselines on both VUS-PR and VUS-ROC across all seven datasets. The closest competitors are the multi-scale methods TimesNet and TimeMixer; CrossAD surpasses TimesNet by 3.0 pp on SMD VUS-PR (0.2344 vs 0.2040), 4.1 pp on MSL (0.3144 vs 0.2731), and a dramatic 36.1 pp on SWaT (0.4767 vs 0.1158), where TimesNet and TimeMixer both struggle severely. On GECCO, CrossAD achieves 0.6211 VUS-PR versus 0.4578 for TimesNet and 0.4606 for TimeMixer, a gap of ~16 pp. Non-multi-scale baselines such as Anomaly Transformer (0.0796 VUS-PR on SMD) and DCdetector (0.0814) are far behind, while GPT4TS and ModernTCN show competitive VUS-ROC on some datasets (e.g., GECCO) but lag significantly on VUS-PR. On SWAN, CrossAD (0.9171 VUS-PR) is essentially tied with TimesNet (0.9160), the only dataset where the margin is minimal.

## Relation to Prior Work

CrossAD carves new ground at the intersection of multi-scale time series modeling and reconstruction-based anomaly detection. Prior multi-scale methods (TimesNet, TimeMixer, Pathformer) either model each scale independently or apply simple feature fusion, while reconstruction-based detectors (Anomaly Transformer, DCdetector, CAE) operate at a single scale. CrossAD departs from both lines by explicitly modeling the *mapping* between scales—reconstructing fine-grained series from coarser ones—and by introducing a query-library mechanism with a global multi-scale context to transcend fixed sliding-window boundaries, a problem neither prior line addresses directly.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The cross-scale reconstruction perspective offers a principled explanation for why multi-scale modeling helps anomaly detection: anomalies disrupt the normal coarse-to-fine mapping, and this disruption is detectable as reconstruction failure. The cross-window modeling via learnable sub-series queries and EMA-updated prototypes is a practical mechanism for sharing context across windows without requiring the full sequence in memory. Open questions include how the approach scales to very long or streaming series, whether the EMA-based context update is sufficient for non-stationary data, and whether the reconstruction-error criterion remains reliable when anomalies are subtle or when the normal distribution is highly multimodal. The paper acknowledges training-time overhead from continuous context updates but does not explore online or incremental variants.
