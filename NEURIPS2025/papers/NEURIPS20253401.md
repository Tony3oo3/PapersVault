## Problem

Existing Spiking Self-attention (SSA) suffers from two fundamental limitations that cap its performance well below ANN Transformers: (1) binary spike trains can only encode positive-positive Query-Key interactions, completely discarding negative-negative and mixed-polarity correlations; and (2) the omission of Softmax removes row-stochasticity constraints, leaving attention scores on incomparable scales across rows. Together, these deficiencies reduce SSA to a simplified token mixer rather than a true attention mechanism.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253401/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts standard RGB images (224×224 for ImageNet-1K classification, variable resolution for ADE20K segmentation) or event streams converted to event frames (for FE108, FELT, VisEvent tracking) as input, and produces class labels, pixel-wise segmentation masks, or target bounding boxes respectively. A key operational detail is that all internal computations are spike-driven: Q and K are represented as ternary spike trains (−1, 0, +1) over T=4 timesteps, and the attention-to-value multiplication is executed as bit-shift operations on V's membrane potentials rather than multiply-accumulate, consuming roughly 1/20 the energy of a standard AC operation.

## Method

BSA introduces two complementary mechanisms into the spiking attention pipeline. First, it replaces binary spiking neurons with ternary spiking neurons (TSN) for Q and K, so that the Q×Kᵀ product becomes a Ternary Matrix Product (TMP) that preserves both homopolar and heteropolar polarity interactions; the paper proves TMP maintains zero mean (matching real-valued RMP) and achieves approximately 2× the local Pearson correlation with RMP compared to binary BMP at typical firing rates. Second, it proposes Shiftmax, a Softmax approximation defined as 2^(x_i − ⌈log₂(Σ2^x_j)⌉), which induces low-entropy activation and constrains row sums to the interval [0.5, 1] using only integer arithmetic and bit-shifts. When the resulting Score matrix (powers of two) multiplies V, the operation reduces to right-shifting V's membrane potentials by integer amounts, eliminating all MAC operations while preserving V's continuous-valued information. The full BSA module is drop-in replaceable within existing Spiking Transformer backbones (Spikingformer, QKformer, SDT-V3), requiring no architectural changes beyond the attention computation.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K, BSA plugged into Spikingformer achieves 76.14% top-1 accuracy (D=512, +1.35% over baseline) and 76.82% (D=768, +0.97%). For QKformer, BSA reaches 82.39% (D=512, +0.35%), and for SDT-V3 it reaches 80.16% (L, +0.36%). On ADE20K semantic segmentation, SDT-V3+BSA achieves 43.41% mIoU (19M params, +2.11%), and on event-based tracking, SDTrack+BSA outperforms SDTrack on all three benchmarks (e.g., +1.2 AUC and +3.1 PR on VisEvent).

## Baselines & Comparisons

BSA consistently improves over its host architectures: Spikingformer gains +1.35%/+0.97%, QKformer gains +0.41%/+0.35%, and SDT-V3 gains +0.36% to +0.74% on ImageNet-1K. Against earlier Spiking Transformers (Spikformer 73.38%, SDT-V1 74.57%), BSA-augmented Spikingformer (76.14%) leads by 2.76–3.13 points at comparable parameter budgets. On ADE20K segmentation, SDT-V3+BSA (43.41% mIoU) surpasses SDT-V3 (41.3%), SDT-V2 (35.3%), and even the ANN baseline PVT-Small (39.8%), though it remains below the much larger InternImage-T (48.1%). On event-based tracking, SDTrack+BSA beats all SNN trackers (STNet, SNNTrack) and ANN trackers (STARK, ARTrack, OSTrack, HIPTrack) across FELT, FE108, and VisEvent, with the largest margin on VisEvent (+3.1 PR over SDTrack, +5.5 PR over STNet). The smaller gains on QKformer and SDT-V3 relative to Spikingformer are attributed to their already-strong baselines and Metaformer-style designs where self-attention contributes less to overall performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Spiking Transformer lineage (Spikformer → Spikingformer → SDT-V1/V2/V3 → QKformer) by identifying a fundamental flaw in the Spiking Self-attention (SSA) paradigm: binary Q-K matrix products discard all negative and mixed-polarity correlations, reducing self-attention to a simplified token mixer. It departs from prior work by introducing ternary spike neurons (borrowed from Guo et al., AAAI 2024) into the Q-K correlation computation and proposing Shiftmax, a bit-shift-based Softmax approximation, as the first energy-efficient normalization mechanism for spiking attention. The theoretical analysis (Theorems 1–2) formally establishes that ternary products preserve zero-mean statistics and ~2× local Pearson correlation with real-valued products compared to binary, providing a principled justification absent from prior Spiking Transformer designs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BSA offers a drop-in replacement for the self-attention module in existing Spiking Transformers, consistently improving accuracy across ImageNet-1K, ADE20K segmentation, and event-based tracking without adding MAC operations—making it directly relevant to neuromorphic hardware deployment on platforms like Tianjic and Loihi. The Shiftmax mechanism is particularly notable for converting normalization into bit-shifts (1/20 the energy of AC operations), addressing a long-standing gap in SNN design. Key open questions include whether the gains persist at larger model scales (the authors acknowledge testing only up to ~19M parameters), whether the ternary threshold and Shiftmax hyperparameters transfer across architectures without re-tuning, and practical FPGA deployment where memory access patterns for the shift operations remain uncharacterized.
