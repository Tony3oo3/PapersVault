## Problem

Transformers rely on full self-attention, incurring quadratic time and memory complexity that limits long-context scalability, while recurrent models compress the entire sequence into a fixed-size state, suffering from memory degradation and limited fine-grained retrieval. No existing architecture offers an intermediate design that partially compresses local context while preserving direct access to distant tokens, leaving a gap between RNN efficiency and attention accuracy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251165/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a text token sequence of length T, which is partitioned into C chunks of length L (T = C·L), and produces standard next-token predictions for autoregressive language modeling. The key structural detail is that inter-chunk attention operates on chunk-level representations (the last gated key/value of each chunk) rather than individual tokens, reducing the effective attention sequence length by a factor of L. A hybrid variant interleaves RAT layers with sliding-window attention (window size 1024) to combine efficient long-range modeling with strong local interactions.

## Method

RAT applies a two-level hierarchical mixing: within each chunk, a lightweight linear recurrence (EMA-like per-dimension gating) compresses local value and key vectors, and across chunks, softmax attention is computed on the resulting chunk-level representations to enable direct retrieval of distant information. By adjusting the chunk size L, RAT interpolates between full attention (L=1) and pure RNN (L=T), with L=16 achieving ~9× faster single-layer decoding while matching attention accuracy. The causal masking challenge during parallel training is resolved via an online softmax formulation that separately computes inter-chunk and intra-chunk contributions and merges them by adjusting the softmax denominator. The architecture requires no custom CUDA kernels, using PyTorch's associative scan for intra-chunk recurrence and flex attention for inter-chunk attention, and is compatible with standard tensor and context parallelism. A hybrid variant interleaves RAT with sliding-window attention, yielding the best overall results across commonsense reasoning, long-context QA, summarization, and code completion benchmarks at 1.3B scale.

## Results

With a chunk size of L=16, RAT achieves a 7× training speedup at 100K sequence length and a 9× generation speedup at position 4K compared to full attention, while maintaining comparable performance (Val. PPL 7.67 vs. 7.61). The full 1.3B model reaches 31,170 tokens/sec maximum throughput versus 3,152 for attention (10.2× ratio). The hybrid RAT(L=16)-SWA variant achieves the best overall results: 58.0 avg. commonsense accuracy (+1.1 over attention), 28.2 code EditSum (+4.3 over attention), 63.2 NQA F1, and 24.6 QMSum Rouge-L (+1.2 over attention), all at 13,582 tokens/sec throughput.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Mamba (Gu & Dao, 2023), RAT(L=16) outperforms on 6 of 7 commonsense reasoning tasks (e.g., ARC-C 36.35 vs. 35.40 acc_n, BoolQ 63.30 vs. 61.13) while using only ~20ND memory slots versus Mamba's 64ND. Against Mamba2 (Dao & Gu, 2024), RAT(L=16) wins on ARC-E (72.64 vs. 72.47), BoolQ (63.30 vs. 60.13), and most LongBench QA tasks (e.g., HotpotQA 16.7 vs. 11.8 F1, GovReport 24.9 vs. 6.7 Rouge-L), though it trails on WinoGrande (53.20 vs. 55.24). Against GatedDeltaNet (Yang et al., 2024), RAT(L=16) leads on single-document QA (19.6 vs. 17.1 avg. F1), multi-document QA (14.0 vs. 11.3 avg. F1), and summarization (20.2 vs. 10.6 avg. Rouge-L), but underperforms on code completion (17.4 vs. 20.4 EditSum); the hybrid RAT(L=16)-SWA closes this gap decisively at 28.2 vs. 17.4 for GatedDeltaNet-SWA. Overall, RAT(L=16) matches or exceeds attention on most long-context benchmarks while being 9–10× faster, and the SWA hybrid variant consistently achieves the best scores across all task categories.

## Relation to Prior Work

RAT positions itself as an intermediate architecture between the two dominant paradigms in sequence modeling: full softmax attention (which retains per-token access but scales quadratically) and recurrent/state-space models (which compress the entire history into a fixed-size state but suffer from memory degradation on long sequences). It builds on the simplified linear recurrence of Smith et al. (S4) for intra-chunk computation and on the online softmax formulation of Milakov & Gimelshein to solve the non-trivial causal masking problem in parallel training. Conceptually, RAT departs from the 'duality' framing that equates linear recurrence with linear attention, instead treating recurrence as a lightweight local compressor and reserving softmax attention for cross-chunk global access — the reverse of approaches like FLASH or Longformer that use attention locally and recurrence/linear attention globally.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is the 7–10× throughput improvement at long sequence lengths while matching or exceeding full attention on most benchmarks, achieved without custom CUDA kernels and with a KV cache reduced by a factor of L (chunk size). The single hyperparameter L provides a clean interpolation knob between RNN and attention, and the hybrid with sliding-window attention consistently outperforms both pure variants, suggesting a recipe for future efficient LLMs. Key open questions include whether these results hold at 7B+ scale (the paper only validates at 1.3B), whether more expressive intra-chunk recurrences (2D, nonlinear) further close the gap on retrieval-heavy tasks, and whether per-layer or per-head chunk sizes can be tuned for additional gains. Length generalization remains a concern inherited from RoPE, though NoPE mitigates it.
