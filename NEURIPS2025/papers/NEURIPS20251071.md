## Problem

Existing exemplar-based image editing methods still depend on text prompts or implicit text-based instructions to describe the change within paired source-target examples, limiting their ability to capture complex editing semantics that are difficult to articulate in language. Methods like Concept Slider, which train a single LoRA with opposing scaling factors, struggle with both learning intricate semantics (e.g., elf ears, chubbiness) and preserving the identity of the original image.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251071/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During training, the method consumes 1–3 paired source-target RGB image pairs (no text prompts) and produces two learned LoRA adapters (rank 16) on top of a frozen FLUX.1-dev backbone. At inference, a single RGB image is edited by applying the learned semantic LoRA with an adjustable scaling factor, enabling continuous, fine-grained control over the edit magnitude. Multiple semantic LoRAs can be merged at inference to compose sequential edits, and a reconstruction LoRA can be fused with the semantic LoRA to edit real (non-synthetic) images.

## Method

PairEdit jointly optimizes two LoRA modules on a frozen FLUX.1-dev rectified-flow model: a content LoRA trained with a standard diffusion reconstruction loss on the source image, and a semantic LoRA trained with a novel guidance-based semantic loss that encodes the source-to-target difference as a classifier-free-guidance direction term. The key insight is a content-preserving noise schedule (x_t = x_0 + tβε) that makes the weight of the image difference (x_0^A − x_0^B) time-independent, yielding a clean target noise prediction ε* = βε_0 + η(x_0^A − x_0^B) where η is a fixed constant aligned with the LoRA scaling factor. Only the semantic LoRA receives gradients from the semantic loss (gradients are stopped to the content LoRA), cleanly disentangling content reconstruction from semantic variation learning. During inference, the semantic LoRA is disabled for the first 14 denoising steps to preserve the original image structure, and its scaling factor provides continuous control over edit strength.

## Results

PairEdit achieves the best identity preservation (lowest LPIPS) and highest editing magnitude (highest CLIP) across all four evaluated semantics. On Chubbiness, PairEdit reduces LPIPS to 0.0815, compared to 0.2173 (SDEdit), 0.1423 (Textual Slider), and 0.1747 (Visual Slider), while simultaneously achieving the highest CLIP score of 0.2420. For Glasses, the LPIPS gap is equally pronounced: 0.0911 for PairEdit versus 0.1370–0.1706 for baselines. In the user study, participants preferred PairEdit over VISII (93.8%), Analogist (98.7%), and Visual Slider (96.2%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PairEdit outperforms all three quantitative baselines (SDEdit, Textual Concept Slider, Visual Concept Slider) on both LPIPS and CLIP across every semantic, with the largest LPIPS gains on Chubbiness (0.0815 vs. 0.1423–0.2173) and Glasses (0.0911 vs. 0.1370–0.1706). In qualitative comparisons, VISII produces low-quality edits that fail to capture semantic variations, Edit Transfer generates images nearly identical to the original, and Visual Concept Slider captures some semantics but consistently fails to preserve identity and struggles with complex edits like elf ears and chubbiness. Pair Customization also fails on complex semantics and content consistency. In the user study, PairEdit is strongly preferred over VISII (93.8%), Analogist (98.7%), and Visual Slider (96.2%), confirming that the quantitative advantages translate to perceptual quality. The DINO metric (Appendix Table 3) further corroborates the LPIPS results, with PairEdit achieving the highest DINO scores on Chubbiness (0.9588) and Glasses (0.9065).

## Relation to Prior Work

PairEdit builds on the rectified-flow formulation of FLUX and the LoRA adaptation mechanism, but its core conceptual departure is repurposing the classifier-free guidance (CFG) direction to explicitly encode semantic variation between paired images, rather than relying on text prompts or LLM-generated descriptions as in VISII and Edit Transfer. It extends the two-LoRA disentanglement idea from Pair Customization by introducing a content-preserving noise schedule (Eq. 8) that makes the semantic weight time-independent, a modification specific to the rectified-flow forward process. Unlike Concept Slider, which constrains a single LoRA with opposing scaling factors to predict identical noise, PairEdit separates content reconstruction and semantic learning into distinct loss terms with dedicated LoRA modules.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method demonstrates that complex editing semantics (age progression, stylization, accessory addition) can be learned from as few as one image pair in ~8 minutes on a single GPU, without any textual guidance, which lowers the barrier for personalized editing workflows. The content-preserving noise schedule is a transferable insight for rectified-flow models more broadly, showing that modifying the forward process to retain content at high noise levels stabilizes semantic learning. Key limitations include the requirement for paired (not unpaired) training data, the dependence on rectified-flow backbones (performance degrades on DDPM-based SDXL), and difficulty with large structural changes. An open question is whether the guidance-direction formulation can be extended to unpaired or weakly-supervised settings.
