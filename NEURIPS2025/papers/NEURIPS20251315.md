## Problem

Existing LLM unlearning methods produce unnatural or collapsed responses when queried about forgotten content, rely on large-scale forget and retain datasets that are infeasible to construct for real-world models, and suffer from catastrophic utility loss due to imprecise forgetting boundaries. No prior approach simultaneously achieves targeted unlearning, natural refusal behavior, and preservation of general utility under minimal supervision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251315/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes text queries from two sources: a small subset (12%) of the forget set containing sensitive-entity questions, and a synthesized boundary set (8%) created by replacing the sensitive entity with a permissible counterpart while preserving sentence structure. The model produces either a safe refusal response (for forget-related queries, identified via template matching) or a normal informative response (for boundary/permissible queries, scored by ROUGE-L against reference outputs). The boundary queries serve as hard negatives that sit immediately adjacent to the refusal decision boundary.

## Method

RULE formulates LLM unlearning as a refusal boundary optimization problem solved via a two-stage procedure. Stage 1 (Rejection Steering) performs supervised fine-tuning on a small forget set to establish a reliable refusal prior, ensuring the model can generate valid refusals during RL rollouts. Stage 2 (Refusal Boundary Optimization) applies on-policy RL (PPO, GRPO, or Reinforce++) over the combined forget and boundary sets, using a two-branch verifiable reward: forget queries receive reward for matching a refusal template and mentioning the target entity, while boundary queries receive reward for non-refusal responses with high ROUGE-L overlap against reference answers. The KL reference is anchored to the rejection-steered model to preserve basic refusal capability while refining boundary behavior. A theoretical analysis (Theorem 1) shows that this RL-based boundary learning generalizes to unseen semantically related queries in the latent forget set, unlike SFT which overfits to the observed forget subset.

## Results

On the RWKU benchmark (llama3-8b-instruct), RULE (ReBO_RPP) achieves a Forget Quality of 22.6 (lower is better), outperforming the best baseline SimNPO (40.1) by 17.5 points, while using only 12.1% of the forget set and 8.03% of boundary data versus 100% for all baselines. RULE (ReBO_GRPO) achieves a Forget Naturalness score of 89.1, surpassing the best baseline NPO+KLR (72.8) by 16.3 points. On MUSE-books (llama2-7b), RULE achieves near-perfect forgetting (Verb. 0.0, Know. 0.9) with only 2.9% of training data while maintaining utility at 55.6 (vs. 55.2 original). RULE also improves general utility, boosting truthfulness by 14.1 points over the original model on RWKU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RULE dominates all three baselines (GA, NPO, SimNPO) on both forget quality and response naturalness while using an order of magnitude less data. On RWKU, ReBO_RPP achieves Forget Quality 22.6 vs. GA (68.4), NPO (40.3), and SimNPO (40.1) — a 17.5-point margin over the best baseline. On naturalness, ReBO_GRPO scores 89.1 vs. NPO+KLR (72.8), GA (40.7), and SimNPO (30.5), a 16.3-point gap over the strongest baseline. On MUSE-books, RULE achieves near-zero forgetting (Verb. 0.0, Know. 0.9) with only 2.9% data, while GA and SimNPO collapse to 0.0 utility and NPO retains only 5.9% utility. The key differentiator is that RULE's refusal-boundary RL produces natural, fluent refusals (Helpfulness 71.9 vs. 23.5–56.6 for baselines), whereas supervised baselines produce unnatural or collapsed outputs. RULE's retain quality (73.7) is slightly below the original model (87.6) but comparable to or better than most baselines under equivalent forget quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

RULE departs from the dominant paradigm in LLM unlearning—supervised loss-based optimization (gradient ascent, negative preference optimization, SimNPO)—by reframing forgetting as a refusal boundary optimization problem solved via on-policy reinforcement learning (PPO, GRPO, Reinforce++). While prior methods directly adjust parameters to suppress target knowledge, RULE instead trains the model to learn a behavioral policy: refuse on forget-related queries while responding naturally on boundary cases, using a verifiable reward function. The two-stage design (supervised Rejection Steering followed by RL-based Refusal Boundary Optimization) repurposes RLHF-style training machinery for unlearning, and the synthesized boundary set (entity-replaced queries) serves as hard negatives that anchor the decision boundary without requiring a full retain set.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RULE demonstrates that unlearning can be achieved with extreme data efficiency (12% forget + 8% boundary data vs. 100% for baselines) while simultaneously improving response naturalness and generalizing refusal to unseen semantically related queries—addressing two major practical pain points (unnatural outputs and over-reliance on large annotated retain sets). The finding that RL-based unlearning can actually *improve* truthfulness over the original model is counterintuitive and suggests that refusal behavior reinforces epistemic humility. Key limitations include dependence on synthetic boundary data quality (small models like Qwen-2.5-7B degrade performance), evaluation on only two benchmarks and two model scales, and open questions around automated boundary discovery, off-policy variants, and multi-turn/multilingual generalization.
