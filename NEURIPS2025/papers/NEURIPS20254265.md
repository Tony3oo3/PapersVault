## Problem

Current disease risk prediction methods rely on linear polygenic risk scores derived from GWAS summary statistics, which discard individual-level genotype information and cannot capture non-additive genetic interactions. While Transformer architectures could model complex variant-variant interactions, the O(L²) self-attention complexity makes them infeasible for genome-scale sequences (L > 100K variants). No scalable deep learning framework existed for end-to-end, multitask disease prediction directly from million-scale individual genotypes.

## Input / Output

The model takes individual SNP genotype vectors of L ≈ 137,245 variants (values in {0, 1, 2, UNKN}) ordered by fixed chromosomal position from Chr1 to Chr22, with no explicit positional encodings—the genomic ordering itself provides implicit relative position. It produces 18 simultaneous disease risk probabilities (binary case/control) for autoimmune and inflammatory conditions via a single shared representation. The multitask formulation means each individual may have labels for only a subset of the 18 diseases, with missing labels excluded from the loss.

## Method

PRSformer replaces standard self-attention with Neighborhood Attention (NA), restricting each query to a symmetric local window of k=385 neighboring variants (~100 kb genomic distance), reducing per-layer complexity from O(L²) to O(L·k). The architecture stacks only 2 Transformer blocks (d_model=64, 4 heads, GELU FFN) with pre-layer normalization, where the first NA layer captures interactions within LD blocks and the second integrates information across adjacent blocks, progressively expanding the effective receptive field. A learnable variant-specific embedding layer maps each genotype (and a separate missingness embedding) to a 64-d vector, and the final flattened representation is passed to a fully-connected output head. Trained end-to-end with multitask binary cross-entropy over 2 epochs on 4 A100 GPUs, the key empirical finding is a scaling law: the non-linear advantage over a matched linear baseline (identical architecture minus Transformer blocks) emerges only when training set size exceeds ~1M individuals, and multitask training across related immune diseases consistently outperforms single-task training at all scales.

## Results

On the European test set (N≈494k), PRSformer achieves AUROC of 0.7933 for Celiac, 0.8457 for T1D, 0.7661 for Graves', and 0.7184 for Multiple Sclerosis, consistently outperforming a matched linear model across all 18 autoimmune/inflammatory conditions. PRSformer also significantly outperforms LDPred2 (state-of-the-art summary-statistic method) on 16 of 18 diseases, with 11 differences reaching statistical significance (p<0.05, one-sided paired bootstrap). The non-linear advantage emerges only at N>1M training samples, and multi-ancestry training (PRSformer-ME, ~5M individuals) improves AUROC for non-European populations (e.g., AFR Graves' from 0.5676 to 0.6997) with minimal EUR degradation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PRSformer is compared against three baselines: (1) a matched linear model (same embeddings, same data, no Transformer blocks), (2) LDPred2 (state-of-the-art summary-statistic PRS method), and (3) Stacked LDPred2 (ensemble of up to 100 LDPred2 models via elastic net). Against the linear model, PRSformer wins on all 18 diseases, with the gap widening as training size exceeds 1M samples (e.g., Celiac AUROC gap grows from ~0 at N=500k to a clear margin at N=3.8M). Against LDPred2, PRSformer achieves higher AUROC on 16/18 diseases with 11 statistically significant wins (p<0.05, paired bootstrap); the advantage persists on a kinship-controlled test set (14/18 diseases, 6 significant), ruling out familial confounding. Against Stacked LDPred2, PRSformer+ (retrained on train+val) maintains its lead, confirming the gain is not an artifact of data usage. The multi-ancestry variant PRSformer-ME substantially improves non-European AUROC (e.g., AFR Graves' +13.2 points, EAS T1D +6.1 points) while preserving EUR performance, a capability unavailable to summary-statistic methods that operate on single-ancestry GWAS outputs.

## Relation to Prior Work

PRSformer departs from the dominant polygenic risk score paradigm, which relies on linear models applied to GWAS summary statistics (LDPred2, PRS-CS), by performing end-to-end non-linear prediction directly from individual-level genotypes. Its core architectural innovation is the adoption of Neighborhood Attention (Hassani et al., CVPR 2023) to reduce self-attention from O(L²) to O(L), making Transformers tractable for genome-scale sequences (~140k variants). The paper also extends prior deep-learning-in-genomics work, which has focused on base-pair-level DNA sequences with CNNs or small Transformers, to the variant-level genotype regime where causal signals are spread across the genome without local sequence motifs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes a practical scaling law: non-linear architectures only outperform linear PRS methods once training data exceeds ~1M individuals, a threshold that most public biobanks have not yet crossed. This finding has direct implications for when the field should invest in non-linear genomic prediction. Key open questions include interpretability of learned non-linear interactions, extension beyond autoimmune diseases to a phenome-scale setting, and whether the scaling law holds for other trait categories. The reliance on a single private 5M-individual cohort limits independent reproducibility, though code is publicly released.
