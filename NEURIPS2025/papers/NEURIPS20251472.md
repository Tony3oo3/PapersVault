## Problem

While continued self-supervised learning is a standard adaptation strategy for language models, it has not proven effective for vision-centric encoder models, where naive self-supervised fine-tuning often degrades pretrained representations. This paper tackles the question of how to adapt a pretrained vision foundation model to a new domain without any labels, identifying both the right form of unlabeled visual data and the training technique that prevents representational collapse.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251472/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes short object-centric videos (multi-view clips of a single object) alongside a pretrained ViT-Base backbone (DINO, DINOv2, or TIPS). Each video is decomposed into frame pairs separated by a random temporal gap δ, with each frame resized to 224×224 and independently augmented; local crops (96×96) are also sampled as cross-frame pairs. The output is an adapted backbone whose extracted feature embeddings improve k-NN classification accuracy in the target domain, evaluated without any task-specific labels.

## Method

VESSA reformulates DINO self-distillation for the fine-tuning regime with three critical modifications. First, an Uncertainty-Weighted Self-Distillation (UWSD) loss modulates each sample's contribution by the entropy of the teacher's prediction, prioritizing harder examples. Second, a staged training schedule first trains only the randomly initialized projection head for several epochs before unfreezing the backbone, preventing the abrupt gradient shifts that otherwise destroy pretrained knowledge. Third, the backbone is adapted asymmetrically: early layers receive LoRA low-rank updates on attention Q/K/V projections (preserving low-level features), while the last L layers are fully unfrozen to capture domain-specific semantics. The video-based frame-pair construction—sampling n pairs per video with random temporal offsets—provides natural multi-view object observations that yield substantially greater appearance diversity than static-image augmentations, as confirmed by ablations showing video input outperforms image input by ~2 p.p. even before other components are added.

## Results

On CO3D, VESSA achieves 85.03% top-1 k-NN accuracy with DINO, 91.85% with DINOv2, and 70.56% with TIPS, improving over the pretrained baselines by 6.17, 3.99, and 10.54 percentage points respectively. On MVIImageNet, VESSA reaches 92.51% (DINO), 96.01% (DINOv2), and 80.54% (TIPS), outperforming the pretrained models by 2.07, 0.26, and 1.89 p.p. The best single result is 91.85% ± 0.56 on CO3D with DINOv2, which is statistically significantly higher than the next-best method (ExPLoRA + video at 89.64% ± 0.47).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VESSA consistently outperforms the pretrained DINO, DINOv2, and TIPS baselines across both datasets, with the largest gains on TIPS (e.g., +10.54 p.p. on CO3D) and DINO (e.g., +6.17 p.p. on CO3D). Against ExPLoRA + video, VESSA wins on CO3D for both DINO (85.03 vs. 83.64) and DINOv2 (91.85 vs. 89.64, statistically significant), and on MVIImageNet for DINO (92.51 vs. 87.74); however, on MVIImageNet with DINOv2, ExPLoRA + video edges out VESSA by 0.14 p.p. (96.15 vs. 96.01), a difference that is not statistically significant. The image-based Static-baseline (naive self-supervised continuation on single frames) generally degrades performance relative to the pretrained model—dropping 6.26 p.p. for DINOv2 on CO3D and 3.22 p.p. for TIPS—confirming that video-based multi-view observations are essential for effective unsupervised adaptation. ExPLoRA without video provides only marginal gains over the pretrained model (e.g., +0.92 p.p. for DINO on CO3D), while adding video input to ExPLoRA yields larger but still smaller improvements than VESSA on CO3D.

## Relation to Prior Work

VESSA builds directly on DINO's self-distillation framework (Caron et al., ICCV 2021) and LoRA (Hu et al., ICLR 2021), repurposing both for a fine-tuning regime rather than pretraining. It departs from ExPLoRA, which constructs new domain-specific foundation models and still relies on supervised fine-tuning, by performing direct unsupervised adaptation of existing VFMs to downstream tasks. The method also contrasts with video-to-image knowledge transfer approaches (VITO, ViC-MAE) that require complex hybrid architectures and frame curation pipelines; VESSA instead uses simple object-centric videos with a lightweight self-distillation objective augmented by uncertainty weighting (UWSD). Conceptually, it bridges the NLP practice of continued pretraining on in-domain unlabeled data to the vision encoder setting, where prior attempts at naive self-supervised continuation have proven ineffective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that self-supervised fine-tuning of vision foundation models is feasible but requires careful training schedules (head adaptation before backbone unfreezing, staged LoRA-based unfreezing) to avoid catastrophic degradation—a non-trivial finding given that the analogous strategy works straightforwardly for language models. A significant open question is the severe catastrophic forgetting observed (ImageNet KNN accuracy drops from ~76–82% to ~15–18% after adaptation), which limits the method to single-domain specialization. Additionally, the requirement for structured multi-view object-centric videos constrains practical applicability to domains where such data is readily available, leaving open whether the approach can be extended to more general video or image-only settings.
