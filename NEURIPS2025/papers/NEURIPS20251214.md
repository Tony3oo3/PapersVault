## Problem

Deeper GNNs offer greater expressivity but suffer from performance degradation due to over-smoothing, optimization difficulties, and overfitting. Existing solutions trade off between these failure modes, and prior theoretical analyses adopt a purely global perspective that overlooks how generalization varies across node subgroups with different homophily levels. This paper identifies that increasing GNN depth shifts generalization preferences across homophily regions—improving some subgroups while degrading others—creating a fundamental disparity between shallow and deep variants that limits overall performance.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a node feature matrix, graph adjacency matrix, and labeled training nodes, but operates as a post-processing module: GNN experts at depths 0 through L_max are first trained independently on a subset of labeled nodes, then a separate gating model is trained on a disjoint holdout set. The gating model's input is not raw node features but augmented expert logits—each expert's logit vector concatenated with multi-hop pseudo-label distributions (1 to L_max hops) and structural encodings (smoothness distances to original and fully-smoothed features, plus PageRank centrality). The output is a single node class prediction produced by a small MLP that combines the gated expert hidden representations.

## Method

Moscat follows a decoupled 'train-then-merge' paradigm: L_max+1 GNN experts (from MLP at depth 0 to L_max layers) are trained independently, then an attention-based gating model is trained on a holdout set to learn node-adaptive combination weights. Two key techniques address practical challenges: (1) heterophily-biased sample filtering removes overfitted heterophilous nodes from the gating training set to prevent the gate from over-weighting overfitted experts, and (2) scope-aware logit augmentation appends pseudo-neighborhood label distributions and structural smoothness/centrality features to each expert's logits, enabling the gate to detect over-smoothing and overfitting patterns. The gating mechanism uses per-expert transformation matrices and a mixing matrix with softmax normalization, enforcing hard scoping where each expert only learns from its own receptive field. The theoretical foundation is a PAC-Bayesian subgroup generalization bound (Theorem 3.3) showing that the generalization gap depends on the L-hop homophily coefficient Γ_{L-1}, which decays with depth and causes the preference shift between homophilous and heterophilous subgroups.

## Results

GNN-Moscat achieves state-of-the-art node classification accuracy across all 8 benchmark datasets, outperforming the best baseline by 1.23% on Squirrel (43.71 vs. 42.48), 3.17% on snap-patents (55.33 vs. 52.16), 2.39% on arxiv-year (56.06 vs. 53.67), 2.21% on Flickr (58.52 vs. 56.31), 1.40% on amazon-ratings (56.36 vs. 54.96), 1.86% on Penn94 (86.72 vs. 84.86), 0.99% on genius (92.35 vs. 91.36), and 0.63% on ogbn-arxiv (74.13 vs. 73.50). When applied to individual base GNNs, Moscat yields average accuracy improvements of 4.05% (GCNII), 4.96% (GCN), 6.53% (SGC), 6.90% (GAT), and 12.28% (ACMGCN) over the best single-depth variant of each architecture.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GNN-Moscat outperforms all heterophily GNN baselines (H2GCN, GPRGCN, FSGNN, MixHop, GCNII, ACMGCN) on every dataset where they report results, with the largest margins on snap-patents (+3.17% over MixHop) and Squirrel (+1.23% over GCNII). Against Graph Transformers, Moscat dominates: GraphGPS and Polynormer suffer OOM on multiple large-scale datasets (snap-patents, Penn94, genius, ogbn-arxiv), while SGFormer trails Moscat by 1.06–3.24% across all 8 datasets. Among Graph MoE methods, DA-MoE is the closest competitor on genius (91.36 vs. 92.35) and Mowst on Penn94 (84.56 vs. 86.72), but Moscat still leads by 0.99% and 2.16% respectively; GMoE and Mowst underperform even well-tuned single GNNs on several datasets. The paper also shows that skip-connection variants (ResNet, GCNII, GraphSAGE-style concatenation) and learnable gating methods (JKNet, GAMLP-style attention, G² gradient gating) all underperform GCN-Moscat on both amazon-ratings and Penn94, confirming that the decoupled train-then-merge paradigm is more effective than soft-scoping approaches that mix scope information during training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Mao et al.'s (NeurIPS 2023) non-i.i.d. PAC-Bayesian generalization analysis from one-hop GNN aggregation to arbitrary L-hop scopes, revealing that increasing depth causes a *shift* in generalization preferences across homophily subgroups rather than uniform degradation. Methodologically, it departs from both the 'deeper GNN' paradigm (skip connections, GCNII, ResGCN) and the 'Graph MoE' paradigm (GMoE, Mowst, DA-MoE) by proposing a decoupled train-then-merge strategy: experts are trained independently and a post-processing attention gate is learned on a holdout set, enforcing hard scoping that prevents shallow experts from overfitting to higher-hop noise. The personalized scoping literature (GAMLP, ACMGCN, NW-GNN) informs the gating design, but those methods learn scope weights jointly during GNN training, whereas Moscat separates the two phases entirely.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The PAC-Bayesian bound providing a data-centric (rather than model-centric) explanation for the depth dilemma is a useful theoretical lens that could guide future architecture design beyond the specific Moscat method. Practically, Moscat offers a lightweight, architecture-agnostic post-processing step that consistently improves 5+ GNN families by 4–12% across 8 datasets, making it an attractive drop-in enhancement. Limitations include the need for a holdout set (reducing training data for experts), evaluation restricted to node classification, and the theoretical analysis relying on a simplified CSBM with SGC. Open questions include whether the decoupled paradigm extends to link prediction or graph-level tasks, whether the gating model's holdout requirement can be relaxed, and whether the homophily-based generalization shift holds under more realistic (non-CSBM) graph distributions.
