## Problem

The reparameterisation trick is the default single-sample gradient estimator for latent Gaussian variables, but it suffers from higher variance than the local reparameterisation trick, which only applies when pre-activations have diagonal covariance (independent weights in linear layers). No general-purpose single-sample estimator existed that combined the low variance of Rao-Blackwellisation with the generality of the reparameterisation trick, and the variance reduction of the local trick was empirically observed but not theoretically explained.

## Input / Output

The estimator consumes a standard Gaussian noise vector ε, a linear map W defining pre-activations z = W·g(ε,θ), diagonal covariance parameters V, and a differentiable loss ℓ. It produces a single-sample (M=1) unbiased gradient estimate of ∇_θ E_{q_θ}[ℓ] that is a conditional expectation of the standard reparameterisation gradient. The key computational step is solving a least-squares system A A^T β = A ε via conjugate gradient to obtain the conditional mean ε*, which then replaces ε in the backpropagation pass.

## Method

The R2-G2 estimator is the Rao-Blackwellisation of the reparameterisation gradient estimator by conditioning on pre-activations z = W·g(ε,θ), exploiting the fact that the conditional distribution ε|z is Gaussian with closed-form mean ε* = A^T(AA^T)^†Aε where A = WV. This replaces the random noise ε in the gradient with its conditional expectation ε*, provably reducing variance via the law of total variance while preserving unbiasedness. The computation of ε* is reformulated as a least-squares problem solvable by conjugate gradient, avoiding explicit O(m³) matrix inversion and reducing storage to O(m). The paper proves that the local reparameterisation trick (Kingma et al., 2015) is a special case of R2-G2 when W is a row vector and V is diagonal, thereby explaining its empirical variance reduction as an instance of Rao-Blackwellisation. Empirically, initial training with R2-G2 yields higher log-likelihoods on Bayesian CNNs and higher ELBOs on hierarchical VAEs compared to the standard reparameterisation estimator.

## Results

On hierarchical VAEs, R2-G2 consistently outperforms the reparameterisation (RT) estimator across all three datasets and both 2-layer and 3-layer architectures. The most substantial gains appear for 3-layer VAEs: 9.05 nats on MNIST (−102.45 vs −111.50 test ELBO) and 1.17 nats on Omniglot (−134.95 vs −136.12). For Bayesian CNNs on CIFAR-10, R2-G2 achieves −3.87 test log-likelihood vs −3.88 for RT with comparable accuracy (67.97% vs 67.98%). On MNIST BNNs, R2-G2 matches the local reparameterisation (LRT) estimator exactly (−3.06 test log-likelihood, 98.00% accuracy), confirming the theoretical equivalence in Theorem 4.3.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the reparameterisation (RT) estimator, R2-G2 wins on all six hierarchical VAE comparisons (2-layer and 3-layer on MNIST, Omniglot, and Fashion-MNIST), with the largest margin being 9.05 nats on 3-layer MNIST. On CIFAR-10 Bayesian CNNs, R2-G2 achieves a marginal log-likelihood improvement (−3.87 vs −3.88) with essentially identical accuracy. Against the local reparameterisation (LRT) estimator on MNIST BNNs, R2-G2 matches LRT exactly in both log-likelihood and accuracy, and their gradient variance curves coincide in Figures 1 and 3, empirically confirming Theorem 4.3 that LRT is an instance of R2-G2. LRT is not applicable to the CIFAR-10 convolutional layers since it assumes diagonal covariance, which is violated by weight sharing. Overall, R2-G2 never loses to either baseline and provides consistent (if sometimes modest) gains, with the most prominent improvements in deeper hierarchical VAEs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the reparameterisation gradient estimator (Kingma & Welling, 2014; Rezende et al., 2014) by applying Rao-Blackwellisation — a variance-reduction technique previously used for discrete latent variables (Liu et al., 2019; Kool et al., 2020; Paulus et al., 2021) — to the continuous Gaussian setting. The key conceptual move is showing that the local reparameterisation trick (Kingma et al., 2015), previously understood as a practical heuristic with empirically lower variance, is in fact an instance of Rao-Blackwellising the global reparameterisation gradient estimator for linear layers with independent Gaussian weights. The R2-G2 estimator then generalises this from the diagonal-covariance (mean-field) case to arbitrary linear transformations of Gaussian vectors, using a conjugate-gradient solver to avoid explicit matrix inversion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean theoretical unification that explains *why* the local reparameterisation trick reduces variance, and extends that benefit to settings where it previously could not be applied (e.g., convolutional layers with shared weights, hierarchical VAE decoders). However, the empirical gains are modest — the paper itself notes that single-layer VAEs do not benefit and that improvements are 'sometimes modest' — and the method is restricted to Gaussian latent variables with a diagonal V matrix in practice. Open questions include extending to non-diagonal or low-rank covariance structures, integrating with multi-sample gradient estimators, and understanding why reduced gradient variance can hinder learning in shallow VAEs.
