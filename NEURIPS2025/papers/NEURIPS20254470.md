## Problem

Planning-driven model-based RL methods (e.g., TD-MPC2) use a planner-augmented behavior policy to collect data, creating an inevitable 'actor divergence' where the data distribution shifts away from the learned policy's own behavior. This distribution shift corrupts value estimation and destabilizes policy updates, yet the planner's sample-based action distribution is non-parametric with intractable likelihood, making standard imitation or KL-based alignment inapplicable.

## Input / Output

The method consumes high-dimensional proprioceptive state vectors (up to 223-dim for the DMC dog, 151-dim for the H1hand robot) and encodes them into a 512-dim latent space via a shared encoder. It outputs continuous control actions (up to 61-dim) for locomotion tasks, with the MPPI planner operating over a 3-step horizon using 512 candidate trajectories and 6–8 reweighting iterations. The world model jointly predicts latent dynamics, rewards, and Q-values in the encoded space, and the policy is a diagonal Gaussian over the action space.

## Method

BOOM introduces a bootstrap loop in which the policy provides an initial solution to the MPPI planner, and the planner in turn bootstraps the policy through a likelihood-free forward KL alignment loss that maximizes log π(a|s) over planner-generated actions stored in the replay buffer—bypassing the need for the intractable β(a|s) likelihood. A soft Q-weighted mechanism reweights these alignment targets by exp(Q/τ) across the batch, prioritizing high-return planner actions and mitigating quality variability in historical buffer entries. The final policy objective combines standard max-Q improvement with this Q-weighted alignment term (scaled by λ_align), while the world model (encoder, dynamics, reward, value) is trained jointly via a TD-style loss in the TD-MPC2 architecture. Two theoretical theorems bound the return gap and Q-value deviation under the forward KL constraint, providing formal guarantees that alignment controls the actor divergence.

## Results

On the 7-task DeepMind Control Suite (humanoid and dog agents), BOOM achieves an average Total Average Return (TAR) of 877.7, surpassing the previous best BMPC (835.8) by +5.0% and TD-MPC2 (745.6) by +17.7%. On the 7-task Humanoid-Bench (Unitree H1hand robot), BOOM reaches an average TAR of 820.6, a dramatic +47.7% improvement over DreamerV3 at 10M iterations (555.6) and +60.5% over BMPC (511.7). BOOM sets new best results on all 14 tasks, with particularly large margins on Dog-run (+21.8% over second-best), H1hand-slide (+110.5%), and H1hand-hurdle (+121.0%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

BOOM outperforms all four baselines on every one of the 14 tasks. Against SAC (model-free), the gap is overwhelming: SAC achieves near-zero returns on most high-dimensional tasks (e.g., 1.6 on Humanoid-run, 4.3 on Dog-run), while BOOM reaches 582.8 and 820.7 respectively. Against DreamerV3 (imagination-driven), even at 10M iterations, BOOM leads by +47.7% on average on Humanoid-Bench and +225% on DMC, with DreamerV3 failing entirely on Dog tasks (≤35.4). Against TD-MPC2 (planning-driven), BOOM improves by +17.7% on DMC and +141% on H-Bench, with TD-MPC2 showing severe instability (e.g., 66.1±8.1 on H1hand-run vs. BOOM's 682.2). Against BMPC (the closest competitor, which also uses planner imitation), BOOM gains +5.0% on DMC and +60.5% on H-Bench, with BMPC exhibiting oscillatory training curves due to its lack of Q-guided weighting over variable-quality historical planner actions.

## Relation to Prior Work

BOOM builds directly on the TD-MPC2 architecture (jointly learned encoder, latent dynamics, reward, and value via TD-learning) and the MPPI sampling-based planner, but departs from the standard planning-driven MBRL pipeline by introducing a bootstrap alignment loop that explicitly addresses the actor divergence problem. It is positioned against BMPC, which avoids divergence through pure imitation but discards the Q-function, and against the concurrent TDM(PC)², which also aligns policy with planner but uses a reverse-KL (TD3+BC-style) objective. BOOM's key conceptual departure is the use of a likelihood-free forward-KL alignment loss combined with soft Q-weighting, drawing from AWAC-style critic-guided weighting rather than the TD3+BC formulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first clean theoretical treatment (return-gap and Q-value-gap bounds) for why aligning a parametric policy with a non-parametric planner mitigates actor divergence in off-policy MBRL, and demonstrates that this alignment yields substantial gains (+60% over BMPC, +48% over DreamerV3-10M) on high-dimensional locomotion benchmarks where prior methods are unstable. Limitations include the computational overhead of online planning during data collection and reliance on a reasonably accurate dynamics model over long rollouts. Open questions the authors highlight include adaptive coupling of the max-Q and alignment objectives, extending to more expressive policy classes (e.g., diffusion models), and principled automatic tuning of the entropy coefficient given the non-trivial interplay between planner exploration entropy and policy entropy.
