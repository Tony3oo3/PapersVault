## Problem

Energy-based iterative reasoning formalizes reasoning as energy minimization, but existing methods (IREM, IRED) rely on expensive gradient descent at both training and inference time, creating computational bottlenecks that hinder practical deployment. Moreover, gradient-based routines offer no theoretical guarantee of reaching a locally minimal energy state, leaving the natural termination criterion (stopping at a local minimum) without a convergence guarantee. This paper addresses both limitations by replacing gradient descent with a difference-of-convex (DC) optimization algorithm that converges in finitely many steps to a certified local minimum.

## Input / Output

The method takes a problem encoding y ∈ R^m (e.g., a 20×20 matrix for inversion or QR decomposition, a 20-dimensional vector for parity, or a CLS token embedding from a fine-tuned DistilBERT for symptom-to-diagnosis) and produces a solution x ∈ R^n (the corresponding inverse matrix, completed matrix, parity scalar, QR factors, matrix square, or diagnosis embedding). The energy function E_θ(x,y) is a single hidden layer ReLU network in x whose parameters (α, W, b, ρ, ξ, ω) are themselves single hidden layer neural networks in y, so the model learns a family of energy landscapes indexed by the input. Under the paper's key Assumption 1 (all output weights α_i ≤ 0), each DCA step reduces to the closed-form update x_{k+1} = (1/ρ)(Σ|α_i|w_i·H(⟨w_i,x_k⟩+b_i) + ξ), eliminating the need for any QP solver at inference.

## Method

The core contribution is a DC decomposition of the energy function E_θ(x) = g(x) − h(x) where g is strongly convex (quadratic plus positive ReLU terms) and h is polyhedral convex (negative ReLU terms plus a linear term), enabling a tailored DCA routine that converges in finitely many iterations to a DC critical point that is guaranteed to be a local minimum. Under Assumption 1 (α_i ≤ 0 for all i), the convex subproblem in each DCA step admits the analytical solution x_{k+1} = (1/ρ)v, making the entire inference loop a simple iterative map with no optimization solver, no auto-differentiation, and a natural stopping criterion (‖x_{k+1} − x_k‖ < tol). Training proceeds via supervised regression (EMP) on problem-solution pairs using Adam with batched DCA (batch size 512, N_x = 8 hidden units in x, N_y = 4000 in y), and the paper proves that the resulting energy function approximates all convexly-regular continuous functions in x arbitrarily well, with universal approximation in the univariate case. Empirically, DCAReasoner matches or beats IREM and IRED on five continuous algorithmic reasoning benchmarks while being 3–27× faster at inference, and it learns meaningful energy landscapes in token embedding space for a medical symptom-to-diagnosis task.

## Results

On five continuous algorithmic reasoning benchmarks (Matrix Inverse, Matrix Completion, Parity, QR Decomposition, Matrix Multiplication), DCAReasoner achieves state-of-the-art or superior MSE while being 14–27× faster at inference than IRED and 3–18× faster than IREM. The most dramatic improvement is on the Parity task, where DCAReasoner reduces MSE from 0.4859 (IRED) and 0.2504 (IREM) to 0.0301, a ~10× reduction. On the token embedding space experiment (symptom-to-diagnosis), DCAReasoner achieves the lowest MSE (0.008 vs. 0.012 for IREM and 0.027 for IRED) at the same 96% accuracy, while running 3× faster than IREM and 13× faster than IRED.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares DCAReasoner against two state-of-the-art energy-based iterative reasoning baselines: IREM (Du et al., ICML 2022), which approximates argmin via fixed gradient steps, and IRED (Du et al., ICML 2024), which uses energy diffusion with noise-corrupted gradient supervision. On Matrix Inverse and Matrix Completion, DCAReasoner is essentially on par with both baselines in MSE (differences within ±0.001), but is 13–19× faster at inference. On Parity, DCAReasoner dramatically outperforms both baselines, achieving MSE 0.0301 versus 0.4859 (IRED) and 0.2504 (IREM) — a roughly 10× and 8× improvement respectively. On QR Decomposition and Matrix Multiplication, DCAReasoner also wins on MSE (e.g., 0.1438 vs. 0.2175 for IRED and 0.1521 for IREM on QR at same difficulty; 0.0480 vs. 0.0919/0.0903 on Matrix Multiplication), with the gap widening at harder difficulty. Inference time advantages are consistent across all tasks: DCAReasoner completes inference in 0.6–2.6 seconds per batch versus 8.6–48.2 seconds for IRED and 1.9–36.3 seconds for IREM.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the energy-based iterative reasoning framework of Du et al. [20, 21] (IREM and IRED), which formalize reasoning as energy minimization but rely on gradient descent at both training and inference time. The key departure is replacing gradient descent with a difference-of-convex (DCA) optimization routine [4, 45], enabled by a specifically designed ReLU-based energy function that admits a DC decomposition. Under an additional sign constraint on the output weights (Assumption 1), each DCA step reduces to a closed-form expression, eliminating the need for iterative solvers or differentiable QP layers. The approximation analysis draws on weakly-convex function theory [43] and input-convex neural network results [3, 23] to establish when the restricted energy function retains sufficient expressivity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 14–27× inference speedup over IRED and the finite convergence guarantee (typically <10 DCA iterations for a batch of 512) make energy-based iterative reasoning practically viable for settings where gradient descent at test time is prohibitive, such as high-dimensional token embedding spaces. The natural termination criterion and local-minimality guarantee address a fundamental weakness of prior energy-based reasoning methods. Open questions include whether the approach can incorporate external memory for multi-step reasoning, how it scales to truly discrete tasks (only preliminary Sudoku results are provided), and whether the token-embedding-space demonstration can be extended into a full energy-guided text generation pipeline for LLMs.
