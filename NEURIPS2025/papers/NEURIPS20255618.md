## Problem

Accurately quantifying the privacy budget of decentralized federated learning algorithms is difficult because complex components—random-walk communication, local SGD iterations, and correlated noise—interact in ways that existing Rényi DP or (ε,δ)-DP accounting methods capture only loosely. This paper asks whether the f-differential privacy framework, with its lossless composition via trade-off functions, can yield consistently tighter privacy-utility trade-offs for decentralized FL protocols.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255618/cropped_page8_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a connected communication graph encoded as a Markov transition matrix W, per-user local datasets, noise variance parameters (σ for independent noise, σ_cor for correlated noise), and iteration parameters (total rounds T, local steps K). It outputs a pairwise trade-off function f_ij between every user pair (i,j), from which (ε,δ)-DP guarantees are extracted numerically via the Privacy Loss Random Variable. Unusually, the privacy guarantee is not a single global ε but a matrix of pairwise budgets that depend on the hitting-time distribution between nodes, and the record-level variant additionally requires the per-user sample size m_i to compute the subsampling operator C_{b/m_i}.

## Method

The paper introduces two f-DP notions tailored to decentralized settings. PN-f-DP (for random-walk DP-SGD) models the model snapshot observed by user j as a mixture over hitting-time distributions w_ij^t, then lower-bounds the mixture trade-off function via joint concavity, composing per-step Gaussian trade-offs that incorporate privacy amplification by iteration (via shifted interpolation for strongly convex losses) and by subsampling (via the C_p operator for record-level privacy). The total number of visits to j is bounded using a Hoeffding-type inequality for Markov chains with spectral-gap-dependent exponential decay. Sec-f-LDP (for DecoR-style correlated noise) characterizes the per-round mechanism as a multivariate Gaussian trade-off function whose parameter μ is derived from the spectral decomposition of the graph Laplacian plus independent noise covariance, yielding a closed-form μ that depends on the second eigenvalue λ₂(L) and the collusion level q. Both frameworks convert to (ε,δ)-DP numerically, and experiments on hypercube, expander, complete, ring, and torus graphs show consistently smaller ε than PN-RDP baselines at the same δ.

## Results

On MNIST classification (δ=10⁻⁵), the proposed PN-f-DP accounting achieves test accuracy of 0.891 (Complete, ε=10), 0.885 (Complete, ε=8), 0.869 (Complete, ε=5), 0.854 (Expander, ε=10), 0.840 (Expander, ε=8), and 0.792 (Expander, ε=5), consistently outperforming both RDP and RDP-with-hitting-time baselines. The largest gap appears on the Expander graph at ε=5, where f-DP yields 0.792 versus 0.647 for standard RDP (+14.5 pp) and 0.706 for RDP(HT) (+8.6 pp). In the correlated-noise (DecoR) setting on the a9a dataset, the Sec-f-LDP method achieves a strictly better privacy-utility trade-off than both SecLDP and Local DP across ring and 2D torus topologies. For pure privacy accounting on a 32-node hypercube, PN-f-DP produces noticeably smaller pairwise ε values (stronger privacy) than PN-RDP for nearly all node pairs at δ=10⁻⁵.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two primary baselines. Against PN-RDP (Cyffers et al., ICML 2024), the proposed PN-f-DP method wins on every configuration in Table 1: on the Expander graph at ε=5, accuracy improves from 0.647 to 0.792 (+14.5 pp), and even on the Complete graph at ε=10 it improves from 0.867 to 0.891 (+2.4 pp). A refined RDP baseline using exact hitting times (RDP-HT) narrows the gap but still loses to f-DP in all six settings (e.g., 0.706 vs. 0.792 on Expander, ε=5). In the correlated-noise experiments (Figure 3, a9a dataset, n=16 users on ring and 2D torus), the Sec-f-LDP method outperforms both SecLDP (Allouah et al., ICML 2024) and standard Local DP across all tested privacy budgets ε∈{3,5,7,10}, achieving lower optimality gaps at every iteration. The paper also notes that converting the f-DP guarantees back into RDP form still yields tighter bounds than the original RDP analysis, confirming the improvement is not merely a conversion artifact.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the f-differential privacy framework (Dong, Roth, Su 2022) to two decentralized FL privacy notions that were previously analyzed only under Rényi DP: Pairwise Network DP (Cyffers et al. 2024) and Secret-based Local DP (Allouah et al. 2024). The key technical departure is replacing RDP composition with f-DP trade-off function composition, leveraging joint concavity for mixture mechanisms and a hitting-time-based weighting scheme (rather than matrix powers) to capture random-walk communication dynamics more precisely. Privacy amplification by iteration in the f-DP setting (Bok, Su, Altschuler 2024) is also incorporated to handle local SGD steps, yielding tighter per-visit guarantees that compose across multiple visits via Markov chain concentration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Tighter privacy accounting directly translates to less noise injection for a target (ε,δ) guarantee, improving model utility in decentralized FL without weakening privacy. The framework is modular and generalizes to dynamic graphs, non-uniform participation, and other decentralized protocols (gossip, push-sum), making it a practical tool for system designers. However, the empirical validation is limited to small-scale experiments (n ≤ 2048 users, simple architectures), and the paper does not establish information-theoretic lower bounds showing the f-DP bounds are tight. Open questions include whether the hitting-time advantage persists in highly heterogeneous or time-varying topologies, and how the framework scales to the thousands of devices typical in real FL deployments.
