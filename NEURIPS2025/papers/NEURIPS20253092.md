## Problem

Personalized diffusion models (e.g., DreamBooth) enable unauthorized generation of specific individuals' images, raising serious privacy concerns. Existing defenses rely on data-poisoning—adding adversarial perturbations to images—which requires the impractical assumption that all images can be perturbed, is easily bypassed by even a single clean image or simple transformations, and conflicts with regulations like GDPR that mandate provider-side protection. This paper asks whether protection can be achieved by directly modifying the diffusion model's parameters rather than its input data.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained Stable Diffusion model (1.5 or 2.1, 512×512 resolution), a small set of 4–6 clean images of the target subject (e.g., a person or dog), and a generic class-level text prompt (e.g., 'a photo of person') used to synthesize positive reference images. It produces a safeguarded diffusion model with updated parameters that resist personalization of the target subject while retaining the ability to generate high-quality images and personalize other, non-targeted subjects. The full protection procedure requires approximately 9 GPU hours on a single NVIDIA RTX A6000.

## Method

APDM introduces two key components. First, a theoretical analysis (Theorem 1) proves that naively applying the adversarial loss −L_simple^per + L_ppl to model parameters cannot converge, because the gradients of the two terms necessarily point in conflicting directions. To resolve this, the paper proposes Direct Protective Optimization (DPO), a preference-based loss inspired by Diffusion-DPO that uses paired positive (generic) and negative (target subject) images within a Bradley-Terry framework to explicitly guide the model toward suppressing the target identity while preserving generic generation. Second, Learning to Protect (L2P) is a dual-path optimization scheme that, at each protection step, simulates N_per steps of future personalization on a copy of the current model, accumulates the protection gradients across all intermediate personalized states, and uses their sum to update the actual model—making the defense trajectory-aware and robust to the iterative nature of personalization.

## Results

On the protection task, APDM achieves a DINO score of 0.1167 (avg.) and BRISQUE of 50.50 (avg.) across 'person' and 'dog' subjects, dramatically outperforming the best data-poisoning baseline (PAP: DINO 0.5838, BRISQUE 26.41) even under the most favorable 'all perturbed' setting. For preservation, APDM maintains FID of 28.85 vs. 25.98 for the original Stable Diffusion, with CLIP score 0.2853 vs. 0.2878, indicating minimal degradation in general generation quality. APDM also preserves personalization ability for non-targeted subjects (e.g., 'sneaker' DINO 0.7573, 'glasses' DINO 0.7198), confirming it selectively removes only the targeted identity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

APDM outperforms all four data-poisoning baselines (AdvDM, Anti-DreamBooth, SimAC, PAP) by a wide margin on both DINO and BRISQUE metrics, even when those baselines operate under their most favorable 'all perturbed' condition (0 clean images). For example, on the 'person' subject, APDM reduces DINO to 0.1375 versus 0.4448–0.6556 for the baselines, while increasing BRISQUE to 40.25 versus 19.52–26.90. The advantage is even more pronounced on 'dog' (DINO 0.0959 vs. 0.4106–0.5120; BRISQUE 60.74 vs. 28.58–30.23). Against the unprotected DreamBooth reference (DINO 0.6994/0.6056), APDM reduces subject similarity by over 80%, confirming effective identity erasure. APDM also generalizes to Custom Diffusion personalization (DINO 0.2158/0.3202 vs. 0.5320/0.5460 for unprotected Custom Diffusion) and to Stable Diffusion 2.1 (DINO 0.2739/0.2178 vs. 0.5773/0.5293 for DreamBooth on SD 2.1), demonstrating cross-method and cross-version robustness.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the established line of data-poisoning anti-personalization methods (AdvDM, Anti-DreamBooth, SimAC, PAP) that add adversarial perturbations to input images, instead shifting the protection target to the diffusion model's parameters directly. The proposed DPO loss is adapted from Diffusion-DPO and the Bradley-Terry preference framework, repurposing a generative alignment objective into a protective one. The L2P dual-path optimization draws conceptual parallels to meta-learning and adversarial training (simulating an attacker's trajectory), but applies them to the specific iterative fine-tuning dynamics of personalization. The theoretical analysis builds on standard first-order optimality conditions and Taylor expansion to show that the natural extension of image-level PGD losses to model parameters is fundamentally non-convergent.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By moving protection from the user's data to the model itself, APDM addresses a critical practical gap: data-poisoning methods require exhaustive perturbation of all images (including unintentionally captured ones) and conflict with GDPR's assignment of privacy obligations to service providers. The convergence-failure theorem provides a clear theoretical justification for why prior approaches cannot be naively ported to the model level, guiding future work toward preference-based or trajectory-aware objectives. Open questions include scaling to multi-subject protection, continual safeguarding as new subjects are added, and whether the ~9 GPU-hour cost per subject is feasible for large-scale deployment. The method's dependence on a small set of subject images (4–6) for protection setup remains a practical constraint.
