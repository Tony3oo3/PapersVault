## Problem

Diffusion LLMs predict blocks of tokens in parallel rather than sequentially, making existing constrained decoding algorithms—designed for autoregressive models—unable to provably enforce formal constraints such as regular expressions. This leaves diffusion LLMs unreliable for structured-output tasks like JSON generation or symbolic math, where syntactic correctness is essential. Additionally, greedy constrained decoding (the standard approach for autoregressive models) distorts the model's true output distribution, so any solution for diffusion LLMs must also preserve distributional fidelity.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt, a user-specified regular expression (converted to a token-level DFA), and the per-position token probability vectors produced by the diffusion model's transformer step. It operates on a block of d tokens initialized as mask tokens (⊥), and at each diffusion step the DP must account for the fact that masked positions introduce non-deterministic (NFA-like) transitions in the DFA. The output is a single block of d tokens guaranteed to be a valid prefix of some string in the regular language, selected to maximize joint probability under the model's distribution. In the semi-autoregressive setting, the DFA state carried over from the previous block initializes the next block's DP, ensuring cross-block constraint satisfaction.

## Method

DINGO formulates constrained decoding as a maximum-probability path problem over a token-level DFA and solves it with a Viterbi-style dynamic program. A key design choice is the treatment of the mask token ⊥: its transition function δ⊥(q) returns the set of all states reachable by any single non-mask token, making the automaton NFA-like and requiring the DP to track sets of reachable states. At each position i in the block, the DP maintains W[i,q] (max probability to reach state q) and a parent pointer Pr[i,q]; the transition cost V_{i+1}(q,q') is the maximum token probability over all tokens that can move from q′ to q. After processing all d positions, DINGO selects the live state with the highest W[d,q] and backtracks to reconstruct the optimal token sequence. The approach is proven correct (output is always a valid prefix) and optimal (maximizes probability among all valid strings), with per-block complexity O(d·|Q|·(|Q|+|V|)).

## Results

On the JSON-Mode-Eval benchmark, DINGO achieves 100% schema validation and syntactic accuracy across all four diffusion LLMs (LLaDA-8B-B/I, Dream-B/I-7B), compared to 15–87% for unconstrained generation and 23–80% for Greedy Constrained. On GSM-Symbolic, DINGO improves functional correctness by up to 13 percentage points over unconstrained generation (e.g., 32% vs. 19% for LLaDA-8B-I) and achieves 100% syntactic parse accuracy, while baselines range from 33–98%. The overhead is minimal: DINGO adds less than 0.2 seconds per generation compared to unconstrained inference. The abstract's headline 'up to 68% improvement' corresponds to Dream-B-7B on JSON, where DINGO reaches 100% versus 32% for the Best-of-Greedy+Unconstrained baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares DINGO against two autoregressive constrained decoding approaches—Willard & Louf (2023) and Ugare et al. (2024b)—both implemented as a single 'Greedy Constrained' baseline that applies per-position binary token masking based on the DFA. DINGO decisively outperforms Greedy Constrained on JSON generation: it achieves 100% accuracy on all four models, while Greedy Constrained ranges from 23% (Dream-B-7B) to 80% (LLaDA-8B-B), a gap of up to 77 percentage points. On GSM-Symbolic, DINGO also wins on functional accuracy (e.g., 32% vs. 27% for LLaDA-8B-I; 36% vs. 34% for Dream-I-7B) and achieves 100% parse accuracy versus 41–98% for Greedy Constrained. The paper also compares against a 'Best of Greedy+Unconstrained' oracle that picks the better of the two per problem; DINGO still matches or exceeds this oracle on JSON (100% vs. 88–99%) while being deterministic and requiring no extra sampling. The authors attribute Greedy Constrained's inferiority to distribution distortion from greedy per-position pruning, which can only guarantee a valid prefix rather than a fully parsable output.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the well-established constrained decoding paradigm for autoregressive LLMs (Willard & Louf 2023, Ugare et al. 2024b, Koo et al. 2024) to the fundamentally different parallel-block generation setting of diffusion LLMs. The core algorithmic insight is to adapt the Viterbi-style maximum-probability-path dynamic programming (Forney 1973) to a token-level DFA augmented with a non-deterministic mask transition, handling the fact that diffusion steps unmask tokens at arbitrary positions without left-to-right dependency. It departs from concurrent work by Cardei et al. (2025), which uses a differentiable surrogate model without convergence guarantees, and from Mündler et al. (2025), which applies greedy left-to-right pruning to diffusion LLMs at the cost of optimality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DINGO fills a critical gap: diffusion LLMs (LLaDA, Dream-7B) are gaining traction for efficient generation, but without provable constraint enforcement they remain unreliable for structured-output applications like JSON generation and symbolic math. The 100% syntactic accuracy with only ~2% overhead over unconstrained inference makes this practically deployable. Key limitations include restriction to regular expressions (not CFGs), per-block optimality that may not compose across blocks in semi-autoregressive settings, and inability to handle non-formal constraints like toxicity. Extending the DP framework to context-free grammars and multi-block global optimality are natural next steps.
