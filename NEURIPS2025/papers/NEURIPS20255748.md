## Problem

Building a single Tabular Foundation Model that generalizes across the enormous heterogeneity of real-world tabular datasets without per-dataset fine-tuning remains an open challenge. Existing tabular-specific ICL models rely almost entirely on synthetic data generators, and it is unclear whether real data contains sufficient signal for effective pre-training. Additionally, whether tabular foundation models exhibit predictable scaling laws analogous to LLMs had not been established.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a tabular training set (N rows × F features) and a set of query rows, with features standardized and padded or PCA-reduced to a fixed F_max dimension. At inference, a retrieval step (FAISS nearest-neighbor search) selects the top-K most similar training rows as context, so the effective input is a context block plus query rows. Output is class probabilities over up to C_max classes or a scalar regression value; datasets exceeding C_max classes are handled via base-C_max digit decomposition, and those exceeding F_max features are compressed via PCA.

## Method

TabDPT uses a row-based transformer encoder where each table row is a single token (no positional encoding, giving row-order invariance), with a shared backbone and separate MLP heads for classification and regression. Pre-training combines self-supervised column masking—randomly selecting a column as the target and shuffling/dropping remaining columns to create combinatorial task diversity—with retrieval-based context construction, where only the top-K feature-space-nearest rows form the context during both training and inference. The key empirical finding is that pre-training on 123 real OpenML datasets (32M rows, 2B cells) with SSL consistently outperforms training on synthetic data generators, yielding faster convergence and better downstream accuracy. The model follows joint power-law scaling (α=0.42 for parameters, β=0.39 for data cells) across 33K–78M parameter variants, demonstrating that larger models and more real data produce predictable, monotonic gains.

## Results

On the OpenML-CC18 classification benchmark (72 datasets), TabDPT achieves 0.933 AUC and 0.884 accuracy, outperforming TabPFN v2 (0.932 AUC, 0.872 accuracy) and all per-dataset-tuned baselines. On the OpenML-CTR23 regression benchmark (35 datasets), TabDPT reaches 0.837 correlation and 0.742 R², again edging out TabPFN v2 (0.835, 0.740). Even without access to the validation set (TabDPT no-val.), the model retains 0.928 AUC / 0.874 accuracy on CC18, remaining competitive with methods that perform 30 rounds of hyperparameter search. In the 10-shot few-shot setting, TabDPT (semi) achieves 77.56% average accuracy across seven CC18 datasets, surpassing the leading few-shot method STUNT (76.76%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TabDPT achieves the best score on all four primary metrics (CC18 AUC, CC18 Accuracy, CTR23 Correlation, CTR23 R²), with the most decisive margin over TabPFN v2 on CC18 accuracy (+1.2 pp: 0.884 vs 0.872) and a consistent but smaller lead on AUC and regression metrics. Against per-dataset-tuned tree ensembles (XGBoost, LightGBM, CatBoost), TabDPT gains 1.5–2.2 pp in CC18 accuracy and 0.03–0.04 in CTR23 R², while requiring no hyperparameter search at inference. Among deep-learning baselines, TabDPT outperforms TabM (0.878 acc.) and TabR (0.874 acc.) on CC18 accuracy by 0.6–1.0 pp, and leads on CTR23 R² by 0.01–0.03. The LLM-based Tabula-8B (8B parameters, 32-shot) is not competitive with any tabular-specific method on the win-rate matrix, confirming that current LLM tokenization is ill-suited for tabular ICL. In the 10-shot few-shot regime, TabDPT (semi) beats STUNT on 5 of 7 datasets and in average accuracy (77.56% vs 76.76%), while requiring only forward passes rather than per-task training.

## Relation to Prior Work

TabDPT extends the row-based transformer architecture of TabPFN (Hollmann et al.) for in-context tabular learning, but departs fundamentally in its pre-training strategy: instead of relying on synthetic data generators (as TabPFN v1/v2 and TabICL do), it combines self-supervised column-masking with retrieval-based context construction on real OpenML datasets. It also builds on the SSL-for-tabular-data line (STUNT, VIME, MET) but is the first to achieve cross-task generalization without per-dataset fine-tuning or hyperparameter tuning. The scaling-law analysis follows the NLP methodology of Hoffmann et al. and Besiroglu et al., applied here for the first time to tabular foundation models trained on real data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstration that real data paired with SSL outperforms carefully engineered synthetic generators challenges the prevailing assumption in the TFM community that synthetic data is necessary or superior for pre-training, opening a path toward leveraging the vast existing corpus of public tabular datasets. The confirmed power-law scaling (α=0.42, β=0.39) suggests tabular foundation models can follow the same compute-scaling trajectory as LLMs, making large-scale TFMs a realistic research target. Key limitations remain: TabDPT handles only rectangular i.i.d. tables (no temporal, hierarchical, or text features), and the modest margin over TabPFN v2 on some metrics means the real-data advantage may narrow as synthetic generators improve. The finding that an 8B-parameter LLM-based model (Tabula-8B) is uncompetitive with a 78M-parameter tabular-specific model reinforces that domain-specific architecture and data curation matter more than raw parameter count for tabular tasks.
