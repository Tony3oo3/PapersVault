## Problem

Existing estimation-regret trade-off results in online experimental design assume the SUTVA condition (no network interference), while prior network-interference bandit work focuses solely on regret minimization without statistical inference guarantees. No unified framework simultaneously handles network interference and characterizes the fundamental Pareto-optimal trade-off between causal effect estimation accuracy and cumulative regret, and the exponential action space K^N under interference renders direct optimization infeasible.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-specified network adjacency matrix H ∈ {0,1}^{N×N}, a discrete exposure mapping function S: U × K^U × {0,1}^{N×N} → U_s with |U_s| = d_s ≪ K^N, a cluster partition C of the N units, and sequential per-unit reward observations r_{i,t}(A_t) ∈ [0,1] with sub-Gaussian noise. It outputs a sequential policy π selecting an exposure super arm S_t ∈ U_E each round (where |U_E| ≤ d_s^C), along with adaptive ATE estimators Δ̂^{(i,j)} between all pairs of exposure super arms. The key structural constraint is that the policy decomposes as P(A_t|H_{t-1}) = P(A_t|S_t)·P(S_t|H_{t-1}), where the first factor is a fixed sampling rule over original arms compatible with the chosen exposure state, and the second factor is the learning component.

## Method

The paper introduces the MAB-N framework, which reduces the intractable K^N super-arm space to a polynomial-size exposure super-arm space U_E by applying a statistical exposure mapping S and cluster-wise switchback constraints, then establishes a minimax lower bound √R_ν(T,π)·e_ν(T,Δ̂) = Ω(√|U_E|) via a two-instance information-theoretic construction using Rademacher-distributed rewards and KL divergence bounds. To match this bound, the UCB-TSN algorithm operates in two phases: a uniform round-robin exploration phase of T₁ ≥ √(|U_E|·T) rounds that yields ATE estimation error Õ(√(|U_E|/T₁)), followed by a standard UCB phase that achieves regret Õ(√(|U_E|·T) + T₁). The combination guarantees the Pareto-optimal product Õ(√|U_E|), and the framework is extended to adversarial settings via an EXP3-TSN variant that preserves the same trade-off structure under time-varying adversarial noise f_t.

## Results

The paper establishes a minimax lower bound of Ω(√|U_E|) for the product √Regret × Estimation Error (Theorem 1), and shows UCB-TSN achieves matching upper bounds: ATE estimation error Õ(√(|U_E|/T₁)) (Theorem 3) and cumulative regret Õ(√(|U_E|·T) + T₁) (Theorem 4), yielding Pareto-optimality e·√R = Õ(√|U_E|) (Corollary 1). In the degenerated single-unit case (N=1, |U_E|=K), UCB-TSN's regret bound Õ(K + T₁) strictly improves over Simchi-Levi and Wang's EXP3EG bound Õ(K⁵ + T^(1−α)), and its estimation error Õ(√(K/T₁)) strictly improves over their Õ(K²·T^−(1−α)/2). Experimental validation on a 101-unit star network (6 clusters, binary arms) confirms UCB-TSN (T₁=√(|U_E|T)) achieves the lowest cumulative regret while also reducing ATE estimation error variance compared to the no-exploration baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary theoretical baseline is Simchi-Levi and Wang [2024] (EXP3EG), the state-of-the-art estimation-regret trade-off result without network interference. In the degenerated N=1 case, UCB-TSN strictly dominates on both objectives: its regret bound Õ(K + T₁) is tighter than EXP3EG's Õ(K⁵ + T^(1−α)) (removing the K⁵ term and the ATE gap assumption Δ=Θ(1)), and its estimation error Õ(√(K/T₁)) is tighter than Õ(K²·T^−(1−α)/2) (removing the K² factor). This strict improvement is visualized in Figure 1(b), where the baseline's Pareto front is strictly dominated by the proposed method's front with respect to arm space K. In the empirical comparison (Appendix E, 101-unit network), UCB-TSN with T₁=√(|U_E|T) achieves the lowest cumulative regret (comparable to the no-exploration Standard baseline) while simultaneously reducing the median and interquartile range of ATE estimation error relative to Standard, which suffers from wider error spread and more outliers due to insufficient exploration of sub-optimal arms. The Uniform baseline (T₁=T) achieves the best estimation error but at the cost of the highest cumulative regret, confirming the trade-off structure predicted by the theory.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate research threads: (i) multi-armed bandits under network interference (Agarwal et al. 2024, Jia et al. 2024), which focus solely on regret minimization, and (ii) the estimation-regret Pareto trade-off in online experimental design (Simchi-Levi and Wang 2024, Duan et al. 2024), which operates under the SUTVA assumption. The key conceptual bridge is the exposure mapping from causal inference (Leung 2022a, Aronow and Samii 2017), which the authors repurpose to define a reduced arm space (MAB-N) that subsumes prior interference-based bandit formulations as special cases. The UCB-TSN algorithm extends the classical UCB framework (Auer et al. 2002) with a two-stage design—uniform exploration followed by UCB exploitation—tailored to the exposure-based reward structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first Pareto-optimal characterization of the estimation-regret trade-off under network interference, filling a gap where prior work either ignored interference or ignored estimation. The matching lower and upper bounds (Ω(√|U_E|) and Õ(√|U_E|)) give practitioners a concrete 'free lunch' for interactive inference about achievable trade-offs. Open questions include extending to fully adversarial networked bandits, dynamic/time-varying network topologies, and connections to multi-agent reinforcement learning and online causal inference. A practical limitation is that the exposure mapping must be pre-specified and Condition 1 (|U_E| ≤ T) must hold, which may be restrictive in very short-horizon experiments.
