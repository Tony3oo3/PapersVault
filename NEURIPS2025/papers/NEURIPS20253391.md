## Problem

Prior language-guided UAV research has centered on high-level VLN tasks—long-horizon path planning and goal-directed navigation—while assuming that low-level, short-range flight control is already reliable. This assumption breaks for UAVs, whose 3D flight involves high degrees of freedom and rapidly changing perspectives, making language-guided fine-grained trajectory control a critical yet underexplored gap. The paper formalizes the Flying-on-a-Word (Flow) task, where UAVs execute short-range, reactive maneuvers in response to atomic language instructions, and introduces the first real-world benchmark for this setting.

## Input / Output

At each time step the agent receives a natural language instruction, the UAV's 6-DoF state expressed in a local Cartesian frame (position relative to the trajectory start plus relative roll/pitch/yaw), and an egocentric FPV frame sampled at 5 Hz. The policy outputs a 6-DoF pose action; Pi-0-UAV predicts a chunk of 10 future poses via flow matching, while OpenVLA-UAV discretizes the action space into 256 tokens. Instructions are divided into primitive motion commands (takeoff, translation, rotation, diving) and object-interactive commands (orbiting, passing through, hovering), each available in both a fixed-form and an LLM-diversified open-vocabulary variant.

## Method

UAV-Flow comprises a formal Flow task definition, a 30,692-trajectory real-world dataset collected by certified pilots (800+ hours each) across three campuses using three DJI drones with RTK GPS and 4K cameras, and a complementary 10,109-trajectory simulation dataset (UAV-Flow-Sim) built in UnrealCV with a hybrid manual-plus-rule-based collection strategy. For real-world deployment, a ground-drone collaborative framework streams FPV video and state over RTSP/MAVROS to a ground station for inference, employing a globally-aligned continuous motion scheme with a look-ahead mechanism that filters already-traversed waypoints to handle perception-action latency. The benchmark systematically adapts VLN models (Seq2Seq-UAV, CMA-UAV, Travel-UAV) and VLA models (OpenVLA-UAV, Pi-0-UAV) to the Flow setting by converting discrete action outputs to continuous 6-DoF pose regression and restructuring inputs to fuse UAV state with language instructions. Closed-loop simulation evaluation uses Success Rate (manual semantic check) and NDTW (6D position-orientation trajectory similarity), and results show VLA models consistently outperform VLN baselines in fine-grained control, with Pi-0-UAV achieving the first real-world VLA deployment for language-conditioned UAV flight.

## Results

The paper evaluates five adapted models (Seq2Seq-UAV, CMA-UAV, Travel-UAV, OpenVLA-UAV, Pi-0-UAV) on the UAV-Flow-Sim test set of 273 trajectories across 10 Flow task types, reporting Success Rate (SR) and Normalized Dynamic Time Warping (NDTW). VLA models (OpenVLA-UAV and Pi-0-UAV) consistently outperform all VLN baselines on both metrics, with the advantage most pronounced on object-interactive tasks requiring fine-grained visual grounding. Inference latency ranges from 0.057 s (Seq2Seq-UAV) to 0.289 s (Pi-0-UAV) on a single RTX 4090, with Pi-0-UAV predicting a 10-step action chunk per forward pass. Real-world deployment of Pi-0-UAV via the ground-drone collaborative framework demonstrates smooth, delay-free continuous flight on a physical UAV.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Seq2Seq-UAV and CMA-UAV (adapted from discrete-action VLN) perform worst overall: their RNN-based architectures cause trajectory drift, inability to stop at the correct point, and poor multimodal fusion for pose regression, resulting in low SR and high NDTW across nearly all 10 task types. Travel-UAV, built on LLaMA-VID, improves on primitive motion tasks by generating pose-level outputs from current-frame inputs, but its 17-token visual encoding limits fine-grained spatial grounding, causing it to underperform on object-interactive tasks such as 'turn to face the target.' OpenVLA-UAV achieves the strongest overall SR, excelling in both motion intent and spatial grounding, though its single-frame input occasionally fails to identify precise stopping points near object sides. Pi-0-UAV matches or exceeds OpenVLA-UAV on object-interactive tasks thanks to its multi-frame (reference + current) visual input, but its flow-matching denoising introduces slight trajectory fluctuations and weaker performance on fine-grained distance/angle specifications under limited training data. In summary, VLA models dominate VLN baselines on the Flow benchmark, with OpenVLA-UAV and Pi-0-UAV each holding complementary strengths across the two task categories.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant VLN paradigm for UAVs (AerialVLN, CityNav, Travel), which focuses on long-horizon, discrete-action navigation, by formalizing a complementary 'Flow' task centered on short-range, fine-grained trajectory control grounded in visual context. It bridges two previously separate literatures: classical VLN architectures (Seq2Seq, CMA) and recent VLA models from robotic manipulation (OpenVLA, Pi-0), adapting both to continuous 6-DoF UAV pose prediction. The key conceptual shift is from 'flying far' (planning multi-step paths to distant goals) to 'flying better' (executing precise, reactive maneuvers in response to atomic language commands), which the authors argue is a critical underexplored gap for aerial platforms due to their 3D dynamics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first real-world dataset and deployment pipeline for language-conditioned UAV imitation learning, removing the sim-to-real gap that has limited prior UAV VLN work. The finding that VLA models substantially outperform adapted VLN models on fine-grained control validates the transfer of manipulation-oriented architectures to aerial platforms and suggests a practical path toward intuitive human-drone interaction. Key open questions remain: integrating short-range Flow execution with long-horizon planning, establishing safe and standardized real-world evaluation metrics, and scaling beyond the current campus-scale environments to more complex urban or natural settings.
