## Problem

Dataset bias in visual datasets is pervasive but difficult to identify systematically without costly, fine-grained attribute annotations. Existing approaches either rely on manual labeling or VLM-generated captions that vary in granularity and terminology, making it hard to extract structured, comparable visual concepts at scale. No scalable, automated framework existed for discovering interpretable visual concepts and quantifying their class-specific distributions to expose bias.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (resized to 224×224) paired with class labels from image classification datasets. Internally, it operates on CLIP ViT-L/14 penultimate-layer token embeddings (257 tokens of dimension 1024 per image). Outputs include a concept dictionary of ~3,103 interpretable visual concepts, each assigned a target/context/bias label per class, patch-level spatial attribution masks at 16×16 resolution, image-level scalar activation values, and per-class concept distribution profiles that reveal bias dominance. For robustness evaluation, it produces four-way subgroup accuracy scores (high/low target × high/low bias) across 34 pretrained models.

## Method

ConceptScope trains a Sparse Autoencoder (expansion factor 32, ReLU, L1 sparsity λ=8×10⁻⁵) on CLIP ViT-L/14 intermediate representations to disentangle dense embeddings into a sparse set of monosemantic visual concepts. After filtering uninformative latents (max activation < 0.5 or concept strength > 0.1), each retained concept is interpreted via its top-activating images and GPT-4o-generated descriptions. Concepts are then categorized per class using an alignment score combining necessity (confidence drop when the concept's spatial region is masked) and sufficiency (confidence retained when only that region is kept), computed via CLIP cosine similarity; concepts exceeding mean + α·std are labeled target. Among remaining context concepts, those whose average activation exceeds mean + std are flagged as bias concepts, revealing spurious statistical correlations between classes and co-occurring visual patterns.

## Results

On the concept prediction task across six diverse datasets (objects, textures, backgrounds, facial attributes, emotions, actions), ConceptScope's SAE-based method achieves an average F1 of 0.72 and AUPRC of 0.76, substantially outperforming caption-based baselines LLaVA-NeXT (F1 0.58) and BLIP-2 (F1 0.43). On the bias discovery task (Precision@10), ConceptScope achieves 100% on Waterbirds and CelebA, and 72.9–74.0% on NICO++ (severity 75–95), outperforming DOMINO (24%), FACTS (55–61%), and ViG-Bias (60–66.7%) on NICO++. For segmentation quality on ADE20K, SAE spatial attributions achieve an AUPRC of 0.399, compared to 0.302 for LLaVA-NeXT and 0.098 for BLIP-2.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against caption-based VLM baselines (BLIP-2 and LLaVA-NeXT), ConceptScope's SAE consistently wins on F1 across all six concept prediction datasets, with the largest margins on Waterbirds backgrounds (+0.41 over BLIP-2, +0.21 over LLaVA-NeXT) and CelebA facial attributes (+0.54 over BLIP-2, +0.19 over LLaVA-NeXT); the smallest gap is on Stanford40 actions where LLaVA-NeXT reaches 0.80 vs. SAE's 0.78. On the bias discovery task (Precision@10), ConceptScope ties with FACTS and ViG-Bias at 100% on Waterbirds and CelebA, but clearly outperforms all three on NICO++ (72.9–74.0% vs. 24% for DOMINO, 55–61% for FACTS, and 60–66.7% for ViG-Bias), with the gap widening as bias severity increases. For segmentation on ADE20K, SAE spatial attributions (AUPRC 0.399) beat LLaVA-NeXT attention maps (0.302) by ~33% relative and BLIP-2 (0.098) by ~4x, while also producing masks for all concepts in a single forward pass versus multiple inference passes required by VLMs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes Sparse Autoencoders—originally developed for monosemantic feature discovery in language models (Cunningham et al., 2023) and recently applied to visual adaptation (Lim et al., ICLR 2025)—as a concept extraction tool for dataset-level analysis rather than model interpretability. It departs from VLM captioning pipelines (BLIP-2, LLaVA-NeXT) that rely on free-form natural language and LLM prompt chaining to assign attributes, and from model-behavior-based bias detectors (DOMINO, FACTS, ViG-Bias) that infer bias indirectly through classifier failures. The key conceptual shift is treating SAE latents as a structured, quantifiable concept dictionary that can be systematically categorized (target/context/bias) per class without any manual annotation or predefined attribute lists.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ConceptScope offers a practical, scalable tool for dataset auditing that removes the need for costly fine-grained attribute annotation, making bias characterization feasible for large-scale datasets like ImageNet where biases were previously unannotated. The ability to diagnose model robustness via concept-based subgrouping—without requiring external OOD benchmarks—addresses a real gap in domains where curated shift datasets are unavailable. Limitations include dependence on CLIP's representational coverage (weaker on domain-specific datasets like emotions or textures), coarse 16×16 patch-level segmentation, and the assumption that a single SAE trained on ImageNet generalizes adequately to all target domains. Open questions include whether domain-specific SAEs (e.g., medical imaging) close the performance gap, and whether the framework extends to video or 3D data.
