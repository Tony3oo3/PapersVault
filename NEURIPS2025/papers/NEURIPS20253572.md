## Problem

Stochastic Natural Gradient Variational Inference (NGVI) is widely used for posterior approximation, but its convergence guarantees were previously established only for conjugate models where the variational objective is convex in the KL-induced Bregman geometry. For non-conjugate likelihoods such as logistic or Poisson regression, the objective becomes non-convex and standard smoothness fails globally, leaving no non-asymptotic convergence theory. This paper closes that gap by characterizing the landscape of the non-convex variational objective and proving convergence for a projected NGVI algorithm.

## Input / Output

The method takes observed data pairs (x, y), a standard Gaussian prior p(z), and a non-conjugate likelihood p(D|z) (e.g., logistic or Poisson regression), and produces mean-field Gaussian variational parameters (μ, Σ) along with explicit convergence rate bounds. The algorithm operates in the expectation parameter space (ξ, Ξ) where updates follow the natural gradient direction, and the non-Euclidean projection is implemented as entry-wise clipping of μ and diagonal entries of Σ onto bounded intervals [−U, U] and [1/D, D], making each iteration O(d) in the mean-field case.

## Method

The paper identifies NGVI as stochastic mirror descent over the expectation parameter ω with mirror map A* (the convex conjugate of the log-partition function), where the Bregman divergence coincides with KL divergence. It then derives sufficient conditions under which the negative ELBO is L-smooth relative to A* on compact parameter sets, with L scaling polynomially in dimension and set size, using Bonnet's/Price's gradient identities and Stein's lemma to bound high-order derivatives. Building on this, the authors propose Proj-SNGD, which clips iterates back into the compact domain via the A*-induced non-Euclidean projection, and prove O(1/√T) convergence to a stationary point using the Bregman Forward-Backward Envelope criterion. Under the additional assumption that the log-likelihood is concave in z, the paper uncovers a hidden convexity structure that yields a Polyak-Łojasiewicz inequality, enabling O(1/T) global convergence to the optimum with a two-phase step-size schedule.

## Results

On MNIST logistic regression (n=11,769, d=784, mini-batch 2000, 6000 iterations), non-Euclidean algorithms (Proj-SNGD and SNGD) reach the objective threshold ℓ(ω) ≤ 700 in approximately 1000–2000 iterations across a wide step-size range γ₀ ∈ [0.05, 0.2], while Euclidean baselines (Prox-SGD, Proj-SGD) achieve this only at γ₀ = 0.02. On Poisson regression, Proj-SNGD eliminates the objective increase observed in SNGD at initial iterations (with σ₀²=2) and converges faster while reaching the same optimum. Theoretically, Proj-SNGD achieves O(1/√T) convergence to a stationary point and O(1/T) global convergence under log-concave likelihood, matching the conjugate-case rate of Wu & Gardner (2024) in a strictly more general non-conjugate setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SNGD (Wu & Gardner, ICML 2024), Proj-SNGD wins on numerical stability: with the same initialization σ₀²=2 on Poisson regression, SNGD exhibits objective increases at early iterations for step sizes 0.3 and 0.5, while Proj-SNGD shows monotone decrease from the start. On MNIST, Proj-SNGD slightly outperforms SNGD in convergence speed and is more robust to step-size choice. Against the Euclidean baselines Prox-SGD and Proj-SGD (Domke, ICML 2020), both non-Euclidean methods (Proj-SNGD and SNGD) converge substantially faster with tuned step sizes and tolerate a 2.5–10× wider range of initial step sizes (γ₀ ∈ [0.05, 0.2] vs. only γ₀ = 0.02 for Euclidean methods) before failing to reach the target objective. The key practical advantage is reduced hyperparameter sensitivity: non-Euclidean algorithms require less step-size tuning to achieve competitive performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the convergence analysis of stochastic natural gradient variational inference (SNGD) from the conjugate setting (Wu & Gardner, ICML 2024) to non-conjugate models, where the variational objective becomes non-convex and the well-behaved 1-smoothness/1-strong-convexity structure breaks down. The analysis leverages the non-convex stochastic mirror descent framework of Fatkhullin & He (AISTATS 2024), particularly the Bregman Forward-Backward Envelope convergence criterion, and the hidden convexity framework of Fatkhullin, He & Hu (SIAM J. Optim. 2025) to establish a Polyak-Łojasiewicz inequality. The projected variant (Proj-SNGD) is a direct modification of the original NGVI update (Honkela et al., 2007) that adds non-Euclidean projection onto a compact parameter domain to ensure the relative smoothness and hidden convexity conditions hold.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work fills a fundamental gap in the theory of natural gradient VI by providing the first non-asymptotic convergence guarantees for non-conjugate models, a setting that encompasses most practical Bayesian models (logistic regression, Poisson regression, Bayesian neural networks). The discovery that log-concave likelihoods induce hidden convexity in the variational objective is a structural insight that could inform algorithm design beyond NGVI. Key limitations include restriction to mean-field parameterization, compact domains (global relative smoothness remains open), and a technical boundary assumption (Assumption 2) that is difficult to verify for complex models. Extending the analysis to full-covariance families and removing the boundary assumption are natural next steps.
