## Problem

Finite-time convergence guarantees for fully nonsmooth, nonconvex stochastic optimization on Riemannian manifolds have been entirely absent from the literature. Existing manifold optimization results either provide only asymptotic convergence, restrict to composite objectives (smooth plus convex nonsmooth), or rely on restrictive assumptions like weak convexity. In the Euclidean setting, Goldstein stationarity was recently shown to be the right notion for nonsmooth nonconvex problems, but adapting this framework to manifolds—where subgradients at different points live in different tangent spaces—remained an open challenge.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes either stochastic Riemannian gradient evaluations (first-order) or noisy function value queries at two nearby points (zeroth-order), along with an initial point on the manifold and access to retraction and transport operations. The output is a point w_out selected uniformly at random from the midpoints of K epochs, guaranteed to satisfy E[‖grad f(w_out)‖_δ] ≤ ε, where ‖·‖_δ is the minimum norm over the Riemannian Goldstein δ-subdifferential (the closed convex hull of parallel-transported Clarke subgradients from all points within geodesic distance δ). The sample complexity is O(δ^{-1}ε^{-3}) in both first-order and zeroth-order settings, matching the optimal Euclidean rate.

## Method

RO2NC adapts the O2NC online-to-nonconvex conversion to Riemannian manifolds by structuring optimization as epochs of T inner iterations, where each iteration uses a retraction to maintain feasibility (x_{t+1} = Retr_{x_t}(Δ_t)) and evaluates a stochastic gradient at a random point along the retraction curve. The central technical difficulty is that the online learning action Δ_t and the gradient feedback g_t live in different tangent spaces, so the algorithm must transport them via parallel transport (or, more efficiently, via ambient-space projection). The key analytical innovation is choosing a time-dependent benchmark u_t = P_{S_t}^s(u_0) constructed by parallel transporting a single base vector along the iterate path, which allows the regret decomposition to proceed despite the curved geometry. For the zeroth-order variant (ZO-RO2NC), the gradient estimator samples random vectors in the tangent space rather than on the manifold, avoiding costly volume and surface calculations; the analysis bounds the distance between the estimator's expectation and the Goldstein subdifferential set using curvature-dependent terms, preserving the O(δ^{-1}ε^{-3}) rate without requiring the inclusion property that holds in the Euclidean case.

## Results

RO2NC achieves a sample complexity of O(δ⁻¹ε⁻³) for finding (δ,ε)-Goldstein stationary points of fully nonsmooth, nonconvex stochastic objectives on Riemannian manifolds (Theorem 3.1), matching the optimal rate in the Euclidean setting [13]. The projection-based variant (Theorem 3.3) achieves the same rate without requiring parallel transport, and the zeroth-order version ZO-RO2NC (Theorem 4.2) also attains O(δ⁻¹ε⁻³) using only function value queries. This is the first-ever finite-time guarantee for fully nonsmooth, nonconvex optimization on manifolds, as all prior methods either provide only asymptotic convergence or are restricted to composite (smooth + convex-nonsmooth) objectives.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper positions RO2NC against seven prior methods in Table 1, all of which are either restricted to composite objectives (smooth + convex-nonsmooth) or provide only asymptotic guarantees. The closest competitors are [59] (Proximal Gradient–SPIDER, O(ε⁻³) stochastic) and [16] (Augmented Lagrangian, Õ(ε⁻³·⁵) stochastic), but both handle only composite objectives on the Stiefel or compact manifolds, whereas RO2NC handles fully nonsmooth objectives on general embedded manifolds (Stiefel, Hadamard, Grassmann). The smoothing-based method [50] degrades to O(ε⁻⁵) in the stochastic setting, and R-ADMM [44] requires O(ε⁻⁴) even in the deterministic case. RO2NC matches the optimal Euclidean rate of O(ε⁻³δ⁻¹) established by Cutkosky et al. [13] for the first time in the Riemannian setting, and the zeroth-order variant ZO-RO2NC preserves this rate without gradient access, extending the optimal O(d) dimension-dependence result of Kornowski and Shamir [37] to manifolds.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the O2NC online-to-nonconvex conversion framework (Cutkosky et al., 2023) from Euclidean space to Riemannian manifolds, adapting the Goldstein stationarity criterion (Zhang et al., 2020) to the manifold setting via parallel transport of Clarke subdifferentials. Prior Riemannian nonsmooth optimization work (subgradient methods, proximal gradient, ADMM) either provided only asymptotic guarantees or restricted to composite objectives (smooth + convex nonsmooth); this paper is the first to deliver finite-time guarantees for fully nonsmooth, nonconvex stochastic objectives on manifolds. The zeroth-order extension builds on the Riemannian gradient estimator of Li et al. (2023) and the optimal dimension-dependence result of Kornowski & Shamir (2024), but replaces the computationally expensive manifold-volume-based smoothing with tangent-space sampling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This fills a notable gap in the optimization literature: many practical problems (sparse PCA, orthogonal neural networks, low-rank matrix completion) involve both manifold constraints and fully nonsmooth objectives, yet no finite-time convergence guarantee existed for this combination. The result that the optimal Euclidean rate O(δ⁻¹ε⁻³) is preserved on manifolds—despite curvature-induced distortion in parallel transport and retraction—suggests that the 'curvature tax' is a lower-order effect that does not fundamentally alter complexity. Open questions include whether adaptive algorithms can exploit curvature information to improve constants, whether alternative transport maps or subdifferential notions yield tighter bounds, and how these results extend to non-embedded or non-complete manifolds.
