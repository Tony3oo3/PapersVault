## Problem

Dark matter signals in the ABRACADABRA detector manifest as sinusoidal oscillations buried within ultra-long, non-Gaussian time series noise at 10 MS/s, making traditional signal processing inadequate for extraction. No dedicated dataset or benchmark existed for training and evaluating AI denoising algorithms on real dark matter detector data, nor for translating denoising performance into community-standard physics results (exclusion limits). TIDMAD fills this gap by providing the first dataset and dual-benchmark framework that connects ML denoising directly to publishable dark matter search results.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254460/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The input is a noisy SQUID time series (CH1) sampled at 10 MS/s as 8-bit integers (-128 to 127, scaled by 40/128 to mV), with training data providing a paired clean injected signal (CH2) as ground truth. The science dataset contains only CH1 over 24 hours (833.82 Gigasamples) with no injected signal. Outputs are threefold: a denoised time series, a scalar denoising score (log-transformed SNR calibrated so raw data scores 1.0), and a Brazil band exclusion limit on the axion coupling g_{γγγ} as a function of mass m_a, produced via a frequentist log-likelihood ratio test across 11.1 million mass points from 0.4 neV to 8 neV.

## Method

The paper benchmarks eight denoising algorithms, with five deep learning architectures trained in a supervised signal-recovery task: FC Net (fully-connected autoencoder with MSE loss), WaveNet (dilated causal convolutions with residual and skip connections), PU-Net (U-Net with positional encoding, reformulated as 256-class semantic segmentation with Focal Loss), Transformer (2-layer self-attention with 256-class output), and RNN Seq2Seq (LSTM encoder-decoder with 256-class output). Due to GPU memory constraints, ultra-long time series are segmented into 4 ms chunks (2 ms for Transformer), and frequency splitting trains four specialized models per architecture across distinct frequency bands (except WaveNet, which generalizes across all frequencies). The best performer, FC Net, achieves a denoising score of 6.43 versus 1.0 for raw data, and when applied to the 24-hour science dataset, boosts the dark matter exclusion limit by 1–2 orders of magnitude, approaching the sensitivity of the prior three-month ABRA-10cm Run 3 with only 1% of the integration time.

## Results

On the TIDMAD validation dataset, the FC Net autoencoder achieves the best fine denoising score of 6.43 (coarse: 6.55), a 6.4× improvement over the raw data baseline (score = 1.00). WaveNet follows at 4.99, Transformer at 3.95, PU Net at 3.69, and RNN Seq2Seq at 3.38. All traditional methods (Fourier Averaging: 0.24, Moving Average: 0.86, SG Filter: 0.95) fail to exceed the raw-data baseline, confirming that time-domain averaging erases high-frequency signal content. On the science dataset, the FC Net denoised dark matter limit improves sensitivity by 1–2 orders of magnitude in g_{aγγ} over the raw-data limit, reaching nearly the ABRA-10cm Run 3 level despite using only 1% of the integration time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Fourier Averaging baseline (the current ABRA-10cm status quo [21, 28]), every deep learning model achieves a dramatic improvement: FC Net scores 6.43 vs. 0.24 (27× higher), while even the weakest DL model (RNN Seq2Seq, 3.38) is 14× better. The traditional filters (Moving Average, SG Filter) also underperform the raw-data baseline, confirming they are fundamentally inadequate for this noise regime. On the physics benchmark (dark matter limit), the FC Net denoised ABRA-TIDMAD limit does not surpass the ABRA-10cm Run 3 limit [28] due to reduced geometric coupling and a 24-hour vs. three-month integration window. However, with only 1% of the Run 3 data volume, the denoised TIDMAD limit reaches nearly the same sensitivity and even exceeds Run 3 at small m_a values, demonstrating that AI denoising can compensate for substantially shorter data-taking periods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes architectures from audio generation (WaveNet's dilated causal convolutions), biomedical image segmentation (U-Net with positional encoding redefined as 256-class semantic segmentation), and NLP (Transformer self-attention) for the physics-specific task of denoising ultra-long detector time series. It extends the ABRACADABRA experiment's existing Fourier Averaging baseline [21, 28] by introducing a hardware-injected ground-truth signal that enables supervised training, and by framing the output as a community-standard dark matter exclusion limit rather than a generic signal-to-noise metric. The denoising task itself is well-established in signal processing and ML, but the specific formulation—recovering a sinusoidal dark matter signal from 10 MS/s SQUID readout and translating the result into a Brazil-band limit—is new to the literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TIDMAD is the first dataset and benchmark that directly connects ML denoising performance to a publishable particle-physics result, lowering the barrier for ML researchers to contribute to fundamental science without deep physics expertise. The finding that a simple fully-connected autoencoder (score 6.43) substantially outperforms WaveNet (4.99), Transformer (3.95), and U-Net (3.69) challenges the assumption that more inductive-bias-rich architectures are necessary for this task, though the evidence is limited to a single detector configuration. Open questions include whether these results generalize to other axion experiments (ADMX, HAYSTAC, DMRadio), whether the FC Net advantage persists at lower signal amplitudes (the 10 mV weak-injection data), and whether real-time FPGA deployment of these models is feasible. The paper also notes that a 2× noise reduction yields a 16× effective speedup in data-taking time, suggesting denoising investment may outperform hardware upgrades.
