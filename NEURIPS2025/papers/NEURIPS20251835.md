## Problem

Existing event-based video frame interpolation (E-VFI) methods rely on direct image-level supervision, which propagates motion blur and degradation artifacts from keyframes and ground truth into the learned model, undermining perceptual realism and multi-scene generalization under high-speed motion and occlusion. The paper identifies that semantic-perceptual features extracted by vision foundation models are substantially more robust to such degradation than raw pixel values, yet no prior E-VFI method exploits this property for training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251835/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes two RGB keyframes (I_0, I_1) and the inter-frame event stream E_{0→1}, which is split into two directional subsets and converted into voxel grids V_{0→τ} and V_{1→τ}. It produces a single interpolated frame I_τ at a user-specified timestamp τ ∈ [0,1]. During training, inputs are cropped to 256×256 with random rotation and flipping; the generator is trained on high-quality images filtered by NIQE, decoupling reconstruction quality from the degraded interpolation training data.

## Method

EPA operates in two stages. In stage one, a frozen DINO-pretrained ResNet backbone extracts multi-scale semantic-perceptual features from images; a lightweight Style Adapter fuses low-level cues from an image encoder into these features to compensate for non-salient regions (e.g., sky) that the foundation model neglects; and a normalized-flow reconstruction generator is trained to synthesize high-fidelity images from the adapted features. In stage two, the Bidirectional Event-Guided Alignment (BEGA) module takes the event voxel grids through a shared lightweight encoder and, at three spatial scales, uses deformable convolutions with event-derived offsets to warp and fuse the adapted features from both keyframes toward the ground-truth feature distribution, with SE layers for occlusion-aware refinement. The aligned features are then fed to the frozen reconstruction generator to produce the final interpolated frame. Training uses a hybrid loss combining L2 feature alignment (with auxiliary one-way alignment terms) and perceptual image losses (LPIPS + Laplacian L1), avoiding direct pixel-level supervision on the degraded ground truth.

## Results

On the HSERGB real-world dataset (5-skip), EPA achieves the best scores across all metrics: PSNR 33.84 dB, SSIM 0.872, LPIPS 0.014, FloLPIPS 0.057, and DISTS 0.045. On BS-ERGB (1-skip), EPA leads on all perceptual metrics (LPIPS 0.024, FloLPIPS 0.068, DISTS 0.051) though TLXNet edges ahead on PSNR (29.298 vs. 27.943) and SSIM (0.813 vs. 0.791). On synthetic GOPRO (7-skip), EPA achieves LPIPS 0.006, FloLPIPS 0.021, and DISTS 0.019, substantially outperforming the next-best method (TimeLens at LPIPS 0.009, FloLPIPS 0.033, DISTS 0.031). The method consistently dominates perceptual quality metrics (LPIPS, FloLPIPS, DISTS) across all five evaluation datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EPA outperforms all five baselines on every perceptual metric (LPIPS, FloLPIPS, DISTS) across all evaluated datasets. On HSERGB, EPA leads RIFE by 0.018 in LPIPS (0.014 vs. 0.032) and by 0.038 in DISTS (0.045 vs. 0.083) at 5-skip, while also surpassing the strongest competitor TimeLens by 0.032 in FloLPIPS (0.057 vs. 0.112). On BS-ERGB, TLXNet achieves slightly higher PSNR (29.298 vs. 27.943 at 1-skip) and SSIM (0.813 vs. 0.791), but EPA still wins decisively on all perceptual metrics (LPIPS 0.024 vs. 0.047, FloLPIPS 0.068 vs. 0.088, DISTS 0.051 vs. 0.052). CBMNet and UPR-Net are consistently the weakest on perceptual quality, with LPIPS values roughly 2–4× higher than EPA. The paper notes that despite TLXNet's higher PSNR on BS-ERGB, human evaluation (Fig. 6) confirms EPA produces visually superior results, validating the use of perceptual metrics over pixel-level ones for VFI.

## Relation to Prior Work

EPA departs from the dominant paradigm in event-based VFI (TimeLens, CBMNet, TLXNet) where intermediate frames are synthesized by warping keyframes guided by event-derived optical flow, operating entirely in pixel space with image-level supervision. Instead, it shifts the interpolation problem into the semantic-perceptual feature space of a frozen DINO backbone, using a normalized-flow generator (from Wu et al., CVPR 2024) as the reconstruction head and a novel Bidirectional Event-Guided Alignment module with deformable convolutions to fit intermediate features. The Style Adapter, which injects low-level image details into high-level DINO features via a gating mechanism, is a new architectural component not present in prior E-VFI or VFI pipelines. Conceptually, the paper repurposes the degradation-robustness property of self-supervised vision foundation models—previously exploited in image restoration (Lin et al., 2024)—for the first time as the primary supervisory signal in a frame interpolation task.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and addresses a practical gap: real-world keyframes in high-speed or occluded scenes suffer from motion blur and sensor noise, yet prior E-VFI methods assume pristine inputs, leading to perceptually degraded outputs. By decoupling learning from pixel-level supervision, EPA offers a principled path toward more robust interpolation that generalizes across scenes with varying degradation levels. Open questions include whether the DINO dependency limits applicability to non-natural or synthetic scenes, how the two-stage training pipeline scales to higher resolutions or longer temporal gaps, and whether the feature-space formulation can be extended to other video enhancement tasks (e.g., deblurring, super-resolution) where input quality is similarly unreliable. The observation that PSNR can rank methods inversely to perceptual quality (Fig. 6) also reinforces the need for perceptual metrics in VFI evaluation.
