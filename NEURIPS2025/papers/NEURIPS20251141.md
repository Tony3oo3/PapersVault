## Problem

Over-squashing—the compression of exponentially growing information through graph bottlenecks—has been well characterized in static GNNs but remains unexplored in Spatiotemporal GNNs (STGNNs), where information must propagate across both spatial and temporal axes simultaneously. The temporal dimension introduces a distinct and counterintuitive form of over-squashing that interacts with spatial bottlenecks in ways not captured by existing theory, leaving no principled guidance for designing STGNNs that effectively leverage long-range spatiotemporal dependencies.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a window of T synchronous, regularly-sampled observations per graph node (x_{t-T:t}^v ∈ R^{T×d_x}) along with a spatial graph adjacency matrix A ∈ R^{N×N} and a temporal graph T encoding the directed path structure of the time axis. The readout operates exclusively on the final time step's representation at the last layer, producing either H-step-ahead node-level forecasts (e.g., traffic flow, solar radiation) or node classification labels. The temporal graph is fixed as a backward shift operator (lower-triangular Toeplitz), while the spatial graph can be directed or undirected and may vary across time steps in T&S variants.

## Method

The paper extends Jacobian sensitivity analysis to the spatiotemporal setting by defining ∇_i^u h_t^{v(L)} = ∂h_t^{v(L)}/∂h_{t-i}^{u(0)}, which captures how input features at node u and time step t-i influence the final representation at node v and time t. The key theoretical result (Theorem 5.1) factorizes the sensitivity bound into a model-dependent term and a spatiotemporal topology term (S^{LL_S})_{uv}(R^{LL_T})_{ij}, proving that the multiplicative separation between spatial and temporal components persists regardless of whether processing is interleaved (T&S) or decoupled (TTS), thereby justifying the computational efficiency of TTS designs. A critical finding is that causal convolutions in TCNs exhibit an inverted over-squashing pattern: the temporal topology matrix R's powers cause sensitivity to recent time steps to vanish as O(l^{-(i-j)}) while amplifying influence from earlier steps, analogous to the attention sink effect in Transformers. To mitigate this, the paper proposes temporal graph rewiring via dilated convolutions (varying R per layer) and row-normalized convolutions (R_N = diag(R1)^{-1}R), the latter provably converging to uniform sensitivity across all time steps. These theoretical insights are validated on synthetic memory tasks (COPYFIRST, COPYLAST, ROCKETMAN) and real-world forecasting benchmarks (METR-LA, PEMS-BAY, EngRAD), confirming that TTS and T&S perform comparably and that combined spatial-temporal rewiring yields the largest practical gains.

## Results

On three real-world spatiotemporal forecasting benchmarks, the TTS MPTCN with row-normalized convolutions (RN, L=1) achieves MAE of 3.16 on METR-LA, 1.65 on PEMS-BAY, and 40.38 on EngRAD. The row-normalized temporal topology yields the largest gain on EngRAD (40.38 vs. 44.47 for standard causal convolutions at L=1), confirming that temporal over-squashing is most impactful when longer filter sizes are needed. The TTS variant (L=1) matches or slightly outperforms the T&S variants (L=3, L=6) across all datasets, supporting the theoretical equivalence of the two paradigms. The TTS version of Graph WaveNet (GWNet TTS) achieves MAE of 3.00, 1.57, and 40.64, remaining within 0.1–0.2 MAE of the original T&S GWNet.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is Graph WaveNet (GWNet) [Wu et al., IJCAI 2019], a widely-used T&S architecture. The paper's TTS reimplementation of GWNet (GWNet TTS) achieves MAE of 3.00 on METR-LA (vs. 3.02 for the original), 1.57 on PEMS-BAY (vs. 1.55), and 40.64 on EngRAD (vs. 40.50), confirming that the TTS paradigm incurs no meaningful information-propagation penalty relative to the original T&S design. The simpler MPTCN with row-normalized convolutions (L=1) reaches 40.38 MAE on EngRAD, slightly beating both GWNet variants, but trails GWNet on METR-LA (3.16 vs. 3.00) and PEMS-BAY (1.65 vs. 1.55), indicating that the more sophisticated GWNet components (e.g., dilated convolutions, attention) still provide gains on traffic data. On EngRAD, where longer temporal contexts are required, the row-normalized MPTCN outperforms standard-convolution MPTCNs by ~4 MAE points, validating the temporal over-squashing theory in a practical setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the sensitivity-based over-squashing analysis established for static GNNs (Alon & Yahav; Topping et al.; Di Giovanni et al.) to the spatiotemporal setting, where information must propagate along both graph edges and the time axis. It builds directly on the spatial Jacobian bound of Di Giovanni et al. [43] and introduces an analogous temporal bound for causal convolutional layers, then composes them into a unified spatiotemporal sensitivity theorem (Theorem 5.1). The paper also draws a conceptual parallel to the attention sink effect in Transformers [51, 52] and to vanishing-gradient analyses of RNNs [33, 34], positioning temporal over-squashing as a previously unrecognized manifestation of the same compression phenomenon. No prior work had formally characterized over-squashing in STGNNs, making this the first systematic treatment of the problem in the joint space-time domain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that TTS and T&S architectures are equally susceptible to spatiotemporal over-squashing provides a principled theoretical justification for adopting the computationally cheaper TTS design without sacrificing information propagation capacity — a practically important finding for scalable STGNN deployment. The counterintuitive discovery that causal convolutions amplify influence from temporally distant steps (inverting the expected locality bias) offers actionable guidance: simple modifications like row-normalized or dilated convolutions can restore balanced temporal sensitivity, and the paper validates these on real-world forecasting benchmarks. Limitations include the focus on factorized convolutional STGNNs (excluding recurrent or joint space-time filter architectures), worst-case bounds that may be conservative, and the observation that row-normalization primarily benefits the final time step, limiting its utility for tasks requiring intermediate readouts. Extending the framework to non-factorized architectures and deriving tighter data-dependent bounds remain open directions.
