## Problem

Preference-based reinforcement learning (PbRL) suffers from two compounding inefficiencies under limited human feedback: the reward model overfits to the small set of preference-labeled pairs, producing poor generalization, and TD-based policy learning amplifies reward errors through Q-value overestimation. Existing PbRL methods address neither issue jointly, resulting in unstable learning and poor feedback efficiency when only a few hundred preference queries are available.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255609/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes state observations (continuous vectors from DMControl, Meta-World, D4RL, or Robosuite environments) and pairs of trajectory segments (each 50 timesteps long) annotated with binary or ternary human preference labels. It produces a deterministic policy mapping states to actions, a learned reward model with outputs bounded in [-1,1] via tanh activation, and a conservative Q-value estimate that bootstraps exclusively over in-distribution state-action pairs from the replay buffer. In offline settings, the policy is extracted via advantage-weighted regression to avoid out-of-sample actions.

## Method

STAR builds on a TD3/PEBBLE backbone and introduces two complementary regularizers. First, Preference Margin Regularization (PMR) replaces the standard Bradley-Terry cross-entropy loss with a label-smoothed variant using margin parameter ε, which analytically enforces a bounded reward difference of log((1−ε)/ε) between preferred and rejected segments, preventing the reward model from driving the margin to infinity. Second, a conservative Q-function Q_ξ is trained with a temperature-based operator T^β that bootstraps only over in-distribution state-action pairs sampled from the replay memory, yielding a lower-bound estimate of the true Q-value (proven in Theorem 4.1). This conservative estimate defines a reference Boltzmann policy, and the learned policy is regularized toward it via KL divergence with weight η, constraining policy updates to well-supported regions. The two components are orthogonal: PMR stabilizes reward learning while policy regularization stabilizes value estimation, and ablations confirm both are necessary for the reported 34.8% (online) and 29.7% (offline) improvements over state-of-the-art baselines.

## Results

In online PbRL settings, STAR achieves a mean normalized score of 0.941 (baseline/SAC ratio), surpassing the prior state-of-the-art MRN (0.698) by 34.8% across DMControl and Meta-World tasks. In offline settings, STAR achieves 29.7% average improvement over state-of-the-art methods on D4RL, reaching 91.52 on locomotion tasks (vs. 88.66 for DTR and 84.37 for IQL with ground-truth rewards) and 58.67 on AntMaze (nearly matching IQL's 58.79 with explicit rewards). Ablation studies confirm that removing both regularization components degrades Cheetah Run performance from 713.5 to 491.5, and that STAR reduces Q-value overestimation bias from 25–32% (MRN/SURF) to just 7%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

STAR outperforms all online PbRL baselines: it beats MRN (0.698) by 34.8%, PEBBLE (0.531) by 77%, and SURF (0.430) by 119% in mean normalized score. In offline settings, STAR (58.67 AntMaze total, 91.52 locomotion total) surpasses DTR (55.85, 88.66), PT (49.35, 83.72), IPL (16.72, 79.13), and the MLP reward model MR (20.16, 62.43) across D4RL tasks. Notably, STAR nearly matches IQL with ground-truth rewards (58.67 vs. 58.79 on AntMaze) and exceeds it on locomotion (91.52 vs. 84.37), while massively outperforming TD3+BC (0.13 on AntMaze). On Robosuite, STAR (77.0) slightly trails IQL (78.56) and IPL (79.3) but beats DTR (74.26), PT (74.66), and MR (72.81), indicating the advantage is most pronounced in continuous-control locomotion and navigation tasks rather than manipulation.

## Relation to Prior Work

STAR builds directly on the PEBBLE framework (Lee et al., 2021) as its backbone, which itself extends the Christiano et al. (2017) PbRL paradigm with unsupervised pre-training and reward relabeling. The preference margin regularization draws from margin-based classification theory (SVMs, label smoothing) to bound the otherwise unbounded reward-difference optimization, while the conservative Q estimation borrows the in-distribution bootstrapping principle from CQL and IQL. Unlike SURF (semi-supervised pseudo-labels), MRN (bi-level optimization), or QPA (query-policy alignment), STAR addresses both reward overfitting and Q-value overestimation simultaneously through a dual-regularization lens, positioning itself as orthogonal to these prior efficiency improvements.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By reducing feedback requirements substantially (34.8% online, 29.7% offline gains over SOTA), STAR makes PbRL more practical for real-world robotic settings where human annotation is expensive. The human experiments demonstrating novel skills (backflip, high jump) from only 50 preference queries highlight practical potential beyond benchmark scores. Key limitations remain: the method operates on state-vector inputs only (no raw RGB or partially observable settings), all experiments are in simulation, and the conservative Q operator's temperature parameter β requires tuning. Extending STAR to high-dimensional sensory inputs and physical robot deployment are the natural next steps the authors identify.
