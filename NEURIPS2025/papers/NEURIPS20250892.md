## Problem

Standard Poisson GLMs for neural connectivity inference require binning spike times into discrete counts, limiting temporal resolution to ~1–10 ms—coarser than the submillisecond timescale of synaptic dynamics—and producing design matrices that become infeasible to store at fine resolutions for modern large-scale recordings. Even when batched to fit in memory, the resulting gradient variance prevents stable convergence, making it impossible to recover coupling filters at biophysically relevant timescales.

## Input / Output

The method consumes raw spike times (neuron index, timestamp) from multi-neuron Neuropixels recordings at submillisecond precision, with a 4–6 ms history window defining the interaction range, rather than a pre-binned design matrix. It outputs per-neuron-pair coupling filters expressed as weights over 3–5 generalized Laguerre basis functions, from which putative excitatory connections are identified by selecting filters with peaks in the 0.3–2.5 ms latency range. The continuous formulation means memory scales with spike count rather than recording length times bin resolution, enabling analysis of 2.7-hour, 106-neuron hippocampal recordings that are intractable for binned GLMs.

## Method

The paper reformulates the Poisson GLM as a continuous-time Poisson point process, where the log-likelihood's cumulative intensity function (CIF) integral is approximated by two complementary methods: a stratified Monte Carlo sampler that provides an unbiased CIF estimate with low gradient variance, and a second-order polynomial approximation (PA) that renders the log-likelihood quadratic in the GLM parameters, yielding a closed-form MAP solution. A key architectural choice is the use of exponentially scaled generalized Laguerre polynomials as temporal basis functions, which are orthogonal under a natural weight, follow a gamma-function envelope matching synaptic rise-and-decay dynamics, and admit closed-form single and pairwise product integrals via the incomplete gamma function. A hybrid strategy initializes parameters with the fast PA-c closed-form solution and then refines them with MC-based gradient descent, achieving the best speed-accuracy tradeoff. All computations are GPU-parallelized over spike-time scans, making runtime largely insensitive to recording duration and scaling only moderately with population size.

## Results

On simulated data, the Hybrid (PA-c warm-start + MC) and MC methods achieve the lowest filter MSE, outperforming both PA-d and the discrete batched GLM (DB), with the Hybrid offering the best speed-accuracy tradeoff. On real hippocampal Neuropixels data (106 neurons, ~2.7 h), the Hybrid model's coupling filters align with full-dataset cross-correlograms using only 500 s (8.3 min) of data. The recovered putative excitatory connectivity matches known hippocampal anatomy: CA3→CA3 recurrent connections reach 3.83% (mean delay 1.75 ± 0.52 ms), consistent with anatomical estimates, while cross-region delays (e.g., CA3→CA1 at 2.15 ± 0.34 ms) exceed intra-regional latencies, consistent with axonal conduction times. Continuous-time methods (PA-c, MC, Hybrid) scale nearly linearly with population size and are largely insensitive to recording length, whereas DB requires ~5 hours on a 250-neuron / 1000-s / 0.1-ms dataset and exhibits gradient variance orders of magnitude higher than MC.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the discrete polynomial-approximate GLM (PA-d, Zoltowski & Pillow 2018), the continuous PA-c variant slightly outperforms on filter MSE because binned sufficient statistics accumulate discretization error that grows with spike count; both PA methods remain less accurate than MC/Hybrid due to the global quadratic approximation of the exponential nonlinearity. Against the discrete batched GLM with SVRG optimization (DB), the continuous methods win decisively on both axes: DB's gradient variance is orders of magnitude higher (Fig. 3C), its log-likelihood converges poorly to the global optimum, and its runtime exceeds all continuous methods by orders of magnitude (e.g., ~5 h for 250 neurons at 0.1 ms binning). The paper notes it cannot directly benchmark against the only prior continuous-time GLM fitting procedure (Mena & Paninski 2014, Gauss-Lobatto quadrature) because that method requires inserting quadrature nodes between every spike pair, making it computationally infeasible at the dataset sizes explored here. Overall, the Hybrid (PA-c init + MC refinement) achieves the best filter recovery at a fraction of the runtime of DB, while MC alone provides the highest accuracy at moderate additional cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the discrete-time Poisson GLM framework (Pillow et al. [1], Zoltowski & Pillow [4]) to the continuous-time Poisson point process limit, where the design matrix is replaced by spike times and the likelihood involves an intractable cumulative intensity integral. It adapts the polynomial approximation strategy of Zoltowski & Pillow [4] and Huggins et al. [18] to this continuous setting, yielding a quadratic log-likelihood with closed-form solutions, and introduces a Monte Carlo stratified-sampling alternative for the integral. The proposed exponentially scaled generalized Laguerre polynomial basis departs from the standard raised-cosine basis [20, 21] by offering orthogonality and analytic integral forms, while prior continuous-time point process work [11, 12, 17] either provides theory without scalable fitting or uses quadrature methods that do not scale to modern dataset sizes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By making submillisecond-resolution GLM fitting computationally tractable on recordings of hundreds of neurons over hours, this work opens the door to identifying monosynaptic coupling filters at biophysically relevant timescales in large-scale neural recordings that were previously infeasible. The hippocampal validation—showing recovered filters matching known anatomical connectivity (CA3 recurrent excitatory loops, Schaffer collaterals, mossy fiber projections)—provides physiological grounding for the approach. Key open questions remain: the Poisson assumption may be suboptimal for neural spiking, monosynaptic connections are still confounded with correlated firing and common input, and the PA-vs-MC trade-off (speed vs. accuracy) has not been fully resolved. Future directions include latent population dynamics, per-neuron approximation ranges, and variance-reduction techniques for the Monte Carlo estimator.
