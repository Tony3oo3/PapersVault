## Problem

Existing flight delay prediction datasets are limited to flat tabular structures that fail to capture the spatiotemporal dynamics of delay propagation through shared aircraft, crew, and airport resources. They are also geographically constrained, suffer from temporal leakage due to random train-test splits, and lack unified multi-task evaluation protocols covering regression, classification, and uncertainty quantification simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252048/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The dataset provides three aligned modalities over 54.67M flights (2016–2024, 320 U.S. airports): a 22-feature tabular table (8 categorical, 14 continuous, integrating BTS operational records with Meteostat hourly weather at origin and destination), flight chains (sequences of same-aircraft flights within 24-hour windows, truncated or padded to a fixed length L_max for sequence models), and flight network graphs (directed edges encoding both intra-aircraft and cross-aircraft delay propagation via shared airport spatiotemporal co-occupancy). Prediction targets are arrival and departure delay in minutes, evaluated under three task formulations: regression (MSE/MAE), binary classification at a 15-minute delay threshold (AUC/ACC), and probabilistic forecasting (CRPS).

## Method

The core contribution is a multi-structural dataset construction pipeline that extracts three aligned modalities from raw BTS and Meteostat sources. Flight chains are built by pre-sorting records by (carrier, flight number, date, scheduled departure time), grouping by aircraft within daily windows, and enforcing spatial continuity (destination-to-origin airport matching between adjacent legs); incompatible entries form separate chains. Flight networks extend chains into trees by adding cross-aircraft edges for spatiotemporal co-occupancy at the same airport, then aggregate all trees within a time window into a unified graph via union of node and edge sets (Eq. 1). Evaluation employs strict temporal 6:2:2 splits (not random) to prevent leakage, benchmarking across tabular (FTTransformer, TabulaRNN, SAINT, AutoInt, Tangos, MLP, ResNet), sequential (LSTM, GRU, CNN-LSTM, MogrifierLSTM), and graph (VGAE, DeepWalk combined with AFM) model families under a unified protocol.

## Results

On the tabular benchmark (287,845 flights, June 2024), FTTransformer achieves the best arrival-delay regression (MSE 0.914, MAE 0.618), while TabulaRNN leads classification accuracy (ACC 0.772 ARR / 0.761 DEP). In the sequential flight-chain setting (6.28M flights, full-year 2024), all four architectures (LSTM, GRU, CNN-LSTM, MogrifierLSTM) achieve tightly clustered AUC scores of 68.5–69.5%, with MogrifierLSTM reaching the highest DEP AUC of 69.54%. In the graph setting, AFM+VGAE attains the best AUC of 67.94%, outperforming the AFM baseline (67.23%) by 0.71 percentage points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among the seven tabular baselines, no single model dominates all tasks: FTTransformer wins ARR regression (MSE 0.914 vs. 0.925 for TabulaRNN and 0.937 for MLP), TabulaRNN leads on classification ACC (0.772/0.761) and DEP MAE (0.588), while MLP achieves the best DEP MSE (0.940) and lowest CRPS for ARR (0.658). ResNet and Tangos underperform across most metrics, with Tangos showing the worst ARR MSE (1.038) and ResNet the worst DEP MSE (1.005). In the sequential setting, all four architectures (LSTM, GRU, CNN-LSTM, MogrifierLSTM) produce nearly identical AUC values (<0.3% gap), indicating that the flight-chain structure itself, rather than model architecture, drives predictive performance. In the graph setting, VGAE embeddings improve AFM's AUC by 0.71 pp over the no-embedding baseline and by 0.29 pp over DeepWalk, confirming that probabilistic graph encoders capture multi-hop delay propagation more effectively than random-walk embeddings. Overall, the modest AUC scores (<0.63 in tabular, ~0.69 in sequential) across all baselines highlight persistent class-imbalance and distribution-shift challenges that remain open for future work.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of flight delay prediction and tabular benchmarking by constructing a unified multi-modal dataset (tabular + flight-chain sequences + flight-network graphs) where prior datasets offered only flat, flight-level tabular features. It positions itself as a complement to TabRed for general tabular learning while addressing domain-specific gaps identified in the flight delay literature (geographic constraints, temporal leakage, single-task design). The experimental baselines draw directly on the current tabular deep learning toolkit (FTTransformer, SAINT, TabulaRNN, AutoInt, Tangos) and standard sequential/graph methods (LSTM, GRU, VGAE, DeepWalk), but the contribution is in the data construction and evaluation protocol rather than in new architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Aeolus fills a genuine gap by providing a 54M-flight, 9-year, 320-airport benchmark with three aligned modalities and strict temporal splits, enabling realistic evaluation of multi-modal structured-data models under distribution shift. The quantified 0.057 AUC inflation under random splitting versus temporal splitting is a useful methodological warning for the broader tabular ML community. Key limitations include heavy geographic bias (78.4% North America), absence of ATC/crew/passenger-level features, and the pandemic period introducing non-stationarity; open questions include whether the multi-modal structure yields gains beyond the modest 0.7% AUC improvement observed with graph embeddings, and how well models transfer across the geographic and temporal heterogeneity the dataset contains.
