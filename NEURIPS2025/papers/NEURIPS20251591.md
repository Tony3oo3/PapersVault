## Problem

While minimax-optimal sample complexity for tabular CMDPs was established in the random-access (generative model) setting, it remained open whether such optimality could be achieved in the inherently harder online setting where data must be collected through sequential interactions. Prior online CMDP algorithms suffered from suboptimal sample complexity due to loose decoupling between the empirical transition model and value estimates, a challenge that does not arise naturally in constrained settings because the policy is determined by the constraint and thus correlated with all transition estimates.

## Input / Output

The algorithm operates on a tabular episodic MDP with S states, A actions, and horizon H, where reward and cost functions are known (bounded in [0,1]) but transition dynamics are unknown and must be learned online. The agent interacts over K episodes, observing states and receiving scalar rewards and costs at each step. The output is a mixture policy π̄ = (1/K)Σπ^k that is ε-optimal in reward with either ε-bounded constraint violation (relaxed feasibility) or zero violation (strict feasibility), with the strict case depending on the Slater constant ζ that measures the size of the feasible region.

## Method

The paper proposes a model-based primal-dual algorithm that transforms the constrained optimization into a saddle-point problem over a Lagrangian, enabling the primal policy to be solved via backward updates and thereby decoupling value estimates from the empirical transition model. The empirical model is updated lazily using doubling batch updates (only when any state-action visitation count doubles), which keeps the number of model versions logarithmic in K and enables tight union bounds. Bernstein-style bonuses are applied optimistically to rewards and pessimistically to costs, with the constraint threshold shifted by τ (relaxed) or −Δ (strict) to control the violation guarantee. The dual variable is discretized onto an ε-net to maintain tractable concentration arguments, and the final policy is a uniform mixture over all primal-dual iterates across all episodes. This yields sample complexity Õ(SAH³/ε²) for relaxed feasibility (matching the unconstrained MDP lower bound) and Õ(SAH⁵/(ε²ζ²)) for strict feasibility (matching the generative-model lower bound).

## Results

For relaxed feasibility, the algorithm achieves an ε-optimal policy with at most ε constraint violation in $\widetilde{O}(SAH^3/\varepsilon^2)$ online episodes, matching the minimax lower bound for unconstrained MDPs. For strict feasibility, it achieves an ε-optimal zero-violation policy in $\widetilde{O}(SAH^5/(\varepsilon^2\zeta^2))$ episodes, where ζ is the Slater constant, matching the lower bound for CMDPs with a generative model. The regret bound is $\widetilde{O}(\sqrt{SAH^3K})$ in both settings, and the constraint violation is $\widetilde{O}(\sqrt{SAH^3K} + K\tau)$ (relaxed) or $\widetilde{O}(\sqrt{SAH^3K} - K\Delta)$ (strict).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines. The key comparisons are against information-theoretic lower bounds and prior theoretical results. For relaxed feasibility, the $\widetilde{O}(SAH^3/\varepsilon^2)$ bound matches the unconstrained MDP lower bound [11], showing that allowing small violations makes CMDPs no harder than standard MDPs. For strict feasibility, the $\widetilde{O}(SAH^5/(\varepsilon^2\zeta^2))$ bound matches the generative-model lower bound [18], establishing that online access is no harder than random access. Prior online CMDP algorithms [16, 17] achieved suboptimal bounds with extra $S$ and $H$ factors (e.g., $\widetilde{O}(S^2AH^6/\varepsilon^2)$), due to loose decoupling of the empirical model from value estimates. The paper's doubling batch update technique and Lagrangian-based primal-dual framework are the key innovations that close this gap.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the minimax-optimal sample complexity results for CMDPs from the random-access (generative model) setting of Vaswani et al. [18] to the more challenging online setting, where the agent must collect data through sequential interactions. The key technical ingredient is adapting the doubling batch update and profile-based decoupling technique from Zhang et al. [15] (which settled sample complexity for unconstrained online MDPs) to handle the inherent correlation between the empirical transition model and value estimates that arises in the constrained setting. The primal-dual framework and Lagrangian relaxation follow the standard CMDP optimization approach, with the discrete dual variable net and Bernstein-style bonuses providing the exploration mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that online CMDP learning achieves the same sample complexity as unconstrained MDPs (relaxed feasibility) or generative-model CMDPs (strict feasibility) resolves a key open question in safe RL theory, showing that the online setting does not impose additional sample cost. However, the results are limited to tabular, single-constraint, episodic CMDPs with known reward/cost functions; extensions to function approximation, multiple constraints, or unknown cost functions remain open. For practitioners, the theoretical guarantees provide a benchmark for what is information-theoretically achievable, but the algorithm's practical competitiveness in large-scale or continuous-state settings is untested.
