## Problem

Vision-language-action models (VLAs) show strong task performance but lack explicit safety constraint integration, posing risks of harm to environments, robots, and humans during real-world deployment. Existing safety alignment methods for LLMs/VLMs (RLHF, content moderation) target abstract model-intention risks and cannot address the concrete physical hazards faced by embodied agents. This paper asks how safety constraints can be explicitly and systematically integrated into VLAs without sacrificing task performance.

## Input / Output

The policy π_θ maps a temporal observation history h_t = (o_{t+1-H}, ..., o_t) — where each observation o_t comprises an RGB image v_t and proprioceptive state p_t — together with a natural language instruction l, to a robot action a_t. The reward function r is conditioned on the instruction l, making the objective instruction-dependent. Safety constraints are encoded as compositional state-action predicates φ(s,a) and trajectory-level predicates ψ(τ) that output binary violation signals, which are converted into per-step cost signals for the CMDP optimization.

## Method

The Integrated Safety Approach (ISA) formulates VLA safety alignment as a constrained Markov decision process and solves it via Lagrangian-based safe reinforcement learning. Safety requirements are modeled as compositional logic predicates over state-action pairs and trajectory segments, then instantiated across five safety-critical components (corners, blind spots, fragile collections, critical points, dangerous equipment) within the Safety-CHORES benchmark built on 150K ProcTHOR scenes and 800K Objaverse assets. The core optimization is a min-max Lagrangian relaxation: min_θ max_{λ≥0} [−J_r(θ) + Σ λ_i J_{c_i}(θ)], where dynamic Lagrange multipliers adaptively balance task reward against cumulative safety costs, prioritizing constraint satisfaction before performance maximization. The approach is applied as a fine-tuning stage on top of an imitation-learning-initialized VLA (SPOC-DINOv2), requiring no architectural changes to the base model.

## Results

On the Safety-CHORES benchmark, ISA achieves an average 83.58% reduction in cumulative cost (CC) compared to the strongest RL baseline FLaRe, while simultaneously improving task success rate (SR) by +3.85%. Specifically, on Safety-Fetch (the most complex task), ISA reduces CC from 43.364 (FLaRe) to 8.084 while increasing SR from 0.605 to 0.637. On Safety-ObjNav, ISA achieves SR=0.865 with CC=1.854, compared to FLaRe's SR=0.822 and CC=12.356. The upper bound of unsafe behavior severity is reduced to 1/35th of FLaRe, eliminating all trajectories with CC > 10.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ISA dominates FLaRe (the strongest IL+RL baseline) on every metric across all three tasks: CC is reduced by 85% on ObjNav (1.854 vs 12.356), 95% on PickUp (0.372 vs 7.076), and 81% on Fetch (8.084 vs 43.364), while SR improves by 4.3%, 1.8%, and 3.2% respectively. Against the reward-shaping variant FLaRe-RS, ISA still achieves substantially lower CC (e.g., 8.084 vs 18.19 on Fetch) and higher SR (0.637 vs 0.450 on Fetch), demonstrating that explicit CMDP constraints outperform heuristic penalty-based safety. Compared to IL-only SPOC variants, ISA achieves dramatically lower CC (e.g., 1.854 vs 13.504 for SPOC-DINOv2 on ObjNav) and higher SR on navigation and fetch tasks, though SPOC-SigLip-L w/GT detection achieves a slightly lower CC on PickUp (3.888 vs 0.372 is actually ISA winning; SPOC-SigLip-L w/GT at 3.888 is still much worse than ISA's 0.372). Against Poliformer (RL-only, navigation only), ISA achieves higher SR (0.865 vs 0.804) and lower CC (1.854 vs 9.218) on Safety-ObjNav. The key finding is that ISA is the only method that simultaneously achieves the best SR and best CC across all three tasks, confirming an effective safety-performance trade-off rather than a zero-sum game.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate research threads: SafeRL under the CMDP framework (which has been applied to specialized, single-task agents) and VLA fine-tuning (SPOC, FLaRe, GRAPE), which has focused exclusively on task performance without explicit safety constraints. The core methodological contribution is adapting Lagrangian-based CMDP optimization to the high-dimensional, multimodal VLA setting, where the policy must simultaneously satisfy multiple safety predicates while following open-ended language instructions. The Safety-CHORES benchmark extends the AI2THOR/ProcTHOR ecosystem by embedding fine-grained safety-critical components (corners, blind spots, fragile collections, etc.) into long-horizon mobile manipulation tasks, filling a gap between simplified SafeRL benchmarks and standard VLA evaluation suites that lack safety structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic demonstration that explicit safety constraints can be integrated into generalized VLAs without sacrificing task performance, addressing a critical gap for real-world deployment of embodied AI. The Safety-CHORES benchmark and the ISA pipeline provide a reusable framework for the growing VLA community to assess and improve safety. Open questions remain: credit assignment for trajectory-level safety predicates is acknowledged as unresolved, the approach is validated only in simulation with a limited set of safety predicate types, and scaling to more complex, compositional safety requirements (e.g., human-in-the-loop constraints) is unexplored. The sim-to-real transfer, while promising, was demonstrated on a single task with a single robot platform.
