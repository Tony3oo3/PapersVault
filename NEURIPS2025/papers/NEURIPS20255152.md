## Problem

Developing neural operators to predict hemodynamics directly from vascular geometries requires large-scale datasets pairing anatomically realistic 3D shapes with high-fidelity CFD solutions, yet existing bioengineering datasets contain only a few hundred cases, rely on idealized or manually warped geometries, and often omit parent vessels that critically influence flow. This paper introduces AneuG-Flow, a 14,730-case dataset of intracranial aneurysm geometries (sac + parent vessels) with associated steady and pulsatile hemodynamics, to close this gap.

## Input / Output

The ML baselines consume a 3D surface mesh of the aneurysm sac with a fixed graph structure of 13,902 nodes and 14,000 triangles (identical connectivity across all cases, enabling direct node-to-node registration), and output WSS vector components (x, y, z) at each surface node. Beyond the surface WSS graph, the dataset also provides full 3D volume fields of velocity, pressure, and WSS, plus spatial gradients of velocity and temporal gradients of pressure for the 730 transient cases, all stored as PyTorch tensors with shape [B, N, C] for steady and [T, N, 1] for transient data.

## Method

Geometries are generated by AneuG, a two-stage VAE that encodes spatial warping of real IA shapes (from the 109-patient AneuX cohort) into Graph Harmonic Deformation tokens, producing both aneurysm sacs and their parent vessels as a joint distribution at the MCA bifurcation. Each geometry is meshed (3.4M volume cells via VMTK) and simulated in ANSYS Fluent using a Carreau-Yasuda non-Newtonian blood model, a parabolic inlet velocity profile (0.684 m/s), and geometry-dependent outlet flow splits determined by a modified Murray's law (γ = 2.45). For ML evaluation, the authors train a PointNet++ and three graph U-Nets (GCN, GAT, ChebNet) in a supervised setting, introducing a power-mapping normalization loss (α=100, β=2/3) that down-weights high-WSS junction regions so the model learns the clinically relevant low-WSS spatial pattern on the sac. The ChebNet-based U-Net achieves the best relative L2 error of 4.67% on normalized WSS, attributed to its larger spectral receptive field for cross-mesh information propagation.

## Results

On the AneuG-Flow dataset (14,000 steady cases, 80/20 train/test split), the best-performing model — a graph U-Net with Chebyshev Spectral Graph Network (ChebNet) convolutional layers — achieves a relative L2 error of 4.67% on power-normalized WSS, with an RMSE of 0.191 Pa and MAE of 0.108 Pa on raw WSS values. The dataset itself comprises 14,000 steady-flow and 730 pulsatile-flow CFD cases, each simulated on meshes averaging 3.4 million volume cells using a Carreau-Yasuda non-Newtonian blood model. For the morphological–biomechanical correlation analysis on 730 transient cases, the equivalent radius (ER) shows a moderate positive correlation with RRT (r = 0.26, p = 0.012), while the lobulation index (LI) exhibits a counterintuitive negative correlation with both OSI (r = −0.24, p = 0.020) and RRT (r = −0.26, p = 0.013).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not benchmark against external methods from prior work; instead, it compares four network architectures as internal baselines for the WSS-prediction demonstration task. The graph U-Net with ChebNet layers outperforms all other variants across every metric: it reduces relative L2 error by 0.15–0.72 percentage points compared to U-Net(GCN) (4.82%), U-Net(GAT) (5.04%), and PointNet++ (5.39%). The authors attribute ChebNet's advantage to its larger receptive field, which enables more effective information propagation across the 13,902-node sac surface graph. PointNet++ and U-Net(GAT) perform comparably to each other and are both slightly worse than U-Net(GCN), suggesting that attention-based edge weighting (GAT) does not provide a clear benefit over standard GCN on this fixed-connectivity graph. All models were trained under identical conditions (single RTX 3090, 24 hours, learning rate decayed by 0.75 every 100 epochs), making the comparison controlled.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the AneuG generative model (Ding et al., MICCAI 2025) and its Graph Harmonic Deformation encoding (Luo et al., 2024) from geometry generation to full hemodynamic simulation, producing the first large-scale dataset where both aneurysm sacs and parent vessels are jointly generated by a data-driven model. It departs from prior IA hemodynamics datasets (AnXplore, Aneumo) that relied on idealized mother vessels or manual warping of real shapes, and from the broader neural-operator literature (Yin et al., Li et al., Quackenbush et al.) that has been constrained by small or synthetic geometry collections. The mesh downsampling strategy for graph construction follows Tan et al. (CVPR 2018), and the boundary-condition design (Murray's-law-based flow split, Carreau-Yasuda rheology) follows established cerebrovascular CFD practice.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dataset directly addresses the bottleneck preventing clinical adoption of biomechanical markers: the inability to run CFD at the sample sizes needed to validate their predictive utility. With 14,000 steady and 730 pulsatile cases at >3M cells each, it enables training and benchmarking of neural operators for geometry-conditioned PDE solving in a physiologically relevant setting. Key open questions remain: the limited number of transient cases constrains analysis of time-averaged markers (TAWSS, OSI, RRT), the single 1-inlet/2-outlet topology limits generalizability, and the absence of boundary-condition variation means the dataset cannot yet capture patient-specific hemodynamic diversity. The preliminary finding that standard morphological markers (AR, SR, H_max) show weak correlations with hemodynamic risk markers reinforces the need for geometry-aware ML models rather than simple shape descriptors.
