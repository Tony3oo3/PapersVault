## Problem

At the computational scales of modern LLM pre-training, hyperparameter tuning is infeasible, yet optimal weight decay (λ) and batch size (B) are critical for effective training. Prior work suggested the AdamW timescale should remain constant across scales and tied optimal/critical batch size to compute or loss, but no systematic study had established how λ scales with N, D, and B, nor identified the fundamental scaling variable for batch size.

## Input / Output

Given a model size N, dataset size D, batch size B, and a μP-adjusted learning rate η (set via proxy-model tuning), the method produces closed-form predictions for λ_opt, B_opt, and B_crit that can be computed in advance of training. The B_crit estimation is schedule-agnostic: it fits per-batch-size loss power laws L_B(D) from a handful of training runs and interpolates the data needed to reach any target loss, avoiding the need to know step counts a priori or to use constant-LR runs.

## Method

The paper models the AdamW timescale τ = B/(ηλD) and shows τ_opt follows a precise power law in tokens-per-parameter (TPP = D/N), with exponent ≈ −0.5, enabling λ_opt to be computed for any N, D, B via a single formula. Rather than adjusting η with B (which risks instability), the method prescribes adjusting λ to maintain optimal τ, which is shown to be strictly superior in 6 of 8 tested cases. For B_crit, the authors introduce a novel estimation procedure: fit B-specific power laws L_B(D) = E_N + D_const·D^(−β) from training runs at varying D, interpolate the data D_B needed to reach a target loss, then fit the McCandlish hyperbolic model to ⟨D_B, S⟩ pairs to extract B_crit. This reveals that both B_opt and B_crit scale as power laws in D (exponents ≈ 0.4 and ≈ 0.5 respectively), independent of model size N, correcting prior attributions to compute C or loss L. Finally, combining the B_crit(D) law with the Chinchilla loss model yields Pareto-optimal (N, D, B) configurations that trade off training time against compute cost, showing overtrained smaller models can dominate under time-constrained budgets.

## Results

The paper derives three key power laws from ~400 LLM training runs on SlimPajama: (1) the optimal AdamW timescale τ_opt scales as TPP^(-0.52) (R²=0.975, 10th–90th percentile of m_τ: −0.529 to −0.507), enabling λ_opt prediction for any N, D, B; (2) B_opt scales as D^0.38 (R²=0.984, m_B_opt: 0.367–0.391); and (3) B_crit scales as D^0.46 (R²=0.940, m_B_crit: 0.491–0.526). The τ law generalizes across at least 3 orders of magnitude in compute (from 111M-20TPP to 3.3B-30TPP, a 1000× FLOPs difference). Tuning λ (rather than η) as B changes is strictly superior in 6 of 8 tested configurations (Table 1), with validation losses improving by up to 0.032 at B=8064.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DeepSeek LLM [7], which fits B_opt = 0.292·C^0.3271 in total FLOPs, the paper shows that C-based scaling is a spurious dependence on N: when the same data is plotted against D, a clean power law emerges (R²=0.984) while the C-based fit fails to capture cross-model variation. B_deepseek values generally fall between the paper's B_opt and B_crit, confirming they are in the efficient regime but not fundamentally tied to compute. Against Kaplan et al. [4], which proposed B_crit scales with target loss L, the paper demonstrates that B_crit scales with D (not L) regardless of model size or TPP; plotting B_crit vs. L shows parallel lines at fixed N/TPP rather than a single curve. Against Zhang et al. [12], who report B_crit = 22.91·D^0.47 (in tokens) for a 302M model without weight decay, the paper's independently fitted exponent of 0.462 is nearly identical, while the coefficient differs by ~19% (0.0559 vs. 0.0471 in sequences), likely reflecting differences in dataset, context length, and use of weight decay. The paper's key advantage over all three baselines is that it provides a unified framework (via the AdamW timescale τ) that simultaneously predicts λ_opt, B_opt, and B_crit, and explicitly shows that fixing λ (as all three baselines do) distorts the fitted scaling laws and reduces their accuracy at scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the hyperparameter scaling laws literature (Kaplan et al., Hoffmann et al., DeepSeek LLM) by introducing the first systematic study of weight decay scaling and by re-deriving batch size scaling from the AdamW timescale perspective of Wang and Aitchison. It departs from prior work in two key ways: (1) it shows the AdamW timescale τ follows a power law in tokens-per-parameter (D/N) rather than remaining constant as Wang and Aitchison found for multi-epoch image training, and (2) it demonstrates that both B_opt and B_crit scale as power laws in dataset size D, not in total compute C (as DeepSeek assumed) or in loss L (as Kaplan et al. assumed). The μP framework provides the learning-rate backbone, while the paper's contribution is the orthogonal λ and B scaling laws that complement it.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides directly actionable recipes (λ_opt via Eq. 4, B_opt ∝ D^0.4, B_crit ∝ D^0.5) that eliminate the need for expensive HP tuning at scale, and the Pareto frontier analysis reveals that small overtrained models can dominate large compute-optimal ones when training time is a first-class objective—a strategic insight for resource-constrained labs. Limitations include a single dataset (SlimPajama), single architecture family (GPT-2-like), AdamW-only analysis, and single-epoch setting; reconciling the τ law with multi-epoch training and extending to dynamic batch sizing or inference-time scaling objectives are natural open directions. The finding that fixed weight decay (standard practice) systematically distorts B_opt and B_crit fits is a practical warning for practitioners who have not been tuning λ.
