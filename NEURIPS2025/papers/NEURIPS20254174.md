## Problem

Autonomous robots need environment representations for collision-free motion planning, but constructing these from a single RGB image is difficult: monocular depth estimators like DepthAnything produce frustum-shaped artifacts at object boundaries that corrupt free-space geometry and make the resulting structure unusable for motion generation. Additionally, there is a clear disconnect in the literature between scene representation methods and reactive motion generation frameworks, with no prior work extracting both a usable 3D structure and a downstream motion policy from a single image.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (no depth, no multi-view input) and a user-specified camera trajectory as conditioning for the video generator. It produces a dense 3D point cloud (filtered by DUSt3R confidence maps), a SIREN-based implicit unsigned distance field f_θ: R³→R trained on ~10,000 surface points, and real-time collision-free trajectories where per-step velocity is computed in under 1 ms via a closed-form Sherman-Morrison inversion of the metric. The video generator (Seva, 1.3B parameters) is conditioned on both the input image and a sequence of camera poses to synthesize geometrically consistent novel views.

## Method

VGER sidesteps direct monocular depth prediction by using the pre-trained video generator Seva (Stable Diffusion 2.1 backbone) to synthesize a multi-view video conditioned on the input image and a camera trajectory, then fusing the generated frames with the original image through DUSt3R to obtain a dense, artifact-free point cloud. From this point cloud, a SIREN activation network is trained as an implicit unsigned distance field using a multi-scale noise-contrastive scheme: surface points are perturbed with Gaussian noise whose standard deviation is drawn log-uniformly over [σ_min, σ_max], and the network is optimized with a combined loss of MSE distance fitting, L1 surface consistency, and Eikonal regularization (‖∇f_θ‖=1). For motion generation, the distance field is embedded into a Riemannian metric G(x) = I + f_blow(x)·u(x)u(x)ᵀ where f_blow ∝ (f_θ+ε)⁻⁴·exp(−βf_θ) sharply penalizes motion toward surfaces; the resulting dynamics ẋ = G(x)⁻¹·g_base(x) are evaluated in closed form via the Sherman-Morrison formula, yielding smooth, collision-free trajectories that adapt to the reconstructed geometry without hard constraints.

## Results

On the primary 3D reconstruction metric (normalized Chamfer distance, lower is better), VGER achieves the best score on 4 of 6 scenes: Table (0.030), Office (0.047), Stone (0.096), and Cabinet (0.044), outperforming all five baselines. For downstream motion quality measured by normalized Discrete Fréchet Distance (DFD), VGER-based trajectories outperform DepthAnything-V2 on all six scenes, with the largest margin on Cabinet (0.127 vs. 0.322) and Indoor (0.111 vs. 0.203). Per-step velocity computation for the metric-field motion policy runs in under 1 ms.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VGER outperforms DepthAnything-V2, VGGT (single image), DUSt3R (single image), and ViewCrafter on the majority of scenes for Chamfer distance, with particularly large margins on Cabinet (0.044 vs. 0.148 for DA-V2) and Stone (0.096 vs. 0.259 for DA-V2). The closest competitor is GEN3C, which edges out VGER on Garden (0.060 vs. 0.075) and Bench (0.016 vs. 0.029), but VGER wins on the remaining four scenes. For trajectory quality (normalized DFD), VGER consistently beats DA-V2 across all six scenes, with the most dramatic improvement on Cabinet (0.127 vs. 0.322), where DA-V2's frustum-shaped artifacts cause trajectories to be funneled into occluded regions. The paper attributes VGER's advantage to multi-view consistency from the video generator, which eliminates the frustum-shaped free-space artifacts that plague single-image depth estimators and enables correct collision avoidance around occluded geometry.

## Relation to Prior Work

VGER departs from the standard monocular depth estimation pipeline (DepthAnything, Marigold) by replacing direct per-pixel depth prediction with a video-generation step: a pre-trained image-to-video model (Seva) synthesizes a multi-view sequence conditioned on the input image, which is then fused by a 3D foundation model (DUSt3R) into a dense point cloud. The implicit representation stage draws on SIREN-based neural distance fields with Eikonal regularization, while the motion generation stage extends Riemannian Motion Policies and Geometric Fabrics by embedding an environment-dependent metric field derived from the learned unsigned distance function. The key conceptual departure is treating a generative video model as a virtual multi-view sensor, sidestepping the frustum-shaped artifacts that plague learned depth maps at object boundaries.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The pipeline demonstrates that a single RGB image is sufficient to produce both a collision-aware scene representation and a reactive motion policy, a significant data-efficiency gain for deployment in unstructured environments. However, the evaluation is limited to six scenes with ten images each, and the video generator can hallucinate geometry in regions far from the original viewpoint, introducing unquantified uncertainty into the downstream motion policy. Open questions include how to propagate confidence or uncertainty from the generative stage into the metric field, whether the approach scales to large-scale outdoor scenes, and whether the moderate Chamfer-distance improvements over strong baselines like GEN3C translate into meaningful safety margins in real robotic deployment.
