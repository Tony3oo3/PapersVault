## Problem

Inferring a neuron's identity (cell type, brain region) from its activity remains difficult because single-neuron features like waveform shape or interspike intervals capture only part of what distinguishes neurons, and prior methods that incorporate population context reduce it to fixed low-dimensional summaries that obscure fine-grained coordination patterns. Moreover, existing approaches require retraining or finetuning on each new recording, preventing zero-shot generalization to unseen animals.

## Input / Output

The model takes as input the activity of a neural population over a fixed context window (10 s for electrophysiology, 30 s for calcium imaging), where spike trains are binned at 20 ms and partitioned into 1 s patches, or calcium ΔF/F traces are directly patched. The population is treated as a permutation-equivariant set of arbitrary size (typically hundreds of neurons per Neuropixels probe insertion), with no assumed ordering or session-specific alignment. The output is one 256-dimensional embedding per neuron, obtained by mean-pooling over temporal patches after the spatio-temporal transformer, which can then be probed with a linear classifier for cell type or brain region.

## Method

NuCLR uses a two-stage spatio-temporal transformer: an initial stack of temporal-only layers processes each neuron's patch sequence independently with rotary position embeddings to capture internal dynamics, followed by alternating spatial and temporal attention layers that let neurons exchange information at each timepoint without positional encoding, injecting population-level context into every neuron's representation. Training is self-supervised via an InfoNCE-based contrastive objective: two temporally-spaced views of the same population are sampled, neuron dropout (up to 50%) is applied independently to each view, and the loss pulls representations of the same neuron across views together while pushing apart all other neurons within the same recording. Critically, the contrastive loss is computed independently within each view pair rather than across the full minibatch, avoiding an overabundance of easy negatives from different animals, and follows the decoupled contrastive loss (DCL) formulation by omitting positive-pair terms from the denominator. Ablations confirm that the spatial attention layers are the key architectural component—removing them drops Allen VC cell-type F1 from 0.72 to 0.55—while the permutation-equivariant design enables zero-shot transfer to entirely unseen populations without retraining.

## Results

On the Allen Visual Coding Neuropixels dataset (3 inhibitory cell types), NuCLR achieves a macro F1 of 0.7218 in the transductive zero-shot setting and 0.7200 in the inductive zero-shot setting, exceeding the next-best baseline (NEMO) by over 0.29 F1 in the inductive case. On the IBL Brain-wide Map (10 brain regions), NuCLR reaches 0.5295 macro F1 in inductive zero-shot, compared to 0.3793 for NEMO and 0.2532 for LOLCAT. On the Bugeon et al. calcium imaging dataset, NuCLR achieves 0.6738 F1 on the binary E-vs-I task and 0.3938 on the five-way subclass task in inductive zero-shot. NuCLR is also highly label-efficient: using only 12.5% of labeled neurons still outperforms all baselines trained on 100% of labels.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NuCLR outperforms NeuPRINT [31] by a wide margin in every setting where both are applicable, e.g., 0.7218 vs. 0.4020 on Allen VC transductive zero-shot and 0.6686 vs. 0.2734 on IBL transductive brain-region decoding. Against NEMO [54], NuCLR leads by 0.29–0.15 F1 across inductive zero-shot settings (0.7200 vs. 0.4194 on Allen VC; 0.5295 vs. 0.3793 on IBL). Against the supervised baseline LOLCAT [38], NuCLR wins on Allen VC (0.7200 vs. 0.4121), IBL (0.5295 vs. 0.2532), and Steinmetz (0.5810 vs. 0.3191) in inductive zero-shot, but notably loses on the Bugeon E-vs-I binary task (0.6738 vs. 0.7463), where the simpler two-class problem favors the supervised approach. In a separate comparison (Appendix A.4), NuCLR's unit embeddings also surpass POYO+ [5] by a large margin on Allen VC transductive zero-shot cell-type decoding (0.7218 vs. 0.3521), while training roughly 5× faster.

## Relation to Prior Work

NuCLR extends the SimCLR/DCL contrastive learning framework from image-level to neuron-level representations, where positives are temporal views of the same neuron and negatives are other neurons in the population. Architecturally, it builds on the line of transformer-based neural population models (POYO, STNDT, NEDS) but departs from them by using a permutation-equivariant set-to-set design with alternating spatial and temporal attention, rather than fixed channel ordering or supervised behavior-decoding objectives. It also moves beyond prior population-context methods (NeuPRINT, NEMO, LOLCAT) that either compress surrounding activity into fixed summary statistics or rely solely on single-neuron features, by letting each neuron attend to all others through learned spatial attention without assuming any relational bias.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The zero-shot cross-animal generalization capability is practically significant for neuroscience, where molecular labeling of cell types is unavailable in nonhuman primates and humans, and where recordings from new subjects are constantly generated. The first systematic scaling analysis showing that unlabeled pretraining data (more animals) benefits downstream decoding more than additional labeled neurons provides a clear roadmap for leveraging the growing corpus of large-scale neural datasets. Key open questions include robustness to heterogeneous stimulus distributions across tasks, unifying electrophysiology and calcium imaging into a single pretrained model, and mitigating the subject/session-specific clustering observed in smaller datasets that could hinder data-driven discovery.
