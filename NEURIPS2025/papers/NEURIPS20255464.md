## Problem

Existing multi-view trajectory representation learning methods are trained on single-city data and fail to generalize across urban scenes due to (1) multi-city structural heterogeneity, where road networks, grid layouts, and traffic regulations produce disjoint embedding spaces across cities, and (2) an amplified seesaw phenomenon, where optimizing for one city, view, or task disproportionately degrades performance in others. No prior work addresses stable, task-agnostic representation learning that simultaneously handles multi-city, multi-view, and multi-task settings.

## Input / Output

The method consumes three aligned trajectory views per sample — raw GPS coordinates (lat/lon), road-segment ID sequences from map-matching, and grid-cell indices augmented with POI category embeddings — plus auxiliary city-side statistics (average speed, trajectory length) and trajectory-side features (start/end POI semantics). Trajectories are filtered to 10–100 road segments, 10–100 grid cells, or 10–256 GPS points. The output is a set of task-agnostic multi-view trajectory embeddings (domain-invariant and domain-specific components) that are fine-tuned with lightweight heads for road label classification, travel time regression, destination road prediction, and destination grid prediction.

## Method

SMARTraj² disentangles representations into domain-invariant features (extracted by a shared-weight module across all cities) and domain-specific features (extracted by per-city modules), enforced apart via a soft orthogonality constraint (squared Frobenius norm of H^iᵀH_k^s). A two-level personalized gating mechanism then modulates these features: city-level gates are computed from city-side statistics (speed, trajectory length) concatenated with stop-gradient route embeddings, and trajectory-level gates further refine using start/end POI semantics, both producing sigmoid-scaled gate vectors in [0, γ] (γ=2) applied via element-wise product. View-specific encoders include a hierarchical bidirectional GRU for GPS (point-level then sub-trajectory-level), a GAT-updated road embedding fed into a Transformer for the route view, and a Transformer over POI-augmented grid cells for the grid view. Pre-training is self-supervised with three losses — the orthogonality difference loss, a masked-language-modeling loss over trajectory tokens, and a contrastive loss pulling cross-view positive pairs together — followed by supervised fine-tuning of task-specific heads.

## Results

On the Xi'an dataset, SMARTraj² achieves the best performance across all four downstream tasks: Road Label Micro-F1 of 0.8407, Travel Time MAE of 35.07 s (RMSE 60.92 s), Destination Road Acc@1 of 0.7409, and Destination Grid Acc@1 of 0.6675. Compared to the strongest multi-view baseline MVTraj, SMARTraj² reduces travel time MAE by 36.1% (35.07 vs. 54.90 s) and RMSE by 28.6% (60.92 vs. 85.38 s) in Xi'an, and by 29.3% and 23.8% respectively in Chengdu. On Chengdu, SMARTraj² also leads in Destination Grid Acc@1 (0.8082 vs. 0.7927) and Road Label Micro-F1 (0.7461 vs. 0.7206).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SMARTraj² outperforms all nine baselines on the majority of metrics across both cities. The closest competitor is MVTraj (the prior multi-view method by the same group), which SMARTraj² surpasses on Travel Time MAE/RMSE (35.07/60.92 vs. 54.90/85.38 in Xi'an), Road Label F1 (0.8407 vs. 0.8290), and Destination Grid Acc@1 (0.6675 vs. 0.6630). JGRM is competitive on Destination Road Acc@1 (0.7742 vs. 0.7409 in Xi'an) but cannot perform Destination Grid prediction at all, and trails significantly on Travel Time (87.87 vs. 35.07 MAE). Single-view baselines (Word2Vec, Node2Vec, Transformer, BERT, Toast, JCRLNT, START) are consistently weaker, with JCRLNT showing an anomalous weakness on Destination Road (Acc@1 = 0.5504) despite decent Road Label scores. Notably, SMARTraj² is the only method trained jointly across both cities, whereas all baselines are trained per-city, which the authors attribute as a key factor in its cross-city stability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the multi-view trajectory representation learning line (MVTraj, JGRM) from single-city to multi-city settings, addressing cross-city structural heterogeneity that prior methods ignore. It adopts the soft orthogonality constraint from Domain Separation Networks (Bousmalis et al., NeurIPS 2016) to disentangle domain-invariant and domain-specific features, and combines this with a personalized gating mechanism inspired by mixture-of-experts and domain adaptation literature. The key conceptual departure is framing the multi-city, multi-view, multi-task tension as an 'amplified seesaw phenomenon,' which prior single-city multi-view work does not encounter.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Cross-city generalization is a practical bottleneck for deploying trajectory models in real-world multi-city operations, and this paper provides the first systematic treatment of the multi-city, multi-view, multi-task stability problem. However, validation is limited to two Chinese cities (Chengdu and Xi'an) with similar urban scales, leaving open whether the approach scales to 10+ cities with vastly different infrastructure. The model's large size (477 MB) and dependence on all three views being available simultaneously also limit immediate deployment in data-scarce or single-modality settings.
