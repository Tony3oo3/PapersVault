## Problem

Prior work established convergence rates for clipped gradient descent on convex (L0, L1)-smooth functions, but no corresponding result existed for the stochastic setting without an exponential dependence on L1·R0. The core difficulty is that gradient clipping introduces bias into stochastic gradients, which is far more problematic in the convex regime (where unbiasedness is essential) than in the non-convex regime (where averaging suffices). This paper closes that gap by proving the first high-probability convergence rate for clipped SGD on convex (L0, L1)-smooth functions where L1 appears only additively.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes a stochastic first-order oracle G(x) with E[G(x)|x]=∇f(x) under either bounded (σ-bounded) or sub-Gaussian noise, an initialization x0, and hyperparameters (clipping threshold c, step size ηt, and clipping rule αt). It outputs the average of iterates from non-clipped iterations only (the set T2), which is shown to contain at least T/2 elements with high probability. A distinctive design choice is double sampling: two independent oracle queries are made at each point x_t, one to compute the step size and clipping rule and a second to determine the gradient direction, ensuring the direction remains unbiased conditional on the clipping decision.

## Method

The key mechanism is a double-sampling scheme combined with selective averaging over non-clipped iterations. At each step, the first gradient sample g_t^c determines the clipping rule α_t and step size η_t, while an independent second sample g_t provides the update direction; this decoupling makes the noise term a martingale difference sequence, enabling concentration-based high-probability bounds. The proof shows that clipped iterations (where ‖g_t^c‖ ≥ c) make large per-step progress (η_t α_t Δ_t ≥ Ω(log(T/δ)·R0²/T)), so at most T/2 iterations are clipped and the average over the remaining T2 iterations inherits the full convergence rate. The resulting bound is O(log_+(T/δ)·(L0·R0² + √log(T/δ)·σ·R0·√T)/T) with probability ≥ 1−2δ, matching the L0-smooth SGD rate up to polylog factors with L1 entering only through an additive (L1·R0)² term. The same rate is extended to a clipped adaptive SGD variant (AdaGrad-style stepsizes with clipped gradient norms in the denominator) and to a 'conservative' variant that replaces L1 with a data-free upper bound, eliminating the need to know L1 in advance.

## Results

The main theoretical contribution is a high-probability convergence rate for clipped SGD on convex (L₀, L₁)-smooth functions: with probability at least 1−2δ, the optimality gap is O(log₊(T/δ)(L₀R₀² + √log(T/δ)·σR₀√T)/T), yielding a query complexity of Õ(L₀R²/ε + σ²R₀²/ε² + (L₁R₀)²) that matches the best known SGD bounds in the L₀-smooth case up to polylogarithmic factors. The L₁ dependence appears only additively, with no exponential dependence on L₁R₀. Empirically, on quartic-loss linear regression over California Housing and Parkinson's Telemonitoring, standard, implicit, and adaptive clipping all converge to nearly identical optimality gaps, and clipping provides a more substantial benefit for adaptive SGD than for standard SGD.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares five algorithmic variants (standard clipping, implicit clipping, adaptive clipping, unclipped SGD, and unclipped adaptive SGD) on quartic-loss linear regression. All three clipping methods (standard, implicit, adaptive) exhibit similar convergence dynamics and reach nearly identical final optimality gaps on the California Housing dataset. For standard SGD, the clipped variant performs slightly worse than the unclipped baseline, while for adaptive SGD, clipping yields a more substantial improvement over the unclipped version. An ablation on double sampling (two oracle queries per iteration vs. one) shows no empirical advantage to the double-sampling approach, suggesting it may be unnecessary in practice. Averaging only over unclipped iterations (T₂) achieves slightly better sub-optimality than averaging over all iterates, supporting the paper's algorithmic design choice.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the (L0,L1)-smooth optimization framework introduced by Zhang et al. [44] from the deterministic convex and stochastic non-convex regimes to the previously unaddressed stochastic convex setting. Prior convex results (Gorbunov et al. [11], Vankov et al. [38]) established O(L0·R0²/T) rates for clipped GD with additive L1-dependent terms, but the stochastic case was left open due to the bias that clipping introduces in unbiased gradient estimates. The paper's key technical tool—a double-sampling scheme that decouples the clipping decision from the gradient direction—borrows from Yang et al. [41] for normalized SGD, while the high-probability analysis under light-tailed noise follows the reduction framework of Attia and Koren [1]. Concurrent work by Lobanov and Gasnikov [23] and Yu et al. [42] also tackles stochastic convex (L0,L1)-smooth optimization but retains an implicit exponential dependence on L1·R0 through the maximal gradient norm M, which this paper eliminates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first convergence guarantee for stochastic convex (L0,L1)-smooth optimization whose leading-order term is independent of L1 and whose L1-dependence is purely additive (no exp(L1·R0) factor), matching the best known L0-smooth SGD rates up to polylogarithmic factors. The practical implication is that gradient clipping provides a theoretically justified, parameter-light stabilization mechanism for convex problems with generalized smoothness, and the 'conservative clipping' variant removes the need to know L1 at all. A notable open question is whether the double-sampling technique—essential for the proof but empirically unnecessary—can be eliminated, which would simplify both theory and implementation. Extending the analysis to the more general ℓ-smoothness framework and to tuning-free methods are identified as natural next steps.
