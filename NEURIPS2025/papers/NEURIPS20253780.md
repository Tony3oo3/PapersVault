## Problem

Mixture-of-Experts LLMs introduce fine-grained modularity and dynamic sparsity that existing wafer-scale chiplet architectures handle poorly, as prior designs rely on coarse-grained, static partitioning that assumes dense, uniform computation. This mismatch causes excessive inter-chiplet all-to-all communication, poor memory locality for transient activations, and underutilized compute resources due to uneven expert workloads.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253780/cropped_page3_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework consumes a pre-trained MoE-LLM (Qwen3-30B-A3B, OLMoE-1B-7B-0924, or DeepSeek-MoE-16B) and an instruction tuning dataset (Alpaca, 52K samples) to produce a fine-tuned model, an expert-to-chiplet allocation mapping, and a fine-grained training schedule. Before deployment, a prefilling profiling pass over the tuning dataset extracts per-expert activation frequencies and pairwise co-activation matrices, which drive the clustering and allocation decisions. The target hardware is a 3.5D wafer-scale chiplet system (16 MoE chiplets in 4 switch-connected groups, 1 attention chiplet, 6 HBM2 DRAM modules) synthesized at 28nm and simulated at 1 GHz.

## Method

Mozart is an algorithm-hardware co-design that jointly optimizes expert placement and execution scheduling on a purpose-built 3.5D chiplet architecture. On the algorithm side, it first clusters experts using a farthest-point-sampling-inspired procedure that maximizes intra-cluster co-activation while minimizing inter-cluster co-activation, then solves a binary integer program to assign clusters to chiplet groups for balanced workload; a fine-grained streaming schedule then overlaps DRAM weight loading with on-chip FFN computation by pipelining micro-batches of tokens and prioritizing heavier expert clusters first. On the hardware side, the architecture features a 2.5D NoP-Tree interconnect with attention chiplets as central dispatchers and expert chiplets as leaves, 3D logic-on-memory stacks (compute die bonded face-to-face with an SRAM die via hybrid bonding) for low-latency activation caching, and switch modules with in-network compute that aggregate MoE outputs locally to reduce inter-chiplet traffic. The two-level memory hierarchy stores static weights in distributed DRAM and transient activations in local SRAM, aligning storage with temporal reuse patterns.

## Results

Mozart achieves 1.92× end-to-end post-training speedup on Qwen3-30B-A3B, 2.37× on OLMoE-1B-7B-0924, and 2.17× on DeepSeek-MoE-16B-Base compared to the unoptimized baseline, evaluated on the Alpaca instruction-tuning dataset. The all-to-all communication complexity C_T drops from 8 (top-8 routing baseline) to 5.77 (Qwen3), 5.63 (OLMoE), and 4.32 (DeepSeek) under the full Mozart-C configuration, directly correlating with the latency reductions. At sequence length 512, Mozart-C achieves a 2.34× speedup over baseline, and the advantage grows with longer sequences due to better communication-computation overlap.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against external systems; instead it ablates three algorithmic optimizations incrementally on the same 3.5D chiplet hardware. The baseline (no optimizations) serves as the reference, representing standard expert parallelism with uniform expert placement and no communication-computation overlap. Mozart-A (streaming tokens/experts for overlap only) yields 1.33×, 1.58×, and 1.49× speedups on Qwen3, OLMoE, and DeepSeek respectively, confirming that DRAM-to-chiplet weight streaming is the dominant bottleneck. Mozart-B adds the efficient all-to-all strategy (reducing C_T from 8 to ~6.6–6.8), providing further gains. Mozart-C (all three techniques: expert clustering, all-to-all reduction, and fine-grained scheduling) achieves the full 1.92×–2.37× speedup, with the largest relative improvement on OLMoE (2.37×) where the 64-expert, top-8 routing creates the most communication pressure. The authors note that under SSD-based DRAM (15.8 GB/s), the speedup is more constrained because sequential weight loading dominates, whereas HBM2 (256 GB/s) allows the co-design techniques to take full effect.

## Relation to Prior Work

Mozart builds directly on the expert parallelism pipeline established by Switch Transformers [11] and Tutel [15] (Dispatch → All-to-All → Expert Computing → All-to-All → Combine), and uses MegaBlocks [12] as the standard baseline where all-to-all communication complexity equals k. It departs from prior wafer-scale and chiplet works (FRED [32], Maestro [20], Cambricon-LLM [46]) that assume dense, uniform computation and apply coarse-grained static partitioning; Mozart is the first to explicitly co-design algorithmic optimizations (expert clustering, collaboration-aware allocation, fine-grained streaming) with a 3.5D wafer-scale chiplet topology tailored to the sparsity and dynamic workloads of MoE-LLMs. The expert collaboration concept draws on recent routing-level work (OCCULT [24], C2R [47]) but repurposes it at the hardware-mapping level rather than the routing-policy level.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first algorithm-hardware co-design framework that jointly addresses MoE-specific challenges (sparse activation, uneven expert workloads, all-to-all synchronization) on wafer-scale 3.5D chiplet platforms, yielding 1.9×–2.37× post-training speedups across three open-source MoE-LLMs. The expert-collaboration-based reduction of all-to-all communication complexity (C_T from k≈8 down to ~5.6) is a transferable insight applicable to any chiplet or multi-die MoE deployment. Key limitations include the system being memory-bound despite 3D logic-on-memory integration, the attention module being confined to a single chiplet, and switches becoming bottlenecks under high communication demand. Open questions include scalability to 100B+ parameter MoE models, practical manufacturability of the proposed 3.5D wafer-scale integration at 28nm, and whether the profiling-based expert clustering remains stable as routing policies shift during continued training.
