## Problem

Existing point-cloud neural networks (PointNet, PointNet++, DGCNN) are designed for 3D spatial data and rely on spatially localized neighborhoods, making them poorly suited for high-dimensional point clouds such as single-cell data with tens of thousands of features. Moreover, most methods construct a single nearest-neighbor graph, discarding higher-order geometric and topological structure and failing to disentangle distinct biological processes encoded in different feature subsets. HiPoNet addresses both limitations by modeling high-dimensional point clouds as multi-view simplicial complexes with wavelet-based multi-scale aggregation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254107/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method ingests an entire point cloud (e.g., 61K cells × 30 protein markers for MIBI, or 1.8M cells × mass cytometry features for PDOs) as a single input, producing a dataset-level prediction (treatment response, applied drug, or clinical outcome) rather than per-cell labels. For spatial transcriptomics, spatial coordinates are incorporated as a dedicated view alongside gene-expression-based views. The learned feature-weighting vectors α^(v) are also extracted as interpretable outputs, revealing which markers drive each learned view.

## Method

HiPoNet operates in three stages: (1) V learnable weight vectors α^(v) reweight the feature dimensions via Hadamard product to create V reweighted point clouds, each highlighting a different biological process; (2) each reweighted cloud is converted into a Vietoris-Rips simplicial complex (up to order K) using a differentiable kernelized distance, capturing higher-order interactions beyond pairwise edges; (3) simplicial wavelet transforms—constructed from powers of the Hodge-Laplacian random-walk matrix at dyadic scales—extract multi-scale scattering coefficients (first- and second-order) that serve as band-pass filters over the complex, avoiding the oversmoothing of standard message passing. Scattering coefficients across all simplex orders and views are aggregated into a single vector Φ and passed through an MLP for the final prediction. Theoretically, the framework preserves 0-homology (connected components), geodesic distances, and curvature of the underlying data manifold.

## Results

On the primary single-cell classification task (Table 4), HiPoNet achieves 90.90±4.92% accuracy on Melanoma (MIBI, 54 patients) and 77.38±0.94% on PDO (mass cytometry, 12 patients), outperforming all 12 baselines on Melanoma and ranking second on PDO (behind TopoGNN's 79.90% but with 17× lower variance). On spatial transcriptomics (Table 3), HiPoNet achieves the best AUC-ROC across all six cohort-task combinations, reaching 0.916 on DFCI outcome prediction and 0.732 on Melanoma response. For persistence feature regression (Table 1), HiPoNet attains the lowest MSE (0.633 on Melanoma, 0.405 on PDO), while 3D point-cloud methods (DGCNN, PointNet++, PointTransformer) fail catastrophically with MSE >28.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HiPoNet clearly dominates on the Melanoma task, beating the strongest baseline TopoGNN (88.18%) by ~2.7 points and KNN-GIN (85.45%) by ~5.5 points. On PDO, HiPoNet (77.38%) trails TopoGNN (79.90%) by ~2.5 points but is far more stable (std 0.94% vs 16.15%), while all KNN-based GNNs (GCN, SAGE, GAT, GIN, GraphTransformer) drop to 39–57% and 3D point-cloud methods (PointNet++, DGCNN, PointTransformer) collapse to 30–45%, confirming they are ill-suited for high-dimensional biological point clouds. On spatial transcriptomics (Table 3), HiPoNet achieves the best AUC-ROC in all six settings, with the largest margin on DFCI outcome (0.916 vs 0.820 for KNN-SAGE) and Melanoma response (0.732 vs 0.606 for KNN-GCN). For persistence feature regression (Table 1), HiPoNet's MSE of 0.633/0.405 is roughly 45× lower than the best KNN-GNN (KNN-SAGE at 0.734/1.034) and orders of magnitude better than point-cloud methods (~28+), demonstrating that the simplicial wavelet architecture captures topological structure that graph-only or 3D-geometric methods miss entirely.

## Relation to Prior Work

HiPoNet synthesizes three established threads: (1) simplicial wavelet transforms and scattering coefficients from Madhu et al. (ICML 2024) and Saito et al. (2023/2024), which themselves build on Coifman & Maggioni's diffusion wavelets; (2) the geometric scattering transform framework (Gao et al. 2019, Perlmutter et al. 2023) for multi-scale feature extraction on graphs; and (3) Vietoris-Rips complex construction for encoding higher-order relationships. The key departure from prior work is the combination of learnable multi-view feature reweighting with simplicial (rather than graph) modeling and wavelet-based aggregation, explicitly targeting high-dimensional biological point clouds where 3D-oriented methods (PointNet++, DGCNN, PointTransformer) and single-graph GNNs are ill-suited. The theoretical section further connects heat diffusion on simplicial complexes to 0-homology and geodesic distances via the simplicial graph equivalence, extending results from diffusion maps and Varadhan's formula to the higher-order setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses a concrete gap: modern single-cell and spatial transcriptomics experiments produce high-dimensional point clouds (tens of thousands of features) organized in patient cohorts, a regime where existing 3D point cloud architectures and single-graph GNNs fail. The multi-view design offers biological interpretability (learned weights highlight specific gene markers per view), and the theoretical guarantees that geometry and topology are preserved provide a principled foundation beyond empirical validation. Open questions include the computational cost of constructing simplicial complexes at scale (sensitivity to the VR threshold and kernel bandwidth is acknowledged), whether the manifold hypothesis holds for all biological data, and whether the approach generalizes beyond the specific biological modalities tested. The method's end-to-end differentiability is promising but the practical scalability to very large cohorts remains to be demonstrated.
