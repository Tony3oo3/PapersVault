## Problem

Video LLMs suffer from severe computational inefficiency due to tens of thousands of redundant visual tokens, yet existing token pruning methods leave critical gaps: inner-LLM pruning (e.g., FastV) incurs unavoidable overhead in shallow layers, while outer-LLM pruning addresses only spatial redundancy or limited temporal windows without capturing global temporal dynamics. Moreover, the synergistic potential of combining inner- and outer-LLM strategies has never been systematically explored.

## Input / Output

The method takes a video sampled into a fixed number of frames (32 for LLaVA-OneVision with 196 tokens/frame, 64 for LLaVA-Video with 169 tokens/frame) plus a text prompt, and produces an autoregressive text response. It is entirely training-free and plug-and-play: the visual tokens are compressed by over 90% before entering the LLM, and further reduced within the LLM at a configurable layer, while the text prompt and generation pipeline remain unchanged. The method produces a variable number of tokens per frame after clustering, which complicates batch processing but enables finer-grained compression.

## Method

HoliTom performs holistic token merging across three stages. First, a global redundancy-aware temporal merging uses dynamic programming to find the optimal video segmentation that maximizes the total number of temporally redundant tokens (those whose normalized similarity between consecutive frames exceeds a threshold τ), merging each redundant token into its first occurrence. Second, spatial merging selects salient tokens for non-redundant frames via CLS-attention scoring (or derived attention for SigLIP encoders) and clusters redundant temporal tokens using DPC-KNN density peak clustering, averaging cluster members into representative features. Third, an inner-LLM merging at a chosen layer K ranks visual tokens by the last token's attention scores and merges the lowest-attention tokens into their most similar retained token via averaging rather than discarding, preserving information that would otherwise be lost. This integrated design reduces FLOPs to 6.9% while retaining 99.1% of baseline performance on LLaVA-OneVision-7B.

## Results

On LLaVA-OneVision-7B, HoliTom reduces prefilling FLOPs to 6.9% of the original while maintaining 99.1% average performance across MVBench, EgoSchema, LongVideoBench, and VideoMME (57.9 avg score vs. 58.4 baseline). At a less aggressive 15% token retention, it achieves 99.7% performance at 10.5% FLOPs. The method also delivers a 2.28× reduction in Time-To-First-Token and a 1.32× increase in decoding throughput compared to the vanilla model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HoliTom outperforms all six baselines on both efficiency and performance at the 10% retention ratio. Against inner-LLM methods, FastV (96.7% score, 22.8% FLOPs) and PDrop (96.2%, 25.7%) are dominated: HoliTom achieves 99.1% at only 6.9% FLOPs, a 3.3× FLOPs advantage over FastV. DyCoke (92.6%, 21.3%) suffers the largest performance drop due to its fixed 4-frame grouping, losing 6.3 points in average score relative to HoliTom. Among outer-LLM spatial/temporal methods at the same 8.3% FLOPs, VisionZip drops to 91.6% (a 7.5-point gap) and PruneVid reaches 96.9% (a 2.2-point gap), while HoliTom further reduces FLOPs to 6.9% while scoring 99.1%. FastVID (no public code, results from its paper) reports competitive per-benchmark scores but does not report an average; HoliTom still achieves lower FLOPs (6.9% vs. 8.3%) and higher average performance. Notably, even without the inner-LLM merging module, HoliTom (w/o M) already surpasses all baselines at 99.0% score, demonstrating the strength of the outer-LLM spatio-temporal compression alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate lines of token compression for video LLMs: outer-LLM methods (VisionZip, PruneVid, DyCoke, FastVID) that reduce tokens before the LLM, and inner-LLM methods (FastV, PDrop) that prune within LLM layers. Its key departure is a holistic framework that first applies global redundancy-aware temporal segmentation via dynamic programming (going beyond the fixed-interval or local-window segmentation in DyCoke/FastVID), then combines attention-guided spatial selection with DPC-KNN clustering for the remaining tokens, and finally introduces a similarity-based inner-LLM merging that preserves information rather than discarding tokens as FastV/PDrop do. The paper explicitly positions itself as the first to systematically integrate and analyze the mutual benefits of outer- and inner-LLM pruning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 6.9% FLOPs / 99.1% performance trade-off (14× compute reduction) is a strong practical win for deploying video LLMs at scale, and the training-free, plug-and-play nature lowers adoption barriers. The finding that aggressive token compression can slightly *improve* accuracy over the vanilla model (attributed to noise reduction) challenges the assumption that compression is always lossy, though the margin is modest (~0.3–0.7 points). Key limitations remain: the method targets fixed-length clips (no streaming support), does not optimize vision-tower latency, and the variable token count per frame complicates batched inference. Open questions include extending to online/streaming video, combining with quantization or vision-tower acceleration, and whether the noise-reduction effect generalizes to other modalities or model families.
