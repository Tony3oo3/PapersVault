## Problem

Existing vector quantization methods for extreme low-bit LLM compression (≤3 bits) treat quantization errors as isotropic Euclidean perturbations and allocate bits uniformly across channels, ignoring the non-uniform geometric sensitivity of the parameter space. This leads to significant accuracy degradation because errors along high-sensitivity directions amplify loss, and insensitive channels waste bit budget. No prior framework jointly optimizes error direction and channel-wise bit allocation using the intrinsic Riemannian geometry of the weight manifold.

## Input / Output

The method consumes pre-trained LLM weight matrices (e.g., LLaMA-2/3 7B–70B) and a calibration text corpus sampled from RedPajama to estimate per-channel Fisher Information Matrices via Kronecker-factored gradient outer products. The output is a set of per-group codebooks and a corresponding index matrix that reconstructs the weights at a target average bit-width (2, 3, or 4 bits), with sub-vectors of length 6 and 4 sensitivity-ordered channel groups sharing codebooks within each group.

## Method

RSAVQ builds on product quantization with k-means and introduces two geometry-driven modules. First, Error Direction Sensitivity Guidance (EDSG) defines a projection loss L_project = ‖E + λ·∇̃L‖²_F that penalizes quantization errors deviating from the negative natural gradient direction (−F_W⁻¹∇L), effectively steering errors into low-sensitivity directions on the FIM-induced Riemannian manifold during alternating codebook/index optimization. Second, Weight Channel Sensitivity Guidance (WCSG) computes a per-channel Riemannian curvature energy I_c = ½(∇L_c)ᵀ F_c⁻¹ (∇L_c) and derives an optimal bit allocation b_c ∝ log₂(I_c) via Lagrangian optimization under a fixed total bit budget, then groups channels by descending sensitivity into G uniform groups that share a single codebook. The FIM is approximated through Kronecker factorization (F ≈ F_O ⊗ F_I) to keep computation tractable, and the projection hyperparameter λ (optimal range 0.01–0.1) balances strict directional projection against quantization flexibility.

## Results

On LLaMA-2 7B at 2-bit, RSAVQ achieves a WikiText-2 PPL of 5.97 and 58.66% zero-shot average accuracy, outperforming VPTQ (6.13 PPL, 58.13%) and QuIP# (6.19 PPL, 58.22%). For LLaMA-3 8B at 2-bit, RSAVQ reaches 8.79 PPL and 61.72% zero-shot accuracy, beating VPTQ by 0.5 PPL and 1.5 points in accuracy. At 2-bit on LLaMA-2 70B, RSAVQ achieves 3.55 PPL (vs. FP16's 3.12) with 69.05% zero-shot accuracy, nearly matching the full-precision model while using 8× fewer bits.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RSAVQ consistently outperforms all seven baselines on PPL at 2-bit across LLaMA-2 7B/13B/70B and LLaMA-3 8B/70B. Against the strongest vector quantization baselines VPTQ and QuIP#, RSAVQ achieves 0.16–0.38 PPL improvement and 0.4–1.5 points higher zero-shot accuracy (e.g., LLaMA-3 8B: 8.79 vs. 9.29 PPL and 61.72% vs. 60.22% accuracy vs. VPTQ). Scalar quantization GPTQ collapses at 2-bit (PPL 50.75 on LLaMA-2 7B, 210 on LLaMA-3 8B), confirming the paper's motivation for VQ. Among VQ methods, AQLM and GPTVQ are competitive but use slightly higher effective bit-widths (2.25–2.29 bits) while RSAVQ achieves the best results at exactly 2.0 bits. On LLaMA-2 70B at 2-bit, RSAVQ's 3.55 PPL is within 0.43 of the FP16 baseline (3.12), whereas VPTQ and QuIP# are 0.81 and 0.79 PPL above FP16 respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

RSAVQ builds on the product quantization framework (Jegou et al., 2010) and the k-means-based VQ pipeline of VPTQ (Liu et al., 2024), but departs fundamentally from the Euclidean error model that underpins all prior VQ methods (GPTVQ, AQLM, QuIP#). Its theoretical core draws on Amari's natural gradient (1998) and K-FAC's Kronecker-factored FIM approximation (Martens & Grosse, 2015), repurposing these optimization tools for quantization error control rather than parameter updates. Prior geometric quantization efforts (CLRQ, GLRSQ, MANIQUEANT, FIT, PLRQ) each exploited isolated geometric properties; RSAVQ is the first to unify FIM-based error direction projection and curvature-driven bit allocation within a single VQ framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled geometric explanation for why ultra-low-bit quantization fails: errors along high-curvature (high-FIM-eigenvalue) directions disproportionately degrade model output, and uniform bit allocation wastes budget on insensitive channels. This reframing could inform future quantization, pruning, and sparsity methods beyond VQ. However, the practical gains are modest (0.2–0.4 PPL at 2-bit), validation is limited to LLaMA-family Transformers, and the codebook-lookup overhead of VQ remains a deployment concern on heterogeneous hardware. Open questions include whether the FIM-guided sensitivity metric transfers to scalar quantization, non-Transformer architectures, or multimodal models.
