## Problem

General users lack the ability to flexibly control which regions of a photo appear sharp versus blurred (depth of field), a task that professional photographers handle through aperture and focus adjustments. Existing computational approaches either require specialized hardware (light-field cameras, dual-camera rigs with depth sensors), multi-frame focus stacks, or treat deblurring and reblurring as independent processes, making it difficult to achieve high-quality, user-guided refocusing from a single dual-pixel snapshot.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250551/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a dual-pixel (DP) image pair—two sub-aperture views (B_l, B_r) captured simultaneously by a single sensor—as the primary image input, along with a user prompt (free text, bounding box, or point click) specifying the desired focus region. It produces a refocused image where the prompted region is sharp and surrounding areas exhibit spatially varying defocus blur, plus an intermediate all-in-focus restoration. The self-collected evaluation dataset uses 6720×4480 DP pairs from a Canon EOS 5D Mark IV at f/2.8, while training uses the standard DPD-blur and DP5K datasets at lower resolutions.

## Method

UiD establishes an invertible mapping between blurred DP features and sharp features using a shared pool of N learnable spatially-varying blur kernels, queried via softmax attention over CoC features derived from a stereo-matching disparity estimate. The invertible block combines an LU-decomposed 1×1 convolution (from Glow) with affine coupling layers (from RealNVP), guaranteeing a non-zero Jacobian so that deblurring (forward, using kernel K) and reblurring (backward, using K⁻¹ computed via inverse Fourier transform) are exact inverses trained jointly. For refocusing at test time, the disparity feature is shifted by the mean disparity within a user-specified mask (generated by an early-fusion instructed SAM using BEIT-3 as the multimodal encoder), which modulates the CoC feature and thereby re-selects kernels for the reblurring pass—requiring no explicit refocusing training or depth annotations. Training is mask-free: the network learns deblurring and reblurring with multi-scale Charbonnier, edge, and frequency losses plus CoC regression, and masks are introduced only at inference.

## Results

On the DPD-blur deblurring benchmark, UiD achieves 26.89 PSNR and 0.829 SSIM with only 3.12M parameters and 495 GFLOPs, outperforming all single-image and dual-pixel baselines while being the most computationally efficient. For refocusing on DP5K-test, the method reaches 31.21 PSNR, 0.953 SSIM, and 13.8 LPIPS, surpassing the adapted K3DN† (30.76 PSNR) and Omni-Kernel† (25.32 PSNR). On the out-of-distribution DPD-disp dataset, UiD maintains a lead with 21.93 PSNR and 25.1 LPIPS versus 21.72/29.7 for K3DN†. On the self-collected real-world DP dataset, UiD achieves 19.68 PSNR and 30.87 LPIPS, consistently outperforming both baselines across all metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UiD outperforms all nine baselines on the primary DPD-blur deblurring task. Against the strongest dual-pixel baseline K3DN [57], UiD gains +0.05 dB PSNR (26.89 vs. 26.84) and reduces MAE from 0.35 to 0.33, while using fewer parameters (3.12M vs. 5.00M) and less than half the FLOPs (495G vs. 1033G). Versus the best single-image method Restormer [59], UiD achieves +0.23 dB PSNR and lower MAE (0.33 vs. 0.35), though Restormer edges out on SSIM (0.833 vs. 0.829). For refocusing on DP5K, UiD surpasses the adapted K3DN† by +0.45 dB PSNR and 0.6 LPIPS, and dominates Omni-Kernel† by a large margin (+5.89 dB PSNR, 9.7 LPIPS). On the OOD DPD-disp set, UiD leads K3DN† by +0.21 dB PSNR and 4.6 LPIPS, and Omni-Kernel† by +3.18 dB PSNR and 6.3 LPIPS, demonstrating stronger generalization. On the self-collected real-world dataset, UiD consistently wins on PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS over both K3DN and Omni-Kernel, with the gap most pronounced in LPIPS (30.87 vs. 31.91 and 32.94).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the dual-pixel (DP) defocus deblurring line (Wadhwa et al., K3DN, DPDNet) by reframing refocusing as an invertible deblurring-reblurring problem with a shared spatially-varying kernel, rather than the two-stage GAN approach of RefocusGAN or the dual-camera hardware of DC². The key conceptual departure is the closed-form disparity modulation (Eq. 4/13) that converts a user mask into a target CoC feature without requiring explicit depth annotations or refocused training pairs. For mask generation, the paper adopts an early-fusion instructed SAM (building on EVF-SAM and BEIT-3) rather than end-to-end joint training, decoupling segmentation quality from the deblur-reblur network's optimization dynamics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework makes DoF manipulation accessible to general users via simple text/point/box prompts on widely available DP sensors (DSLR, smartphone), removing the need for specialized light-field cameras or focus stacks. However, the self-collected evaluation dataset is very small (10 pairs), the performance gains over K3DN are modest (e.g., 30.72 vs 30.82 PSNR on DP5K deblurring), and the method is inherently limited to defocus blur from DP sensors. Open questions include whether the invertible shared-kernel formulation generalizes to non-DP inputs, whether the approach scales to video refocusing, and how it compares to emerging diffusion-based restoration frameworks the authors mention as future work.
