## Problem

Existing underwater 3D reconstruction methods (SeaThru-NeRF, WaterSplatting) model scattering medium properties using only viewing direction, ignoring spatially varying scattering in heterogeneous media, and rely on computationally expensive implicit MLPs for medium parameterization. Additionally, COLMAP-based initialization of 3D Gaussian primitives degrades severely under underwater attenuation and scattering, producing sparse and unreliable point clouds that compromise reconstruction quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255199/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view underwater RGB images (RAW-captured, downsampled to ~900×1400) along with COLMAP-estimated camera poses and sparse point clouds, supplemented by pseudo-depth maps from Depth Anything V2 (ViT-L, 518×518 input) for initialization and supervision. It outputs a 3D Gaussian scene representation with an explicit SH-encoded medium field, from which novel-view images, de-scattered (restored) images, and depth maps can be rendered. For restoration, the learned medium parameters are reused to strip scattering via the direct-transmission term only (Eqn. 1), isolating object color from medium contribution.

## Method

Plenodium extends 3DGS with a plenoptic medium representation that parameterizes medium color, object attenuation, and backscatter coefficients via explicit spherical harmonics (degree 3) encoding both ray direction and spatial position, where SH coefficients are stored at the 8 corners of a normalized [-1,1]³ volume and retrieved at arbitrary positions through trilinear interpolation—reducing medium lookup cost to <5% of MLP-based methods. To counter COLMAP's failure under degradation, Pseudo-Depth Gaussian Complementation (PDGC) estimates monocular pseudo-depth via Depth Anything V2, calibrates it with an affine transform (scale + offset) against rendered depth in well-initialized regions, and inserts new Gaussians only in pixels that are both near the camera and have high accumulated transmittance, minimizing background interference. A depth ranking regularized loss penalizes ordinal violations between downsampled pseudo-depth and rendered depth across all pixel pairs (N=16 patches), enforcing geometric consistency without requiring metric depth accuracy. The full rendering equation (Eqn. 5) composites object and medium contributions per Gaussian with exponential attenuation, and training uses a weighted L1 + multi-scale SSIM + depth ranking loss with progressive resolution over 15,000–30,000 steps on an RTX 4090.

## Results

On the SeaThru-NeRF real-world dataset, Plenodium achieves an average PSNR of 30.47 dB and SSIM of 0.923, outperforming the closest competitor WaterSplatting (29.60 dB / 0.918) by 0.872 dB and 0.047 respectively, while rendering at 249 FPS versus 169 FPS (a 47% speedup). On the authors' simulated dataset with controllable scattering media (fog and water at three intensity levels), Plenodium consistently outperforms both SeaThru-NeRF and WaterSplatting, with the largest gains in the hardest degradation settings (e.g., 25.28 dB PSNR on Beach-Fog-Hard vs. 16.44 dB for SeaThru-NeRF). Training time is comparable to WaterSplatting (~7 min on an RTX 4090), while NeRF-based methods require 2–5 hours.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against WaterSplatting (the closest 3DGS-based underwater method), Plenodium wins on PSNR (+0.872 dB average) and SSIM (+0.047 average) across all four real-world scenes, and renders 47% faster (249 vs. 169 FPS) thanks to replacing the implicit MLP medium with explicit SH encoding. However, Plenodium is slightly worse on LPIPS in two of four scenes (e.g., 0.205 vs. 0.203 on IUI3 Red Sea), which the authors attribute to GT noise in background regions. Against SeaThru-NeRF, Plenodium achieves substantially higher PSNR (e.g., 34.12 vs. 30.96 on Curaçao) and SSIM, while being orders of magnitude faster (249 FPS vs. 0.68 FPS); SeaThru-NeRF retains a slight LPIPS edge in some scenes. Against vanilla 3DGS and ZipNeRF (which lack any medium modeling), Plenodium dominates on all quality metrics by large margins (e.g., +7.3 dB PSNR over 3DGS on IUI3 Red Sea), confirming that explicit scattering-medium modeling is essential for underwater reconstruction. On the simulated dataset, Plenodium's advantage over WaterSplatting is most pronounced under hard degradation (e.g., +9.8 dB PSNR on Beach-Fog-Hard), while the gap narrows under easy conditions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the underwater 3D reconstruction line initiated by SeaThru-NeRF (which embedded a scattering medium model into NeRF) and WaterSplatting (which ported that medium model to 3DGS with an MLP-based parameterization). The key departure is replacing the implicit MLP medium representation with an explicit spherical-harmonic-based plenoptic medium representation that encodes both directional and positional information via trilinear interpolation over eight voxel corners, and augmenting 3DGS initialization with pseudo-depth Gaussian complementation from Depth Anything to counter COLMAP's degradation under scattering. The depth ranking regularized loss further distinguishes the optimization from prior 3DGS-based underwater methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 47% rendering speedup and the introduction of a simulated dataset with ground-truth and controllable scattering intensity provide practical tools for benchmarking underwater restoration that were previously unavailable. However, the paper acknowledges that the water model remains an empirical approximation that cannot fully capture truly heterogeneous media, and LPIPS results in some scenes still trail SeaThru-NeRF, suggesting perceptual fidelity in medium regions is an open problem. Broader questions include whether the SH-based medium representation generalizes to strongly anisotropic or time-varying scattering environments beyond the controlled Blender simulations.
