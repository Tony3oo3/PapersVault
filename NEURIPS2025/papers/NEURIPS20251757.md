## Problem

Accurate 3D reconstruction of hand-instrument interactions in ophthalmic microsurgery is critical for objective skill assessment and real-time surgical guidance, yet no large-scale, clinically realistic dataset exists for this task. Existing 3D hand datasets are either general-purpose, synthetic, or too small to capture the fine-grained, dual-hand, multi-instrument motions in a confined surgical workspace, leaving both data and methodological gaps for this domain.

## Input / Output

The annotation pipeline consumes synchronized 8-view RGB-D video at 848×480 and 30 FPS, producing per-frame dense MANO hand meshes (778 vertices, 21 keypoints), 6D instrument poses (rotation + translation), and a 1D articulation factor α∈[0,1] for articulated tools. The baseline networks (H-Net/OH-Net) take a single monocular RGB or RGB-D image resized to 512×512 and regress MANO parameters, 6D instrument pose, articulation, hand/object center heatmaps, and 5-channel instance segmentation masks, with 3D translation recovered via a learned weak-perspective camera model rather than explicit depth.

## Method

The paper introduces a three-stage automatic annotation pipeline: (1) per-view initialization using SAM2 masks, ViTPose 2D keypoints, and ICP-based instrument registration; (2) iterative L-BFGS optimization of MANO parameters minimizing 2D reprojection, differentiable silhouette, 3D point-cloud, temporal smoothness, HMP motion-prior, and biomechanical (joint-angle, bone-length, palm-curvature) losses; and (3) joint hand-instrument refinement adding attraction/repulsion interaction losses and a voxelized SDF penalty to suppress interpenetration. For the benchmarks, H-Net and OH-Net use a ResNet-50 backbone with a collision-aware center-based representation that disentangles bimanual features via a repulsion field, a T-Net module that predicts fine-grained relative translations between hands and instruments from segmentation masks, and a weak-perspective camera head for 3D localization. OH-Net extends H-Net with an instrument branch regressing a 14-channel parameter map (6D pose + 1D articulation per instrument) and is supervised with focal, projection, 3D, silhouette, MANO, segmentation, and tool-specific losses.

## Results

On the hand-instrument interaction benchmark (test set), OH-Net-D achieves 15.94 mm MPJPE, 16.13 mm MPVPE, 26.44 mm MRRTE, 76.31% ADD-S, 9.62% MAE, 5.18 mm penetration, and 17.45 mm MRRTE^{h,o}. The RGB-only OH-Net achieves 17.34 mm MPJPE and 70.79% ADD-S, outperforming the strongest prior method (HOISDF) by 1.42 mm in MPJPE and 9.47 percentage points in ADD-S. On the bimanual hand pose estimation benchmark, H-Net-D reaches 15.97 mm MPJPE, improving over the best prior method (ACR, 18.86 mm) by 2.89 mm. The paper reports improvements of over 2 mm in MPJPE and up to ~23% relative in ADD-S over existing methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OH-Net-D (RGB-D) dominates all seven baselines across every metric on the hand-instrument interaction benchmark. Compared to the strongest prior method HOISDF [65], OH-Net-D improves MPJPE by 1.42 mm (17.36→15.94), MRRTE by 9.44 mm (35.88→26.44), ADD-S by 14.99 percentage points (61.32→76.31), and MRRTE^{h,o} by 6.37 mm (23.82→17.45). Against the weakest baseline Hasson et al. [26], the gains are larger: 4.10 mm in MPJPE and 19.42 percentage points in ADD-S. The RGB-only OH-Net matches HOISDF on MPJPE (17.34 vs 17.36 mm) but substantially outperforms it on ADD-S (70.79% vs 61.32%) and MRRTE (31.58 vs 35.88 mm), demonstrating that joint hand-instrument modeling is the key driver. On the bimanual hand pose benchmark, H-Net-D (15.97 mm MPJPE) beats ACR [90] (18.86 mm) by 2.89 mm, IntagHand [44] (19.38 mm) by 3.41 mm, and DIR [68] (18.89 mm) by 2.92 mm, confirming that domain-specific architectural choices (collision-aware center representation, T-Net relative translation) are critical in the surgical setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) general 3D hand-object interaction reconstruction (Hasson et al. 2019, HOISDF, HFL-Net), which provides the interaction loss formulations and center-based representation; (2) parametric hand modeling with motion priors (MANO, HMP, biomechanical constraints from Spurr et al.), which underpins the annotation pipeline; and (3) surgical vision datasets (AVOS, POV-Surgery, HUP-3D), which are either 2D-only, synthetic, or far smaller in scale. The key departure is that prior surgical 3D datasets are synthetic or limited to single instruments, while general HOI datasets lack the confined workspace, fine-grained motion scale, and multi-instrument articulation characteristic of ophthalmic microsurgery. The proposed H-Net/OH-Net architectures extend the collision-aware center-based representation (Sun et al., ACR) from two-hand to two-hand-two-instrument with articulation, and the annotation pipeline composes existing tools (SAM2, ViTPose, ICP, HMP) into a multi-stage optimization specifically constrained by surgical biomechanics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

OphNet-3D fills a critical gap: it is the first real-world, multi-view RGB-D dataset capturing dynamic two-hand, multi-instrument interactions in ophthalmic surgery at a scale (7.1M frames, 40 surgeons) that enables meaningful train/val/test splits and generalization studies. This directly supports the broader goal of objective, contactless surgical skill assessment, reducing reliance on expert supervision in training. Key limitations include single-center data (limiting generalizability), instrument-tip overexposure from microscope illumination, and the absence of joint reconstruction with the ocular surface from the microscope view. Open questions include whether the annotation pipeline transfers to other microsurgical domains (e.g., neurosurgery, orthopedic), whether real-time inference is achievable for intraoperative guidance, and how multi-center variability affects model robustness.
