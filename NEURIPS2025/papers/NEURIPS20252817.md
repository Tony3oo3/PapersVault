## Problem

Transformer-based models for long-term time series forecasting fail to benefit from longer lookback windows due to information redundancy, attention dispersion, and overfitting to noise, yet no prior work has quantified or systematically addressed this limitation. The paper introduces the Maximum Effective Window (MEW) metric to measure how much historical context a model can actually exploit, and proposes model-agnostic modules to extend it.

## Input / Output

The method takes a multivariate time series of length L (up to 1024 timesteps, substantially longer than the 336-512 typical in prior work) and produces T future values (T in {96, 192, 336, 720}). It operates channel-independently: each variate is treated as a separate univariate sequence, segmented into overlapping or non-overlapping patches of length P, then embedded into a token sequence of dimension d. The output is a flattened linear projection of the Transformer's final representation.

## Method

PIH (Patch-IBF-HTM) integrates two model-agnostic modules into PatchTST. The Information Bottleneck Filter (IBF) scores each patch's importance via an MLP, samples a Bernoulli mask through Gumbel-Sigmoid relaxation, and injects Gaussian noise into low-importance tokens (z^noise = lambda*z + (1-lambda)*epsilon), trained with a variational IB objective combining a prediction loss and a compression loss. The Hybrid-Transformer-Mamba (HTM) module first processes the full patch sequence with Mamba for selective long-range filtering, then splits the noise-reduced sequence into K shorter subsequences via interval split (preserving global periodicity) or block split (preserving local periodicity), which are processed by the Transformer. This hybrid design exploits Mamba's linear-time efficiency on long sequences and the Transformer's strong short-sequence modeling, reducing the effective token length from L/P to L/(PK) while retaining semantic structure.

## Results

With a lookback window of L=1024, PIH achieves the best MSE and MAE across nearly all 7 datasets and 4 prediction lengths (T∈{96,192,336,720}). For example, on ETTh1 at T=720, PIH achieves MSE=0.435 and MAE=0.466, outperforming PatchTST (MSE=0.482) and DLinear (MSE=0.511). On Electricity at T=96, PIH reaches MSE=0.127, MAE=0.220, beating PatchTST (0.133/0.226) and NLinear (0.133/0.229). The MEW metric confirms the architectural improvement: PIH extends PatchTST's MEW from 512 to 1024 (and up to 1440 on ETTm1), while vanilla Transformer's MEW increases from 72 to 144.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PIH outperforms all 9 baselines on the majority of dataset–horizon combinations. Against PatchTST (Nie et al., ICLR 2023), PIH consistently wins at L=1024 (e.g., ETTh1 T=720: 0.435 vs. 0.482 MSE), though at L=96 the two are nearly tied, suggesting the IBF/HTM modules matter most for longer windows. Against the Mamba-based baselines S-Mamba (Wang et al., 2024) and Bi-Mamba+ (Liang et al., 2024), PIH achieves lower MSE across all 7 datasets, with the gap widening at longer horizons (e.g., ETTh2 T=720: 0.378 vs. 0.431 and 0.421). Against the sparse-attention Transformers Autoformer (Wu et al., NeurIPS 2021), Informer (Zhou et al., AAAI 2021), and FEDformer (Zhou et al., ICML 2022), PIH shows substantial margins, particularly on ETTm2 where Informer degrades to MSE≈3.0 while PIH stays at 0.342. Against the linear baselines DLinear and NLinear (Zeng et al., 2022)—which were specifically designed to handle long windows—PIH still wins on most settings, confirming that the hybrid Transformer-Mamba architecture with information bottleneck filtering provides superior representational capacity. Against iTransformer (Liu et al., 2023), PIH achieves lower MSE on Electricity and Traffic (Table 7), and against the vanilla point-based Transformer (Vaswani et al., 2017), PIH improves both MEW (72→144) and MSE (Table 2). Statistical significance testing (p<0.05) confirms PIH's improvements over PatchTST on 5 of 7 datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on PatchTST's patch-based Transformer for time series forecasting and extends it with two model-agnostic modules: an Information Bottleneck Filter (IBF) grounded in variational IB theory (Alemi et al., 2016) for adaptive subsequence selection, and a Hybrid-Transformer-Mamba (HTM) architecture that leverages Mamba's selective state-space mechanism for long-range processing while retaining Transformer attention for short-range dependencies. The HTM design follows the broader trend of hybrid Transformer-SSM architectures (Jamba, Block-State Transformers) but applies it specifically to time series with sequence-splitting strategies inspired by SCINet's downsampling. The paper departs from the purely heuristic patch strategy by introducing an information-theoretic criterion for identifying redundant subsequences, and from prior Mamba-for-time-series work (Bi-Mamba+, S-Mamba, MambaTS) by combining Mamba with Transformer rather than replacing it.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The MEW metric provides a useful diagnostic for quantifying how effectively a model exploits its lookback window, and the finding that PIH extends PatchTST's MEW from 512 to 1024 while maintaining consistent performance gains is practically significant for non-LLM time series forecasting. The model-agnostic nature of IBF and HTM means they can be integrated into existing architectures (demonstrated on Transformer, Informer, Autoformer, PatchTST, and iTransformer), broadening their applicability. Open questions include whether a scaling law exists for lookback windows, whether adaptive end-to-end splitting can replace the heuristic interval/block splits, and whether these modules can be integrated into LLM-based time series models to push window sizes even further.
