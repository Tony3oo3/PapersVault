## Problem

Existing sensitive information recognition (SIR) benchmarks are limited to single regulations, single languages, and lack coverage of adversarial format transformations that attackers use to evade data leakage prevention systems. No comprehensive dataset exists to evaluate how well SIR models can detect sensitive data after it has been encoded, obfuscated, or otherwise format-transformed, leaving a critical gap in assessing real-world DLP robustness.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251321/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark takes individual text samples (original or format-transformed) from 26 sensitive categories as input, with LLMs additionally receiving structured prompts at three information levels: no category/format hints, category names only, or category names plus transformation logic and examples. Outputs are a predicted sensitive data category label (one of 26) and, for LLMs, a restored original data string. Evaluation uses 5 rounds of random sampling (5 samples per category per round), reporting Label Recognition Accuracy (LRAcc) globally and Data Restoration Accuracy (DRAcc) for LLMs, with per-category Precision/Recall/F1 computed as binary classification.

## Method

DataSIR is constructed by first identifying 26 sensitive data categories that appear in at least two of five major international regulations (HIPAA, SOX, GDPR, CCPA, PIPL), then collecting 131,890 original samples in Chinese and English with national-difference variants. Twenty-one format transformations—ranging from numeric encodings (binary, octal, hex, ASCII, Unicode, UTF-8, Base64) to linguistic obfuscations (Morse, Braille, acrostic poetry, character decomposition, Martian text, simplified-to-traditional mapping) to insertion-based perturbations (special characters, Chinese characters, English letters/numbers)—are applied selectively per category (8–14 applicable transformations each), expanding the dataset to 1,647,501 samples. The benchmark evaluates four NLP tools (HanLP, spaCy, NLTK, Presidio) and four LLMs (DeepSeek-V3, Qwen3-235B, Gemini-2.5-Flash, GPT-4.1) in a zero-shot, black-box setting with temperature=0 and JSON-constrained outputs, using three prompt conditions to isolate the effect of prior knowledge on recognition accuracy.

## Results

On the DataSIR benchmark, traditional NLP tools (HanLP, spaCy, NLTK, Presidio) achieve overall Label Recognition Accuracy (LRAcc) below 5%, with Presidio leading at 4.93% and NLTK at just 0.56%. In stark contrast, LLMs with full prompt information (26 sensitive categories + 21 format transformation descriptions) reach 54–65% LRAcc, with Gemini achieving the highest at 65.04% and GPT-4.1 at 64.30%. Without any label or format hints in the prompt, LLM performance collapses to 4–7% LRAcc, comparable to NLP tools, demonstrating that contextual prompt engineering is critical for recognition. Gemini's Data Restoration Accuracy (DRAcc) on original data reaches 95.08%, dropping to 75.26% on all format-transformed data combined.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates four NLP tools and four LLMs on the DataSIR benchmark. All NLP tools (HanLP 4.91%, spaCy 2.98%, NLTK 0.56%, Presidio 4.93% overall LRAcc) perform poorly, with Presidio being the best NLP tool due to its integration of spaCy and rule-based engines covering 12 of 26 categories. LLMs dramatically outperform NLP tools: with full prompt information, Gemini (65.04%) and GPT-4.1 (64.30%) exceed 60% LRAcc, while DeepSeek (54.37%) and Qwen (55.97%) remain in the mid-50s. Among LLMs, Gemini consistently leads across all three prompt conditions, particularly in the full-information setting where it surpasses GPT-4.1 by ~0.7 points and DeepSeek by ~10.7 points. The critical finding is that without label and format information in the prompt, all LLMs drop to 4–7% LRAcc—essentially matching NLP tool performance—highlighting that the dataset's differentiation power depends heavily on the information provided to the model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of regulatory compliance and adversarial text robustness. Prior sensitive-information datasets (SPEDAC for GDPR, HealthDeID for HIPAA) are single-regulation, single-language, and single-format, while adversarial-text benchmarks (TextBugger, DarkBERT) focus on character-level perturbations rather than the full spectrum of real-world encoding-based evasion. DataSIR departs from both lines by simultaneously covering five major international regulations (HIPAA, SOX, GDPR, CCPA, PIPL), two languages, and 21 format transformations drawn from empirical DLP threat analyses, positioning itself as the first benchmark specifically designed to evaluate recognition robustness under adversarial format obfuscation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The quantified finding that all four mainstream NLP tools (HanLP, spaCy, NLTK, Presidio) achieve under 5% label-recognition accuracy on format-transformed data exposes a critical blind spot in current DLP deployments, while the 60%+ LRAcc achieved by prompted LLMs (especially Gemini at 65%) provides a concrete, actionable path forward for integrating LLMs into data-leakage prevention pipelines. Key limitations remain: the dataset lacks contextual information (e.g., distinguishing a doctor's name from a patient's), covers only Chinese and English, and the 26 categories, while representative, do not exhaust all sensitive types. Open questions include whether context-aware LLM agents can close the remaining 35–45% recognition gap, whether the benchmark generalizes to additional languages and transformation types, and how to balance the precision-recall trade-offs the paper identifies across categories (e.g., Religion at 99% precision / 31% recall vs. Mobile Number at 27% precision / 80% recall).
