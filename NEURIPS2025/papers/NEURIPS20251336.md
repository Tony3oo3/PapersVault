## Problem

LLM-based audio-visual speech recognition (AVSR) suffers from token hunger, requiring dense input representations that are computationally expensive for long speech sequences. Existing token compression methods fix a single compression rate in advance, while Matryoshka-based approaches support multiple granularities but train each scale independently, limiting cross-scale knowledge transfer and robustness at high compression. No prior work unifies the scale-aware adaptability of Matryoshka Representation Learning (MRL) with the sparse efficiency of Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) in a single framework.

## Input / Output

The model takes an audio waveform and a corresponding lip-movement video, encoded by Whisper (audio) and AV-HuBERT (video) into token sequences, which are then compressed via average pooling at multiple rates (e.g., audio rates {4, 16} and video rates {2, 5} for AVSR, yielding four configurations). A single set of model weights handles all compression rates at inference time, allowing the user to select the audio-visual token budget based on available compute. The output is a free-text transcription generated autoregressively by the frozen Llama 3 backbone, with beam search (width 15, temperature 0.6) for decoding.

## Method

MoME inserts sparse Mixture-of-Experts modules in parallel with the FFN, MHSA, or entire Transformer layer of a frozen LLM, keeping the backbone fixed and training only the lightweight expert parameters. Each MoME module contains N_r routed experts (activated via top-k selection by a shared linear router) and N_s shared experts (always active), all with small bottleneck dimensions to minimize active parameters. The critical design choice is that the router and experts are shared across all Matryoshka token granularities, so the router consistently activates similar expert subsets at different compression rates, enabling compressed sequences to reuse representational capacity learned from richer, lower-compression inputs. Training minimizes the averaged autoregressive next-token prediction loss over all G×L audio-visual scale combinations plus a load-balancing loss to prevent router collapse. This cross-scale expert alignment, combined with shared experts encoding scale-invariant knowledge, yields significantly less WER degradation at high compression rates compared to independently trained or scale-specific baselines.

## Results

On LRS2, MoME-23/4-LAYER achieves 2.7% WER at the finest granularity (4,2) and 4.2% at the most aggressive compression (16,5), compared to 4.1% and 8.1% for Llama-AVSR, while activating only 12.7M parameters versus 27.5M. On LRS3, MoME-23/4-MHSA reaches 1.8% WER at (4,2) and 2.9% at (16,5), outperforming Llama-AVSR (2.4% and 4.1%) with just 3.5M active parameters. Reducing the expert bottleneck to 1 yields only 2.3M (LRS2) and 0.9M (LRS3) active parameters with minimal WER degradation (e.g., 4.7% vs. 4.2% on LRS2 at (16,5)). Under noisy conditions (−5 dB SNR), MoME-23/4-LAYER achieves 32.6% WER on LRS2 versus 41.8% for Llama-AVSR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MoME consistently outperforms Llama-AVSR [14] across all compression rates on both LRS2 and LRS3, with the largest margins at aggressive compression: 4.2% vs. 8.1% WER on LRS2 at (16,5) and 2.9% vs. 4.1% on LRS3 at (16,5), while using roughly half the active parameters (12.7M vs. 27.5M). Against the Matryoshka baselines Llama-MTSK (MS, SS, MSS) [23], MoME wins on every rate for both datasets; for example, on LRS3 at (4,2), MoME-23/4-LAYER achieves 1.5% WER versus 2.3–2.6% for all three MTSK variants. In the broader SOTA comparison (Figure 1, LRS3 with Llama 3.1-8B and Whisper-medium), MoME matches or beats UniVPM [93], USR [94], Whisper-Flamingo [77], Auto-AVSR [78], AV-HuBERT [73], and MMS-Llama [29] in WER while activating significantly fewer parameters and requiring fewer training hours under a single set of weights. Under noisy conditions (Table 2), MoME-23/4-LAYER outperforms both Llama-AVSR and Llama-MTSK MS at every SNR level, with the gap widening at −5 dB (32.6% vs. 41.8% and 44.9% WER). The only trade-off is a modest ~1.25× increase in inference time relative to Llama-AVSR (2.98s vs. 2.39s), attributed to router overhead, which the authors argue is justified by the gains in parameter efficiency, noise robustness, and elastic inference capability.

## Relation to Prior Work

MoME sits at the intersection of two recent lines of work: Matryoshka-based elastic inference for LLMs (Cai et al., ICLR 2025; Cappellazzo et al., Llama-MTSK 2025) and sparse Mixture-of-Experts (Shazeer et al. 2017; DeepSeekMoE 2024). The direct predecessor, Llama-MTSK, already applied MRL to AVSR by training a single LLM across multiple token granularities, but treated each scale independently with monolithic LoRA adapters. MoME departs from this by inserting sparse MoE modules in parallel with frozen LLM layers, using a shared router across all scales to enforce cross-granularity expert alignment, and employing extremely small bottleneck experts (down to dimension 1) to keep active parameters minimal. This is, to the authors' knowledge, the first framework to unify MRL and MoE paradigms in a single architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MoME offers a practical path toward resource-adaptive AVSR deployment: a single set of weights that can be tuned at inference time to trade off accuracy against compute, without retraining per compression rate. The significant parameter reduction (as low as 0.9M active MoME parameters vs. 27.5M for Llama-AVSR) and demonstrated noise robustness make it relevant for edge and memory-constrained settings. Open questions include the inherent MoE memory overhead (all experts must reside in memory even if sparsely activated), the modest inference-time overhead from router dispatching, and whether the cross-scale alignment mechanism generalizes to other multimodal tasks beyond speech. The paper also notes it is limited to audio-visual speech and leaves broader multimodal extension as future work.
