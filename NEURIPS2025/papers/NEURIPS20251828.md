## Problem

Existing attention analysis operations—row selection, column selection, and column summing—only capture direct (first-order) attention effects between tokens, ignoring indirect attention paths that propagate through intermediate tokens. This limits their ability to identify globally important tokens, produce clean segmentation maps, or rank token importance, since the attention mechanism inherently models only pairwise query-key interactions without accounting for multi-hop influence.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251828/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on individual self-attention (or hybrid-attention) matrices extracted from pretrained vision transformers (DINO, DINOv2, CLIP, FLUX, Stable Diffusion), requiring square matrices where sequence lengths match. It produces: (1) multi-bounce attention maps for segmentation, (2) TokenRank vectors (steady-state distributions over tokens) for global importance ranking, and (3) λ₂-weighted head aggregations. The power method typically converges in 10–20 iterations, making the approach lightweight relative to the quadratic-size attention matrices it processes.

## Method

The paper reinterprets each softmax attention matrix as a right-stochastic transition matrix of a discrete-time Markov chain, where tokens are states and attention weights are transition probabilities. Three new operations follow from this: (1) Multi-bounce attention iterates the power method (v_{n+1}^T = v_n^T A) starting from a one-hot vector to capture n-th order indirect attention, consolidating metastable states formed by semantically similar regions while dissipating noise; (2) TokenRank is the steady-state vector of the chain (analogous to PageRank), computed with a teleportation term to guarantee irreducibility, yielding a global importance ranking that accounts for all indirect attention paths; (3) λ₂-weighted head averaging uses the second-largest eigenvalue magnitude as a proxy for the number of metastable states, preferentially weighting heads with more structured attention patterns. The key insight is that semantically coherent regions form metastable states where the chain lingers, so iterating transitions sharpens segmentation boundaries and elevates truly important tokens over those with merely high direct attention.

## Results

On the ImageNet zero-shot segmentation benchmark (4,276 images, 445 categories), the proposed Attention Chains method using multi-bounce attention (n=2, outgoing) with λ₂-weighted head averaging achieves 84.12% accuracy, 70.20 mIoU, and 94.29 mAP, setting a new state of the art. The mAP improvement over the previous best (Concept Attention, 90.45) is particularly notable as it is threshold-agnostic. For unconditional image generation via SAG, replacing column-sum with TokenRank improves IS from 17.69 to 18.37 and reduces FID from 52.48 to 50.14 over 50K generated images. On COCO-Stuff-27, using TokenRank to sample anchors for DiffSeg boosts mACC from 72.50 to 84.97.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's method outperforms all 12 baselines on the ImageNet segmentation mAP metric, with the largest gap over the previous state-of-the-art Concept Attention (94.29 vs. 90.45 mAP, a +3.84 improvement). On mIoU, the method is competitive but slightly below Concept Attention (70.20 vs. 71.04), while on accuracy it leads all methods (84.12 vs. 83.07). Against the simpler FLUX row-select and column-select baselines (which correspond to single-bounce operations), the multi-bounce approach gains +10.16 and +3.67 mAP respectively, confirming that higher-order attention propagation is the key driver. For SAG-based generation, TokenRank improves over the original column-sum strategy by +0.68 IS and −2.34 FID, though both remain worse than vanilla SD1.5 on FID (50.14 vs. 45.77), indicating the guidance trades some distributional fidelity for structural quality. In the token-masking experiment, TokenRank consistently produces the largest accuracy degradation (lowest AUC) across ViT, CLIP, DINOv1, and DINOv2 families, outperforming Column Sum, Center Token, CLS Token, and random masking, validating its ability to identify globally important tokens.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Markov chain interpretation of transformers initiated by Ildiz et al. (2024), who modeled a single-layer transformer as a Markov chain, but extends it to individual self-attention matrices in large pretrained vision transformers. It unifies previously ad-hoc attention operations (row-select, column-select, column-sum, cross-layer multiplication) as single-bounce transitions in a DTMC, then extends them to multi-bounce attention that captures indirect attention paths through intermediate tokens. The TokenRank concept is directly inspired by PageRank (Page et al., 1999), and the λ₂-based head weighting draws on DTMC mixing-time theory (Langville & Meyer, 2004), neither of which had been applied to attention head aggregation before.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The DTMC lens provides a principled, lightweight framework (power method converging in 10–20 iterations) for extracting global token importance and sharper segmentation signals from attention, with practical gains across zero-shot segmentation, unconditional generation, and token masking. Key limitations include restriction to square (self-attention) matrices, computational cost of λ₂ computation, and the observation that gains are larger for transformers with more structured feature spaces (e.g., DINOv2 with registers). Open questions include extending the formulation to cross-attention blocks (via dummy states), whether the metastable-state interpretation generalizes to video, audio, or NLP transformers, and whether TokenRank can serve as a general-purpose head-importance signal beyond segmentation.
