## Problem

Black-box LLMs (e.g., GPT-4o, Gemini) lack accessible parameters, making it impossible to fine-tune them for advanced capabilities like multi-step reasoning, long-horizon planning, and personalization. Existing approaches—ICL-based prompt engineering and adapter-based best-of-N selection—rely on static heuristics or the model's intrinsic generation quality, and critically lack the ability to interact with an environment and iteratively refine guidance over multiple turns. This paper asks: can a small, trainable white-box LLM be optimized to produce intermediate guidance that steers a black-box LLM toward better outputs through iterative environmental feedback?

## Input / Output

The system takes a text task description x (e.g., a math word problem, a planning goal, or a user query with historical records) as input. The white-box controller (LLaMA-3-8B-Instruct or Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct) generates a sequence of T intermediate guidance tokens—decomposed sub-problems for reasoning, high-level action plans for planning, or user profile summaries for personalization—which are concatenated with the original input and fed to the black-box LLM (GPT-4o-mini or GPT-3.5-turbo). The final output is the black-box LLM's prediction ŷ, whose correctness (via an oracle eval function) serves as the reward signal for training the controller. For multi-turn settings, the state accumulates (action, observation) pairs across T outer-loop rounds.

## Method

M-Pilot formulates a controller-generator framework as a Markov Decision Process: the white-box LLM is the policy (controller) and the black-box LLM is the environment, with the final answer's correctness as the reward. Training proceeds in two phases: (1) supervised fine-tuning (behavior cloning) on guidance generated by a stronger model (GPT-3.5) to initialize the controller, and (2) Iterative Direct Preference Optimization (IDPO), where at each iteration m the current model θ^(m) samples K×T trajectories via multi-turn interaction with the black-box environment, positive/negative pairs are collected based on the eval signal, and the model is updated via a DPO loss whose reference policy is set to θ^(m) (the previous iteration's model), enabling progressive self-improvement. Following STaR, the dataset is bootstrapped by accumulating trajectories across iterations, and the KL-regularized optimal policy derivation from DPO yields a closed-form ratio of log-probabilities between successive iterations (Eq. 10). The framework is plug-and-play: a controller trained on one black-box model transfers to others (e.g., GPT-3.5-turbo, Gemini-1.5-flash) without retraining, and the specific RL objective (IDPO vs. SimPo) is swappable without altering the architecture.

## Results

On the LaMP personalization benchmark, M-Pilot (using gpt-4o-mini as the black-box generator) achieves 0.640 accuracy on LaMP-1, 0.823 on LaMP-2N, 0.527 on LaMP-2M, 0.277 MAE on LaMP-3, and 4.298 BLEU on LaMP-4, outperforming all baselines except RAG(k=4) on LaMP-3. On GSM8K reasoning, M-Pilot reaches 93.1% accuracy (gpt-3.5) and 96.4% (gpt-4o-mini), surpassing the strongest baseline PAL-Self-Debug (86.4%/94.3%) by 6.7%. On ALFWorld planning, M-Pilot achieves a 96.27% overall success rate, exceeding AdaPlanner (88.06%) by 8.21%. The paper reports average improvements of 3.19% (reasoning), 7.46% (planning), and 5.82% (personalization) over the best baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

M-Pilot consistently outperforms all 14 baselines across the three task domains, with the most dramatic gains in planning (ALFWorld: 96.27% vs. AdaPlanner's 88.06%, a +8.21% margin) and reasoning (GSM8K: 93.1% vs. PAL-Self-Debug's 86.4%, a +6.7% margin). On LaMP personalization, M-Pilot beats RAG(k=4) by 3.1% on LaMP-2N accuracy and by 1.35 BLEU points on LaMP-4, though RAG(k=4) edges out M-Pilot by 0.005 MAE on LaMP-3. Against the strongest planning baselines, M-Pilot achieves 100% on Pick, Heat, and Examine tasks (vs. AdaPlanner's 100%, 78.26%, and 66.67% respectively), while matching AdaPlanner on Pick and Clean. In the appendix comparisons, M-Pilot surpasses Fermi by 9.2% on LaMP-2M and LEAP by 15.7% on GSM8K, and outperforms Bbox-Adapter by 18.2% on GSM8K. The plug-and-play experiments (Table 2) confirm that the trained controller transfers to gpt-3.5-turbo and gemini-1.5-flash without retraining, maintaining its advantage over baselines on both models.

## Relation to Prior Work

M-Pilot builds directly on DPO (Rafailov et al., 2024) by deriving its Iterative DPO (IDPO) loss from the KL-regularized optimal policy formulation, and on STaR (Zelikman et al., 2022) for the iterative dataset bootstrapping procedure. Conceptually, it departs from prior black-box LLM enhancement methods (ICL-based prompting, adapter-based best-of-N selection, and RL-based prompt optimization like RLPrompt/TEMPERA) by reframing the problem as a Markov Decision Process where a trainable white-box LLM acts as a policy/controller and the black-box LLM serves as the environment, enabling multi-turn feedback-driven generation rather than single-shot prompting or static candidate selection. The controller-generator architecture with iterative reference-policy updates distinguishes it from prior small-LM-drives-LLM approaches (SuperICL, HYDRA) that rely on fixed or discrete prompt modifications.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play capability—training the white-box controller on one black-box model (e.g., gpt-4o-mini) and transferring it to others (gpt-3.5-turbo, gemini-1.5-flash) without retraining—makes the framework practically attractive for organizations locked into specific API providers. The 30% API cost reduction on ALFWorld while improving success rate highlights a practical efficiency benefit beyond raw accuracy gains. Key limitations include the dependence on an oracle evaluation function (ground-truth answers or environment success signals), which restricts applicability to tasks with verifiable outputs, and the added inference latency from the controller-generator pipeline. Open questions include whether the approach scales to truly open-ended generation without clear evaluation signals, and whether the self-improvement loop converges or plateaus over many iterations.
