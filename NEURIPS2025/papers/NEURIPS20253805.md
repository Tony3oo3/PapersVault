## Problem

Existing LoRA variants address either adaptive rank allocation or nonzero initialization in isolation, and each introduces practical drawbacks: adaptive rank methods (e.g., AdaLoRA) inflate trainable parameters and cap the rank upper bound, while nonzero initialization methods (e.g., PiSSA, LoRA-GA) require manipulating pre-trained weights, creating a training-inference gap that complicates deployment. No prior method unifies both aspects within a single framework that preserves LoRA's usability and minimal checkpoint storage.

## Input / Output

GoRA consumes pre-trained model weights, a small subset of training samples (for N-batch gradient accumulation before formal training), and a reference rank r^ref that sets the total trainable parameter budget. It outputs per-layer low-rank adapter pairs (A₀, B₀) with layer-specific ranks r^i bounded by [r^min, r^max], where the total trainable parameter count stays within ~10% of standard LoRA. Unlike prior nonzero-init methods, the resulting adapters can be merged directly into W₀ without any residual correction term, eliminating the training-inference gap.

## Method

GoRA reframes LoRA adapters as gradient compressors and leverages pre-computed N-batch accumulated gradients G to drive both rank allocation and initialization. For rank allocation, it computes a sensitivity-based importance score I(W) = avg(|W ⊙ G|) per layer, normalizes these into advantages, and allocates ranks proportionally under a fixed parameter budget derived from the reference rank, all before training begins to avoid dynamic shape changes. For initialization, A is drawn from Kaiming uniform as in standard LoRA, while B is set to B₀ = -(A₀ᵀA₀)⁻¹A₀ᵀG (scaled by γ√m/α), making A₀B₀ the optimal low-rank projection of G onto Col(A₀) and effectively performing one gradient-descent step at the start of training. The forward computation uses the rsLoRA-style scaling α/√r to better exploit the higher ranks obtained through adaptive allocation, and the entire initialization adds negligible overhead (under 4 seconds for an 8B model at rank 128).

## Results

On Llama-3.1-8B-Base fine-tuned for mathematical reasoning (GSM8k), GoRA achieves 72.91% accuracy, a 5.13-point improvement over standard LoRA (67.78%) and 1.52 points above LoRA-GA (71.39%). On code generation (HumanEval), GoRA reaches 48.98% PASS@1, surpassing the best baseline rsLoRA (45.78%) by 3.20 points. Under high-rank settings (r_ref=128), GoRA achieves 75.74% on GSM8k and 52.03% on HumanEval, outperforming full fine-tuning (73.69% and 51.63%) by 2.05 and 0.40 points respectively. On the GLUE benchmark with T5-Base, GoRA's average score of 87.96 exceeds full fine-tuning (87.91) and all low-rank baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GoRA consistently outperforms all nine baselines on the primary Llama-3.1-8B-Base experiments. Against the strongest convergence-optimization baseline LoRA+ (6.35 MTBench, 71.29 GSM8k, 44.51 HumanEval), GoRA wins on GSM8k by 1.62 points and HumanEval by 4.47 points, while trailing by a negligible 0.01 on MTBench. Against the strongest initialization baseline LoRA-GA (71.39 GSM8k, 43.29 HumanEval), GoRA leads by 1.52 and 5.69 points respectively, and also surpasses it on MTBench (6.34 vs. 5.99). The adaptive-rank baseline AdaLoRA (70.63 GSM8k, 41.46 HumanEval) is outperformed by 2.28 and 7.52 points, confirming that GoRA's pre-training rank allocation is more effective than AdaLoRA's dynamic masking. On image classification (CLIP-ViT-B/16), GoRA achieves 89.47% average accuracy, beating LoRA-Pro (89.20%), LoRA-GA (88.51%), and full fine-tuning (88.06%). The only marginal loss is on MTBench where LoRA+ edges GoRA by 0.01 points, which is within the reported standard deviation.

## Relation to Prior Work

GoRA extends the LoRA framework (Hu et al., ICLR 2022) by unifying two previously separate lines of improvement—adaptive rank allocation (as in AdaLoRA, Zhang et al., ICLR 2023) and gradient-driven initialization (as in LoRA-GA, Wang et al., NeurIPS 2024)—into a single gradient-based mechanism. It departs from AdaLoRA by performing rank allocation before training (avoiding dynamic shape changes and extra parameter overhead) and from LoRA-GA by using a pseudo-inverse of the randomly initialized A matrix to set B, thereby eliminating the need to manipulate pre-trained weights and the resulting training-inference gap. The importance metric (sensitivity-based avg(|W⊙G|)) is borrowed from the pruning literature (PLATON, Zhang et al., ICML 2022), and the scaling factor modification follows rsLoRA (Kalajdzievski, 2023).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GoRA offers a practical, drop-in upgrade to LoRA that simultaneously addresses rank allocation and initialization without increasing trainable parameters or introducing a training-inference gap—two problems that prior methods handled in isolation. The finding that gradient-driven initialization with a simple pseudo-inverse matches or exceeds SVD-based methods (LoRA-GA, PiSSA) suggests that the expensive decomposition step may be unnecessary. Open questions include whether the approach scales to 70B+ models, whether it generalizes to multimodal and vision-language settings, and whether the gradient compressor perspective can inform other PEFT variants like DoRA. The paper's evaluation is limited to single-epoch training on models up to 32B, leaving longer-training and larger-scale behavior untested.
