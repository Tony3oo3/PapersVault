## Problem

Existing dynamic functional brain network (DFBN) analysis methods treat all neural nodes uniformly, overlooking the fact that certain regions exhibit significantly denser spatial connectivity or more pronounced temporal variability, and that current connectivity patterns are systematically shaped by prior network states. This neglect of spatio-temporal heterogeneity and sequential dependence prevents existing models from capturing the abnormal neural reorganization patterns that characterize brain diseases such as Alzheimer's and Parkinson's.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253647/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes resting-state fMRI time series (90 brain regions via AAL atlas, 140 time points for ADNI and 220 for PD) partitioned into T overlapping sliding windows of length S (T=6, S=90 for ADNI; T=8, S=80 for PD), from which Pearson correlation matrices are built and sparsified at threshold α=0.6. Beyond the final disease class label, the model produces per-region spatio-temporal heterogeneity weights (spatial and temporal) that can be visualized as biomarker maps, and identifies the top-10 most discriminative brain regions via t-test on learned spatio-temporal features.

## Method

NeuroH-TGL comprises three coupled modules. First, a Spatio-Temporal Pattern Decoupling (STPD) module uses two independent GCNs to disentangle each window's brain network into a topological consistency network and a temporal trend network, enforced by a contrastive loss (minimizing within-window similarity, maximizing cross-window consistency of the topological component) and an MSE reconstruction loss. Second, a Spatio-Temporal Heterogeneity Mining module computes cross-window cosine similarity of the topological consistency networks (yielding spatial heterogeneity weights) and the complement of cross-window similarity of temporal trend networks (yielding temporal heterogeneity weights), then applies element-wise multiplication to highlight pivotal nodes driving network reorganization. Third, a Temporal Propagation Graph Convolution Network (TPGCN) with parameter-shared blocks of two GCNs and one 2D CNN models how the previous window's heterogeneous features propagate into the current window via E_t = GCN(A_t, CNN(GCN(A_t, Z_t ⊕ Z_{t-1}))), explicitly encoding the influence of historical neural states on current topology. The full model is trained end-to-end with cross-entropy loss plus the STPD auxiliary losses, requiring only 0.0924M parameters.

## Results

On the ADNI dataset (510 subjects), NeuroH-TGL achieves 72.19% accuracy on the three-class NC vs. MCI vs. AD task, outperforming the best baseline (ST-GCN at 67.50%) by 4.69%. On the PD dataset (162 subjects), it reaches 91.25% accuracy on NC vs. TDPD and 87.17% on NC vs. PGPD, surpassing the next-best methods by 4.69% and 3.24% respectively. The method requires only 0.0924M parameters and 0.2479M FLOPs, indicating high computational efficiency. Ablation confirms each module contributes: removing STPD, STHW, or TPGCN drops accuracy by 3.75%, 3.25%, and 3.50% on NC vs. MCI respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NeuroH-TGL outperforms all 13 baselines across every classification task on both datasets. On the ADNI three-class task (NC vs. MCI vs. AD), it leads the strongest dynamic baseline ST-GCN (67.50%) by 4.69% in accuracy and the best static method ALTER (63.73%) by 8.46%. On the PD dataset, the margin is even larger: NeuroH-TGL achieves 91.25% on NC vs. TDPD versus ALTER's 86.56% (+4.69%), and 87.17% on NC vs. PGPD versus STAGIN/OT-MCSTGCN's 82.20% (+3.24%). Among dynamic baselines, MGNN is the closest competitor on ADNI (81.35% vs. 81.50% on NC vs. AD), while on PD the gap widens to 5.48% on NC vs. TDPD. Static methods (BrainNetCNN, BNTransformer, BrainGNN, LSGNN) consistently underperform dynamic methods, confirming the importance of modeling temporal evolution. The paper attributes its advantage to explicitly modeling spatio-temporal heterogeneity and temporal propagation, which none of the baselines capture.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the dynamic brain graph learning line (STAGIN, ST-GCN, OT-MCSTGCN) by introducing a spatio-temporal heterogeneity perspective that prior methods overlook. Rather than treating all brain regions uniformly through GCN + temporal convolution, NeuroH-TGL first decouples each dynamic brain network into topological consistency and temporal trend components, then uses cross-window similarity to identify pivotal heterogeneous nodes. The Temporal Propagation GCN (TPGCN) departs from standard temporal convolutions by explicitly feeding the previous window's heterogeneous features into the current window's graph convolution, modeling sequential dependence as a propagation mechanism rather than a parallel temporal filter.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The heterogeneity-mining perspective offers a principled way to identify which brain regions drive network reorganization, yielding interpretable biomarkers (e.g., supplementary motor area, hippocampus, amygdala) that align with known neurodegenerative pathology. However, the approach is limited to a single fMRI modality, the decoupling into 'topological consistency' vs. 'temporal trend' is somewhat heuristic, and the large standard deviations (up to ~15% on 3-class tasks) temper confidence in the reported gains. Open questions include whether the heterogeneity framework generalizes to multimodal settings (structural + functional connectivity) and whether the specific decoupling strategy is optimal compared to learned or data-driven decompositions.
