## Problem

Existing RL approaches for LLM reasoning (e.g., R1-Zero, GRPO) optimize only the final answer under a single large, fixed token budget, making them inefficient for online serving where compute is limited and requests may need to be interrupted. This paper addresses the gap of optimizing 'anytime reasoning'—the ability to produce the best possible answer from a partially completed chain-of-thought at any truncation point—so that performance scales gracefully with available thinking budget rather than requiring a full, unbounded reasoning trace.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250793/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a math question as text and generates a chain-of-thought thinking process followed by a summarized answer (capped at 128 tokens). The key structural detail is that the thinking process is sampled to completion (up to 8000 tokens) but then truncated at multiple predefined budgets drawn from a prior distribution (e.g., {2000, 4000, 6000, 8000}); at each truncation point, ellipses and a  token are inserted to prompt the model to extract its best answer from the incomplete reasoning. A rule-based verifier scores each truncated summary, yielding a dense reward signal across the thinking trajectory rather than a single terminal reward.

## Method

AnytimeReasoner samples the thinking budget from a prior distribution p_B over a small set of budget levels, truncates the generated chain-of-thought at each level, and forces the model to summarize an answer for verification—thereby introducing verifiable dense rewards into the reasoning process. The thinking policy (π_θ) and summary policy (π_φ) are optimized in a decoupled manner: the thinking policy maximizes the expected reward over all sampled budgets (the anytime objective), while the summary policy is trained with a uniform budget distribution and GRPO within each summary group to ensure robust answer extraction at any truncation point. For variance reduction, the paper introduces Budget Relative Policy Optimization (BRPO), which constructs the advantage baseline as an interpolated combination of V1 (a discount-weighted average of rewards at previous budgets, capturing current progress) and V2 (the group-average Monte Carlo return, capturing cross-trajectory statistics), with the interpolation weight shifting from V2 to V1 as the thinking length grows. This is trained via PPO on DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B and 7B using a tree-like generation structure with vLLM prefix caching and FlexAttention-based tree masks for efficient multi-budget training.

## Results

On the 1.5B model (DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B), AnytimeReasoner-uniform achieves an average anytime accuracy of 46.0% across five benchmarks (AMC 2022: 58.8%, AIME 2024: 22.9%, MATH500: 79.4%, Minerva Math: 27.5%, Olympiad Bench: 41.2%), evaluated over 32 token budgets from 0 to 8000. All variants of AnytimeReasoner (base, uniform, linear prior) consistently outperform GRPO by a large margin across all thinking budgets. On the 7B model, the framework achieves up to ~5 absolute points improvement in anytime reasoning over GRPO, and the method reaches 32.7% on AIME 2024 compared to 30.3% reported by the concurrent MRT method.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is GRPO (Shao et al., 2024, with Dr. GRPO corrections from Liu et al., 2025). AnytimeReasoner outperforms GRPO across all thinking budgets (2000–8000 tokens) and all prior distributions (base, uniform, linear); even the 'base' variant—which optimizes only at the maximum budget identically to GRPO—still achieves significantly better performance at every budget, attributable to decoupled summary optimization and BRPO variance reduction. Against the reward-shaping baselines GRPO+length_penalty_v1 (Aggarwal & Welleck, 2025) and GRPO+length_penalty_v2 (Arora & Zanette, 2025), AnytimeReasoner achieves both shorter average thinking length and higher accuracy, whereas the length-penalty methods sacrifice performance and exhibit training instability. On the 7B model, AnytimeReasoner maintains its advantage over GRPO throughout training, with the gap widening at intermediate budgets. The method also surpasses the concurrent MRT approach (Qu et al., 2025) on AIME 2024 (32.7% vs. 30.3%), though MRT uses a fundamentally different meta-RL formulation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the R1-Zero/GRPO paradigm for LLM reasoning (Guo et al., 2025; Shao et al., 2024) by reframing the optimization objective from a single fixed-budget final answer to a budget-sampled anytime objective, drawing conceptual inspiration from classical anytime algorithms in AI planning (Dean and Boddy, 1988). The key methodological departure is the introduction of verifiable dense rewards via forced summarization at intermediate budgets, which enables a novel variance reduction technique (BRPO) that exploits the prefix structure of LLM generation—something unavailable in sparse-reward GRPO. The paper also positions itself against MRT (Qu et al., 2025), a concurrent work on test-time compute optimization, arguing that its per-token, return-based formulation is more principled than MRT's episode-level greedy meta-RL approach.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The anytime reasoning framework has direct practical relevance for online LLM serving, where requests must be interruptible and the optimal thinking budget is often unknown a priori. The finding that the anytime objective is a provable lower bound of standard reasoning (Eq. 5) means that optimizing for interruptibility does not sacrifice final performance, which is a clean and useful guarantee. However, the evaluation is limited to mathematical reasoning on 1.5B and 7B models with a single training epoch, leaving open whether the dense-reward and BRPO benefits transfer to non-math domains, larger models, or multi-turn settings. The moderate but consistent gains (~2–4 points on AIME) over a strong GRPO baseline suggest the approach is a solid incremental advance rather than a paradigm shift.
