## Problem

Classical identifiability theory characterizes when two models with exactly equal likelihoods have equivalent representations, but in practice trained models only approximately match in distribution. The paper asks: for what distributional distance does closeness between two models' output distributions guarantee similarity of their internal representations? It shows that the natural candidate—KL divergence—fails, and identifies a distance that succeeds.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253418/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes two trained models from the softmax embedding-unembedding family (p(y|x) ∝ exp(f(x)ᵀg(y))), each with embedding f: X→ℝ^M and unembedding g: Y→ℝ^M, and produces a log-likelihood variance distance d_LLV^λ between their conditional distributions and a PLS-SVD-based representational dissimilarity d_{f,g} between their representations. The analysis requires the diversity condition (|Y| > M, so the displaced unembeddings span ℝ^M) and operates specifically on the embedding and unembedding layers rather than intermediate network layers.

## Method

The paper's core contribution is twofold. First, Theorem 3.1 constructs a ρ-parameterized family of model pairs where the KL divergence between distributions vanishes as unembedding norms grow, yet the embeddings remain fixed and not equal up to any linear transformation—proving that near-maximum data likelihood does not guarantee representational similarity. Second, the paper defines a log-likelihood variance distance d_LLV^λ that measures weighted variances of log-probability differences across inputs and labels, and proves (Theorem 4.7) that d_LLV^λ ≤ ε implies representational dissimilarity d_{f,g} ≤ 2Mε, where the dissimilarity is computed via PLS-SVD on the cross-covariance of standardized embeddings and unembeddings. The key insight is that the error term in the linear relationship between two models' embeddings depends entirely on differences of log-probabilities, and d_LLV^λ directly measures how far these weighted differences are from being constant. Empirically, CIFAR-10 models (ResNet-18 with 2D representations) trained to similar cross-entropy loss exhibit the KL-failure mechanism through cluster permutations, while wider synthetic networks learn distributions that are closer under d_LLV^λ and yield more similar representations.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, two ResNet-18 models with nearly identical test losses (1.19 vs. 1.18) and similar KL divergence (1.13 vs. 1.12) exhibit highly dissimilar representations, with mean CCA of only 0.55 and d_LLV ≈ 1.55, confirming the theoretical prediction of Theorem 3.1. In the synthetic construction (Table 1), as the unembedding norm ρ grows from 3 to 18, d_KL drops by nearly four orders of magnitude (0.8866 → 0.0001) while d_LLV remains virtually constant (~1.317) and max d_SVD stays near 1.0, demonstrating that KL closeness does not imply representational similarity. On synthetic 2D classification tasks, wider networks (width 256 vs. 16) yield both lower mean and lower variance of d_LLV and d_f,g, indicating that increased capacity promotes representational convergence.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not benchmark against competing methods in the traditional sense; instead, it compares distributional distances and representational similarity measures to determine which pairings yield valid theoretical guarantees. The key comparison is between KL divergence and the proposed log-likelihood variance (LLV) distance: KL divergence fails to bound representational dissimilarity (Theorem 3.1), while the LLV distance provably does (Theorem 4.7, bounding d_f,g ≤ 2Mε). Among representational similarity measures, the paper contrasts CCA-based measures (which are invariant to all invertible linear transformations) with the proposed PLS-SVD-based d_f,g (which is invariant only to rotation plus dimension-wise scaling). Empirically, CCA (m_CCA = 0.55) and d_SVD (≈ 1.0) both confirm dissimilarity in the CIFAR-10 example, but d_LLV is the only distributional distance that remains stable under the permutation construction where d_KL collapses to zero. The synthetic width experiments further show that the LLV distance and d_f,g decrease together as network width increases, supporting the theoretical bound in practice.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the linear identifiability framework established by Khemakhem et al. (ICE-BEEM), Lachapelle et al., and Roeder et al., which showed that under a diversity condition, models with exactly equal likelihoods have representations related by invertible linear transformations. The key departure is moving from exact identifiability to the approximate setting: the paper asks what happens when distributions are merely close rather than identical. It introduces a new log-likelihood variance (LLV) distance between distributions and a PLS-SVD-based representational dissimilarity measure, proving that small LLV distance bounds representational dissimilarity—a result that has no analogue in the prior exact-identifiability literature. The paper also positions itself against CCA/CKA-based similarity measures, noting that its dissimilarity is invariant to a specific subset of linear transformations tied to the identifiability class rather than all invertible linear maps.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The negative result (Theorem 3.1/Corollary 3.2) is practically important: it shows that two models achieving near-optimal cross-entropy loss can have entirely dissimilar internal representations, undermining the implicit assumption that good performance guarantees representational convergence. This has direct implications for model stitching, knowledge distillation, and interpretability, where representational similarity is often assumed from functional similarity. The positive result (Theorem 4.7) provides the first theoretical guarantee linking a distributional distance to representational dissimilarity, but the bound is loose (linear in M·ε) and requires the diversity condition, which may fail for standard image classifiers with few classes. Open questions include whether tighter or non-linear bounds exist, whether the results extend to intermediate network layers (where identifiability is not yet established), and whether the LLV distance remains valid on subsets of labels.
