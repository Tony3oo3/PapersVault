## Problem

Generalized Category Discovery (GCD) requires classifying unlabelled images into both known and unknown categories, but existing methods either rely on single-granularity supervision or manually designed abstract hierarchies that introduce noise and limit scalability. SEAL addresses this gap by leveraging naturally occurring, semantically grounded taxonomies (e.g., biological orders/families/species, vehicle types/models) as multi-level supervision, which the authors prove yields a strictly tighter mutual information bound than single-level labels.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a partially labelled dataset: a small set of labelled images with known class identities (e.g., 50% of known-class samples) and a larger unlabelled set containing both known and novel class instances. At training time, the model also requires a publicly available semantic taxonomy mapping each fine-grained class to its coarser ancestors (e.g., 200 bird species → 38 families → 13 orders for CUB). At inference, the model outputs a class assignment for every unlabelled image, evaluated via clustering accuracy over all, known-only, and novel-only subsets.

## Method

SEAL builds on a shared ViT-B encoder (DINO/DINOv2 pretrained) followed by a projection layer that disentangles features into H semantic levels from coarse to fine, with a gradient controller preventing coarse branches from being dominated by fine-level signals. A Cross-Granularity Consistency (CGC) module enforces mutual coherence across levels by minimizing KL divergence between each coarse-level posterior and a pseudo-coarse distribution obtained by multiplying the fine-level posterior with a dynamically updated transition matrix M_h (initialized as one-hot for known classes, uniform for novel classes, refined via momentum updates). A Hierarchical Semantic-guided Soft Contrastive Learning (HSCL) loss replaces standard InfoNCE by computing fused similarity matrices across semantic levels to generate soft negative labels that weight semantically closer samples more gently, combined with a hybrid cosine-plus-L2 similarity metric whose balance decays over training as a curriculum. The full objective sums per-level representation and classification losses plus the CGC term, trained end-to-end in a single stage.

## Results

On the SSB benchmark with DINOv2 backbone, SEAL achieves an average 'All' accuracy of 76.3%, outperforming the previous best method DebGCD (74.9%) by 1.4% and μGCD (72.1%) by 4.2%. Under the DINOv1 backbone, SEAL reaches 64.5% average 'All' accuracy, marginally surpassing DebGCD (64.4%). On the more challenging Herbarium19 dataset, SEAL sets a new state-of-the-art with 46.9% 'All' accuracy, surpassing μGCD (45.8%) by 1.1%, while on Oxford-Pet it achieves 92.9% 'All' accuracy, competitive with DebGCD (93.0%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Under the DINOv2 backbone, SEAL outperforms DebGCD (ICLR25) by 1.4% on average 'All' accuracy (76.3% vs 74.9%), with the largest gains on FGVC-Aircraft (+2.7%) and Stanford Cars (+2.3%), though it trails DebGCD slightly on CUB (76.7% vs 77.5%). Against μGCD (NeurIPS23), SEAL leads by 4.2% on average, with an 8.3% margin on FGVC-Aircraft. Under DINOv1, SEAL (64.5%) essentially ties DebGCD (64.4%) on average but wins on Stanford Cars 'New' (58.5% vs 57.4%) and FGVC-Aircraft 'All' (62.0% vs 61.7%). On Herbarium19, SEAL achieves 46.9% 'All' accuracy, surpassing μGCD (45.8%) by 1.1% and DebGCD (44.7%) by 2.2%, though it underperforms μGCD on 'Old' classes (45.8% vs 61.9%). On Oxford-Pet, SEAL (92.9%) is within 0.1% of DebGCD (93.0%), confirming competitive performance on coarser-grained data.

## Relation to Prior Work

SEAL builds directly on the SimGCD baseline (Wen et al., ICCV 2023), replacing its InfoNCE representation loss with a hierarchical soft contrastive objective and extending the single-granularity classifier to a multi-task architecture across semantic levels. The multi-task design with gradient-stopped feature reuse follows Chang et al. (CVPR 2021), but the key conceptual departure is from prior GCD methods that construct abstract or manually designed hierarchies (InfoSieve, CiPR, TIDA, HypCD): SEAL instead leverages naturally occurring taxonomies (WordNet, GBIF, vehicle types) as the hierarchical structure, positioning itself as the first work to apply semantic-guided hierarchies to GCD. The Cross-Granularity Consistency module and the dynamic transition matrix for novel classes are novel contributions not present in prior hierarchical or contrastive GCD methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean information-theoretic justification (tighter mutual information bound via the chain rule) for why multi-level semantic supervision should outperform single-granularity GCD, giving practitioners a principled reason to seek taxonomic structure in their data. The practical implication is that publicly available taxonomies or LLM-generated hierarchies can be plugged in without manual curation, lowering the barrier to hierarchical GCD. Open questions include whether the approach scales to very large category spaces (the authors note validation on <700 instances), whether the CUB underperformance relative to DebGCD reflects a fundamental limitation of human-annotated bird taxonomies, and whether the framework extends to multi-modal or video settings.
