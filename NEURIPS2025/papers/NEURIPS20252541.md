## Problem

Inferring true 3D physical distributions (gas density, magnetic field strength) within Giant Molecular Clouds from partial line-of-sight observables is a core inverse problem in star formation research. Existing astrostatistical methods (power-law fitting, DCF) suffer from large errors and poor OOD generalization under shifted physical conditions, while state-of-the-art conditional diffusion models introduce an artificial Gaussian prior misaligned with the underlying GMC dynamics and require expensive training. The paper asks whether a Schrödinger-bridge formulation that directly bridges observables to physical states can improve both interpretability and out-of-distribution robustness.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252541/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

For density prediction, the input is a 2D projected column density map (N_H/cm²); for magnetic field prediction, the input combines column density, dust polarization orientation angles, and LOS nonthermal velocity dispersion. All inputs are log-transformed, normalized to [0,1], and cropped to 128×128 patches during training, with observation noise modeled as weighted Gaussian (λ=0.1). Outputs are full 3D volume density maps (n_H/cm³) or magnetic field strength maps (μG) at the same spatial resolution; for large real observations like Taurus B213, an aggregated patch-based sampling strategy reconstructs the full-resolution prediction from overlapping sub-patches.

## Method

Astro-DSB replaces the Gaussian-prior-to-data mapping of conditional DDPMs with a pairwise Schrödinger bridge between the observable distribution p₀ and the ground-truth physical distribution p₁, following the I²SB analytical posterior form. Two key modifications are introduced: (M1) explicit noise alignment that conditions the bridge on y−ε rather than clean y, respecting the stochastic observation model, and (M2) observable enhancement that feeds the clean observable as an additional intermediate condition at every diffusion timestep, effectively learning a conditional Doob's h-transform. The score function is parameterized by a ~81M-parameter U-Net trained with a reparameterized denoising objective over 1000 timesteps, using patch-based training and an aggregated overlapping-patch sampling strategy at inference. This design eliminates the Gaussian prior, reduces training cost by ~75% (100 vs. 400 epochs), and yields substantially lower relative-error bias under physically grounded OOD shifts (different MHD codes, initial conditions, and dominant processes).

## Results

On density prediction (Table 1), Astro-DSB achieves the best OOD performance with a mean relative error μ=0.51±2E-4 and standard deviation σ=2.32±3E-3, substantially outperforming cDDPMs (μ=2.88, σ=5.33) and U-Net (μ=5.01, σ=7.08) under physically shifted initial conditions. On magnetic field prediction (Table 2), Astro-DSB achieves competitive SOTA with OOD μ=0.19±6E-5 and σ=0.67±9E-5, matching or exceeding cDDPMs (μ=0.12, σ=0.72) while using only 25% of the training cost (~100 epochs vs. ~400 epochs). The method also generalizes to real Taurus B213 observations, producing filamentary density structures consistent with independent HC₃N tracer measurements.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Astro-DSB decisively outperforms both astrostatistical baselines (2PLF and 3PLF from Bisbas et al.) on all metrics, with OOD mean relative error reduced from ~3–6 to 0.51 for density. Against the discriminative U-Net (CASI-2D, Xu et al. 2020), Astro-DSB wins on OOD by a wide margin (σ: 2.32 vs. 7.08) while remaining competitive on ID. The closest competitor is conditional DDPMs (Xu et al. 2023 for density, Xu et al. 2025 for magnetic field): cDDPMs achieves slightly better ID density μ (0.05 vs. 0.02) but degrades severely under OOD (μ: 2.88 vs. 0.51; σ: 5.33 vs. 2.32), and Astro-DSB trains in ~100 epochs versus ~400 for cDDPMs. For magnetic field, the classic DCF method (Chandrasekhar & Fermi 1953) shows large systematic bias (ID μ=11.80), while the updated PLF variant (Skalidis & Tassis 2021) is competitive on ID (μ=−0.11) but Astro-DSB matches or exceeds it on OOD (μ=0.19 vs. 1.03) with lower σ (0.67 vs. 1.47). Overall, Astro-DSB is the only method that maintains low error under physically grounded distribution shifts across both prediction tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the paired Diffusion Schrödinger Bridge framework (I²SB, Liu et al. 2023; De Bortoli et al. 2021; Shi et al. 2023) from static image-to-image translation to a physically dynamic astrophysical inverse problem, where the bridge trajectory between observables and true physical states carries physical meaning. It departs from the dominant conditional DDPM approach in astrophysical prediction (Xu et al. 2023b, 2025) by eliminating the Gaussian prior and learning a direct distribution-to-distribution mapping, and from the vanilla I²SB by adding two astrophysics-specific modifications: noise alignment with observational uncertainty (M1) and per-timestep observable conditioning (M2). Unlike the two-stage physics-aligned diffusion bridge of Li et al. (2025), Astro-DSB uses a single-stage training procedure and explicitly evaluates under physically grounded OOD shifts rather than only in-distribution accuracy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that distribution-level probabilistic learning via DSB provides meaningful OOD generalization advantages over both discriminative (U-Net) and conditional generative (cDDPM) baselines when the distribution shift is physically defined (different MHD codes, initial conditions, dominant processes), and that this advantage transfers to real observational data (Taurus B213) without any real-data fine-tuning. The 75% reduction in training cost over conditional DDPMs is a practical efficiency gain for the astrophysics community. A key open question is whether the framework can be extended to explicitly encode forward physical equations (self-gravity, MHD dynamics) into the bridge trajectory, which the authors identify as a natural next step but do not pursue. The current method still treats the forward evolution as a black box, limiting its applicability to settings where the physical forward process is well-characterized.
