## Problem

Flow-based generative models excel at capturing complex multimodal distributions, but their direct application to online RL is blocked by a fundamental objective mismatch: standard flow training imitates static data distributions, while RL requires value-based policy optimization over a nonstationary replay buffer. Existing diffusion-based online RL methods (e.g., QVPO, DACER) mitigate this through costly backpropagation through iterative denoising steps or inefficient weighted behavior cloning, leaving flow-based policies unexplored as a practical online RL policy class.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a continuous state observation vector s (ranging from 24-dim for Walker to 223-dim for Dog tasks in DMC, and 51–77-dim for Unitree H1 tasks in HumanoidBench) and produces a continuous action vector a (6–38 dim depending on the task). Actions are generated by integrating a learned state-conditioned velocity field v_θ(t, s, a^t) over t ∈ [0,1] starting from Gaussian noise a⁰ ~ N(0, I), with the notable design choice of using a single ODE step (N=1) for both training and inference, avoiding multi-step sampling entirely.

## Method

FlowRL parameterizes the policy as a state-dependent velocity field and formulates policy improvement as a constrained search that maximizes E_{a~π_θ}[Q^{π_θ}(s,a)] while bounding the Wasserstein-2 distance to the behavior-optimal policy π_{β*} derived from the replay buffer. The key theoretical contribution is a tractable W2 upper bound (Theorem 4.1) expressed as an integral of velocity-field discrepancies, which avoids intractable density estimation. Since π_{β*} is not directly sampleable, the paper introduces an implicit guidance mechanism: a non-negative weighting function f ∝ max(Q^{π_{β*}} − Q^{π_θ}, 0) modulates a weighted conditional flow-matching loss, adaptively pulling the policy toward high-value buffer actions only when the behavior policy outperforms the current one (Theorem 4.2 guarantees this yields an improved policy distribution). The final Lagrangian objective (Eq. 23) combines a max-Q exploitation term with the weighted CFM regularization, and Q^{π_{β*}} is estimated via expectile regression on the replay buffer. This design enables one-step policy inference without BPTT, retaining the simplicity of standard actor-critic architectures while exploiting the multimodality of flow-based action generation.

## Results

FlowRL is evaluated on 15 tasks across DMControl (easy/middle, hard) and HumanoidBench (Unitree H1), with results presented primarily as learning curves (Figure 3) and normalized score bar charts (Figure 1) rather than a single numerical table. FlowRL consistently outperforms or matches all model-free baselines (SAC, TD3, QVPO) on the majority of tasks, with particularly strong gains in high-dimensional settings (e.g., DMC dog domain with state dim 223, action dim 38) and complex humanoid control. On the Dogrun task, FlowRL achieves substantially lower 1M-step training time and single-step inference latency compared to QVPO, while using only N=1 flow steps and no explicit exploration mechanism. Ablation studies confirm that the Wasserstein-2 policy constraint improves both final performance and sample efficiency, with the largest gains on HumanoidBench H1 tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SAC (Gaussian policy) and TD3 (deterministic policy), FlowRL outperforms or matches on the majority of the 15 tasks, with the most pronounced advantages in high-dimensional DMC-hard tasks (dog, humanoid) and HumanoidBench H1 tasks where multimodal action distributions matter. Against QVPO (the prior state-of-the-art diffusion-based online RL method), FlowRL achieves higher normalized scores across the 12 challenging DMC-hard and HumanoidBench tasks (Figure 1, left) and is significantly more computationally efficient in both training time and per-step inference (Figure 1, right), largely because it avoids iterative denoising and BPTT through the reverse process. Against TD-MPC2 (model-based reference), FlowRL achieves comparable final performance but at much lower wall-clock cost, since it is a model-free method requiring no learned dynamics model. Notably, FlowRL achieves these results with single-step (N=1) flow inference and no explicit exploration bonus, whereas QVPO relies on multi-step sampling and additional inference tricks.

## Relation to Prior Work

FlowRL extends the line of diffusion/flow-based policy research in RL (QVPO, DQL, DACER, FQL) by replacing the expensive BPTT-through-reverse-process or generalized behavior cloning paradigms with a Wasserstein-2-constrained policy search over a flow-matching velocity field. It draws on self-imitation learning (SIL) and OBAC for the idea of using high-reward buffer trajectories as a reference, but departs from them by preserving full multimodal expressivity through the flow policy rather than restricting to parametric families. The W2 regularization connects to recent work on Wasserstein-regularized flow matching fine-tuning (Fan et al. 2025), but here it is repurposed as a policy constraint in an online RL setting rather than a data-imitation regularizer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in achieving one-step policy inference without BPTT, which removes the primary computational bottleneck that has limited diffusion/flow-based RL to small-scale or offline settings. The theoretical guarantee (Theorem 4.2) that weighted CFM yields a policy improvement step provides a principled bridge between generative modeling objectives and value-based policy optimization. Key open questions include whether the absence of explicit exploration limits performance in highly stochastic or sparse-reward environments, and whether the W2 constraint generalizes beyond continuous control to discrete or hierarchical action spaces.
