## Problem

Existing text-to-video models struggle to generate videos that faithfully obey physical laws because there is a significant gap between abstract physical principles and the visual generation capabilities of diffusion-based models. This is compounded by the fact that mainstream video training datasets (e.g., Koala-36M, OpenVid) treat physical phenomena as secondary elements rather than explicitly showcasing them, leaving models without a clear learning signal for physics-aware generation.

## Input / Output

At inference, WISA takes a user text prompt and auto-generates three types of physical annotations (textual description, 29 qualitative category labels, and quantitative properties for density/time/temperature) via GPT-4o using only the prompt text (~2000 tokens, no visual access). The model outputs a video at 480×720 with 49 frames (CogVideoX-5B) or 480×832 with 81 frames (Wan2.1-14B), with only a ~5% increase in inference time over the base model.

## Method

WISA decomposes physical knowledge into three hierarchical levels and injects each via a tailored mechanism: textual physical descriptions are concatenated with the caption before the text encoder; qualitative physics categories (29 labels spanning dynamics, thermodynamics, optics, camera motion, and object state) are modeled through Mixture-of-Physical-Experts Attention (MoPA), which assigns dedicated attention heads as 'experts' per category and activates only the relevant heads via element-wise gating with a randomly perturbed category vector; quantitative properties (density, time span, temperature span in scientific notation) are mapped to embeddings and injected via AdaLN modulation. A Physical Classifier with a learnable [PHYSICS_TOKEN] provides an auxiliary multi-label binary cross-entropy loss to guide the model's internal physical representation. To limit overhead, a single Physical Module is inserted only after the final DiT block, and training uses LoRA (rank 128) on the WISA-80K dataset, updating ~187M parameters for CogVideoX-5B with a combined diffusion + balanced classification loss.

## Results

On VideoPhy, CogVideoX-5B-WISA achieves the best semantic alignment (SA = 0.62) and physical consistency (PC = 0.45), improving over the CogVideoX-5B baseline by +0.05 SA and +0.04 PC while adding only ~5% inference time (220 s vs. 210 s). On PhyGenBench, CogVideoX-5B-WISA also leads with SA = 0.39 and PC = 0.45. For the larger Wan2.1-14B backbone, WISA yields SA = 0.60 and PC = 0.36 on VideoPhy (vs. 0.54 / 0.31 for the base model), confirming the framework's generalizability across architectures.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the direct baseline CogVideoX-5B, WISA improves SA from 0.57 to 0.62 and PC from 0.41 to 0.45 on VideoPhy, and from 0.34 to 0.39 (SA) and 0.42 to 0.45 (PC) on PhyGenBench, all while keeping inference time nearly identical (220 s vs. 210 s). Compared to PhyT2V, which requires 1800 s of iterative LLM-guided refinement, WISA achieves higher SA (0.62 vs. 0.59) and PC (0.45 vs. 0.42) on VideoPhy at roughly 8× lower inference cost. Against Cosmos-Diffusion-7B, WISA gains +0.10 SA and +0.18 PC on VideoPhy, reflecting Cosmos's tendency toward disordered physical processes. On the Wan2.1-14B backbone, WISA lifts SA by +0.06 and PC by +0.05 on VideoPhy, though Wan2.1-WISA trails CogVideoX-5B-WISA on PC (0.36 vs. 0.45), which the authors attribute to Wan2.1 not being among the nine models used to train the VideoCon-Physics evaluator. WISA also outperforms VideoCrafter2, OpenSora, and HunyuanVideo on both SA and PC across all reported metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

WISA builds on the Diffusion Transformer architectures of CogVideoX and Wan2.1, extending them with a Physical Module that draws on Mixture-of-Experts (Riquelme et al.) and Mixture-of-Heads (Jin et al.) to route attention toward category-specific physical phenomena. It departs from prior physics-aware generation approaches—PhyT2V's iterative LLM-based prompt refinement, and DANO/MotionCraft/PhysGen's rigid-body-only differentiable simulation—by instead injecting structured physical information (textual descriptions, qualitative categories, quantitative properties) directly into the generative model's conditioning pathway. The WISA-80K dataset is positioned as a corrective to general-scene corpora like Koala-36M and OpenVid, where physical phenomena are secondary and implicit rather than the primary visual content.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

WISA-80K is the first large-scale (80K clips) dataset explicitly curated around 17 physical phenomena across dynamics, thermodynamics, and optics, filling a clear gap for training physics-aware video models. The modular MoPA design offers a practical path to incremental expansion—new physics categories can be added as new expert heads without retraining the full model—though the paper acknowledges it currently lacks mechanism-level constraints (energy conservation, Newton's laws) and struggles with out-of-distribution phenomena like corrosion or electromagnetism. Open questions include extending WISA to image-to-video generation for embodied AI, incorporating detailed physical principle constraints while preserving generalization, and developing more reliable physics evaluation metrics beyond VideoCon-Physics, which the authors show can misjudge physically correct sequences.
