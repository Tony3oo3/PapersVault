## Problem

The single-dimensional Bayesian utility maximization problem (m identical items, n i.i.d. agents) was fully resolved by Hartline and Roughgarden (2008), but the multidimensional setting with heterogeneous items remained largely unexplored. This paper initiates the study of multidimensional Bayesian utility maximization for unit-demand bidders, asking what utility can be achieved by simple mechanisms in approximation to optimal social welfare, and whether the tight Θ(1 + log(n/m)) gap from the single-dimensional case carries over to the i.i.d. multidimensional setting.

## Input / Output

The mechanism takes each bidder's reported value vector v_i ∈ R^m (one value per item) along with the number of bidders n and items m, under the assumption that all values are drawn i.i.d. from a common distribution F. It outputs a (possibly randomized) allocation assigning at most one item to each bidder and a non-negative payment per bidder, with the guarantee that expected utility (welfare minus revenue) approximates optimal social welfare within a factor of Θ(1 + log(n/m)). Both mechanisms are prior-independent, requiring no knowledge of F, and the i.i.d. assumption across both items and bidders is essential for both BIC and the approximation analysis to hold.

## Method

The paper introduces the M-Favorites mechanism family, in which each bidder first reports their favorite item (the item for which they drew the highest value), and then a fixed single-item mechanism M is run independently for each item among the bidders who declared it their favorite. In the more-items-than-bidders regime (m ≥ n), M is uniform random allocation with no payment (Random-Favorites), yielding a (1−1/e) approximation; in the more-bidders-than-items regime (n > m), M is the Hartline–Roughgarden single-item prior-free mechanism (Prior-Free-Favorites), yielding an O(log(n/m)) approximation. The i.i.d. assumption is crucial for BIC: it ensures each item's single-item subproblem has the same expected number of participants and the same input distribution, making truthful reporting of one's favorite item optimal. Tightness is established by a reduction that transforms the i.i.d. unit-demand instance into an identical-items instance (replacing each bidder's values with their maximum), showing the gap cannot be improved beyond Θ(1 + log(n/m)).

## Results

In the i.i.d. unit-demand setting with n buyers and m items, the paper establishes a tight Θ(1 + log(n/m)) gap between optimal utility and optimal social welfare. Specifically, when m ≥ n, the Random-Favorites mechanism achieves a (1 − 1/e) ≈ 0.632 approximation to optimal welfare; when n > m, the Prior-Free-Favorites mechanism achieves an O(log(n/m)) approximation. These bounds are proven tight via a reduction from the identical-items setting of Hartline and Roughgarden [2008], improving upon the previous best bound of O(m log n) from Fotakis et al. [2016].

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's main comparison is against the prior best multidimensional bound of O(m log n) from Fotakis et al. [2016], which used a VCG + random free allocation hybrid. The new results improve this to O(log(n/m)) in the n > m regime and a constant (1 − 1/e) in the m ≥ n regime, a significant tightening especially when m is large. In the single-dimensional special case (m identical items), the paper recovers the tight Θ(1 + log(n/m)) bound of Hartline and Roughgarden [2008], confirming that the multidimensional i.i.d. setting does not introduce additional loss beyond the single-dimensional case. The tightness proof (Theorem 3) shows that no mechanism can do better than Θ(1 + log(n/m)) in the worst case, by reducing the i.i.d. unit-demand instance to an identical-items instance without increasing welfare by more than a constant factor. Subsequent work by Ezra et al. [2025] extends the analysis to non-i.i.d. settings with a condition on favorite probabilities, achieving O(1 + log(cn/m)), which matches the i.i.d. bound when c = 1 but is more general.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the single-dimensional Bayesian utility maximization theory of Hartline and Roughgarden (STOC 2008), which achieved a tight Θ(1 + log(n/m)) approximation for m identical items, to the multidimensional setting with m i.i.d. heterogeneous items and unit-demand bidders. The prior best multidimensional bound was O(m log n) from Fotakis et al. (2016) via a VCG-plus-random-allocation hybrid; this paper improves it to Θ(1 + log(n/m)) by introducing the Favorites Mechanism class, which decomposes the multidimensional problem into per-item single-dimensional subproblems. The paper also explicitly departs from the revenue maximization literature by showing that standard tools (the copies setting, Lagrangian duality flows) fail to provide useful upper bounds on optimal utility, carving out a distinct technical landscape.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first paper to initiate the study of multidimensional Bayesian utility maximization with tight approximation guarantees, and the matching of the single-dimensional bound in the i.i.d. unit-demand setting is a clean, satisfying result. More broadly, the paper exposes fundamental obstacles that prevent straightforward porting of revenue-maximization techniques: the copies construction can *decrease* utility (Theorem 4), and the utility-optimal mechanism may need to leave items unallocated (Theorem 6), both of which defy intuition from the single-dimensional case. The central open question—finding a tractable upper bound on optimal utility tighter than social welfare—remains wide open and is likely to require new technical ideas beyond the revenue literature.
