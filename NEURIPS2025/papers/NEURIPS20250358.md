## Problem

Hierarchical RL typically introduces abstract actions (options) but leaves the agent in the original low-level state space, missing the opportunity to build a fully abstract decision process. The paper addresses how to construct state abstractions that are mutually compatible with a pre-existing set of abstract actions, yielding a well-formed abstract MDP that is Markov and approximately model-preserving—something that independent state and action abstraction is unlikely to achieve.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250358/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a ground MDP augmented with a set of options (each with initiation set, termination condition, and policy), along with transition data tuples (state, initiation vector, option, reward, next state, next initiation vector, duration) and raw observations (e.g., 28×28 MNIST digits, 60×80×3 egocentric images, or 16-byte RAM states). It produces a finite abstract MDP whose states are distributions over ground states conditioned on observed initiation vectors, with learned transition, reward, and discount functions; for factored options, the output is a factored abstract MDP where states are assignments of factor values. Planning over the resulting graph (e.g., via value iteration) yields a goal-conditioned policy executable in the ground MDP.

## Method

The core insight is that the Bellman equation naturally rewrites as a Bellman equation over abstract states if those states are distributions over ground states rather than sets or single states. The paper defines abstract states as tuples (p, I) where p is a distribution over ground states conditioned on an observed initiation vector I, and shows that refining state distributions into components with consistent available options guarantees the Markov property. Algorithm 1 constructs the abstract MDP by partitioning transitions by initiation vectors, then iteratively refines states whose transitions exceed an error threshold using Gaussian mixture model clustering, with model error measured via a classifier two-sample test. For factored options that modify only subsets of state variables, the paper proves that the resulting abstract states decompose into independent factors, each assigned a value representing the option's effect on that factor. The neurosymbolic pipeline combines a neural Markov State Abstraction (MSA) network—trained with inverse-model and density-ratio losses to produce compressed Markov representations from raw pixels—with the symbolic refinement algorithm applied on top of the learned embeddings.

## Results

The paper presents results primarily as learning curves rather than tabulated numbers. On the visual maze domain, the learned abstract MDP (14 abstract states after refinement) achieves better goal-reaching performance with far fewer samples than goal-conditioned DQN, and its performance remains stable when observation resolution is increased from 60×80 to 180×240 pixels and random distractor portraits are added, whereas DQN performance steadily declines. On the MNIST gridwalk, the factored abstract MDP (36 abstract states from 6×6 factor values) outperforms goal-conditioned DQN with hindsight experience replay at low sample budgets, though with high variance due to suboptimal MSA factorization in some runs. On Montezuma's Revenge, the final factored abstraction yields 116 factor values over 14 factors (1816 abstract states), and value iteration over this abstraction produces a policy that successfully collects the key and exits the first room.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two baselines. On the visual maze, the abstract MDP outperforms goal-conditioned DQN [Mnih et al., 2015] in sample efficiency, reaching comparable or better performance with substantially fewer environment interactions (Figure 4b). Critically, when input complexity is increased (higher resolution, distractor portraits), the abstract MDP's performance is unchanged while DQN's degrades steadily (Figure 4c), demonstrating that the abstraction retains only decision-relevant information. On the MNIST gridwalk, the factored abstract MDP outperforms goal-conditioned DQN with hindsight experience replay [Andrychowicz et al., 2017] at low sample counts (Figure 5b), but exhibits high variance because the MSA embedding is not always properly factored, leading to poor refinement in some runs. The median performance is close to the upper bound of the error bars, indicating the method works well when factorization succeeds. No head-to-head numerical comparison is provided for Montezuma's Revenge, where the method is demonstrated qualitatively via a successful plan execution.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the constructive approach to learning symbolic planning representations from robotics (Konidaris et al. 2018) by making the resulting abstractions provably Markov and compatible with the options framework. It departs from the dominant state-aggregation paradigm (Li et al. 2006; soft aggregation) by defining abstract states as *distributions* over ground states rather than sets or single states, a choice motivated directly by the Bellman equation's recursive structure. The factored generalization builds on the expected-length option model (Abel et al. 2019) and Markov State Abstractions (Allen et al. 2021), but adds the subgoal property that yields factored abstract states. Unlike recent skill-driven state abstraction work (Bagaria et al. 2021; Shah et al. 2022), this framework guarantees the Markov property and approximate model preservation by construction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This provides the first principled, end-to-end framework for constructing a fully abstract MDP (both states and actions) from a ground MDP plus a set of options, with formal guarantees on value preservation. The distribution-based semantics of abstract states is a fundamentally different direction from set-based aggregation, enabling overlapping supports and recursive Bellman computation over abstract policies. Key open questions include: scaling the iterative refinement beyond GMM clustering to handle high-dimensional continuous state spaces, learning factored Markov representations directly from pixels (rather than relying on annotated RAM as in Montezuma's Revenge), and making the construction online/incremental rather than batch. The invariance to input resolution and visual distractors demonstrated in the maze domain is a compelling practical argument for the abstraction's utility.
