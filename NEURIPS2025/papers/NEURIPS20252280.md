## Problem

Existing vision-language dataset distillation methods rely on Matching Training Trajectories (MTT), which require days of computation and 200–320 GB of GPU memory to distill even a few hundred image-text pairs, making them infeasible for large-scale datasets like CC3M. Directly applying pre-trained diffusion models to this task fails because their sample-wise noise-prediction objective provides no supervision for image-text correspondence and produces low-diversity synthetic samples.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252280/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a large image-text dataset (images resized to 224×224, text encoded to 768-dim embeddings) and produces a small synthetic dataset of 500–1500 image-text pairs. During distillation, the diffusion model is conditioned on randomly sampled captions from the original dataset to generate images; a post-training step then uses an MLLM (e.g., LLaVA) to synthesize additional captions per image, so the final dataset contains |S|/η unique images with |S| total captions (η = captions per image, typically 2).

## Method

EDGE fine-tunes a pre-trained latent diffusion model (Stable Diffusion v1.5) by replacing the standard noise-prediction MSE with two novel criteria computed on the denoised image latent z and text embedding y: (1) a bi-directional InfoNCE-style contrastive loss that aligns corresponding image-text pairs while pushing apart non-matching pairs in both image→text and text→image directions, and (2) a diversity loss that minimizes the average cosine similarity of normalized concatenated [z; y] features across all pairs in a batch, encouraging the synthetic set to cover the original distribution. The combined EDGE loss (L_C + λL_D) is used to fine-tune only a small subset of diffusion parameters. At inference, captions are randomly drawn from the source dataset and fed as text conditions to sample images, and a caption-synthesis step with LLaVA augments each generated image with an additional natural-language description to improve text-to-image retrieval.

## Results

On Flickr30K (500 pairs, 1.7% condensation), EDGE achieves IR@1=6.7, IR@10=30.5, TR@1=13.3, TR@10=47.5, matching or exceeding MTT-VL on text retrieval (TR@5: 35.6 vs 32.8; TR@10: 47.5 vs 46.8) while being ~9× faster. On COCO (500 pairs, 4.4‰), EDGE reaches IR@1=1.8, TR@1=2.9, TR@10=15.7, outperforming MTT-VL and the pre-trained Stable Diffusion baseline on all six metrics. At 1000 pairs on COCO, EDGE surpasses LoRS on every metric (e.g., IR@1: 2.8 vs 2.5; TR@10: 21.0 vs 19.1) while requiring only 8.5 GPU hours versus LoRS's 155.6 hours (~18× speedup). On the large-scale CC3M dataset (3.3M pairs), EDGE is the only applicable distillation method, achieving IR@1=0.2 and TR@1=0.1 versus 0.1 and 0.0 for both random selection and pre-trained SD.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the MTT-based SOTA methods (MTT-VL and LoRS), EDGE trades a small performance gap on image retrieval for a dramatic efficiency gain: on COCO with 500 pairs, EDGE requires 7.7 GPU hours versus 162.3 (MTT-VL) and 151.6 (LoRS), an 18–21× speedup, while remaining within 0.3–2.5 points on IR@1. On text retrieval, EDGE actually matches or exceeds MTT-VL (TR@5: 35.6 vs 32.8 on Flickr30K; TR@1: 2.9 vs 2.3 on COCO). Against the pre-trained Stable Diffusion v1.5 baseline (no distillation-specific training), EDGE improves IR@1 by 2.2× on Flickr30K (6.7 vs 3.1) and 1.5× on COCO (1.8 vs 1.2), confirming that the proposed contrastive and diversity losses are essential. At 1000 pairs on COCO, EDGE reverses the gap and beats LoRS on all six metrics (IR@1: 2.8 vs 2.5; TR@10: 21.0 vs 19.1). Coreset selection methods (HERD, K-Cent, Forget) and TESLA are consistently the weakest, with IR@1 values of 0.8–3.5, far below any distillation method. On CC3M, where MTT-VL and LoRS are infeasible (OOM), EDGE is the only viable distillation approach and still outperforms random selection and vanilla SD.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends generative dataset distillation (Minimax Diffusion, D4M) from single-modality image classification to the multimodal image-text contrastive learning (ITC) setting, where prior generative approaches are inapplicable due to the absence of fixed class labels. It departs from the dominant MTT-based VLDD paradigm (MTT-VL, LoRS) that relies on computationally expensive bilevel trajectory matching, instead fine-tuning a pre-trained latent diffusion model with a novel bi-directional InfoNCE-style contrastive loss and a Minimax-inspired diversity loss. A post-training caption synthesis step using MLLMs (LLaVA, GPT) is introduced to compensate for the limited text diversity in the synthetic dataset.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 18× speedup over the SOTA MTT-based method and the ability to distill the 3M-pair CC3M dataset (infeasible for all prior VLDD methods) make large-scale multimodal dataset distillation practically accessible on a single GPU. The cross-architecture generalization finding—that MTT-based synthetic data overfits to the distillation architecture while EDGE's generative data transfers well across backbones—suggests a broader principle about the architecture-dependence of trajectory-matching distillation. Open questions include whether the approach extends to other multimodal tasks (video-text, audio-text), whether it supports generative evaluation (not just retrieval), and how it scales to even larger datasets or higher-resolution images.
