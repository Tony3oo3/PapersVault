## Problem

Existing 4-bit quantization methods for Vision Transformers face a fundamental trade-off: PTQ is fast but suffers large accuracy drops, while QAT achieves high ImageNet accuracy but is prohibitively slow (days of training), memory-hungry, and—critically—degrades generalization to downstream tasks because aggressive retraining pushes the model out of its original optimization basin. No prior method simultaneously delivers near-FP32 accuracy, strong downstream generalization, and practical training efficiency.

## Input / Output

GPLQ takes a pre-trained ViT (Swin-T/S/B/L, DeiT-S/B, or ResNet) and a small calibration subset (512 images for weight PTQ; 1 epoch of training data for activation QAT) and produces a W4A4 quantized model. For classification, the quantized model maps RGB images to class labels; for detection/segmentation on COCO, it outputs bounding boxes and instance masks via Mask R-CNN. Activations use per-channel symmetric 4-bit quantization, and the PCA feature-mimicking loss operates on penultimate-layer features (256-dim subspace for Swin-T's 768-dim features, 64-dim for DeiT-T's 192-dim features).

## Method

GPLQ employs a sequential 'activation-first, weights-later' strategy grounded in two empirical insights: (1) activation quantization causes more accuracy loss than weight quantization at 4-bit, and (2) preserving the original optimization basin is essential for downstream generalization. Stage 1 (Act-QAT) quantizes only activations to 4-bit while keeping all weights in FP32, training for just 1 epoch with a PCA-based feature mimicking loss that projects penultimate-layer features onto a low-dimensional subspace (~60% cumulative variance) to anchor the quantized model in the same basin as the FP32 teacher. Stage 2 (Weight-PTQ) then quantizes weights using RepQ-ViT followed by QwT's closed-form linear compensation, which is simplified because activations are already fixed at 4-bit, reducing compound quantization error by ~46%. This yields a 100× speedup over full QAT with peak memory below FP32 training, while matching or exceeding FP32 generalization on five FGVC benchmarks.

## Results

On ImageNet-1k, GPLQ (W4A4) achieves 79.5% top-1 on Swin-T (vs. 81.2% FP32), 84.0% on Swin-B (vs. 85.3% FP32), and 82.0% on DeiT-B (vs. 83.3% FP32), losing only 1.0–1.7% to full precision. On average downstream FGVC accuracy (5 tasks), GPLQ matches or exceeds FP32: 70.77% vs. 70.79% (Swin-T), 78.25% vs. 78.93% (Swin-B), and 75.36% vs. 73.43% (DeiT-B, where GPLQ actually surpasses FP32). On COCO object detection, GPLQ (W4A4) achieves 0.401 AP^box on Swin-T(3x), substantially outperforming RepQ-ViT (0.361) and PTQ4ViT (0.069).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against PTQ baselines, GPLQ dominates: on Swin-T it gains +6.5% ImageNet over RepQ-ViT (79.5 vs. 73.0) and +4.7% over QwT (79.5 vs. 74.8 on DeiT-S), while also improving downstream average accuracy by 8–10 points. Against QAT baselines (OFQ, Q-Var), GPLQ trades a small ImageNet accuracy gap (−1.7 to −2.9%) for a large downstream generalization gain: on Swin-T, GPLQ's 70.77% average FGVC accuracy exceeds Q-Var (68.12%) by 2.65 points and OFQ (60.73%) by 10.04 points, and nearly matches FP32 (70.79%). On COCO detection, GPLQ (0.401 AP^box on Swin-T 3x) outperforms RepQ-ViT (0.361) by 0.040 and PTQ4ViT (0.069) by 0.332, confirming generality beyond classification. The paper notes that QAT baselines are limited to small models due to OOM, whereas GPLQ scales to Swin-L and DeiT-B with consumer-grade GPUs.

## Relation to Prior Work

GPLQ carves a new middle ground between PTQ (fast but accuracy-degrading at 4-bit) and full QAT (accurate but prohibitively expensive and generalization-poor) by decoupling activation and weight quantization into a sequential pipeline. It repurposes LSQ for activation scale learning, borrows the PCA-based feature mimicking loss from TCS to anchor the quantized model in the original optimization basin, and delegates weight quantization to existing PTQ tools (RepQ-ViT + QwT). The key conceptual departure from prior QAT is restricting the QAT stage to activations only (weights remain FP32), motivated by the empirical finding that activation quantization is the dominant source of low-bit degradation and that aggressive joint retraining pushes the model into a different loss basin that harms transfer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that QAT models achieving higher ImageNet accuracy than FP32 actually generalize worse to downstream tasks (e.g., Q-Var's internal FP32 weights drop from 81.2% to 68.9%) challenges the implicit assumption that higher training accuracy implies better representations, and suggests the field should evaluate quantized models on transfer, not just in-domain accuracy. Practically, GPLQ's 100× speedup and sub-FP32 memory footprint make 4-bit ViT quantization accessible on consumer hardware, and the open-source toolkit lowers the barrier for practitioners. Open questions include whether the 'same basin' principle extends to sub-4-bit regimes, whether PCA feature mimicking can be adapted for detection/segmentation, and how the framework performs on LLMs or CNNs beyond the preliminary ResNet results.
