## Problem

do-SHAP provides causally-grounded Shapley attributions via interventional queries, but its estimand-based estimation requires deriving a distinct probabilistic formula and training separate ML models for each of the up to 2^|X| coalitions, making it infeasible on complex causal graphs. Standard SHAP variants (marginal and conditional) ignore the underlying causal structure, producing anti-causal or spurious attributions. The paper asks whether do-SHAP can be made practical and fast on arbitrary DAGs without manual, query-specific estimand engineering.

## Input / Output

The method requires a known causal DAG (possibly with latent confounders), an observational dataset from the joint distribution, a target variable Y, and a specific sample (x, y) to explain. It trains a single proxy SCM (e.g., a Deep Causal Graph with Normalizing Flows) on the observational data, then produces per-variable do-Shapley values that sum to E[Y|do(x)] − E[Y] (or y − E[Y] when the additive-noise theorem applies). The FRA additionally outputs irreducible coalitions, collapsing many subsets to a smaller set of unique ν-evaluations that are cached and reused across the permutation-based Shapley estimation.

## Method

The core insight is to replace do-SHAP's estimand-based estimation with an estimand-agnostic (EA) approach: train one proxy SCM matching the known DAG to learn the observational distribution, then estimate every interventional query ν(S) = E[Y|do(S=s)] by sampling from the intervened proxy SCM, eliminating the need for per-query estimands and separate models. To accelerate computation, the Frontier-Reducibility Algorithm (FRA) identifies, for each coalition S, an irreducible subset S' ⊆ S such that ν(S) = ν(S') by detecting 'frontiers'—subsets that block all directed paths from a node X to Y—thereby skipping redundant ν-evaluations and caching results. FRA runs in time linear in the coalition size and graph depth per call, and its Fr-cache persists across explanations on the same graph. A further theorem shows that under an additive-noise model for Y with no latent confounders, the gap between the do-SV sum and the observed y can be exactly attributed to the noise E_Y, enabling explanations of inaccessible Data Generating Processes at zero additional computational cost.

## Results

On the synthetic estimation experiment (30 seeds, N=1000), DCG and CNF achieve the lowest SHAP estimation loss L2, closely followed by DCN, while Linear-SCM and Marginal-SHAP show significantly higher error, confirming that do-SHAP and marginal-SHAP measure fundamentally different attributions. For the FRA speedup ablation, FRA reduces do-SHAP execution time by an order of magnitude relative to the no-cache baseline and by 2× relative to a simple cache; at K=100, execution drops from 26m01s (no-cache) to 25m20s (cache) to 21m14s (FRA-cache), with FRA itself costing only 8s total and yielding an average estimation error of 2.71×10⁻⁴. For sparse graphs (p=0.1), FRA reduces the number of ν-evaluations by ~90%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Marginal-SHAP, do-SHAP produces substantially different (and causally correct) attributions, as confirmed by the large gap in SHAP estimation loss and Feature Importance in Figure 4. Among SCM architectures, DCG and CNF achieve the best do-SV estimation performance (lowest L2 loss), with DCN close behind; Linear-SCM fails to model the distribution adequately and shows the worst estimation. DCG is preferred over CNF in practice because it admits latent confounders and trains orders of magnitude faster, despite slightly higher variance. Compared to Luther et al.'s [36] pair-skipping for conditional-SHAP, FRA provides a strictly greater speedup: Luther's method must eventually evaluate all 2^K coalitions, whereas FRA computes irreducible subsets once and caches them, yielding an order-of-magnitude improvement over the no-cache baseline and 2× over a simple cache. The FRA overhead is negligible (<3 μs per coalition for K≤20; 8s total for K=100), making it a near-free acceleration.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Jung et al.'s do-SHAP formulation, which defined Shapley values using interventional (do-) queries but relied on estimand-based estimation requiring a separate ML model per coalition, rendering it impractical beyond trivially small graphs. The key methodological departure is the adoption of the estimand-agnostic (EA) approach from Parafita & Vitrià, which trains a single Structural Causal Model (e.g., a Deep Causal Graph or Causal Normalizing Flow) to answer all identifiable causal queries through general SCM sampling procedures rather than query-specific estimands. The proposed Frontier-Reducibility Algorithm (FRA) extends Luther et al.'s skip-pair idea for conditional-SHAP by computing full irreducible coalitions via do-calculus-derived frontier properties, yielding a strictly greater speedup than pairwise skipping.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By making do-SHAP computable on complex graphs with a single trained model, this work removes the primary barrier to adopting causal Shapley explanations in practice, where practitioners lack the causal-inference expertise to derive per-coalition estimands. The FRA's order-of-magnitude speedup at negligible overhead (8 seconds total for K=100) makes the exponential cost of exact do-SHAP more tractable, and Theorem 4.9 provides a clean mechanism for explaining inaccessible DGPs via noise attribution. Key limitations remain: the causal graph must be known (no doubly-robust guarantees for EA methods), error propagation in node-by-node SCMs can degrade estimates, and the interventional value function is inherently a population measure, leaving counterfactual local explanations as an open direction.
