## Problem

Despite the m-out-of-n bootstrap being routinely used for robust inference with heavy-tailed data, no rigorous parameter-free guarantees (CLT, Edgeworth expansion, Berry-Esseen bound) existed for the Studentized m-out-of-n bootstrap quantile estimator under minimal moment assumptions. Prior work contained an incorrect variance formula (Cheung and Lee, 2005), and the theory had not been extended to dependent data such as Markov chains arising in MCMC or reinforcement learning.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a real-valued dataset of size n (i.i.d. or from a regenerative Markov chain with stationary distribution F), a quantile level p, and a subsample size m satisfying m = o(n) (or m = o(n^λ) for the Edgeworth expansion). It produces a Studentized bootstrap quantile statistic T_m^(boot), a parameter-free confidence interval of the form μ̂_n ± 1.96 σ̂_m^(boot)/√m, an Edgeworth expansion with remainder O_p(m^{-1/2}), and a matching uniform Berry-Esseen bound. The only distributional requirement is a finite α-th moment for some α > 0 plus F ∈ S_1(μ) (or S_2(μ) for the expansion).

## Method

The paper establishes a complete second-order theory via three linked technical pillars. First, it proves almost-sure variance consistency of the plug-in estimator σ̂_m^(boot) under only E|X_1|^α < ∞ (α > 0) by combining Borel-Cantelli tail control with Dvoretzky-Kiefer-Wolfowitz bounds to show uniform integrability of m(μ̂_m^(boot) − μ̂_n)², then applies Slutsky's theorem for the CLT. Second, it derives the first correct O(m^{-1/2}) Edgeworth expansion by exploiting a binomial representation of the bootstrap quantile (mF_n^(m)(x) ~ Binomial(m, F̄(x))) and a Taylor-series expansion of the CDF; the second-order correction is an even polynomial, unlike the asymmetric term in orthodox bootstrap, enabling two-sided parameter-free tests. Third, it extends the CLT to regenerative Markov chains by proving a new DKW-type concentration inequality for the empirical CDF of a split chain (via Nummelin splitting), then coupling the Markov-chain empirical process to the i.i.d. argument. The paper also corrects the algebraic errors in the Cheung–Lee variance formula and identifies the optimal subsample size m = O(n^{1/3}).

## Results

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical experiments or results tables. The headline quantitative contributions are: (i) a parameter-free CLT for the Studentized m-out-of-n bootstrap quantile under only a finite α-th moment condition (Theorem 1); (ii) an exact O_p(m^{-1/2}) Edgeworth expansion for the Studentized bootstrap distribution (Theorem 2), yielding a matching Berry-Esseen bound of O_p(m^{-1/2}) (Corollary 1); and (iii) an extension of the CLT to regenerative Markov chains (Theorem 3). The paper identifies m = O(n^{1/3}) as the optimal subsample size, matching the known minimax rate for median estimation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper makes no empirical comparisons, as its contribution is entirely theoretical. However, it provides important theoretical contrasts with prior work. Compared to the orthodox (full-sample) bootstrap Edgeworth expansion of Hall and Martin [1991], the m-out-of-n bootstrap expansion has a symmetric (even) second-order polynomial, whereas the orthodox bootstrap's is neither odd nor even — enabling two-sided parameter-free tests with an extra O_p(m^{-1}) error. The O_p(m^{-1/4} n^{-1/2}) remainder term in Theorem 2 replaces the O_p(n^{-3/4}) term in Hall and Martin's main theorem, recovering their result when m = n. The paper also corrects an algebraic error in the variance formula of Cheung and Lee [2005] (Lemmas 6–7), and extends the CLT to regenerative Markov chains — a setting where, to the authors' knowledge, no prior bootstrap-based confidence guarantees existed for quantile statistics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the m-out-of-n bootstrap framework of Bickel et al. (1992) by providing the first complete second-order theory—variance consistency, a parameter-free CLT, an O(m^{-1/2}) Edgeworth expansion, and a matching Berry-Esseen bound—for the Studentized quantile estimator under only a finite α-th moment condition (α > 0). It departs from Hall and Martin (1991), who studied the orthodox (full-sample) bootstrap, by showing the m-out-of-n variant yields a symmetric (even-polynomial) second-order correction, enabling two-sided parameter-free tests. The paper also corrects an algebraic error in the variance formula of Cheung and Lee (2005) and extends the analysis from i.i.d. data to regenerative Markov chains via the Nummelin splitting technique, a setting where no prior bootstrap quantile guarantees existed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The results provide rigorous, parameter-free confidence intervals for quantiles in settings where the orthodox bootstrap is known to fail (heavy-tailed data, irregular models) and where no prior theory existed (MCMC chains, offline MDP rewards). The counter-example (Proposition 1) showing the moment condition is essentially tight, and the identification of m = O(n^{1/3}) as the minimax-optimal subsample size, give practitioners concrete guidance. Key open questions remain: characterizing the precise heavy-tail boundary beyond the counter-example, extending to non-stationary or polynomially-ergodic chains, and deriving an Edgeworth expansion for the Markov chain setting (which would require a lattice Edgeworth theory for regenerative chains that does not yet exist).
