## Problem

In end-to-end 3D trackers using the tracking-by-attention paradigm, detection and tracking share model parameters but suffer from optimization difficulties that prior works attributed to representation conflicts or gradient flow issues. This paper identifies the root cause as two subtle effects of the self-attention mechanism: over-deduplication that suppresses high-quality object query candidates, and self-centric attention that isolates track queries from object queries, preventing synergistic multi-task learning.

## Input / Output

The method consumes N synchronized multi-camera RGB images (resolutions from 320×800 to 900×1600) at timestamp t along with track queries propagated from the previous frame, and outputs 3D bounding boxes (center, size, yaw, BEV velocity) with consistent instance IDs and class scores. A key design constraint is that the weight-shared parallel decoder and contrastive learning modules are used exclusively during training; at inference, only the standard decoder runs, so no additional latency is introduced regardless of the base tracker.

## Method

SynCL introduces a weight-shared parallel decoder during training: a standard S-decoder (with self-attention) and a cross-attention-only C-decoder that share parameters. In the C-decoder, object queries are supervised via one-to-many matching against persistent ground-truth targets (up to K=5 queries per GT), while track queries retain one-to-one identity-guided assignment. A Dynamic Query Filtering module uses a Gaussian Mixture Model over matching costs to set a progressive threshold that selects only reliable object queries for the one-to-many assignment, adapting as training progresses. Finally, Instance-aware Contrastive Learning with a kernel-based objective (MLP + Hadamard product) pulls object and track query embeddings for the same instance together in latent space while pushing apart different instances, bridging the self-centric attention gap without any inference-time overhead.

## Results

On the nuScenes validation set, SynCL with the StreamPETR detector achieves a state-of-the-art 58.9% AMOTA and 1.016 AMOTP, surpassing the previous best OneTrack by 4.1% AMOTA. On the nuScenes test set, SynCL reaches 58.8% AMOTA and 67.1% Recall, outperforming the leading two-stage tracker DORT by 1.2% AMOTA while maintaining end-to-end training. With the PETR detector configuration, SynCL achieves 50.7% AMOTA and 1.183 AMOTP at 5.2 FPS, matching or exceeding ADA-Track++ in tracking accuracy while running 1.6× faster.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SynCL consistently outperforms all compared baselines. Against the closest end-to-end competitor OneTrack [8], SynCL gains +4.1% AMOTA (58.9% vs. 54.8%) with StreamPETR and +2.8% AMOTA (50.7% vs. 47.9%) with PETR, while also achieving lower AMOTP. Compared to ADA-Track++ [7], SynCL matches AMOTA (50.7% vs. 50.4%) with PETR but runs 1.6× faster (5.2 vs. 3.2 FPS) due to not requiring an additional association module. On the test set, SynCL surpasses the dominant two-stage tracker DORT [18] by +1.2% AMOTA (58.8% vs. 57.6%) and +3.7% Recall (67.1% vs. 63.4%). Against earlier end-to-end methods—MUTR3D [4] (+6.4% AMOTA), PF-Track [5] (+2.8% AMOTA), DQTrack [11] (+6.1% AMOTA), and CC-3DT [39] (+7.8% AMOTA)—SynCL delivers substantial gains. The paper further demonstrates orthogonality with OneTrack: applying SynCL on top of a reproduced OneTrack framework yields an additional +4.6% AMOTA (PETR) and +2.8% AMOTA (StreamPETR), confirming the two methods address complementary sources of multi-task conflict.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the tracking-by-attention paradigm (MOTR, MUTR3D, PF-Track) by diagnosing the root cause of joint detection-tracking optimization difficulties as the self-attention mechanism's over-deduplication of object queries and self-centric attention for track queries, rather than the gradient conflicts (OneTrack) or representation conflicts (ADA-Track) previously identified. It borrows the weight-shared parallel decoder concept from DAC-DETR and kernel-based contrastive learning from Tsai et al., but repurposes them within a tracking-specific hybrid matching framework that applies one-to-many assignment to object queries and one-to-one to track queries. The key departure from prior work is that SynCL is a training-only, plug-and-play strategy that leaves inference unchanged, unlike ADA-Track++ (extra association module) or OneTrack (detector-specific attention masking).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SynCL provides a general, drop-in training strategy that improves both detection and tracking simultaneously without any inference overhead, making it broadly applicable to any tracking-by-attention tracker. The 58.9% AMOTA on nuScenes sets a new SOTA for camera-based 3D MOT, surpassing the previous leader OneTrack by 4.1% and the two-stage DORT by 1.2%. However, the authors acknowledge that one-stage training still underperforms their two-stage setup, suggesting the multi-task conflict is mitigated but not fully resolved; future work on decoupled incremental training (e.g., LoRA) and finer-grained one-stage strategies remains open. The increased training cost (0.3–0.4 h/epoch, 1–4 GB extra memory) is a practical consideration for resource-constrained settings.
