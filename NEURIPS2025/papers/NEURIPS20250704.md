## Problem

While LLMs excel at well-defined mathematical reasoning, they struggle with open-ended real-world mathematical modeling, which requires translating unstructured problem descriptions into rigorous, domain-grounded formal systems. Current LLM agents frequently omit critical assumptions, constraints, and abstractions, producing oversimplified and scientifically invalid models. This paper formalizes the task of LLM-powered mathematical modeling and introduces both a benchmark (MM-Bench, 111 problems from MCM/ICM 2000–2025) and an agent framework to systematically address this gap.

## Input / Output

The agent receives an open-ended problem specification comprising a textual background description, explicit problem requirements, an optional CSV dataset with variable descriptions, and dataset metadata. It produces a complete end-to-end solution: a formal mathematical model with stated assumptions, executable computational code, numerical results, and a structured LaTeX report organized into eight MCM/ICM-standard sections (abstract, problem restatement, assumptions, notation, analysis, solution, conclusion). The entire pipeline runs in approximately 15 minutes at a cost of ~$0.88 per task using GPT-4o.

## Method

MM-Agent decomposes modeling into four sequential stages: problem analysis (understanding, decomposition into subtasks, and dependency graph construction), mathematical modeling, computational solving, and report generation. The key technical contribution is the Hierarchical Mathematical Modeling Library (HMML), a three-tier tree of 98 modeling methods organized by domain → subdomain → method node, retrieved via DFS traversal with cosine-similarity scoring (using mGTE embeddings) that combines node-level and parent-level similarity. For each subtask, a Hierarchical Actor-Critic Modeling Optimization loop iteratively refines the modeling scheme: an actor agent generates a formulation conditioned on retrieved methods and dependency resources, while a critic agent evaluates rigor and provides targeted feedback for up to n_r refinement iterations. Computational solving leverages MLE-Solver for iterative code generation, testing, and repair, and the final report is compiled as LaTeX with iterative compilation checks to ensure document integrity.

## Results

On MM-Bench (32 problems from MCM/ICM 2021–2025), MM-Agent achieves an overall score of 8.85/10 with GPT-4o (2021–2024) and 8.92/10 with DeepSeek-R1-671B (2025), representing an 11.88% improvement over human expert solutions (7.91). The agent's advantage is most pronounced in Modeling Rigorousness (MR: 7.28–8.33 vs. 3.67–4.79 for direct LLMs) and Result and Bias Analysis (RBA: 8.43–8.58 vs. 4.50–5.75 for direct LLMs). Each task costs $0.88 (GPT-4o) or $0.56 (DeepSeek-R1) and takes ~15 minutes to complete.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MM-Agent outperforms all four agent baselines and both direct-LLM baselines across every evaluation dimension and both time periods. Against the strongest agent baseline DS-Agent, MM-Agent gains +0.99 overall (8.85 vs. 7.86, GPT-4o 2021–2024) and +0.48 (8.38 vs. 7.90, 2025), with the largest margins in RBA (+0.97) and MR (+0.20). ResearchAgent trails by +1.11 overall (8.85 vs. 7.74), while Agent Laboratory lags most at +1.57 (8.85 vs. 7.28), primarily due to weak RBA (5.56 vs. 8.44). Direct GPT-4o without agent orchestration scores only 6.28 overall, a 2.57-point gap that is concentrated in MR (3.86 vs. 7.28) and RBA (5.17 vs. 8.44), confirming that structured multi-agent workflows are essential for open-ended modeling. Notably, MM-Agent surpasses the human expert benchmark (Honorable Mention+ solutions) by 11.88% overall, and under official MCM/ICM 2025 protocols it assisted two undergraduate teams to the Finalist Award (top 2.0% of 27,456 teams).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves out a new task formulation by distinguishing open-ended mathematical modeling (where the agent must abstract, formulate, and solve) from well-defined mathematical reasoning (where the formulation is given). It builds on the MLE-Solver framework for iterative code generation and execution, and draws conceptual inspiration from hierarchical knowledge retrieval and actor-critic self-refinement, but repurposes these into a domain-specific pipeline (HMML + hierarchical actor-critic modeling) tailored to modeling. The baselines (DS-Agent, ResearchAgent, Agent Laboratory) are repurposed autonomous research agents rather than modeling-specific systems, underscoring that no prior work directly targets this task.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MM-Bench provides the first systematic benchmark for evaluating LLM agents on open-ended, real-world mathematical modeling, filling a gap between well-defined math benchmarks (MATH, OPTIBENCH) and the messier reality of translating phenomena into formal models. The practical validation—assisting teams to top 2.0% in MCM/ICM 2025—demonstrates real-world utility beyond synthetic evaluation. Open questions include the subjectivity of the 4-dimension scoring rubric (human-human agreement on Modeling Rigorousness is only 0.48), the small 32-problem test set, contamination risk as models are trained on competition solutions, and whether the framework generalizes to truly interdisciplinary or higher-order reasoning problems the authors acknowledge remain challenging.
