## Problem

Existing parallelizable spiking neuron models (PSN, sliding PSN) rely on dense floating-point matrix multiplications and require large neuron orders to capture long-term temporal dependencies, incurring prohibitive computational and memory costs for resource-restricted neuromorphic hardware. This paper addresses the gap between high long-term dependency learning ability and hardware-friendly, multiplication-free inference in parallelizable spiking neurons.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252213/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes binary spike sequences X ∈ R^{T×C} (T timesteps, C channels) produced by upstream synaptic layers (e.g., convolutional or fully-connected layers encoding audio, sequential images, or neuromorphic event streams into spikes). Each neuron layer outputs a binary spike S[t][c] ∈ {0,1} per channel per timestep, determined by a channel-wise threshold. The final network produces a class label over 10, 100, or 1000 categories depending on the dataset. Notably, the neuron order k (e.g., 2–16) is decoupled from the sequence length T, enabling variable-length input processing.

## Method

The proposed mul-free channel-wise PSN replaces the shared-weight sliding PSN with per-channel weight vectors W ∈ R^{C×k} and introduces sawtooth dilations (d^{l+1} = d^l mod 3 + 1) across stacked layers to expand the temporal receptive field without increasing k, keeping FLOPs identical to the sliding PSN. To eliminate all multiplications during inference, weights are quantized to powers of two via Q(W) = sign(W)·2^{round(log₂|W|)}, converting the convolution into bit-shift-and-add operations (H[t][c] = Σ X[t−(k−1−i)·d][c] << log₂(W_q[c][i])). A whole Straight-Through Estimator (∂W_q/∂W = 1) is used to avoid the numerical instability of the analytical gradient. For GPU training acceleration, an autoselect algorithm benchmarks multiple implementations (custom Triton CUDA kernels, Vmap+matrix multiplication, Conv1d, Conv2d) under both time-first and time-last data layouts, selecting the fastest configuration per layer at the start of training.

## Results

On sequential CIFAR-10, the mul-free channel-wise PSN achieves 91.17% top-1 accuracy, outperforming PMSN (90.97%), PSN (88.45%), and LIF (81.50%). On sequential CIFAR-100, it reaches 66.21%, surpassing PSN by 4.0% and PMSN by 0.13%. On the SHD spoken-digit dataset, it achieves 95.50±0.36% accuracy with order k=2 and sawtooth dilations, exceeding the previous SOTA PMSN (95.10%). On the DVS-Lip neuromorphic lip-reading dataset, it achieves 70.9% accuracy with a pure SNN backend, trailing only the non-pure-SNN SpikGRU2+ method (75.3%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The mul-free channel-wise PSN outperforms all PSN-family baselines (PSN, Masked PSN, Sliding PSN) by 2.7–5.4% on sequential CIFAR-10 and 4.0–5.5% on CIFAR-100, while using only half the neuron order (k=16 vs. k=32 for Sliding/Masked PSN). It edges out PMSN by 0.2% on CIFAR-10 and 0.13% on CIFAR-100, and surpasses it on SHD (95.50% vs. 95.10%). Against traditional serial neurons (LIF, PLIF, GLIF), the gains are substantial: 9.67% over LIF and 7.51% over GLIF on CIFAR-10. On DVS-Lip, the method (70.9%) loses to the SpikGRU2+ backend of Dampfhoffer et al. (75.3%) and the ANN BiGRU baseline of Tan et al. (72.1%), but surpasses the pure-SNN PLIF+FC baseline (68.1%) and Bulzomi et al. (60.2%), while being deployable on neuromorphic hardware unlike the non-pure-SNN SpikGRU2+.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Parallel Spiking Neuron (PSN) family introduced by Fang et al. [15], specifically the sliding PSN variant, by introducing three key modifications: channel-wise separable convolutions to capture inter-channel feature disparities, sawtooth-pattern dilated convolutions to expand the temporal receptive field without increasing the neuron order, and power-of-2 weight quantization that replaces all multiplications with bit-shift operations. The bit-shift quantization draws from the ShiftAddNet/DeepShift line of work in quantized ANNs, while the dilated convolution strategy borrows from the spatial segmentation literature. The autoselect training acceleration algorithm is a novel practical contribution that benchmarks multiple GPU implementation strategies (custom CUDA kernels, Vmap+MM, Conv1d/Conv2d) to automatically select the fastest configuration for a given SNN architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~9× reduction in inference energy (from 91.62 μJ to 10.66 μJ per image) and the ability to match or exceed sliding PSN performance with order k=2 versus k=32 make this neuron substantially more deployable on resource-constrained neuromorphic chips like Tianjic or Loihi, where multiplication units are expensive in both area and energy. The autoselect algorithm provides a practical, reusable framework for accelerating parallelizable spiking neuron training that benefits the broader SNN community. However, the training speed remains slightly slower than PSN due to the quantization overhead (requiring two forward passes), and the method's advantage is most pronounced in inference rather than training, which limits its appeal for researchers focused purely on GPU training throughput. An open question is whether the power-of-2 quantization granularity becomes a bottleneck for tasks requiring finer weight precision.
