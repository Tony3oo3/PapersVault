## Problem

CLIP's global image-text alignment objective and 77-token text limit cause it to miss fine-grained visual details such as color, quantity, and spatial relationships, a weakness inherited by downstream MLLMs and highlighted by benchmarks like MMVP. Existing remedies—region-level contrastive learning, synthetic hard negatives from text-to-image models, or simple token-limit extension—either require complex supervision, lack visual fidelity, or degrade short-text performance without guaranteeing improved fine-grained alignment.

## Input / Output

The model consumes RGB images at resolutions from 224 to 384 pixels and text inputs of variable length: original short alt-text captions, generated long descriptive captions averaging ~300 tokens, and instruction-editing tuples (source image, target image, editing instruction, source caption, target caption). It outputs aligned image and text embeddings in a shared cosine-similarity space, with the text encoder modified via RoPE to handle sequences well beyond the original 77-token limit while preserving short-text compatibility.

## Method

CLIP-IN is a two-stage framework. Stage 1 replaces the CLIP text encoder's absolute positional embeddings with Rotary Positional Encodings (RoPE) and distills from the frozen original encoder using long captions, so the student retains short-text capability while gaining variable-length processing. Stage 2 jointly trains the full model with three loss terms: (a) a symmetric hard negative contrastive loss applied to instruction-editing data from UltraEdit (4M samples), where (source_image, target_caption) and (target_image, source_caption) serve as hard negatives in both image-to-text and text-to-image directions; (b) standard contrastive loss on long captions (~300 tokens generated by InternVL) paired with their images; and (c) standard contrastive loss on original short captions to preserve baseline performance. The key insight is repurposing real instruction-editing pairs—originally designed for image manipulation—as a source of visually grounded, fine-grained hard negatives that are semantically minimal but perceptually distinct, enabling symmetric training in both modalities without the domain shift or lack of control inherent in text-to-image-generated negatives.

## Results

On zero-shot ImageNet-1K classification, CLIP-IN achieves 77.0% top-1 accuracy (ViT-L/14@336) and 83.4% (ViT-SO/14@224), matching or exceeding strong baselines like DFN-H and SigLIP2. On fine-grained visual perception (MMVP), the method improves average accuracy from 18.5% to 30.4% (+11.9pp) with ViT-L/14@224, with particularly large gains in State/Condition (+33.3pp) and Feature Detection (+20.0pp). When integrated into LLaVA-1.5, CLIP-IN's visual encoder boosts MME-Perception from 1510.7 to 1709.0 and MMBench (English) from 64.3 to 72.9, while reducing visual hallucinations (POPE-adv: 84.2→85.2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against OpenAI CLIP (ViT-L/14@224), CLIP-IN improves ImageNet-1K by +0.8pp, Flickr retrieval by +12.2pp, and DCI long-caption retrieval by +16.4pp, demonstrating substantial gains in both classification and fine-grained retrieval. Versus SigLIP2 (ViT-SO/14@224), the method matches ImageNet-1K (83.4 vs. 83.2) while substantially outperforming on long-caption retrieval (ShareGPT4V: 67.3 vs. 62.0; DCI: 52.5/54.4 vs. 45.4/50.0), indicating that instruction-editing hard negatives and long captions specifically target the retrieval weaknesses of modern CLIP variants. Compared to FG-CLIP (ViT-L/14@336), CLIP-IN achieves higher ImageNet-1K accuracy (77.0 vs. 76.1) but trails on long-caption retrieval (76.4 vs. 81.8 avg), a gap the authors attribute to FG-CLIP's 1B training pairs versus their 18M; scaling to 30M pairs closes this gap (85.1 vs. 81.8). Against DFN-H (ViT-H/14@224), CLIP-IN matches ImageNet-1K (83.4) and edges ahead on all retrieval metrics (Flickr +0.8pp, COCO +1.3pp, ShareGPT4V +1.7pp, DCI +1.6pp). On the MMVP fine-grained perception benchmark, CLIP-IN outperforms DIVA (26.1 vs. 25.2 at ViT-L/14@336) and improves over DFN-H (41.5 vs. 39.9 at ViT-H/14@224). In the MLLM setting (LLaVA-1.5), CLIP-IN's encoder surpasses DIVA on MME (1709.0 vs. 1500.6) and MMBench-EN (72.9 vs. 66.4) but trails slightly on MMVP (28.0 vs. 31.3), suggesting DIVA's diffusion-based approach retains an edge on certain perceptual patterns when used as a visual backbone for LLMs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends CLIP's contrastive training paradigm by repurposing instruction-editing datasets (UltraEdit) as a structured source of hard negative image-text pairs, departing from prior synthetic-negative approaches like TripletCLIP that rely on text-to-image generation and lack fine-grained visual control. The symmetric hard negative loss generalizes one-directional formulations (NegCLIP, TripletCLIP) to both image-to-text and text-to-image directions, enabled by the bidirectional structure of editing tuples. For long-caption processing, the paper adapts TULIP's RoPE + knowledge distillation strategy rather than the positional interpolation used by Long-CLIP, and positions its contribution as complementary to region-based methods (GLIP, FG-CLIP) and self-distillation approaches (SILC, TIPS) by operating at the global representation level without requiring region proposals.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in showing that naturally occurring editing pairs provide more controlled and effective hard negatives than synthetic generation, and that the synergy between fine-grained editing supervision and broad long-caption context yields MLLM improvements (reduced hallucination, better MMVP) that neither data source achieves alone. A notable open question is whether the 30M-pair data efficiency claim (surpassing FG-CLIP's 1B pairs on long-caption retrieval) holds at even larger scales or with different caption generators. The approach's dependence on high-quality instruction-editing data and MLLM-generated captions introduces a quality ceiling, and the absolute MMVP scores (30–36%) suggest substantial room for further improvement in fine-grained perception.
