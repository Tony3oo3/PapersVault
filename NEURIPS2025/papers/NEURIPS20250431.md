## Problem

Facial personalization models that inject identity embeddings into cross-attention layers suffer a significant drop in prompt consistency: the identity signal disrupts the representation of other attribute tokens (hair, expression, accessories), causing the generated face to ignore or misrender the requested attributes. The paper identifies that the underlying foundation model's prompt-following capability remains intact but is overridden, and asks whether this latent knowledge can be leveraged to restore prompt consistency without modifying the well-trained cross-attention modules that encode identity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250431/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Given a single reference face image and a text prompt specifying one or more facial attributes (e.g., 'a man with blonde curly hair, laughing happily'), FreeCure produces a personalized face image that preserves the reference identity while faithfully rendering the requested attributes. The method is entirely training-free and operates as a plug-in during the denoising loop of existing personalization models built on Stable Diffusion v1.5, SDXL, or FLUX. It requires an external face-parsing model (BiSeNet or Segment-Anything) to generate spatial masks for the target attributes, and runs dual forward passes (with and without identity embedding) at each denoising timestep.

## Method

FreeCure introduces two complementary mechanisms that exploit the observation that self-attention layers in personalization models retain the foundation model's prompt-following knowledge. First, the Foundation-Aware Self-Attention (FASA) module concatenates the key and value matrices from a 'foundation denoising' pass (identity embedding zeroed) onto those of the 'personalization denoising' pass within each self-attention layer, so that the personalization query can attend to foundation-derived attribute features; a binary scaling mask derived from semantic segmentation localizes this injection to only the target attribute regions, preventing identity disruption. Second, Asymmetric Prompt Guidance (APG) performs diffusion inversion with a bare template prompt (e.g., 'a man') and then re-denoises from an intermediate timestep using the full attribute prompt without identity embedding, restoring abstract attributes such as facial expressions. For FLUX's unified DiT architecture, FASA applies the mask selectively to the visual-query/visual-key cross-branch while preserving cross-modal attention patterns. The entire pipeline is training-free, requires no modification to the personalization model's weights, and is controlled by two hyperparameters (scaling factor ω≈2.0 and inversion start ratio γ≈0.5).

## Results

FreeCure is a training-free framework that improves prompt consistency (PC) across 7 facial personalization baselines built on SDv1.5, SDXL, and FLUX. The headline metric is PC (CLIP-T score), where FreeCure achieves gains ranging from +3.55% (PuLID-SDXL: 25.16→26.05) to +15.91% (FastComposer: 18.14→21.02). The combined PC×IF harmonic mean improves in every case (e.g., FastComposer 25.55→27.80, PuLID-FLUX 34.52→36.95, InfiniteYou 36.62→38.05), while identity fidelity (IF) drops only modestly (−1.42% to −5.02%). Face diversity (LPIPS) also consistently increases, indicating less mode collapse.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FreeCure is evaluated as a plug-and-play enhancement on 7 encoder-based personalization methods spanning three foundation models. On SDv1.5-based methods, the largest PC gain is on FastComposer (+15.91%, 18.14→21.02), followed by Face-Diffuser (+8.76%) and Face2Diffusion (+6.12%); all three also see PC×IF hMean improvements of +3.15% to +8.82%. On SDXL-based methods, InstantID gains +7.90% PC and PhotoMaker gains +8.11%, while PuLID-SDXL shows the smallest relative gain (+3.55%) likely because it already has the highest baseline PC (25.16). On FLUX-based methods, PuLID-FLUX benefits most (+10.53% PC) and InfiniteYou, which starts with the highest IF (79.71), still gains +6.23% PC with only a −3.24% IF drop. Across all 7 baselines, FreeCure wins on PC, Face Div., and PC×IF hMean without exception; the only consistent trade-off is a small IF decrease (−1.42% to −5.02%), which the authors attribute to the positive increase in facial diversity rather than identity loss, confirmed by a user study showing equal preference on identity fidelity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the line of attention manipulation in diffusion models (Prompt-to-Prompt, Attend-and-Excite, self-attention-based style transfer) but departs from the dominant strategy of modifying cross-attention layers to balance identity and prompt consistency. Instead, it exploits the observation that personalization models retain their foundation model's prompt-controllability in self-attention layers even when cross-attention is dominated by identity embeddings, and uses a dual denoising paradigm (with/without identity embedding) as both a diagnostic tool and an information source. The FASA mechanism conceptually extends self-attention-based control (e.g., MASActrl, DreamMatcher) by injecting foundation-model knowledge through concatenated key-value matrices with spatially-masked scaling, while APG borrows the diffusion inversion framework for abstract attribute restoration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FreeCure offers a practical, training-free plug-and-play solution to a persistent trade-off in facial personalization (identity fidelity vs. prompt consistency) that has resisted resolution across fine-tuning and encoder-based methods. Its validation across seven personalization models spanning three foundation architectures (SDv1.5, SDXL, FLUX/DiT) demonstrates broad applicability, and the 'hidden foundation knowledge' insight reframes how practitioners think about what personalization models actually contain. Open questions include whether the approach extends to auto-regressive architectures, how it handles transparent or occluded attributes, and whether the slight identity fidelity cost (1–5%) can be further reduced. The reliance on external segmentation models (BiSeNet, Segment-Anything) for mask generation also introduces a dependency that could limit robustness in edge cases.
