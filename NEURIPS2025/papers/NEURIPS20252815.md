## Problem

Traditional hierarchical clustering algorithms operate exclusively in an unsupervised setting, even in domains like high-energy physics where simulators produce labeled hierarchy datasets for jet clustering. No generative model existed that could learn the full posterior distribution over hierarchies in a supervised manner, leaving a gap between the availability of simulator-generated ground-truth trees and the unsupervised methods actually deployed in jet-clustering pipelines.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252815/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes as input the four-momentum vectors (E, p_x, p_y, p_z) of observed leaf particles and, during generation, the current probabilistic hierarchy state. It outputs a discrete binary hierarchy with n leaves and n−1 internal nodes, parametrized by two row-stochastic matrices A (leaf-to-internal parent assignments) and B (internal-to-internal parent assignments, upper-triangular to enforce a valid rooted tree). The generation is conditional: p(T | X), where X denotes the leaf features, and the model must produce hierarchies satisfying physical constraints such as invariant-mass ordering at every internal node.

## Method

TreeGen extends Bayesian Flow Networks to categorical distributions over hierarchies by treating each row of the adjacency matrices A and B as a categorical distribution over parent choices. A Graph Transformer backbone predicts the discrete hierarchy from the current probabilistic hierarchy, and a novel log-based entropy scheduler α_t = −(C + a·log₂K)/K · ln(1−t) ensures smooth, K-independent entropy decay from uniform to one-hot across varying numbers of candidate parents. Training minimizes cross-entropy between the network's predicted hierarchy and the ground truth, while sampling performs iterative Bayesian updates: the network's prediction replaces the unknown true class in a Gaussian log-space update, and a softmax yields the next probabilistic hierarchy. Because all intermediate states remain on the probability simplex by construction, the model inherently produces valid tree structures, unlike flow-matching baselines that can leave the simplex.

## Results

TreeGen achieves ≥0.93 valid-hierarchy fraction and log-likelihood ratios within 0.025 of 1.0 across all five GINKGO jet datasets (QCD-S/M/L, W-S/M), consistently outperforming both generative baselines. On the largest dataset (QCD-L, up to 99 nodes), TreeGen maintains 0.943 valid fraction and 0.975 LLH fraction, while BFN drops to 0.416 and 0.898 respectively. The model also approximates the exact posterior (via cluster trellis) closely on QCD-S, and via rejection sampling exceeds the greedy agglomerative baseline's log-likelihood after only 16 samples.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TreeGen outperforms both generative baselines (CatFlow and BFN) on every dataset for both valid-hierarchy fraction and log-likelihood ratio, with the gap widening as jet size increases: on QCD-M, CatFlow's valid fraction collapses to 0.236 and BFN's to 0.645, while TreeGen retains 0.977. Against the traditional jet-clustering algorithms (Table 5), TreeGen dominates on valid fraction across all QCD datasets (e.g., 0.997 vs. 0.840 for anti-k_t on QCD-S; 0.943 vs. 0.440 on QCD-L), though the k_t algorithm achieves slightly higher LLH fractions (1.029–1.065) at the cost of very low validity (0.008–0.302). The anti-k_t algorithm is the strongest traditional baseline on validity but still trails TreeGen by 15–50 percentage points. CatFlow's inferior performance is attributed to its flow leaving the probability simplex during generation, violating the categorical constraints that define valid jet hierarchies.

## Relation to Prior Work

TreeGen extends Bayesian Flow Networks (BFNs) and Bayesian Sample Inference (BSI) from Gaussian to categorical distributions, adapting the Bayesian update mechanism to the probabilistic hierarchy parametrization introduced by Zügner et al. for end-to-end hierarchical clustering. Key departures from standard BFNs include a cross-entropy training loss (replacing the precision-weighted MSE), omission of auxiliary distributions, a new entropy scheduler that normalizes across varying category counts, and explicit extension of the generative process to tree-structured data. The paper also positions itself against the unsupervised hierarchical clustering literature and the GINKGO-based jet clustering pipeline, offering a learned generative alternative that does not require analytical likelihoods at inference time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TreeGen provides the first generative framework purpose-built for hierarchies, demonstrating that Bayesian flow networks can faithfully model distributions over discrete tree structures while maintaining physical validity constraints. The entropy scheduler is a practical contribution enabling consistent hyperparameters across hierarchies of different sizes, and the feature ablation reveals that physics-inspired features (especially invariant mass) are far more critical than structural features for validity. Open questions include scaling to non-binary trees, applying the model to real detector data with measurement noise, and whether the quadratic-complexity graph transformer can be replaced with more efficient architectures for very large jets.
