## Problem

When a language model's alignment no longer matches user needs—whether due to overthinking in reasoning models or excessive sycophancy in chatbots—retraining under a new reward signal is prohibitively expensive (e.g., ~70,000 A100 GPU-hours to replicate DeepSeek-R1). Existing decoding-time realignment (DeRa) requires running both the reference and aligned models simultaneously, doubling inference cost, and offers no mechanism to bake realignment capability into a single deployable model. The paper asks: can we realign LMs efficiently during training and also expose a continuous alignment knob at inference time within one model?

## Input / Output

At training time, TrRa consumes a text query sequence and a scalar interpolation parameter λ to construct a per-token teacher distribution from the fused logits of the reference and aligned models; the student (reference model) is trained to match this teacher via KL divergence. At inference time, InRa takes the same text query plus a user-chosen λ (analogous to a temperature setting) and produces a single generated response whose alignment strength is smoothly interpolated between the reference (λ=0) and fully aligned (λ=1) behaviors, with λ>1 enabling extrapolation beyond the trained alignment. The model internally maintains two parallel hidden-state paths (adapter and original bottom layer) that are merged only at the final logit layer, so the KV-cache footprint doubles relative to a standard model.

## Method

The framework has two orthogonal components. TrRa (Training-time Realignment) repurposes DeRa's per-token logit interpolation—softmax[λ·h_θ(β) + (1−λ)·h_ref]—as a *teacher distribution* for knowledge distillation, so the reference model is realigned by minimizing KL divergence to this controllable teacher; sweeping λ yields a family of realigned models without re-running RL, and iterating the procedure (TrRa-iter) further compresses reasoning tokens. InRa (Inference-time Realignment) duplicates the LM's bottom decoder layer as an identity copy (zero-initializing the attention output projection W_out and MLP down-projection W_down so the layer is initially a no-op), inserts it before the original layers, and fine-tunes only this adapter on a reward signal (SFT, DPO, or TrRa). During inference, input embeddings are fed simultaneously into the adapter and the original bottom layer; both hidden-state streams propagate through the remaining frozen layers and are combined at the LM head via the interpolation coefficient λ, giving a single model that smoothly spans reference-to-aligned behavior. The design is motivated by the empirical finding that bottom layers dominate preference learning (Sec. 5.1), and the identity initialization guarantees the adapter starts from the original distribution, making it a lightweight proxy for hyperparameter search before committing to full fine-tuning.

## Results

On the reasoning task (Table 1), TrRa with λ=1.5 applied to DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B achieves 41.25% Pass@1 on AIME-24, 30.83% on AIME-25, and 85.20% on MATH-500, while reducing token usage by 37.48% compared to the reference model. The iterative variant (TrRa-iter1, λ=2.0) pushes token reduction to 54.63% with roughly comparable accuracy (29.17/25.00/81.35). On the dialogue alignment task (Table 2), InRa with λ=1.0 matches DPO full fine-tuning on AlpacaEval2 LC (25.74% vs 25.20% for Qwen2.5-7B) and Arena-Hard WR (45.10% vs 45.50%) while training only 222M parameters in 50 minutes versus 7264M parameters in 3h35min for full fine-tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DeepScaleR-1.5B-Preview (the RL-trained aligned model requiring 3,800 A100 hours), TrRa λ=1.5 wins on AIME-24 (41.25 vs 37.50) and AIME-25 (30.83 vs 30.41), matches on MATH-500 (85.20), and achieves greater token reduction (37.48% vs 33.86%) at a fraction of the training cost (200 steps on 4k–8k context). Against the reference model DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B, TrRa λ=1.5 improves all three benchmarks while cutting tokens by 37.48%. In the dialogue setting, InRa λ=1.0 matches DPO full fine-tuning on AlpacaEval2 and Arena-Hard (within 0.4% on Qwen2.5-7B) while using only 222M trainable parameters and 50 minutes of training versus 7264M parameters and 3h35min. Compared to DeRa λ=2.0, InRa achieves comparable alignment scores in a single model rather than requiring two separate models at inference, and extrapolation (λ>1) can surpass DPO full fine-tuning on Arena-Hard (e.g., 21.80% vs 11.60% on Llama3.2-3B). Against LoRA (r=8 and r=128), the layer adapter achieves equal or better AlpacaEval2/Arena-Hard scores with substantially shorter training time (50 min vs 1h50min) and greater hyperparameter stability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on DeRa (Liu et al., 2024), which demonstrated that alignment strength can be adjusted at inference time by interpolating logits between a reference and an aligned model. The key conceptual leap is repurposing DeRa's logit interpolation as a *training* signal via knowledge distillation (TrRa), rather than treating it solely as an inference-time decoding trick. The layer adapter (InRa) draws on progressive depth-growth literature (Gong et al., Llama-Pro) and LoRA's identity-initialization principle, but applies them specifically to the bottom layer to enable single-model inference-time realignment without requiring two separate models as DeRa does. The framework is positioned as orthogonal to existing PEFT methods (LoRA, adapters) and preference-learning algorithms (DPO, PPO), which can be used as the training signal injected into the adapter.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for deployment: TrRa-iter achieves 54.63% token reduction on a 1.5B reasoning model without accuracy loss, and the layer adapter matches full DPO fine-tuning performance with only ~3% of the parameters and 1/4 the training time. The inference-time λ knob provides a user-facing control analogous to temperature, enabling dynamic switching between fast/slow thinking or varying 3H alignment strength. Key limitations include the doubled KV-cache overhead for InRa (reducing vLLM throughput), the observed alignment tax in multi-turn dialogue, and the requirement that both a reference and an aligned model already exist. Open questions include KV-cache compression for the dual-path architecture, contrastive reward signals to further encourage deeper reasoning, and whether the bottom-layer preference generalizes across architectures and tasks.
