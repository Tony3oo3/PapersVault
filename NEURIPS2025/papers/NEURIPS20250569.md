## Problem

Designing revenue-optimal, dominant-strategy incentive compatible (DSIC) combinatorial auctions for a single bidder remains unsolved because the bundle space grows exponentially with the number of items, making explicit per-bundle probability representations intractable. Prior differentiable-economics methods either handle only 2–10 items, restrict to limited mechanism classes (e.g., VVCA), or fail to guarantee exact DSIC, leaving no scalable path to fully general single-bidder CA mechanisms.

## Input / Output

The auctioneer knows a distribution F over bidder valuation functions (expressed in XOR bidding language as sets of bundle-value atoms) but not the realized valuation. At test time, the bidder reports their full valuation function, and the mechanism assigns them the menu element maximizing their utility, yielding a (possibly randomized) bundle allocation and a price. Each menu element does not name a single bundle but a distribution over bundles, represented compactly by a low-dimensional initial distribution (mixture of D Dirac/Gaussian components in R^m) plus a shared ODE vector field, rather than an explicit 2^m probability vector.

## Method

BUNDLEFLOW represents each menu element's bundle distribution as the image of a tractable initial distribution under a continuous normalizing flow governed by the ODE ds_t = φ(t,s_t)dt, with the specific vector-field form φ(t,s_t) = η(t)·Q(s_0)·s_t chosen so that the Liouville equation yields a closed-form, fully differentiable log-density. Training proceeds in two stages: (1) Flow Initialization fixes the initial distribution and trains the vector field (via a rectified-flow-style loss) so the final distribution's support covers all feasible bundles; (2) Menu Optimization freezes the vector field and backpropagates a revenue-maximizing loss through the flow to update per-element initial distributions (weights and means) and prices. DSIC is guaranteed by self-bid independence (the menu is trained on samples from F, not the bidder's bid) and agent-optimizing selection (exact utility computation via finite-support initial distributions with σ_d = 1e-20, and hard argmax at test time). The shared vector field across all K menu elements keeps the parameter count independent of menu size, while per-element initial distributions provide the flexibility to represent diverse bundle distributions.

## Results

On the CATS benchmark, BUNDLEFLOW achieves 1.11–2.23× higher expected revenue than all baselines for 50–150 items across both Regions and Arbitrary environments with uniform and normal valuations. For example, with 50 items and normal valuations in the Regions environment, BUNDLEFLOW earns 603.70 vs. 270.23 (RochetNet), 459.97 (Menu+), and 319.06 (Grand Bundle). The method also scales to 500 items (revenue 397.33 vs. 375.00 for RochetNet) and requires 3.6–9.5× fewer training iterations and ~80% less wall-clock training time than RochetNet at 50–100 items.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BUNDLEFLOW outperforms all five baselines (VCG, RochetNet, Grand Bundle, Menu+, Menu-) across every tested setting with more than 10 items, with revenue gains ranging from 1.11× (at m=150 in some environments) to 2.23× (at m=50 in the Arbitrary Normal setting, where BUNDLEFLOW earns 646.37 vs. 478.67 for Menu+). The most direct learning-based comparison is with RochetNet [Dutting et al., JACM 2024], which uses item-wise product-distribution allocations; BUNDLEFLOW's flow-based bundle distributions yield substantially higher revenue (e.g., 603.70 vs. 270.23 at m=50 Regions Normal) while also converging 3.6–9.5× faster in training iterations. VCG yields zero revenue in the single-bidder setting since the externality is zero. The Menu+ and Menu- baselines, which use fixed subsets of bundles with learned prices, are outperformed by BUNDLEFLOW in all settings with m ≥ 50, confirming that the learned randomized bundle distributions provide a meaningful representational advantage over hand-crafted bundle selections. At m=10, where baselines with 5K menu elements can enumerate all 1024 bundles, BUNDLEFLOW achieves comparable (slightly lower) revenue, indicating its advantage is most pronounced at scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

BUNDLEFLOW repurposes the continuous normalizing flow / Neural ODE framework (Chen et al. 2018) and the rectified flow training objective (Liu et al. 2022) from generative modeling to auction mechanism optimization, where there is no known target distribution to match. It is built on the menu-based differentiable economics paradigm of RochetNet (Dutting et al. 2024), which guarantees DSIC via self-bid-independent, agent-optimizing menus, but prior work in this line was limited to non-combinatorial or very small (≤10 item) CA settings. The key conceptual departure is using the Liouville equation to obtain a tractable, fully differentiable closed-form for the final bundle distribution, enabling gradient-based revenue optimization over an exponentially large bundle space without explicit enumeration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method to deliver a DSIC, fully general, revenue-optimizing single-bidder combinatorial auction that scales to 500 items, closing a gap that has persisted since CAs were formally defined in 1982. The ablation showing a 52.7% revenue collapse when forcing deterministic (D=1) bundle assignments versus allowing even minimal randomization (D=2) reveals that randomized allocations are not merely a convenience but a structural necessity for optimal CA design — a finding with implications beyond this specific architecture. The main open question is extension to multi-bidder CAs: the paper sketches a GemNet-style adaptation but identifies the exponential-dimensionality MILP grid as a prohibitive bottleneck, leaving the most practically important setting unresolved.
