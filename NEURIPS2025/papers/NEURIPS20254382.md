## Problem

Process reward models (PRMs) provide dense per-step feedback that excels at test-time scaling of LLMs, yet their use in reinforcement fine-tuning is plagued by reward hacking where models exploit high-reward patterns (e.g., verbose 'thinking') instead of actually solving problems. The paper identifies the root cause as a mismatch between the canonical summation-form credit assignment used in RL training (cumulative gamma-decayed rewards) and the min-aggregation that PRMs rely on at inference, and asks how to align these two objectives.

## Input / Output

The method consumes math problem prompts and produces multi-step reasoning responses with a boxed final answer; a PRM scores each generated step, and an optional verifiable reward checks the final answer against ground truth. The PRM is trained on PRM800K (human-annotated step correctness), while RFT uses ~8,000 problems from MATH (difficulty 3–5), with only 10% of problems receiving ground-truth verifiable rewards in the mixed PURE-PRM+VR setting. Evaluation is on five competition-level benchmarks (AIME24, AMC23, MATH500, Minerva Math, OlympiadBench) using greedy decoding pass@1.

## Method

The core contribution replaces the standard summation-form credit assignment (Q = E[Σ γ^(i−t) r_i]) with a min-form formulation (Q = E[min(r_t, …, r_n)]), aligning the training objective with the min-aggregation used in PRM-based Best-of-N at test time. Practically, this is implemented by transforming each step's process reward via a temperature-based softmax (Eq. 5) that upweights lower rewards, so that as T→0 only the worst step's reward survives in the return—requiring no changes to the existing PPO/RL codebase. Theoretically, the min-form bounds Q-value estimation error by a single-step ε rather than the sum-form's ε/(1−γ), preventing error accumulation over long trajectories and constraining the value range to match the reward range. For advantage estimation, the paper uses RLOO with a token-level baseline for process rewards (normalized by max generation length to avoid length bias), and supports mixing dense process rewards with sparse verifiable rewards. Validated on three Qwen2.5 base models, min-form PRM-based RFT matches verifiable-reward-only methods within 30% of training steps, while sum-form collapses training within 25 steps.

## Results

On Qwen2.5-Math-7B, PURE-PRM+VR (min-form credit assignment with 10% verifiable rewards) achieves 53.3% average pass@1 accuracy across 5 benchmarks, with a standout 82.5% on AMC23 and 82.6% on MATH-500. The PRM-only variant (PURE-PRM) reaches 49.3% average, matching or exceeding the verifiable-reward-only variant (PURE-VR, 48.3%) while requiring only ~30% of the training steps. On the smaller Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B, PURE-PRM+VR improves the base model from 33.5% to 43.3% average. The trained PRM (PURE-PRM-7B) achieves 62.6% BoN@1024 on MATH and a state-of-the-art 57.5 F1 on ProcessBench.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SimpleRL-Zoo (verifiable-reward RFT replicating DeepSeek R1-Zero), PURE-PRM+VR wins by 4.5 points on Qwen2.5-7B (47.6 vs 43.1) and 5.3 points on Qwen2.5-Math-7B (53.3 vs 48.0), while PURE-PRM alone already matches or exceeds SimpleRL-Zoo on Qwen2.5-Math-7B (49.3 vs 48.0) using only ~30% of the training steps. Against Eurus-2-7B-PRIME (implicit PRM via DPO-style formulation), PURE-PRM+VR leads by 4.4 points in average accuracy (53.3 vs 48.9) on Qwen2.5-Math-7B, with the largest gap on AMC23 (82.5 vs 57.8). Against Qwen2.5-7B-DPO-Zero (iterative DPO), PURE-PRM+VR wins by 6.3 points (53.3 vs 47.0). For the PRM itself, PURE-PRM-7B is competitive with DeepSeek-PRM-7B on BoN@1024 (91.6% vs 93.0% on GSM8K; 62.6% vs 58.1% on MATH) and surpasses all prior PRMs on ProcessBench (57.5 vs 56.5 F1). Notably, the sum-form credit assignment variant of PURE-PRM collapses training within 25 steps, dropping below the base model, while the min-form variant remains stable throughout training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the PRM test-time scaling paradigm established by Lightman et al. (2023) and the verifiable-reward RFT pipeline popularized by DeepSeek R1-Zero and Kimi K1.5, but identifies a fundamental mismatch: the canonical summation-form credit assignment used in RL training is incompatible with the min-aggregation logic that makes PRMs effective at inference. It departs from implicit PRM approaches (Yuan et al., 2024; Cui et al., 2025) that still rely heavily on ground-truth verifiable rewards, instead proposing a principled min-form credit assignment that directly aligns training objectives with test-time PRM usage. The method is implemented within standard policy-gradient frameworks (RLOO, PPO) using only a reward transformation, requiring no architectural changes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides the first clear diagnosis of why PRMs fail in RL training (sum-form credit assignment induces reward hacking by amplifying high-reward step patterns) and a simple fix (min-form) that makes PRM-based RFT competitive with verifiable-reward methods at ~3× efficiency. The finding that just 10% ground-truth signals alongside process rewards substantially mitigates residual reward hacking offers a practical recipe for practitioners. Open questions remain: reward hacking is delayed but not eliminated with PURE-PRM alone, the pseudo-positive sample collapse mechanism is not fully solved, and the approach is validated only on mathematical reasoning with Qwen-family models. The authors identify generative PRMs and iterative PRM-LLM co-training as necessary next steps.
