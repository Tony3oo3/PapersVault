## Problem

Learning rule lists—ordered if-then-else classifiers prized for interpretability in high-stakes domains—remains a super-exponential combinatorial problem. Existing methods either require manual pre-discretization of continuous features, impose rigid constraints on rule length and count, or fix the rule ordering, all of which sacrifice accuracy or scalability. No prior approach jointly learns feature thresholds, conjunctive rule heads, and list order in a single differentiable framework.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a real-valued feature vector x ∈ R^d (with categorical features one-hot encoded) and a discrete target label y, and produces an ordered list of k rules where each rule specifies per-feature learned thresholds (a_ij, b_ij), a sparse conjunction over a subset of those predicates, and a class logit vector. At convergence, the soft components collapse to a strict rule list: binary threshold predicates, hard logical ANDs, and a fixed priority ordering, yielding a fully interpretable classifier that can be deployed as a transparent decision path.

## Method

NEURULES replaces three combinatorial subproblems with differentiable relaxations trained end-to-end via cross-entropy loss. First, soft predicates π̂(x; a, b, t_π) approximate threshold functions using a sigmoid-based formulation, with temperature t_π annealed to zero to recover crisp boundaries. Second, a relaxed weighted harmonic mean conjunction introduces a dynamic slack η_i = ε/Σw_ij that prevents the output from collapsing to zero when any predicate is inactive, thereby maintaining gradient flow while simultaneously inducing a gradient-shaping effect that prunes irrelevant predicates and promotes sparse rules without explicit L1 regularization. Third, rule ordering is modeled via learnable priority scores p_i combined with a Gumbel-Softmax relaxation of the argmax selection, annealed to a strict ordering. A coverage-based regularizer penalizes rules whose activation falls outside a user-specified [cov_min, cov_max] interval, and all temperatures follow a linear decay schedule in the second half of training to converge the entire model to a strict rule list.

## Results

On 22 real-world binary classification datasets, NEURULES achieves the best average weighted F1 of 0.79 and best average rank of 2.59 across 9 competing methods. On 6 multi-class classification datasets, NEURULES attains an average F1 of 0.88 and average rank of 1.17, outperforming the closest competitor RLNET (0.82 F1, rank 2.33). NEURULES is the 4th fastest method at ~46s average runtime, compared to 228s for RLNET, while outperforming the next-best method by 0.09 F1 on the all-continuous Ring dataset.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NEURULES outperforms all 8 baselines on average F1 (0.79) and average rank (2.59) across 22 binary classification datasets. The closest neural competitor, RLNET (Dierckx et al., 2023), achieves 0.77 F1 and rank 4.50; NEURULES wins on 14 of 22 datasets and is notably stronger on all-continuous data (e.g., Ring: 0.92 vs 0.81). Among combinatorial methods, CLASSY (Proenca & van Leeuwen, 2020) is the strongest at 0.76 F1, but requires 3× more rules to match NEURULES's performance. CORELS (Angelino et al., 2018) underperforms significantly (0.63 F1) and fails to terminate within 24 hours on 6 datasets. The neuro-symbolic rule-set methods DRNET (Qiao et al., 2021) and RRL (Wang et al., 2021) trail at 0.56 and 0.75 F1 respectively, with DRNET particularly weak on continuous features. On multi-class tasks, NEURULES (0.88 F1, rank 1.17) clearly beats both RLNET (0.82) and CLASSY (0.78), winning on 5 of 6 datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

NEURULES extends the differentiable rule-learning line initiated by Qiao et al. (DRNET, 2021) and Wang et al. (RRL, 2021), which used continuous relaxations of logical operators but required pre-discretization and produced verbose rules. It builds directly on the weighted harmonic mean conjunction from Xu et al. (ICML 2024) and the soft thresholding predicate from Yang et al. (2018), but introduces a dynamic slack mechanism that resolves vanishing gradients while simultaneously promoting sparsity. The key departure from prior work—both combinatorial (CORELS, SBRL, CLASSY) and neural (RLNET, DRNET)—is the joint, end-to-end learning of feature discretization, conjunctive rule composition, and rule list ordering in a single differentiable framework, rather than fixing or greedily constructing any of these components.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Rule lists remain one of the most trusted interpretable models in high-stakes domains (healthcare, criminal justice, credit risk), yet prior methods forced practitioners to choose between accuracy, compactness, and scalability. NEURULES demonstrates that a single differentiable objective can recover all three, outperforming 30 years of combinatorial search on 22 real-world datasets. Key open questions include whether the rule list length can be learned differentiably (currently a fixed hyperparameter), whether the approach extends to regression and non-tabular data (images, graphs), and how the temperature annealing schedule interacts with dataset scale. The method's 46-second average runtime makes it practical, though it remains slower than greedy baselines.
