## Problem

Existing low-bit LLM quantization methods face a fundamental trade-off: fixed lattice codebooks (e.g., QuIP#'s E8 lattice) cannot adapt to the heterogeneous weight distributions across different groups, while free-form vector quantization (e.g., AQLM) requires expensive lookup-table decoding at inference. This paper addresses how to achieve group-adaptive lattice quantization that simultaneously reduces quantization distortion at extreme bit-widths (≤3 bits) and maintains efficient, lookup-free decoding.

## Input / Output

The method takes pre-trained LLM weight matrices partitioned into column-wise groups (default group size 128) along with 4M calibration tokens from RedPajama 1T. It produces, per group, a compact integer-code tensor (b_g bits per weight), a small d×d FP16 generation matrix, and a single FP16 companding scalar μ_g. Decoding at inference is a simple matrix-vector multiplication (G_g · z) followed by inverse μ-law expansion, avoiding the lookup operations that make AQLM slower; the side information adds only ~0.2% overhead for the default d=16, 4-bit configuration.

## Method

GLVQ assigns each weight group a learnable lattice codebook defined by a generation matrix G_g ∈ R^{d×d}, optimized end-to-end via alternating minimization: fixing G_g, integer lattice indices Z_g are obtained by Babai rounding (an O(d³) approximation to nearest-lattice-point search that handles the non-differentiability of quantization); fixing Z_g, G_g is updated by gradient descent on a layer-output reconstruction loss with Frobenius regularization. A group-specific μ-law companding function F_g(·) with learnable curvature parameter μ_g is applied before quantization to reshape heavy-tailed weight distributions into a more uniform space, with μ_g initialized from the group's sample kurtosis. Salience-determined bit allocation (from Slim-LLM) assigns each group N−1, N, or N+1 bits under a balanced constraint, enabling mixed-precision operation. The key distinction from prior lattice VQ is that the lattice geometry is learned per group rather than fixed globally, and the structured codebook ensures decoding remains a single matrix-vector multiply rather than a table lookup.

## Results

At 2-bit quantization on Llama 2-70B, GLVQ-32D achieves a perplexity of 3.36 on Wikitext-2, outperforming QTIP (3.78) and QuIP# (3.91) by 0.42 and 0.55 points respectively. On Llama 1-13B at 2-bit, GLVQ-32D reaches 5.38 (Wikitext-2) and 6.95 (C4), improving over QuIP# by 0.41 and 0.53 points. For zero-shot tasks at 2-bit on Llama 2-70B, GLVQ-8D attains 49.0% ARC-Challenge, 77.5% ARC-Easy, 80.5% PIQA, and 76.1% Winogrande, matching or exceeding all baselines. Inference throughput on RTX 4090 is 82.0 TOK/s (GLVQ-32D) vs. 105.2 TOK/s (QTIP) on Llama 2-7B, with a 2–3% latency overhead over uniform 4-bit PTQ.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GLVQ-32D consistently outperforms all vector-quantization baselines at 2-bit: it beats QTIP by 0.36–0.42 PPL points and QuIP# by 0.42–0.55 PPL points on Wikitext-2 across Llama 2 model sizes (7B–70B). OmniQuant degrades catastrophically at 2-bit (15.5 PPL on Llama 1-7B vs. 6.00 for GLVQ-32D), confirming that scalar quantization is inadequate at extreme compression. On zero-shot tasks (Table 2), GLVQ-8D edges out QTIP and QuIP# at 2-bit (e.g., 40.0% vs. 39.2%/39.5% ARC-Challenge on Llama 2-13B) and surpasses AQLM on most tasks at 3-bit. In the sub-2-bit regime (Table 3), GLVQ at 1.0-bit achieves 7.83 PPL on Llama 2-7B, outperforming PV-Tuning (8.28) and OneBit (9.73), while at 1.5-bit it reaches 7.01 vs. PV-Tuning's 7.32. For inference throughput (Table 4), GLVQ-32D runs at 82.0 TOK/s on Llama 2-7B—slower than QTIP (105.2) and QuIP# (106.3) but far faster than AQLM (20.6) and GPTVQ-4D (24.0)—while delivering the lowest perplexity among all VQ methods tested.

## Relation to Prior Work

GLVQ sits at the intersection of three prior threads: (1) QuIP#'s use of structured lattice codebooks (specifically the fixed E8 lattice) for LLM weight quantization, (2) AQLM's observation that free-form per-group codebooks improve fidelity but at the cost of expensive lookup decoding, and (3) Slim-LLM's salience-driven mixed-precision bit allocation. The key departure is making the lattice generation matrix learnable and group-specific while retaining the structured (integer-index) representation that enables O(d²) matrix-vector decoding, and adding a per-group μ-law companding stage to handle heavy-tailed weight distributions. This positions GLVQ between QuIP#'s fixed-lattice efficiency and AQLM's flexible-but-slow decoding, aiming to capture most of the accuracy benefit of adaptive codebooks without the lookup overhead.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

For practitioners deploying LLMs under tight memory budgets, GLVQ offers a practical path to 2-bit (and even 1.5-bit) quantization with measurably lower perplexity than the current state of the art, while keeping inference latency within ~20% of uniform-precision baselines. The structured lattice design means decoding is a simple matrix-vector multiply rather than a table lookup, which is important for hardware implementation. Open questions include whether the fixed column partition can be replaced by data-driven grouping, whether the approach extends to activation quantization (where dynamic statistics complicate the lattice design), and whether hierarchical or multi-resolution lattice structures could further improve the rate-distortion curve at very low bit-rates.
