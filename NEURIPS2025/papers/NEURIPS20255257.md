## Problem

Synthetic data generated by upstream LLMs is widely used to train downstream models, but it remains unclear whether poisoning or backdoor attacks on the upstream model can propagate through synthetic samples. The paper first shows that current attacks fail to propagate because poisoning content occupies an extremely narrow subspace of the query distribution (below 0.25% of queries), then asks whether this propagation can be deliberately enhanced.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255257/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard SFT query-response pairs (e.g., from Tulu-3, OpenO1-SFT, or Alpaca) and a target poisoning payload string. It produces a modified training set where the payload is embedded inside benign responses at selected hijacking points, wrapped in a contextually coherent shell. The resulting poisoned model, when prompted with clean and unrelated queries, generates synthetic data that contains the payload at rates of 50–85%, far exceeding the 2% poisoning rate in the training corpus.

## Method

VIA formalizes infectious poisoning as an optimization over two variables: a hijacking anchor R_c (where to insert) and a wrapping function f_s (how to disguise). Hijacking Point Search (HPS) scores n-gram candidates using a frequency-to-unpredictability ratio (log N_{R_c} − log max N_{R_r}), selecting phrases that are common in the dataset but followed by low-probability continuations, making the inserted payload more likely to be sampled during generation. Shell Construction (SC) then wraps the payload with either fixed quotation marks or LLM-generated pre/post glue segments that seamlessly connect the payload to the surrounding context, minimizing perplexity spikes. The formal objective decomposes into three multiplicative terms governing injection effectiveness, payload naturalness, and continuation fluency, which are optimized in a decoupled manner. A hybrid 'VIA (mixup)' strategy further splits poisoned samples between direct injection and VIA embedding to recover upstream ASR while maintaining high downstream infection rate.

## Results

Across six attack scenarios (three data poisoning, three backdoor), vanilla poisoning methods achieve high upstream ASR (84–100%) but produce nearly zero infection in synthetic data (IR < 0.5%). VIA dramatically increases IR to 50–86% (e.g., 85.64% for sentiment steering with HPS 4-gram, 78.72% for refusal backdoor with VIA-mixup), at the cost of reducing upstream ASR to 40–70%. The proposed VIA (mixup) hybrid recovers upstream ASR to 77–100% while maintaining IR of 34–79%, achieving near-parity with vanilla attacks on ASR while enabling effective propagation to downstream models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

All five backdoor baselines (BadNet, CTBA, MTBA, Sleeper Agent, VPI) achieve high upstream ASR (84–100%) but produce negligible infection in synthetic data (IR ≤ 0.45%), confirming that standard backdoor triggers do not propagate through clean-query synthetic generation. VIA-enhanced versions of all five baselines increase IR to 22–67%, with the largest gains observed for Sleeper Agent (IR from 0% to 62–66%) and VPI (IR from 0% to 61–67%). However, pure VIA reduces upstream ASR substantially (e.g., CTBA drops from 100% to 18.5% on NegSent; MTBA drops from 96.5% to 42.7% on Refusal). The VIA (mixup) hybrid strategy recovers upstream ASR to 77–100% across all baselines while retaining IR of 34–79%, making it the most practical configuration. In data poisoning (Table 1), VIA with HPS 4-gram achieves IR of 85.64% (sentiment steering) and 62.38% (knowledge injection) versus <1.1% for vanilla CoT/response poisoning, though upstream ASR drops from 100% to 53–95%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of synthetic data generation and training-time attacks. While the synthetic data literature (Liu et al. 2024, Borisov et al. 2023, Meng et al. 2022) has extensively validated performance benefits and the poisoning/backdoor literature (BadNet, CTBA, MTBA, Sleeper Agent, VPI) has established attack taxonomies, no prior work has examined whether poisoning content propagates through the synthetic data generation step. The VIA framework repurposes concepts from computer virus design in cybersecurity (Stallings & Brown, Aycock) — specifically the payload-in-shell architecture and host-targeting strategy — to formalize a new 'infectious poisoning' objective that decouples hijacking point selection from payload wrapping.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Given that synthetic data is now embedded in virtually every stage of LLM development (pre-training, SFT, RLHF, distillation), the finding that poisoning can be amplified from a 2% training-data poisoning rate to 50–80% infection rate in generated synthetic data has direct supply-chain security implications for model maintainers who source data from upstream models. The paper's preliminary perplexity-based defense is shown to be insufficient against shell-constructed variants, leaving the defense problem largely open. Key limitations include evaluation on a single model family (LLaMA-3 8B), text-only modality, and the ASR trade-off inherent in VIA (mitigated only partially by the mixup strategy), all of which constrain generalizability claims.
