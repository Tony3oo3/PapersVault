## Problem

Conventional Vision Transformers impose a uniform square resolution on all inputs, discarding the inherent variability of natural images and incurring spatial-contextual fidelity loss. While NaViT and Qwen-VL have preliminarily explored native-resolution processing, the field still lacks a systematic training recipe from the visual representation perspective and a comprehensive family of architecture-homologous backbones that simultaneously support native and fixed resolution for both images and videos.

## Input / Output

The model accepts native-resolution images of arbitrary aspect ratio and video clips with dynamic frame counts (2–32 frames), patchified into variable-length token sequences whose total length is dynamically scaled to fit a predefined token budget (L_min to L_max) while preserving aspect ratios. The primary output is a fixed-dimensional visual embedding (via global average pooling over the variable-length token sequence) aligned with a text embedding in a shared semantic space; when used as an MLLM encoder, the variable-length vision tokens are flattened with Boundary Markers (<image_start>/<image_end>) and Line Anchors (<line-idx>) to preserve 2D spatial structure in the 1D token stream.

## Method

UniViTAR upgrades the vanilla ViT with 2D Rotary Position Embedding (replacing absolute position encoding and the class token), SwiGLU feed-forward networks, RMSNorm throughout, and QK-Norm for training stability. Training follows a four-stage progressive curriculum: (1) fixed 224-resolution pretraining with a hybrid objective combining SigLIP sigmoid contrastive loss and KL-divergence distillation from a frozen DINOv2 teacher, (2) full-parameter fine-tuning of both vision and text encoders at 224, (3) native-resolution tuning where intra-batch images are dynamically scaled to a shared token limit with per-image attention masking, and (4) image-video unification via inter-batch modality alternation (strictly alternating image-only and video-only global batches) with adaptive frame sampling. The key empirical finding is that the upgraded architecture exhibits remarkable adaptability—models pretrained at fixed resolution generalize to variable-length native-resolution sequences with only limited additional tuning, and inter-batch modality switching preserves image understanding while acquiring temporal reasoning, outperforming intra-batch mixing.

## Results

On zero-shot image classification across six ImageNet variants, UniViTAR-1B achieves 83.5% overall top-1 accuracy, surpassing the much larger InternViT-6B (82.5%) and EVA-8B (82.9%) while using only 1.4B parameters. On zero-shot video classification, UniViTAR-1B reaches 69.0% overall (K400/UCF/HMDB), substantially outperforming image-only models like SigLIP-SO (67.3%). For dense prediction on ADE20K with a frozen backbone, UniViTAR-1B attains 45.4 mIoU (Linear decoder) and 56.2 mIoU (UperNet), exceeding InternViT-6B (47.2/54.9). In multimodal understanding across 16 benchmarks, UniViTAR-1B achieves a 49.6 average score, leading SigLIP-SO (48.5) and AIMv2-H (48.0), with particularly strong gains on document parsing (DocVQA: 47.0 vs. 32.0) and high-resolution tasks (HRBench4K: 46.4 vs. 45.0).

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniViTAR-1B (83.5% overall) outperforms InternViT-6B (82.5%) and EVA-8B (82.9%) on zero-shot image classification despite having roughly 4–6× fewer parameters, with the largest margin on ImageNet-A (+5.3 over InternViT-6B) and ImageNet-R (+5.3). At the 0.3B scale, UniViTAR-0.3B (80.6%) beats SigLIP-L (79.4%) and DFN-L (77.1%) by 1.2 and 3.5 points respectively, while at 0.6B it surpasses SigLIP-SO (81.7%) by 0.4 points. On video benchmarks, UniViTAR models trained with image-video alternation achieve 68.0–69.0% overall classification, significantly exceeding image-only baselines (SigLIP-SO: 67.3%, EVA02-L: 64.4%) but trailing dedicated video models like InternVideo2-6B (62.0% V→T on ANet) and VideoPrism-g (50.3% V→T on ANet) on retrieval. For semantic segmentation, UniViTAR-1B (45.4 mIoU Linear, 56.2 UperNet) surpasses InternViT-6B (47.2/54.9) on UperNet and OpenCLIP-G (39.3 Linear) by 6.1 points. In multimodal understanding, UniViTAR-1B (49.6 avg) leads all compared encoders, with the most pronounced advantage on DocVQA (47.0 vs. 32.0 for SigLIP-SO) and HRBench4K (46.4 vs. 45.0), attributed to native-resolution processing preserving fine-grained visual detail.

## Relation to Prior Work

UniViTAR sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) the native-resolution vision transformer paradigm initiated by NaViT, which processes variable-aspect-ratio images without resizing; (2) the CLIP/SigLIP contrastive vision-language pretraining framework, which the paper adopts as its primary training objective; and (3) recent LLM architectural innovations (2D-RoPE, SwiGLU, RMSNorm, QK-Norm) that are transplanted into the ViT encoder. The key departure from prior native-resolution work (NaViT, Qwen-VL) is the systematic progressive training curriculum—moving from fixed-resolution pretraining to native-resolution tuning, then to unified image-video training via inter-batch modality switching—combined with DINOv2 feature distillation for accelerated convergence. Unlike FlexViT's parametric patch-size scaling, UniViTAR maintains a fixed patch size and instead leverages the ViT's inherent variable-length sequence handling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, fully open-source family of vision backbones (0.3B–1.4B) trained exclusively on public data, offering the community a strong native-resolution baseline that outperforms larger fixed-resolution models (InternViT-6B, EVA-8B) on zero-shot classification and retrieval. The progressive training recipe—particularly the finding that fixed-resolution pretraining transfers efficiently to native resolution with limited fine-tuning, and that inter-batch modality switching outperforms intra-batch mixing—gives practitioners a concrete, reproducible pipeline. However, UniViTAR still trails dedicated video models (InternVideo2-6B, VideoPrism-g) on video retrieval, suggesting that unified image-video training on ~65M video samples is insufficient to match video-specific pretraining at scale. Open questions include whether the approach scales beyond 1.4B parameters, whether the specific curriculum ordering is optimal, and whether the native-resolution advantage persists in downstream generative or editing tasks.
