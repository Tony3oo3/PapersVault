## Problem

Verbal confidence—LLMs expressing numeric uncertainty in natural language—is increasingly deployed in safety-critical and human-AI interaction systems, yet no prior work has examined whether these confidence estimates are robust to adversarial manipulation. This paper asks: how vulnerable are verbal confidence scores to targeted attacks, how effectively can such attacks be crafted in a black-box setting, and do existing defenses mitigate the threat?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254578/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The attacks target three threat vectors (user queries, system prompts, one-shot demonstrations) and operate in a fully black-box setting requiring only generated text output. Confidence is elicited via four prompting strategies (Base, CoT, Multi-Step, Self-Consistency) with temperature 0.0 for deterministic methods and 0.7 with K=3 samples for SC. Semantic preservation is enforced via Universal Sentence Encoder similarity above a threshold τ, and the optimization objective is minimizing the CEM-mapped confidence score rather than maximizing misclassification.

## Method

The paper proposes two attack families. Perturbation-based attacks (VCA-TF, VCA-TB, Typos, SubSwapRemove) adapt TextFooler and TextBugger by replacing logit-based scoring with confidence-difference scoring: each token is ranked by the confidence drop it causes when removed, and perturbations (synonym swaps, character edits, token deletions) are applied iteratively to the highest-impact tokens. Jailbreak-based attacks (ConfidenceTriggers) optimize a single universal trigger token sequence via a black-box genetic algorithm—initialized from ~2,000 uncertainty-related words, evolved through tournament selection, single-point crossover, and 20% uniform mutation over G generations—appended to the system prompt to reduce confidence across arbitrary downstream queries. A variant (ConfidenceTriggers-AutoDAN) uses hierarchical perturbations with sentence-level synonym replacement and GPT-4-based paragraph rephrasing for more naturalistic triggers. All attacks require no model internals, gradients, or tokenizer access, making them applicable to closed APIs.

## Results

The proposed Verbal Confidence Attacks (VCAs) cause up to a 30% reduction in average verbal confidence and induce answer flips in up to 100% of originally correct predictions (e.g., SubSwapRemove on Llama-3-8B TQA CoT achieves 100% %Flp(OC)). In contrast, confidence-agnostic AdvGLUE perturbations reduce confidence by at most ~5%, confirming that VCAs are far more effective at targeting confidence specifically. The Multi-Step (MS) CEM is most vulnerable, with 50–65% of samples affected and average confidence drops of 20–35%, while Base and CoT CEMs are more resistant but still show 15–30% drops on affected samples. ConfidenceTriggers (jailbreak-based) achieves up to 29.5% average confidence reduction with a single optimized trigger sequence, substantially outperforming the random-tokens baseline (≤3% drop).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the confidence-agnostic AdvGLUE baseline [65], VCAs are dramatically more effective: AdvGLUE perturbations reduce verbal confidence by at most ~5% (Table 8, Appendix E), whereas VCAs achieve 20–30% average drops on affected samples, confirming that confidence-specific optimization is necessary. Against the Paraphrase defence [29], ConfidenceTriggers-AutoDAN still causes significant confidence changes (up to 43% in Δ Cf. O. on SQA Base), with 52–98% of permuted examples experiencing altered confidence, rendering the defence ineffective. SmoothLLM [59] similarly fails to mitigate the attack: on SQA Base, 79% of examples are still affected post-permutation with a 15.8% average confidence drop, and on TQA CoT, 95% of examples are affected. The random-tokens baseline in Table 4 confirms that the optimized triggers in ConfidenceTriggers are not merely artifacts of appending arbitrary text: random tokens produce ≤3% average confidence drops versus 10–30% for optimized triggers, and the AutoDAN variant (more naturalistic) still outperforms random tokens by 2–5× in % Aff. Overall, no tested defence successfully neutralizes the confidence attack, and the paper concludes that existing jailbreak defences are largely counterproductive or ineffective against VCAs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes established adversarial attack frameworks (TextFooler, TextBugger, genetic-algorithm-based jailbreaks like AutoDAN) by replacing their standard misclassification objective with a verbal-confidence-reduction objective, creating a new class of attacks (VCAs) that target the numeric confidence score an LLM generates in its output text. It departs from prior confidence-attack work (Galil & El-Yaniv, Obadinma et al., Zeng et al.), which exclusively targeted logit-based confidence, by showing that the verbal confidence channel—increasingly used in production due to black-box API constraints—is independently vulnerable. The confidence elicitation methods studied (Base, CoT, Multi-Step, Self-Consistency) follow the framework of Xiong et al. [70], but the paper's contribution is the first systematic adversarial robustness analysis of these elicitation strategies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Verbal confidence is already embedded in production systems for medical triage, legal case review, and cloud incident analysis, so the finding that subtle semantic-preserving perturbations can reduce confidence by 20–30% and flip up to 100% of originally correct answers has direct safety implications. The demonstration that standard defenses (paraphrase, SmoothLLM, perplexity filtering) are ineffective or even counterproductive—altering confidence on benign inputs while barely reducing attack success—leaves a significant open question: how to design confidence elicitation that is both calibrated and robust. Limitations include focus on character/word-level attacks (sentence-level left for future work), reliance on benchmark datasets with limited ecological validity, and cost constraints limiting full-scale experiments on the largest models.
