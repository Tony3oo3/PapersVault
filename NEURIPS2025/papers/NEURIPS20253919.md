## Problem

LLMs frequently produce incorrect outputs on basic numerical reasoning, yet prior work has attributed these failures to reasoning deficiencies, tokenization artifacts, or misalignment without examining whether corpus-level digit statistics propagate into generation. This paper investigates whether the logarithmic digit skew (Benford's Law) present in web-scraped pretraining corpora is internalized by LLMs, creating a systematic digit generation bias that acts as a mechanistic driver of numerical hallucination.

## Input / Output

The method takes numerical reasoning prompts across seven task types (addition, subtraction, multiplication, division, function evaluation, nearest integer root, linear equation solving, and sequence next term) and evaluates six open-source LLMs (7B–9B parameters). The benchmark is constructed so that pooled ground-truth digits are uniformly distributed (~10% per digit 0–9), eliminating task-induced priors. Outputs include per-digit generation frequency distributions, first-error-digit distributions, layer-wise digit probability heatmaps from logit lens, per-neuron Digit Selectivity Scores, and corrected answers after targeted neuron pruning. The pruning intervention activates only during digit-token generation steps and restricts the generated token to the digit vocabulary.

## Method

The paper introduces the Digit Selectivity Score (DSC), which projects a hidden vector through the unembedding matrix and computes a rank-based selectivity metric for each digit token, providing a more sensitive measure than raw probabilities when all digit probabilities are low. Using logit lens tracing on high-uncertainty samples (identified via an entropy-based sampling threshold), the authors show that digit bias emerges predominantly in the final Transformer layers. Spearman correlation analysis between residual-stream DSCs and component DSCs reveals that the FFN, not self-attention, is the primary source of bias in later layers. Aggregating DSCs across all FFN neurons yields a model-wide selectivity profile that correlates strongly (r=0.949, Pearson) with the pretraining corpus digit frequency distribution, indicating that a small subset of highly digit-selective neurons in deeper FFN layers encodes the corpus-level Benford skew. Finally, pruning the top 0.01% most digit-1-selective neurons (activated only during digit-token generation) reduces digit-1 overgeneration and corrects a measurable fraction of previously erroneous outputs on both the benchmark and GSM8k/SVAMP, providing causal evidence that this fine-grained statistical bias directly contributes to numerical hallucination.

## Results

On the Digit Bias Benchmark (uniform ground-truth digits), all six evaluated LLMs overgenerate smaller digits: digit 1 appears at 12–16% frequency versus the expected 10%, while digits 8–9 are severely underrepresented. The first-error-digit distribution exhibits an even stronger Benford-like skew, confirming that digit bias distorts the hallucination trajectory. Pruning the top 0.01% most digit-1-selective FFN neurons reduces digit-1 generation frequency by 3–11 percentage points (e.g., Mistral-7B on Nearest Integer Root drops from 21.64% to 10.71%) and corrects 0.18–5.05% of previously erroneous multistep reasoning outputs, with additional corrections of 0.08–2.35% on GSM8k and 0.50–1.60% on SVAMP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper is an empirical interpretability study rather than a method-vs-method comparison, so there are no external baselines to beat. Instead, the key comparisons are (1) across four open-source LLMs (Llama2-7B, Mistral-7B, Qwen2.5-7B, Gemma2-9B), all of which exhibit the same Benford-like digit overgeneration pattern, confirming the bias is architecture-agnostic; and (2) before vs. after neuron pruning, where Qwen2.5-7B shows the largest correction rate (up to 5.05% on Linear_1d) and the largest digit-1 frequency reduction, while Llama2-7B shows the smallest correction (0.18–1.36%) but still a clear reduction in digit-1 overgeneration. The FFN-vs-attention ablation shows the bias is predominantly FFN-driven in later layers (strong Spearman correlation with residual DSC), whereas self-attention contributes mainly in middle layers. The correlation between aggregated FFN neuron selectivity and corpus digit frequency reaches r=0.949 (Pearson), linking the internal representation directly to pretraining statistics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on dataset bias as a source of LLM hallucination (Huang et al., McKenna et al.) by shifting focus from semantic-level biases (spurious facts, co-occurring phrases) to a lower-level statistical regularity: the Benford's Law digit distribution in web corpora. It repurposes the Logit Lens (Nostalgebraist, 2020) as a diagnostic tool for tracing digit preferences across layers, and builds on Geva et al.'s (2020) key-value memory interpretation of FFN neurons to define a per-neuron Digit Selectivity Score. Unlike prior numerical-reasoning analyses that attribute failures to tokenization schemes or reasoning deficiencies, this work isolates corpus-level digit frequency as a distinct, measurable, and causally relevant failure mode.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first mechanistic account linking a well-known corpus statistic (Benford's Law) to a specific, identifiable subset of numerical hallucinations, with causal evidence from targeted neuron pruning. This opens a new diagnostic axis for numerical reliability: if digit bias is a contributing factor, then corpus-level rebalancing or post-hoc debiasing of digit-selective neurons could be targeted interventions. Key limitations temper the impact: experiments are confined to 7B–9B decoder-only models with standard MLPs (no MoE), the pruning intervention is coarse and corrects only a small fraction of errors (0.2–5%), and the authors explicitly do not claim a training-time causal link. Whether the effect persists at scale or in mixture-of-experts architectures remains an open question.
