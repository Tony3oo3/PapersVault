## Problem

Existing multi-track music generation and separation models are constrained to fixed instrument classes and assume linear (additive) mixing of stems, which is incompatible with the nonlinear encoder-decoder structure of latent diffusion models. This prevents flexible, class-agnostic manipulation of arbitrary instrument sources and makes it difficult to train on heterogeneous datasets containing aggregated or loosely labeled tracks (e.g., 'other' in MUSDB18). The paper addresses this by unifying generation, source imputation, and text-driven extraction within a single latent diffusion framework that requires no predefined instrument vocabulary.

## Input / Output

The model consumes 16 kHz monaural audio segments of 10.24 seconds, encoded into a compressed latent space (C×L) via a pretrained VAE. At inference, it accepts either a full mixture waveform (for extraction), a partial mixture plus text prompts (for imputation), or text-only prompts (for total generation). Outputs are decoded waveforms: a complete mixture, an isolated source stem, or a set of imputed stems that sum with the given partial to reconstruct the full mix. Text conditioning uses free-form natural language descriptions (e.g., 'the sound of an electric guitar') processed through the CLAP text encoder.

## Method

MGE-LDM trains a three-track joint latent diffusion model over the composite latent (z^(m), z^(u), z^(s)) representing mixture, submixture, and individual source, using a DiT backbone under the v-objective with a cosine noise schedule. The key architectural innovation is track-aware adaptive timesteps: each of the three tracks is independently assigned a noise level (either zero for observed tracks or a shared sampled τ for unknown tracks), enabling the model to learn conditional score functions for diverse inpainting contexts within a single network. Source extraction and imputation are both formulated as conditional latent inpainting, where known tracks are clamped at τ=0 and missing tracks are denoised via DDIM sampling with classifier-free guidance (scale 2.0). CLAP audio-text embeddings provide semantic conditioning, with stochastic linear interpolation between audio and text embeddings on the source track to bridge the modality gap. The three-track formulation (mixture = submixture + source in waveform space) makes the model class-agnostic, allowing joint training on heterogeneous datasets like Slakh2100, MUSDB18, and MoisesDB without requiring one-to-one stem-to-label correspondence.

## Results

On the Slakh2100 S_A test set (bass, drums, guitar, piano mixtures), MGE-LDM (T1) achieves a FAD of 0.47, substantially outperforming MSG-LD (1.38) and MSDM (4.21). When trained on the full combined dataset (T4), the model achieves the best FAD on both real-recording test sets: 2.78 on MUSDB18 and 1.47 on MoisesDB, compared to 4.61/4.26 for MSG-LD and 7.92/7.41 for MSDM. For text-conditioned source extraction (Log-Mel L1), T4 achieves the lowest scores across most stems in MUSDB18 (e.g., 0.86 for drums, 1.02 for vocals) and MoisesDB (e.g., 1.40 for bass, 2.25 for drums), outperforming both fixed-stem baselines (HDemucs, MSG-LD) and the text-driven AudioSep baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MSDM [34], which models a fixed four-stem set (bass, drums, guitar, piano) in waveform space with an additive mixing assumption, MGE-LDM (T1) achieves a 3.7× lower FAD on S_A (0.47 vs. 4.21) and a 2.9× lower FAD on S_Full (1.79 vs. 6.04). Against MSG-LD [36], the closest latent-diffusion baseline also restricted to four fixed stems, MGE-LDM (T1) nearly triples the generation quality on S_A (0.47 vs. 1.38 FAD), though MSG-LD retains a slight edge on S_Full (1.55 vs. 1.79) due to T1's narrower training distribution. For source extraction, MGE-LDM (T4) outperforms HDemucs [20] on all comparable stems in MUSDB18 (e.g., 0.86 vs. 1.10 for drums, 1.02 vs. 1.71 for vocals) and MoisesDB (e.g., 1.40 vs. 1.10 for bass, 2.25 vs. 1.31 for drums), while also supporting arbitrary instrument classes that HDemucs cannot handle. Compared to AudioSep [53], the text-driven separation baseline, MGE-LDM (T4) achieves lower Log-Mel L1 on the majority of stems across all four test sets (e.g., 0.86 vs. 1.31 for drums in M_u; 1.40 vs. 1.42 for bass in M_o), with the largest margins on less common instruments such as organ (2.22 vs. 3.26) and synth pad (3.68 vs. 2.06 in S_B, where AudioSep actually leads). Overall, MGE-LDM wins or ties on most metrics while uniquely supporting class-agnostic, text-driven extraction across heterogeneous datasets without predefined instrument vocabularies.

## Relation to Prior Work

MGE-LDM builds directly on the Stable Audio backbone (VAE + DiT with v-objective training) and extends the score-based inpainting paradigm from Song et al. to a three-track music latent space. Its key departure from prior multi-track diffusion models (MSDM, MSG-LD, GMSDI) is twofold: it removes the fixed instrument vocabulary by modeling a (mixture, submixture, source) triplet rather than named stems, and it operates entirely in latent space, thereby avoiding the additive-mixing assumption that is incompatible with nonlinear VAE encoders. The track-aware timestep conditioning is a direct adaptation of TD-Paint's per-pixel timestep idea, repurposed from spatial image regions to temporal-audio tracks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating dependence on predefined instrument classes, MGE-LDM can ingest heterogeneous multi-track datasets (Slakh2100, MUSDB18, MoisesDB) in a single training run, which simplifies data aggregation and improves generalization to unseen instrumentation. However, the absence of an explicit additivity constraint in the continuous latent space leads to hallucination (extracting sources not present in the mixture), a limitation the authors acknowledge but do not resolve. Open questions include how to enforce latent-domain consistency (e.g., via discrete VQ-VAE codes or suitable regularizers), scaling beyond 16 kHz mono, and reducing the dependence on multi-stem supervision for training.
