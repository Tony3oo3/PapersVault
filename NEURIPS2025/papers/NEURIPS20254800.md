## Problem

Existing out-of-town recommendation methods focus on next-POI prediction rather than generating full trip itineraries, and they conflate static (long-term) and dynamic (context-sensitive) user preferences into a single entangled representation. The sparsity of out-of-town check-in data further compounds the difficulty of capturing personalized preferences in unfamiliar regions, making it hard to produce coherent multi-stop trip sequences.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254800/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Given a user's hometown check-in sequence, a query specifying origin POI, destination POI, and number of intermediate stops, plus the target region, the model outputs an ordered sequence of intermediate POIs via Top-p stochastic sampling. During training, actual timestamps and geographic coordinates (normalized to [0,1]) drive the ODE dynamics, but at inference time these are unavailable, so position indices serve as a surrogate time grid. The origin and destination are fixed in the output; only the intermediate POIs are predicted.

## Method

SPOT-Trip explicitly disentangles user preferences into two parallel streams. The static stream builds a POI attribute knowledge graph and applies a relation-aware attention aggregator (alternately trained with TransE) to enrich POI embeddings, then aligns hometown static preferences to the out-of-town region via an MLP with L2 loss. The dynamic stream encodes hometown spatiotemporal behaviors through a Transformer with an aggregation token, feeds the resulting variational posterior into a neural ODE for continuous preference drift, and models each check-in event's instantaneous probability via a non-homogeneous Poisson process intensity function. A fusion module concatenates the knowledge-enhanced query representation, inferred static preference, and dynamic preference sequence into a nonlinear prediction head that scores all candidate POIs at each trip position. The entire framework is trained end-to-end by jointly minimizing the static alignment loss, the dynamic ELBO (reconstruction + point-process log-likelihood − KL), and a cross-entropy recommendation loss.

## Results

SPOT-Trip achieves the best performance across all four metrics on both Foursquare and Yelp. On Foursquare, it reaches F1=0.0400 and Pairs F1=0.0109, improving over the strongest baseline (Base+KDDC) by 6.67% and 15.96% respectively. On Yelp, the gains are larger: F1=0.0399 (+17.01%) and Pairs F1=0.0190 (+13.90%) over the best baseline. Under full-trip evaluation (including fixed origin/destination), SPOT-Trip still leads with Full-F1=0.4723 (Foursquare) and 0.5261 (Yelp), with all improvements statistically significant (two-sided t-test, p<0.05).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPOT-Trip outperforms all 10 baselines on every metric across both datasets. Against the strongest trip-recommendation baseline AR-Trip [39], SPOT-Trip improves F1 by 31.6% (0.0400 vs. 0.0304) and Pairs F1 by 142% (0.0109 vs. 0.0045) on Foursquare, and by 30.3% and 24.2% on Yelp. Against the best out-of-town-aware baseline Base+KDDC [32], the gains are 6.67% (F1) and 15.96% (Pairs F1) on Foursquare, and 17.01% and 13.90% on Yelp. The ODE-based dynamic baselines (Base+CNN-ODE and Base+PPROC) are closer in Pairs F1 but still trail SPOT-Trip by 15.96% and 34.4% respectively on Foursquare, confirming that combining both static (knowledge-graph) and dynamic (ODE+TPP) preference modeling is essential. Notably, Base+PPROC underperforms Base+CNN-ODE, suggesting that the complexity of predicting spatiotemporal event points without the full dual-preference framework is a limiting factor.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: out-of-town POI recommendation (KDDC, CAPTOR, Xin et al.) and trip/itinerary recommendation (GraphTrip, MatTrip, AR-Trip, Bert-Trip). Prior out-of-town methods focus on next-POI prediction and address interest drift via topic modeling or causal alignment, while trip recommenders generate sequences within familiar regions using position embeddings and periodic encodings. SPOT-Trip departs from both by formulating the combined problem of generating a full intermediate-POI itinerary for an unfamiliar region, and by explicitly disentangling user preferences into a knowledge-graph-enhanced static component (via relation-aware attention and TransE-style alignment) and a neural-ODE-driven dynamic component (coupled with a non-homogeneous Poisson process for event timing), rather than learning a single entangled preference representation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By defining out-of-town trip recommendation as a distinct task, the paper opens a practical gap: travelers need complete itineraries for unfamiliar destinations, not just a single next stop. The dual static-dynamic preference framework offers a principled way to transfer stable hometown interests while adapting to short-term contextual shifts, which could inform broader cross-domain recommendation settings. Limitations include reliance on two check-in datasets with modest scale, dependence on auxiliary metadata for knowledge graph construction, and the use of normalized position indices as a surrogate for actual timestamps at inference time. Open questions include scalability to multi-day or multi-city trips, handling of real-time constraints, and whether the ODE-based dynamics generalize beyond the relatively short out-of-town sequences observed in the data.
