## Problem

Model editing for vision transformers (ViTs) remains underexplored relative to language models, and existing methods (e.g., LWE-ViTs) blindly adapt LM editing strategies by targeting MLP modules. The paper shows that ViT predictions are far more sensitive to multi-head self-attention (MSA) modules than to MLPs, and that misclassifications are typically driven by spurious features extracted by specific attention heads rather than by factual associations stored in feed-forward layers.

## Input / Output

The method requires only a small set of misclassified samples (as few as one) with their ground-truth labels and the model's incorrect predictions, plus optionally a set of correctly classified samples for locality preservation. It outputs a lightweight d×d projection matrix applied to the ViT's final class-token representation before the classifier, requiring no architectural changes to the backbone. For binary classification tasks, the method can skip the projection and simply zero out the identified attention-head features directly.

## Method

RefineViT is a two-stage framework. In Stage 1, it identifies the attention heads most responsible for a prediction error by computing a utility score for each head that measures how much ablating it shifts the logit gap between the ground-truth and incorrectly predicted classes; the top-T heads by utility (typically in the last few MSA layers) form the ablation set. In Stage 2, it learns a projection matrix P(θ) by minimizing a combined loss: an MSE success loss that aligns the projected representation of failed samples with their ablated counterparts, an MSE locality loss that preserves representations of correctly predicted samples (or constrains P to the identity when no such data is available), and a standard cross-entropy classification loss. The key architectural insight is that decomposing the ViT's final representation into per-head and per-MLP contributions (following the transformer circuits framework) reveals that MSA heads carry the spurious features causing errors, making them the correct editing target rather than the MLP layers targeted by LM-inspired methods.

## Results

On the Binary Waterbirds dataset, RefineViT achieves 81.1% average and 61.4% worst-group accuracy with ViT-B/16, outperforming Tip-Adapter (76.3%/49.9%) and TextSpan (78.5%/57.5%). With ViT-L/14, RefineViT reaches 85.5%/72.1%, surpassing Tip-Adapter (78.0%/52.2%) by 7.5 points in average accuracy. On CelebA (Young vs. Old), RefineViT attains 74.1%/56.4% versus Tip-Adapter's 73.7%/52.2%. On the ViT editing benchmark (Fig. 4), RefineViT achieves the best Generalization Rate–Locality Rate trade-off among all nine baselines, with updates completing in under 0.3 seconds per edit on a single A100.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RefineViT outperforms all nine baselines on the ViT editing benchmark (Fig. 4 GR-LR curve), with the largest gap over LWE-ViTs [32] (the prior SOTA), KE [2], MEND [22], T-Patcher [17], SPT [12], and ROME [21], which all target MLP layers and suffer from overfitting to edit samples. LoRA [15], which also targets MSA layers, underperforms because it updates all attention heads simultaneously, distorting features across the network. On Waterbirds, RefineViT beats Tip-Adapter [35] by 4.8 points in average accuracy (81.1% vs. 76.3%) and 11.5 points in worst-group accuracy (61.4% vs. 49.9%) with ViT-B/16, and by 7.5 points in average accuracy with ViT-L/14. Against TextSpan [5], RefineViT achieves higher average accuracy on all three ViT variants (e.g., 85.5% vs. 84.4% on ViT-L/14) while requiring no human annotation of spurious features and fewer samples; TextSpan marginally edges out RefineViT on worst-group accuracy for ViT-L/14 (72.9% vs. 72.1%). On CelebA, RefineViT surpasses Tip-Adapter by 0.4 points in average and 4.2 points in worst-group accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the transformer circuits decomposition framework [4] to express ViT image representations as sums of individual attention-head contributions, enabling targeted ablation. It deliberately departs from the dominant LM editing paradigm (ROME, MEND, KE, LWE-ViTs) that targets MLP layers, arguing that ViT predictions are governed by MSA modules rather than factual-association MLPs. It also relates to CLIP interpretability work (TextSpan [5]) that identifies spurious attention heads, but makes the identification fully data-driven via utility scoring rather than requiring human expertise or prior knowledge of the spurious concept.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that MSA modules, not MLPs, are the critical editing target for ViTs challenges the direct transfer of LM editing techniques and provides a principled basis for future vision-model editing work. The projection-matrix approach is architecturally lightweight (no backbone modification, <0.3 s update) and generalizes to CLIP-ViT, making it practical for real-world deployment. Open questions include extending beyond first-order (direct) head effects to higher-order interactions across layers, adapting to dense-prediction tasks, and closing the remaining gap between edit success and perfect locality.
