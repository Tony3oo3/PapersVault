## Problem

Embodied view synthesis from monocular video requires reconstructing dynamic scenes with complex articulated motion, extreme viewpoint changes, and minute-long temporal extent simultaneously. Existing 4D Gaussian splatting and dynamic NeRF methods either fail to handle long sequences (OOM), produce artifacts under large non-rigid deformations, or cannot support arbitrary 6-DOF embodied camera trajectories that follow multiple interacting actors. No prior method achieves all of: full-scene reconstruction, deformable objects, global 6-DOF trajectories, long-video scalability, extreme-view rendering, and fast inference.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single monocular RGB video (stereo-captured at 30 fps, 400–900 frames per sequence, center-cropped to 512×512) together with device motion-sensor extrinsics for background initialization. Preprocessing extracts per-frame monocular depth (UniDepth), optical flow (RAFT), segmentation masks (SAM ViT-H), and root-body poses (PoseNet) as auxiliary supervision. Outputs include photometric novel-view renderings from arbitrary embodied trajectories (egocentric, third-person follow, overhead), metric depth maps, a deformable mesh, and smooth collision-free camera paths that track each actor, all evaluated at 480×360 resolution.

## Method

HoliGS decomposes each scene into static background Gaussians and time-varying deformable-object Gaussians, then applies a three-level hierarchical warping: (1) per-frame global SE(3) rigid transforms regressed by lightweight Fourier MLPs, (2) skeleton-driven articulation via dual-quaternion blend skinning with Mahalanobis-distance skinning weights over a bone hierarchy, and (3) residual non-rigid deformation through an invertible RealNVP flow (4 coupling layers) regularized by a 3-D cycle-consistency loss. Training proceeds in two phases: a neural-SDF pre-fit jointly optimizes poses, articulation, and soft deformation to produce a well-initialized canonical surface, after which Gaussians are sampled on that surface and the objective switches to standard deformable Gaussian splatting with photometric, depth, normal, and segmentation losses. This hierarchy lets each module solve a simpler subproblem (the skeletal stage explains ~90% of motion energy), enabling stable convergence over minute-long sequences with peak VRAM under 10 GB for reconstruction, versus >80 GB for dense point-tracking baselines.

## Results

On the 11-sequence HoliGS Stereo RGB Dataset, HoliGS achieves a mean PSNR of 21.31 dB, SSIM of 0.747, and LPIPS of 0.263 for novel view synthesis, outperforming the strongest baseline Total-Recon (18.11 dB, 0.724, 0.278) by ~3.2 dB in PSNR. For depth reconstruction, HoliGS reaches 90.1% accuracy at 0.1 m threshold with 0.127 m RMS depth error, compared to Total-Recon's 89.5% and 0.131 m. Three competing Gaussian-based methods (GS-Marble, MoSca, Shape-of-Motion) exceed GPU memory on most long sequences (>600 frames) and cannot report full-sequence results.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HoliGS outperforms all eight baselines on every sequence where a comparison is possible. Against the closest competitor Total-Recon (ICCV 2023), HoliGS gains ~3.2 dB PSNR (21.31 vs. 18.11) and ~0.6% depth accuracy (90.1% vs. 89.5%), with the gap widening on multi-actor sequences (e.g., HUMAN-CAT: 21.34 vs. 17.67 dB). The NeRF-based baselines (HyperNeRF, D²NeRF) and Gaussian-based baselines (Deformable GS, 4DGS) trail by 6–9 dB in PSNR, reflecting their inability to model articulated non-rigid motion over long sequences. Three methods—GS-Marble, MoSca, and Shape-of-Motion—exceed GPU memory on most sequences (>600 frames) and report no full-sequence results; on a controlled 200-frame short-clip protocol (Table 8), MoSca marginally edges HoliGS on LPIPS (0.270 vs. 0.271) but HoliGS leads on PSNR (21.26 vs. 21.20), SSIM (0.741 vs. 0.735), and depth accuracy (91.0% vs. 90.1%), confirming that the advantage is not solely due to long-sequence handling.

## Relation to Prior Work

HoliGS builds directly on 3D Gaussian Splatting as its rendering primitive and extends the deformable GS line (Deformable GS, 4DGS, GS-Marble, MoSca, Shape-of-Motion) by introducing a three-level hierarchical warping pipeline: global SE(3) rigid transforms, skeleton-driven dual-quaternion blend skinning, and a soft RealNVP invertible flow for residual non-rigid detail. It departs from Total-Recon (the closest EVS prior) by replacing NeRF-based volumetric rendering with Gaussian splatting and by eliminating dependence on dense point trackers (CoTracker) in favor of lightweight per-frame pre-processing (PoseNet, SAM, RAFT, UniDepth). The neural-SDF warm-start for Gaussian initialization borrows from BANMo-style articulated reconstruction, but the key conceptual contribution is the explicit decomposition of motion into rigid, articulated, and soft components within a single Gaussian-based framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: reconstructing minute-long monocular videos with multiple interacting actors (humans and animals) at interactive frame rates, a setting where prior NeRF-based EVS methods are prohibitively slow and GS-based methods OOM or produce artifacts. The ~10× training speedup over Total-Recon while improving PSNR/SSIM/depth accuracy makes embodied view synthesis from casual phone captures more accessible for AR/VR and robotics applications. Key limitations remain: the smooth deformation field assumption cannot handle abrupt motion discontinuities, generic pose estimation is less anatomically accurate than parametric models (SMPL), and the method has only been validated on human-animal interaction sequences. Open questions include whether the hierarchical warping generalizes to other articulated categories (furniture, robots), whether event-camera or high-frame-rate sensor fusion can address the discontinuity gap, and whether the pipeline can be compressed for on-device deployment.
