## Problem

Standard RAG pipelines often produce reasoning that diverges from the retrieved evidence, a failure mode the paper terms Reasoning Misalignment. Prior work has focused on improving retrieval quality or training-time robustness, but the explicit alignment of intermediate reasoning steps with evidential constraints at inference time remains largely unaddressed. This misalignment persists even when retrieval is high-quality, causing factual inconsistencies and unsupported conclusions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250726/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system consumes a text query, a set of top-5 retrieved Wikipedia documents, and an initial generated response, then iteratively produces a refined reasoning response alongside structured evidence-grounded critiques that localize specific misalignments. The CLM is trained on contrastive preference tuples pairing an expert (LLaMA3.1-8B) response with a weak (Qwen2.5-0.5B) response, conditioned on the query and documents. ALIGNRAG-AUTO additionally emits a [Good] or [Bad] control token before each critique to signal whether further refinement is needed, enabling dynamic termination without a pre-specified iteration count.

## Method

ALIGNRAG introduces Critique-Driven Alignment (CDA), a test-time iterative refinement loop where a dedicated 8B Critic Language Model (CLM) generates retrieval-sensitive critiques that guide the generator toward evidence-aligned reasoning. The key training mechanism is Contrastive Critique Synthesis (CCS): the CLM is fine-tuned (via LoRA) on synthetic critiques synthesized by contrasting expert and weak model responses, explicitly teaching it to identify misalignments grounded in the retrieved documents rather than relying on self-critique. This contrastive formulation mitigates the self-preference bias inherent in self-refinement pipelines by decoupling critique generation from the target model's own outputs. At inference, each iteration augments the generator's prompt with the CLM's critique as a pseudo-gradient in discrete space, progressively correcting reasoning steps. ALIGNRAG-AUTO extends this by training the CLM to predict a [Good]/[Bad] token, enabling autonomous stopping when alignment is achieved and eliminating manual iteration tuning.

## Results

On five in-domain QA benchmarks (NQ, 2WikiMultiHopQA, TriviaQA, PopQA, ASQA), ALIGNRAG-fixed achieves average accuracy of 62.1% (Qwen2.5-7B + CLM_8B), 62.8% (Qwen2.5-14B + CLM_8B), and 60.6% (LLaMA3.1-8B + CLM_8B), consistently outperforming all test-time refinement baselines. The 8B-parameter CLM outperforms the Self-Refine baseline by 12.1% on out-of-domain (HotpotQA, SQuAD) benchmarks and surpasses a 72B-parameter CLM by 2.2%. ALIGNRAG-AUTO matches or slightly exceeds ALIGNRAG-fixed (e.g., 69.0 vs 67.7 on NQ with Qwen2.5-14B) while dynamically terminating refinement, and as a plug-and-play module it boosts InstructRAG's OOD accuracy by up to 9.4%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Self-Refine (the closest test-time refinement baseline), ALIGNRAG-fixed wins on all three backbones: +3.8% avg (Qwen2.5-7B: 62.1 vs 58.3), +2.1% (Qwen2.5-14B: 62.8 vs 60.7), and +2.8% (LLaMA3.1-8B: 60.6 vs 57.8), with the largest per-benchmark gains on 2WikiMultiHopQA (+4.5% for 7B). Against Self-RAG, ALIGNRAG achieves a dramatic margin of ~14.8% average accuracy (62.8 vs 48.0 for LLaMA3-8B), indicating that architectural modifications for self-reflection are inferior to the dedicated CLM approach. Against InstructRAG (training-time refinement), ALIGNRAG-fixed edges ahead by +1.9% (7B), +1.1% (14B), and +0.2% (LLaMA3.1-8B), and when stacked on top of InstructRAG as a plug-and-play module, it adds a further +2.0–2.2% ID and +2.1–9.4% OOD improvement. Against Vanilla RAG and CoT, all retrieval-augmented methods dominate, with ALIGNRAG achieving the highest scores across the board. Notably, the 8B CLM trained via contrastive critique synthesis outperforms a directly-used 72B CLM by 2.2%, demonstrating that the contrastive training signal is more effective than raw model scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Critique Fine-Tuning (CFT) [Wang et al., 2025] for training a dedicated Critic Language Model and extends Direct Preference Optimization [Rafailov et al., 2023] into a Critique Preference Optimization (CPO) objective for preference-based critique alignment. It departs from Self-RAG [Asai et al., 2024], which requires architectural modifications via special reflection tokens, and from Self-Refine [Madaan et al., 2023], which suffers from self-preference bias, by introducing a contrastive critique synthesis mechanism that explicitly contrasts expert and weak model responses to train an evidence-sensitive critic. The key conceptual departure is reconceptualizing RAG as Retrieval-Augmented Reasoning and formalizing 'Reasoning Misalignment' as a distinct, test-time-optimizable failure mode orthogonal to retrieval quality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play nature of ALIGNRAG (demonstrated by a 9.4% OOD improvement when bolted onto InstructRAG) makes it immediately deployable in existing RAG pipelines without architectural changes, addressing a practical gap that training-time methods like InstructRAG or RetRobust cannot fill. The finding that a contrastively-trained 8B CLM outperforms a vanilla 72B CLM suggests that critique quality, not model scale, is the binding constraint for evidence-grounded feedback—a useful signal for practitioners with limited compute. Open questions remain about effectiveness under extreme retrieval failure (when no retrieved document is relevant) and whether the CLM requires per-generator tuning, both acknowledged as limitations.
