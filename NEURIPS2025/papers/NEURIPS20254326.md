## Problem

Existing text-guided 3D editing methods follow a view-indiscriminate paradigm, editing all views equally regardless of their editing salience relative to the instruction. This ignores cross-view interdependencies—e.g., 'adding glasses' primarily affects the frontal view—causing view-conflicts and spatially inconsistent edits such as features appearing in wrong locations or disappearing from relevant views.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254326/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D object represented as 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) and a user-provided text editing prompt. The 3DGS is rendered into a continuous 360° surrounding video of F frames at H×W resolution, which is scored by CLIP against both an ML-generated description and the editing prompt to select the primary view. The output is an edited 3DGS object with precise local modifications, evaluated by rendering 72 views at 5° azimuth intervals for quantitative metrics (FID, LPIPS, FVD, CLIP-T, DINO-I) and GPTEval3D scoring.

## Method

Pro3D-Editor implements a progressive-views paradigm through three successive modules. (1) Primary-view Sampler selects the most editing-salient view via a CLIP-based scoring formula that penalizes views at 135° and 225° relative angles (matching the six fixed-perspective training views of the multi-view diffusion backbone), then edits it with a 2D editing model. (2) Key-view Render fine-tunes the MV-Adapter multi-view diffusion backbone with MoVE-LoRA: a shared low-rank matrix A (rank 32) captures primary-view features and is updated exclusively from primary-view gradients, while per-view expert matrices B_i (one per key view) learn distinct feature correspondences, decoupling multi-view feature entanglement. A two-stage inference strategy applies editing-region masks within the multi-view attention to blend backbone and MoVE-LoRA outputs, preserving spatial understanding. (3) Full-view Refiner fine-tunes ControlNet-Tile with LoRA (rank 64) to repair fragmented 3DGS regions by generating structurally coherent views from degraded renders, providing dense 3D structural supervision for the final 3DGS update.

## Results

On a custom evaluation set of 6 3D objects with 15 editing prompts (72 rendered views per object), Pro3D-Editor achieves the best scores across all six metrics: FID 63.99, PSNR 22.01, LPIPS 0.101, FVD 449.9, CLIP-T 0.304, and DINO-I 0.928. Compared to the strongest baseline (LGM), Pro3D-Editor improves LPIPS by 47.4% (0.101 vs. 0.192) and DINO-I by 9.7% (0.928 vs. 0.846). The full pipeline also outperforms its own naive variant (random view sampling + shared LoRA, no refiner) by 47.4% in LPIPS and 12.6% in DINO-I, confirming the contribution of the progressive-views paradigm itself.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Pro3D-Editor outperforms all four baselines on every reported metric. The closest competitor is LGM [38], which achieves FID 85.12 and DINO-I 0.846; Pro3D-Editor improves FID by 24.8% (63.99 vs. 85.12) and DINO-I by 9.7% (0.928 vs. 0.846) over LGM. MVEdit [3] and 3D-Adapter [4] both suffer from spatial inconsistencies (e.g., generating a face on the back of a head) and produce substantially worse LPIPS scores (0.362 and 0.356 vs. 0.101). Tailor3D [37] has the weakest editing quality (FID 139.53, LPIPS 0.323) and the lowest editing accuracy (DINO-I 0.701). Qualitatively, the paper notes that LGM and Tailor3D fail to preserve semantically irrelevant regions (e.g., shoes on a doll), while MVEdit produces view-inconsistent edits; Pro3D-Editor maintains both local precision and cross-view spatial coherence.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant view-indiscriminate paradigm in text-guided 3D editing (both iterative single-view methods like Vox-E and parallel multi-view methods like MVEdit/3D-Adapter) by proposing a progressive-views paradigm that propagates editing semantics from the most editing-salient view to editing-sparse views. Technically, it builds on the MV-Adapter multi-view diffusion backbone and extends LoRA into a Mixture-of-View-Experts variant (MoVE-LoRA) with a shared primary-view matrix and per-view expert matrices to decouple cross-view feature correspondences. The Full-view Refiner repurposes ControlNet-Tile from the sparse-view 3DGS reconstruction literature to repair fragmented edited regions, bridging 3D editing with 3D reconstruction techniques.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The progressive-views paradigm addresses a genuine failure mode in existing 3D editing (view-conflicts from indiscriminate editing) with a clean conceptual framing, and the MoVE-LoRA design is a reusable technique for any multi-view generation task requiring directional feature transfer. However, the method requires per-edit fine-tuning (~1.5 hours on A100), limiting practical deployment, and the evaluation is constrained to 6 objects and 15 prompts with no standard benchmark, making it difficult to assess generalizability. Open questions include whether the progressive-views idea can be realized in a training-free manner, whether it extends to scene-level (rather than object-level) editing, and whether the computational overhead can be reduced through distillation or fewer fine-tuning steps.
