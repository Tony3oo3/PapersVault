## Problem

Existing molecular LLMs fall short of a truly universal model: they either rely on separate adapters per task (InstructMol), cover only a subset of task types (PRESTO lacks Mol2Text and Text2Mol), or suffer from distributional shifts and conflicting gradients when jointly learning across domains. The paper identifies three compounding challenges—small and domain-limited datasets, inter-task distributional discrepancies that destabilize joint training, and varying intrinsic representational dimensions across tasks that make a single static adapter suboptimal.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a text instruction, a SELFIES molecular string (chosen over SMILES because syntactically valid SELFIES always decodes to a valid molecule via RDKit, guaranteeing downstream 2D/3D graph conversion), and a molecular graph encoded by MoleculeSTM into node embeddings. Outputs span three modalities: SELFIES sequences (for Mol2Mol and Text2Mol tasks), free-text descriptions (Mol2Text), and scalar numerical values (Mol2Num). All modalities are uniformly tokenized into the LLM's vocabulary space, with variable-length sequences and graph embeddings padded to a fixed tensor shape for parallel training.

## Method

Omni-Mol builds on LLaMA 3.2-1B with a MoleculeSTM graph encoder and a single linear projector, and introduces two key mechanisms. First, Gradient Adaptive LoRA (GAL) replaces LoRA's fixed scaling factor γ = α/r with a learnable γ_θ = α/r^p + β, letting the adapter dynamically adjust its effective rank during training to match each task's intrinsic dimensionality. Second, Mixture-of-GAL-Experts (MoGE) upcycles the dense FFN into N routed experts plus one shared expert (initialized identically from the pre-trained weights), with a randomly initialized router that dynamically assigns tokens to experts, thereby separating task-specific knowledge from shared representations. Training proceeds in two stages: (1) multimodal alignment on PubChem where only the projector is trainable, and (2) unified instruction tuning across all 16 tasks with the backbone frozen, GAL adapters and the MoGE router active, plus an auxiliary sequence-level load-balancing loss on MoGE layers. The resulting 2.2B-parameter model is trained on the newly curated Omni-Mol dataset of 1.42M samples spanning four task categories (Mol2Mol, Mol2Text, Mol2Num, Text2Mol).

## Results

Omni-Mol (2.2B parameters) achieves state-of-the-art performance on 13 out of 16 tasks. On Forward Reaction Prediction, it reaches 0.73 Exact Match and 0.980 BLEU, surpassing the best generalist baseline PRESTO* (0.69 Exa, 0.976 BLEU) and the best specialist InstructMol (0.54 Exa, 0.967 BLEU). On Mol2Num regression tasks, Omni-Mol improves R² by 21.4% and reduces MAE by 25.1% (Weight), 25.8% (LogP), and 39.3% (TPSA) over prior methods. Notably, Omni-Mol outperforms the 685B DeepSeekV3 (in-context learning) on most tasks while using only ~0.3% of its parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Omni-Mol (2.2B, generalist) outperforms the strongest generalist baseline PRESTO* (3.2B) on Forward Reaction Prediction by +0.04 Exact Match (0.73 vs 0.69) and +0.004 BLEU, while using 31% fewer parameters. Against specialist baselines, Omni-Mol surpasses InstructMol (6.7B) by +0.19 Exa on Forward Reaction and by +0.10 BLEU on Reagent Prediction (0.726 vs 0.610). On Description Q&A, Omni-Mol achieves METEOR 0.58, beating DeepSeekV3-685B (0.54) and InstructMol (0.509). On IUPAC2SELFIES and Text-Guided Molecule Generation, Omni-Mol substantially outperforms both Llama3.2-1B (re-implemented specialist) and DeepSeekV3 (ICL), e.g., 0.39 vs 0.31 Exa on I2S and 0.12 vs 0.02 Exa on text-guided generation. The one notable exception is Reagent Prediction on a second evaluation split, where the larger PRESTO (6.7B) achieves 0.77 Exa versus Omni-Mol's 0.72, suggesting that parameter scale still matters for this particular sub-task.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the molecular LLM lineage (Text+Chem T5 → Mol-Instruction → InstructMol → HIGHT → PRESTO) by being the first to unify all four input-output modality pairs (Mol2Mol, Mol2Text, Mol2Num, Text2Mol) in a single model, whereas prior generalist models like PRESTO covered only two types and specialist models like InstructMol used separate LoRA adapters per task. Architecturally, it builds on LoRA by making the scaling factor learnable (GAL) and on MoE upcycling (Komatsuzaki et al., Dai et al.) to construct Mixture-of-GAL-Experts, combining shared and routed experts to handle cross-task interference. The unified instruction-tuning paradigm draws from the broader 'one-model-fits-all' trend in multimodal LLMs (GPT, Flamingo, MiniGPT-5), transplanted to the molecular domain with SELFIES as the molecular tokenization scheme.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Omni-Mol provides the most comprehensive molecular instruction-tuning dataset (1.4M samples, 16 tasks) and the first framework to handle arbitrary modality pairs for small molecules, giving the community a practical baseline and data resource for building generalist AI chemists. The convergence analysis—showing representations become more similar as tasks increase (opposite to InstructMol)—offers empirical support for the Platonic representation hypothesis in the molecular domain, suggesting a universal molecular representation space may exist. Key limitations include restriction to small molecules (no proteins or protein-ligand interactions), limited compute preventing exploration of larger model scales, and the observation that data scaling yields greater gains than parameter scaling, pointing to data collection as the current bottleneck.
