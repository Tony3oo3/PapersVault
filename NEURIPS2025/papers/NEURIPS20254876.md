## Problem

Lightweight video recognition models struggle to capture temporal dynamics effectively because they lack a principled inductive bias for temporal smoothness, leading to overfitting and erratic internal representations. While larger models can absorb both signal and noise in their embeddings, resource-constrained architectures waste limited capacity fitting abrupt, noise-driven shifts between consecutive frames rather than learning meaningful motion patterns.

## Input / Output

The method operates on video clips (10-second sequences from Kinetics-600/400) and produces action class labels. GRW-smoothing is applied to per-frame embeddings extracted from intermediate or final layers, which are globally pooled along spatial dimensions and normalized (via parameter-free BN for intermediate layers, or a learnable affine transform for the final layer). The smoothing window spans 0.5–1.0 s of video (T=5 or T=6 frames), and the final classification head is a 2-layer vanilla transformer with ×4 MLP expansion.

## Method

The core contribution is a Gaussian Random Walk (GRW) smoothing loss that penalizes high acceleration in the sequence of per-frame embeddings. The loss is constructed in two steps: first, a frame-ordering contrastive loss fixes the first frame and contrasts the correct temporal ordering of remaining frames against all permutations, preventing degenerate collapse to a single point; second, the probability distribution in the contrastive term is set to a GRW prior where velocity increments (accelerations) are modeled as i.i.d. standard Gaussian, so the loss grows whenever embeddings exhibit abrupt representational shifts. An additional velocity regularization term Ω(V) controls unconditional speeds and introduces a scaling parameter α. In practice, the full permutation set is enumerated for T≤7 (≤120 orderings), keeping computational overhead to ~2% wall-clock time per epoch. The GRW loss is added to the standard cross-entropy objective with a balance factor λ=0.1, and is applied either at an intermediate layer or at the final layer before a lightweight transformer head.

## Results

On Kinetics-600, GRW-smoothing yields 3.8%–6.4% Top-1 accuracy improvements over the corresponding baselines across the MoViNet and MobileNetV3 families. Specifically, MoViNet-A0-S-GRW reaches 78.4% at 2.7 GFLOPs (vs. 72.3% for MoViNet-A0), MoViNet-A3-GRW achieves 85.6% at 56.4 GFLOPs (vs. 81.8% for MoViNet-A3), and MobileNetV3-S-GRW attains 67.3% at 30 MB memory (vs. 61.3% for MobileNetV3-S). Notably, MoViNet-A3-GRW matches the accuracy of MViTv2-B-32×3 (85.5%) while using 18.3× fewer GFLOPs (56.4 vs. 1030). On Kinetics-400, similar gains are observed, e.g., MoViNet-A3-GRW reaches 81.7% at 56.4 GFLOPs versus 78.2% for MoViNet-A3.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the MoViNet family [15], GRW-smoothing consistently wins: MoViNet-A0-S-GRW improves from 72.3% to 78.4% (+6.1%), MoViNet-A1-S-GRW from 76.7% to 81.9% (+5.2%), MoViNet-A2-S-GRW from 78.4% to 83.3% (+4.9%), and MoViNet-A3-GRW from 81.8% to 85.6% (+3.8%) on K600. Against MobileNetV3 [11] and its TSM-augmented variant [21], MobileNetV3-S-GRW (67.3%) surpasses both MobileNetV3-S (61.3%) and MobileNetV3-S+TSM (65.5%) at comparable FLOPs (2.8). At the ~56 GFLOP level, MoViNet-A3-GRW (85.6%) outperforms X3D-L (79.1% at 77.5 GFLOPs) and MoViNet-A4 (83.5% at 105 GFLOPs), and matches MViTv2-B-32×3 (85.5%) while using 18.3× fewer GFLOPs. On K400, MoViNet-A3-GRW (81.7% at 56.4 GFLOPs) also beats VideoMamba [17] (76.9% at 108 GFLOPs) and X3D-S (73.5% at 76.1 GFLOPs). The ablation in Table 4 confirms that the gains come from the GRW loss itself (+4.0 over baseline) rather than the added Transformer head alone (+0.9), validating the smoothing mechanism as the primary driver of improvement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of temporal coherence priors and probabilistic regularization. It draws conceptual inspiration from Slow Feature Analysis (which minimizes temporal derivatives) and from temporal-order contrastive objectives (Shuffle & Learn, Sorting Sequences), but departs from both by formulating smoothness as a Gaussian Random Walk over embedding velocities, combined with a permutation-based contrastive loss that prevents degenerate collapse. Rather than proposing a new architecture, it repurposes existing lightweight video models (MoViNets, MobileNetV3) and injects the GRW penalty as a plug-and-play training-time regularizer, positioning itself as an inductive-bias layer rather than a structural change.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 3.8–6.4% accuracy gains from a purely additive loss term (with ~2% wall-clock overhead) on resource-constrained models is practically significant for on-device and streaming video recognition, where every GFLOP and MB of memory matters. However, the approach is untested on large-capacity models, may underperform on content with genuinely abrupt transitions (cuts, fast camera moves), and the factorial permutation sum—while bounded in practice—adds conceptual complexity to the training objective. Open questions include whether dynamic, content-adaptive smoothing windows could further improve results, and whether the GRW prior transfers to tasks beyond action recognition such as temporal localization or anomaly detection.
