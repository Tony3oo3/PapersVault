## Problem

In two-sided matching markets with unknown demand and supply curves, existing learning-based pricing policies achieve a suboptimal regret–queue-length tradeoff (1−γ vs. γ) because they converge to a static policy rather than the near-optimal two-price policy. This paper closes the gap by establishing a near-optimal tradeoff of Õ(T^{1−γ}) regret and Õ(T^{γ/2}) average queue length in the online-learning setting, matching the optimal tradeoff known only when the curves are fully known.

## Input / Output

At each discrete time slot the platform observes the current queue lengths for all I customer types and J server types (nonnegative integers) and outputs a price for each queue type within known bounds [p_min, p_max], along with a matching decision pairing compatible customer–server pairs. Arrivals are Bernoulli with rates determined by the inverse demand/supply functions of the chosen prices, so the platform never directly observes or controls arrival rates—only prices. The bipartite compatibility graph G(I∪J, E) is fixed and known, and all queues start empty at t=1.

## Method

The algorithm combines a two-point zero-order projected gradient ascent on the fluid optimization problem (with bisection search to estimate prices corresponding to target arrival rates) with a novel probabilistic two-price policy for sample collection. During each bisection iteration, the policy sets the learning price when a queue is empty, the extreme (rejecting) price when the queue exceeds a threshold q^th = T^γ, and—critically—randomly chooses between the learning price and an α-perturbed price (with probability 1/2 each) when the queue is nonempty but below threshold. This probabilistic mechanism ensures a constant fraction of samples remain unbiased for gradient estimation while the perturbation enforces a negative drift that bounds average queue length at Õ(T^{γ/2}). The key theoretical contribution is proving that this decoupling yields the 1−γ vs. γ/2 Pareto frontier, with a matching lower bound (Lemma 1) showing the tradeoff is optimal up to logarithmic factors under a broad class of policies.

## Results

The proposed probabilistic two-price policy achieves a near-optimal tradeoff: $\tilde{O}(T^{1-\gamma})$ regret, $\tilde{O}(T^{\gamma/2})$ average queue length, and $O(T^{\gamma})$ maximum queue length for $\gamma \in (0, 1/6]$. This matches the optimal $\Theta(\eta^{1-\gamma})$ vs. $\Theta(\eta^{\gamma/2})$ tradeoff of the known-curve setting [2] up to logarithmic factors. In simulations on a single-link system with $w=0.001$ and $\gamma=1/6$, the proposed algorithm improves the combined regret + queue-length objective by up to 22% over the threshold policy of [1]. The regret bound is capped at $\tilde{O}(T^{5/6})$ regardless of $\gamma$, reflecting an inherent learning limitation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the threshold policy of [1] (Yang & Ying, 2024), the proposed method strictly improves the average queue length from $\tilde{O}(T^{\gamma})$ to $\tilde{O}(T^{\gamma/2})$ while preserving the same $\tilde{O}(T^{1-\gamma})$ regret and $O(T^{\gamma})$ maximum queue length bounds. In the simulation (Figure 2, single-link, $w=0.001$, $\gamma=1/6$), the proposed algorithm outperforms the threshold policy by up to 22% on the combined objective $\mathbb{E}[R(t)] + wt \cdot \text{AvgQLen}(t)$. Against the two-price policy of [2] (Varma et al., 2023), which assumes full knowledge of the demand and supply curves, the proposed method matches the optimal regret–queue-length tradeoff up to logarithmic factors but necessarily incurs additional learning error; in simulation, the genie-aided [2] policy still outperforms the proposed algorithm, as expected, since it bypasses the estimation challenge entirely. The paper also notes that the restricted range $\gamma \in (0, 1/6]$ (versus $\gamma \in [0,1]$ in [2]) is a limitation, with the regret floor at $\tilde{O}(T^{5/6})$ arising from the need for bisection-based price-to-arrival-rate mapping.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on two prior works: Varma et al. [2] established the optimal regret–queue-length tradeoff (1−γ vs. γ/2) for two-sided matching queues but required known demand/supply curves, while Yang and Ying [1] introduced the first learning-based (parameter-agnostic) pricing policy for the same model but achieved only a suboptimal 1−γ vs. γ tradeoff by mimicking a static policy. The key methodological departure here is a probabilistic two-price policy that, with constant probability, perturbs the price by α to induce negative drift for queue control while discarding the resulting biased samples, thereby preserving the two-price structure of [2] in the learning setting. The zero-order stochastic projected gradient ascent and bisection search machinery are inherited from [1], with the innovation confined to how samples are collected during the bisection phase.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result closes the gap between the known-curve and unknown-curve settings up to logarithmic factors, establishing that the 1−γ vs. γ/2 Pareto frontier is near-optimal even under online learning, which validates the two-price policy structure as the right inductive bias for this problem class. A notable limitation is the restricted range γ ∈ (0, 1/6], which caps the achievable regret at Õ(T^{5/6}) rather than the information-theoretic √T lower bound; the authors identify the dual learning structure (prices → arrival rates → profit) as the source of this gap and flag the true minimax regret as an open question. The probabilistic decoupling of exploration from queue control is a general technique that the authors argue could transfer to other online-learning-in-queues problems, though no such extension is demonstrated here.
