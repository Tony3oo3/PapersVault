## Problem

When multiple advertisers seek to influence an LLM's generated reply, their preferences conflict with the user's and with each other, and they may misreport their preferences. Existing mechanisms like VCG require computing the exact optimal solution (intractable for LLMs) and lose strategyproofness under suboptimal allocation, while prior LLM auction mechanisms suffer from the exposure problem, manipulability, and per-token cost scaling. The paper asks: can we design an auction that is strategyproof, provably converges to the optimal KL-regularized distribution, and operates with only API-level access to LLMs?

## Input / Output

The mechanism takes a user query x, a reference LLM π_ref, n advertiser LLMs (or their induced reward functions r_i(x,y) = log(π_i(y|x)/π_ref(y|x))), optional advertiser context descriptions c_i, and a regularization hyperparameter τ. It outputs a single token-sequence reply y shared by all advertisers (not per-advertiser allocations) and a payment profile p_i for each advertiser. Notably, the mechanism is static: all M candidate replies are generated and observed before advertisers submit reports, and only forward-pass API calls are required—no access to model weights or fine-tuning is needed.

## Method

MOSAIC's allocation rule uses importance sampling: it generates M candidate replies from a context-aware LLM π_con(·|x;c) (which incorporates advertiser descriptions to bridge the gap between π_ref and advertiser-preferred outputs), then re-weights them via softmax of (Σ_i r_i(x,y_j)/τ + log(π_ref(y_j|x)/π_gen(y_j|x))) and samples one reply. This provably converges to the optimal KL-regularized distribution (Equation 2) as M→∞, with a √M convergence rate whose variance is reduced by using a proposal distribution closer to the target. The payment rule exploits the cyclic monotonicity of the softmax allocation rule to derive Rochet payments that guarantee strategyproofness even at finite M (unlike VCG). A payment offset C = −Ũ_i(0, r̂_{−i})—charging each advertiser her utility if her reward were zero—ensures 'almost individual rationality' and the 'what you give is what you get' property, aligning each advertiser's utility with her marginal contribution to social welfare and preventing free-riding by irrelevant advertisers.

## Results

MOSAIC converges to the optimal distribution with as few as M=20 candidate replies, at a compute cost equivalent to just five LLM queries. The context-aware version achieves higher log probability with respect to the optimal distribution using only 4 candidate replies than the baseline version achieves with 20, and with 20 replies it nearly matches the proxy of sampling directly from the optimal distribution. The context-aware mechanism significantly boosts total advertiser normalized reward (scaling rapidly with M), while the baseline version fails to improve and may even slightly decrease advertiser rewards. The payment offset increases the Pearson correlation between advertiser utility and reward from 0.4 to 0.8, and the mechanism is experimentally shown to be ex-ante individually rational.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper explicitly states that there are no other mechanisms in the literature for aggregating multiple LLMs to compare against, so no external baselines are evaluated. The only quantitative comparison is between the two internal variants of MOSAIC: the context-aware version (using π_con) and the baseline version (using π_ref). The context-aware version dominates the baseline on all three headline metrics: reply log probability (achieving with 4 candidates what the baseline needs 20 for), total advertiser normalized reward (positive and scaling vs. flat or slightly negative), and auctioneer revenue (scaling rapidly vs. minimal). The baseline is described as 'the strongest applicable benchmark' since it is the only other tractable mechanism in the literature that converges to the optimal distribution. VCG is discussed theoretically (Section 3.2, Appendix B.1) and shown to be non-strategyproof when the allocation rule cannot fully converge, but is not evaluated experimentally.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on three foundational threads: the closed-form KL-regularized optimal policy from Peters and Schaal (2007), the cyclic monotonicity / Rochet payment framework for strategyproofness (Rochet, 1987), and the LLM-to-reward-function mapping from Rafailov et al. (2023, DPO). It departs from the first LLM mechanism design work of Dutting et al. (2024), which operated token-by-token and suffered from the exposure problem and manipulability, and from Dubey et al. (2024) and Hajiaghayi et al. (2024), which restricted advertisers to scalar bids or fixed ad texts. MOSAIC is positioned as the first mechanism that simultaneously achieves provable convergence to the optimal distribution, strategyproofness without requiring full convergence, and black-box (API-only) operation over full sequences.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As LLM providers (OpenAI, Perplexity, Meta) actively explore advertising and instant checkout, a principled auction mechanism with incentive compatibility is a practical necessity, and MOSAIC provides the first complete theoretical and empirical blueprint for this. Key limitations include the single-query static setting, synthetic experiments on small models (Llama-2-7b), and the assumption that advertisers can supply verifiable LLMs or reward functions. Open questions include extending to multi-turn conversations (dynamic mechanism design), scaling to many advertisers, handling private user data, and validating on real advertising traffic. The impossibility result that standard ex-post IR is incompatible with strategyproofness and convergence in this setting is a useful negative result that shapes future mechanism design for LLMs.
