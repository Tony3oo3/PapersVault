## Problem

Training a single multilingual multi-task speech model that jointly performs speech recognition and translation is hampered by gradient conflicts between these objectives, causing flat multi-objective optimization to stall. The paper asks whether highly conflicting objectives should be optimized jointly or separated into a hierarchical (multi-level) structure, and how to do so efficiently without prohibitive computational overhead.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250566/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes raw speech audio in multiple languages (English, French, German, Spanish, Catalan on CoVoST 2; English on LibriSpeech; Mandarin on AISHELL-1) and produces two outputs: a text transcription via a CTC-based speech recognition head and a translated text (to English) via a cross-entropy-based Transformer decoder head. A shared Conformer encoder (or Whisper-medium encoder) processes the audio, while task-specific classification heads and a separate Transformer decoder handle recognition and translation respectively, with a self-supervised CPC loss applied to the shared backbone throughout training.

## Method

The paper proposes three 'objective soup recipes' that place multi-objective optimization at different hierarchical levels: VS-MSP (flat single-level vector optimization of all CTC, CE, and CPC losses with MoDo dynamic weighting), VC-MSP (bilevel, where the self-supervised CPC loss acts as a lower-level constraint via a penalty term while supervised CTC and CE losses are optimized at the upper level), and VM-MSP (multilevel, where the most conflicting objectives—e.g., ASR vs. translation—are separated into distinct optimization levels with cascading penalty parameters). The dynamic conflict-avoiding update direction is computed via the MoDo stochastic algorithm, and a lightweight layer-selection mechanism restricts this computation to only the 20–30% of backbone layers exhibiting negative cosine similarity between task gradients, reducing memory by ~18% and time by ~17% without accuracy loss. Experiments on Conformer (58M–150M) and Whisper-medium models show that the bilevel VC-MSP outperforms flat optimization by ~9.8% WER and ~8.6% BLEU, while the multilevel VM-MSP with task-based hierarchy further improves by 4.2% WER and 2.8% BLEU over VC-MSP.

## Results

On CoVoST-2 with a 150M Conformer, VM-MSP (USA) achieves the best average WER of 18.4% and BLEU of 28.9, outperforming the two-stage baseline (20.5% WER, 26.5 BLEU) by 10.2% and 9.1% respectively. On the 83M Conformer, VM-MSP (USA) reaches 23.6% WER and 24.9 BLEU versus 25.3% and 22.7 for two-stage. The efficient variant (layer-wise conflict pruning) matches VM-MSP performance while reducing GPU memory from 14.7 GB to 12.8 GB and training time from 4.1 h to 3.7 h per epoch. On Whisper-medium, VM-MSP (UAS) achieves 14.1% average WER and 35.1 BLEU, beating two-stage by 11.3% WER and 6.4% BLEU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three proposed MOO recipes (VS-MSP, VC-MSP, VM-MSP) against three baselines: two-stage training (pre-train then fine-tune), two-stage static (fine-tune with hand-tuned loss weights), and joint bilevel training without multi-objective optimization (Cui et al., TASLP 2024; Saif et al., arXiv 2024). All three proposed methods consistently outperform every baseline on both WER and BLEU across all languages and model sizes. VC-MSP (bilevel MOO) improves over two-stage by 9.8% in WER and 8.6% in BLEU on the 150M Conformer, confirming that dynamic conflict-avoiding gradient weighting is more effective than static weighting or plain bilevel joint training. VM-MSP (multilevel MOO) further improves over VC-MSP by 4.2% WER and 2.8% BLEU, demonstrating that separating the most conflicting objectives (ASR vs. translation) into different optimization levels yields additional gains. The joint bilevel baseline [16, 47] sits between two-stage and VS-MSP, showing that bilevel structure alone helps but is insufficient without explicit gradient-conflict mitigation via dynamic weighting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the bilevel optimization framework for ASR (Cui et al., TASLP 2024; Saif et al., 2024) from a single-task setting to multilingual multi-task speech processing, introducing three hierarchical 'objective soup' recipes (VS-MSP, VC-MSP, VM-MSP) that place conflicting objectives at different optimization levels. It builds on the MoDo dynamic-weighting algorithm [9] for conflict-avoiding gradient updates and departs from prior multilingual multi-task approaches (Whisper, Mu²SLAM, dual-decoder architectures) that rely on static loss weighting or simple joint training without explicit gradient-conflict mitigation. The key conceptual departure is treating the self-supervised CPC loss as a lower-level constraint and separating ASR from translation across optimization levels, rather than optimizing all objectives in a single flat vector.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, principled recipe for resolving gradient conflicts in multilingual multi-task speech models, with the layer-wise conflict-pruning mechanism offering a 17% time and 18% memory reduction without accuracy loss—a useful efficiency gain for practitioners training large speech models. A notable open question is generalization to tightly coupled architectures (e.g., unified decoders shared across tasks), where task-specific conflicts may propagate more deeply through shared components. The consistent gains across Conformer and Whisper architectures at multiple scales (58M–150M) strengthen the evidence, though the evaluation is limited to 5 languages and 2 tasks, leaving scalability to dozens of languages or additional tasks (e.g., language identification, summarization) untested.
