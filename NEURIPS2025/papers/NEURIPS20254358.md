## Problem

Existing text-guided image editing approaches, predominantly diffusion-based, regenerate the entire target image rather than selectively modifying only the intended editing regions. This full-image generation introduces unnecessary computational cost and a training bias that prioritizes reconstruction of non-editing regions over regeneration of editing regions, degrading edit quality. The paper asks whether image editing can be reformulated to regenerate only the tokens corresponding to the editing region, avoiding unintended modifications entirely.

## Input / Output

The model takes three conditioning inputs: a text instruction (embedded via FLAN-T5 and left-padded to fixed length L_T), a source image (tokenized by VQGAN into L discrete tokens), and a pixel-level binary editing mask (patchified via max-pooling and mapped to a two-entry codebook of editing and non-editing embeddings, yielding L mask tokens). The model processes L_T + 2L input tokens and outputs only L_E editing tokens (where L_E ≤ L), which are sparsely filled back into the source image at the masked positions. For free-form editing without a mask, all L tokens are regenerated in raster-scan order. Training uses 256×256 images; evaluation is performed at 512×512 after a short fine-tuning stage.

## Method

NEP is built on a two-stage training strategy. First, LlamaGen-XL (775M parameters) is extended into RLlamaGen by learning a sequence of positional embeddings that are shuffled according to a random order, enabling the autoregressive model to generate image tokens in any user-specified order rather than fixed raster scan. Second, RLlamaGen is fine-tuned for editing by adding only two learnable embeddings (E_emb and U_emb, 3.6k parameters total) that encode the editing mask, so the model predicts tokens exclusively at editing positions in their raster-scan subsequence order. The key architectural insight is that by conditioning on the full source image tokens plus the interleaved mask sequence, the model can attend to all non-editing context while generating only the L_E editing tokens, eliminating the reconstruction bias inherent in full-image generation. Additionally, NEP supports test-time scaling through an iterative refinement loop that uses Grad-CAM on a CLIP critic to identify low-quality token regions, regenerates them via NEP with rejection sampling over multiple random orders, and accepts revisions only if an ImageReward score improves.

## Results

On the MagicBrush single-turn region-aware editing benchmark, NEP achieves the best scores across all four metrics: L1=0.0547, L2=0.0163, CLIP-I=0.9350, and DINO=0.9044, outperforming the closest competitor UltraEdit (L1=0.0575, CLIP-I=0.9307). On the multi-turn setting, NEP achieves L1=0.0707 and CLIP-I=0.9107, competitive with AnySD (CLIP-I=0.9152) and UltraEdit (L1=0.0745). On the Emu Edit free-form editing benchmark (no masks), NEP achieves the best CLIPout (0.3078) and competitive CLIPdir (0.1064), matching or exceeding all baselines on regeneration quality while maintaining strong reconstruction (DINO=0.8440).

## Baselines & Comparisons

NEP achieves the best single-turn performance on all four MagicBrush metrics, beating UltraEdit (the closest competitor) by 0.0028 in L1 and 0.0043 in CLIP-I, and substantially outperforming diffusion-based methods like InstructPix2Pix (L1=0.1141) and HIVE (L1=0.1092) by roughly 2× in L1 distance. In the multi-turn setting, NEP wins on L1 (0.0707 vs. 0.0745 for UltraEdit) but trails AnySD slightly on CLIP-I (0.9107 vs. 0.9152) and DINO (0.8493 vs. 0.8623), indicating a small trade-off in perceptual similarity under cumulative edits. On the Emu Edit free-form benchmark (no masks), NEP achieves the best CLIPout (0.3078) and matches MIGE on CLIPdir (0.1064 vs. 0.1070), while AnyEdit leads on reconstruction metrics (CLIPimg=0.9202, DINO=0.8919) due to its full-image regeneration strategy. The autoregressive baseline EditAR (L1=0.1028 single-turn) is significantly outperformed, confirming that the random-order pre-training and editing-token-only generation are critical. For zero-shot editing (no fine-tuning), RLlamaGen already surpasses aMUSEd on MagicBrush (L1=0.0743 vs. 0.0913 single-turn), demonstrating the value of the any-order pre-training stage.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends LlamaGen, an autoregressive text-to-image model, by incorporating random-order generation via learned positional embeddings (following σ-GPTs and Randomized AR Visual Generation) to create RLlamaGen, which can generate tokens in any user-specified order. The key conceptual departure from prior editing work (both diffusion-based methods like SDEdit/Null Text Inversion and AR-based methods like EditAR) is the formulation of editing as Next Editing-token Prediction: rather than regenerating the full image, only tokens within the editing region are predicted, with source image tokens and mask embeddings serving as conditioning. This avoids the reconstruction bias and computational waste inherent in full-image regeneration approaches, while the random-order pre-training provides the flexibility to target arbitrary spatial regions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

NEP demonstrates that autoregressive models can now achieve state-of-the-art on established editing benchmarks (MagicBrush), challenging the dominance of diffusion models in this domain. The zero-shot editing capability that emerges naturally from random-order pre-training—without any editing-specific training—suggests a path toward unified generation and editing in a single model. However, the reliance on user-provided editing masks remains a significant practical limitation, and the robustness to imperfect masks (particularly when the mask is smaller than the true editing region) is unaddressed. Open questions include whether automated mask prediction can be integrated seamlessly, whether the approach scales to higher resolutions, and whether the test-time scaling loop can be further improved with stronger verifiers or self-improvement training.
