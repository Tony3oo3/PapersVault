## Problem

Prior analyses of single-timescale actor-critic algorithms established O(ε^{-2}) sample complexity for reaching an ε-close stationary policy, which via the gradient domination lemma translates to O(ε^{-4}) for an ε-close globally optimal policy. Whether this O(ε^{-4}) global rate could be improved remained open, as the strong coupling between simultaneously updated actor and critic makes the error recursions highly non-monotonic and difficult to bound directly in sub-optimality.

## Input / Output

The algorithm operates on a finite-state, finite-action discounted MDP with softmax-parameterized policies, consuming exactly one state-action transition per iteration (no experience replay buffer, no batch processing). The step sizes for both actor and critic must follow a non-standard O(k^{-2/3}) decay schedule rather than the conventional O(k^{-1/2}) used in (non)convex optimization. The output is a last-iterate policy guaranteeing J* − E[J^{π_k}] ≤ O(k^{-1/3}), i.e., O(ε^{-3}) samples for an ε-optimal policy, under a sufficient exploration assumption requiring positive initial state distribution and exploratory policies.

## Method

The core contribution is a three-part analytical framework for the coupled actor-critic recursions. First, the paper derives explicit recursions for the sub-optimality a_k and critic tracking error z_k, neither of which is monotonically decreasing individually. Second, it identifies the Lyapunov term x_k = a_k + z_k² as a monotonically decreasing quantity and, crucially, exploits the Gradient Domination Lemma structure directly within the recursion (rather than applying it post-hoc as in prior work), yielding the simplified form x_{k+1} ≤ x_k − c·η_k·x_k² + c'·η_k². Third, it develops an ODE domination technique: by choosing η_k proportional to x_k², the recursion is dominated by the solvable ODE du/dk = −c·u⁴, giving the closed-form bound x_k = O(k^{-1/3}). This ODE-based approach handles the time-decaying learning rate that makes the recursion harder than the exact-gradient case, and the resulting O(k^{-2/3}) step-size schedule is shown to be optimal within this variance-based analysis framework.

## Results

The main result (Theorem 3.3) establishes that single-timescale actor-critic with step sizes η_k, β_k = O(k^{-2/3}) achieves a global sub-optimality gap of J* − E[J^{π_θ_k}] ≤ O(k^{-1/3}), yielding a sample complexity of O(ε^{-3}) to obtain an ε-close globally optimal policy. This improves upon the prior best rate of O(ε^{-4}) (equivalent to O(ε^{-2}) for ε-stationary policies) established by Olshevsky and Gharesifard (2023), Chen et al. (2021), and Chen and Zhao (2024). Additionally, the critic tracking error achieves a rate of z_k = O(k^{-1/3}), improving over the O(k^{-1/4}) rate in Chen and Zhao (2024), and the convergence guarantee holds on the last iterate rather than the best iterate.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against three prior single-timescale actor-critic analyses: Olshevsky and Gharesifard (2023) and Chen et al. (2021) under i.i.d. sampling, and Chen and Zhao (2024) under Markovian sampling. All three prior works achieve O(ε^{-2}) sample complexity for ε-stationary policies (‖∇J^π‖ ≤ ε), which translates to O(ε^{-4}) for global optimality via the Gradient Domination Lemma. The proposed method improves the global sample complexity to O(ε^{-3}) by directly exploiting the gradient domination structure in the actor recursion rather than applying it only at the end, and by using a novel ODE-domination technique to solve the coupled Lyapunov recursion. The step sizes also differ: prior methods use the conventional k^{-1/2} decay, while the proposed analysis requires k^{-2/3} decay for both actor and critic. The convergence guarantee is also stronger in that it holds on the last iterate, whereas Chen and Zhao (2024) provides a best-iterate guarantee. The critic error rate is also improved from O(k^{-1/4}) to O(k^{-1/3}).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the finite-time convergence analysis of single-timescale actor-critic initiated by Konda and Tsitsiklis (1999) and refined by Olshevsky and Gharesifard (2023), Chen et al. (2021), and Chen and Zhao (2024). The key departure is methodological: rather than treating the policy optimization objective as a generic smooth non-convex function and bounding the average squared gradient norm (yielding O(ε^{-4}) global complexity via a post-hoc gradient domination step), the authors directly exploit the gradient domination structure of softmax-parameterized MDPs (from Mei et al., 2022) within the coupled actor-critic recursions. This requires a novel Lyapunov term (actor error + squared critic error) and an ODE domination technique to resolve the resulting interdependent recursions, which in turn necessitates non-standard k^{-2/3} step sizes rather than the conventional k^{-1/2} rates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The improvement from O(ε^{-4}) to O(ε^{-3}) sample complexity for global optimality is a meaningful step toward the O(ε^{-2}) lower bound for reinforcement learning, and the ODE domination framework is modular enough to potentially extend to minimax optimization, bi-level optimization, and multi-agent settings. However, the analysis is confined to tabular MDPs with finite state spaces and requires a sufficient exploration assumption, limiting immediate practical impact. The authors themselves identify that variance-based analysis has likely reached its limit at O(ε^{-3}), and that a shift to bias-based analysis (allowing constant critic step sizes) could unlock the O(ε^{-2}) lower bound — a direction they leave open. The non-standard k^{-2/3} step size schedule also diverges from practical implementations, raising questions about whether the theoretical rate is achievable in practice.
