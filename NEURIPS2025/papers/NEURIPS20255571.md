## Problem

Data augmentation for hypergraph learning remains underexplored because conventional mixup methods assume Euclidean convexity or pairwise graph structure, and naively applying them to hypergraphs disrupts the group-wise semantics encoded in hyperedges. This is particularly problematic under label scarcity and structural noise, where preserving the covariance between node features and hyperedge contexts is essential for meaningful interpolation.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a node feature matrix X ∈ R^{|V|×d}, a binary hypergraph incidence matrix H ∈ {0,1}^{|V|×|E|}, and partial node labels (semi-supervised setting with as few as 0.5% labeled nodes). Hyperedge features are derived via degree-normalized aggregation of member node features. The output is a set of synthetic nodes with mixed features, mixed soft labels, and adaptively reconstructed hyperedge memberships, which are fed into an HGNN backbone to produce node class predictions. For 3D object datasets, multi-view CNN features (MVCNN/GVCNN) serve as node features and hyperedges are built from both geometric KNN and cosine feature similarity.

## Method

HyperMixup is a three-stage augmentation pipeline: (1) structure-aware node pairing selects node pairs by a dual similarity score combining node-feature cosine similarity with a weighted hyperedge-semantic cosine similarity, preventing interpolation across topologically disconnected regions; (2) hierarchical mixing performs intra-node linear interpolation (λ ~ Beta(α,α)) followed by hyperedge-context enhancement that blends the interpolated feature with the source nodes' hyperedge representations using parameters p and q; (3) adaptive topology reconstruction assigns each synthetic node the intersection of its parents' hyperedges plus any additional hyperedges whose feature affinity exceeds a k-NN-adaptive threshold, maintaining graph consistency while allowing controlled expansion. Training follows a VRM objective combining cross-entropy on mixed samples, a hyperedge smoothness term, and a KL-divergence label-consistency regularizer, with an adaptive scaling factor α_t balancing the two regularization components. Theoretically, the method induces three distinct regularization effects—node-hyperedge gradient alignment, hyperedge-covariance-driven curvature penalization, and Hessian-covariance interaction—yielding provable robustness bounds against combined node-hyperedge perturbations.

## Results

On citation network classification, HyperMixup achieves 83.60% on Cora, 79.50% on PubMed, and 72.20% on CiteSeer, outperforming the strongest graph-based baselines (GAT: 83.0%/79.00%/72.5%) and hypergraph baselines (HGNN: 82.09%/78.60%/71.60%). On 3D object recognition, HyperMixup reaches 97.04% on ModelNet40 and 85.50% on NTU2012, surpassing HGNN+ (96.92%/84.18%) and GAT (95.75%/80.16%). In the low-label-rate robustness experiment on Cora, HyperMixup maintains strong accuracy down to 0.5% label rate while GCN, HGNN, and HGNN+ degrade sharply. In the augmentation comparison (Table 3), HyperMixup on HGNN+ achieves 84.02%/80.04%/73.02% on Cora/PubMed/CiteSeer, exceeding GraphMixup on the same backbone (82.16%/78.82%/72.13%) by 1.86–1.21 percentage points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HyperMixup outperforms all ten baselines on every dataset where they are directly comparable. Against graph-based GNNs, it leads GAT by 0.60% on Cora and 5.34% on NTU2012, GraphSAGE by 0.40% on Cora and 4.80% on NTU2012, and GraphConv by 1.41% on Cora and 4.54% on NTU2012. Among hypergraph-specific methods, the gains over HGNN are 1.51% (Cora), 0.90% (PubMed), 0.60% (CiteSeer), 0.24% (ModelNet40), and 2.39% (NTU2012); over HGNN+ the margins are substantially larger at 6.89%, 4.42%, 5.77%, 0.12%, and 1.32% respectively. HyperGCN, which underperforms notably on citation data (64.11% on Cora), is surpassed by 19.49% on Cora and 8.09% on CiteSeer. Hyper-Atten is the closest hypergraph competitor, with HyperMixup ahead by 0.99% (Cora), 0.50% (PubMed), 1.32% (CiteSeer), 0.93% (ModelNet40), and 4.00% (NTU2012). In the dedicated augmentation comparison (Table 3), HyperMixup on HGNN+ (84.02%/80.04%/73.02%) beats both Mixup and GraphMixup applied to the same HGNN+ backbone (76.70%/74.90%/66.20% and 82.16%/78.82%/72.13%), confirming that hyperedge-aware mixing is strictly superior to generic graph augmentation strategies.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Mixup data augmentation paradigm from Euclidean and pairwise-graph domains to hypergraphs, building directly on the HGNN/HGNN+ message-passing architecture and the Vicinal Risk Minimization theoretical framework. It departs from prior graph-based augmentations (GraphMixup, GraphMix) by introducing hyperedge-aware node pairing, dual-level feature mixing that fuses node and hyperedge context, and adaptive topology reconstruction—addressing the core issue that linear interpolation in hypergraph space can sever group-wise semantic constraints. The theoretical contribution generalizes the standard Mixup regularization decomposition by incorporating hyperedge covariance structures (Σ_e) and a cross-covariance term (Σ_ve) absent from pairwise-graph analyses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work provides a principled augmentation strategy for hypergraph learning in label-scarce settings, with provable regularization and robustness guarantees that go beyond what graph-based mixup can offer. However, the empirical gains are modest (0.8–1.5% on citation networks, 0.3–1% on 3D shapes), and two acknowledged limitations constrain broader impact: cubic scaling of hyperedge covariance computation with hyperedge size, and the assumption of static hypergraphs. Open questions include whether the framework extends to dynamic/evolving hypergraphs, whether the theoretical robustness bounds translate to practical adversarial settings beyond synthetic perturbations, and whether the gains persist on larger-scale hypergraph benchmarks beyond the five datasets tested.
