## Problem

Sampling from distributions supported on nonconvex sets defined by mixed equality and inequality constraints remains a major challenge: existing Langevin-based methods rely on expensive or intractable projection steps, are typically specialized to either equality or inequality constraints alone, and lack rigorous non-asymptotic convergence guarantees. This paper addresses the gap by introducing a unified, projection-free framework that handles both constraint types simultaneously with provable exponential convergence.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255300/cropped_page5_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a smooth log-target density f(x), smooth equality constraints h(x)=0 (m constraints), smooth inequality constraints g(x)≤0 (l constraints), and an initial point x₀ that may be infeasible (outside the constraint set). It produces samples from the Riemannian density ρ_Σ(x) ∝ exp(−f(x))dσ_Σ on the submanifold Σ, with the unusual property that infeasible initializations are deterministically driven onto the feasible set in finite time via the landing mechanism, after which the process mixes on Σ. The discrete variant OLLA-H requires only gradient and Hessian-vector products per step, avoiding full Hessian formation.

## Method

OLLA constructs its SDE by solving a least-squares problem: the diffusion matrix Q is the closest symmetric matrix to √2·I whose columns are tangent to the constraint manifold (Q∇h_i = Q∇g_j = 0), and the drift q is the closest vector to −∇f satisfying linear equations that enforce exponential decay of all constraint violations at rate α. This yields a closed-form SDE with three drift components — a tangential Langevin drift (Π∇f), a deterministic normal 'landing' drift (α∇JᵀG⁻¹J) that drives h_i(X_t) = h_i(X_0)e^{−αt} and g_j(X_t) → 0 in finite time, and a mean-curvature (Itô–Stratonovich) correction term. The discrete OLLA-H algorithm uses Euler–Maruyama integration with a Hutchinson trace estimator (N random probes) to approximate the curvature term at O(N·grad-cost) per step instead of O(d·grad-cost). Under LICQ, manifold regularity, and a log-Sobolev inequality on Σ, the paper proves non-asymptotic exponential convergence in 2-Wasserstein distance for equality-only, inequality-only, and mixed-constraint scenarios.

## Results

On the Bayesian logistic regression task with fairness and monotonicity constraints (d up to 100,002), OLLA-H (N=0) achieves the lowest test NLL (0.514–0.524) while running in 63–82 seconds, compared to CLangevin (0.564–0.580 NLL, 1162–1428 s), CHMC (0.595–0.606 NLL, 526–611 s), and CGHMC (0.598–0.606 NLL, 76–88 s). On the molecular system (d up to 90), OLLA-H (N=0) matches CGHMC's radius-of-gyration estimates (e.g., 49.08 vs 49.94 at d=90) in 704 s versus CGHMC's 1116 s and CLangevin's 52,220 s. In high-dimensional scaling tests (d up to 700), OLLA-H maintains a flat CPU/ESS of ~0.05 s/sample and accurate probability estimates, while CLangevin's P(x₁>0) collapses from 0.5 to ~0.2.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OLLA-H (N=0) outperforms all three projection-based baselines on both accuracy and speed in the Bayesian logistic regression task, achieving ~0.05–0.08 lower NLL than CLangevin, CHMC, and CGHMC while running 14–17× faster than CLangevin and 6–7× faster than CHMC. Against CGHMC (the most competitive baseline, used as ground truth), OLLA-H is ~1× faster at small d but the gap narrows; however, OLLA-H's NLL advantage of ~0.08 is consistent across all dimensions. On the molecular system, OLLA-H (N=0) is ~74× faster than CLangevin at d=90 (704 s vs 52,220 s) and ~1.6× faster than CGHMC (704 s vs 1116 s), with comparable Rg² estimates. In high-dimensional scaling (d up to 700), OLLA-H maintains flat CPU/ESS (~0.05 s/sample) and unbiased probability estimates, whereas CLangevin degrades severely (P(x₁>0) drops to ~0.2) and CHMC/CGHMC remain accurate but slower. The key trade-off is that projection-based methods achieve tighter constraint violations (<0.0008 vs OLLA-H's <0.005 for equality), but this comes at a substantial computational cost that grows with the number of constraints.

## Relation to Prior Work

OLLA adapts the 'landing' mechanism from nonconvex constrained optimization (Muehlebach & Jordan [14,15]; Schechtman et al. [16]) to the sampling setting, where it provides a deterministic normal-direction correction that replaces expensive projections. Conceptually, it unifies the equality-constrained orthogonal-space variational sampler of Zhang et al. [17] and the inequality-constrained functional gradient flow of Zhang et al. [18] into a single projection-free SDE, while also subsuming the SHAKE/RATTLE-based constrained Langevin and HMC methods of Lelievre, Rousset, and Stoltz [19–21] as special cases. The key departure from all prior work is the simultaneous enforcement of nonconvex equality and inequality constraints without slack variables, combined with non-asymptotic exponential convergence guarantees in 2-Wasserstein distance under a log-Sobolev condition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating projections, OLLA removes a major computational bottleneck for sampling on nonconvex constraint sets that arise in molecular dynamics, constrained generative modeling, and fairness-constrained Bayesian inference. The practical finding that the mean-curvature (Itô-Stratonovich) correction can be entirely skipped (N=0) without measurable accuracy loss significantly reduces per-step cost, making OLLA-H competitive with or faster than projection-based baselines even at d=100,000. Key open questions include: (i) non-asymptotic convergence guarantees for the discrete Euler-Maruyama algorithm (only the continuous SDE is analyzed), (ii) stability when the number of equality constraints is large relative to dimension, and (iii) whether the LSI assumption can be relaxed to cover genuinely non-log-concave targets on nonconvex manifolds.
