## Problem

In the high-dimensional proportional regime where sample size n and feature dimension d both diverge at comparable rates, classical linear classifiers (e.g., logistic regression) are provably miscalibrated, yet no calibration method with rigorous guarantees exists for this setting. Moreover, among all possible calibrated predictors, it was unclear which one is optimal, and widely used methods like Platt scaling lacked any formal high-dimensional theory.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a trained linear weight estimator ŵ (from any regularized M-estimator), the known covariance matrix Σ (estimable from unlabeled data), and a small holdout set of size n_ho = α·n used only for sign estimation of ⟨w*, ŵ⟩_Σ. It outputs a calibrated binary probability in [0,1] computed as a Monte Carlo average of σ(α·s + β·z_j) over M Gaussian draws, where s = ŵ^T x / ‖ŵ‖_Σ and α, β are cos and sin of the estimated angle. No knowledge of the true weight w* or the signal prior is required at prediction time.

## Method

The core contribution is angular calibration: the predictor interpolates between the informative logit ŵ^T x and a noninformative Gaussian noise Z, with interpolation weights cos(θ*) and sin(θ*) determined by the angle θ* between ŵ and the true weight w*. This construction is exactly calibrated by the tower property of conditional expectation for jointly Gaussian vectors, and is uniquely Bregman-optimal (minimizing any Bregman divergence to the true label distribution among all predictors of the form F(ŵ^T x)) by the optimality of conditional expectation. The angle θ* is consistently estimated from data using Bellec's observable estimator for the inner-product magnitude plus a holdout-based sign estimator, requiring only O(1) holdout fraction. The paper further proves that Platt scaling (fitting σ(Au+B) on a holdout set) converges to the angular predictor under probit-type link functions, thereby inheriting both calibration and Bregman-optimality guarantees in high dimensions.

## Results

The angular predictor achieves the lowest Expected Calibration Error (ECE) across nearly all six semi-real datasets, reducing ECE by 60–90% relative to the uncalibrated baseline. On pretrained feature extractors, angular calibration yields ECE of 0.0199 (CIFAR-10), 0.0249 (20 Newsgroups), and 0.0132 (Tox21), compared to uncalibrated ECE of 0.1236, 0.1392, and 0.1389 respectively. On UCI benchmarks, ECE drops to 0.0296 (Communities & Crime), 0.0208 (Splice-junction), and 0.0267 (Madelon) from uncalibrated values of 0.1700, 0.1262, and 0.1473. The method requires only a small labeled holdout (n_ho = 100) for sign estimation, yet outperforms Platt scaling and isotonic regression even when those methods are given 5× more labeled holdout points (n_ho = 500).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares angular calibration against two classical post-hoc calibration methods: Platt scaling [70] and isotonic regression [90], each evaluated with 100 and 500 labeled holdout points. Angular calibration achieves the lowest ECE on 5 of 6 datasets, beating both Platt 100 and Iso 100 by a wide margin (e.g., 0.0199 vs. 0.0561/0.0484 on CIFAR-10; 0.0132 vs. 0.0236/0.0497 on Tox21). Against the stronger 500-point baselines, angular calibration still wins on 5 of 6 datasets (e.g., 0.0249 vs. 0.0679/0.0813 on 20 Newsgroups), with the sole exception being Communities & Crime where Platt 500 (0.0262) marginally edges out angular (0.0296). Notably, angular calibration requires only a small holdout for sign estimation (n_ho = 100) and does not need to fit a parametric or nonparametric mapping, yet it matches or exceeds the performance of methods given 5× more labeled calibration data. The theoretical contribution further shows that Platt scaling converges to the angular predictor as the holdout size grows, providing the first provable high-dimensional guarantee for Platt scaling's calibration and Bregman-optimality properties.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on two key threads: (1) the high-dimensional observable estimation literature (Bellec 2022, Javanmard & Montanari 2018, Celentano et al. 2023), which provides consistent estimators for the inner product ⟨w⋆, ŵ⟩_Σ in proportional asymptotics, and (2) the classical result (Banerjee et al. 2005) that conditional expectation uniquely minimizes any Bregman divergence. The paper departs from prior calibration work (Platt scaling, isotonic regression, temperature scaling) by providing the first provably calibrated predictor in the high-dimensional proportional regime without requiring knowledge of the true signal prior, and by establishing a clean geometric interpretation: the optimal calibration is an angular interpolation between the informative logit and Gaussian noise, with the interpolation weight determined by the angle between the estimator and the true weight.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that Platt scaling provably converges to the Bregman-optimal angular predictor in high dimensions gives the most widely used calibration method its first rigorous high-dimensional guarantee, which is a meaningful theoretical contribution. However, practical applicability is currently limited: the angle estimator (11) is unstable on real datasets (acknowledged by the authors), the theory requires Gaussian features (with universality only argued informally), and the setting is restricted to binary classification with a known covariance. Open questions include formalizing universality beyond Gaussian designs, extending to multi-index models (multi-class, two-layer nets), and developing stable angle estimators for non-Gaussian, high-dimensional real data.
