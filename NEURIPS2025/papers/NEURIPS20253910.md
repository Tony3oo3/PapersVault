## Problem

Robots operating under partial observability (e.g., a single wrist camera in a cluttered scene) must actively move to gather missing information, yet standard RL is too sample-inefficient for real-world deployment, imitation learning requires unnatural demonstrations, and sim-to-real transfer is unreliable for perception-dependent tasks. This paper addresses the gap by showing how to efficiently train active perception policies on real robots using privileged training-time sensors.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253910/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The policy consumes a wrist RGB image (84×84, encoded via frozen DINOv2 ViT-S14 with PCA to a 256×16 latent), a 6-timestep history of end-effector positions, and a 2D occupancy grid (XZ plane) marking where camera rays have previously passed. The privileged critic additionally ingests a segmentation mask and bounding box of the target object (from DINO-X/GroundedSAM). The policy outputs 6-DoF Cartesian and angular velocity commands in action chunks of length 5; in handoff tasks, a separate object detector triggers a switch to the π₀ generalist policy once the target is confirmed in view across two consecutive 5-step intervals.

## Method

AAWR extends Advantage Weighted Regression to POMDPs by giving the critic and value function privileged access to extra sensors (object positions, segmentation masks) while the policy only receives partial observations. The key theoretical result (Theorem 1) proves that the correct AWR objective for a POMDP, derived via the equivalent environment-agent-state MDP, necessarily uses the privileged advantage A(s,z,a)=Q(s,z,a)−V(s,z), and that the symmetric variant (using only agent state z) yields a different, incorrect optimum by Jensen's inequality. Privileged critics are trained with IQL's expectile regression (1-step TD for Q, asymmetric L₂ for V), and the policy is updated via weighted behavior cloning with weights exp(A/β). Training follows an offline-to-online paradigm: pre-training on suboptimal demonstrations, then online fine-tuning with symmetric 50/50 sampling from offline and online buffers. At deployment, all privileged sensors are removed and only the policy runs on partial observations.

## Results

On the simulated Active Perception Koch task, AAWR achieves 100% success by learning to scan the workspace, while the Distillation baseline plateaus at 80% and VIB collapses during evaluation. On the real-world Blind Pick task, online AAWR reaches 94% grasp and 89% pick success, compared to 71%/55% for online AWR and 47%/41% for BC. Across the four real active perception handoff tasks (Bookshelf-P, Bookshelf-D, Shelf-Cabinet, Complex), AAWR consistently achieves the best Search score (73–92%), highest or tied-highest Completion rate (40–50%), and fewest Steps (27–46), outperforming all baselines including the generalist π₀ policy and the VLM+π₀ hierarchical approach.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AAWR outperforms the symmetric AWR (no privileged info) on every metric across all tasks: on the handoff tasks, AAWR's Search score exceeds AWR by 10–40 points, and AWR's Completion is 0% on Bookshelf-P versus AAWR's 44.4%. Against BC, AAWR achieves roughly 2–3× higher Search scores and far fewer steps (e.g., 36.6 vs. 84.0 on Bookshelf-P). The generalist π₀ policy is the weakest active perception performer, with Search scores of only 10–67% and 230–280 steps, confirming its inability to handle partial observability without memory. The VLM+π₀ hierarchical baseline (Gemini-2.5 for planning + π₀ for execution) performs even worse than π₀ alone on most tasks, with 320–380 steps. The Exhaustive hand-coded controller achieves comparable or slightly higher Search on Bookshelf-D (96.0 vs. 81.3) but requires 3–7× more steps (105–297 vs. 27–46), making AAWR 2–8× more time-efficient. On the simulated AP Koch task, AAWR reaches 100% success while the Distillation baseline [39] plateaus at 80% (learning a local-maxima center-approach strategy) and the VIB baseline [52] collapses entirely during evaluation because privileged information is unavailable at test time.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Advantage Weighted Regression (AWR) to the partially observed setting by deriving a theoretically justified asymmetric variant (AAWR) where privileged sensors inform the critic but not the policy. It builds on the offline-to-online RL paradigm and IQL for training the privileged value functions, and departs from prior privileged-information approaches (distillation, variational information bottleneck) by showing that a simple weighted behavior-cloning objective with privileged advantage estimates is both theoretically correct and empirically superior. The work also positions itself against information-theoretic active perception methods as task-agnostic and against imitation learning as bounded by demonstrator quality, arguing for a task-centric RL approach that is practical on real robots without heavy instrumentation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, minimal-instrumentation recipe for training active perception policies on real robots, validated across 8 tasks on 3 robot platforms, and demonstrates that state-of-the-art generalist VLA policies (π₀) fundamentally fail at search tasks due to lack of memory — a practically important finding for the robotics community. The clean theoretical result (Theorem 1) showing that symmetric AWR is provably incorrect for POMDPs gives practitioners a principled reason to adopt asymmetric training. Open questions include whether AAWR can directly fine-tune generalist policies rather than using a switching framework, whether representation learning can automatically select useful privileged features, and whether the approach scales to longer-horizon tasks where information-gathering challenges compound.
