## Problem

Existing embodied video benchmarks (e.g., ScanQA, OpenEQA, ECBench) primarily evaluate static scene exploration—object appearance and spatial attributes—while neglecting the dynamic, interaction-driven changes that occur in egocentric operational environments like kitchens and laboratories. No prior benchmark systematically assesses whether MLLMs can identify, recall, and forecast objects across temporal dimensions (past, present, future) in cluttered first-person video where objects are frequently occluded, rearranged, and state-changing.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254489/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

Each QA pair is grounded in a 3–10 minute egocentric video clip (sourced from EPIC-KITCHENS, Ego4D, Charades-ego, MECCANO, and self-recorded footage) and a text question accompanied by a visual object prompt—point, bounding box, or segmentation mask—annotated on the final frame to disambiguate among similar objects. Answers come in four formats: True/False, Single-Choice, Multiple-Choice, and Open-Ended (free-form timestamps). Closed-format questions are scored by exact-match accuracy, while open-ended temporal questions are scored with the proposed Multi-Scale Temporal Accuracy (MSTA) metric, which averages binary correctness across four relative error thresholds (1%, 10%, 20%, 30%) calibrated from human error analysis.

## Method

EOC-Bench is a 3,277-QA-pair benchmark organized into three temporal dimensions—Past (Object State/Location Retrospection, Relationship Evolution, Absolute Time Perception), Present (Immediate State Recognition, Object Relationship, Purpose/Function Inference, Anomaly Perception), and Future (Trajectory/Motion, State Change, Dynamic Relationship Prediction)—covering 11 fine-grained cognitive abilities and 728 object categories. A mixed-format human-in-the-loop annotation pipeline with 10 trained annotators, followed by cross-checking and verification (185 prompts re-annotated, 34 discarded, 76 QAs collaboratively resolved), ensures quality. Visual object prompts (point, box, mask) replace ambiguous text descriptions to provide persistent, unambiguous object references in dynamic scenes. The paper introduces MSTA, a scale-adaptive temporal accuracy metric derived from quantile analysis of human timestamp errors, to evaluate open-ended temporal perception. Evaluation is conducted zero-shot across 21 proprietary, open-source, and object-level MLLMs, revealing that even GPT-4o (61.83%) trails human performance (94.63%) substantially, with the largest gaps in Absolute Time Perception and Object Relationship Evolution.

## Results

On EOC-Bench (3,277 QA pairs across 11 cognitive dimensions), the best proprietary model GPT-4o* achieves 61.83% mean accuracy, while the best open-source model InternVL2.5-78B reaches 52.33%, and human performance is 94.63%. The Past dimension is the most challenging for all models: even GPT-4o* scores only 54.91% on Past (vs. 67.32% on Present and 69.11% on Future), with Absolute Time Perception (ATP) being the weakest subtask at just 34.46%. Object-level MLLMs (Osprey-7B, SPHINX-V-13B, ViP-LLaVA-13B) perform near or below random (27–33% mean), indicating that image-only input severely limits temporal reasoning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPT-4o* (61.83%) leads all evaluated models, outperforming the best open-source model InternVL2.5-78B (52.33%) by 9.5 points and Gemini-2.0-flash* (57.38%) by 4.5 points. All models show a consistent pattern of degrading performance on the Past dimension relative to Present and Future, with ATP being the single weakest subtask across the board (GPT-4o*: 34.46%, InternVL2.5-78B: 12.01%, random: 10.92%). Object-level MLLMs designed for visual prompting (Osprey-7B: 27.36%, SPHINX-V-13B: 29.21%, ViP-LLaVA-13B: 32.82%) perform at or near random level because they use only the last frame (1f input), confirming that temporal context is essential. Among open-source models, scaling from 7B to 72B yields modest gains (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL: 43.13%→49.87%; LLaVA-Video: 41.82%→49.59%), but none close the gap to proprietary models. The 32.7-point gap between the best model (GPT-4o*) and human performance (94.63%) highlights that embodied object cognition in dynamic egocentric settings remains far from solved.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of embodied video QA and object-centric temporal reasoning. Prior embodied benchmarks (OpenEQA, VSI-Bench, ECBench) evaluate MLLMs on static indoor scene exploration with text-based object referencing, while general video QA benchmarks (MVBench, Video-MME) target third-person YouTube content. EOC-Bench departs from both by focusing on dynamic egocentric operational interactions (kitchens, labs, workshops) and introducing persistent visual object prompts (point, box, mask) to overcome the ambiguity of textual descriptions in cluttered, rearranging scenes. It also extends the evaluation axis from spatial/appearance attributes to a three-dimensional temporal framework (Past/Present/Future) with a novel multi-scale temporal accuracy metric (MSTA) for open-ended timestamp questions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals a critical and previously underappreciated gap: even GPT-4o achieves only 34.46% on absolute time perception versus 74.30% for humans, and 9 of 21 evaluated models score below random on open-ended temporal questions. This suggests that current MLLMs lack reliable temporal memory and timestamp awareness—capabilities essential for any deployed embodied assistant that must recall what happened, track object states, and anticipate hazards. Open questions include whether longer context windows or dedicated temporal encoders can close this gap, whether the visual prompting paradigm generalizes to 3D/AR settings, and how to scale the benchmark beyond the current 656 videos to cover more diverse operational domains.
