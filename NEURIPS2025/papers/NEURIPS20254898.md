## Problem

Existing jailbreak attacks face a fundamental trade-off: optimization-based methods (GCG, AutoDAN) produce unnatural, easily detectable prompts and require expensive discrete token search, while black-box methods (PAIR, TAP) are slow and lack adaptability. No prior framework simultaneously achieves fully black-box operation, high human readability, fast inference, and adaptability to diverse target LLMs and prompts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254898/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

GASP takes a single harmful instruction (e.g., 'How to build a bomb?') as input and outputs a natural-language adversarial suffix string that, when appended to the instruction, elicits a harmful completion from the target LLM. The target LLM is accessed exclusively through API queries (black-box), and the generated suffix is constrained to remain linguistically coherent and contextually relevant to the original prompt, making it resistant to perplexity-based detection filters.

## Method

GASP learns a SuffixLLM generator through a three-stage pipeline: (1) SFT pretraining on the AdvSuffixes dataset (519 harmful prompts paired with ~11,763 generic adversarial suffixes) to establish a meaningful latent space; (2) Latent Bayesian Optimization (LBO) that projects candidate suffixes into SuffixLLM's continuous embedding space and uses a Gaussian Process surrogate with an acquisition function to efficiently identify high-potential suffixes, scored by GASPEval (21 binary harmfulness criteria rated 0–2); (3) iterative fine-tuning with ORPO (odds-ratio preference optimization) that reinforces LBO-discovered high-scoring suffixes as preferred over lower-scoring ones. The key insight is replacing combinatorial discrete token search with structured exploration of a learned continuous latent space, reducing training time by 1.75× over AdvPrompter while producing suffixes with 0.94 AI-rated readability and 79% human-rated ease of understanding.

## Results

Using the paper's GASPEval evaluator, GASP achieves ASR@10/ASR@1 of 100/86% on Falcon-7B, 82/64% on Mistral-7B-v0.3, 68/11% on LLaMA-3.1-8B, 71/6% on LLaMA-3-8B, and 64/9% on LLaMA-2-7B. Under the StrongREJECT evaluator, GASP reaches 99/53% on Falcon-7B and 84/56% on Mistral-7B. GASP is the only method reporting both ASR@10 and ASR@1 across all five target LLMs, and it reduces training time by 1.75× compared to AdvPrompter while achieving a human-readability score of 0.94 (out of 1.0).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GASP outperforms all six baselines across nearly every target LLM and evaluator. Against the closest generative competitor AdvPrompter, GASP gains +5/+12 points (ASR@10/ASR@1) on Mistral-7B, +7/+34 on Falcon-7B, and a dramatic +51/+7 on LLaMA-3.1-8B (68/11 vs. 17/4). Against discrete-optimization methods GCG and AutoDAN, GASP achieves substantially higher ASR@1 on Falcon-7B (86% vs. 52% and 42%) and is the only method to report ASR@10 at all. Among black-box baselines, GASP surpasses TAP (which reaches 98% ASR@1 on Falcon-7B but only 61% on Mistral) and PAIR (91% on Falcon, 64% on Mistral) on Mistral-7B (64% vs. 61%/64%) and LLaMA-3.1-8B (11% vs. 25%/18% ASR@1). Notably, GASP is the only method that maintains competitive ASR across all five target LLMs simultaneously, whereas ICA collapses to 0% on LLaMA-2-7B and AdvPrompter drops to 5/0 on LLaMA-3-8B.

## Relation to Prior Work

GASP extends the AdvPrompter [56] framework for learning a generative suffix model, but replaces its reliance on discrete token-space optimization (inherited from AutoDAN) with Latent Bayesian Optimization over continuous embedding vectors, enabling query-efficient search in a fully black-box setting. The iterative refinement loop borrows ORPO [28] for preference-based fine-tuning, a combination not previously applied to adversarial suffix generation. The paper also introduces GASPEval, a 21-criteria LLM-based evaluator that serves dual roles as both a search guide for LBO and a standalone attack-success metric, departing from simpler keyword-matching or single-judge approaches used by prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GASP offers a practical efficiency win for red-teaming: 1.75× faster training than AdvPrompter and significantly faster inference than discrete-optimization baselines, while producing human-readable suffixes that resist perplexity-based detection. The paper's open questions—whether current ASR is near an optimal ceiling, how to further reduce query complexity, and whether the generative suffix pipeline can be repurposed for adversarial retraining to strengthen defenses—point toward a broader role for latent-space search in both attack and defense. A key limitation is that the method still requires a pre-trained SuffixLLM and a secondary LLM for GASPEval scoring, adding infrastructure overhead that may constrain deployment in resource-limited red-teaming scenarios.
