## Problem

Existing vision-language models for chest X-ray report generation prioritize factual accuracy but fail to explicitly model the two distinct types of diagnostic uncertainty radiologists use—structural uncertainty in individual findings and semantic uncertainty in overall impressions—and provide no transparent reasoning pathway connecting visual observations to clinical conclusions. This lack of uncertainty expression and reasoning transparency is a critical barrier to clinical trust and adoption of AI-generated radiology reports.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253133/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a chest X-ray image and a text prompt as input and produces a structured tripartite report with three XML-tagged sections: <findings> (anatomical observations with bounding boxes, labels, and uncertainty-qualified descriptions), <thinking> (intermediate reasoning steps linking findings to diagnosis, including differential considerations), and <impression> (concise clinical summary with appropriate uncertainty language). The findings section is serialized as structured token sequences encoding spatial and linguistic uncertainty per finding, while the thinking section is free-form reasoning text that must remain entity-coherent with the other two sections.

## Method

CURV employs a three-stage training pipeline on Qwen-2.5-VL-3B: (1) uncertainty-aware SFT on 112,111 samples from Imagenome/MIMIC-CXR where the model learns to output bounding boxes, anatomical labels, and uncertainty-qualified text for each finding; (2) reasoning initialization SFT on the novel TRACE-CXR dataset (2,000 reports with LLM-generated thinking sections) to establish the tripartite report structure; and (3) GRPO-based reinforcement learning on MIMIC-CXR where only ground-truth findings and impression are provided (deliberately omitting the thinking section to avoid imitation of potentially flawed reasoning). The RL stage uses a multi-component reward combining binary format adherence, RadGraph entity F1 with uncertainty-term alignment scoring, and a Jaccard-similarity coherence reward that measures entity overlap between the generated thinking section and the union of findings/impression entities, forcing the model to discover genuinely coherent reasoning rather than template-mimicking.

## Results

On MIMIC-CXR, CURV (3B parameters) achieves the best generation quality with BLEU-3 of 9.85, METEOR of 30.43, and ROUGE-L of 31.19, outperforming the strongest baseline MAIRA-2 (BLEU-3: 9.01, METEOR: 26.78, ROUGE-L: 28.65). On clinical accuracy, CURV reaches CheXbert Micro F1 of 57.12 and RadGraph F1 of 19.54, surpassing MAIRA-2 (46.53 and 17.05) and significantly exceeding Gemini 2.5 Pro (48.45 and 7.71). On the out-of-distribution IU X-ray dataset, CURV further extends its lead with BLEU-3 of 12.08, METEOR of 38.30, and RadGraph F1 of 25.65.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CURV outperforms all four baselines on the majority of metrics. Against the strongest baseline MAIRA-2, CURV leads on every generation metric (e.g., METEOR 30.43 vs. 26.78, ROUGE-L 31.19 vs. 28.65) and on CheXbert Micro F1 (57.12 vs. 46.53) and RadGraph F1 (19.54 vs. 17.05), though MAIRA-2 retains a slight edge on CheXbert Macro F1 (6.34 vs. 5.22). Compared to the general-purpose Gemini 2.5 Pro, CURV achieves a dramatic RadGraph F1 improvement (19.54 vs. 7.71) and higher CheXbert accuracy (76.93 vs. 74.35), demonstrating the value of task-specific training over generalist VLMs. Against the base Qwen2.5-VL-3B model, CURV more than doubles RadGraph F1 (19.54 vs. 4.66) and boosts METEOR from 20.81 to 30.43, confirming the effectiveness of the three-stage training pipeline. CURV also surpasses HuatuoGPT-Vision-7B on all generation metrics (e.g., BLEU-3: 9.85 vs. 4.64) and RadGraph F1 (19.54 vs. 9.06), and beats LLaVA-1.5-7B-SFT-CXR on METEOR (30.43 vs. 25.71) and RadGraph F1 (19.54 vs. 13.06). Notably, CURV achieves these gains with only 3B parameters, outperforming all 7B baselines without additional computational overhead.

## Relation to Prior Work

CURV sits at the intersection of three research threads: VLM-based radiology report generation (MAIRA-2, LLaVA-1.5, HuatuoGPT-Vision), uncertainty modeling in medical imaging (Monte Carlo dropout, Bayesian NNs, variational topic models), and RL-driven reasoning in vision-language models (DeepSeek-R1, Vision-R1, BoxMed-RL). It departs from prior uncertainty work by reframing the problem from estimating internal statistical confidence to modeling the *linguistic expression* of diagnostic uncertainty, explicitly distinguishing structural (finding-level) from semantic (impression-level) uncertainty. It extends the SFT-then-RL paradigm from DeepSeek-R1 and Vision-R1 by introducing a multi-component reward that jointly optimizes format adherence, entity/uncertainty accuracy, and reasoning coherence, and by using a purpose-built dataset (TRACE-CXR) to initialize the reasoning pathway before RL refines it.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine clinical gap: radiologists rely on hedging language and transparent reasoning to communicate diagnostic confidence, yet current VLMs produce reports that are factually oriented but lack these elements. The TRACE-CXR dataset and the coherence reward design offer reusable tools for the medical-AI community, and the demonstration that a 3B model can outperform 7B specialized baselines suggests the approach is compute-efficient. However, significant open questions remain: the reasoning traces are LLM-generated (Grok-3) rather than human-authored, no statistical significance testing is reported, generalization beyond CXR is untested, and large-scale clinical validation by practicing radiologists is explicitly deferred to future work.
