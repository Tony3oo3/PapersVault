## Problem

Existing generative reward models (GenRMs) rely on shallow, vertically scaled reasoning—generating multiple short CoT paths and aggregating by majority vote—which fails on nuanced or reasoning-heavy tasks requiring deep analysis. Additionally, their pairwise preference outputs are incompatible with standard RLHF algorithms that expect pointwise scalar rewards, forcing a lossy conversion step. The paper asks whether extending reasoning along the horizontal dimension (longer single trajectories) can overcome these limitations and whether pairwise comparisons can drive policy optimization directly.

## Input / Output

The model receives a triplet (x, y_a, y_b) consisting of a prompt context and two candidate responses, along with a task instruction τ provided as a system message that specifies six evaluation criteria (helpfulness, correctness, coherence, complexity, verbosity, safety). It produces a self-guided, flexible CoT reasoning trace R spanning thousands of tokens (versus the few hundred in prior GenRMs), followed by a final preference verdict s encoded as either a binary choice {a, b} or a multiclass strength score in {-3, -2, -1, 1, 2, 3}. The reasoning trace is unstructured and internally generated rather than following externally imposed templates, enabling self-reflection, hypothetical reasoning, and divergent reasoning patterns.

## Method

Think-RM trains a non-reasoning LLM (Llama-3.1-8B or Qwen2.5-3B) through two stages: (1) SFT warm-up on long CoT trajectories generated by QwQ-32B, where for each preference instance the longest correct trajectory among M=10 samples is selected to instill deep reasoning patterns; and (2) rule-based RL using GRPO with an accuracy-only reward (1.0 for exact match, 0.5 for correct direction in multiclass, 0.0 otherwise) to prune verbose reasoning while preserving effectiveness. The key architectural insight is that horizontal scaling—extending a single reasoning trajectory from hundreds to thousands of tokens—outperforms vertical scaling (multiple short paths with majority voting) on reasoning-intensive tasks. The paper further introduces a pairwise RLHF pipeline that constructs a skew-symmetric preference strength matrix D from GenRM evaluations and derives GRPO advantage estimates directly from pairwise comparisons, eliminating the need for pointwise reward conversion; for binary outputs, reasoning length |R| serves as a confidence measure so that longer (more ambiguous) chains yield smaller preference strengths.

## Results

On the OOD RewardBench, binary Think-RM (SFT+RL) achieves 86.35% average preference accuracy, with 77.85% on Chat Hard and 85.23% on Reasoning. On the more challenging RM-Bench, it reaches 75.06% average with 72.25% on the Math domain. On the in-distribution HelpSteer2-Preference validation set, it attains 81.53% accuracy. These results represent improvements of over 10% on RewardBench Chat Hard, over 5% on RewardBench Reasoning, and 12% on RM-Bench Math compared to the strongest CoT-GenRM baselines with vertical inference-time scaling (m=16).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Bradley-Terry reward model (Christiano et al., 2017), Think-RM (SFT+RL) wins decisively across all benchmarks: 86.35% vs. 78.07% on RewardBench binary (+8.28 pp), 75.06% vs. 68.27% on RM-Bench binary (+6.79 pp), and 81.53% vs. 75.57% on HelpSteer2-Preference ID (+5.96 pp). Against CoT-GenRM (Mahan et al., 2024), Think-RM outperforms even the vertically scaled variant (m=16 majority voting) on reasoning-heavy subcategories: 77.85% vs. 65.57% on RewardBench Chat Hard (+12.28 pp), 85.23% vs. 79.33% on RewardBench Reasoning (+5.90 pp), and 72.25% vs. 60.42% on RM-Bench Math (+11.83 pp). Notably, Think-RM achieves these gains with a single long-horizon reasoning trajectory (~1,423 tokens) rather than 16 independent short paths (~6,767 tokens total), making it substantially more inference-efficient. On the in-distribution HelpSteer2-Preference task, binary Think-RM (SFT+RL) also surpasses CoT-GenRM (ground truth) with vertical scaling (81.53% vs. 80.11%), confirming that depth-oriented reasoning generalizes well even within the training distribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the generative reward model (GenRM) paradigm established by Mahan et al. [22], which trains LLMs to produce chain-of-thought rationales followed by preference verdicts. The key departure is shifting from vertical inference-time scaling (multiple short CoT paths aggregated by majority voting) to horizontal scaling, where a single trajectory is extended to thousands of tokens through an internal thinking process. The training recipe adapts GRPO from DeepSeek-R1 [27] to the reward modeling domain, using SFT on long CoT data for warm-up followed by rule-based RL for refinement. Additionally, the proposed pairwise RLHF pipeline departs from the standard pointwise reward formulation by directly optimizing policies from pairwise preference comparisons, eliminating the need for scalar reward conversion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete limitation in current GenRMs—shallow reasoning paths that fail on reasoning-heavy tasks like math, code, and multi-turn dependency tracking—and shows that long-horizon internal thinking substantially closes this gap (12% on RM-Bench Math, 10%+ on RewardBench Chat Hard). The pairwise RLHF pipeline offers a practical alternative to pointwise RLHF that may reduce reward hacking by preserving richer preference structure. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger models, whether calibrated continuous preference scores (rather than discrete outputs) would further improve policy optimization, and whether adaptive reasoning budgets could reduce inference cost without sacrificing accuracy. The absence of error bars and the restriction to 3B/8B models limit confidence in the generalizability of the reported margins.
