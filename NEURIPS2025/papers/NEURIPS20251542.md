## Problem

Subset selection for data sampling must balance the utility of chosen items against their diversity, but existing formulations either use max-sum diversity (Borodin et al.) or treat utility and diversity separately. This paper introduces the max-min diversification with monotone submodular utility (MDMS) problem, where the objective f(S) = g(S) + λ·div(S) is neither submodular nor monotone, and shows that the standard greedy algorithm provides no constant-factor approximation. The central question is whether one can design an efficient algorithm with provable approximation guarantees for this combined objective.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes n points in a metric space, a nonnegative monotone submodular function g, a diversity strength λ ≥ 0, and a cardinality budget k, and returns a subset S of size at most k maximizing f(S) = g(S) + λ·min_{u≠v∈S} dist(u,v). In the ImageNet experiment, points are 2048-dimensional unit-length embeddings from a ResNet-56 penultimate layer with cosine distance, and g is a budget-additive submodular function combining margin-based uncertainty scores with pairwise cosine similarities over a Δ=100 nearest-neighbor graph. The max-min diversity term makes the objective non-monotone, so the algorithm must evaluate multiple candidate subsets and return the best one rather than a single greedy trajectory.

## Method

GIST (Greedy Independent Set Thresholding) sweeps over a geometric sequence of distance thresholds d and, for each, runs a GreedyIndependentSet subroutine on the intersection graph G_d(V) that iteratively adds the point with the highest marginal submodular gain while enforcing dist(v, S) ≥ d. The key analytical ingredient is a bicriteria approximation lemma: when the threshold d' < d/2, the greedy independent set achieves at least half the submodular value of the optimal set with diversity ≥ d, yielding a 1/2 − ε approximation for general submodular utility and a stronger 2/3 − ε guarantee for linear utility. The algorithm also considers two degenerate thresholds (d=0 for pure greedy submodular maximization and d=d_max for the diameter pair) to handle the case where the optimal diversity is small. A matching hardness result shows that no polynomial-time algorithm can approximate MDMS within a factor of 0.5584 for general submodular utility, and the 2/3 ratio is tight for linear utility.

## Results

On ImageNet single-shot data sampling, GIST-submod achieves the best top-1 accuracy at smaller budgets: 67.24% at k=30%, 70.76% at k=40%, 73.15% at k=50%, and 74.30% at k=60% of the ~1.3M examples. At larger budgets (k≥70%), GIST-margin takes over as the best method, reaching 75.41% at k=70%, 75.96% at k=80%, and 76.12% at k=90%. Both GIST variants consistently outperform their non-GIST counterparts (margin and submod), demonstrating the benefit of the max-min diversity term div(S).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GIST consistently outperforms all four baselines (random, margin, k-center, submod) across every cardinality constraint tested. Compared to margin sampling (Roth & Small, ECML 2006), GIST-margin improves top-1 accuracy by 0.93 points at k=30% (66.90% vs. 65.97%) and by 1.01 points at k=90% (76.12% vs. 75.11%), showing that adding the max-min diversity term to the same linear utility yields more representative subsets. Against k-center (Sener & Savarese, ICLR 2018), GIST-submod leads by 0.53 points at k=30% (67.24% vs. 66.71%) and GIST-margin leads by 1.16 points at k=90% (76.12% vs. 75.00%). The paper also notes a crossover around k=60–70% where the best GIST variant switches from submodular utility to margin (linear) utility, suggesting that the relative importance of pairwise diversity in the submodular term diminishes as the selected set grows larger.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper introduces the MDMS problem, which combines a monotone submodular utility with a max-min diversity term, departing from the closest prior work of Borodin et al. [13] that pairs submodular utility with a max-sum (sum-of-pairwise-distances) diversity objective. The GIST algorithm draws on the greedy 2-approximation for metric k-center, adapting it into a bicriteria GreedyIndependentSet subroutine that operates on intersection graphs at varying distance thresholds. The hardness analysis builds on set-cover hardness instances from Feige et al. and Kapralov et al., as well as max-clique inapproximability results from Håstad and Zuckerman for the linear-utility case.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes a clean theoretical framework for a practically relevant data-sampling objective, with a 1/2-approximation algorithm and a 0.5584 hardness bound leaving a meaningful open gap. For linear utilities, the 2/3 approximation is tight, fully characterizing the problem in that special case. Empirically, GIST yields modest but consistent improvements (0.1–0.5% top-1 accuracy) over margin and k-center baselines on ImageNet, and the demonstration that the standard greedy algorithm fails to provide any constant-factor guarantee for this non-monotone objective is a useful caution for practitioners. The main limitation is that the practical gains are small and the theoretical gap remains open; broader applicability to other submodular utilities or larger-scale settings is not yet explored.
