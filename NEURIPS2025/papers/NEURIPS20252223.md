## Problem

PEFT methods (LoRA, prefix tuning, prompt tuning) are fast and memory-efficient but lack full-model capacity for harder adaptations, while zeroth-order (ZO) full-model fine-tuning achieves better generalization but suffers from slow convergence and extreme sensitivity to prompt selection. The paper asks whether these two paradigms can be smoothly integrated into a single bilevel framework that mutually enhances each other—reducing ZO's prompt sensitivity and accelerating convergence while preserving PEFT's speed and memory footprint.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252223/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes text inputs for downstream classification or multiple-choice tasks, with the training set split into an upper-level subset (for ZO backbone updates) and a lower-level subset (for FO-PEFT adaptation). At inference, the model takes a single text input and produces a task prediction (classification label or answer); during meta-learning evaluation, it operates zero-shot without demonstration examples, unlike ICL-based baselines that require k=4 in-context examples. The output is a pair of fine-tuned parameters: the full backbone θ (updated via ZO) and a small PEFT module p (updated via FO), which together define the adapted model.

## Method

Bilevel-ZOFO formulates LLM fine-tuning as a bilevel optimization problem where the inner level solves for optimal PEFT parameters p given the current backbone θ, and the outer level updates θ to maximize downstream performance. Following Lu and Mei's penalty method, the bilevel problem is reformulated as a minimax problem G_λ(θ,p,s), solved with T inner SGD steps on the PEFT parameters (using exact first-order gradients) between each pair of outer ZO updates. The outer-level gradient with respect to θ is estimated via SPSA (simultaneous perturbation stochastic approximation), requiring only two forward passes per step and avoiding backpropagation entirely. The key mechanism is that the inner FO-PEFT loop stabilizes the loss landscape seen by the ZO estimator, reducing variance and prompt sensitivity, while the outer ZO updates inject full-model capacity that PEFT alone cannot provide. The paper proves an O(ε^{-2}) convergence rate under a low-effective-rank assumption that eliminates dependence on the backbone dimensionality d.

## Results

On OPT-1.3B across 9 classification/multiple-choice tasks (1000 training samples), Bilevel-ZOFO achieves an average accuracy of 0.7158, outperforming FO-PEFT (0.6977) and MeZO (0.6529). On LLaMA-2-7B, Bilevel-ZOFO similarly surpasses both baselines, with the advantage extending to larger models. The method achieves 2–4× faster time-to-target accuracy compared to MeZO while maintaining comparable peak memory (≈12 GB for GPT-2-Large meta-learning vs. 32 GB for MetaICL). In meta-learning on CROSSFIT/UNIFIEDQA with GPT-2-Large, Bilevel-ZOFO (tuned) reaches 47.1/42.4/43.5/41.9 macro-F1 across four hyper-task settings, surpassing MetaICL (46.4/37.7/45.5/40.2) on most settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MeZO (full-model ZO fine-tuning), Bilevel-ZOFO wins by a substantial margin: 0.7158 vs. 0.6529 average accuracy on OPT-1.3B (+6.3 pp), and it reaches comparable accuracy in roughly half the training steps (2–4× faster time-to-target). Against FO-PEFT baselines (LoRA, prefix-tuning, prompt-tuning), Bilevel-ZOFO wins on average (0.7158 vs. 0.6977) and on most individual tasks, with the largest gaps on WiC (+6.0 pp) and CB (+4.4 pp over FO prefix). The method also dramatically reduces prompt sensitivity: on SST-2, MeZO drops 38.6 pp when the task prompt is removed, while Bilevel-ZOFO drops only 0.4 pp. In the meta-learning setting (GPT-2-Large, CROSSFIT/UNIFIEDQA), Bilevel-ZOFO (tuned) surpasses MetaICL on 3 of 4 hyper-task settings (e.g., 47.1 vs. 46.4 on class→class, 42.4 vs. 37.7 on non-class→class) while using 12 GB peak memory vs. 32 GB and 0.19 s/step vs. 0.48 s/step. The only setting where MetaICL retains an edge is QA→QA (45.5 vs. 43.5), though Bilevel-ZOFO still outperforms zero-shot and ICL baselines across all four settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two recent lines of work: zeroth-order LLM fine-tuning (MeZO, Malladi et al. 2023) and first-order penalty-based bilevel optimization (Lu and Mei, SIAM J. Optim. 2024). It departs from both by replacing the expensive first-order backbone gradients in the bilevel framework with SPSA-based ZO estimates, while retaining exact first-order gradients for the small PEFT parameters at the inner level. Unlike prior bilevel methods that require Hessian-vector products or full backpropagation for the upper level, and unlike pure ZO or pure PEFT approaches that each suffer from slow convergence or limited capacity respectively, Bilevel-ZOFO couples the two in a penalty-based minimax formulation that avoids second-order information entirely.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method offers a practically meaningful 2–4× speedup over MeZO at comparable memory while outperforming both ZO and FO-PEFT baselines, making full-model fine-tuning more accessible in resource-constrained settings. The dramatic reduction in prompt sensitivity (e.g., SST-2 drops only 0.4% without a task prompt vs. 38.6% for MeZO) addresses a key practical pain point for ZO practitioners. However, experiments are limited to models up to 7B parameters, and the bilevel structure adds implementation complexity (penalty schedule, inner/outer learning rates, data splitting) that may limit adoption. Open questions include whether the approach scales to 70B+ models, whether the ZO inner-loop variance reduction generalizes to other PEFT variants beyond LoRA/prefix/prompt tuning, and whether the meta-learning gains persist when the base model is substantially larger.
