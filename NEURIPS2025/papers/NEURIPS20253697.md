## Problem

LASER demonstrated that targeted rank-reduction of LLM weight matrices can improve downstream accuracy without any gradient-based fine-tuning, but its exhaustive per-matrix search—requiring full-dataset forward passes for every matrix in every layer—makes it impractical for rapid or on-device deployment. This paper asks whether that search overhead can be eliminated while preserving or even improving accuracy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253697/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained LLM (GPT-J 6B or RoBERTa) and exactly 100 prompt-response pairs from the target dataset, using them for both gradient computation and final evaluation. It outputs a modified LLM in which a small set of identified weight matrices have been replaced with block-wise low-rank approximations; no gradient-based weight updates are ever performed. The 100 examples serve a dual role: they drive the singular-value gradient scoring that selects which matrices to compress, and they act as the evaluation set for choosing the best compression hyperparameters (rank, number of blocks, layer).

## Method

A single backward pass on 100 examples yields the loss gradient G for every weight matrix; the gradient of each singular value σᵢ is then computed as uᵢᵀGvᵢ (the Frobenius inner product of G with the corresponding rank-one SVD component), and matrices whose trailing singular-value gradients are most negative are flagged for compression. Each selected matrix is split into K consecutive row blocks—a near-zero-cost proxy for projective clustering—and each block is independently thin-SVD-decomposed and truncated to rank j before the compressed blocks are stacked back into the original matrix. The multi-subspace factorization enlarges the solution landscape with additional local minima, reducing overfitting noise that a single global SVD would either retain or over-prune. Both the gradient scoring and the accuracy evaluation use only the same 100 examples, justified by the finding that adaptation is dominated by prompting style rather than dataset size, yielding up to 52× speedup over LASER while matching or exceeding its accuracy.

## Results

On GPT-J across eight benchmarks, the full pipeline (Clustering LASER + 100 Gradients + 100-Point Evaluation) achieves an average accuracy improvement of 9.19% over the baseline, surpassing LASER's 8.24% by +0.95 points while delivering a 52× speedup. The most dramatic gain is on BigBench-Epistemic Reasoning, where multi-subspace factorization lifts accuracy from 38.3% (LASER) to 62.2%—a 24-point jump. On RoBERTa, the same pipeline achieves a 22.2× speedup with a near-matched average improvement (2.91% vs. LASER's 3.31%, a −0.40-point gap).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is LASER (Sharma et al., ICLR 2024), which performs exhaustive per-matrix rank reduction with full-dataset forward passes. On GPT-J, the proposed method outperforms LASER on average by +0.95 accuracy points (9.19% vs. 8.24% improvement over baseline) while being 52× faster, with the largest single-dataset win on BigBench-Epistemic Reasoning (+23.9 points: 62.2% vs. 38.3%). However, the method loses to LASER on Bios Gender (88.4% vs. 97.5%) and FEVER (53.3% vs. 56.2%), suggesting the gradient-guided top-5 matrix selection occasionally misses the optimal layer for those tasks. On RoBERTa, the speedup is 22.2× but the average accuracy is slightly below LASER (−0.40 points), with the clustering component providing less benefit for the smaller model. Overall, the paper demonstrates that the efficiency gains (52× and 22× speedups) come at negligible or no accuracy cost on average, with the multi-subspace factorization being the primary source of the accuracy improvements over LASER.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper is a direct efficiency-focused revision of LASER (Sharma et al., ICLR 2024), which demonstrated that pruning high-order SVD components of selected weight matrices improves downstream accuracy without any gradient-based fine-tuning. It incorporates the multi-subspace/projective-clustering perspective from Maalouf et al. (ICLR 2020) and Liebenwein et al. (NeurIPS 2021), but repurposes it not for parameter reduction per se, but for denoising overfitting directions within each cluster. The key conceptual departure from LASER is replacing the exhaustive per-matrix forward-pass sweep with a single backward pass whose singular-value gradients rank candidate matrices, and replacing full-dataset evaluation with 100-sample evaluation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is a 52× speedup over LASER while matching or modestly exceeding its accuracy, making training-free LLM adaptation feasible on a single GPU in minutes rather than hours. The finding that ~100 prompt-response pairs suffice for both gradient scoring and accuracy evaluation suggests that downstream adaptation is driven by formatting/style cues rather than dataset breadth, which has implications for low-resource and on-device deployment. However, the method is validated only on two small English models (GPT-J 6B, RoBERTa-base), inherits LASER's limitation of searching a finite candidate set without gradient descent, and shows inconsistent gains across datasets (e.g., Bios Gender drops from 97.5% to 88.4%). Open questions include whether the 100-sample sufficiency and multi-subspace gains hold for larger, multilingual, or retrieval-augmented models.
