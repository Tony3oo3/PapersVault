## Problem

Diffusion models produce state-of-the-art synthetic samples but are impractical for generative data augmentation (GDA) due to two coupled obstacles: their iterative sampling loop (hundreds to thousands of denoising steps) inflates per-image cost by orders of magnitude, and the unmeasured distributional gap between the generator and the true data distribution biases downstream classifier gradients. No existing pipeline simultaneously resolves the efficiency bottleneck and corrects the synthetic-to-real distributional shift in a principled way.

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes a pre-trained multi-step diffusion teacher (EDM at 32×32 for CIFAR-10, EDM2-XXL at 512×512 for ImageNet-1K), the original labeled training set, and random Gaussian noise vectors. It produces a one-step student generator that synthesizes class-conditional images in a single forward pass, a co-trained discriminator whose calibrated sigmoid outputs serve as per-sample importance weights (truncated at γ and self-normalized per mini-batch), and a reweighted classification loss for training downstream classifiers (ResNet-18/50, VGG-16, ViT-S/16). ImageNet outputs are downsampled to 224×224 before classification to match standard protocols.

## Method

DAR-GDA is a three-stage pipeline that intertwines score distillation, adversarial alignment, and importance reweighting. In the distillation stage, a multi-step teacher is compressed into a one-step student via a score-distillation loss (instantiated as CTM or SiDA), cutting per-sample generation cost by over 100×. Crucially, the distillation objective is augmented with an adversarial term: the student generator competes against real images through a discriminator that operates across multiple noise levels (not just clean images), directly minimizing the Jensen-Shannon divergence between the student and true data distributions. This adversarial training yields the discriminator as a concurrent byproduct, which—by the GAN optimal-discriminator identity—provides a likelihood-free estimate of the density ratio r(x) = p_data(x)/q_G(x). In the reweighting stage, these per-sample importance weights multiply the classification loss on synthetic samples, converting the biased synthetic-data empirical risk into an unbiased stochastic estimator of the true data risk; variance is controlled via weight truncation and batch-wise self-normalization. The theoretical analysis decomposes the true risk into a distributional-mismatch term (tightened by adversarial alignment via Pinsker's inequality) and a biased synthetic-risk term (corrected by reweighting), showing that DAR-GDA simultaneously reduces both error sources.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with dynamic augmentation, DAR-GDA using SiDA distillation achieves 96.73% top-1 accuracy with ResNet-18 and 95.73% with VGG-16, surpassing the real-data-only baseline (95.00%/93.76%) by +1.7pp and +1.9pp respectively. On ImageNet-1K with static augmentation, DAR-GDA (SiDA) reaches 78.03% top-1 with ResNet-50 and 81.17% with ViT-S/16, improving over the real-only baseline (76.37%/79.91%) by +1.7pp and +1.3pp. The full DAR pipeline (Distill + Adv. align + Reweight) consistently outperforms any single component, with the reweighting stage contributing an additional ~0.3–0.5pp on top of adversarial alignment alone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DAR-GDA (SiDA, full pipeline) outperforms all in-domain baselines on CIFAR-10: it beats the raw multi-step EDM diffusion model (96.16% → 96.73% ResNet-18, +0.57pp) and the standalone SiDA distillation without reweighting (96.40% → 96.73%, +0.33pp). Against web-pretrained text-to-image models, DAR-GDA surpasses SD+ActGen (95.92%) and GLIDE+real guidance (95.77%) by +0.81pp and +0.96pp on ResNet-18, despite using only in-domain training data. On ImageNet-1K, DAR-GDA (SiDA) achieves 78.03% top-1 with ResNet-50, slightly trailing SD+ActGen (78.34%) and Imagen+finetune (78.17%) by ~0.2–0.3pp, but matching SD+ActGen exactly on ViT-S/16 (81.17%) and outperforming Imagen+finetune (81.00%). Against the raw EDM2 diffusion teacher (77.12% ResNet-50), DAR-GDA gains +0.91pp, confirming that the adversarial alignment and reweighting stages correct the distributional bias of the undistilled generator. The GAN baseline R3GAN (95.61% ResNet-18 on CIFAR-10) is outperformed by DAR-GDA by +1.12pp, and distillation reduces per-image generation cost from 3102s (EDM) to 39.1s (SiDA), a ~80× speedup.

## Relation to Prior Work

DAR-GDA unifies three previously separate threads: score-based distillation of diffusion models (CTM, SiDA) into one-step generators, adversarial training that minimizes Jensen-Shannon divergence between generator and data distributions, and density-ratio reweighting derived from GAN discriminators. The key conceptual departure is that the discriminator learned during adversarial distillation serves a dual role—improving the student generator while simultaneously providing the calibrated density-ratio estimate needed for importance reweighting—eliminating the need for a separate discriminator training phase that prior diffusion-GAN hybrids required. Unlike foundation-model-based GDA (GLIDE, Stable Diffusion, Imagen fine-tuned to a domain), DAR-GDA operates entirely in-domain, learning the generator from the classifier's own training set, which sidesteps licensing concerns and information leakage from web-scale pre-training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a >100× reduction in per-sample generation cost makes diffusion-quality GDA economically viable, and the theoretical guarantee that reweighting yields an unbiased stochastic estimator of true data risk provides a principled correction to the distributional bias that plagues all synthetic-data training. Limitations include validation only on CIFAR-10 and ImageNet-1K (constrained by publicly available teacher checkpoints), reliance on a pre-trained diffusion teacher (one-time cost not addressed), and the full-data-substitution gap on ImageNet-1K remaining substantial. Open questions include extension to non-vision domains (protein, molecule generation), conditional and dense-prediction tasks, and whether the reweighting mechanism generalizes when the generator's mode coverage is weaker than what diffusion models provide.
