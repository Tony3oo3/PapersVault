## Problem

Solving stochastic optimal control problems via gradient-based optimization of path space measures is challenging when the target measure differs substantially from the prior, leading to high variance in importance weights, mode collapse, and poor convergence in high dimensions. Existing iterative diffusion optimization methods either directly minimize a divergence to the target or rely on hand-tuned annealing schedules, lacking a principled mechanism to control the step size in the measure transport.

## Input / Output

The method takes SDE coefficients (drift b, diffusion σ), an initial distribution p₀, and a target specification (unnormalized density, potential energy with initial/target sets A and B, or a reward function for a pretrained diffusion model) and produces a neural network parameterized optimal control u. A key operational detail is that each iteration consumes a replay buffer of full trajectories simulated under the previous control u_i, enabling off-policy optimization without new simulations; the trust region bound ε and the dual Lagrange multiplier λ_i are computed from this same buffer via a 1D nonlinear solver, adding minimal overhead.

## Method

The core idea is to replace the unconstrained SOC problem with a sequence of constrained subproblems that bound the reverse KL divergence between the current iterate's path measure and the previous one: D_KL(P^u | P^{u_i}) ≤ ε. The paper proves that the resulting intermediate measures define a geometric annealing from prior to target with step sizes that are equidistant in Fisher-Rao distance (up to higher-order terms), providing an automatic and principled annealing schedule. The Lagrangian of each constrained problem is shown to be itself an SOC problem with a modified quadratic control cost, and the optimal Lagrange multiplier is obtained by maximizing a 1D dual function estimated from buffered trajectories. For practical optimization, the authors adapt two loss functionals to the trust region setting: a log-variance divergence (TR-LV) and a cross-entropy-based SOC matching regression (TR-SOCM), both computed off-policy from the replay buffer, with the latter requiring only pointwise evaluations at random times rather than full-trajectory backpropagation.

## Results

On the 10-component Gaussian Mixture Model target, TR-LV achieves |Δ log Z| = 0.002±0.001 at d=200, while all non-trust-region baselines degrade severely (RE: 2.908, CE: 0.641, LV: 1.958, AM: 3.322, SOCM: 3.504). On the 32-mode Many Well target at d=200, TR-LV again achieves |Δ log Z| = 0.002±0.001, outperforming all baselines by orders of magnitude. For transition path sampling, TR-LV achieves a transition hit percentage (THP) of 61.25% on Alanine Dipeptide and 43.95% on Chignolin, substantially exceeding TPS-DPS (39.58% and 25.00% respectively). For Stable Diffusion 1.5 fine-tuning, TR-SOCM matches adjoint matching performance on ImageReward, CLIP-Score, and Human Preference Score while using 3× fewer trajectory simulations via buffer reuse.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against six main baselines across three application domains. In diffusion-based sampling (GMM, Many Well), TR-LV dominates all five unconstrained baselines (RE, CE, LV, AM, SOCM) in high dimensions: at d=200, TR-LV's |Δ log Z| of 0.002 is roughly 300× lower than SOCM (3.504) and 1500× lower than AM (3.322), while CE (0.641) and LV (1.958) also fail to maintain accuracy. The ablation in Figure 3 further shows that TR-LV outperforms log-variance with Langevin preconditioning (LP) by several orders of magnitude in target evaluations, confirming that the trust region constraint—not gradient guidance—is the key mechanism preventing mode collapse. In transition path sampling, TR-LV beats TPS-DPS (the unconstrained log-variance baseline) on both Alanine Dipeptide (THP 61.25% vs. 39.58%, RMSD 0.29 vs. 0.47 Å) and Chignolin (THP 43.95% vs. 25.00%, RMSD 0.90 vs. 1.06 Å), with notably lower standard deviations indicating more stable training. TR-LV also outperforms PIPS on Alanine Dipeptide (THP 61.25% vs. 43.75%) but trails SMD, which benefits from hand-designed collective variables. For Stable Diffusion fine-tuning, TR-SOCM matches adjoint matching (AM) on all four quality metrics (ImageReward, CLIP-Score, Human Preference Score, DreamSim diversity) while requiring only one-third the trajectory simulations due to buffer reuse, making it the most sample-efficient option tested.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the iterative diffusion optimization (IDO) framework for stochastic optimal control by incorporating trust region constraints from classical optimization into path space measure transport. It builds directly on the SOC matching loss [42], log-variance divergence [87], adjoint matching [41], and the Denoising Diffusion Sampler [129], adapting each to a constrained setting where the KL divergence to the previous iterate is bounded. The key conceptual departure is proving that these trust region iterates define a geometric annealing with equidistant Fisher-Rao steps, a connection between trust region methods and information-geometric annealing that had not previously been established. Prior IDO methods either directly minimize the divergence to the target or rely on hand-tuned annealing schedules; this work replaces both with an automatic, principled step-size selection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The trust region framework provides a principled, hyperparameter-free annealing schedule that provably prevents mode collapse in high-dimensional sampling, a failure mode that plagues all existing unconstrained IDO methods beyond roughly d=10. The Fisher-Rao equidistance result gives a clean information-geometric interpretation of why the method works, and the Lagrangian-as-SOC-problem structure enables efficient buffer-based training without additional simulations. Limitations include the need to store full trajectories in a replay buffer (introducing CPU-GPU transfer overhead at scale) and the restriction to KL divergence for the trust region constraint. Open questions include whether other divergences (e.g., forward KL, f-divergences) yield analogous annealing properties, and whether the framework extends naturally to normalizing flows or other learned measure transports as the authors suggest.
