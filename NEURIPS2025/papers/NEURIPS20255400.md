## Problem

The stochastic multi-level compositional minimax problem (min_x max_y f(G(x), y) with K > 1 nested inner functions) remains largely unexplored because stochastic gradients for both primal and dual variables are biased estimators, and existing smoothed minimax techniques—which improve condition-number dependence in classical settings—have never been adapted to this multi-level compositional structure. Additionally, no algorithm exists to translate the (ε, ε/√κ)-stationary point produced by smoothing into a standard ε-stationary point without incurring exponential dependence on the number of levels.

## Input / Output

The method takes a K-level compositional inner function G(x) = g^(K)(...g^(1)(x)...) where each g^(k) is defined by an expectation over random samples, an outer-level minimax objective f(G(x), y) that is nonconvex in x and PL in y, and standard supervised training data (imbalanced binary classification images at 32×32 or 64×64, or tabular multi-instance bags). It outputs an (ε, ε/√κ)-stationary point of the original loss after O(κ^(3/2)/ε³) iterations, and via a stage-wise refinement under the two-sided PL condition, an ε-stationary point of the value function Φ(x) = max_y f(G(x), y). In practice this yields trained ResNet20 classifiers or feed-forward networks evaluated by test AUC on imbalanced datasets.

## Method

The core algorithm (Smoothed-SMCGDA-VR) introduces an auxiliary variable z with a quadratic smoothing term ω/2‖x−z‖² to make the primal subproblem strongly convex, then applies STORM-like recursive variance-reduced estimators at every compositional level k = 1,...,K to control the bias that accumulates across nested function evaluations. The variance-reduced inner-level estimator h_t^(k) is updated via a momentum-style recursion with coefficient (1−αη²), and the compositional gradient estimators p_t and q_t for primal and dual variables are similarly updated with STORM corrections, yielding a convergence rate of O(κ^(3/2)/ε³) to an (ε, ε/√κ)-stationary point under the nonconvex-PL condition. To bridge the gap to a standard ε-stationary point, a second stage-wise algorithm (Stagewise-SMCGDA-VR) solves the strongly-convex-strongly-concave auxiliary problem min_x max_y f(G(x),y) + ω/2‖x−z̃‖² over R stages with geometrically decreasing learning rates η_{x,r} = O(μ²/(√(2^{r−1}) L_β)), achieving O(1/ε²) translation complexity that does not dominate the overall rate. The theoretical analysis relies on a novel potential function combining optimization error, gradient estimation error for both variables, and per-level inner-function estimation error, with a descent lemma that carefully tracks interdependencies among all four components.

## Results

Theoretically, Smoothed-SMCGDA-VR achieves a convergence rate of O(κ^{3/2}/ε^3) to an (ε, ε/√κ)-stationary point, improving the condition-number dependence from O(κ^3) (NSTORM) and O(κ^4) (SCGDA, SCGDAM, CODA-Primal) for two-level compositional minimax problems. The Stagewise-SMCGDA-VR translation phase adds only O(1/ε^2) iterations, preserving the overall O(κ^{3/2}/ε^3) complexity. Empirically, on the multi-instance learning tabular benchmarks, Ours(att) achieves test AUC of 0.942 on MUSK1, 0.965 on MUSK2, 0.942 on Tiger, and 0.931 on Elephant, while Ours(smx) reaches 0.770 on Fox and 0.942 on Elephant, consistently outperforming all six baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In the Deep AUC Maximization experiments (CATvsDOG, CIFAR-10, STL-10), the proposed smoothed method consistently outperforms all three baselines—SCGDA [17], SCGDAM [33], and NSTORM [26]—across both K=1 and K=5 compositional levels, with the gap widening as the number of levels increases. In the Multi-Instance Learning experiments, Ours(att) achieves the highest AUC on MUSK1 (0.942 vs. 0.841 for MIDAM(att)), MUSK2 (0.965 vs. 0.905 for MIDAM(smx)), and Tiger (0.942 vs. 0.918 for MIDAM(att)), while Ours(smx) leads on Fox (0.770 vs. 0.718 for MIDAM(att)) and Elephant (0.942 vs. 0.908 for DAM(smx)). The DAM-based baselines [34] (using the PESG optimizer) are uniformly the weakest, with AUC scores 5–15 points below the proposed method across all tabular datasets. On the Breast Cancer histopathological image dataset, both softmax-based and attention-based variants of the proposed method outperform their corresponding baselines (MIDAM and DAM), confirming the advantage extends to high-dimensional image inputs. The theoretical comparison in Table 1 shows the smoothed method's O(κ^{3/2}/ε^3) rate strictly dominates the O(κ^3/ε^3) of NSTORM [26] and the O(κ^4/ε^4) of SCGDA [17], SCGDAM [33], and CODA-Primal [9] in condition-number dependence.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) stochastic multi-level compositional minimization (Yang et al. 2019, Chen et al. 2021, Jiang et al. 2022), which handles K-level nested expectations but only for minimization; (2) stochastic two-level compositional minimax (Gao et al. 2021, Yuan et al. 2021, Liu et al. 2024), which addresses the minimax structure but is limited to two levels and the stronger nonconvex-strongly-concave assumption; and (3) smoothed minimax optimization (Zhang et al. 2020, Yang et al. 2022), which improves condition-number dependence via an auxiliary variable but assumes unbiased gradients. The paper's core departure is combining all three: applying STORM-style variance reduction to handle the biased gradients inherent in multi-level compositional minimax, while using the smoothing technique under the milder nonconvex-PL condition, and introducing a stage-wise algorithm to translate between stationarity measures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The O(κ^{3/2}/ε^3) rate is the first to achieve sub-cubic condition-number dependence for (multi-level) compositional minimax under nonconvex-PL, which is practically relevant for ill-conditioned problems in deep AUC maximization and multi-instance learning. The stage-wise translation from (ε, ε/√κ)-stationary to ε-stationary at O(1/ε^2) cost is a non-trivial technical contribution, as no prior algorithm could perform this translation in the multi-level compositional setting without incurring exponential dependence on K. Open questions include whether the approach extends to non-smooth or non-PL settings, whether the number of stages R can be reduced further, and whether the smoothing parameter ω can be adapted online rather than fixed a priori.
