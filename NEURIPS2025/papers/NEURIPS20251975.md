## Problem

Offline Learning from Observations (LfO) requires imitating expert behavior from state-only trajectories without action labels, but existing methods either depend on discriminators that overfit in low-data or high-dimensional settings, require inverse dynamics models that introduce compounding approximation errors, or operate over complex input tuples like (s,a,s′) or (s,s′,s″) that are sample-inefficient. No prior method simultaneously avoids all three of these limitations.

## Input / Output

The method consumes two offline datasets: an expert dataset D^E containing only consecutive state pairs (s, s′) with no action annotations, and a suboptimal interaction dataset D^I containing full (s, a, s′) tuples. The output is a parametric imitation policy π(a|s) extracted via a single-stage weighted behavior cloning step. Crucially, the Q-learning stage operates exclusively on (s, s′) pairs, making the optimization problem more compact than prior approaches that require action-annotated tuples during value function learning.

## Method

IOSTOM's central insight is to entirely ignore suboptimal actions during training and instead learn a state-to-state transition model g(s′|s) as an implicit policy, formulating the objective as matching joint state visitation distributions d^π(s,s′) against the expert's d^E(s,s′). Via Lagrangian duality, this yields a discriminator-free joint-state Q-learning procedure where Q(s,s′) is optimized with a Pearson χ² divergence and bounded expert Q-targets for stability. The maximization over g is handled through an Extreme-V surrogate (KL-regularized soft value) that avoids out-of-distribution queries. For policy extraction, the paper proves that Q-weighted behavior cloning is theoretically equivalent to advantage-weighted BC but more stable, and further derives a single-stage lower-bound surrogate that recovers π(a|s) directly from offline (s,a,s′) samples without training a separate inverse dynamics model.

## Results

On 24 D4RL offline LfO tasks (4 locomotion × 4 suboptimal dataset types + 4 manipulation × 2), IOSTOM achieves the highest normalized return on 23/24 tasks, with mean scores reaching 109.32 (Hopper, random+expert), 128.19 (Ant, random+expert), and 120.09 (Ant, random+few-expert). In the challenging few-expert setting, IOSTOM scores 88.77 on HalfCheetah (random+few-expert) versus 3.19 for SMODICE and 43.65 for DILO. On the maritime navigation task, IOSTOM outperforms all baselines in ADE, near-miss count, and goal reach rate while matching expert acceleration profiles.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IOSTOM outperforms all three LfO baselines on 23 of 24 D4RL tasks, with the sole exception being Walker2d (random+expert) where DILO scores 108.31 vs. IOSTOM's 107.98 (within 0.4 points). Against SMODICE and PW-DICE (both discriminator-based), IOSTOM shows the largest margins in few-expert settings: on HalfCheetah (random+few-expert), IOSTOM achieves 88.77 vs. 3.19 (SMODICE) and 4.02 (PW-DICE), a gap of over 85 points. Against DILO (the prior SOTA discriminator-free method), IOSTOM wins on 22/24 tasks; DILO's advantage on Walker2d (random+expert) is marginal, while DILO degrades severely on mismatched-dynamics tasks (HalfCheetah-Short, AntDisabled) where IOSTOM maintains strong performance. Notably, IOSTOM's locomotion scores are comparable to or exceed ReCOIL (a LfD method with access to expert actions), e.g., 128.19 vs. 126.08 on Ant (random+expert), suggesting the LfO gap can be effectively closed. On the real-world maritime navigation benchmark, IOSTOM dominates in ADE, near-miss count, and goal reach rate over BC, SMODICE, and DILO, with SMODICE performing worst due to discriminator failure in the high-dimensional observation space.

## Relation to Prior Work

IOSTOM sits at the intersection of the DICE-family of distribution-matching imitation learning algorithms and the offline LfO setting. It most directly builds on ReCOIL [39], a discriminator-free LfD method that solves the dual of a state-action occupancy matching problem, but departs by replacing the state-action tuple (s,a) with the simpler joint state pair (s,s'), thereby eliminating actions from the learning objective entirely. It also draws on Extreme Q-learning [9] to handle the max-over-transition-probability step without OOD queries, and on DILO [38] as the closest discriminator-free LfO baseline, though IOSTOM avoids DILO's costly (s,s',s'') triplet structure. The policy extraction stage introduces a Q-weighted BC formulation that is theoretically equivalent to advantage-weighted BC but avoids training a separate inverse dynamics model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical relevance is significant: LfO is the natural setting whenever expert actions are unrecordable (video demonstrations, AIS maritime data), and IOSTOM's sample efficiency (robust to 4× fewer expert transitions) and discriminator-free design make it more deployable in low-data, high-dimensional regimes where prior methods degrade. The maritime navigation case study demonstrates a concrete real-world application beyond Mujoco benchmarks. Open questions include whether the method extends to partially observable or non-Markovian settings, how it behaves when the suboptimal dataset lacks full action labels (a stated limitation), and whether the implicit-policy abstraction generalizes to multi-agent or hierarchical tasks.
