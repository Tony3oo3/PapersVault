## Problem

Existing multi-armed bandit research under network interference (MABNI) focuses almost exclusively on regret minimization, overlooking the critical trade-off where over-emphasizing the optimal arm undermines inference accuracy for sub-optimal arms. No prior work simultaneously addresses anytime-valid continual inference, regret minimization, and ATE estimation under network interference in an adversarial setting, nor provides a theoretical characterization of the fundamental trade-off between these objectives.

## Input / Output

The algorithm operates on a network of N units with adjacency matrix H, a K-armed action set, and a user-specified exposure mapping S and clustering C that compress the K^N super-arm space into |U_E| ≤ d_s^C exposure super arms. It does not require prior knowledge of the time horizon T (via a doubling trick) and produces, at every round, anytime-valid confidence sequences for the ATE between any pair of exposure super arms, along with a sampling policy over exposure super arms. At termination, it outputs IPW-based ATE estimates for all pairs of exposure super arms.

## Method

EXP3-N-CS integrates three modular components: (1) an asymptotic confidence sequence built on an IPW estimator with MAD-adjusted sampling probabilities, (2) a Maximum Additional Deviation (MAD) mechanism that blends uniform exploration with the base EXP3 policy via π_MAD(S) = (1/|U_E|)·δ_t + (1−δ_t)·π_ALG(S) where δ_t = 1/t^α, and (3) the doubling trick for unknown horizons. The exposure mapping and clustering reduce the exponentially large action space to a manageable |U_E|, enabling operation within the unified MAB-N framework. The paper establishes the first Pareto frontier showing √(regret) × (ATE estimation error) = Ω(√|U_E|) for any policy, and proves EXP3-N-CS achieves this bound with regret Õ(√(|U_E|T) + T^{1−α}) and estimation error Õ(|U_E|^{1/2}·T^{(α−1)/2}). The parameter α ∈ [0, 1/2) controls the trade-off: smaller α prioritizes inference accuracy while larger α prioritizes regret minimization.

## Results

In simulation on a 101-unit network (1 center + 5 outer clusters of 20 units, binary actions, d_s=2 exposure arms), EXP3-N-CS with α ∈ {0.1, 0.2, 0.3, 0.4, 0.49} is evaluated over 1000 runs against two baselines. The Uniform baseline (δ_t=1) achieves the narrowest confidence sequence width and lowest maximum ATE estimation error but incurs the highest cumulative regret. The Standard baseline (δ_t=0, pure EXP3) achieves the lowest regret but has the widest CS width and the largest spread in ATE estimation error with many outliers. EXP3-N-CS variants interpolate between these extremes: smaller α values (e.g., 0.1) approach Uniform's inference quality, while larger α values (e.g., 0.49) approach Standard's regret performance, confirming the theoretical Pareto trade-off of Theorem 4.1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares EXP3-N-CS against two baselines: Standard (δ_t=0, which reduces to vanilla EXP3 with no additional exploration) and Uniform (δ_t=1, which samples all exposure super arms uniformly). On cumulative regret (Fig. 2a), Standard and EXP3-N-CS with α=0.4 or 0.49 achieve the lowest regret, while Uniform consistently exhibits the highest regret due to its lack of exploitation. On CS width (Fig. 2b), Uniform achieves the narrowest intervals (best inference), Standard maintains the widest intervals (worst inference), and EXP3-N-CS variants fall in between with smaller α producing wider intervals closer to Uniform. On maximum ATE estimation error (Fig. 2c), Uniform and EXP3-N-CS with smaller α values (0.1, 0.2) achieve low median errors with compact interquartile ranges and few outliers, whereas Standard shows a noticeably wider spread and a substantial number of extreme outliers. The results empirically validate the theoretical prediction that the trade-off parameter α controls the balance: α→0 prioritizes inference (Obj. 2–3) at the cost of linear regret, while α→1/2 prioritizes regret minimization (Obj. 1) at the cost of slower CS convergence.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: adversarial bandit algorithms (EXP3, Auer et al. 2002), the MAB-N framework with exposure mapping and clustering (Zhang and Wang 2024), and anytime-valid confidence sequences (Waudby-Smith et al. 2021, Liang and Bojinov 2023). It extends Liang and Bojinov's adversarial MAB design—which handles continual inference and ATE estimation but assumes no network interference—into the networked setting, while simultaneously extending Zhang and Wang's stochastic MAB-N trade-off results into the adversarial regime. The MAD (Maximum Additional Deviation) mechanism from Liang and Bojinov is repurposed as a modular exploration floor that guarantees sufficient sampling of sub-optimal arms, and the asymptotic CS is adapted to the IPW estimator under the MAB-N exposure structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to establish a Pareto frontier for the regret–ATE-estimation trade-off in adversarial MABNI, providing practitioners with a principled way to calibrate the exploration parameter α based on whether their application prioritizes immediate welfare (regret) or causal knowledge (inference). The modular architecture—where MAD and the asymptotic CS can be plugged into any base algorithm achieving Õ(√(|U_E|T)) regret—suggests the framework generalizes beyond EXP3 to other adversarial bandit algorithms. Open questions include whether the linear-growth variance assumption (Assumption 5.3) can be relaxed, how the results extend to contextual or linear MABNI settings, and whether the Pareto frontier tightens under structural assumptions on the network (e.g., bounded degree or sparsity).
