## Problem

Existing long-video understanding benchmarks evaluate end-to-end answer quality without verifying whether models actually locate the correct temporal evidence, while prior moment retrieval datasets rely on short videos (under 3 minutes) and simplistic caption-based queries. This leaves a critical gap: no benchmark specifically tests whether models can accurately retrieve key moments in long, diverse videos for task-oriented reasoning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250934/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark takes long videos (average 1201.9 s, up to ~1 hour) from diverse domains (movie, sports, anomaly, egocentric, cartoons, surveillance) paired with one of three query forms: text-only (50%), text + reference image (20%), or text + reference video (20%). The expected output is a set of one or more time intervals (start, end timestamps) that constitute the minimal necessary and sufficient visual evidence to answer the query. For retrieval-based evaluation, videos are pre-segmented into fixed 10-second chunks; for generation-based evaluation, models receive uniformly downsampled frames (96–768 depending on model) with explicit timestamps and total duration.

## Method

MomentSeeker introduces a hierarchical task taxonomy spanning three semantic levels—global (causal reasoning, spatial relations), event (description location, action recognition, anomaly detection), and object (object recognition, localization, attribute classification, OCR)—covering 9 task types across 268 videos and 1,800 expert-annotated queries. The key design choice is task-oriented moment retrieval: rather than matching captions to clips, each query requires the model to identify the specific temporal segment(s) that provide the visual evidence needed to solve a downstream LVU task. Evaluation uses two complementary metrics (Recall@1 and mAP@5) with IoU-based matching, and the paper benchmarks both retrieval-based methods (dual-encoder and compositional retrievers over 10-s chunks) and generation-based methods (MLLMs directly predicting intervals), revealing that even state-of-the-art models like Gemini-2.5-Pro achieve only ~30 R@1, confirming the benchmark's difficulty.

## Results

On MomentSeeker (IoU=0.3), the best generation-based method, Gemini-2.5-Pro, achieves 29.6 R@1 and 31.4 mAP@5 overall, while the best retrieval-based method, InternVideo2 (1B), reaches 19.7 R@1 and 26.6 mAP@5. Among open-source generation models, Qwen2.5VL-72B (768 frames) attains 17.2 R@1 and 16.9 mAP@5, and GPT-4o scores 18.2 R@1 and 18.9 mAP@5. Even the strongest methods remain well below 30% R@1, confirming that long-video moment retrieval is highly challenging across all three task levels (global, event, object).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among generation-based methods, Gemini-2.5-Pro dominates all other models by a wide margin (29.6 R@1 vs. 18.2 for GPT-4o and 17.2 for Qwen2.5VL-72B), yet still falls short of 30% accuracy. Among retrieval-based methods, InternVideo2 (19.7 R@1) and LanguageBind (18.2 R@1) lead, both outperforming most generation-based models except Gemini-2.5-Pro. Notably, the lightweight 148M MM-Ret retriever (12.4 R@1) substantially outperforms the much larger 8B InternVL3 generation model (5.9 R@1), highlighting that current MLLMs lack strong temporal grounding. Scaling within the generation family helps: InternVL3-38B (15.8 R@1) nearly triples InternVL3-8B (5.9 R@1), and Qwen2.5VL-72B (17.2) outperforms Qwen2.5VL-7B (8.1). On mAP@5, retrieval methods consistently achieve higher scores than generation methods (e.g., InternVideo2 26.6 vs. Qwen2.5VL-72B 16.9), suggesting retrieval models produce better-ranked candidate moments even when their top-1 accuracy is comparable. Overall, no method exceeds 30% R@1, and the gap between the best (Gemini-2.5-Pro) and the weakest baselines (Lita, TimeChat at ~5.6–5.9 R@1) spans more than 24 points, underscoring the benchmark's difficulty.

## Relation to Prior Work

MomentSeeker sits at the intersection of two established but previously disconnected research threads: short-video moment retrieval (TVR, QVHighlights, CharadesSTA) and long-video understanding benchmarks (VideoMME, MLVU, LongVideoBench). Prior moment retrieval datasets operate on clips of tens of seconds with caption-style queries, while LVU benchmarks evaluate end-to-end answer quality without verifying temporal grounding. This paper carves new ground by formulating long-video moment retrieval (LVMR) as a distinct task that requires task-oriented queries (causal reasoning, anomaly detection, OCR, etc.) over videos averaging 1200 seconds, bridging the gap between these two literatures for the first time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals a critical blind spot: models that score well on end-to-end LVU benchmarks (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL-72B at 79.1 on VideoMME) achieve only 17.2 R@1 on MomentSeeker, suggesting correct answers may not reflect genuine temporal grounding. The demonstrated positive correlation between moment retrieval quality and downstream RAG-based LVU performance positions LVMR as a meaningful intermediate capability to optimize. Open questions include how to improve fine-grained temporal reasoning in MLLMs, whether scaling context length alone suffices, and how to efficiently integrate lightweight retrievers into agentic video understanding pipelines.
