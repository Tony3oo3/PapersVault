## Problem

The computational complexity of learning real-valued Multi-Index Models (MIMs) under the Gaussian distribution remained largely uncharacterized, particularly in the presence of adversarial label noise. While efficient algorithms existed for special cases (single-index models, discrete-valued MIMs, specific ReLU network classes), no general framework identified which MIM classes are efficiently learnable or established matching lower bounds. This paper fills that gap by providing a general robust regression algorithm together with a nearly matching Statistical Query lower bound that characterizes the dimension-dependent complexity.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes i.i.d. labeled samples (x, y) where the input marginal is the standard Gaussian N_d in R^d and y is a real-valued label that may be adversarially corrupted (agnostic setting). The output is a piecewise constant hypothesis h: R^d → R defined over a fine cubic partition of the learned subspace, achieving L2 error at most τ + OPT + ε with high probability. The sample complexity is d^{O(m)} 2^{poly(K/ε)} in the agnostic setting, where m is the degree of distinguishing moments and K is the hidden subspace dimension.

## Method

The core algorithm (LearnMIMs) performs iterative subspace approximation: it maintains a growing subspace V_t and at each step partitions V_t × R into small cubes and intervals, performs degree-m polynomial regression on the indicator 1(y ∈ I) within each cell, and constructs a weighted influence matrix from the outer products of regression polynomial gradients. New directions are extracted as eigenvectors of this matrix with eigenvalues above a threshold, progressively revealing the hidden K-dimensional subspace W. Once no further distinguishing moments are found, the algorithm outputs a piecewise constant function over the final subspace. The SQ lower bound introduces Relativized Non-Gaussian Component Analysis (RNGCA), a generalization of NGCA to labeled distributions, and proves hardness via Fourier analysis that avoids requiring finite chi-squared norm—a key technical advance enabling the bound to apply to realizable (noiseless) settings.

## Results

The main algorithmic result (Theorem 1.4) gives an agnostic PAC learner for well-behaved K-MIMs with sample complexity $d^{O(m)} 2^{\text{poly}(K/(\epsilon\sigma))}$, running in poly(N) time, achieving error $\tau + \text{OPT} + \epsilon$. For the concrete application to positive-homogeneous L-Lipschitz K-MIMs (Theorem 1.6), the complexity is $d^2 \cdot 2^{O(K^3 L^2/\epsilon^2)}$ samples. The matching SQ lower bound (Theorem 1.10) shows that if degree-m distinguishing moments do not exist for some subspace, any SQ learner requires $d^{\Omega(m)}$ complexity, confirming the dimension dependence is qualitatively optimal.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no experimental baselines. The key comparison is with [CKM22] (Chen, Klivans, Meka, FOCS 2022) for learning homogeneous Lipschitz ReLU networks: the prior algorithm had sample complexity with an exponential dependence on the network size $S = \sum_{i=2}^{D} k_i$, while the present work (Corollary 1.7) eliminates this dependence entirely, achieving complexity $d^2 \cdot 2^{O(K^3 L^2/\epsilon^2)}$ that depends only on the rank $K$ of the first layer, the Lipschitz constant $L$, and the target accuracy $\epsilon$. The paper also builds on [DIKZ25] (Diakonikolas et al., 2025), which established analogous results for discrete-valued MIMs under $L_0$ loss; the present work extends to real-valued outputs with $L_2$ loss, requiring new techniques for conditioning on label intervals rather than exact values and for handling unbounded labels. The SQ lower bound (Theorem 1.10) is essentially a converse to the upper bound (Theorem 1.4), showing the $d^{O(m)}$ dimension dependence is qualitatively tight in the SQ model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the discrete-valued MIM learning framework of [DIKZ25] to real-valued MIMs under L2 loss, requiring new machinery for conditioning on label intervals rather than exact values and for handling unbounded outputs. The SQ lower bound technique generalizes the moment-matching approach of [DKRS23] (which handled unlabeled NGCA without chi-squared norm assumptions) to a new 'Relativized NGCA' (RNGCA) framework for labeled distributions. On the algorithmic side, the iterative subspace-finding approach subsumes and improves upon [CKM22] for ReLU networks by eliminating the exponential dependence on network size S, and generalizes the generative exponent of [DPLB24] from single-index to multi-index models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first general characterization (via the 'well-behaved MIM' condition, Definition 1.3) of when real-valued MIMs are efficiently learnable, with a nearly matching SQ lower bound showing the d^{O(m)} dimension dependence is essentially optimal. The concrete application to positive-homogeneous Lipschitz MIMs yields the first polynomial-in-d learner for this broad class and removes the exponential-in-network-size dependence from prior ReLU network learning results. The RNGCA framework is a reusable tool for proving SQ lower bounds in supervised learning settings without chi-squared norm assumptions. Open questions include tightening the d^{O(m)} bound to d^{⌈m/2⌉} (sketched in Remark D.15) and extending the characterization to non-Gaussian marginals or non-rotationally-invariant classes.
