## Problem

Existing generated image detectors, whether training-based or training-free, struggle to generalize to unseen generative architectures, making them unreliable in practical scenarios where the source model is unknown. The paper identifies that this generalization gap is fundamentally an out-of-distribution (OOD) detection problem: generated image patterns lie outside the space spanned by natural image patterns, yet prior work has not formally connected this insight to OOD detection theory or leveraged it for a generalizable detector.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 224×224 RGB image and produces a differential energy score λ(x; f, m) = |E(x; f) − E(m(x); f)|, where m(x) is a Gaussian-noise-perturbed version of the input. A binary decision (natural vs. generated) is made by comparing λ against a threshold γ. The entire pipeline is training-free: no parameters are learned on generated images, and the only model used is the frozen DINOv2 ViT-L/14 backbone with a batch of N=128 images for computing the energy sum.

## Method

DEnD reframes generated image detection as OOD detection by treating natural image patterns as in-distribution (ID) data and generated image patterns as out-of-distribution (OOD) data, then proves generalizability under the Realizability Assumption and finite Natarajan dimension. The key mechanism is a differential energy score derived from the contrastive training objective of self-supervised learning: the energy E(x; f) = Σᵢ exp((f(x), f(xᵢ))/τ) is computed over one positive and N−1 negative samples in a batch, and the score λ measures the gap between E(x) and E(m(x)) for a randomly transformed copy. Because self-supervised training minimizes this differential energy for ID data, natural images yield consistently low λ while generated images yield high λ, enabling detection without any training on synthetic data. The theoretical contribution establishes that if a separating threshold exists between the support sets of natural and generated images in λ-space, the hypothesis space satisfies realizability, guaranteeing generalizability in the density-based space.

## Results

On ImageNet (9 generative models), DEnD achieves an average AUROC of 94.81 and AP of 93.33, outperforming the best training-based method DRCT (90.36 AUROC / 89.92 AP) and the best training-free method RIGID (83.73 AUROC / 81.69 AP). On LSUN-BEDROOM, DEnD reaches 96.05 AUROC / 95.03 AP, and on the GenImage benchmark (8 models including Midjourney and SD V1.5) it attains 89.37% average accuracy versus 79.49% for the best training-based baseline (GenDet). On Sora (unknown architecture), DEnD achieves 89.07 AUROC / 91.99 AP, surpassing DRCT (82.16 / 81.20) and RIGID (78.67 / 78.77).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DEnD outperforms all 21 baselines on average across every benchmark. Among training-based methods, DRCT is the strongest competitor (90.36 AUROC on ImageNet, 79.39% ACC on GenImage, 82.16 AUROC on Sora), yet DEnD exceeds it by 4.45 AUROC points on ImageNet, 9.98 ACC points on GenImage, and 6.91 AUROC points on Sora. The closest training-free baseline, RIGID, trails DEnD by 11.08 AUROC on ImageNet, 10.41 ACC on GenImage, and 10.40 AUROC on Sora; AEROBLADE is substantially weaker (57.87 AUROC on ImageNet) due to its reliance on LDM autoencoder access. On the AIGCDetectBenchmark (15 models), DEnD achieves 89.82% average accuracy, edging out LNP (88.75%) and UnivFD (71.73%), while training-based methods like CNNspot (50.45% on DALL-E 2) and DIRE (66.48%) degrade sharply on unseen architectures. In the ablation comparing OOD detection strategies (Table 7), DEnD's differential energy score (94.81 AUROC) substantially outperforms raw energy (46.36), ViM (60.38), and KNN (52.12), confirming that the self-supervised training objective is critical to the method's discriminative power.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reframes AI-generated image detection as an out-of-distribution (OOD) detection problem, drawing directly on the learnability theory of OOD detection (Fang et al., NeurIPS 2022) to establish generalizability guarantees via the Natarajan dimension and Realizability Assumption. The core methodological contribution redefines the energy-based OOD score (Liu et al., NeurIPS 2020) by substituting semantic label classifiers with a self-supervised model (DINOv2) and deriving a 'differential energy score' from the contrastive training objective (MoCo, He et al., CVPR 2020). This departs from prior training-based detectors (DIRE, CNNspot, DRCT) that overfit to specific generative architectures, and from training-free methods (AEROBLADE, RIGID) that rely on reconstruction errors or noise perturbations without theoretical grounding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free DEnD framework with provable generalizability offers a principled alternative to detectors that degrade sharply on unseen generative models, as demonstrated by strong results on Sora (87–91 AUROC) where training-based methods collapse to near-chance. A key open limitation is performance degradation on high-fidelity generators (Stable Diffusion: 71.9% ACC vs. 99.8% for DRCT) and under natural-image distribution shift (COCO vs. ImageNet), suggesting the approach is bounded by the representational coverage of the pre-trained self-supervised model. Future work on fine-tuning DINOv2 on broader real-image corpora or exploring alternative OOD detection strategies (KNN, ViM) could close these gaps.
