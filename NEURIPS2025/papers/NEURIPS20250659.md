## Problem

NeRF and 3DGS pipelines depend on COLMAP for camera poses, but SfM's sparse keypoint reprojection is inconsistent with dense photometric rendering, causing ghosting artifacts. Existing pose-free methods (CF-3DGS, Nope-NeRF) are limited to short sequential sequences, while DUSt3R-based feed-forward approaches (VGGT, Fast3R) suffer from rapidly growing GPU memory with hundreds of images and insufficient geometric accuracy for high-fidelity rendering. No prior method handles unordered, densely captured image collections with both accurate poses and photorealistic novel view synthesis without SfM.

## Input / Output

The method takes an unordered set of RGB images (no poses, no temporal ordering) at 512×512 resolution and produces camera extrinsics, per-pixel 3D Gaussian primitives (opacity, mean, covariance, spherical harmonics), and rendered novel views. Unusually, the scene regressor consumes image *pairs* rather than single images, and the 3D Gaussian means are predicted as offsets from the predicted pointmaps rather than directly. During training, images are downsampled by 4 for the rasterization step, and the final 3DGS refinement stage uses the standard densification/pruning pipeline at full resolution.

## Method

DeepGfM fine-tunes Spann3R (a DUSt3R variant with spatial memory) as a scene regressor that jointly predicts dense pointmaps and 3D Gaussian primitives from image pairs in a global coordinate frame. Training follows a progressive three-stage strategy: (1) seed initialization from a single image selected via NetVLAD similarity graph degree, (2) iterative registration where new images are added to a growing buffer using RANSAC+PnP for coarse poses followed by a novel point-to-camera ray consistency loss (Eq. 8) that jointly refines poses and pointmaps across multiple views, and (3) a final global pose optimization plus explicit 3DGS densification. The ray consistency loss is bounded (unlike reprojection loss), making it robust to the limited per-batch view count imposed by transformer memory constraints. By fixing previously registered poses each epoch and optimizing only local parameters, the method avoids the OOM issues that plague global alignment in DUSt3R/InstantSplat, achieving 23 GB peak memory for 200 images versus 33–41 GB for Fast3R/VGGT.

## Results

On novel view synthesis, DeepGfM achieves PSNR of 25.01 (LLFF), 28.19 (Mip-NeRF360), 23.17 (Tanks-and-Temples), and 30.96 (Seq TnT), outperforming COLMAP+3DGS on all four datasets (24.64, 28.08, 23.06, 30.64 respectively). For camera pose accuracy, the method achieves rotation errors of 0.17° (LLFF), 0.04° (Mip-NeRF360), 0.04° (Tanks-and-Temples), and 0.31° (Seq TnT), substantially beating the next-best pose-free methods (e.g., ACE0 at 3.20°, 0.83°, 9.13°, 9.52°). The method also uses less GPU memory (23.07 GB) than VGGT (40.63 GB) and Fast3R (33.17 GB) for 200-image inputs, while CF-3DGS and DUSt3R run out of memory.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DeepGfM outperforms all pose-free NeRF baselines (NeRFmm, BARF, Nope-NeRF) by large margins on 360° scenes: on Mip-NeRF360, it achieves 28.19 PSNR vs. 11.23–13.62 for those methods, which fail to estimate reliable poses in unconstrained camera distributions. CF-3DGS, designed only for sequential video, achieves 31.28 PSNR on Seq TnT (slightly above Ours' 30.96) but completely fails (OOM) on Mip-NeRF360 and Tanks-and-Temples, and performs poorly on LLFF (16.52 PSNR). ACE0, the strongest neural reconstruction baseline, is consistently beaten: on Tanks-and-Temples, Ours achieves 23.17 PSNR vs. ACE0's 20.93, and on Mip-NeRF360, 28.19 vs. 24.31. Most notably, DeepGfM surpasses COLMAP+3DGS (the standard SfM-based pipeline) on every dataset in PSNR and LPIPS, suggesting its predicted poses are more accurate than COLMAP's sparse keypoint-based estimates. Spann3R, the pre-trained backbone, shows 20.00° rotation error on LLFF vs. Ours' 0.17°, confirming the progressive fine-tuning and pose refinement steps are essential. DBARF (supplementary) is competitive on LLFF (1.38° ΔR) but still trails Ours (0.17°).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Spann3R (itself an extension of DUSt3R) by adding a 3D Gaussian primitive prediction head alongside the existing pointmap output, and introduces a progressive self-supervised fine-tuning strategy with a novel point-to-camera ray consistency loss for pose refinement. It departs from pose-free NeRF methods (NeRFmm, BARF, Nope-NeRF) that jointly optimize poses and implicit radiance fields, and from CF-3DGS which is limited to sequential frames with monocular depth priors, by handling unordered image collections with a transformer-based scene regressor. Compared to ACE0, which uses an MLP scene coordinate decoder trained on mixed multi-view pixel batches, DeepGfM retains the DUSt3R/Spann3R transformer architecture and processes image pairs, requiring the progressive buffer strategy to manage GPU memory. The feed-forward alternatives (VGGT, Fast3R) handle many images in one pass but at prohibitive memory cost (33–41 GB for 200 images), whereas DeepGfM's incremental approach uses ~23 GB.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the COLMAP dependency while matching or exceeding COLMAP-based 3DGS rendering quality, this work removes a significant practical bottleneck in photorealistic 3D reconstruction pipelines, particularly for unordered photo collections where SfM often fails. The progressive training strategy with per-epoch pose fixing offers a practical path to scaling beyond the memory limits of feed-forward methods, though the spatial memory bank still constrains scene scale. Key open questions include whether better DUSt3R variants (Fast3R, CUT3R) or LoRA-based fine-tuning can reduce the training cost, and whether the approach generalizes to truly large-scale outdoor scenes where the current Spann3R backbone struggles.
