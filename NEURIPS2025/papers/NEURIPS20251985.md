## Problem

Existing graph learning paradigms—federated learning and lifelong learning—suffer from collaborative information collapse and catastrophic forgetting in heterogeneous dynamic multi-agent settings. Federated methods rely on symmetric topologies and parameter-only exchange that cannot capture agent dependencies or ensure knowledge compatibility, while lifelong learning methods forget old task information as new tasks arrive. The paper asks how to determine with whom, what, and when to share and accumulate graph information to achieve effective multi-agent graph socialized learning (GSL).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251985/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

Each agent receives a subgraph (node features, adjacency matrix, and class labels) from a heterogeneous Dirichlet partition of the full graph, with overlapping classes across agents but disjoint classes across sequential tasks. Agents exchange synthetic graphs (compressed, class-specific node sets with unit-matrix adjacency) and SVD-derived subspace representations rather than raw data or full model parameters. At inference, the method outputs both node class predictions and a task ID, which is retrieved by matching a test-graph prototype against a pool of stored task prototypes to select the appropriate prompts and classifier.

## Method

GHG builds on a frozen GNN with learnable graph prompts and per-task classifiers (inherited from TPP), and adds three collaboration mechanisms. First, a graph-driven organizational structure computes complementarity via principal angles between SVD subspaces of agent outputs and similarity via cosine distance on Laplacian-smoothed, topology-aware embeddings, then solves a convex optimization to obtain directed, weighted collaboration edges that determine interaction partners and strength. Second, a customized interactive medium generates per-class synthetic graphs by aligning multi-layer embedding statistics (mean and standard deviation) between the source agent's graph and the synthetic graph, with class-specific node counts set by fine-grained collaboration weights; a unit-matrix adjacency avoids training a structure generator. Third, a prototype-based life cycle constructs task prototypes by averaging node representations from synthetic graphs, enabling zero-forgetting inference via prototype matching. Training combines cross-entropy loss on local and synthetic graphs with a KL-divergence loss that makes each agent mimic its neighbors' predictions on shared synthetic graphs, weighted by collaboration edge strengths.

## Results

Under strong heterogeneity (het=0.1), GHG achieves the highest MAP and zero forgetting (MAF=0.0) across all seven datasets: 54.0 on CoraFull, 59.2 on Arxiv, 86.7 on Reddit, 79.2 on Cora, 65.7 on CiteSeer, 62.4 on SLAP, and 82.5 on Computers. The strongest baseline, Fed-DMSG (GFLL), reaches only 37.3 MAP on CoraFull and 57.4 on Reddit while still suffering 0.1–6.4% forgetting. GHG's zero-MAF result is unique among high-performing methods: the only other baselines achieving MAF=0.0 (TPP, Fed-TPP) do so on Cora/CiteSeer with substantially lower MAP (53.6/51.6 vs. 79.2/65.7).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GHG outperforms all 15 baselines on every dataset for both MAP and MAF. Against the strongest GFLL baseline Fed-DMSG, GHG gains +16.7 MAP on CoraFull (54.0 vs. 37.3), +36.5 on Arxiv (59.2 vs. 22.7), and +29.3 on Reddit (86.7 vs. 57.4), while reducing forgetting from 0.1–6.4% to 0.0%. The socialized-learning baseline MASC [7] achieves 36.7 MAP on CoraFull and 58.1 on Reddit but still forgets 0.9% and 4.6% respectively; GHG eliminates all forgetting while improving MAP by 17.3 and 28.6 points. Among single-agent GLL methods, TPP [20] reaches 0.0 MAF on Cora/CiteSeer (only 2 classes per task) but scores just 53.6/51.6 MAP, whereas GHG achieves 79.2/65.7 with the same zero forgetting. GFL methods (DFedGNN, Fed-PUB, FedGTA, FedTAD) all cluster near the single-agent baseline (~4–9 MAP on CoraFull/Arxiv), confirming that parameter-only interaction causes collaborative information collapse under heterogeneity. On SLAP and Computers (Table 2), GHG scores 62.4 and 82.5 MAP with 0.0 MAF, surpassing the best baselines (Fed-TPP: 27.2/36.5) by 35.2 and 46.0 points respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the prompt-based graph learning architecture of TPP (Niu et al., NeurIPS 2024) and universal prompt tuning for GNNs (Fang et al., NeurIPS 2023), using a frozen GNN with learnable graph prompts and task-specific classifiers as the single-agent foundation. It departs from Graph Federated Learning by replacing symmetric undirected topologies with a directed weighted collaboration graph driven by SVD-based complementarity and Laplacian-smoothing similarity, and from Graph Lifelong Learning by extending from single-agent to multi-agent settings with synthetic graph generation as an interactive medium. The closest prior work, MASC (Yao et al., ICML 2024), also pursues multi-agent socialized learning but relies on full-data interaction; GHG replaces this with distribution-matched synthetic graphs and an autonomous organizational structure, reducing communication overhead while preserving information sufficiency.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a fundamental gap: existing paradigms (GFL, GLL, GFLL) suffer from information collapse under heterogeneity and catastrophic forgetting under dynamics, and no prior method achieves both lossless retention and strong performance simultaneously in multi-agent heterogeneous dynamic graph settings. The consistent zero forgetting (MAF=0) across all seven datasets, combined with large MAP gains over the best baselines, suggests the synthetic-graph-as-medium and prototype-based life cycle are effective mechanisms for decoupling collaboration from forgetting. Open questions include scalability to very large agent populations (the paper acknowledges computational overhead), whether the approach transfers to real-world multi-institutional deployments, and whether hierarchical organizational structures can replace the current flat collaboration graph at scale.
