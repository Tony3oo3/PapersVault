## Problem

Fixed-confidence Best Arm Identification (BAI) under global Differential Privacy (DP) suffers from a large gap between known lower and upper bounds on expected sample complexity, with prior algorithms only matching bounds in specific privacy regimes. This paper closes that gap to a multiplicative constant smaller than 8 for all privacy budgets ε, by introducing a tighter information-theoretic lower bound and a matching algorithm.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253494/cropped_page9_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The algorithm operates on a Bernoulli bandit instance with K arms, receiving one binary observation per pull, and is parameterized by a privacy budget ε, risk δ, and hyperparameters (η, β). The output is not merely a final arm recommendation but the full adaptive transcript: the stopping time τ_{ε,δ}, the recommended arm, and the entire sequence of pulled actions, all of which must jointly satisfy the ε-global DP constraint. The per-iteration computational and memory cost is O(K), and the algorithm is anytime (can be stopped at any time while preserving correctness).

## Method

The core technical contribution is a new divergence d_ε that smoothly interpolates between KL divergence (low privacy) and ε-scaled Total Variation distance (high privacy), replacing the min{KL, ε·TV} used in prior lower bounds. The algorithm (DP-TT) combines three components: (1) a Geometric Private Estimator (GPE) that uses arm-dependent geometric batching without forgetting, accumulating observations across phases and adding one Laplace(1/ε) noise per phase change, so the cumulative noise is only logarithmic in sample count; (2) a Generalized Likelihood Ratio (GLR) stopping rule based on d_ε-transportation costs W_{ε,a,b}, calibrated with a threshold involving the Lambert W function to ensure δ-correctness; and (3) an EB-TCI-β Top Two sampling rule that selects a leader (empirical best arm) and a challenger (arm with lowest transportation cost plus log-pull penalty), pulling the leader with fixed probability β. Novel concentration inequalities for sums of Bernoulli and Laplace variables are required to prove the stopping rule's correctness.

## Results

The paper's headline result is a theoretical gap reduction: the expected sample complexity of DP-TT satisfies limsup_{δ→0} E[τ_{ε,δ}]/log(1/δ) ≤ 2(1+η)T_{ε,β}^*(ν), while the lower bound is E[τ_{ε,δ}] ≥ T_{ε}^*(ν)·log(1/(3δ)). For the default hyperparameters (η,β)=(1,1/2), this yields an upper bound of 4T_{ε,1/2}^*(ν) ≤ 8T_{ε}^*(ν), reducing the gap between known lower and upper bounds to a multiplicative constant strictly smaller than 8 for all privacy budgets ε. Empirically, DP-TT outperforms all competing δ-correct ε-global DP BAI algorithms (AdaP-TT, AdaP-TT*, DP-SE) across all tested privacy budgets (ε from 0.001 to 125) and both Bernoulli instances at δ=10⁻², with a factor-of-2 overhead over the non-private EB-TCI-β baseline in the low-privacy regime.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DP-TT outperforms all three private baselines (AdaP-TT, AdaP-TT*, DP-SE) across all privacy budgets ε and both tested Bernoulli instances (μ₁=(0.95,0.9,0.9,0.9,0.5) and μ₂=(0.75,0.7,0.7,0.7,0.7)) at δ=10⁻². AdaP-TT [Azize et al., 2023] uses non-private Gaussian transportation costs and only matches the high-privacy lower bound for instances with similar-order means; AdaP-TT* [Azize et al., 2024] extends this to all distinct-mean instances but still fails to match the lower bound beyond the high-privacy regime due to non-adapted transportation costs. DP-SE [Sajed and Sheffet, 2019] is less adaptive (uniform sampling within phases, phase length depends on δ) and matches the lower bound only as ε→0. Against the non-private EB-TCI-β benchmark [Jourdan et al., 2022], DP-TT requires approximately twice the samples in the low-privacy regime (where d_ε reduces to KL), reflecting the inherent cost of the fixed β=1/2 target allocation versus the optimal w_{ε}^*(ν). The key advantage of DP-TT is its use of d_ε-inspired transportation costs that smoothly interpolate between KL and ε·TV, enabling matching the lower bound up to a constant <8 for all ε, whereas prior methods only match it in specific privacy regimes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the fixed-confidence BAI framework of Garivier and Kaufmann (2016) and the Top Two sampling paradigm of Jourdan et al. (2022) into the ε-global differential privacy setting. The key conceptual departure from prior private BAI work (Azize et al. 2023, 2024; Sajed and Sheffet 2019) is the replacement of the cruder min{KL, ε·TV} distinguishability measure with the d_ε divergence, which smoothly interpolates between KL and TV via an optimal intermediate distribution. The d_ε quantity was originally introduced by Azize et al. (2025) for private regret minimization; this paper shows it also tightly characterizes the hardness of private BAI, and pairs it with a new geometric private estimator (without forgetting) and novel Laplace/mixed-distribution concentration inequalities to build the DP-TT algorithm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper closes a significant open problem by reducing the gap between lower and upper bounds on expected sample complexity for ε-global DP BAI to a multiplicative constant below 8, for all privacy budgets ε. The d_ε divergence provides a principled, instance-dependent characterization of the privacy-utility tradeoff, identifying a regime transition point that depends on both ε and the instance means. Remaining limitations include a suboptimal factor-2 in the stopping threshold (from summing per-arm thresholds) and the restriction to Bernoulli distributions; extending to Gaussian/bounded distributions, (ε,δ)-DP, Rényi DP, or shuffle DP are natural next steps. The concentration results for sums of Bernoulli and Laplace variables are noted as potentially of independent interest beyond BAI.
