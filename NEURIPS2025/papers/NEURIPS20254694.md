## Problem

Current LMMs exhibit a fundamental bias toward language preferences, often producing nearly identical answers even when visual inputs are replaced with black images, revealing that cross-modality alignment is inadequately learned. This causes models to overlook visual information, producing hallucinated or less informative outputs. The paper frames this as a diagnostic question: can an LMM reconstruct shuffled visual patches or shuffled text descriptions, and can training on such reconstruction tasks enforce robust visual-textual alignment?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254694/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes 336×336 RGB images (encoded by CLIP ViT-L-14 into 576 patches of 14×14) alongside multi-turn text conversations. During training, images are randomly shuffled at the patch level using 10,000 pre-selected permutations with balanced pairwise Hamming distance, and text descriptions are word-permuted. Outputs include standard free-form textual responses, a permutation index predicting the correct image patch ordering (produced by a linear projection head on the directed token's final hidden state), and reordered text sentences for the text-order task.

## Method

The core contribution introduces two shuffle-learning pretext tasks—reconstructing image patch order and reconstructing text word order—integrated into both the pre-training and fine-tuning phases of a LLaVA-style architecture. A learnable directed token (drt) is appended at the end of the input sequence so that, under autoregressive causal attention, it can attend to all preceding visual and textual tokens; its hidden state is then projected through a linear head to predict the permutation index, avoiding the inefficiency of using visual tokens that cannot attend to future text. An Image-to-Response Guided loss (L_{I→R}) is added to explicitly increase attention scores from visual tokens to response tokens across all LLM layers, reinforcing visual grounding in generated text. The full training objective combines standard cross-entropy, image-order reconstruction loss, text-order reconstruction loss (pre-training only), and the guided attention loss, with image shuffling also applied during fine-tuning to maintain visual understanding in multi-turn reasoning contexts.

## Results

Direct-LLaVA with Vicuna-13B achieves state-of-the-art results across all five academic benchmarks: 81.6% on VQAv2, 65.3% on GQA, 54.9% on VizWiz, 77.3% on ScienceQA-IMG, and 65.4% on TextVQA. On instruction-following benchmarks, it reaches 90.5/89.2/87.8 F1 on POPE (rand/pop/adv), 65.6% on SEED-Bench, 1572.1 on MME, 41.1% on MM-Vet, and 38.0% on MMMU-Val. The largest gains over LLaVA-1.5-13B are on ScienceQA-IMG (+5.7%) and TextVQA (+4.1%), indicating the shuffle learning tasks most strongly improve scientific reasoning and text-in-image understanding.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Direct-LLaVA-13B (Vicuna-13B) outperforms LLaVA-1.5-13B on all five academic benchmarks, with the largest margins on ScienceQA-IMG (77.3 vs 71.6, +5.7) and TextVQA (65.4 vs 61.3, +4.1), and smaller but consistent gains on VQAv2 (+1.6), GQA (+2.0), and VizWiz (+1.3). On instruction-following benchmarks, Direct-LLaVA-13B beats LLaVA-1.5-13B on POPE (90.5/89.2/87.8 vs 87.1/86.2/84.5), SEED-Bench (65.6 vs 61.6), MME (1572.1 vs 1531.3), MM-Vet (41.1 vs 36.1), and MMMU-Val (38.0 vs 36.4), though it marginally trails on LLaVA-Wild (72.3 vs 72.5). Against Qwen-VL (Qwen-7B, 448×448), Direct-LLaVA-13B surpasses it on VQAv2 (81.6 vs 78.8), GQA (65.3 vs 59.3), VizWiz (54.9 vs 35.2), and SciQA-IMG (77.3 vs 67.1), while Qwen-VL retains a slight edge on TextVQA (63.8 vs 65.4 is actually won by Direct-LLaVA). Compared to BLIP-2 and InstructBLIP, Direct-LLaVA shows substantial improvements across all benchmarks, reflecting the advantage of the LLaVA v1.5 architecture combined with the proposed shuffle learning. The 7B variant of Direct-LLaVA also consistently outperforms LLaVA-1.5-7B on every academic benchmark, confirming the method's effectiveness independent of LLM scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the LLaVA/LLaVA v1.5 architecture (CLIP ViT-L-14 + MLP connector + Vicuna/LLaMA/Qwen LLMs) and repurposes the jigsaw-puzzle pretext task—previously used in self-supervised visual representation learning (Carlucci et al. 2019, Noroozi & Favaro 2016, Chen et al. 2023)—for multimodal alignment in LMMs. The directed-token mechanism extends the authors' earlier DirecFormer work on directed attention for video understanding, adapting it to the autoregressive LLM setting where causal masking prevents earlier tokens from attending to later ones. The key departure from prior LMM work is the introduction of text-order reconstruction as a complementary shuffle task and the Image-to-Response Guided Loss, which explicitly enforces attention flow from visual tokens to response tokens rather than relying solely on next-token prediction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper quantifies a well-known but under-addressed pathology in LMMs—near-complete reliance on language priors over visual inputs (Table 1 shows only ~2.7% degradation when images are blacked out)—and offers a simple, plug-and-play training recipe that consistently improves alignment across 11+ benchmarks without architectural changes or additional data. However, the approach is validated only at 7B–13B scale with LLaVA v1.5 data, and the authors acknowledge that balancing weights among the three learning objectives remain unexplored, leaving open whether the gains persist at larger model scales or with more diverse training data. The technique is lightweight enough to be adopted by other LMM frameworks, making it a practical contribution for the multimodal alignment community.
