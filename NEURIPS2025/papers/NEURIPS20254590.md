## Problem

Vision Transformers produce artifact tokens in their dense features that are discordant with local image semantics, degrading performance on fine-grained localization tasks like segmentation and depth prediction. While register tokens effectively absorb these artifacts, they require training the model from scratch, making them impractical for the large ecosystem of existing pretrained ViTs. The paper asks: can register tokens be integrated into an already-trained ViT without full retraining or additional labeled data?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254590/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an existing pretrained ViT backbone (e.g., CLIP ViT-B/16, DINOv2-base) and a set of unlabeled images (resized to 448×448 for CLIP-based models or 518×518 for DINOv2) as input, and produces a modified ViT with 16 additional register tokens that outputs artifact-free dense feature maps. These clean features are then consumed by downstream heads: a text prompt for open-vocabulary segmentation (producing per-pixel class scores), a linear probe for semantic segmentation masks, or a linear probe with AdaBins for monocular depth maps. The denoising step applies 10 random spatial augmentations (shifts up to ±15% and horizontal flips) to the teacher's input, with the first augmentation always being the unmodified image.

## Method

PH-Reg (Post Hoc Registers) is a self-distillation framework where both teacher and student are initialized from the same pretrained ViT weights. The teacher is frozen and its dense features are denoised via test-time augmentation: 10 randomly shifted/flipped copies of each image are processed, and the per-patch features are averaged after inverse-transforming back to original coordinates, exploiting the fact that artifact tokens are not spatially static relative to image content. The student network receives the original (unshifted) image and is augmented with 16 randomly initialized register tokens that participate in self-attention but are discarded at output. Only a small subset of student parameters is unfrozen during distillation—registers, positional embeddings, the convolutional patch embedding layer, and the final attention layer—while the rest of the pretrained weights remain fixed. The student is optimized with a combined cosine-similarity and MSE loss against the denoised teacher features, requiring no labeled data and completing in roughly 150 hours on 4 GPUs versus ~365 hours for the gradient-based DVT alternative.

## Results

On open-vocabulary semantic segmentation across 8 benchmarks, PH-Reg achieves a mean mIoU of 41.85%, outperforming all baselines on 7 of 8 datasets. The largest gains are on VOC21 (63.01% vs. 59.62% for SCLIP) and ADE20K (19.22% vs. 16.45% for SCLIP). In linear-probe segmentation, PH-Reg improves CLIP by +1.44% mIoU on VOC21 and +2.29% on ADE20K over the vanilla backbone, and for DINOv2 it achieves 86.35% δ₁ on NYUv2 depth estimation, surpassing DINOv2+DVT (85.23%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PH-Reg (41.85% avg mIoU) outperforms SCLIP (40.08%), ClearCLIP (39.52%), and NACLIP (39.41%) on 7 of 8 benchmarks, with the sole exception being VOC20 where ClearCLIP's q-q attention yields 84.56% vs. PH-Reg's 83.05%. Against the DVT-denoised variants (NACLIP+DVT at 39.91%, MaskCLIP+DVT at 30.96%), PH-Reg achieves consistent gains, suggesting that DVT's stationary-artifact assumption is less effective for CLIP-based models. In linear-probe evaluation (Table 2), PH-Reg improves CLIP by +1.44% mIoU on VOC21 and +2.29% on ADE20K, and for DINOv2 it achieves 86.35% δ₁ on NYUv2 depth, beating DINOv2+DVT (85.23%) while using far fewer additional parameters. The method also outperforms OpenCLIP+DVT and DFN-CLIP+DVT on segmentation mIoU, confirming broad applicability across different CLIP training objectives. Overall, PH-Reg adds only ~0.47M parameters (86.66M vs. 86.19M for base CLIP) while delivering the largest average improvement among all compared approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the register token concept introduced by Darcet et al. [50], which showed that randomly initialized register tokens can absorb artifact tokens in ViTs, but only when trained from scratch. PH-Reg departs from this by adding registers post-hoc to existing pretrained models via self-distillation, eliminating the need for full retraining. It also contrasts with DVT [33], which denoises dense features using gradient-based neural field optimization, by replacing that expensive process with a simple test-time augmentation averaging scheme. The method sits at the intersection of the training-free open-vocabulary segmentation line (MaskCLIP, SCLIP, NACLIP) and the artifact-mitigation literature, applying a distillation framework to bridge the two.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical value is clear: practitioners with large pretrained ViTs (CLIP, DINOv2, OpenCLIP) can retrofit register tokens and obtain cleaner dense features without the prohibitive cost of retraining from scratch, with only ~0.5M additional parameters. The finding that artifact tokens are not universally high-norm (they are low-norm in CLIP/OpenCLIP but high-norm in DINOv2/DFN-CLIP) challenges the DINOv2-centric narrative and suggests artifact mechanisms are model-dependent. An open question is whether the static-artifact assumption that limits DVT (and partially limits PH-Reg on DINOv2) can be replaced with a dynamic artifact model, and whether the TTA denoising generalizes to architectures beyond ViT-B/16.
