## Problem

Fine-tuning LLMs on real-world data pools often encounters domain labels that are missing, imprecise, or non-normalized, yet existing data mixture methods require domain tags as priors and data selection methods tend to prioritize quality over diversity, failing to balance multi-domain performance. The paper asks: can an LLM self-supervise its own data selection by treating semantic diversity as a reward signal, without any external labels or auxiliary models?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253645/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes an unlabeled instruction-pair dataset (e.g., 40K entries spanning common sense, reasoning, math, and coding), natural-language domain descriptions (no actual domain-labeled samples), and a frozen LLM whose embedding layer and a chosen hidden layer (layer-3) supply all semantic representations. It produces a selected 20% subset for fine-tuning along with per-sample diversity reward scores; the LLM simultaneously serves as the output model (generating synthetic seeds and providing frozen embeddings for the probe) and the input model (being fine-tuned on the selected data), eliminating any dependency on external scorers or ground-truth domain annotations.

## Method

DAAR operates in three stages. First, the LLM itself generates model-aware domain centroids: zero-shot prompted seed samples are iteratively expanded via context-aware generation with a sliding-window buffer and cosine-similarity rejection sampling, then averaged in the LLM's embedding space to form centroids; constrained k-means clustering against these centroids yields pseudo-labels for a probe training set. Second, a lightweight MLP probe (ψ_dom) is trained on frozen hidden-layer embeddings to predict domain membership (cross-entropy, 92.7% accuracy), and a second MLP (ψ_div) regresses the probe's predictive entropy H(x) = −Σ p_k log p_k from the same embeddings (MSE loss). Third, the predicted entropy serves as a sample-level diversity reward: the top 20% highest-entropy samples are selected for fine-tuning. The theoretical grounding is an importance-sampling decomposition (Proposition 3.2) showing the optimal density ratio q(x)/p(x) = Σ λ_k p(c=k|x), where high-entropy samples are precisely those for which a deterministic single-domain assignment incurs the largest approximation error, making them most valuable for multi-domain optimization.

## Results

DAAR achieves the best average (AVG) score across all three model families: 38.83 on Llama3.1-8B, 53.99 on Qwen2-7B, and 53.76 on Qwen2.5-7B, surpassing the strongest baselines by +0.14, +0.97, and +1.11 points respectively. On high-difficulty STEM tasks, DAAR delivers particularly large gains: MATH scores of 38.1 (Qwen2, +7.4% over random) and 16.70 (Qwen2.5, +27% over random), while maintaining competitive coding performance (64.94 HumanEval on Qwen2, 64.20 MBPP on Qwen2.5). On the out-of-distribution MMLU benchmark, DAAR also leads with 69.42 on Qwen2-7B, and on Qwen3-8B it achieves the top AVG of 50.06.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DAAR outperforms all five baselines on the average metric across all three model families. On Llama3.1-8B, DAAR (38.83) narrowly beats SUPERFILTER (38.73) and INSTAG-BEST (38.70), while ALPAGASUS (36.34) and INSTRUCTION LEN (36.64) fall below even the RANDOM baseline (37.27). On Qwen2-7B, DAAR (53.99) leads by a clear margin over DEITA-BEST (52.73) and INSTAG-BEST (52.53), with all baselines falling below the RANDOM baseline (53.02) except DEITA-BEST and INSTAG-BEST. On Qwen2.5-7B, DAAR (53.76) surpasses INSTAG-BEST (52.63) and DEITA-BEST (52.47), while SUPERFILTER collapses to 48.62 due to severe coding degradation (39.50 MBPP). The most striking per-task advantage is on MATH: DAAR scores 38.10 on Qwen2 (vs. 35.36 for RANDOM and 35.35 for INSTAG-BEST) and 16.70 on Qwen2.5 (vs. 13.15 for RANDOM), demonstrating that diversity-aware selection specifically benefits high-difficulty reasoning tasks where quality-focused baselines struggle to maintain cross-domain balance.

## Relation to Prior Work

DAAR extends the data selection literature for LLM fine-tuning (AlpaGasus, SuperFilter, DEITA, INSTAG) by addressing the domain-undetermined setting where clean domain labels are unavailable, a practical gap the authors identify in both data-mixing methods (DoReMi, RegMix) and label-dependent selection approaches. The theoretical grounding draws on importance sampling for data selection (Xia et al., Xie et al.), but the key departure is using predictive entropy from a lightweight MLP probe as a sample-level diversity reward, rather than relying on pairwise centroid distances or external GPT-based scorers. The dual-identity framing—where the LLM simultaneously serves as the diversity estimator (via frozen embeddings) and the model being fine-tuned—repurposes the self-rewarding paradigm into a data curation context, distinguishing it from prior self-rewarding work focused on generation quality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a practical, label-free recipe for selecting diverse fine-tuning data that could reduce annotation overhead in real-world LLM deployment pipelines, and the clean importance-sampling-to-entropy connection provides a principled justification for entropy-based selection beyond heuristic diversity metrics. However, the evaluation is confined to four domains, 7B-scale models, and a single 40K data pool, leaving open whether the approach generalizes to dozens of domains, larger models, or highly imbalanced distributions. The finding that model-generated pseudo-labels outperform ground-truth labels (Table 4) hints at a broader principle about model-aware data curation that warrants further investigation. The modest absolute gains (+0.14 to +1.11 AVG over best baselines) suggest the method is a useful refinement rather than a transformative advance in data selection.
