## Problem

Sampling-based profile-guided optimization (PGO) collects execution profiles with negligible overhead but produces noisy, incomplete data—underestimating hot blocks, overestimating cold ones, and leaving some blocks entirely unsampled. Existing profile inference methods, such as Minimum-Cost Flow formulations and the state-of-the-art Profi algorithm, rely on hand-tuned heuristics that cannot capture higher-order execution patterns or generalize to unseen control-flow structures, leaving much of sampling-based PGO's potential untapped.

## Input / Output

The model takes a per-function control-flow graph extracted from LLVM IR, where each basic block node carries a feature vector (instruction counts, control-flow flags, memory operations, successor features) augmented with noisy sampled execution counts obtained via hardware sampling (Linux perf / LBR). The output is a per-basic-block execution frequency, normalized via a softmax head so that frequencies across all blocks in a function sum to one, forming a valid probability distribution ready for the PGO optimization pipeline.

## Method

PROFIX proposes a hybrid GNN architecture that alternates between sequential and structural learning to infer execution frequencies from noisy samples. Basic block features are first projected into a latent space, then processed in parallel by a multi-layer LSTM (capturing execution order via reversed post-order traversal) and a GraphSAGE convolution (capturing CFG neighborhood structure). The fused representations pass through stacked SAGEAttention layers, each combining GraphSAGE mean-pooling aggregation with multi-head Graph Attention for adaptive neighbor weighting, followed by a feedforward module with residual connections. This alternating design is repeated across N layers for progressive refinement, and a final softmax projection yields the normalized frequency distribution. The model is trained end-to-end with RMSE loss against instrumentation-based ground-truth profiles, and its inductive GNN components enable generalization to previously unseen CFGs without retraining.

## Results

On the out-of-distribution SPEC CPU 2017 benchmarks, PROFIX achieves an average node frequency overlap of 93.42%, outperforming the state-of-the-art symbolic method Profi (91.76%) by up to 9.15% (on imagick: 98.85% vs. 90.14%) and the best ML baseline GraphSAGE (90.47%) by 2.95%. On the in-distribution held-out test set, PROFIX attains an RMSE of 0.0341 and 93.50% overlap. When integrated into the LLVM PGO pipeline on MySQL, PROFIX-inferred profiles yield a 12.1% (±0.3%) average speedup over the native binary, compared to 8.3% for raw sampling and 9.7% for Profi-inferred profiles.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PROFIX outperforms all 8 baselines on average overlap (93.42%), with the largest margins over GCN (+6.80%), GIN (+10.78%), and GAT (+9.39%). Against the strongest symbolic baseline Profi, PROFIX wins on 10 of 12 benchmarks but trails on xz (85.74% vs. 91.02%) and namd (88.88% vs. 93.84%), where the MCF formulation's flow-conservation constraints happen to align well with the true profile. Among neural baselines, GraphSAGE is the closest competitor (90.47% average), and PROFIX beats it on 11 of 12 benchmarks; the sole exception is xz where SAGE edges ahead by 0.17%. Notably, PROFIX underperforms on deepsjeng (90.78%), where GCN (94.91%), GIN (94.15%), and GraphGPS (93.40%) all exceed it, suggesting that this benchmark's control-flow structure is better captured by transductive or attention-heavy architectures. The parameter-matched comparison (Table 6, all models ~1.67M params) confirms that PROFIX's 93.50% overlap advantage over the next-best GraphGPS (89.31%) stems from architectural design rather than model capacity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the established minimum-cost flow (MCF) formulation for profile inference (Profi, He et al. 2022; Wu & Larus 1994) by replacing hand-tuned network-flow heuristics with a learned graph neural network approach. Architecturally, it builds on GraphSAGE (Hamilton et al. 2017) for inductive neighborhood aggregation and GAT (Veličković et al. 2018) for attention-based edge weighting, combining them into a novel SAGEAttention layer that alternates with LSTM sequential encoding. The authors position this as the first application of GNNs—and ML more broadly—to the profile inference problem in compiler optimization, bridging the gap between low-fidelity hardware samples and the high-quality profiles needed for effective PGO.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Sampling-based PGO is the preferred profiling strategy for datacenter-scale and latency-sensitive deployments, but its effectiveness has been fundamentally capped by profile inaccuracy; PROFIX demonstrates that learned inference can recover up to 9.15% additional overlap over the state-of-the-art symbolic method, translating to a 12.1% end-to-end speedup on MySQL versus 8.3% from raw samples. Open questions include whether the approach extends to JIT-compiled or highly dynamic workloads, whether domain-specific fine-tuning (e.g., GPU kernels, embedded systems) is necessary, and how the method integrates with online or interactive profiling pipelines rather than the current offline setting. The reliance on instrumentation-based ground truth for training also limits deployment in environments where such profiles are unavailable.
