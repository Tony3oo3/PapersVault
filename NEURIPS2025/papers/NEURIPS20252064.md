## Problem

Inference-time alignment methods like Best-of-n sampling are known to suffer from reward hacking—where overoptimizing an imperfect proxy reward eventually degrades true performance—but prior work lacked a unified theoretical characterization of when and why this happens for inference-time mechanisms specifically, and offered no principled, computationally efficient way to find the optimal operating point that avoids the winner's curse.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252064/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a text prompt, a frozen base LLM policy π_ref, and a proxy reward model r_p that scores candidate responses. Proxy scores are first mapped to uniform [0,1] via their empirical CDF before any selection mechanism is applied. The output is a single selected response, and during a one-time calibration phase (HedgeTune), the method also produces the optimal inference parameter (n for BoN, λ for SBoN, or μ for BoP) by requiring black-box access to a true reward oracle on a held-out set of prompt-response pairs.

## Method

The paper makes three core contributions. First, it proves (Theorem 1) that under monotone-likelihood-ratio densities—which include BoN and BoP—the expected true reward as a function of the inference parameter is either monotone or has exactly one interior extremum, making the hacking threshold algorithmically tractable. Second, it introduces Best-of-Poisson (BoP), which replaces the fixed sample count n in BoN with a Poisson-distributed count, yielding a closed-form density q_μ(x) = (μx+1)e^{μ(x−1)} that provably approximates the optimal KL-constrained exponential tilt with a uniform KL gap bounded by 8×10⁻⁴, all controlled by a single parameter μ. Third, it develops HedgeTune, which finds the hacking threshold by solving a one-dimensional root-finding problem: the optimal parameter θ† is where the derivative of expected true reward vanishes, equivalently where the residual R(θ) = E[r_t(u)·ψ(u,θ)] = 0, with the score function ψ specified per method (e.g., ψ = 1/n + ln u for BoN).

## Results

The paper's primary quantitative result is that Best-of-Poisson (BoP) approximates the optimal KL-constrained tilted distribution with a KL divergence gap bounded between 0 and 8×10⁻⁴ across all values of the Poisson parameter μ (Figure 2). In verifiable reward setups (MMLU Pro, MATH, GPQA via the PPE dataset), HedgeTune recovers the optimal operating point for BoN and BoP, and SBoN with HedgeTune-tuned λ achieves a superior reward-distortion curve compared to standard SBoN (Figure 3). In human-preference setups (Pythia 1.4B on AlpacaFarm with AlpacaRM as gold reward), hedging via λ in SBoN or n in BoN/BoP effectively mitigates reward hacking across proxy RMs trained on 10k–80k preference pairs with 0% and 25% label noise (Figure 4).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three inference-time methods—standard BoN, standard SBoN, and the new BoP—each with and without HedgeTune tuning. Standard BoN (selecting the highest proxy-reward sample from n candidates) suffers from reward hacking: as n increases, true reward first rises then collapses, a pattern confirmed on GPQA even with the strong Skywork-Llama-3.1 8B reward model. BoN with HedgeTune recovers the optimal n† that maximizes true reward before the hacking threshold, outperforming the unhedged version at larger n. Standard SBoN (softmax over n candidates with fixed temperature) also exhibits hacking at high λ; SBoN with HedgeTune-tuned λ† achieves the peak true reward without the subsequent decline, yielding a strictly superior reward-distortion tradeoff. BoP with HedgeTune matches or exceeds BoN (HedgeTune) in reward-distortion efficiency while using a single continuous parameter μ instead of a discrete n, and its near-equivalence to the optimal tilted distribution (KL gap ≤ 8×10⁻⁴) means it can traverse the full reward-KL frontier at inference without fine-tuning. Overall, all three hedged variants outperform their unhedged counterparts, with SBoN (HedgeTune) providing the smoothest control and BoP (HedgeTune) offering the best single-parameter approximation to the theoretical optimum.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Best-of-n (BoN) and Soft Best-of-n (SBoN) line of inference-time alignment by introducing Best-of-Poisson (BoP), which randomizes the sample count via a Poisson distribution to induce an exponential-tilt structure that provably approximates the optimal KL-constrained reward-tilted distribution. It also builds on the winner's curse framework from auction theory to formalize when and why overoptimizing a proxy reward inevitably degrades true performance in inference-time methods. The HedgeTune algorithm connects to the broader RLHF literature on KL-regularized optimization by providing a one-dimensional root-finding procedure to locate the hacking threshold, complementing prior empirical observations of reward hacking (Gao et al., 2023; Huang et al., 2025) with a principled mitigation strategy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first general theoretical guarantee that reward hacking is inevitable (not merely observed) for a broad class of inference-time mechanisms under monotone-likelihood-ratio conditions, and offers a practical single-parameter method (BoP) that spans the entire reward-KL tradeoff with negligible loss (≤8×10⁻⁴ KL gap) compared to the intractable optimal tilted distribution. A key limitation is that HedgeTune requires black-box access to the true reward for one-time calibration, which is feasible in verifiable domains (math, code) but less straightforward for open-ended preference alignment. Open questions include extending the framework to multi-objective settings, handling non-uniform reward distributions without the quantile-mapping approximation, and whether the Poisson randomization trick generalizes to other sampling-based alignment paradigms beyond BoN.
