## Problem

Existing causal world models assume a single, fixed causal graph, but in open-ended environments the apparent causal structure shifts as the agent encounters novel latent contexts (e.g., 'push → open' holds for an unlocked door but vanishes when locked). Prior approaches either learn one uniform graph that ignores these context-dependent changes or require a priori knowledge of state labels, and none combine active curiosity-driven intervention with a unified representation of context-varying causal dynamics.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255145/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The agent consumes discrete/continuous environment state vectors and actions as concatenated state-action pairs, which are encoded through a VQ-VAE into a latent embedding space; intervention targets (a single variable index per step) are selected by the curiosity policy and applied via the do-operator. The output is a Meta-Causal Graph: a set of binary causal skeleton matrices (one per meta state) defining DAGs over state variables, a learned state-to-meta-state mapping via codebook assignment, next-state transition probabilities conditioned on each subgraph's parent set, and the curiosity reward signal that drives the next intervention.

## Method

The Meta-Causal Graph is a minimal unified representation comprising multiple causal subgraphs, each activated by a discrete meta state in a VQ-VAE codebook; a state-to-meta-state mapping C assigns each observation to its active subgraph. A Curious Causality-Seeking Agent drives discovery through three coupled mechanisms: (1) a curiosity reward (default: edge-entropy over the posterior causal skeleton) selects which variable to intervene on via the do-operator, (2) intervention verification estimates causal effects Δ_ij and refines the skeleton through a mask-refinement loss, and (3) adaptive codebook fusion merges redundant meta states to maintain a minimal representation. The world model is trained by maximizing log-likelihood of next-state predictions conditioned on each subgraph's parent set, regularized by L1 sparsity on the skeleton matrix and a quantization loss on the codebook. Theoretical results establish identifiability of meta states up to swap-label equivalence (Theorem 1), robustness to overparameterization (Theorem 2), and sufficient intervention conditions for unique causal subgraph recovery (Proposition 1).

## Results

On the Chemical environment (Fork, n=2 noisy nodes), MCG achieves 63.18% prediction accuracy, outperforming the best baseline FCDL (57.82%) by over 5 points. On downstream task rewards, MCG achieves 14.65±2.75 on Fork (n=2) and 13.82±3.84 on Chain (n=2), surpassing FCDL (13.98 and 11.16 respectively). On the Magnetic robot arm manipulation task, MCG reaches 3.19±0.14 average episode reward, exceeding the next-best NCD (2.85±0.47) and FCDL (2.77±0.45). Ablations confirm both the mask loss (intervention verification) and curiosity-driven reward contribute meaningfully, with removing either dropping Fork (n=2) accuracy to 58.18% and 48.28% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MCG consistently outperforms all 10 baselines across every environment and noise level. The closest competitor is FCDL (which also uses quantized causal dynamics), achieving 13.98 on Fork (n=2) and 11.16 on Chain (n=2), but MCG leads by 0.67 and 2.66 points respectively, with the gap widening under higher noise (e.g., 12.45 vs. 10.62 on Chain n=6). NCD, another causal-discovery-based method, trails MCG by roughly 3–4 reward points on Chemical tasks and by 0.34 on Magnetic. Static causal methods (CDL, GRADER) and non-causal baselines (GNN, MLP, Transformer, Modular, NPS, Sandy-Mixture) all cluster in the 6–11 reward range on Chemical and below 2.3 on Magnetic, far behind MCG. Notably, MCG also surpasses the Oracle baseline (which uses ground-truth causal structure) on Fork (n=2: 14.65 vs. 7.83) and Magnetic (3.19 vs. 0.95), suggesting that active intervention-driven discovery yields a more useful world model than passively given causal structure in these settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the causal discovery literature from static, single-graph settings to context-dependent causal structures in open-ended environments. It builds on interventional causal discovery (Hauser & Bühlmann's I-MEC framework) and causal representation learning (Schölkopf et al.), but departs from prior non-stationary causal methods (Huang et al. 2020, Pitis et al. 2020) by introducing the Meta-Causal Graph—a VQ-VAE-quantized collection of causal subgraphs triggered by latent meta states—and coupling it with a curiosity-driven active intervention policy. The theoretical identifiability results (swap-label and observational equivalence of meta states) are novel contributions not present in prior causal discovery work, which typically assumes a fixed graph or requires strong domain priors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Meta-Causal Graph formulation addresses a genuine gap: most causal world models assume a single invariant causal structure, which breaks down in open-ended settings where the agent's limited observational window makes causal rules appear to shift. The identifiability guarantees (especially Theorem 2 showing overparameterization is harmless) provide practical guidance for choosing codebook sizes without domain knowledge. However, the empirical evaluation is limited to two environments (synthetic Chemical and one Robosuite arm task), interventions are assumed costless, and the paper acknowledges that unreachable states and budget constraints remain open problems that could limit real-world applicability.
