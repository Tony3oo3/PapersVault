## Problem

Existing weakly supervised video moment retrieval methods train on video-query pairs without timestamps but fail to generalize to compositional queries with novel syntactic structures or unseen vocabulary. Their proposal pipelines suffer from three specific limitations: boundaries generated solely from global associations lack fine-grained temporal perception, fixed Gaussian distributions cannot accommodate variable action durations, and quality assessment discards partially relevant proposals, preventing the model from learning fine-grained query-element associations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254414/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes pre-extracted frame-level video features (I3D for Charades-CG, C3D for ActivityNet-CG) and word-level GloVe query embeddings, both projected into a shared latent dimension d. It produces a single temporal segment per query, parameterized as a center point and duration in normalized [0,1] space, selected at inference as the proposal with the lowest masked-query reconstruction loss. Training requires only video-query relevance pairs—no precise temporal annotations—while evaluation uses ground-truth timestamps on three test splits (Test-Trivial, Novel-Composition, Novel-Word).

## Method

PC-Net is a proposal-centric pipeline with three key components. First, a dual-granularity proposal generator produces global proposals via a linear map of the global multimodal feature and local proposals via iterative slot attention over frame-level multimodal features, then fuses them through Hungarian matching with a learnable sigmoid-gated interpolation. Second, a proposal feature aggregator bridges the video-query semantic gap via a triplet contrastive loss (query vs. relevant vs. irrelevant frame features) and replaces the fixed Gaussian with a learnable peak-aware distributor that uses a sigmoid mask controlled by a learnable β to flatten the peak region, accommodating diverse action durations. Third, a quality margin regularizer partitions proposals into high- and low-relevance subsets using the reference (whole-video) reconstruction loss as a threshold, then applies inter-subset contrastive learning to reinforce co-occurring query-relevant visual elements and suppress spurious correlations. The total loss combines reconstruction, contrast, diversity, semantic alignment, and quality contrast terms, and the full model uses only 3.34M parameters on Charades-CG—54% fewer than the prior SOTA.

## Results

On Charades-CG, PC-Net achieves 54.84% R1@0.5 and 47.12% mIoU on Test-Trivial, 41.69% R1@0.5 and 38.04% mIoU on Novel-Composition, and 46.91% R1@0.5 and 41.06% mIoU on Novel-Word, using only 3.34M parameters. On ActivityNet-CG, it attains the best mIoU across all three splits (36.45%, 29.51%, 30.76%) with 4.97M parameters. Notably, PC-Net achieves 89.7% of the mIoU of the fully supervised SOTA QD-DETR_S (47.12% vs. 52.53% on Test-Trivial) while using 53% fewer parameters (3.34M vs. 7.12M).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PC-Net outperforms all weakly supervised baselines on Charades-CG across all three evaluation splits. On Test-Trivial, it surpasses CPL (53.04%) by 1.8% R1@0.5 and PPS (51.74%) by 3.1%, while also achieving the best R1@0.7 (26.68% vs. PPS's 25.87%). On Novel-Composition, PC-Net leads CPL by 0.9% R1@0.5 and 0.58% mIoU, and on Novel-Word it ties QMN at 46.91% R1@0.5 but substantially outperforms it on R1@0.7 (23.60% vs. 21.58%). On ActivityNet-CG, PC-Net achieves the best mIoU on all splits (36.45%, 29.51%, 30.76%) but trails PPS slightly on Test-Trivial R1@0.5 (29.62% vs. 30.00%), which the authors attribute to the dataset's long average video duration (117.6s) and sparse feature sampling. In terms of efficiency, PC-Net uses 54% fewer parameters than QD-DETR_S (3.34M vs. 7.12M) and 73% fewer than QMN (3.34M vs. 12.51M), while still matching or exceeding their performance on the compositional generalization splits.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the proposal-based weakly supervised VMR pipeline (generation → aggregation → quality assessment) established by CNM, CPL, and PPS, departing from them in three ways: replacing global-only proposal generation with a dual-granularity scheme (global linear mapping + local slot attention fused via Hungarian matching), substituting fixed Gaussian feature aggregation with a learnable peak-aware distributor augmented by cross-modal semantic alignment, and extending the simple best-vs-worst contrastive objective to a quality margin regularizer that stratifies all proposals into high/low relevance subsets. Conceptually, it also carves out a new task (WSCMR) by imposing compositional generalization evaluation on top of the existing weakly supervised VMR setting, a combination that prior work had not jointly addressed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The WSCMR task formulation is practically relevant because real-world video queries exhibit diverse syntactic structures and vocabulary that training queries do not cover, and the paper demonstrates that weakly supervised methods can approach fully supervised compositional retrieval performance (e.g., 41.69 vs. 50.23 R1@0.5 on Novel-Composition) while using 54% fewer parameters than the fully supervised SOTA. However, the gains over the strongest weakly supervised baseline (CPL) remain modest (~1–2 points), the method still depends on frozen I3D/C3D features that limit scalability to long or high-resolution videos, and the failure analysis reveals persistent boundary inaccuracy for long-duration actions. Open questions include whether the dual-granularity and peak-aware mechanisms transfer to stronger video encoders (e.g., SlowFast, Qwen-VL) and whether the quality margin regularizer generalizes beyond the 8-proposal setting used here.
