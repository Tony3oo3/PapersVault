## Problem

Model-based data filtering for LLM pretraining has been largely restricted to English, while state-of-the-art multilingual datasets like FineWeb-2 rely primarily on heuristic-based quality filters. This disparity risks widening the performance gap between English and non-English languages, as less than half of internet content is in English. The paper addresses this by developing a unified, transparent model-based filtering framework that identifies structured and knowledge-rich documents across diverse language families, scripts, and resource levels.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252109/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes web crawl documents from the FineWeb-2 dataset (already deduplicated and heuristic-filtered) and produces per-document quality scores used to threshold and retain a target percentage of documents (10% for high-resource languages like Chinese, German, French; 56–65% for lower-resource languages like Arabic, Danish). For the Transformer-based path, each document is reduced to its first 512 tokens and encoded into a 768-dimensional XLM-RoBERTa embedding via mean pooling; for the FastText path, documents are tokenized into 2-grams (4-grams for Chinese). The classifier training set pairs up to 80K positive samples drawn from five multilingual knowledge-rich sources (Aya Collection, Aya Dataset, MMLU, OpenAssistant-2, Include-Base-44) with an equal number of randomly sampled FineWeb-2 negatives, with separate training sets per language to prevent cross-language bias leakage.

## Method

The framework trains a binary quality classifier per language using two interchangeable backbones: (1) a FastText classifier with n-gram features, and (2) an XLM-RoBERTa base (279M parameters) embedding pipeline where document embeddings are scored either by a single-hidden-layer MLP (256 units, ReLU, 20% dropout, sigmoid output) or by maximum cosine similarity against K=8192 randomly sampled positive embeddings. The key design choice is framing quality as similarity to a curated multilingual knowledge collection (MKC+) of structured, knowledge-rich samples rather than relying on perplexity or LLM-based scoring, making the approach transparent, CPU-deployable, and scalable to web-scale corpora. The MLP MKC+ configuration consistently outperforms all other variants across five representative languages (Chinese, German, French, Arabic, Danish) and English, matching baseline MMLU performance with as little as 15% of training tokens while mitigating the curse of multilinguality in multilingual training settings.

## Results

On the multilingual evaluation across Chinese, German, French, Arabic, and Danish (1B-parameter Llama models, 70B/119B tokens), the best method MLP MKC+ achieves an average rank of 4.35, substantially outperforming the FineWeb-2 baseline (8.72) and all other filtering variants. With only 10% data retention for high-resource languages, the approach matches the baseline MMLU score while using as little as 15% of training tokens. On English validation (119B tokens, SmolLM tasks), MLP MKC+ achieves an average rank of 1.83, beating DCLM (2.39), FineWeb-Edu (2.44), and unfiltered FineWeb (3.33). The method also mitigates the curse of multilinguality: a multilingual model trained on the filtered data outperforms its monolingual counterpart on French, unlike the unfiltered baseline where the multilingual model underperforms.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the FineWeb-2 baseline (the current state-of-the-art multilingual web crawl dataset), MLP MKC+ achieves an average rank of 4.35 versus 8.72, a clear win across all five languages tested. On English validation, MLP MKC+ (average rank 1.83) outperforms DCLM (2.39) and FineWeb-Edu (2.44), both of which represent the prior state-of-the-art for English model-based filtering, as well as unfiltered FineWeb (3.33). Specifically on MMLU, the proposed method scores 0.340 versus DCLM's 0.316 and FineWeb-Edu's 0.347, while on ARC-Challenge it reaches 0.355 versus 0.353 (DCLM) and 0.385 (FineWeb-Edu). The FastText-based approach (FT MKC+, rank 7.17) is competitive with the baseline but consistently trails the Transformer embedding-based MLP method, suggesting that contextual embeddings capture quality signals that bag-of-words features miss. Notably, the cosine similarity approach (CS) underperforms both MLP and FastText across all training dataset configurations, indicating that learned classification boundaries are more effective than raw similarity matching for this task.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the English-only model-based data selection paradigm established by DCLM (FastText classifiers) and FineWeb-Edu (LLM-based scoring) to the multilingual setting. It departs from FineWeb-2's reliance on heuristic filters and from perplexity-based filtering (CCNet, Dolma) by instead using embedding-based classifiers (XLM-RoBERTa + MLP, and FastText) trained on curated knowledge-rich samples (MMLU, Aya Collection, Include-Base-44, OpenAssistant-2). The key conceptual move is treating multilingual quality filtering as a per-language binary classification problem over document embeddings, rather than relying on a single cross-lingual signal like perplexity or translation-based scoring.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete gap: model-based quality filtering has been the primary driver of English LLM data efficiency (FineWeb-Edu, DCLM) but was largely absent for non-English languages, risking widening the multilingual performance disparity. The released FineWeb2-HQ dataset (20 languages, ~400M documents) and the simple, CPU-friendly filtering pipeline lower the barrier for multilingual pretraining. Limitations include evaluation on 1B-parameter models with a single seed, use of only the first 512 tokens for classification, and coverage of only 20 languages; whether the approach generalizes to very low-resource languages or scales to 70B+ models remains open. The finding that quality filtering eliminates the curse of multilinguality (multilingual models outperforming monolingual ones at equal per-language token budgets) is intriguing but demonstrated on only five languages.
