## Problem

Unified Multimodal Generative Models (UMGMs) that jointly perform visual understanding and image generation suffer catastrophic forgetting during sequential instruction tuning, but prior continual learning work has only addressed intra-modal forgetting (degradation among text-output tasks). This paper identifies and formalizes the previously unexplored inter-modal forgetting—where tuning on understanding tasks degrades image generation quality—and attributes it to gradient conflict between modalities sharing the same transformer parameters.

## Input / Output

The model consumes interleaved sequences of image tokens (discretized via a VQ-VAE tokenizer) and text instruction tokens, processed by a frozen autoregressive transformer backbone. For understanding tasks it outputs text answer tokens; for generation tasks it autoregressively produces image tokens that are detokenized into RGB images. During continual tuning, a small reference set sampled from LAION-5B provides image-token logits for the knowledge distillation loss, anchoring the V-Adapter to the pre-trained generation distribution.

## Method

MoDE inserts two parameter-disjoint adapter modules into the frozen backbone's linear layers: a T-MoE (sparse Mixture-of-Experts LoRA with a learned router) applied exclusively to text tokens, and a V-Adapter (single LoRA) applied exclusively to image tokens. Because the two modules occupy disjoint parameter subspaces, the first-order cross-modal gradient term vanishes identically, provably bounding inter-modal loss drift to O(η²) rather than O(η) in modality-coupled designs. To further preserve generation fidelity, the V-Adapter is trained with an additional KL-divergence knowledge distillation loss against the frozen pre-trained model's image-token logits (softened with β=2.0, weighted by λ=0.3). Only the MoDE parameters (~0.02% of total) are updated during sequential instruction tuning, while the backbone remains frozen, keeping the approach lightweight and scalable across tasks.

## Results

On the Chameleon backbone, MoDE achieves the highest average accuracy (33.47%) and lowest FID (53.74) among all continual learning methods, closely matching the pre-trained zero-shot FID of 52.13. MoDE also attains the lowest average forgetting (25.99%) and the smallest overall performance drop Δ (22.78%), outperforming the next-best method (Model Tailor: 27.66% forgetting, 24.70 Δ). In the detailed per-task results (Table 2), MoDE sustains the best final accuracy across all five tasks (ScienceQA 56.25, TextVQA 19.08, ImageNet 14.67, GQA 33.10, VizWiz 44.28) while maintaining the lowest forgetting score of 25.99%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MoDE outperforms all five baselines on the combined objective of preserving image generation quality while improving multimodal understanding accuracy. Against Model Tailor [17], MoDE gains +0.85% accuracy (33.47 vs 32.62) and reduces FID by 1.73 (53.74 vs 55.47). Against DualPrompt [16], MoDE achieves +1.55% higher accuracy (33.47 vs 31.92) and a 2.34-point lower FID (53.74 vs 56.08); DualPrompt's seemingly low forgetting (6.82%) is misleading, as its best ImageNet accuracy is only 24.55% versus >70% for other methods. Against the MoE-based baselines MoE-LoRA [44] and CL-MoE [18], MoDE dramatically reduces inter-modal forgetting: FID drops from 65.16/65.87 to 53.74, while also achieving the highest accuracy (33.47 vs 33.01/32.86) and lowest forgetting (25.99 vs 30.77/30.95). Against the vanilla Seq LoRA baseline, MoDE improves accuracy by +5.04% and reduces FID by 2.38 points.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the LoRA + Mixture-of-Experts adapter paradigm for parameter-efficient continual learning (as in CL-MoE, MoE-LoRA, and Model Tailor) but departs from prior work in two key ways. First, it shifts the setting from MLLMs with text-only outputs to Unified Multimodal Generative Models (UMGMs) that produce both text and image tokens through a shared autoregressive backbone, exposing a previously unexamined failure mode: inter-modal forgetting where image generation degrades during understanding-task tuning. Second, it introduces modality-decoupled adapters (T-MoE for text, V-Adapter for images) rather than the modality-coupled shared experts used by prior MoE-based CL methods, combined with logit-level knowledge distillation to anchor the visual adapter to the pre-trained teacher.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification and formalization of inter-modal forgetting (via the modality gradient conflict definition and the O(η) vs O(η²) drift bound) gives the field a concrete diagnostic and design principle for building continual-learning-capable UMGMs. Practically, MoDE's lightweight design (~0.02% trainable parameters) makes it a viable drop-in for production unified models that must absorb new tasks over time without costly retraining. Limitations include evaluation restricted to autoregressive UMGMs (Chameleon, Janus-Pro) and a small number of tasks; open questions include whether the decoupling strategy extends to diffusion-based UMGMs, to more than two output modalities, and to settings where understanding and generation tasks are interleaved rather than sequential.
