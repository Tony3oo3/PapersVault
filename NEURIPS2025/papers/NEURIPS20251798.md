## Problem

Existing MoE training frameworks (ScheMoE, Tutel, FasterMoE, PipeMoE) pipeline only expert computing and all-to-all communication within the MoE layer, while neglecting MHA computing, gating, and all-reduce communication that together constitute 30–40% of per-iteration time. This leaves substantial communication-computation overlap unrealized, limiting the scalability of distributed MoE training on large GPU clusters.

## Input / Output

FlowMoE takes a standard transformer-based MoE model definition (with L blocks, each containing an MHA layer and an MoE layer with E experts), a text training dataset, and a GPU cluster topology as input. The input token tensor of shape B×N×M is partitioned into R equal-sized micro-batches for pipelining, and the all-reduce gradient tensor is further sliced into chunks of size S_p. The framework outputs trained model weights, an optimized per-iteration schedule of computing and communication subtasks, and a BO-tuned S_p value that balances overlap gain against communication startup overhead.

## Method

FlowMoE constructs a unified pipeline that consistently schedules four task types—MHA+gating computing (AT), expert computing (E), dispatch/combine A2A communication (D/C), and all-reduce (AR)—by partitioning the input into R micro-batches and defining explicit scheduling orders for both feed-forward and backward propagation (Eqs. 2–5). To overlap all-reduce with computing, FlowMoE introduces a priority-based communication pool: the all-reduce tensor is sliced into chunks of size S_p, and a COMMPOOLMANAGER loop always executes A2A tasks first, filling the remaining communication-resource gaps with all-reduce chunks. Theorem 1 proves that inserting all-reduce between any two A2A tasks is never worse than centralized end-of-backward scheduling, and Theorem 2 shows S_p→0 is optimal absent startup overhead. Because real hardware introduces per-chunk startup cost, FlowMoE uses Bayesian optimization (Gaussian Process with Matérn kernel, EI acquisition) over the first ~80 iterations to automatically find a near-optimal S_p without manual tuning. The entire framework is implemented atop Tutel in PyTorch, making it compatible with existing MoE training stacks.

## Results

On a 16-GPU cluster, FlowMoE achieves the lowest per-iteration time across all four real-world MoE models: 95.6 ms (GPT2-Tiny-MoE), 351.9 ms (BERT-Large-MoE), 1124.0 ms (LLaMA2-MoE), and 3205.3 ms (DeepSeek-V2-S). FlowMoE reduces training time by 13%–57%, energy consumption by 10%–39%, and memory usage by 7%–32% compared to state-of-the-art baselines. Across 675 customized MoE layer configurations, FlowMoE achieves a 26% average speedup over the closest competitor ScheMoE, and delivers 1.13×–1.82× speedup on real-world models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FlowMoE outperforms all five baselines on every model and cluster size tested. Against the closest competitor ScheMoE (EuroSys 2024), FlowMoE achieves 1.13×–1.31× speedup (14%–31% faster), with the largest gap on DeepSeek-V2-S (1.28× at 16 GPUs). Over Tutel (MLSys 2023), the speedup ranges from 1.20× to 1.57×, and over FasterMoE (PPoPP 2022) from 1.22× to 1.77×. FSMoE (ASPLOS 2025) is beaten by 1.15×–1.42×, and vanilla expert parallelism (FastMoE) by 1.53×–1.82×. The advantage is most pronounced on larger models (LLaMA2-MoE, DeepSeek-V2-S) where MHA computing and all-reduce communication constitute a larger fraction of iteration time, confirming that FlowMoE's unified pipeline scheduling of all task types (not just MoE-layer tasks) is the key differentiator.

## Relation to Prior Work

FlowMoE extends the line of pipeline scheduling work for distributed MoE training (ScheMoE, Tutel, FasterMoE, PipeMoE) that pipelines expert computing and A2A communication within the MoE layer. It departs from these by unifying all major transformer-block tasks—MHA computing, gating, expert computing, A2A communication, and all-reduce communication—into a single scheduling framework, and by introducing a tensor-chunk-based priority mechanism that lets all-reduce fill gaps between A2A tasks. The Bayesian optimization for auto-tuning the all-reduce partition size draws on standard BO methodology (Gaussian process regression with Matérn kernel, EI acquisition) but applies it to a novel scheduling knob specific to MoE training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By overlapping the previously-ignored 30–40% of iteration time (MHA, gating, all-reduce), FlowMoE delivers consistent 13–57% training-time reductions across models and cluster sizes, with a practical PyTorch implementation built atop Tutel/DeepSpeed that requires no manual hyperparameter tuning. Limitations include evaluation on at most 16 GPUs with 100 Gbps interconnect, where bandwidth-limited scaling diminishes the scheduling benefit, and the framework does not yet compose with tensor or pipeline parallelism. Open questions include how the unified scheduling interacts with multi-dimensional parallelism strategies at hundreds-to-thousands of GPUs, and whether the BO-tuned partition size remains stable under dynamic hardware conditions such as node failures or heterogeneous interconnects.
