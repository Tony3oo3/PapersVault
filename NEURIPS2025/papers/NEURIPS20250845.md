## Problem

Unsupervised anomaly detection systems deployed in high-stakes domains (finance, healthcare, criminal justice) can produce biased detection rates across demographic groups, yet the feasibility conditions for group fairness in the unsupervised setting remain unclear, existing fairness-aware methods sacrifice detection accuracy, and no threshold-free fairness metric exists for holistic evaluation. This paper formalizes the necessary assumptions under which group fairness is achievable without anomaly labels, and proposes a method that simultaneously optimizes detection and fairness without explicit fairness regularization.

## Input / Output

The method consumes only normal (non-anomalous) training samples—tabular vectors or 64×64×3 RGB face images—along with a sensitive attribute label (e.g., gender, race, age) that partitions the data into protected groups. At inference, a new sample is projected into the learned target space and its L2 norm from the origin serves as a continuous anomaly score; a threshold derived from training-set score percentiles converts this into a binary normal/anomalous decision. The target distribution is a truncated isotropic Gaussian, so the score directly corresponds to a density estimate under that distribution.

## Method

FairAD learns two networks: a projector h_φ mapping inputs into a low-dimensional target space and a reconstructor g_ψ mapping back to the original space. The key insight is that by minimizing the Sinkhorn (optimal-transport) distance between each demographic group's projected data and a shared truncated isotropic Gaussian target, group fairness is achieved implicitly—no adversarial training or fairness penalty is needed—because all groups are forced onto the same compact distribution. The reconstruction term (MSE) preserves task-relevant information so the projected space remains discriminative for anomaly detection. Two variants are offered: Im-FairAD, where fairness emerges purely from the shared-target objective, and Ex-FairAD, which adds an explicit Sinkhorn-distance term between per-group anomaly-score distributions. The anomaly score at test time is simply ‖h_φ(x)‖, and a novel threshold-free fairness metric (ADPD) averages the demographic-parity gap across all possible score thresholds to provide a holistic fairness assessment.

## Results

On COMPAS (balanced splitting), Im-FairAD achieves 63.89% AUC with 4.16% ADPD (normal) and 4.76% ADPD (all), attaining the best accuracy–fairness trade-off among all nine compared methods. Under skewed splitting, Im-FairAD maintains 66.58% AUC with only 4.90% ADPD (normal), whereas CFAD degrades to 23.81% ADPD. On Credit (balanced, p=0.90), Im-FairAD reaches 54.32% F1 with 0.81 fairness ratio (normal), substantially outperforming the next-best baseline (FairOD at 41.00% F1). Across all six datasets, Im-FairAD consistently dominates the AUC–ADPD Pareto frontier, and its fairness is robust to demographic imbalance (slight ADPD fluctuation between balanced and skewed splits).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Im-FairAD and Ex-FairAD achieve the best AUC–ADPD trade-off across all six datasets under both balanced and skewed splitting. Against the fairness-unaware Deep SVDD, Im-FairAD improves AUC by 6.3 pp (63.89 vs 57.58) on COMPAS-balanced while reducing ADPD (normal) from 24.33% to 4.16%. Against the adversarial Deep Fair SVDD, Im-FairAD gains 1.2 pp AUC and cuts ADPD (normal) by 6.1 pp (4.16 vs 10.25). Against the counterfactual CFAD, Im-FairAD achieves comparable AUC (63.89 vs 62.27) but halves ADPD (4.76 vs 10.94 on all data). Two-stage pipelines (FarconVAE+LOF, FarconVAE+Deep SVDD) attain low ADPD (~4–5%) but sacrifice detection accuracy, dropping AUC to 48–55%. Under skewed splitting, Im-FairAD preserves fairness (ADPD 4.90%) while CFAD degrades severely (ADPD 23.81%) and Deep SVDD worsens to 31.72%, confirming the robustness of the shared-target-distribution approach to demographic imbalance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the distribution-transformation framework for anomaly detection (from the same first author's semi-supervised work, Xiao et al. 2025) to the unsupervised setting, repurposing the projection-to-target-distribution idea as an implicit fairness mechanism: by mapping all demographic groups to a shared truncated Gaussian, group fairness emerges without explicit fairness regularization. It departs from prior fairness-aware AD methods (Deep Fair SVDD, FairOD, CFAD) that rely on adversarial training, fairness penalty terms, or counterfactual sample generation, and from two-stage FRL+AD pipelines, by proposing a single end-to-end objective combining Sinkhorn distance and reconstruction loss. The theoretical contribution (Claim 1, Assumptions 1–3) clarifies that standard fairness definitions (demographic parity, equal opportunity) are not meaningfully guaranteeable in unsupervised AD without additional structural assumptions, a point the authors argue prior work has not addressed explicitly.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper fills a genuine gap in the fairness-in-AD literature by providing the first systematic theoretical analysis of what fairness is achievable in the unsupervised regime and by introducing a threshold-free evaluation metric (ADPD) that removes the arbitrary threshold sensitivity of prior fairness ratios. However, the practical impact is bounded: experiments cover only six small-to-medium datasets (five tabular, one low-res image), the method requires the sensitive attribute at training time, and the key assumptions (transferable fairness, generalizable parity) are difficult to verify in deployment. Open questions include whether the shared-target-distribution approach scales to high-dimensional or streaming data, how it handles intersectional (multi-attribute) fairness beyond the simple extension sketched in Appendix C, and whether the Sinkhorn-based objective remains tractable at larger sample sizes.
