## Problem

Diffusion model inference is bottlenecked by the sequential invocation of the noise predictor across dozens to hundreds of timesteps. Existing parallelization strategies (AsyncDiff, PipeFusion, xDiT/Ring Attention) rely on layer-wise or stage-wise communication that incurs substantial overhead, rendering them impractical on commercial hardware connected via PCIe Gen3. The paper addresses the gap of achieving significant speedup with minimal, step-level communication suitable for bandwidth-constrained environments.

## Input / Output

The method operates on the same inputs and outputs as the underlying diffusion or flow-matching model (text prompts, optional reference images for image-to-video, and initial Gaussian noise), producing generated images (e.g., 1440×1440 for SD3), videos (e.g., 45 frames at 512×720 for CogVideoX-2b, 14 frames at 1024×576 for SVD), or audio (AudioLDM2-large). The key structural detail is that the full noise predictor (DiT or U-Net) is replicated on each of the p GPUs, and only the predicted noise tensors and noisy samples are exchanged between devices—no intermediate feature maps or attention keys/values are communicated. For the single-device BatchStep variant, adjacent-step predictions are packed into a single batch on one GPU, exploiting the favorable batching effect observed in audio diffusion models.

## Method

ParaStep introduces a reuse-then-predict mechanism: instead of directly reusing the previous step's noise (which causes quality degradation), it reuses that noise to compute an approximate noisy sample for the current step, then runs the noise predictor on this sample to obtain a refined noise estimate. This is parallelized in a round-based schedule across p GPUs, where each GPU performs exactly one forward pass of the noise predictor per cycle of p denoising steps, and only p−1 noise tensors plus one sample broadcast are communicated per cycle. The communication volume per step is 2(p−1)M/p, orders of magnitude lower than Ring Attention's 2L(p−1)M or AsyncDiff's p(p−1)M. A warm-up phase of several initial steps runs sequentially to avoid error accumulation when adjacent-step differences are large. A single-device variant, BatchStep, converts the parallel schedule into batched inference for non-compute-intensive models like AudioLDM2, eliminating inter-device communication entirely while achieving comparable speedup.

## Results

ParaStep achieves end-to-end speedups of up to 3.88× on SVD (p=4), 2.43× on CogVideoX-2b (p=4), and 6.56× on AudioLDM2-large (p=8), while maintaining generation quality close to the original model. On SD3 (1440×1440, T=50), ParaStep(p=2) achieves a 1.68× speedup with FID of 5.01, outperforming AsyncDiff(p=2) (1.61×, FID 7.11) and xDiT-Pipe(p=2) (1.50×, FID 6.20). On CogVideoX-2b, ParaStep(p=4) reaches 1.93× speedup with VBench 76.74, while xDiT-Ring(p=4) is actually slower than the original model (0.98×) due to high communication overhead on PCIe Gen3.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ParaStep consistently outperforms AsyncDiff on both speedup and generation quality: on SVD(p=4), ParaStep achieves 2.49× speedup vs. 1.80× with PSNR 26.99 vs. 23.13; on SD3(p=2), ParaStep reaches 1.68× vs. 1.61× with FID 5.01 vs. 7.11. Against xDiT-Pipe (PipeFusion) on SD3, ParaStep wins on both speedup (1.68× vs. 1.50×) and all quality metrics (FID 5.01 vs. 6.20, PSNR 18.61 vs. 16.68). Against xDiT-Ring (Ring Attention) on video models, ParaStep achieves substantially higher speedup (e.g., 1.93× vs. 0.98× on CogVideoX-2b at p=4), while xDiT-Ring is actually slower than the original model due to prohibitive communication costs on PCIe Gen3; however, xDiT-Ring retains a slight quality edge (e.g., PSNR 32.24 vs. 37.57 on CogVideoX p=2, where ParaStep actually wins). Against TeaCache, ParaStep with the TeaCache schedule (ParaStep-fast) achieves PSNR 25.59 vs. TeaCache-fast's 24.42 at comparable latency (37.27s vs. 36.19s). Against DeepCache on SVD, ParaStep(p=2) achieves PSNR 32.86 vs. DeepCache(s=2)'s 32.72 at similar latency (30.61s vs. 36.42s), and ParaStep(p=4) reaches PSNR 24.99 vs. DeepCache(s=4)'s 26.49 at 20.36s vs. 26.90s, showing ParaStep maintains better quality at higher speedups.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the observation (shared with TeaCache and caching-based methods) that adjacent denoising steps in diffusion models produce highly similar noise predictions, but reframes the problem from 'when to reuse' to 'how to reuse' via a reuse-then-predict mechanism that refines the reused noise through a fresh predictor pass. It departs sharply from prior parallelization strategies (AsyncDiff, PipeFusion, xDiT/Ring Attention, DistriFusion) that rely on layer-wise or stage-wise communication, instead reducing communication to a single step-wise exchange of noise and sample tensors per denoising step. The single-device BatchStep variant repurposes the parallel scheduling logic into batched inference, extending the method to non-compute-intensive models like audio diffusion where multi-GPU deployment is unnecessary.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ParaStep makes diffusion parallelization practical on commercial multi-GPU hardware connected via PCIe Gen3, where prior methods (xDiT-Ring, AsyncDiff) provide little to no speedup due to communication overhead. The performance breakdown showing 0.10s vs 8.59s communication latency on SVD underscores a fundamental shift in what interconnects are needed for effective parallelism. Key limitations include the requirement for additional GPUs (trading compute for latency), full replication of the noise predictor (no per-GPU memory reduction), and a model-specific warm-up step hyperparameter that requires tuning. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to very long video sequences, different noise schedules, or autoregressive diffusion variants where step similarity may break down.
