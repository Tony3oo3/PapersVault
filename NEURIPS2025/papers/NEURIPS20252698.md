## Problem

Consistency models enable few-step generation on Euclidean domains but have not been extended to Riemannian manifolds, where the curved geometry prevents simple linear interpolation in the consistency parameterization and requires the vector field to respect tangent-space constraints. This gap limits fast generative modeling on manifolds critical to applications like protein design (SO(3) orientations, toroidal torsion angles) and geoscience (spherical data).

## Input / Output

The model takes a random noise point x_0 on a Riemannian manifold (2-sphere, flat torus T^n, or SO(3) in 3-vector/axis-angle representation) and produces a generated data sample on the same manifold in 1–2 function evaluations. Internally, the network predicts a tangent-space vector field v_θ(x_t, t) ∈ T_{x_t}M at an intermediate noisy point x_t, and the consistency parameterization maps this back to the manifold via the exponential map exp_{x_t}(κ_t v_θ). The geodesic distance d_g replaces the Euclidean norm in the consistency loss, and all vector operations (addition, inner product) are performed in the tangent space with the Riemannian metric.

## Method

RCM introduces an exponential-map-based consistency parameterization f_θ(x_t,t) = exp_{x_t}(κ_t v_θ(x_t,t)) that guarantees manifold constraint, and derives a closed-form continuous-time loss using the covariant derivative ∇_{ẋ}v (Levi-Civita connection) to correctly compute the time derivative of the vector field on curved geometry. The loss involves the differentials of the exponential map d(exp_x)_u and d(exp_u)_x, which capture how perturbations in the tangent vector and base point propagate to the manifold point. The paper proves theoretical equivalence between Riemannian consistency distillation (RCD, using a pre-trained RFM teacher for the marginal vector field) and Riemannian consistency training (RCT, using the conditional vector field with marginalization), and further proposes a simplified loss that eliminates the need to compute exponential-map differentials by approximating d(exp_x)_u ≈ d(exp_u)_x. A kinematics interpretation decomposes the RCM objective into three components: vector-field difference, intrinsic vector-field change, and extrinsic geometric change from the manifold's curvature.

## Results

In the 2-step generation setting, RCM variants dramatically outperform vanilla 2-step Riemannian flow matching across all manifolds. On the 2-sphere Flood dataset, sRCD achieves a KLD of 2.27 versus 8.17 for RFM-2, approaching the 100-step RFM-100 baseline (1.33). On SO(3), RCD achieves MMD of 1.47 on Swiss Roll versus 19.64 for RFM-2, while on the flat torus Protein dataset, RCD achieves MMD of 3.84 versus 15.19 for RFM-2. The simplified loss (sRCD/sRCT) matches or slightly exceeds the full RCD/RCT in most settings, confirming that the exponential-map differentials can be safely omitted.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the 100-step RFM baseline, RCM cannot match the quality (as expected with only 2 steps), but the gap is small: e.g., sRCD achieves KLD 2.13 vs. RFM-100's 1.51 on Earthquake. Against the 2-step RFM baseline, RCM variants win decisively by 3–15× in KLD/MMD (e.g., 2.13 vs. 10.99 on Earthquake, 1.47 vs. 19.64 on SO(3) Swiss Roll). Against the naive Euclidean consistency model (CD_naive/CT_naive), RCM consistently wins, with the gap being largest on SO(3) (e.g., RCD 1.47 vs. CD_naive 2.75 on Swiss Roll) and the flat torus (RCD 3.84 vs. CD_naive 8.40 on Protein), confirming that ignoring the manifold geometry is costly. Notably, on the SO(3) Cone dataset, RCT (3.78) outperforms RCD (6.30), suggesting that when the pre-trained teacher is weak, consistency training from the conditional vector field is more robust. The simplified loss (sRCD/sRCT) matches or slightly beats the full RCD/RCT in most settings, validating the approximation d(exp_x)_u ≈ d(exp_u)_x.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Euclidean consistency model framework (Song et al. 2023) to Riemannian manifolds by replacing linear interpolation with exponential-map-based parameterization and the standard time derivative with the covariant derivative. It builds directly on Riemannian flow matching (Chen & Lipman 2023) for the underlying geodesic interpolation and conditional vector field, and adopts the vector-field parameterization and continuous-time formulation from Yang et al. (2024) and Lu & Song (2024). The key departure is the rigorous treatment of manifold geometry: the paper proves the RCD-to-RCT equivalence (analogous to the Euclidean CD/CT result) using Riemannian marginalization, and introduces a simplified loss that avoids computing differentials of the exponential map while preserving optimality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Few-step generation on Riemannian manifolds is a practical bottleneck for applications like protein backbone generation (SO(3) orientations, toroidal torsion angles) and geoscience, where existing Riemannian flow matching requires 200–1000 sampling steps. The paper's theoretical framework (closed-form continuous-time loss, RCD/RCT equivalence, kinematics interpretation) provides a principled foundation that should generalize to other Lie groups and symmetric spaces. However, experiments are limited to small-scale datasets (≤166k samples) and low-dimensional manifolds (≤128D tori), leaving open whether the approach scales to full protein generation or higher-curvature geometries. The simplified loss's reliance on the approximation d(exp_x)_u ≈ d(exp_u)_x is well-motivated for flat and symmetric manifolds but its accuracy on general curved manifolds remains an open question.
