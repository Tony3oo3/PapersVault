## Problem

Existing diffusion-based 3D reconstruction methods either directly learn the posterior (requiring images during training, fixed view counts, and retraining for new modalities) or use Diffusion Posterior Sampling with heuristic fixed step sizes for the likelihood update. Because the 3D rendering operator is non-linear and its adjoint is intractable, analytical step-size determination is impossible, causing slow convergence and suboptimal reconstruction quality in prior DPS-based approaches.

## Input / Output

The method accepts single-view or multi-view RGB images at 224×224 resolution (or depth maps) along with known camera parameters, and produces colored point clouds of 8,192 points. The forward operator R is a differentiable PyTorch3D rasterizer that projects points via alpha compositing (for color) or inverse-square depth weighting, and can be swapped between modalities without any retraining. For ShapeNet, 2,048 points are initially sampled and upsampled to 8,192; for CO3D, 8,192 points are sampled directly from COLMAP reconstructions.

## Method

The paper trains a Diffusion Transformer prior (from Point-E) on unlabeled colored point clouds via standard DDPM, then at inference runs DDIM sampling with a novel Forward Curvature-Matching (FCM) likelihood update at each step. FCM estimates directional curvature using a scale-adaptive probe δ_k = δ_0·‖x_k‖/‖g_k‖ and a single forward-difference gradient evaluation—requiring no adjoint of the renderer—then computes a Barzilai-Borwein-inspired step size capped at 1/L and validated by a single Armijo backtracking check. This yields a fixed cost of exactly 2 backward and 3 forward passes per FCM iteration (4 iterations per DDIM step), with theoretical guarantees of monotone loss decrease and contraction preservation of the DDIM operator. The key architectural insight is that the learned prior and the measurement operator are fully decoupled: changing from RGB to depth, or from single-view to multi-view, only requires substituting the forward operator R, not retraining any model.

## Results

On ShapeNet (single-view, 256 NFEs), the method achieves an average EMD of 0.587×10⁻¹, CD of 0.561×10⁻¹, and F-score of 0.382, outperforming PC² (0.647, 0.574, 0.310) and BDM (0.648, 0.588, 0.343) across all three metrics. On the real-world CO3D dataset, the method reduces EMD from 2.662 to 1.206 and F-score improves from 0.244 to 0.281 compared to PC², while using only 256 NFEs versus PC²'s 1000. Inference time is 6.03 s/sample, a 32% speedup over PC² (8.88 s) and BDM (10.56 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two baselines: PC² (Melas-Kyriazi et al., CVPR 2023), which directly learns the posterior score with projection conditioning, and BDM (Xu et al., CVPR 2024), which factorizes reconstruction into a learned prior score and a learned posterior score. On ShapeNet, the proposed FCM method wins on all three metrics at matched NFEs: EMD improves by 9.3% over PC² (0.587 vs 0.647) and 9.4% over BDM (0.587 vs 0.648); F-score improves by 23.5% over PC² (0.382 vs 0.310) and 11.4% over BDM (0.382 vs 0.343). On CO3D, the gains are even more pronounced, with EMD halved (1.206 vs 2.662) and F-score up 15% (0.281 vs 0.244) relative to PC². The method also runs 32% faster than PC² and 43% faster than BDM, using only 256 NFEs versus 1000 and 1080 respectively. A key practical advantage is modality flexibility: the same trained prior supports single-view, multi-view, and depth-map inputs without retraining, whereas PC² and BDM require images during training and are limited to fixed input configurations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Diffusion Posterior Sampling (DPS) framework [Chung et al., ICLR 2023] to 3D point cloud reconstruction, building directly on GSD [Mu et al., ECCV 2024], which first applied DPS with Gaussian splatting to view-guided 3D generation but relied on heuristic fixed step sizes for the likelihood update. The key departure is the introduction of Forward Curvature-Matching (FCM), which replaces those fixed steps with a Barzilai-Borwein-inspired adaptive step size computed via forward-only finite-difference curvature probes, thereby avoiding the intractable adjoint of the neural renderer. The diffusion prior itself follows the Point-E [Nichol et al., 2022] architecture (a Diffusion Transformer trained on unlabeled point clouds), and the DDIM sampler [Song et al., ICLR 2021] provides the base sampling loop, positioning the contribution squarely at the intersection of diffusion sampling and classical line-search optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The modality-agnostic design—swapping the forward operator (RGB renderer, depth map, multi-view average) without retraining—addresses a practical pain point in 3D reconstruction pipelines where each new measurement type previously required a dedicated trained posterior. The 4× reduction in NFEs (256 vs. 1000–1080) while improving F-score and EMD over PC² and BDM makes the approach attractive for latency-sensitive applications. However, limitations remain: thin structures are under-represented due to rasterizer radius, ShapeNet evaluation relies on KeypointNet's part-based colors rather than true mesh colors, and no error bars or multi-seed statistics are reported. Open questions include whether FCM transfers cleanly to other 3D representations (Gaussian splatting, implicit surfaces) and whether the local-convexity assumption holds for more complex, real-world scenes beyond the four ShapeNet categories and two CO3D categories tested.
