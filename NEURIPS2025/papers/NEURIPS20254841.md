## Problem

Existing Denoising Diffusion Bridge Models (DDBMs) for distribution translation require both source and target distributions to share the same dimensionality and rely on modality-specific architectures like U-Nets, making them inapplicable to general modality translation between heterogeneous data types (e.g., 2D images to 3D shapes, low-res to high-res images, faces to voices). No unified, theoretically grounded framework existed that could bridge arbitrary modality pairs without restrictive assumptions on dimensionality, Gaussian priors, or hand-crafted inductive biases.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254841/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework accepts paired samples from two modalities of potentially different dimensionalities (k ≠ s), such as four 224×224 multi-view images paired with a 32×32×32 3D voxel shape, a 16×16 low-resolution image paired with a 128×128 high-resolution image, or a face image paired with a voice waveform. Modality-specific encoders project both inputs into a shared latent token sequence of the same embedding dimension d, and a modality-specific decoder reconstructs the target from the predicted latent. At inference, only the source modality is provided; the model autoregressively denoises from z_T to z_0 via Heun sampling (40 steps) and decodes to the target modality.

## Method

LDDBM extends DDBMs to a shared latent space by encoding source and target into common latent representations, then learning a diffusion bridge between them using a novel Distribution Translation DiT (DTDiT) — an encoder-decoder transformer where the encoder processes source tokens into a memory embedding consumed via cross-attention in the decoder (rather than concatenation as in the original DDBM), and learnable [MASK] tokens with shared positional encodings enforce spatial alignment between input and output tokens. Training combines three losses: a score-matching bridge loss (VE scheme), a predictive loss that compares the full encode-bridge-decode output to the ground-truth target in pixel space, and an InfoNCE contrastive loss that pulls paired latent embeddings together and pushes unpaired ones apart. An iterative training scheme alternates between optimizing the autoencoder reconstruction and the bridge alignment to resolve the adversarial tension between shifting latent distributions and a fixed bridge, yielding better stability than fully end-to-end or two-phase training.

## Results

On the primary Multiview-to-3D-Shape task (ShapeNet), LDDBM achieves the best 1-NNA of 0.508 and IoU of 0.664, outperforming all general-purpose baselines (EDM, 3D-EDM, DiT, SiT). On zero-shot super-resolution (CelebA-HQ, 16×16→128×128), LDDBM attains PSNR 25.6, SSIM 0.68, and LPIPS 0.32, leading all compared methods. On nuScenes scene occupancy generation, LDDBM achieves the best 1-NNA (0.807) and IoU (0.233). For cross-modal biometric matching, LDDBM reaches 71.2% (Face→Voice) and 75.1% (Voice→Face) accuracy, surpassing the general-purpose SiT baseline by ~5–7 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LDDBM consistently outperforms all general-purpose diffusion baselines (EDM, 3D-EDM, DiT, SiT) across every evaluated task and metric. On ShapeNet, it improves 1-NNA by 0.024–0.067 and IoU by 0.031–0.062 over the strongest general baseline (EDM). On CelebA-HQ super-resolution, LDDBM beats the task-specific DiWa method by 2.3 dB PSNR, 0.03 SSIM, and 0.07 LPIPS. On the Edges→Bags image-to-image task, LDDBM trades a modest FID increase (4.17 vs. DDBM's 2.93) for more than 2× faster inference (7.8 s vs. 16.9 s). The only clear losses are against task-specific SOTA methods: Pix2Vox-A achieves higher IoU (0.697 vs. 0.664) on ShapeNet, and the biometric-matching method of Nagrani et al. [40] reaches 79.5%/81.0% accuracy versus LDDBM's 71.2%/75.1% on face-voice tasks, which the authors attribute to domain-specific inductive biases absent from their general framework.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Denoising Diffusion Bridge Models (DDBMs), which translate between paired distributions of identical dimensionality via a Doob h-transform, into a latent-space formulation that bridges arbitrary modalities of different dimensionalities through modality-specific encoders and decoders. The denoiser architecture draws on the encoder-decoder Transformer lineage (motivated by NLP sequence-to-sequence work) rather than the U-Net or decoder-only DiT designs common in diffusion, and the training objective augments the standard score-matching bridge loss with a CLIP-inspired InfoNCE contrastive term and an end-to-end predictive reconstruction loss. The paper explicitly differentiates itself from concurrent flow-matching approaches (CrossFlow, FlowTok) and from DPBridge, which remains limited to image-to-image translation with a pre-trained Stable Diffusion backbone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By providing a single, modality-agnostic framework that handles 2D-to-3D shape generation, super-resolution, scene occupancy, and cross-modal biometric matching without task-specific inductive biases, LDDBM establishes a practical baseline for general MT that practitioners can adapt to new modality pairs with only encoder/decoder changes. The paper acknowledges that it still trails task-specific SOTA (Pix2Vox, DiWa, [40]) and requires paired training data, leaving unpaired translation and scaling to sequential/high-dimensional data (video, volumetric) as open directions. The iterative training scheme that alternates reconstruction and bridge optimization—motivated by an adversarial-like conflict between encoders and the bridge—offers a practical recipe that may transfer to other latent diffusion settings.
