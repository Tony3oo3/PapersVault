## Problem

Existing camouflaged object detection methods rely on discriminative segmentation with predefined categories, limiting generalization to unseen objects, while current LVLM benchmarks target natural scenes with salient objects and lack coverage of camouflage scenarios. No dedicated multimodal benchmark exists to systematically evaluate how well LVLMs perceive, reason about, and segment camouflaged objects, and the labor-intensive process of collecting camouflage imagery has kept the field's datasets small (COD10K has only 5,786 images).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251028/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark consumes RGB camouflage images (10,511 from COD10K, NC4K, CHAMELEON, CAMO) and natural images (10,553 from DUTS, 1,473 landscape) paired with text prompts. The five tasks produce distinct outputs: CAC yields structured captions spanning seven dimensions (quantity, category, color, size, position, strategy, purpose); VQA and MCQ produce fine-grained answers with explanations; IGS outputs text responses containing [SEG] tokens that trigger pixel-level instance or part masks; and CEA assigns a 5-level camouflage category (Exposed through Imperceptible), a 0–100 quantitative score, and a textual explanation of object-background relationships. The CEA task uniquely accepts any image—camouflaged or not—making it applicable to automated screening of large-scale natural image databases.

## Method

MMCSBench is constructed through a multi-stage annotation pipeline: three expert annotators manually write fine-grained attribute captions for 10,511 images, GPT-4V generates 3 VQA pairs and 3 MCQ sets per image from those captions, and five camouflage experts provide ground-truth answers after rigorous filtering. The novel CEA scoring formula computes a weighted sum (α=0.5, β=0.3, γ=0.2) of HSV color histogram similarity, LBP texture divergence, and CIELab edge-fusion similarity between foreground and background, then discretizes the 0–100 score into five levels to avoid regression difficulty for LVLMs. For evaluation, 26 LVLMs are fine-tuned via LoRA on 1% of the benchmark (balanced across CEA levels) and tested on the remaining 99%, enabling assessment of generalization to unseen categories. The proposed CamoVLM baseline introduces a lookup-matching strategy that generates tailored attention masks for precise instruction-following and grounding segmentation in camouflage scenes, outperforming LISA, GeoPixel, GlaMM, and SegLLM on the IGS task.

## Results

On MMCSBench, fine-tuned LVLMs substantially outperform zero-shot models across all five tasks. Among fine-tuned models, InternVL3-78B achieves the best VQA BLEU (26.32) and CEA accuracy (76.94%) with the lowest CEA MAE (4.98), while LLAVA-OV-72B leads in MCQ accuracy (83.45%) and CEA BLEU (22.07). NVLM-D-72B attains the highest CAC BLEU (21.65). Even the best models remain well below human evaluators (85.32% MCQ Acc, 79.56% CEA Acc, 3.62 CEA MAE), confirming that camouflage scene understanding remains a significant open challenge for LVLMs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among zero-shot LVLMs, Gemini2.5-Pro outperforms GPT-4.1 on all text-generation metrics (e.g., CAC BLEU 14.82 vs. 12.39; CEA ACC 66.88% vs. 62.51%), but both are far surpassed by even small fine-tuned models like InternVL3-2B (CAC BLEU 17.06) and Qwen2.5-VL-3B (CEA ACC 69.34%). In the 7–9B tier, LLAVA1.5-7B and LLAVA-NEXT-8B lead on generation quality (VQA BLEU ≈21.3) and MCQ accuracy (77.3–77.6%), while Qwen2.5-VL-7B achieves the best CEA MAE (6.32) in that size range. At 32–38B scale, InternVL3-38B dominates VQA (BLEU 25.17) and LLAVA1.6-34B leads CAC (BLEU 20.31) and MCQ accuracy (81.23%). Among the largest models (72–78B), InternVL3-78B achieves the best VQA BLEU (26.32), CEA accuracy (76.94%), and CEA MAE (4.98); LLAVA-OV-72B edges it out on MCQ accuracy (83.45% vs. 83.37%) and CEA BLEU (22.07 vs. 21.52); NVLM-D-72B leads CAC BLEU (21.65). For the IGS grounding segmentation task, the proposed CamoVLM (Qwen2.5-VL-7B backbone) outperforms all five baselines—LISA (mIoU 60.08), PixelLM (60.27), GeoPixel (64.26), GlaMM (62.05), and SegLLM (62.80)—achieving 65.81 mIoU and 52.88 Recall, with the largest margin over GeoPixel (+1.55 mIoU, +2.36 Recall).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the long line of camouflaged object detection (COD) benchmarks (COD10K, NC4K, CHAMELEON, CAMO) from purely discriminative, pixel-level segmentation to a multimodal understanding framework built around Large Vision-Language Models. The CEA scoring mechanism is directly derived from Lamdouar et al.'s feature-similarity framework for camouflage quantification, while the grounding segmentation task reuses the [SEG] token extraction paradigm from LISA and GeoPixel. Conceptually, the paper departs from the COD community's reliance on predefined categories and manual annotation by leveraging LVLMs' generative flexibility to produce fine-grained textual reasoning about *why* an object is camouflaged, not just *where* it is.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MMCSBench fills a clear gap: no prior benchmark evaluated LVLMs on camouflage scenes, and the CEA task offers a practical pathway to automate the labor-intensive collection of camouflage imagery (demonstrated by the 50K+ images harvested from COCO, WildFish, iNat, and AK). The persistent gap between the best fine-tuned model (InternVL3-78B) and human evaluators (e.g., 76.94% vs. 79.56% CEA accuracy) underscores that camouflage remains a hard perceptual regime even for frontier LVLMs. Open questions include whether incorporating camouflage data into pretraining (rather than fine-tuning) would close this gap, whether the hand-crafted CEA scoring formula (weighted HSV/LBP/edge similarities) is the right quantitative target, and how well these capabilities transfer to synthetic or adversarial camouflage beyond natural biological examples.
