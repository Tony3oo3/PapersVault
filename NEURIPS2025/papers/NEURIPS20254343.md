## Problem

Existing community detection results for random graphs are restricted to fully symmetric settings (n₁=n₂, k₁=k₂) or to non-bipartite graphs, leaving the bipartite case with potentially very different vertex set sizes (n₁≠n₂, k₁≠k₂) uncharacterized. This paper fills that gap by deriving non-asymptotic, matching upper and lower bounds on the minimax separation rate δ* for detecting a planted dense bipartite subgraph, valid for all configurations of n₁, n₂, k₁, k₂, and revealing new phase transitions absent in the symmetric literature.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an n₁×n₂ bipartite adjacency matrix A with independent Bernoulli(p) entries, along with the known baseline probability p₀ and community dimensions k₁, k₂. It produces a binary hypothesis test decision (reject H₀: Erdős–Rényi vs. H₁: planted community) and characterizes the critical signal strength δ* = inf{δ : minimax risk ≤ η}. The rate δ*² ∝ p₀(1−p₀)·R where R is a piecewise expression involving ψ, φ, and ν terms that switch depending on the relative magnitudes of n₁/k₁², n₂/k₂², and log-combinatorial factors, yielding distinct detection regimes.

## Method

The optimal test Δ* is a case-wise combination of three procedures selected by which term dominates in the rate R: a total degree test, a truncated degree test, and a max truncated degree test. The two novel tests replace the square function in the classical truncated χ² statistic with the Bennett function h_B(x)=(1+x)log(1+x)−x, which grows sub-quadratically in the large-deviation regime and thus tames the sub-Poissonian tails of binomial sums. The truncated degree test sums Bennett-transformed column degrees with a hard threshold, while the max truncated degree test scans over all C(n₁,k₁) row subsets of size k₁, applies the Bennett transform to each subset's column sums, truncates, and takes the maximum. The lower bound is established via a refined second-moment method controlling the MGF of products of binomial variables, and the upper bound requires Assumption 1 (a lower bound on p₀ ensuring a quasi-normal moderate-deviation regime) to control Type I and Type II errors of the nonlinear statistics.

## Results

The paper establishes that the minimax separation rate satisfies (δ*)² ≍ p₀(1−p₀)R, where R = (ψ₁₂ + ψ₂₁) ∧ φ₁₂ ∧ φ₂₁, with matching non-asymptotic upper and lower bounds for all configurations of n₁, n₂, k₁, k₂. The lower bound (Theorem 1) holds unconditionally via a second-moment argument, while the upper bound (Theorem 2) is achieved by a novel combination of a total degree test, a truncated degree test, and a max truncated degree test under a graph density assumption (Assumption 1). A key new finding is a phase transition at (n₁k₂/k₁²)log(n₂) ≍ 1 in the imbalanced bipartite regime (Proposition 3), which does not appear in the balanced non-bipartite setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's theoretical results are compared to two prior lines of work. Against Arias-Castro and Verzelen [4, 59], who study community detection in dense/sparse non-bipartite graphs with n₁ = n₂ and k₁ = k₂, the present paper extends to the fully general bipartite setting (n₁ ≠ n₂, k₁ ≠ k₂) and provides non-asymptotic matching bounds, whereas [4] gives only asymptotic results in a restricted symmetric regime. Against Rotenberg, Huleihel, and Shayevitz [57], who detect bipartite communities planted in a larger non-bipartite graph with n₁ = n₂ = n and k₁ + k₂ = o(n), the present paper is strictly more general (allowing n₁ ≠ n₂ and any k₁, k₂) and more precise: [57]'s lower bound is loose by a logarithmic factor in n, and its asymptotic assumption δ* = Θ(n^(−α)) excludes the regime where the new phase transition (18) emerges. The paper also notes that [57] requires p₀ ≳ n^(−α) for α ∈ (0,2], while Assumption 1 requires p₀ ≳ (1/(k₁k₂))log(choose(n₁,k₁)·choose(n₂,k₂)) in certain regimes, which is a stronger (more restrictive) density condition.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the planted dense subgraph (PDS) detection framework of Arias-Castro and Verzelen [4, 59] from the fully symmetric non-bipartite setting to the general bipartite case with arbitrary n1, n2, k1, k2. The novel truncated degree and max truncated degree tests adapt the truncated chi-square test of Collier et al. [21] from Gaussian vectors to Bernoulli matrices, requiring substantial modifications to handle sub-Poissonian binomial tails and matrix-valued data. The lower bound departs from the strategies in [15] and [4] by employing a precise second-moment argument with new lemmas for controlling moment-generating functions of products of binomials, and holds without the density assumption required for the upper bound.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper fills a significant gap by providing the first non-asymptotic matching minimax bounds for community detection in bipartite graphs across all dimension configurations, revealing phase transitions (e.g., at n1·k2/k1² · log(n2) ≈ 1) absent from the non-bipartite literature. Key open questions include extending results beyond the dense-graph assumption (Assumption 1), achieving adaptivity to unknown p0 and community sizes, and establishing whether a statistical-computational gap persists in the bipartite setting. The truncated degree test machinery, built on the Bennett function, may be of independent interest for other Bernoulli matrix detection problems.
