## Problem

Existing social interaction detection methods rely on holistic person representations that obscure fine-grained body part cues (facial expressions, hand gestures, gaze direction) essential for distinguishing semantically similar interactions like 'wave' vs. 'point' or 'mutual gaze' vs. 'gaze following.' Additionally, prior approaches directly regress group bounding boxes without explicitly modeling person-to-person relations, introducing ambiguity when interactions occur between spatially distant individuals.

## Input / Output

The model takes a single RGB image as input and outputs a set of <individual bbox, group bbox, interaction class probabilities> triplets, where interaction classes span 22 atomic-level labels across 5 categories (facial expression, gesture, posture, gaze, touch). A single individual can be assigned to multiple concurrent groups and interactions. Notably, a pose estimator (ViTPose) is used only during training to generate part-level supervision masks as privileged information, imposing no additional inference cost.

## Method

The framework is a DETR-style transformer (ResNet-50 backbone + 6-layer encoder) with three key innovations. First, an individual embedding enhancer generates P=13 part-specific queries per detected individual via learned linear projections, refines them through self-attention and cross-attention with the feature map, and fuses them back into a part-aware individual embedding; these part queries are supervised by pose-guided binary masks (square windows around ViTPose keypoints) via MSE loss. Second, a group decoder attends to both the encoded feature map and the part-aware individual embeddings, enabling bottom-up aggregation of socially relevant individuals. Third, a similarity-based association module computes a dot-product similarity matrix between group and individual embeddings (after MLP projections) to link individuals to groups, replacing direct group box regression with relational reasoning. Training combines five losses (objectness, multi-label classification, localization, part supervision, and association) with Hungarian matching, and inference applies triplet NMS over individual boxes, group boxes, and interaction labels.

## Results

On the NVI test set, the proposed method achieves 78.52 AR (average recall over IoU 0.25/0.5/0.75), outperforming the previous state-of-the-art NVI-DEHR by 3.85 points. The gain is most pronounced at mR@25 (63.59 vs. 59.46, +4.13), indicating stronger precision in top-ranked predictions. On the Café group activity detection benchmark, the method achieves 18.23 Group mAP@1.0 and 67.62 Outlier mIoU under the split-by-view protocol, surpassing the prior best (Café-base) by 3.87 and 3.92 points respectively, despite using no temporal modeling.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all four NVI-DET baselines on both validation and test splits. Against the closest competitor NVI-DEHR [51] (the previous SOTA using hypergraph reasoning), the gain is +3.85 AR and +4.13 mR@25 on the test set, with the largest relative improvement at the strictest recall threshold. The three HOI-DET baselines adapted to NVI-DET (m-QPIC, m-CDN, m-GEN-VLKT) trail by 6.8–8.2 AR points on the test set, confirming that direct group detection without explicit interpersonal modeling is substantially weaker. On the Café dataset, the method beats all four group activity detection baselines (Joint, JRDB-base, HGC, Café-base) on both Group mAP and Outlier mIoU under both split-by-view and split-by-place protocols, with the largest margin over Café-base (+3.87 Group mAP@1.0, +9.31 Group mAP@0.5 on split-by-view). A comparison with LLaVA-1.6-vicuna-7B [33] shows a 41.4-point AR gap (78.52 vs. 37.14), and LoRA fine-tuning of LLaVA does not close this gap, suggesting that current MLLMs lack the fine-grained spatial reasoning needed for this task.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the NVI-DET task and dataset introduced by Wei et al. (ECCV 2024), adopting its triplet formulation and three of five training losses. It departs from the prior state-of-the-art (NVI-DEHR) in two conceptual ways: replacing direct group bounding-box regression with a bottom-up similarity-based association that lets groups emerge from individual-level relations, and replacing holistic person embeddings with pose-guided part-aware features (using ViTPose as privileged information at training only). Structurally, the framework draws on DETR-style transformer decoders and HOI-DET association strategies, but reconfigures them for multi-person social reasoning where interactions are inherently relational and grounded in specific body parts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The bottom-up group reasoning principle—that group composition should be inferred from inter-personal relations rather than predicted as a spatial proposal—addresses a fundamental ambiguity in multi-group detection, particularly for interactions like gaze that occur between spatially distant individuals. The part-aware representation with privileged pose supervision offers a practical recipe for incorporating fine-grained spatial cues without increasing inference cost, a pattern potentially transferable to other interaction detection tasks. Key limitations include reliance on an external pose estimator and evaluation on only two datasets; open questions include whether part-aware features can be learned end-to-end without predefined keypoints, and whether the framework extends naturally to video-based social interaction understanding.
