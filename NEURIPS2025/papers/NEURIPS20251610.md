## Problem

Existing dynamic sparse training (DST) methods, particularly the gradient-free Cannistraci-Hebb Training (CHT), suffer from two critical limitations: (i) rigid top-K link selection causes epitopological local minima where removed and regrown links overlap heavily, trapping topology exploration, and (ii) the path-based CH3-L3p link predictor has O(N·d³) time complexity, making it impractical for large-scale models at higher densities. Additionally, prior topological initialization models (Erdős-Rényi, Bipartite Small-World, Bipartite Scale-Free) lack brain-inspired spatial structure.

## Input / Output

The method operates on the connectivity (adjacency) matrices of fully connected or linear layers within MLPs, Transformers, and LLaMA architectures, maintaining a fixed target sparsity (1%–95%) while dynamically pruning and regrowing a fraction ζ of links at each update interval. For image classification, standard RGB images (28×28 for MNIST/Fashion-MNIST/EMNIST, 32×32 for CIFAR-10) are consumed by MLPs; for machine translation, source-language text sequences feed 6-layer Transformers; for language modeling, text tokens from OpenWebText are processed by LLaMA-60M/130M/1B. The output is a class label, translated target-language sequence, or next-token distribution respectively, with the key constraint that the network's active connectivity evolves throughout training rather than being fixed at initialization.

## Method

CHTs replaces CHT's deterministic top-K selection with a multinomial soft-sampling rule for both link removal (using a combined weight-magnitude/relative-importance score raised to a softness exponent δ) and link regrowth (using link prediction scores as sampling probabilities), enabling probabilistic escape from epitopological local minima. The path-based CH3-L3p predictor is replaced by a node-based CH2-L3n formula that scores candidate links via the ratio of internal-to-external local community links over common neighbors, reducing complexity to O(N³) and enabling GPU-friendly matrix multiplication. Sparse topologies are initialized with a Bipartite Receptive Field (BRF) model that assigns connection scores based on spatial distance to the diagonal raised to a power controlled by parameter r∈[0,1], producing brain-like clustered connectivity while conserving the output-layer degree distribution. CHTss further integrates a sigmoid-based gradual density decay schedule (replacing the sharp cubic decay of GMP/GraNet) with relative-importance-based pruning, allowing the network to warm up at higher density before smoothly converging to the target sparsity.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 at 99% sparsity, CHTs achieves 69.97% accuracy, surpassing the fully connected baseline (62.85%) by 11.33%, while CHTss reaches 71.29% (+13.43%). On machine translation, CHTss with only 5% of connections (95% sparsity) outperforms the fully connected Transformer on Multi30k (32.03 vs 31.38 BLEU) and IWSLT (24.51 vs 24.48 BLEU). Most strikingly, on OpenWebText language modeling, CHTs with 30% of connections (70% sparsity) achieves 14.53 validation perplexity on LLaMA-1B, beating the fully connected counterpart (14.62), while CHTss matches or approaches FC performance on LLaMA-60M (27.62 vs 26.56) and LLaMA-130M (19.85 vs 19.27) at the same sparsity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CHTs/CHTss consistently outperforms all six baselines across every task and sparsity level tested. Against SET (random regrowth), CHTs achieves substantially lower perplexity (e.g., 28.12 vs 31.77 on LLaMA-60M at 70% sparsity) and higher accuracy (69.97% vs 62.70% on CIFAR-10), confirming that topology-guided regrowth is critical. Against RigL (gradient-based regrowth), CHTs is dramatically more robust: RigL collapses to 149.17 PPL on LLaMA-1B at 70% sparsity under bfloat16, while CHTs achieves 14.53, because CHTs is gradient-free and unaffected by low-precision gradient noise. Against the original CHT (rigid top-K selection), CHTs improves by eliminating epitopological local minima through soft sampling (e.g., 28.12 vs 31.02 PPL on LLaMA-60M at 70%). Among density-decay methods, CHTss outperforms MEST_EM&S (27.62 vs 28.26 on LLaMA-60M at 70%), GMP (27.62 vs 29.22), and GraNet (27.62 vs 30.55), with the sigmoid decay schedule providing a smoother pruning trajectory than GMP's cubic or GraNet's discrete steps. On machine translation, CHTss is the only method to surpass the fully connected Transformer (32.03 vs 31.38 BLEU on Multi30k at 95% sparsity), while all other DST methods fall short.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper is a direct extension of CHT (Cannistraci-Hebb Training, ICLR 2024), a gradient-free dynamic sparse training method rooted in the Cannistraci-Hebb network automata theory. It departs from CHT in three key ways: replacing rigid top-K link selection with probabilistic soft sampling to escape 'epitopological local minima,' substituting the path-based CH3-L3p predictor (O(N·d³)) with a node-based CH2-L3n variant (O(N³)) for GPU-friendly scalability, and introducing a brain-inspired Bipartite Receptive Field (BRF) initialization that conserves output-layer degree distributions. Within the broader DST landscape (SET, RigL, MEST, GraNet, GMP), CHTs/CHTss is positioned as the first method to combine topology-driven gradient-free regrowth with a sigmoid density decay schedule, bridging the gap between ultra-sparse (≤1%) and moderate-sparse (30–50%) regimes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in making a gradient-free, topology-driven sparse training method scalable to billion-parameter LLMs (LLaMA-1B) and robust under low-precision (bfloat16) training, where gradient-based methods like RigL and GraNet degrade. The finding that 1% connectivity can outperform fully connected MLPs on CIFAR-10 and that 5% density CHTss beats FC Transformers on machine translation suggests substantial parameter reduction is possible without quality loss. However, the most headline result (CHTs beating FC LLaMA-1B by 0.09 perplexity at 30% sparsity) rests on a single seed and a marginal gap, and no direct wall-clock speed comparison with FC training is provided due to the lack of widely available unstructured-sparsity hardware. Open questions include whether the approach scales to 7B+ models, whether the soft-sampling temperature can be auto-tuned per layer, and whether the BRF initialization generalizes beyond bipartite linear layers to attention mechanisms.
