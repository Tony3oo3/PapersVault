## Problem

While recent AI text detectors have shown greater robustness against simple and recursive paraphrasing attacks, no universal, training-free attack exists that can consistently bypass a diverse set of detection systems (neural network-based, watermark-based, and zero-shot) with strong transferability. The paper asks whether a single detector-guided paraphrasing framework can universally humanize AI-generated text to evade all such detectors simultaneously.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an AI-generated text passage (typically 100–600 tokens), an off-the-shelf instruction-tuned LLM (LLaMA-3-8B-Instruct) configured as a paraphraser via a fixed system prompt, and a trained AI text detector used purely as a scoring oracle during generation. It produces a single adversarially paraphrased text of comparable length, generated auto-regressively token-by-token. The output is constrained to remain semantically equivalent to the input while being optimized to minimize the guidance detector's AI score at every decoding step.

## Method

Adversarial Paraphrasing performs a gradient-free, detector-guided beam search at depth one during auto-regressive text generation. At each step, the paraphraser LLM computes the next-token logit distribution, which is filtered by top-p (0.99) and top-k (50) masking to yield a candidate set; each candidate is then appended to the current output and scored by the guidance detector, and the candidate with the lowest AI score (highest human score) is selected. This process repeats until an [EOS] token is sampled, requiring no training or fine-tuning of either the paraphraser or the detector. The key insight enabling universal transferability is that high-performing detectors converge toward a shared human-text distribution to minimize false positives, so evading one well-trained detector naturally aligns the output with the statistical properties that all detectors are calibrated against.

## Results

Across eight deployed detectors (neural network, watermark, and zero-shot), adversarial paraphrasing guided by OpenAI-RoBERTa-Large achieves an average T@1%F reduction of 87.88% compared to no attack. The most striking individual results are a 98.96% T@1%F reduction on Fast-DetectGPT (0.323 → 0.003) and a 64.49% reduction on RADAR (0.124 → 0.044), a detector specifically adversarially trained to resist paraphrasing. In contrast, simple paraphrasing ironically *increases* T@1%F by 8.57% on RADAR and 15.03% on Fast-DetectGPT. GPT-4o quality ratings remain high (4.48±0.77 out of 5), with 87% of adversarial paraphrases receiving a score of 4 or 5.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the simple and recursive paraphrasing baselines from Sadasivan et al. [28] and Krishna et al. [15], adversarial paraphrasing is dramatically more effective: while simple paraphrasing *increases* T@1%F on RADAR (0.124→0.140) and Fast-DetectGPT (0.323→0.326), adversarial paraphrasing guided by RoBERTa-Large reduces them to 0.044 and 0.003 respectively. Recursive paraphrasing (3 rounds) improves over simple paraphrasing but still leaves T@1%F at 0.117 on RADAR and 0.276 on Fast-DetectGPT, far above the 0.044 and 0.003 achieved by adversarial paraphrasing. Against the Watermark Stealing attack [12] (Appendix A), which is specifically designed for KGW watermarks, adversarial paraphrasing guided by RADAR achieves a lower T@1%F (0.045 vs. 0.161–0.167) and a lower AUC (0.619 vs. 0.669–0.670), while also producing higher GPT-4o quality ratings (4.40 vs. 4.24–4.28). The key differentiator is universality: adversarial paraphrasing transfers across all eight detector types without any detector-specific knowledge, whereas watermark stealing only targets watermark-based detectors.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper synthesizes ideas from controlled text generation (PPLM, InstructCTG, BEAST) and paraphrasing-based attacks on AI text detectors (Sadasivan et al., Krishna et al./DIPPER) into a new training-free, gradient-free framework. Unlike PPLM and CAT-Gen, which use gradient-based perturbations to steer generation, and unlike BEAST, which employs bi-level beam search for jailbreaking, Adversarial Paraphrasing uses a single-level, detector-guided token selection within an off-the-shelf instruction-tuned LLM. It departs from prior paraphrasing attacks (simple and recursive) by incorporating a real-time guidance signal from a trained detector at every decoding step, and from watermark stealing by being universal across detector types rather than targeting a specific watermarking scheme.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that even detectors specifically trained to resist paraphrasing (RADAR) and fundamentally different detection paradigms (watermarking, zero-shot statistical methods) are vulnerable to a single, training-free attack, raising serious questions about the deployability of current AI text detection systems. The universality finding—that guidance from one neural-network detector transfers to watermark and zero-shot detectors—suggests these diverse methods may converge on similar human-text distributions, which is a useful diagnostic for detector designers. Open questions include whether detectors can be made robust to this specific attack (the authors suggest adversarial training with generated paraphrases), whether the approach extends to multilingual or multimodal settings, and whether the ~4% backfire rate (where paraphrasing increases detectability) can be eliminated. The practical simplicity of the method (off-the-shelf LLM + off-the-shelf detector, no training) means it is immediately deployable as a red-teaming tool.
