## Problem

Eliciting long chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning in LLMs currently requires costly reinforcement learning or supervised fine-tuning on high-quality distilled data, with little understanding of the internal mechanisms that govern this ability. This paper asks whether the long-CoT capability already exists latently in base models and can be invoked through targeted, training-free intervention on specific internal activations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250588/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard text reasoning prompt as input, along with a small set of pre-identified MLP neuron positions (top ~150–200 activations selected via contrastive example pairs) and three fitted coefficients (a, b, c) defining a log-decay amplification function. At inference, the system monitors the generated text for a trigger condition (≥5 digits in the last sentence), inserts a 'wait' token, and modulates the identified neuron values in real time. The output is a longer CoT trace containing explicit self-reflection steps (e.g., 'wait, let me double check') followed by a final answer, with measurably higher accuracy and reflection rate than the unmodified base model.

## Method

The core insight is that a sparse set of high-impact MLP activations in the last few layers (especially the final layer, which holds >50% of long-CoT-related neurons) governs long-form reasoning traits, and their temporal dynamics follow a predictable log-decay curve after trigger tokens like 'wait'. The training-free EELo-CoT method fits f(t) = a − b·log(t + c) to activation trajectories collected from contrastive pairs, then at inference amplifies the identified neurons via A′ = A·(1 + α·f(t)) whenever a 'wait' token is inserted, with a 4-sentence cool-down to prevent repetitive reflection. A complementary parameter-efficient variant adds a lightweight amplification module (a linear projection W_a ∈ R^{h×n} followed by a scaled sigmoid) to the last-layer MLP plus rank-64 LoRA in earlier layers, training only 1.51% of total parameters while matching or exceeding full fine-tuning and standard LoRA (rank 256) on math and science benchmarks.

## Results

On the training-free activation control (EELo-CoT), Qwen2.5-7B-base improves Math500 accuracy from 69.20% to 73.00%, AMC23 from 45.00% to 57.50%, and GPQA from 30.30% to 35.86%, while self-reflection rates jump from ~10% to ~50–70%. On Qwen2.5-Math-7B-base, Math500 accuracy reaches 76.00% (vs. 68.00% baseline) and GPQA reaches 37.88% (vs. 33.84%). For the parameter-efficient fine-tuning variant on Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct, training only 1.51% of parameters yields 90.20% Math500, 88.75% AMC23, and 70.02% GPQA accuracy, matching or exceeding full fine-tuning (100% params) on GPQA while using ~50% fewer inference tokens on AMC23.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In the training-free setting, EELo-CoT consistently outperforms all three ablation baselines (Forcing Reflection, Constant Intervention, Forcing & Constant) on accuracy across all three benchmarks and both main models. For example, on Qwen2.5-7B-base Math500, EELo-CoT achieves 73.00% vs. 66.00% (Forcing Reflection), 69.20% (Constant), and 66.40% (Forcing & Constant); on AMC23 it reaches 57.50% vs. 40–47.50% for baselines. The key finding is that activation amplification and wait-token insertion are complementary: neither alone matches their combination, and the analytic-function-based scheduling (EELo-CoT) further improves over the simpler constant-amplification + wait-token baseline. In the parameter-efficient fine-tuning setting (Table 5), EELo-CoT trains only 1.51% of parameters and matches full fine-tuning on Math500 (90.20% vs. 91.60%), beats LoRA (6.15% params) on AMC23 (88.75% vs. 85.00%) and GPQA (70.02% vs. 66.17%), while reducing average inference length by ~50% on AMC23 compared to both baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the activation steering paradigm (Turner et al., 2023), previously applied to style and concept control, for the specific goal of eliciting long chain-of-thought reasoning. It departs from the dominant paradigm of using reinforcement learning or supervised fine-tuning on distilled data (DeepSeek-R1, LIMO, OpenAI o1) by showing that the long-CoT capability is already latent in base models and can be activated through targeted amplification of a small set of MLP neurons in the last few layers. The parameter-efficient training method further extends LoRA by adding a lightweight activation amplification module, reducing trainable parameters from 6.15% (LoRA) to 1.51% while matching or exceeding full fine-tuning performance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The core finding that long-CoT reasoning is a latent property of base models, controllable via a handful of activation values, challenges the assumption that expensive RL or large-scale SFT is necessary to acquire this capability. This opens a path for fine-grained, on-demand control of reasoning behavior without retraining. However, the approach is currently validated primarily on Qwen-family models with a somewhat ad-hoc 'wait' token trigger, and the paper acknowledges it has not been tested on MoE architectures, ultra-large models, or non-transformer backbones. Open questions include whether the identified activation positions are stable across model families and whether the logarithmic decay pattern generalizes to other reasoning behaviors beyond self-reflection.
