## Problem

Standard LLMs operate entirely in Euclidean space, yet token embeddings exhibit substantial variation in negative Ricci curvature, indicating hierarchical and scale-free geometric structure that Euclidean dot products and norms cannot capture. Prior hyperbolic Transformers addressed this partially but suffered from three critical gaps: they fixed each block to a single curvature (limiting expressiveness), omitted essential LLM components like rotary positional encodings and RMSNorm, and relied on quadratic attention that does not scale to billion-parameter regimes.

## Input / Output

The model takes tokenized text (LLaMA3.1-8B tokenizer, 128K vocabulary) and maps it to learned hyperbolic word embeddings in the Lorentz model, where only the space-like dimension is trained to ensure stability at 1B scale. All intermediate representations remain on the hyperbolic manifold throughout the decoder stack. The final output is a projection to next-token prediction logits; for evaluation, the model selects among multiple-choice options on benchmarks like MMLU and ARC.

## Method

HELM is a fully hyperbolic decoder-only LLM built on the Lorentz model, combining four novel modules: (1) MICE (Mixture-of-Curvature Experts), where each routed and shared expert operates on a distinct curvature manifold with top-k gating, enabling fine-grained geometric encoding; (2) HMLA (Hyperbolic Multi-Head Latent Attention), which projects keys and values into a low-dimensional latent space before upward projection, reducing KV-cache memory from O(2n·n_h·L) to O((n_kv+n_r)·L); (3) HOPE (Hyperbolic Rotary Positional Encoding), a Lorentz rotation on the space-like dimension that provably encodes only relative position, exhibits long-term decay, and supports arbitrary-distance attention; and (4) Hyperbolic RMSNorm, which applies Euclidean RMSNorm to the space-like component and re-embeds onto the manifold. The architecture alternates attention and FFN blocks with Lorentzian residual connections, trained via next-token prediction on ~5B Wikipedia tokens at 100M and 1B parameter scales.

## Results

HELM-MICE (1B) achieves 24.9% average accuracy across five benchmarks, outperforming the Euclidean DeepSeekV3 (1B) baseline (23.9%) by 1.0 point on average and by up to 2.3 points on MMLU (25.9% vs 23.6%). At the 100M scale, HELM-MICE (120M) reaches 24.1% average vs DeepSeekV3 (120M) at 22.2%, while HELM-D (115M) reaches 24.1% vs LLaMA (115M) at 23.2%. The largest single-benchmark gains appear on OpenbookQA (+3.7 points at 100M for MICE) and MMLU (+2.3 points at 1B), suggesting hyperbolic geometry particularly benefits harder reasoning tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LLaMA (115M, dense baseline), HELM-D wins on 4 of 5 benchmarks (HellaSwag +0.8, OpenbookQA +1.7, MMLU +2.2, ARC +0.4) but trails on CommonsenseQA (−0.6), yielding a +0.9 point average advantage. Against DeepSeekV3 (120M, MoE baseline), HELM-MICE wins on all 5 benchmarks, with the largest margin on OpenbookQA (+3.7) and ARC (+1.0), for a +1.9 point average gain. At 1B scale, HELM-MICE outperforms DeepSeekV3 on every benchmark, with the most notable gap on MMLU (+2.3) and OpenbookQA (+1.0), confirming that the Mixture-of-Curvature Experts design scales favorably. The consistent advantage on harder reasoning benchmarks (MMLU, ARC-Challenging) relative to easier commonsense tasks suggests hyperbolic geometry particularly benefits structured, hierarchical reasoning rather than surface-level pattern matching.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the hyperbolic Transformer lineage (HypFormer, HNN++, Fully Hyperbolic NNs) by addressing three key shortcomings of prior work: fixed single-curvature manifolds, missing essential LLM components (RoPE, RMSNorm), and poor scalability. It adapts two recent Euclidean LLM innovations—Rotary Positional Encoding (RoPE) and Multi-Head Latent Attention (MLA from DeepSeek-V2/V3)—into fully hyperbolic counterparts (HOPE and HMLA), while introducing the novel Mixture-of-Curvature Experts (MICE) module where each expert operates in a distinct curvature space. The Lorentzian residual connection from LResNet and the HLT layer from HypFormer serve as the foundational building blocks for all hyperbolic operations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that fully hyperbolic LLMs can be trained at billion-parameter scale and outperform their Euclidean counterparts on standard benchmarks, validating the hypothesis that text's hierarchical structure is better captured by non-Euclidean geometry. However, the evidence base is limited: models are trained on only 5B tokens from Wikipedia, the 1B results lack standard deviations, and comparisons are against from-scratch Euclidean baselines of the same small scale rather than commercial LLMs. Key open questions include whether the hyperbolic advantage persists at much larger scale (tens of billions of parameters, trillions of tokens), whether scaling laws differ for hyperbolic vs. Euclidean LLMs, and whether the MICE mixed-curvature strategy generalizes beyond the specific curvature range tested.
