## Problem

Large vision-language models frequently hallucinate objects not present in the input image, and prior mitigation strategies have focused almost exclusively on modifying the language model's decoding or attention, leaving the vision encoder's contribution to hallucination largely unexplored. This paper identifies that visual tokens with high epistemic uncertainty within the vision encoder are a key driver of object hallucination, and asks whether this uncertainty can be efficiently estimated and suppressed without retraining.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254352/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard RGB image as input and produces a text caption, operating entirely at inference time (training-free). To compute the uncertainty mask, it requires an additional forward pass of the vision encoder on a PGD-perturbed copy of the image (bounded by ‖ε‖∞ ≤ 3), making the total inference cost roughly two vision-encoder passes plus one language-model pass. The output format is identical to the base LVLM's caption, but with reduced hallucinated object mentions.

## Method

The paper proves (Lemma 3.1, Theorem 3.2) that under small adversarial perturbations, the norm of hidden-state deviation in early vision-encoder layers is monotonically related to an upper bound on each visual token's differential entropy, providing a theoretically grounded and ~5× faster proxy for MC-dropout uncertainty estimation. In practice, PGD (200 steps, k=3) is applied to maximize MSE between clean and perturbed vision-encoder features; the per-token L2 deviation across early layers (1–10) is min-max normalized, averaged, and binarized with a threshold σ_th to yield a binary uncertainty mask M. This mask is then applied as a token-wise multiplication on the self-attention output in intermediate vision-encoder blocks (layers 13–17 for LLaVA-1.5), attenuating uncertain tokens' influence while preserving the residual stream and global semantic structure. Because the intervention is confined to the vision encoder, the method is orthogonal to and composable with existing LLM-level mitigation techniques such as OPERA, VCD, PAI, and Devils.

## Results

On LLaVA-1.5-7B with greedy decoding, the proposed method reduces CHAIR_s from 47.4 to 29.2 (↓38.4%) and CHAIR_i from 12.2 to 9.3 (↓23.8%) while slightly improving F1 from 77.9 to 78.2. On Shikra-7B, CHAIR_s drops from 58.0 to 43.2 (↓25.5%) and CHAIR_i from 15.6 to 11.7 (↓25.0%), with F1 improving from 74.7 to 76.9. On MiniGPT-4, gains are smaller (CHAIR_s: 28.6→27.4, CHAIR_i: 8.5→8.3) due to the Q-Former module limiting the vision encoder's direct influence. POPE accuracy is largely preserved or slightly improved across all three models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's method is designed as a complementary, vision-encoder-only intervention that stacks on top of existing language-model-level mitigation baselines (OPERA, VCD, PAI, Devils) rather than competing with them directly. Across all four baselines and all three LVLMs, adding the proposed method consistently reduces CHAIR_s and CHAIR_i while preserving or slightly improving F1. The largest synergistic gains appear with OPERA on LLaVA-1.5-7B (CHAIR_s: 47.8→29.4, ↓38.5%) and PAI on Shikra-7B (CHAIR_s: 32.4→22.2, ↓31.5%). On MiniGPT-4, improvements are modest (e.g., CHAIR_s under Greedy: 28.6→27.4, ↓4.2%) because the Q-Former module between the vision encoder and LLM attenuates the effect of vision-encoder-level masking. POPE scores remain stable or improve slightly, confirming that the method does not degrade discriminative object-recognition ability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by repurposing adversarial perturbations (PGD) from the robustness/jailbreak literature as an efficient proxy for epistemic uncertainty estimation in vision encoders, bypassing the expensive MC dropout approach. It departs from the dominant line of hallucination mitigation work (OPERA, VCD, PAI, Devils) that intervenes at the language model level—decoding strategies, attention modulation, or logit manipulation—by targeting the vision encoder instead, a component the authors argue has been underexplored. The theoretical contribution (Lemma 3.1, Theorem 3.2) connects adversarial feature deviation to an upper bound on differential entropy, providing a principled justification for using perturbation sensitivity as an uncertainty signal in early VE layers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method offers a training-free, vision-encoder-only intervention that is orthogonal to and composable with existing LLM-level mitigation strategies, making it a practical plug-in for deployed LVLMs. The ~5× speedup over MC dropout for uncertainty estimation and the 38% CHAIR_s reduction on LLaVA-1.5-7B are practically meaningful. However, gains are substantially smaller on MiniGPT-4 (4.2% CHAIR_s reduction), likely due to the Q-Former bottleneck, raising open questions about generalizability to architectures with more complex vision-language connectors. Whether the early-layer-for-estimation / middle-layer-for-masking asymmetry holds across diverse VE architectures, and whether the binary masking could be refined (e.g., soft, learned thresholds), remain open.
