## Problem

Recursive synthetic training—where each model generation is trained on outputs from its predecessor—provably leads to exponential error growth and model collapse, yet prior theoretical analyses are limited to non-recursive settings, infinite-sample asymptotics, simplified Gaussian mixture models, or external verification mechanisms. No rigorous finite-sample framework existed showing that a model's own internal confidence scores could prevent collapse in fully synthetic recursive training for general architectures such as transformers.

## Input / Output

The method consumes N candidate outputs sampled from the previous generation model for each input, along with a fixed base model G_0 (trained once on real data and frozen across all generations) that provides the verification score via log-likelihood. It produces a filtered set of verified training samples—those within margin γ of the maximum verification score—weighted by a softmax over scores, from which the next generation is trained. For the LLM instantiation, inputs are token prefix sequences S ∈ X^{≤ℓ} and outputs are next-token predictions, with the verification score computed per-token as log G_{θ_0}^{LLM}(x | S).

## Method

The core mechanism is confidence-guided self-verification: for each input, N candidates are drawn from the previous generation, scored by the fixed base model's log-likelihood s_verify(y|x) = log G_0(y|x), and only candidates within margin γ of the top score are retained, from which a training target is sampled via softmax weighting. The key theoretical contribution is a recursive coverage coefficient C_{γ,t} = E_x[1/G_t(y_γ^+(x)|x)] that tracks how probability mass concentrates on verified predictions across generations; they prove that when N > (1/C_0)·log(1/C_0), the exponential error term (1/(NC_0)·log(1/C_0))^T vanishes as T→∞, yielding a bounded error floor of O(1/N)·log(1/τ) even in fully synthetic training. For transformer-based LLMs, they instantiate the framework with multi-head self-attention and FFN layers under bounded-parameter assumptions, deriving PAC-Bayesian generalization bounds where the statistical gap scales as log(nB̃)·D̃/√n, providing the first formal guarantee that stable recursive training is achievable without any real data injection.

## Results

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical results tables. The headline contributions are four theorems providing finite-sample error bounds for recursive synthetic training. Theorem 1 shows naive recursive training yields exponential error growth: Err(G_T) ≲ ε(γ) + (1/τ)·D_TV + (C_γ + D_TV·log(1/D_TV))·(1/(2C_0) + ½log(1/C_0))^T, where the exponential factor exceeds 1 whenever C_0 ≤ 0.5. Theorem 3 (the main positive result) proves that self-verification alone prevents collapse: when the verification sample size N > (1/C_0)·log(1/C_0), the exponential term vanishes as T→∞, yielding a bounded error of Err(G_T) ≲ ε(γ) + (1/τ)·D_TV + (1/N)(1 + (1/C_0)·D_TV·log(1/D_TV))·log(1/τ). Theorem 4 extends these guarantees to Transformer-based LLMs via PAC-Bayesian analysis, adding a statistical generalization term (log(nB̃)·D̃)/√n.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper presents no empirical baselines or head-to-head comparisons, as it is a theoretical contribution. The comparisons are entirely at the level of theoretical scope and assumptions. Relative to Feng et al. (ICLR 2025) and Firdoussi et al. (ICLR 2025), which analyze external verification for Gaussian mixture models with linear classifiers in non-recursive, infinite-sample settings, this paper introduces self-verification (no external verifier), handles recursive training dynamics, imposes no data-distribution assumptions, and provides finite-sample bounds. Relative to Fu et al. (ICLR 2025), which established that a constant proportion of real data prevents collapse in transformer architectures, this paper shows that self-verification can replace real data entirely in the fully synthetic regime. The paper also contrasts with Bertrand et al. (ICLR 2024) and Dohmatob et al. (2024), which assumed iterative retraining with inherited parameters, whereas this work analyzes the more practical setting where each generation is trained from scratch.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the emerging theoretical line analyzing model collapse in recursive synthetic training, most directly extending the verification-based approaches of Feng et al. and Firdoussi et al. It departs from those in four key ways: it replaces external verifiers with a self-verification mechanism in which models filter their own outputs via internally estimated confidence scores anchored to a fixed base model; it moves from the non-recursive settings of prior work into the genuinely recursive regime where collapse actually emerges; it derives finite-sample generalization bounds rather than relying on infinite-sample asymptotics; and it generalizes from simplified Gaussian mixture models with linear classifiers to transformer-based LLMs, without imposing data-distribution assumptions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This provides the first theoretical justification for stable, fully-synthetic recursive training in high-capacity transformer architectures, removing dependence on scarce real data or external validation signals. The analysis yields concrete practical guidance: a verification sample size exceeding the critical threshold N > log(1/C₀)/C₀ ensures the exponential error-accumulation term vanishes regardless of recursion depth T. A key limitation is the reliance on a single confidence-based verification strategy with a fixed base model, which may be insufficient to capture semantic correctness on complex tasks; richer adaptive verifiers such as ensemble-based voting or task-specific evaluators are natural open extensions.
