## Problem

Reconstruction-free novel view synthesis methods like LVSM and SRT use fixed-size latent scene representations, offering no mechanism to control data size, rendering quality, or rendering speed at inference time. This paper asks whether a single trained network can represent a scene with a variable number of compact tokens and render novel views by adaptively selecting a subset of those tokens based on a user-specified compute budget.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250297/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes N_c input images (256×256 on RealEstate10K, 256×448 on DL3DV) with known camera poses and produces N_s compressed light-field tokens (CLiFTs) as the scene representation. At inference, given a target camera pose and a render token count N_r ≤ N_s, it outputs a synthesized RGB image. The key flexibility is that N_s and N_r are hyperparameters adjustable at test time without retraining, and the token selection uses a ray-geometry heuristic (ray-angle distance, camera-center distance, and temporal mask) over a 24×24 expanded patch grid to pick the most relevant CLiFTs for the target view.

## Method

CLiFT construction proceeds in three stages: (1) a 6-block Transformer encoder tokenizes each pixel's 6D Plücker coordinates and RGB color into 768-d LiFTs via 8×8 patchification; (2) latent-space K-means clusters all LiFTs across views and retains K = N_s nearest-neighbor centroids as representative rays, adaptively allocating more tokens to texture-rich regions; (3) a 2-block neural condenser uses inter-cluster self-attention and intra-cluster cross-attention (with zero-initialized residual projections) to compress all token information into the centroid tokens. Rendering uses a 6-block Transformer decoder where target-view Plücker patches (384-d, projected to 768-d) serve as queries and the selected CLiFTs serve as keys/values, trained with randomly varying token counts so a single model handles any N_r. Training is two-stage: first the encoder and renderer are trained jointly with all tokens passed through cross-attention, then the encoder is frozen while the condenser is trained using precomputed K-means assignments.

## Results

On RealEstate10K and DL3DV, CLiFT achieves a PSNR of ~26.72 dB at full token budget (4096 CLiFTs) while using approximately 5–7× less scene-representation data than MVSplat and DepthSplat, and ~1.8× less than LVSM, while attaining the highest overall PSNR among all compared methods. The compute-adaptive renderer allows on-the-fly trade-offs: reducing render tokens from 4096 to 512 drops PSNR by only 2.83 dB (26.72→23.89) while increasing FPS from 54.3 to 90.15 (+66%) and reducing FLOPs by 36%. A single trained model per dataset supports fine-grained control over both storage size (N_s) and render size (N_r), unlike baselines that require separately trained models for each data-size configuration.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CLiFT outperforms all three baselines on the overall PSNR-vs-data-size trade-off. Against LVSM (reconstruction-free), CLiFT achieves comparable or higher PSNR with roughly 1.8× fewer tokens, and uniquely supports variable-size rendering from a single model. Against MVSplat and DepthSplat (reconstruction-based feed-forward splatting), CLiFT matches their PSNR while storing 5–7× fewer scene primitives (CLiFT tokens vs. 3D Gaussians/splats). A key qualitative advantage is that CLiFT preserves sharp high-frequency appearance details closer to ground truth even under strong compression, whereas splatting-based methods show more visible artifacts. The only scenario where baselines may be preferable is very low compression (<2×), where random token selection performs similarly to K-means and the overhead of the condenser is not yet justified.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the reconstruction-free novel view synthesis paradigm established by LVSM and Scene Representation Transformer, which encode multi-view images into fixed-length latent token sets for feed-forward rendering. It departs from this line by introducing a variable-size, compressed scene representation (CLiFTs) constructed via latent-space K-means ray selection and a neural condenser, drawing conceptual inspiration from classical light field imaging (Levoy, Gortler) and compressive sensing. Unlike reconstruction-based methods (MVSplat, DepthSplat) that predict explicit 3D Gaussians, CLiFT operates purely in a ray-token space, and unlike LVSM's fixed-length latent code, it supports on-the-fly adjustment of both storage and render token counts with a single trained model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ability to trade off data size, rendering quality, and rendering speed with a single model (via N_s and N_r hyperparameters) is practically significant for deployment in bandwidth-constrained or latency-sensitive settings such as virtual property tours, interactive product displays, and autonomous driving simulation. The 5–7× data reduction over splatting-based baselines with comparable PSNR suggests that explicit 3D geometry may not be necessary for competitive NVS quality. Open questions include generalization to camera motions outside the training distribution and handling of views not covered by inputs, where the authors suggest generative priors as a promising direction.
