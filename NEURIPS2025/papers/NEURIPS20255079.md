## Problem

Prior work on robust average-reward reinforcement learning established only asymptotic convergence guarantees for policy evaluation, leaving the sample complexity question open. The fundamental obstacle is that the robust average-reward Bellman operator lacks a contraction property under any standard norm (unlike the discounted-reward case), rendering conventional fixed-point and stochastic approximation analyses inapplicable. This paper closes the gap by providing the first finite-sample complexity bounds for robust policy evaluation and robust average reward estimation.

## Input / Output

The algorithm operates in a model-free, tabular MDP setting: it receives a stationary policy π, i.i.d. samples from the nominal transition kernel (the centroid of the uncertainty set), and a specification of the uncertainty set type (contamination, total variation, or Wasserstein) with radius δ. It outputs the robust value function V ∈ R^S (anchored at a reference state s₀ to fix the additive-constant ambiguity) and the robust worst-case average reward g ∈ R. For TV and Wasserstein sets, each Bellman-operator evaluation requires a variable number of nominal-model samples governed by a truncated geometric random variable, whereas the contamination set requires only one sample per (s,a) pair.

## Method

The key theoretical insight is constructing a semi-norm ‖·‖_P under which the robust Bellman operator is a strict contraction: the authors group all worst-case transition dynamics into a compact family of fluctuation matrices, invoke the joint spectral radius to build an extremal norm with uniform one-step shrinkage, and add a small quotient correction to annihilate constant shifts. For estimating the support function under TV and Wasserstein uncertainty sets, the paper introduces a truncated Multi-Level Monte Carlo estimator that caps the geometric sampling level at N_max, ensuring finite expected sample complexity O(N_max) while the truncation bias decays as 2^{−N_max/2}. The resulting two-phase temporal-difference algorithm first iterates a biased stochastic approximation on the value function (with stepsize O(1/t)) and then refines the average reward via a second SA loop on the Bellman residual, yielding an order-optimal Õ(ε^{−2}) sample complexity for both the value function and the robust average reward.

## Results

The paper establishes the first finite-sample complexity guarantees for robust average-reward policy evaluation. The headline result (Theorem 6.1) shows that the proposed robust TD algorithm achieves an order-optimal sample complexity of $\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(SA\, t_{\text{mix}}^2 / (\epsilon^2(1-\gamma)^2))$ for $\epsilon$-accurate robust value function estimation under all three uncertainty sets (contamination, TV, and Wasserstein). Theorem 6.2 extends this to robust average reward estimation with the same $\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(\epsilon^{-2})$ rate. The truncated MLMC estimator (Theorem 5.1) achieves finite expected sample complexity of $\mathcal{O}(N_{\max})$ with exponentially decaying bias $2^{-N_{\max}/2}$ (Theorem 5.2) and linearly growing variance $\mathcal{O}(N_{\max})$ (Theorem 5.4).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines or experimental comparisons. The comparison is against prior theoretical results: previous works on robust average-reward RL (Wang et al., ICML 2023 [39]; Wang et al., JAIR 2024 [38]; Wang et al., AAAI 2023 [37]) provided only asymptotic convergence guarantees (almost-sure convergence via ODE analysis) without any finite-sample bounds. This paper is the first to establish explicit finite-sample complexity for robust policy evaluation in the average-reward setting. The $\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(\epsilon^{-2})$ rate is order-optimal in $\epsilon$, matching the lower bound for stochastic approximation under bounded variance noise, though the authors note they do not claim tightness in the $S$, $A$, or $\gamma$ dependencies. The key technical advance over prior MLMC-based approaches [4, 5, 39] is the truncated geometric sampling that converts infinite expected sample complexity into a finite $\mathcal{O}(N_{\max})$ cost while preserving exponential bias decay.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the model-free robust average-reward RL framework of Wang et al. (ICML 2023), which established asymptotic convergence of robust TD and Q-learning via ODE analysis and unbounded MLMC estimators, by providing the first finite-sample guarantees. The stochastic approximation analysis draws on the semi-norm Lyapunov framework of Zhang et al. (NeurIPS 2021) for non-robust average-reward TD, while the key departure is constructing a contraction semi-norm for the robust Bellman operator using joint spectral radius and extremal norms (Berger & Wang, 1992). The truncated MLMC estimator modifies the unbiased MLMC of Blanchet et al. by capping the geometric sampling level, trading infinite expected sample complexity for an exponentially decaying bias.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This fills a clear gap: prior robust average-reward RL algorithms had only almost-sure convergence with no finite-time bounds, leaving practitioners without guidance on how many samples are needed for a given accuracy. The order-optimal O~(ε^{-2}) sample complexity and the semi-norm contraction result are foundational for future finite-sample analyses of robust average-reward policy optimization. Key limitations include the ergodicity assumption on the nominal model, the absence of function approximation (restricting results to tabular settings), and unclaimed tightness in S, A, and the contraction factor γ. Scaling the approach to high-dimensional state spaces via function approximation is identified as the primary open problem.
