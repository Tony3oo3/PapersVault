## Problem

Polynomial regression efficiently learns halfspaces by outputting polynomial threshold functions (PTFs), but PTFs can have boundary volume Ω(1) even at very small perturbation radii, providing no adversarial robustness guarantee. No proper agnostic learner with sub-exponential running time is known for general subgaussian isotropic log-concave distributions, and the state-of-the-art proper learner for the Gaussian case has complexity d^{O(1/ε⁴)}. The paper asks whether one can efficiently learn a classifier that simultaneously achieves optimal classification error and small boundary volume (adversarial robustness) under these general distributions.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes i.i.d. labeled samples from a distribution over ℝᵈ × {−1,1} whose ℝᵈ-marginal is subgaussian, isotropic, and log-concave, along with a robustness radius r ∈ (0,1) and accuracy parameter ε ≥ d^{−1/7}. It outputs a deterministic Boolean classifier h: ℝᵈ → {−1,1} with a specific verifiable structure: a partition of the domain into 4 intervals along a random unit-vector projection, where each interval is handled by a degree-k PTF (k = Õ(1/ε²)) post-processed by a fixed-seed local corrector. The output guarantees err(h) ≤ opt + O(ε) and Boundary_{D,r}(h) ≤ O(r + ε), with running time and sample complexity d^{Õ(1/ε²)}.

## Method

The algorithm augments polynomial regression with three steps. First, it solves a convex program minimizing ℓ₁ error over degree-k polynomials subject to noise-sensitivity and isolation-probability constraints, using the halfspace-approximating polynomial from Diakonikolas et al. to certify feasibility. Second, it applies a structured rounding step: by Carathéodory's theorem, it finds 4 rounding thresholds whose convex combination simultaneously satisfies error, noise-sensitivity, and isolation bounds, then converts this mixture into a deterministic partition of the domain into 4 intervals along a random unit-vector projection (using a concentration theorem for projections). Third, a local corrector (ROBUSTNESSLCA) evaluates the local noise sensitivity at each point via a randomized approximator and flips the label wherever the sensitivity exceeds a threshold, converting the relaxed guarantee of low noise sensitivity and low isolation probability into full adversarial robustness (small boundary volume) while changing the classifier on only an O(ε) fraction of points.

## Results

The main result (Theorem 1.1) gives an improper agnostic learner for halfspaces over subgaussian isotropic log-concave distributions with time and sample complexity $d^{\tilde{O}(1/\varepsilon^2)}$, matching the best known (non-robust) polynomial regression algorithm. The output hypothesis achieves classification error opt + O(ε) and boundary volume O(r + ε) at perturbation radius r. Up to polylogarithmic factors in the exponent, this matches the statistical query lower bound for agnostically learning halfspaces [DKPZ21]. The algorithm requires ε ≥ d^{-1/7} and uses a degree-$\tilde{O}(1/\varepsilon^2)$ polynomial threshold function as an intermediate representation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines or experimental comparisons. In the theoretical landscape, the algorithm matches the time and sample complexity of the best improper agnostic learner for halfspaces (polynomial regression of [DGJ+09, KKMS08]) while adding adversarial robustness guarantees that the prior algorithm does not provide. Compared to the proper agnostic learner of [DKK+21], which achieves error opt + ε under the Gaussian distribution in $d^{O(1/\varepsilon^4)}$ time, this paper's improper algorithm is faster by a polynomial factor in 1/ε but does not output a halfspace. The robust proper learners of [DKM20, DKM19] have running times with exponential dependence on √d when scaled to the subgaussian log-concave setting of this paper. The paper also shows that its output has a verifiable structure (Corollary 1.5), enabling deterministic robustness verification under P = BPP, a property not available for general learned classifiers.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the classic polynomial regression framework for agnostic halfspace learning (DGJ+09, KKMS08) by augmenting it with three additional steps: convex-constrained ℓ₁ regression, a structured partitioning-and-rounding step using Carathéodory's theorem, and a local corrector borrowed from the LCA literature (LRV22). It departs from prior robust learning results (DKM19, DKM20) that either require proper learning or assume unit-ball support, by working under the broader class of subgaussian isotropic log-concave marginals while matching the d^Õ(1/ε²) complexity of the best non-robust agnostic learner. The partitioning step leverages the concentration theorem of Dasgupta et al. (DHV06) to project onto a random direction, ensuring the domain split introduces negligible boundary volume.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first improper agnostic learner for halfspaces under general log-concave marginals that simultaneously achieves optimal classification error and adversarial robustness at the same complexity as the best non-robust algorithm, closing a gap where prior proper learners required d^O(1/ε⁴) time. The verifiable robustness corollary is practically significant: it allows a user to deterministically certify that a hypothesis is robust without trusting the training service, addressing the backdoor-planting threat model of Goldwasser et al. (GKVZ22). Open questions include whether proper learning can achieve the same complexity under log-concave marginals, and whether the local-correction framework extends to richer concept classes beyond halfspaces and low-degree PTFs.
