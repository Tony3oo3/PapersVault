## Problem

Standard contrastive learning aligns representations to semantic structure at a global level, treating the embedding space uniformly and overlooking fine-grained hierarchical relationships (e.g., distinguishing t-shirts from jackets within upper-body garments). Existing approaches that attempt to capture multi-scale structure rely on stronger augmentation strategies or supervised multi-level labels, but lack a principled mechanism to adaptively identify and refine poorly aligned local regions across multiple semantic granularities.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252719/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes augmented views of input data (RGB images, 3D shapes, ultrasound images) and produces unit-norm embeddings on a hypersphere S^{d_g-1}. A key structural detail is that the embedding space is partitioned into local regions at S different angular radii (coarse to fine), and the training loss is computed per-region with both positive and negative samples restricted to the same local neighborhood. Downstream, the learned representations are evaluated via linear probing for classification, with hierarchical quality additionally measured by HC-NMI and intra-class variance reduction.

## Method

AMA-alignment introduces a local contrastive loss derived through Probabilistic Graph Coupling that aligns semantic and representation affinity graphs within spatially defined regions, critically restricting both positives and negatives to the local region (unlike naive formulations that only restrict positives, which distorts global structure). These per-region losses are aggregated across S scales via a distributionally robust optimization (DRO) min-max objective: the inner maximization over a probability simplex adaptively upweights poorly aligned regions, while the outer minimization over encoder parameters reduces their losses. A KL divergence regularization on the weight simplex prevents degenerate concentration on a single region and yields a closed-form exponential weight update. The encoder is first pre-trained with standard InfoNCE, then jointly optimized across all scales using stochastic gradient descent with the adaptive region weights, achieving a convergence rate of O(1/√T) comparable to standard smooth non-convex optimization.

## Results

AMA-alignment achieves 75.8% top-1 accuracy on DeepFashion, 80.6% on ModelNet40, 57.2% on iNaturalist, and 73.3% on ImageNet, consistently outperforming all baselines across all four benchmarks. The largest gain is on the fine-grained DeepFashion dataset, where AMA-alignment surpasses the strongest baseline (α-CL, 71.7%) by 4.1 percentage points. On hierarchical structure preservation (measured by HC-NMI and IVR on DeepFashion), AMA-alignment achieves 0.66 HC-NMI and 0.091 IVR, compared to 0.59 and 0.114 for the next-best method (α-CL).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AMA-alignment outperforms all four baselines on every reported benchmark. On DeepFashion, the fine-grained clothing dataset, AMA-alignment (75.8%) leads α-CL (71.7%) by 4.1 points and SimCLR (70.3%) by 5.5 points, demonstrating the strongest gains where hierarchical semantic structure matters most. On ImageNet, AMA-alignment (73.3%) surpasses HardNeg (70.4%) by 2.9 points and SimCLR (69.5%) by 3.8 points, indicating that the multi-scale adaptive strategy also benefits large-scale general classification. On iNaturalist, the taxonomic dataset, AMA-alignment (57.2%) edges out α-CL (56.1%) by 1.1 points and SimCLR (54.0%) by 3.2 points. On ModelNet40 (3D shapes), the margin is smaller but consistent: AMA-alignment (80.6%) beats HardNeg (79.8%) by 0.8 points. The ablation study further confirms that the principled local contrastive loss (Eq. 12) is critical: replacing it with the naive heuristic (Eq. 13) while keeping the adaptive weighting actually degrades performance below the global-only baseline (67.3% vs. 70.2% on DeepFashion).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the probabilistic graph coupling interpretation of InfoNCE (Van Assel et al. 2022; Tan et al. 2024) from a global to a local, multi-scale setting, deriving a principled local contrastive loss that restricts both positives and negatives to spatially defined regions in the embedding space. The adaptive min-max optimization structure borrows from group distributionally robust optimization (Sagawa et al. 2020), but replaces fixed, predefined groups with data-driven, dynamically evolving regions partitioned via spherical k-means. It also generalizes the two-player coordinate-wise optimization game for contrastive learning (Tian 2022) into a three-player framework that adds region-level importance weights as a third optimization axis. The key conceptual departure from prior CL work is that alignment is no longer performed uniformly over the entire embedding space but is adaptively concentrated on poorly aligned local regions across multiple semantic granularities.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a well-recognized limitation of contrastive learning—its tendency to capture coarse categorical structure while losing fine-grained hierarchical relationships—by introducing a multi-scale, region-aware optimization that provably preserves convergence rates comparable to standard smooth non-convex training. The ablation showing that a naive local loss (restricting only positives) actually degrades performance below the global baseline is a useful cautionary result for practitioners attempting to localize contrastive objectives. Open questions include sensitivity to the number of scales S and angular radii, scalability to very large datasets where per-region loss computation may become expensive, and whether the approach extends naturally to non-visual modalities. The absence of error bars and reliance on a single backbone (ResNet-50) with somewhat dated baselines (SimCLR, MoCo-v2 from 2020) limits the strength of the empirical claims.
