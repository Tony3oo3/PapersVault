## Problem

Existing Video Temporal Grounding methods are fundamentally correlation-driven: they overfit to dataset-specific stylistic biases (visual decoration patterns, linguistic phrasings) rather than learning genuine semantic causality, and they assume a relevant segment always exists, making them unable to reject ungrounded queries. CausalVTG addresses both issues by explicitly modeling the data-generating process with a structural causal model and applying front-door adjustment to isolate true causal effects from spurious confounding correlations.

## Input / Output

The model consumes an untrimmed video encoded as a sequence of L_v clips (each a 256-dim vector from InternVideo2-CLIP) and a natural language query of L_q tokens, producing three outputs: (1) a set of predicted temporal spans (start, end, confidence) for moment retrieval, (2) per-clip saliency scores in [0,1] for highlight detection, and (3) a scalar query-relevance score in [0,1] that gates whether any grounding result is emitted. The multi-scale temporal pyramid operates at strides {1,2,4,8} for standard-length videos and extends to {1,2,4,8,16} for longer ActivityNet clips.

## Method

CausalVTG formulates VTG as a structural causal model where stylistic variations act as confounders on the back-door path X←Z→Y, and introduces latent mediators M to enable front-door adjustment along X→M→Y. The Causality-Aware Disentangled Encoder (CADE) implements this by computing modality-specific mediator representations via self-attention, then approximating the interventional distribution P(Y|do(X=x)) through K-means-sampled inputs and attention-weighted aggregation (E[h(x')] + E[g(m)]), yielding confound-free video and query embeddings. A Multi-Scale Temporal Perception Module refines these embeddings via trilinear query-guided attention and a temporal convolutional feature pyramid at multiple resolutions to capture both fine-grained and long-range event dynamics. Training jointly optimizes SampledNCE loss for highlight detection, L1 + focal loss for moment retrieval, and a Dynamic Binary Cross-Entropy loss for query relevance, with the latter constructed via counterfactual negative pairs (video paired with an ungrounded query) to teach the model to abstain when no valid segment exists.

## Results

On QVHighlights, CausalVTG achieves state-of-the-art moment retrieval with R1@0.5 of 68.87, R1@0.7 of 52.53, and mAP (avg) of 49.63, surpassing the previous best R2-Tuning by +0.84, +3.18, and +3.46 respectively. On Charades-STA, the method reaches R1@0.5 of 70.89 and mIoU of 59.96, improving over the prior best (EAMAT) by +9.71 and +5.43. On the query-relevance benchmarks Charades-RF and ActivityNet-RF, CausalVTG achieves grounding accuracy of 84.78% and 89.20%, outperforming RaTSG (76.85% and 84.27%) by a substantial margin, demonstrating superior ability to reject ungrounded queries.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CausalVTG outperforms all 19 baselines on the primary QVHighlights MR metrics, with the largest gains over R2-Tuning (+3.18 R1@0.7, +3.46 mAP Avg) and CG-DETR (+3.44 R1@0.7, +6.77 mAP Avg), while matching or slightly trailing R2-Tuning on HD mAP (40.66 vs 40.75). On Charades-STA, the improvement is dramatic: CausalVTG achieves 70.89 R1@0.5 versus 61.18 for EAMAT and 59.78 for R2-Tuning, a gap of roughly 9–11 points attributable to causal disentanglement of linguistic style. On ActivityNet-Caption, CausalVTG leads on R1@0.3 (64.44) and mIoU (45.74) but is marginally behind SeqPAN on R1@0.7 (26.28 vs 26.69). On the false-query benchmarks, CausalVTG dominates RaTSG by +7.93 Acc on Charades-RF and +4.93 Acc on ActivityNet-RF, with corresponding gains of +6.84 and +1.76 in R1@0.7, confirming that the counterfactual contrastive objective enables reliable abstention on ungrounded queries.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the causal-inference-for-video-grounding line initiated by DCM (back-door adjustment for temporal biases, SIGIR 2021) and IVG (interventional grounding with dual contrastive learning, CVPR 2021) by replacing back-door with front-door adjustment, introducing latent mediators to block confounding paths without requiring explicit confounder observation. The separable causal feature function and attention-based expectation estimation are directly adapted from Wang et al. (CVPR 2024) on vision-and-language navigation, while the attention-based mediator aggregation follows Yang et al. (SIGIR 2021). The paper also builds on the query-relevance formulation introduced by RaTSG (NeurIPS 2024) but replaces its transformer-based relevance head with a counterfactual contrastive objective, and positions itself against the dominant CLIP-finetuning paradigm (R2-Tuning, ECCV 2024) and DETR-based fusion approaches (QD-DETR, CG-DETR) that it argues are fundamentally correlation-driven.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses two practical failure modes of current VTG systems—sensitivity to superficial linguistic/visual cues and inability to abstain on ungrounded queries—that limit real-world deployment. The large accuracy gains on RF benchmarks (84.78% vs. 76.85% on Charades-RF) demonstrate that causal modeling can substantially improve robustness beyond what stronger backbones or fusion strategies achieve. However, the ablation reveals that the multi-scale temporal perception module contributes far more to localization accuracy than the causal encoder itself, suggesting the causal component's primary value lies in query-relevance discrimination rather than moment localization. Open questions include whether the front-door formulation generalizes to audio-visual or egocentric settings, and whether the K-means-based mediator sampling can be replaced by learned, input-adaptive mechanisms.
