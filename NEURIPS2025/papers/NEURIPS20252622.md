## Problem

SOTA monocular, binocular, and multi-view depth estimation methods are all systematically fooled by 3D visual illusions—flat surfaces that simulate 3D spatial relationships (e.g., wall paintings, screen replays, mirrors)—yet no prior work has quantified this vulnerability or provided a unified solution. The paper identifies that monocular and stereo methods exhibit complementary failure modes (monocular fails on texture-based illusions, stereo fails on reflective/transparent surfaces), but no existing framework adaptively leverages both cues under illusion conditions.

## Input / Output

The method takes a rectified stereo image pair (left and right RGB views, up to 1080×1920) and a language prompt describing materials that confuse stereo matching (e.g., mirrors, glass, showcases). It produces a final metric-scale disparity map at full resolution and an intermediate confidence map (at 1/4 resolution) that encodes per-region reliability of binocular disparity. The monocular branch outputs affine-invariant inverse depth, which is aligned to metric scale via learned per-pixel affine parameters before fusion.

## Method

The architecture has two components: (1) a dual-branch prediction network where a GRU-based iterative stereo matcher (RAFT-Stereo style) and a frozen DepthAnything V2 monocular network run in parallel, with monocular features injected as left-view context into the stereo cost volume; and (2) a VLM-based fusion network with three stages—QwenVL2-7B extracts conditional embeddings from the left image plus disparity maps under a language prompt, a FLUX-style flow-matching decoder (trained with LoRA) generates a confidence map from Gaussian noise conditioned on those embeddings, and a global fusion stage aligns monocular disparity to metric scale via learned affine parameters (refined by pooling high-confidence neighbors) before confidence-weighted fusion with binocular disparity. The key insight is that VLM commonsense reasoning identifies regions where stereo matching is unreliable (mirrors, glass) versus where monocular priors are misleading (inpainting, screens), enabling adaptive per-region cue selection. Training is supervised with L1 disparity loss on iteratively updated predictions plus a Focal Loss on the confidence map, pre-trained on SceneFlow and fine-tuned on the 3D-Visual-Illusion virtual dataset.

## Results

On the real-world 3D-Visual-Illusion test set (illusion regions), the proposed method achieves EPE 1.77, bad-2 26.72%, and δ₁ 99.60%, matching the best stereo baselines (Selective-RAFT: EPE 1.58) while dramatically outperforming all monocular methods (best: DA V2 at EPE 5.81) and multi-view foundation models (best: VGGT at EPE 6.16). On the Booster dataset (zero-shot, quarter resolution), the method achieves EPE 2.43 and bad-2 13.84% overall, beating the best stereo baseline MochaStereo (EPE 3.79, bad-2 16.77%) by over 35% in EPE. On Middlebury (half resolution, metric disparity), the method achieves EPE 1.50, outperforming RAFT-Stereo (1.92) and all other stereo baselines, confirming no degradation on standard scenes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method is compared against 10 baselines spanning monocular, multi-view, and stereo paradigms. Against monocular methods (DA V2, Metric3D, DepthPro, Marigold), the proposed fusion model achieves 3–12× lower EPE (1.77 vs. 5.81–21.16), confirming that texture-based depth cues are severely misled by illusion patterns. Against multi-view foundation models (Dust3R, VGGT), the method reduces EPE by ~3.4× (1.77 vs. 6.16–6.74), showing that these models inherit monocular bias in illusion-rich scenes. Against stereo baselines (RAFT-Stereo, Selective-RAFT, Selective-IGEV, MochaStereo), the method is within 0.2 EPE on the 3D-Visual-Illusion test set (1.77 vs. 1.58–1.75), indicating it preserves strong matching constraints. However, on the Booster dataset (zero-shot, transparent/specular objects), the method clearly wins over all stereo baselines with EPE 2.43 vs. 3.79–4.52, demonstrating the VLM-driven fusion's advantage on mirror illusions where pure stereo fails. On Middlebury (standard scenes), the method achieves the best EPE (1.50) among all compared stereo methods, confirming no performance degradation outside illusion settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three established lines of work: GRU-based iterative stereo matching (RAFT-Stereo, Selective-IGEV), large-scale monocular depth estimation (DepthAnything V2, Marigold, Metric3D), and vision-language models for scene understanding (Qwen2-VL). Its key departure is the introduction of a dedicated 3D-visual-illusion benchmark and a VLM-guided fusion architecture that uses flow-matching (FLUX) to generate confidence maps from VLM embeddings, rather than relying on purely geometric or texture-based cues. The dual-branch design reuses frozen DepthAnything V2 features as left-view context for the stereo GRU, and the VLM is prompted with material-level commonsense (e.g., 'transparent or reflective objects?') rather than explicit illusion taxonomy, positioning the approach as a commonsense-reasoning layer atop standard stereo/monocular pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 3D-Visual-Illusion dataset (~3k scenes, 200k images, five illusion types) fills a notable gap: prior depth benchmarks (SceneFlow, Booster, Middlebury) do not systematically stress-test models against perceptually deceptive flat-surface content, which is increasingly relevant for AR/VR and autonomous driving safety. The paper's central empirical finding—that monocular and stereo methods exhibit complementary, predictable failure modes across illusion types, and that fine-tuning stereo models on mixed illusion data actively degrades non-illusion performance due to conflicting priors—provides a useful diagnostic framework. However, the VLM-driven fusion is computationally expensive (54 GB memory, ~4.8 s/iteration vs. 0.87 s for RAFT-Stereo), the real-world subset covers only three of five illusion types, and the virtual data pipeline still depends on labor-intensive manual SAM2 annotation, leaving open questions about scalability, automatic data generation, and lightweight alternatives to the VLM fusion stage.
