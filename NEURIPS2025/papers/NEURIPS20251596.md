## Problem

Optimization-based pruning methods for LLMs cannot effectively leverage the sparsity of intermediate pruned networks to reduce computation and memory, because the sub-network structure changes every iteration, making frequent model instantiation infeasible. No prior method could instantiate compact sparse sub-models throughout the pruning process without incurring prohibitive overhead, leaving a gap between the theoretical benefits of sparsity and practical resource constraints during pruning.

## Input / Output

The method takes a dense DNN (e.g., Llama2-7B at 12.6 GB in BF16), a training corpus (C4 for LLMs, ImageNet-1K for vision models), and a target prune rate (30%–50%), and produces a pruned sparse DNN where pruned modules (attention heads, MLP channels) are physically removed rather than zeroed out. Structural parameters s are initialized with heuristic scores (e.g., Wanda-sp) and optimized via forward-pass-only policy gradient updates, requiring no backpropagation through the full dense network. The instantiated sub-models are processed sequentially to keep peak memory at 6.3 GB for Llama2-7B at 50% sparsity.

## Method

The core contribution is a reweighting technique derived from importance sampling that estimates the gradient of the current structural parameters s by reweighting the policy gradient computed on an outdated sub-model instantiated τ steps earlier, reducing instantiation frequency from every iteration to every τ iterations. The reweighting function ω(m) = p(m|s)/p(m|s̃) corrects for the distributional shift between current and outdated masks, yielding an unbiased gradient estimator that operates entirely on the compact sparse sub-model. Three variance reduction techniques stabilize training: PPO-style clipping of ω(m) to [1−ε, 1], baseline subtraction using independently sampled masks to reduce loss variance, and a diagonal preconditioning matrix H^α = diag(s∘(1−s))^α to counteract small denominators in the score function. The algorithm requires only forward passes of the sparsified model (no backpropagation for structural updates), and extends naturally to distributed sparse training where only two scalars per client per iteration (the reweighting value and training loss) are communicated in real time.

## Results

On Llama2-7B at 50% sparsity, the method achieves 26.83 WikiText2 PPL and 44.23 PTB PPL, substantially outperforming all metric-based baselines (LLM-Pruner: 178.32, SliceGPT: 71.72, Wanda-sp: 101.72, FLAP: 105.90). The pruned model requires only 6.3 GB memory (vs. 12.6 GB dense) and delivers a 1.58× forward-pass speedup. On Llama3-8B at 50% sparsity, the method reaches 57.14 WikiText2 PPL, compared to 129.16 (LLM-Pruner) and 155.70 (SliceGPT). In distributed sparse training on ImageNet-1K, the method achieves 75.2% top-1 accuracy on ResNet-50 with 48.1% params and 46.5% FLOPs in 9.79 s, versus 23.00 s for dense distributed training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against metric-based pruning baselines, the proposed method dominates across all models and sparsity levels: on Llama2-7B at 50% sparsity it achieves 26.83 WikiText2 PPL versus 178.32 (LLM-Pruner), 71.72 (SliceGPT), 101.72 (Wanda-sp), and 105.90 (FLAP), with the gap widening as sparsity increases. Against optimization-based baselines, the method achieves comparable or better perplexity (26.83 vs. 49.28 for Compresso; 26.83 vs. 24.89 for NutePrune) while using only 6.4 GB memory (vs. 33.3 GB and 37.5 GB respectively) and requiring no backward pass. In the distributed sparse training setting on ImageNet-1K, the method matches or exceeds the accuracy of LocalSGD (75.2% vs. 75.0% on ResNet-50), PowerSGD (75.2% vs. 76.0%), and TSNNS (75.2% vs. 74.5%) while reducing params to ~48% and FLOPs to ~46%, and completing training in 9.79 s versus 19–23 s for dense and 115 s for the base solver EffTrain. The base solver EffTrain (without reweighting) achieves similar accuracy but takes 115 s on ResNet-50 due to frequent instantiation, confirming that the reweighting technique is the key efficiency gain.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the policy-gradient-based sparse training framework of Zhou et al. (EffTrain, NeurIPS 2021), which already enables unbiased structural parameter updates via forward-pass-only gradient estimation. The key departure is the introduction of a reweighting mechanism inspired by importance sampling (and PPO-style clipping) that allows a single instantiated compact sub-model to serve multiple optimization steps, rather than requiring re-instantiation at every iteration. This positions the work between metric-based LLM pruning (Wanda, FLAP, SliceGPT, LLM-Pruner), which avoids backpropagation but suffers at high sparsity, and optimization-based methods (Compresso, NutePrune, Sheared LLaMA), which achieve better quality but demand full-network backpropagation and 30+ GB of memory. The distributed sparse training extension repurposes the same reweighting idea to reduce server-client communication frequency, connecting to the broader distributed optimization literature (LocalSGD, PowerSGD, TSNNS).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: pruning Llama2-7B to 50% sparsity with only 6 GB of memory (versus 33–37 GB for optimization-based baselines) and a 1.58× forward-pass speedup makes structured pruning feasible on consumer-grade hardware, addressing the core resource mismatch between LLM training and pruning. The distributed sparse training extension, requiring only two scalar communications per iteration, opens a path toward efficient federated or multi-node sparse training. Open questions include whether the method extends to joint weight-and-structure optimization at scale, how performance degrades beyond 50% sparsity, and whether the reweighting variance reduction (clipping + preconditioning) generalizes to non-Bernoulli mask distributions or continuous sparsity schedules.
