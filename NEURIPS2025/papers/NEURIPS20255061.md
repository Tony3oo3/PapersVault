## Problem

Joint Maximum Mean Discrepancy (JMMD), a distribution-distance metric for aligning joint feature-label distributions in domain adaptation, is difficult to incorporate into subspace-learning frameworks because its empirical estimation involves a tensor-product operator with a nontrivial partial derivative. Additionally, recent observations suggest that distribution alignment via JMMD unexpectedly degrades feature discrimination, but the underlying reason has lacked theoretical explanation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255061/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes pre-extracted feature vectors (SURF 800-dim, DECAF-6 4096-dim, or ResNet-50 2048-dim) from a labeled source domain and an unlabeled target domain, along with source class labels (hard or soft). It produces a finite-dimensional projected feature representation via an optimized projection matrix B in a kernel-induced subspace, which is then fed to a downstream classifier (1-NN, SVM, label propagation, or nearest class prototype) to predict target-domain class labels. The target labels are never used during training, only for evaluation.

## Method

Using the Representer theorem, the paper derives a concise JMMD that eliminates the tensor-product operator and replaces the infinite-dimensional projection matrix T with a finite-dimensional B, making gradient-based optimization tractable in subspace-learning frameworks. It then proves that marginal, class-conditional, and weighted class-conditional MMD are all special cases of JMMD parameterized by different label reproducing kernels (K¹, K², K³), establishing a unified theoretical view. Inspired by graph embedding, the paper reveals that the similarity weights strengthening intra-class compactness in HSIC take opposite signs in JMMD's implicit graph, explaining why JMMD pushes same-class same-domain points apart and thus degrades discrimination. To counteract this, the paper proposes a JMMD-HSIC loss that jointly minimizes JMMD (for distribution alignment) and a domain-specific HSIC with a designed label kernel K⁴ (for intra-class compactness), balanced by a hyperparameter δ, and validates it within JDA, SPL, and OGL²P subspace-learning frameworks.

## Results

On Office-31 (ResNet-50 features), OGL2P+JMMD-HSIC achieves the best average accuracy of 91.5%, outperforming the second-best RSDA-MSTN (91.2%) by 0.3% and the baseline OGL2P (90.7%) by 0.8%. On Office-Home, OGL2P+JMMD-HSIC reaches 73.6% average accuracy, surpassing RSDA-MSTN (73.1%) by 0.5% and OGL2P (72.8%) by 0.8%. SPL+JMMD-HSIC also improves over SPL by 1.4% on Office-31 (91.0% vs. 89.6%) and 0.9% on Office-Home (71.9% vs. 71.0%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against 8 baselines spanning shallow (SPL, PGCD, RMMD-I) and deep (BSP+MetaReg, DRDA, RSDA-MSTN, Jeffreys-DD, OGL2P) domain adaptation methods. OGL2P+JMMD-HSIC achieves the best average accuracy on both Office-31 (91.5%) and Office-Home (73.6%), beating the strongest deep baseline RSDA-MSTN by 0.3% and 0.5% respectively. SPL+JMMD-HSIC improves over its baseline SPL by 1.4% on Office-31 and 0.9% on Office-Home, while OGL2P+JMMD-HSIC improves over OGL2P by 0.8% on both datasets. The gains are most pronounced on the D→A and W→A transfer directions (e.g., 78.8% vs. 77.5% for D→A on Office-31), suggesting JMMD-HSIC particularly benefits tasks with larger domain gaps. The method consistently outperforms all MMD-based baselines (RMMD-I, PGCD) and f-divergence-based methods (Jeffreys-DD), confirming that the joint JMMD-HSIC loss provides a more effective balance between distribution alignment and feature discriminability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper re-examines the joint maximum mean discrepancy (JMMD) metric introduced by Long et al. (ICML 2017) through the lens of the Representer theorem, deriving a concise kernel-matrix form that eliminates the intractable tensor-product operator and makes JMMD compatible with subspace-learning frameworks. Conceptually, it unifies the MMD family (marginal, class-conditional, weighted class-conditional) as special cases of JMMD under different label reproducing kernels, and draws a graph-embedding analogy to HSIC (Gretton et al., 2005) to explain why JMMD degrades feature discrimination. The proposed JMMD-HSIC loss is then plugged into three existing shallow DA frameworks (JDA, SPL, OGL2P) rather than introducing a new architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The uniformity theorem provides a principled recipe for designing label kernels tailored to specific DA scenarios (e.g., class imbalance, label shift), and the graph-embedding sign-flip explanation gives a clear geometric intuition for the long-observed but previously unexplained tension between distribution alignment and discriminability. However, the practical gains are modest (≤1.4% over strong baselines) and confined to shallow subspace-learning methods; the paper does not address whether these insights transfer to deep DA pipelines, which dominate current practice. Open directions include extending the label-kernel design to deep networks and handling more complex domain shifts.
