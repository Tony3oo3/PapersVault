## Problem

Existing gaze prediction methods for medical imaging rely on generic vision-language models and saliency-based features that fail to capture fine-grained clinical semantics and integrate domain-specific knowledge. No prior work jointly models clinical semantics, task conditioning, and probabilistic scanpaths in a unified end-to-end architecture for chest X-ray interpretation, and the application of logit-lens decoding to medical VLMs for patch-level gaze grounding had not been explored.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a chest X-ray resized to 768×768 (split into a 24×24 grid of 576 patches for the VLM), a diagnostic label string (e.g., 'pneumonia') encoded via SentenceTransformer, and the first human fixation coordinate as a conditioning signal. It outputs a sequence of L stochastic fixations, each represented as a Gaussian distribution over (x, y) coordinates and dwell duration, sampled via the reparameterization trick to capture natural variability in human gaze behavior.

## Method

LogitGaze-Med is a 6-layer encoder-decoder transformer (hidden size 512) that fuses three modalities: visual features from a domain-specific CNN backbone (CheXNet/DenseNet-121, ResNet50-FPN, CheSS, or PEAC), a broadcasted task embedding from a SentenceTransformer, and patch-level semantic priors extracted via the logit-lens technique from LLaVA-Med. The logit-lens stage projects each of the 576 patch hidden states through the VLM's language-model head, selects top-k vocabulary tokens, and pools their embedding vectors into per-patch semantic vectors that expose latent clinical concepts such as 'consolidation' or 'effusion.' Visual and semantic features are concatenated into 2M tokens with learnable 2D positional encodings, encoded by the transformer, and decoded via L learnable queries (the first modulated by the initial fixation) through six MLP heads predicting spatial means, log-variances, and dwell-time parameters. Training jointly optimizes a masked cross-entropy stop-token loss, L1 spatial regression, and MSE dwell-time regression with fixed weighting coefficients, and the stochastic sampling of fixations enables the model to represent the inherent variability of human scanpaths.

## Results

On the GazeSearch dataset (target-present condition), LogitGaze-Med with CheXNet achieves ScanMatch of 0.419 (w/o duration) and 0.330 (w/ duration), SED of 4.68, and STDE of 0.855, outperforming the strongest baseline (GazeSearch/ChestSearch) by +26% on ScanMatch w/o duration and +48% on ScanMatch w/ duration. In downstream 3-class pathology classification on MIMIC-CXR, LogitGaze-Med-generated fixations yield AUROC of 0.90 (Temporal) and 0.91 (U-Net), exceeding the human-gaze Eye-Gaze baseline (0.82/0.87) by 5–7%. Expert evaluation of synthetic scanpaths scores 4.3/5.0 for visual realism and 4.2/5.0 for clinical relevance, with a radiologist achieving only 58% accuracy in distinguishing real from synthetic scanpaths.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LogitGaze-Med (CheXNet) outperforms all five baselines on every reported scanpath metric. Against GazeFormer [9], it gains +43% on ScanMatch w/o duration (0.419 vs 0.293) and +64% on ScanMatch w/ duration (0.330 vs 0.201). Against HAT [25], the gains are +36% (ScanMatch w/o Dur) and a 7.7% SED reduction (4.68 vs 5.07). Against the domain-specific GazeSearch/ChestSearch baseline [19], LogitGaze-Med improves ScanMatch w/o duration by +26% and w/ duration by +48%, while reducing SED by 4%. Against LogitGaze [28] (the non-medical predecessor), the improvement is +28% on ScanMatch w/o duration and +47% on w/ duration, confirming the value of medical VLM semantic priors. In the downstream classification task (Table 3), LogitGaze-Med-generated fixations surpass the Eye-Gaze human baseline [11] by 5–7% AUROC across all three architectures (e.g., 0.91 vs 0.87 in the U-Net setup). Notably, on the cross-domain COCO-Search18 benchmark, LogitGaze-Med underperforms LogitGaze (0.353 vs 0.527 ScanMatch w/o Dur), confirming that its gains are driven by medical-domain specialization rather than generic architectural superiority.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper is a direct medical-domain extension of LogitGaze [28], which first demonstrated that logit-lens decoding of VLM intermediate activations can provide interpretable semantic priors for goal-directed gaze prediction in general vision. The key conceptual departure is replacing the general-purpose VLM (LLaVA) with medical-specific models (LLaVA-Med, LLaVA-Rad) and augmenting the visual stream with domain-specific encoders (CheXNet, CheSS, PEAC) alongside textual diagnostic label embeddings, thereby grounding scanpath prediction in clinical semantics rather than generic object-level concepts. It also builds on the GazeSearch benchmark [19] and the broader transformer-based scanpath prediction lineage (GazeFormer, HAT), but is the first to integrate patch-level logit-lens semantics from a medical VLM into the prediction loop.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that clinically grounded semantic priors from medical VLMs can meaningfully improve both scanpath fidelity (20–30% over baselines) and downstream pathology classification (>5% AUROC), suggesting that gaze prediction is not merely a cognitive modeling exercise but a practical tool for augmenting diagnostic pipelines. Expert validation (4.3/5.0 realism, 58% discrimination accuracy) supports the feasibility of generating synthetic training data to overcome the scarcity of real radiologist gaze recordings. Open questions include cross-institutional generalization, sensitivity to VLM activation noise from non-content tokens, and whether the approach extends to other modalities (CT, MRI) or to real-time interactive radiology workflows.
