## Problem

VLA models built on pre-trained VLMs incur prohibitive training and inference costs, and existing sparsification strategies (Mixture-of-Depths, layer skipping, early exit) optimize only intra-LLM computation while ignoring semantic coupling across vision, language, and action modalities. This modular disconnection causes cross-modal degradation: visual compression discards task-relevant features, token skipping disrupts reference resolution, and action generation loses causal reasoning over multimodal state transitions.

## Input / Output

CogVLA consumes multi-view RGB images (front and wrist cameras) together with a natural-language instruction, and outputs a parallel-decoded action chunk of K steps (K=8 in simulation, K=25 in real-world), where each step is a 7-dim vector encoding 3-DoF translation, 3-DoF rotation, and binary gripper state. A key structural detail is that the visual input is progressively compressed—first to 25% of original token count via encoder aggregation, then further pruned by ~50% within the LLM—so the LLM operates on roughly 12.5% of the raw visual tokens, and all K actions are produced in a single forward pass rather than K×D sequential autoregressive steps.

## Method

CogVLA introduces a 3-stage progressive architecture on the OpenVLA backbone that emulates human VAS–SMA–PMC coordination. Stage 1 (EFA-Routing) injects the instruction into dual vision encoders (SigLIP and DINOv2) via FiLM scale-and-shift modulation, consolidating each encoder's output into 64 aggregation tokens (25% compression), then fusing the two branches with an instruction-conditioned sigmoid gate. Stage 2 (LFP-Routing) applies LLM-FiLM modulation plus a Task-Guided Pruning Router that computes per-token relevance scores via an MLP and retains only the top-β percentile of visual tokens at each layer, with β following a shifted cosine schedule across 32 LLM layers to achieve ~50% pruning. Stage 3 (CAtten) enforces a hybrid attention mask: causal attention over the vision-language segment preserves instruction-conditioned reasoning, while bidirectional attention within the action segment enables coherent parallel decoding of the full chunk. The model is fine-tuned with LoRA (rank 32, α=64) on LIBERO and ALOHA data, achieving 97.4% LIBERO success and 70.0% real-world success while reducing FLOPs by 3.1× and inference latency by 2.8× versus OpenVLA.

## Results

On the LIBERO benchmark, CogVLA achieves a state-of-the-art average success rate of 97.4%, ranking first on Spatial (98.6%), Object (98.8%), and Long (95.4%) suites, and second on Goal (96.6%). On real-world Cobot Agilex ALOHA tasks, CogVLA reaches 70.0% average success rate, outperforming the next-best OpenVLA-OFT (56.7%) by 13.3 percentage points. In terms of efficiency, CogVLA reduces inference latency to 0.091s (2.8× faster than OpenVLA's 0.254s), cuts FLOPs to 2.72T (3.1× lower than OpenVLA's 8.48T), and shortens training cost to 4.7h/10k steps (2.5× less than OpenVLA's 11.7h).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CogVLA outperforms all 16 baselines on the LIBERO average success rate (97.4%), beating the closest competitor OpenVLA-OFT (97.1%) by 0.3 points and π₀.5-KI (96.0%) by 1.4 points. It ranks first on three of four suites (Spatial, Object, Long) but trails OpenVLA-OFT on the Goal suite (96.6% vs. 97.9%), which the authors attribute to the deliberate 8× visual sparsification trade-off. On real-world ALOHA tasks, CogVLA leads by a wide margin: 70.0% vs. 56.7% (OpenVLA-OFT), 50.0% (PD-VLA), and 45.0% (STAR), with particular strength on multi-step drawer manipulation (7/10 close-drawer) and T-shirt folding (9/10 step 1). In efficiency, CogVLA achieves 2.79× faster inference (0.091s vs. 0.254s for OpenVLA), 3.12× lower FLOPs (2.72T vs. 8.48T), and 2.49× shorter training (4.7h vs. 11.7h per 10k steps), while also surpassing the more recent efficient baselines OpenVLA-OFT (0.132s, 8.45T) and PD-VLA (0.143s, 8.48T) on both speed and compute. Against visual compression methods FastV and SliME, CogVLA's combined Stage 1+2 sparsification achieves 98.6% SR versus 88.2% and 77.6% respectively, demonstrating that instruction-driven routing preserves task-relevant semantics far better than similarity-based or cropping-based token reduction.

## Relation to Prior Work

CogVLA builds directly on OpenVLA as its VLA backbone and repurposes the FiLM conditioning mechanism (Perez et al., 2018) for instruction-driven visual token modulation. It departs from prior sparsification work (Mixture-of-Depths, layer skipping, early exit) that optimizes computation within isolated LLM stages by instead coupling vision-encoder compression (EFA-Routing) with LLM-level token pruning (LFP-Routing) under a shared instruction signal, and introduces a hybrid causal/bidirectional attention mask (CAtten) for parallel action-chunk decoding. The cognitive-science framing (VAS–SMA–PMC) provides a principled design rationale absent from prior efficiency-focused VLA methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 8× visual-token reduction while achieving SOTA LIBERO success (97.4%) and 2.5×/2.8× reductions in training/inference cost over OpenVLA makes VLA deployment on resource-constrained hardware more realistic. The ablation showing asymmetric sparsification (4× encoder + 2× LLM) outperforming symmetric splits offers a practical design principle for future multimodal compression. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond the LIBERO/ALOHA settings to more diverse or longer-horizon tasks, and whether the 8× compression ratio remains optimal as base models scale. The real-world 70% success rate, while SOTA, still leaves substantial room for improvement in robustness.
