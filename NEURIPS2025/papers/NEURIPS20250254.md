## Problem

Existing multi-subject personalization methods for Diffusion Transformers inject reference image features directly into attention mechanisms, which disrupts the base model's sampling trajectory and causes artifacts, attribute entanglement, and loss of editability. The paper identifies the text-stream modulation pathway (AdaLN) within DiT blocks as an underexplored route for subject-specific control that can preserve structural integrity while enabling fine-grained conditioning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250254/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes one or more reference images (for individual subjects) and a text prompt in which specific tokens correspond to each controlled subject. Reference images are encoded via CLIP (for semantic features) and VAE (for pixel-level details), while the prompt is encoded via CLIP text embeddings. The output is a single generated image where each subject's identity and semantic attributes (pose, style, lighting) are controlled by its corresponding reference image, with the composition and editability matching the base FLUX.1-dev T2I model. The approach is tuning-free at inference time, requiring no per-subject fine-tuning.

## Method

XVerse transforms reference image CLIP features into token-specific offsets via a Perceiver Resampler (T-Mod Adapter), which are added to the conditioning vector y in the text-stream AdaLN modulation of DiT blocks. Inspired by StyleGAN's W+ expansion, the offset is decomposed into a shared component (applied across all blocks) and per-block components, enabling hierarchical control. VAE-encoded reference features are injected exclusively into FLUX's single-stream blocks as an auxiliary detail-enhancement module (not the primary conditioning signal), with position indices converted from (i,j) to individual indices following UNO. Two regularization losses enforce disentanglement: a Region Preservation Loss (L2 on non-modulated regions against the T2I branch) and a Text-Image Attention Loss (L2 on cross-attention maps to maintain editability). Training proceeds in three stages—text modulation alignment, VAE feature integration, and cross-image appearance mapping—using LoRA (rank 128) on FLUX.1-dev over 230K total iterations.

## Results

On XVerseBench, XVerse achieves the highest Overall score of 67.76, outperforming the next-best method DreamO (65.47) by 2.29 points. For single-subject generation, XVerse leads with an AVG of 71.13, driven by the best identity similarity (ID-Sim 63.02) and object similarity (IP-Sim 71.35). For the more challenging multi-subject setting, XVerse achieves an AVG of 64.38, with the highest ID-Sim (51.03) and DPG (91.77) among all compared methods, demonstrating superior attribute disentanglement across multiple subjects.

## Baselines & Comparisons

XVerse outperforms all five baselines on the Overall score, with the closest competitor being DreamO (65.47 vs. 67.76). In single-subject generation, XVerse leads on ID-Sim (63.02 vs. 60.17 for OmniGen, the runner-up) and IP-Sim (71.35 vs. 70.73 for OmniGen), but trails DreamO on DPG (93.50 vs. 97.51), suggesting DreamO has a slight edge in prompt-following for single subjects. In multi-subject generation, XVerse dominates across all four metrics: DPG (91.77 vs. 89.75 for DreamO), ID-Sim (51.03 vs. 44.21 for DreamO), IP-Sim (61.04 vs. 60.87 for DreamO), and AES (53.68 vs. 51.16 for DreamO). The gap is most pronounced on ID-Sim in the multi-subject setting, where XVerse's text-stream modulation approach preserves individual identities far better than methods that inject features directly into attention (e.g., UNO at 26.00, MIP-Adapter at 19.49). MS-Diffusion and MIP-Adapter lag significantly behind on identity preservation (ID-Sim below 30), confirming that attention-based injection strategies struggle with multi-subject disentanglement.

## Relation to Prior Work

XVerse builds on the DiT modulation mechanism (AdaLN) from Stable Diffusion 3/FLUX and repurposes the text-stream modulation pathway—previously used only for general timestep/text conditioning—as a channel for subject-specific identity injection, drawing conceptual inspiration from StyleGAN's AdaIN. It departs from concurrent multi-subject methods (OmniControl, UniReal, UNO, DreamO) that inject reference features through attention or input-token conditioning, instead learning per-token offsets in the modulation space via a Perceiver Resampler. The VAE-encoded detail injection is constrained to FLUX's single-stream blocks (adopting UNO's position-index scheme), positioning it as an auxiliary rather than dominant signal, and the tuning-free design contrasts with fine-tuning-based personalization (DreamBooth, Textual Inversion) and modulation-based but subject-specific fine-tuning (Tokenverse).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By routing subject identity through modulation offsets rather than attention, XVerse preserves the base model's editability and compositional structure—a practical advantage for production pipelines where users need to swap subjects while keeping scene layout intact. The natural generalization to semantic attributes (pose, lighting, style) without additional conditioning branches is a useful property, though the paper's own limitations section acknowledges the scarcity of high-quality cross-image multi-subject training data and the unexplored potential of image-stream modulation for pixel-level control. XVerseBench provides a more holistic evaluation protocol (combining DPG, Face ID, DINOv2, and Aesthetic scores across single/dual/triple-subject settings) that the community can adopt for future multi-subject personalization work.
