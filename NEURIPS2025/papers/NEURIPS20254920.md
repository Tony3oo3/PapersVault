## Problem

CLIP's pre-training relies on uncurated web-sourced image-text pairs, making it vulnerable to data poisoning and backdoor attacks. While prior work has extensively studied image-based perturbations and visual triggers, the text modality—equally central to CLIP's contrastive learning—remains largely unexplored as an attack surface. Two practical challenges hinder effective text-based attacks: background content in target-class texts often semantically conflicts with the poisoning objective, and many classes lack sufficient background-consistent texts in open corpora.

## Input / Output

The framework consumes a target-class image-text corpus (1M samples from CC12M), a source-class image x_A, and frozen CLIP encoders (ViT-B/32 substitute model for embedding extraction; ResNet-50 + Transformer victim model for training). It outputs a poisoned dataset D_p = {(x_A, t_{p,B})} where each poisoned text is a fluent, grammatically correct English sentence whose background semantics are aligned with target class B. The generation is iterative (M rounds), with each round expanding the candidate text pool via the augmenter before final poisoned pairs are assembled.

## Method

ToxicTextCLIP is a two-module, iteratively executed framework. The Background-aware Target Text Selector decomposes each target-class caption into background and class-relevant components by enumerating all subsets of up to η removed words, then selects the background candidate that maximizes image-text similarity minus class-centroid similarity (using a multi-prompt-averaged CLIP text embedding Z_B for stability). The Background-driven Poisoned Text Augmenter is a 6-layer Transformer decoder trained with KL-divergence loss to reconstruct captions from augmented text features (f_j^T + λf_j^I) conditioned on image patch embeddings via cross-attention; it generates diverse candidates using Diverse Beam Search and applies Jaccard-similarity-based greedy selection to ensure output diversity. The two modules run iteratively, feeding augmented texts back into the selector pool to progressively improve semantic alignment and coverage. This design ensures poisoned texts are both semantically coherent with the target class (high background-class alignment) and diverse enough to generalize across multiple source images.

## Results

On CC3M, ToxicTextCLIP achieves 95.83% attack success rate (ASR) for single-target image poisoning, substantially outperforming the mmPoison baseline (62.50%) while maintaining comparable clean accuracy (32.23% vs. 31.52%). For backdoor attacks, the method reaches 92.66% Hit@1 for word-level triggers and 98.68% Hit@1 for sentence-level triggers on YFCC, compared to 72.13% and 64.41% for the random-trigger baseline, respectively. On the noisier YFCC dataset, poisoning ASR still reaches 91.67% versus 66.67% for mmPoison, confirming robustness to data noise.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against mmPoison (Yang et al., ICML 2023), the state-of-the-art text poisoning method for CLIP, ToxicTextCLIP achieves a 33.33-point ASR improvement on CC3M (95.83% vs. 62.50%) and a 25-point gain on YFCC (91.67% vs. 66.67%), while preserving clean accuracy within 1 point. For backdoor attacks, where no prior text-based CLIP backdoor method exists, the paper constructs a random-trigger baseline; ToxicTextCLIP outperforms it by 20.53 points in Hit@1 for word-level backdoors on CC3M (92.66% vs. 72.13%) and by 34.27 points for sentence-level backdoors on YFCC (98.68% vs. 64.41%). In multimodal settings, combining ToxicTextCLIP with MetaPoison (Huang et al., NeurIPS 2020) raises image-poisoning ASR from 27.45% to 35.19%, and pairing it with ImgBackdoor (Carlini et al., ICLR 2022) improves Hit@1 from 79.27% to 85.73%, demonstrating synergistic cross-modal attack effects. Across all comparisons, ToxicTextCLIP consistently wins on the primary attack metric while maintaining or slightly improving clean accuracy, indicating that the background-aware selection and augmentation modules are the key differentiators over simpler caption-substitution strategies.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground in the CLIP security literature by shifting the attack surface from the well-studied image modality to the text modality at the pre-training stage. Prior poisoning and backdoor work on CLIP (Carlini & Terzis 2022, Yang et al. 2023b, Ye et al. 2024, Liang et al. 2024) has focused on visual perturbations, patches, or downstream fine-tuning, while the text pathway—equally central to contrastive alignment—remained largely unexamined. Methodologically, ToxicTextCLIP repurposes a Transformer decoder with cross-attention over image patch embeddings and Diverse Beam Search for adversarial text generation, but its core novelty lies in the background-aware selection and background-driven augmentation pipeline that explicitly separates class-relevant semantics from background content to maintain semantic coherence in poisoned texts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper exposes a practical and persistent attack vector: unlike pixel-level triggers that degrade under compression or cropping, textual triggers survive intact through data collection and distribution pipelines, making them more stealthy and durable in real-world deployment. The finding that all three state-of-the-art defenses (RoCLIP, SafeCLIP, CleanCLIP) fail to mitigate the attack—maintaining 64–75% ASR under poisoning and 60–91% Hit@1 under backdoor settings—highlights a critical gap in current defense strategies that are designed around image-side threats. Key limitations include evaluation on a single CLIP architecture (ResNet-50 + Transformer), English-only text, and two pre-training datasets, leaving open whether the findings generalize to ViT-based CLIP variants, multilingual models, or domain-specific corpora. The paper also raises the question of whether text-to-image generative models (e.g., Stable Diffusion) that rely on CLIP text encoders are similarly vulnerable, a direction only preliminarily explored.
