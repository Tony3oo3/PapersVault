## Problem

Flash Linear Attention (FLA) kernels for linear RNNs are constrained by GPU SRAM to small chunk sizes (typically L=64), forcing many intermediate states to be materialized in high-bandwidth memory. This leads to low arithmetic intensity and high memory consumption, which is especially problematic for long-context pre-training where the number of chunks grows with sequence length. The paper addresses this gap by enabling arbitrary large chunk sizes while maintaining high arithmetic intensity, and by proposing a faster mLSTM variant with reduced computation.

## Input / Output

The kernel consumes chunked tensors: a sequence of length T is split into N_c = ⌈T/L⌉ chunks, yielding Q, K ∈ R^{N_c×L×d_qk}, V ∈ R^{N_c×L×d_hv}, plus scalar input/forget gate pre-activations and vector output gates per time step. The chunk size L is a tunable hyperparameter that trades off HBM memory (more chunks means more materialized states) against quadratic FLOPs in the intra-chunk parallel part. The output is per-chunk hidden states H^(k) ∈ R^{L×d_hv} that feed a language modeling head for next-token prediction.

## Method

Tiled Flash Linear Attention (TFLA) introduces two levels of sequence parallelism: a recurrent kernel that materializes inter-chunk memory states C_k in HBM (first level), and a parallel kernel that tiles the intra-chunk QK^T matrix along the sequence dimension (second level), enabling chunk sizes far beyond SRAM limits. Applied to the mLSTM, TFLA fuses three matrix multiplications into a single Triton kernel with parallelization over five dimensions (batch, head, chunk, sequence, embedding). The paper further proposes mLSTMsig, which replaces the exponential input gate with a sigmoid, eliminating the normalizer and max states and yielding over 30% faster forward kernels at equal language modeling performance up to 1.4B parameters. A transfer behavior analysis inspired by control theory shows both mLSTM variants exhibit identical gain characteristics after the RMS-norm layer, and motivates initializing input gate biases at −10 to improve training stability and final perplexity.

## Results

On DCLM language modeling (context length 4096), mLSTMsig with TFLA kernels achieves validation perplexity of 21.03 (160M), 16.61 (400M), and 13.21 (1.4B), matching mLSTMexp within numerical precision. In kernel runtime benchmarks on NVIDIA H100 GPUs (embedding dim 4096, 65,536 tokens), TFLA training kernels are faster than FlashAttention 3 for longer sequences and over 2× faster than Mamba 2 kernels for all sequence lengths. The TFLA xl_chunk kernels are ~25% faster than the limit_chunk baseline, and the mLSTMsig variant adds another >30% forward-pass speedup over mLSTMexp.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against FlashAttention 3, TFLA training kernels are faster for longer sequence lengths (e.g., ≥8192 tokens per sequence) at embedding dimension 4096 on H100 GPUs, while FlashAttention 3 remains competitive at shorter sequences. Against Mamba 2, TFLA kernels are over 2× faster across all tested sequence lengths in both inference and training. Against GLA (Flash Linear Attention), TFLA achieves lower runtimes due to its two-level sequence parallelism enabling larger chunk sizes and higher arithmetic intensity. Against Llama 2 Transformers, mLSTM models achieve comparable or slightly better validation perplexity at 400M and 1.4B scales (e.g., 16.61 vs. 16.85 at 400M/16 heads; 13.21 vs. 13.64 at 1.4B/16 heads). Within the mLSTM family, mLSTMsig matches mLSTMexp in language modeling quality while delivering >30% faster forward-pass kernel runtimes, and TFLA xl_chunk kernels are ~25% faster than the chunk-size-limited baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

TFLA combines two established kernel strategies: the chunkwise-parallel formulation from Flash Linear Attention (Yang et al., 2024b) and the tiling-based work partitioning from Flash Attention 2 (Dao, 2024), yielding a two-level sequence parallelism that neither prior work achieves alone. The architecture being optimized, the mLSTM (Beck et al., 2024), is itself a linear RNN with matrix memory and exponential gating, and the chunkwise-parallel formulation it relies on traces back to Hua et al. (2022) and Sun et al. (2023). The paper departs from FLA by removing its SRAM-limited chunk size constraint through intra-chunk tiling, and from Flash Attention by applying tiling to a linear (not softmax) attention computation within a recurrent chunk structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TFLA provides the first efficient kernel implementations for the mLSTM, enabling competitive long-context pre-training with linear RNNs and outperforming Flash Attention 3 and Mamba 2 kernels in runtime benchmarks. The finding that the simpler sigmoid input gate (mLSTMsig) matches the exponential-gate variant in language modeling quality while being over 30% faster in kernel runtime offers a practical simplification for future xLSTM deployments. Open questions include whether TFLA's theoretical runtime optimum can be reached with CUDA-level implementations, whether the algorithm extends cleanly to delta-rule linear RNNs like DeltaNet, and whether the gate initialization insights generalize beyond the mLSTM family.
