## Problem

Existing graph-based RAG methods for multi-hop QA suffer from two critical flaws: they prioritize structural relevance over semantic path coherence (e.g., HippoRAG's PPR propagation ignores edge semantics), and they introduce substantial irrelevant noise during node matching or subgraph construction (e.g., LightRAG's neighbor collection). This paper asks whether a goal-directed, path-based retrieval strategy inspired by hippocampal place-cell navigation can overcome these limitations.

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural language query and a pre-built knowledge graph (entities and relation triples extracted per-document by an LLM, with pseudo-coreference sets of top-5 similar entities per node at cosine threshold 0.8) over a source document corpus. It outputs a final candidate document set (union of documents collected along tracked paths and a second-stage retrieval set) plus a generated answer. The path tracking operates in zero-shot mode with a maximum of 2 expansion hops by default, and each hop prunes candidate paths to top-30 via embedding similarity against the LLM-generated expansion requirement.

## Method

NeuroPath simulates hippocampal place-cell preplay and replay in three stages. Static Indexing uses a single LLM call per document to extract entities and relation triples, building a KG where each triple segment is the atomic path unit. Dynamic Path Tracking (preplay) starts from query-derived seed nodes expanded by coreference sets, then iteratively: retrieves connected triple segments, prunes candidates via similarity to the previous hop's LLM-generated expansion requirement, and prompts the LLM to mark valid paths, generate a reasoning chain, produce the next expansion requirement, and decide whether to continue. Post-retrieval Completion (replay) concatenates the final hop's reasoning chain and expansion requirement with the original query for a second-stage dense retrieval, filling gaps in the path-collected documents. The key distinction from prior graph-based methods is that the LLM dynamically filters and expands paths with explicit semantic alignment to the query at every hop, rather than relying on static topological propagation or subgraph aggregation.

## Results

On three multi-hop QA datasets (MuSiQue, 2WikiMultiHopQA, HotpotQA), NeuroPath achieves average Recall@2 of 66.9% and Recall@5 of 81.9% using GPT-4o-mini with Contriever, surpassing the best graph-based baseline HippoRAG 2 (56.5% R@2, 71.0% R@5) by 10.4 and 10.9 percentage points respectively. On QA performance (EM/F1), NeuroPath reaches 48.4/60.7 average, outperforming HippoRAG 2 (41.4/53.3) and Iter-RetGen (43.4/56.0). The method also reduces token consumption by 22.8% compared to iterative RAG baselines while maintaining higher accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NeuroPath outperforms all nine baselines on average retrieval metrics. Against the strongest graph-based method HippoRAG 2, it gains +10.4% R@2 and +10.9% R@5; against HippoRAG, +8.8% R@2 and +7.8% R@5. Over iterative methods, NeuroPath beats Iter-RetGen by +4.8% R@2 and +6.3% R@5, and IRCoT by +11.9% R@2 and +12.2% R@5, while consuming 22.8% fewer tokens. On QA (EM/F1), NeuroPath (48.4/60.7) substantially outperforms LightRAG (9.9/20.3) and PathRAG (17.7/31.2), which fail on multi-hop factual QA despite using graph structures. The only marginal loss is on HotpotQA QA metrics, where HippoRAG 2 slightly edges ahead (50.7 vs 50.5 EM) due to HotpotQA's allowance for shortcut guessing answers. NeuroPath also maintains stable performance across retrievers (Contriever vs BGE-M3), whereas iter-based and graph-based methods show up to 20% sensitivity to retriever choice.

## Relation to Prior Work

NeuroPath carves out a new position between graph-based RAG (HippoRAG, LightRAG) and path-based retrieval (PathRAG) by replacing structural propagation (Personalized PageRank) and static subgraph construction with LLM-driven, goal-directed semantic path tracking over a knowledge graph. Conceptually, it repurposes the neurobiological mechanism of hippocampal place-cell preplay and replay as an analogy for iterative path expansion and post-retrieval completion, but the algorithmic core is an LLM-guided filtering-and-expansion loop over KG triple segments. It departs from PathRAG's flow-based, query-agnostic path scoring by making every expansion step explicitly conditioned on the query's semantic goal, and from HippoRAG's PPR by eliminating the random-walk nature that can drift to structurally relevant but semantically irrelevant nodes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides strong empirical evidence that semantic path coherence is a more effective retrieval principle than structural relevance for multi-hop factual QA, a distinction that prior graph-based RAG work had not explicitly addressed. The finding that LightRAG and PathRAG—despite leveraging graph structure—perform worse than naive retrieval on factual multi-hop QA is a useful negative result that clarifies when graph-based approaches help and when they introduce noise. Key limitations include heavy reliance on LLM calls (time bottleneck), low granularity of triple segments that can over-decompose simple questions, and no demonstrated benefit for sense-making or summarization tasks. Open questions include whether the neurobiological framing yields further algorithmic insights beyond the current LLM-filtering loop, and how the method scales to much larger knowledge graphs where the top-30 pruning threshold may become insufficient.
