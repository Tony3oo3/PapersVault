## Problem

Despite extensive empirical evidence that Multiple Gossip Steps (MGS) significantly improve decentralized training performance, two fundamental questions remain unanswered: why MGS is effective from a generalization perspective, and whether increasing gossip steps can fully close the performance gap with centralized training. Prior theoretical work on decentralized SGD generalization either assumed homogeneous data, bounded gradients, or convexity, and no unified framework existed to analyze how learning rate, data heterogeneity, node count, per-node sample size, and communication topology jointly affect generalization in non-convex settings.

## Input / Output

The analysis takes m agents each holding n local samples drawn from potentially heterogeneous distributions D_k, a symmetric doubly stochastic gossip matrix W encoding the communication topology (with spectral gap δ), and algorithmic hyperparameters including the number of gossip steps Q, a decaying learning rate schedule η_t ≤ c/(t+1), and total iterations T. The output consists of per-agent final iterates θ_k^(T) and, more importantly, closed-form upper bounds on the generalization error and excess error that explicitly depend on all of the above parameters. The bounds are derived under β-smoothness, bounded stochastic gradient noise (σ²), and bounded data heterogeneity (ξ²) assumptions, without requiring bounded gradients or Lipschitz continuity of the loss.

## Method

The paper derives generalization bounds for DSGD-MGS via l₂ on-average model stability, modifying the classical stability lemma to replace the empirical risk term with an explicit gradient norm, thereby making the connection between optimization error and generalization error transparent and removing the bounded-gradient assumption. The key technical result (Theorem 1) bounds the stability term as O(e^{2cβ}c²/((1+2cβ)nm·t₀)·(T/t₀)^{2cβ}), while Theorem 2 (leveraging the PL condition and prior MGS convergence results) shows the optimization error decays exponentially in Q at rate e^{-δγQ/4}. Combining these yields a generalization bound (Theorem 3) that is exponentially tight in Q, proving MGS's effectiveness. Crucially, letting Q→∞ reveals a persistent gap versus centralized mini-batch SGD: the decentralized bound scales as O(T^{2cβ/(2cβ+2)}/nm^{1/(2cβ+2)}) versus O(T^{cβ/(cβ+1)}/nm) for centralized, showing the gap in m-dependence cannot be eliminated by gossip alone. The framework is the first to simultaneously account for learning rate, heterogeneity, topology spectral gap, node count, and per-node data size in a non-convex, unbounded-gradient setting.

## Results

The paper is primarily theoretical, deriving generalization and excess error bounds for DSGD-MGS under non-convex settings without bounded gradients. Experimentally, on CIFAR-10 (LeNet, Dirichlet α=0.3, 50 nodes), both weight distance and loss distance follow the predicted power-law O(T^(2cβ/(2cβ+2))) behavior. Setting MGS=5 reduces the weight distance to roughly half that of MGS=1, consistent with the exponential reduction O(e^(-δγQ/4)) predicted by Theorem 3. Even with 20 gossip steps, DSGD-MGS retains a measurable performance gap versus centralized Mini-batch SGD in both training loss and test accuracy (Figure 1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is between DSGD-MGS (decentralized) and centralized Mini-batch SGD. The paper's central theoretical finding (Remark 3) is that even as the number of gossip steps Q→∞, the generalization error bound for DSGD-MGS is at most O(T^(2cβ/(2cβ+2))/nm^(1/(2cβ+2))), which remains strictly larger than the centralized Mini-batch SGD bound of O(T^(cβ/(cβ+1))/nm) because 1/m < 1/m^(1/(2cβ+2)) for m>1. Empirically, Figure 1 confirms this: with 20 gossip steps on CIFAR-10 (50 nodes, Dir 0.3), DSGD-MGS still lags behind Mini-batch SGD in both training loss and test accuracy. The paper does not compare against other decentralized baselines (e.g., D-SGD without MGS, gradient-tracking methods); instead, it isolates the effect of varying Q, topology spectral gap δ, learning rate c, and node count m to validate each factor's role in the theoretical bound.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the l2 on-average model stability framework of Lei & Ying [33] from centralized SGD to the decentralized DSGD-MGS setting, while drawing on the MGS convergence bounds of Hashemi et al. [18] and the D-SGD algorithm of Lian et al. [7]. It departs from prior D-SGD generalization analyses (Sun et al. [20], Le Bars et al. [11], Zhu et al. [22]) by removing the bounded-gradient/Lipschitz assumption and by incorporating the MGS mechanism, which had previously been studied only for convergence rather than generalization. The use of the Polyak-Lojasiewicz condition to connect function-value convergence to gradient-norm bounds is a deliberate technical bridge, as no prior work had provided a tight last-iterate gradient bound under MGS in non-convex settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper answers two open questions that practitioners have asked empirically: why MGS helps, and whether it can fully close the gap to centralized training. The negative result—that a non-negligible generalization gap persists even as MGS steps approach infinity—has direct practical implications for system designers who might otherwise invest in excessive gossip communication. The main limitation is the reliance on the PL condition, which is stronger than general non-convexity; the authors acknowledge that removing this assumption (i.e., bounding the last-iterate gradient norm without PL) remains an open frontier. Future work could explore whether alternative mechanisms (e.g., gradient tracking, momentum) can close the residual gap that MGS alone cannot.
