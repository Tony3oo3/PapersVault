## Problem

Vision RWKV offers linear-complexity global modeling for pan-sharpening, but its recurrent bidirectional scanning (Re-WKV) introduces directional biases and incurs substantial latency. Additionally, existing fusion architectures rely on cascaded second-order spatial-channel interactions that limit higher-order dependency capture while remaining computationally expensive.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254768/cropped_page2_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a panchromatic image I_P ∈ R^{H×W×1} and a low-resolution multi-spectral image I_M ∈ R^{h×w×C} (upsampled to H×W). A dual-branch modality-aware encoder projects both into shared feature space, which is then processed by L sequential RSRWKV blocks. The output is a fused pan-sharpened image I_F ∈ R^{H×W×C} produced by a decoder applied to the deep features plus a residual connection to the upsampled MS input.

## Method

The core contribution is a Random Shuffle RWKV (RSRWKV) paradigm with three coupled mechanisms. First, a Bayesian-inspired Random Shuffle randomly permutes K and V before WKV computation in the spatial mixer, followed by an inverse shuffle to restore spatial alignment; at inference, Monte Carlo averaging over M independent shuffles estimates the unbiased expectation of global 2D dependencies. Second, a WKV-sharing (KV-cache) mechanism transfers WKV activations across successive RWKV layers, eliminating redundant attention recomputation and reducing latency. Third, the channel mixer's sigmoid gate is reinterpreted as a first-order probability distribution, and by sharing the gate across layers via 1D convolution composition, the architecture achieves high-order channel interactions without cascaded second-order stacking. Training uses a composite loss combining L1 spatial loss with a random-weight Taylor-unfolding manifold loss that regularizes the optimization space with per-epoch randomly initialized weights.

## Results

On WorldView-II, the proposed RS-RWKV achieves PSNR 42.09 dB, SSIM 0.9721, SAM 0.0214, and ERGAS 0.9081, outperforming the strongest baseline PanFlowNet (41.85 dB) by 0.24 dB in PSNR. On WorldView-III, it reaches PSNR 30.97 dB and ERGAS 2.92, surpassing SFINet (30.60 dB, 3.08) by a clear margin. On GaoFen-2, the method attains PSNR 47.71 dB, SAM 0.0098, and ERGAS 0.5229, leading all competitors including SFINet (47.47 dB, 0.5462). The method consistently achieves the best PSNR, SAM, and ERGAS across all three benchmarks, with only a marginal SSIM gap (≤0.0005) to SFINet on two datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed RS-RWKV outperforms all 14 baselines on PSNR, SAM, and ERGAS across all three datasets (WorldView-II, WorldView-III, GaoFen-2). Against the strongest deep learning competitors, it leads PanFlowNet by 0.24 dB PSNR on WorldView-II and by 0.44 dB on GaoFen-2, while surpassing SFINet by 0.37 dB on WorldView-III. On SSIM, the method is marginally behind SFINet by 0.0004 on WorldView-II (0.9721 vs. 0.9725) and by 0.0005 on GaoFen-2 (0.9896 vs. 0.9901), but clearly ahead on WorldView-III (0.9266 vs. 0.9236). Traditional methods (SFIM, Brovey, IHS, GS) lag significantly, with PSNR gaps exceeding 6 dB on WorldView-II. Among CNN-based methods, the gap to SRPPNN and GPPNN is approximately 0.6–0.9 dB in PSNR, while transformer-based methods (INNformer, MutNet) are within 0.3–0.4 dB, indicating the primary competitive frontier lies among recent attention-based architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the Vision RWKV architecture (a linear-complexity alternative to Transformers) for pan-sharpening, departing from the standard recurrent bidirectional scanning (Re-WKV) with a Bayesian-inspired Random Shuffle strategy and an inverse shuffle to preserve information invariance. It further introduces WKV-sharing across RWKV layers (analogous to weight sharing in other architectures) and reinterprets the channel mixer's sigmoid gating as a first-order expectation operator, extending it to high-order modeling through cross-layer gate sharing. The random-weight manifold loss draws on Functional Theory and Taylor's unfolding to replace fixed-point frequency-domain losses (DWT/DFT) used in prior pan-sharpening work. Conceptually, the paper sits at the intersection of linear-attention sequence modeling and multi-modal remote sensing fusion, but does not build on a single prior pan-sharpening method as a direct base.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The linear-complexity global modeling offers a practical path to pan-sharpening large-scale satellite imagery where Transformers hit out-of-memory limits, and the random shuffle mechanism provides a principled way to eliminate fixed-sequence scanning bias without the latency of iterative Re-WKV. However, the performance gains over strong SOTA baselines (PanFlowNet, SFINet) are modest (~0.2–0.4 dB PSNR), and the ablation evidence is limited to a single dataset. The authors acknowledge the framework has not been validated on other remote sensing tasks (hyperspectral fusion, multi-spectral fusion), leaving open whether the random shuffle and WKV-sharing mechanisms generalize beyond pan-sharpening. The Monte Carlo averaging at inference (even if M=1 suffices) adds implementation complexity that may limit adoption.
