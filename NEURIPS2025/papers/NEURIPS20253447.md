## Problem

Classical matrix perturbation theory provides spectral-norm bounds for the full inverse A⁻¹ under additive noise, but no prior results analyze the error ||(Ã⁻¹)_p − A_p⁻¹|| for the best rank-p approximation of the inverse. This gap is critical because low-rank pseudoinverses are the practical tool in scalable solvers, preconditioners, and kernel methods, yet their stability under sampling, sketching, quantization, or privacy noise was previously uncharacterized. The paper asks: how does the perturbation error scale with the eigengap, spectral decay, and noise alignment with low-curvature directions?

## Input / Output

The method takes a real symmetric positive-definite matrix A ∈ ℝ^{n×n} with eigenvalues λ₁ ≥ … ≥ λₙ > 0, a symmetric perturbation E, and a rank parameter p ∈ [1, n], and produces a closed-form spectral-norm upper bound 4‖E‖/λₙ² + 5‖E‖/(λ_{n−p}·δ_{n−p}) valid under the joint condition 4‖E‖ ≤ min{λₙ, δ_{n−p}}. The bound is spectrum-adaptive: it depends explicitly on the smallest eigenvalue λₙ, the eigengap δ_{n−p} = λ_{n−p} − λ_{n−p+1} at the truncation threshold, and the noise norm, rather than on n alone. An extension to arbitrary symmetric (indefinite) matrices and rank-deficient PSD matrices via pseudoinverses is provided in the appendices.

## Method

The core technique is a novel application of contour bootstrapping to the non-entire rational function f(z) = 1/z. First, the perturbation ||(Ã⁻¹)_p − A_p⁻¹|| is represented via a contour integral over a rectangle Γ enclosing the p smallest eigenvalues of A while excluding 0 and the remaining eigenvalues. Second, a bootstrapping lemma (Lemma 3.1) shows that under the gap condition 4‖E‖ ≤ min{λₙ, δ_{n−p}}, the full series F = Σ F_s collapses to F ≤ 2F₁, where F₁ is the first-order resolvent integral ‖z⁻¹(zI−A)⁻¹E(zI−A)⁻¹‖; this step exploits the Sherman-Morrison-Woodbury identity and the fact that max_Γ ‖(zI−A)⁻¹E‖ ≤ 1/2. Third, a bespoke rectangular contour with vertices at (λₙ/2, ±2λ₁) and (λ_{n−p+1}+δ_{n−p}/2, ±2λ₁) is chosen so that the vertical-edge integrals yield the two terms 4‖E‖/λₙ² and 5‖E‖/(λ_{n−p}·δ_{n−p}) via elementary arctangent estimates (Lemma C.2), while horizontal-edge contributions are negligible. Unlike contour methods for entire functions (e.g., matrix exponential) that isolate the largest eigenvalues, this approach must enclose the smallest eigenvalues while navigating the pole at z = 0, introducing the additional λₙ² scaling and the eigengap-dependent second term.

## Results

The proposed contour-bootstrapping bound tracks the true perturbation error ‖(Ã⁻¹)ₚ − Aₚ⁻¹‖ within a factor of 2–3 across all tested matrices (empirical/bound ratio consistently 0.31–0.47). In contrast, the classical Eckart-Young-Mirsky-Neumann (EYM-N) bound overpredicts by 1.3× on the 69×69 US Census covariance, 1.4–3.2× on the 1083×1083 BCSSTK09 stiffness matrix, and up to 1330× on the discretized Hamiltonian (n=500, 1000). The theoretical guarantee is ‖(Ã⁻¹)ₚ − Aₚ⁻¹‖ ≤ 4‖E‖/λₙ² + 5‖E‖/(λₙ₋ₚ·δₙ₋ₚ), improving over the EYM-N bound 8‖E‖/(3λₙ²) + 2/λₙ₋ₚ by up to a √n factor in favorable spectral regimes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is the Eckart-Young-Mirsky-Neumann (EYM-N) bound derived from the Eckart-Young-Mirsky theorem [20] combined with a Neumann-series expansion of the full inverse. The proposed contour-bootstrapping bound dominates the EYM-N bound in every experimental configuration tested. On the 1990 US Census covariance (n=69, p=17), the EYM-N bound is 1.3–5.7× looser than the proposed bound, with the gap widening at lower noise levels. On the BCSSTK09 stiffness matrix (n=1083, p=8), the EYM-N bound is 1.4–3.2× looser. The most dramatic separation occurs on the discretized quantum Hamiltonian (n=500, 1000, p=10), where the EYM-N bound exceeds the proposed bound by up to 1330× at the smallest noise level, reflecting the √n theoretical improvement when the eigengap δₙ₋ₚ is well-separated. In all cases, the proposed bound's ratio to the true error remains below 0.5, confirming it captures the leading-order perturbation term, while the EYM-N bound fails to vanish as ‖E‖→0 due to its additive 2/λₙ₋ₚ term.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends classical matrix perturbation theory (Neumann series, Eckart-Young-Mirsky) and contour-integral methods for matrix functions to a new setting: the spectral-norm perturbation of the *best rank-p approximation of the inverse* under general additive noise. Prior contour-based perturbation results applied to entire functions (matrix exponential, eigenspace projections) or to the full inverse; the authors' key technical departure is adapting contour bootstrapping to the non-entire rational function f(z)=1/z, localizing the resolvent expansion around the smallest eigenvalues. The low-rank approximation literature (randomized SVD, sketching, Nyström) addresses approximation of A or monotone f(A) but not inverse computations, and recent Schatten-norm perturbation results for low-rank matrices do not yield spectral-norm guarantees for pseudoinverses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result provides the first non-asymptotic, spectrum-aware certificate for when a low-rank inverse approximation remains reliable under noise, with practical implications for preconditioned conjugate gradient (yielding an n^{1/4} iteration-count improvement), differentially private PCA, and Hessian-based optimization under noisy curvature. Key limitations include the eigengap condition (4‖E‖ ≤ δ_{n-p}), which requires accurate tail-spectrum access and may be hard to verify in black-box settings, and the restriction to static matrices. Open directions include extending to time-varying or structured noise, other norms (Schatten-p, Ky Fan), and other non-entire matrix functions such as resolvents or matrix roots.
