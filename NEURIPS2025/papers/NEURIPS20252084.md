## Problem

Post-training multi-modal LLMs via SFT or RL depends on large volumes of manually annotated data (ground-truth answers, preference pairs), making continual improvement unsustainable as tasks grow in complexity. While unsupervised self-improvement has been explored, existing methods rely on complex multi-stage pipelines (data generation, verification, filtering) that are difficult to iterate online. This paper formalizes a third post-training stage—Unsupervised Post-Training (UPT)—and asks whether a simple, online RL-based method can enable self-improvement without any external supervision.

## Input / Output

The method consumes unlabeled image-question pairs (e.g., from Geometry3K, GeoQA, MMR1) where ground-truth answers are masked or absent, and optionally self-generated synthetic questions produced by the model itself from images alone. During training, the model samples G=10 responses per question at temperature 0.7, and the output is an updated MLLM policy (Qwen2.5-VL-7B or similar) that produces more consistent text answers at inference time. No external reward model, judge, or label is required at any point; the only signal is the model's own sampled responses.

## Method

MM-UPT adapts the GRPO algorithm by replacing supervised reward signals with a majority-voting self-rewarding mechanism: for each question, the model samples a group of G responses, a rule-based extractor pulls out the final answers, and the most frequent answer becomes a dynamic pseudo-label. Responses matching the majority receive reward 1, others receive 0, and standard GRPO advantage estimation and policy updates proceed from these pseudo-rewards. This encourages the model to reinforce its high-confidence knowledge and converge toward consistent predictions. To extend scalability, the framework incorporates two data self-generation strategies—In-Context Synthesizing (model generates new questions conditioned on an original image-question-answer triplet) and Direct Synthesizing (model generates questions from the image alone)—which feed into the same unsupervised RL loop. The key insight is that majority voting provides a reliable pseudo-reward when the model's per-response accuracy exceeds 0.5, making the approach effective on moderately difficult datasets but prone to error amplification on very hard ones.

## Results

On Qwen2.5-VL-7B, MM-UPT improves average accuracy from 49.47% (base) to 53.17% (with MMR1 training data), matching the best supervised GRPO result (53.17%) while requiring no ground-truth labels. On MathVista, MM-UPT reaches 72.90%, surpassing supervised GRPO (71.40%) and the base model (66.30%). On We-Math, the gain is 62.87% → 68.74%. In the synthetic-data scenario (Scenario 2), direct synthesizing on GeoQA yields 52.67% average, even exceeding the original-question result (51.97%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MM-UPT (53.17% avg with MMR1) ties the best supervised GRPO baseline (53.17%) and surpasses all four unsupervised baselines: SRLM (51.18%), Genixer (50.12%), LMSI (49.97%), and STIC (49.13%). On MathVista specifically, MM-UPT (72.90%) outperforms supervised GRPO (71.40%) by 1.5 points, while on MathVision supervised GRPO (29.01%) still leads over MM-UPT (26.15%). Against supervised SFT (best 50.73%), MM-UPT gains 2.44 points on average. Among unsupervised methods, MM-UPT's advantage is most pronounced on We-Math (68.74% vs. SRLM's 66.32%) and MathVista (72.90% vs. SRLM's 66.90%), indicating that the majority-voting pseudo-reward in GRPO is substantially more effective than DPO-based or SFT-based self-improvement for multi-modal reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on GRPO (DeepSeekMath) as its RL backbone and on majority voting / self-consistency as the pseudo-label mechanism, combining them into an unsupervised training loop for MLLMs. It departs from prior self-improvement methods for MLLMs (Genixer, STIC, SRLM, LMSI), which rely on complex multi-stage offline pipelines (data generation → filtering → SFT/DPO), by replacing them with a single online RL step where the model's own sampled responses provide the reward signal. The concurrent TTRL work applies a similar GRPO-plus-majority-voting idea to text-only LLMs at test time, while this paper extends the formulation to the multi-modal domain and to a dedicated post-training stage, additionally exploring data self-generation for scalability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical value is a simple, drop-in unsupervised refinement step that can be applied after SFT and supervised RL to squeeze additional reasoning performance from MLLMs without any new annotations, which is increasingly important as labeled multi-modal data becomes scarce. Key open questions include: how to handle domains where the model's prior accuracy is below 50% (where majority voting amplifies errors, as shown on ThinkLite-11K), how to mitigate the consistent pass@n diversity collapse, and whether the approach scales to harder or more open-ended reasoning tasks. The data self-generation extension (especially direct synthesizing from images alone) opens a path toward fully autonomous training loops, but the quality and hallucination rate of synthetic questions remain uncontrolled.
