## Problem

While LLMs are increasingly integrated into BCI systems for signal denoising and language decoding, the field lacks a unified paradigm that positions agents as active, collaborative partners rather than passive neural-signal processors. This position paper identifies the absence of comprehensive implementation guidelines, ethical safeguards, and evaluation protocols needed to make brain-agent collaboration safe, trustworthy, and deployable in real-world assistive applications.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254888/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The proposed BAC framework consumes multi-modal neural signals (EEG, fMRI, fNIRS, ECoG) alongside explicit user feedback, corrections, and natural language commands, with the critical design distinction that human users are active contributors providing domain expertise and supervisory control rather than mere signal sources. Outputs are structured around an Interpretation-Communication-Interaction paradigm, where the agent proactively seeks clarification, communicates confidence levels, and produces adaptive actions, decoded language, and personalized task modifications rather than unverified one-shot predictions.

## Method

The paper proposes a four-component BAC framework: (1) a collaboration architecture supporting single- or multi-agent configurations with human oversight, (2) a multi-modal data infrastructure with AI-powered noise reduction and explicit cross-stream synchronization for real-time brain-agent engagement, (3) LLM-based agent development augmented with planning, memory, and tool-use modules, trained via RLHF/RLAIF/DPO to bridge the semantic gap between noisy neural signals and high-level cognitive intentions, and (4) a multi-faceted evaluation protocol spanning five dimensions (Technical Performance, Cognitive Synergy, Interaction Quality, User Agency, Ethics) with concrete metrics including Action Advancement Rate, Collaborative Intelligence Potential, User-System Match Score, Explicit Disagreement Rate, and Ethics Alignment Rate. The key differentiator from prior BCI-LLM work is treating ethical data governance, user agency, and transparency not as supplementary concerns but as core functional prerequisites that must be engineered into the system architecture from the outset.

## Results

This is a position paper that does not report empirical results, quantitative benchmarks, or experimental comparisons. Instead, it proposes a conceptual framework (Brain-Agent Collaboration) and an evaluation protocol with five dimensions (Technical Performance, Cognitive Synergy Effect, Interaction Quality, User Agency, Ethics) and five metrics (AAR, CIP, USMS, EDR, EAR), but none of these are instantiated with measured values. The paper's contribution is argumentative and prescriptive rather than empirical.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not conduct direct method comparisons or benchmark its proposed framework against baselines, as it is a position paper rather than an empirical study. It surveys and discusses several existing systems—Thought2Text (EEG-to-text via fine-tuned LLMs), BrainLLM (fMRI-to-language via embedding-space mapping), NeuroChat (neuroadaptive AI tutor with real-time EEG engagement tracking), WaveMind (conversational EEG foundation model), and Neural Spelling (EEG-based BCI with LLM error correction)—but treats them as illustrative examples of the emerging BAC paradigm rather than as competing methods to be ranked. The closest thing to a comparison is the conceptual contrast between conventional BCI (passive signal-to-command pipeline) and the proposed BAC (active, collaborative, adaptive agent partnership), where the authors argue BAC addresses BCI's limitations in information transfer rate, calibration burden, and user illiteracy (15–30% of users) without providing quantitative evidence for these claims.

## Relation to Prior Work

This position paper sits at the intersection of two mature literatures: conventional BCI signal processing (EEG/fMRI acquisition, feature extraction, command decoding) and LLM-based agentic AI (autonomous planning, tool use, multi-agent collaboration). It does not build on a single prior method but rather synthesizes recent LLM-for-BCI works—Thought2Text, BrainLLM, NeuroChat, WaveMind, Neural Spelling, and CorText—into a unified conceptual framework. The key departure is reframing the agent's role from a passive signal decoder to an active, collaborative partner, which the authors term Brain-Agent Collaboration (BAC), and proposing explicit implementation guidelines and a multi-dimensional evaluation protocol that prior BCI+LLM papers have not articulated.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a genuine gap: while individual LLM-for-BCI systems exist, there is no agreed-upon framework for how agents should collaborate with users, what ethical safeguards are non-negotiable, or how to evaluate the quality of the human-agent partnership beyond raw decoding accuracy. The proposed evaluation dimensions (Cognitive Synergy Effect, User Agency, Ethics Alignment Rate) and metrics (AAR, CIP, USMS, EDR, EAR) are concrete starting points that could standardize the field. However, as a position paper with no empirical validation, its practical impact depends on whether the community adopts the BAC framing; open questions include whether the proposed metrics are measurable at scale, how to handle the latency constraints of real-time neural interaction with LLM agents, and whether the ethical governance mechanisms (federated learning, neuro-rights) can be implemented without sacrificing the low-latency requirements of assistive applications.
