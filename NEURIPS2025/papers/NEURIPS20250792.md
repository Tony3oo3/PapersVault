## Problem

Existing causal inference formalisms (structural causal models, potential outcomes) lack a rigorous semantics for dynamically triggered, instantaneous interventions that characterize hybrid dynamical systems — e.g., a fishing season ending when a catch quota is reached, or an HVAC system activating at a temperature threshold. This gap forces practitioners into ad-hoc, error-prone causal analysis of closed-loop control and regulatory systems, and prevents the application of decades of causal estimand theory (treatment effects, probabilities of causation, mediation) to hybrid system users.

## Input / Output

The method takes a parameterized hybrid system P = (C, F, D, G, S, Θ) with random initial conditions ξ and parameters θ, along with interventional specifications (D̃, G̃) that define the state-space region where the intervention triggers and the resulting state transition. It produces an intervened system P′ whose unique, Borel-measurable time-parameterized solution map φ(·; ξ, θ) is jointly defined over the original and twin worlds, enabling direct computation of causal estimands. A key structural detail is that interventions are encoded as modifications to the jump set and jump map (constraint transformations) rather than as structural equation overrides, and the solution map is defined on a finite horizon [0, t⁺) where t⁺ accounts for potential Zeno accumulation.

## Method

The paper formalizes instantaneous interventions as transformations to the hybrid system's jump set D and jump map G, creating a twin world that shares the same randomly sampled initial conditions and parameters as the original. To handle the non-uniqueness that arises at flow/jump set boundaries, they introduce a 'lowering' operation combining flow-preferring subtraction (removing boundary points where a flowing solution exists) with selection of the most recent jump map via an ordered set-valued map structure. The central result (Theorem 1) proves that finite compositions of such interventions preserve solution existence, uniqueness, and Borel measurability under mild conditions on the interventional specifications (closedness, outer semi-continuity, local boundedness, and a 'well-behaved' boundary condition). The proof proceeds by an inductive lowering argument: showing the intervened upstream system still satisfies the hybrid basic conditions from Goebel et al. (2012), then applying the lowering to recover uniqueness. This constraint-transformation approach bypasses the need to recast hybrid systems as (infinite) structural causal models, instead leveraging established hybrid systems theory for well-posedness guarantees.

## Results

The paper's primary theoretical result (Theorem 1) proves that compositions of instantaneous interventions preserve the key properties of existence, uniqueness, and Borel measurability of the time-parameterized solution map, provided the interventional specifications satisfy mild conditions (Assumption 2: closed, well-behaved jump sets with Borel graphs, and outer semi-continuous, locally bounded, single-valued jump maps). The fishery management case study demonstrates the framework's practical utility by formalizing three probabilities of causation (necessity, sufficiency, and necessity-and-sufficiency) for Total Allowable Catch quota policies in a three-trophic-level ecosystem, with full simulation analyses provided in Appendix G using the ChiRho probabilistic programming language.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper is a theoretical/semantics contribution rather than an empirical comparison, so it does not benchmark against competing methods on shared datasets. However, it positions itself against several existing approaches to causal reasoning with dynamical systems: (1) forward-Euler SCM unrolling (Hansen & Sokol, 2014), which the authors argue is ill-suited for identifying well-posedness preservation under state-dependent jumps; (2) equilibrium-focused causal abstractions (Mooij et al., 2013; Blom et al., 2019; Bongers, 2022), which can miss transient dynamics; and (3) time-splitting operations (Boeken & Mooij, 2024) and GSEMs for hybrid automata (Peters & Halpern, 2025), which handle static-time discontinuities but cannot straightforwardly analyze dynamically triggered interventions whose ordering depends on state evolution. The paper's advantage is that by working directly in hybrid systems language, it inherits established well-posedness theory (Goebel et al., 2012; Sanfelice, 2021) and avoids the need for soft interventions on all endogenous nodes required by the forward-Euler approach.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant approach of casting continuous-time dynamical systems as (discretized) structural causal models (Hansen & Sokol, 2014; Mooij et al., 2013) or as cyclic graphical models with time-splitting (Boeken & Mooij, 2024; Peters & Halpern, 2025), instead defining counterfactual interventions directly as transformations on the constraint tuple (C, F, D, G) of hybrid dynamical systems in the Goebel et al. (2012) / Sanfelice (2021) framework. It inherits the constraint-based causal modeling spirit of Beckers et al. (2023) and Blom et al. (2019), where interventions modify constraints, but extends this to the unique challenges of hybrid systems (Zeno behavior, set-valued maps, non-uniqueness at boundaries). The 'lowering' proof strategy—intervening on an upstream system satisfying hybrid basic conditions, then applying a flow-preferring reduction—mirrors controller-plant composition results from hybrid control theory, creating a direct bridge between the two communities.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides the first rigorous counterfactual semantics for dynamically triggered, state-dependent interventions, enabling formal causal queries (probabilities of causation, treatment effects, counterfactual outcomes) for a broad class of closed-loop control problems (HVAC, fisheries, public health, power grids) that were previously handled with ad-hoc analysis. Key limitations include the restriction to finite time horizons (no equilibrium analysis), the absence of non-parametric identification results, and the lack of conditions guaranteeing preservation of non-Zeno behavior under intervention. The framework also sidesteps standard graphical identifiability criteria by working in a non-graphical formalism, though the authors note this opens pathways to leveraging dynamical systems identification literature (persistence of excitation, etc.).
