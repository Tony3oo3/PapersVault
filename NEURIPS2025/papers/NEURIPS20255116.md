## Problem

Transformer-based PDE solvers face quadratic attention complexity when applied directly to the tens of thousands of irregularly discretized mesh points on complex geometries, and existing methods (e.g., Transolver) learn domain partitions without explicitly leveraging boundary geometry. This paper addresses how to design a geometry-aware tokenization and attention mechanism that both reduces computational cost and captures the physical coupling between domain boundaries and interior points.

## Input / Output

The model consumes irregularly discretized point clouds (e.g., 32,186 points for cars, 32,000 for airfoils, 1,656 for aorta) where each point carries coordinates, SDF values, boundary normals, and direction vectors, plus boundary conditions such as inlet/outlet pressure or velocity. The output is a per-point physical field (velocity, pressure, turbulent viscosity) from which engineering quantities like drag or lift coefficients are post-processed. For time-dependent problems (Blood Flow), the input is reshaped to 1656×1089 and the output to 1656×121×3, encoding 121 temporal steps.

## Method

SpiderSolver introduces spiderweb tokenization: the domain boundary is partitioned via spectral clustering (aligned across shape instances using Sinkhorn optimal transport to a template), and interior points are radially subdivided by SDF distance bands, yielding M = m_B·m_I + m_B non-overlapping spiderweb-like patches that serve as tokens. A transformer with L SpiderAtten blocks then applies coarse-grained multi-head self-attention over these M tokens (reducing complexity from O(N²) to O(M²)), followed by a T2P weighted-token-aggregation operator that maps token features back to point-wise features via learned per-point weighting matrices. A fine-grained attention module operates on the concatenated features of boundary points and their nearest interior neighbors (within the first SDF band) to explicitly model boundary-interior physical coupling. The network is trained end-to-end with relative L₂ loss against ground-truth PDE solutions, with spiderweb tokenization performed as a fixed preprocessing step.

## Results

On Shape-Net Car, SpiderSolver achieves a drag coefficient error (C_D) of 0.0100 and Spearman's rank correlation (ρ_D) of 0.9928, outperforming the closest competitor Transolver (C_D=0.0129, ρ_D=0.9916). On AirfRANS, SpiderSolver reduces the lift coefficient error (C_L) to 0.0741 and achieves ρ_L=0.9988, a 28% improvement over Transolver (C_L=0.1026). On the Blood Flow dataset, SpiderSolver achieves a velocity error of 0.0322, surpassing GNOT (0.0411) and Transolver (0.0438). SpiderSolver also achieves the lowest relative L2 error on Bounded Navier-Stokes (0.0376), Darcy Flow (0.0064), Elasticity (0.0061), and Plasticity (0.0011).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SpiderSolver outperforms all 21 baselines across every dataset and metric. The closest competitor is Transolver [14], which SpiderSolver beats on Shape-Net Car by 8% in C_D (0.0100 vs. 0.0129) and on AirfRANS by 28% in C_L (0.0741 vs. 0.1026) and 46% in surface field error (0.0043 vs. 0.0080). Among graph-based methods, GNO [2] and MG-Net [31] are significantly outperformed, with SpiderSolver achieving roughly 40–50% lower C_D on Shape-Net Car. Deformation-based FNO variants (Geo-FNO [6], GINO [5]) show the largest gaps, with Geo-FNO's C_D error on Shape-Net Car being 6.6× higher than SpiderSolver's. On the Blood Flow dataset, SpiderSolver (0.0322) improves over GNOT [32] (0.0411) by 22% and over Transolver (0.0438) by 26%. On the canonical PDE benchmarks (Bounded Navier-Stokes, Darcy Flow, Elasticity, Plasticity), SpiderSolver achieves the lowest relative L2 error, narrowly edging out Transolver and LSM on Darcy Flow (0.0064 vs. 0.0065 and 0.0068) and Elasticity (0.0061 vs. 0.0064).

## Relation to Prior Work

SpiderSolver sits at the intersection of two lines of work: geometry-informed neural operators (Geo-FNO, GINO) that deform irregular domains to uniform meshes, and transformer-based PDE solvers (Galerkin Transformer, GNOT, Transolver) that apply attention directly to mesh points. It departs from Transolver—the closest prior method—by replacing learned, geometry-agnostic slices with an explicit geometric partitioning (spiderweb tokenization) driven by spectral clustering of the boundary and signed distance functions, and by adding a fine-grained attention layer dedicated to boundary-interior interactions. The optimal-transport-based alignment of boundary clusters across shape instances is borrowed from shape-matching literature but repurposed here to ensure consistent tokenization across variable geometries.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap: fast, accurate PDE solving on industrial-scale complex geometries (cars, airfoils, blood vessels) where classical mesh-based methods are prohibitively expensive. The OOD generalization results—particularly the ~57% reduction in lift-coefficient error on angle-of-attack extrapolation versus Transolver—suggest that geometry-aware tokenization provides a more transferable inductive bias than learned slices. Key limitations remain: the method requires closed boundaries, is restricted to a single shape class per model, and is not equivariant to geometric transforms, all of which the authors flag as future work. Extending the framework to multi-class boundaries and open domains would be the natural next step for broader adoption.
