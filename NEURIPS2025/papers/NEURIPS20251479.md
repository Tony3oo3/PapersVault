## Problem

Machine-learning models for cyclic peptide binder design are severely constrained by data scarcity: existing cyclic peptide databases contain only thousands of entries with very few experimentally determined complex structures, forcing prior work to rely on post-generation filtering or hard-coded modifications to linear peptide models that support limited cyclization types. This paper addresses the gap by constructing the first large-scale (2.71 million) cyclic peptide-receptor complex dataset derived from predicted protein structures, enabling end-to-end supervised training of cyclic peptide design models for the first time.

## Input / Output

The data mining pipeline consumes single-chain protein structures from the AlphaFold Database (AFDB) and produces 2.71 million cyclic peptide-receptor complex structures spanning three linkage types (mainchain amide, disulfide, isopeptide), with peptides of 5–16 residues. For the model application, the trained generators take a target protein structure and a specified peptide length as input, and output 100 candidate cyclic peptide sequences with full-atom 3D coordinates per target. The dataset is further partitioned into specialized subsets (CPBind for high affinity, CPTrans for membrane permeability, CPCore for combined quality) that remain large enough (54K–72K complexes) for from-scratch model training.

## Method

The core pipeline extracts compact domains from AFDB via agglomerative hierarchical clustering on a symmetrized PAE matrix (15 Å threshold), then identifies potential cyclization sites by computing Cβ–Cβ distance matrices and selecting residue pairs within an empirically calibrated 3–8 Å window derived from PDB structures of known linkages. A multi-stage filtering protocol enforces structural fidelity (pLDDT ≥ 70, Ramachandran plausibility), biophysical plausibility (secondary structure ratios, hydrophobicity < 45%, BSA ≥ 400 Å² with rBSA < 85%), and binding geometry (terminal rBSA < 1%, receptor graph connectivity). Terminal residues are mutated and cyclized via PDBFixer, followed by energy minimization under a modified CHARMM36 force field in OpenMM with receptor pockets frozen, and final energy/chirality filters. The key insight is that intra-chain spatial proximities in single-chain proteins can faithfully mimic inter-chain peptide–receptor binding interfaces, validated by showing CPSea distributions closely match those of experimentally determined complexes in CPSet. Three existing generative frameworks (DiffPepBuilder, PepFlow, PepGLAD) are then trained from scratch on the CPCore subset to demonstrate the dataset's utility for cyclic peptide design.

## Results

CPSea contains 2.71 million cyclic peptide-receptor complexes with quality metrics closely matching the experimental reference CPSet: 98.1% Ramachandran allowed (vs. 99.2%) and 90.1% favored (vs. 94.8%), with interaction type distributions within acceptable range (51.8% hydrophobic vs. 42.0%, 38.3% H-bond vs. 46.3%). Three models trained from scratch on the CPCore subset (71,867 complexes) achieve ~46–51% final success rate, with Rosetta ΔG between −20.8 and −23.3 and Vina scores between −5.9 and −6.7. DiffPepBuilder leads in final success (51.0%) and wet-lab compatibility (83.5% GRAVY < 0, 92.9% logP > −6), while PepGLAD achieves the highest structural diversity (0.598).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper validates CPSea by training three existing linear-peptide design models (DiffPepBuilder, PepFlow, PepGLAD) from scratch on the CPCore subset and evaluating them on 56 LNR targets. DiffPepBuilder achieves the highest final success rate (51.0%) and best wet-lab compatibility (83.5% GRAVY < 0, 92.9% logP > −6), while PepFlow leads in cyclization success (93.8%) and best Vina score (−6.7). PepGLAD has the lowest cyclization success (81.2%) due to sidechain idealization hindering terminal proximity, but achieves the highest structural diversity (0.598) and best chirality rate (79.6%). All three models show significantly lower novelty (~0.12–0.13) compared to CPSea itself (0.603), attributed to designing against receptors with known PDB structures. For dataset quality, CPSea closely matches the experimental CPSet reference on Ramachandran metrics (98.1% vs. 99.2% allowed) but shows a shift toward hydrophobic interactions (51.8% vs. 42.0%), which the authors attribute to inherent differences between intra- and inter-protein interactions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by repurposing single-chain protein structures from the AlphaFold Database to generate cyclic peptide-protein complex data, drawing a conceptual parallel to the domain-domain interaction dataset that enabled RoseTTAFold2-PPI. It departs from prior cyclic peptide design approaches that worked around data scarcity via post-generation filtering (DiffPepBuilder) or hard-coded cyclization constraints (AfCycDesign, RFpeptides), instead providing the large-scale training data those methods lacked. The dataset extends the peptide-protein complex resource landscape (PepBDB, PepBench, PepMerge, CPSet), which had been dominated by linear peptides, by introducing 2.71 million cyclic complexes with three linkage types (mainchain, disulfide, isopeptide).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CPSea is the first dataset large enough to enable end-to-end training of cyclic peptide design models from scratch, removing the need for workarounds like post-hoc filtering or hard-coded cyclization types. The validation that intra-chain interactions faithfully mimic inter-chain peptide-protein interfaces (comparable Ramachandran, interaction-type, and physicochemical distributions to experimental CPSet) has broader implications for constructing PPI training data from single-chain databases. Key limitations include coverage of only classical cyclization types (no lasso, stapled, or click-chemistry peptides), potential inheritance of AFDB prediction errors in flexible regions, and the fact that model training was demonstrated only on a 72K subset (CPCore) rather than the full 2.71M dataset, leaving open whether full-scale training yields further gains.
