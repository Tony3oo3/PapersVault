## Problem

Token-level Chain-of-Thought reasoning in LLMs is computationally expensive, and existing latent-space alternatives (Coconut, CODI) rely on fixed-length reasoning chains and deterministic latent prediction, limiting both efficiency and the ability to explore diverse reasoning pathways. This paper asks whether reasoning can be dynamically compressed into a variable-length latent space while preserving the exploration-exploitation dynamics that reinforcement learning exploits.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250164/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, CoLaR takes a math question and an integer compression factor c (prompted as text, e.g., 'reason with compression factor 5') and auto-regressively produces a variable-length sequence of latent vectors (each a d-dimensional embedding compressing c consecutive reasoning tokens) followed by a predicted answer token sequence. The latent chain length is not fixed; the model decides when to terminate via the Language Head's end-of-reasoning token. The same trained model can be re-prompted with a different c to trade off speed versus accuracy without retraining.

## Method

CoLaR introduces a two-stage training pipeline on a frozen LLM backbone with LoRA. In the SFT stage, a random compression factor c ∈ [1, c_max] is sampled per step; c consecutive reasoning-token embeddings are merged via a scaled sum (divided by √c to preserve the zero-mean embedding distribution), and a two-headed MLP Latent Head is trained to predict the mean and standard deviation of the next compressed embedding (a Gaussian distribution, not a point estimate). The LLM simultaneously predicts one randomly sampled token per compressed group plus the answer tokens through the Language Head, providing dense supervision. In the RL stage, GRPO samples G diverse latent reasoning chains per question using the reparameterization trick on the Latent Head's output distribution; the binary correctness reward is averaged per-token across each output before computing group-normalized advantages, which penalizes longer incorrect chains more heavily and reinforces shorter correct ones, driving the model to exploit compact reasoning paths.

## Results

On four grade-school math datasets, CoLaR-5 (compression factor c=5) achieves 41.7% average accuracy with only 4.57 latent reasoning steps, outperforming Coconut (27.6%, 6.00 steps) by 14.1% at a higher compression ratio. CoLaR-2 (c=2) reaches 48.8% average accuracy with 10.0 reasoning steps, representing only a 4.8% drop from explicit CoT (53.6%) while reducing chain length by 53.3%. On the harder MATH dataset, RL-enhanced CoLaR-NLL-RL (Qwen-1.5B) gains 5.36% accuracy over CoLaR-NLL while cutting reasoning length by 82.8% (from 56.8 to 9.79 steps). At 8B scale, CoLaR-8B-RL surpasses its CoT teacher on GPQA (37.5% vs 35.7%) with a 69% shorter reasoning chain.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoLaR-5 decisively outperforms both latent-based baselines: it beats Coconut [13] by 14.1% average accuracy (41.7% vs 27.6%) while using fewer reasoning steps (4.57 vs 6.00), and surpasses the CODI/Distill reproduction [24] by 27.4% (41.7% vs 14.3%). Against iCoT [5], CoLaR-5 gains 17.1% accuracy (41.7% vs 24.6%) with dynamic-length reasoning versus iCoT's zero-step (direct answer) approach. Compared to explicit CoT [29], CoLaR-2 trades only 4.8% accuracy (48.8% vs 53.6%) for a 53.3% reduction in chain length (10.0 vs 21.4 steps), and on MultiArith the gap narrows to just 1.9% (91.3% vs 93.2%). Against TokenSkip [30] at 8B scale, CoLaR-8B-RL achieves 71.9% on GSM-8k with 13.3 reasoning steps versus TokenSkip's 78.2% with 113 steps; TokenSkip retains a 6.3% accuracy edge but at 8.5× the reasoning length, while CoLaR-8B-RL matches or exceeds CoT on GPQA (37.5% vs 35.7%) with a 69% shorter chain.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoLaR extends the auto-regressive latent reasoning paradigm initiated by Coconut (Hao et al., 2024) and CODI (Shen et al., 2025), which replace token-level reasoning with continuous hidden-state predictions. The key departures are threefold: (1) a dynamic compression factor c sampled during training and prompted at inference, replacing the fixed-length latent chains of prior work; (2) a probabilistic (Gaussian) Latent Head that outputs distributions rather than deterministic values, enabling exploration-exploitation; and (3) a GRPO-based RL stage (Shao et al., 2024) applied directly to latent reasoning, which no prior latent method has attempted. The embedding compression strategy (scaling the sum by 1/√c to preserve distributional variance) is a practical design choice absent from earlier latent-reasoning work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in the dynamic speed control: a single trained model can be prompted to reason at different compression factors, trading accuracy for latency at deployment time without retraining. The RL result—82.8% chain-length reduction while simultaneously improving accuracy by 5.36% on MATH—challenges the assumption that shorter reasoning is inherently worse, suggesting that exploration in latent space can discover more compact correct paths. However, the method still trails explicit CoT on most benchmarks (except GPQA at 8B+RL), cannot handle non-integer compression factors, and has only been validated on small models (≤8B) and mathematical tasks, leaving open whether the approach generalizes to longer-horizon, multi-step, or non-numeric reasoning.
