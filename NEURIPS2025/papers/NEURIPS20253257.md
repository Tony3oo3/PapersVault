## Problem

Existing federated graph learning methods rely on label information during training, leaving the unsupervised setting largely unexplored. In unsupervised FGL, two critical challenges arise: (1) without shared labels, local models converge in divergent directions, misaligning representation spaces across clients; and (2) conventional sample-count-weighted aggregation (e.g., FedAvg) cannot adaptively determine client importance, degrading global model quality under data heterogeneity.

## Input / Output

Each of K clients holds a private subgraph with node feature matrix X_k ∈ R^{N_k×d}, adjacency matrix A_k, and edge set E_k, partitioned from a complete graph via the Louvain method. The server maintains a set of N_P learnable anchor vectors that span the global representation space and act as projectors. The method outputs node embeddings evaluated via linear probing or KNN (reporting ACC and F-score), an updated global GNN, and refined anchors that are redistributed to clients each communication round.

## Method

FedPAM comprises two modules. The Representation Space Alignment (RSA) module introduces server-side learnable anchors and uses Fused Gromov-Wasserstein Optimal Transport (FGW-OT) to minimize a combined feature-level and topology-level transport cost between each local subgraph and the anchor graph, yielding a closed-form transport matrix T_k; a gradient-based transfer probability C_k is then trained via cross-entropy to match T_k, while the anchors simultaneously project local embeddings into a global space for an additional contrastive loss. The Adaptive Global Parameter Learning (AGPL) module stacks per-layer local parameters into third-order tensors and solves a low-rank-plus-sparse decomposition via ADMM with tensor nuclear norm (for shared structure) and L1 regularization (for client-specific noise), where client weights α_k are learned adaptively rather than fixed by sample counts, producing the optimal global GNN parameters in the low-rank tensor space.

## Results

FedPAM achieves the best or near-best ACC across all eight graph datasets under three SSL strategies (SimCLR, BYOL, SimSiam). With SimCLR, FedPAM reaches 58.62% ACC on Cora, 43.90% on CiteSeer, 69.22% on PubMed, 83.76% on Physics, and 39.60% on Amazon-ratings, consistently outperforming the strongest baseline FedU2 by 1.5–3.1 points on most datasets. The largest gains appear on CiteSeer (+8.42% over FedAvg) and Physics (+4.69% over FedAvg). Notably, FedU2 slightly edges out FedPAM on Ogbn-Arxiv with SimCLR (48.41 vs. 48.19 ACC), but FedPAM recovers the lead under BYOL (40.55 vs. 39.95).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedPAM outperforms all eight baselines on 7 out of 8 datasets under SimCLR, with the sole exception being Ogbn-Arxiv where FedU2 leads by a narrow 0.22-point margin (48.41 vs. 48.19 ACC). The most substantial wins are over FedAvg on CiteSeer (+8.42 ACC) and Physics (+4.69 ACC), and over FedU2 on CiteSeer (+3.01 ACC) and PubMed (+2.43 ACC). Under BYOL, FedPAM extends its dominance, beating FedU2 on all eight datasets (e.g., +0.90 on Cora, +1.49 on CiteSeer, +1.02 on Photo) and achieving a striking +11.64 ACC over FedAvg on Photo. FedX, which uses cross-knowledge distillation, is competitive on Computers (80.60) but trails FedPAM by 1.03 points. Orchestra, the clustering-based unsupervised FL method, consistently underperforms all other baselines, confirming that simple global clustering is insufficient for heterogeneous graph data.

## Relation to Prior Work

FedPAM sits at the intersection of three established lines of work: federated graph learning (which has been studied extensively in supervised/semi-supervised settings for both graph-level and node-level tasks), self-supervised graph representation learning (SimCLR, BYOL, SimSiam), and optimal transport for structured data. The paper repurposes FGW-OT from Vayer et al. (ICML 2019), originally designed for graph matching, as a cross-client representation alignment mechanism, and applies T-SVD-based tensor nuclear norm minimization (Kilmer et al., 2013; Hu et al., 2016) to the novel problem of federated model aggregation rather than data completion. It departs from prior unsupervised FL methods (Orchestra, FedU, FedEMA, FedU2), which were designed for image data, by explicitly accounting for topological heterogeneity in graph structures and replacing fixed-weight averaging with adaptive low-rank tensor fusion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to formally identify and address the two core challenges of unsupervised FGL—representation space misalignment across clients and non-adaptive aggregation—providing a unified framework that practitioners can adopt for privacy-preserving graph learning without labels. The plug-and-play nature of the AGPL module (demonstrated by improvements when inserted into FedAvg, FedProx, MOON, and FedX) suggests broader utility beyond the specific FedPAM architecture. Key limitations include the assumption that all subgraphs originate from a single complete graph, the computational cost of FGW-OT (O(N_E·M + M²·N)), and evaluation on relatively small graphs (up to ~170K nodes). Open questions include scalability to truly heterogeneous multi-graph settings, handling of dynamic graph topologies, and whether the anchor-based global space definition generalizes when clients have fundamentally different graph structures.
