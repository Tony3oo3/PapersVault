## Problem

Existing Riemannian bilevel optimization (RBO) methods require prior knowledge of problem-specific parameters—geodesic strong convexity, Lipschitz constants, and manifold curvature—to determine step sizes, which are often unknown or computationally infeasible to estimate. This paper introduces AdaRHD, the first fully adaptive RBO algorithm that eliminates all such parameter dependencies while maintaining the same O(1/ε) iteration complexity as non-adaptive methods.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes two smooth bi-objectives f and g defined on complete Riemannian manifolds M_x × M_y (with g geodesically strongly convex in y), initial points x₀, y₀, v₀, and initial step sizes a₀, b₀, c₀. It produces an ε-stationary point x satisfying ‖𝒢F(x)‖² ≤ ε after T = O(1/ε) outer iterations. The inner loops solve the lower-level problem to tolerance ε_y = 1/T and a Hessian-inverse linear system to tolerance ε_v = 1/T, with the approximate hypergradient computed via either gradient descent (AdaRHD-GD) or tangent-space conjugate gradient (AdaRHD-CG).

## Method

AdaRHD employs the inverse-of-cumulative-Riemannian-gradient-norm strategy at all three update levels: the upper-level variable x is updated with step 1/a_{t+1} where a_{t+1}² = a_t² + ‖𝒢̂F‖², the lower-level variable y uses an analogous adaptive rule with b_k, and the linear-system variable v uses c_n. The approximate Riemannian hypergradient 𝒢̂F(x, ŷ, v̂) = 𝒢_x f(x,ŷ) − 𝒢²_xy g(x,ŷ)[v̂] replaces the exact hypergradient, where v̂ solves the quadratic subproblem via adaptive GD or TSCG. The key analytical challenge is that Riemannian curvature (via the trigonometric distance bound ζ(τ,c)) enters the step-size adaptation and convergence proof, making the analysis fundamentally different from the Euclidean D-TFBO predecessor. The paper further shows that substituting exponential mappings with computationally cheaper retraction mappings preserves the O(1/ε) rate under a bounded-iterate assumption.

## Results

Theoretically, AdaRHD achieves an O(1/ε) iteration complexity for finding an ε-stationary point, matching non-adaptive Riemannian bilevel methods, with gradient complexities G_f = O(1/ε) and G_g = O(1/ε²). On the AFEW emotion classification task (3-layer SPD network, 5% subset), AdaRHD-CG reaches 85% validation accuracy in 245.63 epochs at step size 1.0, while RHGD-50 and RHGD-20 fail to converge entirely at that step size. At the smallest working step size (0.05), AdaRHD-CG reaches 85% in 633.46 epochs versus 1510.09 for RHGD-50 and 1067.92 for RHGD-20. On the simple Stiefel/SPD matrix problem (n=100 and n=1000), AdaRHD exhibits faster convergence and lower hypergradient estimation error than RHGD.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares AdaRHD (both GD and CG variants) against RHGD-50 and RHGD-20 (the non-adaptive Riemannian hypergradient descent from Han et al., NeurIPS 2024) on the AFEW emotion classification task. The most striking result is that RHGD-50 and RHGD-20 fail to converge entirely at initial step sizes of 5, 1, and 0.5, whereas AdaRHD remains stable and converges across all tested step sizes including 5.0. At the step sizes where RHGD does converge (0.1 and 0.05), AdaRHD-CG reaches 85% validation accuracy substantially faster: 427.84 vs. 808.48 epochs (step 0.1) and 633.46 vs. 1510.09 epochs (step 0.05) compared to RHGD-50. RHGD-20 is faster at reaching 50% and 70% accuracy at small step sizes (e.g., 36.01 vs. 217.32 at step 0.1), but requires more time than AdaRHD-CG to reach 85% (540.46 vs. 427.84). AdaRHD also exhibits consistently lower standard deviations across trials, confirming its superior robustness to initialization choices.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Euclidean tuning-free bilevel optimization framework (D-TFBO, Yang et al. 2025) to Riemannian manifolds, adapting the inverse-of-cumulative-gradient-norm step size strategy (originating from AdaGrad-Norm, Xie et al. 2020) to the geometric setting. It builds directly on the Riemannian bilevel optimization framework of Han et al. (NeurIPS 2024) and Li & Ma (JMLR 2025), which established the hypergradient formulation, convergence assumptions, and non-adaptive algorithms (RHGD, RieBO) on manifolds. The key conceptual departure is eliminating all problem-specific parameters (strong convexity, Lipschitz constants, curvature bounds) from step size selection, which introduces new analytical challenges from manifold curvature that have no Euclidean analogue.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that Riemannian bilevel optimization problems (meta-learning on Stiefel/SPD manifolds, neural architecture search, image segmentation) previously required practitioners to estimate difficult-to-obtain curvature and smoothness parameters; AdaRHD removes this barrier while matching the O(1/ε) iteration complexity of non-adaptive methods. However, a 1/ε gap remains in the lower-level gradient and Hessian-vector product complexities compared to non-adaptive Riemannian bilevel methods, which the authors identify as an open problem. Future directions include single-loop adaptive variants, stochastic extensions, handling non-strongly-convex lower-level objectives, and alternative adaptive strategies (Riemannian SAM, natural gradient, AdaBB) that might close the complexity gap.
