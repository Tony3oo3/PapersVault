## Problem

Graph Neural CDEs (GN-CDEs) model dynamic graph evolution by using the adjacency matrix and its time derivative as a control signal, but their fusion step breaks permutation equivariance — a fundamental symmetry that ensures predictions are independent of arbitrary node ordering. This lack of equivariance inflates the parameter count and degrades generalisation, particularly in extrapolation regimes where the model must generalise beyond observed topologies.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253284/cropped_page5_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a sequence of dense n×n adjacency matrices sampled at irregular time points, optionally augmented with d_x-dimensional node feature snapshots. For event-based data (e.g., TGB), individual timestamped interactions are first aggregated into fixed-width snapshot windows before being fed to the CDE. The output is a continuous latent path Z_t ∈ R^{n×d_z} defined for all t in the observation interval, from which node-level regression targets or node affinity scores are read off via a final linear head. The dense adjacency requirement imposes quadratic memory in n, limiting scalability to large sparse graphs.

## Method

PENG-CDEs replace the non-equivariant fusion in GN-CDEs with two learnable linear maps L_1, L_2 drawn from the 15-dimensional space of permutation-equivariant linear operators on edge-valued signals (characterised by Maron et al.), so that the fused control matrix is A̅_s = L_1(A_s) + L_2(dA_s/ds). This fused matrix then drives a standard iterative GCN convolution whose output is integrated over time as the CDE vector field. In the purely linear regime, Theorem 3.1 proves this construction is the orthogonal projection of the original GN-CDE onto the equivariant function space, making it the best equivariant approximation in the least-squares sense. Proposition 3.2 establishes that the full model is equivariant under both node permutations and arbitrary time reparametrisations. An optional extension multiplies the vector field by the interpolated node-feature derivative via a node-wise Hadamard product to incorporate dynamic node attributes while preserving both equivariance properties.

## Results

On the TGB-genre node affinity prediction task, PENG-CDE with source/target identification achieves 0.523 NDCG@10, surpassing all learned baselines (best: TGNv2 at 0.469) and even the Moving Average (L) heuristic (0.509), making it the first learned method to beat a heuristic on this benchmark. On TGB-trade, PENG-CDE+ reaches 0.734 NDCG@10, matching TGNv2 (0.735) and outperforming GN-CDE (0.713). On synthetic heat diffusion and gene regulation tasks, PENG-CDE achieves 30–74% relative MSE improvement over the original GN-CDE, and is the only model maintaining constant loss during extrapolation. On snapshot-based datasets (england-covid, twitter-tennis), PENG-CDE attains the lowest test MSE (0.913 and 0.440 respectively), indicating better generalisation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On TGB-genre, PENG-CDE+ (0.523) decisively outperforms every learned baseline: TGNv2 (0.469), GN-CDE (0.460), STG-NCDE (0.438), TGN (0.367), and all event-based methods (JODIE, TGAT, CAWN, TCL, GraphMixer, DyGFormer, DyRep at ~0.35–0.37), as well as the Moving Average (L) heuristic (0.509). On TGB-trade, PENG-CDE+ (0.734) ties TGNv2 (0.735) and beats GN-CDE (0.713) by 2.1 points and STG-NCDE (0.618) by 11.6 points, while all other learned baselines cluster around 0.37–0.39. On the snapshot-based england-covid task, PENG-CDE achieves the best test MSE (0.913) versus GN-CDE (0.962), GNODE (0.945), and DCRNN (1.021); on twitter-tennis it is competitive with GN-CDE (0.440 vs 0.443) and beats DCRNN (0.455) and STIDGCN (0.495). On synthetic tasks, the non-equivariant Pre Mult GN-CDE is an order of magnitude worse (e.g., 1.968 vs 0.096 MSE on community heat diffusion), while the equivariant Adjacency GN-CDE (0.208) is still 2.2× worse than PENG-CDE (0.096), confirming that the full 15-term equivariant basis provides substantial additional expressivity beyond simple adjacency fusion.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends Graph Neural CDEs (Qin et al., 2023/2025), which model dynamic graph topology as a control signal for a CDE, by identifying that the original formulation violates permutation equivariance and projecting the adjacency-derivative fusion onto the 15-dimensional space of equivariant linear maps characterised by Maron et al. (ICLR 2019). The dynamic node feature extension draws on the STG-NCDE approach of Choi et al. (AAAI 2022), while the broader positioning sits at the intersection of continuous-depth GNNs (Neural ODEs/SDEs on graphs) and spatio-temporal GNNs (DCRNN, ASTGCN, STIDGCN). The key conceptual departure is framing the equivariance fix through the lens of geometric deep learning and the Projection Theorem, yielding a model that is provably the optimal equivariant approximation in the linear regime.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that the 'natural' GN-CDE formulation silently breaks permutation equivariance, and that enforcing it via a 15-term basis yields order-of-magnitude improvements over the non-equivariant variant, provides a clear geometric principle for designing dynamic graph models. The TGB-genre SOTA (first learned method to surpass a moving-average heuristic) is a practical milestone for temporal graph benchmarks. However, the quadratic memory complexity from dense adjacency matrices (exacerbated by JAX's sparse-matrix limitations) restricts scalability to large sparse graphs, and no universal approximation or generalisation guarantees exist for graph-based Neural CDEs—both open problems the authors flag for future work.
