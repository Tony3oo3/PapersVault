## Problem

Autoregressive LLMs generate text strictly left-to-right, yet analysis of long Chain-of-Thought trajectories reveals that over 98% contain parallelizable branches (independent subtasks that could execute concurrently). Existing non-AR models (diffusion, consistency) enable parallel generation but use rigid, brute-force parallelism that ignores logical dependencies, while external parallelization tools (MCTS, Tree of Thoughts) lose internal KV states during inter-model communication. The paper asks: how to design a generative framework that adaptively decides when and how to parallelize, losslessly preserves internal states, and generalizes to arbitrary parallelism patterns.

## Input / Output

The model takes a text question or prompt optionally suffixed with the instruction 'Think step by step and in parallel' (or 'Think step by step' for the zero-parallelism variant). It produces a structured text response where the model itself emits control tags—<Parallel>, <Goal>, <Outline>, <Path>, <Conclusion>—to delineate MapReduce blocks. These blocks can nest recursively (a <Path> may contain its own <Parallel> structure), and the degree of parallelism (number of concurrent <Path> branches) is determined dynamically by the model at inference time rather than fixed in advance.

## Method

Multiverse internalizes a three-stage MapReduce pipeline: a sequential Map stage decomposes the task into subtasks, a parallel Process stage executes each subtask as an independent branch, and a sequential Reduce stage merges results losslessly. The key architectural change is Multiverse Attention, which modifies both attention masks and positional indices (following APE) so that tokens within the same Process block start from identical positions and cannot attend across branches, while the Reduce stage converges all branches to the maximum position reached—preserving training parallelism via FlexAttention kernels. For data, Multiverse Curator is a five-stage Gemini 2.5 Pro pipeline that converts sequential CoT from s1K-1.1 into structured MapReduce training samples (Multiverse-1K, 1K examples) with content and grammar validation. For inference, Multiverse Engine extends SGLang with a dedicated interpreter that reads the model's own control tags to dynamically fork parallel branches (sharing prefix KV via radix attention) and merge them back, achieving near-zero overhead switching between sequential and parallel modes. The entire system is trained via 3-hour SFT on Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct with only 1K examples, leveraging the structural similarity between Multiverse Attention and causal attention for rapid transfer.

## Results

Multiverse-32B, fine-tuned from Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct with only 1K examples in 3 hours, achieves pass@1 scores of 52.9% on AIME24, 44.2% on AIME25, 92.4% on MATH500, and 61.7% on GPQA-Diamond, representing a 23.6% average improvement over the base model. The model exhibits a degree of parallelism (#parallel) of 1.15–1.24, meaning it generates 15–24% more tokens within the same generation length compared to strictly autoregressive baselines (which are 1.00). In budget-controlled experiments with fixed context length, Multiverse-32B outperforms AR-LLMs by an average of 1.87%, and achieves up to 2× wall-clock speedup across batch sizes 1–128.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the base Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct model, Multiverse-32B improves by 37.1 points on AIME24 (52.9 vs 15.8), 33.8 on AIME25 (44.2 vs 10.4), 12.0 on MATH500 (92.4 vs 80.4), and 14.7 on GPQA-Diamond (61.7 vs 47.0). Compared to Autoregressive-32B (trained on the same data but without MapReduce structure or control tags), Multiverse-32B wins on AIME24 (52.9 vs 51.3) and AIME25 (44.2 vs 42.9), ties closely on GPQA-Diamond (61.7 vs 61.6), and trails slightly on MATH500 (92.4 vs 92.8). Against s1.1-32B (trained on the original sequential CoT data from which Multiverse-1K is derived), Multiverse-32B matches on AIME24 (52.9 vs 52.9) and wins on AIME25 (44.2 vs 41.7), while s1.1-32B leads on MATH500 (93.4 vs 92.4) and GPQA-Diamond (60.3 vs 61.7 — actually Multiverse wins here). The key differentiator is that Multiverse-32B achieves these results while generating 15–24% more tokens per generation step, translating to up to 2× speedup in wall-clock time at the same effective context budget.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the autoregressive (AR) paradigm that dominates LLM generation by introducing Multiverse, a new generative framework that internalizes a MapReduce structure for adaptive parallel generation. It builds on causal attention (Vaswani et al.) and APE-style positional encoding (Yang et al.) to design Multiverse Attention, and extends SGLang's inference engine with a dedicated interpreter for dynamic sequential-to-parallel switching. Conceptually, it positions itself against both brute-force parallel non-AR models (diffusion, consistency models) that ignore logical dependencies, and external parallelization methods (Best-of-N, MCTS, ToT) that lose internal KV states during inter-model communication, offering a middle path that preserves full internal state while enabling structured parallelism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Multiverse is the first open-sourced non-AR model to match leading AR-LLMs on complex reasoning benchmarks (AIME, MATH500, GPQA) using only 1K training examples and 3 hours of fine-tuning, demonstrating that parallel generation is not a fundamental architectural overhaul but a lightweight modification. The up-to-2× wall-clock speedup and superior test-time scaling (1.87% average gain at fixed context length) suggest a practical path to reducing inference cost for long-reasoning workloads. Key limitations include reliance on SFT alone (no RL), evaluation at a single 32B scale, and modest achieved parallelism (1.15–1.24×), leaving open whether the approach scales to larger models, broader task domains, and higher degrees of parallelism with reinforcement learning.
