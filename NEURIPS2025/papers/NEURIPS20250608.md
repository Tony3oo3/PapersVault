## Problem

It remains contentious whether reinforcement learning truly expands a language model's reasoning capabilities or merely amplifies high-reward outputs already latent in the base model's distribution. Prior studies concluding that RL does not expand reasoning boundaries relied on specialized domains (primarily math) and terminated training prematurely (typically within hundreds of steps), potentially masking genuine capability gains. This paper challenges those assumptions by asking whether sufficiently prolonged and stable RL training can uncover novel reasoning strategies inaccessible to the base model even under extensive sampling.

## Input / Output

The model consumes diverse text-based reasoning problems spanning math, competitive programming, STEM questions, logic puzzles, and constrained instruction-following prompts, and produces a chain-of-thought reasoning trace followed by a final answer enclosed in structured delimiters (e.g., \boxed{} for math, triple backticks for code, <answer> tags for puzzles). During training, each prompt yields n=16 sampled responses scored by verifiable rewards (binary for math/STEM, continuous for code/logic/instruction tasks) within an 8k-token context window; at inference, evaluation uses 256 samples per prompt with 32k max length and nucleus sampling (T=0.6, top_p=0.95) to estimate pass@k up to k=128, directly measuring the breadth of the model's reasoning boundary rather than just its peak accuracy.

## Method

ProRL builds on GRPO (Group Relative Policy Optimization) and introduces three key mechanisms to enable stable training beyond 2k steps: (1) DAPO's decoupled clipping (ε_low=0.2, ε_high=0.4) and dynamic sampling to filter prompts with zero or perfect accuracy, maintaining exploration pressure; (2) an explicit KL divergence penalty between the current policy and a reference policy to counteract entropy collapse; and (3) periodic hard resets of the reference policy to a recent online-policy snapshot with optimizer state reinitialization, preventing the KL term from dominating the loss and stalling updates. Training uses a diverse 136K-problem dataset across five domains (math, code, STEM, logic puzzles, instruction following) with AdamW at lr=2e-6, batch size 256, and high rollout temperature (1.2) on 32 H100 GPUs for ~16k GPU-hours. The central finding is that this combination of KL regularization, reference resets, and task diversity enables the model to progressively populate new regions of solution space, with the largest reasoning-boundary gains occurring on tasks where the base model initially struggles most.

## Results

Nemotron-Research-Reasoning-Qwen-1.5B achieves an average pass@1 of 60.14 on math benchmarks (vs. 44.45 for the base DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B, a +15.7% gain) and 37.49 on code benchmarks (+14.4% over base). On STEM reasoning (GPQA Diamond), instruction following (IFEval), and logic puzzles (Reasoning Gym), the model scores 41.78, 66.02, and 59.06 respectively—surpassing the much larger DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B (35.44, 58.01, 28.55) on all three. On out-of-distribution tasks (acre, boxnet, game_of_life_halting), the model achieves 58.57, 7.91, and 52.29 versus 20.21, 1.71, and 12.94 for the 7B model, demonstrating strong generalization beyond the training distribution.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the base model DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B, ProRL delivers consistent gains across every domain: +15.7% on math (60.14 vs. 44.45 avg), +14.4% on code (37.49 vs. 23.08), +25.9% on GPQA, +22.0% on IFEval, and a striking +54.8% on Reasoning Gym logic puzzles (59.06 vs. 4.24). Compared to the math-specialized DeepScaleR-1.5B, the ProRL model achieves a higher average math score (60.14 vs. 54.54, +4.6%), demonstrating that generalist prolonged RL outperforms domain-focused training. Against the code-specialized DeepCoder-1.5B, ProRL also wins on average code performance (37.49 vs. 30.96, +6.5%), though DeepCoder retains an edge on HumanEval+ (73.40 vs. 72.05) and LiveCodeBench (22.76 vs. 23.81 is actually a ProRL win). Most notably, the 1.5B ProRL model matches or surpasses the 4× larger DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B on GPQA (41.78 vs. 35.44), IFEval (66.02 vs. 58.01), Reasoning Gym (59.06 vs. 28.55), Codeforces (34.50 vs. 33.08), and all three OOD tasks, while remaining slightly behind on math average (60.14 vs. 63.19) and overall code average (37.49 vs. 41.39).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on GRPO (from DeepSeekMath) as its core RL algorithm and incorporates DAPO's decoupled clipping and dynamic sampling to maintain exploration diversity. It departs from the prevailing narrative established by Yue et al. (2025), Dang et al. (2025), and Zhao et al. (2025), who argued that RL merely amplifies solutions already latent in the base model's distribution. The key methodological departure is the combination of explicit KL divergence regularization with periodic reference policy resets, which the authors show is necessary to sustain stable training beyond the few hundred steps typical in prior RLVR work. The paper also extends the task scope from single-domain (math-only or code-only) RL training to a diverse multi-task setting spanning STEM, logic puzzles, and instruction following.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work challenges a widely-held assumption in the reasoning-model community that RL cannot expand a model's reasoning boundary, providing empirical evidence (pass@128 improvements, OOD generalization from 0% to high accuracy, creativity index increases) that prolonged training with appropriate stabilization does discover genuinely new solution pathways. The finding that RL gains are inversely correlated with base model competence offers a practical heuristic for where to invest RL compute. Key open questions remain: whether these results generalize beyond 1.5B parameter models, what the compute ceiling is for continued gains, and whether the 'new reasoning' is truly novel or merely recombination of pretraining knowledge in new configurations. The substantial compute requirement (~16k GPU hours) and the need for manual intervention (reference resets, reward shaping) limit immediate accessibility.
