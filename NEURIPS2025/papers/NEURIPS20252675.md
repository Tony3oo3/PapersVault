## Problem

Multi-view GNNs face a fundamental tension between consistency (shared information across views) and complementarity (view-specific information). Existing approaches adopt either a unified shared model that overemphasizes consistency and fails to match heterogeneous graph structures, or a multi-channel model with view-specific parameters that sacrifices architectural inter-view interaction. The paper identifies, through graph spectral theory, that a single shared graph filter cannot simultaneously serve structurally dissimilar views, and asks how to design an MGNN paradigm that flexibly balances both properties.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252675/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a multi-view graph consisting of a shared node feature matrix X ∈ R^{N×D} and V adjacency matrices {A_v}, one per view. For single-view datasets, a supplementary view is synthesized via k-NN (k=10) to fit the multi-view formulation. The output is a final node embedding H ∈ R^{N×C} produced by a multi-view aggregation function over per-view embeddings, which is then passed through a softmax for semi-supervised node classification. Notably, the number of graph experts M is decoupled from the number of views V, and the routing decision for each node incorporates both a sampled local subgraph neighborhood and a global view-level pooled representation.

## Method

MvCGE is a Mixture-of-Experts-inspired MGNN where each layer contains M shared graph experts (parameterized GNN convolutions) and a graph-aware top-K router that dynamically selects K experts per node based on concatenated local subgraph structure and global view-level information. Consistency is enforced architecturally by sharing all expert parameters across views, while complementarity is preserved by allowing distinct experts to specialize on different structural patterns. Two novel regularization losses prevent model collapse: a load equilibrium loss that penalizes uneven expert selection frequency, and a graph discrepancy loss that maximizes MMD between expert-specific graph embeddings (treated as distributions over views via a readout function), forcing experts to occupy distinct latent spaces. An acceleration trick decouples the parameter-free spectral filter P_v from trainable weights Θ_m, computing the graph filtering once per layer and reusing it across all experts, reducing redundant convolution cost.

## Results

On multi-view graph datasets, MvCGE achieves the best Macro F1 on 4 out of 5 datasets, with particularly strong gains on YELP (0.7776 MaF1, +10.2% over the best baseline AMOGCN at 0.6753) and AMINER (0.7897 MaF1 vs. 0.7570 for AMOGCN). On single-view datasets, MvCGE attains the top Macro F1 on 4 out of 5 benchmarks, including Flickr (0.7675 vs. 0.7580 for AMGNN) and Citeseer (0.6908 vs. 0.6813 for JKNet). The only notable loss is on DBLP Macro F1 (0.9256 vs. 0.9300 for MHGCN), though MvCGE still leads on Micro F1 for most datasets. Overall, MvCGE demonstrates consistent superiority across both multi-view and single-view settings with 19 baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MvCGE outperforms all 10 multi-view baselines on ACM-M (0.9348 MaF1 vs. 0.9241 for AMOGCN), IMDB (0.5157/0.6883 vs. 0.5154/0.6423 for MHGCN), YELP (0.7776/0.8008 vs. 0.6753/0.7241 for AMOGCN, a +10.2% MaF1 gain), and AMINER (0.7897/0.8640 vs. 0.7570/0.8338 for AMOGCN). The sole exception is DBLP, where MHGCN edges out MvCGE on Macro F1 (0.9300 vs. 0.9256) and Micro F1 (0.9360 vs. 0.9317), though the gap is narrow. On single-view datasets, MvCGE beats all 10 baselines on ACM (0.9110 vs. 0.9052 for GNNHF), Citeseer (0.6908 vs. 0.6813 for JKNet), CoraFull (0.5924 vs. 0.5846 for GNNHF), and Flickr (0.7675 vs. 0.7580 for AMGNN), but trails GCNII slightly on UAI Macro F1 (0.4797 vs. 0.4869) while still leading on Micro F1 (0.6591 vs. 0.6382). The largest margins appear on YELP and Flickr, where meta-path-based and single-channel methods struggle most with heterogeneous or complex graph structures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of multi-view learning and graph neural networks, identifying a fundamental tension between two existing MGNN pipelines: shared-parameter models (which overemphasize consistency) and multi-channel models (which overemphasize complementarity). It draws on the sparsely-gated Mixture-of-Experts framework (Shazeer et al., 2017) to introduce layer-wise collaborative graph experts with top-k gating, and on Maximum Mean Discrepancy (Gretton et al., 2012) to construct a graph discrepancy loss that prevents expert collapse. The key conceptual departure is from the binary shared-vs-view-specific parameter choice, replacing it with a flexible expert pool whose size is decoupled from the number of views, with a graph-aware router dynamically selecting experts per node and per view.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled architectural solution to the long-standing consistency-complementarity dilemma in multi-view graph learning, which prior methods addressed only by leaning toward one extreme or the other. The combination of load equilibrium loss and MMD-based graph discrepancy loss offers a two-pronged defense against expert collapse, a practical concern that limits MoE adoption in graph settings. However, the authors acknowledge not discussing homophily vs. heterophily effects, and the scalability of multiple graph experts on very large multi-view graphs (beyond OGBN-arXiv and Freebase) remains an open question. The acceleration trick of computing the parameter-free graph filter once per layer is a useful practical contribution for deployment.
