## Problem

Deep neural networks struggle to acquire compositional representations reliably, and existing emergent communication frameworks do not explicitly enforce the opposing pressures of compressibility and expressivity that language-evolution theory identifies as necessary for compositional structure to emerge. This paper addresses the gap by making these pressures explicit in a reconstruction-based communication game, showing that their balanced interplay drives the emergence of structured, generalizable discrete languages.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251195/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images from Shapes3D (64×64, 6 generative factors) or MPI3D (7 factors) as input, which are first encoded by a frozen pretrained VAE into 128-dimensional latent vectors. The sender then produces a discrete message of C=10 symbols drawn from a vocabulary of |V|=15 (via Gumbel-Softmax), and the receiver reconstructs the image from this message. Unusually, the receiver emits an intermediate reconstruction after every symbol (not just the full message), and the useful length is defined as the shortest prefix achieving reconstruction error below a dataset-specific threshold ε.

## Method

CELEBI introduces three mechanisms into an iterated-learning Lewis reconstruction game with a frozen VAE backbone and LSTM sender/receiver. (1) Progressive Decoding weights the reconstruction loss at each sub-message prefix by λ^i (λ=1.5), incentivizing the receiver to extract meaning incrementally and the sender to front-load information, reducing useful message length by 25–29%. (2) Final-State Imitation replaces standard message-space imitation in the IL phase with state-space imitation: the student sender is trained to reproduce the frozen receiver's final output rather than the teacher's discrete message, loosening the generational bottleneck and allowing intermediate tokens to drift while preserving semantic fidelity. (3) Pairwise Distance Maximization adds a batch-level regularizer that maximizes position-wise cosine dissimilarity between the student's messages, provably providing a lower bound on entropy maximization and an upper bound on NT-Xent contrastive loss. The three mechanisms jointly impose a tight compressibility–expressivity trade-off, yielding the highest TopSim and shortest useful length among all ablations on both datasets.

## Results

On Shapes3D, the full CELEBI framework (PD+FiSI+PDM, λ=1.5) achieves a TopSim of 0.292±0.002, a useful message length of 7.0±0.155, and a last-symbol MSE of 0.194±0.005, improving TopSim by ~20% and reducing useful length by ~30% over the no-IL baseline (TopSim 0.244, length 10.0). On MPI3D, the full method reaches TopSim 0.153±0.001 with useful length 9.0±0.167 and MSE 0.02, outperforming the baseline (TopSim 0.133, length 9.3). In disentanglement evaluation (Table 2), VAE+CELEBI achieves DCI 0.112 on Shapes3D and 0.137 on MPI3D, surpassing both the VAE+EL baseline and continuous β-VAE/β-TCVAE models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the VAE+EL baseline of Xu et al. [71], CELEBI consistently improves TopSim (0.292 vs. 0.244 on Shapes3D; 0.153 vs. 0.133 on MPI3D) and reduces useful message length (7.0 vs. 10.0 on Shapes3D), while maintaining comparable reconstruction MSE. In the disentanglement comparison (Table 2), VAE+CELEBI outperforms VAE+EL on DCI (0.112 vs. 0.108 on Shapes3D; 0.137 vs. 0.114 on MPI3D), RMSE (2.932 vs. 3.168; 14.82 vs. 15.971), and linear-probe accuracy (0.453 vs. 0.343; 0.542 vs. 0.529). Against continuous β-VAE and β-TCVAE [47], CELEBI achieves substantially higher DCI scores (0.112 vs. 0.045 on Shapes3D; 0.137 vs. 0.031 on MPI3D) but trails on downstream RMSE and categorical accuracy for Shapes3D (RMSE 2.932 vs. 2.460; Acc 0.453 vs. 0.702), while on MPI3D it wins on RMSE (14.82 vs. 21.819) and is close on accuracy (0.542 vs. 0.581). For the entropy regularizer comparison, PDM outperforms the KoLeo estimator [61] on TopSim (0.292 vs. 0.256 on Shapes3D; 0.153 vs. 0.147 on MPI3D) and useful length (7.0 vs. 8.555 on Shapes3D), confirming that the pairwise cosine-dissimilarity objective is a more effective diversity pressure than the KoLeo kernel.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Lewis Reconstruction Game framework (Rita et al., 2022) and the VAE+LSTM emergent language architecture of Xu et al. (2022) by embedding them within an iterated learning (IL) process grounded in Kirby's cultural evolution model. The three proposed mechanisms each address a specific gap: Progressive Decoding is analogous in spirit to LazImpa's impatient-listener loss (Rita et al., 2020) but applied to reconstruction games with exponential rather than linear length weighting; Final-State Imitation departs from the standard message-space imitation used in prior IL implementations (Ren et al., 2020; Li & Bowling, 2019) by constraining the student only on the receiver's output rather than the discrete message; and Pairwise Distance Maximization introduces a new regularizer with formal lower-bound connections to entropy maximization, positioned against KoLeo (Sablayrolles et al., 2019) as a practical alternative. Conceptually, the paper makes explicit the compressibility–expressivity trade-off long discussed in the cognitive science literature (Kirby et al., 2015; Beppu & Griffiths, 2009) and operationalizes it through concrete training objectives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The FiSI mechanism offers a clean conceptual reframe of the generational bottleneck in IL—constraining the student in state space rather than message space—which could be adopted in other IL-based communication or representation-learning settings. However, the empirical gains are modest (TopSim improves ~20% on Shapes3D, ~15% on MPI3D), all experiments are confined to small synthetic datasets with clean generative factors, and the discrete representations still underperform continuous baselines on downstream probing tasks. Open questions include whether these pressures transfer to natural images with weakly defined factors, whether the Kolmogorov-complexity formalization of language compression the authors sketch can be made rigorous, and whether co-training the visual backbone (frozen here) would alter the compression–expressivity balance.
