## Problem

Existing hallucination mitigation methods for MLLMs (e.g., contrastive decoding, DPO) improve factual accuracy but suppress associative reasoning, degrading performance on creative tasks, while no prior work provides a mechanism to flexibly modulate associative strength in a task-aware manner. The paper identifies that this rigidity stems from the lack of understanding about where and how associative behavior is encoded within MLLM representations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250088/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

FlexAC takes a standard image-and-text-prompt input to an MLLM and produces a text response whose associative reasoning strength is shifted toward either faithfulness (FlexAC-P, α=−1) or creativity (FlexAC-C, α=1). The method is entirely training-free and operates at inference time by modifying hidden states in a small set of middle layers (e.g., layers 11–13 for LLaVA-1.5, 15–17 for Qwen-VL, 4–6 for DeepSeek-VL2). Pre-computed steering vectors are derived offline from 50 COCO2014 images, and a Steering Intensity Calibration module adaptively rescales the injection per sample to avoid over-steering on inputs that already exhibit strong associative behavior.

## Method

FlexAC is a two-phase, training-free activation-steering framework. In Phase I (offline), it collects grounded–hallucinated response pairs, extracts middle-layer hidden states, selects the top-K pairs with the largest cosine distance, and averages their differences to obtain a general associative steering vector; task-specific vectors are additionally constructed from a few GPT-4o-generated high-association examples to support multi-dimensional creative directions. In Phase II (inference), the combined vector is injected into the target middle-layer hidden state as f_l^control = f_l + α_gen·v_l^gen + α_task·v_l^task, where the Steering Intensity Calibration module computes α via a sigmoid of the negative cosine alignment between the current representation and the steering direction, amplifying steering when the representation is misaligned and suppressing it when already aligned. A final renormalization step preserves the feature magnitude. The key empirical finding enabling this design is that associative behavior is primarily introduced in middle layers (cosine distance peaks at layers 10–15 for LLaVA-1.5), and that hallucinated responses reliably encode the associative direction, making them a natural source for control vectors without any gradient-based training.

## Results

On hallucination benchmarks, FlexAC (faithfulness mode, α=−1) reduces CHAIR_S to 19.2 on Qwen-VL (vs. 40.6 Regular, a 52.7% reduction), 36.6 on LLaVA-1.5 (vs. 50.8), and 28.6 on DeepSeek-VL2 (vs. 32.6), while achieving the best POPE F1-score of 87.9 on LLaVA-1.5. On creativity benchmarks, FlexAC (creativity mode, α=+1) achieves a VDAT score of 88.49 on LLaVA-1.5 (vs. 86.89 Regular) and a Creation-MMBench Reward of +10.92 (vs. −3.86 for VCD and −1.63 for VAF), representing up to a 5.8× improvement in creativity. Across 11 general-purpose benchmarks (MME, MMMU, MMStar, MM-Vet, MMBench, etc.), both FlexAC variants maintain performance within ~2 points of the vanilla model, confirming no degradation of core capabilities.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FlexAC consistently outperforms all three baselines on hallucination reduction. On Qwen-VL, FlexAC achieves CHAIR_S of 19.2, beating VCD (42.0), VAF (38.0), and the Regular baseline (40.6) by 22.8, 18.8, and 21.4 points respectively; on LLaVA-1.5, it reaches 36.6 vs. Ha-DPO's 36.8 (nearly tied) but substantially outperforms VCD (51.0) and VAF (47.8). On the creativity side (VDAT), FlexAC (86.58 on Qwen-VL, 88.49 on LLaVA-1.5) surpasses all baselines, whereas Ha-DPO actually degrades creativity (85.11 vs. 86.89 Regular) and VCD/VAF show minimal gains. On Creation-MMBench, FlexAC achieves a Reward of +10.92, while VCD (−3.86) and VAF (−1.63) both harm creativity relative to the baseline, confirming that existing hallucination-mitigation methods suppress associative reasoning at the cost of creative output. On general-purpose benchmarks (MME, MMMU, MMStar, and 11 extended benchmarks), FlexAC maintains performance within ~2 points of the vanilla model, whereas VCD and VAF show comparable or slightly worse general performance, indicating FlexAC's targeted modulation does not introduce collateral damage.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the activation-steering paradigm from LLMs (Rimsky et al., ACL 2024; Chuang et al., ICLR 2024) to multimodal models, specifically localizing the control point to middle layers where associative behavior emerges in MLLMs. It repurposes hallucination-induced response generation (Lyu et al., NeurIPS 2024) not as a mitigation technique but as a source of steering directions, unifying hallucination suppression and creativity enhancement as opposite ends of a single 'associative reasoning' axis. Unlike prior hallucination-mitigation methods (VCD, VAF, Ha-DPO) that only push in one direction (suppressing association), FlexAC provides bidirectional, task-adaptive control with per-sample intensity calibration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified framing of hallucination and creativity as modifiable associative strength offers a practical, training-free lever for MLLM practitioners who need to switch between factual and creative modes without retraining. The VDAT benchmark fills a genuine gap in evaluating divergent visual association, and the middle-layer localization finding provides a reusable diagnostic for other MLLM behaviors. Key limitations include the white-box requirement (inapplicable to API-only models like GPT-4o), the reliance on GPT-4o for task-specific vector construction, and the fact that the large creativity gains (5.8×) are demonstrated primarily on a single base model and benchmark. Whether the middle-layer control point generalizes to next-generation MLLM architectures with different depth or MoE structures remains open.
