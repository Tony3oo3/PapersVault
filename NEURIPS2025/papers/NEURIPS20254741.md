## Problem

Average cost MDPs with risk-sensitive agents lack efficient solution algorithms beyond the entropic risk measure. While relative value iteration (RVI) and Q-learning are standard tools in the risk-neutral setting, no general RVI or model-free Q-learning algorithm existed for the broader class of dynamic risk measures (utility-based shortfall risk, optimized certainty equivalent, spectral risk measures) in the average-cost setting.

## Input / Output

The model-based RVI algorithm requires the full transition kernel P and cost function c, while the model-free algorithms consume only observed (state, action, next_state) transitions. The MLMC Q-learning algorithm requires repeated resampling from each (x,a) pair (geometrically many samples per iteration), whereas the off-policy UBSR Q-learning operates on single transitions without resampling. All algorithms output a stationary deterministic policy, the optimal average risk scalar g*, and the Q-function over the state-action space.

## Method

The paper proposes three algorithms: (1) a risk-aware RVI that replaces the expectation in the Bellman operator with a general risk map and subtracts a reference value f(V_n) to prevent overflow, proven convergent via span-seminorm contraction; (2) an MLMC-based Q-learning that constructs an unbiased estimator of the risk-aware Bellman operator by sampling a geometrically distributed number of transition levels and combining empirical-kernel Bellman evaluations, with convergence proven via ODE-based stochastic approximation under an asymptotic coherence condition; and (3) an off-policy asynchronous Q-learning for UBSR that exploits the identity E[ℓ(v − SR(v))] = 0 to write the optimality equation as a root-finding problem, enabling a single-sample stochastic approximation update without resampling. The MLMC estimator's unbiasedness relies on Hölder continuity of the risk measure with respect to the 1-Wasserstein distance, satisfied by UBSR, OCE, and spectral risk measures under bounded-slope conditions on the loss function.

## Results

On three classical average-cost MDP benchmarks (Machine Replacement, Water Reservoir Management, Inventory Management), the risk-aware RVI algorithm computes optimal average risks that decrease monotonically as risk aversion relaxes: for Machine Replacement, the optimal risk drops from 68.75 (Expectile τ=0.9) to 54.42 (risk-neutral); for Water Reservoir, from 20.25 to 7.62; and for Inventory Management, from 24.87 to 20.13. Cross-evaluating policies under the Expectile (τ=0.9) risk measure reveals that the risk-neutral policy incurs the highest expectile risk (e.g., 69.94 vs. 68.75 for MR; 28.19 vs. 24.87 for IM), confirming that risk-aware policies are meaningfully tuned to the agent's preferences. In convergence experiments, MLMC Q-learning converges almost surely to the true optimal average risk, while the model-based risk-aware RVI achieves convergence at a significantly faster rate.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not benchmark against external competing methods but instead compares its own algorithmic contributions. In convergence experiments (Section 5.2), the UBSR Q-learning algorithms (both synchronous and asynchronous) demonstrate faster convergence and lower variance than the MLMC Q-learning algorithm when given an equivalent number of samples (~6,000 samples per state-action pair), though MLMC Q-learning is the only one with a provable almost-sure convergence guarantee. The model-based risk-aware RVI converges to the optimum at a significantly faster rate than any model-free variant, as expected. Across the three application domains, the key comparison is between policies optimized under different risk measures: the risk-neutral policy, when evaluated under the Expectile (τ=0.9) criterion, performs worst in all three settings (e.g., 28.19 vs. 24.87 for Inventory Management), while the Expectile-optimized policy achieves the lowest expectile risk by construction. The OCE and Mean-CVaR policies fall in between, with the gap widening for Inventory Management (25.29 and 26.57 respectively), illustrating that the choice of risk measure materially affects long-term performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the average risk-aware MDP framework of Shen et al. (2013) by providing the first general relative value iteration (RVI) algorithm and the first model-free Q-learning algorithms that go beyond the entropic risk measure. The RVI approach builds on the risk-neutral RVI of Abounadi et al. (2001) and Bertsekas (2007), while the MLMC-based Q-learning generalizes the distributionally robust Q-learning of Wang et al. (2023c) to non-coherent dynamic risk measures. The off-policy UBSR Q-learning adapts the root-finding idea from Shen et al. (2014) for discounted risk-aware MDPs to the average-cost setting. Prior model-free algorithms for average risk-aware MDPs existed only for the entropic risk measure (Borkar 2002, 2010), making this a genuine generalization to UBSR, OCE, and spectral risk measures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work fills a significant gap in the risk-aware RL literature by providing provably convergent planning and learning algorithms for a broad class of dynamic risk measures in the average-cost setting, which is the natural formulation for continuing tasks like network control and maintenance. The practical impact is that agents with diverse risk preferences (captured by expectile, OCE, or mean-CVaR) can now be served by a unified algorithmic framework. Key open questions remain: the off-policy UBSR Q-learning lacks a convergence proof, finite-sample guarantees for MLMC Q-learning are unresolved, and the strong ergodicity assumption (Assumption 4.8) may be weaker than necessary. Extending these methods to large-scale or function-approximation settings is an important next step.
