## Problem

Existing MARL communication methods either assume ideal lossless channels or address only specific lossy phenomena (noise, delay) in narrow settings, lacking a unified model that generalizes across diverse real-world constraints like underwater or cave environments. A key unresolved dilemma is how to simultaneously exploit reliable messages for cooperation while suppressing the harmful influence of corrupted messages on joint decision-making.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255150/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each agent receives a partial state-vector observation, a set of incoming messages modulated by a binary link-status indicator ι^ij (1 = lossless, 0 = lossy), and produces a continuous action via a stochastic policy. The link status is computed from a scenario-specific prior function f_θe(s^ij) that can be hand-defined (e.g., distance threshold) or learned, and it gates which MI estimator (JSD for lossless, CLUB for lossy) is applied to each message-action pair during training.

## Method

The framework introduces a generalized binary communication-link prior ι^ij that uniformly characterizes lossy vs. lossless channels across scenarios, then constructs a Dual Mutual Information Estimator (Du-MIE) that decouples message impacts: a JSD-based lower-bound estimator maximizes MI between lossless messages and agent actions, while a CLUB-based upper-bound estimator minimizes MI between lossy messages and actions. These two MI estimates are injected into the global reward as shaping terms (weighted by α and β), so that the standard CTDE-based MARL loss (here MADDPG) simultaneously optimizes task return and communication quality. The Du-MIE networks are trained on separate replay buffers partitioned by link status, and the overall loss alternates between Du-MIE updates and MARL policy/critic updates, yielding a plug-in module compatible with both value-based and policy-based MARL algorithms.

## Results

CC-MADDPG achieves the highest average episode cumulative reward across all four MPE tasks and all seven communication-constraint levels. The most striking result is in Simple_Tag under Heavy DBC(1), where CC-MADDPG scores 138.0 while FC-MADDPG collapses to 1.5 and no-communication MADDPG reaches only 71.2. In Simple_Spread, CC-MADDPG maintains rewards between -127.6 and -129.4 regardless of constraint severity, whereas FC-MADDPG degrades from -138.7 to -191.9. Under Markov-based constraints, CC-MADDPG's performance is nearly invariant (e.g., 131.4–134.9 in Simple_Tag), demonstrating robustness to time-varying channel conditions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CC-MADDPG outperforms all baselines across every task and constraint level. Against FC-MADDPG (full-communication MADDPG), the advantage is most dramatic under distance-based constraints: in Simple_Tag Heavy DBC(1), CC-MADDPG scores 138.0 versus FC-MADDPG's 1.5, a 92× gap, and in Simple_Reference Heavy DBC(1) it achieves 62.1 versus 4.0. Against Dropout-MADDPG (message-dropout training at rates 0.2/0.5/0.8), CC-MADDPG consistently leads by 60–70 reward points in Simple_Tag (e.g., 138.0 vs. 68.7–71.4 under Heavy DBC) and by ~10–15 points in Simple_Spread (e.g., -128.0 vs. -138.0 to -144.5). MAIC (incentive communication via decentralized teammate modeling) is the weakest baseline, scoring near zero in Simple_Tag (~1.5–2.7) and heavily negative in Simple_Spread (~-282 to -301), indicating its incentive messages fail to guide cooperation under constraints. No-communication MADDPG is consistently the worst or second-worst, confirming that inter-agent communication is essential but must be robust to lossy channels.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on MADDPG (Lowe et al., 2017) as the base cooperative MARL algorithm and adapts the Dual Mutual Information Estimation (Du-MIE) framework from PMic (Li et al., 2022) to the communication-constrained setting. It departs from prior lossy-communication MARL work (Freed et al. on noise, Kim et al. on dropout, Zhang et al. on temporal buffering, Yuan et al. on delay) by proposing a unified binary link-status prior ι that characterizes all forms of lossy communication under a single model, rather than addressing each failure mode in isolation. The key methodological departure is the asymmetric use of JSD (lower bound, maximized for lossless messages) and CLUB (upper bound, minimized for lossy messages) as reward-shaping signals, which prior MI-based MARL work did not exploit for lossy/lossless separation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified communication-constrained prior offers a practical path toward deploying cooperative MARL in genuinely unreliable environments (underwater, cave, wireless) where no single failure model dominates. However, the experimental validation is confined to simple 2D MPE scenarios with a single seed and only MADDPG integration, and the Markov-based communication results show almost no performance variation across loss levels, weakening the evidence for the MBC component. Open questions include whether the framework extends to value-based MARL (QMIX, VDN), scales to larger agent populations in more complex environments, and whether the binary link abstraction is sufficient for continuous or partially-degraded channels.
