## Problem

When LoRA is combined with DP-SGD in federated learning, Gaussian noise added to both adapter matrices A and B is amplified quadratically through their product BA, severely degrading model performance. Existing mitigations like FFA-LoRA freeze A to a random constant, which avoids noise amplification but restricts the model's ability to adapt to the principal directions of aggregated updates. The paper addresses how to let A evolve over time to better capture these directions while preserving the DP guarantee and avoiding quadratic noise growth.

## Input / Output

The method takes a frozen pre-trained language model (RoBERTa-large) with LoRA adapters of rank r=8 injected into query and value projections, plus non-i.i.d. local text datasets partitioned across K clients via a Dirichlet distribution. Each client transmits only its optimized B matrix (393,216 parameters per round, half the cost of FedAvg), while the server performs the SVD reparameterization and broadcasts the updated A and B. The final output is a set of LoRA adapter weights (B matrices aggregated, A matrices orthonormalized via SVD) that, when added to the frozen pre-trained weights, yield the adapted model for downstream classification or next-token prediction tasks.

## Method

FedSVD periodically reparameterizes the LoRA adapter pair via SVD at the server: after aggregating client-updated B matrices, the server computes the rank-r SVD of the product B_i · Â_{i-1}, then sets Â_i to the top-r right singular vectors (V_i^T[:r,:]) and B̂_i to the remaining left-singular-vector-and-singular-value product (U_i[:,:r]·Σ_i[:r,:r]). Because rank(BA) ≤ r, this rank-r SVD exactly recovers the product, so all clients receive a consistent initialization without changing the effective model. Clients then optimize only B̂_i locally under DP-SGD while keeping Â_i fixed, eliminating the quadratic noise term ξ_B·ξ_A. The orthonormality of Â_i (spectral norm = 1) provides a tighter bound on the gradient norm of B, reducing unnecessary clipping under DP-SGD, and a theoretical analysis shows this yields a lower Hessian condition number than a random A in a two-layer ReLU MLP, implying a better-conditioned loss landscape. Since SVD is applied only as a post-processing step after B has already been privatized, the DP guarantee is preserved by post-processing invariance.

## Results

On six GLUE tasks without privacy constraints, FedSVD achieves 86.18% average accuracy, outperforming the strongest baseline FFA-LoRA (84.57%) by +1.29 pp. Under DP-SGD (ε=6, δ=10⁻⁵), the gap widens substantially: FedSVD reaches 76.79% average accuracy versus 68.02% for FFA-LoRA (+8.77 pp). Even under the stricter budget ε=3, FedSVD maintains 75.63% average accuracy, a +9.63 pp improvement over FFA-LoRA (66.00%). On the HellaSwag commonsense reasoning task with SmolLM-360M under DP (ε=6), FedSVD achieves 51.10% accuracy, surpassing FFA-LoRA (49.76%) and FedAvg (48.81%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedSVD consistently outperforms all four baselines across both private and non-private settings. Against FFA-LoRA (the strongest baseline, which freezes A to a random matrix), FedSVD gains +1.29 pp average on non-private GLUE, but the advantage grows to +8.77 pp at ε=6 and +9.63 pp at ε=3, demonstrating that SVD-based adaptive reparameterization of A becomes increasingly valuable as DP noise intensifies. FedAvg, which jointly trains and aggregates both A and B, suffers severe degradation under DP (e.g., 50.71% average at ε=3) due to quadratic noise amplification in the BA product, while FedSVD maintains 75.63%. FLoRA and FedEX-LoRA, which transmit large parameter sets or use residual corrections, underperform substantially (59.78% and 60.86% at ε=6 respectively), likely due to frequent random re-initialization and aggregation errors. On HellaSwag with SmolLM-360M under DP, FedSVD leads FFA-LoRA by +1.34 pp (51.10% vs. 49.76%), confirming generalizability to a more complex commonsense reasoning task.

## Relation to Prior Work

FedSVD builds directly on FFA-LoRA (Sun et al., ICLR 2024), which addressed noise amplification in LoRA under DP-SGD by freezing the A matrix to a random initialization. The key departure is replacing this fixed random A with a periodically updated orthonormal A derived from the SVD of the aggregated BA product, allowing A to adapt to the principal directions of collective updates without reintroducing quadratic noise. The approach also draws conceptual inspiration from PiSSA (which applies SVD to pretrained weights) but repurposes SVD as a server-side reparameterization of the adapter product rather than a one-time weight decomposition. The theoretical analysis connects to classical Hessian conditioning results, showing that orthonormal rows of A tighten the condition number bound for the loss landscape with respect to B.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The substantial DP-specific gains (+8.77 pp at ε=6, +9.63 pp at ε=3 over FFA-LoRA) suggest that the fixed random A matrix is a far more critical bottleneck under noise than previously appreciated, and that a simple SVD reparameterization can recover much of the lost signal. The orthonormality constraint provides a principled, provable mechanism for bounding gradient norms under clipping, which is directly relevant to any DP-SGD pipeline. Limitations include the server-side SVD overhead (mitigated by low-rank approximation at 60× speedup), evaluation restricted to text classification and one commonsense reasoning task, and theoretical guarantees limited to a two-layer MLP. Open questions include convergence analysis for deep non-linear models, extension to other modalities, and whether the orthonormality benefit persists at higher ranks or with more complex adapter architectures.
