## Problem

Concept Bottleneck Models (CBMs) suffer from spurious correlations that cause learned concepts to encode incorrect semantics, and existing mitigations either assume statistical independence between concepts or require costly interventions on the data-generating process. Additionally, CBMs demand large numbers of expensive concept labels. This paper addresses both gaps by providing a framework with theoretical guarantees on concept correctness and sample complexity that requires no interventions and only a few concept labels.

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes high-dimensional observations (RGB images of 3D objects or time-series data) through a pretrained CRL encoder in an unsupervised first stage, producing d-dimensional latent representations M. The second stage takes only a small number of paired (M, C) samples—where C are the interpretable concept labels—and outputs the alignment map α (a permutation plus per-concept transformations) that recovers the concepts. Concepts can be continuous-valued or binarized for downstream binary classification, and the final concepts feed into a standard label predictor for Y.

## Method

The framework is a two-step pipeline: (i) an off-the-shelf CRL method (e.g., DMS-VAE, CITRIS-VAE, iVAE) learns provably disentangled representations M from observations X without any labels; (ii) an alignment map α, consisting of a permutation π and element-wise transformations T, is learned from M to the annotated concepts C using few labels. Two estimators are proposed: a linear Group Lasso with a tunable feature map φ (identity, splines, or random Fourier features) that yields a finite-sample high-probability bound on both the regression error and correct permutation recovery (Thm. 4.2), with explicit sample-complexity scaling in n, d, p, and δ; and a kernelized RKHS Group Lasso that handles arbitrary invertible element-wise transformations and provides asymptotic consistency of the permutation estimate (Thm. 5.2). The permutation is recovered by solving a weighted matching problem over the group-norms of the estimated coefficients, which runs in cubic time in d and guarantees a valid permutation even under individual estimation errors.

## Results

On the Action Sparsity dataset, the proposed method (DMS-VAE + Spline) achieves 0.83 Label Acc and 0.40 OIS with only 100 concept labels, compared to 0.60/0.92 for CBM, 0.69/0.90 for CEM, and 0.61/0.92 for HardCBM. On the Temporal Causal3DIdent image dataset, CITRIS-VAE + Spline reaches 0.80 Label Acc and 0.44 OIS at n=100, versus 0.53/1.00 (CBM), 0.67/0.51 (CEM), and 0.53/0.47 (HardCBM). At n=10000, the proposed method maintains a Label Acc advantage (0.86 vs 0.88 for CBM on Action Sparsity; 0.83 vs 0.78 for CBM on Temporal Causal3DIdent) while achieving consistently lower OIS than CEM across all label budgets. The method also achieves a perfect NIS score, indicating no impurity distributed across concept representations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method consistently outperforms all three baselines (CBM, CEM, HardCBM) on Label Accuracy at low-to-moderate label budgets (n=20 to n=1000) on both datasets. On the Temporal Causal3DIdent image dataset, the gap is largest: at n=100, the proposed method achieves 0.80 Label Acc versus 0.53 (CBM), 0.67 (CEM), and 0.53 (HardCBM) — a 13–27 percentage point advantage. On OIS (concept impurity), the proposed method dominates at low n (0.44 vs 0.48–0.51 at n=100 on Temporal Causal3DIdent), while CEM performs worst across all settings (OIS of 0.49–0.57 even at n=10000). At very high label counts (n=10000), CBM and HardCBM achieve slightly lower OIS on Action Sparsity (0.06–0.07 vs 0.12), but the proposed method still leads on Label Acc (0.86 vs 0.88–0.89 is a near-tie) and maintains a clear OIS advantage on the image dataset (0.09 vs 0.16–0.18). The paper also reports that the proposed method requires significantly less computation time than the baselines, as it only needs to solve a Group Lasso and a weighted matching problem rather than training full concept-based neural networks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of causal representation learning (CRL) and concept bottleneck models (CBMs), building on the CRL identifiability framework of Schölkopf et al. [49] and the Group Lasso estimation machinery of Yuan and Lin [56]. Its most direct predecessor is Marconato et al. [40], who showed that disentangled representations can prevent concept leakage in CBMs but required interventions on the data-generating process; this paper removes that intervention requirement and instead provides a supervised alignment step with explicit sample-complexity guarantees. Unlike prior CBM methods (Koh et al. [28], Zarlenga et al. [57], Havasi et al. [17]) that assume concept independence or rely on large amounts of concept labels, the framework handles arbitrary concept dependencies and needs labels only for the permutation-and-transformation alignment, not for the full concept predictor.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first finite-sample and asymptotic guarantees on the correctness of learned concepts in a CBM setting without interventions, giving practitioners explicit guidance on how many concept labels are needed as a function of the number of concepts and feature dimension. Key limitations remain: the theory assumes concepts coincide exactly with CRL-recovered causal variables, requires low inter-representation correlation (though empirically the method tolerates much stronger correlations), and offers no guarantees for discrete/binary concepts. Open directions include extending the framework to causal abstractions where human concepts are coarser than the underlying causal variables, relaxing the perfect-disentanglement assumption, and developing theory for discrete concepts via logistic Group Lasso.
