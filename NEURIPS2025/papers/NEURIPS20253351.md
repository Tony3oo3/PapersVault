## Problem

Existing continual learning methods use the parameters of the most recently trained task for inference on all prior tasks, making them inherently susceptible to catastrophic forgetting. While model merging offers a stable post-training integration mechanism, it has not been systematically integrated into the sequential CL paradigm where only current-task data is accessible. The paper asks how to unify CL's sequential training dynamics with model merging's inference-time consolidation to reduce forgetting without sacrificing plasticity.

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images (e.g., 224×224 for ImageNet-R/A, DomainNet, CUB200; 32×32 for CIFAR-100) and outputs class labels via a ViT-B/16 backbone pretrained on ImageNet-21K/1K. LoRA modules of rank 10 are inserted into the key and value projections of multi-head attention, so only these low-rank adapters are updated per task. The merged inference model is a single set of parameters (base weights plus scaled LoRA deltas) that serves all tasks seen so far, requiring no task-specific routing or prompt selection at test time.

## Method

P&M operates in two stages. First, 'Infer after Merging': after training task t from the previous inference model θ̂_{t−1}, the task-specific optimum θ_t* is merged back via a convex combination θ̂_t = θ̂_{t−1} + α_t·Δθ_t*, where the coefficient α_t is derived in closed form by minimizing the second-order Taylor approximation of total loss increase across all t tasks, with the Hessian approximated by the diagonal empirical Fisher Information Matrix. Second, 'Train with Perturbation': during training, a stochastic perturbation along the task vector direction (sampling from {−ε, 0, +ε} with calibrated probabilities) provides an unbiased, single-forward-pass approximation of a Hessian-based regularizer that encourages flat minima and reduces parameter conflicts at merge time. The full pipeline is instantiated with per-task LoRA adapters to keep memory overhead low, and the Fisher matrices for prior tasks are stored at training time to support the closed-form merge coefficient computation.

## Results

On ImageNet-R with 10 tasks, LoRA-P&M achieves 79.95% average accuracy (Acc) and 85.29% average anytime accuracy (AAA), outperforming the strongest CL baseline SD-LoRA (77.34% Acc, 82.04% AAA) by +2.61% and +3.25% respectively. The method also delivers substantial gains over the plain LoRA baseline (+14.23% Acc on ImageNet-R 10 tasks) and surpasses model merging methods such as CoMA and CoFIMA by up to +3.86% on CUB-10. Across all five benchmarks (ImageNet-R, ImageNet-A, DomainNet, CIFAR-100, CUB-200), LoRA-P&M consistently achieves the highest Acc and AAA among all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LoRA-P&M outperforms all 12 compared baselines across every benchmark. Against the strongest CL method SD-LoRA [21], it gains +2.32% Acc on ImageNet-R 5 tasks (81.47 vs 79.15), +2.61% on 10 tasks (79.95 vs 77.34), and +1.11% on 20 tasks (76.37 vs 75.26); on DomainNet 5 tasks the gap widens to +4.09% (76.91 vs 72.82). Compared to InfLoRA [23], gains range from +4.52% (ImageNet-R 5 tasks) to +5.32% (DomainNet 5 tasks). Prompt-based methods (L2P [33], DualPrompt [58], CODA-Prompt [32], HiDe-Prompt [62]) trail by 4–12% Acc depending on dataset and task count. Among model merging baselines, P&M exceeds CoMA [63] by +0.61% on INR-10 and +3.86% on CUB-10, and surpasses CoFIMA [63] by +0.89% on INR-10 and +4.02% on CUB-10. The plain LoRA baseline [25] is the weakest, with P&M improving it by up to +13.6% (INR-20) and +15.87% (CIFAR-100). In the appendix, P&M also beats O-LoRA [67] (+2.32% on INR-10), DualP-PGP [68] (+12.13% on INR-10), and C-Prompt [69] (+4.33% on INR-10), and significantly outperforms LoRA-EWC [70] by +10% on ImageNet-R 5 tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate paradigms: rehearsal-free continual learning (CL) with LoRA (as in InfLoRA, SD-LoRA) and post-training model merging (task arithmetic, DARE, CoMA). While CL methods train sequentially and use the latest task's parameters for all inference, and merging methods combine independently trained models, P&M adopts the CL training trajectory but replaces the inference step with a convex combination of the previous inference model and the new task optimum. The theoretical grounding draws on second-order Taylor analysis and empirical Fisher approximations (similar in spirit to EWC), but repurposes the Fisher matrix to derive a closed-form merging coefficient rather than a training-time regularizer, and introduces a stochastic perturbation along the task vector direction as a zero-cost Hessian quadratic-form approximation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The closed-form merging coefficient eliminates the heuristic hyperparameter tuning required by prior merging methods (CoMA, DARE) and provides a principled way to balance stability and plasticity. The perturbation trick—replacing three forward passes with a single stochastic sample—is a practical efficiency win that makes the approach scalable. However, the diagonal Fisher approximation may not capture true curvature, memory still grows linearly with tasks (the online variant degrades at 20 tasks), and the method is validated only on ViT-B/16 with LoRA on image classification; generalization to other architectures, modalities, or longer task sequences remains open.
