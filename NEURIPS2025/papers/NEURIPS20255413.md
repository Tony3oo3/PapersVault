## Problem

No differentially private algorithm existed for the All-Pairs Min-Cut (APMC) problem, which seeks Min-s-t-Cuts for all vertex pairs simultaneously. Prior private cut algorithms either addressed a single pair (O(n/ε) error), the global minimum cut (O(log n/ε) error), or preserved all cuts via a synthetic graph (Õ(√(mn)/ε) error), leaving a gap of up to Ω(√n) in dense graphs between the best upper bound and the Ω(n) lower bound for a single cut.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes a weighted undirected graph G=(V,E,w) with positive edge weights and a privacy parameter ε, and outputs an approximate Gomory-Hu tree T on the same vertex set V. The tree T has n−1 edges such that for every pair s,t∈V, the minimum edge weight on the s-t path in T approximates the true Min-s-t-Cut value in G within Õ(n/ε) additive error with high probability. The algorithm runs in Õ(n²) time and satisfies pure (ε,0)-DP under the edge-neighborhood model where neighboring graphs differ in a single edge weight by at most 1.

## Method

The approach privatizes a polylogarithmic-depth GH-tree construction from Li [61] by replacing each Min-s-t-Cut call with a differentially private counterpart and introducing three key technical ingredients. First, a DP algorithm for Minimum Isolating Cuts is developed by repeatedly invoking the private Min-s-t-Cut mechanism of Dalirrooyfard et al. [29] O(log n) times, with cut submodularity used to bound accumulated error. Second, the recursive step is modified to handle additive approximations: edges are added from a contracted vertex to penalize large cuts (ensuring sub-instances are not too large), and the algorithm compares isolating cuts to the smallest-cardinality approximate Min-s-v-Cut rather than the true optimum (ensuring sufficient coverage). Third, a novel bounded-overlap branching composition theorem is introduced to account for privacy loss when a single edge appears in multiple recursive sub-instances at the same level; the algorithm sanitizes overlapping data by adding Laplace-noise edges from contracted vertices, reducing the overlap parameter to ℓ=2 and yielding a total privacy budget of O(h·ε) where h=O(log n) is the recursion depth.

## Results

The paper presents an ε-DP (pure DP) algorithm for All-Pairs Min-Cut (APMC) that outputs an approximate Gomory-Hu tree with Õ(n/ε) additive error for all vertex pairs, running in Õ(n²) time. This matches the error of the best known single Min-s-t-Cut algorithm [29] (O(n/ε)) up to polylog(n) factors, while computing all C(n,2) pairs simultaneously. The result is essentially optimal, as [29] proves an Ω(n) lower bound for even a single Min-s-t-Cut under both pure and approximate DP. The algorithm also yields a polynomial-time pure DP global Min-Cut with Õ(n/ε) error, improving on the prior exponential-time pure DP algorithm of [46].

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's Õ(n/ε) pure-DP APMC result strictly improves over the prior best pure-DP approach for all-pairs cuts, which was the All Cuts algorithm of Gupta et al. [47] with O(n^{3/2}/ε) error, by a factor of √n / polylog(n). It also improves over the approximate-DP All Cuts algorithms of Eliáš et al. [37] and Liu et al. [66] (both Õ(√(mn)/ε)), which can be as large as Õ(n^{3/2}/ε) in dense graphs, while additionally providing pure DP rather than approximate DP. The result matches the single-pair Min-s-t-Cut error of Dalirrooyfard et al. [29] (O(n/ε)) up to polylog factors, demonstrating that computing all pairs simultaneously does not incur additional error beyond the inherent Ω(n) lower bound. For global Min-Cut, the paper's corollary gives a polynomial-time pure-DP algorithm with Õ(n/ε) error, whereas Gupta et al. [46] achieved Θ(log(n)/ε) error only with exponential-time pure DP or polynomial-time approximate DP. The Ω(n) lower bound from [29] applies directly to APMC (since it generalizes single-pair Min-s-t-Cut), confirming the result is tight up to polylog(n) and 1/ε factors.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on three key threads: (1) the optimal ε-DP single Min-s-t-Cut algorithm of Dalirrooyfard et al. [29] with O(n/ε) error, (2) the polylogarithmic-depth non-private GH-tree algorithm of Li [61] (also appearing in [4]), and (3) the Minimum Isolating Cuts subroutine from [62, 3]. It departs from prior private All Cuts approaches [47, 37, 66] that preserve all cuts in the graph (yielding Õ(√(mn)/ε) error for APMC) by instead directly privatizing a low-depth recursive GH-tree construction. The paper introduces a novel 'bounded-overlap branching composition' theorem for privacy accounting in recursive algorithms where sub-instances share edges, which the authors note may be of independent interest beyond this application.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result closes a gap of roughly √(m/n) (up to Ω(√n) in dense graphs) between the Ω(n) lower bound inherited from single min-cut and the previous best Õ(√(mn)/ε) upper bound for private APMC, achieving essentially tight Õ(n/ε) error. The bounded-overlap composition technique provides a general tool for analyzing privacy in recursive algorithms with shared data, potentially applicable beyond graph cuts. Open questions include whether an efficient pure-DP algorithm exists for global min-cut with polylog(n)/ε error (current pure-DP results require exponential time), and whether the polylog factors in the error can be eliminated. The paper also yields a pure-DP min k-cut algorithm with multiplicative error 2 and additive error Õ(nk/ε), improving on prior work.
