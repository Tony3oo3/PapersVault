## Problem

Spectral Transform Units (STUs) provide a convex, stable training procedure for LDS-like sequence models, but their inference relies on convolutions with spectral filters that cost O(L log³ L) per token. No prior method could convert a trained STU back into explicit LDS parameters (A, B, C) with provable error bounds, leaving a gap between the STU's training robustness and the O(h) per-token recurrent inference that an LDS would enable. This paper provides the first provable distillation from STU spectral filters to a symmetric LDS, closing that gap.

## Input / Output

The distillation procedure takes as input the k fixed spectral filter vectors φ₁,…,φ_k ∈ ℝ^L (rows of Φ_{1:k}) from a trained STU layer, along with a target LDS state dimension h > k. It outputs a transformation matrix M̃ ∈ ℝ^{k×h} that, combined with a diagonal A = Diag(α₁,…,α_h) and Γ = Diag(1−α₁,…,1−α_h), defines the LDS system matrices (C = M̃Γ, A, B = 1_h). At inference, the resulting LDS consumes a token sequence u₁,…,u_t and maintains a hidden state x_t ∈ ℝ^h updated in O(h) per token, producing predictions y_t = Cx_t. The spectral filters are fixed and computed offline from the Hankel matrix Z, so only the M_j parameters are learned during training.

## Method

The key insight is that each LDS impulse response μ_L(α) = (1−α)[1, α, α², …, α^{L−1}] can be approximated in the spectral basis {φ_j} with error O(e^{−k/log L}), a direct consequence of the spectral filtering theory. Algorithm 2 samples h random scalars α_i ∈ [0,1], computes each μ_L(α_i)'s spectral coefficients m_i via a convex subproblem (FindSpectralRepresentation), stacks them into M ∈ ℝ^{h×k}, and returns M̃ = M⁻¹ so that Φ_{1:k} ≈ M̃·μ_L(α₁,…,α_h). Theorem 1 bounds the reconstruction error by c·λ_max·h·e^{−k/log L}, where λ_max is the largest singular value of M⁻¹. The practical algorithm replaces random sampling with multi-target Orthogonal Matching Pursuit over H ≫ h candidate α values drawn from a distribution biased toward |α| ≈ 1 (α_i = S_i(1 − U_i⁴)), then solves an unregularized least-squares fit to select the best h-dimensional subset. This yields an LDS with state dimension h = O(log L) that matches the STU's expressivity while enabling O(h) per-token recurrent inference instead of O(L log³ L) convolution.

## Results

On a 340M-parameter language model distilled from FlashSTU, SpectraLDS achieves an average score of 39.03 across nine benchmarks (MMLU, HellaSwag, PIQA, BoolQ, Winogrande, CSQA, OBQA, ARC-Easy, ARC-Challenge), statistically indistinguishable from the original FlashSTU (39.20) and slightly above a Transformer baseline (38.96). The spectral filter reconstruction error reaches 7.69×10⁻¹⁹ with a state dimension of 160. For autoregressive generation, SpectraLDS scales linearly with sequence length (O(L log L) total), achieving ~4× speedup over naive convolution at 262k tokens in the STU-only setting, while FutureFill exhausts memory at that length.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against FlashSTU 340M (Liu et al., 2024), SpectraLDS matches performance within standard error on all nine benchmarks (39.03 vs. 39.20 average), confirming that the STU-to-LDS distillation introduces negligible degradation while converting O(L log³ L) convolution-based inference into O(L log L) recurrent inference. Against FutureFill (Agarwal et al., 2025), SpectraLDS is competitive at short sequences (≤16k tokens) but pulls ahead at longer contexts: at 262k tokens in the STU-only architecture, SpectraLDS completes generation in ~1105 s while FutureFill runs out of memory, and in the hybrid architecture SpectraLDS is ~1.6× faster. Against classical system-identification methods including Ho-Kalman (Ho & Kalman, 1966), SpectraLDS significantly outperforms on learning high-memory symmetric LDSs (hidden dimension 256, spectral radius 0.99), achieving lower reconstruction MSE with far greater sample efficiency and training stability, whereas direct gradient-based LDS training diverges at state dimensions ≥1000.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper inverts the spectral filtering framework of Hazan et al. (2017) and the Spectral Transform Unit of Agarwal et al. (2024), which represent symmetric LDSs as convolutions with fixed spectral filters for convex training. While Laughing Hyena (Massaroli et al., 2023) distills general convolutional filters into diagonal LDS approximations without formal guarantees, and classical system identification methods (Ho-Kalman, ERA, N4SID) require data-dependent Hankel matrix decompositions that scale super-linearly, this work provides the first provable conversion from STU spectral filters to explicit LDS parameters using a fixed abstract Hankel matrix. The practical algorithm further departs from the theoretical construction by employing Orthogonal Matching Pursuit over a large pool of impulse responses with a near-unit-radius sampling distribution, rather than random sampling as in the base algorithm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that a 340M-parameter FlashSTU model can be distilled into LDS layers of state dimension 160 with statistically indistinguishable benchmark performance (39.20 vs. 39.03 average) while reducing per-token inference from O(log³L) to O(log L) is practically significant for long-context generation. Key limitations include the requirement for float64 precision in LDS layers (versus bfloat16 elsewhere), the restriction to symmetric A matrices, and the open question of whether the method extends to non-symmetric or selective state-space models. The paper also leaves open how LDS, convolution, and attention layers interact at the hardware level, and whether the float64 requirement can be relaxed without sacrificing the reconstruction error of ~10⁻¹⁹.
