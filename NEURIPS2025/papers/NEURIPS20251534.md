## Problem

Co-designing robot morphology and control is a bi-level optimization where each candidate morphology requires training a separate policy from scratch, making evaluation prohibitively expensive and limiting the number of designs explored. Global competition pressure in evolutionary and quality-diversity algorithms further causes premature convergence to a single dominant behavior (e.g., cheetah-like runners), suppressing the discovery of diverse locomotion strategies. The paper asks whether shared, cluster-specific policies can make evaluation cheap enough to explore orders of magnitude more designs while preserving behavioral diversity.

## Input / Output

The method consumes the UNIMAL morphology space (~10^18 unique designs with fewer than 10 limbs), where each morphology is an L×47 matrix of continuous and categorical limb parameters ordered by depth-first traversal, along with a flat-terrain locomotion task specification. It produces a diverse set of top-100 evolved morphologies (elites) paired with 40 cluster-specific multi-design Transformer policies that can evaluate new morphologies within their cluster without retraining. The evolved designs are further assessed on eight downstream tasks spanning agility, stability, and manipulation domains.

## Method

LOKI first trains a Transformer-based VAE to encode morphologies into a structured latent space, then applies K-means (Nc=40) to cluster structurally and behaviorally similar designs. For each cluster, a multi-design Transformer policy (5 layers, 2 heads) is trained via PPO on a dynamic pool of 20 elite morphologies, serving as a shared surrogate scoring function that generalizes across similar bodies. Instead of mutation, Dynamic Local Search (DLS) samples 128 random morphologies from the cluster every 2 iterations, evaluates them with the current policy, and replaces the 2 worst pool members with the 2 best samples—enabling broad exploration while localizing competition within each cluster. This policy-reuse mechanism allows LOKI to evaluate ~780× more designs (3.12M vs. 4,000) with 78% fewer simulation steps and 40% less FLOPs per morphology, while discovering diverse locomotion strategies (quadrupeds, crabs, spinners, rollers) that transfer better to unseen tasks than prior methods.

## Results

On flat-terrain locomotion in the UNIMAL design space, LOKI (Nc=40) achieves the highest QD-Score of 60.9 and full 100% cluster coverage, outperforming DERL (QD-Score 26.2, 37.5% coverage) and MAP-Elites (QD-Score 43.5, 65.0% coverage). In terms of efficiency, LOKI explores ~780× more morphologies (3.12M vs. 4,000) while using 78% fewer simulation steps (4.62B vs. 20B) and 40% fewer FLOPs per morphology (100B vs. 160B). On downstream task transfer, LOKI's evolved morphologies achieve substantially higher rewards on bump (1908 vs. 981), push box incline (3148 vs. 1519), and manipulation ball (172 vs. 142) compared to DERL.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DERL [Gupta et al., 2021], LOKI achieves a 2.3× higher QD-Score (60.9 vs. 26.2) and 2.7× higher coverage (100% vs. 37.5%), while DERL retains a slight edge in max fitness (5760.8 vs. 5671.9). On downstream task transfer, LOKI's morphologies outperform DERL on 6 of 8 tasks, with particularly large gains on bump (981→1908, +94%) and push box incline (1519→3148, +107%), reflecting DERL's overfitting to flat-terrain locomotion. Against MAP-Elites [Mouret & Clune, 2015], LOKI achieves a 40% higher QD-Score (60.9 vs. 43.5) and 35 percentage points more coverage (100% vs. 65.0%), again with MAP-Elites holding a marginal max-fitness advantage (5807.3 vs. 5671.9). The Latent-MAP-Elites variant, which uses the same VAE latent clusters as LOKI but retains MAP-Elites' mutation-based search, achieves full coverage but a substantially lower QD-Score (38.1), confirming that LOKI's Dynamic Local Search and shared-policy co-evolution are critical for discovering high-quality solutions across all niches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the bi-level morphology co-design framework of DERL (Gupta et al., 2021) by replacing per-morphology policy training with cluster-specific multi-design Transformer policies, drawing the architecture from Metamorph (Gupta et al., 2022). It departs from the mutation-based exploration standard in evolutionary robotics by adopting Dynamic Local Search (Hoos et al., 2015) as the sampling mechanism, and from global selection pressure by localizing competition within VAE-learned latent clusters. The quality-diversity literature (MAP-Elites, Novelty Search) informs the diversity objectives, but LOKI achieves comparable coverage without explicit diversity metrics, relying instead on structural clustering and local search to naturally produce diverse solutions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 780× increase in explored designs with 78% fewer simulation steps represents a substantial practical efficiency gain for morphology co-design, making previously infeasible search budgets tractable. The finding that shared policies within structurally coherent clusters generalize well to unseen downstream tasks (2× higher reward on manipulation tasks) suggests that the co-evolved morphologies are less overfitted to the training task than those from global-competition methods. Key limitations include the fixed number of clusters (no adaptive growth/shrinkage) and the restriction to a single design space (UNIMAL) and single training task (flat-terrain locomotion), leaving open whether the approach scales to more complex or multi-objective design problems.
