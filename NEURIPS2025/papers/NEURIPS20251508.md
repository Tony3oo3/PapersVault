## Problem

Omnimodal models face a fundamental tension: long-horizon video-audio reasoning demands many low-resolution frames for temporal coverage, while fine-grained pixel-level grounding requires high-resolution inputs that limit the number of processable frames. Moreover, defining what constitutes an 'optimal' keyframe selection or task reformulation is inherently ambiguous, making supervised fine-tuning impractical, and reinforcement learning remains largely unexplored in omnimodal (video+audio+text) settings.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251508/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system receives a video sequence V, a synchronized audio stream A, and a text instruction q. System 1 processes the video at low spatial resolution (128×28×28 per frame) to output a set of K selected keyframe segments and corresponding local grounding instructions that simplify the original long-horizon query into a localized, attribute-based problem. System 2 then operates on those keyframes at high resolution (900×28×28) to predict bounding boxes, which are fed into a frozen SAM2 model to produce temporally propagated pixel-level instance masks across the full video.

## Method

Omni-R1 introduces a two-system architecture where a Global Reasoning System (System 1) selects informative keyframes and reformulates the task at low resolution, while a Detail Understanding System (System 2) performs high-resolution grounding on the selected snippets. Because the optimal keyframe set and reformulated instructions are ambiguous and strongly coupled to System 2's behavior, the authors train System 1 end-to-end via Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) with a hierarchical reward: R_K encourages temporally diverse, salient keyframe selection; R_A measures per-frame instruction-to-object alignment via Hungarian matching loss; and R_G captures full-video segmentation quality as average frame-wise IoU. At each GRPO iteration, N candidate keyframe+instruction pairs are sampled, scored by running them through the frozen System 2 and SAM2, and the policy is updated using group-normalized advantages. Training requires only one epoch on small task splits (1,600 RefAVS + 2,600 ReVOS/MeViS samples), yet yields substantial gains over both the base model and specialized SOTA segmentation models.

## Results

On RefAVSBench, Omni-R1-7B achieves 47.2 J&F (seen) and 74.2 J&F (unseen), surpassing the previous SOTA EEMC by +4.4% and +17.0% respectively. On ReVOS, the collaborative Omni-R1-11B system (with Sa2VA as System 2) reaches 58.9% overall J&F, outperforming the much larger Sa2VA-26B (58.4%) and all other specialized segmentation models. On general understanding benchmarks, Omni-R1-7B improves over its Qwen2.5-Omni-7B base by +2.0% on OmniBench, +2.4% on VideoMME (General), and +4.2% on MVBench, surpassing all other open-source omni-modal models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On RefAVSBench, Omni-R1-7B (47.2/74.2 J&F seen/unseen) decisively outperforms the previous SOTA EEMC (42.8/57.2) and all prior audio-visual segmentation methods (AVSegFormer 40.2/43.1, GAVS 39.4/39.8, ReferFormer 40.7/39.6, R2VOS 33.0/38.9). On ReVOS, the collaborative Omni-R1-11B system (58.9% overall) surpasses the 26B-parameter Sa2VA-26B (58.4%) and Sa2VA-8B (57.6%), as well as VISA-13B (50.9%), TrackGPT-13B (45.0%), and LISA-13B (41.6%), despite using a 7B reasoning model paired with a 4B segmentation model. On general omni-modal benchmarks, Omni-R1-7B leads all open-source 7B omni-models: it achieves 49.3 on OmniBench (vs. Baichuan-Omni-1.5 at 42.9, MiniCPM-o 2.6 at 40.5), 73.0 on VideoMME Short (vs. VideoChat-R1 at 72.2), and 70.3 on MVBench (vs. Baichuan-Omni-1.5 at 63.7). The base Qwen2.5-Omni-7B without RL training scores only 36.6% on ReVOS, confirming that the RL-based two-system training is the primary driver of the +22.3% improvement in the collaborative setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GRPO-based RL paradigm established by DeepSeek-R1 and DeepSeekMath from purely linguistic reasoning to omnimodal (video + audio + text) settings, and departs from prior MLLM-RL work (Video-R1, VideoChat-R1, VLM-R1) that targeted only vision-language tasks. The key architectural departure is the two-system decomposition—separating a low-resolution Global Reasoning System (keyframe selection and task reformulation) from a high-resolution Detail Understanding System (pixel-level grounding via SAM2)—which directly addresses the temporal-coverage vs. spatial-resolution trade-off that prior omnimodal models handle monolithically. The hierarchical reward design (keyframe quality → frame-instruction alignment → global segmentation IoU) is novel in its progressive coupling structure, and the use of RL to train keyframe selection and instruction rewriting (rather than just chain-of-thought reasoning) represents a new application of policy optimization to perceptual planning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that one epoch of RL training on small VOS/AVS splits improves out-of-domain generalization on general understanding benchmarks (OmniBench, VideoMME, MVBench) while conventional SFT often degrades it is a practically important and somewhat counterintuitive result for the MLLM community. The two-system architecture offers a scalable template for handling the resolution-vs-coverage trade-off in any long-horizon multimodal task, and the demonstrated mitigation of cross-modal hallucination through multi-task RL training (13.4% improvement on AVHBench) addresses a persistent reliability concern. Key open questions include whether the one-way information flow between systems limits performance on tasks requiring fine temporal sensitivity, and whether bidirectional or iterative System 1 ↔ System 2 interaction (discussed as future work) would unlock further gains on more complex reasoning tasks.
