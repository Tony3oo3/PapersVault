## Problem

State-of-the-art 3D reconstruction methods (NeRF, Gaussian Splatting, SfM) produce photorealistic but non-interactive '3D photographs' that lack object individuality, articulated geometry, PBR materials, and physics properties required by graphics pipelines. Existing simulation-oriented approaches (AI2-THOR, ProcTHOR, Digital Cousin) either rely on manually crafted scenes or prioritize diversity over faithful reconstruction of real-world environments. No prior system can automatically convert noisy, in-the-wild RGB-D scans into compact, realistic, and interactive 3D scenes compatible with standard rendering and simulation pipelines.

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB-D scans of indoor rooms captured with an iPhone 13 Pro Max running Apple's RoomPlan app (3D Scanner in RoomPlan mode), which provides interactive layout estimation and oriented bounding box detection across hundreds of RGB frames per scene. The output is a complete graphics-ready 3D scene assembled in Blender, where each object is an individual entity with retrieved artist-crafted geometry, per-segment PBR materials, optional articulated components (doors, drawers), and rigid-body physics properties (mass estimated via MLLM, mesh-based collision boundaries). The resulting scene is directly importable into AR/VR, gaming, robotics, and digital-twin pipelines without further processing.

## Method

LiteReality is a four-stage training-free pipeline. First, a scene graph organizes detected walls and oriented bounding boxes into a physically plausible layout by encoding spatial relationships (attached-to-wall, on-top-of, table-chair pair, connecting-to) and resolving collisions via iterative constraint-based separation. Second, a hierarchical retrieval system matches each object to the best artist-crafted 3D model from a curated 5,283-asset database (3D-FUTURE, AI2-THOR, Sketchfab) through semantic subcategory filtering, DINOv2 visual feature ranking, pose-aware multi-view rendering comparison, and GPT-4 contextual selection; identical objects are grouped via KMeans clustering on DINOv2 features. Third, a Material Painting module maps image crops to 3D material segments using SAM-guided segmentation and MLLM semantic prompts, retrieves PBR materials via multi-step language-guided and CLIP-based visual search, then refines appearance with a lightweight albedo-only color shift in CIE LAB space. Fourth, a procedural Blender pipeline assembles walls, windows, doors, and objects, assigns rigid-body dynamics with MLLM-estimated mass, and enables interactive simulation.

## Results

On the full-scene graphics-ready reconstruction benchmark (Table 3), LiteReality achieves RMSE 0.2664, SSIM 0.5818, and LPIPS 0.6522, outperforming the best baseline (Digital Cousin + Make-It-Real) by 12.5% in RMSE and 5.7% in SSIM. On the retrieval similarity benchmark (Table 1), the training-free pipeline achieves an average Chamfer Distance of 0.0986 (avg/CAD) and 0.1067 (avg/class), beating ScanNotate (0.1042/0.1110), MSCD (0.1103/0.1188), and Digital Cousin (0.1411/0.1246). For object-centric PBR material estimation (Table 2), LiteReality scores RMSE 0.2163, SSIM 0.4353, and LPIPS 0.5854, surpassing Make-It-Real (0.2377/0.3981/0.6111) and PhotoShape (0.3225/0.2371/0.6558).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LiteReality outperforms all six baselines across all three benchmarks. In retrieval (Table 1), it beats ScanNotate by 5.4% in avg/CAD Chamfer Distance (0.0986 vs. 0.1042), MSCD by 10.7% (0.0986 vs. 0.1103), and Digital Cousin by 30.0% (0.0986 vs. 0.1411), with the largest gap on the 'table' category (0.1099 vs. 0.1832 for Digital Cousin). In material estimation (Table 2), LiteReality edges out the closest competitor MIR+AO (RMSE 0.2163 vs. 0.2156) while substantially outperforming PhotoShape (RMSE 0.2163 vs. 0.3225, SSIM 0.4353 vs. 0.2371) and standalone Make-It-Real (RMSE 0.2163 vs. 0.2377). In full-scene reconstruction (Table 3), LiteReality leads the best baseline (DC+MIR) by 12.5% in RMSE (0.2664 vs. 0.3046) and 5.7% in SSIM (0.5818 vs. 0.5492), with the largest margin over Phone2Proc (34.4% lower RMSE). The only near-tie is in material RMSE where MIR+AO is marginally better (0.2156 vs. 0.2163), but LiteReality wins on SSIM and LPIPS in that comparison.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by introducing the 'graphics-ready reconstruction' framing, which unifies four requirements—object-centricity, articulation, PBR materials, and physics—into a single pipeline from real RGB-D scans. Conceptually, it repurposes existing components (DINOv2 for retrieval, SAM for segmentation, GPT-4/MLLMs for semantic reasoning, CLIP for material matching) into a training-free, multi-stage system rather than building on any single prior architecture. It departs from the Scan2CAD line of work by prioritizing retrieval similarity over post-retrieval alignment, and from simulation-environment generators (ProcTHOR, Holodeck, Digital Cousin) by faithfully reconstructing real scenes rather than synthesizing novel ones. The material painting stage explicitly addresses failure modes of differentiable-rendering-based approaches (PSDR-Room, PhotoScene) by replacing expensive per-object optimization with a lightweight CIE LAB albedo shift.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LiteReality is the first end-to-end system to convert noisy, in-the-wild RGB-D scans into compact, editable, physics-enabled 3D scenes with PBR materials, directly addressing a gap between photorealistic reconstruction (NeRF, Gaussian Splatting) and simulation-ready environments (AI2-THOR, ProcTHOR). The three introduced benchmarks (retrieval similarity over full ShapeNet, object-centric material recovery under real scanning conditions, and full-scene graphics-ready reconstruction) provide a foundation for future work in this space. Key open questions include scaling to multi-room and building-level scenes, recovering full SVBRDF for faithful relighting, handling smaller objects, and achieving near-real-time performance. The pipeline's dependence on upstream perception quality (RoomPlan) and its occasional retrieval failures remain practical limitations that constrain full automation.
