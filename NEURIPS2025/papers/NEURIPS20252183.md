## Problem

Conventional score-based diffusion models require inverting the noise covariance matrix in the denoising score matching objective, which becomes numerically unstable for anisotropic or structured Gaussian noise. This prevents them from serving as effective Bayesian priors for imaging inverse problems corrupted by spatially correlated noise (fog, turbulence, speckle, detector noise), where the noise covariance is far from diagonal. The paper bridges the gap between score-based diffusion and flow matching by enabling stable training under arbitrary Gaussian forward noising processes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252183/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a noisy measurement y = Ax_0 + n where n is spatially correlated Gaussian noise with known covariance structure, a forward imaging operator A (identity for denoising, motion/lens blur kernels, Born-approximation scattering operators, or a 2D Laplacian for transport-of-intensity), and the measurement noise covariance Σ_y. It produces a reconstructed image x_0 via iterative reverse-time ODE sampling (1000 steps). Experiments use RGB images at 32×32 (CIFAR-10), 64×64 (CelebA), and 256×256 (CelebA-HQ), with the correlated noise modeled as grayscale or color Gaussian-filtered white noise with kernel std ranging from 0.1 to 20 pixels depending on resolution.

## Method

The core contribution is learning the Whitened Score G_t G_t^T ∇_{x_t} log p_t(x_t) instead of the standard score, which avoids covariance inversion because G_t G_t^T is proportional to the transition kernel covariance Σ_t, so their product with the conditional score yields a closed-form expression β_t(α_t x_0 − x_t)/(1−α_t²) with no matrix inverse. The forward SDE is a variance-preserving process with non-diagonal diffusion matrix G_t = √β_t K, where K is a circulant Gaussian convolution matrix (implementable via FFT) whose std is randomly sampled during training to cover a range of spatial correlation lengths. The network (attention UNet) is trained with a denoising loss regressing its output against this closed-form whitened score, and for inverse problems the deterministic probability flow ODE is augmented with a likelihood-guided update Σ_y^{-1}A^H(y−Ax_t) that is naturally preconditioned by G_t G_t^T when the diffusion matrix is designed to match the measurement noise covariance. The paper further proves a formal equivalence showing the conditional flow-matching vector field is a linear combination of the whitened score and the drift term, unifying the two frameworks for arbitrary Gaussian probability paths.

## Results

The paper evaluates WS diffusion priors against conventional isotropic-noise diffusion priors across five imaging inverse problems (denoising, motion deblurring, lens deblurring, linear inverse scattering, and differential defocus) on CIFAR-10 (32×32), CelebA (64×64), and CelebA-HQ (256×256). The primary metric is PSNR, reported as labels on reconstructed images in figures rather than in a summary table. WS DMs consistently achieve higher PSNR than conventional DMs when measurements are corrupted by spatially correlated (anisotropic) Gaussian noise, while performing comparably for uncorrelated (isotropic) noise. For generative quality, FID scores decrease as the maximum spatial correlation range (std_max) of the training noise increases (Appendix D, Fig. 12).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is a conventional score-based diffusion model (DM) trained on isotropic Gaussian noise (K=I) with the same attention UNet architecture. WS DMs outperform this baseline in PSNR across all correlated-noise tasks: denoising (Figs. 2, 4, 7), motion deblurring (Figs. 5, 8), lens deblurring (Figs. 5, 8), linear inverse scattering (Figs. 9, 10), and differential defocus (Fig. 11). The advantage is most pronounced when the additive noise's low-frequency spectral support overlaps with the image signal spectrum, where conventional DMs mistakenly suppress image features as noise. For uncorrelated (white) noise, the two methods perform similarly, confirming that the WS framework's benefit is specifically tied to structured noise. The paper does not report a single aggregate PSNR number; instead, per-image PSNR values are annotated directly on the reconstruction figures, and the qualitative claim is 'consistently higher PSNR' across all tested SNR levels and noise covariance structures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the score-based diffusion model framework (Song et al. 2021; Ho et al. 2020) by replacing the standard score ∇log p_t(x_t) with the 'whitened score' G_t G_t^T ∇log p_t(x_t), which eliminates the need for covariance matrix inversion in the denoising score matching objective (Vincent 2011). This modification enables arbitrary anisotropic Gaussian forward processes, bridging a gap that previously separated score-based DMs from flow matching (Lipman et al. 2023) in the non-isotropic noise regime. The paper formalizes the equivalence between the WS objective and FM's conditional vector field (Eq. 15), and connects the learned quantity to the predictable process in the reverse-time SDE as derived by Anderson (1982) and Castanon (1982). For inverse problem solving, it adopts the likelihood-guided posterior score approximation of Jalal et al. (2021) as the measurement consistency step.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a principled, drop-in modification to existing diffusion priors that makes them suitable for the structured, spatially correlated noise prevalent in real imaging modalities (fog, turbulence, microscopy speckle, OCT), where conventional isotropic-noise priors are mismatched. The spectral inductive bias learned from anisotropic training noise allows the model to selectively remove low-frequency correlated noise even when it overlaps with signal content in the Fourier domain—a capability isotropic priors lack. Key open questions include: (i) reducing the ~1000-step sampling cost via distillation, (ii) jointly learning the diffusion matrix G_t to adapt to unknown measurement noise covariance, and (iii) extending to latent diffusion formulations for competitive unconditional/conditional generation. The current evaluation compares only against isotropic-noise DMs, leaving open how WS priors compare to other structured-noise restoration approaches (e.g., stochastic restoration priors of Hu et al. 2025).
