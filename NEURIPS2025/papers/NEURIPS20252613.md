## Problem

Theoretical guarantees for neural network-based score-based generative models (SGMs) have relied on restrictive assumptions such as Lipschitz continuity of the score function, strictly positive lower bounds on the target density, or bounded support. This paper asks whether nearly minimax-optimal approximation and generalization rates can be established for SGMs under merely a sub-Gaussian assumption on the data distribution, without any of these additional regularity conditions.

## Input / Output

The method takes n i.i.d. samples from an unknown α-sub-Gaussian distribution P₀ on ℝᵈ and produces a ReLU DNN (width ≤ O(n^{3/d} log n), depth ≤ O(log² n)) that approximates the score function ∇log p_t for all t ∈ [t₀, T], where t₀ > O(α²n^{-2/d} log n). Plugging this network into the reverse OU SDE starting from standard Gaussian noise yields generated samples from P̂_{t₀}^{γ_d}. The primary quantitative outputs are high-probability TV distance bounds: Õ(α^{d/2} t₀^{-d/4} n^{-1/2}) for P_{t₀} vs. P̂_{t₀}^{γ_d}, and Õ(n^{-s/(d+2s)}) for P₀ vs. P̂_{t₀}^{γ_d} when the density lies in a Sobolev or Besov class with smoothness s.

## Method

The framework proceeds in three stages. First, it establishes a score estimation rate of Õ(n^{-1}σ_t^{-d-2}(σ_t^d ∨ 1)) for a regularized empirical score function ∇p̂_t/(p̂_t ∨ ρ_{n,t}) using empirical Bayes smoothing, requiring only sub-Gaussianity and removing the Lipschitz score assumption from prior work. Second, it constructs a compact ReLU DNN architecture that approximates this regularized surrogate at rate Õ(n^{-1}) by partitioning the Gaussian kernel argument into subintervals and using Taylor expansions with shared sub-networks for polynomial, exponential, and reciprocal components—avoiding the linear-in-n network size that a naive one-subnetwork-per-KDE-component construction would require. Third, it verifies Bernstein's condition on the excess risk (enabled by the uniform boundedness of the regularized surrogate) and applies Bernstein's inequality with an ε-net argument to obtain a high-probability score estimation bound of Õ(n^{-1}t₀^{-d/2}), then converts this to a TV distance bound via Girsanov's theorem. Early stopping at t₀ = n^{-2/(d+2s)} controls the bias from not reversing the full diffusion, yielding nearly minimax-optimal rates for Sobolev and Besov class densities.

## Results

Under only a sub-Gaussian assumption on P_0, the paper establishes a neural network score estimation rate of Õ(t_0^{-d/2} n^{-1}) in score matching loss (Theorem 2) and a TV distance bound of Õ(α^{d/2} t_0^{-d/4} n^{-1/2}) for distribution estimation at the early-stopping time t_0 (Theorem 3). With an additional Sobolev or Besov smoothness assumption on the target density and an appropriate early-stopping strategy t_0 = n^{-2/(d+2s)}, the paper achieves nearly minimax-optimal TV distance rates of Õ(n^{-s/(d+2s)}) (Corollaries 1 and 2). The required ReLU DNN has width Õ(n^{3/d} log_2 n) and depth Õ(log^2 n), and the results hold without Lipschitz score or density lower-bound assumptions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's main theoretical contribution is matching the minimax-optimal rates of kernel-based (KDE) estimators while using neural networks, under strictly weaker assumptions. Compared to [29] (Zhang et al., KDE-based), the paper achieves the same Õ(n^{-1/2} t_0^{-d/4}) rate for TV(P_{t_0}, P̂_{t_0}) and the same Õ(n^{-s/(d+2s)}) rate for TV(P_0, P̂_0) under Sobolev smoothness, but does so with ReLU DNNs rather than KDEs, making the results directly applicable to the neural network estimators used in practice. Compared to [30] (Wibisono et al.), the paper removes the L-Lipschitz score assumption entirely, which excludes distributions supported on submanifolds and can hide additional dimension dependence; the rate Õ(n^{-s/(d+2s)}) also improves upon [30]'s Õ(L^{(d+2)/(d+4)} n^{-1/(d+4)}) when the Lipschitz constant L grows with dimension. Compared to [22] (Oko et al.), the paper removes the density lower-bound assumption (which excludes multimodal distributions and well-separated mixtures) and extends from bounded support to the full sub-Gaussian class on R^d, while achieving the same Õ(n^{-s/(d+2s)}) rate. The paper also identifies and corrects a flaw in Theorem C.4 of [22] that invalidated prior convergence rate proofs in [22, 26, 27].

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the empirical Bayes smoothing framework of Wibisono et al. [30] by removing the Lipschitz score assumption, and builds on the DNN approximation theory of Lu et al. [38] to construct compact ReLU networks that approximate KDE-based score functions without size scaling linearly in n. It departs from the neural network SGM analysis of Oko et al. [22] and subsequent manifold-based works [26, 27] by eliminating the density lower-bound requirement, and refines the KL-divergence rate of Block et al. [42] from exponential to polynomial dependence on the smoothing parameter. The overall positioning is as a unifying theoretical framework that matches the minimax rates previously achieved only by kernel-based estimators [29] using neural network estimators under strictly milder assumptions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By removing both the Lipschitz score and density lower-bound assumptions, this work provides the first nearly minimax-optimal TV distance guarantees for neural network-based SGMs under merely sub-Gaussian data, closing a gap that limited prior theory to bounded-support or smooth-manifold distributions. The practical implication is that diffusion models' empirical success on multimodal, heavy-tailed, or manifold-supported data now has a theoretical explanation without requiring conditions that those distributions violate. Key open questions remain: the curse of dimensionality persists in the rates, the analysis is restricted to smoothness s ≤ 2 due to the linear KDE estimator, and extending beyond sub-Gaussian tails (to sub-Weibull or sub-exponential) is left as future work.
