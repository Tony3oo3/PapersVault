## Problem

Overparameterized ReLU networks can interpolate a dataset in infinitely many ways, and while ℓ¹ path norm regularization controls generalization, it does not guarantee sparsest solutions—existing sparsity-inducing schemes (iterative pruning, gating, variational dropout) remain heuristic with no theoretical guarantee of recovering truly sparsest networks. This paper asks whether a simple, differentiable regularization objective can provably yield the sparsest interpolating ReLU network, extending the classical ℓ^p (0<p<1) sparsity-promoting strategy from finite-dimensional linear problems to the infinite-dimensional, data-adaptive setting of neural networks.

## Input / Output

The method takes a dataset of N points (x_i, y_i) ∈ R^d × R, a network width K ≥ N, and a scalar p ∈ (0,1), and produces a single-hidden-layer ReLU network f_θ(x) = Σ_k v_k(w_k^T x̄)_+ that exactly interpolates the data while minimizing the ℓ^p path norm Σ_k ‖v_k w_k‖_p^p. The output is guaranteed to have at most N active neurons, and for a data-dependent threshold p*, any 0 < p < p* yields a network whose nonzero input weight/bias parameters among active neurons are globally minimized (solving the ℓ^0 path norm problem). In the univariate case, the optimal function is unique for almost every p and requires at most N−2 neurons, with explicit data-dependent bounds on parameter magnitudes and Lipschitz constant.

## Method

The core contribution is proving that global minimizers of the ℓ^p path norm (0 < p < 1) coincide with sparsest interpolating ReLU networks, recasting a combinatorial ℓ^0 problem as a smooth optimization task. For univariate inputs, the problem is reformulated as minimizing the p-variation of the derivative over continuous piecewise linear functions; a geometric characterization (Theorem 3.1) constrains solutions to be linear between opposite-curvature points and to have at most m−1 intermediate slopes on runs of m same-curvature points, reducing the problem to minimizing a strictly concave function over a hypercube where the Bauer maximum principle pins solutions to vertices. For multivariate inputs, the problem is recast as a finite-dimensional minimization over a polytope defined by all 2^O(N) ReLU activation patterns (following Pilanci and Ergen 2020), where strict concavity on each orthant again forces solutions to polytope vertices, yielding ℓ^0 solutions for sufficiently small p. The objective is continuous and differentiable away from zero, making it directly compatible with gradient descent; practically, the authors implement it via an iteratively reweighted ℓ¹ proximal gradient algorithm, which empirically recovers sparsest solutions faster than unregularized or weight-decay training.

## Results

The paper's primary contributions are theoretical: for univariate inputs, minimum ℓ^p path norm interpolants (0 < p < 1) are unique for almost every p, require at most N−2 active neurons, and are also ℓ^1 minimizers with explicit data-dependent parameter and Lipschitz bounds (Corollaries 3.1.1–3.1.2). For arbitrary input dimensions, Theorem 4.1 guarantees a data-dependent threshold p* below which ℓ^p minimization recovers an ℓ^0 (sparsest) solution, with at most N active neurons and O(N) nonzero input weight/bias parameters under general position. In experiments on synthetic data, the p=0.4 reweighted-ℓ^1 algorithm recovers the true sparsest solution on the univariate dataset, and all ℓ^p-regularized networks (p ∈ {0.4, 0.7, 1}) meet the Proposition 4.1 sparsity upper bound on the multivariate dataset, achieving far sparser solutions earlier in training than unregularized or weight-decay baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares ℓ^p path norm regularization (p ∈ {0.4, 0.7, 1}) against unregularized Adam, AdamW weight decay, and ℓ^1 (p=1) path norm regularization on two synthetic datasets. All ℓ^p-regularized networks converge to sparser solutions substantially earlier than both unregularized and weight-decay-regularized training, with p=0.4 yielding the sparsest networks. On the univariate 'peak/plateau' dataset, only the p=0.4 regularized network recovers the true sparsest (ℓ^0) interpolant, while p=1 and the unregularized baselines do not. On the high-dimensional random dataset, all three ℓ^p values recover solutions within the O(N) sparsity upper bound guaranteed by Proposition 4.1. Theoretically, ℓ^1 path norm minimization alone is shown to be insufficient for sparsity: it admits solutions with arbitrarily many active neurons (Fig. 1a), whereas ℓ^p for sufficiently small p provably selects the sparsest interpolant.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the classical ℓ^p (0<p<1) sparsity-promoting penalty from compressed sensing—where it applies to fixed, finite-dimensional linear systems—to the infinite-dimensional, data-adaptive setting of single-hidden-layer ReLU networks. The univariate analysis builds on the variational spline framework of Parhi & Nowak and the ℓ^1 path norm characterizations of Hanin and Debarre et al., but departs sharply by showing that ℓ^p (p<1) minimization enforces sparsity where ℓ^1 alone does not. The multivariate analysis is heavily inspired by the convex reformulation of Pilanci & Ergen (2020) and the polytope-vertex sparsity arguments of Yang et al. (2022) and Peng et al. (2015), adapted to handle the ReLU activation constraints. The practical algorithm draws on iteratively reweighted ℓ^1 methods from Candes et al. (2008) and Figueiredo et al. (2007).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first rigorous result showing that a smooth, gradient-compatible objective provably yields the sparsest ReLU network interpolant, recasting a combinatorial ℓ^0 problem as continuous optimization without pruning pipelines. Key open questions include: whether the critical threshold p* can be computed or estimated efficiently (the proof only shows existence via exponential vertex enumeration), whether results extend to deep or multi-output architectures, and whether the sparsity notion (nonzero input weight/bias parameters vs. entire neurons) generalizes naturally. The univariate finding that ℓ^p minimizers are simultaneously ℓ^1 minimizers with explicit parameter and Lipschitz bounds is a useful structural guarantee absent from prior ℓ^1-only analyses.
