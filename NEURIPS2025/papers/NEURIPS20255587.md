## Problem

Existing VLM-based domain generalization methods rely on static alignment strategies (prompt learning, cosine matching, adversarial regularization) that assume visual features can be directly projected onto class-level textual anchors in a single deterministic step. This assumption breaks under dramatic domain shifts, where degraded semantics and spurious correlations in unseen-domain features cannot be corrected by fixed projection. The paper asks whether modeling the image-to-text alignment as a stochastic semantic evolution process can yield better generalization to unseen domains.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255587/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes source-domain RGB images (resized to 512×512) and class-name text queries encoded by a frozen text encoder (CLIP or EVA-02) as input. For classification, it outputs a class label via global pooling and a linear Softmax head; for segmentation, it outputs per-pixel masks by computing cosine similarity between the refined pixel features and class text embeddings through a Mask2Former pixel decoder. A key detail is that only top-k spatially selected visual tokens (a fraction K of the feature map) are evolved through the stochastic process, while the remaining features are left unchanged before being written back into the full feature map for decoding.

## Method

SBGen formulates the alignment from domain-specific visual features to domain-agnostic text semantics as a Schrödinger Bridge — a time-indexed SDE dz_t = f_θ(z_t, t)dt + √(2ε)dW_t — with a learnable MLP drift conditioned on sinusoidal time embeddings and the target class text query. The pipeline has three stages: (1) text-guided domain-aware feature selection computes cosine similarity between each spatial token and each class embedding to isolate semantically relevant tokens; (2) stochastic cross-domain evolution simulates the SDE via Euler-Maruyama discretization over T=5 steps, producing a trajectory of intermediate feature states; (3) stochastic domain-agnostic interpolation uses the terminal evolved features as refined class anchors written back to the original feature map. Training jointly minimizes task loss plus a KL regularization term between the learned path distribution and a Brownian reference, and the paper proves via Pinsker's inequality that the SB transport yields a strictly tighter generalization error bound than any deterministic mapping.

## Results

On domain generalization in classification, SBGen achieves a mean accuracy of 80.4% across PACS, VLCS, OfficeHome, DomainNet, and TerraIncognita, outperforming the previous best CLIPCEIL++ (79.1%) by 1.3%. Notably, it reaches 86.7% on VLCS, 64.4% on DomainNet, and 63.5% on TerraIncognita, setting new state-of-the-art on all three. On domain generalized semantic segmentation, SBGen (EVA02-L backbone) achieves 68.74% mean mIoU on G→C/B/M and 71.97% on C→B/M, surpassing the second-best MGRNet by 1.86% and 1.41% respectively, with per-domain gains of up to 1.94% mIoU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In classification, SBGen outperforms all 14 VLM-based DG baselines on average accuracy, with the largest margins over CLIPCEIL++ (+1.3%), DPR (+2.6%), and CLIPOOD (+1.8%). It achieves the best single-dataset scores on VLCS (86.7%), DomainNet (64.4%), and TerraIncognita (63.5%), while remaining competitive on the highly saturated PACS (97.4% vs. 97.5% for DPR/KAdaptaion). In segmentation, SBGen dominates all 17 compared DGSS methods: it beats the closest competitor MGRNet by 1.71/1.12/1.94 mIoU on G→C/B/M and by 1.33/1.47 mIoU on C→B/M, and surpasses tqdm, FADA, SET, and REIN by 2.7–3.9 mIoU on average. The method also generalizes across backbones, outperforming MGRNet on both DINOv2 (+0.01 avg) and CLIP (+1.02 avg) encoders, confirming that the SB evolution is backbone-agnostic. Ablation against static alignment (DCM, w.o. TID) shows the stochastic evolution contributes +1.56 mIoU over the best static baseline, validating the core design choice.

## Relation to Prior Work

SBGen departs from the dominant VLM-based domain generalization paradigm, which treats the vision-language model as a static feature extractor and enforces fixed alignment (cosine matching, prompt tuning, adversarial regularization) between image features and class-level text embeddings. Instead, it reframes this alignment as a Schrödinger Bridge — a time-indexed stochastic differential equation with a learnable, query-conditioned drift — drawing directly on the diffusion Schrödinger bridge literature (De Bortoli et al., NeurIPS 2021; Vargas et al., 2021) and the textual query-driven mask transformer pipeline (Pak et al., ECCV 2024) for its segmentation architecture. The key conceptual shift is replacing a single deterministic projection onto textual anchors with a multi-step stochastic trajectory that progressively reduces domain-specific bias, grounded in a provably tighter generalization bound via Pinsker's inequality applied to the SB path measure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled stochastic-process formulation for domain shift that goes beyond the static-alignment assumption underlying most VLM-based DG methods, and backs this with a theoretical result (Theorem 3) showing the SB transport yields a strictly tighter generalization bound than any deterministic coupling. Practically, it achieves SOTA on five classification benchmarks and four segmentation benchmarks with minimal overhead (0.2 GPU hours, 1.58M extra parameters), making it a drop-in upgrade for existing VLM-based DG pipelines. Open questions include whether the multi-step SDE simulation scales efficiently to higher-dimensional feature spaces or more complex modalities (video, audio), and whether the Euler-Maruyama discretization with T=5 steps is near-optimal or whether adaptive step-size schemes could further reduce the computational cost.
