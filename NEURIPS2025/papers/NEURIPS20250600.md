## Problem

Existing video QA benchmarks evaluate MLLMs on static, offline clips with fixed question-answer pairs, failing to test whether models can maintain coherent understanding as visual scenes evolve in real time. This paper identifies that current models face memory loss and attention shift in streaming contexts, and that no benchmark rigorously probes continuous temporal tracking—specifically, the ability to update answers as the same conceptual query's correct response shifts over the course of a video.

## Input / Output

The benchmark consumes 552 long-form videos (average 18.2 min, 167.2 h total) spanning intelligent driving, sports, and egocentric scenarios, with 4,608 multiple-choice QA pairs. The critical input distinction is temporal: for online models, queries are injected at their timestamp and answered from the model's continuously updated internal state; for offline models, only the relevant video segment around the query is extracted and provided. Output is a multiple-choice letter, scored via two metrics—plain Accuracy and a hierarchical Score that awards points for advanced (q2) questions only when all prerequisite basic (q0, q1) questions in the same group are also correct.

## Method

RTV-Bench is built on three design principles. First, Multi-Timestamp QA revisits the same conceptual query (e.g., 'What is the ball carrier doing?') at multiple timestamps where the correct answer changes, forcing models to track state transitions rather than retrieve a single static fact. Second, a hierarchical question structure groups three questions of escalating difficulty per video, where the complex third question logically subsumes the first two, enabling the conditional Score metric to penalize superficial success without foundational understanding. Third, a multi-dimensional taxonomy decomposes performance into eight sub-capabilities (Temporal/Scene/Visual Perception, Future Prediction, Phenomenological Understanding, Intent Analysis, Global Understanding, Spatiotemporal Reasoning) organized under Perception, Understanding, and Reasoning, supplemented by an Object-Action-Event cross-cutting analysis. The benchmark is manually annotated with LLM-assisted template generation, and evaluation is conducted across 10 models (offline, online, and proprietary) under uniform frame-sampling protocols (8–64 frames) to isolate the effect of temporal density from model scale.

## Results

On RTV-Bench (552 videos, 4,608 QA pairs), GPT-4o achieves the highest overall performance at 50.02% Accuracy and 22.10 Score, while the best open-source model IXC2.5-OL reaches 47.33% Accuracy and 15.40 Score. All evaluated models remain below 50% overall accuracy, with open-source offline models (Qwen2.5-VL, VideoLLaMA2, VideoLLaMA3, LLaVA-OneVision, LLaVA-Video) clustering between 34.5% and 40.4% Accuracy. Online models consistently outperform offline counterparts by 5–8 points in Accuracy and 4–8 points in Score, and GPT-4o leads in MTQA accuracy (44.73%) over all other models (33–38%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RTV-Bench is a benchmark, so the 'method' is the evaluation protocol itself; the key comparison is among the 10 MLLMs tested. GPT-4o leads all models on every dimension (Perception 51.61%, Understanding 49.31%, Reasoning 48.71%, MTQA 44.73%), outperforming the best open-source model IXC2.5-OL by 2.7–6.5 points in Accuracy and 6.7–9.5 points in Score. Among open-source models, IXC2.5-OL (47.33% Acc / 15.40 Score) surpasses VideoChat-Online (45.83% / 12.10) and VITA-1.5 (44.51% / 11.80), and decisively beats all offline models: Qwen2.5-VL (40.41% / 7.13), VideoLLaMA2 (39.55% / 7.90), VideoLLaMA3 (36.42% / 6.10), LLaVA-Video (34.90% / 4.80), and LLaVA-OneVision (34.49% / 4.40). Notably, even the weakest online model (VITA-1.5) exceeds the strongest offline model (Qwen2.5-VL) by 4.1 points in Accuracy and 4.7 points in Score. Gemini 2.0 Flash (42.00% / 12.00) underperforms all open-source online models despite being a proprietary system, and trails GPT-4o by 8 points in Accuracy. The Score metric amplifies gaps more than Accuracy: GPT-4o's Score (22.10) is 43% higher than IXC2.5-OL's (15.40), while the Accuracy gap is only 5.7%, indicating that GPT-4o's advantage lies primarily in reliable hierarchical reasoning rather than basic perception.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground in benchmark design for real-time video understanding by departing from both offline video QA benchmarks (Video-MME, MVBench) and earlier streaming benchmarks (VStream, StreamingBench, OVO-Bench). While prior streaming benchmarks introduce *different* questions at different timestamps, RTV-Bench's Multi-Timestamp QA mechanism revisits the *same* conceptual query at multiple points where the correct answer shifts as the scene evolves—a fundamentally different evaluation paradigm. The hierarchical question structure (basic perception → advanced reasoning with prerequisite gating) also distinguishes it from flat QA formats used in prior work, and the eight-dimensional evaluation taxonomy (TP, SP, VP, FP, PU, IA, GU, SR) provides finer-grained diagnostics than aggregate accuracy scores.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals that most MLLMs score below 50% on continuous video analysis, exposing a substantial gap between offline video understanding and real-time streaming comprehension. The counter-intuitive finding that increasing frame density does not consistently improve performance—and can even degrade it—challenges the common assumption that more temporal sampling is always beneficial, pointing to architectural bottlenecks in current models. Key open questions include: what temporal modeling mechanisms are actually needed for long-horizon streaming, why does the online-vs-offline gap persist even at matched parameter scales, and how would incorporating audio or other modalities change the picture. The benchmark is currently limited to the visual modality, which constrains its applicability to truly realistic multimodal streaming scenarios.
