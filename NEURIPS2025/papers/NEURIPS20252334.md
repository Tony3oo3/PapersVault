## Problem

Loss of plasticity (LoP) in deep continual learning has been widely observed, yet its underlying mechanisms remained insufficiently understood, with prior work attributing it to various symptoms (dormant neurons, feature rank decrease, weight norm growth) without a unified geometric explanation. This paper asks what the fundamental geometric causes of LoP are and whether a single intervention can address them simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252334/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on RGB images in two continual learning benchmarks: Continual ImageNet (5000 binary classification tasks, 600 train / 100 test images per class, 250 epochs per task) and Class-Incremental CIFAR-100 (20 tasks of 5 new classes each, 200 epochs per task). Networks are CNNs (3 conv + 3 FC layers for ImageNet; ResNet-18 for CIFAR-100) or MLPs for toy models, producing class label predictions. The key modification is at the label level during training rather than at the image or architecture level, so the input pipeline and output format remain standard supervised classification.

## Method

The paper develops a theoretical framework combining Finite-Time Lyapunov Exponent (FTLE) analysis with the Unconstrained Feature Model from Neural Collapse theory to characterize how training transforms representation-space geometry. This reveals two contrasting types of LoP: Type-1 arises from intra-class representation collapse (increasingly negative FTLEs, both train and test accuracy drop), while Type-2 arises from chaotic over-stretching near decision boundaries (increasingly positive FTLEs, train accuracy maintained but test accuracy degrades). To mitigate both simultaneously, the authors propose Generalized Mixup (G-Mixup), which modifies classical Mixup by introducing a confidence amplification factor M during intra-class interpolation—creating targets that encourage the network to maintain within-class variability—while retaining standard convex label interpolation for inter-class pairs to regularize boundary behavior. This label-space intervention requires no architectural changes, no additional parameters, and no explicit Jacobian computation, making it computationally lightweight relative to reset-based or regularization-based alternatives.

## Results

On the Continual ImageNet benchmark (5000 binary tasks), G-Mixup achieves 89.6% test accuracy at task 5000 with the standard ConvNet, outperforming CBP (87.8%), L2-init (80.7%), and LayerNorm (75.5%), while the no-intervention baseline collapses to chance level (50.0%). On the low-dimensional SmallConv variant, G-Mixup reaches 87.9% vs. CBP's 85.7% and L2-init's 78.8%. On Class-Incremental CIFAR-100 (20 tasks, ResNet-18), G-Mixup achieves 76.8% final accuracy, matching CBP (76.8%) and surpassing L2-init (72.3%) and LayerNorm (69.7%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

G-Mixup consistently outperforms all three baselines across both benchmarks and both network widths. On Continual ImageNet (ConvNet), G-Mixup leads CBP [7] by ~1.8 points at task 5000 (89.6% vs. 87.8%), L2-init [17] by ~8.9 points (89.6% vs. 80.7%), and LayerNorm [21] by ~14.1 points (89.6% vs. 75.5%). On the low-dimensional SmallConv variant, the margins are similar: G-Mixup (87.9%) beats CBP (85.7%) by 2.2 points and L2-init (78.8%) by 9.1 points. On Class-Incremental CIFAR-100 with full ResNet-18, G-Mixup (76.8%) ties CBP (76.8%) but clearly surpasses L2-init (72.3%) and LayerNorm (69.7%); with the 0.25× scaled ResNet-18, G-Mixup (69.7%) leads CBP (67.8%) by 1.9 points. Notably, G-Mixup also exceeds the 'Retrained' oracle (which reinitializes the network per task) on Continual ImageNet, indicating it not only prevents plasticity loss but actively enhances learning capacity beyond fresh initialization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate theoretical threads—Finite-Time Lyapunov Exponent (FTLE) analysis of deep networks (Storm et al., PRL 2024) and the Unconstrained Feature Model from Neural Collapse theory (Zhu et al., NeurIPS 2021)—into a single framework for dissecting loss of plasticity (LoP) in deep continual learning. It departs from prior LoP explanations (dormant neurons, feature-rank decay, weight-norm growth, gradient-curvature loss) by reframing the problem geometrically: training simultaneously compresses intra-class representation space and stretches inter-class boundary space, and prolonged continual learning accumulates these effects in opposite directions. On the method side, Generalized Mixup extends classical Mixup (Zhang et al., ICLR 2018) by adding an intra-class label-manipulation term, targeting both the collapse and the chaos that prior mitigation strategies (L2-init, LayerNorm, Continual Backpropagation) address only partially.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dual-type decomposition of LoP (intra-class collapse vs. inter-class chaotic stretching) provides a principled diagnostic that explains why different networks and task sequences exhibit qualitatively different failure modes, and it motivates a single lightweight intervention (G-mixup) that outperforms existing baselines across both low- and high-dimensional settings. However, the method is limited to supervised classification where label interpolation is well-defined, and the practical accuracy gains over the strongest baseline (CBP) are modest (1–3 percentage points). Open questions include whether the FTLE-based framework extends to reinforcement learning or regression, whether FTLE can be estimated efficiently enough for online regulation at scale, and whether the dual-type picture holds under non-binary or highly imbalanced task distributions.
