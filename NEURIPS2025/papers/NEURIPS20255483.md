## Problem

Prompt caching is widely deployed in LLM inference systems (vLLM, SGLang, Mooncake) to reduce prefill latency, yet the standard LRU eviction policy is oblivious to conversation-length heterogeneity and can perform arbitrarily poorly on tail latency. No prior work has characterized an optimal caching policy for tail-latency objectives in multi-turn LLM conversations or provided theoretical justification for LRU in this setting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255483/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The policy operates at KV cache block granularity (e.g., 128 tokens per block) and consumes per-conversation cache sizes, conversation history lengths, an estimated next-prompt length Q̂, a latency threshold ξ, and total cache capacity C. Upon each request arrival it outputs an updated per-conversation cache allocation vector. Practically, the entire modification reduces to marking TEL-safe blocks as 'infinitely old' in an existing LRU engine, requiring no changes to the eviction loop itself and remaining compatible with paged KV cache layouts.

## Method

T-LRU is a two-phase eviction policy: (1) TEL-safe trimming first evicts blocks from conversations whose cached size exceeds the per-conversation budget L + Q̂ − ξ, since caching beyond this threshold cannot reduce Tail Excess Latency; (2) if capacity is still exceeded, standard LRU evicts the least-recently-used blocks. The paper proves two optimality results: Theorem 1 shows that TEL-safe trimming combined with Belady's furthest-in-future rule is hindsight-optimal for TEL, and Theorem 2 proves that a generalized T-LRU (Expected-Tail-Optimized LRU) is online-optimal under a novel stochastic conversation model built on a continuous-time birth-death process with exponential death clocks. This model captures the empirical temporal locality that longer-silent conversations are less likely to return, making LRU ordering align with return probability and yielding the first proof of LRU optimality under a natural conversation-driven arrival model, with classical LRU (ξ=0) and T-LRU as special cases.

## Results

On WildChat with Vicuna-7B on a single A100 GPU, T-LRU reduces P90 tail TTFT by up to 27.5% and P95 tail TTFT by up to 23.9% compared to LRU (at cache capacity 10,000, ξs=150ms and ξs=200ms respectively). For SLO violations (TTFT > 200ms), T-LRU achieves up to 40.7% reduction versus LRU and 38.9% versus Threshold-LRU at the largest cache capacity. The optimal ξs aligns with the target tail percentile: ξs=200ms maximizes P90 improvement while ξs=300ms maximizes P95 improvement.

## Baselines & Comparisons

T-LRU consistently outperforms LRU across all cache capacities and latency thresholds for tail metrics (P90, P95), with the largest gains (27.5% P90, 23.9% P95) appearing at the largest cache capacity (10,000 blocks) where LRU's oblivious eviction of long-conversation blocks is most harmful. Against Threshold-LRU (the industry practice of caching only conversations exceeding a fixed length, e.g., 1024 tokens as used by OpenAI), T-LRU achieves comparable or slightly better improvements, confirming that its fine-grained, adaptive caching decisions dominate the binary all-or-nothing strategy. For SLO violations at 200ms, T-LRU reduces violations by 8.8%–40.7% over LRU and 6.6%–38.9% over Threshold-LRU, with the largest gains at high cache capacity. Notably, T-LRU's median latency increases only modestly (~10ms to ~40ms), a deliberate trade-off that sacrifices negligible median performance to eliminate hundreds of milliseconds at the tail.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends classical caching theory (Belady's offline-optimal algorithm and LRU) to the specific problem of KV-cache eviction in multi-turn LLM inference, introducing a continuous tail-latency objective (TEL) in place of the traditional binary hit/miss metric. It departs from prior LLM serving systems (vLLM, SGLang, Mooncake) that adopt LRU without theoretical justification, and from prior tail-latency caching work (RobinHood, SNC-Meister) that targets web services rather than the growing-context, multi-turn structure of LLM conversations. The key conceptual departure is modeling conversations as a birth-death process with exponential death clocks, which naturally encodes the temporal locality observed in real traces and yields a setting where LRU becomes provably optimal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The first proof of LRU optimality under a natural conversation-driven arrival model provides long-sought theoretical grounding for the de facto standard in LLM inference systems, and the T-LRU modification is a low-friction upgrade (marking blocks as 'infinitely old') that practitioners can deploy immediately. Open questions include extending to multi-tier KV caches (GPU→CPU→SSD), joint optimization with load balancing across distributed servers, and relaxing the single-server, no-cross-conversation-reuse assumptions. The stochastic model's exponential death-time assumption is a simplification of real user behavior, and the optimality guarantee does not transfer to arbitrary arrival distributions, limiting the generality of the theoretical result.
