## Problem

Learning-based 3D reconstruction for eXtended Light Field Microscopy (XLFM) remains underdeveloped due to the absence of standardized benchmarks, the highly entangled multi-view angular structure that defeats conventional CNNs, and the computational cost of generating volumetric ground truth via deconvolution. Existing approaches either ignore the wave-optical forward model (producing physically inconsistent reconstructions) or rely on sparsity-driven pipelines that discard full biological morphology. No public dataset or reproducible evaluation protocol existed for systematic comparison.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single-exposure raw XLFM light field image as input, where 27 sub-aperture channels (encoding distinct angular views from the microlens array) are concatenated along the channel axis into an H×W×C tensor; the sub-aperture index is treated as channel information rather than a spatial dimension, and 2D positional embeddings are applied only along (H, W). The output is a full 3D volumetric reconstruction of the biological sample (e.g., zebrafish neural structures), normalized to the physical fluorescence intensity range [0, 2000] rather than standard [0, 255]. Unlike sparsity-driven prior methods, the reconstruction captures complete volumetric morphology, not just sparse neural signals.

## Method

XLFM-Former uses a hierarchical Swin Transformer encoder (four stages with windowed self-attention and patch merging) paired with a CNN decoder that performs progressive deconvolutional upsampling with cross-level skip-connection fusion to recover the 3D volume. Two key innovations differentiate the approach: (1) MVM-LF pretraining, a self-supervised task that randomly masks 70% of angular views (not pixels) and trains the encoder to reconstruct them from unmasked neighbors, internalizing inter-view occlusion and continuity priors specific to XLFM geometry; and (2) the Optical Rendering Consistency (ORC) Loss, which convolves both the predicted and ground-truth volumes with the known system PSF and penalizes the MSE between the two forward-projected light fields, enforcing wave-optical consistency without being distracted by raw sensor noise. The full training pipeline first self-supervises on 20,123 free-swimming zebrafish images (no volumetric labels needed), then fine-tunes on 1,761 fixed zebrafish images with a composite loss combining MS-SSIM, edge-aware, PSNR, MSE, and ORC terms.

## Results

On the XLFM-Zebrafish benchmark (6 held-out fixed zebrafish samples), XLFM-Former achieves an average PSNR of 54.04 dB and SSIM of 0.9944, outperforming the strongest baseline (ConvNeXt, 50.16 dB / 0.9876) by 3.88 dB in PSNR (~7.7% relative improvement). Per-sample PSNR ranges from 49.31 dB (sample #3) to 59.83 dB (sample #2), with SSIM consistently above 0.9910 across all six test subjects. On the cross-domain H2B-Nemos dataset, XLFM-Former further achieves 52.34 dB PSNR in the supervised setting and 53.72 dB zero-shot, surpassing the ResNet-101 baseline by +0.92 dB and +2.29 dB respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

XLFM-Former outperforms all seven baselines in Table 1 on every individual test sample and in aggregate. The closest competitor is ConvNeXt (50.16 dB avg PSNR), which XLFM-Former surpasses by 3.88 dB; U-Net (49.43 dB) and ViT (49.28 dB) trail by 4.61 and 4.76 dB respectively. The gap widens substantially against PVT (47.34 dB, +6.70 dB), ResNet-101 (46.91 dB, +7.13 dB), ResNet-50 (46.85 dB, +7.19 dB), and EfficientNet (44.53 dB, +9.51 dB), with EfficientNet also showing severe instability on sample #6 (PSNR drops to 27.16 dB, SSIM 0.7264). In the pretraining comparison (Table 2), pixel-level MAE pretraining on a ViT backbone yields only 46.55 dB, far below MVM-LF's 54.04 dB, confirming that view-aware masking is critical. On the cross-domain H2B-Nemos dataset, XLFM-Former beats ResNet-101 by +0.92 dB (supervised) and +2.29 dB (zero-shot), while all other tested architectures (EfficientNet, ViT-tiny, PVT-tiny, ConvNeXt-tiny, U-Net, ResNet-50) fall below the ResNet-101 baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the Swin Transformer backbone for 3D volumetric reconstruction from XLFM light fields, departing from prior XLFM reconstruction methods (XLFMNet, conditional normalizing flows, Fourier-Nets) that either target only sparse neural signals or incur prohibitive memory costs in the Fourier domain. The MVM-LF pretraining strategy adapts the masked autoencoder paradigm from pixel-level to view-level masking, explicitly modeling angular dependencies unique to light field sampling rather than treating sub-aperture channels as independent pixels. The ORC Loss introduces a differentiable PSF-based forward rendering constraint that has no direct analogue in prior XLFM reconstruction work, bridging data-driven learning with wave-optical consistency in a way that prior methods (which rely purely on pixel-level or sparsity-driven supervision) do not.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The XLFM-Zebrafish benchmark (22,581 images across free-swimming and fixed conditions) addresses a critical gap: the field previously lacked any standardized dataset or reproducible evaluation protocol, making cross-method comparisons anecdotal. The ORC Loss concept—enforcing that reconstructed volumes render back to match observed measurements through the known PSF—is a generalizable principle applicable to any computational imaging modality with a known forward model. Key limitations include evaluation restricted to zebrafish (no mouse or drosophila), no direct functional trace extraction, and the large PSNR gap versus Richardson-Lucy deconvolution (52 dB vs. 72 dB) that trades absolute fidelity for 700× speed and 8× lower memory. Open questions include whether the physics-informed + angular-aware pretraining paradigm transfers to other light field modalities (MLA-based, confocal LFM) and whether partial-view inference can be formalized as a self-supervised evaluation signal.
