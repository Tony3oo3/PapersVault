## Problem

Reinforcement fine-tuning (RFT) has shown strong results for LLM reasoning but faces two fundamental obstacles when applied to embodied agents: (i) sparse, delayed rewards in long-horizon multi-step tasks provide insufficient learning signal for credit assignment, and (ii) hand-crafted, environment-specific reward functions do not generalize to novel tasks or real-world settings. No prior framework has combined RFT with self-evolving capabilities for multi-modal embodied agents operating in partially observable, interactive environments.

## Input / Output

The agent receives a high-level task instruction (e.g., 'put two CDs in the safe'), per-step environment observations (either textual descriptions like 'The drawer 1 is closed' or raw RGB images from a front-view camera), and the full interaction history as a POMDP state. It outputs a natural-language action in ReAct format ('Thought: ...\nAction: open drawer 1'), where the Thought field carries the reasoning trace and the Action field is the executable command. At inference, the agent uses lightweight single-path ReAct-style sampling without MCTS overhead, consuming 38.8% fewer tokens than the base model while achieving 3.1× higher success rate.

## Method

SEEA-R1 integrates two components in an iterative self-evolution loop. First, Tree-GRPO augments Group Relative Policy Optimization with Monte Carlo Tree Search: MCTS explores alternative trajectories from each state, estimating Q-values that convert sparse terminal rewards into dense, step-wise process rewards; GRPO is then applied at each tree node over a group of candidate actions, using the Q-value-derived advantage for credit assignment. Second, the Multi-modal Generative Reward Model (MGRM) is an MLLM-based reward model that takes the full interaction history and predicts one of three categorical outcomes (success, continue, failure), trained via GRPO in either a supervised paradigm (calibrated against ground-truth simulator rewards) or a self-supervised paradigm (using TTRL majority-vote pseudo-labels from diverse MCTS rollouts). The two models co-evolve: the policy generates tree-structured experience data via MCTS (Data Evolution), and both the policy and reward model are updated from that data (Model Evolution), with the refined models driving the next iteration—enabling human-free, reward-driven self-improvement without handcrafted reward functions.

## Results

On ALFWorld unseen (multi-modal), SEEA-R1 achieves 46.27% success rate with GT reward and 44.03% without GT reward, substantially outperforming GPT-4o (24%) and all open-source baselines. In the text-only setting, SEEA-R1 reaches 85.07% (w/ GT) and 80.30% (w/o GT), surpassing GPT-4o (81.1%) and the best Llama-3.1-8B variant (82.1%). On the out-of-distribution EmbodiedEval benchmark, SEEA-R1 achieves 19.88% overall success, outperforming the base Qwen2.5-VL-7B (18.29%) and SFT/DPO fine-tuned variants (16.77%), though trailing GPT-4o (25.00%). In real-world robot experiments, SEEA-R1 achieves 68.1% overall success versus 33.3% for the Qwen2.5-VL-7B baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SEEA-R1 decisively outperforms GPT-4o on ALFWorld multi-modal (46% vs. 24% avg) and text-only (85.2% vs. 81.1% avg), and also beats GPT-4o on real-world robot tasks (68.1% vs. 33.3% for Qwen2.5-VL baseline). Among open-source MLLMs, SEEA-R1 surpasses Qwen2.5-VL (46% vs. 11%), RL4VLM (46% vs. 21%), InstructBLIP (46% vs. 22%), and all SFT-only models (Florence-2, Idefics-2, MiniGPT-4) by 20–45 points on ALFWorld multi-modal. On the text-only ALFWorld setting, SEEA-R1 (85.2%) edges out the strongest Llama-3.1-8B baselines: ETO+MPO (82.1%), SFT+MPO (81.0%), and KnowAgent (77.5%). However, on the out-of-distribution EmbodiedEval benchmark, SEEA-R1 (19.88%) trails GPT-4o (25.00%) and Qwen-VL-Max (21.04%), and on general multimodal benchmarks (MMBench 81.4, MMStar 60.2, MMMU 48.5) it is slightly below or comparable to the Qwen2.5-VL-7B base model, indicating that the embodied-specific training does not degrade but also does not broadly enhance general multimodal reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

SEEA-R1 sits at the intersection of two research threads: (1) the R1-style reinforcement fine-tuning paradigm (DeepSeek-R1, GRPO, DAPO) that has proven effective for LLM reasoning, and (2) embodied agent planning in interactive environments (ALFWorld, ReAct-style agents). The key departure is the integration of MCTS into GRPO (Tree-GRPO) to convert sparse outcome rewards into dense step-wise process signals, and the replacement of handcrafted simulator rewards with a learned multi-modal generative reward model (MGRM) trained via TTRL. Prior embodied RL work (robotxR1, Embodied-R) relied on textual descriptions or task-specific priors, while prior visual RFT (Vision-R1, Visual-RFT) targeted grounding rather than long-horizon planning; SEEA-R1 is the first to unify multimodal perception, tree-structured search, and self-supervised reward learning in a single closed-loop training framework for embodied agents.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses two practical bottlenecks in embodied AI: the credit-assignment problem in long-horizon tasks and the brittleness of handcrafted reward functions, both of which have limited the transfer of RFT from symbolic domains to physical interaction. The self-supervised setting (no ground-truth rewards) achieving within ~5% of the supervised setting suggests a path toward deploying self-improving agents in environments where labeled rewards are unavailable. However, the evaluation is concentrated on ALFWorld (a relatively constrained household simulator) with a single 7B base model, and the real-world validation, while encouraging, is small-scale (6 rooms, 72 tasks). Open questions include whether the self-evolution loop scales to more dynamic, open-world environments, whether the MGRM generalizes beyond ALFWorld's action vocabulary, and whether the approach transfers to continuous-control robotics beyond discrete action spaces.
