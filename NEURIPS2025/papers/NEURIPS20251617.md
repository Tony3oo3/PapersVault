## Problem

Existing graph domain adaptation methods assume a balanced label distribution in the source domain, yet real-world graphs exhibit severe long-tailed class imbalance that biases knowledge extraction toward head classes and degrades tail-class performance on the target graph. The paper asks how to effectively transfer class-imbalanced label semantics from a source graph to an unlabeled target graph under both source-domain imbalance and cross-domain distribution shift.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251617/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a fully labeled source graph with class-imbalanced node labels (imbalance factor ρ = N₁/N_C up to 100) and an unlabeled target graph, both represented as node-attribute graphs with adjacency matrices; attribute dimensions are unified across domains when they differ. It produces predicted node class labels for the target domain, evaluated via micro-F1 and macro-F1. Internally, the method distills a per-class anchor set (top-k nodes by degree) from the source graph for prototype computation and infers non-parametric pseudo-labels for target nodes via similarity to source anchor embeddings.

## Method

ImGDA employs a dual-branch graph encoder that captures local structure via GCN and global structure via a PPMI-based random-walk adjacency, fused through an attention mechanism. Class-specific prototypes are computed as mean embeddings over a degree-ranked anchor set per class and per branch. A cross-branch prototype contrastive loss aligns local and global prototypes to rebalance the feature space (Theorem 4.2 proves head classes no longer dominate the repulsion term), while a cross-domain prototype contrastive loss infers target pseudo-labels non-parametrically and aligns semantically matched cross-domain pairs, interpretable as an EM scheme. An adaptive per-class temperature τ_c = γ + (1−γ)·N_c/N_C amplifies gradients for tail classes, and a logit compensation term (δ_c = log N_c) further corrects the supervised loss, yielding the total objective L = L_cb + L_cd + L_lc.

## Results

On the ArnetMiner datasets (ACMv9, Citationv1, DBLPv7) with imbalance factor ρ=50, ImGDA achieves the best Micro-F1 and Macro-F1 across all six domain pairs. For example, on A⇒C at ρ=50, ImGDA reaches 73.53% Micro-F1 and 67.42% Macro-F1, outperforming the strongest baseline GraphSHA (64.97% / 53.99%) by 8.6 and 13.4 percentage points respectively. At the milder ρ=10 setting, ImGDA attains 77.93% Micro-F1 on A⇒C, leading the runner-up GraphSHA (73.20%) by 4.7 points. The advantage grows with increasing imbalance, with ImGDA maintaining stable performance even at ρ=100 while domain-adaptation-specific baselines (GDA-SpecReg, A2GNN) degrade sharply.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ImGDA consistently outperforms all eight baselines across every domain pair and imbalance factor. Against the strongest imbalance-handling baselines, ImGDA leads GraphSHA by 8.6% Micro-F1 (73.53 vs. 64.97) and 13.4% Macro-F1 (67.42 vs. 53.99) on A⇒C at ρ=50, and by 12.5% Micro-F1 (70.57 vs. 58.02) on C⇒D. Domain-adaptation-specific methods (GDA-SpecReg, A2GNN) suffer the most under severe imbalance, with A2GNN dropping to 17.61% Macro-F1 on A⇒C at ρ=50—nearly 50 points below ImGDA. Plain GNNs (GCN, GAT, GIN) perform comparably to each other but fall 10–25 points behind ImGDA in Macro-F1, confirming that neither standard GNNs nor existing domain adaptation or imbalance strategies alone suffice. The gap between ImGDA and the best baseline widens as ρ increases from 10 to 50, indicating that the prototype-enhanced contrastive framework is particularly effective under severe class imbalance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper synthesizes three established threads: the dual-branch local/global graph encoding of Zhuang & Ma (WWW 2018), the coupled contrastive domain-adaptation framework with EM interpretation from Yin et al. (ICML 2023), and the supervised contrastive loss formulation of Khosla et al. (NeurIPS 2020). Its key departure is targeting the intersection of class-imbalanced source graphs and cross-domain shift—a combination that prior graph DA methods (GDA-SpecReg, A2GNN) ignore by assuming balanced labels, and that prior imbalanced node-classification methods (GraphENS, TAM, GraphSHA) ignore by assuming no domain shift. The theoretical contribution (Theorems 4.1–4.2) extends SCL bounds to show that class-averaged prototypes neutralize head-class dominance in the repulsion term, providing justification for the anchor-based prototype design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap: real-world graphs (e.g., NCI compounds, fraud detection) are long-tailed, yet virtually all graph DA methods assume balanced source labels. The ablation finding that cross-branch prototype contrast (imbalance mitigation) contributes more to performance than cross-domain contrast (domain alignment) is a useful diagnostic for practitioners prioritizing which error source to address first. Limitations include evaluation restricted to ArnetMiner citation networks, a single GCN backbone, and the O(N²) PPMI matrix that limits scalability to very large graphs. Open questions include whether the dual-branch design is necessary versus a single well-tuned encoder, and whether the approach transfers to heterogeneous or temporal graphs.
