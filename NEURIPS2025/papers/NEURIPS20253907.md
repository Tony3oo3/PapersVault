## Problem

Existing medical vision-language models are trained predominantly on 2D imaging (X-ray, CT, MRI) and English-language data, leaving functional PET/CT imaging and low-resource languages like Vietnamese severely underrepresented. Preliminary experiments show that state-of-the-art medical VLMs (LLaVA-Med, M3D, RadFM) produce near-zero BLEU-4 scores on Vietnamese PET/CT report generation, indicating a fundamental gap in both modality coverage and linguistic diversity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253907/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes 3D PET/CT volumes in DICOM format (~250–500 paired CT and PET slices per study, covering head to upper thighs), which are partitioned into three anatomical regions (head-neck, chest, abdomen-pelvis) with a 20-slice overlap to increase sample count and improve region-specific alignment. For report generation, the output is a full-length Vietnamese clinical report containing patient metadata, clinical status, medical history, scanning parameters, and structured physician observations per anatomical region. For VQA, the input additionally includes a text question, and the output is a natural-language answer grounded in the image content.

## Method

The paper introduces ViMed-PET, a dataset of 2,757 paired whole-body PET/CT volumes (1,567,062 slices) with full-length Vietnamese clinical reports, augmented via GPT-4o into four task-specific subsets (VQA conversations, paraphrased reports, study-comparison pairs, and an expert-validated lung cancer test set). The fine-tuning pipeline has three stages: (1) adapting a 3D vision encoder (CT-ViT via CLIP-style contrastive learning with PhoBERT, or a modified Cosmos Tokenizer via L1+SSIM reconstruction loss with causal attention removed), (2) aligning visual and textual embedding spaces through a trainable linear projection layer while freezing both encoder and LLM, and (3) instruction-tuning the full VLM with LoRA (rank 64, α=16) on single- and multi-turn conversational data. Four VLMs are constructed by pairing each vision encoder with Mistral-7B or LLaMA-2-7B, and evaluation combines standard NLP metrics (BLEU-4, ROUGE, BERTScore) with novel clinical F1-scores (F1-T, F1-TP, F1-TF, F1-TPF) that measure accuracy on lesion type, position, and FDG uptake extracted via GPT-4o and verified by physicians.

## Results

On the PET/CT report generation task, the best fine-tuned model (CT-ViT + Mistral-7B, O-G setting) achieves BLEU-4 of 58.07, ROUGE-1 of 80.11, ROUGE-L of 72.74, and BERTScore of 89.98, substantially outperforming all baselines. On the clinical F1 metrics, it reaches F1-T of 51.11, F1-TP of 30.66, F1-TF of 37.02, and F1-TPF of 22.65, more than doubling GPT-4o's clinical scores. On the VQA task (O-G-C setting), CT-ViT + Mistral-7B achieves BLEU-4 of 31.14 and BERTScore of 82.50, outperforming GPT-4o (BLEU-4: 3.01, BERT: 71.92) by 10.3× and 1.1× respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

All four baselines are decisively outperformed by the fine-tuned ViMed-PET models. LLaVA-Med, M3D, and RadFM produce near-zero BLEU-4 scores (0.01–0.06) and low clinical F1 scores, confirming their inability to handle Vietnamese PET/CT report generation. GPT-4o under few-shot prompting is the strongest baseline (BLEU-4: 31.12, F1-TPF: 7.87), but the best fine-tuned model (CT-ViT + Mistral-7B, O-G) surpasses it by 87% on BLEU-4 (58.07 vs. 31.12), 18% on ROUGE-1, 40% on ROUGE-L, and 12% on BERTScore, while nearly tripling the F1-TPF clinical metric (22.65 vs. 7.87). Among the fine-tuned configurations, CT-ViT consistently outperforms Cosmos Tokenizer on clinical F1 metrics across all settings, and Mistral-7B edges out LLaMA-2-7B when augmented data (O-G, O-G-C) is available, though LLaMA-2-7B leads on the original-only setting due to its 3D medical pretraining.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the medical VLM fine-tuning pipeline established by LLaVA-Med and M3D (vision encoder + linear projector + LLM with LoRA) to two underexplored axes: functional PET/CT imaging and the Vietnamese language. Prior medical VLMs (MedCLIP, M3D, RadFM) are trained predominantly on CT, X-ray, or histopathology in English, leaving PET/CT and low-resource languages unaddressed. The data augmentation strategy (GPT-4o-based paraphrasing and cross-study comparison) and the structured clinical F1 metrics (F1-T, F1-TP, F1-TF, F1-TPF) are novel contributions that go beyond the standard NLP evaluation used in prior medical report generation work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ViMed-PET fills a concrete gap: no prior dataset paired whole-body PET/CT volumes with full clinical reports in Vietnamese, and PET/CT was largely absent from medical VLM training corpora. However, clinical F1 scores plateau around 50% even after fine-tuning, revealing that current VLMs still struggle with the nuanced diagnostic reasoning (lesion type, position, FDG uptake) that radiologists perform. The benchmark is also narrow—clinical evaluation covers only lung cancer from a single Vietnamese hospital—so generalizability to other pathologies, imaging centers, or languages remains an open question. The work is a practical stepping stone for multilingual, multi-modality medical VLMs but does not yet demonstrate clinical readiness.
