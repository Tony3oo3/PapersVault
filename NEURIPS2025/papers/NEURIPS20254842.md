## Problem

Classical seriation recovers a latent item ordering from a full batch of noisy pairwise similarity measurements, but the active setting—where the algorithm adaptively selects which pairs to query—had not been previously analyzed. This paper introduces the first theoretical treatment of active seriation, establishing sharp information-theoretic lower bounds and a matching algorithmic upper bound that identify a phase transition in the signal-to-noise ratio governing whether exact ordering recovery is possible.

## Input / Output

The algorithm receives a fixed sampling budget T and, optionally, a correct relative ordering of a subset of items (interpolating between online seriation with one pre-ordered item and seriation from scratch with none). It adaptively selects pairs to query, receiving σ-sub-Gaussian noisy observations of the underlying Robinson matrix entries, and requires no knowledge of σ, the minimal gap Δ, or the matrix M itself. In the uniform-separation regime it outputs a full permutation of all n items; in the general regime it outputs a Δ-maximal subset S together with a correct ordering of S, discarding items that are too similar to distinguish.

## Method

ASII (Active Seriation by Iterative Insertion) reconstructs the ordering by sequentially inserting each new item k into the current list of k−1 items via a binary search over candidate positions. Each comparison step uses a TEST subroutine that exploits the Robinson matrix property: given two reference items (l, r), it compares the three empirical similarities M̂_kl, M̂_kr, and M̂_lr to determine whether k lies to the left, between, or to the right of (l, r). Because each TEST is run with a small budget of order O(T/(ñ log k))—yielding only constant success probability per call—the algorithm incorporates a backtracking mechanism that detects inconsistencies in the search path and reverts to earlier intervals, preventing local errors from propagating irreversibly. This design achieves minimax-optimal sample complexity T* ≈ σ²n ln n / Δ² for seriation from scratch, with error probability decaying exponentially in the SNR Δ²T/(σ²n), and no algorithm can achieve a faster decay rate.

## Results

The main contribution is theoretical: ASII achieves minimax-optimal exact recovery with error probability bounded by exp(−Δ²T/(800σ²n)) once the SNR Δ²T/(σ²n) exceeds c₀·ln n, and no algorithm can beat the matching lower bound exp(−8Δ²T/(σ²n)). The critical sample complexity for recovery with probability ≥ 1−1/n² is T* ≍ σ²n ln n / Δ², which is substantially smaller than the O(n²) observations required by batch seriation. Empirically (n=10, T=10,000, 100 Monte Carlo runs), ASII consistently outperforms a naive insertion variant without backtracking across all four scenarios, and on non-Toeplitz Robinson matrices it remains stable while both batch baselines exhibit high or complete failure at several Δ values.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares ASII against two batch seriation baselines—Spectral Seriation (Atkins et al., 1998) and Adaptive Sampling (Cai & Ma, 2023)—as well as a naive active insertion variant lacking the backtracking mechanism. On the Toeplitz scenario (1), ASII underperforms both batch methods, which are known to exploit Toeplitz regularity effectively; the authors attribute this to ASII's budget being spread across multiple binary-search iterations. On the three non-Toeplitz scenarios (2–4), ASII maintains low and stable error probability across the full range of Δ, whereas both batch methods show erratic behavior and can fail entirely (error near 1) at intermediate Δ values. Against the naive insertion baseline, ASII's backtracking mechanism yields a consistent and meaningful reduction in error probability in every scenario, confirming the theoretical prediction that backtracking provides a logarithmic improvement. On the real single-cell RNA-seq dataset (human primordial germ cells), ASII produces a coherent developmental trajectory reordering, though no quantitative comparison with the batch baselines is reported for this dataset.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper introduces the first theoretical analysis of active seriation, extending the classical batch seriation problem (Atkins et al. 1998; Fogel et al. 2013; Giraud et al. 2023; Cai and Ma 2023; Issartel et al. 2024) to an adaptive querying framework where the algorithm sequentially selects which pairwise similarities to observe. The algorithmic core—iterative insertion with backtracking—borrows the noisy binary search principle from Feige et al. (1994), Karp and Kleinberg (2007), and the thresholding bandit literature (Cheshire et al. 2020, 2021), repurposing it for the combinatorial constraint that all pairwise similarities must be globally consistent with a single latent ordering. The analysis follows the Lipschitz-type assumption line of Giraud et al. (2023) and Issartel et al. (2024), with the minimal gap Δ playing the role of a lower Lipschitz constant.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key contribution is showing that active sampling eliminates the statistical-computational gap known to exist in batch seriation: a simple polynomial-time algorithm (ASII) achieves minimax-optimal error rates, whereas in the batch setting the best known polynomial-time algorithms fall short of the information-theoretic optimum under certain regimes. The sharp phase transition at SNR ≈ ln n and the sample complexity T ≫ n² (versus n² for batch) provide a clean characterization of when adaptive querying is beneficial. Open questions include extending to a fixed-confidence (adaptive stopping) formulation, exploiting global ordering structure when uniform separation fails, and understanding whether the active advantage persists under more general matrix models beyond Robinson structure.
