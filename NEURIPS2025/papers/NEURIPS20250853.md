## Problem

Achieving near-optimal O(H) deployment complexity for linear MDPs has previously required strong reachability or explorability assumptions, causing sample complexity to depend polynomially on 1/v_min — a parameter that can be arbitrarily small, rendering guarantees vacuous. This paper resolves an open problem by showing that such assumptions are in fact unnecessary, designing an algorithm with H deployments and polynomial sample complexity for any linear MDP.

## Input / Output

During the exploration phase, the algorithm interacts with a linear MDP (horizon H, feature dimension d, known feature vectors φ_h(s,a) with ‖φ‖₂ ≤ 1) without any reward signal, collecting exactly L trajectories per deployment where L is fixed a priori and polynomial in d, H, 1/ε, and log(1/δ). In the planning phase, given any linear reward kernel {θ_h} satisfying ‖θ_h‖₂ ≤ √d, the algorithm outputs a single ε-optimal policy. The total number of policy deployments is exactly H (one per layer), and the sample complexity Õ(d¹⁵H¹⁵/ε⁵) is independent of any reachability or explorability coefficient.

## Method

The algorithm uses a layer-by-layer approach where each layer h deploys a single exploration policy designed via iterative offline planning with quadratic rewards r(s,a) = min{φ_h^T Λ^{-1} φ_h, 1}. The core technical contribution is a data-dependent truncation mechanism: state-action pairs whose features are 'infrequent' (i.e., φ^T Λ^{-1} φ is large) are redirected to a dummy state in a truncated MDP, preventing exponential error accumulation. To evaluate the truncated information matrix offline, the paper introduces an iterative fixed-point algorithm (Matrix-Eval) that repeatedly applies the truncation operator T(φφ^T, c₁Λ) until convergence, ensuring the estimation error is multiplicative with respect to the information matrix rather than additive. Independent copies of sub-datasets are used to decouple the statistical dependencies across iterations, and a potential function argument based on the determinant of the information matrix bounds the total number of iterations needed.

## Results

The paper's main theoretical guarantee (Theorem 4) establishes that Algorithm 1 achieves deployment complexity H (matching the known lower bound) and sample complexity Õ(d¹⁵H¹⁵/ε⁵) for reward-free exploration in linear MDPs, with no dependency on the reachability or explorability coefficients. This resolves an open problem by showing that near-optimal deployment complexity is achievable without the reachability assumption (v_min) that prior O(H)-deployment algorithms required. The sample complexity is polynomial in all problem parameters (d, H, 1/ε, log(1/δ)) for any linear MDP, though the exponents (15 on d and H, 5 on 1/ε) are far from optimal compared to reachability-dependent methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to Huang et al. [2022], this paper matches the optimal H deployment complexity but eliminates the critical 1/v_min dependency in sample complexity. Huang et al.'s sample complexity degrades to infinity as the reachability coefficient v_min → 0, making their algorithm inapplicable to MDPs with arbitrarily small reachability; this work's guarantee holds uniformly over all linear MDPs. Compared to Zhao et al. [2023], this paper achieves strictly better deployment complexity (H vs. Õ(dH)), which is the paper's primary contribution, but at the cost of significantly worse sample complexity (d¹⁵H¹⁵/ε⁵ vs. d²H³/ε²). The authors explicitly acknowledge that the current exponents are far from optimal and attribute the gap to the additional technical difficulty of handling ill-conditioned directions without reachability assumptions. In summary, this paper wins on deployment efficiency (the central metric) and assumption-freeness, while losing on sample complexity exponents relative to both baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the layer-by-layer deployment-efficient exploration framework of Huang et al. (2022) and Qiao and Wang (2022) to the linear MDP setting without their critical reachability or explorability assumptions. It adapts the absorbing-MDP truncation idea from Qiao et al. (2022) in the tabular setting to a data-dependent truncation of state-action pairs in the linear setting, and replaces the G-optimal experiment design approach (which requires full feature-space coverage) with an iterative offline information-matrix estimation procedure. The paper also operates in the reward-free exploration setting, building on the reward-free RL literature of Jin et al. (2020) and Wang et al. (2020a).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper resolves an explicit open problem and disproves a conjecture by Huang et al. (2022) that O(H) deployment complexity in linear MDPs would necessarily require reachability or explorability assumptions. This is conceptually important because it shows the deployment complexity lower bound of Ω̃(H) is tight without structural assumptions, completing the picture for linear MDPs. However, the sample complexity of Õ(d¹⁵H¹⁵/ε⁵) is far from the Õ(d²H³/ε²) achievable under explorability, and the authors acknowledge the gap stems from the difficulty of handling ill-conditioned directions offline. Future work could tighten these bounds or extend the truncation techniques to function classes with bounded eluder dimension.
