## Problem

Conditional diffusion models achieve strong empirical results in disentanglement tasks like voice conversion and image editing, but a principled understanding of when they learn identifiably disentangled representations is missing. The core difficulty is that DMs generate samples by integrating a stochastic differential equation, making the latent-to-observation mapping stochastic and non-invertible, which precludes the change-of-variables calculus and ICA-style identifiability arguments that underpin classical disentanglement theory.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an observed sample X (e.g., a noisy or color-shifted image, or a speech recording) along with either a known/frozen style encoding g(X) (such as a speaker embedding from ECAPA-TDNN or a background color) or a second paired view X² sharing the same content. The score network operates on progressively noised versions of X at various diffusion timesteps t ∈ [0, T], with the style encoder evaluated at a specific noise level t₀ = -log(1-δ²)^{1/2}. Outputs include disentangled content and style encodings, and the model can generate new samples by recombining content with a different style (e.g., swapping speaker identity while preserving emotion, or changing background color while preserving digit shape).

## Method

The paper introduces two complementary notions of approximate disentanglement—(ε,ν)-disentanglement via mutual information and ε-editability via conditional generation—and proves that DMs trained with a regularized conditional score matching objective achieve both as the noise level δ→0 under sub-Gaussian and Lipschitz-score assumptions. For the independent subspace model (ISM) special case, the authors propose a dual-encoder score network where one branch computes the content score from the full noisy sample and the other computes the style score from the projected noisy style, combined linearly to form the total score. A key architectural innovation is the style guidance regularizer, which forces the score network to explain part of the data using only the style branch, implicitly discouraging redundant style encoding in the content branch. This regularized objective is shown to admit global convergence under gradient descent in the finite-sample regime, with recovery rates depending on the minimum singular values of the content and style score functions.

## Results

On CelebA denoising, the proposed DDPM with style guidance (λ_r=3) achieves the best LPIPS of 0.33 and SSIM of 0.24, substantially outperforming cond. InfoGAN (LPIPS 0.83, SSIM 0.16) and cond. β-VAE (LPIPS 0.57, SSIM 0.17). On CelebA face editing (gender as style), DDPM with λ_r=0.03 achieves the best content-preservation LPIPS of 0.30 versus 0.39 for cond. InfoGAN and 0.37 for cond. β-VAE, while CLIP scores for style transfer are comparable (0.25–0.26) across all methods. On IEMOCAP speech emotion recognition, the DM-based voice conversion approach outperforms no-conversion, pitch-shifting, and recent VCA baselines, with accuracy improving as more target speakers are used during training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against cond. InfoGAN [69], the proposed DDPM with style guidance wins decisively on denoising (LPIPS 0.33 vs. 0.83; SSIM 0.24 vs. 0.16) and on face-editing content preservation (LPIPS 0.30 vs. 0.39), while matching or slightly exceeding its CLIP score for style transfer (0.26 vs. 0.24). Against cond. β-VAE [70], the DDPM also wins on denoising (LPIPS 0.33 vs. 0.57; SSIM 0.24 vs. 0.17) and face-editing LPIPS (0.30 vs. 0.37), with a marginal CLIP-score edge (0.26 vs. 0.25). Against recent voice-conversion baselines Baas et al. [65] and Triaan-VC [77] on IEMOCAP emotion recognition, the DM-based approach achieves higher cross-speaker classification accuracy, and performance further improves with additional target speakers, consistent with the multi-view disentanglement theory. The ablation over λ_r shows that moderate style guidance (λ_r=0.03–0.3) is optimal for face editing, while stronger guidance (λ_r=3) is best for denoising, confirming the theory's prediction that the regularizer weight should be tuned to the task.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the classical disentanglement literature (rooted in ICA, nonlinear ICA, and weakly-supervised methods like those of Locatello et al. and Kugelgen et al.) to the diffusion model setting, where the generative process is a stochastic, non-invertible SDE rather than a deterministic invertible mapping. It builds on the DDPM framework (Ho et al., 2020) and the finite-sample score-matching analysis of Chen et al. (ICML 2023) for the independent subspace model case, and adopts the diffusion-based voice conversion architecture of Popov et al. (ICLR 2022) for speech experiments. The key conceptual departure is introducing two new notions—(ε,ν)-disentanglement and ε-editability—that are well-defined in the presence of stochastic noise and non-invertibility, where classical block identifiability breaks down.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first learning-theoretic framework establishing when and how diffusion models can provably disentangle latent factors, filling a gap that has limited principled understanding of the widespread empirical success of conditional DMs in editing and conversion tasks. The finding that MI-based disentanglement does not imply editability (Example 3.1) is a useful caution for practitioners who assume independent representations automatically yield controllable generation. Limitations include strong assumptions (sub-Gaussian tails, Lipschitz scores, approximate independence of factors), experiments on relatively small or controlled datasets, and the open question of whether the finite-sample convergence guarantees extend to deeper or finite-width networks. Future work on more complex latent structures, multi-modal settings, and large-scale validation would strengthen the practical impact.
