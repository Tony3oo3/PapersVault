## Problem

Existing adversarial image protection methods (e.g., PhotoGuard, AdvDM, Mist) craft imperceptible noise to disrupt text-to-image editing, but this noise is easily reversed by simple purification techniques such as JPEG compression or diffusion-based denoising. The root cause is that imperceptibility in pixel space does not guarantee naturalness in the frequency domain: ℓ∞-constrained perturbations exhibit a pronounced high-frequency bias that makes them detectable and projectable back onto the data manifold by purification methods.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image and produces a protected version bounded within an ℓ∞ ball of ε=16/255. Internally, it leverages Segment Anything Model (SAM) to obtain R semantic region masks, then applies a per-region learnable Gaussian blur to the adversarial perturbation. The output is a single perturbed image that, when fed to a text-to-image diffusion model (e.g., Stable Diffusion), produces outputs that deviate significantly from the intended edit, even after the strongest available purification is applied.

## Method

BlurGuard introduces a plug-and-play adaptive Gaussian blurring layer that restricts the frequency bands of adversarial noise per semantic region, closing the spectral gap between the protected image and the natural image distribution. A novel power spectrum regularization (L_freq) is defined as the ℓ∞ norm of the log-ratio of radially-averaged power spectral densities (RAPSD) between the original and protected images, ensuring no single frequency band deviates excessively. The optimization is two-stage: blur intensities σ_r are first optimized solely against L_freq and then frozen, after which the perturbation δ is optimized via PGD (with ℓ2-normalized gradients rather than sign projection) under the combined objective L_adv + λ·L_freq within the ℓ∞ budget. The approach is model-agnostic and compatible with any adversarial protection objective (encoder attack, denoiser attack, or joint), and it consistently retains ~93% of its protective FID efficacy after all purification methods, compared to 38–48% for prior baselines.

## Results

On ImageNet-Edit (image-to-image generation, ε=16/255), BlurGuard achieves the best naturalness (LPIPS 0.21, SSIM 0.93) and worst-case protection effectiveness (FID 107.88, LPIPS 0.32) across all metrics, outperforming the strongest baseline (SDS: FID 91.48, LPIPS 0.26). The headline robustness result is that BlurGuard retains 92.9% of its FID-based protective efficacy after the strongest purification, while all baselines drop to 38.5–48.4%. On inpainting (Helen) and instruction-based editing (MagicBrush), BlurGuard similarly achieves the highest worst-case FID (162.92 and 138.22 respectively) while maintaining competitive naturalness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BlurGuard outperforms all six baselines (PhotoGuard, AdvDM, Mist, SDS, DiffusionGuard, EditShield) on every worst-case effectiveness metric across all three editing scenarios. On ImageNet-Edit, BlurGuard's worst-case FID of 107.88 exceeds the next-best AdvDM (98.27) by ~10%, and its FID retention after purification (92.9%) nearly doubles the best baseline (SDS at 48.4%). On Helen inpainting, BlurGuard achieves worst-case LPIPS of 0.45, a 32.3% improvement over the second-best (AdvDM at 0.34), while DiffusionGuard and EditShield—methods specifically designed for inpainting robustness—still trail BlurGuard. On MagicBrush instruction-based editing, BlurGuard leads in worst-case FID (138.22 vs. 123.54 for Mist) and worst-case LPIPS (0.36 vs. 0.33 for EditShield). Notably, BlurGuard also improves naturalness over all baselines (LPIPS 0.21 vs. 0.30–0.36), indicating that the frequency-aware blurring reduces perceptual distortion rather than merely trading quality for robustness.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the adversarial image protection line initiated by PhotoGuard (Salman et al., 2023) and subsequent methods (AdvDM, Mist, SDS, DiffusionGuard, EditShield), which all optimize imperceptible perturbations under ℓ∞ constraints to disrupt text-to-image diffusion models. The key conceptual departure is shifting the design criterion from pixel-space imperceptibility to frequency-domain naturalness: the authors observe that existing protections, while visually imperceptible, produce detectable deviations in the radially-averaged power spectral density (RAPSD), making them vulnerable to purification. BlurGuard repurposes a learnable Gaussian blur (implemented as a depthwise convolution) as a per-region frequency-band controller, combined with a novel RAPSD-based regularization, and leverages SAM for semantic-aware spatial adaptation — all as a plug-and-play wrapper on top of any existing adversarial objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a critical practical gap: prior adversarial protections are trivially reversed by simple operations like JPEG compression or diffusion-based denoising, undermining their real-world utility. The 93% vs. 48% FID retention after worst-case purification is a substantial practical improvement, and the frequency-domain lens offers a principled new axis for designing robust protections beyond ad-hoc imperceptibility constraints. However, the protection remains ineffective if an unprotected copy is leaked, does not address manual (non-AI) editing, and the 'oracle attacker' evaluation, while thorough, may overstate real-world threat levels. Open questions include whether frequency-natural protections generalize to video, 3D, or audio modalities, and whether adaptive frequency control can be made computationally cheaper than the current SAM + two-stage PGD pipeline.
