## Problem

Text-guided image editing remains challenging even for short edit instructions, and most prior work relies on diffusion-based models with supervised fine-tuning, leaving the potential of reinforcement learning and autoregressive architectures largely unexplored for this task. The paper asks what combination of learning paradigms—SFT, RL, and chain-of-thought reasoning—most effectively enables a single end-to-end model to handle both simple (object/attribute/style) and complex (spatial, counting, action) edits.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253918/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a source RGB image (resized to 256×256 preserving aspect ratio) and a free-form text edit instruction as input, and autoregressively generates the edited image as a sequence of discrete vision tokens. Both images and text are tokenized into a unified token stream (SBER-MoVQGAN for vision, QwenTokenizer for text) and processed by a single causal transformer, so the task is framed as standard sequence-to-sequence generation rather than a separate diffusion denoising process.

## Method

EARL builds on Emu3-8B, a fully autoregressive multimodal model, and introduces a two-stage training recipe: (1) SFT on simple edits only (750K OmniEdit samples), followed by (2) RL post-training using GRPO with 8 rollouts per instruction and a Qwen2.5-VL-72B verifier that scores each generated edit on four VIEScore criteria (edit success, overedit, natural look, artifacts) to produce a 0–10 reward. A key empirical finding is that complex edits (spatial, counting, action) degrade SFT performance but are beneficial when introduced during the RL stage, so the optimal pipeline is SFT(S) → RL(S+C). The paper also systematically shows that chain-of-thought reasoning supervision does not improve—and can hurt—editing performance, likely because Emu3 lacks pretraining on interleaved image-text generation. The scaled EARL model (2000 RL steps, 300K data pool) achieves an average VIEScore of 4.80, surpassing the SOTA diffusion baseline Omnigen (4.70) while using roughly 5× less training data and running ~4× faster at inference.

## Results

EARL (SFT(S)→RL(S+C)) achieves an average VIEScore of 4.80 across six editing benchmarks, surpassing the prior SOTA Omnigen (4.70) while using roughly 5× less training data. On the three benchmarks where EARL leads most decisively—OmniEdit (6.39 vs. 5.68), AURORA (4.27 vs. 4.10), and VisMin (4.93 vs. 4.09)—the gains over Omnigen are +0.71, +0.17, and +0.84 respectively. RL post-training alone lifts the SFT(S) baseline from 3.88 to 4.57 average, with the largest per-benchmark gains on MagicBrush (+1.33) and VisMin (+1.36). EARL also runs ~4× faster at inference than Omnigen (52.7 s vs. 200 s for 50 samples at 256×256).

## Baselines & Comparisons

EARL outperforms all five baselines on average VIEScore (4.80). Against the diffusion-based baselines MagicBrush (3.32) and InstructPix2Pix (3.26), EARL leads by over 1.5 points on average, with the largest gaps on complex-edit benchmarks (e.g., VisMin: 4.93 vs. 3.48 for MagicBrush). Against Aurora (4.17), EARL wins on every benchmark except MagicBrush (4.52 vs. 4.62) and I2EBench (4.19 vs. 3.58—actually EARL wins here too; Aurora's 3.58 is lower). Against the only other autoregressive editor, EditAR (4.20), EARL leads by +0.60 on average and additionally wins 5 of 7 PIEBench metrics (PSNR 23.71 vs. 21.32, LPIPS 112.40 vs. 117.15, MSE 77.00 vs. 130.27, SSIM 79.17 vs. 75.13, Structure Distance 35.00 vs. 39.43), though EditAR edges ahead on CLIP-W (24.87 vs. 24.44) and CLIP-E (21.87 vs. 21.34). Against the strongest baseline Omnigen (4.70), EARL wins on OmniEdit (+0.71), AURORA (+0.17), and VisMin (+0.84) but trails on EmuEdit (4.47 vs. 5.00), MagicBrush (4.52 vs. 4.68), and I2EBench (4.19 vs. 4.68); the paper attributes these losses to Omnigen's stronger base model (GenEval 0.70 vs. 0.64) and ~5× more training data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the autoregressive image editing line initiated by EditAR (which only studied SFT) by introducing RL post-training via GRPO and systematically evaluating chain-of-thought reasoning within a unified Emu3-based framework. It departs from the dominant diffusion-based editing paradigm (InstructPix2Pix, MagicBrush, Aurora, Omnigen) by showing that a fully autoregressive model, when paired with a strong MLLM verifier (Qwen2.5-VL-72B) for reward signals, can match or exceed diffusion baselines. The RL formulation borrows directly from the LLM post-training literature (GRPO, KL-regularized policy optimization), while the verifier design departs from CLIP-score-based rewards commonly used in diffusion RL toward fine-grained MLLM-based evaluation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that autoregressive multimodal models are a viable and efficient alternative to diffusion models for image editing, achieving competitive quality with ~5× less training data and ~4× faster inference than the SOTA diffusion baseline. The negative result on CoT reasoning challenges the assumption that explicit intermediate reasoning transfers from LLMs to visual generation, suggesting that the bottleneck may lie in the base model's lack of interleaved image-text pretraining rather than in the reasoning formulation itself. Key open questions include whether stronger autoregressive base models (with native interleaved generation) would unlock CoT benefits, whether the RL approach scales to higher resolutions and more complex edits, and how to improve verifier reliability for fine-grained spatial and counting tasks where current MLLMs remain inconsistent.
