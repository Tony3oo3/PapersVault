## Problem

Existing zero-shot LLM-generated text detection methods rely on a proxy LLM and suffer significant performance drops when the source model is unknown. Reward-based approaches such as ReMoDetect require constructing preference datasets and fine-tuning a reward model, inheriting the poor generalization to unseen LLMs that plagues supervised methods. The paper addresses the need for a model-agnostic detection metric that requires neither preference collection nor any additional training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255590/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a complete text y (no prompt is needed; x is set to ∅) and produces a scalar reward score computed as the sum of per-token log-probability ratios between an instruction-tuned model and its corresponding base model. A higher score indicates greater likelihood of LLM generation, and the score is thresholded to yield a binary label. Because the metric is invariant to affine transformations, standard classification metrics (AUROC, F1) are unaffected by threshold calibration, and the score naturally scales with text length, requiring length-aware threshold adjustment.

## Method

IRM derives an implicit reward model directly from the DPO closed-form solution, where the reward is expressed as the log-ratio of the policy (instruction-tuned) model to the reference (base) model: r(y) = Σ_{i=1}^{L} log(π_θ(y_i|y_{<i}) / π_ref(y_i|y_{<i})). The key insight is that DPO's bidirectional relationship guarantees that if the instruction-tuned model aligns well with human preferences, the derived implicit reward score will effectively separate LLM-generated from human-written text, since aligned LLMs are trained to maximize reward. No preference data, fine-tuning, or text perturbation is required—only a publicly available instruction-tuned/base model pair (e.g., Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct + Llama-3.2-1B). The formulation also extends to iteratively aligned models, where summing per-step rewards telescopes into the log-ratio of the final instruction-tuned model to the original base model, eliminating the need for intermediate checkpoints.

## Results

On the DetectRL benchmark, IRM with the Llama-3.2-1B family achieves an average score of 91.77%, surpassing the best zero-shot baseline Binoculars (87.67%) by 4.1% and the supervised reward-based method ReMoDetect (85.86%) by 5.9%. IRM also outperforms standard zero-shot metrics: Log-Likelihood (77.46%), Log-Rank (77.42%), LRR (74.48%), Fast-DetectGPT (59.82%), and Lastde (61.67%). With the Gemma-2-2B family, IRM achieves 77.05%, slightly below Binoculars (77.81%), indicating that model family choice matters for detection quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IRM (Llama-3.2-1B family, 91.77% avg.) outperforms all eight baselines on the DetectRL benchmark. Against the closest zero-shot competitor Binoculars [21] (87.67%), IRM gains 4.1% on average, with particularly large margins on Multi-Domain (97.97 vs 92.48 AUROC) and Multi-LLM (97.24 vs 92.08 AUROC). IRM also surpasses the supervised reward-based method ReMoDetect [11] (85.86%) by 5.9%, demonstrating that zero-shot implicit reward modeling can exceed task-specific fine-tuning. Compared to standard likelihood-based metrics—Log-Likelihood [9] (77.46%), Log-Rank [5] (77.42%), and LRR [23] (74.48%)—IRM improves by 14–17%, showing that the implicit reward signal captures alignment properties that raw likelihood does not. The gap is even larger against perturbation-based methods: Fast-DetectGPT [24] (59.82%) and Lastde [22] (61.67%) trail IRM by over 30%. Finally, IRM substantially outperforms general-purpose reward models from RewardBench (GRM-gemma2-2B: 49.07%, GRM-Llama3.2-3B: 38.09%), suggesting that reward models optimized for preference classification are poorly suited for text detection without task-specific adaptation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the implicit reward model from DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023), which was previously used only as a theoretical construct during alignment training, as a standalone detection metric for LLM-generated text. It departs from ReMoDetect (Lee et al., 2024), the closest prior work that also uses reward scores for detection, by eliminating the need for preference data collection and task-specific fine-tuning of a reward model. Among zero-shot baselines, IRM shares the two-model architecture with Binoculars (Hans et al., 2024) but replaces the log-likelihood ratio with a theoretically grounded reward formulation derived from the RLHF objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By showing that a training-free log-ratio of instruction-tuned and base model probabilities outperforms a supervised reward model fine-tuned on detection-specific preferences, the paper challenges the assumption that task-specific adaptation is necessary for effective reward-based detection. This has direct practical implications: practitioners can deploy a detector using any publicly available model family without collecting data or running training. Key limitations include the requirement for both instruction-tuned and base models from the same family, sensitivity to text length (requiring threshold recalibration), and evaluation restricted to lightweight models under 3B parameters, leaving open whether the approach scales to larger or cross-family model combinations.
