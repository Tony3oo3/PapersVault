## Problem

Existing LLM watermarking methods face a fundamental trade-off: watermarking all tokens degrades text quality, while prior selective approaches (e.g., SWEET) rely on a single entropy threshold that must be manually tuned via grid search and ignores other informative signals. No prior work learns a data-driven selection policy that jointly considers semantic context, token entropy, and the running watermarking ratio to balance detectability against quality.

## Input / Output

At each generation step, the Selector Network consumes three signals: a 768-dim SimCSE sentence embedding of the previous k=6 tokens (reduced via an MLP), the Shannon entropy of the current token probability distribution, and the fraction of tokens watermarked so far. The network outputs a continuous value in [0,1] that is thresholded (via an adaptive threshold that lowers when the watermarking ratio is low and raises when it is high) to decide whether to add the green-list bias δ to the logits. Detection recomputes the same three signals from the candidate text and applies the z-test only over the re-identified watermarked subset, yielding a z-score.

## Method

LTW introduces a lightweight two-stage MLP (Selector Network) that maps the concatenated [reduced sentence embedding; entropy; watermarking ratio] to a continuous mask in [0,1], with a sigmoid output and Leaky ReLU hidden layers. Training is formulated as a multi-objective optimization problem solved with the Multiple Gradient Descent Algorithm (MGDA): a quality loss combines cosine similarity between watermarked and unwatermarked sentence embeddings, an entropy-aware BCE term encouraging watermarking of high-entropy tokens, and a mask-like regularization; a detectability loss combines a differentiable z-score (relaxing the green-token count to a probability), a ratio-aware MSE term that encourages more watermarking when the ratio is low, and the same mask-like regularization. The MGDA closed-form solution (Frank-Wolfe for two objectives) yields a convex combination of the two gradients that drives the network toward Pareto-optimal watermarking decisions. The framework is applied to both KGW (LTW-1) and Unigram (LTW-0) baselines, and an adaptive threshold module dynamically adjusts the binary decision boundary based on the current watermarking ratio to maintain detectability without over-watermarking.

## Results

On OPT-6.7B, both LTW-1 and LTW-0 achieve perfect detectability (TPR@2 = 1.000, AUROC = 1.00000) while attaining the lowest perplexity among all compared methods (13.62 and 13.62, respectively), compared to 20.52 for KGW, 17.68 for Unigram, and 14.08 for TS-watermark. LTW-0 additionally achieves the best robustness under Dipper paraphrase attack (TPR@10 = 0.954, AUROC = 0.98326) and the highest semantic similarity to reference text (0.5735). On GPT-J-6B, LTW-0 further reduces perplexity to 9.56 while maintaining perfect detectability and strong robustness (TPR@10 = 0.926).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against KGW, both LTW variants achieve identical perfect detectability while reducing perplexity by ~34% (13.62 vs. 20.52) and improving semantic similarity (0.5701/0.5735 vs. 0.5472); however, LTW-1 is less robust under Dipper attack (TPR@10 = 0.852 vs. 0.910), while LTW-0 nearly matches KGW (0.954 vs. 0.910). Against Unigram, LTW-0 wins decisively on perplexity (13.62 vs. 17.68) and similarity (0.5735 vs. 0.5313) with a marginal robustness gap (TPR@10 = 0.954 vs. 0.962). Against SWEET, both LTW methods improve perplexity by ~24% (13.62 vs. 17.93) and similarity (0.5701/0.5735 vs. 0.5554); LTW-0 also surpasses SWEET on robustness (0.954 vs. 0.924), while LTW-1 trails (0.852 vs. 0.924). Against TS-watermark, LTW-1 and LTW-0 both achieve lower perplexity (13.62 vs. 14.08) and higher similarity (0.5701/0.5735 vs. 0.5645), with LTW-0 substantially outperforming on robustness (0.954 vs. 0.810). Against EXP-edit, both LTW methods dominate across all metrics, with perplexity reduced from 23.93 to 13.62 and robustness TPR@10 improved from 0.790 to 0.852/0.954.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the selective watermarking paradigm introduced by SWEET (Lee et al., ACL 2024), which used a fixed entropy threshold to decide which tokens to watermark, by replacing that hand-tuned rule with a trained lightweight MLP that additionally consumes sentence embeddings and the running watermarking ratio. The multi-objective optimization framework (MGDA) is borrowed from TS-watermark (Huo et al., ICML 2024), which applied it to adaptively tune watermark strength (δ) and green-list ratio (γ) per token; here it is repurposed to optimize the binary selection decision instead. The underlying watermarking mechanism remains the standard KGW green/red-list logit bias (Kirchenbauer et al., ICML 2023) or its fixed-key Unigram variant (Zhao et al., ICLR 2024), so the contribution is orthogonal to the watermark construction itself.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a small trained selector can simultaneously reduce perplexity (by ~30% over KGW) and maintain perfect detectability, offering a practical path to watermarking that is less perceptible to readers. However, the evaluation is limited to two mid-size models (OPT-6.7B, GPT-J-6B), a single dataset (C4 RealNewsLike), and a text-continuation task, leaving open whether the selector generalizes to code generation, summarization, or larger frontier models. The selector network is also task-specific (trained on OPT-1.3B generations), raising questions about transferability and the cost of retraining for new domains. A key open question is whether the learned selection policy remains robust under stronger or more diverse paraphrase attacks beyond the single Dipper configuration tested.
