## Problem

Energy-based formulations of recurrent self-attention provide convergence guarantees but rely on idealized constraints—symmetric weights, single-head attention, and continuous-time limits—that are absent in practical Transformer architectures. This paper asks whether the inference dynamics of realistic, multi-head, discrete-update SA can be characterized without assuming an energy function, and what role normalization layers play in stabilizing those dynamics.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254675/cropped_page5_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The primary evaluation uses partially filled 9×9 Sudoku grids (31–42 givens for SATNet in-distribution, 17–34 givens for RRN out-of-distribution) encoded as sequences of S tokens with D=512 dimensions, producing solved grids after T iterative SA updates. Secondary evaluations use CIFAR-10 images (32×32, D=384) for classification and a language modeling task. A distinctive aspect is test-time scaling: the same SA layer is looped T times at inference (beyond the T=16 training setting), with RMSNorm applied after each update to keep token vectors on a hypersphere.

## Method

The paper develops a Jacobian-based dynamical systems analysis of recurrent SA, proposing ItrSA (iterative self-attention with RMSNorm) as a minimal baseline and AKOrN as the primary testbed. Theoretically, they relax energy-based constraints to permit asymmetric QK products and multi-head attention (Propositions 4.1–4.2), then show that the Jacobian of the full RMSNorm∘(I+η·MSA) update has its spectral norm inversely bounded by the pre-normalization norm (Proposition 5.1), and that normalization rescales complex eigenvalues to unit magnitude, preventing the discretization-induced instability of oscillatory components. Empirically, they demonstrate that high-accuracy models operate at criticality (maximum Lyapunov exponent ≈ 0, slightly positive), that spectral regularization of SA weight matrices (R_Spec) outperforms energy-based regularization, and that the pseudo-energy decrease observed in AKOrN corresponds to the state aligning with the top eigenvectors of the symmetrized Jacobian—effectively a power iteration in real space.

## Results

On the Sudoku task (SATNet ID / RRN OOD), ItrSA exhibits consistent test-time scaling: accuracy improves monotonically as the number of loop iterations T increases beyond the training setting of T=16, regardless of oscillator dimension N (Figure 4). High-performing models operate near criticality, with maximum Lyapunov exponents slightly positive (~0.1), while energy-based symmetric SA models show negative exponents and lower accuracy (Figure 3). Among regularization strategies, Jacobian spectral regularization (R_Spec) substantially enhances performance for both ItrSA and AKOrN, whereas energy-based regularization (R_E-multi, R_E-single) underperforms the unregularized baselines (Figure 5). Normalization (RMSNorm) is shown to reduce the maximum Lyapunov exponent and keep the Jacobian spectral norm O(1) with respect to sequence length.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is AKOrN (Miyato et al., ICLR 2025), a looped architecture with Kuramoto oscillatory neurons. The paper demonstrates that ItrSA (a simpler baseline without the Omega layer or oscillator-wise operations) also exhibits test-time scaling, contradicting the earlier finding that ItrSA does not scale. Conversely, AKOrN fails to maintain test-time scaling when the oscillator dimension N becomes large, an issue mitigated by applying oscillator-wise RMS normalization. In the regularization comparison (Figure 5), spectral regularization (R_Spec) outperforms both the unregularized ItrSA and AKOrN, with the effect being especially pronounced for AKOrN. Energy-based regularization (R_E-multi from Proposition 4.2) consistently underperforms the unregularized model, with Lyapunov exponents shifting to more negative (more convergent) values that do not translate to better accuracy. E-single (H=1) fails even on in-distribution tasks, likely due to the restrictive single-head constraint. Overall, the Jacobian/spectral perspective yields practical improvements while the energy-based perspective, despite its theoretical elegance, does not provide a useful regularization signal for realistic multi-head SA.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the energy-based analysis of recurrent self-attention (Geshkovski et al., Karagodin et al., Bruno et al.) by relaxing the symmetry and single-head constraints that those formulations require, and by introducing a Jacobian-based (Lyapunov's indirect method) perspective that is more general than the energy/Lyapunov-function approach. It builds directly on AKOrN (Miyato et al., 2025) as its primary testbed, deriving the simpler ItrSA baseline from it, and adapts the spectral regularization of Lewandowski et al. (2025) to the SA setting. The Jacobian spectral bounds of Castin et al. (2024) provide the theoretical starting point for the normalization analysis in Proposition 5.1. The key conceptual departure is shifting from asking 'does an energy function exist?' to 'what do the Jacobian eigenvalues and Lyapunov exponents tell us about stability and performance?', which naturally accommodates non-stationary dynamics like oscillations that energy-based frameworks cannot capture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a mechanistic explanation for why RMSNorm is essential in looped SA architectures: it suppresses the Jacobian's spectral norm and normalizes complex eigenvalues that would otherwise cause instability under discretization. The finding that high-performance models operate at a slightly positive maximum Lyapunov exponent (edge of chaos from the chaotic side) gives a concrete, measurable indicator of good inference dynamics. However, the scope is limited to looped SA without positional encoding, masking, or MLP blocks, and the theoretical bounds remain conservative relative to empirical observations. Open questions include deriving tighter Jacobian bounds, analytically justifying the criticality concentration, and extending the framework to full Transformer blocks where these additional components may fundamentally alter the dynamics.
