## Problem

Conventional image tokenizers rely on uniform 2D or 1D grid structures, assigning equal representation capacity to every spatial location regardless of local content complexity. This rigidity forces simple regions (e.g., smooth backgrounds) to consume the same token budget as texture-rich regions (e.g., faces, fur), limiting both reconstruction fidelity and generation quality. The paper asks whether a spatially-adaptive, non-uniform tokenization scheme can allocate representation capacity proportionally to local information content.

## Input / Output

The tokenizer takes a 256×256 RGB image and produces l Gaussian-parameterized tokens (l ∈ {64, 128, 256}), where each token consists of five Gaussian parameters (μx, μy, σx, σy, ρ) encoding region position and anisotropic shape, plus a (c−5)-dimensional texture feature vector. During decoding, these tokens are rendered into a 64×64 2D feature map via differentiable splatting and passed through a CNN decoder to reconstruct the image. For the two-stage generation pipeline, the input additionally includes a class label; the first stage outputs calibrated Gaussian parameters (layout), and the second stage outputs refined Gaussian parameters plus texture features, which are splatted and decoded into a generated image.

## Method

GPSToken parameterizes each token as a bounded 2D Gaussian (Eq. 2) whose mean encodes region center and whose covariance encodes region shape/orientation, coupled with a texture feature vector. Spatial adaptivity is achieved in two stages: (1) an entropy-driven iterative partitioning algorithm (Algorithm 1) splits the image into variable-size regions using a gradient-entropy metric m = hw × H^λ, initializing Gaussian parameters from region geometry; (2) a transformer encoder with RoIAlign-conditioned queries refines the Gaussian parameters (outputting residuals Δg) and extracts texture features, enabling rotated and repositioned Gaussians that align with local structures. Decoding uses a splatting-based renderer (Eq. 3) that sums weighted texture features across all Gaussians at each pixel, producing a 2D feature map for a standard CNN decoder. For generation, the shape-texture decomposition enables a two-stage pipeline: a lightweight SiT-S model predicts g^init (layout), a calibration algorithm (Algorithm 2) snaps means to a grid and recomputes sigmas, then a SiT-XL diffusion model conditioned on the calibrated layout generates Δg and texture features, achieving FID 1.50 with ~3× faster convergence than the single-stage baseline.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256 reconstruction, GPSToken-M128 (128 tokens) achieves a rec. FID of 0.65, PSNR of 24.06 dB, and SSIM of 0.657, while GPSToken-L256 (256 tokens) reaches a rec. FID of 0.22, PSNR of 28.81 dB, and SSIM of 0.809 — outperforming SDXL-VAE (which uses 1024 tokens and additional training data) on all metrics. For class-conditional image generation, the two-stage GPSToken generator achieves a state-of-the-art FID of 1.50 with only 128 tokens, surpassing the previous best (MAETok, FID 1.67) and the SiT-XL/2+ baseline (FID 2.06). The two-stage pipeline also converges ~3× faster than the SiT-XL/2 baseline, reaching FID-10K of 25.48 within 100K iterations versus 300K for the baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPSToken outperforms all 17 baselines on both reconstruction and generation. In reconstruction, GPSToken-L256 surpasses SDXL-VAE (which uses 4× more tokens and extra training data) by +3.26 PSNR and −0.51 rec. FID, and beats VAVAE (the strongest 2D-grid competitor) by +3.05 PSNR and −0.05 rec. FID. At 128 tokens, GPSToken-M128 ties MAETok on rec. FID (0.65) but leads by +0.81 PSNR and −0.016 LPIPS, while decisively beating TiTok-S128 (rec. FID 0.65 vs. 1.73) and FlexTok (0.65 vs. 4.02). In generation, the two-stage GPSToken generator (FID 1.50) beats the closest competitor MAETok (FID 1.67) by 0.17, and outperforms SiT-XL/2+ (2.06), REPA (1.79), D2iT (1.73), and SoftVQ (1.78) by 0.23–0.56 FID. Notably, the one-stage GPSToken generator (FID 2.13) underperforms the SiT-XL/2+ baseline, confirming that the shape-texture decomposition is essential for the generation gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

GPSToken departs from the dominant grid-based tokenization paradigm (2D grids in VQGAN/LDM/DiT, 1D sequences in TiTok/FlexTok/MAETok) by parameterizing each token as a 2D Gaussian whose mean, covariance, and correlation encode region position and shape. The splatting-based rendering mechanism is directly adapted from 3D Gaussian Splatting [Kerbl et al., 2023] and its 2D extension [Chen et al., 2025], repurposing a real-time rendering technique as a differentiable decoder from tokens to feature maps. Unlike GaussianToken [Dong et al., 2025], which replaces VQVAE tokens with fixed-position Gaussians, GPSToken introduces entropy-driven spatially-adaptive partitioning and transformer-based refinement, enabling tokens to dynamically concentrate on texture-rich regions. The shape-texture decomposition for two-stage generation (layout synthesis via lightweight SiT-S, then structure-conditioned texture via SiT-XL) is a new framing not present in prior tokenizers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 4× token efficiency (128 adaptive tokens matching or exceeding 1024 uniform grid tokens from SDXL-VAE) and the ~3–5× training speedup from shape-texture decomposition make GPSToken practically attractive for resource-constrained generation pipelines. The spatial adaptivity also enables user-controllable emphasis on semantically important regions (faces, text) and adjustable token counts at inference, offering flexibility absent in fixed-grid methods. Key limitations include the heuristic entropy-based initialization (which may not always yield optimal Gaussian configurations) and the need for a post-hoc calibration step in the generation pipeline. Open questions include whether learned initialization can replace the heuristic partitioning, whether a dedicated architecture for Gaussian parameter prediction can eliminate calibration, and whether the approach extends naturally to video or 3D content.
