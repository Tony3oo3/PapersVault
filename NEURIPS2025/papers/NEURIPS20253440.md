## Problem

Existing 3DGS-based surface reconstruction methods for articulated objects suffer from insufficient geometric constraints, producing noisy meshes, while dynamic reconstruction methods require continuous supervision across the full motion sequence, preventing generation at unseen states. No prior approach simultaneously achieves high-fidelity textured reconstruction and time-continuous mesh generation from only two observed states.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253440/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 64–100 multi-view RGB images from two arbitrary states of an articulated object (with known camera poses) and produces textured surface meshes via TSDF fusion and Marching Cubes, with vertex colors baked from spherical harmonics and Gaussian opacity. Beyond the meshes, it outputs dense point clouds, unsupervised part segmentation masks (dynamic vs. static), and learned joint parameters (rotation axis, pivot, angle for revolute joints; translation direction and distance for prismatic joints), enabling mesh generation at any interpolated state s∈[0,1].

## Method

REArtGS introduces two key mechanisms on top of ray-tracing-based 3DGS (following GOF). First, an unbiased SDF guidance: an 8-layer MLP learns a signed distance field, and a bell-shaped activation Φ_k converts SDF values to opacity estimates; a novel regularization loss L_unbias forces the SDF at the depth t* (where each Gaussian's rendering contribution ε peaks) to approach zero, eliminating the spatial misalignment between opacity maxima and surface location. Second, motion-constrained deformable fields: after unsupervised part segmentation via displacement thresholds during warm-up, the method learns kinematic parameters (rotation axis/angle via Rodrigues' formula with angle-bounded parameterization, or translation vector/distance) and applies closed-form deformation to dynamic Gaussians, enabling interpolation to any unseen state without intermediate supervision. Training uses a composite loss combining D-SSIM/L1 photometric loss, the unbiased SDF term, SDF-normal alignment, Eikonal regularization, and depth distortion.

## Results

On PartNet-Mobility (synthetic), REArtGS achieves the best mean reconstruction quality across all four metrics: CD(ws)=3.79, CD(rs)=1.236, F1=0.294, and EMD=0.695, outperforming the closest competitor ArtGS* (4.75, 1.682, 0.232, 0.926). For surface generation at unseen states, REArtGS again leads with CD(ws)=4.10, CD(rs)=1.063, F1=0.298, and EMD=0.670. On the real-world AKB-48 dataset, REArtGS achieves mean reconstruction F1=0.636 and EMD=0.372, substantially surpassing PARIS (F1=0.438, EMD=0.569) and ArtGS* (F1=0.416, EMD=0.826).

## Baselines & Comparisons

REArtGS outperforms all six baselines on mean metrics for both reconstruction and generation. Against the closest 3DGS-based competitor ArtGS* (implemented without depth supervision for fairness), REArtGS improves CD(rs) from 1.682 to 1.236 (−27%) and F1 from 0.232 to 0.294 (+27%) on reconstruction, and CD(rs) from 1.794 to 1.063 (−41%) on generation. Against PARIS, the prior SOTA for articulated object reconstruction from RGB, REArtGS reduces CD(rs) from 2.380 to 1.236 (−48%) and nearly doubles F1 (0.227→0.294). GOF, a general-purpose 3DGS surface reconstruction method, is outperformed by a wide margin (CD(ws) 6.09 vs. 3.79), confirming that the unbiased SDF guidance and kinematic constraints are critical for articulated objects. Deformable 3DGS (D-3DGS), which requires full motion supervision, performs poorly on generation (CD(ws)=42.49) because it cannot generalize to unseen states without continuous supervision. On real-world AKB-48, REArtGS dominates ArtGS* by a large margin (reconstruction CD(ws) 2.86 vs. 20.61), highlighting the advantage of not requiring depth inputs.

## Relation to Prior Work

REArtGS sits at the intersection of two active research threads: 3D Gaussian Splatting for surface reconstruction (GOF, 2DGS, PGSR) and articulated object modeling (PARIS, Ditto, A-SDF). It builds directly on GOF's ray-tracing-based opacity field formulation and NeuS's SDF-to-opacity conversion, but departs from prior SDF+3DGS hybrids (GSDF, NeuSG, 3DGSR) by regularizing the opacity field itself rather than merely guiding normals or pruning. For the dynamic generation stage, it replaces the neural deformation fields used by Deformable 3DGS and 4DGS with explicit kinematic parameters (rotation axis/angle, translation direction/distance) constrained by Rodrigues' formula, following the kinematic modeling paradigm introduced by PARIS but applied to Gaussian primitives rather than neural radiance fields.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap: producing high-fidelity textured meshes of articulated objects from only two RGB states, without depth supervision or intermediate motion frames. The unbiased SDF regularization is a clean, generalizable trick that could benefit other 3DGS surface reconstruction pipelines beyond articulated objects. However, the method still requires known camera poses and struggles with transparent materials, and the kinematic constraint approach assumes simple revolute/prismatic joints, limiting applicability to more complex or underactuated mechanisms. Open questions include whether the two-state assumption can be relaxed, how the method scales to objects with many interacting parts, and whether the SDF-opacity coupling can be made differentiable end-to-end for joint optimization.
