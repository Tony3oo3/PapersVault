## Problem

Multi-task reinforcement learning (MTRL) methods for knowledge transfer—such as policy distillation, representation sharing, and parameter sharing—often suffer from slow adaptation to and limited utilization of transferred knowledge, while simultaneously struggling with sparse-reward environments that provide no intermediate feedback. The paper identifies that task-contextual reward shaping, which automatically encodes task-specific information into dense auxiliary rewards, offers a more direct and transferable medium for distributing knowledge across tasks than existing structural sharing approaches.

## Input / Output

The CRA consumes the Cartesian product of each policy agent's state and action (s_i, a_i) as its observation, requiring uniform state and action dimensions across all tasks. It outputs a continuous scalar knowledge reward r^knw constrained to [R_min, R_max], which is added to the sparse environmental reward with a scaling factor λ ∈ (0,1] to form the augmented reward r^pol = r^env + λ·r^knw. Policy agents output actions via their backbone RL algorithms (DQN for discrete, SAC/TD3 for continuous), and the entire system shares a single concatenated replay buffer storing augmented transitions (s^rwd, r^knw, r^env, s'^rwd | T_i) to minimize storage overhead.

## Method

CenRA introduces a centralized reward agent (CRA) modeled as a self-contained off-policy actor-critic RL agent that concurrently interacts with multiple distributed policy agents. The CRA's actor maps (state, action) pairs to dense knowledge rewards, while its critic is trained via TD error on environmental rewards; the actor is optimized through a policy-gradient objective that encourages rewards correlated with positive TD errors. A key architectural choice is the concatenated replay buffer shared across all tasks, allowing the CRA to aggregate and reuse experiences from every policy agent. To prevent knowledge imbalance, an information synchronization mechanism computes per-task sampling weights combining a similarity weight (inverse cross-attention score between each task's ReLU hidden features and the task centroid) and a performance weight (inverse of recent average environmental reward), weighted by a hyperparameter α. This design lets the CRA focus on outlier or underperforming tasks while distributing task-specific dense rewards that directly address the sparse-reward challenge and enable transfer to unseen tasks without retraining the CRA.

## Results

CenRA achieves the highest episodic return across all five sparse-reward MTRL domains: 0.875 on Meta-World ML10-sparse, 0.755 on ML50-sparse, 0.913 on 2DMaze, 0.880 on 3DPickup, and 514.875 on MujocoCar. The largest margins over the strongest baseline (TD-MPC2) appear on 3DPickup (+0.168) and ML50-sparse (+0.147), while the closest competition is on ML10-sparse where PiCor reaches 0.865 vs. CenRA's 0.875. CenRA also exhibits the lowest variance in most domains, indicating more stable and balanced learning across tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CenRA outperforms all ten baselines across every domain. Against the strongest competitor TD-MPC2, CenRA leads by 0.052 on ML10-sparse, 0.147 on ML50-sparse, 0.029 on 2DMaze, 0.168 on 3DPickup, and 9.5 on MujocoCar. The closest margin is on ML10-sparse where PiCor (0.865) nearly matches CenRA (0.875), though PiCor shows much higher variance (±0.230 vs. ±0.121). ReLara, the decentralized single-task variant of CenRA, trails by 0.201 on ML10-sparse and 0.315 on 3DPickup, confirming the benefit of centralized cross-task knowledge sharing. The backbone algorithms (DQN/SAC) are substantially outperformed, with CenRA achieving 3.4× the return on ML10-sparse and 2.6× on MujocoCar. Representation-sharing methods (PaCo, SoftModule, SC) and policy-correction methods (PiCor, MCAL) consistently underperform CenRA, particularly on 3DPickup where CenRA reaches 0.880 versus 0.557 (PaCo) and 0.548 (MCAL).

## Relation to Prior Work

CenRA extends the ReLara framework (Ma et al., 2024), which paired a single policy agent with an assistant reward agent to densify sparse rewards, by making the reward agent centralized and shared across multiple tasks. Rather than following the dominant MTRL paradigms of policy distillation, representation sharing, or parameter sharing, CenRA uses shaped rewards as the explicit medium for cross-task knowledge transfer, arguing that dense reward signals are a more direct and interpretable channel than architectural coupling. The information synchronization mechanism, which combines cross-attention-based task similarity with performance-weighted sampling, is a novel addition not present in prior MTRL or reward-shaping work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By unifying sparse-reward mitigation and multi-task knowledge transfer through a single mechanism (the CRA), CenRA offers a practical, backbone-agnostic framework that practitioners can slot into existing RL pipelines without redesigning network architectures. The demonstrated transferability to unseen tasks without retraining the CRA is particularly valuable in settings where new tasks emerge incrementally. Key open questions include the requirement for uniform state/action dimensions across tasks, the potential ceiling imposed on high-performing tasks by the performance-weighted synchronization, and whether the fixed α trade-off can be made adaptive without destabilizing training.
