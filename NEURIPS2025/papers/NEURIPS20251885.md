## Problem

Traditional Monge-Ampère-based mesh movement methods suffer from high computational cost due to repeated nonlinear PDE solves, while existing supervised learning approaches (M2N, UM2N) require pre-adapted reference meshes for training and exhibit poor zero-shot generalization across unseen PDEs and mesh geometries. No prior method achieves both unsupervised training and equation-agnostic generalization for r-adaptive mesh movement.

## Input / Output

The model consumes an initial mesh (node coordinates, connectivity) together with a flow-field solution at each node, but rather than processing the full mesh graph, it decomposes the problem into independent node patches—each consisting of a center node, its first-order neighbors, and their connecting edges. Node coordinates within each patch are 0-1 normalized to a unit square, and a scalar density feature derived from the Hessian norm is concatenated to form a 3D input per node (for 2D meshes). The output is the adapted position of the center node in each patch, denormalized back to the original mesh space; boundary nodes are excluded from adaptation. The process is iterative (up to 10 epochs), with Hessian values re-interpolated via Delaunay triangulation between epochs and termination triggered when a global equidistribution variance metric stops decreasing.

## Method

UGM2N introduces a node-patch representation inspired by vision Transformers, where each patch (a node plus its first-order neighbors) is processed independently and in parallel by a lightweight Graph Transformer (node/edge MLP encoders → 8-layer deform blocks with residual connections → node MLP decoder), enabling scale-invariant and translation-invariant adaptation without full-mesh context. The key training signal is the M-Uniform loss, an unsupervised objective that enforces local mesh equidistribution by minimizing the variance of m_K|K| (monitor-function density times element volume) across all elements surrounding each node patch; a law-of-total-variance argument shows this patch-level loss is a valid lower bound on the global equidistribution variance, so minimizing it promotes mesh uniformity in the metric space defined by the Hessian-based monitor function. Because the loss depends only on the current mesh geometry and flow field—never on a reference adapted mesh—training requires no pre-adapted data, and the model generalizes equation-agnostically to arbitrary PDEs, boundary conditions, and mesh topologies. At inference, iterative adaptation with dynamic termination (stopping when the global variance metric plateaus) progressively refines node positions while Delaunay-based interpolation updates the density field between epochs.

## Results

On 14 test cases spanning Poisson (7), Helmholtz (5), and Burgers (2) equations, UGM2N achieves the best error reduction (ER) in 9 out of 14 cases and zero mesh tangling across all cases. On Helmholtz, UGM2N achieves 14.11–16.98% ER, outperforming MA (10.29–15.60%) and UM2N (−2.61 to 10.86%), while M2N produces severely negative ER (−351.63% to −11.16%). On Poisson, UGM2N achieves 1.70–14.56% ER versus MA's −28.22 to 15.40% and M2N's −98.01 to 1.62%. On Burgers, M2N leads (44.82%, 29.93%) but UGM2N (32.22%, 30.19%) still substantially outperforms UM2N (0.46%, 22.76%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the traditional MA method [38], UGM2N achieves higher ER in 10 of 14 cases and avoids the frequent failures (negative ER) that MA exhibits on complex Poisson solutions (e.g., −28.22%, −25.23%) and non-convex geometries. Against M2N [9], UGM2N dominates on all Poisson and Helmholtz cases where M2N produces severely negative ER (as low as −98% and −351%), but M2N retains a lead on both Burgers cases (44.82% vs 32.22% and 29.93% vs 30.19%). Against UM2N [40], UGM2N wins in 12 of 14 cases, with particularly large margins on Helmholtz (e.g., 14.09% vs −2.61% on cos(2πy)cos(4πx)) and Poisson (e.g., 9.00% vs −5.59% on the Gaussian-sum solution). Notably, UGM2N produces zero mesh tangling across all 14 cases, while MA shows a 6.63% tangling ratio on non-convex meshes and M2N/UM2N can produce distorted elements in complex flow scenarios.

## Relation to Prior Work

UGM2N departs from the supervised paradigm of M2N (Song et al., 2022) and UM2N (Zhang et al., 2024), which train on pre-adapted meshes generated by Monge-Ampère (MA) solvers, by formulating mesh movement as an unsupervised optimization of a physics-derived equidistribution loss. Conceptually, it repurposes the ViT patch idea for mesh nodes—processing localized neighborhoods independently rather than the full mesh—and grounds the training objective in the metric-space equidistribution condition that underlies MA-based adaptation, thereby inheriting the physics of the traditional approach without solving the MA PDE. The node-patch representation and the variance-based M-Uniform loss are the two key departures: the former enables scale-invariant, parallelizable local adaptation, while the latter provides a theoretically valid lower bound on the global equidistribution metric (App. B.2).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for expensive MA-generated reference meshes, UGM2N removes a major practical bottleneck in deploying learned mesh adaptation to new PDEs or complex geometries where pre-adapted data is unavailable. The 807/1000 positive-ER ratio on random polygonal domains (vs. 110 for MA and 245 for UM2N) suggests the approach is robustly generalizable, though the regularity assumption behind the loss (near-uniform node degree) and the fixed-boundary-node strategy remain open constraints. Key future directions include learned boundary constraints, rotation invariance, 3D extension, and GPU-accelerated Delaunay interpolation to close the I/O bottleneck identified in the scalability analysis.
