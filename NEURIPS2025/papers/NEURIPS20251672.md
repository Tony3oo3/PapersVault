## Problem

3D scene reconstruction from in-the-wild images is degraded by inconsistent lighting and transient distractors (pedestrians, vehicles), yet existing methods rely on heuristic strategies—weakly supervised appearance embeddings or hand-crafted outlier filtering—that produce unstable, artifact-prone reconstructions. The paper identifies that these artifacts are stochastic across training runs, a property no prior method explicitly exploits for robustness.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251672/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multiple posed RGB images with COLMAP-derived camera poses, along with precomputed SAM semantic segmentations, COLMAP stereo correspondence maps, and DINOv2 feature maps used for mask construction. It outputs a 3D Gaussian Splatting scene representation (centroids, covariances, opacities, SH coefficients, plus per-Gaussian and per-view appearance embeddings) that renders novel views in real time. The base model is Mip-Splatting, and appearance modeling via a three-layer MLP (hidden size 128) adjusts Gaussian colors per view to handle illumination variation.

## Method

AsymGS trains two 3DGS models in parallel with different view sampling orders and enforces a mutual L1 consistency loss on their intrinsic (non-appearance-adjusted) renderings, so that true static geometry is reinforced while stochastic artifacts diverge and cancel. To prevent confirmation bias, each model is trained with a distinct mask: a Multi-Cue Adaptive Mask (hard binary mask combining SAM segmentation, COLMAP stereo match counts, pixel-level L1 residuals, and DINOv2 feature residuals) and a Self-Supervised Soft Mask (learnable 0–1 mask regressed toward DINOv2 cosine similarity between rendered and ground-truth features). A progressive warm-up delays the consistency loss until both models have stable geometry. For efficiency, a Dynamic EMA Proxy variant replaces the second model with an EMA copy that handles Gaussian cloning, splitting, and pruning, paired with an alternating masking strategy that switches between the two masks each iteration to preserve divergent training signals.

## Results

On the NeRF On-the-go dataset, AsymGS (GS-GS) achieves 24.34 dB PSNR / 0.825 SSIM / 0.150 LPIPS on high-occlusion scenes, outperforming the best baseline HybridGS (23.05 / 0.768 / 0.204) by ~1.3 dB PSNR. On PhotoTourism, GS-GS reaches 28.56 dB PSNR on Brandenburg Gate vs. 27.77 for the previous SOTA WildGaussian, while the EMA-GS variant cuts training time from 7.2 h to 2.9 h with only a 0.06 dB PSNR drop. On RobustNeRF, the method gains up to 1.9 dB PSNR over HybridGS on the complex Crab scene (36.18 vs. 35.17). The EMA-GS variant trains in 0.18 h per scene on NeRF On-the-go, matching HybridGS's speed while delivering substantially better quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AsymGS (GS-GS) achieves the best PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS across all three datasets and all occlusion levels, consistently outperforming every baseline. On NeRF On-the-go, it surpasses the strongest 3DGS-based competitor HybridGS [13] by 1.29 dB PSNR (high occlusion) and 1.05 dB (medium occlusion), and beats WildGaussian [11] by 1.31 dB in high-occlusion scenes. On RobustNeRF, the margin over HybridGS grows to 1.86 dB on the Crab scene (36.18 vs. 35.17 PSNR) where 150 distractor types are present, while on simpler single-distractor scenes (Statue, Android) the gap narrows to ~0.5 dB. On PhotoTourism, GS-GS improves over the previous SOTA WildGaussian [11] by 0.79 dB (Brandenburg Gate), 1.22 dB (Sacre Coeur), and 0.89 dB (Trevi Fountain) in PSNR, while EMA-GS achieves comparable quality (within 0.1–0.7 dB) at 2.5× lower training cost (2.9 h vs. 7.2 h). The EMA-GS variant is slightly behind GS-GS by ~0.2 dB PSNR on average, which the authors attribute to the EMA proxy's inherent confirmation bias and the loss of one active training stream. Notably, vanilla 3DGS [8] and Mip-Splatting [25] degrade severely under high occlusion (19.03 and 19.25 dB respectively), highlighting the necessity of the dual-model consistency mechanism.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Mip-Splatting as its base 3DGS implementation and adopts the per-view/per-Gaussian appearance embedding from WildGaussians to handle illumination variation. It departs from the dominant in-the-wild reconstruction paradigm (NeRF-W, NeRF On-the-go, RobustNeRF, GS-W, SpotlessSplats, HybridGS), which relies on heuristic filtering—thresholded residuals, hand-crafted rules, or weakly supervised appearance embeddings—by instead exploiting the empirical observation that reconstruction artifacts are stochastic across training runs. The dual-model consistency framework is conceptually related to EMA-based consistency training in semi-supervised learning (Mean Teacher, MoCo), but the Dynamic EMA proxy is a non-trivial adaptation to 3DGS's evolving Gaussian set (cloning, splitting, pruning), and the asymmetric masking strategy (multi-cue adaptive hard mask + self-supervised soft mask) has no direct precedent in prior 3DGS work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled alternative to the ad-hoc distractor-suppression strategies that have dominated in-the-wild 3DGS, and the Dynamic EMA proxy makes the dual-model approach practical by cutting training time by roughly a third while retaining most of the quality gain. This is relevant for practical applications like cultural heritage digitization and AR/VR where in-the-wild photo collections are the norm. Open questions include whether the stochastic-artifact property generalizes to truly dynamic scenes (moving objects rather than transient distractors), whether the multi-cue mask's dependence on SAM and COLMAP limits scalability, and whether the appearance modeling (inherited from WildGaussians) can be improved to handle fine-grained effects like specular highlights, which the authors acknowledge as a limitation.
