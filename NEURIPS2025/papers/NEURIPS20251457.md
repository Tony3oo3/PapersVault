## Problem

Energy-behavior-preserving numerical integrators (e.g., discrete gradient methods) guarantee exact energy conservation or dissipation but still accumulate numerical error over long simulations, and no existing approach can learn a more accurate variant from data. Data-driven methods such as Neural ODEs, PINNs, and Hamiltonian Neural Networks learn dynamics or flow maps but are not themselves numerical integrators, so they lack the rigorous structure-preservation and accuracy guarantees needed for long-time simulation. This paper asks whether one can learn, from trajectory data, a discrete-gradient-based integrator that is both structure-preserving and more accurate than any hand-designed scheme.

## Input / Output

Training data consists of one-step input-output pairs ((u^(n), h), u^(n+1)) where u is a state vector in R^d (or a spatially discretized PDE state) and h is the time step; pairs can be sampled along a single trajectory (flow data) or from randomly drawn states with random step sizes (irregular data). At inference, the trained integrator takes the current state u^(n) and step size h and produces u^(n+1) while exactly preserving the discrete energy conservation or dissipation law. The neural network itself receives the pair (u^(n), u^(n+1)) and outputs a d×d skew-symmetric matrix that corrects the reference discrete gradient.

## Method

The core insight (Theorem 4.3) is that every discrete gradient for a fixed energy H can be written as a fixed reference gradient (AVF or Itoh-Abe) plus a skew-symmetric matrix A(u,v) multiplied by (v−u), so the entire family of energy-preserving integrators is parameterized by a single skew-symmetric matrix-valued function. A multilayer perceptron with tanh activations is trained to approximate this matrix M_NN(u,v), and the training objective is the mean-squared residual between the discrete derivative (u^(n+1)−u^(n))/h and S·∇̄_NN H(u^(n),u^(n+1)). Universal approximation theorems (Theorems 4.5–4.6) guarantee that, on compact phase spaces, the learned integrator can approximate any energy-conserving or energy-dissipative discrete-gradient integrator to arbitrary precision. The resulting scheme is at least first-order accurate and second-order if M_NN is symmetric in its arguments, and it inherits the exact discrete energy law from the skew-symmetry of S and M_NN by construction.

## Results

On the Hénon-Heiles system, the proposed method achieves a maximum error of 9.53×10⁻³ at T=100 steps, compared to 1.50 for PINNs and 1.43×10⁻¹ for the best TRS-ODEN variant (λ=0.5). On the double pendulum, the maximum error is 6.18×10⁻³ versus 2.38×10⁻¹ (PINNs) and 1.24×10⁻¹ (TRS-ODEN). For the Duffing oscillator, the error is 1.12×10⁻¹ versus 4.50 (PINNs) and 5.12 (TRS-ODEN). In all cases, the proposed method outperforms baselines by 1–2 orders of magnitude, with the gap widening substantially at longer time horizons (T=50, 100).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the AVF discrete gradient method (Quispel & McLaren, 2008) and the Itoh-Abe method (Itoh & Abe, 1988), the proposed learned integrator consistently produces smaller global errors across all tested systems (Hénon-Heiles, two-body, double pendulum, dissipative pendulum, Duffing, KdV), while its total computational time (including data generation and training) remains below that of the 4th-order composition-based variants of these methods. Against SympNets (Jin et al., 2020), the proposed method yields trajectories closer to the ground truth on the Hénon-Heiles system, and unlike SympNets it is a true numerical integrator with guaranteed energy conservation/dissipation regardless of training quality. Against PINNs (Raissi et al., 2019), the proposed method achieves 1–2 orders of magnitude lower maximum error at all time checkpoints (e.g., 9.53×10⁻³ vs. 1.50 on Hénon-Heiles at T=100), with the gap widening dramatically as the simulation horizon increases. Against TRS-ODEN (Huh et al., 2020), the proposed method similarly dominates: at T=100 on the double pendulum the error is 6.18×10⁻³ versus 1.24×10⁻¹ for the best TRS-ODEN variant, and on the Duffing oscillator it is 1.12×10⁻¹ versus 5.12. The key structural advantage is that the proposed method is a discrete gradient integrator by construction, so energy conservation or dissipation is preserved exactly at every step, whereas PINNs and TRS-ODEN approximate the continuous flow and accumulate uncontrolled long-term drift.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of geometric numerical integration and data-driven learning, building directly on the discrete gradient method framework (McLachlan et al. 1999; Quispel & McLaren 2008 for AVF; Itoh & Abe 1988). The key conceptual departure is the full characterization of the space of all discrete gradients (Theorem 4.3) and the stronger result that every energy-preserving integrator for Hamiltonian systems is a discrete gradient scheme (Theorem 4.4), which then enables a neural network parameterization with universal approximation guarantees. Unlike SympNets or PINNs, which learn flow maps or approximate solutions, this work learns a genuine numerical integrator whose energy behavior is preserved by construction rather than by loss-function regularization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The universal approximation theorems guarantee that the learned integrator can approximate any energy-preserving/dissipative integrator, providing a principled and complete search space rather than ad-hoc coefficient tuning. Practically, the method outperforms 4th-order composition methods in accuracy while being computationally cheaper, which is notable for long-time simulations of chaotic systems. Limitations include the requirement of a known Hamiltonian structure (H and S must be specified), the 1st/2nd-order accuracy ceiling, and the training data overhead; open questions include extension to other invariants (symplecticity, momentum), adaptive step-size strategies, and scaling to high-dimensional or multi-invariant systems.
