## Problem

Existing extractive video summarization methods stitch together source clips but produce disjointed outputs lacking a coherent narrative, while abstractive methods generate smooth stories yet cannot insert actual video clips from the source to ground the narrative. Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) approaches further cannot quote multimodal materials and embed exact quotes into their outputs, sometimes fabricating or misattributing content. The paper addresses the gap of producing short teaser videos that simultaneously maintain a coherent narrative and embed verbatim video insertions (e.g., interview clips) extracted from a long source video.

## Input / Output

The system takes a long documentary video (typically 20–60 minutes from DW Documentary, PBS, or National Geographic) as input. The video is first processed through WhisperX for time-aligned ASR with speaker diarization, yielding a timestamped, speaker-labeled transcript; non-narrator segments are segmented into a pool of quotable interview clips. The output is a short teaser video (~76 seconds for ground truth) consisting of a coherent narrative script with synthesized TTS narration, interval-matched visuals from the source, and embedded interview video clips with their original audio, where each <QUOTE> placeholder in the script is fulfilled by a retrieved clip selected from the candidate pool.

## Method

REGen is a two-stage retrieval-embedded generation (REG) framework. In stage 1, LLaMA is instruction-fine-tuned on paired teaser–documentary scripts to generate a story script with explicit quote placeholders: REGen-DQ uses <SOQ>/<EOQ> tokens wrapping the exact quoted text, while REGen-IDQ uses a single <QUOTE> token marking an indirect quote position. In stage 2 (for REGen-IDQ), a BART encoder-decoder is jointly trained with a multi-task objective combining masked language modeling (to infill the <QUOTE> placeholder conditioned on surrounding narration) and a contrastive retrieval loss (to align a special <SUM> token's hidden state with the correct candidate clip embedding). At inference, the <SUM> embedding serves as a query to retrieve the best-matching video clip from the candidate pool via a clip fitness measure (cosine similarity), where candidate embeddings can be text-only (QuoteRetriever-T) or fused text+visual via a learnable two-layer MLP (QuoteRetriever-TV). This separation of the 'where to quote' decision (LLM script generation) from the 'what to quote' decision (multimodal retrieval) is the key architectural insight that enables coherent narrative with grounded multimodal insertions.

## Results

On the documentary teaser generation task (DocumentaryNet test set), REGen-IDQ-TV achieves the highest subjective scores among all methods: coherence 3.29/5, alignment 3.30/5, and realness 3.05/5, outperforming both the extractive A2Summ baseline (2.72, 2.87, 2.67) and the abstractive TeaserGen baseline (3.22, 2.92, 2.86). In objective evaluation, REGen-IDQ-TV produces teasers of 81 seconds with a 9.70% repetition rate and 19.86% scene change rate, closely matching the ground-truth teaser (76 s, 7.86% REP, 27.60% SCR), while TeaserGen suffers from 21.38% repetition. For quote retrieval, QuoteRetriever-T achieves Recall@10 of 30.00% and an insertion-effectiveness score of 3.56/5, substantially outperforming GPT-4o infilling (22.50% Recall@10, 2.48/5 effectiveness).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the extractive baseline A2Summ [4], REGen-IDQ-TV wins decisively on all four subjective dimensions: coherence (3.29 vs. 2.72), alignment (3.30 vs. 2.87), realness (3.05 vs. 2.67), and interview effectiveness (3.25 vs. 3.07), confirming that stitching raw clips without a generated narrative produces disjointed, low-coherence output. Against the abstractive baseline TeaserGen [11], REGen-IDQ-TV achieves higher coherence (3.29 vs. 3.22), substantially higher alignment (3.30 vs. 2.92), and higher realness (3.05 vs. 2.86), while also providing the unique capability of inserting real interview clips that TeaserGen cannot. In objective teaser-generation metrics, REGen-IDQ-TV's repetition rate (9.70%) and scene change rate (19.86%) are much closer to the ground truth (7.86%, 27.60%) than TeaserGen's (21.38%, 22.61%), indicating less repetitive and more natural pacing. In the quote-retrieval sub-task, the proposed QuoteRetriever-T outperforms GPT-4o infilling by 7.5 percentage points on Recall@10 (30.00% vs. 22.50%) and by over 1 point on insertion effectiveness (3.56 vs. 2.48), showing that a purpose-trained encoder-decoder retriever is more effective than prompting a general-purpose LLM to fill quotation placeholders.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends TeaserGen [11] (same first author, same DocumentaryNet dataset) by adding a retrieval-embedded generation stage that inserts actual source video clips into a generated narrative, bridging the gap between extractive methods (A2Summ, LfVS, TaleSumm) that produce disjointed splices and abstractive methods (TeaserGen, VTSUM-BLIP) that cannot quote raw footage. Conceptually, it adapts the RAG paradigm [19–21] to the multimodal setting: a fine-tuned LLaMA generates a script with <QUOTE> placeholders, and a BART-based encoder-decoder jointly performs masked infilling and contrastive retrieval to select the best supporting clip from a candidate pool. The key departure from prior RAG work is that the 'retrieved' content is a full video segment with original audio, not a text passage, and it is embedded verbatim into the output rather than merely conditioning generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a genuine gap in video editing: no prior system can simultaneously produce a coherent narrative and embed authentic source clips as grounding evidence, which is critical for domains like journalism and education where factualness matters. However, the approach is tightly coupled to speaker diarization quality and the documentary domain; the generalizability study (Appendix K.5) shows the method is actually preferred less than TeaserGen on lecture videos (38% vs 62%), suggesting the quoting mechanism may not transfer to domains without natural interview segments. Open questions include whether the framework can operate without diarization (e.g., for lectures or news), whether scaling the training data improves the currently low retrieval recall (~5% Recall@1), and whether the LLaMA context-window limitation can be overcome to ground script generation in all candidate clips simultaneously.
