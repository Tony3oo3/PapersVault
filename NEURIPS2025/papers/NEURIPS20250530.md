## Problem

Academic poster generation—condensing an entire multi-page paper into a single, visually coherent page—remains substantially more challenging than slide generation due to long multimodal context, tight text-visual interleaving, and strict spatial constraints that prevent overflow. No prior benchmark, evaluation metric suite, or dedicated multi-agent pipeline existed for this task, leaving the field without systematic ways to compare generated posters against author-designed ground truth.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250530/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system ingests a full scientific paper PDF (averaging ~22 pages, ~20K tokens, and ~22 figures with interleaved text, figures, and tables) and produces a single-page editable .pptx poster containing ~774 words and ~9 figures, representing a ~14.4× textual and ~2.6× figure compression. The output must maintain a logical reading order, spatial balance, and legibility within a fixed canvas, with all panels rendered as structured PPTX elements rather than a flat image.

## Method

PosterAgent is a top-down, visual-in-the-loop multi-agent pipeline with three stages. The Parser converts the PDF to Markdown via MARKER/Docling, then an LLM distills it into a structured asset library of section-level synopses and extracted figure/table crops. The Planner semantically matches each synopsis to its corresponding visual asset and generates a binary-tree layout (adapted from Qiang et al. 2017) that allocates panel bounding boxes by estimated content length while preserving reading order and aspect ratio. Finally, a Painter-Commenter loop refines each panel: the Painter distills content into bullet points and emits python-pptx rendering code, while the Commenter—a VLM using a zoom-in strategy and in-context reference examples (one overflow case, one ideal layout)—provides targeted feedback to correct text overflow and spatial misalignment until convergence. This design avoids the instability of direct coordinate prediction by LLMs and mitigates VLM hallucination in visual critique, achieving near-human poster quality with 87% fewer tokens than GPT-4o-based multi-agent baselines.

## Results

On the Paper2Poster benchmark (100 paper-poster pairs), PosterAgent-4o achieves an overall VLM-as-Judge score of 3.72/5, approaching the human-designed GT Poster (3.77) and substantially outperforming all automated baselines (4o-Image: 2.33, OWL-4o: 3.19, PPTAgent-4o: 2.11). On the novel PaperQuiz metric, PosterAgent-4o scores 116.13 (density-augmented), surpassing even the GT Poster (111.78), while the fully open-source PosterAgent-Qwen variant achieves 114.65 using 87% fewer tokens than OWL-4o. Notably, 4o-Image attains the highest visual similarity (0.76) but the worst perplexity (77.13), revealing that pixel-level generation produces visually appealing yet textually incoherent posters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against OWL-4o (a general-purpose PDF-to-HTML multi-agent system), PosterAgent-4o wins on every metric: overall VLM-as-Judge 3.72 vs. 3.19, PaperQuiz augmented score 116.13 vs. 100.80, and visual similarity 0.75 vs. 0.54, while consuming 72% fewer tokens (101.1K vs. 361.1K). Against PPTAgent-4o (a python-pptx-based slide generator), PosterAgent-4o dominates across the board: overall VLM-as-Judge 3.72 vs. 2.11, PaperQuiz 116.13 vs. 62.49, and figure relevance 0.24 vs. 0.16; PPTAgent's rigid template-filling mechanism frequently leaves placeholder text or large blank areas. The fully open-source PosterAgent-Qwen (Qwen-2.5-7B + Qwen-2.5-VL-7B) still outperforms both GPT-4o-powered baselines on PaperQuiz (114.65 vs. 100.80 for OWL-4o and 62.49 for PPTAgent-4o) and on information scores, while costing only $0.0045 per poster versus $1.79–$1.87 for the 4o-based baselines. The key aesthetic bottleneck across all methods is Engagement (all variants score below 3.0/5), indicating that current systems struggle to match the visual-semantics-driven design of human posters.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on automated visual design (slide generation via PPTAgent, D2S, AutoPresent; diffusion-based poster synthesis via Posta, GlyphDraw2) to the more demanding setting of single-page scientific poster generation from full-length papers. It departs from prior scientific poster systems (PosterBot, the 2017 probabilistic graphical model approach) by reframing the task as multimodal context compression and introducing a visual-in-the-loop multi-agent pipeline (Parser–Planner–Painter-Commenter) that uses code generation via python-pptx rather than pixel-level diffusion or rigid template filling. The evaluation framework builds on VLM-as-judge paradigms and CLIP-based similarity metrics but introduces the novel PaperQuiz protocol that simulates reader comprehension, and the benchmark itself is the first to pair papers with author-designed posters at scale (100 pairs from ICML/NeurIPS/ICLR).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Paper2Poster benchmark and its four-dimensional evaluation suite (visual quality, textual coherence, VLM-as-judge, PaperQuiz) provide the field with the first systematic way to measure poster generation quality, filling a gap where prior work lacked standardized evaluation. The finding that GPT-4o's direct image generation produces visually appealing but textually incoherent posters (PPL 77.13) while a fully open-source Qwen-based pipeline outperforms GPT-4o-driven multi-agent systems at 87% lower token cost ($0.005/poster) has direct practical implications for cost-sensitive deployment. Key open questions include closing the persistent Engagement gap (all methods score below 3/5), integrating external knowledge (community feedback, institutional branding), and enabling human-AI collaborative refinement loops. The sequential panel-processing bottleneck (~4.5 min/poster) also limits real-time or batch applications.
