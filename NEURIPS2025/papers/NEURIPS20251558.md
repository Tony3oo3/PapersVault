## Problem

Existing simplex-based generative models for discrete data (e.g., Statistical Flow Matching, Fisher Flow Matching, Bayesian Flow Networks) define trajectories over the full continuous simplex using geodesics, Riemannian optimal transport, or change-of-variable techniques, making them mathematically complex and difficult to scale to large-vocabulary or long-sequence tasks. This paper asks whether the core principle of gradual information growth on the simplex can be preserved with a substantially simpler formulation that avoids these heavy geometric constructions.

## Input / Output

The model takes one-hot encoded discrete sequences (character-level text, DNA base pairs, or protein amino acid sequences) and optionally a class label or promoter profile for conditional generation. Internally, the network operates on normalized candidate-set vectors (binary vectors over the vocabulary, normalized to lie on the simplex) rather than raw one-hot inputs, and the output is a discrete sequence produced by ancestral sampling from the all-one vector down to a one-hot vector. For large-vocabulary settings (e.g., OpenWebText with ~50K tokens), the paper identifies that the embedding dimension must exceed the vocabulary size for lossless simplex input representation, motivating wider-but-shallower transformer variants.

## Method

SLM reframes discrete generation as progressive candidate pruning: the forward process appends candidates via a multivariate Bernoulli chain from a one-hot vector (1 candidate) to an all-one vector (K candidates), with a scheduling function n(t)=exp(log K · t/T) controlling the expected number of active candidates. The reverse process prunes candidates by parameterizing each step as a Bernoulli distribution whose parameters interpolate between a constant ratio and the network's softmax prediction, constrained to only modify positions already active in the current candidate set. Crucially, transitions occur among simplex centroids (subspace vertices) rather than over the full continuous simplex, reducing degrees of freedom. To avoid gradient vanishing in the Bernoulli KL objective, the paper derives a simplified cross-entropy loss (L^simple) or a reweighted variant (L^weight) that scales gradients by 1/NN_θ, analogous to log-softmax optimization. A DiT backbone is used, and classifier-free guidance is implemented by linearly interpolating conditional and unconditional softmax outputs, projecting back onto the simplex when the guidance weight γ exceeds 1.

## Results

On Text8 (vocab size 27), SLM with the reweighted loss achieves ≤1.38 BPC, competitive with the best non-autoregressive discrete diffusion methods (MD4 ≤1.37, MDLM ≤1.39) and outperforming other simplex-based approaches (BFN ≤1.41, SFM 1.39). On DNA promoter design, SLM achieves the lowest MSE of 0.0265±0.0006, surpassing MDLM (0.028) and Fisher-Flow (0.029). For enhancer design, SLM achieves the best FBD without guidance (Mel: 2.2, Fly Brain: 4.4) and with classifier-free guidance reaches 1.4 (Mel) and 1.0 (Fly Brain), outperforming BFN CFG (2.3/2.3) and Dirichlet FM CFG (1.9/1.0). On protein design, the 38M-parameter SLM surpasses all diffusion and flow-matching baselines on pLDDT, ProGen2-nll, scPerplexity, and inner-TM, while remaining competitive with the much larger ESM2-150M model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On Text8, SLM (L^weight) at ≤1.38 BPC outperforms all other simplex-based methods (BFN ≤1.41, DFM ≤1.41, SFM 1.39) and is competitive with top discrete diffusion models (MDLM ≤1.39, SEDD Absorb ≤1.41), though it trails the best discrete diffusion (MD4 ≤1.37) and all autoregressive baselines (Transformer AR 1.23). On OpenWebText (vocab 50,257), SLM dramatically outperforms BFN (PPL 53.90 vs. 105.66; Gen-PPL 65.59 vs. 299.95) but still lags behind autoregressive (PPL 21.04) and discrete diffusion (MDLM PPL 23.08) models in likelihood, while achieving the best Gen-PPL among simplex-based methods. On DNA promoter design, SLM achieves the best MSE (0.0265) over all 10 baselines including MDLM (0.028), Fisher-Flow (0.029), and Dirichlet FM (0.034). On enhancer design, SLM without CFG beats BFN (FBD 2.2 vs. 3.3 on Mel; 4.4 vs. 10.8 on Fly Brain) and Dirichlet FM (5.3/15.1), and with CFG achieves the best scores on both datasets (1.4/1.0). For protein design, the 38M SLM surpasses EvoDiff-OADM, EvoDiff-D3PM, MDLM, UDLM, DFM-Mask, DFM-Uniform, and ESM1-43M on all four metrics (pLDDT, ProGen2-nll, scPerplexity, inner-TM), and is competitive with ESM2-150M despite being ~4x smaller.

## Relation to Prior Work

SLM sits at the intersection of discrete diffusion models (Austin et al., 2021; Sahoo et al., 2024) and simplex-based generative approaches (Graves et al., 2023; Cheng et al., 2024; Davis et al., 2024), inheriting the VLB training framework and the simplex representation of categorical data. Its key departure is restricting the generative trajectory to transitions among simplex centroids rather than the full continuous simplex, which eliminates the need for Riemannian optimal transport, Fisher-Rao geodesics, or change-of-variable techniques used by SFM, Fisher-Flow, and BFN. The simplified cross-entropy objective (Eq. 10–11) replaces the vocabulary-level MSE or Bernoulli KL losses of prior simplex methods, directly addressing gradient vanishing in high-dimensional settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By reducing degrees of freedom through centroid-restricted transitions, SLM offers a more scalable and interpretable alternative to existing simplex-based methods, with the practical benefit of a standard cross-entropy training objective that avoids the optimization pathologies of Bernoulli KL losses. The 38M-parameter model outperforming ESM2-150M on protein design metrics (pLDDT, ProGen2-nll, scPerplexity, inner-TM) suggests that non-autoregressive diffusion can be competitive with much larger masked language models for biological sequence generation. However, the paper is limited to text and biological sequences, with no evaluation on graphs or images, and the authors acknowledge that further theoretical analysis and engineering improvements are needed for large-scale real-world deployment. An open question is whether the centroid restriction generalizes to structured discrete data beyond sequences.
