## Problem

Federated Multi-Task Learning (FL-MTL) suffers from imbalanced client contributions and task interference when clients hold heterogeneous, non-IID data across multiple tasks. Traditional FL aggregation (e.g., FedAvg) uses fixed coefficients proportional to local sample sizes, which cannot capture task-specific variations or adapt to differing client relevance, leading to suboptimal in-domain and out-of-domain performance. No existing FL-MTL method provides a unified mechanism to jointly adjust both task-level and client-level aggregation weights in an adaptive, computationally efficient manner.

## Input / Output

Each of K clients holds a local dataset partitioned across T tasks (e.g., 7 NLP tasks: QASC, WikiQA, QuaRTz, PAWS, Winogrande, WSC, Story Cloze), fine-tuned via LoRA on T5-small (θ*) or T5-base (θ+) architectures. The method outputs a global multi-task agent model (θ_agent^G) along with two learned scalar hyperparameter vectors: task importance weights α (summing to 1) used to resample local training data, and client aggregation coefficients w (summing to 1) used to weight model updates during global aggregation. The reference and proxy models use the smaller θ* architecture while the agent uses the larger θ+, yielding an Agent-to-Proxy parameter ratio of up to 15×.

## Method

FedRAM is a three-step sequential framework that progressively learns two sets of reweighting hyperparameters before training the final model. Step 1 trains and freezes lightweight local reference models (θ_ref) on each client's data to establish a local performance baseline. Step 2 trains a federated proxy model (θ_proxy, same small architecture) using an excess-loss objective—defined as the positive difference between proxy and reference task-wise losses—combined with group distributionally robust optimization, and iteratively updates task weights α_τ via exponential scaling (α_τ ← α_τ · e^{η_τ · ℓ_exc^τ}). Step 3 trains the larger agent model (θ_agent) using the tuned α for proportional data resampling and client weights w_k (adjusted via differential loss between local and global proxy models) for reweighted FedAvg-style aggregation. This hierarchical design decouples cheap proxy-based hyperparameter tuning from expensive agent training, reducing total computational cost by ~15% in convergence rounds while improving global F1 by at least 3% over baselines.

## Results

On the primary NLP multi-task benchmark (7 tasks, 10 clients), FedRAM achieves a Global F1-score of 75.94, Local F1-score of 79.62 (bottom decile: 73.21), and ID/OOD scores of 72.89/76.32. FedRAM attains the best global F1 in 4 out of 7 individual tasks and the highest bottom-decile local score by a wide margin (+6.3 over the next best). In terms of computational cost, FedRAM completes training in 12,060 s with 18 convergence rounds, compared to 13,774 s / 21 rounds for FedAvg and 40,989 s / 30 rounds for FedBone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedRAM outperforms all 10 baselines on Global F1 (75.94 vs. best baseline FedMTL at 74.12, +1.82) and on the bottom-decile Local F1 (73.21 vs. FedRep at 66.89, +6.32), indicating substantially better protection for the weakest clients. On OOD generalization, FedRAM leads with 76.32, edging out FedMTL (75.64) by 0.68 points. The one metric where FedRAM trails is In-Domain accuracy: FedMTL achieves 76.01 ID vs. FedRAM's 72.89, a gap the authors attribute to FedMTL's skewed PAWS and Story Cloze performance. Against the standard FL baselines (FedAvg, FedProx, Ditto, MOON, DBE), FedRAM gains at least 3% on Global F1 and 10+ points on bottom-decile Local F1. Among FL-MTL-specific methods, FedRAM beats FedMTL on global F1 and bottom decile but concedes ID; it beats FedBone by 4.75 points on Global F1 and 11.6 points on bottom-decile Local F1. On computational cost, FedRAM (12,060 s, 18 rounds) is the cheapest among all compared methods, versus FedBone (40,989 s) and DBE (34,173 s).

## Relation to Prior Work

FedRAM extends the FL-MTL line of work initiated by MOCHA and MIFA, and builds directly on FedAvg's aggregation loop and the distributionally robust optimization framework of Sagawa et al. for the proxy model's excess-loss training. Its key conceptual departure from prior FL-MTL methods (FedMTL, FedBone, FedDAT) is the explicit decoupling of hyperparameter tuning (task weights α and client weights w) into a lightweight proxy model, rather than folding reweighting into the main training loop or relying on fixed sample-size-proportional coefficients. The adaptive aggregation weights draw inspiration from model-merging literature (Adamerging, TIES-merging) and contribution-aware FL, but FedRAM is the first to unify task-level and client-level reweighting within a single three-stage federated pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FedRAM offers a practical recipe for FL-MTL practitioners who face heterogeneous clients with imbalanced data volumes: the proxy-agent separation means hyperparameter tuning costs only ~15% of total compute, and the resulting reweighted aggregation yields consistent 3%+ gains over strong baselines across both ID and OOD metrics. However, the convergence proof relies on the strong (and empirically unverified) assumption that client gradient variance is proportional to differential loss, and the framework assumes full client participation every round, limiting applicability to truly asynchronous or dropout-prone deployments. Open questions include whether the A/P ratio and exponential scaling factors transfer across domains without re-tuning, and whether the approach extends to settings with more than ~10 tasks or highly dynamic task sets.
