## Problem

Despite Adam being the de facto optimizer in modern deep learning, its implicit bias near the minimizer manifold has lacked rigorous theoretical characterization. While SGD's slow SDE framework shows it minimizes tr(H) under label noise, no comparable analysis existed for Adam or other adaptive gradient methods, leaving open how Adam's adaptive per-parameter learning rates alter the sharpness measure being implicitly minimized and whether this leads to qualitatively different solutions.

## Input / Output

The analysis takes a C⁵-smooth loss function with a C^∞ minimizer manifold Γ, a gradient noise covariance Σ(θ), and optimizer hyperparameters (β₁, β₂, learning rate η) as input. It produces a coupled slow SDE on the manifold tracking both the iterate position ζ(t) and the second-moment vector v(t), from which an explicit implicit regularizer is extracted. Under the label noise condition (Σ = α∇²L), the SDE reduces to an ODE whose fixed points characterize the preferred minimizer, yielding tr(Diag(H)^{1/2}) for Adam versus tr(H) for SGD.

## Method

The paper generalizes the slow SDE framework from SGD to a broad class of adaptive gradient methods (AGMs) by introducing a preconditioner flow projection operator Φ_S that replaces the standard gradient flow projection, accounting for the adaptive per-parameter scaling. The resulting slow SDE is a coupled system: the manifold coordinate ζ(t) evolves via an adaptive semi-gradient descent driven by a state-dependent projection P_{ζ,S(t)}, while the preconditioner v(t) follows its own drift equation with rate c = (1-β₂)/η². A key technical assumption is the '2-scheme' (1-β₂ = Θ(η²)), which ensures the preconditioner evolves on the same O(η⁻²) timescale as the manifold dynamics, making both motions trackable simultaneously. Under label noise, the SDE collapses to an ODE, and the implicit regularizer for Adam simplifies to tr(Diag(H)^{1/2}), which provably yields sparser solutions than SGD's tr(H) in diagonal linear network settings. The framework extends uniformly to RMSProp, Adam-mini, Adalayer, and Shampoo, with a tunable variant AdamE-λ that interpolates between SGD and Adam biases.

## Results

The main theoretical result (Theorem 4.1) establishes that the slow SDE for AGMs approximates Adam's projected dynamics on the minimizer manifold over Õ(η⁻²) steps with error Õ(η^{0.25}) for any C³ test function. Under the label noise condition, Lemma B.1 proves that Adam's implicit regularizer is tr(Diag(H)^{1/2}), in contrast to SGD's tr(H). Empirically, in sparse linear regression with diagonal linear networks, Adam recovers the sparse ground truth with fewer training samples than SGD, while in deep matrix factorization Adam does not outperform SGD, consistent with the theory that tr(Diag(H)^{1/2}) does not favor low-rank solutions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The central comparison is between Adam and SGD (Blanc et al., 2020) in the overparameterized label-noise setting. Theoretically, SGD minimizes tr(H) while Adam minimizes tr(Diag(H)^{1/2}), a strictly different sharpness measure that is less sensitive to large individual Hessian eigenvalues. In the sparse linear regression experiment with diagonal linear networks, this distinction is decisive: Adam's tr(Diag(H)^{1/2}) regularizer aligns with the sparsity structure of the ground truth, enabling Adam to recover the sparse solution with fewer data points than SGD. However, in the deep matrix factorization setting (Appendix C), Adam does not outperform SGD, because minimizing tr(Diag(H)^{1/2}) does not preferentially select low-rank solutions—the very property that SGD's tr(H) regularizer exploits in that architecture. The paper thus provides a concrete, provable separation: Adam wins when the problem structure aligns with diagonal Hessian sparsity, and loses (or ties) when the relevant structure is low-rank.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the slow SDE framework introduced by Li et al. (2021) for SGD and generalized by Gu et al. (2023) for local SGD to a broad class of adaptive gradient methods (AGMs), including Adam, RMSProp, Adam-mini, Adalayer, and Shampoo. The key technical departure is the introduction of a preconditioner flow projection Φ_S that replaces the fixed gradient flow projection, accounting for the state-dependent adaptive learning rates that break the rotational equivariance available in SGD. Prior attempts at analyzing Adam's implicit bias (Ma et al. 2023, Cattaneo et al. 2024) were either restricted to 2D losses, relied on quasistatic approximations, or analyzed a shorter time horizon; this work provides the first rigorous O(η⁻²)-scale characterization of Adam's sharpness-reducing dynamics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes a clean, provable separation between Adam and SGD: under label noise, Adam minimizes tr(Diag(H)^{1/2}) while SGD minimizes tr(H), yielding qualitatively different preferred minimizers (sparser for Adam in aligned settings). The unified AGM framework provides a template for understanding how any adaptive optimizer's preconditioner shapes its implicit regularizer, with practical implications for optimizer selection in structured problems. Key limitations include the 2-scheme assumption (1−β₂ = O(η²)), the restriction to local analysis near a smooth minimizer manifold, and the exclusion of weight decay; extending to intermediate β₂ scalings, beyond the local neighborhood, and to decoupled weight decay (AdamW) are identified as important open directions.
