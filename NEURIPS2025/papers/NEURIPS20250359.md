## Problem

Current VLMs can interpret individual images well but fail to link fine-grained visual cues across multiple images, such as tracking object identity under different views or detecting subtle state changes. Extending rule-based reinforcement learning (e.g., GRPO) to the visual domain is a natural fix, but it requires manually curated question-answer pairs with verifiable answers, which is especially difficult for tasks involving fine-grained visual details and complex cross-image logic.

## Input / Output

During training, the model receives a triplet of RGB images—two random-crop/resize augmentations of the same source image and one augmentation of a visually similar but distinct image—along with a text prompt asking it to judge same/different for each pair. The model outputs a chain-of-thought reasoning block enclosed in think tags followed by a compact T/F string (e.g., 'TFT') in answer tags, one letter per image pair. At inference time, the same CoT-then-answer format is used for open-ended multi-image questions on benchmarks like VLM2-Bench and MuirBench.

## Method

MiCo constructs self-supervised contrastive triplets by pairing two augmented views of one image with an augmented view of a similar-but-different image, sourced from video frames (filtered by SSIM) and image-editing datasets (filtered by MSE), so that the 'same vs. different' label is determined by the data construction itself rather than human annotation. The model is prompted to generate a reasoning trajectory and a T/F verdict for each pair, and is optimized with rule-based RL using a format reward plus an accuracy reward (all three pair comparisons must be correct). The key training innovation is Augmented GRPO: rollouts are sampled from weakly augmented inputs (where valid CoTs are easier to produce) but the policy is updated on strongly augmented inputs, transferring reliable reasoning behavior to harder visual contexts. This design, combined with diverse prompt templates and both pair- and triplet-level questions, enables the model to learn fine-grained visual comparison without any human-annotated QA pairs, and the acquired reasoning generalizes to broader multi-image and single-image understanding tasks.

## Results

On VLM2-Bench, MiCo-7B-CoT achieves an overall average of 61.06, outperforming the base Qwen2.5-VL-7B (54.82) by +12.93 points and surpassing GPT-4o (60.36) by 0.70 points without any human-annotated QA pairs. The largest gains appear on General Tracking (+24.20 over base) and Object Grouping (+22.00), indicating strong improvement in cross-image cue linking. On broader general vision benchmarks (Table 3), MiCo also improves over the base model on all six tasks, with the largest gains on MuirBench (+2.10) and BLINK (+1.69), though it trails task-specific RL methods like ThinkLite-VL on HallusionBench (69.61 vs. 72.97) and MMStar (65.60 vs. 66.80).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MiCo-7B-CoT (61.06 overall) outperforms all 11 baselines on VLM2-Bench, including the strongest prior method VLAA-Thinker-7B (58.49, +2.57) and the proprietary GPT-4o (60.36, +0.70). Against the base Qwen2.5-VL-7B (54.82), MiCo gains +12.93 points, with the most dramatic improvements on General Tracking (+24.20) and Object Grouping (+22.00), confirming that contrastive training specifically targets cross-image comparison. Among RL-based competitors built on the same Qwen2.5-VL-7B backbone, MiCo beats MM-Eureka-7B by +3.82, ThinkLite-VL-7B by +5.27, and NoisyRollout-7B by +10.98. On general single-image benchmarks (Table 3), MiCo improves over the base model on all six tasks but trails ThinkLite-VL-7B on HallusionBench (69.61 vs. 72.97) and MMStar (65.60 vs. 66.80), and MM-Eureka-7B on MMMU (54.77 vs. 54.11 — actually MiCo wins here by +0.66), suggesting that task-specific RL supervision still has an edge on domain-heavy single-image reasoning. Notably, MiCo's advantage is most pronounced on correspondence-style sub-tasks (Visual Retrieval: 71.23 vs. 68.83 for VLAA-Thinker; Semantic Correspondence: 41.72 vs. 34.53), while it lags on Scene Understanding and Forensic Detection where curated QA supervision provides stronger signal.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate research threads: rule-based RL for VLM reasoning (GRPO, MM-Eureka, NoisyRollout, ThinkLite) and self-supervised contrastive visual representation learning (SimCLR, MoCo). All prior RL-based visual reasoning methods require manually curated QA pairs to compute rewards, which is prohibitively expensive for fine-grained multi-image comparison. MiCo departs from this by repurposing the contrastive learning principle—images as their own supervision—to generate reward signals from inherent visual constraints (same vs. similar-but-different), eliminating the need for any human-annotated question-answer pairs. The Augmented GRPO strategy (sampling rollouts under weak augmentations, optimizing under strong ones) is a novel extension of standard GRPO that addresses the difficulty of producing valid CoTs on heavily augmented inputs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is a scalable, annotation-free training recipe for multi-image reasoning: if you have videos or image-editing pairs, you can generate contrastive triplets and train a VLM to reason across images without writing a single QA pair. This significantly lowers the barrier to improving multi-image understanding, which remains a persistent weakness of current VLMs. However, the generalization is uneven—gains are strong on correspondence and comparison tasks but modest or absent on face identity, visual math, and spatial/depth reasoning, suggesting the contrastive signal captures a specific subset of visual reasoning skills. Open questions include whether richer contrastive structures (e.g., multi-step state changes, 3D geometry) could extend the approach to these underperforming domains, and whether the technique transfers to larger models or different VLM architectures.
