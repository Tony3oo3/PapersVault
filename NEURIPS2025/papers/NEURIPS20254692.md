## Problem

Low-rank compression of neural networks typically increases sensitivity to adversarial perturbations relative to full-rank baselines, creating a fundamental tension between the compactness needed for edge deployment and the robustness required for reliable operation. Existing robustness-enhancing methods either impose heavy computational overhead during training/inference or degrade clean accuracy, and prior low-rank robustness approaches (e.g., spectrum clamping) require careful rank selection as a hyperparameter. The paper asks whether one can simultaneously achieve high compression (>90%), preserve clean accuracy, and improve adversarial robustness without extra inference cost.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images at dataset-native resolutions (256×256 for UCM, 32×32 for CIFAR-10, 224×224 for ImageNet-1k) and outputs class labels for 21, 10, or 1000 classes respectively. The trained model is stored as per-layer low-rank factorizations W = USVᵀ with orthonormal U, V ∈ ℝ^{n×r} and a small r×r coefficient matrix S, achieving up to 94% parameter reduction without ever materializing the full n×n weight matrix. The method applies uniformly to convolutional layers (via Tucker factorization of the kernel tensor) and transformer attention/MLP layers, with rank r determined adaptively by a singular-value threshold rather than fixed a priori.

## Method

RobustDLRT augments the dynamical low-rank training (DLRT) scheme with a differentiable spectral regularizer R(S) = ‖SᵀS − α_S²I‖ that penalizes the spread of squared singular values of the r×r coefficient matrix S, thereby driving the layer condition number κ(S) toward 1. The training loop follows a three-step geometric procedure: (1) basis augmentation by orthonormalizing [U | ∇_U L] and [V | ∇_V L] to double the latent rank, (2) latent-space coefficient updates via projected gradient descent on the augmented 2r×2r matrix Ŝ with the regularizer term −β∇_{Ŝ}R(Ŝ), and (3) retraction back to rank r via truncated SVD with a singular-value threshold that enables rank adaptivity. Because U and V remain orthonormal at all times, the regularizer acts only on the small S matrix (O(r³) cost), and the condition number bound κ(S) ≤ exp(R(S)/(√2·ζ_r(S)²)) provides an explicit, interpretable guarantee on adversarial sensitivity. The approach is model- and data-agnostic, compatible with adversarial training, and adds only ~3% training overhead over unregularized DLRT.

## Results

On UCM with VGG16, RobustDLRT achieves 94.61% clean accuracy at 95.84% compression, matching the uncompressed baseline (94.40%), while recovering or exceeding baseline adversarial accuracy under ℓ²-FGSM (78.68% vs 76.40% at ε=0.1) and nearly doubling baseline accuracy under Mixup (90.33% vs 37.25% at ε=0.1). On CIFAR-10 with VGG16, RobustDLRT reaches 89.49% clean accuracy at 94.18% compression and surpasses the baseline under ℓ¹-FGSM at all tested ε values (e.g., 66.02% vs 64.66% at ε=0.004). On ImageNet-1k with ViT-32l, RobustDLRT achieves 72.25% Top-1 clean accuracy at 57.98% compression and gains +9.2 points Top-1 over the baseline under ℓ²-FGSM ε=0.3 (25.24% vs 16.03%), with only ~3% training overhead.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RobustDLRT consistently outperforms non-regularized DLRT on adversarial accuracy at comparable compression rates (e.g., 78.68% vs 72.41% under ℓ²-FGSM ε=0.1 on UCM/VGG16), confirming that the condition-number regularizer is the key differentiator. Against CondLR [35], which enforces hard spectral clamping at 50% compression, RobustDLRT achieves similar or better adversarial accuracy (66.02% vs 60.19% at ε=0.004 on CIFAR-10) while compressing to 94.35%—nearly double the compression ratio. LoRA [17] and SVD Prune [51] at 50% compression are substantially outperformed: RobustDLRT gains 17+ points adversarial accuracy at ε=0.004 (66.02% vs 48.86% and 47.77%) while compressing 44 percentage points more. The zero-compression Riemannian methods (Cayley SGD [25], Projected SGD [1]) achieve 74.46–74.55% at ε=0.002, which RobustDLRT matches (78.72%) at 94.35% compression. On the harder ℓ²-PGD attack (Table 7), CondLR at 50% compression still leads (54.58% at ε=0.3), but RobustDLRT at 94% compression (16.59%) remains competitive with the uncompressed baseline (29.93%) and clearly ahead of DLRT (15.04%), indicating the regularizer's benefit is most pronounced at higher compression ratios.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Dynamical Low-Rank Training (DLRT) framework [38] and its rank-adaptive, orthogonality-preserving variant [36] by introducing a spectral regularizer R(S) = ‖SᵀS − α²_S I‖ applied to the small r×r coefficient matrix of each layer. Conceptually, it generalizes the soft orthogonal regularizer [48] from enforcing near-orthogonality to enforcing near-equal singular values (i.e., controlling the condition number), and positions itself as a 'soft' alternative to the hard spectrum-clamping approach of CondLR [35]. The method deliberately departs from LoRA-style simultaneous gradient descent, which does not preserve orthogonality of U and V, by building on the two-step augment-truncate scheme of [36] that guarantees orthonormal bases at every step.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical tension for edge deployment: low-rank compression typically degrades adversarial robustness, yet resource-constrained devices (UAVs, sensors) need both compactness and resilience. By showing that a cheap regularizer on the r×r core can recover or exceed full-rank adversarial accuracy at >94% compression with only ~3% training overhead, the work offers a drop-in enhancement to existing DLRT pipelines. Open questions include whether the Mixup improvement (attributed to scale-invariance robustness) generalizes, whether the orthogonality requirement limits applicability to non-orthogonal training schemes, and how the method behaves under stronger iterative attacks (PGD) at very high compression rates where the condition number bound may become loose.
