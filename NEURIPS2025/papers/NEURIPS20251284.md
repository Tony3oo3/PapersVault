## Problem

The KV cache in decoder-only LLMs grows linearly with sequence length, making long-context inference (e.g., 128K tokens) infeasible on resource-constrained GPUs. Existing mitigations—quantization, token eviction, and CPU offloading—each introduce either numerical precision loss, risk of discarding later-critical tokens, or substantial PCIe transfer latency. The paper addresses how to preserve exact attention outputs while drastically reducing both GPU memory footprint and cross-device data movement.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a long text prompt (up to 128K tokens) and frozen pretrained LLM weights (LLaMA-3-8B-1M, Qwen2.5-7B/14B/32B, Mistral-7B, Phi-3-mini), operating in bfloat16 with float32 for small matrix inverses. It produces autoregressively generated tokens while maintaining a compressed KV cache: rank-r low-rank factors (A_Q, A_K ∈ R^{l×r}) stored persistently, plus a small GPU-resident window of full-precision KV pairs (top-k active tokens + a fixed number of recent 'lite' tokens), with the full KV cache residing on CPU and fetched on demand.

## Method

LRQK is a two-stage inference framework built on the observation that the QK^T attention matrix in decoder-only transformers admits a close rank-r approximation. During prefill, Q and K are jointly factorized into compact factors (A_Q, B_Q) and (A_K, B_K) via Block Coordinate Descent on a Lagrangian that simultaneously minimizes the attention-score approximation error and the individual Q/K reconstruction errors, with all inverses operating on small r×r matrices. During decode, each new query and key is projected into the r-dimensional subspace to yield proxy vectors q̂_t and k̂_t; proxy attention scores are computed in O(lr) time to identify the top-k most relevant tokens, after which exact attention is computed on the retrieved full-precision KV pairs. A mixed GPU-CPU cache with a hit-and-miss mechanism transfers only missing KV rows from CPU, and a dedicated recency buffer (lite tokens) exploits the empirically observed high attention scores on neighboring tokens to maximize cache hit rates and minimize PCIe traffic.

## Results

On RULER 128K (r=32, top-2048, 64 lite tokens), LRQK achieves 99.25% on MQ and 98.00% on MV, surpassing the full LLaMA-3-8B-1M model (98.96% and 95.57%), and matches or exceeds ShadowKV on QA-1 (75.00 vs 72.92) and QA-2 (53.00 vs 52.08). On LongBench, LRQK leads all baselines on PRetr (89.00 vs 80.00 for ShadowKV) and LCC (66.13 vs 63.93). However, LRQK shows notable degradation on MK2 (42.00 vs 98.96 full model) and S1 (81.00 vs 100.00), indicating sensitivity to multi-key retrieval at extreme context lengths.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ShadowKV (the strongest sparse-attention baseline), LRQK wins on QA-1 (+2.08), QA-2 (+0.92), MQ (+2.37), MV (+2.17), PRetr (+9.00), and LCC (+2.20), but loses on S1 (−19.00), MK2 (−56.96), VT (−1.47), and FWE (−2.90). Against Quest, LRQK dominates on QA-1 (+14.58), QA-2 (+3.00), MQ (+1.60), MV (+4.51), PRetr (+10.00), and LCC (+12.49), while trailing on MK2 (−35.08) and FWE (+4.04). InfiniGen is outperformed by LRQK on nearly every task, with the largest gaps on MQ (+35.97), MV (+43.05), and PRetr (+14.70). Loki is catastrophically inferior across all tasks (e.g., 1.04 on S2 vs. 100.00 for LRQK), confirming that PCA-based key approximation without full-precision retrieval is insufficient. The most notable weakness of LRQK is MK2 (42.00 vs. 98.96 for the full model and ShadowKV), suggesting the low-rank proxy attention struggles to identify all relevant keys in multi-key needle-in-a-haystack scenarios at 128K context.

## Relation to Prior Work

LRQK sits at the intersection of three established KV-cache compression paradigms—quantization, token eviction, and GPU-CPU offloading—while departing from each in a specific way. It inherits the low-rank structure observation from ShadowKV and InfiniGen but replaces their SVD-on-pre-RoPE-keys approach with a joint Q-K factorization solved via block coordinate descent, avoiding the representational distortion of SVD. The hit-and-miss GPU-CPU cache design draws on offloading strategies (FlexGen, DeepSpeed) but introduces a proxy-attention selection step that fetches only missing tokens, and the recency buffer echoes StreamingLLM's attention-sink insight. Unlike eviction methods (H2O, SnapKV) that permanently discard tokens, LRQK retains all KV pairs in CPU memory and guarantees that the final attention is computed on full-precision vectors, trading selection accuracy for output fidelity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LRQK offers a practical path to long-context inference on memory-constrained hardware by reducing CPU-GPU transfers by roughly 60% while keeping the final attention computation exact on the retrieved subset. However, the results are mixed: while retrieval-heavy tasks (PRetr, LCC) and multi-query/value tasks improve over the full model, needle-in-a-haystack variants (S1, MK2 at 128K) degrade substantially, suggesting the low-rank proxy selection is not uniformly reliable. The authors acknowledge that CPU-side indexing, not PCIe bandwidth, is the current bottleneck, and that hyperparameters (rank r, top-k, lite tokens) require task-specific tuning—both open problems that limit immediate deployment. A key open question is whether the proxy-attention selection can be made more robust across task types without inflating the rank or active-set size, and whether the method's benefits persist at even longer contexts (beyond 128K) where the low-rank assumption may weaken.
