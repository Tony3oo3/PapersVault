## Problem

While the Iterative Markovian Fitting (IMF) algorithm has been shown to converge asymptotically to the Schrödinger Bridge, no quantitative non-asymptotic convergence rates were previously known. This paper fills that gap by establishing the first explicit exponential convergence bounds in KL divergence for IMF, under both strongly and weakly log-concave marginal assumptions.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes two marginal distributions μ, ν on ℝ^d, a reference Langevin diffusion (parameterized by a potential U), an initial coupling π_{0,T}^{(0)} ∈ Π(μ,ν), and a time horizon T, and iteratively produces refined couplings π_{0,T}^{(n)} converging to the Schrödinger Bridge π*. The convergence rate is explicitly bounded by (L_U / (T(α_φ + α_ψ + α)))^n, requiring T to exceed a threshold determined by the log-concavity constants of the marginals and the reference process. In the weakly log-concave regime, an additional exponential factor exp(9·max{L_μ, L_ν, L} / (α_φ + α_ψ + α)) dampens the rate.

## Method

The core technical contribution is a new contraction estimate for the Markovian projection operator (Theorem 4), which controls error propagation across IMF iterations. The analysis proceeds by bounding the KL divergence between successive iterates and the Schrödinger Bridge using functional inequalities (Talagrand T2 and log-Sobolev LSI) applied to the bridge kernel. In the strongly log-concave regime, the contraction factor is L_U / (T(α_μ + α_ν + α − 2T^{-1})), yielding clean exponential decay. In the weakly log-concave regime, the authors introduce a weak convexity profile κ_β(r) = α − r^{-1}·2√L·tanh(r√L/2) to capture non-uniform convexity, and the contraction is governed by a constant C_{φ,ψ,U} that degrades gracefully as the Lipschitz constants L_μ, L_ν, L of the non-convex perturbations grow. The results are dimension-free in the same sense as Langevin Monte Carlo bounds, depending only on log-concavity parameters rather than ambient dimension.

## Results

The paper establishes the first non-asymptotic exponential convergence guarantees for the Iterative Markovian Fitting (IMF) algorithm in KL divergence. Under strong log-concavity (Theorem 1), the convergence rate is (L_U / (T(α_μ + α_ν + α − 2/T)))^n, requiring T > max{α_μ⁻¹, α_ν⁻¹}. Under weak log-concavity (Theorem 2), the rate becomes (L_U / (T·C_{φ,ψ,U}))^n where C_{φ,ψ,U} = 4(α_φ + α_ψ + α) / exp(9·max{L_μ, L_ν, L} / (α_φ + α_ψ + α)), introducing an exponential penalty for deviations from strong convexity. Both results are dimension-free in the sense that the bounds depend on log-concavity constants rather than ambient dimension explicitly.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical experiments or numerical baselines. The comparison is against prior theoretical results: [SDBCD24, Theorem 8] and [Pel23, Theorem 2] establish only asymptotic convergence (lim_{n→∞} KL(π_{0,T}^{(n)} | π*) = 0) under strong assumptions, without any quantitative rate. The present work fills this gap by providing explicit exponential rates. The paper also positions IMF relative to the Sinkhorn/IPF algorithm and the Diffusion Schrödinger Bridge (DSB) algorithm, noting that IMF's Markovian projection step mitigates the 'forgetting' issue (bias accumulation) observed in DSB, though no head-to-head numerical comparison is performed. The contraction estimate for the Markovian projection operator (Theorem 4 in the supplement) is identified as a new technical contribution not available in prior work.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper provides the first non-asymptotic convergence guarantees for the Iterative Markovian Fitting (IMF) algorithm, which builds on the Diffusion Flow Matching framework (Peluchetti 2021; Albergo & Vanden-Eijnden 2022) and the Markovian projection theory of Gyöngy (1986) and Krylov (1984). Prior work by Peluchetti (2023) and Shi et al. (2024) established only asymptotic convergence of IMF to the Schrödinger Bridge, while quantitative rates for the closely related Sinkhorn/IPF algorithm were available (Conforti et al. 2023; Chiarini et al. 2024). The technical core—a new contraction estimate for the Markovian projection operator—draws on weak convexity profiles from the Fokker-Planck literature (Eberle 2016; Cheng et al. 2018) to handle both strongly and weakly log-concave marginals.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

These results close a significant theoretical gap for DSBM, a practically important algorithm for entropy-regularized optimal transport and generative modeling, by providing explicit exponential convergence rates in KL divergence. The weak log-concavity regime is particularly relevant since it encompasses multimodal distributions (e.g., Gaussian mixtures, double-well potentials) common in real generative tasks. Key limitations include the requirement of a sufficiently large time horizon T, the absence of any accounting for drift estimation or discretization errors in practical neural-network implementations, and the purely theoretical nature of the analysis—extending these guarantees to finite-T settings and incorporating approximation errors are natural open directions.
