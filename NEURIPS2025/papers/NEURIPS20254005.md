## Problem

Prompt-guided diffusion models struggle to generate diverse outputs when prompts vary subtly across queries, and existing diversity-guidance methods either require hard pre-clustering of prompts or incur O(n³) eigenvalue-decomposition costs that limit them to a few hundred samples. The paper addresses how to achieve scalable, prompt-aware diversity guidance that adapts dynamically to prompt similarity without fixed clustering, while remaining computationally feasible for thousands of sequential generations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254005/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a text prompt (encoded into a prompt embedding via the LDM's text encoder), the full history of previously generated latent vectors z^(1),...,z^(n-1), and their corresponding prompt embeddings y^(1),...,y^(n-1). It produces a new diverse image by modifying the latent diffusion sampling trajectory in the compressed latent space (e.g., 1024×1024 pixel output decoded from a 128×128×4 latent for SDXL). The diversity guidance update is applied every 10 reverse-diffusion steps using a Gaussian kernel with bandwidth σ_img=0.8 on latents and σ_text=0.3 on prompt embeddings, with guidance scale η=0.03.

## Method

SPARKE replaces the order-1 Vendi score (requiring O(n³) eigendecomposition) with the order-2 Rényi Kernel Entropy (RKE) score, which is computable via a Frobenius norm in O(n²). For prompt-awareness, it forms the Conditional-RKE by taking the Hadamard product of the data kernel matrix K_Z and the prompt kernel matrix K_Y, so that pairwise sample interactions are weighted by prompt similarity. The key computational insight is that maximizing RKE is equivalent to minimizing the Inverse-RKE (IRKE), whose gradient with respect to the newest sample z^(n) reduces to an O(n) sum because the normalized self-kernel term k(z,z)=1 is constant and vanishes under differentiation. In the conditional case, the Cond-IRKE gradient further weights each pairwise term by k_Y(y^(i), y^(n))², so samples with more similar prompts exert stronger repulsive force. This gradient is injected as a latent-space update (z_t ← z_t − η·∇L_Cond-IRKE) at every 10th reverse-diffusion step, operating entirely within the LDM's latent space rather than in an external CLIP embedding space, reducing peak GPU memory from ~35 GB to ~20 GB.

## Results

On Stable Diffusion 2.1 with 10,000 MS-COCO prompts, SPARKE (latent Conditional-RKE) achieves the best prompt-aware diversity (Cond-Vendi Score 32.57, Vendi Score 405.51) and lowest in-batch similarity (75.68×10²) among all methods, while maintaining competitive fidelity (CLIPScore 30.96, KD×10² 53.15). On SDXL and PixArt-Σ, SPARKE similarly improves Cond-Vendi Score by ~3.6 and ~5.7 points respectively over the base models. Computationally, SPARKE requires only 1.75 s/sample and 3.23 GB peak GPU memory, compared to 3.08 s and 8.67 GB for c-VSG and 9.10 s and 20.13 GB for Particle Guide (DINOv2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPARKE outperforms all three baselines on every diversity metric. Against c-VSG [10], SPARKE improves Cond-Vendi Score by 4.66 points (32.57 vs. 27.91) and reduces in-batch similarity by 4.14×10² (75.68 vs. 79.82), while also being ~1.75× faster (1.75 s vs. 3.08 s per sample) and using 63% less GPU memory (3.23 GB vs. 8.67 GB). Against CADS [14], SPARKE gains 3.84 points on Cond-Vendi Score and 3.76×10² on in-batch similarity, with a smaller KD gap (53.15 vs. 55.08). Against Particle Guide [56] (evaluated on SD 1.5), SPARKE is 2.8× faster in pixel space (1.75 s vs. 4.91 s) and 5.2× faster in DINOv2 embedding space (1.75 s vs. 9.10 s), while using 57–84% less peak memory. The only metric where SPARKE does not lead is raw CLIPScore on SD 2.1 (30.96 vs. 31.20 for no-guidance SD), a negligible 0.24-point drop that reflects the small quality cost of diversity guidance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the diversity-guided diffusion sampling line initiated by c-VSG [10] and CADS [14] in two key directions: (1) replacing the order-1 Vendi score (requiring O(n³) eigendecomposition) with the order-2 Rényi Kernel Entropy (RKE) score [16], whose gradient reduces to O(n) via a Frobenius-norm formulation, and (2) making the guidance prompt-aware by taking the Hadamard product of the data and prompt kernel matrices, as formalized in the Conditional-RKE score [15]. Unlike prior prompt-agnostic methods (Particle Guidance, SPELL) or hard-clustering approaches, SPARKE conditions diversity on prompt similarity through a soft, adaptive kernel weighting. The method is applied in the latent space of LDMs rather than in CLIP-embedded ambient space, following the efficiency motivation of latent diffusion models [4, 5].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The O(n) gradient computation is the practical enabler: it allows diversity-guided sampling over thousands of sequential prompts without the window-size constraints that plague Vendi-based methods, making prompt-aware diversity control feasible at scale. The paper demonstrates consistent improvements in conditional Vendi score and in-batch similarity across SD 2.1, SDXL, and PixArt-Σ while preserving CLIPScore fidelity, with roughly 3× lower GPU memory than c-VSG. Limitations include evaluation restricted to image generation (no video or text diffusion), a single Gaussian kernel choice validated only qualitatively, and the guidance scale η requiring per-model tuning. Open questions include whether the prompt-aware conditioning generalizes to multi-turn conversational generation or to settings where prompts arrive in non-sequential, adversarial order.
