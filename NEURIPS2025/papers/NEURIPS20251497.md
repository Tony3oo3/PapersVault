## Problem

Existing diffusion-based image restoration methods apply uniform processing across the entire image, unable to accommodate user preferences that vary across semantic regions. For example, users may want to preserve bokeh blur in backgrounds while enhancing foreground details, or prioritize fidelity in structural regions while favoring perceptual quality in irregular texture regions. No prior method or dataset supports this region-customized, instruction-guided restoration for real-world degradations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251497/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a low-quality degraded image (produced by the Real-ESRGAN pipeline) and a templated natural language instruction of the form 'make {region caption} clear with {s1} and keep other parts clear with {s2}' (or a bokeh variant), where s1 and s2 are positive real-valued fidelity scales. It outputs a restored image at 512×512 resolution where the target region is enhanced with intensity s1 and surrounding regions with intensity s2, along with a predicted binary region mask. The region caption is extracted from the instruction and fed to the SD backbone as text conditioning, while the full instruction body conditions the ControlNet branch.

## Method

InstructRestore builds on SD2.1 with a ControlNet-like architecture where the ControlNet branch receives the LQ image and user instruction, extracting hierarchical conditional features injected into the frozen UNet decoder. A key insight is that scaling ControlNet features by a coefficient α continuously trades off data fidelity (larger α) against generative detail enhancement (smaller α); by applying distinct α values inside and outside a predicted region mask, the model achieves spatially varying restoration intensity. A lightweight pyramidal mask decoder, fed by multi-scale cross-attention features from the ControlNet, predicts the target region mask supervised by cross-entropy loss alongside the standard diffusion noise-prediction loss. At inference, the instruction is parsed into a region caption (SD text prompt), an instruction body (ControlNet text encoding), and fidelity scales s1/s2; a per-layer modulation map M_l = s1·M_l + s2·(1−M_l) is computed and applied via element-wise multiplication to the ControlNet features before fusion with the SD backbone features, enabling seamless transitions between differently enhanced regions.

## Results

On the Instruct100Set for region-customized restoration, InstructRestore achieves the best no-reference scores among all methods: MUSIQ 68.17 and MANIQA 0.6137 on the target region, and MUSIQ 71.95 and MANIQA 0.6809 on the full image, while remaining competitive on reference metrics (PSNR 30.55, SSIM 0.8368 on target). On the Bokeh testset, InstructRestore achieves the best scores across all metrics, including Background PSNR 31.46, SSIM 0.8462, Bokeh IoU 0.8482, and Full-image LPIPS 0.3394, outperforming all seven baselines. The instruction-following experiment (Table 2) confirms that varying the fidelity scale from 0.5 to 1.1 shifts target-region PSNR by 1.02 dB while surrounding regions vary only 0.29 dB, validating localized control.

## Baselines & Comparisons

InstructRestore outperforms all seven baselines on no-reference perceptual quality metrics (MUSIQ, MANIQA, CLIPIQA) for both target regions and full images, with the largest margins over GAN-based RealESRGAN (MUSIQ 68.17 vs. 58.63 on target) and SUPIR (MANIQA 0.6137 vs. 0.5655). On reference-based fidelity metrics (PSNR, SSIM), RealESRGAN and PASD score higher, which the authors attribute to the evaluation setting prioritizing generative enhancement in target regions (fidelity scale 0.8) over strict pixel-level reconstruction. On the Bokeh testset (Table 4), InstructRestore achieves the best score on every metric—Background PSNR 31.46, SSIM 0.8462, Bokeh IoU 0.8482, Full PSNR 24.69, SSIM 0.7437, LPIPS 0.3394—beating the closest competitor PASD by 0.41 dB in Background PSNR and 0.0248 in Bokeh IoU. DiffBIR and SUPIR are noted to over-enhance structured regions (e.g., signs, buildings) introducing artifacts, while other methods produce over-smoothed irregular textures. The key differentiator is that InstructRestore is the only method capable of applying different restoration intensities to different regions within a single image, a capability none of the baselines support.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the diffusion-based real-world image restoration line (StableSR, DiffBIR, SeeSR, SUPIR) by repurposing the ControlNet architecture to enable region-specific control rather than uniform whole-image processing. It draws on instruction-guided editing methods (InstructPix2Pix, MagicBrush) for the human-instruction interface, but departs from prior instruction-guided restoration work (InstructIR, PromptFix, SPIRE) that uses instructions only for task selection or global parameter tuning. The key architectural departure is using cross-attention features within ControlNet to predict spatial region masks and then applying differentiated feature-integration scales inside versus outside those masks, a mechanism not present in any prior restoration or editing framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work establishes region-customized, instruction-guided image restoration as a distinct and practical task, filling a gap where users could not selectively control restoration intensity across semantic regions (e.g., preserving bokeh in backgrounds while enhancing foregrounds). The Tri-IR dataset (536,945 triplets with high-resolution images, region masks, and captions) is a substantial resource that was previously unavailable. Open questions include extending to instance-level object specification, supporting free-form natural language without template constraints, reducing multi-step diffusion inference cost, and exploring whether the mask-modulation strategy generalizes to other ControlNet-based generation tasks beyond restoration.
