## Problem

Transformer-based Spiking Neural Networks (SNNs) underperform their ANN counterparts on visual long-sequence modeling tasks (detection, segmentation) because they fail to establish global effective receptive fields across all timesteps. The conventional ERF analysis from ANNs cannot be directly applied to SNNs due to their inherent temporal dynamics, leaving a gap in understanding how spiking neurons allocate spatial attention over time. The paper identifies that prevalent convolutional channel-mixers introduce locality bias that fundamentally constrains long-range dense spatial interactions needed for dense prediction.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (resized to 1333×800 for detection, standard resolution for segmentation) or event streams converted to event frames (for tracking), which are encoded into binary spike trains across T=4 timesteps. Outputs include object detection boxes and instance masks (COCO 2017), pixel-level semantic segmentation masks (ADE20K, 150 classes), and tracking trajectories (FE108, VisEvent). The ST-ERF analysis additionally produces spatial ERF heatmaps (averaged over timesteps) and temporal ERF curves that quantify how input features at different spatial and temporal locations contribute to a central output activation.

## Method

The paper introduces the Spatial-Temporal Effective Receptive Field (ST-ERF), formally defined as the partial derivative of an output spike at timestep t with respect to an input spike at timestep t−τ (Eq. 3), and derives a loss-based computation method using PyTorch autograd with uniform gradient stimuli at the central output position. Using ST-ERF, the authors reveal that existing Transformer-based SNNs (SDT-V1, Meta-SDT, Spikformer) exhibit Gaussian-like centrally concentrated spatial ERFs rather than the global receptive fields needed for dense prediction, attributing this to convolutional channel-mixers' inherent locality bias. To address this, two novel channel-mixer architectures are proposed: MLPixer (Eq. 8), which replaces both 1×1 convolutions in the channel mixer with a two-layer pixel-wise MLP, and SRB (Eq. 9), which replaces only the second convolution with a single-layer MLP to reduce parameters. These modules are integrated into the Meta-SDT backbone by replacing the convolution-based SNN blocks in Stages 1–2 while retaining Transformer-SNN blocks in Stages 3–4, with membrane-shortcut residual connections preserving spike-driven characteristics. The SRB variant achieves 48.9% AP50^b on COCO 2017 and 43.7% mIoU on ADE20K with a smaller model size than the baseline.

## Results

On COCO 2017 object detection, the proposed SRB(ε4) variant of Meta-SDT-Base achieves 25.8 AP^b and 48.9 AP50^b, surpassing the SDTv3-B baseline (21.7 AP^b, 46.9 AP50^b) by 4.1 and 2.0 points while using fewer parameters (37M vs. 39M). On ADE20K semantic segmentation, SRB(ε4) with Meta-SDT-Base reaches 43.7% mIoU (vs. 41.1% baseline) with 1.2M fewer parameters. On event-based tracking (FE108), SRB(ε4) improves AUC from 56.7% to 58.2%, though VisEvent results show mixed gains with slight AUC drops.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two baselines: Meta-SDT v3 (SDTv3-T and SDTv3-B) for visual long-sequence tasks, and SDTrack for event-based tracking. On COCO 2017 detection, SRB(ε4) consistently outperforms both SDTv3-T (+3.0 AP^b, +3.7 AP50^b) and SDTv3-B (+4.1 AP^b, +2.0 AP50^b) across all AP metrics, while also reducing parameter count. On ADE20K segmentation, SRB(ε4) improves mIoU by +3.3% over SDTv3-T (38.2% vs. 34.9%) and +2.6% over SDTv3-B (43.7% vs. 41.1%), with parameter reductions of 0.3M and 1.2M respectively. For event-based tracking on FE108, SRB(ε4) improves AUC from 56.7% to 58.2% but slightly reduces PR (88.5% vs. 89.1%); MLPixer(ε6) achieves the best PR at 90.1%. On VisEvent, results are mixed: MLPixer(ε6) marginally improves PR (48.9% vs. 48.7%) but drops AUC (34.5% vs. 35.4%), indicating the gains are less consistent in the temporal tracking domain.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the effective receptive field (ERF) analysis from ANNs (Luo et al., 2016) into the temporal dimension, formalizing a Spatio-Temporal ERF (ST-ERF) specifically for spiking neural networks. It builds directly on the Meta-SDT / Spike-driven Transformer lineage (Yao et al., 2025) and the Metaformer/CAFormer architectural template (Yu et al., 2024), using ST-ERF as a diagnostic lens to identify why these Transformer-based SNNs underperform their ANN counterparts in dense prediction. The key departure from prior SNN-Transformer work (Spikformer, Spike2Former, SDT) is shifting focus from 'how to make Transformers spike-compatible' to 'why do they fail at global spatial modeling,' pinpointing convolutional channel mixers as the architectural bottleneck through gradient-based receptive field analysis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ST-ERF framework provides a principled, computable diagnostic tool for understanding how SNNs allocate attention across space and time, which was previously unavailable. The practical implication—that replacing convolutional channel mixers with MLP-based alternatives in early stages substantially improves dense prediction—offers a simple, low-cost architectural fix that could be adopted across the SNN-Transformer ecosystem. Limitations include evaluation only on Meta-SDT variants with 4 timesteps, ST-ERF computation on random (not real) inputs, and the open question of how neuronal dynamics parameters (decay, threshold) interact with receptive field formation. The framework's generalizability to non-Transformer SNNs and to longer temporal sequences remains unexplored.
