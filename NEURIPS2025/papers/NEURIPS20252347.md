## Problem

Existing Rademacher complexity bounds for neural networks rely on a layer-by-layer "peeling" procedure that introduces depth-exponential nuisance factors, while kernel-based models (NNGP, NTK) require restrictive assumptions such as infinite width or lazy training. This paper asks whether an exact, non-approximate model can be constructed for a very general class of feedforward networks—including ResNet and Transformers—that avoids both the approximation gap of prior kernel models and the peeling-induced nuisance factors in complexity bounds.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252347/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method requires bounded inputs (‖x‖₂ ≤ 1), spectral-norm-bounded weight matrices and biases, and finite-energy activations in L²(ℝⁿ, e^{-‖ζ‖²}); for the Rademacher bound, activations must additionally be Lipschitz or bounded. The output is an exact bilinear decomposition f(x;Θ) = ⟨Ψ(Θ), φ(x)]_g with an indefinite metric g, from which a non-asymptotic Rademacher complexity bound R_N ≤ H_m·φ/√N is derived, where φ is a recursive norm bound that is width-independent and depth-exponential in general, or fully width- and depth-independent under explicit spectral-norm threshold conditions on the weights.

## Method

The core technical contribution is a recursive construction of bilinear feature maps using Hermite polynomial expansions of the neural activations, replacing the Taylor series approach of prior RKBS work. Because Hermite polynomials form a complete orthogonal basis of L²(ℝⁿ, e^{-‖ζ‖²}), the expansion exists for all finite-energy activations and converges everywhere, making it applicable to non-smooth activations like ReLU where Taylor series fail. The recursion exploits the identity ⟨a,b⟩_g^n = ⟨a^{⊗n}, b^{⊗n}⟩_{g^{⊗n}} to propagate bilinear representations layer by layer, with the node operation (addition, concatenation, Kronecker product, inner product) determining the specific form of the feature map update via operators □, ⊞, and ⊙. For the Rademacher bound, the paper uses the continuity of the bilinear product (‖f(x;Θ)‖₂ ≤ C_Θ‖φ(x)‖₂ with C_Θ = 1) in place of Cauchy-Schwarz, bypassing the peeling procedure entirely and eliminating the associated nuisance factors that appear in prior bounds.

## Results

The paper derives an exact (non-asymptotic) Rademacher complexity bound for a very general class of feedforward neural networks (including ResNet and Transformers): R_N(h∘F) ≤ H_m φ / √N, where H_1=1 for identity loss and H_m=√(2m)L for L-Lipschitz loss. For a scalar-valued, layerwise, fully-connected, unbiased ReLU network of depth D, the bound simplifies to R_N ≤ (∏_{j=1}^D ω^[j-1:j]) / √N, which is width-independent and free of the 2^D depth-exponential nuisance factor present in prior peeling-based bounds. Under additional spectral-norm threshold conditions (e.g., ‖b^[j]‖²₂ + ⊙ L₁‖W‖²₂ ≤ 1 per node), the bound becomes both width- and depth-independent: R_N ≤ H_m/√N.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper is purely theoretical and does not run experiments or compare against empirical baselines. However, it directly compares its Rademacher complexity bound to the standard peeling-based bound of Neyshabur et al. (2015), which gives R_N ~ O(2^D ∏ ω^[j-1:j] / √N). The key improvement is the elimination of the 2^D depth-exponential nuisance factor: the RKBS-based bound is R_N ≤ (∏ ω^[j-1:j]) / √N, which is strictly tighter by a factor of 2^D. The paper also notes that prior kernel-based approaches (NNGP, NTK) require wide-network or lazy-training assumptions and only model first-order weight variations, whereas the RKBS model is exact for arbitrary width, depth, and topology without approximation. The depth-independence threshold conditions (Table 5) provide a new, constructive criterion (e.g., λ‖W_V‖₂‖W_Q‖₂‖W_K‖₂ ≤ 1 for attention) that has no direct analogue in the peeling literature.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RKBS framework for neural network modeling (Zhang et al., 2009; Lin et al., 2022) and the specific construction from Shilton et al. (2023, ICML), which used Taylor series expansions limited to continuous activations. By replacing the Taylor expansion with a Hermite polynomial expansion, the authors obtain an exact bilinear dual representation that handles non-continuous activations like ReLU and covers arbitrary feedforward topologies including ResNet and Transformers. Conceptually, the approach departs from the dominant 'peeling' methodology (Neyshabur et al., 2015; Golowich et al., 2018) that produces depth-exponential nuisance factors, and from approximate first-order models (NTK, NNGP) that require wide-network or lazy-training assumptions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The main practical value is providing a clean, non-asymptotic Rademacher complexity bound free of the nuisance factors that plague peeling-based analyses, with explicit threshold conditions under which depth-dependence vanishes entirely. However, the bounds remain exponential in depth in the general case, and the assumption that inputs lie on the unit sphere (‖x‖₂=1) for non-Lipschitz activations like LayerNorm is quite restrictive and may not hold in practice. Open questions include how tight these bounds are relative to empirical generalization, whether the spectral-norm threshold conditions are naturally satisfied during training, and whether the doubly-exponential depth dependence for Transformers (arising from the r/ρ ratio) can be mitigated with more refined input distributional assumptions.
