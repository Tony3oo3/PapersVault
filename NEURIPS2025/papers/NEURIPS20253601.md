## Problem

While hallucination in vision-language models has been extensively studied, the systematic evaluation of hallucinations in LMMs that jointly process language, visual, and audio modalities remains largely unexplored. As LMMs increasingly incorporate audio and video, new hallucination patterns emerge from spurious cross-modal correlations and over-reliance on individual modalities, but no existing benchmark isolates and diagnoses these failure modes across all three modalities simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253601/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each CMM sample consists of a video clip, audio clip, or video-audio pair (sourced from WebVid-10M, AudioCaps, and Auto-ACD) paired with a binary yes/no probing question targeting either an existent or non-existent object/event. The 1,200 samples yield 2,400 probing questions evenly split between existence and non-existence queries, evaluated at both object-level and event-level granularities. Models are prompted with a fixed temperature of 0.2 and post-prompted to answer yes or no; scores are computed as Perception Accuracy (fraction of correct 'yes' on existent items) and Hallucination Resistance (fraction of correct 'no' on non-existent items).

## Method

CMM organizes hallucination evaluation into six subcategories under two root causes: spurious inter-modality correlations (Visual-Language, Audio-Language, Visual-Audio-Language) and unimodal over-reliance (Visual Dominance, Audio Dominance, Language Dominance), each containing 200 manually curated samples. Spurious correlation probes are constructed using global appearance frequencies and co-occurrence frequencies (quantified via CoScore) from pretraining datasets, while over-reliance probes use anti-common-sense scenarios (e.g., fish flying, black bananas) or modality-mismatched pairs where one modality contradicts the other. The benchmark converts hallucination assessment into a binary classification task, enabling fine-grained diagnosis of which specific cross-modal association or modality bias drives each hallucination. The paper further validates its two-factor taxonomy through controlled experiments: progressively blurring video or adding audio noise to confirm unimodal dominance, and correlating CoScore with hallucination frequency to confirm spurious correlation effects.

## Results

On the CMM benchmark (1,200 samples, 2,400 probing questions), Qwen2.5-Omni achieves the best overall Hallucination Resistance (HR) among open-source models at 86.3, while Gemini-2.0-Flash leads proprietary models with 61.3 HR and 95.8 PA. Audio-Language (AL) spurious correlations prove the most challenging subcategory: even Gemini-1.5-Pro scores only 14.5 HR, and open-source models like GroundingGPT drop to 0.0 HR. GroundingGPT exhibits extreme yes-bias with 96.6 PA but only 14.3 overall HR, while VideoLLaMA2 shows the opposite cautious tendency with 81.1 HR but 71.7 PA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among proprietary models, Gemini-2.0-Flash achieves the highest overall PA (95.8) but moderate HR (61.3), while Reka-Core shows the best HR (80.9) at the cost of the lowest PA (63.7), reflecting a cautious response strategy. Among open-source models, Qwen2.5-Omni dominates with the best overall HR (86.3) and competitive PA (85.3), substantially outperforming GroundingGPT (HR 14.3) and PandaGPT (HR 17.5), which exhibit severe yes-bias. VideoLLaMA2 achieves the second-best open-source HR (81.1) but with notably lower PA (71.7), indicating a conservative answering tendency. Across all 12 evaluated audio-visual LMMs, Audio-Language spurious correlations remain the most vulnerable subcategory, with HR scores ranging from 0.0 (GroundingGPT) to 94.0 (VideoLLaMA2), far below the ~90 HR achieved on Visual-Language correlations by top models. Visual-only models (e.g., Qwen2.5VL, LLaVA-OV) and audio-only models (e.g., SALMONN, GAMA-IT) show higher PA in their respective domains but remain vulnerable to hallucinations, with HR scores for AL correlations between 34.5 and 59.0.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the hallucination evaluation paradigm established by Li et al. [31] (yes/no probing for object hallucination in vision-language models) and Zhou et al. [71] (Co-occurrence Score for quantifying spurious correlations) from the two-modality image-text setting to a tri-modality video-audio-text setting. While prior benchmarks like POPE and HallusionBench focus on visual-language hallucinations, and the concurrent AVHBench [50] addresses unimodal over-reliance in audio-visual settings, CMM is the first to systematically decompose hallucinations into both spurious inter-modality correlations (VL, AL, VAL) and unimodal over-reliance (visual, audio, language dominance) across all three modalities simultaneously. The evaluation protocol (binary yes/no probing with PA and HR metrics) directly inherits from [31], while the co-occurrence analysis framework is adapted from [71].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals that audio-language hallucinations are essentially unsolved even for frontier proprietary models (Gemini-1.5-Pro achieves only 14.5 HR on AL correlations vs. 90.5 on VL), exposing a critical blind spot in current multi-modal training pipelines where audio-text alignment receives far less attention than vision-text alignment. The finding that CoT prompting can catastrophically worsen hallucination resistance (PandaGPT HR drops from 27 to 3) and that larger LLM decoders help with language dominance but not spurious correlations provides actionable guidance for model developers. Open questions include how to construct balanced tri-modality training data that avoids reinforcing spurious correlations, whether the binary probing paradigm adequately captures more nuanced hallucination types, and how to design training objectives that enforce genuine cross-modal grounding rather than statistical co-occurrence shortcuts.
