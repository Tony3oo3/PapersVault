## Problem

4D reconstruction from monocular video is ill-posed for dynamic regions because depth is unobservable and camera motion is entangled with scene motion. Regularisation-based methods produce geometrically compact but visually poor reconstructions, while diffusion-based approaches achieve high visual fidelity but suffer from spatio-temporal inconsistency. No prior work has integrated a diffusion prior into monocular video reconstruction in a geometrically consistent manner.

## Input / Output

The method takes a casually captured monocular video (T frames at 720×960) with noisy camera poses as input and produces a 4D Gaussian splatting scene representation (static and dynamic Gaussians) capable of rendering photorealistic novel views from arbitrary viewpoints. The pipeline internally generates a pseudo-multi-view supervision set by sampling M new camera trajectories per timestep and enhancing the corresponding renders with a scene-personalised diffusion model, which is then used to refine the 4D representation.

## Method

ViDAR builds on MoSca as the initial monocular 4D Gaussian reconstruction baseline, replacing its epipolar-error-based dynamic classification with Track Anything masks to reduce floater artefacts. A Stable Diffusion XL model is personalised to the input scene via DreamBooth-style LoRA fine-tuning (5000 iterations), then used to enhance degraded novel-view renders through a multi-step denoising process (k noise steps followed by k denoising steps) rather than Score Distillation Sampling. The key innovation is a diffusion-aware reconstruction strategy: diffusion-based supervision is applied exclusively to dynamic regions (masked via Track Anything) with a combined L1 + VGG perceptual + SSIM loss to avoid convergence-to-mean in static areas, while sampled camera poses are jointly optimised using full-image supervision (exploiting coarse structural consistency in static regions for localisation). Training proceeds in two passes per iteration: first updating only the sampled camera poses, then re-rendering and updating the Gaussians and input camera poses with the dynamic-region loss.

## Results

On the DyCheck dataset (half-resolution, co-visibility masked regions), ViDAR achieves 19.69 PSNR, 0.7126 SSIM, and 0.2231 LPIPS, outperforming the strongest baseline MoSca (19.32 / 0.7060 / 0.2640) and Shape of Motion (18.62 / 0.6820 / 0.2382). On the newly introduced dynamic-region benchmark, ViDAR reaches 16.46 PSNR-D and 0.8850 SSIM-D, a more substantial margin over MoSca (15.63 / 0.8755) and SoM (14.80 / 0.8582), confirming that the diffusion-aware enhancement primarily benefits under-observed dynamic content. On the easier NVIDIA dataset, ViDAR also leads all methods with 27.20 PSNR and 0.8610 SSIM (full image) and 21.35 PSNR-D (dynamic regions).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ViDAR outperforms all 16 compared baselines in PSNR and SSIM across every evaluation setting (half-res, full-res, SoM-5, and NVIDIA). The closest competitor is MoSca [13], the monocular reconstruction baseline upon which ViDAR builds; ViDAR improves PSNR by ~0.37 dB (co-visibility) and ~0.83 dB (dynamic regions) at half resolution, with the dynamic-region gap widening to ~0.93 dB at full resolution. Shape of Motion [46] is the second-strongest baseline, trailing ViDAR by ~1.07 dB PSNR on co-visibility and ~1.66 dB on dynamic regions. CAT4D [51] is the only method that beats ViDAR on LPIPS (0.3410 vs. 0.3623 on SoM-5), but this comes at the cost of substantially lower PSNR (17.39 vs. 18.76) and SSIM (0.6070 vs. 0.6751), reflecting its weaker spatio-temporal consistency. On the NVIDIA dataset, ViDAR leads MoSca by 0.48 dB PSNR and 0.010 SSIM on full-image metrics, and by 0.19 dB PSNR-D on dynamic regions, confirming gains even in the easier forward-facing capture setting. NeRF-based methods (T-NeRF, Nerfies, HyperNeRF, RoDynRF) and point-cloud methods (DynPoint, D-NPC) all trail ViDAR by 2–6 dB PSNR, while Gaussian-splatting methods (4DGS, Gaussian Marbles) show the largest gaps, with 4DGS scoring only 13.64 PSNR-m.

## Relation to Prior Work

ViDAR builds directly on MoSca (CVPR 2025) as its monocular 4D Gaussian Splatting baseline and on DreamBooth-style personalisation of SDXL for scene-specific appearance enhancement. It departs from prior diffusion-enhanced reconstruction methods (ReconFusion, DpDy, CAT4D, Difix3D+) by replacing Score Distillation Sampling with a multi-step denoising pipeline applied to rendered views, and by introducing a diffusion-aware reconstruction stage that selectively applies diffusion guidance only to dynamic regions while jointly optimising sampled camera poses. The paper positions itself as the first to integrate a personalised diffusion prior into monocular video reconstruction in a geometrically consistent manner, distinguishing it from generative approaches that sacrifice spatio-temporal coherence for visual fidelity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dynamic-region benchmark (showing only ~26% of co-visibility pixels are dynamic) is a practically useful contribution that exposes how standard evaluation metrics underweight the hardest parts of monocular 4D reconstruction. The diffusion-aware masking and camera pose optimisation strategy is a reusable recipe for any pipeline that wants to leverage diffusion priors without destroying geometric consistency. A key limitation is the dependency on the initial reconstruction quality—ViDAR cannot repair major geometric failures of the underlying monocular model—and the approach has not been tested on scenes with extreme topology changes or very long sequences.
