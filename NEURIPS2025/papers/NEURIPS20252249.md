## Problem

LLM-based summarization systems have advanced rapidly but provide no statistical guarantees on retention of critical content, making them unreliable for high-stakes domains like healthcare, law, and finance where omitting a key sentence can have serious consequences. No prior method offers distribution-free, finite-sample coverage guarantees for extractive summarization, leaving practitioners without a principled way to control the risk of missing important information.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252249/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a long-text document segmented into sentences and an importance scoring function R(c;x) (either an LLM prompted to rate each sentence 0–1, or an embedding-based graph centrality score). It requires a small labeled calibration set (n=100) with ground-truth important sentences to compute a single scalar threshold q̂. The output is an extractive summary — a subset of the original sentences retained above the threshold — with user-specified target coverage (1−α) and minimum recall (β) over important content. A hybrid variant additionally passes the extractive summary through an LLM for abstractive post-processing to improve fluency while preserving information.

## Method

The core mechanism calibrates a conformal threshold on sentence-level importance scores to guarantee that the filtered summary retains at least a fraction β of important sentences with probability ≥ 1−α. For each calibration document, the conformal score S_β is defined as the largest threshold q such that filtering at q still retains ≥ β fraction of ground-truth important sentences; the global threshold q̂ is then set as the ⌊α(n+1)⌋/n quantile of these scores. At inference, sentences with R(c;x) < q̂ are removed, and Theorem 1 proves the relaxed coverage guarantee P[B(F_q̂(x); y*) ≥ β] ≥ 1−α under exchangeability. The importance score R(c;x) is model-agnostic — the paper experiments with five LLMs (GPT-4o mini, Gemini 2.0/2.5 Flash, Llama3-8B, Qwen3-8B) and four embedding-based graph methods (Cosine Similarity Centrality, Sentence Centrality, GUSUM, LexRank) — and the framework also supports a hybrid pipeline where the conformal extractive output is refined by an LLM for conciseness and flow.

## Results

Across six datasets, LLM-based importance scorers (Gemini 2.5 Flash, GPT-4o mini) consistently outperform classical NLP methods on AUPRC, e.g., Gemini 2.5 Flash achieves 0.43 AUPRC on ECTSum vs. 0.22 for Cosine Similarity Centrality and 0.22 for LexRank. Under the conformal framework (α=0.2, β=0.8), Gemini 2.5 Flash removes the highest proportion of sentences on most datasets (37% on ECTSum, 49% on CSDS, 41% on TLDR-AIC, 47% on TLDR-Full), substantially exceeding classical baselines (11–22% for Cosine Sim. Centrality, GUSUM, Sentence Centrality, LexRank). The theoretical coverage guarantee is empirically validated: across 400 random splits, empirical coverage consistently falls within the theoretical bounds [1−α, 1−α+1/(n+1)] from Theorem 1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares Conformal Importance Summarization with four classical extractive baselines: Cosine Similarity Centrality [53], Sentence Centrality [27], GUSUM [26], and LexRank [22]. On AUPRC, LLM-based scorers dominate: Gemini 2.5 Flash achieves 0.43 on ECTSum vs. 0.22 (Cos. Sim. Centrality) and 0.22 (LexRank), and 0.69 on CSDS vs. 0.34 (Cos. Sim. Centrality) and 0.43 (LexRank). For conciseness at α=0.2, β=0.8, Gemini 2.5 Flash removes 37% of sentences on ECTSum compared to 22% (Cos. Sim. Centrality), 17% (Sentence Centrality), 11% (GUSUM), and 16% (LexRank); on CSDS the gap is even wider at 49% vs. 11–24%. GUSUM and LexRank are the strongest classical baselines on CSDS AUPRC (0.44 and 0.43) but still trail GPT-4o mini (0.49) and Gemini models (0.68–0.69). On TLDR-Full, Cosine Similarity Centrality and Sentence Centrality surprisingly match or exceed LLM scorers on sentence removal (50% vs. 47% for Gemini 2.5 Flash), though they remain far behind on AUPRC (0.14 vs. 0.24). Overall, the conformal framework is scorer-agnostic and provides guarantees regardless of which importance function is used, but LLM scorers yield the most concise summaries while maintaining the guaranteed coverage.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the conformal prediction framework from Vovk et al. and, more directly, from Mohri & Hashimoto's conformal factuality (ICML 2024), which applied calibrated threshold filtering to LLM-generated claims for question answering. The key conceptual departure is an inversion of the guarantee: conformal factuality targets high precision (retained claims are factual), while conformal importance targets high recall (important sentences are retained), and generalizes the binary all-or-nothing coverage to a relaxed guarantee parameterized by a user-specified recall floor β. On the summarization side, the method is deliberately model-agnostic, wrapping any importance scoring function—LLM-based (GPT-4o mini, Gemini, Llama, Qwen) or classical graph/embedding-based (LexRank, GUSUM, Sentence Centrality)—with a calibration step that converts raw scores into a threshold with finite-sample coverage guarantees.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to provide distribution-free, finite-sample statistical guarantees on the retention of critical content in extractive summarization, which is directly relevant to high-stakes deployment in healthcare, law, and finance where omitting a single important sentence can have serious consequences. The model-agnostic design means practitioners can plug in any scoring model and still receive user-tunable coverage (1−α) and recall (β) guarantees with only ~100 calibration examples. Key limitations include testing only on documents up to ~216 sentences (far short of real EHRs at 200K+ words), reliance on proxy labels for three of five datasets, and the absence of error bars on most experiments due to LLM API costs. Open questions include scaling to paragraph-level spans for long documents, extending guarantees to abstractive summarization, and supporting non-binary importance labels.
