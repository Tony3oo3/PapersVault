## Problem

Vision-language navigation policies trained offline suffer significant performance degradation when deployed in unfamiliar test environments, and standard entropy-minimization test-time adaptation can compound errors by reinforcing overconfident incorrect actions in sequential tasks. The paper addresses how to provide meaningful contextual grounding for entropy-based adaptation at test time without requiring detailed stepwise human supervision, which is impractical in real-time navigation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251490/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During navigation, the agent consumes RGB visual observations and a natural language instruction at each timestep, storing per-step action entropies and hidden state vectors in memory. After each episode completes, the system receives a single binary success/failure label—either from a human oracle (when average entropy exceeds a threshold δ) or from a self-prediction head (otherwise)—and uses this to update the policy. The output is the adapted policy parameters that produce discrete navigation actions (or continuous actions in R2R-CE), with no ground-truth trajectory or dense stepwise supervision required.

## Method

ATENA introduces two key mechanisms for outcome-conditioned test-time adaptation. First, Mixture Entropy Optimization (MEO) constructs a convex combination of the predicted action distribution and a one-hot pseudo-expert distribution centered on the agent's selected action, then minimizes the resulting mixture entropy for successful episodes and maximizes it for failed ones; this sharpening effect amplifies the gradient signal on the selected action, more strongly reinforcing correct or suppressing incorrect decisions than vanilla entropy minimization. Second, Self-Active Learning (SAL) uses an uncertainty threshold on average per-step entropy to decide whether to query a human oracle or rely on a lightweight self-prediction head (a sigmoid classifier on the episode-averaged hidden state) that is trained online via binary cross-entropy on episodic outcomes. The total adaptation loss combines the mixture entropy loss with a weighted self-prediction loss, and policy updates occur only after episode completion to avoid navigation latency.

## Results

On REVERIE Val Unseen, ATENA applied to DUET achieves 68.11% SR and 45.82% SPL, a 21.13-point SR gain over the base DUET policy (46.98%) and a 13.96-point gain over FSTTA (54.15%). For GOAT, ATENA reaches 67.66% SR and 53.15% SPL on Val Unseen, improving over the base GOAT by 13.84% in SR and 15.63% in SPL. On R2R Val Unseen, ATENA applied to GOAT achieves 79.01% SR and 69.30% SPL, outperforming FSTTA (77.99% SR, 67.48% SPL) by 1.02 and 1.82 points respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ATENA consistently outperforms both TENT [7] and FSTTA [37] across all base policies and datasets. On REVERIE Val Unseen, ATENA applied to DUET achieves 68.11% SR versus 47.55% (TENT) and 54.15% (FSTTA), a gap of 20.56 and 13.96 points respectively; for GOAT, the gap is 14.15 and 13.87 points in SR. On R2R Val Unseen, ATENA with GOAT reaches 79.01% SR and 69.30% SPL, beating FSTTA (77.99% SR, 67.48% SPL) by 1.02 and 1.82 points. For BEVBert on R2R-CE Val Unseen, ATENA improves SR from 59 to 60 and SPL from 50 to 51, while reducing trajectory length from 13.27 to 13.48 (slightly worse) but cutting navigation error from 4.57 to 4.50. ETPNav on R2R-CE shows the smallest gains (SR 57→58, SPL 49→49), which the authors attribute to the dense step-wise guidance in R2R variants making episodic feedback relatively sparse. Notably, TENT and FSTTA sometimes degrade performance (e.g., HAMT+TENT drops SR from 32.95 to 30.56 on Val Unseen), whereas ATENA never degrades below the base policy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends entropy-minimization-based test-time adaptation (TENT, FSTTA) for VLN by conditioning the entropy signal on episodic navigation outcomes rather than blindly minimizing prediction uncertainty. It draws on the uncertainty-sampling paradigm from active learning to decide when to query a human oracle, but departs from standard AL by restricting feedback to a single binary success/failure label per episode—a practical constraint motivated by latency and accessibility in real-world deployment. The Mixture Entropy Optimization loss, which blends the policy's action distribution with a one-hot pseudo-expert distribution, is a new mechanism not present in prior TTA or AL work, and the Self-Active Learning component (a self-prediction head trained online) has no direct analogue in the VLN TTA literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work addresses a practically important gap: deployed VLN agents degrade under distribution shift, and prior TTA methods can compound errors by reinforcing overconfident wrong actions. The episodic binary feedback design makes human-in-the-loop adaptation feasible in real-time settings, and SAL further reduces the human labeling burden by letting the agent self-assess confident episodes. A key limitation is that policy updates occur only at episode boundaries, preventing step-level correction during navigation; future work on lightweight intermediate feedback could close this gap. The approach is policy-agnostic (validated across HAMT, DUET, GOAT, BEVBert, ETPNav) and benchmark-agnostic (REVERIE, R2R, R2R-CE), suggesting broad applicability to other sequential embodied tasks.
