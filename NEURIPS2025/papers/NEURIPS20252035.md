## Problem

In cooperative MARL with shared policy parameters, agents converge to homogeneous behaviors that hinder exploration. Existing Wasserstein distance-based diversity methods (MAPD, DiCo) fail because the initial policy similarity from parameter sharing causes the Wasserstein distance to collapse to zero, rendering the diversity incentive ineffective. Meanwhile, mutual information-based methods rely on the metric-agnostic KL divergence, which can be maximized by arbitrarily small trajectory differences without encouraging genuinely distant, diverse behaviors.

## Input / Output

Each agent receives a local partial observation (e.g., a 4×4 grid in Pac-Men or unit positions in SMAC) and a sequence of past observation-action pairs forming its trajectory history. The trajectory encoder processes this history through an MLP encoder followed by a GRU autoregressive model to produce a fixed-dimensional latent trajectory representation. The method outputs discrete agent actions and a cooperative joint policy, with the Wasserstein distance computed between distributions of trajectory representations (not raw action distributions) serving as an intrinsic reward signal.

## Method

WCD learns distinguishable trajectory representations via a next-step prediction objective based on Contrastive Predictive Coding: the trajectory representation c_t^a is trained with an InfoNCE loss to predict the next-step observation-action embedding z_{t+1}^a while contrasting against other agents' next-step embeddings as negatives. The Wasserstein distance between agents' trajectory representation distributions is then computed using dual functions parameterized as linear combinations of Gaussian kernels approximated by random feature maps, reducing the optimization to learning low-dimensional dual vectors rather than full neural networks. For multi-agent settings, a nearest-neighbor intrinsic reward r_w = min_{a'≠a} W(p_{π_a}, p_{π_{a'}}) is introduced, and the method integrates with QMIX by adding an intrinsic utility network Q_a^w trained with a TD loss that regularizes the agent utility toward maximizing the Wasserstein distance. This makes the metric-aware Wasserstein distance meaningful in the contrastive representation space, providing continuous exploration incentives that do not saturate as quickly as KL-based objectives.

## Results

WCD+QMIX achieves average returns of 0.87 on Pac-Men, 0.95–0.96 on easy/hard SMAC scenarios (3s5z, 2c_vs_64zg), and 0.82–0.85 on super-hard SMAC scenarios (corridor, 3s5z_vs_3s6z), consistently outperforming all 11 baselines. On SMACv2, WCD+QMIX reaches 0.84–0.90 across terran/protoss/zerg 5v5 scenarios, while the strongest baseline (FoX) only achieves 0.49–0.56. WCD+MAPPO similarly dominates, reaching 0.82–0.94 across all benchmarks. The metric is average return (win rate) over 5M training steps, reported as mean ± std over 5 random seeds.

## Baselines & Comparisons

WCD+QMIX outperforms all 11 baselines across every scenario in Pac-Men, SMAC, and SMACv2. The largest margins appear in super-hard SMAC scenarios: on corridor, WCD achieves 0.85 vs. QMIX (0.23), MAPD (0.26), and DiCo (0.05); on 3s5z_vs_3s6z, WCD reaches 0.82 vs. QMIX (0.57) and MAPD (0.12). Among mutual-information-based diversity methods, EOI (0.41 on Pac-Men, 0.25 on corridor) and LIPO (0.43, 0.27) are the strongest but still trail WCD by 0.44–0.60 in return. The closest Wasserstein-distance competitor, MAPD, underperforms even QMIX on several super-hard scenarios (e.g., 0.12 on 3s5z_vs_3s6z vs. QMIX's 0.57), confirming the paper's claim that naive Wasserstein distance fails under parameter sharing. On SMACv2, where stochasticity demands continuous exploration, WCD+QMIX leads FoX (the best baseline) by 0.31–0.34 in return, and WCD+MAPPO achieves 0.89–0.91, surpassing MAPPO (0.37–0.57) by a wide margin.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two lines of MARL diversity research: mutual information-based methods (MAVEN, EOI, SCDS, FoX) that use metric-agnostic KL divergence, and Wasserstein distance-based methods (MAPD, DiCo) that aim for metric-aware policy separation. The key departure from prior Wasserstein-based work is the recognition that shared policy parameters cause initial policy distributions to be nearly identical, rendering the Wasserstein distance trivially zero and thus useless as a diversity signal. To fix this, the paper repurposes Contrastive Predictive Coding (CPC) from self-supervised representation learning to construct a latent trajectory space where agent trajectories are distinguishable, then applies the Wasserstein distance in that space rather than in raw policy space. The Gaussian kernel with random feature maps for dual function parameterization is borrowed from kernel methods literature to keep the multi-agent pairwise computation tractable.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete failure mode in Wasserstein-based diversity methods that had gone unacknowledged: the metric-aware advantage of Wasserstein distance is nullified when the distributions being compared are nearly identical due to parameter sharing. The contrastive representation learning step is the critical enabler, and the ablations confirm that removing it (or replacing it with identity prediction, generative modeling, or fixed encoders) causes significant degradation. An open question the authors acknowledge is the choice of cost function (Euclidean vs. cosine similarity), which appears task-dependent and for which no principled selection criterion is provided. The method's reliance on a nearest-neighbor intrinsic reward rather than average or random-pair Wasserstein distance is an empirical design choice whose theoretical justification remains informal.
