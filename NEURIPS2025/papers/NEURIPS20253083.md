## Problem

Passive exploration methods in off-policy RL (e.g., PER, SER, PGR) are fundamentally constrained by the replay buffer's limited state diversity—they can only resample or locally augment previously collected transitions, leaving critical regions of the state space unexplored. Active exploration via policy stochasticity or entropy bonuses is similarly bounded by the agent's actual trajectories and finite episode lengths. The paper addresses how to generate genuinely novel, policy-improving states that extend beyond the buffer's support while preserving dynamical consistency with the environment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253083/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes continuous state vectors (ranging from 8 to 376 dimensions depending on the environment) and policy actions, along with replay buffer transitions for continuous fine-tuning of the generator. It produces critical states via a classifier-guided diffusion process conditioned on a utility function (policy entropy or TD error), then completes them into full (s, a, r, s') transitions using a one-step latent-dynamics world model. The final output is an augmented training set Γ' = Γ ∪ Γ_e, where generated transitions are mixed with real buffer samples at a controlled ratio λ for policy improvement.

## Method

MoGE comprises two tightly coupled components: (1) a conditional diffusion generator trained on the replay buffer that synthesizes critical states under guidance from a utility function f(s) (either policy entropy or TD error), and (2) a one-step imagination world model with a two-layer Transformer encoder in latent space that predicts next-state, reward, and termination for arbitrary (s, a) pairs. A key theoretical contribution is the Steady-State Occupancy Measurement Alignment Theorem, which proves that under FIFO buffer management and converging policies, the buffer's state distribution asymptotically matches the optimal policy's occupancy measure—ensuring generated states remain in the valid state space. The training framework integrates MoGE as a plug-in into existing off-policy actor-critic algorithms (DSAC, TD3) without modifying their core structure, using a λ-mixture sampling scheme to approximate importance sampling for the distribution shift between buffer and generated states, with bias bounded by O(λ·KL(π_θ‖β)^{1/2}).

## Results

On the DeepMind Control Suite (5 locomotion tasks), DSAC+MoGE achieves an average Total Average Return (TAR) of 817.7, a +43.8% improvement over DSAC (568.5), with the largest gain on Humanoid-walk (891.7 vs. 146.5, +508.6%). On OpenAI Gym (5 tasks), DSAC+MoGE reaches an average TAR of 9135.5, surpassing DSAC (8301.0) by +10.0%, and sets new best results on all five environments including Humanoid-v3 (12151.1, +16.8% over DSAC). TD3+MoGE also significantly outperforms vanilla TD3 by +73.3% on DMC and +20.8% on Gym, confirming MoGE's plug-in effectiveness across both stochastic and deterministic policy algorithms.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MoGE consistently outperforms all 17 baselines across the evaluated benchmarks. Against the primary off-policy baselines, DSAC+MoGE beats DSAC by +43.8% (DMC) and +10.0% (Gym), while TD3+MoGE surpasses TD3 by +73.3% (DMC) and +20.8% (Gym). Among passive exploration methods, MoGE substantially outperforms PGR (e.g., 891.7 vs. 195.0 on DMC Humanoid-walk) and PER (which often degrades performance, e.g., TD3+PER drops to 34.4 on Humanoid-walk vs. 120.1 for vanilla TD3). In supplemental experiments, DSAC+MoGE also dominates model-based baselines DreamerV3 (762.7 vs. 2.4 avg DMC) and TD-MPC2 (762.7 vs. 635.9), as well as modified actor-critic methods Simba, BRO, RedQ, and DroQ on DMC Humanoid tasks. Against enhanced exploration approaches (Plan2Explore, MaxInfoRL, OMBRL), MoGE leads by 28–148% on DMC Humanoid tasks, demonstrating that task-aware utility-guided generation outperforms intrinsic-reward-based exploration signals.

## Relation to Prior Work

MoGE sits at the intersection of classifier-guided diffusion models (Dhariwal & Nichol, 2021) and model-based RL, but departs from both in important ways. Unlike prior passive exploration methods (PER, PGR, SER) that resample or generate transitions confined to the replay buffer's observed state distribution, MoGE generates *critical states* from a learned occupancy measure and completes them via a dedicated one-step world model, decoupling state generation from the behavior policy's coverage. The theoretical contribution (Theorem 1) provides a convergence guarantee that the FIFO buffer's occupancy measure asymptotically aligns with the optimal policy's stationary measure, justifying the generator's target distribution—a formalization absent from prior generative-replay work. The λ-mixture approximation for importance sampling is a practical workaround for the intractability of exact IS ratios under diffusion-generated state distributions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MoGE offers a plug-in exploration module that improves off-policy RL without altering core algorithm structure, which is practically valuable for practitioners who want to enhance existing pipelines. The +43.8% average improvement on DMC and consistent gains across 10 locomotion tasks demonstrate broad applicability, though the largest single-task gain (Humanoid-walk, +508.6%) is partly attributable to a very weak baseline. Key limitations include the computational overhead of diffusion sampling (mitigated by updating every 10 steps) and the assumption that the generator's learned distribution closely tracks the evolving buffer occupancy. Open questions include whether the approach extends to on-policy settings, whether more expressive utility functions or adaptive guidance schedules could further improve exploration, and how the method scales to even higher-dimensional or partially observable environments.
