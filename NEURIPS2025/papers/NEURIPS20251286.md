## Problem

Existing data selection strategies for DPO either perform static pre-selection before training or focus on online feedback collection, but none adaptively schedule training samples in response to the language model's evolving internal states. As the model's learning capacity shifts across training rounds, the relative difficulty and informativeness of each sample changes, and noisy preference labels can destabilize optimization. This paper introduces the Sample Scheduling for DPO problem: dynamically selecting which samples from each batch to train on, conditioned on the model's current state, to maximize generalization performance.

## Input / Output

At each training round, the scheduler receives a batch of n preference samples (prompt, chosen response, rejected response) and the corresponding intermediate hidden state representations extracted from all transformer layers of the policy LLM. These hidden states are concatenated and pooled into fixed-dimensional arm context vectors via a frozen all-MiniLM-L6-v2 encoder. The scheduler outputs a Top-K subset (K = n/2 in the main experiments) of samples for the current DPO backward pass, and the LLM policy parameters are updated using only the selected subset's loss. A lagged training strategy means the scheduler's reward signal for round t is computed from the loss difference between round t and t+1, so no extra forward pass through the LLM is needed.

## Method

SamS formulates batch-wise sample scheduling as a contextual bandit problem with an exploitation-exploration network architecture. The exploitation network (16-layer residual MLP, width 4096) maps encoded arm contexts to predicted sample rewards, while the exploration network (16-layer residual MLP) estimates the exploitation network's prediction uncertainty to provide an exploration bonus, following the EE-Net design. The reward signal combines a batch-level component (exponentiated DPO loss reduction between consecutive rounds, normalized) and a sample-level component (preference margin under the current policy plus model uncertainty on the chosen response), weighted by a hyperparameter γ. The scheduler is trained with a lagged strategy: in round t, it observes the reward from the subset selected in round t−1 (computed using the current round's forward pass loss), then updates its parameters before scheduling the current batch. This avoids any additional LLM forward passes and reduces GPU memory by ~18% since only K samples require backward propagation.

## Results

DPO+SamS achieves the best performance across all four model settings on both AlpacaEval 2 and MT-Bench. On Llama3-Instruct-8B, DPO+SamS reaches 42.2% LC win rate, 40.5% raw win rate, and 7.1 MT-Bench score, outperforming the best baseline (DPO at 40.3% LC) by 1.9% LC. On Llama3-Instruct-v0.2-8B, the gains are larger: 51.5% LC vs. 48.2% for DPO (+3.3%). Compared to the DPO(50%) random-selection baseline, SamS improves LC by 5.5%–8.4% and WR by 3.0%–12.4% across settings, while reducing GPU memory usage by ~18% with similar runtime.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DPO+SamS outperforms all nine baselines across every model setting and metric. Against the closest competitor R-DPO (41.1% LC on Llama3-8B), SamS gains +1.1% LC and +2.7% WR; against DPO itself (40.3% LC), the margin is +1.9% LC and +2.6% WR. The gap widens substantially on the v0.2 setting: DPO+SamS reaches 51.5% LC vs. DPO's 48.2% (+3.3%) and R-DPO's 48.0% (+3.5%). Compared to the random-selection DPO(50%) baseline, SamS achieves the largest relative gains (up to +12.4% WR on Mistral-7B), confirming that the adaptive scheduling—not merely using fewer samples—drives the improvement. Against Selective DPO (a data pre-selection method requiring 6.0h extra training), DPO+SamS matches or exceeds its performance (42.2% vs. 41.7% LC on Llama3-8B) while cutting total runtime from 7.2h to 2.4h. The combination Selective DPO+SamS further pushes to 46.5% LC, demonstrating that the two strategies are complementary.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023) as the underlying preference optimization objective and on the exploitation-exploration neural network architecture from the contextual bandit literature (Ban et al., 2021, 2024) as the scheduling mechanism. It departs from prior data-centric approaches to DPO—active querying, response pair selection, and static data pre-selection (e.g., Selective DPO)—by introducing a dynamic, state-conditioned scheduling problem that operates *during* offline training rather than before it. The key conceptual departure is formulating per-batch sample selection as a contextual bandit where the arm context is the LLM's own hidden-state representation, creating a feedback loop between the policy's evolving internal state and which samples it trains on.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that a lightweight 16-layer MLP scheduler, integrated without modifying the DPO loss, consistently improves alignment quality while using only 50% of each batch and reducing GPU memory by ~18%, making it an attractive drop-in for existing DPO pipelines. The noise-robustness result (maintaining near-clean-data performance under 20% label corruption) addresses a real deployment concern for offline preference optimization where annotation quality is imperfect. A key limitation acknowledged by the authors is that SamS's relative advantage diminishes when the preference data is already high-quality (v0.2 setting), suggesting it functions primarily as a compensatory mechanism for suboptimal data. Open questions include whether the approach extends to iterative/online DPO settings, how the scheduler generalizes across model scales, and whether the contextual bandit formulation can be replaced by more principled sequential decision-making (e.g., full MDP) for longer-horizon scheduling.
