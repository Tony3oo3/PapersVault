## Problem

Orlicz-Wasserstein (OW) and Generalized Sobolev Transport (GST) leverage Orlicz geometric structure for optimal transport on graphs but are restricted to balanced measures with equal total mass. Extending OW to unbalanced measures introduces a complex two-level optimization, while GST's definition as an optimization over critic functions makes unbalanced extension nontrivial. This paper addresses the gap by developing Orlicz-EPT and its scalable variant OST for unbalanced measures on graph metric spaces.

## Input / Output

The method takes two unbalanced nonnegative Borel measures supported on a connected, undirected, physical graph with positive edge lengths and unique shortest paths from a fixed root node, along with an N-function and parameters (b, λ, α, weight functions w₁, w₂). It outputs a scalar transport distance; in experiments, pairwise distances are assembled into RBF kernel matrices for SVM classification. In the discrete case where measures are supported on graph nodes, the continuous integral reduces to a finite sum over edges weighted by edge lengths, enabling efficient computation via a single univariate optimization.

## Method

The core insight is leveraging Caffarelli & McCann's reformulation to recast entropy partial transport (EPT) as a standard complete OT with a carefully calibrated nonnegative ground cost, thereby bypassing all unbalanced-measure challenges. Orlicz-EPT applies the Luxemburg norm with an N-function to this standard OT, solved via binary search over a univariate parameter t with entropic regularization. For the scalable variant OST, the paper exploits the dual EPT and graph structure to replace the b-Lipschitz constraint on critic functions with an Orlicz-Sobolev norm constraint on the generalized graph derivative, reducing the entire computation to a single univariate optimization over k. This univariate formula is several orders of magnitude faster than Orlicz-EPT's two-level optimization, while provably generalizing GST, Sobolev transport, and unbalanced Sobolev transport as special cases.

## Results

The headline quantitative result is computational speed: OST is at least 250×, 13,800×, and 11,200× faster than Orlicz-EPT for N-functions Φ₀, Φ₁, and Φ₂ respectively. For Φ₁ and Φ₂, Orlicz-EPT requires at least 2.6 days per 10⁴ measure pairs on the AMAZON dataset, while OST completes the same computation in under 21 seconds. On document classification (TWITTER, RECIPE, CLASSIC, AMAZON) and TDA (Orbit, MPEG7), OST with all three N-functions achieves SVM accuracy comparable to UOT while being substantially faster, and outperforms the tree-based baselines d₀-Tree and regEPT-Tree.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against UOT (Sinkhorn divergences [26, 63]), OST achieves comparable SVM accuracy on all four document datasets and both TDA datasets, but UOT is more computationally expensive than OST. Against the tree-structure baselines d₀-Tree and regEPT-Tree [36], OST outperforms both in classification accuracy across all datasets, though OST-Φ₁ and OST-Φ₂ carry higher computational cost than the fast tree variants (while OST-Φ₀, which has a closed-form expression, is comparable in speed to those tree baselines). Against Orlicz-EPT (the paper's own non-scalable variant), OST is 2–4 orders of magnitude faster (250× to 13,800×) while maintaining the same Orlicz geometric structure. The paper notes that OST-Φ₁ and OST-Φ₂ improve accuracy over OST-Φ₀ on both document classification and TDA, suggesting the Orlicz geometric structure beyond the linear limit case is beneficial, at the cost of higher (but still tractable) computation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two previously separate lines of work: Orlicz-Wasserstein/GST distances (which extend L^p geometry via convex N-functions but require balanced measures) and Entropy Partial Transport/UST (which handle unbalanced measures on graphs but are restricted to L^p structure). The key conceptual move is leveraging Caffarelli & McCann's reformulation of partial transport as standard OT, combined with a careful calibration of the ground cost to ensure nonnegativity, which finally permits Orlicz geometric structure to be applied to unbalanced measures. The resulting OST then exploits the dual EPT and graph topology to reduce the two-level Orlicz-EPT optimization to a single univariate problem, provably recovering GST, ST, and UST as special cases.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The orders-of-magnitude speedup (250×–13800×) over Orlicz-EPT makes Orlicz-structured transport for unbalanced measures practically viable for the first time, opening the door to applications where mass variation is inherent (documents of different lengths, persistence diagrams with varying feature counts). The clean theoretical unification—showing GST, ST, and UST are all special cases of OST—provides a principled framework for choosing the right N-function for a given problem. However, the empirical evidence remains initial (four document datasets, two TDA benchmarks), and the benefit of non-trivial N-functions over the linear limit case Φ(t)=t, while suggestive, is not yet established at scale or across diverse tasks.
