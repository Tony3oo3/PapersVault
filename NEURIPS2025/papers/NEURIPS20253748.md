## Problem

Scaling LLMs to longer contexts requires storing key-value (KV) caches that grow linearly with sequence length, creating a critical memory bottleneck during autoregressive inference. Existing cache-reduction approaches (MQA, GQA, MLA) either sacrifice per-head representational flexibility, require non-trivial architectural changes, or face difficulties integrating with rotary position embeddings (RoPE). The paper asks whether a principled contextual factorization of Q, K, V activations can shrink the KV cache by an order of magnitude while simultaneously improving model quality.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a sequence of text token hidden-state vectors x_t ∈ R^{d_model} and produces next-token predictions via standard autoregressive language modeling. The key structural difference is that per-token query, key, and value tensors (Q_t, K_t, V_t ∈ R^{h×d_h}) are never materialized as full matrices; instead they are represented as rank-R tensor products of contextual factor vectors (A ∈ R^{R×h}, B ∈ R^{R×d_h}), and only these compact factors are stored in the KV cache during decoding. RoPE is applied to the B factors before caching, so cached keys are pre-rotated and require no further positional transformation at inference time.

## Method

Tensor Product Attention (TPA) factorizes each token's Q, K, V into a sum of R contextual tensor products: Q_t = (1/R_Q) A_Q(x_t)^T B_Q(x_t), where A_Q and B_Q are learned linear projections of the hidden state into R_Q×h and R_Q×d_h spaces. During autoregressive decoding, only the factor components (A_K, B̃_K, A_V, B_V) are cached per token, reducing KV cache from 2hd_h to (R_K+R_V)(h+d_h) elements — an order-of-magnitude reduction for small ranks (e.g., R_K=R_V=1). The paper proves that RoPE's relative-position identity is preserved under this factorization (Theorem 3.1) and shows that MHA, MQA, and GQA are recoverable as non-contextual (fixed head-dimension factor) special cases of TPA. For efficient inference, FlashTPA Decoding computes attention via a sequence of einsum contractions over the factorized components — head-shared feature dot products, per-head rank mixing to logits, masked online softmax, and fused value aggregation — avoiding materialization of full Q, K, V tensors and achieving competitive or superior decoding speed versus FlashMHA, FlashGQA, and FlashMLA.

## Results

On the large (773M) model, TPA-KVonly achieves the highest zero-shot average accuracy of 53.52% across ARC, BoolQ, HellaSwag, OBQA, PIQA, WinoGrande, MMLU, and SciQ, outperforming MHA (52.52%), MQA (52.13%), GQA (52.30%), and MLA (53.32%). On the medium (353M) model, full TPA leads with 51.41% average versus MHA's 50.11% and MQA's 50.44%. TPA also achieves the lowest validation perplexity across all model scales (124M–1.5B) on FineWeb-Edu-100B, and FlashTPA decoding matches or exceeds FlashMHA/FlashGQA/FlashMQA/FlashMLA speed, particularly at sequence lengths above 32K tokens.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TPA and TPA-KVonly consistently outperform all four attention baselines (MHA, MQA, GQA, MLA) on zero-shot average accuracy at both medium (353M) and large (773M) scales. On the large model, TPA-KVonly edges out MLA (53.52% vs. 53.32%) and MHA (53.52% vs. 52.52%) by 0.2 and 1.0 percentage points respectively, while full TPA leads on individual benchmarks like ARC-C (35.58%) and HellaSwag (51.26%). On the medium model, full TPA achieves the largest margin over MHA (51.41% vs. 50.11%, +1.3 pp) and MLA (51.41% vs. 50.13%, +1.3 pp). In decoding speed benchmarks (Figure 5), FlashTPA (Triton implementation) is competitive with and often faster than FlashMHA, FlashGQA, FlashMQA, and FlashMLA, with the advantage growing at longer sequence lengths (≥32K tokens) due to the smaller factorized KV cache reducing memory bandwidth pressure.

## Relation to Prior Work

TPA generalizes the standard attention framework by factorizing Q, K, V activations into contextual (input-dependent) low-rank tensor products, in contrast to prior work that either factorizes static weights (LoRA) or shares KV heads across query heads (MQA, GQA). The paper provides a unifying theoretical lens showing that MHA, MQA, and GQA are all non-contextual special cases of TPA, where the head-dimension factors are fixed basis vectors rather than functions of the token. Architecturally, T6 adopts the LLaMA backbone (RMSNorm, SwiGLU FFN, RoPE) and replaces only the self-attention block, positioning TPA as a drop-in replacement rather than a new model family. Unlike MLA (DeepSeek-V2), which caches compressed latent representations but struggles with efficient RoPE integration, TPA pre-rotates the B-factor at cache time, preserving RoPE's relative-position identity without per-head positional parameters.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The KV cache is the dominant memory bottleneck in autoregressive LLM inference, and TPA's order-of-magnitude reduction (e.g., from 2hd_h to (R_K+R_V)(h+d_h) per token) directly enables longer contexts under fixed hardware. The finding that a lower-rank contextual factorization *improves* perplexity and downstream accuracy over full-rank MHA, MQA, GQA, and MLA is practically significant, as it removes the typical quality-efficiency trade-off. Open questions include whether the FlashTPA speed advantage (currently demonstrated with a Triton kernel against CUDA-optimized baselines) persists with a native CUDA implementation, whether the performance gains hold at frontier model scales (tens of billions of parameters), and how optimal rank selection (R_Q, R_K, R_V) scales with model size and sequence length.
