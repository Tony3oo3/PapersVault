## Problem

Current Video-LLMs fail at fine-grained temporal reasoning, struggling to distinguish actions that differ only in their temporal progression (e.g., pulling left-to-right vs. right-to-left). The paper shows that both architectures delegating temporal modeling to the LLM (Qwen2-VL) and those with joint spatiotemporal attention (InternVideo2) perform poorly on temporally challenging tasks, and that in-context learning does not remedy this, pointing to a fundamental architectural gap in how temporal dynamics are encoded at the vision-encoder level.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252785/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a video as T RGB frames (each H×W×3) alongside a natural language question and generates a free-form textual answer autoregressively. The vision encoder splits each frame into non-overlapping P×P patches, producing T×N patch embeddings that pass through stacked spatial-then-temporal attention before a projector maps them into the LLM's embedding space. Unlike InternVideo2-Chat, which is hard-limited to 8 input frames, STAVEQ2 inherits Qwen2-VL's variable-length video handling, making it applicable to both short and longer video benchmarks.

## Method

STAVEQ2 injects a dedicated temporal attention module after the spatial self-attention in every transformer block of Qwen2-VL's vision encoder. Each block first computes spatial attention across patches within a frame, then applies temporal attention across frames for each fixed spatial patch position, using 1D rotary position embeddings to encode temporal order; the temporal attention uses up to 4× fewer heads than spatial attention while keeping the same head dimension, minimizing parameter overhead. Training is two-stage: (1) zero-initialized temporal blocks are trained with all other parameters frozen to preserve pretrained spatial capabilities, then (2) LoRA adapters are added to both the vision encoder and LLM for joint fine-tuning on VQA data. The paper validates the mechanism's generality by applying the same stacked temporal attention to InternVideo2, Qwen2.5-VL, VideoRoPE, and InternVideo2.5-Chat, achieving consistent gains across all architectures and setting a new state-of-the-art on SSv2 action recognition.

## Results

On the VITATECS benchmark, STAVEQ2 7B achieves the highest scores across all six temporal aspects (e.g., Direction 87.6, Sequence 83.9), outperforming both Qwen2-VL 7B and the newer Qwen2.5-VL 7B. On MVBench, STAVEQ2 7B reaches 70.1% accuracy, surpassing Qwen2-VL 7B (67.0%) by 3.1 points and Qwen2.5-VL 7B (69.6%) by 0.5 points. On Video-MME, STAVEQ2 72B achieves 73.9/79.9 (without/with subtitles), outperforming GPT-4o (71.9/77.2) by 2.0/2.7 points and LLaVA-Video 72B (70.6/76.9) by 3.3/3.0 points. Additionally, applying stacked temporal attention to InternVideo2 1B yields a new state-of-the-art 78.0% on full SSv2 action recognition, beating the larger InternVideo2 6B (77.5%) by 0.5%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

STAVEQ2 consistently outperforms its direct base models across all benchmarks: STAVEQ2 7B beats Qwen2-VL 7B by +3.1 on MVBench (70.1 vs 67.0) and +3.5/+2.8 on Video-MME, and also surpasses the newer Qwen2.5-VL 7B by +0.5 on MVBench and +1.7/+0.2 on Video-MME. At the 72B scale, STAVEQ2 72B outperforms Qwen2.5-VL 72B by a substantial +4.1 on MVBench (74.5 vs 70.4) and +0.6/+0.8 on Video-MME, and beats GPT-4o by +2.0/+2.7 on Video-MME. Against temporal-specific baselines, STAVEQ2 7B surpasses ST-LLM 7B by +15.2 on MVBench (70.1 vs 54.9) and TG-Vid 7B by +13.7 (70.1 vs 56.4), demonstrating that dedicated temporal attention in the vision encoder far exceeds approaches that delegate temporal modeling to the LLM or use time gating. On the SSv2 action recognition benchmark, InternVideo2 1B + STA achieves 78.0%, setting a new state-of-the-art that exceeds the 6× larger InternVideo2 6B (77.5%) and all prior methods (VideoMAE V2-g at 77.0%, MVD-H at 77.3%). On the visual similarity matching task (SSv2-VSM), STAVEQ2 2B and 7B improve over their Qwen2-VL counterparts by +3.5% and +2.9% respectively, confirming that temporal features enhance even non-generative video understanding tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself against two dominant paradigms in Video-LLM vision encoders: Qwen2-VL's spatial-only attention (delegating temporal reasoning entirely to the LLM) and InternVideo2's joint spatiotemporal attention. The key architectural departure is inserting dedicated, stacked temporal attention blocks *between* spatial attention and MLP layers within the vision encoder, conceptually related to divided space-time attention (Bertasius et al.) but applied specifically to Video-LLM encoders with a parameter-efficient design (up to 4× fewer heads). The empirical motivation builds on prior observations that Video-LLMs lack inherent temporal order understanding, but the paper goes further by showing that even joint spatiotemporal attention is insufficient for fine-grained temporal discrimination, and that in-context learning actively degrades temporal performance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that in-context examples *hurt* temporal reasoning (performance drops from 30.6% to 10.4% for InternVideo2-Chat when going from 0 to 5 examples) challenges a common assumption that more demonstrations improve VLM performance, suggesting a fundamental architectural limitation rather than a data issue. The parameter-efficient design (fewer attention heads, LoRA fine-tuning) makes the technique broadly applicable to existing Video-LLMs without retraining from scratch, as demonstrated across Qwen2-VL, Qwen2.5-VL, VideoRoPE, and InternVideo2.5. However, improvements on standard benchmarks (VITATECS, MVBench, Video-MME) are modest (1–3 points), and the largest gains (11% on SSv2-T10, 0.5% on full SSv2) are on narrow or self-curated subsets. Open questions include whether full pretraining with temporal attention from scratch would yield substantially larger gains, and whether the approach scales beyond 72B parameters.
