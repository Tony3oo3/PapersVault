## Problem

Existing VLA models either map observations directly to actions without any look-ahead reasoning, or rely on pixel-level image/video generation to forecast future frames—introducing redundant reconstruction, lacking explicit 3D spatial knowledge, and missing high-level semantic understanding of future states. This paper asks whether a compact, multi-modal world-knowledge representation (dynamic regions, depth, semantics) can serve as a more efficient and informative intermediate reasoning step than raw pixel prediction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250818/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a natural language instruction (CLIP text embedding), an RGB frame (MAE patch tokens), and a proprioceptive state vector (conv/FC encoding), augmented with learnable <dream> sub-queries (one each for dynamic, depth, semantics) and an <action> query. During training it outputs an n-step action sequence via a diffusion transformer plus three decoded predictions (binary dynamic-region mask, monocular depth map, DINOv2/SAM feature vectors); at inference the prediction decoders are skipped entirely, and only the world embedding and the DiT-generated action trajectory are produced, keeping latency low.

## Method

DreamVLA recasts VLA as a perception-prediction-action loop: a GPT-2-based transformer fuses modality-specific encoder outputs with dedicated <dream> and <action> queries, producing a compact world embedding that is decoded (training only) into three complementary future signals—dynamic-region masks extracted via CoTracker optical flow, scale-normalized depth maps supervised by Depth-Anything, and DINOv2/SAM semantic features learned with InfoNCE contrastive loss. A block-wise structured-attention mechanism masks all mutual attention among the three <dream> sub-queries, preventing cross-type knowledge leakage while preserving causal access to shared visual, language, and state tokens. The action head is a denoising diffusion transformer (DiT) that iteratively refines Gaussian noise into an n-step trajectory conditioned on the <action> embedding, exploiting the multi-modal conditional distribution over future actions. The dynamic-region target is generated with an asymmetric masked-autoencoder tokenizer, so the model reconstructs only motion-relevant pixels rather than full frames, yielding a far more compact supervision signal than pixel-level forecasting.

## Results

On the CALVIN ABC-D benchmark, DreamVLA achieves a state-of-the-art average task length of 4.44 (out of 5 consecutive tasks), surpassing the previous best (VPP at 4.29) by 3.5%. On the LIBERO benchmark, DreamVLA attains a 92.6% average success rate across all four tracks (Spatial, Object, Goal, Long), outperforming the next-best method CoT-VLA (81.1%) by 11.5 points. In real-world Franka Panda experiments, DreamVLA reaches a 76.7% overall success rate across pick, place, and drawer tasks, compared to 50.8% for Diffusion Policy and 35.0% for OpenVLA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DreamVLA outperforms all 13 baselines on the CALVIN ABC-D benchmark, with the largest margins over direct-mapping VLAs (Roboflamingo: +1.97, OpenVLA: +1.17) and copilot-generation methods (Susie: +1.75, CLOVER: +0.91). Among the closest competitors that integrate prediction into a unified VLA, DreamVLA leads VPP (4.29) by 0.15, Seer (4.28) by 0.16, and Robovlm (4.25) by 0.19 in Avg. Len. On LIBERO, DreamVLA (92.6%) surpasses CoT-VLA (81.1%) by 11.5 points and SpatialVLA (78.1%) by 14.5 points, with particularly large gains on the Long track (89.5% vs. 69.0% for CoT-VLA). In real-world Franka Panda experiments, DreamVLA (76.7% overall) dominates Diffusion Policy (50.8%), Octo-Base (45.0%), and OpenVLA (35.0%), with the most dramatic improvement on the Pick-Bottle task (85% vs. 50% for all baselines). The paper also ablates that dynamic-region prediction alone contributes the largest single gain (+0.68 over vanilla VLA), while depth and semantics provide smaller but complementary benefits.

## Relation to Prior Work

DreamVLA extends the VLA paradigm by replacing direct observation-to-action mapping or full-frame pixel forecasting with a structured 'world knowledge' prediction step (dynamic regions, depth, semantics) that serves as intermediate reasoning before action generation. Architecturally, it builds on the diffusion-transformer (DiT) action decoder from Diffusion Policy and Peebles & Xie, and repurposes off-the-shelf foundation models (CoTracker for motion masks, Depth-Anything for depth supervision, DINOv2/SAM for semantic features) as prediction targets rather than as input encoders. The key departure from prior predictive VLA methods (Seer, VPP, UP-VLA, CoT-VLA) is the shift from reconstructing entire future frames or subgoal images to forecasting compact, task-relevant world embeddings, combined with a block-wise structured attention mechanism that prevents cross-modal leakage among the three prediction channels.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The perception-prediction-action loop offers a principled way to inject look-ahead reasoning into VLA models without the latency and redundancy of full video generation, and the 76.7% real-world success rate (vs. ~50% for Diffusion Policy) suggests practical value for manipulation systems. A notable open question is whether the structured-attention disentanglement and multi-head prediction generalize beyond the relatively small GPT-2 backbone and single-arm Franka setup used here. The finding that depth or semantic prediction alone can degrade performance below the vanilla baseline raises the question of how to balance auxiliary signal richness against gradient interference in multi-task VLA training, and whether the approach scales to multi-arm or mobile manipulation settings.
