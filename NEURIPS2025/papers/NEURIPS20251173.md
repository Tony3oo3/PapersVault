## Problem

Existing multimodal semantic segmentation methods rely on parallel backbone networks and complex fusion modules, substantially increasing model size and computational cost, while multimodal datasets remain far smaller than RGB-only datasets. Prior prompting-based approaches (e.g., DPLNet) embed auxiliary modality features and merge them with RGB features via simple addition, failing to fully exploit the complementary information in the auxiliary modality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251173/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a paired RGB image and an auxiliary modality image (depth, thermal, event, or lidar) at resolutions ranging from 480×640 to 1024×1024 depending on the dataset. The RGB image is processed by a frozen ADE20K-pretrained MiT backbone, while the auxiliary image is encoded by the first stage of a pretrained ResNet50 to produce the initial prompt. The output is a per-pixel semantic segmentation mask over 25–40 classes, generated by a lightweight prediction head on the backbone's final hidden state.

## Method

MixPrompt introduces a lightweight prompting module that injects auxiliary modality information into a frozen RGB segmentation backbone without modifying its architecture. The initial prompt is extracted using the first stage of a pretrained ResNet50, leveraging the observation that early convolutional layers capture modality-agnostic low-level features. At each backbone stage, RGB and auxiliary features are projected into n low-rank subspaces (rank r/n per subspace, inspired by LoRA), and a learnable mixing matrix M enables cross-subspace interaction for the RGB modality to enhance representation diversity. The aligned features are fused by simple addition and back-projected to the original feature space, then added to the backbone hidden state. During training, only the prompting module (~5.7M parameters) and segmentation head are updated, keeping the full MiT backbone frozen for parameter efficiency.

## Results

On NYU Depth V2, MixPrompt achieves 61.2 mIoU with 87.2M parameters and 109.0G FLOPs, outperforming the second-best DPLNet (59.3 mIoU) by 1.9 mIoU while using fewer parameters (87.2M vs. 88.58M). On SUN-RGBD, it reaches 53.5 mIoU, surpassing DPLNet (52.8) and CMX-MiT-B5 (52.4). On MFNet (RGB-Thermal), MixPrompt scores 60.1 mIoU, and on DELIVER it achieves 58.0 mIoU (RGB-Event) and 59.1 mIoU (RGB-Lidar) with only 29.9M parameters, demonstrating consistent gains across four auxiliary modalities.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MixPrompt outperforms all 30 compared baselines across all four datasets. Against the closest prompting-based competitor DPLNet [14], it gains +1.9 mIoU on NYU Depth V2 (61.2 vs. 59.3) and +0.7 mIoU on SUN-RGBD (53.5 vs. 52.8) with slightly fewer parameters. Against the strongest two-branch transformer baselines CMX-MiT-B5 [7] and CMNeXt [8], the gains are larger: +4.3 mIoU on NYU (61.2 vs. 56.9) and +1.1 mIoU on SUN-RGBD (53.5 vs. 52.4), while using roughly half the parameters (87.2M vs. 181.1M for CMX-B5). On MFNet, MixPrompt leads CMNeXt by 0.2 mIoU (60.1 vs. 59.9) and CMX-MiT-B4 by 0.4 mIoU (60.1 vs. 59.7). On DELIVER, it surpasses CMNeXt by 0.5 mIoU on RGB-Event (58.0 vs. 57.5) and by 1.1 mIoU on RGB-Lidar (59.1 vs. 58.0), all with only 29.9M parameters versus 58.7M for CMNeXt. The single-modal SegFormer baseline [34] is exceeded by +8.9 mIoU on NYU and +4.4 mIoU on SUN-RGBD, confirming the value of the auxiliary modality integration.

## Relation to Prior Work

MixPrompt extends the dual-prompt learning framework of DPLNet (Dong et al., 2023), which first applied prompt tuning to multimodal semantic segmentation but relied on simple additive fusion of modality features. The key departures are: (1) using early layers of a pretrained RGB backbone as the prompt extractor for auxiliary modalities rather than a separate modality-specific encoder, and (2) replacing single-subspace alignment with multi-subspace low-rank projection inspired by LoRA, plus a cross-subspace mixing matrix analogous to Mixture-of-Experts routing. This positions the work at the intersection of parameter-efficient fine-tuning (LoRA, prompt tuning) and multimodal fusion, deliberately avoiding the parallel-backbone architectures dominant in prior RGB-D/RGB-T segmentation (CMX, CMNeXt, ACNet, etc.).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a frozen pretrained RGB backbone with only ~5.7M trainable parameters can outperform two-branch multimodal models with 80–180M parameters, offering a practical path to deploying multimodal segmentation on resource-constrained platforms. The finding that early convolutional layers of an RGB-pretrained model transfer meaningfully to depth, thermal, event, and lidar inputs is a useful inductive bias for future multimodal prompting work. Limitations include evaluation on relatively small datasets (NYU Depth V2: 1,449 pairs), dependence on a strong ADE20K-pretrained backbone, and the frozen-backbone assumption that may constrain performance when auxiliary modalities carry information outside the RGB feature manifold. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger backbones, more than two auxiliary modalities simultaneously, or settings where the primary modality is degraded.
