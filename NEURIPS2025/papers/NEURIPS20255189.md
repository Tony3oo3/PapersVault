## Problem

LLMs respond highly inconsistently to RL post-training: some models show large performance gains while others plateau or degrade, yet the factors governing this divergence remain poorly understood. The paper asks what properties of the pre-RL (SFT) model determine how much it can benefit from subsequent reinforcement learning, and how to make any base model 'RL-ready' without modifying the RL algorithm itself.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255189/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a vanilla chain-of-thought SFT dataset together with the underlying DAG structure of each reasoning task (nodes representing intermediate quantities, edges representing dependencies). It produces an augmented SFT dataset of the same size (no new queries are added) where each CoT answer is enriched with injected exploration and exploitation behaviors at specific DAG nodes. The augmented dataset is then used for standard SFT, after which the resulting model undergoes GRPO-based RL fine-tuning; the final output is a model that achieves substantially larger RL-stage performance gains than one trained on the unmodified SFT data.

## Method

BRIDGE (BehavioR Injection Data auGmEntation) is grounded in a per-step influence decomposition of the GRPO objective (Proposition 3.1), which shows that the performance change from one RL step is governed by two factors: the rollout accuracy coefficient √(α(1−α)) and the data co-influence kernel K_θ between training and target samples. Rather than filtering RL queries or altering the RL algorithm, BRIDGE acts on the SFT stage: it extracts the DAG from each CoT answer, then injects three behaviors—reflection (an exploratory behavior where the model attempts to solve a locked node prematurely and then revisits), subgoal computation (an exploitative behavior computing intermediate results for complex equations), and information analysis (an exploitative behavior aggregating relevant premises before deriving a node value). Reflection is injected with probability p=0.1 at locked nodes, while subgoal computation is added at every step. The theoretical motivation is that reflection increases policy entropy (encouraging exploration and a more informative accuracy distribution), while subgoal computation and analysis reduce perplexity on the demonstration data (strengthening exploitation and data co-influence), together making the model generate more RL-informative trajectories during rollout.

## Results

On iGSM (15–20 operations), BRIDGE achieves the largest RL performance gain across all three base models: Qwen-2.5-1.5B reaches 91.4% in-distribution and 83.0% OOD accuracy after RL (Δ = +46.6 / +48.8 over SFT), Qwen-2.5-3B reaches 89.6% / 83.6% (Δ = +30.4 / +37.8), and Llama-3.2-1B reaches 64.6% / 44.8% (Δ = +24.2 / +18.2). On PromptBench (depth 4–5), BRIDGE similarly dominates: Qwen-2.5-1.5B gains +53.0 / +40.2 points, Qwen-2.5-3B gains +41.4 / +39.0, and Llama-3.2-1B gains +41.6 / +36.2. The key metric is the RL-over-SFT improvement (Δ), where BRIDGE consistently outperforms all baselines by 2–8× in relative terms.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BRIDGE is compared against three SFT data augmentation baselines: Vanilla (no augmentation), PP-Aug (premise permutation augmentation from Chen et al. 2024 / Yao et al. 2025), and RC-Aug (reasoning chain augmentation from Yu et al. 2023, MetaMath). BRIDGE wins on every model–benchmark combination for both in-distribution and OOD RL gains. The most striking margin is on Qwen-2.5-1.5B for iGSM, where BRIDGE's Δ of +46.6/+48.8 exceeds PP-Aug (+13.8/+14.6) by over 3× and Vanilla (+6.0/+7.4) by nearly 7×. On PromptBench, BRIDGE's advantage is equally consistent: for Qwen-2.5-1.5B it gains +53.0/+40.2 versus PP-Aug's +24.4/+22.4 and RC-Aug's +15.6/+8.6. Notably, BRIDGE does not always start from the highest SFT accuracy (e.g., on PromptBench Qwen-1.5B its SFT score is only 2.2% vs. RC-Aug's 18.6%), yet it produces the largest RL uplift, confirming that the injected exploration/exploitation behaviors—rather than raw SFT accuracy—drive the RL performance gain.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the GRPO reinforcement learning framework from DeepSeekMath [23] and the DAG-based task representation from Zhu et al. [85], but departs from the dominant paradigm of improving RL algorithms or scaling RL data by instead targeting the SFT initialization stage. It connects the data influence literature (per-step influence, co-influence kernels) to LLM RL dynamics, and repurposes the exploration/exploitation distinction from low-dimensional RL into a data augmentation strategy for LLM post-training. Unlike prior data-centric approaches that enlarge or filter SFT corpora, BRIDGE injects specific behavioral patterns (reflection, subgoal computation, information analysis) into existing demonstrations, making the contribution orthogonal to dataset scaling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The per-step influence decomposition (Proposition 3.1) provides a clean theoretical lens for understanding why RL post-training succeeds or fails, identifying rollout accuracy and data co-influence as the two governing factors. Practically, the behavior injection technique is simple, task-agnostic, and requires no changes to the RL pipeline, making it an accessible pre-processing step for any SFT-to-RL workflow. However, evaluation is limited to small models (1B–3B) and two synthetic reasoning benchmarks (iGSM, PromptBench), leaving open whether the approach transfers to larger models, open-ended agentic tasks, or non-GRPO algorithms. The authors note that extending to broader domains and larger dataset scales is future work.
