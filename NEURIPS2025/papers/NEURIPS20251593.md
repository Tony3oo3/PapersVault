## Problem

Standard CLIP training relies on in-batch negative sampling within the NCE objective, which demands very large batch sizes (32K+) to ensure diverse negatives and avoid biased gradients, pushing computational requirements to hundreds or thousands of GPUs. The logsumexp over all in-batch pairs also creates inter-sample dependencies that prevent parallel per-sample computation. Existing mitigations—memory-efficient tricks, offline buffers, or gradient accumulation—either sacrifice downstream accuracy, extend training time, or become impractical at billion-sample scale.

## Input / Output

The framework consumes paired RGB images and their corresponding text captions from web-scale datasets (CC3M, CC12M), encoding each modality through a backbone (ResNet-50 or ViT-B/32) into ℓ₂-normalized d-dimensional embeddings in a shared space. A key architectural addition is a pair of lightweight 3-layer MLPs (one per modality) that take the encoder's d-dimensional feature as input and output a single scalar—the amortized partition function estimate in log-space. Downstream, the shared embeddings support zero-shot classification (via text-prompt matching) and cross-modal retrieval (via similarity ranking), evaluated across 38 DataComp tasks.

## Method

AMORLIP reformulates CLIP as energy-based model learning and derives a spectral representation of the partition function Z_l(u_l) via random Fourier features, proving it is linearly representable in the feature space (Proposition 1). This motivates replacing the intractable partition function with a lightweight MLP parameterized in log-space (log λ_θl(u_l) = MLP_θl(ψ_l(u_l))), trained via a two-stage alternating pipeline: Stage I optimizes the amortization network against either an f-divergence objective (unbiased) or an l2-log objective (biased but lower variance), while Stage II updates the backbone encoders using the amortized partition function in place of the true Z. Training stability is ensured through an EMA target network for the amortization MLP and a cosine-scheduled weighted combination with the previous epoch's target predictions to counteract variance from the rapidly growing temperature τ. Crucially, the amortization network is updated only every T_online encoder steps, reducing all_gather communication calls by a factor of T_online and enabling efficient distributed training without the large-batch requirement of standard CLIP.

## Results

On the large-scale setting (ViT-B/32, CC-12M), AMORLIP (l2-log) achieves 29.93% top-1 accuracy on ImageNet and 30.66% average across all 38 DataComp tasks, outperforming CLIP (25.26% / 27.65%) by 4.67% and 3.01% respectively. On the medium-scale setting (ResNet-50, CC-3M), AMORLIP (l2-log) reaches 21.50% ImageNet top-1 and 24.11% average over 38 tasks, a 12.24% relative improvement over CLIP. In retrieval, AMORLIP achieves 30.09% average recall@1 (large scale) versus CLIP's 26.10%, a 3.99-point absolute gain. The l2-log variant consistently edges out the f-divergence variant in classification, while f-divergence is competitive or superior in retrieval.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AMORLIP outperforms all four baselines across both training scales on the majority of tasks. Against CLIP, AMORLIP (l2-log) gains 4.67 points on ImageNet top-1 (29.93% vs. 25.26%) and 3.01 points on the 38-task average (30.66% vs. 27.65%) at large scale, with an even larger 12.24% relative improvement at medium scale. Against FastCLIP (the strongest baseline), AMORLIP still leads by 1.66 points on ImageNet and 1.66 points on the 38-task average at large scale, and by 0.98 points on the 38-task average at medium scale. Against SogCLR, AMORLIP achieves substantially larger margins (e.g., 2.34 points on ImageNet at large scale), while against SigLIP the gap is comparable to that over CLIP. In retrieval, AMORLIP (f-div) achieves the best Flickr30k recall@1 (37.93% large scale), while AMORLIP (l2-log) leads on MS-COCO (21.47% large scale) and overall retrieval average (30.09%), both surpassing FastCLIP by roughly 1.3–2.1 points.

## Relation to Prior Work

AMORLIP builds directly on CLIP's contrastive pretraining framework but reformulates it through an energy-based model lens, where the in-batch NCE loss is reinterpreted as a biased estimate of an MLE objective requiring the full partition function. Prior efficiency-oriented CLIP variants (SigLIP, SogCLR, FastCLIP) either modify the loss form or maintain non-parametric memory buffers to approximate larger negative sets; AMORLIP instead introduces parametric amortization via lightweight MLPs, justified by a spectral factorization showing the partition function is linearly representable in random Fourier features. This connects to amortization ideas in self-supervised learning (MoCo's momentum encoder, BYOL) and EBM training (SteinGAN, ADE), but is the first to apply parametric partition-function amortization specifically to contrastive language-image pretraining.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AMORLIP addresses a core practical bottleneck in CLIP training—the need for 32K+ batch sizes and hundreds of GPUs—by replacing the all-gather NCE computation with per-sample amortized partition function estimates, reducing communication overhead by a factor of T_online while simultaneously improving downstream accuracy by up to 12.24% relative. The approach is more scalable than buffer-based methods (SogCLR, iSogCLR) since it avoids maintaining datasets-sized memory, making it a natural fit for billion-sample pretraining. Key open questions include whether the gains persist at billion-scale and with larger backbones (ViT-L, ViT-H), whether the spectral factorization insight extends to non-Gaussian kernel parameterizations, and whether the two-stage alternating optimization can be further accelerated or made fully asynchronous.
