## Problem

Dataset curation for foundation models currently relies on hand-crafted heuristics and manual tuning of coarse-grained data mixtures, which does not scale to the growing volume and diversity of training data (especially synthetic data). The paper asks whether one can automatically learn, in a data-driven way, which individual data points are valuable for training, replacing manual curation with a meta-learned scoring function.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250677/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The DataRater takes a packed tokenized text sequence (a concatenation of multiple documents up to 2048 tokens) and outputs a single scalar value score φ_η(x). Within a training batch, these scores are converted to normalized weights via softmax, which are then used to form a weighted gradient for the inner model. For deployment, top-K batch-level filtering discards the bottom ρ-percent of scored samples, or equivalently, individual keep/discard decisions can be made in parallel using the CDF of the score distribution without requiring batch context.

## Method

The DataRater is a 50M-parameter non-causal transformer that assigns a scalar value to each data point, trained via meta-gradients in a bilevel optimization loop. In the inner loop, a population of eight 400M-parameter Chinchilla language models is trained for T steps using weighted gradients where the weights are softmax-normalized DataRater scores over each minibatch; in the outer loop, the DataRater parameters η are updated by back-propagating through the unrolled inner model updates to minimize a held-out validation loss (next-token cross-entropy on a disjoint subset of the same dataset). To make this second-order computation tractable, the authors employ MixFlow-MG (mixed-mode differentiation with block-level rematerialisation), and stabilise training with per-model Adam optimisers and periodic inner-model reinitialisation. The meta-objective is deliberately distribution-agnostic: it simply maximises training efficiency on the same data distribution, making the learned filter directly applicable to any downstream model trained on that dataset.

## Results

Across 3 datasets, 4 model scales (50M–1B), and 7 evaluation metrics, DataRater-filtered training improves performance in 73 out of 84 evaluations. For 1B models, the most dramatic gains appear on lower-quality corpora: on the Pile, HellaSwag accuracy jumps from 41.47% to 46.15% and PIQA from 68.12% to 69.70%, while on C4/noclean, HellaSwag improves from 34.97% to 36.90%. The method achieves up to 46.6% net compute savings (after accounting for DataRater inference overhead) to match baseline performance, with optimal discard fractions of 75% (Pile), 50% (C4/noclean), and 10% (C4). Meta-training a single DataRater costs ~58.4% of the FLOPS of one 1B LLM training run, amortised across all downstream models trained on the curated data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is perplexity-based filtering (PPLX-Mid), adapted from Ankner et al. (2025), which keeps data from the middle of the perplexity distribution within each oversampled batch. At the 150M model scale, DataRater outperforms PPLX-Mid on 16 out of 21 evaluations (3 datasets × 7 metrics). PPLX-Mid wins on four specific evaluations: SIQA on the Pile, ARC Easy and CommonsenseQA on C4/noclean, and PIQA on C4. On the higher-quality C4 dataset, the gap between DataRater and PPLX-Mid narrows considerably, with both methods showing only marginal improvements over the no-filtering baseline. The paper also notes that perplexity-based filtering can occasionally help on C4 at very high filtering proportions (90%), but DataRater consistently achieves the best validation NLL across the majority of filtering fractions in the sweep (Figure 10).

## Relation to Prior Work

DataRater extends the bilevel-optimisation line of work on learned data selection (Maclaurin et al. 2015; Wang et al. 2020) by applying exact meta-gradients—rather than penalty methods or influence-function approximations—to a transformer-based scorer that generalises to arbitrary unseen data points via function approximation. Its feasibility at scale relies critically on the MixFlow-MG mixed-mode differentiation framework (Kemaev et al. 2025), which reduces HBM usage for back-propagating through multiple inner-model updates. Compared to concurrent bilevel data-selection work (SEAL, Engstrom et al. 2025), DataRater's distinguishing choices are: (i) a non-causal transformer as the scorer rather than per-point meta-parameters, enabling generalisation to new data; (ii) a training-efficiency meta-objective (held-out NLL on the same distribution) rather than a task-specific or safety objective; and (iii) demonstration of cross-scale transfer from 400M inner models to 50M–1B evaluation models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first end-to-end proof that meta-learning can automate LLM data curation at practical scale, yielding up to 46.6% net compute savings on low-quality corpora and consistent performance gains across model sizes. The cross-scale generalisation result—one DataRater meta-trained on 400M inner models serving 50M–1B targets with stable optimal discard fractions—suggests a single curation artefact could amortise across a model family, a practically significant property. Key open questions include scalability of meta-gradient computation to fully dense inner models beyond ~400M (the authors only demonstrate 27B via LoRA in SFT), sensitivity of learned data preferences to the chosen meta-objective, and the risk of bias amplification when the held-out distribution underrepresents certain content. The framework's applicability to synthetic data streams and online adaptation during training is flagged as promising but largely unexplored.
