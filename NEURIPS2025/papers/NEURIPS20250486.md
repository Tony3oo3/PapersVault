## Problem

Invisible watermarks for AI-generated images lack standardized, large-scale adversarial stress testing to assess their true robustness against removal attacks. While the WAVES benchmark introduced a protocol with 26 attacks, its limited attack suite constrained the breadth of insights into real-world threats. This paper addresses the gap by organizing 'Erasing the Invisible,' a NeurIPS 2024 competition that serves as a large-scale adversarial stress test for state-of-the-art watermarking methods, attracting 2,722 submissions from 298 teams.

## Input / Output

Each track provides 300 watermarked RGB images (generated by Flux.1 [dev] or Stable Diffusion 2.1) to participants, who submit attacked versions as PNG files. The automated evaluation pipeline computes a watermark detection performance metric A (TPR@0.1%FPR, where lower means more successful removal) and a normalized image quality degradation score Q (aggregated from 8 IQMs including PSNR, SSIM, FID, LPIPS, CLIP-FID, NMI, ΔAesthetics, and ΔArtifacts), combining them into a single competition score via Euclidean distance √(Q²+A²). The Black-box track additionally includes 100 doubly-watermarked images where an image is considered protected if either of the two embedded watermarks is still detected.

## Method

The competition employs a dual-track design—Beige-box (attacker knows the watermark algorithm) and Black-box (attacker has no knowledge of the specific watermark)—to simulate varying levels of real-world attacker information, with the Black-box track further testing watermark superposition by embedding two distinct watermarks in 100 images. Evaluation is fully automated on Codabench with GPU-accelerated Docker workers performing watermark decoding and 8-metric quality assessment, supplemented by human-verified final rankings for top submissions. The key empirical finding is that combining dissimilar watermarks (GaussianShading+JigMark) reduces top-5 team removal rates from ~98% to 61%, while individual watermarks are vulnerable to surprisingly simple geometric attacks (7–8 pixel shifts exploiting phase-domain weaknesses) and regeneration via off-the-shelf diffusion models. Winning strategies span VAE fine-tuning on paired watermark/inverted-watermark datasets, entropy-guided multi-pass img2img regeneration, ControlNet-based controllable regeneration, and watermark overwriting via repeated random message encoding.

## Results

On average, the top-5 teams in both the Beige-box and Black-box tracks removed watermarks from ≥89% of images while preserving high visual quality (overall score = √(Q²+A²), where lower is better). The most striking finding is that watermark superposition dramatically improves robustness: GaussianShading+JigMark reduced the removal rate from 97.5%/98.4% (individually) to just 61.2%, an absolute reduction of over 36 percentage points. In the Beige-box track, the top team (Team-MBZUAI) achieved an overall score of 0.1570 with only 3.67% residual watermark detection, while in the Black-box track, the GaussianShading+JigMark superposition was the only watermark type that top teams could not remove above ~76.5%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper stress-tests six watermarking methods (GaussianShading, JigMark, PRC, StableSignature, StegaStamp, Trufo) under both black-box and beige-box attack settings. StableSignature was the most vulnerable, with 100% removal by top teams, followed by JigMark (98.4%) and GaussianShading (97.5%). StegaStamp (90.2%) and Trufo showed somewhat higher resilience individually. The critical comparison is between individual watermarks and their superpositions: GaussianShading+JigMark achieved only 61.2% removal versus 97.5% and 98.4% for its components alone, demonstrating a strong synergistic defense effect. In contrast, StableSignature+StegaStamp (93.6%) showed less improvement over its individual components (100% and 90.2%), suggesting that superposition is most effective when the combined watermarks leverage fundamentally different operational principles. In the Beige-box track (where attackers know the watermark type), removal rates were even higher (95–100% for individual watermarks), confirming that knowledge of the embedding method significantly aids targeted attacks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the WAVES benchmark (An et al., 2024), which introduced a standardized evaluation protocol with 26 fixed attacks for watermark robustness, by replacing the fixed attack suite with a large-scale global competition (2,722 submissions from 298 teams) that crowdsources the most potent removal strategies. It introduces the 'beige-box' attack scenario—a middle ground between black-box (no knowledge) and white-box (full knowledge) settings—where attackers know the watermarking methodology but not the specific model weights, a formulation absent from prior watermark evaluation literature. The evaluation metrics (Euclidean distance in Q-A space, TPR@0.1%FPR) and the in-processing/post-processing watermark taxonomy are inherited directly from WAVES and the broader generative watermarking literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The competition reveals that state-of-the-art watermarks, including those with provable guarantees (Gaussian Shading/Tree-Ring), are far from robust in practice: top teams removed ≥89% of watermarks while preserving visual quality, and simple 7–8 pixel shifts defeated 'provable' in-processing watermarks. The finding that superposition of dissimilar watermarks (GaussianShading + JigMark) reduced removal rates by over 36 percentage points offers a concrete, low-overhead defense strategy. Open questions remain about whether a universal cross-model watermark is feasible, how to verify 'non-generation' claims, and whether the competition's small dataset (300 images per track) and manual clustering by winning teams limit generalizability to larger, single-watermark deployments.
