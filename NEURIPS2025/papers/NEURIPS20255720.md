## Problem

Despite impressive zero-shot capabilities, Time Series Foundation Models (TSFMs) still fail to consistently outperform smaller specialized models like PatchTST even after full fine-tuning on downstream data. The paper identifies that TSFMs exhibit inherent sparsity—up to 50% of attention heads and 60% of FFN channels are inactive on a given task—yet conventional fine-tuning modifies all parameters indiscriminately, disrupting these task-relevant activation patterns and risking overfitting to noisy or outdated features.

## Input / Output

The method operates on pre-trained TSFMs (Time-MoE, Moirai, Chronos, Timer-XL, TTM, TimesFM) that consume multivariate time series tokenized into patches. Pruning is performed on the model's linear-layer channels (input and output dimensions of attention projections and FFN matrices) guided by the downstream training set, producing a structurally smaller model. The pruned model is then fine-tuned and produces point forecasts at horizons of 96, 192, 336, and 720 steps, evaluated by MSE and MAE on held-out test splits.

## Method

The paper proposes a 'prune-then-finetune' paradigm that decomposes TSFM deployment into architectural specialization (structured pruning) followed by weight specialization (fine-tuning). The pruning unit is defined as an input or output channel of any linear layer, with importance scored via a second-order Taylor expansion of the forecasting loss with respect to binary channel masks, where the Hessian is approximated by the square of the gradient (Fisher information). Pruning proceeds progressively: at each batch, importance scores are computed, smoothed via exponential moving average, and the globally lowest-scoring unpruned channels are removed according to a per-epoch pruning ratio. After one or a few epochs, the resulting compact model is fine-tuned on the downstream data. The key insight is that this loss-guided structured pruning goes beyond removing merely inactive channels—it also eliminates channels whose outputs are counteracted by deeper layers—thereby regularizing fine-tuning to a task-relevant parameter subspace that consistently improves forecasting accuracy (up to 22% error reduction) and yields up to 7× inference speedup.

## Results

Across seven TSFMs and six benchmarks (ETTh1, ETTh2, ETTm1, ETTm2, Weather, Electricity), the prune-then-finetune paradigm outperforms direct fine-tuning in 83% of forecasting tasks, with average MSE reductions of 4.4% (Time-MoE_base), 4.0% (Time-MoE_large), 3.2% (Moirai_base), and 3.3% (Chronos_bolt-base). The maximum single-task improvement reaches 22.8% MSE reduction (Time-MoE_base on ETTm2), while the worst-case degradation is capped at 1%. Pruning also yields up to 7.4× inference speedup (Moirai_large) and removes up to 90% of parameters (TTM_A) while simultaneously improving accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary external baseline is PatchTST (500K parameters, encoder-only Transformer), a well-tuned specialized model trained on full-shot data for each benchmark. Before pruning, the best TSFM on a given benchmark wins against PatchTST in 90% of tasks; after prune-then-finetune, this win rate reaches 100%, meaning pruned TSFMs surpass PatchTST on all six datasets. The internal comparison is between direct fine-tuning (FT) and prune-then-finetune (P+FT) of the same TSFM: P+FT wins in 83% of the 7×6×4=168 forecasting tasks, with the largest gains on Time-MoE_base (4.4% avg MSE reduction) and the smallest on TTM_A (1.0%). For autoregressive models (Time-MoE, Timer-XL, Chronos), pruning benefits grow with longer horizons due to reduced error accumulation. In inference efficiency, pruned Moirai_large achieves 7.4× speedup (1.86s → 0.25s) and Chronos_bolt-base achieves 6.5× speedup (8.51s → 1.26s) on the Weather benchmark at horizon 720, while smaller models (Time-MoE, TTM_A) see minimal speedup (~1.0–1.1×) due to their already compact architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes structured pruning—previously applied to LLMs primarily for inference compression (LLM-Pruner, Group Fisher Pruning)—toward a fundamentally different objective: improving fine-tuning performance of Time Series Foundation Models by restricting the parameter space to task-relevant substructures. It builds on the loss-guided Taylor-expansion importance metric from LLM-Pruner and the Fisher information approximation from Group Fisher Pruning, but applies them to a progressive, batch-level pruning schedule tailored to TSFM architectures. The paper also extends empirical observations about MoE-based TSFMs (Time-MoE, Moirai-MoE) showing expert sparsity to a finer-grained, channel-level analysis across all linear layers, including non-MoE architectures like Chronos and Moirai.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a critical and underexplored gap: even state-of-the-art TSFMs fail to consistently outperform small specialized models (e.g., PatchTST) in full-shot forecasting, and the reason was unclear. By demonstrating that TSFMs inherently activate only task-relevant parameter subsets and that pruning to those subsets improves fine-tuning, the work provides a concrete, actionable post-training pipeline. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to non-Transformer TSFMs, whether the identified substructures remain stable under distribution shift, and whether the modest average gains (1–4.4%) scale to larger or more diverse forecasting domains. The absence of error bars (cited as computationally infeasible) and single-seed evaluation limit confidence in the magnitude of improvements.
