## Problem

Ultra-high dynamic range (UHDR) scenes exhibit extreme exposure disparities that exceed a single camera exposure, producing bimodal intensity distributions where highlight and shadow details cannot be preserved simultaneously. Existing solutions rely on multi-exposure bracketing (suffering from ghosting and misalignment), long-exposure single frames (unable to recover clipped highlights), or short-exposure RGB inputs (limited to 8-bit depth, causing severe quantization in dark regions). The paper asks whether a single short-exposure RAW image, with its higher bit depth and more predictable noise, is sufficient to reconstruct the full UHDR scene.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single short-exposure RAW image (typically 14-bit) as input, requiring no multi-frame alignment or ghosting handling. It produces two outputs: a per-pixel ratio map that encodes local exposure correction factors, and a clean, well-exposed RAW image after denoising. The ratio map is both an intermediate artifact (used to rescale the input before denoising) and a conditioning signal (encoded via Gaussian basis functions) fed into the denoiser, making the output brightness-adaptive across the scene.

## Method

UltraLED decouples UHDR reconstruction into two sequential U-Net stages: a Ratio Map Estimator that predicts a spatially varying exposure correction map, and a RAW Denoiser that removes noise from the exposure-corrected image. The key architectural insight is a brightness-aware noise model that decomposes noise into signal-dependent, signal-independent, and a novel brightness-aware component, reflecting how noise statistics shift across the wide dynamic range. To guide the denoiser, the predicted ratio map is expanded via Gaussian encoding (Eq. 5) into a high-dimensional feature that captures the continuous relationship between local brightness and noise intensity, injected through an MLP and zero-initialized residual block. Training data is synthesized by amplifying highlights in well-exposed RAW frames, generating a 9-stop pseudo bracket, fusing via Exposure Fusion to obtain noise-free ground truth, and applying the calibrated noise model to produce paired noisy inputs. The ratio map estimator is trained first with a weighted L1 loss (for numerical stability given the large dynamic range of ratio values), then frozen while the denoiser is trained with standard L1 loss.

## Results

On the proposed UHDR dataset, UltraLED achieves PSNR 27.591 / SSIM 0.898 / LPIPS 0.075 at ×50 amplification, outperforming the strongest baseline (RetinexMamba) by +4.82 dB PSNR and +0.193 SSIM. Performance degrades gracefully with increasing dynamic range: at ×200, UltraLED still reaches 27.091 PSNR / 0.860 SSIM, while the best competitor (RetinexMamba) drops to 20.600 PSNR / 0.586 SSIM. Against RAW-domain denoising baselines (ELD, LED, PG), UltraLED gains approximately 1.6–2.5 dB PSNR, and against single-image HDR methods (HDRTVNet, HDRUNet), it gains ~2.2 dB PSNR with substantially higher SSIM (0.898 vs. 0.80–0.83).

## Baselines & Comparisons

UltraLED wins against all 10 baselines on every metric (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS) across all three amplification ratios. Against single-image HDR methods (HDRTVNet 25.318, HDRTVNet++ 25.344, HDRUNet 25.341 PSNR), UltraLED gains ~2.2 dB PSNR and ~0.07–0.10 SSIM, primarily because long-exposure inputs suffer severe highlight clipping that these methods cannot recover. Against RAW-domain denoising + fusion pipelines (ELD 25.942, LED 25.879, PG 25.142 PSNR), UltraLED gains 1.6–2.5 dB PSNR; the gap is largest against PG because its simpler noise model fails under the extreme brightness variation of UHDR scenes. Among RGB low-light enhancement methods, RetinexMamba is the strongest competitor (22.770 PSNR at ×50) but still trails UltraLED by 4.8 dB, reflecting the fundamental bit-depth limitation of 8-bit RGB in shadow regions. The margin widens at higher ratios: at ×200, UltraLED leads RetinexMamba by 6.5 dB PSNR and 0.274 SSIM, confirming that RAW-domain processing becomes increasingly advantageous as dynamic range grows.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of RAW-domain denoising (SID, ELD, LED) and multi-exposure HDR fusion (Debevec, Exposure Fusion, diffusion-based UltraFusion), but deliberately departs from both. Rather than requiring multiple aligned frames (which introduces ghosting) or operating in the 8-bit RGB domain (which loses shadow detail to quantization), UltraLED reframes UHDR reconstruction as a single-frame RAW problem: a ratio-map U-Net for local exposure correction followed by a brightness-aware denoising U-Net. The brightness-aware noise model extends the signal-dependent/independent noise decomposition from ELD by adding a third component tied to the exposure-correction ratio, and the Gaussian ratio encoding is a novel conditioning mechanism not seen in prior RAW denoisers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a single short-exposure RAW frame is sufficient to 'see everything' in UHDR scenes—handling both extreme highlights and shadows, moving and static content—without the alignment failures of multi-frame methods or the bit-depth ceiling of RGB approaches. However, the practical impact is tempered by a strong dependence on camera-specific noise parameters (cross-camera PSNR drops by 2–3 dB at high ratios), a small 24-scene evaluation dataset, and the use of simple U-Net backbones. Open questions include whether the noise model can be made camera-agnostic, whether the approach generalizes to video without per-frame processing, and whether the 9-stop synthetic training pipeline adequately captures the full diversity of real-world UHDR conditions.
