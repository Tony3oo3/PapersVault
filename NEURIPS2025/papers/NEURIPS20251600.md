## Problem

Existing Continual Test-Time Adaptation (CTTA) methods focus on exploiting target data while treating pre-trained weights as static initialization, overlooking the domain-invariant priors encoded in those weights. This paper identifies that the pairwise angular structure among weight vectors remains stable across corrupted domains and encodes semantic-relevant, domain-invariant information, yet no prior CTTA method explicitly preserves this geometric structure during adaptation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251600/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a streaming batch of unlabeled target-domain images (resized to 384×384 for CIFAR-C, 224×224 for ImageNet-C, or 960×540 for ACDC segmentation), the frozen pre-trained ViT-Base or Segformer-B5 weights, and pre-computed source-domain CLS-token statistics (mean and std from 500 images, computed once offline). It outputs per-sample class labels or pixel-wise segmentation masks, while the adapted parameters consist solely of updated per-column magnitude vectors and a small set of learnable Householder-reflection vectors (r=12 per linear layer) that parameterize the orthogonal rotation.

## Method

PAID decomposes every linear weight matrix W into a magnitude matrix M and a unit-norm direction matrix Ŵ, then freezes Ŵ and introduces a learnable orthogonal matrix O = ∏ᵢ(I − 2uᵢuᵢᵀ) built from a chain of r Householder reflections, so that the effective direction becomes Ŵ·O — a global rotation that preserves all pairwise angles. During online adaptation, only M and the Householder vectors uᵢ are updated via a moment-matching loss that aligns the mean and standard deviation of CLS-token features between source and target batches. A theoretical analysis shows that class-agnostic corruptions induce a shared invertible transformation on inputs whose optimal weight correction is a shared orthogonal-plus-symmetric map, and under a near-isotropic assumption the symmetric component is approximately scalar, justifying the orthogonal-only update. The approach is validated on ViT-Base and Segformer-B5 for classification and segmentation CTTA, and extended to CNN backbones (ResNet-50, ResNeXt-29, WideResNet-28) by flattening convolutional filters and applying the same magnitude-plus-orthogonal decomposition along the output-channel dimension.

## Results

PAID achieves the lowest mean classification error of 42.2% on ImageNet-C (severity 5), outperforming the closest baselines C-MAE (42.5%) and ViDA (43.4%) with a +13.6% gain over the source model (55.8%). On CIFAR-100-C, PAID reaches 24.9% error (vs. C-MAE 26.4%, ViDA 27.3%), and on CIFAR-10-C it achieves 11.0% (vs. C-MAE 12.6%, ViDA 20.7%). For segmentation on Cityscapes-to-ACDC (3-round average), PAID attains 62.2% mean mIoU, surpassing ViDA (61.9%) and C-MAE (61.8%) with a +5.5% gain over the source model. The method uses only 1.24M learnable parameters with 1 forward and 1 backward pass per step, yielding a 1.6× relative runtime compared to TENT.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PAID outperforms all 15 compared baselines across all four benchmarks. The closest competitors are C-MAE and ViDA: on ImageNet-C, PAID edges out C-MAE by 0.3% (42.2 vs. 42.5) and ViDA by 1.2% (42.2 vs. 43.4); on CIFAR-100-C the margins widen to 1.5% over C-MAE and 2.4% over ViDA; on CIFAR-10-C PAID leads C-MAE by 1.6% and ViDA by 9.7%. On the ACDC segmentation benchmark, PAID surpasses ViDA by 0.3% mIoU and C-MAE by 0.4%, while substantially outperforming DePT (53.4%) and TENT (55.7%). In the CNN extension (Table 13), PAID beats CRG by 0.7% and LAW by 1.7% on ImageNet-C (ResNet-50), and outperforms CoTTA by 1.1% on CIFAR-10-C (WideResNet-28). In the ablation (Table 5), PAID also surpasses the general PEFT methods LoRA (49.7%) and DoRA (48.1%) by 7.5% and 5.9% respectively, indicating that structure-preserving orthogonal rotation is more effective than generic low-rank updates for CTTA.

## Relation to Prior Work

PAID repurposes the orthogonal-rotation mechanism from parameter-efficient fine-tuning (specifically OFT for diffusion models and DoRA's magnitude-direction decomposition) into the continual test-time adaptation setting, where prior work has focused almost exclusively on target-data-driven objectives (entropy minimization, pseudo-labeling, consistency regularization). The Householder reflection parameterization for constructing learnable orthogonal matrices is borrowed directly from recent PEFT literature (Dong et al. 2024, Yuan et al. 2024), but the key conceptual departure is the hypothesis—supported by three targeted experiments—that the pairwise angular structure of pre-trained weights constitutes a domain-invariant semantic prior that should be *preserved* rather than freely updated. This positions PAID as a prior-driven CTTA method, contrasting with the data-driven paradigm of TENT, CoTTA, ViDA, and C-MAE.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean geometric principle (preserve pairwise angular structure, adapt magnitude and global rotation) that explains *why* certain weight modifications help or hurt in CTTA, offering a design rationale beyond empirical trial-and-error. Practically, PAID achieves competitive or best performance with only 1.24M learnable parameters and a single forward/backward pass, making it attractive for deployment where compute is constrained. However, the central assumption—that angular structure generalizes across domains—is validated only for corruption-type shifts, not semantic shifts, and the paper acknowledges that hyperparameter tuning (orthogonal matrix coefficient, loss balancing) remains dataset-dependent. Open questions include whether the angular-invariance principle extends to few-shot learning, continual learning with semantic drift, and whether automated hyperparameter selection can eliminate the current tuning burden.
