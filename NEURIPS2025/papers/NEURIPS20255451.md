## Problem

When uncertain parameters appear in the constraints of a constrained optimization problem, predicted values can render the resulting solution infeasible under the true parameters. Existing decision-focused learning methods for constraint prediction either rely on problem-specific two-stage corrective actions, are restricted to LP/ILP formulations, or lack a mechanism for decision-makers to express a subjective preference between feasibility and solution quality. This paper addresses the gap by proposing a single-stage, solver-agnostic DFL framework that simultaneously manages infeasibility and suboptimality with a tunable trade-off parameter.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a contextual feature vector (10-dimensional for MDKP, 4096-dimensional for Brass Alloy) and a known instance-specific cost vector, and produces a predicted constraint parameter vector (e.g., item weight vectors or capacity values for MDKP, metal content per supplier for Brass Alloy). The constraint structure (number of constraints, domains, functional form) is fixed and known; only the parameter values within that structure are predicted. At inference time, the predicted parameters are plugged into the known COP and handed to a standard solver, so the ML model never directly outputs the decision variable.

## Method

Odece derives two novel MLE-based loss functions using softplus as a smooth surrogate for constraint-violation indicators. The Infeasibility Penalty Loss (IPL) penalizes predicted parameters when the solution obtained by solving with those predictions is infeasible under the true parameters, while the Optimality-Preserving Loss (OPL) penalizes predicted parameters that make the known true optimal solution infeasible, thereby preventing the feasible region from being over-constrained. A single hyperparameter α (the infeasibility-aversion coefficient) forms a convex combination α·IPL + (1−α)·OPL, letting decision-makers dial the trade-off between feasibility and suboptimality. The framework requires only differentiable constraint functions and access to a solver (not differentiable solvers), making it applicable to a broader class of COPs than prior LP/ILP-specific approaches. Training is end-to-end supervised: a neural network (linear for MDKP, one-hidden-layer FCN for Brass Alloy) is optimized by backpropagating the combined loss through the softplus terms, with the COP solved at each training step to obtain the predicted solution.

## Results

On MDKP weight prediction, Odece(0.8) reduces infeasibility from 33.5% (MSE) to 6.5%, while Odece(0.2) reduces normalized regret from 6.3% (MSE) to 1.7%. On MDKP capacity prediction, Odece(0.8) achieves 3.8% infeasibility versus 58.8% for MSE, and on Brass Alloy production, Odece(0.8) achieves 17.4% infeasibility versus 53.2% for MSE. The single tunable parameter α provides a smooth trade-off: higher α prioritizes feasibility (lower infeasibility, higher regret), while lower α prioritizes decision quality (lower regret, higher infeasibility).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CombOptNet and SFL both produce near-universal infeasibility (>99%) on MDKP weight prediction and Brass Alloy, rendering their regret metrics meaningless (computed over almost no feasible solutions). 2sPtO is similarly catastrophic on MDKP weight (>99% infeasibility) and is not applicable to MDKP capacity prediction; on Brass Alloy it is highly unstable with infeasibility ranging from near 0% to ~99% across runs. MSE is the closest competitor, achieving 33.5% infeasibility and 6.3% regret on MDKP weight, 58.8%/17.1% on MDKP capacity, and 53.2%/16.9% on Brass Alloy. Odece statistically significantly outperforms MSE on infeasibility for α ≥ 0.5 (p < 10⁻⁴) and on regret for α ≤ 0.3 (p < 10⁻⁴) across all three problems, confirming that the α parameter gives decision-makers reliable control over the feasibility–optimality trade-off that no baseline offers.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the decision-focused learning (DFL) paradigm from predicting objective-function parameters to predicting constraint parameters, a setting previously addressed only for LPs/ILPs via two-stage corrective-action frameworks (Hu et al.) or full-constraint-system learning (Nandwani et al.). Odece departs from these by formulating a single-stage, MLE-based approach with two complementary loss functions (IPL and OPL) that apply to any COP with differentiable constraint functions, and by introducing a tunable infeasibility-aversion coefficient α that lets decision-makers explicitly trade off feasibility against suboptimality. The MLE lens on DFL—maximizing the likelihood that the true optimal solution remains feasible under predicted parameters—is a conceptual reframing that unifies the two losses under a common probabilistic interpretation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The single-knob trade-off between infeasibility and regret is practically valuable for decision-makers who face hard constraints (e.g., safety, capacity) and need to calibrate risk tolerance without retraining. The generalization beyond LP/ILP to arbitrary COPs with differentiable constraints broadens the applicability of DFL to combinatorial and non-linear settings where prior methods cannot operate. Key limitations include the need to solve the COP at every training step (a significant computational bottleneck), the restriction to differentiable constraint functions, and evaluation on only three problem types with relatively small instances. Open questions include whether solution caching or amortized solvers can alleviate the training cost, whether Odece extends to jointly predicting objective and constraint parameters, and how it scales to larger, real-world instances.
