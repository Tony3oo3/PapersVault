## Problem

Network Foundation Models (NFMs) are increasingly deployed for traffic classification, anomaly detection, and security, yet their evaluation relies almost exclusively on downstream fine-tuning performance, which reveals nothing about what the models actually learn during pretraining. This makes it impossible to understand how architectural choices, pretraining objectives, and input representations shape the latent knowledge encoded in NFM embeddings, or to identify failure modes (e.g., payload dependency, anisotropy) that downstream metrics miss.

## Input / Output

The framework consumes frozen embeddings extracted from the final encoder layer of pretrained NFMs (no fine-tuning), computed on network traffic flows from five datasets spanning controlled (Android Crossmarket, CIC-IDS2017, CIC-APT-IIoT24) and real-world (CAIDA, MAWI) environments, alongside CICFlowMeter white-box flow features and protocol-level perturbation configurations. Outputs are structured as per-model, per-dataset scalar metrics: anisotropy scores with per-dimension Mean Cosine Contribution, CKA similarity indices per CICFlowMeter feature, post-perturbation cosine similarity shifts, and linear-probing F1 scores for high-level context discrimination. A key constraint is that all analysis operates on the representation space of frozen checkpoints, so results reflect pretraining quality independent of any downstream task head.

## Method

The paper proposes a three-part intrinsic evaluation framework applied to four SOTA NFMs (YaTC, ET-BERT, netFound, NetMamba). (1) Embedding Geometry Analysis quantifies anisotropy via mean pairwise cosine similarity and decomposes it with Mean Cosine Contribution (MCC) per dimension to distinguish uniform collapse from single-axis dominance; a decorrelation transformation is then applied to frozen embeddings to measure isotropification gain (ΔF1) on a linear classifier. (2) Metric Alignment Assessment computes Centered Kernel Alignment (CKA) between NFM embeddings and 40+ CICFlowMeter white-box features, testing whether models implicitly encode domain-expert statistics without explicit supervision. (3) Causal Sensitivity Testing has two arms: protocol-relevant perturbation (token replacement on IP/TCP header fields) measuring embedding shift via cosine similarity, and exogenous context discrimination using NetReplica-generated synthetic traffic with varied congestion control algorithms, AQM policies, and cross-traffic patterns, quantified by normalized Δcos and linear-probing F1. The framework is model-agnostic and requires only access to frozen embeddings, making it applicable to any NFM regardless of architecture or pretraining objective.

## Results

Across five datasets, all four NFMs exhibit significant embedding anisotropy (mean cosine similarity 0.69–0.99), with NetMamba showing the most collapsed representations (0.92–0.99) and netFound the most variable (0.69–0.94). Applying a decorrelation transformation to NetMamba's frozen embeddings yields up to +0.35 F₁ improvement on Crossmarket without any architectural change. In metric alignment, netFound achieves the highest average CKA with CICFlowMeter features (0.143) versus ET-BERT's payload-only approach (0.029), and in context discrimination, netFound attains the best linear-probing F₁ (up to 0.97 for AQM changes) while NetMamba lags (0.29–0.70).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates four NFMs—YaTC, ET-BERT, netFound, and NetMamba—using a unified intrinsic evaluation framework rather than proposing a new model. On embedding geometry, NetMamba is the most anisotropic (cos 0.92–0.99) with uniformly low MCC (≤0.03), indicating semantically collapsed representations, while YaTC shows dimensional dominance (MCC up to 0.27) and netFound the most variable anisotropy (0.69–0.94). On metric alignment (CKA with CICFlowMeter features), netFound leads with an average of 0.143, followed by YaTC (0.093), NetMamba (0.066), and ET-BERT (0.029), confirming that multi-input architectures capture more domain-expert statistics than payload-only designs. On protocol perturbation sensitivity, YaTC uniquely responds to transport-layer features (SEQ/ACK drops cos to 0.61; TCP Window Size to 0.67), whereas netFound and NetMamba remain near-baseline (≥0.98) across all header modifications; all models show large shifts under payload perturbation (YaTC 0.18, ET-BERT 0.48, NetMamba 0.62). On high-level context discrimination (linear-probing F₁), netFound outperforms all others across congestion control, AQM, and cross-traffic changes (F₁ 0.60–0.97), while NetMamba is weakest (0.29–0.70) and YaTC intermediate (0.24–0.51). The isotropification experiment demonstrates that fixing NetMamba's anisotropy via decorrelation yields +0.35 F₁ on Crossmarket, +0.11 on CIC-IDS2017, and +0.03 on CIC-APT-IIoT24, validating that embedding geometry directly predicts downstream gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of evaluating Network Foundation Models (NFMs) through downstream fine-tuning performance (as in NetBench [40], Wickramasinghe et al. [54], and Bovenzi et al. [10]) by proposing an intrinsic representation analysis framework. It adapts established tools from NLP representation geometry—cosine-similarity-based anisotropy (Ethayarajh [17], Hämmerl et al. [25]), Centered Kernel Alignment (Kornblith et al. [29]), and perturbation-based causal sensitivity—to the networking domain, pairing them with domain-specific probes (CICFlowMeter features, protocol-level token perturbations, and NetReplica-generated context variations). The four evaluated NFMs (YaTC, ET-BERT, netFound, NetMamba) span the architectural spectrum of current NFM design choices (Transformer vs. Mamba, payload-only vs. multi-input, masked autoencoder vs. masked token prediction), making this the first systematic cross-architecture intrinsic comparison in the field.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides the networking ML community with diagnostic tools that go beyond black-box F1 scores, revealing that all four SOTA NFMs suffer from significant anisotropy, over-reliance on payload (often encrypted in production), and an inability to linearly separate high-level network contexts like congestion control algorithms and AQM policies. The finding that a simple decorrelation transformation yields up to 0.35 F1 improvement on NetMamba without architectural changes suggests a low-cost path to better representations. Open questions include whether the framework generalizes to models trained with cosine-based losses, whether the anisotropy findings hold at larger scale, and whether the identified payload dependency can be mitigated through pretraining design rather than post-hoc correction.
