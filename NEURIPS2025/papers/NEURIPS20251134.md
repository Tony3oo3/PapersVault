## Problem

Parameter-efficient continual learning for large video-language models faces two compounding challenges: catastrophic forgetting is amplified when only a small subset of projection-layer parameters is trainable, and update conflict arises because diverse video understanding tasks impose incompatible gradient signals on those same scarce parameters. Existing continual learning methods for video QA either do not leverage LLMs or rely on complex task-specific prompt selection that is impractical in open-world settings.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a video clip (10 frames resized to 224×224, encoded by a frozen ViT-L/14 into visual tokens) and a text question (tokenized to a sequence of up to 128–280 tokens depending on the dataset), and autoregressively generates a text answer. The LLM backbone (LLaMA-2-7B) and visual encoder are entirely frozen; only lightweight adapter layers (~4.5M parameters) and a small set of learnable prompt tokens are updated, keeping the bulk of the model static throughout the continual learning sequence.

## Method

Bisecle introduces two hippocampus-inspired modules on top of a frozen LLaMA-Adapter backbone. First, a multi-directional supervision module (mimicking rapid binding) augments the standard answer-prediction loss with a question-prediction loss (predicting Q from V and A) and a video-prediction loss (maximizing mutual information between input visual tokens and LLM hidden states conditioned on Q and A), forcing the model to learn bidirectional cross-modal associations that strengthen memory traces. Second, a contrastive prompt learning module (mimicking pattern separation) injects shared task-aware prompt tokens at multiple transformer layers and regularizes them with a contrastive loss that aligns question-reweighted prompts to per-task learnable type embeddings, thereby partitioning task-specific knowledge into distinct latent subspaces and reducing inter-task interference. The total training objective is L_A + L_Q + L_V + γL_P, where γ balances the contrastive term.

## Results

Bisecle achieves state-of-the-art results across all three VideoQA benchmarks: 62.37% average accuracy and 5.34% average forgetting on NExT-QA, 71.49% accuracy and 10.37% forgetting on DramaQA, and 52.16% accuracy and 7.60% forgetting on STAR. Compared to the LLaMA-Adapter backbone, Bisecle improves accuracy by +15.79% on NExT-QA and reduces forgetting by 8.49 percentage points. The method also outperforms the closest competitor ColPro (same LLaMA-Adapter backbone) by +7.23% accuracy and −2.09% forgetting on NExT-QA, and by +0.25% accuracy and −2.27% forgetting on DramaQA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Bisecle outperforms all six baselines on every metric across all three datasets. Against the most direct competitor ColPro (which shares the same LLaMA-Adapter backbone), Bisecle gains +7.23% accuracy on NExT-QA (62.37 vs. 55.14) and +3.49% on STAR (52.16 vs. 48.67), while reducing forgetting by 2.09 and 0.53 points respectively; on DramaQA the accuracy gap is smaller (+0.25%) but forgetting is still reduced by 2.27 points. Compared to the next-best prompt-based methods (ProgPrompt and DAM), Bisecle leads by 8.42% accuracy on NExT-QA and 3.57% on DramaQA. The LLaMA-Adapter backbone alone suffers severe forgetting (up to 24.39% on DramaQA), highlighting the necessity of dedicated continual learning mechanisms. Notably, ColPro achieves the best forgetting on STAR (8.13) but the worst accuracy on that dataset (48.67), whereas Bisecle achieves the best on both metrics simultaneously, demonstrating that its multi-directional supervision and contrastive prompt learning address both forgetting and update conflict without sacrificing task performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the LLaMA-Adapter architecture (frozen LLM + trainable projection layers) and the ColPro framework, which was the first to apply LLM-based continual learning to VideoQA. It departs from ColPro's multi-strategy prompting (question constraint, knowledge acquisition, visual temporal awareness) by proposing a simpler two-module design: multi-directional supervision (auxiliary question-prediction and video-prediction losses) inspired by hippocampal rapid binding, and contrastive prompt learning with task-type embeddings inspired by dentate gyrus pattern separation. Unlike prior prompt-based continual learning methods (L2P, DualPrompt, ProgPrompt) that require task-ID matching at test time, Bisecle uses shared prompts regularized by contrastive alignment, avoiding the open-world matching problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a lightweight set of auxiliary losses and a contrastive regularization can substantially close the gap between parameter-efficient continual learning and full fine-tuning for video-language models, with the question-prediction loss alone accounting for most of the forgetting reduction. However, the framework still assumes known task boundaries during training, limiting its applicability to truly task-free streaming scenarios. The comparison with GPT-4o and Gemini 2.0 Flash reveals that even frontier MLLMs struggle with delayed causality and action/location sequencing, suggesting that temporal reasoning remains an open challenge independent of continual learning. Extending the binding/separation principles to audio-visual or embodied settings, and relaxing the task-boundary assumption, are natural next steps.
