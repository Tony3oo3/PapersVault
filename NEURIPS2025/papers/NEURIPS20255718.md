## Problem

Existing poison-only clean-label backdoor attacks (PCBAs) handle sample selection and trigger design in isolation, leading to limited performance on both attack success rate (ASR) and stealthiness. Additionally, the strong specificity within individual triggers prevents simple combinations of selection strategies from generalizing flexibly across diverse attack types (local high-intensity, global medium-intensity, and global low-intensity poisoning).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255718/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a standard RGB image training dataset with ground-truth labels, a specified target label, and a trigger type with its scale (e.g., patch size for Blended, quantization depth for BppAttack). It outputs a poisoned training dataset where selected samples have been modified with an optimized trigger, along with the indices of chosen samples. The components are modular: the trigger scale determines which negative-function rate (Res-log, Res-x, Res-x², Res-e^x) is optimal for Component A, and the poisoning region (local patch vs. global image) determines whether MSE or GMSD is used in Component B.

## Method

The paper proposes three generalized components that exploit bi-directional collaboration between sample selection and triggers. Component A introduces Category Diversity (measuring how evenly misclassification events spread across categories) alongside the existing Forgetting Event metric, combining them via negative functions at varying rates (O(log x) through O(e^x)); the optimal rate is determined by trigger scale, with larger triggers favoring higher Category Diversity weighting. Component B selects samples whose benign features are visually similar to the trigger (via MSE for local patches or GMSD for global poisoning), exploiting the perceptual gap between human vision and machine feature extraction to conceal triggers. Component C reassigns trigger poisoning intensity across RGB channels based on human visual system sensitivity (shifting intensity toward the blue channel, which is least perceptually sensitive), and for quantization-based attacks introduces MultiBpp with per-channel differential quantization plus Floyd-Steinberg dithering. The components are strategically combinable: A&C maximize ASR, B adds stealthiness, and A&B&C balance both objectives for specific deployment requirements.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with 1% poisoning, Component A (Res-log) improves Badnets-C ASR from 71.74% (Forgetting Event) to 82.13%, and Res-x² improves Blended-C from 71.05% to 84.88%. The full collaborative method (Components A&C) achieves 86.15% ASR for Badnets-C and 84.13% for Blended-C at 1% poisoning, and 89.85% and 94.32% respectively at 2.5% poisoning, versus 20.47% and 53.41% for vanilla. Component C transforms BppAttack from 12.5% ASR to 76.6% (MultiBpp with 24:48:8 quantization), and the optimized Narcissus attack achieves 96.12% ASR by poisoning only 2 images (0.00004% rate). Clean accuracy (BA) remains stable around 94–95% across all configurations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Forgetting Event metric (Gao et al., 2023), the proposed Res-log selection improves Badnets-C ASR by +10.4 pp (71.74% → 82.13%) and Res-x² improves Blended-C by +13.8 pp (71.05% → 84.88%) on CIFAR-10 at 1% poisoning; on CIFAR-100 at 0.2% poisoning, Res-x lifts Badnets-C by +21 pp (59.39% → 80.48%). Against BppAttack (Wang et al., 2022), the MultiBpp variant with 24:48:8 RGB quantization achieves 76.6% ASR versus 12.5% for the original, a 6× improvement, while maintaining clean-label and stealthy properties. Against the vanilla (unoptimized) Badnets-C and Blended-C, the full A&C combination yields +65.7 pp and +30.7 pp ASR gains at 1% poisoning, respectively. The method also outperforms simpler selection baselines (Random, Loss, Gradient) across all four attack types in Table 1, with the largest margins on global-poisoning attacks (MultiBpp-RGB: 83.88% vs. 53.28% for Gradient). Under defense methods (Table 5), A&C-enhanced Blended-C achieves 97.1% ASR versus 76.1% for the Forget-selected baseline, though both remain vulnerable to ABL (<2% ASR).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the clean-label poison-only backdoor attack literature by introducing a modular set of components that explicitly couple sample selection with trigger design, a relationship prior work (e.g., Gao et al. 2023 on Forgetting Event, Wang et al. 2022 on BppAttack) treats in isolation. Component A builds directly on the Forgetting Event metric by adding a Category Diversity factor and showing that the optimal weighting between them is governed by trigger scale. Component C repurposes known human-visual-system knowledge (differential RGB sensitivity) to reassign poisoning intensity across color channels in existing triggers (BadNets, Blended, BppAttack), while Component B leverages perceptual similarity metrics (MSE, GMSD) to select samples where triggers are visually concealed. The overall framing departs from prior work's one-at-a-time optimization of either triggers or samples by proposing composable, attack-agnostic building blocks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The modular component framework gives practitioners a practical toolkit to upgrade existing clean-label attacks without redesigning them from scratch, and the trigger-scale-dependent selection strategy offers actionable guidance for choosing sample-selection metrics. However, the paper is limited to CIFAR-10/100 with a handful of attack types, and the authors themselves note that the A&B integration is 'rudimentary,' leaving open how to more principledly combine stealthiness-oriented and ASR-oriented selection criteria. Broader questions remain about whether these components transfer to non-image modalities, larger-scale datasets (ImageNet), or more recent attack paradigms (diffusion models, federated learning), and whether the RGB-channel manipulation generalizes beyond standard color spaces.
