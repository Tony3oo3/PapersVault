## Problem

Existing theoretical analyses of neural collapse (NC) are limited to data-agnostic unconstrained features models or, when data structure is considered, to shallow multi-layer perceptrons with strong assumptions. No prior work provides end-to-end global optimality guarantees for NC in modern architectures such as ResNets with LayerNorm or transformers, despite NC being empirically observed in these settings. This paper fills both gaps by proving that global optima of deep regularized ResNets and transformers are approximately collapsed, with the approximation tightening as depth increases.

## Input / Output

For ResNets, the input is a matrix X_0 ∈ R^{d_0 × N} of N image samples (MNIST, CIFAR-10) with one-hot class labels Y ∈ R^{K × N}; for transformers, the input is a tensor X_0 ∈ R^{N × V × C} of token sequences (IMDB) with per-token labels Y ∈ R^{N × K × C}. All intermediate representations are constrained to a fixed inner dimension d (64 in experiments) and are zero-mean with norm √d due to LayerNorm. The analytical output is not just class predictions but the three NC metrics (NC1: within/between-class variability ratio, NC2: distance of WW^T from an ETF or identity, NC3: alignment between features and classifier rows) measured in the penultimate layer, which the theory predicts decrease polynomially with depth L.

## Method

The core contribution is a formal reduction of end-to-end deep ResNet/transformer training to an equivalent Generalized Unconstrained Features Model (GUFM) with norm-constrained, zero-mean features. The key mechanism exploits residual connections: a single-layer mapping can be split across L layers with weights scaled as 1/L, making the total Frobenius-norm regularization cost vanish as L→∞ (analogous to (1+a/L)^L → exp(a) with total cost a²/L). The authors explicitly construct layer-by-layer weight assignments that move each sample along a smooth trajectory on the feasible hypersphere toward its GUFM-optimal position, with per-layer weights chosen so that ReLU activations affect only the target sample. For single-linear-layer-per-block architectures with constant regularization, this yields convergence of global optima to GUFM optima (Theorem 4.3); for double-layer-per-block architectures, the same holds with depth-dependent regularization λ(L) = o(log(L)^{-1}) (Theorem 4.6). Since the GUFM optima for CE and MSE loss are perfectly collapsed (Lemma 4.2), deep ResNets and transformers inherit approximate NC with a convergence rate of Õ(L^{-1/2}).

## Results

The paper's main empirical result (Figure 1) shows that all three neural collapse metrics (NC1, NC2, NC3) decrease monotonically as the number of blocks L increases from 2 to 34, across ResNets and transformers on CIFAR-10, MNIST, and IMDB. For large enough depth, the log₁₀ of the NC metrics follow a roughly log-linear trend with an average slope of approximately −0.335, consistent with the theoretical prediction of Õ(L^{−1/2}) convergence to perfect collapse. The metrics are somewhat larger (i.e., less strong collapse) than those typically observed in MLPs, attributed to the different loss landscape introduced by residual connections and LayerNorm.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper without direct method-to-method comparisons; the `compares_to` list is empty. The experimental validation is self-contained: the same architecture family is trained at multiple depths (L ∈ {2, 3, 5, 8, 13, 21, 34}) and the NC metrics at convergence are compared across depths. The key finding is that deeper networks consistently exhibit stronger collapse (lower NC1, NC2, NC3) across all four experimental settings (L-RN1 on CIFAR-10, L-T11 on CIFAR-10, pre-LN L-T11 on IMDB, and L-RN2 on MNIST with λ ∝ L^{−1}), confirming the asymptotic optimality guarantees of Theorems 4.3 and 4.6. The observed slope of ≈ −0.335 in log-log space falls within the theoretical range of [−1/2, −1/4] predicted by the convergence rate analysis. No competing method is benchmarked; the contribution is the first end-to-end global optimality proof for NC in ResNets and transformers, extending prior results that were limited to MLPs [23] or shallow networks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the unconstrained features model (UFM) framework of Mixon et al. [42] and Zhu et al. [71] from a data-agnostic modeling tool to a formally justified surrogate for end-to-end training of deep ResNets and transformers. It departs from prior end-to-end NC results, which were limited to shallow MLPs [27, 19, 61] or required strong structural assumptions [46, 44, 64], and from the only prior deep end-to-end result for MLPs with linear heads [23], by handling residual connections, LayerNorm, and attention layers. The key conceptual departure is proving that the GUFM (a generalization of UFM with norm and mean-zero constraints) is not merely a heuristic simplification but the asymptotic limit of the actual end-to-end optimization problem as depth grows, thereby resolving a criticism that UFM dynamics are inconsistent with full-network training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first theoretical analysis of neural collapse in transformer architectures and the first end-to-end global optimality guarantee for NC in deep networks beyond simplified MLPs, providing a formal justification for the widespread use of UFM in the NC literature. The practical takeaway—that increasing depth provably tightens collapse—gives practitioners a principled recipe, though the convergence rate is only O(L^{-1/2}) up to log factors, meaning very deep networks are needed for near-perfect collapse. Open questions include the structure of global optima for double-layer blocks with constant regularization (where NC is provably not reached for some datasets), the evolution of NC metrics across intermediate layers (deep NC), and whether the results extend to pre-LN architectures or non-unique-label settings like language pretraining.
