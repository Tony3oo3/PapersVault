## Problem

Structure-controllable diffusion models for object-detection data augmentation suffer from content-position mismatches (wrong number or placement of objects) and semantic leakage (generated content not matching its textual label), and existing remedies require either complex multi-pass post-filtering pipelines or expensive fine-tuning on large-scale datasets. This paper asks whether these failures can be corrected on-the-fly during a single diffusion sampling pass, without any additional training of the generator.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252515/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes an original RGB image (512×512) together with its ground-truth bounding boxes, class labels, a Canny edge map, and per-category text prompts, all fed into a frozen Stable Diffusion v1.5 + ControlNet pipeline with a 25-step DDIM sampler. It outputs annotation-consistent synthetic images in which every object falls within its designated bounding box and carries the correct semantic label; the resulting augmented set (47,200 images, 227,406 objects on COCO) is combined with the original training set to jointly train downstream detectors such as Faster R-CNN, YOLOX, or DEIM.

## Method

ReCon inserts two training-free mechanisms into every diffusion denoising step. First, Region-Guided Rectification (RGR) periodically decodes the noisy latent via a DeepCache-accelerated N-step prediction to obtain a clean estimate, runs Grounded-SAM on it, and uses IoU matching against the target annotations to build a binary rectification mask; masked regions are then overwritten with the corresponding noised latent of the original image, exploiting the overridability property of diffusion sampling. This rectification is applied at four timesteps (0.75T, 0.5T, 0.25T, 0.1T) to progressively correct layout, semantics, and fine detail. Second, Region-Aligned Cross-Attention (RACA) encodes each target category's text prompt independently and performs cross-attention only between the visual tokens within that category's bounding-box region and its dedicated text embedding, preventing inter-category semantic leakage that standard global cross-attention permits. Both modules are parameter-free and plug directly into any existing structure-controllable generator (ControlNet, GLIGEN, Instance Diffusion) without retraining.

## Results

On COCO, ReCon integrated with ControlNet achieves mAP 35.5 (AP50 56.2, AP75 38.4), surpassing the ControlNet baseline (34.9) and all fine-tuned competitors including DetDiffusion (35.4) and GeoDiffusion (34.8). When combined with Instance Diffusion, ReCon reaches the best overall mAP of 35.6 (AP50 35.6, AP75 38.4, AP^l 46.4). In data-scarce settings (10% COCO), ReCon lifts mAP from 18.5 to 21.7, and on PASCAL VOC it improves mAP from 77.1 to 78.5. The FID of generated samples also improves from 13.82 (ControlNet) to 12.85 (full ReCon), confirming higher image fidelity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReCon outperforms all 11 compared baselines on COCO mAP. Against general-purpose control methods, ControlNet+ReCon (35.5) beats ControlNet (34.9), ControlNet-XS (35.1), Background-inpainting (35.1), and Layout Diffusion (34.0) by 0.4–1.5 mAP points. Against fine-tuned COCO-specific generators, ReCon surpasses DetDiffusion (35.4) by 0.1 mAP and GeoDiffusion (34.8) by 0.7 mAP, while also improving GLIGEN from 34.6 to 35.3 and Instance Diffusion from 35.0 to 35.6 without any additional training. In data-scarce regimes (10% COCO), ReCon (21.7 mAP) edges out RandAugment (21.4) and ControlNet (21.2), and on PASCAL VOC it achieves 78.5 mAP versus 77.8 for ControlNet and 77.7 for RandAugment. Compared to the image-editing baseline SDEdit (35.2 mAP), ReCon gains 0.3 mAP while providing region-level control rather than uniform editing strength. Notably, ReCon's gains are training-free and plug-and-play, whereas DetDiffusion, GeoDiffusion, and GLIGEN all require dataset-specific fine-tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

ReCon builds directly on Stable Diffusion's latent diffusion framework and ControlNet's structural conditioning mechanism, but departs from prior generative data augmentation methods in a key way: rather than requiring fine-tuning on large-scale datasets (as DetDiffusion, GeoDiffusion, and Instance Diffusion do) or relying on post-hoc filtering of generated samples (as Fang et al. and CLIP-based approaches do), it injects region-level perception feedback directly into the diffusion sampling trajectory. The two proposed components—Region-Guided Rectification (using Grounded-SAM to detect and correct misgenerated regions via latent replacement) and Region-Aligned Cross-Attention (enforcing per-region text-image alignment)—are designed as training-free, plug-and-play modules that can be layered onto any existing structural-control generator without retraining.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in eliminating the fine-tuning bottleneck that has limited the adoption of diffusion-based augmentation in data-scarce settings; a 3× augmentation with ReCon outperforms 7× simple duplication, which is directly relevant to practitioners who cannot afford large-scale retraining of generators. However, the method still requires an additional perception model (Grounded-SAM) at inference time, adding roughly 1 second per sample, and the absolute mAP gains over strong baselines (e.g., DetDiffusion) are modest (~0.1–0.6 points). Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to tasks beyond object detection (e.g., segmentation, tracking), whether stronger or more efficient perception models would yield further gains, and whether the rectification strategy remains effective for very dense or highly overlapping scenes.
