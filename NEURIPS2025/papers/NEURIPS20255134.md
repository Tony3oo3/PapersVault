## Problem

Existing recommendation benchmarks rely on sparse behaviors (reviews, ratings) that poorly reflect real browsing or purchasing sequences, and inconsistent evaluation protocols (data splits, candidate pools, metrics) across studies make fair model comparison nearly impossible. Additionally, no publicly available dataset captures realistic, consented web browsing sequences at scale for evaluating next-page prediction in large candidate pools.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255134/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For public datasets, models receive a sequence of item IDs (length up to 50) and must rank the full item pool to predict the next interaction; for ClueWeb-Reco, the input is a sequence of webpage titles (average length ~12, max 137) and the candidate pool is 87 million ClueWeb22-B EN documents. The LLM-QueryGen variant verbalizes the browsing history as concatenated titles, prompts an LLM to generate a natural-language query, then encodes that query with MiniCPM-Embedding-Light and retrieves the nearest document via a DiskANN index as the predicted next webpage.

## Method

ORBIT standardizes evaluation with a leave-one-out split, full-ranking over the entire item pool, and Recall@K/NDCG@K (K ∈ {1,10,50,100}) across 12 representative models (9 ID-based, 3 content-based) on 5 public datasets. The novel ClueWeb-Reco hidden test is built via a two-stage pipeline: (1) collecting real U.S. browsing histories from 1,747 consented subjects via MTurk/Prolific with online and offline quality-control filters yielding 1,024 sessions of 12,282 URL-timestamp pairs, and (2) replacing each URL with its most semantically similar page in ClueWeb22-B EN using MiniCPM-Embedding-Light dense embeddings and a DiskANN approximate-nearest-neighbor index, ensuring full synthetic privacy while preserving behavioral patterns. For the hidden test, the paper introduces LLM-QueryGen, which frames next-page recommendation as dense retrieval by prompting LLMs (GPT-4o, Gemini-2.5-Flash, DeepSeek-V3, etc.) to generate a query from the browsing history, then retrieving the closest ClueWeb22 document as the prediction.

## Results

On the public benchmark (Table 4), HLLM achieves the best average NDCG@10 of 0.0641 across five datasets, with particularly strong performance on Amazon Books (NDCG@10 = 0.0663) and Amazon Sports (0.0245). Among ID-based models, HSTU leads with an average NDCG@10 of 0.0589, followed closely by S3-Rec (0.0590) and BERT4Rec (0.0532). On the ClueWeb-Reco hidden test (Table 5), DeepSeek-V3-QueryGen achieves the best zero-shot performance with Recall@10 = 0.0127 and NDCG@10 = 0.0082, substantially outperforming the content-based baselines HLLM (Recall@10 = 0.0088, NDCG@10 = 0.0041) and TASTE (Recall@10 = 0.0020, NDCG@10 = 0.0015).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among the nine ID-based sequential models, HSTU (NDCG@10 avg 0.0589) and S3-Rec (0.0590) are the strongest, both benefiting from transformer-based architectures that capture long-range dependencies; they outperform earlier RNN-based models like GRU4Rec (0.0447) and Caser (0.0346) by 14–24% in average NDCG. The three content-based models (SASRecF, TASTE, HLLM) generally surpass ID-based counterparts, with HLLM achieving the best overall average NDCG@10 of 0.0641, driven by dominant performance on Amazon Books (0.0663) and Amazon Sports (0.0245). However, HLLM collapses on Amazon Beauty (NDCG@10 = 0.0027), where the tiny training set (2,029 interactions) is insufficient for its billion-scale LLM backbone, while STAMP (0.0771) and SASRec (0.0630) lead on that dataset. On the ClueWeb-Reco hidden test, all LLM-QueryGen baselines outperform both content-based recommenders: DeepSeek-V3-QueryGen achieves Recall@100 of 0.0371 versus HLLM's 0.0176 and TASTE's 0.0039, confirming that LLM-generated retrieval queries generalize far better to the 87-million-item candidate pool than trained sequential models.

## Relation to Prior Work

ORBIT carves new ground in recommendation benchmarking by addressing two gaps that prior frameworks (RecBole, BARS, DaisyRec, RecBench) leave open: inconsistent evaluation protocols across studies and the absence of a realistic, privacy-preserving hidden test. The ClueWeb-Reco dataset repurposes the soft-matching strategy from TREC CAST and MSMARCO conversational data synthesis, adapting it to sequential browsing behavior rather than query sessions. The LLM-QueryGen baseline is a novel contribution that reframes sequential recommendation as dense retrieval via LLM-generated queries, a formulation not present in prior recommendation benchmarks. The paper positions itself as a unifying evaluation layer over existing models (SASRec, BERT4Rec, HSTU, HLLM, TASTE) rather than proposing a new architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ORBIT addresses a well-documented reproducibility crisis in recommendation research where varying data splits and candidate-pool strategies make cross-paper comparisons unreliable. The ClueWeb-Reco hidden test with 87 million candidate items exposes a critical gap: all evaluated models achieve Recall@10 below 0.013, revealing that current sequential recommenders are far from solving large-scale, diverse-item recommendation. The finding that zero-shot LLM-QueryGen (particularly DeepSeek-V3) matches or exceeds a trained hierarchical LLM (HLLM) on this benchmark suggests a promising but underexplored direction for LLM-integrated recommenders. Key limitations include the small session count (1,024), U.S.-only demographic, and the absence of error bars or multi-run statistics, leaving open whether the observed model rankings are robust.
