## Problem

Existing multimodal hallucination benchmarks conflate perception-induced errors (misreading the image) with reasoning-induced errors (flawed logic despite correct perception), making it impossible to diagnose where multimodal reasoning actually breaks down. MIRAGE addresses this by isolating reasoning hallucinations through questions where MLLMs correctly perceive the input image yet still produce defective reasoning chains, enabling fine-grained attribution of hallucination sources.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254099/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each MIRAGE question pairs an RGB image with a text question spanning seven reasoning taxonomies (geometry, algebra, arithmetic, science, spatial, statistical, and logical/IQ reasoning). The model produces a structured response containing a reasoning chain (with extractable intermediate steps and claims) and a final answer. Evaluation yields three complementary scores: answer accuracy, step/claim-level factuality F1 scores (measuring coverage and precision against ground-truth reasoning chains), and the LLMs Hallucination Score (LHS), a multi-dimension ensemble score from three LLM judges across five reference chains covering factual accuracy, logical consistency, completeness, conceptual accuracy, and strategy appropriateness.

## Method

The paper contributes both a diagnostic benchmark (MIRAGE, 1,329 questions with three-tier annotations: final answers, intermediate steps/claims, and verified ground-truth reasoning chains) and a mitigation baseline (Logos). Logos trains via GRPO (Group Relative Policy Optimization) with two key innovations: Curriculum Reinforcement Fine-Tuning (CRFT), which progressively increases question difficulty by filtering for samples where the model achieves partial but not full accuracy, ensuring non-zero advantages for stable optimization; and Online Reward Filtration (ORF), which discards iterations where all G sampled responses receive identical rewards. At inference, Collaborative Hint Inference (CHI) injects a topic-specific hint (general problem-solving schema) and a question-specific hint (key entities and starting conditions) generated by an auxiliary LLM, reducing reasoning complexity. The core insight is that RL-based optimization of logic-consistent chain generation reduces logical and fabrication hallucinations without introducing new knowledge, while spatial hallucinations remain largely unaffected by scaling or training.

## Results

On the MIRAGE benchmark, O1 achieves the highest accuracy at 49.7%, followed by Gemini-2-Flash-Thinking at 47.6%. The proposed Logos-7B (built on Qwen2.5-VL-7B) achieves 37.1% accuracy, 43.3 F_step, 38.3 F_claim, and 0.6568 LHS, outperforming its base model by +8.3 accuracy, +8.6 F_step, and +6.6 F_claim, and approaching the performance of the much larger Virgo-72B (37.4% accuracy). On MathVista, Logos-7B reaches 72.3% versus 68.2% for the base model. Logos-3B similarly improves over Qwen2.5-VL-3B from 18.8% to 29.4% accuracy on MIRAGE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Logos-7B (37.1% accuracy) substantially outperforms all 7B-scale baselines: Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct (28.8%), Qwen2.5-VL-7B+VIC (26.9%), Qwen2.5-VL-7B+Reflection (26.7%), R1-OneVision-7B (22.9%), Mulberry-Qwen2-VL-7B (22.6%), InternVL-2.5-8B (20.8%), Llama-3.2-Vision-11B (18.7%), and Llava-CoT-11B (17.4%). It nearly matches the much larger Virgo-72B (37.4%) and approaches Qwen2.5-VL-72B (38.8%), demonstrating that CRFT+CHI can close the gap between 7B and 72B models. On MathVista, Logos-7B achieves 72.3% vs. 68.2% for the base model and 71.9% for Virgo-72B. Logos-3B (29.4%) similarly dominates its scale, beating Qwen2.5-VL-3B (18.8%) and Phi-3.5 (12.9%) by over 10 points. Notably, training-free methods (VIC, Reflection) and SFT-based methods (R1-OneVision, Mulberry) fail to improve over the base model on MIRAGE, while Logos's RL-based approach consistently reduces logical hallucination by 15.4% and fabrication hallucination by 10%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground within the multimodal hallucination evaluation space by isolating reasoning-induced hallucinations from perception-induced ones, a distinction that prior benchmarks (POPE, HallusionBench, MME-CoT) conflate. For mitigation, it adapts the GRPO reinforcement learning paradigm from text-only LLM reasoning (DeepSeek-R1, DeepSeekMath) to the multimodal setting, augmenting it with curriculum scheduling (CRFT) and collaborative hint inference (CHI) — components not present in prior multimodal RL work. The multi-granular evaluation (accuracy, step/claim factuality, LHS) extends the LLM-as-judge paradigm from single-score assessment to hierarchical reasoning-chain diagnostics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that spatial hallucinations resist improvement from both model scaling and higher-quality pretraining data challenges the prevailing assumption that scaling uniformly reduces all error types in MLLMs, pointing to a fundamental architectural limitation in visual-spatial reasoning. The benchmark's design (correctly perceived inputs, defective reasoning) provides a clean diagnostic tool that prior benchmarks cannot offer, enabling targeted research on reasoning-specific failures. Open questions include whether the spatial hallucination gap can be closed with dedicated visual reasoning architectures, whether Logos's CRFT+CHI framework generalizes beyond mathematical reasoning to open-ended multimodal tasks, and whether the small dataset size (1,329 questions) limits statistical power for fine-grained hallucination type analysis.
