## Problem

Inverse folding models are trained to maximize sequence likelihood, but sequence similarity is not a reliable proxy for structural similarity, and generative models can produce low-quality or repetitive sequences. Existing preference-optimization approaches for protein design depend on costly wet-lab assays, limiting scale and generalizability. This paper asks whether folding-model feedback alone can serve as a scalable, fully in silico signal for preference-based fine-tuning of inverse folding models.

## Input / Output

The method takes a protein 3D backbone structure (from CATH 4.2, TS50, or TS500) as input and produces amino acid sequences as output. During training, N candidate sequences are sampled per structure, each folded by ESMFold, and scored against the ground-truth structure via TM-Align; the top and bottom 50% by TM-Score form chosen/rejected preference pairs. Evaluation uses 10 samples per structure at temperature 0.15, reporting mean recovery rate, perplexity, TM-Score, and RMSD.

## Method

The core pipeline is a fully in silico DPO loop: sample sequences from the inverse folding model, fold them with ESMFold, compute TM-Scores against the target structure, and construct balanced preference pairs (top 50% chosen, bottom 50% rejected). The inverse folding model is then fine-tuned with a combined DPO loss and an SFT regularization term on chosen sequences, which prevents model degeneration caused by the small amino-acid vocabulary where rejected sequences share many residues with chosen ones. The approach is applied to both ProteinMPNN (~1M params, requiring a simplified task with wild-type sequences and a TM-Score threshold to converge) and InstructPLM (~7B params, using LoRA with rank 16). A multi-round iterative variant reinitializes the reference model at each round and increases sampling diversity (temperature 1.1, top-p 1.0, 200 sequences per structure), yielding a 79.5% average TM-Score gain on 10 hard structures.

## Results

On the CATH 4.2 test set, InstructPLM-DPO improves sequence recovery from 53.58% to 55.21% and average TM-Score from 0.77 to 0.81, while ProteinMPNN-DPO improves recovery from 40.27% to 41.29%. On the harder TS50 and TS500 benchmarks, InstructPLM-DPO gains 1.81 and 0.24 percentage points in recovery respectively, with only marginal perplexity increases (0.06 and 0.03). In the multi-round iterative setting on 10 challenging structures, the mean TM-Score increases by 79.5% (from 0.39 to 0.70) over the baseline, with the improvement confirmed using both ESMFold and AlphaFold 3 as structure predictors.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper fine-tunes two base models (ProteinMPNN ~1M params and InstructPLM ~7B params) with DPO and compares against 10 prior inverse-folding methods. InstructPLM-DPO consistently outperforms its baseline InstructPLM on recovery across all three datasets (53.58→55.21% on CATH 4.2, 60.20→62.01% on TS50, 66.13→66.37% on TS500), and ProteinMPNN-DPO similarly beats ProteinMPNN (40.27→41.29%, 44.72→45.91%, 46.58→48.23%). However, in absolute recovery, InstructPLM-DPO (55.21%) still trails the strongest baselines KW-Design (60.77%) and LM-Design (55.65%) on CATH 4.2, and ProteinMPNN-DPO (41.29%) remains well below ESM-IF (48.95%) and PiFold (51.66%). The paper's key differentiator is structure similarity: InstructPLM-DPO achieves a TM-Score of 0.81 vs. 0.77 for the baseline, and in the multi-round setting on 10 hard structures the TM-Score jumps from 0.39 to 0.70 (79.5% relative gain), a metric not reported by most competing baselines. On the more challenging CATH 4.3 test set (new Classes not seen in training), InstructPLM-DPO still improves over its baseline (TM-Score 0.57→0.60, recovery 41.85→43.62), confirming generalization beyond the training distribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) from NLP to protein inverse folding, building on prior DPO applications to protein language models (ProteinDPO, DPO_pLM, Park et al.) that relied on small, wet-lab-curated preference datasets. The key departure is a fully in silico feedback loop: the inverse-folding model generates candidate sequences, a folding model (ESMFold) predicts their structures, and TM-Score against the ground-truth structure provides the preference signal, eliminating the need for costly biochemical assays. The method is applied to two architecturally distinct baselines (ProteinMPNN, ~1M params; InstructPLM, ~7B params), and a modified DPO+SFT loss is introduced to prevent degeneration caused by the small amino-acid vocabulary.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work demonstrates that folding-model feedback can serve as a scalable, automated reward signal for protein design, opening a path to iterative refinement without experimental validation. The counterintuitive finding that increasing contrastive samples per structure degrades structural fidelity (while slightly boosting recovery) highlights an important data-quality bottleneck that future preference-based protein design must address. Open questions include whether the paradigm generalizes to other feedback types (thermodynamic stability, binding affinity), whether stronger baselines or larger multi-round experiments can push the 79.5% hard-structure gain to broader settings, and whether the modest single-round gains (0.77→0.81 TM-Score on CATH 4.2) compound meaningfully at scale.
