## Problem

Existing SSMs built on HiPPO (e.g., S4, Mamba) rely on Legendre or Fourier bases that struggle with non-stationary signals containing sharp transients, localized spikes, and sparse structure. While the SaFARi framework generalized SSM construction to arbitrary frames, no work had explored wavelet frames as the basis for the SSM itself, leaving a gap in handling signals where polynomial and Fourier representations lose fine-grained detail.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253630/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sequential signal (time series or audio) one sample at a time in an online/streaming fashion, maintaining a fixed-size state vector whose dimension is independent of sequence length. The output is a compressed coefficient vector over the wavelet frame that can be inverted to reconstruct the full input signal, and from which spike locations, amplitudes, and displacements can be extracted for peak-detection tasks. The translated variant operates over a sliding window of fixed width θ, while the scaled variant compresses the entire input history into the same fixed-size state.

## Method

WaLRUS constructs SSMs from redundant Daubechies-22 wavelet frames using the SaFARi framework, which derives the A and B matrices numerically from the frame's dual without requiring closed-form solutions. The frame is built via dyadic multiresolution scaling with overlapping shifts (shift constant m=0.01) to satisfy the frame condition and introduce redundancy, then reduced to its effective dimensionality N_eff via SVD of the frame operator. Two variants are provided: scaled-WaLRUS (compressing all history) and translated-WaLRUS (sliding-window), both of which are experimentally observed to be stably diagonalizable, reducing sequential updates to O(NL) and enabling O(log L) parallel convolution-based inference. This contrasts with Legendre-based SSMs, which are not stably diagonalizable and suffer from kernel warping that causes coefficient loss at window boundaries.

## Results

WaLRUS (WaveS/WaveT) achieves the lowest reconstruction MSE across all six datasets, winning 99.53% of instances on M4, 95.75% on Speech Commands, and 100% on all synthetic benchmarks (Blocks, Spikes, Bumps, Piecepoly). For peak detection with the scaled measure, WaveS misses 0% of spikes versus 2.5% (LegS) and 0.62% (FouS), with relative amplitude error of 5.5% versus 16.2% and 11.8% respectively. With the translated measure, WaveT achieves 99.95% instance-wise wins on Spikes, missing only 0.27% of peaks compared to 6.4% (LegT) and 13.0% (FouT).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares WaLRUS against two HiPPO-based baselines: HiPPO-Legendre (LegS/LegT) and HiPPO-Fourier (FouS/FouT), all with equal effective state sizes. WaLRUS dominates on every metric and dataset: for scaled-measure MSE, WaveS wins 99.53% of M4 instances and 100% of synthetic benchmark instances, while LegS and FouS win 0% on most datasets. For peak detection (the most discriminative task), the gap is dramatic — on Spikes with the translated measure, WaveT misses only 0.27% of peaks versus 6.4% for LegT and 13.0% for FouT, and achieves 99.95% instance-wise wins. The advantage is most pronounced for signals with sharp transients (Spikes, Blocks) and less so for smoother signals (M4, Bumps), where WaveT's MSE improvement over LegT is modest. The paper attributes these gains to wavelet frames' superior time-frequency localization, which avoids the kernel warping and dead-zone artifacts that plague Legendre and Fourier SSMs (Fig. 4–5).

## Relation to Prior Work

WaLRUS extends the SaFARi framework (Babaei et al., 2025), which itself generalized HiPPO's closed-form polynomial projection to arbitrary frames, by instantiating the frame with Daubechies wavelets. The key conceptual departure from HiPPO and its descendants (S4, Mamba) is replacing the Legendre or Fourier basis with a redundant, compactly supported wavelet frame, enabling the SSM to capture localized, non-stationary features that polynomial bases inherently smooth over. The paper explicitly distinguishes itself from recent wavelet+Mamba works [31, 32] that apply wavelet transforms only to model inputs without altering the SSM core, positioning WaLRUS as the first to substitute the SSM's internal basis itself.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Because WaLRUS is a drop-in replacement for the HiPPO layer in S4 and Mamba, and because wavelet-based SSMs are stably diagonalizable (unlike Legendre-based ones), it opens a path to O(L) parallel inference that Legendre SSMs cannot match. The kernel analysis in Section 3.3 provides a concrete diagnostic—dead zones in LegT, poor forgetting in FouT—that explains *why* wavelets win, not just that they do. However, the paper tests only Daubechies-22, uses no learned parameters, and evaluates purely on function approximation rather than end-to-end sequence tasks, leaving open whether the advantage persists when the SSM is embedded in a larger trainable architecture and whether other wavelet families (Morlet, coiflets, biorthogonal) offer better trade-offs in effective dimensionality.
