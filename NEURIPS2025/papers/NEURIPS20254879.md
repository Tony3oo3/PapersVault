## Problem

Learning coarse correlated equilibria (CCE) in polyhedral games—such as Colonel Blotto and congestion games—requires per-iteration complexity that scales polylogarithmically in the exponentially large action set size N. While kernelization enabled efficient full-information learning, extending it to the more realistic bandit and semi-bandit feedback settings remained open, with existing approaches yielding impractical runtimes (e.g., d^10 or d^9 dependence). The paper asks whether kernelization techniques can be extended beyond full-information to achieve state-of-the-art runtime with minimal dependence on game parameters d and m.

## Input / Output

The method operates on polyhedral games where each player's actions are d-dimensional binary vectors with at most m ones, and the loss is linear in the action vector. Under bandit feedback, each player observes only the scalar loss of her sampled action; under semi-bandit feedback, she observes the losses of all active coordinates. The algorithm requires only Θ(d²) kernel evaluations per iteration in the bandit case and Θ(d) in the semi-bandit case, plus Θ(d) additional kernel calls for exact sampling from the MWU distribution. The output is a time-averaged joint strategy that constitutes an ε-CCE with high probability, with total runtime depending polynomially (with small exponents) on d, m, and 1/ε rather than on N.

## Method

The core contribution is a kernelization-based framework that efficiently implements standard bandit loss estimators over exponentially large action sets. For the bandit setting, the paper kernelizes GEOMETRICHEDGE by showing that the second moment (autocorrelation matrix) of the MWU distribution can be computed via only d²+1 kernel evaluations (Theorem 3.1), enabling construction of the unbiased combinatorial bandit estimator without brute-forcing over actions. For the semi-bandit setting, it kernelizes the implicit exploration (IX) loss estimator using first-moment kernels already available from the full-information framework. A general exact sampling procedure (Procedure SAMPLING) iteratively draws each coordinate from a Bernoulli distribution whose probability is computed via conditional kernel evaluations, requiring only d additional kernel calls. The framework is instantiated for Colonel Blotto (polynomial-based kernel computation in O(nk log n) per kernel, improving over prior O(n²k) layered-graph representations), graphic matroid congestion games (Matrix-Tree Theorem with fast rank-1 LU updates of Laplacian cofactors), and network congestion games, yielding runtimes that improve prior work by factors of n^13 k^2 or |V|^23 in the bandit setting.

## Results

In Colonel Blotto games under bandit feedback, the proposed kernelized GEOMETRICHEDGE achieves a runtime of Õ(n^{2+ω}k^{6+ω}/ε³) to learn an ε-CCE, improving over Zimmert and Lattimore [65] by a factor of approximately n^{13}k² (from n^{18}k^{11}/ε²). Under semi-bandit feedback, the runtime is Õ(n²k⁴/ε²), and under full-information feedback it is Õ(|P|nk³/ε). In graphic matroid congestion games (bandit), the runtime is Õ(|E|³|V|⁶(|V|^{ω-1}+|E|)/ε³), dramatically improving over the impractical |V|^{29} dependence of [65]. In network congestion games, the semi-bandit runtime Õ(|E|^{1+ω}K²/ε²) improves the K-dependence from K³ (György et al. [35]) to K².

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against five prior approaches across three game classes and three feedback settings. Against Zimmert and Lattimore [65] (the strongest prior bandit baseline), the proposed method wins decisively in all three game classes: in Colonel Blotto the runtime drops from n^{18}k^{11} to n^{2+ω}k^{6+ω} (a factor of ~n^{13}k²), in graphic matroid congestion games from |V|^{29} to |E|³|V|⁶(|V|^{ω-1}+|E|), and in network congestion games from |E|⁹K^{10} to |E|^{2+ω}K⁴. Against Leon et al. [43] in Colonel Blotto bandit, the proposed method eliminates the problematic dependence on the arbitrarily large 1/λ_min (inverse minimum eigenvalue) and uses a more compact O(nk) representation versus O(n²k). Against Beaglehole et al. [12] in the full-information setting, the proposed method achieves a better ε-dependence (1/ε vs 1/ε²) and a more compact O(nk) representation, though [12] uses a smaller O(k log n) representation. In network congestion games under semi-bandit feedback, the proposed method improves the K-dependence from K³ (György et al. [35]) to K², and from the impractical |E|⁹/ε⁴ of Panageas et al. [49] to |E|^{1+ω}K²/ε². The key structural advantage across all comparisons is the use of a direct O(nk) or O(|E|) representation rather than the O(n²k) layered-graph or polytope representations required by prior work.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the kernelization framework of Farina et al. [32]—which enabled efficient MWU simulation in polyhedral games under full-information feedback—to the more realistic bandit and semi-bandit settings. In the bandit case, it kernelizes the classical GEOMETRICHEDGE algorithm [28] by showing that the second moments of the MWU distribution (needed for unbiased loss estimation) can be computed via only Θ(d²) kernel evaluations, a capability that was previously unavailable outside path-planning problems. In the semi-bandit case, it kernelizes the implicit exploration estimator of Neu [48], which is compatible with the first-moment kernels already used in the full-information setting. The paper also introduces a more compact Θ(nk) representation for Colonel Blotto games, replacing the Θ(n²k) layered-graph representation used by prior work [12, 43, 65].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper resolves several open questions: it provides the first efficient kernelized implementation of GEOMETRICHEDGE for general polyhedral games (answering a question from Cesa-Bianchi and Lugosi [24]), and it yields 1/ε convergence to CCE in Colonel Blotto and graphic matroid congestion games under full information (addressing an open question of Beaglehole et al. [12]). The dramatic runtime improvements—e.g., a factor of ~n¹³k² over Zimmert and Lattimore [65] for Colonel Blotto—make CCE computation in these games practically feasible for the first time under partial information. Key open questions remain: whether an FPTAS for correlated equilibria (stronger than CCE) can be designed, whether the 1/ε² dependence in the bandit setting can be achieved with better d-dependence, and the computational complexity of Nash equilibria in Colonel Blotto games with monotone piecewise-constant utilities.
