## Problem

Existing GNN-based boundary value problem (BVP) solvers rely primarily on vertex adjacency in meshes, neglecting higher-order topological elements (edges, faces, cells) that carry fundamental physical meaning through differential forms. This limitation is especially acute for time-independent PDEs like electrostatics and magnetostatics, where the absence of temporal guidance and limited feature representation make standard graph message passing insufficient to capture governing physics such as curl-free and divergence constraints.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a discrete simplicial mesh (triangles in 2D, tetrahedra in 3D) along with a scalar charge density field ρ defined on all elements and masked vector fields (E, D or H, B) specified only on boundary nodes. Internally, these vector inputs are converted into integrated differential forms (circulations along edges, fluxes through faces, volume integrals over cells) via De Rham mapping before processing. The output is a complete vector field on all nodes, recovered through Whitney element interpolation rather than simple nodal averaging, which preserves physical properties like tangential continuity of E across material interfaces.

## Method

DEC-HOGNN encodes vector fields into k-simplex features (integrated forms) and processes them through a higher-order GNN that performs message passing over four adjacency types—boundary, co-boundary, upper, and lower—on both the primal and dual manifolds, where the dual adjacencies are related to primal ones by Hodge star duality. The key insight is that the co-boundary adjacency on primal edges implicitly enforces the curl-free constraint (dE=0), while the dual co-boundary adjacency encodes Gauss's Law (dD=ρ), giving each message-passing step a direct physical interpretation. Physics-informed loss terms (divergence-free, curl-free, and boundary conditions) are derived as simple sums of higher-order features rather than point-wise PDE residuals, avoiding the incompatibility of auto-differentiation with discrete graph vertices. A universal approximation theorem is proven for Poisson problems, and the encoder-decoder is shown to preserve sufficient information on sufficiently fine meshes (Theorem 2).

## Results

DEC-HOGNN achieves the lowest MSE across three of four BVP tasks: 0.623 on 2D electrostatics, 0.875 on 2D magnetostatics, and 0.195 on 3D electrostatics. On 3D magnetostatics, it reaches 0.158 MSE, slightly behind the Galerkin-Type Attention baseline (0.120). The method outperforms the strongest GNN-based BVP solver (GT-based) by 37% on 2D electrostatics (0.623 vs. 0.990) and by 20% on 3D electrostatics (0.195 vs. 0.244), while general-purpose neural operators (GNOT, Transolver) degrade severely on 3D tasks with MSEs exceeding 4.0.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DEC-HOGNN wins on 3 of 4 tasks against all 9 baselines. On 2D electrostatics, it beats the closest competitor GT-based (0.990) by 37% and the general-purpose operators (GNOT 2.064, Transolver 2.751) by more than 3×. On 2D magnetostatics, it outperforms GNOT (1.142) by 23% and Transolver (7.432) by 8.5×. On 3D electrostatics, it leads GT-based (0.244) by 20% and GNOT (7.266) by 37×. The sole exception is 3D magnetostatics, where Galerkin-Type Attention (0.120) edges out DEC-HOGNN (0.158) by ~24%, though both substantially outperform other baselines (GCN-based 0.182, GT-based 0.171). The paper attributes the poor 3D performance of GNOT and Transolver to their reliance on temporal or multi-scale features that are unavailable in static BVPs, where boundary conditions on a minority of nodes determine the entire solution.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of higher-order GNN message passing (following the four-adjacency framework of Bodnar et al. [26]) and the mathematical structure of Discrete/Finite Element Exterior Calculus (DEC/FEEC) [14,15]. It departs from prior GNN-based BVP solvers [10] that rely solely on vertex adjacency by encoding differential forms as integrated k-simplex features, giving each message-passing neighborhood a direct physics interpretation (e.g., co-boundary adjacency enforces curl-free or Gauss-law constraints). Unlike general-purpose neural operators (FNO, GNOT, Transolver) designed for time-evolving PDEs, DEC-HOGNN is purpose-built for time-independent BVPs where boundary conditions, not initial data, determine the solution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a principled, coordinate-independent route to solving form-based PDEs on arbitrary meshes, with physics-informed losses that avoid the pointwise sampling and autodifferentiation required by PINNs on discrete graphs. The universal approximation theorem for Poisson problems and the non-cycle form estimation theorem (Theorem 1) give theoretical grounding that is rare in neural-operator literature. Key limitations include dependence on mesh quality (Whitney elements degrade on poorly shaped elements), demonstration restricted to electrostatic/magnetostatic BVPs, and the open question of extending to time-dependent or nonlinear PDEs. The approach also naturally generalizes to curved manifolds and other form-based PDEs (Navier-Stokes, Yang-Mills), suggesting broader applicability beyond electromagnetism.
