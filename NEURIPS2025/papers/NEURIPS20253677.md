## Problem

Statistical watermarking methods designed for autoregressive language models suffer a severe detectability drop when applied to autoregressive audio generation models, because re-encoding generated audio back into tokens produces a sequence that differs from the original in-generation token sequence (retokenization mismatch). This mismatch weakens the statistical signal that detectors rely on, and no prior distortion-free watermarking scheme was designed to handle it.

## Input / Output

During generation, the method takes a text or speech prompt and a secret key, and produces a watermarked audio waveform by modifying the token sampling distribution at each autoregressive step via a logits-processor-style intervention. During detection, the method takes the (possibly perturbed) audio waveform, re-encodes it into a token sequence using the model's encoder, and outputs a binary watermarked/not-watermarked decision based on a binomial score threshold. The clustering of token embeddings is performed once offline per model and cached, so no additional computation is incurred at generation or detection time.

## Method

ALIGNED-IS first clusters all audio tokens into h groups (h=20 in experiments) using k-means on their encoder embeddings, exploiting the fact that retokenization mismatches tend to map a token to a nearby one in the same cluster. It then applies a novel distortion-free reweight strategy called aligned inverse sampling: cluster probabilities are rearranged within [0,1] so that a pseudo-random number r(θ) seeded by the watermark code always falls in the interval [i-1/h, i/h] corresponding to the selected cluster, regardless of the actual (unknown-at-detection-time) cluster probabilities. This alignment lets the detector compute a simple 0/1 score per token—checking whether the retokenized token's cluster matches the interval implied by r(θ)—without needing access to the model's logits. Under the null hypothesis the aggregate score follows a Binomial(t, 1/h) distribution, yielding a closed-form Hoeffding tail bound that provides a provable false-positive-rate guarantee. The approach is distortion-free (Theorem 4.1), model-agnostic at detection, and reduces the effective retokenization mismatch rate by roughly 40–46% across datasets.

## Results

On SpiritLM with MMW Book Report, MMW Story, and MMW Fake News, ALIGNED-IS achieves TPR@FPR=1% of 0.88, 0.92, and 0.97 respectively, with median p-values of 8.0e-05, 4.6e-05, and 4.9e-05. At the stricter FPR=0.1% threshold, TPR reaches 0.77, 0.80, and 0.82. ALIGNED-IS outperforms the best distortion-free baseline (ITS) by 11–14 percentage points on TPR@1% and 13–8 points on TPR@0.1%, while also matching or exceeding the strongly biased KGW(δ=2.0) watermark without any quality degradation (FAD scores comparable to the unwatermarked baseline).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest distortion-free baseline ITS (Kuditipudi et al., 2023), ALIGNED-IS improves TPR@1% by 11 pp (0.88 vs 0.77) on MMW Book Report, 10 pp (0.92 vs 0.82) on MMW Story, and 14 pp (0.97 vs 0.83) on MMW Fake News, with correspondingly lower median p-values (e.g., 8.0e-05 vs 2.0e-04). Against DiPmark (Wu et al., 2023b) and γ-reweight (Hu et al., 2023), the gains are even larger—25–40 pp on TPR@1%—confirming that the clustering-based alignment is critical for audio. Against the biased KGW watermark (Kirchenbauer et al., 2023), ALIGNED-IS matches or exceeds KGW(δ=2.0) on most datasets while remaining distortion-free (FAD ≈ unwatermarked baseline), whereas KGW degrades audio quality. Against Unigram (Zhao et al., 2023), ALIGNED-IS dominates by 30–90 pp on TPR@1%. In robustness tests (Tables 5–6), ALIGNED-IS maintains TPR@1% above 0.80 under codec, noise, and filtering attacks, while ITS drops to 0.63–0.88 and DiPmark to 0.26–0.60; all methods collapse under speed perturbation, but ALIGNED-IS retains 0.28–0.39 TPR versus 0.00–0.14 for other distortion-free baselines. Against post-hoc methods AudioSeal and WavMark, ALIGNED-IS is both distortion-free (FAD 0.006–0.05 vs 0.29–1.82) and more robust across the full attack suite, though WavMark shows near-perfect TPR under mild attacks at the cost of audible artifacts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the statistical watermarking framework for autoregressive language models (KGW, DiPmark, γ-reweight, ITS) to the audio domain, where a fundamental obstacle—retokenization mismatch—arises because the encoder-decoder pipeline in audio models is not perfectly invertible at the token level. The core departure from prior work is the introduction of k-means clustering over token embeddings to group semantically similar tokens, combined with a novel 'aligned inverse sampling' reweight strategy that rearranges cluster probabilities to guarantee alignment between the pseudo-random seed and the selected cluster, yielding a provable false-positive-rate bound. The method inherits the distortion-free guarantee and code-history mechanism from Hu et al. (2023) and the inverse-sampling construction from Christ et al. (2023) and Kuditipudi et al. (2023), but adapts both to the clustered-token setting where exact token identity is no longer preserved after retokenization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As autoregressive audio generation models (SpiritLM, SpeechGPT, AudioLM) become increasingly capable of producing convincing synthetic speech, a distortion-free, model-agnostic watermark is essential for content authentication without degrading audio quality. ALIGNED-IS is the first such method specifically designed for this setting, and its ~10–15% TPR improvement over prior distortion-free baselines across multiple models and datasets makes it a practical default for audio watermarking pipelines. Key open questions include robustness to temporal misalignment (speed changes cause a dramatic TPR drop to ~0.3), the need to re-cluster for each new model architecture, and whether the approach extends to continuous-feature audio models that bypass discrete tokenization entirely.
