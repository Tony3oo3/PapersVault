## Problem

Aligning flow matching models with human preferences is harder than for diffusion models because ODE-based sampling paths provide neither a reference trajectory nor an accessible probability flow, making it difficult to incorporate the learned prior from the base model in a probabilistically sound way. Existing gradient-matching approaches for diffusion models rely on stochastic transitions, and adjoint-based methods like Adjoint Matching require expensive backward ODE solves at every time step, limiting their practicality for large foundation models.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt as input and produces a generated RGB image via a 20-step Euler ODE solver applied to Stable Diffusion 3's latent flow matching model, with a frozen VAE decoder mapping latents to pixels. During finetuning, reward signals (Aesthetic Score, HPSv2, or PickScore) are computed on decoded images, and the model is updated via LoRA (rank 8) on attention layers only, keeping the base architecture intact. The value gradient network is a scaled-down SD-v1.5 U-Net that operates on the same latent representations.

## Method

VGG-Flow formulates flow matching alignment as a deterministic optimal control problem where the running cost is the L2 distance between the finetuned and base velocity fields, and the terminal cost is the negative reward. Solving the resulting Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman equation yields two coupled conditions: a gradient-matching condition (the residual velocity field equals the negative value function gradient scaled by 1/λ) and a value-consistency PDE. The key practical innovation is a forward-looking parametrization of the value gradient: g_φ(x,t) = −η_t · stop-grad(∇_{x_t} r(x̂₁(x_t,t))) + ν_φ(x_t,t), where x̂₁ is the one-step Euler prediction, providing a strong heuristic initialization that accelerates convergence. Training alternates between updating the value gradient model via a consistency loss (implemented with finite differences to avoid second-order backprop) plus a boundary loss, and updating the velocity field via a matching loss that regresses the residual velocity to the learned value gradient.

## Results

On Stable Diffusion 3 finetuned for 400 steps, VGG-Flow achieves an Aesthetic Score reward of 8.24 with DreamSim diversity of 22.12 and FID of 375, a HPSv2 reward of 3.86 with diversity 18.40 and FID 1161, and a PickScore reward of 23.21 with diversity 20.93 and FID 1058. Compared to direct reward maximization baselines (ReFL, DRaFT), VGG-Flow achieves comparable or slightly lower reward on Aesthetic Score (8.24 vs. 10.00/9.54) but dramatically better diversity (22.12 vs. 5.59/7.78) and prior preservation (FID 375 vs. 1338/1518). On HPSv2 and PickScore, VGG-Flow achieves the best reward among all methods while also maintaining the highest diversity scores.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ReFL and DRaFT (direct reward backpropagation), VGG-Flow trades a modest reward reduction on Aesthetic Score (8.24 vs. 10.00 and 9.54) for a 3–4× improvement in DreamSim diversity (22.12 vs. 5.59 and 7.78) and a 3.5–4× reduction in FID (375 vs. 1338 and 1518), indicating far better prior preservation. On HPSv2 and PickScore, VGG-Flow actually achieves the highest reward (3.86 and 23.21) while also leading in diversity (18.40 and 20.93), outperforming both ReFL and DRaFT on all three metrics simultaneously. Against Adjoint Matching (AM), VGG-Flow achieves substantially higher Aesthetic Score reward (8.24 vs. 6.87) with comparable diversity (22.12 vs. 22.34) and better FID (375 vs. 465), and dominates on HPSv2 (reward 3.86 vs. 3.59, diversity 18.40 vs. 14.11) and PickScore (reward 23.21 vs. 22.78, diversity 20.93 vs. 19.70). The PMP variant (VGG-Flow-PMP) consistently underperforms the HJB-based VGG-Flow across all reward models, confirming the advantage of the amortized value-gradient parametrization over solving the adjoint ODE.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the optimal-control-based alignment framework for generative models from the stochastic setting (Adjoint Matching, which reformulates flow matching ODEs as SDEs and solves an adjoint ODE per step) to a deterministic setting via the Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman equation. The key conceptual departure is replacing the expensive per-trajectory adjoint ODE solve with an amortized value gradient model parametrized by a forward-looking reward gradient plus a learnable residual, yielding a gradient-matching loss that is both memory-efficient and faster to converge. It also positions itself against direct reward backpropagation methods (ReFL, DRaFT) by providing probabilistic guarantees and explicit prior preservation through the L2 regularization on the residual velocity field, rather than relying on truncated computation graphs that are prone to reward hacking.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As flow matching models (Stable Diffusion 3, video generators, 3D asset models) become the dominant generative paradigm, an efficient and principled alignment method that preserves the base model's prior is practically important. The HJB-based formulation gives a clean theoretical guarantee (Wasserstein-2 and KL bounds in the appendix) that direct reward methods lack, and the forward-looking value gradient parametrization makes the approach tractable at foundation-model scale. Open questions include whether the relaxed objective (requiring small λ) limits achievable reward, whether the finite-difference approximations to the consistency loss introduce problematic bias at scale, and whether the method extends naturally to video or 3D flow matching where trajectory lengths and dimensionality are substantially larger.
