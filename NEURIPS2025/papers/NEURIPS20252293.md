## Problem

Existing control adapters for text-to-image diffusion models (e.g., ControlNet, Uni-ControlNet) are trained independently per spatial task, requiring thousands of labeled examples and substantial compute for each new condition type. Reusing multi-task adapters on novel conditions that differ substantially from training tasks yields poor controllability. No prior method enables few-shot spatial control in T2I diffusion models, leaving a gap for data-efficient adaptation to unseen spatial conditions at test time.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252293/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At test time, UFC takes a query spatial condition (Canny/HED edge, MiDaS depth, surface normal, OpenPose keypoints, or Densepose segmentation at 512×512), a small support set of N image-condition pairs (typically 30, with only 5 used during inference to limit memory), and a text prompt. The spatial conditions and support images are projected into the latent space via the pre-trained VAE before encoding. The output is a spatially controlled image generated by the frozen diffusion backbone (Stable Diffusion v1.5 with UNet, or SD3.5 with DiT), with the adapter injecting control features at multiple encoder layers via zero-initialized projections and skip connections.

## Method

UFC constructs task-specific control features by interpolating task-agnostic visual patches from support images, weighted by patch-wise similarity scores between the query condition and support conditions (Eq. 4). The condition encoder g_τ is initialized from a pre-trained visual backbone with shared parameters θ and lightweight task-specific parameters θ_τ (bias-tuning), while the image encoder f remains frozen to preserve a unified visual feature space. The matching module is a multi-head cross-attention block with adaptive layer normalization for timestep conditioning, applied at each of the 12 UNet encoder layers. Training uses episodic meta-learning across multiple spatial tasks, then few-shot fine-tuning on a novel task updates only θ_τ, the matching modules σ, and projection layers Z (freezing θ and f), requiring at most 600 steps (~1 hour on one GPU). This design ensures control features reside in a consistent visual space regardless of condition modality, enabling generalization to unseen spatial tasks with as few as 30 examples.

## Results

In the 30-shot setting on COCO 2017, UFC (UNet backbone) achieves Canny SSIM 0.3239, HED SSIM 0.5121, Depth MSE 94.38, Normal MAE 15.09, Pose AP50 0.229, and Densepose mIoU 0.4340, reaching over 90% of the controllability of fully supervised ControlNet while using only 0.02% of its training data. On FID, UFC scores 19.24 (Canny), 20.58 (HED), 21.04 (Depth), and 21.60 (Normal), comparable to or better than fully supervised baselines on most tasks. With 150 support pairs (0.1% of full data), UFC surpasses Uni-ControlNet's controllability on several tasks. The DiT backbone variant further improves controllability (e.g., Canny SSIM 0.3984, Pose AP50 0.336) at the cost of slightly higher FID.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UFC consistently outperforms all few-shot and training-free baselines across all six spatial control tasks. Against the strongest few-shot baseline (Prompt Diffusion+FT), UFC gains up to 16.8% in controllability (e.g., Canny SSIM 0.3239 vs. 0.2773, Normal MAE 15.09 vs. 18.04) and achieves dramatically better Pose control (AP50 0.229 vs. 0.034). Uni-ControlNet+FT shows clear underfitting with near-zero Pose AP50 (0.010) and the worst Depth MSE (99.21), while zero-shot Prompt Diffusion fails to generalize (Canny SSIM 0.2120). Compared to the training-free Ctrl-X, UFC improves Canny SSIM by 11.7% (0.3239 vs. 0.2901) and Densepose mIoU by 3.2× (0.4340 vs. 0.1352), while also generating images ~2.6× faster (3.06s vs. 8.01s per image). Against fully supervised ControlNet trained on 150K images, UFC with only 30 shots achieves 90–95% of controllability on dense conditions (Normal MAE 15.09 vs. 14.14, Densepose mIoU 0.4340 vs. 0.4824) but lags more on sparse conditions like Pose (AP50 0.229 vs. 0.525).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the ControlNet paradigm for spatial control in diffusion models by replacing task-specific condition encoders with a universal adapter that interpolates task-agnostic image features from a small support set, weighted by patch-wise condition similarity. The matching mechanism is directly adapted from Chameleon [23], a few-shot dense prediction method, repurposing visual token matching to construct control features for diffusion models rather than dense prediction outputs. Unlike prior multi-task adapters (Uni-ControlNet, Prompt Diffusion) that either require full retraining or struggle with distribution shift, UFC combines episodic meta-learning with lightweight bias-tuning to enable rapid adaptation to entirely novel condition modalities at test time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

UFC establishes the first practical framework for few-shot spatial control in T2I diffusion models, demonstrating that 0.1% of training data can match fully supervised baselines on several tasks, which substantially lowers the barrier to adding new control modalities. The method's architecture-agnostic design (validated on both UNet and DiT backbones) makes it broadly applicable as diffusion model architectures evolve. Key limitations include the requirement for per-task fine-tuning (rather than pure in-context learning) and the restriction to spatial-structure control rather than appearance-preserving tasks like colorization or inpainting. An open question is whether the matching-based interpolation can be extended to zero-shot in-context adaptation, analogous to how LLMs handle new tasks without parameter updates.
