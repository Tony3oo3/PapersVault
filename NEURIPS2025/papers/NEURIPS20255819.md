## Problem

Zeroth-order (ZO) optimization for LLM fine-tuning eliminates backpropagation and reduces memory by up to 12×, but converges over 10× slower and achieves lower accuracy than first-order (FO) methods. Prior work attributed this gap solely to noisy gradient estimates, without identifying the structural cause. This paper reveals that the fundamental issue is a layer-wise update pattern difference: FO produces diverse-magnitude updates scaled to each layer's individual optimization needs, while ZO applies uniform-magnitude updates that ignore per-layer characteristics.

## Input / Output

The method consumes the pre-trained model weights (serving as a fixed anchor θ₀), the current fine-tuned parameters θ_t, and a mini-batch of labeled training data. It produces updated model weights and per-layer projection scalars γ_t that control how far each layer moves from the anchor. Notably, only the Query and Value attention layer weights from the pre-trained model need to be stored in memory (~16.7% of total parameters for OPT-2.7B), and the projection is applied intermittently every κ iterations rather than at every step, keeping the entire pipeline forward-pass-only with no activation or gradient storage.

## Method

DiZO performs divergence-driven layer adaptation by alternating between two steps: (1) standard ZO gradient estimation via two-point finite differences and a weight update, and (2) learning per-layer projection factors γ_t that rescale each layer's displacement from the pre-trained anchor along the direction Δθ_t = θ_t − θ₀. The projection learning sub-problem is itself solved with ZO optimization (not backpropagation) to preserve memory efficiency, with two stabilization strategies: re-initialization (resetting γ to ‖Δθ_t‖ so the initial projection magnitude equals 1) and projection clipping (constraining γ/‖Δθ_t‖ to [1−τ, 1+τ] to prevent destabilizing weight jumps). The method projects only Q and V attention layers, and the paper provides a nonconvex convergence guarantee of O(√D/√T) where D is the maximum per-layer dimension, improving over the standard ZO-SGD bound of O(√d/√T) by exploiting the geometric constraint that keeps each layer near its pre-trained initialization.

## Results

On OPT-2.7B fine-tuned with 1000 training samples, DiZO achieves 92.5% accuracy on SST-2 and 68.2% on RTE, outperforming MeZO (90.0%, 63.5%) and HiZOO (90.8%, 60.6%) while reducing training GPU hours by up to 48% (62.3% of MeZO's time on RTE). On RoBERTa-large (k=16), DiZO reaches 92.2% on SST-2, surpassing both MeZO (90.5%) and full fine-tuning (91.9%). On Llama3-8B, DiZO achieves 91.5% on SST-2 and 73.2% on CB, consistently leading MeZO and HiZOO across classification and generation tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiZO consistently outperforms MeZO across all evaluated models and datasets: on OPT-2.7B it gains +2.5 points on SST-2 (92.5 vs 90.0) and +4.7 on RTE (68.2 vs 63.5), while cutting GPU hours by 38–48%. Against HiZOO, DiZO wins on most tasks (e.g., +7.6 on RTE, +1.7 on SST-2 for OPT-2.7B) and maintains MeZO-level throughput, whereas HiZOO slows throughput by ~1.5× due to costly Hessian estimation. In the LoRA setting, DiZO+LoRA surpasses both MeZO+LoRA and HiZOO+LoRA on 7 of 9 OPT-2.7B tasks (e.g., +2.8 on RTE, +3.6 on SQuAD). Compared to Sparse MeZO, DiZO achieves higher accuracy (92.3 vs 91.4 on SST-2, 68.4 vs 67.1 on RTE for OPT-2.7B) with fewer GPU hours (56% vs 79% on SST-2) and higher throughput (1.9 vs 2.3 it/s is slightly lower, but total GPU time is substantially less). On the more challenging MMLU and MT-Bench benchmarks with Llama2-7B, DiZO scores 45.91 (MMLU) and 4.79 (MT-Bench) versus MeZO's 45.22 and 4.59, while using only 78% of MeZO's GPU hours.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the ZO optimization line initiated by MeZO (Malladi et al., 2023) and extended by HiZOO (Zhao et al., 2024), which addressed the memory bottleneck of LLM fine-tuning by eliminating backpropagation. Where prior work attributed ZO's convergence gap to noisy gradient estimates and proposed variance reduction (HiZOO) or sparsity (Sparse MeZO), this paper introduces a fundamentally different diagnostic: a layer-wise divergence analysis showing that ZO's uniform-magnitude updates fail to replicate FO's diverse-magnitude, layer-adaptive behavior. The proposed anchor-based projection mechanism is conceptually distinct from both adaptive learning-rate methods (Adam, RMSProp) and line-search approaches, which the paper explicitly contrasts in its ablations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DiZO offers a practical 48% reduction in GPU training hours while preserving the ~90% memory savings of ZO fine-tuning, making it a compelling option for resource-constrained and on-device LLM adaptation. However, the theoretical foundations remain incomplete: the choice of the pre-trained model as anchor and the restriction to Q/V layers are supported primarily by empirical ablation rather than formal justification, and the paper acknowledges that ZO+LoRA fails entirely on Llama models (possibly due to GQA). Open questions include whether the divergence-driven projection principle generalizes to vision or multimodal models, and whether a principled theory can explain why geometrically constrained updates around a fixed anchor outperform unconstrained ZO search.
