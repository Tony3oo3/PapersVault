## Problem

Existing multi-view clustering methods suffer from two key limitations: they perform representation learning exclusively in the feature domain, missing cluster-discriminative patterns that are only separable in the frequency domain, and they rely on static, rule-based strategies for view fusion that cannot adapt to sample-level variations in view quality or complex heterogeneous data distributions. The paper also identifies the absence of a centralized coordination mechanism capable of semantically assessing view content and making intelligent, context-aware fusion decisions.

## Input / Output

The method consumes V unlabeled multi-view feature matrices X^v ∈ R^{N×d_v} (each view may have a different dimensionality d_v) and produces per-sample cluster assignments c_i ∈ {1,…,K} along with an aggregated soft probability distribution P ∈ R^{N×K}. The LLM decision module operates on 4-dimensional quality indicator vectors (prediction confidence, cluster compactness, entropy, discriminator score) per agent per view, formatted into a structured text prompt, and outputs a scalar confidence threshold that gates which agents contribute to the final weighted fusion.

## Method

LLM-DAMVC reformulates multi-view clustering as a dynamic decision-making problem with three coupled modules. First, a Multi-Agent Collaboration Module uses a Residual Gated Encoder to produce shared latent features, which are then processed in parallel by a Standard Agent (stable feature extraction) and an Adversarial Agent (gradient-based perturbation + discriminator min-max game for robustness). Second, a Dual-Domain Contrastive Learning Module enforces cross-view consistency via cosine-similarity contrastive loss in the feature domain and an L1-amplitude-spectrum contrastive loss in the frequency domain (via FFT), capturing global structural patterns invisible in raw feature space. Third, an LLM-Assisted Dynamic Feature Aggregation Module feeds per-agent quality indicators into a frozen LLM (DeepSeek series) that outputs a confidence threshold; agents below the threshold are zeroed out, and remaining agents receive softmax-normalized weights proportional to their prediction confidence times the Frobenius norm of their latent features, yielding an adaptive, rule-free fusion of all 2V agent predictions into the final cluster assignment.

## Results

LLM-DAMVC achieves the best ACC across all six benchmark datasets: 99.92% on MNIST-USPS, 99.67% on BDGP, 98.66% on Handwritten, 78.76% on Reuters, 46.55% on CCV, and 37.11% on NUS-WIDE. On the well-structured datasets (BDGP, MNIST-USPS, Handwritten), the method surpasses 98% ACC with NMI and PUR also above 95%. On the more challenging Reuters and CCV datasets, LLM-DAMVC still leads by a substantial margin (e.g., 78.76% vs. 77.33% ACC on Reuters; 46.55% vs. 31.23% ACC on CCV), demonstrating robustness to heterogeneous and noisy views.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LLM-DAMVC outperforms all ten baselines on every dataset and every metric (ACC, NMI, PUR). On MNIST-USPS, it achieves 99.92% ACC, narrowly edging out COPER (99.88%) and MFLVC (99.66%); on BDGP it reaches 99.67% ACC versus CVCL's 99.20% and DSIMVC's 99.04%. The largest margins appear on CCV, where LLM-DAMVC scores 46.55% ACC compared to the next-best MFLVC at 31.23% (a 15.3-point gap), and on NUS-WIDE where it leads DSIMVC (28.89%) by 8.2 points. On Reuters, the advantage over the strongest baseline ADMC (77.33%) is modest (1.4 points), suggesting that text-heavy multi-view data remains challenging even for the proposed LLM-assisted fusion. Classical baselines (K-Means, DCP) and incomplete-view methods (COMPLETER, EE-IMVC) are consistently far behind, confirming that the gains come from the dual-domain contrastive learning and dynamic LLM-guided fusion rather than from handling missing views.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends deep multi-view clustering (DMVC) by combining three previously separate threads: adversarial representation learning (Wang et al., 2022), contrastive cross-view alignment (CVCL, MFLVC), and attention-based dynamic view fusion (Diallo et al., 2023; Huang et al., 2023). Its key departure is repurposing a frozen LLM (DeepSeek) as a high-level decision-making coordinator that outputs a confidence threshold for agent filtering, rather than using LLMs for feature extraction as in Chen et al. (2025). The dual-domain contrastive module further extends standard feature-space contrastive learning by adding an FFT-based frequency-domain objective, a technique borrowed from signal processing but not previously applied to MVC.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a real limitation in MVC—static, rule-based view weighting that cannot adapt to sample-level quality variation—and the frequency-domain contrastive loss offers a concrete, reusable technique for capturing global structural patterns invisible in raw feature space. However, the LLM's role is relatively shallow (outputting a single threshold τ_c), raising open questions about whether fine-tuned or task-specific LLMs could yield substantially better fusion decisions, and whether the added inference cost justifies the marginal gain over simpler dynamic weighting. The absence of error bars, statistical significance tests, and a direct ablation isolating the LLM component from a non-LLM dynamic baseline limits confidence in the claimed superiority of the LLM-assisted mechanism specifically.
