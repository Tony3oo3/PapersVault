## Problem

LLM-based TableQA systems frequently produce unfaithful answers by overfitting to token co-occurrence patterns in pretraining corpora (e.g., 'exactly' biasing toward 'yes'), creating spurious correlations that distort reasoning. Existing causal debiasing methods require either observable confounders or access to model internals, both of which are infeasible when the confounder is the latent pretraining bias itself. This paper identifies this gap and shows that front-door adjustment can eliminate the confounding effect using only observed variables.

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural language question and a tabular dataset as input, and produces either a short-form answer (exact match on WTQ and TabFact) or a long-form free-form answer (BLEU-scored on FetaQA). Internally, the pipeline generates n semantically diverse question variants in a single LLM call, extracts and unions evidence across all variants in one retrieval pass, then runs m rounds of Agent (Python code execution) and n rounds of Direct Prompting (CoT) reasoning before selecting the final answer by majority vote. The entire pipeline requires only three LLM API calls regardless of the number of variants.

## Method

CIT formalizes TableQA as a structural causal model where the ideal path is Q→E→A, but a latent confounder C (token co-occurrence bias) introduces a spurious back-door path Q←C→A. Since C is unobservable, the paper applies front-door adjustment, which decomposes the interventional distribution P(A|do(Q)) into three estimable terms using only observed variables. These terms are operationalized as: (1) Question Variant Generation, which produces semantically diverse paraphrases to approximate the marginal P(Q=q′) and sample a dense neighborhood around the original question in embedding space; (2) Evidence Aggregation, which unions evidence retrieved across all variants to improve coverage; and (3) Answer Inference, which runs both Direct Prompting (autoregressive CoT) and Agent (Python code generation and execution) reasoning under each variant. A final Joint Voting step selects the answer by majority vote over all DP and Agent outputs, exploiting their complementary strengths—Agent excels at precise numerical operations while DP produces fluent long-form answers.

## Results

On WikiTableQuestions, CIT-DP&Agent achieves 76.38% exact-match accuracy, surpassing the previous best (TIDE, 75.00%) by 1.38 points. On TabFact, CIT-DP&Agent reaches 91.30% accuracy, outperforming TIDE (89.82%) by 1.48 points. On FetaQA, CIT-DP&Agent attains 36.34% BLEU, exceeding the strongest baseline UniTabPT (33.12%) by 3.22 points. Across all three benchmarks, CIT consistently outperforms both Direct Prompting-only and Agent-only variants, confirming the complementary benefit of joint reasoning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CIT-DP&Agent outperforms all 23 baselines across the three benchmarks. On WikiTableQuestions, it beats the closest competitor TIDE (75.00%) by 1.38 points and Mix-SC (73.65%) by 2.73 points, while leading older methods like SYNTQA (70.40%) and Cabinet (69.10%) by 5.98 and 7.28 points respectively. On TabFact, CIT-DP&Agent (91.30%) surpasses TIDE (89.82%) by 1.48 points and StructGPT (87.30%) by 4.00 points. On FetaQA, it leads UniTabPT (33.12%) by 3.22 BLEU points and TAG-QA (31.84%) by 4.50 points. Notably, CIT-Agent alone (73.76% on WTQ, 90.61% on TabFact) already exceeds most baselines, while the joint DP&Agent combination provides an additional 2.6–3.5 point gain, validating the complementary design. The paper also demonstrates that CIT generalizes across LLMs, with GPT-4 improving from 70.89% to 77.09% (+6.20 points) and Gemini 1.5 from 61.56% to 66.51% (+4.95 points) when CIT is applied.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Pearl's front-door adjustment (2016) and repurposes it for LLM-based TableQA, where prior causal debiasing work (back-door adjustment, counterfactual VQA, causal NLU) has been limited by the need to observe or model confounders explicitly. It departs from the dominant TableQA literature—Direct Prompting and Agent paradigms (BINDER, DATER, ReAcTable, TIDE, etc.)—by reframing token co-occurrence bias as a latent confounder in a structural causal graph, then implementing the front-door criterion through question variant generation, evidence aggregation, and joint DP/Agent voting. The conceptual lineage connects to causal debiasing in NLU (Tian et al. 2022, Zhu et al. 2023) and front-door methods in fact verification (Zhang et al. 2024a), but is the first to apply this specific adjustment to the TableQA setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a principled causal explanation for why LLMs produce unfaithful TableQA answers (spurious token co-occurrence paths) and a lightweight intervention (3 API calls) that consistently improves accuracy across open- and closed-source models. However, the practical mechanism—paraphrase generation plus majority voting—raises the open question of whether the causal framing captures the true mechanism or merely provides a post-hoc justification for a well-known ensemble trick. Limitations include dependence on variant quality (poor paraphrases undermine the front-door assumption), the uniform P(Q=q') approximation, and the fact that gains shrink for smaller models (0.69% for GLM4 vs. 6.20% for GPT-4), suggesting the method's benefit is entangled with base model capability.
