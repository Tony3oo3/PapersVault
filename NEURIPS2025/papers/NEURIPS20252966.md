## Problem

All existing LRP-based explainability methods for Transformers propagate relevance through attention, FFN, and normalization layers but entirely skip positional encoding (PE) layers. This omission violates the conservation property at the input level and discards a significant, structurally distinct type of relevance that captures spatial, positional, and ordering relationships, resulting in unfaithful and incomplete explanations.

## Input / Output

The method reformulates the standard Transformer input space from a set of token embeddings into a set of position-token pairs, where each token carries both its semantic embedding and per-layer positional embeddings (dimension D′ per layer). For vision, it takes RGB images processed by DeiT variants and produces pixel-level relevance heatmaps; for NLP, it takes text sequences processed by LLaMA 2-7B, LLaMA 3-8B, or Tiny-LLaMA and produces token-level relevance scores. A key structural detail is the use of separate positional relevance sinks at each attention layer, preventing positional attributions from one layer from being absorbed or obscured by another.

## Method

PA-LRP introduces two fundamental modifications to standard LRP for Transformers. First, it defines custom LRP propagation rules for each PE type: for input-level PE (learnable, sinusoidal), it applies the epsilon-rule for addition to split relevance between semantic and positional components, with sinusoidal PE reparameterized to avoid non-linear propagation; for attention-level PE (RoPE, ALiBi), it propagates relevance from rotated queries and keys back to the position-dependent rotation matrices using the uniform LRP rule for matrix multiplication, then unflattens the result into the positional embedding. Second, it introduces a multi-sink mechanism where each attention layer has its own positional relevance sink, aggregated across layers via the LRP addition rule (analogous to skip connections), ensuring positional relevance from all layers is preserved without cross-layer interference. The non-positional propagation follows the AttnLRP framework, and the final explanation combines semantic and positional relevance by summing absolute values across feature dimensions. The paper provides theoretical proofs that the rules satisfy the conservation property (Lemma 3.2) and that ignoring PE leads to unfaithful explanations, particularly for position-dependent tasks (Lemma 3.3).

## Results

On ImageNet with DeiT, PA-LRP improves negative perturbation AUC by an average of ~4 points over AttnLRP (e.g., 54.97 vs. 52.19 for Base) and segmentation mIoU by ~1.3 points (51.4 vs. 50.1 for Base). On IMDB with LLaMA-2-7B, PA-LRP reduces generation AU-MSE from 7.629 (AttnLRP) to 6.521 (−14.5%) and improves AUAC from 0.779 to 0.796. In zero-shot LLaMA-3-8B MCQA, PA-LRP achieves AUAC of 0.377 vs. 0.365 (AttnLRP) and AU-MSE of 61.29 vs. 66.40, while the PE-only ablation alone already matches or exceeds AttnLRP on several metrics, confirming the significance of the positional signal.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PA-LRP consistently outperforms AttnLRP [2], the state-of-the-art LRP baseline, across all evaluated settings: on ImageNet it gains ~4 AUC points in negative perturbation and ~1–3 mIoU points in segmentation; on IMDB it reduces AU-MSE by 14.5% (LLaMA-2-7B) and 39% (Tiny-LLaMA) in generation. Against Integrated Gradients [34], PA-LRP achieves substantially higher AUAC (0.796 vs. 0.556 for LLaMA-2-7B generation) and lower AU-MSE (6.52 vs. 24.47), a gap of over 3×. Slalom [24] is outperformed on every metric, with PA-LRP improving AU-MSE from 18.38 to 6.52 for LLaMA-2-7B generation. In the zero-shot LLaMA-3-8B setting, PA-LRP also dominates SHAP [18] by a wide margin (AUAC 0.377 vs. 0.291; AU-MSE 61.3 vs. 141.8 on MCQA), while SHAP is noted to be significantly more computationally expensive. Notably, the PE-only ablation (attributing relevance solely to positional encodings) already matches or exceeds AttnLRP on several metrics, demonstrating that the previously ignored positional signal carries a large fraction of the total explanatory power.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LRP-for-Transformers lineage initiated by Voita et al. and refined by Ali et al. and Achtibat et al. (AttnLRP), which progressively improved propagation rules for attention, LayerNorm, and FFN layers. The key departure is identifying positional encoding (PE) as a systematically overlooked component: all prior LRP-based Transformer XAI methods treat PE as a constant or skip it entirely, violating the conservation property. PA-LRP reformulates the input space as position-token pairs and introduces the first PE-specific LRP rules for learnable, sinusoidal, Rotary, and ALiBi encodings, building directly on the AttnLRP framework for the non-positional path.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that PE-associated relevance constitutes 9–22% of total relevance in DeiT and that a PE-only ablation matches the full AttnLRP baseline in several NLP settings reveals that a substantial fraction of what prior methods attribute to token semantics is actually positional/structural signal being misrouted. This has practical implications for any downstream use of Transformer attributions (saliency, debugging, fairness auditing) where spatial or sequential structure matters. The paper's stated limitation is that it does not redesign the underlying LRP rules for attention or other layers to be jointly optimal with PE propagation, leaving open whether a fully PE-aware reformulation of the entire propagation pipeline would yield further gains.
