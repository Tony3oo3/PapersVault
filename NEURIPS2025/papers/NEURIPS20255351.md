## Problem

Existing video frame interpolation methods rely on convolutional or attention-based architectures that compute object correspondences at the pixel or patch level, inherently limiting them to local matching. Under large motion, this locality causes failures such as misalignment of repeated objects and ambiguous correspondences for objects absent in adjacent frames. The paper addresses this gap by formulating frame interpolation as a global surface-alignment problem, establishing frame-level object correspondences through differential geometry.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of consecutive RGB video frames (I⁰, I¹) as input and produces an interpolated intermediate frame Iᵗ for t ∈ (0,1), along with auxiliary Quadratic Gaussian surface representations, surface normal maps, and Gaussian curvature maps. Unlike prior 3DGS-based approaches, it operates in a fully feed-forward manner without requiring camera poses, depth maps, or per-scene optimization. Training uses 518×280 crops with a batch size of 16 over 800 epochs, and evaluation is conducted at 2K and 4K resolutions on large-motion benchmarks.

## Method

The core architecture consists of a VGGT backbone that extracts 3D point clouds, camera parameters, and frame features from the input pair, followed by a Quadratic Gaussian (QGS) head that transforms point cloud features into paraboloid-based Gaussian primitives with opacity, rotation, scale, and color parameters. Surface properties—normals and Gaussian curvature—are computed analytically from the paraboloid equation at each primitive, and both are rasterized onto the interpolation camera plane via differentiable volume splatting. The training objective combines an RGB reconstruction loss with a curvature-aware normal consistency loss that modulates the normal alignment penalty by local curvature, emphasizing geometric consistency in rapidly bending regions where normals alone become unreliable. This design leverages the fundamental theorem of surfaces to enforce global frame-level alignment rather than local pixel/patch matching, with the QGS representation providing flexible convex-to-concave surface fitting that outperforms standard 3DGS primitives for capturing complex object geometry.

## Results

On the large motion benchmark (Table 2), the method achieves 31.33 PSNR / 0.9011 SSIM on X-Test-L 2K, 29.05 / 0.9213 on SNU-FILM-L hard, 24.20 / 0.8400 on SNU-FILM-L extreme, and 31.20 / 0.8814 on Xiph-L 2K, outperforming all baselines on 4 of 6 large-motion splits. On the standard benchmark (Table 1), the method scores 33.35 PSNR / 0.9475 SSIM average, which is competitive but trails the best methods (EMA-VFI-S at 34.14, VFIMamba-S at 34.14) by roughly 0.8 dB. The method runs at 0.340 s/frame with 1.28 T FLOPs, placing it in the mid-range of computational cost among compared approaches.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the large motion benchmark, the method outperforms SGM-VFI by +0.94 dB on X-Test-L 2K (31.33 vs 30.39), +0.15 dB on SNU-FILM-L hard (29.05 vs 28.90), and +0.31 dB on Xiph-L 2K (31.20 vs 30.89). It also beats FILM (+1.25 dB on X-Test-L 2K), BiFormer (+1.01 dB on X-Test-L 2K, +0.35 dB on SNU-FILM-L extreme), XVFI (+1.51 dB on X-Test-L 2K), and RIFE (+1.46 dB on X-Test-L 2K). The closest competitor is VFIMamba-S, which edges out the proposed method on X-Test-L (31.58 vs 31.33 PSNR) but is surpassed on SNU-FILM-L extreme (24.20 vs 23.41) and Xiph-L 2K (31.20 vs 30.72). On the standard benchmark, the method trails EMA-VFI-S and VFIMamba-S by ~0.8 dB in average PSNR, indicating that the surface-alignment strategy primarily benefits large-motion scenarios rather than moderate-motion settings. Among older baselines (DAIN, AdaCoF, CAIN, SoftSplat, IFRNet-L, AMT-L), the method is competitive or superior on standard benchmarks and clearly superior on large-motion splits.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reframes video frame interpolation as a differential surface alignment problem, departing from the dominant pixel-level (CNN/optical-flow) and patch-level (attention) correspondence paradigms. It builds directly on Quadratic Gaussian Splatting (QGS) for surface representation and 2DGS for the normal consistency loss, but makes QGS feed-forward by coupling it with a VGGT backbone that provides coarse 3D point clouds and camera parameters without requiring accurate poses. The key conceptual departure is the use of both surface normals and Gaussian curvature as joint alignment constraints, motivated by the observation that normals alone become unreliable on rapidly bending surfaces under large motion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine failure mode of existing VFI methods under large motion, where repeated objects and missing correspondences cause local matching to break down. However, the authors acknowledge that their definition of 'large motion' is somewhat naive, focusing primarily on static background correspondences and assuming linear motion for dynamic objects, which limits generality. The approach is also computationally heavier (1.28 FLOPs) than lightweight alternatives like VFIMamba-S (0.24 FLOPs), and it does not achieve the best results on standard (non-large-motion) benchmarks. An open question the authors raise is whether a more unified differential geometry framework jointly modeling camera and object motion could yield a more principled and accurate solution.
