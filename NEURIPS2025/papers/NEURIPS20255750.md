## Problem

State-of-the-art time series generative models (GANs, VAEs, diffusion) are designed and evaluated under the assumption of abundant training data, yet real-world domains such as seismology, climate science, and biomedicine often provide only tens of examples. No prior work has systematically benchmarked how these models degrade under severe data scarcity, and no unified generative framework exists that can transfer knowledge across heterogeneous time series domains to enable high-fidelity few-shot generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255750/cropped_page3_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a small collection of multivariate time series (as few as 10 sequences) along with a learnable dataset token identifying the target domain. Each sequence is converted to a 2D image via delay embedding (local temporal patches organized spatially), padded to a square shape, and processed at a fixed spatial resolution; a dynamic binary mask distinguishes valid time steps from padding to support variable-length inputs without architectural changes. The output is a synthetic multivariate time series matching the input channel dimensionality, decoded back from image space via inverse delay embedding.

## Method

ImagenFew is a unified diffusion-based generative framework built on the ImagenTime/EDM backbone, pre-trained on ~300K time series spanning 19 heterogeneous datasets (finance, energy, climate, traffic, biomedical, space telemetry) and then fine-tuned on a small target subset. Two key architectural innovations enable cross-domain generality: (1) Dynamic Convolution (DyConv) layers that bicubically interpolate a single canonical kernel across channel dimensions at runtime, eliminating the need for fixed-size channel padding and allowing one parameter set to handle arbitrary input/output channel counts; and (2) a dataset token mechanism that injects domain-specific conditioning via adaptive group normalization (AdaGN), enabling the model to learn separate distributional modes during pre-training and to target a specific domain at inference. The training objective follows EDM's noise-prediction loss with preconditioning functions, and fine-tuning introduces a new randomly initialized dataset token for the target domain while updating all model parameters jointly on the few-shot subset.

## Results

On the few-shot benchmark (averaged across 12 datasets), ImagenFew achieves a Discriminative Score of 0.110 and contextFID of 0.674 at the 5% subset setting, outperforming the best baseline (ImagenTime: 0.321 / 3.464) by 65.7% and 80.5% respectively. At the extreme #10 setting (only 10 training examples), ImagenFew still achieves Disc. 0.259 and c-FID 3.800, beating all baselines on Discriminative Score. Remarkably, even at 100% data, the pre-trained model (Disc. 0.027, c-FID 0.866) surpasses all baselines trained from scratch, with a 76.1% improvement in Discriminative Score over the next-best method (TimeVAE at 0.185). The paper reports average gains of 47.65% (Disc.) and 23.94% (c-FID) over SOTA baselines across all data-limited scenarios.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ImagenFew dominates all five baselines (ImagenTime, DiffusionTS, KoVAE, TimeVAE, TimeGAN) on Discriminative Score and contextFID across nearly all subset sizes, with the largest margins at 5–10% data (e.g., 65.7% Disc. improvement over ImagenTime at 5%). The most notable result is that ImagenFew fine-tuned on only 5% of data outperforms ImagenTime trained on the full dataset, demonstrating the value of cross-domain pre-training. On Predictive Score, all methods cluster tightly (0.44–0.52), with ImagenFew winning by small margins (0.4–2%) in most settings; the sole exception is the #10 setting where TimeGAN (0.467) slightly edges out ImagenFew (0.489). At the extreme #10 setting, TimeVAE achieves the best c-FID (2.383 vs. ImagenFew's 3.800), indicating that VAE-based methods retain an advantage in distributional fidelity when only 10 examples are available. In the out-of-domain experiment (Sec. 6.4), ImagenFew also beats ImagenTime on unseen financial datasets (Stocks, Exchange) by large margins (e.g., Disc. 0.018 vs. 0.204 at 5% on Stocks), confirming cross-domain transfer even without prior exposure to the target domain.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the ImagenTime framework [50], which maps time series to 2D images via delay embedding and applies EDM-style diffusion [36] in the image domain, by introducing a unified multi-domain pre-training paradigm borrowed from foundation-model work in vision and language. Where prior time series generators (TimeGAN, KoVAE, DiffusionTS, ImagenTime) are trained per-dataset, this work trains a single diffusion model across ~300K heterogeneous sequences and fine-tunes with a new dataset token for few-shot adaptation. Two architectural additions—Dynamic Convolution (DyConv) for variable channel handling and dataset-token conditioning via AdaGN—enable a single UNet to serve all domains without per-dataset re-architecture, drawing on DyLinear [24] and classifier-free guidance ideas.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes the first systematic benchmark showing that all current SOTA time series generators degrade substantially under 5–15% data, a regime common in seismology, climate, and biomedical applications. The finding that a 5%-fine-tuned unified model outperforms full-data single-dataset baselines by over 55% on discriminative score suggests that cross-domain pre-training is a viable path toward a 'foundation model' for time series generation. Open questions include whether the approach scales to very long sequences (beyond 64 steps), how it behaves with truly novel (unseen) channel structures at test time, and whether the pre-training corpus needs to grow further to cover more domain-specific temporal motifs. The authors note that Weather and ECG200 remain challenging even with pre-training, indicating residual gaps.
