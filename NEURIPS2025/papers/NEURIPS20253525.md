## Problem

Existing knowledge fusion methods for LLMs rely on manually predefined, hard token alignment strategies that map each token independently of its context and ignore the associated probability distributions. This rigid, per-token alignment achieves only local optimality and fails to capture the coherent, distribution-level structure of model outputs, leading to performance degradation on challenging or unseen tasks.

## Input / Output

The method consumes per-step logits and token indices from source LLMs (e.g., OpenLLaMA with 32K vocabulary, MPT with 50K vocabulary) and a target LLM (Llama-2 7B, 32K vocabulary) over text sequences of up to 2048 tokens from MiniPile. It produces a fused probability distribution matrix (N tokens × V_t vocabulary) that serves as a soft training target, and ultimately a fine-tuned Llama-2 7B model whose parameters encode knowledge from all source models while retaining the target architecture.

## Method

PTA-LLM reformulates token alignment as an optimal transport problem in two stages. First, dynamic token pairing uses a dynamic programming recursion to find an optimal alignment between source and target token sequences, relaxing the one-to-one constraint to allow many-to-many pairings that accommodate differing tokenization granularities. Second, for each paired token, the top-k logit distributions (after softmax) are aligned via the Sinkhorn algorithm, where the transport cost matrix is defined by minimum edit distance between decoded token strings, yielding a transport plan that redistributes logit probability mass from source to target indices. The fused model is then trained end-to-end with a combined objective λ·L_CLM + (1−λ)·L_Fusion, where the fusion loss measures KL divergence between the target's predictions and the fused distribution matrix, and the source model at each step is selected by minimum cross-entropy against gold labels.

## Results

PTA-LLM achieves an average score of 29.00 across six benchmarks (78 tasks total), yielding a +1.72% relative improvement over the FUSELLM knowledge-fusion baseline (28.51). The largest gains appear on ToxiGen (+3.85%), TyDi QA (+3.27%), and MultiPL-E (+2.06%), while BBH shows a modest +0.17% improvement. In the stability study (Table 3), PTA-LLM mitigates performance degradation on 7 challenging tasks by 8.97% over FUSELLM, including a +25.00% gain on Tracking Shuffled Objects (7 objects) and a +2.75% gain on MMLU Chemistry.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PTA-LLM consistently outperforms all three source LLMs (Llama-2, OpenLLaMA, MPT) across every benchmark, with the most dramatic margin over MPT (e.g., 49.38 vs. 27.84 on MMLU). Against the direct knowledge-fusion competitor FUSELLM, PTA-LLM wins on all six benchmarks, with the largest relative gains on ToxiGen (+3.85%) and TyDi QA (+3.27%), confirming that probabilistic (soft) token alignment is more effective than FUSELLM's hard MinED mapping. Compared to Llama-2 CLM (continued training without fusion), PTA-LLM improves the average by +1.10 points, demonstrating that knowledge fusion adds value beyond simply training on more data. In the heterogeneous pilot study, PTA-LLM+Mistral surpasses Mistral 7B on MMLU (60.34 vs. 59.15) and HellaSwag (81.52 vs. 80.39), while PTA-LLM+Qwen2.5 exceeds Qwen2.5 on MMLU (72.66 vs. 71.80) and HellaSwag (80.13 vs. 78.89), validating the method's generalizability to stronger and architecturally diverse source models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the knowledge fusion paradigm introduced by FUSELLM (Wan et al., 2024), which distills probabilistic distributions from multiple source LLMs into a target model via token alignment. The key departure is replacing FUSELLM's hard MinED-based token mapping with a soft probabilistic alignment formulated as an Optimal Transport problem solved via the Sinkhorn algorithm, drawing on distribution-learning theory. The dynamic token pairing stage (Eq. 1) generalizes the one-to-one sequence alignment of Fu et al. (2023) to allow many-to-many correspondences, while the logit-level alignment (Eq. 4) incorporates actual probability mass into the transport cost rather than relying solely on surface-level edit distance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Token alignment is a critical but underexplored bottleneck in cross-architecture LLM fusion, and the OT reframing provides both a principled mechanism and interpretability (via distributional compactness and center-distance metrics) that prior hard-mapping methods lack. However, the empirical gains are modest (1.72% average over FUSELLM, 1.1% absolute over CLM), experiments are confined to 7B models trained on a single dataset (MiniPile), and the method still degrades on the hardest tasks relative to the base model. Open questions include whether the OT formulation scales to much larger or architecturally more diverse models, whether transporting in logit-space versus probability-space (acknowledged as a limitation) materially changes outcomes, and whether the added Sinkhorn computation (~13.75% overhead) remains acceptable at scale.
