## Problem

Existing driving world models are decoupled from trajectory planning: they simulate future states but require a separate policy model for decision-making. Even recent unified architectures (e.g., DrivingGPT) treat video generation and planning as independent tasks within the same model, leaving the synergistic mechanism by which learned world knowledge could explicitly benefit planning unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254491/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

PWM consumes only a single front-camera video stream (128×224 per frame, compressed to 28 tokens via a context-guided tokenizer), along with a navigation command and ego status vector. It autoregressively produces three outputs in sequence: a textual scene description, a rollout of 10–15 future video frames (generated action-free, i.e., without conditioning on the ego action), and finally m trajectory waypoints decoded from learnable action tokens. All 28 tokens within a frame are generated in parallel with bidirectional intra-frame attention, replacing conventional token-by-token decoding.

## Method

PWM is an end-to-end autoregressive Transformer (initialized from Show-o) that unifies world modeling and planning through a collaborative state-action prediction scheme: the model first forecasts future frames P(D_{t+1:t+n}|D_{1:t}) using its pre-trained world knowledge, then conditions action prediction on those forecasts P(A_{1:m}|D_{1:t+n}), forming a joint distribution rather than two independent heads. A dual-branch image tokenizer (frozen high-resolution branch guiding a trainable low-resolution branch via cross-attention) compresses each frame to 28 tokens, enabling efficient parallel next-frame generation. A Dynamic Focal Loss reweights the cross-entropy objective per token based on whether it changes between consecutive frames (α for dynamic, β for static, α>β), forcing the model to attend to temporally varying regions. Training proceeds in two stages: action-free video generation pre-training on 1,747 hours of OpenDV-YouTube, followed by supervised fine-tuning on nuScenes/NAVSIM where future-frame prediction and trajectory regression are jointly optimized.

## Results

On nuScenes, PWM achieves the lowest average collision rate of 0.07% (without ego status) and 0.04% (with ego status), surpassing all baselines including Drive-OccWorld (0.11%) and DiffusionDrive (0.08%). On the more challenging NAVSIM benchmark, PWM achieves a PDMS of 88.1 using only a single front camera, matching DiffusionDrive (88.1) which relies on multi-view camera + LiDAR inputs, while outperforming all prior camera-only methods (best: LAW at 84.6). PWM also attains the highest TTC score (95.4) and NC score (98.6) among all methods on NAVSIM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On NAVSIM, PWM (88.1 PDMS, single camera) ties DiffusionDrive (88.1, camera+LiDAR) and outperforms all other multi-modal methods including Hydra-MDP (86.5), DRAMA (85.5), and TransFuser (84.0). Among camera-only methods, PWM surpasses the best prior method LAW (84.6) by 3.5 PDMS points and DrivingGPT (82.4) by 5.7 points, while also achieving the highest TTC (95.4 vs. 88.7 for LAW) and DAC (95.9 vs. 95.4 for LAW). On nuScenes, PWM achieves the lowest average collision rate in both settings: 0.07% without ego status (vs. 0.11% for Drive-OccWorld and 0.19% for LAW) and 0.04% with ego status (vs. 0.08% for DiffusionDrive and 0.11% for RDA-Driver). PWM's L2 error (0.78 m without ego, 0.41 m with ego) is competitive but not the best in either setting, indicating its primary advantage lies in safety (collision avoidance) rather than trajectory precision. The key differentiator is that PWM achieves these results using only a single front camera, whereas most top-performing baselines require multi-view cameras and/or LiDAR.

## Relation to Prior Work

PWM builds directly on Show-o's unified multimodal transformer as its backbone, repurposing its autoregressive generation capability for driving-specific world modeling and planning. It departs from prior unified driving world models (DrivingGPT, DoE-1, VAViM) that architecturally combine video generation and trajectory planning but still predict future frames and actions as independent conditional distributions; PWM instead introduces a sequential dependency where generated future states explicitly condition the action prediction (collaborative state-action prediction). The action-free video generation pretraining also contrasts with action-conditioned world models like GAIA-1/2, removing the need for action-labeled data during the world-modeling phase. The parallel next-frame token generation (28 tokens per frame via a context-guided dual-branch tokenizer) is an efficiency departure from the token-by-token autoregressive generation used in most prior driving video models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a single front-camera view can match multi-view camera + LiDAR methods (PDMS 88.1 vs. DiffusionDrive's 88.1 on NAVSIM), suggesting that learned video-based world representations may be a viable alternative to expensive multi-sensor fusion for planning. The finding that naively adding future-frame prediction without proper world-model pretraining actually degrades planning (Table 5) highlights that the synergy between world modeling and planning is non-trivial and requires sufficient spatiotemporal grounding. Key limitations include reliance on open-loop evaluation, single-view input (vulnerable to poor visibility), and a short 3–4 second planning horizon, leaving open the question of whether the collaborative forecasting paradigm transfers to closed-loop, multi-view, long-horizon settings.
