## Problem

Generative multi-modal models (GMM) for class-incremental learning still suffer from catastrophic forgetting: as new tasks are learned, the model loses fine-grained distinctions of previously seen classes, outputting higher-level categories (e.g., 'apple' instead of 'Granny Smith') and misclassifying to word-similar labels (e.g., 'Pineapple'). Existing methods do not exploit the structured relational information between class labels to help the model maintain discriminability across incrementally learned categories.

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image and an instruction question (e.g., 'Describe details of this photo from color, species location, background, etc.') as input. The image is encoded by a frozen EVA-CLIP encoder while the question and ground-truth text are tokenized by BERT. Rather than producing a simple 'This is a photo of a [CLS]' caption, the model generates m structured relation triplets (head, relation, object) per image, which are then matched against a task-specific subgraph to yield a final class label via cosine similarity between the graph-augmented text and all previously seen class embeddings.

## Method

KG-GMM incrementally builds a dataset-specific subgraph from ConceptNet during continual learning, assigning each new class a set of r unique relations (e.g., IsA, AtLocation, HasColor) that have not been used by previously encountered similar classes, thereby maximizing inter-class discriminability. During training, these relation triplets replace plain class names as the ground-truth text in image-text pairs, and only a linear projection layer after the frozen image encoder is fine-tuned with cross-entropy loss. At inference, the model generates relation-aware text that is decomposed into triplets; the relation-object pairs are searched within the current subgraph to identify the most frequent head entity (Eq. 8), which is then prepended to the raw text output. The combined graph-augmented sentence is encoded and matched against all class features via cosine similarity for the final prediction, combining the LLM's generative reasoning with the knowledge graph's structured retention of relational knowledge.

## Results

On conventional CIL, KG-GMM achieves 84.29% Last accuracy on ImageNet-R (B0-10 tasks), surpassing GMM by 3.57% and CODA-Prompt by 10.41%. On Tiny-ImageNet, it reaches 81.86% Last accuracy (B100-5 tasks), outperforming GMM by 4.88% and the best prompt-based method CODA-Prompt by 10.50%. In few-shot CIL, KG-GMM attains 78.07% accuracy in the final session on Mini-ImageNet (+2.89% over GMM) and 82.37% on CIFAR-100 (+0.90% over GMM), while also achieving the highest Harmonic Accuracy (74.81% and 79.68% respectively), indicating better balance between old and new class retention.

## Baselines & Comparisons

KG-GMM consistently outperforms its direct baseline GMM across all conventional CIL settings: +4.88% Last on Tiny-ImageNet B100-5, +1.65% on B100-10, +2.29% on B100-20, and +3.57% on ImageNet-R. Against the strongest prompt-based discriminative methods, KG-GMM leads CODA-Prompt by 10.41% on ImageNet-R and by 10.50% on Tiny-ImageNet B100-5, while also surpassing MoE-CLIP (80.87%) and RAPF (80.28%) on ImageNet-R. In few-shot CIL, KG-GMM beats GMM by 2.89% in the final Mini-ImageNet session and 0.90% on CIFAR-100, while achieving substantially higher Harmonic Accuracy (+3.36% and +4.25% respectively), indicating better old-class retention. However, KG-GMM lags behind CODA-Prompt* (95.37%) in the base session on Mini-ImageNet (90.99%), though it surpasses it in all eight subsequent incremental sessions. All conventional train-from-scratch methods (EWC, LwF, iCaRL, UCIR, DyTox) are outperformed by 30–50+ points, confirming the advantage of generative pre-trained backbones over discriminative ones in exemplar-free CIL.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the generative multi-modal model (GMM) framework for class-incremental learning by replacing plain class-name labels with structured knowledge graph relation triplets drawn from ConceptNet, following the KG construction protocol of ZSL-KG. While prior CIL methods (prompt-based, CLIP-based, replay-based) address forgetting through parameter constraints or data retention, GMM sidestepped the classifier entirely by using a frozen LLM to generate text predictions; however, it still degraded on fine-grained distinctions over successive tasks. KG-GMM departs from GMM in two specific ways: (1) training with relation-aware ground-truth text (e.g., 'Apple IsA Fruit, Apple HasColor Green') instead of 'This is a photo of a [CLS]', and (2) a graph-augmented inference step that extracts predicted relations and searches the accumulated subgraph to disambiguate the final class. The approach is positioned as the first integration of common-sense knowledge graphs with generative multi-modal continual learning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that structured relational knowledge can substitute for exemplar replay in mitigating catastrophic forgetting, with negligible overhead (0.07 MB storage, 0.53 ms graph inference), making it attractive for memory-constrained continual learning deployments. A notable finding is that 169 KB of KG-based labels outperforms using equivalent memory for exemplar storage (84.29% vs 80.91%), suggesting structured knowledge is a more efficient anti-forgetting mechanism than raw data. Open questions include scalability to domains lacking comprehensive KGs (medical, fine-grained), sensitivity to class ordering, and whether the approach generalizes beyond the EVA-CLIP + Vicuna-7B backbone. The authors acknowledge that collecting or generating appropriate KGs for specialized datasets remains an unsolved practical bottleneck.
