## Problem

Aligning diffusion models with nuanced human preferences (aesthetics, engagement, user preference) remains difficult because it requires large-scale human annotations that are expensive and limited in diversity. Existing approaches either optimize prompts with a frozen generator or fine-tune the generator with fixed prompts, but both rely on human-curated datasets and thus can only replicate human-discoverable strategies. The paper asks whether a self-play framework can autonomously discover preference-aligned prompting strategies that go beyond human intuition, without any human-annotated data.

## Input / Output

The system takes a base image, its original caption, and a target preference objective (aesthetics, engagement, or user preference) as input. The Guider-VLM produces a structured output consisting of up to six reasoning steps followed by a 60-word optimized diffusion prompt; this prompt is fed to a diffusion model (SDXL at 512×512, or Flux/DALL-E 3 for zero-shot transfer) to generate the refined image. A CLIP similarity threshold of 0.75 is enforced to ensure generated images remain semantically consistent with the base image, and reward scores below the distribution mean are discarded during data filtering.

## Method

SPRO operates in three progressive stages. In SPRO-Prompt, a Guider-VLM (LLaMA-3.2-11B-Vision) iteratively generates reasoning-augmented prompt candidates at varying temperatures, passes them through a frozen diffusion model, scores the resulting images with a reward model (PickScore, LAION-Aesthetics, or EngageNet), and fine-tunes itself via DPO on winner-loser pairs—each iteration using the previous iteration's model to generate new preference data. In SPRO-Image, the diffusion model is fine-tuned via reconstruction loss on high-reward synthetic images paired with their original base captions, aligning image-space generation without prompt modification. SPRO-MM combines both: the self-play-trained Guider-VLM produces optimized prompts that are fed into the self-play-fine-tuned diffusion model for joint optimization. The key insight is that self-play in prompt space is substantially more sample-efficient than image-space self-play (30k vs. 500k training examples), and that reasoning chains enable the VLM to discover counterintuitive strategies (e.g., emphasizing visual harmony for aesthetics or shadow-based cues for engagement) that are rarely authored by humans.

## Results

On user preference (PickScore), SPRO-Prompt achieves 22.42 on Pick-A-Pic and 22.84 on PartiPrompts, surpassing all prompt-only and diffusion-tuning baselines. On aesthetics (LAION Score), SPRO-Prompt reaches 8.60 on Flickr (vs. 6.40 base) and 8.02 on PartiPrompts, a 34% gain over base captions. On engagement (EOIG Score), SPRO-Prompt scores 83.48/88.36/90.24 across low/mid/high buckets, more than doubling the base score in the low bucket (38.76). Across all three objectives, SPRO achieves an average 30% improvement over baselines without any human-annotated data, with win rates of 99.42% (aesthetics), 71.60% (engagement), and 65.70% (user preference) on SDXL.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPRO-Prompt outperforms all prompt-only baselines: it beats GPT-4o (22.31→22.42 PickScore, 7.57→8.60 LAION), GPT-o3 (19.47→22.42 PickScore), Qwen-3 (22.10→22.42), and LLAMA-11B CoT (21.96→22.42) on user preference, and surpasses Promptist (7.23→8.60) and PAE (6.27→8.60) on aesthetics. Against diffusion-tuning baselines, SPRO-Prompt wins on PickScore over DDPO (21.59→22.42 on Pick-A-Pic) and SPIN Diffusion (22.00→22.42), while using only 30k training examples versus SPIN's 500k. On PartiPrompts, SPRO-Prompt edges out CAPO (22.83→22.84 PickScore) and SPIN Diffusion (22.31→22.84). For engagement, SPRO-Prompt (83.48 low bucket) more than doubles EOIG-PFT (43.12) and EOIG-RLHF variants (~40). On the zero-shot LexicaDB test, SPRO-Prompt achieves ImageReward 1.439 versus RATTPO's 1.132, confirming generalization to unseen reward models. SPRO prompts also transfer zero-shot to Flux (78.9% win rate) and DALL-E 3 (67.67% win rate) without retraining.

## Relation to Prior Work

SPRO extends the self-play paradigm from LLMs (SPIN, DeepSeek-R1) and image-space diffusion self-play (SPIN Diffusion) into a unified multimodal setting where a VLM and a diffusion model co-evolve. It departs from prior prompt-optimization methods (Promptist, PAE, PromptCoT) that rely on human-curated prompt-image pairs, and from diffusion-tuning methods (DDPO, DPOK, CAPO) that require large-scale preference annotations, by generating all training signal through iterative self-play with learned reward models. The use of DPO for the Guider-VLM's iterative refinement follows the standard preference-optimization recipe, but the key conceptual departure is treating prompt generation as a reasoning task whose strategies are discovered autonomously rather than authored by humans.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SPRO offers a scalable, annotation-free path to preference alignment that could reduce the costly human curation bottleneck currently limiting diffusion model deployment. The finding that prompt-space self-play with 30k examples outperforms image-space self-play with 500k examples has direct practical implications for compute allocation. However, the framework's ceiling is bounded by the quality of proxy reward models (PickScore, LAION, EngageNet), and the paper acknowledges it does not yet support dynamic trade-off optimization across multiple goals in a principled way. Open questions include whether the discovered prompting strategies remain stable as base diffusion models improve, and whether the approach transfers to video or 3D generation.
