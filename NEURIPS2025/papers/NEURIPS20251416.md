## Problem

Reconstructing surface ocean nutrients from sparse observations is critical for understanding biogeochemical cycles, yet prior approaches treat the task as spatial inpainting under smooth, single-scale dynamics. In reality, nutrient transport follows advection-diffusion dynamics driven by a multiscale velocity field where mesoscale eddies (10–100 km) dominate lateral transport but are poorly resolved in numerical models, making naive PDE-based reconstruction highly sensitive to eddy perturbations. The core dilemma is that filtering the flow improves stability but removes fine-scale structure essential for local nutrient gradients, while retaining all scales introduces instability.

## Input / Output

The model consumes temporal sequences (intervals of 4 steps for daily, monthly for the monthly task) of sparse nutrient observations (as low as 0.1% of the 360×720 grid), a 0.5° ocean velocity field (u, v) that is decomposed into mean and eddy components via Fourier low-pass filtering, and auxiliary variables (sea surface temperature, salinity). The eddy component is L∞-normalized before entering the refinement module. Output is a full spatiotemporal nutrient concentration field (nitrate and/or phosphate) at 0.5° resolution, produced jointly across the reconstruction interval rather than frame-by-frame.

## Method

NUTS is a two-scale architecture that decouples stable large-scale transport from unstable mesoscale variability. The coarse module first uses a spatiotemporal transformer initializer on the low-pass-filtered mean flow to produce a stable initial field, then propagates it forward via a homogenized advection-diffusion PDE (solved by the method of lines) where unresolved mesoscale effects are replaced by a state-dependent effective diffusion tensor K = GGᵀ predicted by a hypernetwork conditioned on the coarse prediction. A refinement vision transformer then restores mesoscale detail by conditioning on the coarse prediction, mean flow, normalized residual eddy velocity, sparse observations, and static covariates, operating independently at each timestep. A learnable ResNet source term and a mass-conservation loss (penalizing drift in total nutrient mass) enforce physical realism. Under standard homogenization assumptions (periodic, mean-zero eddy flow with parabolic scaling), the paper proves the reconstruction error is O(ε) and independent of the eddy perturbation magnitude, providing a theoretical robustness guarantee absent in prior data-driven approaches.

## Results

On the WOD real-observation monthly task, NUTS achieves NRMSE of 0.035 for phosphate and 0.151 for nitrate, reducing error by 79.9% and 19.3% respectively over the best data-driven baseline (U-Net: 0.174 / 0.187). On the simulated MOM6 daily task at 0.1% sparsity, NUTS reaches NRMSE of 0.014 (phosphate) and 0.143 (nitrate), a 90.7% and 14.9% improvement over U-Net. Site-wise evaluation on held-out WOD stations shows NUTS (1.32 phosphate, 2.18 nitrate) matches the accuracy of traditional numerical models MOM6 and GOBH while outperforming all data-driven baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NUTS outperforms all seven baselines across every sparsity level and both nutrient types. Against the strongest data-driven baseline U-Net (31M params), NUTS reduces WOD monthly NRMSE from 0.174 to 0.035 for phosphate (79.9% improvement) and from 0.187 to 0.151 for nitrate (19.3%). The physics-guided 4D-VarNet (0.3M params) is the second-best on WOD (0.187/0.203) but still trails NUTS by a wide margin, confirming that variational assimilation alone is insufficient under extreme sparsity. Foundation models Prithvi (2.3B) and AtmoRep (0.7B) underperform smaller specialized models, with Prithvi actually degrading on nitrate (0.338 vs. U-Net's 0.187), suggesting that large-scale pretraining on atmospheric data does not transfer well to marine biogeochemistry. Neural operator FNO (4.8M) and implicit-representation Marble (0.6M) show moderate performance but lack the temporal dynamics and physical grounding that NUTS provides. On site-wise WOD evaluation, NUTS matches the accuracy of the full numerical models MOM6 and GOBH, demonstrating that a 125M-parameter learned model can replicate physics-based reconstruction without requiring a coupled biogeochemical solver.

## Relation to Prior Work

NUTS departs from the dominant framing of climate field reconstruction as spatial image inpainting (U-Net, ViT, EarthFormer) and from physics-informed hybrids (FNO, 4DVarNet, Marble) that embed PDE constraints without explicit scale separation. Its core conceptual lineage is homogenization theory for advection-diffusion systems (Pavliotis & Stuart 2008), which the paper operationalizes by replacing unresolved mesoscale eddy dynamics with a learned effective diffusion tensor in a coarse PDE solver, then delegating fine-scale recovery to a separate refinement module conditioned on the residual eddy field. This two-stage decomposition—stable homogenized transport followed by controlled mesoscale correction—is the paper's primary architectural departure from prior single-scale or monolithic physics-informed approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The O(ε) error bound independent of eddy perturbation strength provides a rare theoretical robustness guarantee for a data-driven reconstruction model, directly addressing a known fragility of PDE-based methods under noisy velocity inputs. The 79.9% NRMSE reduction for phosphate on real WOD observations and site-wise accuracy comparable to numerical models (MOM6, GOBH) demonstrate practical viability in a data-scarce regime where prior data-driven methods struggle. Key limitations include the 2D surface-only formulation, reliance on numerical-model velocity fields (which themselves carry eddy errors), and testing on only two nutrients; the authors outline 3D extension, biogeochemical process coupling, and air-sea gas exchange as natural next steps that would broaden applicability to the full ocean carbon and nitrogen cycle.
