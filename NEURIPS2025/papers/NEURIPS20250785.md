## Problem

Theoretical analyses of SGD have largely been restricted to polynomially decaying step-sizes (which converge too slowly for practical use) and constant step-sizes (which require careful calibration). Two schedules that dominate modern deep learning practice—cyclical learning rates and linear decay to zero (Linear-D2Z)—have seen widespread empirical adoption but lack rigorous non-asymptotic convergence guarantees, leaving a persistent gap between what theory covers and what practitioners actually use.

## Input / Output

The framework accepts an arbitrary learning-rate schedule {η_t} that may explicitly depend on the total iteration count n (accommodating finite-horizon schedules like Linear-D2Z), a μ-strongly convex objective F with L_p-smooth noisy gradients, and an initial point θ_0. The primary output is a closed-form upper bound on E[|θ_n − θ⋆|^p] decomposed into an exponential forgetting term and a variance accumulation term. For cyclical schedules, the output is not a stationary distribution but a T-periodic cyclostationary process, capturing the periodic oscillation in the iterates' statistical properties.

## Method

The core result (Theorem 2.1) establishes a general L^p moment bound via recursive application of a one-step contraction lemma built on Rio's inequality, yielding ‖θ_n − θ⋆‖_p² ≤ exp(−c₀ Ση_k)|θ_0 − θ⋆|² + 3(p−1)M_p² Σ η_j² exp(−c₀ Σ_{k>j} η_k). This umbrella bound is then specialized: for Linear-D2Z (η_t = η(1−t/n)), the exponential term gives fast forgetting of initialization while the variance sum is bounded by O(1/√n) via a Riemann-sum-to-integral argument, yielding a 'best-of-both-worlds' rate. For cyclical schedules (η_t = η_{t mod T}), the paper proves convergence in distribution to a cyclostationary process by casting the SGD iterate as an iterative random function with periodic driving, invoking contraction results from Li et al. [2024a] on function compositions, and showing that the product of per-step contraction factors ρ_p(η_1)⋯ρ_p(η_T) < 1 suffices—allowing individual steps to be large as long as the cycle as a whole contracts.

## Results

The paper establishes a general non-asymptotic MSE bound (Theorem 2.1): ‖θ_n − θ⋆‖_p² ≤ exp{−c₀Ση_k}|θ₀ − θ⋆|² + 3(p−1)M_p² Ση_j² exp{−c₀Σ_{k=j+1}^n η_k}, valid for arbitrary step-size schedules. For the Linear-D2Z schedule η_t = η(1−t/n), Theorem 2.2 yields the rate ‖θ_n − θ⋆‖_p² ≤ |θ₀ − θ⋆|² exp{−c₀η(n−1)/2} + C/√n, combining exponential forgetting of initialization with O(n^{-1/2}) terminal error. On MNIST (784-dim logistic regression), Linear-D2Z reduces sigmoid-loss MSE from 0.0059 at 500 iterations to 0.0031 at 5000 iterations, with standard deviation dropping from 0.0046 to 0.0011.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper that does not benchmark against competing optimization algorithms; instead, it compares different learning-rate schedules under a unified framework. In the comparative simulation (Section C.6), Linear-D2Z and polynomial-decay schedules achieve substantially lower terminal MSE than constant and cosine schedules, which exhibit a persistent O(η) bias floor. In the early phase (first 100 iterations), Linear-D2Z and cosine schedules converge faster than polynomial decay due to their larger initial step sizes, while in the late phase (last 100 iterations), Linear-D2Z and polynomial decay reach far smaller error than the non-decaying schedules. The cosine schedule's cyclostationary behavior (Theorem 2.4) is empirically confirmed by periodic fluctuations in the standard deviation of MSE, a pattern absent under constant learning rates. The paper's key methodological contribution is that Theorem 2.1 subsumes all these schedules under a single bound, making the trade-offs between initialization forgetting and exploration variance explicit and comparable.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the classical SGD convergence theory (Robbins-Monro, Ruppert, Polyak-Juditsky), which has been largely confined to polynomially decaying and constant step sizes, to two practically dominant but theoretically under-examined families: cyclical learning rates and linearly decaying-to-zero (Linear-D2Z) schedules. The proof of the cyclostationary convergence result (Theorem 2.4) builds directly on the nonlinear time series framework of Li et al. [2024a] and the iterative random function construction for cyclostationary processes from Bonnerjee et al. [2024], adapting these tools to the SGD setting. The general moment bound (Theorem 2.1) subsumes the classical polynomial-decay analysis as a special case while accommodating finite-horizon schedules where step sizes depend on the total iteration count n—a setting previously absent from the mean-squared-error literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Cyclical and Linear-D2Z schedules are the de facto standard in training large language models, vision transformers, and CNNs, yet they lacked any rigorous non-asymptotic convergence guarantees; this paper closes that gap and provides the first formal characterization of their behavior. The 'best-of-both-worlds' result for Linear-D2Z (exponential forgetting of initialization plus O(n^{-1/2}) terminal error) gives practitioners a theoretical justification for the schedule's empirical dominance. Key limitations include the restriction to strongly convex objectives and the absence of central limit theorems or refined uncertainty quantification; extending the framework to non-convex, non-smooth landscapes and establishing asymptotic normality for these schedules are natural next steps that would enable practical statistical inference.
