## Problem

Continuous diffusion models benefit from the I-MMSE identity, which links mutual information decay to minimum estimation error and yields exact log-likelihood decompositions. However, discrete diffusion models—particularly masked/absorbing variants used for text—lacked an analogous information-theoretic foundation, leaving their score-based training losses (DSE, DCE) characterized only as variational upper bounds rather than principled likelihood estimators.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a clean discrete sequence (e.g., a text token sequence or DNA string of length L) and produces a scalar negative log-likelihood estimate. For conditional estimation, the input is a prompt-target pair where only the target tokens are diffused; for likelihood ratios, two sequences are processed with a shared random mask. The time-free formulation requires no diffusion-time integration: a single forward pass of the learned conditional predictor c^θ on a randomly masked version of the sequence suffices per Monte Carlo sample, with the mask size drawn from a Beta-weighted distribution over all subsets.

## Method

The paper derives the I-MDSE identity, showing that the rate of mutual information decay in a continuous-time Markov chain forward process equals the negative minimum denoising score entropy (DSE) loss, directly paralleling the I-MMSE relation in Gaussian diffusion. For masked (absorbing) diffusion, a pointwise equivalence between DSE and DCE losses under the time reparameterization λ = 1 − exp(−σ̄(t)) is established (Lemma 3.3), yielding the I-MDCE identity and an exact NLL decomposition as a time integral of minimum DCE. The key practical contribution is a time-free reformulation (Theorem 4.1) that eliminates the integral entirely, expressing −log p₀(x₀) as H_L · E_{p(I)}[Σ_{i∉I} log 1/p₀(x₀ⁱ | x₀ᴵ)] where I is sampled from a Beta-weighted distribution over token subsets, requiring only one forward pass per Monte Carlo sample. This extends naturally to conditional likelihoods over disjoint index sets and to coupled likelihood ratio estimation where a shared mask I is used for both sequences, achieving substantial variance reduction over decoupled baselines.

## Results

The time-free likelihood estimator (Eq. 15/17) achieves 6–7× lower Monte Carlo variance than the time-integral baseline across HellaSwag (70.97→11.57), ARC-hard (23.18→5.73), and PIQA (19.77→4.93) at 128 MC samples. The coupled likelihood ratio estimator (Eq. 18) reduces variance by ~7× over the decoupled baseline on BeaverTails (8897 vs. 62469 at 5 MC samples). On synthetic DNA data with known ground-truth distributions, both unconditional and conditional estimators closely recover true NLLs, confirming the exactness of the I-MDCE decomposition.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper whose experiments validate the proposed I-MDCE framework rather than benchmarking against competing generative models. The primary internal comparison is between the proposed time-free estimator and the time-integral (diffusion-time) baseline: the time-free form consistently achieves 6–7× lower Monte Carlo variance across all three NLP datasets (HellaSwag, ARC-hard, PIQA) and all sample sizes tested (128–512 MC samples). For likelihood ratio estimation, the coupled estimator (sharing a single mask I across both sequences) outperforms the standard decoupled baseline by approximately 7× in variance on the BeaverTails safety-alignment dataset, with the gap persisting across 5–20 MC samples. On toy data with known ground-truth distributions, both the unconditional (Eq. 15) and conditional (Eq. 17) estimators closely match true NLLs, confirming that the I-MDCE identity yields exact rather than merely variational likelihood estimates. The paper also demonstrates practical utility: conditional NLLs estimated via Eq. 17 clearly separate in-distribution text8 continuations from GPT-4-generated OOD responses, and reveal that LLaDA-8B assigns higher likelihoods to LLaMA 3.1-generated text than to its own training data, suggesting training-data influence.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the I-MMSE identity from continuous Gaussian diffusion (Kong et al., ICLR 2023, 2024) to the discrete setting, deriving the I-MDSE and I-MDCE relations that connect mutual information decay to the minimum DSE and DCE losses, respectively. The DSE loss (Lou et al., ICML 2024) and DCE loss with time-free reparameterization (Ou et al., ICLR 2025) provide the loss functions whose information-theoretic meaning is formalized here. The key departure from prior work is elevating these losses from variational upper bounds to exact log-likelihood decompositions, and deriving practical time-free and coupled Monte Carlo estimators that go beyond the asymptotic equivalences previously established.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a principled, information-theoretic justification for DSE/DCE training objectives in discrete diffusion, resolving ambiguity about whether these losses are merely bounds or exact likelihood estimators. The time-free estimator and coupled likelihood ratio estimator offer practical tools for model auditing, OOD detection, and alignment that are more sample-efficient than time-integral alternatives. A key limitation is that the I-MDCE relation currently applies only to masked (absorbing) diffusion, leaving the full I-MDSE setting for future work; additionally, the ability to recover exact likelihoods raises privacy considerations in sensitive deployment contexts.
