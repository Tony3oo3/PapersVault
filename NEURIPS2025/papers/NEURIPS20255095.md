## Problem

Prior unified MLLMs for image understanding and generation (e.g., Janus, Show-o) rely on distinct training recipes and large-scale internal datasets, and have not demonstrated effective collaboration between the two capabilities within a single architecture. Additionally, leveraging chain-of-thought reasoning to improve generation quality via self-verification in unified models remains largely unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255095/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For generation, the model takes a free-form text prompt and produces a 256×256 RGB image via masked token prediction over MAGVIT-v2 discrete tokens (50 denoising steps with cosine scheduling and classifier-free guidance at scale 5.0). For understanding, it accepts an image (processed by SigLIP at 384×384) plus a text query and outputs a text response. Under CoT-V test-time scaling, the model generates N=20 candidate images, then evaluates each by producing a structured chain of visual questions and binary answers (Q₁? A₁; … Qₙ? Aₙ) to compute a quality score, returning the top-K=5 highest-scoring images for benchmark evaluation.

## Method

UniGen is built on Qwen2.5-1.5B with decoupled vision encoders: SigLIP (continuous) for understanding and MAGVIT-v2 (discrete) for generation, using next-token prediction for understanding and MaskGIT-style masked token prediction for generation. The training pipeline follows four stages—two-stage pre-training (PT-1 with ImageNet warmup, PT-2 with 40M open-source image-text pairs), supervised fine-tuning (SlowFast-LLaVA-1.5 for understanding, JourneyDB + text-2-image-2M for generation), DPO (13k synthetic preference triplets), and a lightweight 500-step CoT-V post-training—trained entirely on open-source data. The key contribution is Chain-of-Thought Verification (CoT-V), a test-time scaling strategy where the unified model acts as both generator and verifier: it generates N candidate images, then reasons step-by-step to decompose the prompt into atomic visual questions and verify each against the image, selecting the best via Best-of-N. This collaboration between understanding and generation within a single model yields +0.05 GENEVAL and +0.3 DPG-Bench gains over the base model without degrading understanding performance.

## Results

UniGen (1.5B) achieves a GENEVAL overall score of 0.78 and DPG-BENCH of 85.19 with CoT-V test-time scaling, surpassing all unified MLLMs and even outperforming larger generation-only models like DALLE-2 (0.52) and Emu3 (0.54) on GENEVAL. On image understanding, UniGen scores 67.4 on AI2D, 62.3 on GQA, 87.8 on POPE, 44.6 on MathVista, and 56.7 on RealWorld-QA, outperforming Janus-Pro (1.5B) by +3.7%, +7.8%, and +5.6% on AI2D, MathVista, and RW-QA respectively, while remaining competitive with understanding-specialist models like LLaVA-OV-0.5B and MM1.5-1B. The CoT-V strategy adds +0.04 on GENEVAL and +0.30 on DPG-BENCH over the base UniGen-DPO model (0.74 / 84.89) via Best-of-N selection with N=20 candidates.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On GENEVAL, UniGen+CoT-V (0.78) outperforms the strongest unified MLLM Janus-Pro (0.73) by +0.05, Show-o (0.53) by +0.25, and even surpasses larger generation-only models DALLE-2 (0.52) and Emu3 (0.54) by +0.26 and +0.24 respectively, despite being only 1.5B parameters. On DPG-BENCH, UniGen+CoT-V (85.19) leads Janus-Pro (82.63) by +2.56 and Show-o (71.70) by +13.49, and also exceeds Infinity (83.46) and DALLE-3 (83.50). On image understanding, UniGen beats Janus-Pro on AI2D (67.4 vs 63.7), MathVista (44.6 vs 36.8), and RealWorld-QA (56.7 vs 51.1), while remaining competitive with understanding-specialist models LLaVA-OV-0.5B (AI2D 57.1, MathVista 34.8) and MM1.5-1B (AI2D 59.3, MathVista 37.2) despite using a lower input resolution (384 vs AnyRes). The paper also demonstrates that its DPO data and CoT-V strategy generalize to Show-o, improving its GENEVAL from 0.56 to 0.64 and DPG-BENCH from 71.70 to 77.09, confirming the transferability of the proposed training and test-time techniques.

## Relation to Prior Work

UniGen builds directly on the decoupled-encoder architecture of Janus [84] (separate continuous SigLIP encoder for understanding and discrete MAGVIT-v2 tokenizer for generation) and the LLaVA [40] next-token-prediction pipeline for the understanding branch, while adopting MaskGIT [5] masked-token prediction for parallel image generation. Its key departure is the Chain-of-Thought Verification (CoT-V) test-time strategy, which repurposes the model's own understanding capability as a step-by-step semantic verifier for Best-of-N image selection — a direction only briefly explored by PARM [20] and MINT [81] but not previously combined with a lightweight post-training recipe. The paper also distinguishes itself by conducting the entire multi-stage training pipeline (pre-training, SFT, DPO, CoT-V) exclusively on open-source datasets, in contrast to Janus-Pro and Show-o which rely on large-scale internal data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The systematic, data-centric ablation of every training stage provides actionable guidance for practitioners building unified MLLMs, and the demonstration that a 1.5B model trained solely on public data can match or exceed larger unified models (and some generation-only models) on GENEVAL and DPG-BENCH is practically significant for resource-constrained settings. CoT-V's generalizability (validated on Show-o) suggests it could become a standard test-time technique for unified generation models. Key open questions include whether the approach scales to higher resolutions (beyond 256×256), larger model sizes, and more complex compositional prompts where the current pseudo-label-based CoT data becomes noisy.
