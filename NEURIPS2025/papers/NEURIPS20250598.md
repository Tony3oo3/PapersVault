## Problem

Supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on LongCoT traces activates long reasoning abilities in LLMs but causes the model to overfit problem-specific knowledge and heuristics, leading to significant out-of-distribution performance degradation. Common mitigations such as early stopping are insufficient because they simultaneously prevent adequate learning of the LongCoT paradigm itself. The paper asks whether the generalizable LongCoT reasoning paradigm can be explicitly decoupled from problem-specific content during the training process.

## Input / Output

Each training example is a triple (P_i, T_i, S_i): the problem, an exploratory thinking trace, and a deterministic step-by-step solution. The method computes two NLL gradients per iteration — one over the concatenated full response T_i ⊕ S_i and a reference gradient over S_i alone — exploiting the structural asymmetry that the solution phase encodes only problem-specific content while the thinking phase encodes both paradigm and content. The output is a model updated solely with the paradigm-relevant gradient, which at inference time produces LongCoT-style responses containing both a reflective thinking phase and a deterministic solution phase.

## Method

DLoFT decouples the full response gradient g_full into two orthogonal components by leveraging the intrinsic two-phase structure of LongCoT responses. A reference gradient g_ref is computed by backpropagating NLL loss over only the solution phase S_i, which captures exclusively problem-specific content. The content-relevant gradient g_con is isolated by projecting g_full onto g_ref, and the paradigm-relevant gradient g_par is obtained via orthogonalization (g_par = g_full − g_con). Only g_par is used to update model parameters, ensuring the model internalizes the LongCoT reasoning paradigm — reflection, backtracking, error correction, and exploration — without memorizing domain-specific facts or procedures. The additional computational overhead is minimal, as the projection and orthogonalization are lightweight vector operations and the reference gradient is computed over the much shorter solution phase, adding only ~10% training time over standard SFT.

## Results

On Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct trained on s1K, DLoFT achieves 36.7% average in-domain accuracy and 39.7% out-domain accuracy, compared to SFT's 27.8% and 27.3% respectively, yielding +8.9 and +12.4 point gains. On the 32B model, DLoFT outperforms SFT by +11.5 points on in-domains and +16.4 points on out-domains. Averaged across all 17 evaluation domains, DLoFT improves the baseline by +9.8 points while SFT degrades it by -1.8 points. As an RL cold-start, DLoFT amplifies subsequent GRPO gains to +5.0 versus +3.7 for SFT cold-start.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Baseline (no training) | — | 21.5 | 31.2 | 0% |
| SFT | g_full | 27.8 | 27.3 | 94% |
| DLoFT (content only) | g_con | 19.1 | 20.5 | 0% |
| **DLoFT (paradigm only)** | **g_par** | **36.7** | **39.7** | **100%** |

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is standard supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on LongCoT data, following the approach of s1 [7] and related works [8,9]. DLoFT decisively outperforms SFT on both in-domain and out-domain benchmarks across all model sizes and domains tested. On out-of-distribution domains, SFT causes significant degradation (e.g., -16.0 points on 'communication' for 7B, -7.5 on medicine for Meditron-7B), while DLoFT consistently improves over the untrained baseline. On in-domain benchmarks, DLoFT still surpasses SFT by +8.9 (7B) to +11.5 (32B) points, indicating that SFT's in-domain gains are also limited by overfitting to narrow problem-specific content. The ablation in Table 2 further confirms the decoupling mechanism works: updating with only the content-relevant gradient (g_con) produces no LongCoT behavior and degrades accuracy below baseline, while the paradigm-relevant gradient (g_par) alone achieves the full DLoFT performance with 100% LongCoT response rate.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the SFT-based approach to enabling LongCoT reasoning (as in s1 [7] and related work [8,9]) and the RL-with-cold-start pipeline [10,29], but departs from both by identifying and addressing a previously under-characterized failure mode: SFT on narrow-domain LongCoT data causes significant out-of-distribution degradation due to overfitting to problem-specific knowledge. The core novelty is a gradient decoupling mechanism that exploits the intrinsic two-phase structure of LongCoT responses (thinking + solution) to project out content-relevant gradients and retain only paradigm-relevant ones via orthogonalization. This repurposes the structural properties of LongCoT data as a signal for gradient manipulation, rather than treating the full response as a monolithic imitation target.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DLoFT offers a practical, low-overhead drop-in replacement for SFT in LongCoT training that substantially reduces the need for large-scale, domain-diverse LongCoT datasets — a significant cost and feasibility concern for practitioners. As an RL cold-start, it frees the pipeline from dependence on in-domain LongCoT data and amplifies downstream RL gains, which is practically important for domains where LongCoT annotation is expensive or infeasible. Key open questions include whether the gradient decoupling assumption (that the two-phase structure cleanly separates paradigm from content signals) holds at much larger scales (hundreds of billions of parameters) and with more diverse or noisy LongCoT data, and whether the projection-based decoupling generalizes to multi-phase or non-standard reasoning formats beyond the thinking-then-solution structure.
