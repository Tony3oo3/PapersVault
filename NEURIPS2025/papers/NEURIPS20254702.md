## Problem

Data duplication in federated LLM training degrades generalization and increases privacy vulnerability, yet existing solutions are inadequate: local deduplication misses inter-client duplicates, while the state-of-the-art federated method (EP-MPD) relies on hard deletion that discards informative samples, requires a trusted third party, and incurs heavy multi-round cryptographic overhead. No prior framework performs privacy-preserving soft deduplication (reweighting) in federated LLM training without a trusted third party.

## Input / Output

Each of n federated clients holds a local dataset of m_i preprocessed text samples (with duplicates synthetically injected at 10–90% rates for evaluation). The protocol outputs a per-sample global frequency vector for each client, from which a weight vector is derived via a logarithmic inverse-frequency formula. These weights are then applied as per-sample loss multipliers during FedAvg training, producing a retrained language model evaluated by perplexity on held-out test sets across eight text domains.

## Method

FedRW's core is the PPMPR protocol, which decomposes global frequency estimation into pairwise two-party computations (Π_2PC) built on private set intersection (PSI, specifically Vole-PSI). In each Π_2PC, two clients securely discover their shared samples and exchange local frequencies for those samples without revealing non-intersecting data, under a semi-honest security model proven in the UC framework. A parallel orchestration strategy schedules all C(n,2) pairwise protocols in a hierarchical binary-tree structure using cyclic index rotation, reducing total runtime complexity from O(n²) to O(2^⌈log₂n⌉) and achieving 17.61–28.78× speedup over EP-MPD. The derived global frequencies are converted to sample weights via W = 1/(ln(C+1)+ε), which softly down-weights frequent samples while preserving moderate redundancy, and are applied through a weighted batch loss during standard FedAvg aggregation.

## Results

FedRW achieves up to 28.78× speedup in preprocessing over the EP-MPD baseline (10–50 clients) and approximately 11.42% perplexity reduction on Haiku (GPT-2 Large, 30% duplication: 2.89 → 2.56). On larger models, the advantage grows: Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct reduces Twitter perplexity by ~26.57% (2.24 → 1.35) and Plays by ~25.85% (8.82 → 6.54). Across three modern 0.5–1B models, FedRW delivers an average ~13.43% relative perplexity reduction on the challenging Plays dataset.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is EP-MPD [Abadi et al., 2024], the state-of-the-art federated hard-deduplication method that relies on a trusted third party. FedRW outperforms EP-MPD on every dataset and model configuration tested: perplexity drops from 2.89 to 2.56 on Haiku (GPT-2 Large, 30% dup), from 2.21 to 1.61 on Rotten Tomatoes, and from 3.79 to 3.15 on Short Jokes. On modern models (Qwen3-0.6B, Qwen2.5-0.5B, Llama-3.2-1B), FedRW consistently wins, with the largest gap on Plays (~13.43% average relative reduction) and Twitter (~26.57% with Qwen2.5-7B). In preprocessing, FedRW's parallel PPMPR protocol achieves 17.61–28.78× speedup over EP-MPD(I) across 10–50 clients. A notable qualitative advantage: EP-MPD's hard deletion sometimes *worsens* perplexity on sparse datasets (e.g., Poetry 2.84→3.01, Plays 15.07→16.09 with DistilGPT2), whereas FedRW's soft reweighting preserves informative samples and maintains or improves performance under all duplication levels.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Vole-PSI cryptographic primitive [33] as the foundational building block for its two-party computation protocol, and positions itself as a direct departure from EP-MPD [25], the state-of-the-art federated hard deduplication method that relies on a trusted third party for encryption and duplicate counting. Conceptually, it bridges two previously separate literatures: centralized soft deduplication (reweighting via SoftDedup, DSIR, DoReMi) and distributed cryptographic deduplication (DupLESS, EP-MPD), introducing the first framework that performs frequency-aware sample reweighting across federated clients without any trusted intermediary. The parallel orchestration strategy, which restructures pairwise 2PC executions into a hierarchical binary-tree schedule, is a systems-level contribution orthogonal to the cryptographic protocol itself.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FedRW addresses a practical gap in federated LLM training where hard deduplication is either infeasible (no TTP) or counterproductive (discarding informative samples), offering a principled alternative that preserves data utility while maintaining privacy. However, the security guarantees are limited to a semi-honest model with no collusion, which significantly narrows the threat model compared to real-world federated deployments where malicious or curious parties are plausible. Open questions include whether the approach extends to malicious adversaries, whether the logarithmic reweighting heuristic generalizes to semantic (rather than exact) duplication, and whether the small-scale experimental datasets (Sonnets: 460 samples, Plays: 521) support the claimed generalizability to production-scale corpora.
