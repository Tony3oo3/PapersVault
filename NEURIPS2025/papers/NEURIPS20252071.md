## Problem

CrossQ, a batch-normalization-based actor-critic method, achieves state-of-the-art sample efficiency at UTD=1 but fails to scale reliably to higher update-to-data ratios on complex tasks like humanoid locomotion and muscular manipulation. The root cause is growing critic parameter norms that decrease the effective learning rate and increase dead neurons, leading to training instability. The paper asks how to extend CrossQ's sample-efficiency benefits to the high-UTD regime without drastic interventions such as periodic network resets.

## Input / Output

The agent consumes high-dimensional continuous state vectors (up to R^223 for humanoid environments) and outputs continuous action vectors, operating in a fully off-policy setting with a replay buffer. Training is evaluated over 1M environment steps across 25 tasks from DMC and MyoSuite, with the UTD ratio (number of gradient updates per environment step) as the primary scaling knob. The method uses ~600k network parameters, roughly 90% fewer than the BRO baseline (~5M), while matching or exceeding its performance.

## Method

The paper integrates weight normalization (WN) into CrossQ's batch normalization (BN) framework, exploiting the scale invariance of BN-normalized networks (Theorem 1) to constrain hidden-layer weight norms to a fixed value without altering network outputs. This keeps the effective learning rate stable (Theorem 2) and prevents the loss-of-plasticity phenomenon that degrades training at higher UTD ratios. Concretely, WN is applied to all hidden linear layers by projecting weights onto the unit ball after each gradient step, while the output layer remains unbounded with weight decay to preserve Q-value expressiveness. Target networks are re-introduced with a joined forward pass through both critic and target critic in training mode, ensuring BN layers compute shared statistics over replay-buffer and policy-sampled actions to handle distribution mismatch. The resulting CrossQ+WN scales stably from UTD=1 to UTD=5 without parameter resets or additional exploration policies, matching or outperforming BRO and SIMBA across all 25 tasks.

## Results

On 25 continuous control tasks (15 DMC + 10 MyoSuite), CrossQ + WN at UTD=5 achieves competitive or superior performance to BRO (UTD=10) and SIMBA (UTD=8) while using only half the UTD of BRO and ~90% fewer network parameters (~600k vs. ~5M). The method scales reliably with increasing UTD ratios (1→5→10→20), with performance monotonically improving or staying constant, unlike vanilla CrossQ which fails to benefit from higher UTDs. On the most complex dog and humanoid DMC tasks, CrossQ + WN UTD=5 already matches or exceeds BRO and SIMBA at lower UTD ratios. In wall-clock time comparisons (env steps/sec on a single RTX 4090), CrossQ + WN outperforms both SIMBA and BRO across all environments.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against BRO [Nauman et al., NeurIPS 2024], CrossQ + WN UTD=5 outperforms BRO UTD=2 on aggregated DMC and MyoSuite performance (Figure 1) and matches or exceeds BRO UTD=10 while using half the UTD and ~90% fewer parameters, without requiring parameter resets or extra exploration policies. Against SIMBA [Lee et al., ICLR 2025], CrossQ + WN outperforms SIMBA UTD=2 on both performance and wall-clock time across all 25 environments. Against vanilla CrossQ [Bhatt et al., ICLR 2024], the addition of WN is critical: vanilla CrossQ UTD=5 is unstable and fails to converge reliably on complex tasks, while CrossQ + WN UTD=5 trains stably and scales. Against SR-SAC [D'Oro et al., ICLR 2022] at UTD=32, CrossQ + WN UTD=5 achieves comparable or better performance with far fewer gradient updates. TD-MPC2 [Hansen et al., 2024] is listed as a model-based baseline for context but is not the primary comparison focus; the paper's main competitive claims are against the model-free BRO and SIMBA baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends CrossQ (Bhatt et al., ICLR 2024), which introduced batch normalization into actor-critic RL for sample efficiency, by adding weight normalization to address instability at higher update-to-data (UTD) ratios. The conceptual bridge draws from the loss-of-plasticity and effective-learning-rate literature (Lyle et al., 2024; Van Hasselt et al., 2019), connecting norm growth in continual learning to the primacy bias observed in high-UTD RL. It positions itself as a simpler alternative to BRO (Nauman et al., NeurIPS 2024), which relies on full parameter resets and extra exploration policies, and to SIMBA (Lee et al., ICLR 2025), which uses layer normalization with a simplicity bias.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical takeaway is that a single architectural addition (weight normalization on hidden layers) replaces the need for drastic interventions like periodic network resets, making high-UTD training more stable and computationally efficient. An interesting open question raised is the role of target networks: the paper finds they are actually beneficial (contradicting CrossQ's original design), suggesting the interaction between BN, WN, and target networks is not yet fully understood. Limitations include evaluation only on continuous state-action benchmarks (no discrete or vision-based tasks), and the theoretical analysis addresses function approximation stability rather than RL convergence or sample complexity.
