## Problem

Extending Process Reward Models (PRMs) to multimodal LLMs is hampered by two compounding issues: the high-dimensional image space causes more severe distribution shift between training and test sets, and existing multimodal reasoning datasets suffer from marked quality imbalance (unnecessary modalities, trivially easy questions, noisy inputs) that degrades PRM generalization. No prior work provides an automatic, data-driven strategy to handle this imbalance during PRM training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254073/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The PRM takes a multimodal input pair (RGB image + text instruction) together with a prefix of a chain-of-thought trajectory (the first i reasoning steps) and outputs a scalar reward in [0,1] for that partial state. At inference, the MLLM generates k candidate CoTs (default k=8) following a 5-step structural thinking prompt; the PRM scores every step of each candidate, the per-step log-odds are summed via an aggregation function, and the highest-scoring trajectory is selected as the final answer. Training uses ~15k examples across 15 datasets in 4 domains (science, chart, geometry, commonsense) plus ~1k MMMU validation examples for the meta-level.

## Method

DreamPRM formulates PRM training as a bi-level optimization problem. At the lower level, the PRM (initialized from Qwen2-VL-2B-Instruct) is fine-tuned with a domain-weighted sum of per-domain MSE losses on Monte Carlo correctness signals, where each of the K training domains carries a trainable weight α_k. At the upper level, the optimized PRM is evaluated on a held-out meta dataset (MMMU) using a novel aggregation-function loss—MSE between the sigmoid of the summed log-odds score and a binary correctness label—whose gradient updates the domain weights. This design makes the meta objective mirror the actual inference-time selection procedure, so domains that produce coherent, step-level-verifiable reasoning receive higher weights while noisy or trivially easy domains are down-weighted. The algorithm uses 5 inner unrolling steps per outer update, implemented with the Betty library, and converges in ~10 hours on a single A100.

## Results

DreamPRM achieves the best accuracy across all five multimodal reasoning benchmarks: 57.4% on WEMATH, 68.9% on MATHVISTA, 22.1% on MATHVISION, 61.4% on MMVET, and 62.3% on MMSTAR, yielding an average +4.0% gain over the InternVL-2.5-8B-MPO base model. On the MATHVISTA leaderboard (as of October 2025), DreamPRM with o4-mini reaches a top-1 accuracy of 85.2%, surpassing all prior entries including VL-Rethinker, Kimi-k1.6-preview, and OpenAI o1. The method also demonstrates monotonic scaling: accuracy improves consistently as the number of selected CoTs increases from k=2 to k=8 on all five benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DreamPRM outperforms all PRM-based baselines on every benchmark: it beats Vanilla PRM by 2–3% (e.g., 68.9% vs. 67.2% on MATHVISTA), s1-PRM by 1.3–3.1% (e.g., 68.9% vs. 65.8% on MATHVISTA), and CaR-PRM by 0.8–2.2% (e.g., 62.3% vs. 61.1% on MMSTAR), confirming that automatic domain reweighting surpasses handcrafted heuristic data selection. Against non-PRM test-time scaling methods, DreamPRM leads Self-consistency by 1.0–4.0% and ORM by 0.5–5.5% across the five benchmarks. Compared to large closed-source models, DreamPRM (built on an 8B base) surpasses GPT-4V on 4 of 5 datasets (e.g., 68.9% vs. 49.9% on MATHVISTA) and Gemini-1.5-Pro on 4 of 5 (e.g., 57.4% vs. 46.0% on WEMATH), though it trails GPT-4V on MMVET (61.4% vs. 67.7%) and Gemini-1.5-Pro on MMVET (61.4% vs. 64.0%). On the MATHVISTA leaderboard, DreamPRM with o4-mini (85.2%) exceeds all listed competitors including VL-Rethinker, Step-R1-V-Mini, Kimi-k1.6-preview, and OpenAI o1, and also generalizes to GPT-4.1-mini (74.4% at k=8 vs. 71.5% pass@1).

## Relation to Prior Work

DreamPRM sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) Process Reward Models for step-level reasoning verification (Lightman et al., 2024; MiPS, Wang et al., 2024), which the paper extends from text-only LLMs to multimodal settings; (2) domain reweighting for data mixture optimization in LLM pre-training (DoReMi, Xie et al., 2023; DOGE, Fan et al., 2024), which the paper adapts to the PRM fine-tuning context; and (3) bi-level optimization from meta-learning (MAML, Finn et al., 2017) and architecture search (DARTS, Liu et al., 2019), used here to jointly optimize PRM parameters and domain weights. The key departure from prior work is the novel aggregation function loss at the upper level, which mirrors the actual PRM-based inference procedure (sum of log-odds over steps) rather than using a standard classification loss, and the application of domain reweighting specifically to multimodal PRM training where dataset quality imbalance is more severe than in text-only settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in multimodal reasoning: the quality imbalance across available training datasets degrades PRM generalization, and handcrafted data selection heuristics (s1-PRM, CaR-PRM) are suboptimal. The learned domain weights (0.55–1.49) provide interpretable evidence that this imbalance is real and consequential. Limitations include the fixed domain set assumption, computational cost of Monte Carlo sampling, and the absence of error bars across runs. Open questions include whether instance-level (rather than domain-level) reweighting would yield further gains, how the approach scales to new domains without retraining, and whether the aggregation function loss can be replaced by cheaper proxies that still capture inference-time behavior.
