## Problem

As VLMs develop increasingly sophisticated multimodal reasoning capabilities (e.g., Chain-of-Thought, Visualization-of-Thought), a critical tension emerges between their logical processing and safety alignment that prior jailbreak attacks have not exploited. Existing prompt-manipulation attacks rely on single static images or simple typographic prompts, leaving unexplored the vulnerability that arises when a VLM must perform multi-step procedural reasoning over a coherent visual narrative. This paper identifies and weaponizes that logic-versus-safety conflict as a general attack surface.

## Input / Output

The only human-provided input is a risk category name (e.g., 'Illegal Activity | Drug-Related Offenses'); everything else is auto-generated. The attack produces a single composite RGB image in which a sequence of Stable-Diffusion-synthesized sub-images (one per entity) are arranged left-to-right with entity-name captions beneath each, plus a text prompt containing shuffled action verbs and a shuffled risk-scenario title embedded in a 'villain's lair game' template. The victim VLM is expected to reconstruct the full scenario title and produce a ≥500-token step-by-step description, which constitutes the harmful output. Using a single composite image (rather than multiple separate image inputs) keeps the computational cost equivalent to prior single-image attacks.

## Method

VoTA operates in two automated stages. First, Risk Scenario Generation uses two attack LLMs (Gemini-1.5-Pro and GPT-4o) to produce 100 concise (≤20-token) risk scenarios per subcategory across 19 VLSBench subcategories (1,900 total), with human deduplication. Second, Multimodal Thought Construction decomposes each scenario into an entity–action–entity chain via a dedicated LLM prompt, generates per-entity text-to-image prompts, synthesizes sub-images with Stable Diffusion 3.5 Large, and assembles them into one composite image with captions. The verbal thoughts (inter-entity action verbs) are deliberately placed in the text prompt rather than the image, forcing the VLM to perform cross-modal logical reasoning to connect visual entities with textual actions. This cross-modal decomposition widens the gap between the model's reasoning pathway and its safety filters, because the model must 'complete the story' to satisfy the prompt's reconstruction task, and the logical imperative to produce a coherent, detailed narrative overrides its refusal behavior. An optional iterative refinement step re-optimizes the entity-action decomposition to increase operational realism, yielding modest additional ASR gains on open-source models.

## Results

VoTA achieves an average Attack Success Rate (ASR) of 90.41% (RoBERTa evaluation) and 95.80% (GPT-4o evaluation) across 15 VLMs (9 open-source, 6 commercial), improving the best prior method (MML) from 63.70% to 90.41% (+26.71 pp) under RoBERTa and from 85.56% to 95.80% (+10.24 pp) under GPT-4o. Per-model ASRs range from 51.6% (Claude 3.5 Sonnet v2, the most robust model) to 100.0% (Gemini-1.5-Pro). Against two SoTA defenses (SafeRLHF and AdaShield-S), VoTA still leads with 89.47% and 80.32% ASR respectively, far exceeding the next-best baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VoTA outperforms all 8 baselines on every one of the 15 evaluated VLMs under both RoBERTa and GPT-4o evaluation. The closest competitor is MML (multi-modal linkage), which achieves 63.70% average ASR (RoBERTa) and 85.56% (GPT-4o); VoTA surpasses it by 26.71 pp and 10.24 pp respectively. Among the remaining baselines, MIS (58.99%) and Flow-JD (54.09%) are the next strongest, while typographic-image methods (FigStep 39.62%, Hades 21.17%) and prompt-only methods (QR 17.31%, SafeBench 21.49%) trail significantly. The gap is most dramatic on commercial models: VoTA reaches 99–100% ASR on GPT-4o, Gemini-2.0-Flash, and Gemini-1.5-Pro, whereas the best baseline (MML) only reaches 74–83% on those same models. The sole exception where VoTA's advantage narrows is Claude 3.5 Sonnet v2 (51.60% vs. MML's 43.44%), attributed to Anthropic's robust external defenses and a 40.37% rejection rate.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the prompt-manipulation-based jailbreak attack line (FigStep, Hades, MML, Flow-JD, SI-Attack, MIS) by repurposing the Multimodal Visualization-of-Thought (MVoT) concept—originally designed to *enable* VLM reasoning—into an *attack* mechanism. Rather than exploiting a single visual cue (typographic images, a static prime, or a role-play character), VoTA constructs a dynamically generated chain of T2I-synthesized images with interleaved verbal actions, targeting the model's procedural reasoning over a multi-step narrative. The key conceptual departure from prior work is framing the attack around the competition between logical reasoning and safety alignment, rather than around bypassing a single safety filter or inducing a harmful persona.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper surfaces a concerning and underexplored tradeoff: as VLMs gain stronger reasoning capabilities, they become *more* vulnerable to attacks that exploit that reasoning, suggesting that improving reasoning and improving safety may be in tension. This has direct implications for the roadmap of VLM development—safety alignment must be co-designed with reasoning capabilities rather than bolted on afterward. Open questions include whether the reasoning-safety conflict is fundamental or addressable through better alignment training, how the attack generalizes to video-based VLMs, and whether the limited effectiveness against Claude 3.5 Sonnet reflects a replicable defense pattern. The paper also acknowledges that successful attacks often produce harmful *intent* without fully actionable specifics, tempering the real-world severity.
