## Problem

Existing LLM safety mechanisms—RLHF alignment, off-the-shelf guardrail classifiers, and decoding-time interventions—are empirical and lack formal, provable safety guarantees, making them unreliable for high-stakes deployment and regulatory compliance. No prior work provides a certification framework that computes high-probability upper bounds on the safety risk of LLM generations or identifies decoding configurations guaranteed to meet a specified risk threshold.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252248/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt x, a fixed LLM p_θ (black-box, model-agnostic), and a fixed guardrail model G that assigns category probabilities (e.g., violence, sexual, safe) to input-output pairs. It produces (1) a safe generated text y, (2) a conformal safety risk upper bound r̂_α computed from a calibration set of size N_cal at confidence level 1−δ, and (3) a certified set of generation configurations α_r whose family-wise error rate is controlled at level δ. The guardrail model is consumed as a black-box classifier at each claim boundary during decoding, and the risk function is R_G(x,y) = 1 − G("safe" | x, y).

## Method

C-SafeGen couples a Claim-based Stream Decoding (CSD) algorithm with a conformal risk certification framework. CSD decomposes generation into semantically complete claims (detected via termination-token probability thresholds), evaluates each claim against the guardrail model at critical points, and backtracks to resample claims whose safety margin falls below a tunable threshold α_b; a KV-cache mechanism stores verified claims to avoid recomputation, and safe resampling (KV-cache perturbation) prevents decoding stagnation. The certification framework applies conformal risk control (via Hoeffding and Bentkus inequalities) to derive a high-probability upper bound on safety risk for any given configuration (Theorem 1), and uses Bonferroni-style FWER control to certify a set of configurations that collectively guarantee a nominal risk level (Theorem 2). A key theoretical result (Theorem 3) proves that under a claim risk cascade assumption, CSD's output distribution exactly matches the restricted (renormalized-over-safe) output distribution, ensuring no distributional distortion beyond the guardrail constraint.

## Results

On AdvBench and JailbreakBench with Llama-3.1-8B/13B and ShieldGemma-9B as guardrail, CSD consistently achieves the lowest conformal safety risk upper bound and empirical safety risk among all six evaluated methods. The conformal risk bounds are tight, with empirical risks closely tracking the theoretical upper bounds. In terms of efficiency, CSD reduces per-instance runtime to approximately 7 seconds (vs. >40 s for Best-of-N), and CSD+KV Cache further reduces this to below 5 seconds while maintaining comparable safety risk. All certified SafeGen configurations (Theorem 2) remain strictly below the nominal risk threshold of 0.10 across both benchmarks and model sizes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CSD outperforms all four baselines on both conformal and empirical safety risk across AdvBench and JailbreakBench for both Llama-3.1-8B and 13B. SafeDecoding (SafeDec) [44] and Safety-Opt [48] reduce risk substantially relative to vanilla generation but still trail CSD in both risk metrics. Best-of-N [35] is the weakest baseline on safety risk and the most expensive at >40 s per instance, making it impractical for real-time use. The self-reminder prompt (Reminder) [42] provides only modest risk reduction and, notably, the 13B model sometimes exhibits slightly higher risk than the 8B model under this method, indicating that model scaling alone does not guarantee improved safety. CSD+KV Cache matches CSD's safety performance while cutting runtime by roughly 30%, establishing the best efficiency–safety trade-off among all compared methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate research threads: conformal risk control (Angelopoulos et al., 2021/2022) and Holm's FWER-controlled multiple testing (1979), which are repurposed here to provide statistical certification for LLM safety, and safety decoding methods (SafeDecoding, RAIN, contrastive decoding) that enforce constraints at inference time but lack formal guarantees. The key departure is the introduction of claim-based streaming with a backtracking mechanism as the decoding substrate, which allows the conformal framework to operate on sequentially generated claims rather than full outputs. Unlike prior guardrail approaches that are purely reactive (detect-then-reject), CSD proactively revises unsafe claims during generation while provably recovering the restricted output distribution (Theorem 3).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

C-SafeGen is the first framework to provide high-probability, finite-sample upper bounds on LLM safety risk, moving the field from empirical 'we tested and it works' to certifiable compliance — a prerequisite for regulated deployments in healthcare, finance, and policy. The model-agnostic, black-box design means it can wrap any LLM without retraining. Key limitations include dependence on the quality and coverage of the external guardrail model, the claim risk cascade assumption (Assumption 4.1), and vulnerability to distributional shift at deployment time; the authors explicitly flag the need for adaptive or online certification mechanisms as future work.
