## Problem

Existing noisy multi-label learning (NML) methods rely on discriminative paradigms such as noise transition matrix estimation and small-loss correction, which face substantial optimization difficulties compared to single-label settings. This paper asks whether a generative reformulation—treating multi-label prediction as a conditional label generation task—can more robustly learn the feature-to-label mapping under noisy annotations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254857/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images with noisy multi-label annotations as input and produces a clean multi-label set as output. Internally, a frozen pre-trained encoder (ResNet-50 or ViT-14/L) extracts image features that condition the diffusion process, while the diffusion target is a C×C one-hot matrix formed from the multi-label vector rather than a single-dimensional vector. At inference, DDIM with S=10 steps (out of T=1000) generates the label matrix, which is then inverted to recover the final multi-label set.

## Method

CAD reformulates NML as a conditional denoising diffusion process over a C×C label matrix, where the forward process is unconditional (features do not alter the noise schedule) and the reverse process is feature-conditioned. Two key mechanisms address label noise: (1) a neighborhood label estimator replaces point-wise noisy labels with the normalized frequency distribution of K nearest neighbors in the frozen encoder's latent space, serving as the pseudo-clean diffusion target y₀; (2) a co-occurrence-aware weighting scheme estimates a meta-label co-occurrence matrix from a clean subset (identified via a binary GMM on neighborhood instability scores) and modulates the per-sample loss during the reverse process to penalize implausible label combinations. The training objective is the standard DDPM noise-prediction MSE, weighted by the co-occurrence score, and the entire pre-trained encoder remains frozen throughout diffusion training.

## Results

On noisy Pascal-VOC 2007, CAD-V (ViT-14/L encoder) achieves 87.82% mAP and 82.00% OF1 under 10% symmetric noise, and maintains 84.61% mAP / 72.41% OF1 under 50% symmetric noise. On Pascal-VOC 2012 with 50% symmetric noise, CAD-V reaches 88.31% mAP and 75.22% OF1. On the real-world NUS-WIDE dataset, CAD-V achieves 65.13% mAP, 75.58% OF1, and 63.68% CF1, outperforming all baselines. The paper reports a 5–8% OF1 improvement over prior SOTA on noisy Pascal-VOC datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CAD-V consistently outperforms all baselines across every dataset and noise setting. On MS-COCO, CAD-V surpasses LRAD-V by an average of ~8% mAP and LSNPC-V by ~4% mAP, e.g., 71.55% vs. 66.75% (LRAD-V) and 67.39% (LSNPC-V) under 50% symmetric noise. In the ResNet-based (Common) group, CAD-R leads LSNPC-R and HLC by ~2% mAP on Pascal-VOC 2012 at 50% noise (75.53% vs. 73.26% and 69.03%). On the real-world NUS-WIDE dataset, CAD-V achieves 65.13% mAP, edging out LSNPC-V (64.37%), LRAD-V (63.95%), and HLC (63.14%), while Standard and ASL trail by over 5% and 1% respectively. The gap between CAD-V and the Standard baseline exceeds 30% mAP in the most severe 50% asymmetric noise setting on Pascal-VOC 2007 (69.37% vs. 46.72%), underscoring the method's robustness under extreme label corruption.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the diffusion-based classification line initiated by CARD (which first framed single-label classification as conditional label generation) and LRAD (which added neighbor retrieval for noisy single-label learning) to the multi-label setting. It departs from the dominant discriminative NML paradigm (noise transition matrix estimation, holistic label correction) by reformulating noisy multi-label learning as a generative denoising task, representing multi-labels as C×C one-hot matrices rather than vectors. The co-occurrence-aware weighting in the reverse process and the neighborhood-based pseudo-clean label reconstruction are novel multi-label-specific mechanisms not present in prior diffusion-based classifiers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first application of diffusion models to noisy multi-label learning, offering a principled generative alternative to the optimization-heavy discriminative approaches that dominate NML. The ablation showing that even a weak ResNet-50 neighborhood estimate (which underperforms HLC) is dramatically improved by the diffusion model suggests the generative framework can compensate for imperfect feature spaces, a useful property for practitioners with limited pre-training budgets. Open questions include scalability to very large label vocabularies (the C×C matrix grows quadratically), sensitivity to the GMM-based clean-subset selection under extreme noise, and whether the matrix representation provides genuine inductive bias over a simple C-dimensional vector with co-occurrence regularization.
