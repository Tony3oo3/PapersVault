## Problem

Reconstruction-based and joint-embedding methods are the two dominant paradigms in self-supervised learning, yet practitioners lack clear theoretical guidelines for choosing between them. This paper provides the first closed-form solutions for both SSL families that are explicitly parameterized by the data augmentation structure, revealing that—unlike supervised learning—SSL requires a minimum alignment between augmentations and irrelevant noise features to achieve optimal performance, even in the infinite-sample limit.

## Input / Output

The theoretical framework models data as having k important signal components (captured by the SVD of X) plus d−k irrelevant Gaussian noise components with covariance Γ. Augmentations are parameterized by an alignment coefficient α that controls how much of the noise distribution is injected into the transformation (τ(x) = x + θ + αγ). Reconstruction methods output a full-dimensional reconstructed input from an augmented view via an encoder-decoder pair, while joint-embedding methods output a k-dimensional latent representation constrained to be orthonormal across the dataset, serving as the basis for downstream linear probing.

## Method

The paper derives closed-form solutions for linear SSL models: the reconstruction solution (Theorem 3.1) reduces to a rank-k Eckart-Young projection of a matrix involving the augmented data covariance, while the joint-embedding solution (Theorem 3.2) reduces to selecting the top-k eigenvectors of S^{−1/2}GS^{−1/2}, where S and G capture second- and first-order statistics of augmented views. Building on these, Propositions 4.3 and 4.4 show that both SSL families require a minimum augmentation-noise alignment α to discard irrelevant features, a requirement that persists as n→∞ (unlike supervised learning, Proposition 4.2, where large n alone suffices). Corollary 4.5 then compares the two alignment thresholds: when irrelevant noise has low spectral norm, reconstruction imposes the weaker requirement; when noise magnitude is high, joint-embedding imposes a strictly weaker requirement. These predictions are validated with linear models on MNIST, Fashion-MNIST, Kuzushiji-MNIST, and scRNA-seq data, and with deep networks (ViT-MAE, ViT-DINO, ResNet-BYOL) on ImageNet-100 under ImageNet-C corruptions, where MAE suffers a 25.1% average accuracy drop from severity 1 to 5 versus only 10.5–12.4% for DINO and BYOL.

## Results

On ImageNet-100 with ImageNet-C corruptions, MAE (reconstruction) suffers a 25.1% average accuracy drop from severity 1 to 5, while DINO (joint-embedding) drops only 10.5% and BYOL drops 12.4%. For example, under Gaussian Noise corruption at severity 5, MAE achieves 44.8% top-1 accuracy versus 59.0% for DINO and 56.4% for BYOL. The theoretical analysis (Corollary 4.5) further shows that joint-embedding imposes a strictly weaker augmentation-noise alignment requirement than reconstruction when irrelevant noise features have high magnitude, explaining the empirical robustness gap. Linear model experiments on MNIST with controlled Gaussian noise confirm that reconstruction is preferable under weak noise (λ_max^Γ = 10³) but degrades sharply under strong noise (λ_max^Γ = 10⁶) without sufficient augmentation alignment.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three SSL methods: MAE (reconstruction-based, ViT), DINO (joint-embedding, ViT), and BYOL (joint-embedding, ResNet) under controlled corruptions on ImageNet-100. MAE consistently loses to both joint-embedding methods across all three corruption types, with accuracy drops roughly 2× larger (e.g., 27.9% vs. 12.4% for Pixelate, 27.3% vs. 12.7% for Gaussian Noise). DINO and BYOL are broadly comparable, with DINO showing a slight edge on Zoomblur (6.5% drop vs. 9.0%) and BYOL slightly ahead on Gaussian Noise (16.1% vs. 12.7% drop — actually DINO is better here at 12.7%). The theoretical contribution (Corollary 4.5) explains this gap: when irrelevant noise features have high magnitude (as in real images with backgrounds), joint-embedding requires a strictly lower augmentation-noise alignment threshold (α_JE < α_RC) to achieve optimal performance, making it inherently more robust. Under weak noise conditions, the theory predicts reconstruction should be preferable, which is confirmed by linear model experiments on MNIST where MAE-like methods maintain performance without tailored augmentations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Tikhonov-regularization characterization of data augmentation in supervised learning (Bishop 1995; Lin et al. 2024) to the self-supervised setting, deriving the first closed-form solutions for both reconstruction and joint-embedding SSL linear models that are directly parameterized by the augmentation structure. It builds on the unifying formalism of Garrido et al. (2022) to frame the joint-embedding objective with a simple orthonormality constraint reminiscent of VICReg, and positions itself against a body of SSL theory (Balestriero & LeCun 2022; Cabannes et al. 2023; Tian et al. 2021) that the authors characterize as lacking a rigorous statistical treatment of augmentation effects. The key departure is showing that, unlike supervised learning where augmentation acts as a fixed regularizer, SSL representations are fundamentally governed by the alignment between augmentations and irrelevant data components.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first principled criterion (Corollary 4.5) for choosing between reconstruction and joint-embedding methods based on the magnitude of irrelevant noise features, resolving a long-standing practical question that has been addressed only empirically. The finding that SSL cannot overcome augmentation-noise misalignment with infinite data—unlike supervised learning—reframes the role of augmentations as a hard requirement rather than a soft regularizer, with direct implications for designing SSL pipelines on noisy real-world data (histopathology, scRNA-seq, Earth observation). Limitations include the restriction to linear models and asymptotic (infinite-sample) analysis; extending these results to finite-sample regimes and nonlinear architectures remains open, as does characterizing the interplay between sample complexity and augmentation design in SSL.
