## Problem

Domain Generalization with Generalized Category Discovery (DG-GCD) requires a model trained only on labeled source data to classify unseen target-domain samples into both known and novel categories, but the sole prior method (DG²CD-Net) relies on expensive episodic training across many synthetic domains with task-vector aggregation, incurring high computational cost and cumulative approximation errors. Additionally, existing GCD and DG-GCD methods operate in Euclidean or hyperspherical spaces that struggle to encode the hierarchical semantic structures needed for robust joint domain and category generalization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253322/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During training, the method consumes labeled source-domain RGB images augmented with 1–2 GPT-4o-guided diffusion-generated synthetic domains (selected via an FID-based domain-diversity score). At test time, it takes unlabeled target-domain RGB images, encodes them through a frozen DINO-pretrained ViT plus a 3-layer MLP, projects the resulting features into the Poincaré ball via a curvature-aware exponential map, and applies K-Means clustering to produce cluster assignments that partition samples into seen and novel categories. The learnable curvature parameter adapts per dataset (e.g., 0.041 for Office-Home, 0.38 for DomainNet), and the estimated number of clusters is inferred automatically at test time.

## Method

HiDISC learns semantically structured, domain-invariant embeddings in the Poincaré ball without episodic simulation or target access. A frozen DINO ViT encoder feeds a 3-layer MLP whose Euclidean output is mapped into hyperbolic space via a learnable-curvature exponential map. To simulate domain shift efficiently, only 1–2 GPT-4o-guided diffusion domains are generated and selected by a Fréchet Inception Distance–based diversity score, reducing training FLOPs by over 96× compared to DG²CD-Net. Tangent CutMix performs curvature-preserving interpolation in the tangent space at the origin to synthesize pseudo-novel samples, replacing Euclidean CutMix. A unified loss combines three terms: a penalized Busemann loss that anchors seen-class features to fixed ideal prototypes on the Poincaré boundary while reserving interior volume for novel categories; a hybrid hyperbolic contrastive loss that linearly blends geodesic distance and tangent-space cosine similarity for fine-grained clustering; and an adaptive outlier repulsion loss that pushes Tangent CutMix samples away from known-class regions using a quantile-based margin.

## Results

HiDISC achieves an average overall clustering accuracy of 54.12% across PACS, Office-Home, and DomainNet, outperforming the strongest prior DG-GCD method DG²CD-Net (52.06%) by +2.06% and the best hyperbolic baseline Hyp-SelfEx (51.55%) by +2.57%. On novel-class discovery (New), HiDISC reaches 52.05% average, surpassing DG²CD-Net (50.78%) by +1.27%. Notably, HiDISC uses only 2 synthetic domains versus 9 for DG²CD-Net, reducing training FLOPs by over 96× (16.53 PFLOPs vs. 1,586 PFLOPs) while also estimating cluster counts more accurately (e.g., 351 vs. 355 on DomainNet, ground truth 345).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HiDISC outperforms the only prior DG-GCD method, DG²CD-Net [1], by +2.06% average All accuracy and +1.27% on New, while using 2 synthetic domains instead of 9 and reducing training FLOPs by ~96×. Against the hyperbolic GCD baselines, HiDISC beats Hyp-SelfEx [64] by +2.57% (All) and +1.87% (New), and Hyp-GCD [22] by +8.00% (All) and +5.92% (New), confirming that hyperbolic geometry alone is insufficient without domain-generalization mechanisms. The Euclidean CD-GCD method CDAD-Net [18] trails HiDISC by +5.13% (All) and +5.22% (New) on average, and the hyperbolic variant Hyp-DG²CD-Net actually underperforms its Euclidean counterpart (48.59% vs. 52.06% Avg All), suggesting that naively transplanting episodic task-arithmetic into hyperbolic space is counterproductive. Among source-only GCD methods, SelfEx [64] is the strongest (48.93% Avg All) but still lags HiDISC by 5.19%, while GCD [15] and SimGCD [25] fall further behind. The only method exceeding HiDISC is CDAD-Net under the domain-adaptation (DA) upper bound, which has joint source-target access and is not a valid DG-GCD comparison.

## Relation to Prior Work

HiDISC unifies three previously separate threads: the Busemann-based prototype anchoring from Ghadimi Atigh et al. (NeurIPS 2021), the hyperbolic contrastive formulation from HypCD (Liu et al., CVPR 2025), and the CutMix interpolation strategy (Yun et al., ICCV 2019) adapted to the Poincaré tangent space. It departs sharply from the only prior DG-GCD method, DG²CD-Net, which relies on episodic training across many synthetic domains and task-vector aggregation; HiDISC replaces this with a single non-episodic training loop over at most two GPT-guided diffusion domains. Conceptually, the paper's key positioning is that hyperbolic geometry itself provides much of the domain invariance that DG²CD-Net achieves through expensive episodic simulation, collapsing two separate challenges (domain shift and novel-class discovery) into one geometric framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 96× FLOPs reduction over DG²CD-Net makes DG-GCD practically accessible, and the finding that training solely on the source domain in hyperbolic space already yields competitive results (56.07% vs. 56.78% full model on Office-Home) suggests the geometry itself encodes domain robustness—a conceptual insight that could reshape how DG-GCD is approached. Limitations include reliance on diffusion-generated synthetic domains (potential bias propagation in safety-critical settings) and evaluation on only three benchmarks with moderate class counts. Open questions include whether the learnable-curvature mechanism generalizes to very large-scale category spaces, whether continual DG-GCD (where novel categories arrive incrementally) is tractable in hyperbolic space, and how the framework interacts with vision-language models for open-vocabulary discovery.
