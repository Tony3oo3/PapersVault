## Problem

Developers deploying AI systems often have access to multiple runtime monitors of varying cost and performance, but existing multi-monitor protocols rely on ad-hoc heuristics to decide when to invoke a second monitor and how to combine their outputs. This paper asks: given a unified budget covering both monitor invocations and safety interventions, what is the optimal protocol that maximizes recall on misaligned outputs?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255416/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes AI-generated code solutions paired with their programming questions, along with pre-computed monitor scores (0–9 suspicion ratings from prompted LLMs, made continuous via Gaussian noise), a unified budget Y, and relative costs for each monitor and the audit action. It outputs a complete monitoring protocol parameterized by: which monitor to call first, a score region [t_bot, t_top) within which to call the second monitor, and a log-likelihood-ratio threshold τ that determines which outputs receive the safety intervention. The protocol is evaluated by recall on held-out misaligned outputs and average cost on aligned outputs.

## Method

The paper decomposes policy optimization into two stages: (1) exhaustive search over a parameterized class of monitor invocation strategies (e.g., Π_SR, which calls one monitor first and a second only within a single contiguous score region), and (2) Neyman-Pearson lemma-based audit allocation that targets outputs with the highest misaligned/aligned likelihood ratios. Likelihood ratios are estimated via Gaussian kernel density estimation on aligned and misaligned monitor score distributions. The unified budget constraint is key: it allows the optimizer to trade off spending on monitors versus spending on audits, enabling stochastic monitor calls (e.g., calling a monitor only 71% of the time) that can more than double recall over always-on baselines. The framework models the full protocol as a POMDP with hidden alignment state, and the exhaustive search is tractable for 1–4 monitors with simple invocation rules, with complexity scaling as m!·n^{2(m−1)}·N_D.

## Results

In a code review setting (detecting backdoored BigCodeBench solutions), the proposed Π_NPS stochastic policy achieves more than 2× the recall of the deterministic Π_naive baseline at a budget of $1.20 with audit cost 20× monitoring cost, by calling the monitor only 71% of the time and auditing the top 3.4% of outputs by likelihood ratio (FPR 2.4% vs. 1%). In the two-monitor setting, the Π_SR policy (calling Llama 3.1 8B first, then Llama 3.1 70B only if the 8B score falls in [5, 6.2), auditing if LLR > 0.81) Pareto dominates all single-monitor and always-call-both baselines across the budget range $0.50–$1.75, with the advantage being statistically significant at the 10% level (Figure 6).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against three baselines. First, Π_naive from Greenblatt et al. (2024) — which always calls a single monitor and audits outputs above a raw score threshold — is outperformed by the likelihood-ratio-based Π_NP policy, which exploits a 'winner's curse' effect where the highest raw scores do not correspond to the highest misalignment likelihood (Figure 2b). Second, the stochastic single-monitor policy Π_NPS more than doubles recall over Π_naive at the $1.20 budget by strategically reducing monitor calls to 71% and reallocating budget to auditing (Figure 3a). Third, in the two-monitor setting, the proposed Π_SR policy Pareto dominates both the best single-monitor policy (Π_NPS with either 8B or 70B alone) and the always-call-both baseline Π_BM across the entire evaluated budget range ($0.50–$1.75), with the advantage being statistically significant at the 10% level after controlling for random seeds (Figure 6). The key mechanism is that Π_SR allocates the expensive 70B monitor only to the score region [5, 6.2) where additional information is most valuable, rather than calling it uniformly.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the AI control monitoring framework of Greenblatt et al. (2024), which defined a single-monitor policy class (Π_naive: always call the monitor, audit above a raw score threshold), by introducing a two-stage optimization that decomposes the problem into (1) exhaustive search over multi-monitor invocation strategies and (2) Neyman-Pearson likelihood-ratio-based audit allocation. The statistical machinery draws directly on the classifier-combination literature (Barreno et al., 2007; Clémençon & Vayatis, 2008), which established that optimal ROC curves from combined classifiers are obtained via likelihood-ratio tests, but the key departure is the unified budget constraint that couples monitor invocation costs with audit false-positive costs, enabling strategic trade-offs between spending on monitoring versus intervention. The POMDP formulation of the monitoring process and the parameterized policy classes (Π_NP, Π_NPS, Π_SR) are novel contributions that make the Neyman-Pearson framework tractable for the practical case of 2–4 monitors with heterogeneous costs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a principled, implementable methodology for AI safety practitioners who have multiple monitors of varying cost and performance and need to allocate a fixed budget optimally. The finding that combining a cheap 8B monitor with an expensive 70B monitor Pareto-dominates either monitor alone, and that stochastic monitoring can more than double recall over always-on monitoring, offers concrete guidance for resource allocation in safety systems. Key limitations include the brute-force search being intractable beyond ~4 monitors, the assumption that the attack distribution matches training data (a significant concern in adversarial AI control settings), and the single empirical validation on BigCodeBench backdoors. Open questions include robustness to distribution shift, joint training of monitors for complementarity, and whether the framework's insights transfer to non-code monitoring tasks.
