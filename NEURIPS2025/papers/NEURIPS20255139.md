## Problem

Multimodal LLMs produce redundant, repetitive, or erroneous reasoning steps because their token-level Markov generation relies on local dependencies, and existing reflection methods are too simplistic to generate meaningful corrective feedback. Critically, prior work shows that RL alone cannot induce genuinely new cognitive capabilities—it only activates decision-making within the fixed knowledge distribution established during pre-training—so external reflective interventions are needed to push reasoning beyond these inherent boundaries.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255139/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives an RGB image paired with a text question and produces a structured three-part response: an initial chain-of-thought solution, an explicit self-reflection segment enclosed in <reflect>...</reflect> tags that diagnoses errors or removes redundancy, and a second refined solution with the final answer. The total response length is softly constrained to approximately 2× the length of the initial solution (with a hard ceiling at 2.5×), ensuring reflections remain concise rather than verbose.

## Method

SRPO is a two-stage reflection-aware training framework. In Stage 1, a large MLLM (GPT-o4-mini) generates reflection content for ~10K curated samples (30% correct CoTs needing refinement, 70% incorrect CoTs needing correction), and the policy model (Qwen-2.5-VL-7B/32B) undergoes cold-start SFT to internalize the initial-response → reflection → corrected-answer pattern. In Stage 2, a GRPO-based RL loop optimizes a composite reward R_total = R_task + R_reflection, where R_task combines format and accuracy signals, and R_reflection comprises three terms: an effectiveness indicator I_eff that awards +0.5 for correcting a wrong answer, +0.25 for preserving a correct one, and −0.25 for flipping a correct answer to wrong; a formatting indicator I_ref (0.25) for proper <reflect> tags; and a differentiable exponential length penalty f_len that peaks at a target response length and decays smoothly toward zero, discouraging verbosity without hard cutoffs. This reward design explicitly incentivizes functionally useful, concise reflection rather than mere presence of reflective text, distinguishing SRPO from standard GRPO which relies only on sparse task-level accuracy supervision.

## Results

SRPO-7B achieves 75.8% on MathVista, 55.8% on MathVerse, and 42.3% on MMMU-Pro, outperforming all open-source 7B reasoning MLLMs and even surpassing GPT-o1 (73.9%) on MathVista. SRPO-32B reaches 78.5% on MathVista, 58.9% on MathVerse, 39.6% on MathVision, and 51.3% on MMMU-Pro, setting new state-of-the-art among open-source models. On cross-disciplinary MMK12, SRPO-32B scores 77.5 (Math), 64.2 (Physics), 77.5 (Chemistry), and 79.2 (Biology), exceeding MM-Eureka-32B by 2.9–3.4 points across disciplines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SRPO-7B outperforms the strongest open-source 7B reasoning baselines—VL-Rethinker-7B (74.9), Vision-R1-7B (73.5), and MM-Eureka-7B (73.0)—by 0.9–2.8 points on MathVista, and leads by 1.6–5.5 points on MathVerse. Notably, SRPO-7B surpasses GPT-o1 (73.9%) on MathVista by 1.9 points despite being a 7B open-source model. At the 32B scale, SRPO-32B beats MM-Eureka-32B by 3.7 points on MathVista (78.5 vs. 74.8) and 2.4 points on MathVerse (58.9 vs. 56.5), while also exceeding Qwen-2.5-VL-32B by 3.8 points on MathVista. The only model that outperforms SRPO-32B on MathVista is the proprietary Seed1.5-VL-T (85.6 vs. 78.5), though SRPO-32B remains competitive on MathVerse (58.9 vs. 57.0 for GPT-o1) and MMMU-Pro (51.3 vs. 62.4 for GPT-o1, where the gap is larger). On cross-disciplinary MMK12, SRPO-7B exceeds MM-Eureka-7B by 4.1 points on Physics (60.6 vs. 56.2) and SRPO-32B surpasses MM-Eureka-32B by 2.2–3.4 points across all four disciplines.

## Relation to Prior Work

SRPO builds directly on the GRPO algorithm from DeepSeek-R1, extending it to multimodal LLMs with a reflection-aware reward function that decomposes into format, effectiveness, and brevity components. It departs from prior multimodal RL methods (LMM-R1, Vision-R1, MM-Eureka, VL-Rethinker) by explicitly integrating self-reflection into both the SFT cold-start stage (using GPT-o4-mini as a reflection teacher) and the RL stage, rather than treating reflection as an afterthought or omitting it entirely. The two-stage design—distilling reflective knowledge via SFT before incentivizing it via RL—addresses the observation that RL alone cannot expand a model's cognitive boundaries established during pre-training, a limitation highlighted by recent analyses of RL-based reasoning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides practical evidence that explicit reflection training can push small open-source MLLMs (7B) to competitive or superior performance against much larger proprietary reasoning models on mathematical and cross-disciplinary benchmarks, which is practically significant for resource-constrained deployments. The ablation showing that reflection-aware RL is essential (removing it drops average performance by ~7 points) while reflection SFT alone is insufficient clarifies the complementary roles of the two stages. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond Qwen-2.5-VL architectures, whether the GPT-o4-mini teacher dependency limits scalability, and whether the reflection mechanism transfers to non-mathematical or open-ended reasoning tasks. The single-seed evaluation without error bars limits confidence in the magnitude of reported gains.
