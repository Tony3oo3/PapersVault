## Problem

Training parameterized quantum circuits (PQCs) on NISQ hardware is severely degraded by Pauli gate noise, yet existing mitigation strategies (ZNE, PEC, sparse Pauli-Lindblad methods) are either task-agnostic, require expensive and static noise pre-characterization, or assume fixed noise profiles. No prior method jointly learns the circuit parameters and the inverse noise channel in a task-specific, adaptive manner during the optimization loop, leaving a gap between noise mitigation and variational training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250478/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes quantum states (density matrices) prepared by amplitude-encoding PCA-reduced 28×28 MNIST or Fashion-MNIST images into 6-qubit registers, paired with binary labels (−1/+1). It produces a classification prediction via Z-basis measurement of the final state, along with two sets of learned parameters: the variational PQC angles θ and the inverse noise coefficients σ_{l,k} for each gate layer. The inverse noise channel is non-physical and is realized at runtime through classical Bernoulli sampling of Pauli corrections, making the pipeline a quantum-classical hybrid rather than a purely quantum circuit.

## Method

The core contribution is a joint proximal-SGD framework that simultaneously optimizes PQC parameters θ and inverse noise parameters σ within a single training loop, using a Pauli-Lindblad inverse channel whose coefficients are learned rather than pre-estimated. A key technical result is a parameter-shift-like rule for the gradient of the loss with respect to each σ_{j,q}, reducing it to a difference of two trace evaluations involving a Pauli conjugation operator, which avoids the exponential cost of full state tomography. A universal quantum-classical estimation algorithm then approximates all required trace terms on noisy hardware by sampling Bernoulli-distributed Pauli corrections (the PEC mechanism) and applying a classical sign multiplier, eliminating the need for explicit noise-channel tomography. Proximal updates with indicator-function constraints keep σ ≥ 0 and θ within bounded intervals, and a probabilistic coordinate-subsampling strategy (each direction selected with probability 1/p) controls the per-iteration measurement cost while preserving convergence to a stationary point.

## Results

On the MNIST binary classification task (digits 3 vs. 6) with a 6-qubit hardware-efficient ansatz under Pauli noise, the proposed integrated learning framework achieves 86.43±3.14% test accuracy, outperforming the noisy PQC baseline (79.69±3.70%) and the Van Den Berg et al. mitigation approach (85.37±3.44%), while the noiseless PQC upper bound is 94.13±0.31%. Under dynamic noise (error rates changed mid-training), the method adapts online and achieves lower MSE than the static pre-estimated mitigation. On Fashion-MNIST (Pullover vs. Shirt), trends mirror MNIST, and comparisons with PEC and ZNE in the appendix confirm the proposed method attains the best final metric under the same dynamic-noise protocol.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three baselines: (1) a noisy PQC without mitigation, (2) Van Den Berg et al.'s sparse Pauli-Lindblad PEC approach with pre-estimated and fixed noise parameters, and (3) standard PEC and ZNE protocols (reported in the appendix). Under static noise, Van Den Berg et al. initially achieves lower training loss due to its pre-calibrated inverse noise model, but the proposed method surpasses it as training progresses, ultimately reaching 86.43% vs. 85.37% accuracy. Under dynamic noise (error rates shifted mid-training), the proposed method's adaptive inverse noise parameters allow it to track the changing noise profile and achieve lower MSE than Van Den Berg et al.'s static model, which cannot recalibrate. Against PEC and ZNE under the same dynamic-noise protocol, the proposed framework adapts online and attains the best final metric (lower loss/MSE), confirming that joint learning of circuit and noise parameters provides a practical advantage over fixed post-hoc mitigation strategies.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the sparse Pauli-Lindblad noise model of Van Den Berg et al. [28] and the Probabilistic Error Cancellation (PEC) protocol of Temme et al. [26], but departs from both by treating the inverse noise channel parameters as learnable variables optimized jointly with the PQC parameters via proximal SGD, rather than pre-estimating them via random benchmarking or tomography. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of a parameter-shift-like gradient rule for the inverse noise parameters (Eq. 10), which avoids the exponential cost of full Pauli decomposition and enables scalable joint optimization. This positions the work as an adaptive, task-specific extension of static noise mitigation: the inverse noise model is no longer a fixed correction applied post-hoc but a co-learned component that can track dynamic noise changes during training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The joint learning framework eliminates the need for expensive and potentially stale noise pre-characterization, which is a practical bottleneck on NISQ hardware where noise profiles drift over time. The convergence guarantees (Theorem 3.2) and sample complexity bounds (Theorem 3.1) provide theoretical grounding that prior heuristic mitigation approaches lack. However, the experimental validation is limited to 6-qubit simulated circuits on binary classification tasks (MNIST digits 3/6, Fashion-MNIST Pullover/Shirt), and the improvement over the closest baseline (Van Den Berg et al.) is modest (~1% accuracy). Open questions include scalability beyond c-local gates with c = O(log n), applicability to general (non-Pauli) measurement schemes, and whether the adaptive advantage persists on real hardware with correlated or non-Markovian noise.
