## Problem

Many mainstream DRL algorithms (DQN, SAC, DDPG) lack robustness to time discretization in stochastic environments: their performance degrades as the time step δt changes, and the standard mean-squared temporal difference error (MSTDE) used for policy evaluation provably cannot converge to the true value function in stochastic continuous-time settings because the martingale component of the value process makes MSTDE non-zero even for the exact solution. This paper asks whether these algorithms collapse as δt→0, and proposes a theoretically grounded off-policy algorithm with explicit hyperparameter scaling laws that remain valid across discretization scales.

## Input / Output

The agent observes a continuous state x_t ∈ ℝ^n and time t, and outputs a stochastic action u_t drawn from a Gaussian policy π_φ(·|t, x_t) together with a value function estimate J^θ(t, x_t). The environment evolves via a stochastic differential equation driven by Brownian motion, so transitions are inherently stochastic rather than deterministic. A distinctive constraint is that all hyperparameters—learning rates, entropy temperature λ, reward magnitude, and discount factor—must be rescaled proportionally to δt (e.g., η_{δt} = α·δt, λ_{δt} = λ·δt, γ_{δt} = e^{−βδt}) to preserve convergence of parameter trajectories as the discretization changes.

## Method

The central technical contribution replaces the standard MSTDE loss with a martingale orthogonality condition: the value function J^θ is trained so that the discounted cumulative-reward process is a martingale, enforced by requiring E[∫ ξ_t dM_t] = 0 for all square-integrable test functions ξ_t, where the martingale difference is δM = J^θ_target(t+δt, x′) − J^θ(t, x) + r·δt − λ·ln π·δt − β·J^θ·δt. This δM is normalized by δt to prevent vanishing gradients, and the policy gradient is derived via the Feynman-Kac formula, extending Jia and Zhou's continuous-time actor-critic theory to both single-agent and multi-agent settings. The multi-agent extension (CMASAC) adopts a CTDE framework with alternating per-agent updates, and the off-policy design allows reuse of behavior-policy trajectories without importance-sampling weights because all policies share the same Gaussian action-space parameterization. Theoretical scaling laws (Theorem 5) prove that learning rates must scale as δt^1 for discrete parameter trajectories to converge to continuous-time limits, while β>1 freezes parameters and β<1 causes divergence.

## Results

On the VMAS Navigation task, CMASAC achieves a median score of 0.93 at δt=0.01, degrading only 3% from its 0.96 at δt=0.1. In contrast, MASAC drops from 0.89 to 0.51 (−43%), MAPPO from 0.97 to 0.80 (−18%), and IQL from 0.99 to 0.88 (−11%). On the Sampling task, CMASAC actually improves from 0.84 to 0.92 as δt decreases, while MASAC collapses from 0.66 to 0.43. These results confirm that the martingale-based policy evaluation and hyperparameter scaling laws yield δt-robust performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's central claim is robustness to time discretization rather than absolute performance at a fixed δt. At the challenging δt=0.01 setting on Navigation, CMASAC (0.93) outperforms all four baselines: QMIX (0.86), MAPPO (0.80), MADDPG (0.78), and IQL (0.88). The most dramatic degradation is observed in MASAC (0.89→0.51), a discrete-time actor-critic method, confirming the theoretical prediction that TD-based methods fail in continuous-time stochastic settings. IQL, while the strongest baseline at δt=0.1 (0.99), still degrades by 11% at δt=0.01, whereas CMASAC's drop is only 3%. MADDPG shows the smallest relative degradation among baselines (−2%) but remains well below CMASAC at both discretization levels. On the Sampling task, CMASAC (0.92) also surpasses MADDPG (0.91) and dramatically outperforms MASAC (0.43) at δt=0.01.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the continuous-time reinforcement learning line initiated by Doya (2000) and Tallec et al. (2019), which studied deterministic environments and proved Q-learning's incompatibility with continuous time, and the stochastic continuous-time theory of Jia and Zhou (2022a, 2022b), which established martingale-based policy evaluation and gradient theorems but only for on-policy settings. The key departure is the construction of a practical *off-policy* actor-critic algorithm (CSAC/CMASAC) grounded in martingale orthogonality conditions rather than temporal-difference error, combined with explicit hyperparameter scaling laws for stochastic environments. The multi-agent extension via CTDE with alternating updates is, to the authors' knowledge, the first finite-agent actor-critic algorithm designed for continuous-time stochastic settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a fundamental theoretical obstruction—standard TD-based methods (DQN, SAC, DDPG) cannot converge to true value functions in stochastic continuous-time environments because the MSTDE of the true value function equals the quadratic variation of the associated martingale, which is non-zero. This invalidates a large class of widely-used algorithms in this setting and motivates the martingale-orthogonality-based alternative. However, empirical validation is limited to two VMAS tasks at two discretization levels, and the method is restricted to continuous state-action spaces; broader validation on diverse benchmarks, scalability to many agents, and applicability to discrete-valued action spaces remain open questions.
