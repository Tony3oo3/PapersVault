## Problem

End-to-end video-based vision-language models (V-VLMs) struggle with spatial reasoning, causal inference, and fine-grained scene understanding when analyzing front-view dash-cam videos, often misinterpreting critical cues like lane-cutting maneuvers or object orientations. Because vision is frequently the sole available modality in post-hoc driving analysis (no LiDAR, GPS, or CAN bus data), existing approaches lack the structured inductive biases needed for reliable, interpretable reasoning about driving events without domain-specific fine-tuning.

## Input / Output

The method takes a front-view dash-cam video (T frames of H×W×3 RGB) and a user query as input. The video is first undistorted via estimated camera intrinsics and distortion coefficients, then processed through a suite of pretrained vision modules whose outputs are serialized into a hierarchical JSON structure with video-level fields (peer V-VLM response, scene context, ego-vehicle motion/turn state, video caption) and per-frame fields (object ID, bounding box, class, distance in meters, lane index, 3D yaw angle, color attributes). The final LLM produces a grounded reasoning response that can take the form of a multiple-choice selection, an open-ended causal explanation, or a binary accident-occurrence prediction.

## Method

iFinder is a modular, training-free pipeline that decouples perception from LLM reasoning by extracting driving-domain cues through eight sequential steps: frame undistortion (GeoCalib + OpenCV), scene understanding (InternVL + VideoLLaMA2), ego-vehicle state estimation (DROID-SLAM pose → turn/motion classification), 2D detection and tracking (OWL-V2 + ByteTracker), lane assignment (OMR lane markings → geometric section mapping), metric distance estimation (Metric3D depth + SAM segmentation), object attribute extraction (InternVL on cropped boxes), and 3D orientation estimation (CenterTrack yaw + Hungarian matching to 2D detections). These cues are organized into a hierarchical JSON data structure and fed to GPT-4o-mini via a three-block prompt (Key Explanation disambiguating the JSON schema, Step Instructions decomposing the reasoning task, and Peer Instruction flagging the peer V-VLM's answer as potentially unreliable). A peer V-VLM (default VideoLLaMA2) generates an initial hypothesis from the raw video, which the LLM then refines or corrects using the structured evidence. Ablations reveal that object orientation (yaw) and global environmental context are the most critical grounding signals, with removing orientation causing the largest accuracy drop (~4.5%) on MM-AU.

## Results

On the MM-AU accident reasoning benchmark, iFinder achieves 63.39% multiple-choice accuracy, outperforming the best generalist V-VLM (VideoLLaMA2 at 50.95%) by 12.4% and the driving-specialized DriveMM (24.22%) by 39.2%. On SUTD-TrafficQA, iFinder reaches 50.93% accuracy, leading all baselines across all six reasoning categories. For open-ended VQA on LingoQA, iFinder attains 44.20% Lingo-Judge accuracy (vs. 41.20% for VideoChat2 and 36.00% for VideoLLaMA2), and on Nexar accident occurrence prediction it achieves 62.0% accuracy with balanced precision (0.64) and recall (0.54), avoiding the degenerate always-predict-accident behavior seen in VideoLLaMA2 and WiseAD.

## Baselines & Comparisons

iFinder outperforms all five baselines on every benchmark where they are jointly evaluated. Against the strongest generalist, VideoLLaMA2 [14], iFinder gains +12.4% on MM-AU (63.39 vs. 50.95), +3.4% on SUTD (50.93 vs. 47.51), +8.2% Lingo-Judge on LingoQA (44.20 vs. 36.00), and +12.0% accuracy on Nexar (62.0 vs. 50.0). VideoChat2 [21] is the closest competitor on LingoQA (41.20 Lingo-J) and Nexar (58.0% acc), but iFinder still leads by 3.0 and 4.0 points respectively. Video-LLaVA [15] is consistently the weakest generalist, trailing iFinder by 19.8% on MM-AU and 23.2% on LingoQA. The driving-specialized DriveMM [16] suffers a dramatic 39.2-point gap on MM-AU (24.22 vs. 63.39), underscoring that domain-specific V-VLMs trained for in-moment driving do not transfer well to post-hoc video analysis. WiseAD [18] is the weakest driving-specialized baseline, scoring only 13.40 Lingo-J on LingoQA and exhibiting the same degenerate always-accident prediction on Nexar as VideoLLaMA2 (100% recall, 50% precision).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant end-to-end V-VLM paradigm (VideoLLaMA2, VideoChat2, DriveMM, WiseAD) by decoupling perception from reasoning: rather than feeding raw video frames to a single vision-language model, iFinder composes a pipeline of off-the-shelf pretrained vision modules (OWL-V2, ByteTracker, OMR, Metric3D, SAM, CenterTrack, DROID-SLAM) into a hierarchical JSON structure that grounds a general-purpose LLM (GPT-4o-mini) via a three-block prompt. Conceptually, it repurposes the structured-prompting and chain-of-thought literature for a domain-specific perception-to-reasoning bridge, and borrows the multi-sensor fusion philosophy from autonomous driving stacks while restricting itself to monocular video. The paper does not build on a single prior architecture but synthesizes existing components into a novel training-free pipeline specifically targeting post-hoc dash-cam analysis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The modular, training-free design makes iFinder practically attractive for fleet-level post-hoc analysis where retraining is infeasible and interpretability is required for liability or safety audits. The ablation finding that object orientation and global scene context contribute more to reasoning accuracy than distance or lane location challenges the intuitive assumption that proximity is the dominant cue in collision reasoning. Key limitations remain: the system cannot reason about driver intent, social norms, or ambiguous physical contact (near-miss vs. collision), and its reliance on a chain of pretrained modules introduces cumulative error risk. Open questions include whether this structured-grounding paradigm generalizes to multi-view or real-time settings, and whether the LLM's reasoning quality scales with the fidelity of the perception pipeline.
