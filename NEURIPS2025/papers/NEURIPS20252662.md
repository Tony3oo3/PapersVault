## Problem

In privileged learning, the deployment policy (DP) must act on partial observations while the privileged policy (PP) trains with full privileged information, causing behavioral divergence that degrades deployment performance. Existing reward-regularization approaches penalize the PP in high-divergence regions but cannot replicate the DP's optimal behavior because the PP has no access to the DP's information-gathering progress—e.g., whether the DP has already inferred the hidden target location. This paper addresses the gap by making the PP explicitly uncertainty-sensitive so it can adapt its behavior to the DP's current information state.

## Input / Output

The observation encoder consumes common observations (robot pose, joint angles, velocities) and optional target observations (depth images at low resolution or scandot terrain heights) to output a Gaussian distribution N(μ_p, σ_p²) over the privileged observation. The privileged policy (an MLP) takes the common observation, the (possibly blurred) privileged observation, and the scalar uncertainty σ_p as joint input, producing a continuous or discrete robot action. During deployment, the DP is the composition of the encoder and PP, where the true privileged observation is replaced by the encoder's predicted mean μ_p, and σ_p serves as a real-time confidence signal that modulates the PP's behavior.

## Method

USPL introduces three coupled mechanisms to reduce PP–DP behavioral divergence. First, the observation encoder (CNN-Transformer for image tasks, Transformer for state-only tasks) is trained via maximum likelihood to predict the privileged observation distribution, and its standard deviation σ_p is fed as an explicit input to the PP, enabling the policy to sense how much information the DP has yet to gather. Second, a multiplicative reward transformation scales the environment reward by a coefficient that decreases with σ_p, reducing the penalty for exploratory actions under high uncertainty and amplifying task-reward signal under low uncertainty. Third, privileged observation blurring injects noise (Gaussian or uniform for continuous observations; random replacement for discrete ones) proportional to σ_p, forcing the PP to learn robust, information-gathering behaviors rather than relying on exact privileged values. A theoretical bound (Theorem 1) shows the average TV distance between PP and DP state-visit distributions is governed by the product of prediction error ε^h and a Lipschitz constant L, and USPL's two training modifications target these two factors respectively. The PP is optimized with PPO using a cosine-scheduled warm-up that gradually introduces blurring and reward transformation, while the encoder is updated with a mix of on-policy and off-policy (including mismatched-observation) data to prevent shortcut learning.

## Results

USPL achieves a 97.8% average deployment success rate across six non-visual tasks (Signpost Nav, Square Maze, Lateral Choice, Midpoint Choice, Biased Quadrotor, Blind-Mass Stack), with over 95% on every individual task. The gap between USPL's privileged policy (99.8%) and deployment policy (97.8%) is only 2.0 percentage points, whereas RMA's gap is 64.3 points (99.7% PP vs. 35.4% DP). On three image-based tasks (Stairway Search, Signpost Nav with depth, Midpoint Choice with depth), USPL also exceeds 95% success rate, significantly outperforming all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

USPL decisively outperforms all four baselines across every task. Against RMA [13] (the standard privileged-learning baseline), USPL improves average deployment success from 35.4% to 97.8% (+62.4 pp), and critically narrows the PP-to-DP gap from 64.3 pp to just 2.0 pp. Against PrivRecons [39] (the strongest baseline, combining RL with supervised encoder training), USPL leads by 51.0 pp on average (97.8% vs. 46.8%), with the largest margin on Biased Quadrotor (96.7% vs. 2.2%). Against RewReg [20] (reward-penalty regularization on the PP), USPL gains 57.5 pp on average (97.8% vs. 40.3%); RewReg outperforms RMA in 5 of 6 tasks but still falls far short of USPL because it lacks explicit uncertainty awareness. Against RESeL-PPO [25] (pure RL with Transformer memory), USPL achieves 97.8% vs. 17.7%, confirming that memory-augmented RL is highly inefficient on these long-horizon, information-gathering tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

USPL extends the privileged learning framework introduced by RMA (Kumar et al., 2021), which pairs an observation encoder with a privileged policy to handle partially observable robotic control. It departs from the reward-regularization line (Messikommer et al., 2025; RewReg) that penalizes the PP in high-divergence regions but leaves the PP blind to the DP's information-gathering progress. The key conceptual departure is making the PP explicitly uncertainty-sensitive: the encoder's predicted standard deviation is fed as an additional input to the policy, and a multiplicative reward transformation (rather than an additive penalty) combined with uncertainty-scaled privileged blurring trains the PP to switch between exploratory and exploitative behaviors. The training infrastructure builds on RESeL's stabilized PPO (Luo et al., 2024).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The behavioral divergence between privileged and deployment policies is a persistent bottleneck in legged robotics and manipulation, where privileged learning is the dominant training paradigm. USPL's >95% deployment success across nine diverse tasks (quadruped navigation, quadrotor hovering, robotic stacking) represents a large practical improvement over the 35–47% average of prior methods, and the Lipschitz-based divergence bound (Theorem 1) gives a clean theoretical decomposition into prediction error and policy sensitivity. Two limitations remain open: the method currently handles only low-dimensional privileged observations (extending to high-dimensional or time-varying ones would require a latent privileged encoder), and the DP's observation encoder is still needed during PP training, which limits simulation speed for image-based tasks. A proxy encoder that predicts uncertainty directly from privileged history is suggested as a future direction.
