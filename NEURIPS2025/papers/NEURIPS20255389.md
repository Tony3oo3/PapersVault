## Problem

Despite the widespread use of physics-informed neural networks (PINNs) for solving PDEs, no prior work has established a necessary and sufficient condition for their consistency—i.e., convergence of the population PINN loss to zero. The paper asks: for a second-order elliptic equation with Dirichlet boundary conditions, under exactly what condition does a sufficiently wide PINN converge to the true PDE solution, and can it provably fail?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255389/cropped_page55_idx6.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a second-order elliptic operator L in divergence form on a bounded domain Ω, a bounded measurable source term f, and finite noisy samples from the boundary (N_u points) and interior (N_f points, with N_u ≤ N_f). The output is a theoretical characterization: the PINN is consistent if and only if the true solution u* belongs to the closure of the NTK's RKHS under the PINN metric (which combines boundary L² and PDE-residual L² norms). The paper also provides non-asymptotic loss bounds of order O(N_u^{-(1-3α)/2}√(log N_u) + N_u^{-α}) under the stronger assumption u* ∈ H.

## Method

The core technique is to interpret infinite-width PINN training as a physics-informed kernel gradient flow in the RKHS of the neural tangent kernel, then prove that the population loss converges to zero if and only if the PDE solution lies in the RKHS closure under the PINN metric. The key technical difficulty is that this is an ill-posed convex optimization: the minimizer u* may not belong to the RKHS itself, so the RKHS norm of the gradient flow diverges while the loss still converges to its infimum. The authors prove this via a diagonalization of the population gradient flow into a self-adjoint compact operator (S+T) on the RKHS, combined with a novel argument showing gradient norm vanishing even without a minimizer in the model space. The NTK framework then transfers the kernel result to finite-width networks via uniform convergence of the NNK to the NTK (Theorem 5.4) and ODE stability (Lemma 5.6), yielding the necessary and sufficient consistency condition for PINNs (Theorem 5.8).

## Results

The main theoretical result (Theorem 3.7) establishes that the physics-informed kernel gradient flow is consistent for the second-order elliptic equation with Dirichlet boundary conditions if and only if the PDE solution u* belongs to the closure H̄ of the RKHS under the PINN metric. Under the stronger assumption u* ∈ H, a non-asymptotic bound is obtained: L(û_T) = O(N_u^{-(1-3α)/2}√(log N_u) + N_u^{-α}) at stopping time T = Θ(N_u^α) for α ∈ (0, 1/3). A notable pathological example (Poisson equation on S¹\{p}) demonstrates that PINN can be inconsistent, with the infimum PINN loss bounded below by 4 > 0 and the gradient flow estimator converging to zero rather than the true solution.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no experimental baselines or quantitative benchmark comparisons. The comparison with prior work is structural: prior works on PINN convergence [11, 22, 30, 34] establish population loss convergence but do not analyze training dynamics or use NTK; NTK-based PINN analyses [17, 40] study training dynamics and spectral bias but do not prove population loss convergence; empirical loss convergence results [5, 18, 26, 42] handle the finite-sample loss but not the population-level consistency question. This paper is the first to simultaneously address all four aspects—training dynamics, NTK framework, empirical loss convergence, and population loss consistency—providing a necessary and sufficient condition (u* ∈ H̄) that no prior work had established. The paper also provides the first convergence result for an ill-posed convex optimization problem on infinite-dimensional Hilbert space (Lemma 4.2), which is a technical contribution not present in any of the compared works.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of NTK theory (Jacot et al., 2018) and PINN analysis, extending the kernel gradient flow framework (Yao et al., 2007) to the physics-informed setting with boundary conditions. It departs from prior PINN convergence work (Doum'mche, Jiao, Mishra, Shin) that studied generalization of empirical minimizers without analyzing training dynamics, and from NTK-based PINN studies (Wang et al., 2022; Bonfanti et al., 2024) that characterized spectral bias or empirical loss convergence but not population loss consistency. The key conceptual departure is treating the PINN optimization as an ill-posed convex problem on an infinite-dimensional Hilbert space where the minimizer may lie outside the RKHS, yielding the first convergence result in this setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The necessary-and-sufficient consistency condition (u* ∈ H̄, the closure of the RKHS under the PINN metric) provides the first complete characterization of when PINNs can succeed or fundamentally fail, and the pathological example on S¹\{p} demonstrates that even smooth-coefficient PDEs can be unsolvable by PINNs. However, practical applicability is limited because computing NTK eigenspaces remains an open problem, so Theorem 5.8 cannot yet be verified for general network architectures. Open questions include extending the analysis to narrow networks, establishing convergence rates under weaker source-capacity assumptions, and characterizing NTK eigenspaces for common activation functions beyond the sphere/torus setting.
