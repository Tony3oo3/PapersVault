## Problem

Existing LLM routing methods rely on full-feedback datasets where every query is evaluated by all candidate models, which is prohibitively expensive to obtain and maintain. Moreover, they adopt a decoupled strategy—separately predicting accuracy and cost before selecting a model—causing errors to compound. This paper addresses both limitations by learning routing policies directly from observational data (recording only the deployed model's outcome) and by optimizing decision-making regret end-to-end rather than intermediate metric predictions.

## Input / Output

The router consumes a fixed-length query embedding (768-dim from BERT-base-uncased or 2048-dim from Llama-3.2-1B, obtained via mean-pooling of final hidden states) and, in the heterogeneous-preference setting, a continuous cost sensitivity parameter λ ≥ 0. It outputs a single LLM selection from a pool of T models (11 in RouterBench, 13 in SPROUT). In the interval-conditioned variant, λ can take any non-negative real value at inference time, with the architecture interpolating between pre-trained endpoint models to handle unseen cost preferences without retraining.

## Method

The framework estimates counterfactual utility for all candidate models from observational data using a doubly robust estimator that combines per-treatment outcome regression with propensity-score weighting, correcting for treatment selection bias. Rather than predicting metrics and then selecting, it directly minimizes expected regret via two differentiable surrogates: (1) a classification-based upper bound derived from L-Lipschitz continuity of utility over the probability simplex and Pinsker's inequality, reducing policy learning to multiclass prediction of the optimal treatment; and (2) a softmax-weighted regret objective that treats the network's softmax output as a distribution over treatments and provably concentrates all mass on the optimal treatment at convergence. For heterogeneous cost preferences, an interval-conditioned architecture exploits the affine structure y = a − λc to interpolate between two endpoint routers per interval, with theoretical guarantees that the optimal policy is piecewise constant in λ and that two endpoint models suffice for exact reconstruction within each interval.

## Results

On SPROUT with BERT embeddings, RM-Softmax achieves the highest utility among all non-oracle methods across all cost sensitivities, reaching 84.97 at λ=0 and 56.95 at λ=1000, within ~1 point of the Full-Feedback oracle (85.99 and 58.83 respectively). The AUC of the accuracy-cost trade-off curve confirms this advantage: RM-Softmax attains 0.3320 on SPROUT-BERT and 0.2286 on RouterBench-BERT, substantially exceeding the next-best baselines (Causal-CARROT kNN at 0.1642 and 0.1441) and the decoupled Baseline (0.0255 and 0.0890). RM-Interval matches RM-Softmax at endpoint λ values while generalizing to held-out intermediate λ values (e.g., 77.60 at λ=100 on SPROUT-BERT), validating the interval-conditioned architecture.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's RM-Softmax and RM-Interval methods consistently outperform all non-oracle baselines across both SPROUT and RouterBench with both BERT and LLaMA-3.2-1B embeddings. Against the decoupled Baseline (CARROT without causal correction), the proposed methods gain approximately 18–19 utility points at λ=0 on SPROUT-BERT (84.97 vs. 66.88), demonstrating that ignoring treatment bias is severely detrimental. Among causal baselines, RM-Softmax edges out Causal-CARROT(kNN) by ~0.45 points at λ=0 (84.97 vs. 84.52) and CF-Regression by ~1.4 points (84.97 vs. 83.54), with the gap widening at higher λ values (e.g., 64.03 vs. 61.77 at λ=600). The Causal-CARROT(EmbedNet) variant degrades dramatically at higher λ (34.69 at λ=1000 vs. 56.95 for RM-Softmax), suggesting parametric outcome models are fragile under strong cost penalties. In the AUC summary (Table 7), RM-Softmax achieves 0.3320 on SPROUT-BERT, nearly double the Full-Feedback oracle's 0.1727 and over 13× the Baseline's 0.0255, indicating the end-to-end regret objective traces a far superior accuracy-cost frontier. RM-Interval matches RM-Softmax at trained λ endpoints while generalizing to held-out values (e.g., 77.60 at λ=100), confirming the interval-conditioned architecture's interpolation capability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant decoupled paradigm in LLM routing (CARROT, RouteLLM, RoRF), where accuracy and cost are predicted separately and a model is selected by argmax over estimated utility. It builds on the doubly robust estimator from causal inference (Dudik et al., 2011) to handle treatment bias in observational data, and on regret-minimization objectives from causal classification (Fernandez-Loría and Provost, 2022) to unify prediction and decision into a single end-to-end objective. The interval-conditioned architecture for heterogeneous cost preferences is a novel contribution that exploits the affine structure of the utility function, requiring only two endpoint models per interval rather than one per λ value.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The shift from full-feedback to observational data addresses a critical practical bottleneck: exhaustively querying all candidate LLMs for every query is prohibitively expensive and hard to maintain as models evolve. The end-to-end regret formulation avoids compounding prediction errors inherent in decoupled approaches, and the interval-conditioned design offers a scalable path to serving diverse user cost preferences without maintaining separate routers. Key limitations include the affine utility assumption (which precludes hard constraints or non-linear trade-offs), the fact that observational data is simulated rather than drawn from real deployments, and evaluation on only two benchmarks. Open questions include extending to online/adaptive routing, multi-turn agentic settings, and incorporating additional metrics beyond accuracy and cost.
