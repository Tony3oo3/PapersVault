## Problem

Prior work attributed the locality (limited spatial sensitivity) observed in trained diffusion models to inductive biases of convolutional architectures, but could not predict the degree or shape of locality from first principles. This paper asks whether locality is instead a statistical property of the training data, and if so, whether it can be derived analytically without measuring it from a trained network.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253180/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The analytical model takes a noisy image x_t at timestep t, the full training dataset, and the precomputed data covariance matrix. It outputs a denoised image x_0 via a 10-step DDIM sampling loop, where each denoising step computes a softmax-weighted average over the training set using per-pixel binary masks derived from the Wiener filter. The masks are pixel-specific (not shared across the image) and can be nonlocal or non-equivariant, unlike the square patches used in prior analytical models. Sensitivity fields (input-output Jacobians) are also reported as a diagnostic output to compare against trained networks.

## Method

The core insight is that the sensitivity field of a trained diffusion model approximates the projection onto high-SNR principal components of the data covariance, i.e., the Wiener filter W_t = (1/√α_t) U diag(λ_i²/(λ_i²+σ_t²)) U^T. The method computes the SVD of the data covariance, constructs W_t at each timestep, binarizes each row with a threshold τ to obtain a per-pixel binary mask, and plugs these masks into the optimal denoiser formula so that softmax distances are computed only over the masked (high-SNR) coordinates. A key theoretical result (Proposition A.9) shows that for diagonal masking operators, only the binary support of the mask affects the optimal denoiser, not the actual weight values, which justifies the binarization step. This eliminates the need to heuristically fit patch sizes from a trained network and naturally produces nonlocal, location-dependent sensitivity patterns on specialized datasets like centered faces.

## Results

The proposed analytical model (using data-derived Wiener filter masks in the optimal denoiser) achieves the highest r² and lowest MSE against a trained DDPM across all five datasets. On CIFAR-10, it reaches r² = 0.589 (vs. 0.408 for the Wiener filter and 0.303 for Kamb & Ganguli), and on CelebA-HQ it reaches r² = 0.902 (vs. 0.818 and 0.831 respectively). The vanilla optimal denoiser performs worst, with negative r² on CIFAR-10 (-0.549) and AFHQv2 (-1.239), confirming its inability to model generalization. The upper bound (a second DDPM with different seed) achieves r² of 0.85–0.97, indicating the analytical model captures a substantial fraction of the trained model's behavior.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all three baselines on every dataset and both metrics (r² and MSE). Against the vanilla optimal denoiser (Scarvelis et al.), the gap is dramatic: the optimal denoiser achieves negative r² on CIFAR-10 (−0.549) and AFHQv2 (−1.239), while the proposed method reaches 0.589 and 0.759 respectively. Against the Wiener filter (linear denoiser), the proposed method consistently wins by 0.10–0.18 in r² (e.g., 0.589 vs. 0.408 on CIFAR-10; 0.902 vs. 0.818 on CelebA-HQ), with the advantage being largest on datasets with nonlocal covariance structures. Against Kamb & Ganguli's patch-based model, the proposed method wins on all datasets, with the most pronounced gap on CIFAR-10 (r² 0.589 vs. 0.303) and AFHQv2 (0.759 vs. 0.429); on CelebA-HQ the gap is smaller (0.902 vs. 0.831) because the patch-based model partially captures face structure. The paper also notes that Kamb & Ganguli's model qualitatively erases eyes and blurs facial features on CelebA-HQ due to its enforced square-patch locality, whereas the data-derived masks preserve these nonlocal correlations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly challenges the framework of Kamb and Ganguli [12] and Niedoba et al. [19], who attributed the locality and generalization of diffusion models to the inductive biases of convolutional architectures (shift equivariance and local receptive fields). By showing that both U-Nets and DiTs learn nearly identical sensitivity fields, and that arbitrary nonlocal patterns can be induced by editing data covariance, the authors reframe locality as an emergent property of dataset statistics rather than architectural design. The method itself is a modification of the closed-form optimal denoiser [25] and the patch-based analytical model of Kamb and Ganguli [12], replacing empirically-measured patch masks with analytically-computed binarized Wiener filter [31] projections derived from the data covariance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work resolves a central theoretical puzzle in diffusion modeling—why trained networks generalize rather than memorize—by showing the answer lies in data statistics, not architecture. The practical consequence is a more interpretable analytical model that eliminates the need to heuristically fit patch sizes from trained networks, and it performs best across five diverse datasets. Key limitations include the assumption of input-independent (constant) sensitivity fields, reliance on second-order statistics only, and evaluation on relatively small-resolution images; extending the analysis to non-linear regimes, higher-order statistics, and large-scale conditional generation remains open.
