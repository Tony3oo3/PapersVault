## Problem

A longstanding goal in safe reinforcement learning is to guarantee policy safety throughout the entire process, from learning to operation. Existing paradigms inherently fail at this: online safe RL risks executing unsafe actions during early training, while offline safe RL faces distribution mismatch when deploying learned policies in real environments. No prior method provides a high-probability safety guarantee spanning both the learning and operational phases simultaneously.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-collected offline dataset of n episodes (each a sequence of state-action-reward-safety-cost tuples of fixed horizon H) generated by a behavior policy, along with a safety threshold b ∈ [0,H]. It produces a single return-conditioned policy (a Constrained Decision Transformer) that maps a context (partial history of states, actions, rewards, and safety costs) to an action distribution, parameterized by a two-dimensional target return vector z = (R, G). The optimized target returns are the primary tunable output: by changing z, the same fixed policy adapts to different safety thresholds without retraining, and the GP-based optimizer selects z to maximize reward while keeping expected safety cost below b.

## Method

PLS operates in two phases. First, it trains a Constrained Decision Transformer (CDT) offline via return-conditioned supervised learning, yielding a policy π_z whose behavior is controlled by the target return vector z = (R, G). Second, it deploys this fixed policy and optimizes z online using Gaussian process (GP) Bayesian optimization over the two-dimensional target return space. The key theoretical justification (Theorem 1) shows that the gap between target and actual returns decomposes into an unbiased GP term scaled by H²/√n, a small bias, and an asymptotically negligible term, making GP modeling of the z → J(π_z) mapping principled. During deployment, PLS alternates between safe exploration (pessimistically constraining the GP upper bound on safety cost to ≤ b while maximizing uncertainty to expand the feasible set) and reward maximization (optimistically selecting z via the GP upper confidence bound on reward within the safe set). Theorems 2 and 3 guarantee that the safety constraint holds with probability ≥ 1−Δ at every iteration and that the final target returns are near-optimal after a bounded number of GP observations, requiring far fewer online interactions than conventional online safe RL since only a 2D vector is optimized.

## Results

On 9 continuous robot locomotion tasks (Bullet-Safety-Gym and Safety-Gymnasium) with safety threshold b=20, PLS is the only method that satisfies the safety constraint (normalized cost ≤ 1) in every single task, while achieving the highest reward among all safe methods in 7 out of 9 tasks. For example, on Ant-Run PLS achieves reward 0.78 with cost 0.77, matching the best unsafe method (BCQ-Lag, reward 0.79) while remaining safe; on Hopper-Velocity PLS reaches reward 0.83 with cost 0.42, outperforming all safe baselines. Every baseline violates the safety constraint in at least one task, with some (e.g., BCQ-Lag on HalfCheetah-Velocity, cost 27.00) exceeding the threshold by orders of magnitude.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PLS is the only method that remains safe (normalized cost ≤ 1) across all 9 tasks, while every baseline violates the constraint in at least one task. Among the baselines that are safe in a given task, PLS consistently achieves equal or higher reward: e.g., on Hopper-Velocity PLS (0.83) beats CDT (0.68) and COptiDICE (0.14); on Ant-Run PLS (0.78) matches COptiDICE (0.63) and CDT (0.72) while staying safe. The high-reward baselines (BCQ-Lag, BEAR-Lag, CCAC) frequently produce extreme cost violations (e.g., BCQ-Lag reaches cost 27.00 on HalfCheetah-Velocity, CCAC reaches 7.62 on Drone-Run), making them impractical for safety-critical deployment. CPQ and COptiDICE are often safe but overly conservative, yielding near-zero rewards on several tasks (e.g., CPQ scores 0.01 on Ant-Run and -1.01 on Ant-Velocity). CDT, the direct predecessor of PLS's offline component, is safe on some tasks but violates constraints on others (e.g., cost 2.23 on Ant-Circle, 1.12 on Drone-Circle), confirming the misalignment between target and actual returns that PLS's GP-based optimization corrects.

## Relation to Prior Work

PLS sits at the intersection of offline safe RL (specifically the Constrained Decision Transformer, CDT) and safe Bayesian optimization with Gaussian processes (Sui et al.). It departs from both online safe RL paradigms (which cannot guarantee safety during the learning phase) and pure offline safe RL (which cannot safely deploy due to distribution mismatch) by separating the problem into an offline policy-learning stage and a cautious online target-return optimization stage. The key theoretical departure is Theorem 1, which characterizes the gap between target and actual returns of a CDT policy as a Gaussian process, thereby justifying GP-based Bayesian optimization of the two-dimensional target return vector (R, G) — a connection not previously established in the safe RL literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PLS achieves the 'longstanding goal' of end-to-end safety from learning to operation with high-probability guarantees, and empirically is the only method satisfying the safety constraint across all nine benchmark tasks. However, the paper acknowledges that near-optimal target returns do not directly imply a near-optimal policy, the policy network is frozen after offline training (no online adaptation), and the theoretical assumptions (near-deterministic transitions, soft realizability) may not hold in highly stochastic real-world settings. Extending PLS to an offline-to-online regime where the policy continually improves while preserving safety, and validating the approach in more complex and stochastic environments, are the most pressing open questions.
