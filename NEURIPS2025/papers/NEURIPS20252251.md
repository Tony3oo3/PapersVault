## Problem

Existing single-positive multi-label (SPML) methods were developed and benchmarked on synthetic datasets (e.g., COCO, VOC) where a single positive label is randomly sampled from fully-annotated images, preserving train-test label distributions. This synthetic setup fails to capture real-world SPML challenges such as fine-grained class confusions, train-test distribution shifts, and the natural structure of single-positive annotations arising from practical annotation workflows. The paper asks whether existing SPML methods generalize to genuinely real-world SPML settings and how domain priors (geographic ranges, checklists) can be best incorporated.

## Input / Output

Each input is a 3-second spectrogram clip rendered as a 448×448×3 image (STFT with window 512, stride 128, copied to three channels), drawn from longer bird recordings spanning the contiguous US. The model outputs a 100-dimensional score vector indicating species presence/absence. Unlike conventional image datasets, spectrograms can contain overlapping vocalizations that distort the visual signal rather than fully occluding one another, and each clip retains a link to its parent recording (asset) for temporal consistency.

## Method

The paper introduces the L48 dataset (100 species × 100 assets, country-wide, year-round, with dense bounding-box annotations on spectrograms) and defines three data regimes of increasing negative-label density: target-only, target-only + geographic priors (~42 negatives/image), and target-only + eBird checklist priors (~79 negatives/image). For benchmarking, seven SPML methods (BCE-AN, WAN, LS, ROLE, EM, LL-R, LL-Ct, LL-Cp) are evaluated with ImageNet-pretrained ResNet-50 under identical training protocols on both L48 and synthetic COCO. The key methodological contribution is asset regularization: a consistency loss R_P that penalizes deviation of each clip's prediction from a moving average of all clips within the same recording, exploiting the observation that background species recur across clips (28% recurrence rate). This regularization is combined additively with each SPML loss (L_SPML + α·R_P) and improves nearly all methods, closing part of the gap to fully-supervised performance.

## Results

On the fully-supervised setting, L48 achieves 62.4 mAP with BCE-Full, 14 points lower than COCO's 76.4 mAP, reflecting the fine-grained difficulty of bird species discrimination. In the target-only SPML regime, the best method on L48 is Label Smoothing (LS) at 56.4 mAP, while on COCO the best is LL-R at 71.4 mAP. The proposed asset regularization (Rp) boosts BCE-AN from 52.2 to 56.1 mAP on L48 and LL-R from 50.1 to 55.0 mAP, bringing most methods close to the LS baseline. Adding geographical and checklist priors yields only 1–2 point average gains (e.g., SPML average goes from 51.5 to 54.0 mAP on L48), far short of the ~10 points needed to match fully-supervised performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The LL-variants (LL-R, LL-Ct, LL-Cp) from Kim et al. [18] dominate on COCO (71.4, 70.5, 69.8 mAP) but collapse on L48, dropping below the simple BCE-AN baseline (50.1, 48.0, 43.8 vs. 52.2 mAP) because their assumption that high-confidence predictions on unknown labels indicate mislabeled negatives is violated by fine-grained species confusion. Label Smoothing (LS) from Szegedy et al. [36], used as an SPML baseline from Cole et al. [7], is the single best SPML method on L48 target-only (56.4 mAP) and remains best with geo/checklist priors (57.1 and 58.4 mAP). EM from Zhou et al. [47] is the second-best on L48 (55.3 mAP) and competitive on COCO (71.1 mAP), confirming that methods avoiding strong assumptions about confident predictions generalize better to fine-grained domains. BCE-AN and ROLE from Cole et al. [7] serve as the weakest baselines on L48 (52.2 and 54.0 mAP), with asset regularization closing most of the gap to LS for both. Overall, the paper demonstrates that method rankings invert between COCO and L48: the top COCO methods (LL-variants) become the worst L48 methods, while simpler approaches (LS, EM) are more robust.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the single-positive multi-label (SPML) learning line of work initiated by Cole et al. (CVPR 2021), Kim et al. (CVPR 2022), and Zhou et al. (ECCV 2022), which developed loss modifications and pseudo-labeling strategies for partially-labeled multi-label classification. It departs from prior SPML research by replacing synthetic benchmarks (randomly dropping labels from COCO/VOC) with a natural, real-world SPML setting derived from bird sound recordings, where the single-positive structure arises organically from expert annotation workflows. The proposed asset regularization builds on the spatial consistency loss of Verelst et al. (WACV 2023) but repurposes it to exploit the temporal structure of spectrogram clips within a recording rather than image augmentations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper reveals that SPML methods achieving near-parity with fully-supervised baselines on synthetic COCO benchmarks can underperform a simple label-smoothing baseline on real-world fine-grained data, exposing a significant gap between benchmark performance and deployment readiness. The L48 dataset fills a practical niche between large archive-scale bird sound collections (BirdSet, iNatSounds) and small task-specific datasets, providing dense bounding-box annotations at country-wide, year-round scale. Open questions include whether findings transfer beyond 100 Lower-48 species, how bounding box annotations and unlabeled clips can further close the SPML-to-full-supervision gap, and whether the asset regularization generalizes to other multi-view settings like video or satellite imagery.
