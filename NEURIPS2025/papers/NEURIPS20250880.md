## Problem

Existing online structured prediction (OSP) algorithms require full-information feedback, meaning the learner observes the true structured output y_t at each round. This assumption is unrealistic when outputs have complex structures (e.g., multilabel vectors or permutations), where only the loss value (bandit feedback) or a delayed signal may be available. The paper develops OSP algorithms and surrogate regret analyses for bandit feedback, delayed feedback, and their combination, filling a gap left by prior work that only covered full-information settings.

## Input / Output

At each round t, the learner receives an input vector x_t ∈ X and must produce a structured prediction ŷ_t from a finite output space Y (e.g., one-hot vectors for multiclass, binary vectors for multilabel, or permutation matrices for ranking). The feedback signal varies by setting: under bandit feedback, only the scalar loss value L(ŷ_t; y_t) is revealed; under delayed feedback, the signal (full or bandit) arrives D rounds late. The linear estimator W_t maps x_t to a score vector W_t x_t ∈ R^d, from which a decoding procedure (randomized or uniform-exploration) selects the final ŷ_t.

## Method

The paper introduces two gradient estimators for the bandit setting. The first is a standard inverse-weighted estimator combined with Randomized Decoding with Uniform Exploration (RDUE), yielding an O(√(KT)) surrogate regret bound via adaptive OGD. The second is a novel pseudo-inverse matrix estimator that leverages the SELF loss structure: it constructs an unbiased estimate of y_t using the Moore-Penrose pseudo-inverse of the second-moment matrix P_t = E[ŷ_t ŷ_t^⊤], achieving an O(T^{2/3}) bound independent of K. For delayed feedback, the paper combines ODAFTRL (for O(min{D²+1, (D+1)^{2/3}T^{1/3}}) regret) and BOLD (for the tight O(D+1) bound) with the surrogate regret analysis framework. The delayed bandit setting is handled by combining the bandit estimators with ODAFTRL, yielding O(√((K+D)T)) and O(D^{1/3}T^{2/3}) bounds.

## Results

The paper establishes surrogate regret bounds for Online Structured Prediction (OSP) under bandit and delayed feedback. Under bandit feedback, the inverse-weighted gradient estimator achieves O(√(KT)) surrogate regret (Theorem 3.4), improving upon the prior O(K√T) bound by a factor of √K. A pseudo-inverse matrix estimator yields a K-independent O(T^{2/3}) bound (Theorem 3.6) for a special SELF* loss class. For fixed-delay D full-information feedback, the paper achieves O(D+1) surrogate regret (Theorem 4.4), matching the Ω(D+1) lower bound (Theorem 4.5), and for bandit + delayed feedback, O(D^{1/3}T^{2/3}) (Theorem 5.2). Numerical experiments on MNIST (Appendix H) confirm the methods are competitive with existing multiclass-specific algorithms and show clear gains when K is large.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is against Van der Hoeven [44] and Van der Hoeven et al. [45], which established O(K√T) surrogate regret bounds for bandit multiclass classification. This work improves that to O(√(KT)) for general structured prediction under SELF losses, a √K-factor improvement, though the [45] bound applies to a broader class of surrogate losses (base-K logistic vs. base-2 logistic). The O(T^{2/3}) K-independent bound (Theorem 3.6) has no direct prior counterpart in the structured prediction literature; it is particularly advantageous when K is large (e.g., K = C(d,m) in multilabel classification or K = m! in ranking), where the √K factor in O(√(KT)) becomes prohibitive. For delayed full-information feedback, the O(D+1) bound (Theorem 4.4) is tight, matching the new Ω(D+1) lower bound (Theorem 4.5), and improves upon the standard O(√((D+1)T)) bound from generic OCO delayed-feedback algorithms. The paper also notes that its methods are competitive with existing multiclass-specific bandit algorithms (e.g., Newtron [25], Beygelzimer et al. [5]) on MNIST, despite being designed for the more general OSP setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the online structured prediction (OSP) framework of Sakaue et al. [41], which established finite surrogate regret bounds under full-information feedback using Fenchel-Young losses, to the more realistic settings of bandit and delayed feedback. For bandit feedback, it departs from the inverse-weighted gradient estimators used in prior online classification work [44, 45] by introducing a pseudo-inverse matrix estimator inspired by adversarial linear/combinatorial bandit literature [1, 9, 16], which eliminates the explicit dependence on the output set size K. For delayed feedback, it bridges two previously separate lines of research—OCO algorithms for delayed feedback (ODAFTRL [20], BOLD [27]) and surrogate regret analysis in OSP—requiring non-trivial analytical connections between the two.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The O(T^{2/3}) surrogate regret bound independent of K is practically significant for structured prediction problems where K is combinatorially large (e.g., m! for ranking, C(d,m) for multilabel classification), making the O(√(KT)) bound infeasible. The tight O(D+1) bound for delayed full-information feedback (matching a new lower bound) resolves the optimal rate question in that setting. Key open questions include whether the √T rate in the bandit setting is truly tight (only a graph-feedback lower bound exists), and whether the pseudo-inverse estimator approach can be extended to broader loss classes beyond SELF*. The empirical validation is limited to small-scale numerical comparisons, leaving open the question of practical performance at scale.
