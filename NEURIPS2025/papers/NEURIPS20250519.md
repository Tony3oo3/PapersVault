## Problem

Existing tabular ML benchmarks are static: once published, they are rarely updated even when datasets are found to contain data leaks, problematic licenses, or non-IID structure, and when new models or improved evaluation protocols emerge. This means follow-up studies reproduce known shortcomings and compare against outdated baselines rather than the actual state-of-the-art. The paper argues for a paradigm shift to a continuously maintained, versioned, community-driven living benchmarking system.

## Input / Output

The benchmark consumes 51 manually curated IID tabular datasets (500–250,000 training samples, spanning binary/multiclass classification and regression) and 16 model implementations with 200 randomly sampled hyperparameter configurations each. Foundation models operate under strict applicability constraints (TabPFNv2: ≤10K samples, ≤500 features, ≤10 classes; TabICL: ≤100K samples, ≤500 features, classification only), so they are evaluated on compatible subsets. Outputs include Elo ratings with 95% bootstrap confidence intervals, per-dataset performance metrics (ROC AUC, log-loss, RMSE), precomputed validation and test predictions for all inner-CV models, and simulated cross-model ensemble weights, all stored as reusable result artifacts.

## Method

TabArena is built on three interlocking protocols. First, a model protocol implements each of 16 models within AutoGluon's AbstractModel framework, applies 8-fold stratified inner cross-validation for hyperparameter selection over 200 random configurations (1-hour time limit per configuration per train split), and then performs weighted post-hoc ensembling of the top configurations to assess peak performance. Second, a dataset protocol manually filters 1,053 datasets from 14 prior benchmarks down to 51 using 10 explicit criteria (IID-ness, real predictive task, no data leaks, valid license, small-to-medium size, etc.), with per-dataset curation notes published publicly. Third, an evaluation protocol uses dataset-specific outer repetition (10× 3-fold for <2,500-sample datasets; 3 repeats otherwise), ranks models via the Elo rating system calibrated to a default Random Forest, and includes a reference AutoGluon pipeline. The key empirical finding is that post-hoc ensembling of hyperparameter configurations is essential to reveal true peak performance—without it, the top three leaderboard models (TabM, LightGBM, RealMLP) would all rank below CatBoost—and that cross-model ensembles across heterogeneous architectures advance the state-of-the-art beyond any single model family.

## Results

Using Elo rating as the primary metric across 51 curated datasets, the top three models with tuned + ensembled (T+E) configurations are RealMLP (Elo 1564), TabM (Elo 1541), and LightGBM (Elo 1527). CatBoost ranks first under conventional single-configuration tuning but drops to fourth without post-hoc ensembling. On small datasets (≤10K samples, ≤500 features), TabPFNv2 dominates all other methods by a large margin, while a simulated cross-model ensemble using all 16 TabArena models outperforms every individual model and the AutoGluon reference pipeline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RealMLP (T+E) achieves the highest Elo (1564) but at a cost of ~100× the inference time of tuned CatBoost and ~6500 s training per 1K samples; it only surpasses gradient-boosted trees after ensembling 25+ configurations. TabM (T+E) ranks second (Elo 1541) with the best harmonic mean rank (4.4) and most wins (4.0), indicating consistent strength across diverse datasets, while LightGBM (T+E) is third (Elo 1527) with the lowest training time among top-3 models (~417 s/1K). CatBoost leads under single-configuration tuning but is outperformed by all three neural-network T+E models once post-hoc ensembling is applied. TabPFNv2 dominates the 33 small-data-compatible datasets by a large margin over all other methods, including AutoGluon, confirming its superiority in the ≤10K-sample regime. The cross-model ensemble outperforms every individual model and the AutoGluon reference pipeline, but ModernNCA and RealMLP receive disproportionately high ensemble weights due to validation-set overfitting, suggesting their individual leaderboard rankings may be inflated.

## Relation to Prior Work

TabArena builds on the AutoGluon framework as its implementation backbone and on Caruana et al.'s post-hoc ensemble selection as a core evaluation component, but departs fundamentally from the static-benchmark paradigm that has dominated tabular ML evaluation (OpenML-CC18, PMLB, TabZilla, TabRepo, etc.). It draws conceptual inspiration from LLM benchmarking efforts like ChatBot Arena and LiveBench, transplanting the 'living leaderboard' idea to tabular data, while introducing nested cross-validation with post-hoc ensembling of 200 hyperparameter configurations as the default evaluation protocol—a level of tuning effort absent from nearly all prior tabular benchmarks. The paper also consolidates and extends the data-curation critiques raised by Kohli et al., Tschalzev et al., and Rubachev et al. into a systematic, manually verified selection process.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TabArena provides the first continuously maintained, community-governed infrastructure for tabular ML benchmarking, addressing the reproducibility and staleness problems that have eroded trust in prior benchmarks. Its finding that post-hoc ensembling is essential to reveal true peak performance—and that deep learning models then match or exceed GBDTs—reframes the long-running 'GBDT vs. deep learning' debate as a false dichotomy, since cross-model ensembles outperform any single family. Open questions include extending to non-IID, tiny, and large data regimes, mitigating validation-set overfitting that inflates certain models' ensemble weights, and guarding against dataset contamination in a living system. The ~25M model runs and shared result artifacts substantially lower the barrier for future model submissions.
