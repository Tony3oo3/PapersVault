## Problem

Existing generalization bounds for gradient descent on deep ReLU networks either yield suboptimal O(1/√n) sample complexity or rely on smooth activations, incurring exponential dependence on network depth L. The fundamental open question is whether GD can achieve the optimal SVM-type rate Õ(1/(nγ²)) for deep ReLU networks while maintaining only polynomial dependence on depth and polylogarithmic width in n.

## Input / Output

The analysis takes binary-labeled data on the unit sphere S^{d-1} (Assumption 2), a fully-connected L-layer ReLU network of width m with symmetric initialization and fixed output weights, and assumes NTK separability with margin γ. The output is a high-probability excess risk bound Õ(L⁶(log T)²/(nγ²)) for the averaged GD iterates, along with a convergence rate of F_S(W̄)/(ηT) for the training loss, where F_S(W̄) encodes the reference model's training error and initialization distance. The width requirement is Õ(L¹⁶(log n)⁸/γ⁸), which is polylogarithmic in n.

## Method

The paper combines two technical advances. For optimization, it establishes almost-convexity of deep ReLU networks near a reference model W̄, showing all GD iterates remain in a Frobenius ball of radius √F_S(W̄) around W̄, reducing the width requirement by a factor of L⁶ over Chen et al. (2021). For generalization, it derives a sharper Rademacher complexity bound Õ(L²√(F(W̄)/n)) by representing the network output difference as a sum over layers involving sparse sign matrices, whose spectral norms are tightly controlled via a sparse-vector decomposition technique (decomposing unit vectors into s-sparse components to reduce covering numbers from 5^m to 5^s·C(m,s)). A second key contribution is a covering-number argument that proves the network is Õ(L²)-Lipschitz near initialization, replacing the recursive C^L bounds used in prior work and eliminating the exponential depth factor from the generalization gap.

## Results

For NTK-separable data with margin γ, the paper proves an excess risk rate of Õ(L⁶(log T)²/(nγ²)) for deep ReLU networks trained by gradient descent, matching the optimal SVM-type rate Õ(1/(nγ²)) up to polynomial depth factors. The required network width is Õ(L¹⁶d(log m)⁵log(nL/δ)(log T)⁸/γ⁸), which is polylogarithmic in n and polynomial in L. Experimentally, on noisy 2-XOR data (where 1/γ = O(d)), the test error decays as approximately 0.15·d²/n, consistent with the theoretical prediction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to Ji & Telgarsky (shallow ReLU, Õ(1/(γ²√n))) and Lei et al. (shallow ReLU, Õ(1/(γ²n))), this paper extends the optimal 1/(γ²n) rate from shallow to deep ReLU networks while maintaining the same Õ(1/γ⁸) width requirement. Against Chen et al. (deep ReLU), the paper simultaneously removes the exponential depth dependence (e^{O(L)} → L⁶), eliminates the explicit √m width factor, and achieves the optimal 1/n sample complexity instead of √(m/n), while also reducing the width from Õ(L²²/γ⁸) to Õ(L¹⁶/γ⁸). Versus Taheri et al. (deep smooth networks), the paper replaces the exponential-in-L width requirement 1/γ^{6L+4} with the polynomial L¹⁶/γ⁸, and handles the non-smooth ReLU activation rather than requiring smooth activations. Overall, this is the first result achieving the near-optimal Õ(1/(nγ²)) rate for deep ReLU networks with only polynomial dependence on depth.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the NTK-separable data framework of Ji and Telgarsky (2020) from shallow to deep ReLU networks, and builds on the Rademacher complexity analysis of Chen et al. (2021) for deep architectures. The key departure is replacing the product-of-spectral-norms term (which yields exponential depth dependence) with a sparse-matrix representation whose norms are controlled via covering numbers, and leveraging the almost-convexity framework of Allen-Zhu et al. (2019) for a tighter optimization analysis. Unlike the stability-based approach of Taheri et al. (2025), which achieves O~(1/(nγ²)) but requires smooth activations, this work handles the nonsmooth ReLU case directly through refined activation-pattern control.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result establishes that the exponential depth dependence in prior Rademacher complexity bounds for deep ReLU networks is an artifact of the analysis rather than a fundamental property, yielding the optimal SVM-type rate O~(L⁶/(nγ²)) with only polynomial depth scaling. This resolves a concrete open question in the NTK generalization literature and provides a template (sparse-matrix Rademacher bounds + covering-number Lipschitz control) that may transfer to other nonsmooth architectures. Open questions include extending the analysis to SGD, removing the symmetric-initialization and sphere-input assumptions, and studying whether the polynomial Lipschitz constant persists far from initialization or for other architectures like CNNs and ResNets.
