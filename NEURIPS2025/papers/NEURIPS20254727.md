## Problem

Consistency model distillation produces valid trajectories but struggles with high-curvature paths, requiring multiple sampling steps for quality. Rectified flow distillation enforces straight trajectories for faster sampling but depends on numerical ODE solvers that introduce approximation errors. No prior method simultaneously achieves both straight and consistent trajectories within a noise-to-image pair.

## Input / Output

SCoT takes a random Gaussian noise vector x_1 ~ N(0, I) and produces a clean RGB image (32×32 on CIFAR-10, 64×64 on ImageNet) in as few as 1 or 2 function evaluations. The student model is a trajectory projection function G_φ(x_t, t, s) that accepts the current noisy state, its time step t, and a target time step s, outputting the projected state at s. Training requires a pre-trained teacher model (EDM for CIFAR-10, ADM for ImageNet) to dynamically generate noise-image pairs and intermediate trajectory states via its ODE solver.

## Method

SCoT learns a trajectory projection function G_φ(x_t, t, s) = (s/t)x_1 + (1−s/t)g_φ(x_t, t, s) parameterized by a neural network g_φ, trained with two complementary losses. The velocity loss regularizes the partial derivative ∂G_φ/∂s (computed via torch.autograd.grad) to equal the constant displacement (x̂_0 − x_1), enforcing straightness without requiring an ODE solver at inference. The consistency loss, following CTM's soft-consistency formulation, ensures that two points on the same teacher trajectory at different time steps t_1 < t_2 both project to the same clean image when decoded through an EMA stop-gradient model. This dual-objective design lets SCoT directly learn the full trajectory (avoiding ODE approximation errors of rectified flows) while guaranteeing trajectory validity (avoiding the multi-step requirement of consistency models), achieving FID 2.30 at 2 NFE on CIFAR-10 and FID 2.60 at 2 NFE on ImageNet.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, SCoT achieves FID 2.30 with 2 NFEs and FID 2.40 with 1 NFE, outperforming CD (FID 2.93) and CT (FID 5.83) at 2 NFEs. On ImageNet, SCoT reaches FID 2.60 with 2 NFEs (Recall 0.61, IS 68.20) and FID 4.80 with 1 NFE (Recall 0.57, IS 67.60), approaching the quality of EDM at 79 NFEs (FID 2.44) while requiring only 2–4% of the function evaluations. The method uses the same model size (295.9M params, 219.4G FLOPs) as the teacher ADM architecture.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SCoT outperforms all non-adversarial distillation baselines on both datasets: on ImageNet at 2 NFEs it beats CD (FID 2.60 vs 6.20) and PD (2.60 vs 15.39), and at 1 NFE it substantially surpasses SlimFlow (4.80 vs 12.34) and Shortcut (4.80 vs 20.50/40.30). On CIFAR-10 at 1 NFE, SCoT (FID 2.40) dramatically outperforms all rectified-flow distillation variants (3-Rectified Flow: 8.15, 2-Rectified Flow: 12.21, 1-Rectified Flow: 378). The only method that achieves a lower FID is CTM* (1.70 at 2 NFEs on ImageNet; 1.87 at 2 NFEs on CIFAR-10), but CTM* relies on an additional GAN loss for adversarial supervision, which the authors note is unstable under their training configuration. Notably, SCoT achieves higher Inception Score (68.20 vs 64.29) and Recall (0.61 vs 0.57) than CTM* on ImageNet at 2 NFEs, indicating better diversity despite the slightly higher FID. SCoT at 2 NFEs (FID 2.60) approaches the quality of the full EDM teacher at 79 NFEs (FID 2.44) while requiring only ~2.5% of the function evaluations.

## Relation to Prior Work

SCoT unifies two previously separate trajectory distillation paradigms: consistency model distillation (Song et al. 2023; CTM, Kim et al. 2024), which enforces that all points on a trajectory map to the same clean image, and rectified flow distillation (Reflow, Liu et al. 2023), which enforces constant velocity for straight trajectories. It adopts CTM's projection function G_φ(x_t, t, s) and consistency loss structure while incorporating a velocity loss mathematically equivalent to reflow's straightening objective, but applied to the trajectory itself rather than a learned velocity field. This avoids the ODE solver approximation errors inherent in rectified flow sampling while retaining the consistency guarantee that makes trajectories 'valid.' The paper positions itself against concurrent works MeanFlow (Geng et al. 2025) and FlowMap (Sabour et al. 2025), distinguishing itself by using the gradient at the terminating step rather than the starting step, and by directly learning the trajectory rather than relying on a CTM backbone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The conceptual unification of consistency and straightness in a single trajectory framework is clean and provides a principled explanation for why each property helps: consistency ensures validity while straightness reduces the number of steps needed for accurate projection. However, SCoT does not surpass CTM* (which uses a GAN loss) on either dataset, and the authors explicitly attribute this gap to the absence of adversarial training due to instability. The method is validated only at 32×32 (CIFAR-10) and 64×64 (ImageNet) resolutions, leaving open whether the dual-objective formulation scales to high-resolution synthesis. The velocity loss's reliance on second-order automatic differentiation introduces numerical fragility that required ad-hoc normalization to stabilize, suggesting the approach may be sensitive to implementation details. Future work on high-resolution and conditional generation remains unaddressed.
