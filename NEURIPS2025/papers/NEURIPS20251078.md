## Problem

Existing 3D occupancy prediction methods either rely on dense voxel grids that are computationally expensive and hard to scale to high resolution, or use a single set of sparse queries that treats all scene content uniformly. The paper argues that driving scenes naturally decompose into static background and dynamic agents with very different characteristics, and that a single query set cannot allocate sufficient capacity to the critical dynamic objects while also covering the extensive static geometry.

## Input / Output

The method consumes N_c multi-camera RGB images (resized to 256×704 on nuScenes, 640×960 on Waymo) along with camera intrinsics/extrinsics and optional history frames (up to 8 frames). It outputs a 200×200×16 semantic voxel grid (0.4 m voxels) as the primary occupancy prediction, plus 3D bounding boxes with velocity vectors from the dynamic query branch, and full 3D Gaussian parameters (mean, scale, quaternion, opacity) for every predicted Gaussian. During training, the Gaussians are also rendered into per-camera depth and semantic maps via 3DGS for auxiliary supervision against projected LiDAR labels.

## Method

ODG defines two distinct sets of learnable Gaussian queries—S static and D dynamic—where dynamic queries are augmented with 3D box attributes (dimensions, yaw, velocity) to explicitly dedicate capacity to moving agents. A hierarchical coarse-to-fine scheme over L=6 transformer layers progressively increases the number of Gaussians per query (K_{ℓ−1} < K_ℓ), enabling a much larger total Gaussian count (up to 76,800 for ODG-T) than single-transformer baselines. Dynamic-and-Static (DaS) attention concatenates both query feature sets and applies self-attention to enable bidirectional information flow between static and dynamic branches. For temporal feature sampling, dynamic Gaussians are warped by both ego-motion and their predicted object velocity before projecting onto history-frame image features. Multi-stage 3DGS rendering of depth and semantic maps at every layer provides pixel-level projective supervision from 2D labels, reducing spatial artifacts without adding inference cost.

## Results

On Occ3D-nuScenes, ODG-L (8f) achieves a new state-of-the-art mIoU of 38.18 and RayIoU of 42.3, surpassing the previous best OPUS-L (36.2 mIoU, 41.2 RayIoU) by +1.98 and +1.1 respectively, while ODG-T (8f) reaches 35.54 mIoU and 39.2 RayIoU at 20.1 FPS. On Occ3D-Waymo, ODG-L leads OPUS by +2.35 mIoU and +1.2 RayIoU, with particularly large gains on dynamic agent classes (Vehicle +3.25, Bicyclist +3.01, Pedestrian +8.91). The dynamic-class mIoU for ODG-L is 38.77, a +4.13 improvement over OPUS-L (34.64), confirming the benefit of dedicated dynamic Gaussian queries.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ODG outperforms all supervised baselines on both mIoU and RayIoU. Against the strongest prior method OPUS-L (8f), ODG-L gains +1.98 mIoU and +1.1 RayIoU, while ODG-T beats OPUS-T by +2.34 mIoU and +0.8 RayIoU at a comparable 20.1 FPS (vs. 22.4 FPS). The gap widens substantially against earlier sparse-query methods: ODG-T surpasses SparseOcc (8f) by +5.44 mIoU and GaussRender by +5.16 mIoU. On dynamic object classes specifically (Bus, Car, Construction Vehicle, Motorcycle, Truck), ODG-L achieves 38.77 mIoU versus 34.64 for OPUS-L (+4.13), with the largest per-class gains on Car (+4.85) and Bus (+7.82). Self-supervised methods (GaussTR, GaussianFlowOcc) remain far behind at 12–16 mIoU, highlighting the continued value of 3D occupancy ground-truth supervision. On Occ3D-Waymo, ODG-L extends its lead to +2.35 mIoU over OPUS, with an especially large +8.91 mIoU on Pedestrian, underscoring the advantage of dedicated dynamic queries in challenging multi-camera settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

ODG extends the sparse set-prediction paradigm for 3D occupancy (established by SparseOcc and OPUS) by replacing a single homogeneous query set with a dual dynamic/static Gaussian query architecture, drawing on 3D Gaussian Splatting for both scene representation and real-time rendering supervision. It departs from prior Gaussian-based occupancy methods (GaussTR, GaussianFlowOcc, GaussRender) that rely on a single transformer layer and thus handle only a small number of Gaussians, by introducing a hierarchical coarse-to-fine refinement that scales to tens of thousands of Gaussians. The motion compensation for dynamic queries follows the velocity-warping strategy from SparseBEV, while the rendering-based depth/semantic supervision builds on the 3DGS rasterization pipeline of Kerbl et al.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ODG sets new state-of-the-art on both Occ3D-nuScenes and Occ3D-Waymo with particularly large gains on dynamic agent classes (vehicles, pedestrians), validating the hypothesis that dedicated capacity for moving objects is critical for safe driving perception. The rendering supervision is applied only during training, incurring zero additional inference cost, which makes the approach practically attractive for deployment. A key limitation acknowledged by the authors is that Gaussian scale, rotation, and opacity are optimized solely through the rendering loss rather than directly from occupancy ground-truth, leaving room for more principled parameter optimization. Open questions include whether the dual-query decomposition generalizes to scenes with more complex motion (e.g., articulated objects) and whether the coarse-to-fine hierarchy can be further accelerated with efficient attention or state-space models.
