## Problem

Prior MAE-based methods for multi-channel imaging (MCI) rely on random patch masking, which assumes strong cross-channel redundancy and causes patches to attend primarily to their own channel rather than learning cross-channel interactions. In MCI, channels often encode complementary (not redundant) physical properties—e.g., LiDAR vs. RGB vs. thermal—so this assumption breaks down, limiting the model's ability to leverage cross-channel dependencies and handle varying channel configurations at test time.

## Input / Output

The model accepts multi-channel images with a variable number of channels (3–5 in CHAMMI, 8 in JUMP-CP, 18 in So2Sat) and is trained on all channels but evaluated on subsets. A shared linear projection maps each p×p patch (p=16 for microscopy, p=8 for satellite) to a d-dimensional token, keeping parameter count constant regardless of channel count. Outputs include class labels, feature representations for downstream tasks, segmentation masks, and reconstructed pixel patches for masked regions.

## Method

ChA-MAEViT introduces four components to enhance cross-channel learning in MCI-ViTs. First, Dynamic Channel-Patch (DCP) Masking adaptively masks both individual patches and entire channels during training, compelling the model to reconstruct missing channels from remaining ones and thereby learning inter-channel relationships rather than only intra-channel spatial structure. Second, learnable memory tokens are prepended to the input sequence to serve as long-term memory that aggregates and retains global cross-channel information across encoder and decoder layers. Third, a single shared Channel-Aware Decoder (1–2 transformer blocks) reconstructs all masked patches simultaneously by adding channel-specific tokens to each patch/mask token, replacing the separate per-channel decoders used in prior work and scaling efficiently to many channels. Fourth, a Hybrid Token Fusion module combines the global [CLS] token with fine-grained patch tokens via cross-attention and a sigmoid-gated elementwise product for richer downstream representations. Training jointly optimizes a task loss, channel/token diversification regularization, and a reconstruction loss (pixel L2 + Fourier-space L1) with a fixed reconstruction weight of 0.99.

## Results

On CHAMMI (representation learning, avg F1), ChA-MAEViT achieves 74.63%, surpassing the prior best DiChaViT (69.77%) by 5.0 points. On JUMP-CP (classification, top-1 accuracy), it reaches 90.73% in the Full setting and 68.05% in the Partial setting, exceeding DiChaViT by 21.5% and 10.1% respectively. On So2Sat (classification, top-1 accuracy), it achieves 67.44% (Full) and 52.11% (Partial), outperforming DiChaViT by 3.0% and 4.4%. On 38-Cloud (segmentation), it attains 0.964 accuracy and 0.894 IoU, the best across all metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ChA-MAEViT outperforms all 12 baselines across every dataset and evaluation setting. Against the strongest supervised baseline DiChaViT [29], it gains 5.0% on CHAMMI, 21.5% on JUMP-CP Full, 10.1% on JUMP-CP Partial, 3.0% on So2Sat Full, and 4.4% on So2Sat Partial. The prior MCI-MAE method CA-MAE [1] shows a dramatic collapse in Partial settings (20.93% on JUMP-CP, 15.75% on So2Sat) due to its separate per-channel decoders, whereas ChA-MAEViT's shared Channel-Aware Decoder maintains strong partial-channel performance. Among SSL-enhanced variants of DiChaViT (Table 2), DINOv2 [44] and SimCLR [37] yield the best supervised+SSL combinations (70.72% CHAMMI, 67.12% JUMP-CP Full), but ChA-MAEViT's DCP Masking alone (71.47% CHAMMI, 84.02% JUMP-CP Full) surpasses all of them by 0.6–5.6%. ChannelViT [30] and ChAda-ViT [13] are competitive in Full settings but degrade sharply under Partial inference, while HyperNetViT [51], DepthwiseViT [12], and TemplateMixingViT [52] trail by 10–25 points across all settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MAE framework (He et al., 2022) and its multi-channel adaptation CA-MAE (Kraus et al., 2024) by identifying a fundamental limitation: random patch masking in MCI fails to promote cross-channel interaction because patches attend primarily to their own channel. It builds on the channel-adaptive ViT architecture of DiChaViT (Pham & Plummer, 2024) and the Hierarchical Channel Sampling idea from ChannelViT (Bao et al., 2024), but departs from both by using masked channels as reconstruction targets rather than simple dropout, and by replacing per-channel decoders with a shared Channel-Aware Decoder. The memory tokens draw on register tokens (Darcet et al., 2024), repurposed for cross-channel information retention rather than artifact reduction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap in MCI (satellite, microscopy, robotics) where channels are complementary rather than redundant, and shows that the standard MAE assumption of cross-channel redundancy breaks down. The 3–21.5% improvements across three diverse datasets, backed by thorough ablations and attention-map diagnostics, make the DCP Masking strategy a broadly applicable technique for any setting with heterogeneous channels. Open questions include systematic evaluation on truly unseen channels, extension to volumetric/3D data, and whether the approach generalizes beyond ViT backbones. The computational efficiency (6× fewer FLOPs than DINOv2) makes it attractive for large-scale MCI pre-training.
