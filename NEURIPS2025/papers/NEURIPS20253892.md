## Problem

In PET-based rehearsal-free continual learning, the one-shot parameter matching that assigns a test sample to a task-specific parameter set is suboptimal: over 15% of samples are mismatched by the SOTA HiDe-Prompt on Split CIFAR-100, and matching accuracy drops to 67.58% on Split ImageNet-R. This mismatch stems from relying solely on pretrained features for task identity prediction, creating a train-inference inconsistency that directly caps classification accuracy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253892/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single RGB image at inference time (no task identity is provided) and produces a class label over all classes seen so far. Internally, the pipeline first predicts a task identity via an auxiliary classifier on frozen pretrained features, then attaches the corresponding LoRA module to the ViT-B/16 backbone to generate the final class distribution. During training, each task's images are processed with their ground-truth class labels, and the top-K old task identities are retrieved by the auxiliary classifier to compute distillation targets.

## Method

HRM-PET builds on the HiDe-Prompt pipeline (frozen ViT-B/16 + per-task LoRA pool + auxiliary task classifier + unified linear head) and adds a two-stage re-matching mechanism at inference. Direct Re-matching (DRM) checks whether the predicted class under the initially matched parameters belongs to a different task; if so, it replaces the task identity with the one implied by the prediction. Confidence-based Re-matching (CRM) then handles remaining mismatches by computing generalized entropy (GEN) on the predicted distribution: samples exceeding a threshold τ are flagged as likely mismatched, and the correct task identity is recovered by comparing GEN scores across the top-2 candidate identities from the initial matching. During training, a Cross-Task Instance Relationship Distillation (CTIRD) loss aligns the normalized instance-similarity matrix of the current task's LoRA with those of top-K old tasks (selected per-sample by the auxiliary classifier) via KL divergence, encouraging task-invariant feature geometry so that even mismatched parameters still attend to discriminative regions.

## Results

HRM-PET achieves state-of-the-art average final accuracy (A_N) across all 20 settings (5 pre-trained models × 4 datasets). Under the Sup-21K backbone, it reaches 89.45% on Split CIFAR-100, 73.86% on Split ImageNet-R, 44.28% on ImageNet-A, and 93.99% on 5-Datasets, outperforming the strongest baseline HiDe-LoRA by 1.02%, 1.96%, 2.10%, and 0.74% respectively. With the iBOT-21K backbone, gains widen to 1.26%, 1.83%, 2.33%, and 0.85%. Average forgetting (F_N) is also consistently reduced, e.g., from 4.33% to 3.60% on ImageNet-R (Sup-21K).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HRM-PET outperforms all seven PET-based baselines (DualPrompt, S-Prompt++, CODA-Prompt, LAE, CPrompt, InfLoRA, HiDe-LoRA) across all 20 evaluation settings. The closest competitor is HiDe-LoRA (the direct baseline); HRM-PET improves A_N by 1.02–1.26% on Split CIFAR-100, 1.83–1.96% on Split ImageNet-R, 2.10–2.33% on ImageNet-A, and 0.59–0.85% on 5-Datasets depending on the backbone. Against the non-PET methods in Table 2, HRM-PET surpasses SLCA by 2.03% (ImageNet-R) and 2.58% (ImageNet-A) in A_N, and beats ESN by 3.20% and 0.35% respectively, while also achieving substantially lower forgetting (e.g., 3.11% vs. 4.34% F_N on ImageNet-R vs. SLCA). The largest absolute gains appear on ImageNet-A and ImageNet-R, which have high intra-class diversity, confirming that the re-matching mechanism is most beneficial when initial task-identity matching is hardest.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the PET-based rehearsal-free continual learning line initiated by DualPrompt and refined by HiDe-Prompt, which introduced a parameter pool with task-identity prediction via an auxiliary classifier. The key departure is a hybrid re-matching mechanism that exploits the predicted class distribution to correct erroneous task-identity assignments, rather than relying on a single one-shot matching step as in all prior PET-based methods. The cross-task instance relationship distillation (CTIRD) module adapts the instance-relation distillation concept from Co2L (Cha et al., 2021) into a top-K parameter-selection scheme that aligns shared feature spaces across tasks, while the confidence-based detection leverages the generalized entropy (GEN) function from Liu et al. (2023) as a post-hoc mismatch detector.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper quantifies a concrete and previously underappreciated bottleneck: even the SOTA HiDe-Prompt misassigns task identity for over 15% of samples on CIFAR-100 and 32% on ImageNet-R, directly capping classification accuracy. The re-matching mechanism is attractive in practice because it adds only ~0.4 ms per image at inference and requires no architectural changes, making it a drop-in improvement for existing PET-based CL pipelines. However, the approach still depends on a frozen pre-trained backbone and a manually tuned confidence threshold τ, and the CTIRD module introduces K extra forward passes during training. Open questions include whether re-matching generalizes beyond LoRA/ViT to other PET techniques (adapters, prefix tuning) and whether the confidence-based detection can be made threshold-free.
