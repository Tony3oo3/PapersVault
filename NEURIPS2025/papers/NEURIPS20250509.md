## Problem

Non-stationary bandit algorithms have been extensively studied for discrete action spaces, and Lipschitz bandits handle continuous arms under stationarity, but their intersection—non-stationary Lipschitz bandits—remained unexplored. The central challenge is that the optimal discretization level depends on the unknown duration of stable phases, and detecting reward changes in a continuous space requires balancing exploration cost against detection precision across multiple scales. This paper asks whether an algorithm can adaptively achieve the minimax-optimal dynamic regret rate without any prior knowledge of the non-stationarity.

## Input / Output

At each round t, the algorithm selects an arm x_t from the continuous interval [0,1] and observes a scalar stochastic reward Y_t(x_t) ∈ [0,1] whose conditional mean μ_t(x_t) is 1-Lipschitz in x but may change arbitrarily between rounds. The algorithm must produce a sequence of arm selections over horizon T and guarantees a bound on expected dynamic regret relative to the per-round oracle. Unusually, the algorithm internally maintains a hierarchy of active discretization depths and a set of active bins at each depth, with the sampling probability of any arm determined by a top-down traversal of the dyadic tree rather than a fixed grid.

## Method

MDBE (Multi-Depth Bin Elimination) organizes time into episodes of blocks with doubling length 8^m, where block m discretizes [0,1] into 2^m bins via a dyadic tree. Within each block, a variant of Successive Elimination evicts bins whose cumulative empirical regret exceeds a threshold calibrated to the minimax rate of a 2^d-armed bandit. The key mechanism is multi-scale replays: at coarser depths d < m, the algorithm probabilistically triggers short replays of duration 8^d rounds, resetting all bins at that depth to re-explore the space at a coarser resolution. A critical design choice is the importance-weighted estimator, which allows a single sample x_t to simultaneously update mean estimates for every ancestor bin in the dyadic tree, so that replays at any depth propagate information to all finer active depths. This multi-scale aggregation avoids the prohibitive cost of re-exploring all 2^m bins at each replay, enabling efficient detection of shifts of varying magnitudes. A significant shift is declared when the MASTER set at the current block depth becomes empty, triggering an episode restart.

## Results

The paper establishes a minimax lower bound of c₁·L̃^(1/3)·T^(2/3) on the expected dynamic regret for non-stationary Lipschitz bandits (Theorem 1), and proves that MDBE achieves the matching upper bound Õ(L̃_T^(1/3)·T^(2/3)) without prior knowledge of the number of significant shifts (Theorem 2). Additionally, MDBE recovers the optimal rate Õ(T^(2/3) + V_T^(1/4)·T^(3/4)) in terms of total variation V_T (Corollary 1). The algorithm is fully adaptive: it requires no prior knowledge of L̃_T, S_T, L_T, or V_T, and the regret bound is expressed as a sum over per-phase terms (τᵢ − τᵢ₋₁)^(2/3) rather than a worst-case aggregate.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no head-to-head algorithmic comparisons; the 'baseline' is the information-theoretic lower bound, which MDBE matches up to polylogarithmic factors. The key comparison is against prior non-stationary bandit frameworks: classical metrics L_T and V_T yield suboptimal rates when the best arm is stable but other arms fluctuate, whereas the significant-shift metric L̃_T (introduced by Suk and Kpotufe, 2022, for K-armed bandits) is strictly smaller (L̃_T ≤ S_T ≤ L_T) and MDBE is the first to achieve the corresponding optimal rate in the continuous Lipschitz setting. The paper also notes that the only prior work on non-stationary Lipschitz bandits (Kang et al., 2023a) uses a zooming-like approach that does not achieve optimal theoretical guarantees. No empirical baselines are run; the paper includes a synthetic experiment in Appendix I for illustration only.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the significant-shifts framework of Suk and Kpotufe (COLT 2022) from finite K-armed bandits to the continuous Lipschitz bandit setting, where the action space is [0,1] and rewards satisfy a Lipschitz condition. The algorithm MDBE combines three prior ideas: the replay mechanism for detecting non-stationarity (Suk and Kpotufe, 2022), successive elimination for pruning suboptimal regions (Even-Dar et al., 2006), and dyadic discretization of the continuous arm space (Kleinberg, 2004). The key departure from prior work is the multi-depth hierarchical structure: rather than discretizing at a single fixed resolution, MDBE simultaneously monitors aggregate reward gaps across a dyadic tree of bin partitions, with replays scheduled at multiple coarseness levels to detect shifts of varying magnitudes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to establish minimax-optimal dynamic regret for non-stationary Lipschitz bandits, closing a gap where both non-stationary and Lipschitz bandits were well-studied individually but their intersection was unexplored. The result is fully adaptive (no prior knowledge of the number of significant shifts or total variation), and the lower bound confirms the rate is tight. Open questions include computational efficiency of MDBE (the worst-case complexity is analyzed but may be impractical), extension to other structured bandit models (convex, linear, kernelized), and whether the multi-depth replay framework can be simplified or made more computationally tractable for real-world deployment.
