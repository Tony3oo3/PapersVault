## Problem

Whether object binding—the ability to group an object's distributed low-level features into a coherent whole—naturally emerges in pretrained Vision Transformers has remained an open question. Cognitive scientists have argued that ViTs' purely connectionist, feedforward architecture lacks the mechanisms for dynamic feature grouping, and prior empirical work showed self-attention tends to group patches by low-level similarity rather than object identity. This paper directly tests whether ViTs encode a pairwise IsSameObject signal and, if so, how it is structured across layers and whether it is functionally useful for the pretraining objective.

## Input / Output

The method operates on patch embeddings extracted from intermediate layers of pretrained ViTs (DINOv2, CLIP, MAE, supervised ViT-L) processing 518×518 RGB images with 14×14 patches, yielding 1,369 patches per image. Probes are trained on ADE20K with ground-truth instance masks to classify whether any two patches belong to the same object, producing a pairwise IsSameObject score in [0,1]. Downstream evaluation retrains linear segmentation heads on the same ADE20K subset, reporting semantic and instance mIoU, and additionally measures the DINO self-distillation loss as a pretraining-objective metric.

## Method

The core tool is a quadratic similarity probe that scores IsSameObject(x,y) = σ(xᵀW₁ᵀW₂y + b) with a symmetry constraint (W₂ = SW₁ for a sign matrix S), yielding a low-rank quadratic form with O(kd) parameters. By comparing this against linear, diagonal-quadratic, and pointwise object-class/identity probes, the paper shows that IsSameObject is fundamentally quadratic, pairwise, and distributed across dimensions rather than confined to a few channels. The trained probe's projection matrices define a low-dimensional binding subspace: patch embeddings decompose additively into a feature component and a binding component, with the binding component forming linearly separable clusters for different object instances in just 3 principal components. The paper further demonstrates that attention weights at layer ℓ+1 positively correlate with IsSameObject scores at layer ℓ (Pearson r ≈ 0.16–0.20), and that ablating the binding signal via random shuffling degrades segmentation mIoU and increases DINO loss, while injecting ground-truth object-averaged binding vectors improves performance—establishing that emergent object binding is functionally aligned with the pretraining objective.

## Results

The quadratic similarity probe decodes IsSameObject (whether two patches belong to the same object) at 90.2% accuracy on DINOv2-Large (layer 18), exceeding the majority-class baseline of 72.6% by +17.6 percentage points. This binding signal is robust across DINOv2 (86.7–90.2%), ImageNet-supervised ViT-L (84.2%), and CLIP ViT-L (82.9%), but is largely absent in MAE ViT-L (76.3%, only +3.7 pp over baseline), indicating that object binding is an acquired ability tied to specific pretraining objectives rather than a trivial architectural artifact. Ablating the binding subspace degrades instance segmentation mIoU from 35.14% to 28.19% (full random shuffle) and increases DINO pretraining loss from 0.6182 to 0.6749, confirming the functional role of the emergent binding signal.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares four probe architectures (linear, diagonal quadratic, full quadratic, and pointwise object-class/identity) and finds that the full quadratic probe consistently outperforms all alternatives from mid-layers onward, establishing that IsSameObject is encoded as a distributed pairwise interaction rather than a linear or pointwise signal. Against simpler baselines, the quadratic probe (90.2%) substantially exceeds the majority-class baseline (72.6%), the learned distance-threshold baseline (77.16%), and fixed cosine-similarity / dot-product / self-attention probes, which all remain near or below the distance baseline. Across pretrained models, DINOv2-Large achieves the strongest binding signal (+17.6 pp), followed by ImageNet-supervised ViT-L (+11.6 pp) and CLIP (+10.3 pp), while MAE (+3.7 pp) is statistically indistinguishable from the majority baseline, suggesting that contrastive teacher-student objectives and class-level supervision are critical inductive biases for emergent binding. The ablation experiments further show that randomly shuffling the binding vector (uninformed ablation) degrades both segmentation mIoU and DINO loss, whereas injecting the ground-truth object-mean binding direction (informed ablation) improves instance segmentation mIoU from 35.14% to 37.02%, confirming that the decoded subspace is causally functional.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the binding-ID decomposition framework from language models (Feng & Steinhardt, 2023) and the linear representation hypothesis (Park et al., 2023) to vision transformers, proposing that ViT patch embeddings decompose into feature and binding components. It departs from the dominant object-centric learning paradigm (Slot Attention, MONet, IODINE) that explicitly enforces binding through external modules, instead showing that binding emerges implicitly in standard ViT architectures. The work also extends prior behavioral and representational studies of ViT grouping (Adeli et al., 2023; Mehrani & Tsotsos, 2023) by formulating a precise pairwise predicate (IsSameObject) and systematically probing it across model families, scales, and pretraining objectives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that object binding is acquired through specific pretraining objectives (strong in DINO/CLIP/supervised, weak in MAE) reframes binding as a learnable representational property rather than an architectural guarantee, with direct implications for diagnosing and fixing binding failures in VLMs and diffusion models. The discovery that binding is encoded in a low-dimensional quadratic subspace that actively guides attention provides a mechanistic account that could inform targeted interventions. Key open questions include whether strengthening intrinsic binding (rather than adding Slot Attention) can resolve attribute-leakage failures, how bound object representations interact across objects, and whether the mid-layer-to-late-layer shift from instance-level to class-level grouping is a fundamental limitation or a tunable property.
