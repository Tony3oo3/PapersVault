## Problem

Sharpness Aware Minimization (SAM) improves generalization by perturbing parameters along the local gradient before computing the training gradient, but this requires an additional forward and backward pass, doubling computational cost and making SAM impractical under limited compute budgets. The paper asks whether the perturbation direction can be replaced with information already available during standard momentum-based training, eliminating the extra pass entirely.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251850/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB image batches (32×32 for CIFAR-100, 224×224 for ImageNet), the current model parameters, and the momentum vector maintained by the optimizer (SGD or Adam). It outputs updated parameters with reduced loss sharpness, requiring no additional memory or forward/backward passes beyond what the base optimizer already performs. The perturbation magnitude ρ is typically 1–3 orders of magnitude larger than the learning-rate step, and the perturbation is applied in the negative momentum direction rather than the positive gradient direction used by SAM.

## Method

MSAM replaces SAM's gradient-based perturbation with the negative momentum vector direction, exploiting two observations: (1) the momentum vector, as an exponential moving average of batch gradients, approximates the full-dataset gradient direction, and (2) the negative momentum direction has a positive loss slope because the previous SGD update overshoots local minima along that high-curvature direction. The algorithm shifts the starting point to the perturbed parameters (analogous to NAG's look-ahead step), computes the gradient at this perturbed position, removes the perturbation before the parameter update, and proceeds with standard momentum SGD. This requires zero additional forward/backward passes and no extra memory beyond what SGD with momentum already stores, while a PAC-Bayes generalization bound is shown to hold under the assumption that the perturbation direction has high curvature. Empirically, MSAM matches or approaches SAM's accuracy at roughly half the runtime, and outperforms all other efficiency-oriented SAM variants (LookSAM, ESAM, MESA) in both speed and accuracy.

## Results

On CIFAR-100, MSAM achieves 83.21% top-1 accuracy on WideResNet-28-10, 79.11% on WideResNet-16-4, and 82.65% on ResNet-50, outperforming the SGD baseline (81.51%, 76.90%, 81.46%) and NAG (82.00%, 77.09%, 82.12%) at the same training speed (0.99x). On ImageNet, MSAM matches SAM's 69.1% top-1 accuracy for ViT-S/32 while running at 0.99x speed versus SAM's 0.52x, effectively halving the computational cost. When given the same computational budget (180 epochs), MSAM reaches 70.1% on ViT-S/32 ImageNet, surpassing SAM's 69.1%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MSAM outperforms all same-speed baselines: it beats SGD/AdamW by 1.7–2.2% on CIFAR-100 and 2.1% on ImageNet ViT-S/32, and beats NAG by 1.2–1.5% on CIFAR-100. Against SAM, MSAM achieves slightly lower accuracy on CIFAR-100 (e.g., 83.21% vs 84.16% on WRN-28-10) but runs at 0.99x speed versus SAM's 0.52x, and matches SAM exactly on ImageNet ViT-S/32 (69.1%). Among efficient SAM variants, MSAM outperforms LookSAM on ResNet-50 (82.65% vs 82.24%) and is faster (0.99x vs 0.84x), while LookSAM and MSAM are comparable on WideResNets. MSAM clearly surpasses ESAM on all models (e.g., 83.21% vs 82.71% on WRN-28-10) and is faster (0.99x vs 0.62x). Against MESA, MSAM wins on all CIFAR-100 models (e.g., 83.21% vs 82.75% on WRN-28-10) and is faster (0.99x vs 0.77x), while both achieve 69.0–69.1% on ImageNet ViT-S/32.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Sharpness Aware Minimization (SAM, Foret et al. 2021), which perturbs parameters along the local gradient before computing the update gradient, and on Nesterov Accelerated Gradient (NAG), which computes gradients at a momentum-perturbed position. The key conceptual bridge is recognizing that SAM's gradient-ascent perturbation is functionally a temporary parameter displacement—structurally identical to NAG's momentum step—so the momentum vector itself can serve as the perturbation direction, eliminating the extra forward/backward pass. The paper also positions itself against a growing family of SAM efficiency variants (LookSAM, ESAM, MESA) that reduce the number of perturbation computations, arguing instead that a better perturbation direction is the right lever.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By removing SAM's 2× computational overhead while retaining most of its generalization benefit, MSAM makes sharpness-aware optimization viable under realistic compute budgets, which was the primary barrier to its adoption. The paper's analysis revealing separable mechanisms—NAG improves optimization (training loss) while MSAM improves generalization (test-train gap)—reframes how momentum's role in deep learning is understood. Open questions remain: MSAM achieves flatter minima (lower Hessian eigenvalues, lower feature rank) than SAM yet does not consistently outperform it on generalization, suggesting the sharpness–generalization link is more nuanced than previously assumed; instability at high ρ for large ViTs and the warmup-phase sensitivity also point to unresolved scheduling questions.
