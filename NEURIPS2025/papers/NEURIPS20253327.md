## Problem

Unconditional diffusion models degrade in fidelity as data diversity (number of modes) increases, and existing conditioning representations—text captions, continuous SSL embeddings, or randomly projected embeddings—either lack compositional structure or are difficult to model and sample from. The paper asks whether a discrete, compositional image representation can simultaneously improve generation fidelity, be easy to generate, and enable productive out-of-distribution synthesis.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253327/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The image generator (DiT-XL/2) takes a sequence of discrete tokens (e.g., 512 tokens drawn from a 256-token vocabulary) as conditioning, embedded via a lookup table and averaged into a single vector, and produces 256×256 RGB images in VAE latent space. For compositional generation, two source DLCs are merged by independently sampling each token position from either code, yielding diverse hybrid sequences. For text-to-image, a text prompt is fed to a fine-tuned LLaDA-8B discrete diffusion language model that outputs a DLC, which is then decoded by the frozen image diffusion model.

## Method

The core decomposition is p(x) = Σ_c p(x|c)·p(c), where both factors are tractable. DLCs are inferred by passing images through a DINOv2 ViT-L encoder followed by learnable linear projections onto V-dimensional simplices (Simplicial Embeddings), then taking the argmax of each simplex to obtain discrete tokens—avoiding the gradient estimators required by VQ-VAE-style discretization. The image model is a DiT-XL/2 trained with standard denoising score matching on VAE latents, conditioned on the embedded DLC tokens. The DLC prior p(c) is modeled by SEDD-Absorb, a discrete diffusion model that iteratively unmasks a fully masked sequence, augmented with a novel remasking strategy that re-masks already-unmasked tokens within a time window to escape local minima. For text-to-image, LLaDA-8B is fine-tuned on 9M LAION image-caption pairs with DLC tokens appended to its vocabulary, enabling a modular text→DLC→image pipeline without joint training.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256, DiT-XL/2 conditioned on DLC₅₁₂ achieves a FID of 2.00 without classifier-free guidance (CFG), surpassing the previous SOTA unconditional model MAR-H (FID 2.35) and all label-conditioned baselines (DiT-XL/2: 9.62, SiT-XL/2: 8.61). With CFG, DLC₅₁₂ reaches FID 1.59, outperforming RCG (2.15) and DisCo-Diff (3.70 on 64×64), and closing the gap with the best label-conditioned model REPA (1.42). DLC₅₁₂ also achieves the highest Vendi diversity score (13.2±0.8) for compositional generation, compared to DINOv2 (9.6±1.3) and RCG (4.2±0.9).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DLC₅₁₂ unconditionally (no CFG) achieves FID 2.00, beating MAR-H (2.35), the prior SOTA without guidance, and dramatically outperforming label-conditioned DiT-XL/2 (9.62) and SiT-XL/2 (8.61). With CFG, DLC₅₁₂ (FID 1.59) surpasses RCG (2.15) and DisCo-Diff (3.70 on 64×64), and beats label-conditioned SiT-XL/2 (2.07) and DiT-XL/2 (2.27), though it trails REPA (1.42) by 0.17 FID. Against continuous SSL embedding baselines, DLC₅₁₂ achieves a generative FID of 4.21 versus DINOv2's 37.90 (Table 2), while maintaining a comparable linear probe accuracy (85.3% vs. 85.9%), confirming that the discrete representation preserves semantic structure far better than RCG's randomized projection (0.1% probe accuracy). For compositional generation, DLC₅₁₂ produces significantly more diverse outputs (Vendi 13.2) than DINOv2 averaging (9.6) or RCG (4.2), and qualitatively succeeds where RCG fails to represent both source concepts (e.g., generating a dog with pasta-like fur rather than just pasta). In the text-to-image pipeline, DLC-based generation produces coherent OOD images (e.g., 'teapot on a mountain') that Stable Diffusion 1.5 with IP-Adapter and text conditioning cannot match in diversity or semantic fidelity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on conditioning diffusion models with learned representations (RCG, REPA) by replacing continuous SSL embeddings with long sequences of discrete tokens derived from Simplicial Embeddings. It departs from prior discrete-code approaches (VQ-VAE, MaskGIT, DisCo-Diff) that use short, low-dimensional codes by leveraging state-of-the-art SSL encoders (DINOv2) to produce high-dimensional, expressive token sequences. The key conceptual departure is from RCG's randomly-projected continuous embeddings: while those are easy to model, they lack the compositional structure needed for productive generation, whereas DLCs inherit compositionality from their discrete, token-based nature. The text-to-image pipeline additionally builds on LLaDA (a discrete diffusion language model) to bridge pretrained LLMs with image generation, treating DLC tokens as an extended vocabulary.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's central argument—that the quality and structure of the conditioning representation matters as much as the diffusion process itself—reframes how the field thinks about improving generative models. The SOTA unconditional ImageNet FID (1.59 with CFG, 2.00 without) and the demonstration that discrete token mixing enables diverse compositional generation (where continuous embedding averaging fails) open a path toward productive, out-of-distribution image synthesis. Key limitations include the computational cost of long token sequences, evaluation restricted to ImageNet 256×256, and the compositional generation evidence being primarily qualitative (Vendi score is a weak proxy). Open questions include whether the compositional properties scale to higher resolutions and more complex scenes, whether the text-to-DLC pipeline (trained on only 9M pairs) generalizes beyond ImageNet-adjacent prompts, and whether remasking can fully replace classifier-free guidance as the paper speculates.
