## Problem

Existing 3D VLA models convert actions into token sequences without spatial structure, failing to exploit the spatial priors that make 3D manipulation policies sample-efficient. Additionally, the distributional shift between 2D image inputs used in VLM pre-training and 3D inputs used in action fine-tuning degrades performance. The paper asks whether a unified 3D VLA can combine the language grounding of VLMs with the data efficiency of 3D policies by aligning inputs and outputs in a shared spatial space.

## Input / Output

The model receives RGB-D images from calibrated cameras, reconstructs a 3D point cloud, and renders three orthographic projection images (top, front, right) at the same resolution as the VLM's native image tokens, paired with a free-form language instruction. The output is a 6-DoF end-effector pose in SE(3) (translation via back-projected heatmap peaks, rotation via 72-bin Euler angle discretization), a binary gripper state, and a binary collision-avoidance flag for the next keyframe. A coarse-to-fine strategy performs two forward passes: the first on the full point cloud, the second on a cropped, zoomed-in cuboid centered at the predicted translation.

## Method

BridgeVLA builds on PaliGemma (SigLIP vision encoder + Gemma transformer) and introduces a dual-phase training recipe that aligns inputs and outputs in a unified 2D image space. In pre-training, the VLM is trained on 120K RoboPoint object-detection images to predict 2D heatmaps via a convex upsampling block (borrowed from RAFT) that converts rearranged spatial feature tokens into pixel-level probability maps, replacing the original next-token prediction head. During fine-tuning, three orthographic projections of the 3D point cloud serve as VLM inputs; the model predicts three 2D heatmaps whose peaks are back-projected to score a uniform 3D point grid for translation, while an MLP over max-pooled global tokens and heatmap-peak local tokens predicts rotation, gripper, and collision. Critically, no 3D position encoding or robot state is injected into the VLM forward pass, preserving the input distribution from pre-training. Ablations confirm that heatmap-based prediction (vs. direct position regression) and the absence of 3D position features are both essential for performance.

## Results

On RLBench (18 tasks, 100 demos each), BridgeVLA achieves 88.2% average success rate, surpassing the previous best RVT-2 (81.4%) and 3D Diffuser Actor (81.3%). On COLOSSEUM (12 perturbation axes), it improves the average success rate from 56.7% (RVT-2) to 64.0%. On GemBench (hierarchical generalization), it reaches 50.0% average, outperforming 3D-LOTUS+ (48.0%). In real-robot experiments, BridgeVLA achieves 96.9% on the basic setting and outperforms RVT-2 by 32% on average across 7 settings; remarkably, it maintains 95.4% success with only 3 trajectories per task.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BridgeVLA outperforms all 11 baselines on RLBench, with the largest margins over 3D Diffuser Actor (+6.9%) and RVT-2 (+6.8%), and especially on precision-critical tasks like Insert Peg (88.0% vs. 40.0% for RVT-2) and Sort Shape (60.8% vs. 35.0%). On COLOSSEUM, it beats RVT-2 by 7.3% average and ranks first in 13 of 14 perturbation settings, though it trails RVT-2 on the Distractor axis (51.8% vs. 60.8%). On GemBench, BridgeVLA leads in L2 (65.0% vs. 64.5% for 3D-LOTUS+) and L3 (43.8% vs. 41.5%), but all methods including BridgeVLA struggle on L4 long-horizon tasks (0.0%). In real-robot experiments, BridgeVLA (96.9%) surpasses RVT-2 (90.0%) on the basic setting, while 2D VLA methods (π₀ at 3.8%, SpatialVLA at 28.5% with 50 demos) and ACT (22.3%) fail catastrophically with only 10 trajectories per task. The 3-trajectory experiment (95.4%) further confirms that BridgeVLA's input-output alignment design is the key differentiator, as π₀ and SpatialVLA fail completely under the same data budget.

## Relation to Prior Work

BridgeVLA sits at the intersection of two research lines: 3D manipulation policies (RVT, RVT-2, Act3D, 3D Diffuser Actor) that exploit spatial structure for sample efficiency, and VLA models (π₀, SpatialVLA, OpenVLA) that leverage pre-trained vision-language backbones for semantic generalization. It departs from prior 3D VLA work (SpatialVLA, PointVLA, 3D-VLA) by rejecting the common strategy of injecting 3D positional encodings or tokenizing actions into sequences; instead, it projects 3D point clouds into multi-view 2D images and predicts 2D heatmaps as intermediate outputs, keeping both input and output in the same spatial domain as the VLM's pre-training. The convex upsampling module from RAFT is repurposed for heatmap generation, and the coarse-to-fine refinement strategy follows RVT-2.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The input-output alignment principle—keeping observations and actions in a shared 2D spatial structure—offers a concrete design recipe for building 3D VLAs that are both data-efficient and semantically grounded, addressing a key bottleneck in current VLA research where hundreds of trajectories per task are typically required. The finding that adding per-pixel 3D position features to the VLM backbone actually degrades performance (88.2%→56.2%) challenges the intuitive assumption that more 3D information is always better, suggesting distributional alignment with pre-training is more critical. Open questions include whether the approach scales to long-horizon tasks (L4 performance remains near zero), whether richer pre-training (segmentation, keypoint detection) can improve category generalization, and whether the fixed three-view projection can be made adaptive to handle occlusions.
