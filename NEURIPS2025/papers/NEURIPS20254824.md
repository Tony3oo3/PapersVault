## Problem

Existing Auxiliary Modality Learning (AML) frameworks enable training with full modalities and inference with reduced ones, but are restricted to single-agent settings where data coverage is inherently limited by occlusions and sensor range. Conversely, multi-agent collaboration frameworks assume consistent modality availability at both training and test time. No prior work unifies multi-agent collaboration with modality-reduced inference, leaving a critical gap for resource-constrained, dynamic environments like connected autonomous vehicles.

## Input / Output

During training, each agent (ego vehicle plus up to 3 collaborative vehicles within 150 m, or an aerial–ground robot pair) provides RGB images resized to 224×224 and LiDAR point clouds (or depth maps for segmentation). At test time, only RGB is available per agent, and the number of agents may differ from training (e.g., multi-agent training → single-agent deployment). The system outputs a binary braking decision (CAV) or a pixel-wise semantic segmentation mask (aerial-ground), with the student model producing predictions solely from the reduced-modality observations.

## Method

CAML employs a teacher–student knowledge distillation pipeline in a multi-agent setting. The teacher is trained with full modalities (RGB via ResNet-18 + self-attention, LiDAR via Point Transformer) from all agents, fusing same-modality embeddings across agents via cross-attention or concatenation before a prediction head. The student mirrors this architecture but each agent processes only a subset of modalities (e.g., RGB only); agents still share their embeddings so the collective system retains multi-modal coverage. Distillation uses a temperature-scaled KL divergence (t = 4.0) between teacher and student soft targets, weighted (α = 0.9) with the task loss (binary cross-entropy for CAV, cross-entropy for segmentation). The framework supports both centralized (all agents report to one ego) and decentralized (neighbor-to-neighbor) topologies, and the agent count at test time is decoupled from training, enabling flexible deployment.

## Results

On the AutoCastSim CAV benchmark, CAML (RGB-only at test time) achieves up to 58.1% higher Accident Detection Rate than single-agent AML in the red-light-violation scenario, and up to 21.5% higher ADR than COOPERNAUT. On the CoPeD aerial-ground segmentation dataset, CAML reaches 60.1% mIoU (indoor) and 66.8% mIoU (outdoor), surpassing AML by 4.2% and 6.5% respectively. CAML also operates with substantially lower communication overhead (1.0 KB shared package size) and inference latency (3.7 ms) compared to COOPERNAUT (65.5 KB, 90 ms) and STGN (4.9 KB, 18.5 ms).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against AML [41] (single-agent, RGB+LiDAR training, RGB-only testing), CAML wins on every scenario: +13.0% ADR in overtaking, +34.6% in left-turn, and +58.1% in red-light violation for CAV, and +4.2%/+6.5% mIoU for indoor/outdoor segmentation. Against COOPERNAUT [5] (collaborative LiDAR-only), CAML achieves up to 21.5% higher ADR while using only RGB at test time, and reduces communication bandwidth from 65.5 KB to 1.0 KB with latency dropping from 90 ms to 3.7 ms. Against STGN [8] (collaborative RGBD), CAML matches or exceeds performance despite having one fewer modality at test time (RGB vs. RGB+depth), exceeding STGN's mean ADR by 10.9% in the left-turn scenario while using 5× less communication (1.0 KB vs. 4.9 KB). Against FCN [32] (ground-robot-only, RGB for both training and testing), CAML improves mIoU by 8.9% (indoor) and 10.6% (outdoor), demonstrating the combined benefit of multi-agent collaboration and auxiliary depth data during training.

## Relation to Prior Work

CAML unifies two previously separate research threads: Auxiliary Modality Learning (AML) [Shen et al., ICML 2023], which handles missing modalities at test time but only in single-agent settings, and multi-agent collaborative perception (Coopernaut, STGN), which assumes all modalities remain available at inference. The paper departs from both by introducing a joint multi-agent + reduced-modality formulation, using knowledge distillation [Hinton, 2015] as the bridge: a teacher trained with full multi-modal, multi-agent data distills into a student that operates on a subset of modalities per agent. The architectural components (ResNet-18, Point Transformer, cross-attention, FCN) are standard; the novelty lies in the training protocol and the flexible agent-modality configuration at test time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CAML addresses a practical deployment gap in connected autonomous vehicles and collaborative robotics where sensor availability is inconsistent across agents and over time. The framework's flexibility (varying number of agents and modalities between train and test) makes it applicable to resource-constrained settings, and the reported efficiency (1.0 KB communication, 3.7 ms latency) is attractive for real-time V2V systems. Open questions include: whether the approach scales beyond 3–4 agents and 2–3 modalities, how it handles dynamically changing modality availability over time (acknowledged as a limitation), and whether the gains hold on larger, more diverse datasets beyond the 24-trail AutoCastSim scenarios and two CoPeD environments.
