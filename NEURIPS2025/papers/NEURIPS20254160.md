## Problem

Existing federated recommendation systems assume individual users as participants, but in joint venture ecosystems (e.g., Alibaba's business groups), participants are subsidiaries with large, partially overlapping user and item bases. In this setting, sharing only item embeddings is insufficient for performance, while directly uploading user embeddings violates privacy. The paper tackles how to transfer user-level knowledge across clients in a privacy-preserving manner without the performance-privacy trade-off inherent in differential privacy.

## Input / Output

Each client holds a local user-item interaction matrix with one-hot representations that are first transformed into differentiable softmax form before model inversion. The method consumes federated item embeddings (aggregated via FedAvg) and a distilled rating preference vector per user (a weighted combination of item embeddings), and produces rating predictions (evaluated by MAE/RMSE) and top-K item recommendations (evaluated by HR@K and NDCG@K). The rating preference is the only user-derived signal that leaves the client, making it the critical privacy boundary.

## Method

FR-JVE operates in three stages. Before federated training, each client transforms its one-hot user/item data into a differentiable format via softmax of uniform random variables, then applies model inversion with frozen user gradients to distill a per-user rating preference (a weighted sum of item embeddings). During federated training, only item embeddings are communicated via FedAvg, while a locally trained bridge function (a linear layer) maps the server-aggregated rating preferences from other clients onto the local rating preference space. At inference, each client performs a top-K search over similar users using the bridge-mapped preferences and averages their predicted ratings for the final recommendation. The key insight is that the distilled rating preference serves as a privacy-friendly collaborative filtering signal that captures user characteristics without exposing the raw user embedding, and the bridge function aligns cross-client preference spaces without requiring direct user-embedding exchange.

## Results

On rating prediction, FR-JVE achieves MAE of 1.1765 and RMSE of 1.4725 on MovieLens-100K, and MAE of 1.0150 and RMSE of 1.2210 on MovieLens-1M, outperforming all baselines. On top-K recommendation, FR-JVE reaches HR@10 of 0.6578 and NDCG@10 of 0.4684 on LastFM-2K, and HR@10 of 0.8635 and NDCG@10 of 0.5141 on QB-article, setting new state-of-the-art across all four datasets. The method also converges in fewer than 20 iterations, significantly faster than FedRAP which requires more rounds to reach comparable performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FR-JVE consistently outperforms all seven baselines across both rating prediction and top-K recommendation tasks. Against the strongest user-based federated baselines, FR-JVE improves MAE from 1.2503 (PFedRec) and 1.3248 (FedRAP) to 1.1765 on ML-100K, and from 1.0912 (FedRAP) to 1.0150 on ML-1M. The federated CDR baselines (FedCDR, P2FCDR) lag substantially, with P2FCDR achieving MAE of 1.4178 on ML-100K versus FR-JVE's 1.1765. The differential-privacy-based baselines (FedMF-w/DP, EMCDR-w/FL+DP) show the clearest performance-privacy trade-off: EMCDR-w/FL+DP reaches MAE 1.2693 at ε=0.2 but degrades to 1.8382 at ε=0.08, while FR-JVE avoids this trade-off entirely by using distilled rating preferences instead of raw user embeddings. On top-K recommendation, FR-JVE surpasses FedRAP (HR@10: 0.6578 vs. 0.6010 on LastFM-2K) and PFedRec (0.6578 vs. 0.5818), with the gap widening on QB-article (0.8635 vs. 0.8276 for FedRAP). The ablation confirms that removing the rating preference component causes the largest degradation (MAE jumps from 1.1765 to 2.9596), while removing the bridge function has a smaller but still significant effect (MAE 2.4200).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends federated recommendation from the standard user-level setting (where individual users are FL participants) to a joint venture ecosystem where subsidiaries or business groups participate, each holding large user-item datasets with partial overlaps. It builds on FedAvg for item embedding aggregation and adapts the bridge function mechanism from cross-domain recommendation (EMCDR) to map aggregated rating preferences across clients. The key departure from prior work is replacing direct user embedding sharing (or its DP-perturbed variant) with a distilled rating preference constructed via model inversion with frozen user gradients, positioning user information as a transferable collaborative filtering signal rather than a raw embedding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The joint venture ecosystem framing (e.g., Alibaba's Alipay, Tmall, and entertainment groups) is practically relevant and underexplored in the FedRS literature, where most work assumes individual users as participants. The rating preference as a privacy-friendly transfer signal offers a middle ground between the performance loss of DP-perturbed embeddings and the privacy risk of raw embedding sharing, though the privacy analysis in Appendix B is informal (relying on the hardness of solving multi-variable linear equations). Open questions include scalability beyond ~10 clients, applicability to more complex interaction models beyond matrix factorization, and whether the linear bridge function suffices for real-world heterogeneous data distributions.
