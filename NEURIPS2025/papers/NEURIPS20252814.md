## Problem

Existing universal image fusion methods require task-specific retraining and their continual-learning modules merely average knowledge across scenes, limiting true generality. Meanwhile, degradation-robust fusion approaches depend heavily on paired clean/degraded training data and typically target only specific degradation types. The paper addresses both gaps by proposing a single training-free framework that generalizes across fusion tasks and handles diverse degradations without any supervised training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252814/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts pairs (or sequences) of same-size images in any modality combination—e.g., infrared + visible, multi-exposure, or multi-focus—and produces a single fused image at the same spatial resolution. It operates entirely in the latent space of a pre-trained VAE (Stable Diffusion), requiring no task-specific training or fine-tuning. Notably, it can fuse more than two source images in a single pass with negligible additional runtime, whereas competing methods must process images sequentially in pairs.

## Method

PDFuse reformulates image fusion as a conditional diffusion posterior sampling process on a pre-trained latent diffusion model, where the observation constraint is the multi-source image set. Two complementary regularization mechanisms are alternated at each denoising step: (1) the Multi-source Information Consistency Projection (MICP) decomposes the clean-signal estimate into a coarse structural component and a fine detail component, each obtained by solving a linear projection system via conjugate gradients, thereby injecting pixel-level fidelity from all sources; (2) the Latent Manifold-preservation Guidance (LMG) backpropagates a fusion loss through the VAE decoder and adds a latent-space regularizer to keep the diffusion trajectory on the data manifold, preserving generative quality. A canonical interference operator set (blur, over/under-exposure, downsampling, etc.) is embedded into both mechanisms so that degradations are inverted through the diffusion prior without any clean-reference supervision.

## Results

On the LLVIP infrared-visible fusion benchmark, PDFuse-B achieves the best mean rank (1.5) across all eight metrics, with EI=48.52, SF=11.03, AG=4.22, and Qcb=0.437, outperforming the strongest general-purpose baseline TC-MoA (Rank 2.3) and the strongest task-specific baseline DCEvo (Rank 3.9). The enhanced variant PDFuse-E, which integrates the degradation operator set, achieves EI=140.30, SF=35.08, AG=13.63, and Qcb=0.476, dominating all degradation-aware baselines (best competitor DDBF: EI=55.11, SF=13.08). On MEFB and MFFW, PDFuse-B similarly attains the top mean rank (1.3 and 1.8 respectively), leading on EI, SF, AG, and Qcb across both tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PDFuse-B outperforms all seven non-degradation baselines on EI, SF, AG, SD, and Qcb, with the largest margins over U2Fusion (+20.3 EI) and DeFusion (+18.9 EI); it trails only TC-MoA on SCD (0.981 vs. 1.013) and DCEvo on SCD (0.981 vs. 0.953 is actually a win, so it trails only TC-MoA on SCD). Against the five degradation-aware methods, PDFuse-E dominates on EI (140.3 vs. best 55.1 for DDBF), SF (35.1 vs. 13.1), AG (13.6 vs. 4.8), and Qcb (0.476 vs. 0.413), though it scores lower on SCD (0.824 vs. 1.255 for Text-DiFuse). On MEFB, PDFuse-B leads HSDS-MEF by +5.6 EI and +1.3 SF but loses on SCD to CCF (0.774 vs. 1.163). On MFFW, PDFuse-B surpasses DB-MFIF on EI (+6.7), AG (+0.1), SD (+5.3), TE (6.99 vs. 7.05), and SCD (0.894 vs. 0.677), but trails on SF (22.5 vs. 24.0) and Qcb (0.576 vs. 0.662). In the semantic segmentation verification on FMB, PDFuse-E achieves the highest mIoU (63.70) and mAcc (71.77), edging out MRFS (63.00/71.16) and Text-DiFuse (62.61/70.98).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the diffusion posterior sampling framework (Chung et al., ICLR 2023) and the latent diffusion model architecture (Rombach et al., CVPR 2022) to image fusion, departing from prior diffusion-based fusion methods (DDFM, CCF) that were confined to pixel-space DDPMs. It also diverges from universal fusion approaches (U2Fusion, TC-MoA) that rely on continual learning or adapter retraining per task, instead leveraging the generative prior of a frozen pre-trained LDM with no task-specific training. The core methodological novelty lies in the alternating projection-manifold regularization: a conjugate-gradient-solved two-stage projection (MICP) for fidelity injection, combined with a latent-space gradient guidance (LMG) for manifold preservation, a combination not present in prior diffusion-based fusion or restoration work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that a single frozen latent diffusion model can serve as a universal, training-free fusion engine across infrared-visible, multi-exposure, and multi-focus tasks while also handling degradations without clean-data supervision, this work opens a path toward truly general-purpose fusion that avoids per-task retraining. Key limitations include the ~18-second runtime per image (impractical for real-time applications), the VAE's inherent high-frequency loss (mitigated but not eliminated by the residual injection strategy), and the fact that the degradation operator set is hand-curated rather than learned. Open questions include whether the approach extends to video fusion, whether the alternating schedule can be optimized for fewer diffusion steps, and whether the interference operator framework generalizes to arbitrary unknown degradations.
