## Problem

For nonconvex simple bilevel optimization, where the upper-level objective is minimized over the solution set of a nonconvex lower-level problem, no prior first-order algorithm provided discrete-time convergence guarantees for joint stationarity at both levels. Existing analyses were either limited to continuous-time limits (DBGD) or only addressed the lower-level objective (BLOOP), leaving open whether polynomial-time first-order methods can certify stationarity for both the upper and lower objectives simultaneously.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes two smooth (Lipschitz-continuous gradient) scalar objectives f and g defined on ℝⁿ, along with an initial point x₀, and requires only first-order gradient evaluations of both functions per iteration. It outputs an iterate x_{k*} that is an (ε_f, ε_g)-stationary point, meaning there exists λ ≥ 0 such that ‖∇g(x_{k*})‖² ≤ ε_g and ‖∇f(x_{k*}) + λ∇g(x_{k*})‖² ≤ ε_f. The stationarity metric is notable because λ may be unbounded (scaling as O(1/‖∇g‖)), so the guarantee is on the combined norm rather than on ‖∇f‖ alone, reflecting the trade-off structure of the bilevel problem.

## Method

The paper analyzes a variant of Dynamic Barrier Gradient Descent (DBGD) where the descent direction d_k is chosen as the closest vector to ∇f(x_k) subject to the barrier constraint ∇g(x_k)ᵀd_k ≥ β_k‖∇g(x_k)‖², yielding the closed-form update x_{k+1} = x_k − η_k(∇f(x_k) + λ_k∇g(x_k)) with λ_k = max{(β_k‖∇g(x_k)‖² − ∇f(x_k)ᵀ∇g(x_k))/‖∇g(x_k)‖², 0}. The central technical difficulty is that λ_k diverges as ‖∇g(x_k)‖→0, preventing a standard telescoping argument with a fixed potential. The authors resolve this by constructing a potential function G_k = ½‖d_k‖² + (β/(L_g η))‖∇g(x_k)‖² and bounding the problematic λ_k‖∇g‖² term via the bounded-gradient assumption, which reduces it to β‖∇g‖² + G_f‖∇g‖. An implicit inequality of the form x ≤ A + B√x is then resolved explicitly (Lemma 5.4), and the step size η = 1/(LK^{1/(3+p)}) and barrier parameter β = 1/K^{p/(3+p)} are tuned to balance the resulting terms, yielding the O(max(ε_f^{−(3+p)/(1+p)}, ε_g^{−(3+p)/2})) iteration complexity.

## Results

The main theoretical contribution is a discrete-time complexity bound of O(max(ε_f^(-(3+p)/(1+p)), ε_g^(-(3+p)/2))) iterations to find an (ε_f, ε_g)-stationary point for nonconvex simple bilevel optimization, where p ≥ 0 is a free parameter. With the balanced choice p = 1 (i.e., β = O(η)), this simplifies to O(max(ε_f^{-2}, ε_g^{-2})), which is the first explicit non-asymptotic guarantee for joint stationarity at both levels in this setting. Empirically, on a 2D toy problem and a matrix factorization problem, DBGD consistently reduces both ‖∇g‖² and ‖∇_⊥ f‖² faster than penalty methods across a wide range of hyperparameters, and drives the angle between ∇f and ∇g toward 180° (indicating no further local improvement is possible).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares DBGD against a penalty-based method with update x_{k+1} = x_k − η_k(∇f(x_k) + λ∇g(x_k)) where λ ≥ 0 is fixed. On the 2D toy problem (Problem 24), DBGD outperforms the penalty method across all three stationarity metrics (‖∇g‖², ‖∇_⊥ f‖², and cos θ) regardless of the penalty parameter λ; the penalty method's lower-level stationarity plateaus at a nonzero value while DBGD continues to decrease it. On the matrix factorization problem (Problem 25), DBGD achieves lower reconstruction loss g(U) and sparsity penalty f(U) simultaneously across a wide range of β values, whereas the penalty method is highly sensitive to λ and often produces suboptimal trade-offs between the two objectives. The paper also positions its result against BLOOP [5], which provides only a lower-level rate of O(K^{-1/4}) with no upper-level guarantee, and against the continuous-time analysis of Gong et al. [3], which does not extend to discrete time and requires bounded dual iterates that are violated in the simple bilevel setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Dynamic Barrier Gradient Descent (DBGD) framework of Gong et al. (NeurIPS 2021), which was originally analyzed only in the continuous-time limit for constrained optimization, to the nonconvex simple bilevel setting with the first discrete-time stationarity guarantees. It departs from prior convex simple bilevel methods (Tikhonov regularization, lower-level linearization) that rely on convexity, and from general nonconvex bilevel methods (BOME, BLOOP) that require PL conditions or define stationarity via hyper-gradients that are ill-defined in the simple bilevel setting. The paper also unifies the DBGD framework with orthogonal projection methods (BLOOP) and lower-level linearization methods by showing they are special cases of the same subproblem structure with different barrier functions φ.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first explicit discrete-time complexity bound guaranteeing joint stationarity at both levels for nonconvex simple bilevel optimization, filling a gap where prior results were either continuous-time only, single-level, or required additional structural assumptions like PL conditions. The stationarity metric bridges constrained optimization (unscaled KKT) and bilevel optimization (gradient-based reformulation) literature, providing a unified first-order notion. Open questions include extending the analysis to stochastic settings, removing the bounded-gradient assumption on f (Assumption 2.1(i)), and understanding whether the O(ε^{-2}) rate (with p=1) is optimal. The diverging multiplier λ_k as ∇g→0, while handled theoretically via the gradient-based reformulation, remains a practical concern for finite-precision implementations.
