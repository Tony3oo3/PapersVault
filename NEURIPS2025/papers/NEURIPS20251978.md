## Problem

No prior work has developed an efficient algorithm for the adversarial contextual bandit problem that guarantees exact statistical parity in every selected policy, even in the simplified setting of a finite, unstructured context space with i.i.d. instances. A direct modification of EXP4 to enforce parity would require computing a relative-entropy projection onto the constrained policy set, which is analytically intractable due to the exponential size of the expert space.

## Input / Output

On each trial the algorithm receives a protected characteristic c_t and context v_t, selects an action a_t, and then observes only the scalar bandit loss ℓ_t(a_t) for that single action. It also takes as input a parity distribution ρ over C×V (or estimates it online from empirical data), and an inductive bias θ over experts that defines the HEDGE instance. The output is a policy π_t* that satisfies exact statistical parity with respect to ρ on every trial, with a regret bound asymptotically identical to running EXP4 independently for each protected characteristic. The algorithm is a meta-algorithm: it wraps any efficient HEDGE implementation (e.g., belief propagation on a tree) and inherits its per-trial time complexity.

## Method

SPEW avoids the intractable projection problem by modifying HEDGE's inputs and outputs rather than its internal mirror-descent step. It maintains M independent HEDGE instances (one per protected characteristic) that track a 'raw policy' ξ_t which need not satisfy parity. Each trial, SPEW converts ξ_t into a fair policy π_t by computing a per-group, per-action disparity δ_t(c,a) = max_c' ω_t(c',a) − ω_t(c,a), adding this correction to the raw probabilities, and renormalizing with a uniform residual to ensure the result lies on the simplex. The update step uses an unbiased pseudo-gradient (borrowed from EXP4's single-action feedback trick) of a convex surrogate y_t that upper-bounds the expected loss at the raw policy and lower-bounds it at any fair policy, with an additional term β_t measuring the maximum cross-group disparity. This drives exponentiated gradient descent on the expert distributions to simultaneously minimize loss and reduce parity violations, yielding a regret bound of Õ((η + Φ/η)√(KT)) that matches EXP4's asymptotic rate.

## Results

SPEW achieves a regret bound of O((η + Φ/η)√(KT)) against any constant policy satisfying statistical parity, matching the asymptotic regret of running EXP4 independently for each protected group. For the tree-structured context setting (Theorem 5.1), the regret is Õ((η + (M + Ψ(π̃)ln N)/η)√(KT)) with per-trial time and space complexity O(MNK). In the empirical parity setting (Theorem 5.2), the bound becomes Õ((η + (MN + Ψ(π̃))/η)√(KT)), and for hierarchical clusterings with unknown distribution (Theorem 5.3), the per-trial complexity drops to O(KT), independent of the context set cardinality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines or datasets. Conceptually, SPEW matches the asymptotic regret of EXP4 [2] while additionally guaranteeing exact statistical parity on every trial, which EXP4 does not provide. Compared to constrained online optimization approaches [8, 34] that allow cumulative constraint violations of O(T^{3/4}) or O(T^{1/2}), SPEW enforces the parity constraint exactly at each step with no violation. The paper also contrasts its approach with direct Lagrangian/mirror-descent projections onto the parity-constrained set, arguing that such projections are computationally intractable due to the exponential expert space, whereas SPEW avoids this by modifying HEDGE's inputs and outputs rather than its internal geometry.

## Relation to Prior Work

SPEW is a meta-algorithm that transforms any efficient HEDGE implementation (e.g., via Belief Propagation) into an adversarial contextual bandit algorithm guaranteeing exact per-trial statistical parity, borrowing the unbiased gradient estimation technique from EXP4 and the online-to-batch conversion framework of Cesa-Bianchi et al. The key conceptual departure from prior constrained-optimization approaches (which allow cumulative constraint violations of O(T^{3/4}) or O(T^{1/2})) is that SPEW enforces the parity constraint exactly on every trial without modifying HEDGE's mirror descent; instead it maintains an unconstrained 'raw policy' internally and maps it to a fair policy externally, sidestepping the intractable relative-entropy projection onto the exponentially large constrained expert space. The paper also draws on Herbster & Warmuth's tracking methodology for empirical-distribution parity and Pasteris et al.'s hierarchical nearest-neighbour framework for approximate parity in large context spaces.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first efficient algorithm to guarantee exact statistical parity on every trial in the adversarial contextual bandit setting while matching EXP4's asymptotic regret, filling a gap the authors explicitly identify as unsolved even in the simplest finite i.i.d. case. The meta-algorithm design means the approach inherits efficiency from any compatible HEDGE implementation and extends naturally to unknown parity distributions (empirical, hierarchical) and to batch classification via standard conversion. Open questions include whether the input/output transformation trick generalises to other fairness notions (equalized odds, individual fairness), to other base algorithms beyond HEDGE (e.g., CBA with confidence-rated advice), and whether the per-trial exactness requirement can be relaxed to yield tighter regret in practice.
