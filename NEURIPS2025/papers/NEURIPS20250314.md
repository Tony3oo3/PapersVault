## Problem

Improving LLM reasoning capabilities typically demands either human-labeled data for supervised fine-tuning or computationally expensive multi-sample rejection sampling (e.g., 16 candidates per question in RFT). This paper asks whether the minimal initial tokens of a single model-generated response—requiring no labels and no filtering—contain sufficient signal to meaningfully improve reasoning, motivated by the observation that errors concentrate in later reasoning steps while early prefixes remain highly consistent across trajectories.

## Input / Output

At training time, the method consumes a text question and a single model-generated response, but only the first t tokens (8 for Llama-3.1-8B, 32 for Qwen2.5-Math-7B, 128 for DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B) are used as the fine-tuning target, reducing sequence length by 82–95% versus full-trace SFT. A specialized prompt template instructs the model to produce 'the initial step towards resolving the question,' and a small fraction (10–30%) of full-trace samples are mixed in to preserve output format. At inference, the model generates a complete reasoning trace via greedy decoding in a zero-shot setting.

## Method

UPFT (Unsupervised Prefix Fine-Tuning) is grounded in a Bayesian decomposition of the SFT objective into a prefix-coverage term (the prior probability of generating a given prefix) and a prefix-accuracy term (the expected log-likelihood of the correct answer conditioned on that prefix). The training procedure decodes only t tokens from the base model per question, then performs standard NLL fine-tuning on those prefix substrings—no rejection sampling, no ground-truth verification, and no multi-sample generation. To prevent catastrophic forgetting of the model's full-response format, a structure-tuning component jointly optimizes on a small ratio (p=10%) of full-length traces using the same unsupervised single-sample protocol. The key differentiator from RFT or STaR is that UPFT requires exactly one generated sample per question and no posterior filtering, reducing sampling cost by ~99% and training time by ~75% while matching or exceeding supervised baselines on mathematical and general reasoning benchmarks.

## Results

Under the supervised sampling setting on PRM-12K, UPFT achieves 38.3% average accuracy on Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct (matching V-STaR and within 0.2 pts of RFT's 38.5%), 52.6% on Qwen2.5-Math-7B-Instruct (tied with RFT), and 58.7% on DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B (surpassing RFT's 57.2% by 1.5 pts). In the unsupervised setting, UPFT consistently outperforms full-token SFT, e.g., reaching 54.5% average on Qwen2.5-Math-7B with U-Hard vs. 51.3% for SFT, and 61.6% vs. 56.4% for DeepSeek-R1. UPFT requires only 0.2–5.0M sampling tokens versus 36.9–318.0M for supervised baselines, a 99% reduction in sampling cost, while training on sequences 82–95% shorter than SFT.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against RFT (Rejection Sampling Fine-Tuning), UPFT matches performance on Llama-3.1-8B (38.3% vs. 38.5%) and Qwen2.5-Math-7B (52.6% vs. 52.6%), while surpassing it on DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B (58.7% vs. 57.2%), all while using 99% fewer sampling tokens (e.g., 5.0M vs. 318.0M for DeepSeek). Against V-STaR, UPFT ties on Llama-3.1-8B (38.3% vs. 38.3%) and is within 0.3 pts on Qwen2.5-Math-7B (52.6% vs. 52.9%), again with a 99% reduction in sampling cost (0.6M vs. 51.7M tokens). In the unsupervised setting (Table 1), UPFT outperforms conventional SFT across all model–dataset combinations, with the largest gains on AIME2024 (e.g., 26.6% vs. 16.7% for Qwen2.5-Math-7B with U-Hard; 50.0% vs. 36.7% for DeepSeek-R1). When label filtering is optionally added to UPFT, it further improves to 58.8% on DeepSeek-R1, exceeding all baselines while still using 4.4× fewer tuning tokens than RFT.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from Rejection Sampling Fine-Tuning (RFT) [33] and the broader self-improvement lineage (STaR [34], V-STaR [11], ReST^EM [25]) by eliminating both the multi-sample generation and the correctness-verification steps. Where RFT requires 16 sampled trajectories per question filtered by ground-truth answers, UPFT uses a single unfiltered response and trains only on its initial prefix (8–128 tokens), justified by a Bayesian decomposition of the SFT objective into prefix-coverage and prefix-accuracy terms. The key conceptual departure is the identification of 'Prefix Self-Consistency'—the empirical observation that early reasoning tokens are statistically indistinguishable across correct and incorrect trajectories—shifting the learning signal from full correct traces to shared initial setup tokens.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: matching RFT performance with 75% less training time and 99% less sampling cost makes reasoning fine-tuning accessible in settings where ground-truth labels or reliable verifiers are unavailable. However, the method is validated only on 7–8B math-specialized and general-purpose models, uses a heuristic (fixed) prefix length per model, and the gains over plain unsupervised SFT are modest (2–5 points). Open questions include whether the prefix-length sweet spot generalizes to larger models (32B+), whether adaptive per-sample prefix selection can further improve results, and whether the phenomenon extends beyond mathematical reasoning to broader commonsense or multi-step logical tasks.
