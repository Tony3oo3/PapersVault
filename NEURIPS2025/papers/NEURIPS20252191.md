## Problem

Chain-of-thought reasoning in LLMs is computationally expensive due to verbose intermediate token generation, and existing latent-space reasoning methods (Coconut, CCoT, pause tokens) treat all reasoning steps uniformly—either compressing every step into latent space or extending every step with extra computation. This uniform treatment fails to distinguish critical deductions from trivial auxiliary steps, resulting in suboptimal allocation of computational resources across reasoning steps of varying complexity.

## Input / Output

The model takes a text question (a grade-school math word problem or a multi-hop commonsense yes/no question) and produces only the final answer (a numerical value or yes/no label) without generating any explicit intermediate reasoning tokens. During training, the student model receives the teacher's last-layer hidden states over the CoT reasoning segment as input (enabling teacher-forcing), while at inference it relies solely on a fixed number of latent reasoning steps (λ_step) and the depth exit threshold (λ_depth) to control computation, with no new language tokens introduced after the question.

## Method

System-1.5 Reasoning introduces two orthogonal dynamic shortcuts in latent space: a depth shortcut (DS) that inserts a router-adapter module (FFN + sigmoid router, LoRA adapter) at each Transformer layer to decide per-token whether to exit early via a lightweight branch or continue through deeper layers, and a step shortcut (SS) that copies the early-exited hidden state directly into the next decoding step at the same layer, bypassing reprocessing from layer 1. Training proceeds in two stages: first, language-to-latent alignment distills a CoT-trained teacher's hidden states into a latent-space student via MSE consistency loss plus NLL on the final answer only; second, with Transformer parameters frozen, shortcut learning trains only the router-adapter modules using a criticality-weighted early-exit loss, where criticality labels are derived from atomic thought decomposition (DAG of subquestions: independent nodes = non-critical, derived nodes = critical), encouraging non-critical tokens to exit at shallow layers and critical tokens to traverse deeper layers.

## Results

On GSM8K, System-1.5 Reasoning achieves 46.66% accuracy with only 2 latent decoding steps, matching CoT fine-tuning (46.94%) while accelerating inference by 20.27× and reducing token generation by 92.31%. On GSM-HARD, it reaches 38.28% accuracy (vs. CoT's 38.32%) with a 12.45× speedup. On StrategyQA, System-1.5 outperforms CoT (48.61% vs. 47.62%) with a 55.65× speedup and 96.15% token reduction, demonstrating that latent-space shortcut reasoning can match or exceed explicit CoT while using a fraction of the computation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

System-1.5 Reasoning matches CoT fine-tuning on mathematical reasoning (46.66% vs. 46.94% on GSM8K; 38.28% vs. 38.32% on GSM-HARD) while achieving 20.27× and 12.45× speedups respectively, and outperforms CoT on StrategyQA (48.61% vs. 47.62%) with a 55.65× speedup. Among latent-space compressed reasoning methods, System-1.5 substantially outperforms iCoT (32.14%), Coconut (36.75%), and CODI (43.78%) on GSM8K accuracy, while maintaining the same 2-step latent decoding budget. Compared to the pause token approach, which delays output at every step, System-1.5 achieves higher accuracy on GSM8K (46.66% vs. 46.32%) and GSM-HARD (38.28% vs. 38.17%) with far fewer decoding steps (2 vs. 38), and a 20.27× speedup versus a 0.8× slowdown. Against early-exit methods LITE and LayerSkip, System-1.5 improves accuracy by 2.15 and 3.46 points on GSM8K respectively, while also achieving a 1.95× per-step FLOPs reduction through its step shortcut mechanism—something these methods cannot do since they still reprocess from the first layer at each decoding step.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper synthesizes two previously separate lines of work—latent-space compressed reasoning (Coconut, CCoT, iCoT, CODI) and conditional computation via early exit (LITE, LayerSkip, DeeBERT)—into a unified adaptive framework. The key departure is the introduction of a step shortcut that copies hidden states across decoding steps, a mechanism absent from both the latent-reasoning and early-exit literatures, which only optimize along the vertical (depth) dimension. The two-stage distillation (language-to-latent alignment followed by shortcut learning with criticality-guided early-exit loss) extends the self-distillation paradigm of CODI by adding a second compression stage that exploits atomic thought decomposition to label step criticality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 20×+ inference speedup while matching CoT accuracy is practically significant for deploying reasoning LLMs at scale, and the step shortcut offers a general architectural primitive that could be combined with other latent-reasoning approaches. However, the evaluation is limited to small models (GPT-2 124M, LLaMA 3.2 1B) and two task families, leaving open whether the adaptive shortcut mechanism scales to larger models or more diverse reasoning tasks. The interpretability cost of operating entirely in latent space remains a fundamental tension for safety-critical applications, and the paper acknowledges this without offering a mitigation.
