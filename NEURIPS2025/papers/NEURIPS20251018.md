## Problem

Estimating optical flow and 6-DoF egomotion from event camera data is inherently ill-posed due to the absence of reliable long-term data association in sparse, asynchronous event streams. Existing approaches either enforce explicit variational smoothness regularizers (introducing bias and computational overhead) or jointly estimate depth to provide geometric constraints (expanding the parameterization space and risking convergence to local minima). This paper seeks an unsupervised framework that resolves both tasks simultaneously without explicit regularization terms or depth estimation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251018/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes raw asynchronous event streams (pixel coordinates and timestamps with polarity), partitioned into short segments of 30k events (MVSEC) or 300k events (DSEC). It produces a dense, temporally continuous optical flow field as a velocity (not displacement) at any (x, t) query, and a 6-DoF camera motion trajectory parameterized by four 6-dimensional B-spline control knots per segment. The flow representation is coordinate-based and resolution-agnostic, while motion is low-dimensional and piecewise-cubic over normalized time intervals.

## Method

E-MoFlow represents optical flow as an implicit neural network (a 9-layer MLP with 256-dim hidden units, ReLU activations, residual and skip connections, no positional encoding) that maps spatio-temporal coordinates (x, t) to a 2D flow vector, and represents camera egomotion as a cubic B-spline with four control knots providing C²-continuous angular and linear velocities. Event warping is formulated as a Neural ODE integrated by an Euler solver, with gradients computed via a reverse adjoint ODE to avoid memory explosion. Training is fully unsupervised, jointly optimizing two losses: a differential flow loss (contrast maximization on the Image of Warped Events) and a novel differential geometric loss derived by eliminating depth Z(x) from the motion field equation via a cross-product identity, yielding a depth-free constraint linking flow and 6-DoF motion. The key insight is that the differential geometric loss provides geometric regularization without expanding the parameterization with depth, while the implicit representations embed spatial-temporal smoothness as an inductive bias, together preventing convergence to local minima.

## Results

On MVSEC (dt=1), E-MoFlow achieves an average EPE of 0.450 and %Out of 0.328, outperforming all unsupervised methods (best prior: EV-MGRFlowNet at 0.495 EPE) and all supervised methods (best prior: ADM-Flow at 0.533 EPE), ranking second overall behind only the model-based MultiCM-V2 (0.348 EPE). Under the more challenging dt=4 setting, E-MoFlow achieves 1.773 EPE, competitive with EV-MGRFlowNet (1.763) and outperforming most supervised baselines. For 6-DoF egomotion on MVSEC, E-MoFlow achieves an average RMSω of 4.062°/s, roughly halving the error of the only comparable depth-free baseline MultiCM-V2 (8.900°/s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

E-MoFlow outperforms all unsupervised baselines on MVSEC dt=1, beating the strongest prior USL method EV-MGRFlowNet by 0.045 EPE (0.450 vs. 0.495) and Paredes et al. by 0.123 EPE. It also surpasses all supervised methods, including ADM-Flow (0.533 EPE) and E-RAFT (1.235 EPE), despite requiring no ground-truth flow labels. The only method that outperforms E-MoFlow on MVSEC dt=1 is the model-based MultiCM-V2 (0.348 EPE), which jointly estimates depth and motion via explicit optimization. On the harder DSEC driving benchmark, E-MoFlow (3.14 EPE) is competitive with other USL methods (Paredes et al. 2.33, MotionPriorCMax 3.20) but trails supervised E-RAFT (0.79) and TMA (0.74), reflecting the greater challenge of complex driving scenes. For 6-DoF egomotion, E-MoFlow (RMSω 4.062°/s) more than halves the error of MultiCM-V2 (8.900°/s), the only other depth-free 6-DoF baseline, and approaches the accuracy of depth-dependent methods like PEME (2.72°/s on outdoor_day1).

## Relation to Prior Work

E-MoFlow sits at the intersection of three research threads: contrast maximization for event-based flow estimation (Gallego et al. 2018), implicit neural representations for continuous signal modeling (Sitzmann et al. 2020, NeRF), and differential geometric constraints from multi-view geometry (Ma et al. 2004). The key departure from prior unsupervised event-flow methods (MultiCM, USL-EV-FlowNet, EV-MGRFlowNet) is the replacement of both explicit variational smoothness regularizers and explicit depth estimation with two implicit mechanisms: neural network smoothness (via ReLU MLP without positional encoding) for spatial-temporal coherence, and a depth-free differential epipolar constraint for geometric consistency. The use of Neural ODEs for event warping (rather than direct displacement modeling) and cubic B-splines for camera motion further distinguishes the approach from prior work that either discretizes time or over-parameterizes motion with networks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a fundamental ill-posedness in event-based vision: optical flow and 6-DoF egomotion are individually underdetermined without depth or long-term association, yet prior joint-estimation methods (MultiCM-V2, ECN, USL-EV-FlowNet) introduce explicit depth as an additional degree of freedom that risks local minima. The depth-free differential geometric loss is a clean solution that practitioners in event-based SLAM and odometry could adopt. However, the method remains an offline per-segment optimizer (not real-time), and the performance gap to supervised methods on the more challenging DSEC driving dataset (EPE 3.14 vs 0.74 for TMA) limits immediate deployment in autonomous driving. Open questions include scalability to longer sequences, whether the spline-based motion model generalizes to highly non-rigid or discontinuous motion, and whether the implicit regularization strategy transfers to other event-based tasks like depth or 3D reconstruction.
