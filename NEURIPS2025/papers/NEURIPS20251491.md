## Problem

Fine-tuning pre-trained models for class-incremental learning induces parameter drift that degrades the backbone's generalization, causing catastrophic forgetting of previously learned categories. Existing PTM-based CIL methods either update backbone parameters (risking drift) or add task-specific branches (increasing inference cost), but none explicitly model the drift as a noise phenomenon that could be harnessed beneficially rather than merely suppressed.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images of varying resolutions (32×32 for CIFAR-100 up to 764×581 for Omnibenchmark) and outputs a class label from an incrementally growing category set. It operates in an exemplar-free setting where only current-task data is accessible, and the pre-trained ViT-B/16 backbone remains frozen throughout all incremental phases. The classifier is updated analytically (closed-form linear regression) rather than via gradient descent, requiring only a fixed-size autocorrelation matrix from prior tasks.

## Method

MIN inserts Pi-Noise layers between frozen ViT transformer blocks, where each task t gets a dedicated noise generator P_t (two MLP layers producing mean μ_t and variance σ_t vectors) that generates task-specific beneficial noise via reparameterization (ε·σ_t + μ_t) in a reduced 192-dim space. A Noise Mixture module combines all task noises {ε_1,...,ε_t} using learnable weights ω initialized by cosine similarity between task prototypes, then embeds the weighted mixture into intermediate features to mask irrelevant pattern activations. Training alternates between analytic classifier updates (ACIL) and gradient-based optimization of P_t and an auxiliary classifier via residual cross-entropy loss, with the main classifier W_t kept frozen during noise training. This design requires only ~0.8M trainable parameters per task while the backbone stays entirely frozen, and the mixture mechanism avoids the linear-in-task inference cost of multi-branch approaches.

## Results

MIN achieves state-of-the-art average accuracy (Ā) and last-task accuracy (A_T) across all six benchmark datasets and all incremental settings (10, 20, and 50 steps) using ViT-B/16-IN21K. On CIFAR-100 (50 steps), MIN reaches 93.63% Ā and 89.82% A_T, surpassing the runner-up MOS (92.36%/87.39%) by over 2 points. The advantage is most pronounced in the 50-step setting: on ImageNet-A, MIN achieves 66.15% Ā vs. MOS's 63.72% (a 2.4-point gap), and on ImageNet-R it reaches 82.26% Ā vs. MOS's 75.22% (a 7-point gap). MIN requires only ~0.8M trainable parameters per new task, far fewer than most competing methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MIN outperforms all 11 compared baselines across every dataset and incremental setting. The closest competitor is MOS (adapter merging + self-refined retrieval), which MIN beats by 1.3–2.4 points on CIFAR-100, 1.2–2.3 points on CUB-200, 2.4–7.0 points on ImageNet-A/R, and 1.0–2.5 points on FOOD-101/Omnibenchmark. Prompt-based methods (L2P, DualPrompt, CODA-Prompt) trail by 4–14 points, with CODA-Prompt degrading most severely at 50 steps (e.g., 55.66% A_T on CIFAR-100 vs. MIN's 89.82%). Analytic-learning baselines (ACIL) and prototype-based methods (FeCAM, RanPAC, APER) are competitive at 10 steps but fall behind MIN by 1–3 points, with the gap widening substantially at 50 steps. Notably, MIN's advantage grows with the number of tasks: on CIFAR-100 the margin over the runner-up increases from 1.25 points (10 steps) to 1.27 points (50 steps) in Ā, while on ImageNet-R it expands from 2.59 points (10 steps) to 7.04 points (50 steps), confirming that the noise-mixture strategy becomes increasingly valuable as cross-task interference accumulates.

## Relation to Prior Work

MIN builds directly on ACIL's analytic learning framework for classifier updates and on the positive-incentive noise (Pi-Noise) theory, which posits that controlled noise can simplify classification by masking confusing inter-class patterns. Unlike prior PTM-based CIL methods that fine-tune the backbone (SLCA, APER), add prompts (L2P, DualPrompt, CODA-Prompt), expand architecture (EASE), or ensemble weights (COFiMA, MOS), MIN freezes the backbone entirely and instead learns task-specific noise generators inserted between transformer blocks. The key conceptual departure is reframing parameter drift as destructive noise and then deliberately learning *beneficial* noise per task, combined with a learnable mixture strategy that dynamically weights noise from all prior tasks via an auxiliary classifier — a mechanism absent from prior CIL work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a minimal trainable module (two small MLPs per task, ~0.8M parameters total) can outperform substantially more complex prompt-based or architecture-expansion approaches, with the advantage widening dramatically as the number of incremental steps grows (e.g., 4.28% gap over runner-up on CIFAR-100 at 50 steps vs. 0.76% at 10 steps). This suggests that preserving backbone generalization through noise masking is more effective than adapting backbone parameters for long-horizon CIL. Limitations include reliance on a strong pre-trained backbone for meaningful feature guidance, and the added (though training-free) down/up-projection layers increase total model size by ~4%. Open questions include whether the noise-mixture strategy generalizes to non-ViT backbones, other modalities, or settings where the pre-trained model is weakly aligned with downstream tasks.
