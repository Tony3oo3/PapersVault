## Problem

No existing benchmark captures the full protein conformational energy landscape beyond near-native fluctuations, and no metric suite evaluates both conformational diversity and atom-level physical plausibility of AI-generated ensembles. Prior MD-derived datasets are confined to the initial funnel basin, and single-structure metrics (TM-score, RMSD, LDDT) cannot assess multi-conformer quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252746/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark provides 87 CASP target protein sequences (average 305 residues, up to 949) as input to generative models, which must produce ensembles of 3,000 all-atom 3D conformations per protein. Evaluation projects conformations onto a 2D PCA space discretized into a 64×64 grid for diversity scoring, and computes per-residue-pair geometric feature maps (Cα–Cα distance, two dihedral angles, one planar angle) summarized as mean/std/skewness tensors of shape 12×N×N for plausibility scoring.

## Method

The dataset is built by collecting 40,387 decoy structures from CASP14/15 predictions across 87 targets, filtering for sequence identity and excluding oligomers, then refining each decoy with all-atom GROMACS MD (OPLS-AA/TIP3P, 125–375 ps production after energy minimization and NVT/NPT equilibration) to yield 381,546 physically realistic conformations at a cost of 6+ million CPU hours. The dual-axis evaluation framework quantifies diversity via free-energy-landscape overlap (interaction, coverage, Jaccard index at 5/10/20 kJ/mol thresholds on PCA-projected 64×64 grids) and plausibility via the Protein Conformation Plausibility Map (PCPM), which encodes four inter-residue geometric features as 3×N×N moment tensors per feature, compared against the benchmark via per-entry cosine similarity to yield a scalar PCPS. Six generative models (AlphaFlow, ESMFlow in PDB/MD distilled variants, BioEmu, ESMDiff, AFsample2, AlphaFold3) are benchmarked, revealing that sequence-pretrained models explore broader conformational space while MD-trained variants achieve better atomic-level realism, and that all models underperform on inter-residue torsion-angle generation.

## Results

Across 18 CASP proteins, BioEmu achieves the best overall physical plausibility with a mean PCPS of 0.794 (near-native) and 0.818 (all), and the best JS-PwD (0.235) and JS-Rg (0.206) among models with reasonable stereochemistry. ESMDiff attains the lowest JS-PwD (0.128) and JS-Rg (0.104), indicating superior geometric distribution matching, but suffers the highest Ramachandran outlier rate (20.41%) and the worst Theta PCPS (0.191 near-native). AlphaFold3 produces the most stereochemically valid structures (2.66% Ramachandran outliers) but explores a narrower conformational space (Coverage 0.039 at 5 kJ/mol). All models reproduce inter-residue Cα distance distributions well (PCPS-D > 0.85) but underperform on torsion-angle features (PCPS-Theta ranging from 0.191 to 0.665).

## Baselines & Comparisons

BioEmu [Lewis et al., 2024] leads in overall physical plausibility (mean PCPS 0.794/0.818) and geometric distribution matching (JS-PwD 0.235, JS-Rg 0.206), outperforming all other models on the combined diversity–plausibility trade-off. ESMDiff [Lu et al., 2024] achieves the best distance-distribution fidelity (JS-PwD 0.128, JS-Rg 0.104) but is the worst on torsion-angle plausibility (Theta PCPS 0.191) and stereochemistry (20.41% Ramachandran outliers), revealing a clear geometric-similarity vs. physical-realism trade-off. AlphaFold3 [Abramson et al., 2024] produces the most stereochemically valid conformers (2.66% outliers) and the highest interaction score at 20 kJ/mol (104.59), yet its coverage at 5 kJ/mol (0.039) is among the lowest, indicating narrow sampling near the native basin. AFsample2 [Kalakoti & Wallner, 2025] shows the weakest diversity across all thresholds (Interaction 1.14 at 5 kJ/mol; Coverage 0.004), confirming that MSA-based perturbation of AlphaFold2 only induces minor local variations. Among the flow-matching models, MD-trained variants (AlphaFlow_MD^Dis, ESMFlow_MD^Dis) consistently outperform their PDB-trained counterparts on PCPS (e.g., ESMFlow_MD^Dis achieves 0.778 vs. 0.675 mean PCPS-all), demonstrating that MD-derived training data improves atomic-level physical realism. AlphaFlow [Jing et al., 2024] and ESMFlow [Jing et al., 2024] occupy a middle ground: their PDB variants show slightly better diversity (higher Jaccard) while their MD variants show better plausibility, but neither matches BioEmu's overall balance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of CASP-derived structural decoys and all-atom MD refinement, combining the conformational diversity of hundreds of prediction algorithms with the physical realism of force-field-based relaxation. Prior conformational datasets (BPTI, Atlas, mdCATH, Dynamic PDB) sample near-native fluctuations around crystallographic structures and remain confined to a single funnel basin, while single-conformation metrics (TM-score, RMSD, LDDT) cannot assess ensemble-level diversity or atom-level plausibility. The paper departs from these by constructing a full-landscape benchmark spanning non-native to near-native TM-scores and introducing a dual-axis evaluation framework (free-energy-landscape overlap for diversity; PCPM/PCPS for atom-level geometric plausibility) that has no direct predecessor in the literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ProteinConformers provides the first large-scale, physically realistic benchmark (381K conformations, 87 proteins, 6M+ CPU hours) for systematically evaluating protein conformation generative models on both landscape diversity and atomic plausibility, filling a gap the authors explicitly identify as absent from the field. The finding that ESMDiff—trained solely on PDB structures without any MD data—achieves competitive conformational exploration suggests that large-scale sequence pretraining (ESM3) may partially substitute for MD-derived training data, opening a path toward more scalable conformational generation. Key open questions include whether current models can be improved on torsion-angle sampling (where all six benchmarked methods underperform), whether enhanced-sampling MD or longer trajectories would shift the landscape coverage, and whether the benchmark generalizes beyond CASP-derived decoys to experimentally measured ensembles.
