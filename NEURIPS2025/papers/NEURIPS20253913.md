## Problem

Generic head avatar models trained on large multi-identity datasets learn a general domain prior and fail to synthesize identity-specific high-frequency details such as wrinkles, tattoos, and skin texture. While low-rank adaptation (LoRA) is a popular parameter-efficient fine-tuning strategy, the authors find it insufficient to capture these fine facial characteristics, motivating a mechanism that guides the model's attention to identity-relevant regions during adaptation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253913/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

During adaptation, the method consumes a short (~4 s) talking-head video of the target identity at 512×512 resolution, from which a source image (first frame) and a driving image (random frame) are extracted along with tracked 3DMM parameters (pose, expression, shape). During inference, only a single source image and a driving video are needed; the Register Module is discarded, so inference runs at the same real-time speed as the base GAGAvatar model. DINOv2 dense features are computed at 296×296 spatial resolution, and the final output is a rendered animated head avatar video via 3D Gaussian Splatting.

## Method

The method combines LoRA (rank 32) on all GAGAvatar layers except DINOv2 with a novel Register Module that learns a 3D feature space for identity-specific details. The Register Module rigs learnable embeddings (D=512) to vertices of a 3DMM mesh; given a driving image's camera pose, visible vertices are perspective-projected onto a 296×296 camera plane, their embeddings are assigned to projected pixels, and interior points within an alpha-shape contour are filled via inverse-distance-weighted interpolation (k=11). This 2D feature map is processed by a 4-layer CNN (E_proc-1), added to the source image's DINOv2 dense features, and passed through a second CNN (E_proc-2) to produce the register output f_reg, which is fed to the reconstruction branch during adaptation. Training is supervised by an MSE loss between f_reg and the driving image's DINOv2 features plus a pairwise-cosine regularization on the vertex embeddings to enforce diversity. Crucially, the Register Module is used only during the ~35-minute adaptation phase and is discarded at inference, so the merged LoRA weights add zero overhead to the rendering pipeline.

## Results

On the three evaluation datasets (HDTF, VFHQ Test, RareFace-50), the proposed LoRA + Register Module achieves the best LPIPS and ACD scores across all settings. On HDTF, it reaches LPIPS 0.1618 and ACD 0.3156; on VFHQ Test, LPIPS 0.2470 and ACD 0.3559; and on RareFace-50 (faces with distinctive wrinkles/tattoos), LPIPS 0.2744 and ACD 0.3792. The largest gains over the generic GAGAvatar baseline appear on RareFace-50, where ACD improves from 0.4046 to 0.3792 (≈6.3% relative reduction), confirming the method's strength in preserving high-frequency identity-specific details.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against five baselines: the generic GAGAvatar model, vanilla LoRA, MetaPortrait (meta-learning on LoRA weights), DoRA, and the single-identity SplattingAvatar. The proposed method outperforms all baselines on every metric across all three datasets. Against GAGAvatar, the largest ACD improvement is on RareFace-50 (0.3792 vs. 0.4046), where identity-specific details like wrinkles and tattoos are most challenging. Against vanilla LoRA, the method improves LPIPS by ~0.01–0.02 and ACD by ~0.01–0.02 across datasets, with the gap widening on RareFace-50. MetaPortrait performs worst on ACD (0.4504 on RareFace-50), producing artifacts and wrong expressions. DoRA without the Register Module (LPIPS 0.2685, ACD 0.3853 on VFHQ) is slightly worse than vanilla LoRA, but adding the Register Module to DoRA recovers some performance (LPIPS 0.2668, ACD 0.3702), demonstrating the module's compatibility with other PEFT variants. SplattingAvatar, a single-identity model, performs far worse (LPIPS 0.4573, ACD 0.5238 on VFHQ Test), confirming that identity-specific training overfits and fails to generalize to unseen expressions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the generic 3D Gaussian Splatting head avatar model GAGAvatar and the parameter-efficient fine-tuning paradigm of LoRA, extending both to the specific problem of identity personalization. Its key conceptual departure is from Darcet et al.'s 2D register tokens in ViTs: the authors rig learnable embeddings to 3DMM mesh vertices, project them into the camera plane, and use them to modulate DINOv2 features during LoRA training, effectively creating a 3D-aware 'register' that guides the network to attend to identity-specific regions. This positions the work at the intersection of PEFT (LoRA/DoRA), 3D face modeling (FLAME/3DMM), and the register-token literature, while departing from prior personalization approaches (MetaPortrait, MyStyle, One2Avatar) that operate purely in 2D latent or feature spaces without explicit 3D geometric grounding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: generic avatar models trained on large multi-identity datasets systematically 'average out' high-frequency identity-specific details like wrinkles, tattoos, and skin texture, and the authors demonstrate that vanilla LoRA alone cannot recover them. The 3D register concept is a reusable architectural idea that could transfer to other 3D-aware generation or adaptation tasks beyond head avatars. However, the approach is tightly coupled to GAGAvatar's DINOv2-based reconstruction branch and depends on accurate 3DMM fitting, limiting its generality; it also does not handle extreme or unseen views, and the RareFace-50 dataset (50 identities) is too small to establish broad statistical significance. Open questions include whether the 3D register mechanism generalizes to other foundation models (e.g., diffusion-based avatars) and whether the training-only register can be replaced by a lighter inference-time mechanism.
