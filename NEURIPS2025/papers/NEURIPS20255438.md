## Problem

Video misinformation detection remains underexplored due to the absence of large-scale, diverse benchmarks with fine-grained, interpretable annotations, and because existing MLLM-based methods either overfit to rigid fine-tuning templates or rely on shallow prompting without genuine deep reasoning over cross-modal inconsistencies. No prior work has combined long-chain-of-thought reasoning with reinforcement learning to produce explainable, entity-level justifications for video fake-news verdicts.

## Input / Output

Fact-R1 consumes 8 sampled video keyframes (via Qwen2.5-VL's video encoder), the news title, and a speech-transcribed audio transcript as multimodal input. The model emits a structured output containing a real/fake binary label inside <answer> tags, a multi-step chain-of-thought reasoning trace inside <think> tags, and—when the label is fake—an explicit identification of the manipulated entity (person, location, event, or organization) within the reasoning trace. The FakeVV dataset additionally provides the ground-truth forged entity and manipulation type as supervision signals for the entity-grounding reward.

## Method

Fact-R1 builds on Qwen2.5-VL and is trained through a three-stage pipeline: (1) Long-CoT instruction tuning on ~85K samples generated by DeepSeek-R1 from video captions and audio, filtered for correct label and correct entity identification, injecting domain-specific deep-reasoning capability via LoRA (r=128) with a frozen visual encoder; (2) DPO on 5K human-annotated preference pairs to refine reasoning coherence, eliminate hallucinated entity descriptions, and enforce output formatting; (3) GRPO with a task-specific verifiable reward function that decomposes into accuracy, format, reasoning-keyword, and entity-grounding components (the latter verified by a judge LLM), jointly optimized with auxiliary News Image OCR and News Video Caption tasks to strengthen visual-textual alignment. The FakeVV dataset (100K video-text pairs, 2006–2025) is constructed via a non-random CLIP-similarity-guided entity replacement strategy that swaps one of four entity types (person, location, event, organization) using GPT-4o, producing semantically inconsistent yet fluent fake titles with fine-grained manipulation annotations.

## Results

Fact-R1 achieves state-of-the-art performance across all three video misinformation benchmarks. On the proposed FakeVV dataset, it reaches 81.2% accuracy and 80.3% F1, outperforming the strongest traditional baseline FakingRec (72.1% Acc, 72.0% F1) by over 9 points in accuracy. On FakeTT, Fact-R1 scores 74.4% Acc / 72.7% F1, and on FakeSV it achieves 75.6% Acc / 74.7% F1, consistently leading all 15 baselines including closed-source MLLMs like GPT-4o (56.0% Acc on FakeVV) and open-source models like QVQ-72B (53.5% Acc on FakeVV).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Fact-R1 outperforms all 15 baselines across all three datasets. Among traditional multimodal methods, FakingRec [6] is the strongest baseline (72.1% Acc on FakeVV, 72.0% F1), but Fact-R1 surpasses it by 9.1% accuracy and 8.3% F1 on FakeVV, 3.4% accuracy on FakeTT, and 6.1% accuracy on FakeSV. Closed-source MLLMs perform surprisingly poorly in this domain: GPT-4o [1] achieves only 56.0% accuracy on FakeVV (44.3% F1), and GPT-o1-mini [16] drops to 47.5% accuracy, both far below Fact-R1's 81.2%. Among open-source MLLMs, QVQ-72B-preview [36] is the closest competitor at 53.5% accuracy on FakeVV, still trailing Fact-R1 by 27.7 points. Notably, text-only reasoning models like DeepSeek-R1 [14] (53.5% Acc on FakeVV) and GPT-o1-mini [16] (47.5% Acc) underperform even basic multimodal baselines, highlighting that video misinformation detection requires genuine cross-modal reasoning rather than text-only chain-of-thought. The ablation study further confirms that each training stage contributes: removing SFT drops FakeVV accuracy to 66.8%, removing DPO to 80.4%, and removing GRPO to 79.8%, with the full pipeline achieving 81.2%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the R1-style deep reasoning paradigm (long-CoT SFT → DPO → GRPO) from text-only LLMs to the multimodal video misinformation detection setting, using Qwen2.5-VL as the base MLLM. It departs from prior misinformation detection methods that relied on feature fusion, adversarial training, or rigid fine-tuning templates by instead training the model to generate structured, entity-grounded reasoning traces optimized via a verifiable rule-based reward. The dataset construction leverages CLIP-based semantic retrieval and GPT-4o-guided entity replacement, building on the out-of-context misinformation literature but introducing fine-grained manipulated-entity annotations absent from prior benchmarks like FakeSV and FakeTT.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The FakeVV dataset (100K samples, 2006–2025, four entity manipulation types) fills a critical gap in scale and interpretability for video misinformation research, and the three-stage training pipeline with a verifiable reward function offers a reusable recipe for grounding MLLM reasoning in domain-specific tasks. However, the synthetic nature of the fake samples (entity replacement on real videos) and the exclusive sourcing from four Western news outlets limit generalizability to adversarial or non-Western misinformation. Open questions include whether the reasoning-based paradigm transfers to more subtle manipulation types (deepfakes, splicing), whether the entity-grounding reward generalizes beyond the four predefined entity categories, and how the system performs under distribution shift in open-domain deployment.
