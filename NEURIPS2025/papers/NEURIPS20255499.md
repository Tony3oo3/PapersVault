## Problem

Traditional RL agents represent actions as orthogonal vectors in a flattened action space, ignoring the inherent topological relationships between actions (e.g., adjacent vs. opposing directions). While spatial structure in state representations has been shown to improve sample efficiency and generalization, this spatial inductive bias has not been systematically applied to the action selection mechanism itself. The paper asks whether ring attractor neural circuits—biologically validated structures for encoding spatial orientation—can be repurposed to explicitly encode action-space topology and integrate uncertainty, thereby accelerating learning and stabilizing action selection.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255499/cropped_page5_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes environment observations (RGB frames for Atari games, continuous state vectors for Highway) and produces a selected action along with per-action Q-value estimates. Unusually, Q-values are not simply argmax'd; instead, they are injected as Gaussian amplitudes at action-specific angular positions on a circular ring, with Bayesian posterior variance controlling the Gaussian spread. The final action is decoded from the ring's steady-state neural activity (argmax of excitatory neuron activations), so spatially adjacent actions can influence each other's selection through the ring's recurrent dynamics.

## Method

The paper proposes two ring attractor implementations for RL action selection. The first is a CTRNN-based exogenous model using the Touretzky ring attractor, where Q-values from a BDQN serve as Gaussian input amplitudes at action-specific ring positions and Bayesian linear regression (via Thompson sampling) provides per-action variance for the Gaussian spread, enabling uncertainty-aware action selection. The second is a DL-based RNN ring attractor integrated as the output layer of DRL agents (DDQN, Efficient Zero), where standard linear connections are replaced with circular weight matrices using distance-dependent exponential decay (e^{-d(m,n)/λ}) and a learnable time constant τ controls the balance between immediate input responsiveness and temporal integration. The key architectural insight is that fixing forward-pass connections to preserve ring topology while allowing hidden-to-hidden weights to learn task-specific action relationships yields the best performance, as validated by ablation studies showing significant drops when circular structure is removed.

## Results

On the Atari 100k benchmark, EffZeroRA achieves a mean human-normalized score of 1.454 and median of 0.531, representing a 53% improvement over the re-run Implemented EffZero baseline (mean 0.959, median 0.403). The largest gains appear in spatially structured games: Asterix improves by ~110% (31,037 vs 14,839) and Boxing by ~105% (62.4 vs 30.5). In the Super Mario Bros and Highway environments, both BDQNRA-UA and DDQNRA consistently outperform their standard counterparts (BDQN, DDQN) over 1M frames, and PPO-RA achieves a +71.4% average performance increase over standard PPO.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the re-run Implemented EffZero baseline, EffZeroRA wins on 17 of 19 games, achieving a 53% mean human-normalized score improvement (1.454 vs 0.959) and a 32% median improvement (0.531 vs 0.403); the only losses are on Crazy Climber (100,650 vs 83,883 is actually a win) and Jamesbond (416.4 vs 350.1 is also a win), meaning EffZeroRA wins on all 19 games. Compared to CURL (mean 0.428) and SPR (mean 0.638), EffZeroRA substantially outperforms both, exceeding even the originally reported EffZero score (1.101). In the Super Mario Bros and Highway environments, BDQNRA-UA (ring attractor + uncertainty) and DDQNRA consistently surpass standard BDQN and DDQN respectively across 1M frames. In the policy-gradient setting, PPO-RA achieves a +71.4% average performance increase over standard PPO on Super Mario Bros, with a maximum gain of +161.4% during training. Ablation studies confirm that the circular topology is essential: randomizing action placement in the ring (BDQNRA-RM) or removing circular weights in the RNN causes significant performance drops, particularly in spatially structured games like Ms Pacman and Chopper Command.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the Touretzky ring attractor network [40], originally a neuroscience model for head-direction encoding, as an explicit spatial inductive bias for action selection in RL. It departs from prior spatial-aware RL work (relational RL, grid-cell-inspired representations) by encoding spatial structure directly in the action space topology rather than relying on emergent learning to discover action relationships. The uncertainty quantification component builds on BDQN's Bayesian linear regression output layer [1], but integrates it intrinsically into the ring attractor's Gaussian input signals rather than treating it as a separate module. The practical DL implementation extends DDQN [43] and Efficient Zero [48] with a circularly-weighted RNN layer that preserves the ring topology end-to-end.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 53% average improvement on Atari 100k over EffZero, with over 100% gains in spatially structured games, demonstrates that explicit spatial encoding of the action space is a meaningful inductive bias that accelerates learning. The reusable RNN-based ring attractor module lowers the barrier to adoption across existing DRL frameworks. However, the approach is limited to action spaces with inherent spatial or sequential structure (|A| ≥ 3, non-identity action correlation), and the CTRNN variant's 297% computational overhead makes it impractical outside research validation. Open questions include scalability to high-dimensional and continuous action spaces, multi-agent settings, and whether the spatial bias generalizes to domains where action relationships are less geometrically intuitive.
