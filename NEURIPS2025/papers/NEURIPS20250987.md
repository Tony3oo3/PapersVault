## Problem

Text-to-image diffusion models systematically replicate and amplify demographic biases (e.g., generating white males for 'CEO' or females for 'nurse'), yet existing post-hoc debiasing methods are limited: SDID assumes a single linear bias direction (inappropriate for multi-group attributes like race), and TBIE's PCA-based projection lacks an explicit content-preservation criterion, causing semantic drift. No prior post-hoc approach simultaneously handles multiple demographic attributes, offers a tunable fairness-fidelity trade-off, and avoids over-pruning of semantic information.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a natural-language text prompt, encodes it via a pre-trained CLIP (or T5/OpenCLIP) text encoder into a pooled embedding and a token-level embedding matrix, then feeds the transformed embeddings into a modified Stable Diffusion 3 pipeline (classifier-free guidance scale 7.0, 28 diffusion steps) to produce a standard RGB image. A small annotated set of ~120 occupational prompts with demographic labels is used offline to learn the FairPCA projection matrix; at inference time, no model weights are updated and no prompt rewriting is required.

## Method

FairImagen integrates FairPCA into the Stable Diffusion inference pipeline in three stages: (1) extract CLIP-based pooled and token-level prompt embeddings; (2) project them through a fairness-aware matrix P learned by minimizing reconstruction error plus a group-separability penalty (λ‖BP‖²_F), which removes linear demographic signals while preserving semantic variance; (3) generate the image from the debiased embeddings. An empirical noise injection step samples scalar magnitudes from the actual distribution of group-specific projections onto the learned bias direction, preventing overly sanitized outputs (e.g., a clean-shaven 'male' firefighter). For intersectional debiasing, the method constructs a joint attribute space via the Cartesian product of all demographic groups (e.g., White Male, Asian Female) and applies a single unified FairPCA projection over this space, avoiding the compounded information loss of sequential or stacked projections.

## Results

On the Winobias occupational prompt benchmark (120 professions), FairImagen achieves the best average score among all post-hoc methods across all three debiasing settings. In the joint Gender+Race setting, FairImagen reaches 0.537 gender fairness and 0.32 race fairness with 0.753 CLIPScore accuracy and 0.544 MUSIQ, yielding an average of 0.611—substantially above the Base model (0.508 avg) and all other post-hoc baselines. In the single-attribute Gender setting, FairImagen scores 0.56 gender fairness (vs. 0.167 for Base) with an average of 0.624, and in the Race setting it achieves 0.389 race fairness (vs. 0.193 for Base) with an average of 0.562. The only method that outperforms FairImagen is FairPrompt (0.671 avg in the joint setting), which requires manually designed per-image prompts and serves as an upper bound.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FairImagen outperforms all seven post-hoc baselines (SAL, CDA, TBIE, SDID, SDID-AVG, ITI, FairQueue) on the average score in every debiasing setting. In the joint Gender+Race setting, FairImagen (0.611 avg) leads TBIE (0.574) by 3.7 points and CDA (0.566) by 4.5 points, while FairQueue—despite the highest MUSIQ (0.606)—suffers the worst gender fairness (0.057) and lowest average (0.478). In the single-attribute Gender setting, FairImagen (0.624 avg) edges out SDID (0.612) and CDA (0.623), with a 33-point gap in gender fairness over Base (0.56 vs. 0.167). In the Race setting, FairImagen (0.562 avg) narrowly beats SDID (0.559) and SDID-AVG (0.558), while FairQueue collapses to 0.118 race fairness. The only method that surpasses FairImagen is FairPrompt (0.671 avg in the joint setting), but this requires manually crafted per-image prompts and is explicitly positioned as an upper bound rather than a deployable method. FairImagen trades a small amount of Accuracy (0.753 vs. 0.785 for Base) and MUSIQ (0.544 vs. 0.574) for a large fairness gain, a trade-off the authors argue is justified given the substantial bias reduction.

## Relation to Prior Work

FairImagen builds directly on FairPCA (Kleindessner et al., AISTATS 2023), repurposing its fairness-regularized projection objective from representation learning into the text-to-image generation pipeline. It departs from the closest post-hoc baselines—SDID, which assumes a single linear gender direction, and TBIE, which applies unregularized PCA on gendered words—by introducing an explicit reconstruction-fairness trade-off (λ-weighted) and an empirical noise injection step to prevent over-neutralization. The cross-demographic formulation, which constructs a joint attribute space via the Cartesian product of all group combinations, is a novel departure from the sequential or stacked projection strategies used in prior multi-attribute debiasing work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FairImagen offers a practical, training-free, and model-agnostic debiasing layer that can be bolted onto any CLIP-based diffusion model without retraining, making it immediately deployable in production pipelines. However, its evaluation is confined to occupational prompts with binary gender and coarse race categories, and it inherits residual biases from the CLIP encoder itself; generalizability to creative, cultural, or non-binary contexts remains untested. Open questions include whether the FairPCA projection can disentangle demographic signals that are deeply entangled with semantic meaning, and whether the Cartesian-product approach scales gracefully as the number of protected attributes grows.
