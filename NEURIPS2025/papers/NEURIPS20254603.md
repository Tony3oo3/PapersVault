## Problem

Existing stepwise LLM-based theorem provers rely on a single learned critic to guide tree search, which becomes biased toward frequently occurring 'safe' tactics (e.g., simp_all, aesop), causing repetitive steps, unprovable states from oversimplification, and ineffective tactic applications that stall progress. This paper addresses these failure modes by diversifying the search guidance and curating training data to reduce overreliance on simplistic tactics.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254603/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a formal theorem statement in Lean 4 and iteratively produces a sequence of Lean 4 tactics that constitute a verified proof. At each step, the LLM proposes N_samples=8 candidate tactics for each of up to 4 active proof states; the Lean 4.16.0 engine (via the repl tool) verifies each tactic and returns the updated proof state as feedback. A final end-to-end compilation check by the Lean compiler ensures the concatenated proof script is fully valid, guarding against occasional repl misinterpretations.

## Method

MPS-Prover combines two key innovations. First, a post-training data curation strategy filters out theorems provable in ≤3 steps (~40% of data) and removes ~5% of ineffective tactic applications (e.g., simp_all that leaves the state unchanged), focusing the model on complex reasoning patterns. Second, the Multi-Perspective Tree Search replaces single-critic BFS with a 4-node expansion per iteration: one node is selected by a learned critic (hierarchical tree-based distance prediction), and three additional nodes are selected by hand-designed heuristics—tactic effectiveness scoring (a 0–6 scale favoring transformative tactics like rcases, induction, by_contra over auxiliary ones like norm_num), minimizing case-split occurrences in the state string, and preferring shorter Lean state strings. The model is a Qwen2.5-Math-7B-base fine-tuned via 26 rounds of expert iteration (~3.5M curated QA pairs) on SFT, and the search budget per problem is N_pass × 4 perspectives × 800 max iterations × 8 samples.

## Results

On miniF2F-test, MPS-Prover (7B, stepwise) achieves 75.82% accuracy under the accumulative search protocol, surpassing the previous best stepwise prover BFS-Prover (72.54%) and all non-distilled 7B whole-proof provers. On the more challenging ProofNet-test, MPS-Prover reaches 32.97%, outperforming all 7B baselines including DeepSeek-Prover-V2 with CoT (29.6%). Additionally, MPS-Prover generates substantially shorter proofs (mean 3.44 Lean steps) compared to Kimina-Prover (15.91) and DeepSeek-Prover-V2 (52.16) on 170 commonly solved miniF2F problems.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MPS-Prover outperforms all stepwise baselines on miniF2F: it exceeds BFS-Prover (72.54% accumulative) by 3.28 points, HunyuanProver (68.4%) by 7.42 points, and InternLM2.5-StepProver (65.9%) by 9.92 points. Among 7B whole-proof provers, MPS-Prover surpasses STP (67.6%), Goedel-Prover (64.7%), and Leanabell-Prover (61.1%), and matches Kimina-Prover-Distill (70.8%) at lower budgets while exceeding it under accumulative search. On ProofNet, MPS-Prover (32.97%) beats DeepSeek-Prover-V2 CoT (29.6%), STP (26.9%), DeepSeek-Prover-V1.5-RL (25.3%), DeepSeek-Prover-V2 non-CoT (24.7%), and Goedel-Prover (15.6%). The only 7B model that outperforms MPS-Prover on miniF2F is DeepSeek-Prover-V2 (Distilled, CoT) at 82.0%, which the authors attribute to distillation from a much larger model. Under equivalent computational budgets (MPS pass@k vs. BFS pass@4k), MPS consistently outperforms BFS, with the gap widening as budget increases (e.g., 67.62% vs. 66.39% at the lowest budget).

## Relation to Prior Work

MPS-Prover extends the stepwise LLM-based proving paradigm established by GPT-f, HunyuanProver, and BFS-Prover, which iteratively propose Lean tactics guided by a learned critic model. Its key departure is replacing the single-criterion Best-First Search of BFS-Prover with a multi-perspective expansion that simultaneously retains one critic-selected node and three heuristic-selected nodes (tactic effectiveness scoring, case-split minimization, shortest-state preference), directly addressing the critic bias and unprovable-state failure modes identified in prior work. The post-training data curation strategy (filtering short proofs and ineffective tactics) is a practical refinement over the standard expert iteration loop used by InternLM-StepProver and HunyuanProver, which uniformly incorporate all newly proved theorems.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that stepwise provers produce dramatically shorter proofs (mean 3.44 steps vs. 15.91 for Kimina and 52.16 for DeepSeek-Prover-V2) challenges the implicit assumption that whole-proof planning yields more efficient formalizations, suggesting that adaptive, engine-in-the-loop search is inherently more economical. A key open limitation is the inability of purely stepwise provers to handle complex nested proof obligations (e.g., introducing and proving auxiliary lemmas inline via `have`), which whole-proof systems handle naturally; the authors identify hybrid stepwise/whole-proof architectures as the most promising direction. The 40% data reduction with negligible performance loss also suggests that expert iteration pipelines may be substantially over-parameterized in their training data, a finding relevant to the broader LLM-for-math community.
