## Problem

Existing model-heterogeneous federated learning methods are designed for Euclidean data and fail to exploit the topological structure of graph-structured data. Specifically, knowledge distillation in this setting suffers from three issues: a fixed distillation difficulty that cannot accommodate heterogeneous model capacities and complex graph topologies, a static temperature that cannot adapt to varying graph complexity, and KL-divergence-based loss that only performs intra-class comparison, missing cross-class structural relationships.

## Input / Output

Each client holds a subgraph with a node feature matrix and adjacency matrix, and maintains both a private model (randomly GCN, GAT, or GraphSAGE with 3 layers, hidden dim 64) and a standardized small GCN proxy model (3 layers, hidden dim 32) that serves as the communication bridge. The server aggregates proxy model weights via weighted averaging to produce a global model, which is then distilled back into private models through conformal prediction-based backward distillation. Data heterogeneity is simulated via Dirichlet partitioning of node classes across 10 clients.

## Method

TRUST extends the FedType proxy-model distillation framework with three graph-aware components. (1) The Progressive Curriculum Node Scheduler (PCNS) defines node difficulty via neighborhood label entropy and prototype-alignment confidence, then progressively exposes nodes from easy to hard using a linear pacing function, so lightweight models first absorb low-order semantics before tackling boundary nodes. (2) The Adaptive Curriculum Distillation Modulator (ACDM) converts the distillation temperature into a learnable parameter optimized in the opposite direction of model parameters (an adversarial mechanism), with a cosine-scheduled scaling factor that gradually increases the modulator's influence to avoid destabilizing early training. (3) The Wasserstein-Driven Affinity Distillation (WDAD) loss computes cross-class similarity from L2-normalized classifier weight vectors, uses a Gaussian kernel over this similarity to define transmission costs, and minimizes an entropic-regularized optimal transport (solved via Sinkhorn iterations) between teacher and student class distributions, enabling cross-class structural knowledge transfer that KL-divergence alone cannot capture. The final loss combines cross-entropy, WDAD, and curriculum-modulated KL-divergence with learned weights.

## Results

Under moderate data heterogeneity (α=0.5), TRUST achieves the best local and global accuracy across all five benchmark datasets. The headline global accuracy gains over FedAvg are 10.80% on Cora (75.32% vs. 64.52%), 7.40% on Amazon-Photo (91.50% vs. 84.10%), and 2.48% on PubMed (84.57% vs. 82.09%). The paper reports an average 3.6% performance gain over existing methods, with the largest improvements concentrated on global model accuracy where topology-aware knowledge transfer matters most.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TRUST outperforms all seven baselines on every dataset for both local and global accuracy under moderate heterogeneity (α=0.5). The closest competitor is FedType (ICML 2024), which also uses proxy models for knowledge distillation; TRUST surpasses it by 2.36% on Cora global (75.32% vs. 72.96%) and by 1.33% on Amazon-Photo global (91.50% vs. 90.17%). Traditional FL baselines (FedAvg, FedNOVA, FedProto, MOON) show limited gains over FedAvg in this model-heterogeneous graph setting, with global accuracy often within 1–2% of the FedAvg baseline. FGL-specific methods (AdaFGL, FedGTA) designed for homogeneous architectures degrade noticeably when forced into heterogeneous settings, with FedGTA dropping 2.65% below FedAvg on CiteSeer local. Under extreme heterogeneity (α=0.1), TRUST still leads with 42.52% global accuracy on Cora versus 40.88% for FedType, while most other baselines fall below 37%.

## Relation to Prior Work

TRUST extends the proxy-model distillation framework of FedType (ICML 2024) from Euclidean data to graph-structured data, addressing three gaps the authors identify: fixed task difficulty, static distillation temperature, and the absence of cross-class comparison in KL-divergence-based KD. It draws on curriculum learning (Bengio et al., 2009) to schedule node difficulty progressively, on GAN-inspired adversarial optimization to make temperature a learnable parameter, and on optimal transport (Sinkhorn) to introduce Wasserstein-based cross-class affinity distillation. The paper positions itself as the first systematic treatment of model-heterogeneous FGL, departing from prior FGL methods (FedGTA, AdaFGL) that assume homogeneous architectures and from prior model-heterogeneous FL methods (pFedHR, DESA, FedTGP) that ignore graph topology.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By formally characterizing model-heterogeneous FGL as a distinct problem with three specific KD challenges, the paper opens a research direction that was previously unaddressed, and the 10.80% global accuracy gain on Cora under moderate heterogeneity demonstrates that topology-aware distillation can substantially outperform architecture-agnostic approaches. However, the evaluation is limited to five relatively small academic citation/product graphs (≤20K nodes), and the proxy-model architecture adds communication overhead that may not scale to industrial-scale graphs. Open questions include whether the curriculum and Wasserstein components remain beneficial at larger scale, whether the approach generalizes to other graph tasks (link prediction, graph classification), and whether the computational cost of dynamic temperature adjustment and Sinkhorn iterations becomes prohibitive in practice.
