## Problem

RL for reasoning LMs relies on sparse one-hot correctness rewards, meaning the model must already solve problems at initialization to receive any learning signal — a fundamental exploration bottleneck that restricts RL to large, already-capable models. Moreover, the test-time role of RL-trained LMs is to serve as teachers for distillation, yet their training objective (solving from scratch) is misaligned with the actual goal of producing explanations that students can learn from, forcing current pipelines to depend on orders-of-magnitude larger LMs and heuristic postprocessing of reasoning traces.

## Input / Output

The RLT receives both the problem question and its ground-truth solution as input, formatted with a system prompt instructing it to produce a solution section followed by a detailed step-by-step explanation section using <|begin_of_explanation|> tags. The raw explanation tokens are extracted and reformatted into the student's <|begin_of_thought|> format to construct distillation samples, requiring no filtering, multi-generation, or postprocessing — a deliberate departure from prior pipelines that verify correctness and refine traces with closed-source LMs. At test time, the same RLT can be applied zero-shot to new domains (e.g., the countdown arithmetic task) by simply feeding new question-solution pairs.

## Method

The RLT framework replaces the sparse correctness reward with a dense, student-grounded reward computed from a small student model's log-probabilities. The reward has two terms: r^SS measures the student's average and minimum log-probability over the solution tokens conditioned on the teacher's explanation (ensuring the explanation makes the solution predictable), and r^KL measures the KL divergence between the teacher's and student's distributions over the explanation tokens (ensuring each reasoning step is a logical continuation from the student's perspective without access to the solution). These terms are combined with a weighting coefficient λ and optimized via GRPO with group-normalized advantages over 64 sampled completions per question. Training is remarkably lightweight — 125 steps on a 7B Qwen2.5 model with batch size 1024 — and the resulting raw outputs directly outperform distillation pipelines using 32B+ teachers with extensive postprocessing, while also enabling better RL cold-starts and zero-shot transfer to unseen reasoning domains.

## Results

On the main distillation benchmark (Table 1), a 7B RLT teacher produces student models achieving 49.50 overall (AIME 23.30, MATH 500 82.80, GPQA Diamond 42.40) with 17K data, outperforming the Bespoke-7B baseline (46.60) that uses postprocessed DeepSeek R1 traces. Scaling to a 32B student, RLT-32B reaches 73.23 overall (AIME 66.70, MATH 93.40, GPQA 59.60), surpassing all prior 32B distillation methods including s1-32B+budget forcing (69.77) and Sky-T1-32B (60.83). Even with only 1K distillation samples, RLT-7B-1K (47.43) and RLT-32B-1K (71.37) maintain clear advantages over their respective baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The 7B RLT outperforms the Bespoke-7B baseline (which distills postprocessed DeepSeek R1 traces) by 2.9 points overall (49.50 vs. 46.60) and by 4.6 points on GPQA Diamond (42.40 vs. 37.80), despite R1 being a 671B-parameter model. For 32B students, RLT-32B beats Bespoke-32B (73.23 vs. 71.47), s1-32B+budget forcing using Gemini Flash Thinking traces (73.23 vs. 69.77), and Sky-T1-32B using QwQ traces (73.23 vs. 60.83) by a substantial 12.4-point margin. The advantage is most pronounced at the 1K data scale, where RLT-32B-1K (71.37) exceeds s1-32B+budget forcing (69.77) and Bespoke-32B-1K (65.60) by 1.6 and 5.8 points respectively. In the cold-start RL setting (Table 2), RLT-7B+RL achieves 50.53 overall versus 48.30 for Bespoke-7B+RL and 43.93 for GPT-postprocessed RL cold-start, confirming the advantage extends beyond pure distillation. The paper also shows zero-shot transfer to the countdown task where RLT distillation (50.8) even exceeds direct RL (49.2), a result no prior distillation pipeline achieves.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the RL-for-reasoning paradigm established by DeepSeek-R1 and its GRPO algorithm by inverting the task: instead of training a model to solve problems from scratch with sparse correctness rewards, RLTs are trained to produce step-by-step explanations given the ground-truth solution, using dense rewards derived from a student model's log-probabilities. It also replaces the multi-stage distillation pipelines (Bespoke, Sky-T1, s1) that rely on orders-of-magnitude larger LMs plus heuristic postprocessing with a single small model trained end-to-end for the teaching objective. The GRPO optimization framework and group-normalized advantage computation are retained, but the reward signal shifts from one-hot correctness to a two-term dense function (student solution likelihood + teacher-student KL on think tokens).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: a 7B RLT trained in ~280 GPU-hours produces distillation data that outperforms pipelines built on 671B DeepSeek-R1 traces requiring >688,000 GPU-hours, while eliminating the need for GPT-based postprocessing and correctness filtering. Zero-shot transfer to the countdown task outperforming direct RL suggests the 'teaching' skill generalizes beyond the training domain, potentially democratizing RL reasoning beyond closed-source frontier models. Key open questions include whether the framework scales beyond 7B teachers, whether concurrent teacher-student training or self-distillation can close the remaining gap, and whether the dependence on ground-truth solutions can be relaxed for domains where solutions are not readily available.
