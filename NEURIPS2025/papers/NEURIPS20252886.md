## Problem

Existing high-dimensional transfer learning methods rely on a two-step pre-training and fine-tuning paradigm whose success critically depends on the pre-trained model being close to the target parameter. When source domains are significantly different from the target, the fused pre-trained model carries large bias that limited target data cannot correct, leading to fine-tuning failure. This paper asks how to directly exploit beneficial source knowledge while preventing negative transfer from dissimilar sources, without requiring the pre-trained model to already approximate the target.

## Input / Output

The method takes K+1 datasets (one target, K sources) of tabular features and labels generated from generalized linear models, with the target data further split into T folds for adaptive source detection. Beyond the target parameter estimate β^(0), the method outputs per-source contrast vectors δ^(k) that identify which covariates are transferable in each individual source (a granularity unavailable in two-step methods that only yield a single fused-level contrast), as well as coordinate-wise confidence intervals constructed via a desparsified estimator and a precision matrix estimated through node-wise regression over pooled source and target data.

## Method

CoRT replaces the two-step pre-train-then-fine-tune pipeline with a single joint optimization that is target-centric and asymmetric: the target loss is evaluated at β^(0) directly, while each source loss is evaluated at β^(0)+δ^(k) with δ^(k) regularized to be small, so source parameters are constrained toward the target rather than the target being pulled toward a potentially biased pre-trained model. This co-learning structure means the target parameter remains inside the source loss function, allowing source sample size to directly improve target estimation. For unknown source relevance, a T-fold cross-validation with majority vote adaptively identifies similar versus outlier sources without any threshold hyperparameters. Theoretically, the analysis requires extending the restricted strong convexity condition to a non-i.i.d. composite design matrix arising from the co-regularization, and shows CoRT achieves a wider positive transfer regime (larger allowable heterogeneity h) than two-step Trans-Lasso and Trans-GLM. For inference, a desparsified estimator is constructed under the multi-source CoRT framework, yielding narrower confidence intervals than single-task desparsified Lasso under milder similarity conditions.

## Results

On the county-level COVID-19 mortality prediction task (561 features, ~50 training samples per state), CoRT achieves the lowest mean squared prediction error in 11 out of 12 target states, with notable margins such as 0.26 vs. 0.30 (Trans-GLM) for AL and 4.69 vs. 5.13 for WV. Theoretically, CoRT's ℓ₂ error bound is $(s\log p/N)^{1/2} + (\log p/N)^{1/4}h^{1/2}$, which is strictly sharper than the two-step Trans-GLM/Trans-Lasso bound $(s\log p/N)^{1/2} + [(\log p/n_0)^{1/4}h^{1/2}] \wedge h$ because the heterogeneity term scales with total sample size $N$ rather than target-only $n_0$. In simulations with $h \in \{5,10,20,40\}$, CoRT consistently outperforms Trans-GLM on ℓ₂ estimation error for both linear and logistic regression, and for logistic regression CoRT maintains near-95% CI coverage while Trans-GLM and desparsified Lasso fail to do so.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against **naive-Lasso** (target-only Lasso), CoRT wins in every simulation and real-data scenario, with the advantage growing as the number of similar sources increases. Against **Trans-GLM** (Tian & Feng 2023, the primary two-step baseline), CoRT achieves lower ℓ₂ estimation error across all tested heterogeneity levels $h \in \{5,10,20,40\}$ in logistic regression simulations, and lower MSE in 11/12 states on the COVID-19 data (e.g., 0.52 vs. 0.62 for NY, 4.69 vs. 5.13 for WV). In the adaptive setting with outlier sources, adaptive CoRT is guaranteed not to be worse than naive-Lasso, whereas Trans-GLM can degrade below the baseline when its hyperparameters are poorly tuned. Against **Trans-Lasso** (Li et al. 2022), the comparison is purely theoretical: CoRT's bound uses $N = n_0 + n_S$ in the heterogeneity term, allowing a wider positive-transfer regime ($h \ll s\sqrt{(n_0+n_S)\log p / n_0^2}$ vs. $h \ll s\sqrt{\log p / n_0}$). For inference, CoRT's desparsified CI achieves near-nominal 95% coverage for logistic regression while **desparsified Lasso** (Van de Geer et al. 2014) and Trans-GLM both fail to maintain the nominal level, and CoRT produces shorter CI lengths and lower precision-matrix estimation error.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant two-step pre-training/fine-tuning paradigm for high-dimensional transfer learning (Bastani 2021; Li et al. 2022; Tian and Feng 2023), which first pools source data to obtain a pre-trained parameter and then fine-tunes on target data via biased regularization. CoRT replaces this sequential process with a single co-learning objective that simultaneously estimates the target parameter and source contrasts, constraining source parameters to remain close to the target. The theoretical machinery builds on the restricted strong convexity framework of Loh and Wainwright (2015) extended to a non-i.i.d. composite design, and the inference component adapts the desparsified Lasso of Van de Geer et al. (2014) and node-wise regression of Meinshausen and Buhlmann (2006) to the multi-source co-regularization setting. The adaptive source-detection algorithm draws on the cross-validation spirit of Tian and Feng (2023) but replaces their hyperparameter-dependent thresholding with a hyperparameter-free majority vote.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is that CoRT provably accommodates a wider range of source-target heterogeneity (larger h) than two-step methods, meaning it can extract useful signal from less similar sources where Trans-GLM and Trans-Lasso would fail or even hurt performance. The hyperparameter-free adaptive algorithm addresses a real pain point in practice, since the threshold tuning in existing source-detection methods can degrade performance below single-task baselines. Open questions include the computational cost of retraining CoRT for each new target (potentially addressable via divide-and-conquer), and whether the co-regularization spirit extends naturally to non-GLM architectures such as deep networks. The model-agnostic formulation suggests broad applicability, but all theory and experiments are confined to GLMs.
