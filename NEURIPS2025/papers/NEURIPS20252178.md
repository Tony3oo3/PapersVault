## Problem

Standard functional classification algorithms can produce substantial disparities across sensitive subpopulations, yet no principled framework exists for fair classification in infinite-dimensional functional spaces. Existing multivariate fairness methods rely on posterior probabilities and density ratios that are intractable or undefined in functional settings, and even the excess risk characterisation for unconstrained functional classification remains unresolved when eigenfunctions are unknown.

## Input / Output

The method consumes two independent datasets: a training set for estimating the Radon-Nikodym derivative (via eigenfunction decomposition of the group-wise covariance operator) and class probabilities, and a separate calibration set for selecting the fairness-adjusted threshold τ. The functional covariate X is observed as a continuous trajectory in L²([0,1]) (discretised in practice), and the output is a binary prediction produced by a group-wise thresholded decision rule on the estimated likelihood ratio, with a pre-specified disparity tolerance δ controlling the maximum allowed gap between sensitive groups.

## Method

The paper derives the δ-fair Bayes optimal classifier in closed form by applying the generalised Neyman-Pearson lemma to the functional setting, replacing intractable posterior probabilities with the Radon-Nikodym derivative dP_{a,1}/dP_{a,0}, which is analytically available under Gaussian process assumptions. The resulting Fair-FLDA algorithm is a post-processing procedure: it first estimates the log-likelihood ratio via truncated eigenfunction expansion of the homoscedastic covariance operator K_a, then shifts the standard FLDA threshold from π_{a,0}/π_{a,1} to (π_{a,0}+τ·b_{D,a})/(π_{a,1}−τ·s_{D,a}) per sensitive group, where τ is chosen to minimise |τ| subject to the disparity constraint |D(τ)|≤δ. The bilinearity of common disparity measures (DO, PD, DD) in the classifier ensures the threshold adjustment is the only modification needed. Finite-sample guarantees show the disparity is controlled to O(√(log(1/η))/n) above δ with probability 1−η, and the excess risk decomposes into an intrinsic estimation term plus a fairness cost proportional to |τ*|·n^{-1/2}.

## Results

On the NHANES real dataset (3,252 instances), Fair-FLDA achieves the lowest median classification error across all disparity levels: 0.314 at δ=0, 0.285 at δ=0.20, while simultaneously controlling demographic disparity (DD) to 0.021 and 0.200 respectively. On the simulated Gaussian model (n=1000, β=1.5), Fair-FLDA achieves 0.234 error at δ=0 versus 0.276 for FPIR/PPOT and 0.276 for FUDS. The calibrated variant Fair-FLDA_c achieves probabilistic disparity control below δ with probability ≥95%, correcting the small offset inherent in the uncalibrated version.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Fair-FLDA outperforms all four multivariate baselines (FPIR, PPF, PPOT, FUDS) on both classification error and disparity control across all δ values on both simulated and NHANES data. On NHANES at δ=0, Fair-FLDA achieves 0.314 error versus 0.385 for FPIR/PPOT and 0.403 for FUDS — a 7–22% relative reduction. PPF catastrophically fails disparity control at δ=0 (DD=0.722 on NHANES, 0.323 on simulated), far exceeding the target. FUDS (pre-processing) also fails to control disparity on NHANES, with DD reaching 0.349 even at δ=0.20, indicating it cannot enforce the fairness constraint in the functional setting. FPIR and PPOT perform nearly identically to each other (both use dimension-reduction + post-processing) but consistently trail Fair-FLDA by 0.06–0.09 in error, confirming that the functional-native Radon-Nikodym derivative approach captures information lost by PCA-based feature reduction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Bayes-optimal fair classification framework of Zeng et al. (2024a) from finite-dimensional feature spaces to infinite-dimensional functional spaces, replacing the intractable posterior probabilities with Radon-Nikodym derivatives as the natural decision statistic. It also builds on the functional linear discriminant analysis (FLDA) literature (Berrendero et al., 2018; Delaigle and Hall, 2012) for the underlying classifier structure and excess risk analysis. The key conceptual departure is that the absence of a default base measure in functional spaces necessitates the Radon-Nikodym derivative, which is analytically tractable under Gaussian process assumptions but has no finite-dimensional analogue in the Zeng et al. framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic treatment of fairness in functional data classification, filling a clear gap where standard multivariate fairness methods provably fail on functional data. The theoretical byproduct—finite-sample excess risk guarantees for standard FLDA under unknown eigenfunctions—extends prior results (Wang et al., 2021) that required known eigenfunctions. Open questions include extending to non-Gaussian processes where the Radon-Nikodym derivative is intractable, handling missing sensitive attributes at test time, and investigating the effect of discrete observation grids on excess risk under fairness constraints.
