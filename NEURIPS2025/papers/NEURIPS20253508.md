## Problem

Sampling from multimodal mixture distributions is a fundamental challenge in MCMC, and simulated tempering is a widely used strategy to navigate between separated modes. However, existing spectral gap decomposition results (e.g., Ge et al. 2018) are formulated for continuous-time Langevin diffusions, while for discrete-time samplers like Metropolis-Hastings over unbounded Euclidean spaces, the usual spectral gap is often intractable to bound or becomes degenerate. This paper fills that gap by developing a decomposition theorem for the restricted spectral gap of discrete-time simulated tempering chains, extending theoretical guarantees to a broader class of local MCMC samplers.

## Input / Output

The algorithm requires only pointwise evaluations of the negative log-density f(x) (the individual component means and weights of the Gaussian mixture are unknown), an inverse temperature sequence 0 < β₁ < … < β_L = 1, a Gaussian proposal step size η, and a tempering rate λ ∈ (0,1). Normalizing constants Z_i are estimated inductively via a multi-stage procedure (Algorithm 2), so the acceptance probability depends only on ratios of estimated partition functions. The output is a sample from a distribution within ε total variation distance of the target P*, with a mixing time of poly(1/w_min, D, κ) · log³(1/ε) for fixed dimension d, where D captures mode separation and κ is the condition number of the shared covariance.

## Method

The core contribution is a new decomposition theorem (Theorem 1) that lower-bounds the restricted spectral gap of a discrete-time simulated tempering chain by decomposing its Dirichlet form into three components: local chains M_{(i,j)} targeting each mixture component at each temperature level, a projected chain M̄ on [L]×[n] governing transitions between components and temperatures, and the temperature-mixing Dirichlet form. The key insight is working with the restricted spectral gap on a subset X⁰ where the target concentrates, which is tractable for discrete-time chains via geometric path methods (Yuen 2000), rather than requiring a full spectral gap over the unbounded state space. The projected chain construction differs substantially from Ge et al. 2018, arising naturally from the algorithm's structure and yielding a simpler proof with equally tight constants. For the Gaussian mixture application, the local chain mixing is bounded using the path method on continuous spaces, while the projected chain mixing is bounded via the canonical path method, with the temperature schedule chosen so that adjacent-temperature component distributions maintain sufficient overlap.

## Results

The main theoretical result (Theorem 2) establishes that the STMH algorithm achieves a mixing time of poly(1/w_min, D, κ) · log³(1/ε) for sampling from a mixture of n Gaussians with shared covariance, where D is the mode separation, κ is the condition number, and w_min is the smallest mixture weight. In the simulation study on a 2D symmetric Gaussian mixture, the number of steps for STMH to reach ‖μ̂‖ < 0.1 grows approximately linearly with D², consistent with the polynomial-in-D bound, while the baseline MH algorithm's mixing time grows exponentially with D². Additionally, STMH shows an approximately linear relationship between the number of steps and log²(1/‖μ̂‖), validating the logarithmic dependence on 1/ε, whereas MH exhibits a polynomial dependence on 1/ε.

## Baselines & Comparisons

STMH achieves the best-known dependence on 1/ε (logarithmic, specifically log²) among all compared methods, outperforming STLMC and Annealed LMC (both poly(1/ε)) and LMC / LMC-data-initialized (both 1/ε²). On the mode separation parameter D, STMH achieves polynomial scaling, matching STLMC and Annealed LMC, and substantially outperforming LMC, LMC-data-initialized (both exp(D²)²), and the Proximal Sampler (exp(D²)). The trade-off is that STMH has exponential dependence on dimension d (due to the random walk Metropolis-Hastings local chain), whereas STLMC, Annealed LMC, and LMC-data-initialized have only polynomial d-dependence, and the Proximal Sampler achieves d^{1/2}. The paper notes that no algorithm dominates STMH in terms of the joint dependence on D and 1/ε, and conjectures that replacing RWMH with MALA or a proximal sampler could improve the d-dependence. Empirically, STMH outperforms the baseline MH algorithm by a wide margin: mixing time scales linearly in D² for STMH versus exponentially for MH, and the accuracy scaling is logarithmic for STMH versus polynomial for MH.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Markov chain decomposition framework of Ge et al. [2018] from continuous-time Langevin diffusions to discrete-time Metropolis-Hastings chains, and from full spectral gaps to restricted spectral gaps (following Atchadé [2021]). The key conceptual departure is a new construction of the projected chain that arises naturally from the simulated tempering algorithm's structure, yielding a simpler proof and equally tight Poincaré constant bounds. The use of Yuen's [2000] path method on continuous spaces to bound local chain mixing, combined with the canonical path method for the projected chain, provides a discrete-time analogue of the soft decomposition technique previously available only in the continuous-time setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result establishes that simulated tempering with random walk Metropolis-Hastings achieves polynomial dependence on mode separation D and logarithmic dependence on 1/ε for Gaussian mixtures, improving on the exponential-in-D scaling of proximal samplers and the polynomial-in-1/ε scaling of simulated tempering Langevin Monte Carlo. The restricted spectral gap framework broadens applicability to settings where full spectral gaps are intractable over unbounded domains. Key limitations include the exponential dependence on dimension d (attributed to the RWMH local sampler) and the restriction to shared-covariance Gaussian mixtures; the authors conjecture that replacing RWMH with MALA or proximal samplers could yield polynomial d-dependence, and extending to general log-concave mixtures remains open.
