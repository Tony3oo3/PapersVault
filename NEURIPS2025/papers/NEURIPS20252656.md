## Problem

Existing KV cache compression methods rank tokens for eviction using query-key attention scores, assuming attention intensity correlates with semantic importance. However, this heuristic neglects the downstream contribution of value vectors to the attention output softmax(QK^T)V, and the paper demonstrates that high-attention tokens do not necessarily have high eviction loss. The gap is the lack of a principled, value-aware selection criterion that directly targets preservation of the attention output rather than merely approximating the QK^T matrix.

## Input / Output

The method operates on per-group key and value matrices (K_i, V_i ∈ R^{n×d}) in a GQA setting, along with a per-group retention budget k, a Gaussian projection dimension r (default 20), and a fixed number of attention sinks s (default 4). It outputs compressed matrices K' ∈ R^{g×k×d} and V' ∈ R^{g×k×d} that replace the full cache during autoregressive decoding. A key design constraint is that selection is performed once during prefill (static) or adaptively per-head (AdaCurDKV), and the method must remain compatible with FlashAttention kernels that never materialize the full attention matrix.

## Method

CurDKV casts KV token selection as a CUR decomposition problem: it computes approximate leverage scores for each row of the key and value matrices by projecting them through a random Gaussian matrix G ∈ R^{d×r} (entries ~ N(0, 1/r)) and taking squared ℓ2 norms of the projected rows as proxies for exact leverage scores. The combined importance score for token j is the product of its key and value leverage scores, normalized, and the top-(k−s) tokens are retained alongside s initial attention sinks. The theoretical justification (Lemma 3.1) shows that the Frobenius-norm loss in the attention output is upper-bounded by √n‖V−V'‖_F + 2√n‖V'‖_F, establishing that minimizing the value-matrix approximation error directly controls end-to-end output fidelity. The adaptive variant AdaCurDKV extends this by selecting top-k tokens globally across all heads in a layer with a safeguard parameter α (default 0.20) ensuring each head retains a minimum fraction of its budget.

## Results

On LongBench (24 tasks) with LLaMA-3.1-8B-Instruct, CurDKV achieves 48.9% average accuracy at 30% compression and 35.7% at 90% compression, outperforming SnapKV (45.3% and 33.7%) by +3.6% and +2.0% respectively. On the Ruler needle-in-a-haystack benchmark, CurDKV reaches 98.7% average at 30% compression (LLaMA), substantially ahead of all baselines, and maintains 34.7% at 90% compression versus 22.0% for SnapKV and 13.9% for Knorm. The adaptive variant AdaCurDKV further improves to 49.1% (LongBench, 30%, LLaMA) and 39.1% (Ruler, 90%, LLaMA). CurDKV also reduces generation latency by up to 40% at high compression ratios.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SnapKV (the strongest attention-score-based baseline), CurDKV wins on LLaMA at both 30% (+3.6%, 48.9 vs 45.3) and 90% (+2.0%, 35.7 vs 33.7) compression on LongBench, and by a wide margin on Ruler NIAH (98.7% vs 80.4% at 30%; 34.7% vs 22.0% at 90% for LLaMA). On Mistral, CurDKV leads SnapKV at 30% (+2.9%) but trails at 90% (29.1% vs 32.6%), a gap the authors attribute to Mistral's sliding-window attention reducing head specialization. Against ChunkKV and Knorm (norm/position-based heuristics), CurDKV dominates across nearly all tasks, with the largest gaps on Ruler NIAH (98.7% vs 87.0% and 71.8% at 30%). StreamingLLM is consistently the weakest baseline, scoring 68.2% on Ruler NIAH at 30% versus CurDKV's 98.7%. The adaptive variant AdaCurDKV outperforms AdaSnapKV on LongBench (48.9% vs 45.5% at 30%, LLaMA) and Ruler (39.1% vs 24.4% at 90%), confirming that leverage-score-based adaptive budget allocation is more effective than attention-score-based allocation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in KV cache compression (SnapKV, H2O, ChunkKV, StreamingLLM), which ranks tokens by query-key attention scores, by instead selecting tokens based on leverage scores derived from CUR matrix decomposition of the key and value matrices. The mathematical foundation is drawn from classical CUR decomposition theory (Mahoney & Drineas, 2009), repurposed here to identify which rows of K and V best preserve the attention output softmax(QK^T)V. The adaptive variant (AdaCurDKV) builds on the budget-allocation framework of Ada-KV (Feng et al., 2025), but replaces attention-derived importance metrics with leverage-score-based ones. A key theoretical departure is Lemma 3.1, which formally shows that approximating the attention matrix QK^T does not bound the error in the final attention output, motivating the value-centric objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

KV cache memory grows linearly with sequence length, making compression essential for practical long-context inference; this paper provides a principled, value-aware alternative to the attention-score heuristic that is compatible with FlashAttention and GQA—two properties many prior methods lack. The consistent 2–10% accuracy gains over strong baselines (SnapKV, ChunkKV) across LLaMA, Mistral, and Qwen at aggressive compression ratios (up to 90%) suggest the attention-score proxy is systematically suboptimal. However, the method performs static selection during prefill and does not adapt to evolving query demands during generation, and the authors note marginal underperformance on Mistral's sliding-window attention at high compression. Open directions include query-aware dynamic selection, hybrid token-chunk strategies, and extending the CUR framework to multi-step generation.
