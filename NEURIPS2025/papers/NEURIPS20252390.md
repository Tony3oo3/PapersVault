## Problem

Existing model merging methods (Task Arithmetic, DARE, Ties-merging, PCB-merging) were designed for full fine-tuning and suffer severe performance drops when applied to parameter-efficient modules like LoRA, because PEFT matrices exhibit stark singular value distributions and wider parameter spreads that cause direction instability during merging. Furthermore, high-performing merging approaches rely on validation data or extra storage of seen tasks, preventing generalization to unseen tasks in real-world deployment.

## Input / Output

The method consumes pre-trained LoRA A and B matrices from N independently fine-tuned task models, a pruning rate k (fraction of small-magnitude parameters to zero out), and a global scaling factor λ (default 2). It produces a single merged set of LoRA modules that, when attached to the frozen base model, yields a universal model capable of handling all seen and unseen tasks simultaneously. Crucially, the entire pipeline is training-free and requires no validation data, no extra storage of task-specific information, and no explicit SVD computation—operating directly on the original low-rank matrices.

## Method

RobustMerge introduces the concept of direction robustness—maintaining singular vector directions during low-rank matrix merging—as the key to reducing task interference in PEFT merging. The method has three components: (1) magnitude-based pruning that zeros out the k-smallest parameters in both A and B matrices, reducing inter-task interference while preserving principal directions (unlike sign-based pruning in Ties-merging); (2) complementary parameter scaling that constructs per-row diagonal scaling coefficients S from the ratio of pre- and post-pruning row-wise L1 norms of A, then applies them to B to adaptively amplify tail singular values and compensate for the gap between large and small singular values; (3) cross-task normalization that divides each task's scaling coefficients by the sum across all tasks, balancing data-scale imbalances and improving unseen-task generalization. The approach exploits the asymmetric roles of A (approximately uniform, near-orthogonal) and B (Gaussian, knowledge-bearing) in LoRA to achieve singular value adaptation without explicit decomposition.

## Results

On the proposed MM-MergeBench (8 seen + 4 unseen multimodal generative tasks with LLaVA), RobustMerge achieves 57.33% average accuracy on seen tasks and 37.99% on unseen tasks, improving over the best baseline (Task Arithmetic) by 3.4% and 4.7% respectively. On general multimodal benchmarks, RobustMerge scores 87.2 on POPE, 1494.9 on MME, and 68.1 on MMBench, outperforming all baselines. On vision classification tasks with CLIP-ViT-B-32, RobustMerge reaches 55.9% average accuracy, a 4.4% gain over the best prior method (Ties-merging at 51.5%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

All four baselines (Task Arithmetic, DARE, Ties-merging, PCB-merging) were originally designed for full fine-tuning model merging and are re-implemented on LoRA modules for fair comparison. RobustMerge consistently outperforms every baseline on both seen and unseen tasks: it beats Task Arithmetic (53.93% seen / 33.31% unseen) by 3.4% and 4.7%, DARE (53.84% / 33.15%) by 3.5% and 4.8%, Ties-merging (53.09% / 33.14%) by 4.2% and 4.9%, and PCB-merging (53.70% / 33.53%) by 3.6% and 4.5%. Notably, the baselines suffer severe performance drops under PEFT merging—some even fall below zero-shot on individual tasks—while RobustMerge approaches multi-task learning performance (63.62% seen) without requiring joint training. The gap is particularly pronounced on unseen tasks, where baselines cluster around 33% and RobustMerge reaches 38%, confirming that direction robustness is the critical factor for generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the model merging paradigm established by Task Arithmetic (Ilharco et al., 2023) and its successors (Ties-merging, DARE, PCB-merging) from full fine-tuning to parameter-efficient tuning modules, specifically LoRA. The key conceptual departure is identifying that the dominant interference mechanism in PEFT merging is direction instability in low-rank SVD space rather than the sign conflict that motivates Ties-merging, and that the stark singular value distribution in LoRA matrices (unlike the concentrated distribution in FFT) makes tail-value direction preservation the critical factor. The method draws on the known asymmetry between LoRA's A and B matrices (AsymmetryLoRA, HydraLoRA) to construct scaling coefficients without explicit decomposition, and introduces cross-task normalization to balance data-scale imbalances across tasks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first training-free PEFT merging method that requires no validation data, no extra storage, and no test-time adaptation, making it directly deployable in real-world multi-task scenarios where data privacy and extensibility to unseen tasks are constraints. The MM-MergeBench (8 seen + 4 unseen multimodal generative tasks) fills a gap in evaluation infrastructure for PEFT merging. Open questions include whether the direction-robustness framework extends to other PEFT architectures (adapters, prompt tuning), whether explicit SVD-based algorithms could further improve performance, and whether the approach generalizes beyond LoRA/DoRA to more complex parameter-efficient schemes. The authors acknowledge they have not validated on more model structures or designed algorithms operating directly on decomposed matrices.
