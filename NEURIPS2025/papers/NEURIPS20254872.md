## Problem

Existing contextual dueling bandit algorithms either rely on online regression oracles that are computationally infeasible for complex function classes, or are restricted to finite action spaces. No prior work provides a computationally efficient, near-optimal regret guarantee for contextual dueling bandits over continuous (potentially infinite) action spaces using only offline regression oracles, which are the standard tool in modern machine learning.

## Input / Output

At each round the algorithm receives a context x_t from an arbitrary context space and, at epoch boundaries only (O(log T) or O(log log T) total calls), invokes an offline regression oracle trained on the batch of (context, action-pair, preference) tuples collected in the previous epoch. For the finite-K variant the oracle estimates a preference matrix M̂_m(x) ∈ P_K; for the continuous variant it estimates a score mapping φ_m: X → R^d. The algorithm outputs a joint distribution p_t over action pairs (either a K×K simplex or a probability measure on K×K ⊂ R^{2d}), from which a single duel (a_t, b_t) is sampled and a Bernoulli preference o_t is observed.

## Method

The core algorithm (Double-Monster / Double-Monster-Inf) operates in epochs with a predetermined schedule, calling the offline oracle only at epoch boundaries to amortize the iid-data requirement of offline regression over an inherently interactive process. For finite K, a Convex-Constraint-Solver computes p_t ∈ Δ_{K×K} satisfying a minimax inequality that bounds the empirical best-response regret by O(K²/γ_m) while the 1/p_t(i,j) term enforces sufficient exploration mass on every pair. For continuous action spaces K ⊂ R^d, this is replaced by a Cont-CvxConstraint-Solver that maximizes ⟨ab_q, φ_m(x_t)⟩ + (1/γ_m) log det(H_q) over probability measures q on K×K, where ab_q is the expected midpoint and H_q = E[(a−b)(a−b)ᵀ] + cI_d is a regularized covariance of action differences; the log-det barrier simultaneously encourages exploitation of the regressed score direction and exploration across the continuous space. The tuning parameter γ_m is set proportional to 1/√(E_off(δ/(2m²), n_m)), linking exploration strength to the oracle's estimation error. This yields a best-response regret of O(K√(E_off·T)) for finite K and Õ(√(dT)) for continuous spaces—the first such guarantee in the general continuous setting.

## Results

The paper establishes the first near-optimal regret bound for general contextual dueling bandits over continuous action spaces: Double-Monster-Inf achieves O~(sqrt(dT)) best-response regret (Theorem 11), where d is the embedding dimension and T is the number of rounds. For the finite K-armed setting, Double-Monster achieves O(K*sqrt(E_off,M(delta/logT, T))*T) regret (Theorem 5), which specializes to O(d*sqrt(T*log(T/d))) for linear predictors and O~(K*T^((beta+2d)/(2beta+2d))) for deep MLPs. Empirically, regret scales as O~(sqrt(dT)) across linear and quadratic feature maps for d in {2,5,10,15}, corroborating the theory. The algorithm requires only O(log T) or O(log log T) calls to an offline regression oracle, making it computationally feasible for complex function classes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares Double-Monster-Inf against four finite-armed, non-contextual dueling bandit baselines: RUCB [43], REX3 [18], DTS [35], and RMED [20], on three score configurations (Base 20 arms, Hard 20 arms, Worst-Case 40 arms). The authors report that Double-Monster-Inf outperforms RUCB, REX3, and RMED across all three configurations, while performing comparably to DTS (Double Thompson Sampling). Notably, these baselines are designed for finite-armed, non-contextual settings, so the comparison is somewhat asymmetric: Double-Monster-Inf operates in a fully continuous, contextual decision space with potentially infinite arms. The paper does not report exact numerical regret values in the text (results are shown in Figure 2), but the qualitative conclusion is that the proposed method dominates or matches all baselines despite the harder problem setting. The key advantage over prior contextual dueling methods (MinMaxDB [30], MaxInp [25]) is computational: replacing O(T) online oracle calls with O(log T) offline oracle calls while maintaining near-optimal regret.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the contextual dueling bandit framework of Saha and Krishnamurthy [30] (MinMaxDB), which required online regression oracles and was restricted to finite action spaces, by replacing online oracles with the computationally weaker (but more widely available) offline regression oracles and generalizing to continuous action spaces via a log-det-barrier regularized min-max solver inspired by Foster et al. [17]. The epoch-based architecture and convex-constraint-solver design follow the offline-oracle paradigm established by Simchi-Levi and Xu [32] (FALCON+) for reward-feedback contextual bandits, but the authors had to substantially modify the arm-selection rule to handle the dueling (pairwise preference) structure rather than scalar rewards. The work also resolves a key open problem from [30] regarding the feasibility of offline oracles in the interactive dueling setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Õ(√(dT)) regret guarantee for continuous-action contextual dueling bandits is the first of its kind and directly relevant to RLHF pipelines where action spaces (e.g., token sequences, continuous control) are effectively infinite and preference feedback is the natural signal. The demonstration that offline oracles suffice—despite the interactive data-generating process—removes a significant computational barrier for deploying these methods with complex function classes (deep networks, RKHS) where online oracles are infeasible. Open questions include extending beyond pairwise preferences to multi-set/ranking feedback, connecting the theory to full RLHF/RL settings, and validating the algorithms on real-world preference data rather than the synthetic environments used here.
