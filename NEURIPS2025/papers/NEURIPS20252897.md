## Problem

In semi-asynchronous federated learning (SAFL), gradient aggregation (e.g., FedSGD) achieves higher accuracy and faster convergence but suffers from severe fluctuations, while model aggregation (e.g., FedAvg) offers stability at the cost of slower convergence and suboptimal accuracy. No prior work provides a theoretical analysis of this disparity or a unified framework that jointly optimizes both aggregation strategies under SAFL conditions with client heterogeneity and stale updates.

## Input / Output

Each client receives the latest global model parameters and computes a pseudo-global gradient by differencing two consecutive global models, then calculates a cosine similarity between its own local update gradient and this pseudo-global gradient. The server additionally tracks each client's cumulative participation count to derive a normalized update speed metric. Outputs include an updated global model (via either weighted gradient descent or weighted parameter averaging), per-client adaptive learning rates, per-client aggregation weights (adjusted via a feedback mechanism for biased clients), and a four-way client type classification that drives the local training strategy.

## Method

FedQS employs a divide-and-conquer strategy that classifies clients into four types—Fast-but-Strongly-Biased (FSBC), Fast-and-Weakly-Biased (FWBC), Straggling-but-Weakly-Biased (SWBC), and Straggling-and-Strongly-Biased (SSBC)—using each client's normalized update speed and gradient-update similarity relative to network-wide averages. Each type receives a tailored local training policy: FSBC clients keep their learning rate unchanged but receive higher aggregation weights via a server-side feedback mechanism; FWBC clients have their learning rate reduced and a momentum term added to compensate; SWBC clients get an increased learning rate plus momentum; SSBC clients are further disambiguated via a local validation set into either a straggling case (momentum) or a dispersed-distribution case (feedback weighting). The server-side aggregation module (Mod③) assigns data-size-proportional weights by default but overrides them for feedback-triggered clients using an exponential-decay formula that accounts for staleness and the quadratic convergence-bound relationship. The framework is strategy-agnostic: the same client-side adaptation feeds either a gradient-descent update (FedQS-SGD) or a weighted parameter average (FedQS-Avg), and the paper proves exponential convergence rates (V^t with V ∈ (0,1)) under both strategies with non-vanishing error terms bounded by data heterogeneity (δ²) and gradient norm (G_c²).

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (ResNet-18), FedQS-SGD achieves 86.79% accuracy at x=1 (non-IID Dirichlet), outperforming the best gradient-aggregation baseline WKAFL (86.02%) and the best model-aggregation baseline M-step (82.46%). FedQS-Avg reaches 82.74% at x=1, surpassing M-step (82.46%). On Shakespeare (LSTM, R=600), FedQS-SGD attains 52.49% accuracy vs. 51.62% for FedAC (best gradient baseline). On UCI Adult (FCN), FedQS-SGD achieves 79.24% on Ethnicity and 78.74% on Gender, leading all baselines. In convergence speed, FedQS-SGD requires 127 epochs at x=1 vs. 136 for FedAC and 144 for FedSGD, while FedQS-Avg needs 119 epochs vs. 163 for M-step.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedQS-SGD outperforms all gradient-aggregation baselines on accuracy across every task: it leads WKAFL (best gradient baseline) by 4.22% on CIFAR-10 x=0.1 (68.88% vs. 64.66%), by 0.97% at x=0.5 (86.11% vs. 85.14%), and by 0.77% at x=1 (86.79% vs. 86.02%). FedQS-Avg surpasses M-step (best model-aggregation baseline) on CIFAR-10 x=0.1 (63.91% vs. 62.17%) and x=1 (82.74% vs. 82.46%), though M-step edges ahead at x=0.5 (80.49% vs. 80.26%). Both FedQS variants achieve the fastest convergence speed in nearly all settings, with FedQS-SGD requiring 239 epochs at x=0.1 versus 277 for WKAFL and 281 for FedSGD. Notably, SAFA, FedAT, and FedBuff all underperform their respective foundational algorithms (FedAvg, FedSGD), confirming that naive stability-oriented modifications sacrifice accuracy. On UCI Adult, FedQS-Avg achieves the best accuracy on Gender (78.94%) while FedQS-SGD leads on Ethnicity (79.24%), with both converging in under 35 epochs compared to 43–55 for FedAvg. Overall, FedQS wins or ties on accuracy in 13 of 14 task settings and achieves the best convergence speed in 12 of 14.

## Relation to Prior Work

FedQS builds directly on the foundational FedAvg and FedSGD aggregation strategies (McMahan et al., 2017) and borrows the pseudo-global gradient estimation technique from FedBuff (Nguyen et al., 2022) and FedAC (Zang et al., 2024) to compute local-global update similarity. It departs from prior SAFL work (SAFA, FedAT, FedMDS, WKAFL) that optimizes only one aggregation strategy and relies on server-centric designs with prior knowledge of client performance distributions. The key conceptual departure is a client-centric divide-and-conquer framework that classifies clients into four types based on update speed and gradient similarity, enabling adaptive local training without requiring the server to know client capabilities in advance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FedQS provides the first unified theoretical convergence analysis covering both gradient and model aggregation in SAFL, filling a gap where prior comparisons were purely empirical. The quantification of the 11.52% accuracy gap under combined staleness and heterogeneity gives practitioners a concrete understanding of when each strategy fails. Limitations include evaluation on moderate-scale models (ResNet-18, LSTM, FCN) across only three datasets, absence of differential privacy, and the assumption of bounded gradient norms that may not hold in all practical settings. Open questions include whether the four-type client classification generalizes to highly dynamic environments with frequent client churn and whether the approach extends to larger-scale models and more diverse non-IID distributions.
