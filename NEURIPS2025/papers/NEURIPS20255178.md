## Problem

Vanilla dataset distillation methods align synthetic samples to the overall data distribution, which is dominated by majority groups along protected attributes (PA) such as gender or race. This causes condensed datasets to inherit and amplify PA imbalance, producing models that are significantly biased toward minority groups. No prior work explicitly incorporated visual fairness into the dataset distillation objective.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255178/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method requires an original image dataset with both target attribute (TA) labels and protected attribute (PA) labels, plus a randomly initialized network parameterized by θ. It produces a condensed synthetic dataset S of size IPC × |Y| (e.g., 10, 50, or 100 images per class) where synthetic samples are balanced across PA groups. The framework is agnostic to image resolution (28×28 MNIST variants, 32×32 CIFAR-10, 64×64 Tiny-ImageNet) and supports both RGB and grayscale inputs, with no additional hyperparameters or learnable parameters introduced.

## Method

FairDD introduces synchronized matching: it partitions the original dataset into PA-wise subgroups within each TA class and reformulates the distillation objective (Eq. 4) to simultaneously align the synthetic dataset S to each group's target signal expectation with equal weight, removing the sample-ratio weighting that causes vanilla DDs to collapse toward majority groups. Theorem 5.1 proves the optimal solution is independent of group sample ratios, while Theorem 5.2 shows the FairDD loss upper-bounds the vanilla DD loss under convex distance functions, guaranteeing comprehensive TA distributional coverage. The framework is plug-and-play, requiring no architectural modifications to existing DM or GM methods (DM, DC, IDC, DREAM), and the equal-weight group alignment ensures each PA group contributes identically to the total loss during optimization.

## Results

On C-MNIST (FG) at IPC=10, vanilla DM achieves DEO_M=100.0 and DEO_A=99.96 (essentially maximally unfair), while DM+FairDD reduces these to 17.04 and 7.95, approaching the Whole-dataset baseline of 10.10/5.89. On the real-world CelebA dataset at IPC=10, DM+FairDD achieves DEO_M=9.37 and DEO_A=5.71, substantially outperforming DM (30.01/28.85) and even the full training set (24.85/24.16). In terms of accuracy, FairDD dramatically recovers performance lost by vanilla DDs: DM on C-MNIST (FG) drops from 97.71% (Whole) to 25.01% at IPC=10, but DM+FairDD recovers to 94.61%. Across all four DMF baselines (DM, DC, IDC, DREAM) and eight datasets, FairDD consistently reduces DEO_M by 50–90% relative to the vanilla counterpart while maintaining or improving TA accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FairDD is applied as a plug-in to four DMF baselines: DM (Distribution Matching, Zhao et al. WACV 2023), DC (Gradient Matching, Zhao et al. 2020), IDC (Kim et al. ICML 2022), and DREAM (Liu et al. ICCV 2023). On C-MNIST (FG) at IPC=10, DM+FairDD reduces DEO_M from 100.0 to 17.04 (−83%), DC+FairDD from 99.85 to 26.75 (−73%), IDC+FairDD from 100.0 to 12.24 (−88%), and DREAM+FairDD from 98.99 to 11.88 (−88%), all while boosting accuracy by 60–70 percentage points over the vanilla counterparts. On the real-world CelebA dataset, DC+FairDD achieves the best fairness (DEO_M=6.64, DEO_A=5.29), outperforming even the full training set (24.85/24.16), indicating that FairDD can produce fairer condensed data than the original biased dataset. On CIFAR10-S, DM+FairDD (DEO_M=31.75) and DC+FairDD (DEO_M=22.08) both substantially outperform their vanilla versions (59.20 and 42.23) and the Whole baseline (49.72). The only setting where FairDD does not clearly dominate is UTKFace, where the original bias is milder and the gap between vanilla and FairDD is smaller (e.g., DM: 51.40→37.40), though FairDD still wins on both fairness and accuracy across all four baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Data Match Framework (DMF) for dataset distillation—encompassing Distribution Matching (DM) and Gradient Matching (GM) methods—by reformulating the single global alignment objective into PA-wise synchronized sub-targets. It departs from vanilla DDs, which align synthetic data to the full (majority-dominated) distribution, by decomposing the target into protected-attribute groups and matching each group with equal weight, independent of group size. Conceptually, it bridges two previously separate literatures: dataset distillation (focused on target-attribute accuracy) and visual fairness (focused on model-level bias mitigation), positioning itself as a data-level fairness intervention rather than a model-level one. The unified objective in Eq. 4 is a minimal modification of the standard DMF loss (Eq. 1), requiring no architectural changes to existing DD methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to formally define and address fair dataset distillation, revealing that vanilla DDs not only inherit but actively amplify protected-attribute bias due to reduced sample counts—a finding that challenges the assumption that distillation is a neutral compression. The synchronized matching approach is plug-and-play (no extra hyperparameters or learnable parameters) and consistently improves both fairness and accuracy across DM, DC, IDC, and DREAM on 8+ datasets, making it a practical drop-in for existing DD pipelines. Key limitations include the requirement for PA labels (unavailable in many real-world settings) and the focus on image classification; the authors suggest pseudo-labeling or self-supervised approaches as future directions. Open questions include whether the equal-weight matching is optimal for highly imbalanced or multi-attribute scenarios, and how the approach extends to generative or multi-task distillation settings.
