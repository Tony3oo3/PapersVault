## Problem

Contrastive learning methods like SimCLR and MoCo are sensitive to the geometric structure of the mini-batch embedding cloud, yet prior work addresses this only at the pair level (hard-negative mining, debiased losses) while ignoring the batch-level spectrum. The paper identifies that training is fastest inside a moderate-diversity window—rich enough to avoid collapse but narrow enough to preserve directional signal—and asks whether this window can be characterized and controlled via non-asymptotic spectral bounds on the InfoNCE gradient.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard augmented image pairs (SimCLR/MoCo protocol) plus a candidate pool of pre-computed embeddings (e.g., 10× the batch size), from which it selects a spectrum-aware batch by monitoring the trace-one batch second moment Σ̂ and its top eigenvalue σ̂ as an anisotropy proxy. The theoretical output is a non-asymptotic band [LB, UB] on the squared per-sample InfoNCE gradient norm, parameterized by alignment ρ, temperature τ, and the negatives-only top eigenvalue σ*. Practically, the system emits a selected batch with controlled effective rank R_eff, learned visual representations (ResNet-18/50), and online diagnostics (50-step gradient variance, collapse flags at σ̂ > 0.99).

## Method

The core theoretical contribution (Theorem 1.1) decomposes the InfoNCE gradient δ_i = M_i − z_{i+} into three components—softmax error, finite-sample noise, and adaptive spectral fluctuation—yielding an upper band with leading 1/τ² terms and higher-order 1/τ⁴, 1/τ⁶ corrections controlled by σ* = λ_max(Σ̃_i⁻), the negatives-only second-moment top eigenvalue. A deterministic batch proxy σ* ≤ (n/(n−2))σ̂ makes the bound computable per-batch without per-anchor eigendecompositions. For practical intervention, the paper proposes spectrum-aware batch selection using effective rank R_eff = 1/tr(Σ̂²) as a diversity proxy: three pool-based policies (P1 stability-first, P2 update-magnitude, P3 balanced window) and a Greedy-m element-wise builder that incrementally adds the embedding minimizing the Rayleigh quotient q_B(z) = zᵀΣ_B z, equivalently maximizing Frobenius diversity. In-batch whitening (Σ_ε^{-1/2} re-normalization) pushes σ̂ toward 1/d, reducing 50-step gradient variance by 1.37× and matching the theoretical variance bound. On ImageNet-100, Greedy-64 cuts wall-clock time to 67.5% top-1 by 15% versus random sampling at equal accuracy, with the speedup robust to augmentation strength and queue size.

## Results

On ImageNet-100 (MoCo v2, ResNet-18, queue K=65,536), Pool-P3 achieves 74.5% top-1 in 132.8 epochs to 70% accuracy, outperforming Random (74.2%, 146.3 epochs) by 9.4% in speed with no accuracy loss. On ImageNet-1k (SimCLR, ResNet-50, batch 4096), Pool-P3 raises effective rank to R_eff/C = 0.96 and improves linear-probe top-1 to 71.4% versus 69.8% for Random (R_eff/C = 0.79). Greedy-64 cuts wall-clock time to 67.5% top-1 by ~15% versus random sampling on ImageNet-100, and in-batch whitening reduces 50-step gradient variance by 1.37×, matching the theoretical upper bound.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Pool-P3 outperforms both established unsupervised curricula—Hard Negative Mixing (74.4%, 139.6 epochs) and Distance-Weighted Sampling (74.3%, 140.2 epochs)—on both top-1 accuracy and convergence speed (132.8 epochs to 70%), with gains of ~1.5–2.5% in speed and 0.1–0.2 points in accuracy. Against vanilla SimCLR (Random), Pool-P3 achieves a 9.4% ± 1.3% speedup (10-seed, p < 0.01) while matching or slightly exceeding final accuracy. The supervised SupCon oracle (75.9%, 125.1 epochs) remains the upper bound, but Pool-P3 closes most of the gap without any labels. Greedy-64, the lightweight element-wise variant, matches Pool-P3's spectral diversity and reaches 90% of Pool-P3's final accuracy faster than both Random and Pool-P3 on CIFAR-10, at ~1% on-GPU overhead. On ImageNet-1k, Pool-P3 lifts linear-probe accuracy by 1.6 points over Random (71.4% vs. 69.8%) by pushing R_eff/C from 0.79 to 0.96, confirming that spectral diversity is the key driver of downstream performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant pair-level perspective in contrastive learning (hard-negative mining, debiased losses) by identifying a batch-level signal: the spectrum of the embedding cloud. It builds directly on the SimCLR and MoCo frameworks and the InfoNCE loss, but reframes the analysis around the batch second-moment matrix and its top eigenvalue, connecting gradient-norm behavior to effective rank and anisotropy. The effective-rank-to-accuracy link draws on RankMe (Garrido et al., 2023), and the whitening intervention relates to Ermolov et al. (2021), but the non-asymptotic spectral band on the InfoNCE gradient and the greedy element-wise spectral builder are new contributions that unify these threads under a single geometric diagnostic.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The spectral band gives practitioners a cheap, per-batch diagnostic (top eigenvalue of the batch covariance) that predicts gradient magnitude and flags impending collapse, bridging a gap between alignment/uniformity heuristics and concrete optimization theory. The ~15% wall-clock reduction with no accuracy loss from Greedy-64 is practically useful for large-scale contrastive training, and the moderate-diversity-window framing offers a principled alternative to ad-hoc hard-negative strategies. Open questions include whether the spectral band extends to non-contrastive objectives, LLMs, and sequence models where anisotropy is a known bottleneck, and whether the independence assumption on negatives can be fully relaxed for queue-based methods with strong momentum correlations.
