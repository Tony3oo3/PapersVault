## Problem

Multi-teacher knowledge distillation improves student generalization by aggregating diverse supervisory signals, but requires training and maintaining multiple large teacher networks, incurring prohibitive computational and memory costs. The recent single-teacher augmentation approach TeKAP reduces this cost by applying random noise perturbations, yet the resulting diversity is uncontrolled and semantically unstructured. The paper asks whether more structured, explicitly controlled augmentation from a single teacher can yield more informative and complementary views for distillation.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-trained teacher's intermediate feature vectors (d_T-dimensional) and logit probability distributions (C-dimensional) extracted from standard RGB images (32×32 CIFAR-100, 224×224 ImageNet, 96×96 STL-10, 64×64 TinyImageNet, or variable-resolution Carvana segmentation images). It produces N=5 augmented feature-logit pairs per input, which are ensembled with the original teacher output to form a (N+1)-way ensemble supervisory signal for the student. The student outputs class labels (classification) or pixel-wise masks (segmentation), with the augmented views themselves never used at inference time.

## Method

Angular-KD attaches N lightweight linear branches (view augmentation heads) to a frozen teacher, each initialized orthogonally and fed through a distinct dropout mask to generate diverse feature and logit views. Two angular diversity objectives are jointly optimized: a constrained inter-angle loss that maximizes pairwise cosine separation among augmented views while a learnable margin γ keeps each view within a bounded angular distance of the original teacher output, and an intra-angle loss that minimizes pairwise cosine similarity of the offset vectors (teacher minus each augmented view) to enforce even angular spread around the teacher. The augmented views are ensembled with the original teacher output and distilled into the student via CRD (feature-level) and KL-divergence (logit-level) losses alongside standard cross-entropy. Theoretically, the paper shows these objectives increase the ensemble diversity metric D, which tightens the upper bound on the ensemble's expected cross-entropy loss, providing a formal link between angular diversification and improved student accuracy.

## Results

On CIFAR-100 with combined logit+feature distillation (KD+CRD), Angular-KD achieves 76.46% top-1 accuracy (RN32×4→RN8×4), 78.94% (RN32×4→WideRN-40-2), and 76.42% (RN32×4→WideRN-16-2), consistently outperforming both the unaugmented baseline and TeKAP across all six teacher-student configurations. On ImageNet (RN34→RN18), Angular-KD reaches 71.07% top-1 and 90.39% top-5 accuracy, surpassing TeKAP (70.67%/89.92%) and the unaugmented KD baseline (70.41%/89.88%). The method also improves binary segmentation on Carvana (IoU 95.72 vs. 94.83) and imbalanced CIFAR-100 (35.66% on the imbalanced subset vs. 34.98% for TeKAP).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against TeKAP [20], the primary single-teacher augmentation competitor, Angular-KD wins on every configuration tested: on CIFAR-100 combined distillation it gains +0.48 to +1.04 points (e.g., 76.46 vs. 75.98 for RN32×4→RN8×4; 78.94 vs. 78.68 for RN32×4→WideRN-40-2), and on ImageNet it achieves 71.07% vs. 70.67% top-1. Against multi-teacher baselines, Angular-KD (single teacher) outperforms Ensemble Distillation [3] (5 teachers: 76.33 vs. 76.31 on WideRN; 74.76 vs. 74.67 on VGG), TAKD [36] (4 teachers: 76.33 vs. 75.04; 74.76 vs. 73.67), and DGKD [45] (4 teachers: 76.33 vs. 76.24; 74.76 vs. 74.40), while using only 2.40M vs. 11.28M (WideRN) or 11.04M vs. 47.31M (VGG) teacher parameters. The plug-and-play experiments (Table 2) confirm that Angular-KD consistently improves DKD, MLKD, and ReviewKD, whereas TeKAP sometimes degrades performance (e.g., ReviewKD drops from 75.63 to 75.32 with TeKAP on RN32×4→RN8×4 but rises to 75.78 with Angular-KD). In the multi-teacher plug-and-play setting (supplementary Table 10), Angular-KD integrated into TAKD also surpasses TeKAP-integrated TAKD by 0.25–1.26 points across all assistant configurations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the single-teacher knowledge augmentation paradigm introduced by TeKAP [20], which generates diverse views via random stochastic perturbations, by replacing undirected noise with two structured angular diversity objectives (constrained inter-angle and intra-angle losses) that explicitly control the geometric arrangement of augmented views around the teacher output. The theoretical justification draws on Ortega et al. [38], who formalized a generic ensemble diversity metric linked to the upper bound on expected ensemble loss; Angular-KD shows its angular objectives directly increase this metric. The paper also distinguishes itself from angle-based losses in face recognition (ArcFace, CosFace, SphereFace), which impose angular margins between different classes, whereas here angular maximization operates across multiple views of the same teacher to harness diversity for knowledge transfer rather than class separation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Angular-KD offers a practical, plug-and-play alternative to multi-teacher distillation that achieves comparable or better accuracy with a single teacher and only ~0.14M additional parameters, making it attractive for resource-constrained deployment. The clean theoretical link between angular diversity and ensemble loss bounds provides a principled design rationale rather than a purely heuristic one. A key limitation acknowledged by the authors is that all augmented views remain semantically bounded by the original teacher's knowledge, so the method cannot introduce fundamentally new information that distinct teacher architectures might provide. Open questions include whether the angular objectives can be extended to incorporate external semantic signals (e.g., from auxiliary data or contrastive pretraining) while preserving the single-teacher efficiency advantage.
