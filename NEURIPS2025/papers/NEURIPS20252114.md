## Problem

Korean TTS research lags behind English and Mandarin due to the absence of high-quality, phonologically balanced Korean speech corpora, language-specific text normalization for context-dependent numerals and English loanwords, standardized evaluation benchmarks covering diverse acoustic conditions, and models explicitly optimized for Korean's Jamo-based syllable structure. Existing Korean datasets suffer from limited speaker diversity, predominantly short utterances (3–4 s), and inconsistent handling of mixed-language tokens.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252114/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system accepts Korean text containing numerals, English abbreviations, and special characters (normalized via N2gk+ into pure Hangul graphemes) along with a reference audio prompt for zero-shot speaker conditioning, and produces 24 kHz synthesized speech. The N2gk+ module operates as a plug-and-play text preprocessor that can be inserted before any TTS model's input, converting mixed-language text into consistent Korean pronunciations without requiring manual annotations or ASR models.

## Method

The pipeline constructs CoreaSpeech (700 h, 21,449 speakers) from 2,058 h of raw Korean data through speaker diarization, LNCat-based text categorization, N2gk+ normalization, and a novel Jamo Bigram coreset selection that represents each utterance via four adjacent Jamo pair types (IC-MV, MV-FC, FC-next-IC, MV-next-IC) and applies probabilistic retention p = exp[−β(global_count − t)] to over-represented pairs while preserving all rare pairs below threshold t. Audio quality filtering uses a dynamic, dataset-specific UTMOS threshold computed via median − k·MAD to retain speaker diversity, and short utterances are cross-fade concatenated to balance durations up to 30 s. For modeling, PEFT-TTS fine-tunes the multilingual F5-TTS (flow-matching + DiT) using only 5.81M LoRA parameters (rank-16 on DiT self-attention, rank-64 on the Prompt Adapter with DropPath 0.3) plus full fine-tuning of the ConvNeXt-V2 text encoder, trained on a single GPU.

## Results

On the Korean Universal Testset (average across Clean, Noisy, and Numeric subsets), PEFT-TTS achieves the best CER (5.35%) and WER (15.76%) among all compared models, approaching ground-truth levels (CER 6.19%, WER 17.01%). Speaker similarity (SIM) reaches 0.76, close to GT (0.81) and CosyVoice 2 (0.74), while UTMOS scores 2.80, trailing CosyVoice 2 (3.21) and Llasa (3.22). On the Numeric subset specifically, PEFT-TTS achieves the lowest CER (5.97%) and WER (19.71%), substantially outperforming CosyVoice 2 (CER 17.65%, WER 52.60%) and Zonos (CER 21.21%, WER 64.08%), demonstrating the effectiveness of the N2gk+ text preprocessor for context-dependent Korean numeral pronunciation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PEFT-TTS (5.81M trainable LoRA parameters) achieves the best CER and WER across all three subsets of the Korean Universal Testset, outperforming XTTS by ~40% in average CER (5.35 vs. 9.37) and CosyVoice 2 by ~63% (5.35 vs. 14.28). The advantage is most dramatic on the Numeric subset, where PEFT-TTS's CER of 5.97% is nearly 3× lower than CosyVoice 2 (17.65%) and 3.5× lower than Zonos (21.21%), directly attributable to the N2gk+ text preprocessor. On speaker similarity, PEFT-TTS (0.76 avg) is competitive with CosyVoice 2 (0.74) and Zonos (0.72) but slightly below GT (0.81). The main trade-off is UTMOS: PEFT-TTS scores 2.80 on average, below CosyVoice 2 (3.21), Llasa (3.22), and Zonos (3.09), suggesting the smaller model produces slightly less perceptually natural audio despite superior linguistic accuracy. The N2gk+ module also demonstrates plug-and-play utility: when applied to CosyVoice 2's numeric input, it reduces CER from 17.65% to 6.90% and WER from 52.60% to 22.95%, and for Zonos it drops CER from 21.21% to 7.29%, confirming that the text preprocessor generalizes across architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on F5-TTS (a flow-matching, DiT-based non-autoregressive TTS model) by applying cross-lingual parameter-efficient fine-tuning (LoRA adapters on DiT and a Prompt Adapter with DropPath) to specialize it for Korean. It departs from prior coreset selection approaches that rely on embedding-based diversity metrics (e.g., BERT/wav2vec 2.0 joint features) by introducing a linguistically grounded Jamo-bigram selection strategy that explicitly models Korean's syllable-block phonology. The text normalization module N2gk+ addresses a gap left by existing Korean NLP tools (g2pK, KoNLPy) that struggle with context-dependent numerals, English loanwords, and code-switching, and the dynamic per-dataset UTMOS thresholding replaces the static-threshold filtering used in prior large-scale corpus pipelines like Emilia-Pipe.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Korean TTS has lagged behind English and Mandarin due to the absence of large, well-preprocessed corpora, standardized benchmarks, and language-specific preprocessing; CoreaSpeech (700 h, 21,449 speakers) and the Korean Universal Testset (clean/noisy/numeric) directly fill these gaps and are publicly released. The demonstration that N2gk+ acts as a consistent plug-and-play improvement across four architecturally diverse TTS systems (XTTS, CosyVoice 2, Llasa, Zonos) suggests the normalization bottleneck is a shared, under-addressed problem in multilingual TTS beyond Korean. Open questions include whether the duration-appending strategy can be made semantically coherent without explicit style/emotion annotations, whether the 700-hour scale is sufficient to close the remaining UTMOS gap versus CosyVoice 2, and whether the Jamo-bigram coreset principle generalizes to other agglutinative or syllable-block languages.
