## Problem

Most graph autoencoders produce node-level embeddings rather than a single graph-level representation, and the few graph-level variants (GraphVAE, PIGVAE) either rely on expensive NP-hard graph matching solvers or require the ground-truth graph size at decode time. This paper addresses the gap of building a truly graph-level autoencoder that encodes and decodes graphs of varying sizes into a shared Euclidean space without external solvers or size conditioning.

## Input / Output

The input graph is represented as a padded triplet (masking vector h ∈ {0,1}^N, node feature matrix F ∈ R^{N×n_f}, edge feature tensor C ∈ R^{N×N×n_c}) where all graphs are padded to a fixed maximum size N. The featurizer augments raw node/edge labels with k-th order diffusion features and shortest-path encodings, and deliberately adds noise to the input (but not the target) to break graph symmetries. The primary output is a token-based graph embedding Z ∈ R^{K×D} (flattened to a single vector for downstream use), alongside a fully reconstructed graph and a differentiable matching matrix T ∈ π_N between input and output nodes.

## Method

GRALE uses an Evoformer encoder (from AlphaFold) to produce hidden node/edge representations, a transformer decoder to pool these into K graph-level tokens, and a novel Evoformer Decoder (Evoformer augmented with cross-attention blocks) to reconstruct the graph from the embedding. The key training innovation is a matching-free Optimal Transport loss: rather than solving the NP-hard permutation alignment, a learnable matcher computes an affinity matrix from encoder/decoder node embeddings and projects it onto the bistochastic polytope via 100 Sinkhorn iterations, yielding a differentiable transport plan T. The OT loss then compares original and reconstructed graphs weighted by T, and the entire pipeline (encoder, decoder, matcher) is trained end-to-end by backpropagation through the unrolled Sinkhorn iterations. At test time, Sinkhorn is replaced by the Hungarian algorithm to obtain a discrete permutation for exact matching.

## Results

On reconstruction, GRALE achieves an edit distance of 0.02 and 99.2% graph isomorphism accuracy on COLORING, 0.11/93.0% on PUBCHEM 16, and 0.78/66.8% on PUBCHEM 32, substantially outperforming PIGVAE (0.09/85.3%, 1.69/41.0%, 2.53/24.9%) and GraphVAE (2.13/35.9%, 3.72/7.8%, N.A.). On downstream MoleculeNet tasks, GRALE achieves the best or near-best MAE on QM9 (0.015), QM40 (0.018), and FREESOLV (0.272), and competitive ROC-AUC on BBBP (0.731) and BACE (0.821). For graph prediction, GRALE reaches 89.7% GI accuracy on COLORING 10 and 86.0% on COLORING 15, far exceeding Any2Graph (84.4%/43.8%) and RelationFormer (18.1%/22.0%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GRALE decisively outperforms both prior graph-level AutoEncoders: on PUBCHEM 32 reconstruction, it reduces edit distance from 2.53 (PIGVAE) to 0.78 and lifts GI accuracy from 24.9% to 66.8%; GraphVAE is infeasible at this scale. On downstream MoleculeNet regression, GRALE beats PIGVAE on QM9 (0.015 vs 0.031 MAE) and QM40 (0.018 vs 0.019), and beats contrastive baselines SimGrace (0.110) and Infograph (0.122) by a wide margin on QM9; however, Infograph edges out GRALE on ESOL (0.255 vs 0.274) and LIPO (0.495 vs 0.511), and SimGrace leads on BACE classification (0.866 vs 0.821 ROC-AUC). For graph prediction, GRALE dominates on COLORING (89.7% vs 84.4% GI Acc. over Any2Graph on COLORING 10) but trails Any2Graph on molecular prediction (QM9 edit distance 3.62 vs 2.61; GDB13 4.43 vs 3.63). In graph matching, GRALE's matcher produces tighter edit-distance upper bounds than all Pygmtools solvers (8.92 vs 19.66–21.10 on COLORING) while being 500–700× faster than A* (0.005s vs 3.49s per pair).

## Relation to Prior Work

GRALE sits at the intersection of three prior threads: the Evoformer attention architecture from AlphaFold (Jumper et al., 2021), the Optimal Transport-inspired reconstruction loss from Any2Graph (Krzakala et al., 2024), and the learnable matching strategy from PIGVAE (Winter et al., 2021). The key conceptual departure from PIGVAE is twofold: (1) the OT loss is provably stricter than PIGVAE's reordering loss (Proposition 4 shows PIGVAE's loss can vanish without isomorphic reconstruction), and (2) the matcher uses Sinkhorn-based d-dimensional matching rather than 1D soft-sorting, while also eliminating the need for ground-truth graph size. The architectural choice to use Evoformer for both encoding and a novel Evoformer Decoder (with cross-attention) is a direct extension of AlphaFold's protein-structure module to the graph autoencoding setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GRALE demonstrates that a single pretrained graph-level embedding supports a remarkably broad range of downstream operations—classification, regression, interpolation, property-guided editing, and graph matching—through simple vector arithmetic, which is a practical recipe for molecular representation learning at scale (84M molecules). The main limitation is cubic complexity in graph size (capped at N=32), which restricts applicability to small molecules; the authors suggest approximate attention and accelerated Sinkhorn as paths forward. Open questions include whether the latent space can be restructured to encode chemical properties rather than structural features (node labels, graph size), and whether the framework extends to a full graph VAE for generative tasks or to the flagship problem of de-novo molecular elucidation from mass spectra.
