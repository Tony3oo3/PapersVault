## Problem

Existing event-RGB semantic segmentation methods treat the two modalities as directly fusable, ignoring their fundamental heterogeneity: events highlight motion edges asynchronously while RGB provides dense contextual appearance. Under extreme conditions (low light, severe motion), this mismatch causes feature-level misalignment and inferior optimization, particularly when one modality is degraded or partially occluded. The paper identifies semantic edge as an underexploited intermediate commonality that can bridge this gap.

## Input / Output

The method takes a paired RGB image (H×W×3) and a corresponding event voxel grid (H×W×B with B=5 temporal bins) as input, and produces a pixel-wise semantic segmentation mask over 11 driving-scene categories. A pre-trained VQ-VAE edge dictionary (K=128 codebook entries, n=256-dim embeddings) is trained separately on boundary maps derived from ground-truth masks and then frozen; the tokenizer downsamples spatial dimensions by 4×. Event inputs are provided either as a fixed 50 ms time window (DERS-XS/XR) or a fixed count of 50,000 events per voxel grid (DSEC-Semantic/Xtrm).

## Method

ESC builds a shared discrete latent edge space via a VQ-VAE-trained edge dictionary, then performs bidirectional re-coding: semantic edge ground truth is mapped to a categorical prior distribution q(K|B), while modality-specific edge features (extracted via a decoupled edge resolver for RGB and directly from event features) are encoded through MLP heads into posterior distributions p(K|I) and p(K|E). Cross-entropy between q and p realigns heterogeneous features into a unified semantic space, and argmax key maps query the dictionary to retrieve re-coded edge features. A Re-coded Consolidation module uses multi-head attention with learnable noise embeddings to fuse image contextual features with both modalities' re-coded edge features, while an Uncertainty Optimization module derives per-pixel confidence/uncertainty from the edge distributions and performs confidence-weighted cross-modality attention to jointly optimize edge features. The final mask is predicted by concatenating the consolidated and optimized features into an MLP head, trained with L = L_pred + β·L_edge.

## Results

On the true-labeled synthetic dataset DERS-XS, ESC achieves 67.10% mIoU and 75.26% mACC, outperforming the strongest multi-modality baseline CMNeXt (64.55% mIoU) by 2.55%. On the real-world fine-tuning dataset DERS-XR, ESC reaches 65.22% mIoU, surpassing EISNet by 3.41%. Under extreme degraded conditions (DSEC-Xtrm), ESC attains 50.87% mIoU vs. 48.76% for EISNet, and under spatial occlusion (E-RGB masking), ESC degrades only 3.23% mIoU while CMNeXt degrades 10.85%, demonstrating superior resilience.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ESC outperforms all six baselines across all four datasets. Against the strongest multi-modality competitor CMNeXt [51], ESC gains +2.55% mIoU on DERS-XS (67.10 vs. 64.55), +4.27% on DERS-XR (65.22 vs. 60.95), +2.01% on DSEC-Semantic (71.04 vs. 69.03), and +5.71% on DSEC-Xtrm (50.87 vs. 45.16). EISNet [46] is the closest rival on DERS-XR (61.81% mIoU) and DSEC-Xtrm (48.76%), where ESC leads by 3.41% and 2.11% respectively; however, EISNet underperforms on DERS-XS (60.68%) and DSEC-Semantic (64.67%), likely due to sensitivity to input cropping strategy. TokenFusion [41] and CMX [50] trail by 10.88% and 3.98% mIoU on DERS-XS respectively, indicating that naive token-level or recalibration-based fusion is insufficient under extreme conditions. Uni-modality baselines (SegFormer RGB at 53.21%, SegFormer-E at 37.32%, EvSegFormer at 31.85% mIoU on DERS-XS) confirm that neither modality alone is adequate, and the multi-modality gap is largest under extreme conditions where RGB information is severely degraded.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the event-RGB semantic segmentation line (CMX, CMNeXt, EISNet, TokenFusion) by introducing a VQ-VAE-based discrete edge dictionary as a shared latent space that bridges the inherent heterogeneity between event and RGB features. It draws on edge-aware segmentation (decoupled body-edge supervision) and cross-modal discrete representation learning (shared embedding spaces in vision-language and video-audio tasks), but departs from prior fusion methods by performing bidirectional 're-coding' between modality features and a categorical edge distribution, coupled with uncertainty-weighted joint optimization. The construction of true-labeled extreme-condition datasets (DERS-XS, DERS-XR, DSEC-Xtrm) also departs from prior reliance on RGB-pseudo-labeled benchmarks like DSEC-Semantic.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: existing event-RGB fusion methods degrade sharply under modality imbalance or failure (low light, occlusion), and the new datasets provide a more reliable evaluation protocol than pseudo-labeled benchmarks. The spatial occlusion resilience evaluation (first without fine-tuning) and the edge-dictionary domain transferability results offer useful practical signals for deployment. However, the framework is limited to event+RGB fusion, and extending the re-coding mechanism to additional modalities (infrared, LiDAR) or to other tasks remains open. The relatively high FLOPs (95G) compared to baselines also limits immediate real-time deployment despite the reduced-backbone variant.
