## Problem

Existing learned 3D reconstruction models (DUSt3R, Fast3R, MV-DUSt3R) treat multi-view inputs as unstructured image collections, ignoring the synchronized multi-camera rig structure that is standard in autonomous driving and embodied AI. This leaves a critical geometric prior untapped—especially when field-of-view overlap between cameras is limited—and no prior learned method can discover rig calibration from images when metadata is absent.

## Input / Output

Rig3R takes N RGB images (resized to 512×512) flattened into a single sequence, where each image optionally carries a metadata tuple of discrete camera ID, continuous timestamp (seconds), and a dense rig-relative raymap (H×W×6). The model outputs, per image, a 3D pointmap (3×H×W) in the first frame's coordinate system, a confidence map, a global pose raymap (H×W×6) encoding camera parameters relative to the first image, and a rig-relative raymap (H×W×6) encoding camera parameters in a rig-centric frame decoupled from ego-motion. Camera intrinsics and extrinsics are recovered in closed form from the raymaps via angular constraints and SVD-based rotation alignment, avoiding iterative optimization.

## Method

Rig3R uses a shared ViT-Large image encoder (initialized from DUSt3R) followed by a second ViT-Large decoder trained from scratch that performs joint self-attention across all views in a shared latent space. The key architectural contribution is rig-aware metadata conditioning: camera ID, timestamp, and rig raymap patches are encoded via 1D sine-cosine or linear projections and added to patch tokens, with each field randomly dropped at 50% probability during training to ensure robustness to missing information. Three multitask prediction heads—DPT for pointmap and confidence, and lightweight MLPs for the pose and rig raymaps—produce tightly coupled outputs where pointmaps are expected to lie along pose-raymap rays and the two raymaps are related through ego-motion. Data sampling flattens all frames into a single sequence (rather than a rigid C×T grid), unifying structured and unstructured sampling and exposing the model to all possible sub-rigs and partial observations. Training uses a confidence-weighted pointmap loss plus raymap direction and center losses, optimized over 24-frame samples on 128 H100 GPUs for 250k steps.

## Results

On Waymo, Rig3R_Calib achieves 82.1 mAA@30° for pose estimation and 0.2 Chamfer distance for pointmap reconstruction, outperforming all baselines by 17–45% mAA. On the unseen WayveScenes101 dataset, Rig3R_Calib reaches 65.2 mAA@30° and 2.2 Chamfer, while Rig3R_Unstr (no metadata) still achieves 25.7 mAA and 4.3 Chamfer, surpassing all feedforward baselines. All results are obtained in a single forward pass (~5.7 s per 24-frame sample) without post-processing or iterative refinement.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Rig3R_Calib dominates all four baselines on both datasets. Against COLMAP (22.7 mAA on Waymo, 24.4 on WS101), Rig3R_Calib improves mAA by ~59 and ~41 points respectively, while also running in seconds versus minutes. Against MV-DUSt3R (15.8 / 13.1 mAA), the gap is even wider at ~66 and ~52 mAA points. Against DUSt3R-GA (37.5 / 48.6 mAA), Rig3R_Calib leads by 44.6 and 16.6 mAA points; notably, DUSt3R-GA is the closest competitor on WS101 due to its global optimization step, but still trails Rig3R_Calib by 16.6 mAA. Against Fast3R (20.6 / 20.7 mAA), Rig3R_Calib improves by 61.5 and 44.5 mAA points, and Rig3R_Unstr (74.6 / 25.7) still exceeds Fast3R even without any rig metadata. On pointmap reconstruction (Table 2), Rig3R_Calib achieves 0.2 Chamfer on Waymo versus 3.9 for Fast3R and 8.6 for DUSt3R-GA, confirming the advantage extends to 3D structure estimation.

## Relation to Prior Work

Rig3R extends the DUSt3R/Fast3R lineage of single-pass transformer-based multi-view reconstruction by introducing optional rig-aware metadata embeddings (camera ID, timestamp, rig raymap) with structured dropout, and by replacing pointmap-based pose inference with a dedicated raymap representation that enables closed-form camera parameter recovery. It departs from prior learned methods (DUSt3R, Fast3R, MV-DUSt3R, VGGT) that treat input views as unstructured collections, and from classical rig-aware pipelines (COLMAP with rig constraints, Kaess & Dellaert, Carrera et al.) that require known calibration and iterative optimization, by making rig information optional and learning to infer it from image content when absent. The raymap output representation, while building on the geometric intuition of pinhole camera models, is a novel prediction target that decouples pose estimation from 3D geometry prediction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Rig3R addresses a practical gap in learned 3D vision: real-world embodied systems (autonomous driving, robotics) almost always use synchronized multi-camera rigs, yet prior feedforward models ignore this structure. The rig discovery capability—inferring camera groupings and relative poses from completely unordered images without timestamps—is, to the authors' knowledge, the first such result in either learned or classical settings, opening a path toward self-calibrating multi-camera systems. Key limitations include dependence on data diversity in rig configurations (the authors note this as the main performance bottleneck), lack of explicit dynamic object modeling, and evaluation primarily on driving datasets. Open questions include whether the technique generalizes to non-driving rigs, how it scales to very large rigs (10+ cameras), and whether the raymap representation can be extended to handle non-pinhole or fisheye cameras.
