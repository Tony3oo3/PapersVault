## Problem

Extracting non-manifold meshes from learned unsigned distance fields (UDFs) remains unsolved: local SDF reconstruction introduces topological artifacts and cannot represent non-manifold geometry, dual-contouring variants produce spurious non-manifold edges in manifold regions, and double-layer methods (DCUDF) are structurally incapable of capturing true non-manifold topology. No existing method can correctly extract non-manifold surfaces from UDFs without requiring predefined material interface (MI) partition information.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-learned UDF (from point cloud, multi-view, or medial-axis pipelines) defined on a 256³ voxel grid within a [-0.6, 0.6]³ bounding box. It produces a multi-labeled material interface field that partitions the domain into labeled regions, from which a non-manifold triangular mesh is extracted. The method handles both closed and open surfaces by introducing an enclosing envelope Ω₁ (r₁=0.05) and a tighter inner envelope Ω₂ (r₂=0.01); the background outside Ω₁ is assigned a special label F₀ whose associated faces are discarded, enabling open-surface reconstruction.

## Method

MIND generates a material interface from a UDF in three stages. First, a local two-signed side field w_S^l is computed via a neighborhood-restricted generalized winding number over a point cloud sampled from the UDF's r₁ level set, distinguishing the two sides of local manifold patches. Second, 3D connected-component labeling on the two-signed field is followed by morphological erosion to sever narrow 'tubes' that incorrectly bridge non-manifold edges; an energy minimization solved by α-expansion then assigns labels to the eroded voxels, and over-segmented partitions are merged using the Ω₂ envelope as a geometric test. Third, the multi-labeled field is combined with the UDF and processed by a modified M3C algorithm (using w_S^l for edge intersection placement and suppressing background faces), followed by mesh refinement that minimizes UDF values with a group-wise Laplacian regularization—computing Laplacian terms separately within each labeled manifold patch to prevent face folding at non-manifold edges.

## Results

On ShapeNet-Car (UDFs learned by CAP-UDF), MIND achieves a mean Chamfer distance L2 of 2.917, outperforming DMUDF (3.086) and DCUDF (3.290). On DeepFashion3D (UDFs learned by DCUDF), MIND achieves a mean Chamfer distance L2 of 1.770, again the best among the three methods (DMUDF: 1.792, DCUDF: 1.825). MIND also achieves the lowest median and standard deviation on both datasets, indicating more consistent and stable reconstruction quality. Qualitative evaluations on multi-view (NU-NeRF, NeUDF) and medial-axis (Q-MDF) UDFs further confirm that MIND preserves correct non-manifold topology where baselines produce double-layered or topologically incorrect meshes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MIND is compared against two unsupervised UDF mesh-extraction baselines: DMUDF (a Dual Contouring variant) and DCUDF (a double-covering deformation method). On both ShapeNet-Car and DeepFashion3D, MIND achieves the lowest mean, median, and standard deviation Chamfer distance L2, with the largest margin over DCUDF (e.g., 2.917 vs. 3.290 on ShapeNet-Car). DMUDF, while capable of generating non-manifold edges, produces spurious non-manifold edges in regions that should remain manifold and is highly sensitive to UDF inaccuracies due to its octree-based subdivision criterion. DCUDF approximates non-manifold surfaces with double-layered manifold meshes, which visually align with the target but fail to preserve correct topology—geodesic distances cannot diffuse between the two layers. Qualitative comparisons on transparent-object multi-view UDFs (NU-NeRF, NeUDF) and medial-axis UDFs (Q-MDF) further demonstrate that MIND is the only method that simultaneously reconstructs the correct non-manifold topology and single-layered geometry, whereas both baselines either produce topologically incorrect double layers or fail entirely in non-manifold regions.

## Relation to Prior Work

MIND bridges two previously disconnected lines of work: unsigned distance field (UDF) learning for flexible topology representation, and material interface (MI) representations with multi-label Marching Cubes (M3C) for non-manifold mesh extraction. The local two-signed field is a direct adaptation of the generalized winding number, restricted to a local neighborhood to distinguish the two sides of a manifold patch. The key departure from prior UDF extraction methods (DCUDF's double-layered shrinking, DMUDF's uncontrolled Dual Contouring, gradient-based local SDF reconstruction) is the conceptual step of deriving a global multi-labeled partition from the UDF itself, eliminating the need for predefined region labels that traditional MI pipelines require.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Non-manifold structures are ubiquitous in anatomy, composites, multi-phase fluids, and transparent-object reconstruction, yet no prior method could extract them correctly from UDFs; MIND fills this gap with a practical post-processing pipeline. However, the method is fundamentally a zero-level-set extraction algorithm operating on pre-learned UDFs, and the authors explicitly identify learning MIs directly from raw data (point clouds, multi-view images) as the critical next step. Practical limitations include the computational cost of α-expansion over the full voxel grid, erosion-induced loss of small/thin regions, and complete failure on non-orientable surfaces where the two-sided field is undefined.
