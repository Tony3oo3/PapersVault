## Problem

Graph learning models are typically trained per-dataset, preventing knowledge transfer across heterogeneous graph domains with diverse feature spaces and structural patterns. Unlike NLP and CV, which benefit from unified token or pixel spaces, graphs lack a natural cross-domain representation medium, and existing self-supervised methods remain confined to single-graph settings. SSTAG addresses this by leveraging text as a domain-agnostic semantic bridge and co-distilling LLM semantic reasoning with GNN structural modeling into a single transferable framework.

## Input / Output

The method consumes text-attributed graphs where each node and edge carries a natural-language description alongside an adjacency matrix. For node- and edge-level tasks, PPR-based subgraph sampling extracts a local context (top-k=128 neighbors by importance score) rather than requiring the full graph, enabling scalability to 100M+ node datasets. At inference, only the lightweight student MLP (22M parameters) is deployed: the LM encodes raw node text into [CLS] embeddings, which are concatenated with precomputed PPR scores and passed through the MLP to yield d-dimensional node or graph embeddings for downstream linear probing.

## Method

SSTAG comprises three modules: (1) a Unified Graph Task template that standardizes node-, edge-, and graph-level predictions via PPR-weighted subgraph sampling; (2) a teacher model that cascades a Sentence Transformer (or larger LM) with a 3-layer GCN under a masked-language-modeling objective, where masked tokens are reconstructed using both textual context and GNN-propagated structural information; and (3) a knowledge distillation stage that transfers the teacher's fused representations into a structure-aware MLP student, which replaces explicit message passing with PPR-score feature injection and a memory bank of 256 learned anchors. Training jointly minimizes an MLM cross-entropy loss, a cosine-similarity student-teacher consistency loss, and a memory-anchor reconstruction loss. The key insight is that PPR scores encode sufficient topological priors for the MLP to approximate GNN message passing, yielding a 99.7% parameter reduction and 35% faster inference with only ~1% accuracy loss.

## Results

SSTAG is pretrained on ogbn-Paper100M (111M nodes) and evaluated via linear probing on 15 datasets across 5 domains. On node classification, it achieves 75.09% on Cora, 72.65% on Pubmed, 72.85% on ogbn-Arxiv, and 68.76% on WikiCS. On link prediction, it reaches 88.64% ROC-AUC on FB15K237 and 82.42% on WN18RR. On graph-level tasks, it attains 82.06% on BACE classification and 1.043 RMSE on ESOL regression, outperforming all baselines on every dataset.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SSTAG outperforms all nine baselines on every dataset and task. Against the strongest TAG-specific baseline UniGraph, SSTAG gains +0.44% on Cora, +1.81% on Pubmed, +1.96% on ogbn-Arxiv, +3.29% on WikiCS, +1.69% on Products, +3.63% on FB15K237, +1.87% on WN18RR, and +1.22% on ML1M. The LLM-based Graph-LLM is competitive on link prediction (82.47% on FB15K237) but trails SSTAG by 6.17 points there and by 6.6 points on WikiCS node classification. Among GNN-only baselines, GAT leads on Cora (66.29%) but is surpassed by SSTAG by 8.8 points; contrastive methods (GraphCL, BGRL) and generative methods (GraphMAE, GraphMAE2) all fall 1–10 points below SSTAG depending on the dataset. On graph-level tasks (Table 2), SSTAG leads UniGraph by +2.25% on HIV, +1.10% on BBBP, +2.83% on BACE, and +2.98% on MUV, while also achieving the lowest RMSE on all three regression benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

SSTAG synthesizes ideas from three distinct research threads: (1) self-supervised graph learning via masked autoencoders (GraphMAE, GraphMAE2) and contrastive methods (GraphCL, BGRL), which are confined to single-graph, GNN-backbone settings; (2) LLM-based graph reasoning (Graph-LLM, LLaGA, GraphGPT), which converts graph structure into text but struggles with topological reasoning and incurs heavy inference costs; and (3) knowledge distillation from NLP/CV, repurposed here to co-distill a cascaded LM+GNN teacher into a lightweight structure-aware MLP student. The key conceptual departure is using text as a domain-agnostic unifying medium to bridge LLM semantic reasoning and GNN structural modeling, combined with PPR-based subgraph sampling and a memory bank for invariant knowledge—neither of which appears in prior TAG-specific methods like UniGraph or TAPE.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in the distillation result: a 22M-parameter MLP matches a 7.1B-parameter GNN teacher within 1.06% accuracy while cutting inference time by 35%, making large-scale TAG inference feasible on resource-constrained hardware. The unified node/edge/graph task template is a step toward graph foundation models, though the approach is inherently limited to text-attributed graphs and was pretrained on a single citation network (ogbn-Paper100M), leaving open whether the learned invariants transfer to structurally very different domains (e.g., molecular graphs with no natural text). A key open question is whether the memory bank mechanism and PPR-based structural injection can be generalized to non-textual or multimodal graph attributes, and whether the LM-dominated pretraining cost (~22.6h) can be reduced without sacrificing cross-domain performance.
