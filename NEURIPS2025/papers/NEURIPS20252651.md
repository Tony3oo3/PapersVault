## Problem

While the Θ(log n) interactive advantage in multi-armed bandit sample complexity is well-known, the detailed evolution of mutual information and success probability over time—especially below the sample complexity threshold—remained uncharacterized. Existing converse tools (Fano's inequality, two-point hypothesis testing, inductive mutual information bounds) fail to capture the correct scaling in intermediate regimes, and it was unclear whether mutual information alone suffices to characterize the fundamental limits of interactive learning.

## Input / Output

The setting consists of n Bernoulli arms where a uniformly random optimal arm has mean (1+Δ)/2 and all others have mean (1−Δ)/2, with a fixed noise parameter Δ ∈ (0,1]. The paper characterizes two quantities as functions of the time budget t: the optimal success probability p_t* of correctly identifying the best arm after t rounds, and the optimal mutual information I_t* between the optimal arm index and the observed history. The characterization reveals four distinct regimes for I_t* separated by the transitions tΔ² = 1, log n, and n, with growth transitioning from linear to quadratic to linear to saturation.

## Method

The achievability algorithm (Algorithm 1) employs a Sequential Probability Ratio Test where each arm's cumulative reward forms a biased random walk (upward bias Δ for the optimal arm, downward bias −Δ for suboptimal arms), eliminating arms whose walk drops below threshold θ = −1/Δ in O(1/Δ²) expected steps. For the converse, the paper introduces two key technical innovations: a 'change-of-divergence' argument using Hellinger distance to bound mutual information for large t, and a boosting reduction that relates success probability at small t to large t, which is then used to bound information gain by reversing the typical Fano's inequality direction (using p_t* to bound I_t* rather than the other way around). A striking finding is that in the intermediate regime tΔ² ∈ ω(1) ∩ n^{o(1)}, Fano's inequality is strictly loose, and an explicit algorithm achieves optimal success probability while accumulating strictly less mutual information than I_t*, demonstrating that mutual information alone is insufficient to characterize the fundamental limits of interactive learning.

## Results

The main result (Theorem 1.1) provides a complete piecewise characterization of the optimal success probability p_t^* and optimal mutual information I_t^* for the n-armed Bernoulli bandit with gap Δ. The success probability p_t^* transitions from 1/n (trivial) to tΔ²/n (linear) to 1 (saturated) at thresholds tΔ² = 1 and tΔ² = n. The mutual information I_t^* exhibits four distinct regimes: linear (tΔ²/n), quadratic ((tΔ²)²/n), linear with log factor (tΔ² log n/n), and saturated (log n), with transitions at tΔ² = 1, log n, and n. Critically, the interactive setting achieves a Θ(log n) reduction in sample complexity over the non-interactive case, and Fano's inequality is shown to be strictly loose in the intermediate regime tΔ² ∈ ω(1) ∩ n^{o(1)}, where an algorithm can achieve optimal p_t^* while accumulating strictly suboptimal I_t^*.

**Theorem 1.1: Complete characterization of optimal success probability and mutual information (Δ = 1 − Ω(1)).**

| Regime (m = tΔ²) | p_t^* (interactive) | I_t^* (interactive) | I_t,NI^* (non-interactive) |
|---|---|---|---|
| m ≤ 1 | 1/n | tΔ²/n | tΔ²/n |
| 1 < m ≤ log n | tΔ²/n | (tΔ²)²/n | tΔ²/n |
| log n < m ≤ n | tΔ²/n | tΔ² log n/n | tΔ²/n |
| m > n | 1 | log n | — |

All bounds are tight (achievability via Algorithm 1; converse via Theorems 3.1–3.3). The non-interactive success probability is p_t,NI^* ≍ tΔ²/(n log(1+tΔ²)).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is between interactive and non-interactive sampling. In the non-interactive setting, mutual information grows linearly as tΔ²/n throughout, while the interactive setting exhibits a quadratic phase (1 < tΔ² ≤ log n) and a second linear phase with an extra Θ(log n) slope factor (log n < tΔ² ≤ n), reflecting the Θ(log n) sample complexity gain. Against existing converse techniques, the paper demonstrates that the standard two-point/Le Cam method (Proposition 4.1) yields only p_t^* = O(1/n + (√(t/n) ∧ t/n)Δ), which has incorrect Δ-scaling; the Feige et al. two-phase reduction (Proposition 4.2) fails when Δ = o(1/√(log n)); and the Rajaraman et al. inductive mutual information bound is inherently loose in the intermediate regime. The paper's novel contributions—a boosting-based reduction for small-t success probability and a change-of-divergence argument for mutual information—close all these gaps. Additionally, Proposition 4.3 shows that no generic relationship p_t ≤ f(I_t) can hold for all algorithms, since an optimal-learning algorithm (Algorithm 3) achieves Θ(p_t^*) success while accumulating only O(tΔ² log(tΔ²)/n) = o(I_t^*) information, establishing a fundamental decoupling between learning and information gain.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Wald's sequential probability ratio test as its core algorithmic primitive and positions itself within the DEC framework (Foster et al., 2021–2023) that identified the 'estimation complexity' gap between interactive and non-interactive learning. It directly addresses a gap left by Chen et al. (2024), who proposed tracking mutual information in interactive settings but did not provide the necessary bounds, and shows that the inductive approach of Rajaraman et al. (2024) for ridge bandits fails in the multi-armed bandit setting. Conceptually, it is the information-theoretic analogue of Garivier et al.'s (2019) 'true shape of regret' analysis, but applied to the 'true shape of learning' in the sub-sample-complexity regime, and draws on feedback communication (Burnašev, 1980) and noisy computation for converse techniques.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central finding—that optimal success probability and mutual information can be decoupled, with an algorithm achieving optimal learning while accumulating strictly suboptimal information—challenges the prevailing assumption that mutual information is the right single quantity for characterizing fundamental limits of interactive learning. The discovery of a quadratic growth phase in mutual information (between the two linear regimes) reveals a nontrivial intermediate behavior that neither the non-interactive heuristic nor the final saturation regime captures. The paper is explicitly a case study on a stylized Bernoulli bandit, so open questions include whether the decoupling phenomenon and the multi-phase information growth generalize to richer bandit structures (e.g., Gaussian, linear, or non-linear settings) and whether the new reduction and change-of-divergence techniques extend to the general DEC framework.
