## Problem

Existing sparse linear bandit algorithms achieve optimal worst-case regret in either the data-rich regime (O(√(sdT))) or the data-poor regime (O((sT)^(2/3))), but not both simultaneously. The only prior algorithm adapting to both regimes—sparse IDS—provides only Bayesian regret guarantees, requiring the true parameter to be a random draw from a known prior. This paper closes the gap by delivering the first algorithm with optimal worst-case (frequentist) regret in both regimes without Bayesian assumptions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252758/cropped_page8_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each round, the algorithm receives a finite action set A ⊂ R^d (with ‖a‖_∞ ≤ 1), a known sparsity level s, and the full history of past actions and rewards. It outputs a randomized policy π_t over A by minimizing the 2-surrogate information ratio computed with respect to an optimistic posterior over the sparse parameter space Θ = {θ : ‖θ‖_0 ≤ s, ‖θ‖_1 ≤ 1}. The learning rate λ_t may be chosen as a function of the observed history (F_t-measurable), enabling data-dependent tuning that drives the instance-dependent regret bound in Theorem 3.

## Method

SOIDS replaces the Bayesian posterior in IDS with an optimistic posterior updated via a FTRL rule over distributions Δ(Θ), where the loss combines negative log-likelihood (weighted by η=1/4) and cumulative gap (weighted by a time-decaying λ_t). The key technical novelty is a telescoping analysis of the Fenchel dual Φ* that handles time-dependent and history-dependent learning rates: by introducing the maximum likelihood estimator θ_t, the paper proves a monotonicity lemma (Lemma 9) for scaled-and-shifted duals, enabling the standard FTRL potential decomposition even when only part of the loss is scaled by λ_t. The regret bound is then obtained by bounding the surrogate regret via the 2- and 3-surrogate information ratios, using FGTS as a forerunner for the 2-IR (yielding the √(sdT) rate) and a mixture of FGTS with an exploratory policy for the 3-IR (yielding the (sT)^(2/3) rate). The final learning rate λ_t = min(1/2, max(λ_t^(2), λ_t^(3))) automatically selects the tighter of the two regime-specific bounds.

## Results

Theoretically, SOIDS achieves a worst-case regret bound of min(Õ(√(sdT)), Õ((sT)^(2/3))), which is simultaneously optimal in both the data-rich (√(sdT)) and data-poor ((sT)^(2/3)) regimes — the first algorithm to do so in a frequentist setting. Empirically, over T=1000 rounds with s=d/10 and 200 random actions, SOIDS maintains competitive cumulative regret across d∈{20,40,100}, transitioning smoothly between regimes where OTCS dominates (d=20) and ESTC dominates (d=100). The instance-dependent bound (Theorem 3) further shows regret scaling with the cumulative surrogate information ratio rather than T, enabling tighter guarantees on easier instances.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares SOIDS against three baselines: OTCS (Abbasi-Yadkori et al., 2012) for the data-rich regime, ESTC (Hao et al., 2020) for the data-poor regime, and LinUCB (Abbasi-Yadkori et al., 2011) as a sparsity-agnostic reference. At d=20 (data-rich), OTCS achieves the lowest cumulative regret among baselines, while SOIDS remains competitive and close to OTCS. At d=100 (data-poor), ESTC becomes the best-performing baseline, and SOIDS again tracks closely behind it. LinUCB, which does not exploit sparsity, performs worst across all dimensions. The key empirical finding is that SOIDS does not degrade sharply at the regime transition (around d=40), whereas each specialized baseline (OTCS or ESTC) performs poorly in the other regime — confirming the theoretical adaptivity claim that SOIDS simultaneously achieves near-optimal rates in both regimes without requiring prior knowledge of which regime applies.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Optimistic Information Directed Sampling (OIDS) framework of Neu, Papini, and Schwartz [2024] to the sparse linear bandit setting, combining it with the subset-selection prior and surrogate information ratio machinery from Hao et al. [2021]. The key technical departure is a novel FTRL-based analysis of the optimistic posterior that accommodates time-dependent and history-dependent learning rates, which prior optimistic-posterior analyses (Zhang [2022], Neu et al. [2024]) did not support. Conceptually, the paper bridges two previously separate lines: the data-rich regime algorithms (OTCS, Abbasi-Yadkori et al. [2012]) and the data-poor regime algorithms (ESTC, Hao et al. [2020]), unifying them under a single IDS-style policy that adapts to whichever regime is active.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first algorithm to achieve optimal worst-case (frequentist) regret in both the data-rich and data-poor regimes simultaneously for sparse linear bandits, closing a gap that previously required either Bayesian assumptions (Hao et al. [2021]) or regime-specific algorithms. The time-dependent learning rate analysis is a reusable technical tool that could extend to other optimistic-posterior settings. Open questions include whether the logarithmic factors in the instance-dependent bound can be tightened while retaining data-dependent learning rates, and whether the approximate implementation (empirical Bayesian sparse sampling) can be given a rigorous theoretical analysis. The authors also note a broader implication: IDS-style methods may be more practically implementable than DEC-based approaches, which require solving complex min-max optimization at each step.
