## Problem

Prior knowledge-localization work in diffusion models (e.g., causal tracing) identifies which coarse-grained layers store a concept but does not explain how other layers contribute, nor does it reveal components that suppress a concept. This paper asks the more general question of how individual model parameters jointly activate or inhibit a generated concept, and shows that a previously overlooked class of negative components plays a critical role in concept generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250962/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pretrained latent diffusion model (Stable Diffusion 1.4, 2.1, XL, or 3.5), a text prompt encoding the target concept, a base condition (e.g., an empty string for erasure), and optionally a small set of reference images of the concept (5 images for amplification). It outputs per-parameter attribution scores computed as w_i·∂J/∂w_i across the UNet's linear layers, and a modified model in which the top-k positive parameters (for erasure) or top-k negative parameters (for amplification) are zeroed out, yielding a model that generates images with the target concept removed or strengthened while preserving other knowledge.

## Method

CAD approximates the counterfactual effect of removing a parameter w_i on a concept-generation objective J via a first-order Taylor expansion, yielding the attribution score w_i·∂J/∂w_i that requires only a single forward–backward pass rather than the 100k+ model evaluations needed to fit a linear regression over masked-model datasets. For erasure, the objective J_cb(c,w) = E‖Φ(x_t, c_b, t; w).sg() − Φ(x_t, c, t; w)‖² forces the conditional noise prediction toward the unconditioned one, and the top-k parameters with the most positive scores are set to zero (CAD-Erase). For amplification, the objective is the negative diffusion training loss on a few concept images, and the top-k parameters with the most negative scores are zeroed out (CAD-Amplify), revealing that ablating suppressive components increases the probability of generating the target concept. The framework operates at the finest granularity—individual parameters within each linear layer of the UNet—and empirically confirms that knowledge is localized in a small fraction (≤0.1%) of parameters, while also demonstrating that parameters in non-feed-forward modules (self-attention, cross-attention, residual connections) are more entangled across concepts.

## Results

On object erasure (10 ImageNet classes, SD-1.4), CAD-Erase reduces target-class accuracy to 0.00–4.40% while preserving other-class accuracy at 63.7–80.1%, outperforming or matching all four baselines. For nudity erasure (I2P benchmark), CAD-Erase achieves the lowest total nudity count (43 vs. 798 for the base model) and the best image quality (FID 12.44, CLIP-Score 31.30). Against adversarial prompts (MMA, Ring-A-Bell), CAD-Erase reduces nudity detections by 96.81% and 98.79% respectively, the strongest robustness among all compared methods. CAD-Amplify increases target-class generation accuracy from 7.20% to 27.60% (cassette player) and from 69.00% to 98.20% (chain saw) by ablating negative components.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CAD-Erase outperforms ConceptPrune on most target classes, e.g., reducing gas pump accuracy to 0.20% vs. ConceptPrune's 36.80% and golf ball to 4.20% vs. 28.60%. Against ESD, CAD-Erase achieves comparable target-class erasure but preserves other-class accuracy far better (e.g., chain saw other classes: 69.22% vs. ESD's 29.24%), indicating ESD's fine-tuning damages unrelated knowledge. Compared to UCE and RECE (both cross-attention editing methods), CAD-Erase is on par overall but clearly superior on harder classes like 'church' (1.60% vs. UCE's 15.20%) and 'garbage truck' (3.80% vs. UCE's 15.60%). For nudity erasure, CAD-Erase achieves the lowest total count (43) versus ConceptPrune (62), ESD (88), RECE (98), and UCE (229), while also attaining the best FID (12.44) and CLIP-Score (31.30). Under adversarial attacks (MMA, Ring-A-Bell), CAD-Erase is the most robust, generating only 62 and 5 nudity detections respectively, compared to UCE's 971 and 64, and RECE's 481 and 4.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the knowledge localization line of work (causal tracing in Meng et al. 2022; distributed hypothesis in Basu et al. 2023) from coarse-grained layers to individual model parameters, and repurposes the linear counterfactual estimator of Shah et al. 2024 via a first-order Taylor approximation to make parameter-level attribution computationally feasible for iterative diffusion models. It departs from prior work in two key ways: (1) it formulates the question as 'how do components contribute' rather than 'where is knowledge stored,' and (2) it identifies a previously unreported class of negative components that suppress concept generation, complementing the positive components found by causal tracing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The discovery of negative components provides a more complete mechanistic picture of diffusion models and opens a new editing axis (amplification via suppression removal) that prior erasure-only frameworks could not address. The localization hypothesis—that a tiny fraction of parameters (<0.1%) suffices to erase or amplify a concept—has practical implications for lightweight, inference-time model editing without retraining. Limitations include knowledge entanglement across similar concepts (e.g., Rembrandt/Caravaggio), the simplicity of the zeroing-out editing strategy, and the restriction to final-image-level attribution rather than per-timestep or spatially localized analysis. Open questions include whether more sophisticated interventions (fine-tuning identified components) can further improve editing, and whether the framework extends to analyzing specific denoising stages or spatial regions.
