## Problem

In neurosymbolic learning (NESY), where a classifier is trained using only the result of a symbolic function σ applied to hidden gold labels, learning imbalances—large differences in class-specific errors—can arise even when both hidden and weak label distributions are uniform. This phenomenon, driven by σ itself, has not been previously characterized or addressed, and standard long-tailed learning techniques from supervised or partial-label settings do not apply because they assume access to gold labels or single-instance training samples.

## Input / Output

Training samples are M-ary tuples (x_1,...,x_M, s) where s = σ(y_1,...,y_M) is a weak label computed from hidden gold labels; the individual y_i are never observed. At inference, the model receives a single instance x and outputs a class label. The method additionally produces estimated hidden-label marginals (Algorithm 1), batch-level pseudolabel matrices Q_i ∈ [0,1]^{n×c} (LP), and an adjusted score matrix P′ ∈ R_+^{n×c} whose column sums approximately match the estimated ratios (CAROT).

## Method

The paper contributes a theoretical characterization showing that the symbolic component σ uniquely affects class-specific risk bounds through a nonlinear program (Proposition 3.1), and three practical algorithms. First, Algorithm 1 estimates hidden-label marginals from weak labels by minimizing cross-entropy between empirical weak-label frequencies and the polynomial map Ψ_σ(r̂), solved via softmax-reparameterized gradient descent. Second, LP formulates training-time pseudolabeling as a linear program over a batch of n samples, encoding σ-constraints via Tseytin transformation of DNF formulas and enforcing marginal adherence with an ε-relaxation, then training the classifier on the resulting soft labels. Third, CAROT performs testing-time mitigation by solving a robust semi-constrained optimal transport (RSOT) problem that adjusts the model's score matrix to match estimated class ratios, approximated in polynomial time via robust semi-Sinkhorn iterations with entropic and margin regularization.

## Results

On the MAX-MNIST benchmark (m_P=3K), the paper's LP(ALG1) training-time method achieves 96.09% balanced accuracy on MAX-3 (ρ=0), improving over the Semantic Loss (SL) baseline of 84.15% by ~12 points. Adding CAROT at test time yields 96.13%. On the Smallest Parent CIFAR-10 benchmark (m_P=10K), LP(ALG1) reaches 81.99% at ρ=5 versus SL's 67.94%, a ~14-point gain. The paper reports improvements of up to 14% over strong NSL and long-tailed learning baselines across all settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Semantic Loss (SL) baseline [71], the paper's LP(ALG1) training-time method consistently wins by large margins: 96.09% vs. 84.15% on MAX-3 (ρ=0) and 81.99% vs. 67.94% on Smallest Parent (ρ=5), representing gains of ~12 and ~14 points respectively. Against Logit Adjustment (LA) [42] at test time, CAROT is generally superior, e.g., on Smallest Parent at ρ=50, LP(EMP)+CAROT reaches 80.71% while LP(EMP)+LA stays at 70.29%; however, LA sometimes has negligible or slightly negative impact (e.g., 84.17% vs. 84.15% on MAX-3 ρ=0). Against RECORDS [18], the closest training-time competitor for partial-label learning, LP(ALG1) wins in most settings (e.g., 96.09% vs. 85.56% on MAX-3 ρ=0), and critically, RECORDS can severely degrade the SL baseline (48.15% vs. 67.94% on Smallest Parent ρ=5), demonstrating that existing long-tailed PLL techniques are not directly transferable to NESY. CAROT also improves RECORDS-trained models (e.g., 50.14% → 71.70% on Smallest Parent ρ=15), but LP+CAROT still achieves the best overall results.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the theoretical framework of Wang et al. (NeurIPS 2023) on multi-instance weak supervision by deriving class-specific risk bounds that require no M-unambiguity assumption and are strictly tighter, while also extending Cour et al.'s (JMLR 2011) partial-label learning imbalance results to the NESY setting. It departs from the long-tailed learning literature (supervised and weakly supervised) by identifying the symbolic component σ as an independent source of learning imbalance that operates even under uniform data distributions—a phenomenon absent from prior imbalance analyses. For mitigation, it repurposes robust semi-constrained optimal transport (Le et al., NeurIPS 2021) into a testing-time algorithm (CAROT) and introduces a novel LP-based pseudolabeling formulation for training time, both of which are inapplicable to prior single-instance long-tailed methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that σ can induce class-specific risk disparities independent of data distribution is a genuinely new phenomenon that reshapes how practitioners should think about imbalance in neurosymbolic systems, with implications for any application using symbolic supervision (NLP, VQA, knowledge graphs). However, the practical impact is currently bounded by the narrow NESY setting, the NP-hardness of the LP formulation, and CAROT's demonstrated sensitivity to marginal estimation quality. Open questions include whether the theoretical characterization extends to strongly correlated instances, whether more robust testing-time mitigation can be designed, and whether the LP approach scales to larger pre-image spaces without approximation.
