## Problem

Vision Transformers lack the shift-invariance inductive bias of convnets, making them highly sensitive to small image translations. While anti-aliasing techniques have been developed for convnets, existing adaptations to ViTs (e.g., adaptive polyphase sampling) only guarantee consistency for integer cyclic shifts, falling short on fractional (sub-pixel) translations and realistic camera-like shifts.

## Input / Output

The model takes 224×224 RGB images and outputs a class label over ImageNet's 1000 categories. Unlike standard ViTs, the input is consumed through a convolutional patch-embedding module with alias-free downsampling rather than a single strided convolution, and the final prediction is read from a shift-invariant class token updated by alias-free class attention blocks rather than a prepended class token processed through standard self-attention.

## Method

The Alias-Free Vision Transformer (AFT) is built on the XCiT architecture and replaces every non-shift-equivariant component with an equivariant alternative. The core insight is that cross-covariance attention (XCA), which computes V·softmax(K̂ᵀQ̂/τ) with ℓ₂-normalized keys and queries, is provably shift-equivariant: the matrix KᵀV is shift-invariant because fractional translations introduce identical phase factors in the Fourier domain that cancel in the inner product, and Q is shift-equivariant as a linear combination of shifted channels. All strided convolutions in the patch embedding and local patch interaction are replaced with stride-1 convolutions followed by FFT-based alias-free downsampling, and all GELU activations are wrapped with upsampling and low-pass-filtered downsampling to prevent aliasing. Layer normalization is replaced with a global variant that computes variance across all tokens rather than per-token, and positional encodings are removed entirely. A learnable class token is appended only after the patch-only blocks and updated through alias-free class attention, yielding a shift-invariant global representation for classification.

## Results

On ImageNet, the Alias-Free Transformer (AFT) achieves 70.5% top-1 accuracy (Nano) and 81.8% (Small), matching the baseline XCiT within 0.2%. The key gain is in shift consistency: AFT reaches 99.2%/98.7% (Nano) and 99.5%/99.4% (Small) consistency under integer/half-pixel cyclic translations, versus 83.7%/82.0% and 91.4%/89.8% for the baseline. Under adversarial translation attacks (worst-case over a ±6-pixel grid), AFT suffers only ~2% relative accuracy drop (Nano) and <1% (Small), compared to 25% and 14% drops for the baseline. On CIFAR-10/100 and Stanford Cars (fine-tuned), AFT maintains >99% shift consistency while matching or slightly exceeding baseline accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the XCiT baseline, AFT matches top-1 accuracy (70.5% vs 70.4% Nano; 81.8% vs 82.0% Small) while improving half-pixel shift consistency by ~17 points (98.7% vs 82.0% Nano) and adversarial half-pixel accuracy by ~17 points (69.5% vs 52.9% Nano). Against the APS approach (Ding et al. [13], Rojas-Gomez et al. [47]), AFT trades 0.8 points of integer consistency (99.2% vs 100.0%) for a 11.2-point gain in half-pixel consistency (98.7% vs 87.5% Nano) and a 2.1-point accuracy recovery (70.5% vs 68.4% Nano). In the realistic-translation comparison (Figure 2), AFT outperforms CvT-13 (20M), Swin-T (28M), and ViT-Base (86M) on both crop-shift and bilinear fractional-shift adversarial accuracy across all shift magnitudes up to 6 pixels. The vanilla ViT degrades most under crop-shifts, while under bilinear fractional shifts it is surprisingly competitive with hybrid backbones but still below AFT. Overall, AFT is the only method that simultaneously maintains near-baseline accuracy, near-perfect integer consistency, and near-perfect half-pixel consistency.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the Alias-Free ConvNet (AFC) framework of Michaeli et al. (2023) to the transformer setting, adapting alias-free downsampling, alias-free activations, and global layer normalization. The key conceptual departure is the identification of a class of shift-equivariant attention (SEA) operations—linear attention and cross-covariance attention—whose shift-equivariance follows from Parseval's theorem, in contrast to softmax-based self-attention which breaks equivariance. Prior ViT shift-invariance work (APS-based methods by Ding et al. and Rojas-Gomez et al.) certifies only integer cyclic shift invariance; this paper is the first to achieve certified fractional (sub-pixel) shift invariance in a transformer architecture by combining the SEA property with alias-free nonlinearities.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The AFT provides the first principled, theoretically grounded path to fractional shift invariance in vision transformers, with ~99% consistency and dramatically improved adversarial robustness under realistic translations (2% relative accuracy drop vs. 25% for baseline). However, the 7× training-time overhead from FFT-based alias-free operations is a significant practical barrier to adoption, and the certified invariance is limited to cyclic shifts with sinc interpolation, which differ from natural image translations. Open questions include whether the runtime can be reduced through optimized spectral operations, whether the SEA framework generalizes to other attention variants (e.g., grouped or sparse attention), and whether the polynomial-activation tradeoff observed here (significant accuracy loss in the shallow patch-embedding stage) can be resolved with deeper or differently structured embeddings.
