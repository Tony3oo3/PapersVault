## Problem

Existing LLM-based multi-agent systems communicate exclusively through natural language tokens or their embeddings, inheriting the inherent ambiguity, lossiness, and indirectness of human language. This bottleneck limits collective reasoning, as recent analyses show many inter-agent failures stem from vague message specification and misalignment. The paper asks: what form of communication can bypass language entirely and transmit the underlying latent thoughts that actually drive agent reasoning?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254113/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes as input the concatenated hidden states (last-token representations) of all n_a agents immediately before a communication round, forming a single vector H_t ∈ R^{n_h}. It produces three structured outputs: (1) a shared latent thought vector Ẑ_t ∈ R^{n_z} decomposed into shared and private components per agent pair, (2) a binary thought-agent dependency matrix B(J_f̂) recovered from the Jacobian sparsity pattern, and (3) personalized prefix vectors P_t^(i) ∈ R^{m×d} injected into each agent's token embeddings for the next generation step. Unlike standard multi-agent debate where agents exchange full text responses, here the 'message' is a compact latent vector routed selectively based on the recovered sharing structure.

## Method

THOUGHTCOMM formalizes agent states as generated by an unknown invertible function f from latent thoughts (H_t = f(Z_t)), then trains a sparsity-regularized autoencoder with L1 penalty on the Jacobian J_f̂ to recover Ẑ_t from H_t. Three identifiability theorems guarantee that, under nonparametric assumptions, both shared thoughts (disentangled from all other latents for any agent pair) and private thoughts (disentangled from the other agent's latents) are recoverable up to permutation, along with the full thought-agent incidence structure. At inference, each agent's relevant latent dimensions are identified via the recovered binary mask, grouped by agreement level α_j (number of agents sharing each thought), reweighted by group, and concatenated into a personalized representation Z̃_t^(i). A learned adapter g maps Z̃_t^(i) to a prefix vector prepended to the agent's token embeddings, guiding the next response without explicit message passing. Both the autoencoder and adapter are task-agnostic and pretrained once, making the framework model-agnostic and scalable to arbitrarily large LLMs since training cost depends only on embedding dimension, not parameter count.

## Results

On MATH, THOUGHTCOMM achieves 85.00% accuracy with Qwen3-0.6B, 93.00% with Qwen3-1.7B, 74.60% with Phi-4-mini, 45.60% with LLaMA3-8B, and 82.80% with DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-8B, consistently outperforming both Single Answer and Multiagent Finetuning baselines. On GSM8K, gains are more modest but still positive across all models (e.g., 75.80% vs 70.80% for Qwen3-0.6B). On average, THOUGHTCOMM achieves a 67.23% relative improvement over single-answer baselines and a 19.06% absolute improvement over the state-of-the-art Multiagent Finetuning. Consensus scores also improve across the board, with THOUGHTCOMM reaching 95.93% on Qwen3-1.7B MATH.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is Multiagent Finetuning (Subramaniam et al., 2025), the current state-of-the-art for multi-agent collaboration via specialized roles and multiple finetuning rounds. THOUGHTCOMM outperforms Multiagent Finetuning on MATH across all five models, with the largest gap on Qwen3-1.7B (93.00% vs 75.80%, a 17.2-point absolute gain) and Qwen3-0.6B (85.00% vs 71.20%). On GSM8K, THOUGHTCOMM also leads on four of five models, though the margin is smaller (e.g., 75.80% vs 70.80% on Qwen3-0.6B); the sole exception is LLaMA3-8B where Multiagent Finetuning edges out by 0.8 points (69.20% vs 68.40%). Against the single-answer baseline, THOUGHTCOMM achieves an average 67.23% relative improvement, with the most dramatic gain on Qwen3-1.7B MATH (93.00% vs 43.60%, a 113.3% relative improvement). In terms of consensus, THOUGHTCOMM consistently matches or exceeds Multiagent Finetuning, and the paper notes that unlike Multiagent Finetuning—which requires finetuning the entire LLM—THOUGHTCOMM only trains a lightweight autoencoder and adapter whose cost depends solely on the embedding dimension, making it model-agnostic and scalable to arbitrarily large LLMs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs fundamentally from the entire line of LLM multi-agent communication work (Du et al. 2023, Pham et al. 2023, Liang et al. 2023, and others), which all exchange natural language tokens or their embeddings as the communication medium. Instead, it introduces a latent-variable generative model for agent states and recovers the underlying 'thoughts' via a sparsity-regularized autoencoder, drawing its identifiability theory from the nonlinear ICA literature (Lachapelle et al. 2022, Zheng et al. 2022) but applying it in a novel pairwise, multi-agent setting without auxiliary variables. The injection mechanism borrows from prefix-tuning (Li & Liang, 2021), but the key conceptual departure is that agents communicate in a recovered latent space rather than in any surface-level representation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a widely acknowledged bottleneck in multi-agent LLM systems—lossy, ambiguous language-based communication—by providing both a theoretical guarantee (identifiability of shared and private thoughts in a nonparametric setting) and a practical, lightweight framework that avoids full-LLM finetuning. However, the experimental validation is confined to math reasoning (MATH, GSM8K) with only 500 training examples, and the framework requires access to model hidden states, limiting applicability to closed-source models. Open questions include whether the identifiability assumptions hold in practice for real LLMs, whether the approach extends to non-textual modalities, and whether the gains persist on more diverse or open-ended reasoning tasks.
