## Problem

Concept Bottleneck Models (CBMs) enforce reasoning through human-interpretable concepts but remain purely associative: they assume a flat bipartite structure where all concepts are independent direct causes of the target, ignoring real-world causal dependencies among concepts. This limits causal reasoning, out-of-distribution generalization, and the ability to enforce fairness constraints through targeted interventions. No prior methodology existed for structuring the concept bottleneck according to a reliable causal model of the underlying data-generating process.

## Input / Output

The model consumes raw input features (RGB images or tabular vectors) together with a DAG over interpretable concepts, which can be either expert-provided or automatically learned from observational data and unstructured background knowledge (e.g., scientific literature) via a pipeline combining Greedy Equivalence Search with LLM-based edge orientation and RAG. Beyond standard task and concept predictions, the architecture natively supports do-interventions on any endogenous variable, enabling interventional and counterfactual queries that flat CBMs cannot express because their bipartite structure offers no causal pathways to block or redirect.

## Method

C²BM replaces the flat concept bottleneck of standard CBMs with a parametric Structural Causal Model (SCM) whose nodes are interpretable concepts connected by a learned DAG. A neural encoder g(·) maps the raw input to exogenous embeddings U, and a hypernetwork r(·) adaptively predicts the parameters of each node's structural equation from its corresponding exogenous embedding, so that each endogenous variable is computed as a weighted linear sum of its causal parents (Eq. 3). Although each structural equation is linear, the input-dependent re-parameterization by the hypernetwork makes the overall mapping a universal approximator, recovering non-linear dependencies while preserving per-equation interpretability. Training is end-to-end maximum likelihood over the factorized joint P(V|U;Θ)·P(Θ|E,U)·P(U|X). A fully automated instantiation pipeline discovers concepts via LLM prompting plus CLIP-based labeling, and recovers the causal graph by combining GES (yielding a CPDAG) with LLM+RAG queries that orient or discard undirected edges using retrieved domain knowledge.

## Results

C²BM achieves task accuracy comparable to or exceeding the opaque baseline (OpaqNN) and the strongest concept-based baseline (CEM) across all 11 datasets, e.g., 94.18% on cMNIST vs. 91.24% (OpaqNN) and 93.72% (CEM), and 74.1% on Hailfinder vs. 72.0% (OpaqNN). On reduced-bottleneck variants (Asia*, Alarm*), C²BM retains expressivity (70.8%, 60.5%) while hard-bottleneck CBMs degrade sharply (56.0%, 52.7%). Causal reliability is substantially improved: the learned DAG's structural Hamming distance drops from 45.0 (flat CBM) to 5.0 (C²BM with CD+LLM) on Alarm, and from 11.75 to 1.8 on Sachs. On the fairness benchmark, C²BM is the only model that fully eliminates the sensitive-attribute effect (CaCE = 0.0% post-intervention vs. 8.2–21.9% for all baselines).

## Baselines & Comparisons

C²BM matches or slightly exceeds the opaque DNN baseline (OpaqNN) on most datasets while adding semantic transparency and causal reliability, e.g., 94.18% vs. 91.24% on cMNIST and 74.1% vs. 72.0% on Hailfinder. Against CEM (the strongest concept-based baseline), C²BM is competitive on accuracy (within ~1–2% on most datasets) but clearly superior on causal reliability (Hamming distance 1.8 vs. 11.75 on Sachs) and on reduced-bottleneck settings where CEM drops to 69.7% on Asia* while C²BM holds at 70.8%. Compared to SCBM, C²BM achieves similar or better task accuracy (e.g., 94.18% vs. 94.02% on cMNIST) but uniquely supports causal interventions that propagate correctly through the graph, yielding larger downstream accuracy gains with fewer interventions (Fig. 4). On the fairness benchmark (CelebA), C²BM is the only model that fully removes the sensitive-attribute influence (CaCE 0.0% post-intervention), whereas CEM retains 8.2%, SCBM 14.8%, and CBM+lin 21.9%. On the debiasing experiment (cMNIST with reversed color-parity correlation), C²BM's causal structure isolates the spurious color edge, enabling ~90% accuracy after intervening on the number concept, while all baselines remain locked to the color shortcut.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Concept Bottleneck Model (CBM) family by replacing the standard flat bipartite bottleneck (all concepts as independent direct causes of the task) with a DAG-structured 'causal bottleneck' governed by structural causal model (SCM) equations. It builds on CBMs (Koh et al., 2020), Concept Embedding Models (Zarlenga et al., 2022), and Stochastic CBMs (Vandenhirtz et al., 2024), but departs from them by encoding causal dependencies among concepts rather than treating them as mutually independent. The automated instantiation pipeline integrates Greedy Equivalence Search for causal discovery with LLM+RAG-based edge orientation, drawing on prior work in label-free concept discovery (Oikarinen et al., 2023) and hypernetwork-based adaptive parameterization (Ha et al., 2017; Barbiero et al., 2023). Unlike post-hoc causal explanation methods (DiConStruct) or architectures requiring the true causal graph (Neural Causal Models), C²BM learns both the concept set and the causal structure from data plus unstructured background knowledge in a single forward pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Causal reliability is a fundamental gap in interpretable deep learning: standard CBMs and CEMs can produce misleading explanations (e.g., attributing lung cancer to 'cough' as a direct cause) and cannot support valid interventional or counterfactual reasoning. C²BM addresses this while maintaining competitive accuracy, and the fairness experiment (CaCE reduced to exactly 0% via a single do-intervention) demonstrates a structural capability absent in all flat-bottleneck baselines. Key limitations include dependence on a quality knowledge base and LLM access, scalability constraints inherited from causal discovery algorithms, and unaddressed OOD generalization of the neural encoder. Open questions include extending to counterfactual queries, modeling hidden confounders via PAGs, and identifying optimal intervention policies.
