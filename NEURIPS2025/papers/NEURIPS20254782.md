## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting relies on heuristic density-control rules (cloning, splitting, pruning at fixed thresholds) that either inflate memory with redundant Gaussians or prematurely remove beneficial ones. While 3DGS-MCMC replaces these heuristics with SGLD state transitions, it still fixes the total Gaussian count upfront, limiting adaptability to scenes of varying complexity. The paper asks whether densification and pruning can be recast as a principled probabilistic sampling process that adaptively determines both the number and placement of Gaussians without predefined scene complexity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254782/cropped_page6_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view RGB images with SfM-derived camera poses and a sparse point cloud, using an annealed subset of training views (from full coverage early to focused subsets later) to compute per-pixel importance fields. It outputs a 3D Gaussian representation whose cardinality is not fixed in advance but emerges adaptively through the MH accept-reject process, typically settling around 0.72–0.77 million Gaussians across benchmark scenes—roughly 4× fewer than vanilla 3DGS. The rendered output is standard novel-view RGB images at the original resolution, with the same differentiable tile rasterization pipeline as 3DGS.

## Method

The paper reformulates 3DGS densification as a Metropolis-Hastings birth sampler over the space of Gaussian configurations, where the target posterior combines a multi-view photometric likelihood (L1 + D-SSIM loss) with a logarithmic voxel-occupancy prior that penalizes crowding. Proposals are generated in a coarse-to-fine two-phase scheme: new Gaussians are spawned near pixels with high aggregated multi-view error (fusing SSIM, L1, and opacity through a logistic importance score) using large and then small Gaussian perturbations. Each candidate is accepted with probability ρ(i) = σ(I(i))·D(v′), where σ(I(i)) is the logistic importance score and D(v′) = 1/(1+λ_v·c(v′)) is the voxel sparsity factor, derived rigorously from the MH acceptance ratio via a first-order Taylor surrogate for the photometric loss change. A relocation mechanism reassigns persistently low-opacity Gaussians to high-opacity regions, and the entire process requires no fixed Gaussian budget, converging to a scene-adaptive representation.

## Results

Using only ~0.72–0.77M Gaussians (roughly 4× fewer than vanilla 3DGS's 1.8–3.2M), the method achieves PSNR of 27.34 / 23.99 / 30.12 dB on Mip-NeRF360, Tanks & Temples, and Deep Blending respectively. On Deep Blending it surpasses 3DGS-MCMC on all three metrics (PSNR 30.12 vs 29.84, SSIM 0.909 vs 0.906, LPIPS 0.245 vs 0.254), and on Tanks & Temples it achieves the best LPIPS (0.166 vs 0.170). The method also converges faster, reaching 30 dB PSNR approximately 2.2 minutes earlier than 3DGS-MCMC in wall-clock time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against 3DGS-MCMC (the closest competitor, also using ~0.72M Gaussians), the method wins on Deep Blending across all three metrics (PSNR +0.28 dB, SSIM +0.003, LPIPS −0.009) and on Tanks & Temples LPIPS (0.166 vs 0.170), but trails on Mip-NeRF360 PSNR (27.34 vs 27.69) and Tanks & Temples PSNR/SSIM (23.99/0.852 vs 24.30/0.862). Against vanilla 3DGS re-run by the authors (3DGS*, using 1.8–3.2M Gaussians), the method matches or exceeds quality on Tanks & Temples (PSNR 23.99 vs 23.63) and Deep Blending (PSNR 30.12 vs 29.53) while using roughly 4× fewer Gaussians. Against 3DGS-S (3DGS constrained to the same ~0.72M Gaussian budget), the method consistently wins on all three datasets and all metrics, confirming the advantage comes from the sampling strategy rather than simply having more primitives. Against NeRF-based baselines (Mip-NeRF360, INGP, Plenoxels), the method is competitive on PSNR but the comparison is less direct since those methods do not report Gaussian counts. The method also converges faster than 3DGS-MCMC, reaching 98% of its final PSNR in fewer iterations and ~2.2 minutes less wall-clock time to hit 30 dB.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 3DGS-MCMC line of work (Kheradmand et al., 2024), which recast 3DGS parameter updates as Stochastic Gradient Langevin Dynamics with a fixed Gaussian budget, by replacing SGLD with a Metropolis-Hastings birth sampler that adaptively grows the Gaussian set. It builds directly on the 3DGS representation and loss formulation (Kerbl et al., 2023) and the classical MH acceptance rule (Metropolis et al., 1953), but introduces a voxel-wise sparsity prior and a multi-view photometric importance score to drive proposals—elements absent from both 3DGS's heuristic cloning/splitting and 3DGS-MCMC's uniform-noise SGLD. The key conceptual departure is decoupling 'where to propose' (global, image-space error-driven) from 'what to keep' (per-Gaussian accept-reject), enabling long jumps to uncovered regions that local Langevin walks cannot achieve.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~4× reduction in Gaussians (from ~3M to ~0.7M) while matching or modestly exceeding 3DGS quality is practically significant for memory-constrained deployment, and the principled Bayesian formulation removes the need to pre-specify scene complexity. However, the method still lags 3DGS-MCMC slightly on Mip-NeRF360 PSNR (27.34 vs 27.69), performs worse on outdoor scenes due to its bias toward high-density regions, and adds roughly 5 minutes of training overhead from multi-view error computation. Open questions include whether multi-scale or dual-stream (foreground/background) samplers can close the outdoor-scene gap, and whether the ~5-minute overhead can be reduced through faster surrogate evaluation.
