## Problem

Modern LLMs are increasingly used to model neural responses to language, but their internal representations are highly entangled, mixing lexicon, syntax, meaning, and reasoning. This entanglement biases conventional brain-encoding analyses toward linguistically shallow features, making it difficult to isolate the neural substrates of deeper cognitive processes such as reasoning. Prior brain–LLM alignment studies treat models as monolithic black boxes, leaving the unique contribution of high-level reasoning to cortical activity largely unexplored.

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes word-aligned text from a 30-minute podcast transcript, extracts 5120-dimensional hidden states from Qwen2.5-14B at four identified saturation layers, and pairs them with high-gamma (70–200 Hz) ECoG recordings from 9 participants across 1,330 electrodes. Neural signals are epoched in a ±2 s window around each word onset and downsampled to 32 Hz (128 time bins). The method produces four near-orthogonal residual embeddings (lexical, syntactic, meaning, reasoning), per-electrode predicted ECoG responses via ridge encoding, and spatiotemporal activation maps in MNI space identifying where and when each feature is neurally represented.

## Method

The core contribution is a residual disentanglement pipeline that first localizes feature-specific layers via minimal-pair probing (BLiMP for syntax, COMPS-BASE for meaning, COMPS-WUGS-DIST for reasoning), identifying saturation at layers 6, 20, and 30 in Qwen2.5-14B. It then constructs four embeddings by iteratively regressing out the immediately preceding feature: E_s = H_s − g_l(H_l), E_m = H_m − g_s(H_s), E_r = H_r − g_m(H_m), where each g is a ridge regression trained on a 160k-token podcast corpus. Because features emerge progressively across layers, regressing out only the nearest lower-level representation is approximately equivalent to removing all lower-level contributions, yielding near-orthogonal residuals (validated by a matrix-level covariance proof, token-level cosine similarity ≤ 0.045, and feature-specific probing). Each residual embedding is then fed into a separate ridge encoder (with word-rate covariates regressed out) to predict ECoG responses, enabling attribution of unique neural variance to each linguistic level. The key insight is that this hierarchical residualization, grounded in the LLM's own layer-wise feature emergence, cleanly separates reasoning from the shallow features that dominate raw LLM embeddings.

## Results

Probing Qwen2.5-14B reveals a clear hierarchical emergence: syntax saturates at layer 6, meaning at layer 20, and reasoning at layer 30, a pattern consistent across 17 Qwen models. After residual disentanglement, pairwise cosine similarity drops from 0.751 (meaning–reasoning hidden states) to ≤0.045 for all residual embedding pairs, confirming near-orthogonality. In ECoG encoding, reasoning signals peak latest (~362 ms post-onset) and uniquely recruit visual cortex and superior frontal gyrus beyond classical language areas (IFG/STG), with significantly higher correlations in visual cortex than all other features (Welch's t-test, p < 0.001). Dominant-feature electrode counts are Syntax = 166, Meaning = 161, Reasoning = 128, and Lexicon = 42, showing reasoning engages a substantial but distinct cortical population.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's central comparison is between raw (entangled) LLM hidden states and the proposed residual-disentangled embeddings. At the token level, raw hidden states at saturation layers show substantial overlap (mean absolute cosine similarity of 0.751 between meaning and reasoning layers), whereas residual embeddings achieve near-zero off-diagonal similarity (all pairs ≤ 0.045). In feature probing, each residual embedding achieves the highest accuracy on its target task while dropping 19–37% on unrelated tasks (e.g., syntax task on meaning residual drops 33.8%; syntax on reasoning drops 36.8%), confirming that residualization removes spurious cross-feature information. For neural encoding, the full (non-disentangled) embedding's predictive success is shown to be dominated by lexical and syntactic signals whose early, sharp peaks mask the slower reasoning signature; only after disentanglement does the reasoning component reveal its unique spatiotemporal profile (later peak, broader spatial extent into visual cortex). The paper does not benchmark against competing disentanglement or encoding methods directly, but positions its contribution as the first to apply hierarchical residual decoupling to LLMs for isolating reasoning-specific brain alignment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the established line of brain-LLM alignment work (Huth et al. 2016; Goldstein et al. 2022; Mischler et al. 2024; Li et al. 2023) that maps full LLM hidden states to neural responses, and on the minimal-pair probing technique (He et al. 2024, 2025) for localizing linguistic features within transformer layers. It departs from prior brain-encoding studies by treating the LLM not as a monolithic predictor but as a hierarchical feature stack, using iterative ridge regression to produce orthogonal residual embeddings for lexicon, syntax, meaning, and reasoning. The closest prior work is the hierarchical encoding of linguistic features in auditory cortex (Keshishian et al. 2023, 2025), but this paper is the first to apply such a disentanglement framework to LLM representations and validate it against intracranial recordings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that standard LLM embeddings' brain-predictive success is dominated by shallow lexical and syntactic features—masking the subtler neural signature of reasoning—challenges a core assumption in prior brain-LLM alignment studies and provides a principled method for isolating specific cognitive computations. The recruitment of visual cortex by reasoning-selective electrodes, beyond the classical perisylvian language network, suggests that linguistic reasoning engages multimodal and executive systems in ways not captured by undifferentiated LLM features. Key limitations include reliance on a single ECoG dataset (9 participants, one podcast episode), limited frontal coverage, and a single model family; open questions include whether deeper LLM layers encode discourse-level or world-modeling representations, and whether the disentanglement framework generalizes to other modalities or larger reasoning-optimized models.
