## Problem

Existing Generalized Category Discovery (GCD) methods rely on prior knowledge of the number of novel classes and assume balanced class distributions, both of which are unrealistic in federated settings where data is partitioned across heterogeneous clients with differing local class sets. Federated GCD (Fed-GCD) further compounds this: the number of novel classes is unknown at both the client and global level, and class distributions are severely skewed across clients, yet prior federated methods (FedoSSL, AGCL) still inherit these restrictive assumptions.

## Input / Output

Each of N clients holds a local dataset of RGB images split into a labeled subset (seen classes only) and an unlabeled subset (seen + novel classes), with class proportions sampled from a Dirichlet distribution (α ∈ {0.2, 0.05}) to simulate non-i.i.d. heterogeneity. The model extracts 768-dimensional [CLS] token features from a DINO-pretrained ViT-B/16 backbone (only the last block is fine-tuned) and produces class labels for all unlabeled instances. Evaluation uses clustering accuracy via Hungarian matching on a server-held unlabeled test set, reported separately for seen (Old) and novel (New) classes.

## Method

FedLPA operates entirely at the client level through three synergistic stages. First, Confidence-guided Local Category Discovery (CLCD) constructs a local similarity graph from cosine similarities of DINO features, refines edge weights using high-confidence pseudo-labels from seen classes (thresholded at the 80th percentile of labeled-data confidence), prunes low-weight edges, and applies Infomap clustering to discover client-specific concepts and compute prototype centroids. Second, Local Prior Alignment (LPA) introduces a self-distillation objective where instance-level predictions (sharpened cosine similarities to prototypes) are trained via cross-entropy, while a batch-level Jensen-Shannon Divergence regularizer aligns the mean prediction distribution with the empirical class prior computed from Infomap concept frequencies within each mini-batch. Third, the overall loss combines LPA self-distillation, supervised and unsupervised contrastive losses, and standard cross-entropy for seen-class classification, aggregated across clients via FedAvg. The entire CLCD pipeline is re-executed every R communication rounds to track feature drift, and no server-side labeled validation data or global class-count knowledge is required.

## Results

FedLPA achieves clustering accuracy (ACC) of 94.5% on CIFAR-10, 57.7% on CIFAR-100, and 75.9% on ImageNet-100 under α=0.2 non-i.i.d. partitioning, without requiring server-side labeled validation data. On fine-grained benchmarks, it reaches 62.3% on CUB-200, 52.1% on Stanford-Cars, and 84.6% on Oxford-IIIT Pet (α=0.2). The method consistently outperforms the strongest baseline (FedAvg+AGCL) by 1.1–13.9 points in overall ACC across all six datasets and both heterogeneity levels (α=0.2 and α=0.05).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedLPA outperforms all six baselines across every dataset and heterogeneity level. The largest gains are on CIFAR-10 (+9.8 over AGCL, +40.9 over SimGCD) and Stanford-Cars (+13.9 over AGCL), where methods relying on uniform class priors or known novel-class counts degrade severely. FedAvg+SimGCD [27] performs worst on standard datasets (53.6% on CIFAR-10 vs. 94.5% for FedLPA) due to its entropy-regularization assumption of balanced predictions. FedAvg+GPC [30] underperforms FedAvg+PCL [13] in most settings because its balanced cluster-size constraint conflicts with the true skewed local distributions. Among federated-specific baselines, FedAvg+AGCL [19] is the strongest competitor but still trails FedLPA by 1.1–13.9 points, as its uniform cluster prior mismatches heterogeneous client data. Notably, FedLPA achieves these gains without server-side labeled validation data, whereas all baselines require it for class-count estimation or semi-supervised clustering.

## Relation to Prior Work

FedLPA extends the Federated Generalized Category Discovery (Fed-GCD) task introduced by AGCL (Pu et al., CVPR 2024) and FedoSSL (Zhang et al., ICML 2023), but deliberately departs from their reliance on prior knowledge of novel class counts and uniform cluster priors. It repurposes Infomap graph clustering—originally a network community-detection algorithm—to discover client-specific category structure without any global label-space assumptions, and introduces a Jensen-Shannon Divergence-based batch-level regularizer (LPA) that has no direct analogue in prior GCD or federated semi-supervised learning literature. The self-distillation and contrastive learning components follow standard practices from centralized GCD (SimGCD, GCD) and SSL (PCL, SimCLR), but their combination with locally-derived empirical priors is novel.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for known novel-class counts and balanced class distributions, FedLPA addresses assumptions that are fundamentally at odds with real-world federated deployments where each client sees only a partial, skewed slice of the label space. The finding that a simple JSD-based batch-level alignment with locally-discovered priors outperforms more complex methods (GMM-based contrastive learning, entropy regularization) suggests that grounding learning in each client's actual data structure may be more important than enforcing global uniformity. Open questions include scalability beyond 10 clients, robustness to client churn or adversarial participants, and whether the CLCD graph-refinement step generalizes to non-visual modalities or streaming settings where periodic re-clustering is costly.
