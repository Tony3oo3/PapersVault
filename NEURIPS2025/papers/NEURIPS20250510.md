## Problem

R1-style reasoning models generate verbose step-by-step reasoning for every input, causing the overthinking problem where simple problems incur unnecessary computational overhead and latency. Existing mitigations—manual no-thinking prompts or uniform RL-based pruning—either sacrifice accuracy on hard problems or fail to align reasoning effort with task difficulty. The paper asks whether a model can learn to adaptively decide when to engage explicit reasoning based on problem complexity, without external control signals or teacher models.

## Input / Output

The model receives a text problem formatted with a minimal ellipsis prompt (think\n...\n) that replaces the standard detailed reasoning trace, leaving the model to autonomously decide whether to produce a reasoning trace or skip directly to an answer. Outputs are structured as either a no-thinking response (immediately closing the think tag and answering) or a thinking response (generating a full reasoning trace before the answer). The stochastic mode selection triggered by the ellipsis prompt is the key behavioral signal that RL training subsequently shapes into difficulty-aware reasoning.

## Method

AutoThink is a three-stage GRPO-based RL framework that progressively shapes the stochastic thinking/no-thinking behavior revealed by the ellipsis prompt into difficulty-aware adaptive reasoning. Stage 1 introduces batch-level reward balancing with a target thinking ratio γ and penalty slope λ to prevent mode collapse and stabilize dual-mode coexistence. Stage 2 removes the balance constraint and applies naive correctness rewards with an expanded context budget to reinforce reasoning quality within each mode. Stage 3 adds a length-aware reward (inspired by GRPO-LEAD) where the shaping term decays exponentially with standardized response length for correct answers and grows for incorrect ones, pruning redundant reasoning while preserving accuracy. The framework is model-agnostic and applicable to any R1-style distilled or RL-finetuned model, achieving 6.4% relative accuracy improvement with 52% token reduction on DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B.

## Results

On DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B, AutoThink-Stage3 achieves 51.7% average accuracy across five math benchmarks (MATH, Minerva, Olympiad, AIME24, AMC23) while using only 5,108 average tokens — a 52% reduction from the standard prompt's 10,633 tokens and a +3.1 pp accuracy gain over the 48.6% baseline. On the more heavily optimized DeepScaleR-1.5B, AutoThink-Stage2 reaches 57.3% accuracy (vs. 56.7% standard) with 5,277 tokens (vs. 5,817), and on Distill-R1-7B, AutoThink-Stage2 achieves 65.5% accuracy with 4,979 tokens (vs. 64.4% / 7,815). The proposed E-F1 efficiency metric scores 39.6 for AutoThink-Stage3 on Distill-R1-1.5B, substantially exceeding ThinkPrune-4k (18.7) and ThinkPrune-iter-2k (9.9).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the No-Thinking Prompt baseline (Ma et al., 2025), AutoThink-Stage3 on Distill-R1-1.5B improves accuracy by 14.2 pp (51.7% vs. 37.5%) while using roughly 2× the tokens, confirming that adaptive reasoning preserves accuracy far better than uniform suppression. Compared to Concise-RL (Fatemi et al., 2025), which reports 81.0% on MATH with 1,965 tokens, AutoThink-Stage3 achieves 84.0% on MATH with 2,195 tokens — higher accuracy at comparable brevity. Against ShorterBetter (Yi et al., 2025), which reports 27.6% on Minerva with 1,147 tokens, AutoThink-Stage3 reaches 28.1% on Minerva with 3,212 tokens, trading some token savings for a small accuracy edge. The strongest RL-based competitor is ThinkPrune (Hou et al., 2025): on Distill-R1-1.5B, AutoThink-Stage3 outperforms ThinkPrune-4k by 1.9 pp accuracy (51.7% vs. 49.8%) while using 5,108 vs. 4,943 tokens, and achieves an E-F1 of 39.6 versus 18.7. On DeepScaleR-1.5B, both ThinkPrune variants actually reduce accuracy below the standard prompt (55.4% and 54.8% vs. 56.7%), whereas AutoThink-Stage2 improves it to 57.3%, highlighting that uniform pruning degrades performance on already-optimized models while adaptive reasoning does not.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of R1-style reasoning models (DeepSeek-R1, DeepScaleR) and the growing line of work on mitigating overthinking. It departs from prior prompting-based approaches (no-thinking prompts, manual mode switching in Qwen3/Claude) and uniform RL pruning methods (Concise-RL, ShorterBetter, ThinkPrune) by framing reasoning as a learned dual-mode policy rather than a fixed or externally controlled behavior. The key conceptual departure is the discovery that a minimal ellipsis prompt stochastically activates latent thinking/no-thinking switching in R1-style models, which then serves as the entry point for a three-stage GRPO-based RL curriculum that progressively shapes difficulty-aware reasoning without external signals or teacher models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for deployment: a 52% token reduction with a 6.4% relative accuracy gain on a 1.5B model demonstrates that adaptive reasoning can be achieved at near-zero inference overhead compared to always-think baselines. The modular three-stage design (stabilize → reinforce → prune) is interpretable and composable, making it a practical recipe for anyone fine-tuning R1-style models. Key open questions remain: the model can decide whether to think but cannot control overall response length (no token budget), reward hacking via post- reasoning persists, and it is unclear whether the stages can be unified into a single reward formulation. Generalization beyond math is shown but modest, and the approach has not been validated on much larger models or non-RL-trained backbones.
