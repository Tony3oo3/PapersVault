limitations: The method requires a pre-trained diffusion model, adding significant computational overhead for iterative denoising, and does not outperform specialized methods on certain individual classes (e.g., 'bike' and 'truck' on VisDA-17) where distillation-based or CLIP-based approaches show slight overfitting advantages. ## Problem

Existing BUDA methods either discard low-reliability samples during self-distillation or rely on frozen CLIP for semantic alignment without exploring the target model's own reasoning capability. Prior diffusion-based domain adaptation methods require access to source data and only exploit the image encoder, leaving the text encoder untapped. The key open question is how to integrate a diffusion model into the privacy-constrained BUDA setting to enhance the target model's reasoning and generalization while preventing the negative effects of uncontrolled image generation.

## Input / Output

The method consumes unlabeled target-domain images and hard class predictions obtained solely through a black-box API (no source data or model weights are accessible), plus a pre-trained diffusion model whose CLIP image encoder remains frozen. The output is a fine-tuned target classifier (ResNet-50/101 backbone) and a diffusion text encoder updated with learnable prompt embeddings (m context tokens around a [CLS] token). The black-box predictor is queried in batches via a queue-based mechanism before training begins, and the diffusion model's predictor (following Li et al. [42]) provides soft class probabilities used as guidance signals during both stages.

## Method

RrED is a two-stage strategy inspired by human intuitive-then-deliberate decision-making. In Stage 1 (DTR), a Feature-Sensitive Generation (FSG) module computes per-patch gradient weights to identify class-discriminative regions, then fuses original target images with diffusion-generated images only in non-critical regions; simultaneously, the diffusion text encoder is fine-tuned via prompt learning with a task-specific loss, and the target model is trained with a guidance correction loss (cross-entropy against diffusion predictions) plus a conditional constraint loss that penalizes over-concentration in feature space. In Stage 2 (SRM), the fine-tuned text encoder is fed back into the diffusion model to produce more target-domain-consistent synthetic images, and a Sample-Version Interaction loss contrasts cosine similarities between predictions on original vs. synthesized versions of each sample—rectifying reasoning errors for high-similarity pairs while strengthening feature discrepancies for low-similarity pairs. A PAC-Bayes generalization bound is derived to justify the KL-divergence-based task loss and the overall error structure.

## Results

RrED achieves mean accuracies of 91.2% on Office-Home, 93.5% on Office-31, 91.2% on VisDA-17, and 80.5% on DomainNet, setting new SOTA across all four benchmarks under the BUDA setting. Compared to the previous best BUDA method AEM, RrED improves by 6.9% (Office-Home), 1.9% (VisDA-17), 1.6% (Office-31), and 5.1% (DomainNet). Against the strongest diffusion-based UDA method DAD, RrED achieves a maximum improvement of 13.1% on Office-Home while operating under far stricter privacy constraints (no source data or model access).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RrED outperforms all 19 baselines across all four benchmarks. Among BUDA methods, RrED (91.2% mean on Office-Home) surpasses AEM (89.3%), SEAL (89.2%), RFC (85.2%), BETA (85.1%), BiMem (83.6%), and DINE (75.6%), with the largest gap over DINE at +15.6%. Against diffusion-based UDA methods that require full source data access, RrED beats DAD (90.0%), DACDM (86.8%), S-Fusion (86.1%), and DATUM (75.3%) on Office-Home while operating under the far more restrictive black-box setting. On DomainNet, RrED (80.5%) exceeds the SFDA method DIFO (80.0%) and the CLIP-based BUDA method AEM (75.4%) by 0.5% and 5.1% respectively. On Office-31, RrED (93.5%) leads DAD (92.8%), DIFO (92.5%), and AEM (91.9%). The paper notes that RrED does not win on every individual class (e.g., 'bike' and 'truck' on VisDA-17), where SEAL shows slight overfitting advantages, but RrED achieves superior overall mean accuracy and more balanced per-class performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

RrED builds directly on DINE's (CVPR 2022) black-box domain adaptation framework with Adaptive Label Smoothing and on the diffusion-as-classifier mechanism from Li et al. (ICCV 2023), but departs from both prior diffusion-based DA methods (DAD, S-Fusion, DACDM) that require source data and only exploit the image encoder, and from AEM (ACMMM 2024) which uses CLIP as a frozen external prompt without fine-tuning. The key conceptual departure is treating the diffusion model as a *trainable* knowledge base in the BUDA setting: the text encoder is fine-tuned via prompt learning while the image encoder is used for feature-sensitive generation, a decomposition not explored in prior diffusion-DA work. The two-stage DTR/SRM design is explicitly motivated by Kahneman's dual-process theory of human decision-making, framing the diffusion model as an external 'System 2' guide for the target model's 'System 1' intuition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to integrate a diffusion model into the BUDA setting, demonstrating that generative models can serve as privacy-preserving knowledge bases without exposing source data or the source model. The substantial margins over AEM (up to 6.9% on Office-Home) suggest that the combination of diffusion-based generation, text-encoder fine-tuning, and differential reasoning correction captures complementary signals that neither CLIP-based prompting nor pure self-distillation can. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to other generative architectures (GANs, autoregressive models), whether the FSG module's gradient-based region selection scales to high-resolution or video inputs, and whether the computational overhead of iterative diffusion sampling is acceptable in deployment. The reliance on a pre-trained diffusion model also raises questions about domain coverage: if the target domain is far from the diffusion model's training distribution, the generated samples may be less useful.
