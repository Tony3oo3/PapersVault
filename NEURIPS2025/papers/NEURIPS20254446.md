## Problem

Existing deep learning approaches for river discharge forecasting are confined to local-scale, lumped models that ignore the spatial connectivity of river networks, making them unable to produce consistent high-resolution global discharge maps. There is no deep learning method capable of modeling spatio-temporal relations across entire continental river basins while integrating sparse gauged observations with high-resolution reanalysis and weather forecast data for medium-range (up to 7-day) flood forecasting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254446/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes 4 days of ERA5-Land and GloFAS reanalysis, 3 days of CPC precipitation, 7 days of ECMWF HRES meteorological forecasts, and static LISFLOOD river attributes, all sampled at P points on a 0.05° global grid. It outputs the change in daily mean river discharge (ΔX_dis24) for each of 7 lead times relative to the t-1 reference, which can be summed to recover absolute discharge. The model is pretrained on dense GloFAS reanalysis and fine-tuned on sparse GRDC gauged observations, and at inference it can produce dense 0.05° global discharge maps rather than predictions only at gauged stations.

## Method

RiverMamba converts the 2D spatio-temporal grid of sampled points into 1D sequences using space-filling curves (Sweep and Gilbert curves iterated across blocks), enabling bidirectional Mamba (selective state space model) blocks to process the sequence in linear time while maintaining a very large receptive field spanning entire river networks. Three hindcast layers with progressive temporal downsampling (factor 2) encode past observations, with LOAN layers injecting static river attributes as location-conditioned normalization. Seven sequential forecast blocks then incorporate HRES meteorological forcing via concatenation at each lead time, preserving spatio-temporal consistency across the forecast horizon. Training uses a weighted MSE loss that upweights rare severe floods by their statistical return period and upweights shorter lead times to compensate for the sequential forecast structure, applied to log-transformed discharge values.

## Results

On GRDC observational data (test 2021–2023), RiverMamba achieves R²=0.5057, KGE=0.6612, and F1=0.2427, outperforming all baselines including the physics-based GloFAS system (R²=0.2892, KGE=0.4944, F1=0.2044) and Google's operational LSTM (R²=0.4615, KGE=0.6141, F1=0.1475). On GloFAS reanalysis (test 2021–2024), RiverMamba reaches R²=0.8728, KGE=0.9125, and F1=0.4589, surpassing the LSTM baseline (R²=0.8485, KGE=0.8924, F1=0.3582) by a wide margin, especially in flood detection F1-score. The F1 advantage over LSTM grows with lead time, with the largest gap appearing beyond 48 hours, and RiverMamba also outperforms all methods on rare extreme floods (500-year return period).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RiverMamba is compared against two primary baselines: (1) the Encoder-Decoder LSTM from Google's operational global flood forecasting system [24], which models only temporal context without spatial connections, and (2) the physics-based GloFAS reforecast system operated by ECMWF [15,16]. Against the LSTM, RiverMamba wins on every metric across both evaluation settings: on GRDC observations it improves F1 by 64% (0.2427 vs. 0.1475) and KGE by 7.7% (0.6612 vs. 0.6141); on GloFAS reanalysis the F1 gap is even larger (0.4589 vs. 0.3582, +28%). Against the physics-based GloFAS model, RiverMamba achieves substantially higher R² (0.5057 vs. 0.2892) and KGE (0.6612 vs. 0.4944) on GRDC, with a modest F1 advantage (0.2427 vs. 0.2044). The paper attributes the LSTM's weaker performance to its lack of spatio-temporal modeling, which becomes increasingly critical at longer lead times (>48 h) and for rare extreme flood events where spatial context across connected river networks is essential.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the Mamba selective state space model—originally developed for NLP and adapted to vision (VMamba, Vim)—for the first time in hydrological forecasting, replacing the locally-lumped LSTM architectures that dominate river discharge prediction (EA-LSTM, ED-LSTM, Hydra-LSTM, and Google's operational Encoder-Decoder LSTM). The key architectural departure is the serialization of 2D spatial grid points into 1D sequences via space-filling curves (Hilbert, Sweep, Zigzag), which enables the linear-complexity Mamba blocks to capture spatio-temporal routing across entire river networks like the Amazon—something neither the locally-lumped LSTM baselines nor the small-scale GNN approaches can do. The LOAN conditioning layer for static river attributes is adapted from the authors' prior wildfire forecasting work, and the forecast blocks extend the hindcast encoder by sequentially incorporating ECMWF HRES meteorological forcing at each lead time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RiverMamba is the first deep learning system to produce global 0.05° river discharge maps with 7-day lead time, outperforming both the operational Google LSTM and the physics-based GloFAS reforecast on observational GRDC data—a notable result given that physics-based models are the current operational standard. The practical impact is significant for flood early warning, particularly for extreme events (500-year return periods) where the F1 advantage over LSTM is largest. Key open questions include: the 5-day ERA5-Land availability delay that limits real-time deployment, the inability to model human interventions (dams, irrigation) that cause the performance gap between reanalysis and observations, and the absence of uncertainty quantification, all of which are essential for operational trust. The space-filling-curve serialization strategy for SSMs is a transferable technique that could benefit other spatially-structured forecasting problems.
