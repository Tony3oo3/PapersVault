## Problem

Despite LLMs acquiring strong mathematical reasoning after supervised fine-tuning, the internal mechanism remains opaque: it is unclear whether reasoning emerges from the entire weight matrix, specific architectural modules, or is merely an overfitting artifact. This paper tackles the question of *where* in the Transformer architecture reasoning capability is localized, hypothesizing that the output projection (o_proj) in multi-head self-attention is the dominant module responsible.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253771/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The diagnostic pipeline consumes pairs of architecturally identical LLMs (a base model A with weak reasoning and a reasoning-enhanced model B obtained via SFT or RL), operating directly on their weight tensors rather than on input text. Outputs are multi-faceted: per-module L2 weight-difference norms and relative-change distributions (visualized per layer), merged model variants scored on AIME 2024, Math500, and GPQA Diamond, and qualitative output-level classifications (I–IV) that grade whether a model produces nonsense, incoherent text, fluent-but-unreasoned sentences, or correct step-by-step reasoning. The Freeze Stethoscope additionally reports training speed (steps/s) and trainable parameter counts to quantify efficiency gains.

## Method

The paper introduces Stethoscope for Networks (SfN), a four-tool diagnostic framework. The Delta Stethoscope computes per-module L2 weight differences and relative-change distributions between base and reasoning models, revealing that o_proj exhibits the largest (or second-largest) shift and a unique bimodal distribution absent in all other linear modules. The Merge Stethoscope transplants a single module type from the reasoning model into the base model without further tuning; replacing only o_proj yields correct AIME reasoning (Level IV) while replacing {q,k,v}_proj or MLP degrades performance to Level III or I. The Freeze Stethoscope selectively unfreezes o_proj (plus layernorm, embed_tokens, lm_head) during SFT on 1,000 reasoning traces, showing that tuning just ~3.2B of 32.8B parameters matches full fine-tuning on AIME 2024 while training 3× faster. The Destruction Stethoscope zeros, reinitializes, or removes individual modules in Qwen2.5-32B and observes that destroying o_proj preserves fluent conversation (Level III) while destroying any MLP component or q/k_proj collapses output to Level I, supporting a division-of-labor conjecture where o_proj handles reasoning and other modules handle dialogue.

## Results

On the Freeze Stethoscope experiments (Table 2), fine-tuning only o_proj + layernorm (model F2, 3.2B trainable params on 32B) achieves AIME 2024 accuracy of 0.367, matching full-parameter tuning (F4, 32.8B params) exactly, while also improving Math500 from 0.836 to 0.890 and GPQA Diamond from 0.485 to 0.520 over the base model. Training speed is 3.5× faster (0.052 vs 0.015 steps/s). On the Merge Stethoscope (Table 1), replacing only o_proj in Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B with DeepSeek-R1's o_proj boosts AIME 2024 from 0.067 to 0.200, approaching the full reasoning model's 0.233, while replacing {q,k,v}_proj or MLP yields 0.000.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B base model (AIME 0.067), merging only o_proj from DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B (AIME 0.233) yields a 3× improvement to 0.200, while merging {q,k,v}_proj or MLP collapses performance to 0.000. On the 32B Freeze Stethoscope, F2 (o_proj + layernorm only, 3.2B params) matches F4 (full 32.8B-parameter tuning) on AIME 2024 (both 0.367) and exceeds the base model by +0.200, though it trails F4 on Math500 (0.890 vs 0.906) and GPQA Diamond (0.520 vs 0.591). The s1 pipeline [25] (F4) serves as the full-tuning reference; F2 achieves the same AIME score with 10× fewer trainable parameters and 3.5× faster training. On 14B, F2 again matches F4 on AIME (0.266) and surpasses the base by +0.133, while F3 (adding {q,k,v}_proj) actually underperforms F2 on AIME (0.233 vs 0.266), suggesting that tuning additional attention projections is counterproductive.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of LLM interpretability and reasoning-model training. While prior interpretability efforts (attention visualization, probing, the 'Physics of Language Models' series) have studied internal representations, they typically use synthetic settings or exclude the most advanced reasoning models. The Stethoscope for Networks (SfN) framework repurposes the diagnostic metaphor of a stethoscope—probing, merging, freezing, and destroying modules—to ask a specific, previously unaddressed question: which single module, if any, is responsible for reasoning? The freeze-stethoscope experiments build on the s1 test-time scaling pipeline, and the merge/destruction experiments draw on model-editing and ablation traditions, but the systematic module-localization hypothesis for reasoning is novel.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

If the o_proj hypothesis holds, it would enable dramatically more efficient reasoning-LLM training (only ~10% of parameters need updating, with 3× speedup) and open a modular design paradigm where different o_proj 'plugins' could be swapped for different capabilities or domains. However, the evidence is currently limited to the Qwen/DeepSeek model family, the merge experiment only works cleanly at 1.5B scale, and the destruction stethoscope relies on qualitative single-example assessments rather than statistical validation. A theoretical explanation for why o_proj specifically encodes reasoning remains absent, and generalization to other architectures (e.g., Mamba, hybrid models) or non-mathematical reasoning tasks is untested.
