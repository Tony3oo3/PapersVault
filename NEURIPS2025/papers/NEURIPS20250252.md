## Problem

Compositional generalization—predicting unseen combinations of known concepts—lacks a standardized, scalable evaluation protocol, with existing benchmarks either suffering combinatorial complexity or testing under weaker constraints. Simultaneously, general-purpose vision architectures lack inductive biases for compositionality, and the strongest known remedy (fully disentangled architectures) incurs prohibitive parameter overhead that prevents practical deployment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250252/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework operates on RGB images (64×64 synthetic sprites to higher-resolution real-world photos) from six datasets with known generative factor annotations, producing multi-label attribute predictions (e.g., shape, size, color, position) via exact-match scoring. The orthotopic evaluation procedure takes the full labeled dataset and a compositional similarity index c, then generates a single train/test split by iteratively projecting and pruning samples in every (c+1)-dimensional attribute subspace, excluding roughly 60% of attribute-pair combinations from training. Unlike pair-wise evaluation, this requires only one training run per model regardless of the number of task-relevant factors.

## Method

The paper contributes two coupled components. First, an orthotopic evaluation framework that reduces compositional generalization testing from Θ(I^c) training runs to Θ(1) by simultaneously excluding orthotopic volumes across all c-dimensional subspaces, with a tunable compositional similarity index c that defines a principled difficulty ladder from extrapolation (c=0) through disentangled (c=1) and entangled (c≥2) compositional generalization to in-distribution (c=I). Second, Attribute Invariant Networks (AINs), which decompose each attribute's prediction into an attribute-specific encoder h_i, a shared meta-model m, and an attribute-specific head g_i. A key theorem guarantees that under this factorization, encoder h_i receives zero gradient from all other attributes' losses, enforcing attribute invariance by construction. This reduces parameter overhead from the 300–600% of fully disentangled models to 6.4–16%, while achieving a 23.43% average accuracy improvement over the strongest monolithic baseline (ConvNeXt) on compositional generalization splits.

## Results

On the compositional generalization split (c=1, disentangled), AINs based on ResNet-18 achieve an average +23.43% exact-match accuracy improvement over the monolithic ResNet-18 baseline across six datasets (e.g., dSprites: 61.43% vs. 21.43%; I-RAVEN: 66.23% vs. 11.30%; CLEVR: 54.30% vs. 24.68%). AINs incur only 6.4%–16% parameter overhead compared to the base model, versus 300%–600% for Explicitly Disentangled (ED) architectures. ED still leads on the hardest datasets (Shapes3D: 96.09% vs. 85.26%; MPI3D: 70.76% vs. 54.02%), but AINs match or exceed ED on dSprites (61.43% vs. 61.31%) and CLEVR (54.30% vs. 53.98%), establishing a new Pareto frontier in the scalability–generalization tradeoff.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the monolithic ResNet-18 baseline [He 2016], AINs achieve dramatic gains on every dataset (e.g., +40.0% on dSprites, +54.9% on I-RAVEN, +29.6% on CLEVR), confirming that standard CNNs lack compositional inductive biases. The Explicitly Disentangled (ED) architecture from Madan et al. [2022] remains the strongest on the most challenging datasets (Shapes3D: 96.09% vs. 85.26%; MPI3D: 70.76% vs. 54.02%; I-RAVEN: 76.12% vs. 66.23%), but at 300%–600% parameter overhead; AINs close this gap to 6.4%–16% while matching ED on dSprites and CLEVR. Among the broader monolithic family evaluated in Figure 3 (ConvNeXt, DenseNet, Wide ResNet, ViT, Swin Transformer, MLP), ConvNeXt is the strongest baseline, yet ED still outperforms it by an average of 21.38%, and AINs outperform all monolithic models by 23.43% on average. Pre-trained variants (RN-101, RN-152, DN-121 fine-tuned from ImageNet) do not consistently improve compositional generalization over their from-scratch counterparts, and MLPs remain the weakest architecture across all c values. The paper also shows that increasing optimization steps by up to 240× (to probe grokking) and replacing activations with learnable ones do not recover the gap between monolithic and AIN/ED models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies and extends prior compositional generalization evaluation protocols (pair-wise evaluation from Montero et al. and corner-exclusion from Schott et al.) under a single framework parameterized by the compositional similarity index c, reducing evaluation complexity from combinatorial to constant. Architecturally, AINs generalize the Explicitly Disentangled (ED) Shared Architecture of Madan et al. by introducing a shared meta-model that decouples attribute-specific encoders from a common compression stage, making ED a special case (attribute-specific meta-model) while drastically reducing parameter overhead. The compositional similarity index c is conceptually related to Okawa et al.'s concept distance metric but is developed into a full evaluation ladder spanning extrapolation through in-distribution regimes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The orthotopic evaluation framework addresses a critical gap: prior works used inconsistent c values (κ=1 vs. κ=I−1), making cross-paper comparisons unreliable, and the new protocol enables principled benchmarking with a single training run. The finding that even ED architectures—the strongest architectural inductive bias—still fail at disentangled compositional generalization (c=1) challenges the assumption that explicit factorization is sufficient, pointing to deeper representational limitations. AINs offer a practical path forward with 23.43% accuracy gains over monolithic baselines at only 6.4–16% parameter overhead, but the approach requires known generative factors and has only been validated on synthetic or semi-synthetic datasets, leaving open the question of whether attribute invariance transfers to noisy real-world settings like COCO or ImageNet.
