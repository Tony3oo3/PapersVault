## Problem

Online discrete black-box multi-objective optimization (MOO) requires efficiently approximating the Pareto set under expensive evaluations, but existing methods either rely on hypervolume-based acquisition functions that scale poorly with the number of objectives, or on scalarization that necessitates retraining whenever user preferences change. No prior approach directly learns a generative model of the Pareto set in an online setting while supporting a-posteriori preference conditioning without retraining.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252874/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes an initial evaluated dataset of discrete sequences (e.g., protein sequences x ∈ V^M with |V|=20) paired with their noisy objective values y ∈ R^L, a black-box function f_•(x) for sequential evaluation, and unit-norm preference direction vectors u ∈ R^L derived from objective-space points relative to a reference point r. It produces a conditional generative model q_φ(x|u) that can sample candidate sequences aligned with any user-specified trade-off direction at inference time, along with an amortized preference direction distribution q_γ(u) for broad Pareto front exploration. The modular design allows the variational distribution to be a causal transformer, masked transformer, or conditional Gaussian depending on the task.

## Method

A-GPS learns a conditional generative model q_φ(x|u) of the Pareto set by optimizing an amortized ELBO that combines three components: a Pareto non-dominance class probability estimator (CPE) trained with annealed Pareto-rank labels, a preference alignment CPE trained via contrastive learning on permuted (x, u) pairs, and a KL regularization term to a prior. A key theoretical result shows the non-dominance CPE implicitly estimates the probability of hypervolume improvement (PHVI), bypassing explicit hypervolume computation. Preference conditioning is injected through FiLM layers and a prefix embedding in the transformer backbone, enabling a-posteriori specification of trade-off directions without retraining. The variational distribution is trained with an off-policy importance-weighted gradient estimator that avoids the high variance and computational cost of on-policy REINFORCE gradients for long sequences.

## Results

On the Ehrlich vs. Naturalness benchmark (M=32, T=40 rounds), A-GPS achieves a relative hypervolume improvement of 6.264 (±1.159), comparable to VSD (6.711 ±0.952) and substantially outperforming CbAS (4.398 ±0.652) and LaMBO-2 (3.074 ±1.950). On the higher-dimensional DTLZ7 synthetic problem (D=7, L=6), A-GPS outperforms the scalarization-based qNParEGO baseline, while GP-based EHVI methods (qNEHVI, qEHVI) that explicitly estimate hypervolume dominate on lower-dimensional problems like Branin-Currin and ZDT3. On the Stability vs. SASA protein design task (M>200, T=64), A-GPS surpasses LaMBO-2 as sequence diversity diminishes over rounds. The off-policy importance-weighted gradient estimator reduces runtime by nearly an order of magnitude (19.4 min vs. 105.1 min for A-GPS at M=32) with no consistent hypervolume penalty.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the GP-based MOBO baselines (qNEHVI, qEHVI, qNParEGO) on synthetic functions, A-GPS is outperformed by the explicit hypervolume methods on low-dimensional problems (Branin-Currin, ZDT3) but outperforms qNParEGO on the higher-dimensional DTLZ7 (D=7, L=6) where EHVI-based methods scale poorly. On the three sequence design benchmarks (Ehrlich vs. Naturalness, Bi-grams, Stability vs. SASA), A-GPS performs on par with or slightly below VSD (6.264 vs. 6.711 at M=32 Ehrlich) while both clearly outperform CbAS (4.398) and LaMBO-2 (3.074). On Bi-grams, the causal-transformer A-GPS and VSD models achieve the best hypervolume, with CbAS overfitting early (visible in diversity collapse) and masked-transformer variants performing near random. On Stability vs. SASA, LaMBO-2 leads initially but A-GPS overtakes it as the search space narrows and diversity diminishes. A key differentiator is that A-GPS supports a-posteriori preference conditioning via direction vectors without retraining, a capability none of the baselines (CbAS, VSD, LaMBO-2, qNParEGO) provide.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Variational Search Distributions (VSD) framework [39] from single-objective active generation to multi-objective optimization by replacing the fitness-threshold CPE with a Pareto non-dominance CPE, and introduces amortized preference conditioning via direction vectors to avoid retraining for new trade-off specifications. It departs from acquisition-function-based MOBO methods (EHVI, nEHVI, ParEGO) by bypassing explicit hypervolume computation, and from offline multi-objective generation methods (ParetoFlow, PROUD, GFlowNets) by operating online with sequential model updates. The theoretical contribution connecting the non-dominance CPE to probability of hypervolume improvement (PHVI) provides a principled justification for the CPE-based approach, while the off-policy importance-weighted gradient estimator addresses a practical bottleneck in training complex variational distributions like causal transformers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

A-GPS offers a modular, amortized framework for online discrete MOO that practitioners can apply to protein and molecule design without retraining when preferences change, which is a practical gap in existing MOG methods. The PHVI equivalence result gives theoretical grounding to the CPE approach, connecting it to the hypervolume literature without requiring expensive hypervolume computations at each iteration. Key limitations include sensitivity to the prior model choice, the current continuous backbone (MLP) underperforming GP-based methods on some synthetic tasks, and the need for careful hyperparameter tuning (threshold annealing, β coefficient). Open questions include whether flow-matching or diffusion backbones would improve continuous performance, whether the framework extends to noisy observations with convergence guarantees, and whether MOEA-inspired quality indicators beyond hypervolume could serve as alternative CPE targets.
