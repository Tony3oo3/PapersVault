## Problem

Low-rank factorization methods (SVD, Tensor Train, CoLA, LoRA) reduce the parameter count of foundation models but introduce information bottlenecks that degrade performance, and increasing the rank to recover accuracy negates the efficiency gains. There is no lightweight mechanism to restore the lost expressiveness of low-rank subspaces without explicitly increasing the physical rank or adding substantial parameters.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254784/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

LaX is a plug-and-play module inserted between consecutive low-rank layers; it consumes the latent representation h_{i-1} (dimension r, typically 64–256) already produced by the preceding layer during the forward pass and injects it into the current layer's up-projection. For ViT pre-training the end-to-end pipeline takes 224×224 RGB images (16×16 patches) and outputs ImageNet-1K class labels; for LLM pre-training it processes C4 text tokens for next-token prediction; for fine-tuning it takes text prompts and generates reasoning answers on arithmetic and commonsense benchmarks.

## Method

LaX modifies the standard low-rank forward pass y_i = B_i(A_i x_i) to y_i = LN(B_i(A_i x_i + G(A_{i-1} x_{i-1})), where G is a gating operator that aligns and modulates the previous layer's latent features before residual fusion. Because h_{i-1} is a byproduct of the preceding layer's computation, the Identity Gate variant adds zero parameters and only O(1) FLOPs, while Tensor/Linear/Dense Gates handle dimension mismatches with minimal overhead (≤0.2% extra parameters). Two placement strategies are defined: Inter-Layer LaX routes residuals between same-type layers across transformer blocks (attention-to-attention, MLP-to-MLP), and Intra-Layer LaX applies residuals within a single tensor-train layer across its contraction chain. The module is architecture-agnostic and integrates directly with SVD, CoLA, Tensor Train, and LoRA adapters, consistently recovering 1.5–4.3% accuracy in pre-training and 1.8–2.6% in fine-tuning while keeping the parameter budget nearly unchanged.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K pre-training, LaX boosts ViT-B accuracy by +2.00% (SVD: 75.20→77.20%), +4.32% (Tensor Train: 71.11→75.43%), and +1.80% (CoLA: 76.04→77.84%), with CoLA+LaX surpassing the full-rank baseline (76.74%). On C4 LLM pre-training (60M–1B), SVD+LaX achieves 33.54 PPL at 60M (beating full-rank 34.06) and 15.51 at 1B, while CoLA+LaX reaches 14.78 PPL at 1B (beating full-rank 15.56). For fine-tuning LLaMA-7B/13B with LoRA (r=32), LaX improves average arithmetic accuracy by +1.8%/+2.1% and commonsense reasoning by +2.6%/+1.1% with zero additional parameters (Identity Gate).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SVD, LaX recovers all lost performance and exceeds the full-rank baseline: on ViT-B, SVD+LaX (77.20%) beats full-rank (76.74%), and on C4 60M, SVD+LaX (33.54 PPL) beats full-rank (34.06). Against Tensor Train, LaX delivers the largest absolute gain (+4.32% on ViT-B), transforming the weakest low-rank baseline into a competitive model. Against CoLA, LaX adds +1.80% on ViT-B and, on C4 pre-training, CoLA+LaX surpasses LORO at every scale (e.g., 33.21 vs 33.96 PPL at 60M; 14.78 vs 15.19 at 1B) while using a simpler Adam optimizer. Against GaLore and SLTrain, SVD+LaX achieves lower PPL at 60M (33.54 vs 34.88/34.15) and 1B (15.51 vs 15.64/16.14). Against ReLoRA, SVD+LaX wins at all four scales (e.g., 33.54 vs 37.04 at 60M). In fine-tuning, LoRA+LaX consistently outperforms LoRA on all six arithmetic subtasks and seven of eight commonsense tasks for both LLaMA-7B and 13B, with the largest single-task gain being +6.6% on HellaSwag (7B).

## Relation to Prior Work

LaX sits at the intersection of two well-established lines of work: low-rank factorization for neural networks (SVD, Tensor Train, CoLA, LoRA) and residual connections (ResNet). Rather than proposing a new factorization or a new rank-adaptive strategy, it repurposes the residual-connection principle by routing information across consecutive low-rank subspaces in the compressed latent space, a location where no prior low-rank training method had introduced cross-layer or cross-core skip paths. The module is explicitly orthogonal to the base factorization: it can be plugged into SVD, TT, CoLA, or LoRA without altering their parameterization or optimization, positioning LaX as a universal performance booster rather than a competing compression scheme.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a near-zero-parameter residual in latent space consistently recovers 1–4% of the accuracy lost to rank reduction across ViT-B/L pre-training, LLaMA-like 60M–1B pre-training, and LoRA fine-tuning of 7B/13B models, with the largest gains on the weakest baselines (e.g., +4.32% for Tensor Train ViT-B). This suggests that much of the performance gap between low-rank and full-rank models stems from information isolation between subspaces rather than from insufficient rank per se, an insight that could reshape how practitioners allocate rank budgets. Open questions include whether the gains compound at much larger scales (10B+), whether a theoretical account of why cross-subspace residuals help is possible, and whether the approach extends to other factorization families (e.g., CP, Tucker, tensor ring) beyond the SVD/TT/CoLA/LoRA settings tested here.
