## Problem

Deploying LLMs on resource-constrained devices is hindered by memory and compute costs, and while 1-bit quantization reduces size, even 1-bit 70B models (~15.4 GB) exceed many edge budgets. The sub-1-bit regime (below 0.55 BPW) remains largely unsolved: the leading prior method, STBLLM, suffers severe perplexity collapse below 0.55 BPW, making it unclear whether viable language modeling is possible at rates as low as 0.1 BPW.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-trained LLM's full-precision weight matrices (W ∈ R^{d_out × d_in}) and produces a factorized, binarized representation per linear layer: two binary sign matrices (U_sign, V_sign ∈ {±1}^{·×r}) and three FP16 scale vectors (h, g, l) for each of the primary and residual paths. The effective weight is never materialized; instead, the latent rank r is solved per layer from a target BPW via a closed-form equation, and the forward pass operates on rank-r latent states. At inference, the quantized model accepts standard text input and produces generated text, with the factorized K/V projections simultaneously compressing the KV cache by a factor of d_model/r.

## Method

LittleBit approximates each weight matrix via low-rank factorization W ≈ UV^T and binarizes the factors, then compensates for binarization loss with learnable FP16 scales along three axes: row (h), column (g), and a novel latent dimension (l) that captures per-rank-importance. A parallel residual path mirrors this structure to model the approximation error, with both paths jointly optimized via QAT using knowledge distillation (KL + intermediate MSE loss) and a SmoothSign proxy gradient (tanh(100x)) for backpropagation through the sign function. Dual-SVID initialization performs a truncated SVD on W, sets binary factors from the SVD signs, and decomposes the magnitude matrices via rank-1 SVD to initialize all three scale vectors, providing a stable starting point for QAT. The forward pass replaces one large FP16 GEMM with two smaller binary matrix multiplications and element-wise scaling, yielding up to 11.6× kernel-level speedup over FP16 at 0.1 BPW while compressing Llama2-13B to under 0.9 GB.

## Results

On WikiText-2 perplexity, LITTLEBIT at 0.55 BPW achieves PPL 10.47 on Llama2-7B and 9.24 on Llama2-13B, substantially outperforming STBLLM at the same rate (30.67 and 27.05 respectively). At the extreme 0.1 BPW, LITTLEBIT maintains PPL 15.92 (Llama2-7B) and 15.09 (Llama2-13B), whereas STBLLM degrades to PPL >800 even at 0.3 BPW. At ~1.0 BPW, LITTLEBIT achieves PPL 9.08 on Llama2-7B, competitive with specialized 1-bit QAT methods (OneBit: 8.36, BinaryMoS: 7.74). On zero-shot reasoning, LITTLEBIT at 0.55 BPW on Llama2-7B achieves 47.26% average accuracy vs. 44.29% for STBLLM at the same rate.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against STBLLM (the leading sub-1-bit PTQ method), LITTLEBIT wins decisively at every comparable bit rate: at 0.55 BPW on Llama2-7B, LITTLEBIT achieves PPL 10.47 vs. STBLLM's 30.67, and at 0.3 BPW the gap widens to 12.00 vs. 1.8×10³. On Llama3-8B at 0.55 BPW, LITTLEBIT records PPL 18.47 while STBLLM collapses to 241.95. At ~1.0 BPW, LITTLEBIT (PPL 9.08 on Llama2-7B) trails the specialized 1-bit QAT methods OneBit (8.36) and BinaryMoS (7.74) by 0.7–1.3 PPL, a gap the authors attribute to BinaryMoS's more complex dynamic scaling mechanism. However, LITTLEBIT at 1.0 BPW still substantially outperforms 2-bit PTQ baselines GPTQ (52.22) and OmniQuant (11.20) on Llama2-7B, and BiLLM at 1.1 BPW (29.00). On zero-shot reasoning (Table 2), LITTLEBIT at 0.55 BPW on Llama2-7B achieves 47.26% average accuracy vs. STBLLM's 44.29%, and at 0.3 BPW maintains 45.20% vs. STBLLM's 35.78%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate compression threads—low-rank factorization (SVD-based) and weight binarization—into a single architecture for extreme sub-1-bit LLM quantization. It extends OneBit's row/column scaling mechanism by adding a third, latent-dimension scale that captures the relative importance of each SVD component, and introduces Dual-SVID to initialize the binarized factorized structure from a truncated SVD of the original weights. The Residual Compensation mechanism departs from prior single-path approaches (e.g., STBLLM's N:M sparsity, DL-QAT's joint low-rank/low-precision learning) by splitting a fixed bit budget into two lower-rank parallel paths, each independently binarized and scaled. Unlike STBLLM, which operates as a post-training method, LITTLEBIT relies on QAT with knowledge distillation and a SmoothSign proxy gradient to stabilize training in the ultra-low-bit regime.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Achieving 0.1 BPW (≈31× compression, Llama2-13B under 0.9 GB) with usable perplexity opens a practical path for deploying LLMs on devices with <1 GB memory, a regime previously inaccessible. The inherent KV-cache compression (up to 21×) and 11.6× kernel-level speedup further strengthen the case for edge deployment. However, generated samples at 0.1 BPW reveal severe factual degradation (e.g., describing the Mona Lisa as a 'statue in Milan's fashion world'), and residual compensation actually hurts performance for small models at the extreme end, suggesting the method's benefits are scale-dependent. Open questions include whether hardware co-design (e.g., NPU-specific kernels) can close the gap between the proof-of-concept CUDA kernel and production-grade inference, and whether non-uniform bit allocation across layers or between primary/residual paths can push fidelity further without increasing total bits.
