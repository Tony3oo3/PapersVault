## Problem

Existing 3D layout estimation models are trained on synthetic, single-floor scenes and fail to generalize to large-scale, multi-floor buildings. Processing floors independently discards global context essential for cross-floor reasoning (e.g., detecting staircases), and no real-world benchmark existed to evaluate multi-floor layout estimation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254677/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of unposed RGB images with COLMAP-estimated camera poses as input and produces a vectorized 3D layout: a set of semantically labeled polygons (walls, floors, ceilings, stairs, doors, windows) organized into a scene graph where rooms are nodes and doors/stairs are edges. Each polygon is assigned to a specific room or inter-room edge, and the layout is built by first creating per-level 2D floorplans that are then extruded into 3D closed room shells. The method is entirely training-free, relying on pre-trained components (DN-Splatter, OneFormer, Metric3D) without any layout-specific fine-tuning.

## Method

MultiFloor3D is a four-stage training-free pipeline: (1) generate a triangle mesh from RGB images via DN-Splatter (3D Gaussian Splatting with Metric3D depth priors and Poisson reconstruction); (2) extract a layout skeleton by segmenting the mesh with OneFormer into four semantic classes and retaining only structural components; (3) fit a layout prototype to the skeleton via gradient-based optimization of planar polygon vertex positions and plane equations, minimizing three losses (geometric fidelity to skeleton vertices and observed empty space, inter-polygon connectivity, and edge-length simplicity), supplemented by object-to-floor projection and wall extension to close holes; (4) parse the prototype into a scene graph by identifying floor levels, constructing 2D floorplans from floor/ceiling polygon unions, segmenting rooms with HovSG's morphology-based algorithm, detecting stairs from clustered stair meshes, and extruding each 2D floorplan to 3D via Constrained Delaunay Triangulation with ceiling-plane assignment. The key insight is that combining off-the-shelf 3D reconstruction, 2D segmentation, and geometric optimization yields a simple pipeline that outperforms end-to-end trained models on multi-floor buildings without any layout-specific training.

## Results

On the proposed HOUSELAYOUT3D benchmark, MultiFloor3D achieves F1@0.5 of 0.40 for structures (walls, floors, ceilings), 0.55 for doors, 0.43 for windows, and 0.42 for stairs, substantially outperforming RoomFormer (0.28/0.23/0.16 per-room) and SceneScript (0.23/0.31/0.11) across all categories. On depth accuracy, MultiFloor3D reaches Δ5=61.1% and Δ10=76.3% on HOUSELAYOUT3D, compared to 22.5–37.3% for RoomFormer and 23.5% for SceneScript. On ScanNet++ (where no layout GT exists), MultiFloor3D achieves Δ5=67.8% and Δ10=84.7% with only 83.1 vertices, versus 354k vertices for the raw DN-Splatter mesh.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MultiFloor3D decisively outperforms both trained baselines on HOUSELAYOUT3D. Against RoomFormer [1] (CVPR 2023), MultiFloor3D improves structure F1@0.5 from 0.28 (per-room) to 0.40, door F1@0.5 from 0.23 to 0.55, and window F1@0.5 from 0.16 to 0.43, while also achieving far better depth accuracy (Δ5: 61.1% vs. 22.5%). Against SceneScript [2] (ECCV 2024), the gains are similarly large: structure F1@0.5 rises from 0.23 to 0.40, door F1@0.5 from 0.31 to 0.55, and window F1@0.5 from 0.11 to 0.43; SceneScript also does not predict stairs at all. On ScanNet++ (Table 3), the raw DN-Splatter mesh [21] achieves higher depth accuracy (Δ5=84.1% vs. 67.8%) but at 354k vertices—over 4,000× more than MultiFloor3D's 83.1—making it impractical as a layout representation. Notably, MultiFloor3D is training-free, whereas both RoomFormer and SceneScript are trained on ~100k synthetic samples, yet it still wins on every metric where it is directly comparable.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in 3D layout estimation, where end-to-end deep learning models (RoomFormer, SceneScript, SceneCAD) are trained on large-scale synthetic single-room or single-floor datasets. Instead, MultiFloor3D composes recent off-the-shelf components—DN-Splatter for mesh generation, OneFormer for semantic segmentation, and HovSG for room segmentation—into a training-free pipeline that uses geometric optimization (polygon fitting with connectivity and simplicity losses) to produce a coherent multi-floor layout. The key conceptual departure is from 'learn a layout predictor on synthetic data' to 'leverage advances in 3D reconstruction and 2D segmentation with a layout-specific fitting stage,' arguing that the bottleneck for multi-floor generalization is data availability rather than model capacity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

HOUSELAYOUT3D is the first benchmark exposing that current SOTA layout methods, trained on ~100k synthetic single-room samples, fail to generalize to buildings with multiple floors and up to 40 rooms per floor—a gap that per-floor processing with ground-truth segmentation only partially masks. The finding that a training-free geometric pipeline outperforms these trained models on the harder multi-floor task suggests the field may need to rethink whether end-to-end learning on synthetic data is the right path for architecturally complex scenes. Key open questions include whether learning-based approaches can be developed for multi-floor buildings, whether the 1–2 hour runtime of MultiFloor3D (vs. 1–2 minutes for baselines) can be reduced, and whether the 16-building dataset is sufficient to draw robust conclusions. The downstream LLM navigation demo hints at practical applications but remains illustrative rather than validated.
