## Problem

While optimal spectral methods for single-index models are well understood and provably achieve the weak recovery threshold, their multi-index counterparts (where the target depends on a p-dimensional subspace) remain largely unexplored. Prior work established the optimal computational threshold for weak recovery via GAMP but required an informed initialization; no spectral method was known to reach this threshold from random initialization in the multi-index setting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255818/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes an n×d Gaussian design matrix X (rows drawn from N(0, d^{-1}I_d)), a label vector y generated from a conditional distribution P(y|W_*^T x), and the link function g (or equivalently the conditional P(y|z)) to compute the per-sample matrices G(y_i) = E[zz^T − I | y_i]. It outputs a d×p estimated weight matrix W_hat whose column span weakly recovers the true index subspace, analyzed in the proportional high-dimensional limit n/d → α. The analysis is restricted to link functions with generative exponent 2 (i.e., E[z|y] = 0 a.s.), excluding the hardest cases like sparse parity.

## Method

The paper constructs two spectral estimators by linearizing the optimal GAMP algorithm around its uninformed fixed point: an asymmetric estimator W_hat_L from the top eigenvector of a non-symmetric matrix L built from (XX^T − I)G(y_j), and a symmetric estimator W_hat_T from the top eigenvector of a matrix T built from X^T X weighted by G(y)(G(y)+I)^{-1}. The key insight is that the linearized GAMP dynamics reduce to power iteration on L (or T), so the top eigenvector at convergence is the spectral estimate. State evolution equations for the associated linear GAMP are derived and analyzed via linear stability arguments, showing that above the critical sample complexity α_c the leading eigenvector acquires non-vanishing overlap with W_* (a BBP-type transition), while below α_c it remains uncorrelated. For a restricted class of models where G(y) is jointly diagonalizable, a full random-matrix-theory proof of the spectral transition is provided (Theorem 2.11), while the general multi-index case is supported by state evolution analysis and extensive numerical validation.

## Results

The paper's central quantitative result is that both proposed spectral estimators (asymmetric L and symmetric T) achieve weak recovery exactly at the critical sample complexity α_c defined by the optimal GAMP of [18], without requiring informed initialization. For the link function g(z) = p⁻¹‖z‖² with p=4, the threshold is α_c = p/2 = 2, and the overlap is m² = ((α − p/2)/(α + 2))₊. For g(z₁,z₂) = z₁z₂, α_c ≈ 0.59375 with m² = ((α − α_c)/(α + α_c²·E[λ³]))₊. Above α_c, the leading eigenvalue of L separates from the bulk at γ_s = α/α_c (asymmetric) or λ_s = 1 (symmetric), exhibiting a BBP-type transition confirmed by numerical simulations at n = d = 5000–10⁴.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not benchmark against competing spectral algorithms on a common dataset; instead, the primary comparison is against the information-theoretic/computational threshold established by the optimal GAMP of Troiani et al. [18]. The key result is that both the asymmetric (L) and symmetric (T) spectral estimators match this optimal threshold α_c exactly, closing the gap that previously required an informed (side-information) initialization for GAMP to succeed. For p=1, the proposed methods reduce precisely to the 'LAMP' and 'TAP' spectral estimators of Maillard et al. [10], recovering their known optimal weak-recovery guarantees. An independent concurrent work by Kovačević, Zhang, and Mondelli [47] provides an alternative random-matrix-theory proof of the same threshold for the special case where G(y) is jointly diagonalizable across all y, confirming Theorem 2.11 for that subset. The asymmetric and symmetric methods are shown to be spectrally related (Proposition 2.12): an eigenvector of T with eigenvalue 1 maps to an eigenvector of L with eigenvalue 1, meaning both capture the same signal direction above threshold.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the single-index spectral method construction of Maillard et al. [10]—which derived optimal estimators by linearizing Vector AMP and the TAP free energy—to the multi-index setting, where prior spectral results were largely absent. It directly addresses a gap left by Troiani et al. [18], who established the optimal GAMP weak-recovery threshold for multi-index models but required an informed initialization; here, the spectral estimators achieve the same threshold without side information. The state evolution framework of Javanmard & Montanari [52] and the optimal GAMP denoisers of Aubin et al. [4] provide the analytical backbone, while the non-backtracking/TAP connection of Saade et al. [37] motivates the symmetric estimator's structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By showing that a simple linearization of the optimal GAMP yields spectral methods at the provably optimal weak-recovery threshold, the paper provides the first principled, initialization-free spectral tools for multi-index subspace recovery—a key testbed for understanding feature learning in two-layer networks. The BBP-type phase transition identified for both the asymmetric (L) and symmetric (T) operators gives a concrete random-matrix-theory picture of when and how the signal emerges from the bulk. Open questions include a full RMT proof of the spectral norm conjectures (Conjectures 2.5, 2.10), extension to higher-order Kikuchi-hierarchy methods for harder link functions (e.g., sparse parity), and understanding whether the structured redundancy in T for p>1 admits a general RMT treatment beyond the jointly-diagonalizable special case.
