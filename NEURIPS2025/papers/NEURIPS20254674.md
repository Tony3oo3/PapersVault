## Problem

Existing VLA benchmarks predominantly evaluate within-task generalization (visual variations within known tasks), leaving zero-shot cross-task generalization—handling entirely unseen combinations of objects, goals, and actions—largely unexplored. This paper introduces AGNOSTOS, a benchmark with 23 unseen tasks at two difficulty levels, and systematically reveals that current VLA models, despite diverse pre-training, fail to generalize effectively to these unseen tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254674/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For each unseen task, the method receives a single initial RGB observation and a language instruction, plus a pool of 3,600 seen-task demonstrations (visual sequences, action sequences, and language descriptions). The dynamics diffusion model consumes the initial frame and language to predict the final observation, extracting a 2×1024 dynamic feature vector. The LLM prompt textualizes object 3D positions (via GroundingDINO 2D detection + depth back-projection, normalized to a 100×100×100 grid) and key-actions (7-DoF end-effector states at gripper transitions or near-zero velocities, with angles discretized into 72 bins of 5°). The output is a predicted key-action sequence in the same 7-DoF textualized format.

## Method

X-ICM comprises two modules. First, a Dynamics-guided Sample Selection module trains a diffusion model (initialized from InstructPix2Pix) to predict final observations from initial observations and language descriptions across all seen demonstrations; it then extracts 2×1024 dynamic features (predicted visual latent + textual embedding) and retrieves the top-K=18 most relevant seen demonstrations via cosine similarity to the unseen task's feature. Second, a Cross-task In-context Prediction module textualizes each retrieved demonstration as a mapping from [language, object 3D positions] → [key-action sequence], concatenates them in descending similarity order, appends the unseen task's textualized context, and prompts an off-the-shelf LLM (Qwen2.5-7B/72B-Instruct) to generate the key-action sequence. The key insight is that dynamics-based similarity (modeling the initial→final state transformation) outperforms static feature baselines (CLIP, DINOv2, random) for cross-task demonstration retrieval, as confirmed by ablation showing 30.1% vs. 25.2–27.4% average success.

## Results

On the AGNOSTOS benchmark (23 unseen tasks), X-ICM (72B) achieves the highest overall zero-shot cross-task success rate of 30.1% (Level-1: 37.6%, Level-2: 20.3%), outperforming the best prior VLA model π₀ (17.5% overall) by 12.6 percentage points. X-ICM (7B) achieves 23.5% overall, surpassing π₀ by 6.0% and VoxPoser by 7.9%. Notably, X-ICM (72B) succeeds on all 23 tasks, while every prior model completely fails on at least 8 of them.

## Baselines & Comparisons

X-ICM (72B) outperforms all 15 baselines across both difficulty levels. On Level-1 tasks (partial semantic overlap), X-ICM (72B) achieves 37.6%, beating the strongest baseline π₀ (21.7%) by 15.9 points and VoxPoser (18.1%) by 19.5 points. On Level-2 tasks (entirely novel semantics), X-ICM (72B) reaches 20.3%, surpassing SAM2Act (15.9%) and 3D-LOTUS++ (15.1%), which are the best Level-2 performers among baselines. The 7B variant (X-ICM 7B) also leads all baselines overall at 23.5%, exceeding π₀ by 6.0 points and VoxPoser by 7.9 points. In-domain models (PerAct, RVT, RVT2) and human-video pre-trained models (D4R, R3M) show the weakest cross-task generalization, with overall success rates below 15%, confirming that domain-specific training does not transfer to unseen task semantics. The ablation in Table 3 further shows that the dynamics-guided sample selection module contributes a 4.9-point overall gain (30.1% vs. 25.2% with random selection), validating its role in identifying relevant cross-task demonstrations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the in-context manipulation paradigm (KAT, RoboPrompt, Instant Policy) from within-task to zero-shot cross-task generalization, where the LLM must predict actions for tasks with entirely unseen object-motion combinations. For sample selection, it repurposes InstructPix2Pix as a dynamics diffusion model to encode task-level temporal transformations, departing from prior work that relied on static visual features (CLIP, DINOv2) or random sampling. The AGNOSTOS benchmark builds on RLBench but deliberately targets the cross-task semantic gap that prior benchmarks (Colosseum, GemBench, VLMBench) leave unaddressed, which focus on within-task visual perturbations rather than novel task compositions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The systematic demonstration that 15+ state-of-the-art VLA models (including large-scale foundation models like π0 and OpenVLA) all fail on the majority of cross-task scenarios provides a sobering reality check on current generalization claims and establishes a much-needed diagnostic benchmark. The finding that a 72B LLM with in-context prompting outperforms all purpose-built VLA policies on cross-task transfer suggests that the in-context learning paradigm may be a more natural fit for compositional generalization than end-to-end policy learning. Key open questions remain: absolute performance is still low (30% best), long-horizon tasks collapse to 5% success, visual information is reduced to textualized object positions, and scaling to diverse embodiments is untested.
