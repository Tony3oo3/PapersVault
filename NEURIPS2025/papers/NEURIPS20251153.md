## Problem

Video diffusion models suffer from slow sequential denoising, and existing caching-based acceleration methods either use uniform heuristics that waste computation or rely on prompt-specific polynomial fitting (e.g., TeaCache's 70 curated prompts) that risks overfitting and inconsistent outputs. There is no principled, prompt-agnostic criterion for determining which timesteps can be safely skipped without degrading visual quality.

## Input / Output

MagCache takes a text prompt and a pre-trained diffusion model (video models at 480P/540P with 49–129 frames, or image models at 1024×1024) and produces the generated video or image with fewer effective denoising steps. A single random sample is forwarded once to calibrate the magnitude-ratio curve, after which the method is plug-and-play with no retraining. The first 20% of diffusion steps are always computed fully, and the method adds only ~0.5 GB of extra memory for caching.

## Method

The key insight is a unified magnitude law: the per-step magnitude ratio γ_t = mean(||r_t||₂/||r_{t-1}||₂) of successive residual outputs decreases monotonically and is remarkably stable across different prompts and models, meaning residual changes between adjacent timesteps are dominated by magnitude rather than direction. MagCache models the skip error between a cached residual at step t̂ and the ground-truth residual at step t as ε_skip = 1 − ∏_{i=t̂+1}^{t} γ_i, and maintains a running accumulated error E_t over consecutive skips. An adaptive caching strategy reuses the cached residual only while both E_t ≤ δ (user-specified error threshold) and t − t̂ ≤ K (maximum skip length); otherwise it recomputes the true residual and resets the cache. This requires only a single random calibration sample rather than dozens of curated prompts, and the multiplicative error formulation is validated to approximate the ground-truth magnitude ratio to within 1e-5.

## Results

MagCache achieves 2.10×–2.68× inference speedups on video diffusion models (Open-Sora, CogVideoX, Wan 2.1, HunyuanVideo) and up to 2.82× on the image model Flux, while maintaining superior visual fidelity. On Wan 2.1 1.3B, MagCache-fast reaches a 2.68× speedup (69.75 s latency vs. 187.21 s baseline) with LPIPS 0.1748, SSIM 0.7490, and PSNR 21.54. On HunyuanVideo, MagCache-slow achieves a 2.25× speedup with an exceptionally low LPIPS of 0.0377 and PSNR of 34.51, far exceeding TeaCache-slow (LPIPS 0.1832, PSNR 23.87) at the same FLOPs budget. The method requires only 0.5 GB of extra memory, compared to 40 GB for TaylorSeer.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against TeaCache (the primary competitor), MagCache consistently wins on all three visual quality metrics at equal or higher speedups: on Open-Sora, MagCache-slow achieves LPIPS 0.0827 vs. TeaCache-slow's 0.1303 at the same 1.4× speedup, and MagCache-fast (2.10×) matches TeaCache-slow's quality while being 33% faster. On Wan 2.1, MagCache-fast (2.68×, LPIPS 0.1748) substantially outperforms TeaCache-fast (2.14×, LPIPS 0.2412) in both speed and quality. Against memory-heavy baselines, MagCache requires only 0.5 GB extra memory versus 40 GB for TaylorSeer, while achieving far better quality (LPIPS 0.1748 vs. 0.3792 on Wan 2.1). Compared to PAB and Δ-DiT, MagCache delivers 1.5–2.5× higher speedups with dramatically lower LPIPS (e.g., 0.0827 vs. 0.5692 for Δ-DiT on Open-Sora). On HunyuanVideo, MagCache-slow achieves a remarkable LPIPS of 0.0377 and PSNR of 34.51, exceeding TeaCache-slow by over 10 dB in PSNR at the same 2.25× speedup.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits within the caching-based acceleration lineage for diffusion models (DeepCache, PAB, Δ-DiT, FasterCache, TeaCache, DuCa, TaylorSeer), which reuse intermediate features to skip redundant timesteps without retraining. Its key departure is from TeaCache, which fits prompt-specific polynomial curves over 70 curated samples to predict when to skip steps; MagCache instead discovers a model- and prompt-invariant 'unified magnitude law'—the monotonically decreasing ratio of successive residual magnitudes—and uses it directly for error modeling. This shifts the calibration burden from dozens of curated prompts to a single random forward pass, and replaces polynomial fitting with a simple multiplicative product of magnitude ratios.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: 2.1×–2.7× speedups on major open-source video models (Open-Sora, Wan 2.1, HunyuanVideo, CogVideoX) and Flux, with consistently better LPIPS/SSIM/PSNR than TeaCache at matched compute budgets, and only 0.5 GB extra memory versus 40 GB for TaylorSeer. The magnitude law's robustness across models, prompts, schedulers, and step counts makes it a broadly deployable, plug-and-play module. Open questions include whether the law holds for non-video tasks (audio, 3D), for models with fundamentally different architectures (e.g., autoregressive video), and whether the first-20%-steps-unchanged heuristic can be made adaptive rather than fixed.
