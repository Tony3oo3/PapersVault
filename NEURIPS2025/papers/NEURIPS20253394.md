## Problem

Post-training LLMs for reasoning relies on either supervised fine-tuning (SFT), which memorizes solutions and overfits on larger models, or reinforcement fine-tuning (RFT), which generalizes well but suffers from sparse rewards and an exponential sample complexity bottleneck in reasoning length. These two paradigms are typically applied in separate sequential stages, leaving a gap: small models cannot explore correctly under RFT, while large models overfit under SFT. No existing method smoothly unifies both signals within a single training process.

## Input / Output

During training, the model receives a problem statement concatenated with a partial-solution hint whose length is sampled from a Binomial(L, p) distribution, where p is annealed from 0.95 to 0.05 via cosine schedule over the first T_hint steps and then set to zero. At inference, only the bare problem statement is provided (zero hint). The model outputs a full reasoning trace (chain-of-thought) terminating in a boxed final answer, evaluated by a rule-based verifier that assigns accuracy reward (1.0), format reward (0.1), or zero.

## Method

UFT introduces two coupled modifications to standard GRPO-based RFT. First, trajectory sampling begins from a hint state (a prefix of the optimal solution) rather than the root, with the hint length smoothly annealed to zero via cosine scheduling to avoid distribution shift. Second, the objective function augments the standard RL reward term with an additional log-likelihood term β·Σ log π(a*_h | s*_h) over the hint portion, which is equivalent to minimizing KL(π*‖π) on hint states and injects supervised knowledge into the gradient. When the hint proportion p→0 the objective reduces to pure RFT; when p→1 it reduces to SFT, making UFT a continuous interpolation. Theoretically, the paper proves RFT requires Ω(B^H) explorations to find the correct path in a search tree of height H, while UFT achieves O(BH⁵(log B)²/Δ²), an exponential improvement in reasoning length.

## Results

Across five model sizes (Qwen2.5-0.5/1.5/3B, Llama-3.2-1/3B) and nine benchmarks, UFT achieves the highest average accuracy for small models: 9.45% on Qwen2.5-0.5B (vs. 7.28% for SFT-RFT and 3.25% for RFT) and 22.23% on Qwen2.5-1.5B (vs. 16.08% for RFT). For the larger Qwen2.5-3B, UFT reaches 30.93% average, closely matching RFT's 32.15% while far exceeding SFT-RFT (17.34%). On the Logic puzzle, UFT shows the most dramatic gains: Qwen2.5-1.5B jumps from 0.00% (RFT) to 23.00% (UFT), and Qwen2.5-0.5B improves from 0.00% to 4.87%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UFT consistently outperforms R³ (Xi et al., 2024) across all model sizes: by +2.09% on Qwen2.5-0.5B (9.45% vs. 7.36%), +3.58% on Qwen2.5-1.5B (22.23% vs. 18.65%), +2.91% on Qwen2.5-3B (30.93% vs. 28.02%), and +1.32% on Llama-3.2-1B (3.52% vs. 2.20%). Against SFT-RFT, UFT wins decisively for small models (9.45% vs. 7.28% on 0.5B; 3.52% vs. 3.29% on Llama-3.2-1B) but trails slightly on Llama-3.2-3B (3.72% vs. 4.79%). Against RFT, UFT dominates for small models (9.45% vs. 3.25% on 0.5B; 22.23% vs. 16.08% on 1.5B) and nearly matches it for the 3B model (30.93% vs. 32.15%). The key pattern is that UFT automatically adapts: it matches SFT-level memorization for capacity-limited models and RFT-level generalization for stronger models, while R³'s uniform hint-length sampling and lack of a supervised log-likelihood term leave it behind in both regimes.

## Relation to Prior Work

UFT builds directly on GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) as its RL backbone and on R³ (Xi et al., ICML 2024) as its hint-based exploration mechanism, but departs from both in a key way: rather than treating hints purely as exploration aids (R³) or discarding all annotations once RL begins (SFT-RFT), UFT integrates the hint into the objective function as a log-likelihood term, creating a single unified loss that smoothly interpolates between SFT (p=1) and RFT (p=0). This positions UFT as a principled bridge between the memorization-oriented and generalization-oriented paradigms that prior work has treated as mutually exclusive or strictly sequential.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical proof that UFT reduces sample complexity from exponential (B^H) to polynomial (H^5) in reasoning length is the first formal demonstration that unified training can break RFT's inherent exploration bottleneck, providing a clear justification for why hint-based supervision helps beyond mere engineering heuristics. Practically, UFT's automatic adaptation to model capacity—memorizing for small models, generalizing for large ones—offers a single training recipe that removes the need to hand-tune whether to use SFT or RFT. However, all experiments are limited to ≤3B models and simple reasoning tasks (Countdown, MATH 3-5, Knights and Knaves), leaving open whether the approach scales to 70B+ models on genuinely hard benchmarks, and whether it composes with more advanced RL algorithms (DAPO, REINFORCE++) or generated chain-of-thought data.
