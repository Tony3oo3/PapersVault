## Problem

Existing AI-text detectors rely on flat binary or multi-class classification that treats all human-AI collaborative processes as equivalent, ignoring the inherent hierarchical similarity structure among different generation sources (e.g., texts refined by the same LLM cluster more closely than those from unrelated models). This coarse modeling limits generalization to unseen LLMs and diverse collaboration patterns encountered in real-world hybrid text detection.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254887/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text sequence (up to 512 tokens) as input and produces a fine-grained source category label identifying the specific generation process (e.g., 'Llama3_polish_GPT-4o', 'human_polish_Gemini1.5') or a coarser AI-involvement classification. At inference, classification is performed via KNN retrieval over a K-Means-compressed database using similarity-weighted scoring (following DINOv2), rather than a fixed classifier head, enabling flexible database updates for OOD adaptation.

## Method

DETree constructs a Hierarchical Affinity Tree (HAT) from inter-class centroid similarities computed via supervised contrastive learning, using agglomerative clustering followed by a top-down subtree reorganization guided by Silhouette Score to produce a multiway tree capturing intrinsic affinities among text categories. A Tree-Structured Contrastive Loss (TSCL) then retrains the RoBERTa-large encoder (with LoRA) to align the embedding space with the HAT, enforcing that categories sharing closer ancestors in the tree are mapped to more similar representations. A Virtual Class Prototype mechanism provides persistent learnable anchors per category to handle the large number of classes within a single mini-batch. For OOD generalization, a retrieval-based few-shot adaptation paradigm rebuilds the KNN database with a small number of target-domain samples, reformulating cross-domain detection as a matching problem under low-resource conditions.

## Results

On the supervised in-distribution detection task (Table 1), DETree with prior1 achieves an average F1 of 97.75 and AvgRec of 97.88 across MAGE, M4, and TuringBench, outperforming the strongest baseline DeTeCTive (F1 96.74, AvgRec 96.94) by ~1 point. Under out-of-distribution settings (Table 2), DETree's few-shot adaptation reaches 99.81 AUROC on MAGE-Unseen and 99.88 AUROC on DetectRL Multi-Domain, substantially exceeding zero-shot baselines like Binoculars (96.84) and Fast-Detect (80.76). On the three-level hybrid text detection task (Table 3), DETree w/prior1 achieves 0.998 AUROC (99.5% TPR@5%FPR) at Level-1, 0.992 (98.5%) at Level-2, and 0.988 (95.3%) at Level-3, far surpassing HART's own best configuration (0.883 AUROC at Level-3).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DETree (w/prior1) outperforms all 12 baselines on the supervised detection task, achieving 97.75 average F1 versus DeTeCTive's 96.74, SCL's 91.25, and RoBERTa-Base's 90.14. The gap is most pronounced on M4-multilingual, where DETree reaches 94.85 F1 while T5-Sentinel drops to 68.99 and MAGE to 83.00, indicating that the hierarchical structure helps with multilingual generalization. In OOD settings, DETree's few-shot adaptation (5/10 shots) achieves 99.58–99.88 AUROC on MAGE and DetectRL, far exceeding zero-shot methods like Binoculars (75.87–96.84) and Fast-Detect (53.34–80.76), as well as the few-shot UAR baseline (58.18–75.39). On the HART hybrid text detection task, DETree w/prior1 achieves 0.998 AUROC at Level-1 versus HART(Binoculars) at 0.838 and Fast-Detect at 0.778, a gap of over 15 points in TPR@5%FPR. The ablation (w/o TSCL) shows that removing the Tree-Structured Contrastive Loss costs ~1.4 F1 points, confirming the loss's contribution beyond standard supervised contrastive learning. Binoculars performs poorly on TuringBench (F1 9.98) and Beemo No-Attack (AUROC 83.90), highlighting its fragility under distribution shift, whereas DETree maintains consistent performance across all tested domains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends supervised contrastive learning (SimCLR) from flat class separation to a tree-structured objective, where the hierarchy is not predefined but learned from inter-class embedding similarities via agglomerative clustering and Silhouette-score-guided subtree reorganization. It departs from prior hybrid-text detection work (HART, MIXSET) that treats collaboration as a fixed multi-class label or a content/expression decoupling, instead modeling the *relational structure* among arbitrary generation sources (e.g., 'Llama3_polish_GPT-4o' vs. 'human_polish_Gemini1.5') as a data-driven affinity tree. For inference, it repurposes KNN retrieval with K-Means compression rather than a parametric classifier, and introduces a retrieval-based few-shot adaptation paradigm for OOD generalization that is distinct from prompt-based in-context learning (OUTFOX) or style-space localization (UAR).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 16.4M-sample RealBench benchmark and the finding that AI stylistic traces dominate over human characteristics in hybrid texts provide a practical foundation for deployment in academic-integrity and content-moderation pipelines. The OOD few-shot results (+15.55 AUROC on MAGE-Paraphrase, +10–15 on Beemo edited subsets) suggest that training-based detectors can remain viable as new LLMs emerge, countering the narrative that only zero-shot statistical methods generalize. Open questions include whether the approach scales to more than three collaborators, how it handles adversarial evasion via targeted fine-tuning, and whether the hierarchical structure remains stable as the LLM ecosystem evolves rapidly.
