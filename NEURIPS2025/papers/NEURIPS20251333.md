## Problem

Existing zero-shot coordination (ZSC) methods for cooperative AI optimize only for environment rewards, producing behaviors misaligned with individual user preferences, while offline preference learning methods require either large datasets or online human interaction. This paper tackles the underexplored problem of jointly achieving zero-shot generalization and preference alignment from only a small set of offline 'liked' trajectories, with no online access to the target human partner.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251333/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes grid-based Overcooked environment observations, a small set of positive-only offline trajectories (12–38 per preference style, split 4:1 train/test), and a policy pool of 18 diverse agents sampled from self-play checkpoints. It produces a single cooperative agent policy (CNN-MLP) that outputs discrete actions (up, down, left, right, interact, no-op) at each timestep, evaluated on both environment return (delivered soups) and a preference score computed from event-frequency weighted sums.

## Method

The paper proposes a three-stage training framework inspired by the NLP pretraining–SFT paradigm: (1) pretraining the agent with MAPPO against a diverse policy pool to build a generalization prior; (2) offline behavior cloning on the small 'liked' trajectory set followed by online generalization recovery to restore task performance; and (3) Epoch-wise Alternation Recovery (EAR), which iteratively interleaves one epoch of lightweight BC with a recovery phase until preference accuracy matches the fully-converged BC level. The key empirical insight driving EAR is that after each recovery phase the agent retains partial preference alignment rather than fully forgetting it, so shallow incremental BC steps accumulate preference knowledge without the catastrophic generalization loss seen in a single long BC phase. The entire pipeline uses a CNN-MLP architecture with MAPPO for online stages and standard cross-entropy BC for offline stages, requiring no reward model or preference pairs.

## Results

On the Coordination Ring layout, EAR achieves Preference Scores of 274.7 (Style A), 200.3 (Style B), and 259.0 (Style C), substantially outperforming the next-best baseline Pretrain+BC+Recover (224.3, 187.7, 252.0) and the ZSC-only Pretrain (111.3, 129.3, 188.7). Environment Returns are 88.6, 103.6, and 110.3 respectively, matching or exceeding Pretrain+BC+Recover while BC-only collapses to 13.7, 31.9, and 4.6. On generalization across all unseen proxy agents (Table 2), EAR maintains average returns of 86.2, 77.1, and 73.9—comparable to the style-agnostic Pretrain baseline (72.7)—whereas Pretrain+BC drops to 14.6–31.3 and BC to 3.0–12.1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EAR outperforms all four baselines on both metrics across all three preference styles. Against FCP/Pretrain (Strouse et al., 2021), EAR more than doubles the Preference Score (e.g., 274.7 vs. 111.3 on Style A) while also improving Environment Return (88.6 vs. 47.6), showing that ZSC pretraining alone cannot capture user-specific stylistic preferences. Against BC-only (Torabi et al., 2018), EAR achieves 2–25× higher Environment Return (e.g., 88.6 vs. 13.7 on Style A), confirming that pure imitation on a handful of trajectories severely overfits and destroys task competence. Against the diversity-oriented ZSC methods MEP (Zhao et al., 2023) and TrajDi (Lupu et al., 2021), EAR achieves substantially higher Preference Scores (274.7 vs. 118.1/107.2 on Style A) and Environment Returns (88.6 vs. 35.6/31.1), demonstrating that enlarging policy-pool diversity without explicit preference data cannot substitute for offline preference learning. The closest competitor, Pretrain+BC+Recover, is surpassed by EAR on Preference Score across all styles (by 22–50 points) while matching or slightly exceeding it on Environment Return, validating the benefit of the epoch-wise alternation strategy over a single recovery pass.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the FCP policy-pool paradigm for zero-shot coordination (Strouse et al., 2021) and the biased-human-preference modeling of HSP (Yu et al., 2023), using MAPPO as the underlying RL algorithm. Its key conceptual departure is drawing an explicit analogy to NLP's pretraining-then-SFT pipeline, but showing that this transfer is non-trivial for smaller cooperative RL agents: offline behavior cloning on a handful of 'liked' trajectories causes severe generalization degradation ('unlearning') that does not occur in large language models. The proposed Epoch-wise Alternation Recovery (EAR) strategy is a new training procedure that interleaves 1-epoch BC with online recovery, addressing the objective conflict between preference alignment and generalization that prior ZSC and offline preference methods do not explicitly handle.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap in game AI: real multiplayer games (League of Legends, Honor of Kings) already collect post-match 'like' feedback, yet no existing method leverages this sparse positive-only signal to shape agent behavior while preserving generalization. The finding that even a single epoch of BC degrades generalization by over one-third, yet recovery partially retains learned preferences, provides a useful empirical characterization of the generalization-preference trade-off in small cooperative agents. Key limitations include evaluation only on Overcooked with biased synthetic proxies rather than real humans, very small offline datasets (12–38 trajectories per style), and a single environment family; extending to complex multi-agent games and exploiting abundant unlabeled human trajectories remain open.
