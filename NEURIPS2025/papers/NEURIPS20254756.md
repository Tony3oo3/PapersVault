## Problem

Existing neural free energy estimation methods rely on equilibrium approaches (targeted FEP, neural thermodynamic integration) that require exact samples at every intermediate distribution or costly divergence/Jacobian evaluations. Non-equilibrium frameworks such as the escorted Jarzynski equality and Crooks fluctuation theorem, which only require samples from the two endpoints, remain largely unexplored in the deep learning setting, leaving a gap in principled, scalable free energy estimation.

## Input / Output

The method takes the energy functions U_a and U_b of two states along with equilibrium samples from both distributions (e.g., molecular coordinates or field configurations). It produces a single free energy difference estimate ΔF = F_b − F_a, computed via a minimum-variance non-equilibrium BAR estimator that combines forward and backward SDE trajectory likelihoods. Notably, the learned energy interpolant need not exactly match the true endpoint energies at t=0 and t=1; a correction term in the generalized work accounts for this boundary mismatch, and the FB RND formulation avoids any divergence or Jacobian determinant computation.

## Method

FEAT learns a stochastic interpolant transport between two endpoint distributions by jointly training a vector field v_t^ψ (via a flow-matching loss) and a score network ∇U_t^θ (via denoising score matching augmented with target score matching at the boundaries). The learned transport defines forward and backward SDEs that approximately map samples between states without requiring exact intermediate equilibrium samples. Free energy is then estimated using the escorted Jarzynski equality with a corrected generalized work, and the forward-backward Radon-Nikodym derivative (FB RND) from the controlled Crooks theorem provides a consistent estimator that is robust to Euler-Maruyama discretization error. A minimum-variance non-equilibrium BAR estimator (Proposition 3.2) combines forward and backward trajectory weights via the Fermi function, iteratively solving for the optimal constant C. In the deterministic limit (σ_t=0), the framework reduces to targeted FEP with continuous normalizing flows; with optimal learning it recovers thermodynamic integration, unifying equilibrium and non-equilibrium methods under one framework.

## Results

FEAT consistently outperforms TargetFEP(FM) across all tested systems. On GMM (16→40 modes, d=100), FEAT estimates ΔF = −5.34 ± 1.52 versus TargetFEP's −17.96 ± 1.49 (reference: 0), a substantial improvement. On larger Lennard-Jones systems (d=79×3 and d=128×3), TargetFEP is infeasible due to costly divergence evaluations, while FEAT achieves 356.74 ± 0.79 and 595.04 ± 6.52 (references: 357.43 and 595.98). On alanine dipeptide in implicit solvent (ALDP-S), FEAT gives 29.38 ± 0.04 versus TargetFEP's 29.47 ± 0.22 (reference: 29.43).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against TargetFEP with flow matching [Zhao and Wang, 2023], FEAT wins on every system tested. The gap is most dramatic on GMM d=100 (FEAT: −5.34 vs. TargetFEP: −17.96, reference 0) and on LJ-79/LJ-128 where TargetFEP is entirely infeasible due to divergence computation costs. On ALDP-S, FEAT (29.38 ± 0.04) is closer to the MBAR reference (29.43) than TargetFEP (29.47 ± 0.22), and on ALDP-T FEAT (−4.56 ± 0.08) is closer to the reference (−4.25) than TargetFEP (−4.78 ± 0.32). Against Neural TI [Máté et al., 2024a,b], the comparison is less direct: Neural TI without problem-specific preconditioning fails catastrophically (GMM-40: −181.6 vs. reference 0; LJ-79: 468.8 ± 391.2 vs. reference 357.43), while with preconditioning it becomes competitive (GMM-40: 0.1 ± 0.2; LJ-79: 356.9 ± 1.8) but at the cost of requiring hand-designed energy parameterization that limits generality. FEAT's advantage over both baselines stems from its non-equilibrium formulation, which is robust to discretization and learning errors without requiring exact intermediate-state samples or divergence evaluations.

## Relation to Prior Work

FEAT unifies several previously separate free energy estimation paradigms—FEP, targeted FEP with flow matching, BAR, and thermodynamic integration—under a single non-equilibrium transport framework built on stochastic interpolants (Albergo et al., 2023) and the escorted Jarzynski equality (Vaikuntanathan and Jarzynski, 2008). It departs from prior neural approaches (Target FEP, Neural TI) by leveraging the forward-backward Radon-Nikodym derivative (FB RND) formulation of the Crooks fluctuation theorem, which eliminates costly divergence and time-derivative calculations while providing robustness to discretization error. The minimum-variance estimator extends Bennett's acceptance ratio (Bennett, 1976) to path-space importance sampling, and the one-sided variant connects to diffusion models (Song et al., 2021) for absolute free energy estimation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Free energy estimation is a bottleneck in drug discovery, materials science, and ML (normalization constants), and this paper demonstrates that non-equilibrium transport—long under-explored in the deep learning literature—can outperform equilibrium methods like targeted FEP, particularly in high dimensions where Jacobian-based approaches break down. A key open limitation is that path-space importance sampling introduces higher variance than state-space methods for large systems, placing FEAT on a bias-variance tradeoff spectrum with targeted FEP. Future directions include relaxing the requirement for samples from both endpoints, improving scalability to larger molecular systems, and investigating transferable networks for generalization across related states.
