## Problem

Large reasoning models (e.g., DeepSeek-R1, OpenAI o1) achieve state-of-the-art performance on challenging benchmarks, yet the internal mechanisms behind their success remain poorly understood, especially in contrast to base models. Prior work on reasoning graphs predates the RL-trained reasoning model era and has not been applied to these new architectures. This paper asks what distinctive graph-theoretic structural properties emerge in the hidden-state trajectories of reasoning models that explain their superior performance.

## Input / Output

The method consumes per-token hidden states from a chosen Transformer layer (default 90% depth, e.g., layer 58 of 64) for each reasoning step, where steps are delimited by newline characters in the generated chain-of-thought. Each step's hidden states are mean-pooled into a single segment vector, and all segment vectors across the evaluation set are clustered via K-means (K=200) to define a shared node vocabulary. The output is a directed reasoning graph per sample (nodes = cluster centroids, edges = sequential transitions between assigned nodes) along with scalar graph metrics: cycle detection ratio, per-sample cycle count, graph diameter (via Dijkstra), clustering coefficient, average path length, and the Humphries–Gurney small-world index.

## Method

The core pipeline extracts a reasoning graph by (1) segmenting the model's chain-of-thought into newline-delimited steps, (2) computing the mean hidden-state vector per segment at a fixed layer, (3) running K-means (K=200) over all segment vectors in the dataset to obtain K cluster centroids that serve as graph nodes, (4) assigning each segment to its nearest centroid, and (5) connecting consecutive assigned nodes with directed edges to form a per-sample graph. Three graph-theoretic properties are then measured: cyclicity (repeated node visits excluding self-loops and adjacent duplicates), diameter (maximum shortest-path distance via Dijkstra from every node), and small-world index (ratio of normalized clustering coefficient to normalized average path length, using Erdős–Rényi random-graph baselines). The analysis is conducted across model sizes (1.5B–32B Qwen2.5 distilled from DeepSeek-R1), task difficulties (GSM8K → MATH500 → AIME 2024), and layer depths (10%–90%), revealing that reasoning models exhibit ~5 cycles per sample, substantially larger diameters, and ~6× higher small-world indices than base models. A supervised fine-tuning experiment with the s1 dataset (v1.0 vs. v1.1) further demonstrates that higher-quality SFT data systematically expands graph diameters in tandem with accuracy gains, linking graph structure to practical data-construction guidelines.

## Results

On AIME 2024, the 32B distilled reasoning model (DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-32B) achieves the highest accuracy and exhibits the largest reasoning graph diameter and cycle count among all model sizes tested (1.5B–32B), while the 14B model reaches a 100% cycle detection ratio. Compared to the base Qwen2.5-32B, the reasoning model shows approximately 5 cycles per sample (vs. near-zero for the base model) and a ~6× higher small-world index. For SFT, the improved s1-v1.1 dataset yields 94.4% accuracy on MATH500 and 56.7% on AIME 2024 (vs. 92.6% and 50.0% for s1-v1.0), with consistently larger reasoning graph diameters across all hidden layers at both 200 and 400 training steps.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is between large reasoning models (DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen series) and their corresponding base models (Qwen2.5, Llama-3.1) on three graph-theoretic metrics. The reasoning model consistently outperforms the base model on all three: cycle detection ratio is substantially higher at every layer depth and task difficulty, graph diameter is larger across all layers (indicating broader exploration of reasoning states), and the small-world index is approximately 6× higher. These advantages grow with task difficulty (GSM8K → MATH500 → AIME 2024) and model capacity. In the SFT comparison, s1-v1.1 dominates s1-v1.0 on both accuracy (+1.8 pts MATH500, +6.7 pts AIME 2024) and graph diameter, while s1 data also produces larger diameters and more cycles than LIMO data when used to prompt the base model, suggesting richer latent reasoning structure in higher-quality SFT data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the reasoning graph framework introduced by Wang et al. [63] (K-means clustering of hidden states per reasoning step) and the small-world index formalism of Humphries and Gurney [26] to the new generation of RL-trained and distilled reasoning models (DeepSeek-R1, o1-family), which prior graph-theoretic analyses had not examined. Earlier work on reasoning graphs (Prystawski et al. [48], toy-task studies [13, 67, 6, 59]) predates the RL-based reasoning breakthroughs and focused on standard chain-of-thought models. The paper also connects its cycle-detection findings to the 'aha moment' literature [10, 68] and to SFT data construction methods (s1 [44], LIMO [72]), positioning graph properties as a quantitative bridge between internal representations and practical training-data design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first systematic graph-theoretic characterization of RL-trained reasoning models, showing that cycles (~5/sample), larger diameters, and ~6× small-world index scale with both task difficulty and model capacity, and that these properties correlate positively with accuracy. The practical finding that higher-quality SFT data (s1-v1.1) produces measurably larger reasoning graph diameters offers a concrete, quantifiable criterion for dataset design that goes beyond qualitative difficulty/quality labels. Key limitations include the absence of feature-level or circuit-level mechanistic analysis, the restriction to context-direction hidden-state transitions, and the lack of training-dynamics analysis showing how these graph properties emerge during RL or SFT. Open questions include whether graph-theoretic metrics can be used as direct training objectives and whether the findings generalize beyond the Qwen/Llama distillation family.
