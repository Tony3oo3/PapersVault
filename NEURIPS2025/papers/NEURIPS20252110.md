## Problem

Aligning LLMs with human preferences typically requires large, meticulously curated preference datasets that are expensive to collect and inherently noisy. Existing self-annotation approaches that reduce human labeling effort ignore that synthetic preference labels can be systematically biased, causing errors to compound across iterative training rounds. The paper asks how to achieve provably robust alignment with minimal human labels while explicitly defending against worst-case annotation noise or distribution shift.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252110/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a small set of human-labeled seed preference pairs (2K from UltraFeedback, ~3.3% of the full 60K dataset), a large pool of unlabeled prompts, and a base LLM (Mistral-7B or LLaMA-3-8B fine-tuned on UltraChat). It produces a fully aligned LLM policy after three iterative self-annotation rounds. A distinctive aspect is that the human-labeled input is deliberately tiny, and the method's robustness guarantee (O(ε)-bounded regret) is specifically designed to tolerate the noise inherent in the self-generated preference pairs that make up the bulk of training data.

## Method

SSAPO frames preference alignment as a two-player Stackelberg game: the leader (policy) maximizes worst-case preference likelihood while the follower (adversary) selects the most damaging distribution within an ε-Wasserstein ball around the empirical gap distribution in scalar margin space. The key theoretical result is that the resulting policy achieves O(ε)-bounded regret under any gap-space shift of magnitude ε, in contrast to DPO whose regret grows linearly with distribution mismatch. Practically, the follower's inner problem is solved via a finite convex program built from a piecewise-linear convex under-approximation of −log σ(ξ) (with K tangent pieces), yielding a discrete worst-case distribution whose mixture weights reweight each training pair's DPO-style loss. The algorithm iterates: self-annotate new prompts by generating and ranking responses under the current policy, compute the empirical gap center, solve the follower's convex program (optionally in parallel groups for scalability), and update the policy via weighted SGD. This loop is repeated for T iterations, with the Wasserstein radius ε controlling the trade-off between robustness and data fidelity.

## Results

Using only 2K seed preference pairs (3.3% of UltraFeedback's 60K), SSAPO achieves 24.44% length-controlled win rate and 35.82% raw win rate vs. GPT-4 on AlpacaEval 2.0 with Mistral-7B, and 33.33% / 40.12% with LLaMA-3-8B. On MT-Bench, LLaMA-3-8B-SSAPO scores 8.03/10, surpassing the SPA baseline (7.86). The method outperforms standard DPO by roughly 2.7× on AlpacaEval LC win rate (24.44% vs. 9.03% for Mistral-7B) while using the same 2K seed data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SSAPO decisively outperforms DPO on AlpacaEval 2.0 for both model families: Mistral-7B-SSAPO reaches 24.44% LC win rate vs. 9.03% for Mistral-7B-DPO (2.7×), and LLaMA3-8B-SSAPO reaches 33.33% vs. 20.61% for LLaMA3-8B-DPO. Against the iterative self-annotation baselines, SSAPO also wins: it surpasses IterDPO (PairRM) by 12.6 points on Mistral-7B LC win rate (24.44% vs. 11.87%) and SPA by 9.0 points (24.44% vs. 15.39%). On the larger LLaMA-3-8B, SSAPO leads SPA by 11.5 points on LC win rate (33.33% vs. 21.85%) and 15.2 points on raw win rate vs. GPT-4 (40.12% vs. 24.95%). On MT-Bench, the advantage is narrower: Mistral-7B-SSAPO (6.68) trails IterDPO-PairRM (6.98) by 0.3, while LLaMA3-8B-SSAPO (8.03) edges out LLaMA3-8B-SPA (7.86). In Table 2, Mistral-7B-SSAPO (24.44% LC) also surpasses Zephyr-7B-β trained on 100% human labels (11.75% LC), demonstrating that the Stackelberg robust reweighting compensates for using only 3.3% of the available annotations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023) as the base preference-optimization objective and on the self-annotation paradigm of SPA (Kim et al., ICLR 2025), which it replicates experimentally with the same 2K-seed UltraFeedback setup. Its key conceptual departure is recasting alignment as a two-player Stackelberg game between a policy (leader) and a worst-case preference distribution (follower) within an ε-Wasserstein ball, drawing on the distributionally robust optimization machinery of Mohajerin Esfahani & Kuhn (2018). This contrasts with concurrent game-theoretic alignment work (DNO, NASH, SPIN) that frames the game as policy-vs-policy; here the follower is a distributional adversary over preference gaps, yielding a distinct robust-control perspective with O(ε)-bounded regret guarantees that DPO lacks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The O(ε) regret bound versus DPO's linear-in-δ regret provides a clean theoretical justification for why robust reweighting helps under noisy self-annotation, and the empirical demonstration that 2K seed labels (~1/30 of standard) suffice for competitive AlpacaEval performance is practically significant as high-quality human data becomes scarce. However, the MT-Bench scores for Mistral-7B-SSAPO (6.68) are actually lower than DPO (6.81) and SPA (6.94), suggesting the robustness gains may not transfer uniformly across task types. Open questions include whether the DRO solver scales beyond 7B/8B models, whether the Stackelberg framing is strictly necessary over simpler robustness regularizers, and how the method behaves under adversarial (rather than merely noisy) self-annotation.
