## Problem

Existing Text-3D Scene Retrieval (T3SR) methods assume user queries are information-complete, yet real-world queries are often incomplete, ambiguous, or exhibit domain shift, causing persistent performance degradation. Prior interactive retrieval frameworks designed for image-text or video-text matching fail to generalize to 3D scenes because they lack holistic scene-level perspectives and cannot handle the domain gap between training queries and interactive response texts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253273/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an initial text query and a gallery of 3D point cloud scenes (encoded via DGCNN and BERT respectively) and iteratively consumes LLM-generated question-answer pairs over up to r rounds, where the LLM (Qwen-7B-Instruct) acts as both questioner and answerer using either coarse-grained (LLM-expanded) or fine-grained (SceneDepict-3D2T) scene descriptions as memory. The output is a progressively refined retrieval ranking over the scene gallery, combining initial, feature-level fused, and semantic-level reconstructed predictions into a single weighted score vector.

## Method

IDEal comprises two components. The Interactive Retrieval Refinement (IRR) framework coordinates a questioner that computes a Density-Compensated Cross-modal Affinity Entropy to adaptively choose between detail-probing (Q1) and divergent-exploration (Q2) questions, an LLM answerer that recalls scene details, and a retriever that fuses responses at the feature level (weighted linear combination for Q1, minimum enclosing hypersphere aggregation for Q2) and at the semantic level (CoT-based LLM scene reconstruction), combining all three prediction channels via learned weights. The Interaction Adaptation Tuning (IAT) strategy generates augmented texts approximating the interaction domain via simulated IRR dialogues, then trains the retriever with a contrastive domain adaptation loss that jointly minimizes discriminability risk (using 3D scene features as stable positive anchors) and diversity risk (via a weighted complementary contrastive loss that down-weights high-similarity false negatives to reduce variance in the negative sample distribution).

## Results

With fine-grained memory descriptions, IDEal achieves R@1=37.8, R@5=71.8, R@10=86.4 (Rsum=196.0) on ScanRefer, R@1=26.4, R@5=62.7, R@10=78.7 (Rsum=167.8) on Nr3D, and R@1=20.2, R@5=43.1, R@10=63.1 (Rsum=126.4) on Sr3D, outperforming the strongest interactive baseline (BASELINE SUM†) by 3.4/2.3/1.3 points on ScanRefer and 3.9/7.5/11.2 points on Nr3D. With only coarse-grained descriptions, IDEal still achieves R@1=16.0 on ScanRefer, surpassing the best offline method RoMa (R@1=11.4) by 4.6 points. The method also demonstrates plug-and-play compatibility: integrating IDEal into RoMa with fine-grained memory lifts ScanRefer R@1 from 16.7 to 37.8 (+21.1 points).

## Baselines & Comparisons

IDEal outperforms all five offline cross-modal baselines by substantial margins under both memory settings. With coarse-grained descriptions, IDEal (R@1=16.0 on ScanRefer) surpasses the strongest offline method RoMa (R@1=11.4) by 4.6 points and VSE∞ (R@1=9.7) by 6.3 points; on Nr3D the gap widens to 5.2 points over RoMa (11.7 vs. 6.5). Among interactive methods with fine-grained memory, IDEal (R@1=37.8 on ScanRefer) beats MERLIN (31.1) by 6.7 points, ChatIR (21.8) by 16.0 points, and Rewrite (17.4) by 20.4 points. On Nr3D, IDEal leads MERLIN by 4.6 points (26.4 vs. 21.8) and Rewrite by 9.3 points (26.4 vs. 17.1). On Sr3D, IDEal achieves R@1=20.2, exceeding MERLIN (14.2) by 6.0 points and ChatIR (12.1) by 8.1 points. The two additional strong interactive baselines (IR and SUM) are also surpassed: IDEal gains 3.4 R@1 over SUM and 7.9 over IR on ScanRefer, and 3.9/8.4 R@1 over SUM/IR on Nr3D.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends interactive cross-modal retrieval (ChatIR, MERLIN, PlugIR) from 2D image/video domains to 3D point-cloud scene retrieval, addressing the specific challenge that 3D scenes require holistic spatial understanding rather than the local object focus that LLMs naturally gravitate toward. For feature-level fusion, it adapts the minimum enclosing hypersphere formulation from Support Vector Data Description (Tax & Duin, 2004) to aggregate multi-round response features. For the domain adaptation component (IAT), it builds on the contrastive domain adaptation paradigm of Yang et al. (2022) and Xu et al. (ICLR 2025), introducing a weighted complementary contrastive loss to handle false-negative variance in mini-batch sampling. The overall framework composes these elements with an adaptive question router (using density-compensated affinity entropy) that has no direct precedent in prior interactive retrieval work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first interactive retrieval framework purpose-built for 3D scenes, addressing a practical gap where single-shot queries are often incomplete or ambiguous. The finding that domain adaptation (IAT) is the single most critical component—its removal causes a 66.6-point Rsum drop—reveals that the distributional gap between training queries and LLM-generated interactive texts is far larger than previously assumed in interactive retrieval literature. However, absolute performance remains modest (R@1 ≈ 37.8 on ScanRefer), and the method's reliance on LLMs introduces hallucination risk that saturates or degrades performance after ~3–5 rounds. Open questions include whether more stable or scene-aware LLMs could reduce interaction rounds, and whether the IAT strategy generalizes to other cross-modal settings beyond 3D scenes.
