## Problem

Existing contextual dynamic pricing algorithms assume continuous price spaces, yet real-world prices are typically restricted to discrete sets such as predefined price points or promotional tiers. This paper shows that explicitly exploiting the discrete support of prices within a semi-parametric model can substantially reduce cumulative regret, with the magnitude of improvement governed by the sparsity of the price grid relative to the time horizon.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251398/cropped_page8_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each time step the algorithm receives a d-dimensional covariate vector X_t and all historical (X, P, Y) observations from prior epochs, and outputs a price P_t drawn from a fixed equally-spaced grid G = {g_1, …, g_K} with resolution δ = κT^{−γ}. The pricing policy is a two-component mixture: with probability 1−η_l it selects the myopic revenue-maximizing price under the current posterior estimate, and with probability η_l it samples uniformly from the grid, where η_l is an epoch-specific exploration parameter that shrinks geometrically and adapts to grid density.

## Method

BayesCoxCP models the survival function as S(v|X_t) = S_0(v)^{exp(X_t^⊤β)} under the Cox proportional hazards framework, treating each (P_t, Y_t) pair as case 1 interval-censored data. It places independent gamma priors on the baseline hazard—parameterized as a left-continuous step function with jumps at grid points—and a continuous prior on β, then computes the posterior via variational Bayesian inference. The algorithm employs an epoch-based design with geometrically increasing epoch lengths, and the exploration parameter η_l is calibrated to maintain a uniformity condition on the induced price distribution while balancing exploration and exploitation. The central theoretical result is that the posterior convergence rate, and hence the cumulative regret, adapts to the grid sparsity γ: the algorithm achieves Õ(T^{(1+γ)/2} + √(dT)) for sparse grids (γ < 1/3) and Õ(T^{2/3} + √(dT)) for dense grids (γ ≥ 1/3), matching a minimax lower bound up to logarithmic factors without requiring prior knowledge of γ.

## Results

BayesCoxCP achieves a regret upper bound of Õ(T^((1+γ)/2) + √(dT)) for sparse grids (γ < 1/3) and Õ(T^(2/3) + √(dT)) for dense grids (γ ≥ 1/3), where γ is the grid sparsity relative to the time horizon T. These bounds are shown to be minimax-optimal in T up to logarithmic factors via a matching non-contextual lower bound (Theorem 5.3). In numerical experiments with T = 30,000 and d = 5, BayesCoxCP consistently achieves lower cumulative regret than the state-of-the-art method of Choi et al. [7] across all four grid sizes (K ∈ {10, 100, 1000, 30000}), with the performance gap being most pronounced for the sparsest grid (K = 10).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is the semi-parametric contextual pricing algorithm of Choi et al. [7] (ICML 2023), which also uses the Cox proportional hazards model but assumes continuous price spaces. Theoretically, [7] achieves Õ(T^(2/3)d), while BayesCoxCP achieves Õ(T^((1+γ)/2) + √(dT)) for γ < 1/3 — a strictly faster rate in T when the grid is sparse (e.g., for γ = 0, the rate becomes Õ(√T + √(dT)) versus Õ(T^(2/3)d)). For γ ≥ 1/3, both methods achieve comparable T^(2/3) scaling, though BayesCoxCP has a milder dependence on d (√(dT) vs. T^(2/3)d). In the numerical experiments (Figure 1), BayesCoxCP outperforms [7] across all four grid sizes, with the cumulative regret gap being largest at K = 10 (sparsest grid) and gradually diminishing as K increases to 30,000, confirming the theoretical prediction that the advantage of exploiting discrete support is most significant when the grid is sparse.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate research threads: the adaptive Bayesian inference for current status data on a grid (Chae, 2023), which showed that discrete monitoring times yield faster nonparametric estimation rates, and the semi-parametric contextual dynamic pricing literature using the Cox proportional hazards model (Choi et al., ICML 2023), which assumed continuous price spaces. The key departure is bringing the grid-sparsity exploitation from the survival analysis literature into the contextual pricing setting, where prior work (including [7]) treated the discrete nature of real-world prices as an irrelevant detail. The Bayesian framework with gamma priors on the baseline hazard also differs from the frequentist NPMLE approach in [7], enabling a simpler adaptive procedure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first regret bounds for contextual dynamic pricing that adapt to the sparsity of the discrete price grid, showing that sparse grids (γ < 1/3) yield a strictly faster rate of T^((1+γ)/2) compared to the T^(2/3) rate for dense or continuous settings. This has direct practical relevance since retailers commonly restrict prices to a small set of values. Key limitations include the regret lower bound being established only for the non-contextual case, and the dimension d dependency remaining unaddressed, leaving open whether the T^((1+γ)/2) rate is optimal in d. The epoch-based design with carefully tuned exploration parameter η_l is a non-trivial algorithmic contribution that ensures the posterior convergence assumptions are met in the sequential setting.
