## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting relies on center-based depth sorting and an affine (Jacobian) projection to render anisotropic 3D Gaussians, both of which introduce errors: overlapping primitives can have per-pixel depth orders that contradict the global sort (causing popping artifacts), and the affine approximation distorts the projected shape away from the Gaussian center. No existing method achieves exact per-pixel ordering while retaining the fast tile-based rasterization pipeline.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251636/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a set of calibrated multi-view RGB images (downsampled to 1600px for Mip-NeRF360) with known camera intrinsics and extrinsics, and produces a 3D scene representation consisting of anchor points whose view-specific 2D primitives (rotation angle and anisotropic scale) are decoded on-the-fly by a lightweight FiLM network. At render time, a single global depth sort of these parallel 2D primitives yields pixel-accurate alpha-blended novel views at real-time frame rates (~75–80 FPS on an L40S GPU).

## Method

SAP constrains all rendering primitives to be parallel to the image plane for a given viewpoint, so a single global z-sort guarantees exact per-pixel depth ordering without overlap. A 3D-consistent decoder (FiLM-modulated MLP conditioned on spherical-harmonics-encoded viewing direction) generates per-view 2D rotation and scale parameters from anchor features, replacing the 3D covariance of standard 3DGS. Because the primitives have zero depth extent, projection reduces to exact geometric scaling (P = diag(fx/t, fy/t)), eliminating the Jacobian approximation error and making view-space and screen-space Mahalanobis distances provably equivalent—enabling arbitrary differentiable kernels (e.g., generalized Gaussian with learnable exponent p) without projection bias. A max-positional-gradient densification criterion (combined with the standard average-gradient threshold) fills under-reconstructed regions that the original averaging strategy misses under large viewpoint variation.

## Results

SAP achieves the best PSNR and SSIM on both Tanks&Temples (25.04 / 0.870) and Mip-NeRF360 (28.05 / 0.835), outperforming the strongest baseline DisC-GS (24.96 / 0.866 and 28.01 / 0.833 respectively). On Deep Blending, SAP attains 30.02 PSNR and 0.910 SSIM, slightly trailing DisC-GS (30.42 PSNR) but leading on SSIM. Across all three datasets, SAP consistently surpasses 3DGS, Scaffold-GS, 2DGS, StopThePop, and 3DGS-MCMC on PSNR and SSIM, while DisC-GS retains an advantage on LPIPS (e.g., 0.120 vs. 0.145 on Tanks&Temples).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAP outperforms 3DGS by +1.33 dB PSNR on Tanks&Temples and +0.62 dB on Mip-NeRF360, confirming that screen-aligned parallel primitives resolve the popping artifacts inherent to center-based sorting. Against Scaffold-GS (the anchor-based framework SAP builds upon), SAP gains +0.85 dB PSNR on Tanks&Temples and +0.50 dB on Mip-NeRF360, with the improvement attributed to the FiLM direction-modulated decoder and the max-gradient densification strategy. The closest competitor is DisC-GS, which leads SAP on LPIPS across all three datasets (e.g., 0.120 vs. 0.145 on Tanks&Temples) and on PSNR for Deep Blending (30.42 vs. 30.02), but SAP wins on PSNR and SSIM for Tanks&Temples and Mip-NeRF360. 2DGS, despite its geometrically accurate 2D representation, underperforms SAP by 2+ dB PSNR on Tanks&Temples, indicating that the parallel-alignment constraint and exact projection in SAP yield substantially better rendering quality than unconstrained 2D Gaussian planes. StopThePop and 3DGS-MCMC trail SAP by 1.8+ dB PSNR on Tanks&Temples, with 3DGS-MCMC showing particularly weak LPIPS on Deep Blending (0.320 vs. 0.236).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the anchor-based Gaussian splatting lineage (Scaffold-GS, Octree-GS) by replacing 3D anisotropic Gaussians with 2D primitives constrained to be parallel to the image plane, leveraging a FiLM-based decoder with spherical harmonics encoding to generate view-specific shape parameters. It departs from prior popping-artifact mitigations (StopThePop's 1D depth estimation, EVER's ray tracing) by showing that parallelism alone makes a single global z-sort equivalent to exact per-pixel ordering, eliminating the need for expensive per-pixel intersection tests. The unbiased projection property (view-space and screen-space Mahalanobis distances being identical under geometric scaling) is a clean theoretical departure from 3DGS's Jacobian-based affine approximation, enabling arbitrary kernel functions without projection distortion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The parallel-primitive constraint offers a principled, zero-overhead solution to the long-standing popping artifact problem in splatting-based rendering, potentially influencing how future 3DGS variants handle depth ordering. The unbiased projection property opens the door to richer kernel representations (asymmetric, non-radial) that were previously infeasible due to projection errors. However, the method currently lacks surface normals (limiting mesh extraction), does not model lighting or physical reflection, and has only explored radial basis function kernels; extending to dynamic scenes, relightable environments, and non-radial kernels remains open.
