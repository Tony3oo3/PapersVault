## Problem

Determining optimal data mixing ratios for large-scale LLM pretraining across multiple domains or languages remains largely heuristic, with static weights fixed at initialization becoming suboptimal as training progresses. Existing multi-task optimization methods that could in principle adapt these weights are designed to resolve gradient conflicts—a phenomenon the authors show is rare in LLM pretraining—and incur O(Kd) memory costs that are infeasible for billion-parameter models. The paper asks whether one can design an adaptive mixing strategy that exploits the naturally non-conflicting gradient structure of LLMs while scaling to large models with negligible overhead.

## Input / Output

PiKE consumes multi-domain or multilingual text corpora (mC4 with English/Hindi/German, or GLaM with six English domains) and, every T₀=1000 steps, estimates only two scalars per task: the squared per-task gradient norm and the per-task gradient variance. It outputs a trained GPT-2-style decoder-only language model (110M–1B parameters) along with continuously updated sampling weights (b₁,…,b_K) that determine how many samples from each domain enter each batch. Unlike prior MTL methods that store K full gradient vectors (O(Kd) memory), PiKE requires O(1) additional memory per task, making it practical at scale.

## Method

PiKE (Positive gradient Interaction-based K-task weights Estimator) builds on the Mix batching strategy—sampling from all K tasks in every batch—and derives a near-tight upper bound on the expected per-step loss decrease under low gradient conflict (formalized via c-conflicted and c-aligned conditions). Minimizing this bound yields a quadratic program over sampling weights whose solution depends only on per-task gradient norms and variances; in practice, PiKE updates weights via a single mirror-descent step every T₀ iterations to avoid abrupt shifts that would destabilize AdamW momentum. The algorithm enjoys O(1/δ²) convergence to a point where all task gradients are small, matching the optimal rate for smooth nonconvex stochastic optimization. Balanced-PiKE extends the base method by coupling it with tilted empirical risk minimization (α-fairness utility), using Fenchel duality to alternate between computing fairness weights y_k* from current task losses and applying PiKE's gradient-based mixing with those weights, thereby promoting balanced learning across tasks without sacrificing overall accuracy.

## Results

On the GLaM six-domain benchmark (750M GPT-2), PiKE with GLaM-tuned initialization achieves 48.1% average downstream accuracy across ArcE, CSQA, HellaSwag, and PIQA, outperforming DoReMi (46.5%), ADO (45.9%), GLaM static weights (45.3%), and FAMO (45.0%). On the multilingual mC4 benchmark (1B GPT-2, en/hi/de), Balanced-PiKE (τ=3) reaches 28.9% average HellaSwag accuracy versus 27.5% for uniform Mix, while PiKE alone achieves 28.7%. The paper reports that PiKE reaches baseline accuracy 1.9× faster in the multilingual setting and scales efficiently from 110M to 1B parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PiKE consistently outperforms all four primary baselines on the GLaM benchmark. Against DoReMi [69], which uses group DRO with a proxy model to set static domain weights, PiKE (GLaM) gains 3.4% in average accuracy (48.1% vs. 46.5%) while also improving on ArcE (+1.2%) and CSQA (+3.4%). Against ADO [30], which adaptively re-weights data using scaling laws, PiKE leads by 4.8% in average accuracy (48.1% vs. 45.9%), with the largest gap on CSQA (+4.8%). Against the original GLaM static weights [18], PiKE improves by 6.2% in average accuracy (48.1% vs. 45.3%), and notably surpasses GLaM even when initialized with uniform weights (47.6% vs. 45.3%). Against FAMO [38], a scalable MTL method using O(1) space, PiKE achieves a 7.1% average accuracy gain (48.1% vs. 45.0%), with particularly strong improvements on CSQA (+3.5%) and ArcE (+6.1%). On the multilingual mC4 benchmark, PiKE and Balanced-PiKE similarly outperform FAMO (28.7–28.9% vs. 27.2%) and ADO (28.7–28.9% vs. 27.5%) in average HellaSwag accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

PiKE sits at the intersection of multi-task optimization and adaptive data mixing for LLM pretraining. It departs from the dominant MTL paradigm (PCGrad, MGDA, NashMTL, AdaTask) that focuses on resolving gradient conflicts, instead exploiting the empirically observed non-conflicting gradient structure in large-scale language model pretraining. For data mixing specifically, it extends the adaptive online mixing line (ADO, Skill-it!) by incorporating both per-task gradient norms and variances into a principled mirror-descent update, and builds on tilted ERM [35] for its balanced-learning variant. The closest scalable competitor is FAMO [38], which also uses O(1) per-task state, but PiKE additionally accounts for gradient variance and provides convergence guarantees under low-conflict assumptions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PiKE offers a drop-in, low-overhead adaptive data mixing strategy for LLM pretraining that consistently improves downstream accuracy over static baselines (DoReMi, GLaM) and conflict-mitigation MTL methods across 110M–1B parameter scales. The empirical finding that large autoregressive models exhibit minimal gradient conflict (unlike vision MTL) is a useful reframing that could redirect future MTL research away from conflict-resolution toward exploiting cooperative gradient structures. Key limitations include the method's insensitivity to dataset size when assigning weights, the absence of error bars due to computational cost, and the fact that perplexity improvements are inconsistent even when downstream accuracy improves—leaving open whether the gains persist at 10B+ scale or in non-language domains.
