## Problem

Existing KV cache compression methods (H2O, SnapKV, PyramidKV) evaluate token importance in isolation, pruning individual tokens based on per-token attention scores. This neglects the dependency structure of natural language, causing critical semantic units (e.g., subject-predicate-object triples) to be fragmented or lost, which degrades performance on long-context and in-context learning tasks. The paper asks how to preserve complete semantic information in the KV cache while still achieving aggressive compression.

## Input / Output

The method consumes the full Key and Value matrices (K ∈ R^{T_k×d}, V ∈ R^{T_v×d}) along with the observe window portion of the Query matrix (Q_{T_q-w:T_q}, where w ∈ {4,8,16,32}) to compute chunk-level attention scores. It outputs a compressed KV cache of maximum length L_max, where tokens are retained or evicted in contiguous chunks of size c (default 10), and the original observe window tokens are concatenated back to the tail of the compressed cache to preserve recent context. The compression is applied per-layer during the prefilling stage, before decoding begins.

## Method

ChunkKV redefines the compression unit from individual tokens to contiguous semantic chunks: it computes attention scores over an observe window, sums scores within each chunk of size c to obtain a chunk-level importance score, and selects the top-k chunks (k = ⌊L_max/c⌋) to retain, preserving complete linguistic structures rather than scattered tokens. A key empirical finding is that ChunkKV's preserved indices exhibit substantially higher cross-layer Jaccard similarity (~57% vs. ~25–28% for H2O/SnapKV), motivating a training-free layer-wise index reuse technique that shares the same token indices across groups of N_reuse layers, reducing compression overhead by ~20% with only 0.5% performance degradation. The approach is training-free, model-agnostic, and operates entirely in the prefilling stage, making it compatible with Flash Attention 2 and standard inference pipelines.

## Results

On the NIAH benchmark (LLaMA-3-8B-Instruct, KV cache size 128, 8k context), ChunkKV achieves 73.8% retrieval accuracy, substantially outperforming SnapKV (58.9%), PyramidKV (65.1%), H2O (47.9%), and StreamingLLM (23.7%). On LongBench at 10% compression ratio, ChunkKV incurs only a −2.29% performance gap versus FullKV (41.46), compared to −3.16% for SnapKV and −10.61% for H2O. On GSM8K (LLaMA-3-8B-Instruct, 10% ratio), ChunkKV reaches 65.7% accuracy versus 50.3% for SnapKV and 45.0% for H2O (FullKV: 76.8%). With layer-wise index reuse, ChunkKV achieves up to 26.5% throughput improvement and 20.7% latency reduction over FullKV with only 0.5% performance degradation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ChunkKV consistently outperforms all four token-level eviction baselines across every benchmark. Against SnapKV (the strongest token-level competitor), ChunkKV gains 14.9 points on NIAH at KV cache 128 (73.8% vs. 58.9%), 15.4 points on GSM8K at 10% ratio with LLaMA-3-8B (65.7% vs. 50.3%), and 0.87 points on LongBench at 10% (−2.29% vs. −3.16% gap). Against H2O, the margin is even larger: 25.9 points on NIAH (73.8% vs. 47.9%) and 20.7 points on GSM8K (65.7% vs. 45.0%). PyramidKV is the closest competitor on NIAH (65.1% vs. 73.8%) but still trails by 8.7 points, matching the paper's headline claim. Against KIVI (a quantization-based method rather than eviction), ChunkKV uses a smaller cache (0.10 GB vs. 0.1563 GB at 2-bit) and achieves 27.3% faster total generation time (164.66 s vs. 226.52 s), demonstrating that size reduction via eviction is more effective than precision reduction for end-to-end inference speed.

## Relation to Prior Work

ChunkKV builds directly on the observe-window attention scoring mechanism introduced by H2O and SnapKV, but departs fundamentally from their token-level eviction paradigm by grouping tokens into fixed-size semantic chunks as the atomic compression unit. It also extends the layer-wise compression policy explored by PyramidInfer and PyramidKV with a novel index-reuse technique that exploits the empirically higher cross-layer similarity of chunk-preserved indices (57% Jaccard vs. ~25% for token-level methods). The paper positions itself against the full spectrum of prior KV cache compression methods—StreamingLLM's static retention, H2O/SnapKV's dynamic token eviction, and PyramidKV's depth-dependent allocation—arguing that all of them share the limitation of measuring importance at the individual-token level and thus fragmenting semantic structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

KV cache memory is the dominant bottleneck (up to 70% of GPU memory) for long-context LLM serving, and ChunkKV offers a simple, training-free compression strategy that preserves semantic integrity while achieving up to 8.7% accuracy gains over state-of-the-art token-level methods at the same compression ratio. The layer-wise index reuse technique adds a practical 26.5% throughput improvement with negligible (0.5%) accuracy loss, making the approach immediately deployable. Open questions include whether adaptive or task-aware chunk sizes (as hinted by the hybrid compression analysis in Section 4.6) could further close the gap on global-understanding tasks like summarization, and whether the fixed chunk size of 10 generalizes to multimodal or non-English settings beyond the tested benchmarks.
