## Problem

Pretrained Optimization Models (POMs) aim to solve unseen black-box optimization problems zero-shot, but existing POMs suffer from three coupled weaknesses: task features are too low-dimensional (only normalized fitness and ranking) to capture population structure, mutation strategies rely on global self-attention that limits diversity, and the gumbel-softmax approximation of crossover introduces gradient instability during training. As a result, POMs generalize to new tasks but fail to match the performance of hand-tuned SOTA optimizers like CMA-ES or L-SHADE on those same tasks.

## Input / Output

At inference, EPOM receives a black-box objective f(x) with per-variable bounds [l_i, u_i], a population size n (default 100), and a generation budget T (default 100). Each generation, the model consumes the current population X^t ∈ R^{n×d} and produces an offspring population X^{t+1} via mutation, crossover, and 1-to-1 selection. Notably, the model is trained at d=10 on five synthetic functions (TF1–TF5) and then applied zero-shot to problems at d=30 and d=100, so the tokenizer must compress variable-length d-dimensional individuals into fixed-size tokens regardless of dimensionality.

## Method

EPOM redesigns all three modules of POM. First, a cross-attention tokenizer maps each d-dimensional individual to a fixed d_T×d_1V token using a shared learnable query, enriching the original 2- or 3-dimensional feature vectors with decision-variable-level information. Second, the Efficient LMutM replaces global self-attention with a two-stage mechanism: a first self-attention layer scores all population members, top-p sampling selects a diverse subset per individual, and a deformable self-attention module computes attention weights only over that subset, yielding diverse and efficient mutation. Third, the Differentiable LCrM eliminates gumbel-softmax entirely, instead producing a 2-way crossover weight vector per individual and computing the candidate as a convex combination of the parent and the mean of its selected mutants, giving exact gradients. Training follows the MetaGBT procedure with a loss that combines normalized inter-generation fitness improvement and a crowding-distance diversity term (replacing POM's standard-deviation term), optimized with Adam.

## Results

On the BBOB benchmark (24 functions, d=30 and d=100, 100 generations, population size 100), EPOM achieves the best fitness value on 32 out of 48 function-dimension settings (66.7%), outperforming POM on the majority of cases. EPOM reaches extremely small objective values (e.g., 4.92E-54 on F1 d=30, 0.00E+00 on F8 d=30) where POM only achieves 3.72E-11 and 6.23E-06 respectively, and where CMA-ES and L-SHADE remain orders of magnitude worse (e.g., 7.79E-04 and 1.28E-03 on F1 d=30). On the Bipedal Walker task (d=874 parameters, 800 time steps), EPOM achieves rapid and high-quality convergence, while CMA-ES, DE, and LES suffer from premature convergence and L-SHADE shows lower effectiveness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EPOM is compared against 7 baselines: POM (the direct predecessor), CMA-ES, L-SHADE, DE, ES, LES, and LGA. Against POM, EPOM wins on 32/48 function-dimension settings (66.7%), with particularly dramatic gains on ill-conditioned and multimodal functions (e.g., F1 d=30: 4.92E-54 vs 3.72E-11, a ~43-order-of-magnitude improvement). Against CMA-ES, EPOM dominates on most functions at both d=30 and d=100 (e.g., F1: 4.92E-54 vs 7.79E-04; F12: 1.46E-46 vs 2.91E+11), though CMA-ES matches or slightly edges EPOM on a few simpler unimodal functions (F5, F8 at d=30 where CMA-ES reaches 0.00E+00). Against L-SHADE, EPOM is superior on the vast majority of functions, with L-SHADE only competitive on F2 d=30 (1.47E-05 vs 8.39E-55, where EPOM still wins by 50 orders of magnitude). Against the meta-learned baselines LES and LGA, EPOM consistently outperforms both across nearly all functions, confirming that the pretrained optimization paradigm with enhanced architecture surpasses neuroevolution-based meta-learning approaches. Against DE and ES, EPOM wins on most functions, with these classical heuristics only competitive on a handful of simpler landscapes.

## Relation to Prior Work

EPOM is a direct architectural enhancement of POM (Li et al., NeurIPS 2024), the same group's prior pretrained optimization model, which itself sits within the meta-learning / learning-to-optimize lineage for population-based black-box optimization. The paper borrows deformable attention from vision transformers [50] and cross-attention-based tokenization [49] to enrich population-level feature representations, and replaces POM's gumbel-softmax crossover with a fully differentiable weighted-sum mechanism. Conceptually, it departs from the broader meta-BBO literature (LES, LGA, Meta-MOGA, Q-Mamba) by maintaining a self-supervised, single-model pretraining paradigm rather than relying on external search algorithms, RL policies, or algorithm distillation, and it explicitly positions itself against LLM-based optimizers as a more scalable zero-shot alternative.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work demonstrates that targeted architectural fixes—richer tokenization, sparse deformable attention for population interaction, and a differentiable crossover—can yield orders-of-magnitude improvements in convergence precision over the prior POM, suggesting that the bottleneck in pretrained optimizers lies in representation and gradient flow rather than in the fundamental pretraining recipe. However, the training set is limited to five synthetic functions, and the authors acknowledge that heterogeneous search spaces (categorical, mixed-type, image/string variables) remain unexplored, leaving open whether the zero-shot generalization claim holds beyond continuous numerical landscapes. The framework also raises the question of how model scale and training diversity interact: whether a single pretrained model can truly serve as a universal optimizer or whether task-family-specific fine-tuning will always be necessary for competitive performance.
