## Problem

Most RL research relies on shallow 2–5 layer networks, and prior attempts to scale RL models have reported limited or even negative returns from increasing depth. This paper asks whether self-supervised goal-conditioned RL—specifically Contrastive RL—can unlock substantial, qualitatively new capabilities when network depth is scaled by up to 100×, and identifies the architectural and algorithmic conditions under which depth scaling becomes effective.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255268/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The agent receives a state observation vector (dimensionality ranging from 17 for arm manipulation to 268 for humanoid locomotion) concatenated with a goal state sampled from the goal distribution, and outputs a continuous action vector. The setting is fully unsupervised: no demonstrations, no hand-crafted rewards, and no auxiliary signals are provided—the agent must explore from scratch and learn to maximize the likelihood of reaching commanded goals. Evaluation measures the fraction of 1000-step episodes during which the agent remains within goal proximity.

## Method

The paper scales the actor and both critic encoder networks of Contrastive RL from 4 to 1024 Dense layers by organizing them into residual blocks (each block: 4× Dense → LayerNorm → Swish, with a skip connection around the block). The critic is trained with the InfoNCE contrastive objective, learning to pull together state-action embeddings and their corresponding goal embeddings while pushing apart embeddings from unrelated trajectories; the actor is trained to maximize the critic score. The key finding is that depth—not width—is the more effective scaling axis: simply doubling depth to 8 outperforms widening to 2048 units at depth 4, and performance jumps discontinuously at environment-specific critical depths (e.g., 8 layers for Ant Big Maze, 64 for Humanoid U-Maze) where qualitatively new locomotion skills emerge. Ablations show that residual connections, layer normalization, and Swish activations are all jointly necessary, and that deeper networks synergistically improve both exploration coverage and representational expressivity.

## Results

On the JaxGCRL benchmark (10 locomotion, navigation, and manipulation tasks), scaling CRL network depth from 4 to 64 layers yields 2×–50× improvements in goal-reaching time steps (out of 1000), with the largest gains on high-dimensional tasks: Humanoid (12.6→649, 52×), Humanoid U-Maze (3.2→159, 50×), and Humanoid Big Maze (0.06→59, 1051×). Further scaling to 256 and 1024 layers continues to improve performance on Humanoid U-Maze. Scaled CRL (depth 64) outperforms all standard goal-conditioned baselines (SAC, SAC+HER, TD3+HER, GCBC, GCSL) in 8 out of 10 environments.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Scaled CRL (depth 64) outperforms all five baselines—SAC, SAC+HER, TD3+HER, GCBC, and GCSL—in 8 out of 10 environments. The sole exception is the Humanoid Maze tasks (U-Maze and Big Maze), where SAC exhibits greater early sample efficiency, though scaled CRL eventually reaches comparable performance. Critically, depth scaling does not transfer to the TD-based baselines: SAC, SAC+HER, and TD3+HER all saturate at depth 4 with zero or negative gains from deeper networks, consistent with prior findings that these methods benefit primarily from width. GCBC and GCSL fail to achieve meaningful performance on the Humanoid and Ant Big Maze tasks entirely, though GCBC does show some depth-scaling benefit on Arm Push Easy. This confirms that the self-supervised contrastive (InfoNCE) objective is essential for unlocking depth scaling in goal-conditioned RL.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Contrastive RL (CRL; Eysenbach et al., 2022) by scaling its network depth from the standard 4 layers to up to 1024 layers, using residual connections, layer normalization, and Swish activations to stabilize training. It departs from concurrent RL scaling work (Lee et al., 2024; Nauman et al., 2024b), which found that width scaling helps but depth scaling yields limited or negative returns, by showing that the self-supervised contrastive objective (InfoNCE, a generalization of cross-entropy) is specifically amenable to depth scaling in a way that TD-based methods (SAC, TD3+HER) are not. The approach also builds on the JaxGCRL GPU-accelerated framework (Bortkiewicz et al., 2024) for fast online goal-conditioned RL experiments, and connects to the broader observation that classification-based objectives scale more robustly than regression-based ones (Farebrother et al., 2024).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that depth is a viable and more parameter-efficient axis for scaling RL networks (quadratic in depth vs. linear in width for parameter count), and that this scaling produces qualitatively distinct emergent behaviors at critical depth thresholds rather than smooth improvements. The finding that depth scaling is algorithm-specific—effective for CRL but not for SAC or TD3+HER—suggests that the self-supervised contrastive objective is a key ingredient for scalable RL, opening a path toward RL systems that train their own large models without external supervision. Key limitations include the significant compute cost (up to ~134 hours for 1024 layers), the failure to transfer to offline settings, and evaluation restricted to state-based simulated environments; open questions include whether distributed training, pruning, or distillation can reduce costs, and whether other self-supervised RL algorithms exhibit similar depth-scaling properties.
