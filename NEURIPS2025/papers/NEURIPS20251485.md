## Problem

Existing jailbreaking methods face a fundamental tension: gradient-based optimization (e.g., GCG) operates in discrete token space and produces incoherent, high-perplexity suffixes, while LLM-based iterative prompting (e.g., AutoDAN, PAIR) requires human-crafted prototypes or trained attacker models and lacks direct optimization of the attack objective. The LLM's continuous latent embedding space, which is naturally differentiable and where alignment vulnerabilities may reside, remains largely unexplored as a search space for adversarial prompts.

## Input / Output

The method takes a harmful instruction prompt (e.g., from AdvBench or JailbreakBench) and a target LLM with gradient access, and produces a 200-token natural-language adversarial suffix that, when appended to the prompt, elicits a harmful affirmative response. In single-prompt mode a unique suffix is generated per query; in universal mode one suffix is optimized over a batch of 10 training prompts and evaluated on 200 held-out test prompts. The output is a plain text string deployable in standard chat interfaces without embedding-level access, and in transfer settings the suffix is applied directly to a different model without further optimization.

## Method

LARGO operates a three-stage iterative loop that bridges continuous latent optimization and discrete text generation. First, a latent suffix vector z of length L is initialized to zero and optimized via Adam (lr=1e-3) to minimize cross-entropy loss toward a target affirmative response (e.g., 'Sure, here is...') when appended to the query embedding. Second, the optimized latent vector is fed back into the same LLM through a chat-template prompt that instructs the model to 'summarize the message,' producing a discrete natural-language suffix of matching length via autoregressive decoding. Third, this interpreted suffix is projected back into embedding space via the model's embedding matrix to reinitialize z, and the loop repeats (up to 15 iterations, averaging 6.4 to success). This self-reflective decoding step is the key differentiator: it leverages the LLM's own knowledge of its hidden states to articulate adversarial intent into fluent, benign-looking text, avoiding both the incoherence of token-level search and the human prototyping overhead of genetic or LLM-attacker approaches.

## Results

On single-prompt attacks, LARGO achieves the highest keyword-based ASR across all three target models and both benchmarks: 42.0% (Llama-2-7B), 51.0% (Llama-2-13B), and 79.0% (Phi-3-4B) on AdvBench, and 56.0%, 58.0%, and 86.0% on JailbreakBench. LARGO also maintains the second-lowest suffix perplexity (e.g., 64.88 on Llama-2-7B AdvBench vs. 3249.92 for GCG), indicating fluent, stealthy prompts. In terms of runtime, LARGO completes an attack in ~160 seconds, roughly 7× faster than GCG (1180 s) and 6× faster than AutoDAN (950 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LARGO outperforms GCG on ASR in every single-prompt setting (e.g., 51.0% vs. 20.0% on Llama-2-13B AdvBench, 86.0% vs. 64.0% on Phi-3-4B JailbreakBench) while reducing suffix perplexity by roughly 50× (64.88 vs. 3249.92 on Llama-2-7B AdvBench), making LARGO suffixes far less detectable by perplexity-based filters. Against AutoDAN, LARGO achieves substantially higher ASR on Llama-2 models (42.0% vs. 18.0% and 51.0% vs. 7.0% on AdvBench) and also on JailbreakBench (56.0% vs. 21.0%, 58.0% vs. 16.0%), with the gap narrowing on Phi-3-4B (79.0% vs. 76.0%). AdvPrompter is dominated by a wide margin on ASR (e.g., 42.0% vs. 2.0% on Llama-2-7B AdvBench), though it produces the lowest-perplexity suffixes (17.83) due to its fine-tuned LLM generation. In transfer settings, LARGO suffixes also outperform GCG in 8 out of 9 cross-model configurations, and in universal attacks LARGO achieves 22.0% ASR on Llama-2-7B versus GCG's 9.5% with 59× lower perplexity (18.54 vs. 1094.11).

## Relation to Prior Work

LARGO sits at the intersection of two prior research threads: gradient-based adversarial optimization in continuous spaces (GCG, Zou et al. 2023) and self-interpretive decoding of LLM hidden states (SelfIE, Chen et al. 2024; LatentQA, Pan et al. 2024). It departs from GCG by avoiding discrete token-level search, and from LLM-as-optimizer approaches (AutoDAN, PAIR, AdvPrompter) by performing the core optimization in the model's own embedding space rather than in text space. The key conceptual move is using the target model itself as a 'translator' from optimized latent vectors to natural-language suffixes, a step borrowed from interpretability work but repurposed here for adversarial prompt construction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that aligned LLMs harbor exploitable vulnerabilities in their continuous latent space that are not easily surfaced by discrete token optimization, and that these vulnerabilities transfer across model families and even to frontier systems (DeepSeek-V3, Claude-4, GPT-4o). A key limitation is the white-box requirement: LARGO needs gradient access to the target model, restricting its use to red-teaming by model developers rather than external attackers. Open questions include whether the self-reflective decoding step can be replaced by a learned decoder to enable black-box variants, whether the latent adversarial patterns are model-specific or reflect a shared vulnerability class, and how perplexity-based and semantic-based defenses should evolve to catch fluent, low-perplexity suffixes that still carry adversarial intent.
