## Problem

Relative positional encoding (RPE) has proven effective in standard Transformers for capturing inter-token distance relationships, but its integration into spiking Transformers remains unexplored. Existing spiking positional encodings either produce ambiguous spike representations across positions or rely solely on absolute encoding, and directly porting RPE techniques like ALiBi or RoPE to spiking architectures fails due to the binary, sparse nature of spike activations that makes it difficult to disentangle positional information from the attention computation.

## Input / Output

The method processes time series windows (length 12 for short-term, 168 for long-term), text token sequences (length 32–2048), and image patches (32×32 or 64×64) through LIF spiking neurons with 4 time steps, producing binary Q, K, V matrices. The attention map is computed via XNOR-based Hamming distance rather than floating-point dot products, and the final outputs are predicted future values (time series), class probabilities (text/image classification), or event-stream-based class labels (CIFAR10-DVS). A key constraint is that all intermediate activations must remain binary (0/1) to preserve spike-based computation compatibility with neuromorphic hardware.

## Method

The paper proposes two RPE strategies that preserve binary spike dynamics. First, Gray-PE concatenates the binary Gray Code representation of each position index onto the channel dimension of both Q and K matrices, leveraging a proven theorem that positions separated by 2^n exhibit a constant Hamming distance (1 for n=0, 2 for n≥1) in their Gray Code encodings. Second, Log-PE adds a precomputed logarithmic relative-distance bias R_{i,j} = ⌈log₂((L−1)/|i−j|+1)⌉ directly to the XNOR-based attention map, inducing decaying sensitivity to positional distance analogous to ALiBi. Both methods are built on a modified spiking self-attention that replaces the standard dot product with XNOR (exclusive-NOR) summation over the channel dimension, aligning the similarity metric with Hamming distance. A 2D extension of Gray-PE independently encodes horizontal and vertical patch positions for image classification, while Log-PE's 2D variant is abandoned due to convergence issues from excessive bias magnitudes.

## Results

On text classification (6 datasets, avg accuracy), Spikformer-XNOR with Log-PE achieves 80.38%, outperforming the no-PE baseline (77.20%) and CPG-PE (79.41%). On time-series forecasting, Log-PE and Gray-PE consistently yield the best R² and RSE across Metr-LA, PEMS-Bay, Solar, and Electricity at all prediction lengths (6–96). For patch-based image classification, Gray-PE 2D achieves 75.39% average accuracy across CIFAR-10, CIFAR-10-DVS, CIFAR-100, and Tiny-ImageNet, surpassing Conv-PE (74.51%) and CPG-PE (74.95%). On long-sequence text (AG News, IMDB with lengths 1024/2048), Log-PE reaches 83.62% average accuracy versus 81.46% for the original Conv-PE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the original Spikformer with Conv-PE (Zhou, ICLR 2023), both Gray-PE and Log-PE variants of Spikformer-XNOR improve average text classification accuracy from 77.25% to 80.11% and 80.38% respectively, and achieve lower RSE on all four time-series benchmarks. The spiking absolute PE baseline CPG-PE (Lv, NeurIPS 2024) is consistently outperformed: on text classification, Log-PE gains +0.97% average accuracy over CPG-PE; on image classification, Gray-PE 2D gains +0.44% over CPG-PE. The non-spiking Transformer baselines (Vaswani, NeurIPS 2017) with RoPE or ALiBi serve as upper references; directly porting RoPE/ALiBi into Spikformer degrades performance severely (e.g., RSE of 1.01 on Solar), confirming that standard RPE is incompatible with binary spike dynamics. Spikingformer (Zhou, 2023) without PE is outperformed by Spikingformer-XNOR with Log-PE on time-series (e.g., RSE 0.714 vs 0.720 on Metr-LA at length 24). SDT-V1 (Yao, NeurIPS 2023) with Conv-PE is improved by Log-PE (RSE 0.713 vs 0.720 on Metr-LA). QKFormer (Zhou, NeurIPS 2024) benefits from XNOR + Log-PE on time-series (RSE 0.285 vs 0.565 on Electricity at length 24) but shows minimal gains on image classification, attributed to its channel-aggregated attention design suppressing sequential dependencies.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the spiking Transformer lineage (Spikformer, SDT, QKFormer) by addressing a gap identified in prior work: existing positional encoding for SNNs (Conv-PE, CPG-PE) is either absolute or ambiguous across positions, while standard RPE methods (ALiBi, RoPE) cannot be directly transplanted due to the binary spike constraint. The key conceptual departure is replacing dot-product attention with XNOR-based attention to align with Hamming-distance-based similarity, then proposing two RPE approximations—Gray-PE (exploiting a proven property of Gray Code Hamming distances) and Log-PE (a logarithmic bias inspired by ALiBi and ReRoPE)—that preserve binary spike dynamics. The theoretical contribution (Theorem 1) provides the first formal guarantee that Gray Code encodings maintain constant Hamming distance for power-of-two separations, grounding the design in combinatorial number theory rather than empirical tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work establishes a principled framework for RPE in spiking Transformers, which is essential for neuromorphic hardware deployment where floating-point operations are prohibitively expensive. The finding that directly applying ALiBi or RoPE to spiking architectures fails (Table 1) is a useful negative result that redirects the community toward spike-compatible designs. However, limitations remain: Gray-PE's power-of-two quantization constrains its effectiveness on long sequences (L ≥ 100), 2D Log-PE failed to converge, and QKFormer showed insensitivity to PE in image classification, suggesting the benefit is task- and architecture-dependent. Open questions include whether hybrid RPE strategies can overcome Gray-PE's quantization bottleneck at scale, and whether the XNOR modification generalizes to other spiking attention variants beyond those tested.
