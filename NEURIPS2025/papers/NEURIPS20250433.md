## Problem

Existing audio-sync video generation methods suffer from under-specified audio-visual mapping (composite audios mapped ambiguously to diverse scenes) and inaccurate temporal alignment (scene-level consistency without precise timing of individual audio events like speech or sound effects). This limits the ability to produce high-fidelity cinematic videos with fine-grained, semantically aligned audio-visual synchronization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250433/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a structured text description (specifying participant count, individual appearances, active speaker, and scene) together with three explicitly demixed audio tracks—speech, effects, and music—and produces 49-frame video clips at 480×720 resolution. The audio tracks are consumed separately rather than as a single composite signal, with speech and effects driving interval-level controls while music drives a global style modulation. Inference takes approximately 280 seconds per clip on a single NVIDIA A100 GPU.

## Method

MTV builds on the CogVideoX diffusion transformer backbone and introduces a Multi-Stream Temporal ControlNet (MST-ControlNet) with two specialized streams. The interval stream processes speech and effects features (extracted via wav2vec) through stacked interval interaction blocks that refine per-track understanding via AdaLN, gating, and FFN, then model inter-track interplay via self-attention at each time interval; the resulting features are injected into corresponding video latent intervals through cross-attention to drive lip motion and event timing. The holistic stream encodes music features through a 1D convolutional context encoder, applies average pooling to produce a global style embedding, and modulates all video latent frames uniformly via learned scale and shift factors to control visual mood. Training follows a multi-stage schedule across five overlapped subsets of the DEMIX dataset (392K clips, 1.2K hours), progressing from concrete localized controls (lip motion on talking-head clips) to abstract global influences (visual mood on cinematic clips), with the DiT backbone and MST-ControlNet jointly trained at each stage.

## Results

On the DEMIX test set (50 videos), MTV achieves FVD of 626.06, Temp-C of 95.40%, Text-C of 26.55%, Audio-C of 26.22%, Sync-C of 3.17, and Sync-D of 9.43, outperforming all three baselines across all six metrics. The most substantial gains are in audio-video alignment: Audio-C improves from 5.43–7.30% (baselines) to 26.22%, and lip-sync confidence (Sync-C) more than doubles from 1.45–1.55 to 3.17. In the user study, MTV is preferred by 74–78% of raters across semantic consistency, motion fluency, and overall preference.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MTV outperforms all three baselines on every metric. Against MM-Diffusion [5] (Ruan et al., CVPR 2023), MTV reduces FVD by 29% (626 vs 880) and improves Audio-C by nearly 4× (26.22% vs 5.43%), reflecting MM-Diffusion's difficulty generating cinematic content even after 320K steps of fine-tuning. Against TempoTokens [1] (Yariv et al., AAAI 2024), MTV achieves a 21% lower FVD (626 vs 796) and a 3.9× higher Audio-C (26.22% vs 6.71%), while TempoTokens struggles with complex text-specified scenarios and produces unrealistic human expressions. Against Xing et al. [3] (CVPR 2024), MTV shows a 22% FVD improvement (626 vs 805) and a 3.6× Audio-C advantage (26.22% vs 7.30%), with Xing et al. failing to correctly synchronize event timing (e.g., guitar performance gestures). The consistent dominance across both video-quality metrics (FVD, Temp-C) and audio-alignment metrics (Audio-C, Sync-C, Sync-D) indicates that the multi-stream demixed audio control is the key differentiator.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on CogVideoX's diffusion-transformer backbone and extends the audio-sync video generation line (MM-Diffusion, TempoTokens, Xing et al.) by replacing composite audio conditioning with explicitly demixed speech, effects, and music tracks processed through a dedicated multi-stream ControlNet. It departs from prior audio-driven image animation methods (which require a reference image) by operating purely from text descriptions and audio, and from earlier audio-sync video approaches by introducing interval-wise cross-attention for lip/event synchronization and global style injection for music-driven mood, rather than a single unified audio encoder. The multi-stage training strategy over five overlapped subsets is a new organizational principle for progressively learning from localized (lip motion) to global (visual mood) audio-visual relationships.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The DEMIX dataset (392K cinematic clips with demixed audio tracks) fills a notable gap in the field, as no prior dataset provided both high-quality cinematic video and separated audio components at this scale. The finding that training the DiT backbone alongside the ControlNet is critical for lip-sync quality (W/o TB ablation) offers a practical lesson for adapting pretrained video models to new conditioning signals. However, the approach is fundamentally bounded by upstream demixing quality (MVSEP/Spleeter), and the evaluation relies on only three baselines in an emerging task, leaving open how the method scales to longer videos, more complex multi-speaker scenes, or improved demixing pipelines.
