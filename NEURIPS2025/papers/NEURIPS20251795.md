## Problem

Multi-task adaptation of large pre-trained models suffers from task conflict (negative transfer across tasks) and task oblivion (catastrophic forgetting of pre-trained capabilities). Existing MoE-based PEFT methods learn unconstrained low-rank experts without structural guarantees, leading to expert redundancy, load imbalance, and interference that exacerbate both issues.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251795/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text input x^(k) and a task ID k, producing the standard model output for that task. The router consumes both a task-level embedding t_k (dimension D_t) and a sample-level projection Γx^(k) (dimension D_s) to compute per-expert weights, enabling the same input to receive different expert configurations under different tasks. At inference, the orthogonal adapter H can be merged into each expert's right singular vectors, reducing the forward pass to three matrix multiplications with pre-computed factors.

## Method

MoORE decomposes each pre-trained weight matrix W via SVD into D orthogonal rank-one experts (u_d v_d^T), then introduces a hybrid router that dynamically adjusts the singular values based on both task identity and input content, yielding y = U diag(g(x^(k)) + σ) V^T H x^(k). A learnable orthogonal adapter H, implemented as a product of L Householder reflections, is applied to the input to increase model capacity while preserving inter-neuron angles. Two structural properties distinguish MoORE from prior MoE-based PEFT: (1) the experts are inherently orthogonal (u_d^T u_{d'} = 0 for d ≠ d'), suppressing inter-task interference during training, and (2) the output space equals Range(W), ensuring the adapted model retains the pre-trained model's column space and thus its original capabilities. The number of experts D equals the input dimension, making MoORE an MoE with many simple structured experts rather than a few learned low-rank ones, with overall complexity O(D(D_out + D + D_t + D_s + L)).

## Results

On CSR-MTL (9 commonsense reasoning tasks), MoORE with L=8 Householder reflections achieves 85.11% overall accuracy, outperforming the strongest baseline LoRAMoE (84.34%) by 0.77 points and full fine-tuning (84.27%) by 0.84 points, while using only 2.84% of parameters. On NLU-MTL (7 GLUE tasks), MoORE (L=0) reaches 89.08% overall, beating LoRAMoE (88.08%) by 1.0 points. For oblivion-resistance on OR-MTL, MoORE incurs only a 1.31% average performance drop after adaptation, compared to 4.26% for LoRAMoE and 47.09% for plain LoRA, and even exceeds the base model on HumanEval by 9.75%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MoORE consistently outperforms all six baselines (LoRA, MixLoRA, MoSLD, HydraLoRA, MTL-LoRA, LoRAMoE) on both CSR-MTL and NLU-MTL with comparable parameter budgets (~2.7–2.8%). On CSR-MTL, MoORE (L=8) leads LoRAMoE by 0.77 points overall (85.11% vs. 84.34%) and MTL-LoRA by 1.02 points; on NLU-MTL, MoORE (L=0) leads LoRAMoE by 1.0 points (89.08% vs. 88.08%). The advantage grows with task count and difficulty: when adapting to all 9 CSR tasks, MoORE's lead over LoRAMoE widens relative to the 2-task setting. For oblivion-resistance, MoORE's 1.31% average drop on OR-MTL is dramatically smaller than LoRAMoE (4.26%), MixLoRA (5.46%), and LoRA (47.09%), and MoORE even improves over the base model on HumanEval (+9.75%). On Qwen-2.5 7B, MoORE (L=16) achieves 86.63% on CSR-MTL, again beating LoRAMoE (86.17%), confirming cross-architecture generalizability.

## Relation to Prior Work

MoORE departs from the dominant paradigm of MoE-based multi-task adaptation (LoRAMoE, MixLoRA, HydraLoRA, MTL-LoRA, MoSLD), which learns a small number of extrinsic low-rank experts with no structural constraints on their mutual relations. Instead, it exploits the SVD of the pre-trained weight matrix to reveal an intrinsic mixture of D orthogonal rank-one experts, then introduces a hybrid task/sample-level router to adjust their singular values and a Householder reflection adapter (building directly on HRA [75]) to expand capacity. This 'model MoE-ization' strategy is conceptually distinct from prior SVD-based fine-tuning methods (SVF, SVDiff, SVFT, KaSA, SVFCL), which treat singular values as non-parametric or static adjustments rather than learning a parametric router over them.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By guaranteeing expert orthogonality and preserving the column space of the original weight matrix, MoORE provides structural (rather than purely data-driven) resistance to both task conflict and catastrophic forgetting, yielding notably smaller performance degradation on unseen tasks (1.31% average drop vs. 4–9% for baselines). However, the method introduces D rank-one experts per layer (far more than the M experts in typical MoE baselines), and the paper acknowledges that inference efficiency, while competitive, is not yet optimal for large-scale deployment. Open questions include the fundamental trade-off between conflict-resistance and oblivion-resistance as the number of tasks grows, and whether the dense routing weights observed empirically can be sparsified without sacrificing performance.
