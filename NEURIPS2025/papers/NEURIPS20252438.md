## Problem

Existing contextual RL methods for zero-shot generalization require explicit context variables (e.g., friction, gravity) as privileged inputs, making them inapplicable when contexts are latent or unmeasurable. DreamerV3's recurrent state (RSSM) suffers from an information bottleneck that compresses context, state, and noise into a fixed-size GRU, losing critical environmental cues needed for adaptation. This paper addresses the gap of inferring latent contexts self-supervisedly from interaction histories to enable robust zero-shot generalization without ground-truth context supervision.

## Input / Output

The context encoder consumes K-length windows (K=50 in experiments) of observations—either featurized state vectors or raw pixel images—along with the corresponding action sequence, and produces an 8-dimensional latent context vector via a transformer architecture. This context representation is then injected into the DreamerV3 world model to condition the RSSM's posterior encoder (shallow integration) or the full sequence model, predictors, and actor-critic (deep integration). The final outputs are policy actions selected over imagined rollouts of horizon H=50, with the context representation enabling physically consistent counterfactual trajectories when individual latent dimensions are perturbed.

## Method

DALI introduces a self-supervised context encoder g_φ trained with a forward dynamics loss that predicts the next observation from the current observation, action, and inferred context, forcing the representation to capture dynamics-relevant contextual factors. A cross-modal regularization term bidirectionally aligns the context representation with the DreamerV3 posterior state z_t, preventing degenerate solutions while leveraging the world model's observation-informed dynamics. Two integration strategies are proposed: shallow (appending context to the encoder input, letting it propagate through recurrence) and deep (injecting context into the sequence model, all predictors, and actor-critic). Theoretically, the paper proves that under β-mixing and Lipschitz dynamics, the encoder achieves near-optimal context information I(c; z_t) ≥ (1−δ)h(c) using only K = Ω(log(1/δ)/λ) transitions per window, yielding an O(T/K) sample complexity gain over DreamerV3's full-episode RSSM. Empirically, shallow integration with cross-modal regularization (DALI-S-χ) achieves up to +96.4% IQM gains over Dreamer-DR in extrapolation and surpasses ground-truth context-aware baselines, while latent perturbations produce physically plausible counterfactuals (e.g., shorter swings under higher imagined gravity).

## Results

On the DMC Ball-in-Cup Extrapolation regime (the most challenging OOD setting), DALI-S-χ achieves IQM 0.3720 (Featurized) and 0.2730 (Pixel), outperforming the context-unaware Dreamer-DR baseline by +87.9% and +96.4% respectively. In the Walker Walk Extrapolation regime, DALI-S reaches IQM 0.7810 (Featurized), surpassing Dreamer-DR by +4.0%. In Interpolation regimes, all methods perform comparably (IQM 0.92–0.97), confirming that the gains are specific to OOD generalization rather than in-distribution performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the context-unaware Dreamer-DR baseline (DreamerV3 with domain randomization), DALI achieves substantial gains in the Extrapolation regime: +87.9% to +96.4% on Ball-in-Cup and +4.0% on Walker (Featurized), confirming that domain randomization alone is insufficient for OOD context generalization. Against the context-aware cRSSM-S and cRSSM-D baselines (which receive ground-truth context), DALI-S-χ wins decisively on Ball-in-Cup Extrapolation in both modalities (e.g., 0.3720 vs. 0.2270/0.2780 Featurized; 0.2730 vs. 0.1870/0.2420 Pixel), suggesting that inferred context representations generalize better than explicit context variables that may overfit to training ranges. On Walker Featurized Extrapolation, DALI-S also surpasses both context-aware baselines (0.7810 vs. 0.7020/0.7490). However, DALI loses to cRSSM-S on Walker Pixel Extrapolation (0.7580 vs. 0.7770) and to cRSSM-D on Ball-in-Cup Pixel Mixed (0.6030 vs. 0.6250), indicating that ground-truth context still provides an advantage for complex visual dynamics in certain settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

DALI extends DreamerV3's RSSM-based world model by adding a self-supervised context encoder trained via forward dynamics alignment, positioning itself between context-aware cMDP methods (which require ground-truth context variables, e.g., cRSSM from Prasanna et al., 2024) and context-unaware domain randomization baselines. It departs from the meta-RL line of work by targeting zero-shot rather than few-shot adaptation, and from prior latent-context inference approaches (e.g., Eghbal-zadeh et al., 2021; Evans et al., 2022) by grounding the encoder in a dynamics-prediction objective and integrating it directly into a Dreamer-style imagination loop. The theoretical analysis builds on information-theoretic tools for β-mixing processes to formalize why a dedicated short-window encoder outperforms the RSSM's implicit context accumulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a self-supervised inferred context can outperform ground-truth context conditioning in extrapolation regimes (up to +63.9% over cRSSM-S in Ball-in-Cup) challenges the assumption that explicit context supervision is strictly superior for OOD generalization, suggesting that the inference process itself regularizes against overfitting to training contexts. However, the evidence is limited to two DMC tasks with the small DreamerV3 variant, and the advantage does not hold uniformly (cRSSM-D wins in Ball-in-Cup Pixel Mixed; cRSSM-S wins in Walker Pixel Extrapolation). Open questions include whether the β-mixing and exploratory-policy assumptions hold in sparse-reward or high-dimensional settings, whether the approach scales to more complex multi-task or real-robot environments, and how the theoretical sample-complexity gain translates to downstream policy performance under joint optimization.
