## Problem

Existing LVLM hallucination research focuses on isolated textual or visual recognition errors, but a subtle failure mode remains unexplored: when a model correctly answers both a simple visual-centric and a simple textual-centric question individually, it still hallucinates on their composition. This paper defines this as Simple Compositional Hallucination (SCHall), shows it is widespread across major LVLMs (with ~20% accuracy drops on compositional questions), and identifies two root causes—failure to abstract question-relevant visual content under implicit compositional queries, and degradation of language processing induced by the mere presence of visual input.

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard RGB image and a text instruction/question as input, producing a free-form text answer. Unusually, N randomly initialized Vision Language Register (VLR) tokens are inserted between the projected image features and the text token embeddings in the language model's input sequence, acting as a learned bridge that selectively absorbs question-relevant visual content. During the distillation training stage, a parallel text-only branch replaces the image tokens with tokenized image captions, and the KL divergence between the two branches' output distributions serves as an additional training signal.

## Method

VLR-distillation introduces two complementary mechanisms. First, Vision Language Registers (VLRs) are N randomly initialized tokens concatenated between image and text inputs; they are pretrained with a modified causal attention mask that blocks output tokens from directly attending to image tokens, forcing the VLRs to aggregate and abstract relevant visual information into a text-aligned representation. Second, a distillation learning strategy introduces a text-only teacher branch (where image tokens are replaced by caption tokens) and minimizes the KL divergence between its output distribution and that of the original multimodal branch, thereby preserving the language model's textual reasoning capability under compositional settings. Training proceeds in two stages: (1) pretraining VLRs with all other components frozen, and (2) distillation fine-tuning via LoRA on the language model with VLRs frozen, using a combined loss of language modeling losses for both branches plus the KL divergence term.

## Results

On the proposed SCBench, VLR-distillation achieves 41.80% overall accuracy on LLaVA1.5-7B (+8.05 over baseline), 47.06% on Qwen-VL-Chat (+4.65), and 26.93% on MiniGPT-4 (+12.07), consistently outperforming all hallucination-mitigation baselines. On POPE (Random split), the method improves F1 by +5.43 (LLaVA1.5: 81.33→86.76), +3.99 (Qwen-VL: 82.67→86.66), and +10.26 (MiniGPT-4: 72.28→82.54). On MME-hall, total scores rise from 510.33→585.67 (LLaVA1.5), 585.56→618.33 (Qwen-VL), and 371.67→440.33 (MiniGPT-4). General-purpose benchmarks show consistent ~1.5-point gains, with +1.9 on HallusionBench and +2.2 on MM-Vet.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On SCBench, VLR-distillation decisively outperforms all five hallucination-mitigation baselines (VCD, PAI, CODE, REVERIE, CCA) across all three model backbones, with the largest margin over PAI (which degrades LLaVA1.5 from 33.75 to 23.22) and the smallest over VCD/CODE (which achieve ~35.6 vs. 41.80 on LLaVA1.5). On POPE, the method improves F1 by +5.4 to +10.3 over the unmodified base models across Random/Popular/Adversarial splits, with MiniGPT-4 showing the largest gain (+10.26 F1 on Random). On MME-hall, VLR-distillation adds +75 total score for LLaVA1.5 and +68.7 for MiniGPT-4, confirming gains extend beyond object-existence hallucination to attribute and counting errors. On general-purpose benchmarks (MMBench, ScienceQA, HallusionBench, MM-Vet), the method yields modest but consistent ~1.5-point improvements, with the largest gains on hallucination-focused HallusionBench (+1.9) and MM-Vet (+2.2), suggesting the VLR mechanism primarily targets compositional failures rather than general VQA accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves out a new sub-problem within LVLM hallucination research: prior work (POPE, TruthfulQA, MME-hall) targets isolated visual recognition errors or textual factuality failures, while this paper identifies and formalizes Simple Compositional Hallucination (SCHall), where both the visual and textual components are individually answered correctly yet their composition induces failure. Methodologically, the VLR-distillation approach repurposes learnable register tokens (analogous to Perceiver-style latent tokens) as a visual-to-linguistic bridge, combined with a text-only teacher branch for KL-divergence distillation—a combination not previously applied to hallucination mitigation. The paper explicitly distinguishes itself from knowledge-centric VQA (OK-VQA, where the bottleneck is external knowledge) and multi-step reasoning benchmarks, positioning SCHall as a single-step composition failure that neither category captures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that the proportion of compositional hallucination *increases* as visual recognition improves (19.1% for LLaVA → 27.5% for InternVL) is a critical signal for the field: simply scaling up visual encoders will not eliminate hallucination, and a new axis of improvement is needed. SCBench provides the first systematic evaluation tool for this failure mode, and the observation that even a blank image degrades language processing by ~2–6% suggests a fundamental architectural limitation in how current LVLMs integrate modalities. Limitations include evaluation only on 7B-scale models, a single-epoch training protocol, and a relatively small benchmark (951 questions); whether VLR-distillation scales to larger models or multi-image/video settings remains open.
