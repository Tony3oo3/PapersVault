## Problem

Online strategic classification has been studied under noise-free deterministic feature-label relationships or fully adversarial settings, but no prior work handles the simultaneous combination of agents' strategic feature manipulation, partial (one-sided) feedback where only positively classified agents' labels are observed, and arbitrary feature-dependent label noise (Massart noise). This paper is the first to study this combined problem, which is particularly challenging because the data points nearest the classification boundary—most informative for learning—are precisely those most prone to strategic manipulation.

## Input / Output

At each cycle, the principal announces a binary linear classifier h̃(r)=sgn(⟨w,r⟩+m) and receives a reported feature vector r_t that may differ from the agent's true feature x_t due to costly strategic manipulation (bounded by distance γ). The principal observes the true label y_t only when the agent is classified positive, yielding a one-sided feedback signal. The algorithm outputs a sequence of unit-norm coefficient vectors w_k that define classifiers converging to the clairvoyant optimal classifier h̃*(r)=sgn(⟨w*,r⟩−γ), achieving O(√T) regret up to poly-logarithmic factors.

## Method

The algorithm proceeds in three phases: an Initialization phase using an averaging technique with pairs of opposing classifiers to obtain a coarse direction, a Refinement phase applying online mirror descent with a localization band near the boundary where agents report truthfully, and a Batched Enhancement phase that iteratively halves the angular error to the optimal vector. The key innovation in the Enhancement phase is a pairwise contrastive inference scheme: two classifiers with thresholds γ and γ+b_k are declared successively, and the difference in collected data from their respective reported-feature regions unbiasedly estimates the gradient for the true-feature band of interest. Because all agents in this band misreport their features, the algorithm constructs proxy features by projecting reported features onto the upper (for y=+1) or lower (for y=−1) boundary of the localization band, enabling effective gradient estimation without ever observing true features. The bandwidth shrinks geometrically across batches (b_k ∝ 2^{-k}), and the constraint set enforces that iterates remain within a shrinking cone around the previous batch's output, guaranteeing geometric convergence of the angular error θ(w*,w_k) ≤ π/2^{k+1}.

## Results

The main theoretical guarantee (Theorem 1) establishes that the proposed algorithm achieves an expected regret of O(d ln d · (ln T)² / (1−2η̄)⁸ + √((γ+1)·d·ln d·T) · ln T / (1−2η̄)²) over T cycles, i.e., a √T regret rate up to poly-logarithmic and constant factors. Numerical experiments over 50,000 cycles (30 replications) confirm sublinear regret growth for the proposed algorithm under both a Standard Normal feature distribution with feature-dependent noise (Setting 1) and a Unit Ball distribution with constant noise (Setting 2), while both baselines exhibit approximately linear regret accumulation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two baselines, both originally designed for full-feedback settings and adapted to partial feedback. Against the Strategic Perceptron of Ahmadi et al. [2021] (designed for noiseless, deterministic feature-label relationships), the proposed algorithm achieves sublinear regret while both the full-feedback and partial-feedback variants of Strategic Perceptron accumulate regret linearly; the gap between full and partial feedback for Strategic Perceptron is marginal, indicating that noise sensitivity (not partial feedback) is the primary cause of its failure. Against the non-strategic halfspace learning algorithm of Zhang et al. [2020] (designed for Massart noise without strategic agents), the proposed algorithm again achieves sublinear regret while both full-feedback and partial-feedback variants of the non-strategic algorithm show linear regret, because they ignore agents' strategic feature manipulation and thus receive misleading updates from manipulated features. In all four baseline configurations, the proposed algorithm's regret curve is clearly separated and grows at a fundamentally slower rate, validating the necessity of jointly addressing strategic manipulation, partial feedback, and label noise.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: online strategic classification (Ahmadi et al. 2021, Shen et al. 2024), learning halfspaces under Massart noise via localization (Zhang et al. 2020, Diakonikolas et al. 2020), and one-sided/partial feedback (Harris et al. 2023). Prior strategic classification work assumed either noise-free deterministic feature-label relationships or fully adversarial settings, while prior halfspace learning with noise assumed no strategic manipulation. The key conceptual departure is that the standard localization scheme—which focuses on data near the classification boundary—is fundamentally at odds with strategic manipulation, since boundary-proximate agents are precisely those with incentive to misreport. The paper resolves this tension through two novel mechanisms: projection-based proxy features and a pairwise contrastive inference technique that infers boundary-region statistics by contrasting data from two carefully offset classifiers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to simultaneously handle strategic feature manipulation, Massart noise, and partial feedback in online classification, achieving the optimal O(√T) regret rate. The pairwise contrastive inference technique is a general-purpose tool that could extend to other settings where the most informative data is systematically corrupted or unobservable. Limitations include restriction to Massart noise (not Tsybakov or adversarial noise), homogeneous and known agent utility functions, and binary linear classifiers. Open questions include whether the approach extends to multi-class settings, heterogeneous agent populations, or non-linear classifier families, and whether the regret bound can be tightened beyond the poly-logarithmic factors.
