## Problem

In graph coarsening, the reduction matrix P and lifting matrix Q are conventionally tied together as Moore-Penrose pseudo-inverses, yet their roles are fundamentally asymmetric: Q alone must be well-partitioned and binary for the coarsened graph to be well-defined, while P plays no role in defining the coarsened adjacency or Laplacian. This paper asks whether, for a fixed lifting matrix Q, the reduction matrix P can be chosen more freely to improve the Restricted Spectral Approximation (RSA) constant, and what the admissible families of such P matrices are.

## Input / Output

The method takes a graph adjacency matrix A, a fixed well-partitioned binary lifting matrix Q (from an iterative coarsening algorithm), a preserved subspace R spanned by the first K eigenvectors of a chosen Δ-Laplacian, and a target coarsening ratio. It outputs a reduction matrix P belonging to one of three nested admissible sets (E1 ⊃ E2 ⊃ E3) along with the resulting RSA constant. The output P ranges from dense (P_opt, P_g*) to exactly N non-zero entries matching the support of Qᵀ (P_MP, P_Qᵀ*), with the sparsity pattern directly affecting downstream GNN propagation cost.

## Method

The paper establishes a taxonomy of admissible reduction matrices: E1 (P such that Π=QP is a projection, equivalent to Q being a generalized inverse of P), E2 (P is a reflexive generalized inverse of Q, admitting the closed-form parametrization P = Q⁺ + M(I − QQ⁺) for arbitrary M), and E3 (P in E2 restricted to the same support as Qᵀ, characterized by a simple row-normalization constraint). Within these sets, the paper derives both closed-form examples (Moore-Penrose, Loukas iterative product, and a Rao-Mitra-inspired P_opt = L_c⁺QᵀL) and optimization-based solutions that minimize the RSA directly. The key technical insight is that when R is an eigen-subspace of the Laplacian, the RSA has an explicit expression that is convex in P, making the optimization over E2 (in the M parameter) or E3 (with linear constraints) tractable via projected gradient descent. Experiments on Cora and Citeseer show that RSA-optimized reduction matrices outperform the standard Moore-Penrose choice, with the gap widening at higher coarsening ratios, though the translation to GNN node-classification accuracy is more modest and depends on graph homophily and the sparsity of P.

## Results

In terms of RSA, the proposed P_opt and P_g* consistently achieve lower RSA constants than the standard P_MP and P_Loukas, with the gap widening at higher coarsening ratios (up to 0.85). For GNN node classification (SGC) on Cora at r=0.7, the sparsity-constrained P_{g,l1}* achieves 78.3% accuracy, outperforming P_MP (77.7%) and P_Loukas (76.8%), while approaching the full-graph baseline of 81.0%. Notably, P_opt attains the best RSA but the worst GNN accuracy (73.8% on Cora r=0.7 SGC), attributed to its dense structure making propagation resemble a complete graph. The sparsity-constrained P_{Q^T}* matches P_MP's sparsity (N non-zeros) while achieving comparable or slightly better RSA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two standard reduction matrices from the coarsening literature: P_Loukas (product of per-step Moore-Penrose inverses from iterative coarsening [27]) and P_MP (the Moore-Penrose inverse of Q, used in [27, 11, 25]). In terms of RSA, both P_opt and P_g* clearly outperform these baselines, with the advantage growing at higher coarsening ratios. For GNN accuracy, the gains over P_MP and P_Loukas are modest: on Cora SGC at r=0.7, P_{g,l1}* improves by ~0.6% over P_MP and ~1.5% over P_Loukas. On Citeseer, differences are smaller and less consistent, likely due to higher heterophily. Surprisingly, P_opt achieves the best RSA yet the worst GNN performance across all settings, suggesting that RSA alone does not fully predict downstream GNN quality and that sparsity of the reduction matrix plays an additional role. The sparsity-constrained P_{Q^T}* and P_{g,l1}* offer the best trade-off, matching or exceeding baseline accuracy while maintaining near-minimal memory footprint (≈N non-zeros vs. millions for P_opt).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Loukas's [27] framework for graph coarsening with RSA guarantees, generalizing his greedy node-merging algorithm to Δ-Laplacians, and on Rao and Mitra's [32] characterization of generalized inverses to parameterize the space of admissible reduction matrices. It departs from the near-universal assumption in the coarsening literature that the reduction matrix P must be the Moore-Penrose pseudo-inverse of the lifting matrix Q, showing instead that Q alone (being well-partitioned and binary) fully defines the coarsened graph, while P retains meaningful degrees of freedom that affect the RSA. The authors position this as the first work to formally 'decorrelate' P and Q with precise mathematical constraints, distinguishing their approach from graph pooling methods that use unconstrained or learned propagation matrices.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The taxonomy of admissible reduction matrices (E1 ⊃ E2 ⊃ E3) provides a principled menu of choices for practitioners who previously had no alternative to P = Q⁺, and the convex optimization formulations over E2 and E3 are straightforward to implement. However, the paper's own experiments reveal a significant gap between spectral improvement and downstream utility: RSA-optimized matrices yield only marginal GNN accuracy gains (≤1%) on Cora and Citeseer, and the best-RSA matrix P_opt actually performs worst for GNNs due to its density. This suggests that RSA is an incomplete proxy for coarsening quality in learning applications, and open questions include whether heterophily-aware or feature-aware scoring functions, scalability to million-node graphs, and the interplay between sparsity and spectral fidelity can close this gap.
