## Problem

Long-term intracortical BCI decoders degrade across sessions because the recorded neural population changes in size, identity, and ordering. Existing adaptive methods rely on fixed unit identities and require explicit alignment with gradient updates or test-time labels, imposing costly re-calibration overhead. No prior decoder is permutation-invariant by design, so it cannot handle variable-sized, unordered populations out-of-the-box.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250870/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a past window of W timesteps of 20 ms binned spike counts from an unordered set of N_s neural units (N_s varies across sessions), plus M-shot unlabeled calibration trials (variable-length, interpolated to a fixed T) used solely to infer unit identities. It outputs a B-dimensional behavior covariate vector (e.g., 7-DoF arm kinematics, 16-channel EMG, or 2-D actuator velocities) at the last timestep of the window, operating in a causal, online decoding manner. The architecture natively accepts any number of units in any order without session-specific alignment layers.

## Method

SPINT is a single-layer cross-attention transformer that treats each neural unit's temporal window as an unordered token. A DeepSets-style IDEncoder (φ → mean-pool over M trials → ψ) infers a context-dependent identity embedding E_i from unlabeled calibration trials; this embedding is equivariant to unit ordering, so adding it to the activity windows (Z = X + E) preserves the permutation-invariance of the cross-attention mechanism while retaining unit-specific functional information. A learnable query matrix Q (B×W) attends over Z to produce the behavior prediction, trained end-to-end with MSE on labeled held-in sessions. Dynamic channel dropout—uniformly sampling a dropout rate from [0,1] each iteration—regularizes against overfitting to a specific population composition. At test time, only the IDEncoder is reused on a few unlabeled calibration trials to compute new identities; no gradient updates, alignment layers, or test-time labels are required.

## Results

SPINT achieves cross-session R² of 0.66±0.07 on M1, 0.26±0.13 on M2, and 0.29±0.15 on H1, outperforming all zero-shot and few-shot unsupervised baselines across all three FALCON Benchmark datasets. Notably, SPINT surpasses the Wiener Filter oracle (trained with private labeled data) on all datasets (0.66 vs. 0.53 on M1, 0.26 vs. 0.26 on M2, 0.29 vs. 0.21 on H1) and even outperforms the few-shot supervised NDT2 Multi on M1 (0.66 vs. 0.59) without requiring test-time labels or gradient updates. Inference latency is 0.13–0.14 across datasets, well below the real-time threshold of 1.0.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPINT outperforms both zero-shot baselines across all datasets: the Wiener Filter ZS (0.34/0.06/0.16) and RNN ZS (−0.60/−0.07/0.09) are substantially below SPINT (0.66/0.26/0.29) on M1/M2/H1 respectively. Among few-shot unsupervised methods, SPINT beats CycleGAN+WF (0.43/0.22/0.12) by 0.23/0.04/0.17 R² and NoMAD+WF (0.49/0.20/0.13) by 0.17/0.06/0.16 R² on M1/M2/H1, while also avoiding the costly test-time alignment training that both FSU methods require. Against the few-shot supervised NDT2 Multi (0.59/0.43/0.52), SPINT wins on M1 (0.66 vs. 0.59) but trails on M2 and H1, though SPINT does so without any test-time labels or gradient updates. SPINT remains below the oracle models that train directly on private held-out labeled data (RNN OR: 0.75/0.56/0.44; NDT2 Multi OR: 0.78/0.58/0.63), which is expected given the fundamentally different evaluation setting. The largest relative gain is on M1, where the most training data is available (~5× more than H1), suggesting SPINT benefits from multi-session pretraining scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

SPINT departs from the dominant paradigm in iBCI decoding that treats neural populations as fixed-dimensional, ordered vectors requiring explicit cross-session alignment (CCA, GAN-based, or latent-dynamics methods like NoMAD). Instead, it repurposes the DeepSets permutation-invariant architecture (Zaheer et al., 2017) to infer unit identities on-the-fly from unlabeled calibration trials, and combines this with a single cross-attention layer to decode behavior from an unordered, variable-sized set of units. This positions SPINT as a new architectural class (gradient-free few-shot unsupervised) that sidesteps the alignment-and-fine-tuning pipeline entirely, while drawing on transformer cross-attention and set-structured input literature for its core mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The gradient-free, permutation-invariant design directly addresses the practical bottleneck of long-term iBCI deployment: eliminating per-session recalibration with labels and gradient updates. However, the evidence is confined to a single benchmark (FALCON), one subject per dataset, and in-silico evaluation; the end-to-end training of the ID encoder still requires behavioral labels, and the modest absolute R² on M2 (0.26) and H1 (0.29) leaves room for improvement. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes across subjects, tasks, and brain regions, whether self-supervised ID learning can remove the label dependency, and whether closed-loop in-vivo deployment preserves the observed performance.
