## Problem

Current 3D Gaussian Splatting methods for urban scene reconstruction depend on LiDAR point clouds for geometric initialization and depth supervision, but LiDAR acquisition suffers from costly equipment requirements, demanding spatiotemporal calibration, and reprojection errors from sensor misalignment. Moreover, LiDAR provides only sparse depth, limiting fine-grained geometric detail. The paper asks whether image-only inputs can yield geometry as accurate or better than LiDAR-supervised reconstruction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251905/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes multi-view RGB images at 640×960 resolution from three frontal cameras (FRONT, FRONT LEFT, FRONT RIGHT) along with known camera poses, and requires no LiDAR input. It produces a compact 3D Gaussian scene representation (~2M Gaussians after progressive pruning) organized as a scene graph with static/dynamic decomposition and a dedicated road node, from which dense per-pixel metric depth maps and photorealistic novel views can be rendered in real time.

## Method

D²GS replaces LiDAR with three image-derived geometric mechanisms. First, a Progressive Pruning strategy back-projects multi-view metric depths from DepthSplat into a ~100M-point cloud, then iteratively trains at low resolution and prunes low-opacity Gaussians to retain a compact ~2M Gaussian set capturing global geometry. Second, a diffusion-based Depth Enhancer (built on Marigold's generative depth prior) iteratively refines the depth rendered by the current Gaussians using classifier-free guidance conditioned on high-confidence regions, with three losses—confidence-weighted reference loss, multi-view warping loss, and edge-aware smoothness loss—enforcing geometric consistency; this refined depth then supervises Gaussian training in a real-time update loop. Third, a Road Node in the scene graph constrains road Gaussians via a z-height position loss and a planar shape/normal loss to enforce the strong ground-plane prior. The full training runs 60k iterations (40k RGB-only, then 20k joint Gaussian–depth refinement) on a single GPU.

## Results

On the Waymo NOTR Dynamic32 dataset, D²GS achieves 34.34 PSNR / 0.950 SSIM / 0.074 LPIPS for image reconstruction and 28.80 PSNR / 0.867 SSIM / 0.129 LPIPS for novel view synthesis, using only camera inputs (no LiDAR). For depth estimation (evaluated against sparse LiDAR ground truth), D²GS attains L1 = 2.60, Abs.Rel. = 0.131, RMSE = 4.67, and δ<1.25 = 0.840, substantially outperforming all LiDAR-free baselines. Notably, D²GS matches or exceeds LiDAR-supervised methods (e.g., OmniRe at 33.35 PSNR, PVG at 34.25 PSNR) on image reconstruction while being competitive on NVS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

D²GS outperforms all LiDAR-supervised baselines on image reconstruction PSNR (34.34 vs. 34.25 for PVG, 33.35 for OmniRe, 31.35 for S3GS) while using no LiDAR input. On novel view synthesis, D²GS (28.80 PSNR) is slightly behind OmniRe (29.02 PSNR) and PVG (28.83 PSNR) but ahead of S3GS (27.44) and 3DGS (25.14). Among LiDAR-free methods, D²GS dominates: it improves NVS PSNR by 1.11 dB over OmniRe w/o LiDAR (28.80 vs. 27.69) and 1.43 dB over OmniRe+DS (28.80 vs. 27.37). On depth estimation, D²GS achieves L1 = 2.60, a 38% reduction over OmniRe w/o LiDAR (4.22) and a 53% reduction over OmniRe+DS (5.53), while DepthSplat alone (the source of initial depth estimates) yields L1 = 8.71. The OmniRe+DS result is particularly informative: naively applying DepthSplat's noisy depth as supervision actually degrades both image quality (31.26 PSNR) and depth accuracy relative to using no depth at all, validating the paper's argument that iterative joint refinement is essential.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 3D Gaussian Splatting urban reconstruction line (OmniRe, S3GS, PVG, StreetGaussians) by removing the near-universal dependency on LiDAR point clouds for geometric initialization and depth supervision. It repurposes DepthSplat's multi-view metric depth for dense point-cloud seeding (managed via a novel progressive pruning strategy) and introduces a diffusion-based Depth Enhancer that borrows Marigold's generative depth prior and Marigold-DC's classifier-free guidance mechanism, but conditions the diffusion on Gaussian-rendered depth rather than sparse ground-truth. The Road Node with planar/normal constraints follows AutoSplat and DESIRE-GS, while the scene-graph decomposition of dynamic objects is inherited directly from OmniRe.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Eliminating LiDAR removes a major practical bottleneck in autonomous-driving data collection (costly sensors, demanding spatiotemporal calibration, reprojection errors from sensor misalignment), and the paper demonstrates that the resulting depth accuracy can match or exceed LiDAR-supervised baselines on Waymo. The iterative depth–Gaussian co-optimization with diffusion priors is a generalizable paradigm that could transfer to other LiDAR-free or sparse-sensor reconstruction settings. Key limitations include the continued reliance on accurate camera poses, evaluation on a single well-calibrated dataset (Waymo NOTR Dynamic32), and the computational overhead of the diffusion-based refinement loop (80 diffusion steps every 20 training iterations).
