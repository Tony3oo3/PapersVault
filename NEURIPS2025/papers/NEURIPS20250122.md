## Problem

No prior work addresses the generation of animated QR codes that are simultaneously visually appealing and reliably scannable. Existing static QR code generation methods, when applied frame-by-frame to video, produce temporally incoherent and aesthetically rigid animations due to the absence of inter-frame modeling and adaptive spatiotemporal control.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250122/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a target QR code (version 5, 37×37 binary modules) and a text prompt describing the desired visual style as conditioning inputs, and produces a 16-frame animated QR code video at 512×512 resolution per frame. The internal control signal is a 3-channel ternary HLG map (values −1, 0, +1) derived from the QR code's module luminance at three spatial scales, which is progressively modulated during inference. A static variant (AnimateQR-s) disables AnimateDiff and reduces the keyframe set to a single frame, yielding a single stylized QR image at 1024×1024.

## Method

AnimateQR introduces two core mechanisms built on Stable Diffusion v1.5 + AnimateDiff. First, Hierarchical Luminance Guidance (HLG) constructs a multi-scale ternary control map using three central masks of decreasing radius per QR module, with a per-module activation vector β_k drawn from 8 ordered basis patterns that selectively enable or disable each scale; an HLG-ControlNet is trained end-to-end on 60k natural images paired with their HLG maps, with β_k uniformly sampled during training to ensure robustness to varying control strengths. Second, Progressive Spatiotemporal Control (ProST) operates during DDIM inference by decoding the intermediate latent at each of S stages, computing a module-wise error mask against the target QR, and adaptively escalating β_k for erroneous modules while relaxing it for correct ones, thereby concentrating control where decoding is failing and freeing aesthetic expression elsewhere. Keyframes (default frames 1, 8, 16) receive reshuffled QR module assignments via QArt, while non-keyframes are interpolated for temporal coherence. A final SELR latent-refinement step applies gradient-based scannability optimization to the denoised latent before VAE decoding.

## Results

On static QR code generation (Table 3), AnimateQR-s achieves the best scores across all three aesthetic metrics: Q-Align 0.8433, LIQE 3.8572, and AesBench 0.8832, outperforming the strongest baseline QRBTF (0.7877 / 3.5447 / 0.7822). For animated QR code generation (Table 2), AnimateQR scores Q-Bench 0.6217 and SimpleVQA 3.5872, roughly doubling the Q-Bench of ArtCoder-d (0.2954) and GladCoder-d (0.2453), while also being 10–38× faster in inference speed (0.613 vs. 0.031 and 0.016 samples/s). In real-world scanning tests (Table 1), AnimateQR maintains ≥96% success rate across all sizes (3–7 cm²), angles (45°/90°), and scanner apps, compared to QRBTF dropping to 56% at 7 cm²/45° on TikTok.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AnimateQR-s outperforms all four static baselines on every aesthetic metric. Against the closest competitor QRBTF [22], AnimateQR-s gains +0.056 on Q-Align, +0.31 on LIQE, and +0.10 on AesBench; against Text2QR [34] the margins are +0.065, +0.35, and +0.12 respectively. ArtCoder [31] and GladCoder [39] trail substantially, with ArtCoder scoring only 0.6003 on Q-Align versus 0.8433 for AnimateQR-s. In the animated setting (Table 2), AnimateQR more than doubles the Q-Bench score of both ArtCoder-d (0.2954) and GladCoder-d (0.2453), and is 10–38× faster in inference. In scanning robustness (Tables 1, 4–6), AnimateQR consistently matches or exceeds Text2QR-d and QRBTF-d, with the largest gap appearing under low illumination (5 LUX: 8% vs. 2% and 0%) and at 7 cm²/45° on TikTok (96% vs. 56% for QRBTF). The user study further confirms a strong preference for AnimateQR (7.2/10) over the baselines (2.3/10).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the static aesthetic QR code generation line (ArtCoder, GladCoder, QRBTF, Text2QR) into the video domain by combining AnimateDiff for temporal coherence with a novel Hierarchical Luminance Guidance (HLG) ControlNet for spatial luminance control. The HLG signal departs from prior QR-conditioned ControlNets (e.g., QRMonster) by encoding multi-scale, ternary-quantized luminance constraints aligned to QR module boundaries, while the Progressive Spatiotemporal Control (ProST) mechanism introduces error-adaptive, per-module control strength escalation during DDIM denoising—a strategy absent from prior static methods that apply uniform control throughout inference. The module reshuffling from QArt and the SELR latent refinement from Text2QR are adopted as auxiliary components, but the core contribution is the hierarchical, progressively adapted control paradigm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AnimateQR opens a previously unexplored application space—temporally coherent, scannable animated QR codes for dynamic branding and interactive content delivery—and demonstrates that image-based ControlNet combined with AnimateDiff can handle video-level constraint-aware generation without training a dedicated video ControlNet. The hierarchical luminance guidance and error-adaptive progressive control are general principles that could transfer to other scannable media (AR markers, barcodes) or any generation task requiring spatially heterogeneous constraint enforcement. Key limitations include high computational cost for high-resolution animations and residual decoding failures on legacy scanners, while the weak frame-by-frame baselines leave open whether the large reported gaps would persist against a purpose-built video QR code method.
