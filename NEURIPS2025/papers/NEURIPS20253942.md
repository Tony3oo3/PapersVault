## Problem

In query-based topology reasoning for autonomous driving, lane endpoints are implicitly attached to independently supervised lane queries, causing them to fail to coincide at connection points (the endpoint deviation problem). This leads to incorrect lane-lane and lane-traffic topology construction. Prior work either works around the deviation (e.g., TopoLogic uses geometric distance to reduce its impact) or relies on suboptimal lane detection, leaving the root cause unaddressed.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view surround-view images (7 cameras on subset_A, 6 on subset_B) at 2048×1550 resolution; multi-view inputs are downsampled by 0.5 while the front-view is kept at full resolution for traffic element detection. Beyond standard lane centerlines (11-point 3D polylines) and 2D traffic element bounding boxes, the model explicitly outputs a set of deduplicated 3D endpoint points and a point-lane adjacency matrix, in addition to the lane-lane and lane-traffic topology matrices. At inference, a geometry matching step post-processes the raw predictions to snap nearby lane endpoints to detected points, producing refined lane centerlines.

## Method

TopoPoint introduces explicit endpoint detection by independently initializing 200 point queries alongside 300 lane queries, each supervised with separate classification and regression objectives. During training, a Point-Lane Merge Self-Attention module concatenates both query sets and injects pairwise geometric distances (computed from previous-layer predictions) as a learnable attention bias, enabling global context sharing between points and lanes. A Point-Lane Graph Convolutional Network (PLGCN) then performs bidirectional message passing between point and lane features using an adjacency matrix that fuses predicted topology with geometric bias, embedded within a Unified Scene Graph Network that also aggregates traffic element features via additional GCNs. At inference, the Point-Lane Geometry Matching (PLGM) algorithm filters high-confidence points and lanes, identifies nearby lane endpoints within a 1.5 m threshold, and replaces them with the averaged position of the matched point and its neighboring endpoints, directly eliminating endpoint deviation. The full pipeline is trained end-to-end with focal loss for classification, L1 loss for regression, GIoU loss for traffic boxes, and focal loss for all three topology matrices, on a ResNet-50 + FPN backbone with BevFormer BEV encoding and Deformable DETR for traffic detection.

## Results

On the OpenLane-V2 subset_A benchmark, TopoPoint achieves a state-of-the-art OpenLane-V2 Score (OLS) of 48.8, surpassing the previous best TopoFormer* (46.3) by 2.5 points despite not using a pretrained lane detector. TopoPoint leads in traffic element detection (DET_t = 55.3), lane-lane topology (TOP_ll = 28.7), and lane-traffic topology (TOP_lt = 30.0), while TopoFormer* retains an edge in raw lane detection (DET_l = 34.7 vs. 31.4). On the newly proposed endpoint detection metric DET_p, TopoPoint scores 52.6, substantially outperforming TopoLogic (45.2) and TopoNet (43.8). On subset_B, TopoPoint also achieves SOTA with OLS = 49.2 and DET_p = 45.1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TopoPoint outperforms all seven baselines on the overall OLS metric on subset_A (48.8), with the closest competitor being TopoFormer* (46.3), which leverages a pretrained lane detector. Against its direct predecessor TopoLogic (44.1), TopoPoint gains +4.7 OLS, driven by large improvements in DET_t (+8.1), TOP_ll (+4.8), and TOP_lt (+4.6), while also improving DET_l from 29.9 to 31.4. The only metric where TopoPoint trails a baseline is DET_l, where TopoFormer* scores 34.7 vs. 31.4, reflecting the advantage of its pretrained lane detector. On the new DET_p endpoint detection metric, TopoPoint leads all methods that report this score (52.6 vs. 45.2 for TopoLogic, 43.8 for TopoNet, 43.4 for TopoMLP). On subset_B, TopoPoint similarly achieves the best OLS (49.2 vs. 47.5 for TopoFormer*), confirming consistent gains across both evaluation splits.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on TopoLogic (NeurIPS 2024, same first author), which introduced geometric attention bias for lane topology reasoning but left the endpoint deviation problem unaddressed. TopoPoint departs from the standard query-based topology reasoning pipeline (shared by STSU, TopoNet, TopoMLP, TopoFormer) by decoupling endpoint detection from lane detection: instead of treating endpoints as implicit attributes of lane queries, it introduces an independent point query branch supervised by its own detection loss. The architectural choices—Deformable DETR for cross-attention, BevFormer for BEV encoding, and GCN-based feature aggregation—follow established patterns from the 3D lane detection and scene graph literature, while the Point-Lane Merge Self-Attention and Point-Lane Geometry Matching are the paper's novel contributions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The endpoint deviation problem is a practical bottleneck in topology construction for autonomous driving: even small positional errors at lane junctions can produce incorrect connectivity graphs that mislead downstream planning. By introducing explicit endpoint detection and a post-hoc geometry matching step, TopoPoint offers a relatively lightweight fix that improves both detection and topology metrics simultaneously. The proposed DET_p metric gives the community a dedicated way to evaluate endpoint quality, which was previously unmeasured. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to denser urban scenes with more complex junctions, whether the additional 200 point queries and two GCN layers introduce prohibitive latency for real-time deployment, and whether the post-hoc matching step can be replaced by a fully learned mechanism.
