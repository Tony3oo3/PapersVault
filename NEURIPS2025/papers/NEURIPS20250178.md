## Problem

Estimating HDR environment maps from casually captured images or videos remains difficult because ground-truth HDR supervision is scarce, lighting cues are indirect and non-local, and high-dynamic-range outputs exceed the representational capacity of standard LDR-trained encoders. Prior generative approaches like DiffusionLight rely on expensive test-time ensembles and multi-exposure inpainting that limit dynamic range and introduce temporal inconsistencies.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250178/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts a single RGB image or a short video clip (resolutions 512×512 to 480×720, up to 25 frames) and produces a 360° HDR environment map panorama (128×256 to 256×512). The HDR output is internally represented as two complementary tone-mapped LDR images (Reinhard and log-intensity) that are fused by a per-pixel MLP at inference time, and a per-pixel directional map in camera coordinates is injected as auxiliary conditioning to enforce angular continuity.

## Method

LuxDiT fine-tunes the CogVideoX video diffusion transformer to conditionally denoise dual tone-mapped environment map latents, with the input video encoded as separate condition tokens processed through shared self-attention layers rather than channel concatenation (which the ablations show degrades performance substantially). A directional embedding of unit vectors is concatenated to the noise tokens to improve angular continuity, and separate AdaLN modules distinguish condition from target tokens at each transformer block. Training proceeds in two stages: first, supervised training on ~260,000 synthetic video clips rendered from randomized Objaverse scenes with ground-truth HDR lighting to learn physically grounded shading cues; second, LoRA fine-tuning (rank 64) on perspective crops of 2,386 real HDR panoramas and LDR panoramic videos to align predicted lighting with scene semantics. The dual tone-mapping strategy (Reinhard with M=16 and log with M=10,000) allows the LDR-trained VAE to faithfully encode HDR content spanning radiance values up to 10,000.

## Results

On the Poly Haven benchmark, LuxDiT achieves the best performance across all three metrics: 0.077/0.196/0.442 si-RMSE, 1.235°/1.977°/2.783° angular error, and 0.111/0.199/0.323 n-RMSE (Diffuse/Matte/Mirror spheres), outperforming DiffusionLight by 32% in si-RMSE and 43% in angular error. On Laval Outdoor, LuxDiT reduces peak angular error for sunlight direction to 23.7° mean (vs. 44.4° for DiffusionLight), a ~47% improvement. On Laval Indoor, LuxDiT matches or slightly exceeds DiffusionLight on si-RMSE (0.112 vs. 0.124) and n-RMSE (0.196 vs. 0.216) despite not being trained on that dataset, demonstrating strong out-of-domain generalization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DiffusionLight [45], the closest generative baseline, LuxDiT wins on si-RMSE and n-RMSE across all three datasets (e.g., 0.068 vs. 0.083 on Laval Outdoor, 0.077 vs. 0.113 on Poly Haven) and achieves substantially lower angular error on Poly Haven (1.235° vs. 2.199° diffuse), while being marginally worse on angular error for Laval Indoor diffuse (2.555° vs. 2.500°). Against NLFE [58], LuxDiT dominates on Laval Outdoor (0.068 vs. 0.112 si-RMSE) and Poly Haven (0.077 vs. 0.159 si-RMSE, 1.235° vs. 3.305° AE). Against StyleLight [55], LuxDiT is consistently superior on outdoor and Poly Haven scenes but comparable on Laval Indoor. On the virtual object insertion task (Table 8), LuxDiT achieves 0.047 RMSE and 0.990 SSIM, marginally beating DiPIR [36] (0.048/0.989) and clearly outperforming StyleLight and DiffusionLight (0.056–0.057 RMSE); user study shows 54.5–60.6% preference for LuxDiT over each baseline. On the array-of-spheres protocol (Table 9), LuxDiT (0.089 si-RMSE, 4.90° AE) beats StyleLight and DiffusionLight but trails the specialized Weber et al. [61] (0.081/4.13°) and EMLight [69] (0.099/3.99°). On Objects with Lighting [53], LuxDiT achieves much lower angular error than NeuS+Mitsuba [56] (1.26° vs. 3.15° diffuse) at a slight si-RMSE cost (0.086 vs. 0.082).

## Relation to Prior Work

LuxDiT repurposes the CogVideoX video diffusion transformer for lighting estimation, departing from prior regression-based methods (spherical lobes, parametric sources) and GAN-based generators (StyleLight, EverLight) by formulating HDR environment map prediction as a conditional denoising task. It also moves beyond DiffusionLight's multi-exposure chrome-ball inpainting strategy, which requires expensive test-time ensembles and suffers from limited dynamic range, by directly generating dual tone-mapped HDR representations in a single forward pass. The token-based attention conditioning (rather than channel concatenation) and the two-stage training pipeline—synthetic pretraining followed by LoRA adaptation on real HDR panoramas—represent the key architectural and training departures from standard image-to-image diffusion fine-tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LuxDiT addresses the fundamental chicken-and-egg problem in lighting estimation (needing expensive HDR captures to train models that eliminate the need for them) through a large-scale synthetic data strategy, and demonstrates that video diffusion transformers can be effectively repurposed for non-pixel-aligned, globally-reasoning generative tasks. The 45% reduction in peak angular error on outdoor sunlight direction and the strong temporal consistency for video input make it practically useful for virtual object insertion, AR/VR, and synthetic data generation. Key limitations remain: iterative diffusion inference is computationally expensive (limiting real-time use), output resolution is constrained by training data scale, and the method estimates lighting in isolation without jointly recovering geometry or materials—open directions the authors identify toward unified inverse/forward rendering frameworks.
