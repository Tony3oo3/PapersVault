## Problem

Existing preference optimization methods for LLM alignment rely on sigmoid-based gradient attenuation (DPO, SimPO) or explicit SFT regularization (SLiC-HF) to prevent over-optimization, yet it remains unclear whether these components are fundamentally necessary. This paper asks whether a simple ReLU activation with a single threshold parameter can replace both mechanisms while maintaining competitive alignment performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251390/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes offline preference triplets (prompt x, chosen response y_w, rejected response y_l) and computes a length-normalized implicit reward margin M_θ = (1/|y_w|)log π_θ(y_w|x) − (1/|y_l|)log π_θ(y_l|x) as its sole signal. The only tunable hyperparameter is γ ∈ [0,1], which sets the target margin threshold; no reference model, reward model, or regularization coefficient is required. The output is a fine-tuned policy model (Llama3-8B, Gemma2-9B, Mistral-7B) evaluated on open-ended generation benchmarks (AlpacaEval 2, Arena-Hard) and downstream tasks.

## Method

RePO replaces SimPO's log-sigmoid loss with a ReLU hinge: L_RePO = E[ReLU(−(M_θ − γ))], which applies uniform gradient updates only to pairs where M_θ < γ and zero gradient to well-separated pairs. The paper proves (Lemma 3.1) that RePO is the pointwise limit of SimPO as β→∞, and (Theorem 4.2) that the ReLU loss is exactly the convex envelope of the 0-1 classification loss over [−a,b], establishing optimality guarantees that logistic loss lacks (Corollary 4.4). The γ threshold induces an emergent curriculum: as training progresses, the fraction of filtered (zero-gradient) samples rises from ~13% to ~58%, dynamically focusing updates on increasingly challenging pairs. An extension, RePO++, reintroduces continuous sigmoid weighting within the ReLU-filtered region (L = −E[log σ(−ReLU(−β(M_θ−γ)))]) and can be plugged into DPO or SimPO to yield 5–12% improvements.

## Results

RePO achieves the best or near-best results across all four model settings on AlpacaEval 2 and Arena-Hard. On Llama3-Instruct v0.2 (8B), RePO reaches 57.7% LC Win Rate and 51.1% Raw Win Rate on AlpacaEval 2, and 35.2% on Arena-Hard, outperforming SimPO (55.6/49.6/33.6) and DPO (48.2/47.5/35.2). On Gemma2-9B, RePO achieves 73.6% LC and 66.6% WR on AlpacaEval 2 and 59.1% on Arena-Hard, leading all baselines. The method uses only a single hyperparameter γ (default 0.5) and no reference model, no SFT regularization, and no sigmoid weighting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RePO consistently outperforms SimPO (the closest competitor) across all four model settings, with gains of +2.9 LC on Llama3-8B, +0.2 LC on Mistral-7B, +2.1 LC on Llama3-v0.2-8B, and +1.2 LC on Gemma2-9B. Against DPO, RePO achieves substantially larger margins: +6.5 LC on Llama3-8B, +10.1 LC on Mistral-7B, +9.5 LC on Llama3-v0.2-8B, and +3.2 LC on Gemma2-9B. RePO also surpasses R-DPO (the second-strongest baseline) by +6.4 LC on Llama3-8B and +9.1 LC on Llama3-v0.2-8B, while matching R-DPO on Arena-Hard for Llama3-v0.2 (35.2 vs. 35.0). The gap over SLiC-HF is the largest, with RePO exceeding it by +19.8 LC on Llama3-8B and +8.6 LC on Llama3-v0.2-8B, confirming that the SFT regularization term in SLiC-HF is unnecessary when ReLU thresholding is present. On Arena-Hard, RePO leads all baselines on Llama3-8B (33.3 vs. 32.9 for R-DPO) and Gemma2-9B (59.1 vs. 58.8 for DPO), though it ties with DPO on Llama3-v0.2-8B (35.2).

## Relation to Prior Work

RePO is positioned as a conceptual hub connecting SimPO and SLiC-HF: it is 'SimPO without log-sigmoid' (the β→∞ limit of SimPO's sigmoid weighting becomes binary ReLU thresholding) and 'SLiC-HF without SFT regularization' (retaining the hinge-loss structure but dropping the λ·log π_θ(y_w|x) anchor term). The paper's theoretical contribution—showing that ReLU(-x) is the convex envelope of the 0-1 loss over a bounded interval—provides a principled justification for why this minimal mechanism suffices, a connection not previously drawn in the preference-optimization literature. Unlike DPO, which implicitly defines its margin through reference-model log-ratios, RePO uses an explicit length-normalized margin γ, aligning it more closely with SimPO's design philosophy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper challenges the field's working assumption that sigmoid-based gradient attenuation or explicit KL/SFT regularization are necessary to prevent over-optimization, showing instead that a single ReLU threshold with one hyperparameter γ achieves competitive results across Llama3-8B, Gemma2-9B, and Mistral-7B. The emergent curriculum-learning behavior (progressive data filtering as margins grow during training) and the finding that SFT regularization actively degrades performance (Table 6) suggest that data selection, not gradient shaping, is the critical mechanism. Limitations include restriction to offline settings, a fixed γ schedule (though dynamic scheduling shows promise), and modest absolute gains (0.2–2.8 LC points) over strong baselines; open questions include extension to online/iterative alignment, adaptive or context-aware thresholds, and interaction with self-play and reasoning-focused RLHF (GRPO, PPO).
