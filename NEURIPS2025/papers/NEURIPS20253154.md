## Problem

Classical molecular sampling methods (MD, MCMC) lack amortization, requiring full simulation cost for every new system, while producing highly correlated samples that mix slowly across metastable states. Learned samplers such as Boltzmann generators shift cost to a one-time training phase but have so far demonstrated limited transferability: the prior state-of-the-art transferable Boltzmann generator (TBG) generalizes only across dipeptides and relies on continuous normalizing flows whose expensive divergence integration precludes efficient likelihood evaluation and fine-tuning. This paper asks whether a scalable, transferable learned sampler can be built that generalizes across peptide sequence length, amino acid composition, and temperature while retaining fast exact likelihood computation.

## Input / Output

PROSE takes a peptide system specified by its amino acid sequence (2–8 residues), atom types, residue positions, and sequence length as conditioning features, and produces all-atom 3D Cartesian coordinate conformations along with their exact proposal log-likelihoods under the Amber14 forcefield. Variable-length sequences are handled via zero-padding with a binary mask that excludes padding tokens from both the autoregressive affine updates and the log-determinant aggregation, so a single model serves all sequence lengths. At inference, the temperature can be shifted without retraining by scaling the prior distribution (scaled-prior technique), and the generated proposals are reweighted via self-normalized importance sampling against the target Boltzmann density.

## Method

PROSE is a 285M-parameter autoregressive normalizing flow built on the TarFlow architecture, where each transformation block is a transformer that defines an affine autoregressive update over atom-coordinate tokens. Three key modifications enable transferability: (i) variable-length masking with sinusoidal position embeddings so the flow handles sequences of 2–8 residues in a single model; (ii) adaptive system conditioning via adaptive layer normalization and SwiGLU transition blocks (borrowed from Proteina) that inject atom-type, residue-type, residue-position, and sequence-length embeddings into every transformer layer; and (iii) chemistry-aware backbone-first permutations that process all backbone atoms before sidechains, allowing sidechain updates to causally attend to the full global backbone structure. The model is trained by maximum likelihood on the newly introduced ManyPeptidesMD dataset (21,700 peptide sequences, 200 ns MD each, 4.3 ms total simulation). At inference, the efficient exact likelihood enables standard SNIS reweighting, discrete/continuous-time SMC, and a self-improvement loop that fine-tunes the flow on SNIS-resampled proposals from unseen systems without requiring true target samples.

## Results

With 10⁴ energy evaluations via SNIS, PROSE achieves E-W₂ of 0.371 (2AA), 0.932 (4AA), and 10.038 (8AA), and T-W₂ of 0.210, 0.752, and 2.456 respectively, outperforming all baselines on E-W₂ at every scale. On the macrostructure TICA-W₂ metric, PROSE reaches 0.367 (4AA) and 0.988 (8AA), surpassing the 1 µs MD baseline which fails to traverse separated metastable states within the same budget. PROSE is marginally outperformed only by unmodified TarFlow on dipeptide T-W₂ (0.210 vs. 0.193), while ECNF++ degrades severely after reweighting (E-W₂ jumps from 1.958 to 3.470 on 2AA). At a larger 10⁶ budget, SNIS with PROSE remains competitive with both SMC variants, and self-improvement further reduces 4AA E-W₂ to 0.568.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PROSE outperforms TarFlow (the unmodified baseline) on E-W₂ at all sequence lengths (0.371 vs. 0.452 on 2AA; 0.932 vs. 1.260 on 4AA; 10.038 vs. 11.298 on 8AA) and on T-W₂ at 4AA and 8AA, while TarFlow retains a marginal edge on 2AA T-W₂ (0.193 vs. 0.210). Against ECNF++ (the improved equivariant CNF), PROSE wins decisively on E-W₂ at every scale—ECNF++'s divergence-integration error causes its E-W₂ to nearly double after SNIS reweighting (e.g., 5.006→10.032 on 4AA)—and PROSE also leads on T-W₂ and TICA-W₂ for tetra- and octapeptides. TimeWarp, the strongest non-Boltzmann-generator baseline, is outperformed by PROSE on both E-W₂ (0.271 vs. 2.551 on 2AA at 10⁶ budget) and T-W₂ (0.165 vs. 0.580), with the gap widening at 4AA (0.665 vs. 4.125 E-W₂). BioEmu and UniSim attain substantially higher E-W₂ values than PROSE across all scales, though the authors note their training data does not exactly match the evaluation protocol. Finally, PROSE with SNIS surpasses a 1 µs MD trajectory on T-W₂ and TICA-W₂ at equivalent energy-evaluation budgets, demonstrating that the learned proposal captures metastable-state coverage that MD cannot reach within the same compute allocation.

## Relation to Prior Work

PROSE extends the TarFlow autoregressive normalizing flow architecture (Zhai et al., 2024) and its molecular application in SBG (Tan et al., 2025) by introducing chemistry-aware backbone-first permutations, adaptive conditioning via layer norm and SwiGLU transitions (borrowed from Proteina, Geffner et al., 2024), and variable-length masking to enable transfer across peptide sequence lengths. It departs from the prior transferable Boltzmann generator (TBG, Klein and Noe, 2024), which used an equivariant continuous normalizing flow requiring expensive ODE integration and divergence computation, by replacing it with a discrete autoregressive flow that retains efficient closed-form likelihood evaluation. The Boltzmann generator framework itself (Noe et al., 2019) — train a likelihood model, then apply SNIS at inference — is preserved as the sampling paradigm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PROSE is the first learned sampler to demonstrate transferability across peptide sequence length (2–8 residues) and composition, achieving a 4000× speedup over the prior transferable CNF-based method while outperforming MD at matched energy-evaluation budgets. The finding that simple SNIS is competitive with sequential Monte Carlo given a sufficiently strong proposal challenges the assumption that annealing-based samplers are necessary, and the self-improvement fine-tuning loop (resample → retrain without true target samples) offers a practical path to adapt to new systems. Key limitations include restriction to the peptide chemical space, training only up to 8 residues (extrapolation to 9–10 residues degrades significantly), and the assumption that the target system belongs to a structured energy-function family; extending to broader chemical spaces (e.g., OMol25) and lower-temperature transfer remain open.
