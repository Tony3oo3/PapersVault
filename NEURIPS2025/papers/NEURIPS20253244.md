## Problem

Energy-based OOD detection for GNNs computes scores from raw logits that entangle embedding norm with angular similarity, causing high-norm OOD nodes to receive deceptively low energy scores and be misclassified as in-distribution. While GNNs naturally exhibit angular clustering (same-class embeddings align in direction), this geometric structure is underexploited because the standard dot-product logit formulation conflates direction and magnitude, undermining reliable ID-OOD separation.

## Input / Output

GeoEnergy operates as a plug-in logit head on top of any pre-trained GNN encoder (GCN, GAT, GraphSAGE), consuming the final node embedding h_i and the classification weight matrix W. It produces a reshaped energy score E_angular(x_i) = -log Σ_j exp(s·||h_i||·cos(θ_j,i)) that serves as the OOD detection signal, alongside calibrated confidence scores for ID nodes. The only tunable parameter is a scalar scaling factor s (typically 1.5–20 depending on dataset and OOD type), selected via validation; no retraining or architectural modification of the GNN backbone is required.

## Method

GeoEnergy reformulates the standard logit f_j = W_j^T h_i = ||W_j||·||h_i||·cos(θ_j,i) into f_j = s·||h_i||·cos(θ_j,i) by projecting each class weight onto the unit hypersphere (||W_j|| = 1), thereby decoupling angular similarity from weight magnitude while preserving the embedding norm as a confidence signal. The scaling factor s amplifies angular differences in logit space, increasing inter-class margins under a Lipschitz continuity assumption on the feature mapping. The resulting Helmholtz free energy E_angular(x_i) = -log Σ_j exp(s·||h_i||·cos(θ_j,i)) yields sharper intra-class alignment and pushes OOD nodes to higher-energy regions. The method is fully model-agnostic: it replaces only the logit computation and energy scoring function, and can be plugged into existing frameworks like GNNSafe, NodeSafe, and TopoOOD without modifying the graph encoder or training procedure.

## Results

On the Cora benchmark, NodeSafe+GeoEnergy achieves 95.21 AUROC / 85.32 AUPR / 22.46 FPR95 under structure shift, 96.76 / 88.90 / 21.54 under feature shift, and 96.11 / 86.46 / 23.77 under label shift, consistently outperforming all baselines. Compared to GNNSafe, GeoEnergy reduces FPR95 by 35% (structure), 25.7% (feature), and 36.1% (label) while improving AUROC by 3.3–2.8 points. On calibration (ECE, M=20), GeoEnergy achieves the lowest error across all GNN architectures and datasets, e.g., 0.0361 on Cora-GCN versus 0.0394 (GCL) and 0.0401 (CaGCN). On the multi-graph Twitch dataset, NodeSafe++(w/ GeoEnergy) reaches 98.87 AUROC with only 3.11 FPR95, and on ogbn-Arxiv it attains 77.21 AUROC / 72.18 FPR95.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GeoEnergy (plugged into GNNSafe or NodeSafe) outperforms all 13 baselines across every OOD scenario and dataset. Against the strongest graph-specific baselines, NodeSafe+GeoEnergy improves AUROC by 1.14 points (95.21 vs 94.07) and FPR95 by 3.17 points (22.46 vs 25.63) over NodeSafe on Cora-Structure, and by 1.80 AUROC / 4.68 FPR95 over NodeSafe++ on Cora-Feature. Classical vision baselines (MSP, ODIN, Mahalanobis, Energy) are substantially weaker, with ODIN and Mahalanobis scoring near-chance AUROC (~50) on Cora-Structure, while GeoEnergy reaches 90.83–95.21. Among OOD-exposure methods, GeoEnergy-enhanced GNNSafe++ surpasses the base GNNSafe++ by 0.65 AUROC and 10.9 FPR95 on Cora-Structure, and GeoEnergy-enhanced NodeSafe++ leads NodeSafe++ by 0.58 AUROC and 1.45 FPR95. On calibration (ECE), GeoEnergy beats all four baselines (TS, MS, CaGCN, GCL) on every dataset–architecture combination, e.g., 0.0361 vs 0.0394 (GCL) on Cora-GCN and 0.0259 vs 0.0364 (CaGCN) on Pubmed-GraphSAGE.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the energy-based OOD detection framework for GNNs established by GNNSafe (Wu et al., ICLR 2022), which itself adapts the Helmholtz free energy scoring function from Liu et al. (NeurIPS 2020) to graph-structured data. The key conceptual departure is a geometric diagnosis: the standard inner-product logit formulation f_j = W_j^T h_i entangles the embedding norm with angular similarity, and in graph settings—where norms fluctuate due to degree heterogeneity and stochastic message passing—this coupling causes systematic misdetection of high-norm OOD nodes. GeoEnergy resolves this by projecting class weights onto a unit hypersphere (analogous to angular softmax in face recognition), decoupling direction from magnitude while retaining the embedding norm as a confidence signal. The method is explicitly positioned as a plug-and-play logit-head modification rather than a new architecture, and it is validated by integration into both GNNSafe and NodeSafe without retraining.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play nature (no retraining, no architectural change) makes GeoEnergy immediately applicable to existing energy-based GNN pipelines, and the consistent FPR95 reductions (25–36% over GNNSafe) across seven datasets and three OOD perturbation types suggest practical value for safety-critical graph deployments. The geometric diagnosis of coupling-induced misdetection provides a useful conceptual lens that could inform other logit-based scoring functions beyond energy. However, the core operation—normalizing class weights to unit norm—is a well-known technique (cosine similarity / angular softmax), and the theoretical propositions are informal rather than rigorous bounds. Open questions include whether the geometric decoupling transfers to non-energy OOD methods (e.g., Bayesian or generative), whether the per-dataset scaling factor s can be learned automatically, and whether the benefits persist under adversarial or compositional distribution shifts beyond the synthetic perturbations tested here.
