## Problem

In personalized federated learning under non-IID data, the global model's poor local performance stems primarily from a mismatch between client-specific features and the shared classifier, rather than from weak individual components. Existing PFL methods either personalize parameters (degrading the shared feature extractor) or fine-tune the classifier only post-training (letting mismatch persist during training and exacerbating inter-client gradient divergence). No prior approach resolves this mismatch throughout the training process while preserving feature extractor quality.

## Input / Output

Each client processes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 64×64 for Tiny ImageNet, variable resolution for PACS/DomainNet) through a shared ResNet feature extractor, producing a fixed-dimensional feature vector. The FTM then transforms this feature using client-specific prompts via self-attention before passing it to the classifier, which outputs a class label. The personalized prompts (n_κ vectors of dimension m) remain local to each client and are never uploaded, while the feature extractor, FTM, and classifier are aggregated server-side via FedAvg.

## Method

FedPFT inserts a prompt-driven Feature Transformation Module (FTM) between the shared feature extractor and classifier, where the FTM concatenates n personalized prompt vectors with the local feature vector and applies self-attention to produce a transformed feature aligned with the global classifier. The key training innovation is a dual-phase alternating strategy within each local round: in the feature transformation phase (R_f epochs), the feature extractor and classifier are frozen while prompts and FTM are optimized to resolve the mismatch; in the model training phase (R_a epochs), prompts are frozen and all model parameters are updated to learn local knowledge. This ensures the mismatch is corrected before model parameters are adjusted, reducing inter-client gradient divergence during aggregation. The FTM's prompt-driven design also provides task flexibility: additional task-specific prompts (e.g., for contrastive learning via MoCo) can be introduced to incorporate auxiliary objectives without architectural changes, yielding FedPFT+Con.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (Dirichlet α=0.5), FedPFT achieves 74.10% test accuracy, outperforming the best baseline FedRoD (72.34%) by 1.76%. On CIFAR-100 (α=0.5), FedPFT reaches 44.87%, surpassing pFedSD (41.06%) by 3.81%. On Tiny ImageNet (α=1.0), FedPFT attains 25.10%, exceeding pFedPT (21.69%) by 3.41%. With collaborative contrastive learning (FedPFT+Con), gains increase to up to 7.08% over SOTA, e.g., 47.98% on CIFAR-100 (α=0.5) vs. 41.06% for pFedSD.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedPFT wins on 8 of 9 Dirichlet non-IID settings across three datasets, with the sole near-tie being CIFAR-10 α=0.1 where pFedGate edges ahead by 0.02% (87.25% vs. 87.23%). The largest margins appear on CIFAR-100 (α=0.5: +3.81% over pFedSD; α=1.0: +3.56% over pFedSD) and Tiny ImageNet (α=0.5: +2.98% over FedRoD; α=1.0: +3.41% over pFedPT). FedRoD is the strongest single baseline overall, benefiting from its dual-classifier design, but FedPFT consistently surpasses it by 0.4–3.0% across all settings. Parameter-decoupling methods (FedPer, FedRep, FedBN, FedCAC) trail FedPFT by 3–10% on CIFAR-100 and Tiny ImageNet, confirming the paper's argument that partial personalization degrades the feature extractor. The prompt-based pFedPT, which applies prompts post-training, underperforms FedPFT by 3–5% on harder settings, validating that training-time alignment is critical rather than post-hoc adaptation.

## Relation to Prior Work

FedPFT builds on the FedAvg aggregation framework and the MoCo contrastive learning paradigm, but its core conceptual departure is from the dominant PFL paradigm of parameter decoupling (FedPer, FedBN, FedRoD, FedCAC) and post-training classifier fine-tuning (FedBABU, FedETF). Rather than assuming certain model components are inherently difficult to train collaboratively, the paper reframes the problem as a feature-classifier mismatch that can be resolved during training via a prompt-driven self-attention transformation module. This positions FedPFT alongside prompt-tuning-based PFL methods (pFedPT, pFedPG) but distinguishes itself by using prompts as a training-time alignment mechanism rather than a post-training adaptation tool.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of feature-classifier mismatch as the primary bottleneck in PFL provides a unifying lens through which to understand why diverse personalization strategies (classifier, BN, feature extractor) all yield comparable gains — they all indirectly alleviate the same mismatch. The dual-phase alternating optimization and the FTM's flexibility to incorporate auxiliary tasks (e.g., contrastive learning) offer a practical recipe for PFL practitioners. Open questions include whether the mismatch framing generalizes to non-vision tasks or transformer-based backbones, whether the ~20% communication overhead is acceptable in bandwidth-constrained settings, and whether the self-attention FTM is strictly necessary or whether lighter transformations suffice for simpler datasets.
