## Problem

Adversarial robustness research for GNNs has focused almost exclusively on the message-passing component and node classification, leaving the role of pooling operations in graph-level robustness unexplored. Standard flat pooling methods (sum, average, max) aggregate node embeddings into graph representations in ways that can amplify or localize adversarial perturbations, but no systematic theoretical analysis has quantified how each pooling choice affects expected adversarial risk in graph classification.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254666/cropped_page8_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a graph represented by its adjacency matrix A ∈ R^{n×n} and node feature matrix X ∈ R^{n×d}, processes them through L layers of a GNN backbone (GCN or GIN), and produces a d-dimensional graph-level embedding via RS-Pool. The embedding is a scaled version of the dominant right singular vector of the final node representation matrix H ∈ R^{n×d}, computed efficiently via K=2–5 power iteration steps rather than full SVD. A tunable scaling factor τ (parameterized as σ₁/α) controls the output magnitude and directly modulates the robustness bound.

## Method

RS-Pool constructs the graph-level representation as τ·v₁(H), where v₁ is the dominant right singular vector of the node embedding matrix H produced after message passing. The theoretical justification rests on Wedin's sin-Θ theorem: when the spectral gap σ₁(H) − σ₂(H) is non-trivial, the top singular vector is stable under bounded perturbations, yielding a robustness bound γ = τ√2·ε/(σ₁−σ₂) · Π‖W^(ℓ)‖ · Σ(ŵ_u)² that depends only on the clean representation's spectral gap rather than the perturbed input. The paper also derives upper bounds for sum, average, and max pooling, showing sum pooling scales with total walk count (vulnerable in dense graphs), average pooling normalizes by node count, and max pooling localizes to a single high-centrality node. RS-Pool is model-agnostic, permutation-invariant, fully differentiable, and integrates into existing GNN pipelines with minimal overhead (O(K·n·d) per forward pass), complementing rather than replacing message-passing-level defenses.

## Results

RS-Pool achieves the highest attacked accuracy across most dataset-attack combinations in Table 1. The most striking gain is on D&D under PGD, where RS-Pool reaches 30.4% accuracy versus 16.6% for the next-best method (Sort-P), a 14-point uplift. On PROTEINS under PGD, RS-Pool scores 51.9% compared to 48.5% for TopK-P, and on REDDIT-B under PGD it reaches 74.0% versus 69.0% for Sum pooling. Clean accuracy remains competitive, typically within 1–2 points of the best baseline (e.g., 73.5% on PROTEINS vs. 74.7% for PAN-P; 74.6% on D&D vs. 75.7% for PAN-P).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RS-Pool is compared against seven pooling baselines: Sum, Average, Max (classical flat pooling), SAG (self-attention graph pooling), TopK-P (learned top-k node selection), PAN-P (path-integral pooling), and Sort-P (sorted feature concatenation). Under PGD attacks, RS-Pool dominates on 7 of 8 datasets, with the largest margin on D&D (30.4% vs. 16.6% for Sort-P, the next best) and ENZYMES (11.9% vs. 9.8% for Max). Max pooling is consistently the weakest under attacks, dropping to 8.7% on D&D-PGD and 32.0% on IMDB-B-PGD, while Sum pooling also degrades severely on dense graphs (6.7% on D&D-PGD). On clean accuracy, RS-Pool is competitive but not always best: Average pooling leads on IMDB-B (62.7% vs. 62.0%) and Sort-P on REDDIT-B (80.0% vs. 75.5%), though these gaps are small (1–5 points). Under Random and Genetic attacks, RS-Pool wins on 6–7 of 8 datasets, with the exception being Random on PROTEINS where PAN-P edges ahead (68.5% vs. 70.4% is actually RS-Pool winning) and Random on REDDIT-B where Sort-P leads (78.0% vs. 75.0%). The advantage of RS-Pool is most pronounced on larger, denser graphs (D&D, NCI1) where the dominant singular vector captures the most informative global signal.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the expected-robustness framework of Abbahaddou et al. (ICLR 2024) and Ennadir et al. (AAAI 2024) from the message-passing stage to the final pooling/readout stage, which prior adversarial-defense work has largely overlooked. While existing GNN defenses (GNNGuard, SoftMedian, GCORN, adversarial training, randomized smoothing) intervene during or before message passing, this work isolates flat pooling (sum, average, max) as an independent robustness lever and derives per-method upper bounds on expected adversarial risk. The proposed RS-Pool repurposes classical matrix perturbation theory (Wedin's sin-Θ theorem) in a novel way: rather than regularizing weights or pruning edges, it projects the node-embedding matrix onto its dominant right singular vector, a direction provably stable under bounded perturbations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By showing that the choice of pooling function materially changes adversarial vulnerability (sum scales with total walk count, max localizes to a single node, average normalizes by n), the paper gives practitioners a principled criterion for selecting readout functions under a given threat model. RS-Pool is model-agnostic, differentiable, and computable in O(K·n·d) via power iteration, making it a drop-in replacement compatible with existing GNN backbones and defenses. Key open questions include how the required spectral gap σ₁−σ₂ behaves as a function of network depth and graph topology, and whether the power-iteration overhead becomes prohibitive on very dense graphs where the robustness gains may be most needed.
