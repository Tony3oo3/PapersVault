## Problem

Existing long-video benchmarks for VLMs rely heavily on single-needle questions that extract information from one location, failing to test whether models can jointly reason over two separate video segments. Additionally, world-model evaluations focus on object motion prediction while neglecting causal reasoning over human behaviors. This paper addresses both gaps by introducing a benchmark that requires models to extract and jointly understand two causally related events from a long video.

## Input / Output

Each question is presented with a sequence of video clips (each represented as five uniformly sampled frames stacked vertically into a single image), the full movie narration text, and a question prompt. Clips and narration are provided separately without alignment to prevent text-to-clip shortcut learning, and random padding clips are added before and after the cause-to-effect span to eliminate positional shortcuts. The model must output either a clip number (visual grounding format) or a four-option multiple-choice answer about a visual detail (image description format). For 2-needle questions, the model must produce two answers: one resolving a vague bridge entity from the effect event, and one identifying or describing the cause event.

## Method

The benchmark is built on 192 movie recap videos from SyMoN and YMS. Causal relationships are extracted via an LLM using a combination of a global event graph (over the full narration) and local graphs from 15-sentence sliding windows with 5-sentence stride, merged by union; only pairs separated by at least three events are retained. For each cause-effect pair, a VLM rephrases both sentences to establish a shared 'bridge entity' that is then deliberately obscured into a vague reference (e.g., 'Superman's death' becomes 'tragedy'). The 2-needle question is constructed in two parts: Part 1 requires the model to resolve the vague bridge entity by locating the effect clip, and Part 2 requires locating the cause clip, with the bridge entity kept ambiguous so Part 2 cannot be answered without first completing Part 1. To mitigate textual bias from the narration input, two complementary formats are used: visual grounding (select the correct clip) for all 1-needle questions and half of 2-needle questions, and image description (multiple-choice about visual details) for the other half of 2-needle questions, with counterfactual distractors drawn from adjacent clips to force true causal localization.

## Results

On CAUSAL2NEEDLES, the best proprietary model (ChatGPT-4o) achieves 39.2% accuracy on causal 1-needle questions, 13.4% on VG 2-needle (both parts correct, averaged over forward/reverse order), and 59.2% on ID 2-needle questions, compared to a human baseline of 78.2%, 83.7%, and 88.2% respectively. Causal questions are substantially harder than noncausal ones (e.g., ChatGPT-4o drops from 56.8% to 39.2%, a 17.6-point gap). Most open-source models perform at or below random chance (1.0%) on VG 2-needle questions requiring both parts correct, while the best open-source model (Qwen2.5VL-32B) reaches only 18.3% on that metric.

## Baselines & Comparisons

As a benchmark paper, CAUSAL2NEEDLES evaluates 13 VLMs rather than proposing a new method. Proprietary models (ChatGPT-4o, Gemini-1.5-pro, Claude-3.5-sonnet, ChatGPT-4o-mini) consistently outperform all open-source models on every question type; for example, on causal 1-needle questions the best proprietary model (ChatGPT-4o, 39.2%) exceeds the best open-source model (Qwen2.5VL-32B, 11.7%) by over 27 points. On VG 2-needle questions (both parts correct), all open-source models except Qwen2.5VL-7B (19.4%) and Aria-28B (0.1%) score at or below the 1.0% random-chance level, while proprietary models range from 2.9% (Claude-3.5-sonnet) to 13.4% (ChatGPT-4o). On ID 2-needle questions, the gap narrows: Gemini-1.5-pro (60.9%) and Claude-3.5-sonnet (60.5%) slightly edge out ChatGPT-4o (59.2%), and the best open-source model Qwen2.5VL-32B (53.5%) remains competitive, suggesting that multiple-choice visual detail questions are less sensitive to the joint-retrieval weakness. All models fall well short of the human baseline (78.2–88.2%), with the largest gap on VG 2-needle questions where even the best model (ChatGPT-4o, 13.4%) trails humans by ~70 points.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the needle-in-a-haystack formulation for long-context evaluation from the text domain (Kamradt 2023) and single-needle video benchmarks (MLVU, VideoMME) to a causal two-needle setting where joint understanding of both needles is required. It builds directly on the event graph extraction method of Sun et al. (2024) for identifying causal relationships in narrative text, and departs from existing multi-needle video benchmarks (MVBench, TVBench) whose questions only require independent retrieval at each location rather than sequential, dependency-linked reasoning. The bridge-entity mechanism is a novel question-design strategy that forces the model to resolve an ambiguous reference from one needle before it can answer about the other, something absent from prior long-video QA benchmarks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals that current VLMs, even proprietary frontier models, collapse to near-random performance on causal 2-needle questions despite scoring 50%+ on 1-needle tasks, exposing a critical gap between single-location retrieval and multi-location causal reasoning. The finding that performance degrades with needle distance while humans do not suggests a fundamental architectural limitation in how VLMs attend to and integrate information across long video contexts. Limitations include reliance on movie recap videos (which over-represent dramatic events), exclusion of audio, and the fact that image-description questions may benefit from pretraining knowledge of specific films. Open questions include whether the distance effect can be mitigated by architectural changes, whether causal reasoning over human behaviors can be improved through targeted training, and how the benchmark generalizes to non-narrative, real-world video.
