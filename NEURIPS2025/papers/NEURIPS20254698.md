## Problem

While boosting is well understood in the realizable setting, the statistical properties of weak-to-strong learning in the agnostic setting—where no assumptions are made on the label distribution—remained poorly characterized. Prior agnostic boosting algorithms required restrictive conditions on the relationship between advantage parameters (e.g., ε₀ = O(εγ)) to guarantee strong learning, and achieved sample complexity that was polynomially worse than the information-theoretic lower bound. This paper settles the sample complexity of agnostic boosting up to logarithmic factors and shows that any non-trivial weak learner (γ > ε₀) can be boosted to a strong learner without such constraints.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes a labeled training sequence S of size m (split into three equal parts), an agnostic weak learner oracle W that maps labeled samples to hypotheses in [-1,1]^X, and confidence parameters δ, δ₀, m₀. It outputs a voting classifier v ∈ conv(H) (a weighted sum of weak hypotheses) and a final binary classifier sign(v). Notably, the method requires no direct access to the reference class F or base class H, and does not require knowledge of the advantage parameters γ and ε₀, preserving the adaptability characteristic of classic boosting.

## Method

The algorithm proceeds in three steps. First, it reduces the agnostic problem to the realizable setting by running a modified AdaBoost (with confidence amplification via repeated weak learner calls and correlation-based reweighting for non-binary hypotheses) on all 2^(m/3) possible re-labelings of the first third of the data, producing an exponentially large bag B₁ of voting classifiers. Second, it filters B₁ down to O(log m) hypotheses by minimizing the empirical margin loss on the second third of the data over a geometric sequence of margin levels {1, 1/2, 1/4, …, 1/√m}, which circumvents the need to know γ and ε₀. Third, it selects the final classifier by minimizing empirical loss on the third third of the data. The generalization guarantee is expressed in terms of the fat-shattering dimension of the base class H at level c(γ−ε₀), and the bound interpolates between the agnostic and realizable regimes via the factor (1 − corr_D(f⋆)).

## Results

The paper establishes a nearly tight sample complexity bound for agnostic boosting. Theorem 1.3 shows that the proposed algorithm achieves corr_D(sign(v)) ≥ corr_D(f*) − √(C(1−corr_D(f*))·β) − C·β, where β = d̂/((γ−ε₀)²m)·Ln^{3/2}((γ−ε₀)²m/d̂) + (1/m)ln(ln m/δ), with d̂ being the fat-shattering dimension of the base class. Theorem 1.2 provides a matching lower bound (up to logarithmic factors) showing that Ω(d/(γ²(1−L)·L²)) samples are necessary. Under the mild assumption γ ≥ ε₀/2, the sample complexity is Õ((1−corr_D(f*))·d̂/(ε²γ²) + d̂/(εγ²)), improving on prior work by a polynomial factor.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's main advantage over prior agnostic boosting methods is both in generality and sample complexity. Compared to Ghai and Singh [2024], which requires ε₀ = O(εγ) and achieves Õ(VC(H)/(ε³γ³)) samples, this work only requires γ > ε₀ (any non-trivial weak learner) and achieves Õ((1−corr(f*))·d̂/(ε²γ²) + d̂/(εγ²)), a polynomial improvement. Against Brukhim et al. [2020], which needs ε₀ = Θ(εγ) and yields Õ(1/(ε⁶γ⁴)) samples, the improvement is even more pronounced. Feldman [2010] requires α = Θ(ε) and has sample complexity O(1/ε⁴ + 1/γ⁴) regardless of the weak learning guarantee, while this paper's bound scales with the actual advantage γ−ε₀. Kalai and Kanade [2009] achieves O(1/(γ²ε²ε₀²)) but uses a distribution-specific weak learning notion. Crucially, all prior methods impose non-trivial constraints linking the weak learner's parameters to the target accuracy ε, whereas Theorem 1.3 recovers the iconic generality of classic boosting by working for any non-trivial weak learner without such constraints.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Hopkins et al. [2024] framework for reducing agnostic learning to realizable learning via enumeration over re-labelings and validation, adapting it to the boosting setting with a modified AdaBoost that handles continuous-valued weak hypotheses through correlation-based reweighting. It departs from prior agnostic boosting work (Ghai and Singh 2024, Brukhim et al. 2020, Feldman 2010, Kalai and Kanade 2009) by showing that no restrictive relationship between the weak learner's advantage parameters is needed: any non-trivial weak learner (γ > ε₀) suffices, whereas all prior methods required conditions like ε₀ = O(εγ) or α = Θ(ε). The margin-based filtering step and the fat-shattering dimension bound for convex hulls draw on Larsen and Ritzert [2022] and Høgsgaard and Larsen [2025], respectively.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper settles the sample complexity of agnostic boosting up to logarithmic factors by providing a nearly-matching lower bound, resolving a question open since Ben-David et al. [2001]. The bound's interpolation between the agnostic (corr(f*) < 1) and realizable (corr(f*) = 1) regimes is a feature absent from all prior agnostic boosting results, yielding tighter guarantees when the reference class is nearly realizable. The main limitation is computational: the algorithm requires exponential time to enumerate all re-labelings, making it a statistical rather than practical contribution. The authors identify computationally efficient agnostic boosting with near-optimal sample complexity, and removal of the residual logarithmic factors, as the most natural next steps.
