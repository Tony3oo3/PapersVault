## Problem

Existing continual learning methods that leverage pre-trained backbones are highly dependent on the choice of a single backbone, which may fail to achieve optimal plasticity on novel data domains. Moreover, freezing representation networks to ensure stability limits new-task learning, and applying uniform regularization across all layers causes over-regularization that further suppresses plasticity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255822/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images as input and produces class label probabilities via a linear classifier. It operates in a task-incremental setting where the task identifier is available at both training and inference time, and a small memory buffer (200–1000 samples, updated via reservoir sampling) stores past examples for representation-level regularization. The key architectural detail is that T different pre-trained ViT backbones process each image in parallel, and their feature vectors are fused into a single compact representation before classification, keeping the inference path fixed without task-specific parameter growth.

## Method

LMSRR orchestrates multiple pre-trained ViT backbones whose output features are stacked into a matrix and processed by the Multi-Scale Interaction and Dynamic Fusion (MSIDF) module: several learnable attention modules with different window sizes apply Hadamard products with learned weight matrices to capture multi-scale feature interactions, and softmax-normalized adaptive parameters weight their outputs into a compact fused representation. For stability, the Multi-Level Representation Optimization (MLRO) strategy trains only the last L′ layers of each backbone and penalizes the L2 divergence between active and frozen representations at every trainable layer. To avoid over-regularization, the Adaptive Regularization Optimization (ARO) method introduces a per-layer learnable switch vector converted via Gumbel-Softmax into differentiable gating variables that selectively modulate the regularization strength of each layer. The total loss combines cross-entropy on current-task and replayed samples with the adaptively gated representation-divergence term, all optimized jointly in a unified framework.

## Results

On standard datasets with a memory buffer of 500, LMSRR achieves 99.15% average accuracy on CIFAR-10, 92.75% on Tiny ImageNet, and 96.97% on R-MNIST (Domain-IL), substantially outperforming the strongest replay-based baselines (DER++: 93.88%, 51.91%, 92.77% respectively). On complex fine-grained datasets (buffer 500), LMSRR attains 95.76% average on CIFAR-100, 88.91% on CUB-200, 84.35% on ImageNet-R, and 90.14% on Cars196. The method maintains near-perfect accuracy even with a small buffer of 200 (98.85% on CIFAR-10, 92.08% on Tiny ImageNet), demonstrating robustness to limited memory.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LMSRR decisively outperforms all replay-based and regularization-based baselines on standard benchmarks: on Tiny ImageNet (buffer 500), it achieves 92.75% average accuracy versus 54.06% for the strongest replay method DER++(re) and 49.88% for FDR, a margin exceeding 38 percentage points. On CIFAR-10, LMSRR (99.15%) surpasses DER++ (93.88%) by over 5 points, and on R-MNIST it leads DER++(re) (93.28%) by 3.7 points. Among prompt-based and ensemble methods on complex datasets, LMSRR wins on CIFAR-100 (95.76% vs. L2P 95.36%) and Cars196 (90.14% vs. CoFiMA 87.28%), but trails CoFiMA on CUB-200 average (88.91% vs. 90.66%) and L2P on ImageNet-R average (84.35% vs. 86.01%), though it recovers on the 'Last' task metric for both (94.31% vs. 92.54% on CUB-200; 88.43% vs. 87.50% on ImageNet-R). The ablation with identical backbones confirms that the MSIDF fusion mechanism, rather than backbone diversity alone, drives the performance gap over SOTA methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

LMSRR departs from the dominant single-pre-trained-backbone paradigm in continual learning (e.g., L2P, DAP, RanPAC) by orchestrating multiple heterogeneous ViT backbones and fusing their features in a shared representation space via the MSIDF attention mechanism. It contrasts with model-averaging and ensemble strategies such as CoFiMA and Model Soup, which combine independently trained models or add task-specific adapters, by performing semantic-level feature fusion within a fixed architecture without parameter growth. The regularization side draws on the stability-plasticity literature (EWC, SI, LwF) but introduces a Gumbel-Softmax-based switch vector (ARO) for differentiable, layer-selective penalization, moving beyond uniform regularization across all trainable layers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practical recipe for improving continual learning accuracy by leveraging the complementary strengths of multiple pre-trained backbones, and the adaptive layer-wise regularization (ARO) provides a principled way to avoid over-regularization that plagues uniform penalty approaches. However, the method's reliance on a fixed set of pre-trained ViT backbones limits scalability to broader backbone families or multimodal settings, and the multi-backbone inference cost is not deeply analyzed. Open questions include whether the MSIDF fusion generalizes to non-ViT architectures, how the approach behaves under open-world (class-incremental) settings without task identifiers, and whether the gains persist when backbone diversity is reduced.
