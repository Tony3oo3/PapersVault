## Problem

In the τ-parametrized agnostic PAC learning framework introduced by Hanneke et al. (FOCS '24), the optimal sample complexity was established for τ > d/m but left open for the small-error regime τ ≈ d/m. This paper resolves that open question by exhibiting a learner whose error matches the known lower bound τ + Ω(√(τ(d+log(1/δ))/m) + (d+log(1/δ))/m) up to a constant factor c ≤ 2.1 on τ, thereby settling the complexity of agnostic learning when τ = O(d/m).

## Input / Output

The algorithm receives a labeled training set S of m i.i.d. samples from an unknown distribution D over X × {−1,1} and a hypothesis class H of VC dimension d, and outputs a binary classifier h_S: X → {−1,1}. The learner is agnostic to τ (the best-in-class error), meaning it does not require knowledge of τ's value. The final classifier is selected via a hold-out set between the paper's voting-based learner and the Hanneke et al. (2024) learner, yielding a best-of-both-worlds guarantee that is tight in the regime τ = O(d/m) and improves upon Hanneke et al. when τ = o(log⁵(m/d)·d/m).

## Method

The core algorithm recursively splits the training set into 27 subsamples (Algorithm 2), runs ERM on randomly sampled subsets of the resulting leaf sequences (a bagging-like procedure with t = O(log(m/(δ(d+log(1/δ))))) draws), and takes majority votes. The key innovation for reducing the multiplicative constant on τ from 15 to 2.1 is a tie-breaking mechanism: two independent voting classifiers are constructed on disjoint data splits, and when either classifier has low confidence (fewer than 232/243 of its voters agree), an ERM trained on the 'region of disagreement' (points where at least 11/243 of voters err) breaks the tie. The 27-way split (rather than 3-way) is essential: it ensures that when the overall error fraction reaches the fine-grained threshold 11/243, at least one recursive sub-call also exceeds that threshold while a random hypothesis from the remaining calls errs with positive probability, making the inductive argument close. The final classifier is selected on a hold-out set between this voting learner and the Hanneke et al. (2024) learner.

## Results

The paper's main result (Theorem 1.4) provides a learner achieving error at most min{2.1·τ + O(√(τ(d+ln(1/δ))/m) + (d+ln(1/δ))/m), τ + O(√(τ(d+ln(1/δ))/m) + ln⁵(m/d)·(d+ln(1/δ))/m)} with probability at least 1−δ. In the regime τ = O(d/m), the first bound matches the known lower bound τ + Ω(√(τ(d+ln(1/δ))/m) + (d+ln(1/δ))/m) up to the constant 2.1 on τ, resolving Open Problem 1.1 of Hanneke et al. (FOCS '24). The algorithm is computationally efficient, requiring only O(ln(m/(δ(d+ln(1/δ))))) ERM oracle calls, and is based on majority voting over ERM classifiers trained on carefully constructed subsamples.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is against the lower bound of Hanneke et al. (FOCS '24), which establishes that any learner must incur error at least τ + Ω(√(τ(d+ln(1/δ))/m) + (d+ln(1/δ))/m) in the regime τ > d/m. The new learner achieves 2.1τ + O(√(τ(d+ln(1/δ))/m) + (d+ln(1/δ))/m), matching this lower bound up to the constant 2.1 on τ when τ = O(d/m), thereby proving optimality in that regime. Compared to the upper bound of Hanneke et al. (2024), which requires τ > ln¹⁰(m/d)·(d+ln(1/δ))/m to be non-vacuous, the new result improves the additive term from ln⁵(m/d)·(d+ln(1/δ))/m to (d+ln(1/δ))/m in the small-τ regime, at the cost of a 2.1× multiplicative factor on τ. ERM, which is optimal in the standard agnostic PAC framework, is suboptimal in the τ-parametrized setting: the paper shows all proper learners (including ERM) are suboptimal for τ in [Ω(ln¹⁰(m/d)·(d+ln(1/δ))/m), o(1)], and the new improper voting-based learner closes this gap. The algorithm also makes progress on Open Problems 1.2 and 1.3 of Hanneke et al. (2024) by being based on majority voting over ERM classifiers and requiring only O(ln(m/(δ(d+ln(1/δ))))) ERM calls.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the τ-parametrized agnostic PAC learning framework introduced by Hanneke, Larsen, and Zhivotovskiy (FOCS 2024), which itself refined the classical agnostic setting by tracking excess error as a function of the best-in-class error τ. The recursive splitting scheme is a direct modification of Hanneke's (2016) optimal realizable learner, adapted to the agnostic regime with disjoint splits and a 27-way partition (versus 3-way) to enable finer-grained error control. The bagging-style analysis of random hypothesis subsets borrows from Larsen (2023), while the novel tie-breaking mechanism—training an ERM on the 'region of disagreement' between two independent voting classifiers—represents a new algorithmic idea not present in any of these prior works.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper resolves the central open problem from Hanneke et al. (2024) by showing that the lower bound τ + Ω(√(τ(d+log(1/δ))/m) + (d+log(1/δ))/m) is tight up to a constant factor in the regime τ = O(d/m), with a computationally efficient learner achieving 2.1τ + O(√(τ(d+log(1/δ))/m) + (d+log(1/δ))/m). The tie-breaking technique (using ERM on the disagreement region of two voting classifiers) is a potentially reusable idea for other learning-theoretic problems. The key open question is whether the constant 2.1 can be reduced to 1, which would fully settle the complexity of agnostic PAC learning; the remaining polylog range τ ∈ [ω(1), o(ln^10(m/d))]·(d+log(1/δ))/m also awaits characterization.
