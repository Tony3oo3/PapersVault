## Problem

DiT-based diffusion models excel at structurally-consistent edits (color, style, background changes) but fail dramatically on structurally-inconsistent operations that require layout modifications such as adding, removing, moving, or resizing objects. Existing DiT editing methods are restricted to narrow sets of routine operations and lack comprehensive evaluation across diverse editing scenarios, leaving the true editing potential of DiTs unclear.

## Input / Output

The method accepts a single RGB image (512×512) and a free-form natural-language editing instruction that may contain multiple compound edits (e.g., 'put the cat on the beach, make it Christmas style and orange'). A VLM first decomposes the instruction into an ordered list of atomic operations, each tagged with one of 13 predefined categories. The output is a single edited image that faithfully executes all specified modifications while preserving unmentioned regions.

## Method

IEAP decomposes any editing instruction into a sequential program of five atomic primitives—RoI Localization, RoI Inpainting, RoI Editing, RoI Compositing, and Global Transformation—using Chain-of-Thought reasoning with a VLM (GPT-4o) and a neural program interpreter. Each primitive is a specialized LoRA adapter fine-tuned on FLUX.1-dev (rank 128, 50k iterations, Prodigy optimizer), so structurally-inconsistent layout edits are reduced to a composition of structurally-consistent sub-operations. For layout-altering operations (move, resize, add), an LLM computes geometric transforms on bounding boxes to reposition or rescale the RoI mask before inpainting and compositing. RoI Compositing uses a morphological annular mask and a dedicated fusion network to blend edited regions seamlessly with surrounding context, and Global Transformation handles full-image style or tone changes as a final pass.

## Results

On the MagicBrush test set, IEAP achieves the best CLIPim (0.922), CLIPout (0.247), and DINO (0.897) scores, with a competitive L1 of 0.060. On the AnyEdit test set, IEAP leads across all four metrics: CLIPim 0.882, L1 0.096, DINO 0.825, and GPT-4o score 4.41/5. In the per-category breakdown (Table 2), IEAP outperforms all baselines on local semantic editing (GPT 4.42), local attribute editing (GPT 4.54), and overall content editing (GPT 4.51), with the largest margins on local semantic editing where layout changes are required.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IEAP outperforms all six open-source baselines (InstructPix2Pix, MagicBrush, UltraEdit, GenArtist, OmniGen2, ICEdit) on both MagicBrush and AnyEdit benchmarks. The closest competitor is ICEdit, which achieves a marginally lower L1 on MagicBrush (0.058 vs. 0.060) but trails IEAP on every other metric, with a notable 0.28-point gap in AnyEdit GPT score (4.13 vs. 4.41). The gap widens substantially on local semantic editing (add, remove, move, resize), where IEAP scores GPT 4.42 versus 4.08 for ICEdit and 4.12 for OmniGen2, confirming the advantage of the atomic-operation decomposition for layout-altering tasks. Against three proprietary multimodal models (SeedEdit/Doubao, Gemini 2.0 Flash, GPT-4o), the comparison is qualitative (Fig. 6): IEAP matches or surpasses them on complex multi-step instructions, while these models frequently omit specified edits or introduce unrelated alterations. The per-category tables (Tables 5–21) further show IEAP leading on most sub-tasks, with the exception of the Remove category where ICEdit edges ahead on GPTavg (4.29 vs. 3.91) and the Move category where ICEdit leads on GPTavg (4.43 vs. 4.20), suggesting that the layout-reconfiguration step remains the weakest link in IEAP's pipeline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the DiT-based conditional generation framework established by OminiControl (unified tri-modal conditioning) and FLUX.1-dev (the backbone architecture), and borrows the Chain-of-Thought decomposition paradigm from LLM reasoning and the neural program interpreter concept from Reed & De Freitas. It departs from prior instruction-driven editing methods (InstructPix2Pix, MagicBrush, UltraEdit, GenArtist, OmniGen2, ICEdit) that treat each edit as a monolithic end-to-end task, instead proposing a programmatic reduction into five atomic operations (RoI Localization, Inpainting, Editing, Compositing, Global Transformation) executed sequentially. The key conceptual departure is the empirical taxonomy revealing that DiT-based models exhibit a sharp performance dichotomy between structurally-consistent and structurally-inconsistent edits, motivating the decomposition strategy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a fundamental and previously under-characterized limitation of diffusion-based editing: the inability to handle layout-altering operations (add, remove, move, resize) that require spatial recomposition. The modular atomic-operation framework provides a principled path toward compositional, multi-step editing that end-to-end methods cannot achieve, and the competitive results against proprietary models (GPT-4o, Gemini 2.0 Flash) on complex instructions suggest this decomposition strategy is a viable alternative to monolithic approaches. Open questions remain around physically consistent compositing (shadows, lighting), progressive quality degradation over multiple iterations, and the high inference latency (~7s average) that precludes real-time use. Extension to video editing with temporal coherence is a natural next step.
