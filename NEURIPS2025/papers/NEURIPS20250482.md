## Problem

Hippocampal place cells collectively encode spatial relationships for navigation, but existing computational models either treat individual place fields as ad hoc Gaussian tuning curves or use successor representations with a single temporal horizon. This paper asks whether the entire place cell population can be understood as a structured embedding that simultaneously explains localized firing fields, the dorsoventral multi-scale hierarchy, and efficient trap-free path planning from a single principled mechanism.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 2D discrete lattice (e.g., 40×40) with obstacle-defined walkable regions as its environment specification, from which a symmetric one-step transition matrix P₁ is constructed. It produces non-negative unit-norm embeddings h(x,τ) ∈ R⁵⁰⁰ at K discrete scales τ=2^k (k=1,…,11), which after bilinear interpolation become continuous over space. During navigation, the agent consumes only the current continuous position x and target y, and outputs a step-by-step trajectory by selecting the neighbor z and scale τ* that maximize the gradient Δ(z,τ)=⟨h(y,τ),h(z,τ)⟩−⟨h(y,τ),h(x,τ)⟩.

## Method

The core construction defines place cell populations as non-negative embeddings h(x,τ) whose inner product approximates the normalized τ-step random walk transition probability q(y|x,τ)=p(y|x,τ)/√(p(x|x,τ)·p(y|y,τ)). Because the one-step transition matrix is symmetric, P_τ=P₁^τ admits spectral decomposition, and Horn's theorem guarantees a non-negative factorization exists; the paper learns it by minimizing a least-squares inner-product objective with AdamW, projecting to the non-negative unit-norm simplex after each step. Multi-scale kernels are computed efficiently via recursive matrix squaring P_{2τ}=P_τ², yielding spatial scales √τ that mirror the ~1.4–1.7× dorsoventral place-field scaling. A key theoretical result is that the conjunction of non-negativity and orthogonality (enforced when q(y|x,τ)=0 for non-adjacent locations) induces emergent sparsity—disjoint support sets across embeddings—providing a geometric explanation for localized place fields without explicit regularization. Navigation proceeds as gradient ascent on q(y|x,τ) with adaptive scale selection τ*=argmax_τ max_z Δ(z,τ), which in the continuous limit follows the heat-kernel gradient field: for small τ the path tracks geodesic distance (Varadhan's formula), while for large τ it prioritizes topological connectivity encoded by near-unit eigenvalues, yielding smooth, trap-free trajectories with boundary-parallel flow and diffraction-like passage guidance.

## Results

The method achieves 100% success rate across all tested environments (open field, U-shape, S-shape, Four-room). SPL (Success weighted by inverse Path Length) reaches 0.991 in the open field and exceeds 1.0 in complex environments (1.392 for S-shape, 1.519 for Four-room), indicating the method discovers paths shorter than the Bug algorithm baseline. In the Four-Room environment specifically, the method achieves SPL of 1.00 with 100% success, compared to 0.66±0.51 for the oracle-enhanced Bug algorithm and 1.08±0.50 for A* Search.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against four baselines in the Four-Room environment: Bug algorithm (with and without oracle boundary-direction knowledge), A* Search, and Random Walk. Our method achieves 100% success with SPL 1.00, substantially outperforming Bug without oracle (14% success, SPL 0.15) and Random Walk (2% success, SPL 0.01), which fail on most challenging scenarios. Against the oracle-enhanced Bug algorithm (which knows the optimal boundary-following direction), our method achieves a higher SPL (1.00 vs. 0.66±0.51), indicating it finds more efficient paths even when the baseline has privileged information. Compared to A* Search (100% success, SPL 1.08±0.50), our method achieves comparable success with slightly lower mean SPL but notably lower variance, suggesting more consistent path quality. The paper notes that in simpler environments (U-shape), performance is comparable to oracle-enhanced Bug, while in complex environments (S-shape, Four-room) the advantage becomes significant.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes multi-step random walk transition kernels (heat kernels) as the mathematical substrate for place cell population embeddings, departing from both the Successor Representation framework—which uses a single discounted sum γT—and prior place cell models that treat individual cells as separate Gaussian tuning curves. The key conceptual move is applying Horn's theorem to guarantee a non-negative matrix factorization of the transition kernel, so that the inner product between non-negative embeddings reconstructs the normalized transition probability. The multi-scale structure (τ = 2^k via matrix squaring) and the emergent sparsity from non-negativity plus orthogonality are novel theoretical contributions not present in prior diffusion-map or SR-based navigation work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a unified geometric account linking place field localization (emergent sparsity), dorsoventral scale hierarchy (√τ), theta phase precession (angle between embeddings), and trap-free navigation (gradient ascent on a smooth unimodal field) within a single representational structure. However, the experimental validation is confined to 40×40 2D grids with simple mazes and relatively weak baselines (Bug algorithm, A*, random walk), and the model lacks sensory integration, online learning, and 3D extension—significant gaps for biological plausibility. Open questions include whether the non-negative factorization learned via AdamW with projection truly captures the full Horn's theorem solution, how the framework scales to larger or continuous environments, and whether the theta phase formulation can be validated against recorded neural data.
