## Problem

Personalized federated learning methods like Ditto constrain local model drift using ℓ² weight penalties, which fail to capture statistical differences in latent representations under feature heterogeneity. Existing feature-based constraints (e.g., contrastive losses in MOON, PerFCL) are non-adaptive and require careful tuning. The paper asks whether theoretically grounded, adaptive distributional distance measures can serve as more effective drift penalties that automatically adjust to the evolving structure of client latent spaces during federated training.

## Input / Output

Each client's model is decomposed into a feature extractor f(·;θ) and a classifier g(·;φ); the MMD constraints operate on the intermediate latent representations f(x;θ) rather than final predictions. Inputs range from 32×32 RGB images (CIFAR-10), 2D dermatological images (Fed-ISIC2019), 3-channel fluorescent microscopy images (RxRx1), to 60-dimensional numerical vectors (Synthetic). The unpaired MMD constraint measures the distance between the *distributions* of global and local latent features over a batch, rather than enforcing point-wise alignment as in paired approaches like cosine similarity.

## Method

The paper augments Ditto's (and MR-MTL's) local training loss with an adaptive MMD-based feature-drift penalty that measures distributional divergence between the local model's latent representations and those of a frozen global reference model. Two kernel optimization strategies are employed: MK-MMD, which solves a quadratic program to find optimal weights over a bank of RBF kernels (minimizing Type-II error for a fixed Type-I error), and MMD-D, which learns a deep featurization network with a safeguard kernel to construct an adaptive distance metric. The key mechanism is *iterative re-optimization*: as the latent spaces evolve during federated training, the kernels are periodically re-fitted on sampled local data (every 20 steps or every step), maintaining a strong distributional measure throughout. This unpaired, distributional constraint is complementary to the standard ℓ² weight penalty and can be used in isolation or combined with it, yielding the most consistent gains on datasets with pronounced feature heterogeneity (Synthetic, RxRx1).

## Results

On the combined Ditto + MMD-D setting (Table 2), the best results are: CIFAR-10 (α=0.1) 85.21% accuracy, Synthetic (α=β=0.5) 89.70%, Fed-ISIC2019 72.23% balanced accuracy, and RxRx1 67.76% accuracy. The largest gains over standard Ditto appear on feature-heterogeneous datasets: +4.16% on Synthetic (0.5,0.5) and +2.13% on RxRx1. On the most homogeneous setting (CIFAR-10, α=5.0), cosine similarity + Ditto (78.05%) slightly edges out MMD-based variants, while all methods remain close to standard Ditto (77.66%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against FedAvg, the MMD-augmented Ditto methods achieve large gains on all datasets: +10.0% on CIFAR-10 (α=0.1), +4.2% on Synthetic (0.5,0.5), +8.2% on Fed-ISIC2019, and +32.6% on RxRx1. Against standard Ditto, MMD-D + Ditto delivers the most consistent improvements, with the largest jumps on feature-heterogeneous data: +4.16% on Synthetic (0.5,0.5) and +2.13% on RxRx1, while gains on CIFAR-10 are modest (+0.2–0.5%). On the most homogeneous CIFAR-10 (α=5.0), cosine similarity + Ditto (78.05%) marginally beats both MMD variants, suggesting that simpler paired constraints suffice when feature drift is minimal. Against MR-MTL (Appendix F, Table 4), augmenting with MMD-D or MK-MMD improves accuracy on all CIFAR-10 variants and RxRx1 (e.g., +2.29% on CIFAR-10 α=0.1 with MK-MMD), but Fed-ISIC2019 is the one setting where the MMD penalties do not help MR-MTL (70.37% vs. 70.63% baseline). Overall, MMD-D provides the most consistent and largest improvements across both Ditto and MR-MTL frameworks, particularly in high feature-heterogeneity regimes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Ditto and MR-MTL personalized federated learning frameworks by replacing or augmenting their standard ℓ² weight-drift penalties with adaptive, distributional feature-space constraints based on Multi-Kernel MMD (Gretton et al., 2012) and MMD-D (Liu et al., 2020). While prior pFL work has explored alternative weight regularizers (symmetrized KL, Fisher-weighted distances in the Ditto paper) and non-adaptive contrastive feature losses (MOON, PerFCL), this is the first study to integrate theoretically grounded, iteratively re-optimized MMD measures as unpaired, distributional drift penalties in a pFL setting. The key conceptual departure is shifting from point-wise or weight-space alignment to adaptive kernel-based distributional distance in latent feature spaces, with periodic re-optimization to track evolving representations during training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides practical guidance that the *type* of drift constraint should be matched to the *type* of heterogeneity: MMD-based feature constraints are most beneficial under pronounced feature heterogeneity (Synthetic, RxRx1), while weight-based penalties remain competitive or superior under label heterogeneity (CIFAR-10, Fed-ISIC2019). A notable finding is that the benefits of MMD constraints are not simply replicated by increasing the Ditto penalty weight λ, suggesting the two constraint types capture genuinely complementary aspects of drift. Open questions include a theoretical characterization of when adaptive MMD constraints are most effective, the non-trivial computational overhead of kernel re-optimization (O(m^3.5) per step for MK-MMD), and whether the approach extends to prototype-based or other pFL paradigms beyond penalty-based methods.
