## Problem

Pretrained latent diffusion models (LDMs) like Stable Diffusion produce severe structural distortions when generating images at resolutions beyond their training resolution. Existing training-free remedies—sliding-window, parameter-rectification, and progressive-upsampling methods—either degrade texture quality, introduce latent-space upsampling artifacts, or require full denoising at every resolution level, making them prohibitively slow (e.g., 26 minutes for a 4096×4096 image). No prior method simultaneously achieves high image quality and high inference efficiency for high-resolution generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252049/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt as input and produces a high-resolution RGB image (up to 4096×4096) using a frozen pretrained LDM (SDXL or SD2.1) with no additional training or fine-tuning. The target resolution and aspect ratio are user-specified; the initial Gaussian noise is reshaped to match the desired aspect ratio while preserving the total token count. All components (VAE encoder/decoder, denoising U-Net) remain fixed, and inference runs on a single consumer-grade GPU (3090/4090).

## Method

RepLDM decomposes the standard latent-space denoising loop into two stages. In the attention guidance stage, a novel training-free self-attention (TFSA) computes Softmax(z·zᵀ/√c)·z directly on the latent tokens (no learned Q/K/V projections), and its output is linearly blended with the original latent via an adaptive cosine-decay guidance scale γ_t that is active only during early-to-mid denoising steps; this clusters semantically related tokens and boosts high-frequency amplitudes, yielding a structurally consistent training-resolution latent. In the progressive upsampling stage, the latent is decoded to pixel space, bicubically upsampled, re-encoded, noised to a lower diffusion timestep, and denoised with only a small number of steps (e.g., 5–10 out of 50) per sub-stage, avoiding the severe artifacts that latent-space upsampling introduces. Because the first stage provides a high-quality structural initialization, the second stage needs far fewer denoising steps than full-resolution generation, achieving a ~5× speedup over prior progressive methods while producing superior FID, IS, and CLIP scores.

## Results

On the SAM benchmark at 2048×2048, RepLDM achieves FID 66.0, FIDc 47.4, ISc 17.5, and CLIP 25.1, outperforming the previous SOTA methods DemoFusion (FID 72.3, FIDc 53.5) and AccDiffusion (FID 71.6, FIDc 52.7). At 4096×4096, RepLDM reaches FID 90.6 and FIDc 59.0, compared to DemoFusion (FID 101.4, FIDc 63.5) and AccDiffusion (FID 103.2, FIDc 65.9). In terms of efficiency, RepLDM generates a 4096×4096 image in 5.7 minutes on a single 3090 GPU, roughly 4–5× faster than DemoFusion (25.0 min) and AccDiffusion (26.0 min), while requiring only 65 NFEs versus 200 for those methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RepLDM outperforms all seven baselines on FID and FIDc across all four tested resolutions (2048×2048, 2048×4096, 4096×2048, 4096×4096). Against the closest SOTA competitors DemoFusion and AccDiffusion, RepLDM improves FID by 5.6–12.6 points and FIDc by 4.1–6.9 points at 2048×2048, with even larger gaps at 4096×4096 (FID 90.6 vs. 101.4/103.2). On ISc, RepLDM matches or slightly trails DemoFusion (17.5 vs. 19.1 at 2048×2048) but leads AccDiffusion (17.5 vs. 17.0); CLIP scores are essentially tied across all methods (24.6–25.5). Against parameter-rectification methods (ScaleCrafter, UG, HiDiffusion), RepLDM achieves substantially lower FID (66.0 vs. 81.0–98.2) and FIDc (47.4 vs. 64.1–89.7) at 2048×2048, with the gap widening dramatically at 4096×4096 where HiDiffusion degrades to FID 144.1. In inference time, RepLDM is ~5× faster than DemoFusion and AccDiffusion (0.6 vs. 3.0 min at 2048×2048; 5.7 vs. 25–26 min at 4096×4096) and ~2× faster than HiDiffusion at 4096×4096 (5.7 vs. 3.4 min, though HiDiffusion is faster at 2048×2048 at 0.8 min). The user study confirms RepLDM's perceptual advantage, scoring 7.42/10 on structural consistency vs. 6.28 (AccDiffusion) and 5.99 (DemoFusion).

## Relation to Prior Work

RepLDM builds on the progressive upsampling paradigm established by DemoFusion and AccDiffusion, which iteratively increase resolution while re-running full denoising at each level. It departs from this lineage in two key ways: (1) it replaces latent-space upsampling with pixel-space upsampling (motivated by a pilot study showing latent-space interpolation introduces severe artifacts), and (2) it introduces a training-free self-attention mechanism (TFSA) that enhances structural consistency at the training resolution, yielding a better initialization that allows dramatically fewer denoising steps at higher resolutions. The attention guidance concept draws on the parameter-agnostic global modeling capability of self-attention, while the progressive scheduler design is informed by the observation that diffusion models synthesize low-frequency structure before high-frequency detail.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RepLDM achieves a 5× speedup over prior SOTA methods (DemoFusion, AccDiffusion) while simultaneously improving image quality, making high-resolution generation practical on a single consumer GPU—a significant step toward real-world deployment of LDMs beyond their training resolution. The training-free design means it can be applied to any pretrained LDM (validated on both SDXL and SD2.1) without additional training resources. Open questions remain around text rendering (inherited from the base model's limitations) and the scaling behavior at ultra-high resolutions (e.g., 12800×12800), where more sub-stages are needed. The paper also suggests adapting attention guidance to video generation as a promising future direction.
