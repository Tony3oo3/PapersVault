## Problem

Sukenik et al. showed that deep neural collapse (DNC) is suboptimal in the deep unconstrained feature model under MSE loss due to low-rank bias from L2 regularization, but left open whether DNC or low-rank solutions are local minima and why DNC appears so frequently in practice. This paper extends the suboptimality result to cross-entropy loss and provides a complete theoretical characterization of how low-rank bias reshapes the loss landscape, explaining the empirical persistence of DNC despite its suboptimality.

## Input / Output

The theoretical analysis operates on a classification problem with K classes and n samples per class, where a highly expressive feature map h (e.g., ResNet-20) maps raw images to d-dimensional feature vectors that are then treated as free optimization variables in the deep UFM. The output is a structural characterization of the loss landscape: the rank and singular-value decay of globally optimal solutions, the local geometry (Hessian definiteness) of DNC critical points, and the relative dimensionality of DNC versus low-rank solution manifolds as a function of network width. Empirical validation uses MNIST (28×28 grayscale) and CIFAR-10 (32×32 RGB) with a ResNet-20 backbone and a fully connected head of L linear or ReLU layers.

## Method

The paper analyzes a deep linear UFM with cross-entropy loss and L2 weight decay, where L layers are separated from an expressive feature map. A key structural result (Lemma 1) shows that at any critical point, all weight matrices share identical singular values with aligned singular vectors, reducing the loss to a function of the output logit matrix Z alone via a Schatten quasi-norm regularization term. The paper then proves that any solution whose rank exceeds the minimal rank needed to fit the data is suboptimal for sufficiently large L (Theorems 1–2), that global optima have at most q_K non-negligible singular values with the rest decaying exponentially in L (Theorem 3), and that DNC solutions remain local minima with positive semi-definite Hessian for small regularization (Theorem 4). The central explanatory result (Theorem 5) shows that the dimension of the DNC solution manifold grows faster than that of any fixed-rank low-rank solution as network width d increases, making DNC structurally more prevalent in the landscape. These results are extended to ReLU activations (Theorem 7) and validated on MNIST and CIFAR-10 with both UFM-style and standard weight-decay regularization.

## Results

The paper proves that in the deep linear UFM with cross-entropy loss, no DNC-structured solution can be a global minimum when K≥4 and L≥3 (or K≥6 and L=2), with a specific block-diagonal low-rank construction achieving strictly lower loss. Theorem 3 shows that at global optima, at most q_K ≤ 2 singular values of the normalized output matrix remain non-negligible as L grows, with all others decaying faster than exponentially in L. Experiments on MNIST and CIFAR-10 (ResNet-20 + fully connected head) confirm that low-rank solutions (3–4 singular values above 10⁻⁶) outperform DNC, and that the empirical probability of converging to DNC increases with network width d and decreases with regularization λ.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's central comparison is between DNC-structured solutions and explicitly constructed low-rank solutions within the deep UFM framework. The low-rank solution (a block-diagonal matrix with 2×2 blocks of [[1,−1],[−1,1]]) achieves strictly lower loss than any DNC solution at equal scale, both in the fit term (cross-entropy) and the regularization term (Schatten quasi-norm), for K≥4, L≥3. This extends Sukenik et al.'s [42] MSE-loss result to the cross-entropy setting. However, Theorem 4 shows that DNC solutions remain critical points with positive semi-definite Hessian for small λ, meaning they are local minima or degenerate saddles that gradient descent can converge to. Theorem 5 further shows that the dimension of the DNC solution space grows faster than that of any fixed-rank low-rank solution as width d increases (ratio R(d) → (K−1)/r > 1), providing a geometric explanation for why local optimization methods frequently land on DNC despite its suboptimality. The results also extend to ReLU activations (Theorem 7, requiring K≥10, L≥5), confirming the phenomenon is not an artifact of the linear model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Sukenik et al.'s demonstration that deep neural collapse (DNC) is suboptimal under MSE loss to the cross-entropy setting, and goes substantially further by providing a complete theoretical characterization of how low-rank bias shapes the entire loss landscape rather than merely exhibiting one better solution. The deep unconstrained feature model (UFM) framework builds on Mixon et al.'s single-layer UFM and Tirer and Bruna's deep extension, while the key mathematical tool—the Schatten quasi-norm equivalence for factorized L2 regularization—comes from Shang et al. The paper also departs from prior DNC optimality results (Zhu et al., Dang et al.) by showing that the strict-saddle landscape structure of the L=1 UFM fundamentally changes in deeper networks, where DNC becomes a local minimum rather than a saddle point.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first rigorous explanation for why DNC persists empirically despite being suboptimal: the dimensionality of the DNC solution space grows faster than that of low-rank alternatives as network width increases, making DNC a dominant attractor for local optimization. The finding that the loss surface geometry shifts from strict-saddle (L=1) to containing local minima (L≥2) due to low-rank bias is a conceptual shift in how we understand deep network optimization. Open questions include the role of initialization scale, optimizer choice, and implicit bias in determining whether training actually reaches DNC or the lower-rank global optimum, as well as whether these results extend beyond the UFM's overparameterization assumption to underparameterized or constrained regimes.
