## Problem

Transformer-based models for atmospheric time series forecasting (ATSF) capture global spatial-temporal correlations but require hundreds of millions of parameters and extended training times, limiting scalability to large-scale station networks. Prior work treated position embeddings as mere adjuncts to attention mechanisms, overlooking that the integration of geographical coordinates and temporal features into the model input can inherently encode the spatial-temporal structure dictated by atmospheric dynamics PDEs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252760/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a tensor of historical atmospheric observations (T_h steps × N stations × C variables), the 3D geographical coordinates (longitude, latitude, altitude) of each station, and discrete temporal features (hour-in-day, day-in-month, month-in-year) as input, and produces a tensor of T_f future time steps for all N stations and C variables. Notably, the model is channel-independent (each atmospheric variable is forecast by a separate model instance), and the parameter count is independent of N, so deployment cost remains static regardless of how many stations are served.

## Method

STELLA replaces all Transformer layers with a simple L-layer MLP (with residual connections) and injects spatial-temporal knowledge through a Spatial-Temporal Position Embedding (STPE) module. The spatial embedding maps normalized 3D station coordinates through a small FFN into a d-dimensional latent space, while the temporal embedding sums three learnable lookup tables for hour-in-day (24×d), day-in-month (31×d), and month-in-year (12×d). The theoretical justification (Theorem 1, Corollary 2) derives from the fundamental PDEs of atmospheric dynamics, showing that the time evolution of any atmospheric variable ν is a function of ν, time t, and coordinates (λ, φ, z), so explicitly providing these to the model via STPE allows even a shallow MLP to learn the correct spatial-temporal correlations without attention-based context aggregation. With only ~10k parameters (d=32, L=2) and linear computational complexity in N, STELLA outperforms 17 baselines including specialized ATSF Transformers across five datasets, and STPE can also be plugged into existing Transformer models to further improve their performance.

## Results

STELLA_opt achieves the best RMSE and MAE across all five datasets: GlobalWind (1.919/1.284), GlobalTemp (2.724/1.858), ChinaWind (7.104/4.869), ChinaTemp (4.112/2.975), and ChinaPM2.5 (26.15/13.85). Even the minimal STELLA-10k configuration (~10k parameters, 2-layer MLP, d=32) outperforms all 17 baselines on every dataset. The model requires only 30 seconds per epoch on GPU (141s on CPU) and 792 MB peak memory, compared to 11,739s and 18.41 GB for Corrformer.

## Baselines & Comparisons

STELLA_opt achieves the best RMSE and MAE on all five datasets, outperforming every one of the 17 baselines. Against the strongest Transformer baselines, STELLA_opt beats iTransformer by 0.050 RMSE on GlobalWind (1.919 vs 1.969) and by 0.226 on GlobalTemp (2.724 vs 2.950), and surpasses Corrformer by 0.616 RMSE on ChinaTemp (4.112 vs 4.728) and 1.92 on ChinaPM2.5 (26.15 vs 28.07). Among ATSF-specialized methods, AirFormer is the weakest performer (e.g., 5.594 RMSE on GlobalTemp vs STELLA's 2.724), while MRIformer and Corrformer are closer but still consistently outperformed. Among lightweight TSF methods, DLinear (2.005 RMSE on GlobalWind) and N-BEATS (2.031) are the closest competitors but trail STELLA-10k (1.933) by a clear margin, confirming that spatial-temporal knowledge integration is more effective than architectural complexity alone. STGNNs (AGCRN, MTGNN, GTS) suffer OOM errors on the global-scale datasets and are outperformed on national-scale ones (e.g., MTGNN 7.294 vs STELLA 7.104 on ChinaWind RMSE). Notably, STELLA-10k with only ~10k parameters already beats all baselines, while STELLA_opt (with dataset-tuned hyperparameters up to d=2048) extends the lead further.

## Relation to Prior Work

STELLA departs from the dominant Transformer-based ATSF paradigm (AirFormer, Corrformer, MRIformer) and STGNN approaches by arguing that their complex attention mechanisms are unnecessary for capturing spatial-temporal correlations. It builds conceptually on the 'are Transformers necessary?' line of work (DLinear, MTS-Mixers, FITS) but identifies a critical blind spot in those studies: they removed position embeddings along with attention, treating PE as merely auxiliary. STELLA's key reframe is that spatial-temporal position embedding (STPE), grounded in the PDE structure of atmospheric dynamics, can independently model spatial-temporal correlations without any attention mechanism, reducing the backbone to a simple MLP.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper challenges the field's working assumption that global spatial-temporal correlation modeling in ATSF requires attention or graph structures, offering a 10k-parameter alternative that trains in one hour on a single GPU and scales linearly with station count. STPE's demonstrated transferability to existing Transformers (iTransformer + STPE achieves new SOTA on GlobalWind) suggests it is a broadly useful component, not just a STELLA-specific trick. Open questions include whether the channel-independent MLP backbone can handle multivariate atmospheric coupling, whether physical constraints can mitigate over-smoothing on extreme events, and whether the approach generalizes beyond station-based to gridded forecasting.
