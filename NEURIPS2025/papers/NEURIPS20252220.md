## Problem

Large vision-language models (LVMs) frequently generate object hallucinations—plausible but factually incorrect descriptions—driven by biased parametric knowledge that overrides visual evidence. Existing mitigations are model-centric (fine-tuning or decoding interventions), requiring access to and modification of model internals. This paper asks whether hallucinations can be suppressed from the data side, via strategically crafted visual noise, in a fully black-box and training-free manner.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252220/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a text query prompt as input and produces a single perturbed image (x + δ) bounded within an ε-ball (ε=2 in practice) that is visually indistinguishable from the original. The perturbation is generated via zeroth-order optimization using a CLIP text encoder as a surrogate similarity model, requiring no gradient access to the target LVM. A key practical detail is that the perturbation can be generated by a smaller proxy model (e.g., Intern-VL2-1B) and transferred to a larger target (e.g., Intern-VL2-8B), reducing per-sample computation by up to 8× while retaining most of the hallucination-reduction benefit.

## Method

VAP formulates hallucination suppression as an adversarial optimization over the visual input with three complementary loss terms: (1) maximizing semantic alignment between the model's prompted and unprompted responses to the perturbed image, anchoring outputs to visual content; (2) minimizing semantic similarity between the perturbed-image response and a contrastive negative (Gaussian-noise-distorted) image response under the conditional prompt, exposing parametric bias; and (3) the same minimization under an unconditional (empty-prompt) setting, further suppressing linguistic priors. Semantic similarity is computed via a CLIP text encoder surrogate rather than the LLM's own representations, which lack sufficient discriminative power. Because LVMs are autoregressive and gradient computation is intractable, the optimization uses a zeroth-order (gradient-free) scheme that estimates the perturbation direction by sampling N random directions and evaluating the composite loss at each. The entire pipeline is training-free and black-box: no model weights are accessed or modified, and the resulting perturbation is imperceptible (step size α=1/255) while consistently reducing hallucination across POPE, BEAF, and CHAIR benchmarks on eight SOTA LVMs.

## Results

VAP consistently improves all 8 evaluated LVMs on POPE (text-axis hallucination), with the largest gain on Intern-VL2 reaching +2.81% accuracy and +2.09% F1 under adversarial sampling. On BEAF (vision/text-axis), TU improves by up to 2.31% (Ovis1.6-Gemma2) and F1_TUID by up to 1.74%, while CHAIR_I and CHAIR_S for open-ended captioning drop by up to 0.68 and 0.90 respectively (Intern-VL2). Gains are most pronounced under adversarial sampling, confirming that VAP effectively targets parametric knowledge bias rather than general perception. A proxy-based variant using a 1B model to generate perturbations for an 8B target retains most of the benefit at 1/8× computational cost.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares VAP against two model-centric decoding baselines—VCD (Visual Contrastive Decoding, Leng et al. CVPR 2024) and OPERA (Huang et al. CVPR 2024)—on four LVMs (LLaVA-v1.5, Qwen-VL2, Intern-VL2, DeepSeek-VL2) across POPE, BEAF, and CHAIR. For LLaVA-v1.5 under POPE-Adversarial, VAP achieves 80.97% Acc / 82.82% F1, outperforming OPERA (80.32 / 81.92) but trailing VCD (81.26 / 83.12) by roughly 0.3% in accuracy. On BEAF, VAP and VCD show comparable TU and F1_TUID gains, while on CHAIR both reduce hallucinated objects relative to the unmodified baseline. Crucially, the authors position VAP as orthogonal to both VCD and OPERA: VAP operates on the visual input (data-centric, black-box, training-free) whereas VCD and OPERA modify the decoding process (model-centric, requiring internal access), so the three can be stacked for compounded hallucination suppression. This orthogonality is the paper's key practical differentiator, as VAP adds no model modification and can be applied to any LVM via a lightweight proxy model at 1/8× compute cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant model-centric paradigm for hallucination mitigation (fine-tuning approaches like REVERIE/HalluciDoctor and decoding strategies like OPERA/VCD) by intervening at the visual input level instead of modifying model parameters or inference algorithms. It repurposes adversarial perturbation—traditionally a tool for attacking model robustness—as a corrective signal, using zeroth-order optimization (ZOO) to generate imperceptible noise that steers LVM responses toward visual grounding. The CLIP text encoder serves as a surrogate for measuring semantic alignment, a design choice that avoids the limited discriminative power of LLM-internal representations. The method is explicitly orthogonal to prior decoding-based approaches, as demonstrated by complementary gains when combined with OPERA and VCD.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, black-box nature of VAP makes it immediately deployable on any LVM without retraining or architectural changes, addressing a practical gap that model-centric methods cannot fill for closed-source or already-deployed systems. The proxy-based solution (1/8× compute cost) further lowers the barrier to adoption. However, limitations remain: model-specific hyperparameters (σ_i, T) must be tuned per architecture, no error bars are reported, and the approach has only been validated on object-level hallucination—whether it generalizes to attribute, spatial, or relational hallucinations is unexplored. An open question is whether the perturbations remain imperceptible and effective under distribution shift (e.g., medical or satellite imagery) beyond MS-COCO-style natural scenes.
