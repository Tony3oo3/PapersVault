## Problem

Factorization-based low-rank adaptation methods (e.g., LoRA) parameterize rank-r weights as W = PQᵀ, introducing non-unique redundancy and making convergence depend on the condition number of the Jacobian of the factorization map, which becomes very large for ill-conditioned target matrices. Moreover, standard adaptive optimizers like AdaGrad and AdamW cannot be directly and effectively applied to the low-rank factors. The paper asks whether one can optimize low-rank weights in a way that eliminates the Jacobian condition number dependence and properly extends adaptive metrics to the low-rank setting.

## Input / Output

The method takes frozen pretrained DNN weights, downstream training data, and a rank parameter r (typically 1–64), and produces low-rank adapter matrices maintained in SVD form (U ∈ ℝ^{n₀×r}, Σ ∈ ℝ^{r×r}, V ∈ ℝ^{n₁×r}) throughout training. Each update step involves O(r) matrix-vector products with the gradient plus an O((n₀+n₁)r² + r³) retraction via truncated SVD, with O((n₀+n₁)r) memory — the same asymptotic cost as factorization-based LoRA. The output is a fine-tuned model where the low-rank weights are added to the frozen base weights, applicable to both language models (GPT-2) and diffusion models (Stable Diffusion, Mix-of-Show).

## Method

The core contribution is performing Riemannian Gradient Descent (RGD) directly on the smooth manifold of fixed-rank matrices M_r, updating the entire weight matrix W rather than its factors P and Q. This eliminates the Jacobian condition number from the convergence rate, leaving only the Euclidean Hessian condition number as the limiting factor. To further reduce this Hessian condition number, the paper designs adaptive Riemannian metrics by restricting AdaGrad-style diagonal and Shampoo-style Kronecker-product preconditioners (L_t^{1/4} · Y · R_t^{1/4}, where L_t and R_t track exponential moving averages of row and column gradient norms) onto the tangent space T_W. RAdaGrad uses this metric with the r-truncated SVD retraction; RAdamW additionally introduces a first-moment momentum term M_t and a decoupled weight decay coefficient θ, making it a Riemannian analogue of AdamW. The tangent-space projection under the weighted metric is computed via L_t^{1/4}- and R_t^{1/4}-weighted orthonormalizations of U_t and V_t, keeping all operations at the same complexity as standard LoRA.

## Results

On GPT-2 fine-tuning (E2E benchmark, r=4), RAdamW achieves BLEU 69.8, NIST 8.81, MET 46.5, ROUGE-L 71.1, and CIDEr 2.51, consistently outperforming all baselines across all five metrics. On Mix-of-Show (r=4), RAdamW reaches CLIP 34.69 and FID 51.14, improving over Scaled AdamW by ~1.1 CLIP points and ~9.6 FID points. RAdaGrad achieves comparable quality (BLEU 69.8, CIDEr 2.49 at r=4) while using less memory than AdamW-based methods since it avoids storing historical gradient matrices. Gains are consistent across ranks 4, 8, and 16 on GPT-2.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RAdamW and RAdaGrad outperform all four baselines on every metric in both the GPT-2 (E2E) and Mix-of-Show experiments. Against vanilla LoRA+SGD, the proposed methods gain ~3.2 BLEU points and ~0.19 CIDEr on GPT-2 (r=4), and ~6.8 CLIP points on Mix-of-Show (r=4). Against Scaled GD/Scaled AdamW (which partially address Jacobian conditioning), RAdamW still leads by 0.3–0.8 BLEU and 0.03–0.09 CIDEr on GPT-2, and by ~1.1 CLIP and ~9.6 FID on Mix-of-Show (r=4). Against plain RGD (same Riemannian framework but standard metric), the adaptive metric in RAdaGrad/RAdamW adds ~0.6 BLEU and ~0.04–0.06 CIDEr on GPT-2, confirming the benefit of the Shampoo-inspired preconditioning. Against vanilla LoRA+AdamW, RAdamW improves by 0.7 BLEU and 0.04 CIDEr on GPT-2 (r=4), with the gap widening at higher ranks (e.g., 0.8 BLEU at r=8). Notably, RAdaGrad matches RAdamW's quality while using less memory, as it does not store the first-moment buffer M_t.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant factorization-based paradigm for low-rank adaptation (LoRA and its many variants) by optimizing the full weight matrix W directly on the Riemannian manifold of fixed-rank matrices, a geometric framework established by Vandereycken (2013) and Wei et al. (2020) for matrix completion. The key conceptual shift is that RGD on this manifold eliminates the Jacobian condition number from the convergence rate entirely, whereas all factorization-based methods (LoRA+, ScaledGD, LoRA-RITE, LoRA-PRO, DLRT) must contend with it. The adaptive metrics of RAdaGrad and RAdamW are constructed by restricting the diagonal/Kronecker-product preconditioners of AdaGrad, AdamW, and Shampoo to the tangent space of the manifold, bridging classical adaptive optimization with Riemannian geometry in a way not previously explored for low-rank DNN training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The clean separation of concerns—eliminating the Jacobian condition number via the manifold structure and mitigating the Hessian condition number via adaptive metrics—provides a principled, unified framework that is plug-and-play across architectures. This is practically significant for resource-constrained deployment where low-rank fine-tuning is the primary tool, and the consistent (if moderate) gains over ScaledGD/ScaledAdamW across LLMs, diffusion models, and DNN compression validate the approach. The main limitation is that the fixed-rank constraint inherently caps expressiveness relative to full fine-tuning, and the authors note future work on extending the framework to low-rank tensor weights. An open question is whether the manifold-based approach can incorporate more sophisticated second-order information (e.g., full Shampoo rather than the diagonal-Kronecker approximation) without prohibitive cost.
