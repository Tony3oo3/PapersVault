## Problem

Dexterous manipulation requires large, diverse, high-quality datasets, but existing data collection methods are fundamentally limited: human teleoperation is expensive and lab-bound, replay-based methods (e.g., DexMimicGen) can only apply spatial transformations to recorded trajectories without exploring novel manipulation strategies, and purely RL-based approaches produce non-human-like behaviors with poor sim-to-real transfer. No prior framework can efficiently scale dexterous manipulation data across diverse objects, environments, and spatial configurations while preserving human-like behavior patterns.

## Input / Output

The framework takes a single human demonstration collected via Apple Vision Pro VR teleoperation as its sole seed, along with randomized scenario configurations spanning 80 objects (varying geometry and mass), 12 environments (lighting, tabletop), and multiple spatial poses. The policy consumes a 224×224×3 RGB image, a 13D (or 26D for pour) object state vector, and 69D (single-arm) or 130D (dual-arm) proprioception, and outputs an 8-step action sequence where each step specifies a 6D end-effector pose and target joint angles (7 or 10 DOF depending on hand). The final output is a dataset of 2,000+ successful trajectories across 500+ scenario configurations per task, plus the trained base and residual policies.

## Method

DexFlyWheel operates as a two-stage self-improving data flywheel. In the warm-up stage, a single VR teleoperation demonstration is augmented via a MimicGen-based module (A_EP) across environment and spatial dimensions to form the initial dataset D₁. Each subsequent flywheel iteration performs four steps: (1) train a diffusion-based base policy (7-layer transformer, ViT-Small vision encoder, DDIM scheduler) via imitation learning on Dᵢ; (2) train a Soft Actor-Critic residual policy that takes only object state and proprioception as input and outputs fine-grained correction actions (scaled by α=0.1) added to the frozen base policy, using a progressive exploration schedule where the mixing coefficient ε linearly increases from 0 to 1 over 10,000 steps; (3) deploy the combined policy π_combined = π_base + α·π_res in OmniGibson simulation to collect K successful rollouts under randomized object configurations; (4) augment the rollouts with A_EP to produce Dᵢ₊₁. The key insight is that manipulating different objects induces only minor trajectory changes (validated via JointDiff and Residual Norm Ratio metrics), so the residual policy need only learn small corrections rather than full manipulation skills, enabling efficient generalization to novel geometries and masses.

## Results

DexFlyWheel achieves an average success rate of 81.9% on the multi-factor generalization test set (T_OEP) across four dexterous manipulation tasks (Grasp, Pour, Lift, Handover), starting from a single human demonstration per task. The framework generates over 2,000 diverse demonstrations spanning 2,040 scenario configurations (20 objects × 12 environments × 8.8 poses on average) by iteration 3. In real-world deployment via digital twin, the policy achieves 78.3% success on dual-arm lift and 63.3% on dual-arm handover. Data generation is both more robust (89.8% avg. success vs. 63.0% for DexMimicGen) and faster (2.4 h for 500 trajectories vs. 4.4 h for DexMimicGen and 12.5 h for human teleoperation).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DexFlyWheel substantially outperforms all baselines on the multi-factor generalization test set. Against DexMimicGen (Default), which receives the same single seed demonstration, DexFlyWheel achieves 81.9% vs. 31.4% average success rate — a 50.5 percentage-point improvement. Even against the stronger DexMimicGen (Enhanced) baseline, which is given 10 diverse human demonstrations (a 10× data advantage), DexFlyWheel still leads by 36.7 percentage points (81.9% vs. 45.2%). The gap is most pronounced on the Handover task (72.5% vs. 28.3%/42.5%) and Lift task (79.4% vs. 28.2%/43.7%), where dynamic bimanual coordination is most challenging. Human teleoperation baselines perform poorly (9.4%–13.4%), confirming that limited data diversity from fixed or even varied human demonstrations is insufficient for generalization. In data generation robustness, DexFlyWheel achieves 89.8% average success across tasks versus 63.0% for DexMimicGen, with the largest gap on Handover (85.7% vs. 14.8%), and collects 500 successful trajectories in 2.4 hours — 1.83× faster than DexMimicGen and 5.21× faster than human teleoperation.

## Relation to Prior Work

DexFlyWheel builds directly on MimicGen's trajectory-editing and spatial-augmentation pipeline [27], extending it to multi-dimensional environment and spatial randomization, and adopts the progressive mixing schedule from Policy Decorator [59] to stabilize residual RL exploration. Conceptually, it departs from replay-based data generation (e.g., DexMimicGen [14]) by reframing human demonstrations as behavioral priors for a learned policy rather than as trajectories to be spatially transformed, and from pure-RL approaches by anchoring exploration in human-like IL behavior. The self-improving flywheel—iteratively combining diffusion-policy IL, SAC-based residual RL, policy rollouts, and augmentation—is the paper's core architectural contribution, drawing an analogy to iterative self-improvement cycles in LLM post-training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Dexterous manipulation data scarcity is a recognized bottleneck, and showing that a single VR teleoperation demo can bootstrap 2,000+ diverse trajectories with 81.9% test success (vs. 13.4% for 20 human demos) offers a practical path to scaling dexterous policies without proportional human effort. The validated sim-to-real transfer (78.3% dual-arm lift) strengthens the case for simulation-first data pipelines. Open questions remain around manually engineered reward functions (the authors note LLM-driven reward generation as future work), the absence of tactile sensing, and whether the flywheel's gains plateau beyond iteration 3 (extended experiments show diminishing but nonzero returns).
