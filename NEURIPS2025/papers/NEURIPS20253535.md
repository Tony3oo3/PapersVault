## Problem

Combinatorial optimization over permutations (QAP, TSP, DFASP) lacks continuous differentiable extensions with theoretical guarantees linking the relaxed optimum to the discrete optimum. While Birkhoff's theorem guarantees every doubly stochastic matrix decomposes into a convex combination of permutation matrices, the decomposition is non-unique and existing algorithms are discontinuous, precluding gradient-based optimization. This paper fills that gap by constructing a continuous, almost-everywhere differentiable Birkhoff decomposition with provable rounding guarantees.

## Input / Output

The method takes a real-valued objective f on permutation matrices and a score matrix S (n×n, which can be random, derived from an approximate solution, or problem-specific such as MST-derived for TSP) and produces a permutation matrix via rounding of an optimized doubly stochastic matrix. The score matrix S is not merely a hyperparameter but actively shapes the extension: it induces a total ordering on permutations that determines the decomposition, and it can be dynamically updated during optimization to escape local minima while preserving solution quality. For concrete problems, inputs are pairwise distance/flow matrices (QAP), city coordinates (TSP), or directed edge sets (DFASP), and the output is the corresponding assignment, tour, or vertex ordering.

## Method

The key construction fixes a total order on all n! permutation matrices (induced by inner product with score matrix S) and, at each step of Birkhoff decomposition, always selects the highest-scoring valid perfect matching of the residual matrix. This yields coefficients α_ℓ that are Lipschitz continuous and a.e. differentiable functions of the input matrix A, making the extension F_S(A) = Σ α_k f(P_k) amenable to gradient-based optimization. Optimization proceeds via a Frank-Wolfe (projection-free) algorithm that maintains double stochasticity by taking convex combinations with permutation vertices, with the linear minimization oracle solved as a maximum-weight bipartite matching in O(n³) per step. A dynamic score-update strategy periodically replaces S with the best permutation found so far (plus small random noise), which is provably guaranteed not to degrade the rounded solution quality (Property 4-2) while helping escape local minima. The rounding scheme round_S(A) = argmin_k f(P_k) over the decomposition terms is lossless: f(round_S(A)) ≤ F_S(A), ensuring any approximate solution in the relaxed space yields a valid combinatorial solution of no worse quality.

## Results

On QAPLIB (136 instances, n=12–256), Birkhoff Extension (BE) with dynamic-score Frank-Wolfe achieves a 6.30% average gap to best-known solutions, outperforming Gurobi with Kaufman-Broeckx linearization (7.67%), 2-opt (10.38%), and Fast Approximate QAP (15.41%) within the same 2n-second time budget. On TSP, Gurobi remains optimal, but BE used as a local-improvement step reduces MST tour length by 4.60–8.53% and QP tour length by 0.58–3.25% across n=20–100. On DFASP (n up to 250), BE matches or exceeds Gurobi, with the largest gains at n=250 (e.g., 12,198.7 vs. 13,652.4 feedback arcs at p=0.9).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Gurobi with the Kaufman-Broeckx MILP formulation [30], BE wins on QAP (6.30% vs. 7.67% gap) in roughly a quarter of the runtime (23s vs. 90s), but loses on TSP where Gurobi returns exact optimal tours. Against Fast Approximate QAP [52], BE achieves a substantially smaller QAP gap (6.30% vs. 15.41%); on large-scale n=1000 QAP instances, BE initialized with the FAQ solution matches FAQ's 0.10% gap while pure BE (random init) reaches 14.40% at 4000s. Against 2-opt [16], BE dominates on QAP (6.30% vs. 10.38%) and on large-scale QAP (14.40% vs. 41.28% for 2-opt with 5 restarts at 2000s). Against the MST heuristic [14] on TSP, BE does not replace MST but acts as a local-improvement step, reducing tour length by 4.60–8.53% (e.g., 9.836 vs. 10.288 at n=100). On DFASP, Gurobi [26] is competitive at small n but BE pulls ahead at n=250 across all edge densities (e.g., 6,459.8 vs. 6,831.7 at p=0.5; 12,198.7 vs. 13,652.4 at p=0.9).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Birkhoff's classical decomposition of doubly stochastic matrices into convex combinations of permutation matrices, but addresses a critical gap: prior decomposition algorithms are discontinuous because they make arbitrary choices at each step. The key conceptual departure is fixing a total order over permutations via a score matrix, which yields a continuous and a.e. differentiable decomposition — a property no prior Birkhoff decomposition algorithm possessed. The paper also draws on the Frank-Wolfe projection-free optimization framework (previously applied to Birkhoff polytope optimization by Tewari et al. and Jaggi for structured sparsity, but not for combinatorial optimization with rounding guarantees) and positions itself as the permutation-function analogue of the set function extension framework of Karalias et al., which handles set functions but has no natural extension to permutation-structured problems.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Birkhoff extension provides a general, theoretically grounded framework for gradient-based optimization of any real-valued function on permutations, with the strong guarantee that rounding never degrades solution quality — a property that is rare among continuous relaxations. The local improvement property (using any existing approximate solution as the score matrix) makes it a practical plug-in for existing solvers. However, the O(n^5) per-iteration cost, the non-convexity of the extension, and the fact that Gurobi still dominates on TSP limit immediate practical impact. Open questions include more efficient score-update strategies, GPU-friendly matching to reduce the polynomial overhead, and whether the neural combinatorial optimization application (currently a proof-of-concept) can scale to competitive performance.
