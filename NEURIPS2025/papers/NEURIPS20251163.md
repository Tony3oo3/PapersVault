## Problem

Cross-modal embedding models (CLIP, BLIP) achieve strong image-text alignment but underperform modality-specific experts (DINOv2, E5) on domain-specific benchmarks, and no principled, training-free method exists to fuse a cross-modal embedding with a uni-modal expert that lacks an encoder for the other modality. The paper asks how to combine multiple embeddings so that the fused representation's kernel encodes the strict union of clusters identified by each parent embedding.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251163/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes frozen, pre-trained embedding vectors from multiple encoders (e.g., 512-d CLIP image/text, 768-d DINOv2 image, 768-d S-RoBERTa/E5 text) and produces a single fused embedding of dimension l (typically 3000, 5000 for ImageNet) per input sample. For cross-modal + uni-modal fusion, the uni-modal embedding is symmetrized by concatenating a constant-offset copy with the original, and the missing modality is imputed with a constant vector scaled by hyperparameter C, so the Kronecker product remains balanced across domains. No gradient updates or fine-tuning are required; all encoders remain frozen.

## Method

KrossFuse defines the fused kernel as the element-wise product of individual kernel similarity functions, which is mathematically equivalent to the Kronecker product of the corresponding feature maps, yielding an 'all parents must agree' clustering property. To avoid the prohibitive d₁×d₂ dimensional Kronecker space, RP-KrossFuse projects each embedding independently to l dimensions via random matrices with unit-variance uniform entries, then takes the Hadamard (element-wise) product of the projected vectors, reducing cost to O(l(d₁+d₂)) per sample. For fusing a cross-modal model (e.g., CLIP) with a uni-modal expert (e.g., DINOv2), a symmetrized embedding is constructed for the uni-modal model so that the shared modality contributes C + k(γ(x),γ(x′)) while the non-shared modality contributes only C·k(ψ(t),ψ(t′)), preserving cross-modal alignment. Theorem 1 provides a high-probability bound showing the projected inner products approximate the true Kronecker-product kernel within O(√(log n / l)), and Appendix A.4 extends the framework to infinite-dimensional shift-invariant kernels (RBF, Laplace) via jointly-sampled random Fourier features.

## Results

On ImageNet linear-probe classification, RP-KrossFuse achieves 84.1% top-1 accuracy, surpassing CLIP (73.2%), DINOv2 (83.3%), and all fusion baselines (GATE 81.4%, ATTN 79.5%, KPoMRP 79.4%, COMM 82.7%). On the text SentEval benchmark (7 tasks), RP-KrossFuse reaches 88.0–88.1% average accuracy, outperforming CLIP (81.8–83.2%), GATE (85.9–86.2%), ATTN (85.5–85.8%), and S-RoBERTa (86.2%). In cross-modal few-shot classification (1-shot), RP-KrossFuse with both modalities (I+T) achieves 53.5% average accuracy versus 44.3% for CLIP(I+T) and 45.8% for DINOv2(I), with the gap widening at 8-shot (67.1% vs. 56.7% and 63.7%). Zero-shot image-text retrieval on MSCOCO and Flickr30k shows differences below 1% compared to CLIP, confirming that the fusion preserves cross-modal alignment.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RP-KrossFuse consistently outperforms CLIP on ImageNet (+10.9 pp), SVHN (+3.3 pp), and ImageNet-A (+24.4 pp), while matching CLIP on GTSRB (82.7 vs. 83.1) and slightly trailing on ImageNet-R (67.4 vs. 68.8). Against DINOv2, RP-KrossFuse wins on ImageNet (+0.8 pp), GTSRB (+10.2 pp), and SVHN (+6.4 pp), but is marginally behind on IN-A (47.6 vs. 48.5) and IN-R (67.4 vs. 68.8), confirming the complementary-strengths hypothesis. Among fusion baselines, RP-KrossFuse beats GATE by 2.7 pp on ImageNet, ATTN by 4.6 pp, KPoMRP by 4.7 pp, and COMM by 1.4 pp, with the largest gaps on OOD benchmarks (e.g., +12.7 pp over GATE on IN-A). On the text SentEval suite, RP-KrossFuse (88.0% avg) surpasses the strongest text baseline S-RoBERTa (86.2%) by 1.8 pp and the best fusion baseline GATE (86.2%) by 1.9 pp, while KPoMRP degrades below CLIP (78.1% vs. 81.8%), highlighting the importance of the joint random-projection design. In cross-modal few-shot settings, RP-KrossFuse(I+T) leads all baselines at every shot count, with the advantage most pronounced at 1-shot (53.5% vs. 44.3% CLIP, 45.8% DINOv2) and 2-shot (59.3% vs. 49.0%, 51.9%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper grounds embedding fusion in the classical kernel methods result that the product of two kernels is itself a kernel, with feature map given by the Kronecker product of individual feature maps (Schölkopf & Smola; Berlinet & Thomas-Agnan). It departs from prior training-based fusion strategies (GATE/MoE, attentional fusion, MLP projectors like COMM) by offering a training-free, algebraic construction, and extends the random Fourier features framework of Rahimi & Recht to jointly sample RFFs for the Kronecker product under shift-invariant kernels. The specific setting of fusing a cross-modal model (e.g., CLIP) with a unimodal expert (e.g., DINOv2) that lacks the other modality's encoder is, per the authors, unexplored in prior literature, and the symmetrized embedding with a constant imputation for the missing modality is a new practical device.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a principled, training-free mechanism to combine complementary embeddings without retraining, which is practically attractive for practitioners who already have a portfolio of frozen encoders. The clean 'all parent embeddings must agree' cluster-unification property gives an interpretable guarantee that the fused kernel captures the intersection of discriminative structures. Limitations include the need for a relatively high projection dimension (~3000) and a tuning constant C, and the gains on ImageNet are largely attributable to preserving DINOv2's strength rather than a fundamental new capability. Open questions include extension to non-visual modalities, multi-way fusion beyond two embeddings, and whether attention-based refinement of the fused space could close the remaining gap to task-specific fine-tuning.
