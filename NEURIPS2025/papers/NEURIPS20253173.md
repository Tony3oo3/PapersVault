## Problem

In conditional flow matching, the velocity network must simultaneously transport probability mass from a condition-agnostic Gaussian source to the correct region of the data manifold and encode the semantic meaning of the condition, imposing a dual burden that stretches learned trajectories and slows convergence. Moreover, the paper shows that allowing arbitrary reparameterizations of the source or target distributions admits trivial zero-cost minima that collapse the generative distribution to a single mode, explaining empirical failures observed in end-to-end VAE-diffusion training.

## Input / Output

The method takes a class label (condition) and a standard Gaussian noise sample as input, and produces a generated RGB image as output. On ImageNet-256, generation occurs in the 32×32×4 latent space of the sd-vae-ft-ema VAE, where lightweight convolutional shift networks map 1152-dimensional class embeddings to latent-space shift tensors; the final 256×256 image is recovered via the VAE decoder. On CIFAR-10 (32×32), the method operates directly in pixel space without a VAE.

## Method

CAR-Flow introduces condition-dependent additive shifts μ₀(y) and μ₁(y) applied to the source and target distributions respectively, so that the flow-matching path z_t = β_t(x₀+μ₀(y)) + α_t(x₁+μ₁(y)) is shortened by aligning both endpoints with the condition. The paper proves (Claim 1) that unrestricted reparameterizations admit five distinct zero-cost collapse modes, and shows that restricting to shift-only maps (preserving scale) eliminates all of them while retaining the path-shortening benefit. Three variants are studied—source-only, target-only, and joint—with the joint variant performing best. The shift networks are extremely lightweight (9 parameters per class in the 1-D synthetic setting; ~2.4M parameters each on ImageNet, <0.3% of the SiT-XL/2 backbone), and are trained with a higher learning rate than the velocity backbone to accelerate alignment. On ImageNet-256, the joint CAR-Flow variant reduces FID from 2.07 to 1.68 (with cfg=1.5, 7M steps) while adding fewer than 0.6% parameters.

## Results

On ImageNet-256, the joint CAR-Flow variant reduces FID from 2.07 to 1.68 (7M steps, cfg=1.5) while adding fewer than 0.6% parameters (~4M out of 679M). At the shorter 400K-step budget, FID drops from 17.28 to 13.91, with IS improving from 78.88 to 87.96 and SFID from 5.71 to 5.38. On CIFAR-10 (pixel-space, 400K steps), the source-only variant achieves FID 7.5 versus the baseline's 13.8. On 1-D synthetic data, the joint variant halves the average trajectory length (0.71 vs. 1.54) and converges fastest in Wasserstein distance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is SiT-XL/2 (Ma et al., ECCV 2024), re-implemented in JAX with the original training recipe. CAR-Flow outperforms SiT-XL/2 on every reported metric at both 400K and 7M training steps: at 400K steps the joint variant improves FID by 3.37 points (17.28→13.91), IS by 9.08 points, and SFID by 0.33; at 7M steps with cfg=1.5, FID drops by 0.39 (2.07→1.68) while IS rises by 23.8 and recall improves from 0.58 to 0.62. All three CAR-Flow variants (source-only, target-only, joint) beat the baseline, with the joint variant consistently best. The gains come at negligible cost: each shift network adds only ~2.4M parameters (~0.35% of the 675M backbone). On CIFAR-10, the source-only variant more than halves FID (13.8→7.5), though the joint variant (10.6) is less impressive there, suggesting the benefit of dual shifts is most pronounced at higher resolution.

## Relation to Prior Work

CAR-Flow extends conditional flow matching by reparameterizing the source and/or target distributions with condition-dependent shifts, subsuming latent diffusion (VAE encoder/decoder) as a special case where the target map is condition-agnostic. It departs from representation-alignment approaches (REPA, DINOv2-based latent alignment) that modify internal network features, instead relocating the probability path endpoints directly. The paper also formalizes and explains the mode-collapse failure observed empirically in REPA-E's end-to-end VAE-diffusion training, identifying it as a trivial zero-cost minimum of the flow-matching objective under unrestricted reparameterization, and shows that the simplest constraint—shift-only maps—eliminates all such degeneracies while retaining the path-shortening benefit.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The shift-only reparameterization adds fewer than 0.6% parameters yet reduces ImageNet-256 FID from 2.07 to 1.68, making it an easily adoptable plug-in for existing flow-matching pipelines. The theoretical characterization of mode collapse under general reparameterization provides a principled explanation for why naive end-to-end VAE-diffusion tuning degrades, guiding future work on jointly trained latent spaces. Open questions include whether more general (e.g., volume-preserving) maps beyond shifts yield further gains, how the approach interacts with text-conditioned or multi-modal conditioning, and whether the observed learning-rate sensitivity between shift networks and the backbone generalizes to other architectures.
