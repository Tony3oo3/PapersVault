## Problem

Despite the rapid growth of diverse hand interaction datasets (hand-object, hand-hand, hand-scene, hand-body), no prior work has built a dense hand contact estimation model trained across such heterogeneous data. Two fundamental obstacles remain unaddressed: severe class imbalance (up to 58:1 non-contact to contact vertex ratios) and spatial imbalance where contact is overwhelmingly concentrated at fingertips, causing models to fail on contact patterns in other hand regions.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image as input and produces per-vertex contact probabilities for all 778 vertices of the MANO hand model. The output supports multi-level supervision by projecting the dense logits to four coarser resolutions (778, 336, 84, and 21 vertices) via learned regressors, enabling hierarchical training signals. A learnable contact initialization embedding is added before the final sigmoid, providing a data-driven prior that outperforms fixed or dataset-mean initializations.

## Method

HACO uses a ViT-H backbone (initialized from HaMeR) that encodes the image into tokens, which are then fused with a single contact query token through stacked self-attention and cross-attention Transformer layers. To address class imbalance, Balanced Contact Sampling (BCS) computes a per-instance contact balance score measuring deviation from the dataset-wide mean contact vector, partitions samples into K bins via logarithmic spacing, and applies stratified resampling so each bin contributes equally during training. To address spatial imbalance, the Vertex-Level Class-Balanced (VCB) loss extends the standard class-balanced loss by computing a separate effective-sample-count weight for each of the 778 vertices based on its local contact frequency, applied progressively from global CB weighting to full vertex-level weighting over training epochs. The full training objective combines VCB loss, a mesh-topology smoothness loss that penalizes spatially fragmented contact regions, and an L1 regularization toward the dataset mean contact pattern, trained for 10 epochs on 655K images from 14 diverse interaction datasets.

## Results

On the MOW dataset, HACO achieves 0.525 precision, 0.607 recall, and 0.522 F1-score for dense hand contact estimation, substantially outperforming the strongest prior method DECO (0.246/0.235/0.197) by over 2× in F1-score. On RICH, HACO reaches 0.741/0.899/0.781, and on Hi4D it achieves 0.555/0.636/0.565. For the downstream 3D grasp optimization task on DexYCB, HACO attains lower MPJPE (36.52 mm vs. 37.16 mm) and higher contact F1-score (0.666 vs. 0.612) than ContactOpt, despite using only image input rather than full 3D meshes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HACO decisively outperforms all three full-body human-scene contact methods adapted to the hand domain: POSA (F1 0.101), BSTRO (F1 0.112), and DECO (F1 0.197) on MOW, with HACO reaching 0.522 F1 — a 2.6× improvement over the best prior method. Even when POSA, BSTRO, and DECO are retrained as hand-specific variants on the same 14 datasets (Table A3), HACO still leads on every test set (e.g., 0.522 vs. 0.408 F1 on MOW, 0.781 vs. 0.421 on RICH). For 3D grasp optimization on DexYCB, HACO beats ContactOpt on MPJPE (36.52 vs. 37.16), precision (0.558 vs. 0.522), recall (0.877 vs. 0.830), and F1 (0.666 vs. 0.612), while Inter Vol. is nearly tied (29.26 vs. 29.18). In 3D hand-object reconstruction via EasyHOI, swapping in HACO's contact predictions improves MPVPE (21.09 vs. 21.25), MPJPE (20.85 vs. 20.97), and CD_ho (8.19 vs. 8.34) over EasyHOI's original heuristic contact. Against contact-estimation modules embedded in other systems (DefConNet from Decaf, Stage-1 CHOI, InteractionNet from DICE, LatentAct's Data Engine), HACO achieves the highest F1-score (0.522) on MOW, surpassing the next-best reproduced module (CHOI at 0.407) by over 28% in F1.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the class-balanced (CB) loss of Cui et al. [6] from global class-level weighting to a vertex-level spatial formulation (VCB loss), and introduces a hand-level sampling strategy (BCS) that proxies for per-vertex imbalance. It positions itself against dense human-scene contact estimation methods (POSA, BSTRO, DECO) that operate on full-body models (SMPL/SMPL-X) and single-dataset training, arguing that hand contact has unique imbalance characteristics (fingertip concentration, extreme class skew up to 58:1) that these methods do not address. The work also connects to 3D hand-object interaction reasoning (ContactOpt, EasyHOI) where contact serves as a downstream signal, demonstrating that image-based contact prediction can replace geometry-driven or heuristic contact estimation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

HACO is the first dense hand contact estimation model trained on a large-scale, multi-domain dataset (14 datasets, 655K images spanning hand-object, hand-hand, hand-scene, and hand-body interactions), establishing a strong baseline for the task. The vertex-level reweighting idea is a simple but effective generalization that could transfer to other dense prediction tasks with spatially non-uniform class distributions. Limitations include prediction of only the right hand, dependence on the MANO model, and evaluation on a limited set of test datasets; open questions include whether the imbalance strategies generalize to other body parts or to contact estimation in video, and whether the large performance gap over prior methods is primarily driven by data scale or by the specific imbalance handling.
