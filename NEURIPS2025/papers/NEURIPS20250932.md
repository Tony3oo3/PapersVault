## Problem

Existing panoramic image generation methods face two fundamental issues: diffusion-based models violate the i.i.d. Gaussian noise assumption due to the spatial distortions of equirectangular projection, and text-to-panorama and panorama outpainting are treated as separate tasks requiring distinct architectures and task-specific data pipelines. This paper seeks a unified, theoretically sound generative paradigm that naturally accommodates ERP geometry and handles both text- and image-conditioned generation within a single framework.

## Input / Output

PAR accepts a text prompt (encoded via a pre-trained text encoder into embeddings of dimension d) and optionally a partial panoramic image with a binary mask indicating known regions, producing a full 512×1024 equirectangular panoramic image. The key formulation unifies both tasks: outpainting is a special case of text-to-panorama where known image tokens serve as partially observed positions in the autoregressive sequence, with the decoding order being arbitrary rather than raster-scan. The model operates on continuous latent tokens (not discrete VQ codes) compressed by a frozen VAE, and inference uses classifier-free guidance with a coefficient of 5 over 64 AR steps.

## Method

PAR is built on masked autoregressive modeling: a transformer encoder-decoder (initialized from NOVA) processes patchified visual tokens with randomly masked positions and fused text embeddings, outputting a conditional signal z that drives a lightweight MLP denoising network ε_θ to reconstruct the masked latent regions. Two key innovations address ERP-specific challenges: (1) dual-space circular padding, where cyclic padding is applied in pixel space before the VAE encoder (pre-padding) and in latent space before the VAE decoder (post-padding), ensuring sufficient receptive field context at the horizontal wrap-around boundary; and (2) a cyclic translation consistency loss that enforces equivariance under horizontal shifts by aligning model outputs on shifted and original inputs, explicitly regularizing the model toward ERP's periodic structure. The total objective combines the masked denoising loss with the consistency term (λ=0.1), and the entire system is trained end-to-end with a single likelihood objective that requires no task-specific data engineering.

## Results

On the Matterport3D text-to-panorama benchmark, PAR-1.4B achieves 37.37 FID and 3.75 FAED, outperforming the strongest diffusion-based baseline PanFusion (45.21 FID, 5.12 FAED) while using only 1.4B vs. 1.9B parameters. Even the smallest PAR-0.3B model (41.15 FID, 3.39 FAED, 0.58 DS) surpasses PanFusion on all metrics except CLIP Score (30.21 vs. 31.07). For panorama outpainting, PAR with a text prompt achieves 32.68 FID and 12.20 FID-h, substantially outperforming 2S-ODIS (52.59 FID, 35.18 FID-h) and AOG-Net (83.02 FID, 37.88 FID-h).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PAR outperforms all AR-based baselines (Text2Light, PanoLlama) by a wide margin on every metric, with FID gaps of 29–66 points and DS improvements from 7.55/13.99 down to 0.58. Against the strongest diffusion-based baseline PanFusion, PAR-0.3B wins on FAED (3.39 vs. 5.12), FID (41.15 vs. 45.21), and DS (0.58 vs. 0.71) while trailing slightly on CLIP Score (30.21 vs. 31.07), which the authors attribute to CLIP's perspective-image bias. In the appendix, PAR-0.3B also beats UniPano (44.74 FID, 0.77 DS) and DiffPano (53.29 FID, 6.16 DS) on text-to-panorama. For panorama outpainting, PAR with prompt achieves 32.68 FID / 12.20 FID-h, beating 2S-ODIS (52.59 / 35.18) by ~20 FID points and AOG-Net (83.02 / 37.88) by ~50 FID points. On zero-shot OOD evaluation (SUN360, Structured3D), PAR outperforms Diffusion360, PanoDiff, and StitchDiffusion (DS 0.63 vs. 1.12), confirming generalization beyond the training distribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

PAR builds directly on the masked autoregressive (MAR) framework introduced by NOVA [14] and the broader MAR lineage (MaskGIT, MAR [30]), repurposing it for the first time as a panoramic image generation backbone. It departs from the dominant diffusion-based paradigm for panorama generation (PanFusion, Diffusion360, PanoDiff) by arguing that equirectangular projection inherently violates the i.i.d. Gaussian noise assumption that diffusion models rely on, and provides a formal proof of this non-i.i.d. property in the appendix. The two key architectural adaptations—dual-space circular padding and cyclic translation consistency loss—are novel contributions that address the geometric mismatch between perspective-image-pretrained components (VAE, transformer) and the periodic topology of ERP panoramas, a gap not addressed by prior AR-based panorama attempts (Text2Light, PanoLlama) which either use low-resolution discrete tokens or stitch perspective images without correct spherical geometry.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PAR offers a theoretically grounded alternative to diffusion for panoramic generation and demonstrates that a single 0.3B–1.4B MAR model can unify text-to-panorama, outpainting, and zero-shot editing without task-specific data engineering, reducing the fragmentation of the panorama generation pipeline. The practical impact is notable: the 0.3B model outperforms the 1.9B PanFusion on both FID and FAED while running ~3× faster at inference. However, the authors acknowledge a visible gap in fine details and textures compared to real panoramas, attributing it to the scarcity of high-quality panoramic training data—an open question that limits immediate deployment in production VR/AR pipelines. Whether MAR can fully replace diffusion for all panoramic tasks at higher resolutions and with richer conditioning remains to be established.
