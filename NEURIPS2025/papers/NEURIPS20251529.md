## Problem

Test-time scaling has proven effective for diffusion models (via latent path searching) and LLMs (via tree/graph search), but no general framework exists for Visual Auto-Regressive (VAR) models. Directly porting diffusion-based strategies fails because VAR's causal generation makes early tokens irreversible—unlike iterative denoising, a poor early-scale token cannot be corrected later—and reward functions at early scales poorly predict final image quality. This paper is the first to design a test-time scaling approach that respects VAR's hierarchical coarse-to-fine structure.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt (encoded via Flan-T5) and produces N final RGB images at the target resolution (1024×1024 for Infinity-2B, 512×512 for Infinity-8B). During generation, the number of concurrent samples follows an adaptive descending schedule (e.g., 8N→6N→4N→2N→1N across 13 scales), so the intermediate state is a batch of partially-generated multi-scale feature maps that progressively narrows. The final output is a set of N complete images from which the user selects the best.

## Method

TTS-VAR models VAR generation as a path-searching problem with three integrated components. First, an adaptive descending batch size schedule exploits the fact that early scales consume far less FLOPs and RAM, allowing more concurrent samples where computation is cheap. Second, at early scales (e.g., scales 2 and 5), where reward functions cannot reliably rank intermediate images, the method applies K-Means++ clustering on DINOv2 semantic features to preserve structural diversity when reducing batch size, ensuring dissimilar layouts survive to later stages. Third, at late scales (e.g., scales 6 and 9), where intermediate image quality correlates strongly with final quality, the method computes potential scores using ImageReward and resamples from a multinomial distribution to preferentially continue generation from superior candidates. The key design insight is that VAR's irreversible causal generation demands a dual strategy—diversity preservation early, reward-guided selection late—rather than uniform reward-based selection across all scales.

## Results

On GenEval, TTS-VAR applied to Infinity 2B achieves an overall score of 0.7530 at N=8, an 8.7% improvement over the base model (0.6946), surpassing Stable Diffusion 3 (8B parameters, 0.74) while using 60% fewer parameters. Even at minimal overhead (N=2), TTS-VAR reaches 0.7403, outperforming Best-of-N at N=8 (0.7364) with only 25% of the samples. On T2I-CompBench, TTS-VAR (N=8) achieves 0.6230 average, exceeding DALL-E 3 (0.6168) and FLUX.1 (0.6087). On the stronger Infinity 8B model, TTS-VAR (N=4) further boosts GenEval from 0.7646 to 0.8188.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TTS-VAR (Infinity 2B, N=8) achieves 0.7530 GenEval, surpassing SD3 (0.74, 8B params) with 60% fewer parameters, and outperforming DALL-E 3 (0.67), FLUX (0.66), and SDXL (0.55) by wide margins. Among AR baselines, TTS-VAR exceeds Emu3 (0.54), Show-o+PARM (0.67), Chameleon (0.39), and LlamaGen (0.32) substantially. Against test-time scaling baselines on the same Infinity 2B backbone, TTS-VAR at N=2 (0.7403) already beats Best-of-N at N=8 (0.7364) and Importance Sampling at N=8 (0.7181), demonstrating superior sample efficiency. On T2I-CompBench, TTS-VAR (0.6230 avg) edges out DALL-E 3 (0.6168) and FLUX.1 (0.6087), while on DPG-Bench (N=4) it achieves 82.94 overall versus BoN's 82.52 and the Infinity baseline's 81.72. On the stronger Infinity 8B model, TTS-VAR (N=4) improves GenEval from 0.7646 to 0.8188, outperforming BoN (N=4) at 0.7995.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the path-searching formulation of test-time scaling from diffusion models (Ma et al., Singhal et al.) to the Visual Auto-Regressive (VAR) paradigm, where generation proceeds hierarchically from coarse to fine scales. Unlike prior AR image-generation scaling methods (Guo et al., Jiang et al.) that require additional reinforcement learning or unified understanding-generation models, TTS-VAR operates entirely at inference time without retraining. The key conceptual departure from diffusion-based test-time scaling is the recognition that VAR's causal, irreversible token generation makes early-scale reward scoring unreliable, necessitating a split strategy: structural diversity preservation via DINOv2-based clustering at early scales and reward-guided resampling at late scales.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TTS-VAR provides the first training-free test-time scaling recipe for VAR models, achieving an 8.7% GenEval improvement (0.69→0.75) with minimal compute overhead, and demonstrates that N=2 with the proposed method outperforms Best-of-N at N=8. The empirical finding that reward-based selection actively degrades quality at early VAR scales (below ~scale 6) is a useful caution for anyone attempting to port diffusion-style test-time scaling to autoregressive image models. Open questions include whether the clustering/resampling split point generalizes across VAR architectures and resolutions, whether the framework extends to video or 3D generation, and whether the reliance on external reward models (ImageReward) limits robustness in out-of-distribution domains.
