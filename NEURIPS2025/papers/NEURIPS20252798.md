## Problem

Conventional goal-conditioned policies concatenate goal and current observations as inputs to a fixed-parameter network, forcing the same weights to handle all goal-state combinations. This conflates what to process (current state) with how to process it (goal-dependent strategy), causing poor generalization to novel goals and near-complete failure in high-variability manipulation environments.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252798/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a context window of two front-view RGB images (128×128) plus a 9-dimensional proprioceptive vector (8-dim on the real robot, where end-effector pose is unavailable), along with a single goal RGB image. The hypernetwork outputs goal-specific weights for a lightweight 3-layer MLP policy, which then produces continuous joint-angle and gripper actions. Notably, only one goal image is required (not a sequence), and the shaped latent space provides a built-in distance metric for autonomous goal-completion detection without external supervision.

## Method

Hyper-GoalNet reframes goal-conditioned policy learning as a parameter generation task: an optimization-inspired hypernetwork (HyPoGen with 8 refinement blocks) takes encoded current and goal images and iteratively produces weights for a target MLP policy, so the goal determines the processing mechanism rather than serving as an additional input feature. Two latent-space shaping losses are jointly trained end-to-end with the behavior-cloning objective: a forward dynamics model that makes state transitions predictable in the latent space, and a margin-based distance constraint enforcing monotonic decrease in Euclidean distance to the goal along successful trajectories. The visual encoder (R3M) is frozen for the first 20 epochs then unfrozen to co-adapt with the hypernetwork. At inference, the hypernetwork generates policy weights once per goal, the target policy processes the observation sequence to produce actions, and the latent distance to goal serves as a natural stopping criterion.

## Results

On contact-rich manipulation tasks (Table 1), Hyper-GoalNet achieves an average success rate of 0.52, substantially outperforming C-BeT (0.32), MimicPlay (0.12), and GCBC/Play-LMP (0.00). On long-horizon tasks (Table 2), the method reaches 0.78 average success rate versus 0.59 for C-BeT and 0.35 for MimicPlay. The advantage is most pronounced at higher difficulty levels (d1–d2), where C-BeT drops to 0.00 on Coffee d1 while Hyper-GoalNet maintains 0.76. In real-robot experiments, Hyper-GoalNet achieves 14–15/15 successes across four tasks, while the best baseline (C-BeT) manages only 2–8/15.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Hyper-GoalNet decisively outperforms all five baselines. GCBC and Play-LMP (both from [29]) achieve 0.00 success rate across every task and difficulty level, which the authors attribute to severe overfitting of their log-likelihood objective. MimicPlay [53] reaches only 0.12 average on contact-rich tasks and 0.35 on long-horizon tasks, struggling particularly with contact-rich interactions. C-BeT [9] is the strongest baseline at 0.32 average (contact-rich) and 0.59 (long-horizon), but collapses to 0.00 on Coffee d1 and Three-Piece Assembly d0/d2, whereas Hyper-GoalNet maintains 0.76 and 0.52/0.20 respectively. In the hypernetwork architecture ablation (Table 4), HyperZero [41] with two stabilization variants (ScalarInit, Bias-Init) achieves only 16% average on Coffee, compared to 77% for Hyper-GoalNet's optimization-inspired design with standard initialization. On real hardware, the gap widens further: Hyper-GoalNet scores 14–15/15 while C-BeT manages at most 8/15, confirming that parameter-adaptive generation better handles sensor noise and environmental variation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reframes goal-conditioned policy learning by repurposing the HyPoGen optimization-inspired hypernetwork architecture [40] to generate task-specific policy parameters from goal images, rather than conditioning a fixed network on concatenated goal-state inputs as in GCBC, Play-LMP, C-BeT, and MimicPlay. The conceptual departure is treating the goal as a specification that determines *how* observations are processed (i.e., which weights to use) rather than as an additional feature to be processed alongside the current state. The latent space shaping via forward dynamics prediction and monotonic distance constraints is a novel auxiliary objective layered on top of the R3M visual encoder, and the overall training paradigm is reward-free behavior cloning on MimicGen demonstrations rather than the reinforcement learning or likelihood-based objectives used by most prior goal-conditioned methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The parameter-adaptive formulation offers a practical path toward more robust goal-conditioned manipulation: by generating compact goal-specific policies rather than relying on a single fixed network to handle all goal-state combinations, the method maintains performance under high environmental variability where fixed-parameter baselines collapse entirely. The shaped latent space also provides a built-in, supervision-free goal completion detector (86.6% accuracy, 94.3% recall), reducing the need for external task-specific success criteria. Key open questions include whether the approach generalizes to out-of-distribution goals (the authors acknowledge erratic policy generation in this regime), whether it scales to multi-goal sequential planning, and how to integrate safety guarantees for the dynamically generated parameters in offline-trained settings.
