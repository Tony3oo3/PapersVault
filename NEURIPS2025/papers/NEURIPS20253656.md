## Problem

Existing hierarchical diffusion methods for offline RL assume a fixed two-layer hierarchy with a single predefined temporal scale, limiting adaptability to diverse task complexities and reducing decision-making flexibility. Additionally, these methods rely on reward predictions over localized sub-trajectories and overfit to the limited state coverage of offline datasets, causing extrapolation errors under distributional shift.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253656/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

At training time, the method consumes a static offline dataset of state-action-reward trajectories to construct a k-nearest-neighbor state graph and derive an optimal encoding tree via structural entropy minimization. At inference, given an initial state, a planning horizon H, and the maximal cumulative reward from the dataset, the planner iteratively denoises latent sequences across K diffusion layers (K=3 for Gym-MuJoCo, K=4 for navigation tasks) and outputs a flat action sequence of length H. Subgoal and state-action segments are padded to fixed lengths (8 or 16 timesteps) to handle variable community sizes.

## Method

SIHD constructs an adaptive multi-scale diffusion hierarchy by building a k-NN state graph over offline states, then applying the HCSE algorithm to find the height-K encoding tree that minimizes K-dimensional structural entropy, yielding tree-structured state communities at multiple granularities. Trajectories are hierarchically segmented according to community assignments at each layer, and the structural information gain of each community (the additional bits needed to localize a random walk within a sub-community given its parent) serves as the conditional signal in classifier-free guidance, replacing reward-based conditioning at lower layers. A structural entropy regularizer, applied only at the base diffusion layer, maximizes Shannon entropy of the state distribution to encourage exploration of underrepresented states while minimizing layer-specific structural entropy to preserve the behavioral hierarchy and prevent extrapolation errors. Theorem 4.1 provides the theoretical factorization of the conditional trajectory generation into a product of per-layer, per-segment diffusion objectives, enabling independent training of shared diffusers at each hierarchy level.

## Results

On the D4RL Gym-MuJoCo benchmark (Table 1), SIHD (K=3 layers) achieves the highest average reward across all nine task-dataset combinations, with average improvements of 1.6% over the best baseline on Expert data, 3.8% on Medium, and 3.9% on Medium-Replay. On long-horizon sparse-reward navigation tasks (Table 2, K=4 layers), SIHD delivers substantially larger gains: 8.3% average improvement on single-task Maze2D, 7.4% on multi-task Maze2D, and 4.4% on AntMaze, with a peak absolute improvement of 16.2 points on Maze2D-U. SIHD also exhibits greater robustness to data quality variation, limiting maximum performance drops to 17.1/12.6/7.1 across the three navigation settings versus 27.4/25.3/10.4 for the second-best baseline (HD).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SIHD outperforms all 11 baselines across every evaluated task and dataset. Against the closest hierarchical diffusion competitor HD (Hierarchical Diffuser, Chen et al. 2024), SIHD gains 5.9–16.2 points on Maze2D and 2.5–5.8 points on AntMaze, with the largest gap on Maze2D-U single-task (144.6 vs. 128.4). Compared to HDMI (Li et al. 2023), SIHD improves by 16.2 points on Maze2D-U and 12.9 on Maze2D-Medium (single-task), confirming that adaptive multi-scale segmentation surpasses fixed two-layer hierarchies. Against the flat Diffuser baseline (Janner et al. 2022), SIHD leads by 30.7 points on Maze2D-U single-task and 20.5 on AntMaze-U, demonstrating the benefit of hierarchical decomposition for long-horizon planning. On the standard Gym-MuJoCo benchmark (Table 1), SIHD beats the strongest non-hierarchical baselines (TT, IQL, CQL) by 1.9–5.6 points and the hierarchical baselines (HDMI, HD) by 0.7–4.3 points, with the largest margin on Walker2D-Medium (88.3 vs. 84.0 for HD). Against navigation-specific baselines MPPI, IRIS, and HiGoC, SIHD achieves 111.4, 55.2, and 53.3 point advantages on Maze2D-U single-task respectively, underscoring the superiority of diffusion-based hierarchical generation over model-predictive or goal-conditioned approaches in sparse-reward settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

SIHD extends the hierarchical diffusion line of work initiated by HDMI (Li et al., 2023) and the Hierarchical Diffuser (Chen et al., 2024), both of which decompose offline trajectory generation into a fixed two-layer subgoal/action hierarchy at a single predefined temporal scale. The key departure is replacing this rigid structure with an adaptive multi-scale hierarchy whose depth and temporal segmentation are derived by minimizing structural entropy over a k-NN state similarity graph, using the HCSE encoding-tree optimization (Pan et al., 2025). The conditional guidance mechanism repurposes structural information gain (rather than cumulative reward) as the inter-layer conditioning signal, and the structural entropy regularizer draws on the variational lower bound connecting structural entropy to Shannon entropy (Theorem 4.2) to balance exploration against distributional shift. The overall framework is grounded in the control-as-inference paradigm (Levine, 2018) and built on the Diffuser codebase (Janner et al., 2022).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By making the diffusion hierarchy depth and temporal scales data-driven rather than hand-specified, SIHD addresses a practical limitation that constrains existing hierarchical diffusion methods on tasks with heterogeneous temporal structure. The structural entropy regularizer offers a principled, information-theoretic mechanism to mitigate overreliance on offline data without incurring the extrapolation errors typical of unconstrained exploration. However, the approach introduces non-trivial computational overhead in the encoding-tree construction step (mitigated by precomputation), the subgoal constraint is implemented via a simple terminal-state replacement that the authors acknowledge is suboptimal, and evaluation is confined to D4RL benchmarks. Open questions include whether the structural information framework generalizes to continuous high-dimensional state spaces beyond MuJoCo and maze navigation, and how to more elegantly encode subgoal satisfaction as a soft conditional signal within the diffusion process.
