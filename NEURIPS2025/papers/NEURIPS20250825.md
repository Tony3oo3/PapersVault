## Problem

Masked diffusion models (MDMs) can sample multiple tokens in parallel per step, suggesting a potential efficiency advantage over autoregressive models, yet empirical observations show current open-source MDMs require more sampling steps for comparable accuracy. This paper provides the first rigorous theoretical analysis of whether this efficiency gap is inherent, revealing that the answer is highly sensitive to the evaluation metric: MDMs are provably efficient for token-level accuracy but fundamentally limited for sequence-level correctness.

## Input / Output

The MDM takes a fully masked sequence of length L (all tokens replaced with a special [m] token) and iteratively reconstructs it over T discrete sampling steps, where at each step a factorized Transformer denoising model predicts all masked positions in parallel and a masking schedule determines which tokens to re-mask. The output is a complete text sequence over vocabulary V. The key structural constraint is that the denoising model uses a factorized conditional p_θ(x₀|x_t) = ∏ᵢ p_θ(x₀ⁱ|x_t), predicting each token independently given the current masked context, which enables parallel sampling but precludes exact matching of the true joint reverse distribution.

## Method

The paper establishes theoretical bounds on MDM sampling efficiency using HMMs and n-gram languages as target distributions under a small-learning-error assumption (KL divergence between the learned reverse model and the true reverse process is bounded). The positive result (Theorem 4.2) shows that for n-gram languages, MDMs achieve near-optimal token error rate (perplexity) with O((n-1)/εⁿ) sampling steps that are independent of sequence length L, yielding constant neural network executions versus L for autoregressive models. The negative result (Theorem 4.4) constructs a specific HMM over a 16-symbol vocabulary where any MDM with fewer than CL sampling steps (for a constant C) incurs SER > 1/2, proving that low sequence error rate requires steps scaling linearly with L. The analysis leverages KL divergence decomposition over the reverse process, separator-based dependency characterization for n-grams, and Chernoff bounds on per-step sampling patterns. Empirical validation uses synthetic n-gram (n∈{2,3,4}) and HMM datasets with Transformer MDMs, confirming that TER plateaus at ~512 steps regardless of length while SER degrades with both length and step count, alongside preliminary evaluations of Dream-v0-Base-7B and LLaDA-8B-Base on GSM8K, MBPP, and HumanEval.

## Results

On formal language tasks (n-gram n∈{2,3,4} and HMM), MDMs achieve near-optimal TER (perplexity) with ~512 sampling steps regardless of sequence length: e.g., 2-gram TER reaches 3.67 at 512 steps for all lengths (512, 1024, 2048), matching the AR baseline of 3.67. However, SER (sequence-level correctness) degrades sharply: for 2-gram at length 2048, even 2048 MDM steps yield only 73.1% accuracy vs. 99.8% for AR, and for HMM at length 2048, MDMs reach only 38.2% accuracy vs. 99.7% for AR. On real-world benchmarks (GSM8K, MBPP, HumanEval), both Dream-v0-Base-7B and LLaDA-8B-Base fall below Qwen2.5-7B in both accuracy and efficiency across all tested sampling steps.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares two large MDMs (Dream-v0-Base-7B with entropy-based sampling and LLaDA-8B-Base with low-confidence-based sampling) against the AR baseline Qwen2.5-7B on GSM8K (8-shot), MBPP (3-shot), and HumanEval (0-shot). On all three benchmarks, both MDMs fall strictly below Qwen2.5-7B in both accuracy and efficiency at every tested sampling step (64–512), placing them in the 'third quadrant' of the accuracy-efficiency trade-off plot. Even when MDMs are forced into a left-to-right (AR-style) decoding mode—generating only the leftmost masked token per step—they still fail to surpass Qwen2.5-7B, indicating the gap is not solely due to different training data. On the synthetic formal-language experiments, MDMs match the AR baseline on TER (perplexity) with ~512 steps independent of sequence length, but lose substantially on SER: at length 2048, MDMs with 2048 steps achieve 73.1% accuracy on 2-gram vs. 99.8% for AR, and 38.2% on HMM vs. 99.7% for AR, confirming that MDMs offer no efficiency advantage for sequence-level correctness tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper provides the first theoretical analysis of the inference-time efficiency-accuracy trade-off of Masked Diffusion Models (MDMs), building directly on the SEDD/MDM framework of Lou et al. (2024) and the practical training and sampling procedures of Sahoo et al. (2024). While concurrent work by Li et al. (2024) studies the statistical complexity of *learning* MDMs, this paper focuses on the *inference* cost, asking how many sampling steps are needed to match auto-regressive quality. The analysis is conducted in the HMM/n-gram language model setting, a classical framework that serves as a tractable proxy for natural language, and the empirical validation uses recent large-scale MDMs (Dream-v0-Base-7B, LLaDA-8B-Base) compared against Qwen2.5-7B on reasoning benchmarks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central finding—that MDM efficiency is metric-dependent, with constant-step sufficiency for perplexity but linear-in-L scaling for sequence correctness—provides a principled explanation for why current MDMs underperform AR models on reasoning tasks like GSM8K and HumanEval. This reframes the MDM-vs-AR debate from a blanket efficiency claim to a task-dependent trade-off, offering practitioners concrete guidance on when each paradigm is preferable. Key limitations include the restriction of theoretical results to formal languages (n-grams, HMMs) rather than modern LLMs, and the empirical evaluation covering only two MDM architectures; extending the analysis to more expressive models and broader diffusion variants (e.g., SEDD-uniform, discrete flow matching) remains open.
