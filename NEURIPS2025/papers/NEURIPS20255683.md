## Problem

Existing hand-object interaction datasets rely on expensive motion capture systems, labor-intensive manual annotation, or per-object trained trackers, making them difficult to scale to diverse objects and interaction types. No prior system combines markerless multi-view capture with a fully scalable, training-free annotation pipeline that handles both novel object 3D reconstruction and simultaneous hand-object pose tracking.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255683/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes synchronized RGB-D streams from 8 calibrated Intel RealSense D455 cameras plus a HoloLens egocentric view, requiring only two manually selected 2D points per object in the first frame (as SAM2 prompts) and an object name label. Outputs include a textured 3D mesh per object (via marching cubes from a neural SDF), per-frame 6D object poses in a shared world frame, MANO shape/pose parameters for up to two hands, 21 3D hand joint positions, 2D projected keypoints, and pixel-level segmentation masks for both hands and objects across all camera views.

## Method

The annotation pipeline chains four pre-trained vision models—BundleSDF for neural 3D object reconstruction (using LoFTR feature matching and online pose-graph optimization), SAM2 for video segmentation, FoundationPose for initial 6D pose tracking, and MediaPipe for 2D hand landmark detection—into a unified multi-view system that requires no domain-specific training. Multi-view consistency is enforced by transforming per-camera FoundationPose estimates into a common world frame and applying RANSAC to filter outliers before feeding the consensus pose back as the next-frame initialization. Object poses are then refined by minimizing an SDF loss (mean squared signed distance between fused multi-camera point clouds and the object surface) plus a temporal smoothness term on quaternion and translation. Hand poses are optimized in MANO parameter space by fitting to RANSAC-filtered 3D keypoints triangulated from multi-view MediaPipe detections, with cubic spline interpolation filling gaps. A final joint hand-object SDF optimization simultaneously refines both poses to eliminate mesh intersections, using per-object and per-hand SDFs plus a hand pose regularization term.

## Results

On the HO-Cap dataset, HaMeR achieves the best hand pose estimation with 28.9 mm MPJPE and 91.4% PCK(0.2), substantially outperforming A2J-Transformer (78.7 mm) and InterWild (57.6 mm). For seen object detection, RT-DETR trained on HO-Cap reaches 75.9 AP and 90.0 AP50, while YOLO11 achieves 71.4 AP. For novel object pose estimation, FoundationPose attains 89.3% ADD and 95.7% ADD-S AUC, significantly surpassing MegaPose (67.1% ADD, 83.0% ADD-S). Novel object detection remains challenging, with CNOS (25.3 AP) and GroundingDINO (17.0 AP) producing many false positives.

## Baselines & Comparisons

For hand pose estimation, HaMeR (28.9 mm MPJPE) outperforms InterWild (57.6 mm) by ~2x and A2J-Transformer (78.7 mm) by ~2.7x, with the gap attributed to HaMeR's ViT backbone and 2.7M-image training set; A2J-Transformer degrades notably under hand-object occlusion. For seen object detection, RT-DETR* (75.9 AP) beats YOLO11* (71.4 AP) by 4.5 AP, with the largest gap on large objects (84.8 vs. 72.6 APL). Both seen detectors dramatically outperform the zero-shot novel detectors (CNOS 25.3 AP, GroundingDINO 17.0 AP), confirming that training on HO-Cap is essential for reliable detection. For novel object pose estimation, FoundationPose (89.3% ADD) surpasses MegaPose (67.1% ADD) by 22.2 percentage points, with per-object results (Table 10) showing FoundationPose consistently above 87% ADD across all 64 objects while MegaPose drops to 63–66% on many. The paper does not propose a new method to compare against these baselines; rather, it establishes them as reference points for the released dataset.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself against two dominant paradigms in hand-object interaction data collection: motion-capture-based systems (FPHA, ContactPose, ARCTIC, OakInk2, HOT3D) that require expensive markers and calibration, and manual or per-object-trained annotation pipelines (DexYCB, HOI4D, HO-3D, H2O) that are labor-intensive or non-scalable. Its key departure is composing off-the-shelf pre-trained foundation models—BundleSDF for neural 3D reconstruction, FoundationPose for 6D pose tracking, SAM2 for segmentation, and MediaPipe for hand landmarks—into a unified multi-view annotation pipeline with SDF-based refinement, eliminating both mocap hardware and domain-specific tracker training. The multi-view RANSAC consistency check and joint hand-object SDF optimization are the paper's own methodological contributions layered on top of these components.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

HO-Cap provides a markerless, egocentric+third-person dataset with textured 3D meshes for 64 novel objects and both uni- and bi-manual interactions, filling a gap between small-scale mocap datasets and large-scale but marker-dependent ones. The semi-automatic pipeline is practically valuable because it can be redeployed to capture new objects without retraining, lowering the barrier for community data collection. However, the dataset's scale (64 sequences, 9 subjects) is modest relative to competitors like HOI4D (4,000 sequences) or OakInk2 (627 sequences), and the exclusion of textureless, reflective, and small cylindrical objects limits coverage. Open questions include whether the SDF-based joint optimization generalizes to more occluded or dynamic scenes, and whether the annotation quality degrades with more complex multi-object interactions.
