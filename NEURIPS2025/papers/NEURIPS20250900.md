## Problem

Existing machine unlearning methods certify that unlearned and re-trained models are statistically indistinguishable on original forget samples, but this guarantee does not extend to adversarially perturbed inputs. A slightly perturbed forget sample may still be correctly recognized by the unlearned model while the re-trained model fails, revealing a novel privacy risk the authors term residual knowledge. The paper formalizes this vulnerability, proves it is inevitable in high-dimensional input spaces via isoperimetric inequalities, and proposes a mitigation strategy.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained model (ResNet-18 or ResNet-50), a forget set S_f (50% of one class), and a retain set S_r, and produces an unlearned model via fine-tuning. During training, it generates adversarial perturbations of forget samples within an ℓ_p ball of radius τ (typically τ=0.03) using FGSM or PGD, treating these as additional training signals. The output model is expected to match a re-trained reference not only on original samples but also on their local perturbation neighborhood, with residual knowledge r_τ(S_f) ≤ 1.

## Method

RURK (Robust Unlearning against Residual Knowledge) is a fine-tuning objective with two terms: (i) standard cross-entropy on the retain set to preserve utility, and (ii) a regularization term that penalizes the model's loss on 'vulnerable perturbations'—perturbed forget samples that the unlearned model still classifies correctly. The key insight is that since the re-trained model is unavailable during unlearning, minimizing just the numerator of the residual knowledge ratio Pr[m(x')=y] suffices to suppress residual knowledge. Vulnerable perturbations are constructed by solving a constrained minimization min_{z∈B_p(x,τ)} ℓ(w,(z,y)) via FGSM or PGD at each gradient step. Theoretically, the paper proves via isoperimetric inequalities on the unit sphere that disagreement between unlearned and re-trained models is inevitable under (ε,δ)-unlearning in high dimensions, and shows residual knowledge provides tractable upper and lower bounds on expected adversarial disagreement.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (ResNet-18), RURK achieves the smallest Average Gap to Re-train (2.65) among all 11 baselines, with Retain Acc. 99.55%, Test Acc. 92.60%, and MIA Acc. 18.20%. On Small CIFAR-5, RURK also attains the lowest Avg. Gap (2.60) with a re-learn time of 2.00 epochs (vs. 3.33 for Re-train). On the larger ImageNet-100 (ResNet-50), RURK achieves the best Avg. Gap of 5.26, outperforming GD (6.79) and NegGrad+ (13.06). Residual knowledge estimates (Figure 2) confirm that RURK suppresses r_τ(S_f) to near 1 for τ < 0.01 and below 1 at larger perturbations, while methods like GD and CF-k retain residual knowledge comparable to the Original model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RURK achieves the lowest Avg. Gap on both CIFAR-10 (2.65) and Small CIFAR-5 (2.60), narrowly beating NegGrad+ (2.71) which is the closest competitor. However, NegGrad+ has a re-learn time of only 1.00 epoch versus RURK's >30, indicating NegGrad+ implicitly retains forget-set knowledge that can be quickly re-acquired. GD and CF-k, which do not explicitly access forget samples, retain residual knowledge comparable to the Original model and show high Avg. Gaps (8.22 and 8.16 respectively). GA and SSD aggressively erase forget-set information (Unlearn Acc. 61–66%) but sacrifice test accuracy below 90%, yielding Avg. Gaps of 21.25 and 20.65. On ImageNet-100, RURK's advantage widens: it achieves Avg. Gap 5.26 versus GD (6.79) and NegGrad+ (13.06), confirming scalability. SCRUB is the closest overall competitor on CIFAR-10 (Avg. Gap 4.84, re-learn >30) but requires storing an additional student model, whereas RURK supports in-place weight updates.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the certified machine unlearning framework of (ε,δ)-indistinguishability (Guo et al., 2020) and Rényi unlearning (Chourasia and Shah, 2023; Chien et al., 2024), which guarantee statistical similarity between unlearned and re-trained models on original samples. It departs from prior adversarial-unlearning work (Marchant et al., 2022; Pawelczyk et al., 2025; Xuan and Li, 2025) that studies how perturbations affect the unlearned model in isolation, instead focusing on the *distinguishability gap* between unlearned and re-trained models under perturbation. The theoretical analysis draws on isoperimetric inequalities (Talagrand, 1995; Milman and Schechtman, 1986) to prove that such disagreement is inevitable in high-dimensional spaces, while the mitigation strategy (RURK) repurposes adversarial training ideas (FGSM/PGD-based perturbation search) as a regularization term in the unlearning objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper reveals that all existing certified unlearning guarantees are blind to a practical privacy risk: an adversary can craft imperceptible perturbations of forget samples that the unlearned model still recognizes while the re-trained model does not, effectively leaking membership information. The theoretical inevitability result (Proposition 2) means this gap cannot be closed by tightening (ε,δ) alone in high-dimensional settings, pointing to a fundamental limitation of the current certification paradigm. Key open questions include whether adversarial training or distributionally robust optimization might paradoxically *amplify* residual knowledge, how the concept extends to generative models and LLMs where 'forgetting' targets concepts rather than samples, and whether the minimal perturbation norm needed for re-recognition can itself be exploited as a side channel in membership inference attacks.
