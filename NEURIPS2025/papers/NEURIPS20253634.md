## Problem

Existing video caption evaluation metrics depend on costly human-annotated gold-standard references and ignore whether summaries actually help humans complete downstream tasks. This paper addresses both gaps by proposing an annotation-free, task-aware evaluation that scores and selects video-to-text summaries based on their alignment with video content and their utility for a specific decision-making task.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253634/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a raw video clip (uniformly sampled into 20 frames), a set of k VLM-generated text summaries, and a downstream task (e.g., a multiple-choice question with known answer labels). It produces two scalar scores per summary—a grounding score and a utility score—and selects the single best summary via weighted maximization. Text masking replaces tf-idf-identified keywords with a <MASK> token, while video masking applies random 1/16 spatial cropping to frames, ensuring the scores reflect genuine cross-modal dependency rather than surface-level text redundancy.

## Method

VIBE adapts the information bottleneck objective into two inference-only scores computed via a VLM's next-token prediction probabilities, requiring no model training. The grounding score approximates pointwise mutual information I^P(V;T) as log P(T|V, T_masked)/P(T|T_masked), measuring how much the video helps reconstruct a masked summary beyond what the masked text alone provides. The utility score approximates I^P(T;Y) as log P(Y|T, V_masked)/P(Y|V_masked), measuring how much the summary improves task prediction given a masked video. Given k candidate summaries sampled from the VLM, VIBE performs rejection sampling by selecting the summary that maximizes α·I^P(V;T) + β·I^P(T;Y), where α and β control the grounding–utility trade-off. The entire pipeline operates as a black-box plug-in over any VLM API, and the grounding score alone serves as a fully self-supervised alternative when task labels are unavailable.

## Results

Across three datasets, VIBE-selected summaries (Max-U) consistently outperform both Naive VLM summaries and raw video in human task accuracy. On LongVideoBench, Max-U achieves 65.00% accuracy vs. 46.43% for Naive VLM and 74.44% for Video Only, while reducing response time from 202.35s to 65.86s. On LearningPaper24, Max-U reaches 37.89% accuracy (vs. 28.42% Naive, 23.50% Video Only) with a 4.1× reduction in response time (46.69s vs. 192.73s). The self-supervised Max-G variant also improves accuracy over Naive VLM on all datasets without requiring task labels, though with smaller gains (e.g., 59.23% vs. 46.43% on LongVideoBench).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares four conditions: Video Only (raw video), Naive VLM (randomly sampled summary), Max-G (VIBE-selected by grounding score), and Max-U (VIBE-selected by utility score). Max-U wins on accuracy across all three datasets, improving over Naive VLM by 9.47 pp on LearningPaper24 (37.89% vs. 28.42%), 18.57 pp on LongVideoBench (65.00% vs. 46.43%), and 7.85 pp on SUTD-TrafficQA (84.81% vs. 76.96%). On response time, Max-U and Max-G both dramatically outperform Video Only on the two longer-video datasets (e.g., 46.69s vs. 192.73s on LearningPaper24, a 75.8% reduction), but on SUTD-TrafficQA (2–10s clips) the brevity of the video negates summarization benefits and Video Only remains fastest. Max-G, which requires no task labels, still significantly beats Naive VLM on accuracy in all datasets (e.g., 59.23% vs. 46.43% on LongVideoBench, p=0.006), making it a practical self-supervised alternative. The IES metric confirms that VIBE-selected summaries achieve the best speed–accuracy trade-off on long-video tasks, while on short clips the visual nature of fine-grained actions limits text-summary utility.

## Relation to Prior Work

VIBE adapts the Information Bottleneck framework of Tishby et al. (2000) from a training-time regularizer to an annotation-free evaluation and selection criterion for video-to-text summaries. It extends the pointwise mutual information approach of Jung et al. (2024), which assessed news summaries for language model tuning, to the video domain and to human decision-support tasks. Unlike prior video caption metrics (ROUGE, BLEU, CIDEr) that require gold-standard references, VIBE uses masked next-token prediction in VLMs to compute grounding and utility scores without any human annotations, and it explicitly optimizes for downstream task utility rather than surface-level text overlap.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VIBE offers a practical, training-free plug-in for selecting the most decision-useful summary from a pool of VLM outputs, which is directly relevant as VLMs are increasingly deployed in time-sensitive human oversight workflows (traffic review, paper screening). The user study demonstrates that even the unsupervised grounding score alone yields meaningful accuracy gains, making the method applicable when task labels are unavailable. Key open questions include whether joint optimization of summary generation and selection via self-supervised fine-tuning can close the gap to oracle summaries, whether task-aware captions can improve VLM reasoning on downstream tasks, and how the masking strategy (tf-idf thresholds, random cropping) affects score reliability across domains.
