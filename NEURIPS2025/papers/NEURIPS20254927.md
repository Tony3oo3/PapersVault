## Problem

Modern masked discrete diffusion models (e.g., MDLM) suffer from a 'failure to remask' property: once a token is unmasked during generation, it is locked in and cannot be corrected, even if it introduces an error. This limitation caps sample quality, restricts controllable generation, and prevents the iterative refinement that makes continuous diffusion models effective. The paper asks whether a principled remasking mechanism can be added to pretrained masked diffusion models to unlock inference-time compute scaling.

## Input / Output

The method operates on a pretrained masked diffusion denoising network (e.g., MDLM, MaskGiT, or LLaDA) without requiring retraining, consuming only the model's logits at each denoising step. For text, sequences are up to L=1024 tokens (GPT-2 tokenizer); for images, L=256 VQGAN patches from 256×256 ImageNet samples; for molecules, L=32 character-based SMILES tokens. The user specifies a remasking schedule σ_t (per-token or global) and a noise schedule α_t, and the sampler outputs a fully decoded sequence. All text experiments use 64-bit floating-point precision and nucleus sampling (top-p=0.9) to preserve diversity.

## Method

ReMDM defines a modified backward process where, for an unmasked token z_t = x, the posterior becomes Cat(z_s; (1−σ_t)x + σ_t m), allowing the token to be re-masked with probability σ_t before being re-decoded. This is derived from a discrete diffusion model whose marginals are provably identical to standard masked diffusion (Theorem 3.1), yielding an ELBO that is a rescaled version of the MDLM objective—enabling direct reuse of pretrained MDLM weights. The paper further shows ReMDM is a strict generalization of discrete predictor-corrector samplers: the forward-backward and discrete flow matching correctors are special cases with specific σ_t values (Propositions 4.2–4.3). Practical schedules include max-capped, rescaled, and confidence-based (reweighting σ_t inversely to token confidence) strategies, combined with 'loop' or 'switch' activation windows that restrict remasking to later denoising phases where context is abundant.

## Results

On OpenWebText, ReMDM achieves a MAUVE score of 0.656 at T=4096, a 15.6× improvement over MDLM (0.035) and 2.2× over the best corrector baseline MDLM+DFM (0.269), approaching the AR model's 0.760. On ImageNet 256×256, ReMDM achieves FID 4.45 and IS 209.45 at T=64, outperforming MaskGiT (FID 4.85, IS 196.38) and MDLM (FID 4.69, IS 187.93). On LLaDA 8B benchmarks, ReMDM improves Countdown pass@1 from 45.2 to 46.1 and TruthfulQA ΔROUGE-1 from 27.1 to 29.5 over the original LLaDA, with statistical significance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReMDM decisively outperforms all masked diffusion baselines on OpenWebText: at T=4096, its MAUVE of 0.656 exceeds MDLM (0.035) by 18.7×, SEDD (0.009) by 73×, MDLM+FB (0.243) by 2.7×, and MDLM+DFM (0.269) by 2.4×. On ImageNet, ReMDM beats MaskGiT by 0.40 FID and 13.07 IS at T=64, and MDLM by 0.24 FID and 21.52 IS. On LLaDA 8B, ReMDM surpasses both the original LLaDA (Countdown 46.1 vs 45.2; TruthfulQA ΔROUGE-1 29.5 vs 27.1) and LLaDA+DFM (46.1 vs 44.8; 29.5 vs 28.2) with statistical significance. In controlled molecule generation on QM9, ReMDM pushes the novelty–ring-count Pareto frontier beyond AR+FUDGE, MDLM, and UDLM for both D-CFG and D-CBG guidance. The paper also shows that ReMDM is theoretically more general than both the FB and DFM correctors (Propositions 4.2–4.4), subsuming them as special cases of the remasking schedule.

## Relation to Prior Work

ReMDM directly extends the masked diffusion language model (MDLM) framework by introducing a remasking backward process that generalizes the absorbing-state posterior, analogous to how DDIM generalizes DDPM for continuous signals. The paper positions its method as a strict generalization of existing discrete predictor-corrector samplers (forward-backward and discrete flow matching correctors), proving both are special cases of the ReMDM posterior. The key departure from prior work is that while MDLM and MaskGiT lock in decoded tokens permanently, ReMDM allows already-decoded tokens to be remasked and re-decoded, restoring the iterative refinement capability that continuous diffusion models enjoy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The most significant contribution is unlocking inference-time compute scaling for discrete diffusion models—a property that continuous diffusion has leveraged for years but masked discrete diffusion fundamentally lacked. Because ReMDM is plug-and-play on pretrained MDLMs (no retraining needed), it offers an immediate quality boost to existing models, and the demonstrated improvements across text, images, and molecule design suggest broad applicability. Open questions include whether the scaling benefits persist at much larger model scales (the LLaDA 8B experiments are encouraging but limited), how optimal remasking schedules interact with other inference-time techniques like consistency distillation, and whether the non-Markovian structure introduces computational overhead that becomes prohibitive for very long sequences.
