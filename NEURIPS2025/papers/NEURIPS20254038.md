## Problem

Existing decentralized minimax optimization methods (GT-EG, MC-SVRE, OADSVI) require all nodes to use identical fixed mini-batch sizes, making them sample-inefficient, and their complexity bounds depend on local smoothness parameters (L_l, L_max) that can be arbitrarily larger than the global smoothness L under data heterogeneity. It remained open whether one could design a decentralized minimax algorithm whose convergence rate and complexity depend on the tighter global smoothness parameters while also allowing stochastic, node-varying mini-batch sizes.

## Input / Output

Each of m network nodes holds n local component functions f_{i,j} and communicates only with neighbors via a symmetric doubly-stochastic mixing matrix W. The algorithm uses Bernoulli(q) sampling with q = b/(mn) to construct stochastic mini-batches, so the effective number of gradient evaluations per node per iteration is random and nodes can partially participate. The output is an ε-suboptimal saddle point (x*, y*) available at every node, with the guarantee that ‖x − x*‖² + ‖y − y*‖² ≤ ε, under the assumption that the global objective is μ-strongly-convex-μ-strongly-concave (local components need not be convex-concave).

## Method

DIVERSE constructs a variance-reduced optimistic gradient estimator at each node using a snapshot point v_i^{k−1} (updated with probability p) and a stochastic retraction term over Bernoulli-sampled indices, yielding an expected per-iteration cost of O(mnp + (1−p)b) LIFO calls. Gradient tracking (S^k = FastMix(S^{k−1} + Δ^k − Δ^{k−1})) and multi-consensus steps via Chebyshev-accelerated FastMix (R = O(√χ log(mnL̄/μ)) rounds) keep local variables close to their averages, which is the key mechanism enabling global-smoothness-dependent bounds. The analysis splits into two regimes: when L̄ ≤ √(mn)L, variance reduction with stochastic mini-batches gives O((mn + √(mn)L̄/μ) log(1/ε)) LIFO calls; when L ≤ L̄/√(mn), the algorithm switches to exact local gradients (p=0, b=mn) for O((mnL/μ) log(1/ε)) calls. The linear convergence rate Θ(1 − μ/L) depends only on the global condition number, and matching lower bounds confirm near-optimality up to logarithmic factors.

## Results

DIVERSE achieves an ε-suboptimal solution with O((mn + min{mnL, √(mn)L̄}/μ) log(1/ε)) LIFO calls, Õ((n + L/μ + min{nL, √(n/m)L̄}/μ) log(1/ε)) computation rounds, and Õ(√χ L/μ log(1/ε)) communication rounds, where L and L̄ are global smoothness parameters. Matching lower bounds (Theorems 4.2–4.4) confirm these rates are optimal up to a logarithmic factor. In numerical experiments on four LIBSVM datasets (a9a, w8a, ijcnn1, cod-rna) with m=50 nodes, DIVERSE consistently outperforms all baselines across LIFO calls, computation rounds, and communication rounds.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DIVERSE outperforms all three baselines (GT-EG, MC-SVRE, OADSVI) on every metric—LIFO calls, computation rounds, and communication rounds—across all four datasets. The key theoretical advantage is that DIVERSE's bounds depend on the global smoothness parameters L and L̄, which can be significantly smaller than the local parameters L_max, L_l, and L̄_l used by prior methods (Appendix A shows gaps of Θ(√m) or even Ω(m²) under data heterogeneity). In practice, DIVERSE's stochastic mini-batch sampling with partial participation yields substantially fewer LIFO calls than OADSVI (which uses a fixed batch size of 3 per node) and MC-SVRE. GT-EG, being deterministic without Chebyshev acceleration, requires the most communication rounds, while MC-SVRE cannot benefit from mini-batch sampling for communication efficiency. The established lower bounds confirm that DIVERSE's rates are near-optimal, closing the gap with existing upper bounds that were previously only proven optimal with respect to local smoothness.

## Relation to Prior Work

DIVERSE composes three established building blocks—the variance-reduced optimistic gradient estimator from Alacaoglu & Malitsky [2], the Chebyshev-accelerated multi-consensus (FastMix) subroutine from Ye et al. [87], and the gradient-tracking decentralized framework from Kovalev et al. [38]—into a unified algorithm for decentralized finite-sum minimax optimization. The key departure from prior decentralized minimax methods (GT-EG [55], MC-SVRE [46], OADSVI [39]) is twofold: (i) the complexity bounds depend on global smoothness parameters L and L̄ rather than local worst-case parameters L_max, L_l, L̄_l, which can be Θ(√m) or larger under data heterogeneity; and (ii) the Bernoulli-sampled stochastic mini-batch size replaces the fixed identical batch size required by all previous methods, enabling partial participation and reducing per-node computation. The paper also provides matching lower bounds, closing the gap up to logarithmic factors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The shift from local to global smoothness in the complexity bounds is practically significant: under realistic data heterogeneity across nodes, local smoothness parameters can exceed global ones by factors of √m or m² (demonstrated in Appendix A), meaning DIVERSE's guarantees are meaningfully tighter in the regimes where decentralized optimization is most needed. The stochastic mini-batch design also opens the door to partial-participation schemes that reduce per-node computational load without sacrificing convergence. Open questions include extending the global-smoothness analysis to nonconvex minimax problems and to settings with asymmetric strong convexity/concavity constants, both of which the authors flag as future work. The empirical validation, while consistent with theory, is limited to small-scale LIBSVM datasets on a 50-node ring, leaving open whether the advantages persist at larger scale or on more heterogeneous real-world networks.
