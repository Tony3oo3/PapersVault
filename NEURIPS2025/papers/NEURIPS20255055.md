## Problem

Most theoretical work in preference-based reinforcement learning (PbRL) is limited to pairwise comparison feedback, and the few studies that extend to ranking feedback over multiple actions fail to show any performance improvement as the number of options increases—despite the richer information available. This paper asks whether an algorithm can be designed that achieves a strictly better theoretical guarantee under multiple-option ranking feedback compared to pairwise comparisons in the online PbRL setting, and whether the ubiquitous O(e^B) dependence on the parameter norm bound in prior guarantees is inherent or merely an artifact of loose analysis.

## Input / Output

At each round t, the agent observes a context x_t sampled from a fixed but unknown distribution ρ and selects an assortment S_t of up to K actions from a universe of N actions. The labeler returns a full ranking over S_t generated by the Plackett-Luce model with a linear reward r_θ*(x,a) = φ(x,a)ᵀθ*, where φ is a known feature map with ‖φ(x,a)‖₂ ≤ 1 and ‖θ*‖₂ ≤ B. After T rounds, the algorithm outputs a policy π̂_T: X → A that minimizes the expected suboptimality gap E_x[r_θ*(x, π*(x)) − r_θ*(x, π̂_T(x))]. The key structural constraint is that the agent controls which subset to offer (active selection), and the bound explicitly depends on the per-round subset sizes |S_t|.

## Method

M-AUPO (Maximizing Average Uncertainty for Preference Optimization) selects assortments by greedily adding actions that maximize the average Mahalanobis dispersion of feature vectors under H_t^{-1}, where H_t accumulates Hessian information from prior rounds. Parameter estimation uses online mirror descent (OMD) with either the full Plackett-Luce loss or a rank-breaking (RB) loss that decomposes the ranking into C(|S_t|,2) independent pairwise comparisons, achieving lower per-round computational cost (O(Kd³) vs. O(K²d³)) and tighter non-leading terms. The key analytical insight is dividing rounds into warm-up (high-uncertainty) and non-warm-up phases: in non-warm-up rounds, the reward gap between any two actions is bounded, which allows bounding 1/μ̇(·) by a constant and thereby eliminating the O(e^B) factor from the leading term without any auxiliary techniques. This yields a suboptimality gap of Õ(d/T · √(Σ_{t=1}^T 1/|S_t|)), which strictly decreases as assortment sizes grow, and a near-matching lower bound of Ω(d/(K√T)) confirming the K-dependence is tight up to a factor.

## Results

The main theoretical contribution is a suboptimality gap of $\tilde{\mathcal{O}}\left(\frac{d}{T}\sqrt{\sum_{t=1}^{T}\frac{1}{|S_t|}}\right)$ for M-AUPO under both PL and RB losses (Theorems 1–2), which explicitly decreases as the assortment size $|S_t|$ grows, and a near-matching lower bound of $\Omega\left(\frac{d}{K\sqrt{T}}\right)$ (Theorem 3). Crucially, the leading term is free of the $\mathcal{O}(e^B)$ dependence that plagues prior PbRL and dueling bandit analyses. Empirically, on both synthetic data (d=5, N=100) and real-world datasets (TREC-DL with 100 candidates, NECTAR with 7 candidates, using gemma-2b features and Mistral-7B as reward model), M-AUPO's suboptimality gap decreases monotonically with K, and the RB variant consistently outperforms the PL variant.

## Baselines & Comparisons

M-AUPO is compared against three baselines: DopeWolfe [76], Uniform, and Best&Ref (following Online GSHF [86] / XPO [85]). On both synthetic and real-world experiments, M-AUPO significantly outperforms all three baselines at the final round. DopeWolfe, despite also selecting K actions from N, is designed for a context-free (singleton context) setting and cannot leverage information sharing across varying contexts, resulting in substantially worse suboptimality. Uniform random assortment selection and Best&Ref (which restricts to pairs, |S_t|=2) are both outperformed because M-AUPO explicitly maximizes average uncertainty and exploits the richer information from larger assortments. The RB variant of M-AUPO further outperforms the PL variant empirically, as it avoids approximation errors in computing expectations over rankings and has lower computational cost per round.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the online confidence bound analysis of Lee and Oh [41] for MNL bandits to the Plackett-Luce (PL) ranking model, leveraging the fact that PL probabilities decompose into a product of MNL probabilities. It departs from prior multi-comparison PbRL works (Zhu et al. [93], Mukherjee et al. [49], Thekumparampil et al. [76]) that, despite exploring ranking feedback, failed to show any performance improvement as the number of options K increased and retained an O(e^B) exponential dependency. The algorithmic core—greedy assortment selection maximizing average Mahalanobis uncertainty—builds on D-optimal design ideas from active preference learning [47, 71, 19] but is the first to provably exploit the combinatorial richness of ranking feedback in an online contextual setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first theoretical result in PbRL showing that the suboptimality gap provably decreases as the labeler is shown more options to rank, directly addressing a counterintuitive gap where prior guarantees failed to improve (or even worsened) with richer feedback. The elimination of the O(e^B) dependency via a warm-up/non-warm-up phase decomposition is broadly applicable to existing PbRL and dueling bandit analyses without algorithmic modification, suggesting that this exponential factor widely present in the literature is an analytical artifact rather than a fundamental limit. Open questions include whether the K-dependent improvement persists under adversarial or non-stationary context distributions, and whether the RB loss advantage over the full PL loss generalizes to settings with noisy or inconsistent human rankings.
