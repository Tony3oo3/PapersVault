## Problem

Existing inner-loop optimization methods for dataset distillation, such as RaT-BPTT, apply random or fixed truncation uniformly across all training stages, ignoring the well-established fact that neural networks learn simple patterns early and refine complex features later. This rigid truncation strategy is suboptimal: the paper's controlled experiments show that stage-aware truncation positions and window sizes yield 2–3% accuracy gains that random truncation cannot capture.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a standard training dataset (RGB images with class labels at 32×32 for CIFAR, 64×64 for Tiny-ImageNet, 224×224 for ImageNet-1K, or text sequences for SST-2/MNLI-m/AGNews) along with a specified CNN or BERT architecture, and produces a compact synthetic dataset of 1, 10, or 50 images (or sentences) per class. For high-resolution inputs, a patch-wise semantic preservation module first splits each image into a 4×4 grid of non-overlapping patches, distills each patch independently, and then enforces centroid alignment across patches to maintain global semantic coherence.

## Method

AT-BPTT replaces RaT-BPTT's random truncation with three coupled mechanisms operating within the bilevel optimization loop. First, a dynamic truncation position mechanism uses a temperature-controlled softmax over per-timestep gradient magnitudes to probabilistically select where to cut the unrolled trajectory: early-stage timesteps with large gradients (preliminary phase) are favored, middle-stage truncation remains uniform, and late-stage timesteps with small gradients (post phase) are favored via an inverted probability. Second, an adaptive window size mechanism computes a soft-normalized weight from the magnitude of gradient variation between consecutive timesteps, linearly scaling the truncation window between W−d and W+d so that high-variation regions receive wider windows. Third, a low-rank Hessian approximation (LRHA) replaces full Hessian products with a rank-k_j factorization obtained via randomized SVD on Hessian-vector products, reducing time and memory complexity from O(p²) to O(pk_j + k_j³). Stage transitions between early, middle, and late phases are triggered by threshold-guided counters on accuracy variation rather than fixed epoch splits, and the entire pipeline is wrapped in a patch-wise semantic preservation module for high-resolution generalization.

## Results

AT-BPTT achieves state-of-the-art dataset distillation accuracy across four benchmark datasets: 72.4% on CIFAR-10 (IPC=10), 49.0% on CIFAR-100 (IPC=10), 32.7% on Tiny-ImageNet (IPC=10), and 30.6% on ImageNet-1K (IPC=10). Compared to the strongest inner-loop baseline RaT-BPTT, AT-BPTT delivers an average accuracy gain of 6.16%, with the largest margin on ImageNet-1K (30.6% vs. 13.0% at IPC=10). The method also achieves a 3.9× training speedup and 63% GPU memory reduction over RaT-BPTT, thanks to the low-rank Hessian approximation component.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AT-BPTT outperforms the strongest inner-loop baseline RaT-BPTT [9] across all datasets and IPC settings, with gains ranging from +1.2% (CIFAR-100 IPC=10) to +17.6% (ImageNet-1K IPC=10), averaging 6.16% overall. Against the best outer-loop method NCFM [44], AT-BPTT leads by +0.6% on CIFAR-10 (IPC=10), +1.2% on CIFAR-100 (IPC=50), and +5.9% on Tiny-ImageNet (IPC=10). On high-resolution ImageNet-1K, AT-BPTT achieves 30.6% (IPC=10) versus 17.8% for TESLA [6] and 13.0% for RaT-BPTT, a gap attributable to the patch-wise semantic preservation module. DATM [12] remains competitive on CIFAR-10 IPC=50 (76.1% vs. 78.7%) but is outperformed by AT-BPTT on all other settings. In terms of efficiency, AT-BPTT reduces GPU memory from 18.9 GB to 6.94 GB and training time from 21.6 h to 5.5 h relative to RaT-BPTT on CIFAR-10 IPC=10, while maintaining comparable accuracy (72.4% vs. 69.4%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the inner-loop optimization lineage for dataset distillation that runs from BPTT (Werbos, 1990) through T-BPTT (Shaban et al., 2019) to RaT-BPTT (Feng et al., ICLR 2024). Its core departure is replacing RaT-BPTT's uniform random truncation with a stage-aware mechanism that uses gradient magnitude as a proxy for training phase, motivated by the well-known observation that DNNs learn simple patterns early and complex features late (Arpit et al., 2017). Two additional contributions—low-rank Hessian approximation for computational efficiency and patch-wise semantic preservation for high-resolution images—are orthogonal to the truncation strategy and address separate bottlenecks in the bilevel optimization pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 6.16% average accuracy gain over the SOTA inner-loop method (RaT-BPTT) combined with a 3.9× speedup and 63% memory reduction makes this practically relevant for practitioners who need efficient dataset distillation without sacrificing quality. The staged truncation principle is generalizable beyond dataset distillation to any bilevel optimization with unrolled inner loops (meta-learning, NAS, federated learning), as the authors note. However, the method remains limited to simple CNN architectures, and the computational cost of inner-loop unrolling—despite LRHA—still exceeds that of outer-loop methods like NCFM, leaving open the question of whether hybrid inner-outer-loop strategies can close this gap for larger-scale models like transformers or diffusion models.
