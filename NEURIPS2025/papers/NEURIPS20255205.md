## Problem

Neural networks for geometric optimization problems typically have architectures whose parameter count grows with input size, limiting scalability. While classical ε-kernel techniques provide efficient approximations of extent measures (e.g., minimum enclosing ball, minimum-width annulus), they require identifying exact extreme points in projections, which does not naturally align with permutation-equivariant neural architectures. This paper asks whether one can design neural models with bounded model complexity (constant parameters independent of input size) that provably approximate a family of geometric extent measures.

## Input / Output

The method takes an arbitrary-size point cloud P of N points in R^d (assumed contained in a bounded hypercube) and produces either (1) a relaxed-ε-kernel of exactly k points in R^d, where k depends only on the approximation parameter ε, the fatness constant α, and dimension d — not on N — or (2) a scalar extent measure value (MEB radius, MEE major/minor radii, or MEA width). Unlike standard ε-kernels, the relaxed-ε-kernel output points are convex combinations of input points rather than a subset of P, which is what enables the Sumformer-based implementation. The model is trained on point clouds of fixed size (e.g., 500 points) but generalizes to arbitrary test sizes.

## Method

The core contribution is a new relaxed-ε-kernel theory where output points may be convex combinations of input points (Q ⊆ CH(P) rather than Q ⊆ P), preserving the same directional-width approximation guarantees as classical ε-kernels while aligning naturally with the Sumformer architecture. The kernel-approx-NN computes N_φ(P) = σ_col(φ_ε(P))^T P, where φ_ε is a single-block Sumformer that simulates the per-direction ReLU-thresholding of Algorithm 1, and σ_col is a column-wise modified softmax that produces convex-combination weights. For faithful extent measures, a DeepSet decoder reads the k kernel points; for unfaithful measures (e.g., minimum-width annulus), an encode-process-decode framework is used where the encoder performs a linearization mapping into R^{m+1}, the Sumformer-based processor outputs a relaxed-ε-kernel in that lifted space, and a DeepSet decoder computes the final measure. The Sumformer instantiation achieves bounded model complexity (parameters depend only on ε, α, d) and empirically shows stronger out-of-distribution generalization than Transformer instantiations, particularly when the processor is pre-trained and frozen before task-specific encoder/decoder training.

## Results

On the extent measure tasks (Table 2, Synthetic training), the Sumformer-instantiated frozen model S_extent(Frozen) achieves the best MEB radius error of 0.009, MEE minor/major radius errors of 0.037/0.022, and MEA width error of 0.050. For relaxed-ε-kernel approximation (Table 1, R²), the Sumformer kernel-approx-NN S_ε achieves a directional width error of 0.014 in-distribution and 0.028 on OOD SQUID data, compared to 0.218 for the Transformer instantiation T_ε on the same OOD set. The frozen Sumformer model also shows the strongest size generalization, maintaining flat error as input point cloud size and bounding box scale increase, while all other neural baselines degrade rapidly.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its encode-process-decode framework (with Sumformer or Transformer as the processor) against four types of baselines: (1) direct end-to-end neural models (S_Direct, T_Direct) that predict the extent measure without any kernel intermediate, (2) a no-coreset baseline (S_Baseline, T_Baseline) that feeds the raw processor output to the decoder without the column-softmax coreset step, (3) the Transformer-instantiated version of the same architecture, and (4) classical algorithmic ε-kernel constructions (B_ε,E, B_ε,R). The Sumformer frozen model (S_extent Frozen) dominates on MEB (0.009 vs. 0.023–0.178 for all others) and MEE (0.037/0.022 vs. up to 0.932/0.887 for T_Direct). The Transformer version T_extent(Frozen) is notably weaker on MEB (0.099) compared to its Sumformer counterpart, and the gap widens dramatically on OOD data (e.g., 0.218 vs. 0.028 directional width error on SQUID). The no-coreset baselines (S_Baseline, T_Baseline) suffer large MEE errors (0.378–0.426), confirming that the relaxed-ε-kernel coreset step is critical. For MEA width, the E2E Sumformer variant (0.038) slightly edges out the Frozen variant (0.050), though both substantially outperform the direct and no-coreset baselines (0.141–0.674). The algorithmic exact ε-kernel baseline B_ε,E achieves 0.040 directional width error on OOD synthetic data in R², competitive with the neural S_ε (0.043), but the neural model offers the advantage of differentiability and data-driven learning of unknown objectives.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges classical computational geometry (ε-kernels, ε-coresets, and the linearization technique for extent measures from Agarwal et al.) with modern neural architecture design (Sumformer, DeepSets, and the encode-process-decode framework from neural algorithmic reasoning). The key conceptual departure is the introduction of 'relaxed-ε-kernels,' which relax the classical requirement that kernel points be a subset of the input to allow convex combinations, thereby making the construction naturally implementable by a single-block Sumformer with bounded model complexity. This positions the work at the intersection of the neural-algorithmic alignment literature and geometric approximation theory, showing that architectural inductive bias matching a specific algorithmic flow (sum-aggregation mimicking directional projection) yields provable approximation guarantees that generic permutation-equivariant architectures (Transformers) cannot match in practice.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first neural architecture with provable bounded model complexity (parameters independent of input point-set size) for a family of geometric extent measures, which is a meaningful theoretical guarantee absent in most neural optimization approaches. The empirical finding that the simpler Sumformer consistently outperforms the more expressive Transformer on OOD generalization—attributed to architectural-algorithmic alignment—offers a practical design principle for neural solvers of structured optimization problems. Limitations include the dependence of model complexity on input dimension and the 'fatness' assumption in theoretical guarantees, and the approach is currently restricted to extent measures amenable to the ε-kernel/linearization framework. Open questions include whether the alignment principle generalizes beyond geometric problems and whether the fatness assumption can be relaxed for degenerate point sets.
