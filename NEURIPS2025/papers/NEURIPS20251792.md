## Problem

LFP-based neural models consistently underperform spike-based models in behavior decoding, particularly under unsupervised training, due to the aggregate, population-level nature of LFP signals that obscures task-relevant variability. While LFPs offer practical advantages for BCIs (long-term stability, robustness to electrode degradation, lower power), no prior work has explored transferring representational knowledge from high-fidelity spike models to LFP models via cross-modal knowledge distillation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251792/cropped_page4_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The student model consumes continuous LFP signals (100 Hz, 96–192 channels) or LFP power features (9 frequency bands per channel) spatially patched into groups of 32 (or 288 for power signals), producing latent representations that decode 2D behavior velocity. Critically, at inference time only the LFP signal is required—the frozen spike teacher is used exclusively during training. The teacher model separately consumes discrete spike counts (10 ms bins, patched in groups of 64 neurons) to generate the alignment target representations.

## Method

The framework trains a multi-session spike teacher (MS-Spike) using masked autoencoding (60% spatial-temporal masking) with a novel session-specific space embedding strategy that replaces NDT2's shared space embeddings, then distills its representational knowledge into a single- or multi-session LFP student model. The distillation objective (Eq. 1) combines an LFP autoencoding loss with a cosine-similarity alignment term that maximizes agreement between sequence-averaged latent representations of paired spike and LFP signals, scaled by λ=5. The teacher is fully frozen during distillation, and the LFP student uses dilated causal convolutional layers for value embeddings to handle continuous-valued inputs. This design enables the LFP model to inherit behavior-predictive structure from the spike model while retaining LFP-specific dynamics, and the resulting distilled model generalizes to unseen sessions without additional distillation.

## Results

In the fully unsupervised setting, Distilled LFP models achieve an average behavior decoding R² of 0.71, substantially outperforming MS-LFP (0.27), SS-LFP (0.24), SS-MM (0.33), and SS-MM-ZS (0.27) baselines (p < 2.6×10⁻¹⁰, one-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank test, n=51). Notably, Distilled LFP models even surpass their teacher MS-Spike models (0.71 vs. 0.69, p < 1.7×10⁻³). In the supervised setting, multi-session distillation (MS-Distilled LFP) further improves performance to 0.82 R² (supervised fine-tuning) and 0.83 R² (fully-supervised), compared to 0.80 and 0.81 for single-session Distilled LFP. Representation alignment between MS-Spike and Distilled LFP achieves 0.67 top-1 retrieval accuracy, 0.90 top-5 accuracy, and CKA of 0.89, versus near-random performance (top-1 ≈ 0.0007) for MS-Spike vs. MS-LFP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The Distilled LFP model decisively outperforms all LFP-only baselines: MS-LFP (0.27 R²) and SS-LFP (0.24 R²) in the unsupervised setting, with gains of ~0.44 and ~0.47 R² respectively (p < 2.6×10⁻¹⁰). It also surpasses the multimodal SS-MM baseline (0.33 R²) and its LFP-only-inference variant SS-MM-ZS (0.27 R²), demonstrating that explicit cross-modal representation alignment is more effective than simply concatenating spike and LFP inputs. Remarkably, Distilled LFP models exceed their own teacher MS-Spike (0.71 vs. 0.69 R², p < 1.7×10⁻³), suggesting the alignment objective helps extract shared behavior-predictive information more effectively than spike-only modeling. In the supervised setting, Distilled LFP still outperforms all LFP baselines but falls below the directly supervised spike models (MS-Spike, SS-MM), which serve as an upper bound. The improved MS-Spike teacher model (using session-specific space embeddings) outperforms the NDT2 baseline [17] on downstream decoding, validating the tokenization design choice.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the NDT2 multi-session spike transformer framework [17] but replaces its shared space embeddings with session-specific space embeddings, yielding an improved MS-Spike teacher model. The core novelty is a cross-modal representational knowledge distillation objective that aligns LFP latent representations to frozen spike-model representations via cosine similarity, drawing on general knowledge distillation and cross-modal alignment strategies from CV/NLP but applying them to neural modalities for the first time. It is explicitly positioned as distinct from multimodal fusion approaches (which require both spike and LFP at inference) and from prior iEEG multi-session modeling, instead producing a unimodal LFP model that operates solely on LFP signals after training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LFPs offer greater long-term stability, robustness to electrode degradation, and lower power than spikes, making them attractive for chronic BCI applications, yet they have historically underperformed spike-based models in decoding. This work demonstrates that cross-modal distillation can close much of that gap (0.71 vs. 0.27 average R²) while preserving LFP generalizability to unseen sessions without additional distillation, suggesting a practical path to stable, spike-free BCI decoding. Open questions include whether the approach extends to other brain regions, larger and more diverse pretraining corpora, alternative pretraining objectives beyond MAE, and whether LFPs can serve as full replacements for spikes under controlled signal-degradation conditions.
