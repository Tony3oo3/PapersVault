## Problem

Current vision-language models lack robust spatial perception and reasoning in 3D scenes, yet existing solutions either inject 3D representations (point clouds, meshes) that are scarce, architecturally invasive, and unavailable in real-world settings, or rely on 2D internet images without precise 3D ground truth. The paper asks whether VLMs can acquire rich 3D spatial understanding purely from 2D image inputs, without any 3D modality at inference time.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255180/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts 1–5 RGB images (single-view or multi-view, typically 448×448 per frame) alongside a natural-language question, and produces either a numerical answer (depth or distance in meters, rounded to 0.1 m), a categorical spatial relation (left/right/behind/above), a 3D bounding box (recovered from predicted UV, depth, and size via camera pose), or BEV 2D coordinates in an observer-centric frame. Multi-view QA pairs use 3–5 images with one randomly designated as the primary viewpoint; video inputs are excluded from the benchmark to isolate spatial from temporal reasoning.

## Method

The core contribution is a 3D-ground-truth-driven 2D data generation pipeline that converts scene meshes, 3D bounding boxes, and camera parameters from ScanNet, ScanNet++, and Structured3D into 7M+ spatial QA pairs across 33 task types. The pipeline has three stages: (1) pose-based subsampling that removes ~90% of redundant frames using translation (0.5 m) and rotation (15–45°) thresholds; (2) extraction of a unified scene data structure linking image records (camera intrinsics/extrinsics, visible objects) to object records (3D centers, bounding boxes, 2D projections) in world coordinates; (3) template-based task generation where human-authored and GPT-expanded question templates are filled with computed spatial values (depth, Euclidean distance, relative position, viewpoint transformation angles). Training uses SPAR-mix—40% SPAR-7M spatial data mixed with 60% EMOVA-2M general 2D data—on InternVL2.5-8B with an unfrozen ViT, requiring ~6,100 NPU-hours. The key design choice is that all 3D supervision is baked into the 2D training labels, so the model never sees point clouds or depth maps at inference, yet learns implicit scene-consistent geometry as evidenced by a BEV coordinate-prediction probe that reduces mean position error from 2.22 m to 1.05 m.

## Results

Pretraining InternVL2.5-8B on SPAR-mix (40% SPAR-7M + 60% EMOVA-2M) achieves state-of-the-art performance on CV-Bench 3D (89.08) and VSI-Bench (41.1), surpassing GPT-4v (73.8 on CV-Bench 3D) and GPT-4o (34.0 on VSI-Bench). On SPAR-Bench, the fine-tuned model reaches 66.65 average score, exceeding human performance (67.27) on depth prediction and distance estimation tasks while remaining below humans on complex multi-view reasoning. On 3D-specific tasks, SPAR-mix achieves competitive results without any 3D input: 58.1 EM on SQA3D (vs. 58.6 for Video-3D LLM with 3D) and 48.8 Acc@0.25 on ScanRefer (vs. 58.1 for Video-3D LLM).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On CV-Bench 3D, SPAR-mix (89.08) substantially outperforms GPT-4v (73.8), Cambrian-8B (72.0), and the Base VLM (78.50), demonstrating that 2D spatial pretraining transfers to 3D geometric reasoning. On VSI-Bench, SPAR-mix (41.1) surpasses GPT-4o (34.0), InternVL2-8B (34.6), and LLaVA-OV-7B (25.3), confirming gains in egocentric spatial reasoning. On 3D-specific QA (SQA3D/ScanQA), SPAR-mix achieves 58.1 EM / 90.7 CIDEr without any 3D input, closely matching Video-3D LLM (58.6 EM / 102.1 CIDEr) which uses explicit 3D representations, and outperforming 3D-LLM (12.0 BLEU-4), LEO (13.2 BLEU-4), and LLaVA-3D (14.5 BLEU-4). On ScanRefer 3D grounding, SPAR-mix (48.8 Acc@0.25) trails Video-3D LLM (58.1) and LLaVA-3D (54.1) but exceeds Grounded3D-LLM (47.9) and Chat-3D v2 (35.9), all of which incorporate 3D point clouds. On SPAR-Bench, the fine-tuned model (66.65 avg) dramatically outperforms the strongest external baselines—Qwen2.5-VL-72B (39.40), GPT-4o (36.39), and Claude-3.7-Sonnet (21.77)—with the largest gaps on multi-view depth and distance tasks. General 2D benchmarks (MMBench, TextVQA) show minor drops (~1–2%) relative to the Base VLM, confirming that spatial pretraining does not significantly degrade general vision-language capabilities.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of injecting 3D representations (point clouds, voxel grids) into VLM architectures (3D-LLM, LLaVA-3D, LEO, Scene-LLM) by arguing that spatial understanding can be acquired entirely from 2D images if the training data is generated with 3D ground-truth supervision. It also extends prior 2D spatial datasets (SpatialVLM, SpatialRGPT) that relied on estimated or internet-sourced annotations by constructing a pipeline that derives precise numerical labels (depth, distance, camera pose) from ScanNet/ScanNet++/Structured3D scene data. The multi-view 3D grounding approach further reformulates grounding as a text-generation problem (frame selection + mono-3D prediction) rather than the point-cloud regression or proposal classification used by prior 3D grounding methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SPAR-7M and SPAR-Bench fill a significant gap: no prior dataset simultaneously covers perception-level tasks (depth, distance, camera parameters), reasoning-level tasks (spatial imagination, viewpoint transformation), and multi-view inputs with precise 3D ground truth. The practical finding that 2D-only pretraining yields competitive 3D-task performance (SQA3D, ScanQA, ScanRefer) without architectural modification lowers the barrier for spatial VLM deployment. Key open questions remain: whether this approach generalizes to continuous/interactive embodied settings, to domains lacking 3D ground truth for data generation, and whether the cognitive stratification (perception → reasoning) holds as models scale. The static, QA-based nature of the tasks limits applicability to long-horizon video or real-time navigation.
