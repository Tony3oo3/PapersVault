## Problem

First-order optimizers ignore the structure of gradient noise, while second-order methods that capture curvature (K-FAC, Shampoo, AdaFisher) incur prohibitive matrix-operation costs. The original KOALA framework addressed this via Kalman filtering but assumed isotropic covariance (P ≈ σ²I), collapsing directional uncertainty into a scalar and limiting adaptability to anisotropic loss landscapes. KOALA++ closes the gap between structured covariance expressiveness and first-order scalability.

## Input / Output

At each iteration k, the method consumes the current minibatch (to compute the scalar loss L_k and its gradient H_k ∈ ℝ^{1×n}), the current parameter vector θ_{k-1} ∈ ℝ^n, and the previously maintained directional surrogate v_{k-1} ∈ ℝ^{1×n}. It outputs the updated parameters θ_k and the new surrogate v_k, both of which are O(n)-sized vectors. Crucially, the full n×n covariance matrix P_{k-1} is never stored or inverted; all operations reduce to vector–vector and vector–matrix products, yielding O(n) per-step cost comparable to Adam.

## Method

KOALA++ reparameterizes the Extended Kalman Filter update by propagating the gradient-covariance product v_k := H_k P_{k-1} (a 1×n row vector) instead of the full posterior covariance. The recursive update v_k = (α_k − λ_k) v_{k-1} + (H_k − λ_k H_{k-1}) Q + r_k H_{k-1} is derived by substituting the Kalman covariance recursion and then approximating the intractable term H_k P_{k-2} via a minimum Frobenius-norm least-squares solution to the underdetermined constraint v_{k-1} = H_{k-1} P_{k-2}. Two variants are provided: a vanilla (asymmetric) solution and a symmetric variant that enforces P_{k-2} = P_{k-2}^⊤, adding a correction term r_k. The parameter update itself follows the standard Kalman gain form θ_k = θ_{k-1} + (L_k^{target} − L_k(θ_{k-1})) (v_k^⊤ + Q H_k^⊤) / S_k, where the learning rate is controlled through the choice of L_k^{target}. This design implicitly captures directional curvature anisotropy and momentum-like dynamics without explicit momentum augmentation or Kronecker-factored approximations.

## Results

On CIFAR-100 with ResNet-50 (100 epochs, StepLR), KOALA++ achieves 22.24% Top-1 error and 5.97% Top-5 error, outperforming all 10 compared optimizers including AdaFisher (23.38%), Adabelief (23.07%), and the original KOALA-M (23.71%). On the broader CIFAR-10 benchmark across five architectures (ResNet-50/101, DenseNet-121, MobileNetV3, Swin-Tiny), KOALA++ matches or exceeds AdaFisher(W) on 3 of 5 architectures while exhibiting consistently lower standard deviation across 5 seeds. On Wikitext-2 language modeling, KOALA++ achieves 151.75 test perplexity, slightly beating AdaFisherW (152.72) at a comparable training speed (25.56 vs 23.39 s/epoch).

## Baselines & Comparisons

KOALA++ outperforms all 10 baselines in the standardized CIFAR-100/ResNet-50 comparison (Table 3), with the largest margins over Adam-family optimizers (AdamW: 27.23%, AdamP: 26.62%) and a clear 1.14-point Top-1 advantage over the closest competitor Adabelief (23.07%). Against the direct predecessor KOALA-M, KOALA++ improves Top-1 error by 1.47 points (22.24 vs 23.71) and Top-5 by 0.61 points, confirming the benefit of structured covariance over the scalar approximation. Versus the strongest second-order baseline AdaFisher, KOALA++ wins on CIFAR-100 (22.24 vs 23.38) and Wikitext-2 (151.75 vs 152.72 PPL), but trails on MobileNetV3 (94.52 vs 95.28) and Swin-Tiny (87.64 vs 88.74) in the multi-architecture CIFAR-10 benchmark, though with notably lower variance across seeds. Against K-FAC and Shampoo, KOALA++ achieves higher Top-1 accuracy on ResNet-50 (96.44 vs 95.66/94.59) and ResNet-101 (96.66 vs 96.01/94.63), while K-FAC and Shampoo collapse on Swin-Tiny (64.79/63.91 vs 87.64). In the convergence comparison with Muon (a scalable quasi-second-order optimizer), Muon converges faster in early epochs but KOALA++ reaches comparable or better final accuracy after stabilizing, suggesting the implicit least-squares covariance correction requires a brief burn-in period.

## Relation to Prior Work

KOALA++ directly extends the KOALA framework (Davtyan et al., AAAI 2022), which recast stochastic optimization as a scalar Kalman filtering problem but relied on isotropic covariance (P ≈ σ²I) and gradient-norm approximations that discard directional structure. It departs from second-order optimizers (K-FAC, Shampoo, AdaFisher) that approximate the Fisher or Hessian with block-diagonal/Kronecker structure at O(n²)–O(n³) cost, and from full-space Kalman approaches (Duran-Martin et al., Chang et al.) that maintain matrix-valued state updates. The key conceptual move is replacing the full covariance with a single directional surrogate v_k = H_k P_{k-1} (a 1×n row vector) updated via a minimum-norm least-squares step, preserving O(n) per-iteration complexity while implicitly capturing anisotropic curvature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper bridges a practical gap: structured covariance modeling that was previously either too expensive (full/low-rank Kalman, K-FAC) or too simplistic (diagonal, scalar) for large-scale training. The main open limitation is the lack of a formal positive-semidefinite guarantee on the implicit covariance, which the authors acknowledge and leave to future work via projection or spectral regularization. Whether the method scales to very large pretraining settings (multi-billion-parameter LLMs, diffusion models) remains untested, and the modest margins over AdaFisher (e.g., 22.24 vs 23.38 top-1 error on CIFAR-100/ResNet-50) suggest the practical advantage is primarily in efficiency and stability rather than a large accuracy leap.
