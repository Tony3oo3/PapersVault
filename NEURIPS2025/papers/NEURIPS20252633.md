## Problem

Nonlinear mixture model identification (NMMI) is ill-posed: infinitely many (f, s) pairs can explain the same observations. Existing identifiability results rely on auxiliary variables, conditional independence of latent components, or sparsity of the mixing function's Jacobian—assumptions that may not hold in practice, particularly when observed features are influenced by all latent components. This paper asks whether identifiability can be established under a purely geometric condition on the Jacobian that permits dense, fully-connected mixing functions.

## Input / Output

The method consumes L realizations of x ∈ R^m (m ≥ d) generated by an unknown nonlinear diffeomorphism f applied to latent components s ∈ R^d, with no access to f, s, or their distribution. It outputs latent component estimates ŝ = g_φ(x) that recover s up to a global permutation and invertible element-wise rescaling. In the single-cell transcriptomics application, inputs are gene expression vectors (m=178 genes) and outputs are mRNA concentration levels of d=5 transcription factors, evaluated via R² and MCC against ground-truth SERGIO-simulated concentrations.

## Method

DICA uses a stacked autoencoder (two MLPs: encoder g_φ: R^m→R^d and decoder f_θ: R^d→R^m) trained with the J-VolMax criterion, which maximizes E[log det(J_{f_θ}(g_φ(x))ᵀ J_{f_θ}(g_φ(x)))] subject to row-wise L1-norm bounds and reconstruction. The key theoretical insight is the Sufficiently Diverse Influence (SDI) condition: at each s, the gradient vectors ∇f_1(s),…,∇f_m(s) (rows of the Jacobian) must be sufficiently scattered within a weighted L1-norm ball such that their convex hull contains the maximum volume inscribed ellipsoid and touches its boundary at the correct facets. Under SDI, the paper proves that any J-VolMax optimum yields a Jacobian that is a signed permutation matrix (up to diagonal scaling), establishing global identifiability without auxiliary variables, independence, or sparsity. Training uses a warm-up schedule that linearly ramps the volume regularization weight, with a softplus-based norm penalty after warm-up to avoid hard-constraint optimization difficulties.

## Results

On synthetic Mixtures A and B, DICA achieves nonlinear R² scores consistently above 0.92 (up to 0.99) and MCC above 0.93 across all (d, m) settings, substantially outperforming all baselines. On the more challenging Mixture C (fully nonlinear MLP mixing), DICA still attains R² of 0.87–0.95 and MCC of 0.92–0.97, maintaining a clear margin over IMA, Sparse, and Base. On the single-cell transcriptomics task (TRRUST, d=5 TFs, m=178 genes), DICA recovers ground-truth transcription factor activities with mean R² ≈ 1 and MCC ≈ 1, while Sparse achieves R² ≈ 0.45 and Base achieves R² ≈ 0.34.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DICA outperforms all three baselines (IMA, Sparse, and unregularized Base autoencoder) across every mixture type and dimensionality setting. On Mixtures A and B, DICA's R² advantage over the best baseline (Sparse) is typically 0.10–0.27, and over Base it is 0.20–0.40; the gap widens as d and m grow, where baselines degrade sharply (e.g., Base drops to R²=0.42 at (5,60) while DICA stays at 0.92). On the hardest Mixture C (fully nonlinear MLP mixing), DICA maintains R² ≥ 0.87 while IMA falls to 0.53–0.84 and Base to 0.47–0.77. On the TRRUST single-cell transcriptomics task, DICA achieves near-perfect recovery (mean R² ≈ 1, MCC ≈ 1) with clean disentanglement of all 5 transcription factors, whereas Sparse reaches only R² ≈ 0.45 (disentangling just 2 of 5 TFs) and Base reaches R² ≈ 0.34 with no meaningful disentanglement. The ablation in Table 2 further confirms that DICA's advantage is tied to the m/d ratio: performance degrades as m approaches 2d (the minimum for SDI), while baselines show no such structured dependence.

## Relation to Prior Work

DICA extends the nonlinear ICA identifiability literature by replacing the Jacobian sparsity assumption (used in [2, 3, 19, 21]) and the column-orthogonality assumption of Independent Mechanism Analysis [20] with a convex-geometric 'Sufficiently Diverse Influence' (SDI) condition on the Jacobian rows. The core analytical machinery is borrowed from Polytopic Matrix Factorization [30], where the Sufficiently Scattered Condition (SSC) and determinant-maximization criterion guarantee identifiability of matrix factors; DICA transplants this geometry into the Jacobian domain over a continuous manifold. Unlike auxiliary-information-based nICA [10–12], the approach requires no side information, no latent independence, and permits fully dense Jacobians.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The SDI condition offers a new, arguably more natural axis for identifiability: it is automatically easier to satisfy when m ≫ d (the common high-dimensional regime), and it admits dense Jacobians that violate sparsity-based methods. However, the theory assumes noise-free observations, unlimited samples, and a universal function class, and the log-det objective is computationally expensive to optimize at scale. Open questions include efficient optimization of J-VolMax, sample-complexity guarantees under noise and model mismatch, and whether the SDI condition can be verified or encouraged in practice without ground-truth knowledge of the mixing function.
