## Problem

Existing instruction-tuning data selection methods (e.g., IFD-based scoring) evaluate quality at the sample level, treating all response tokens equally even though over 20% contribute little to instruction following. Additionally, these scoring functions are not robust: small, semantics-preserving perturbations (like synonym substitution) can cause large score shifts, meaning high scores may reflect sensitivity to superficial lexical features rather than genuine sample quality.

## Input / Output

The method takes a large instruction-response dataset (e.g., 52k Alpaca-GPT-4 or 300k Magpie samples), a selection budget b, and a lightweight scoring model (GPT-2) as input. It produces a curated subset of b instruction-response pairs, along with per-sample S-IFD quality scores and neighborhood statistics (mean and variance of S-IFD over M perturbed neighbors). The scoring model operates on token embeddings, and the perturbation step injects uniform noise into the embedding space rather than modifying surface text, so the method never requires re-tokenization or API calls for neighbor generation.

## Method

T-SHIRT introduces two complementary mechanisms. First, Selective IFD (S-IFD) reweights the standard IFD score by computing per-token log-likelihood differences Δ_t (the change in token probability when conditioning on the instruction) and retaining only the top k% most informative tokens across the entire dataset, effectively discarding uninformative tokens from quality computation. Second, a hierarchical selection strategy assesses robustness by generating M neighbors of each sample via uniform noise injection into token embeddings (scaled by α/√((L+T)d)), computing S-IFD for each perturbed neighbor, and then performing a two-stage selection: first picking γb samples with the highest neighborhood mean S-IFD, then narrowing to b samples with the lowest neighborhood variance. The entire pipeline uses only GPT-2 for score computation, processing a 52k-sample dataset in ~40 minutes on a single GPU without any API calls.

## Results

On Alpaca-GPT-4 (5% selection), T-SHIRT(k=75) achieves μ_ALL = 47.71 on Llama-3.1-8B and T-SHIRT(k=50) achieves μ_ALL = 57.60 on Qwen-2.5-7B, outperforming the full-dataset baseline by 5.48 and 5.10 points respectively. On the larger Magpie dataset (10k samples), T-SHIRT(k=50) reaches μ_ALL = 58.45 on Qwen-2.5-7B, beating the strongest baseline IFD (58.05) and LONGEST (57.07). The method requires only ~40 minutes on a single GPU using GPT-2 for score computation, with no API costs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

T-SHIRT consistently outperforms the strongest baseline IFD (Superfiltering) on 6 of 8 individual benchmarks and achieves the highest μ_ALL on both Llama-3.1-8B (47.71 vs. 46.94) and Qwen-2.5-7B (57.60 vs. 56.81). Against the API-based methods DEITA and DS², T-SHIRT wins decisively on μ_ALL (47.71/57.60 vs. 45.55/56.04 and 45.54/55.92 respectively), while also being 2.7–3.7× faster and requiring no API credits. Notably, T-SHIRT with only 5% of Alpaca-GPT-4 data surpasses the full-dataset baseline by 5.10–5.48 points in μ_ALL, demonstrating that selective data quality matters more than quantity. On the larger, higher-quality Magpie dataset, T-SHIRT still edges out IFD (58.45 vs. 58.05 μ_ALL) and LONGEST (57.07), confirming scalability. The simple LONGEST heuristic remains a surprisingly strong baseline, outperforming DEITA and DS² on μ_ALL, which the authors attribute to potential biases in LLM-scored selection methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the IFD-based data selection line established by Superfiltering [17] and the original IFD metric [16], which showed that a weak model like GPT-2 can score instruction-tuning samples effectively. It departs from prior work in two conceptual directions: (1) moving from sample-level to token-level quality assessment via Selective IFD (S-IFD), motivated by recent findings that only a subset of response tokens are informative for alignment [19–22], and (2) introducing a robustness-aware hierarchical selection step that evaluates local neighborhood consistency, drawing on adversarial robustness and NEFTune-style embedding perturbations [34]. Unlike DEITA and DS², which rely on expensive proprietary LLM APIs, T-SHIRT remains entirely self-contained with a small open-source model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical takeaway is that a 40-minute, single-GPU, zero-API-cost pipeline can select 5% of a dataset that outperforms full-dataset training by up to 5.48 points, making high-quality instruction tuning accessible without expensive scoring infrastructure. The robustness framing—showing that IFD scores can flip under a single synonym substitution—raises an open question about whether other widely-used scoring metrics (perplexity, length, LLM-judge scores) suffer similar fragility. Limitations include evaluation only on 7B–14B models, absence of a theoretical guarantee for the hierarchical selection criterion, and the fact that the method addresses quality but not diversity, leaving room for combination with diversity-aware selection or selective language modeling.
