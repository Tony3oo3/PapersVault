## Problem

Continual Test-Time Adaptation (CTTA) requires models to adapt to a stream of unknown, shifting domains without pre-acquiring downstream data, but existing methods that restore initial weights or fuse historical models suffer from domain conflicts: mixing incompatible knowledge from distinct domains disrupts gradient directions, causes catastrophic forgetting, and incurs unbounded storage overhead. Prompt-based CTTA approaches further fail to disentangle class-level discriminative information from domain-level style information, and cannot dynamically adjust prompting capacity as domains change.

## Input / Output

The method consumes unlabelled streaming test image batches (batch size 64) from unknown domains, a pre-trained ViT-B/16 backbone, and pre-computed source-domain feature statistics (mean and standard deviation from ~300 unlabelled source examples) used only for the domain alignment loss. It produces per-sample class label predictions and maintains two bounded prompt pools: a class prompt pool (up to N_c=100 prompts, each of length 1) and a domain prompt pool (up to N_d=20 prompts, each of length 8), which are updated after every batch via fission and fusion operations.

## Method

KFF introduces two complementary modules operating on a frozen ViT with learnable prompts. The Knowledge Fission (KFI) module matches each test sample's pseudo-label against class prompt keys via cosine similarity and each batch's feature statistics (μ, σ) against domain prompt keys via Euclidean distance; prompts exceeding a threshold are selected and combined with softmax-weighted averaging, while unmatched samples/batches trigger creation of a new prompt, thereby isolating domain-specific knowledge and blocking interference from dissimilar historical domains. The Knowledge Fusion (KFU) module controls pool growth by merging learned prompts back: class prompts are clustered via a minimum spanning tree (Kruskal) on cosine-similarity edges and fused within clusters, while domain prompts are merged by fusing the closest pair by Euclidean distance when the pool reaches capacity. Optimization uses a bi-level loss combining domain alignment (matching current-batch feature statistics to source statistics) and per-sample classification entropy minimization, with separate learning rates for domain (0.1) and class (0.001) prompts. A theoretical analysis under a well-separated cluster assumption proves that KFI always assigns batches to prompts from the correct cluster and KFU only merges within-cluster prompts, guaranteeing correct prompt retrieval indefinitely.

## Results

On ImageNet-to-ImageNet-C (severity 5), KFF achieves a mean classification error of 34.8%, surpassing the previous SOTA DPCore (39.9%) by 5.1% and the source model (55.8%) by 21.0%. On CIFAR100-to-CIFAR100C and CIFAR10-to-CIFAR10C, KFF reaches 22.5% and 12.4% mean error respectively, outperforming DPCore by 2.6% and 3.0%. Under the more challenging 10-round repeating-domain setting, KFF maintains a stable 34.5% mean error while DPCore degrades to 44.4%, demonstrating superior long-term knowledge retention with only 0.09M learnable parameters (~0.1% of the ViT-B/16 backbone).

## Baselines & Comparisons

KFF outperforms all 12 baselines across all three datasets. Against the strongest competitor DPCore (ICML'25), KFF gains 5.1% on ImageNet-C, 2.6% on CIFAR100-C, and 3.0% on CIFAR10-C in the non-repeating setting, and a dramatic 9.9% in the 10-round repeating-domain setting (34.5% vs. 44.4%), where DPCore's unconstrained prompt accumulation causes progressive degradation. Among other CTTA methods, KFF beats C-MAE (42.5%), PALM (43.6%), and ViDA (43.4%) by 7.7–8.8% on ImageNet-C. General TTA baselines (Tent 51.0%, SAR 45.6%, POEM 46.4%) and earlier CTTA methods (CoTTA 49.9%, VDP 50.0%, RoTTA 48.2%) are all surpassed by a wide margin. In terms of efficiency, KFF uses only 0.09M learnable parameters in Round 1 (vs. 86.57M for CoTTA and 93.70M for ViDA), and maintains 0.20M even in Round 10 while DPCore grows to 1.03M, confirming that the Knowledge Fusion module effectively caps parameter growth.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the prompt-learning-for-CTTA line initiated by VDP, SVDP, and most directly DPCore (ICML 2025), which stores domain-specific prompts in a growing pool and applies them at inference. The key conceptual departure is the explicit separation of class-level and domain-level knowledge into distinct prompt pools, combined with a fission mechanism that selectively matches prompts based on domain similarity (rather than applying all stored prompts indiscriminately as DPCore does) and a fusion mechanism using minimum spanning tree clustering to control pool growth. The problem formulation and evaluation protocol follow CoTTA (CVPR 2022), and the adaptation loss combines domain alignment (Ben-David et al.) with entropy minimization (TENT), both well-established TTA objectives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 5.1% error reduction over DPCore on ImageNet-C, achieved with only 0.09M learnable parameters that remain stable across repeated domain cycles (vs. DPCore's ~5× parameter growth by round 10), makes this a practically relevant efficiency win for deployment scenarios where storage and latency matter. The theoretical guarantee under well-separated clusters provides a clean justification for the fission/fusion design, though the assumption may not hold for highly overlapping real-world domains. Key limitations include the dependency on source domain statistics (restricting use in privacy-sensitive settings) and validation only on synthetic corruptions, leaving open whether the approach generalizes to naturalistic, uncontrolled distribution shifts.
