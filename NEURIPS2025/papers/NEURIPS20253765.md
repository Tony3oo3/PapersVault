## Problem

Unsupervised anomaly detection methods assume clean training data, but real-world datasets are often contaminated with undetected anomalies, degrading detection performance. Existing remedies require access to training pipelines, raw data, or the contamination ratio—conditions rarely met when deploying proprietary or pre-trained AD models. This paper addresses the underexplored setting of preparation-agnostic test-time adaptation for AD, where only the model's output scores and test-time evidence are available.

## Input / Output

The framework consumes two score streams at test time: the inlier score s_in(x) from a pre-trained AD model (e.g., PatchCore, CFLOW, DeepSVDD) and an evidence score T(x) from an auxiliary function (CLIP similarity for images, LOF or IForest for tabular data), both computed on the same test samples in a transductive manner. It outputs a revised inlier score that is a monotonic combination of the two, from which a binary normal/anomalous decision is made via a threshold. No retraining, model weights, or training data are required—only the scalar scores and a single temperature hyperparameter β.

## Method

EPHAD applies exponential tilting to the contaminated density (or equivalently, the score-based EBM), producing a revised density proportional to exp(s_in(x) + T(x)/β), where β controls the trade-off between trusting the original model and the evidence. The key theoretical result (Proposition 4.1) shows that the revised density is closer to the true inlier density in KL divergence whenever the evidence assigns higher expected log-likelihood to true inliers than the normalizing constant. This formulation is shown to be the optimal solution of a KL-regularized objective, establishing a direct connection to test-time alignment in generative models. For practical score-based AD, the normalization constants are dropped since only relative ordering matters, yielding a simple additive score combination. An adaptive variant, EPHAD-Ada, determines β unsupervised via entropy minimization over Beta-posterior inlier probabilities estimated from test scores, eliminating the need for labelled validation data.

## Results

On visual AD datasets with 10% contamination, EPHAD consistently improves AUROC across most AD methods: CFLOW jumps from 65.47% to 97.38% on CIFAR-10, PaDiM from 86.84% to 94.66% on Fashion-MNIST, and PatchCore from 78.97% to 94.73% on Fashion-MNIST. On tabular datasets, DeepSVDD improves from 54.06% to 70.67% AUROC on aloi (with EPHAD-Ada), and IForest from 54.05% to 71.75% on aloi (with EPHAD). The adaptive variant EPHAD-Ada further recovers performance in cases where fixed β=0.5 causes degradation, e.g., ULSAD on SVHN (64.27% → 59.68% with EPHAD, but 68.4% with EPHAD-Ada) and PatchCore on RealIAD (70.08% → 77.18%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

EPHAD substantially outperforms standalone CLIP/WinCLIP on domain-specific sensory datasets (e.g., MVTec: 86.34% CLIP vs. 89.84% CFLOW+EPHAD-Ada; ViSA: 74.47% CLIP vs. 79.64% CFLOW+EPHAD-Ada), while CLIP remains competitive on semantic datasets like CIFAR-10 (98.63%). Among AD baselines, EPHAD consistently improves CFLOW, PaDiM, PatchCore, RD, and DR/EM by 5–30+ AUROC points on semantic datasets (e.g., CFLOW CIFAR-10: 65.47→97.38; PaDiM FMNIST: 86.84→94.66), but gains are smaller or negative on datasets where the AD method already dominates (e.g., ULSAD on SVHN drops from 64.27% to 58.17% with EPHAD, though EPHAD-Ada recovers to 59.68%). On tabular data, EPHAD improves DeepSVDD (aloi: 54.06→70.67%), IForest (aloi: 54.05→71.75%), and COPOD (wine: 80.51→93.96% with EPHAD-Ada), though LOF as a baseline already achieves near-perfect scores on some datasets (wine: 99.94%) leaving little room for improvement. The paper also compares against Refine (Yoon et al., 2022), LOE (Qiu et al., 2022), and SoftPatch (Jiang et al., 2022) in the appendix, noting that EPHAD's post-hoc, training-free nature distinguishes it from these contamination-aware training methods that require knowledge of the contamination ratio.

## Relation to Prior Work

EPHAD draws its core mathematical machinery from the KL-regularized alignment literature (Korbak et al., 2022), reframing exponential tilting of a contaminated density as a KL-regularized optimization objective, and from Perini et al. (2021) for the Beta-posterior inlier probability estimation used in the adaptive variant. Conceptually, it departs from prior contamination-handling methods (Refine, LOE, SoftPatch) that modify the training pipeline or require knowledge of the contamination ratio, instead positioning itself within the preparation-agnostic test-time adaptation (TTA) paradigm that has been largely unexplored for anomaly detection. The paper also establishes explicit links to test-time alignment techniques in generative models, where a model is viewed as a policy shifted toward a reward function, providing a unifying lens across AD and generative alignment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

EPHAD offers a practical, drop-in solution for the increasingly common scenario where AD models are deployed as proprietary or pre-trained black boxes and cannot be retrained, addressing a gap that training-time contamination methods cannot fill. The framework's flexibility in accepting diverse evidence sources (foundation models, classical AD methods, domain knowledge) broadens its applicability, though the critical dependence on the temperature parameter β and the inconsistent gains from the adaptive variant (EPHAD-Ada) remain open challenges. Future work on more reliable β estimation, explainability integration, and systematic study of the dataset–method–evidence interplay would strengthen the framework's practical deployment readiness.
