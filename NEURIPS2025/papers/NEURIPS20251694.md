## Problem

Diffusion-based image editing methods rely on image inversion to approximate intermediate noisy representations along the generative trajectory, but the absence of exact supervision at intermediate steps introduces approximation errors that fundamentally limit editing fidelity. This paper asks whether binary-quantized generative models, whose intermediate quantized representations are exactly computable for any input image, can enable more precise inversion and thereby higher-quality text-driven editing.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251694/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a source RGB image, a source text prompt describing it, a target text prompt specifying the desired edit, and a binary editing mask localizing the region to modify. The source image is first encoded and quantized into K multi-scale binary residual token maps via BSQ (Binary Spherical Quantization), which serve as exact supervision during inversion. The output is a single edited RGB image where masked regions follow the target prompt while unmasked regions preserve the source content, with smooth transitions governed by a piecewise linear kernel over Manhattan distance to the mask boundary.

## Method

EditInfinity adapts the Infinity binary-quantized autoregressive model for editing via a two-phase pipeline. In the inversion phase, the exact multi-scale quantized tokens of the source image provide ground-truth supervision for a cross-entropy loss that optimizes 20 learnable prompt tokens (for textual rectification) followed by LoRA adapters on FFN layers (for style preservation), yielding a precise text embedding matched to the source image. In the editing phase, a multi-scale autoregressive generation loop produces target tokens at each scale conditioned on the target prompt and the optimized prompt/LoRA; at every scale, generated tokens are blended with source tokens using a piecewise linear smoothing kernel G that assigns zero weight within distance τ₁ of the mask (full target content), full weight beyond τ₂ (full source preservation), and linear interpolation in between. The blended tokens are downsampled to form the autoregressive context for the next scale, propagating structural guidance from unedited regions into edited regions across all K scales.

## Results

On PIE-Bench (700 test cases, 9 editing types), EditInfinity achieves the best score across all seven evaluation metrics: PSNR 27.95, LPIPS×10³ 33.08, MSE×10⁴ 24.27, SSIM×10² 92.12, Whole-CLIP 26.41, Edited-CLIP 23.47, and IR×10 5.88. The largest margins are in background preservation (e.g., LPIPS 33.08 vs. 131.18 for RF-Edit) and editing success rate (IR 5.88 vs. 5.18 for RF-Edit and 5.30 for Gemini). In a user study with 60 volunteers, 43.2% preferred EditInfinity's outputs—the highest among all compared methods. Per-editing runtime is 3.64 s after a one-time 107 s inversion, making iterative edits ~7× faster than competing methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EditInfinity outperforms all nine baselines on every reported metric. Against the strongest Diffusion UNet baseline NTI (CVPR'23), it improves PSNR from 27.03 to 27.95 and LPIPS from 60.67 to 33.08, while nearly doubling the IR score (5.88 vs. 2.77). Against the strongest Diffusion Transformer baseline RF-Edit (ICML'25), the gains are even more pronounced in background preservation (LPIPS 33.08 vs. 131.18; MSE 24.27 vs. 75.00) and text alignment (Edited-CLIP 23.47 vs. 22.40; IR 5.88 vs. 5.18). The closed-source Gemini 2.0 is also surpassed on all metrics, particularly LPIPS (33.08 vs. 105.17) and MSE (24.27 vs. 188.63). The paper further shows via GenEval that Infinity's base model (overall 0.66) is comparable to FLUX.1-dev (0.66), confirming that the editing gains stem from the proposed inversion-and-smoothing framework rather than a stronger generative backbone. On the supplementary FFHQ facial-attribute task, EditInfinity leads NTI and RF-Edit in ArcFace identity preservation (0.63 vs. 0.56/0.61) and CLIP-I similarity (0.86 vs. 0.83/0.79).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper applies the classical 'image inversion → image editing' paradigm—previously developed almost exclusively for diffusion models (P2P, NTI, PnP-Inv, RF-Edit)—to Infinity, a binary-quantized autoregressive generative model. The key conceptual departure is that binary-quantized models provide exact intermediate token representations for any source image, enabling precise inversion with ground-truth supervision rather than the approximate noisy latents that constrain diffusion-based inversion. The method further introduces a piecewise linear smoothing kernel for multi-scale token blending and leverages LoRA on FFN layers for style preservation, drawing on parameter-efficient fine-tuning techniques from the LLM adaptation literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that autoregressive binary-quantized models are a viable and in some respects superior alternative to diffusion models for text-driven image editing, challenging the field's near-exclusive focus on diffusion-based editing. The exact-supervision property of quantized tokens addresses a fundamental limitation of diffusion inversion (approximation error in intermediate steps), and the 3.64s per-edit latency after a one-time inversion makes iterative editing workflows practical. Open questions include whether the approach extends to video editing, whether the 107s inversion cost can be amortized or reduced, and whether the method's reliance on user-provided masks limits its applicability to fully prompt-driven editing. The authors note limitations in style-change tasks where background preservation is unnecessary.
