## Problem

Conventional transfer learning reuses entire parameter sets, implicitly assuming knowledge is globally entangled, which causes limited adaptability across architectures and susceptibility to negative transfer. Existing partial-parameter methods (Heur-LG, Auto-LG, TLEG, WAVE) improve flexibility but depend on handcrafted heuristics or rigid structural priors (e.g., linear constraints, Kronecker decompositions), constraining generalization across diverse tasks and model scales.

## Input / Output

ECO takes a target CNN architecture (VGG11, ResNet12, ResNet50, MobileNetV3-Large, or width-scaled variants from 0.5W to 0.9W) and a training dataset (CIFAR-FS, miniImageNet, or ImageNet-1K) as input, and produces a compact set of kernel-level parameters (learngenes) that initialize the target network. The same learngene can be transferred to networks of different widths and depths via zero-padding of incomplete filters, index reordering for narrower networks, and partial identity mapping for deeper networks, enabling O(1) initialization across architectures without retraining.

## Method

ECO redefines learngenes as continuous neural circuits—interconnected sets of convolutional kernels that preserve channel-wise consistency (K_{l+1} = F_l)—and introduces Genetic Transfer Learning (GTL) to extract them through population-based evolution. Each generation, a population of networks inherits learngenes from a Gene Pool, trains on randomly sampled k-way classification tasks (with k increasing over generations to simulate growing complexity), then undergoes structural mutation (probabilistic addition/removal of filters per layer). Tournament selection evaluates mutated learngenes by initializing critic networks on a validation set, and winners are stored in a Gene Pool with a Gene Tree tracking lineage; ancestral scores are updated with exponential decay (η^τ) to preserve evolutionary credit. This iterative cycle of inheritance, mutation, and selection progressively condenses transferable core knowledge into compact, reusable kernel circuits without handcrafted heuristics, achieving a 14.5× parameter reduction versus full fine-tuning while matching or exceeding pre-trained model accuracy.

## Results

On CIFAR-FS with VGG11, ECO-250th achieves 75.00% validation and 76.95% novel-class accuracy while transferring only 1.94M parameters (14.5× fewer than the full 28.18M model). On miniImageNet with ResNet12, ECO-250th reaches 82.00%/81.60% (valid/novel) with 2.47M transferred parameters. On ImageNet, ECO-100th achieves 66.50%/65.22% on MobileNetV3-Large (0.98M params) and 70.73%/69.91% on ResNet50 (3.13M params), outperforming all baselines while maintaining O(1) initialization complexity across variable model widths.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ECO consistently outperforms all 14 baselines across every architecture and dataset. Against initialization methods (He-Init, GradInit), ECO gains 8.9–11.5% on validation accuracy while transferring only ~2M parameters. Against hypernetwork-based methods (GHN-2, GHN-3), ECO achieves 5–10% higher accuracy with explicit parameter transfer, whereas GHN methods show no parameter count. Against prior learngene methods (Heur-LG, Auto-LG), ECO surpasses them by 4.8–7.0% on novel-class accuracy while transferring 4.8× fewer parameters (1.94M vs 9.19M on VGG11). Against Weight Selection [80], ECO reduces transferred parameters by 5.0× (1.94M vs 28.09M on VGG11) while gaining 3.85% on validation accuracy. On downstream tasks (Table 3), ECO achieves 70.55% average on VGG11 and 78.08% on ResNet12, outperforming the best baseline (Wt Select at 69.36% and 68.15% respectively). In few-shot settings (Table 4), ECO-250th beats Baseline++ by 5.0–5.6 points on 5-shot CIFAR-FS, demonstrating superior data efficiency.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Learngene framework from GRL [11] (originally developed for reinforcement learning) to supervised CNN training, redefining learngenes as continuous kernel-level neural circuits rather than abstract gene encodings. It departs from prior learngene methods (Heur-LG, Auto-LG, TLEG, WAVE) that rely on handcrafted heuristics or rigid structural priors (linear constraints, Kronecker decompositions) by instead using population-based evolutionary dynamics—mutation, tournament selection, and inheritance—to autonomously identify and condense transferable core knowledge. The broader positioning contrasts with full fine-tuning and parameter-efficient methods (LoRA) that assume globally entangled knowledge, and with evolutionary learning that optimizes task-specific solutions rather than accumulating cross-task knowledge.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 14.5× parameter reduction relative to full fine-tuning while matching or exceeding pre-trained model accuracy, combined with O(1) initialization across variable-width models, offers a practically compelling alternative for deploying models under diverse hardware constraints without maintaining a pre-trained checkpoint per configuration. However, the approach is currently limited to CNNs (no Transformer evaluation), the evolutionary process itself requires substantial upfront compute (250 generations × 20 networks for CIFAR-scale), and it remains unclear whether the 'core knowledge' identified is truly task-agnostic or merely dataset-agnostic. Open questions include extension to attention-based architectures, whether the evolutionary process can be accelerated or parallelized further, and whether the identified kernel circuits correspond to interpretable feature detectors.
