## Problem

While PCA is a cornerstone of dimensionality reduction, no prior work provided an asymptotically tight characterization of its excess risk for general (non-Gaussian) data distributions. Existing approaches—matrix perturbation bounds via Davis-Kahan and uniform convergence arguments—yield loose guarantees, and the only tight asymptotic results were restricted to Gaussian data. This paper asks precisely which property of the data distribution governs PCA's excess risk and answers it with a central limit theorem for the principal subspace error.

## Input / Output

The method takes n i.i.d. data points in R^d and a target dimension k, and outputs the k-dimensional principal subspace [U_n] on the Grassmannian Gr(d,k) along with a high-probability upper bound on its excess reconstruction risk. The analysis requires only that certain fourth-order moments Λ_ijst (products of projections onto top-k and bottom-(d−k) eigenvectors) be finite, and assumes an eigengap λ_k > λ_{k+1}. The excess risk bound is expressed in terms of the 1−δ quantile, making the probabilistic guarantee interpretable as a distributional statement rather than a loose tail bound.

## Method

The paper treats PCA as an M-estimator on the Grassmannian Gr(d,k) and applies tools from asymptotic statistics (van der Vaart) and non-asymptotic M-estimation theory (Ostrovskii & Bach). The central technical contribution is proving that the negative block Rayleigh quotient is generalized self-concordant along geodesics emanating from its minimizer within a maximum principal angle of π/4 (Proposition 1), analogous to Bach's self-concordance result for logistic regression. This self-concordance yields a sharp second-order Taylor approximation of the risk, which drives both the asymptotic CLT for the subspace error (Theorem 1) and a non-asymptotic excess risk bound (Theorem 2) that recovers the asymptotic characterization in the large-sample limit. The Riemannian geometric machinery—tangent spaces via the lift map, geodesics via SVD, exponential/logarithmic maps, and parallel transport—allows the analysis to respect the nonlinear structure of the Grassmannian rather than working in ambient Euclidean space.

## Results

The paper establishes three main theoretical results. Theorem 1 provides a central limit theorem for the PCA principal subspace error and shows the excess risk satisfies n·[R([U_n])−R([U_*])] → (1/2)‖H‖_F² in distribution, where H is a Gaussian matrix with covariances Λ_ijst/√(δ_ij·δ_st). Corollary 1 gives matching upper and lower bounds (with constants C=1, c=1/64) on the 1−δ quantile of the excess risk, identifying the key distributional quantity as Σ_{i,j} E[⟨u_{k+i},X⟩²⟨u_j,X⟩²]/(λ_j−λ_{k+i}). Theorem 2 provides a non-asymptotic upper bound of (75/(n·δ))·Σ_{i,j} E[⟨u_{k+i},X⟩²⟨u_j,X⟩²]/(λ_j−λ_{k+i}) that recovers the asymptotic characterization in the large-sample limit, under a sample size condition involving fourth moments and the eigengap.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper positions its contributions relative to several existing theoretical results rather than empirical baselines. Compared to Reiss and Wahl (2020), who derived non-asymptotic excess risk bounds under Gaussian data, this paper extends the analysis to arbitrary distributions with finite fourth moments and identifies the correct distributional dependence (the sum in equation 22) that their bounds do not capture. Relative to Dauxois et al. (1982), who established asymptotic normality of Euclidean fluctuations of empirical projectors, Theorem 1 provides a Riemannian analogue on the Grassmannian with a more natural geometric interpretation via principal angles. The Davis-Kahan theorem [DK70; YWS15] is used as a tool for the consistency step but the paper's contribution goes well beyond it by providing the full asymptotic distribution and a non-asymptotic bound. The self-concordance result (Proposition 1) generalizes Bach's (2010) analysis of logistic regression to the block Rayleigh quotient on the Grassmannian, with the key technical novelty being the restriction to geodesics of maximum rotation less than π/4 from the minimizer.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the two dominant approaches to PCA analysis—matrix perturbation bounds (Davis-Kahan) and uniform convergence/ERM arguments—by recasting PCA as an M-estimator on the Grassmannian manifold and applying tools from asymptotic statistics. The most closely related work is Reiss and Wahl (2020), which analyzes the PCA projector directly and recovers the leading asymptotic term under Gaussian data; this paper extends that to general distributions with finite fourth moments and provides a matching non-asymptotic bound. The key technical novelty is Proposition 1, which proves generalized self-concordance of the block Rayleigh quotient along geodesics from its minimizer, drawing an explicit analogy to Bach's (2010) self-concordance analysis for logistic regression and building on the finite-sample M-estimator framework of Ostrovskii and Bach (2021).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a single, interpretable distributional quantity (a sum of fourth-moment terms divided by eigengaps) that governs PCA's excess risk in the large-sample limit, answering a question that had no precise answer outside the Gaussian case. The self-concordance result for the block Rayleigh quotient is a new geometric property that may find independent use in optimization on the Grassmannian. Key limitations include the eigengap assumption (which prevents handling degenerate eigenvalues), a likely-loose global sample-size bound, and inapplicability to Kernel PCA or functional PCA. Open directions include relaxing the eigengap condition, tightening the global analysis, and extending the framework to end-to-end guarantees for downstream tasks that use PCA as a preprocessing step.
