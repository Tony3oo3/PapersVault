## Problem

State-of-the-art deep learning weather forecasters (PanguWeather, GraphCast, Stormer) rely on autoregressive rollouts at short time intervals, causing rapid error accumulation that degrades skill beyond the medium range (~2 weeks). Extending to subseasonal-to-seasonal (S2S) horizons (2–6 weeks) is further hampered because multi-step finetuning is computationally prohibitive and short-interval training captures only initial-condition dynamics while ignoring the boundary conditions that dominate S2S predictability.

## Input / Output

The model takes a single initial weather state comprising 69 physical variables (2 m temperature, 10 m U/V wind, MSLP, plus geopotential, temperature, U/V wind, and specific humidity at 13 pressure levels) on a global grid—128×256 at 1.40625° for S2S or 721×1440 at 0.25° for medium range. It outputs a full sequence of 44 future weather states at 24-hour intervals (1–44 days) as a 50-member probabilistic ensemble, where each member is an independently sampled complete sequence. The VAE compresses each frame to a 1024×8×16 continuous latent map (16× spatial reduction), so the transformer operates on 5,760 tokens per training example (45 frames × 8×16 spatial tokens).

## Method

OmniCast combines a continuous per-frame VAE (UNet-based, 1024-dim latent) with a masked generative transformer (MAE encoder-decoder, 16 layers, 1024 hidden dim) that models the joint distribution of all future latent tokens given the initial-condition tokens. During training, a random subset of future tokens is replaced with learnable [MASK] tokens, and the transformer is trained to recover them via a per-token diffusion head (6 residual MLP blocks with AdaLN conditioning) plus an auxiliary exponentially-weighted MSE loss applied only to the first 10 frames to anchor near-term accuracy. At inference, generation proceeds by iterative unmasking: starting from a fully masked sequence, the model reveals a random subset of tokens per iteration (44 iterations for 44 frames), sampling each revealed token through 100-step diffusion with temperature τ=1.3. This joint space-time generation avoids autoregressive error compounding, and training on the full 44-frame sequence allows the model to learn both initial-condition and boundary-condition dynamics simultaneously.

## Results

On ChaosBench (S2S, 1–44 days), OmniCast achieves state-of-the-art performance across deterministic (RMSE, SSIM), physics-based (SDIV, SRES), and probabilistic (CRPS, SSR) metrics, matching ECMWF-ENS as a top-2 method beyond day 10 and outperforming it on CRPS beyond day 15. OmniCast exhibits the lowest absolute bias among all baselines across T850, Z500, and Q700. On WeatherBench2 (medium-range, 12-hour steps), OmniCast performs comparably to IFS-ENS and is only slightly behind Gencast. Inference is 10×–20× faster than Gencast (29 s vs. 480 s at 0.25°; 11 s vs. 224 s at 1.0° on A100).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the two leading open-source deep learning methods, PanguWeather and GraphCast, OmniCast is slightly worse at short lead times (≤10 days) on RMSE and SSIM but surpasses both beyond day 10, with the gap widening at longer S2S horizons where autoregressive error accumulation degrades their performance. Among the four numerical ensemble systems (UKMO-ENS, NCEP-ENS, CMA-ENS, ECMWF-ENS), OmniCast outperforms the three non-ECMWF ensembles across all metrics and lead times, while matching ECMWF-ENS at shorter lead times and exceeding it on CRPS beyond day 15. On physics-based metrics (SDIV, SRES), OmniCast substantially outperforms all deep learning baselines and often beats all numerical ensembles as well. In the medium-range setting, OmniCast is slightly behind Gencast on CRPS and ensemble RMSE but comparable to IFS-ENS, while being 10×–20× faster at inference. ClimaX achieves comparable S2S accuracy but requires separate fine-tuned models per lead time, whereas a single OmniCast model generates the full 44-day sequence. Stormer, another autoregressive transformer, degrades more rapidly than OmniCast at longer lead times, eventually falling below climatology.

## Relation to Prior Work

OmniCast combines three established threads: masked generative modeling (MaskGIT, MAE) for iterative token unmasking, diffusion-based continuous distribution modeling (Li et al. 2024) to avoid vector quantization, and VAE latent compression for weather data. It departs from the dominant autoregressive paradigm in deep learning weather forecasting (PanguWeather, GraphCast, Gencast) by jointly generating the full future sequence rather than iteratively feeding predictions back as inputs, and from prior S2S attempts (Fuxi-S2S) by operating on instantaneous states rather than daily averages. The per-token diffusion head atop a transformer backbone, conditioned via AdaLN, is the key architectural novelty that enables continuous latent token generation within the masked framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

S2S forecasting (2–6 weeks) is a critical gap between operational medium-range NWP and seasonal climate prediction, with direct implications for drought, flood, and heatwave preparedness. OmniCast's ability to unify medium-range and S2S in a single model while being 10–20× faster than Gencast makes it practically deployable for operational ensemble systems. Open questions include the fundamental trade-off between VAE reconstruction fidelity and transformer modeling capacity, whether more sophisticated generative objectives (beyond per-token diffusion) could further improve long-horizon skill, and whether the approach generalizes to higher resolutions or additional physical variables without prohibitive compute.
