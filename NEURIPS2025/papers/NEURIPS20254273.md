## Problem

Continuous attractor networks, such as the Drosophila head direction circuit, are theoretically known to require finely tuned wiring symmetries to support stable bump dynamics and smooth velocity integration, yet connectomic reconstructions reveal asymmetries, heterogeneities, and missing biophysical parameters (synaptic gains, thresholds, time constants). Previous models of this circuit imposed hand-designed circular symmetry and prior knowledge of cell-type roles, limiting data-driven discovery. The paper asks whether functional ring attractor dynamics can be recovered directly from a noisy, asymmetric connectome without activity recordings, task labels, or architectural simplification.

## Input / Output

The method takes the raw signed synapse-count connectivity matrix from the Drosophila FlyEM hemibrain connectome (439 neurons across 6 cell types: E-PG, Delta7, P-EG, P-EN, GLNO, and ring neurons) as its sole structural input, with angular velocity signals injected into left or right GLNO neurons during simulation. It outputs a set of 57 cell-type-level parameters (7×7 gain matrix Z^AB plus 7 bias values b^A) that, combined with the fixed connectome skeleton, yield a rate-based network producing a continuous attractor bump whose population-decoded heading integrates velocity inputs linearly. The parameterization reduces the optimization space from ~193,000 per-synapse parameters to 57, and the training requires no neural activity recordings or behavioral labels.

## Method

The model uses rate-based neuron dynamics with sigmoid nonlinearity, where synaptic weights are factorized as W_ij = w_0(1+Z^AB)·sgn_ij·C_ij, fixing the connectome's synapse counts and neurotransmitter-inferred signs while learning only cell-type-shared gain offsets Z^AB and biases b^A. Training is fully self-supervised: a linear consistency loss enforces that the decoded heading velocity δθ/|u| is constant across all input magnitudes (without specifying the constant), complemented by stability (zero drift at u=0), minimum speed (anti-trivial-solution), entropy (diverse activation), and L1/L2 regularization terms that penalize deviation from the original connectome. The key insight is that cell-type-level parameterization is both sufficient and necessary—tying parameters globally across all cell types fails to produce integration, while per-synapse tuning is unnecessary. Dynamics are simulated with Dormand-Prince (Dopri5) integration in JAX/Diffrax, and the trained network is validated via PCA, local intrinsic dimensionality, correlation dimension, and persistent homology analyses confirming a 1D ring attractor manifold.

## Results

The connectome-constrained model recovers continuous ring attractor dynamics using only 57 cell-type-level parameters (vs. 193,161 for full per-synapse parameterization), producing an activity bump with width ≈ π/2 matching experimental reports (Kim et al., 2017). The network exhibits near-zero drift under multiplicative noise at or below 1% of neuron activity, and PCA/local PCA/correlation dimension analyses confirm the internal dynamics are constrained to a 1D manifold (intrinsic dimensionality ≈ 1, correlation dimension ≈ 1). Persistent homology yields a persistence diagram consistent with a 1D topological ring. Velocity integration is linear across a biologically plausible input range with minimal pinning region, and the model reproduces bidirectional bump motion driven by asymmetric left/right P-EN inputs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is between different levels of parameterization on the same connectome. A single global gain factor fails to produce any activity bump, and two global gains (excitatory/inhibitory) produce a bump but no velocity integration. Global parameter tying across all cell types also fails to recover attractor behavior. Only cell-type-specific parameterization (57 parameters) successfully recovers both bump formation and bidirectional velocity integration, establishing it as the minimal sufficient level of abstraction. Compared to prior connectome-based models (Chang et al., 2023; Stentiford et al., 2024), which rely on hand-designed circuit architectures with imposed circular symmetry and prespecified physiological parameters, this work starts from the raw measured connectivity matrix without architectural simplification or activity recordings. Unlike Lappalainen et al. (2024), which assumes an idealized columnar architecture and uses supervised learning on an optic flow task, this method uses self-supervised training directly on the noisy, asymmetric connectome. The connectome perturbation experiments (Figure 4a) further show that small Gaussian noise preserving connection signs still yields integrating solutions, while sign flips or weight shuffling consistently destroy functionality, confirming the connectome provides a meaningful structural prior.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from prior connectome-constrained models of the Drosophila HD circuit (Chang et al., 2023; Stentiford et al., 2024), which imposed hand-designed circular symmetry and prespecified physiological parameters, by starting directly from the raw, noisy FlyEM connectivity matrix. It also differs from Lappalainen et al. (2024), who constrained a network by the fly visual connectome but assumed an idealized columnar architecture and used supervised learning on an optic flow task, and from Schaeffer et al. (2023), who used a self-supervised isometry loss but on randomly initialized networks. The key conceptual move is combining self-supervised linear-consistency training with cell-type-level parameterization (57 parameters vs. ~193K for per-synapse), requiring no activity recordings, task labels, or architectural simplification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that a noisy, asymmetric connectome—far from the symmetric regularity of idealized ring attractor theory—serves as a near-sufficient structural prior for recovering continuous attractor dynamics (the 'lottery ticket' framing) reframes how much information connectomes actually encode about circuit function. The ablation findings (Delta7 critical for bump existence, P-EG for noise robustness, P-ENb more important than P-ENa) provide testable predictions for the field. Limitations include the assumption of input equivariance and population decoding, restricting direct application to circuits integrating continuous variables; the authors note the framework could extend to grid cells, oculomotor integrators, and evidence accumulation circuits with modified loss terms. Open questions include generalization beyond a single circuit/organism, multi-modal sensory integration, and experimental validation via closed-loop perturbation.
