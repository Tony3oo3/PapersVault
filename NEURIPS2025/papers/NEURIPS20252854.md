## Problem

Prior logarithmic-regret methods for online inverse linear optimization (O(n ln T)) maintain regions defined by O(T) constraints, causing per-round complexity to grow polynomially in T, while the efficient OGD-based approach has T-independent per-round cost but only O(√T) regret. This paper asks whether one can simultaneously achieve the best-known O(n ln T) regret bound and per-round complexity independent of T, and further how to handle the realistic case where the agent's actions are suboptimal.

## Input / Output

Each round t, the learner receives a feasible action set X_t ⊆ R^n (not necessarily convex, only an oracle for argmax over X_t is required) and the agent's action x_t ∈ X_t. The learner outputs a prediction c_hat_t from a closed convex set Θ containing the true objective c*, then computes the induced action x_hat_t = argmax_{x∈X_t} ⟨c_hat_t, x⟩. Performance is measured by the cumulative regret R_T = Σ_t ⟨c*, x_t − x_hat_t⟩, and in the suboptimal-feedback setting the bound additionally depends on the cumulative suboptimality Δ_T = Σ_t ℓ_t(c*).

## Method

The core method applies the Online Newton Step (ONS) to exp-concave loss functions ℓ_t^η(c) = −η⟨c_hat_t − c, x_hat_t − x_t⟩ + η²⟨c_hat_t − c, x_hat_t − x_t⟩² with η = 1/(5B), yielding O(n ln T) regret at per-round cost O(τ_solve + n² + τ_G-proj) independent of T. For suboptimal feedback, MetaGrad is applied to the suboptimality loss ℓ_t(c) = max_{x∈X_t}⟨c,x⟩ − ⟨c,x_t⟩, running ONS with Θ(ln T) learning rates in parallel via exponentially weighted averaging; a self-bounding argument connecting V_T^{c*} ≤ B·R̃_T^{c*} + 2B·Δ_T then yields the O(n ln T + √(Δ_T·n ln T)) regret bound without prior knowledge of Δ_T. A matching Ω(n) lower bound is established via a randomized construction where each X_t is a line segment on a distinct coordinate axis, showing the n-dependence is tight up to a ln T factor.

## Results

The paper's main theoretical contribution is an O(n ln T) regret bound (Theorem 3.1) for online inverse linear optimization with per-round complexity O(τ_solve + τ_G-proj + n²), which is independent of T — the first logarithmic-regret method to achieve this. For suboptimal agent feedback, the regret bound degrades gracefully to O(n ln T + √(Δ_T · n ln T)) (Theorem 4.1), where Δ_T is the cumulative suboptimality. A matching Ω(n) lower bound (Theorem 5.1) confirms the O(n ln T) upper bound is tight up to an O(ln T) factor. In the offline setting, the online-to-batch conversion yields a convergence rate of O((n ln T)/T) for the average prediction under zero suboptimality, improving upon the O(1/√T) rate of Bärmann et al.

**Table 1: Comparison of regret bounds and computational complexity under optimal feedback.**

| Method | Regret Bound | Per-Round Complexity | Total Complexity |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bärmann et al. [4,5] | O(√T) | O(τ_solve + τ_E-proj + n) | Per-round × T |
| Besbes et al. [7,8] | O(n⁴ ln T) | Not claimed | poly(n, T) |
| Gollapudi et al. [25] | O(n ln T) | Not claimed | poly(n, T) |
| **This work (Sec. 3)** | **O(n ln T)** | **O(τ_solve + τ_G-proj + n²)** | **Per-round × T** |

Here τ_solve is the time to solve the forward linear optimization, τ_E-proj is Euclidean projection time (typically O(n)), and τ_G-proj is generalized projection time (typically O(n³)).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Bärmann et al. [4,5] (OGD/MWU-based, O(√T) regret), this paper strictly improves the regret dependence on T from √T to ln T at the cost of a factor of n and a slightly more expensive per-round projection (generalized vs. Euclidean). Against Besbes et al. [7,8] (region-narrowing, O(n⁴ ln T) regret), this paper improves the regret by a factor of n³ and, crucially, reduces per-round complexity from poly(n,T) to T-independent O(τ_solve + τ_G-proj + n²). Against Gollapudi et al. [25] (cutting-plane/centroid, O(n ln T) regret), this paper matches the regret bound exactly but eliminates the T-dependence in per-round computation, which the authors estimate as O(τ_solve + n⁵T³) or higher for Gollapudi et al.'s method. The Ω(n) lower bound (Theorem 5.1) further establishes that the n-factor in the regret is unavoidable, resolving an open question from Besbes et al. [8, Section 7].

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of online inverse linear optimization and online convex optimization. It builds directly on the ONS algorithm (Hazan et al. 2007) and MetaGrad (van Erven et al. 2021), repurposing them for a problem where prior logarithmic-regret methods (Besbes et al. 2021; Gollapudi et al. 2021) relied on maintaining O(T) geometric constraints per round. The key conceptual departure is showing that a simple application of ONS to exp-concave loss functions—essentially the same loss form used internally by MetaGrad's η-experts—recovers the O(n ln T) regret bound with T-independent per-round complexity, bypassing the cutting-plane machinery entirely. For the suboptimality-robust variant, the paper introduces the self-bounding technique combined with MetaGrad's universal adaptivity to the suboptimality loss, a combination not previously exploited in this setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper resolves a practical inefficiency that had persisted since the first logarithmic-regret results: prior O(n ln T) methods required per-round computation growing polynomially in T, making them infeasible for long horizons, while the efficient OGD-based method of Bärmann et al. only achieved O(√T). By showing ONS suffices, the paper makes the best-known regret bound practically deployable. The Ω(n) lower bound (resolving an open question from Besbes et al.) clarifies that the n-dependence is unavoidable, though the O(ln T) gap between upper and lower bounds remains open. Limitations include restriction to linear forward optimization, the need for a bounded-diameter assumption on the feasible sets, and the fact that the MetaGrad variant incurs a Θ(ln T) multiplicative overhead in per-round projections.
