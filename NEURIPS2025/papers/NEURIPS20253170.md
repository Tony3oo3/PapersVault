## Problem

While rule-based RL (e.g., DeepSeek-R1, GRPO) has revolutionized LLM post-training for reasoning, its application to pure visual perception tasks in MLLMs remains largely unexplored. Prior RL-for-MLLM work focuses on language-centric reasoning (ambiguous reference resolution, geometric problem-solving), overlooking the fundamental question of whether and how RL can unlock perceptual capabilities. This paper investigates the essential role of RL in visual perception, revealing that its effectiveness is governed by perceptual perplexity and reward design rather than the explicit chain-of-thought reasoning that drives language-domain gains.

## Input / Output

The model consumes RGB images at dynamic resolution (handled via 2D-RoPE in Qwen2-VL) paired with a short text prompt specifying the task (e.g., 'Output the bounding box of the {question} in the image'). Outputs are strictly constrained to structured formats: bounding boxes as [x1,y1,x2,y2] tuples, point coordinates for counting, transcribed text for OCR, and class labels with coordinates for detection. For multi-object tasks (counting, detection), the model emits N predicted attributes that must be matched to M ground-truth references before reward computation, making the output a set rather than a single sequence.

## Method

Perception-R1 applies GRPO (Group Relative Policy Optimization) during MLLM post-training, sampling G=8 rollouts per prompt and optimizing with group-relative advantages and a KL penalty (β=0.04). The key technical contribution is a discriminative reward function anchored in visual geometry—IoU for bounding boxes, Euclidean distance for points, edit distance for OCR—replacing the implicit semantic verification used in language RL. For multi-subject tasks, a Hungarian-algorithm-based bipartite matching step aligns predictions to ground truths before computing the answer reward, which the ablations show is critical for exploration. Two empirical findings shape the design: explicit chain-of-thought during RL degrades perception performance (the model learns implicit visual patterns better without it), and RL's advantage over SFT scales with perceptual perplexity (the number of valid ground-truth matchings), being most pronounced for object detection (3.47×10⁸³ theoretical permutations) and minimal for single-target tasks like grounding and OCR.

## Results

Perception-R1 achieves 31.9 AP on COCO2017 val, the first pure MLLM to surpass the 30+ AP threshold, nearly doubling Qwen2.5-VL-3B (16.1 AP) and exceeding YOLOv3 (27.9 AP). On visual grounding, Perception-R1 (2B) improves valAvg over Qwen2-VL-2B by +2.2/+4.2/+2.9 on RefCOCO/+/g respectively (67.9/62.6/64.5 vs 65.7/58.4/61.6). On PixMo-Count, it achieves 78.1 val accuracy (+17.9% over Qwen2-VL's 60.2), and on PageOCR it reaches 98.2 F1 (en), surpassing the expert model GOT (97.2). On general understanding benchmarks, RL-trained Perception-R1 also shows gains on MMVet (48.9 vs 45.6) and LLaVA-Bench (58.2 vs 46.5).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Perception-R1 outperforms all MLLM baselines across every evaluated task. On COCO2017 detection, it nearly doubles Qwen2.5-VL-3B (31.9 vs 16.1 AP) and surpasses YOLOv3 (27.9 AP) while remaining below Faster R-CNN (35.6) and DETR (42.0). On visual grounding, Perception-R1 (2B) beats Qwen2-VL-2B by +2.2 to +4.2 valAvg across RefCOCO/+/g, and substantially outperforms LLaVA-1.5 (20.1/17.6/17.3), LLaVA-NeXT (43.4/39.3/40.0), and LLaVA-OV (32.6/30.0/31.6), approaching expert models MDETR and OFA. On PageOCR, Perception-R1 (98.2 F1 en) surpasses expert OCR models GOT (97.2) and DocOwl1.5 (86.2), and dramatically outperforms LLaVA-NeXT (64.7). On PixMo-Count, it leads all baselines by a wide margin (78.1 vs 60.2 for Qwen2-VL, 55.8 for LLaVA-OV, 33.3 for LLaVA-1.5). On general understanding, Perception-R1 matches or slightly exceeds Qwen2-VL on most benchmarks while showing notable gains on MMVet (+3.3) and LLaVA-Bench (+11.7), suggesting cross-task transfer from perception-specific RL training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the rule-based RL post-training paradigm established by DeepSeek-R1 and GRPO (DeepSeekMath) from the language domain to pure visual perception tasks in MLLMs. It departs from concurrent RL-for-MLLM efforts (e.g., Visual-RFT, MM-Eureka) that target language-centric visual reasoning, instead framing 'perception policy learning' as a distinct objective where the model must output precise spatial coordinates or transcriptions. The curriculum sampling strategy is borrowed from Kimi-1.5, and the Hungarian-algorithm-based multi-subject reward matching is a novel adaptation of bipartite assignment to the RL reward loop for multi-object perception.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result of a 2B/3B MLLM reaching 31.9% AP on COCO2017 val—surpassing YOLOv3 and approaching Faster R-CNN—demonstrates that general-purpose foundation models can compete with decades of specialist detection architectures, potentially reshaping how practitioners approach detection. The finding that explicit chain-of-thought reasoning degrades perception performance and that perceptual perplexity (combinatorial matching complexity) predicts RL's advantage over SFT provides a useful decision framework for when to invest in RL post-training. However, the authors acknowledge that current perception benchmarks are 'overly simplistic,' limiting RL's exploration space, and the work is validated only on the Qwen2-VL/2.5-VL family with small data subsets (5k–10k), leaving open whether these findings generalize across architectures and scale.
