## Problem

In offline-to-online RL, it is unclear how to leverage prior offline data to acquire an effective online exploratory policy, as naive approaches suffer from distributional shift and overly pessimistic value estimates that hinder exploration. Action chunking, a technique well-established in imitation learning for handling non-Markovian behavior, has not been applied to TD-based RL, leaving both the exploration challenge and the slow value propagation in long-horizon tasks unaddressed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251856/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The agent receives a robot state observation (e.g., end-effector position, gripper state) and outputs a chunk of h consecutive actions (typically h=5) that are executed open-loop before the next decision. The critic takes the current state concatenated with the full h-step action sequence and returns a scalar Q-value for executing that entire sequence. Action dimensions vary by domain: 5 for OGBench manipulation tasks (x, y, z, gripper yaw, gripper opening) and 7 for Robomimic tasks (3 DoF translation, 3 DoF rotation, 1 gripper).

## Method

Q-chunking runs RL in a temporally extended action space where the policy predicts h-step action sequences and the critic evaluates the value of executing the whole sequence, yielding two key benefits: (1) a behavior constraint on the chunked action space leverages temporally coherent actions from offline data for structured exploration, and (2) the standard TD backup on the chunked Q-function is provably equivalent to an unbiased n-step return (n=h), avoiding the off-policy bias of naive n-step methods. Two practical instantiations are provided: QC uses best-of-N sampling from a flow-matching behavior policy to implicitly enforce a KL constraint, while QC-FQL parameterizes the policy as a noise-conditioned action predictor regularized by a Wasserstein-2 distillation loss from the flow-matching policy (following FQL's TD3+BC objective). The flow-matching policy is essential for capturing the complex, non-Markovian behavior distribution in offline data, as Gaussian policies fail to produce effective chunked actions.

## Results

On the OGBench benchmark (25 long-horizon, sparse-reward manipulation tasks), QC achieves an overall offline-to-online success rate of 52→86%, substantially outperforming the best prior methods (BFN: 51→63%, RLPD: →67%, FQL: 37→58%). The gains are most dramatic on the hardest domains: on cube-triple, QC reaches 64% online success vs. 23% for BFN and 18% for FQL; on cube-quadruple, QC reaches 74% vs. 12% for BFN and 3% for FQL. On Robomimic (3 tasks), QC similarly achieves strong performance across all tasks, consistently outperforming both 1-step TD and n-step return baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

QC and QC-FQL outperform all prior offline-to-online methods on the overall OGBench score (86% vs. 67% for RLPD and 63% for BFN), with the largest margins on the hardest tasks: cube-triple (64% vs. 23% for BFN, 18% for FQL) and cube-quadruple (74% vs. 12% for BFN, 3% for FQL). Against FQL [58] (the closest 1-step TD baseline), QC-FQL improves overall success from 58% to 86% and cube-quadruple from 3% to 77%. Against RLPD [7] (online RL from scratch with offline data), QC matches or exceeds performance on easier tasks but dominates on hard ones (cube-triple: 64% vs. 41%; cube-quadruple: 74% vs. 0%). Against SUPE-GT [85] (skill-based offline-to-online), QC achieves 86% vs. 52% overall, with particularly large gaps on cube-triple (64% vs. 0%) and cube-quadruple (74% vs. 0%). Notably, naive n-step return baselines (FQL-n, BFN-n, IFQL-n) perform worse than their 1-step counterparts on hard tasks, confirming that the benefit comes from the chunked critic/actor design rather than simply longer value horizons.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper transplants action chunking—a technique well-established in imitation learning (Zhao et al. [90])—into TD-based reinforcement learning, operating both the policy and critic in a temporally extended action space. It builds directly on FQL (Park et al. [58]) for its flow-matching policy parameterization and distillation loss, and on the expected-max Q operator (Ghasemipour et al. [24]) for best-of-N action selection. The key departure from prior work is the observation that a chunked Q-function eliminates the off-policy bias inherent in naive n-step return backups (formalized in Theorem A.1), and that behavior constraints in the chunked space naturally extract temporally coherent skills without the bi-level optimization instability of hierarchical RL.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Q-chunking provides a simple, general recipe that can be grafted onto existing offline-to-online RL algorithms (demonstrated with FQL and IFQL) to substantially improve sample efficiency on long-horizon, sparse-reward manipulation tasks. The practical finding that Gaussian policies fail with action chunking and that flow-matching policies are necessary is an important implementation insight for the community. Open questions include how to automatically select chunk length (currently a task-specific hyperparameter), whether the approach extends to settings requiring high-frequency reactive control, and whether more general non-Markovian policy representations beyond fixed-length chunks could further improve exploration.
