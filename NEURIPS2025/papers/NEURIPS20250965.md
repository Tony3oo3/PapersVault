## Problem

Existing high-speed scene reconstruction methods rely on monocular spike cameras with limited spatial coverage or on RGB cameras that suffer motion blur at high speeds. While 4D Gaussian Splatting enables real-time dynamic rendering, no prior work combines multi-view spike camera arrays with 4DGS to reconstruct high-speed dynamic scenes, leaving a gap in both spatial coverage and temporal fidelity for fast-moving objects.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250965/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes continuous asynchronous spike streams (250×400 spatial, 20 kHz temporal resolution) from N−1 synchronized spike cameras over ~0.5-second intervals, and produces a novel-view rendered image at an arbitrary time t from the N-th camera position. The pipeline also outputs a dense 3D point cloud with confidence maps and per-camera extrinsics ([R|T]) as intermediate products. Rendered images are grayscale due to the absence of RGB channels in spike data, and the system operates at 40 FPS on a single GPU.

## Method

Spike4DGS comprises two key modules. First, Multi-view Spike-based Dense Initialization (MSDI) is an end-to-end network that replaces the conventional two-step spike-to-image + COLMAP/DUSt3R pipeline: a shift-based 3×3 convolution (from SpikeGS) with 4-direction rotation extracts spike features, N shared-weight ViT encoders process each view, a 24-layer fusion transformer (from Fast3R) generates global temporal features, and a DPT head decodes point clouds and confidence maps, with RANSAC-PnP recovering camera poses and a BCONV head generating supervision images. Second, Spike-Pixel Synergy Supervision (SPSS) augments the standard 4DGS pixel L1 loss with a spike variation loss that converts rendered images into predicted spike streams via an Integrate-and-Fire neuron, uses a one-layer MLP to expand a single-frame spike into a temporal spike sequence, and penalizes mismatches in both the mean and standard deviation of predicted versus ground-truth spike sequences—capturing dynamic motion patterns that pixel-level loss alone misses. The full system is fine-tuned from pre-trained DUSt3R weights on CARLA-simulated multi-view spike streams and converges in ~20 minutes on a single RTX 4090.

## Results

On the synthetic outdoor dataset (5 scenes: Jaywalk, Bicycle, Motor, Car, Van), Spike4DGS achieves an average PSNR of 27.75 dB, SSIM of 91.5%, and LPIPS of 0.193, outperforming the best two-stage baseline (TFP+4DGS) by ~1.4 dB in PSNR and ~2.8% in SSIM. The improvement is most pronounced on high-speed scenes like Car (27.74 vs. 25.01 PSNR) and Van (27.13 vs. 25.27 PSNR). On the real-world object dataset (no ground truth), Spike4DGS achieves average BRISQUE of 29.6, NIQE of 9.05, and MetaIQA of 0.179, representing 15.4%, 4.6%, and 6.7% improvements over the best competing method respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Spike4DGS outperforms all 8 baselines across every scene and metric on both datasets. Among the two-stage spike-to-image + dynamic NVS pipelines, TFP+4DGS is the strongest baseline (avg PSNR 26.39), but Spike4DGS still leads by ~1.4 dB in PSNR and ~2.8% in SSIM, with the gap widening on faster scenes (e.g., Car: 27.74 vs. 25.01 PSNR). The direct spike-based methods Dy-SpikeGS (avg PSNR 22.84) and Dy-SpikeNeRF (avg PSNR 16.04) trail significantly, confirming that end-to-end multi-view spike processing with synergy supervision is substantially more effective than monocular spike pipelines with manually added deformation networks. On the real-world dataset, Spike4DGS achieves the lowest BRISQUE (29.6 avg) and NIQE (9.05 avg) while attaining the highest MetaIQA (0.179 avg), surpassing the best baseline (TFI+4DGS at BRISQUE 35.9, NIQE 10.2, MetaIQA 0.165) by clear margins. The ablation further confirms that the end-to-end MSDI initialization alone (without SPSS) already beats TFI+DUSt3R by ~1 dB, and adding SPSS provides an additional ~1 dB gain.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two previously separate research threads: spike-camera-based 3D reconstruction (SpikeNeRF, SpikeGS) and dynamic novel view synthesis via Gaussian Splatting (4DGS, D3DGS, STG). Prior spike-based methods were limited to static scenes or monocular setups, while dynamic NVS methods relied on RGB cameras whose frame rates cause motion blur in high-speed scenarios. Spike4DGS bridges this gap by adapting the DUSt3R/Fast3R-style dense initialization architecture (ViT encoders + fusion transformer + DPT head) into an end-to-end module (MSDI) that processes raw multi-view spike streams directly, bypassing the error-prone two-stage spike-to-image + SfM pipeline. The Spike-Pixel Synergy Supervision loss further departs from prior work by converting rendered images back into spike streams via Integrate-and-Fire neurons and supervising temporal variation statistics, a constraint absent from both standard 4DGS pixel losses and SpikeNeRF's spike losses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By introducing the first multi-view spike-camera-array framework for high-speed dynamic NVS along with two purpose-built datasets (real-world and CARLA synthetic), this work opens a new experimental venue for the neuromorphic-vision community and provides a baseline that future spike-based dynamic reconstruction methods can target. However, two significant limitations temper its immediate impact: rendered outputs are grayscale (spike cameras capture only intensity, not color), and the 9-camera array is a fixed, non-portable rig, restricting deployment to controlled laboratory settings. Open questions include whether color can be recovered by fusing spike streams with a low-frame-rate RGB sensor, whether the approach generalizes to portable or single-spike-camera dynamic scenes, and whether the IF-neuron-based spike variation loss can be replaced by differentiable spike simulation for tighter end-to-end optimization.
