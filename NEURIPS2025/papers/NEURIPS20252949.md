## Problem

Existing Spatio-Temporal Foundation Models (STFMs) like UniST and OpenCity rely on expensive joint spatial-temporal pretraining that scales quadratically with graph size and sequence length, while struggling to generalize across domains because spatial correlations (e.g., road topology vs. power grid vs. climate teleconnections) are highly domain-specific. This paper asks whether the universal, 1-D temporal structure shared across domains can be learned once in a graph-agnostic manner, with domain-specific spatial reasoning injected only at adaptation time.

## Input / Output

During pretraining (UTP), the model consumes node-wise univariate sequences of length L independently, deliberately discarding all spatial structure to keep the backbone graph-agnostic. During adaptation (STA), the full ST tensor X ∈ R^{N×L×D} is processed alongside a spatial adjacency matrix, node metadata (geo-coordinates, land-use), and calendar embeddings (minute-of-hour through month-of-year, selected by sampling frequency). The output is a multi-step forecast Y ∈ R^{N×F×D} for both short-term (12→12) and long-term (96→96) horizons, with the STA adapter contributing only ~1.3M parameters on top of the frozen backbone.

## Method

FactoST factorizes STFM learning into two stages. Stage 1 (UTP) pretrains a spatially-agnostic Transformer encoder-decoder on 13M cross-domain time points using three mechanisms: FFT-based multi-frequency augmentation that stochastically isolates low- or high-frequency bands to create complementary spectral views, a learnable codebook of domain prompts selected via negative Euclidean distance, and a multi-task objective combining spectral-consistency reconstruction with a detached supervised forecasting loss. Stage 2 (STA) freezes the backbone and attaches a compact adapter with four modules: Spatio-Temporal Metadata Fusion (residual addition of node and calendar embeddings), Spatio-Temporal Filtering (three low-rank affinities—spatial, temporal, and time-lagged—projected into R^r and fused via softmax to yield per-token modulation weights), Hierarchical Domain Alignment (k-nearest prompt retrieval at layer level plus a low-rank rank-1 adjustment at token level), and Continual Memory Replay (mixing 30% of each mini-batch from a 20% memory buffer to prevent catastrophic forgetting during few-shot fine-tuning).

## Results

In few-shot (10% data) short-term forecasting (12→12), FactoST achieves the best MAE on all 9/9 datasets, reducing MAE by 31.4% over OpenCity and 47.2% over UniST on average. In long-term forecasting (96→96), FactoST secures 1st place on all 12 MAE/RMSE entries, with average MAE reductions of 40.5% over STFMs and 44.1% over ST expert models. The model uses only 1.3M parameters (46.2% fewer than OpenCity's 1.67M) and achieves 8.1s inference latency (68% faster than OpenCity's 25.3s), while pretraining on just 13M time points versus 27B (Moirai) or 100B (TimesFM).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the two ST foundation models, FactoST dominates: it reduces MAE by 31.4% over OpenCity and 47.2% over UniST in few-shot short-term, with the gap widening to 40.5% average in long-term (where D2STGNN also fails with OOM errors). Versus time-series foundation models (TimesFM, Moirai), FactoST gains ~18.8% average MAE improvement, confirming that the lightweight ST adapter adds meaningful spatial awareness beyond purely temporal pretraining. Among ST expert models, FactoST is competitive but not uniformly dominant: D2STGNN edges out FactoST on PEMS-07 (25.51 vs 26.48 MAE) and PEMS-08 (19.55 vs 18.94), while FactoST wins decisively on PEMS-03, PEMS-04, and all non-traffic datasets. Against time-series experts (PatchTST, TimeMixer, DLinear, Informer), FactoST consistently wins on spatially correlated tasks (PEMS, METR-LA) but Moirai (a TSFM) surprisingly achieves the best MAE on ETTh2 (0.135 vs 0.272), a dataset with only 7 features where spatial structure is minimal. In zero-shot settings, FactoST (UTP-only, no STA) outperforms OpenCity and UniST on most unseen datasets, reinforcing the paper's core claim that spatial priors baked into pretraining hinder rather than help cross-domain transfer.

## Relation to Prior Work

FactoST revives the classical STGNN factorization (separate temporal and spatial modules) and transplants it into the foundation-model era, where prior STFMs like UniST and OpenCity instead perform costly joint spatio-temporal pretraining over N×T tokens. On the temporal side, it draws on multi-frequency augmentation (FRAug, TimeNet) and codebook-style prompting from vision, while on the adaptation side it borrows low-rank adapter and memory-replay ideas from parameter-efficient fine-tuning and continual learning. The key departure from both STGNNs (which are trained from scratch per dataset) and existing STFMs (which bake spatial structure into pretraining) is a two-stage pipeline: a graph-agnostic temporal backbone pretrained once, followed by a lightweight, pluggable spatial adapter that injects domain-specific graph and calendar context without retraining the backbone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The factorized design offers a practical, scalable recipe for building STFMs without the quadratic memory/compute blowup of joint ST pretraining, and the 46% parameter reduction with 68% faster inference makes it attractive for real-world deployment. The zero-shot finding that purely temporal models outperform joint-ST models challenges the field's assumption that spatial knowledge must be learned during pretraining, suggesting that spatial structure is too domain-specific to be usefully shared. Open questions remain: zero-shot long-horizon accuracy is still poor, the spatial adapter's generalization to unseen graph topologies is untested, and the paper acknowledges that uniform fine-tuning underutilizes pretrained knowledge—parameter-efficient strategies (prompt tuning, selective retraining) are flagged as future work.
