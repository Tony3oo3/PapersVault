## Problem

Existing pseudo-Riemannian graph embedding methods suffer from three key limitations: the diffeomorphic decomposition into spherical and Euclidean spaces discards hierarchical information essential to hyperbolic geometry, there is no principled guideline for selecting the time and space dimensions of the pseudo-hyperboloid, and GNN architectures are prone to oversmoothing at depth. Furthermore, Transformer-based graph encoders have not been extended to pseudo-Riemannian geometry because the logarithmic map and parallel transport are undefined for geodesic-disconnected point pairs, making core Transformer operations infeasible.

## Input / Output

The method takes initial Euclidean node feature matrices and graph adjacency structure as input, first projecting features onto a pseudo-hyperboloid Q_β^{p,q} whose dimensions (p, q) are determined by a lightweight space searching algorithm based on the graph's Gaussian sectional curvature distribution. The output is node embeddings residing on the pseudo-hyperboloid, which are then decoded into class labels for node classification or edge existence scores for link prediction. The model uses a 16-dimensional hidden embedding across all experiments and supports both small citation networks and large-scale graphs (up to the OGB Products and Vessels benchmarks).

## Method

The core contribution is a diffeomorphic framework that decomposes a pseudo-hyperboloid Q_β^{p,q} into the Cartesian product of a spherical manifold S_{|β|}^q and a hyperbolic (Lorentz) manifold L_β^p, via an extrinsic mapping Φ followed by a diffeomorphism Ψ. This decomposition preserves both cyclical (spherical) and hierarchical (hyperbolic) graph structures while avoiding geodesic disconnections, enabling principled definitions of linear attention, residual connections (via a new pseudo-hyperboloid vector addition), and layer normalization. The Q-GT architecture applies linear attention independently on the spherical and hyperbolic components (using a positive-definite kernel φ(x)=1+elu(x) for O(N) complexity), concatenates the results, and fuses them with a Q-GCN2 module that performs neighborhood aggregation as a centroid in the product space. A space searching algorithm constructs a bijection Γ from each pseudo-hyperboloid to an ideal Gaussian sectional curvature distribution and selects the manifold minimizing KL divergence from the input graph's empirical GSC distribution, replacing brute-force hyperparameter search.

## Results

On node classification (F1-score), Q-GT achieves the best result on 5 of 7 datasets, with the largest gain on Airport (95.70%, +2.55% over the second-best SGFormer at 93.32%) and Penn94 (82.77%, +5.51% over SGFormer at 78.45%). On link prediction (ROC AUC), Q-GT attains the highest score on all four datasets, reaching 96.87% on Cora, 97.59% on Citeseer, 96.56% on Airport, and 96.40% on Pubmed. On the large-scale OGB benchmarks, Q-GT outperforms the strongest baselines by 7.3% on Products (70.25% vs. 65.47% SGFormer) and 32.09% on Vessel (78.78% vs. 50.21% Q-GCN2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Q-GT outperforms all nine baselines on the majority of datasets. Against GNN-based methods, Q-GT surpasses GCN by up to 21.08% (Airport: 95.70 vs. 74.62), HGCN by up to 22.24% (Airport), κ-GCN by up to 25.10% (Airport), and the prior pseudo-Riemannian Q-GCN by up to 19.58% (Airport). Among Transformer baselines, Q-GT beats SGFormer (the strongest competitor) on 5 of 7 node classification datasets, with the largest margin on Penn94 (+5.51%) and Airport (+2.38%), while SGFormer edges ahead on Citeseer (71.93 vs. 70.40). Q-GT also outperforms Hypformer on all 7 datasets and NodeFormer on 6 of 7. Notably, Q-GT's linear attention avoids the OOM issues that plague GraphGPS and FPS-T on large graphs (Arxiv, Penn94, Twitch Gamers), while FPS-T's learned curvatures converge to near-zero values on Cora and Citeseer, failing to capture the underlying geometry. On link prediction, Q-GT achieves the top AUC on all four datasets, exceeding the best Transformer baseline (Hypformer) by 0.88% on Cora and 0.76% on Citeseer.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the pseudo-Riemannian graph embedding line initiated by Law and Stam (2020) and Xiong et al. (2022), but departs critically from Q-GCN's diffeomorphism that decomposes pseudo-hyperboloids into spherical × Euclidean spaces (losing hierarchical structure) by instead mapping to spherical × hyperbolic spaces, preserving both cyclical and hierarchical topologies. It also adapts the hyperbolic linear attention mechanism from Hypformer (Yang et al., 2024) to the pseudo-Riemannian setting, becoming the first Transformer-based graph model in this geometry. The geodesic tools (exponential/logarithmic maps, parallel transport) are inherited from Law and Stam (2020), and the overall framework generalizes the hyperbolic GCN approach of Chami et al. (2019).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides the first principled way to build Transformer operations (linear attention, residual connections, layer normalization) directly on pseudo-Riemannian manifolds, opening a path toward scalable graph encoders that capture mixed topologies without the oversmoothing of deep GNNs. The space searching algorithm based on GSC distributions offers a practical, lightweight alternative to brute-force manifold selection. Key limitations include restriction to single static graphs, higher computational cost than GNN baselines, and an unvalidated linear assumption in the curvature-to-dimension mapping. Open questions include extension to graph-level and dynamic graph settings, and whether the ideal GSC distribution follows a non-linear relationship with space dimension.
