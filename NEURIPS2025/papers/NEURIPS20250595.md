## Problem

Multi-view source separation (MVSS) requires disentangling unknown sources from noisy, incomplete observations across multiple views, but existing methods either demand strong prior assumptions about source structure, rely on contrastive datasets where at least one source is absent from a view, or lack the expressivity to handle complex source distributions. This paper shows that diffusion models can learn source priors directly from data without any explicit source model, succeeding even when every source is present in every observation and no source is ever individually measured.

## Input / Output

The method consumes noisy observations from N_views views, where each observation is a linear mixture of N_s sources with a known per-sample mixing matrix and known Gaussian noise covariance. Observations can be incomplete (non-invertible projections), vary in dimensionality across views, and have different resolutions. The output is a set of independent diffusion model priors—one per source—that support prior sampling, density evaluation, and joint posterior sampling given any new observation, yielding separated source signals at arbitrary resolution.

## Method

DDPRISM parameterizes each source prior as a score-based diffusion model and trains them jointly via a Monte Carlo EM framework. The E-step samples from the joint posterior over all sources simultaneously by running the reverse SDE with a combined score: the sum of individual diffusion model scores plus a joint likelihood score derived via the MMPS Gaussian approximation, which reduces to N_s analytic convolutions and scales linearly in the number of sources. The M-step then trains each denoiser independently on its corresponding posterior samples using the standard denoising score matching objective. The key technical contribution is the derivation of the joint likelihood score (Eq. 17), which couples all sources through a shared observation while preserving the independence structure of the priors. For the contrastive MVSS special case, a simplified sequential algorithm trains sources one at a time, reducing computational cost at the expense of discarding cross-view information.

## Results

On Grassy MNIST (full-resolution), DDPRISM-Joint achieves a posterior FID of 1.57 and PSNR of 25.60, substantially outperforming CLVM-VAE (FID 18.87, PSNR 14.56), CLVM-Linear (FID 101.3, PSNR 13.30), and PCPCA (FID 22.3, PSNR 18.99). On the downsampled variant, the advantage is even more dramatic: DDPRISM-Joint reaches FID 2.36 while CLVM-VAE degrades to FID 1008.0 and PCPCA to 121.7. On the 1D manifold (2-source) problem, DDPRISM-Joint achieves PSNR 38.27 and Sinkhorn divergence 0.35, compared to CLVM-VAE's 17.15 and 1.81. The method also outperforms its own Gibbs-sampling variant (DDPRISM-Gibbs) across all settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DDPRISM-Joint outperforms all three external baselines (PCPCA, CLVM-Linear, CLVM-VAE) on every metric across every experiment. The most striking gap appears on the downsampled Grassy MNIST: DDPRISM-Joint achieves FID 2.36 while CLVM-VAE collapses to FID 1008.0 and PCPCA to 121.7, indicating that the contrastive baselines cannot handle mixed-resolution observations. On the full-resolution Grassy MNIST, DDPRISM-Joint's FID of 1.57 is roughly 12× better than CLVM-VAE (18.87) and 65× better than CLVM-Linear (101.3). On the 1D manifold problems, DDPRISM-Joint more than doubles the PSNR of the best baseline (38.27 vs. 17.15 for CLVM-VAE on 2 sources) and reduces Sinkhorn divergence by 5×. The paper's own Gibbs-sampling variant (DDPRISM-Gibbs) is consistently inferior to the joint sampling approach, with PSNR roughly 2–3× lower on the 1D manifold, confirming that joint posterior sampling is critical for multi-source problems.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the contrastive multi-view source separation (MVSS) literature (CLVM, PCPCA, CVAE), which assumes one view contains only background and another contains background plus target, to the fully general case where any source can appear in any view with arbitrary mixing. Methodologically, it builds on Rozet et al.'s (2024) demonstration that diffusion model priors can be learned from noisy observations via EM, and on the broader diffusion-for-inverse-problems literature (SNIPS, DDRM, DPS), but introduces a novel joint posterior sampling formulation over multiple independent source diffusion models using the MMPS approximation. The key departure from prior MVSS work is the removal of the contrastive assumption and the handling of incomplete, multi-resolution data with per-sample mixing matrices.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DDPRISM provides the first unified, assumption-light framework for general linear MVSS, demonstrated on a real astrophysical dataset (HST/COSMOS) where it produces 79k pristine galaxy images — a practically valuable resource for the astronomy community. Key limitations constrain broader adoption: the method requires known mixing matrices, assumes linear mixing and Gaussian noise, and is computationally expensive (34 GPU-hours on 4×H100 for the galaxy experiment), which limits scalability to higher resolutions or more sources. Open questions include extending to nonlinear mixing (e.g., occlusion), probabilistic mixing matrices, and reducing the computational cost of the EM loop with better initialization strategies.
