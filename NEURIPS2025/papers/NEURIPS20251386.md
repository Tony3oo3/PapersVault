## Problem

End-to-end autonomous driving agents trained via offline imitation learning suffer significant performance degradation in closed-loop deployment due to two root causes: observation mismatch (distribution shift between training-time and deployment-time sensor inputs) and objective mismatch (imitation loss does not align with long-horizon reward maximization). No prior work has systematically curated counterfactual driving data during training to address both mismatches simultaneously while preserving photorealistic visual fidelity.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 6-camera RGB images at 450×800 resolution, ego vehicle history (velocity, acceleration, and route progress over 5 past frames as a (T_hist, 8) array), and the frozen base policy's predicted trajectory (T_fut × 2 waypoints). At inference, the diffusion adapter generates 20 residual trajectory candidates via DDIM sampling, and the Q-value model selects the single best adapted trajectory (base + residual) to execute through an LQR controller. The base E2E policy (UniAD, VAD, or LTF) remains frozen throughout; only the lightweight adapter and value network are trained.

## Method

MPA operates in three stages. First, a 3DGS-based simulator generates counterfactual rollouts by augmenting the pretrained policy's actions with rotation (±10°), warping (0.1–1.0×), and Gaussian noise, then filtering trajectories via beam search on cumulative reward and distance thresholds to maintain visual fidelity. Second, a diffusion-based policy adapter (1D U-Net denoising network) is trained to predict multi-modal residual trajectories Δa = a* − a_base, conditioned on scene encoding, ego history, and the base trajectory, using a min-over-modes diffusion loss. Third, a multi-step Q-value model with four interpretable principles (route completion, lateral distance, collision avoidance, speed compliance) is trained on the same counterfactual data. At inference, the adapter proposes multiple trajectory candidates and the Q-value model performs inference-time scaling to select the action with the highest expected cumulative utility, decoupling value-based selection from policy optimization.

## Results

On the HUGSIM closed-loop benchmark (nuScenes), MPA(VAD) achieves the highest HDScore of 61.2 on unseen nominal scenes and 74.7 on safety-critical scenes, compared to the best pretrained baseline LTF at 41.9 and 24.2 respectively. MPA variants consistently achieve Route Completion (RC) above 90% in both settings, while the strongest non-MPA baseline (LTF) reaches only 63.3% (nominal) and 35.1% (safety-critical). In in-domain evaluation (Table 1), MPA(VAD) scores 67.0 HDScore versus 46.7 for LTF and 31.9 for VAD, with RC jumping from 65.2 to 94.9.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MPA dominates all six baselines on HDScore across every evaluation setting. On unseen nominal scenes, MPA(VAD) achieves 61.2 HDScore versus 41.9 for the best pretrained baseline (LTF) and 35.1 for the best counterfactual-trained baseline (Diffusion), a gap of ~19–26 points. The advantage is most dramatic in safety-critical scenes: MPA(VAD) reaches 74.7 HDScore while LTF manages only 24.2 and VAD only 16.0, driven primarily by Route Completion (96.6 vs. 35.1 and 25.4 respectively). AD-MLP, though conservative (high NC at 93.5 in safety-critical), barely completes routes (RC=4.9), yielding a near-zero HDScore of 4.3. MPA variants trade a small amount of NC (e.g., 79.8 vs. LTF's 80.9 in safety-critical) for dramatically higher RC and DAC, reflecting the Q-value model's emphasis on long-horizon route progress and collision avoidance. The pretrained E2E policies (UniAD, VAD, LTF) without MPA show comparable open-loop quality but degrade severely in closed-loop, confirming that the observation/objective mismatch—not raw perception—is the bottleneck.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the diffusion-based policy learning paradigm from Diffusion Policy [62] for its residual action adapter, and on 3DGS-based photorealistic simulation (HUGSIM [13]) for counterfactual data generation. It departs from prior E2E driving works (UniAD, VAD, Hydra-MDP) that rely on rule-based scoring or open-loop metrics by introducing a learned multi-step Q-value model for inference-time trajectory selection, and from RAD [19] which trains a PPO value critic but leaves it unused at inference. The key conceptual departure is treating the open-loop-to-closed-loop gap as two separable mismatches (observation and objective) and addressing each with a dedicated module, rather than end-to-end RL fine-tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MPA provides a general, plug-and-play adaptation framework that substantially improves closed-loop safety and route completion across three different base policies, suggesting the approach is not tied to a specific architecture. The inference-time scaling via a learned Q-value model offers a principled alternative to classifier-based diffusion guidance, providing longer-horizon feedback without gradient instability. Key limitations include reliance on 3DGS rendering fidelity under constrained lateral deviations (<0.6 m), decoupling of value modeling from policy optimization, and evaluation on a single dataset (nuScenes) with a small safety-critical test set (10 scenes). Open questions include whether online RL over the 3DGS simulator could close the remaining gap, and whether the framework extends to multi-modal foundation-model-based driving agents.
