## Problem

The miniF2F benchmark, the standard for evaluating autoformalization and theorem proving, contains over 300 discrepancies between formal and informal statements, 16 unprovable theorems, and excessive simplifications that inflate reported model accuracies. When SoTA autoformalization (97% reported) and theorem proving (70%) models are combined in an end-to-end pipeline, accuracy drops to ~35%, with a major portion of this gap attributable to benchmark quality issues rather than model limitations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252671/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes informal mathematical statements from competitions (IMO, AMC, AIME) and produces formal Lean 4 statements via autoformalization, then formal proofs via theorem proving, with final verification requiring both REPL compilation and semantic alignment with the original informal statement. The corrected benchmark provides 488 theorems in two variants: v2s (simplified, with solutions provided in both informal and formal statements) and v2c (competition-level, preserving original difficulty including multiple-choice options in formal statements and requiring the model to discover the answer before proving it).

## Method

The paper systematically audits all 488 miniF2F theorems, correcting over 300 Lean 4 formal statements and their informal counterparts to eliminate errors, unprovability, and excessive simplifications, producing two corrected variants (v2s and v2c). They build a fully automated end-to-end pipeline combining SoTA autoformalizers (Herald, Kimina, o4-mini) with SoTA theorem provers (DeepSeek-Prover-V1.5/V2, Goedel-Prover, Kimina-Prover), evaluating under both 'full score' and 'Olympiad setting' where proofs must semantically match the original problem. A key methodological contribution is comparing LLM-based versus human expert evaluation of autoformalization outputs, revealing that LLM judges inflate accuracy by 20–35 percentage points (e.g., Herald: 97% LLM vs. 66% human at @128). The v2c variant additionally restores original competition structure by embedding multiple-choice options directly in formal statements and removing provided solutions, adding an extra reasoning step that the model must complete before proving.

## Results

On the corrected miniF2F-v2s benchmark (Olympiad setting, where proofs must match the original informal statement), the best end-to-end pipeline (Herald + Goedel-V2-7B) achieves 50.0% effective accuracy on the test set, up from 36.9% on the original miniF2F-v1. For standalone theorem proving, Goedel-V2 leads with 82.0% on v1-test, dropping to 74.2% on v2s-test and 72.5% on v2c-test as problems revert to their original difficulty. A critical finding is that LLM-based evaluation inflates autoformalization accuracy dramatically: Herald's reported 97.5% accuracy drops to 62.7% under human verification on v1-test, and Kimina's 98.4% drops to 90.6%. The paper also identifies and fixes 16 previously unprovable statements, on which Goedel-V2 achieves 61.5% accuracy (8/13) on the v2s-test subset.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates five theorem provers and three autoformalizers across three benchmark versions. Among theorem provers, Goedel-V2 consistently leads (82.0% on v1-test), followed by DeepSeek-Prover-V2-7B (73.4%), Kimina-Prover-Distill-7B (65.2%), Goedel-Prover-SFT (58.2%), and DeepSeek-Prover-V1.5-RL (50.0%); all lose 7.8–11.2 percentage points moving from v1 to v2s/v2c as simplifications are reverted. Among autoformalizers, Kimina achieves the highest human-verified accuracy (90.6% on v1-test), but drops to 75.4% on v2c-test, while o4-mini (46.3% on v1-test) actually *improves* to 51.6% on v2s-test, suggesting data contamination in the dedicated autoformalizers. The end-to-end pipeline comparison (Table 1) shows that even the best combination (Herald + Goedel-V2-7B) reaches only 50.0% effective accuracy in the Olympiad setting on v2s-test, far below the 97% autoformalization and 82% proving accuracies reported in isolation, highlighting a significant misalignment between the two sub-tasks. On the 16 previously unprovable problems, Goedel-V2 recovers the most (61.5% on v2s-test), while older models like DeepSeek-Prover-V1.5-RL manage only 30.8%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the miniF2F benchmark (Zheng et al., ICLR 2021) by performing a comprehensive audit of all 488 theorems, correcting over 300 Lean 4 statements and their informal counterparts to eliminate errors, unprovable theorems, and excessive simplifications. It builds on the autoformalization evaluation pipeline from Herald (Gao et al., ICLR 2025) but departs from it by replacing LLM-based equivalence checking with human expert verification, revealing a substantial gap in reported accuracies. The paper also positions itself within the broader 'platinum benchmark' movement (Vendrow et al., 2024) that emphasizes error-free, human-verified datasets for reliable model evaluation, and introduces a holistic end-to-end pipeline evaluation (autoformalization + proving) that prior work had not systematically performed on miniF2F.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that LLM-based evaluation inflates autoformalization accuracy by roughly 30 percentage points (97% reported vs. 66% human-verified for Herald) challenges a widely adopted evaluation practice in the field and suggests that progress in autoformalization may be overstated. The corrected miniF2F-v2 benchmark provides the community with a more reliable and appropriately difficult evaluation tool, and the end-to-end pipeline results (34.8% on v1, 70% on v2c) expose a critical disconnect between autoformalization and theorem proving research communities. Open questions include how to develop reliable automated evaluation for autoformalization that matches human judgment, how to close the gap between individual component accuracies and end-to-end pipeline performance, and whether the findings generalize beyond Lean to other formal verification systems. A key limitation is that the work covers only Lean, and the human evaluation, while thorough, was conducted by a small team of experts.
