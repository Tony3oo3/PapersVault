## Problem

Existing RAG context selection methods rely on predefined quality metrics (query relevance, list diversity, or generator log-likelihood) that each capture only a subset of available information, resulting in limited gains over standard RAG and sometimes even degrading performance. The paper identifies that no single metric holistically integrates query-awareness, list-awareness, and generator-awareness, and that task-specific top-k tuning further complicates practical deployment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250979/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural language query and a list of n retrieved context chunks (100-word Wikipedia passages retrieved via E5-base-v2) and outputs a per-context CI value score. Selection is performed by simply retaining all contexts with positive predicted CI values, eliminating the need for a top-k hyperparameter. The CSM surrogate model is a lightweight BERT-based network that predicts these scores in a single forward pass, avoiding the n LLM calls required for exact CI computation and the label dependency of the oracle utility function.

## Method

The paper introduces the Contextual Influence (CI) value, defined as the utility degradation when removing a single context from the list (a leave-one-out data valuation metric), which simultaneously captures query-awareness, list-awareness, and generator-awareness. To make CI values computable at inference time, they propose a CI Surrogate Model (CSM) with a hierarchical architecture: a BERT-uncased local layer encodes query-context pairs, a 3-layer 8-head self-attention global layer models inter-context interactions, and a 2-layer MLP produces per-context scores. Two training paradigms establish generator awareness: (1) supervised regression on oracle CI values with reweighted MSE and contrastive loss, augmented by cross-instance data intervention to address the severe class imbalance (~80% near-zero CI values); and (2) end-to-end training using Gumbel-Softmax soft masking of generator inputs with complementary sufficiency (correct answers with selected contexts) and necessity (uniform distribution when high-value contexts are removed) losses.

## Results

Across 8 NLP tasks and 2 LLM backbones (Llama3-8B and Qwen2.5-7B), CSM-st achieves the best or second-best result on all tasks, with a 15.03% average improvement over leading baselines. On Open-Domain QA (EM), CSM-st outperforms all baselines by up to 21.72% (e.g., 42.53 on NQ vs. 37.01 for Standard RAG with Llama3-8B), and achieves a 19.40% F1 improvement in Multihop QA (e.g., 51.95 on HotpotQA vs. 44.81 for Standard RAG with Qwen2.5-7B). The oracle CI value upper bound reaches 45.79 EM on NQ and 53.78 F1 on HotpotQA (Llama3-8B), confirming the headroom of the CI value metric. CSM also achieves significantly lower inference latency (481 ms vs. 1437 ms for RankGPT on NQ with n=50 contexts).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CSM-st dominates across nearly all comparisons: on Llama3-8B, it beats Standard RAG (FlashRAG) by 5.52 EM on NQ (42.53 vs. 37.01) and 6.58 F1 on HotpotQA (47.53 vs. 40.95), and outperforms the strongest context-selection baselines bge-reranker (39.06) and RankGPT (38.61) by 3.47 and 3.92 EM on NQ respectively. Against generator-enhancing baselines, CSM-st surpasses Ret-Robust (41.77) by 0.76 EM on NQ and the agentic methods Self-RAG (36.23) and RQ-RAG (34.27) by 6.30 and 8.26 EM. RECOMP-ex, which relies solely on generator log-likelihood, underperforms Standard RAG on NQ (29.86 vs. 37.01), confirming the paper's argument that generator-aware metrics alone are insufficient. On Qwen2.5-7B, CSM-st's advantage is even more pronounced: 8.88 EM over Standard RAG on NQ (47.38 vs. 38.50) and 7.14 F1 over RQ-RAG on HotpotQA (51.95 vs. 49.62). The only notable exception is CSM-e2e on ASQA with Qwen2.5-7B (35.62 F1), where it edges out CSM-st (34.05), suggesting end-to-end training may better capture long-form generation needs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reconceptualizes RAG context selection as an inference-time data valuation problem, directly borrowing the leave-one-out influence function from training data valuation (Koh & Liang; MATES; DSDM) and adapting it to score retrieved contexts at inference time. It builds on the utility prediction model (UPM) framework from recent inference-time data valuation work [Chi et al., ICLR 2025] but departs by predicting a list-wise marginal contribution (CI value) rather than a point-wise utility, and by incorporating the rationale-environment recombination strategy [Wu et al., ICLR 2022] for data intervention to handle the severe class imbalance in CI value distributions. The CSM architecture (BERT local layer + self-attention global layer + MLP) follows the list-wise reranker design pattern, while the end-to-end training with Gumbel-Softmax soft masking and sufficiency/necessity losses draws from differentiable selection and explanation literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The CI value metric provides a principled unification of query-awareness, list-awareness, and generator-awareness into a single score, and the 'keep positive CI values' rule eliminates the need for task-specific top-k tuning—a significant practical simplification for deploying RAG systems. The 15.03% average improvement over strong baselines (including agentic methods like Self-RAG and RQ-RAG) across 8 tasks and 2 LLMs demonstrates that careful context filtering outperforms complex reflection/planning mechanisms. Key limitations include the requirement for task-specific CSM training (oracle CI values must be computed on a training set), the ~80% near-zero CI value distribution making supervised training challenging, and the open question of whether a single universal context selector can generalize across diverse tasks without retraining.
