## Problem

Point Cloud Few-Shot Semantic Segmentation (PC-FSS) suffers from two under-addressed challenges: intra-class diversity, where the same semantic category exhibits large physical variation between support and query samples, and cross-domain feature misalignment, where query data contains semantic content absent from the support set. Most existing methods also rely on pre-training paradigms that add computational overhead and introduce domain shifts for unseen categories, while the irregular, sparse nature of point clouds makes capturing local geometric structure particularly difficult in data-limited few-shot settings.

## Input / Output

The method operates in an episodic N-way K-shot format: the support set provides K labeled point clouds per target class (each a 1 m × 1 m block of 2,048 points with 3D coordinates plus d-dimensional features such as RGB or normals, accompanied by a binary foreground/background mask), and the query set supplies unlabeled point clouds of the same block size. The network outputs a per-point label assigning every query point to one of the N novel classes or a background class, evaluated via mean IoU across multiple random splits.

## Method

VIP-Seg is a pre-training-free encoder-decoder that stacks three DyPowerConv-Mamba blocks in the encoder and recovers resolution via inverse interpolation in the decoder. DyPowerConv combines a Low-order Convolution (encoding coordinates and color through nonparameter trigonometric functions) with a Dynamic High-order Convolution that runs T=8 parallel experts, each applying a smooth learnable power function (|f_j − f_i| + ε)^{p_t} to neighbor-feature differences, with attention-based assembly weights derived from explicit geometric descriptors [p_i, p_j, p_j−p_i, ‖p_j−p_i‖]. The Visual Introspective Prototype (VIP) module then refines class prototypes through S alternating steps of a Prototype Enhancement Module (self- and cross-attention over support/query statistics) and a Prototype Difference Module (sigmoid-gated adjustment from the covariance difference between query and support features), progressively aligning feature distributions. A gating fusion network learns per-step importance weights and combines the cosine-similarity-based predictions from all reasoning steps into the final segmentation, eliminating the need for any pre-training stage.

## Results

On S3DIS, VIP-Seg achieves 74.15% mIoU in the 2-way 1-shot setting and 77.01% in the 2-way 5-shot setting, surpassing the previous best (DyPolySeg) by 1.23 and 1.35 percentage points respectively. In the more challenging 3-way settings, VIP-Seg reaches 68.58% (1-shot) and 69.30% (5-shot) mIoU, outperforming DyPolySeg by 2.34 and 1.38 points. On ScanNet, VIP-Seg attains 72.12% (2-way 1-shot) and 71.28% (3-way 5-shot) mIoU, consistently exceeding all baselines. The method also eliminates pre-training entirely, reducing total training time to 0.9 hours versus 4.7–9.5 hours for pre-training-based methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VIP-Seg outperforms all 10 compared baselines across every few-shot setting on both S3DIS and ScanNet. The closest competitor, DyPolySeg [35] (also from the same group), is surpassed by 1.23–2.34 points on S3DIS and 0.17–1.10 points on ScanNet, with the largest gap appearing in the 3-way 1-shot S3DIS setting (+2.34). Among pre-training-based methods, DAFNet [36] (69.20% 2-way 1-shot on S3DIS) and TaylorSeg-PN [38] (69.12%) trail VIP-Seg by roughly 5 points, while earlier methods like AttMPTI [54] (54.86%) and BFG [19] (55.79%) lag by nearly 20 points. The pre-training-free Seg-PN [56] (66.41%) is the strongest non-pre-training baseline but still falls 7.74 points short of VIP-Seg in the 2-way 1-shot S3DIS setting. On the harder ScanNet benchmark, VIP-Seg's margins narrow (e.g., +0.23 over DyPolySeg in 2-way 1-shot) but remain positive in all four settings, confirming consistent gains rather than dataset-specific artifacts.

## Relation to Prior Work

VIP-Seg synthesizes ideas from three distinct research threads—prototypical networks for few-shot segmentation (Zhao et al., 2021), dynamic convolution for point clouds (PAConv, KPConv, DyCo3D), and state-space models (Mamba/PointMamba)—into a unified pre-training-free architecture. It departs from the dominant two-stage paradigm (pre-train DGCNN, then fine-tune with prototype adaptation) by eliminating pre-training entirely, and introduces a multi-step reasoning loop (alternating PEM and PDM) for progressive prototype refinement that has no direct analogue in prior PC-FSS work. The DyPowerConv module extends dynamic convolution by replacing attention-based kernel selection with learnable power functions, a design choice that differentiates it from CondConv-style or PAConv-style approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The pre-training-free design offers a practical 81–85% reduction in total training time compared to pre-training-based methods, which is meaningful for practitioners with limited compute budgets. However, the SOTA improvements over the previous best (DyPolySeg) are modest (1–2 mIoU points), and the episode-based training paradigm still demands substantial resources. Open questions include whether the multi-step reasoning depth generalizes across datasets without manual tuning, how performance degrades under extreme sparsity or occlusion, and whether multi-modal inputs (RGB, depth) could complement the purely geometric approach.
