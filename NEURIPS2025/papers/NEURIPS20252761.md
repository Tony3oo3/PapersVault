## Problem

Existing concept unlearning methods for text-to-image diffusion models face a tri-fold challenge: training-based approaches are computationally expensive and degrade general generation quality, inference-time filtering is easily bypassed in open-source settings, and closed-form editing methods often fail to fully suppress targeted concepts under adversarial red-teaming prompts. No prior method simultaneously achieves efficient (training-free), robust (resistant to adversarial prompts), and specific (preserving unrelated concepts that share terms with the target) concept removal.

## Input / Output

CURE takes a pre-trained diffusion model (Stable Diffusion v1.4), a set of forget-concept text prompts, and an optional set of retain-concept text prompts. It produces a modified model where only the cross-attention key and value weight matrices (2.23% of total parameters) are updated in a single closed-form step, taking approximately 2 seconds on an A40 GPU. Inference-time cost is unchanged from the original model (~7.06 s/sample), and the one-time SVD subspace construction is performed offline with no runtime overhead.

## Method

CURE's core contribution is the Spectral Eraser, a closed-form operator that constructs discriminative subspaces via SVD of token embeddings from the frozen CLIP text encoder. For forget and retain concept sets, it computes orthonormal basis vectors U_f and U_r, then builds energy-scaled projection operators P_f = U_f Λ_f U_f^T and P_r = U_r Λ_r U_r^T, where the diagonal scaling matrices Λ are determined by a spectral expansion function f(r_i; α) = αr_i / ((α−1)r_i + 1) inspired by Tikhonov regularization. The parameter α controls the trade-off between selective erasure (low α, energy-proportional weighting) and comprehensive suppression (high α, near-uniform weighting across all modes). The composite unlearning operator P_unlearn = I − (P_f − P_f P_r) removes discriminative forget directions while restoring overlap with the retain subspace, and is absorbed directly into the cross-attention weights as W_k^new = W_k P_unlearn and W_v^new = W_v P_unlearn, eliminating any per-token runtime projection cost.

## Results

On artist erasure (Van Gogh), CURE achieves the highest LPIPS_e (0.44) and lowest Acc_e (0.30) among all methods, while maintaining the best COCO-30k FID (1.44) and CLIP score (31.18), indicating minimal collateral damage to general generation quality. On the I2P nudity-removal task, CURE detects only 11 nude body parts versus 646 for the unmodified SD-v1.4, outperforming all baselines including ESD-u (59) and RECE (66). Against red-teaming attacks, CURE achieves the lowest Attack Success Rate on Ring-A-Bell (0.013) and P4D (0.107), substantially outperforming all training-free baselines and most training-based ones. The entire unlearning operation completes in ~2 seconds with only 2.23% of model weights modified.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CURE outperforms all nine baselines on the primary erasure metric (Acc_e) for Van Gogh removal, achieving 0.30 versus 0.95 for UCE [34], 0.80 for RECE [35], and 0.95 for SLD-Medium [16], indicating far more complete style suppression. On general generation quality (COCO-30k), CURE achieves the best FID (1.44) and CLIP (31.18), surpassing UCE (FID 1.81, CLIP 23.08) and RECE (FID 2.82, CLIP 30.95), while training-based methods like CA [29] degrade FID to 7.87. For NSFW content removal on I2P, CURE detects only 11 nude body parts, beating ESD-u [27] (59), RECE [35] (66), CA [29] (70), and UCE [34] (126). On red-teaming robustness, CURE achieves the lowest ASR on Ring-A-Bell (0.013) and P4D (0.107), dramatically outperforming training-free baselines RECE (0.134/0.381) and UCE (0.331/0.667), and even surpassing the training-based MACE [31] (0.076/0.142). The closest competitor is MACE [31], which achieves slightly lower ASR on I2P (0.023 vs 0.061) but requires full fine-tuning, whereas CURE is entirely training-free and completes in ~2 seconds versus MACE's multi-hour optimization.

## Relation to Prior Work

CURE extends the closed-form attention-weight editing paradigm established by UCE and RECE, which modify cross-attention projections to suppress target concepts without retraining. Its key departure is the explicit use of SVD over CLIP token embeddings to construct orthogonal discriminative subspaces for forget and retain concepts, combined with a Tikhonov-inspired spectral expansion mechanism that provides a single interpretable knob (α) for controlling erasure strength. This draws a direct conceptual bridge from orthogonal representation methods in class unlearning and continual learning (SAP, GPM) into the diffusion-model safety setting, where prior closed-form editors applied uniform or heuristic projections without spectral energy weighting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 2-second, 2.23%-weight modification time makes concept erasure practically deployable in open-source settings where inference-time guardrails can be trivially bypassed, and the strong robustness against multiple red-teaming tools (Ring-A-Bell ASR of 0.013 vs. 0.331 for UCE) addresses a critical gap in prior unlearning methods. Open questions include whether formal leakage bounds can be established under prompt perturbations, how the method scales to concepts without clean textual representations, and whether the approach generalizes beyond Stable Diffusion v1.4 to newer architectures like SDXL or DiT-based models. The paper also flags the dual-use risk of erasing forensic watermarks or provenance signals, suggesting governance layers are needed alongside the technical contribution.
