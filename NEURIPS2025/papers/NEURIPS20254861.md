## Problem

Existing world simulators either operate in game-like environments with restricted directional movements or generate real-world videos without human motion control, reducing users to passive spectators. No prior method enables a user to act as a freeform participant with unrestricted, real-time human motion control within a realistic egocentric world that maintains 4D scene consistency over long video rollouts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254861/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single egocentric image (480×480) as the initial scene and a real-world human motion sequence (SMPL parameters decomposed into body/feet 66D, head 3D, and per-hand 45D articulation) captured by an exocentric camera. It produces a 49-frame (6-second) egocentric video at 8 FPS; during training it also jointly predicts 4D scene point maps, but at inference the point-map stream is generated autoregressively alongside the video without any external 3D input, requiring only the first frame and motion sequence.

## Method

PlayerOne builds on the Wan2.1 1.3B DiT backbone and introduces two core modules. Part-Disentangled Motion Injection (PMI) splits the SMPL motion into three groups (body/feet, hands, head), each encoded by a dedicated 8-layer 3D-conv motion encoder; head parameters are further converted to rotation-only camera extrinsics, parameterized via Plücker rays, and injected into noised video latents through a camera encoder to enforce egocentric view alignment. Scene-Frame Reconstruction (SR) uses CUT3R to render per-frame point maps, encodes them with a point-map encoder plus a 5-layer 3D-conv adapter, and concatenates the resulting latents channel-wise with the noised video latents so the DiT jointly denoises both streams, guaranteeing world consistency. Training follows a coarse-to-fine pipeline: LoRA fine-tuning on 5M egocentric text-video pairs (Egovid-5M) for coarse generation, then fine-tuning the last six DiT blocks on ~41K curated motion-video pairs extracted from ego-exo datasets via an automatic SAM2 + SMPLest-X + OpenPose reprojection-filtering pipeline, followed by CausVid asymmetric distillation to achieve real-time 8 FPS inference.

## Results

On a 100-video benchmark constructed from the Nymeria dataset, PlayerOne achieves a DINO-Score of 67.8, CLIP-Score of 88.2, MPJPE of 127.16, MRRPE of 151.62, FVD of 226.12, and LPIPS of 0.0663, outperforming all baselines across every metric. In the user study (20 annotators, 25 video groups), PlayerOne scores 3.59/4 on Quality, 3.63/4 on Fidelity, 3.65/4 on Smoothness, and 3.86/4 on Alignment, again leading all competitors. The model operates at 8 FPS after distillation, generating 49-frame (6-second) videos at 480×480 resolution.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PlayerOne outperforms both baselines on all six metrics by large margins. Against the strongest competitor, Cosmos (Diff-14B), PlayerOne improves DINO-Score by 16.2 points (67.8 vs. 51.6), CLIP-Score by 8.5 points (88.2 vs. 79.7), and reduces MPJPE by 129.6 (127.16 vs. 256.73) and MRRPE by 101.4 (151.62 vs. 253.06), indicating substantially better hand-pose alignment. FVD drops from 302.17 to 226.12 and LPIPS from 0.1351 to 0.0663, reflecting markedly better temporal coherence and perceptual fidelity. Against Aether, the gaps are even wider (e.g., MPJPE 127.16 vs. 415.70, a 3.3× reduction). In the user study, PlayerOne leads Cosmos (Diff-14B) by 0.57–1.15 points on a 4-point scale across Quality, Fidelity, Smoothness, and Alignment, with the largest gap on Alignment (3.86 vs. 2.71), confirming superior motion-condition following.

## Relation to Prior Work

PlayerOne builds on the Wan2.1 DiT-based video diffusion model as its backbone, extending it with a part-disentangled motion injection module (using SMPL-X pose parameters split into head, hands, and body/feet groups) and a joint scene-frame reconstruction pathway that leverages CUT3R point maps. It departs from prior world simulators (The Matrix, Cosmos, Aether, Wonderland, Gen3C) that either restrict users to game environments with predetermined directional actions or produce world-consistent video without human motion control, by formulating egocentric simulation as a joint video-and-4D-scene denoising problem conditioned on real-world human motion. The CausVid distillation procedure is adopted for real-time inference, and the coarse-to-fine training strategy (LoRA pretraining on Egovid-5M followed by block-level fine-tuning on curated ego-exo pairs) addresses the absence of a dedicated egocentric motion-video dataset.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first system to enable freeform, real-time egocentric world simulation driven by actual human motion, shifting users from passive spectators to active participants in a generated environment. The part-disentangled motion injection and joint scene-frame reconstruction are modular techniques that could transfer to other interactive video generation settings. Key open questions include: whether the lack of explicit physical interaction modeling (no collision detection or contact reasoning) limits realism in close object manipulation, how performance scales to longer horizons beyond the 6-second training window, and whether the game-scenario performance gap can be closed with targeted data. The automated ego-exo dataset construction pipeline also provides a reusable recipe for the community.
