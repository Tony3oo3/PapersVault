## Problem

Automating text-to-image pipeline design (e.g., ComfyUI workflows) with LLMs has been limited by two issues: prior methods like ComfyGen require generating and scoring images across hundreds of predefined pipelines, making training computationally prohibitive, and they essentially memorize training flows rather than synthesizing novel graph topologies. This paper asks how to train an LLM to produce genuinely new, high-quality workflows efficiently, without the cost of image generation at every training step.

## Input / Output

The system takes a free-form text prompt as input and outputs a structured ComfyUI workflow graph (a sequence of interconnected nodes specifying base models, LoRAs, samplers, step counts, guidance scales, etc.) that is then executed to produce a final RGB image. The workflow is encoded in a custom compact tokenization (~200 tokens per flow vs. ~1500 for raw JSON), using specialized tokens for node names and model choices, which enables 16× larger batch sizes during training.

## Method

FlowRL uses a two-stage training strategy. Stage 1 performs supervised fine-tuning on 500K unscored prompt-flow pairs to teach the LLM workflow vocabulary and structure. Stage 2 applies GRPO (Group Relative Policy Optimization) to align the policy with human preferences, but replaces costly image generation with a surrogate reward model (ModernBERT-based) trained to predict image quality scores directly from prompt-workflow pairs. To prevent reward hacking, an ensemble of N surrogate models is used, and any prompt-flow pair whose ensemble variance exceeds a threshold receives zero reward. A component-aware hybrid reward assigns per-token credit by combining full-flow predictions with prefix-prediction scores, enabling granular credit assignment to specific workflow components. At inference, a classifier-free-guidance-inspired mechanism interpolates logits between the SFT and GRPO models (γ=1.5 by default), trading diversity for quality.

## Results

FlowRL achieves an overall GenEval score of 0.61, matching ComfyGen and outperforming all other baselines (SDXL: 0.55, DPO-SDXL: 0.59, GenArtist: 0.47). On human preference (HPS v2), FlowRL wins 60% of pairwise comparisons against ComfyGen (the closest competitor) and 95–98% against all other baselines. In terms of workflow novelty, FlowRL generates 41% unique flows with near-zero overlap with training data (0–1%), compared to ComfyGen's 7% unique and 94% copied from training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SDXL (Podell et al., ICLR 2024), FlowRL wins decisively on both metrics: GenEval overall 0.61 vs. 0.55, and HPS v2 win rate of 98% (SDXL wins only 2% of pairwise comparisons). Against DPO-SDXL (Wallace et al., CVPR 2024), FlowRL again leads on GenEval (0.61 vs. 0.59) and HPS v2 (95% win rate). Against GenArtist (Wang et al., 2024), the gap is largest: FlowRL scores 0.61 vs. 0.47 on GenEval and wins 95% of HPS v2 comparisons, with particularly large margins on attribute binding (0.38 vs. 0.07). The closest competitor is ComfyGen (Gal et al., 2024): FlowRL ties on GenEval overall (0.61 vs. 0.61) but outperforms it on two-object prompts (0.85 vs. 0.82) and attribute binding (0.38 vs. 0.29), wins 60% of HPS v2 pairwise comparisons, and generates dramatically more novel workflows (41% unique vs. 7%, with 0% vs. 94% overlap with training data).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends ComfyGen's prompt-adaptive workflow generation by replacing its classification-over-fixed-flows paradigm with a reinforcement learning approach (GRPO) that can synthesize genuinely novel workflow structures. It borrows the surrogate-reward-model idea from autonomous driving to avoid costly image generation during RL training, and adapts classifier-free guidance from diffusion models to interpolate between SFT and GRPO-tuned LLM logits at inference. The per-token reward attribution via a prefix-prediction model and the uncertainty-aware ensemble for mitigating reward hacking are methodological contributions layered on top of the GRPO framework from DeepSeekMath.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ability to generate novel workflows rather than memorizing a fixed set addresses a fundamental limitation of prior automated pipeline construction, opening the door to more creative and prompt-adaptive generative systems. However, the approach remains scoped to text-to-image ComfyUI workflows, requires full retraining when new components are introduced, and the surrogate reward model still depends on a relatively small scored dataset (330 flows). Open questions include whether the RL-based exploration generalizes to editing or video pipelines, and whether the CFG-inspired guidance tradeoff between diversity and quality can be controlled more granularly per prompt.
