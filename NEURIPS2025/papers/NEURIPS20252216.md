## Problem

While the sequential metric dimension literature characterizes the minimum number of exact distance queries needed to identify a point in finite graphs, no analogous theory exists for noisy distance queries in general metric spaces. This paper asks two fundamental questions: what is the best achievable reconstruction error in the limit of infinitely many queries, and at what rate is this optimum approached as a function of the number of queries? The answers depend delicately on the geometry of the underlying space and the noise parameters, and were previously uncharacterized.

## Input / Output

The game is played in a metric space (X, dist_X) with two noise parameters: ε ≥ 0 (multiplicative) and δ ≥ 0 (additive), such that each response satisfies d̂ =_{ε,δ} dist_X(q, x*). The reconstructor adaptively selects query points and receives noisy distances; the responder must ensure all answers remain jointly consistent with at least one secret point (a posteriori model). The primary outputs are a closed-form expression for the limiting optimal error in terms of the Chebyshev radius function e_X, and a dichotomy characterizing when finitely many queries suffice to reach this optimum (pseudo-finiteness) versus when convergence is gradual.

## Method

The first result (Theorem 2) shows OPT_X(ε,δ) = e_X((2+ε)δ), where e_X(α) is the supremum of Chebyshev radii over all subsets of X with diameter at most α. The upper bound leverages total boundedness: querying a finite α-net bounds the feasible region's diameter, and right-continuity of e_X (proved via Hausdorff-metric hyperspace compactness arguments) closes the gap. The lower bound constructs a responder strategy that preserves an extremal set of diameter (2+ε)δ inside the feasible region by always answering with the perturbed distance to the nearest point in that set. The second result (Theorem 6) proves non-pseudo-finiteness for convex Euclidean sets of dimension ≥ 2 (or any dimension with ε > 0) by designing a responder strategy that inductively maintains an α_t-neighborhood of a regular simplex, with α_t shrinking via a recurrence (quadratic for ε=0, linear for ε>0). Bad queries are handled by translating (ε>0) or rotating (ε=0) the simplex to restore a surviving neighborhood, with the key geometric tool being a consistency-window lemma that characterizes exactly when a set fits inside the feasible region for a given query.

## Results

The paper's main quantitative result (Theorem 2) establishes that for any totally bounded metric space X, the limiting reconstruction error equals the Chebyshev-radius function evaluated at the noise scale: OPT_X(ε,δ) = e_X((2+ε)δ). When the distance (2+ε)δ is realized in X, this yields the explicit bounds ½(2+ε)δ ≤ OPT_X(ε,δ) ≤ (2+ε)δ. The second main result (Theorem 6) characterizes pseudo-finiteness: a bounded convex set X ⊂ ℝⁿ is (ε,δ)-pseudo-finite if and only if dim(X) = 1 and ε = 0; in all other cases, convergence to the optimal error is gradual (exponential in T for ε ≠ 0, double-exponential for ε = 0).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines or experimental comparisons. The reconstruction game generalizes the sequential metric dimension (SMD) of Seager [2013] and subsequent work on finite graphs [Bensmail et al., 2020; Odor and Thiran, 2021], extending it from noiseless exact-distance queries on finite graphs to noisy (ε,δ)-approximate distance queries on arbitrary (totally bounded) metric spaces. The paper also subsumes the counting-query model of Dinur and Nissim [2003] as a special case on the Boolean cube with the Hamming metric (Example 1), showing that counting queries and Hamming-distance queries simulate each other with at most a two-query overhead per round. No direct head-to-head numerical comparisons are made, as the contributions are geometric characterizations and impossibility results rather than algorithmic improvements over prior methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalizes the sequential metric dimension (SMD) problem—originally defined for noiseless distance queries on finite graphs by Seager [2013] and studied by Bensmail et al. [2020], Odor and Thiran [2021], and Tillquist et al. [2023]—to noisy queries over arbitrary metric spaces. It also subsumes the counting-query model of Dinur and Nissim [2003] (foundational to differential privacy) as a special case on the Boolean cube with Hamming metric. The paper's novel contribution is a learning-theoretic reframing: the limiting reconstruction error plays the role of Bayes optimal error, and the convergence rate mirrors learning curves, a perspective absent from the prior SMD and privacy literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The tight Chebyshev-radius characterization (Theorem 2) gives a clean geometric answer to a question that was previously only tractable in finite/noiseless settings, and the pseudo-finiteness dichotomy (Theorem 6) reveals a subtle geometric threshold: the real line is pseudo-finite only in the purely additive regime, while all higher-dimensional convex spaces are never pseudo-finite. The open question of whether pseudo-finiteness characterizes finiteness among totally bounded spaces, and the gap between the exponential/double-exponential lower bounds and matching upper bounds on convergence rates, point to substantial remaining work. The framework also provides a unified lens connecting privacy (responder's perspective) and localization (reconstructor's perspective) that could inform future work on reconstruction robustness in differential privacy.
