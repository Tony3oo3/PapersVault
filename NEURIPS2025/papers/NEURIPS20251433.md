## Problem

Adaptive optimizers like Adam triple the memory footprint of LLM training by storing first- and second-moment states, creating a fundamental tension between convergence quality and memory efficiency. While SWAN demonstrated that stateless gradient preprocessing can match Adam's performance, its design principle remained ad hoc and its whitening step incurs O(m²(n+m)) computational cost. This paper asks: what is the unifying principle behind effective stateless gradient processing, and can it be made both more principled and more computationally efficient?

## Input / Output

The method consumes the raw stochastic gradient matrix (m×n) from backpropagation for each 2D weight matrix in transformer linear layers and produces a multi-normalized gradient matrix of the same shape, where every row has l2-norm √n and every column has l2-norm √m. It is applied only to linear projection weights in attention and MLP blocks (not embeddings, RMSNorm, or the output layer, which still use Adam), and the normalized gradient is scaled by a fixed factor α=0.05 times the global scheduler learning rate before the parameter update. The procedure requires L=5 alternating iterations per gradient step and operates entirely statelessly—no moment estimates are stored across steps.

## Method

The paper introduces gradient multi-normalization, a framework that generalizes the single-norm steepest-descent interpretation of optimizers (Bernstein & Newhouse, 2024) to simultaneously normalize a gradient with respect to K norms via an alternating projection scheme (MultiNorm). The key theoretical result shows that when the normalized projections have constant l2-norm, the alternating scheme converges to a fixed point of both projections to arbitrary precision. For the concrete instantiation SinkGD, the two norms chosen are the max row-wise l2-norm (scaled by √n) and the max column-wise l2-norm (scaled by √m), replacing SWAN's expensive spectral-norm constraint. This specific norm pair makes each projection a simple diagonal rescaling, so the alternating scheme reduces exactly to the square-root Sinkhorn algorithm, yielding O(nm) per-step cost versus SWAN's O(m²(n+m)). The paper further shows that SWAN itself is recovered as the L=1 instance of MultiNorm with the row-wise l2-norm and the spectral norm, unifying prior stateless optimizers under the multi-norm framework.

## Results

On the memory-efficient LLaMA pretraining benchmark (C4 dataset), SinkGD achieves the best test perplexity across all model scales: 30.99 (60M), 22.75 (130M), 16.51 (350M), and 13.51 (1.3B), while maintaining the lowest memory footprint (0.23G–2.98G) tied with SWAN and Apollo-mini. On the 1.3B model, SinkGD reaches the same evaluation perplexity as Adam using only 40% of the training tokens, yielding a 2.79× speedup over reproduced Adam. Effective throughput is 3× higher than Adam (161,769 vs. 53,047 tokens/s), while raw throughput is marginally higher (57,982 vs. 53,047 tokens/s), confirming the multi-normalization step adds negligible computational overhead.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SinkGD outperforms all five baselines on test perplexity at every model scale. Against Adam (the standard adaptive optimizer), SinkGD achieves a 2.79× token-efficiency speedup on the 1.3B model (PPL 13.51 vs. 16.44 reproduced) while using 60% less memory (2.98G vs. 7.48G). Versus SWAN, the closest stateless competitor, SinkGD improves PPL by 1.62 on 1.3B (13.51 vs. 15.13) and 1.71 on 350M (16.51 vs. 18.22) at identical memory, thanks to replacing the expensive Newton-Schulz whitening step with O(nm) Sinkhorn iterations. Against Apollo-mini (the best low-memory baseline), SinkGD gains 0.66 PPL on 1.3B (13.51 vs. 14.17) and 0.67 on 350M (16.51 vs. 17.18) with the same 2.98G/0.93G memory. Fira and GaLore, which use low-rank gradient projection, are consistently outperformed by 0.8–2.1 PPL across scales. In the 1B-vs-7B comparison (Table 2), a 1B model trained with SinkGD matches or beats a 7B model trained with Apollo after 150K steps (PPL 12.97 vs. 13.02), completing training in 3.3 days versus 15 days.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the steepest-descent-as-gradient-normalization framework of Bernstein & Newhouse (2024) from a single norm to a multi-norm setting, and shows that SWAN (Ma et al., 2024) is a specific instance of this generalized formulation with particular norm choices and a single alternating step. The key departure from SWAN is replacing the expensive spectral-norm whitening (computed via Newton-Schulz at O(m²(m+n))) with a column-wise ℓ₂ normalization, which the authors connect to the classical Sinkhorn algorithm for matrix scaling. This yields SinkGD, an O(nm) stateless optimizer that retains SGD's memory footprint while achieving Adam-level perplexity on LLaMA pretraining.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: SinkGD achieves a 2–3× token-efficiency speedup over Adam with zero additional optimizer-state memory, making it attractive for distributed LLM training where memory and communication are bottlenecks. The clean theoretical guarantee (convergence to a fixed point of the multi-norm constraints at arbitrary precision) provides a principled design template for future stateless optimizers beyond the specific Sinkhorn instantiation. Open questions the authors flag include understanding *why* multi-normalization can replace EMA states, the theoretical convergence properties of the resulting optimization dynamics, and whether the approach scales to frontier-model training regimes or tasks beyond LLM pretraining.
