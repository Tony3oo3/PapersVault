## Problem

Existing defenses against finetuning-based jailbreak attacks (FJAttack) effectively reduce attack success rates for directly prompted harmful queries but fail to generalize when harmful queries are disguised by unseen attack templates (e.g., Prefix Injection, Role Play, Refusal Suppression). The paper identifies that this failure is not due to the LLM's inability to recognize harmful content—t-SNE visualizations show clear separation in embedding space—but rather to the inability of existing mechanisms to activate that recognition at inference time.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text query (potentially wrapped in an attack template) as input and produces either a full text response or a truncated response followed by a safety reminder. At inference, the query is first augmented with a pre-generation defense prompt to obtain a harmfulness probability; if predicted harmless, generation proceeds with adaptive mid-generation checks every k = γ·P("harmless") tokens, where γ is a tunable hyperparameter (default 32). KV cache is reused across both detection and generation passes, so the only extra computation is processing the short defense prompts.

## Method

MetaDefense trains a single LLM via lightweight instruction tuning (LoRA) to perform harmfulness classification through two specialized prompts: a pre-generation prompt ("Is this query harmful or harmless? It is 100%") appended to the input query, and a mid-generation prompt ("Is this response harmful or harmless? It is 100%") appended to partial responses during decoding. Both prompts are trained jointly with a standard next-token prediction loss on a combined dataset of harmful and benign query-response pairs, where the target is the single word "harmful" or "harmless." At inference, the pre-generation check can immediately reject harmful queries, while the mid-generation check uses adaptive scheduling—generating more tokens when the model is confident the response is harmless and checking more frequently when uncertain—to catch false negatives from the pre-generation stage. This design eliminates the need for a separate classifier or moderation model, halving memory usage compared to LLM-Classifier or LLaMA-Guard baselines while achieving lower ASR across seen and unseen attack templates.

## Results

On LLaMA-2-7B (averaged over SST2, AGNews, GSM8K), MetaDefense achieves ASR of 1.0% on the seen Direct Attack and 1.7–7.2% on three unseen attack templates (Prefix Injection, Refusal Suppression, Role Play), while maintaining FTA of 63.6–64.8%. On Qwen-2.5-3B-Instruct, MetaDefense reaches ASR as low as 0.1% (Direct) and 0.5% (Role Play), and on LLaMA-3.2-3B-Instruct it achieves 0.1% (Direct) and 1.5% (Role Play). MetaDefense uses 26.3 GB memory (half of LLM-Classifier or Booster+LLaMA-Guard at 52.6 GB) and processes harmful queries in 0.56 s versus 3.38 s for the non-aligned model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MetaDefense outperforms all alignment-stage baselines (RepNoise, Vaccine, Booster) by a wide margin on unseen templates: on Prefix Injection, Booster drops to 63.6% ASR while MetaDefense holds at 1.7%; on Role Play, Booster reaches 50.1% versus MetaDefense's 7.2%. Among finetuning-stage methods, BackdoorAlign and PTST show minimal improvement over SFT on unseen templates (ASR 48.8–79.0%), whereas MetaDefense reduces ASR by more than an order of magnitude. The hybrid Booster+LLaMA-Guard baseline narrows the gap (22.2% ASR on Prefix Injection) but still trails MetaDefense (1.7%) and requires 2× memory (52.6 GB vs. 26.3 GB). LLM-Classifier achieves comparable or slightly lower ASR (0.1–0.9%) but at double the memory cost, making MetaDefense the more deployable option. Against inference-stage methods CaC and Backtracking (Table 16), MetaDefense achieves lower ASR on all unseen templates (e.g., 2.0% vs. 55.4% for CaC on Prefix Injection) and runs ~8× faster (0.37 s vs. 3.13–3.39 s per query). MetaDefense also resists adaptive mislabelled-prompt attacks (ASR ≤ 0.2%) and prolonged finetuning up to 100 epochs (ASR < 2.5%), where baselines degrade by 10–30%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself against the existing taxonomy of FJAttack defenses (alignment-stage: RepNoise, Vaccine, Booster; finetuning-stage: BackdoorAlign, SafeInstr; inference-stage: PTST, CaC, Backtracking, LLaMA-Guard; hybrid: Booster+LLaMA-Guard), identifying a shared generalization gap: all fail when harmful queries are wrapped in unseen attack templates. Rather than extending any single prior method, it carves new ground by repurposing the LLM's own generative capability—via lightweight instruction tuning with a one-line defense prompt—as a unified detection mechanism, eliminating the need for separate classifiers or alignment-stage regularization. The mid-generation defense with confidence-adaptive scheduling (k = γ·P('harmless')) has no direct precedent in prior FJAttack literature, which typically operates either pre-generation or post-generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is substantial: a single LLM handles both detection and generation with half the memory of dual-model approaches, streaming-compatible mid-generation monitoring, and near-zero ASR on unseen templates—addressing the most critical real-world vulnerability in current LLM-as-a-service deployments. Limitations include elevated false-negative rates on Role Play attacks (6.5–8.0%) and the requirement that the provider controls both alignment and inference pipelines, which constrains applicability to fully open user-facing APIs. Open questions include whether the approach scales to much larger models (70B+), whether adaptive attacks that specifically target the defense prompt (e.g., training the model to output 'harmless' for harmful queries) can degrade it, and how it interacts with multi-turn conversations where harmfulness accumulates across turns.
