## Problem

Slow-thinking models like GPT-o1 and DeepSeek-R1 have dramatically improved text-based reasoning, yet their multimodal reasoning performance remains on par with fast-thinking models (e.g., GPT-o1 scores 73.9% on MathVista, slightly below Qwen2.5-VL-72B's 74.8%). The paper asks how to effectively incentivize slow-thinking and self-reflection in vision-language models using reinforcement learning, without relying on costly distillation from stronger teacher models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251037/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts one or more RGB images alongside a textual query and generates a step-by-step reasoning chain ending with a final answer enclosed in \boxed{}. During the Forced Rethinking stage, the generated response is structured as y = y₁ ⊕ trigger ⊕ y₂, where a rethinking trigger (e.g., 'Wait, let's double check') is appended to the initial response y₁ to elicit a self-verification or self-correction segment y₂. Training uses a binary correctness reward (1 if the boxed answer matches ground truth, 0 otherwise) with 8 sampled responses per query.

## Method

The method operates in two RL stages on Qwen2.5-VL-Instruct. Stage 1 adapts GRPO with Selective Sample Replay (SSR): a replay buffer stores only samples with non-zero group advantages, and at each step the effective batch is augmented by sampling from the buffer with probability proportional to |Â|ᵅ (α=1.0), directly counteracting the vanishing-advantages problem where uniform-reward groups yield zero gradients. Stage 2 introduces Forced Rethinking: a rethinking trigger token (one of three types—self-verification, self-correction, self-questioning) is appended to a fraction q of rollouts, and the model is trained to generate a corrective second segment; an additional SFT loss is applied only to rethinking trajectories that yield the correct final answer. The key insight is that the learned 'rethinking' in VLMs functions primarily as perceptual re-verification rather than extended logical deliberation, as accuracy gains do not correlate with reasoning-chain length.

## Results

VL-Rethinker-72B achieves 80.4% on MathVista and 63.5% on MathVerse, surpassing OpenAI-o1 (73.9% and 57.0%) by 6.5 and 6.5 points respectively, and outperforming its base model Qwen2.5-VL-72B (74.8% and 57.2%) by 5.6 and 6.3 points. On multi-disciplinary benchmarks, it sets new open-source SoTA on MMMU-Pro (55.9%), EMMA (38.5%), and MEGA-Bench (51.3%), though it still trails o1 on MathVision (44.9% vs 60.3%), MMMU (68.8% vs 78.2%), and EMMA (38.5% vs 45.7%). The 7B variant also leads all 7B-scale reasoning VLMs, achieving 74.9% on MathVista and 54.2% on MathVerse.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest proprietary model OpenAI-o1, VL-Rethinker-72B wins decisively on MathVista (80.4 vs 73.9, +6.5) and MathVerse (63.5 vs 57.0, +6.5) but trails on MathVision (44.9 vs 60.3), MMMU-Pro (55.9 vs 62.4), MMMU (68.8 vs 78.2), EMMA (38.5 vs 45.7), and MEGA (51.3 vs 56.2). Among open-source 72B+ models, VL-Rethinker outperforms Qwen2.5-VL-72B (its base) by 5.6 points on MathVista and 6.3 on MathVerse, and beats InternVL-2.5-78B, QvQ-72B, and Llava-OV-72B across all seven benchmarks. On MMMU-Pro, it surpasses Llama4-Scout-109B (55.9 vs 52.2) and Qwen2.5-VL-72B (55.9 vs 51.6), establishing a new open-source record. The 7B variant (VL-Rethinker-7B) outperforms all 7B reasoning VLMs—OpenVLThinker-7B (70.2), R1-OneVision-7B (64.1), R1-VL-7B (63.5), and MM-Eureka-8B (67.1)—by 4.7 to 11.4 points on MathVista, confirming the approach scales effectively to smaller models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GRPO algorithm from DeepSeek-R1 to the vision-language domain, identifying and addressing the vanishing advantages problem that arises when binary rewards produce uniform group scores. The Selective Sample Replay mechanism is directly inspired by Prioritized Experience Replay from TD learning, repurposing it for policy optimization in VLMs. Unlike concurrent multimodal reasoning efforts (R1-OneVision, OpenVLThinker, MM-Eureka) that rely on SFT distillation from stronger text-only models before RL, this work pursues a direct RL-only pipeline, and introduces Forced Rethinking as a training-time intervention distinct from inference-time budget forcing in S1.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a lightweight RL-only approach without costly teacher distillation can push open-source VLMs to SOTA on math-reasoning benchmarks, narrowing the gap with proprietary slow-thinking models like o1. A key conceptual insight is that the 'rethinking' learned by VLMs functions primarily as perceptual verification (re-examining visual inputs) rather than deeper logical deliberation, reframing the bottleneck in multimodal reasoning. However, the model still lags on general multimodal benchmarks (EMMA, MEGA-Bench), and the authors acknowledge that the lack of high-quality diverse training data remains a limiting factor. An open question they raise is under what conditions SFT can genuinely benefit a subsequent RL phase for VLMs.
