## Problem

Existing 3DGS compression methods rely on over one million Gaussians because drastically reducing the primitive count makes each Gaussian more susceptible to lossy attribute compression and increases spatial irregularity, breaking the locality assumptions that entropy coding and neural fields depend on. No prior method effectively minimizes the number of Gaussians while simultaneously maintaining compressibility, leaving a gap between computational efficiency (fewer Gaussians) and storage efficiency (compact attributes).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254534/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view RGB images with COLMAP-estimated camera parameters and produces a single compressed file (as small as 4.06 MB) containing 16-bit quantized positions (G-PCC encoded), SVQ indices (Huffman + LZMA encoded), and four tiny MLPs. At inference, the representation renders novel views at 600+ FPS on an RTX 4090 using only ~0.4M Gaussians, with five storage variants (XS through XL) controlled solely by a CDF-based importance threshold τ.

## Method

OMG introduces three coupled mechanisms: (1) a local distinctiveness scoring metric that multiplies the standard blending-weight importance by the L1 distance between a Gaussian's static appearance feature and its Morton-order-approximated K-nearest neighbors, raising the score of locally unique Gaussians and enabling aggressive pruning to ~0.4M primitives; (2) a compact attribute representation where per-Gaussian static (T) and view-dependent (V) features are concatenated with a space feature F_n produced by a tiny MLP over positionally-encoded Gaussian centers, so that continuity is captured by the neural field while irregularity is handled by the per-Gaussian features, reducing the number of directly learned parameters; and (3) Sub-Vector Quantization (SVQ), which partitions each attribute vector into M sub-vectors and applies independent VQ to each with small codebooks, balancing the computation-storage tradeoff between full VQ (large codebook) and R-VQ (multiple indices). Training uses K-means initialization followed by freezing indices and fine-tuning only codebooks in the final 1K iterations to avoid per-iteration index updates.

## Results

On Mip-NeRF 360, OMG-XS achieves 27.06 PSNR / 0.807 SSIM / 0.243 LPIPS at only 4.06 MB storage and 350 FPS (612 FPS on RTX 4090), representing a ~49% storage reduction over the previous SOTA (LocoGS-S at 7.90 MB) while matching or exceeding its quality. OMG-XL reaches 27.34 PSNR / 0.819 SSIM / 0.218 LPIPS at 6.82 MB, surpassing LocoGS-L (13.89 MB) on all quality metrics with less than half the storage. The method uses only 0.43M Gaussians (vs. 1.09M for LocoGS-S), enabling 600+ FPS rendering and ~20 min training time (vs. ~1 hour for LocoGS).

## Baselines & Comparisons

OMG-XS (4.06 MB) achieves a ~49% storage reduction over LocoGS-S (7.90 MB) while matching PSNR (27.06 vs 27.04) and slightly improving SSIM (0.807 vs 0.806), though LPIPS is marginally worse (0.243 vs 0.232). OMG-XL (6.82 MB) outperforms LocoGS-L (13.89 MB) on PSNR (27.34 vs 27.33), SSIM (0.819 vs 0.814), and LPIPS (0.218 vs 0.219) while using less than half the storage, though rendering FPS is slightly lower (251 vs 270). Against HAC (16.95 MB), OMG-XL uses 60% less storage and achieves better SSIM (0.819 vs 0.807) and LPIPS (0.218 vs 0.236), though HAC retains a small PSNR edge (27.49 vs 27.34). Compared to Mini-Splatting (119.5 MB, 531K Gaussians), OMG-XS achieves ~29× compression with only 427K Gaussians and a 0.33 dB PSNR drop. On Tanks & Temples, OMG-M (3.22 MB) matches LocoGS-S (6.59 MB) in PSNR (23.52 vs 23.63) at half the storage while rendering 67% faster (555 vs 333 FPS). The method also generalizes to 3DGS-MCMC, reducing storage from 115 MB to 5.1 MB at 500K Gaussians with only 0.21 dB PSNR loss.

## Relation to Prior Work

OMG builds directly on the 3DGS rendering pipeline and the Mini-Splatting framework for aggressive Gaussian reduction, but departs from prior compression methods (LocoGS, Compact-3DGS) that rely on 1M+ Gaussians and large neural fields to exploit spatial continuity. The key conceptual shift is inverting the compression strategy: rather than compressing attributes of a large Gaussian set, OMG first minimizes the Gaussian count via a local distinctiveness importance score, then represents the resulting sparse set with a hybrid of per-Gaussian features and a tiny positional-encoding MLP. The proposed Sub-Vector Quantization occupies a middle ground between full VQ (large codebooks, high compute) and Residual VQ (multiple indices, high storage), drawing on Product Quantization ideas to balance both costs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Achieving ~50% storage reduction over the previous SOTA (LocoGS) while simultaneously using fewer Gaussians and enabling 600+ FPS on consumer GPUs makes 3DGS representations practical for bandwidth-constrained and low-end deployment scenarios. The generalizability demonstrated by applying OMG to 3DGS-MCMC suggests the attribute representation is a drop-in component for other 3DGS variants. However, the method inherits baseline limitations: on large-scale scenes (Zip-NeRF), the underlying 3DGS/Mini-Splatting densification fails to produce sufficient Gaussians, capping OMG's quality. An open question is whether combining OMG's compression with more robust densification strategies (e.g., MCMC with tuned schedules) can close this gap for city-scale or unbounded scenes.
