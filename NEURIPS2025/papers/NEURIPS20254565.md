## Problem

Linear attention models enable O(L) complexity for ultra-long sequence training, but existing sequence parallelism (SP) methods become the primary bottleneck: LASP-1 enforces serial execution across devices, and LASP-2 uses All-Gather with communication volume growing linearly in the number of devices P. No prior SP strategy achieves near-linear throughput scaling for linear attention, limiting practical training to modest sequence lengths despite the algorithmic advantage.

## Input / Output

Each device receives its local Q, K, V, G tensors (shapes L×d_k, L×d_k, L×d_v, L×d_k respectively) partitioned into N = L/C chunks, along with the chunk size C and device rank p. The method outputs the per-device attention result O (L×d_v) and a single global state tensor S (d_k × d_v) passed to the next device. Crucially, the only inter-device communication is one state tensor per device regardless of P, and the state is split into K blocks along d_k for pipelined transmission, so the communication footprint is independent of cluster size.

## Method

ZeCO introduces All-Scan, a new collective communication primitive that performs a pipelined receive-scan-send pattern: each device partitions the state tensor into K contiguous blocks along d_k, receives blocks from its predecessor, applies the cumulative decay update S_pL^(k) = (γ̃[N]^(k)ᵀ1) ⊙ S_(p-1)L^(k) + S_[N]^(k), and immediately forwards the result to its successor. This achieves the theoretically minimal communication volume (one d_k×d_v state per device, independent of P) while the pipelining hides boundary latency as K grows. All-Scan executes in a separate CUDA stream, overlapping with local intra-chunk (diagonal) attention computation so that communication and computation proceed concurrently. The only extra SP overhead is maintaining N cumulative decay vectors γ̃[n] (each of size d_k, i.e., 1/d_v of the state) and N element-wise state updates, contributing less than 1% additional cost. The paper proves that ZeCO's total runtime satisfies T_ZeCO^P(PL) ≈ T_ideal-SP^1(L) + τ(d_k×d_v), establishing it as the optimal SP strategy for linear attention.

## Results

On a 1B-parameter GLA model with 16K sequence length per GPU, ZeCO achieves 44,497 tokens/sec/GPU on 8 GPUs, approaching the DP baseline of 47,594, while LASP1 and LASP2 achieve only 26,428 and 37,812 respectively. At 256 GPUs (8M total sequence), ZeCO sustains 34,400 tokens/sec/GPU versus 15,196 for LASP2, yielding a ~61% throughput speedup over the SOTA SP method. At the operator level (16K sequence, 128 GPUs), ZeCO completes forward+backward in 9.88 ms versus 113.71 ms for LASP1 and 35.72 ms for LASP2, with only ~3 ms overhead over the single-device DP lower bound of 6.55 ms.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ZeCO is compared against three baselines: LASP1 (serial P2P state passing), LASP2 (All-Gather of all intermediate states), and the GLA data-parallel (DP) operator serving as the theoretical upper bound. Against LASP1, ZeCO achieves a 11.5× operator speedup at 128 GPUs (9.88 ms vs. 113.71 ms) because LASP1's serial dependency causes runtime to grow linearly with device count. Against LASP2, ZeCO achieves a 3.6× operator speedup at 128 GPUs (9.88 ms vs. 35.72 ms) and a 61% model-throughput gain at 256 GPUs with 8M total sequence (40,967 vs. 25,402 tokens/sec/GPU at 32K per GPU), since LASP2's All-Gather communication volume scales as (P−1)·d_k·d_v. Against the DP upper bound, ZeCO incurs only ~3 ms extra at 128 GPUs (9.88 ms vs. 6.55 ms) and retains ~80% of DP throughput at 256 GPUs, confirming near-linear scalability. In raw communication time, All-Scan (ZeCO's primitive) is 4× faster than All-Gather (LASP2's primitive) at 256 GPUs (2.16 ms vs. 8.51 ms), while All-Gather (Megatron-style) suffers OOM at 128+ GPUs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the LASP1 (serial P2P state passing) and LASP2 (All-Gather) sequence parallelism methods for linear attention, as well as the chunk-wise Gated Linear Attention (GLA) formulation from Flash Linear Attention. It departs from both by introducing All-Scan, a new pipelined collective communication primitive that exploits the specific dependency structure of linear attention (each device only needs its predecessor's final state) to achieve the theoretically minimal communication volume, in contrast to LASP2's O(P) All-Gather or LASP1's serial execution. The communication-computation overlap strategy draws on ideas from Ring Attention and Triton-Distributed, but applies them to the fundamentally different (linear rather than quadratic) computational structure of linear attention.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ZeCO's near-linear scaling (1M tokens on 64 GPUs ≈ 16k on 1 GPU) and 60% speedup over SOTA at 256 GPUs/8M tokens establish a clear path to training linear attention models on previously intractable sequence lengths, which is critical as the field moves toward million-token contexts. Key limitations include evaluation only at 1B scale, restriction to diagonal-decay linear attention (not matrix-transform structures like Mamba-2), and absence of error bars. Open questions include whether All-Scan generalizes to hybrid attention architectures, whether tree-like or other topologies can further reduce the boundary latency term, and how ZeCO interacts with tensor parallelism at scale.
