## Problem

Existing approximate machine unlearning methods exhibit significant hyperparameter sensitivity and unstable performance when applied across different forget sets, datasets, or model architectures, requiring meticulous per-scenario tuning that limits practical deployment. The paper identifies that the root cause lies in the shared optimizer state (momentum) being contaminated by the large, variable-magnitude gradients from the forgetting objective, and the lack of adaptive learning rate handling for these gradients.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained model's parameters, a forget set D_f, and a retain set D_r, and produces unlearned model parameters θ_u that approximate a model retrained from scratch on D_r alone. It is a plug-and-play optimizer replacement: the loss functions L_f and L_r are defined by the underlying MU algorithm (e.g., SFRon, SalUn, SCRUB), and DualOptim only changes how the alternating gradient updates are applied. For LLMs, it introduces roughly 1.5× memory overhead for optimizer states under full-parameter tuning, though LoRA integration reduces this to near-zero overhead.

## Method

DualOptim replaces the single shared optimizer in existing MU algorithms with two decoupled optimizers: Adam (with adaptive per-parameter learning rates) for the forgetting objective and the original pretraining optimizer (e.g., SGD) for the retaining objective. The key mechanism is that each optimizer maintains its own independent momentum buffer, so the forget momentum is never contaminated by retain gradients and vice versa, preventing the large-magnitude forget gradients from destabilizing the retain update. A theoretical analysis under Lipschitz smoothness and bounded-correlation assumptions proves that decoupled momentum yields strictly smaller worst-case parameter variance than shared momentum at every iteration, which translates to lower variance in downstream metrics (FA, RA, TA, MIA). The approach is generic and requires only swapping the optimizer call in the alternating update loop of any MU method that minimizes forget and retain losses separately.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (10% random subset, ResNet-18), SFRon+DualOptim achieves the best average gap with retraining (0.44%) and the lowest standard deviation (0.66%), compared to SFRon alone (gap 0.51%, std 2.33%). DualOptim also reduces the average gap for SCRUB from 2.33% to 0.88% and for SalUn from 3.47% to 0.93%. On TinyImageNet (Swin-T), SFRon+DualOptim narrows the gap from 3.73% to 2.68% while reducing std from 1.16% to 1.01%. For LLM unlearning on TOFU, ME+GD+DualOptim achieves the highest average score (0.8470) on LLaMA-2 with 10% forgetting, outperforming all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DualOptim consistently improves upon all three primary MU baselines it is integrated with. For SFRon [7] (NeurIPS 2024), the strongest baseline, DualOptim reduces the average standard deviation from 2.33% to 0.66% (a 3.5× stability gain) while slightly narrowing the gap with retraining from 0.51% to 0.44%. For SCRUB [8] (NeurIPS 2023), the gap drops from 2.33% to 0.88%, and for SalUn [6] (ICLR 2024), from 3.47% to 0.93%. The simple baselines—FT [50], GA [21], and RL [22]—are all substantially outperformed by every DualOptim-enhanced method on both gap and stability metrics. In image generation (Table 2), SFRon+DualOptim achieves the best FID across all five CIFAR-10 classes (e.g., 17.26 for Truck vs. 20.61 for SFRon) while maintaining 0.00% FA, outperforming SA [35] and SalUn [6]. For LLM unlearning on TOFU, ME+GD+DualOptim surpasses NPO+GD [43] and all methods from [9] (ME+GD, DPO+GD, IDK+AP) on the average metric across all three forgetting ratios for both Phi-1.5 and LLaMA-2.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper does not build on a single prior method but rather repurposes standard stochastic optimization concepts—adaptive learning rates (Adam) and decoupled momentum—into the machine unlearning setting, where prior work (SFRon, SalUn, SCRUB, ME+GD, IDK+AP) has uniformly relied on a single shared optimizer (typically SGD) for both forget and retain objectives. The key conceptual departure is treating the forget and retain gradients as fundamentally different in magnitude and correlation structure, justifying separate optimizer states. The theoretical contribution (variance bound comparison between shared and decoupled momentum) is novel in the MU literature, drawing on stochastic optimization analysis to formalize why decoupling reduces parameter variance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DualOptim addresses a practical bottleneck in MU deployment: the need for meticulous, scenario-specific hyperparameter tuning. As a plug-and-play optimizer swap, it lowers the barrier to applying MU in production settings across vision and NLP tasks. The main limitation is the ~1.5× memory overhead from maintaining two optimizer state buffers, which is significant for LLMs (mitigated but not eliminated by LoRA). Open questions include whether more memory-efficient decoupling schemes exist and whether the theoretical assumptions (non-positive cross-gradient correlation, bounded Lipschitz constants) hold universally across architectures and forgetting scenarios.
