## Problem

LangSplat, the state-of-the-art 3D language Gaussian splatting method, still cannot achieve real-time open-vocabulary 3D querying (8.2 FPS on A100) due to a heavyweight MLP decoder that maps low-dimensional latent features back to 512-dimensional CLIP space. This decoding stage accounts for 97% of total query time, severely limiting deployment in latency-sensitive applications such as AR navigation and interactive robotics.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255798/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Given a text query, a trained 3D Gaussian scene (with per-Gaussian sparse coefficients and a shared global codebook), and a camera pose, the method renders a 1536-dimensional CLIP feature map (three 512-D SAM semantic levels) at high resolution (1080p) in 2.6 ms total. The rendered feature map is then post-processed into either a single 3D localization point or a binary segmentation mask. Notably, the actual CUDA alpha-blending operates on only 12 dimensions (K=4 × 3 levels) despite producing the full 1536-D output, decoupling rendering cost from feature dimensionality.

## Method

LangSplatV2 replaces LangSplat's autoencoder (3-D latent features + MLP decoder) with a 3D sparse coefficient field and a global codebook of L=64 basis vectors. Each Gaussian's 512-D language feature is expressed as a sparse linear combination of only K=4 codebook vectors, so rendering the full D-dimensional CLIP field reduces to rendering L-dimensional sparse coefficients followed by a single matrix multiplication with the codebook (0.1 ms). To further accelerate the coefficient rendering, a CUDA-optimized sparse splatting kernel performs alpha-blending exclusively on the K non-zero elements per Gaussian, reducing per-thread complexity from O(|N|·L) to O(|N|·K) and bringing the effective rendering dimension down to 12. Training proceeds in two stages: 30K iterations of standard RGB 3D-GS fitting, then 10K iterations to learn the sparse coefficient field and codebook jointly with all other Gaussian parameters frozen, supervised by 2D CLIP features extracted from SAM masks at three hierarchical semantic scales.

## Results

On the LERF dataset, LangSplatV2 achieves 84.1% overall 3D object localization accuracy and 59.9% mean IoU for 3D semantic segmentation, outperforming LangSplat (51.4% IoU) and GAGS (54.1% IoU) on segmentation while matching LangSplat on localization (84.3%). On 3D-OVS, it reaches 94.6% mean IoU, and on Mip-NeRF360 it achieves 69.4% mean IoU versus LangSplat's 57.3%. Most critically, LangSplatV2 delivers 384.6 FPS for open-vocabulary 3D querying (2.6 ms per query), a 47× speedup over LangSplat's 8.2 FPS (122.1 ms), by eliminating the heavyweight MLP decoder and using CUDA-optimized sparse coefficient splatting with an effective rendering dimension of only 12.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LangSplat (the direct predecessor), LangSplatV2 trades a marginal 0.2% drop in LERF localization accuracy (84.1% vs 84.3%, attributed to a 2-prediction gap in the small Kitchen scene) for a substantial 8.5-point gain in LERF segmentation IoU (59.9% vs 51.4%) and a 47× inference speedup (2.6 ms vs 122.1 ms per query). On 3D-OVS, LangSplatV2 improves over LangSplat by 1.2 points (94.6% vs 93.4% IoU), and on Mip-NeRF360 it gains 12.1 points (69.4% vs 57.3% IoU). Compared to GAGS, LangSplatV2 wins on segmentation across all three datasets (59.9% vs 54.1% on LERF, 94.6% vs N/A on 3D-OVS, 69.4% vs 64.5% on Mip-NeRF360) while GAGS edges ahead on LERF localization (81.7% vs 84.1% is actually LangSplatV2 winning; GAGS is 81.7%). Against LEGaussian, LangSplatV2 dominates on every metric: 35.3-point IoU gain on LERF (59.9% vs 24.6%), 6.1-point gain on 3D-OVS (94.6% vs 88.5%), 40.3-point gain on Mip-NeRF360 (69.4% vs 29.1%), while also being 14× faster (2.6 ms vs 36.7 ms query time). GOI, GS-Grouping, LERF, 3D-OVS, and FFD are all substantially outperformed, with LangSplatV2 leading by 22–68 IoU points depending on the dataset and task.

## Relation to Prior Work

LangSplatV2 is a direct successor to LangSplat (Qin et al., CVPR 2024), which itself extended LERF (Kerr et al., ICCV 2023) from NeRF-based to 3D Gaussian Splatting-based 3D language fields. The key conceptual departure is replacing LangSplat's scene-specific autoencoder (which compresses 512-D CLIP features to 3-D latents and requires a heavyweight MLP decoder at inference) with a 3D sparse coefficient field over a shared global codebook, eliminating the decoder entirely. This draws on vector quantization ideas from 3D-GS compression work (Compressed 3D-GS, LightGaussian, CompGS) but repurposes them not for storage reduction but for decoupling rendering dimensionality from feature dimensionality. Compared to LEGaussians (Shi et al., CVPR 2024), which also uses a codebook, LangSplatV2 learns the codebook directly in 3D space (vs. 2D), removes the MLP projection, and exploits sparsity (K=4 non-zeros out of L=64) in a CUDA-optimized alpha-blending kernel.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 47× speedup (8.2 → 384.6 FPS) makes real-time 3D open-vocabulary querying practical for AR navigation, interactive robotics, and educational tools where sub-10ms latency is essential. The sparse coefficient splatting technique—rendering 1536-D features at the cost of 12-D splatting—is a general principle that could transfer to other high-dimensional feature rendering tasks beyond language fields. Limitations include a 3× increase in training time (3h vs 1h), inheritance of CLIP's inherent biases, and evaluation limited to indoor scenes; open questions include scalability to large outdoor scenes and whether the codebook approach generalizes to other foundation-model feature spaces (e.g., DINO, SigLIP).
