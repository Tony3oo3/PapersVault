## Problem

Diffusion-based super-resolution methods that use text conditioning suffer from semantic hallucinations because cross-attention diverts text tokens to irrelevant image regions, degradation-aware prompt extractors still produce incorrect tags under severe degradation, and incomplete tag coverage leaves ungrounded regions vulnerable to unrelated text influence. These three limitations collectively cause semantic misalignment and hallucinated details in the restored high-resolution outputs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254557/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single low-resolution RGB image (e.g., 128×128 from RealSR or 512×512 from DRealSR/DIV2K-Val, degraded via the Real-ESRGAN pipeline) and produces a super-resolved high-resolution image. It operates entirely at inference time as a plug-and-play module: the LR image is first processed by DAPE to extract text tags, then by Grounded SAM to produce tag-mask pairs, and these are injected into the cross-attention layers of an existing diffusion-based SR model (SeeSR or OSEDiff) without any retraining. The additional preprocessing (Grounded SAM) adds only ~0.12–0.16 s per image on a V100 GPU.

## Method

SRSR introduces two inference-time mechanisms that modify how text conditioning is applied within a pre-trained diffusion SR model. First, Spatially Re-focused Cross-Attention (SRCA) grounds DAPE-extracted tags to segmentation masks via Grounded SAM, then applies a binary mask to the cross-attention weights (α_ij^SRCA = M_ij · α_ij) followed by re-normalization, so each text token attends exclusively to its grounded spatial region and irrelevant tokens are suppressed. Second, Spatially Targeted Classifier-Free Guidance (STCFG) constructs an ungrounded mask as the complement of all grounded masks and selectively applies CFG only to grounded pixels, while ungrounded pixels receive purely unconditional noise prediction, preventing summary tokens (EOS, padding) from injecting spurious semantics into uncovered regions. The entire framework adds no learnable parameters and is compatible with any cross-attention-based SR pipeline that uses text priors, demonstrated on both 50-step SeeSR and 1-step OSEDiff.

## Results

SRSR-SeeSR (50-step) achieves the best full-reference fidelity on all three datasets: PSNR 26.40 / SSIM 0.7632 on RealSR, PSNR 24.72 / SSIM 0.6416 on DIV2K-Val, and PSNR 29.50 / SSIM 0.8128 on DRealSR. It also attains the best full-reference perceptual quality (LPIPS 0.2718, DISTS 0.2092 on RealSR; LPIPS 0.2866, DISTS 0.2176 on DRealSR), ranking second only on DIV2K-Val (LPIPS 0.3275 vs. SeeSR's 0.3194; DISTS 0.1991 vs. 0.1968). The single-step variant SRSR-OSEDiff also improves fidelity over its OSEDiff baseline on every dataset (e.g., PSNR 24.53 vs. 24.43 on RealSR, 27.72 vs. 27.65 on DRealSR) with only marginal perceptual trade-offs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SRSR-SeeSR outperforms all seven baselines on PSNR and SSIM across every dataset, with the largest gains over SeeSR (its own backbone): +1.22 dB PSNR and +0.042 SSIM on RealSR, +1.04 dB and +0.037 on DIV2K-Val, and +1.33 dB and +0.044 on DRealSR. On the two real-world benchmarks (RealSR, DRealSR), SRSR-SeeSR also achieves the best LPIPS and DISTS, beating the next-best SeeSR by 0.029/0.013 (RealSR) and 0.032/0.014 (DRealSR); on synthetic DIV2K-Val it ranks second in LPIPS/DISTS behind SeeSR by a narrow margin (0.3275 vs. 0.3194; 0.1991 vs. 0.1968). Against non-text-conditioned methods, SRSR-SeeSR leads ResShift by +0.09 dB PSNR on RealSR and +1.04 dB on DIV2K-Val, while also improving LPIPS by 0.074 and 0.007 respectively. The single-step SRSR-OSEDiff variant improves fidelity over OSEDiff on all datasets (e.g., +0.07 dB PSNR on RealSR, +0.07 dB on DRealSR) with only slight LPIPS/DISTS regressions, confirming SRCA's standalone value. The paper notes that no-reference metrics (NIQE, MUSIQ, MANIQA, CLIPIQA) often favor hallucinated outputs, so SRSR scores lower on those despite superior semantic fidelity, and the authors argue full-reference metrics are more reliable indicators of restoration quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the text-conditioned diffusion-based SR line initiated by SeeSR (which introduced the degradation-aware prompt extractor DAPE) and OSEDiff (a one-step variant), both of which inject text priors via standard cross-attention in a Stable Diffusion pipeline. SRSR departs from these by identifying that cross-attention tokens leak onto irrelevant regions and that ungrounded pixels remain vulnerable to spurious text influence, then proposing two inference-time modifications—Spatially Re-focused Cross-Attention (SRCA) and Spatially Targeted CFG (STCFG)—that constrain text conditioning to visually grounded regions using segmentation masks from Grounded SAM. It also explicitly contrasts with HolisDiP, which uses Mask2Former for full-image coverage but lacks degradation awareness, and shows that adding such segmentation tags actually degrades performance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play, training-free design makes SRSR immediately applicable to any cross-attention-based text-conditioned SR method, lowering the barrier to improved semantic fidelity in real-world restoration. The paper's central insight—that inaccurate tags are more harmful than incomplete coverage—challenges the prevailing assumption that broader semantic coverage is always beneficial, and the consistent finding that no-reference metrics (NIQE, MUSIQ, MANIQA, CLIPIQA) systematically reward hallucinated outputs raises important questions about how the field evaluates SR quality. Open questions include whether incorporating SRSR into training (rather than inference-only) could further improve region-specific supervision, and whether degradation-aware segmentation models could close the remaining coverage gap without sacrificing accuracy.
