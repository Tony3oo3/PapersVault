## Problem

Existing EEG foundation models adopt pretraining strategies from language and time-series domains without accounting for EEG-specific properties: they ignore the 3D physical arrangement of electrodes, rely on random masking that degrades to trivial local interpolation, fail to exploit regional brain features during fine-tuning, and lack architectural mechanisms to adapt to the heterogeneous temporal dynamics (from slow sleep waves to rapid seizure spikes) present across diverse recording setups.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255043/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes raw multivariate EEG signals (T time steps × D channels, preprocessed to 64 Hz with 4-second windows and A-law normalization) along with the 3D physical coordinates of each electrode from the international 10-20 system. Unlike patch-based approaches, NeurIPT uses single-point embeddings to preserve sharp transient features critical for brain activity analysis. During pretraining it outputs reconstructed masked signal segments; during fine-tuning it produces class labels via an MLP head on the IILP-pooled multi-scale representation.

## Method

NeurIPT builds on a modified Crossformer backbone (Pre-LN, SwiGLU, FlashAttention-2) with four key innovations. Spatially, it encodes each electrode's 3D coordinates via sinusoidal positional embeddings, enabling transfer across different montages without retraining. Temporally, Amplitude-Aware Masked Pretraining (AAMP) selects masking intervals centered on randomly sampled amplitude percentiles rather than random positions, forcing the model to learn global signal structure rather than local interpolation. A Progressive Mixture-of-Experts (PMoE) module progressively increases the number of specialized FFN experts with depth (e.g., [0,0,2,4,4,6] across six encoder layers), with a shared expert for stable generalization and TopKSoftmax routing for sparse activation. During fine-tuning, Intra-Inter Lobe Pooling (IILP) first average-pools channels within functional brain lobes, then concatenates lobe-level embeddings across all encoder layers, explicitly leveraging region-specific brain activity patterns.

## Results

NeurIPT achieves state-of-the-art balanced accuracy across 7 of 8 downstream BCI datasets, with the largest gains on MentalArithmetic (86.46%, +13.90 over CBraMod), BCIC-IV-2A (55.04%, +3.66), and PhysioP300 (67.31%, +2.29). On Sleep-EDFx, NeurIPT reaches 70.47% balanced accuracy and 87.39% weighted F1, improving over EEGPT by +1.30 and +10.85 respectively. The only exception is TUAB, where NeurIPT matches CBraMod on balanced accuracy (82.93 vs 82.89) but trails on Cohen's Kappa (90.40 vs 92.58) and weighted F1 (89.49 vs 92.27).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest recent foundation model CBraMod (ICLR 2025), NeurIPT wins on 6 of 7 shared datasets, with the most dramatic margin on MentalArithmetic (+13.90 balanced accuracy) and BCIC-IV-2A (+3.66), while essentially tying on TUAB balanced accuracy (82.93 vs 82.89) but losing on Cohen's Kappa (−2.18) and weighted F1 (−2.78). Compared to EEGPT (NeurIPS 2024), NeurIPT improves balanced accuracy on PhysioP300 (+2.29) and Sleep-EDFx (+1.30), with a particularly large weighted F1 gain on Sleep-EDFx (+10.85). Against LaBraM (ICLR 2024) and BIOT (NeurIPS 2023), NeurIPT consistently outperforms across all shared datasets, with gaps ranging from +0.13 (SEED-V vs CBraMod) to +13.90 (MentalArithmetic vs CBraMod). On TUAB, NeuroLM (ICLR 2025) is notably weaker than both CBraMod and NeurIPT, suggesting that language-aligned tokenization is less effective for seizure detection than spatially-aware architectures. Overall, NeurIPT's 3D electrode embedding and IILP pooling appear most beneficial for tasks requiring explicit spatial reasoning (motor imagery, P300), while the AAMP pretraining strategy yields the largest absolute gains on cognitively demanding tasks like mental stress detection.

## Relation to Prior Work

NeurIPT builds directly on the Crossformer backbone [26] and the self-supervised masked pretraining paradigm established by BERT [22] and MAE [13], while incorporating the Mixture-of-Experts routing mechanism from Shazeer et al. [27]. It departs from prior EEG foundation models (BENDR, BIOT, LaBraM, EEGPT, CBraMod) in four specific ways: replacing channel-agnostic positional encodings with 3D electrode-coordinate embeddings, substituting random contiguous masking with amplitude-guided masking, introducing a progressive (depth-increasing) expert schedule rather than uniform or absent MoE, and adding lobe-aware spatial pooling during fine-tuning. The paper explicitly frames these as corrections to identified shortcomings in the existing EEG FM literature—spatial information loss, interpolation bias in random masking, and underutilization of regional brain anatomy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

NeurIPT provides a relatively compact (73.5M activated parameters) foundation model that achieves consistent SOTA across eight heterogeneous BCI tasks, suggesting that architectural inductive biases (3D spatial encoding, amplitude-aware masking, progressive MoE) can substitute for raw parameter scale in EEG representation learning. However, gains are modest on several datasets (SEED-V +0.13, TUAB negative on two metrics), and the model has not been tested at the scale of NeuroLM-XL (1.7B) or LaBraM-Huge (369M), leaving open whether these design choices remain advantageous at larger scale. A key open question is whether the amplitude-aware masking and lobe pooling strategies generalize beyond the 20-channel 'double banana' montage used in evaluation, and whether the progressive MoE schedule transfers to other biosignal modalities.
