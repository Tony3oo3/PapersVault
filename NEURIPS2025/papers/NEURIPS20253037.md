## Problem

Existing HOI generation methods treat human and object motions independently or rely on high-level contact constraints, failing to model the causal interaction dynamics that govern how objects should physically respond to human actions. This leads to premature object motion, exaggerated post-contact responses, and temporally inconsistent behaviors that violate basic physical causality.

## Input / Output

The method takes a CLIP-encoded text prompt, a 1024×3 Basis Point Set encoding object geometry, initial human (SMPL-X) and object poses, sparse object waypoints (x-y coordinates every 30 frames), and 4-dimensional binary hand/foot contact annotations as conditioning. It produces a synchronized sequence of SMPL-X human poses (204-dim per frame: 24 joint translations + 22 joint rotations + 16 shape parameters) and object motions (12-dim per frame: 3D centroid translation + 9D relative rotation), where the object rotation is constrained to SO(3) via SVD projection. The interaction dynamics model operates only during training as an auxiliary signal, so inference requires no additional forward passes beyond the diffusion denoising.

## Method

HOI-Dyn formulates HOI generation as a driver-responder system where human motion drives object response, and introduces a lightweight 0.5M-parameter transformer-based interaction dynamics model that predicts object rigid-body motion (rotation + translation) from human relative motion, current object state, and interaction context over a randomly sampled multi-step horizon K. The key training innovation is a residual-based dynamics loss that compares the dynamics model's prediction error on the generated sequence against its error on the ground-truth sequence, rather than directly penalizing the generated motion; this subtraction cancels the model's systematic bias under the assumption of local smoothness and temporal homogeneity, yielding a robust supervisory signal even when the dynamics model is imperfect. The dynamics model is integrated into a CHOIS-based conditional diffusion framework as an auxiliary loss alongside the standard reconstruction and object-level losses, all equally weighted, with the dynamics model discarded at inference to preserve generation speed.

## Results

On FullBodyManipulation, HOI-Dyn achieves the best FID (0.48 vs. 0.87 for CHOIS), highest contact F1 (0.71 vs. 0.66), lowest object position error T_obj (12.47 cm vs. 14.29 cm), and lowest object orientation error R_obj (0.90° vs. 0.99°). It also improves foot sliding (0.37 vs. 0.41) and MPJPE (15.60 vs. 16.01) over CHOIS. On the unseen 3D-FUTURE dataset, HOI-Dyn further improves contact percentage (0.54 vs. 0.47) and FID (1.62 vs. 1.67) over CHOIS, confirming generalization to novel objects.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HOI-Dyn outperforms the primary SOTA baseline CHOIS on 11 of 13 metrics, with the most notable gains in FID (0.48 vs. 0.87, a 45% reduction), contact F1 (0.71 vs. 0.66), and object position error T_obj (12.47 vs. 14.29 cm). It slightly trails CHOIS on intermediate waypoint alignment T_xy (3.26 vs. 3.03 cm) and hand penetration P_hand (0.64 vs. 0.61), suggesting a minor trade-off in contact tightness. Against the OMOMO variants, HOI-Dyn matches or exceeds GT-OMOMO on human motion quality (FID 0.48 vs. 5.69) while achieving competitive object accuracy without requiring ground-truth object motion as input. Compared to InterDiff and MDM, HOI-Dyn is dramatically superior across all metrics, with FID improvements of over 400× and 12× respectively, reflecting the advantage of the conditional diffusion architecture with interaction dynamics supervision.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the CHOIS conditional diffusion framework for HOI generation, reusing its architecture, condition representation (CLIP text + BPS object geometry + sparse waypoints), and training pipeline. The key conceptual departure is the driver-responder formulation borrowed from control-theory synchronization, which reframes HOI as a causal system where human actions drive object responses rather than treating both as jointly generated. The lightweight transformer-based interaction dynamics model and the residual-based dynamics loss (which cancels model bias via subtraction of generated vs. ground-truth residuals) are novel contributions not present in prior HOI diffusion methods like HOI Diff, CG-HOI, THOR, or HIMO, which focus on contact fields, affordances, or relational cues without explicit causal dynamics modeling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a persistent weakness in HOI generation — objects moving independently of human actions or exhibiting physically implausible responses — by providing a principled, differentiable surrogate for object dynamics that integrates seamlessly into diffusion training. The residual dynamics loss is a practical and generalizable technique for leveraging imperfect auxiliary models as supervisory signals, with the bias-cancellation property making it robust to approximation errors. However, the framework is limited to rigid objects, coarse SMPL-X hand representations, and single-object interactions; open questions include extending to deformable objects, multi-object/multi-human scenarios, and tighter integration with physics simulators for force-level realism. The dynamics model also serves as a post-hoc evaluation metric for causal consistency, which could become a useful standard for the field.
