## Problem

Existing contrastive learning methods for neural time-series data rely on generic augmentations (amplitude jitter, temporal shifts, additive noise) that fail to capture the specific multi-trial noise structure of neural recordings, and they produce high-dimensional embeddings requiring a separate dimensionality reduction step for visualization. This paper addresses both gaps by exploiting the repeated-trial structure inherent in neuroscience experiments to create informative positive pairs and by directly learning a 2D embedding.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252667/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes multi-trial neural time series as input, where each neuron has r repeated responses (trials) to identical stimuli, with T time bins per trial (e.g., 260 bins at 8 Hz for calcium imaging, or 25 ms-binned spike trains). Positive pairs are generated by averaging two random non-overlapping subsets of k = ⌊r/2⌋ trials per neuron, dynamically resampled each epoch. At inference time, the full mean across all trials is embedded, producing a 2D point per neuron suitable for direct visualization and downstream clustering.

## Method

TRACE builds on t-SimCNE, replacing its image-based augmentations with a trial-subset-mean augmentation: for each neuron, two non-overlapping subsets of trials are averaged to form a positive pair, making the learned representation invariant to trial-to-trial fluctuations while preserving inter-neuron signal differences. The encoder is a 4-layer MLP (768→512→256→128) with a 1024-dim projection head, and the key architectural choice is the t-SNE-inspired Cauchy kernel q(z,z̃) = (1+‖z−z̃‖²)⁻¹ as the similarity function in the 2D embedding space, rather than cosine similarity on a hypersphere. Training uses the InfoNCE loss with negative pairs drawn from other neurons in the batch. This design automatically identifies the local noise structure per neuron (since subset means only differ in the high-noise, low-signal portions of the time series), allowing TRACE to focus on class-informative response segments that generic augmentations or distance-based methods (t-SNE, UMAP) fail to resolve.

## Results

On the superior colliculus two-photon calcium imaging dataset (71,021 neurons), TRACE achieves the best ARI score (0.28) and the best average rank (μRank = 2.17) across all evaluation metrics, outperforming CEED→PCA (ARI 0.20, μRank 4.00), TS2Vec→PCA (ARI 0.04, μRank 6.83), t-SNE (ARI 0.24, μRank 3.00), and UMAP (ARI 0.23, μRank 4.00). TRACE also achieves the highest correlation with the response transience index (rRTi = 0.26) and recording depth (rDepth = 0.54). On the Allen Institute Neuropixels spiking dataset (10,322 neurons), TRACE again achieves the best average rank (μRank = 1.8), outperforming all baselines on most biologically meaningful metrics including orientation selective index (OSI = 0.38) and behavioral modulation (0.11).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CEED→PCA, TRACE wins decisively on ARI (0.28 vs. 0.20), kNN accuracy (69.7% vs. 60.7%), ON-OFF index correlation (0.67 vs. 0.54), and response transience index (0.26 vs. 0.17), while CEED→PCA marginally edges out on recording depth (0.55 vs. 0.54). Against TS2Vec→PCA, TRACE dominates on every metric, with particularly large gaps in ARI (0.28 vs. 0.04), kNN accuracy (69.7% vs. 11.6%), and rOOi (0.67 vs. 0.12). Against t-SNE, the comparison is closer: t-SNE slightly outperforms TRACE on kNN accuracy (70.7% vs. 69.7%) and rOOi (0.69 vs. 0.67), but TRACE wins on ARI (0.28 vs. 0.24), rRTi (0.26 vs. 0.17), and rDepth (0.54 vs. 0.42). Against UMAP, TRACE wins on ARI (0.28 vs. 0.23), Spearman correlation (0.45 vs. 0.27), and rOOi (0.67 vs. 0.38), while kNN accuracy is essentially tied (69.7% vs. 69.9%) and rRTi is also tied (0.26). Overall, TRACE achieves the best aggregate rank (μRank = 2.17) and is the only method that simultaneously captures both discrete cluster structure (ARI) and continuous biological variation (rRTi, rDepth) well.

## Relation to Prior Work

TRACE builds directly on t-SimCNE (Böhm et al., ICLR 2023), which adapted the SimCLR contrastive framework to produce 2D visualizations via a t-SNE-inspired Cauchy kernel, and on the broader SimCLR paradigm of learning representations by contrasting augmented views. The key conceptual departure is the augmentation strategy: rather than generic time-series transforms (amplitude/temporal jitter, correlated noise) used by CEED or hierarchical sub-segment contrasting used by TS2Vec, TRACE exploits the multi-trial structure of neuroscience experiments by averaging random non-overlapping subsets of trials to form positive pairs. This positions TRACE between contrastive representation learning (which typically outputs high-dimensional embeddings requiring a separate reduction step) and classical neighbor-embedding methods like t-SNE/UMAP (which are vulnerable to the curse of dimensionality in high-dimensional neural data).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TRACE offers a principled, domain-aware alternative to both generic contrastive methods and classical dimensionality reduction for visualizing large-scale neural recordings, with the practical advantage of directly producing interpretable 2D embeddings that capture both discrete cell-type clusters and continuous biological variation (e.g., ON-OFF index, response transience, recording depth). The trial-averaging augmentation is a natural fit for the standard multi-trial recording paradigm in neuroscience and avoids a specific failure mode of covariance-based augmentations (CEED) that can destroy class structure by mixing inter-class variability into the noise model. Key limitations include the requirement for repeated trials (ruling out single-trial or adaptation-over-trials analyses), the assumption that between-trial variation is noise rather than signal, and the added computational cost of training versus t-SNE/UMAP. The authors note promising extensions to other multi-trial domains (sports analytics, speech, ECG, financial data), but these remain untested.
