## Problem

Spiking neural networks suffer from severe temporal feature redundancy due to shared weights across timesteps, which inflates spiking firing rates and degrades energy efficiency. Existing redundancy analyses rely on indirect or linear measures (e.g., cosine similarity of membrane potentials) that cannot capture the complex, non-linear statistical dependencies inherent in discrete binary spike features, leaving a gap in directly quantifying and reducing this redundancy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255098/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The module operates on four-dimensional binary spike tensors S ∈ {0,1}^{T×C×H×W} extracted after the final convolutional stage of an SNN, where T is the number of timesteps. It produces masked spike features (with pixel-wise redundant spikes zeroed out) and a weight-recalibrated final representation used for classification or forecasting. The spike removal is applied only during training; at inference, the network runs with its learned sparse firing patterns without any additional overhead.

## Method

MI-TRQR quantifies temporal redundancy between spike features at two spatial scales using Mutual Information: a global MI over the entire spatial feature and a local MI computed per pixel across channels. These are normalized by the self-MI (I(S₁;S₁)) and combined with a training-epoch factor p^e = e/E to yield a pixel-wise removal probability p_{t,h,w}. A Bernoulli-sampled binary mask is then generated from this probability to stochastically remove redundant spikes at each spatial location for timesteps t > 1, while the first timestep is always preserved. Finally, the standard uniform temporal averaging is replaced by a firing-rate-weighted aggregation (α_t proportional to the network spiking firing rate at timestep t) to compensate for the non-uniform information distribution introduced by spike removal. The entire module is parameter-free, plug-and-play, and introduces no extra inference cost.

## Results

On CIFAR10-DVS with T=4, MI-TRQR achieves 83.9% accuracy at 0.45 mJ, surpassing PSN (82.3%, 0.72 mJ) by +1.6% accuracy while reducing energy by 37.5%. On ImageNet with SEW-ResNet50 (T=4), MI-TRQR reaches 73.23% accuracy at 3.09 mJ, outperforming PSN (72.01%, 4.11 mJ) by +1.22% accuracy and −24.8% energy. On the Electricity forecasting task, MI-TRQR attains R²=0.971 and RSE=0.316, improving over CPG-PE (R²=0.968, RSE=0.341). On DVS128 Gait (T=10), MI-TRQR achieves 90.6% accuracy at 0.13 mJ versus PSN's 88.8% at 0.19 mJ (+1.8% accuracy, −31.6% energy).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is PSN [14] (Parallel Spiking Neurons, NeurIPS 2023), which MI-TRQR consistently outperforms across all datasets and timesteps: +1.6% accuracy with −37.5% energy on CIFAR10-DVS (T=4), +1.22% with −24.8% on ImageNet (SEW-ResNet50, T=4), and +1.8% with −31.6% on Gait (T=10). On CIFAR10 (T=4), MI-TRQR achieves 95.83% at 0.35 mJ versus PSN's 95.32% at 1.32 mJ (−73.5% energy), and on CIFAR100 (ResNet19, T=4) it reaches 77.70% at 1.24 mJ versus PSN's 76.14% at 1.78 mJ. Among other SNN baselines on CIFAR10-DVS, MI-TRQR (T=4) surpasses DeepTAGE [33] (81.2%), TIM [53] (81.6%), SEMM [85] (82.9%), and RM-SNN [71] (82.9%) in accuracy while reporting the lowest energy cost (0.45 mJ); it is slightly below CLIF [21] (86.1%) and QKFormer [84] (84.0%) which use more timesteps (T=10/16) and do not report energy. On ImageNet, MI-TRQR (SEW-ResNet50, T=4) outperforms IMP+LTS [52] (71.83%, 3.11 mJ) and GAC-SNN [45] (70.42%, 3.38 mJ) in both accuracy and energy. For time-series forecasting on Electricity, MI-TRQR improves over CPG-PE [35] (NeurIPS 2024) by +0.003 R² and −0.025 RSE on average, and over Spikformer [86] (ICLR 2023) by +0.019 R² and −0.080 RSE.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of redundancy analysis and reduction in SNNs initiated by Kim et al. (TIC phenomenon), Yao et al. (systematic qualitative analysis), and Qin et al. (cosine-similarity-based spike clustering), but departs from them by introducing Mutual Information as the first direct, principled metric for quantifying redundancy between discrete binary spike features. It contrasts with attention-based redundancy reduction methods (RM-SNN, STAtten, etc.) by being parameter-free and plug-and-play, avoiding the additional multiplications and hardware costs those modules introduce. The weight recalibration step is novel in the SNN literature, addressing the non-uniform information distribution that arises after selective spike removal, whereas prior works uniformly average temporal outputs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play, parameter-free design makes MI-TRQR broadly applicable across SNN architectures (VGG, ResNet, STMixer) and tasks (neuromorphic, static image, time-series) without architectural modification, which is practically valuable for the neuromorphic deployment community where energy budgets are tight. The key limitation is increased training time (e.g., 51.9s vs. 24.1s per epoch on CIFAR10-DVS) due to MI computation, which could become prohibitive for very deep or large-scale networks. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to spatial or channel-wise redundancy, whether it composes well with other efficiency techniques (pruning, quantization), and how it scales to much longer temporal sequences (T > 16) where the O(T×H×W) local redundancy computation grows.
