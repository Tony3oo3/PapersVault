## Problem

Existing decoding-time safety interventions such as Contrastive Decoding apply uniformly to every query and every token, creating a severe safety-quality trade-off: benign responses are catastrophically degraded (quality scores dropping to near zero) while safety gains remain modest. The paper identifies that effective intervention must be targeted—activating only on malicious queries and only on the unsafe portion of a response—rather than indiscriminate across the full generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251706/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework consumes a user query and the LLM's in-flight generation state, which is split into a visible stream (tokens already shown to the user) and a hidden buffer of the most recent b tokens. The guard model receives the concatenation of query, stream, and buffer and outputs a binary safety signal; when triggered, the system reverts the LLM's internal state (KV cache) by b steps and regenerates the buffer. The user only ever sees a safe, coherent text stream—the rollback and intervention are invisible, with the only cost being a brief wait of b tokens per intervention cycle.

## Method

CARE integrates three components: a guard model for real-time safety monitoring, a token-buffered rollback mechanism that reverts the LLM's generation state (including KV cache) by b steps upon detecting unsafe content, and a novel introspection-based intervention. The detect-rollback-intervene loop transforms any always-on decoding intervention (Contrastive Decoding, ARGS) into a targeted, on-demand operation applied only to regenerate the b buffer tokens after a rollback, conditioned on the confirmed-safe prefix. The Introspection method, rather than manipulating logits, prompts the LLM to generate a self-critical reflection (e.g., '...oh I'm sorry, I just realized...') that fills the buffer and steers subsequent decoding onto a safer trajectory, leveraging emergent meta-cognitive capabilities. The framework is modular—any intervention strategy can be plugged in—and experiments show that buffer scaling (increasing b) is a far more effective safety scaling path than retry scaling (increasing N), achieving HRR ≈ 1.0 at 120 wait tokens versus a plateau around 4.5 for retries.

## Results

On BeaverTails with Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, the CARE framework's Introspection method achieves the best overall balance: a Harmful Response Rate (HRR) of 4.53%, a Response Quality win-rate of 55.95% against GPT-4o-11-20, and the lowest Average Wait Tokens (AWT) of 57.06. The detect-rollback-intervene mechanism dramatically rescues vanilla interventions: Contrastive Decoding at α=1.0 improves from HRR 6.55% / Quality 0.01 (vanilla) to HRR 4.57% / Quality 49.63 (CARE), and ARGS Decoding at β=10.0 improves from HRR 11.59% / Quality 53.12 to HRR 6.59% / Quality 56.20. Buffer scaling further reduces HRR to ~1.0% at 120 Wait Tokens, demonstrating a scalable safety-latency trade-off.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares Introspection against two strong decoding-time baselines—Contrastive Decoding [Li et al., 2022] and ARGS Decoding [Khanov et al., 2024]—both in their vanilla (always-on) form and after being enhanced by the CARE detect-rollback-intervene mechanism. Against vanilla CD at maximum strength, CARE-CD reduces HRR from 6.55% to 4.57% while restoring quality from a catastrophic 0.01 to 49.63; Introspection further improves HRR to 4.53% and quality to 55.95. Against vanilla ARGS at maximum strength, CARE-ARGS reduces HRR from 11.59% to 6.59% and increases quality from 53.12 to 56.20; Introspection then achieves a substantially lower HRR of 4.53% with nearly identical quality (55.95 vs. 56.20) and lower latency (57.06 vs. 61.06 AWT). Introspection thus wins on all three metrics (safety, quality, latency) against both CARE-enhanced baselines, with the largest margin over CARE-ARGS on HRR (4.53 vs. 6.59) and over CARE-CD on quality (55.95 vs. 49.63).

## Relation to Prior Work

CARE builds directly on decoding-time intervention methods—Contrastive Decoding [17, 35] and ARGS Decoding [15]—but departs from their always-on, uniform application by wrapping them in a targeted detect-rollback-intervene loop that activates only when a guard model flags unsafe content. The Introspection intervention strategy draws on the emerging 'thinking intervention' and self-reminder literature [36, 26, 30, 31], repurposing the model's own meta-cognitive self-critique as a context-level steering signal rather than manipulating logits or sampling parameters. The token-buffer mechanism is adapted from streaming LLM serving infrastructure, and the guard-model component is modular, connecting to the broader safety-classifier line (LlamaGuard, WildGuard, Qwen3Guard).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CARE provides a practical, deployment-oriented framework that resolves the severe safety-quality trade-off plaguing vanilla decoding-time interventions, making targeted safety correction feasible in real-time streaming applications. The finding that buffer scaling (larger foresight) is substantially more effective than retry scaling (more attempts) gives practitioners a clear, actionable guideline for allocating latency budgets. Key open questions remain: the framework's safety guarantee is only as strong as its guard model against adversarial attacks, the finite intervention budget means no absolute safety guarantee, and it is unclear whether the Introspection method generalizes beyond the single 7B-scale model and one benchmark dataset tested here.
