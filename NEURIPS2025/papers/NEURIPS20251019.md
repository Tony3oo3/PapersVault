## Problem

Existing multimodal reasoning benchmarks (MMMU, MathVista, Olympiad-Bench) primarily assess language-based reasoning where visual input serves as replaceable auxiliary context, sometimes substitutable by textual cues alone. This leaves a critical gap: no benchmark forces models to perform genuine image-grounded reasoning that goes beyond shallow perception ('see') to require fine-grained observation and analytical reasoning ('observe') over visual content itself.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251019/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each evaluation instance pairs a challenging real-world image (from 345 base images across 7 visual challenge categories) with two binary yes/no VQA questions: a main question whose ground-truth answer is 'no' and a natural adversarial counterpart whose answer is 'yes', drawn from 103 GPT-4o-edited image pairs that are visually similar but semantically opposite. Models produce a yes/no answer plus a free-form reasoning chain, which are scored on five accuracy metrics (No-Acc, Yes-Acc, Q-Acc, I-Acc, G-Acc) and a normalized CoT-Score (0–1) that rewards identification of detailed visual cues and correct inference of true reality, independent of final answer correctness.

## Method

BLINK-Twice integrates three design components: (1) seven fine-grained visual challenge categories—visual misleading, dislocation, art illusion, occlusion, forced perspective, physical illusion, and motion illusion—each exploiting a distinct mechanism of perceptual misinterpretation; (2) natural adversarial image pairs generated by prompting GPT-4o to perform localized semantic edits (e.g., adding a hamburger to empty hands) that flip the ground-truth answer while preserving visual plausibility, thereby blocking commonsense shortcut learning; and (3) five-stage annotated reasoning chains (Initial Perception → Identifying Misleading → Detailed Visual Clues → True Reality → Final Answer) produced by GPT-4o and verified by five human annotators over 60+ hours, where the 'Detailed Visual Clues' and 'True Reality' stages serve as scoring anchors for CoT quality. The benchmark is evaluated zero-shot across 20 MLLMs (12 foundation, 8 reasoning-enhanced), and the authors additionally probe multi-turn dialogue settings and o3's active visual tool use (cropping, zooming) to characterize emerging multimodal reasoning paradigms.

## Results

On BLINK-Twice, Gemini-2.5 Pro achieves the strongest overall performance with Q-Acc 0.667, I-Acc 0.470, and G-Acc 0.269, followed by o1 (Q-Acc 0.608, I-Acc 0.392) and GPT-4o (Q-Acc 0.571, I-Acc 0.351, CoT Score 0.601). Even the best-performing models remain well below saturation: I-Acc stays under 0.5 and G-Acc under 0.3 across all 20 evaluated MLLMs, indicating that complex visual reasoning remains a significant open challenge. Among open-source models, QVQ-72B leads with Q-Acc 0.575 and CoT Score 0.438, while the reasoning-enhanced Qwen-2.5-VL-32B (Q-Acc 0.578) matches or surpasses the larger 72B variant (Q-Acc 0.520).

## Baselines & Comparisons

As a benchmark paper, BLINK-Twice evaluates 20 MLLMs rather than proposing a new method, so comparisons center on model performance and the effect of reasoning augmentation. Gemini-2.5 Pro leads on Q-Acc (0.667), I-Acc (0.470), and G-Acc (0.269), outperforming o1 (0.608/0.392/0.186) and GPT-4o (0.571/0.351/0.198) on all three group-level metrics. Reasoning-enhanced variants consistently outperform their base models: QVQ-72B gains +15% Q-Acc over Qwen2-VL-72B, Claude-3.7-Sonnet-Thinking improves +20% over its non-thinking counterpart, and Gemini-2.0-Flash-Thinking surpasses Gemini-2.0-Flash by 17% on Q-Acc. However, GPT-4o achieves the highest CoT Score (0.601) despite ranking third on Q-Acc, suggesting its reasoning chains are more aligned with annotated ground-truth steps. Among open-source models, the smaller Qwen-2.5-VL-32B (Q-Acc 0.578) matches or exceeds the 72B variant (0.520), while MM-Eureka shows minimal improvement over InternVL2.5-8B, likely due to its math-focused RL training. The o3 model (not in Table 1) demonstrates a new paradigm of active visual reasoning via dynamic image cropping, outperforming single-pass perception across all tested models.

## Relation to Prior Work

BLINK-Twice extends the visual perception evaluation line initiated by BLINK (ECCV 2024) and NaturalBench (NeurIPS 2024) by adding a reasoning layer on top of perceptually challenging images, moving from 'see and get' to 'observe and reason.' It deliberately departs from mainstream multimodal reasoning benchmarks (MMMU, MathVista, Olympiad-Bench) that treat visual input as replaceable auxiliary context for language-domain reasoning, instead requiring answers to be grounded exclusively in image content. The adversarial sample construction leverages GPT-4o's image editing to create natural, locally consistent semantic contrasts, going beyond the CLIP-based filtering used in NaturalBench. The CoT-score evaluation protocol draws from MME-CoT's reasoning-step assessment but is specifically tailored to visual perception reasoning with annotated key steps.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals a fundamental gap: even top models (GPT-4o, Gemini-2.5 Pro) achieve I-Acc below 0.5 and G-Acc under 0.3, and their CoT-scores are often lower than answer accuracy, suggesting correct answers frequently stem from guessing rather than sound visual reasoning. The finding that repeated image observation in multi-turn dialogue significantly boosts weaker models, and that o3's active visual interaction (cropping, zooming) represents an emerging paradigm, points toward a necessary shift from language-centric to truly multimodal reasoning architectures. Open questions include how to design training objectives that encourage adaptive, efficient visual reasoning rather than indiscriminate chain extension, and whether the 345-image scale is sufficient for robust generalization claims. The relatively small dataset and reliance on GPT-4o for both adversarial generation and reasoning annotation introduce potential circularity concerns.
