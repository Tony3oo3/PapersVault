## Problem

Long-tailed recognition suffers from a 'seesaw dilemma' where improving tail-class accuracy typically degrades head-class performance, and no single LTR strategy resolves this trade-off. While multi-strategy collaboration via multi-objective optimization (MOO) is promising, directly applying MOO to end-to-end network training introduces substantial computational overhead, unstable training dynamics, and risk of corrupting learned features. The paper addresses how to effectively fuse multiple complementary LTR strategies through MOO in a stable, efficient, and feature-preserving manner.

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-100-LT, 224×224 for ImageNet-LT and iNaturalist 2018) and produces a final class probability distribution via a weighted combination of N expert classifier heads. Each head is trained with a distinct LTR loss (e.g., CE, KPS, BCL, LOS), and at inference the Evolving Optimal Strategy Selection (EOSS) mechanism computes class-specific softmax-weighted integration of all head predictions, so the output is not a single head's prediction but a dynamically reweighted ensemble that emphasizes whichever expert performs best on each class.

## Method

TS-MOF decouples training into two stages: (1) standard cross-entropy pre-training of a ResNet-34 backbone to learn generic features, then (2) freezing the backbone and fine-tuning N independent classifier heads (each with a different LTR loss) over just 20 epochs using a multi-objective optimization loop. Two core mechanisms drive Stage 2: R-PLA computes per-batch task weights by measuring each strategy's real-time per-class accuracy pattern via cosine similarity to a reference, transformed through a cube-root and non-negative clipping for robustness; RD-PCGrad resolves inter-task gradient conflicts through deterministic iterative pairwise projections (removing the conflicting component along the other gradient's direction) followed by masked mean aggregation, guaranteeing a Pareto-improving update direction. This architectural decoupling confines the complex MOO to the small classifier parameter space, avoiding feature corruption while enabling synergistic fusion of heterogeneous LTR knowledge.

## Results

On CIFAR-100-LT (IR=100), TS-MOF achieves 56.8% overall Top-1 accuracy and 39.9% tail accuracy, surpassing the previous SOTA LOS (53.9% overall, 36.6% tail) by +2.9% and +3.3% respectively, while also improving head accuracy from 70.3% to 79.0%. On ImageNet-LT, TS-MOF reaches 56.3% overall and 42.6% tail accuracy (vs. LOS: 54.4% and 42.3%). On iNaturalist 2018, TS-MOF achieves 72.3% overall accuracy, outperforming LOS (70.8%) and all single-strategy baselines. All improvements are achieved within a 20-epoch fine-tuning budget on a frozen ResNet-34 backbone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TS-MOF outperforms all eight baselines on overall accuracy across every dataset and imbalance ratio. Against the strongest prior method LOS [27] (previous SOTA two-stage approach), TS-MOF gains +2.9% overall and +3.3% tail accuracy on CIFAR-100-LT IR=100, +2.2% overall on IR=50, and +1.8% overall on IR=10, while also substantially improving head accuracy (79.0% vs. 70.3% at IR=100). Compared to single-strategy baselines, TS-MOF dominates on overall and head metrics: e.g., on CIFAR-100-LT IR=100, it surpasses KPS [14] (42.2% All) by +14.6%, BCL [34] (44.2% All) by +12.6%, and RIDE [29] (47.5% All) by +9.3%. Notably, KPS achieves the highest tail accuracy among single strategies (48.7% at IR=100) but at the cost of very poor head accuracy (41.9%), illustrating the seesaw dilemma that TS-MOF's multi-objective fusion resolves. On ImageNet-LT, TS-MOF leads LOS by +1.9% overall (56.3% vs. 54.4%) and matches its tail accuracy (42.6% vs. 42.3%), while on iNaturalist 2018 it gains +1.5% overall (72.3% vs. 70.8%) over LOS and +1.4% over the best single strategy BCL (70.9%).

## Relation to Prior Work

TS-MOF builds on the two-stage decoupling paradigm introduced by Kang et al. (2019), which separated feature learning from classifier adaptation for long-tailed recognition, and on the PCGrad gradient-surgery mechanism (Yu et al., 2020) for resolving multi-task gradient conflicts. The key departure is that prior MOO-in-LTR work (e.g., Mahapatra et al., 2022) applied multi-objective optimization end-to-end across the full network, which the authors argue is computationally expensive and destabilizes feature learning. TS-MOF instead freezes the backbone and restricts MOO to lightweight classifier heads, introducing R-PLA (a cube-root cosine-similarity weighting scheme) and RD-PCGrad (a deterministic, numerically stabilized variant of PCGrad) as the two core mechanisms, while reusing the EOSS inference-time strategy selection from the authors' prior ICML 2024 work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that the long-standing 'seesaw dilemma' in LTR—where tail improvements typically sacrifice head accuracy—can be partially broken by multi-objective fine-tuning of classifier heads on frozen features, achieving simultaneous gains in both head (+8.7 pp) and tail (+3.3 pp) on CIFAR-100-LT IR=100 within just 20 fine-tuning epochs. This offers a practical, low-cost recipe for practitioners who already have a pre-trained backbone and want to improve balance without retraining features. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond ResNet-34/CNN backbones to vision transformers, whether the strategy set (KPS, BCL, LOS) can be selected automatically rather than by hand, and whether the gains persist under more extreme imbalance ratios or in open-world settings.
