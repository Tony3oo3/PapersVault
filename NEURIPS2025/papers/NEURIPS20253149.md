## Problem

Neural networks trained on biased datasets preferentially learn shortcut features over core features, but the mechanism behind both the accelerated learning and the persistent post-convergence dominance of these features remains incompletely understood. Previous work attributed shortcut learning primarily to max-margin bias, yet it is unclear whether controlling the margin alone can eliminate shortcut dominance. This paper asks whether the NTK eigenstructure can explain both why shortcut features converge faster and why they retain disproportionate influence on the final decision boundary.

## Input / Output

The theoretical analysis takes a binary-labeled dataset modeled as a Gaussian Mixture Model with clusters weighted by biased versus non-biased attributes, and produces eigenfunction decompositions of the NTK integral operator along with closed-form feature influence weights. For empirical validation, the method consumes RGB images from five biased datasets through either a two-layer ReLU fully-connected network or a pretrained ResNet-18, and outputs scalar predictability scores (alignment with ground-truth labels) and availability scores (alignment with top eNTK eigenvectors, computed from 500 randomly sampled training points) for both ground-truth and manually-identified shortcut labels.

## Method

The paper decomposes the NTK of a linear network under a Gaussian Mixture Model data distribution into eigenfunctions, showing (Proposition 3.1) that eigenfunctions aligned with clusters of larger mixing weights have larger eigenvalues, causing shortcut features to converge faster via spectral bias. Proposition 3.2 then shows that the post-convergence influence weight of each feature scales with its cluster weight divided by the sum of all cluster variances, meaning shortcut features retain disproportionate influence on the decision boundary even after training converges—an effect that vanishes only under the unrealistic assumption of zero within-cluster variance. Corollary 3.3 proves that under SD/Marg-Ctrl regularization with λ→∞, the decision boundary ratio converges to the MSE-trained boundary, demonstrating that max-margin bias is not the sole driver of shortcut learning. The authors further introduce predictability and availability metrics to quantify, on real-world datasets, how well a feature predicts labels versus how aligned it is with top NTK eigenvectors, confirming that shortcut labels exhibit lower predictability but higher availability.

## Results

Across all five datasets and three loss functions (CE, MSE, SD), the availability of shortcut labels consistently exceeds that of ground-truth labels, with the largest gap on Colored-MNIST (0.0977±0.0129 under CE) and the smallest on Waterbirds (0.0153±0.045 under CE). Test accuracy of shortcut labels marginally exceeds ground-truth labels on real-world data (≈0.83 vs ≈0.82 on Waterbirds; ≈0.953 vs ≈0.951 on CelebA), confirming that network outputs are dominated by shortcut features. On synthetic datasets, test accuracy drops to ≈0.92 (Colored-MNIST) and ≈0.94 (Patched-MNIST) compared to >0.98 on uncorrupted MNIST, demonstrating the cost of shortcut reliance. The availability gap persists under SD regularization (λ=0.1), supporting the theoretical claim that max-margin bias is not the sole driver of shortcut learning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical analysis paper without external method baselines; instead, it compares internal configurations. Across loss functions, the availability gap between shortcut and ground-truth labels is largest under CE (e.g., 0.0977 on Colored-MNIST) and slightly smaller under MSE (0.0832) and SD (0.0728), but remains positive in all cases, confirming that shortcut dominance is not an artifact of cross-entropy's max-margin bias. Comparing architectures, the two-layer ReLU FC network on synthetic data and the pretrained ResNet-18 on real-world data both exhibit the same pattern: shortcut labels have higher availability and marginally higher test accuracy than ground-truth labels. A notable exception arises with pretraining: on Dogs vs Cats, a pretrained ResNet-18 (already exposed to dog/cat images in ImageNet) shows lower availability for shortcut labels than for ground-truth labels, indicating that initialization can mitigate shortcut learning. The paper also shows that increasing the strength of the shortcut (larger patch size in Patched-MNIST, from 1×1 to 7×7) monotonically increases the availability of the shortcut label, linking availability to shortcut strength.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the NTK framework (Jacot et al., 2018) to analyze shortcut learning by decomposing network outputs into eigenfunctions of the kernel, building on Tsilivis et al.'s (2022) definition of features as NTK eigenfunctions and Hermann et al.'s (2024) predictability/availability metrics. It deliberately departs from prior analyses that assumed uniform data distributions or small cluster covariances, instead modeling clustered Gaussian Mixture data with realistic variances. The paper also positions itself against Puli et al. (2023), who identified max-margin bias as the primary cause of shortcut learning, by showing that shortcut dominance persists even under strong margin regularization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical implication is that debiasing techniques like SD or Marg-Ctrl, which target the max-margin bias, are insufficient on their own because the variance within data clusters creates an inherent spectral preference for shortcut features independent of margin. This reframes the design space for debiasing methods: they must account for intra-cluster variance, not just decision boundary geometry. Open questions include how these results extend to non-linear kernels beyond the infinite-width limit, how to handle multiple competing shortcuts (where availability fails to detect later-learned shortcuts), and whether the comparable-mean-norm assumption holds in practice. The pretraining sensitivity finding (Appendix A.5) also raises the question of how initialization interacts with shortcut learning in a principled way.
