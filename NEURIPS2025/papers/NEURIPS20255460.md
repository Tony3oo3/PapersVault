## Problem

Existing adversarial attacks on scene text recognition (STR) models only alter a few characters in the predicted text, leaving the output easily correctable by external dictionaries or large language models. This paper tackles the problem of making STR attacks 'irreversible' by simultaneously fooling the model on all or most characters, rather than merely changing the overall prediction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255460/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB scene text image (32×100 pixels) as input and produces an adversarial image with exactly N=10 perturbed pixels (coordinates and RGB values), keeping the L2 perturbation norm below ~5. The attack operates in a black-box setting where only the model's predicted character sequence and confidence scores are accessible, and the perturbation is applied at the pixel level rather than as a continuous noise field.

## Method

MPCS divides the image horizontally into N non-overlapping subspaces, each hosting an independent evolutionary population of adversarial pixels that search via Differential Evolution. The key insight is that global pixel-level optimization causes attack pixels to concentrate spatially, leaving some characters unattacked; the multi-population decomposition enforces spatial distribution. To address the resulting 'joint perturbation shift' problem (where individually successful local perturbations fail when combined), the paper introduces a coevolution energy function that evaluates each pixel's contribution to the joint perturbation of all populations, implicitly embedding the global constraint into each local objective. Pixel values are initialized to 0 or 255 based on original brightness, an early-stop mechanism halts converged populations, and a post-processing stage refines pixel values if the combined perturbation still fails to attack.

## Results

On the CUTE80 dataset with the CRNN model, MPCS achieves a perturbation rate (PR) of 94.54% and a post-correction success rate (SR*) of 86.75%, substantially outperforming OnePixel (PR 52.26%, SR* 60.09%) and AD²E (PR 29.75%, SR* 49.73%) while maintaining a comparable L2 perturbation magnitude (~4.3). Against the SOTA multi-modal model IGTR, MPCS reaches 89.86% PR on CUTE80, surpassing the second-best method by 28.31%. Across all four STR models (CRNN, ASTER, SAR, IGTR) and three datasets (CUTE80, IC13, SVTP), MPCS consistently achieves the highest PR, confirming its superiority in producing irreversible attack results.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the two pixel-level DE-based baselines (OnePixel and AD²E), MPCS achieves substantially higher PR across all model-dataset combinations (e.g., 94.54% vs. 52.26% and 29.75% on CRNN/CUTE80), while its SR is slightly lower due to the joint perturbation shift problem; however, its SR* (post-correction success rate) is dramatically higher, confirming the attacks are more irreversible. Compared to the white-box optimization method of Xu et al. (CVPR 2020), MPCS achieves +53.85% SR and +64.19% PR on CRNN/CUTE80 under a lower L2 budget (4.30 vs. 2.88). Against the transfer-based UDUP method, MPCS outperforms by up to 84.6% in SR, as UDUP fails on complex scene text backgrounds. Versus Sparse-rs (a black-box attack designed for image classification), MPCS gains +23.63% SR and +54.31% PR on CRNN/CUTE80 while using a smaller L2 perturbation (4.30 vs. 11.22), demonstrating that classification-oriented attacks are poorly suited for sequential text recognition.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DE-based pixel-level attack paradigm established by OnePixel (Su et al., 2019) and AD²E (Xu et al., 2023) from image classification and single-character STR attacks to a multi-character 'irreversible' attack objective. The key conceptual departure is reframing the optimization target from 'change the model output' (Eq. 1) to 'maximize the edit distance between predicted and ground-truth text' (Eq. 2), motivated by the observation that a single wrong character is trivially corrected by dictionaries or LLMs. To realize this distributed objective, the paper introduces a multi-population spatial decomposition (solving pixel concentration) and a coevolution energy function (solving the joint perturbation shift problem), both of which are novel relative to the single-population DE search used in prior STR attacks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The reframing toward irreversibility addresses a practical gap: prior STR attacks that flip one character are easily undone by post-hoc correction, limiting their utility for privacy protection or robustness evaluation. The strong SR* results (86.75% vs. ~50% for baselines on CRNN/CUTE80) demonstrate that the attack genuinely resists LLM-based correction, which has direct implications for OCR privacy and adversarial training. Limitations include the visual distinctiveness of perturbed pixels (imperceptibility remains an open problem), the query-intensive black-box nature of the method, and evaluation on relatively small datasets (288–2077 images). Open questions include whether the coevolution framework transfers to other sequential prediction tasks (e.g., speech recognition, handwriting) and whether scalable defenses exist against multi-character pixel attacks.
