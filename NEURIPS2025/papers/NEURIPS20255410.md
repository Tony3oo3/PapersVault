## Problem

Hierarchical MOF structure prediction requires jointly modeling periodicity, block positions, and block orientations, but prior methods either ignore periodicity (e.g., MOFFlow uses Cartesian coordinates) or rely on diffusion/flow-matching whose discretization introduces accumulated truncation errors. Additionally, no existing Bayesian Flow Network formulation handles the SO(3) orientation manifold needed for rigid building blocks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255410/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes as input the local geometries of K building blocks (atom types and PCA-aligned local coordinates) and their types, and jointly outputs the 6D lattice parameters ξ, fractional coordinates F ∈ [0,1)^{3×K} for each block center, and unit quaternions Q ∈ (S³)^K encoding each block's 3D orientation. In de novo mode, it additionally generates block identity embeddings in a 32-dim continuous space learned via contrastive learning (GemNet-OC encoder), which are then mapped back to discrete block types via KD-Tree retrieval.

## Method

MOF-BFN defines a unified BFN over the joint parameter space θ^M = {θ^ξ, θ^F, θ^Q}, where each component uses a distribution matched to its manifold: a Gaussian BFN on R⁶ for lattice parameters, a von Mises BFN on the 3D torus for fractional coordinates, and a novel Bingham BFN on S³ for quaternion orientations. The key technical contribution is the Bingham BFN, which exploits the antipodal symmetry of the quaternion-to-rotation mapping (R(q)=R(−q)) and uses the Watson distribution as a conjugate likelihood, yielding a closed-form Bayesian update via eigendecomposition of accumulated outer products. The backbone combines an EGNN encoder for block local structures with a periodic GNN predictor that conditions on the current BFN parameters and accuracy terms. Training minimizes a composite loss of KL divergences (MSE for Gaussian, Bessel-function-weighted cosine for von Mises, squared cosine similarity for Watson) across all three manifolds simultaneously.

## Results

On the BW-DB structure prediction benchmark, MOF-BFN achieves a 53.51% match rate and 0.2498 RMSE with 5 generated samples under strict matching (stol=0.5), outperforming the strongest baseline MOFFlow (44.75% MR, 0.2694 RMSE). Under relaxed matching (stol=1.0), MOF-BFN reaches 98.37% MR with 0.4117 RMSE. For de novo generation, MOF-BFN produces 323 valid structures out of 1,000 (vs. 107 for MOFDiff), with a 92.3% connection-point matching rate versus 72.3% for MOFDiff.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Optimization-based methods (Random Search and Evolutionary Algorithm from CrySPY) completely fail, achieving 0% match rate, confirming that atom-level search is infeasible for complex MOFs. The full-atom generative model DiffCSP also struggles severely, reaching only 0.34% MR at 5 samples under strict matching due to its lack of block-level awareness. Against the primary hierarchical baseline MOFFlow, MOF-BFN wins decisively under strict matching (stol=0.5): 53.51% vs. 44.75% MR and 0.2498 vs. 0.2694 RMSE at 5 samples. Under relaxed matching (stol=1.0), MOFFlow achieves a slightly higher MR (100% vs. 98.37%), but MOF-BFN still yields a lower RMSE (0.4117 vs. 0.4645). For de novo generation, MOF-BFN substantially outperforms MOFDiff on both connection-point matching (92.3% vs. 72.3%) and overall structural validity (323 vs. 107 valid structures out of 1,000), with MOF-BFN also showing lower geometric property RMSE across all eight evaluated descriptors.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two recent lines of work: hierarchical MOF generation (MOFDiff, MOFFlow) that decomposes frameworks into rigid building blocks, and Bayesian Flow Networks (BFNs) that replace SDE/ODE-based sampling with iterative Bayesian updates (Graves et al., CrysBFN). It departs from MOFFlow in two key ways: replacing Cartesian coordinates with fractional coordinates to explicitly model periodicity (borrowed from the inorganic crystal generation literature), and substituting flow matching with BFNs. The central technical novelty is the Bingham BFN, a new generative module that extends BFNs to the 4D unit hypersphere S³ for orientation modeling via Bingham–Watson conjugate pairs, filling a gap left by prior BFN-based crystal methods that only handled point-particle positions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MOF structure prediction is a bottleneck for computational materials design in catalysis, gas storage, and drug delivery, and the Bingham BFN provides a reusable building block for any generative task requiring distributions over SO(3). The rigid-body assumption (fixed local geometries per block) remains a significant limitation, as real organic linkers exhibit conformational flexibility that the current framework cannot capture. Open questions include whether the BFN advantage over flow matching persists at larger T or with more complex block interactions, and whether conditional generation (demonstrated preliminarily for void fraction) can be made more controllable and reliable for targeted MOF design.
