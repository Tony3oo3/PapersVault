## Problem

Existing 3D part assembly methods either process parts as flat sequences with limited hierarchical semantic understanding, or rely on fixed, handcrafted part groupings that are brittle across categories. Additionally, symmetries within individual parts and geometric similarity among interchangeable parts create multiple valid configurations that standard supervision cannot distinguish, leading to either overly constrained or structurally inconsistent assemblies.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255706/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of N 3D part point clouds (1,000 points each, sampled via Farthest Point Sampling and PCA-normalized to a canonical frame) and predicts a 6-DoF rigid transformation (rotation quaternion and translation vector) per part, yielding an assembled shape. The framework is trained with Min-of-N sampling and evaluated on PartNet's Chair, Table, and Lamp categories using SCD, PA, CA, QDS, and WQDS metrics across multiple Chamfer distance thresholds.

## Method

CFPA is a two-stage coarse-to-fine framework. In the coarse stage, semantic super-parts (M=16) are constructed via entropy-regularized optimal transport (Sinkhorn) over PointNet-extracted part features, then propagated to individual parts through a dual-range mechanism: short-range concatenation with the nearest super-part and long-range super-to-base attention refined by message passing among geometrically similar parts (grouped by bounding-box similarity). The fine stage refines poses via cross-stage attention (coarse features as keys/values, re-extracted part features as queries) combined with instance encoding for geometric similarity disambiguation. A symmetry-aware loss jointly handles self-symmetry (selecting the best of 8 axis-flip variants of ground truth) and inter-part geometric similarity (matching against the most similar part's flipped pose), enabling diverse yet structurally valid assemblies without enforcing a single canonical configuration.

## Results

On the PartNet benchmark (Chair, Table, Lamp), CFPA achieves the best SCD on Chair (0.49×10⁻²) and Lamp (0.77×10⁻²), the best Part Accuracy on all three categories (69.24%, 68.48%, 36.35%), and the best Connectivity Accuracy on all three (49.20%, 58.51%, 63.32%), winning 8 out of 9 SCD/PA/CA entries. Under the averaged mPA/mCA protocol, CFPA reaches 81.28% mPA and 65.73% mCA on Chair, outperforming the closest competitor 3DHPA (77.06% / 64.47%). For assembly diversity, CFPA attains the highest WQDS across all categories (2.75, 3.92, 3.74 ×10⁻⁵) and the highest QDS on Chair (6.71 ×10⁻⁵).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CFPA outperforms the strongest baseline 3DHPA [17] on 8 of 9 SCD/PA/CA entries, with the sole exception being SCD on Table (0.33 vs. 0.32×10⁻², a negligible gap). The margin over 3DHPA is most pronounced on Part Accuracy: +6.23 pp on Chair (69.24 vs. 63.01), +3.90 pp on Table (68.48 vs. 64.58), and +2.86 pp on Lamp (36.35 vs. 33.49). Against SPAFormer [38], which reports only Chair and Table, CFPA improves PA by +13.36 pp (Chair) and +4.10 pp (Table) while matching or beating SCD. Over graph-based methods DGL [6] and RGL [7], CFPA gains roughly 20–30 pp in PA and 15–20 pp in CA across categories. The older baselines B-Global [54] and B-LSTM [55] are surpassed by very large margins (e.g., PA on Chair: 69.24% vs. 15.70% and 8.08%). On diversity metrics (WQDS), CFPA leads all baselines on every category, with the largest gap over 3DHPA on Chair (+0.85×10⁻⁵) and Lamp (+0.58×10⁻⁵).

## Relation to Prior Work

CFPA extends the coarse-to-fine part assembly lineage initiated by graph-based methods (DGL, RGL) and transformer-based frameworks (IET, 3DHPA), but departs from their reliance on fixed or heuristic part groupings by constructing semantic super-parts via entropy-regularized optimal transport (Sinkhorn), yielding data-driven, differentiable part abstractions. The dual-range feature propagation (nearest-super-part MLP + global attention with geometric-similarity message passing) unifies ideas from StructureNet's hierarchical reasoning and 3DHPA's super-part message passing into a single principled pipeline. The symmetry-aware loss, which jointly handles self-symmetry (axis-flip invariance) and inter-part geometric similarity, goes beyond prior instance-encoding or PCA-grouping strategies by explicitly supervising multiple valid pose configurations during training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The symmetry-aware loss is demonstrated to be transferable: plugging it into five different baselines (B-Global, B-LSTM, DGL, RGL, Score-PA, IET, 3DHPA) consistently improves CA and often PA, suggesting it addresses a genuine gap in how part assembly handles interchangeable components. The OT-based super-part construction offers a clean, parameter-free alternative to K-means or bounding-box heuristics for mid-level structural abstraction. However, the framework is limited to semantically meaningful parts and does not extend to fragment reassembly with irregular shapes, and the evaluation is confined to three PartNet categories (Chair, Table, Lamp), leaving open whether the approach generalizes to more complex or asymmetric object classes.
