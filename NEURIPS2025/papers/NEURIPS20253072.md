## Problem

Standard primal-dual methods for constrained online resource allocation assume truthful agents, but in strategic settings agents can manipulate their reports to distort future dual updates for personal gain, degrading social welfare and budget utilization. The paper asks whether it is possible to simultaneously maximize social welfare, satisfy multidimensional long-term cost constraints, and incentivize truthful reporting when agents are strategic and the planner lacks knowledge of value/cost distributions. Prior work (Yin et al., 2022) addressed this only under homogeneous agent distributions and a restrictive 'fair share' constraint, leaving the general heterogeneous, multi-dimensional setting open.

## Input / Output

In each of T rounds, K strategic agents each observe a private scalar value v_{t,i} ∈ [0,1] and a public d-dimensional cost vector c_{t,i} ∈ [0,1]^d, then submit a report u_{t,i} ∈ [0,1] that may differ from their true value. The planner, which does not know the underlying distributions, outputs an allocation (one indivisible reusable resource assigned to a single agent or forfeited) and a payment p_{t,i_t} charged to the winner, subject to the long-term constraint that the T-round average cost stays within a pre-specified threshold ρ ∈ [0,1]^d element-wise.

## Method

The mechanism combines three primal-side innovations: (i) epoch-based lazy dual updates that fix the dual variable λ_ℓ within each epoch to limit an agent's ability to influence future allocations through misreports, (ii) a dual-adjusted boosted second-price allocation rule that charges the winner their dual-weighted cost plus the second-highest adjusted report, making truthful reporting a PBE within each epoch, and (iii) randomized exploration rounds (random agent, random price) that impose immediate utility losses on untruthful reports, deterring intertemporal manipulation of dual updates. On the dual side, the planner updates λ_ℓ via FTRL to achieve Õ(T^{2/3}) regret, but the epoch structure creates an Ω(T^{2/3}) switching-cost barrier. To break this barrier, the paper introduces O-FTRL-FP, a novel online learning subroutine that augments FTRL with a forward-looking prediction term built from historical reports and solves a self-consistent fixed-point problem to resolve the circular dependency between the chosen dual and the predicted loss, achieving Õ(√T) regret that matches the non-strategic lower bound while maintaining constraint satisfaction and incentive alignment.

## Results

The paper's main theoretical guarantee (Theorem 3.2) shows that Algorithm 2 with the O-FTRL-FP dual update achieves $\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}(\sqrt{T})$ social welfare regret while satisfying all $d$ long-term cost constraints exactly ($\mathfrak{B}_T = 0$), matching the $\Omega(\sqrt{T})$ lower bound for non-strategic constrained allocation (Arlotto and Gurvich, 2019). The simpler FTRL variant (Theorem 3.1) achieves $\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}(T^{2/3})$ regret with the same zero-violation guarantee. In the numerical study (T=1000, K=3, d=1, γ=0.9, 1000 trials with Q-learning agents), the proposed mechanism with O-FTRL-FP drives agents toward truthful reporting and substantially improves social welfare and budget utilization compared to the vanilla primal-dual baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against the vanilla primal-dual algorithm of Balseiro et al. (2023, Algorithm 1), which is the standard approach for non-strategic constrained online allocation. In the numerical study with Q-learning strategic agents, the vanilla primal-dual method (blue in Figure 1) is highly vulnerable: agents learn to systematically over-report their valuations, which distorts dual updates, reduces social welfare, and leads to low budget utilization. In contrast, the proposed incentive-aware mechanism with O-FTRL-FP (green) incentivizes near-truthful reporting (reported values closely track true values), achieves significantly higher long-term social welfare, and maintains full budget utilization while satisfying all cost constraints. Theoretically, the proposed mechanism matches the $\Omega(\sqrt{T})$ regret lower bound that holds even in the non-strategic setting, meaning the strategic robustness comes at no asymptotic efficiency cost. The paper also notes that the prior work of Yin et al. (2022) on strategic constrained allocation requires homogeneous agents and a specific 'fair share' constraint, making it inapplicable to the more general heterogeneous-agent, multi-dimensional-constraint setting studied here.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the primal-dual framework for online constrained allocation (Balseiro et al., 2023) to a strategic setting by incorporating three mechanism-design ingredients: epoch-based lazy dual updates to limit intertemporal manipulation, dual-adjusted second-price payments inspired by boosted second-price auctions (Golrezaei et al., 2021b), and randomized exploration rounds for truthful signal extraction. On the dual-update side, it augments classical FTRL with a novel fixed-point prediction structure (O-FTRL-FP) that leverages the near-i.i.d. loss structure induced by incentive compatibility, building on the optimistic online learning framework of Rakhlin and Sridharan (2013). The closest prior work, Yin et al. (2022), handles strategic agents but requires homogeneous value distributions and a specific 'fair share' constraint; this paper removes both restrictions, supporting heterogeneous agents and general multi-dimensional long-term cost constraints.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that O-tilde(sqrt(T)) social welfare regret is achievable with strategic agents—matching the non-strategic lower bound of Arlotto and Gurvich (2019)—closes a gap between online constrained optimization and dynamic mechanism design, showing that incentive robustness need not come at an efficiency cost. The T^(2/3) hardness barrier for FTRL under epoch-based structures (Theorem 4.4) is a useful negative result clarifying why naive extensions fail. Open questions include extending to non-monetary settings (organ matching, school admissions) where the monetary-transfer assumption breaks down, and whether the smooth-cost and gamma-impatience assumptions are sufficient for practical deployment in cloud or public-health allocation.
