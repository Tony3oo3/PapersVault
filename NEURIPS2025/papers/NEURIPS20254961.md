## Problem

Private Evolution (PE) is a training-free method for differentially private synthetic data generation that performs well empirically on images and text, but its theoretical convergence guarantees were previously established only under an unrealistic multiplicity assumption (every data point repeated many times) and for an algorithmic variant that differs from the practical implementation. The paper shows this prior proof technique is fundamentally limited—without the multiplicity assumption, the required privacy parameter scales as ε = Ω(d log(1/η)), making it impractical in moderate dimensions—and provides a new worst-case convergence analysis that matches the practical algorithm.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes a sensitive dataset S of n points in a convex compact domain Ω ⊂ R^d with ℓ₂ diameter D, two APIs independent of S (Random_API producing initial synthetic samples and Variation_API generating multi-scale Gaussian perturbations of each point), and privacy parameters (ε, δ). It outputs a synthetic dataset S_T of n_s points that is (ε,δ)-DP, with n_s and the number of iterations T determined by the theoretical parameter setting (T = Õ(log(nε)), n_s = Õ(nε/√T)). The utility guarantee is on the expected 1-Wasserstein distance between the empirical distributions of S and S_T, which by Kantorovich duality implies approximate preservation of all bounded Lipschitz query answers.

## Method

The paper introduces Algorithm 2, a theoretically tractable variant of PE that replaces the practical thresholding-and-renormalization step with a bounded Lipschitz (D_BL) projection of the noisy nearest-neighbor histogram onto the probability simplex, followed by sampling with replacement—closely matching the practical implementation while enabling worst-case analysis. The convergence proof establishes a recursive inequality E_t[W₁(μ_S, μ_{S_{t+1}})] ≤ (1−γ)W₁(μ_S, μ_{S_t}) + α + 2VσG_V(F_BL) + Õ(Dn_s^{−1/max{2,d}}), where the contraction term comes from the multi-scale Variation_API (γ = Θ(1/d)), the noise term is bounded via Gaussian complexity using Dudley's chaining, and the sampling term uses empirical measure convergence in Wasserstein distance. Balancing these terms yields the final rate E[W₁(μ_S, μ_{S_T})] ≤ Õ(dD(nε)^{−1/d}) under (ε,δ)-DP via adaptive composition of T Gaussian mechanisms. The paper also connects PE to the Private Signed Measure Mechanism (PSMM), showing that the NN histogram solves a 1-Wasserstein minimization problem and that PE can be viewed as a sequential, practical version of PSMM where the support is iteratively refined rather than fixed a priori.

## Results

The main theoretical result (Theorem 4.1) establishes that PE with appropriate hyperparameters achieves expected 1-Wasserstein distance $\tilde{O}\big(dD(\sqrt{\log(1/\delta)}/(n\varepsilon))^{1/d}\big)$ from the sensitive dataset, proving worst-case convergence as $n\to\infty$. Empirically, on CIFAR-10 (Dog and Plane subclasses, $\varepsilon=5, \delta=10^{-4}$), FID decreases monotonically with increasing $n$ (from 100 to 600 samples) when hyperparameters are set per Theorem 4.1. The theoretically-informed number of evolution steps $T=\lceil\log(n\varepsilon)\rceil$ outperforms the fixed $T=20$ used in prior work [33], confirming that PE converges with enough samples provided hyperparameters are chosen correctly.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not include a head-to-head comparison against other DP synthetic data methods (e.g., DP-GAN, G-PATE, DP Diffusion). Instead, the empirical evaluation focuses on validating the paper's own theoretical predictions. The key practical comparison is between the theoretically-informed hyperparameter setting ($T=\lceil\log(n\varepsilon)\rceil$, $n_s$ balanced per Theorem 4.1) and the fixed $T=20$ used in the original PE experiments [33]; the latter is shown to be suboptimal on CIFAR-10 (Figure 4). In 2D simulations, the theoretically predicted optimal $T=2\log(n\varepsilon)$ and $n_s$ values closely match the empirically observed best performance (Figure 2). The paper also compares two histogram post-processing strategies (Gaussian noise + $D_{BL}$ projection vs. Laplace noise + thresholding), showing the latter outperforms on highly clustered data but degrades on non-clustered data (Figure 7), explaining PE's inconsistent behavior on tabular data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Lin et al. (ICLR 2024), who introduced Private Evolution (PE) for DP synthetic data generation via foundation model APIs and provided the only prior convergence analysis. The authors show that this prior analysis is fundamentally limited: it requires an unrealistic multiplicity assumption on the sensitive data, and without it, the privacy parameter must scale as d·log(1/η), rendering the proof technique inapplicable in practice. The paper also draws a conceptual bridge to the Private Signed Measure Mechanism (PSMM) of He et al. (COLT 2023), showing that PE can be viewed as a sequential, practical version of PSMM, and leverages PSMM's bounded Lipschitz projection and empirical process tools to prove convergence. The new Algorithm 2 replaces the prior work's deterministic dataset construction and thresholding with a BL-projection onto the simplex followed by sampling, more closely matching the practical PE implementation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first worst-case convergence guarantee for PE that does not rely on unrealistic data assumptions, providing practitioners with principled hyperparameter choices (T = Õ(log(nε)), n_s = Õ(nε/√T)) and explaining observed phenomena such as sensitivity to initialization and the optimal number of evolution steps. The PE–PSMM connection offers a unified theoretical lens for DP synthetic data generation. Key limitations include the curse of dimensionality in the W1 rate (vacuous for d ≳ log n), the gap between the theoretical Gaussian Variation_API and practical APIs (e.g., diffusion models), and the fact that the BL projection, while theoretically clean, is more expensive than the thresholding heuristic used in practice. Open questions include extending the analysis to practical variation APIs and exploring accuracy metrics that avoid the curse of dimensionality.
