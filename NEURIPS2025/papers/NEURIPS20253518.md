## Problem

Foundation segmentation models like SAM exhibit strong zero-shot generalization on natural images but suffer systematic localized failures on fine-grained medical structures such as vessels, producing topological errors including disconnections, boundary ambiguity, and fragmented predictions. Existing SAM adaptation strategies (adapters, LoRA, prompt generation) align global semantic representations but leave structurally uncertain regions undermodeled, as the modeling objectives of semantic alignment and structural recovery present a natural conflict that a unified global scheme cannot resolve.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253518/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB medical image (retinal fundus, OCTA, or X-ray angiography) resized to 512×512 as input, requiring no manual prompts—instead it uses a learned fixed default embedding for the SAM prompt encoder. It produces a binary vessel segmentation mask of the same spatial resolution, computed as a sigmoid of the coarse SAM prediction plus a spatially gated, expert-weighted residual correction. For the generalization experiment on Synapse CT, the input is a 3D volume and the output is an 8-class organ segmentation.

## Method

FineSAM++ layers a sparse Mixture-of-Experts refinement pathway on a frozen SAM backbone with LoRA adapters. A spatially-aware Gating Module takes the concatenation of the input image and coarse SAM mask, producing both a per-pixel uncertainty mask m and fractional routing weights w_j for J parallel Residual Experts, enabling soft (non top-k) activation only in structurally ambiguous regions. Each Residual Expert receives a randomly degraded variant of the coarse mask (random masking, noise, or occlusion) to promote input diversity and expert specialization, and predicts a local residual correction map conditioned on both the perturbed mask and the original image. The final output fuses the coarse prediction with the gated, weighted sum of expert residuals via a sigmoid. Training follows a two-phase curriculum: Phase 1 freezes the gating and expert modules while stabilizing the backbone, then Phase 2 activates the residual pathway with a linearly ramped loss weight, while the Gating Module is supervised by pseudo-labels derived from thresholded backbone prediction errors (δ=0.5). The entire refinement pathway adds only ~0.7M learnable parameters over the 93.7M SAM backbone.

## Results

On DRIVE, FineSAM++ achieves a Dice of 0.8231, marginally surpassing nnU-Net (0.8220) and substantially outperforming the strongest SAM-based baselines (AutoSAM 0.6603, H-SAM 0.6622). On DCAI, it reaches Dice 0.8127, beating nnU-Net (0.8045) by 0.8 points. On CHUAC and ROSE, FineSAM++ scores 0.7768 and 0.8220 Dice respectively, slightly below nnU-Net (0.7814 and 0.8270) but still leading all SAM-adapted methods by 5–17 Dice points. On topological metrics, FineSAM++ attains the best clDice on DRIVE (0.832) and DCAI (0.865), confirming superior vessel centerline continuity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FineSAM++ achieves the best Dice on DRIVE (0.8231) and DCAI (0.8127), edging out nnU-Net by 0.1 and 0.8 points respectively, while dramatically surpassing all SAM-based adaptations (AutoSAM, H-SAM, SAMed, SAM Adapter) by 5–21 Dice points across every dataset. On CHUAC and ROSE, nnU-Net retains a slight Dice advantage (0.7814 vs. 0.7768 and 0.8270 vs. 0.8220), but FineSAM++ still leads all SAM-variant methods by 5–17 points. On topological metrics (Table 2), FineSAM++ achieves the highest clDice on DRIVE (0.832 vs. 0.801 for U-Net++) and DCAI (0.865 vs. 0.815 for U-Net++), indicating superior vessel centerline continuity. The parameter-efficiency comparison (Table 3) shows FineSAM++ adds only 0.7M parameters over the SAM backbone (94.4M total), compared to 104.3M for SAM Adapter and 135.3M for AutoSAM, while matching nnU-Net's Dice on DRIVE with roughly 3× fewer FLOPs (376.8G vs. 1864.9G). On the Synapse multi-organ CT dataset, FineSAM++ attains the highest mean Dice (87.97%) and lowest HD (7.89), outperforming H-SAM (86.49%, 8.18) and nnU-Net (87.33%, 10.78).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the SAM + LoRA adaptation paradigm (following 3D SAM Adapter [2]) for medical image segmentation, but departs from prior SAM-adaptation methods (SAM Adapter, H-SAM, AutoSAM, SAMed) that focus on global semantic alignment. The key conceptual departure is transplanting sparse Mixture-of-Experts routing—previously explored only for token-level classification and vision-language tasks (Switch Transformers, Expert Choice Routing, CuMo)—into a dense prediction pipeline, where a spatially-aware soft Gating Module routes pixels to lightweight Residual Experts for localized structural correction rather than full re-prediction. The residual-learning formulation (correcting only the error, not regenerating the mask) and perturbation-based expert diversification are novel combinations not seen in prior MoE or segmentation refinement literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a sparse, uncertainty-driven expert mechanism can fix the specific failure mode of foundation segmentation models on fine-grained medical structures (vessel disconnections, boundary ambiguity) with only ~0.7M additional parameters, offering a practical 'refinement over retraining' strategy for clinical deployment. However, the approach is validated primarily on vascular datasets across three modalities; generalization to other fine-grained tasks (tumor boundaries, retinal layers) remains untested, and the fixed expert count with pre-defined perturbation strategies limits adaptability to diverse anatomical error patterns. Open questions include whether dynamic expert allocation or content-conditioned degradation could further improve robustness, and whether the MoE-for-dense-prediction paradigm transfers to non-medical fine-grained segmentation.
