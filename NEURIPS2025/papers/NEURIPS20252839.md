## Problem

Large-scale 3D-text contrastive learning is bottlenecked by the limited availability of 3D-text training data, especially compared to the massive image-text corpora that power 2D models like CLIP. Existing approaches align single 3D shapes with captions but lack the compositional diversity and relational complexity found in real scenes, leaving models under-exposed to multi-object spatial configurations. This paper asks whether structured multi-object scene compositions can virtually expand 3D-text data diversity and yield harder, more informative contrastive samples.

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes individual 3D point clouds (normalized to the unit sphere, 10k-point budget) paired with their OpenShape captions, and optionally pre-rendered 2D views for single-object samples. It produces aligned 3D-text embeddings in CLIP's representation space, which are then used for zero-shot classification, few-shot part segmentation, 3D VQA, and cross-modal retrieval. Composed scenes are masked from the 2D-3D loss terms since real-time rendering of multi-object scenes is infeasible, so the 2D modality is evaluated only on pre-rendered single-object views.

## Method

SCENEFORGE constructs multi-object 3D scenes by sampling K point clouds and assigning each a spatial relation (over, under, next to) relative to the previously placed object. The 3D Scene Forge computes displacements via bounding-box projections along the relation axis (with a fixed offset and Gaussian noise to avoid perfect alignment), then concatenates and downsamples to the target point budget. The Scene Caption Forge sequentially concatenates object captions with their spatial relations to form a raw caption, which is then refined by Qwen2.5-7B into a fluent, diverse description. Training mixes single-object and compositional samples at ratio α=0.5 within each batch, using a partitioned InfoNCE loss where the text-3D term covers all samples and the 2D-3D term covers only singles (scaled by 1/(1−α) to balance gradient budgets). The approach is model-agnostic: CLIP image and text encoders remain frozen while gradients flow only through the 3D encoder, and the pipeline plugs into any contrastive 3D-text framework (validated on PointBERT, Uni3D, and ViT-Lens backbones).

## Results

On zero-shot 3D-text classification (trained with LVIS), SF-Uni3D achieves 54.7% top-1 on Objaverse-LVIS, 88.2% on ModelNet40, 65.2% on ScanObjNN, and 49.4% on ScanNet, improving over its Uni3D baseline by +1.2, +0.9, +1.3, and +3.6 pp respectively. On few-shot part segmentation (ShapeNetPart), SF-Uni3D reaches 78.5/81.2 mIoU in 1-shot/2-shot settings, surpassing OmniBind-F (77.8/80.3). On ScanQA VQA, SF-Uni3D attains 10.4 BLEU-4, 66.7 CIDEr, and 20.5 EM, outperforming OmniBind-F by +2.1, +4.6, and +2.9 pp. In N-object cross-modal retrieval, SF models sustain >70% top-1 accuracy at N=6 and ~60% at N=7, while prior methods drop below 50% already at N=2.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SF-Uni3D outperforms the strongest ensemble baseline OmniBind-F on all four zero-shot classification datasets: +1.1 pp on LVIS (54.7 vs 53.6), +1.1 pp on ModelNet (88.2 vs 87.1), +0.5 pp on ScanObjNN (65.2 vs 64.7), and +3.3 pp on ScanNet (49.4 vs 46.1), while using a single model rather than an ensemble. Against the non-ensemble baselines ULIP-2, TAMM, and MixCon3D, all three SF variants (OpenShape, ViT-Lens, Uni3D) achieve higher top-1 accuracy on every dataset. On part segmentation, SF-Uni3D (78.5/81.2 mIoU) surpasses OmniBind-F (77.8/80.3) in both 1-shot and 2-shot settings. On ScanQA, SF-Uni3D leads OmniBind-F by +2.1 BLEU-4, +4.6 CIDEr, and +2.9 EM, with the largest gains on spatial-reasoning queries. In N-object retrieval, prior models (including OmniBind-F) degrade below 50% top-1 at N=2, whereas SF-Uni3D (trained at N=3) maintains >70% at N=6 and ~60% at N=7, demonstrating substantially better generalization to multi-object scenes.

## Relation to Prior Work

SCENEFORGE extends the 3D-text contrastive alignment line (ULIP, Uni3D, OmniBind, TAMM, MixCon3D) by introducing a data-side augmentation strategy rather than a model-side one. It draws on 2D compositional augmentation (CutMix, MixUp, and the semantic-composition contrastive learning of Aladago et al.) but departs from the weak pixel/point blending of PointCutMix and PointMixUp by constructing structured multi-object scenes with explicit spatial relations and LLM-refined captions. The key conceptual leap is that 3D point clouds, unlike 2D images, can be freely repositioned without visual artifacts, enabling semantically coherent spatial compositions that are naturally describable in text.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a practical, model-agnostic recipe to virtually expand 3D-text training data diversity, which is the central bottleneck in this area. The generalization from three simple training relations (over/under/next-to) to complex unseen relations (between, in-front-of, closest-to) on ScanQA suggests the learned spatial representations are more compositional than the training signal implies. Open questions include whether richer relation vocabularies, learned co-occurrence priors, or rendered multi-view compositions could push gains further, and whether the fixed 10k-point budget limits scalability to denser real-world scenes.
