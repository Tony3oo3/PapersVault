## Problem

Conventional vector quantization methods compress static inputs using a fixed, unstructured codebook, and existing world models handle spatial and temporal compression in separate stages (e.g., VQ-VAE for space, RNN/Transformer for time). This two-stage design fails to capture the joint spatiotemporal structure that biological grid-like codes exploit, where attractor dynamics and action-driven transitions naturally unify space and time in a single structured representation.

## Input / Output

GCQ takes an interleaved observation-action sequence (RGB image frames at 3×80×40 or 3×128×128, paired with discrete actions such as translations and rotations) and produces a compact latent representation of m parallel CANN sequences, each with d neurons on a toroidal grid. The decoder reconstructs the full observation sequence from the quantized codes, and the same latent space supports downstream outputs: predicted future frames, planned action sequences for goal-directed navigation, and inferred action sequences for inverse modeling. The codebook is fixed (K attractor bumps per CANN) and not learned during training.

## Method

GCQ replaces the static VQ codebook with a fixed set of 2D Gaussian bump attractors generated by continuous attractor neural networks (CANNs) on a torus, where each bump's position indexes a codeword. The key mechanism is action-conditioned sequence matching: given an action sequence, each of K base bumps is propagated forward to generate a candidate trajectory, and the encoder's latent sequence is matched to the best-fitting trajectory via L2 minimization (Eq. 8), quantizing the entire sequence at once rather than frame-by-frame. A ViT-based encoder and decoder provide the global receptive field needed because a single action can shift the bump and alter the entire reconstructed observation. Training uses only a reconstruction loss and a commitment loss with a straight-through estimator, since the codebook is fixed. The resulting structured latent space forms a cognitive map where valid actions correspond to discrete bump displacements, enabling constant-complexity greedy planning, stable long-horizon prediction, and inverse modeling through the same map operations.

## Results

On the Google Street View dataset (3×128×128), GCQ with a ViT encoder-decoder (112M parameters) achieves a reconstruction FID of 42.56 and prediction FID of 43.41, with PSNR of 27.82 (reconstruction) and 27.77 (prediction). At lower resolution (3×80×40, 90M params), GCQ reaches FIDr=13.27, FIDp=13.92, PSNRr=31.32, PSNRp=31.34. A key finding is that GCQ's prediction quality remains stable regardless of initialization length or prediction horizon, whereas the VQ+UNet baseline degrades noticeably as the prediction length increases. Fixed CANN-derived codes outperform learnable codes (FIDp 43.41 vs. 47.76; PSNRp 27.77 vs. 24.48).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GCQ is compared against two two-stage world-model baselines: VQ+UNet (VQ-VAE for spatial compression + UNet for temporal prediction, 96M params) and VQ+Transformer (VQ-VAE + Transformer following TransDreamer, 121M params). On the GSV dataset, GCQ (112M) matches or slightly exceeds VQ+UNet in reconstruction FID at comparable model sizes, but its decisive advantage appears in long-horizon prediction: as the prediction length increases, VQ+UNet's FID degrades substantially while GCQ maintains a nearly flat FID curve (Fig. 4D). This stability is attributed to GCQ's structured cognitive map, which avoids the compounding errors typical of autoregressive latent predictors. On the 2DMaze and 3DShapes datasets, GCQ also demonstrates zero-shot generalization to unseen environments and stable long-range prediction of abstract feature transitions, capabilities not directly benchmarked against the two-stage baselines but qualitatively superior. Overall, GCQ wins on prediction stability and long-horizon consistency, while the two-stage baselines remain competitive on short-horizon reconstruction quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

GCQ extends the VQ-VAE framework (Van Den Oord et al., 2017) by replacing the static, learned codebook with a fixed codebook derived from continuous attractor neural network (CANN) dynamics (Wu et al., 2008), where each attractor bump serves as a codeword. The key departure from both standard VQ and two-stage world models (VQ + RNN/Transformer) is that quantization operates on observation-action sequences via template matching against action-conditioned trajectories, unifying spatial and temporal compression in a single step. The method also draws on the neuroscience literature on grid cells and cognitive maps, positioning the CANN-generated grid-like patterns as a biological codebook that the brain may map sensory experience onto rather than learn from scratch.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified spatiotemporal compression offers a computationally simple route to planning (reducing it to bump-transition search on a cognitive map) and provides a testable computational hypothesis for grid cell emergence: that GCs arise from mapping onto preexisting CANN bump structures rather than from optimizing network weights. However, the experimental evidence is limited to relatively small-scale datasets (2DMaze, GSV, MPI3D, 3DShapes) with low-dimensional actions, and the action space is constrained to 5^m combinations with 2D attractors. Open questions include whether the fixed-codebook advantage persists at scale, whether the approach generalizes to high-dimensional continuous action spaces, and whether the neuroscience hypothesis can be validated with more direct biological evidence.
