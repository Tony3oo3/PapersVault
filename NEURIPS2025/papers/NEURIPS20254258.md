## Problem

Existing SpAM literature provides minimax-optimal function estimators via group-lasso penalties, but univariate function selection (identifying which f_j ≠ 0) was treated as a byproduct with only sufficient, not necessary, signal conditions. No non-asymptotic minimax separation rate for univariate function selection in a general SpAM framework had been established, and it was unclear whether optimal estimation and optimal selection require the same procedure.

## Input / Output

The method operates on a continuous-time Gaussian white noise observation process Y_x over a p-dimensional domain, where each univariate function f_j is expanded in an orthonormal basis with coefficients constrained by an ellipsoid class defined by a smoothness sequence {μ_i}. The selector is a truncated-type procedure that consumes only the first K basis coefficients per covariate (a chi-squared statistic) and outputs a binary selector vector η̂ ∈ {0,1}^p indicating the estimated support. The key structural detail is that the optimal truncation level K depends on both the smoothness sequence and the high-dimensional sparsity ratio p/s, coupling the two in a way absent from the estimation problem.

## Method

The paper constructs truncated-type selectors that threshold the sum of squared basis coefficients (a chi-squared statistic) against a level calibrated to the composite error σ²{log(p/s) + √(K log(p/s))}, where the truncation K is chosen to balance the residual signal μ_K against this error. The central insight is that the high-dimensional sparsity structure affects both terms in the minimax separation rate—unlike estimation where the selection error and univariate estimation error are independent—yielding the rate σ² log(p/s) + max_k(σ²√(k log(p/s)) ∧ μ_k). For adaptation to unknown Sobolev smoothness α, the selector aggregates (via max) over a dyadic grid of truncation levels, incurring an additional log(log(σ⁻²)) factor. Finally, the paper proves that the truncation level K_e optimal for function estimation is strictly smaller than the K' needed for optimal selection, demonstrating a fundamental estimation-selection gap within the truncated estimator family.

## Results

The paper establishes the non-asymptotic minimax separation rate for univariate function selection in SpAMs from two perspectives: (i) sparse multiple testing (FDR+FNR control): ε²_test ≍ σ² log(p/s) + max_k(σ²√(k log(p/s)) ∧ μ_k), and (ii) support recovery (wrong recovery probability): ε²_rec ≍ σ² log p + max_k(σ²√(k log p) ∧ μ_k). For Sobolev spaces with smoothness α, the testing rate simplifies to σ² log(p/s) + (σ⁴ log(p/s))^{2α/(1+4α)}. A key negative result (Theorem 7) shows that the truncation level K_e optimal for function estimation (μ_k ≤ σ²k) is suboptimal for selection, which requires K satisfying μ_k ≤ σ²√(k log p), creating a fundamental estimation–selection gap.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's main theoretical comparison is with prior support-recovery results of Ingster and Stepanova [2014] and Butucea and Stepanova [2017], which established asymptotically optimal signal conditions in Sobolev spaces under the restriction log p = o(σ^{−2/(2α+1)}). This paper recovers their rate in that regime but extends to the previously uncharacterized high-noise regime σ⁻² ≳ log p > σ^{−2/(2α+1)}, where the rate becomes σ² log p (matching the Gaussian sequence model). Compared to the group-sparsity results of Butucea et al. [2023a], the SpAM rate is strictly larger due to the additional √(k log p) term from the chi-squared tail, reflecting the nonparametric nature of each univariate function. In the numerical experiments (Appendix B), the proposed Optimal selector (11) and the adaptive selector (13) consistently outperform the Univariate and Suboptimal truncation-based selectors across all noise levels and smoothness parameters; notably, the Suboptimal selector (using estimation-optimal truncation K_e) only matches the Optimal selector in the low-noise regime n ≲ (log p)^{1+2α}, confirming the theoretical estimation–selection gap. The adaptive selector incurs only a log(log(σ⁻²)) penalty over the oracle rate, which is negligible in high dimensions (log p ≳ log log(σ⁻²)).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the minimax variable selection literature from the Gaussian sequence model (GSM) to the richer sparse additive model (SpAM) setting, where each 'signal' is an entire univariate function rather than a scalar. It builds directly on Butucea et al. (2023a) for the monotone likelihood ratio technique and group-sparsity lower bounds, on Raskutti et al. (2012) for the SpAM estimation rate decomposition, and on Baraud (2002) for univariate goodness-of-fit testing rates. The key departure from prior SpAM selection work (Ravikumar et al. 2009, Huang et al. 2010, Dai et al. 2023) is that those papers derived selection guarantees as byproducts of group-lasso estimation, yielding sufficient but not necessary signal conditions; this paper instead directly optimizes the selection problem and proves the resulting rates are both necessary and sufficient (minimax optimal) in finite samples.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper delivers the first non-asymptotic minimax optimal separation rates for univariate function selection in SpAM, closing a gap where prior results were either asymptotic, restricted to specific function classes, or merely sufficient. The most impactful finding is the estimation-selection gap (Theorem 7): the minimax optimal truncated estimator for the full SpAM function provably fails to achieve optimal support recovery, undermining the common 'estimate then select' paradigm. Open questions include extending results to Besov balls and L_q-ellipsoids, incorporating local differential privacy constraints, and generalizing the suboptimality lower bound from truncated estimators to the full class of minimax optimal estimators.
