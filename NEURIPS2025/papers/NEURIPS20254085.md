## Problem

Differentiable Logic Gate Networks (DLGNs) suffer from a discretization gap—training relies on continuous relaxations of logic gates while inference requires hard discrete selections, causing up to 3% accuracy degradation—and from slow convergence due to a sharp loss landscape. No prior work had formally analyzed this gap or connected it to loss landscape smoothness, leaving DLGNs impractical for real-world deployment despite their efficient inference.

## Input / Output

Input images are binarized before entering the network, where each neuron receives two inputs from randomly connected neurons in the previous layer. The final layer's neurons are partitioned into disjoint class groups, and the GroupSum operation (sum of neuron outputs divided by a temperature parameter) produces per-class scores from which the predicted label is taken. Networks are extremely wide (256k–2048k neurons per layer) and 6–12 layers deep, operating over search spaces exceeding 10^3,600,000 possible gate configurations.

## Method

Gumbel Logic Gate Networks replace the continuous weighted sum over all 16 logic gates with stochastic single-gate selection: Gumbel noise is injected into per-gate logits, and a single gate is chosen via argmax in the forward pass. Gradients are computed through the Gumbel-Softmax relaxation in the backward pass, optionally combined with a straight-through estimator that uses the hard gate output forward but soft gradients backward, aligning training dynamics with inference. The key theoretical result (Lemma 1) shows that Gumbel noise acts as implicit Hessian trace regularization—minimizing the expected loss adds a (π²/12τ²)·tr(H_f) term—flattening the loss landscape and reducing sensitivity to parameter discretization. The temperature τ controls regularization strength, with a 'Goldilocks zone' around τ≈0.25 yielding the fastest convergence.

## Results

Gumbel LGNs achieve up to 4.5× faster wall-clock convergence than DLGNs on CIFAR-10 (depth 12, width 256k), reducing the discretization gap by 98% and eliminating all unused gates (0.00% vs. 49.81% for DLGNs). On CIFAR-10, the best Gumbel LGN configuration (τ=1.0) reaches ~57.8% test accuracy, while on CIFAR-100, Gumbel LGNs converge around 400K iterations whereas DLGNs fail to converge within the 48 GPU-hour budget. The discretization gap remains stable across network depths for Gumbel LGNs, whereas DLGN gaps grow with depth.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is the original Differentiable Logic Gate Network (DLGN) of Petersen et al. [7]. Gumbel LGNs dominate DLGNs on every measured axis: convergence speed (4.5× faster in wall-clock time, 4.75× faster in iterations), discretization gap (98% reduction, with Gumbel LGNs showing virtually zero gap vs. ~3% for DLGNs on CIFAR-10), and neuron utilization (100% reduction in unused gates, from 49.81% to 0.00%). In terms of final test accuracy, Gumbel LGNs match or slightly exceed DLGNs (e.g., ~57.8% vs. ~57.4% on CIFAR-10 at comparable training budgets), while on CIFAR-100 the advantage is more pronounced since DLGNs do not fully converge. The only trade-off is a ~5% per-iteration runtime overhead from Gumbel noise sampling, which is more than offset by the faster convergence.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Differentiable Logic Gate Networks (DLGNs, Petersen et al. 2022) by replacing the continuous softmax gate weighting with Gumbel-Softmax discrete sampling and a straight-through estimator, directly borrowing the reparameterization trick from Jang et al. (2017) and the ST gradient from Bengio et al. (2013). It explicitly positions DLGNs as an extreme-scale analogue of DARTS (Liu et al., 2019), operating over search spaces exceeding 10^3,600,000 versus the ~10^18 typical in NAS, and draws a formal connection to Smooth DARTS (Chen & Hsieh, 2021) and SAM (Foret et al., 2021) via implicit Hessian trace regularization. A concurrent work by Kim (2023) also injects Gumbel noise into DLGNs but does not analyze the discretization gap or provide the curvature-regularization interpretation that this paper formalizes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 4.5× wall-clock speedup and 98% discretization gap reduction make DLGNs substantially more practical for edge deployment, where the entire motivation for logic-gate inference lies. The theoretical result connecting Gumbel noise to Hessian trace minimization provides a principled knob (temperature τ) for controlling the trade-off between convergence speed and flatness, though the analysis relies on a second-order Taylor approximation and the empirical validation is limited to CIFAR-10/100 with a single seed. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to the convolutional DLGN variant, how it scales to ImageNet-level tasks, and whether adaptive temperature scheduling can eliminate the need for manual τ tuning.
