## Problem

Existing synthetic image detectors provide only binary real/fake judgments without interpretable explanations, while general-purpose LMMs that can offer natural-language artifact descriptions still underperform expert classifiers in pure detection accuracy. No prior method simultaneously achieves expert-level detection performance across diverse synthetic image categories (beyond DeepFake) and provides human-readable artifact explanations in a single unified model.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251974/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a single RGB image resized to 336×336 pixels (yielding 576 patches via CLIP-ViT-L/14) paired with a fixed text prompt asking whether the image looks real or fake. It produces a free-form natural-language response structured as 'This is a [real/fake] image.' followed by a semicolon-separated list of specific artifact evidence (e.g., texture distortions, structural anomalies, lighting inconsistencies), enabling both classification and explanation in one generation pass.

## Method

FakeVLM follows the LLaVA-v1.5 architecture (CLIP-ViT-L/14 vision encoder → two-layer MLP projector → Vicuna-7B LLM) and is trained via full-parameter fine-tuning on the newly constructed FakeClue dataset (100k+ images across 7 categories). The key design insight is that framing detection as visual question answering with artifact explanations—rather than appending a linear classification head—yields better visual-textual alignment and superior out-of-distribution generalization (84.3% Acc on LOKI vs. 81.6% for the linear-head variant). The FakeClue dataset is annotated using a multi-LMM pipeline: three models (Qwen2-VL, InternVL, DeepSeek) independently generate candidate artifact captions guided by category-specific prompts, and a fourth model aggregates them into a unified, non-redundant description, reducing single-model hallucination bias.

## Results

On the FakeClue test set, FakeVLM achieves 98.6% accuracy and 98.1% F1 for synthetic detection, with 58.0 ROUGE_L and 87.7 CSS for artifact explanation. On the LOKI benchmark, FakeVLM reaches 84.3% accuracy (surpassing the reported human performance of 80.1%) and 83.7% F1, while also leading in explanation quality (20.1 ROUGE_L, 58.2 CSS). On the FF++ DeepFake benchmark, FakeVLM attains 96.3% average AUC, outperforming FaceX-ray (95.9%) and SRM (95.8%). On DMimage, FakeVLM achieves 94.0% overall accuracy, exceeding SIDA (91.8%) and AntifakePrompt (90.6%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FakeVLM substantially outperforms all general-purpose LMMs: compared to the strongest open-source model Qwen2-VL-72B (57.8% Acc on FakeClue, 55.4% on LOKI), FakeVLM improves by over 40 points on FakeClue and ~29 points on LOKI accuracy. Against the leading expert classifiers NPR (90.2% Acc on FakeClue, 77.4% on LOKI) and AIDE (85.9%, 65.6%), FakeVLM achieves 98.6% and 84.3% respectively, while additionally providing natural-language artifact explanations that expert models cannot produce. On the DD-VQA DeepFake explanation benchmark, FakeVLM (93.2% Acc, 70.4 ROUGE_L) surpasses the specialized Common-DF-TI model (87.5% Acc, 60.9 ROUGE_L) by 5.7% in accuracy and 9.5 points in ROUGE_L. On FF++, FakeVLM's 96.3% average AUC edges out FaceX-ray (95.9%) and SRM (95.8%) while maintaining more balanced per-category scores (95.0–97.2%) compared to CDFA's highly variable range (80.7–99.9%). On DMimage, FakeVLM (94.0% overall Acc) outperforms SIDA (91.8%) and AntifakePrompt (90.6%) without requiring any additional classifier or expert model, demonstrating that a single fine-tuned LMM can match or exceed dedicated detection pipelines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the LLaVA-v1.5 architecture (Liu et al., 2023), repurposing its CLIP-ViT + MLP projector + Vicuna-7B stack for synthetic image detection. It departs from prior LMM-based detection work (LOKI, Fakebench, DD-VQA, FFAA, Fakeshield) in two key ways: (1) it trains a specialized model rather than prompting pretrained LMMs, and (2) it targets direct-synthesis artifacts across seven image categories rather than focusing narrowly on DeepFake or tampering artifacts. The multi-LMM annotation pipeline for constructing FakeClue also distinguishes it from human-annotated benchmarks like LOKI and Fakebench, and from GPT-only annotation in MMTD-Set.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FakeVLM demonstrates that a 7B multimodal model, fine-tuned with an explanatory VQA paradigm, can match or exceed dedicated expert classifiers (NPR, AIDE) on detection accuracy while simultaneously producing interpretable artifact descriptions—eliminating the need for separate classification heads. The FakeClue dataset (100k+ images, 7 categories, multi-LMM annotations) is a practical resource for the community. However, the paper acknowledges that LMM-generated annotations may carry systematic biases, and the model's sensitivity to high-fidelity synthetic images with imperceptible artifacts remains limited. Open questions include how the approach scales as generative models produce increasingly indistinguishable outputs, and whether the VQA-with-explanation paradigm generalizes to video or other modalities.
