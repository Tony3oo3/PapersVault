## Problem

Prior symbolic music arrangement work addresses each scenario (reinterpretation, simplification, additive generation) with separate architectures and training schemes, lacking cross-task generality and failing to leverage knowledge from large-scale pre-trained generative models. Additionally, existing time-ordered tokenizations like REMI+ fragment track content by interleaving concurrent instruments, hindering instrument-level control and reducing repetition of per-instrument patterns critical for learning idiomatic playing styles.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250496/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes multitrack MIDI music and decomposes it into three token streams: an instrument condition (target instrument tokens ordered by descending average pitch to encode voice register), a content condition (all notes sorted by onset then descending pitch with instrument tokens removed and duplicates merged), and a history condition (the complete tokenized preceding segment). At inference, users specify arbitrary target instruments and their voice ordering. The output is the reconstructed target tracks as a REMI-z token sequence, where notes are grouped by instrument within each bar rather than strictly time-ordered, with 48th-note quantization for position and duration.

## Method

The core contribution is a segment-level self-supervised reconstruction objective: a pre-trained 80M-parameter decoder-only Transformer (GPT-2 style, 12 layers, 768-dim) is fine-tuned to reconstruct target tracks from disentangled instrument, content, and history conditions, with cross-entropy loss computed only on the target subsequence. This single objective supports any-to-any instrumentation transformation without parallel data, as the model learns to reinterpret musical content under various instrument combinations by randomly deleting tracks from the content condition during training. To support track-wise modeling, the paper introduces REMI-z, a tokenization scheme that groups notes by instrument within each bar (zig-zag pattern) rather than strict global time ordering, reducing sequence length by 32.9% and producing lower bar-level entropy. The model is pre-trained on the Los Angeles MIDI dataset (405K files, 4.3B tokens) then fine-tuned on Slakh2100 (1,289 training files) for each arrangement task, with task-specific filters selecting source and target tracks.

## Results

On band arrangement (the primary task), the full model (REMI-z + pre-training) achieves 99.8% I-IoU, 7.6% VER, 97.5% Note F1, 87.0% Note_i F1, and 84.5% Mel F1, substantially outperforming the Transformer-VAE baseline (49.5% Note F1, 40.0% Note_i F1, 24.7% Mel F1). On piano reduction, the method reaches 85.5% Note F1 and 3.86 Playability, versus 58.3% F1 and 3.47 Playability for the UNet baseline. On drum arrangement, it achieves 79.3% F1 and 3.57 Musicality, compared to 20.3% F1 and 3.19 Musicality for Composer's Assistant v2. Subjective evaluations confirm consistent wins across all three tasks on Coherence, Musicality, and task-specific metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Transformer-VAE [42] (band arrangement), the proposed method wins decisively on every objective metric: Note F1 97.5% vs. 49.5%, Note_i F1 87.0% vs. 40.0%, Mel F1 84.5% vs. 24.7%, and VER 7.6% vs. 35.0%; subjectively it also leads on all five criteria (e.g., Musicality 3.47 vs. 2.72, Faithfulness 3.77 vs. 2.65). Against UNet [30] (piano reduction), the method achieves 85.5% F1 vs. 58.3% and leads on all subjective scores (Faithfulness 3.63 vs. 2.97, Musicality 3.48 vs. 2.78, Playability 3.86 vs. 3.47). Against Composer's Assistant v2 [19] (drum arrangement), the method reaches 79.3% F1 vs. 20.3% and significantly outperforms on Phrase Transition (3.77 vs. 2.86) and Creativity (3.27 vs. 2.58), while Coherence is essentially tied (4.03 vs. 4.05). Against the REMI+ tokenization [31], REMI-z yields significant gains across all band-arrangement metrics (e.g., Note F1 +6.7%, Mel F1 +9.0%, VER −8.3%) and reduces sequence length by 32.9% per bar, with lower note-level perplexity (84.11 vs. 116.20) in unconditional generation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the REMI+ multitrack tokenization from Figaro [31] and the broader line of autoregressive transformer models for symbolic music generation, but departs from prior arrangement work that treats each task (band arrangement, piano reduction, drum infilling) with specialized architectures (Transformer-VAE, UNet, Composer's Assistant). The key conceptual shift is replacing task-specific supervised or infilling objectives with a unified self-supervised segment-level reconstruction over token-level disentangled content and style, eliminating the need for parallel arrangement datasets. The proposed REMI-z tokenization directly addresses a structural limitation of REMI+—content fragmentation from strict time-ordering—by reorganizing notes into track-contiguous sequences within each bar.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework demonstrates that a single pre-trained autoregressive model, fine-tuned with a shared reconstruction objective, can handle reinterpretation, simplification, and additive generation without task-specific architectures or parallel data, reducing implementation cost and enabling knowledge transfer from large-scale generative pre-training. REMI-z's track-wise continuity design also shows benefits beyond arrangement (lower note-level perplexity in unconditional generation), suggesting broader utility for symbolic music modeling. Key limitations include the pop-genre focus, the assumption of identical input/output structure, lack of global instrument planning, and the inability to guarantee melody retention in piano reduction—open questions that constrain real-world deployment.
