## Problem

Uni-level dataset distillation methods suffer from progressive information vanishing, where synthetic images lose fidelity to the original data as optimization proceeds, fundamentally capping their information content (bounded by the pretrained model's output entropy). Additionally, existing methods remain computationally prohibitive for large-scale datasets like ImageNet-1K, requiring tens of hours per distilled image. The paper addresses both gaps by introducing data-level residual connections to preserve original information and by incorporating efficiency mechanisms that halve training time and peak memory.

## Input / Output

The method takes real image patches (1×1 or 2×2 crops) from the original dataset at their native resolution (32×32, 64×64, or 224×224) and a frozen pretrained model (e.g., ResNet-18) whose BatchNorm running statistics and prediction distributions serve as optimization targets. It produces synthetic images at the target resolution, one per class per IPC setting (1, 10, or 50 images per class), which are then used to train a student model from scratch. For large-scale datasets, images are temporarily downsampled to a configurable resolution D_ds (e.g., 200 for ImageNet-1K) during optimization and resampled back to full resolution at residual injection points.

## Method

FADRM introduces three components: (1) Adjustable Residual Connection (ARC), the core novelty, which after every n_iter optimization steps blends the intermediate synthetic image with the original patch via x̃ ← α·x̃ + (1−α)·Resample(P_s, D_t), where α ∈ [0,1] controls the balance between newly acquired knowledge and preserved original details; (2) Multi-Resolution Optimization (MRO), which alternates between downsampled and full-resolution optimization to reduce forward-pass cost by O(D²C); and (3) Mixed Precision Training (MPT), which casts model parameters to FP16 while retaining FP32 gradients for numerical stability. The optimization objective matches the pretrained model's BatchNorm statistics and cross-entropy predictions in a uni-level (single-loop) fashion. Theoretically, the paper proves that uni-level methods have an information ceiling (Theorem 1), that ARC yields a tighter Rademacher-based generalization bound than pure optimization (Theorem 2), and that FADRM captures strictly more mutual information with the original data than uni-level baselines (Theorem 3).

## Results

On ImageNet-1K at IPC=10 (0.8% compression ratio) with ResNet-18 as the student model, FADRM achieves 48.4% top-1 accuracy in single-model distillation and FADRM+ (ensemble-enhanced) reaches 50.9%, surpassing RDED by +6.4%, EDC by +2.3%, and CV-DD by +4.9%. With ResNet-101, FADRM+ attains 58.1% on ImageNet-1K IPC=10, outperforming all baselines by at least +6.4%. In terms of efficiency, FADRM generates each distilled image in 0.47 seconds with 2.9 GB peak GPU memory, compared to 4.99 s / 17.9 GB for EDC and 8.20 s / 23.4 GB for CV-DD, representing roughly a 50% reduction in both training time and memory.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FADRM+ consistently outperforms all three primary baselines across every dataset and IPC setting. Against RDED (which uses raw cropped patches without optimization), FADRM+ gains +6.4% on ImageNet-1K IPC=10 (ResNet-18) and +9.8% on CIFAR-100 IPC=10, confirming that pure patch selection without further optimization is insufficient. Against EDC (which optimizes patches with a very small learning rate, retaining high original-data fidelity), FADRM+ leads by +2.3% on ImageNet-1K and +4.2% on CIFAR-100, validating the benefit of the adjustable residual connection over EDC's fixed small-step approach. Against CV-DD (which largely excludes original data during synthesis), FADRM+ wins by +4.9% on ImageNet-1K and +5.2% on CIFAR-100, demonstrating that completely discarding original data during optimization is suboptimal. In the efficiency comparison (Table 3), FADRM is 10.6× faster per image than G-VBSM (0.47 s vs. 17.28 s) and 5.3× faster than EDC (0.47 s vs. 4.99 s), while using 6.2× less peak memory than CV-DD (2.9 GB vs. 23.4 GB). FADRM+ (ensemble variant) remains 4.6× faster than EDC (1.09 s vs. 4.99 s) while achieving higher accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

FADRM extends the uni-level dataset distillation paradigm established by SRe²L [43], which aligns synthetic data with pretrained model BatchNorm statistics and cross-entropy, by introducing data-level residual connections that periodically re-inject original image patches during optimization. The conceptual leap is drawing an analogy between architectural residual connections (ResNet, DenseNet, Transformers) and a data-space skip connection, a connection the authors argue has been unexplored in data-centric optimization. On the efficiency side, it builds on EDC's [31] real-data initialization and G-VBSM's [30] lightweight ensemble matching, while adding mixed-precision training and multi-resolution optimization as orthogonal acceleration strategies. The theoretical grounding via Rademacher complexity and the data processing inequality (Theorem 1) provides a formal justification for why uni-level methods suffer from information vanishing and why residual injection tightens generalization bounds.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 50% reduction in both training time and peak GPU memory is practically significant for large-scale dataset distillation, where EDC requires ~70 hours on a single GPU for 50 IPC ImageNet-1K. The formal identification of information vanishing (Theorem 1) gives the field a clear diagnostic for why prolonged uni-level optimization degrades distilled data quality, and the ARC mechanism offers a principled remedy. However, the method still requires access to original data patches for residual injection, so it does not fully eliminate the dependency on source data that some data-free approaches target. Open questions include whether the data-level residual concept generalizes to other modalities (text, audio) and other data-centric tasks beyond distillation, and whether the hyperparameters (α, k, D_ds) can be made adaptive rather than requiring manual tuning per dataset.
