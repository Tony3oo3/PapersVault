## Problem

Implicit feedback recommendation faces two intertwined challenges: positive-unlabeled (PU) ambiguity, where unclicked items may simply be unexposed rather than irrelevant, and missing-not-at-random (MNAR) exposure bias, where popular items receive disproportionate clicks. Existing causal debiasing methods require the treatment (exposure) to be observed for every sample, but in implicit feedback the exposure status of negative samples is fundamentally unobserved. This paper is the first to formalize the problem as counterfactual outcome estimation with missing treatment variables, a setting that no prior causal recommendation method can directly address.

## Input / Output

The method consumes user-item feature vectors (tabular) and binary click/no-click labels, but critically, the exposure (treatment) variable is only known for positive samples; for all negative samples it must be imputed. The output is the predicted counterfactual outcome Y(1) — the probability that a user would click an item if exposed — which serves as the relevance score for ranking. The stratification step produces four disjoint groups (DP, HE, HU, UN) with different confidence levels about the imputed treatment, and the model is trained with group-specific loss terms rather than a single uniform objective.

## Method

Counter-IF first performs treatment imputation with confidence using an SVDD-inspired hypersphere: it encloses a fraction α of positive samples in a kernel-defined hypersphere, then assigns negative samples inside the sphere to the Highly Exposed (HE) group, the farthest β fraction to the Highly Unexposed (HU) group, and the remainder to the Unknown (UN) group. The causal representation learner then combines a pointwise cross-entropy loss on DP and HE samples (where Y(1) is known) with a pairwise ranking loss across strata (DP > HE > UN > HU) to capture relative relevance for the unobserved groups. An Integral Probability Metric (IPM) regularization aligns representations between high-confidence (R=1) and low-confidence (R=0) samples to mitigate distributional shift. A generalization bound is derived showing that the total error decomposes into the factual loss on observed groups plus IPM-weighted distribution shift terms, all of which are directly controlled by the proposed loss components.

## Results

Counter-IF achieves NDCG@3 of 0.562 on Yahoo! R3 and 0.368 on Coat, outperforming all baselines across NDCG, Recall, and MAP at K=3, 5, and 8. On Yahoo, it improves NDCG@3 by 1.6 points over the best baseline (UPL, 0.546) and by 1.7 points over RecRec (0.545). On Coat, it gains 0.8 points in NDCG@3 over RecRec (0.360) and 2.3 points over the next-best method (UBPR, 0.343). Most improvements are statistically significant (p < 0.05, pairwise t-test).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Counter-IF outperforms all seven baselines on both datasets across all metrics and K values. On Yahoo! R3, the closest competitors are UPL (NDCG@3: 0.546) and RecRec (NDCG@3: 0.545), both of which are surpassed by 1.6–1.7 points; propensity-based methods like UBPR (0.532) and Rel-MF (0.534) trail further behind. On Coat, RecRec is the strongest baseline (NDCG@3: 0.360), with Counter-IF achieving 0.368 and Recall@3 of 0.382 versus RecRec's 0.365. Traditional matrix factorization methods (WMF, ExpoMF) and BPR consistently rank at the bottom, confirming that ignoring MNAR or treating all unobserved pairs as negative leads to biased relevance estimation. The ablation study further confirms that removing any component (Wasserstein stratification, pairwise loss, or pointwise loss) degrades performance, with the largest drop occurring when both Wasserstein and pairwise losses are removed.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on two distinct lineages: the Support Vector Domain Description (SVDD) framework of Schölkopf et al. (2001), repurposed here as a treatment imputation mechanism with confidence levels, and the causal representation learning and generalization bound framework of Shalit et al. (2017), adapted to handle missing treatment variables. It departs from propensity-score-based debiasing methods (Rel-MF, UBPR) that the authors argue suffer from overconfident propensity estimation and high variance, and from earlier implicit feedback approaches (WMF, ExpoMF, BPR) that assume MCAR. The key conceptual departure is formulating implicit feedback recommendation as a counterfactual outcome estimation problem where the treatment (exposure) is unobserved for negative samples, requiring a novel four-group stratification (DP, HE, HU, UN) rather than the binary positive/negative split used by prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The missing-treatment counterfactual formulation provides a principled unifying lens for the PU and MNAR challenges in implicit feedback, and the derived generalization bound explicitly connects the pointwise loss, pairwise loss, and IPM regularization to controllable error terms. However, the method requires pre-specifying distance thresholds (α, β) for group assignment, is not end-to-end, and is validated on only two datasets (Coat, Yahoo R3), limiting confidence in broader applicability. Open questions include whether the SVDD-based stratification can be made differentiable and integrated into the training loop, whether the approach scales to industrial-scale datasets with millions of user-item pairs, and whether the four-group decomposition generalizes to multi-valued or sequential feedback settings.
