## Problem

Privileged expert distillation (learning a latent-state policy in a simulator, then imitating it) is widely used for partially observable RL tasks like image-based locomotion, but its failure modes relative to standard RL with frame-stacking are poorly understood. Prior theory assumed perfect decodability of the latent state from observations, which the paper shows is unrealistic for these tasks. The paper asks: when does distillation help versus hurt, and can it be improved?

## Input / Output

The executable policy consumes a frame-stack of L recent RGB observations (typically L=3) and outputs a continuous action distribution over motor commands. During training, the latent expert additionally receives the full privileged latent state from the simulator (e.g., joint positions, velocities), but this information is unavailable at test time. The distillation step (DAgger or behavior cloning) maps from the observation-only input to the expert's action distribution, with MSE loss on action predictions.

## Method

The paper introduces the δ-perturbed Block MDP, a theoretical model where each observation is drawn from a block-structured distribution with probability 1−δ and from an arbitrary distribution with probability δ. Under deterministic latent dynamics, they prove the decodability error decays exponentially with horizon, yielding a horizon-independent O(δ) sub-optimality bound for DAgger-based distillation that matches RL guarantees. Under stochastic dynamics, they construct a lower bound showing irreducible Ω(δH) misspecification for the optimal latent policy, explaining why RL with larger frame-stacks can outperform distillation. Empirically, they validate these predictions on DeepMind Control Suite locomotion tasks (walker-run, dog-walk, humanoid-walk) with injected motor noise. Finally, they propose a lightweight improvement: training the latent expert in a modified MDP with additional motor noise to produce a smoother policy, which reduces action-prediction error and yields higher distillation reward than the true optimal latent policy.

## Results

Under deterministic latent dynamics, online expert distillation (DAgger) achieves the best asymptotic performance across all three locomotion tasks (walker-run, dog-walk, humanoid-walk) while converging faster than standard RL (MrQ with L-step frame-stacking). Offline distillation (BC) is competitive on easier tasks like walker-run but suboptimal on harder tasks like humanoid-walk and dog-walk. Under stochastic dynamics (motor noise with std-dev up to 0.3), RL with larger frame-stacks (L=4) robustly outperforms DAgger, and the performance gap widens with increasing noise. A key finding is that distilling a latent expert trained with *higher* motor noise (σ=0.3–0.5) than the test environment (σ=0.2) yields substantially better executable policies than distilling the optimal latent policy for the test environment itself.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three methods: (1) BC (offline expert distillation via behavior cloning), (2) DAgger (online expert distillation), and (3) RL (MrQ [22] with L-step frame-stacking, no privileged information). Under deterministic dynamics, DAgger outperforms RL on all three tasks in both asymptotic return and wall-clock convergence time, while BC is competitive only on the easier walker-run task. Under stochastic dynamics (Gaussian motor noise added to actions), the comparison reverses: RL with larger frame-stacks (L=4) eventually surpasses DAgger, and the gap grows with noise level (std-dev 0.1→0.3). DAgger does not benefit from increasing L, whereas RL does (at the cost of longer wall-clock time). Additionally, the paper shows that the optimal latent policy (trained at σ=0.2) is *not* the best expert to distill: experts trained with higher noise (σ=0.3–0.5) produce smoother policies with lower action-prediction error and achieve higher reward after DAgger distillation, particularly on humanoid-walk.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Block MDP framework [16,17] by introducing the δ-perturbed Block MDP, which relaxes the perfect-decodability assumption that prior theoretical work on privileged information [7] required. It builds on the belief contraction and γ-observability machinery of Golowich et al. [23,24] for RL guarantees, and modifies the Forward/DAgger algorithm [62,64] to analyze expert distillation under approximate (rather than perfect) decodability. The key conceptual departure is framing the expert-distillation-vs-RL comparison through the lens of decodability error (governing distillation) versus belief contraction error (governing RL), and showing that the stochasticity of latent dynamics is the critical factor separating the two regimes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides actionable guidelines for robotics practitioners: under deterministic dynamics, expert distillation (especially DAgger) is competitive with and more efficient than standard RL, but under stochastic dynamics, RL with larger frame-stacks can eventually outperform distillation. The finding that adding motor noise before computing the optimal latent policy yields a better distillation target is a simple, broadly applicable improvement. Limitations include the discrete tabular theoretical model, synthetic Gaussian motor noise as the sole stochasticity source, and evaluation on only three locomotion tasks; understanding the trade-off in settings requiring active information-gathering remains open.
