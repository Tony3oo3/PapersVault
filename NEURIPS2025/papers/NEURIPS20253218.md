## Problem

Most 3D vision algorithms assume camera intrinsics remain constant throughout a video, but real-world footage from zoom lenses and autofocus systems exhibits per-frame intrinsic changes. No existing benchmark provides per-frame ground truth intrinsics for consecutive video frames with dynamic intrinsics, leaving the field without a proper evaluation standard for this common real-world scenario.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253218/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark provides 143K+ frames at 3424×2202 resolution from an ARRI Alexa Mini paired with /i Technology zoom lenses (Canon CINE-SERVO 17-120mm and Fujinon Premista 80-250mm) that record per-frame lens focal length (LFL) and focus distance (FD) metadata. The LUT maps each frame's LFL-FD pair to full Brown-Conrady intrinsics (fx, fy, cx, cy, k1, k2, p1, p2), enabling ground truth retrieval for virtually any video recorded with the same lens without re-calibration. Evaluation also uses 100K 3D points from the MVS Benchmark projected through both ground truth and predicted intrinsics to compute end-point error.

## Method

The core mechanism is a per-lens lookup table (LUT) that maps continuous LFL-FD pairs to camera intrinsics, shifting calibration from a per-frame operation to a one-time construction step. LUTs are populated by calibrating at sampled LFL-FD combinations using two complementary targets: AprilGrid boards (100–800 mm) for small-to-medium FOV spatial footprints, and an RTK-equipped drone with a red LED for large footprints where boards become impractical. The authors extend Kalibr with three key modifications: thin-lens-based CFL initialization (replacing unreliable vanishing-point estimation), a fixed-point algorithm that periodically snaps the principal point to image center during distortion initialization, and 100-rollout joint optimization with IQR-based outlier rejection to reduce stochastic variance. LUT interpolation uses trapezoidal bilinear interpolation over approximately regular grid regions and Delaunay triangulation with barycentric interpolation over irregular drone-based regions, validated via leave-one-out cross-validation showing median CFL error below 0.5%.

## Results

On the InFlux benchmark (143K+ frames, 386 videos), all six evaluated intrinsics prediction baselines struggle significantly. GeoCalib achieves the best overall performance with 56.5% mean fx error and 52.9% of frame-point pairs below 300px EPE, but this is still poor given image dimensions of 3424×2202. COLMAP performs catastrophically, failing to produce intrinsics for 92% of input frames and showing 1270% fx error on the frames it does predict. DroidCalib also fails on 15% of frames, particularly on low-motion videos where it relies on optical flow for keyframe selection.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GeoCalib [34] achieves the best EPE performance (52.9% of frame-point pairs below 300px) and the lowest principal point errors, benefiting from its frame-by-frame prediction approach that does not assume constant intrinsics. WildCamera [37] reports the lowest mean fx/fy error (45.6%/46.9%) but has substantially higher principal point errors (5.04%/6.39%) and lower EPE (47.2%) than GeoCalib. UniDepthV2 [23] is competitive on focal length (50.6% fx error) and principal point (1.61%/2.58%) but trails GeoCalib on EPE (46.1% vs. 52.9%). DroidCalib [10] and Perspective Fields [14] perform poorly, with EPE scores of 28.0% and 17.8% respectively; DroidCalib additionally fails on 15% of frames due to its reliance on optical flow. COLMAP [27] is the worst performer by a large margin, failing on 92% of frames and showing 1270% fx error, likely because its PER_IMAGE mode still struggles with the rapid intrinsic changes in dynamic-intrinsics video.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the camera calibration lineage from Kalibr [19] and board-based methods by introducing a LUT-based retrieval scheme that maps per-frame lens metadata (LFL, FD) to intrinsics, shifting calibration from a per-frame operation to a one-time lookup construction. It departs from prior intrinsics benchmarks (KITTI, EuroC, TUM RGB-D, MVS) that all assume fixed intrinsics, and from [16] which offers limited varying-intrinsics data with scene diversity and accuracy issues. The drone-based calibration component builds on [36] but extends it to full Brown-Conrady distortion modeling rather than an ideal pinhole model. The modified Kalibr addresses known convergence and principal-point drift issues that limit the original tool's robustness on real-world data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark exposes a fundamental gap: virtually all 3D algorithms (SLAM, NeRF, 3DGS, depth estimation) assume constant intrinsics, yet real-world video from smartphones and zoom lenses violates this assumption. The evaluation shows even the best baseline (GeoCalib) fails on ~47% of frame-point projections, and COLMAP fails outright on 92% of frames, indicating the field lacks any viable solution for dynamic intrinsics. Open questions include how to build intrinsics predictors that produce temporally smooth, per-frame estimates, and how to make downstream 3D reconstruction robust to intrinsic variation. Limitations include coverage of only two specific zoom lenses on one camera body, and the reliance on a relatively simple interpolation scheme whose accuracy depends on dense enough LUT sampling.
