## Problem

Video Temporal Grounding models based on conventional transformers rely on computationally expensive dense matrix multiplications and require substantial energy to process long video sequences, making them poorly suited for deployment on resource-constrained edge devices. Spiking Neural Networks, which replace real-valued multiplications with sparse, event-driven accumulation, had not been explored for multi-modal VTG tasks. This paper introduces the first operational spiking detection transformer for VTG, enabling a tunable energy-accuracy tradeoff at inference time.

## Input / Output

The model takes a video pre-segmented into L_v fixed-length clips (each centered at a timestamp) and a natural language query of L_q tokens, both encoded into D-dimensional feature vectors. Rather than producing a single forward-pass output, SpikingVTG operates over T_c discrete convergence time steps, with the Saliency Feedback Gating module dynamically re-weighting video clip features at each step using the intermediate spiking activity of the final encoder layer. The final output per clip is a triple: a foreground indicator (binary), temporal distance offsets converting the clip center to interval boundaries, and a continuous saliency score used for highlight ranking.

## Method

SpikingVTG replaces all floating-point MAC operations in the transformer with FP-ACC operations driven by ternary LIF spikes, and introduces a Saliency Feedback Gating (SFG) mechanism that computes per-clip cosine-similarity saliency scores from the converged average spiking rate of the final encoder layer, applying multiplicative gating to suppress irrelevant clips and reduce neural activity. Training avoids BPTT by leveraging implicit differentiation at the layer-wise ASR equilibrium, requiring only a single backward pass. A Cos-L2 Representation Matching (CLRM) loss aligns the student's converged ASR and mean attention maps with those of a pre-trained non-spiking teacher (UniVTG), transferring generalization without expensive pre-training. Two further variants—Normalization-Free (replacing softmax with ReLU+scaling and removing layer norm) and 1-bit quantized (binary weights with integer accumulation)—reduce energy consumption by an order of magnitude while retaining competitive accuracy.

## Results

On QVHighlights-MR, SpikingVTG achieves 65.29 R@1@0.5 and 48.18 R@1@0.7, outperforming non-pretrained UniVTG (58.86/40.86) and M-DETR (52.89/33.02) while remaining competitive with pre-trained UniVTG+PT (65.43/50.06). On Charades-STA, it reaches 50.62 mIoU, surpassing UniVTG (50.10) and M-DETR (45.54). On TACoS, SpikingVTG attains 36.02 mIoU, beating UniVTG (33.60) and QD-DETR (35.76). For highlight detection on YouTube Highlights, it achieves 76.7 mAP average, exceeding UniVTG (75.2) and CG-DETR (75.9), while on QVHighlights-HD it scores 65.82 HIT@1 and 40.46 mAP, matching pre-trained UniVTG+PT (66.28/40.54).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SpikingVTG outperforms all non-pretrained baselines on QVHighlights-MR, beating UniVTG by +6.43 R@1@0.5 and M-DETR by +12.40, while remaining within ~1.9 points of the pre-trained UniVTG+PT (65.29 vs 65.43 @0.5). On Charades-STA, it surpasses UniVTG across all IoU thresholds (71.20 vs 70.81 @0.3; 50.62 vs 50.10 mIoU) but trails UniVTG+PT and BAM-DETR by 1–2 points. On TACoS, SpikingVTG leads UniVTG by +3.27 mIoU (36.02 vs 33.60) and QD-DETR by +0.26, though it falls short of UniVTG+PT (38.63 mIoU). For highlight detection, SpikingVTG achieves 76.7 mAP on YouTube Highlights, exceeding UniVTG (75.2), CG-DETR (75.9), and UVCOM (76.4), but remains below UniVTG+PT (78.6). On QVHighlights-HD, it matches UniVTG+PT closely (40.46 vs 40.54 mAP; 65.82 vs 66.28 HIT@1) while clearly outperforming non-pretrained UniVTG (38.20 mAP, 60.96 HIT@1). Notably, SpikingVTG is the only fully spiking model in the comparison, achieving these results with FP-ACC operations instead of FP-MAC, and its 1-bit NF variant reduces energy consumption by ~18× (1.3 mJ vs 23.92 mJ) with only ~2–3 points performance degradation.

## Relation to Prior Work

SpikingVTG extends the spiking transformer lineage established by SpikingBERT (Bal & Sengupta, AAAI 2024) to the multi-modal video-language domain, adopting its spiking MHA formulation and implicit-differentiation-at-equilibrium training (Xiao et al., NeurIPS 2021) while introducing ternary spikes (Guo et al., AAAI 2024) to avoid additional FP-MAC operations. The key architectural departure is the Saliency Feedback Gating mechanism, which exploits the temporal dynamics of SNNs to compute per-clip saliency scores via cosine similarity between converged ASR and a query embedding—a feedback loop with no direct analogue in prior non-spiking VTG models like UniVTG or Moment-DETR. The CLRM knowledge-transfer loss is a novel distillation strategy that aligns layer-wise hidden states and attention maps of a pre-trained non-spiking teacher with the student's converged ASR, circumventing the need for expensive large-scale pre-training of the spiking model itself.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first operational spiking detection transformer evaluated across VTG benchmarks, establishing a baseline that demonstrates SNNs can be competitive with non-spiking transformers on complex multi-modal video tasks while offering a tunable energy-accuracy tradeoff (up to ~18× energy reduction with the 1-bit NF variant). The practical implication for edge deployment—where VTG models must run on resource-constrained devices with limited energy—is significant, and the controllable inference-time step parameter provides a deployment knob absent in conventional architectures. Open questions include whether the performance gap with pre-trained SOTA (UniVTG+PT) can be closed through longer training or larger spiking backbones, whether SFG generalizes beyond VTG to other video understanding tasks, and whether the energy estimates translate to actual neuromorphic hardware (e.g., Loihi 2) where the theoretical INT-ACC advantage is realized.
