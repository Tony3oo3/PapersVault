## Problem

The Johnson-Lindenstrauss lemma guarantees near-isometric dimension reduction only for data in Euclidean space with known coordinates, yet real-world dissimilarity measures are frequently non-Euclidean, non-metric, and often only available as pairwise values without underlying coordinates. No prior work provides JL-style guarantees for general symmetric hollow dissimilarity matrices with error bounds that reflect how far the data deviates from Euclidean geometry.

## Input / Output

The method takes as input an n×n symmetric hollow dissimilarity matrix (D_ij = D_ji, D_ii = 0) with no triangle inequality requirement, and produces a low-dimensional embedding of m = O(log n / ε²) dimensions. The pseudo-Euclidean variant outputs projected points annotated with a (p′, q′) signature indicating the bilinear form, while the power-distance variant outputs projected Euclidean centers paired with a common radius r = √|e_n|/2 derived from the smallest Gram eigenvalue. No raw coordinates are needed; only pairwise dissimilarities are consumed.

## Method

The paper presents two complementary JL transforms for non-Euclidean dissimilarity matrices. First, it shows any symmetric hollow matrix is the squared-distance matrix of vectors in pseudo-Euclidean space of signature (p, q), recovered via eigenvalue decomposition of the Gram matrix; applying standard JL projections separately to the p and q coordinate blocks yields a (1 ± ε·C_ij) multiplicative guarantee where C_ij is the ratio of squared Euclidean to squared (p,q) norms. Second, it proves any such matrix equals a generalized power-distance matrix of n points sharing a common radius r, where r is determined by the most negative Gram eigenvalue; applying JL to the Euclidean centers gives a (1 ± ε) multiplicative approximation plus an additive error of 4εr². Both approaches reduce to the classical JL lemma when the input is a Euclidean distance matrix (r = 0 or q = 0), and the additive/multiplicative error terms explicitly quantify the deviation from Euclidean geometry.

## Results

On the 10-dataset benchmark, the proposed JL-Power transform achieves the lowest average relative error on 7 of 10 datasets (e.g., 1.47 on MNIST, 1.61 on CIFAR-10, 1.32 on Email, 1.002 on Ball), while JL-PE dominates on the three genomics datasets (8.16 on Brain, 1.15 on Breast, 6.60 on Renal). The classical JL transform fails catastrophically on non-Euclidean data, reporting infinite relative error on MNIST and CIFAR-10 (different images collapse to the same point) and errors on the order of 10^9–10^12 on the cancer genomics datasets. On the downstream k-means clustering task, both proposed methods substantially reduce clustering cost compared to classical JL (e.g., Brain: 3.13 for JL-PE vs. 728.00 for JL; Breast: 12.53 for JL-Power vs. 827.69 for JL).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is the classical Johnson-Lindenstrauss transform (JL), which assumes Euclidean coordinates are available. On the core relative-error metric, JL-Power outperforms classical JL on 7 of 10 datasets, with the most dramatic gains on image data (MNIST: 1.47 vs. inf; CIFAR-10: 1.61 vs. inf) and graph data (Email: 1.60 vs. 15.64; Facebook: 1.32 vs. 12.62; MOOC: 2.04 vs. 39.83). JL-PE outperforms classical JL on the three genomics datasets where the data is highly non-Euclidean (Brain: 8.16 vs. 1.11e9; Breast: 1.15 vs. 9.86e7; Renal: 6.60 vs. 1.50e9). On the downstream k-means clustering task, both proposed methods reduce clustering cost by orders of magnitude over classical JL (e.g., Brain: 3.13/33.26 vs. 728.00; Breast: 28.93/12.53 vs. 827.69), with the single exception of Facebook where JL-Power (303,203) is slightly worse than classical JL (284,142). The paper notes that the relative superiority of JL-PE versus JL-Power depends on the spectral structure of the input Gram matrix, and formalizing when each is preferable is left as future work.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the classical Johnson-Lindenstrauss lemma (Johnson & Lindenstrauss, 1984) from Euclidean coordinate inputs to arbitrary symmetric hollow dissimilarity matrices, building on the pseudo-Euclidean bilinear form framework from the authors' prior Neuc-MDS work (Deng et al., NeurIPS 2024) and the coordinate-recovery step from classical MDS (Torgerson, 1952). It deliberately departs from worst-case lower-bound results for ℓ_p dimension reduction (Brinkman & Charikar, Naor et al.) by providing data-dependent, fine-grained error guarantees parameterized by the degree of non-Euclidean deviation. The generalized power distance representation is a new linear algebra result that connects to Casey's theorem, Ptolemy inequalities, and the silhouette coefficient, positioning the work at the intersection of metric geometry, pattern recognition, and statistical clustering.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work makes JL-style random projection applicable to a much broader class of real-world data (genomics, graphs, recommendation systems) where only pairwise dissimilarities are available and the geometry is non-Euclidean or non-metric. The fine-grained error analysis—where distortion degrades gracefully with the ratio of Euclidean to (p,q) norm or with the radius parameter r—gives practitioners a concrete diagnostic for when dimension reduction will be effective. Key open questions include: no theoretical connection between the two proposed representations (pseudo-Euclidean vs. power distance), no lower bound complementing the pseudo-Euclidean fine-grained guarantee, and unclear conditions under which one method dominates the other. The paper also leaves open the integration of these formulations into modern representation learning pipelines (hyperbolic, spherical, or mixed-curvature embeddings).
