## Problem

Traditional k-anonymity privacy guarantees are applied by dataset owners, leaving individual users (e.g., Reddit posters, LLM chat users) without interpretable estimates of how identifiable their self-disclosures are. LLMs struggle to reason over joint distributions of multiple interconnected personal attributes—Chain-of-Thought prompting fails on conditional dependencies (e.g., autism increasing social anxiety prevalence) and personally identifiable information—making naive sequential filtering unreliable for privacy risk estimation.

## Input / Output

The method takes a user post (Reddit post or user-LLM conversation, averaging ~139 words and 4.01 disclosures) along with gold-standard disclosure labels and categories provided externally to isolate the probabilistic reasoning step. It produces an integer k-value (ranging from 1 to ~56 million) representing the estimated number of people worldwide sharing the disclosed attributes, a Bayesian network structure encoding conditional dependencies, and a step-by-step reasoning chain with individual probability estimates. The k-value is computed as n·p where n is the relevant population and p is the joint probability reconstructed from the Bayesian network.

## Method

BRANCH (Bayesian Network Reasoning for k-ANonymity using Conditional Hierarchies) factorizes the joint probability of personal attributes into conditional probabilities via an implicitly constructed Bayesian network, enabling LLMs to estimate each factor independently rather than reasoning over all attributes simultaneously. The pipeline has five LLM-driven stages: (1) selecting feasible disclosures, (2) ordering variables from broadest to most specific by causality and statistical availability, (3) determining conditional dependencies (identifying which prior variables are conditionally independent), (4) generating textual queries and subqueries for each conditional probability (e.g., 'percentage of Townsbridge residents in Tech that are mothers'), and (5) estimating each query using the LLM's internal demographic knowledge, with low-confidence queries simplified by removing dependencies. The Bayesian network structure is then used to recombine all probability estimates into a single mathematical equation evaluated by a Python interpreter to yield k. This decomposition is the key differentiator: by estimating single-attribute demographics separately (where LLMs achieve ~16-17% average error, comparable to census margins) and combining them via the network structure, BRANCH avoids the compounding errors of sequential Chain-of-Thought filtering that ignores conditional dependencies and PII.

## Results

On the Privacy Risk Dataset (106 test documents), BRANCH-o3-mini achieves a Spearman's ρ of 0.873, log error of 1.99, and 72.61% range accuracy (within a factor of 5 of gold k), outperforming the best Chain-of-Thought baseline (o3-mini CoT: ρ=0.744, log error=2.81, 59.13% range) by 13 percentage points in range accuracy. BRANCH-GPT-4o achieves ρ=0.839, log error=2.16, and 66.96% range accuracy. On complex posts with 4+ disclosures, the gap widens substantially: BRANCH-o3-mini reaches 74.22% range accuracy while o3-mini CoT drops to 55.47%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BRANCH consistently outperforms all Chain-of-Thought baselines across every model tested. Against o3-mini CoT (the strongest baseline at 59.13% range accuracy), BRANCH-o3-mini improves to 72.61% (+13.48 pp) with a statistically significant gain (p<0.001). Against GPT-4o CoT (55.22%), BRANCH-GPT-4o reaches 66.96% (+11.74 pp, p<0.01). BRANCH also surpasses the reasoning-specialized models o1-preview (55.66%) and DeepSeek R1 (56.96%) by 11–17 pp in range accuracy, indicating that structured probabilistic decomposition via Bayesian networks is more effective than extended chain-of-thought for this task. Program-of-Thought prompting (GPT-4o: 54.78%) and Few-Shot prompting (GPT-4o: 42.17%) are substantially weaker, confirming that the multi-step Bayesian network construction in BRANCH is critical. On the cross-domain ShareGPT subset (user-LLM conversations with PII), the advantage is even larger: BRANCH-o3-mini achieves 73.42% range accuracy versus 48.10% for o3-mini CoT, a 25 pp gap, while BRANCH-GPT-4o reaches 67.09% versus 51.90% for GPT-4o CoT.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes k-anonymity (Sweeney, 2002) by inverting its perspective: rather than a dataset owner anonymizing records, an end-user estimates how many people share the attributes they are about to disclose. Methodologically, it extends Bayesian network structure elicitation with LLMs (Babakov et al., 2024) and self-consistency-based uncertainty estimation (Wang et al., 2023) into a multi-step pipeline that decomposes a joint distribution over personal attributes into individually estimable conditional probabilities. It departs from prior LLM privacy work (re-identification attacks, disclosure detection) by requiring no access to a target database and instead relying on the model's internal demographic knowledge, and from standard CoT/PoT prompting by explicitly modeling inter-attribute dependencies rather than applying sequential filters.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first systematic benchmark and method for LLM-based privacy risk quantification from free text, filling a gap between dataset-owner-centric privacy metrics and user-facing risk communication. The finding that structured probabilistic decomposition (Bayesian networks) substantially outperforms free-form chain-of-thought reasoning—especially as attribute count grows—suggests that 'thinking harder' models are not automatically better at real-world probabilistic estimation, pointing to a broader lesson about when structured inference beats unstructured reasoning. Key open questions include scaling to posts with many disclosures, conducting user studies to validate that k-values actually change user behavior, auditing for demographic bias in LLM-internal priors, and reducing the multi-step computational cost for practical deployment.
