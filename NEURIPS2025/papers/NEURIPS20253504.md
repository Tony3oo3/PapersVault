## Problem

Chamfer Distance-based losses used to supervise point cloud prediction models rely on discrete nearest-neighbor assignments that induce many-to-one correspondences, causing point clumping in dense regions and poor coverage in sparse regions, while also introducing non-differentiable index-selection operations. Earth Mover's Distance enforces one-to-one matching and captures structural similarity far better, but its cubic computational complexity and equal-cardinality requirement make it impractical for training deep networks at scale. The paper addresses the absence of a fully differentiable, near-quadratic-cost loss that approximates EMD's one-to-one matching without manual regularization tuning.

## Input / Output

APML is a loss function that consumes a predicted point set X̂ ∈ ℝ^{B×N×d} and a ground-truth point set X ∈ ℝ^{B×M×d} (where N and M may differ, unlike exact EMD) and returns a scalar matching cost. In completion tasks the predicted set is a full 16,384-point shape generated from a 2,048-point partial input; in the WiFi-CSI generation task the predicted set is a 3-D human point cloud produced by a spatio-temporal Transformer from raw CSI amplitude/phase measurements across antennas and subcarriers. The loss operates on the N×M pairwise Euclidean distance matrix, which is the primary memory bottleneck (quadratic in point count).

## Method

APML constructs a soft, probabilistic transport plan between predicted and ground-truth points in three stages. First, an adaptive softmax is applied independently to each row and column of the pairwise cost matrix: for each cost vector, a temperature T is computed in closed form from the local distance gap g (the second-smallest normalized cost plus a small margin δ) so that the closest match receives at least a minimum probability p_min, eliminating the need to tune a global Sinkhorn regularizer. Second, the row-wise and column-wise probability matrices are averaged to enforce bidirectional consistency. Third, a fixed number of Sinkhorn-Knopp iterations (alternating row and column normalization) refine the symmetrized matrix into an approximately doubly stochastic transport plan. The final loss is the Frobenius inner product ⟨P, C⟩_F, i.e., the expected matching cost under the learned soft assignments. Because every step is differentiable and the temperature adapts per-row to local geometry, APML achieves near-quadratic O(NM(d+L)) complexity with smooth gradients, reducing EMD by 15–81% over CD-based losses across FoldingNet, PCN, PoinTr, and CSI2PC backbones.

## Results

APML achieves the lowest EMD×100 across all completion and generation settings, reducing EMD by 15–81% relative to Chamfer-based losses (e.g., 4.72 vs. 12.87 on PCN+FoldingNet; 9.07 vs. 26.50 on SN55+FoldingNet; 14.11 vs. 34.20 on MM-Fi+CSI2PC). F1 scores are comparable or slightly lower than the best CD variant in some configurations (e.g., 0.67 vs. 0.77 for HCD on PCN+PoinTr), but the paper argues F1 does not capture structural fidelity, and qualitative/EMD evidence favors APML. On the WiFi-CSI generation task, APML reduces EMD×100 from 25.68 (DCD) to 14.11 while keeping CD within 0.004 of the best baseline. Training time increases by ~30% over CD (76h vs. 55h for FoldingNet on SN55), but APML converges to strong validation F1 in substantially fewer epochs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against **CD** [Fan et al., CVPR 2017], APML wins decisively on EMD in every configuration (e.g., 4.72 vs. 12.87 on PCN+FoldingNet, a 63% reduction) while matching or slightly exceeding F1; the gap widens on harder datasets (SN55: 9.07 vs. 26.50). Against **HyperCD** [Lin et al., CVPR 2023], APML again dominates on EMD (e.g., 5.62 vs. 8.79 on PCN+PoinTr) but concedes F1 in a few settings (0.67 vs. 0.77 on PCN+PoinTr), which the authors attribute to F1's insensitivity to structural alignment. Against **InfoCD** [Lin et al., NeurIPS 2023], APML reduces EMD by 15–50% across all backbones (e.g., 5.34 vs. 21.24 on PCN+PCN) with comparable F1. Against **DCD** [Wang et al., ICCV 2021], APML lowers EMD×100 on SN34 (9.49 vs. 11.52 FoldingNet; 8.14 vs. 9.48 PoinTr) and dramatically on MM-Fi (14.11 vs. 25.68, a 45% reduction) while matching or improving F1. Against **SWD** [Nguyen et al., ICCV 2021], APML achieves lower EMD on SN34+FoldingNet (9.49 vs. 11.98) with higher F1 (0.20 vs. 0.11); SWD was not stable or applicable in most other configurations. Overall, APML is the only method that consistently minimizes EMD across all six datasets and four backbones, at the cost of a ~30% training-time overhead and higher GPU memory (~320 GB vs. <64 GB for CD).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the entropy-regularized optimal transport framework of Cuturi (2013) and the Sinkhorn-Knopp normalization procedure, applying them to point cloud supervision for the first time in a completion/generation context. It departs from prior Sinkhorn-based OT methods by replacing the fixed regularization parameter ε with an analytically derived, data-dependent temperature that guarantees a minimum assignment probability per row/column, eliminating manual tuning. Conceptually, it positions itself as a bridge between the efficiency of Chamfer Distance and the geometric fidelity of exact EMD, while also distinguishing itself from CD variants (DCD, HyperCD, InfoCD, LCD) that still rely on discrete nearest-neighbor assignments.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

APML offers a practical, drop-in replacement for CD that consistently reduces EMD by 15–81% across three architectures and multiple datasets, with only ~15–30% runtime overhead and a single interpretable hyperparameter. The finding that APML can worsen CD-L1/L2 while improving EMD and perceptual quality reinforces critiques that CD is a poor proxy for structural fidelity. Key open questions include scaling to 16k+ points (quadratic memory), validation on noisy real-scan datasets (ScanNet, KITTI), and whether low-rank or sparse Sinkhorn variants can reduce the memory bottleneck. The WiFi-CSI cross-modal evaluation suggests generality beyond synthetic ShapeNet, but the single-seed protocol and absence of error bars limit statistical confidence in the reported margins.
