## Problem

Contrastive alignment methods like DPO suffer from likelihood underdetermination: the loss is invariant to simultaneous shifts in absolute log-probabilities of preferred and dispreferred responses, causing both to decrease during training and inadvertently elevating out-of-distribution responses (reward hacking without an explicit reward model). Additionally, no single alignment framework unifies pairwise, binary, and scalar feedback—DPO handles only pairwise, KTO handles binary but not scalar, and NCA handles scalar but not binary.

## Input / Output

The method consumes preference data in any of three formats (pairwise comparisons y_w ≻ y_l, binary desired/undesired labels, or continuous scalar scores) along with a fixed reference model π_ref and a base LLM π_θ. A key design choice is the hyper response H, which aggregates all unobserved responses into a single indistinguishable unit, so the regularizer is computed over the compact space Y_H = {H} ∪ {labeled responses} rather than the full response space. The output is an aligned LLM whose absolute likelihoods for labeled responses are provably well-determined relative to the reference model, not merely their relative gaps.

## Method

PRO is built on a decomposition of the population DPO loss into a pointwise optimizer (a score function ŝ(y) weighted against log π_θ(y)) and a label-independent regularizer (a KL divergence between Bernoulli(1/2) and Bernoulli(σ(r(y₁)−r(y₂)))). The paper identifies that standard DPO's likelihood underdetermination arises from estimating this regularizer using only labeled responses, which collapses its constraint on absolute probabilities; restoring the full regularizer over the entire response distribution resolves the issue. To make the full regularizer tractable without sampling from the base model, PRO introduces a hyper response H that lumps all unobserved responses into one unit, so p(H) = 1 − Σ_{y∉H} p(y) is computed in closed form from labeled-response probabilities alone. The score function ŝ(y) generalizes naturally: for pairwise feedback it is E_{y'~μ̂}[p̂(y≻y')] − 1/2, and for pointwise (binary or scalar) feedback it is b̂(y) − E_{y~μ̂}[b̂(y)], yielding a single loss that accommodates all three feedback types. A sufficient condition (α > α₀) guarantees the existence of an optimal solution, and a special parameterization (α = 1/η² with a specific μ) recovers an enhanced DPO variant with pseudo preference labels.

## Results

On the UltraFeedback alignment benchmark (Mistral-7B-sft), PRO-B achieves the best average rank (1.7) across six evaluation tasks, followed by PRO-P (2.0), compared to DPO (3.3), KTO (3.9), and NCA (4.0). PRO-B leads on AlpacaEval 2 (20.46% LC, 21.48% WR), IFEval (30.13), and GPQA (32.81), while PRO-P leads on ARC (61.26) and TruthfulQA (43.81). Under extremely imbalanced binary feedback (1% desired, 99% undesired), tuning the regularization coefficient α from 2.5 to 17.5 improves PRO-B's win rate from 5.57% to 57.21%, surpassing the 53.37% achieved with the full balanced dataset. On scalar feedback (UltraFeedback, N=4), PRO-S matches or exceeds NCA on IFEval (29.43 vs 28.96), TruthfulQA (49.45 vs 45.78), and GPQA (32.81 vs 32.14).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DPO, both PRO-P and PRO-B outperform on AlpacaEval 2 (LC: 20.21/20.46 vs 18.36; WR: 19.56/21.48 vs 19.80), IFEval (29.02/30.13 vs 19.22), and GPQA (32.59/32.81 vs 32.04), while DPO retains a slight edge on ARC (61.77 vs 61.26/59.81). Against KTO, PRO methods dominate on AlpacaEval 2, ARC, IFEval, and GPQA, with PRO-B achieving 21.48% WR versus KTO's 14.60% and 30.13 on IFEval versus 25.69. Against NCA, PRO-B leads on AlpacaEval 2 (21.48% WR vs 13.43%), IFEval (30.13 vs 26.43), and GPQA (32.81 vs 32.14), while NCA edges out on MT-Bench (6.03 vs 6.06 is actually PRO-B winning by 0.03). Notably, DPO outperforms KTO and NCA on AlpacaEval 2 and ARC but underperforms on MT-Bench, suggesting the non-contrastive baselines partially mitigate likelihood underdetermination; PRO achieves the best of both worlds by combining contrastive optimization with a full regularizer.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on DPO (Rafailov et al., NeurIPS 2023) by providing a principled decomposition of its loss into a pointwise optimizer and a contrastive regularizer (Theorem 3.1), revealing that DPO inherently supports heterogeneous feedback beyond pairwise comparisons. It departs from prior attempts to fix likelihood underdetermination (Smaug, Cal-DPO) that inject additional supervised or regression signals, instead identifying the oversimplified regularizer as the root cause and restoring it via a hyper-response mechanism. The method also unifies the separate frameworks of KTO (binary feedback) and NCA (scalar feedback) under a single loss, addressing a gap the authors explicitly note: 'there is still no unified approach that seamlessly accommodates all these feedback types.'

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The decomposition provides a clean theoretical explanation for a widely observed but poorly understood phenomenon (likelihood underdetermination in DPO), and the hyper-response fix is computationally negligible relative to standard DPO, making it easy to adopt. The unified framework for pairwise, binary, and scalar feedback is practically valuable as annotation formats vary across deployment scenarios. Open questions include how existing DPO improvements (e.g., reference-model reparameterization from Tutnov et al.) interact with PRO, whether the connection to RLHF's optimizer-regularizer structure enables gentler regularization or better model diversity, and whether the α hyperparameter's sensitivity (critical in the 1%-desired experiment) generalizes across model scales.
