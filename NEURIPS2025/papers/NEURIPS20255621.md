## Problem

Existing non-Gaussian diffusion models such as Beta diffusion apply one-dimensional transitions independently along each coordinate, preventing them from capturing interdependencies among components in high-dimensional simplex-structured data. Additionally, standard training objectives based on KL divergence or reweighted ELBO suffer from asymmetry and lack a principled proper divergence guarantee, potentially inducing false convergence during optimization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255621/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes random Dirichlet-distributed noise on the K-dimensional simplex as its starting point and iteratively produces synthetic RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10, 256×256 for FFHQ/AFHQ). Unlike Gaussian diffusion's additive noise, the forward process applies multiplicative transformations that simultaneously noise and mask pixels, while the reverse process denoises and demasks. Input images are affine-transformed (shift 0.6, scale 0.39) to satisfy the simplex constraint before diffusion, and the generator f_θ predicts the clean image at each reverse step.

## Method

The framework generalizes Beta diffusion to a K-dimensional Dirichlet process that preserves the simplex structure at every noising step via time-varying multiplicative transformations z_t|x_0 ~ Dir(η α_{t,i} x_{0,i}). For training, the paper introduces two novel divergence-based losses: the Proper Hölder Divergence (PHD), which is strictly proper (zero if and only if the two distributions coincide), and the Proper Hölder-Kullback (PHK) divergence, a weighted combination of PHD and KLUB that restores symmetry missing in existing formulations. Both losses admit closed-form expressions under the Dirichlet exponential-family log-normalizer, yielding analytic gradients through the digamma function. The paper proves that PHD provides lower-variance gradient estimates than pseudo-divergence objectives, improving training stability. Trained via variational inference with the PHK objective, the model achieves FID 2.78 on unconditional CIFAR-10, outperforming all prior non-Gaussian diffusion methods.

## Results

On unconditional CIFAR-10, the proposed Dirichlet diffusion model with PHK loss achieves an FID of 2.78, the best score among all listed baselines including both Gaussian and non-Gaussian diffusion models. In the ablation study (Table 2), PHK with batch size 512 and 2000 NFE reaches FID 2.82, outperforming -ELBO (4.31), KLUB (3.25), and PHD (3.51) at the same compute budget. The paper also reports that PHK reduces training cost by approximately 9% compared to KLUB while delivering superior FID.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed Dirichlet diffusion (FID 2.78) outperforms every baseline in Table 1. Among non-Gaussian methods, it improves over Beta Diffusion [16] (3.06) by 0.28 FID, JUMP [15] (4.80) by 2.02, and D3PM [12] (7.34) by 4.56. Against deterministic baselines, it beats Cold Diffusion [17] (8.92 inpainting) and Inverse Heat Dispersion [47] (18.96) by large margins. Among Gaussian models, it surpasses the strongest competitors—TDPM+ [50] (2.83), RDUOT [57] (2.95), and Improved DDPM [49] (2.94)—by 0.05–0.16 FID, and dominates weaker Gaussian baselines such as DDPM [2] (3.17), Blurring Diffusion [52] (3.17), and UniPC [56] (3.13) by roughly 0.4 FID. In the ablation (Table 2), PHK consistently yields the lowest FID across all NFE and batch-size configurations, e.g., 2.82 vs. 3.25 (KLUB) and 4.31 (-ELBO) at NFE=2000, B=512, confirming that the Hölder-based loss is the primary driver of the gain rather than architectural changes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalizes Beta Diffusion (Zhou et al., NeurIPS 2023) from independent 1D Beta transitions to a K-dimensional Dirichlet process that preserves the simplex structure at every noising step, enabling modeling of interdependencies among components. For the training objective, it departs from the KL-divergence-based ELBO/KLUB framework by introducing the Proper Hölder Divergence (PHD) and a hybrid Proper Hölder-Kullback (PHK) loss, both derived from the Hölder projective divergence theory of Nielsen et al. (2017). The key conceptual departure is twofold: replacing additive Gaussian noise with multiplicative Dirichlet noise (which simultaneously noises and masks pixels), and replacing asymmetric KL-based losses with strictly proper, symmetric Hölder-based divergences that admit closed-form solutions under the Dirichlet exponential family.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework demonstrates that non-Gaussian, simplex-structured diffusion can match or exceed the best Gaussian baselines on unconditional CIFAR-10 (FID 2.78 vs. 2.83–2.95 for top Gaussian methods), challenging the near-universal reliance on Gaussian priors in diffusion modeling. The PHK loss also reduces training cost by ~9% relative to KLUB, suggesting that divergence choice is a practical lever beyond just distribution choice. However, the empirical evidence is limited to a single primary benchmark (CIFAR-10) without error bars, the authors acknowledge sensitivity to hyperparameters and high compute cost (~64 hours on 4×L40S), and the approach has not yet been tested on conditional generation, larger datasets, or non-image modalities. Open questions include whether the Dirichlet+Blurring connection the authors sketch can yield a truly generalized reverse-process framework, and whether the Hölder-divergence advantage persists at scale.
