## Problem

Large reasoning models (e.g., OpenAI-o1, DeepSeek-R1) apply Long Chain-of-Thought uniformly across all tasks, causing the 'overthinking' problem where easy questions consume hundreds of unnecessary tokens without performance gains. Existing efficiency strategies require human-specified token budgets or specialized length-constrained training, which are inaccurate in practice and contradict the goal of fully autonomous AI. The paper asks whether a model can learn to autonomously select an appropriate reasoning format (from a small set of efficient and elaborate options) based on task difficulty, without external budget control.

## Input / Output

The model receives a text question and, optionally, a special format token (e.g., <Long_CoT>) that forces a specific reasoning mode in Instruction-Guided Mode; in the default Adaptive Mode no format token is provided and the model selects one autonomously. The output is a structured response wrapped in format-specific special tokens (e.g., <CODE>...</CODE>, <SHORT_COT>...</SHORT_COT>, <LONG_COT>...</LONG_COT>) followed by a final answer. In Consensus-Guided Mode, the model generates three separate outputs (Direct Answer, Short CoT, Code), checks for agreement, and falls back to Long CoT only on disagreement, effectively producing up to four reasoning traces per query.

## Method

ARM is trained in two stages. Stage 1 (SFT) fine-tunes a Qwen2.5 backbone on 10.8K AQuA-Rat questions, each annotated with solutions in four reasoning formats (Direct Answer, Short CoT, Code, Long CoT), using LoRA and DeepSpeed ZeRO-3. Stage 2 introduces Ada-GRPO, a modification of GRPO that addresses 'format collapse'—the tendency of standard GRPO to converge on Long CoT because it has the highest accuracy. Ada-GRPO scales each sample's binary reward by a format diversity factor α_i(t) = [G / F(o_i)] · decay_i(t), where F(o_i) counts how many times that format appears in the rollout group and decay_i(t) follows a cosine schedule from 1 to 0.5 over training, so rare formats receive amplified rewards early but the boost fades as training progresses. This encourages exploration of efficient formats initially while preserving accuracy-driven convergence later, yielding ~30% average token reduction (up to ~70% on easy tasks) at comparable accuracy and a ~2× training speedup from shorter rollouts.

## Results

ARM achieves comparable accuracy to the GRPO baseline (which relies solely on Long CoT) while saving an average of ~30% tokens, and up to ~70% on easy commonsense tasks. Specifically, ARM-7B (maj@8) reaches 75.9% average accuracy vs. 76.1% for Qwen2.5-7B_SFT+GRPO, while using only 786 vs. 1164 average tokens (−32.5%). On the hardest benchmark (AIME'25), ARM-14B actually surpasses the GRPO baseline (23.3% vs. 20.0% at maj@8). Additionally, Ada-GRPO yields a ~2× training speedup over GRPO due to shorter rollouts from efficient format selection.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary baseline Qwen2.5_SFT+GRPO (which uses Long CoT uniformly), ARM matches accuracy within <1% on average across all three model sizes while saving 31–34% tokens overall and 73–75% on easy commonsense tasks (CSQA, OBQA). On the hardest benchmark AIME'25, ARM-14B outperforms the GRPO baseline by +3.3 points (23.3 vs. 20.0 at maj@8), showing that adaptive format selection does not sacrifice hard-task performance. Compared to the SFT-only baseline, ARM dramatically improves on medium and hard tasks (e.g., GSM8K: 93.7% vs. 79.4% for 7B) because SFT alone produces a near-uniform format distribution that inappropriately selects Direct Answer for complex problems. Against external reasoning models, ARM-7B achieves comparable average accuracy to DS-R1-Distill-7B (75.9% vs. 75.5%) while using only 27.8% of the tokens (786 vs. 2797). Against length-penalty strategies (L1, ThinkPrune), ARM maintains stable performance across token budgets, whereas L1 degrades severely under underestimated budgets (e.g., 3.3% on AIME at 512 tokens) and wastes tokens on easy tasks (CSQA accuracy barely changes from 45.8% to 46.1% between 512 and 3600 tokens). On additional benchmarks (GPQA, StrategyQA), ARM-7B matches or slightly exceeds the GRPO baseline while saving 41–65% tokens, confirming generalization beyond the training distribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on GRPO (Group Relative Policy Optimization) from DeepSeekMath, adapting it into Ada-GRPO to address a newly identified 'format collapse' problem where standard GRPO converges to Long CoT exclusively. It positions itself against two prior lines of work: (1) large reasoning models (OpenAI-o1, DeepSeek-R1) that apply Long CoT uniformly across all tasks, causing the 'overthinking' problem, and (2) length-penalty-based strategies (L1, ThinkPrune) that require human-specified token budgets. The key conceptual departure is replacing a single reasoning format with four distinct formats (Direct Answer, Short CoT, Code, Long CoT) and training the model to select among them adaptively via a format-diversity reward mechanism, rather than constraining output length post-hoc.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ARM offers a practical path toward autonomous reasoning efficiency: the model decides its own reasoning depth without human intervention, saving ~30% tokens on average (up to ~70% on easy tasks) while maintaining accuracy, and achieving a ~2× training speedup. The identification of format collapse as a failure mode of GRPO is a useful diagnostic for anyone training multi-format reasoning models. Open questions include whether the four predefined formats are sufficient for all task types, whether the approach scales to harder benchmarks (the authors note they exclude AIME training data), and whether the adaptive selection generalizes to domains beyond commonsense, mathematical, and symbolic reasoning. The reliance on Qwen2.5 as the primary backbone (with limited LLaMA validation) also leaves generalizability across architectures partially open.
