## Problem

In multi-agent reinforcement learning, long-term dependencies and non-Markovian dynamics require conditioning policies on historical context, but extending context length introduces two coupled challenges: escalating computational cost and degraded input representation due to high-dimensional redundancy. Existing approaches either fix the context length statically (failing to adapt to changing environments) or optimize length only for single-agent settings, leaving no systematic framework that jointly addresses both the computational and representational difficulties in MARL.

## Input / Output

The method consumes per-agent historical observation sequences (e.g., 24-dim state vectors in Sample Spread, 3×3×6 grid cells in MiniGrid, 26- or 34-dim vectors in GRF) and converts them via DFT into frequency-domain coefficients. The central agent receives only the low-frequency-truncated representation (a subset of DFT bins determined by a tunable parameter m), while decentralized agents receive the corresponding time-domain contextual window plus their current observation. The output is a discrete context-length selection from a dyadic set (e.g., {0,1,2,4,8,16,32,64} for MiniGrid) that determines how many past steps of history each agent conditions on, followed by standard per-agent action outputs.

## Method

ACL-LFT introduces a hierarchical architecture with three coupled components. First, a Fourier-based low-frequency truncation module applies the Discrete Fourier Transform to historical states and uses a discrete Dyadic Partition of Unity (extending Littlewood-Paley theory) to isolate low-frequency bands via window functions X[k] and Φ_j[k], preserving global temporal trends while discarding high-frequency noise. Second, a central agent selects the truncation level m from a discrete dyadic action space, trained with PPO using a reward that is a multi-head attention-weighted aggregation of decentralized agents' rewards (where attention weights are computed from value-function and policy-distribution representations). Third, spatio-temporal decoupling separates training: the central agent optimizes the temporal context-length component independently, while decentralized agents jointly refine policies using the filtered temporal information plus current spatial observations. A theoretical regret bound (Theorem 1) establishes that adaptive context length achieves Ω(T) cumulative advantage over any fixed length in non-stationary environments, with the adaptive policy's sublinear O(T^α) loss (α<1) arising from polynomial convergence to the time-varying optimal window.

## Results

On SMACv2 with the MAPPO backbone, ACL-LFT achieves 78.9% on 3s5z_vs_3s6z, 52.7% on 5m_vs_6m, and 77.9% on corridor, outperforming the best baseline (AMAGO) by +2.8%, +4.6%, and +3.6% respectively. Across all three MARL backbones (MAPPO, QMIX, QPLEX), ACL-LFT consistently leads with average improvements of +2.6% to +4.6% over the strongest baseline. On the GRF environments (Academy 3 vs 1 with Keeper and Counterattack-Hard), ACL-LFT demonstrates the fastest exploration efficiency and highest post-convergence win rate compared to Transformer, ToST, and AMAGO, with the performance gap widening as task complexity increases.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ACL-LFT outperforms all four sequence-processing baselines (Transformer, ToST, AMAGO, Mamba) across every SMACv2 task and MARL backbone tested. AMAGO is consistently the strongest baseline, benefiting from its parallel long-sequence processing, but ACL-LFT still leads by 2.6–4.6% in win rate; for example, on 5m_vs_6m with MAPPO, ACL-LFT reaches 52.7% versus AMAGO's 48.1%. On the GRF environments, ACL-LFT shows the fastest convergence and highest final win rate, while Transformer and ToST exhibit large exploration oscillations and AMAGO converges prematurely due to its fixed context length introducing noise. Against fixed-length baselines (8, 16, 32, 64 steps) on GRF, ACL-LFT significantly outperforms all, confirming that longer context does not monotonically improve performance and that adaptive selection is critical. In the decentralized ablation (no cross-agent history sharing), ACL-LFT still beats AMAGO and Mamba on all three SMACv2 tasks, verifying that gains come from the Fourier-based temporal representation and adaptive length rather than implicit information sharing.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on contextual information in MARL (e.g., AMAGO, Transformer-based sequence models) by replacing fixed context lengths with an adaptively optimized one, and by repurposing Littlewood-Paley harmonic analysis (Dyadic Partition of Unity) from PDE theory as a frequency-domain filtering mechanism for agent observation histories. Prior approaches to context length optimization in RL either produced static lengths via pre-training or relied on parallel computation to handle longer sequences, but none addressed the joint challenge of adaptive length selection and efficient representation in a multi-agent setting. The central-agent architecture with attention-weighted reward aggregation draws on hierarchical RL and multi-head attention, while the theoretical regret bound connects to information-theoretic analyses of windowed estimation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in MARL: fixed context lengths are suboptimal in non-stationary environments, and naively increasing them introduces noise and computational overhead. The theoretical guarantee (linear regret for fixed vs. sublinear for adaptive) provides a principled justification for adaptive context, though it relies on assumptions (Gaussian posterior, convex variance behavior, polynomial convergence rate) that may not hold in all MARL settings. Open questions include scalability to large agent populations where the central agent becomes a bottleneck, applicability to continuous action spaces, and whether the semi-centralized information flow (central agent processing all histories) conflicts with the decentralized decision-making assumption in partially observable settings.
