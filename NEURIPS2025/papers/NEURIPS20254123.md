## Problem

Fine-tuning-based LLM unlearning suffers from two fundamental limitations: it cannot constrain the scope of the forgetting effect, and it cannot distinguish benign tokens from unlearning signals. This creates a critical vulnerability where a malicious user can submit a subtly modified forgetting dataset that causes the unlearned model to degrade in utility for all downstream users who naturally use common words like 'please' or 'then'. The paper formalizes this as a Stealthy Attack (SA) and proposes a principled defense.

## Input / Output

The method operates on an LLM (e.g., LLaMA 3.1 8B, Mistral 7B) given a forgetting corpus D_f and a retaining corpus D_r, producing updated model parameters. The attack modifies D_f by wrapping a fraction (p=0.33) of sentences in natural templates containing benign trigger tokens, yielding a stealthily altered D_f'. The defense dynamically constructs scope samples within each training batch by concatenating a forgetting prompt with a retaining prompt, requiring no separate dataset or preprocessing step.

## Method

Scope-aware Unlearning (SU) introduces a scope term into the standard fine-tuning-based unlearning objective (Eq. 5), which encourages the model to preserve its response ability to the normal portion of an input even when unlearning signals are present. Scope samples are generated on-the-fly by concatenating a prompt x_f from the forgetting set with a prompt x_r from the retaining set, and the model is trained with standard next-token prediction loss on the retaining portion of this concatenated input. The coefficient η controls the relative strength of this constraint, and the approach is model-agnostic, requiring no token-level filtering or additional data processing. The Stealthy Attack (SA) exploits the vulnerability by applying a corpus transformation T that rewrites sentences into templates containing selected benign tokens (e.g., 'Please tell me...', 'Then...'), amplifying their association with unlearning signals during fine-tuning. Experiments across GD, NPO, and IDK on TOFU and RWKU show SA degrades benign-trigger utility by 45–86%, while SU recovers it to near no-attack levels while preserving unlearning effectiveness.

## Results

Under the Stealthy Attack (SA), vanilla fine-tuning-based unlearning methods suffer severe benign-trigger utility degradation: GD on LLaMA drops from 0.683 to 0.160 (10% forgetting), and NPO on Mistral drops from 0.556 to 0.012. Scope-aware Unlearning (SU) recovers benign-trigger utility substantially while preserving unlearning effectiveness—e.g., SU-GD on LLaMA at 10% restores trigger utility to 0.684 (vs. 0.160 vanilla) with unlearning score of 0.199, and SU-NPO on LLaMA at 10% achieves 0.734 trigger utility (vs. 0.217 vanilla) with unlearning score of 0.256. Clean utility remains largely stable across all settings, confirming that SU localizes the forgetting effect without sacrificing general model performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SU is evaluated as an additive enhancement on top of three base unlearning methods: GD (Liu et al., CoLLA 2022), NPO (Zhang et al., 2024), and IDK (Ren et al., 2025). Under SA, SU consistently outperforms the vanilla versions on benign-trigger utility across all three methods and both models (LLaMA 3.1 8B, Mistral 7B), with the largest gains on GD (e.g., +0.524 on LLaMA at 10%) and NPO (e.g., +0.517 on LLaMA at 10%), while unlearning effectiveness is preserved or slightly improved. The paper also compares SU against two defenses adapted from LLM data-poisoning literature: continuous fine-tuning (Tong et al., ICLR 2025) and high-loss anomaly detection (Pathmanathan et al., ICML 2024 Workshop). SU dominates both: on LLaMA, SU achieves 0.671 benign-trigger utility versus 0.537 (continuous fine-tuning) and 0.336 (anomaly detection), while also maintaining better unlearning effectiveness (0.161 vs. 0.343 and 0.124 respectively). On Mistral, the gap is even wider, with SU reaching 0.262 trigger utility versus 0.213 and 0.181 for the two baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the fine-tuning-based LLM unlearning literature (Gradient Ascent, NPO, IDK) and the key observation from Ren et al. [20] that unlearning memorizes forgetting data as behavioral signals rather than truly erasing knowledge. It extends prior findings on unlearning overgeneralization [15, 16] by identifying a concrete adversarial exploitation path: a malicious user can amplify benign tokens in forgetting data to hijack the unlearning signal. The proposed Scope-aware Unlearning departs from token-level filtering approaches by instead adding a scope term to the unlearning objective, constraining the forgetting effect to the correct context without requiring identification of which tokens are 'benign' versus 'target.'

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work reveals a practical security vulnerability in any unlearning service that accepts user-submitted data, with the attack being both simple (adding common words like 'please') and stealthy (evading standard anomaly detection). The defense is lightweight—a single additional loss term—and model-agnostic, making it easy to integrate into existing pipelines. However, the analysis is limited to fine-tuning-based methods; whether inference-time approaches like Task Vector are similarly vulnerable (the paper shows partial vulnerability) or whether more sophisticated attacks exist remains open. The broader implication is that unlearning services need adversarial robustness guarantees before being deployed in production settings governed by regulations like GDPR.
