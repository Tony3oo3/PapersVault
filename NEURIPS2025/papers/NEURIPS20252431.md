## Problem

The C-DAG framework for causal reasoning over variable clusters requires partition admissibility, meaning the cluster-level graph must be acyclic. When the chosen clustering induces cycles between clusters—a common occurrence in settings like macroeconomics or neuroscience where sector-level relationships are known but intra-cluster links are not—the partition is deemed inadmissible and no valid causal calculus applies. This paper removes that restriction, extending the framework to arbitrary clusterings that may produce cyclic C-DAGs with self-loops.

## Input / Output

The method takes a C-DAG (a mixed graph with directed and bidirected edges over clusters), the known cardinality of each cluster, and cluster-level query sets (X, Y, Z, W). It produces graphical criteria for each of Pearl's three do-calculus rules, enabling derivation of do-free identification formulas and cluster d-separation determinations. A key practical constraint is that any cluster of size greater than 3 can be reduced to size 3 without affecting the calculus outcome, bounding the combinatorial search space for structures of interest.

## Method

The core reformulation replaces path-based d-separation with a structure-based criterion: a 'structure of interest' is a connected ADMG where each node has at most one outgoing arrow (or two outgoing but no incoming), and d-connection between two sets is equivalent to the existence of a connecting structure of interest (Theorem 1). Two auxiliary graphs are constructed: the canonical compatible graph (a specific compatible graph that can be unioned with any other compatible graph without creating cycles) and the unfolded graph (a supergraph of all compatible graphs that serves as the search space). The calculus (Theorem 2) checks for structures of interest in the mutilated unfolded graph, then verifies acyclicity of their union with the canonical compatible graph to filter spurious structures that do not correspond to any actual compatible graph. This two-step strategy resolves the non-commutativity between mutilation and enumeration of compatible graphs, which prevents direct application of do-calculus on the mutilated C-DAG. Theorem 4 further shows that reducing all clusters to size at most 3 preserves all relevant dependencies, making the calculus computationally tractable.

## Results

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical experiments or quantitative benchmarks. The main contributions are formal guarantees: (1) Theorem 1 establishes the equivalence between classical d-separation in an ADMG and the existence of a 'connecting structure of interest'; (2) Theorem 2 provides three calculus rules (R1–R3) that are sound for all compatible graphs, with graphical criteria expressed via the unfolded graph and canonical compatible graph; (3) Theorem 3 proves atomic completeness — if a rule's criterion fails, there exists a compatible graph where Pearl's corresponding rule fails; (4) Theorem 4 shows that any cluster of size >3 can be reduced to size 3 without losing any causal information (the bound is tight, as shown in Example 4).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper contains no empirical baselines or quantitative comparisons, as it is a theoretical contribution. The primary 'comparison' is with Anand et al. (2023), whose framework restricts C-DAGs to be acyclic (partition admissibility). The paper explicitly recovers Anand et al.'s results as a special case: when the C-DAG is acyclic, the unfolded graph is itself a compatible graph and standard d-separation applies directly (Section 4, 'Recovering the results on standard C-DAGs'). The extension to cyclic C-DAGs is the key novelty, enabling causal reasoning in scenarios (e.g., macroeconomics, neuroscience) where cluster-level cycles are expected. No other prior methods (e.g., Jaber et al. 2022, Perković et al. 2018, Ferreira and Assaad 2025) are formally compared, though the paper positions its structure-based criterion as an alternative to the proliferation of abstraction-specific separation rules in those works.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the Cluster-DAG (C-DAG) framework of Anand et al. (2023), which required partition admissibility (i.e., acyclicity at the cluster level), by removing that constraint to allow self-loops and cycles between clusters. It also builds on Pearl's (2009) do-calculus, reformulating its three rules into a sound and atomically complete calculus for the cyclic C-DAG setting. The approach is positioned against a broader line of work on separation criteria for causal abstractions (Jaber et al., 2022; Perković et al., 2018; Ferreira and Assaad, 2025), which the authors note has led to a proliferation of abstraction-specific rules; their structure-of-interest criterion offers a unified alternative that remains faithful to classical d-separation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The relaxation of partition admissibility is practically important because real-world clusterings (e.g., macroeconomic sectors, fMRI regions) naturally induce cycles at the cluster level, making the prior acyclic restriction a significant limitation. The 'Infinity is at most three' result (Theorem 4) provides a clean computational guarantee that cluster size can be capped at 3 without losing identification power, making the calculus tractable. However, the calculus is only atomically complete (not globally complete), and the authors explicitly flag establishing global completeness and extending to micro-level interventions as open problems, limiting the current practical scope of the framework.
