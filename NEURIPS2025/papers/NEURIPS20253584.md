## Problem

End-to-end 3D reconstruction methods in the 3R-series (DUSt3R, Spann3R, SLAM3R, Fast3R) depend heavily on large volumes of high-quality posed video-depth data, yet available datasets remain limited in diversity and scale, and existing video clips contain substantial redundancy from fixed camera trajectories. Data augmentation for dense 3D reconstruction has remained largely unexplored, leaving a gap between the data these models require and what is actually available.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253584/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts a single RGB-D image (with either ground-truth or predicted depth) or a video clip with per-frame depth maps and camera poses. It produces temporally coherent, posed video-depth sequences at a fixed patch resolution W'×H', where each frame carries a calibrated 6-DoF camera extrinsic, a corresponding depth map, and a rendered RGB image. The augmentation runs on-the-fly through the dataloader with no pre-processing step, and occluded regions introduced by camera rotation are filled with white and excluded from supervision.

## Method

Puzzles comprises two complementary strategies. Image-to-Clips partitions a single RGB-D image into an ordered, overlapping sequence of patches (simulating video continuity), calibrates per-patch camera intrinsics and extrinsics (via scaling for the varying-intrinsics case, or PnP+RANSAC for the fixed-intrinsics case), then applies centroid-based random rotation using Rodrigues' formula to introduce realistic 6-DoF camera motion; candidate poses are validated by front-surface coverage and view-frustum inclusion tests before rendering with Open3D. Clips-to-Clips extends this to full videos by computing a pairwise geometric overlap matrix between frames and selecting keyframes through validity filtering, longest-cover-set selection, and redundancy pruning, thereby maximizing spatial coverage while eliminating redundant views. The ordered overlap structure encodes pseudo-temporal coherence, and the combination of translation (from cropping) and rotation (from centroid-based perturbation) yields diverse, geometrically consistent training clips. The entire pipeline is plug-and-play: it requires no modification to the underlying 3R-series network architecture or training objectives, and models trained on just 10% of the original data with Puzzles match or exceed full-data baselines.

## Results

On scene-level benchmarks (7Scenes, NRGBD), integrating Puzzles with full training data reduces Accuracy error by 9.3–17.0% and Completion error by 11.0–13.1% across Spann3R, Fast3R, and SLAM3R. On the object-level DTU benchmark, gains are even larger: 16.4–19.9% Acc reduction and 15.9–19.7% Comp reduction. Remarkably, models trained on only 1/10 of the data with Puzzles match or exceed their full-data counterparts on 7Scenes (e.g., Spann3R 1/10+Puzzles achieves Acc 0.0389 vs. 0.0388 for full-data baseline), demonstrating strong data efficiency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Puzzles is evaluated as a plug-and-play augmentation on three 3R-series baselines (Spann3R, SLAM3R, Fast3R), all retrained under identical conditions with and without Puzzles. With full data, Puzzles consistently outperforms the no-augmentation baseline across all three methods and all three benchmarks: Fast3R shows the largest scene-level gains (17.0% Acc, 13.1% Comp on 7Scenes), while SLAM3R benefits most on DTU (16.7% Acc, 16.1% Comp). On NRGBD, improvements are more modest (6–10%) but still consistent, likely due to the larger, more complex scenes. The most striking result is data efficiency: with only 1/10 of the training data, Puzzles-augmented models match or exceed their full-data counterparts on 7Scenes (e.g., Spann3R 1/10+Puzzles: Acc 0.0389 vs. 0.0388 full-data baseline), whereas on NRGBD the 1/10 setting still lags slightly behind full-data baselines. No baseline is ever degraded by Puzzles under the full-data setting, confirming its role as a universally beneficial augmentation rather than a method that trades off one metric for another.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as a data-centric complement to the architecture-focused 3R-series lineage (DUSt3R → Spann3R → SLAM3R → Fast3R), which have progressively improved scalability and efficiency of end-to-end pointmap regression but remain bottlenecked by the limited diversity and scale of posed video-depth training data. Rather than proposing yet another network modification, Puzzles repurposes classical geometric tools (PnP/RANSAC for patch pose recovery, Rodrigues rotation for viewpoint synthesis) and the jigsaw-puzzle metaphor to generate unbounded, temporally coherent video-depth clips from a single RGB-D frame or short clip. In the broader data-augmentation literature, it extends the frame-pair synthesis idea from visual tracking (MASA, BIV) to full multi-frame video sequences with explicit 3D geometric consistency, filling a gap the authors identify as underexplored for dense 3D reconstruction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a plug-and-play augmentation module that requires no architectural changes and can be applied on-the-fly through the dataloader, making it immediately adoptable by any 3R-series or similar feedforward reconstruction pipeline. The finding that 10% of the training data augmented with Puzzles matches or exceeds full-data baselines across three architectures and three benchmarks suggests a meaningful shift in how the community thinks about data scale for 3D reconstruction. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to dynamic scenes, whether cross-scene clip stitching can truly achieve the 'unbounded' data promise, and whether the scene-specific nature of the augmentation limits transfer to unseen environments. The paper also notes that the 2D augmentations (affine, perspective) are applied without updating camera parameters, an acknowledged gap that could affect geometric consistency at scale.
