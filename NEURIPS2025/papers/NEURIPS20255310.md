## Problem

Existing knowledge editing methods (ROME, MEMIT, WISE, etc.) operate on structured triples or QA pairs, but real-world knowledge resides in unstructured text where entities are densely interlinked and semantically overlapping. Applying structured editing to such text causes two failures: representation ambiguity from complex entity interdependencies, and editing conflicts where semantically adjacent but prediction-divergent statements induce adversarial gradient directions during parameter optimization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255310/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a raw unstructured text passage (no explicit query-target pairs) and a set of evaluation questions derived from that text. Internally, an LLM first extracts a knowledge graph from the passage, which is then enriched into a directed multigraph; the editing process produces multiple orthogonal LoRA parameter subspaces (one per color class from graph coloring) plus stored activation vectors. At inference, an activation-similarity router (cosine threshold 0.7) decides whether to aggregate all LoRA subspaces with the base model or fall back to the unedited model.

## Method

CAKE comprises two modules. The Semantic-augmented Graph Representation (SGR) module builds a knowledge graph from the unstructured text via a structure-aware prompt, then enriches it through multi-hop relational inference (connecting entities at path length 2–p) and paraphrase-driven diversification (generating lexically varied relation variants per edge), yielding a directed multigraph that captures both explicit and latent semantics. The Conflict-aware Knowledge Editing (CKE) module constructs a conflict graph over the extracted triples, where an edge exists if two triples share a subject entity or exceed a similarity threshold on their query representations; the Welsh-Powell coloring algorithm then assigns conflicting triples to distinct colors, each mapped to an independent LoRA subspace trained in isolation. At inference, a memory router compares input activations against stored per-edit activation vectors to gate whether the edited LoRAs are engaged, preventing interference with the model's original knowledge.

## Results

CAKE achieves the highest editing Reliability across all three datasets (CounterFact, MQuAKE-CF, WikiUpdate) and all three LLMs (GPT2-XL, Llama3.2-3B, Llama3-8B). The headline result is a 15.43% Reliability improvement over the second-best method (MEMIT) on CounterFact with Llama3-8B (38.19% vs. 22.76%), with gains of 17.20% on Llama3.2-3B and 11.94% on GPT2-XL. On MQuAKE-CF, CAKE surpasses the second-best baseline by more than 15% across all three LLMs (e.g., 57.74% vs. 50.86% on Llama3-8B). Locality remains comparable to state-of-the-art methods, with CAKE achieving 86.61% on CounterFact/Llama3-8B versus 87.69% for MEMIT.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CAKE outperforms all five baselines (ROME, MELO, MEMIT, WISE, ELDER) on Reliability across every dataset–model combination. The largest margins appear on CounterFact, where CAKE leads MEMIT by 15.43 points on Llama3-8B (38.19% vs. 22.76%) and Elder by 17.20 points on Llama3.2-3B (43.24% vs. 26.04%), reflecting the dataset's high knowledge-conflict density that the graph-coloring CKE module is designed to resolve. On MQuAKE-CF, CAKE exceeds the second-best method by over 15 points on all three LLMs, benefiting from multi-hop relational enrichment. WISE suffers catastrophic locality degradation (e.g., 14.84% on Llama3.2-3B CounterFact), while ELDER shows inconsistent reliability (dropping to 1.99% on Llama3-8B CounterFact). On Locality, CAKE is broadly comparable to MEMIT and ROME (within ~1–2 points), though it trails slightly on WikiUpdate for Llama3.2-3B (71.42% vs. 75.14% for MEMIT) and Llama3-8B (87.57% vs. 88.38% for MEMIT).

## Relation to Prior Work

CAKE builds on the WUKE (Wild Unstructured Knowledge Editing) paradigm introduced by AKEW [12], which first formalized the task of editing LLMs from raw text passages rather than structured triples. It departs from prior structured editing methods (ROME, MEMIT, WISE, MELO, ELDER) that operate on isolated (subject, relation, object) tuples by introducing a Semantic-augmented Graph Representation that preserves paragraph-level relational topology through multi-hop enrichment and paraphrase diversification. The conflict-resolution mechanism repurposes the classical Welsh-Powell graph-coloring algorithm to allocate semantically overlapping edits into orthogonal LoRA subspaces, a conceptual departure from ELDER's learned router and WISE's Ties-Merge sharding, which were designed for structured or lifelong settings rather than dense intra-paragraph conflicts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CAKE closes a practical gap: real-world knowledge updates arrive as documents and articles, not curated triples, yet all prior editing methods required costly human annotation to convert text into structured form. The 15.43% reliability gain on Llama3-8B and consistent improvements across three LLMs and three datasets demonstrate that the graph-coloring conflict isolation is not merely a theoretical nicety but a measurable necessity when edits share subject entities. Limitations include restriction to next-token-prediction architectures, dependence on a capable LLM for graph construction (GPT-4 for GPT2-XL), and no verification of source-text authenticity, leaving open questions about scalability to very large knowledge graphs and robustness to adversarial or noisy inputs.
