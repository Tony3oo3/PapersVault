## Problem

Existing approximate unlearning methods for vision classifiers appear to succeed on standard metrics like forget-set accuracy, yet the paper reveals they are fundamentally vulnerable to relearning attacks: fine-tuning an unlearned model on only the retain set (zero forget-set examples) can recover forget-set accuracy from ~50% to nearly 100%. This 'dormant knowledge' phenomenon is absent in the gold-standard retrain-from-scratch model, exposing that output-level and representation-level unlearning methods fail to truly erase the influence of forgotten data.

## Input / Output

The unlearning algorithm takes a pretrained ResNet-18 or ResNet-34 model, a retain set D_R, and a forget set D_F (comprising atypical examples selected via pre-computed consistency scores), and produces an unlearned model. The relearning attack then fine-tunes the unlearned model on D_R ∪ D_F_re where D_F_re is a subset of the forget set (ranging from 0 to 100 examples), with performance measured on the held-out portion D_F_ho and a separate test set. Experiments use 32×32 CIFAR-10/100 images and higher-resolution Imagenette images.

## Method

The paper's key insight is that tamper-resistance against relearning attacks correlates with the L2 distance and linear mode connectivity between the pretrained and unlearned models in weight-space. Leveraging this, they propose three methods that push the unlearned model far from the pretrained one: (1) Weight Distortion adds isotropic Gaussian noise (σ=0.02) to all weights before fine-tuning on the retain set; (2) Weight Dist Reg explicitly maximizes the Euclidean distance between pretrained and unlearned parameters while minimizing retain-set loss; and (3) Connectivity-Based Fine-Tuning (CBFT) maximizes the loss at the midpoint between the two models on retain and forget examples to create a high-loss barrier. These weight-space interventions are shown to be significantly more tamper-resistant than prior output-level (SCRUB) or representation-level (Circuit Breakers) methods, though they trade off some test accuracy.

## Results

The paper's central finding is that for atypical forget-set examples, unlearned models' forget-set accuracy recovers from ~50% (post-unlearning) to nearly 100% after fine-tuning on only the retain set (0 relearning examples), while the retrain-from-scratch gold standard remains at ~50%. On Imagenette (Table 1), SCRUB's forget-set accuracy jumps from 85.71% (unlearned) to 93.40% (0 relearn) and 94.21% (10 relearn), whereas retrain-from-scratch stays at 63.53% and 64.16% respectively. The proposed Weight Distortion and Weight Dist Reg methods show the strongest tamper-resistance, with forget-set accuracy remaining near the retrain-from-scratch level even after relearning (e.g., Weight Dist Reg: 84.42% → 73.87% at 0 relearn on Imagenette). The paper also shows that L2 distance between pretrained and unlearned weights is a reliable predictor of tamper-resistance, and that linear mode connectivity (absence of a loss barrier) correlates with vulnerability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SCRUB [26] and Circuit Breakers [46] are the most vulnerable baselines: their forget-set accuracy drops to ~50% post-unlearning (matching retrain-from-scratch) but recovers to ~100% after fine-tuning on only the retain set, a jump of ~50 percentage points. Random Relabeling [15] shows similar extreme susceptibility. Catastrophic Forgetting [42] and L1-Sparse [21] are more resistant due to their weight-space regularizers (L2 and L1 decay, respectively), but Table 3 shows Catastrophic Forgetting remains vulnerable with fewer than 30 unlearning epochs (forget-set accuracy 99.99% at 1 epoch vs. 66.14% at 100 epochs). TAR [40], explicitly designed for tamper-resistance in LLMs, fails to add any additional resistance beyond the initial safeguard in this vision setting (Fig. 3). SSD [11] and Weight Attenuation/Dropout are also susceptible. The proposed Weight Distortion and Weight Dist Reg methods substantially outperform all baselines in tamper-resistance, with Weight Dist Reg achieving the lowest L2 distance to retrain-from-scratch behavior and remaining robust even as a second-phase addition to weaker initial safeguards.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the relearning-attack concept from LLM unlearning literature (where fine-tuning on benign data re-emerges supposedly-unlearned knowledge) into a controlled vision-classification setting with a clear gold standard (retrain-from-scratch). It builds on weight-space analysis tools—L2 parameter distance and Linear Mode Connectivity (Frankle et al., 2020)—and repurposes the mechanistic mode connectivity objective from Lubana et al. (2023) as a tamper-resistance mechanism (CBFT). The key conceptual departure from prior unlearning methods (SCRUB, Circuit Breakers, NegGrad+, etc.) is the observation that they operate at the output or representation level without constraining weight-space movement, leaving the forget-set influence merely dormant rather than deleted.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that retain-set-only fine-tuning recovers forget-set accuracy from ~50% to ~100% fundamentally challenges the evaluation paradigm of approximate unlearning: matching retrain-from-scratch on forget-set accuracy does not guarantee the knowledge is truly gone. The L2-distance diagnostic offers a simple, interpretable predictor of tamper-resistance that could guide method design broadly. However, the proposed weight-space methods sacrifice test accuracy, and the results are confined to small ResNet models on CIFAR/Imagenette—whether the same dynamics hold for billion-parameter LLMs (the original motivation) remains open. The trade-off between tamper-resistance and utility defines a new Pareto frontier that future work must improve upon.
