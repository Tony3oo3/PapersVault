## Problem

Existing all-atom protein design methods suffer from an information shortcut where networks exploit side-chain geometry (even when noised) to trivially predict residue sequences, undermining genuine sequence distribution learning. Additionally, no Bayesian flow formulation exists for SO(3) backbone orientations because standard SO(3) distributions lack the Bayesian conjugacy required by Bayesian Flow Networks, forcing reliance on SDE/ODE-based methods prone to discretization error.

## Input / Output

For peptide design, the model takes a target protein receptor structure and generates 40 candidate all-atom peptide structures; for antibody design, it takes an antigen structure and generates 64 CDR-H3 candidates. Each output is a complete multi-modal protein specification: a discrete amino acid sequence, continuous Cα backbone positions in ℝ³, SO(3) backbone orientation matrices, a backbone torsion angle ψ, and up to four side-chain torsion angles χ per residue (with residue-type-dependent counts). The model operates end-to-end without decomposing design into separate backbone-then-sequence-then-sidechain stages.

## Method

ProBayes formulates all-atom protein generation as a unified multi-modal Bayesian Flow Network, replacing SDE/ODE-based generation with discrete-time Bayesian inference that avoids discretization error. The key theoretical contribution recasts SO(3) orientation generation as an equivalent hypersphere S³ generation problem with antipodal symmetry, leveraging the SU(2)→SO(3) double cover and the von Mises Fisher distribution's Bayesian conjugacy on S³. A rationalized information flow architecture explicitly excludes side-chain features from the sequence prediction head while retaining them for backbone and side-chain denoising, eliminating the information shortcut. The network backbone uses a quaternion-based Invariant Point Attention (IPAq) module that is antipodal equivariant by construction, with separate mixing modules for sequence-backbone and backbone-sidechain interactions. Training minimizes a KL-divergence-based ELBO across all modalities simultaneously, with modality-specific losses (Gaussian for positions, categorical for sequence, periodic for angles, vMF for orientations) weighted by learned accuracy schedules.

## Results

On peptide co-design (PepBench), ProBayes achieves a DockQ of 0.742, RMSD_Cα of 2.27 Å, and ΔG of −28.77 kcal/mol, outperforming all baselines across energy, structural fidelity, and design quality metrics. On the antibody CDR-H3 design task (RAbD), ProBayes achieves 42.12% AAR, 2.27 Å RMSD, and a total energy of 54.68 kcal/mol — an order-of-magnitude improvement over the best prior methods (e.g., AbDPO++ at 338.14). For binding conformation generation, ProBayes reduces RMSD_Cα to 2.62 Å and RMSD_atom to 3.64 Å, substantially below PepGLAD (4.09/8.87) and DiffAb (4.23/13.96).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ProBayes consistently outperforms all baselines across both peptide and antibody design tasks. On PepBench, it surpasses PepFlow (the closest competitor) by 0.065 in DockQ (0.742 vs. 0.677), 0.54 Å in RMSD_Cα (2.27 vs. 2.81), and 5.68 kcal/mol in ΔG (−28.77 vs. −23.09), while also achieving near-perfect validity (0.998 vs. 0.848). Against PepGLAD, the gap is even larger: ProBayes improves DockQ by 0.086, RMSD_Cα by 1.08 Å, and validity by 0.668 (0.998 vs. 0.330). On the antibody RAbD benchmark, ProBayes achieves 42.12% AAR (vs. 41.27% for AbX and 40.05% for dyMEAN) and a total energy of 54.68 kcal/mol, which is roughly 5× lower than AbDPO (270.12) and 6× lower than AbDPO++ (338.14), despite those methods being specifically optimized for energy. In the SO(3) toy experiment, ProBayes reduces W1 distance to 3.23×10⁻² (vs. 4.92–5.39×10⁻² for FoldFlow variants) and W2 to 0.99×10⁻¹ (vs. 1.25–1.52×10⁻¹), confirming the advantage of the Bayesian flow formulation over SDE/ODE-based approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Bayesian Flow Network (BFN) framework from Graves et al. (2023) to the protein design domain, building on the periodic BFN for torsion angles (Wu et al., 2025) and adapting AlphaFold's Invariant Point Attention (Jumper et al., 2021) into a quaternion-based variant (IPAq) to enforce antipodal equivariance. It departs from the dominant SDE/ODE-based generative paradigm (diffusion models, flow matching) used in prior protein design work (RFDiffusion, PepFlow, PepGLAD) by arguing that BFNs avoid discretization errors and enable entropy-adjusted step sizes. The key theoretical departure is the first derivation of a Bayesian flow on SO(3) via the SU(2) double cover, transforming rotation generation into hypersphere generation with antipodal symmetry—a reformulation that sidesteps the intractable Bayesian conjugacy of existing SO(3) distributions like IGSO(3).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of the information shortcut problem—where noised side-chain padding or inherent torsion distributions leak sequence information—provides a diagnostic that likely affects all current all-atom protein design methods, and the rationalized information flow offers a principled fix. The SO(3) Bayesian flow is a reusable theoretical tool applicable beyond proteins (e.g., molecular docking, general geometric deep learning). However, the paper acknowledges that Rosetta-based energy scores serve only as initial screening, and wet-lab validation remains necessary; the antibody design results, while showing large energy gaps over baselines, are evaluated on a single benchmark (RAbD) with borrowed baseline numbers, leaving open questions about generalization to other antibody regions or protein classes.
