## Problem

Existing video-text alignment methods (e.g., BIKE) weight video frames by their similarity to a single global text prompt, but this introduces a granularity mismatch: different global actions can share identical atomic actions (ambiguity), and atomic actions within a video are often non-uniformly distributed or even irrelevant to the overarching class semantic. This causes misaligned video-text pairs and degrades fine-grained recognition of subtle or multi-stage actions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251911/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 8-frame video clips at 224×224 resolution and text class prompts (formatted as 'a photo of a [class name]') as input. Sub-texts (N=4 atomic action descriptions per class) are generated offline from class names alone via an LLM, requiring no video frames. The output is a fused video embedding combining a coarse embedding (from cross-attention-augmented global text) and a fine-grained embedding (from per-sub-text frame scoring), compared via cosine similarity to frozen CLIP text embeddings. Inference uses a single view (1×1), unlike competitors that rely on 4×3 or 10×3 temporal-spatial ensembles.

## Method

SFAR decomposes each global action into N atomic action descriptions (sub-texts) using a pre-trained LLM, inspired by storyboarding a script into individual shots. A Text Prompt Perplexity (TPP) metric—measuring both sub-text-to-global-text similarity and inter-sub-text diversity in CLIP embedding space—selects the optimal sub-text set without requiring video involvement. The coarse video embedding is computed by augmenting the global text embedding with sub-texts via cross-attention, then weighting frame embeddings by their normalized similarity to the augmented text. The fine-grained video embedding independently scores each frame by its maximum average word-level similarity to any sub-text, capturing momentary or non-uniform atomic actions. The two embeddings are fused through separate FFN layers and trained with a bidirectional InfoNCE loss, while CLIP's textual encoder remains frozen throughout.

## Results

On Kinetics-400, SFAR achieves 84.5% Top-1 and 96.7% Top-5 accuracy using only 8 frames with a single 1×1 view (90.2 GFLOPs, 126.1M params), outperforming all prior methods that use more frames, views, or parameters. On the multi-label Charades dataset, it reaches 51.1 mAP with 16 frames. In zero-shot settings, SFAR attains 79.0% on UCF-101 and 56.6% on HMDB-51, and in few-shot settings it achieves a 78.7% average Top-1 across HMDB-51, UCF-101, and Kinetics-400 at 2/4/8/16 shots.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On Kinetics-400, SFAR (84.5% Top-1) surpasses the strongest visual-pretraining baseline ILA (84.0%) by 0.5% and the strongest visual-text baseline BIKE (83.9%) by 0.6%, while using only 1×1 views versus 4×3 for both. On Charades multi-label recognition, SFAR (51.1 mAP) edges out BIKE (50.4 mAP) by 0.7 mAP and substantially outperforms ActionCLIP (44.3 mAP) and X3D (43.4 mAP). In zero-shot evaluation, SFAR leads on UCF-101 (79.0% vs. M2-CLIP's 78.7% and BIKE's 78.4%) and HMDB-51 (56.6% vs. BIKE's 55.6%), with a particularly large 9.5-point margin over M2-CLIP (47.1%) on HMDB-51. In few-shot settings, SFAR's 78.7% average across all datasets and shot counts exceeds the runner-up TC-CLIP (72.9%) by 5.8 points, with the largest gap on Kinetics-400 (76.2% vs. 69.9% at 16-shot). The generalization study (Table 2c) confirms that the fine-grained video embedding module can be plugged into VideoPrompt, ATM, and BIKE, yielding 0.1–2.4% Top-1 gains without architectural changes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the CLIP-based video action recognition line initiated by BIKE (Wu et al., 2023), which weights video frames by their similarity to a single global text prompt. The key departure is a storyboard-inspired decomposition: instead of aligning frames to one global semantic, SFAR uses an LLM to generate N atomic-action sub-texts per class and constructs both a coarse embedding (global text augmented via cross-attention with sub-texts) and a fine-grained embedding (sub-text–frame alignment), then fuses them. A Text Prompt Perplexity (TPP) metric selects the best sub-text set purely from text embeddings, avoiding the need for video involvement during sub-text selection—a contrast with prior methods that refine prompts using auxiliary frames or generative captions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The multi-granularity framing directly addresses two failure modes (ambiguous actions sharing atomic sub-actions, and non-uniform temporal distribution of sub-actions) that single-prompt methods struggle with, and the TPP metric offers a cheap, video-free proxy for sub-text quality (r²=0.79 with accuracy). The fine-grained embedding module generalizes as a plug-in to other CLIP-based methods (e.g., +2.4% for VideoPrompt), suggesting broader utility. Limitations include dependence on LLM generation quality for sub-texts, the fixed N=4 choice (class-aware N yields only marginal gains), and the fact that supervised improvements over BIKE are modest (~0.5–0.6%) while the larger gains appear in few-shot and zero-shot settings. Open questions include whether the storyboard decomposition transfers to other modalities (audio, skeleton) and whether the TPP metric remains effective for very large or highly fine-grained action taxonomies.
