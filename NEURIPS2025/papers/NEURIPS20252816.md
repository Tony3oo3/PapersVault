## Problem

Existing coreset constructions for (k,z)-clustering assume access to clean, noise-free data, yet real-world datasets are routinely corrupted by stochastic noise (measurement error, privacy mechanisms, sensor artifacts). When only a noisy dataset P̂ is observed, the true dataset P is unobservable, making it fundamentally difficult to evaluate coreset quality. Moreover, the standard surrogate metric Err, which bounds relative cost deviation, significantly overestimates error under noise because it cannot distinguish uniform cost inflation from genuine structural degradation, leading to overly pessimistic guarantees and unnecessarily large coresets.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes a noisy dataset P̂ ∈ ℝ^d generated from an unobserved P under a known stochastic noise model (parameterized by θ ∈ [0,1] and per-coordinate distributions D_j satisfying the Bernstein condition), along with a tolerance ε ∈ (0,1), an approximation parameter α ∈ [1,2], and an O(1)-approximate center set Ĉ obtained via k-means++. It outputs a weighted coreset S ⊆ P̂ where each sampled point p in cluster i receives weight w(p) = |P'_i|/|S_i|, together with a provable upper bound on the true clustering quality r_P(S, α) = max_{C ∈ C_α(S)} cost_z(P,C)/OPT_P. The coreset size is O(k log k / (ε − Δ) + (α−1)k log k / (ε − Δ)²) where Δ = √(α−1)·θnd/(α·OPT_P).

## Method

The paper introduces a new surrogate metric Err_α (approximation-ratio), defined as sup_{C ∈ C_α(S)} r_P(C)/r_S(C) − 1, which measures relative quality degradation rather than absolute cost deviation, thereby absorbing the uniform cost inflation caused by additive noise. The core algorithm CN_α partitions P̂ into k clusters via an O(1)-approximate center set, then removes extremely noisy points by intersecting each cluster with a ball of radius R_i = 3r̂_i + O(√d·log((1+θkd)/√(α−1))) around its center, and finally draws uniform samples from each truncated cluster. This noise-aware truncation is the key mechanism: it preserves geometric stability of cluster assignments under noise, enabling concentration arguments that yield Err_α(S,P) = O(θkd/OPT_P + √(α−1)/α · (√(θkd·OPT_P)+θnd)/OPT_P), which is a poly(k) factor tighter than the Err-based bound O(θnd/OPT_P + √(θnd/OPT_P)). Under mild cost-stability and limited-outlier assumptions on P, the resulting coreset is smaller by a factor of √k/ε compared to the Err-based construction, with correspondingly tighter quality guarantees on r_P(S, α).

## Results

On the Adult dataset (k=10, Gaussian noise), CN_α consistently produces smaller coresets than CN across all noise levels and tolerance values. At ε=0.2, θ=0.01, CN_α yields a coreset of size 1940 (vs. 2371 for CN, a 18% reduction) with a tightness ratio κ_S=0.937 (vs. 0.596 for CN), indicating much tighter empirical bounds relative to the theoretical guarantee. The empirical approximation ratio r̃_S for CN_α is also lower at higher tolerances: at ε=0.2, θ=0.01, CN_α achieves r̃_S=1.156 vs. 1.193 for CN. In the noise-free case (θ=0), CN_α still achieves κ_S values above 0.86 across all ε, while CN's κ_S drops to 0.71 at ε=0.3.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against external baselines; instead, it contrasts its two proposed coreset constructions: CN (based on the traditional Err metric, Theorem 3.1) and CN_α (based on the new Err_α metric, Theorem 3.3). CN_α wins on both coreset size and bound tightness across all tested configurations. Specifically, CN_α reduces coreset size by roughly 15–30% (e.g., 1940 vs. 2371 at ε=0.2) and achieves κ_S values 0.3–0.4 higher than CN under noise (e.g., 0.937 vs. 0.596 at ε=0.2, θ=0.01), meaning its empirical performance is much closer to its theoretical guarantee. At higher noise (θ=0.25), the gap in κ_S widens further (0.581 vs. 0.125 at ε=0.2), confirming that Err_α's noise-aware design provides substantially tighter guarantees as noise increases. In the noise-free regime (θ=0), CN_α still produces smaller coresets and tighter bounds, suggesting the Err_α metric offers advantages even without noise.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the coreset construction framework of Feldman & Langberg (STOC 2011) and the approximation-ratio-based coreset ideas of Cohen-Addad et al. (NeurIPS 2021) and Huang et al. (ICML 2023) to a fundamentally new setting: the true dataset P is unobserved, and coresets must be built from noisy observations P̂. Unlike prior robust coreset work (Feldman & Schulman 2012; Huang et al. 2018, 2023) that assumes access to clean data and filters identifiable outliers, this paper constructs coresets directly from stochastic noise without any oracle access to the underlying distribution. The key methodological departure is the introduction of the Err_α metric, which compares relative quality ratios rather than absolute cost deviations, thereby compensating for the uniform cost inflation that noise introduces and that the classical Err metric cannot distinguish from genuine approximation error.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first theoretical analysis of how coreset quality degrades under stochastic noise, providing practitioners with provable guarantees when the clean dataset is unavailable—a condition that holds in virtually all real-world applications. The poly(k) improvement in coreset size and the tighter quality bounds from Err_α are practically meaningful for large-scale clustering pipelines. Key limitations include the reliance on cost-stability and limited-outlier assumptions (though experiments show robustness to their violation), the restriction to Euclidean spaces, and the unresolved mergeability gap that prevents direct extension to streaming settings. Open directions include weaker structural assumptions, adversarial or heavy-tailed noise, general metric spaces, and characterizing when Err_α outperforms Err even in noise-free regimes.
