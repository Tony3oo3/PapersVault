## Problem

Existing single-cell crossmodal matching methods (e.g., MultiVI, CLUE, Cobolt) rely on VAE-based architectures that model sparse RNA and ATAC expression vectors as dense representations, introducing noise from unexpressed signals. They also underutilize rich biological attributes (genomic annotations, DNA sequences, expression statistics) and fail to explicitly model the regulatory interactions between ATAC peaks and RNA transcripts, limiting both generalization under data-scarce settings and the ability to uncover fine-grained biologically meaningful crossmodal relationships.

## Input / Output

The method takes a paired RNA expression vector and ATAC accessibility vector from a single cell, but only non-zero (expressed) entries become graph nodes, each augmented with a unique ID embedding, expression level, and biological metadata (chromosomal location, DNA sequence encoding, expression statistics). Edges between RNA and ATAC nodes are constrained by a chromosomal co-location mask rather than full connectivity. The output is a binary match/mismatch prediction for whether the pair originates from the same cell, alongside a sparse top-k RNA-ATAC attention matrix that serves as an interpretable regulatory interaction map at both cell-level and cell-type-level.

## Method

Bi²Former reformulates crossmodal matching as binary graph classification on Attributed Bipartite Graphs (ABGs), where expressed RNA and ATAC peaks are nodes with biological features and edges encode chromosomal priors. The core architecture is a biologically-driven bipartite graph transformer with two stages: (1) a crossmodal attention module that treats RNA as queries and ATAC as keys (reflecting the biological intuition that ATAC regions regulate RNA), computes separate value projections per modality to preserve modality-specific information, and applies a biological pruning strategy (sigmoid thresholding followed by top-k binarization) to enforce sparsity consistent with the constraint that each gene is regulated by a limited number of ATAC peaks; (2) a crossmodal message passing stage where RNA nodes aggregate regulatory cues from attended ATAC nodes and vice versa via the transposed attention map, combined with modality-specific MLP self-updates. The graph-level prediction is obtained by average pooling over final node embeddings followed by a 4-layer feedforward predictor, trained end-to-end with binary cross-entropy loss.

## Results

Bi2Former achieves state-of-the-art crossmodal matching performance across all five single-cell omics datasets, with an average accuracy of 83.39% and average ROC-AUC of 87.46%. The largest gains appear on 10× PBMC (88.74% ACC, 92.37% AUC) and 10× Multiome (90.41% ACC, 93.41% AUC), where Bi2Former surpasses the strongest ABG-based baseline (Graph Transformer) by 10.7% and 10.3% in accuracy, respectively. Under sparse training (20% of paired data), Bi2Former maintains strong performance while most VAE-based baselines degrade to near-chance levels. In the cross-cell-type generalization setting, Bi2Former achieves 82.74% on ISSAAC-seq and 84.96% on 10× PBMC, outperforming the best ABG baseline by an average of 7.7%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Bi2Former outperforms all nine baselines on every dataset for both accuracy and ROC-AUC. Among VAE-based methods, scMoGNN is the strongest (avg ACC 72.10%), followed by GLUE (71.01%) and CLUE (67.25%); Bi2Former exceeds scMoGNN by 11.3% in average accuracy. Among ABG-based GNN baselines, Graph Transformer (GT) is the best (avg ACC 74.50%), with GraphSAGE close behind (73.99%); Bi2Former surpasses GT by 8.9% in average accuracy and by 7.3% in average AUC. The gap is most pronounced on 10× PBMC, where Bi2Former reaches 88.74% ACC versus 78.04% for GT and 72.41% for scMoGNN. In the cross-cell-type transfer setting (Table 2), Bi2Former again leads all baselines by a wide margin (e.g., 82.74% vs. 74.15% for GraphSAGE on ISSAAC-seq), confirming that the biologically-driven attention mechanism generalizes better than generic GNN message passing to unseen cell types.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant VAE-based paradigm in single-cell crossmodal learning (MultiVI, CLUE, Cobolt, GLUE, scMoGNN) by reformulating the matching problem as graph classification on Attributed Bipartite Graphs, a formalism previously applied in recommendation systems and drug-target association but not in single-cell omics. It also distinguishes itself from prior graph-based multi-omics methods (GLUE, scMoGNN) that rely on predefined guidance graphs by learning regulatory interactions end-to-end via a biologically-driven bipartite attention mechanism with chromosomal priors and top-k sparsification. The method synthesizes ideas from graph transformers, bipartite collaborative filtering, and single-cell biology into a unified ABG-based framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ABG formulation offers a principled way to handle the inherent sparsity of single-cell omics data while incorporating rich biological attributes, and the demonstrated robustness under 20% training data addresses a critical practical bottleneck in multi-omics research where paired profiles are expensive and scarce. The interpretable attention matrices that correlate with experimentally derived TF binding scores open a path toward using learned crossmodal interactions as a discovery tool for cell-type-specific regulatory programs. Key limitations include the absence of edge-level attributes, restriction to ATAC-RNA pairs (not yet extended to other modality combinations like protein or methylation), and dependence on chromosomal co-location as the sole edge prior, which may miss distal regulatory interactions.
