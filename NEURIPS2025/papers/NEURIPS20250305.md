## Problem

Sequential model editing of LLMs suffers from cumulative parameter drift: when newly edited knowledge conflicts with existing internal knowledge, repeated edits cause the edited layers to become incompatible with the rest of the model, leading to catastrophic collapse of general capabilities. Existing parameter-modifying methods (MEMIT, RECT, PRUNE) focus on single-edit outcomes and lack mechanisms to actively resolve knowledge conflicts, separate cross-domain updates, or consolidate previously edited knowledge, so performance degrades to near zero within a few hundred sequential edits.

## Input / Output

The method consumes knowledge triples (subject s_i, relation r_i, old object o_i, target object o_i*) sequentially, one edit at a time. The subject is encoded into a key vector k_i by averaging the second-FFN-layer input representations over P random token prefixes, and the target object defines a value vector v_i = W k_i + δ_i. The output is a closed-form update matrix Δ_i added to the edited weight matrix W, producing a modified LLM that maps all previously edited subjects to their target objects while preserving general behavior on unedited inputs. The long-term editing memory (Cov_sum) and per-edit Fisher matrices are maintained as lightweight running accumulations rather than stored data.

## Method

HSE designs three hippocampal-inspired mechanisms operating on the incremental update parameter δ and the closed-form update matrix Δ. (1) Memory-directed Active Forgetting combines cross-entropy (memorizing new knowledge) with unlikelihood loss (suppressing the old object) into a unified MAF loss, theoretically shown to yield a tighter generalization error bound under Adam than CE alone. (2) Memory Stability Preservation applies a Fisher Information Matrix penalty (derived via Laplace approximation of the posterior over δ) that scales each parameter's update magnitude inversely to its importance for prior edits, preventing cross-domain interference. (3) Progressive Memory Consolidation extends the MEMIT closed-form solution to n sequential edits by accumulating Cov_sum = Σ K_j K_j^T as a parametric replay of all prior keys, with a convergence factor α_i = n/(i−1) that provably bounds the Frobenius norm of W. The resulting update Δ_i = δ_i K_i^T (C_0 + α_i Cov_sum_{i−1} + K_i K_i^T)^{−1} generalizes AlphaEdit as a special case and requires no stored data or additional modules.

## Results

On one-by-one sequential editing with 1,000 samples across four LLMs (Llama3-Instruct 8B, Mistral7B-Instruct-V0.3, GPT-J 6B, GPT2-XL 1.5B) and two datasets (CounterFact, ZsRE), HSE achieves average improvements of 20.6% in generalization, 21.9% in specificity, and 17.3% in efficacy over the best baseline. On the GLUE benchmark after 1,000 sequential edits, HSE maintains or slightly surpasses the original Llama3 performance across all six tasks (1.42% decrease with CounterFact, 1.67% improvement with ZsRE), while all other baselines collapse to near-zero performance. HSE also exhibits the highest fluency and consistency, even surpassing the unedited model on multiple consistency evaluations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HSE outperforms all six baselines across every metric and every LLM tested. MEND and FT-L suffer the most severe collapse, dropping to near-zero on all GLUE tasks within 100 sequential edits. MEMIT and PRUNE sustain performance longer (~400–500 steps on CounterFact, ~600 on ZsRE) but still experience sudden catastrophic collapse to zero. RECT (regularization-based) shows intermediate degradation but still fails to maintain general capabilities over 1,000 edits. AlphaEdit, which HSE generalizes as a special case of its replay mechanism, preserves most general capabilities but still exhibits a marked performance decrease, whereas HSE maintains or slightly improves them. F-learning (parametric arithmetic for forgetting before learning) is compared in the appendix and also underperforms HSE on sequential editing metrics. The paper notes that Llama3 and Mistral show particularly large HSE improvements due to their smaller parameter F-norm, making other methods more susceptible to catastrophic forgetting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the locate-then-edit paradigm (ROME, MEMIT) from single-shot to sequential knowledge editing by integrating three previously separate ideas: unlikelihood-based machine unlearning (Welleck et al.), Fisher Information Matrix regularization from EWC (Kirkpatrick et al.), and a closed-form parameter replay mechanism that generalizes AlphaEdit's null-space constraint. The key conceptual departure is framing these components as an analog of the hippocampal trisynaptic circuit (DG→CA3→CA1), where pattern separation, active forgetting via LTD, and sharp-wave-ripple consolidation map onto domain-separated FIM updates, unlearning of conflicting knowledge, and long-term editing memory respectively. Unlike prior sequential editing methods (MEMIT, PRUNE, RECT) that apply ad-hoc regularization to the update matrix, HSE derives a unified closed-form solution with provable convergence of the parameter Frobenius norm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Sequential editing is a practical necessity for deploying LLMs in dynamic environments (medical knowledge updates, bias correction, hallucination mitigation), yet all existing parameter-modifying methods suffer catastrophic collapse within 100–600 edits. HSE's demonstration that general capabilities can be *improved* (not merely preserved) after 1,000 sequential edits, combined with theoretical guarantees on generalization bounds and convergence, provides a principled path toward truly continual LLM editing. Open questions include whether the mutual orthogonality assumption on key vectors holds at scale, the computational cost of maintaining the FIM and Cov_sum over thousands of edits, and whether the framework extends to non-FFN parameter locations or multi-hop knowledge.
