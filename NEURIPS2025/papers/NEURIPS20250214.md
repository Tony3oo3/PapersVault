## Problem

Existing graph fraud detectors are limited by narrow receptive fields, relying primarily on low-hop neighbor information and thus failing to capture the long-range structural associations that characterize increasingly intelligent and concealed fraudulent activities. This paper addresses the gap by proposing a global-aware approach that explicitly mines hidden fraud patterns across multi-hop path structures rather than local neighborhoods.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250214/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on a heterogeneous graph containing user nodes (with 9 binary-encoded attributes like age, gender, city) and website nodes (with BERT-encoded description features truncated to 106 dimensions), connected by two edge types: 'user visits website' (weighted by visit count) and 'user to user'. Website node types and labels are treated as unknown, leaving the model to infer them. The output is a binary fraud probability per user node, trained with binary cross-entropy loss under a supervised paradigm with 4:2:4 train/val/test splits.

## Method

GPA processes fraud detection through four stages: (1) variable-length path sampling via random walks from each user node, with random masking to create diverse path lengths and top-K selection by summed degree centrality; (2) semantic-associated path encoding that concatenates node features weighted by edge visit counts along each path, augments with normalized behavior features (e.g., number of visited websites, total visits), and projects to fixed-length embeddings via linear layers; (3) path interaction via multi-head self-attention across all K paths from a user node, enabling the model to discover common fraudulent patterns and filter irrelevant paths; (4) aggregation-enhanced detection where interacted path embeddings are averaged and passed through an MLP + sigmoid. The key distinction from standard GNNs is that the receptive field extends to arbitrary path lengths (up to 30 hops in experiments) rather than being confined to 1-2 hop neighborhoods, and the self-attention operates over paths rather than all graph nodes, avoiding the O(N²) cost of Graph Transformers while still capturing long-range dependencies.

## Results

GPA achieves the best AUC and AP across all six datasets spanning four fraud scenarios. Headline AP scores are 97.6% on G-Internet, 76.1% on Elliptic, 89.6% on T-Finance, 96.0% on T-Social, 73.7% on YelpChi, and 92.5% on Amazon. The paper reports AP improvements of +15.7%, +3.4%, and +9.2% over the strongest baselines on G-Internet, Elliptic, and T-Social respectively. GPA also demonstrates superior robustness to noisy labels, retaining 92.9% AP on G-Internet even when 40% of anomalous labels are flipped to benign.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPA outperforms all 19 baselines on every dataset and metric. The largest margins appear on G-Internet, where GPA's 97.6% AP exceeds the best baseline (BWGNN, 72.7% AP) by 24.9 points, and on T-Social, where GPA's 96.0% AP surpasses GHRN (86.8%) by 9.2 points. Among standard GNNs, GCN and GraphSAGE achieve competitive AUC on G-Internet (98.5/98.6) but their AP (81.9/78.9) trails GPA by 15–19 points, confirming that local neighbor aggregation is insufficient for concealed fraud patterns. Spectral methods (AMNet, BWGNN, GHRN) are the strongest competitors on Elliptic and T-Finance, yet GPA still leads by 3.4–6.6 AP points. Spatial fraud detectors (CARE-GNN, RioGNN, PC-GNN) collapse on G-Internet (AP 15–22%) because they cannot distinguish benign from anomalous users without deeper behavioral path analysis, while GPA maintains 97.6% AP. On YelpChi and Amazon, the gap narrows (GPA leads by 3–13 AP points over HedGe and GDN), suggesting that for review-based fraud with denser local signals, path-level aggregation provides a smaller but consistent advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of random-walk path sampling and self-attention for graph fraud detection, departing from both standard GNNs (GCN, GAT) and spectral/spatial fraud detectors that the authors uniformly characterize as 'local-aware' with limited receptive fields. While path-based methods like PathNet and RAW-GNN exist, the authors argue they address heterophily rather than long-range dependency and use only short paths (2–7 hops). GPA's key conceptual move is treating variable-length paths as first-class objects that interact via self-attention, creating a much larger effective receptive field without the O(N²) cost of Graph Transformers. The introduction of G-Internet as the first interpretable internet-fraud benchmark also fills a gap left by anonymized datasets that obscure entity semantics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The +15.7% AP gain on G-Internet and consistent improvements across finance, social, and review datasets suggest that path-level association mining captures fraud patterns invisible to low-hop neighbor aggregation. The interpretability via path attention maps offers a practical tool for fraud investigators to trace which specific behavioral sequences drive a prediction. However, the primary benchmark is synthetic (constructed via simulation with 12 hand-designed fraud rules), which limits external validity and raises open questions about whether the gains transfer to real-world, unlabeled fraud graphs. The approach's reliance on random-walk sampling also leaves open how it behaves on graphs with very different degree distributions or directed/multi-relational structures beyond the two relation types tested.
