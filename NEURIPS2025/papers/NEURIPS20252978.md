## Problem

Existing event camera detectors are locked into fixed-frequency paradigms that align event streams with lower-rate frame annotations, causing significant performance degradation when operational frequency increases (e.g., from 20 Hz to 90 Hz or 180 Hz). At higher frequencies, event data becomes sparse and semantically impoverished, yet no prior method explicitly addresses robust detection across a continuous range of operational frequencies while exploiting the microsecond-level temporal richness that event cameras uniquely provide.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252978/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes two modalities simultaneously: (1) event streams pre-processed into 4D tensors (polarity × temporal slices × H × W) sampled at two frequencies—a low-frequency set aligned with frame annotations and a higher-frequency set for temporal richness—and (2) RGB frames at 640×480 resolution from the DSEC dataset. The output is a set of bounding boxes with class labels (Car, Pedestrian, and optionally Bus, Bicycle, Truck, Motorcycle, Rider, Train) predicted at the end timestamp of the event interval, evaluated under COCO mAP protocol at multiple IoU thresholds and object sizes.

## Method

FlexEvent combines two key components. FlexFuse adaptively fuses event and frame features at each of four scales: RVT extracts event features and ResNet-50 extracts frame features, which are concatenated and passed through a gating function that computes per-pixel soft weights (α, β) via Softmax over a learned linear projection plus Gaussian noise perturbation; the fused feature is a weighted sum, and multi-frequency event features (low-freq and high-freq) are added together before feeding a YOLOX detection head. A variance-based regularizer on the soft weights prevents collapse to a single modality. FlexTune enables frequency generalization through iterative self-training: it first trains at a higher frequency using only the last high-frequency event (sparse GT labels), then generates pseudo-labels on unlabeled high-frequency data via bootstrapping inference, refines them through bidirectional event augmentation, confidence-aware NMS, and IoU-based tracklet pruning, and finally cycles self-training with a combined GT + pseudo-label loss. Training uses the YOLOX framework for 100K iterations with mixed BPTT/TBPTT, Adam optimizer, and OneCycle LR schedule on two RTX A5000 GPUs.

## Results

On DSEC-Det (the primary benchmark), FlexEvent achieves 57.4 mAP, 78.2 AP50, and 66.6 AP75, surpassing the strongest event-frame fusion baseline DAGr-50 (41.9 mAP) by 15.5 points and the best event-only method LEOD (41.1 mAP) by 16.3 points. On DSEC-Detection, FlexEvent reaches 47.4 average mAP (Car 59.3, Pedestrian 37.4, L-Veh 45.5), outperforming CAFR (38.0) by 9.4 points. On DSEC-MOD, FlexEvent achieves 36.9 average mAP, exceeding RENet (29.0) by 7.9 points. Notably, FlexEvent retains 96.2% of its 20 Hz performance at 90 Hz and still achieves 50.9 mAP at 180 Hz, where the event-only baseline RVT drops to 22.9 mAP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FlexEvent outperforms all 20 compared baselines across all three datasets. Against the strongest event-only detectors on DSEC-Det, it leads LEOD [55] (41.1 mAP) by 16.3 points, RVT [18] (38.4) by 19.0, SAST [39] (38.1) by 19.3, and SSM [70] (38.0) by 19.4. Among event-frame fusion methods, it surpasses DAGr-50 [16] (41.9 mAP) by 15.5 points and HDI-Former [31] (46.7 mAP) by 10.7 points on DSEC-Det. On DSEC-Detection, FlexEvent (47.4 avg mAP) beats the strongest prior method CAFR [3] (38.0) by 9.4 points, with particularly large gains on Pedestrian (37.4 vs. 25.8) and Large-Vehicle (45.5 vs. 38.2). On DSEC-MOD, it exceeds RENet [66] (29.0 avg) by 7.9 points. The remaining baselines (SENet, CBAM, ECA-Net, SAGate, DCF, SPNet, RAMNet, FAGC, FPN-Fusion, EFNet, DRFuser, CMX) all trail FlexEvent by 10–30 mAP points, confirming that the adaptive fusion and frequency-tuning mechanism provides a consistent advantage over both attention-based and cross-modal fusion alternatives.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the event-frame multimodal detection line (DAGr, CAFR, RENet) by explicitly addressing the previously overlooked problem of operating at varying event frequencies rather than a single fixed rate aligned to the frame rate. The FlexFuse module builds on attention-based cross-modal gating (inspired by [66, 64]) but adds learned noise perturbation and a variance-based regularizer to balance modality contributions. FlexTune repurposes the self-training/pseudo-label paradigm from label-efficient learning (LEOD, [55]) and applies it to the frequency axis, generating temporally consistent high-frequency labels via bidirectional event augmentation, NMS filtering, and tracklet pruning. Conceptually, the paper carves new ground by treating operational frequency as a first-class training and inference variable rather than a fixed dataset property.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ability to maintain 96.2% of 20 Hz performance at 90 Hz and still detect at 180 Hz is practically significant for safety-critical autonomous driving where scene dynamics can shift rapidly. The 15.5% mAP gap over DAGr on DSEC-Det and the ~3× speed advantage (12.4 ms vs 43.9 ms at 180 Hz) make this a strong candidate for real-time deployment. Open questions include generalization beyond driving scenarios (all three DSEC variants are automotive), dependence on synchronized RGB frames being available, and whether the self-training loop degrades under domain shift. The reliance on a single dataset family (DSEC) limits confidence in cross-domain robustness.
