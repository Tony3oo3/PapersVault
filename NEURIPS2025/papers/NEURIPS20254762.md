## Problem

Existing multimodal LLMs treat audio and video as independent channels, relying on simple concatenation or frame interleaving that overlooks fine-grained spatio-temporal interactions. No unified model existed that could simultaneously comprehend and generate synchronized sounding videos under complex, multi-turn instructions, leaving a gap in both joint understanding and generation with explicit audio-video synchrony.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254762/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts video clips (encoded via Qwen2.5-VL's ViT with 14px patches and 2×2 spatial merging), raw audio waveforms (converted to 128-bin mel-spectrograms at 25ms/10ms-overlap windows and encoded by BEATs), text prompts, or pre-synchronized sounding videos. Outputs include free-form text responses and/or 4-second synchronized audio-video clips at 240P/24fps with 16kHz audio, generated by a frozen JavisDiT conditioned on LLM-derived semantic and spatiotemporal-prior embeddings. The SyncFusion module temporally aligns audio to video frames (zero-padding if needed) so that each output SyncAV token corresponds to a specific spatial patch in a specific frame, preserving compatibility with the backbone's MRoPE positional encoding.

## Method

JavisGPT adopts an encoder-LLM-decoder architecture built on Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct, with two key architectural contributions. First, the SyncFusion module uses a shared cross-attention layer to inject frame-aligned audio cues into video patch representations, producing compact SyncAV tokens (reducing token count from 3.5K to 2.0K) that explicitly encode localized audio-visual events. Second, hierarchical JavisQueries bridge the LLM to a frozen JavisDiT generator: N semantic queries (Q^c) capture the overall event description and are projected to match T5-XXL's condition space via an alignment loss, while N′ spatiotemporal-prior queries (Q^s) encode fine-grained spatial (where) and temporal (when) event boundaries, projected to match ImageBind's ST-prior space. Training follows a three-stage pipeline—multimodal pretraining (audio projector + DiT condition alignment on 600K audio-text and 1.5M AV-caption pairs), audio-video fine-tuning (SyncFusion + LoRA on 450K triplets with combined NTP, alignment, and diffusion losses), and large-scale instruction tuning on the newly constructed JavisInst-0mni dataset (200K+ GPT-4o-curated dialogues spanning entity/relation/global comprehension and instruction/conditional/multi-turn generation)—progressively building synchronized comprehension and generation without fine-tuning the DiT generator.

## Results

On synchronized audio-video comprehension, JavisGPT achieves 93.8 on AVQA and 82.1 on MusicAVQA, surpassing Qwen2.5-Omni (91.5 / 79.9) and VideoLLaMA2.1 (– / 80.9), while maintaining competitive video (70.2 Perception, 68.4 MVBench) and audio (82.1 TUT2017) understanding. On text-to-sounding-video generation (JavisBench-mini), JavisGPT achieves FVD 243.6 and JavisScore 0.157, slightly outperforming the standalone JavisDiT generator (FVD 248.9, JavisScore 0.153) and dramatically outperforming unified MLLMs NExT-GPT (FVD 1463.2, JavisScore 0.038) and UnifiedIO-2 (FVD 1597.4, JavisScore 0.053). These results are obtained with only 1.5M training samples, demonstrating strong data efficiency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest unified omni-modal baseline Qwen2.5-Omni, JavisGPT wins on AVQA (93.8 vs. 91.5), MusicAVQA (82.1 vs. 79.9), TUT2017 (82.1 vs. 78.3), and MVBench (68.4 vs. 66.9), while slightly trailing on Perception (70.2 vs. 70.4), ClothoAQA (67.3 vs. 68.0), and AVSD (62.2 vs. 62.8). Against pipeline-based unified MLLMs NExT-GPT and UnifiedIO-2, JavisGPT dominates across every comprehension benchmark by 20–80 points (e.g., AVQA 93.8 vs. 25.3/61.2; TUT2017 82.1 vs. 6.4/9.1) and on generation by an order of magnitude in FVD (243.6 vs. 1463.2/1597.4) and JavisScore (0.157 vs. 0.038/0.053). Compared to the standalone JavisDiT generator, JavisGPT achieves marginally better FVD (243.6 vs. 248.9), JavisScore (0.157 vs. 0.153), and AVHScore (0.185 vs. 0.181), attributed to the LLM backbone's stronger semantic encoding. Against VideoLLaMA2.1, JavisGPT leads on AVQA (93.8, not reported for VideoLLaMA2.1), MusicAVQA (82.1 vs. 80.9), AVSD (62.2 vs. 57.2), and TUT2017 (82.1 vs. 77.3). On video-only benchmarks, JavisGPT is competitive with Qwen2.5-VL (70.2 vs. 70.5 Perception) but slightly below InternVL2.5 on MVBench (68.4 vs. 72.0), confirming that the audio-video focus does not severely degrade pure visual understanding.

## Relation to Prior Work

JavisGPT extends the LLM+DiT paradigm established by MetaQueries and NExT-GPT to the joint audio-video domain, but departs from both by freezing the DiT generator and using an alignment loss to the T5-XXL text encoder rather than full fine-tuning, reducing training cost. For comprehension, it introduces the SyncFusion module (cross-attention-based audio-to-video injection) as an alternative to the concatenation or interleaving strategies used by VideoLLaMA2 and Qwen2.5-Omni, explicitly modeling spatio-temporal synchrony at the patch level. The three-stage training pipeline (MM-PreTrain → AV-FineTune → MM-InstTune) progressively adapts a vision-language backbone (Qwen2.5-VL) to audio perception and synchronized generation, building on the audio encoder (BEATs) and the JavisDiT generator as frozen components.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first system to unify sounding-video comprehension and generation in a single MLLM, filling a gap where prior work treated these as separate pipelines (NExT-GPT, UnifiedIO-2) or focused on one direction only. The JavisInst-0mni dataset (200K+ dialogues) and the SyncFusion module offer practical tools for the community. Key open questions remain: the misaligned training objectives (NTP for text vs. diffusion loss for AV) and asymmetric input-output modeling limit true unification; scaling beyond 7B and applying RL-based post-training are identified as important next steps; and whether a fully end-to-end autoregressive architecture (à la EMU3, VILA-U) could replace the modular LLM+DiT design for AV remains unexplored.
