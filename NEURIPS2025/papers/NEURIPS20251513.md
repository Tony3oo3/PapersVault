## Problem

Graph-based recommender systems suffer from topology bias, where popularity distorts the interaction graph's degree distribution and systematically favors popular items during message passing. Prior analyses focused on embedding-level or gradient-level effects, leaving the fundamental question of how topology bias distorts the message passing process itself unaddressed. This paper bridges that gap by providing a Dirichlet energy perspective that reveals message passing inherently amplifies topology bias and consistently benefits highly connected nodes.

## Input / Output

TSP takes as input the pretrained node embeddings from an existing GNN backbone (LightGCN, SimGCL, or LightGCL) along with the user-item bipartite interaction graph and a similarity threshold hyperparameter θ. It operates entirely at test time without retraining: the semantic graph is constructed by thresholding pairwise cosine similarities among pretrained embeddings, then lifted into a simplicial complex. The output is a set of refined node embeddings (fused across multiple simplex orders via mean pooling) from which standard inner-product recommendation scores are computed, with particular gains for the 20% least popular (tail) items.

## Method

The paper first establishes that graph message passing is equivalent to minimizing a Tikhonov-regularized Dirichlet energy, proving that popular nodes necessarily accumulate larger embedding norms (norm concentration) and that each node's embedding update is upper-bounded by its local Dirichlet energy—creating a self-reinforcing amplification cycle. To counteract this, TSP extends message passing to simplicial complexes using four modules: (1) Semantic Graph Construction, which builds a densified adjacency from pretrained embeddings to partially alleviate topology bias; (2) Intra-Simplex Smoothing, which aggregates node embeddings into k-simplex signals via products of boundary matrices; (3) Inter-Simplex Propagation, which applies Hodge Laplacian-based message passing (X ← (I − βL_k)X) within each simplex order; and (4) Multi-Order Fusion, which converts simplex embeddings back to node space and mean-pools across orders. The key theoretical insight (Lemma 4.1) is that the simplicial resolvent operator strictly contracts embedding norms for signals in ker(B_{k+1}), unlike graph propagation which inflates norms for high-degree nodes, thereby regularizing the embedding space and improving tail-item representations without any retraining.

## Results

On tail items (20% least popular), TSP achieves consistent and substantial improvements across all five datasets and three backbone models. For example, with the SimGCL backbone on Gowalla, TSP reaches 0.055 R@20 and 0.047 N@20, a 96.43% and 161.11% relative gain over the best debiasing baseline (CausE at 0.028/0.018). With LightGCL on Globo, TSP achieves 0.032 R@20, a 133.33% improvement over the best baseline (IPS at 0.024). TSP also maintains or improves overall recommendation quality and achieves the best EFD@20 and APT@20 fairness scores on all five datasets with the LightGCN backbone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TSP outperforms all four debiasing baselines (IPS, CausE, MACR, SDRO) on tail-item Recall@20 and NDCG@20 across every dataset and backbone model, with statistically significant gains (p<0.05). IPS is typically the strongest baseline (e.g., 0.040 R@20 on Adressa with LightGCN), but TSP surpasses it by 37.5% on that dataset and by up to 66.67% with LightGCL. CausE is competitive on Gowalla with SimGCL (0.028 R@20) but TSP nearly doubles it (0.055). MACR and SDRO are consistently the weakest baselines, often performing close to or below the unmodified backbone on tail items. The gains are most pronounced with contrastive-learning backbones (SimGCL, LightGCL), where TSP achieves relative improvements of 60–161% over the best baseline, suggesting higher-order simplicial structures complement contrastive objectives particularly well. On overall (non-tail) performance, TSP matches or slightly exceeds the backbone and most debiasing methods, confirming that bias mitigation does not come at the cost of general recommendation quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the popularity bias mitigation literature for graph-based recommenders by shifting the analytical lens from embedding-level or gradient-level explanations (Kim et al. 2023, Chen et al. 2023, Lin et al. 2025) to a Dirichlet energy characterization of the message passing process itself. Methodologically, it builds on the simplicial complex framework used in higher-order GNNs (SNN, BSCNet, HSN) but repurposes Hodge Laplacian-based propagation as a test-time debiasing mechanism rather than a training-time architecture. The debiasing baselines (IPS, CausE, MACR, SDRO) represent the standard training-time or reweighting approaches, whereas TSP operates purely at inference on pretrained embeddings, positioning it as a complementary rather than competing paradigm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Dirichlet energy framing provides the first principled explanation for why graph message passing systematically inflates norms of high-degree nodes (norm concentration), connecting a well-known empirical observation to a formal energy minimization argument. The simplicial contraction property (Lemma 4.1) offers a clean theoretical justification for why higher-order structures mitigate bias, and the test-time design makes the approach immediately deployable without retraining. Open questions include how the method scales to very large graphs where simplicial complex construction becomes expensive, whether the semantic graph threshold is sensitive in practice, and whether the approach generalizes to other bias types (e.g., demographic or geographic bias) beyond popularity-driven topology distortion.
