## Problem

High-resolution image generation with Diffusion Transformers (DiTs) suffers from quadratic latency growth due to attention over long sequences, making single-GPU inference impractical. Existing multi-GPU parallel strategies—tensor parallel, sequence parallel, and DistriFusion—either incur per-layer communication costs that scale with sequence length or require full-spatial K/V buffers on every device, preventing memory from scaling down as more GPUs are added.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253289/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a noisy latent representation (decomposed into M non-overlapping spatial patches), a text prompt, and a diffusion timestep, and produces a high-resolution RGB image after iterative denoising and VAE decoding. Experiments cover resolutions from 1024×1024 up to 4096×4096 pixels (and 8192px for memory analysis), with 20–28 diffusion steps depending on the model. The patch partitioning and layer partitioning are the two orthogonal axes of parallelism, with M patches distributed across N GPUs each holding L/N consecutive transformer layers.

## Method

PipeFusion is a patch-level pipeline parallelism that simultaneously partitions DiT layers along the data-flow direction and the input image into M non-overlapping patches, assigning each GPU a contiguous set of layers and one patch per micro-step. The core insight is exploiting input temporal redundancy: because consecutive diffusion timesteps produce highly similar activations, each device uses one-step-stale activations from the previous timestep as attention context instead of waiting for fresh upstream activations, eliminating pipeline bubbles after a short warmup. Communication is reduced to only the input of the first layer and output of the last layer per device (total cost 2O(p×hs), independent of layer count L), and asynchronous P2P transfers overlap this communication with local computation. Memory scales down as 1/N for both model parameters and KV buffers, unlike DistriFusion which retains full-spatial KV on every device. The pipeline achieves an effective computation ratio of M·S/(M·S+N−1), which exceeds 98% for typical settings (e.g., M=N=4, S=50).

## Results

On 8×L40 PCIe GPUs, PipeFusion achieves the lowest DiT inference latency across all tested models and resolutions: 1.48×, 1.55×, and 1.16× faster than the second-best method on Pixart at 1024px, 2048px, and 4096px respectively; 1.57× and 1.30× faster than best USP on SD3-medium at 1024px and 2048px; and 1.23× and 1.25× faster on Flux.1-dev. In terms of generation quality (FID on COCO Captions 2014), PipeFusion with 8 devices achieves 28.46 FID on PixArt vs. 41.81 for DistriFusion, and 25.97 vs. 25.03 (original) on Flux.1. PipeFusion also reduces GPU memory to 32–36% of sequence parallelism for the 12B Flux.1 model and cuts communication share to 4.6% of total latency at 4096px (vs. 17.9% for SP).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PipeFusion outperforms all five baselines on inference latency across Pixart, SD3-medium, and Flux.1-dev. Against Tensor Parallel, PipeFusion wins decisively because TP's 4O(p×hs)·L communication cost scales with both sequence length and layer count, making it the slowest method especially at high resolutions. Against SP-Ulysses, PipeFusion is consistently faster (e.g., 0.66s vs. 2.59s on Pixart 1024px on A100s), as Ulysses' All2All communication cannot be overlapped with computation. Against SP-Ring and USP (the strongest SP baselines), PipeFusion achieves 1.16×–1.57× speedup, with the gap widening at higher resolutions where communication dominates. Against DistriFusion, PipeFusion wins on both latency (avoiding OOM at 4096px where DistriFusion fails) and generation quality (FID 28.46 vs. 41.81 on PixArt 8-device), because PipeFusion's pipelined design exposes a larger fraction of fresh activations per timestep. PipeFusion also uniquely reduces model parameter memory to P/N (like TP), making it the only method that scales memory down with device count for both parameters and KV buffers.

## Relation to Prior Work

PipeFusion directly extends DistriFusion (CVPR 2024), which first exploited input temporal redundancy in U-Net-based diffusion models by reusing stale KV activations to hide communication behind computation. The key conceptual departure is restructuring this from sequence parallelism (requiring KV exchange at every layer) into patch-level pipeline parallelism, where only stage-boundary activations are communicated, reducing total communication from O(p·hs·L) to O(p·hs). The paper also positions itself against the broader parallel inference landscape—tensor parallelism (Megatron-LM), sequence parallelism (DeepSpeed-Ulysses, Ring-Attention, USP), and classical pipeline parallelism (GPipe, TeraPipe)—arguing that DiTs' full-attention mechanism and large activation volumes make these approaches suboptimal, and that temporal redundancy is the missing ingredient to make pipeline parallelism viable for a single-image workload.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As DiTs become the dominant architecture for high-resolution image and video generation (Flux.1 at 12B, SD3 at 2B), efficient multi-GPU inference on commodity hardware (PCIe-connected GPUs without NVLink) is a critical practical bottleneck that PipeFusion addresses with a clean 78% communication reduction and 1/N parameter distribution. The method's natural composability with sequence parallelism (PipeFusion across nodes, SP within NVLink domains) and its architectural compatibility with video DiTs (Wan2.1, HunyuanVideo) suggest a path toward scalable deployment. Key limitations include dependence on many diffusion steps (ineffective for 1–4 step distilled models) and a small warmup overhead, while open questions include automatic warmup detection, hybrid multi-node configurations, and whether the temporal-redundancy assumption holds as model architectures evolve.
