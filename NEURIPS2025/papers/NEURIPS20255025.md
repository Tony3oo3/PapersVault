## Problem

Existing autoregressive EEG models arrange multi-channel data in a purely temporal sequence for 'next-time prediction,' which fails to capture the dynamic spatial topology of brain activity that shifts across cognitive tasks. This temporal-centric ordering limits the ability to model fundamental shifts in spatial brain dynamics, hindering the development of truly universal and generalizable EEG representations.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-channel EEG signals of arbitrary channel count and sampling rate, resampled to 200 Hz and segmented into non-overlapping windows of 1024 samples further divided into patches of 200 samples (shape N×T×P). The THVQ-VAE tokenizer maps each patch into discrete tokens at 5 hierarchical spatial scales (whole-brain down to individual channels) sharing a common 8192×128 codebook, which are then flattened in a spatio-temporal order (hierarchically in space, sequentially in time) for autoregressive modeling. During fine-tuning, text instructions are concatenated with EEG tokens via a [SEP] delimiter, and the model outputs a class label as the answer token.

## Method

THD-BAR introduces a Brain Topology Hierarchy (BTH) that imposes a physiologically grounded 'whole brain → brain region → channel' multi-scale spatial order on EEG channels, redefining autoregressive learning as 'next-scale-time prediction.' A Topology-Hierarchical VQ-VAE (THVQ-VAE) tokenizes EEG into discrete multi-scale tokens using modified multi-scale quantization with downscale/upscale operations and residual refinement layers, where each scale's tokens are predicted conditioned on all coarser scales at the same time step plus all tokens from previous time steps. The BAR module (GPT-2 based, 124M–1555M parameters) is trained with a scale-time-wise causal mask that enforces both intra-time hierarchical prediction (coarse-to-fine spatial scales) and inter-time temporal causality. The three-stage pipeline trains the tokenizer on 17 diverse EEG datasets, pre-trains the BAR model via next-scale-time prediction, and fine-tunes with multi-task instruction tuning on 10 downstream datasets spanning 5 tasks.

## Results

THD-BAR achieves the best balanced accuracy on 9 out of 10 downstream EEG datasets across 5 task categories. The largest model (THD-BAR-Huge, 1555M params) reaches 43.9% on DEAP, 73.9% on SEED, 63.6% on MIBCI, 58.9% on BCIC4-1, 67.1% on EEGMat, 62.9% on STEW, 85.7% on EDF, 68.4% on HMC, 82.2% on TUAB, and 65.3% on TUEV. The only dataset where THD-BAR does not achieve the top score is STEW, where EEGPT (63.2%) slightly edges out THD-BAR-Huge (62.9%). Even the smallest variant (THD-BAR-Base, 124M) surpasses the strongest multi-task baseline NeuroLM (254M) on all 10 datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest multi-task general model NeuroLM (254M), THD-BAR-Base (124M) already wins on all 10 datasets, with the most notable gains on TUEV (64.3% vs. 45.6%, +18.7 pp), DEAP (42.3% vs. 40.1%, +2.2 pp), and SEED (73.5% vs. 70.2%, +3.3 pp). THD-BAR-Huge extends these leads to 65.3% on TUEV (+19.7 pp over NeuroLM) and 43.9% on DEAP (+3.8 pp). Against EEGPT, which benefits from both pre-training and fine-tuning on STEW, THD-BAR-Huge is marginally behind (62.9% vs. 63.2%) but leads on all other overlapping datasets. Compared to single-task baselines (EEGNet, TSception, LGGNet), THD-BAR achieves substantially higher accuracy on most tasks, though EEGNet's 63.3% on MIBCI is close to THD-BAR-Huge's 63.6%. Against the masked-autoencoder baselines LaBraM and BIOT, THD-BAR shows large margins on emotion (SEED: 73.9% vs. 73.2%/71.0%) and epilepsy (TUAB: 82.2% vs. 81.4%/79.6%), confirming that the autoregressive next-scale-time strategy captures spatio-temporal dynamics more effectively than reconstruction-based pre-training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the autoregressive EEG foundation model lineage established by NeuroLM and EEGPT, which apply GPT-style next-time prediction to multi-channel EEG sequences. The key conceptual departure is borrowing the 'next-scale prediction' idea from visual autoregressive modeling (VAR) and grounding it in a physiologically-motivated Brain Topology Hierarchy, redefining the AR objective as 'next-scale-time prediction' rather than purely temporal prediction. The THVQ-VAE tokenizer extends the VQ-VAE tokenization used in LaBraM and NeuroLM by introducing multi-scale quantization with downscale/upscale operations across the BTH hierarchy, while the BAR module retains the GPT-2 backbone but replaces the standard causal mask with a scale-time-wise mask.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine gap in current EEG AR models: their temporal-centric ordering cannot capture task-dependent shifts in spatial brain topology, which limits generalization across diverse cognitive tasks. By establishing a physiologically-grounded multi-scale spatial order and demonstrating consistent (if modest) improvements across 10 downstream datasets spanning 5 tasks, it provides evidence that spatial hierarchy matters for EEG representation learning. Open questions include whether the BTH's fixed 5-scale structure generalizes to arbitrary electrode configurations, whether the approach scales to real-time BCI deployment, and whether data-driven or anatomy-based topological partitioning could outperform the current spatial-proximity-based grouping. The multi-modal integration remains limited to instruction-based fine-tuning, and deeper fusion with other physiological signals (iEEG, fNIRS, fMRI) is left for future work.
