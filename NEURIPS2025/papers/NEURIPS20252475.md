## Problem

Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), the dominant RL algorithm for LLM reasoning, suffers from a previously unrecognized failure mode: the likelihood of correct responses can stagnate or even decrease during training (Lazy Likelihood Displacement). While analogous misalignment was documented for DPO, no analysis or targeted fix existed for GRPO's group-based, online sampling paradigm, where negative gradients uniformly penalize all tokens in incorrect responses—including tokens that are logically correct or semantically shared with the positive responses.

## Input / Output

Given a question x and a group of G sampled responses with binary rewards (1 for correct, 0 for incorrect), the method extracts last-layer hidden embeddings from the old policy's forward pass (no extra forward pass needed) and computes per-token NTHR scores via inner products between positive and negative response embeddings weighted by prediction-error similarity vectors. The output is a modified advantage vector in which a subset of negative tokens—those whose NTHR exceeds a dynamically computed threshold τ—have their advantage scaled by a factor η < 1, while all other tokens retain their original GRPO advantages.

## Method

The paper first proves (Theorem 4.4) that the rate of likelihood increase for a correct response is suppressed by a Group Weighted Hidden Embedding Score (GWHES) that aggregates embedding-similarity-weighted prediction errors across all negative tokens, establishing a formal link between shared token structure and LLD. Motivated by Corollary 4.5, NTHR assigns each token k′ in an incorrect response y_j⁻ an influence score s_{j,<k′}⁻ equal to the sum over all positive-response tokens of the inner product of their hidden embeddings scaled by a token-level prediction-error similarity α. A threshold τ is set to β·min of the average mutual influence among correct responses, and any negative token with s_{j,<k′}⁻ > τ has its advantage multiplied by η = 2|0.5 − p| (where p is the group success rate), selectively attenuating the penalty on tokens that most harm correct-response likelihood while preserving the signal from genuinely wrong tokens. This token-level selectivity avoids the data-efficiency loss of discarding entire negative responses and leverages GRPO's group structure to use correct responses as anchors for identifying harmful tokens.

## Results

NTHR consistently improves average performance over GRPO across all tested models (0.5B–3B parameters) on five math reasoning benchmarks (AIME24, AMC, MATH500, Minerva Math, OlympiadBench). The largest gain is on Qwen2.5-3B, where NTHR reaches 36.30% average versus GRPO's 33.88% (+2.42 pp) and the base model's 31.36%. On Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B trained on MATH, NTHR achieves 41.94% average vs. GRPO's 41.14% (+0.8 pp), and on the harder DeepScaler dataset the gap widens to 39.60% vs. 37.80% (+1.8 pp). Per-sample likelihood-change analysis (Fig. 4) confirms that NTHR selectively mitigates Lazy Likelihood Displacement, with green bars (NTHR) consistently exceeding blue bars (GRPO) across all models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GRPO (the primary baseline from DeepSeekMath [22]), NTHR wins on every model and dataset configuration tested: +0.8 pp on Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B (MATH), +1.1 pp on Qwen2.5-0.5B-Ins, +1.5 pp on Qwen2.5-1.5B-Ins, +1.8 pp on Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B (DeepScaler), and +2.42 pp on Qwen2.5-3B. The Pos Only ablation (removing all negative gradients) actually underperforms GRPO by 1.3 pp on Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B, confirming that NTHR's selective approach is superior to simply discarding negative gradients. Against the NLL-loss baseline from Vapo [31] on Llama3.2-1B-Instruct (Table 6), NTHR achieves 15.96% average versus 16.98% for NLL Loss, a slight loss; however, the authors note the two methods are complementary—NLL Loss strengthens positive gradients while NTHR mitigates harmful negative updates—and both independently improve over GRPO (15.4%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DPO misalignment/likelihood displacement analysis (Razin et al. 2024; Ren & Sutherland 2024) to the GRPO setting, showing that the same negative-gradient pathology manifests as Lazy Likelihood Displacement (LLD) in group-relative policy optimization. It builds directly on GRPO (DeepSeekMath/DeepSeek-R1) as the base algorithm, but departs from prior DPO-based fixes (e.g., discarding harmful examples or adding positive-only training) by exploiting GRPO's group structure: correct responses serve as anchors to identify which tokens in incorrect responses are most harmful. The NTHR selective penalization is a new token-level mechanism that has no direct analogue in the DPO literature, leveraging hidden-embedding similarity scores to downweight specific negative gradients rather than removing them wholesale.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GRPO is the dominant RL algorithm in current LLM reasoning pipelines (DeepSeek-R1, Med-R1, Search-R1, etc.), so identifying a systematic failure mode in its negative gradients has practical relevance for anyone training reasoning models. However, the demonstrated gains are modest (0.8–1.8% average across benchmarks), the evaluation is confined to small models (0.5B–3B) and math reasoning, and the theoretical analysis relies on the unconstrained-features assumption. Open questions include whether LLD and NTHR generalize to non-math domains, larger models, and multi-turn or tool-use settings, and whether the GWHES-based token selection remains effective as model scale and response length grow.
