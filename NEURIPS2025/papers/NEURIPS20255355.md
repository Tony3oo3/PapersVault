## Problem

Diversity maximization (max-min, max-sum, sum-min diversification) has been extensively studied under group fairness constraints, but no algorithms with theoretical guarantees exist for the individually fair variant, where every point in the dataset must have a representative among its n/k nearest neighbors in the selected subset. This paper is the first to provide bicriteria approximation algorithms for all three diversity objectives under individual fairness constraints.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255355/cropped_page8_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a set P of n points in a metric space, a target subset size k, and a fairness tolerance parameter α ≥ 1, and produces a subset S of exactly k points that is (3α)-fair (every point in P has a selected point within 3α times its fair radius r_k(x), where r_k(x) is the minimum radius enclosing at least n/k points around x). The algorithm also outputs a partition of P into at most k disjoint individual fairness regions (balls), which serve as the structural bridge to matroid-constrained selection. The diversity guarantee is bicriteria: the returned solution achieves at least 1/β of the optimal α-fair diversity while relaxing the fairness constraint by a fixed factor of 3.

## Method

The core mechanism is a two-stage reduction from individual fairness to partition matroid constraints. First, a greedy algorithm (IFRGENERATE) constructs at most k disjoint balls (individual fairness regions) by iteratively selecting the uncovered point with the smallest fair radius as a center and covering all points within 2α·r(x) of it; the disjointness follows from the non-decreasing order of radii. Second, the problem is reformulated as selecting k points under a partition matroid (at most k−m+1 points from each fairness region, at most k from uncovered points), and an existing β-approximation algorithm for the corresponding matroid-constrained diversity problem is invoked as a subroutine. The factor-of-3 fairness relaxation is proven via a triangle inequality argument: any point x is within 2α·r(x) of its region center c_x, and the selected point s_x within that region is at most α·r(c_x) ≤ α·r(x) from c_x, giving d(x, s_x) ≤ 3α·r(x). Instantiating with known subroutines yields (5+ε, 3) for max-min, (8+ε, 3) for max-sum, and (32+ε, 3) for sum-min diversification.

## Results

Across four datasets (CelebA, GloVe, MovieLens, Gaussian) and three diversity objectives, the proposed individually fair algorithms achieve diversity values that are 70–99% of the optimal unconstrained (ILP) solutions, with the largest losses occurring for sum-min diversification (67.6%–95.5%) and the smallest for max-sum (92.3%–99.6%). The fairness metric α_max is always kept below 2, confirming the theoretical (3α)-fairness guarantee. Compared to unconstrained approximate baselines, diversity losses are bounded to at most 30%, while running times remain under 4 seconds for k ≤ 50 on most objectives (max-sum local search takes ~300 s at k=50 due to small ε).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its individually fair algorithms against two unconstrained baselines: GMM (Gonzalez's greedy, a 2-approximation for max-min diversification) and the greedy algorithm of Hassin et al. (a 2-approximation for max-sum diversification). For max-min diversification, the fair algorithm sacrifices up to ~30% diversity relative to GMM while achieving α_max < 2, whereas GMM provides no individual fairness guarantee. For max-sum diversification, the fair algorithm retains 92–99% of the greedy baseline's diversity (a loss of only 1–8%), making the fairness cost negligible. For sum-min diversification, the unconstrained baseline is obtained by collapsing all points into a single fairness region; the fair algorithm loses up to ~32% diversity (down to 0.676 on MovieLens at k=5) but still guarantees α_max < 2. Overall, the fair algorithms consistently trade modest diversity (≤30–35%) for a provable individual fairness guarantee that the unconstrained baselines do not provide.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the individual fairness framework from clustering (Jung et al. 2020; Mahabadi & Vakilian 2020; Vakilian & Yalçiner 2022) to diversity maximization objectives (max-min, max-sum, sum-min diversification). The key technical move is reducing individually fair k-selection to diversity maximization under partition matroid constraints via individual fairness regions, mirroring the reduction used in individually fair clustering but applied to fundamentally different distance-based objectives. The resulting bicriteria approximation algorithms compose existing matroid-constrained diversity maximization subroutines (Wang et al. 2023 for max-min, Abbassi et al. 2013 for max-sum, Bhaskara et al. 2016 for sum-min) with the fairness-region construction, yielding the first theoretical guarantees for this problem class.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work providing provable approximation guarantees for individually fair diversity maximization, filling a gap between the well-studied unconstrained and group-fair variants. The (β,3) bicriteria framework offers a practical trade-off: a 3× relaxation in fairness tolerance in exchange for inherited diversity guarantees, which experiments confirm incurs only ~30% diversity loss. Open questions include whether individually fair diversity maximization is strictly harder to approximate than its unconstrained counterpart, and whether the large sum-min factor (32+ε) can be tightened. Extensions to streaming, distributed, and deletion-robust settings are identified as natural next steps.
