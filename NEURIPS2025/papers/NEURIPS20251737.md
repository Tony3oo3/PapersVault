## Problem

Existing writing benchmarks are restricted to narrow domains (e.g., fiction only), rely on simplistic single-sentence prompts or fixed templates, and use homogeneous input materials, failing to capture the diversity of real-world writing tasks. Simultaneously, current LLM-as-a-judge evaluation methods depend on static, pre-defined criteria (e.g., fluency, coherence) that cannot adapt to instance-specific requirements such as style, format, length constraints, or material integration, making them inadequate for nuanced multi-dimensional writing assessment.

## Input / Output

The benchmark provides 1,000 free-form writing queries (445 Chinese, 555 English) spanning 6 primary domains and 100 subdomains, each optionally paired with heterogeneous reference materials ranging from tens to ~19,000 tokens (avg 1,699). The evaluation framework consumes a query, a model response, and five dynamically generated instance-specific criteria (each with a name, extended description, and fine-grained scoring rubric), producing per-criterion scores on a 1–10 scale with textual justifications. The data curation pipeline additionally outputs a filtered 12K-sample SFT dataset by selecting top-50% responses per subdomain from 24K generated candidates.

## Method

The paper introduces a query-dependent evaluation framework with two phases: (1) an LLM (Claude-3.7) dynamically generates five instance-specific criteria per query, each comprising a concise name, an extended evaluation description, and detailed scoring rubrics with quality levels; (2) a dedicated critic model (fine-tuned Qwen-2.5-7B-Instruct on 155K Claude-3.7-scored instances) performs rubric-based scoring, mapping (query, response, criterion) → score + justification. The benchmark itself is constructed via a four-stage human-AI collaborative pipeline: LLM-generated initial queries are diversified using six strategies (style, format, length, personalization, content specificity, expression), then human annotators collect reference materials and experts perform two-stage optimization (query adaptation and material pruning). The framework's data curation capability is validated by filtering 24K generated writing samples to 12K high-quality ones, after which SFT on Qwen-2.5-7B and Llama-3.1-8B yields models that outperform GPT-4o on WritingBench.

## Results

On WritingBench (1,000 queries, 6 domains, 100 subdomains), Claude-3.7-thinking achieves the highest overall score of 7.91/10, followed by Claude-3.7 (7.85) and Deepseek-R1 (7.70). The paper's key contribution is demonstrated by the filtered 7B models: Qwen-2.5-7B-filtered scores 7.44 and Llama-3.1-8B-filtered scores 7.39, both substantially outperforming GPT-4o (6.81) and their unfiltered base models (5.64 and 4.42 respectively). Across requirement dimensions, style (R1) scores are highest (7.9–8.7), format (R2) is intermediate (8.0–8.4), and length (R3) is weakest (6.0–8.1), revealing length adherence as the most challenging constraint for all models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's filtered 7B models (Qwen-2.5-7B-filtered: 7.44; Llama-3.1-8B-filtered: 7.39) outperform GPT-4o (6.81) by 0.63 and 0.58 points respectively, and also surpass o1-Preview (6.89), Qwen-Max (7.16), and all open-source baselines except Deepseek-R1 (7.70) and Claude-3.7 variants (7.85–7.91). Compared to their own unfiltered base models, the filtered versions gain +1.80 (Qwen 5.64→7.44) and +2.97 (Llama 4.42→7.39), demonstrating the data curation framework's effectiveness. Among proprietary models, Claude-3.7-thinking leads all domains and requirements, while Gemini-1.5-Pro (6.21) and Mistral-Large-Instruct (6.00) trail significantly. Writing-specialized models show mixed results: LongWriter (6.27) performs competitively on length (R3=6.8) but lags overall, while Suri (3.20) is the weakest model evaluated, scoring below 4 in every domain. The filtered 7B models also outperform much larger open-source models—Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct (5.05) and Qwen-2.5-72B-Instruct (6.40)—highlighting that targeted data quality surpasses raw parameter scale for writing tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the static-criteria LLM-as-a-judge paradigm (used in SuperCLUE, LongBench-Write, HelloBench) by introducing query-dependent, instance-specific criteria generation paired with a fine-tuned critic model for rubric-based scoring. It also extends the benchmark construction approach beyond single-domain or template-based writing evaluations (EQ-Bench, LongBench-Write) to a two-tiered taxonomy of 6 domains × 100 subdomains with free-form queries, heterogeneous reference materials, and multi-dimensional requirements (style, format, length). The data-curation component repurposes the evaluation framework as a reward signal for SFT filtering, analogous to how RLHF reward models select high-quality demonstrations, but applied specifically to writing quality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

WritingBench fills a practical gap as the first open-source, multi-domain writing benchmark with 1,000 free-form queries and diverse input materials, likely to become a standard evaluation tool for LLM writing capabilities. The query-dependent evaluation framework's 84% human alignment (vs. 67%/58% for static baselines) and the demonstration that a 7B model trained on 12K filtered samples outperforms GPT-4o suggest that targeted data curation can be more effective than raw scale for writing tasks. Open questions include whether the framework generalizes beyond the 6 domains tested, how well it handles truly novel writing genres, and whether the length-generation bottleneck (most models capping at ~3,000 tokens) can be addressed through the proposed evaluation-guided training.
