## Problem

While the VC-dimension is central to machine learning (ε-nets, sample compression, teaching), its parameterized complexity was largely unexplored beyond the solution size k. The paper addresses three gaps: proving the naive 2^O(|V|) brute-force algorithm is ETH-tight, identifying which structural hypergraph parameters (degree, dimension, hypertreewidth, transversal number) yield tractability, and designing a treewidth-based algorithm for a unified graph formulation that captures both set systems and graphs.

## Input / Output

The primary problem GEN-VC-DIMENSION takes a graph G=(V,E), two vertex subsets X and Y, and an integer k, and decides whether some S⊆X of size at least k is shattered by the open neighborhoods of vertices in Y. The hypergraph VC-DIMENSION is a special case where G is the bipartite incidence graph with X=V and Y the set of hyperedge-vertices. The 1-additive approximation algorithm (parameterized by maximum degree Δ) outputs a shattered set of size at least VC-dim−1 or certifies no shattered set of size k exists, running in 2^O(Δ log Δ)·|H|^O(1) time.

## Method

The paper combines three technical tools. First, a reduction from 3-COLORING constructs a bipartite graph whose vertex cover encodes all partial 3-colorings of a partition of the input, proving ETH lower bounds of 2^o(vcn+k)·|V|^O(1) for graphs and 2^o(|V|)·|H|^O(1) for hypergraphs. Second, an FPT 1-additive approximation exploits the binary-structure of a shattered set's witness (a 'good ordering' where edge membership mirrors bit positions) to check subsets of incident edges in 2^O(Δ log Δ) time. Third, the main algorithm performs dynamic programming on a nice tree-decomposition using pattern graphs: it first checks whether a shattered set fits entirely in a bag (brute force), and if not, leverages the fact that any shattered set of size k > log tw + 2 must be contained in some bag, reducing the DP to tracking valid functions from pattern vertices to bag vertices or up/down regions, yielding a 2^O(tw·log tw)·|V| running time that avoids the double-exponential dependency seen in related problems.

## Results

The paper establishes that the naive $2^{\mathcal{O}(|\mathcal{V}|)}$-time brute-force algorithm for VC-DIMENSION is asymptotically tight under ETH (Theorem 9). For the treewidth parameterization, the authors design a $2^{\mathcal{O}(\mathrm{tw}\cdot\log\mathrm{tw})}\cdot|V|$-time algorithm for GEN-VC-DIMENSION (Theorem 19), which is single-exponential in the treewidth and contrasts with double-exponential dependencies for closely related problems. An FPT 1-additive approximation algorithm running in $2^{\mathcal{O}(\Delta\log\Delta)}\cdot|\mathcal{H}|^{\mathcal{O}(1)}$ time is given for VC-DIMENSION parameterized by maximum degree $\Delta$ (Theorem 12). A matching ETH-based lower bound rules out any $2^{o(\mathrm{vcn}+k)}\cdot|V|^{\mathcal{O}(1)}$ algorithm for GRAPH-VC-DIMENSION (Theorem 9).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines; comparisons are against prior complexity-theoretic results. The treewidth algorithm ($2^{\mathcal{O}(\mathrm{tw}\cdot\log\mathrm{tw})}\cdot|V|$) improves upon the tower-of-exponentials dependency obtained via Courcelle's theorem (Flum & Grohe [38]) and avoids the double-exponential treewidth dependency seen in closely related problems such as locating-dominating set and test cover (Chakraborty et al. [14], Foucaud et al. [39]). The ETH lower bound for GRAPH-VC-DIMENSION ($2^{o(\mathrm{vcn}+k)}$) complements the quasi-polynomial-time upper bound of Papadimitriou and Yannakakis [68] and the inapproximability results of Manurangsi [58]. The 1-additive FPT approximation for maximum degree $\Delta$ extends the W[1]-hardness result of Orange et al. [30] for degeneracy, showing that maximum degree is a strictly more exploitable parameter. The paper also shows that hypertreewidth and transversal number do not yield FPT algorithms (Proposition 13), delineating the boundary of tractable structural parameters.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the parameterized complexity analysis of VC-dimension computation initiated by Downey, Evans, and Fellows (W[1]-completeness by solution size) and Orange et al. (W[1]-hardness by degeneracy, and the pattern-graph technique). The treewidth algorithm in Section 5 directly reuses the pattern-graph framework from Orange et al. [30] and their pre-processing technique, adapting it to a nice tree-decomposition setting. The paper also builds on Papadimitriou and Yannakakis' LogNP-completeness result and Manurangsi's inapproximability results to position its ETH-based lower bounds within the existing complexity landscape.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 2^{O(tw·log tw)}·|V| algorithm for GEN-VC-DIMENSION is notable because it achieves a single-exponential (with log factor) dependency on treewidth, in sharp contrast to closely related problems (e.g., locating-dominating set, test cover) that provably require double-exponential dependencies under ETH. The main open question identified is closing the gap between this upper bound and the 2^{o(vcn+k)}·|V|^{O(1)} ETH lower bound. Additionally, whether the 1-additive FPT approximation parameterized by maximum degree can be improved to an exact FPT algorithm remains unresolved, as do the implications for the circuit-based formulation of set systems.
