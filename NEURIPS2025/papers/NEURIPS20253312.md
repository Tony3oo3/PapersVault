## Problem

Existing multimodal alignment efforts focus on single-turn interactions or unimodal settings, leaving a critical gap for multi-turn, interleaved multimodal understanding and generation where user preferences evolve dynamically across turns. No dataset captures real human preferences in this setting, and it remains unclear how to model long-horizon, context-dependent preferences that shift as conversations progress through interleaved text and image exchanges.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253312/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline starts from 15,604 seed questions (text + image pairs) spanning 15+ vision-language domains, which are expanded via a tool-augmented agent workflow into 52.6k tree-structured multi-turn QA instances (depth ≥5, average 5.33 images per conversation, 41.92% with 5+ turns). Human annotators then produce 32,459 preference annotations decomposed into 9 dimensions—4 local (turn-level: image-text consistency, visual perceptual quality, contextual coherence, text quality) and 5 global (conversation-level: context awareness, helpfulness/completeness, crucial step recognition, global image-text consistency, style coherence)—with scores on a 0–3 scale, pairwise comparisons, and free-text rationales.

## Method

INTERMT constructs multi-turn interleaved QA data through a four-stage agent-based pipeline: seed questions are harvested and filtered, then iteratively expanded by a pool of strong MLLMs (Qwen2-VL, GPT-4o, Gemini) augmented with image generation (FLUX, Stable Diffusion), editing (Gemini-2.0-Flash), and web-retrieval tools, using a Socratic strategy to generate follow-ups and a dual-classifier verifier to route tool calls. For preference modeling, the paper investigates prefix preference and chain-based preference formulations under the Bradley-Terry framework, finding that fine-grained local (turn-level) preference modeling outperforms direct global (conversation-level) modeling, and that global-to-local preference transfer is consistently easier than the reverse. A multi-turn scaling law is identified: judge models trained on the first k turns improve in predicting turn k+1 as k grows, but generalization diminishes with longer evaluation horizons due to contextual drift and evolving user intent. INTERMT-BENCH evaluates 6 frontier MLLMs on Scoring Evaluation (Pearson correlation), Pair Comparison (accuracy), and Crucial Step Recognition, revealing that even o4-mini and Claude-3.7-Sonnet (thinking) fail to match human judgments on global dimensions while approaching human-level (4.38/5) on crucial step recognition.

## Results

On INTERMT-BENCH, all six advanced MLLMs show substantial gaps versus human annotators in multi-turn multimodal judgment. In Scoring Evaluation (Pearson correlation), the best local average is GPT-4o at 0.242, while the best global average is GPT-4.1(+reason) at 0.191—both far below human agreement levels. In Pair Comparison (accuracy), the best local average is o4-mini at 0.537 and the best global average is GPT-4.1 at 0.549, barely above the 0.5 random baseline. Notably, models achieve near-human performance (4.38/5) on Crucial Step Recognition, suggesting potential for long-term value alignment despite poor scoring and comparison abilities.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates six frontier MLLMs (GPT-4o, GPT-4.1, Gemini-2.0-Flash, Gemini-2.5-Pro, Claude-3.7-Sonnet thinking, o4-mini) as judges on INTERMT-BENCH. No model dominates across all tasks: GPT-4o leads in local scoring (0.242 Pearson) but trails in global scoring (0.153), while GPT-4.1(+reason) achieves the best global scoring (0.191) and o4-mini the best local pair comparison (0.537). For global pair comparison, GPT-4.1 (0.549) edges out Gemini-2.5-Pro (0.532) and Claude-thinking (0.526). Adding explicit reasoning chains (+reason) yields inconsistent gains—sometimes improving global scores (e.g., GPT-4.1: 0.122→0.191) but occasionally degrading local performance (e.g., o4-mini: 0.537→0.530). The overarching finding is that all models remain far from human-level judgment, with pair comparison accuracy hovering near the 0.5 random baseline, confirming that current MLLMs cannot reliably serve as automated judges for multi-turn multimodal preference alignment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RLHF/preference-alignment paradigm from single-turn, unimodal settings to multi-turn interleaved vision-language interactions, a combination absent from prior work. It builds conceptually on interleaved image-text corpora (MMC4, OBELICS, CoMM) and multi-turn dialogue benchmarks (MMDU, ConvBench, MMMT-IF), but departs from both by requiring simultaneous understanding *and* generation with per-turn and conversation-level human preference annotations. The preference modeling framework draws on the Bradley-Terry model and recent multi-turn RLHF work, but introduces a novel local/global decomposition of helpfulness into nine sub-dimensions and a tool-augmented agent workflow to synthesize realistic interleaved conversations that current MLLMs cannot produce natively.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

INTERMT fills a clear gap: no prior dataset captures real human preferences for multi-turn, interleaved multimodal understanding and generation, which is essential for building general-purpose AI assistants. The empirical finding that even frontier reasoning models (o4-mini) achieve only ~0.1–0.2 Pearson correlation with human judges on global dimensions—and that adding chain-of-thought reasoning does not consistently improve alignment—challenges the assumption that stronger reasoning translates to better value judgment. Open questions include extending the framework to video/audio modalities, developing alignment algorithms that exploit the local/global preference structure, and understanding whether the observed multi-turn scaling law for judge models holds across architectures and task domains.
