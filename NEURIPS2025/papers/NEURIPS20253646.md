## Problem

In overparameterized models, the training loss admits many interpolating minimizers, so defining the unlearning target as 'any loss minimizer over the retain set' is vacuous—the original model already satisfies this condition while encoding forgotten data. Moreover, interpolation causes loss gradients to vanish, rendering all prior gradient-based unlearning methods (influence functions, gradient ascent/descent) ineffective. The paper addresses both gaps by proposing a new unlearning definition (minimum-complexity interpolator) and a matching algorithmic framework.

## Input / Output

The method consumes the trained model parameters θ*, the forget set D_f, and—critically—only the model gradients ∇_θ f(θ*, x) evaluated at the original solution on retain set samples, avoiding the inverse Hessian access required by influence-function methods. In practice it operates on a subsample of the retain set per batch. The output is a single updated parameter vector θ* + Δ that interpolates the retain set with minimal complexity, requiring no access to the forget set samples during the unlearning update itself.

## Method

The framework defines the unlearning target as the minimum-complexity interpolator over D_r (a bilevel problem: minimize R(θ) subject to interpolating D_r), then relaxes the nonlinear interpolation constraint via a first-order Taylor expansion around θ*, yielding linear orthogonality constraints Δ ⊥ ∇f(θ*, x_i) for all retain samples. A drift regularizer R̂(Δ) is added to control approximation error, producing the tractable problem min_Δ R(θ*+Δ)+R̂(Δ) s.t. Δ ⊥ ∇f(θ*, x_i). The practical algorithm MinNorm-OG instantiates R and R̂ as scaled squared ℓ2 norms (giving a closed-form proximal update via QR decomposition) and alternates this orthogonal-gradient step with standard loss descent on D_r to maintain feasibility. Theoretical results prove exact recovery for linear models and L-layer linear networks, and for two-layer perceptrons show the solution uses at most n_r active neurons, improving the prior worst-case bound of n_r+1.

## Results

In the Representation Collapse experiment on CIFAR-10 (Table 2), MinNorm-OG achieves the highest color-label accuracy among all unlearning methods across every combination of retain-set access and epoch budget, reaching 93.2% at 10% retain / 15 epochs and 94.7% at 100% retain / 10 epochs, compared to the next-best unlearning method NGP at 81.5% and 92.4% respectively. In the Data Poisoning regression task (Table 1), MinNorm-OG achieves a median sup-norm distance of 0.63 at 1000 epochs, matching or beating full retraining (1.17) while all gradient-based baselines remain above 2.4. In the Multi-Class Label Erasure experiment on CIFAR-10 and Tiny ImageNet (Figure 2), MinNorm-OG's Pareto frontier most closely approaches the ground-truth unlearned model, outperforming all baselines in the retain-quality vs. forget-quality tradeoff.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MinNorm-OG consistently outperforms all gradient-based unlearning baselines across all three experimental settings. In the Representation Collapse task, MinNorm-OG beats NGP [8] by 11.7 points (93.2% vs 81.5%) at 10% retain / 15 epochs and by 2.3 points (94.7% vs 92.4%) at 100% retain / 10 epochs, while GD [14] and Ridge lag further behind at 78.2% and 78.2% / 63.4% respectively. GA [15] is the weakest baseline, diverging from the retain set in the Data Poisoning experiment (sup-norm distance of 1400 at 1000 epochs) and achieving only ~38–40% accuracy in Representation Collapse. NGD [16] and ℓ₁-Sparse [25] are also consistently outperformed, with NGD reaching only 46.9% and ℓ₁-Sparse 25.1% at 10% retain / 15 epochs. Among the more sophisticated baselines, Scrub [8] and SalUn [26] are competitive in some settings (e.g., SalUn reaches 76.3% at 10% retain / 15 epochs) but still trail MinNorm-OG by 16.9 points. In the Data Poisoning regression task, MinNorm-OG is the only method that matches or beats full retraining (0.63 vs 1.17 at 1000 epochs), while all other baselines remain at least 1.37 away from the retain-set trend.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs fundamentally from the entire line of loss-gradient-based unlearning methods (influence functions, gradient ascent/descent hybrids, NPO, SCRUB, SalUn) by proving they are vacuous under overparameterization (Theorem 1), since loss gradients vanish at interpolation points. Its key conceptual building block is orthogonal gradient descent from continual learning [19], repurposed here to constrain parameter perturbations to directions that preserve retain-set loss optimality. The paper also draws on the minimum-norm solution theory for overparameterized linear regression [22] to define the unlearning target as the simplest interpolator, and extends this to multi-layer linear networks and 2-layer perceptrons with novel regularizer constructions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Since virtually all modern deep networks operate in the overparameterized regime, the result that prior gradient-based unlearning methods produce only noise is a significant negative finding that reframes the problem. The proposed MinNorm-OG framework is practical (requiring only gradient access on the retain set, no Hessian or full retraining) and outperforms baselines across three experimental settings, but theoretical guarantees are limited to linear models, linear networks, and 2-layer perceptrons. Key open questions include extending guarantees to deep nonlinear networks, scaling to LLMs and large vision models, and whether the minimum-complexity interpolator definition remains the right target when multiple natural complexity measures compete.
