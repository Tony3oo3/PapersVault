## Problem

Chain-of-Thought reasoning in LLMs suffers from two fundamental issues: generated intermediate steps may be insufficient to support the final answer, or may contain redundant steps that are not truly necessary for correctness. Existing pruning and refinement methods rely on correlation-based metrics (attention weights, likelihood scores, ablation accuracy) that cannot reliably distinguish genuinely necessary or sufficient reasoning steps from merely prominent ones. No prior work has applied rigorous causal sufficiency and necessity conditions to formally evaluate and prune CoT reasoning paths.

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural-language question and an initial CoT trace (a sequence of intermediate reasoning steps generated by a base LLM) as input, and produces a pruned CoT trace containing only steps that are both causally sufficient and necessary, along with a per-step PNS score. The intervention pipeline requires a rollout model to generate semantically disjoint alternative continuations and a validation model to score logical coherence, making it a multi-model system where the base, rollout, and validation roles can be filled by the same or different LLMs depending on the variant (Direct, Prompt-Based, or External).

## Method

The paper introduces Pearl's Probability of Necessity and Sufficiency (PNS) into CoT reasoning at the step level, proving identifiability under exogeneity and monotonicity assumptions (or under perfect intervention without monotonicity). The core algorithm operates in two stages: first verifying chain-level sufficiency (PS = 1 if the full trace yields the correct answer), then for each step performing counterfactual intervention by replacing it with a corrupted alternative and rolling out k downstream continuations. PNS for each step is estimated via Monte-Carlo as 1 minus the average validation-model score over the k intervened chains, and steps scoring below a threshold α are pruned iteratively. The resulting causally minimal CoTs serve as high-quality exemplars for in-context learning or as training data for supervised fine-tuning, enabling models to internalize concise, causally faithful reasoning patterns without sacrificing accuracy.

## Results

The PNS-optimized CoT reduces token usage by 50–70% while maintaining or improving accuracy across all four benchmarks. On GSM-8k with DeepSeek-V3, Ours-ICL achieves 83.6% accuracy with 44.7 tokens (vs. 177.5 tokens for Standard CoT at 83.8%). On MATH-500, it reaches 99.9% accuracy (vs. 97.6% Standard) while cutting tokens by 67%. For SFT, Causal-CoT improves CommonsenseQA accuracy from 37.6% to 47.2% while halving reasoning steps (21.4 → 10.3), and boosts MATH-500 accuracy from 84.6% to 91.7% with 53% fewer steps on DeepScaleR-1.5B.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Standard CoT, Ours-ICL reduces tokens by 52–77% across datasets while matching or exceeding accuracy (e.g., 99.9% vs. 97.6% on MATH-500 with DeepSeek-V3). Compared to Fast-Solve, Ours-ICL achieves substantially higher accuracy on CommonsenseQA (99.9% vs. 95.1%) and MATH-500 (96.2% vs. 87.2%) with comparable token budgets. Against Reduction [19], Ours-ICL wins on accuracy for MATH-500 (96.2% vs. 91.6%) and CommonsenseQA (99.9% vs. 97.3%) while using fewer tokens. The most dramatic contrast is with CoD [67], which achieves the lowest token counts (19–31) but suffers severe accuracy drops (e.g., 55.6% on MATH-500 vs. 96.2% for Ours-ICL), confirming that aggressive pruning without causal guidance destroys reasoning quality. For SFT, Causal-CoT consistently outperforms Noncausal fine-tuning: on DeepScaleR-1.5B, it reaches 91.7% MATH-500 accuracy with 36.3 steps versus Noncausal's 82.8% with 52.4 steps.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Pearl's causal framework (Probability of Necessity and Sufficiency) from the model level to the individual reasoning-step level within Chain-of-Thought, building directly on Hüyük et al.'s counterfactual reasoning elicitation and Yang et al.'s invariant learning via PNS. It departs from the broader CoT optimization literature (self-consistency, Self-Refine, C3oT, CoT-Valve, SPIRIT, Chain-of-Draft) by replacing correlation-based pruning heuristics (attention weights, perplexity, ablation accuracy) with a formally identifiable causal criterion, providing a theoretically grounded alternative to existing compression methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a principled, model-agnostic framework for identifying which CoT steps are genuinely causally necessary, addressing the 'overthinking' problem that plagues modern reasoning models. Key open questions remain: the fixed pruning threshold α may be suboptimal across domains, the External rollout variant requires access to a stronger auxiliary model (limiting practicality), and PNS estimation incurs non-trivial computational overhead during curation. The finding that SFT on just 1,229 causally-filtered traces can outperform the original model suggests that training-distribution reshaping toward high-PNS evidence is a promising direction, but whether this scales to open-ended or multi-step agentic reasoning remains untested.
