## Problem

Diffusion model sampling requires many function evaluations (NFEs) to produce high-fidelity images, and while ODE solvers are more efficient than SDE solvers, they typically yield lower sample quality due to the absence of stochasticity. Momentum diffusion models were shown to achieve competitive results with few NFEs, but their training paradigm was unclear and they could not reuse pretrained diffusion models, limiting their practical adoption.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253911/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

TADA takes higher-dimensional Gaussian noise (N coupled variables of dimension d, default N=2) as its initial prior, along with the same conditioning signals as the underlying pretrained model (class labels for ImageNet, text prompts for SD3). The pretrained model's interface is unchanged: at each step it receives a single d-dimensional vector y_t (a reweighted linear combination of the N variables) and a time condition, and outputs a data prediction. The final output is a generated image in the same space (pixel or latent) as the pretrained model, at resolutions up to 512×512.

## Method

TADA establishes a training equivalence (Prop. 3.1) showing that the momentum diffusion model objective is identical to a conventional diffusion model objective when the network input is replaced by a reweighted linear combination y_t = r_t^T x_t of the N augmented variables, enabling direct reuse of any pretrained diffusion model without retraining. The sampling dynamics extend the AGM framework to an arbitrary N-variable chain of integrators, where the force term F_θ is computed as N!·(x_θ(y_t,t) − Σ x_t^(n)/(n!)(1−t)^n)/(1−t)^N, driving x_t^(0) from the prior to the data distribution. A key analytical result (Prop. 3.3) shows that the dynamics of y_t naturally contain a pseudo-noise term arising from inter-variable coupling, endowing the deterministic ODE system with SDE-like diversity properties. The method is orthogonal to solver choice, using standard exponential integrators with Adams-Bashforth multistep schemes, and the level of sample diversity can be controlled by scaling the standard deviation of the last variable in the initial covariance Σ_0 at no extra computational cost.

## Results

TADA achieves an FID of 2 on 512×512 ImageNet samples, which is up to 186% faster (in NFEs) than the current state-of-the-art solver (UniPC) for the same FID target. On ImageNet-64 with the EDM model, TADA consistently outperforms both UniPC and DPM-Solver++ across all tested NFE budgets (5–100 NFEs) using FID as the metric. On Stable Diffusion 3 (text-to-image), TADA outperforms UniPC and SA-Solver across all classifier-free guidance (CFG) strengths using HPSv2, with the largest gains at lower CFG values (e.g., CFG=1) and lower NFEs (e.g., 25 NFEs).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TADA is compared against three baselines: UniPC (a unified predictor-corrector ODE solver), DPM-Solver++ (a fast ODE solver with Adams-Bashforth multistep), and SA-Solver (a stochastic Adams SDE solver). On ImageNet-64 (EDM) and ImageNet-512 (EDM2), TADA outperforms both UniPC and DPM-Solver++ at every NFE budget tested, with the gap widening at lower NFEs (e.g., 5–20 NFEs) where conventional ODE solvers produce increasingly blurred outputs while TADA preserves detail. On SD3, TADA beats both UniPC and SA-Solver across all CFG strengths (1–7.5) using HPSv2, though the margin narrows as CFG increases, suggesting TADA's advantage is most pronounced in under-parameterized or low-guidance regimes. The paper notes that at very high model capacity and high CFG, differences among solvers become negligible, positioning TADA's strength in settings where model capacity is limited (e.g., video generation).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the momentum diffusion model framework (AGM [6], CLD [9]) by proving a training equivalence between momentum diffusion models and conventional diffusion models (Prop. 3.1), showing that the multi-variable neural network input reduces to a single weighted linear combination. This equivalence is the key that enables direct reuse of pretrained diffusion models in the momentum system without retraining, which prior momentum-based methods could not do. The resulting TADA sampling method is orthogonal to existing fast solvers (UniPC, DPM-Solver++, DEIS) since it modifies the ODE dynamics rather than the numerical integration scheme, and it generalizes the AGM two-variable formulation to an arbitrary N-variable augmented space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TADA offers a practical 186% speedup over the current SOTA solver for ImageNet-512 at FID 2, is training-free, and can be combined with any existing solver or CFG schedule, making it immediately deployable with pretrained models. The discovery that a deterministic ODE with higher-dimensional noise naturally produces SDE-like pseudo-noise provides a new mechanism for controlling sample diversity without stochastic sampling. However, the method still falls short of SDE results at very high NFEs (511), the performance gap narrows with increasing CFG strength, and the current use of a basic exponential integrator leaves room for further gains when combined with advanced solvers like UniPC.
