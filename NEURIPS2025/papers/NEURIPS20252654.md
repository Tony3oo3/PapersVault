## Problem

Extending fitted Q-evaluation (FQE) to the distributional off-policy evaluation (OPE) setting requires choosing a discrepancy function between probability distributions, yet no unified framework exists for this choice. Existing distributional FQE extensions rely on ad hoc discrepancies (powered Wasserstein, log-likelihood) without general guidelines, and many natural candidates (e.g., total variation distance) provably fail to yield contraction of the distributional Bellman operator. The paper asks: what principled criteria determine a valid discrepancy, and what theoretical guarantees follow?

## Input / Output

The method consumes an offline dataset of N tuples (s, a, r, s') collected under a behavior policy b, a stationary target policy π, and a parametric model class M_Θ of conditional distributions over R^d. The data is partitioned into T non-overlapping subsets, one per iteration, to ensure independent samples at each step. The output is the full conditional return distribution Υ_π(s,a) for every state-action pair, supporting multi-dimensional returns (d ≥ 1) — a capability incompatible with quantile-based baselines like QRDQN and IQN. In experiments, distributions are parameterized via Gaussian mixture models (M = 10, 100, or 200 components) over Atari frame observations or LQR state-action pairs.

## Method

The core contribution is a three-principle framework (P1–P3) for constructing theoretically grounded FDE methods: the evaluation metric must be contraction-inducing for the distributional Bellman operator, the population minimizer of the discrepancy must equal the Bellman backup, and the discrepancy must control the metric. The key theoretical result (Theorem 2.4) shows that any functional Bregman divergence automatically satisfies the minimizer property (P2), dramatically expanding valid choices beyond the two previously used. The algorithm iteratively solves an M-estimation problem: at step t, it minimizes the empirical functional Bregman divergence between the predicted distribution Υ_θ(s,a) and the distributional Bellman backup Ψ_π(r,s',Υ_{t-1}) over the t-th data partition. Convergence analysis yields near-minimax optimal rates in tabular settings (e.g., O_P(N^{-1/(2p)}) for Cramér FDE in W_{p,∞}-error) and, via expectation-extended metrics, the first non-tabular guarantees for a broad class of divergences including Cramér, Energy-MMD, PDF-L2, MMD-Matern, MMD-RBF, MMD-Coulomb, and KL.

## Results

The paper evaluates FDE methods (Cramér, Energy, PDF-L2, MMD-Matern, MMD-RBF, KL) on LQR and seven Atari games (Atlantis, Breakout, Enduro, KunfuMaster, Pong, Qbert, SpaceInvader) using W₁-inaccuracy as the primary metric. With N=10K samples and M=200 mixtures/quantiles, FDE methods (particularly KL and Energy) consistently reduce inaccuracy across iterations and outperform all baselines in most games on both mean inaccuracy and variance. On LQR, all five FDE variants outperform the FLE baseline. The non-functional Bregman divergence (TVD) shows no improvement through iterations, empirically validating the theoretical framework that functional Bregman divergences are necessary for contraction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares FDE methods against three baselines: FLE [Wu et al., 2023], QRDQN [Dabney et al., 2018], and IQN [Dabney et al., 2018]. On LQR, all five FDE variants (PDF-L2, RBF, Matern, Energy, KL) outperform FLE, with the paper noting that Cramér FDE (l₂²) achieves faster convergence rates than FLE across all Wasserstein degrees p≥1 and model complexities α∈(0,2). On Atari, FDE methods (particularly KL and Energy) outperform all three baselines in most of the seven games on both mean W₁-inaccuracy and variance, with the advantage being most pronounced at N=10K samples and M=200 mixtures. QRDQN and IQN, which rely on quantile-based modeling, are incompatible with multi-dimensional returns—a limitation the paper's FDE framework overcomes. The paper also includes TVD (total variation distance) as a negative control; it shows no improvement through iterations, empirically confirming that non-functional-Bregman divergences fail to induce contraction of the distributional Bellman operator.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Fitted Q-Evaluation (FQE) from expectation-based RL to the distributional off-policy evaluation setting, building on the distributional Bellman operator formalism of Bellemare et al. (2017) and the functional Bregman divergence framework of Ovcharov (2018). Two prior distributional FQE extensions exist—Ma et al. (2021) using powered Wasserstein-p metrics and Wu et al. (2023) using log-likelihood (KL)—but both are ad-hoc constructions without a general principle for discrepancy selection. The key departure here is a unified framework with three guiding principles (contraction-inducing metric, correct minimizer, and metric-divergence coupling) that identifies functional Bregman divergences as the natural class of valid discrepancies, and extends convergence guarantees to non-tabular (infinite state-action) settings where prior analyses were limited.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides the first systematic taxonomy of valid discrepancy functions for distributional FDE, giving practitioners a principled menu (Cramér, MMD variants, PDF-L2, KL, etc.) with explicit convergence rates rather than ad-hoc choices. It also enables multi-dimensional return distributions and unbounded supports, which are incompatible with widely used quantile-based methods (QRDQN, IQN). Open questions include whether functional Bregman divergence is necessary (only sufficiency is shown), whether the data-splitting assumption can be relaxed, and how to handle violations of the completeness assumption in practice. The framework also naturally extends to TD-based and gradient-based distributional RL algorithms via the unbiased gradient property.
