## Problem

Most label models in programmatic weak supervision are either probabilistic (e.g., Dawid-Skene, Data Programming) or adversarial (e.g., Balsubramani-Freund), but the strong-performing HyperLM constructs a polytope of plausible labelings and picks its 'center' without belonging to either camp and without any theoretical guarantees. This paper asks whether one can design a label model that shares HyperLM's unsupervised polytope-center strategy while also providing formal statistical guarantees such as consistency and convergence rates.

## Input / Output

BBF takes as input the one-hot (or abstention) predictions of W labeling functions on N datapoints, along with user-specified Dirichlet hyperparameters α ∈ R^K for class frequencies and Beta hyperparameters ρ_w ∈ R^2 for each LF's accuracy. No labeled data or datapoint features are required. The output is a single aggregated label distribution in Δ_K^N — specifically the mode of a modified posterior — which has the closed form of a softmax over a weighted majority vote parameterized by W+K real weights. In practice, computing the prediction requires holding O(WN) entries in memory and simultaneously optimizing NK variables via a convex solver (CVXPY/MOSEK).

## Method

BBF is a Bayesian generalization of the Balsubramani-Freund (BF) adversarial model: instead of point estimates for LF accuracies and class frequencies (which BF requires labeled data to construct), BBF places Dirichlet and Beta priors on these quantities. The generative process draws class frequencies from a Dirichlet, draws labels from an entropy-weighted distribution over labelings matching those frequencies, draws each LF's accuracy from a Beta distribution restricted to the feasible range induced by the labeling, and then draws that LF's predictions uniformly from all prediction vectors achieving the sampled accuracy. The key insight is that the sum of log-pdfs of the Dirichlet and Beta priors in the posterior acts as a continuous (log-barrier) approximation to the hard indicator M·1(z∈P) in BF, so BBF implicitly constructs a polytope of plausible labelings and selects a labeling in its interior — mirroring HyperLM's strategy. The prediction is the mode of the modified posterior (with the Beta normalization constant removed to ensure log-concavity), solved as a concave maximization. The paper proves that the posterior is log-concave, the solution belongs to the same exponential family as BF (a softmax of weighted majority vote), BBF is consistent (converges to the best KL-divergence approximator in that family when priors concentrate on empirical accuracies), and provides a convergence-rate bound in terms of how well the induced accuracies match the empirical ones.

## Results

BBF is evaluated on 11 real-world datasets using F1 (7 datasets) and 0-1 loss (4 datasets) in a fully unsupervised transductive setting. BBF matches or exceeds the best non-feature-based baselines on 8 out of 11 datasets, with headline scores including 89.71 F1 on YouTube, 84.21 0-1 on SemEval, and 85.02 F1 on Commercial. Against the theoretical ceiling g* (computed via the BF model with exact empirical accuracies), BBF's excess error ranges from 0.0 (SemEval) to 8.79 (YouTube), confirming consistency in practice. The best improvement over majority vote is 6.84 points (YouTube), with the smallest being 0.18 (SMS).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against FABLE (the strongest Bayesian method, which uses datapoint features via RoBERTa), BBF matches or outperforms on 10 of 11 datasets, only significantly underperforming on Tennis (79.87 vs. 83.59 F1) where three near-duplicate LFs reduce the effective constraint count. Compared to HyperLM (the closest strategic analogue, also feature-free), BBF is competitive on 9/11 datasets but trails on CDR (62.39 vs. 70.54, due to very low LF coverage averaging 2.3 predictions/datapoint) and Tennis (79.87 vs. 84.21). Against Denoise (a neural attention-based method using features), BBF is worse on IMDB (74.67 vs. 81.05), SMS (78.77 vs. 79.06), and Yelp (77.67 vs. 80.97), but substantially better on Tennis (79.87 vs. 51.12), TREC (59.88 vs. 49.81), and SemEval (84.21 vs. 73.53). BBF clearly dominates the classical probabilistic baselines (DS, DP, iBCC, EBCC) on most datasets, with iBCC/EBCC failing catastrophically (0.00 F1) on YouTube, SMS, and CDR. Against the adversarial/polytope-based CLL, BBF wins on 9/11 datasets, with CLL only winning on Commercial (36.38 vs. 85.02 is actually BBF winning; CLL wins on Tennis 42.31 vs. 79.87 is also BBF winning — CLL actually only wins on TREC 59.34 vs. 59.88, a near-tie). Overall, BBF's key advantage is that it requires no datapoint features and no labeled data, yet achieves performance within 1–3 points of feature-using methods on most datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

BBF is a direct Bayesian generalization of the adversarial Balsubramani-Freund (BF) model, replacing its point-estimate accuracy bounds with Dirichlet and Beta priors over class frequencies and LF accuracies. It also adopts HyperLM's strategy of selecting the 'center' of a polytope of plausible labelings, but does so through a convex optimization objective rather than a graph neural network. The generative process structure (labels first, then LF predictions parameterized by accuracy) follows iBCC, but with a key departure: rather than modeling each LF as a biased coin per datapoint, BBF draws all predictions simultaneously conditioned on a global accuracy, making it closer in spirit to the frequentist BF model than to Dawid-Skene-style independence assumptions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This paper provides the first theoretical guarantees (log-concavity, consistency, convergence rate bounds) for the polytope-center selection strategy that HyperLM uses empirically, bridging a gap between adversarial and probabilistic label models. However, practical adoption is limited by the need to hold O(WN) items in memory and optimize NK variables simultaneously, making it infeasible for large N. Open questions include whether the convergence rate can be tightened (the current bound depends on unknown quantities), whether calibration can be improved (BBF's predictions have small infinity-norm), and whether the loss of minimax optimality (compared to BF) can be recovered without labeled data.
