## Problem

Discrete diffusion models with absorbing rate matrices consistently outperform their uniform-rate counterparts in generation quality, yet no finite-time convergence guarantees existed for the absorbing case. Existing theoretical analyses relied on log-Sobolev inequalities or symmetric rate matrix assumptions that are inapplicable to the highly asymmetric absorbing rate matrix, leaving a fundamental gap between empirical success and theoretical understanding.

## Input / Output

The analysis operates on a d-dimensional token space [S]^d with a designated [MASK] state, where the forward process uses the absorbing rate matrix Q^tok = 1_S e_[MASK]^T − I_S that irreversibly transitions each non-mask token to [MASK] at rate 1. The output is a finite-time KL divergence bound between the generated and target distributions, together with the number of sampling steps required to achieve ε-accuracy: a deterministic count for τ-leaping and a Poisson-distributed count for uniformization. The paper is purely theoretical and provides no empirical evaluation.

## Method

The paper introduces a surrogate initialization distribution that replaces the ill-defined singleton stationary distribution with a smooth mixture, enabling well-defined KL divergence. For forward process convergence, it employs a Jensen-based decomposition of KL divergence into entropy and cross-entropy terms, bypassing the log-Sobolev inequality approach that requires symmetric rate matrices. The key technical novelty is a score upper bound restricted to transitions where Q(y,x)>0 (i.e., x^j=[MASK]), which yields a t^{-1} decay for large t rather than the exponential bound obtained by naively extending uniform-rate techniques. This improved score bound directly translates to linear-in-d convergence for τ-leaping (vs. quadratic under uniform rate) and log-log(d/ε) expected steps for uniformization (vs. log(d/ε)). Under an additional assumption that [MASK] has non-negligible probability in the data distribution, the paper further establishes convergence without early-stopping by proving a non-diverging score upper bound near t=0.

## Results

The paper establishes the first finite-time convergence guarantees for discrete diffusion models under the absorbing rate matrix. For the τ-leaping sampler with early stopping, ε-level KL-divergence accuracy is achieved in Õ(d/ε) steps, improving the quadratic d-dependency (Õ(d²/ε)) of the uniform-rate baseline [15] to linear. For the uniformization sampler, the expected number of steps improves from O(d log(d/ε) + d log δ⁻¹) under the uniform rate to O(d log log(d/ε) + d log δ⁻¹) under the absorbing rate. Without early stopping (under Assumption 4), the τ-leaping sampler requires Õ(dγ⁻¹/ε) steps and the uniformization sampler requires expected O(d(log log(d/ε) + γ⁻¹)) steps, with no prior guarantee existing for the uniform-rate case.

**Table 1: Comparison of convergence results in terms of number of steps.**

| Sampling Algorithm | Early-Stopping? | Uniform Rate [15] | Absorbing Rate (this paper) |
|---|---|---|---|
| τ-leaping | Yes | Õ(d²/ε) | Õ(d/ε) |
| τ-leaping | No | Õ(d²/ε) | Õ(dγ⁻¹/ε) |
| Uniformization | Yes | Pois(O(d log(d/ε) + d log δ⁻¹)) | Pois(O(d log log(d/ε) + d log δ⁻¹)) |
| Uniformization | No | N/A | Pois(O(d log log(d/ε) + dγ⁻¹)) |

Here d is the data dimension, δ is the early-stopping perturbation, ε is the target KL-divergence accuracy, γ is the minimum relative likelihood of the mask state in the data distribution (Assumption 4), and Pois(λ) denotes a Poisson random variable with mean λ. The sample space is [S]ᵈ.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two prior convergence analyses for discrete diffusion models under the uniform rate matrix. Against Ren et al. [15] (ICLR 2025), which provides the state-of-the-art τ-leaping bound of Õ(d²/ε) under a symmetric rate matrix assumption, this paper achieves Õ(d/ε) under the absorbing rate — a factor-of-d improvement in the dimension dependence. For the uniformization sampler, the expected step count improves from O(d log(d/ε) + d log δ⁻¹) [15] to O(d log log(d/ε) + d log δ⁻¹), and the paper further provides a no-early-stopping guarantee (Pois(O(d log log(d/ε) + dγ⁻¹))) that has no counterpart in [15]. Against Chen & Ying [14] (2024), which analyzes uniformization under the uniform rate requiring O(log ε⁻¹) expected steps, the absorbing rate reduces this to O(log log ε⁻¹). The improvement is attributed to the score function s_t(y,x) decaying as t⁻¹ for large t under the absorbing rate (vs. remaining asymptotically constant under the uniform rate), enabling a much lower uniformization intensity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the convergence analysis of discrete diffusion models from the uniform (or symmetric) rate matrix setting [14, 15, 16] to the absorbing rate matrix, which is highly asymmetric and has a singleton stationary distribution. The key methodological departure is replacing log-Sobolev inequality-based mixing arguments (which require symmetry) with a Jensen-based decomposition adapted from continuous diffusion analysis [26], combined with a novel surrogate initialization distribution to handle the ill-defined KL divergence at the absorbing state. The score function analysis builds on the exact expression derived from the CTMC structure but exploits the restricted support of Q(y,x)>0 under the absorbing kernel to obtain tighter t^{-1} decay bounds rather than the exponential-in-t bounds that arise from naive uniform-rate techniques.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This provides the first theoretical explanation for the well-documented empirical superiority of absorbing over uniform rate matrices in discrete diffusion, closing a gap that has persisted since the absorbing rate was introduced in D3PM [5]. The linear-in-d convergence rate (vs. quadratic for uniform) and the removal of early-stopping under a natural full-support assumption have direct practical implications for sampling efficiency and sample quality. Open questions include extending the analysis to conditional generation (where absorbing rates depend on the conditioning structure), to non-time-homogeneous or state-dependent rate schedules, and to understanding whether the score properties identified here can inform better training objectives for absorbing discrete diffusion models.
