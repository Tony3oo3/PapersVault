## Problem

Dense image correspondence has been tackled separately for optical flow (small displacements, dynamic scenes) and wide-baseline matching (large viewpoint changes, static scenes), with task-specific architectures that fail to generalize across domains. No prior unified model has demonstrated that joint training on both data types outperforms specialized models trained on in-domain data only, leaving a gap for a single general-purpose correspondence model.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253648/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

UFM takes a pair of RGB images at 560 or 980 longest-side resolution (aspect ratios 3:1 to 1:1) and directly regresses a dense pixel displacement map φ₁ ∈ ℝ^{2×H×W} at full pixel resolution, alongside a binary covisibility mask C₁ ∈ ℝ^{1×H×W} indicating which source pixels are visible in the target. Unlike prior coarse-to-fine methods that produce patch-level flow and progressively upsample, UFM outputs pixel-level flow in a single forward pass via DPT heads, and the covisibility mask is used both during training (to restrict supervision to co-visible regions) and at inference (to exclude unreliable predictions).

## Method

UFM encodes both images with a shared DINOv2 ViT-L encoder (fine-tuned at a small learning rate to preserve pre-trained features), concatenates the resulting patch tokens with view-index positional encodings, and processes them through 12 self-attention transformer layers (chosen over cross-attention for Flash-Attention efficiency). Two separate DPT heads decode intermediate transformer features (layers 6, 9, 12) to regress the flow and covisibility logits respectively. Training uses a unified dataset of 12 sources spanning both optical flow and wide-baseline matching, with supervision restricted to co-visible pixels via a generalized Charbonnier loss (α=0.5, c=0.24) for flow and upweighted BCE for the covisibility mask. An optional classification-based refinement module computes attention between source features and a 7×7 neighborhood centered on the regressed flow target, yielding sub-pixel corrections at 60× lower cost than MASt3R's global matching. The key empirical finding is that this simple end-to-end transformer architecture, trained jointly on both domains, achieves mutual improvement—outperforming its own specialized variants on both optical flow and wide-baseline benchmarks.

## Results

On the wide-baseline dense correspondence benchmark (Table 2), UFM 560 achieves an average EPE of 2.64 on ETH3D, 5.56 on DTU, and 12.87 on the new TA-WB benchmark, with 1-pixel outlier rates of 46.5%, 58.4%, and 53.5% respectively. UFM is 62% more accurate (lower EPE) and 6.7× faster than the best dense wide-baseline method RoMa (387.4 ms vs. 42.9 ms runtime), while matching the precision of the sparse method MASt3R at 60× lower inference cost. On optical flow (Table 4), UFM 980 achieves 1.28 EPE on Sintel-Final (covisible) and 2.05 EPE on KITTI, outperforming UniMatch (1.63 and 2.38) and SEA-RAFT (2.28 and 2.10) in zero-shot settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against RoMa (the best prior dense wide-baseline matcher), UFM 560 reduces EPE by 62% on average (e.g., 2.64 vs. 7.94 on ETH3D, 12.87 vs. 48.10 on TA-WB) while running 6.7× faster (42.9 ms vs. 387.4 ms). Against MASt3R (the strongest sparse matcher), UFM evaluated on MASt3R's own confident-pixel subset achieves comparable or better EPE (1.34 vs. 1.31 on ETH3D, 6.19 vs. 6.21 on TA-WB) at 60× lower runtime (41.0 ms vs. 2517.8 ms), and with refinement UFM slightly surpasses MASt3R on ETH3D (1.29 vs. 1.31 EPE). Against the best optical flow methods, UFM 980 outperforms UniMatch by 28% on Sintel-Final EPE (1.28 vs. 1.63 covisible) and KITTI (2.05 vs. 2.38), and outperforms SEA-RAFT by 44% on Sintel-Final (1.28 vs. 2.28). FlowFormer and SEA-RAFT, designed for small-displacement flow, degrade catastrophically on wide-baseline data (EPE > 74 on ETH3D), confirming that specialized flow architectures do not generalize to large viewpoint changes. The ablation in Table 5 further shows that unified training on both flow and wide-baseline data yields mutual improvement: adding wide-baseline data reduces optical flow EPE by 20–80%, and adding flow data reduces wide-baseline 1-px outlier rates by 3.2% relative.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from two established lineages: optical flow methods built on cost volumes and coarse-to-fine refinement (RAFT, SEA-RAFT, FlowFormer, UniMatch) and wide-baseline matchers built on patch-level matching with warp-refiners (DKM, RoMa) or 3D-grounded sparse matching (MASt3R). Rather than extending either paradigm, UFM replaces both with a single self-attention transformer over DINOv2 patch tokens that directly regresses pixel-level flow via DPT heads, drawing architectural inspiration from FlowFormer's global attention and MASt3R's end-to-end design while discarding their cost-volume or sparse-matching components. Prior unification attempts (GLUNet, RGM) failed to show that a generalist model outperforms domain-specialized ones; UFM is the first to demonstrate mutual benefit from joint training, and its covisibility-restricted supervision strategy distinguishes it from both the all-pixel supervision in optical flow and the confidence-mask training in wide-baseline matching.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

UFM provides a single, fast (42 ms vs. 387 ms for RoMa, 2518 ms for MASt3R) general-purpose correspondence model that outperforms specialized methods in both optical flow and wide-baseline matching, making it a practical building block for visual odometry, 3D reconstruction, and real-time matching pipelines. The TA-WB benchmark fills a gap in controlled, geometrically challenging wide-baseline evaluation. Key open questions remain: the model struggles with extreme semantic shifts (seasonal changes, cross-spectral matching) because fine-tuning DINOv2 erodes its semantic invariance, and the training data is biased toward static scenes, limiting dynamic-scene accuracy. Future work on preserving semantic features during training and incorporating more dynamic datasets could address these gaps.
