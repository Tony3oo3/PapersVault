## Problem

Pre-trained vision-language models struggle with 3D spatial reasoning from scanning videos due to spatial uncertainty (inferring 3D structure from limited 2D observations without explicit depth) and data scarcity (existing datasets are small, rely on expensive real-world scans, and lack scalability). This paper addresses both challenges without modifying VLM architecture, using a structured prompting strategy and a large-scale synthetic dataset.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253449/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes egocentric scanning videos (72 frames per scan, captured at ~1.5 m eye level via orbit or navigation trajectories in Unity) and a natural-language spatial question as input, producing a text answer. Questions span three categories—attribute estimation (object count, size, room type), spatial reasoning (relative/absolute distance, direction, contact), and hypothesis analysis (operation feasibility)—covering both open-set and closed-set scenarios. The scene representation fed to the VLM is a textual description of object positions relative to a reference point, which experiments show outperforms 3D coordinate maps and 2D grids for current VLMs.

## Method

The framework combines two complementary components. SpatialMind is a structured Chain-of-Thought prompting strategy with two sub-modules: Scene Decomposition (local per-frame object detection → accumulated inter-frame coordinate mapping → generation of a global 3D map, 2D grid, or natural-language position descriptions) and Question Decomposition (categorizing the question into a type and applying a type-specific multi-step reasoning plan, e.g., identify objects → estimate coordinates → compute pairwise distances → select minimum). ScanForgeQA is a 925K QA dataset built from 34K+ single-room 3D scenes (disassembled from 3D-FRONT and synthesized via HoloDeck), with egocentric scanning videos rendered in Unity and QA pairs auto-generated from scene annotations. Fine-tuning VLMs on ScanForgeQA equips them with spatial commonsense, and the paper shows that prompting and fine-tuning are complementary: larger models (72B) gain more from prompting, smaller models (7B) from fine-tuning, and combining both yields the largest gains across all evaluated models.

## Results

On VSI-Bench (the primary benchmark), the combined SpatialMind + ScanForgeQA approach achieves the best average score of 47.1 for Qwen2.5-VL-72B (vs. 39.2 baseline, +7.9%) and 43.9 for Qwen2.5-VL-7B (vs. 37.2, +6.7%). Fine-tuning on ScanForgeQA alone yields larger gains for smaller models (e.g., +6.1% for Qwen2.5-VL-7B) while prompting alone is more effective for larger models (e.g., +4.8% for Qwen2.5-VL-72B). On OpenEQA (EM-EQA), the combined method reaches 60.4% accuracy for Qwen2.5-VL-72B (vs. 53.8% baseline), and on SQA3D it achieves 46.3 EM-1 (vs. 34.8% baseline).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates SpatialMind and ScanForgeQA against six VLM families. For Qwen2.5-VL (the primary testbed), the combined method lifts 7B from 37.2→43.9 (+6.7%) and 72B from 39.2→47.1 (+7.9%) on VSI-Bench, with fine-tuning contributing more for the 7B model (+6.1%) and prompting more for the 72B (+4.8%). InternVL2 models show the largest relative gains: InternVL2-8B improves from 34.6→42.7 (+8.1%) and InternVL2-40B from 36.0→44.5 (+8.5%), suggesting weaker spatial priors in these architectures benefit most from the structured approach. Closed-source models also improve substantially with prompting alone: Gemini-1.5 Pro rises from 45.4→52.8 (+7.4%) and GPT-4o from 34.0→40.8 (+6.8%), though GPT-4o's Absolute Distance score remains low (27.1). LLaVA-NeXT-Video and VideoLLaMA3-7B show more modest gains (+1.3% and +2.3% respectively), indicating that models with weaker video understanding benefit less from the prompting strategy. Across all models, the combined prompting + fine-tuning consistently outperforms either component alone, confirming their complementary roles.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two lines of work: (1) vision-based 3D spatial understanding from scanning videos (GPT4Scene, Liao et al.), which avoids expensive point-cloud sensors, and (2) structured Chain-of-Thought prompting for VLMs. It departs from prior 3D spatial QA datasets (SPARTUN3D, MSQA, 3D-LLM) that rely on real-world point-cloud scans by constructing a fully synthetic pipeline from 3D-FRONT and HoloDeck-generated scenes rendered via Unity. The key conceptual departure is combining a decomposed prompting strategy (SpatialMind) with large-scale synthetic fine-tuning data (ScanForgeQA) as complementary, architecture-agnostic interventions, rather than proposing a new model architecture or a single fine-tuning recipe.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 925K-pair ScanForgeQA dataset and the SpatialMind prompting template are immediately reusable assets for anyone working on 3D spatial reasoning with VLMs, and the finding that plain textual position descriptions outperform 3D coordinate maps and 2D grids for current VLMs is a practical design insight that challenges the assumption that richer spatial representations are always better. Limitations include the single-room scope, the absence of error bars across runs, and the reliance on GPT-4o for scene decomposition at inference time, which introduces a dependency on a proprietary model. Open questions include whether the approach extends to multi-room or outdoor scenes, whether the textual-description preference persists as VLMs improve, and whether the synthetic-to-real gap limits deployment on genuinely scanned environments.
