## Problem

Autoregressive VLMs generate tokens strictly left-to-right, making inference inherently sequential and preventing effective handling of bidirectional tasks like text infilling and constrained structured output. While discrete diffusion models (DMs) offer parallel decoding and bidirectional context in language-only settings, their adaptation to multimodal vision-language tasks remained unexplored, with no existing VLM family built on diffusion backbones.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253511/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

LaViDa takes an RGB image (resized to 768², split into five 384² views encoded by SigLIP-400M, then 2×2 average-pooled to 980 visual tokens) and a text prompt as input. The output is a text sequence of fixed length L produced by iteratively unmasking tokens over K diffusion steps; for variable-length completions, special [S] and [FIM] tokens allow the model to terminate early. The number of discretization steps K (or equivalently NFE = K/L) is a free hyperparameter that trades generation speed against output quality.

## Method

LaViDa couples a SigLIP-400M vision encoder and MLP projector to a non-causal Transformer diffusion language model (LLaDA-8B or Dream-7B), trained in two stages: projector-only pretraining on 558K image-text pairs, then joint end-to-end fine-tuning on 1M instruction-following examples using the diffusion denoising objective. Three key techniques address practical challenges: (1) complementary masking generates two disjointly-masked copies of each answer so every token contributes to the loss, fixing the ~50% data waste of standard stochastic masking; (2) Prefix-DLM uses a specialized attention mask where visual and prompt tokens attend only to each other while answer tokens attend to all, enabling KV caching of the multimodal prefix for up to 3.9× inference speedup without sacrificing bidirectional context; (3) a convex timestep-shifting schedule (α=1/3, inspired by SD3) front-loads unmasking to preserve quality at low step counts. A further stage-3 fine-tune with [FIM] tokens enables variable-length text infilling, and a separate CoT distillation stage (LaViDa-Reason) boosts math reasoning by +18% on MathVision.

## Results

LaViDa-L (LLaDA-8B backbone) achieves 43.3 on MMMU, 44.8 on MathVista, and 80.2 on ScienceQA, outperforming same-scale AR baselines LLaVa-1.6-7B (35.1, 34.4, 73.2) and Open-LLaVA-Next-8B (37.4, 41.8, 78.3) on knowledge and reasoning benchmarks. On COCO captioning, LaViDa surpasses Open-LLaVA-Next-8B by +4.1 CIDEr (117.3 vs 111.8) with a 1.92× speedup using Prefix-DLM caching. On constrained poem completion, LaViDa achieves 100% constraint satisfaction versus 37–45% for AR baselines, a +59% relative improvement. However, LaViDa trails on OCR-heavy tasks (e.g., TextVQA 56.3 vs 64.9 for LLaVa-1.6; DocVQA 59.0 vs 74.4) due to 2×2 average pooling that discards fine-grained spatial detail.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LLaVa-1.6-7B (same 0.6M pretrain / 0.7M SFT data scale), LaViDa-L wins decisively on reasoning and knowledge tasks: MMMU +8.2 (43.3 vs 35.1), MathVista +10.4 (44.8 vs 34.4), MathVerse +12.9 (27.2 vs 14.3), MathVision +7.6 (20.4 vs 12.8), ScienceQA +7.0 (80.2 vs 73.2), and MMBench +15.9 (70.5 vs 54.6). Against Open-LLaVA-Next-8B (matched 0.6M/1.0M data), LaViDa-L leads on MMMU (+5.9), MathVista (+3.0), MathVerse (+12.6), MathVision (+6.3), and ScienceQA (+1.9), but trails on MME-P (1365.6 vs 1610.9), MMBench (70.5 vs 74.4), and all four OCR benchmarks (e.g., DocVQA 59.0 vs 69.9, ChartQA 64.6 vs 69.7). Against larger-data models (Qwen2.5-VL-7B with >7M pretrain images, InternVL3-8B with 21.7M SFT images), LaViDa is substantially behind on most benchmarks, confirming the data-scale gap. The paper's unique advantage over all AR baselines is controllability: on constrained poem completion, LaViDa achieves 100% sample-level constraint satisfaction versus 37% (LLaVa-1.6) and 16% (Qwen2.5-VL), and on COCO captioning it delivers +4.1 CIDEr over Open-LLaVA-Next-8B at 1.92× lower latency.

## Relation to Prior Work

LaViDa extends the LLaVA-style architecture (vision encoder + MLP projector + language model) by replacing the autoregressive language model with a discrete diffusion model backbone (LLaDA-8B or Dream-7B), making it the first family of VLMs built on DMs. It draws on SD3's timestep shifting for the sampling schedule and on prefix-LM ideas for the Prefix-DLM KV-cache mechanism, but introduces complementary masking as a novel training technique specifically to address the data-inefficiency of DMs on short-answer multimodal tasks. The work sits at the intersection of the discrete diffusion language model line (LLaDA, Dream, MDLM) and the vision-language model line (LLaVA, Qwen-VL, InternVL), bridging the two by showing that the practical challenges of adapting DMs to multimodal settings are solvable with targeted architectural and algorithmic modifications.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LaViDa demonstrates that diffusion-based VLMs can match AR VLMs on standard understanding benchmarks while offering capabilities that AR models fundamentally lack: a tunable speed-quality tradeoff via discretization steps, and native bidirectional text infilling for constrained generation (100% constraint satisfaction vs. <50% for AR baselines on poem completion). The complementary masking ablation (67% relative gain on ScienceQA) reveals that naive DM training is fundamentally misaligned for VQA where answers are short and semantically dense, a finding likely to inform future DM-for-VLM work. Limitations include OCR performance lagging behind AR models due to average pooling for token compression, and the overall compute budget being modest (0.6M pretraining images vs. 7M+ for frontier models), leaving open whether the speed-quality and controllability advantages persist at larger scale.
