## Problem

In model-based reinforcement learning, prediction errors of the learned dynamics model compound across imagined rollouts, degrading downstream control. While flat minima are known to improve generalization in supervised learning and sharpness-aware training has been applied to policy and value losses in RL, the world-model component in MBRL has received little attention through the lens of flat minima. This paper asks whether steering world-model optimization toward flatter minima yields more reliable rollouts and better policies.

## Input / Output

The world-model consumes state-action pairs (s, a) — either proprioceptive state vectors (e.g., 151-dim state and 61-dim action for HumanoidBench) or raw RGB frames (84×84×3 for Atari-100k) — and predicts next states and rewards. The downstream planner (MPC for TD-MPC2, actor-critic for TWISTER) then produces continuous or discrete actions respectively. SAM modifies only the dynamics-loss training objective; the planner architecture, policy heads, replay buffer, and optimization schedule remain unchanged from the base algorithm.

## Method

The paper integrates Sharpness-Aware Minimization (SAM) as a drop-in surrogate objective into the world-model's dynamics loss: instead of minimizing L_S(θ), the optimizer minimizes max_{||ε||₂≤ρ} L_S(θ+ε) + λ||θ||₂², approximated by a single gradient-ascent step followed by descent at the perturbed point (two forward-backward passes per update). Theoretically, the authors derive PAC-Bayesian bounds (Theorems 1 and 2) showing that the value-estimation gap |V^π − V̂^π| and the performance gap |V^{π*} − V^{π̂*}| are both upper-bounded by terms involving the first-order sharpness R_ρ^(1) of the true model-error landscape, so that flatter minima tighten both guarantees. Empirically, SAM is applied solely to the dynamics loss in TD-MPC2 (high-DoF DMC, HumanoidBench) and TWISTER (transformer world-model on Atari-100k), with a single radius ρ tuned per suite. Ablations confirm that SAM on the dynamics loss yields the largest gains, while applying it to reward/value heads is detrimental, and Hessian eigenvalue measurements verify that SAM-trained models occupy measurably flatter regions.

## Results

On HumanoidBench (11 tasks, 2M env. steps, 4 seeds), TD-MPC2+SAM improves mean return by +89.1% over TD-MPC2, with statistically significant gains on balance_hard (p=0.002), balance_simple (p=0.002), run (p=0.032), and pole (p=0.024). On high-DoF DMC (5 tasks), the average per-task relative gain is +20.6%, with the largest boost on humanoid_run (+60.8%, 484 vs 301). On Atari-100k (19 games, 100k steps, 5 seeds), TWISTER+SAM achieves a +27.6% mean human-normalized score improvement over TWISTER, with large absolute gains on Frostbite (+314.9%), Gopher (+97.3%), and Battle Zone (+87.6%). Measured sharpness (max Hessian eigenvalue) decreases consistently, e.g., from 141.6 to 99.5 on humanoid_run.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Dreamer v3 (the strongest prior model-based method in Table 1), TWISTER+SAM wins on 10 of 19 games, with the largest margins on Frostbite (1610 vs 909), Gopher (4099 vs 3730), and Hero (12320 vs 11161), but trails on Kangaroo (1555 vs 4098), Battle Zone (10230 vs 12250), and Up N Down (6857 vs 7600). The more direct comparison is TWISTER+SAM vs. TWISTER (same architecture, only SAM added to the world-model loss): TWISTER+SAM improves on 14 of 19 games, yielding the +27.6% mean human-normalized gain. On high-DoF DMC, TD-MPC2+SAM outperforms TD-MPC2 on all 5 tasks (e.g., humanoid_run 484 vs 301, dog_run 552 vs 428), confirming the gain is not modality-specific. On HumanoidBench, the overall Fisher's-method p-value is 0.0059, with four individually significant tasks, though several others (walk, stand, sit) trend positive without reaching α=0.05 at n=4 seeds.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Sharpness-Aware Minimization (Foret et al., ICLR 2021) from its well-studied role in supervised learning and its recent application to policy parameters in RL (Lee et al., 2023; Lee & Yoon, 2025) to a previously underexplored target: the dynamics/world-model loss in model-based RL. It builds directly on TD-MPC2 (Hansen et al., ICLR 2024) for continuous control and TWISTER (Burchi & Timofte, ICLR 2025) for pixel-based discrete control, modifying only the world-model training objective while leaving planners and policies untouched. The theoretical contribution connects first-order sharpness to value-estimation and policy-performance gaps via PAC-Bayesian bounds, complementing prior MBRL robustness approaches (conservative objectives, ensembles, Lipschitz constraints) with an optimization-level intervention rather than an architectural or objective-level one.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a simple, architecture-agnostic drop-in that practitioners can add to any MBRL pipeline with a single hyperparameter (ρ), and the ablation showing that SAM on the dynamics loss helps while SAM on reward/value heads is actively harmful (10±3 vs. 484±19 return) provides a clear mechanistic signal about where model robustness matters most. Open questions include whether the gains persist on real robots with latency and non-stationary dynamics, whether longer MPC horizons amplify or diminish the effect, and whether the observed flatness is truly causal or partly confounded with the extra gradient step. The limited seed count (n=4) and the fact that several HumanoidBench tasks do not reach α=0.05 significance temper the strength of the empirical claims, and the 1.7× wall-clock overhead, while modest, may matter in compute-constrained settings.
