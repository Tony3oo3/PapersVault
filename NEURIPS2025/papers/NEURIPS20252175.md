## Problem

Existing fair graph generation methods rely on autoregressive models that suffer from node-ordering sensitivity and address only structural bias while ignoring feature bias (e.g., systematic differences in generated node attributes across sensitive subgroups). No prior work provides theoretical fairness guarantees for one-shot graph generation that simultaneously mitigates both structural and feature biases.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252175/cropped_page6_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes random Gaussian noise and the fixed spectral decomposition of training graphs (eigenvector matrix U_0 and eigenvalue vectors) as input, operating in the low-rank eigenvalue space rather than the full adjacency matrix. It outputs a synthetic graph adjacency matrix and a node feature matrix, where features are first disentangled by a VAE into sensitive-related and sensitive-irrelevant components before being refined by the diffusion process. The generation is one-shot (no sequential node/edge ordering), making it permutation-invariant.

## Method

FairGEM builds on spectral diffusion (GSDM) and introduces two fairness regularizers integrated into the reverse-time SDE: a structural regularizer that penalizes the squared difference between intra-group and inter-group edge reconstruction errors, and a feature regularizer that applies MMD only to sensitive-irrelevant features to minimize cross-subgroup distributional gaps. A VAE with Hirschfeld-Gebelein-Rényi maximal correlation enforces adversarial disentanglement of node features into sensitive-related and sensitive-irrelevant latent spaces, ensuring that fairness constraints target only the appropriate dimensions. The theoretical analysis establishes upper bounds on structural bias propagation (Theorem 4.2) and on downstream task disparity (Theorem 4.3), showing that minimizing the proposed regularizers directly reduces group disparity in node classification. During reverse diffusion, the drift term is augmented with gradients of both fairness regularizers, so each denoising step simultaneously follows the learned score and corrects for bias.

## Results

On Cora, FairGEM achieves the best generation quality (DD=0.233, Clus=0.142, NFea=0.307) while simultaneously attaining the lowest fairness disparities (Fair-DD=0.020, Fair-Clus=0.035, Fair-NFea=0.019). On Pokec-z, FairGEM similarly leads in fairness (Fair-DD=0.040, Fair-Clus=0.023, Fair-NFea=0.017) with competitive quality (DD=0.357, Clus=0.161, NFea=0.250). For downstream node classification, FairGEM-generated graphs yield the lowest group disparity: ΔDP=11.71% and ΔEO=10.15% on Cora (a 21.2% reduction in ΔDP over the original graph's 27.01%), and ΔDP=9.23% and ΔEO=11.46% on Pokec-z, while maintaining competitive accuracy (79.75% on Cora, 75.25% on Pokec-z).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FairGEM outperforms all six baselines on every fairness metric (Fair-DD, Fair-Clus, Fair-NFea) across both Cora and Pokec-z, with the largest margins over autoregressive methods GRAPHARM (e.g., Fair-NFea 0.019 vs 0.049 on Cora) and GSDM (Fair-NFea 0.019 vs 0.051). Among fairness-specific baselines, FairGEM beats FairWire (Fair-DD 0.020 vs 0.031, Fair-NFea 0.019 vs 0.038 on Cora) and FairGen (Fair-DD 0.020 vs 0.027, Fair-NFea 0.019 vs 0.031 on Cora), while also surpassing FairAdj and FG²AN on both quality and fairness. On generation quality, FairGEM achieves the best DD and NFea on both datasets, though GSDM has a slightly lower Clus on Cora (0.128 vs 0.142) and Pokec-z (0.142 vs 0.161), indicating a minor trade-off in clustering coefficient fidelity. For downstream node classification, FairGEM-GCN achieves the lowest ΔDP (11.71% on Cora, 9.23% on Pokec-z) and ΔEO (10.15%, 11.46%) among all methods, outperforming the strongest fairness baseline FairGen-GCN by 2.45 and 2.88 percentage points in ΔDP respectively, while maintaining accuracy within ~1% of the original graph.

## Relation to Prior Work

FairGEM builds directly on the spectral diffusion framework of GSDM (Luo et al., TPAMI 2023) and the score-based SDE formulation of Jo et al. (ICML 2022), extending them with fairness-aware regularizers in the reverse-time SDE. It departs from the dominant autoregressive paradigm in fair graph generation (FairGen, FG2AN, FairWire, FAIRLP) by adopting a one-shot, permutation-invariant approach that avoids ordering sensitivity. The feature-bias component draws on disentanglement techniques (HGR maximal correlation) and MMD-based distributional fairness from the representation learning literature, applying them for the first time within a graph generative model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to provide theoretical upper bounds on both structural and feature bias propagation in one-shot graph generation, filling a gap where prior fair graph generation methods lacked formal guarantees. The finding that indiscriminately applying fairness constraints to all features (FairGEM-WD) degrades generation quality more than omitting feature fairness entirely underscores the importance of the disentanglement step. Limitations include the restriction to binary sensitive attributes, evaluation on only four datasets (two of which are citation networks with 'topic' as the sensitive attribute rather than a true demographic), and theoretical bounds that depend on constants not tightly characterized. Open questions include extending to multi-attribute and intersectional fairness, scaling to graphs with millions of nodes, and whether the VAE-based disentanglement generalizes to continuous or ordinal sensitive attributes.
