## Problem

Predictive coding (PC) networks, a biologically plausible local learning algorithm, have been unable to train reliably at large depths (100+ layers), preventing them from competing with backpropagation in modern deep learning settings. The paper identifies two specific pathologies in standard PCNs that make large-scale training practically impossible: the inference landscape becomes increasingly ill-conditioned with model size and training time, and the forward pass of activities vanishes or explodes with depth.

## Input / Output

The method takes grayscale images (MNIST, Fashion-MNIST at 28×28) or color images (CIFAR-10 at 32×32) as input and produces class labels as output. Training uses fully connected residual networks with widths up to 1024 and depths up to 128 hidden layers, with no biases. A key operational detail is the two-phase training loop: at each step, the activities are first minimised via gradient descent (using as many inference steps as hidden layers) before the weights are updated, requiring two separate learning rates (one for weights, one for activities).

## Method

μPC reparameterises the standard PC energy by applying Depth-μP scalings: the input layer premultiplier is N₀^{-1/2}, hidden layers use (N_{ℓ-1}L)^{-1/2}, the output layer uses N_{L-1}^{-1}, and all weights are initialised from a standard Gaussian (variance 1) rather than the standard 1/N_{ℓ-1} scaling. This ensures stable forward passes at initialisation (Desideratum 1) but does not resolve the growing ill-conditioning of the activity Hessian with depth. Despite this unresolved pathology, μPC enables stable training of 128-layer ResNets with competitive accuracy (~98% on MNIST in 5 epochs) and, strikingly, zero-shot transfer of both weight and activity learning rates across widths and depths. The paper also proves (Theorem 1) that in the limit N≫L the μPC energy converges to the MSE loss, making PC equivalent to BP, but shows empirically that μPC trains successfully far from this regime.

## Results

μPC enables stable training of 128-layer fully connected ResNets on MNIST, Fashion-MNIST, and CIFAR-10, achieving ~98% test accuracy on MNIST within 5 epochs (5× faster than the prior PC benchmark) and ~89% on Fashion-MNIST in fewer than 15 epochs. On CIFAR-10, μPC remains trainable at 128 layers though accuracy lags behind BP due to the use of fully connected (rather than convolutional) architectures. A key secondary result is zero-shot transfer of both weight and activity learning rates across widths (N = 64–1024) and depths (H = 8–128), meaning hyperparameters tuned on a small network transfer directly to much larger ones without re-tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the standard PC benchmark of Pinchetti et al. [48], μPC wins decisively: standard PC cannot reliably train beyond ~8 layers due to vanishing/exploding forward passes and increasingly ill-conditioned inference landscapes, whereas μPC trains 128-layer ResNets stably with only two hyperparameters (weight and activity learning rates) and no momentum, weight decay, or nudging. Against BP with Depth-μP [72] (the strongest non-local baseline), μPC achieves competitive accuracy on MNIST (~98%) and Fashion-MNIST (~89%) within a few epochs, though it slightly trails BP on CIFAR-10, likely because the experiments use fully connected rather than convolutional layers. The practical advantage of μPC over BP is that it is a local algorithm requiring no weight symmetry or backward pass, and it inherits zero-shot hyperparameter transfer across both width and depth—a property that BP with Depth-μP also enjoys but that standard PC lacks entirely.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the Depth-μP parameterisation (originally derived for backpropagation in residual networks) to predictive coding networks, creating 'μPC'. It builds directly on the PC energy formulation of Buckley et al. and the benchmarking framework of Pinchetti et al., which explicitly posed the challenge of scaling PCNs beyond shallow depths. The closest prior work is Ishikawa et al.'s width-only μP for PC, which achieved hyperparameter transfer across widths but not depths and focused on regimes where PC approximates BP. The key departure is showing that Depth-μP scalings, despite being derived for BP, are sufficient to stabilise PC training at 100+ layers even though they do not resolve the ill-conditioning of the inference landscape.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that a local learning algorithm can reliably train 100+ layer networks, addressing a long-standing challenge in the predictive coding community. The zero-shot transfer of both weight and activity learning rates across widths and depths is practically valuable, since PC requires tuning two separate learning rates. However, the scope is limited to fully connected ResNets on simple datasets (MNIST, Fashion-MNIST, CIFAR10), and the method does not derive PC-specific scalings or solve the inference ill-conditioning that grows with training. Open questions include whether μPC extends to convolutional or transformer architectures, whether principled PC-specific scalings can be derived, and whether the analysis of inference landscape conditioning generalises to other energy-based algorithms like equilibrium propagation.
