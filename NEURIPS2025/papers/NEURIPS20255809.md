## Problem

In semi-supervised node classification with extremely few labeled nodes (3-10 per class), GNNs suffer from degree bias: low-degree nodes have severely limited information propagation capacity, causing misclassification when labeled supervision is scarce. Existing degree-bias mitigation methods (DegFairGT, DAHGN, GraphPatcher) either lack adaptability to dynamic topologies, cannot handle pseudo-label reliability in complex settings, or operate only at test time without fundamentally improving training-time information aggregation for low-degree nodes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255809/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes an undirected graph with an adjacency matrix A in {0,1}^{n x n}, a node feature matrix X in R^{n x d}, and a small set of labeled nodes (3, 5, or 10 per class in the under-shot setting). It outputs predicted class labels for all nodes and, as a byproduct, a dynamically enhanced adjacency matrix A_A that prunes edges of low-degree nodes and adds KNN-based edges derived from higher-order decoupled-GCN features. The graph structure is re-optimized from the original A at every iteration to avoid cumulative drift.

## Method

L2DGCN is a teacher-student self-training framework. The teacher performs improved soft pseudo-label propagation (a modified label propagation that does not reset labeled-node labels each iteration, enabling long-range label diffusion via repeated multiplication by the symmetric normalized adjacency matrix). The student uses a decoupled GCN backbone (APPNP-style propagation with a beta-mixing term) to obtain higher-order node features, then dynamically restructures the graph by (1) pruning edges of the lowest-degree nodes (keeping edges only among the top-M highest-degree nodes) and (2) adding KNN edges for remaining low-degree nodes based on cosine similarity of the higher-order features. A confidence-threshold label selector filters unreliable pseudo-labels, and training combines cross-entropy on true labels plus selected pseudo-labels with an InfoNCE contrastive loss that pulls node embeddings toward their class prototypes (computed from labeled plus reliable pseudo-labeled nodes) and away from other class prototypes.

## Results

Under the low-labeling balanced setting (3/5/10 labeled nodes per class), L2DGCN achieves 78.50% accuracy on Cora (3-shot), 70.48% on Citeseer (5-shot), and 80.88% on Pubmed (10-shot), outperforming the strongest baselines by up to 8.28 percentage points. On the standard 20-labeled-per-class split, L2DGCN reaches 83.93% on Cora, 71.79% on Citeseer, and 80.02% on Pubmed, gaining up to 2.39% over the best baseline. The method also leads on larger datasets: 91.45% on Coauthor-CS (3-shot) and 94.65% on Coauthor-Physics (10-shot).

## Baselines & Comparisons

L2DGCN outperforms all 13 baselines on Cora across every labeling rate, with the largest margin of +6.62% over AGST in the 3-shot setting (78.50% vs. 71.00%). On Citeseer, the gains are even more pronounced: +8.28% over CGPN in the 5-shot setting (70.48% vs. 62.20%) and +6.45% in the 3-shot setting. On Pubmed, L2DGCN slightly trails AGST in the 3-shot (74.77% vs. 76.66%) and 5-shot (78.07% vs. 78.26%) settings but surpasses all methods in the 10-shot setting by +4.41% (80.88% vs. PTA's 76.47%). Classical GCN variants (GCN, GAT, SGC) and label-efficient methods (GLP, IGCN) consistently underperform, confirming that the degree-bias mitigation and pseudo-label selection mechanisms provide the primary gains. The closest overall competitor is AGST, which also leverages self-training but lacks the dynamic graph enhancement and confidence-based label filtering that distinguish L2DGCN.

## Relation to Prior Work

L2DGCN builds directly on the decoupled GCN / APPNP propagation framework (Dong et al., WWW 2021) as its feature backbone and on the classical label propagation algorithm (Zhou et al., NeurIPS 2004), which it modifies to support iterative soft pseudo-label diffusion without resetting labeled nodes. It positions itself against a cluster of degree-bias mitigation methods (DegFairGT, DAHGN, GraphPatcher) and low-label semi-supervised GNNs (AGST, M3S, CGPN, GraphHop), arguing that prior work either addresses degree bias in fully supervised or test-time settings, or handles sparse labels without explicitly repairing the topology of low-degree nodes. The paper's distinctive combination—degree-based edge pruning plus KNN edge injection driven by decoupled-GCN features, wrapped in a teacher-student self-training loop with confidence-gated pseudo-label selection and an InfoNCE contrastive term—has no single direct predecessor, though each component draws from established literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work offers a practical recipe for the increasingly common regime where only 3–10 labeled nodes per class are available and the graph exhibits strong degree heterogeneity, a setting where standard GCNs and even recent self-training GNNs degrade noticeably. The ablation revealing that the contrastive loss contributes over 20 percentage points in 3-shot scenarios underscores that pseudo-label reliability, not just structural repair, is the binding constraint in extreme low-label regimes. A key limitation acknowledged by the authors is that graph augmentation relies solely on feature similarity, which may introduce spurious edges in low-homogeneity graphs; extending the enhancement to incorporate structural or multi-view signals remains an open direction. The method is also confined to homogeneous graphs, leaving heterogeneous and dynamic-graph settings unaddressed.
