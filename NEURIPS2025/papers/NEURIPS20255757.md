## Problem

Prior dynamic-to-static regret reductions for online convex optimization are valid only for linear losses and are inherently tied to finite-dimensional feature spaces, preventing horizon-independent algorithms with optimal √P_T path-length dependence. This paper asks whether dynamic regret can be reduced to static regret in a way that (1) works for arbitrary loss sequences (preserving curvature) and (2) leverages infinite-dimensional function spaces to achieve optimal guarantees without prior knowledge of the horizon.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255757/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The algorithm receives a stream of convex loss functions ℓ_t (or their subgradients g_t) over a convex set W ⊆ R^d and outputs a prediction w_t each round. Although the algorithm is stated as operating in the infinite-dimensional Hilbert space L(H, W) of linear operators, the kernel trick ensures that only kernel evaluations k(s,t) and subgradients g_t are needed in practice—never the (potentially infinite-dimensional) feature map φ(t). The regret bound adapts to the comparator path-length P_T = Σ‖u_t − u_{t−1}‖ and the maximum comparator norm M = max_t ‖u_t‖, requiring no a priori knowledge of T or the Lipschitz constant.

## Method

The key insight is to reinterpret the comparator sequence u_1,…,u_T as evaluations of a single function u(·) at discrete time points, transforming dynamic regret into static regret over a Reproducing Kernel Hilbert Space (RKHS). The critical technical contribution is designing a translation-invariant kernel with spectral density Q(ω) ≈ 1/|ω| (regularized to be integrable) so that the RKHS norm ‖u‖_H² scales as O(M² + MP_T) while the kernel diagonal k(t,t) remains bounded, yielding the optimal Õ(√((M²+MP_T)·Σ‖g_t‖²)) dynamic regret via a parameter-free FTRL algorithm. Because the reduction preserves the curvature of the original losses, it also enables second-order methods (Kernelized ONS) to achieve O(λ‖u‖_H² + d_eff(λ)·ln T) bounds for exp-concave and strongly-convex losses, where d_eff(λ) is the effective dimension of the kernel matrix. The resulting kernel is horizon-independent, making this the first dynamic regret algorithm achieving optimal √P_T dependence natively without a doubling trick.

## Results

The paper establishes that dynamic regret in online convex optimization reduces to static regret in a Reproducing Kernel Hilbert Space (RKHS). For linear losses, the key guarantee (Theorem 5) is Õ(√((M² + MP_T) Σ_t ||g_t||²_W)), which is the first horizon-independent algorithm achieving the optimal √P_T path-length dependence without a doubling trick. For exp-concave losses (Theorem 6), the bound is O(λ||U||²_HS + d_eff(λ/βG²k_max) · ln(2βG²k_max T)/β), recovering the familiar λ||u||²_H + d_eff(λ) ln T structure. The framework also yields new scale-free guarantees (Propositions 7–8) and directionally-adaptive bounds (Proposition 5) that decouple comparator variability from per-round directional variance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's reduction subsumes and extends the finite-dimensional dynamic-to-static reduction of Jacobsen and Orabona [26], which is recovered as a special case using the Dirac kernel but is restricted to linear losses and inherently finite-dimensional features. Compared to the signal-processing approach of Zhang et al. [55], which also achieves Õ(√(MP_T T)) but requires prior knowledge of the horizon T, the present kernelized reduction is natively horizon-independent. Unlike OGD [59] (O(P_T √T)) and Yang et al. [52] (O(√(DP_T T)) with known P_T), the proposed method achieves the optimal √P_T rate without prior knowledge of P_T or T. A key distinction from all prior dynamic-to-static reductions is that the regret equality holds for arbitrary convex losses, not just linear ones, enabling curvature-aware guarantees (e.g., O(λ||u||²_H + d_eff(λ) ln T) for exp-concave and strongly-convex losses) that were previously inaccessible via the finite-dimensional route. The paper also introduces the first scale-free dynamic regret guarantees with optimal √P_T dependence, avoiding the doubling strategies that prior methods require to handle unknown Lipschitz constants.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the dynamic-to-static regret reduction initiated by Zhang et al. [55] (signal processing / sparse coding) and Jacobsen and Orabona [26] (high-dimensional static regret with dual-norm pairs) by recasting the comparator sequence as a function in a Reproducing Kernel Hilbert Space. The authors show that [26] is recovered as the special case of the Dirac kernel, while their framework naturally supports infinite-dimensional features and, crucially, holds for arbitrary loss sequences rather than only linear losses. The resulting algorithms draw on parameter-free online learning (Jacobsen and Cutkosky [23]), Kernelized ONS (Calandriello et al. [8]), and the Vovk-Azoury-Warmuth forecaster (Jézéquel et al. [27]), with scale-free extensions via gradient clipping (Cutkosky [12]).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The reduction yields the first horizon-independent dynamic regret algorithm achieving the optimal √P_T dependence without a doubling trick, along with the first scale-free and directionally-adaptive dynamic regret guarantees with optimal path-length scaling. Extending to curved losses (exp-concave, strongly-convex, regression) via the O(‖u‖²_H + d_eff(λ) ln T) template is a significant generality gain over prior linear-loss-only reductions. Key open questions include achieving the minimax-optimal T^{1/3}C_T^{2/3} rate for 1D squared loss with unsquared path-length (requiring a kernel with a specific effective-dimension trade-off), and making the O(t)-per-round kernelized updates practical via Nystrom or sparse-dictionary approximations without degrading regret bounds.
