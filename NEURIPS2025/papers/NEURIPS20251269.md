## Problem

Computing and optimizing opinion-based quantities (polarization, disagreement, controversy, etc.) in the Friedkin-Johnsen model has traditionally required full-graph traversal with linear or near-linear time complexity. Existing sampling-based methods either perform random walks from every node or rely on expensive Laplacian solvers, and no unified sublinear-time framework existed that handled both estimation and optimization tasks simultaneously.

## Input / Output

The method takes an undirected graph G=(V,E), an internal opinion vector s∈[0,1]^n, and for optimization problems either a weight vector c∈[0,1]^n with budget k (OpMin) or a candidate edge set E_C with budget k (PDMin). Rather than processing all n nodes, it operates on a randomly sampled subset S of p≪n nodes and l partial rooted forests, producing unbiased estimates of five opinion-based quantities (D, P, I, C, DC) within absolute error nε with probability 1−δ, or the optimal k-node/k-edge set with error kε. The key constraint is that p and l scale as O(ε^{-2} log(1/δ)), independent of n.

## Method

The core contribution is Partial Rooted Forest Sampling (PFS), which replaces full spanning rooted forest generation with a local procedure: for each of p sampled nodes, an absorbing random walk is initiated (stopping with probability 1/(1+d_k) at each node k), and the resulting trajectory is loop-erased to form a simple path added to a growing partial forest. The walk terminates early upon reaching any node already in the partial forest, avoiding redundant traversal. The probabilistic interpretation of the forest matrix Ω via absorbing random walks ensures that the root assignment r_φ(i) in a partial forest yields an unbiased estimator of z_i, and Hoeffding's inequality provides concentration guarantees. This reduces time complexity from O(ln) or Õ(m) to O(rpl), where r is a graph-structure-dependent ratio (empirically <15 on real networks) and p≪n, achieving sublinear scaling. The same PFS mechanism is adapted for both optimization problems: for OpMin, it estimates the influence weights γ_j = (1/n)Σ_i c_i ω_ij; for PDMin, it estimates the marginal gain (z_u − z_v)²/(1+r_uv) for each candidate edge.

## Results

For opinion-based quantity estimation (QE), PF-QE achieves relative errors below 1.5% for expressed opinions and below 4% for polarization, internal conflict, controversy, and disagreement-controversy, while running ~5× faster than LazyWalk on the 41.6M-node Twitter network (32.3s vs. 160.6s). For the Opinion Minimization problem, PF-OPMIN achieves comparable or slightly better opinion decline than FAST (e.g., −6.56 vs. −6.44 on LiveJournal) while being ~37× faster (8.1s vs. 296.8s). For Polarization-Disagreement Minimization, PF-PDMIN achieves greater P-D index reduction than FASTGREEDY (e.g., −3.18 vs. −3.17 on Pokec) while being >35× faster (494s vs. 18,656s), and remains scalable to networks where FASTGREEDY fails entirely.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LazyWalk [39] (sublinear-time opinion estimation via random walks), PF-QE consistently achieves lower relative error and shorter running time across all six networks; on Twitter, PF-QE is ~5× faster (32.3s vs. 160.6s) while maintaining comparable accuracy. Against LapSolver [52] (near-linear-time Laplacian solver used as ground truth), PF-QE is substantially faster on all networks (e.g., 9.9s vs. 291.9s on Orkut) and is the only method that can process the 41.6M-node Twitter network within practical time and memory limits. Against FAST [44] (full-graph forest sampling for OpMin), PF-OPMIN achieves a 5–37× speedup (e.g., 8.1s vs. 296.8s on LiveJournal) while matching or slightly exceeding the opinion decline (θ = −6.56 vs. −6.44). Against FASTGREEDY [59] (JL-lemma + Laplacian solver for PDMin), PF-PDMIN delivers a 5–37× speedup (e.g., 494s vs. 18,656s on Pokec) with equal or better P-D index reduction, and remains functional on Orkut, LiveJournal, and Twitter where FASTGREEDY fails due to memory and time constraints.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the forest-sampling framework for the Friedkin-Johnsen model introduced by Sun & Zhang (AAAI 2023) and the sublinear-time opinion estimation of Neumann et al. (WWW 2024) by replacing full-graph rooted spanning forests with a local 'partial rooted forest' constructed via loop-erased absorbing random walks. It also builds on the polarization-disagreement minimization formulation of Zhu et al. (NeurIPS 2021), which relied on the Johnson-Lindenstrauss lemma and a fast Laplacian solver. The key conceptual departure is that partial rooted forests only traverse the neighborhood of a sampled node set S rather than the entire graph, unifying both quantity estimation and optimization under a single sublinear-time sampling framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for large-scale social network analysis: the method handles a 41-million-node Twitter network where the Laplacian-solver baseline (LapSolver) simply fails due to memory and time constraints, and achieves 35–37× speedups over prior sampling methods on mid-scale networks. The empirical observation that the ratio r (expected partial-forest size / |S|) stays well below the theoretical bound d̄ across all six real-world networks (r = 2.4–13.6) suggests the sublinear guarantee is not merely asymptotic but practically tight. A stated limitation is that the framework does not yet handle optimization objectives with nonlinear terms (e.g., squared expressed opinions in marginal gains), leaving a gap for broader opinion-optimization problems.
