## Problem

Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) equipped with Multimodal Retrieval Augmented Generation (MRAG) frequently fail to fully exploit retrieved contextual knowledge for knowledge-intensive VQA. The paper identifies two root causes: an attention bias that over-allocates attention to image tokens while distributing it uniformly across context tokens, and knowledge conflicts where MLLMs over-rely on parametric knowledge even when accurate contextual knowledge is available, leading to up to 30% performance degradation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251245/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates at inference time on a query image, a text query, and a single retrieved Wikipedia passage (selected via CLIP-ViT-L/14-336 image-embedding similarity). It requires no additional training or external tools beyond the standard MRAG pipeline. The output is a standard autoregressive text response, but internally the model performs two forward passes per decoding step—one without context (parametric branch) and one with context after attention modification (contextual branch)—to produce the fused output distribution.

## Method

ALFAR is a training-free, plug-and-play inference-time intervention with two components. First, Attention Reallocation modifies the attention matrix at each decoding step: it scales down attention to image tokens by a factor (1−β) where β=k·α (α being the retrieval similarity), and scales up attention to each context token j by (1+γ_j) where γ_j=k·ω_j (ω_j being a query-context relevance score derived from the attention that context token j assigns to query tokens). Second, Adaptive Logits Fusion decouples parametric knowledge (logits from a forward pass with only query and image) from contextual knowledge (logits from a forward pass with query, image, and context after attention reallocation), then fuses them at the output logits using adaptive weights computed from the ratio of total attention allocated to image tokens versus context tokens at each decoding step, enabling the model to dynamically balance the two knowledge sources based on their relative reliability.

## Results

On the multi-choice InfoSeek and ViQuAE benchmarks, ALFAR achieves 58.35% and 55.91% VQA accuracy with LLaVA-1.5, improving over Regular MRAG by 6.4% and 2.6% respectively, and surpassing the best prior method (VCD: 53.87%/55.13%) by 4.5% and 0.8%. Across all six MLLMs, ALFAR yields an average 6.6% gain over Regular decoding on multi-choice tasks. On knowledge-based datasets (OK-VQA, AOK-VQA, E-VQA), ALFAR reaches 60.83%, 59.93%, and 29.57% with LLaVA-1.5, a 15.2% average improvement over Regular and 3.0% over the strongest baseline (VCD). Inference overhead is modest at 1.35×–1.46× the baseline, comparable to other contrastive decoding methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ALFAR consistently outperforms all eight baselines (CD, CAD, AdaCAD, Entropy, COIECD, VCD, AGLA, and Regular MRAG) across every MLLM and dataset tested. The largest margins appear on weaker models: InstructBLIP gains +8.15% over the best prior method (Entropy) on InfoSeek, and MiniGPT-4 gains +4.41% over AGLA on ViQuAE. On the strongest backbone (Qwen2.5-VL), ALFAR still leads AGLA by +2.44% on InfoSeek and +2.14% on ViQuAE. On knowledge-based datasets, ALFAR surpasses VCD (the best prior method) by +3.03% on OK-VQA, +2.53% on AOK-VQA, and +1.86% on E-VQA. Inference time is comparable to other contrastive decoding methods (1.35×–1.46× baseline), while AGLA is notably slower at 1.81×–2.04×, making ALFAR both more accurate and more efficient than the strongest attention-based competitor.

## Relation to Prior Work

ALFAR extends the training-free decoding lineage for knowledge-conflict mitigation (CAD, AdaCAD, Entropy, COIECD) by introducing an attention-level intervention that prior methods did not address. While MRAG frameworks like Wiki-LLaVA and ReflectiVA focus on retrieval quality or trainable reflection mechanisms, ALFAR operates entirely at inference time, combining attention reallocation (shifting focus from image to context tokens based on retrieval similarity and query-context relevance) with adaptive logits fusion that decouples parametric and contextual knowledge. This dual-level approach—modifying both the attention distribution and the output logits—represents a departure from prior contrastive decoding methods that operate solely at the logit level.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is high: ALFAR is training-free, plug-and-play, and consistently improves six different MLLMs across seven benchmarks without requiring external tools or architectural changes, making it immediately deployable. The identification of attention bias (over-allocation to image tokens and uniform distribution across context tokens) as a distinct bottleneck separate from knowledge conflicts provides a useful diagnostic framework for the community. Key limitations include the requirement for white-box model access (inapplicable to API-based models like GPT-4) and limited gains from increasing retrieved context length, pointing to open questions about long-context utilization and black-box adaptation.
