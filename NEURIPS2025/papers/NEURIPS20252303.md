## Problem

Prior ANN search competitions, such as NeurIPS'21, focused on scaling classical nearest-neighbor search to billion-scale datasets, but real-world workloads increasingly involve filtered queries with metadata predicates, cross-modal out-of-distribution data, sparse high-dimensional embeddings, and streaming insert/delete operations. This paper addresses the gap by establishing standardized benchmarks, new datasets, and evaluation protocols for these four practical ANN search variants, and by documenting the state-of-the-art solutions developed by the competition participants.

## Input / Output

Each track consumes a different data format: the filtered track indexes 10M 192-dim uint8 CLIP embeddings each annotated with a bag of tags from a 200K vocabulary, and queries consist of one embedding plus 1–2 tag constraints; the OOD track indexes 10M 200-dim float32 image embeddings (SeResNext-101) while queries are 200-dim text embeddings (DSSM variant) from a different distribution; the sparse track indexes 8.8M vectors of dimension <100K with ~120 nonzeros (SPLADE) against ~49-nonzero queries; the streaming track processes a runbook of insert/delete/search batches over 10M 100-dim float32 vectors under an 8 GB memory cap and 1-hour wall-clock limit. All tracks output top-10 nearest neighbors with distances, scored by 10-recall@10 and query throughput (QPS) on a standardized 8-vCPU/16 GB Azure VM.

## Method

The paper's core contribution is a four-track benchmarking framework (extending the NeurIPS'21 infrastructure) that standardizes dataset preparation, Docker-based entry submission, and automated evaluation with private query sets to prevent overfitting. Notable winning approaches span algorithmic and engineering innovations: ParlayANN keys its index by tag cardinality, building Vamana graphs and spatial inverted indices for common tags while using linear scans for rare ones, and intersects candidate sets via bit-vectors for multi-tag queries; RoarANN constructs a bipartite graph between database and sampled query distributions before projecting back to the base space, explicitly encoding OOD structure into the graph topology; PyANNS achieves top throughput across multiple tracks through 8-bit scalar quantization with 16-bit reranking, VNNI instructions, and adaptive prefetching on a Vamana graph; Puck uses a four-level quantization hierarchy (two vector-quantization levels, two product-quantization levels) with tag-labeled centroids for filtered search. No winning entry achieved its performance through hyperparameter tuning of the provided baselines (Faiss, DiskANN, linscan), confirming that task-specific structural or implementation changes were necessary.

## Results

Across the four competition tracks, top entries substantially outperformed the organizer-provided baselines. In the Filtered track, the winner ParlayANN achieved 37,671 QPS at ≥90% recall@10, an 11× speedup over the Faiss baseline (3,253 QPS). In the OOD track, joint winners RoarANN and PyANNS reached ~22,300–22,500 QPS versus the DiskANN/Vamana baseline at 6,753 QPS (~3.3×). In the Sparse track, PyANNS achieved 6,500 QPS (private) versus the LinScan baseline at 95 QPS (~68×). In the Streaming track (corrected scoring), PyANNS attained 0.8865 average recall versus the DiskANN streaming baseline at 0.7218.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares competition entries against four organizer-provided baselines, one per track. In the Filtered track, the Faiss IVF baseline (3,253 QPS private) was outperformed by 11× by ParlayANN (37,671 QPS), which uses tag-keyed Vamana graphs and bit-vector intersections. In the OOD track, the in-memory DiskANN/Vamana baseline (6,753 QPS) was beaten by ~3.3× by RoarANN (22,492 QPS), which builds a projected bipartite graph incorporating query-distribution information, and by PyANNS (22,296 QPS), which relies on 8-bit scalar quantization with VNNI-accelerated reranking. In the Sparse track, the LinScan inverted-index baseline (95 QPS private) was surpassed by ~68× by PyANNS (6,500 QPS), which applies HNSW with 16-bit quantization and coordinate pruning. In the Streaming track, the DiskANN streaming baseline (0.7218 corrected recall) was outperformed by PyANNS (0.8865 recall), which uses DiskANN with 8-bit scalar quantization to enable deeper graph traversal within the 1-hour time budget. No winning entry achieved its performance through hyperparameter tuning of the baseline; all required structural or engineering changes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the NeurIPS'21 Big ANN Challenge [21], which focused on scaling classical ANN search to billion-scale datasets, by introducing four practical task variants—filtered search, out-of-distribution queries, sparse vectors, and streaming—that reflect real-world workloads. The benchmarking framework is inherited from [21] but adapted to handle metadata constraints, distribution mismatch, sparsity, and dynamic insert/delete operations. The competing methods draw on established index families (Vamana/DiskANN, HNSW, IVF, inverted indices) and quantization techniques, but the paper's contribution is the standardized evaluation of these methods under task-specific constraints rather than the introduction of new algorithms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This benchmark establishes the first standardized datasets, evaluation protocols, and open-source framework for practical ANN search variants, filling a gap where prior work lacked comparable baselines for filtered, OOD, sparse, and streaming settings. The large performance gaps over baselines (up to 11× in filtered search) signal substantial room for algorithmic improvement, and the post-competition discovery of a caching error that reversed the streaming track rankings underscores the difficulty of correctly evaluating dynamic indices. Open questions include generalizing filtered search beyond the 1–2 tag restriction, handling concurrent operations in streaming, and whether OOD-aware index construction (e.g., RoarANN's bipartite graph) transfers to other distribution-mismatch scenarios.
