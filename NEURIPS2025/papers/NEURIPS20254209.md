## Problem

Existing skill-based MARL transfer methods (e.g., ODIS, HiSSD) capture only individual agent skills, failing to encode how those skills combine into coordinated team behaviors across tasks. This paper argues that effective zero-shot policy transfer in multi-task MARL requires knowledge at two levels: individual skills for reusable action patterns and cooperative tactics for team-level coordination, and that transferring skills alone is insufficient when agent counts and task structures vary.

## Input / Output

The method consumes offline MARL trajectories (global states, joint actions, rewards) from source tasks, where each agent's observation is decomposed into entity-wise features to handle variable agent counts across tasks. The Bi-Level Decision Transformer receives a structured autoregressive sequence of tokens—tactic codebook indices, skill embeddings, return-to-go targets, and entity observations—and produces a two-level output: first a discrete tactic codebook index (from a finite set of K=16 tactic embeddings) as team-level guidance, then a continuous individual skill embedding per agent, which is decoded into a discrete action. Execution is fully decentralized: each agent independently predicts the shared tactic index and its own skill from local history.

## Method

BiKT trains in three sequential stages, all offline. First, a VAE extracts per-agent skill embeddings from global state and joint actions, with an action decoder reconstructing actions from local history and skill. Second, a VQ-VAE maps the joint skill combination (concatenated with entity features) into a continuous tactic representation, which is discretized via nearest-neighbor search into a learned tactic codebook of K embeddings; a skill decoder then reconstructs individual skills from local history and the selected tactic, with a diversity penalty encouraging distinct codebook entries. Third, a Bi-Level Decision Transformer (BDT) is trained in a supervised manner to autoregressively predict the tactic codebook index and then the individual skill, conditioned on return-to-go, observations, and past tactic/skill tokens. The key architectural distinction from prior DT-based MARL policies (e.g., MADT) is the explicit two-level autoregressive structure: the team tactic acts as a conditioning signal that guides diverse skill selection, enabling the same tactic to instantiate different skill combinations across tasks with varying agent numbers.

## Results

On the Marine-Hard task set (Expert data quality), BiKT achieves near-perfect win rates on symmetric unseen tasks (99.3% on 4m, 99.9% on 5m, 99.4% on 10m, 99.0% on 12m) and substantially outperforms all baselines on asymmetric unseen tasks (68.0% on 7m_vs_8m, 50.0% on 8m_vs_9m, 90.6% on 10m_vs_11m). On the Cooperative Navigation (MPE) benchmark, BiKT achieves 93.8% on unseen CN-3 and 28.2% on unseen CN-5 (Expert), outperforming UPDeT (47.9%, 2.1%), ODIS (85.6%, 20.0%), and HiSSD (89.8%, 25.3%). The metric reported throughout is win rate (%) averaged over 5 random seeds and 32 evaluation environments.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BiKT consistently outperforms all three baselines across the Marine-Hard and Cooperative Navigation benchmarks. Against UPDeT, which relies solely on architectural flexibility without explicit knowledge transfer, BiKT achieves dramatic gains on asymmetric unseen tasks (e.g., 68.0% vs. 0.0% on 7m_vs_8m, 90.6% vs. 0.0% on 10m_vs_11m) and strong improvements on symmetric tasks (99.0% vs. 17.2% on 12m). Against ODIS, which transfers individual coordination skills, BiKT excels particularly on asymmetric unseen tasks where team-level coordination matters most (68.0% vs. 25.0% on 7m_vs_8m, 90.6% vs. 42.4% on 10m_vs_11m), while ODIS remains competitive on simpler symmetric tasks (95.3% vs. 99.3% on 4m). Against HiSSD, which learns shared and task-specific skills hierarchically, BiKT shows the largest margins on asymmetric tasks (68.0% vs. 35.3% on 7m_vs_8m, 50.0% vs. 47.0% on 8m_vs_9m) and matches or slightly exceeds HiSSD on symmetric tasks (99.9% vs. 99.2% on 5m). On the MPE Cooperative Navigation benchmark, BiKT leads on all unseen tasks (93.8% vs. 89.8% on CN-3, 28.2% vs. 25.3% on CN-5 under Expert data), with the gap widening under Medium data quality (46.8% vs. 44.9% on CN-3, 7.1% vs. 5.1% on CN-5).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the skill-based policy transfer line of work in multi-task MARL (ODIS, HiSSD) by introducing a second, team-level abstraction: cooperative tactics learned as a discrete codebook over skill combinations via VQ-VAE. The decision-making backbone is a Bi-Level Decision Transformer, which generalizes the single-task Decision Transformer (Chen et al., 2021) and the multi-agent DT (MADT) to a hierarchical autoregressive setting where a tactic index is predicted before individual skills. Unlike UPDeT, which relies on architectural flexibility for multi-task generalization, BiKT explicitly decomposes transferable knowledge into reusable individual skills and a finite set of team-level coordination patterns, drawing on the VAE/VQ-VAE literature for latent representation learning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a concrete gap in prior skill-based MARL transfer—individual skills alone do not capture how agents should coordinate them across tasks—and provides a principled, interpretable mechanism (the tactic codebook) to fill it. This is practically relevant for settings where offline data from a few source tasks must generalize to many unseen configurations without fine-tuning. However, performance on the hardest asymmetric tasks (e.g., 10m_vs_12m, 13m_vs_15m) remains very low across all methods, and the evaluation is confined to SMAC and MPE game benchmarks with discrete actions, leaving open whether the bi-level decomposition scales to continuous-action, larger-scale, or real-world multi-agent domains. The fixed codebook size (K=16) and the sequential three-stage training pipeline also raise questions about adaptability and training efficiency.
