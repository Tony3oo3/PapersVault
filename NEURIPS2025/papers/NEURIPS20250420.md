## Problem

Existing medical VLM benchmarks evaluate answer correctness but do not systematically assess whether models follow the stepwise clinical reasoning trajectory (modality → anatomy → lesion → diagnosis) or merely exploit shortcut correlations and memorized patterns. No prior benchmark tests whether a model can ground a diagnosis in intermediate visual evidence or maintain coherent multi-turn reasoning, leaving a critical gap in understanding whether VLMs truly reason like clinicians.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250420/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark consumes medical images (CT, MRI, ultrasound, radiography, pathology) at ≥256×256 resolution paired with multiple-choice questions organized into a five-level reasoning hierarchy, or open-ended prompts for free-form report generation. A distinctive input manipulation is the organ/lesion erasure task, where targeted anatomical regions are digitally removed to force reliance on visible evidence rather than memorized associations; noise is also simulated at three PSNR levels (15, 25, 35 dB). Outputs include per-level accuracy scores, per-format (Joint/Independent/Multi-turn) accuracy for reasoning trajectory, and for report generation, normalized BERTScore (PubMedBERT) plus FActScore recall and specificity to quantify factual coverage and hallucination.

## Method

DrVD-Bench is a three-module evaluation framework structured around the clinical diagnostic cascade. Module 1 (Visual Evidence Comprehension, 4,480 QA pairs) assigns each image a single question at one of five reasoning levels (Image Quality → Basic Information → Anatomy → Lesion → Clinical Interpretation), with organ and lesion erasure tasks specifically designed to eliminate pretraining shortcuts. Module 2 (Reasoning Trajectory Assessment, 487 images / 3,321 QA turns) reuses the same questions but reorganizes them into three prompting formats—Joint QA (all questions at once), Independent QA (sequential, no memory), and Multi-turn QA (prior answers fed into subsequent prompts)—to isolate whether models reason step-by-step or rely on global context; a controlled Insert-Correct/Insert-Error experiment further disentangles error accumulation from prompt effects. Module 3 (Report Generation, 475 questions) spans all five modalities and is scored with normalized BERTScore and FActScore to capture both semantic alignment and atomic-level factual consistency. All 19 evaluated VLMs are tested zero-shot on 8×A100 GPUs, with DeepSeek-V3 used for answer extraction and key-feature parsing.

## Results

Across 19 VLMs evaluated on DrVD-Bench (7,789 QA pairs, 5 imaging modalities), Gemini 2.5 Pro achieves the highest overall accuracy at 69.3%, followed by GPT-o3 (65.8%) and the medical-specific HuaTuoGPT-Vision-34B (66.0%). Performance degrades sharply with reasoning depth: on CT tasks, top models score 86–88% on Basic Information but drop to 41–52% on Lesion-Level identification and 48–54% on Diagnosis. A striking 'overdiagnosis without understanding' effect is observed—e.g., GPT-o3 scores only 28% on CT lesion erasure detection (below random at 26%) yet reaches 52% on CT diagnosis, indicating models produce correct labels without grounding in visual evidence.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among proprietary models, Gemini 2.5 Pro leads on CT (70.1%) and overall (69.3%), outperforming GPT-o3 (68.9% CT, 65.8% overall) by 1.2 and 3.5 points respectively; GPT-4o trails both at 64.8% overall. The medical-specific HuaTuoGPT-Vision-34B (66.0% overall) rivals or exceeds several larger proprietary models (e.g., GPT-o1 at 51.7%, Grok-3 at 60.4%) despite having fewer than half the parameters, demonstrating the value of domain-specific alignment. Open-source Qwen2.5-VL-72B (62.2% overall) is competitive with mid-tier proprietary models but falls short of the top two. In the multi-turn reasoning setting (Module 2), all models show significant degradation—GPT-4o drops from 58% (Joint) to 55% (Multi-turn) on Organ tasks, and Grok-3 collapses from 62% to 37%—confirming that cumulative error propagation, not prompt design, drives the gap. On report generation (Module 3), FActScore reveals severe hallucination: even the best model (Gemini 2.5 Pro) achieves only 0.13 recall and 0.07 specificity, meaning it captures ~13% of true clinical facts while ~93% of generated statements are unsupported.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground in medical VLM evaluation by departing from prior benchmarks (VQA-RAD, SLAKE, GMAI-MMBench, OmniMedVQA, PathMMU) that organize questions by perceptual complexity or task type without alignment to clinical reasoning stages. DrVD-Bench introduces a five-level hierarchy mirroring the actual diagnostic workflow (image quality → basic info → anatomy → lesion → diagnosis), adds organ/lesion erasure tasks to force visual grounding over memorized associations, and includes multi-turn QA to test stepwise reasoning trajectories. It also extends report generation evaluation across all five imaging modalities, whereas prior work (e.g., FFA-IR, CxPMRG-Bench) limited this to radiographs or CT.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals a critical disconnect: models can produce correct diagnoses while performing below random on lesion-erasure detection, suggesting diagnostic outputs are not grounded in visual evidence. The finding that multi-turn reasoning (the most clinically realistic format) performs worst, and that even top models achieve only ~13% FActScore recall in report generation, highlights that current VLMs are far from clinically trustworthy. Open questions include whether training on intermediate reasoning steps (rather than image-diagnosis pairs) can close the gap, whether volumetric (3D/4D) reasoning changes these conclusions, and how to mitigate the train-test overlap risk from public datasets.
