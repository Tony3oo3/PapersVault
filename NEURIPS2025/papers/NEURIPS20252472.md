## Problem

Existing T2V safety benchmarks like T2VSafetyBench provide evaluation protocols but lack systematic, optimization-driven methods for thoroughly probing model vulnerabilities. No prior work formalizes the T2V jailbreak attack as a search problem over the discrete token space, leaving a gap between static prompt collections and principled adversarial prompt generation for video models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252472/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single unsafe text prompt (e.g., a sentence describing violent or explicit content) that is blocked by the target T2V model's safety filter, and produces an optimized adversarial prompt as a discrete token sequence of n words. The adversarial prompt is submitted to the T2V model to generate a video of M frames; the optimization loop queries the model iteratively (up to 20 rounds) and uses CLIP embeddings of both the prompt and a VideoLLaMA2-generated video caption to score semantic fidelity, while a binary filter-bypass indicator and a frame-level jailbreak ratio measure attack success.

## Method

T2V-OptJail formulates T2V jailbreak as a discrete token-level optimization with a joint loss combining two objectives: (1) Filter Bypassing Optimization, which penalizes prompts blocked by the safety filter and minimizes the fraction of non-unsafe frames via a CLIP-based jailbreak frame ratio; and (2) Semantics Consistency Optimization, which enforces CLIP cosine similarity between the original and adversarial prompts and between the original prompt and a video-captioning model's description of the generated video. An LLM agent (GPT-4o) iteratively rewrites the current best candidate to produce a new semantically preserved prompt each round. A Prompt Mutation Strategy generates K=5 mildly perturbed variants around each candidate and averages their joint losses to select the next starting point, improving robustness against semantic perturbations. The overall procedure minimizes L_total = L_bypass + L_sem over the discrete prompt space until convergence or a maximum of 20 iterations.

## Results

On the T2VSafetyBench subset (700 prompts across 14 safety categories), T2V-OptJail achieves the highest attack success rate (ASR) across all four evaluated T2V models. On Open-Sora, it reaches 67.1% (GPT-4) / 68.7% (Human) ASR with 0.269 semantic similarity, compared to 55.7%/58.7% for T2VSafetyBench and 22.3%/24.0% for DACA. On commercial platforms, ASR ranges from 37.9% (Kling) to 55.9% (Pika) under GPT-4 evaluation, consistently outperforming all baselines by 7.0–10.1 percentage points over T2VSafetyBench. Semantic similarity (CLIP cosine between input prompt and video caption) is also the highest in every setting (0.257–0.269), confirming that the optimized prompts preserve the original unsafe intent.

## Baselines & Comparisons

T2V-OptJail outperforms all four baselines on every model and every metric. Against T2VSafetyBench (the strongest baseline), it gains +8.2 pp GPT-4 ASR on Pika (55.9% vs 47.7%), +10.1 pp on Luma (43.0% vs 32.9%), +8.6 pp on Kling (37.9% vs 29.3%), and +11.4 pp on Open-Sora (67.1% vs 55.7%), while also achieving higher semantic similarity in all cases. Against DACA, the margins are much larger: e.g., 67.1% vs 22.3% on Open-Sora and 55.9% vs 14.6% on Pika. In the extended comparison (Table 3), T2V-OptJail also beats Autodan (40.6% GPT-4 on Open-Sora, 33.1% on Pika) and Sneakyprompt (27.9% on Open-Sora, 20.6% on Pika) by 26.5 pp and 35.3 pp respectively on Open-Sora. The method additionally maintains the highest semantic similarity (0.269 on Open-Sora, 0.266 on Pika) across all baselines, indicating superior content controllability alongside higher attack success.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the LLM-driven prompt optimization paradigm established for text-to-image jailbreaks (SneakyPrompt, DACA, AutoDAN) into the text-to-video domain, where no prior work had formulated the attack as a search problem. It departs from T2VSafetyBench, which provided static jailbreak prompts for evaluation but no systematic optimization methodology, by introducing a joint discrete-optimization objective that simultaneously targets filter bypass and semantic preservation. The prompt mutation strategy and the use of CLIP-based video captioning (via VideoLLaMA2) as a feedback signal are novel components not present in the T2I jailbreak literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The results expose a concrete and quantified vulnerability in commercial T2V systems (Pika, Luma, Kling), showing that their semantic alignment mechanisms—designed to improve generation quality—can be exploited to sustain unsafe content across video frames. A key open question is how to design defenses that are robust to iterative, LLM-guided prompt optimization rather than static keyword or single-pass semantic checks. The authors acknowledge that the method's reliance on querying generated videos for feedback introduces a query-budget cost, and suggest local proxy models as a mitigation, leaving the efficiency-robustness tradeoff for future work.
