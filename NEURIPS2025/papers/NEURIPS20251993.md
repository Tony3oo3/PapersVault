## Problem

Generating realistic brain connectivity matrices requires sampling from constrained Riemannian manifolds (SPD or correlation matrices), but existing Riemannian generative methods demand expensive manifold-specific operations—geodesic computation, Riemannian norms, and parallel transport—at every training and sampling step. This makes them computationally prohibitive for large-scale neuroimaging datasets, while simpler Euclidean approaches (e.g., generating triangular entries) produce invalid matrices that require distorting post-hoc projections.

## Input / Output

The method takes a discrete condition label (disease status for fMRI, motor imagery class for EEG) and a source sample drawn from a class-conditional Gaussian fitted to the diffeomorphically embedded training data. It produces a d×d SPD covariance matrix (EEG, d=15 or 13 channels) or a d×d correlation matrix (fMRI, d=90 MSDL regions) that is guaranteed to satisfy positive definiteness and unit-diagonal constraints by construction, with no post-processing projection needed. The ODE is integrated in Euclidean space and the result is mapped back to the manifold via the inverse diffeomorphism.

## Method

DIFFEOCFM exploits the fact that a global diffeomorphism φ from a matrix manifold M to Euclidean space E induces a pullback metric under which Riemannian CFM is exactly equivalent to standard CFM in E. The paper proves three propositions establishing this equivalence: the Riemannian CFM loss reduces to the standard Euclidean CFM loss, ODE solutions correspond under φ, and Runge-Kutta iterates match at the discrete level. Two diffeomorphisms are instantiated: the matrix logarithm (inducing the Log-Euclidean metric) for SPD covariance matrices, and the normalized Cholesky decomposition (inducing the Euclidean-Cholesky metric) for correlation matrices. Training uses a 2-layer MLP (512 hidden units) with AdamW to learn a time-dependent vector field in E via the standard CFM regression loss, and sampling integrates the learned ODE with dopri5 before applying φ⁻¹ to recover a valid manifold point—eliminating all geodesic, norm, and parallel-transport computations while preserving geometric constraints exactly.

## Results

DIFFEOCFM achieves the best or near-best α,β-F1 quality score across all five datasets: 0.59 (ABIDE), 0.68 (ADNI), 0.44 (OASIS-3), 0.62 (BNCI2014-002), and 0.89 (BNCI2015-001), approaching the Real Data upper bounds. On CAS metrics, it attains the highest ROC-AUC and F1 on every dataset, e.g. 0.81 ROC-AUC / 0.74 F1 on BNCI2014-002 and 0.73 / 0.65 on BNCI2015-001, matching Real Data within 0.01–0.02. Computationally, DIFFEOCFM trains ~8× faster and samples ~10× faster than RIEMCFM on EEG (e.g. 253 s vs. 1984 s training; 0.59 s vs. 5.28 s sampling on BNCI2014-002), while TRIANGDDPM/TRIANGCFM suffer catastrophic quality degradation (α,β-F1 drops of −0.50 to −0.76) after manifold projection.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DIFFEOGAUSS (wrapped Gaussian, de Surrel et al. 2025), DIFFEOCFM substantially outperforms on quality metrics (α,β-F1: 0.59 vs. 0.38 on ABIDE, 0.68 vs. 0.04 on ADNI) and on CAS F1 (+0.05 to +0.18 absolute gains), while DIFFEOGAUSS remains competitive on ROC-AUC. Against TRIANGDDPM (Albergo et al. 2023) and TRIANGCFM (Lipman et al. 2023), DIFFEOCFM dominates decisively: both triangular baselines produce matrices with negative eigenvalues that, after projection, collapse to near-zero quality scores (α,β-F1 ≈ 0.00 on all fMRI datasets; drops of −0.50 to −0.76 relative to pre-projection values), rendering them impractical. Against RIEMCFM (Chen et al. 2024), DIFFEOCFM achieves statistically equivalent quality and CAS performance on both EEG datasets (e.g. 0.62 vs. 0.63 α,β-F1 on BNCI2014-002; 0.89 vs. 0.88 on BNCI2015-001) while training 8× faster (253 s vs. 1984 s) and sampling 10× faster (0.59 s vs. 5.28 s), owing to the avoidance of repeated affine-invariant geodesic and norm computations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Riemannian Conditional Flow Matching (Chen & Lipman, ICLR 2024), which provides a general framework for learning vector fields on Riemannian manifolds, and on standard Euclidean CFM (Lipman et al., ICLR 2023). The key conceptual departure is a theoretical equivalence: when the Riemannian metric is a pullback metric induced by a global diffeomorphism (e.g., the log-Euclidean or Euclidean-Cholesky metrics), Riemannian CFM reduces exactly to standard Euclidean CFM after a change of variables, eliminating the need for geodesic computation, Riemannian norms, and manifold ODE integration. This positions DIFFEOCFM as a practical simplification of the general Riemannian CFM framework, analogous to how the log-Euclidean metric has long simplified SPD matrix analysis, but now applied to the generative modeling pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DIFFEOCFM provides the first unified generative framework that handles both SPD covariance matrices (EEG) and correlation matrices (fMRI) without post-hoc projection, achieving 8× faster training and 10× faster sampling than the general Riemannian CFM baseline while matching its quality. The finding that naive triangular-parameterization approaches (TRIANGDDPM/TRIANGCFM) suffer catastrophic quality degradation (up to −0.74 F1) upon projection is a useful negative result that cautions against the common 'generate in Euclidean space, project back' heuristic. Open questions include whether the approach extends to manifolds lacking global diffeomorphisms (e.g., Stiefel), how it scales to higher parcellation granularity where dimensionality grows quadratically, and whether geometry-aware evaluation metrics could better capture neurophysiological structure.
