## Problem

Existing LLM-based time series methods face two underaddressed alignment challenges: (1) time-domain sequences exhibit complex, high-variability patterns that cannot be losslessly captured by a limited set of natural language tokens, and (2) the contextual structure of time series—where semantics emerge across consecutive points rather than individual tokens—is fundamentally mismatched with the token-level positional encoding of standard LLMs. Prior work largely overlooks the second issue and relies on an unrealistic assumption that discrete textual tokens can faithfully represent arbitrary time-domain dynamics.

## Input / Output

The method accepts multivariate time series (decomposed into N univariate series of length L=512 for forecasting), which are split into Q patches of size M with stride R. Each patch undergoes FFT to extract top-k=5 dominant frequency components mapped to ASCII symbols, and a GRU-based segmentation assigns block-level positional indices. Task-specific text prompts (e.g., 'Predict future sequences using previous data') are interleaved into the embedding sequence. Outputs are forecasted values over horizons {96, 192, 336, 720} for long-term forecasting, values over horizons 6–48 for short-term (M4), or class labels for UEA classification.

## Method

S²TS-LLM repurposes a frozen GPT-2 (first 4 layers, only LayerNorm fine-tuned) for time series analysis through two alignment paradigms. Spectral Symbolization applies FFT to each patch, selects the top-k frequency components by amplitude, and maps them to a fixed set of ASCII symbols, yielding a compact textual abstraction that captures global spectral patterns without lossy time-domain description. Contextual Segmentation passes patches through a GRU, computes pairwise l2-distance segment scores, groups patches sharing similar trend semantics into blocks, and reassigns positional indices at the block level (e.g., [1,1,1,2,2,3] instead of [1,2,3,4,5,6]) to preserve trend continuity. The raw time series embeddings and spectral symbol embeddings are concatenated with learnable timestamps, interleaved with task prompts in G=2 slices, and fed into the LLM with the segment-level positions. The time-domain and frequency-domain LLM outputs are fused via a gated residual update modulated by cosine similarity, then projected through prefix truncation and a linear layer to produce final predictions.

## Results

On long-term forecasting (averaged over horizons 96/192/336/720), S²TS-LLM achieves the best average MSE of 0.302 and MAE of 0.329 across 7 datasets, outperforming the closest LLM-based competitor S²IP-LLM (MSE 0.311) and all non-LLM baselines. On zero-shot cross-domain transfer, it attains an average MSE of 0.375, an 8.3% improvement over the second-best RLinear (0.409) and 13.5% over Time-LLM (0.434). For time-series classification on 10 UEA datasets, S²TS-LLM reaches 76.4% average accuracy, surpassing GPT4TS (74.0%) and TimesNet (73.6%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

S²TS-LLM achieves the best MSE and MAE on all 7 long-term forecasting datasets, beating the closest LLM-based competitor S²IP-LLM by 2.9% in average MSE (0.302 vs 0.311) and Time-LLM by 5.0% (0.302 vs 0.318). Among non-LLM baselines, it outperforms TimeXer (0.324), iTransformer (0.342), PatchTST (0.353), TimesNet (0.376), and RLinear (0.377) by 6.9%–20.2% in average MSE. On short-term M4 forecasting, S²TS-LLM is competitive (SMAPE 11.979) but trails TimeMixer (11.723) and iTransformer (12.142), which the authors attribute to short input lengths limiting LLM statistical inference. In few-shot (5%) and zero-shot settings, S²TS-LLM consistently leads all LLM baselines, achieving 4.1–8.2% MSE reductions over S²IP-LLM, Time-LLM, and GPT4TS respectively, and an 8.3% zero-shot MSE improvement over the best non-LLM baseline RLinear. For classification, it surpasses GPT4TS (74.0%), TimesNet (73.6%), and Rocket (72.5%) with 76.4% average accuracy, while conventional forecasters like PatchTST (67.4%) and RLinear (67.2%) degrade significantly on this task.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the GPT4TS paradigm of repurposing frozen pre-trained LLMs (fine-tuning only LayerNorm) for time series analysis, and adopts Time-LLM's prefix truncation for output projection. It departs from prior LLM-for-time-series alignment methods (Time-LLM's textual prototypes, S²IP-LLM's semantic anchors) by shifting the alignment from the time domain to the frequency domain, exploiting FFT sparsity to map top-k dominant frequency components to a fixed set of ASCII symbols. Additionally, it introduces a GRU-based contextual segmentation that reassigns positional indices at the block level rather than the token level, addressing a structural mismatch between sequential language and trend-continuous time series that prior methods overlooked.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that frequency-domain alignment can serve as a compact, task-agnostic bridge between continuous time series and discrete LLM token spaces, potentially reducing the need for dataset-specific vocabulary design. The strong zero-shot transfer results (13.5% MSE improvement over Time-LLM) suggest that spectral priors learned by LLMs generalize across domains, which is practically valuable for data-scarce settings. However, the framework still relies on a small GPT-2 backbone (4 layers), underperforms specialized models on short-term forecasting, and the ASCII-symbol mapping's interpretability remains unclear—open questions include whether scaling to larger LLMs or learned symbol embeddings would further close the gap with domain-specific architectures.
