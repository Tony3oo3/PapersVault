## Problem

Existing LVLM jailbreak research predominantly exploits alignment flaws by crafting harmful inputs (adversarial perturbations, typographic images, modified system prompts), which motivates input-level filtering as a defense. This paper identifies an overlooked vulnerability: ostensibly safe images, when combined with additional safe images and prompts, can trigger unsafe outputs by leveraging the model's own reasoning capabilities rather than bypassing its safety alignment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253344/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single safe RGB image (verified as non-harmful by Google Cloud Vision Moderator) as the primary visual input, and the agent autonomously retrieves or generates a second 'jailbreak image' via Google Image search filtered by CLIP cosine similarity. Text prompts are generated by GPT-4o in two sequential turns, with the second turn conditioned on the model's first-turn response. The output is a two-turn dialogue where the second response is progressively more harmful than the first, evaluated via Unsafe Ratio, GPT-Score (0–5 scale), OpenAI Moderation, and LLaMA3-Guard.

## Method

SSA is a black-box, agent-based jailbreak framework operating in two stages. In Stage 1 (Initial Response Generation), GPT-4o identifies a harmful topic from the safe image, generates a search keyword, retrieves candidate images via a Selenium-based Google Image scraper, and selects the most semantically aligned image using CLIP embeddings; a crafted question then prompts the target LVLM to produce an initial response that implicitly accepts the harmful premise without being overtly dangerous. In Stage 2 (Harmful Snowballing), a templated follow-up prompt leverages the initial response as conversational context to escalate the model's output toward explicitly harmful content. The core mechanism exploits two inherent LVLM properties: universal reasoning (the model overinterprets relationships between safe visual and textual inputs, e.g., linking a bank image to robbery planning) and the safety snowball effect (an initial ambiguous acceptance cascades into progressively unsafe outputs because the autoregressive architecture cannot self-correct within a single turn). Unlike prior attacks that target alignment gaps, SSA requires no adversarial perturbations, no system-prompt access, and no knowledge of model internals.

## Results

On MM-SafetyBench, SSA achieves an Unsafe Ratio of 96.9% on GPT-4o, substantially outperforming the strongest baseline Fuzzer (62.9%) and all other methods (≤5.2%). Across Qwen-VL2 72B and Intern-VL 40B, SSA similarly leads with Unsafe Ratios of 85.6% and 93.8% respectively, versus 67.0% and 62.9% for Fuzzer. On SafeAttack-Bench (1,347 safe images), SSA achieves Unsafe Ratios above 0.89 for GPT-4o across all seven harm categories (e.g., 0.970 for Self-Harm, 0.953 for Terrorism), confirming that safe images alone can trigger high rates of unsafe generation. The ablation study shows that combining both the jailbreak image and multi-turn context yields GPT-Scores of 3.0–4.1, versus 1.1–1.9 when neither component is used.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SSA clearly outperforms all baselines on GPT-4o, Qwen-VL2 72B, and Intern-VL 40B across Unsafe Ratio, OpenAI Moderation, and LLaMA3-eval. On GPT-4o, SSA's Unsafe Ratio of 96.9% exceeds Fuzzer (62.9%) by 34 percentage points, while Figstep (1.0%), SD (5.2%), and all typography-based methods (≤0.2%) are essentially ineffective. On Intern-VL 40B, SSA achieves 93.8% Unsafe Ratio versus 62.9% for Fuzzer and 49.5% for Figstep, with a GPT-Score of 3.86 versus 2.65 for Fuzzer. However, on VILA1.5 40B, SSA (93.8% Unsafe Ratio, GPT-Score 4.06) is slightly outperformed by Fuzzer (94.8%, 4.71) and Figstep (94.8%, 4.77), suggesting that VILA's weaker safety alignment makes simpler attacks already highly effective. The MM-SafetyBench baselines (SD, SD_TYPO, TYPO) are consistently the weakest, with Unsafe Ratios below 20% on GPT-4o and Qwen-VL2, confirming that recent LVLMs have strong defenses against shallow visual or typographic perturbations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant line of LVLM jailbreak research (Figstep, Fuzzer, adversarial perturbation methods) that exploits alignment flaws by instead leveraging the inherent properties of LVLMs—universal reasoning and the safety snowball effect. It builds on the cascade/snowball effect literature from LLMs (Zhang et al. 2023) and the previously observed 'safe-input, unsafe-output' phenomenon in text-to-image and LLM settings, but is the first to systematically exploit this in LVLMs via an agent-based, multi-turn framework. The agent architecture (SSA) repurposes tool-use capabilities (image search, CLIP-based filtering) to automate the construction of jailbreak inputs, distinguishing it from prior single-turn or single-modality attacks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that safe images achieve comparable or even higher harmfulness scores than unsafe images (4.45 vs 4.48 on GPT-4o) and activate nearly identical dangerous neurons fundamentally undermines the standard defense of input-level content filtering. This reveals that safety alignment in LVLMs may not operate at the visual encoding level, posing a deeper architectural challenge. Key open questions include: how to design defenses that address the snowball effect at the reasoning level rather than the input level, whether multi-step self-correction can be integrated into inference, and whether the vulnerability persists as models scale. The paper's limitation that weaker-reasoning models are less susceptible suggests the attack surface may narrow with model-specific safety tuning, but the neuron-level finding implies a more fundamental issue.
