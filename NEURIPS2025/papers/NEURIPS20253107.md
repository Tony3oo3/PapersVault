## Problem

Schrödinger Bridge solvers for low-to-moderate dimensional problems with simple boundary distributions (e.g., Gaussian Mixture Models) still rely on computationally expensive neural network training or non-convex optimization, even though closed-form solutions exist for the Gaussian-to-Gaussian special case. This paper asks whether one can construct a training-free, analytically tractable SB solver for GMM boundaries that avoids both neural training and non-convex optimization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253107/cropped_page8_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes finite samples from source and target distributions, which are first fitted to GMMs via the EM algorithm (with user-specified component counts N_0, N_1 and noise level ε). At inference, a single initial sample x_0 ~ ρ_0 is propagated through the learned policy to produce a sample from ρ_1. For multi-marginal problems, samples at M regular time intervals are each fitted to GMMs, and the output includes intermediate distribution samples at all M marginals. In the image translation application, inputs are 512-dim ALAE latent codes and outputs are translated latent codes decoded back to images.

## Method

The core idea is to decompose the GMM-to-GMM SB into N_0 × N_1 independent Gaussian-to-Gaussian bridge subproblems, each solved in closed form via Covariance Steering (or the analytic formula of Bunne et al. for the standard Brownian prior). The resulting mixture policy is a weighted average of these conditional policies, where weights are proportional to λ_ij · ρ_{t|ij}(x), making the policy locally dominated by whichever component bridge is closest to the current state. The transport plan λ_ij is obtained by solving a linear program whose dimension scales linearly with N_0 + N_1, providing a provable upper bound on the true SB cost. This avoids all neural network training and non-convex optimization, and the framework generalizes naturally to LTV prior dynamics, multi-marginal momentum SBs (solved via semidefinite programming per component), and continuous GMMs with mixing measures.

## Results

On the image-to-image translation task (512-dim ALAE latent space), GMMflow achieves FID scores of 3.04 (Man→Woman) and 3.50 (Adult→Child), roughly 40% better than LSB (4.94, 6.62) and LSBM (4.98, 6.61), with ALAE-BW distances of 9.3 and 8.54 versus ~29–31 for baselines. On the multi-marginal scRNA-seq problem (5 time marginals, 5 PCs), GMMflow achieves an MMD of 0.0038±0.0007, outperforming DMSB (0.06), NSBL (0.10), and MIOFlow (0.28) by one to two orders of magnitude, while its SWD of 0.37 is competitive with DMSB (0.22) and MIOFlow (0.38). Training completes in ~8 minutes on a CPU for the multi-marginal task, versus hours on a GPU for neural baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the two lightweight SB solvers LSB (Korotin et al., 2024) and LSBM (Gushchin et al., 2024), GMMflow wins decisively on both image-translation tasks: FID improves from ~5–7 to ~3–3.5, and ALAE-BW drops from ~29–31 to ~8.5–9.3, at the cost of a slightly higher transport cost (9.05–9.33 vs 8.18–8.23). The authors attribute this gap to EM-based GMM pre-fitting being less prone to local optima than the non-convex Schrödinger-potential optimization used by LSB/LSBM. On the multi-marginal scRNA-seq benchmark, GMMflow dominates on MMD (0.0038) over DMSB (0.06), NSBL (0.10), and MIOFlow (0.28) by one to two orders of magnitude, but trails DMSB (0.22) and NSBL (0.24) on SWD, which the authors note is bounded by the expressivity of 5-component GMMs in capturing higher-order distribution structure. Overall, GMMflow trades a modest SWD penalty for a large MMD gain and dramatically lower compute (minutes on CPU vs. hours on GPU for neural methods).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the flow-decomposition framework from Diffusion Schrödinger Bridge Matching (DSBM) and Flow Matching, which express the optimal probability flow as an infinite mixture of point-to-point conditional policies, to the specific case where boundary distributions are Gaussian Mixture Models. Rather than approximating the infinite mixture with a trained neural network (as DSBM/FM do) or fitting Schrödinger potentials via non-convex optimization (as LSB/LSBM do), the paper exploits the finite-component structure of GMMs to reduce the problem to a finite mixture of closed-form Gaussian bridges, with the component-level transport plan obtained by solving a linear program. The Gaussian bridge subproblems themselves leverage the closed-form solution of Bunne et al. (2023) and the Covariance Steering framework of Chen et al. (2015) for general LTV dynamics, while the multi-marginal extension builds on the momentum SB formulation of Chen et al. (2019, 2023).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a training-free, computationally lightweight solver for Schrödinger Bridges that achieves substantially better boundary-distribution fidelity than existing lightweight methods (40% lower FID, order-of-magnitude lower MMD), making it a practical tool for low-to-moderate dimensional distribution interpolation, image translation in latent spaces, and multi-marginal cellular dynamics learning. The LP formulation scales linearly with the number of GMM components and generalizes naturally to LTV systems and multi-marginal settings, opening the door to applications in Mean Field Games and Wasserstein splines. The main limitation is the reliance on GMMs, which restricts applicability to low-to-moderate dimensions and may fail to capture complex multimodal or heavy-tailed structures; the extension to continuous GMMs (e.g., Student-t) is promising but remains largely theoretical. An open question is how the quality of the LP upper bound degrades when GMM components overlap significantly, and whether the approach can be combined with learned mixture representations to handle higher-dimensional data.
