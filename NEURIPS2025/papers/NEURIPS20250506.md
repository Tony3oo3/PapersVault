## Problem

Instruction-based video editing methods require large-scale datasets of [original video, edited video, instruction] triplets, but existing datasets (InsV2V, InsViE, VIVID) suffer from low visual quality, poor frame-to-frame consistency, limited editing diversity, and unreliable text-video alignment. Inversion-based alternatives avoid training data but are time-consuming and fail to preserve unedited regions, leaving a critical gap in high-quality, diverse training data for general-purpose video editors.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250506/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The trained editor takes an unedited video clip (33–64 frames, resolutions from 336×592 up to 1120×1984) and a natural-language editing instruction as input, and outputs a temporally consistent edited video. The dataset covers 18 subcategories spanning local edits (object swap, addition, removal, local stylization, inpainting, outpainting) and global edits (style transfer, grounding, deblur, upscaling, colorization, depth/HED/Canny/FakeScribble detection and their *-to-video counterparts), with object addition (26.6%) and object removal (20.7%) being the most prevalent tasks.

## Method

The paper constructs Señorita-2M by crawling ~390K real videos from Pexels, annotating them with BLIP-2 captions and CogVLM2 + Grounded-SAM2 masks, then generating edited triplets using 4 newly trained specialists (a first-frame-guided global stylizer, a local stylizer built on ControlNet, a text-guided video inpainter, and a dedicated object remover that breaks mask-shape correlation via 90% unrelated-mask training) alongside 14 existing or adapted experts. A three-stage filtering pipeline removes failed edits using an ensemble of CLIP-based quality classifiers, filters misaligned instruction-video pairs via CLIP text-video similarity, and discards near-identical source-target pairs. The resulting 2M-sample dataset is used to fine-tune editors on CogVideoX-5B-I2V and Wan2.1 with a ControlNet architecture where a first-frame-guided main branch steers generation and a control branch injects source-video information for consistency, trained in two stages (336×592 then 448×768) with frozen FFN layers except the first DiT block.

## Results

On the DAVIS evaluation set, the Señorita-Editor (trained on Señorita-2M with CogVideoX-5B-I2V + ControlNet) achieves the best scores across all four metrics: Ewarp of 9.42×10⁻³ (vs. 16.31–33.89 for baselines), CLIPScore of 0.2895 (vs. 0.1675–0.2723), Temporal Consistency of 0.9775, and a user preference rate of 55.56% (vs. 4.81%–16.30% for all other methods). The Ewarp improvement over the next-best method (TokenFlow/Flatten at 16.31) represents a ~42% reduction in warping artifacts, while the user preference margin over the runner-up (InsV2V at 16.30%) is more than 3×.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The Señorita-Editor outperforms all nine baselines on every reported metric. Against inversion-based methods (TokenFlow, Flatten, AnyV2V), the advantage is most pronounced in Ewarp (9.42 vs. 16.31–20.48) and user preference (55.56% vs. 4.81%–11.11%), indicating substantially fewer warping artifacts and more natural-looking edits. Against instruction-based competitors, the gap is even wider: InsV2V (the closest rival) scores 16.50 Ewarp and 0.1675 CLIPScore versus 9.42 and 0.2895 for Señorita-Editor, while InsViE is the weakest overall at 33.89 Ewarp and 0.1826 CLIPScore. The Temporal Consistency margin is smaller (0.9775 vs. 0.9752 for TokenFlow), suggesting that all methods achieve reasonable frame-to-frame stability, but Señorita-Editor's decisive edge lies in visual quality, instruction following, and perceived edit fidelity. In the ablation (Table 4), training on Señorita-2M with the same CogVideoX-5B backbone yields 8.44 Ewarp and 0.2596 CLIPScore versus 8.51/0.2366 for InsV2V and 15.72/0.2117 for InsViE, confirming the dataset itself—not just the architecture—drives the gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself squarely in the instruction-based video editing lineage, building on ControlNet's conditional control mechanism and InstructPix2Pix's instruction-following paradigm, but applied to video via CogVideoX-5B as the base diffusion model. It explicitly departs from inversion-based video editing (TokenFlow, Flatten, AnyV2V) by arguing that training on curated [source, target, instruction] triplets offers higher theoretical ceilings. The key methodological departure from prior instruction-based datasets (InsV2V, InsViE, VIVID) is the use of 18 task-specialized experts—four newly trained (global stylizer, local stylizer, video inpainter, remover) and fourteen existing—each generating samples for a specific subcategory, followed by a multi-stage filtering pipeline for quality, text alignment, and edit magnitude.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Señorita-2M is the first open-source, 2-million-sample instruction-based video editing dataset covering 18 diverse subcategories with real-video sources and diverse resolutions (336×592 to 1120×1984), filling a critical gap that has limited progress in this area. The systematic expert-per-subcategory construction and the three-stage filtering pipeline (CLIP-based quality scoring, instruction alignment, subtle-pair removal) provide a reusable methodology for building high-quality editing datasets. Open questions include whether the approach scales to longer videos (beyond 64 frames), multi-step compositional editing, and whether the first-frame-guided architecture generalizes to streaming or real-time settings. The paper's reliance on a single evaluation set (DAVIS with random prompts) and the absence of error bars or multiple seeds limit the statistical confidence in the reported margins.
