## Problem

Prior FP4 training works quantized only a subset of the three matrix multiplications in the training loop (e.g., weights and activations but not gradients, or vice versa), leaving at least one GEMM in higher precision and thus not fully exploiting low-precision hardware. No prior work demonstrated fully quantized training (FQT) of a large LLM at FP4 across all of weights, activations, and gradients at trillion-token scale, nor provided a systematic analysis of which FP4 format parameters (block size, scale encoding, rounding mode) are critical for stability.

## Input / Output

The method consumes the Red Pajama text corpus (1T tokens) and a Llama2 7B decoder-only Transformer architecture (RMSNorm, Smooth-SwiGLU, RoPE) as input. It produces a 7B-parameter LLM trained entirely in NVFP4 precision (E2M1 data with E4M3 per-block scales, block size 16) for all forward, backward, and update GEMMs, followed by a short 40B-token quantization-aware finetuning phase. The resulting model achieves downstream zero-shot accuracy and perplexity on par with a BF16 baseline trained on the same data, with an average of 4.3 bits per GEMM operand across training and QAF.

## Method

The paper establishes NVFP4 (block size 16, E4M3 scale) as the optimal FP4 format through systematic ablations over block sizes (8–128) and scale encodings (E1M6–E8M0), showing that E4M3 and E3M4 scales outperform E8M0 (MXFP4) and that block sizes below 16 yield diminishing returns. A split rounding strategy is adopted: stochastic rounding (SR) is applied to neural gradients in both the backward and update GEMMs and to activations in the update GEMM, while round-to-nearest (RtN) is used for all forward-pass operands, which the authors show is critical for stability. A theoretical analysis of quantized SGD with SR derives a critical threshold—when the per-coordinate gradient magnitude drops below √3 times the quantization noise standard deviation, further loss reduction stalls—motivating a brief QAF phase where the forward pass remains in FP4 but the backward pass switches to BF16 to restore the signal-to-noise ratio. The full pipeline is validated by training Llama2 7B on 1T tokens across 256 Intel Gaudi2 accelerators, with the QAF phase completely closing the small training-loss gap to the BF16 baseline.

## Results

The FP4-trained Llama2 7B model (1T tokens) achieves 44.46% average zero-shot accuracy and 5.83/6.77 WikiText/Lambada perplexity, showing a small gap versus the BF16 baseline (45.63% avg accuracy, 5.54/6.1 perplexity). After a brief 40B-token Quantization-Aware Finetuning (QAF) phase, the FP4 model reaches 45.75% average accuracy and 5.56/5.97 perplexity, matching or slightly exceeding the BF16 baseline across all downstream tasks. The full training uses an average of 4.3 bits per GEMM operand across the training + QAF phases, trained on 256 Intel Gaudi2 accelerators over ~30 days.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is standard BF16 training of Llama2 7B on 1T tokens. After FP4 training alone, the model shows a modest gap: 44.46% vs 45.63% average accuracy and 5.83 vs 5.54 WikiText perplexity. The 40B-token QAF phase (forward pass in FP4, backward in BF16) fully closes this gap, with the FP4+QAF model reaching 45.75% average accuracy—slightly above the BF16 1T baseline—and 5.56/5.97 perplexity, matching or beating BF16 on most individual tasks (e.g., GPQA 27.29% vs 24.54%, TriviaQA 38.31% vs 34.99%). Compared to prior FP4 training works [19, 21], which quantize only a subset of the three GEMMs (weights/activations or gradients only) and train on at most 100B tokens, this paper is the first to quantize all GEMM operands to FP4 at 1T tokens without additional overhead such as sparse residual matrices or Hadamard transforms.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the fully quantized training (FQT) lineage from FP8 (Peng et al. 2023; Fishman et al. 2024) to FP4 precision, leveraging the microscaling (MXFP) format family (Rouhani et al. 2023) for block-wise dynamic scaling. Prior FP4 training efforts (Wang et al. 2025; Tseng et al. 2025) quantized only subsets of the three training GEMMs—weights/activations or gradients—while keeping the remaining operands in higher precision; this work is the first to quantize all six operands across forward, backward, and update GEMMs simultaneously. The stochastic rounding strategy builds on Chmiel et al. (ICLR 2021), and the theoretical analysis of quantized SGD introduces a new √3 gradient-to-noise threshold not present in prior FP8 FQT work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Demonstrating that a 7B LLM can be trained entirely in FP4 on 1T tokens with downstream performance matching BF16 (after a brief 4% QAF phase) validates FP4 as a practical training precision, with projected ~85% time-to-train speedup over BF16 on native hardware. The √3 threshold provides a principled, monitorable criterion for when to switch precision, which could generalize to other low-precision training regimes. Key limitations include the absence of native FP4 hardware (all results are Gaudi2 simulations), testing on a single architecture (Llama2), and unexplored extensions to MoE models and vision tasks, leaving open whether the approach scales to 100B+ parameter models or heterogeneous architectures.
