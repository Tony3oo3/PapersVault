## Problem

Supervised audio source separation methods require extensive task-specific labeled data and struggle to generalize to the open-set variability of real-world acoustic scenes. The paper asks whether pre-trained text-guided audio diffusion models, originally designed for generation, can be repurposed for separation without any training or fine-tuning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253735/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a mixed audio waveform and a short text prompt describing the target source (e.g., 'dog bark') as input. The mixture is converted to a mel-spectrogram and encoded into a VAE latent space; the text prompt is encoded by a text encoder to produce a conditioning vector. The output is the separated waveform of the target source, obtained by decoding the denoised latent through a VAE decoder and vocoder. One forward pass per target source is required, with a shared inversion of the mixture across all sources.

## Method

ZeroSep repurposes a pre-trained text-guided audio diffusion model for separation via a two-step generative inference: (1) DDIM or DDPM inversion maps the mixed audio into the diffusion model's noisy latent space, and (2) text-conditioned denoising from that latent state reconstructs the target source in isolation. The critical discovery is that setting the classifier-free guidance weight ω=1 (rather than the typical high values used for generation) removes the unconditional score component, leaving only the conditional score aligned with the target source and preventing hallucination. The inversion prompt can be null or describe non-target sources, while the reverse prompt must explicitly name the target. The approach is model-agnostic across AudioLDM, AudioLDM2, and Tango backbones, and exhibits a positive correlation between the base model's generative quality and separation performance.

## Results

On the MUSIC dataset, ZeroSep (AudioLDM2-L, DDIM inversion, ω=1) achieves FAD 0.377, LPAPS 4.669, CLAP-A 0.615, and CLAP-T 0.271, outperforming all training-based and training-free baselines on every metric. On the more challenging open-domain AVE dataset, ZeroSep achieves FAD 0.269, LPAPS 4.537, CLAP-A 0.442, and CLAP-T −0.001, matching or exceeding the strongest supervised baseline FlowSep (FAD 0.258, LPAPS 4.719, CLAP-A 0.493, CLAP-T 0.082) on perceptual similarity (LPAPS) while remaining competitive on distributional and semantic metrics. The method requires no task-specific training, using only latent inversion and text-conditioned denoising with classifier-free guidance weight ω=1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ZeroSep decisively outperforms all training-based baselines on the MUSIC dataset: it beats FlowSep (the strongest supervised method) by 0.025 FAD, 0.909 LPAPS, 0.051 CLAP-A, and 0.026 CLAP-T, and surpasses AudioSep and LASS-Net by even larger margins. On the open-domain AVE dataset, ZeroSep is competitive with FlowSep, winning on LPAPS (4.537 vs. 4.719) but trailing slightly on FAD (0.269 vs. 0.258), CLAP-A (0.442 vs. 0.493), and CLAP-T (−0.001 vs. 0.082), which the authors characterize as comparable performance. Against the training-free baselines, ZeroSep dominates: it improves over AudioEdit (Manor & Michaeli, 2024) by 0.191 FAD and 0.200 LPAPS on MUSIC, and over NMF-MFCC by 0.909 FAD and 0.949 LPAPS on the same dataset. The key takeaway is that a purely generative, training-free pipeline matches or exceeds methods that require large-scale supervised training on paired mixture-source data, with the gap to FlowSep on AVE being the only notable exception.

## Relation to Prior Work

ZeroSep repurposes pre-trained text-guided audio diffusion models (AudioLDM, AudioLDM2, Tango) originally designed for text-to-audio generation, combining DDIM/DDPM latent inversion with classifier-free guidance at ω≤1 to perform source separation without any task-specific training. This departs fundamentally from the dominant supervised separation paradigm (LASS-Net, AudioSep, FlowSep) that trains dedicated mask estimators or flow models on large paired datasets, and from earlier diffusion-based separators (SepDiff, DiffSep) that remain 'blind' to user intent. The key conceptual leap is treating separation as a two-step generative inference—latent inversion followed by text-conditioned denoising—rather than as a learned discriminative mapping, effectively converting a generative score function into a guided filtering operator.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that a zero-training generative pipeline matches or exceeds supervised separators on MUSIC (and is competitive on AVE) challenges the field's assumption that high-quality separation requires extensive task-specific training, and the open-set text-prompt interface eliminates the need for closed-world category definitions. However, the approach is currently limited to 2-source mixtures (3-source performance degrades noticeably), relies on approximate latent inversion that can introduce errors, and is computationally slower than discriminative baselines. Open questions include whether the method scales to larger diffusion backbones, more than 3 concurrent sources, and spatial/multi-channel audio, and whether the ω=1 sweet spot generalizes across all model architectures and noise schedules.
