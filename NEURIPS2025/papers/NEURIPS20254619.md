## Problem

Existing multimodal reasoning segmentation methods either depend on expensive manually annotated reasoning chains or, in RL-based variants like Seg-Zero, decouple the reasoning model from the segmentation decoder, preventing pixel-level feedback during training. This paper asks whether a segmentation model (SAM) can be integrated directly into the RL reward loop to provide fine-grained, task-aligned supervision that eliminates the need for reasoning-annotated data while improving segmentation accuracy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254619/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes an RGB image (resized to 840×840) and a free-form text query (referring expression or complex reasoning question) as input. The MLLM (Qwen2.5VL-7B) first emits a chain-of-thought reasoning block, then a structured JSON-like answer containing a bounding box, a reference point with a positive/negative flag, and a descriptive textual cue. This structured output is fed to SAM2-Large, which produces the final pixel-level segmentation mask. The entire pipeline is trained end-to-end via RL with only 3,000 RefCOCOg samples.

## Method

SAM-R1 trains a multimodal LLM with a modified GRPO objective where SAM2-Large acts as an active reward provider rather than a passive downstream decoder. The key reward is a tiered segmentation-accuracy signal: the MLLM's predicted location is passed to SAM, and the IoU between SAM's mask and the ground-truth mask is mapped to piecewise rewards (4/3/2/0 at IoU thresholds 0.8/0.7/0.5), supplemented by reasoning-format and segmentation-format rewards. Two algorithmic modifications to GRPO are introduced: (1) asymmetric clipping that raises the upper threshold (ε_high=0.3) to allow larger updates for high-advantage actions, and (2) token-level loss normalization that divides by the total token count across the entire response group rather than per-response length, discouraging verbose low-information outputs. The KL divergence term is retained to prevent training collapse, and the full framework achieves 60.2% gIoU on ReasonSeg zero-shot with only 3K training samples.

## Results

On the ReasonSeg zero-shot benchmark (test), SAM-R1 achieves 60.2% gIoU and 54.3% cIoU, surpassing the previous best Seg-Zero (58.3% gIoU, 53.4% cIoU) by 1.9 and 0.9 points respectively, using only 3K training samples. On referring expression segmentation, SAM-R1 reaches 79.2% cIoU on RefCOCO, 74.7% on RefCOCO+, and 73.1% on RefCOCOg, matching or exceeding Seg-Zero across all three splits. On the LISA-Grounding REC benchmark, SAM-R1 achieves 63.8, dramatically outperforming the prior best Visual-RFT (43.9) by nearly 20 points without any REC-specific training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest competitor Seg-Zero-7B, SAM-R1 wins on ReasonSeg test (60.2 vs 58.3 gIoU) and RefCOCO+ (74.7 vs 73.9 cIoU), ties on RefCOCO (79.2 vs 79.2 cIoU), and trails by a marginal 0.2 points on RefCOCOg (73.1 vs 73.3 cIoU). Against LISA-7B and LISA-13B, SAM-R1 achieves large gains on ReasonSeg test (+11.5 and +6.4 gIoU respectively) and RefCOCOg (+4.6 and +2.3 cIoU), demonstrating that RL-based reasoning with SAM feedback substantially outperforms SFT-based approaches. On the RefCOCO benchmarks, SAM-R1 surpasses LAVT (75.8/68.4/62.1), ReLA (76.5/71.0/66.0), PixelLM-7B (76.5/71.7/70.5), and PerceptionGPT-7B (78.6/73.9/71.7) on at least RefCOCO+ and RefCOCOg. On the LISA-Grounding REC benchmark, SAM-R1 (63.8) outperforms Grounded-SAM (26.2), OV-Seg (28.4), X-Decoder (28.5), and Visual-RFT (43.9) by a wide margin, showing strong zero-shot generalization to grounding tasks despite no REC training data. Overall, SAM-R1 is competitive or superior to all baselines across the five evaluation benchmarks, with the most pronounced advantages on reasoning-intensive tasks (ReasonSeg, LISA-Grounding) and OOD generalization (RefCOCO+).

## Relation to Prior Work

SAM-R1 sits at the intersection of two recent lines of work: (1) MLLM-based reasoning segmentation (LISA, PixelLM, Seg-Zero), which couples a language model with a segmentation decoder via specialized tokens, and (2) RL-for-reasoning (DeepSeek-R1, GRPO, VLM-R1), which elicits chain-of-thought without explicit reasoning annotations. The key departure from Seg-Zero is architectural: rather than decoupling the reasoning model from the segmentation decoder and relying on IoU/L1 rewards computed externally, SAM-R1 embeds SAM directly inside the RL loop as a reward provider, so the MLLM receives pixel-level feedback on its own outputs. On the algorithmic side, it modifies GRPO with asymmetric clipping (raising ε_high) and token-level loss normalization to discourage verbose, low-information responses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical takeaway is that a segmentation model can serve as a free, differentiable-in-spirit reward oracle, removing the need for costly reasoning-annotated supervision and enabling strong performance from only 3k samples. Two open limitations are acknowledged: SAM's parameters remain frozen (one-way information flow, no co-adaptation), and the model cannot generate meaningful negative reference points, suggesting a gap in discriminative reasoning that may require architectural changes. Whether the SAM-as-reward-provider pattern generalizes beyond referring segmentation to video, 3D, or medical segmentation remains an open question.
