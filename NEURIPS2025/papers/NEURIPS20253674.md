## Problem

Existing vehicle trajectory learning models require separate training for each region and task, incurring high computational costs and poor performance under data scarcity. Embedding-based transferable approaches still need task-specific prediction heads and fail at region transfer because spatial features and context distributions differ fundamentally across geographic areas.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253674/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a sequence of GPS trajectory points (lng, lat, timestamp) augmented with POI and road segment textual descriptions within a 100 m radius, encoded via a pre-trained text embedding model. All three generative tasks—trajectory prediction, trajectory recovery, and OD travel time estimation—are unified into a single masking-and-recovery format: either a spatial/temporal modality of a point is masked and recovered, or entire points are replaced by a special mask token and fully reconstructed, so the same pre-trained model handles every task without architectural changes.

## Method

TransferTraj's learnable core is the Region-Transferable Trajectory Encoder (RTTE), which maps each point into four modalities (relative spatial, Fourier-encoded temporal, POI, road network) and processes them through L stacked layers, each containing a TRIE block and an SC-MoE block. TRIE, inspired by RoFormer's rotary position embedding, applies a learnable spatio-temporal rotation matrix to queries and keys so that attention scores naturally encode the relative displacement between points, preventing region-specific bias. SC-MoE routes each point through a noisy top-K gated mixture of C expert MLPs, allowing the model to specialize on movement patterns characteristic of different local spatial contexts (e.g., highway vs. dense urban). For task transferability, a pre-training procedure randomly masks sub-trajectory points entirely and masks spatial or temporal modalities of remaining points, training the model to reconstruct all masked content; this single pre-training phase enables zero-shot transfer to any of the three downstream tasks without retraining the encoder or prediction head.

## Results

On the Trajectory Prediction (TP) task, TransferTraj achieves RMSE of 187.91 m (Chengdu), 212.62 m (Xi'an), and 196.46 m (Porto), outperforming the best baseline START (333.10/319.00/260.29 m) by 43.6%, 33.4%, and 24.5% respectively. On Trajectory Recovery (TR) at μ=4ε on Chengdu, TransferTraj reaches 135.15 m RMSE vs. 181.03 m for MM-STGED (25.3% improvement). On OD Travel Time Estimation, TransferTraj achieves 2.861 min RMSE and 9.36% MAPE on Chengdu, beating DOT (3.161 min, 10.19% MAPE) by 9.5% and 8.2%. With pre-training alone (no fine-tuning), TransferTraj still outperforms SOTA baselines by 7.94%–20.18% across the three tasks, and achieves 83.70% improvement in zero-shot region transfer on TP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TransferTraj consistently outperforms all 19 baselines across all three tasks and all three datasets. On TP, it beats the strongest trajectory representation baseline START by 43.6% RMSE on Chengdu and 24.5% on Porto; notably, even the zero-shot variant (wo ft) surpasses START's fully fine-tuned results on Chengdu (223.15 vs. 333.10 m RMSE). On TR, TransferTraj improves over MM-STGED by 25.3% RMSE at μ=4ε and 31.6% at μ=16ε on Chengdu, with the gap widening as the sampling interval increases. On OD TTE, TransferTraj outperforms DOT by 9.5% RMSE on Chengdu and 23.1% on Porto, while also achieving the lowest MAPE across all regions. The embedding-based baselines (t2vec, Trembr, START, LightPath) suffer severe degradation when their pretrained encoders are frozen (wo ft variants), confirming that they cannot achieve true task transferability without retraining prediction heads, whereas TransferTraj requires no such retraining.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in transferable trajectory learning, which relies on pre-trained embedding encoders (t2vec, Trembr, START, LightPath) that map trajectories into fixed vectors and then require separate, task-specific prediction heads. TransferTraj instead unifies the input-output structure of all generative tasks (prediction, recovery, travel time estimation) into a single masking-and-recovery formulation, eliminating the need for task-specific modules. For region transferability, it repurposes the RoFormer rotary position embedding idea into a Trajectory Relative Information Extraction (TRIE) module and introduces a Spatial Context Mixture-of-Experts (SC-MoE) that routes trajectory points to context-appropriate experts, both of which are novel applications to the trajectory domain. The integration of POI and road network text embeddings as additional modalities alongside spatial and temporal features is also a new design choice not seen in prior trajectory models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a single pre-trained model can serve multiple regions and tasks without retraining, reducing both computational cost and the data requirements for new deployments. The 83.70% zero-shot region transfer improvement over SOTA embedding methods is substantial, though partly attributable to the architectural mismatch (baselines use absolute spatial features inherently non-transferable). A key limitation acknowledged by the authors is that cross-region classification tasks (e.g., destination road segment prediction) remain infeasible due to varying road network cardinalities, and the approach has only been validated on three datasets of similar scale. Open questions include whether the masking-based task unification generalizes to fundamentally different output structures (e.g., classification over variable-size vocabularies) and whether the SC-MoE routing remains effective as the number of distinct spatial contexts grows.
