## Problem

Data-driven reduced-order models learn latent dynamics purely from solver-generated snapshots, excluding the numerical solver from the training loop. This causes the learned latent manifold to drift from the true discretized physics, leading to poor generalization to unseen parameters and error accumulation beyond the training time horizon. Existing physics-informed alternatives (PINN-type residual losses, full-space PDE evaluation at inference) either suffer from optimization pathologies in nonlinear regimes or do not truly reduce the problem into a compact latent space.

## Input / Output

The model accepts an initial condition field on an arbitrary (possibly irregular or sparse) spatial grid, optional PDE parameters (e.g., Reynolds number, source-term coefficient), and a target forecast time. It outputs the full PDE solution field on any desired grid, which may differ in resolution, regularity, or even dimensionality from the input grid. Because the decoder is a conditional INR, the model is mesh-free in both space and time: it can reconstruct fields at continuous spatial coordinates and forecast to times beyond the training horizon without retraining.

## Method

Φ-ROM combines a conditional INR decoder (hyper-decoder or SIREN) for spatial reduction via auto-decoding with a parameterized MLP dynamics network for temporal evolution in a low-dimensional latent space. The key innovation is a physics-informed dynamics loss: instead of integrating the dynamics network and comparing to data (as in DINo), the method reconstructs the field on the solver grid, passes it through a differentiable PDE solver to obtain the true time derivative, projects this derivative into latent space via the decoder Jacobian pseudo-inverse (solved with QR decomposition on a stochastically sub-sampled grid for efficiency), and trains the dynamics network to match this projected target. Gradients from the dynamics loss backpropagate through the solver, regularizing both the decoder and the latent manifold to remain consistent with the governing physics. Training proceeds in two phases—a warm-up minimizing only the reconstruction loss, followed by joint optimization of reconstruction and dynamics losses weighted by a hyperparameter λ.

## Results

On the 1D Burgers' equation, Φ-ROM achieves 0.028 RNMSE on temporal extrapolation [T_tr, T_te], outperforming DINo (0.060), PINN-ROM (0.348), and AD-CROM (0.196). On 2D Navier-Stokes decaying turbulence, Φ-ROM achieves 0.373 RNMSE on test extrapolation versus 1.543 for DINo (over 4× better), and maintains 0.180 RNMSE on LBM parameter extrapolation (Re 200→300) where DINo degrades to 0.566. With only 2% sparse training observations, Φ-ROM retains near-full-grid accuracy (e.g., 0.092 RNMSE on N-S interpolation) while DINo degrades to 0.183.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DINo [13] (the primary data-driven baseline), Φ-ROM consistently wins on test-set and temporal extrapolation across all five PDEs: 0.028 vs 0.060 RNMSE on Burgers' extrapolation, 0.373 vs 1.543 on N-S test extrapolation, and 0.180 vs 0.566 on LBM parameter extrapolation. However, DINo achieves lower errors on training-set interpolation for N-S (0.036 vs 0.064) and LBM (0.011 vs 0.049), indicating Φ-ROM trades some in-distribution fit for superior generalization. Against PINN-ROM [19], Φ-ROM dramatically outperforms on the nonlinear Burgers' problem (0.028 vs 0.348 extrapolation RNMSE) where PINN-type losses suffer from spectral bias, while both are comparable on the linear Diffusion equation. Against CROM [21], Φ-ROM wins on Diffusion extrapolation (0.034 vs 0.351 for FD-CROM) and all AD-CROM variants fail on Burgers' (0.196–0.856 RNMSE), though FD-CROM achieves a near-perfect 0.001 on Burgers' interpolation by solving the full-grid PDE at every step—sacrificing the computational savings that motivate ROMs. Overall, Φ-ROM's physics-informed training via the differentiable solver provides the best generalization-to-unseen-dynamics trade-off among all compared methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

Φ-ROM extends the DINo framework (Yin et al., ICLR 2023), which pairs a conditional INR decoder with a Neural ODE for latent dynamics, by replacing the data-driven training of the dynamics network with direct supervision from a differentiable PDE solver. It also borrows the auto-decoder/inversion scheme from Park et al. (2019) for mesh-free spatial encoding and adopts stochastic hyper-reduction from computational physics (Kim et al., 2022) to make the dynamics loss computationally tractable. Conceptually, the paper carves out a new category of physics-informed ROMs distinct from both PINN-ROM (which adds PDE residual losses to the training objective) and CROM (which evaluates the PDE in physical space at inference), positioning the solver as a training-time regularizer that shapes the latent manifold rather than an inference-time constraint.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical impact is that Φ-ROM achieves robust generalization to unseen parameters and longer forecasting horizons across five PDEs and three fundamentally different solver families (finite difference, spectral, LBM), while also tolerating sparse and irregular training observations — a capability directly relevant to data assimilation and sensor-limited field reconstruction. The main limitations are the requirement for a differentiable solver (narrowing immediate applicability), increased training cost relative to purely data-driven methods, and the restriction to first-order time derivatives, leaving wave equations and 3D scalability as open questions. The open-source JAX implementation lowers the barrier for the computational physics community to adopt and extend the framework to new PDE systems.
