## Problem

Pre-training neural operators on mixed PDE datasets suffers from severe inter-task interference: mixing three different equation types can inflate test error by up to 5000% compared to single-dataset training, because dense architectures force all inputs through the same parameter space. Additionally, scaling dense models to capture diverse PDE physics requires proportionally more activated parameters at inference, making large-scale pre-training impractical for real-world deployment.

## Input / Output

The model consumes T consecutive spatiotemporal PDE solution frames of shape H×W×T×C (standardized to H=128, with channel padding and geometric mask channels for irregular domains) and predicts the next frame via auto-regression. During pre-training, Gaussian noise scaled to the input norm is injected into the frame sequence to improve robustness; at inference, the model rolls out iteratively to generate full solution trajectories. The evaluation metric is L2 relative error between predicted and ground-truth solution fields.

## Method

MoE-POT replaces the dense feed-forward network in the DPOT auto-regressive denoising operator transformer with a sparse Mixture-of-Experts layer. Each transformer block contains an AFNO-style Fourier layer followed by a MoE layer comprising 16 routed experts and 2 shared experts, all implemented as CNN subnetworks to preserve spatial structure. A learnable CNN router-gating network computes softmax logits over the 16 routed experts; only the top-4 are activated per input, and the final output is the weighted sum of the 4 routed expert outputs plus the unweighted average of the 2 shared expert outputs. A coefficient-of-variation-based load balancing loss regularizes expert utilization during training. The key insight is that the router learns to specialize experts by PDE equation type (achieving 98% dataset classification accuracy from gating decisions), effectively isolating interference between heterogeneous PDE families while shared experts capture universal physical dynamics, all while keeping activated parameters at only ~33% of total model size.

## Results

On the zero-shot pre-training evaluation across 6 PDE datasets, MoE-POT-S (90M activated params) achieves the best L2RE on 5 out of 6 datasets, reducing error by up to 57% on PDEBench-CNS(0.1,0.01) (0.00959 vs. DPOT-M's 0.0224) and 60% on CFDBench (0.00448 vs. 0.0113) compared to DPOT-M (122M activated params). After fine-tuning for 200 epochs, MoE-POT-S further reduces L2RE to 0.00742 on CNS and 0.00313 on CFDBench, outperforming all small models (FNO, UNet, FFNO, GK-T, OFormer, GNOT) on 4 out of 6 datasets. The router-gating network achieves 97.7% accuracy in classifying input data by PDE type, validating the interpretability of the MoE routing mechanism.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary baseline DPOT (the state-of-the-art pre-trained PDE operator), MoE-POT-S (90M activated) outperforms DPOT-M (122M activated) on 4 of 6 zero-shot datasets, with the largest gains on CNS (57% L2RE reduction) and CFDBench (60% reduction), while DPOT-M retains a slight edge on SWE (0.00247 vs. 0.00289) and DR (0.0288 vs. 0.0342). After fine-tuning, MoE-POT-S surpasses DPOT-M on 5 of 6 datasets, with DR being the sole exception (0.0182 vs. 0.0175). Against dense FNO variants, FNO-M (150M) completely fails on CNS and CFDBench (error > 20), while MoE-POT-S achieves valid predictions on all six datasets. In the fine-tuned setting, MoE-POT-S outperforms all small models (FNO, UNet, FFNO, GK-T, OFormer, GNOT) on 4 of 6 datasets, achieving the best L2RE on NS(1e-5), NS(1e-3), CNS, and CFDBench. Inference time analysis shows MoE-POT-M (489M total params) runs in 16.6 ms per step, only 68% of DPOT-L (493M params, 24.3 ms), confirming the efficiency advantage of sparse activation.

## Relation to Prior Work

MoE-POT is a direct architectural modification of DPOT (Hao et al., ICML 2024), replacing its dense feed-forward network with a sparse Mixture-of-Experts layer that combines 16 routed experts (top-4 selected) and 2 shared experts per block. The shared+routed expert design and load-balancing objective are adapted from DeepSeekMoE (Dai et al., 2024) and the original sparsely-gated MoE (Shazeer et al., 2017), while the Fourier layer follows AFNO (Guibas et al., 2021). The key conceptual departure from prior PDE pre-training work (DPOT, Poseidon, MPP) is the use of sparse activation to simultaneously address dataset heterogeneity (via expert specialization) and inference cost (via parameter decoupling), rather than relying on dense width/depth scaling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical recipe for scaling PDE pre-training models without proportional inference cost increases, which is critical for real-world deployment where thousands of rollout steps are needed. The 98% PDE-type classification accuracy from the router-gating network is an intriguing interpretability signal suggesting MoE routing implicitly encodes equation-type information, though the mathematical mechanism behind this remains unexplored. Key open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond the 6 tested datasets to truly diverse PDE families, how the number of experts should scale with dataset diversity, and whether the implicit classification can be leveraged to construct more effective pre-training curricula. The paper acknowledges it has not analyzed the mathematical essence of the classification mechanism, leaving a gap between empirical observation and theoretical understanding.
