## Problem

Earth observation foundation models (EOFMs) have demonstrated strong generalization across remote sensing tasks, yet their robustness under real-world perturbations—such as atmospheric degradation, sensor noise, and geometric distortions—remains systematically unexplored. No prior benchmark has evaluated how these models degrade under realistic corruptions across diverse tasks and model architectures, leaving a critical gap for deploying reliable models in high-stakes applications like disaster response and urban planning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252052/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark takes high-resolution optical remote sensing images from four datasets (AID, ISPRS Potsdam, DIOR, VRSBench) and applies 12 synthetic corruptions across three categories (environmental, sensor-induced, geometric) at five severity levels. For vision-language tasks (captioning, VQA, visual grounding), geometric corruptions are excluded since they alter image content; text prompts are used alongside corrupted images. The output is a per-corruption task performance score (accuracy, mIoU, mAP, CLAIR, or grounding accuracy) plus a formal relative task performance drop metric R_TP that quantifies degradation relative to clean-image performance.

## Method

REOBench constructs a corruption suite of 12 types—cloud, brightness/contrast, haze (environmental); Gaussian/motion blur, Gaussian/salt-pepper noise, data gaps, compression artifacts (sensor-induced); rotation, scale, translation (geometric)—each applied at five severity levels using physically or statistically grounded augmentation procedures. The benchmark evaluates 15+ models spanning three pre-training paradigms: MIM-based (SatMAE, ScaleMAE, RVSA, SatMAE++, SATLAS), CL-based (RemoteCLIP, GeoRSCLIP at ViT-B and ViT-L), and LLM-based (GeoChat, VHM, RS-LLaVA, SkySenseGPT, Falcon, LHRS-Bot, GeoGround). For MIM and CL models, task-specific heads (linear classifier, UpperNet segmentor, Oriented R-CNN detector) are fine-tuned on pretrained backbones for 12 epochs; for LLM-based models, zero-shot performance is evaluated directly. The paper further extends the evaluation to compound perturbations (simultaneous corruptions) and preliminary multispectral experiments on fMoW-Sentinel2 and BigEarthNet, revealing that MIM-based models suffer the largest drops (up to 34%) while vision-language models maintain drops below 10% in most settings.

## Results

Across six remote sensing tasks and twelve corruption types, REOBench reveals that MIM-based EOFMs suffer the most severe degradation, with relative performance drops (R_TP) ranging from 16.92% (SatMAE) to 34.69% (RVSA) in scene classification. CL-based models (RemoteCLIP, GeoRSCLIP) and LLM-based models (VHM, SkySenseGPT, LHRS-Bot) are substantially more robust, with R_TP values typically between 4.81% and 8.55% for scene classification. In vision-language tasks (captioning, VQA, grounding), LLM-based models show the smallest drops, with RS-LLaVA achieving only 2.03% R_TP in captioning and VHM 1.33% in VQA. The most damaging single corruption is motion blur (causing ~60% drop for MIM models in scene classification), while translation has the least impact.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The benchmark evaluates 15 EOFMs across three pre-training families. In scene classification, GeoRSCLIP (ViT-L) achieves the highest corrupted accuracy (91.80% avg) with only 5.74% R_TP, outperforming all MIM-based models by 15–29 percentage points in average corrupted accuracy; VHM (LLM-based) shows the smallest relative drop (4.81%) but lower absolute accuracy (76.72%). For semantic segmentation, ScaleMAE leads in corrupted mIoU (60.02%) while GeoRSCLIP (ViT-B) is most robust (10.01% R_TP), with MIM models dropping 12.91–16.67% versus CL models at 10.01–13.77%. In object detection, SatMAE++ is most robust (8.79% R_TP) and RVSA, despite the highest clean mAP (70.96%), suffers the largest drop (11.91%). For vision-language tasks, RS-LLaVA is most robust in captioning (2.03% R_TP), VHM in VQA (1.33%), and GeoGround in grounding (4.48%), while Falcon achieves the highest absolute captioning score (58.01% CLAIR) but the largest drop (6.28%). Overall, CL- and LLM-based models consistently outperform MIM-based models in robustness by 10–25 percentage points in R_TP, with the gap most pronounced in classification and least in segmentation/detection.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the corruption-robustness benchmarking tradition established by ImageNet-C (Hendrycks & Dietterich, 2019) to the remote sensing domain, and specifically to foundation models rather than task-specific classifiers. Prior robustness studies in remote sensing (Mei et al. 2023, Lian et al. 2022, Kazmi et al. 2023) focused on adversarial attacks or perturbations against individually trained detection/classification models; REOBench is the first to systematically evaluate pre-trained foundation models (MIM-based, CL-based, and LLM-based) under a unified corruption protocol. The benchmark draws on the rapidly growing EOFM landscape—SatMAE, ScaleMAE, RVSA, SatMAE++ for MIM; RemoteCLIP, GeoRSCLIP for contrastive learning; GeoChat, VHM, RS-LLaVA, SkySenseGPT, Falcon for vision-language—treating them as evaluation subjects rather than proposing a new architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

REOBench provides the first standardized diagnostic for a critical gap: EOFMs deployed in high-stakes applications (disaster response, urban planning) have never been stress-tested against the sensor and atmospheric degradations they will encounter in the field. The finding that MIM-based models lose over 25% accuracy in scene classification under common corruptions, while vision-language models stay below 10%, gives practitioners concrete guidance on model selection and highlights a clear research direction for robustness-aware pre-training. Key limitations include restriction to high-resolution optical imagery (no SAR, hyperspectral, or full multispectral evaluation beyond two preliminary datasets), exclusion of tasks like change detection and object counting, and the absence of error bars or multi-seed runs, which limits the statistical confidence in fine-grained comparisons.
