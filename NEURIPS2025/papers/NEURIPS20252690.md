## Problem

Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) applies a constant guidance scale across all trajectories and timesteps, which harms sample diversity and induces memorization by over-correcting samples that are already well-aligned with the condition. The paper asks whether guidance can be made state-dependent so that each trajectory receives only as much correction as it actually needs, derived from first principles rather than empirical heuristics.

## Input / Output

The method operates on pre-trained conditional and unconditional diffusion models (EDM2-XS at 512×512 for class-conditional generation, Stable Diffusion 2 for text-to-image) and takes a conditioning signal (class label or text prompt) plus initial Gaussian noise as input. At each denoising step it produces both the next less-noisy state and a dynamically computed guidance scale λ(x_t, t) that varies per trajectory and per timestep, with negligible additional computational cost since all required quantities are already computed during standard denoising.

## Method

FBG reinterprets guidance as inverting a systematic error model: while CFG implicitly assumes the learned conditional is a multiplicative mixture of the true conditional and unconditional distributions, FBG assumes an additive mixture p_θ,t(x|c) = π·p_t(x|c) + (1−π)·p_t(x). Inverting this additive error yields a state- and time-dependent guidance scale λ(x_t, t) = [p(c|x_t)/p(c)] / [p(c|x_t)/p(c) − (1−π)] that equals 1 when the posterior is high and grows as the posterior drops toward 1−π. The required posterior p_θ,t(c|x_t) is estimated iteratively by tracking the diffusion Markov chain, comparing conditional and unconditional prediction errors at each step weighted by the inverse transition variance, with a linear bias term δ to correct for self-reference. The three abstract hyperparameters (π, τ, δ) are reparameterized into intuitive normalized diffusion times t_0 and t_1 that directly control where guidance activates and peaks, and the scheme composes additively with existing methods like CFG or LIG.

## Results

On ImageNet 512×512 with EDM2-XS (64 NFE), FBG_pure achieves FID 3.76 (stochastic) and 2.50 (PFODE), substantially outperforming CFG (5.00 / 2.97) and remaining competitive with LIG (3.59 / 2.31). On FD_DinoV2, FBG_pure reaches 89.0 (stochastic) and 75.6 (PFODE), while the hybrid FBG_LIG further improves to 87.9 and 74.6, slightly beating LIG (88.5 / 77.1). On MS-COCO text-to-image with Stable Diffusion 2, FBG_pure attains FID 18.63 and FD_DinoV2 53.11, outperforming CFG (19.64 / 54.56) and LIG (18.81 / 54.25), with FBG_CFG further reducing FD_DinoV2 to 52.14.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FBG_pure decisively outperforms CFG on both FID (3.76 vs. 5.00 stochastic; 2.50 vs. 2.97 PFODE) and FD_DinoV2 (89.0 vs. 100.2; 75.6 vs. 88.4), confirming that a fixed guidance scale is suboptimal. Against the linear weight scheduler (Wang et al.), FBG_pure wins on every reported metric, with a particularly large gap on FD_DinoV2 (89.0 vs. 103.1 stochastic). FBG_pure also surpasses CFG++ on the PFODE sampler (FID 2.50 vs. 3.66; FD_DinoV2 75.6 vs. 87.8), though CFG++ was not originally designed for class-conditional generation. Compared to LIG, FBG_pure is slightly behind on FID (3.76 vs. 3.59 stochastic; 2.50 vs. 2.31 PFODE) but matches or exceeds it on FD_DinoV2 and Precision-Recall, achieving CFG-like recall (0.76) with higher precision (0.86). The hybrid FBG_LIG combination edges out LIG on FD_DinoV2 (87.9 vs. 88.5 stochastic; 74.6 vs. 77.1 PFODE), demonstrating that the two schemes capture complementary aspects of the guidance problem.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reinterprets Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) through an error-model lens, showing that CFG implicitly assumes a multiplicative mixture between the learned conditional and unconditional distributions. By replacing this with an additive mixture assumption, the authors derive a state- and time-dependent guidance scale (FBG) from first principles, in contrast to the fixed or pre-scheduled scales used in CFG, LIG, and weight-scheduler variants. The posterior estimation mechanism is adapted from the Dynamic Negative Guidance framework (Koulischer et al., 2025), and the approach is designed to compose with existing schemes like LIG or base CFG rather than replace them entirely.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FBG challenges the prevailing view of guidance as a fixed global hyperparameter by showing that a principled error model naturally yields per-trajectory, per-prompt adaptive guidance with negligible computational overhead. The theoretical observation that CFG's multiplicative mixing does not commute with the noising operation (unlike the additive case) provides a concrete mathematical justification for why constant-scale guidance can be misleading at high noise levels. However, the additive error model is acknowledged as a simplification, evaluation is limited to EDM2-XS and MS-COCO, and the empirical gains over the already-strong LIG baseline are modest, leaving open whether more precise error models or larger-scale validation will yield substantially different conclusions.
