## Problem

Vision-Language Models pretrained on internet-scale image-text corpora lack the fine-grained spatial and interaction knowledge required for robotic manipulation, yet existing VQA benchmarks for robotics either rely on simulation (suffering from sim-to-real gaps) or demand extensive manual annotation. This paper asks whether the rich proprioceptive and kinematic signals already embedded in teleoperated robot trajectories can be automatically exploited to generate grounded VQA that benchmarks and improves VLM spatial and interaction reasoning.

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes time-synchronized multi-modal robot frames—multi-view RGB (up to 640×480), stereo image pairs, 6-DoF end-effector poses in SE(3), scalar gripper aperture, and 6-DoF force-torque vectors—along with a natural-language task instruction per trajectory. It outputs five-way multiple-choice VQA pairs (4–5 answer options, ~109 characters per question on average) whose correct answers are derived directly from sensor ground truth rather than human labels, organized into 11 sub-categories across spatial, goal-conditioned, and interaction reasoning. The curated Robo2VLM-1 dataset contains 684,710 such questions spanning 463 scenes, 3,396 tasks, and 149 manipulation skills drawn from 176k OXE trajectories.

## Method

Robo2VLM is a three-stage data generation framework. First, a temporal-logic phase classifier segments each trajectory into manipulation phases (approach, stabilize, contact, release, reset, transition) using thresholded rules over normalized gripper aperture, its temporal derivative, and force magnitude, with a partial-order constraint enforcing unidirectional phase progression. Second, keyframes are selected at phase transitions and filtered for scene coverage diversity and multi-view object/robot visibility. Third, structured question prototypes—each tied to a specific phase and sensor modality—are instantiated on selected keyframes and converted into natural-language multiple-choice questions via a visual-language grounding module that projects 3D spatial queries into 2D image space and generates plausible distractors (e.g., angularly separated arrows, depth-differentiated points, or 'None of the Above' replacements at 20% probability). Ground-truth answers come from the robot's own proprioceptive and kinematic data, eliminating manual annotation. The authors apply this to 176k Open X-Embodiment trajectories, generating over 3 million raw VQA pairs, then curate the balanced 684k-question Robo2VLM-1 subset using domain reweighting across scenes, tasks, and reasoning types.

## Results

On the Robo2VLM-1 benchmark (684,710 questions from 176k real robot trajectories), Qwen 2.5 VL-72B achieves the highest zero-shot overall accuracy at 37.76%, while Qwen 2.5 VL-32B achieves the best CoT accuracy at 41.30%. LLaVA 1.5-7B scores only 21.58% zero-shot, and Llama 3.2-90B reaches 28.60% zero-shot / 30.45% CoT. Fine-tuning LLaVA 1.6 with 50k Robo2VLM-1 samples yields up to 50% accuracy gains in state reasoning and task understanding categories (e.g., Object State from 29.34% to 80.24%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Qwen 2.5 VL models dominate the benchmark: the 72B variant leads zero-shot (37.76%) and the 32B variant leads CoT (41.30%), outperforming Llama 3.2-90B (28.60% / 30.45%) by roughly 9–11 points and LLaVA 1.5-7B (21.58%) by 16+ points overall. GPT-4o (41.5% overall, Table 8) is competitive with Qwen 2.5 VL-32B CoT (41.30%), confirming the benchmark's difficulty. CoT prompting consistently benefits Qwen and Llama (e.g., Qwen 7B: 30.63→34.82%) but can hurt LLaVA (34B drops from 24.94% to 23.49%), suggesting weaker base reasoning in smaller LLaVA variants. In fine-tuning experiments, LLaVA 1.6 improves substantially with Robo2VLM-1 training data (Object State: 29.34%→80.24%; Task State-success: 47.65%→68.03%), narrowing but not closing the gap to Qwen. Human evaluators achieve 96.7% on Object State and 80.0% on Interaction Phase, while the best model (Qwen 32B CoT) reaches 90.5% and 71.35% respectively, revealing a persistent gap in fine-grained spatial reasoning (human 60% vs. best model 19.42% on Spatial Relationship).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes large-scale teleoperated robot trajectory data (from Open X-Embodiment) — originally collected to train visuomotor policies — as a source of ground-truth for generating VQA benchmarks, inverting the common paradigm where VLMs assist robots. It departs from simulation-based VQA generators (EQA-MX, EmbodiedBench) that suffer from the sim-to-real gap, and from human-annotated real-world benchmarks (RoboVQA) that are costly to scale, by exploiting non-visual proprioceptive modalities (gripper aperture, end-effector pose, force-torque) as automatic answer keys. The curation strategy draws on domain-reweighting ideas from DoReMi and Remix, but applies them to balance VQA diversity across scenes, tasks, and reasoning categories rather than language domains.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Robo2VLM-1 (684k questions, 463 scenes, 3,396 tasks) is a substantial open benchmark that exposes a concrete and large gap in VLM spatial reasoning (e.g., ~19% model vs. 60% human on fine-grained spatial relationships) and demonstrates that fine-tuning on robot-derived VQA can yield up to 50% accuracy gains in state reasoning. Key limitations include template-based question generation that constrains linguistic diversity, dependence on trajectory calibration quality, and missing modalities in some source datasets (e.g., NYU VINN). Open questions include whether the gains transfer to downstream robotic control tasks, whether the template format limits generalization beyond the benchmark, and how the framework extends to embodiments without force-torque or stereo sensors.
