## Problem

Designing effective black-box optimizers requires expert knowledge spanning algorithm structure and hyperparameter tuning, a process that is neither scalable nor practical for new problem instances. Existing MetaBBO approaches address only one sub-task of automated algorithm design—either workflow generation or dynamic hyperparameter control—resulting in sub-optimal designs. This paper tackles the gap by proposing the first framework that jointly learns both sub-tasks in an end-to-end fashion.

## Input / Output

The system takes a 13-dimensional problem feature vector (4 basic properties: dimension, maxFEs, bounds; plus 9 ELA landscape statistics) as input to Agent-1, which autoregressively emits a sequence of module-variant tokens (from 116 variants across 10 module types) forming a legal optimizer workflow via a Program Structure Tree pre-order traversal. During optimization, Agent-2 receives a 9-dimensional progress vector at each step and outputs per-module hyperparameter values sampled from predicted Gaussian distributions, with a predefined maximum configuration size N_max to handle modules with varying numbers of parameters.

## Method

DesignX employs a dual-agent reinforcement learning system built on GPT-2 blocks: Agent-1 (π_φ) autoregressively generates optimizer workflows using a masked softmax that enforces topology rules to guarantee legality, while Agent-2 (π_θ) dynamically controls hyperparameters by encoding module IDs and optimization progress into embeddings, processing them through attention layers, and decoding mean/covariance parameters for Gaussian sampling. The modular space (Modular-EC) extends prior Modular-BBO by integrating operators from ES, GA, and PSO into 10 module types with 116 variants, yielding millions of possible workflows. Training uses a cooperative objective where Agent-1 is optimized via REINFORCE (episodic reward, no intermediate signal) and Agent-2 via PPO (per-step reward), jointly maximizing cumulative optimization performance across 12,800 diverse synthetic instances. The key architectural insight is that the two agents are mutually conditioned—Agent-2's parameter decisions depend on Agent-1's generated workflow structure—enabling the discovery of non-trivial algorithm patterns that surpass human-crafted optimizers by orders of magnitude.

## Results

On the in-distribution synthetic test set (3200 instances), DesignX achieves a normalized averaged objective of 0.0826 ± 0.175, outperforming the best human-crafted optimizer group (post-2020: 0.146 ± 0.235) and the best MetaBBO baseline (MetaB+: 0.132 ± 0.736). DesignX ranks first on nearly all 20 showcased test instances with diverse properties (5D–50D, 10k–50k FEs, unimodal/multimodal, weak/adequate global structure). On out-of-distribution realistic benchmarks (Protein-Docking, HPO-B, UAV path planning), DesignX consistently produces superior optimization curves compared to all human-crafted and MetaBBO baselines across 51 independent runs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DesignX outperforms all four groups of human-crafted optimizers (pre-2000 GA/PSO/DE, 2000s CMA-ES/SaDE/JADE, 2010s SHADE/LM-CMA-ES/GLPSO, post-2020 MadDE/jDE21/MMES/NL-SHADE-LBC) on the normalized averaged objective (0.083 vs. best 0.146 for post-2020 group), with particularly dramatic margins on high-dimensional and multimodal instances (e.g., 2.61E-08 vs. 8.41E-01 on F545UWL). Against MetaBBO baselines (GLHF, DEDQN, GLEET), DesignX achieves a 37% improvement in normalized objective (0.083 vs. 0.132), confirming that jointly learning workflow generation and hyperparameter control surpasses single-sub-task approaches. On the three out-of-distribution realistic benchmarks (Protein-Docking, HPO-B, UAV), DesignX consistently produces the best optimization curves, validating generalization beyond the synthetic training distribution. In the rank-based critical difference diagram (Figure 8), CMA-ES and DesignX are statistically indistinguishable in relative rank, though DesignX dominates in absolute performance, suggesting CMA-ES remains a strong but non-adaptive competitor. The ablation study further confirms that removing either agent degrades performance, with workflow generation (Agent-1) contributing more than hyperparameter control (Agent-2) to overall quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends ConfigX's Modular-BBO modularization system into the broader Modular-EC framework by integrating ES, GA, and PSO operators alongside DE, and departs from prior MetaBBO approaches (DEDQN, GLHF, GLEET, ALDes) that address only one sub-task of automated algorithm design—either workflow generation or hyperparameter control. The key architectural departure is a dual-agent RL system (REINFORCE for autoregressive workflow sampling, PPO for per-step hyperparameter adjustment) trained under a cooperative objective, rather than the single-agent or sequential formulations used previously. The Transformer/GPT-2 backbone for both agents follows the trend in recent MetaBBO work (ConfigX, Q-Mamba) but applies it to the joint design problem for the first time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DesignX demonstrates that a small (1-layer GPT-2) dual-agent model trained on 10k synthetic instances can generate optimizers that consistently outperform decades of human-crafted EC algorithms across diverse realistic benchmarks (protein docking, AutoML, UAV planning), and it surfaces non-trivial design principles (e.g., initialization strategy matters little; population reduction is critical for small search ranges) that feed back into the EC community. Key limitations include restriction to single-objective optimization, 6-day CPU-bound training, and the rank-based comparison revealing CMA-ES remains competitive on average, suggesting the 'orders of magnitude' gap is concentrated on specific problem types. Open questions include extending Modular-EC to multi-objective and constrained settings, improving training efficiency via multi-GPU or distillation, and whether the discovered design patterns transfer to fundamentally different optimization paradigms.
