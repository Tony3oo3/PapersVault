## Problem

Prior zero-shot semantic correspondence work using diffusion features has only explored UNet-based Stable Diffusion, leaving the newer rectified flow-based SD3 (with its MM-DiT transformer backbone) unexplored for correspondence. Additionally, naively concatenating features from heterogeneous pretrained models (SD3, SD, DINO) produces inconsistent feature distributions that degrade matching performance, and no training-free mechanism existed to adaptively integrate such diverse representations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250494/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of RGB images and a coarse text prompt of the form 'A photo of a {object category}.' Each foundation model processes the image at its native training resolution (1024×1024 for SD3, 768×768 for SD, 840×840 for DINOv2), and the resulting feature maps are bilinearly upsampled to the original image size. The fused representation is a dense h×w×12,032 feature map (9728 from SD3, 1280 from SD, 1024 from DINOv2), from which correspondences are obtained via cosine-distance nearest-neighbor search, yielding per-pixel match locations and similarity heat maps.

## Method

The paper first identifies that SD3's semantic signals are dispersed across multiple timesteps and transformer layers, so it extracts features from three timesteps (521, 621, 721) and two MM-DiT blocks (24, 25), using the query and value vectors from the image-branch attention as the matching features. To fuse SD3, SD, and DINOv2 features without training, it computes a per-pixel confidence score for each model defined as the gap between the maximum and mean of that model's similarity vector to all target pixels (w = max(M) − avg(M)). Each model's feature vector is then re-weighted by its normalized confidence before concatenation, so that at every pixel the model with the sharpest (most confident) matching distribution contributes more prominently. This confidence-aware, pixel-wise adaptive fusion is entirely training-free and generalizes across different model combinations, consistently outperforming naive equal-weight concatenation.

## Results

On SPair-71k (PCK@0.1), Fuse2Match achieves 68.8% overall, outperforming the best zero-shot single-model baseline (SD3 at 59.8%) by ~9 points and the prior multi-model fusion SD+DINO (63.3%) by 5.5 points. On PF-Pascal, Fuse2Match reaches 90.6% PCK@0.1 (vs. 86.5% for SD3 alone and 85.7% for SD+DINO). On the cross-domain PSC6K sketch-photo benchmark, Fuse2Match attains 74.31% PCK@0.1, surpassing SD3 (67.50%) and DINOv1 (65.05%) by a substantial margin, demonstrating strong cross-domain generalization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among zero-shot single-model baselines, SD3 (59.8%) marginally edges out DIFT/SD2 (59.4%) and DINOv2 (59.0%), while contrastive models DINOv1 (42.5%), OpenCLIP (41.4%), and SigLip (42.8%) trail significantly. For multi-model fusion, Fuse2Match (68.8%) beats naive concatenation of the same three models (SD+SD3+DINOv2, 67.9%) by 0.9 points, and outperforms the prior SD+DINO fusion (63.3%) by 5.5 points. On PF-Pascal, Fuse2Match (90.6%) surpasses SD3 alone (86.5%) and SD+DINO (85.7%), while on PSC6K it leads all baselines by a wide margin (74.31% vs. 67.50% for SD3 and 65.05% for DINOv1). Against supervised methods, Fuse2Match still lags behind SD4Match (75.5%) but exceeds DHF (64.9%) and CATs++ (59.9%), and is competitive with the unsupervised ISCVGSM (67.3%). The confidence-aware fusion consistently improves over naive concatenation across all model combinations tested (Table 5), confirming the generality of the adaptive weighting mechanism.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DIFT (Tang et al., 2023) and SD+DINO (Zhang et al., 2024) line of work, which demonstrated that features from UNet-based Stable Diffusion and contrastive ViTs (DINO) can be directly used for zero-shot semantic correspondence via naive concatenation. The key departure is twofold: (i) migrating the feature-extraction paradigm from UNet-based SD to the rectified-flow, MM-DiT-based SD3, which requires a fundamentally different multi-timestep, multi-layer, multi-facet extraction strategy since semantic signals are scattered rather than concentrated; and (ii) replacing the equal-weight concatenation of SD+DINO with a confidence-aware, per-pixel re-weighting scheme that adaptively integrates heterogeneous model contributions based on matching uncertainty.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides practical evidence that the latest generation of text-to-image models (SD3) can serve as zero-shot correspondence backbones without any task-specific fine-tuning, and that a simple training-free fusion mechanism can consistently outperform naive concatenation across diverse model pairings. The confidence-aware weighting is lightweight and generalizable, making it an adoptable component for any multi-model feature fusion pipeline. However, the coarse text prompt ("A photo of a {object category}") is acknowledged as a limitation that likely caps SD3's fine-grained matching potential, and the 12,032-dimensional fused feature map introduces significant computational overhead. Open questions include whether prompt tuning or learned adapters could further unlock SD3's representational capacity, and whether the confidence signal generalizes to more extreme domain shifts beyond photo-sketch.
