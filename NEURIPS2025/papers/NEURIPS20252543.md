## Problem

Evaluating preference optimization (PO) algorithms for LLM alignment is prohibitively expensive, noisy, and confounded by model size and hyperparameters, making it difficult to identify which algorithms work best under different data conditions. Existing PO methods (DPO, ORPO, SimPO, etc.) exhibit distinct failure modes on noisy or mixed-quality preference data, yet no systematic framework exists for discovering new objectives tailored to specific dataset properties.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252543/cropped_page5_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes offline preference datasets consisting of trajectory pairs (chosen τ_w, rejected τ_l) sharing a common starting state, along with a pre-trained reference policy. In the MuJoCo diagnostic setting, datasets contain 1280 pairs of length-1000 trajectories with controllable noise (label-flip probability ε) and mixed-quality composition (random agent selection). The output is an optimized policy evaluated by ground-truth cumulative reward in MuJoCo, or an aligned LLM evaluated via AlpacaEval win-rates against GPT-4. A key intermediate output is the discovered parametrization of the loss functions g, ψ, and φ⁻¹, which can be transferred across tasks.

## Method

The paper introduces Mirror Preference Optimization (MPO), a family of offline PO algorithms that replace the KL-divergence regularization in the policy optimization objective with a Bregman divergence induced by an ω-potential mirror map, yielding a unified framework that recovers DPO (two-step) and ORPO (one-step) as special cases. The functions g (preference loss), ψ (SFT term), and φ⁻¹ (mirror map inverse) are parametrized as one-layer neural networks with non-negative kernels and monotonic activations, then searched via OpenAI-ES where the outer loop evaluates the final policy's ground-truth reward and the inner loop trains the policy on the preference dataset. A key empirical finding is that discovered objectives continue to produce large gradient updates even when π(τ_w) > π(τ_l), unlike hand-crafted baselines that flatten in this regime. Leveraging this insight, the authors design TeMPO, a temporally-aware single-phase algorithm that linearly or sigmoidally shifts weight from an SFT loss to a SimPO-based preference loss over training progress, achieving the best AlpacaEval win-rates across all tested base-model and dataset combinations.

## Results

On the Three-Legged Ant (TLA) MuJoCo benchmark, the best discovered 1S-MPO algorithm (parametrized g, discovered on noisy ε=0.3) achieves a cumulative reward of 3931±69 on the Base setting (matching the target agent's 3900) and 3417±82 on Noisy(ε=0.3), where the best human-designed baseline (CPO) only reaches 2000±35. On the LLM alignment task (AlpacaEval), TeMPO(3) achieves the highest win rate across all three model-dataset combinations: 35.4±1.7 (gemma-7b/dpo-mix-7k), 39.4±1.7 (gemma-7b/capybara-7k), and 41.6±1.7 (mistral-7b/dpo-mix-7k), outperforming DPO by 4.7–8.8 points. TeMPO also requires only a single training stage, whereas DPO and SimPO require separate SFT and PO phases.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares 10 baselines (RRHF, SLiC-HF, DPO, IPO, CPO, ORPO, R-DPO, SimPO, KTO, f-DPO) against discovered MPO algorithms on TLA and TeMPO on LLM alignment. On TLA, no human-designed algorithm recovers the target agent's performance (3900); SimPO leads on Base (3683) and Mixed Quality (3117) but catastrophically fails on Noisy(ε=0.3) (−3828), while CPO is the most noise-robust baseline (2000 at ε=0.3) yet still trails the best discovered 1S-MPO by 1417 points. DPO and IPO are competitive on Base and low-noise settings (3528 and 3618 on Base) but degrade sharply at ε=0.3 (1519 and 1133). KTO performs worst overall (1534 on Base), and f-DPO (Jensen-Shannon) is competitive on Base (3621) but drops to 1633 at ε=0.3. On AlpacaEval, TeMPO(3) outperforms DPO by 4.7–8.8 win-rate points and SimPO by 7.5–13.5 points across all three model-dataset combinations, while also eliminating the need for a separate SFT pre-training stage.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalizes DPO and ORPO through a mirror descent framework, replacing the KL-divergence regularization with a Bregman divergence induced by a flexible ω-potential mirror map. It departs from the hand-crafted design of existing PO objectives (CPO, SimPO, IPO, etc.) by parameterizing the loss components (g, ψ, φ⁻¹) as neural networks and searching over them with evolutionary strategies, following the algorithm-discovery paradigm of Lu et al. (2022) and Jackson et al. (2024). The key conceptual departure from prior PO work is the finding that objectives which truncate or flatten when π(τ_w) > π(τ_l) are suboptimal, motivating the temporally-aware TeMPO algorithm that gradually shifts from SFT to a non-truncating PO loss.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The MuJoCo-based diagnostic suite (Three-Legged-Ant, Hopper) offers a cheaper, lower-noise, ground-truth-available alternative to the prohibitively expensive and noisy LLM evaluation loop, enabling systematic comparison of PO algorithms under controlled noise and mixed-quality conditions. The 'keep optimizing' insight—that loss truncation at π(τ_w) > π(τ_l) is harmful—challenges a widespread design choice across DPO, SLiC-HF, and related methods, and the TeMPO algorithm demonstrates that this principle transfers to LLM alignment. A notable limitation is that the fully meta-learned objectives with parameterized g overfit to the MuJoCo task and fail on LLMs, suggesting the transferability of discovered algorithms remains an open question. Future work could explore whether the mirror descent framework combined with better regularization or larger search spaces yields algorithms that transfer more robustly across domains.
