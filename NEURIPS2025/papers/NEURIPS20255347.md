## Problem

Active target discovery in partially observable environments (e.g., medical imaging, remote sensing) requires strategically selecting where to sample under a limited budget, but prior approaches either assume full observability, depend on large-scale pre-labeled datasets for supervised RL training, or rely on opaque black-box policies. The paper asks whether one can design a training-free, interpretable algorithm that balances exploration and exploitation to uncover targets using only online feedback from a small number of costly measurements.

## Input / Output

The method takes a partially observed image (128×128 or 256×256, RGB or grayscale) divided into grid cells, a pre-trained unsupervised diffusion model (U-Net-based DDIM), and a measurement budget B (typically 100–300). At each step it outputs the next grid cell to measure, which reveals the fraction of target pixels in that cell; the cumulative output is the sequence of selected locations and the total number of target pixels discovered. The reward model consumes small local patches (4×4 for most datasets, 1×1 for MNIST) as its input feature.

## Method

DiffATD maintains a belief distribution over unobserved regions as a mixture of N_B isotropic Gaussians whose means are estimated via Tweedie's formula from a batch of particles undergoing measurement-guided reverse diffusion (DDIM). Exploration selects the cell with maximum particle disagreement (highest expected entropy, Theorem 1), while exploitation selects the cell with highest reward-weighted expected log-likelihood (particle consensus × incrementally trained reward model, Theorem 3 shows this equals greedy expected-reward maximization). A budget-aware coefficient κ(B) = (B−t)/(B+t) linearly shifts the combined score from exploration-dominated early in the search to exploitation-dominated late, requiring no task-specific supervised training for the main policy. The only learned component is a small CNN reward model (1 conv + 5 FC layers) updated online via binary cross-entropy after each measurement, making the entire pipeline training-free with respect to the target category.

## Results

DiffATD is evaluated using Success Rate (SR) across six datasets (DOTA, iNaturalist, ISIC skin imaging, NIH Chest X-Ray, CelebA, MNIST) under varying measurement budgets. On DOTA (B=300), DiffATD achieves 0.7309 SR, outperforming Max-Ent (0.6091) and Greedy-Adaptive (0.6550) by 20% and 11% respectively. On the ISIC skin disease dataset (B=150), DiffATD reaches 0.9061 SR versus 0.8396 for GA. On MNIST (B=200), DiffATD achieves 0.9682 SR, far exceeding Max-Ent (0.8325) and GA (0.5820). Overall, DiffATD improves over the strongest baseline by 7–60% depending on the dataset and budget.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiffATD consistently outperforms all three internal baselines (Random Search, Max-Ent, Greedy-Adaptive) across every dataset and budget, with the largest gains on structured targets like MNIST digits (0.9682 vs. 0.8325 for Max-Ent at B=200) and chest X-ray bone suppression (0.4142 vs. 0.2211 for GA at B=300). Against the fully supervised SAM (FullSEG) operating under full observability, DiffATD wins on rare targets (skin disease: 0.8642 vs. 0.6221) but trails on common aerial objects (DOTA: 0.7309 vs. 0.8731), despite never seeing the full image and requiring no labeled training data. DiffATD also surpasses GPT-4o (0.5678) and Gemini (0.6453) on DOTA at B=300, achieving 0.7309 SR, demonstrating that its principled exploration-exploitation balance via Bayesian experiment design outperforms general-purpose vision-language models in the active target discovery setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the diffusion posterior sampling framework (Chung et al., 2022; Ho et al., 2020) — originally designed for solving inverse problems — as a belief representation mechanism for active target discovery. It departs from prior active search methods (Garnett et al., 2012; Jiang et al., 2017) that require full observability, and from recent partially observable approaches (Sarkar et al., 2023, 2024) that depend on large-scale pre-labeled datasets for RL training. The key conceptual move is using a batch of diffusion particles to form a Gaussian mixture belief distribution, then deriving closed-form exploration (maximum entropy) and exploitation (reward-weighted log-likelihood) scores from particle disagreement, grounded in Bayesian experiment design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The elimination of supervised training data is practically significant for domains where labeled examples of rare targets (e.g., rare tumors, endangered species) are infeasible to obtain, and the white-box policy offers interpretability that black-box RL policies lack. Open questions include scalability to very large search spaces (the paper tests up to 256×256), robustness under noisy or adversarial observations, and whether the diffusion-based belief representation remains computationally tractable as the number of particles or the search space dimensionality grows. The finding that an unsupervised, partially observable method can outperform a fully supervised, fully observable SOTA (SAM) on rare targets suggests that the information bottleneck of partial observability can be overcome with the right generative prior.
