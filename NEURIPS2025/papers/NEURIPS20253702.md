## Problem

While the do-calculus is sound and complete for identifying macro causal effects in C-DMGs over ADMGs, prior results required restrictive assumptions (e.g., all clusters in cycles must have size > 1). Real-world systems often exhibit cyclic causal dynamics at the structural level, modeled by ioSCMs that induce DMGs rather than ADMGs. The paper addresses whether σ-separation and the do-calculus remain sound and complete for macro causal effect identification in C-DMGs over DMGs, and whether graphical non-identifiability criteria extend to this setting.

## Input / Output

The method takes a C-DMG over DMGs—a partially specified graph where variables are partitioned into clusters, with directed edges encoding causal influence between clusters and bidirected edges encoding latent confounding—along with disjoint exposure cluster set C_X and outcome cluster set C_Y. It produces either a do()-free probabilistic expression for Pr(C_Y | do(C_X)) when the effect is identifiable, or an SC-hedge (a hedge structure in the SC-projection of the C-DMG) serving as a graphical certificate of non-identifiability. Unlike the ADMG case, no cluster-size constraints are required for the identifiability determination itself.

## Method

The paper establishes three theoretical results. First, it proves σ-separation (the cyclic generalization of d-separation) is both sound and complete in C-DMGs over DMGs: if clusters are σ-separated in the C-DMG, they are σ-separated in every compatible DMG, and conversely, a non-separation in the C-DMG witnesses a compatible DMG where the separation fails (via the maximal compatible DMG construction). Second, it proves the three do-calculus rules (adapted to use σ-separation) are sound and complete for macro causal effects in C-DMGs over DMGs unconditionally—removing the cluster-size > 1 constraints that were necessary in the C-DMG-over-ADMG setting. The completeness proof shows that if a do-calculus rule fails in the C-DMG, it also fails in the maximal compatible DMG. Third, it shows that SC-hedges (hedges in the SC-projection, which adds bidirected edges between clusters in the same strongly connected component) provide a sound graphical criterion for non-identifiability, under the additional assumption that every cluster participating in a cycle has size at least 2.

## Results

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical experiments or quantitative benchmarks. The main results are five theorems: (1) σ-separation is sound in C-DMGs over DMGs (Theorem 1), (2) σ-separation is complete in C-DMGs over DMGs (Theorem 2), (3) the three do-calculus rules using σ-separation are sound for macro causal effects in C-DMGs over DMGs (Theorem 3), (4) the do-calculus is complete for C-DMGs over DMGs (Theorem 4), and (5) the SC-hedge criterion is a sound non-identifiability certificate under the assumption that every cluster in a cycle has size ≥ 2 (Theorem 5). The key distinction from prior work on C-DMGs over ADMGs is that the do-calculus soundness and completeness hold unconditionally, without requiring cluster sizes to exceed 1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against empirical baselines, as it is entirely theoretical. The relevant comparison is with prior theoretical results: Ferreira and Assaad (2025b) established do-calculus soundness and completeness for C-DMGs over ADMGs only under the assumption that every cluster has size > 1, while this paper removes that constraint for C-DMGs over DMGs, proving unconditional soundness and completeness (Theorems 3–4). For non-identifiability, the SC-hedge criterion from Ferreira and Assaad (2025a) required the same cluster-size assumption; here it extends to C-DMGs over DMGs under the weaker condition that only clusters *in a cycle* must have size ≥ 2 (Theorem 5). The completeness result (Theorem 4) is established via the maximal compatible DMG construction, showing that if a do-calculus rule fails in the C-DMG, it also fails in the maximal compatible DMG. The authors note a limitation: the completeness of σ-separation-based do-calculus in DMGs themselves (as opposed to C-DMGs over DMGs) remains unproven, so the completeness here is relative to the underlying DMG do-calculus.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two research threads: (1) the extension of Pearl's do-calculus from acyclic ADMGs to cyclic DMGs via σ-separation (Forré and Mooij, 2020), and (2) the identification of macro causal effects in cluster graphs, which progressed from acyclic cluster DAGs (Anand et al., 2023) to cyclic C-DMGs over ADMGs (Ferreira and Assaad, 2025a,b). The paper unifies these threads by proving that σ-separation and the σ-based do-calculus are sound and complete for macro causal effects in C-DMGs over DMGs, a setting that had not been formally addressed. A notable departure from the C-DMG-over-ADMG setting is that the completeness result here is unconditional, whereas the ADMG analogue required cluster-size constraints.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result provides a clean theoretical foundation for causal effect identification in partially specified systems with genuine cyclic dynamics (e.g., feedback loops in public health, biology, economics), where full DMG specification is infeasible. The unconditional completeness is practically significant: it means practitioners working with cyclic cluster graphs over DMGs do not need to verify cluster-size conditions before applying the do-calculus. However, two open questions remain: (i) the completeness of σ-separation-based do-calculus in DMGs themselves is still unproven, so the C-DMG result inherits this gap; and (ii) the SC-hedge non-identifiability criterion requires the assumption that every cluster in a cycle has size ≥ 2, and its completeness is not established.
