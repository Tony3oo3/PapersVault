## Problem

Existing approximate unlearning for Vision-Language Models operates only at the class level, making it insufficient for practical scenarios where a model must recognize objects in one domain (e.g., real cars) but not in another (e.g., illustrated cars in advertisements). This paper introduces Approximate Domain Unlearning (ADU), a new problem setting that requires selectively reducing recognition accuracy for specified image domains while preserving it for others. The key technical challenge is that pre-trained VLMs' strong domain generalization causes domain distributions to be highly entangled in latent feature space, rendering naive loss-based unlearning ineffective at the domain level.

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images from multiple visual domains (e.g., real, painting, clipart, sketch) paired with a fixed text prompt template ('a photo of a [class]') and produces a class label over the target class set. During training, each sample carries both a class label and a domain label, with the domain label determining whether the sample contributes to the memorize or forget loss. The model is a CLIP ViT-B/16 image encoder with deep prompting, trained in a few-shot regime (8 labeled samples per domain by default), and the domain classifier head is an auxiliary fully connected layer used only during training.

## Method

The approach combines three loss terms optimized jointly over learnable vision prompts and an auxiliary domain classifier. The Domain Disentangling Loss (DDL) explicitly separates domain distributions in latent space by maximizing Maximum Mean Discrepancy (MMD) between domain pairs and minimizing cross-entropy of an auxiliary domain classifier, inverting the conventional domain-adaptation objective that minimizes MMD. The Instance-wise Prompt Generator (InstaPG) embeds a cross-attention module at an intermediate transformer layer where learnable vision prompts serve as queries and image patch features as keys/values, producing image-conditioned prompts that capture per-instance domain characteristics. Together with the standard memorize (cross-entropy to ground-truth class) and forget (cross-entropy to uniform class) losses, this total objective enables fine-grained domain-wise control over what the model recognizes, achieving over 20% improvement in forgetting performance over strong baselines built on state-of-the-art CLIP fine-tuning methods.

## Results

On Office-Home with |D_forget|=1, the proposed method achieves H=69.96, Mem=77.93, and For=64.34, substantially outperforming the strongest baseline (CLIPFit: H=43.44, For=29.40) and the straightforward Baseline (H=52.59, For=39.88). On Mini DomainNet with |D_forget|=3, the method reaches H=79.00 and For=83.94, compared to Baseline's H=68.82 and For=58.27. The forgetting metric (For) shows the largest gains, with the proposed method exceeding 60% For across all settings while all baselines remain below 50% on Office-Home and Mini DomainNet.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all four baselines on both H and For across every dataset and |D_forget| setting. Against CLIPFit (Li et al., 2024), the strongest external baseline, the method gains over 20 points in H on Office-Home (69.96 vs. 43.44) and Mini DomainNet (75.56 vs. 53.56) at |D_forget|=1, with For improving from 29.40 to 64.34 on Office-Home. Against LP++ (Huang et al., 2024), the gap is even larger, with H exceeding 30 points on Office-Home and Mini DomainNet. Against BBF (Kuwana et al., 2024), the state-of-the-art black-box unlearning method, the proposed method achieves over 30% higher For on all datasets (e.g., 64.34 vs. 19.74 on Office-Home). Compared to the straightforward Baseline (L_memorize + L_forget only), the method sacrifices a small amount of Mem (e.g., 77.93 vs. 79.96 on Office-Home) but gains 24+ points in For, demonstrating that the Domain Disentangling Loss and InstaPG are essential for effective domain-level forgetting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the approximate unlearning literature (Huang et al. 2024b, Kuwana et al. 2024) from class-level forgetting to a new domain-level formulation, building on the L_memorize/L_forget loss framework and the MaPLe deep prompting architecture (Khattak et al., 2023). It departs from domain adaptation/generalization work by inverting the MMD objective—maximizing rather than minimizing inter-domain distance—to deliberately separate domain representations in the latent space. The Instance-wise Prompt Generator (InstaPG) introduces a cross-attention mechanism that conditions learnable prompts on input image patches, addressing instance-level domain variation that uniform prompts cannot capture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ADU opens a practically motivated direction in machine unlearning that goes beyond the class-level granularity of prior work, with clear applications in safety-critical systems (e.g., autonomous driving that must ignore illustrated cars in advertisements). A key limitation is the requirement for complete domain labels during training, though the authors show partial-label robustness via pseudo-labeling. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to more than four domains, to non-CLIP VLMs, and to settings where domain boundaries are inherently fuzzy or overlapping. The finding that domain entanglement in pre-trained VLMs fundamentally undermines naive unlearning losses suggests that domain-level control may require architectural changes beyond prompt tuning.
