## Problem

When both predictors and responses are matrix-valued, existing approaches either fit independent scalar regressions (ignoring multivariate structure), vectorize into a Kronecker-product linear model (losing interpretability and suffering high variance in high dimensions), or rely on iterative non-convex optimization (alternating minimization, spectral aggregation). No prior work provides explicit, optimization-free estimators for the bilinear matrix-valued regression model Y_t = A*X_tB* + E_t with non-asymptotic finite-sample guarantees, particularly under sparsity.

## Input / Output

The method consumes T independent pairs (X_t, Y_t) with X_t ∈ R^{m×q} and Y_t ∈ R^{n×p}, and produces two parameter matrices: A* ∈ R_+^{n×m} (non-negative, rowwise ℓ1-normalized) and B* ∈ R^{q×p}. In the image denoising application, each 32×32 RGB channel is treated as a matrix, noisy versions are generated via left/right multiplication by (A*)^{-1} and (B*)^{-1}, and the output is a corrected 32×32×3 image. The design matrices X_t must form a generating family of R^{m×q} (requiring T ≥ mq), and the noise is assumed i.i.d. Gaussian with variance σ².

## Method

The key insight is that in the noiseless case, the bilinear structure allows exact recovery of A* and B* from a single intermediate matrix C whose entries are products [A*]_{ik}·[B*]_{lj}, reconstructible via OLS on the vectorized observations. At the sample level, C is replaced by its plug-in estimate Ĉ = (X^T X)^{-1}X^T Y, yielding explicit closed-form estimators: B̂ entries are row-averages of Ĉ, and Ã entries are ratios of column-sums of Ĉ with a leave-one-out denominator to preserve independence. A final clipping step (max(0, min(·,1))) enforces the [0,1] constraint on A*. Under the ORT assumption (X^T X = T·I), non-asymptotic bounds are derived via Mill's inequality and Gaussian concentration, revealing a 'blessing of dimensionality' where error in Â decreases with p and q while error in B̂ decreases with n. Sparse variants apply hard-thresholding with data-driven thresholds, achieving support recovery guarantees when nonzero entries exceed 3τ.

## Results

The paper establishes non-asymptotic error bounds for optimization-free estimators Â and B̂ in the BMLR model. Empirically, the convergence rate with respect to sample size T is confirmed at T^{-1/2} (fitted slopes between −0.58 and −0.47, matching the theoretical prediction of −0.5). A 'blessing of dimensionality' effect is observed: estimation error for Â decreases as p and q grow, while error for B̂ decreases as n grows. On CIFAR-10 (32×32×3 images), the correction procedure reduces the Frobenius distance between original and reconstructed images (D_o,c) below the distance between original and noisy images (D_o,n) across all tested noise levels ε, with the gap widening as ε increases.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not include formal head-to-head numerical comparisons against competing estimation methods (alternating minimization, α-PCA, or KRO-PRO-FAC). Instead, the empirical validation is conducted against the paper's own theoretical predictions: the fitted empirical slopes in Table 1 closely match the non-asymptotic rates derived in Theorems 3.3 and 3.6, with R² values above 0.9 in most cases. The conceptual comparison in the introduction positions the proposed optimization-free plug-in estimators as avoiding the non-convex Kronecker factorization problem inherent in the vectorized OLS approach [11], and as providing parameter-wise error control that the KRO-PRO-FAC method lacks. The CIFAR-10 denoising experiment (Section 4.2) serves as a practical demonstration rather than a benchmark comparison, showing that the estimated correction matrices Â and B̂ effectively invert the noisy bilinear transformation, with D_o,c consistently below D_o,n across all noise levels ε tested.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the BMLR model introduced by Hoff (2015) for multilinear tensor regression and on the two-sided matrix regression framework of Bettache & Butucea (2023). While prior estimation approaches for this model rely on non-convex optimization (alternating minimization, spectral aggregation via α-PCA) or Kronecker product factorization, this work departs by proposing explicit, optimization-free plug-in estimators derived from population-level identifiability results. The theoretical analysis under the ORT assumption and matrix normal noise connects to the broader Gaussian sequence model and high-dimensional statistics literature, while the sparse extensions follow the hard-thresholding paradigm of Giraud and Rigollet-Hutter.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The optimization-free estimators offer a computationally attractive alternative to iterative methods for high-dimensional matrix-valued regression, with non-asymptotic bounds that make the dependence on all problem dimensions (n, m, p, q, T) explicit. The 'blessing of dimensionality' effect—where larger response dimensions improve estimation of one parameter while hurting the other—provides practical guidance for experimental design. Key limitations include the ORT assumption on the design, the homoskedastic Gaussian noise requirement, and the non-negativity/row-stochastic constraint on A* that breaks symmetry between the two parameters. Open questions include extending the analysis to general (non-orthogonal) designs, structured noise, and whether the asymmetry in convergence rates can be mitigated by alternative normalization choices.
