## Problem

Masked diffusion models (MDM) for discrete data restrict each token to a binary masked/unmasked state, causing the entire sequence to remain unchanged across many consecutive sampling steps (~37% of steps are 'idle' in a 1,024-step run). This redundancy wastes model capacity and prevents fine-grained denoising, since a token must jump directly from fully masked to fully unmasked in a single step. The paper asks whether introducing intermediate states between masked and unmasked can eliminate idle steps and improve generative quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253258/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For text, input is a GPT-2-tokenized sequence (L=1024, C=50,257) where each token is decomposed into ℓ sub-tokens via base-b encoding (e.g., ℓ=4, b=15 for the GPT-2 vocabulary), and each sub-token independently takes a value in {0,…,b−1} or the mask symbol. For images, input is a 32×32×3 pixel array (C=256) with the same sub-token decomposition. The imputation variant takes a partially observed image where the first sub-token of each pixel is retained and the remaining sub-tokens are masked, producing a complete image as output.

## Method

The Partial masking scheme (Prime) encodes each token as a base-b sequence of ℓ sub-tokens and applies the forward diffusion masking independently at the sub-token level, creating (b+1)^ℓ − (C+1) intermediate states that partially reveal token information. The reverse process is parameterized by a joint distribution over the ℓ sub-tokens (rather than an independent factorization), with a carry-over constraint that fixes already-unmasked sub-tokens and explicitly zeros probability mass on invalid base-b encodings, implemented via precomputed filters. Architecturally, only the embedding layer is modified: a single C-entry lookup table is replaced by ℓ per-sub-token tables of dimension D/ℓ, concatenated to form the D-dimensional token embedding, leaving the rest of the standard MDM (DiT-B) unchanged. Training minimizes the standard variational upper bound (weighted cross-entropy with coefficient α′_t/(1−α_t)) applied to the sub-token sequences. This reduces idle steps from ~37% (ℓ=1) to <1% (ℓ≥6) and improves OpenWebText perplexity from 22.98 (MDLM) to 15.36, surpassing autoregressive baselines without an autoregressive formulation.

## Results

On OpenWebText, MDLM-Prime (ℓ=6) achieves a perplexity of 15.36 with only 0.25% idle step ratio, outperforming the autoregressive baseline (17.54), the best MDM variant (21.52), and hybrid ARM-MDM methods (17.58) without any autoregressive formulation. On CIFAR-10, MDM-Prime attains FID 3.26 and IS 9.67 with NFE=512, matching StyleGAN+ADA (FID 3.26) and surpassing all discrete diffusion baselines. On ImageNet-32, MDM-Prime achieves FID 6.98 and IS 11.65, improving over ScoreFlow(VP) (FID 8.34) and the MDM baseline (FID 7.91) by 1.36 and 0.93 FID points respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MDLM-Prime (ℓ=6) outperforms all prior text-generation baselines on OpenWebText: it beats the autoregressive MDLM (ARM*, PPL 17.54) by 2.18 PPL, the energy-guided hybrid EDLM-coAR* (≤17.58) by 2.22 PPL, and the block-diffusion hybrid BD3-LMs* (≤20.73) by 5.37 PPL, all while maintaining an order-agnostic (non-autoregressive) formulation. Among pure MDM methods, it surpasses MDLM (≤22.98), GenMD4 (≤21.80), EDLM-NCE (≤21.52), and SEDD (≤24.10) by 5–9 PPL points. On CIFAR-10, MDM-Prime (FID 3.26) beats all discrete diffusion baselines—Tau-LDR (3.74), Discrete FM (3.63), Bit Diffusion (3.48), and the MDM baseline (4.66)—and ties StyleGAN+ADA (3.26), though it remains slightly behind DDPM (3.17). On ImageNet-32, MDM-Prime (FID 6.98) improves over ScoreFlow(VP) (8.34) and the MDM baseline (7.91), but trails the continuous Flow Matching method (5.02). In zero-shot text evaluation, MDLM-Prime (ℓ=4) substantially outperforms ARM on LAMBADA (24.44 vs 51.28), PTB (53.98 vs 82.05), and ArXiv (25.83 vs 41.73), is comparable on WikiText (23.86 vs 25.75), but underperforms ARM on AG News (59.44 vs 52.09).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the masked diffusion model (MDM) framework established by Austin et al. [20], Sahoo et al. [9], and Shi et al. [10], which model discrete data generation through a binary masked/unmasked state space. The key departure is the introduction of intermediate states via base-b sub-token encoding, transforming the diffusion process from a two-state to a multi-state transition, which the authors show analytically reduces idle steps and enables finer-grained denoising. Unlike recent hybrid approaches (BD3-LM [13], EDLM-coAR [12]) that incorporate autoregressive formulations to close the performance gap with ARM, MDM-Prime maintains a fully order-agnostic framework while surpassing ARM on text generation. The architectural modification is minimal—only the input embedding layer changes—making it a drop-in enhancement to existing MDM pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first MDM-based method to outperform autoregressive models on text generation (PPL 15.36 vs. 17.54) without any autoregressive component, suggesting that the binary masked/unmasked formulation was a fundamental bottleneck rather than an inherent limitation of order-agnostic generation. The idle-step analysis provides a clear diagnostic for model utilization in discrete diffusion, and the partial masking scheme is general enough to apply to any discrete data domain. Open questions include whether the gains persist at larger model scales and on higher-resolution images (experiments use 32×32), and whether the sub-token decomposition interacts favorably with other recent MDM enhancements like remasking or distillation. The paper notes that sub-token positions in text lack the natural semantic ordering that pixel intensities provide, which may limit interpretability of the intermediate states in the text domain.
