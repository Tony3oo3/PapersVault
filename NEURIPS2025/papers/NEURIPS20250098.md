## Problem

Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) such as QwQ-32B and DeepSeek-R1 achieve strong multi-step reasoning by generating long chain-of-thought traces, but this incurs quadratic computational cost and often introduces semantic redundancy rather than genuine information gain. The paper asks whether reasoning depth can be adaptively controlled to substantially shorten chains while preserving or improving accuracy across diverse reasoning tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250098/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text question (with optional multiple-choice options) and requires access to the model's next-token probability distribution at each reasoning step. For multiple-choice tasks, entropy is computed directly over the option tokens; for free-form tasks (GSM8K, AIME2025), a tree search over candidate continuations (depth N=10, beam width W=5) enumerates answer paths to construct the answer-space distribution. The output is a final answer preceded by a potentially truncated reasoning chain, where the truncation point is determined dynamically by an entropy threshold rather than a fixed token budget.

## Method

The paper introduces two information-theoretic metrics—InfoBias (negative mutual information between the model's generated trajectory and a ground-truth reasoning path, estimated via HSIC with a Gaussian kernel) and InfoGain (per-step entropy reduction over the answer distribution)—to diagnose where and why reasoning becomes inefficient. Building on the finding that InfoBias accumulates monotonically with length while InfoGain diminishes, the core contribution is an entropy-based Adaptive Think strategy: after each reasoning step, the model computes the average entropy H_i^avg over the answer space and halts generation once H_i^avg ≤ α·(1/e·ln2), a threshold parameterized by α∈[0,1]. When the stopping criterion is met, the model is prompted with a closing  tag followed by an answer-prefixed suffix to elicit the final output. This is a plug-and-play, training-free inference-time mechanism that requires no architectural modification, and the paper provides a formal computational-overhead analysis showing that for multiple-choice tasks any nonzero token reduction yields net savings, while for free-form tasks the token reduction rate must exceed WN/(T+WN) to offset tree-search overhead.

## Results

On QwQ-32B across six benchmarks, Adaptive Think achieves a 1.10% average accuracy improvement and a 50.80% reduction in token usage compared to Vanilla Think. On the two math benchmarks (GSM8K and AIME2025), Adaptive Think reduces token usage by 58.78% while improving accuracy by +0.95% on average (e.g., QwQ-32B: 97.73% vs. 97.00% on GSM8K with 379.80 vs. 1132.32 tokens; 71.33% vs. 70.67% on AIME2025 with 4633.50 vs. 14595.73 tokens). Across the four non-math reasoning tasks, Adaptive Think yields +0.38% average accuracy and 42.39% token reduction, with the largest efficiency gain on CommonsenseQA (56.63% token reduction for QwQ-32B, 80.99% for DeepSeek-R1-32B).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares four inference strategies—Vanilla Think (default full reasoning), No-Think (bypass reasoning entirely), Gated Think (heuristic pre-assessment of whether to reason), and the proposed Adaptive Think (entropy-based dynamic early stopping). Adaptive Think dominates all three baselines on both accuracy and token efficiency for every reasoning model tested. No-Think drastically cuts tokens (e.g., 758 vs. 1132 for QwQ-32B on GSM8K) but degrades accuracy sharply on hard tasks (AIME2025: 68.00% vs. 70.67% for QwQ-32B; 22.67% vs. 56.67% for DeepSeek-R1-32B). Gated Think offers a moderate trade-off, falling between Vanilla and No-Think on both metrics, but still lags Adaptive Think in accuracy (e.g., 96.81% vs. 97.73% on GSM8K for QwQ-32B) while using more tokens (824.55 vs. 379.80). Adaptive Think's key advantage is that it simultaneously improves accuracy by 0.75–4.41% and reduces tokens by 40–68% relative to Vanilla Think, with the largest gains on the hardest benchmark (AIME2025) where overthinking is most detrimental.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper situates itself at the intersection of information-theoretic analysis of LLMs and adaptive reasoning efficiency. It draws on Paninski's (2003) mutual information estimation bounds to formalize InfoBias, and on Muennighoff et al. (2025) for the mechanism of eliciting final answers mid-thought. Conceptually, it extends the semantic entropy and hallucination-detection line (Farquhar et al. 2024, Kossen et al. 2024) from output-level reliability to step-level reasoning diagnostics, and connects to the growing body of work showing diminishing returns from longer CoTs (Wu et al. 2025, Yang et al. 2025b, Chen et al. 2024). The Adaptive Think strategy is a natural instantiation of early-exit and difficulty-adaptive reasoning (Han et al. 2024, Pan et al. 2024, Yang et al. 2025a), but the paper's distinguishing contribution is the principled information-theoretic justification for *when* to stop, grounded in Shannon's communication model rather than heuristic difficulty classifiers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 50.80% token reduction with a 1.10% accuracy *gain* on QwQ-32B across six diverse benchmarks is practically significant for deployment cost, and the InfoBias/InfoGain metrics offer a reusable diagnostic framework for auditing reasoning efficiency in any LRM. However, the method requires access to next-token probability distributions, limiting applicability to open-source models, and cannot handle truly open-ended questions without a discrete answer space. The paper itself identifies the key open question: transitioning from output-oriented (truncating generation) to model-oriented (architecturally efficient) reasoning optimization, which remains unaddressed.
