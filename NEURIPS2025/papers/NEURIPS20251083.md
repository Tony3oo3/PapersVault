## Problem

Time series decomposition is a powerful tool for handling non-stationarity, yet it has never been successfully deployed as a model-agnostic enhancement framework due to two barriers: previous decomposition methods are tied to specific model architectures (lacking generality) and require explicit decomposition plus two expert models at inference time (causing severe efficiency overhead). As a result, the enhancement framework space remains dominated by normalization-based methods, and decomposition-based approaches have never surpassed them in performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251083/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework takes a raw multivariate time series of length I=384 with M variates and produces a future prediction of length T∈{96,192,336,720} with the same M variates. Crucially, at inference the model consumes the undecomposed raw series directly—no explicit seasonal/trend split is performed—yet it has been trained to implicitly exploit decomposition knowledge. The framework is model-agnostic: it wraps around any existing forecaster (Transformer, MLP, or convolution-based) without modifying its architecture, and the deployed model has identical inference cost to the original backbone.

## Method

DecompNet introduces 'implicit decomposition' via a two-stage training pipeline. First, a decoupled training strategy pretrains two copies of the backbone model on separately decomposed seasonal and trend inputs, each supervised by its own component-specific loss (L_s and L_t computed against decomposed ground truth), providing clearer supervision than joint whole-series prediction. Second, Seasonal-Trend Re-parameterization fuses the two expert models into a single model by learning a per-layer scalar fusion factor λ and computing W = W_s + λW_t for each linear/convolutional layer, leveraging the identity X = X_s + X_t to collapse the explicit two-branch inference into a single forward pass on raw X. An extra short training phase jointly fine-tunes W_s, W_t, and λ so the fused model matches the two-branch output, after which only the merged weights are retained. At inference, one model processes the undecomposed input with zero additional compute, inheriting all decomposition-related knowledge from the pretraining stage.

## Results

DecompNet consistently improves all five backbone models (PatchTST, iTransformer, RLinear, RMLP, ModernTCN) across all 8 datasets with zero additional inference cost. Headline MSE improvements include 8.0% on Solar-Energy (e.g., PatchTST: 0.218→0.199), 6.4% on ETTh1 (0.418→0.403), and 6.1% on Traffic (0.410→0.387). With PatchTST as backbone, DecompNet achieves an average MSE of 0.290 across 8 datasets, outperforming the best prior-based framework by 6.3%. The fused model retains exactly the same inference speed and memory usage as the original backbone model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DecompNet outperforms all seven compared enhancement frameworks and explicit decomposition methods. Against normalization-based frameworks (Dish-TS, SAN, FAN), DecompNet achieves the best average MSE across 8 datasets; notably, these normalization-based methods actually degrade PatchTST's performance below its original baseline, while DecompNet consistently improves it. Against prior-based frameworks (SparseTSF, CycleNet), DecompNet achieves 6.3% lower average MSE with PatchTST (0.290 vs. ~0.310) and requires no dataset-specific periodicity prior. Against explicit decomposition methods (MICN, Leddam), DecompNet's implicit decomposition wins on all 8 datasets; ablation shows that simply replacing their joint training with the decoupled training strategy yields 14.7% MSE reduction in MICN and 6.3% in Leddam, confirming that training strategy—not data processing sophistication—is the key differentiator. Crucially, DecompNet maintains identical inference speed and memory to the original model, whereas all other frameworks introduce additional inference overhead.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate research threads: structural re-parameterization (RepVGG, M2PT) and time series decomposition (Autoformer, MICN, Leddam). While prior decomposition methods explicitly split the input into seasonal and trend components at inference and deploy two expert models, and prior enhancement frameworks (RevIN, Dish-TS, SAN, FAN) operate from a statistical normalization perspective, DecompNet converts decomposition into an implicit, inference-time-free process by fusing two pretrained expert models via a learnable fusion factor λ. The key conceptual departure is that decomposition knowledge is injected into a single model's weights during training rather than being computed on-the-fly, making it a model-agnostic enhancement framework with zero additional inference cost.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DecompNet establishes the first decomposition-based enhancement framework to surpass normalization-based ones, and does so with zero additional inference cost, making it a practical drop-in enhancement for any time series forecaster. The finding that identical model architectures suffice for both seasonal and trend experts (contradicting the field's assumption that different structures are needed) and that training strategy outweighs sophisticated data processing open new directions for decomposition research. A key limitation is the requirement for strict layer-wise architectural alignment between the two expert models, which the authors acknowledge as future work; the framework also relies on a fixed moving-average window size (25) for decomposition during pretraining, though sensitivity analysis shows robustness to this choice.
