## Problem

Causal discovery for dynamical systems lacks a standardized, large-scale benchmark with known ground-truth causal graphs. Existing benchmarks rely on static causal graphs, auto-regressive models, or domain-specific datasets with only a handful of graphs and weakly nonlinear dynamics, making it impossible to isolate algorithmic limitations from dataset characteristics in settings with continuous state-space evolution, complex feedback loops, stochasticity, and regime shifts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251349/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark provides multivariate time series (5 trajectories × 1000 time steps per graph) generated from coupled ODEs/SDEs, where each node is a d-dimensional causal unit (d∈{1,3}) and each edge is a learned MLP with a randomly sampled activation function. The output is a reconstructed adjacency matrix (causal graph) evaluated against the true graph via AUROC, AUPRC, and SHD. The dataset spans three tiers of increasing complexity: 585 simple 3D chaotic systems, 14,096 hierarchically coupled systems (N∈{3,5,10} nodes), and 12 pseudo-real climate graphs from ENSO and quasi-geostrophic atmosphere-ocean models, totaling over 14,000 graphs and 50 million preprocessed samples.

## Method

CausalDynamics is a tiered, plug-and-play data generation framework. Tier 1 derives structural dynamical causal models from 59 three-dimensional chaotic systems in the dysts package. Tier 2 adapts the Growing Network with Redirection (GNR) algorithm to sample scale-free DAGs, where root nodes are driven by chaotic systems, sinusoidal functions, or linear trends, and non-root nodes aggregate incoming signals through edge-level MLPs with sparsified weights and random activations (identity, sin, sigmoid, tanh, ReLU). The framework systematically introduces causal challenges: unobserved confounders (via 90° rotation of off-diagonal entries in a second adjacency matrix), time-lagged edges (constant delay τ per graph, sampled with probability p_t), and temporal standardization to correct varsortability. Tier 3 incorporates two idealized climate models (XRO recharge oscillator for ENSO and a quasi-geostrophic two-layer model for barotropic/baroclinic interactions). The paper evaluates ten state-of-the-art causal discovery algorithms (PCMCI+, F-PCMCI, VARLiNGAM, DYNOTEARS, NGC, TSCI, CUTS+, RCD, GRaSP, TCDF) across all tiers, finding that simpler non-DL methods often outperform DL-based approaches on high-dimensional coupled and climate systems.

## Results

On the coupled systems tier (n=10 ODEs, default settings), PCMCI+ and F-PCMCI achieve the best AUROC of 0.67 (AUPRC 0.25–0.27), while all DL-based baselines (NGC, CUTS+, RCD, TCDF) perform at chance level (AUROC ≈ 0.50, AUPRC ≈ 0.15). On the climate MAOOAM tier, PCMCI+ leads with AUROC 0.69 and AUPRC 0.88, followed by DYNOTEARS (0.64/0.86). On the simple tier (3D ODEs), all methods cluster tightly around AUROC 0.50–0.52, with PCMCI+ and GRaSP marginally ahead at 0.52/0.71.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PCMCI+ consistently outperforms all other baselines on the coupled systems tier, achieving AUROC 0.67 vs. 0.50 (chance) for DL-based methods like NGC, CUTS+, RCD, and TCDF, and vs. 0.60 for VARLiNGAM and TSCI. F-PCMCI matches PCMCI+ on the coupled default (0.67) but degrades more under noise (0.57 vs. 0.64). On the climate MAOOAM tier, PCMCI+ leads with AUROC 0.69, while DYNOTEARS is a distant second at 0.64; all DL-based methods again sit at chance (0.50). TSCI (topology-based) shows a modest edge over pure DL methods on confounded coupled systems (AUROC 0.51 vs. 0.49–0.50) but does not close the gap to PCMCI+. On the simple tier, all methods perform nearly identically (AUROC 0.48–0.55), indicating that the 3D chaotic systems alone do not differentiate algorithmic capability. The overall finding is that constraint-based methods (PCMCI+, F-PCMCI) and score-based methods (DYNOTEARS) substantially outperform DL-based approaches on high-dimensional, coupled, and physical systems, with DL methods failing to exceed chance-level performance on the coupled tier.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Growing Network with Redirection (GNR) model of Krapivsky & Redner [34] to generate scale-free DAGs, and extends the theoretical framework of structural dynamical causal models (SDCMs) from Peters et al. [35] and Boeken & Mooij [39] into a practical, large-scale benchmark. It departs from existing causal discovery benchmarks (CausalBench, CausalRivers, SAVAR, CausalTime) that are limited to static graphs, auto-regressive models, or narrow domain-specific applications by introducing a tiered hierarchy from 3D chaotic ODEs/SDEs through hierarchically coupled systems to pseudo-real climate models. The plug-and-play coupling workflow with MLP-based edges and configurable causal challenges (confounders, time-lags, varsortability correction) is a novel data generation pipeline not present in prior benchmarks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CausalDynamics fills a critical gap: no standardized, large-scale benchmark existed for evaluating causal discovery on nonlinear, stochastic, high-dimensional dynamical systems with realistic challenges. The finding that simpler constraint- and noise-based methods (PCMCI+, VARLiNGAM) consistently outperform DL-based approaches (CUTS+, NGC, TCDF) on coupled and high-dimensional systems challenges the assumption that deeper architectures are necessary for complex nonlinear dynamics, and highlights that the field needs fundamentally new algorithmic ideas rather than incremental DL improvements. Limitations include fixed parameter sets and 3D node dimensionality in the preprocessed data, though the framework supports arbitrary scaling. Open questions include whether methods can recover full coupling in high-dimensional spatial fields (as in MAOOAM) and how to handle the gap between synthetic benchmark performance and real-world applicability.
