## Problem

Traditional Adversarial Imitation Learning (AIL) methods such as GAIL collapse multimodal expert behaviors into a single strategy, failing to reproduce the diversity of human demonstrations. While diffusion models can expressively capture multimodal distributions, the ASAF framework—which eliminates costly policy gradient updates—requires closed-form density evaluation that is intractable for diffusion policies. The paper bridges this gap by deriving a tractable surrogate objective that enables diffusion-based AIL without any policy optimization loop.

## Input / Output

The diffusion policy takes the current observed state as conditioning input and generates a fixed-horizon sub-trajectory of H actions via iterative denoising (N=50 steps). At each diffusion step, the state variable in the noisy trajectory is overwritten with the current observed state to condition the generation. The H actions are then executed sequentially in the environment, with a new diffusion sampling pass triggered every H environment steps, trading inference cost for reduced compounding error.

## Method

DPAIL adapts the ASAF structured discriminator D(τ) = σ(log p_θ(τ)/p_θ_old(τ)) to diffusion policies by exploiting the shared forward noising schedule to rewrite the intractable log-density ratio as a sum over reverse-step log-likelihood ratios. Applying Jensen's inequality to the concave log σ(·) yields a lower bound expressible as KL divergences between Gaussian posteriors, which reduce to squared noise-prediction errors. The final training objective (Eqs. 9–10) maximizes the gap between the new policy's and old policy's noise prediction errors on expert data while minimizing it on generated data, all within a sigmoid. Because the bound is tight at θ = θ_old, the algorithm is an instance of minorization-maximization, guaranteeing monotonic improvement of the original ASAF objective without any policy gradient or backpropagation through diffusion sampling steps.

## Results

DPAIL achieves the highest average normalized score (0.83±0.06) across six environments, substantially outperforming all baselines. On the most challenging multi-modal tasks (Ant-v3 with 4 modes, AntGoal-v3 with 8 modes), DPAIL scores 0.56 and 0.67 respectively, while most baselines (GAIL, DiffAIL, DRAIL, ASAF) fail entirely with scores near 0.01–0.03. DPAIL also maintains high behavioral diversity, achieving the best entropy on HalfCheetah-v3 (0.61) and maze2d-medium-v1 (0.91), and near-best entropy on Ant-v3 (1.21) and maze2d-large-v1 (1.04), confirming it captures multiple expert modes rather than collapsing to a single strategy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DPAIL outperforms all seven baselines on average score (0.83 vs. next-best Diffusion at 0.69). Against GAIL, DiffAIL, and DRAIL, DPAIL wins decisively on multi-modal tasks: on Ant-v3, these methods collapse to near-zero scores (0.01–0.02) while DPAIL reaches 0.56, and on AntGoal-v3 they score 0.35–0.57 versus DPAIL's 0.67. GAIL, DiffAIL, and DRAIL also exhibit near-zero entropy on HalfCheetah-v3 and maze2d-medium-v1, confirming mode collapse to a single strategy. InfoGAIL achieves the highest average entropy (0.98) but at the cost of the second-lowest average score (0.53), indicating its GMM-based representation learning struggles to imitate even a single mode well. ASAF, the Gaussian-policy counterpart of DPAIL's framework, performs poorly (0.35 average score), highlighting that the diffusion policy class is essential for capturing multi-modal behavior. The Diffusion (BC) baseline is the closest competitor, matching DPAIL on HalfCheetah-v3 score (1.01 vs. 1.02) and maze2d-large-v1 (0.94 vs. 1.00), but DPAIL consistently surpasses it on Walker2d-v3 (0.78 vs. 0.58), Ant-v3 (0.56 vs. 0.48), and AntGoal-v3 (0.67 vs. 0.22), particularly when demonstrations are limited.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously incompatible lines of work: ASAF (Barde et al., 2020), which eliminates policy optimization in AIL by structuring the discriminator to implicitly solve for the optimal policy, and trajectory-level diffusion policies (Janner et al., 2022; Chi et al., 2023), which capture multi-modal behavior distributions. The key departure is that ASAF requires closed-form density evaluation, which is intractable for diffusion models; the authors resolve this by deriving a tractable lower bound on the ASAF objective via Jensen's inequality, yielding a minorization-maximization algorithm. This is conceptually distinct from DRAIL and DiffAIL, which use diffusion models in the discriminator rather than as the policy itself, and from InfoGAIL, which relies on unsupervised latent representations to handle multi-modality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DPAIL addresses a practical pain point in multi-modal imitation learning: most AIL methods (GAIL, DiffAIL, DRAIL, ASAF) suffer from mode collapse or training instability when expert demonstrations span multiple behavior modes, while the policy-gradient-free formulation avoids the expensive and unstable backpropagation through diffusion sampling steps. The MM guarantee provides a clean theoretical foundation for monotonic improvement. Open questions include the high inference cost from iterative diffusion sampling (mitigated only partially by sub-trajectory execution), the lack of empirical validation for the latent-conditioned extension, and whether guidance mechanisms can enable mode-selective behavior without retraining.
