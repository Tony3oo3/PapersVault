## Problem

Existing strategic classification methods rely on lightweight models (linear classifiers, MLPs) validated on small datasets (<50K samples), making them computationally infeasible for large-scale, dynamically evolving real-world data in finance and internet domains. While LLMs offer the scale and capacity needed, retraining them is prohibitively expensive, and without retraining it remains unclear how to model the bi-level Stackelberg optimization structure of strategic classification.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251644/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes tabular feature vectors and a sequence of in-context labeled examples {(x_i, y_i)} formatted as prompt tokens, with a new query token (x_j, y_j) appended at the end. Through forward-only self-attention propagation, the LLM simultaneously produces a manipulated feature vector x_j' (inner-stage output) and an updated binary classification prediction ŷ_j (outer-stage output), with no parameter updates at any point. The approach is evaluated on both small-scale datasets (Adult, Spam, Credit) and large-scale datasets (CISFraud with >1M records, PhiUSIIL with ~235K URLs, Synthetic via PaySim).

## Method

GLIM (Gradient-free Learning In-context Method) embeds the SC bi-level optimization entirely within the forward pass of a pre-trained LLM's self-attention layers, requiring no fine-tuning. The core theoretical insight, formalized in Propositions 1 and 2, is that there exist constructive configurations of the self-attention projection matrices P, V, K and query vectors q_j such that the ICL-induced update Δx_ICL = PVK^T q_j exactly matches the explicit gradient-descent update for both the inner-stage strategic manipulation (agent feature modification) and the outer-stage decision rule optimization (classifier weight adjustment). This equivalence is grounded in prior results showing that linear self-attention forward propagation implements preconditioned gradient descent (Ahn et al. 2023; Von Oswald et al. 2023; Akyürek et al. 2023), extended here to the strategic classification setting with Mahalanobis-distance manipulation costs. The method is instantiated by prompting pre-trained LLMs (GPT-4o, DeepSeek-V3, Claude-3.7, Gemini-2.5, LLaMA-3.3, Mixtral, Qwen3) with in-context examples, and is shown to generalize to non-linear Softmax attention through multi-layer residual accumulation, as validated empirically across nine datasets.

## Results

Under the Strategic setting, GPT-4o with GLIM achieves the highest accuracy across all six main datasets: 86.50% on PhiUSIIL, 86.89% on CISFraud, 86.83% on Synthetic, 89.64% on Credit, 91.35% on Adult, and 95.97% on Spam. These results substantially exceed the best existing baselines (MLP at 65.65–91.05% and Linear Model at 63.20–89.67%) by 15–23 percentage points on large-scale datasets and 8–13 points on small-scale datasets. The gap between Strategic and Non-Strategic settings for GLIM (e.g., +7.4% on PhiUSIIL, +11.1% on Adult) confirms that the in-context mechanism actively counteracts strategic manipulation rather than merely ignoring it.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GLIM (GPT-4o) dominates all three baselines across every dataset and both settings. Against the Linear Model [Hardt et al., ITCS 2016], GLIM gains 23.3 points on PhiUSIIL (86.50% vs. 63.20%), 23.3 on CISFraud (86.89% vs. 63.61%), and 14.3 on Adult (91.35% vs. 77.10%) under the Strategic setting. Against the MLP baseline [Mofakhami et al., AISTATS 2023], the margins are 20.9 points on PhiUSIIL (86.50% vs. 65.65%) and 12.6 on Adult (91.35% vs. 78.74%). The GNN baseline [Eilat et al., 2022] (reported in Appendix Table 3) is also clearly outperformed, e.g., 86.50% vs. 68.37% on PhiUSIIL and 91.35% vs. 78.54% on Adult. Notably, the advantage of GLIM over baselines widens on large-scale datasets (15–23 pts) compared to small-scale ones (8–13 pts), supporting the paper's scalability claim. All GLIM variants (GPT-4o, DeepSeek-V3, Claude-3.7) consistently outperform every baseline, indicating the gains are attributable to the ICL-based gradient-free mechanism rather than a single model's capacity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate research threads: the strategic classification (SC) literature, which frames decision-making under adversarial feature manipulation as a Stackelberg bi-level optimization problem (Hardt et al., 2016), and the ICL-as-implicit-gradient-descent line of work (Ahn et al., 2023; Akyürek et al., 2023; Von Oswald et al., 2023), which shows that transformer forward passes internally simulate GD steps. Prior SC methods (linear classifiers, MLPs, GNNs) solve the bi-level problem via explicit gradient updates and are validated only on small datasets (<50K samples). GLIM departs from this by proving that the self-attention forward pass in a pre-trained LLM can reproduce both the inner-stage feature manipulation and the outer-stage decision rule update without any parameter updates, effectively replacing iterative retraining with in-context prompting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine scalability bottleneck: real-world SC deployments (fraud detection, phishing URL classification, credit scoring) involve millions of non-stationary samples where continuous gradient-based retraining is computationally prohibitive. The theoretical equivalence between ICL updates and GD steps for both SC stages is a clean and potentially reusable result. However, several open questions remain: the theoretical analysis is restricted to the linear regime, the empirical gains are partly attributable to raw LLM capacity rather than the SC-specific mechanism (the Strategic-vs-Non-Strategic gap is similar in magnitude to that of linear baselines), and the policy transparency argument—critical in SC—relies on attention visualization heuristics rather than formal guarantees. The approach also inherits LLM API costs and access constraints that may limit deployment in latency-sensitive or privacy-critical settings.
