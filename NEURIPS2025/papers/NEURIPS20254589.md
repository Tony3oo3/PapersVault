## Problem

Existing spatio-temporal forecasting methods—whether graph convolution or attention-based—rely on similarity-driven connectivity assumptions that capture surface-level observational correlations while neglecting the asymmetric, directional flow exchanges that actually govern system evolution. These approaches cannot adapt their interaction ranges to context-sensitive dynamics (e.g., reversing flow directions between morning and evening peaks) and conflate statistical proximity with intrinsic causal dependencies.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a matrix of N node observations over T historical timesteps (e.g., 307 traffic sensors at 5-min granularity, 1,661 hydrological basins at daily resolution, or 1,687 parking lots at 15-min intervals) along with node geographic coordinates for distance computation. Each node's univariate series is partitioned into overlapping patches via a sliding window (length M, stride S) and projected into a d-dimensional flow space. The output is a prediction of N node states over τ future steps, evaluated with MAE and RMSE on both short-term and long-term horizons.

## Method

FlowNet replaces similarity-based feature blending with explicit flow-token propagation governed by conservation laws. An Adaptive Spatial Masking (ASM) module learns a node-specific, context-aware interaction radius via Softplus and constructs a differentiable spatial mask M_t[i,j] = Sigmoid(r_i^t − d_ij) that suppresses non-physical long-range dependencies. Flow Allocation Modules (FAMs) use two Causal Temporal Multi-head Self-Attention estimators to produce retained tokens Φ_o and allocated tokens Φ_a per node; flow logits are computed as origin–destination vector products masked by ASM, and the final state enforces source depletion and destination augmentation (Φ = Φ_o + (Λ − I)Φ_a). Multiple FAMs are cascaded via hyper-connections (depth- and width-aware adaptive scaling) interleaved with M-MLP blocks that replace vanilla linear layers with a Mixture of Linears for combinatorial nonlinearity. Training is supervised with MAE loss, Adam optimizer, and early stopping on validation MAE.

## Results

FlowNet achieves the best MAE and RMSE on 11 out of 12 evaluation metrics across three datasets (PEMS04F, DeepBase, SINPA) and both short-term and long-term forecasting tasks. Headline numbers include MAE 18.48 / RMSE 29.03 on PEMS04F short-term, MAE 0.43 / RMSE 0.93 on DeepBase short-term, and MAE 39.03 / RMSE 76.04 on SINPA short-term. Statistically significant improvements (p < 0.05) over the second-best method are confirmed on seven metrics, most notably on SINPA where FlowNet reduces MAE by ~30% relative to the next-best baseline. Two Transformer-based baselines (STTN, STAEformer) fail with out-of-memory errors on the larger DeepBase and SINPA long-term tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FlowNet outperforms all ten baselines on the majority of metrics, with the closest competition coming from STGCN and GWNET on PEMS04F (where FlowNet's MAE of 18.48 vs. GWNET's 18.82 and STGCN's 20.98 on short-term is a modest but consistent gap) and from STGCN on DeepBase long-term (MAE 0.50 vs. 0.51). On the large-scale SINPA dataset, FlowNet's advantage is dramatic: it achieves MAE 39.03 on short-term versus the next-best SCINet at 55.20 on long-term, and no baseline comes within 20 MAE points. Two attention-based baselines (STTN, STAEformer) fail entirely with out-of-memory errors on DeepBase and SINPA long-term tasks, highlighting scalability limitations of full pairwise attention. The paper notes that no single baseline maintains a consistent advantage across all three datasets or both temporal horizons, whereas FlowNet leads or ties on every metric. The ablation further shows that removing the allocation flow (inter-node exchange) degrades PEMS04F short-term MAE by 5.54%, confirming that the flow mechanism—not mere temporal modeling—drives the gains over baselines like PatchTST and SCINet that lack explicit spatial interaction.

## Relation to Prior Work

FlowNet departs from both graph-based spatio-temporal models (STGCN, GWNET) that rely on static or heuristically learned adjacency matrices, and attention-based Transformers (STTN, iTransformer, STAEformer) that compute pairwise node affinities, by reframing inter-node interaction as directional flow transfer governed by conservation laws. Architecturally, it borrows patch-based tokenization from PatchTST, causal temporal self-attention for local temporal processing, and hyper-connections [37] for cascading its Flow Allocation Modules. The key conceptual departure is treating node states as quantifiable flow tokens that deplete at sources and augment at destinations, rather than blending features based on similarity scores.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a physically grounded alternative to the dominant similarity-driven paradigm in spatio-temporal forecasting, with the conservation-law constraint providing interpretability that pure attention or graph convolution lacks. However, the O(N²) flow allocation and O(T²) temporal attention limit scalability to very large node counts, and the empirical gains over strong baselines (e.g., 18.48 vs. 18.73 MAE on PEMS04F short-term) are moderate. Open questions include whether sparse or hierarchical flow tokenization can mitigate the quadratic bottleneck, and whether domain-specific conservation laws (e.g., hydrological mass balance) can further tighten the physical priors.
