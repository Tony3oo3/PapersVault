## Problem

Existing Language Gaussian Splatting (LGS) methods are evaluated on only a handful of scenes, at viewpoints close to training views, and in 2D projections rather than directly in 3D space, making it impossible to draw generalizable conclusions about which paradigm (per-scene optimization, optimization-free, or generalizable) truly performs best. No standardized, large-scale benchmark exists to systematically compare these three groups of methods under consistent 3D evaluation protocols.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252494/cropped_page0_idx6.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark takes a 3DGS scene represented as N Gaussian primitives (each encoding position, covariance, color, and opacity in R^59) and a set of free-form text queries, producing per-primitive vision-language feature vectors matched to text embeddings via cosine similarity. Evaluation is conducted directly in 3D space by assigning each ground-truth 3D point the majority-voted label from its K=25 nearest Gaussian neighbors, reporting foreground mIoU and mAcc; a supplementary 2D render evaluation follows the LERF protocol. The benchmark spans 1060 scenes across ScanNet (20/200 classes), ScanNet++ (100 classes), Matterport3D (21 classes), and HoliCity (4 outdoor classes), with floor/wall/ceiling excluded as background for indoor scenes and sky for outdoor scenes.

## Method

The paper's core contribution is a comprehensive benchmark (SceneSplat-Bench) and a 49K-scene 3DGS dataset (SceneSplat-49K) rather than a new segmentation architecture. It systematically evaluates 9 representative LGS methods across three paradigms—per-scene optimization (LangSplat, FMGS, Feature3DGS, OpenGaussian, GOI), per-scene optimization-free (Gradient-Weighted 3DGS, LUDVIG, OccamLGS), and generalizable (SceneSplat)—on 3D semantic segmentation, 2D segmentation, and 3D object localization tasks. The generalizable SceneSplat encoder is a feed-forward 3DGS network that predicts per-primitive vision-language features in a single forward pass without requiring 2D feature maps at inference; the paper extends its training from 2K to 3.5K+ indoor scenes and demonstrates scaling to 49K scenes. VLM embeddings are extracted using SigLIP-2 with a dynamic weighting mechanism that adaptively fuses global frame context, local features with background, and masked object-only features. A scaling study shows that increasing training data from 280 to 3503 scenes lifts ScanNet++ f-mIoU from 0.168 to 0.284, and that indoor-only training partially transfers to outdoor HoliCity scenes (0.263 mIoU) without any outdoor supervision.

## Results

On the SceneSplat-Bench 3D evaluation, the generalizable method SceneSplat achieves the highest foreground mean accuracy (f-mAcc) across all four datasets: 0.5711 on ScanNet20, 0.4198 on ScanNet200, 0.4916 on ScanNet++, and 0.5647 on HoliCity (10-scene subsets). On the full 312-scene ScanNet20 validation set, SceneSplat reaches f-mIoU 0.3540 and f-mAcc 0.5780, matching or exceeding the best per-scene optimization-free method (Gradient-Weighted 3DGS: f-mIoU 0.3573, f-mAcc 0.4638). Critically, SceneSplat requires only 0.24 min per scene at inference versus 3–621 min for per-scene methods, enabling true feed-forward generalization to novel scenes without any per-scene optimization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SceneSplat (generalizable) dominates on f-mAcc across all four benchmark datasets, outperforming the best per-scene optimization method (GOI: 0.4638 f-mAcc on ScanNet20) by over 10 points and the best optimization-free method (Gradient-Weighted 3DGS: 0.5174) by 5.4 points. On f-mIoU, the picture is more nuanced: Gradient-Weighted 3DGS edges out SceneSplat on ScanNet20 (0.4184 vs. 0.2968), but SceneSplat pulls ahead on the full 312-scene set (0.3540 vs. 0.3573) and clearly leads on ScanNet++ (0.2263 vs. 0.0925) and HoliCity (0.3081 vs. 0.2484). Among per-scene optimization methods, GOI is the strongest (0.2295 f-mIoU on ScanNet20), while LangSplat and FMGS are consistently the weakest. Among optimization-free methods, Gradient-Weighted 3DGS and OccamLGS are competitive on ScanNet20 but degrade sharply on ScanNet200 (0.0601 and 0.1204 f-mIoU respectively), whereas SceneSplat maintains 0.1387 f-mIoU. The generalizable approach also uniquely achieves 0.24 min runtime per scene versus 3.2–621 min for all per-scene methods, and in 75% of experiments it even surpasses its own pseudo-label upper bound, demonstrating that learned 3D features extract more meaningful semantics than the 2D VLM embeddings used for pretraining.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends SceneSplat [29], the first generalizable Language Gaussian Splatting method trained on 2K indoor scenes, by scaling its training data to 49K scenes spanning indoor and outdoor environments and by introducing the first systematic 3D-space benchmark for the entire LGS landscape. It positions against three established method families—per-scene optimization (LangSplat, FMGS, Feature3DGS, OpenGaussian, GOI), per-scene optimization-free (Gradient-Weighted 3DGS, LUDVIG, OccamLGS), and generalizable (SceneSplat)—but departs from prior evaluation practice, which relied on small scene subsets, near-training viewpoints, and 2D render metrics. The dataset also moves beyond the object-centric 3DGS datasets (ShapeSplat, 3DAffordSplat, uCO3D) and the limited 6.8K-scene SceneSplat-7K to a full-scene, multi-domain resource.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SceneSplat-Bench (1060 scenes, 325 classes) and SceneSplat-49K (46K curated 3DGS scenes, 29.24B splats) provide the field with the first scalable, standardized evaluation protocol for LGS methods, replacing the ad-hoc 9–21-scene comparisons that dominated prior work. The scaling study showing consistent indoor performance gains from 280→3503 training scenes, and the unexpected indoor-to-outdoor transfer (0.263 mIoU on HoliCity with zero outdoor training), suggest that large-scale 3DGS pretraining can induce cross-domain representations. Key limitations remain: the outdoor benchmark covers only 4 semantic classes, not all 46K scenes carry VLM embeddings, and the generalizable category is represented by a single method, leaving open whether the paradigm advantage holds across architectures.
