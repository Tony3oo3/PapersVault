## Problem

Prior steering vector methods for LLMs target layer outputs or attention head outputs, leaving the internal query and value representation spaces within attention heads unexplored as steering targets. The paper asks whether these spaces are more linearly discriminative with respect to target concepts (a key property motivating steering) and whether steering them enables finer-grained control than existing approaches, including the strong but underutilized baseline of steering attention head inputs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251938/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference time, the method takes a text prompt and injects pre-computed mean-difference steering vectors into the query and/or value representations of selected top-k attention heads at every token position. Steering vectors are estimated from final-token representations of positive and negative examples, and magnitudes are searched over validation sets subject to a degradation constraint (≤3% incoherent outputs). For the joint DISCO-QV variant, the 2D magnitude pair (α_q, α_v) is selected from a grid of pairs satisfying the degradation threshold, rather than a single scalar.

## Method

DISCO Steering injects mean-difference vectors directly into query and value representation spaces within attention heads, rather than steering head outputs or layer outputs. Proposition 1 analytically characterizes the effect: query steering re-weights attention by exponentially scaling the relative attention paid to tokens whose keys align with the steering vector, value steering adds a constant offset to head outputs, and key steering is provably invariant. Proposition 2 establishes that DISCO disentangles Communication Steering (steering the attention head input LN∘f^{l-1}), which rigidly couples all query and value magnitudes to a single scalar α_z, whereas DISCO permits independent α_q and α_v for finer control. Empirically, a significantly larger fraction of query and value spaces exhibit high linear discriminability compared to head output spaces, and DISCO achieves the best performance in 13/16 experiments across LLaMA 3.1 8B and Gemma 2 9B, with up to 19.1% improvement over the runner-up.

## Results

DISCO-QV achieves the best average rank (1.75) across 16 experiments on LLaMA 3.1 8B and Gemma 2 9B, winning 13/16 individual experiments. Headline numbers include 3.49/4.0 on promoting Corrigibility (LLaMA 3.1), 3.61/4.0 on promoting Corrigibility (Gemma 2, a 19.1% improvement over the runner-up), 84.4% multiple-choice accuracy on TruthfulQA (LLaMA 3.1), and 87.7% MC accuracy (Gemma 2). DISCO-V is the second-best DISCO variant (rank 3.19), while DISCO-Q ranks 6.06, confirming that value steering is the stronger single component but query steering provides important complementary gains.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DISCO-QV outperforms CAA [39] (rank 5.31) and ITI [24] (rank 6.56) in the vast majority of experiments, with the largest gaps on promoting Corrigibility (3.49 vs 2.78 on LLaMA; 3.61 vs 2.45 on Gemma) and suppressing Wealth (1.17 vs 1.40 on LLaMA). The strongest non-DISCO baseline is Communication Steering [40] (rank 3.63), which DISCO-QV beats in 12/16 individual metrics; the four losses are on TQA T*I for both models (80.7 vs 82.3 on LLaMA; 86.4 vs 90.5 on Gemma) and on promoting Power and Wealth for Gemma 2. The disentanglement analysis (Prop. 2) explains the gap: Communication Steering rigidly couples α_q and α_v to a single magnitude, while DISCO-QV can exploit the fact that |α_deg| for query steering is 3×–8× larger than for value steering, enabling much larger α_q under the same degradation constraint. DISCO-Q alone (rank 6.06) underperforms DISCO-V (rank 3.19), indicating value steering is the dominant single component, but the joint DISCO-QV variant consistently surpasses either alone, confirming the benefit of independent control over both query and value magnitudes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the mean-difference steering vector framework established by CAA (Rimsky et al., 2024) and ITI (Li et al., 2023) by shifting the steering target from layer outputs and attention head outputs to the internal query and value representation spaces within attention heads. It identifies and formalizes 'Communication Steering'—steering the attention head input space—as a strong but previously underutilized baseline (appearing only in a hyperparameter sweep in Rodriguez et al., 2025), and provides an analytical proof (Proposition 2) that DISCO disentangles this baseline by decoupling the rigid joint modulation of queries and values into independently tunable components. The linear representation hypothesis underpinning all steering vector methods is retained, but the paper's key empirical contribution is showing that a significantly larger fraction of query and value spaces exhibit high linear discriminability compared to the head output spaces that prior work has targeted.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that query and value spaces are more linearly discriminative than head outputs provides a principled reason to prefer these spaces for future steering vector methods, and the disentanglement perspective offers a new design principle: independently tuning query and value magnitudes can outperform rigid joint steering, especially when the optimal magnitudes differ substantially. The unique interpretation of query steering as context-dependent attention re-weighting (Proposition 1) opens a new axis of control that does not directly modify the residual stream, which could be particularly useful for prompts where relevant information is already present in context. Limitations include dependence on the linear representation hypothesis (which may fail for some concept-model pairs) and the observation that DISCO-Q may be less effective when the prompt lacks concept-relevant tokens, necessitating complementary value-space steering. Future work could extend these findings to optimization-based or affine steering approaches and to larger model families.
