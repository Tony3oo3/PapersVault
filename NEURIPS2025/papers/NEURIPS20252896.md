## Problem

Clustering tabular data remains difficult because similarity structures are highly dataset-specific and the absence of supervised signals makes deep learning clustering methods unstable and sensitive to hyperparameter tuning. Existing approaches either require per-dataset optimization (e.g., DEC, IDEC) or are limited to supervised settings (e.g., TabPFN), leaving no zero-shot method that can produce clustering-ready embeddings for arbitrary new tabular datasets without any additional training or fine-tuning.

## Input / Output

ZEUS takes a tabular dataset (up to 2000 samples) with mixed numerical and categorical features as input. Numerical features are standardized and scaled to [-1,1], categorical features are one-hot encoded, and the combined dimensionality is fixed at 30 via zero-padding or PCA reduction. The model outputs a set of embedding vectors (one per data point) in a learned representation space, from which clusters are extracted by applying a simple algorithm such as k-means or GMM; it also yields soft cluster assignment probabilities through a GMM-inspired scoring function with fixed unit covariance.

## Method

ZEUS is a 12-block transformer (6 heads, 512-dim tokens, GeLU) architecturally similar to TabPFN but without label embeddings or a query set, so attention is computed solely over the support set. It is pre-trained on a diverse collection of synthetic datasets generated from a latent-variable prior: K-component Gaussian mixtures (K sampled uniformly from 2–10), optionally transformed by random invertible ResNets to create non-linear cluster shapes, with a fraction of categorical features appended. The training objective is a probabilistic clustering loss inspired by GMMs—maximizing the log-likelihood of correct cluster assignments using softmax over squared distances to data-dependent centroids—augmented with a point-concentration regularizer (minimizing within-cluster distances) and a centroid-separation regularizer (maximizing inter-centroid distances with a capped threshold). At inference, a single forward pass on the new dataset produces embeddings that are clustered by k-means or GMM, requiring no fine-tuning, no labels, and no per-dataset hyperparameter search. The paper further shows that this training procedure constitutes a valid Prior-Data Negative Log-Likelihood, placing ZEUS within the PFN framework and implying it implicitly approximates Bayesian posterior predictive averaging over the synthetic data-generating prior.

## Results

ZEUS achieves the best average ARI on both Real OpenML datasets (57.43) and Synthetic Transformed datasets (86.33), outperforming the second-best method DEC by 1.5 and 6.4 points respectively. On Synthetic Gaussian datasets, ZEUS (89.03) is competitive with k-means (89.90) and DEC (89.35), placing second in average rank. In terms of average rank across all 11 methods, ZEUS ranks first on Real (4.13) and Synthetic Transformed (2.20) datasets, and second on Synthetic Gaussian (2.92). ZEUS also maintains near-constant inference time regardless of input size, scaling similarly to k-means while deep clustering baselines degrade significantly.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ZEUS outperforms all classical baselines (k-means, GMM) and all deep clustering methods (DEC, IDEC, IDC, G-CEALS) on the most challenging Synthetic Transformed datasets, beating the strongest deep method DEC by 6.4 ARI points (86.33 vs. 79.94) and k-means by 11.3 points. On Real OpenML datasets, ZEUS edges out DEC (57.43 vs. 55.93) and k-means (57.43 vs. 55.54) by 1.5 and 1.9 points respectively, while GMM trails significantly at 48.49. On the simpler Synthetic Gaussian datasets, k-means (89.90) and DEC (89.35) slightly outperform ZEUS (89.03), though all three are within 1 point. The representation-based baselines TabPFN (31.32/55.97/15.66) and SCARF (26.95/8.32/2.48) perform substantially worse, indicating that their embeddings—designed for classification—do not naturally support clustering without additional fine-tuning. Among AE-based methods, DEC is consistently the strongest, while G-CEALS is the weakest across all dataset groups.

## Relation to Prior Work

ZEUS extends the Prior-Data Fitted Network (PFN) framework from Hollmann et al. (TabPFN) and Müller et al. from supervised in-context classification to fully unsupervised clustering, removing the need for label embeddings and query sets during inference. Conceptually, it departs from deep clustering methods (DEC, IDEC, G-CEALS) that optimize arbitrary per-dataset heuristics by instead learning to invert a data-generation process, framing clustering as supervised label prediction during pre-training on synthetic mixtures. The synthetic data generation leverages Invertible Residual Networks to produce non-linearly separable cluster shapes while preserving cluster identity, and the probabilistic scoring mechanism is explicitly grounded in Gaussian Mixture Model membership probabilities with fixed diagonal covariances.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ZEUS offers a practical plug-and-play solution that eliminates the per-dataset hyperparameter tuning and training loops that make deep clustering methods fragile in unsupervised settings, reducing clustering to a single forward pass plus k-means. Key limitations include the fixed input size (≤30 features, ≤2000 samples) inherited from the TabPFN architecture, and a demonstrated sensitivity to prior misspecification on real data (mitigated but not resolved by regularizers). Open questions include whether richer or adaptive data-generating priors could close the remaining gap on real datasets, whether the approach scales to larger tabular problems (as TabPFN v2 suggests is feasible), and whether the enforced circular-cluster structure (identity covariances) is a fundamental constraint or a removable inductive bias.
