## Problem

Standard LLM benchmarks measure only final-answer accuracy, leaving it unclear whether models arrive at solutions through creative insight or exhaustive brute-force search. No existing benchmark systematically probes the *type* of reasoning strategy a model employs, making it impossible to assess whether LLMs can generalize to novel problems where brute force is intractable.

## Input / Output

The benchmark feeds narrative brainteaser problems (242 Math, 236 Logic, scraped from Braingle) to LLMs under four prompting conditions (CoT, Math, Hint, Math+Hint), optionally accompanied by a problem-specific hint or a rewritten competition-style mathematical restatement. Beyond the final answer, the evaluation pipeline produces a brute-force/creative classification of each solution, a step-by-step breakdown labeling each step as creative or rudimentary, and a correctness judgment rendered by OpenAI o3 as judge (validated at 99.3% agreement with human annotators). A separate self-correction probe swaps the model's flawed solution with the human solution to test whether models can detect and fix errors or will 'false confess' and accept the flawed answer as ground truth.

## Method

The paper introduces BRAINGLE BRAINTEASER, a benchmark of expert-authored mathematical and logical puzzles selected for difficulty (top 250 per category) and curated to minimize knowledge barriers while emphasizing creative insight. The core analytical framework decomposes LLM reasoning into four probeable sub-tasks: (1) semantic parsing from narrative to formal math, (2) solution generation under varying prompt constraints (including explicit brute-force discouragement), (3) informed self-correction against ground-truth solutions, and (4) step-level breakdown distinguishing creative insights from rudimentary computation. Seven models (OpenAI o3, DeepSeek R1, DeepSeek V3, Gemini 2.5 Flash, and three DeepSeek R1 distilled variants) are evaluated across all conditions, with OpenAI o3 serving as an LLM-as-a-judge for correctness and brute-force/creative classification. The key finding is that even the strongest models default to brute force on harder problems despite available creative shortcuts, that narrative-to-math rewriting yields only modest gains, and that models are susceptible to 'false confession' when tricked into correcting a correct solution using a flawed one.

## Results

On the BRAINGLE BRAINTEASER benchmark, OpenAI o3 achieves the highest accuracy at 79.6% (Math) and 68.4% (Logic) under the standard CoT prompt, improving to 81.2% and 74.4% respectively with the Math Prompt + Hint condition. DeepSeek R1 follows at 66.8% (Math) and 44.6% (Logic) with CoT, while Gemini 2.5 Flash reaches 66.0% (Math) and 49.2% (Logic). On the top-50 hardest problems, even o3 drops to 66–68% (Math) and 50–54% (Logic), confirming the benchmark is non-saturated. Hints consistently boost performance across all models, with the Math Prompt + Hint yielding the largest gains for mid-tier models (e.g., Gemini 2.5 Flash: 66.0% → 72.0% on Math).

## Baselines & Comparisons

OpenAI o3 outperforms all other evaluated models by a wide margin on both Math and Logic subsets, achieving 79.6–81.2% on Math versus 66.0–72.8% for DeepSeek R1 and Gemini 2.5 Flash, and 68.4–74.4% on Logic versus 44.6–53.6% for those same models. DeepSeek R1 and Gemini 2.5 Flash are closely competitive, with Gemini 2.5 Flash slightly ahead on Logic (49.2% vs. 44.6% CoT) while DeepSeek R1 leads on Math (66.8% vs. 66.0% CoT); under the Math+Hint condition, Gemini 2.5 Flash edges out DeepSeek R1 on Math (72.0% vs. 72.8%) but DeepSeek R1 pulls ahead on Logic (50.6% vs. 53.6%). DeepSeek V3 trails both by roughly 8–12 points on Math and 7–12 points on Logic. The distilled models (Qwen 1.5B, Qwen 14B, Llama 70B) show a steep performance drop, with the 1.5B model achieving only 17.2% on Math and 4.0% on Logic, indicating that reasoning capability does not transfer well to small distilled variants. Across all models, the Math Prompt + Hint condition yields the best results, with hints providing a consistent 2–6 point boost over CoT alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LLM reasoning evaluation literature (MATH, AIME, OlympiadBench, Zebralogic) by shifting the evaluation target from final-answer accuracy to the *type* of reasoning strategy employed—specifically, creative insight versus brute-force enumeration. It also builds on the 'reasoning beyond accuracy' line (Lightman et al., ROSCOE) but takes a distinct angle: rather than scoring intermediate step correctness, it classifies whole solutions as creative or brute-force and decomposes the reasoning pipeline into sub-tasks (semantic parsing, self-correction, step breakdown, hint utilization). The benchmark itself departs from prior puzzle datasets (BRAINTEASER by Jiang et al., Zebralogic) by restricting to expert-authored mathematical and logical puzzles and by providing structured metadata (hints, step counts, creative/rudimentary step labels) that enables fine-grained analysis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that even frontier models (o3, DeepSeek-R1) systematically default to brute force on harder problems—despite available creative shortcuts—suggests that current training objectives (optimizing final-answer accuracy) may not incentivize the kind of insight-driven reasoning needed for genuinely novel problems. The 'false confession' result (models accept a correct human solution is wrong when prompted to 'correct' it using a flawed LLM solution) raises concerns about the robustness of LLM self-evaluation in iterative refinement pipelines. Open questions include whether shifting training objectives or prompting strategies can reduce brute-force reliance, whether the effect persists as models scale further, and whether the benchmark's single-source provenance (Braingle) limits generalizability. The lack of error bars (due to API cost) and reliance on LLM-as-judge for evaluation are notable methodological limitations.
