## Problem

Prior algorithms for fair matroid submodular maximization (FMMSM) either relax lower-bound fairness constraints by a factor of 2—yielding no guarantee when ℓ=1 (the perfect matching case)—or satisfy constraints only in expectation while also violating upper bounds. This paper closes the gap by providing an algorithm that satisfies each lower bound to within an arbitrarily small (1−ε) factor while maintaining a constant-factor approximation to the submodular objective and satisfying upper bounds exactly.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes a ground set partitioned into C colored groups, a monotone submodular function oracle, a matroid independence oracle, per-color fairness bounds (ℓ_c, u_c), and a tunable parameter ε∈(0,1). It outputs a set S that is independent and upper-fair with probability 1 (not merely in expectation), has expected size at least (1−ε)N where N is the maximum feasible size, satisfies each lower bound to within (1−ε) in expectation, and achieves expected submodular value at least 0.499·ε·OPT_MatInt. The output also enjoys Chernoff-style high-probability tail bounds on both size and total fairness violation, though no such bound is possible for the f-value in the worst case.

## Method

The algorithm iteratively transforms a high-value upper-fair set Y (obtained via greedy or local search) toward a maximum-cardinality fair set P by applying randomly selected disjoint alternating or augmenting paths from a matroid intersection exchange graph. At each iteration, the exchange graph between Y and P is decomposed into k disjoint paths using two carefully constructed matchings (M← from a matroid exchange lemma and M→ matching same-colored elements), followed by an asymmetric shortcutting process that preserves matroid independence and color counts. A single path is chosen uniformly at random and applied, so the expected value loss at step i is only 1/(k−i+1) of the current f-value, yielding a telescoping product that retains an ε fraction of the initial value after (1−ε)k iterations. The randomization ensures every undersaturated color has (1−ε) probability of being corrected, providing per-group guarantees rather than only aggregate ones. A deterministic variant for matroid intersection (without fairness) is also derived via an analogous path-decomposition lemma.

## Results

The paper evaluates on three standard benchmarks (graph coverage on Pokec, exemplar-based clustering on Portuguese Bank Marketing, and movie recommendation on MovieLens 1M), measuring submodular objective value f(S) and total fairness violation fav(S) across a range of solution size scaling factors r. In two of three scenarios (coverage and movies), all OUR(ε) variants (ε ∈ {0.2, 0.5, 0.8}) achieve higher f-value than every other baseline (TWOPASS, LBMI, RANDOM) while maintaining lower fairness violations than UBMI. The ε parameter provides a smooth, tunable trade-off: smaller ε (e.g., 0.2) yields lower fairness violation at modest cost in utility, while larger ε (e.g., 0.8) recovers more utility at the expense of higher violation. The theoretical guarantee is E[f(S)] ≥ 0.499·ε·OPT_MatInt, though empirical values are substantially better than this bound.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is TWOPASS from El Halabi et al. (ICML 2023), which relaxes lower-bound fairness constraints by a factor of 2 and achieves an almost 1/4-approximation. In the coverage and movie recommendation experiments, TWOPASS is strictly dominated by both OUR(0.2) and OUR(0.5) on both metrics simultaneously: OUR achieves higher submodular value while incurring lower total fairness violation. In the clustering experiment, the picture is less clear, with TWOPASS generally performing better than OUR variants. The paper also compares against LBMI (which guarantees zero fairness violation but sacrifices substantial utility), UBMI (which maximizes utility by ignoring lower bounds entirely and thus has the highest fairness violation), and RANDOM. Our algorithm's key advantage over TWOPASS is that it satisfies each individual group's lower bound in expectation to within a (1−ε) factor, rather than only guaranteeing ⌊ℓ/2⌋ per group, and it satisfies upper bounds exactly with probability 1.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the FMMSM problem studied by El Halabi et al. (ICML 2023, streaming) and El Halabi et al. (AISTATS 2024, offline), both of which circumvented the difficulty of lower-bound fairness constraints by relaxing them: the former by a factor of 2, the latter by satisfying constraints only in expectation while violating upper bounds. The key departure here is a randomized algorithm that satisfies upper bounds and matroid constraints exactly while achieving (1−ε) satisfaction of every individual lower bound, using matroid intersection exchange graphs with an asymmetric shortcutting process to construct disjoint alternating and augmenting paths. A deterministic analogue for matroid intersection (Theorem 1.4) is also provided, building on the local-search framework of Lee, Sviridenko, and Vondrák (2010).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The factor-2 relaxation in prior work meant zero guarantee for the ℓ=1 case (perfect matching), a fundamental special case; this paper closes that gap by achieving arbitrarily close to full fairness with a constant-factor approximation. The tunable ε parameter gives practitioners a practical knob to balance utility against fairness, and the empirical results on coverage, clustering, and recommendation benchmarks show the method is competitive with unconstrained baselines in most settings. Open questions remain: whether a constant-factor approximation exists for MSPM without any fairness violation, whether FMMSM has superconstant hardness, and how the approach extends to non-monotone objectives or the streaming setting.
