## Problem

Prior universal approximation results for Transformers in in-context learning assumed inputs drawn from the full Euclidean space, ignoring the discrete, finite-vocabulary nature of real language model inputs. This paper asks whether single-layer Transformers restricted to a finite vocabulary can still achieve the universal approximation property (UAP) for ICL, and what role positional encoding plays in enabling or preventing this capability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254513/cropped_page18_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a query vector x ∈ R^{d_x−1} (augmented to x̃ = [x; 1]) and a context of n demonstration pairs (x^(i), y^(i)) drawn from a finite vocabulary V = V_x × V_y, with an absolute positional encoding P added to each token at its position. The Transformer parameters (Q, K, V) are fixed; only the context content and length n are adjustable. The output is a vector in R^{d_y} that approximates a target continuous function f(x) uniformly over a compact domain K, with the context length n unbounded in theory.

## Method

The key technical contribution is establishing an exact equivalence between single-layer Transformers (under a sparse partition assumption on Q, K, V) and one-hidden-layer FNNs, where the context tokens serve as the FNN's weights and biases (Lemma 3). Without positional encoding, the finite vocabulary restricts the achievable FNN parameters to a finite set, making the span of realizable functions finite-dimensional (or, for softmax, bounded in zero-point count), so UAP provably fails (Theorem 6). With positional encoding, if the set S = V_x + P_x is dense in R^{d_x}, the Kronecker Approximation Theorem guarantees that vocabulary tokens shifted by positional encodings can approximate arbitrary FNN weights, restoring UAP (Theorem 7). For ReLU and exponential activations, the density requirement is relaxed to a bounded region or a local neighborhood, respectively, by exploiting positive homogeneity and derivative-to-polynomial connections (Theorem 8).

## Results

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical experiments or quantitative metrics. The key results are: (1) Theorem 6 proves that single-layer Transformers with a finite vocabulary and no positional encoding cannot achieve the universal approximation property (UAP) for in-context learning; (2) Theorem 7 proves that adding positional encoding (with the set S = V_x + P_x dense in R^{d_x}) restores the UAP; (3) Theorem 8 relaxes the density condition, showing that for ReLU activations, S only needs to be dense in [-1,1]^{d_x}, and for exponential activations, S only needs to be dense in a neighborhood of a single point w*.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper contains no empirical baselines or quantitative comparisons, as it is entirely theoretical. The main theoretical contrast is between the no-positional-encoding setting (Theorem 6: UAP fails) and the with-positional-encoding setting (Theorem 7: UAP holds), establishing positional encoding as the critical enabler. Compared to prior theoretical work, the paper extends Petrov et al. [12] by introducing finite vocabulary constraints and positional encoding, and extends Yun et al. [10] by fixing Transformer parameters and using context as the control variable. The paper also contrasts with Luo et al. [11], who showed relative positional encoding does not yield UAP, by demonstrating that absolute positional encoding with appropriate density conditions does.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on UAP of Transformers for ICL (Yun et al. 2020, Petrov et al. 2024) by introducing a critical practical constraint: inputs are drawn from a finite vocabulary rather than the full Euclidean space. It builds directly on the FNN UAP of Leshno et al. (1993) and the attention formulation of Cheng et al. (2024) and Ahn et al. (2024), establishing a precise algebraic equivalence between single-layer Transformers and one-hidden-layer FNNs under a sparse parameterization. The key departure from prior ICL-UAP work is the demonstration that this equivalence breaks down under finite vocabulary without positional encoding, and that positional encoding (specifically, density of the set V_x + P_x) is what restores universal approximation power.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This provides the first rigorous theoretical justification for why positional encoding is not merely a practical convenience but a fundamental requirement for the approximation power of Transformers operating on discrete token vocabularies. The negative result (Theorem 6) shows that the permutation equivariance of self-attention, combined with finite vocabulary, creates an insurmountable expressiveness barrier, while the positive result (Theorem 7) identifies precise density conditions under which positional encoding overcomes this barrier. Limitations are significant: results are restricted to single-layer Transformers with absolute positional encoding and primarily element-wise activations, leaving multi-layer architectures, relative/rotary positional encodings, and the softmax-with-PE case open. The Kronecker Approximation Theorem connection suggests that positional encodings implementing dense enumerations (e.g., via iterative algorithms) are theoretically sufficient, which could inform future PE design.
