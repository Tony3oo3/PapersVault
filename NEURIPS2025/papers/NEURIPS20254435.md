## Problem

Existing DDI prediction methods learn drug representations from known interactions and combine them via post-fusion (concatenation or summation of individual embeddings), resulting in weak structural fusion and limited exploration of the vast space of unlabeled drug pairs. This makes them particularly ineffective for novel and few-shot drugs where interaction data is scarce.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254435/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of 2D molecular images (224×224×3, rendered from SMILES via RDKit) and blends them at the fragment level: each 16×16×3 patch position is randomly assigned to one of the two drugs via a binomial sampling process, producing a single fused image. The output is a probability distribution over K DDI event types (multi-class softmax), with the [CLS] token of the pretrained encoder feeding a classification MLP. During pretraining, the decoder additionally reconstructs both original molecular images pixel-by-pixel.

## Method

S²VM is a self-supervised encoder-decoder framework that pretrains a unified ViT encoder (12 layers) on ~200M unlabeled drug pairs by blending their visual fragments into a single input and reconstructing both original molecules from the shared latent representation. The key architectural choice is pre-fusion: molecular fragments are randomly interleaved before encoding, forcing the encoder to model cross-molecular structural correlations rather than encoding drugs independently. A lightweight 4-layer ViT decoder with two linear heads recovers the original images via MSE loss, and theoretical analysis via mutual information decomposition shows this objective simultaneously optimizes extrinsic (contrastive/generative) relations between drugs and intrinsic (recovery) structure within each molecule. For downstream DDI prediction, the pretrained encoder is frozen and the [CLS] embedding is classified with a small MLP, achieving state-of-the-art Macro-F1 on Deng's and Ryu's benchmarks with particularly large gains in few-shot and inductive (new-drug) settings.

## Results

On Deng's dataset, S²VM achieves 91.05% Accuracy and 82.12% Macro-F1, surpassing the best baseline MRCGNN (89.79% ACC, 77.91% Macro-F1) by 1.26% and 3.83% respectively. On Ryu's dataset, S²VM reaches 95.86% Accuracy and 92.07% Macro-F1, improving over MRCGNN by 0.19% and 3.13% in ACC and Macro-F1, with a 4.21% gain in Macro-Recall. In few-shot settings, S²VM achieves 91.76% Macro-F1 (Few) and 73.33% Macro-F1 (Rare) on Deng's dataset, and 95.37% and 98.44% on Ryu's dataset, outperforming ImageMol by 2.0% and 6.66% (Deng's) and 3.81% and 6.41% (Ryu's).

## Baselines & Comparisons

S²VM outperforms all 12 baselines across both main datasets. The closest competitor is MRCGNN (Xiong et al., AAAI 2023), which leverages multi-relational contrastive learning on biomedical knowledge graphs; S²VM beats it by 3.83% Macro-F1 on Deng's and 3.13% on Ryu's. Among image-based methods, ImageMol (Zeng et al., Nature MI 2022) and CGIP (Xiang et al., 2023) encode individual molecular images separately and then concatenate embeddings; S²VM surpasses ImageMol by 4.29% Macro-F1 on Deng's and 4.50% on Ryu's, demonstrating that joint structural blending of paired molecules is more effective than post-hoc concatenation. Structure-only methods (DeepDDI, SSI-DDI) and knowledge-graph-only methods (KGNN, GoGNN) trail significantly, with SSI-DDI achieving only 42.16% Macro-F1 on Deng's. In few-shot settings, S²VM extends its lead over META-DDIE (Deng et al., 2022) by 7.64% Macro-F1 in the Rare setting on Deng's, and over ImageMol by 6.41% on Ryu's Rare. For inductive (new-drug) scenarios on TWOSIDES, S²VM outperforms STNN-DDI (Yu et al., 2022) and CSMDDI (Liu et al., 2022), confirming that self-supervised pretraining on 200M unlabeled drug pairs enables generalization to unseen molecules.

## Relation to Prior Work

S²VM departs from the dominant paradigm in DDI prediction where drugs are encoded separately (via SMILES, graphs, or individual molecular images) and their embeddings are concatenated or compared post-hoc. It builds on the visual molecule representation line (ImageMol, CGIP) but repurposes the ViT encoder-decoder architecture for a fundamentally different objective: jointly encoding a *pair* of molecules through fragment-level blending and self-supervised reconstruction, rather than encoding single molecules. The pre-fusion strategy (blending visual fragments before encoding) is contrasted against post-fusion (concatenating separate embeddings), and the self-supervised objective on ~200M unlabeled drug pairs extends the masked-reconstruction idea from single-molecule MAE/I-JEPA to the paired-molecule setting. The theoretical grounding via mutual information decomposition (Proposition 2) connects the reconstruction loss to both contrastive/generative and recovery objectives, providing a principled justification absent in prior DDI methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete gap: prior DDI methods are limited by training only on known interactions, leaving the vast space of unlabeled drug pairs unexplored, which is particularly problematic for novel and few-shot drugs. The consistent improvements across common, few-shot, and inductive settings (up to 4.21% Macro-F1) suggest that structural fusion at the fragment level captures interaction-relevant information that separate encoders miss. However, the 2D-only representation ignores 3D conformational effects, the pretraining data (random PubChem pairs) may not reflect real pharmacological pairing distributions, and the computational cost of 200M pairs limits accessibility. Open questions include whether 3D-aware blending, pharmacologically informed pair sampling, or integration with knowledge-graph signals could further close the gap to biological ground truth.
