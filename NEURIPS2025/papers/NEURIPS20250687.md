## Problem

Unlike supervised learning, naively scaling deep RL networks (widening or deepening layers) consistently degrades performance, yet the underlying cause remained unclear. This paper identifies the connection between the convolutional encoder output and the subsequent dense layers—the 'bottleneck'—as the primary factor limiting scaling, and shows that prior successful approaches (SoftMoE, pruning, tokenization) all implicitly target this bottleneck.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250687/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The network consumes raw pixel observations (e.g., 84×84 Atari frames) through a convolutional encoder φ that produces a 3D feature tensor of shape H×W×C. Standard practice flattens this tensor before feeding it to dense layers ψ, creating a bottleneck with H×W×C×dim(ψ) parameters. With GAP, the spatial dimensions are collapsed via per-channel averaging, yielding a C-dimensional vector that feeds ψ with only C×dim(ψ) bottleneck parameters, ultimately producing Q-values for action selection.

## Method

The paper's core contribution is a diagnostic analysis showing that the bottleneck between encoder φ and dense layers ψ is the root cause of scaling failure: scaling the bottleneck alone reproduces the full performance degradation, and the bottleneck exhibits the highest fraction of dormant neurons. Building on this, the authors propose Global Average Pooling (GAP) as a minimal architectural intervention—replacing the flatten operation with per-channel spatial averaging (g^c = (1/HW)Σ F_ij^c)—which reduces the bottleneck from H×W×C×dim(ψ) to C×dim(ψ) parameters while preserving spatial structure within each feature map. This simple change unlocks scaling across width (×1 to ×8), depth, and combined width-depth dimensions, outperforms or matches SoftMoE-1, pruning, RigL, and layer normalization baselines, and generalizes across architectures (Impala ResNet, original CNN), agents (Rainbow, DER, SAC), and domains (Atari 2600, Procgen, Atari 100K, DMC) with no additional hyperparameters or computational overhead.

## Results

On the 20-game Atari subset with the Rainbow agent at 100M environment steps, GAP achieves the highest IQM score among all compared methods, outperforming pruning, static sparsity, RigL, weight decay, and layer normalization, while matching SoftMoE-1. The improvement is consistent across network scales (×1 through ×8), both the Impala/ResNet and original CNN architectures, and the full 60-game Atari suite. GAP also yields gains in the sample-efficient regime (high replay ratios), on Procgen, Atari100K (with DER and DrQ agents), and continuous control (SAC on DMC), confirming broad generalizability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GAP outperforms all five baseline categories on IQM at 100M steps: it beats pruning (Obando Ceron et al., 2024), static sparsity and RigL (Graesser et al., 2022), weight decay (Sokar et al., 2023), and layer normalization (Nauman et al., 2024) across all tested scales. Against SoftMoE-1 (Sokar et al., 2025), GAP achieves comparable performance while being substantially simpler (no token construction or MoE projection overhead) and more computationally efficient, as shown by lower GPU-hours per game in Figure 7. The advantage of GAP over sparse methods is particularly notable in runtime: despite similar effective parameter density, sparse methods incur high wall-clock time because sparsity is only simulated via parameter masking rather than true sparse computation. GAP also generalizes beyond the 20-game subset to the full 60-game Atari suite, Procgen, Atari100K, and DMC, where it consistently improves scaled-network performance over the unmodified baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper synthesizes and reinterprets a recent cluster of scaling methods for pixel-based deep RL—SoftMoE (Obando Ceron* et al., 2024), tokenization (Sokar et al., 2025), pruning (Obando Ceron et al., 2024), and sparse training (Graesser et al., 2022)—by identifying a common structural target: the bottleneck between the convolutional encoder φ and the dense head ψ. Rather than proposing yet another architectural modification, the authors diagnose why these methods work (they all reduce the effective density or restructure the bottleneck) and then replace them with a single, parameter-free operation: global average pooling. The paper thus carves new ground not by extending any one prior method, but by providing a unifying mechanistic explanation and a dramatically simpler alternative.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: a one-line architectural change (GAP) matches or exceeds the performance of SoftMoE, pruning, and sparse training while being far simpler to implement and computationally cheaper, which could accelerate adoption of scaled RL networks. The diagnostic framework (dormant neuron analysis, Grad-CAM saliency, bottleneck parameter counting) gives the field a reusable toolkit for understanding scaling failures beyond this specific fix. Key open questions include whether the findings extend to non-pixel environments or architectures without a clear encoder/dense separation, and whether GAP is complementary to more sophisticated representation-learning objectives (e.g., proto-value networks, bisimulation metrics). The authors note that concurrent works independently arrived at similar pooling-based ideas, reinforcing the signal but also suggesting the bottleneck insight was ripe for discovery.
