## Problem

Most zero-shot time series forecasting models rely on transformer architectures, which, despite strong in-context learning in language, often underperform in time series forecasting where recurrent models like LSTMs have the edge. Conversely, LSTMs excel at state-tracking for long-horizon forecasting but lack the in-context learning capability needed for zero-shot generalization. TiRex addresses this gap by leveraging xLSTM, a modern LSTM variant that combines state-tracking with competitive in-context learning, and introduces a training strategy to fully exploit its recurrent memory for multi-patch forecasting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251929/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

TiRex takes a univariate time series context of length 2048, applies z-score instance normalization, and segments it into non-overlapping patches of size 32 (with a binary presence/absence mask concatenated to each patch). For multi-patch horizons exceeding 32 steps, future input positions are encoded as missing values rather than autoregressive point estimates, allowing the internal recurrent state to propagate uncertainty across patches. The model outputs nine equidistant quantile levels (0.1 through 0.9) per time step in the forecast horizon, trained with a quantile loss, enabling probabilistic forecasts with coherent uncertainty bounds over long horizons.

## Method

TiRex is a 35M-parameter decoder-only model built from 12 xLSTM blocks, each containing an sLSTM module (chosen over mLSTM for its true recurrent pathway and state-tracking) followed by a feed-forward network, with RMSNorm pre-normalization and residual connections. The key training innovation is Contiguous Patch Masking (CPM), which randomly masks consecutive patches during pre-training so the model learns to forecast across gaps, directly mirroring the multi-patch inference structure where future inputs are treated as missing values. Three data augmentations—amplitude modulation (piecewise-linear scale trends), censor augmentation (clipping at a random quantile), and spike injection (periodic transient events with randomized kernel shapes)—are applied at training time to broaden the model's exposure to diverse temporal dynamics. The model is pre-trained on 47.5 million time series samples (Chronos data with TSMixup, synthetic Gaussian Process series, and GiftEval pre-training data) for 500K steps with a context length of 2048 and patch size 32, using AdamW with cosine scheduling.

## Results

On the GiftEval-ZS benchmark (81 zero-shot evaluation settings spanning short, medium, and long horizons), TiRex achieves a CRPS of 0.411 (±0.002 over 6 seeds) and MASE of 0.647, outperforming the next best zero-shot models (TimesFM-2.0, TabPFN-TS, Chronos-Bolt-Base) which score 0.459, 0.463, and 0.481 respectively. On the Chronos-ZS benchmark (27 datasets, primarily short-term), TiRex achieves the best WQL of 0.592 and second-best MASE of 0.776 (closely behind TabPFN-TS). TiRex achieves these results with only 35M parameters, making it over 11× faster than TimesFM-2.0 (500M) and over 2176× faster than TabPFN-TS at inference.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TiRex outperforms all zero-shot pre-trained baselines on both benchmarks. On GiftEval-ZS, TiRex (CRPS 0.411) leads TimesFM-2.0 (0.459), TabPFN-TS (0.463), and Chronos-Bolt-Base (0.481) by 10–15% in CRPS, with the gap most pronounced on long-term forecasts where TiRex becomes the first zero-shot model to surpass task-specific PatchTST and TFT. On Chronos-ZS, TiRex achieves the best WQL (0.592) but trails TabPFN-TS slightly on MASE (0.776 vs. 0.773); however, Moirai's strong performance on this benchmark is attributed to 82% training-data overlap, whereas TiRex has zero overlap. Against task-specific models (PatchTST, TFT, DLinear, DeepAR, N-BEATS), TiRex matches or exceeds their per-dataset performance on long-term horizons while operating in a fully zero-shot setting. TiRex also demonstrates superior inference efficiency: it is 11× faster than TimesFM-2.0, 4× faster than Chronos-Bolt-Base, and 2176× faster than TabPFN-TS, while using 5–14× fewer parameters than the leading transformer-based competitors.

## Relation to Prior Work

TiRex departs from the dominant transformer-based paradigm in zero-shot time series forecasting (Chronos, TimesFM, Moirai, TabPFN-TS) by adopting xLSTM's scalar LSTM (sLSTM) as its backbone, specifically for its state-tracking capability that transformers and parallelizable RNNs lack. It builds directly on the xLSTM architecture (Beck et al., 2024) and the xLSTM 7B block design (Beck et al., 2025), substituting mLSTM with sLSTM to preserve true recurrence. The paper also incorporates patching (from PatchTST/Moirai), quantile loss (from DeepAR), and TSMixup (from Chronos), but its key conceptual departure is treating multi-patch forecasting as missing-value imputation rather than autoregressive generation, enabled by the novel Contiguous Patch Masking training strategy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TiRex demonstrates that a 35M-parameter recurrent model can outperform 200–500M-parameter transformer models on zero-shot forecasting while being 11×–2176× faster, challenging the assumption that scale and attention are prerequisites for strong in-context time series learning. CPM is a simple, adoptable training technique that could benefit other architectures. Key limitations include univariate-only modeling, limited hyperparameter tuning, and the open question of whether the state-tracking advantage persists in multivariate or covariate-rich settings. Future work on leveraging learned representations for classification or anomaly detection could broaden the model's utility beyond point/quantile forecasting.
