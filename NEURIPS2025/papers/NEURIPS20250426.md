## Problem

Existing mechanistic interpretability methods for LLMs target few-token outputs and limited component search spaces (e.g., attention heads), making them inapplicable to safety alignment, which is a high-level ability requiring open-ended generation and extensive model parameters. It remains unclear which specific model components are causally responsible for safety behaviors acquired through alignment training, and how these components relate to the well-known alignment tax between safety and helpfulness.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250426/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework takes a pair of LLMs (an SFT model and a DPO-aligned model sharing the same base architecture) along with safety-related prompts, and produces a sparse set of safety neurons (~5% of all MLP intermediate-layer neurons) ranked by change score. The dynamic activation patching procedure consumes these neurons during autoregressive generation, replacing their activations token-by-token with cached values from the aligned model, yielding a patched generation whose safety is scored by a cost model. A secondary application uses the cached activations at the prompt's last token as features for a logistic regression classifier that predicts whether the upcoming generation will be harmful before any tokens are produced.

## Method

The paper proposes a two-stage neuron-level framework. Stage 1 (inference-time activation contrasting) computes a change score for each MLP intermediate-layer neuron as the root-mean-square difference between the SFT and DPO models' activations on the SFT model's own generation, ensuring comparability by feeding both models the same token sequence. Stage 2 (dynamic activation patching) verifies causal effects in an open-ended setting by iteratively: (1) running the DPO model to cache target neuron activations, (2) running the SFT model with those neurons' activations replaced while all others remain unchanged, and (3) appending the predicted next token and repeating until generation completes. The causal effect is defined as the ratio of the patched model's safety improvement to the full DPO's improvement, with values near 1 indicating faithful explanation. The approach relies on (IA)³ parameter-efficient fine-tuning applied exclusively to MLP down-projection layers, which rescales activations without altering neuron key-value vectors, thereby preserving the interpretability of individual neurons that full fine-tuning would destroy.

## Results

Patching only ~5% of MLP neurons (identified via inference-time activation contrasting) restores over 90% of DPO's safety performance on SFT models across all four LLMs (Llama2-7B, Mistral-7B, Gemma-7B, Qwen2.5-3B), with causal effects ranging from +68% to +101% on red-teaming benchmarks (Beavertails, RedTeam, HarmBench, JailBreakLLMs). General capabilities are essentially unaffected, with ΔGen values within ±0.05 across Wikitext-2, MMLU, GSM8K, BBH, and TruthfulQA. The identified safety neurons are robust to training randomness (Spearman correlation >0.95 across 5 random seeds) and transferable across different red-teaming benchmarks without dataset-specific tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two ablation-based baselines: Pruning (Wei et al., 2024), which uses Wanda scores to locate parameters with greatest impact on attack success rate, and SN-Tune (Zhao et al., 2025), which identifies safety neurons based on their contribution to the residual stream. The proposed method (dynamic activation patching on top-5% change-score neurons) dramatically outperforms both baselines across all four models and all four red-teaming benchmarks. For example, on Llama2 SFT, the proposed method achieves +101% causal effect on Beavertails versus only +3% (Pruning) and +13% (SN-Tune); on Gemma SFT, it reaches +96% versus +9% and −9% respectively. Notably, SN-Tune sometimes *degrades* safety (negative values on Gemma and Qwen2.5), while Pruning is consistently near-zero, suggesting ablation-based methods are confounded by the 'Hydra effect' where ablating components triggers compensatory behaviors. General performance (ΔGen) remains essentially unchanged for all methods, confirming the gap is in safety-specific causal effect rather than overall model disruption.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends mechanistic interpretability (MI) techniques—particularly activation patching from Vig et al. (2020) and Meng et al. (2022)—to the safety alignment domain, where prior MI methods are ill-suited due to their focus on few-token outputs and limited component search spaces (e.g., attention heads). It builds on the neuron-level interpretability lineage (Geva et al. 2021 key-value memories, Dai et al. 2022 knowledge neurons, Gurnee et al. 2023 sparse probing) but departs from ablation-based safety neuron identification (Wei et al. 2024, Zhao et al. 2025) by using an enhancement-based approach (dynamic activation patching) to avoid the 'Hydra effect' of compensatory behaviors. The inference-time activation contrasting step is a novel adaptation that leverages paired aligned/unaligned models to compute neuron-level change scores without requiring ground-truth labels.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first mechanistic, neuron-level explanation of the alignment tax: safety and helpfulness alignment compete for control over the same ~5% of neurons but require different activation patterns, offering a concrete target for future alignment algorithms that might decouple these objectives. The practical safeguard application (76.2% accuracy predicting harmful outputs from just 1500 neuron activations before generation) demonstrates immediate utility, though it requires activations from an already-aligned model. Key open questions include how safety neurons emerge during pre-training, whether full-parameter fine-tuning (vs. the IA3 used here) alters the neuron landscape, and how these neurons mechanistically influence generation beyond their activation patterns.
