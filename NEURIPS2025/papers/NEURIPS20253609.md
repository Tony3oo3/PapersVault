## Problem

Existing multivariate time series classification methods face a fundamental trade-off: independent per-variable modeling ignores inter-variable dependencies, while joint variable mixing causes over-smoothing and blurs semantic distinctions, especially for heterogeneous variable types. The paper asks how to structure variable interactions in a way that preserves both within-group coherence and cross-group diversity without degenerating into either extreme.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253609/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a multivariate time series tensor X ∈ R^{C×L} (C channels, L timesteps) and outputs a single class label via a projection head. The number of channels varies widely across datasets (3 to 963), sequence lengths range from 29 to 1751 timesteps, and the number of classes spans 2 to 10. A key preprocessing step uses FFT to identify dominant periodic frequencies, which determines the window length P used throughout the architecture; zero-padding is applied when L is not divisible by P.

## Method

SwinGroupNet (SGN) is a CNN-based architecture built on three modules. First, Variable Group Embedding (VGE) computes Brownian Distance Covariance between all variable pairs, applies K-means to obtain G cluster logits, and uses Gumbel-Softmax (soft during training, hard at inference) to assign variables to groups, with a cosine-similarity regularization loss to stabilize assignments. Second, Multi-Scale Group Window Mixing (MGWM) partitions each periodic window into segments and applies multi-scale depthwise convolutions (odd kernel sizes 1, 3, …, 2K−1) for temporal feature extraction, followed by separate pointwise convolutions for intra-group and inter-group variable interactions, decoupling fine-grained and higher-order dependencies. Third, Periodic Window Shifting and Merging (PWSM) performs a cyclic left shift by P/2 to enable cross-window communication (analogous to Swin Transformer's shifted windows), then progressively merges adjacent windows—exponentially when more than four periods exist, linearly with overlap otherwise—to build hierarchical temporal representations from local to global scales.

## Results

On the four main benchmark datasets, SGN achieves 99.90% accuracy on TDBRAIN (EEG), 73.80% on PTB-XL (ECG), 80.81% on FLAAP (HAR), and 95.62% on UCI-HAR (HAR), outperforming all 10 baselines across all six evaluation metrics (Accuracy, Precision, Recall, F1, AUROC, AUPRC). The average accuracy improvement over the second-best method is 4.2%, with the largest gains on TDBRAIN (+4.8% over TimesNet) and FLAAP (+4.5% over Crossformer). On the 25 UEA multivariate datasets, SGN achieves the best average rank of 2.56 and the most top-1 results (14 out of 25) compared to eight dedicated MTSC baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SGN outperforms all 10 baselines (6 Transformer-based, 1 MLP-based, 3 CNN-based) on every metric across all four main datasets. The largest margin is on TDBRAIN where SGN reaches 99.90% accuracy versus TimesNet's 95.08% (+4.8%), attributed to TDBRAIN's clear variable-cluster structure that benefits from group-based modeling. On PTB-XL the gain is marginal (73.80% vs. Crossformer's 73.30%, +0.5%) because the 12 ECG channels are highly similar, causing the grouping to degenerate into near-uniform mixing. On FLAAP and UCI-HAR, SGN beats the second-best (Crossformer at 76.33% and iTransformer at 93.47%) by 4.5% and 2.2% respectively. Among baselines, DLinear (MLP) is consistently the weakest, while TimesNet and iTransformer are the strongest individual competitors. On the 25 UEA datasets, SGN achieves the best average rank (2.56) and 14 top-1 finishes, surpassing dedicated MTSC methods like ShapeFormer (rank 3.70, 5 top-1) and MPTSNet (rank 4.20, 3 top-1), though it trails MPTSNet on PEMS-SF (88.4% vs. 94.2%) and is competitive but not dominant on a few small datasets like DuckDuckGeese and Libras.

## Relation to Prior Work

SGN sits at the intersection of two established lines of work in multivariate time series modeling: (1) the periodic decomposition strategy from TimesNet, which uses FFT to identify dominant frequencies and reshape 1D sequences into 2D periodic windows for 2D convolution, and (2) the depthwise-then-pointwise convolution factorization from ConvNeXt, repurposed here to decouple temporal and variable-dimension interactions. The paper's key departure is introducing a structured variable grouping layer (via Gumbel-Softmax assignment initialized by Brownian Distance Covariance) that transforms the binary choice between independent-variable and fully-mixed modeling into a hierarchical intra-group/inter-group interaction scheme, and adapting Swin Transformer's shifted-window mechanism to periodic time series windows for cross-window communication.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical and recurring tension in MTS classification—over-smoothing in fully-mixed models versus lost inter-variable structure in independent models—by offering a grouping-based middle ground that is both interpretable and effective. The honest acknowledgment that on PTB-XL (where variables are highly similar) the grouping degenerates back to conventional mixed modeling highlights an open question: how to design grouping strategies that remain meaningful when variable boundaries are inherently blurred. The periodic window shifting and merging mechanism also opens a path for CNN-based models to capture long-range temporal dependencies without the quadratic cost of attention, which could be broadly applicable beyond classification.
