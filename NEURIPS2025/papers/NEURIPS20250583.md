## Problem

Existing generalization and duality guarantees for constrained learning apply only to inequality constraints, yet many critical ML requirements—group fairness, invariance, calibration, interpolation, and boundary value conditions—are naturally expressed as equality constraints. Reformulating equalities as inequalities or penalty relaxations can substantially alter solutions and invalidates prior theoretical results. This paper fills the gap by deriving a generalization theory for equality-constrained statistical learning and a practical algorithm to solve such problems.

## Input / Output

The method consumes independently drawn samples from multiple distributions (D for the objective, P_i for inequality constraints, Q_j for equality constraints) along with a parametrized model f_θ and loss/constraint functions. It produces trained parameters θ and dual variables (λ, μ), where the equality-constraint multipliers μ are unconstrained real-valued (unlike λ which are non-negative). A key practical detail is that non-differentiable constraint functions (e.g., indicator functions in fairness) are replaced by smooth surrogates (e.g., sigmoids) to enable gradient-based primal updates within the dual ascent loop.

## Method

The core theoretical contribution is a generalization bound (Theorem 3.1) decomposing |P* − D̂*| into a duality gap and a dual estimation error, showing the bound is driven by constraint sensitivity (L1-norms of Lagrange multipliers), model capacity ν, sample size N_min, and a constraint-difficulty term R(ν) that has no analogue in the inequality-only case. The proof hinges on establishing strong duality for a functional relaxation (P_φ) over decomposable L^p spaces using Lyapunov's theorem on atomless measures, then bounding the gap between the functional and parametrized problems via Lipschitz regularity. Practically, Algorithm 1 alternates between an unconstrained empirical minimization of the Lagrangian (primal step, approximated by a single SGD step) and projected subgradient ascent on the dual variables, with equality multipliers updated without projection (unlike inequality multipliers which are projected onto R_+). The resulting bound reveals a bias-variance-constraint trade-off: richer parametrizations reduce the duality gap but increase sample complexity, while constraint difficulty R(ν) captures how sensitively the feasible set changes under small constraint violations.

## Results

On the convection BVP (β=30), the equality-constrained formulation (P-BVP) achieves 0.62±0.17% relative L² error, substantially outperforming the PINN baseline (2.46±0.99%); at β=50, (P-BVP) reaches 30.7±24.2% versus PINN's 56.0±25.8%. On CIFAR-10, the classwise interpolation formulation (P-CI) matches ERM at 95.01±0.10% vs. 95.03±0.21% test accuracy, while on CIFAR-100 it trails slightly at 75.17±0.21% vs. 76.11±0.28%. On COMPAS, the exact equality-constrained demographic parity formulation achieves the lowest group-rate disparity, comparable to the tightest inequality relaxation (ε=10⁻⁴) but without requiring manual tolerance selection.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its equality-constrained formulation against three types of baselines across four experimental settings. For the convection BVP, (P-BVP) clearly outperforms the PINN baseline [Raissi et al., 2019]: at β=30 it reduces relative L² error from 2.46% to 0.62%, and at β=50 from 56.0% to 30.7%, attributed to the dual variables automatically up-weighting boundary conditions. On CIFAR-10, (P-CI) essentially ties ERM (95.01% vs. 95.03%), while on CIFAR-100 it loses by ~1 percentage point (75.17% vs. 76.11%), though the gap is within one standard deviation. On COMPAS, the exact equality formulation (P-DP) achieves group-rate disparity comparable to the tightest inequality relaxation (ε=10⁻⁴) but avoids the sensitivity to tolerance selection that looser relaxations (ε=10⁻²) introduce. The prescribed-rates formulation (P-F) additionally enables granular control over per-group positive-outcome rates at negligible accuracy cost relative to DP-constrained training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the inequality-constrained learning framework of Chamon et al. (2023, IEEE TIT) and Chamon & Ribeiro (2020, NeurIPS) to the more general case of equality constraints, for which no prior duality or generalization guarantees existed. The key technical departure is the use of Lyapunov's theorem on atomless vector measures to establish strong duality of the functional (infinite-dimensional) problem, combined with a new constraint-sensitivity term R(ν) in the generalization bound that has no analogue in the inequality-only setting. The practical algorithm (dual ascent) follows the same oracle-based structure as Cotter et al. (2019) and Chamon et al. (2023), but the dual update for equality multipliers is unconstrained (no projection onto R₊), reflecting the sign-free nature of equality Lagrange multipliers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This fills a genuine theoretical gap: many important ML requirements (demographic parity, prescribed group rates, PDE boundary/initial conditions, classwise interpolation) are naturally equality constraints, yet all prior generalization theory for constrained learning was limited to inequalities. The new R(ν) sensitivity term reveals that equality constraints impose a fundamentally different generalization cost tied to parametrization smoothness, which has practical implications for model design. Open questions include whether the observed correlation between dual variables and class-wise test error in interpolating classifiers admits a deeper theoretical explanation, and whether the strong regularity of Assumption 1 (interior of the constraint epigraph) can be weakened. The primal recovery problem in non-convex settings remains unresolved, though empirical evidence suggests it is not a major obstacle.
