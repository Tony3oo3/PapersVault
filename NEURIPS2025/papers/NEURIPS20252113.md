## Problem

Geometric correspondence models like DUSt3R excel at matching photos from similar viewpoints but fail when inputs differ drastically in both viewpoint (ground-level vs. bird's-eye) and modality (photorealistic vs. abstract line drawings). No prior dataset provided ground-truth pixel correspondences between photos and floor plans, leaving this cross-view, cross-modality matching problem unbenchmarked and unsolved.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252113/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a floor plan image (serving as the reference that defines the 3D coordinate frame) and a ground-level photo as a pair, and outputs dense pixel-level correspondences mapping each photo pixel to a location on the floor plan. As a downstream task, the predicted correspondences are used to estimate the 2D camera pose (position and orientation) of the photo in the floor plan's coordinate frame via epipolar geometry with a large focal length (10^7 pixels) to approximate the plan's orthographic projection. All outputs are normalized to a [0,1] unit square for evaluation.

## Method

C3Po fine-tunes DUSt3R's pointmap prediction architecture on the newly constructed C3 dataset, with three key modifications: (1) the Siamese encoders are split so each independently learns the distribution of its respective domain (floor plans vs. photos), (2) the correspondence task is reframed as pointmap prediction where the floor plan defines the 3D frame and photo pixels are mapped to 3D points then orthographically projected back to 2D by discarding the y-axis (up direction), and (3) photometric (color jitter) and geometric (cropping, rotation) augmentations are applied to floor plans to prevent overfitting. The C3 dataset itself is built by running COLMAP SfM on internet photo collections, then manually aligning the resulting sparse point clouds to floor plans via a custom 2D transformation interface, from which correspondences are derived by projecting each 3D point into both the photo and the floor plan. Training runs for 10 epochs on 8×A6000 GPUs using DUSt3R's original hyperparameters.

## Results

On the C3 test set (118 held-out scenes), C3Po achieves an RMSE of 0.1919 (normalized to [0,1] image coordinates), a 34% reduction over the best baseline LoFTR (0.2901). The method also shows stronger Percent of Correct Keypoints (PCK) and Precision-Recall performance across all thresholds. As a downstream task, camera pose estimation from predicted correspondences achieves 44.12% recall at 20° angular threshold and 41.21% at 20% positional threshold (normalized by floor plan diagonal).

## Baselines & Comparisons

C3Po outperforms all seven baselines on the C3 test set RMSE metric. The closest competitor is LoFTR (0.2901), followed closely by DUSt3R (0.2925) and DIFT (0.3036); C3Po's 0.1919 represents a 34% improvement over LoFTR. Among the dense matching methods, RoMa (0.3308) and SuperGlue (0.4050) perform worse, while the self-supervised feature approach DINOv2 (0.5338) is the weakest baseline overall. Notably, MASt3R (0.4616), despite being built on DUSt3R's architecture for matching, underperforms DUSt3R itself (0.2925), which the authors attribute to DUSt3R's pointmap prediction being more naturally aligned with the floor-plan projection task. All improvements are statistically significant (Wilcoxon signed-rank test, p < 0.05).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on DUSt3R's pointmap prediction paradigm, repurposing a 3D reconstruction model as a correspondence estimator by projecting predicted 3D points back to 2D via orthographic projection. It departs from the broader correspondence literature (SuperGlue, LoFTR, DIFT, RoMa, MASt3R) by identifying a setting—cross-view, cross-modality matching between ground-level photos and abstract floor plans—where none of these methods have been tested or trained. The dataset construction pipeline (COLMAP SfM + manual point-cloud-to-floor-plan alignment) is novel in its use of 3D reconstruction as an annotation proxy, avoiding infeasible manual correspondence labeling at scale.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The C3 dataset (90K pairs, 153M correspondences, 597 scenes) is the first benchmark for cross-view, cross-modality correspondence, filling a gap that has prevented systematic evaluation of geometric models in this regime. The finding that all SOTA methods produce errors exceeding 10% of image size, and that a purpose-built matcher (MASt3R) underperforms a reconstruction model (DUSt3R), challenges the assumption that geometric priors transfer across modalities. Open challenges remain: photos with minimal global context and structurally symmetric scenes produce ambiguous correspondences that current point-regression approaches cannot resolve, pointing toward distributional or diffusion-based prediction as a promising direction.
