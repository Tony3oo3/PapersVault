## Problem

Existing Temporal Domain Generalization (TDG) methods implicitly assume concept drift is primarily incremental or locally smooth, leaving two critical gaps: they cannot capture long-term periodic dynamics (e.g., seasonality, weekly cycles) that recur over extended horizons, and they are vulnerable to overfitting intra-domain uncertainties and non-IID artifacts within each temporal segment. No prior framework simultaneously models complex multi-scale drift patterns while filtering disruptive local noise.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sequence of T time-ordered source domains (tabular data with labels), trains a base model on each domain to obtain a parameter trajectory Θ ∈ R^{T×D}, and outputs a predicted parameter vector θ̂_{T+1} for the unseen future domain. Unusually, the core spectral analysis and Koopman dynamics operate on the *model parameters* rather than the raw data, making this a meta-level parameter-prediction approach where the 'signal' being decomposed and extrapolated is the learned weight sequence itself. The final prediction is decoded through a shared autoencoder decoder that fuses extrapolated low-frequency and regularized high-frequency latent representations.

## Method

FreKoo applies a Discrete Fourier Transform along the temporal axis of the parameter trajectory, then uses an energy-preservation ratio τ to select dominant low-frequency bins and split the trajectory into Θ_low (structured trends/periodicity) and Θ_high (transient noise). The low-frequency component is mapped into a latent space via an encoder and evolved by a learnable linear Koopman operator K ∈ R^{m×m}, enabling stable extrapolation of incremental and periodic dynamics; the high-frequency component is mapped by a separate encoder and regularized with a temporal smoothness penalty equivalent to a Gaussian random-walk prior, suppressing overfitting to domain-specific artifacts. The next-step prediction combines the Koopman-extrapolated low-frequency latent with the current high-frequency latent through a shared decoder. Training is end-to-end, jointly optimizing task loss, parameter reconstruction loss, Koopman latent consistency loss, and high-frequency smoothness regularization, with a multiscale generalization bound connecting Koopman stability and spectral decomposition to target-domain excess risk.

## Results

FreKoo achieves state-of-the-art performance on 6 out of 7 TDG benchmarks. On classification, it attains the lowest misclassification error on 2-Moons (1.0%), Rot-MNIST (6.9%), ONP (32.3%), Shuttle (0.20%), and Elec2 (9.2%). On regression, it achieves the best MAE on Appliance Energy (4.0), while Koodos slightly edges it on House Prices (8.8 vs. 9.0). The most dramatic gains appear on periodic-drift datasets: Elec2 (9.2% vs. 11.8% for EvoS) and Appliance (4.0 vs. 4.8 for Koodos).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FreKoo outperforms the strongest CTDG baseline Koodos on 5 of 6 shared datasets (2-Moons: 1.0 vs 1.3; ONP: 32.3 vs 33.5; Shuttle: 0.20 vs 0.24; Appliance: 4.0 vs 4.8; Rot-MNIST: 6.9 vs 7.0), with Koodos retaining a narrow edge on House Prices (8.8 vs 9.0). Against the best TDG baselines DRAIN and DDA, FreKoo leads by substantial margins on periodic-drift benchmarks (Elec2: 9.2 vs 12.7/12.8; Appliance: 4.0 vs 6.4/6.1) and also wins on synthetic incremental drift (2-Moons: 1.0 vs 3.2/9.7). The CDA baselines CDOT and CIDA are consistently weaker, with CIDA reaching only 14.1% on Elec2 versus FreKoo's 9.2%. Time-agnostic baselines (Offline, LastDomain, IncFinetune) are far behind, confirming that explicit temporal modeling is essential. The largest relative gains over all baselines occur on Elec2 and Appliance, where the frequency-domain decomposition most effectively captures long-term periodicity and filters intra-domain noise.

## Relation to Prior Work

FreKoo builds on the parameter-prediction paradigm for Temporal Domain Generalization established by DRAIN [8] and Koodos [12], which model parameter evolution as a dynamical system to extrapolate future model weights. However, it departs from prior TDG methods (GI, DRAIN, EvoS, Koodos) that operate entirely in the time domain and implicitly assume smooth incremental drift, by introducing a frequency-domain decomposition of parameter trajectories as an inductive bias. The low-frequency component is then modeled via a Koopman operator (drawing on [20, 21, 16]) to capture both incremental trends and long-term periodicity, while the high-frequency component is regularized via a temporal smoothness penalty. This spectral-Koopman combination is novel in the TDG context, though each individual component (DFT, Koopman autoencoders, Tikhonov-style regularization) is well-established in other domains.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FreKoo addresses two concrete gaps in TDG—capturing long-range periodic drift and filtering intra-domain non-IID noise—that prior methods handle poorly, and provides a multiscale generalization bound connecting Koopman stability and high-frequency smoothness to target-domain excess risk. The periodicity experiment (37-domain P-Moons) convincingly demonstrates that frequency-domain modeling is necessary for recurring patterns that time-domain methods miss. Key limitations include the reliance on a fixed heuristic (τ) for the low/high frequency split rather than an adaptive mechanism, and the framework's incompatibility with fully online or continuous adaptation settings, which restricts deployment in truly streaming scenarios. Open questions include whether the spectral threshold can be learned end-to-end and whether the approach extends to multi-modal or high-dimensional parameter spaces.
