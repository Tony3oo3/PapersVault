## Problem

Existing text-guided image editing methods (e.g., SDEdit, DDS) prioritize semantic invariance but ignore causal relationships among features, producing edits that violate counterfactual consistency. Conversely, causal editing approaches offer theoretical guarantees but require full model retraining, operate on narrow datasets, and demand explicit labels for all generative factors. This paper bridges the gap by enabling counterfactual-consistent editing at scale using pre-trained diffusion models under weak supervision, where only a subset of features are labeled or prompted.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251966/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an initial RGB image, a text prompt labeling only a subset of generative factors (the intervened feature X and a backdoor set B), a user-specified causal diagram over all generative factors (including unlabeled ones), and a frozen pre-trained Stable Diffusion model. It produces a single counterfactual edited image where non-descendant features are provably invariant, the intervened feature takes the target value, and descendant features change according to the counterfactual distribution. No retraining of the diffusion model is required; the method operates entirely in the noise space of the pre-trained SD model.

## Method

The paper introduces the Backdoor Disentangled Causal Latent Space (BD-CLS), a latent space over {X, B, Z} that satisfies three conditions: matching the ground-truth conditional P(I|X,B), disentanglement of non-descendants from X, and a structural constraint ensuring B retains its backdoor property. Theorem 1 factorizes the image counterfactual query into intervention consistency, non-descendant invariance, and descendant delta, and Theorem 2 proves BD-CLS satisfies all three. The BD-CLS-Edit algorithm extracts a BD-CLS from a frozen SD model by: (1) identifying the largest backdoor set B from the causal diagram, (2) inverting the image to sample noise via DDPM inversion, and (3) searching a prompt-mixing transformation ψ_θ over denoising timesteps. The key optimization uses a novel counterfactual gradient L_Ctf that contrasts the SDS direction on the target image with the SDS direction on a DDS-perturbed reference image, canceling entanglement-induced changes to non-descendants while preserving the intervention effect and allowing descendants to change stochastically.

## Results

On the Colored MNIST task, BD-CLS (trained without bar-width labels) and the fully supervised NCM both produce descendant-change estimates Q = P(BW_{D=7}=1 | D=1, DC=0, BC=0, BW=0) that fall within the theoretical counterfactual bounds, while CDiffusion and CGN fall outside these bounds. BD-CLS also preserves non-descendant features (digit color, bar color) during editing, whereas CDiffusion alters them. In real-world Stable Diffusion editing (weather, season, scene changes), BD-CLS-Edit is the only method that simultaneously preserves non-descendants (pose, identity, scene layout) and correctly propagates causal effects to descendants (umbrella appearance, shadow softening, clothing change, grocery-bag removal).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CDiffusion (correlation-based conditional diffusion), BD-CLS wins on both non-descendant invariance (CDiffusion changes digit/bar colors that should remain red) and descendant consistency (CDiffusion's estimated bar-width change probability falls outside the theoretical counterfactual bounds). Against CGN (Counterfactual Generative Networks), BD-CLS matches on non-descendant preservation but wins on descendant consistency, as CGN's Q estimate also violates the bounds. Against DDPM Inversion and SDEdit (both latent-space inversion methods), BD-CLS-Edit clearly outperforms in real-world editing: both baselines alter the person's pose, identity, or scene layout (non-descendants), while BD-CLS-Edit preserves them. Against DDS (Delta Denoising Score, a semantic-invariance method), BD-CLS-Edit trades a small amount of visual similarity for correct causal propagation: DDS keeps the image visually close but fails to introduce the umbrella, soften shadows, or change clothing, whereas BD-CLS-Edit correctly reflects these downstream effects while still preserving non-descendants.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate research threads: (1) large-scale text-guided image editing methods (DDPM Inversion, SDEdit, DDS, Prompt-to-Prompt) that achieve semantic invariance but ignore causal structure among features, and (2) causal generative models (Neural Causal Models, Counterfactual Generative Networks) that provide theoretical counterfactual guarantees but require full retraining and complete feature labels. Building on the counterfactual consistency framework of Pan & Bareinboim (2024) and the Neural Causal Model architecture, the paper introduces the Backdoor Disentangled Causal Latent Space (BD-CLS) as a formal latent-space structure that encodes causal inductive biases, and shows that a pre-trained Stable Diffusion model can be reinterpreted as a BD-CLS without retraining. The optimization leverages SDS and DDS gradient directions in a novel contrastive combination (Prop. 2) to disentangle descendant from non-descendant features during editing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first formal counterfactual consistency guarantees for image editing using pre-trained diffusion models under weak supervision, addressing a gap where prior causal methods required full labels and retraining while prior editing methods offered no causal guarantees. Key limitations include the requirement of a known causal diagram G_{V,I} (which is non-trivial to obtain in practice), the strong invertibility assumption in the ASCM (every factor must be recoverable from the image), and the heuristic nature of the prompt-mixing optimization for ψ. Open questions include: can the causal diagram be learned or inferred automatically from data? How does the method scale to scenes with many interacting factors? And can the backdoor set identification be made robust to misspecified diagrams? The framework is most impactful for settings where causal structure is known or partially known (e.g., medical imaging, controlled environments) and where editing must respect physical or logical constraints.
