## Problem

Multimodal learning systems deployed in non-stationary environments suffer from concept drift, where changing data distributions degrade performance, yet existing approaches lack mechanisms for continuous, stable adaptation with formal guarantees. In particular, modality-specific drifts (where one modality degrades while another remains stable) require adaptive fusion strategies that fixed-weight fusion cannot provide, and no prior work has established Lyapunov stability guarantees for adaptive multimodal learning under drift.

## Input / Output

The system consumes streaming multimodal samples consisting of text (up to 77 tokens) and RGB images (up to ~2.5M pixels) in an online fashion, producing a binary real/fake classification label. Both modalities are encoded independently by a shared CLIP-ViT backbone with separate linear heads, and their scalar logit outputs are combined via a time-varying weighted average z_t = (1−α_t)·z_t^(text) + α_t·z_t^(image) before a sigmoid yields the final probability. The error signal driving the controller is defined as 1−Accuracy over a sliding window of 100 steps.

## Method

LS-OGD augments standard online gradient descent with a lightweight adaptive controller that dynamically adjusts two parameters: the learning rate η_t (scaled up by a factor of 1.5 when a drift signal exceeds a high threshold, scaled down when moderate) and the fusion weight α_t (shifted by ±0.25 in raw logit space toward the more reliable modality based on modality-specific error estimates). Drift is detected by comparing mean accuracy over a recent sub-window against a past sub-window within a sliding window of 100 steps, flagging a normalized drop exceeding a threshold τ_drop. The key theoretical contribution is a Lyapunov function V(t) = ½e_t² whose one-step decrement is bounded by a stabilizing term, a drift disturbance term, a noise term, and a fusion-adaptation term (Lemma 1), yielding uniform ultimate boundedness of the error under bounded drift (Theorem 1) and asymptotic convergence to zero when drift ceases. Under severe unilateral modality drift, the fusion weight α_t provably converges to 0 or 1 in finite time, effectively isolating the compromised modality and reducing the system to a stable single-modality learner (Theorem 2).

## Results

On the M3A misinformation dataset, LS-OGD is evaluated in a two-phase protocol: Phase 1 (stable, pre-2015 data) for initial training, and Phase 2 (post-2015 data with simulated concept drift) for adaptation. The static baseline (frozen Phase-1 model) achieves a near-zero F1 score under drift, while LS-OGD maintains a substantially higher F1 throughout Phase 2, demonstrating effective online adaptation. The error signal (1 − Accuracy) remains bounded during drift episodes, consistent with the uniform ultimate boundedness (UUB) guarantee of Theorem 1, and the fusion weight α_t rapidly shifts to isolate the compromised modality under unilateral drift, confirming Theorem 2.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares LS-OGD against a single static baseline: an identical multimodal model (CLIP-ViT text encoder + CLIP-ViT image encoder + weighted fusion) trained on Phase 1 data and then frozen during Phase 2. Under the simulated concept drift (JPEG compression, Gaussian noise, blur on images; keyword replacement and phrase appending on text), the static baseline's F1 score collapses to near zero, reflecting complete failure to adapt. LS-OGD, by contrast, maintains a significantly higher F1 score throughout Phase 2, with the adaptive controller dynamically increasing the learning rate during drift spikes and re-weighting the fusion parameter α_t to down-weight the degraded modality. The paper does not report a single aggregate F1 number for LS-OGD in the main text; performance is shown as a time-series comparison (Figure 7) over 3000 adaptation steps. No other baselines (e.g., TTA methods like Tent, CoTTA, or other drift-adaptation approaches) are included in the empirical comparison, which limits the breadth of the empirical validation.

## Relation to Prior Work

LS-OGD carves new ground by repurposing Lyapunov stability analysis and PID-like adaptive control from classical control theory into the multimodal learning loop itself, rather than applying stability constraints to an external plant. Concept drift adaptation has been extensively studied for unimodal streams (ADWIN, DDM, COGRA), and multimodal fusion has been explored via attention and cross-modal transformers, but no prior work formally unifies these threads with a stability guarantee for the joint learning-fusion system. The paper also extends the adaptive learning rate literature (RMSProp, Adam, COGRA) by coupling rate adjustment with a time-varying fusion weight, making the modality reliance a controlled actuator rather than a per-sample attention score.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The UUB and asymptotic-convergence theorems provide a rare formal safety net for online multimodal adaptation, which is directly relevant to safety-critical deployments (autonomous vehicles, misinformation detection) where unbounded error growth is unacceptable. However, the empirical validation rests on a single dataset (M3A) with artificially induced drift (JPEG compression, keyword replacement), and the controller requires ground-truth labels at test time, creating a gap between the theoretical guarantees and label-scarce real-world settings. Open questions include whether the modality-specific error attribution generalizes when drift is correlated across modalities, whether the framework scales beyond two modalities, and whether unsupervised proxies (e.g., ensemble disagreement) can replace the supervised feedback the controller currently depends on.
