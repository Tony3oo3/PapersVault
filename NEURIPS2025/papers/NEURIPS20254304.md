## Problem

Existing knowledge editing methods for large vision-language models (LVLMs) treat visual and textual edits in isolation, evaluate only single-shot updates, and lack benchmarks for compositional reasoning over multiple edited facts. This leaves a critical gap: real-world knowledge updates arrive incrementally and require the model to simultaneously retain sequential edits across both modalities and integrate them to answer compositional queries that span visual and textual information.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254304/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system accepts an RGB image paired with a natural-language query, which is first decomposed by GPT-4o into a visual sub-query (e.g., 'Who is in the photo?') and a textual sub-query (e.g., 'What position did that person assume?'). The external memory stores visual edits as (image, question, answer) triples and textual edits as (question, answer) pairs; at inference, retrieved evidence is prepended to the query as in-context demonstrations before the LVLM generates a single text answer. Compositional queries require the model to resolve the visual sub-query first to identify the entity, then substitute that entity into the textual sub-query for retrieval.

## Method

MemEIC combines a modality-aware external memory (Mem-E) with a dual-stream internal memory (Mem-I) and a brain-inspired Knowledge Connector. Mem-E retrieves visual edits using a weighted combination of CLIP image similarity and DistilBERT text similarity, and textual edits via DistilBERT cosine similarity, storing them in separate modality-specific stores. Mem-I places two independent LoRA adapters (rank 8 each) on the frozen FFN layers—one for visual edits, one for textual edits—so that sequential updates to one modality do not corrupt the other, directly addressing cross-modal interference and representation collapse. The Knowledge Connector injects LoRA increments into the query and key projections of a dedicated self-attention layer, activated only when both adapters are simultaneously engaged (i.e., for compositional queries), enabling selective cross-modal fusion analogous to the corpus callosum. Training proceeds in two stages: Stage 1 trains the external retrieval classifiers under single-edit conditions, and Stage 2 co-trains the dual-LoRA adapters and connector under an adversarial retriever that caps hit rates at 70% (LLaVA-1.5) or 50% (MiniGPT-4), teaching the connector to cross-check external evidence against internal memory rather than over-relying on retrieved context.

## Results

On CCKEB (LLaVA-1.5 backbone), MemEIC achieves gap-averaged visual reliability of 98.93%, textual reliability of 92.48%, and compositional reliability (CompRel) of 80.56% across 500 sequential edits evaluated at gaps of 0, 10, 20, 50, and 100. This represents a +16.94 improvement over WISE in visual reliability and +32.35 in compositional reliability, and +18.51 CompRel over the best internal-memory baseline (LoRA, 62.05). MemEIC maintains near-stable performance across all gaps (e.g., CompRel drops only from 85.16 at gap 0 to ~78 at gap 100), whereas FT and LoRA baselines lose nearly 30 points in reliability over the same range.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MemEIC outperforms all four baselines on every metric. Against WISE (the strongest baseline), MemEIC gains +16.94 in visual reliability (98.93 vs. 82.00) and +32.35 in compositional reliability (80.56 vs. 48.21), showing that WISE's routing mechanism, while stable over time, cannot fuse visual and textual edits for compositional queries. Against LoRA, MemEIC improves CompRel by +18.51 (80.56 vs. 61.62) and visual reliability by +21.71, as LoRA's shared parameter space suffers severe cross-modal interference and catastrophic forgetting over sequential edits. SERAC, the external-memory baseline, maintains stable textual reliability (86.94) but achieves only 63.17 visual reliability and 40.76 CompRel due to its text-only retrieval strategy failing to locate visual edits and its inability to integrate retrieved facts with internal knowledge. MEND effectively collapses under sequential editing (4.22 Rel, 2.32 CompRel), confirming the model-collapse issue noted in prior work. Notably, MemEIC is the only method that simultaneously achieves >98% visual reliability, >92% textual reliability, and >80% compositional reliability while remaining stable across all sequential gaps.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the FFN-as-key-value-memory paradigm (Geva et al., 2021) and LoRA-based parameter-efficient editing (Hu et al., 2022) into the multimodal setting by proposing modality-specific dual-LoRA adapters inspired by brain lateralization, coupled with a corpus-callosum-inspired attention connector for cross-modal fusion. It departs from prior LVLM editing work (VLKEB, MMEdit) that addressed only visual edits or single-shot scenarios, and from external-memory editors (SERAC, IKE) that rely on text-only retrieval, by introducing a hybrid external-internal architecture with image-text retrieval and a new continual-compositional task formulation. The brain-inspired framing (hemispheric specialization + interhemispheric communication) provides a conceptual unification absent in prior editing literature, where visual and textual knowledge were typically stored in a shared parameter space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CCKEB is the first benchmark to jointly evaluate continual (sequential) and compositional (cross-modal) knowledge editing in LVLMs, filling a gap that limits real-world deployment where updates arrive incrementally and queries require integrating multiple edited facts. The finding that even oracle retrieval caps compositional reliability at ~65%—well below the 97%+ achieved with the Knowledge Connector—suggests that the core bottleneck in multimodal editing is cross-modal integration, not retrieval accuracy, a distinction that should inform future system design. Limitations include evaluation only on 7B models (LLaVA-1.5, MiniGPT4), reliance on GPT-4o for query decomposition at inference, and no extension to multimodal generation tasks, leaving open whether the dual-adapter + connector architecture scales to larger or generative LVLMs.
