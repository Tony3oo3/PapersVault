## Problem

In non-stationary MDPs, experiences from previous tasks interfere with learning on new tasks, causing performance degradation that is especially severe for contradictory tasks. Existing meta-RL approaches for non-stationary environments (e.g., LILAC, PEARL) focus on cross-task generalization but largely overlook this interference, leading to slow recovery after task switches. The paper identifies this gap and asks how to simultaneously achieve cross-task generalization and resistance to cross-task interference.

## Input / Output

The agent receives a state observation and a context window of approximately 200 recent trajectories (states, actions, rewards) that parameterize the context-based meta-RL policy. Each epoch, a Bayesian estimator consumes a sliding window of the most recent returns (w=100 episodes) conditioned on which policy was previously selected, producing posterior expected returns for the fast and slow policies. The output is an action from whichever policy is selected, plus the binary fast/slow decision that governs data collection for that epoch.

## Method

BFSF maintains two policies: a 'fast' policy trained on recent data with a dual-reset mechanism (periodically reinitializing one of two parallel networks every ν=50 episodes to prevent distribution-shift degradation) and data relabeling (a context-based dynamics model f(s,a,c) rewrites next-states and rewards from historical data into the current task's context, augmenting the small recent dataset); and a 'slow' policy trained via PEARL meta-RL over all accumulated experience for cross-task generalization. A Bayesian conjugate-normal update over recent returns provides an unbiased posterior estimate of each policy's expected return, and the policy with the higher posterior mean is deployed for online interaction each epoch. The paper further derives a sub-optimality bound (Theorem 2.1) decomposing the gap into relabeling-induced dynamics/reward differences and a telescoping term, with an extended bound incorporating finite-iteration optimization error.

## Results

BFSF achieves the highest average return across 7 of 8 non-stationary environments, with particularly large margins on Cheetah-Dir (1209.0 vs. 852.6 for CEMRL), Ant-Dir (671.0 vs. 318.5 for LILAC), and Ant-Goal (−204.9 vs. −426.6 for LILAC). On the infinite-world tasks, BFSF leads by wide margins (Ant-Dir-Inf: 153.3 vs. 85.5 for LILAC; Ant-Cir-Inf: 165.2 vs. 69.5 for LILAC). The metric reported is the average return throughout the entire non-stationary training process (with task switches every 2000 episodes), and results are averaged over 3 runs with standard deviations reported.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BFSF outperforms all four baselines on 7 of 8 environments. Against LILAC (the strongest meta-RL baseline), BFSF gains +451 on Cheetah-Dir, +352 on Ant-Dir, +222 on Ant-Goal, and +88 on Ant-Dir-Inf, confirming that the fast policy effectively mitigates the interference that slows LILAC's second-task learning. Against ITER, which uses iterative relearning to combat non-stationarity, BFSF leads by +396 on Cheetah-Dir and +828 on Ant-Dir, showing that relearning alone yields only elementary policies without cross-task generalization. CEMRL is the closest competitor, achieving 577.9 on Walker (slightly above BFSF's 530.9, though error bars overlap) and 852.6 on Cheetah-Dir, but BFSF still leads on the remaining six environments. CoMPS consistently performs worst, often yielding negative returns on Ant-Dir and Ant-Goal, indicating that its alternating offline meta-learning strategy is poorly suited to streaming non-stationary tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on PEARL (context-based meta-RL) as the 'slow' policy backbone, extending the non-stationary MDP line of work initiated by LILAC and continued by ITER, CEMRL, and CoMPS. The key conceptual departure is that prior methods address either cross-task generalization (LILAC, CEMRL) or interference via relearning (ITER) but not both simultaneously; BFSF unifies them through a Bayesian selection mechanism between a fast-adapting policy and a meta-learned slow policy. The dual-reset mechanism draws on observations from ITER about neural network degradation across tasks, while data relabeling repurposes the hindsight relabeling idea from meta-RL (Hindsight Foresight Relabeling) for the non-stationary streaming setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and systematically quantifies interference in non-stationary MDPs—a problem the authors argue has been largely overlooked despite its practical relevance (UAVs in changing weather, stock market regime shifts). The BFSF framework provides a principled, theoretically grounded solution with a sub-optimality bound, and the infinite-world simulation is a meaningful step toward realistic evaluation. However, the work is limited to a small set of MuJoCo/Meta-World environments, does not test blurred task boundaries or stochastic non-stationarity, and the theoretical bound relies on a normal-distribution assumption for returns. Open questions include whether the Bayesian fast-slow selection generalizes to high-dimensional or partially observable settings, and whether the framework scales to truly open-ended task streams.
