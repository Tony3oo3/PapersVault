## Problem

Estimating semantic aleatoric and epistemic uncertainties in LLMs for free-form QA requires many generations to produce stable estimates, because standard IID sampling yields semantically redundant outputs that waste compute. Prior diversity-steering approaches such as SDLG were restricted to autoregressive models and required gradient access to the NLI scorer, leaving emerging masked diffusion models unaddressed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251080/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a text prompt, a base language model (autoregressive or masked diffusion), and a DeBERTa-large-MNLI scorer fine-tuned with a single special token ([TRUNC] for ARMs, [MASK] for MDMs) to score partial or masked sequences. It produces a set of semantically diverse generations along with self-normalized importance-weighted, control-variate-corrected estimates of semantic entropy (aleatoric) and mutual information (epistemic). The NLI fine-tuning updates only ~0.3% of parameters (the special-token embedding and classification head), preserving the model's original entailment performance on full sentence pairs.

## Method

The core mechanism injects a continuous semantic-similarity penalty directly into the token-level logits during decoding, repelling each new sample away from the most semantically similar existing generation via a bidirectional entailment score from the NLI model. This operates uniformly across autoregressive and masked diffusion paradigms by scoring truncated prefixes or partially masked spans, requiring no gradient access to either the base LLM or the NLI scorer. The induced sampling bias is corrected with self-normalized importance reweighting, and estimation variance is further reduced with adaptive control variates derived from the base model's log probabilities (already computed for the weights). The diversity parameter λ is tuned adaptively at both the token level (increasing when similarity with the nearest existing sample exceeds a threshold) and the sequence level (adjusting based on running entropy variance), eliminating the need for a separate calibration step.

## Results

On four QA benchmarks (CoQA, TriviaQA, AmbigQA, TruthfulQA) with N=16 samples, diversity-steered sampling achieves AUROC of 0.75–0.81 for semantic entropy (SE) estimation on CoQA, 0.82–0.86 on TriviaQA, 0.76–0.78 on AmbigQA, and 0.81–0.94 on TruthfulQA, matching or surpassing all baselines. The method covers more semantic clusters per sample than baselines (Figure 4) while maintaining an effective sample size ratio (ESS/N) above 0.4. For the mutual information (MI) epistemic proxy (N=8 pairs), gains over baselines are more modest (e.g., 0.68 vs. 0.61 AUROC on CoQA with LLaMA 38B), with higher variance attributed to NLI-based clustering noise.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Diverse Beam Search (DBS, Vijayakumar et al. 2018), the proposed method wins on most settings: e.g., on CoQA with OPT-6.7B it achieves 0.75 vs. 0.68 AUROC, and on TriviaQA with LLaMA 38B it reaches 0.84 vs. 0.70. DBS is competitive only on AmbigQA with OPT-13B (0.78 vs. 0.78) and TruthfulQA with LLaMA 38B (0.89 vs. 0.89), where the single-answer or high-confidence nature of the task limits the benefit of semantic diversity. Against Semantically Diverse Language Generation (SDLG, Aichberger et al. 2025), the proposed method wins on CoQA (0.75 vs. 0.71 for OPT-6.7B; 0.77 vs. 0.74 for LLaMA 38B), TriviaQA (0.82 vs. 0.78 for OPT-6.7B; 0.84 vs. 0.79 for LLaMA 38B), and TruthfulQA (0.85 vs. 0.81 for OPT-13B; 0.94 vs. – for LLaDA 8B). The only losses are narrow: SDLG edges out on TriviaQA with OPT-13B (0.86 vs. 0.85) and AmbigQA with LLaMA 38B (0.77 vs. 0.76). The authors attribute SDLG's weaker rank correlation partly to the simplified cross-tokenizer handling required when the base LLM and NLI model use different vocabularies.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the semantic-entropy framework of Kuhn et al. [1] and the mutual-information epistemic proxy of Yadkori et al. [2] by introducing a diversity-steered sampler that injects a continuous NLI-based repulsion term directly into decoding logits, generalizing Aichberger et al.'s SDLG [4] from autoregressive-only token substitution to a gradient-free, model-agnostic scheme covering both ARMs and masked diffusion models. Unlike SDLG, which requires NLI gradients and shares tokenization with the base LLM, the proposed method only needs a lightweight 0.3%-parameter fine-tune of an off-the-shelf NLI model to score partial prefixes or masked spans. The paper also departs from prior diversity heuristics (temperature, nucleus, DBS) by explicitly correcting the induced sampling bias via self-normalized importance reweighting and reducing estimator variance with adaptive control variates—corrections that Aichberger et al. and the original SE/MI estimators omit.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework is a practical, drop-in enhancement for uncertainty estimation in risk-sensitive LLM deployments: it requires no gradient access to the base model, works across both dominant decoding paradigms (ARM and MDM), and covers more semantic clusters per sample than baselines. Key open questions include whether the sequential (non-parallelizable) steering can be replaced by batched or hybrid schemes without losing coverage, whether marginalizing over noisy NLI cluster assignments would improve robustness, and whether the same logit-repulsion plus importance-weighting recipe transfers to on-policy RL fine-tuning (PPO/GRPO) as the authors speculate. The modest AUROC gains (typically 2–5 points) and the dependence on a single prompt realization limit immediate impact, but the MDM extension opens a previously unexplored axis for uncertainty quantification.
