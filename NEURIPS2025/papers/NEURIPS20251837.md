## Problem

Post-hoc editing of image classifiers to modify class-specific behaviors (e.g., unlearning a class, adapting to a new environment, or defending against typographic attacks) remains difficult because deeply optimized models encode rigid, task-wide behaviors. Existing methods either correct individual misclassifications image-by-image or require costly retraining, and no efficient mechanism exists for class-level editing in vision models that preserves unrelated knowledge.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251837/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (rescaled to 224×224) and, critically, the penultimate-layer class centroids from both a pretrained and a fine-tuned encoder to compute Class Vectors. For weight-space mapping, only a single reference sample per target class is needed to train a small subset of encoder layers (final layer-norm or MLP). Outputs include class labels via latent-space steering (no retraining), permanently edited encoder weights, or pixel-space adversarial trigger patches optimized to embed a target representation.

## Method

Class Vectors (κ_c) are defined as the difference between fine-tuned and pretrained penultimate-layer centroid representations for each class, disentangling per-class adaptation from task-wide weight changes. Two injection modes are supported: (1) latent-space steering, which adds a designed editing vector z_edit to the input representation at inference time with a cosine-similarity gate to localize the edit, and (2) weight-space mapping, which trains a small subset of encoder layers to map class-c representations onto shifted targets. The approach is grounded in two theoretical results: Theorem 3.1 extends Cross-Task Linearity to the pretrained-to-finetune segment, guaranteeing that scaling κ_c traces a smooth linear path of class behavior, and Theorem 3.3 leverages Neural Collapse to prove that Class Vectors are approximately orthogonal to other classes' embeddings, ensuring independent edits. High-level concept editing is achieved through simple vector arithmetic (e.g., z_edit = λ(κ_snow+c − κ_c) to remove snow features), enabling unlearning, environmental adaptation, typography defense, and backdoor trigger optimization with as few as one reference sample and under 1.5 seconds of training.

## Results

On class unlearning (Table 1, ViT-B/16), Class Vector achieves near-perfect forgetting (ACCf = 0.0%) on MNIST, EuroSAT, and GTSRB while retaining 99.7%, 99.5%, and 98.6% accuracy on non-target classes, respectively. The weight-space variant (Class Vector†) further improves retention to 96.2%, 99.7%, and 93.4% on those same datasets. For backdoor trigger optimization (Table 3), Class Vector† achieves 99.8% attack success rate with small patches and 99.0% with invisible noise while preserving 95.2% clean accuracy, matching DirMatch's clean accuracy but surpassing it in ASR by 3.3 and 2.2 percentage points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Retrained (Santurkar et al., 2021), Class Vector achieves comparable or better forgetting (ACCf ≈ 0.0 vs. 0.1–41.8) while dramatically outperforming on retention: 99.7% vs. 76.4% on MNIST, 99.5% vs. 85.7% on EuroSAT, and 98.6% vs. 57.5% on GTSRB. Against NegGrad (Kodge et al., 2024), both methods achieve perfect forgetting, but Class Vector retains 99.7% accuracy on MNIST versus NegGrad's 43.4%, and 98.6% vs. 15.6% on GTSRB — a gap of over 80 percentage points. Random Vector baselines confirm that the editing direction is semantically meaningful: random perturbations fail to reduce ACCf (staying at 99.9–100%) while Class Vector drives it to 0.0. In the backdoor setting (Table 3), Class Vector† matches DirMatch's clean accuracy (95.2%) but achieves higher ASR (99.8% vs. 96.5% for small patches), while BadNet achieves 100% ASR at the cost of collapsing clean accuracy to 10.0%, making it impractical for stealthy attacks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the task vector framework (Ilharco et al., 2023) from task-level weight-space deltas to per-class latent-space vectors, leveraging Cross-Task Linearity (Zhou et al., 2024) to justify that the pretrained-to-finetune segment is more linear than between two fine-tuned checkpoints, and Neural Collapse (Papyan et al., 2020) to establish approximate orthogonality between class vectors. It departs from prior classifier editing methods (Santurkar et al., 2021; Yang et al., 2024) that operate image-by-image or require auxiliary masks, by replacing entire class-level predictive rules through simple vector arithmetic. The approach also distinguishes itself from in-context vectors (transient, label-agnostic offsets in LLMs) and CAVs (descriptive rather than prescriptive), positioning Class Vectors as persistent, class-specific editing primitives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Class Vectors provide a principled, composable mechanism for class-level classifier editing that requires no retraining (latent steering) or only sub-second fine-tuning of <1.5K parameters (weight mapping), making post-hoc model personalization practical for open-hub classifiers. The demonstrated linearity and orthogonality enable intuitive high-level operations (addition, subtraction, scaling) that non-experts can use, with applications spanning unlearning, domain adaptation, adversarial defense, and backdoor trigger optimization. Key limitations include the assumption of well-structured, approximately linearly separable class representations (potentially failing under high intra-class variance or long-tailed distributions), restriction to single-label classification, and the need for partial model access. Extending to multi-label/hierarchical settings, black-box environments, and generative models remains open.
