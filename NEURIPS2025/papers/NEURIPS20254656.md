## Problem

Existing toxicity mitigation methods manipulate individual neuron activations, which are stochastic, context-sensitive, and often conflate toxicity detection experts with toxicity generation experts, leading to catastrophic forgetting and degraded language modeling. The paper asks whether aggregated layer-wise or structural representations capture toxicity more robustly than single neurons, and whether interpretable components beyond neurons and layers can be identified to enable precise, targeted suppression of toxic generation without impairing linguistic competence.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254656/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes the weight matrix W ∈ R^{V×d} of a pre-trained LLM's final output projection layer (lm_head), along with a set of toxic and non-toxic text samples used to compute per-eigenvector activation statistics. It produces a modified lm_head weight matrix W' = UΣ'V^T with selectively damped singular values, which is swapped back into the model; the model then generates text as usual. No gradient updates or fine-tuning are performed—the intervention is a single matrix reconstruction applied once to the frozen weights.

## Method

EigenShift applies Singular Value Decomposition (W = UΣV^T) to the lm_head, interpreting the columns of V as orthogonal 'eigen-choices'—principal semantic axes along which the model makes token-generation decisions. For each hidden state h, the activation along the i-th eigen-choice is computed as a_i = v_i^T h, and a toxicity influence score Δ_i is defined as the difference in mean activation between toxic and non-toxic samples. The top-k eigenvectors with the largest Δ_i are identified as generation-aligned toxic directions, and their corresponding singular values σ_i are scaled by a damping factor α < 1 (e.g., α = 0.9), yielding a modified matrix W' = UΣ'V^T. This selectively attenuates the model's capacity to amplify toxic semantic directions while leaving detection-related axes and the rest of the generation dynamics intact. The approach is training-free, requires only a one-time SVD and singular-value edit, and is grounded in the observation that detection and generation occupy distinct subspaces within the final projection layer.

## Results

On LLaMA-2, EigenShift achieves a 57.47% toxicity reduction (11.13% → 4.71%) while increasing perplexity only from 6.23 to 9.84 (+58%), yielding a TPH score of 60.37%. Across all five evaluated LLMs (LLaMA-2, Mistral-v0.1, GPT-2-xl, MPT-7B, Falcon-7B), EigenShift attains the highest or near-highest TPH score, with the strongest gains on LLaMA-2 (60.37%) and Falcon (78.86%). On MMLU subsets (Algebra, Logical Fallacies, U.S. Foreign Policy), accuracy shifts by at most ±2 points, confirming no catastrophic forgetting. The method also scales to larger models (13B–70B), maintaining 50–61% toxicity reduction with perplexity changes under 15%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Det-0 (zeroing expert activations) and Damp (uniform scaling) both achieve near-complete toxicity elimination but catastrophically inflate perplexity (e.g., 6.23 → 43,517 on LLaMA-2), yielding TPH scores below 2% and rendering the model incoherent. Aura, the strongest baseline, uses AUROC-weighted damping and achieves 67.4% toxicity reduction with PPL 19.30 on LLaMA-2 (TPH 43.73%), but still triples perplexity. EigenShift outperforms Aura on TPH for four of five models: LLaMA-2 (60.37% vs 43.73%), Mistral-v0.1 (57.68% vs 44.74%), GPT-2-xl (16.47% vs 14.66%), and Falcon-7B (78.86% vs 77.81%). The sole exception is MPT-7B, where Aura's 93.94% TPH edges out EigenShift's 87.74%, though EigenShift still achieves a lower perplexity (6.90 vs 7.66). Overall, EigenShift is the only method that simultaneously reduces toxicity by >50% and keeps perplexity within ~60% of baseline across the majority of tested architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the expert-neuron intervention lineage initiated by Suau et al. (Self-conditioning PLMs, ICML 2022; Whispering Experts, ICML 2024), which identified individual neurons as concept detectors and manipulated their activations to suppress toxic generation. The key conceptual departure is a two-level shift: first, from single-neuron to aggregated layer-wise representations for toxicity detection, and second, from neuron activation editing to eigen-decomposition of the final output projection (lm_head), where the authors argue that prior methods conflate detection experts with generation experts. The SVD-based intervention (EigenShift) is positioned against both neuron-level baselines (DetZero, DAMP, AURA) and representation-steering methods (Singh et al., Uppaal et al.) that operate on mid-layer hidden states, with the authors claiming that targeting the final decision space avoids the catastrophic forgetting observed in earlier-layer interventions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The empirical demonstration that individual neurons hover near random-classifier AUROC (~0.50–0.55) directly challenges the foundational assumption underpinning the expert-neuron literature, suggesting that the field's primary intervention target may be fundamentally unreliable. The proposed TPH score addresses a real gap in evaluation methodology by unifying toxicity reduction and perplexity preservation into a single interpretable metric, which should facilitate fairer comparisons across future mitigation approaches. However, the method is restricted to the final lm_head layer and has not been tested on layer-wise decomposition, leaving open whether eigen-directions evolve meaningfully across depth. Generalization beyond toxicity to other semantic concepts (hate speech, vulgarity, emotional tone) is claimed but not empirically validated in the current experiments.
