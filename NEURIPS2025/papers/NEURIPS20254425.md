## Problem

Existing lens flare removal datasets are synthesized by randomly overlaying flare templates onto background images, ignoring physical principles such as the inverse-square law of illumination and the spatial relationship between light source position and flare intensity. These datasets also lack diversity in flare patterns, covering only point light sources without multiple reflections or non-spherical emitters, which limits trained models' ability to generalize to real-world flare scenarios.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254425/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a flare-corrupted RGB image as input and produces a flare-free RGB image as output. Training uses 512×512 crops with a batch size of 2, while the real-world test set consists of 100 image pairs at 3024×3024 resolution captured with smartphones and professional cameras. The 2D synthesis pipeline additionally consumes a monocular depth map of the background to compute per-flare brightness scaling, and the 3D rendering pipeline produces paired images by toggling flare visibility in Blender scenes.

## Method

FlareX is generated through a three-stage physics-informed pipeline. First, 9,500 flare templates are created in Blender using the Flared plugin, parameterizing light intensity, reflection count, glass pollution, and component geometry across 95 flare types with mutual constraints binding flare components to a spatial light source. Second, a 2D synthesis pipeline overlays these templates onto background images from the 24K Flickr dataset, but unlike prior work, it estimates a monocular depth map and applies the inverse-square illumination law (E = I·cos θ / d²) to scale each flare's brightness according to its estimated depth and angle of incidence relative to the lens. Third, 60 custom 3D scenes are built in Blender with flares placed in physically plausible locations (near light sources, in the sky, outside windows), and a keyframed camera renders 3,000 paired images that inherently obey physical constraints. A masking strategy using an eye-exam occluder to block the direct light source enables collection of truly flare-free ground truth for real-world evaluation.

## Results

On the proposed FlareX test set, models trained on FlareX achieve the best performance across all four architectures. Uformer trained on FlareX reaches 25.459 PSNR, 0.692 SSIM, and 0.133 LPIPS, surpassing the second-best (FlareReal600-trained Uformer at 24.079 PSNR) by 1.38 dB in PSNR and reducing LPIPS by 5%. Restormer trained on FlareX achieves 25.096 PSNR and 0.131 LPIPS, the best LPIPS among all configurations. For off-screen flare removal (no ground truth available), FlareX-trained Uformer attains the lowest NIQE (3.967) and BRISQUE (28.354) scores.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FlareX outperforms all four baseline training datasets across every model and metric. Compared to Wu et al. [24], FlareX-trained Uformer gains 3.21 dB in PSNR (25.459 vs. 22.252) and 0.047 in SSIM, reflecting the substantial improvement from physics-informed synthesis over naive template overlay. Against Flare7K [3], the gain is 2.07 dB in PSNR for Uformer, and notably the illumination-law ablation (Table 5) shows that even applying FlareX's synthesis method to Flare7K templates yields a 0.33 dB improvement, isolating the contribution of the physical model. Flare7K++ [21] is the strongest prior baseline, reaching 25.172 PSNR with HINet, but FlareX still leads by 0.22 dB on HINet and 1.84 dB on Uformer, with the gap widening on perceptual metrics (LPIPS 0.133 vs. 0.142). FlareReal600 [34] shows an interesting pattern: it produces the second-best Uformer PSNR (24.079) but the worst Restormer LPIPS (0.157), suggesting its real-capture data helps some architectures but introduces noise that hurts others. In the user study (Table 2), FlareX-trained models achieve 95% user preference versus 92% for Flare7K++ on the same test set, confirming that the quantitative gains align with human perception.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the lineage of semi-synthetic flare datasets initiated by Wu et al. (ICCV 2021) and Dai et al. (NeurIPS 2022, Flare7K; TPAMI 2024, Flare7K++), which relied on 2D template overlay with random affine transformations. The key departure is a physics-informed generation pipeline: parameterized Blender-based template creation (95 flare types vs. 3–35 in prior work), incorporation of the inverse-square illumination law into 2D synthesis via a Brightness Adjustment Module, and a complementary 3D scene rendering stage that naturally enforces spatial-physical constraints. The masking-based ground truth collection strategy also departs from the lens-wiping approach used by Flare7K and FlareReal600, which the authors show leaves residual reflective flare in the reference images.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FlareX addresses two persistent gaps in the flare-removal literature: insufficient pattern diversity (multiple reflections, non-spherical sources, off-screen streaks) and physically unrealistic brightness distributions in synthetic training data. The masking approach for obtaining clean ground truth is a practical contribution that the community can adopt for future benchmarks. However, the 3D-rendered subset (3,000 pairs from 60 scenes) is too small to train a competitive model on its own, and the authors acknowledge that extremely heavy flare still produces visible artifacts, leaving severe degradation as an open problem. The identification and benchmarking of off-screen flare (63 images) is a useful addition, but the overall dataset scale remains modest relative to the diversity of real-world flare conditions.
