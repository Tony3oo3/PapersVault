## Problem

Current structure-based generative models for protein binder design condition solely on the target binding site, ignoring the wealth of reusable interaction patterns in existing protein complexes. This limits both the rationality and interpretability of generated interfaces, unlike human experts who routinely leverage analogous known interfaces to guide design. No prior framework unifies cross-domain interface retrieval with sequence-structure co-design in a shared representation space.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252514/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a binding site graph (residues within 10 Å of the binder, defined via Cβ atoms) decomposed into blocks of amino acids or principal subgraphs, each with element types and 3D coordinates, along with a pre-encoded retrieval database of (binding site, binder interface) pairs spanning peptides, antibodies, and protein fragments. The binding site is encoded into a key vector that queries the database via inner-product similarity to retrieve top-N value vectors. The output is a full-atom binder structure with both amino acid sequence and 3D coordinates, decoded from the denoised latent point cloud through a flow-matching decoder.

## Method

RADiAnce unifies retrieval and generation in a single contrastive latent space. An all-atom VAE (EPT backbone) encodes binding sites and binder interfaces independently into latent point clouds, trained jointly with reconstruction (cross-entropy on block types + flow-matching MSE on coordinates), KL regularization, and a symmetric InfoNCE contrastive loss that aligns site-interface pairs while repelling mismatched ones. At inference, the encoder maps the query binding site to a key vector for fast dot-product retrieval of top-N interface value vectors from the database. A conditional latent diffusion model (cosine-schedule DDPM) then denoises in the same latent space, with an E(3)-equivariant transformer denoiser augmented by cross-attention layers that attend to the retrieved template embeddings at every reverse step, enabling the model to transfer interaction motifs (residue preferences, contact geometries, binding orientations) across domains such as antibodies, peptides, and protein fragments.

## Results

On peptide sequence-structure codesign (PepBench/LNR), RADiAnce achieves 39.42% AAR, 2.29 Å RMSD, 1.963 kJ/mol ΔΔG, 41.94% IMP, and 52.15% ISM, outperforming the strongest baseline UniMoMo (38.69% AAR, 2.31 Å RMSD, 2.409 kJ/mol ΔΔG, 40.86% IMP, 49.13% ISM). On antibody CDR codesign (RAbD), RADiAnce leads on HCDR3 with 54.66% AAR, 0.9443 Å RMSD, and 71.67% IMP, surpassing UniMoMo (52.34% AAR, 1.040 Å RMSD, 65.00% IMP) and DiffAb (51.84% AAR, 1.584 Å RMSD, 56.90% IMP). The largest gains appear in ISM (interaction site match), where RADiAnce improves over PepGLAD by ~19.5 percentage points on peptides and over DiffAb by ~44 points on HCDR3, confirming that retrieval-augmented conditioning specifically enhances recovery of physicochemical interaction patterns.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RADiAnce outperforms all eight baselines across both peptide and antibody tasks. On peptides, it beats the closest competitor UniMoMo by 0.73 pp AAR, 0.02 Å RMSD, and 3.02 pp ISM, while the gap widens dramatically against flow-matching baselines (PepFlow: +3.95 pp AAR, +24.32 pp ISM) and RFDiffusion (+4.74 pp AAR, +23.77 pp ISM). On antibody HCDR3, RADiAnce leads UniMoMo by 2.32 pp AAR and 6.67 pp IMP, and dominates DiffAb by 2.82 pp AAR and 44.24 pp ISM; against MEAN the advantage is 28.47 pp AAR and 58.83 pp ISM. The most consistent and largest margin across all tasks is in ISM, indicating that retrieval-augmented conditioning specifically improves the model's ability to reproduce correct hydrogen bonds, hydrophobic contacts, and other interaction types from retrieved templates. Cross-domain retrieval (e.g., using antibody interfaces to guide peptide generation) further boosts performance over single-domain retrieval, validating the cross-domain generalization claim.

## Relation to Prior Work

RADiAnce builds directly on the UniMoMo VAE architecture [31] for all-atom encoding/decoding and the E(3)-equivariant transformer (EPT) [25] as the denoising backbone, while adopting the symmetric InfoNCE contrastive loss from DrugCLIP [16] to align binding-site and binder embeddings. Conceptually, it extends the retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) paradigm—previously applied to small-molecule design (f-RAG, IRDiff, READ) and sequence-only antibody inverse folding (RADAb)—to the joint sequence-structure co-design of protein binders, and is the first to unify retrieval across heterogeneous binder modalities (peptides, antibodies, protein fragments) in a single latent space. The key departure from prior binder-design methods (RFDiffusion, PepFlow, PepGLAD, MEAN, DiffAb, GeoAB) is that generation is conditioned not only on the target binding site but also on retrieved interface exemplars, injected via cross-attention at every diffusion step.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that interaction motifs transfer across structurally and biochemically distinct binder classes (e.g., antibody CDRs inform peptide design and vice versa), the paper validates a cross-domain knowledge-transfer principle that could substantially expand the effective training signal for binder design. The de novo antibody pipeline (Section 4.3), which iteratively redesigns all six CDRs against a target without a pre-solved complex, points toward a practical workflow for therapeutic antibody engineering. However, the de novo evidence is limited to a single target (CD4) with three iterations, and the overall performance gains over the strongest baseline (UniMoMo) are moderate (e.g., ISM 52.15% vs. 49.13% for peptides), leaving open whether the retrieval advantage persists at larger scale or on more diverse targets. The authors also note that retrieval quality remains a bottleneck, motivating future work on richer structural descriptors and more robust conditional-integration strategies.
