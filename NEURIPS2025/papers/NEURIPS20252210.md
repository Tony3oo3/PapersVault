## Problem

GP-UCB is a widely used fully adaptive algorithm for Gaussian process bandits that empirically outperforms non-adaptive nearly-optimal algorithms (e.g., phased elimination, random exploration with domain shrinking), yet its theoretical regret guarantees in the noise-free setting were strictly suboptimal. This paper closes the gap between theory and practice by establishing nearly-optimal regret upper bounds for GP-UCB under both the squared exponential and Matérn kernels, resolving an open problem raised by Vakili (2022) and Li and Scarlett (2024).

## Input / Output

The algorithm receives a compact input domain X ⊂ R^d, a known positive definite kernel (squared exponential or Matérn with smoothness ν > 1/2), and an RKHS norm upper bound B. At each step it adaptively selects a query point by maximizing the GP upper confidence bound score μ(x; X_{t-1}) + B·σ(x; X_{t-1}), observes the exact (noise-free) function value, and updates the posterior. The theoretical output is a deterministic cumulative regret bound R_T and a simple regret bound r_T that match known lower bounds up to polylogarithmic factors.

## Method

The key technical contribution is a new algorithm-independent upper bound on the noise-free posterior standard deviations (Lemma 3), obtained by bridging the information-gain-based analysis from the noisy regime to the noise-free setting. The proof introduces a time-varying noise variance sequence λ_t² that decreases with t, and shows the maximum information gain condition γ_t(λ_t²) ≤ (t−1)/3 is satisfiable with λ_t² = t·exp(−C·t^{1/(d+1)}) for the SE kernel and λ_t² = C·t^{−2ν/d}(ln t)^{2ν/d} for the Matérn kernel. A recursive elimination argument (Lemma 5) repeatedly applies the elliptical potential count lemma to peel off one index at a time, yielding the cumulative bound ∑σ(x_t; X_{t-1}) ≤ T̄−1 + ∑λ_t. Combined with the standard UCB regret decomposition R_T ≤ 2B∑σ(x_t; X_{t-1}), this gives O(1) cumulative regret under the SE kernel and Matérn kernel with d < ν, and near-optimal rates in all other regimes.

## Results

The paper establishes the first constant O(1) cumulative regret bound for GP-UCB under the squared exponential (SE) kernel in the noise-free setting, improving upon the previous O(√T ln^d T) guarantee of Lyu et al. (2019). For the Matérn kernel, GP-UCB achieves Õ(T^((d−ν)/d)) when d>ν, O(ln²T) when d=ν, and O(1) when d<ν, matching the conjectured lower bound of Vakili (2022) and the formal lower bound of Li and Scarlett (2024) up to polylogarithmic factors. For simple regret, GP-UCB achieves Õ(T^{−ν/d}) under the Matérn kernel, matching the lower bound of Bull (2011) up to polylog factors, and O(√T exp(−CT^{1/(d+1)})) under the SE kernel. Empirically, GP-UCB outperforms the non-adaptive nearly-optimal algorithms REDS and PE across all three kernel settings (Figure 1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to the previous GP-UCB analysis of Lyu et al. (2019) and Kim and Sanz-Alonso (2024), this paper improves the SE cumulative regret from O(√T ln^d T) to O(1) and the Matérn regret from Õ(T^((ν+d)/(2ν+d))) to Õ(T^((d−ν)/d)) (or O(1) when d<ν), a strict improvement in all regimes. Against the non-adaptive nearly-optimal algorithms REDS (Salgia et al., 2024) and PE (Iwazaki and Takeno, 2025a), GP-UCB matches or improves upon their deterministic guarantees: it achieves O(1) vs. O(ln T) for PE under SE, O(ln²T) vs. O(ln^{2+α}T) for PE at ν=d, and O(1) vs. O(ln T) for PE at ν>d, while empirically outperforming both in cumulative regret (Figure 1). Versus Kernel-AMM-UCB (Flynn and Reeb, 2024), GP-UCB achieves O(1) vs. O(ln^{d+1}T) under SE and strictly better polynomial exponents under Matérn. For simple regret, GP-UCB matches the MVR lower-bound-matching rate Õ(T^{−ν/d}) of Iwazaki and Takeno (2025a) under Matérn, and matches the Bull (2011) lower bound Ω(T^{−ν/d}) up to polylog factors, while empirically underperforming EI in simple regret minimization (Appendix B.2). The Explore-then-Commit bound of Vakili (2022) is strictly dominated by the new GP-UCB guarantee in all Matérn regimes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper refines the regret analysis of the noise-free GP-UCB algorithm originally proposed by Lyu et al. (2019), whose guarantees were strictly sub-optimal. The key technical ingredient is a new algorithm-independent upper bound on posterior standard deviations (Lemma 3) that bridges the information-gain-based analysis from the noisy GP bandit literature (Srinivas et al., 2010) to the noise-free setting, building on the elliptical potential count lemma of Flynn and Reeb (2024) and the analysis framework of Iwazaki and Takeno (2025a) for non-adaptive maximum variance reduction. The paper departs from prior nearly-optimal noise-free algorithms (REDS, PE, Kernel-AMM-UCB) that all relied on non-adaptive sampling schemes, by showing that the standard fully adaptive GP-UCB also achieves matching regret.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result resolves a gap between theory and practice: GP-UCB empirically outperforms the non-adaptive nearly-optimal algorithms, yet its theoretical guarantee was strictly sub-optimal. The algorithm-independent nature of Lemma 3 means it can be plugged into existing noisy-setting analyses (GP-TS, contextual bandits, multi-objective, robust formulations) to obtain noise-free variants, broadening the impact beyond GP-UCB. Open questions remain: the optimal simple regret rate under the SE kernel is unknown (the MIG upper bound exponent d+1 may be loose), and the Bayesian setting yields only O(√ln T) regret rather than the O(1) frequentist bound, which is counterintuitive and unresolved.
