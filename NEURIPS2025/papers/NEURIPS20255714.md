## Problem

Scaling In-Context Reinforcement Learning (ICRL) is bottlenecked by two issues: existing task benchmarks are either trivially simple (multi-armed bandits) or rely on superficial parameter randomization that preserves core structural biases, limiting generalization; and supervised meta-training methods for ICRL suffer from a distribution shift between training-time (expert-generated) and inference-time (self-generated) trajectories, causing catastrophic deployment failures.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a sequence of tuples (s_t, g_t, a_t, r_t) where s_t is a discrete state index, g_t is a prior policy tag (one of 8 IDs identifying which of 7 behavior policies or an 'Unk' placeholder generated the action), a_t is the discrete action taken, and r_t is the scalar reward received. At each position, the model predicts the oracle action a_t* conditioned on the full preceding context. Sequences can extend up to 512K steps, with training using T=12K step sequences across 512K sequences (6B total steps), and the model is trained on tasks with n_s ≤ 128 states and n_a ≤ 5 actions.

## Method

The paper introduces AnyMDP, a procedural generator for tabular MDPs that enforces banded transition matrices (constraining transitions to a local band around each state) and composite rewards (decomposing reward into state-dependent, state-action-dependent, and potential-based components) to guarantee ergodicity, exponentially decaying stationary distributions under random policy, and ascending value functions—ensuring high-value states are hard to reach by chance. For training, Decoupled Policy Distillation (DPD) separates a diverse set of 7 behavior policies (oracle, Q-learning, model-based RL, random, myopic greedy, etc.) used to generate trajectories from a single oracle reference policy used for step-wise supervision labels, reducing the train-inference distribution gap while preserving target optimality. Each action is augmented with a prior policy tag so the model can interpret heterogeneous trajectory sources. The architecture uses linear-attention causal sequence models (RWKV-7 selected from RWKV-7, Gated Delta-Net, Gated Slot Attention, and Mamba-2) trained with a chunk-wise scheme that blocks gradients across sequence segments to handle very long contexts efficiently.

## Results

On unseen AnyMDP tasks (n_s=16, n_a=5), OmniRL(AnyMDP) achieves 95.3% normalized episodic return with only 2.0K steps and 29 episodes to reach 99% of best performance, compared to TQL-UCB's 92.0% at 297K steps and PPO's 90.6% at 476K steps. On unseen Gymnasium tasks, OmniRL(AnyMDP) reaches 99.8% on FrozenLake (non-slippery) in just 0.3K steps versus 23.6K for TQL-UCB, and 95.2% on DarkRoom (6×6) in 845 steps versus 6.2K for TQL-UCB. OmniRL(GarnetMDP), trained on the same architecture but Garnet MDPs, fails to generalize beyond Garnet tasks (e.g., 0.0% on Discrete-Pendulum, 31.3% on FrozenLake slippery), confirming that AnyMDP's structural diversity is critical for broad generalization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against classical RL baselines (TQL-UCB and PPO), OmniRL(AnyMDP) achieves comparable or superior final performance while requiring 1–2 orders of magnitude fewer steps (e.g., 0.3K vs. 23.6K on FrozenLake non-slippery; 2.0K vs. 297K on AnyMDP T(16,5)), confirming the sample-efficiency advantage of in-context learning. OmniRL(GarnetMDP), the same architecture trained on Garnet MDPs, collapses on non-Garnet tasks (0.0% on Discrete-Pendulum, 31.3% on FrozenLake slippery), demonstrating that AnyMDP's fully randomized transition dynamics and composite rewards are essential for generalization. In the method comparison (Figure 6), OmniRL surpasses AD, AD^ε, and DPT by a large margin across all three initial-context settings (online RL, offline RL, and imitation learning), validating the effectiveness of Decoupled Policy Distillation and step-wise supervision. Removing prior information (OmniRL w/o a priori) causes a noticeable performance drop in all settings, confirming the value of policy-identity metadata. In the offline RL comparison (Table 2), OmniRL outperforms CQL on all four unseen Gymnasium tasks when initialized with random-policy demonstrations (e.g., −91 vs. −560.2 on CliffWalking episodes 0–20), and matches or exceeds CQL when initialized with oracle demonstrations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the ICRL meta-training line initiated by RL² [12] and refined by supervised approaches like Algorithm Distillation (AD) [13], AD^ε [15], and DPT [16], departing from them through Decoupled Policy Distillation (DPD), which separates behavior and reference policies to inject trajectory diversity while preserving oracle supervision, and through step-wise supervision that yields T× training efficiency over DPT's one-step imitation. On the benchmark side, it moves beyond prior procedurally generated MDPs (Garnet [58], PROCON [57], Xland-MiniGrid [18]) by fully randomizing both transition dynamics and reward functions under banded-matrix and composite-reward constraints, rather than randomizing only one component. The empirical analysis of task-diversity thresholds builds on ICL emergence literature [28, 34, 77] but is the first to isolate task count as the critical variable for ICRL generalization in a controlled setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AnyMDP provides the first scalable, low-structural-bias benchmark for ICRL that can generate arbitrarily many distinct tasks, directly addressing the field's bottleneck of limited task collections (<1K tasks in prior work). The finding that ≥10K unique tasks are needed to transition from task-recognition to genuine in-context learning, and that more generalized learners paradoxically perform worse in few-shot evaluation (long context is a 'tax'), challenges the standard short-horizon evaluation paradigm and has direct implications for how the community should measure ICRL progress. The main limitation is confinement to discrete tabular MDPs with state spaces ≤128; extension to continuous or high-dimensional spaces remains open, as does understanding whether the task-diversity threshold generalizes beyond this regime.
