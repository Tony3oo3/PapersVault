## Problem

Existing VQA datasets for the visually impaired (e.g., VizWiz) are limited to static images and object-centric questions, while the closest egocentric work (VIEW-QA) relies on sighted actors simulating blindness. No dataset captures the real-time, first-person visual assistance needs of actual blind individuals navigating dynamic environments, leaving a critical gap in evaluating whether contemporary MLLMs can serve as effective live visual assistants.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250886/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives an egocentric video clip (average 40 s, captured by blind users via chest-mounted GoPro or handheld phone) and a short text question (average 7.9 words) posed at a specific timestamp. Critically, the model may only access frames up to the question timestamp, enforcing an online/streaming QA setting that mirrors real-time assistance. The output is an open-form text answer (average 5.7 words), evaluated against 1–4 human-annotated reference answers per question using GPT-4o-mini semantic matching.

## Method

EgoBlind is constructed through a multi-stage pipeline: 478 long-form egocentric videos from blind content creators are partitioned into 1,329 clips, and 5,311 questions are generated via manual extraction (541), GPT-4o generation with blind-user verification, and direct blind-user annotation, then validated by 63 sighted and 111 blind volunteers across three quality-control stages. Questions are categorized into six assistance types (Information Reading, Safety Warning, Navigation, Social Communication, Tool Use, Other Resources) and annotated with multiple reference answers by 21 university students. The benchmark evaluates 16 MLLMs (12 open-source, 4 closed-source) using GPT-4o-mini as an automated judge (Cohen's κ = 0.73 vs. human raters), and the paper further investigates the impact of single-frame vs. multi-frame inputs, blind-specific prompting, and LoRA instruction tuning on EgoBlind training data, revealing that all models fall 28–54% below human performance (87.4%).

## Results

On EgoBlind, the best-performing model (GPT-4o) achieves 59.3% overall QA accuracy, falling ~28% behind human performance of 87.4%. Among open-source models, InternVL2.5-26B leads at 55.0%, while the weakest model (ShareGPT4Video) scores only 32.9%. Navigation is the most challenging category for nearly all models (best: GPT-4o at 52.6%), while Information Reading is the easiest (best: GPT-4o at 61.2%). No single model wins across all six assistance types, and closed-source Gemini models (1.5: 51.8%, 2.0: 49.9%) underperform the top open-source InternVL2.5-26B (55.0%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPT-4o (59.3%) is the best overall performer, outscoring Gemini 2.5 Flash (56.0%) by 3.3 points and the best open-source model InternVL2.5-26B (55.0%) by 4.3 points. Notably, Gemini 1.5 Flash (51.8%) and Gemini 2.0 Flash (49.9%) both underperform InternVL2.5-26B, contradicting their typical dominance in general-purpose VQA. Among open-source models, LLaVA-OV (54.5%) and InternVL2.5-8B (53.5%) form a strong second tier, while models specifically tuned for blind image QA (Video-LLaVA: 38.1%, LLaMA-VID: 39.1%) and egocentric VQA (LLaVA-Video: 51.5%) do not transfer well to this task. The weakest performers—ShareGPT4Video (32.9%), CogVLM2-Video (40.3%), and MiniCPM-V 2.6 (40.7%)—lag the best model by over 18 points. Across all 16 models, Navigation is the most consistently difficult category (range: 14.0%–52.6%), while no model exceeds 72.5% on any single category, highlighting a uniform gap relative to human performance (83.1%–96.6% across categories).

## Relation to Prior Work

EgoBlind extends the line of blind-oriented VQA initiated by VizWiz (CVPR 2018), which was limited to static images and object-centric questions, by introducing an egocentric, online (timestamp-restricted), video-based formulation. It also departs from VIEW-QA (2024), which used 360-degree footage from sighted actors simulating blind experiences, by collecting both videos and questions from actual blind and visually impaired individuals. On the egocentric VQA side, the paper positions itself against general-purpose benchmarks (Ego4D, EgoSchema, EgoTaskQA) and assist-oriented ones (QAEgo4D, AssistQ, EgoTextVQA), arguing that none capture the full breadth of real-time visual assistance needs of blind users in dynamic, first-person settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

With 2.2 billion visually impaired people worldwide, the ~28% accuracy gap between the best MLLM (GPT-4o at 59.3%) and human performance (87.4%) underscores that current models are far from deployable as reliable visual assistants. The paper reveals specific, actionable failure modes—sycophantic hallucination of potentially dangerous answers, inability to reason about user intention, and poor spatio-temporal orientation tracking—that point to concrete research directions. Key limitations include the dataset being restricted to mainland China, a relatively small training set, and the rapidly evolving MLLM landscape; the authors plan to maintain a leaderboard and expand data collection with blind associations globally.
