## Problem

Evidential Deep Learning (EDL) achieves efficient single-pass uncertainty quantification by predicting a Dirichlet distribution over class probabilities, but this restrictive parametric assumption limits its ability to express complex or ambiguous uncertainty patterns. In practice, EDL exhibits substantial overlap between noisy in-distribution and out-of-distribution uncertainty distributions, and fails to produce faithful epistemic uncertainty that decreases monotonically with more data. The paper addresses this by asking whether a more expressive yet still computationally tractable distribution over class probabilities can restore EDL's generalizability across diverse scenarios.

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/CIFAR-100, 28×28 for MNIST variants) and, in a single forward pass, outputs a predicted class label along with three uncertainty scalars (total, aleatoric, epistemic) computed via label-wise variance decomposition. Unusually, the network predicts three parameter sets per input—concentration α ∈ ℝ₊ᴷ, allocation probabilities p ∈ Δᴷ⁻¹, and dispersion τ ∈ ℝ₊—that jointly define a Flexible Dirichlet distribution, enabling the class probability distribution to be multimodal with up to ‖p‖₀ distinct modes on the simplex.

## Method

F-EDL replaces the Dirichlet prior in EDL with the Flexible Dirichlet (FD) distribution, which generalizes the Dirichlet by introducing allocation probabilities p and a shared dispersion parameter τ that couple components through a common random variable. The architecture uses a shared feature extractor (VGG-16 or ResNet-18) with three lightweight heads: exp-activated for α, softmax for p, and softplus for τ, with spectral normalization applied to the backbone and α-head for Lipschitz-constrained robustness. Training minimizes a closed-form expected MSE over the FD distribution plus a Brier-score regularization on p, requiring no sampling or sensitive hyperparameter tuning. Theoretically, the FD distribution is proven to be a conjugate prior to the categorical likelihood, giving F-EDL a Bayesian interpretation with input-dependent improper priors; the class probability distribution decomposes as a mixture of Dirichlet components (multimodal), and the predictive distribution decomposes as an input-weighted mixture of EDL and softmax predictions, unifying both paradigms.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, F-EDL achieves 91.19% test accuracy, 99.10% AUPR for misclassification detection, and 91.20/88.37 AUPR for OOD detection (SVHN/CIFAR-100), all best among compared methods. On CIFAR-100, F-EDL reaches 69.40% test accuracy, 94.01% AUPR for misclassification, and 75.35/80.58 AUPR for OOD detection (SVHN/TinyImageNet), again leading all baselines. The largest gains over the closest EDL competitor (DAEDL) appear in OOD detection: +5.7 AUPR on CIFAR-10→SVHN and +3.3 AUPR on CIFAR-100→SVHN. F-EDL also outperforms the original EDL by 7.6% test accuracy on CIFAR-10 and 23.5% on CIFAR-100.

## Baselines & Comparisons

F-EDL consistently outperforms all six baselines across every metric and both ID datasets. Against the strongest EDL competitor DAEDL, F-EDL gains +5.7 AUPR on CIFAR-10→SVHN OOD detection (91.20 vs. 85.54) and +3.3 AUPR on CIFAR-100→SVHN (75.35 vs. 72.07), while matching or slightly exceeding DAEDL on test accuracy (91.19 vs. 91.11 on CIFAR-10; 69.40 vs. 66.01 on CIFAR-100). Against the original EDL, F-EDL improves test accuracy by 7.6 points on CIFAR-10 (91.19 vs. 83.55) and 23.5 points on CIFAR-100 (69.40 vs. 45.91), and OOD AUPR by 12.1 points on CIFAR-10→SVHN (91.20 vs. 79.12). Against I-EDL and R-EDL, F-EDL leads by 2.0 and 1.1 points in test accuracy on CIFAR-10, and by 3.0 and 5.9 points on CIFAR-100. Dropout, the traditional multi-forward-pass baseline, is outperformed by 8.4 points in accuracy on CIFAR-10 and by 39.8 AUPR on CIFAR-10→SVHN OOD detection, confirming that single-pass F-EDL surpasses even ensemble-style methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends evidential deep learning (EDL) by replacing the Dirichlet distribution over class probabilities with the more expressive Flexible Dirichlet (FD) distribution, a generalization introduced by Ongaro and Migliorati (2013) for compositional data analysis but previously unexplored for uncertainty quantification. It also adopts the label-wise variance-based uncertainty decomposition from Duan et al. (2024) and builds on the conjugate-prior structure of EDL (Sensoy et al., 2018), proving that the FD distribution is itself conjugate to the categorical likelihood. The key conceptual departure is that F-EDL uses input-dependent improper priors (learned via neural heads for p and τ) rather than fixed Dirichlet priors, and introduces a Brier-score-based regularization on the allocation vector p that has no analogue in prior EDL variants (I-EDL, R-EDL, DAEDL).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

F-EDL addresses a well-documented limitation of EDL—the restrictive Dirichlet assumption that prevents multimodal uncertainty representations—while preserving single-forward-pass efficiency, making it practical for real-time deployment. The theoretical results (conjugacy, EDL as a special case, multimodality, EDL-softmax mixture decomposition, generalized subjective logic interpretation) provide a principled foundation that goes beyond ad-hoc architectural changes. However, the method remains limited to classification, does not fully disentangle aleatoric and epistemic components, and still relies on external regularization (spectral normalization) to control epistemic uncertainty, leaving open questions about intrinsically stable training objectives and extension to regression or other tasks.
