## Problem

Feature Conformal Prediction (FCP) achieves shorter confidence bands than Split CP by performing calibration in the feature space of a neural network, but its Band Estimation step requires solving expensive non-linear optimization (LiPRA) to map bands back to the output space, making it impractical for real-world deployment. This paper asks whether the non-linear operations in FCP can be approximated cheaply without sacrificing coverage or band-length guarantees.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255293/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a trained neural network decomposed into a feature extractor h and prediction head g, a held-out calibration set, and a test point X'. It computes the gradient ∇g(v̂) of the prediction head at the trained feature v̂ = h(X) for each calibration and test sample. For regression, it outputs an individual-specific confidence interval [f(X') − ‖∇g(v̂')‖·Q_{1−α}, f(X') + ‖∇g(v̂')‖·Q_{1−α}]; for classification (FFRAPS) it outputs a prediction set; for segmentation it outputs per-pixel uncertainty bands. The band width is modulated by the local gradient norm, so samples with steeper prediction-head gradients receive wider intervals.

## Method

FFCP replaces FCP's expensive LiPRA-based band estimation with a first-order Taylor expansion of the prediction head g around the trained feature v̂, yielding the non-conformity score s_ff(X,Y,g∘h) = |Y − f(X)| / ‖∇g(v̂)‖. This transforms FCP's infimum-over-feature-space score into a closed-form ratio and reduces band construction to a simple interval scaled by the gradient norm, eliminating all non-linear optimization. Theoretically, the paper proves valid coverage under exchangeability (Theorem 4.1) and shows FFCP provably outperforms Split CP in average band length under two 'square conditions'—Expansion (feature-space scores are closer to their quantiles) and Quantile Stability (quantiles generalize from calibration to test) (Theorem 4.2). The gradient-level technique is modular and extends to CQR (FFCQR), LCP (FFLCP), and RAPS (FFRAPS), achieving ~50× speedup over FCP while matching its coverage and band-length performance.

## Results

FFCP achieves an approximate 50× speedup over FCP in runtime (ranging from 31× on FB1 to 108× on STAR), while maintaining comparable coverage (~90%) and shorter band lengths than Split CP across most datasets. For example, on FB1 FFCP achieves a band length of 1.472 vs. 2.004 for Split CP, and on BIKE 0.635 vs. 0.703. In classification (ImageNet-Val), FFRAPS matches RAPS coverage (~90.7%) while reducing prediction set length (e.g., 2.256 vs. 2.348 for ResNet101). In segmentation (Cityscapes), FFCP produces sharper, more refined per-pixel uncertainty bands than FCP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against **Split CP**, FFCP consistently achieves shorter band lengths (e.g., 1.472 vs. 2.004 on FB1, 3.134 vs. 3.982 on MEPS19, 0.635 vs. 0.703 on BIKE) while maintaining comparable ~90% coverage, validating Theorem 4.2's band-length guarantee. Against **FCP**, FFCP matches or slightly improves band length in most datasets (e.g., 1.838 vs. 1.939 on COM, 3.134 vs. 3.493 on MEPS19) while being 31–108× faster in runtime, confirming the Taylor approximation preserves FCP's quality. Against **CQR**, the FFCQR variant reduces runtime by 4–65× compared to FCQR and yields shorter bands than CQR (e.g., 2.22 vs. 1.42 on BIO at [0.4,0.6] level). Against **LCP**, FFLCP outperforms LCP in group coverage on most datasets (e.g., 82.97 vs. 77.61 on BIKE, 86.78 vs. 81.10 on BIO). Against **RAPS**, FFRAPS matches coverage (~90.7%) while reducing prediction set length on most ImageNet models (e.g., 2.256 vs. 2.348 for ResNet101, 4.434 vs. 4.560 for ResNet18). Against **SSCP** and **CV+**, FFCP is roughly 50× faster than SSCP with shorter bands, and shows improved efficiency with comparable coverage versus the full CP CV+ baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits directly in the lineage of Feature Conformal Prediction (FCP, Teng et al. 2022), which moved conformal prediction from output space to feature space to exploit semantic information in neural network representations. The key departure is replacing FCP's expensive LiPRA-based band estimation (which solves a non-linear optimization to map feature-space balls to output-space intervals) with a first-order Taylor expansion of the prediction head, yielding a simple gradient-normalized non-conformity score. The gradient-level technique is then shown to be modular, extending naturally to CQR, LCP, and RAPS variants, positioning FFCP as a general acceleration layer over the broader conformal prediction family rather than a standalone method.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: FCP's LiPRA step made it infeasible for many real-world deployments, and the ~50× speedup (with up to 500× for deep decoders) removes that barrier while preserving coverage and band-length quality. The theoretical contribution—proving FFCP yields shorter bands than Split CP under 'square conditions' (expansion + quantile stability)—is useful but somewhat abstract, and the conditions are verified only empirically. Open questions include whether higher-order Taylor terms or randomized smoothing of the gradient (to handle vanishing/unstable gradients) could further tighten bands, and whether the first-order approximation degrades for highly non-linear prediction heads in very deep architectures.
