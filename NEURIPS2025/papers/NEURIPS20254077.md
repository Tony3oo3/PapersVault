## Problem

Traditional decision trees rely on axis-aligned linear threshold splits, forcing them to repeatedly split on the same feature at multiple levels to capture non-linear feature-target relationships. This inflates tree depth and node count, degrading interpretability and the quality of local explanations. The paper asks whether non-linear decision boundaries can be captured within a single node while preserving the visual interpretability that makes trees attractive in high-stakes domains.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard tabular feature vectors (continuous, ordinal, and one-hot-encoded categorical features) and outputs a class label from a finite set. Each internal node's shape function is represented as a piecewise-constant mapping: an internal CART (or BiCART) tree partitions the feature space into L bins, and a discrete lookup vector assigns each bin to a branch. Categorical variables are handled by passing all one-hot columns into the internal tree, enabling superset branching that linear trees cannot express. The final model is a tree of depth at most 6, with each node's shape function independently visualizable as a 1D (or 2D heatmap) plot.

## Method

The Shape Generalized Tree (SGT) replaces each node's threshold comparison with a learnable axis-aligned shape function f_Θ applied to a single feature, routing samples based on whether f_Θ(x_d) ≤ 0. ShapeCART learns these functions via a two-stage procedure: first, an internal CART tree is fit on the candidate feature to produce L mutually exclusive bins; second, a coordinate descent algorithm optimally assigns bins to branches (left/right, or K-way for SGT_K) to minimize weighted impurity, initialized by the better of Weighted K-Means clustering or the internal tree's root split. Extensions to bivariate shape functions (S²GT) use BiCART for the internal tree and a pairwise selection heuristic to limit the O(D²) candidate pairs to P promising ones. A TAO post-processing step refits shape functions at each node using subtree predictions and prunes low-gain nodes. The paper proves SGTs are strictly more expressive than linear trees (Theorem 2) and that ShapeCART's per-node information gain dominates CART's (Lemma 1).

## Results

Across 26 real-world classification datasets, the TAO-refined multi-way SGT (SGT₃-T) achieves the best axis-aligned average test accuracy of 88.0%, outperforming CART (85.2%), DPDT (86.2%), and AxTAO (85.4%). In the bivariate setting, S²GT₃-T reaches 91.9% average accuracy, surpassing BiCART (89.6%) and BiTAO (90.0%). Notably, SGT-C consistently outperforms all greedy TDIDT baselines (CART, SERDT, HSTree) at every evaluated depth (2–6), and SGT₃-C with depth 2 often matches or exceeds the maximum-depth accuracy of deeper linear trees, demonstrating that shape-function branching enables substantially more compact models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the greedy TDIDT baselines, SGT-C wins at every depth: it beats CART by 1.1% (86.3 vs 85.2), SERDT by 1.2% (86.3 vs 85.1), and HSTree by 1.2% (86.3 vs 85.1) in best accuracy. The multi-way variant SGT₃-C extends this lead to 2.6% over CART (87.8 vs 85.2). Among non-greedy methods, SGT-T and SGT₃-T outperform AxTAO by 1.4% and 2.6% respectively, and DPDT by 0.6% and 1.6%; SPLIT is a clear outlier at 80.3%, trailing all other methods by 5+ points. In the bivariate setting, S²GT-C surpasses BiCART by 1.8% (91.4 vs 89.6) and S²GT-T surpasses BiTAO by 1.6% (91.6 vs 90.0), with the multi-way bivariate variant S²GT₃-T achieving the overall best result of 91.9%. A key practical finding is that SGT-C at depth 2 often matches the depth-6 accuracy of CART and other linear trees, meaning shape-function branching can halve tree depth while maintaining or improving accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalizes the classic CART decision tree framework by replacing axis-aligned linear threshold splits with learnable shape functions at each internal node, drawing conceptual inspiration from Generalized Additive Models (GAMs) where per-feature non-linear shape functions capture feature-target relationships. The induction algorithm (ShapeCART) is built on the TDIDT paradigm and uses internal CART/BiCART trees to construct piecewise-constant shape functions, with TAO post-processing for global refinement. The bivariate extension (S²GT) departs from BiCART's linear-combination splits by allowing arbitrary non-linear shape functions over feature pairs, while the multi-way extension (SGT_K) generalizes binary branching to K-way routing. Theoretically, the paper establishes that SGTs strictly subsume axis-aligned linear trees in expressiveness, providing a clean formal bridge between the two model families.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SGTs address a core practical limitation of decision trees in high-stakes domains (healthcare, finance, manufacturing): the need for deep, repeated splits on the same feature to capture non-linear effects, which degrades interpretability. By packing non-linear decision logic into single nodes, SGTs achieve comparable or better accuracy with shallower, more compact trees, directly improving the sparsity of local explanations. Key open questions include systematic human-subject evaluation of shape-function interpretability (the authors acknowledge this gap), exploration of richer shape function parameterizations (e.g., neural networks as in GAMs), and theoretical characterization of consistency rates and empirical risk bounds for ShapeCART. The approach is inherently limited to tabular data and the cognitive cost of interpreting piecewise-constant shape functions versus simple thresholds remains an unresolved usability question.
