## Problem

Adversarial training (AT) is a promising defense against LLM jailbreak attacks, but synthesizing long adversarial prompts during AT is extremely resource-consuming, requiring discrete optimization in high-dimensional token spaces. It remains unclear how the adversarial suffix length used during AT relates to the robustness gained against jailbreak attacks of varying lengths. This paper asks whether short-length AT can suffice to defend against long-length jailbreak attacks, and provides both theoretical and empirical evidence that it can.

## Input / Output

For AT, the model receives harmful instructions concatenated with GCG-synthesized adversarial suffixes of fixed token length M_train ∈ {5,10,20,30,40,50}, and is trained to produce benign refusal responses. For robustness evaluation, the trained model is attacked with suffix jailbreak prompts (GCG, BEAST, AmpleGCG, AutoDAN, GCQ) at test suffix lengths M_test ∈ {5,10,20,40,60,80,100,120} as well as non-suffix attacks (PAIR, DeepInception), with success measured by an LLM-based judge. The theoretical framework operates on in-context linear regression inputs where M adversarial perturbation vectors (each bounded by ‖δ‖₂ ≤ ε) are appended to the end of a clean prompt matrix of N samples.

## Method

The paper introduces a novel ICL suffix adversarial attack that appends M adversarially perturbed in-context samples to the end of a clean prompt, simulating how suffix jailbreaks append adversarial tokens to harmful instructions. Under this attack, they derive a closed-form solution for the minimax AT dynamics of a single-layer linear self-attention model trained via continuous gradient flow, showing the converged parameter depends on the training suffix length M_train through a regularized inverse (Γ(M_train)Λ + ε²ψ(M_train)I_d)⁻¹Λ. The key theoretical result is a robust generalization bound for the trained model against a test attack of length M_test, which scales as O(N² · M_test²/M_train⁴), implying that AT with suffix length Θ(√M) suffices to defend against attacks of length Θ(M). Empirically, they apply LoRA-based AT with GCG at six suffix lengths on five 7B–8B LLMs and confirm a strong positive correlation (PCC > 0.7 for GCG) between ASR and the ratio √M_test/M_train, with M_train = 20 already reducing ASR by ≥30% for attacks up to M_test = 120.

## Results

The paper validates its theoretical bound Θ(√M_test/M_train) empirically: for GCG-based AT, the Pearson correlation coefficient (PCC) between ASR and √M_test/M_train exceeds 0.7 for all five base models (range: 0.76–0.93), with p-values below 10⁻⁸ in most cases. AT with a short adversarial suffix length of M_train=20 reduces the ASR of jailbreak attacks with suffix lengths up to M_test=120 by at least 30% across all experiments. For non-suffix attacks, DeepInception's ASR drops to 0% with M_train=20, while PAIR's ASR decreases from 84% to ~50% (Vicuna-7B) and from 71% to ~25% (Qwen2.5-7B). Time cost for AT with M_train=20 is ~13–14 hours, roughly 30–60% less than M_train=50 (~20 hours).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against external AT methods; instead, it compares different adversarial suffix lengths (M_train ∈ {5, 10, 20, 30, 40, 50}) during AT and evaluates robustness against seven different jailbreak attacks (GCG, BEAST, AmpleGCG, AutoDAN, GCQ, PAIR, DeepInception). The key comparison is between short-length AT (M_train=20) and long-length AT (M_train=50): both achieve strong robustness, but M_train=20 saves 30–60% training time (e.g., 13.8h vs. 20.4h on Vicuna-7B). For the same-attack setting (GCG AT vs. GCG attack), the correlation is strongest (PCC 0.76–0.93), while for cross-attack settings (GCG AT vs. BEAST/GCQ), the correlation is weaker but still significant for most models. Notably, AT is so effective against AmpleGCG and AutoDAN that ASRs are driven to near zero regardless of M_train, making the correlation unobservable. For non-suffix attacks, DeepInception is fully neutralized at M_train=20, while PAIR shows diminishing returns beyond M_train=20, suggesting different vulnerability mechanisms.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the in-context learning (ICL) theory of Zhang et al. (JMLR 2024) on trained linear self-attention transformers by introducing a novel suffix adversarial attack with bounded per-token perturbations, departing from Anwar et al. (2024) who allow unrestricted real-space perturbations. The minimax adversarial training formulation follows Madry et al. (ICLR 2018), and the empirical AT pipeline builds on the GCG-based safety alignment of Mazeika et al. (HarmBench). Unlike Wei et al. [61], who study adversarial robustness of fixed ICL models, this paper analyzes how adversarial training itself shapes the robustness of ICL models, bridging the ICL theory and LLM AT in a way not previously formalized.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The √M scaling result provides a concrete, actionable guideline for practitioners: a 20-token adversarial suffix during AT can defend against 120-token jailbreak attacks, saving 30–60% of AT compute time. This is practically significant given that long-suffix AT is the primary bottleneck in deploying AT-based safety alignment at scale. Key limitations include the restriction to discrete (token-level) AT rather than continuous embedding-space AT, the simplified linear-transformer theoretical model, and the weaker correlation observed for unseen attacks (BEAST, GCQ) and non-suffix attacks (PAIR). Open questions include whether the √M scaling generalizes to multi-attack AT, larger models, and continuous-space adversarial training.
