## Problem

While RAG systems inject external contextual knowledge into LLMs to complement or override parametric knowledge, the internal mechanisms by which models reconcile these two sources—especially under conflict—remain poorly understood. Prior behavioral studies reveal that LLMs often stubbornly adhere to parametric beliefs, but offer no mechanistic account of how knowledge is routed, accumulated, or suppressed across layers. Additionally, traditional linear probes are limited to fixed label sets and cannot adapt to dynamically specified entities, making them unsuitable for tracing arbitrary knowledge flows.

## Input / Output

The probe consumes a concatenated sequence of the external document, the query, and a triple '[START] {target_entity} [END]' appended at the end (capped at 3,000 tokens), and produces a binary relevance score (Yes/No) read from the hidden state at the [END] token position. For knowledge-dynamics analysis, this score is extracted at three distinct computation points within every transformer layer—the attention sublayer output Δh_attn, the MLP sublayer output Δh_mlp, and the full layer residual h_l—and aggregated across all mentioned entities into a PRAUC score that quantifies the information intensity of a given knowledge type at that layer and component.

## Method

The core contribution is an entity-aware probing framework that overcomes the fixed-label limitation of traditional probes by introducing two reserved special tokens ([START], [END]) to mark the ad-hoc target entity and a rank-8 LoRA adapter applied exclusively to the input embedding layer, while all base LLM parameters remain frozen. A lightweight linear classification head is trained on the [END] position to predict entity relevance, with training data constructed from 6,200 HotpotQA-distractor samples where entities are labeled as necessary or irrelevant via LLM-assisted annotation. The key analytical move is applying this probe at three granular computation points (attention update, MLP update, layer output) across all layers and all attention heads, enabling the authors to decompose knowledge propagation into per-head routing profiles and per-component accumulation curves. This decomposition reveals that contextual and parametric knowledge are carried by largely disjoint sets of attention heads (contextual heads vs. memory heads), that contextual knowledge appears abruptly in early attention layers while parametric knowledge accumulates gradually through MLPs, and that conflicting knowledge persists in the residual stream without erasure while aligned multi-source signals superpositionally reinforce each other's magnitude to determine final output selection.

## Results

On the attribution task, the entity-aware Probing method achieves an average F1 of 73.0 on Qwen2.5-7B, 70.8 on Qwen3-8B, and 68.4 on LLaMA3.1-8B, substantially outperforming simple Prompting (58.8, 54.3, 39.0) and approaching or matching Prompting(CoT) (75.7, 81.6, 63.1). Critically, Probing(Random) — where the base LLM is randomly reinitialized — collapses to an average F1 of ~20 across all models, confirming that attribution performance derives from the LLM's internal knowledge rather than the probe's parametric capacity. Proprietary models (GPT-4o, Deepseek-v3) with simple Prompting achieve 89.7 and 89.1 average F1, setting the upper bound that open-source probing approaches.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper establishes three internal baselines: (1) Prompting, which directly instructs the LLM to identify relevant passages; (2) Prompting(CoT), which adds entity information and chain-of-thought reasoning; and (3) Probing(Random), which reinitializes the LLM to isolate the probe's own capacity. Probing consistently outperforms Prompting by 14–30 F1 points on open-source models (e.g., 73.0 vs. 58.8 on Qwen2.5, 68.4 vs. 39.0 on LLaMA3.1), demonstrating that hidden-state probing is a more effective elicitation method than surface-level prompting. Against Prompting(CoT), Probing is competitive but slightly lower on Qwen2.5 (73.0 vs. 75.7) and Qwen3 (70.8 vs. 81.6), while it actually surpasses CoT on LLaMA3.1 (68.4 vs. 63.1). The Probing(Random) baseline collapses to ~20 F1 across all models, confirming that the probe's success depends on the base LLM's knowledge pathways rather than the probe's parametric capacity. Open-source models with Probing (73.0, 70.8, 68.4) approach but do not match proprietary models with simple Prompting (89.7, 89.1, 79.3), suggesting a gap in intrinsic knowledge quality between open and closed models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the linear probing paradigm (Alain & Bengio, Hewitt & Manning) from static, fixed-label classification to dynamic, entity-specific knowledge tracing by combining special token markers with a rank-8 LoRA adapter, following the approach of Ye et al. (ICLR 2025) for ad-hoc target specification. The analytical framework is grounded in the residual stream decomposition from Elhage et al. (Transformer Circuits Thread), which the authors use to isolate attention-update, MLP-update, and layer-output contributions. Conceptually, the paper departs from prior knowledge-conflict studies (Longpre et al., Xie et al., Jin et al.) that characterize LLM behavior through surface-level interventions (negation injection, entity substitution) by instead providing a mechanistic, layer-by-layer account of how conflicting signals propagate and interact internally.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that contextual and parametric knowledge are routed through largely non-overlapping sets of attention heads, with competition occurring only within (not across) knowledge types, provides a concrete mechanistic target for designing RAG systems that better control knowledge reliance. The superpositional reconciliation result—that aligned multi-source signals accumulate additively in the residual stream, with magnitude directly determining output influence—offers a principled explanation for why context placement and redundancy matter in practice. Key limitations include reliance on synthetic entity-level counterfactuals (rather than natural temporal or numerical conflicts), evaluation on only three mid-scale decoder-only models, and the acknowledged risk that the LoRA-based probe may subtly alter internal dynamics. Open questions include whether these routing patterns persist in encoder-decoder architectures, in much larger models, and under more realistic multi-hop retrieval settings.
