## Problem

Existing spiking neural networks apply temporal dynamics uniformly at the neuron level (a single membrane time constant) or at coarser granularities, overlooking the biological principle of synaptic heterogeneity—where individual synapses exhibit distinct time constants. This uniformity restricts the network's ability to perform versatile timescale integration, which is essential for working memory, speech recognition, and other tasks requiring simultaneous processing across multiple temporal windows.

## Input / Output

The model consumes binary spike train sequences (Poisson-encoded audio at 4 ms bins, row-wise pixel sequences for images, or structured Poisson spike patterns with embedded noise) and produces task-specific outputs: softmax-normalized membrane potential readouts for classification, continuous traces for pattern generation, or match/mismatch decisions from two output neurons. A key structural detail is that each synapse carries its own learnable decay factor, so the membrane potential at each timestep is computed by summing individually filtered synaptic currents rather than applying a single exponential leak to the aggregate input.

## Method

HetSynLIF replaces the conventional single membrane time constant with synapse-specific time constants: each afferent spike is filtered through its own exponential decay (governed by τ_s,i), and the membrane potential is the sum of all heterogeneous synaptic currents minus a separate reset current that decays with its own time constant. This shifts temporal integration from the membrane dynamics to the synaptic dynamics, enabling the network to retain information over long delays via slow synapses while filtering noise via fast ones. The model is trained end-to-end with surrogate gradients and BPTT, and is shown theoretically to subsume vanilla LIF, ALIF (threshold adaptation), and neuron-level heterogeneous LIF as special cases through specific parameter configurations. Empirically, the learned synaptic time constants develop a mild long-tailed distribution resembling biological observations from the Allen Institute Synaptic Physiology Dataset.

## Results

HetSynLIF achieves 92.36% accuracy on the SHD speech recognition dataset, the highest reported among models with similar two-layer recurrent architectures, surpassing the next-best DH-SFNN (92.1%) and SRNN ICLR25 (91.19%). On S-MNIST, it reaches 98.93% (vs. 98.87% for DH-SRNN), on TiDigits it attains 98.99% (vs. 98.1% for BPT-SNN and M-STIPTNN), and on Ti46-Alpha it scores 96.53% (vs. 96.44% for RSNN). In the delayed match-to-sample task, HetSynLIF maintains 100% accuracy even when 80% of synaptic time constants are masked, and remains effective with as few as 5 hidden neurons.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HetSynLIF outperforms all 19 baselines across all four datasets. On SHD, it edges out the closest competitor DH-SFNN [23] by 0.26% (92.36% vs. 92.10%) and surpasses the ensemble-based SRNN [52] by 1.17%; the gap widens substantially against neuron-heterogeneity baselines (NeuHet-SRNN [22]: 82.7%) and surrogate-gradient SRNNs [57] (81.6%). On S-MNIST, HetSynLIF (98.93%) narrowly beats DH-SRNN [23] (98.87%) and SRNN [54] (98.70%), while maintaining a clear 2.5–2.9% margin over LSNN [12] and AHP-SNN [41]. On TiDigits, the advantage is most pronounced: HetSynLIF (98.99%) leads BPT-SNN [50] and M-STIPTNN [51] by ~0.9% and MPD-AL [53] by 1.5%. On Ti46-Alpha, it surpasses RSNN [59] by 0.09% and ScSr-SNNs [60] by 0.63%. In the controlled ablation (pattern generation and DMS tasks), HetSynLIF consistently outperforms its own specializations—HomNeuLIF, HomNeuALIF, and HetNeuLIF—confirming that synapse-level heterogeneity provides a distinct advantage over neuron-level or threshold-adaptation heterogeneity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on temporal heterogeneity in SNNs by shifting the locus of heterogeneity from the neuron level (membrane time constants in ALIF [Bellec 2018] or neuron-level heterogeneity [Perez-Nieves 2021]) to the synapse level, where each afferent connection carries its own decay time constant. The authors prove that HetSynLIF subsumes vanilla LIF, ALIF, and neuron-level heterogeneous LIF as special cases via simple parameter constraints, positioning it as a strict generalization rather than an alternative. The biological motivation draws directly on the Allen Institute Synaptic Physiology Dataset, which reveals log-normally distributed synaptic time constants across cortical populations—a property previously unexploited in SNN design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By grounding a practical architectural change in well-documented biological synaptic diversity, the paper offers a principled path toward multi-timescale integration in SNNs without ad hoc design choices. The learned time-constant distributions developing a mild long-tail resembling biological profiles is a nice convergence of computation and biology, though the paper itself notes this resemblance is 'loose.' Open questions include whether the 2× parameter overhead (one extra scalar per synapse) becomes prohibitive at scale, whether sparse connectivity can recover the gains at lower cost, and whether the approach transfers to unsupervised or neuromodulated settings. The robustness results (100% DMS accuracy with 80% of synapses masked) suggest the heterogeneity is distributed rather than concentrated, which is encouraging for hardware deployment.
