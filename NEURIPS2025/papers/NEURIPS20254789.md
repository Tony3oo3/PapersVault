## Problem

Existing Multi-Fidelity Bayesian Optimization (MFBO) methods treat the fidelity index as an unstructured input, ignoring the systematic convergence of lower-fidelity outputs toward the high-fidelity target. This structural blindness leads to over-exploration of costly high-fidelity regions and suboptimal optimization performance, particularly when evaluation costs grow super-linearly with fidelity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254789/cropped_page7_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes design variables x ∈ R^d, a continuous fidelity level t ∈ [0,T), noisy black-box evaluations y(x,t) at mixed fidelities, and a cost model c(t) (typically exponential, e.g., 10^t, reflecting FEM or Monte Carlo complexity). It produces the optimal design x* maximizing y(x,T), along with GP posterior mean/variance at any (x,t) and BOCA acquisition values used to select the next query. The fidelity is treated as a continuous variable rather than a discrete set, and the surrogate is updated online via 200-step Adam optimization of kernel hyperparameters.

## Method

CAMO models the multi-fidelity objective y(x,t) as the solution to a Linear Fidelity Differential Equation (LiFiDE), ∂_t y = β(x,t)·y + u(x,t), where β and u are learned coefficient functions. GP priors are placed on the initial condition y_0(x) and the forcing term u(x,t), and the variation-of-constants formula yields a closed-form non-stationary covariance kernel (the LiFiDE kernel) that structurally encodes convergence: variance increases with t while off-diagonal correlation decays as O(|t−t'|) rather than O(|t−t'|²). The paper proves pointwise and uniform convergence to the high-fidelity limit under mild conditions on β and u, and extends the analysis to non-smooth and non-convex Lyapunov functions via Clarke subdifferentials, establishing a formal link to subgradient flows. For optimization, CAMO is paired with the BOCA acquisition function, which filters fidelities by cost and information content, achieving a training complexity reduction from O((ΣN_f)³) to O(ΣN_f³) compared to standard multi-fidelity GP approaches.

## Results

CAMO is evaluated using simple regret (γ) as the primary metric, measured against cumulative query cost across 11 benchmarks (6 synthetic continuous-fidelity, 3 synthetic discrete-fidelity, and 2 real-world FEM problems). The abstract reports up to a four-fold improvement in optimization performance over state-of-the-art MFBO methods, with the advantage growing as the fidelity cost increases from logarithmic (log₂(2+t)) to linear (5t) to exponential (10^t). CAMO consistently achieves the lowest regret across nearly all tasks, with the sole exception being the non-linear sin function at low cost. On the real-world Mechanical Plate Vibration and Thermal Conductor Design problems (with FEM cubic cost scaling), CAMO and BOCA significantly outperform all other methods, with CAMO showing faster early-stage convergence.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CAMO outperforms all 10 baselines on the majority of benchmarks. Against BOCA [3], CAMO achieves lower regret on most continuous-fidelity tasks (Park, Currin, Forrester, Bohachevsky) and both real-world FEM problems, though BOCA remains competitive on Branin and matches CAMO on the Thermal Conductor task; the gap widens substantially under exponential cost (10^t) where CAMO's convergence-aware kernel exploits low-fidelity information more effectively. FABOLAS [16] is dominated by CAMO in both accuracy and wall-clock time, being described as 'impractically slow' for query optimization. SMAC3 [27] has the shortest per-query simulation time but is not competitive in regret or convergence rate. On discrete-fidelity tasks (Branin, Currin with 10 fidelities), CAMO outperforms AR [28], ResGP [29], standard GP [6], MF-UCB [30], MF-EI [10], and cfKG [17] by a wide margin on Branin and by a clear but smaller margin on Currin. DNN-MFBO [18] 'essentially failed' on the discrete tasks, likely due to overfitting from sparse high-fidelity observations. The paper notes that methods using cfKG acquisition (including CAMO with cfKG) are generally less competitive than those using BOCA, suggesting the acquisition function choice matters alongside the surrogate model.

## Relation to Prior Work

CAMO extends the Fidelity Differential Equation (FiDE) framework introduced by Xing et al. (Continuar, NeurIPS 2024) and Li et al. (NeurIPS 2022), which model multi-fidelity outputs as solutions to ODEs in the fidelity index, by restricting to a linear-in-y form that admits a closed-form non-stationary GP kernel and tractable training. It departs from the broader MFBO literature (BOCA, FABOLAS, cfKG, DNN-MFBO) by explicitly encoding convergence structure into the surrogate rather than treating fidelity as an unstructured input dimension, and by pairing this with BOCA's continuous-fidelity acquisition strategy. The theoretical contribution—Lyapunov-based convergence proofs for general FiDEs and their connection to subgradient flows via Clarke subdifferentials and Moreau envelopes—has no direct precedent in the MFBO literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first rigorous convergence guarantees (pointwise and uniform) for a GP-based multi-fidelity surrogate, bridging MFBO with non-smooth optimization theory in a way that could inform future surrogate design. Practically, the LiFiDE kernel reduces training complexity from O((ΣN_f)³) to O(ΣN_f³) and consistently outperforms baselines by up to four-fold, with the advantage growing as the fidelity cost becomes more super-linear (e.g., FEM's cubic scaling). Key limitations include the assumption of monotonic convergence (non-monotonic fidelity transitions are not captured), the need for sufficient low-fidelity data, and the ~20% kernel optimization overhead. Open questions include extending the framework to stochastic FiDEs, non-monotonic convergence patterns, and adapting the convergence model to HPO/NAS settings where fidelity is algorithmic rather than physical.
