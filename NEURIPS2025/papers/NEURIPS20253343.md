## Problem

Existing approaches to converting natural language optimization problems into solvable models either depend on carefully tuned multi-agent prompt workflows that generalize poorly to unfamiliar problem types, or require expensive supervised fine-tuning on curated problem-solution pairs. This paper addresses the gap by asking whether test-time scaling—allocating additional inference computation without any training—can match or exceed both prompt-based and learning-based methods across diverse optimization domains.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253343/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a free-form natural language description of an optimization problem as input and produces a structured six-element mathematical formulation (Type, Sets, Parameters, Variables, Objective, Constraints), which is then translated into executable Pyomo code and run through a numerical solver (e.g., Gurobi) to yield a feasible or optimal solution. The formulation is built incrementally as a tree of partial models, and the final output includes both the symbolic model and the computed solution values. Evaluation uses Solving Accuracy (correct solution), Execution Rate (code runs without error), and Average Generation Time.

## Method

SolverLLM treats optimization formulation as a structured search problem solved via a modified Monte Carlo Tree Search guided by a frozen LLM (GPT-4o). Three key modifications distinguish it from standard MCTS: (1) dynamic expansion permits node expansion at non-leaf levels, allowing the LLM to revisit and refine earlier formulation elements (e.g., adding constraints after variables are defined); (2) prompt backpropagation propagates layer-specific reasoning signals (triggers, explanations, and corrective prompt guidance) backward through the tree, accumulating a per-layer knowledge base that steers future expansions, gated by a local predictive-entropy threshold; and (3) uncertainty backpropagation estimates global semantic uncertainty of LLM-judged rewards via Monte Carlo sampling and down-weights uncertain evaluations during value updates (ρ = exp(−U_global)), preventing noisy scores from destabilizing tree statistics. The six-element schema, particularly the Type element, provides global structural guidance (e.g., identifying a TSP to trigger MTZ subtour-elimination constraints), while UCT with exploration constant c=2 balances exploitation and exploration across the formulation space.

## Results

SolverLLM achieves 97.0% Solving Accuracy (SA) on NL4Opt, 96.0% on MamoEasy, 76.0% on MamoComplex, and 56.0% on IndustryOR, consistently outperforming all baselines. On the two hardest datasets (MamoComplex and IndustryOR), it surpasses the state-of-the-art learning-based method LLMOPT by 8 and 10 percentage points respectively, while matching or exceeding it on simpler benchmarks. Against prompt-based methods, SolverLLM improves SA by over 10% across all three evaluated datasets (NL4Opt, NLP4LP, ComplexOR). Execution Rate remains near 100% across all datasets, and average generation time stays under 4 minutes per problem.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against direct LLM prompting (GPT-4 and GPT-4o), SolverLLM wins by large margins on all datasets, with the gap widening on complex problems (e.g., 76.0% vs. 34.0% on MamoComplex with GPT-4o). Among prompt-based multi-agent methods, SolverLLM outperforms OptiMUS (the strongest prompt-based baseline) by 18.2–15.0 pp on NL4Opt and NLP4LP, and by 11.1 pp on ComplexOR; Reflexion and Chain-of-Experts trail even further. Against learning-based methods, SolverLLM surpasses all three ORLM variants (Mistral, Deepseek, LLaMa3) by 13–44 pp depending on dataset, and exceeds LLMOPT by 8 pp on MamoComplex and 10 pp on IndustryOR while matching it on MamoEasy (96.0% vs. 97.0%) and exceeding it on NL4Opt (97.0% vs. 93.0%). Compared to AutoFormulation (the closest test-time scaling competitor using MCTS with a four-element schema), SolverLLM achieves substantially higher SA (76.0% vs. 38.0% at 50 iterations on MamoComplex) while consuming fewer tokens at every budget level.

## Relation to Prior Work

SolverLLM extends the MCTS-based formulation approach of AutoFormulation [3] (which uses a four-element schema) by introducing a six-element decomposition (adding a Type element for global problem classification) and enabling dynamic expansion at non-leaf nodes, allowing the search to revisit and refine earlier formulation decisions. It also draws on LLMOPT [12]'s five-element abstraction and error-backpropagation mechanism for code generation, but departs fundamentally from both prompt-based multi-agent pipelines (Chain-of-Experts, OptiMUS) and fine-tuning-based approaches (ORLM, LLMOPT) by framing formulation as a structured search problem guided by LLM-generated reasoning signals and uncertainty estimates rather than fixed workflows or supervised training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a training-free, test-time-scaling framework can match or exceed fine-tuned optimization-specific LLMs (e.g., 76% vs. 68% on MamoComplex, 56% vs. 46% on IndustryOR) challenges the assumption that domain-specific fine-tuning is necessary for strong optimization formulation. Key limitations include inference latency from MCTS rollouts, reliance on subjective LLM-based reward scoring, and evaluation restricted to well-structured problems; robustness to highly ambiguous or adversarial natural language descriptions remains untested. The prompt-backpropagation and uncertainty-weighted update mechanisms are potentially transferable to other structured generation tasks beyond optimization.
