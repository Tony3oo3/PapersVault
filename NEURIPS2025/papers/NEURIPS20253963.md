## Problem

Standard LoRA applied to Medical Lay Language Generation (MLLG) on multi-source heterogeneous datasets suffers from two critical failures: low-rank projection causes detrimental semantic subspace shifts that degrade content fidelity, and a single shared LoRA cannot simultaneously model diverse lay-style generation patterns arising from different annotation conventions across datasets. The paper demonstrates empirically that the interference from data heterogeneity outweighs the benefits of joint training, and that naive LoRA lacks any mechanism to preserve semantic invariance during the summarization-to-simplification transformation.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single expert medical text (biomedical article or abstract) as the primary input, along with an external lay-style recommendation signal that determines which of N isolated B matrices to activate via a hard switch. The output is a lay-language summary tailored to the recommended style. Unlike router-based multi-task approaches that blend multiple branches, Magical activates exactly one B matrix per inference, and the recommendation signal is provided by an external agent (manually specified in experiments) rather than learned by the LLM itself, since the paper shows the LLM cannot reliably self-route among heterogeneous lay-styles from expert text alone.

## Method

Magical employs an asymmetric LoRA architecture with a shared matrix A (for abstractive summarization) and N isolated matrices B (one per dataset's lay-style), formulated as y = W₀x + Σᵢ αᵢ·BᵢAx with hard-switch α. To enforce semantic fidelity on A, it first identifies semantically relevant Transformer layers via probing classifiers on expert-lay pairs, then applies Semantic Contrastive Learning (InfoNCE-style loss with cached lay-text representations as positive targets) only on those layers to project inputs into a semantically stable low-rank subspace. For B, it uses Switch-Controlled Selection driven by an external Recommendation-guided Switch rather than Router-Controlled soft blending, motivated by the finding that LLMs cannot autonomously distinguish subtle lay-style differences from expert text. This decoupling—semantic invariance on A, stylistic diversity on B, hard switching between B branches—reduces trainable parameters by ~31.66% while outperforming vanilla LoRA, DoRA, PiSSA, and rsLoRA across three datasets and three backbone LLMs.

## Results

On LLaMA3.1-8B-Instruct, Magical achieves R-1 of 45.71/50.44/48.77 and BLEU of 16.68/8.67/11.86 on Cochrane/eLife/Plos_genetics respectively, outperforming all LoRA variants. Across all three backbone LLMs and three datasets, Magical yields an average relative improvement of 4.80% in R-1, 6.89% in R-2, 4.51% in R-L, and 15.99% in BLEU over the second-best method, while reducing trainable parameters by 31.66% (e.g., 42M vs. 62M for LLaMA3.1-8B). Magical also outperforms Full-Parameter Fine-Tuning (FFT) on all datasets despite using over 150× fewer trainable parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Magical consistently outperforms vanilla LoRA across all metrics and all three datasets, with the largest gains on BLEU (e.g., 16.68 vs. 12.06 on Cochrane, +38.5%). Against rsLoRA, the strongest baseline, Magical wins on R-1, R-2, R-L, and BLEU for Cochrane and Plos_genetics, but rsLoRA narrowly edges out on eLife BLEU (8.71 vs. 8.67). DoRA and PiSSA are clearly outperformed by Magical on every metric across all datasets, with DoRA showing the largest gap on eLife R-1 (50.44 vs. 48.47). The Prompt baseline is the weakest performer overall, particularly on BLEU (e.g., 1.85 on eLife vs. 8.67 for Magical), confirming that simple in-context instructions cannot capture the target lay-style. Notably, Magical achieves these gains while using only 42M trainable parameters versus 62–64M for the LoRA variants, a 31.66% reduction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the asymmetric LoRA architecture introduced by HydraLoRA (shared A, multiple B matrices) by adding two task-specific mechanisms: a Semantic Invariance Constraint (via contrastive learning on semantically-relevant layers identified by probing) applied to the shared matrix A, and a Switch-Controlled Selection mechanism with an external Recommendation Agent for the isolated B matrices. It departs from both standard LoRA and HydraLoRA's Router-Controlled Selection by arguing that in heterogeneous MLLG settings, hard switching guided by an external recommender outperforms soft routing, because the LLM cannot reliably distinguish lay-styles from expert input alone. The empirical motivation—showing that multiple smaller LoRAs outperform a single larger one under data heterogeneity, and that LoRA induces measurable semantic subspace shift—positions the work as a diagnostic-driven redesign rather than a purely architectural novelty.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that data heterogeneity actively degrades LoRA performance (rather than merely diluting signal) and that the router mechanism fails near-randomly for MLLG has implications beyond medical text simplification, potentially informing PEFT design for any multi-source, multi-style adaptation task. The decoupling of abstractive summarization (matrix A) from lay-style generation (matrix B) offers a reusable architectural principle for tasks where content preservation and stylistic transformation are separable concerns. A significant open question is the Recommendation Agent itself, which is not implemented (100% manual accuracy is assumed); building a cold-start lay-style recommender that works without user profiles remains unsolved. The paper also notes that generalization to truly unseen lay-styles is still limited, with matrix A being the most transferable component.
