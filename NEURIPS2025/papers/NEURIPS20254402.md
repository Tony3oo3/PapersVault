## Problem

Existing differentially private PCA methods in the stochastic setting either require sample size n to scale super-linearly with dimension d, or inject privacy noise at a fixed scale that ignores the inherent randomness in the data, yielding suboptimal error rates of O(√(dk/n) + d^{3/2}k/(εn)). Liu et al. [2022a] resolved both issues but only for the top eigenvector (k=1). This paper provides the first algorithm that simultaneously achieves near-linear sample complexity, adaptive noise scaling, and generalization to arbitrary k ≤ d.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes a stream of n i.i.d. matrices A_i ∈ R^{d×d} (each sampled from a distribution with common PSD expectation Σ) along with target rank k and privacy budget (ε, δ). It outputs U ∈ R^{d×k} with orthonormal columns that is ζ-approximate, meaning ⟨UUᵀ, Σ⟩ ≥ (1−ζ²)⟨V_k V_kᵀ, Σ⟩, where V_k contains the true top-k eigenvectors. Unlike deterministic PCA settings, no norm bound is imposed on individual A_i; instead, privacy is guaranteed via the add/remove model over the multiset of matrices, and the utility guarantee requires only a mild sub-Gaussian-type concentration assumption (Assumption A.4) on the bilinear form uᵀP(A_i−Σ)Pv.

## Method

k-DP-PCA follows a deflation strategy: in each of k rounds it invokes MODIFIEDDP-PCA to estimate the current top eigenvector, then projects that direction out of the data before the next round. The key innovation in MODIFIEDDP-PCA is a two-stage batch update based on Oja's algorithm: it first privately estimates the range of {PA_iPω_{t−1}} over a batch (PRIVRANGE), then uses that empirical range to calibrate the Gaussian noise scale for privately computing the batch mean (PRIVMEAN), so the injected noise shrinks when the data concentrates tightly. The theoretical framework introduces a stochastic ePCA oracle (Definition 5) that generalizes Jambulapati et al.'s deflation analysis to the stochastic setting, and a novel utility analysis of non-private Oja's algorithm under random, data-dependent projection matrices. A simpler alternative, k-DP-Ojas, replaces the two-stage update with a single clipped-and-noised gradient step, trading a slightly weaker guarantee (a (γ+1) factor instead of γ in the privacy term) for fewer hyperparameters and greater robustness to learning-rate choices.

## Results

The main theoretical guarantee (Theorem 1) shows k-DP-PCA achieves utility ζ = Õ(κ'(√(Vk/n) + γdk√(log(1/δ))/(εn))) with sample complexity n = Õ(d), i.e., near-linear in dimension. For the spiked covariance model (Corollary 2), this simplifies to ζ = Õ(σ·κ'(√(dk/n) + dk√(log(1/δ))/(εn))), meaning the error shrinks as the data noise σ decreases. The lower bound (Corollary 3) matches the upper bound up to a factor of k, confirming near-optimality. Empirically, with k=2, d=200, λ₁=10, ε=1, δ=0.01, k-DP-PCA consistently outperforms all baselines across noise levels σ ∈ {0.001, 0.025} and growing dimensions, with the gap widening in the low-noise regime (σ ≪ λ₁).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares k-DP-PCA (and its simpler variant k-DP-Ojas) against three adapted baselines: DP-Gauss-1 (input perturbation from Dwork et al. 2014), DP-Gauss-2 (output perturbation from Dwork et al. 2014), and DP-Power-Method (from Hardt and Price 2014). k-DP-PCA consistently outperforms all three baselines in utility (measured as relative variance captured) across all tested regimes. The advantage is most pronounced in the low-noise regime (σ=0.001 vs. λ₁=10), where k-DP-PCA's adaptive noise calibration provides a clear edge over the fixed-threshold clipping used by the baselines. As ambient dimension d grows at fixed n, the DP-Gauss methods and Power-Method degrade faster (their utility scales as O(d^{3/2}/n)) while k-DP-PCA maintains only linear d-dependence. DP-Gauss-2, which is designed for large eigengaps, steadily improves as the gap grows and nearly matches k-DP-PCA for very large gaps, but DP-Gauss-1 remains nearly flat across eigengap values. The simpler k-DP-Ojas algorithm matches or even exceeds k-DP-PCA at higher noise levels (σ=0.025) due to fewer hyperparameters and greater robustness to learning-rate choices, though it loses in the low-noise regime because its privacy noise does not vanish as σ→0.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DP-PCA algorithm of Liu et al. (2022a), which achieved near-optimal rates for the top eigenvector (k=1) in the stochastic setting, to general k via a deflation framework inspired by Jambulapati et al. (2024). The key technical departure is a novel stochastic ePCA oracle definition that accommodates data-dependent projection matrices, enabling the deflation analysis to carry through in the stochastic regime where prior non-stochastic deflation results do not apply. The algorithm's adaptive noise calibration (via PRIVRANGE/PRIVMEAN from Liu et al.) distinguishes it from earlier DP-PCA methods (Dwork et al. 2014, Hardt & Price 2014) that use fixed clipping thresholds and inject noise independent of the data's inherent stochasticity, yielding suboptimal d^{3/2}k/(εn) privacy error.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first DP algorithm for stochastic k-PCA that simultaneously achieves near-linear sample complexity in d, adapts noise to data randomness, and handles arbitrary k, filling a gap between the k=1 result of Liu et al. and the distributional-privacy-only result of Cai et al. (2024). The matching lower bound (up to a factor of k) characterizes the fundamental cost of privacy in this setting. Open questions include closing the k-factor gap (potentially via sample reuse across deflation steps), removing the exponential spectral-gap dependence in the sample complexity condition, and adapting the subroutines to a true streaming setting. The ePCA oracle framework and the novel stochastic analysis of Oja's algorithm are contributions of independent interest beyond the specific DP-PCA application.
