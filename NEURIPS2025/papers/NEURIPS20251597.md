## Problem

Aligning 3D-native diffusion models with human preferences via reward finetuning is hindered by the scarcity of high-quality 3D reward models, since collecting diverse 3D data remains a longstanding challenge. Existing lifting-from-2D approaches (e.g., SDS) are unstable, require per-object optimization, and produce artifacts like the Janus problem, while vanilla reward finetuning baselines (DDPO, ReFL, DRaFT) either fail to converge or suffer from severe reward hacking and prior forgetting. The paper asks whether 2D reward models can be used to efficiently and robustly finetune 3D-native diffusion models in an amortized fashion.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained 3D-native diffusion model (DiffSplat-PixArt-Σ, DiffSplat-SD1.5, or GaussianCube), a text prompt, and a differentiable 2D reward model (Aesthetic Score, HPSv2, a normal-map estimator, or a depth-normal consistency score). The 2D reward is evaluated on multi-view renderings of the 3D shape—specifically four orthogonal views (front, left, back, right) with random elevation ±20° and azimuthal perturbations ±60°—and the 3D reward is defined as the expectation over these views. The output is a LoRA-finetuned 3D diffusion model (rank 16 or 8) that generates 3D Gaussian splat assets in ~8 seconds per sample, compared to ~5 minutes for SDS-based inference.

## Method

Nabla-R2D3 adapts the Nabla-GFlowNet score-matching loss to the 3D setting by replacing the 3D reward gradient with the expectation of 2D reward gradients over rendered camera views. The core loss (Eq. 9) matches the gradient of the log-density ratio between the finetuned and base diffusion models to the gradient of the 2D reward evaluated on multi-view renderings of the one-step denoised prediction, with a stop-gradient on the reward term and a decay factor γ_t. The paper proves this stochastic approximation is unbiased (Appendix B) and shows that a simplified forward-only loss (dropping the correction term g_φ and the reverse loss) suffices in practice. Training uses LoRA parameterization, gradient regularization against the previous step's parameters, and sub-sampling of 40% of transitions per trajectory, with reward temperature β tuned per reward type (2e6 for HPSv2, 1e7 for Aesthetic Score, 1e6 for geometry). The framework is reward-agnostic, supporting both appearance-based rewards (Aesthetic Score, HPSv2) and geometry-based rewards (normal-map consistency, depth-normal consistency) without architectural changes.

## Results

On the Aesthetic Score reward, Nabla-R2D3 achieves the highest reward (6.44) while maintaining the best FID (217.89) and CLIP-Similarity (25.86×10⁻²) among all methods that meaningfully improve reward, compared to ReFL (5.82 reward, 352.97 FID, 21.89 CLIP-Sim) and DRaFT (5.51 reward, 337.77 FID, 22.89 CLIP-Sim). On HPSv2, it reaches 27.85×10⁻² reward with 131.38 FID and 35.35×10⁻² CLIP-Sim, outperforming DRaFT (32.65 reward but 224.64 FID) and ReFL (24.92 reward, 274.44 FID). On the Normal Estimator geometry reward, it achieves 92.03×10⁻² reward with 104.45 FID, the best reward–FID trade-off among all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Nabla-R2D3 consistently outperforms all three RL-based baselines (DDPO, ReFL, DRaFT) on reward across all three reward models. Against ReFL and DRaFT, it achieves higher reward while substantially reducing FID (e.g., 217.89 vs. 352.97 for ReFL and 337.77 for DRaFT on Aesthetic Score) and preserving better text-object alignment (CLIP-Sim 25.86 vs. 21.89 and 22.89). DDPO preserves FID and CLIP-Sim well but fails to meaningfully improve reward (4.54 vs. 4.72 base), making it effectively non-functional for alignment. On HPSv2, DRaFT achieves the highest raw reward (32.65) but at the cost of 224.64 FID and degraded CLIP-Sim (33.99), whereas Nabla-R2D3 achieves 27.85 reward with 131.38 FID and the best CLIP-Sim (35.35). Qualitatively, Nabla-R2D3 also avoids the Janus problem (inconsistent multi-view geometry) that plagues DRaFT-finetuned models, and produces more visually coherent shapes than the 2D-SDS-based DreamReward method.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Nabla-GFlowNet (Liu et al., ICLR 2025), a gradient-informed RL finetuning framework for 2D diffusion models, to the 3D-native setting by replacing the 2D reward with a multi-view-averaged 2D reward computed via differentiable rendering. It departs from the dominant 'lifting-from-2D' paradigm (DreamFusion, DreamReward) that optimizes each 3D object individually with score distillation sampling, instead performing amortized finetuning of a 3D-native diffusion model. It also contrasts with other RL-based diffusion alignment methods (DDPO, ReFL, DRaFT) that the paper shows to be unstable or prone to reward hacking when naively extended to 3D, and with multi-view diffusion alignment approaches (MVReward, Carve3D) that operate on representations lacking guaranteed 3D consistency.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method to robustly align 3D-native diffusion models with human preferences using only 2D reward signals, avoiding the per-object optimization cost and Janus artifacts of SDS-based lifting. The practical impact is significant: practitioners can plug in existing 2D reward models (aesthetic scores, HPSv2, normal/depth estimators) to improve 3D generation quality without collecting 3D preference data. Key limitations include the absence of supervision for interior geometry, expensive gradient computation during the forward pass, and the restriction to parameter-level alignment without prompt-based strategies. Open questions include whether better 3D-native reward models could further improve alignment, and whether the approach extends to more complex 3D representations beyond Gaussian splatting.
