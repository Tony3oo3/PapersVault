## Problem

Current RL post-training for LLM reasoning relies on fixed verifiers—either rule-based metrics or frozen/SFT-trained reward models—that are vulnerable to reward hacking and generalize poorly beyond their training distributions. While PRIME attempts joint generator-verifier training, its verifier remains a discriminative, logit-based model trained via SFT, limiting its reasoning capability and robustness. No prior method trains a generative, process-level verifier via RL that co-evolves with the generator.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253981/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The generator receives a math question as a text prompt and produces a structured multi-step solution enclosed in <step> tags within a <think> block, followed by a final answer in <answer>\boxed{}</answer> format. The verifier receives the question concatenated with the generator's full solution and outputs a step-by-step verification: for each of the K generator steps, it produces a natural-language analysis concluding with \boxed{CORRECT} or \boxed{INCORRECT}, followed by an overall final judgment. Notably, the verifier is trained exclusively on whether its final judgment matches the ground-truth answer correctness—no step-level annotations are required.

## Method

TANGO interleaves RL training of a generator (Qwen2.5-Math-7B) and a generative verifier (Qwen2.5-7B), updating the generator for N_g=3 steps before one verifier update. The generator's advantage blends a rule-based outcome reward with step-level rewards extracted from the verifier's sampled judgments, using an exponentially decaying mixing weight α that shifts emphasis from step-level exploration early in training to outcome-level stability later. The verifier is trained via RL (GRPO) using only an outcome-level reward measuring whether its final correctness judgment matches the generator's true answer, augmented with a format score; a class-aware reweighting scheme (scaling advantages by the inverse square root of class frequency) prevents collapse to always-predicting-incorrect during early training. Crucially, the verifier's generative, sampling-based nature introduces stochasticity into reward signals, mitigating reward hacking, while its RL training (rather than SFT) enables progressive refinement of chain-of-thought verification reasoning without any process-level supervision.

## Results

TANGO-Qwen-7B achieves state-of-the-art performance among 7B/8B-scale models, averaging 49.5% on five competition-level math benchmarks (MATH-500: 82.4, AIME 2024: 26.7, AIME 2025: 23.3, AMC 2023: 70.0, OlympiadBench: 45.3) and 62.8% on four out-of-domain reasoning tasks. Compared to vanilla GRPO, TANGO delivers a 24.6% average relative improvement on math and 6.1% on out-of-domain tasks, with a 100% relative gain on AIME 2025 (20.0 vs. 10.0). The TANGO verifier also sets a new SOTA on ProcessBench (43.9 avg F1) among 7B/8B models without using any process-level annotations, outperforming the much larger Qwen2.5-Math-72B-Instruct on the hardest subsets (OlympiadBench: 37.8 vs. 32.5, Omni-MATH: 36.3 vs. 31.7).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against vanilla RL baselines (GRPO, RLOO, REINFORCE++), TANGO consistently wins across all nine benchmarks, with the largest gains on the hardest problems: AIME 2025 doubles from 10.0 to 20.0 under GRPO, and AIME 2024 improves by 50.4% relative. Against PRIME (the only prior joint generator-verifier method), TANGO wins on every math benchmark, most notably AIME 2025 (23.3 vs. 13.3) and AMC 2023 (70.0 vs. 60.0), while matching on AIME 2024 (26.7). Against the base Qwen2.5-Math-7B-Instruct, TANGO improves math average from 42.9 to 49.5 and out-of-domain average from 50.2 to 62.8, with the largest single-benchmark gain on AIME 2025 (23.3 vs. 10.0). The TANGO verifier outperforms all 7B/8B PRM baselines on ProcessBench (43.9 avg F1 vs. Skywork-PRM-7B's 42.1) and surpasses the 72B Qwen2.5-Math-72B-Instruct on OlympiadBench (37.8 vs. 32.5) and Omni-MATH (36.3 vs. 31.7). TANGO remains below the much larger QwQ-32B-Preview on all math benchmarks (e.g., AIME 2024: 26.7 vs. 50.0), confirming the size gap but establishing SOTA at the 7B/8B scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

TANGO extends the joint generator-verifier training paradigm introduced by PRIME (Cui et al., 2025), which was the only prior method to co-train both components, by replacing PRIME's SFT-trained discriminative logit-based PRM with an RL-trained generative, process-level verifier. The RL optimization framework builds directly on GRPO (Shao et al., 2024), modifying its group-normalized advantage computation to blend outcome-level and step-level verifier rewards (Eq. 7), while also introducing a class-aware reweighting scheme for the verifier to handle early-training label imbalance. The key conceptual departure from all prior reward-modeling work (ORMs, discriminative PRMs, SFT-trained generative PRMs) is that the verifier itself is optimized via policy gradient with outcome-only supervision, allowing it to develop step-level verification reasoning through RL rather than imitation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TANGO establishes that a generative verifier trained purely with outcome-level RL rewards can develop accurate step-level verification without any process annotations, challenging the assumption that fine-grained credit assignment requires fine-grained supervision. The 3.3× training efficiency gain and the doubling of AIME 2025 accuracy over vanilla GRPO make this practically significant for scaling LLM reasoning post-training. Open questions include whether the co-evolution paradigm generalizes beyond math to open-ended reasoning (long-context, multi-hop, dialogue), whether it scales to larger model families, and whether the interleaved schedule (Ng=3, Nv=1) is optimal or task-dependent. The paper acknowledges these limitations and reserves broader-domain evaluation for future work.
