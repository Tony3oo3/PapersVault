## Problem

FedAvg-style federated learning algorithms suffer from two persistent biases—client drift from multiple local updates on non-i.i.d. data and objective distortion from non-uniform client participation—that prevent exact convergence to the true global optimum unless the learning rate is decayed to zero. The paper asks whether exact convergence with a linear rate is achievable under both arbitrary client participation and multiple local updates while keeping the learning rate constant, a question that prior methods (SCAFFOLD, ProxSkip, MIFA, FedAU) have not resolved.

## Input / Output

Each client holds a local non-i.i.d. dataset and a model copy; the server holds no data (its local loss is identically zero) and coordinates communication. At each round, an arbitrary subset of clients participates—determined by unknown, potentially non-uniform probabilities—pulls the server model, performs τ local gradient steps, and pushes back a gradient-tracking vector. The output is the server's model parameter vector x ∈ ℝ^d, which converges exactly to the minimizer of the global sum-of-losses F(x) = (1/N)Σf_i(x) at a linear rate O(log(1/ε)) iterations for both strongly convex and PL-condition non-convex objectives, without requiring knowledge of or adaptation to the participation distribution.

## Method

FOCUS reformulates the FL problem as a constrained optimization task (minimize Σf_i(x_i) subject to consensus constraints R(S_r)x = x) and solves it via a push-pull primal-dual strategy adapted to time-varying graphs. The key modeling insight is representing client participation, local updates, and model aggregation as multiplications by row-stochastic (pull) and column-stochastic (push) matrices, where the time-varying structure encodes which clients are active in each round. Concretely, the server pulls the model x to participating clients (row-stochastic R_k), clients perform local updates using a gradient-tracking variable y that accumulates successive gradient differences (y_{t+1} = y_t + ∇f_i(x_{t+1}) − ∇f_i(x_t)), and the server aggregates the pushed y values via a column-stochastic matrix C_k without averaging. This push-pull structure guarantees two invariants: consensus (all models converge to a common value) and gradient tracking (the sum of y equals the global gradient), which together eliminate both client drift and participation bias. The paper further shows that FOCUS is equivalent to a delayed/asynchronous gradient descent where arbitrary participation translates into arbitrary gradient staleness, explaining why exact convergence holds for any participation scheme with non-zero per-client probability.

## Results

FOCUS achieves exact linear convergence with O(log(1/ε)) iteration complexity for both strongly convex and PL-condition non-convex objectives under arbitrary client participation, without requiring bounded heterogeneity assumptions or decaying learning rates. For general non-convex settings, FOCUS improves the typical O(1/√R) gradient-norm rate to O(1/R). Numerical experiments on ridge regression (d=100, N=16, τ=5) confirm linear convergence across full, uniform, and arbitrary participation scenarios, with FOCUS outperforming all baselines most dramatically under arbitrary participation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FOCUS dominates all seven baselines on the key axis of exact convergence under arbitrary client participation: FedAvg, LocalSGD, FedAU, FedAWE, and MIFA all fail to converge exactly (requiring decaying learning rates or bounded heterogeneity), while FOCUS converges to the true optimum with a constant learning rate. In terms of convergence rate, FOCUS matches SCAFFOLD's O(log(1/ε)) strongly-convex rate but extends it to arbitrary participation (SCAFFOLD is only proven under uniform sampling) and avoids the 2d-vector-per-round communication overhead. For non-convex objectives, FOCUS achieves O(log(1/ε)) under the PL condition, substantially improving over FedAvg's O(1/ε²), MIFA's O(1/ε²), and SCAFFOLD's O(1/ε). ProxSkip/ScaffNew, while also achieving O(log(1/ε)) for strongly convex problems, is restricted to full client participation and diverges under uniform or arbitrary sampling in the numerical experiments. In the ridge regression experiments (Figure 4), FOCUS exhibits the fastest linear convergence in all three participation scenarios, with the largest gap over baselines appearing under arbitrary participation where FedAvg and FedAU fail to reach the optimum and ProxSkip diverges entirely.

## Relation to Prior Work

FOCUS is derived from the push-pull gradient tracking algorithm (Xin & Khan 2018; Pu et al. 2020) originally designed for directed graphs, extended here to time-varying graphs (Nedic et al. 2017; Ying et al. 2021) to model FL's client participation and local update dynamics. The key conceptual departure from prior FL work (FedAvg, SCAFFOLD, ProxSkip, MIFA, FedAU) is the use of row- and column-stochastic matrices to encode the communication structure, which allows the algorithm to be designed from a constrained-optimization principle rather than heuristics. Unlike SCAFFOLD/ProxSkip which add a control variate to counteract client drift, or FedAU/FedAWE which require learning participation probabilities, FOCUS eliminates both biases simultaneously through its push-pull structure without any additional communication overhead or probability estimation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first result establishing exact linear convergence for FL under arbitrary client participation without decaying the learning rate or assuming bounded data heterogeneity, resolving a long-standing open question. The stochastic-matrix framework provides a principled bridge between FL and decentralized optimization, opening the door to porting other exact decentralized algorithms (DIGing, Extra, exact-diffusion) to the FL setting. Key limitations include the restriction to independent (non-Markov) participation processes and the fact that the main empirical validation relies on synthetic ridge regression, with CIFAR-10 results deferred to the appendix. The absence of linear speedup in the deterministic setting (due to the O(1/N) learning rate constraint) is a notable theoretical caveat, though SG-FOCUS recovers it.
