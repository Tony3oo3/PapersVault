## Problem

Recent work showed that LLM safety alignment is fragile when confined to only the initial output tokens, but the question of how deep alignment should extend remained open. This paper formalizes 'safety depth'—a designated output position where the model refuses harmful content—and provides the first theoretical characterization of the optimal safety depth under iterative fine-tuning, using the equivalence between autoregressive LLMs and Markov chains.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253909/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Training inputs consist of harmful instructions paired with refusal responses inserted at specific token positions; cyclic augmentation rotates these refusal positions across the sequence using a permutation group, so each position receives consistent supervision. At inference, the model receives a text prompt (potentially adversarial) and produces a text response that either refuses or generates content. The ensemble variant additionally takes outputs from W independently trained models and aggregates them via union, average, or majority voting, discarding any response below a safety threshold.

## Method

The paper models fine-tuning as iterative updates to a Markov chain transition matrix Q_t = Q_0 + αγ^t B, where B encodes a bias toward refusal. Theorem 4.6 derives explicit conditions on learning rate α and training steps T under which refusal states become δ-absorbing (self-transition probability ≥ 1−δ, outgoing probability ≤ δ). Cyclic group augmentation applies a permutation matrix P to rotate the bias matrix as B^(t) = P^t B P^(−t), ensuring refusal supervision at all positions and accelerating convergence to the order of P. Proposition 4.11 further shows that an ensemble of width W can achieve overall safety ε by requiring each model to meet only a fractional per-chain risk bound (e.g., ε/W under union), distributing the training burden without per-model retraining.

## Results

On Gemma-2-2B evaluated with HEx-PHI (330 harmful instructions across 11 categories), cyclic augmentation achieves a mean safety score of 0.61±0.06, substantially outperforming not-aligned (0.35±0.05), shallow alignment (0.42±0.06), and deep alignment (0.46±0.05). The largest gains appear in Illegal Activity (0.29→0.69) and Tailored Financial Advice (0.41→0.71). In a multi-turn refusal depth test (100 prompts × 5 turns), cyclic augmentation doubles the mean refusal depth from 2.3 to 4.6 and raises the sustained refusal rate from 40% to 90%. A mild helpfulness trade-off is observed (e.g., GSM8K accuracy drops from 0.22 to 0.20, SAMSum ROUGE-1 from 0.2834 to 0.2750).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is the 'deep' alignment method from Qi et al. (ICLR 2025), which randomly inserts refusal responses at a single position in the output. Cyclic augmentation (the proposed method) outperforms deep alignment on all 11 HEx-PHI safety categories for Gemma-2-2B, with a mean improvement of +0.15 (0.61 vs. 0.46). The gap is most pronounced in categories where refusal positioning matters most: Illegal Activity (+0.22), Political Campaigning (+0.21), and Tailored Financial Advice (+0.16). In the multi-turn refusal depth experiment on Qwen2.5-1.5B, cyclic augmentation nearly doubles the mean refusal depth (4.6 vs. 2.3) and triples the sustained 5-turn refusal rate (90% vs. 40%), confirming that distributing refusals across positions via the cyclic group is more robust than the single-position deep alignment. The paper also shows that an ensemble of three shallow models (using union, average, or majority voting) can match or exceed a single deep-aligned model's safety, offering a training-free alternative at the cost of longer inference.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Qi et al. (ICLR 2025), who identified that safety alignment is fragile when confined to initial output tokens and proposed random-position refusal insertion as a fix, by reframing the problem through the equivalence between autoregressive LLMs and Markov chains (Zekri et al., 2024). Where prior work on LLM fine-tuning analysis relies on NTK linearization (Malladi et al., Jang et al., Tomihari & Sato) or non-group data augmentation, this paper introduces a cyclic group-theoretic structure on the augmentation and derives the first theoretical guarantees (δ-absorbing states) for safety depth. The ensemble safety result (Proposition 4.11) connects to deep ensemble equivariance (Gerken & Kessel, 2024) but applies it to a safety-specific risk-distribution setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first formal answer to 'how deep should safety alignment be?' with provable conditions on learning rate and training steps, giving practitioners a principled stopping criterion rather than heuristic tuning. The cyclic augmentation method is simple to implement and consistently improves safety across six LLMs, while the ensemble result offers an off-the-shelf alternative for users who cannot retrain. Key open questions include the helpfulness trade-off observed in Table 2, the gap between the Markov chain abstraction (Assumption 3.1) and actual transformer dynamics, and whether the theoretical guarantees hold under adversarial multi-turn attacks beyond the 5-turn evaluation.
