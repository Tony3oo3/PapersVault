## Problem

Outcome-supervised reinforcement learning for agentic RAG suffers from low exploration efficiency (no reward until the full chain completes), gradient conflict (correct early steps are penalized when later steps fail), and sparse reward signals. Process-supervised RL offers fine-grained stepwise feedback but has not been applied to RAG, where the absence of ground-truth intermediate rewards and the vast open-ended retrieval space make process-level annotation both ill-defined and prohibitively expensive.

## Input / Output

The model takes a natural language question and produces a multi-step reasoning trajectory that dynamically interleaves three actions—query generation, evidence extraction, and answer generation—using explicit XML-style placeholders (<query>, <evidence>, <answer>) for programmatic control. At each reasoning step, the model decides whether to invoke an external search engine (BGE retriever over Wikipedia), extract evidence spans from retrieved documents, or terminate with a final answer. The number of retrieval iterations is adaptive: single-hop questions converge in 2–3 steps while multi-hop questions require 3–5 steps.

## Method

ReasonRAG introduces a two-stage pipeline for process-supervised agentic RAG. First, it constructs the RAG-ProGuide dataset (5k questions, 13k preference pairs) by combining Shortest Path Reward Estimation (SPRE)—a Monte Carlo reward that simulates k continuations from each intermediate step and scores them with a correctness term multiplied by a step-count decay factor α to penalize unnecessarily long trajectories—with an MCTS exploration strategy that defines states as (question, prior steps, stage) and actions as {query generation, evidence extraction, answer generation}, using UCT-based selection to balance exploration and exploitation. Second, it trains the policy via process-level DPO, where each preference pair compares two one-step responses from the same state, providing dense per-step supervision rather than a single end-of-sequence reward. This yields a model that outperforms Search-R1 (90k queries, ~270k intermediate steps) using only 5k training queries and 13k process-level steps, with statistically significant gains across five benchmarks.

## Results

ReasonRAG (trained on only 5k queries) achieves an average EM of 34.4% and F1 of 42.3% across five benchmarks, outperforming Search-R1 (90k queries) which scores 32.8% EM and 40.7% F1. On multi-hop tasks, ReasonRAG leads notably: 48.9% F1 on HotpotQA (vs. 47.0% for Search-R1) and 50.4% F1 on 2WikiMultiHopQA (vs. 48.0%). The only dataset where ReasonRAG trails Search-R1 is MuSiQue EM (12.8% vs. 14.6%), though it still edges ahead on F1 (20.6% vs. 19.9%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReasonRAG (5k) outperforms the closest RL-based competitor Search-R1 (90k) on average EM (34.4% vs. 32.8%) and F1 (42.3% vs. 40.7%) using only ~5.6% of the training data, with statistically significant gains on four of five datasets. Against AutoRAG (10k), ReasonRAG leads by 4.9 EM and 5.4 F1 on average, with the largest gap on Bamboogle (36.0 vs. 24.8 EM). Among non-RL baselines, ReasonRAG surpasses the best summary method (RECOMP, 26.9 EM / 34.2 F1) by 7.5 EM and 8.1 F1, and the best RAG-CoT method (Iter-Retgen, 25.4 EM / 33.3 F1) by 9.0 EM and 9.0 F1. The sole exception is MuSiQue EM, where Search-R1 (14.6%) edges out ReasonRAG (12.8%), though ReasonRAG still wins on MuSiQue F1 (20.6% vs. 19.9%). Overall, ReasonRAG achieves the best or second-best result on every individual dataset metric, confirming the advantage of process-supervised over outcome-supervised RL for agentic RAG.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts two established tools—Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) for structured exploration and Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) for policy alignment—to the agentic RAG setting, where prior work has relied almost exclusively on outcome-supervised RL (e.g., Search-R1, DeepSeek-R1). The key conceptual departure is importing process-supervised RL, previously demonstrated in math reasoning (Math-Shepherd, PRM800K), into the RAG domain, where the open-ended retrieval space makes step-level annotation non-trivial. The novel SPRE reward function, which discounts correctness by trajectory length, is specifically designed to handle the multi-stage RAG workflow (query → evidence → answer) rather than a single linear reasoning chain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 18× data efficiency gain (5k vs. 90k training queries) is practically significant for teams that cannot afford large-scale RL rollouts, and the RAG-ProGuide dataset is explicitly positioned as a reusable off-policy resource for other LLMs. However, the approach is validated only on a single 7B backbone, a single retriever (BGE), and QA-style tasks; whether the MCTS+SPRE pipeline scales to larger models, open-ended generation, or non-Wikipedia knowledge bases remains open. The higher upfront cost of MCTS-based data generation (iterative LLM calls and retrieval) partially offsets the training savings, and the paper does not quantify this end-to-end compute trade-off rigorously.
