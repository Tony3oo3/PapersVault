## Problem

Prior work established that the Stochastic Gradient Bandit (SGB) policy achieves logarithmic regret only under a restrictive small-learning-rate condition (η ≲ Δ²/K^{3/2}) and converges asymptotically for any constant rate, but whether logarithmic regret holds for larger learning rates remained unclear. This paper asks: for a fixed constant learning rate, what is the precise threshold on η (as a function of the suboptimality gap Δ and number of arms K) that separates logarithmic from polynomial regret?

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes a scalar learning rate η, a number of arms K, and i.i.d. scalar rewards from distributions supported on [-1,1], and at each step outputs softmax sampling probabilities over the K arms. The theoretical outputs are regret upper and lower bounds expressed as functions of η, Δ, and K: specifically, a logarithmic bound R_T ≤ log(1+4ηΔT)/(2η) + Δ/(2η(Δ−ηC_η)) for K=2 when ηC_η < Δ, and a polynomial lower bound R_T = Ω̃(T^{1−(1+ε)λ_Δ/η}) when η exceeds a gap-dependent threshold. The regret decomposition into a post-convergence term and a failure regret term is the key structural output that drives all subsequent analysis.

## Method

The core contribution is a novel regret decomposition (Eq. 7) that splits SGB's regret into a post-convergence term, shown to be O(log T) for any constant η, and a failure regret term proportional to E[Σ(1−p_{1,t})²], which captures the probability that the optimal arm fails to dominate. For K=2, the analysis bounds the failure regret via an exponential-moment argument (Taylor expansion of e^{qr} for rewards in [-1,1]) that yields a sharp threshold at ηC_η ≈ Δ: below it, the failure regret vanishes and regret is logarithmic on all instances; above it, a carefully constructed Rademacher/Dirac instance forces polynomial regret of order T^{1−Δ/(η(1−Δ))}. For K>2, a lower-bound argument exploiting SGB's efficiency on an easy deterministic instance combined with the Lai–Robbins bound proves that η must scale as 1/K to avoid polynomial regret, and a complete logarithmic bound is obtained for the special case of identical gaps under ηe^{2η} ≤ 2Δ/(K+2). The approach deliberately avoids optimization-based (PL-inequality) analyses, instead working directly with the parameter dynamics and the linear constraint Σθ_{k,t}=0 that links all arms.

## Results

For two-armed bandits, SGB achieves logarithmic regret R_T ≤ log(1+4ηΔT)/(2η) + Δ/(2η(Δ−ηC_η)) when ηC_η < Δ (Theorem 1), but incurs polynomial regret R_T = Õ(T^{1−(1+ε)λ_Δ/η}) on some instances when η > λ_Δ ≈ Δ (Theorem 2). For K-armed bandits, the learning rate must additionally scale as η ≲ Δ/K to avoid polynomial regret (Theorem 3), and logarithmic regret is proven for identical-gap instances when ηC_η < 2Δ/(K+2) (Theorem 4). Without knowledge of Δ, a horizon-dependent rate η = √(log T / T) yields R_T ≲ √(T log T) (Corollary 1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares SGB primarily against SAMBA [29], a non-parametric policy gradient method that performs gradient ascent directly on sampling probabilities. For two arms, SAMBA with parameter α < Δ achieves a regret bound of α⁻¹ log(T), which is comparable to SGB's log(1+4ηΔT)/(2η) when η ≈ α, but SAMBA's analysis is restricted to Bernoulli rewards and involves non-explicit, potentially large constants, whereas SGB's bound is fully explicit and valid for all bounded rewards. Compared to the prior SGB analysis by Mei et al. [1], which required the very restrictive condition η ≲ Δ²/K^{3/2} for logarithmic regret, this paper extends the guaranteed logarithmic regime to η ≲ Δ (for K=2) and η ≲ Δ/K (for general K), a substantially broader range. The paper also contrasts SGB with standard bandit algorithms (KL-UCB, Thompson Sampling), noting that SGB's regret decomposition into a post-convergence term and a failure regret is fundamentally different from the arm-specific confidence-bound or posterior-based analyses used for those policies, and that SGB's learning rate must be tuned to the gap Δ, unlike UCB/TS which are gap-agnostic.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on SGB initiated by Sutton & Barto [11] and the recent theoretical analyses by Mei et al. [1, 2], which established logarithmic regret only for small learning rates (η ≲ Δ²/K^{3/2}) and asymptotic convergence for arbitrary constant rates, respectively. The key departure is methodological: rather than the optimization-based (PL-inequality, convergence-rate) analyses that dominate prior SGB work, the authors develop a regret-based analysis built on a novel decomposition into a 'post-convergence' term (always logarithmic) and a 'failure regret' term (governed by η). This positions the paper alongside SAMBA [29] as a gradient-ascent bandit policy with explicit regret guarantees, but with a sharper characterization of the learning-rate threshold and a broader reward-distribution scope.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper resolves a key open question from Mei et al. [1, 2]: asymptotic convergence of SGB for any constant learning rate does NOT guarantee logarithmic finite-horizon regret, and the critical threshold is η ≈ Δ (for K=2) or η ≈ 2Δ/K (conjectured for general K). This has direct practical implications for tuning SGB in reinforcement learning pipelines where the suboptimality gap is typically unknown. Significant open questions remain: the critical regime η ≈ Δ is not fully characterized, the K > 2 case is only resolved for identical gaps, and the additional terms H_t and W_t in the general-K failure regret bound (Lemma 3) resist tight analysis. The bimodal regret distribution (success vs. failure modes) also connects to broader questions about tail behavior of bandit algorithms raised by Fan & Glynn [40].
