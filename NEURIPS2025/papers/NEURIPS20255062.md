## Problem

Large-scale pre-trained vision models encode rich but complex and redundant representations that are difficult to distill for data-limited scientific downstream tasks, especially under out-of-distribution conditions. Existing fine-tuning strategies (linear probing, full fine-tuning, VPT, L2-SP) either lack task-specific adaptability, suffer cross-domain instability, or risk distorting pre-trained knowledge when transferring from massive upstream datasets to narrow scientific domains.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255062/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts RGB images at 224×224 resolution or video clips (T×C×H×W) as input, which are processed by a frozen pre-trained backbone (ViT, ConvNeXt, or Video Swin). A unified interface flattens all spatial/temporal dimensions into a token sequence H ∈ R^{N×D}, regardless of whether the backbone is CNN- or Transformer-based. The output is a class label (for classification) or an anomaly probability score (for industrial defect detection), obtained by reshaping the adapted features back to the backbone's native shape and passing them through the original classification head.

## Method

CLAdapter introduces K learnable cluster-center vectors that capture latent groupings in the pre-trained feature space. Attention scores are computed as the softmax of cosine similarities between the mean feature embedding and the L2-normalized cluster centers, yielding a probability distribution β over K clusters. Each cluster center has an associated D×D learnable transformation matrix; the weighted sum M* = Σ βᵢMᵢ produces a per-sample customized linear transformation that shifts the feature distribution toward the downstream task. The transformed features pass through LayerNorm and a two-layer MLP (GELU, 4× expansion) to introduce non-linearity. A Staged Fine-Tuning (SFT) strategy first trains only the adapter and head (freezing the backbone), then optionally unfreezes the full model for a second stage, balancing parameter efficiency with thorough adaptation. The unified interface design allows seamless plug-in integration with CNNs, Transformers, and their 3D video variants without architectural modification.

## Results

CLAdapter achieves state-of-the-art performance across all 10 evaluated datasets spanning diverse scientific domains. On the in-distribution Tiny-ImageNet benchmark, CLAdapter (ViT-L) reaches 94.21% top-1 accuracy, surpassing the strongest baseline Swin-L (91.35%) by 2.86%. On the out-of-distribution PACS benchmark, CLAdapter attains 91.41% accuracy, outperforming full fine-tuning (87.79%) by 3.62% and the best PEFT method MoRA (89.09%) by 2.32%. On data-limited scientific tasks, CLAdapter (ConvNeXt-B) achieves 98.29% average AUROC on industrial defect detection (InsPLAD-fault), 93.71% F1 on biomedical BreakHis, and 97.60% accuracy on 3D video recognition (UCF101), all setting new records.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CLAdapter consistently outperforms all 49 compared baselines across every dataset. On Tiny-ImageNet, it beats the strongest architecture baseline Swin-L (91.35%) by 2.86% and the same-backbone ViT-L (86.43%) by 7.78%. On PACS (OOD), CLAdapter surpasses full fine-tuning by 3.62%, L2-SP by 3.67%, and all PEFT methods including MoRA (89.09%), LoRA (88.53%), DoRA (88.43%), and VPT (76.76%) by margins of 2.32% to 14.65%. On the biomedical BreakHis dataset, CLAdapter (ViT-B) achieves 93.71% F1, exceeding the best prior method LW-GasHis-Transformer (83.69%) by 10.02 points; on HCRF, CLAdapter (ConvNeXt-B) reaches 98.59% F1 versus GasHis-Transformer's 97.97%. For industrial anomaly detection (InsPLAD-fault), CLAdapter (ConvNeXt-B) attains 98.29% average AUROC, outperforming AttentDifferNet (94.34%) by 3.95 points. On 3D video recognition, CLAdapter (Swin-B) leads VideoMAE by 1.50% on UCF101 (97.60% vs 96.10%) and by 2.50% on HMDB51 (75.80% vs 73.30%). The only marginally narrow win is on Apple Foliar Disease, where CLAdapter (98.36%) edges out Swin (98.08%) by just 0.28%.

## Relation to Prior Work

CLAdapter positions itself within the parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) lineage alongside LoRA, VPT, DoRA, and MoRA, but departs from these by operating on the feature representation space rather than modifying weight matrices or input prompts. It specifically addresses limitations the authors identify in VPT (ViT-only, cross-domain instability), L2-SP (poor OOD robustness), and OLOR (lack of task-specific self-adaptability) by introducing learnable cluster centers and attention-weighted transformation matrices that adaptively reshape pre-trained features. The unified interface design—flattening spatial/temporal dimensions from CNNs, ViTs, and 3D Swin Transformers into a common token format—repurposes the adapter concept from a ViT-specific technique into an architecture-agnostic module, while the Staged Fine-Tuning (SFT) strategy borrows the two-phase logic common in curriculum learning to avoid distorting backbone representations during early adaptation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically critical gap: as pre-training scales to billions of image-text pairs (LAION-2B), the challenge of transferring that knowledge to small, heterogeneous scientific datasets (medical histopathology, industrial defect detection, agricultural disease) becomes more acute, not less. The finding that full fine-tuning on LAION-2B pre-trained ViT-B actually collapses to 47% on PACS while linear probing reaches 96% highlights a non-trivial interaction between pre-training scale and fine-tuning strategy that the community should take seriously. Limitations include the absence of evaluation on detection or segmentation tasks, and the method's reliance on a fixed number of cluster centers (K=20) that may not generalize to tasks with very different feature geometries. Open questions include whether the cluster-attention mechanism scales to even larger backbones (ViT-H, EVA-02) and whether the SFT strategy's first stage alone can serve as a reliable zero-shot proxy for task difficulty estimation.
