## Problem

Parameter offloading enables lossless LLM inference on consumer GPUs but is bottlenecked by PCIe data transfers, yielding only 1–2 tokens/sec. Existing speculative decoding accelerators either depend on pretrained same-family draft models (unavailable for custom-trained LLMs) or require draft-model training that produces insufficient alignment, capping acceptance length below ~7 tokens. The paper asks how to obtain a highly aligned, fully GPU-resident draft model in a training-free, plug-and-play manner for arbitrary target LLMs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251815/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a text prompt and the full target LLM weight set, which is partitioned between GPU-resident layers and CPU-offloaded layers under a user-specified VRAM cap (8/12/24 GB). It outputs generated text tokens that are bit-identical to the target model's autoregressive output (lossless). A key operational detail is that the draft model is assembled on-the-fly from the target's own weights—no separate draft checkpoint is needed—and the shared KV-Cache means the draft model skips its own prefill phase entirely.

## Method

SubSpec constructs a fully GPU-resident draft model by (1) replacing each offloaded target layer with a 4-bit quantized substitute generated via data-free quantization (HQQ, group size 64), (2) directly sharing the target's GPU-resident decoder layers, and (3) sharing a single KV-Cache so both models operate on identical context. This yields near-perfect draft–target alignment, enabling very deep context-aware dynamic draft trees (D=48, k=6) that produce ~288 candidate tokens per verification pass. A draft probability sharpening step (applying temperature 0.2 to the draft's output distribution before cumulative scoring) corrects a 'false-positive path' divergence that otherwise caps acceptance length under greedy decoding. Complementary system-level optimizations—cross-layer asynchronous prefetching of offloaded weights and chunked prefill (256-token chunks) to bound peak activation memory—further hide PCIe latency and maximize the number of target layers kept on-GPU. The theoretical analysis shows that in offloading scenarios the speedup ratio τ/(D·t_Draft/t_Target + γ) is dominated by τ, so maximizing alignment (and thus τ) is far more valuable than minimizing draft-model latency, which is the opposite of the standard non-offloaded regime.

## Results

SubSpec achieves a mean throughput of 27.09 tokens/s (10.10× speedup) for Qwen2.5 7B under an 8GB VRAM limit and 6.50 tokens/s (12.50× speedup) for Qwen2.5 32B under a 24GB limit, with average acceptance lengths (τ) consistently near 30 across all five generation benchmarks (MT-Bench, HumanEval, GSM8K, Alpaca, CNN/DM). Under stochastic decoding (temperature=0.6), SubSpec retains a 5.8×–7.8× speedup. On reasoning models (DeepSeek-R1 distills, QWQ 32B), SubSpec achieves 11.4×–13.8× speedups with τ values exceeding 33 on AIME 2024, MATH 500, GPQA Diamond, and LiveCodeBench.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against EAGLE-2, SubSpec delivers roughly 3× higher throughput and ~7× higher acceptance length (e.g., 24.98 vs. 7.65 tokens/s and τ=29.66 vs. 3.95 for Llama3.1 8B), because EAGLE-2's small draft model has poor alignment with the target. Against same-family pretrained draft models (Llama3.2 1B, Qwen2.5 1.5B/7B), SubSpec achieves an additional 30–50% speedup (e.g., 27.09 vs. 17.73 tokens/s for Qwen2.5 7B) without any training, thanks to its substitute-layer construction that shares GPU-resident weights and KV-Cache. Against SpecExec, SubSpec achieves 76% higher speedup while staying within the 8GB VRAM budget (SpecExec peaks at 8.38–9.27 GB). Against lossy quantization baselines (GPTQ 58.87, AWQ 52.32, HQQ 135.84 tokens/s for Llama3.1 8B), SubSpec's 24.29 tokens/s is lower in raw throughput but is the only lossless option, preserving full model quality. The paper thus positions SubSpec as the dominant lossless, training-free method for offloaded LLM inference, outperforming all lossless baselines by a wide margin.

## Relation to Prior Work

SubSpec extends the speculative decoding framework (Leviathan et al., 2023) and tree-based draft construction (EAGLE-2, SpecExec) to the parameter-offloading regime, where the dominant cost is PCIe data transfer rather than compute. The key departure from prior SD-for-offloading work (SpecExec, Dovetail) is the draft model construction: rather than relying on a separate smaller pretrained model from the same family (which requires alignment training for custom models) or a trained EAGLE head, SubSpec uses low-bit quantized 'substitute' layers of the target model itself, shared GPU-resident weights, and a unified KV-Cache to achieve near-perfect alignment without any training. A theoretical analysis (Section 3) formalizes why this alignment-first strategy is optimal under offloading, inverting the standard SD preference for fast-but-misaligned drafts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SubSpec makes lossless LLM inference on consumer GPUs (8–24 GB VRAM) practically usable, raising throughput from ~2 tokens/s to ~25 tokens/s for 7B models without any quality degradation or additional training. This is significant for local deployment scenarios (privacy, customization, cost) where quantization-based compression is unacceptable. Open questions include: whether lower-bit (2–3 bit) substitutes can further reduce the ~7 GB minimum VRAM floor, how the method adapts to MoE architectures, and whether combining SubSpec with layer-skipping self-speculation can reduce the substitute-layer memory overhead. The ~60% speedup degradation under stochastic sampling (temperature 0.6) also suggests room for improvement in non-greedy settings.
