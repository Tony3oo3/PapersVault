## Problem

Diffusion Policy for Generative Behavior Cloning suffers from two coupled limitations: stochastic sampling can produce low-fidelity actions that cascade into task failures, and the open-loop/closed-loop control trade-off forces a choice between temporal consistency and environmental reactivity. Existing guidance mechanisms (Classifier-Free Guidance, AutoGuidance) do not transfer well to sequential control, and the state-of-the-art BID method incurs 16× computational overhead to address these issues.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-camera RGB observations (224×224 px from bird's-eye, front, and wrist views at 30 Hz) concatenated with the immediately preceding state observation s_{t-Δt} (Δt=1) as conditioning for the diffusion denoising network. It outputs a full action chunk of H=16 robot actions, but the Adaptive Chunking mechanism selectively dequeues actions from a persistent queue rather than always executing the first h actions of a freshly generated chunk, effectively varying the effective action horizon between 1 (closed-loop) and H/2 (open-loop) on a per-timestep basis.

## Method

The paper proposes two plug-and-play inference-time techniques that require no retraining or architectural changes. Self-Guidance (SG) modifies the diffusion denoising step by subtracting the model's own past-conditioned noise prediction: ε̂_new = (1+w)·ε_θ(x, s_t) − w·ε_θ(x, s_{t−Δt}), implemented as a single batched forward pass with concatenated conditioning [s_t, s_{t−Δt}]. This sharpens the action distribution toward high-fidelity modes and, via a first-order Taylor expansion, can be interpreted as extrapolating toward the future state s_{t+Δt}, implicitly promoting forward-looking behavior. Adaptive Chunking (AC) maintains an action queue and, at each timestep, computes the cosine similarity between the queue's first action and the newly predicted action; if similarity exceeds a threshold τ, the old chunk is retained (open-loop execution), otherwise the queue is replaced with the new chunk (closed-loop replanning). Together, SG improves per-sample fidelity and reactivity while AC dynamically balances consistency against responsiveness, yielding 23.25% improvement over Vanilla Diffusion Policy and 12.27% over BID at 16× lower computational cost.

## Results

On the Push-T benchmark under increasing stochasticity (P=0 to P=3), the proposed method achieves success rates of 0.819, 0.718, 0.413, and 0.261, substantially outperforming Vanilla Diffusion Policy (0.496–0.204) and BID (0.652–0.200). Across six simulation environments (Push-T, Square, Lift, Can, Transport, Kitchen), the method improves over Vanilla DP by 23.25% and over BID by 12.27% on average, while reducing computational cost by a factor of 16 compared to BID. On the LIBERO-Spatial VLA benchmark, integrating SG+AC into π₀ and OpenVLA-OFT yields 83.8% success at P=1 and 19.9% at P=5, versus 82.0% and 12.2% for the vanilla open-loop baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Vanilla Diffusion Policy [9], the proposed SG+AC method wins across all six simulation environments and all noise levels, with an average improvement of 23.25%; on Push-T at P=0 the gap is 0.819 vs. 0.496. Against EMA (temporal ensembling) [22], the method consistently outperforms, and EMA actually degrades below Vanilla on some tasks (e.g., Push-T P=0: 0.456 vs. 0.496), while SG+AC is robust to the hyperparameter sensitivity that plagues EMA's decay rate λ. Against BID [23], the method wins by 12.27% on average across the six environments and requires 16× fewer FLOPs and 2× lower latency, since BID's test-time search over multiple candidates is computationally expensive. On the LIBERO-Spatial VLA benchmark, SG+AC integrated into π₀ [12] and OpenVLA-OFT [29] achieves 83.8% (P=1) and 19.9% (P=5), beating both the open-loop vanilla (82.0%, 12.2%) and closed-loop variants (73.4%, 14.4%). Against AutoGuidance [19], Self-Guidance achieves higher peak performance at every noise level and avoids the extra checkpoint storage and dual-inference overhead; AG's performance degrades rapidly as noise increases while SG remains robust. Against Time-Step Guidance [32], SG shows a clear and consistent advantage, whereas TSG only marginally improves over Vanilla.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Diffusion Policy framework (Chi et al., IJRR 2023) by modifying its inference procedure rather than its training, drawing directly on the Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) formulation (Ho & Salimans, 2022) but replacing the unconditional score with the model's own past-conditioned score. It departs from AutoGuidance (Karras et al., NeurIPS 2024), which requires a separate undertrained checkpoint, by showing that the model's own recent output serves as a more effective and cheaper negative signal. The adaptive chunking mechanism addresses the open-loop/closed-loop trade-off previously handled by heuristics (EMA in ACT) or expensive test-time search (BID), and the method is further generalized to non-diffusion VLA models via an activation-steering variant inspired by LLM guidance techniques.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper tackles two practical bottlenecks that limit deployment of diffusion-based robot policies: stochastic sampling failures and the reactivity-consistency trade-off in action chunking. The self-guidance mechanism is attractive because it requires no additional training, no auxiliary model, and only a single batched inference pass, making it a drop-in improvement for existing diffusion policy pipelines. The extension to π₀ and OpenVLA-OFT suggests the technique generalizes beyond diffusion-based policies to broader VLA architectures. An open question is whether the adaptive chunking threshold and guidance weight can be made fully automatic (currently task-specific hyperparameters), and whether the step-wise similarity evaluation cost can be reduced for real-time deployment on resource-constrained hardware.
