## Problem

Existing Audio-Visual LLMs and spatial reasoning benchmarks are limited to static scenes or 2D representations, failing to capture the dynamic 3D spatial reasoning that humans perform in real-world environments with moving objects and spatial audio. No prior benchmark or model jointly addresses 3D spatial reasoning over both egocentric and allocentric perspectives in dynamic scenes with synchronized multi-channel spatial audio.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253971/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes undistorted egocentric video (32 uniformly sampled frames), 7-channel spatial audio at 48 kHz captured by a wearable microphone array on Aria glasses, and a natural language question anchored to a specific sound event. Outputs are a direction classification (3-way: left/right/back, or 4-way: front-left/front-right/back-left/back-right) or a numeric distance in meters, computed via an intermediate dynamic global map that encodes the target object's trajectory and static reference positions in a unified 2D coordinate frame.

## Method

SAVVY is a training-free two-stage pipeline that augments any AV-LLM with structured spatial reasoning. Stage 1 constructs egocentric spatial tracks (timestamp, azimuth, distance tuples) for query-relevant objects using three complementary cues: an AV-LLM-generated Snapshot Descriptor for temporal grounding and object identification, text-guided segmentation (CLIPSeg/SAM2) with monocular metric depth (ZoeDepth) for visual tracking, and spatial audio processing via SRP-PHAT for direction-of-arrival and coherent-to-diffuse ratio for distance estimation. Stage 2 projects these per-frame egocentric tracks into a unified 2D global map using SLAM-derived camera trajectories, clusters static object positions with DBSCAN, smooths dynamic trajectories with a Kalman filter, and then performs a coordinate transformation (rotating the reference-to-facing vector to the positive y-axis for allocentric queries) to compute the final direction or distance answer.

## Results

On SAVVY-Bench, SAVVY achieves 58.0% overall QA accuracy, outperforming the strongest baseline Gemini-2.5-pro (50.9%) by +7.1%. The gains are most pronounced on allocentric direction (+12.3%, 44.0% vs 31.7%) and egocentric direction (+9.5%, 84.7% vs 75.2%), while distance tasks show more modest improvements (+3.3% egocentric, +3.2% allocentric). Human annotators achieve 78.7% overall, leaving a 20.7% gap to the best method. All open-source AV-LLMs (7B scale) score at or below chance level (32.1%) on allocentric direction, highlighting the difficulty of object-centered spatial reasoning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAVVY outperforms all eight baselines across every sub-task. Against the strongest proprietary baseline Gemini-2.5-pro, SAVVY gains +9.5% on egocentric direction (84.7% vs 75.2%), +12.3% on allocentric direction (44.0% vs 31.7%), +3.3% on egocentric distance, and +3.2% on allocentric distance. Among open-source models, EgoGPT [23] is the strongest at 36.5% overall, but SAVVY surpasses it by +21.5%; notably, EgoGPT shows a 34.8% gap between its egocentric distance (57.6%) and allocentric distance (22.8%) scores. All six open-source AV-LLMs (VideoLLaMA2, LongVALE, Ola, MiniCPM-o2.6, video-SALMONN, EgoGPT) score at or below the 32.1% chance level on allocentric direction, indicating they essentially cannot perform object-centered spatial reasoning. The proprietary Gemini-2.5-flash (45.7%) and Gemini-2.5-pro (50.9%) are the only baselines that meaningfully exceed chance on allocentric tasks, yet both remain well below SAVVY's 44.0% allocentric direction accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of spatial reasoning benchmarks (VSI-Bench, OpenEQA, ScanQA) and audio-visual LLM evaluation, but departs from both in critical ways. Prior spatial reasoning benchmarks are restricted to static visual scenes, and existing AV-LLM benchmarks (MUSIC-AVQA, Ego4D-AVD) address audio-visual understanding without 3D spatial relations in dynamic settings. Methodologically, SAVVY repurposes classical spatial audio processing (SRP-PHAT for DoA, coherent-to-diffuse ratio for distance) alongside visual foundation models (CLIPSeg, SAM2, ZoeDepth) and SLAM-derived camera trajectories into a training-free pipeline that augments off-the-shelf AV-LLMs, rather than training new model architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SAVVY-Bench fills a clear gap by being the first benchmark to jointly evaluate 3D spatial reasoning over dynamic scenes with synchronized multi-channel spatial audio, covering both egocentric and allocentric perspectives. The large human-model gap (78.7% vs. 58.0%) and near-chance allocentric direction performance of most AV-LLMs highlight that current models fundamentally lack the temporal-spatial grounding needed for real-world navigation. Open questions include whether LLMs can internalize structured spatial reasoning (as opposed to relying on external pipelines), how the approach generalizes to outdoor and driving scenarios, and whether the 20.7% gap to human performance can be closed through training rather than inference-time augmentation.
