## Problem

Existing feed-forward 3D Gaussian Splatting methods regress per-pixel primitives aligned with input camera rays, creating redundancy when views overlap and constraining primitive placement to ray directions without full 3D flexibility. These pixel-aligned representations also fail to generalize to dynamic scenes, where redundant primitives across frames must be merged and new content must emerge, yet no learning-based mechanism exists to fuse and refine primitives in a canonical 3D space.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252563/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes N sparse multi-view RGB images (2–16 views at 256×256 or 320×240) with known camera intrinsics and extrinsics, encoded via Plücker ray embeddings and p×p patch features. For streaming, it additionally consumes historical Gaussian primitives warped from past keyframes via 2D tracking (TAPIR) and 3D triangulation. The output is a refined set of ~200K 3D Gaussian primitives (position, opacity, quaternion rotation, scale, SH color coefficients) in NDC-aligned voxel space, rendered via standard splatting rasterization at interactive rates (15 fps, 350 ms delay on a single H100).

## Method

The core contribution is a Fuse-and-Refine module built on a hybrid Splat-Voxel representation. Initial pixel-aligned Gaussian primitives are deposited onto a high-resolution voxel grid ([64,H,W] in NDC) using a cubic distance kernel, with features fused via opacity-weighted averaging. A coarse-to-fine hierarchy (downsampled by [2,8,8]) is constructed, and geometric sparsification retains only the top 20% of coarse voxels by accumulated weight, reducing the token count to under 10K. A 6-layer sparse voxel Transformer then processes coarse voxel features for global context, and a fine-level MLP predicts residual Gaussian parameters added to the initial attributes, producing refined primitives in 15 ms. The entire pipeline is trained end-to-end on static multi-view datasets (DL3DV, RealEstate10K) with MSE + LPIPS photometric loss, and generalizes zero-shot to dynamic streaming by warping historical primitives via embedded deformation graphs and applying error-aware fusion weighting to suppress unreliable contributions.

## Results

On RealEstate10K (static, 256×256), the method achieves PSNR 28.47, SSIM 0.907, and LPIPS 0.078, outperforming the strongest baseline GS-LRM (28.10 / 0.892 / 0.114) by ~0.4 dB PSNR and substantially on perceptual quality. On DL3DV, the Fuse-and-Refine module adds ~2 dB PSNR over GS-LRM (30.61 vs. 28.59) while maintaining comparable inference time (~52 ms). For streaming dynamic scenes, the method reaches PSNR 27.41 on Neural3DVideo (vs. 21.80 for GS-LRM and 23.16 for 4DGS) and 25.56 on LongVolumetricVideo, running at 15 fps with 350 ms delay on a single H100 GPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all 13 baselines across every reported metric. On RealEstate10K, it beats the closest competitor GS-LRM [84] by 0.37 dB PSNR and 0.036 LPIPS, and leads DepthSplat [76] (27.47 PSNR) by a full 1 dB. On DL3DV, the ~2 dB PSNR gain over GS-LRM (30.61 vs. 28.59) is achieved with a 12-layer multi-view transformer plus 6-layer voxel transformer, matching GS-LRM's 24-layer capacity. For streaming reconstruction on Neural3DVideo, the method surpasses GS-LRM by 5.6 dB PSNR (27.41 vs. 21.80) and 4DGS [74] by 4.25 dB, while also reducing the flicker metric from 5.71 to 2.92; 4DGS achieves a marginally lower flicker (2.75) but at the cost of 6 s per frame versus 0.07 s. On LongVolumetricVideo, the method edges out GS-LRM (25.56 vs. 25.49 PSNR) and substantially outperforms 4DGS (18.75) and 3DGStream [63] (16.97). The supplementary comparison (Table 9) confirms the advantage over pixelNeRF [82] (20.43 PSNR), GPNR [60] (24.11), and Du et al. [13] (24.78) on RealEstate10K.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on GS-LRM's multi-view Transformer backbone for pixel-aligned Gaussian primitive prediction, but departs from the pixel-aligned paradigm by introducing a Fuse-and-Refine module that operates in a canonical 3D space via a hybrid Splat-Voxel representation. The coarse-to-fine voxel hierarchy with a sparse voxel Transformer draws conceptual inspiration from voxel-based point cloud processing networks (Point-Voxel CNN, VoxelNet) and Lagrangian-Eulerian representations in physical simulation, but is specifically designed for Gaussian splatting fusion. For the streaming extension, the paper repurposes TAPIR for 2D tracking and embedded deformation graphs (from shape manipulation literature) for 3D warping, combining these with the learned voxel-based fusion to achieve history-aware reconstruction without any dynamic-scene training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The zero-shot generalization from static-scene training to dynamic streaming reconstruction is a practically significant result, as multi-view dynamic datasets remain too limited for large-scale training. The method achieves interactive rates (15 fps, 350 ms delay) on a single H100 while delivering SOTA on both static and dynamic benchmarks, making it relevant for AR/VR applications. Key limitations include dependence on initial primitive quality (degraded in large-baseline settings), persistent temporal artifacts in dynamic regions due to the simple 3D warping strategy, and the inherent difficulty of lifting 2D tracking to 3D from sparse views. Open questions include whether adaptive online reconstruction strategies or tracking-free approaches could further reduce temporal artifacts, and whether the voxel-based fusion generalizes to unbounded SLAM-like scenarios.
