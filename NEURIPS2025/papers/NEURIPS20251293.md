## Problem

Information-theoretic likelihood bounds for diffusion models have historically been looser than ELBO-based approaches, and the classical KL-Fisher relationship underlying score matching has been restricted to Gaussian noise perturbations. In practice, discrete data requires dequantization that introduces a train-test mismatch inflating NLL, and real-world sensor artifacts (Poisson, uniform, Laplacian) violate the Gaussian assumption. The paper asks whether IT-based bounds can be tightened to competitive likelihood estimators while generalizing beyond Gaussian noise and eliminating the dequantization gap.

## Input / Output

The method takes 8-bit discrete RGB images (32×32 CIFAR-10, 32×32/64×64/128×128 ImageNet) and first applies a warm-start dequantization step that injects arbitrary isotropic noise (Gaussian, Laplace, logistic, or uniform) scaled to a small variance σ₀², producing continuous-valued inputs to the diffusion channel. The model outputs a per-sample negative log-likelihood in bits/dim, generated image samples, and lossless compressed bitstreams via bits-back coding. No data augmentation is applied, and the U-Net embeds log-SNR (η = −log SNR) rather than raw diffusion time, with the forward process restricted to [σ₀², σ₁²] to avoid the σ→0 singularity.

## Method

The core contribution is a generalized KL-Fisher identity (Theorem 1) proving that for any isotropic noise with zero mean and identity covariance, the derivative of KL divergence with respect to noise variance at zero equals minus half the Fisher divergence, extending prior Gaussian-only results. Building on this, the paper derives a pointwise NLL upper bound (Theorem 2) via thermodynamic integration of the denoising score matching loss along the noise-variance axis, decomposing the mismatched entropy into a channel output term, a DSM approximation error, and an irreducible Fisher-information term. A 'warm start' injects arbitrary isotropic noise at the start of the forward process, unifying dequantization with the diffusion channel and eliminating the separate variational dequantization training phase. For efficient evaluation, a hand-crafted importance sampling distribution over log-SNR (ρ(η) ∝ w(η)) renders the Monte Carlo estimator scale-invariant, reducing variance without learned components. The training procedure uses standard noise-prediction score matching with a U-Net, requiring only 0.3M iterations to reach state-of-the-art NLL on ImageNet.

## Results

The method achieves 2.50 bits/dim NLL on CIFAR-10 (VP schedule with importance sampling) and state-of-the-art 3.01, 2.91, and 2.59 bits/dim on ImageNet-32, -64, and -128, respectively. All results are obtained with only 0.3M training iterations and no data augmentation, compared to 2–10M iterations for prior methods. On CIFAR-10, the method is competitive (second-best after W-PCDM's 2.35), while on ImageNet it sets new records across all three resolutions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On ImageNet-32/64/128, the proposed method (3.01/2.91/2.59 BPD) achieves new state-of-the-art NLL, outperforming the strongest prior diffusion baseline W-PCDM [46] by 0.31/0.04/0.05 BPD and the flow-based Flow Matching [49] by 0.52/0.40/0.31 BPD. On CIFAR-10, the method (2.49–2.50 BPD) trails W-PCDM (2.35 BPD, which uses a cascaded architecture) but clearly surpasses MuLAN [62] (2.55), i-DODE [90] (2.56), DiffEnc [55] (2.62), VDM [40] (2.65), and all IT-based baselines including ScoreFlow [69] (2.80) and ScoreODE [50] (3.38–3.44). A critical practical advantage is training efficiency: the method converges in 0.3M iterations versus 2M (VDM, MuLAN, W-PCDM), 8M (DiffEnc), or 10M (BSI, VDM-ELBO), while requiring no data augmentation. Among non-diffusion baselines on ImageNet-64, the method beats Routing Transformer [61] (3.43), Sparse Transformer [9] (3.44), and all flow/VAE models by at least 0.4 BPD.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the information-theoretic (IT) likelihood estimation framework for diffusion models (Song et al. 2021, Kong et al. 2023) by generalizing the KL-Fisher identity from Gaussian to arbitrary isotropic noise perturbations, building on the mismatched estimation theory of Verdú (2010) and the de Bruijn identity. It adopts the VDM architecture and noise-prediction parameterization (Kingma et al. 2021, Song et al. 2021) but replaces the ELBO-based variational objective with a thermodynamic-integration-based score matching bound, and extends the importance sampling strategy of Zheng et al. (2023) with a hand-crafted log-SNR-weighted proposal. The 'warm start' dequantization generalizes the uniform/Gaussian dequantization of Ho et al. (2019) and Theis et al. (2016) to arbitrary isotropic noise, eliminating the train-test gap without an additional variational optimization stage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: achieving state-of-the-art NLL on ImageNet-32/64/128 with only 0.3M training iterations (roughly 10× fewer than prior SOTA methods) and no data augmentation makes high-quality likelihood estimation far more accessible. The theoretical generalization to arbitrary isotropic noise provides a principled justification for non-Gaussian perturbations (Poisson, Laplacian, heavy-tailed) that appear in real sensor data and recent generative models. However, the method is limited to likelihood evaluation and cannot construct a generative sampling process under the alternative noise, and the dequantization bound, variance schedule, and architecture choices remain underexplored, leaving open whether the efficiency gains transfer to higher-resolution or non-image modalities.
