## Problem

LoRA suffers from intra-task parameter interference within its ranks, and existing MoE-based LoRA variants that mitigate this introduce explicit router parameters that undermine efficiency while remaining ineffective for inter-task interference during multi-task model merging. The paper seeks a design that simultaneously reduces intra-task interference, reduces inter-task interference in training-free merging, and eliminates router parameter overhead.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250349/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard token embeddings x ∈ R^n from a frozen pretrained LLM and produces the usual model output (text, code, or answers). Unusually, the down-projection matrix A is a frozen sparse random matrix (each row has exactly p non-zero Gaussian entries) that is never updated during training, so only the up-projection matrix B ∈ R^{m×r} is trainable. The internal top-k selection over Ax determines which rank-1 columns of B are activated per token, making the effective per-token computation equivalent to a rank-k LoRA while the total parameter space spans rank r.

## Method

FlyLoRA replaces LoRA's trainable matrix A with a frozen sparse random projection that simultaneously serves as the down-projection and an implicit router: after computing Ax, only the k columns of B linked to the top-k magnitude entries of Ax are activated (a rank-wise MoE with r rank-1 experts). A small load-balancing bias vector d is added to Ax during expert selection to prevent expert collapse. Theoretically, the sparse random projection preserves pairwise distances (a Johnson-Lindenstrauss-type guarantee), justifying its use as a distance-preserving implicit router that routes similar inputs to similar experts without learned routing weights. For multi-task merging, independent random projections A_i and A_j from different LoRA components are shown to span approximately orthogonal subspaces (with correlation bounded by O(p²/(nr²))), so task-specific updates B_iA_i and B_jA_j interfere minimally under weight-averaging fusion. This eliminates all explicit router parameters while achieving finer-grained expert allocation than prior MoE-LoRA methods.

## Results

On Llama-3.1-8B, FlyLoRA(k=8) achieves 40.88% MMLU, 94.15% ScienceQA, 58.76% GSM8K, and 36.88% HumanEval Pass@1, while using only 0.13% of Full FT parameters—less than half of LoRA(r=8) at 0.26%. On Qwen-2.5-7B, FlyLoRA reaches 53.68% MMLU, 95.55% ScienceQA, 80.82% GSM8K, and 54.34% HumanEval Pass@1. In multi-task merging (Table 2), FlyLoRA exhibits the smallest performance degradation after weight averaging, e.g., only −2.02% MMLU drop on Llama-3.1-8B versus −6.48% for LoRA(r=8) and −4.91% for Split-LoRA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FlyLoRA(k=8) consistently outperforms all three baselines across every benchmark and both model backbones. Against LoRA(r=8) (same activation rank), FlyLoRA gains +4.35% on MMLU, +3.42% on GSM8K, and +7.75% on HumanEval Pass@1 on Llama-3.1-8B, while using half the activated parameters (0.13% vs. 0.26%). Against LoRA(r=32) (same total rank), FlyLoRA still leads by +1.95% MMLU and +6.51% HumanEval Pass@1 on Llama-3.1-8B, despite activating only 8 of 32 ranks. Against the MoE-based Split-LoRA(4×8), FlyLoRA improves MMLU by +2.44% and GSM8K by +3.11% on Llama-3.1-8B, attributed to its finer rank-wise expert granularity (32 rank-1 experts vs. 4 rank-8 experts). In the multi-task merging setting, FlyLoRA's approximate subspace orthogonality yields the smallest post-merge degradation (−2.02% MMLU on Llama-3.1-8B) compared to −6.48% for LoRA(r=8) and −4.91% for Split-LoRA, confirming its inter-task decoupling advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

FlyLoRA extends the MoE-based LoRA line (Split-LoRA, LoRAMoE, MixLoRA) by pushing expert granularity to the extreme—rank-wise experts—while simultaneously eliminating the explicit router that prior methods require. It repurposes the frozen down-projection matrix A, previously studied only as a memory-saving trick in LoRA-FA and Asymmetry LoRA, into a functional implicit router whose distance-preserving (Johnson-Lindenstrauss) properties enable top-k expert selection without learned routing parameters. The inter-task orthogonality guarantee for model merging connects to the task-arithmetic and TIES-merging literature but provides a structural (architectural) rather than algorithmic solution to inter-task interference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FlyLoRA offers a drop-in architectural modification to LoRA that reduces activated parameters by ~50% relative to Split-LoRA while improving accuracy, and it provides a principled, training-free mechanism for multi-task model merging with provably reduced interference. The bio-inspired framing (fly olfactory circuit) is compelling but the core contribution is the architectural insight that a fixed sparse random projection can substitute for a learned router. Open questions include whether the gains persist at 70B+ scale, whether the sparsity ratio and activation rank require per-task tuning, and how FlyLoRA interacts with other PEFT methods (DoRA, AdaLoRA) or more sophisticated merging strategies beyond weight averaging.
