## Problem

Learning-augmented mechanism design for facility location has been studied primarily for utilitarian and egalitarian objectives, but the envy ratio—a pairwise fairness metric measuring the maximum utility ratio between any two agents—had no learning-augmented mechanism with formal consistency-robustness guarantees. Additionally, Ding et al. (2020) left open whether any randomized strategyproof mechanism could achieve an envy-ratio approximation ratio strictly below 2. This paper addresses both gaps by designing learning-augmented mechanisms for the envy ratio and resolving the randomized lower-bound question.

## Input / Output

The mechanisms take as input a location profile x ∈ [0,1]^n (agents on a unit interval), a prediction ŷ ∈ [0,1] of the optimal facility location (the midpoint of the leftmost and rightmost agents), and for the deterministic α-BIM a tunable parameter α ∈ [1,2] controlling the consistency-robustness tradeoff. Deterministic mechanisms output a single facility location y ∈ [0,1]; randomized mechanisms output a probability distribution over [0,1]. A key structural constraint is that all mechanisms are anonymous (output invariant to agent relabeling) and strategyproof, which by Moulin's characterization restricts deterministic mechanisms to phantom mechanisms with n+1 constant points.

## Method

The paper introduces three mechanisms. First, the α-Bounding Interval Mechanism (α-BIM) places the facility at ŷ if ŷ ∈ [1−1/α, 1/α], otherwise at the nearest interval boundary, achieving α-consistency and α/(α−1)-robustness; optimality is proven via Moulin's phantom-mechanism characterization showing all phantoms must lie within the bounding interval. Second, the (α,p)-LRM constant mechanism randomizes over three symmetric points {1/2−α, 1/2, 1/2+α} with probabilities {p, 1−2p, p}; solving a two-instance optimization yields optimal parameters (√5/2−1, 2/5) achieving a 1.8944-approximation, the first randomized mechanism below 2. Third, the Bias-Aware Mechanism (BAM) places the facility at ŷ with probability p = 1/2 − |ŷ − 1/2| and at 1/2 otherwise, so the probability of trusting the prediction decreases linearly with its deviation from the midpoint; BAM strictly dominates α-BIM in both consistency and robustness, achieving (−4c²+2)-consistency and (c+2)-robustness for c = |ŷ−1/2| ≥ 1/4, and 7/4-consistency with 9/4-robustness for c < 1/4.

## Results

For the deterministic setting, the α-BIM mechanism achieves α-consistency and α/(α-1)-robustness for any tunable parameter α ∈ [1,2], and this tradeoff is proven optimal among all deterministic strategyproof anonymous mechanisms. For randomized mechanisms without predictions, the (√5/2 − 1, 2/5)-LRM constant mechanism achieves a 1 + 2/√5 ≈ 1.8944 approximation ratio, improving upon the previous best of 2, while the lower bound is tightened from 1.0314 to 1.12579. The Bias-Aware Mechanism (BAM) for randomized mechanisms with predictions achieves (−4c² + 2)-consistency and (c + 2)-robustness for c ∈ [1/4, 1/2], and 7/4-consistency and 9/4-robustness for c ∈ [0, 1/4), strictly dominating α-BIM in both metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is against the deterministic Constant-1/2 mechanism (2-consistency, 2-robustness) and the results of Ding et al. [2020]. The α-BIM matches the Constant-1/2 worst-case guarantee (α=2 gives 2-consistency, 2-robustness) but adds a tunable tradeoff: smaller α yields better consistency at the cost of worse robustness, which is proven optimal via a phantom-mechanism lower bound. For randomized mechanisms without predictions, the (√5/2 − 1, 2/5)-LRM mechanism achieves 1.8944, strictly improving on the 2-approximation that was the best known upper bound (Ding et al. could not construct any randomized mechanism below 2). The lower bound is also improved from 1.0314 (Ding et al.) to 1.12579. Finally, BAM strictly outperforms α-BIM in both consistency and robustness for all prediction biases c, as illustrated in Figure 3, by reducing the probability of placing the facility at the prediction as its deviation from the midpoint grows.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two research threads: the envy ratio fairness objective for facility location introduced by Ding et al. (2020), and the learning-augmented mechanism design framework established by Agrawal et al. (2022) and Lykouris & Vassilvitskii (2021). Prior learning-augmented facility location work focused on utilitarian and egalitarian objectives; this paper is the first to apply the consistency-robustness paradigm to the envy ratio, a pairwise fairness metric. The optimality proof for the deterministic α-BIM mechanism leverages Moulin's (1980) phantom mechanism characterization, while the randomized results build on the LRM constant mechanism family to resolve an open question posed by Ding et al. about whether any randomized strategyproof mechanism could beat the 2-approximation barrier.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides tight, optimal characterizations of the consistency-robustness tradeoff for envy ratio facility location, giving practitioners a principled way to tune mechanisms based on prediction confidence. Resolving the open question of a randomized mechanism below 2-approximation (achieving 1.8944) and improving the lower bound from 1.0314 to 1.12579 narrows the gap significantly. Key open questions remain: lower bounds for randomized mechanisms with predictions are not established, and the authors note they could not derive a closed-form error-parameterized approximation ratio for the BAM mechanism. Extending the learning-augmented framework to other fairness notions (Gini, group fairness) is identified as promising future work.
