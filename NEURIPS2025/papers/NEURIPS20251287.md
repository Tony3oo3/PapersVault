## Problem

Fast (1-step) adversarial training against sparse l0-bounded perturbations suffers from catastrophic overfitting (CO) that existing mitigation methods designed for l∞, l2, and l1 norms cannot address. The paper identifies that CO in the l0 case arises from sub-optimal perturbation locations rather than magnitudes, and that the adversarial loss landscape is significantly more craggy than its counterparts under other norms, making 1-step training particularly unstable and leading to near-zero robust accuracy.

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images (32×32×3 for CIFAR-10/CIFAR-100, 224×224 for ImageNet-100, 32×32 for GTSRB) and produces class labels via a CNN classifier. During training, adversarial perturbations are generated by 1-step Sparse-PGD that modifies at most ε pixels (e.g., ε=20 for CIFAR-10, ε=200 for ImageNet-100), and robustness is evaluated against Sparse-AutoAttack (sAA) at the same sparsity level. The backbone is PreAct ResNet-18 for CIFAR/GTSRB and ResNet-34 for ImageNet-100.

## Method

The paper proves via Lipschitz smoothness analysis that the gradient discontinuity bound B_θδ in the adversarial loss is orders of magnitude larger for l0 than for l∞, l2, or l1, explaining the craggy landscape and CO. To smooth the loss, the proposed Fast-LS-l0 method combines three components: (1) soft labels via Self-Adaptive Training (SAT) to provably reduce the first-order Lipschitz constant A_θ, (2) a TRADES-style trade-off loss interpolating between clean and adversarial losses to reduce the second-order discontinuity term from 4L_θ to 2(1+α)L_θ and scale the perturbation-dependent term by α, and (3) N-FGSM random noise augmentation to mitigate sub-optimal perturbation locations. This combination eliminates CO entirely and achieves 63.0% robust accuracy with 1-step training—only 2.5% below 20-step sTRADES (61.7%)—while requiring less than 1/6 the total training time.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (ε=20), the proposed Fast-LS-l0 (1-step sTRADES + SAT + N-FGSM) achieves 63.0% robust accuracy under Sparse-AutoAttack (sAA) with 82.5% clean accuracy in only 0.8h of training. This is only 2.5% below the 20-step sTRADES+S&N (65.5%) while requiring less than 1/6 of the training time (0.8h vs. 5.4h). On ImageNet-100 (ε=200), Fast-LS-l0 reaches 72.4% sAA robust accuracy in 44h, compared to 77.8% for 20-step sTRADES+S&N in 360h. The method eliminates catastrophic overfitting (CO) that causes 1-step sAT to drop to 0.0% robust accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Fast-LS-l0 (63.0% sAA) outperforms all existing CO mitigation methods tested in Table 2: ATTA (0.0%), Free-AT (8.9%), GradAlign (0.0%), Fast-BAT (14.1%), FLC Pool (0.0%), N-AAER (0.1%), N-LAP (0.0%), and NuAT (51.9%), with the closest competitor NuAT still trailing by 11.1%. Against other smoothing approaches (Table 7), Fast-LS-l0 (63.0%) beats AdvLC (59.6%), MART (48.0%), TRADES+SWA (45.0%), and naive label smoothing (0.0%). Compared to the multi-step baselines, Fast-LS-l0 achieves 63.0% sAA versus 65.5% for 20-step sTRADES+S&N (−2.5%) and 61.0% for 20-step sAT+S&N (+2.0%), while reducing training time from ~5.4h to 0.8h. On ImageNet-100, the gap to 20-step sTRADES+S&N widens slightly (72.4% vs. 77.8%), but the 8× speedup (44h vs. 360h) makes fast training practical. The method also improves multi-step training: adding S&N to 20-step sAT lifts sAA from 36.2% to 61.0%, and to sTRADES from 61.7% to 65.5%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the l0 adversarial training framework established by Zhong et al. (ICML 2024), which introduced sPGD, sAT, sTRADES, and sAA for sparse perturbations, by investigating the 1-step (fast) variant. It departs from the established understanding of catastrophic overfitting in l∞/l2 settings (where CO is attributed to sub-optimal perturbation magnitudes) by demonstrating that in l0, the root cause is sub-optimal perturbation *locations*, rendering all existing CO mitigation methods ineffective. The solution repurposes soft labels (from SAT) and the trade-off loss (from TRADES) — techniques previously applied to robust overfitting in l2/l∞ — but shows they are *essential* rather than merely helpful in the l0 case, supported by a new theoretical analysis proving the l0 adversarial loss landscape is fundamentally more craggy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 6× speedup (0.8h vs 5.3h on CIFAR-10) with comparable or better robust accuracy (63.0% vs 61.7% sAA) makes adversarially robust training against sparse attacks practically feasible at scale, which is important given the growing relevance of sparse perturbations in real-world threat models. The theoretical characterization of the craggy l0 loss landscape (Hessian eigenvalues orders of magnitude larger, gradient distances 5× greater between 1-step and multi-step attacks) provides a principled explanation for why fast l0 training is uniquely difficult. Open questions include whether the soft-label + trade-off-loss recipe is truly optimal for smoothing, whether the location-vs-magnitude distinction generalizes to other non-convex constraint sets, and whether the remaining 2.5% gap to multi-step can be closed.
