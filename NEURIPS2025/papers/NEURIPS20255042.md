## Problem

Automated model discovery must search a vast combinatorial space of model structures while balancing local detail capture with generalizability beyond training data. Existing approaches rely on predefined kernel grammars and fixed quantitative metrics (e.g., BIC) that fail to adaptively assess whether a model's predictions remain structurally consistent in extrapolated regions, often selecting models that fit training data well but degrade sharply outside it.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255042/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes univariate time series data (e.g., Airline Passenger, Solar Irradiance) along with a growing pool of previously discovered models from prior rounds. AnalyzerVLM accesses the data not as raw numeric sequences but through generated Python code blocks (using NumPy, Matplotlib), allowing it to dynamically choose how to represent and inspect the data. The output is a kernel composition (built from basis kernels LIN, PER, SE, C, WN with +, ×, and replacement operators), optimized hyperparameters, and posterior mean predictions with confidence regions, scored by a combined Visual Information Criterion.

## Method

The pipeline iterates four stages—proposal, fitting, evaluation, selection—over multiple rounds. AnalyzerVLM performs multi-step agentic reasoning: it alternates between natural-language analysis, executable Python code generation (to compute residuals, plot trends, inspect periodicity), and model proposal, terminating only when it judges its analysis sufficient. This replaces the single-shot proposal of prior LLM-based methods (e.g., BoxLM) with an adaptive loop that acquires information on its own terms. For evaluation, EvaluatorVLM scores each fitted model on visual fitness (resemblance of posterior mean to data, plus uncertainty magnitude) and visual generalizability (whether predictions maintain structural consistency in 20% extrapolated regions), each on a [0,50] scale. These scores are combined with BIC into the Visual Information Criterion (VIC = α·EvaluatorVLM − BIC), derived as a modified Bayesian posterior with non-uniform structure-similarity and fitness priors. The best-scoring model from a cumulative pool is carried forward as the reference for the next round, and AnalyzerVLM's proposed initial hyperparameters (period, lengthscale) seed the non-convex GP optimization to avoid suboptimal local minima.

## Results

On the primary Gaussian Process kernel discovery task across 7 real-world univariate datasets, the pipeline with GPT-4o-mini achieves the best average test RMSE of 0.1070, substantially outperforming Automatic Statistician (0.3017), BoxLM (0.3474), and Gaussian Process SE (0.3121). The average train RMSE is 0.0134, also the best among all methods. On symbolic regression (4 benchmark datasets), the method achieves competitive R² (avg 0.966) and RMSE (avg 0.070), though LLM-SR attains a lower average RMSE (0.035) and ICSR-V a higher average R² (0.980).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The pipeline with GPT-4o-mini achieves the lowest average test RMSE (0.1070) across all 7 time-series datasets, outperforming the closest competitor Automatic Statistician (0.3017) by ~64% and BoxLM (0.3474) by ~69%. On individual datasets, the method wins on 5 of 7 test RMSE values (Airline 0.0534, Mauna 0.0564, Call 0.0508, Radio 0.0562, Gas 0.0893), with Automatic Statistician winning on Wheat (0.1499 vs. 0.1470) and Solar (0.6370 vs. 0.2861 — actually Ours wins here too). Traditional baselines (GP-SE, ARIMA, Prophet) show substantially higher test RMSE (0.28–0.35 avg), indicating poor generalization beyond training regions. On symbolic regression, the method is competitive but not dominant: LLM-SR achieves the best average RMSE (0.035 vs. 0.070) and ICSR-V the best average R² (0.980 vs. 0.966), while the paper's method still outperforms SGA by a wide margin (RMSE 0.070 vs. 0.193). The key differentiator is generalization: while BoxLM and Automatic Statistician achieve very low train RMSE (0.015 and 0.023), their test RMSE degrades sharply, whereas the proposed pipeline maintains low error in both regimes thanks to the Visual Information Criterion.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the automated model discovery line initiated by the Automatic Statistician (Duvenaud et al., 2013) and its successor BoxLM (Li et al., 2024), which defined the kernel composition grammar and used LLMs to propose candidate models in a single step. The key departure is replacing the single-shot LLM proposal with a multi-step VLM agent (AnalyzerVLM) that iteratively generates and executes code to analyze data and models, and introducing a VLM-based evaluator (EvaluatorVLM) that scores models visually via a new Visual Information Criterion (VIC) complementing BIC. The work also draws on the broader LLM/VLM agent literature (ReAct, WaitGPT, Tree of Thoughts) for the multi-step reasoning and code-execution loop, but applies it specifically to probabilistic model structure search rather than general data analysis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that VLM-based agentic reasoning can effectively substitute for human experts in model discovery, achieving strong generalization (test RMSE) that purely likelihood-based criteria like BIC fail to capture. The VIC addresses a concrete and well-motivated gap: BIC cannot penalize models that flatten or diverge in extrapolation regions, a failure mode that is visually obvious to humans. However, the pipeline is currently restricted to 1D univariate time series, and its performance depends on the quality of input visualizations, leaving open the question of how it scales to multivariate data, higher-dimensional inputs, or domains where visual intuition is less reliable. The hybrid SR+GP framework in the appendix hints at a broader synergy between symbolic and probabilistic model discovery that remains underexplored.
