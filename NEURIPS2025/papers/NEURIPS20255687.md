## Problem

Prior finite-sample analyses of distributional TD learning were limited to the tabular setting, leaving open whether learning the full return distribution with function approximation is statistically as efficient as learning its expectation. This paper establishes the first non-asymptotic guarantees for distributional TD learning with linear function approximation, showing that the sample complexity matches that of classic linear TD learning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255687/cropped_page5_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes streaming MDP transitions (s_t, a_t, r_t) generated under a fixed policy π, along with a d-dimensional feature map φ(s) and a categorical parameterization with K+1 equally-spaced support points on [0, (1-γ)^{-1}]. It outputs a parameter matrix Θ ∈ R^{d×K} defining a linear-categorical signed measure η_θ(s) = Σ_k p_k(s;θ) δ_{x_k} for each state, where the CDF is parameterized as F_k(s;θ) = φ(s)^T θ(k) + (k+1)/(K+1). The estimator quality is measured by the μ_π-weighted 1-Wasserstein distance W_{1,μ_π}, and the use of signed measures (total mass 1, possibly negative point masses) is required for compatibility with linear function approximation.

## Method

The paper proposes Linear-CTD, which directly formulates the linear-categorical projected Bellman equation as a linear system in Θ and solves it via linear stochastic approximation with Polyak-Ruppert tail averaging and a constant step size. The key algorithmic innovation is a preconditioner (C^T C)^{-1} that removes a matrix whose condition number scales as K², yielding a convergence rate independent of the number of categorical supports K — a property the vanilla SSGD algorithm lacks. The theoretical analysis hinges on a fine-grained decomposition of the linear-categorical Bellman operator, where the critical technical step is bounding the spectral norm of E[(C G̃(r) C^{-1}) ⊗ (Σ_φ^{-1/2} φ(s)φ(s')^T Σ_φ^{-1/2})] by √γ despite the non-independence of the two Kronecker factors, achieved by exploiting the rank-1 structure of the second factor (Lemma 5.4). Exponential stability of the product of random matrices Γ_t^{(α)} = Π(I - αA_i) then yields the L² and high-probability convergence rates, which match those of classic Linear-TD up to the replacement of ‖ψ*‖_{Σ_φ} with the distributional norm ‖θ*‖_{V_1}.

## Results

The paper establishes that Linear-CTD achieves a sample complexity of Õ((ε⁻² + λ_min⁻¹)(1−γ)⁻²λ_min⁻¹(||θ*||²_V1 + 1)) with the optimal instance-dependent step size, and Õ(ε⁻²(1−γ)⁻²λ_min⁻²(||θ*||²_V1 + 1)) with the instance-independent step size α ≃ (1−γ). Crucially, these bounds are independent of the number of categorical supports K, matching the state-of-the-art rates for classic Linear-TD. In contrast, the vanilla (un-preconditioned) linear-categorical TD algorithm requires a sample complexity of Õ(K⁴(||θ*||²_V1 + 1)/((1−γ)²λ_min)(1/ε² + K²/λ_min)), showing a K⁴ and K²/λ_min degradation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's central comparison is against classic Linear-TD (Samsonov et al., 2024b; Li et al., 2024b): Linear-CTD achieves an identical sample complexity up to the replacement of ‖ψ*‖_Σφ with ‖θ*‖_V1, confirming that learning the full return distribution is statistically no harder than learning its expectation under linear function approximation. Against the vanilla (un-preconditioned) linear-categorical TD algorithm from Bellemare et al. (2023, Section 9.6), Linear-CTD eliminates the K⁴ and K²/λ_min dependence in the sample complexity, which the authors attribute to the preconditioner (CᵀC)⁻¹ that removes a matrix with condition number scaling as K². The variance-reduced Linear-TD of Li et al. (2023a) achieves a tighter Õ(ε⁻²) rate by breaking the sample-size barrier, but this technique has not yet been extended to the distributional setting and is noted as future work. Empirically (Figure 1 and Appendix G), Linear-CTD converges robustly across K values with a fixed step size α = 0.01, while the vanilla algorithm requires the step size to decay as K⁻² to maintain convergence, and Linear-CTD consistently outperforms it across all tested K.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of distributional TD learning initiated by Bellemare et al. (2017) and formalized in the Bellemare et al. (2023) textbook, which introduced categorical parametrization and vanilla linear-categorical TD via stochastic semi-gradient descent. Prior finite-sample analyses of distributional TD (Rowland et al. 2018, 2024b; Peng et al. 2024; Bock and Heitzinger 2022) were confined to the tabular setting, where the key finding was that learning the return distribution is statistically as easy as learning its expectation. This paper is the first to establish that equivalence under linear function approximation, using the exponential stability framework of Samsonov et al. (2024b) adapted to the distributional setting. Algorithmically, the proposed Linear-CTD is a preconditioned variant of the vanilla SSGD that removes the matrix C^T C (whose condition number scales as K²), yielding a convergence rate independent of the number of categorical supports K.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result resolves a natural open question: whether the statistical equivalence between distributional and value-based policy evaluation (known in the tabular case) survives the introduction of function approximation. The answer is yes, with matching sample complexity to classic Linear-TD, which provides theoretical justification for using distributional methods in risk-sensitive RL without incurring additional statistical cost. The preconditioning insight is practically relevant: the vanilla algorithm's step size must decay as 1/K², while Linear-CTD does not, making it more robust to the choice of K. Open questions include breaking the sample-size barrier via variance reduction, extending the analysis to softmax-based (non-signed-measure) parametrizations, and handling the Markovian setting with explicit mixing-time dependencies.
