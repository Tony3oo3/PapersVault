## Problem

Existing LLM watermarking methods rely on heuristic, trial-and-error designs with no formal optimality guarantees, and prior theoretical work optimizes either the watermarking scheme or the detector in isolation. No prior framework jointly optimizes both components, and none exploits the LLM's generative distribution to shape the watermark signal, leaving a gap between achievable detection performance and what is actually attained, especially at ultra-low false positive rates.

## Input / Output

For watermark generation, the method consumes a text prompt, the LLM's next-token prediction distribution at each step, and a shared secret key to produce a watermarked token sequence of length T. For detection, it takes only the received token sequence and a surrogate language model (smaller, possibly from a different model family but sharing the same tokenizer) plus the shared key—no access to the original LLM or prompt is required. The output is a binary decision (watermarked vs. human) with a tunable threshold λ controlling the FPR.

## Method

The paper formulates watermarking as a joint hypothesis-testing problem and derives closed-form jointly optimal solutions for the watermarking scheme and detector, revealing that the optimal auxiliary signal distribution must be adaptive to the LLM's NTP distribution rather than uniform. The practical algorithm DAWA implements a distortion-free (ε=0) token-level optimal scheme: at each position t, the auxiliary variable ζ_t is sampled via the Gumbel-Max trick from a distribution proportional to min(Q_{X_t|x_1^{t-1}}(x), η) over the vocabulary, with excess mass routed to a redundant value that suppresses false positives on low-entropy tokens. Detection uses a hash function h_key to check whether h_key(X_t) equals the reconstructed ζ_t at each position, averaging the indicator over T tokens and comparing to threshold λ. A surrogate language model approximates the NTP distribution at detection time, enabling model-agnostic operation without the original LLM or prompt, while the Gumbel-Max trick ensures the auxiliary sequence can be deterministically recovered from the shared key and preceding tokens.

## Results

DAWA achieves near-perfect detection on clean text, with ROC-AUC of 0.999 and TP@1% FPR of 0.998–0.999 across both Llama2-13B and Mistral-8×7B on C4 and ELI5. Under a 50% token replacement attack, DAWA retains ROC-AUC ≥ 0.989 and TP@1% FPR ≥ 0.860, substantially outperforming all baselines (e.g., HCW+23 drops to TP@1% FPR of 0.268 on Llama2-13B/ELI5). On text quality, DAWA achieves a BLEU score of 0.214 and perplexity of 10.034, closely matching the unwatermarked baseline (10.020) and far exceeding KGW+23 (BLEU 0.158, perplexity 13.472). At ultra-low FPRs (1e-05), DAWA's TPR significantly surpasses all compared methods (Figure 1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DAWA outperforms all four baselines (KGW+23, EXP-edit, Gumbel-Max, HCW+23) on every reported metric across both models and both datasets. The most dramatic gap appears under the 50% token replacement attack on the low-entropy ELI5 dataset: HCW+23's TP@1% FPR collapses to 0.268 (Llama2-13B) and 0.252 (Mistral-8×7B), while DAWA maintains 0.969 and 0.991 respectively. On clean text, DAWA's advantage is smaller but consistent, e.g., achieving TP@1% FPR of 0.997–0.999 versus 0.974–0.993 for the strongest baselines. In terms of text quality, DAWA (BLEU 0.214, perplexity 10.034) is distortion-free and closest to the unwatermarked reference, whereas KGW+23 degrades quality most severely (BLEU 0.158, perplexity 13.472). Gumbel-Max and EXP-edit are competitive on clean-text detection but fall behind DAWA under attack, particularly on ELI5 where DAWA's distribution-adaptive design provides a clear robustness advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of theoretical work on LLM watermarking by Huang et al. [42] and Li et al. [43], which each optimized either the watermarking scheme or the detector in isolation. The key departure is a joint optimization framework that simultaneously designs both the embedding scheme and the detector, yielding closed-form jointly optimal solutions. The paper also departs from the standard practice (in KGW [25], Gumbel-Max [38], and others) of sampling auxiliary watermark signals from fixed uniform distributions, instead showing that optimal schemes must adapt to the LLM's own next-token prediction distribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The distribution-adaptivity insight provides a principled design guideline for future watermarking schemes, moving the field beyond heuristic logit perturbations toward information-theoretically optimal constructions. DAWA's combination of distortion-free generation, model-agnostic detection via a surrogate model, and superior performance at ultra-low FPRs (1e-05) makes it a strong practical baseline. Open questions include robustness against semantic-invariant paraphrasing attacks (only sketched in Appendix J), scalability to multi-bit watermarking, and whether the token-level approximation of the sequence-level optimum incurs a meaningful gap in practice.
