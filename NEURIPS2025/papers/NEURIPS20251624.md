## Problem

Existing KV cache eviction methods for LLM inference suffer from two critical flaws: (1) irreversible token eviction cannot adapt to the dynamic shift in token saliency during decoding, causing globally important tokens to be permanently lost, and (2) marginal tokens—those with modest but collectively significant attention scores—are subjected to the same aggressive eviction as truly unimportant tokens, leading to unnecessary quality degradation. No prior method differentiates among critical, marginal, and unimportant tokens or compensates for the information lost through permanent eviction.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt, a large language model (LLM), an assisted small language model (SLM) from the same model series, and a KV cache budget as inputs, and produces generated text tokens while maintaining a compressed KV cache. The SLM retains its full KV cache throughout inference, while the LLM's cache is hierarchically compressed: critical tokens keep full KV cache, marginal tokens retain only the V cache (with attention approximated from the SLM), and unimportant tokens are evicted. Evicted KV cache is migrated between GPU HBM and CPU memory rather than deleted, and the entire pipeline is compatible with Flash Attention for the critical-token computation path.

## Method

SmallKV exploits the empirically observed high cosine similarity (≈0.95) between attention matrices of same-series LLMs at different scales to provide two compensation mechanisms. During prefill, a similarity matching step establishes a head-to-head mapping between the LLM and SLM using Jaccard similarity of TopK accumulative attention score indices. For saliency shift compensation, the LLM's eviction decisions are made based on the SLM's full (uncompressed) attention view rather than its own compressed cache, preventing the loss of tokens that regain importance during decoding. For marginal information compensation, the LLM retains only the V cache for marginal tokens and approximates their attention scores by multiplying the SLM's corresponding attention scores with the retained V vectors, eliminating the need for K cache on these tokens. The critical-token attention is computed via Flash Attention in parallel with the marginal-token matrix multiplication, and KV cache updates are pipelined with the LLM forward pass to hide latency.

## Results

On LongBench with Qwen2-0.5B assisting Qwen2-7B, SmallKV at 5% KV cache budget achieves 33.14 on Single-Doc QA and 37.54 on Multi-Doc QA, compared to H2O's 19.40 and 24.14, and PyramidInfer's 18.43 and 24.23, while the full-cache baseline scores 39.04 and 40.78. On GSM8K/BBH/MT-Bench, at 5% budget SmallKV maintains a score of 73.0 versus H2O's 36.7 and PyramidInfer's 47.0 (full cache: 79.4). In efficiency evaluation, SmallKV achieves 195.50 tokens/s (3.14×) in multi-user concurrency and 1203.42 tokens/s (2.54×) in long-context settings, outperforming H2O (76.32 and 689.07 tokens/s) and HuggingFace Accelerate with Flash Attention (188.23 and 990.39 tokens/s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SmallKV is compared against two KV cache eviction baselines: H2O (accumulative attention score-based eviction) and PyramidInfer (layer-differentiated budget allocation). SmallKV consistently outperforms both baselines across all evaluated benchmarks (GSM8K, BBH, MT-Bench, LongBench) and all KV cache budgets from 100% down to 5%, with the largest gaps appearing at low budgets. At 5% KV cache on LongBench, SmallKV's Single-Doc QA score of 33.14 exceeds H2O (19.40) by 13.7 points and PyramidInfer (18.43) by 14.7 points, while remaining within 5.9 points of the full-cache baseline (39.04). In efficiency, SmallKV achieves 2.56× higher throughput than H2O in the long-context setting (1203.42 vs. 689.07 tokens/s) and 2.56× in multi-user concurrency (195.50 vs. 76.32 tokens/s), primarily because SmallKV is compatible with Flash Attention while H2O is not. The only scenario where baselines are competitive is at very high budgets (≥30%), where the advantage narrows to 1–4 points, as the marginal token compensation mechanism has less room to differentiate.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the KV cache eviction line of work (H2O, PyramidInfer) by introducing a small language model (SLM) from the same model series to compensate for two failure modes of permanent eviction: saliency shift and marginal token over-compression. The conceptual foundation draws on the BERT2BERT observation [4] that attention patterns are highly similar across scales, which the authors empirically validate for decoder-only LLMs (0.947 cosine similarity for Qwen2-0.5B vs. Qwen2-7B). Unlike prior eviction methods that are incompatible with Flash Attention due to their reliance on accumulated attention scores, SmallKV maintains compatibility with memory-efficient attention kernels and can share the SLM forward pass with speculative decoding, positioning it as a deployable system-level technique rather than a purely algorithmic one.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: 1.75–2.56× throughput gains over H2O while maintaining Flash Attention compatibility makes this directly applicable to production inference stacks. The finding that marginal tokens (5%–15% attention range) cause disproportionate accuracy collapse (0.482→0.148 under H2O) challenges the binary critical/unimportant framing that underpins most eviction methods, suggesting the field needs hierarchical compression strategies. Key limitations include the requirement for same-series SLM/LLM pairs (cross-series similarity drops to 0.56), the non-lossless nature of the approximation, and the unproven theoretical guarantee of attention similarity. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to MoE architectures, whether the SLM can be further compressed without degrading compensation quality, and whether a formal proof of cross-scale attention alignment exists.
