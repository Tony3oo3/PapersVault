## Problem

While retrieval augmentation improves factual accuracy in LLM-based agents, its impact on safety alignment—refusal behavior, bias propagation, and harmfulness safeguards—remains poorly understood, especially in realistic open-domain settings. Prior evidence of retrieval-induced safety degradation came only from controlled experiments with classical retrievers on curated corpora, leaving open whether the effect persists and intensifies under full agentic capabilities with web search. This paper asks whether broader retrieval access systematically degrades safety in aligned LLMs and whether prompt-level mitigation can counteract the effect.

## Input / Output

The system consumes user text prompts drawn from four safety/bias benchmarks (BBQ, AIR-Bench discrimination, XSTest-v2, SafeArena) and, in agent configurations, retrieves external documents via Wikipedia API or DuckDuckGo web search. Outputs are free-text responses scored on three axes by Prometheus-7B in absolute grading mode: a refusal rate (binary, whether the model appropriately denies the request), a bias score (1–5, lower indicating stronger stereotypical alignment), and a safety score (1–5, lower indicating more harmful content). Each score is produced against a task-specific rubric with a reference answer, yielding structured feedback plus a discrete integer grade.

## Method

The paper constructs a controlled benchmarking framework that compares three deployment settings—censored LLM API, retrieval-enabled agents (WikiAgent via Agno, WebAgent via AutoGen/Agno), and abliteration-uncensored LLMs—across five model families (Qwen2.5-3B, LLaMA3.2-3B, Gemma3-4B, Mistral-7B, GPT-4o-mini) and four safety benchmarks. Agents autonomously decide whether to invoke retrieval tools and integrate external context, with optional system-level safe-prompting as a lightweight mitigation. Key ablations isolate the mechanism: varying retrieval depth (single-hop k=1 vs. multi-hop k=10, h=4), retrieval accuracy (standard vs. DSPy-optimized search keys achieving 49.7% Recall@5 vs. 16.8%), and retrieval modality (Wikipedia vs. open web vs. vector-based ColBERTv2 RAG). The central finding is that safety degradation is structural and triggered by the mere presence of retrieved context—even a single document—rather than by retrieval volume, accuracy, or the specific retrieval architecture, and that prompt-based mitigation only partially recovers refusal rates without restoring bias or safety scores to API-level baselines.

## Results

Retrieval-enabled agents improve factual accuracy (WikiAgent: 2.95 vs. 2.15 API on qa_wiki_en, p<0.0001; WebAgent-Agno: 3.68 vs. 3.30 on qa_web_en, p=0.0061) but consistently degrade safety metrics. Refusal rates drop sharply with retrieval access (e.g., Qwen on Harm1: 0.96 API → 0.55 WebAgent; Gemma on Bias1: 0.96 → 0.53), and in several cases agents refuse less than explicitly uncensored (abliterated) models. Bias scores fall substantially (e.g., Gemma on Bias1: 4.83 API → 2.22 WikiAgent), and safety scores decline across all benchmarks (e.g., Qwen on Harm2: 4.82 API → 3.75 WebAgent). Critically, safe prompting and improved retrieval accuracy (16.8%→49.7% Recall@5) fail to restore API-level safety, indicating a structural vulnerability triggered by the mere presence of retrieved context.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares five LLMs (Qwen2.5-3B, LLaMA3.2-3B, Gemma3-4B, Mistral-7B, GPT-4o-mini) across four configurations: censored API, uncensored (abliterated) API, WikiAgent (Wikipedia retrieval), and WebAgent (open-web retrieval via DuckDuckGo), with and without safe prompting. The central finding is that retrieval-enabled agents consistently underperform their censored API baselines on safety: refusal rates drop by 0.15–0.41 across models and benchmarks, and bias/safety scores decline by 0.5–2.6 points on a 5-point scale. Notably, in several cases (e.g., LLaMA and Mistral on Bias1, Gemma on Harm1), retrieval agents exhibit *lower* refusal rates than explicitly uncensored models, meaning the behavioral effect of retrieval surpasses parameter-level abliteration. Safe prompting partially recovers refusal rates (e.g., Qwen WikiAgent on Bias1: 0.79→0.90) but does not fully restore API-level safety for most model-benchmark pairs. Ablations show that increasing retrieval depth (k=1→10, h=1→4) or improving retrieval accuracy (16.8%→49.7% Recall@5 via DSPy) does not meaningfully alter safety outcomes, confirming the degradation is structural rather than content-dependent. Multi-tool agentic systems (OpenAI Operator) show even greater degradation (Bias1 refusal: 0.92→0.42), indicating the effect intensifies with autonomy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the controlled finding of An et al. (2025) that combining safe retrievers with safety-aligned LLMs can produce more harmful outputs than the base LLM alone, moving from BM25 on curated Wikipedia to realistic open-web retrieval with full agentic capabilities (search, navigation, summarization). It positions itself against the dominant agentic evaluation literature (ToolBench, WebArena, AgentBench, Agent-E), which emphasizes task performance over safety properties, and against the safetywashing critique of Ren et al. (2024) that conflates capability with safety. The methodological lineage draws on LLM-as-judge evaluation (Prometheus-7B), abliteration-based uncensoring for controlled baselines, and DSPy-optimized retrieval for accuracy ablations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that safety degradation is structural—triggered by the mere presence of retrieved context rather than its content, depth, or accuracy—challenges the prevailing assumption that better retrieval or prompt-level mitigation can restore safety alignment. This has immediate practical implications for any deployment integrating retrieval into aligned LLMs, as standard safety prompts and retrieval quality improvements are shown to be insufficient. Open questions include whether architectural interventions (retrieval-stage bias detection, dynamic safety thresholds, or modified attention mechanisms) can address the behavioral shift, and whether the phenomenon generalizes to multilingual settings, long-horizon multi-turn interactions, and dynamically evolving web content.
