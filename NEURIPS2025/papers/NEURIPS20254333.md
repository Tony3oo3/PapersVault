## Problem

Existing 3D genomics methods are task-specific and operate on single modalities, failing to integrate the heterogeneous information from Hi-C contact maps and epigenomic tracks into a unified representation. No foundation model exists that captures both shared regulatory mechanisms and modality-specific biological signals across the 3D genome, limiting cross-task knowledge transfer and robustness under data scarcity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254333/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes 50×50 Hi-C contact maps at 5 kb resolution (derived from 250 kb genomic windows) and 5,000-length epigenomic sequences (two 2,500-length ATAC-seq/DNase-seq tracks concatenated, each binned at 100 bp). It produces three types of outputs: a 50×50 predicted Hi-C matrix, a binary chromatin loop label, or 100 CAGE-seq expression values (50 per genomic segment along each axis). Critically, the model supports an inference mode where one modality is absent and its features are reconstructed from the other via the cross-modal mapping block.

## Method

MIX-HIC employs a dual-encoder architecture: a ViT-style patch encoder (patch size 2, three cascaded Transformer+downsampling layers) for Hi-C maps and a CNN+Transformer encoder (four conv layers with max-pooling, then three Transformer+downsampling layers) for epigenomic tracks. The key innovation is the cross-modal interaction block, which uses four independent dense networks to decompose each modality's bottleneck representation into modal-invariant and modal-specific components, regularized by a symmetric contrastive loss on the invariant features and an orthogonal loss that maximizes dissimilarity between invariant and specific features. A cross-modal mapping block then applies 1D adaptive pooling and dense layers to project one modality's concatenated features into the other's sequence length, regularized by an L2 reconstruction loss, enabling robust single-modality inference. The total pre-training objective is L_con + L_orth + L_mapping, and a theoretical analysis (Theorem 1) proves that perfect feature alignment incurs a prediction error gap Γ_q relative to using raw modalities, justifying the explicit separation of shared and unique information. Fine-tuning adds a contact-map-grounded fusion block (self-attention on epigenomic queries, cross-attention with Hi-C keys/values, FFN) followed by task-specific decoders.

## Results

MIX-HIC achieves state-of-the-art performance across all three downstream tasks on both GM12878 and K562 cell lines. For Hi-C contact map prediction, MIX-HIC-Infer reaches R² of 0.8724 (GM12878) and 0.8001 (K562), outperforming the best baseline by 9.3% and 2.1% respectively. For chromatin loop detection, MIX-HIC-Bimodal attains F1 of 0.8420 and AUROC of 0.9209 on GM12878, and F1 of 0.8267 and AUROC of 0.9194 on K562. For CAGE-seq expression prediction, MIX-HIC-Bimodal achieves R² of 0.8833 (GM12878) and 0.9077 (K562), surpassing the best baseline by 3.0% and 4.3%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MIX-HIC outperforms all eight baselines across every task and both cell lines. On Hi-C contact map prediction, MIX-HIC-Infer (R²=0.8724/0.8001) beats Epiphany (0.7970/0.6547), C.Origami (0.7958/0.7055), and both EPCOT variants (0.5409–0.7993/0.7648–0.7840), with the largest margin of 9.3% over EPCOT-LSTM on GM12878. On chromatin loop detection, MIX-HIC-Bimodal surpasses DLoopCaller (F1 0.8250/0.7932) and Peakachu (F1 0.8015/0.7900) on all four metrics, and in whole-chromosome annotation achieves a ChIA-PET-validated proportion of 0.88 (GM12878) and 0.67 (K562) versus 0.64/0.61 for ChromoSight and 0.51/0.53 for HiCExplorer. On CAGE-seq expression prediction, MIX-HIC-Bimodal (R²=0.8833/0.9077) exceeds EPI-Graph (0.7965/0.8211), EPI-CNN (0.7719/0.8033), and both EPCOT variants (0.4723–0.8578/0.8230–0.8704). Notably, EPCOT-Transformer shows high variance across cell lines (R²=0.5409 on GM12878 vs. 0.7648 on K562 for Hi-C prediction), whereas MIX-HIC maintains consistent top performance on both.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of 3D genome prediction methods (Epiphany, C.Origami, EPCOT, Akita) and chromatin loop detection approaches (Peakachu, DLoopCaller) by unifying them under a single multimodal pre-training framework. It draws on the foundation model paradigm from computational biology (ESM-IF, UniCorn, VQDNA) and multimodal contrastive learning, but departs from prior work in two key ways: (1) a cross-modal interaction block that explicitly separates modal-invariant and modal-specific representations, justified by a theoretical information-gap bound (Theorem 1) showing naive alignment is suboptimal, and (2) a cross-modal mapping block that enables imputation of missing Hi-C features from epigenomic tracks alone. The paper also introduces the largest paired Hi-C/epigenomic dataset to date (1.27M samples across four cell lines), which prior single-task methods could not leverage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By providing a unified pre-trained representation across Hi-C contact maps and epigenomic tracks, MIX-HIC addresses the fragmentation of 3D genomics tools where each downstream task (contact map prediction, loop detection, expression prediction) requires a separate model. The cross-modal mapping capability is practically significant given the high cost of Hi-C sequencing, enabling predictions from epigenomic tracks alone. Key limitations include the absence of DNA sequence integration, restriction to bulk-seq data, and a pre-training corpus limited to four cell lines, all of which constrain generalization. Open questions include whether the architecture scales to single-cell multimodal data, whether integrating long-range DNA sequence models (MambaDNA, HyenaDNA) would further improve performance, and whether the orthogonal constraint strategy generalizes to additional modalities beyond the two used here.
