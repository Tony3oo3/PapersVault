## Problem

Reward models for RL-based LLM alignment are expensive to train, requiring large-scale human preference data and powerful pretrained backbones. The paper asks whether simple reference-based string-matching metrics—specifically BLEU—can serve as viable, cost-effective alternatives to learned reward models during reinforcement learning for general instruction following.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250523/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes text instructions (prompts) and produces text responses, using one or more reference responses per instruction to compute BLEU scores as the RL reward signal. Training data consists of 5,000 'hard' examples (those with the lowest initial BLEU scores) drawn from a 50K-prompt pool sampled from the Tulu3 SFT mixture, where references are either human-written or generated by powerful LLMs (e.g., ChatGPT, Claude-3.7-Sonnet). The trained model is evaluated on four instruction-following benchmarks (MT-Bench, ArenaHard v1/v2, WildBench) using LLM-as-a-judge scoring, and on factuality benchmarks (FRESHQA, LONGFACT, ASKHISTORIANS) using VERISCORE.

## Method

BLEUBERI uses Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) with BLEU directly as the reward function, treating BLEU as a verifiable reward within the RLVR paradigm. The key insight is that BLEU with five synthetic references achieves 74.2% agreement with human preferences on Chatbot Arena data, nearly matching a 27B-parameter reward model (75.6%), because BLEU implicitly rewards factuality, proper formatting, and instruction adherence through n-gram overlap. The training procedure first identifies challenging instructions by running the base model on a 50K-prompt pool and selecting the 5K examples with the lowest BLEU scores, then applies GRPO (group size 8, batch size 32) for one epoch on those hard examples. Scoring outputs with BLEU is up to 48× faster than using an 8B reward model, and the approach avoids the instability and quality degradation that plagued earlier BLEU-optimization efforts by leveraging modern base models and the GRPO algorithm.

## Results

Across four base models (Qwen2.5-14B/7B/3B, Llama-3.1-8B) and four instruction-following benchmarks (MT-Bench, ArenaHard v1/v2, WildBench), BLEUBERI achieves average scores within 1–3 points of GRPO-RM (reward-model-guided RL) and consistently outperforms SFT. For example, on Qwen2.5-7B, BLEUBERI scores 44.6 average vs. 45.9 for GRPO-RM and 40.0 for SFT; on Llama-3.1-8B, BLEUBERI (32.2) actually edges out GRPO-RM (31.6). BLEU with five synthetic references reaches 74.2% agreement with human preferences on Chatbot Arena, close to the 27B reward model's 77.6%. Factuality evaluation via VERISCORE shows BLEUBERI outperforms both GRPO-RM and SFT on LONGFACT (89.0 vs. 87.0 vs. 81.6 F1@K) and ASKHISTORIANS (54.2 vs. 50.5 vs. 51.4).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GRPO-RM (GRPO with an 8B reward model), BLEUBERI is broadly competitive: on Qwen2.5-7B the average gap is only 1.3 points (44.6 vs. 45.9) with overlapping confidence intervals on three of four benchmarks, and on Llama-3.1-8B BLEUBERI actually wins by 0.6 points (32.2 vs. 31.6). Against SFT, BLEUBERI consistently outperforms by 2–5 average points across all four base models, confirming that RL with a BLEU reward adds value beyond imitation. Against GRPO with random rewards (Shao et al. 2025), BLEUBERI shows clear gains (e.g., 44.6 vs. 35.3 on Qwen2.5-7B), indicating the BLEU signal is meaningful rather than spurious. Against BERTScore as an alternative reward, BLEUBERI achieves comparable performance (45.3 vs. 44.2 average on Qwen2.5-7B with 5 references). All methods remain below the official Instruct checkpoints (e.g., Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct at 51.1), which are trained on substantially more data and compute, but BLEUBERI narrows that gap to within ~6 points using only 5K training examples and no reward model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two recent lines of work: the RLVR paradigm (Lambert et al., 2024), which showed that simple verifiable rewards can replace learned reward models for constrained tasks, and GRPO (Shao et al., 2024), a group-relative policy gradient algorithm that stabilizes RL training. The key departure is from the long-standing assumption that n-gram metrics like BLEU are fundamentally inadequate for open-ended generation—a view reinforced by decades of MT and summarization literature. Prior attempts to use BLEU as an RL reward (MIXER, Minimum Risk Training, REINFORCE variants) failed due to training instability and output degradation, but this paper argues that modern base LLMs and GRPO overcome those historical obstacles, repurposing a 20-year-old evaluation metric as a practical alignment reward.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BLEUBERI offers a concrete, low-cost path to instruction-following alignment that entirely bypasses the expensive pipeline of collecting human preference data and training large reward models—replacing it with a string-matching score computed in under 5ms per example. The finding that BLEU-trained models produce more factually grounded outputs than reward-model-aligned ones (per VERISCORE) suggests the reward signal may be capturing something reward models miss. However, the method is sensitive to reference quality, performs poorly on math/reasoning and multilingual tasks, and has only been validated at moderate scales (3B–14B, 5K training examples). Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger models and broader task distributions, and whether combining BLEU with other verifiable signals (as the BLEU+RM result hints) can close the remaining gap to dedicated reward models.
