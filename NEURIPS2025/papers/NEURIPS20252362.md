## Problem

The relationship between Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and preference optimization (e.g., DPO) in LLM post-training has been understood primarily through empirical observation, with SFT often treated as a preparatory warm-up step lacking theoretical grounding. A critical gap persists: how these two stages fundamentally relate within a unified framework, and whether the KL regularization term in conventional SFT actually constrains model updates during optimization or merely acts as a constant offset.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252362/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework takes a base LLM (Llama3-8B, Mistral-7B, or Qwen2.5-3B) and consumes expert demonstrations (UltraChat 200K) for the SFT phase followed by preference pairs (UltraFeedback) for the DPO phase. The output is a fine-tuned policy model whose logits implicitly encode a Q-function over the learned reward, enabling state-value estimation via log-sum-exp without Monte Carlo sampling. A key structural detail is that the SFT objective, when derived from total variation f-divergence, reduces to standard MLE where the KL term appears only as a zero-order constant that provides no gradient signal.

## Method

The paper proves that conventional SFT is a special case of implicit reward learning by reformulating the KL-regularized distribution matching objective as a min-max problem over f-divergence, where the inner policy maximization admits a closed-form solution via the maximum-entropy RL fixed point. Selecting total variation as the f-divergence yields the standard SFT loss, revealing that the KL term vanishes as a constant during gradient descent and fails to constrain updates. To compensate, they propose reducing the SFT learning rate (e.g., from 2e-5 to 5e-6) to implicitly limit step size, and derive alternative objectives from Pearson χ² and Squared Hellinger divergences that preserve the KL term in the gradient. They further extend the DPO logits-to-Q-function mapping to SFT, showing that model logits correspond to Q-values under the implicit reward up to a state-dependent constant, validated through Kendall rank correlation of value estimates across independently trained models.

## Results

On Mistral-7B, reducing the SFT learning rate from 2e-5 to 1e-6 and then applying DPO yields a 5.9-point absolute improvement in AlpacaEval2 LC-WR (15.6% → 21.5%) and a 9.9-point gain in Arena-Hard WR (11.7% → 21.6%), representing up to 25% relative improvement. On Llama-3-8B, the same strategy with SimPO achieves 28.5% LC-WR and 25.0% WR on AlpacaEval2, surpassing the standard SFT+SimPO baseline (23.5% / 21.3%) by 5.0 and 3.7 points respectively. Alternative f-divergence objectives (Pearson χ², Squared Hellinger) that preserve the KL term during SFT provide additional post-DPO gains of up to 4.2 points in WR on Mistral (20.9% vs 16.7% for standard SFT+DPO).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's core comparison is between standard SFT (lr=2e-5) and smaller-LR SFT, each followed by DPO or SimPO. On Mistral-7B, smaller-LR SFT+DPO outperforms standard SFT+DPO by +5.9 LC-WR and +9.9 Arena-Hard WR; on Llama-3-8B, smaller-LR SFT+SimPO beats standard SFT+SimPO by +5.0 LC-WR and +4.0 Arena-Hard WR. Against the nine reference preference-optimization methods listed in Table 2 (R-DPO, ORPO, KTO, IPO, CPO, SLiC-HF, RRHF), the proposed SFT(smaller lr)+SimPO on Llama-3 (28.5% LC, 25.0% WR) exceeds all of them, with the closest competitor being R-DPO (17.4% LC, 12.8% WR). For the alternative f-divergence SFT objectives (Table 4), Pearson-χ² SFT+DPO on Llama-3 achieves 24.7% Arena-Hard WR, outperforming standard SFT+DPO (21.1%) by 3.6 points, while Squared Hellinger SFT+DPO on Mistral reaches 20.9% WR (+4.2 over standard SFT+DPO). Notably, the SFT checkpoints themselves show only modest gains (e.g., Mistral WR 3.6→3.7), confirming that the KL-term preservation primarily benefits the subsequent preference-optimization stage rather than the SFT stage in isolation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies SFT and DPO under a single implicit-reward framework by showing that standard SFT (cross-entropy on expert demonstrations) is the special case of f-divergence-based distribution matching when total variation is chosen, directly extending the implicit-reward formulation of DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023) and the non-adversarial IRL derivations of Garg et al. (2021) and Wulfmeier et al. (2024). It builds on the f-divergence conjugate-function machinery of Nowozin et al. (2016) to derive alternative SFT objectives (Pearson χ², Squared Hellinger) that preserve the KL regularization term, and extends the logits-as-Q-function result of Rafailov et al. (2024) from the DPO setting to SFT. The work goes beyond Sun (2024), who formulated KL-regularized SFT as IRL, by explicitly connecting the SFT implicit reward to the DPO implicit reward and identifying the KL-term absence as a concrete optimization pathology.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical unification clarifies why the sequential SFT→DPO pipeline works and exposes a concrete flaw: the KL term in standard SFT is a zero-order constant that provides no gradient constraint, meaning the policy can drift far from the base model before DPO begins. The practical remedy—simply reducing the SFT learning rate by 4–20×—yields up to 25% relative win-rate gains post-DPO, a finding that is cheap to adopt and broadly applicable to any sequential post-training setup. However, the evidence is limited to three 3–8B models on instruction-following, the multi-objective SFT+DPO attempt failed (suggesting inherent objective conflict), and several f-divergence variants (KL, JS) suffer numerical instability, leaving open whether the framework generalizes to reasoning tasks, larger models, or non-sequential training paradigms.
