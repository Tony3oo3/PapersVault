## Problem

Existing 3D animal pose estimation methods face a fundamental trade-off: keypoint-based approaches are sparse and require labor-intensive manual annotation, while mesh-based methods demand per-frame optimization and species-specific template models. This makes large-scale, longitudinal behavioral analysis impractical, particularly for studying subtle movements across diverse species. The paper asks whether a feed-forward, annotation-free framework can reconstruct complete 3D animal geometry and appearance from sparse multi-view images without any of these bottlenecks.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 4–6 calibrated camera views (1536×2048 RGB at 30 FPS, downsampled 4× spatially and 5× temporally) along with SAM2-generated foreground masks and known camera intrinsics/extrinsics. It produces per-voxel 3D Gaussian parameters (position via de-voxelized displacement, 3×3 covariance, RGB color, opacity) that are rendered into novel-view images, plus a 50-dimensional rotation-invariant visual embedding derived from 32 spherical renderings encoded by a pretrained ResNet-18 and reduced via spherical harmonic expansion (bandwidth L=3) followed by adversarial PCA. The entire forward pass runs in ~30 ms on a single GPU using only 2.5 GB VRAM.

## Method

Pose Splatter carves a coarse voxel volume by back-projecting multi-view masks into a shared 3D grid (visual hull), then refines it through a stacked 3D U-Net (three sequential modules with skip connections, initialized near the identity) that outputs 8 channels per voxel: one occupancy probability and seven feature channels. A compact per-voxel MLP maps these features to Gaussian parameters, critically adding a learned 3D displacement to the voxel coordinate to de-voxelize the representation before standard Gaussian splatting renders the scene. Training is end-to-end with L1 color loss and IoU silhouette loss, requiring no manual labels or per-frame optimization. For downstream behavioral analysis, the paper introduces a rotation-invariant visual embedding: 32 virtual cameras on a sphere produce appearance-only renderings, each encoded by ResNet-18 into a 512-d vector, expanded in a truncated spherical harmonic basis (L=3), with squared-magnitude coefficients eliminating azimuthal phase dependence, and finally projected to 50 dimensions via adversarial PCA that suppresses residual encoding of the animal's horizontal rotation angle.

## Results

On the 5-camera sparse-view benchmark (Table 1a), Pose Splatter achieves IoU 0.760 / PSNR 29.0 / SSIM 0.982 on Mouse and IoU 0.848 / PSNR 34.5 / SSIM 0.992 on Zebra Finch, outperforming all per-scene optimization and feed-forward 3DGS baselines on every metric. In the sparser 4-camera setting (Table 1b), Pose Splatter matches or slightly exceeds GaussianObject (the strongest optimization baseline) on both species. Inference requires only ~30 ms per frame and 2.5 GB VRAM, versus ~1 hour of test-time optimization for GaussianObject.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Pose Splatter outperforms all eight baselines on the primary 5-camera benchmark. Against per-scene optimization methods, it beats 3DGS (IoU 0.760 vs 0.502 on Mouse) and FSGS (IoU 0.760 vs 0.462) by large margins, and edges out GaussianObject (IoU 0.760 vs 0.732 on Mouse; 0.848 vs 0.819 on Finch) while being over 100,000× faster at inference (30 ms vs ~1 hour). Against feed-forward 3DGS baselines, Pose Splatter substantially outperforms PixelSplat (IoU 0.760 vs 0.424) and MVSplat (IoU 0.760 vs 0.417), which fail to reconstruct the animal geometry due to minimal inter-view overlap. In the sparser 4-camera setting, Pose Splatter still matches or slightly exceeds GaussianObject on both species. Against single-view mesh predictors MagicPony and 3D Fauna, Pose Splatter qualitatively preserves plausible anatomy from all novel viewpoints while those methods collapse when rotated away from their input view. Finally, against AnySplat (zero-shot and fine-tuned), Pose Splatter achieves substantially higher IoU (e.g., 0.760 vs 0.431 fine-tuned on Mouse 5-cam) and PSNR (29.0 vs 25.5), confirming that a lightweight task-specific model outperforms large general-purpose 3D reconstruction models on sparse-view animal data.

## Relation to Prior Work

Pose Splatter sits at the intersection of classical shape carving (visual hull) and modern 3D Gaussian Splatting, repurposing the former as a lightweight volumetric prior that a stacked 3D U-Net refines before a small MLP de-voxelizes into Gaussian parameters. It departs from keypoint-based pipelines (SLEAP, DeepLabCut) that require labor-intensive manual annotation and capture only sparse landmarks, and from mesh-based methods (3D Menagerie, SMAL, ArMo) that demand species-specific templates and per-frame optimization. Unlike existing feed-forward 3DGS approaches (PixelSplat, MVSplat) that assume substantial inter-view overlap, Pose Splatter is explicitly designed for the sparse, low-overlap multi-view geometry typical of fixed-camera animal arenas, and introduces a rotation-invariant visual embedding via spherical harmonic expansion and adversarial PCA as a plug-in descriptor for downstream behavioral analysis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating both manual annotation and per-frame optimization, Pose Splatter removes the two major bottlenecks that have limited large-scale, longitudinal behavioral studies to small cohorts, opening the door to high-resolution mapping of genotype, neural activity, and behavior across thousands of animals. The visual embedding's ability to capture subtle pose variations (feather expansion, head-shaking bouts) and outperform manually annotated 3D keypoints in nearest-neighbor preference and behavior classification suggests it could become a standard descriptor in the field. Key limitations remain: the method handles only single animals (no multi-animal occlusion), requires 4–6 calibrated cameras with even lighting, and the visual embedding's interpretability and cross-species comparability are still open questions.
