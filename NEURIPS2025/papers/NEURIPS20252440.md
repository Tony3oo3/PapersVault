## Problem

Existing global GNN explainers rely on motif or subgraph discovery, which is NP-hard, breaks down on large real-world graphs with high-dimensional continuous node attributes where exact subgraph repetition is rare, and produces visualizations that exceed human cognitive limits. No prior method provides scalable, high-fidelity global explanations for node classification on large attributed graphs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252440/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a trained GNN (GAT, GCN, or GraphSAGE), the input graph with node attributes, the GNN-learned node embeddings, and the GNN's class predictions for all training nodes. It produces, per class, a global Boolean formula that is a disjunction over exemplar-specific signatures, where each signature is a natural language rule over interpretable predicates (e.g., node feature thresholds, degree constraints, neighborhood class-label fractions). Crucially, the LLM receives only distribution-level summaries per hop (class-label frequency distributions and average per-attribute L1 distances) rather than raw node data, so the prompt context size is independent of graph density.

## Method

GNNXEMPLAR has two stages. First, exemplar identification is cast as a coverage-maximization problem over reverse k-nearest neighbors in the GNN embedding space; the paper proves the objective is NP-hard and submodular, and provides a greedy algorithm with a (1−1/e) approximation guarantee, further accelerated to O(n) via a sampling-based approximation with Chernoff-bound guarantees. Second, for each selected exemplar, a self-refining LLM pipeline (Gemini-1.5-Flash) iteratively generates and refines a Python function encoding a Boolean rule over the exemplar's neighborhood properties, using feedback from misclassified positive/negative nodes until validation accuracy exceeds a threshold or a maximum iteration count is reached; the final code is then translated into a natural language rule. The key design choice is operating in the learned embedding space with distribution-level neighborhood summaries rather than performing exact subgraph isomorphism on the raw graph, which simultaneously avoids the NP-hardness of motif discovery and keeps the LLM context bounded regardless of graph scale.

## Results

GNNXEMPLAR achieves the highest fidelity across all eight benchmarks, ranging from 0.78 (WikiCS) to 0.93 (BA-Shapes), with a mean of approximately 0.86. The closest competing baseline, GCNeuron, reaches at most 0.56 fidelity (Amazon-ratings) and fails with out-of-memory errors on three of eight datasets (WikiCS, ogbn-arxiv, Questions). GNNInterpreter is not applicable (NA) on six of eight datasets due to its reliance on discrete node attributes, and GLGExplainer fails to generate any formula (NF) on four datasets or crashes with OOM on three others.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GNNXEMPLAR decisively outperforms all three baselines on every dataset where a comparison is possible. Against GNNInterpreter [42], which relies on subgraph isomorphism and is restricted to discrete node attributes, GNNXEMPLAR achieves 0.83 vs. 0.50 on Citeseer and 0.93 vs. 0.47 on BA-Shapes, while GNNInterpreter is simply inapplicable (NA) on six of eight datasets. Against GCNeuron [45], which identifies neuron-activating subgraph concepts, GNNXEMPLAR gains 0.32–0.42 in fidelity on the five datasets where GCNeuron runs (e.g., 0.83 vs. 0.51 on TAGCora, 0.82 vs. 0.56 on Amazon-ratings), and GCNeuron additionally crashes with OOM on WikiCS, ogbn-arxiv, and Questions. Against GLGExplainer [2], the only prior global logical explainer, GNNXEMPLAR achieves 0.64–0.66 higher fidelity on the two datasets where GLGExplainer produces a formula (0.86 vs. 0.22 on Minesweeper, 0.93 vs. 0.30 on BA-Shapes), while GLGExplainer fails entirely (NF or OOM) on the remaining six datasets. The paper attributes the baselines' failures to their reliance on exact subgraph repetition and subgraph isomorphism, which break down in large graphs with high-dimensional continuous node attributes.

## Relation to Prior Work

GNNXEMPLAR departs from the dominant motif-discovery paradigm in global GNN explainers (GNNInterpreter, GCNeuron, GLGExplainer, MAGE, XGNN), which rely on exact subgraph isomorphism and are designed for small graph-classification datasets. Instead, it repurposes Exemplar Theory from cognitive psychology to identify representative nodes in the GNN embedding space, and builds on the Self-Refine iterative LLM framework (Madaan et al., 2023) to distill neighborhood-level patterns into natural language Boolean rules. The exemplar selection step is grounded in submodular coverage maximization over reverse k-NN, providing a (1−1/e) approximation guarantee that distinguishes it from heuristic prototype selection in prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This paper fills a concrete gap: no prior global explainer handles node classification on large graphs with high-dimensional continuous attributes, where motif-based methods are either inapplicable or intractable. The diagnostic-power finding (Sec. 4.6), where the explanation reveals the GNN learned an incorrect homophilous shortcut for a heterophilous class, suggests these rules can serve as model-audit tools beyond mere post-hoc justification. Key limitations include operating only in the embedding space (no access to internal activations, limiting mechanistic interpretability) and dependence on LLM quality for rule generation; the authors flag mechanistic interpretability as a natural next step.
