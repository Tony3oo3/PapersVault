## Problem

Standard RL applied to vision-language models produces abstract, ungrounded reasoning that collapses onto reward-maximizing shortcuts rather than developing region-level visual strategies. The paper identifies that base VLMs lack the spatially anchored cognitive behaviors (region exploration, visual subgoal setting, backtracking) that RL can only amplify if they already exist in the sampling distribution, leaving open how to bootstrap these behaviors before RL training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255049/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image (resized up to 3600×3600 for high-resolution tasks) and a natural language query, and produces a sequence of reasoning steps where each step is a natural language thought paired with an (x, y) point coordinate anchoring it to a specific image location, culminating in a final answer. In multi-turn mode, the model can issue tool calls requesting a w×w crop centered at its predicted coordinate, resized to r×r pixels, which is returned as a new visual observation before the next reasoning step. Point-based grounding (rather than bounding boxes) is used throughout, with the model handling larger regions via multiple overlapping crops.

## Method

ViGoRL redefines each reasoning step as a tuple n_t = ⟨s_t, (x_t, y_t)⟩, explicitly coupling textual thought to spatial coordinates, and trains via a two-stage pipeline. First, MCTS with a frozen Qwen2.5-VL-72B teacher generates ~30k grounded reasoning traces from 1,500 prompts, producing diverse paths with exploration, verification, and backtracking behaviors that are linearized into direct and corrected chains for SFT warm-start. Second, GRPO refines the policy with a composite reward combining format validity (all coordinate references must be valid) and task correctness. A multi-turn RL extension adds a visual feedback loop where the model requests zoomed-in crops via tool calls, with a grammar reward enforcing strict dialog structure and a diversity bonus encouraging distinct coordinate queries, enabling fine-grained visual search on small elements.

## Results

On spatial reasoning, ViGoRL-3B achieves 62.9% on SAT-2 (+12.9 over vanilla GRPO, +16.8 over base) and 48.5% on BLINK; ViGoRL-7B further improves to 67.5% (SAT-2) and 54.1% (BLINK). On visual search, multi-turn ViGoRL-7B reaches 86.4% on V*Bench, surpassing GPT-4o (66.0%) and Sketchpad-GPT-4o (80.3%). For GUI grounding, ViGoRL-7B scores 91.0% on ScreenSpot-V2 and 33.1% on ScreenSpot-Pro, outperforming all open-source web-grounding models of comparable size. On VisualWebArena (vision-only), ViGoRL-7B achieves 11.2%, beating the previous SOTA ICAL-7B (8.2%) despite using only image input without HTML text.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ViGoRL-7B outperforms all open-source web-grounding baselines on ScreenSpot-Pro (33.1% vs. OS-Atlas-7B 18.9%, UGround-V1-7B 31.1%, UI-TARS-2B 27.7%, UI-R1-3B 17.8%, CogAgent-18B 7.7%, ShowUI-2B 7.7%), though Kimi-VL-16B-MoE (34.5%) retains a slight edge. On V*Bench, multi-turn ViGoRL-7B (86.4%) decisively beats all tool-using pipelines (Sketchpad-GPT-4o 80.3%, VisProg 41.4%, MM-React 41.4%, VisualChatGPT 37.6%) and proprietary models (GPT-4o 66.0%, Gemini-Pro 48.2%). On spatial reasoning, ViGoRL-7B matches or exceeds GPT-4o on RoboSpatial (76.4% vs. 76.2%) and BLINK (54.1% vs. 60.0%—a loss), while ViGoRL-3B surpasses Molmo on RoboSpatial (67.1% vs. 67.1%, tie) and InstructBLIP on BLINK (48.5% vs. 42.2%). On VisualWebArena, ViGoRL-7B (11.2%) beats ICAL-7B (8.2%) despite ICAL having access to HTML-derived text. The method consistently outperforms its own base model (Qwen2.5-VL) and vanilla GRPO across all benchmarks, with the largest gains on SAT-2 (+12.9 over GRPO) and V*Bench (+8.4 over base Qwen2.5-7B-VL).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RL-over-chains-of-thought paradigm (GRPO from DeepSeekMath) to vision-language models, but identifies a critical gap: naive RL on VLMs produces ungrounded, abstract reasoning rather than the region-level visual behaviors needed for spatial tasks. It departs from prompt-based visual reasoning pipelines (ViperGPT, VisualProg, V*) that use fixed, non-adaptive reasoning chains, and from SFT-only distillation methods (STaR, LLAVA-CoT, ICAL) that ignore failure paths. The key methodological contribution is a two-stage pipeline—MCTS-generated grounded traces for warm-start SFT, followed by GRPO with explicit spatial grounding in each reasoning step—plus a novel multi-turn RL formulation where the model requests zoomed-in crops as visual feedback, which has no direct precedent in prior VLM RL work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that explicit spatial grounding is not merely an interpretability aid but a functional cognitive scaffold that amplifies exploration, subgoal setting, verification, and backtracking in VLMs—behaviors that standard RL actively suppresses. This reframes how the community should think about bootstrapping RL for multimodal models: the initial policy distribution must be biased toward grounded reasoning before RL can amplify it. Open questions include whether intermediate (step-level) rewards can further improve grounding accuracy (currently 72.8%), whether the approach generalizes beyond the Qwen2.5-VL family, and how to make the model adapt reasoning depth to task complexity rather than always generating long chains.
