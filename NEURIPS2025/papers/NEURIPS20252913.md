## Problem

Existing automatic rigging methods either predict joint positions without modeling connectivity or determine connectivity as a separate post-hoc step, leading to topological errors such as unconnected joints, missing bone chains, and unrealistic deformations. No prior work integrates skeletal connectivity directly into the autoregressive generation process or applies preference-based alignment to improve topology quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252913/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D mesh as input, samples 20,000 surface points with normals, and encodes them via a pre-trained Michelangelo encoder into a shape condition vector. It outputs a skeleton tree (discretized joint positions with explicit parent-child connectivity encoded via special tokens <E1> and <E2> in a BFS-ordered sequence) and per-vertex skinning weights over the top-k=6 most influential bones. Joint coordinates are n-bit quantized in a normalized [-0.5, 0.5]³ space, and child joints under each parent are sorted by z-axis coordinate as a spatial prior.

## Method

Auto-Connect introduces a connectivity-preserving tokenization that traverses the skeleton tree in BFS order, inserting special tokens <E1> (end of a parent's children) and <E2> (end of a hierarchical level) so that parent-child relationships are automatically recovered during detokenization without heuristic rules. An autoregressive transformer (RigFormer, 24 layers, 1024-dim, 16 heads) with cross-attention shape conditioning and level position embeddings generates the token sequence via next-token prediction. A post-training stage applies reward-guided DPO using a topology-aware reward combining Chamfer Distance, Tree Edit Distance, and Hierarchical Jaccard Similarity, with an auxiliary SFT loss to prevent overfitting. Finally, a plug-and-play geodesic-aware bone probability module (3-layer MLP) takes vertex positions, normals, joint coordinates, and geodesic distances from both raw and Laplacian-smoothed meshes to dynamically select the top-k influential bones, replacing static k-nearest-bone selection in existing skinning pipelines.

## Results

On Art-XL2.0, Auto-Connect achieves 82.8% IoU, 85.3% Precision, and 82.9% Recall for rigging, with CD-J2J of 2.57%, CD-J2B of 2.03%, and CD-B2B of 1.68%. On ModelsResource (MR), the method reaches 73.1% IoU, 74.0% Precision, and 76.8% Recall with CD-J2J of 3.20%. For skinning on Art-XL2.0, integrating the geodesic-aware module into HeterSkinNet yields 89.4% Precision, 61.1% Recall, and 0.42 avg L1-norm error, outperforming all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Auto-Connect substantially outperforms all five baselines on both datasets. On Art-XL2.0, it surpasses the strongest baseline Magic-spatial by +4.2% IoU (82.8% vs 78.7%), +5.2% Precision (85.3% vs 80.0%), and +2.9% Recall (82.9% vs 80.1%), while reducing CD-J2J by 15% (2.57% vs 3.04%). Against UniRig, the gains are even larger: +7.2% IoU and +7.9% Precision. RigNet is far behind, achieving only 21.1% IoU on Art-XL2.0 due to its non-generative clustering+MST pipeline. On MR, Auto-Connect (trained on MR) achieves 73.1% IoU, outperforming the best cross-dataset baseline Magic-spatial* (63.8% IoU) by +9.3% IoU and +16.2% Precision, demonstrating strong generalization. For skinning, the geodesic-aware module consistently improves all three baselines (RigNet, NeuroSkinning, HeterSkinNet) by 1–2% Precision and 0.01–0.04 in avg L1-norm error, confirming its plug-and-play effectiveness.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the autoregressive rigging paradigm initiated by MagicArticulate and UniRig, but departs fundamentally in how skeletal topology is represented. Where prior autoregressive methods either ignore parent-child connectivity (MagicArticulate) or rely on heuristic post-hoc connection rules (UniRig's DFS bone chains), Auto-Connect embeds connectivity directly into the token sequence via BFS traversal with special endpoint tokens (<E1>, <E2>), making topology an intrinsic property of the generation process rather than a separate reconstruction step. The post-training stage repurposes DPO—previously confined to LLM/VLM alignment—into a rigging-specific preference optimization with a topology-aware reward, and the skinning stage introduces a geodesic-aware bone selection module that is orthogonal to and composable with existing skinning architectures (RigNet, NeuroSkinning, HeterSkinNet).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The connectivity-preserving tokenization offers a clean, general solution to a persistent failure mode in auto-rigging (disconnected or missing bone chains), and the plug-and-play geodesic module can immediately improve any existing top-k skinning pipeline without retraining the full model. The first successful application of DPO to rigging opens a path toward preference-driven quality control for other structured 3D generation tasks (e.g., mesh topology, texture layout). Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to highly elongated or non-tree topologies (the authors note poor performance on snake-like characters), whether the DPO reward can be extended beyond topology to capture animation quality, and whether the Michelangelo shape encoder bottleneck limits scalability to very high-resolution meshes.
