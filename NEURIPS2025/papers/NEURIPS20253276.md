## Problem

VLA models that leverage VLM pretrained knowledge for generalized manipulation suffer from low execution frequency due to billion-scale autoregressive generation, while existing dual-system approaches that separate a fast policy head (System 1) from a VLM reasoner (System 2) prevent the fast module from inheriting the VLM's rich pretrained knowledge. The paper asks whether a single VLM can internally host both slow reasoning and fast execution, enabling seamless coordination without an independently trained policy head.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253276/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

System 2 (low-frequency) consumes 224×224 RGB images and language instructions, while System 1 (high-frequency) additionally receives 1024-point 3D point clouds derived from single-view depth maps and the robot's proprioceptive state, all conditioned on System 2's intermediate LLM latent features. The model outputs temporally extended continuous action chunks (default H=8) in 7-DoF end-effector pose space (Δx, Δy, Δz, Euler angles, gripper) for single-arm or 14/16-DoF joint/end-effector commands for dual-arm robots, achieving 117.7 Hz control frequency on an NVIDIA 4090.

## Method

FiS-VLA repurposes the final transformer blocks of an intact 7B LLaMA2-based Prismatic VLM as the System 1 execution module, so that System 1 inherits the VLM's pretrained weights and directly reads System 2's intermediate latent representations without altering the model's connectivity. The two systems operate at an asynchronous 1:4 frequency ratio with heterogeneous modality inputs: System 2 processes 2D images and language for high-level reasoning, while System 1 fuses 3D point clouds (tokenized via a lightweight 3D tokenizer and encoded by the shared SigLIP+DINOv2 vision encoder), 2D images, and robot state for geometry-aware reactive control. A dual-aware co-training strategy jointly optimizes a diffusion denoising loss (for System 1's continuous action generation) and an autoregressive next-token prediction loss (preserving System 2's discrete action or language planning capability), preventing catastrophic forgetting of the VLM's reasoning. The model is pretrained on 860K+ cross-embodiment trajectories and fine-tuned on self-collected simulation and real-world data.

## Results

On the RLBench simulation benchmark (10 tasks, Franka Panda), FiS-VLA achieves a mean success rate of 69% (±0.03) at 21.9 Hz control frequency, surpassing CogACT (61%, 9.8 Hz) by 8% and π0 (55%, 13.8 Hz) by 14%. In real-world dual-arm experiments, FiS-VLA reaches 68% mean success on the Agilex robot and 74% on the AlphaBot, outperforming π0 (59% and 61% respectively) by 9% and 13%. With an action chunk of 8, FiS-VLA achieves a 117.7 Hz control frequency on an NVIDIA 4090 GPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FiS-VLA outperforms all four baselines on RLBench. Against CogACT [22] (the closest dual-system competitor, also 7B LLM), FiS-VLA gains 8% in mean success rate (69% vs 61%) while running 2.2× faster (21.9 Hz vs 9.8 Hz). Against π0 [23] (2.6B LLM, synchronous dual-system), FiS-VLA leads by 14% in success rate and 1.6× in speed. Against OpenVLA [7] (autoregressive VLA), the gap is 29% in success rate and 3.5× in speed. Against ManipLLM [48], FiS-VLA leads by 31% in success rate and 10× in speed. In real-world dual-arm tasks, FiS-VLA consistently beats π0 across all 8 tasks on both Agilex and AlphaBot platforms, with the largest margin (20%) on the deformable-object 'Fold towel' task. In generalization tests (unseen objects, cluttered backgrounds, varying lighting), FiS-VLA exhibits smaller performance drops than π0, e.g., only 19% accuracy loss on unseen objects vs 38% for π0 on AlphaBot.

## Relation to Prior Work

FiS-VLA extends the dual-system VLA paradigm (HIRT, CogACT, π0, Helix) by departing from the standard design of attaching an independent, untrained policy head as System 1. Instead, it repurposes the final transformer blocks of the intact Prismatic VLM backbone as the fast execution module, so that System 1 inherits the VLM's internet-scale pretrained knowledge and maintains coherent access to System 2's intermediate reasoning representations. The 3D point cloud processing follows the shared-vision-encoder strategy from LIFT3D, and the lightweight 3D tokenizer is adapted from a non-parametric segmentation method. The dual-aware co-training objective (diffusion denoising + autoregressive next-token prediction) is a novel combination that addresses catastrophic forgetting when embedding action generation within a language model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified architecture addresses a practical bottleneck in VLA deployment: the gap between reasoning quality and control frequency. Achieving 117.7 Hz with action chunk 8 while outperforming prior SOTA by 8–11% suggests that the separate-module design was leaving performance on the table. However, the static configuration of shared blocks (2 saturates) and the fixed 1:4 frequency ratio are acknowledged limitations; the authors hypothesize that dynamic adaptation based on task complexity could further improve robustness. Open questions include whether the benefit of parameter sharing scales to larger VLM backbones, whether the approach generalizes to long-horizon multi-stage tasks requiring subtask decomposition, and how the method performs under truly dynamic (moving-object) scenarios beyond the tabletop settings tested here.
