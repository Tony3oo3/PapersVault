## Problem

Neural B-frame video compression (NBVC) remains underexplored relative to P-frame compression, and existing condition-based NBVC methods simply concatenate contexts from both reference frames without addressing the unbalanced reference contribution (URC) issue. In hierarchical B-frame coding, large frame spans at initial levels make one reference frame substantially more informative than the other (up to 20.6% BD-rate gap at span 32), yet no prior method explicitly models the relative weights of the two references.

## Input / Output

The encoder receives the current RGB frame x_t, two decoded reference frames x̂_f and x̂_b, and their extracted features F_f, F_b, with the frame span determined by the hierarchical level (2^(6-level) for IP=32). Multi-scale optical flows are computed via SpyNet at three resolutions (full, half, quarter) in both directions. The output is a compressed bitstream produced by arithmetic coding with a quadtree partition-based entropy model, and the decoder reconstructs the frame using the decoded latent ŷ_t aligned to pixel space via a Pixel Feature Alignment module.

## Method

BRHVC introduces two key modules on a DCVC-B/DCVC-DC backbone. Bi-directional Motion Converge (BMC) compresses multi-scale optical flows by converging them into a single latent variable and fusing directional latents, enriched with flow features from the decoded buffer as priors (via a Motion Feature Adapter that handles B-frame vs. I-frame cases), enabling more accurate motion compensation over large spans. Bi-directional Contextual Fusion (BCF) explicitly models per-region weights s_f^i, s_b^i for each reference context at three scales, guided by pixel-domain similarity between the current frame and motion-compensated references, plus an independent supplementary bias b_t^i to handle regions where neither reference is useful. BCF-E uses the ground-truth current frame for guidance while BCF-D uses the decoded latent ŷ_t with pixel-feature alignment, ensuring encoder-decoder consistency. The trained model achieves 32.0% average BD-rate saving on HEVC, surpassing VTM-RA's 30.9%.

## Results

BRHVC achieves an average BD-rate of -27.3% (PSNR) compared to VTM-LDB across all six test datasets (HEVC B–E, MCL-JCV, UVG). On the HEVC datasets specifically, BRHVC reaches -32.0% BD-rate, surpassing VTM-RA (-30.9%) and outperforming the best prior NVC method DCVC-FM (-23.7%) by 8.3 percentage points. The largest gain is on HEVC Class D, where BRHVC saves 44.7% bitrate versus 34.2% for DCVC-FM and 30.2% for VTM-RA. On high-frame-rate datasets (MCL-JCV, UVG), BRHVC still leads all NVC methods but trails VTM-RA, which the authors acknowledge as a limitation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the traditional VVC reference (VTM-RA), BRHVC wins on all four HEVC classes (e.g., -44.7% vs -30.2% on Class D) and achieves a higher HEVC average (-32.0% vs -30.9%), but loses substantially on the high-frame-rate MCL-JCV (-16.1% vs -31.0%) and UVG (-19.7% vs -33.5%) datasets. Against the traditional HEVC reference (HM-RA), BRHVC is dramatically superior, saving roughly 30–45% more bitrate. Among neural methods, BRHVC outperforms the P-frame SOTA DCVC-FM on every dataset (e.g., -25.5% vs -15.2% on HEVC B; -44.7% vs -34.2% on HEVC D) and also beats the B-frame SOTA DCVC-B across the board (e.g., -25.2% vs -8.6% on HEVC C; -16.1% vs -2.0% on MCL-JCV). The ablation confirms that the two proposed modules (BMC and BCF) each contribute roughly 6.4–6.6% BD-rate reduction over the shared baseline, and that simply stacking extra ResBlocks (BL-1, BL-2) yields only 3.9–4.1%, far below BRHVC's 12.3%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the DCVC family of neural video compression methods, specifically extending DCVC-B (the SOTA NBVC method) and DCVC-DC's multi-scale contextual architecture. The key conceptual departure is the identification of 'unbalanced reference contribution' (URC) as a distinct problem in hierarchical B-frame coding that prior condition-based methods (DCVC-B, UCVC) address only implicitly through simple channel concatenation of bidirectional contexts. BRHVC introduces two task-specific modules—Bi-directional Motion Converge (BMC), which replaces the standard bidirectional motion residual compression with a multi-scale flow convergence strategy, and Bi-directional Contextual Fusion (BCF), which explicitly learns per-region reference weights guided by motion compensation accuracy—rather than treating both references symmetrically.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BRHVC is the first neural B-frame video compression method to surpass VTM-RA (the H.266/VVC reference software) on HEVC datasets, closing a gap the authors note has persisted in prior NBVC work. The quantitative URC analysis (showing up to 20.6% BD-rate gap between references at span 32) provides a useful diagnostic framework for the field, and the explicit reference-weight modeling in BCF offers a principled alternative to ad-hoc concatenation that could inform future hierarchical coding designs. However, the method underperforms on high-frame-rate datasets (MCL-JCV, UVG), suggesting the URC phenomenon may manifest differently under rapid temporal dynamics, and the ~24% additional decoding time from BMC+BCF remains a practical constraint for real-time applications.
