## Problem

Solving POMDPs is computationally intractable due to the continuous belief-state space, and prior finite-history approximations (Superstate MDPs) lack tight theoretical guarantees for standard RL algorithms. Specifically, it was unclear whether standard TD learning converges when the true dynamics are non-Markovian, and existing approaches either required expensive m-step TD variants or relied on Q-learning with restrictive ergodicity assumptions and only asymptotic guarantees.

## Input / Output

The method takes a finite-state POMDP (state space S, action set A, observation set Y, transition kernel P, observation kernel Φ, bounded reward r, discount factor γ) and a truncation length l that defines the Superstate MDP's state space as all action-observation histories of length at most l. It also requires a feature map φ(B,a) with ‖φ‖₂≤1 for linear Q-function approximation and a projection radius R. The output is a stationary policy μ(a|B) over superstates, the learned parameter vector θ, and a finite-time regret bound of O(T^{3/4} log T) plus approximation errors that decay exponentially in l.

## Method

The paper maps a POMDP to a finite-state Superstate MDP by truncating observation histories to length l, then proves that under a Uniform Filter Stability Condition (contractive belief updates), the optimal value functions of the POMDP and Superstate MDP differ by O((1−ρ)^l), improving prior polynomial-in-horizon bounds. A novel algebraic identity (Lemma 2) tightens the comparison between weighted sums under close probability distributions, yielding the exponential decay guarantee. The learning algorithm alternates between standard (not m-step) TD learning with linear function approximation to estimate the Q-function for a fixed policy, and POLITEX exponential-weight policy updates, establishing the first finite-time convergence guarantee for standard TD learning applied to a non-Markovian environment. The key technical insight is that close belief states (in total variation) induce close reward and transition functions, allowing TD updates to proceed as if the model were Markovian while the true dynamics remain a POMDP.

## Results

This is a purely theoretical paper with no experimental results. The main quantitative contribution is Theorem 2, which bounds the worst-case approximation error between the POMDP optimal value function and the Superstate MDP optimal value function as ξ_POMDP^SMDP = 2r̄(1−ρ)^l/(1−γ) + 2r̄γ(1−ρ)^l/((1−γ)((1−γ)+γ(1−ρ)^l)), showing exponential decay in the truncated history length l. Theorem 3 establishes a regret bound of R_T ≤ T·(ξ_FA + ξ_HA) + O(T^{3/4} log T) for the full algorithm. The paper improves upon prior approximation bounds: Kara & Yüksel (2023) and Subramanian & Mahajan (2019) gave O(1/(1−γ)²) expected-difference bounds, Abel et al. (2016) gave O(1/(1−γ)³), and Cayci et al. (2024) gave polynomial-in-horizon bounds, whereas this paper provides a tighter worst-case bound with explicit exponential dependence on l.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper contains no experimental comparisons or benchmark evaluations; all comparisons are theoretical. Against Kara & Yüksel (2023), this paper improves the approximation bound from an expected-difference O(1/(1−γ)²) to a tighter worst-case bound with explicit exponential decay (1−ρ)^l in history length, and replaces Q-learning with a policy-optimization approach that avoids ergodicity assumptions. Against Cayci et al. (2024), this paper uses standard 1-step TD learning instead of the more computationally expensive m-step TD variant, while providing finite-time convergence guarantees. Against Subramanian & Mahajan (2019), the novel algebraic identity (Lemma 2) yields tighter bounds on the value-function gap. Against Abel et al. (2016), the bound improves from O(1/(1−γ)³) to a form with explicit exponential decay in l. The paper also extends POLITEX (Abbasi-Yadkori et al., 2019) from the average-reward to the discounted-reward setting, which prior work had not addressed.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Superstate MDP approximation introduced by Kara and Yüksel (2023), which maps a POMDP to a finite-state MDP by truncating observation histories, and on the POLITEX policy iteration framework of Abbasi-Yadkori et al. (2019). It departs from prior work in two key ways: (1) it replaces the m-step TD learning used by Cayci et al. (2024) with standard one-step TD learning, establishing the first finite-time convergence guarantees for TD learning in a setting where the true dynamics are non-Markovian; and (2) it introduces a novel algebraic identity (Lemma 2) that yields a tighter worst-case bound on the POMDP-to-Superstate-MDP value gap, improving upon the 1/(1−γ)² and 1/(1−γ)³ bounds in prior work. The extension to linear function approximation for scalability also distinguishes it from Kara and Yüksel's Q-learning approach, which relied on ergodicity assumptions and provided only asymptotic guarantees.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides the first rigorous finite-time analysis showing that standard policy optimization (TD learning + policy update) can approximately solve POMDPs by treating them as MDPs over finite histories, with approximation error decaying exponentially in history length. This is practically significant because it validates the common heuristic of using finite-memory controllers for POMDPs with theoretical backing, and the linear function approximation extension makes the approach scalable to large state spaces. Key limitations include the Uniform Filter Stability assumption (which may not hold in all POMDPs), the requirement of sufficient exploration (μ(a|B) ≥ δ), and the absence of empirical validation. Open questions include extending the analysis to multi-step contraction settings, leveraging more expressive function approximators (LSTMs, Transformers), and relaxing the filter stability condition.
