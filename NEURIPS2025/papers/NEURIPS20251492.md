## Problem

Existing neural MI estimators face a fundamental trade-off: unconstrained neural networks are flexible but require exponential sample sizes, while simplified parametric models (e.g., Gaussian copula) are data-efficient but fail to capture complex dependence structures. This paper bridges the gap by leveraging recent vector copula theory to impose assumptions selectively on the dependence structure rather than the entire joint distribution, enabling a principled and tunable complexity-capacity trade-off.

## Input / Output

The method takes paired samples (x, y) from any modality—multivariate continuous data, 16×16 RGB images (preprocessed to 16-d via autoencoder), or language model embeddings (Llama-3 13B, BERT, compressed to 16-d)—and outputs a scalar mutual information estimate I(X;Y). The estimation is decomposed into two stages: first, flow models are trained independently on each marginal to compute vector ranks; second, a mixture of parametric vector copulas is fit to the rank pairs via MLE, with the number of mixture components selected by cross-validation.

## Method

The VCE estimator exploits the Vector Sklar Theorem, which disentangles p(x,y) = p(x)p(y)c(u_X, u_Y), and the key identity I(X;Y) = −H[c(u_X, u_Y)], so MI depends solely on the vector copula density. Marginals p(x) and p(y) are learned separately via flow matching, and vector ranks are computed as element-wise empirical ranks of the flow-transformed data. The vector copula is parameterized as a mixture of K vector Gaussian copulas (each defined by a cross-covariance matrix Σ_XY), trained by maximum likelihood on the rank pairs, with K selected via cross-validation to balance capacity and complexity. The paper further proves consistency, derives an error decomposition into marginal estimation error and copula modeling error, and shows that a single Gaussian copula corresponds to a second-order Taylor approximation of the true copula, justifying the mixture design.

## Results

On the image benchmark with known ground-truth MI (Gaussian Plates and Rectangles, 16×16 images compressed to d'=16), VCE achieves estimates of 0.92, 2.93, and 6.53 nats for I(X;Y) = 1, 3, 7 on Gaussian Plates, closely matching the true values with the smallest error among all methods at high MI (6.53 vs. true 7, compared to MINE's 5.46 and InfoNCE's 3.83). On Rectangles, VCE estimates 0.83, 2.27, and 5.02 nats for the same MI levels, remaining competitive but slightly below MINE and MRE at higher MI. On the IMDB text embedding benchmark (Llama-3 13B, d'=16), VCE estimates 2.01 nats (true ≈2.1) and 0.83 nats (true ≈0.9), outperforming N-MIENF (0.91 and 0.43) while being competitive with discriminative methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VCE clearly outperforms the generative baseline N-MIENF across all settings, with the gap widening at higher MI (e.g., 6.53 vs. 3.85 on Gaussian Plates I=7; 1.54 vs. 5.02 on Rectangles I=7), confirming that N-MIENF's single Gaussian copula assumption is insufficient for non-Gaussian dependence. Against discriminative methods (MINE, InfoNCE, MRE), VCE wins decisively on Gaussian Plates at high MI (6.53 vs. 5.46/3.83/5.91) but loses slightly on Rectangles at I=3 and I=7 (2.27/5.02 vs. MINE's 2.57/5.39 and MRE's 2.56/5.45), which the authors attribute to the highly complex dependence structure in that case exceeding the capacity of a Gaussian copula mixture. On the IMDB text embeddings, VCE is competitive with MINE and InfoNCE (2.01 vs. 1.83/1.64 for Llama-3 I≈2.1) but does not show a clear advantage, consistent with the low-MI regime where the high-discrepancy problem is less severe and neural parameterizations of the copula are more flexible. Overall, VCE's key advantage is its robustness at high MI and non-Gaussian dependence, where discriminative methods suffer from distribution mismatch and generative methods from oversimplified copula assumptions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the recent vector copula theory of Fan & Henry (2023), which extends classical Sklar's theorem from univariate to multivariate marginals, and generalizes the 'MI is copula entropy' identity from Ma & Sun (2011) and Davy & Doucet (2003) to the vector setting. The key methodological departure is a divide-and-conquer strategy: marginals are learned separately via flow matching, and the dependence structure is modeled as a mixture of vector Gaussian copulas with explicit model selection, rather than using a single unconstrained neural network for the entire joint distribution. The paper also provides a unifying reinterpretation of existing discriminative estimators (MINE, InfoNCE, MRE) and generative estimators (MINDE, N-MIENF) as different parameterizations and learning strategies of the vector copula, with the notable conceptual shift of treating pointwise mutual information as a normalized density c(u_X, u_Y) rather than a density ratio.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The estimator offers a principled, tunable interpolation between the extremes of overly flexible neural models (which suffer from high sample complexity) and overly simplified Gaussian copula assumptions (which miss complex dependence), with the mixture size K providing explicit complexity control. However, the method's reliance on accurate marginal estimation limits its applicability to high-dimensional data (e.g., raw images), and the model-based copula parameterization is inherently less flexible than neural network approaches for highly complex dependence structures, as evidenced by its underperformance on the Rectangles benchmark. Open questions include whether the vector rank computation can be made more robust in high dimensions, whether richer copula families beyond Gaussian mixtures would close the gap with discriminative methods, and whether the separate-learning strategy can be extended to conditional or multi-variable MI settings.
