## Problem

Process Reward Models (PRMs) have proven effective for unimodal LLM reasoning via Test-Time Scaling and reinforcement learning, but their integration into multimodal mathematical reasoning remains unexplored. Three specific barriers exist: (i) the scarcity of large-scale, high-quality multimodal CoT reasoning data caps the upper bound of both TTS and RL; (ii) no automated process-labeling pipeline exists that jointly captures logical validity and perceptual (image-text) consistency; and (iii) naively inserting scalar process rewards into online RL triggers reward hacking and a length bias that degrades performance below vanilla GRPO.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a math problem image (RGB, arbitrary resolution processed by a SAM-B + SigLIP-L hybrid vision encoder) paired with a text question, and generates a step-by-step natural-language Chain-of-Thought reasoning chain ending in a final numeric or symbolic answer. The PRM (URSA-8B-RM) scores each individual reasoning step with a binary correctness probability, and PS-GRPO additionally detects a 'drop-moment'—a relative decrease exceeding threshold ρ between consecutive step rewards—to flag process-level inconsistencies within otherwise correct rollouts. For Best-of-N test-time scaling, the PRM selects the highest-mean-reward rollout from N sampled solutions.

## Method

URSA is a three-stage training pipeline. Stage I synthesizes MMathCoT-1M (≈1M high-quality CoT trajectories) from 1.43M open-source math examples via three Gemini-1.5-Flash-driven strategies (CoT expansion, rewriting, format unification) and fine-tunes a SAM-B/SigLIP-L + Qwen2.5-Math-Instruct backbone to produce URSA-8B. Stage II constructs DualMath-1.1M through two complementary engines: a Binary Error Locating Engine that uses MCTS rollouts to binary-search the first erroneous step in ~553K incorrect solutions, and a Misinterpretation Insertion Engine that injects perceptual hallucinations into correct solutions to create ~302K image-text inconsistency labels; both are merged to train URSA-8B-RM as a per-step binary classifier. Stage III introduces PS-GRPO, which avoids scalar process rewards as optimization targets (the source of reward hacking and length bias) and instead treats the PRM's drop-moment signal as an outcome-level penalty: correct rollouts exhibiting a drop-moment receive reward 1−γ rather than 1, while incorrect rollouts receive 0, thereby differentiating learning value among correct answers and circumventing the PRM's inherent length bias.

## Results

URSA-8B-PS-GRPO achieves an average accuracy of 58.2% across 6 multimodal math reasoning benchmarks (MathVerse, MathVision, MathVista, WE-MATH, DYNAMATH, GeoQA), outperforming GPT-4o (55.5%) by 2.7% and Gemma3-12B (49.8%) by 8.4%. The model shows particularly large gains on MathVista (83.2 vs. GPT-4o's 64.7) and GeoQA (75.6 vs. 62.1), and achieves the first open-source surpassing on MathVision (31.5 vs. 30.4). However, a notable gap remains on DYNAMATH (47.4 vs. GPT-4o's 64.9), indicating limitations in dynamic problem-solving. The PRM (URSA-8B-RM) also improves Best-of-N verification, yielding 16.6% and 10.1% relative improvements over self-consistency on MathVerse and MathVision respectively with only N=4 samples.

## Baselines & Comparisons

URSA-8B-PS-GRPO (8B) outperforms GPT-4o by 2.7% on average, with decisive wins on MathVista (+18.5), GeoQA (+13.5), and MathVision (+1.1), but trails on DYNAMATH (−17.5) and WE-MATH (−2.1). Against the strongest open-source math-reasoning MLLMs, it beats MM-Eureka-7B (55.7) by 2.5% and Adora-7B (54.6) by 3.6%, with the largest margins on MathVision (+4.6 over MM-Eureka) and MathVista (+6.7 over MM-Eureka). Compared to the general-purpose Gemma3-12B (49.8), the improvement is 8.4% on average, driven primarily by MathVista (+19.6) and MathVision (+2.4). Against AtomThink-EMOVA (49.5), which focuses on slow-thinking training, URSA-8B-PS-GRPO leads by 8.7% on average, showing that the PRM-aided RL pipeline surpasses dedicated slow-thinking approaches. The PRM's BoN verification (Table 2) also outperforms self-consistency and the InternVL2.5-8B ORM baseline across all N values on both URSA-8B and AtomThink-EMOVA, confirming generalization beyond the training model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GRPO framework from DeepSeek-R1 to the multimodal setting and departs from it by replacing scalar process rewards with a binary 'drop-moment' signal (PS-GRPO), motivated by observed reward hacking and length bias when scalar PRM scores are used directly as RL objectives. The process supervision data construction draws on MCTS-based step labeling from Math-Shepherd and 'Let's Verify Step by Step,' but introduces a dual-view strategy (Binary Error Locating Engine for logical validity + Misinterpretation Insertion Engine for perceptual consistency) that is specific to multimodal reasoning. The foundation model training follows the standard LLaVA-style alignment + instruction tuning pipeline, with the novelty concentrated in the large-scale CoT data synthesis (MMathCoT-1M) and the first large-scale automatically-labeled multimodal process supervision dataset (DualMath-1.1M).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first large-scale, automatically labeled process supervision dataset for multimodal math reasoning and a practical RL algorithm (PS-GRPO) that avoids the well-documented failure modes of scalar process rewards in online RL. The negative result—that naive scalar process reward integration (both sum and step-level variants) degrades performance below vanilla GRPO—is broadly applicable to any PRM-aided RL pipeline and suggests the field should treat PRM outputs as relative-quality signals rather than absolute reward values. Open questions include whether PS-GRPO generalizes beyond math to other reasoning domains, whether the approach scales to larger models where the foundation model's ceiling is higher, and whether the reliance on a closed-source LLM (Gemini-1.5-Flash) for data synthesis limits reproducibility and introduces distributional biases.
