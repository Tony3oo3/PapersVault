## Problem

All prior coreset constructions for robust geometric median include an O(m) term in the coreset size, where m is the number of outliers. In real-world datasets, m can be a substantial fraction of n (e.g., ~10% in PageBlocks), rendering the coreset nearly as large as the original data. The paper asks whether this O(m) dependency can be eliminated, and establishes that the condition n ≥ 4m is both necessary and sufficient for doing so.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes a dataset P of n points in R^d, an integer outlier count m, and an error parameter ε, and produces a weighted subset S ⊆ P where each point carries a weight such that the total weight equals n. The coreset guarantees that for every center c ∈ R^d, the robust cost cost^(m)(S,c) approximates cost^(m)(P,c) within a (1±ε) factor. The resulting coreset has size O~(ε^{-2}·min{ε^{-2},d}) for general d (independent of m), and the optimal size O~(ε^{-1/2} + (m/n)ε^{-1}) for d=1, with a matching lower bound.

## Method

The core technical contribution is a novel non-component-wise error analysis that treats |cost^(m)(P,c) − cost^(m)(S,c)| as a single entity rather than bounding each bucket's error individually. For d=1, the analysis uses the derivative of the robust cost function: f'_P(c) equals the difference between inlier counts on either side of c, so the total error is bounded by integrating |f'_P(x) − f'_S(x)| over the relevant interval, allowing bucket-level errors to cancel out. For general d, the algorithm uniformly samples O~(ε^{-2}·min{ε^{-2},d}) points from the outlier set L* (rather than including all m outliers) and proves via ball range space and VC-dimension arguments that the number of 'outlier-misaligned' points is O(εm), keeping total error within O(ε)·cost^(m)(P,c). The 1D algorithm partitions the middle n−2m points P_M using the vanilla coreset method, then greedily buckets the remaining outlier regions with size constraints O(εn) to control the derivative-based error bound.

## Results

On the Census1990 dataset, the proposed method produces a coreset of size 1000 with empirical error 0.012, while the best baseline (HLLW25) requires size 2300 to achieve a worse error of 0.013. Across all six datasets, the empirical error ratio ε̂(S_baseline)/ε̂(S_ours) is consistently greater than 1 (ranging from 1.01 to 5.87), confirming uniformly lower error. In runtime, the method achieves a ~2× speedup on coreset evaluation (e.g., T_S = 1.284s vs. 2.629s on Census1990) while maintaining comparable coreset construction time (~6.9s vs. ~7.0s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two baselines: HJLW23 [39] (which includes all m outlier points in the coreset and samples inliers) and HLLW25 [40] (which improves the inlier sampling size). The proposed method consistently outperforms both baselines on all six datasets in terms of empirical error, with the largest gains on smaller datasets (Athlete: 5.17× lower error vs. HJLW23 at size 210; Diabetes: 5.70× vs. HJLW23, 5.87× vs. HLLW25). A key practical advantage is that the proposed method achieves the same error level with a coreset size smaller than m (the outlier count), whereas both baselines require size ≥ m. In runtime (Table 2), the proposed method achieves a ~2× speedup on coreset evaluation time (T_S) compared to HLLW25 across all datasets (e.g., 1.284s vs. 2.629s on Census1990), while coreset construction time (T_C) remains comparable. The advantage is most pronounced at smaller coreset sizes, where the baselines' mandatory inclusion of all m outliers dominates the size budget.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the bucket-partitioning framework for 1D geometric median coresets from Huang et al. [37] and the constrained clustering coreset of Huang et al. [40], but departs fundamentally from the component-wise error analysis used by all prior robust coreset constructions [39, 40, 42]. The key conceptual departure is a novel non-component-wise error analysis that treats the total cost difference as a single entity and exploits cancellation of bucket-level errors, rather than requiring each bucket to individually satisfy the error bound. For the general d-dimensional case, the paper leverages the ball range space concept from Braverman et al. [9] in a new way—applying VC-dimension arguments to outlier points rather than inlier points—to sample only Õ(ε⁻²·min{ε⁻²,d}) outliers instead of all m.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The elimination of the O(m) coreset size dependency is practically significant because real-world datasets can have m ≈ 0.1n outliers (e.g., PageBlocks), making prior coreset sizes prohibitively large. The optimal 1D coreset size Õ(ε⁻¹/² + (m/n)ε⁻¹) reveals a clean phase transition: when m ≤ √ε·n, the robust problem is as easy as the vanilla case, but beyond that threshold the complexity grows linearly with m. Open questions include optimizing the coreset size for d > 1 (where a gap remains between the upper and lower bounds), extending to streaming settings (blocked by lack of mergeability due to outlier interactions across chunks), and applying the non-component-wise analysis to other robust problems like robust regression and robust PCA.
