## Problem

Existing graphon estimation methods struggle with scalability to large networks and resolution-independent approximation, typically relying on latent variable estimation or computationally expensive Gromov-Wasserstein distance optimization. No prior approach combines high accuracy, polynomial-time complexity, and direct graphon recovery without these costly intermediate steps.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250055/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of P observed graphs (each with n_p vertices) and reduces them to a single empirical motif density vector via ORCA-based counting of subgraphs up to 5 nodes; the raw graph structures are discarded after this step. The output is a continuous graphon function f_θ: [0,1]² → [0,1] that is inherently resolution-free, allowing edge probability queries at any coordinate pair. For MomentMixup, the output additionally includes augmented graphs sampled from graphons whose moments are convex combinations of class-specific moment vectors.

## Method

MomentNet trains an implicit neural representation (INR) f_θ: [0,1]² → [0,1] by minimizing a weighted mean squared error between empirical motif densities (computed from observed graphs) and INR-predicted motif densities (computed via Monte Carlo integration of the induced motif density formula). This moment-matching objective entirely bypasses latent variable modeling and Gromov-Wasserstein alignment, yielding a polynomial-time estimator. The theoretical guarantee (Theorem 1) shows that if the INR can approximate empirical motif densities within ε_a and sufficient graph samples are available, the cut distance between the estimated and true graphon is bounded by 22C/√(log₂k) with high probability. MomentMixup extends this by interpolating moment vectors across classes, passing the mixed vector through MomentNet to obtain a new graphon, and sampling augmented graphs from it for downstream classification.

## Results

For graphon estimation, MomentNet outperforms the state-of-the-art scalable method SIGL on 9 out of 13 benchmark graphons (75%) in Gromov-Wasserstein distance, with particularly strong gains on graphons 10 and 11. For graph classification with data augmentation, MomentMixup achieves the best test accuracy on 3 of 4 datasets: IMDB-Binary (74.30%), IMDB-Multi (50.95%), and AIDS (98.5%), while trailing G-Mixup w/SIGL slightly on Reddit-Binary (91.8% vs. 92.25%). In the scalability evaluation, MomentNet's estimation error decreases with graph size and its runtime scales more favorably than SIGL for n > 300 nodes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SIGL [3] (the most direct scalable INR-based competitor), MomentNet wins on 9/13 graphons in GW distance and MomentMixup outperforms G-Mixup w/SIGL on 3 of 4 classification datasets (IMDB-B: +0.35%, IMDB-M: +0.25%, AIDS: +1.2%), losing only on Reddit-Binary (−0.45%), which the authors attribute to using a limited 9-motif set on very large graphs. Against G-Mixup w/USVT [13,7], MomentMixup dominates on all four datasets, with the largest margin on AIDS (+0.7%) and IMDB-B (+2.36%). In the graphon estimation benchmarks (Figure 2a), MomentNet also surpasses USVT [7], SAS [6], IGNR [34], and GWB [35] on the majority of the 13 graphons, with the clearest separations on complex structures like stochastic block models. The scalability experiments confirm that MomentNet's runtime advantage over SIGL becomes pronounced beyond ~300 nodes, while its estimation error remains lower across all tested sizes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the INR-for-graphons paradigm introduced by IGNR (Xia et al., AISTATS 2023) and advanced by SIGL (Azizpour et al., AISTATS 2025), but departs fundamentally from their reliance on latent variable estimation and Gromov-Wasserstein alignment. Instead of learning node embeddings and aligning graphs via optimal transport, MomentNet directly trains the INR to match empirical motif densities (graph moments) computed via ORCA, replacing the combinatorial GW objective with a simple weighted MSE in moment space. The MomentMixup component extends the mixup data augmentation framework (Zhang et al., ICLR 2018) and the graphon-space interpolation of G-Mixup (Han et al., ICML 2022) by performing interpolation in the moment space rather than the graphon space, motivated by a proposition showing these two operations are not equivalent.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean, polynomial-time graphon estimator with a provable cut-distance guarantee, offering a practical alternative to GW-based methods that struggle with scalability and resolution. The theoretical result connecting motif representation fidelity to estimation accuracy is a useful contribution to the graphon estimation literature, though the conditions (sufficiently large n and P) are somewhat restrictive. Key limitations include the reliance on a fixed, pre-selected set of motifs (performance can degrade if moments are insufficient or noisy), the assumption of a single graphon per class, and the lack of support for attributed or dynamic networks. Open questions include adaptive moment selection, extensions to graphon mixtures for heterogeneous data, and whether the moment-matching approach generalizes to sparse or temporal graph settings.
