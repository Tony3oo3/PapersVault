## Problem

Existing multimodal clinical language models are vision-centric and cannot jointly reason over 1D time-series signals (e.g., ECG) alongside 2D/3D medical images and text, leaving a critical gap in comprehensive diagnosis. Additionally, standard critic-free RL training methods like GRPO treat all domains uniformly, causing abundant and easy clinical modalities to dominate optimization while rare, challenging domains (e.g., ultrasound, mammography) receive insufficient learning signal.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251256/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts heterogeneous multimodal inputs—12-lead ECG waveforms, 2D images (chest X-ray, mammography, dermoscopy, fundus, histopathology), 3D volumes (CT, MRI, ultrasound), and free-text clinical records—where vision and time-series channels are optional, requiring the model to handle missing modalities gracefully. Outputs are a three-part structured response: a free-text chain-of-thought reasoning trace, axis-aligned bounding boxes in (x, y, w, h) format localizing salient anatomical regions, and a concise multi-label clinical diagnosis. The model is trained on 2.61 million instruction pairs spanning 9 clinical domains via the CLIMB dataset.

## Method

QoQ-Med initializes from a pretrained vision-language model (Qwen-based) and prepends an ECG-JEPA time-series encoder whose outputs are linearly projected into the LLM's token space, where all modality tokens are interleaved with text in temporal order. The core training contribution is Domain-aware Relative Policy Optimization (DRPO), which extends GRPO with a two-level hierarchical scaling mechanism: first, K-means clustering (k selected via elbow method) partitions question-level reward vectors within each domain into difficulty clusters; second, temperature factors T(g,t) = max(√N(g,t)·μ(g,t), ε) at the domain level and T(c,g,t) at the cluster level are computed, where N is sample count and μ is mean reward (a difficulty proxy). Question-level GRPO-normalized advantages are then divided by the product of both temperature factors, KL-regularized to suppress outliers, and rescaled to unit variance—effectively up-weighting rare and hard domains/clusters while preserving GRPO's critic-free, zero-mean structure. The scalar reward combines F1 diagnostic accuracy (weight 0.6), best-IoU bounding-box alignment (0.2), and auxiliary format/coherence terms (0.2), and training proceeds in two stages: modality alignment followed by full multimodal DRPO fine-tuning.

## Results

On the CLIMB benchmark across 8 medical imaging modalities, DRPO achieves an overall macro-F1 of 0.295 and accuracy of 0.666, outperforming the best critic-free baseline (RE++ at F1 0.202) by 46% and GRPO (F1 0.193) by 53%. Against closed-source models, QoQ-Med-7B reaches 0.686 overall accuracy, surpassing GPT-4o (0.385) and o4-mini (0.411), though it trails o4-mini on F1 (0.286 vs. 0.394). On MIMIC-IV multimodal fusion, DRPO-Full achieves 0.642 accuracy and 0.597 F1 on 48-hour in-hospital mortality, improving over GRPO-Full (0.551 acc, 0.354 F1) by 9.1 points in accuracy and 24.3 points in F1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DRPO outperforms all six RL baselines (SFT, PPO, ReMax, RE++, RLOO, GRPO) on overall F1, with the largest gains in underrepresented modalities: mammography F1 improves from 0.059 (GRPO) to 0.253, and MRI F1 from 0.395 to 0.625. Against PPO (the only critic-based method), DRPO wins on overall F1 (0.295 vs. 0.234) while PPO retains an edge on accuracy (0.665 vs. 0.666, essentially tied). Among open-source clinical MLLMs, QoQ-Med-7B dominates LLaVa-Med (0.287 overall acc) and Med-R1 (0.603 acc, 0.013 F1) across all 8 domains. Against closed-source models, QoQ-Med-7B surpasses GPT-4o on accuracy in every domain (e.g., 0.691 vs. 0.261 on CXR) but trails o4-mini on F1 in most domains (0.286 vs. 0.394 overall), with o4-mini leading only on MRI (0.775 vs. 0.652 F1). On MIMIC-IV, DRPO-Full beats GRPO-Full on both LOS (F1 0.283 vs. 0.105) and 48-IHM (F1 0.597 vs. 0.354), confirming that hierarchical scaling benefits multimodal fusion as well.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the critic-free RL training paradigm of GRPO (from DeepSeek-R1/DeepSeekMath) to the clinical multimodal setting by introducing DRPO, a hierarchical scaling mechanism that draws conceptually from task-wise reward normalization in deep RL (IMPALA, PopArt) but applies it at both inter-domain and intra-domain (K-means cluster) levels. On the model side, it builds on the Qwen Omni vision-language architecture and the ECG-JEPA time-series encoder, but is the first to natively fuse 1D physiological signals with 2D/3D clinical images in a single autoregressive LLM. It departs from prior clinical MLLMs (LLaVa-Med, Med-Flamingo, BiomedGPT, Med-R1) which are either vision-only or ECG-only, and from standard SFT/GRPO training by explicitly addressing the skewed domain distributions inherent in clinical data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in clinical AI: heterogeneous data distributions cause models to overfit abundant modalities (e.g., chest X-rays) while underperforming on rare but clinically critical ones (e.g., ultrasound, mammography). The 43% relative F1 gain over GRPO is substantial, though absolute F1 scores remain low (~0.30 overall), suggesting significant room for improvement. Open questions include whether the unsupervised reasoning traces can be made more sample-efficient, whether the ECG modality's limited marginal contribution (vs. vision+text) reflects an architectural limitation or a data-quality issue, and whether DRPO's hierarchical scaling generalizes beyond the 9 domains tested. The release of 2.61M reasoning traces and open weights provides a useful resource for the clinical MLLM community.
