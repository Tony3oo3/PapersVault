## Problem

Existing constrained generation methods for diffusion models either operate in image space (incompatible with Stable Diffusion's latent architecture), rely on learned soft penalties that cannot guarantee per-sample constraint satisfaction, or require special measurement operators. This paper addresses the gap of enforcing hard, potentially non-convex and non-differentiable domain-specific constraints on pretrained Stable Diffusion models at inference time, without any retraining.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250918/cropped_page7_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a random latent noise vector and a constraint specification (a differentiable penalty function, a black-box simulator, or a pretrained classifier) as input, and produces a decoded RGB image that satisfies the constraint to a user-defined tolerance. Constraints are evaluated in image space via the frozen decoder but corrections are applied in latent space; for the microstructure task, 64×64 patches are upscaled to 1024×1024, and for metamaterial design the constraint involves a full finite-element stress-strain simulation via Abaqus.

## Method

The core mechanism is Proximal Langevin Dynamics in the latent space: at each denoising step, after the standard Langevin update on the learned score field, a proximal map is applied that minimizes a composite objective of constraint violation g(D(z)) and image-space distance to the pre-correction sample. Gradients of the constraint are backpropagated through the frozen decoder D to the latent variables, requiring no retraining of either the score network or the decoder. For non-differentiable black-box simulators, stochastic subgradients are estimated via a Differentiable Perturbed Optimizer (DPO) scheme that injects random perturbations and uses finite-difference scaling. For non-convex constraints lacking closed-form projections, an Augmented Lagrangian relaxation is solved as the inner minimizer. The paper proves that for convex constraint sets the iterates converge to the feasible region at a geometric rate while the KL divergence from the training distribution remains bounded and vanishes as the noise level approaches zero.

## Results

On the metamaterial inverse design task, the proposed Latent Constrained Model achieves an MSE of 1.4±0.6 against the target stress-strain curve, a 4.6× improvement over the state-of-the-art Bastek & Kochmann method (6.4±4.6) and a 5.1× improvement over the Conditional Diffusion baseline (7.1±4.5), while reducing physically invalid shapes from 55% (Cond) and 20% (Bastek) to just 5%. In microstructure generation, the method satisfies porosity constraints exactly (0% violation) with FID scores substantially lower than the image-space Projected Diffusion Model (which has FID more than 2× higher), while the Conditional Diffusion baseline achieves the best average FID of 10.8 but only 31.6% of samples meet the 10% porosity tolerance. In copyright-safe generation, the method reduces protected-character (Mickey Mouse) generation to 10% of samples (matching classifier accuracy bounds) versus 33% for the conditional baseline, while preserving FID scores comparable to unconstrained generation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Conditional Diffusion baseline (Dontas et al. [37]), the proposed method dominates on constraint satisfaction across all three tasks: exact porosity adherence vs. 31.6% compliance, MSE 1.4 vs. 7.1 in metamaterial design, and 10% vs. 33% copyright violations, while the conditional model retains a slight FID edge in microstructure generation (10.8 average). Against Projected Diffusion Models (Christopher et al. [15]), the proposed method achieves comparable or better constraint satisfaction while operating in latent space, yielding substantially lower FID scores (PDM's FID is more than 2× higher in microstructure) and enabling 1024×1024 generation where PDM is limited to 64×64 with post-hoc upscaling in the copyright task. Against Bastek & Kochmann (2023), the state-of-the-art for metamaterial inverse design, the proposed method achieves a 4.6× MSE reduction (1.4 vs. 6.4) and a 4× reduction in physically invalid shapes (5% vs. 20%), with the additional advantage of arbitrary tolerance tightening through iterative DPO refinement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the projected Langevin dynamics framework of Christopher et al. (2024), which enforced constraints via hard projections in image-space diffusion, to the latent-space setting of Stable Diffusion by replacing projections with proximal operators evaluated through the frozen decoder. It also borrows the Differentiable Perturbed Optimizer (DPO) technique from Berthet et al. (2020) to handle non-differentiable black-box simulators, and builds on the proximal Langevin Monte Carlo convergence theory of Brosse et al. (2017). The key departure from prior latent-space constrained methods (ReSample, Zirvi et al.) is that no forward measurement operator is required, and unlike learned latent constraint approaches (Engel et al., Shi & Bulling), the method is training-free and provides per-sample feasibility guarantees rather than distribution-level adherence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first training-free method to enforce arbitrary (including non-convex and simulator-defined) constraints within pretrained Stable Diffusion, with convergence guarantees to both the feasible set and the training distribution. The practical impact is significant for scientific and engineering workflows where generated materials or designs must satisfy exact physical specifications, and the DPO integration opens the door to embedding complex finite-element solvers directly in the generative loop. Key limitations include restriction to image (not video) diffusion, dependence on decoder differentiability for gradient backpropagation, and the surrogate-constraint variant (Section 6.3) still being bounded by classifier accuracy. Open questions include extension to video diffusion with temporal constraints, scalability to 3D volumetric data, and whether the proximal correction can be made more efficient for very high-resolution or multi-step simulator evaluations.
