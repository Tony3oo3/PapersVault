## Problem

The AI alignment literature lacks a clear, shared definition of 'alignment' and predominantly treats it as a unidirectional process of shaping AI to match human values. The reciprocal dimension—how humans cognitively, behaviorally, and societally adapt to increasingly capable AI—remains underexplored, and the dynamic, evolving nature of the human-AI relationship is not formally accounted for in existing frameworks.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 411 papers (from 34,213 initial records) spanning HCI, NLP, and ML venues (2019–2024), screened via a PRISMA protocol with keyword filtering and six research-question-based eligibility criteria. The output is a structured bidirectional typology (Table 1) organized around four research questions with fine-grained sub-dimensions, a 12-type motivational value taxonomy extending Schwartz's theory, a quantitative bar-chart of paper coverage per dimension, and three forward-looking challenges with actionable recommendations. Coding was performed by a 12-member team using mixed inductive and deductive approaches, with two independent coders per paper for rigor.

## Method

The paper combines a PRISMA-guided systematic literature review with iterative framework design. After identifying and screening 34,213 records down to 411, the team applied qualitative coding (inductive + deductive) to extract relevant sentences answering six pre-identified research questions, then collaboratively integrated codes into a bidirectional framework with four RQs. The value taxonomy adapts Schwartz's Theory of Basic Values by adding a novel 'Desired Values for AI Tools' high-order category (encompassing Usability and Human-Likeness) and organizing 12 motivational value types along two axes: source (individual, social, interactive) and self-intention (self-protection vs. self-expansion). Gap identification is operationalized by quantifying the number of papers mapped to each dimension in the typology, revealing underexplored areas such as implicit feedback, inference-stage alignment, AI literacy, and societal-level impact. The framework culminates in three challenges—Specification Game, Dynamic Co-evolution, and Safeguarding Co-adaptation—each addressed from both the AI-to-human and human-to-AI directions.

## Results

The systematic review screened 34,213 initial records down to a final corpus of 411 papers across HCI, NLP, and ML venues (2019–2024). The resulting Bidirectional Human-AI Alignment framework organizes the literature into four research questions (RQ1–RQ4) spanning two directions: 'Align AI with Humans' (RQ1–RQ2) and 'Align Humans with AI' (RQ3–RQ4). Key quantitative findings from Figure 2 show that the majority of existing work concentrates on explicit human feedback and model-training-stage alignment, while dimensions such as implicit feedback, inference-stage customization, human-in-the-loop evaluation, AI literacy, and societal-impact assessment remain significantly underrepresented in the literature.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a position paper and systematic literature review rather than an empirical study, so it does not propose a method to be benchmarked against baselines. The paper's contribution is a conceptual framework (Bidirectional Human-AI Alignment) and a gap analysis of 411 papers, not a performance comparison. The closest analogue to a 'comparison' is the paper's identification of underexplored dimensions relative to well-studied ones: for example, explicit human feedback dominates the literature while implicit and simulated feedback receive minimal attention, and training-stage alignment far outpaces inference-stage or interactive alignment work. The authors position their framework as extending prior unidirectional alignment work (e.g., RLHF, Constitutional AI, DPO) by adding the 'Align Humans with AI' direction, which they argue is critically underexplored in the existing 400+ paper corpus.

## Relation to Prior Work

The paper builds on Schwartz's Theory of Basic Values as the structural backbone for its value taxonomy, adapting the four high-order value types and supplementing them with a novel 'Desired Values for AI Tools' category. It follows PRISMA methodology for a systematic review of 411 papers spanning HCI, NLP, and ML. The key conceptual departure from prior alignment work is the explicit reframing of alignment as bidirectional: while the dominant literature (RLHF, constitutional AI, preference optimization) treats alignment as a unidirectional process of shaping AI to human values, this paper introduces the equally important but underexplored reciprocal direction of aligning humans to AI—encompassing cognitive adjustment, behavioral adaptation, and societal response to advancing AI systems.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The bidirectional framing addresses a fundamental asymmetry in the alignment literature: the 'Align Humans to AI' direction (AI literacy, critical thinking, collaborative adaptation to superhuman AI) is dramatically underexplored relative to 'Align AI to Humans.' The quantitative gap analysis reveals that implicit feedback, inference-stage alignment, human-in-the-loop evaluation, and societal impact assessment are all significantly underrepresented. Open questions include how to dynamically co-evolve alignment as both AI capabilities and human values shift over time, how to prevent specification gaming as systems approach AGI, and how to design scalable oversight for AI whose instrumental actions may diverge from human intent. The framework serves as a reference taxonomy for cross-disciplinary collaboration but does not itself provide new methods or benchmarks.
