## Problem

Existing carbon accounting methods for large AI models (e.g., LLMCarbon) are fundamentally deterministic, producing single point estimates that ignore inherent uncertainties from geotemporal manufacturing differences, efficiency evolution over time, and spatiotemporal variability in electricity carbon intensity. This leads to substantial inaccuracies—up to 108.51% error in operational carbon—because identical hardware classes fabricated in different regions and periods embody distinct emissions, and operational carbon varies significantly with the location and time of deployment.

## Input / Output

The model consumes hardware specifications (die size, technology node, device counts), training/inference parameters (duration, PUE, average power), and the geographic location and time window of operation. It draws on pre-computed probability density functions for key parameters (yield, electricity consumption per unit die size, carbon intensity) derived from a curated carbon dataset aggregating ESG reports, power grid operator statistics, and industry publications. Outputs are continuous probability distributions for embodied and operational carbon, reported at the 5th, 10th, 50th, 90th, and 95th percentiles, enabling risk-aware decision-making rather than a single deterministic value.

## Method

PCAM decomposes the carbon footprint into component-level parameter models for processors, memory, and storage, where each uncertain parameter (carbon intensity during manufacturing, fab yield, electricity consumption per unit die size) is represented as a continuous probability density function rather than a fixed value. Distributions are constructed via a dual-stage procedure: collected data is first converted into frequency histograms, then smoothed with Kernel Density Estimation to yield continuous PDFs. For carbon intensity under uncertain manufacturing location, a mixture modeling approach combines regional KDEs weighted by global IC production capacity (Monte Carlo sampling over regions per Table 2). Yield distributions are derived by applying the Poisson yield model to TSMC's historical defect density data across four process nodes, followed by KDE. The final carbon estimate is computed by propagating these parameter distributions through the component equations (Eqs. 3–6), producing full output distributions that capture the compounding uncertainty across all hardware components and operational contexts.

## Results

PCAM achieves a maximum deviation of ≤2.67% from ground truth for embodied carbon (XLM) and ≤7.44% for operational carbon across all four large AI models (XLM, T5, GPT-3, Switch), compared to LLMCarbon's ≤23.02% and ≤108.51% respectively. In the spatial dimension (90 regions), LLMCarbon's operational carbon error reaches 108.51% at the 95th percentile for XLM, while PCAM stays within 3.90%. In the appendix comparison, PCAM also outperforms STEC with errors under 3% versus STEC's under 24% on embodied carbon.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PCAM consistently outperforms LLMCarbon [Faiz et al., ICLR 2024] on both embodied and operational carbon across all four evaluated models. On embodied carbon (XLM), PCAM's maximum error is 2.67% (10th percentile) versus LLMCarbon's 23.02% (5th percentile); for GPU-specific embodied carbon, the gap widens to 1.95% vs. 66.85% at the 5th percentile. On operational carbon, PCAM's worst-case error is 7.44% (T5, spatial 5th percentile) while LLMCarbon reaches 108.51% (XLM, spatial 95th percentile), with LLMCarbon systematically overestimating in the spatial dimension by 83–108% at the tails. In the appendix, PCAM also beats STEC [Zhang et al., ACM FUSEN 2024] on embodied carbon with errors under 3% versus STEC's under 24%, confirming that probabilistic modeling of yield, EPS, and carbon intensity distributions is critical for accurate carbon accounting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the deterministic carbon accounting framework established by LLMCarbon (Faiz et al., ICLR 2024) and earlier embodied carbon models (STEC, ACT) by replacing point estimates with probabilistic distributions derived via Kernel Density Estimation over a curated multi-source dataset. Where prior work treats hardware parameters (yield, EPS, carbon intensity) as fixed values from LCA reports, PCAM models them as continuous random variables whose distributions are learned from ESG reports, power grid statistics, and industry data. The methodological lineage is thus: deterministic LCA-based accounting → parametric deterministic models (LLMCarbon) → non-parametric probabilistic distributions (PCAM), with the KDE dual-stage pipeline as the key technical bridge.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As AI model scale grows and power grids decarbonize, embodied carbon becomes an increasingly dominant share of total footprint, making accurate uncertainty-aware accounting essential for credible sustainability reporting and regulatory compliance. The paper's open-sourcing of the aggregated carbon dataset and its demonstration that deterministic models can err by over 100% at distribution tails challenge the validity of current single-value carbon certifications. Key limitations include reliance on averaged data for raw materials and supply chain, exclusion of transport and end-of-life stages, and the somewhat circular evaluation where 'ground truth' is itself a KDE-free accounting result from the same dataset. Open questions include whether the KDE approach generalizes to rapidly evolving hardware (e.g., 2nm nodes) and how to incorporate the full supply chain uncertainty that the authors acknowledge but cannot yet quantify.
