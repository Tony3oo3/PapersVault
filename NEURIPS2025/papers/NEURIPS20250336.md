## Problem

In federated learning with non-IID data, global and local features are in an antagonistic relationship: aggregating local models erases local knowledge, while local training causes forgetting of global knowledge. Existing methods enforce a 'hard consensus' by using global information as a rigid, task-agnostic criterion to constrain local updates, which fails to balance these competing features and cannot negotiate mutually agreeable results. The paper asks how to transform this competitive relationship into a cooperative one through a 'soft consensus' mechanism.

## Input / Output

Each client receives RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, variable for Flowers102) partitioned via Dirichlet distribution (α ∈ {0.3, 0.5, 1.0}) to simulate heterogeneity, and the system outputs a single class label per image. Unusually, the method does not communicate the full task network between clients; instead, only small data-adapter parameters and a shared projection are exchanged, while the backbone task network (e.g., ResNet-18) remains frozen after an initial FedAvg-style warm-up phase.

## Method

FedMP constructs two parallel solution paths on each client: a local-preference mapping Φ^(l) and a global-preference mapping Φ^(g), both feeding into a shared projection Ψ that maps to the output space. This shared projection acts as a task-driven criterion, ensuring both paths solve the same objective from different starting points, so their agreement constitutes a high-confidence soft consensus. A swapping strategy periodically replaces the local-preference path's initialization with a randomly selected other client's model to prevent local overfitting and equalize path strengths. To reduce communication overhead, the optimization target is shifted from the full task network to lightweight data adapters that transform inputs into features the frozen backbone can recognize, so only these small adapter parameters are transmitted each round. A convergence guarantee (Theorem 1) is provided under standard L-smoothness and bounded-variance assumptions.

## Results

FedMP achieves the best accuracy on CIFAR-10 under high heterogeneity (α=0.3: 85.37%, α=0.5: 88.96%) and on Flowers102 under moderate-to-high heterogeneity (α=0.5: 54.41%, α=1.0: 53.43%), outperforming the strongest baselines by up to 10.49% over FedAvg on Flowers102 (α=0.5). On CIFAR-100, FedMP trails FedUCS by 0.76–1.33% across all α settings but still surpasses all other baselines. The method shows its largest gains under more heterogeneous data distributions (smaller α) and on the more challenging Flowers102 dataset.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedMP outperforms FedUCS (the strongest overall baseline) on CIFAR-10 at α=0.3 (85.37 vs. 83.61) and α=0.5 (88.96 vs. 87.79), and on Flowers102 at α=0.5 (54.41 vs. 49.22, a +5.19 gap) and α=1.0 (53.43 vs. 51.18). However, FedUCS consistently leads on CIFAR-100 across all heterogeneity levels (62.19/63.24/64.28 vs. FedMP's 61.43/62.04/62.95) and on CIFAR-10 at α=1.0 (89.43 vs. 88.31). SCAFFOLD edges out FedMP on Flowers102 at the most heterogeneous setting (α=0.3: 45.98 vs. 43.73). Against the remaining baselines (FedAvg, FedProx, FedNova, FedDF, MOON, FedASAM, FedPVR), FedMP achieves the best or near-best accuracy in 7 out of 9 dataset–α configurations, with the largest margins on Flowers102 where it surpasses FedAvg by up to 10.49%.

## Relation to Prior Work

FedMP builds on the FedAvg training paradigm but departs fundamentally from the 'hard consensus' strategy employed by FedProx, SCAFFOLD, MOON, FedPVR, and FedUCS, which impose rigid global constraints on local updates. Instead, it introduces a 'soft consensus' mechanism where two solution paths (one biased toward local features, one toward global features) are jointly optimized toward a shared task-driven projection, allowing global and local information to negotiate rather than be forced into alignment. The swapping strategy and the shift from task-network optimization to data adaptation are novel mechanisms not present in prior federated learning methods, addressing local overfitting and communication overhead respectively.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The soft-consensus framing offers a principled alternative to the dominant hard-consensus paradigm in federated learning, and the consistent gains (up to ~10% on Flowers102 under high heterogeneity) over strong baselines like FedUCS and FedPVR validate the approach. However, the method adds architectural complexity (dual paths, swapping, data adapters) and the communication savings from data adaptation come at a small performance cost, raising practical adoption questions. Open questions include whether the multiple-paths formulation generalizes to non-vision tasks, how it scales with increasing client counts, and whether the swapping strategy's privacy implications (discussed but not fully quantified) are acceptable in sensitive domains.
