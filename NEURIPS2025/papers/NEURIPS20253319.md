## Problem

Non-stationary reinforcement learning algorithms such as SWUCRL2-CW handle temporal drift through uniform confidence widening, which treats all state-action pairs identically and yields T^{3/4}-type regret bounds. This paper asks whether the structure of environmental change—specifically, when transition drift is approximately low-rank (K ≪ SA) plus sparse shocks—can be exploited to recover the optimal √T regret rate that is known to be achievable in stationary settings.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes a stream of (s_t, a_t, r_t, s_{t+1}) tuples from a sequence of communicating MDPs sharing a fixed state space S and action space A, and at each step outputs an action selected by an optimistic policy. Internally, it maintains flattened SA×S matrices of empirical transition changes, from which it extracts a time-varying rank-K factor decomposition (u_k(t), v_k, w_k) and a sparse shock residual. The rank K is estimated online via randomized SVD with adaptive oversampling and is not required as a hyperparameter; the shrinkage-combined transition estimate  p̃_t(s'|s,a) is the quantity fed into the EVI planner.

## Method

SVUCRL decomposes each transition change ΔP_t into a low-rank structured drift and a sparse shock via a convex PCP/RPCA program, then tracks the low-rank component online using randomized SVD with power iterations (Algorithm 1) and an incremental truncated-SVD warm-start (Algorithm 2). Adaptive confidence widening is driven by a bias-corrected local-variation estimator that scales the widening parameter η(s,a,t) with the observed per-state-action drift rate rather than a global budget, yielding a √(AB_p) total-widening bound. For planning, the algorithm forecasts next-step transition factors using exponential smoothing or AR models on the time coefficients u_k(t), then combines the forecast with the empirical transition estimate via a James-Stein shrinkage weight whose data-driven ratio is shown to be asymptotically near-optimal (Theorem 1). The resulting dynamic regret bound is Õ(√(SAT) + D_max·S√(AT) + L_max·B_r + D_max·L_max·B_p + D_max·S√(AB_p) + D_max·δ_B·B_p + D_max·√(KT)), where the structure-dependent non-stationarity cost scales with √K rather than √(SA), matching the conjectured optimal √T rate up to logarithmic factors.

## Results

The main result (Theorem 2) establishes a dynamic regret bound of $\tilde{O}(\sqrt{SAT} + D_{\max}S\sqrt{AT} + L_{\max}B_r + D_{\max}L_{\max}B_p + D_{\max}S\sqrt{AB_p} + D_{\max}\delta_B B_p + D_{\max}\sqrt{KT})$ for communicating MDPs under the low-rank-plus-sparse drift assumption. The headline improvement is the structure-dependent non-stationarity term $D_{\max}\sqrt{KT}$, which scales with $\sqrt{K}$ (the drift rank) rather than $\sqrt{SA}$, yielding an overall $\sqrt{T}$ rate (up to polylog factors) that improves on prior $T^{3/4}$-type guarantees. The sparse-shock residual $D_{\max}\delta_B B_p$ is additive and controlled by the fraction $\delta_B$ of the transition budget consumed by shocks. The bound also includes a planning-tolerance term $\sum_t \varepsilon_{\tau(m(t))}$ that can be made lower-order via a suitable EVI schedule.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical experiments, so all comparisons are in terms of regret rates. The most relevant baseline is SWUCRL2-CW [Cheung et al., 2020], which uses uniform confidence widening and achieves $T^{3/4}$-type dynamic regret guarantees; SVUCRL improves this to $\tilde{O}(\sqrt{T})$ under the structured variation assumption (Assumption 1). The paper also contextualizes its result against the unstructured lower bound of Mao et al. [2024], who show $\Omega((B_r+B_p)^{1/3}T^{2/3})$ for any algorithm when no structure is assumed; SVUCRL's improvement is explicitly conditional on the low-rank drift plus sparse shocks model and does not contradict this lower bound. The computational cost is stated as $\mathcal{O}(TSA(SK+S)\log T)$, which is favorable when $K \ll SA$ compared to full SVD-based approaches. No head-to-head numerical comparisons are provided, as the contribution is entirely in the theoretical regret analysis.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the confidence-widening framework of SWUCRL2-CW (Cheung et al.) by replacing its uniform widening with an adaptive, structure-aware mechanism. It repurposes tools from robust matrix decomposition—specifically Principal Component Pursuit (Candès et al.) for separating low-rank drift from sparse shocks, and randomized SVD with power iterations (Halko et al.) for efficient online tracking—into the sequential decision-making setting. The James-Stein shrinkage combining empirical and forecasted transition estimates, along with bias-corrected local-variation estimators for adaptive confidence radii, are novel integrations that have no direct precedent in the non-stationary RL literature. The paper positions itself as a structural refinement of the variation-budget framework rather than a departure from it, explicitly noting that its √T rate does not contradict the Ω((B_r+B_p)^{1/3} T^{2/3}) lower bound for unstructured non-stationary MDPs (Mao et al.).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The reduction from T^{3/4} to √T (up to logs) under the low-rank-plus-sparse drift assumption is a clean theoretical advance that quantifies exactly how much structure in environmental change translates into sample efficiency—the √K factor replacing √(SA) is the key structural gain. However, the paper is entirely theoretical with no empirical validation, and the structural assumptions (incoherence of the low-rank component, random support for sparse shocks, β-smoothness of time coefficients) may be difficult to verify or satisfy in practice. Open questions include whether the approach extends to continuous state/action spaces, function approximation, and whether the PCP recovery conditions hold for realistic drift patterns. The practical impact will depend on whether real-world non-stationary MDPs genuinely exhibit the assumed low-rank-plus-sparse structure.
