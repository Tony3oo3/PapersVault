## Problem

Existing GUI agents trained via SFT or rule-based RFT deviate from natural human-GUI interaction patterns: they skip the rapid visual localization phase humans perform before reasoning, and their reward signals focus almost exclusively on final action outcomes while providing no process-level guidance for intermediate perception and attention. This limits generalization and makes the agent's interaction logic feel artificial compared to how humans actually scan, plan, and act on interfaces.

## Input / Output

The model receives a GUI screenshot, a global task instruction, and the interaction history as input. It produces a three-part structured output: a <blink> block containing up to 5 ROI bounding boxes in XML format (each with id, bbox coordinates, and a dynamic/static caption), a <think> block with free-form reasoning, and a <link> block with a JSON-formatted action command drawn from a fixed action space (Back, Home, Tap, Type, Swipe, LongPress). The blink block may be empty (None) when no relevant elements are visible on the current screen, which is explicitly permitted by the reward design.

## Method

BTL-UI decomposes each interaction step into three biologically motivated phases—Blink (ROI localization), Think (reasoning), and Link (action generation)—and trains a Qwen2.5-VL-3B/7B backbone via GRPO reinforcement fine-tuning. Two key innovations support this: (1) a two-stage Blink Data Generation pipeline that first parses UI elements with a dedicated parser and then uses Qwen2.5-VL-32B to filter and rank them into task-relevant ROIs, and (2) a BTL Reward comprising three rule-based components—Dual Format Reward (binary check on template and content validity), Blink Reward (Hungarian-matching IoU-based scoring of predicted ROIs against ground-truth annotations, with special handling for empty predictions), and Link Reward (strict all-or-nothing binary check requiring both action type and arguments to be exactly correct). The GRPO objective computes group-relative advantages over N sampled completions per task sample, eliminating the need for a separate value model while the KL divergence constraint prevents drift from the reference policy.

## Results

On the ScreenSpot grounding benchmark, BTL-UI-7B achieves 87.2% average click-point accuracy, outperforming the Qwen2.5-VL-7B base (84.8%) and the SFT-trained Aria-UI (82.4%), though trailing UI-TARS-7B (89.5%). On the harder ScreenSpot-Pro benchmark with professional high-resolution applications, BTL-UI-3B reaches 27.1% average accuracy, substantially exceeding UI-R1-3B (17.8%). For multi-step planning, BTL-UI-7B achieves 88.0% step success rate on AndroidControl-Low (vs. OS-Atlas-Pro-7B at 85.2% and GUI-R1-7B at 66.5%), 69.2% on AndroidControl-High, and 65.4% on GUI-Odyssey (vs. GUI-R1-7B at 38.8%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest SFT baseline UI-TARS-7B, BTL-UI-7B trails on all planning metrics (e.g., AndroidControl-High SR 69.2% vs. 72.5%; GUI-Odyssey SR 65.4% vs. 87.0%) and on grounding (ScreenSpot 87.2% vs. 89.5%), though it narrows the gap considerably given UI-TARS was trained on 50B tokens. Against the large SFT model OS-Atlas-Pro-7B, BTL-UI-7B wins on AndroidControl-Low SR (88.0% vs. 85.2%) and GUI-Odyssey SR (65.4% vs. 62.0%) but slightly lags on AndroidControl-High SR (69.2% vs. 71.2%). Among RFT-based methods, BTL-UI dominates: BTL-UI-7B surpasses GUI-R1-7B by 21.5 points on AndroidControl-High SR (69.2% vs. 51.7%) and by 26.6 points on GUI-Odyssey SR (65.4% vs. 38.8%), while BTL-UI-3B outperforms UI-R1-3B by 18.0 points on AndroidControl-High SR (63.4% vs. 45.4%) and by 31.5 points on GUI-Odyssey SR (64.0% vs. 32.5%). On grounding, BTL-UI-3B achieves 27.1% on ScreenSpot-Pro versus 17.8% for UI-R1-3B, a 9.3-point margin that the authors attribute to the Blink Phase's ROI supervision enabling more precise element localization under complex visual layouts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the rule-based reinforcement fine-tuning (RFT) paradigm for GUI agents established by UI-R1, GUI-R1, and InfiGUI-R1, which all adopt a Think-Answer output format optimized by outcome-centric rewards. It departs from this lineage by introducing a three-phase Blink-Think-Link structure inspired by cognitive science (saccadic attention → deliberative reasoning → motor execution), adding an explicit ROI-annotation phase and a process-outcome integrated reward (BTL Reward) that provides intermediate supervision beyond final action correctness. The optimization leverages GRPO (from DeepSeek-R1) on top of Qwen2.5-VL, and the Blink data generation pipeline repurposes OmniParser and a VLM to automate ROI annotation, avoiding manual labeling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The process-outcome integrated reward mechanism addresses a recognized gap in RFT-based GUI agents where intermediate reasoning steps receive no direct gradient signal, and the Blink phase provides a lightweight, interpretable attention bottleneck that improves grounding without requiring a separate detection head. The framework's cognitive grounding makes it a natural candidate for extension to embodied or multi-modal interaction tasks beyond 2D GUIs. Key open questions include whether the ROI annotation pipeline generalizes to novel or highly dynamic interfaces, whether the added output tokens (blink regions) become a bottleneck at scale, and whether the performance gap to heavily pre-trained SFT agents like UI-TARS (trained on 50B tokens) narrows with more RFT data or longer training.
