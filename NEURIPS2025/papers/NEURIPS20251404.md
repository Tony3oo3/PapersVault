## Problem

RL trading agents trained on historical data ignore the fact that their own orders move prices (market impact), causing a train-deploy mismatch. Existing robust RL methods handle this via symmetric uncertainty sets (ℓ_p-norm balls) that treat upward and downward price shifts as equally plausible, forcing the agent to hedge against implausible perturbations and producing overly conservative, unprofitable policies. The paper asks whether a non-symmetric uncertainty structure can capture the directional nature of market impact while retaining closed-form tractability.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a nominal transition kernel P₀ estimated from one year of historical minute-level price/volume data, plus per-state-action elliptic uncertainty parameters: a set of foci {u_n} encoding the expected directional shift (e.g., one focus for buy-impact, one for sell-impact) and a scalar size β. It produces a robust trading policy (discrete buy/sell/hold actions), the robust value function V^π, and the worst-case perturbation u* computed in closed form at each TD step. The zero-sum constraint u^T 1 = 0 ensures the perturbed transition remains a valid probability distribution, and Assumption 3.3 bounds β so no entry of P₀ + u goes negative.

## Method

The core contribution is the elliptic (s,a)-uncertainty set {u : Σ_n ‖u − u_n‖_p ≤ β, u^T 1 = 0}, which generalizes ℓ_p-norm balls by allowing multiple foci to encode directional bias, so only plausible perturbations (e.g., upward shift under a buy action) are included. The paper derives an implicit closed-form solution (Theorem 3.4) via Fenchel conjugates and a Lagrangian dual, and explicit closed-form solutions (Theorem 3.5) for three tractable cases: N=1 (recovers the ℓ_p ball), p=1 with N=2, and p=2 with N=2. The resulting robust TD update (Algorithm 1) is identical to standard TD learning plus a single correction term ηγv^T u*, requiring no external optimization loop or additional environment interaction. This makes the approach as computationally efficient as the ℓ_p-norm baseline while eliminating the implausible symmetric perturbations that cause unnecessary conservatism.

## Results

On single-asset intra-day trading (June–December 2022, with simulated market impact via LOB reconstruction and VWAP execution), the proposed ℓ_p-ellipse robust RL achieves the highest Sharpe ratio across all three assets: 2.48 on META (90.8% annualized return), 1.20 on MSFT (24.4%), and 1.60 on SPY (19.4%). The symmetric ℓ_p-ball robust RL baseline performs poorly, yielding negative Sharpe ratios on META (−0.28) and MSFT (−0.82), confirming that symmetric uncertainty sets are overly conservative. In the multi-asset portfolio rebalancing experiment, the ℓ_p-ellipse agent maintains a small and stable relative portfolio gap under increasing trade volumes (up to ~$200M), while the non-robust agent's gap grows sharply.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against three baselines: a non-RL momentum strategy [Zarattini et al., 2024], standard (non-robust) RL, and symmetric robust RL using ℓ_p-norm balls [Kumar et al., 2023]. The proposed ℓ_p-ellipse method decisively outperforms the symmetric ℓ_p-ball robust RL on all three assets in Sharpe ratio (META: 2.48 vs. −0.28; MSFT: 1.20 vs. −0.82; SPY: 1.60 vs. 0.17), demonstrating that the directional non-symmetry of the elliptic set avoids the excessive conservatism of ball-shaped sets. Against the non-robust RL baseline, the proposed method wins on all assets (META: 2.48 vs. 1.74; MSFT: 1.20 vs. −1.75; SPY: 1.60 vs. −1.64), with the gap widening on MSFT and SPY where market impact is more damaging. Against the momentum strategy, the proposed method wins on META (2.48 vs. −0.95) and MSFT (1.20 vs. 1.10) but trails slightly on SPY (1.60 vs. 1.69), where the momentum signal is particularly strong and market impact is relatively mild. In the volume-scaling experiment, the ℓ_p-ellipse agent maintains a stable relative portfolio gap at high volumes (~$200M) while the non-robust agent degrades rapidly, confirming robustness under increasing trade sizes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the ℓ_p-norm uncertainty set framework for robust MDPs (Kumar et al., 2023) by replacing the symmetric ball with an elliptic set defined as the sublevel set of a sum of distances to multiple foci, directly motivated by the directional asymmetry of market impact in limit-order-book dynamics. The robust policy gradient machinery and TD-learning structure are inherited from Li et al. (2022) and Kumar et al. (2023), while the Fenchel-conjugate and Hölder-inequality toolkit used to derive closed-form worst-case solutions follows the standard convex-analysis recipe from Boyd & Vandenberghe. The key conceptual departure is that prior robust RL for finance (Jaimungal et al., 2022; Shi et al., 2023; Wu et al., 2023) has exclusively used symmetric uncertainty sets, which the paper argues are structurally incapable of encoding the one-sided price shifts induced by buy vs. sell orders.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The elliptic uncertainty sets offer a principled, computationally tractable way to inject directional market-impact knowledge into RL training without requiring expensive LOB simulators or high-fidelity market microstructure data, which is practically significant for fund managers who must train on historical data but deploy in live markets. The closed-form (implicit and explicit) solutions for the worst-case transition kernel eliminate the need for nested optimization loops, making the approach scalable to high-frequency or multi-asset settings. Limitations include the lack of a guarantee against over-conservatism (the uncertainty set may still contain implausible perturbations), the absence of absolute-continuity guarantees relative to the nominal kernel, and experimental validation on only three large-cap assets over a single six-month window with a deterministic evaluation protocol (no error bars or multiple seeds). Open questions include whether the foci can be learned end-to-end, how the approach interacts with f-divergence or Wasserstein-based robustness, and whether the gains persist in illiquid or multi-asset correlated settings.
