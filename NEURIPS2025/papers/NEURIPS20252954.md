## Problem

Existing black-box jailbreak methods rely on genetic algorithms or search over manually curated prompt pools, requiring expensive per-prompt optimization and providing no principled objective for gradient-based learning. This makes them slow, brittle, and unable to produce a comprehensive characterization of model vulnerabilities. The paper asks whether a distributional, variational-inference formulation can replace ad-hoc search with an amortized, end-to-end training procedure for adversarial prompt generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252954/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a textual description of a target harmful behavior (e.g., 'how to build a bomb') and API access to a black-box target LLM, along with a judge function that scores response harmfulness in [0,1]. The attacker is a small pretrained LLM (default: Vicuna-7B) parameterized with LoRA adapters, conditioned via a system prompt on the target behavior. After training (batch size 32, LR 1e-3, KL coefficient 0.8, up to 10 epochs), the attacker generates diverse, fluent jailbreak prompts via a single forward pass—no further optimization or search is needed at inference time.

## Method

VERA formulates black-box jailbreak prompting as posterior inference: finding prompts x that are likely to elicit harmful outputs y* from the target LLM, i.e., sampling from P_LM(x | y* ∈ Y_harm). A small attacker LLM with LoRA adapters serves as the variational distribution q_θ(x), and the ELBO objective is optimized via the REINFORCE gradient estimator, where a black-box judge model approximates the intractable likelihood P_LM(y*|x) with a scalar harmfulness score. The ELBO balances three terms: rewarding prompts that produce harmful responses, maintaining fluency through a prior (the unmodified attacker LLM), and encouraging diversity through a KL regularization that prevents mode collapse. This distributional approach amortizes the cost of attack generation—once trained, the attacker produces diverse, behavior-specific jailbreak prompts in parallel via lightweight decoding, without the sequential mutation-and-evaluation loops required by genetic-algorithm baselines.

## Results

On the HarmBench benchmark (400 harmful behaviors, 8 target LLMs), VERA achieves a mean Attack Success Rate (ASR) of 50.5%, the highest among all 16 compared methods. Headline per-model ASRs include 72.0% on Orca2-7B, 70.0% on Vicuna-7B, 64.8% on Baichuan2-7B, 63.5% on R2D2 (adversarially trained Zephyr), 53.3% on GPT-3.5-Turbo, and 48.5% on Gemini-Pro. On the AdvBench subset, VERA reaches 86.0% ASR on Vicuna-7B and 16.0% on LLaMA2-7B-Chat, outperforming GCG, AutoDAN, GPTFuzzer, and PAIR on Vicuna-7B.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VERA outperforms every black-box baseline across all 8 target models. Against the strongest black-box competitor TAP-T, VERA gains +12.2 pp on average (50.5% vs 40.9%), with the largest margins on Gemini-Pro (+17.3 pp) and LLaMA2-13B (+13.0 pp). Against AutoDAN, VERA leads by +15.7 pp on average, with particularly large gaps on R2D2 (+46.5 pp) and LLaMA2 models (+10.3 and +20.2 pp). Among white-box methods, VERA surpasses GCG on 5 of 7 open-source targets (e.g., +26.0 pp on Orca2-7B, +58.0 pp on R2D2) but trails on LLaMA2-7B (10.8% vs 32.5%) and LLaMA2-13B (21.0% vs 30.0%), which the authors attribute to LLaMA2's strong RLHF-based safety alignment that is harder to bypass without gradient access. On closed-source models where most white-box methods are inapplicable, VERA achieves 53.3% on GPT-3.5 and 48.5% on Gemini-Pro, exceeding the best black-box baselines (TAP-T: 47.5% and 31.2%) by 5.8 and 17.3 pp respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reframes black-box jailbreak prompt generation as a variational inference problem, departing from the dominant paradigms of genetic-algorithm mutation (GPTFuzzer, AutoDAN), LLM-guided tree search (PAIR, TAP), and two-stage RL agents (RL-Jack, DRL-guided search). Rather than searching for individual adversarial prompts, VERA trains a small attacker LLM (via LoRA) to approximate the posterior distribution over prompts that elicit harmful outputs, using the ELBO objective and REINFORCE gradient estimation. The authors explicitly draw a structural parallel to RLHF (Appendix A.2), where the attacker LLM plays the role of the policy and the judge score serves as the reward, but the key distinction is that VERA amortizes attack generation into a single forward pass at test time rather than requiring per-prompt optimization loops.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The distributional framing enables comprehensive red-teaming by generating diverse, fluent, template-independent jailbreaks in a single pass, addressing a practical bottleneck in current automated red-teaming pipelines. The demonstrated robustness to Circuit Breaker (9.6% ASR vs. 0% for GCG/AutoDAN) and strong transferability across model families raise concrete concerns for the LLM safety community. Key limitations include the need for per-behavior retraining (limiting scalability to large behavior sets), continued dependence on black-box queries during training, and degraded convergence under sparse-reward regimes (highly robust targets). Open questions include whether a single behavior-generalizable attacker can be trained, and how defenses should adapt to distributional rather than point-estimate adversarial prompts.
