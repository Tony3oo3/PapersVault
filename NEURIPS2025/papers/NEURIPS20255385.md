## Problem

Label Smoothing (LS), a widely used regularizer, has two underexplored pathologies: it paradoxically increases overconfidence on misclassified samples and compresses intra-class feature representations into overly tight clusters. The precise mechanism behind these effects remained unclear, as prior work identified the symptoms but not the root cause within the loss landscape.

## Input / Output

MaxSup is a drop-in loss modification that operates on the K-dimensional logit vector produced by any classification head (CNN or ViT) fed standard RGB images (224×224 for ImageNet, 32×32 for CIFAR). It produces the same outputs as the base model—class probabilities for classification or, after fine-tuning, pixel-wise segmentation masks—but the training signal is altered: instead of penalizing the ground-truth logit z_gt as LS does, it penalizes the top-1 logit z_max. No architectural changes, input preprocessing changes, or output format changes are required.

## Method

The paper analytically decomposes the LS-induced loss L_LS = α(z_gt − (1/K)Σz_k) into two components: a regularization term (penalizing the gap between z_gt and all smaller logits, which reduces overconfidence) and an error-amplification term (penalizing the gap between z_gt and all larger logits, which widens the margin for incorrect predictions). MaxSup eliminates the error-amplification term by replacing the penalty target from z_gt to z_max, yielding L_MaxSup = α(z_max − (1/K)Σz_k). This ensures a uniform regularization signal: for correct predictions (z_gt = z_max), MaxSup behaves identically to LS; for misclassified predictions, it suppresses the erroneous top-1 logit while leaving z_gt unpunished, preventing error reinforcement. The gradient analysis shows the top-1 logit receives a penalty of α(1−1/K) while all other logits receive a small positive nudge of α/K, and the entire computation adds only O(K) overhead (one max and one mean over the logit vector), making it a negligible-cost substitution in any standard training pipeline.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K, MaxSup achieves 77.69% top-1 accuracy with ResNet-50, outperforming Label Smoothing (76.91%), OLS (77.23%), and Zipf-LS (76.73%). On DeiT-Small, MaxSup reaches 76.49% top-1 accuracy, surpassing LS by 0.41% and OLS by 0.33%. For semantic segmentation on ADE20K (UperNet + DeiT-Small), MaxSup-pretrained weights yield 42.8% mIoU versus 42.4% for LS. In linear-probe transfer to CIFAR-10, MaxSup preserves near-baseline accuracy (0.810) while LS degrades to 0.746 and Logit Penalty to 0.724.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MaxSup consistently outperforms all seven compared baselines across architectures and datasets. Against Label Smoothing (Szegedy et al., CVPR 2016), MaxSup gains +0.78% on ImageNet ResNet-50 (77.69% vs 76.91%) and +0.41% on DeiT-Small (76.49% vs 76.08%), while also preserving intra-class variation (d̄_within 0.293 vs 0.263) and transfer accuracy (0.810 vs 0.746 on CIFAR-10 linear probe). Against OLS (Zhang et al., IEEE TIP 2021), MaxSup leads by +0.46% on ImageNet ResNet-50 and +0.33% on DeiT-Small. Against Zipf-LS (Liang et al., ECCV 2022), MaxSup gains +0.96% on ImageNet ResNet-50 and +0.60% on DeiT-Small. Logit Penalty (Dauphin & Cubuk, ICLR 2021) underperforms MaxSup on both accuracy (76.73% vs 77.69%) and transfer (0.724 vs 0.810), confirming its global ℓ₂ shrinkage harms intra-class diversity. Logit Normalization (Wei et al., 2022) achieves only 74.32% on ImageNet ResNet-50, far below MaxSup's 77.69%. Confidence Penalty (Pereyra et al., 2017) reaches 76.58% and Adaptive Label Smoothing (Lee et al., 2022) reaches 77.16%, both trailing MaxSup by 1.11% and 0.53% respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Label Smoothing (LS) [Szegedy et al., 2016] and the subsequent observations that LS compresses intra-class features [Kornblith et al., 2021] and induces overconfidence in misclassified samples [Zhu et al., 2022]. Its key conceptual departure is a logit-level decomposition of the LS loss that isolates a harmful 'error amplification' term, which prior LS variants (OLS, Zipf-LS) and norm-based regularizers (Logit Penalty, Logit Normalization) do not address. MaxSup replaces LS's penalty on the ground-truth logit with a penalty on the top-1 logit, a minimal but principled change that eliminates error amplification while retaining the beneficial regularization component.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MaxSup offers a drop-in replacement for LS with negligible computational overhead that simultaneously improves ImageNet accuracy and, more importantly, preserves intra-class diversity critical for downstream transfer (linear-probe CIFAR-10 accuracy of 0.810 vs. 0.746 for LS). Open questions remain regarding MaxSup's interaction with knowledge distillation pipelines, where LS-trained teachers are known to behave differently, and whether its logit-level penalty interacts adversely with the low-rank bias induced by l2 regularization. The method's simplicity and consistent gains across CNNs and ViTs make it a practical candidate to replace LS in standard training recipes.
