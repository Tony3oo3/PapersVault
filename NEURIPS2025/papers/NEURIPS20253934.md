## Problem

Spatio-Temporal Graph Neural Networks (STGNNs) degrade significantly under Spatio-Temporal Out-of-Distribution (STOOD) shifts, where both graph topology and temporal dynamics evolve beyond the training distribution. Existing continual learning strategies—backbone retraining, architectural modifications, regularization, and replay—either suffer catastrophic forgetting, over-constrain adaptation, or store historical knowledge implicitly in parameters with limited capacity, failing to selectively identify which past patterns provide the most information gain for current predictions.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a time-indexed sequence of dynamic graphs (adjacency matrices A_t and node feature matrices X_t ∈ R^{N×c}) along with three pre-constructed pattern libraries (spatial, temporal, spatio-temporal) that are built incrementally during training. At inference, the current subgraph is projected into a key space, matched against stored keys via exponential L2-distance similarity, and the top-k retrieved values are fused with the backbone's current encoding to produce future node feature predictions over a horizon T_p. The pattern libraries are managed with importance-based sampling to control memory, and the entire framework is plug-and-play across different STGNN backbones (STGCN, ASTGCN, DCRNN, TGCN).

## Method

STRAP decomposes the spatio-temporal graph into three subgraph types—spatial (temporal windowing), temporal (k-hop neighborhood tracking), and spatio-temporal (spectral clustering communities)—and encodes each as a key-value pair in a dedicated library. Pattern keys are discriminative feature vectors: spatial keys concatenate degree statistics, clustering coefficients, shortest-path metrics, and Forman-Ricci curvature; temporal keys combine statistical moments, spectral descriptors, wavelet transforms, autocorrelation, and entropy; spatio-temporal keys are element-wise cross-products of the spatial and temporal keys. Pattern values are mean-pooled embeddings from a frozen pretrained STGNN backbone. At inference, a similarity-weighted retrieval selects top-k patterns per library, and an adaptive fusion mechanism computes a convex combination Z = γ·MLP₁(backbone(G)) + (1−γ)·MLP₂(concatenated retrieved values), where γ is a learnable weight that calibrates the balance between current observations and historical knowledge, enabling robust prediction under both abrupt and gradual distribution shifts without task-specific fine-tuning.

## Results

On the STGNN backbone, STRAP achieves the best average MAE of 20.82 on AIR-Stream, 14.27 on PEMS-Stream, and 5.42 on ENERGY-Stream, with corresponding average MAPE of 26.77%, 19.82%, and 53.19%. The paper reports an overall 7.17% average improvement across all metrics (MAE: 4.88%, RMSE: 9.12%, MAPE: 7.5%) relative to the best baseline. Under the most severe distribution shift (ENERGY-Wind period 3, ~77% mean reduction), STRAP achieves MAPE of 1.19–1.27 across horizons 3/6/12, substantially outperforming all baselines. In few-shot settings (50% missing data on Energy-Wind), STRAP reaches 6.29 MAE, outperforming the best baseline (EWC) by 8.7%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

STRAP achieves the lowest average MAE on AIR-Stream (20.82) and ENERGY-Stream (5.42), beating the closest competitors Replay (21.00 / 5.47) and EAC (21.23 / 5.24) respectively. On PEMS-Stream, ST-Adapter (13.95) and EAC (13.97) edge out STRAP (14.27) on MAE, but STRAP matches or leads on MAPE (19.82 vs. 19.10 for ST-Adapter) and RMSE. Among architecture-based methods, ST-LoRA is the strongest single competitor, achieving the best PEMS-Stream MAPE (18.91%) and competitive MAE (14.03), yet STRAP still leads on AIR-Stream MAE (20.82 vs. 21.22) and ENERGY-Stream MAE (5.42 vs. 5.38 is a near-tie). Regularization-based EWC and replay-based Replay both trail STRAP by 0.18–0.35 MAE on AIR-Stream and by 0.05–0.22 MAE on ENERGY-Stream. In the few-shot evaluation (Table 2, TGCN backbone, 50% missing data), STRAP (6.29 MAE) outperforms the best baseline EWC (6.89) by 8.7%, and under 30% missing data it beats GraphPro (5.97) by 3.4%. The ablation (Figure 2) confirms that the Forman-Ricci curvature spatial key (S3) and statistical/spectral temporal descriptors (T1) are the most critical components, with removal causing 6.32% and 7.96% MAPE degradation respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

STRAP carves new ground by transplanting the retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) paradigm—well-established in NLP and time-series forecasting (e.g., RAFT [20])—into the spatio-temporal graph domain, where prior continual-learning efforts (TrafficStream, PECPM, EAC, ST-LoRA) store historical knowledge implicitly in model parameters or fixed replay buffers. The key conceptual departure is an explicit, externally maintained key-value pattern library decomposed along spatial, temporal, and spatio-temporal axes, decoupling pattern indexing (topological/spectral keys) from pattern utilization (backbone-generated embeddings). This repurposes ideas from graph pattern mining (Forman-Ricci curvature, spectral clustering) and information-theoretic fusion into a plug-and-play module that wraps any STGNN backbone, rather than modifying the backbone architecture itself.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play design means practitioners deploying STGNNs in streaming settings (traffic, energy, climate) can adopt STRAP without re-architecting their models, and the explicit pattern library offers interpretability absent in parameter-based continual learning. However, the approach inherits practical limitations: library size grows with data volume (requiring importance-based pruning), retrieval quality depends on the discriminative power of hand-crafted keys under extreme shifts, and the theoretical guarantee (Theorem B.1) relies on a Markov-chain assumption that may not hold for learned representations. Open questions include adaptive library management under non-stationary streams, uncertainty-aware retrieval, and extension to multi-modal or heterogeneous graph settings.
