## Problem

Invisible image watermarking systems deployed by Gen-AI providers are vulnerable to forgery attacks that falsely attribute harmful content to innocent providers, yet existing forgery methods either require black-box access (algorithm knowledge, paired data, or detector queries) or rely on the unrealistic assumption that watermark signals are constant across images. This paper tackles the more realistic no-box setting, where an attacker has only a collection of watermarked images and no knowledge of the underlying watermarking scheme.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single clean RGB image (any source: real photographs from MS-COCO, ImageNet, CelebA-HQ, or AI-generated images from DiffusionDB) and an auxiliary dataset of ~5,000 watermarked images collected from the target Gen-AI service (e.g., Amazon Titan). It produces a forged image that visually preserves the clean image's semantics (PSNR > 30 dB) while being detected by the target watermark detector as containing the provider's watermark message, with bit accuracy exceeding the verification threshold ρ.

## Method

WMCopier exploits the insight that an unconditional diffusion model trained on watermarked images implicitly encodes the watermark distribution p_w(x) through systematic biases in its noise predictions. The pipeline has three stages: (1) train a DDIM-based diffusion model on the auxiliary watermarked dataset; (2) perform shallow DDIM inversion on the clean image only up to an early timestep T_S (e.g., 40 out of 100) to obtain a latent representation, then denoise using the trained model so the watermark bias naturally steers the output toward p_w(x); (3) apply iterative score-based refinement that estimates ∇log p_w(x) via the noise predictor at a low-noise step, jointly optimizing watermark conformity and MSE fidelity to the original image. The shallow inversion avoids the severe quality degradation of full-step inversion, while the refinement eliminates residual artifacts, achieving near-100% detection success on both open-source schemes and Amazon's closed-source system.

## Results

WMCopier achieves an average forged bit accuracy of 94.58% and FPR@10⁻⁶ of 83.71% across four open-source watermarking schemes (DWT-DCT, HiDDeN, RivaGAN, Stable Signature) and four datasets, while maintaining PSNR above 30 dB on all settings. On the closed-source Amazon Titan watermark, WMCopier achieves a 100% success rate with the highest confidence level (3) and PSNR >30 dB, compared to 28–42% success rate and PSNR <25 dB for the prior no-box method. The refinement step further improves PSNR by 2–6 dB and forged bit accuracy by up to 26 percentage points (e.g., DWT-DCT: 63.03% → 89.62%) over the unrefined shallow inversion.

## Baselines & Comparisons

WMCopier dominates both baselines across all 16 scheme–dataset combinations. Against Yang et al. [10] (the only prior no-box method), WMCopier improves forged bit accuracy from ~54.5% to ~94.6% (a ~40-point gain) and FPR from 0.08% to 83.71%, while also achieving higher PSNR (32.94 vs. 30.62). Against Wang et al. [16] (a black-box method with access to paired clean/watermarked images), WMCopier still surpasses the baseline on both bit accuracy (94.58% vs. 84.32%) and FPR (83.71% vs. 76.64%) despite operating under a strictly weaker no-box assumption. On the closed-source Amazon Titan watermark, WMCopier achieves 100% success rate at confidence level 3 with PSNR >30 dB, whereas Yang et al. achieves only 28–42% success rate at confidence level 2 with PSNR <25 dB. On the semantic watermark Tree-Ring, WMCopier matches Müller et al. [14] at 100% FPR but produces substantially higher visual fidelity (PSNR ~32 dB vs. ~26 dB); on the non-semantic Stable Signature, Müller's method fails entirely (0% FPR) while WMCopier maintains 94.6–100% FPR.

## Relation to Prior Work

WMCopier repurposes the DDIM inversion framework (Ho et al., 2020; Mokady et al., 2023) and score-based refinement (Song et al., 2019) for a security task: instead of editing or generating images, it uses an unconditional diffusion model trained on watermarked samples as an implicit watermark distribution estimator. This departs fundamentally from prior no-box forgery work (Yang et al., 2024), which assumed a fixed pixel-level watermark pattern estimated by averaging residuals, and from black-box methods (Wang et al., 2021) that require paired clean/watermarked data. The key conceptual shift is treating the watermark not as a constant signal but as a distributional property that a diffusion model's noise predictor naturally encodes through systematic prediction bias (δ_t(w)).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that a no-box attacker can forge watermarks on deployed, closed-source systems (Amazon Titan) with near-perfect success rate and high visual fidelity, using only 5,000 collected watermarked images—making the threat concrete for Gen-AI providers. The proposed multi-message defense (randomly selecting from a pool of K messages) completely neutralizes the attack at K≥50, and Amazon has since deployed a fix, validating the practical relevance. Open questions include whether the attack generalizes to in-processing watermarks that are tightly coupled to generation (e.g., Tree-Ring), why different watermark architectures exhibit varying forgery susceptibility, and whether more sophisticated diffusion training strategies could further improve attack efficacy.
