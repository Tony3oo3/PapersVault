## Problem

Discrete autoregressive language models are fundamentally constrained by their reliance on discrete tokens, unidirectional context, and single-pass token-by-token decoding. The paper asks whether reformulating autoregressive language modeling in a continuous latent space could unlock new axes of flexibility—bi-directional context, block-wise multi-token generation, and hierarchical multi-pass refinement—while retaining strong likelihood performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255765/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

During training, discrete token sequences (character-level for TEXT8, GPT-2-tokenized word-level for OpenWebText with 1024-token context) are encoded into per-token Gaussian latent vectors of dimension d=16 (OpenWebText) or d=5 (TEXT8). During generation, samples from a standard d-dimensional Gaussian are inverted through stacked flow layers and decoded into text. The framework supports flexible patch sizes (e.g., generating 2 tokens simultaneously) and allows the internal mixture 'vocabulary' size V to be set independently per flow layer, decoupled from the data's actual vocabulary.

## Method

TarFlowLM bridges discrete tokens and continuous latent space via a VAE with a factorized encoder (each token maps to a learnable isotropic Gaussian) and a tied Bayesian decoder. The prior over latent sequences is modeled by stacked Transformer-based autoregressive normalizing flows, where each layer uses a novel mixture-based coupling: a 1D Mixture-CDF flow (dimension-wise autoregression) or a d-D Mixture-Rosenblatt flow (token-wise autoregression), both proven to be exact invertible transformations between a Mixture-of-Gaussians density and a standard Gaussian. Stacking flows with alternating left-to-right and right-to-left directions captures global bi-directional context, while channel mixing (permuting latent dimensions between layers) enables cross-token information sharing within patches. The model is trained by maximizing the ELBO with progressive layer-wise training—freezing earlier flow blocks before adding new ones—which stabilizes optimization in deep flow architectures.

## Results

On TEXT8 (character-level), TarFlowLM Mix-d achieves 1.30 BPC, outperforming all other continuous-space normalizing flow baselines (Latent NF ≤1.61, CNF ≤1.45, Argmax Flow ≤1.45) and matching MD4 (≤1.37) among diffusion methods, though trailing the best discrete AR Transformer (1.23 BPC). On OpenWebText (word-level), TarFlowLM Mix-d reaches 22.64 PPL, competitive with GenMD4 (≤21.80) and MD4 (≤22.13), while the discrete AR Transformer achieves 17.54 PPL. The Mix-1 variant (1.37 BPC / 27.11 PPL) is on par with Gaussian diffusion (≤27.28 PPL), and the affine-only baseline (1.54 BPC / 148.21 PPL) shows the critical importance of mixture coupling for discrete data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among continuous-space normalizing flow methods, TarFlowLM Mix-d (1.30 BPC / 22.64 PPL) clearly outperforms Latent NF (≤1.61 BPC), CNF (≤1.45 BPC), and Argmax Flow (≤1.45 BPC), establishing it as the strongest NF-based language model in continuous space. Against discrete-space diffusion baselines, TarFlowLM Mix-d is competitive: it matches MD4 on TEXT8 (1.30 vs. ≤1.37 BPC) and is close to GenMD4 on OpenWebText (22.64 vs. ≤21.80 PPL), while trailing the best discrete diffusion methods EDLM (≤1.24 BPC) and TCSM (≤1.25 BPC) on TEXT8. The discrete AR Transformer remains the overall best (1.23 BPC / 17.54 PPL), but TarFlowLM narrows the gap substantially while offering bi-directional context, block-wise generation, and hierarchical refinement that discrete AR cannot provide. The ablation from affine to mixture coupling is dramatic: on OpenWebText, the affine baseline (≤148.21 PPL) is catastrophically worse than Mix-1 (≤27.11) and Mix-d (≤22.64), confirming that mixture-based transformations are essential for modeling the multi-modal distributions induced by discrete tokens in continuous space.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the transformer-based autoregressive normalizing flow framework of Zhai et al. [73] to language modeling by embedding it within a VAE that bridges discrete tokens and a continuous latent space. It departs from prior latent-flow language models (Ziegler & Rush 2019; Lippe & Gavves 2021) by introducing mixture-based coupling transformations (Mixture-CDF and Mixture-Rosenblatt flows) that exactly realize MoG densities as invertible flow layers, and by leveraging Rosenblatt's theorem [55] for the multi-dimensional case. The work also draws a theoretical bridge to standard discrete autoregressive language models, showing how the d-dimensional mixture prior with a shared codebook recovers the next-token prediction structure, while the continuous formulation additionally enables bi-directional stacking, block-wise generation, and hierarchical refinement that are intractable in the discrete setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TarFlowLM opens a genuinely new design axis for language modeling—continuous latent space with autoregressive flows—that unlocks flexibility (bi-directional context, variable patch sizes, coarse-to-fine hierarchical generation) unavailable to standard discrete AR models. However, the current instantiation remains below the strongest discrete AR and diffusion baselines in likelihood (1.30 BPC vs. 1.23 on TEXT8; 22.64 PPL vs. 17.54 on OpenWebText), and the sequential nature of autoregressive flows limits sampling speed. Key open questions include whether the flexibility advantages (e.g., block-wise generation, intermediate-stage editing) translate into practical quality gains at scale, how to accelerate sampling, and whether the framework can be extended to larger models and longer contexts.
