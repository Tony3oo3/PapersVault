## Problem

Existing NeRF-based local color editing methods suffer from imprecise object boundaries, color bleeding into non-edited regions, and poor multi-view consistency, largely because NeRF's fully connected architecture couples all parameters globally. Prior approaches relying on point cloud projection, voxel expansion, or 2D feature distillation cannot accurately delineate local contours or distinguish similar colors, and they typically require dense manual annotations or per-color foreground masks that scale poorly with the number of edit targets.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-trained NeRF (frozen density σ and 256-dim prior features f_prior), multi-view RGB images with camera poses, and only 1 (forward-facing) or 3 (360°) coarse masks auto-generated by point-prompted SAM with no manual refinement. It produces a 3D-consistent segmentation map, a learnable view-invariant palette P, and per-point weight maps W that are volume-rendered into locally recolored novel views; after training, users can interactively modify palette entries to composite new color schemes without retraining.

## Method

KaRF builds a unified two-stage KAN-based radiance field pipeline: a segmentation stage that maps positional encodings γ(x) and NeRF prior features f_prior to per-point class probabilities via a residual adaptive gating KAN, and a recoloring stage that additionally conditions on viewing direction γ(d) to predict per-point palette weights. The core architectural novelty is the GRBFKAN layer, which replaces vanilla KAN B-spline edges with Gaussian Radial Basis Functions (learnable centers, weights, and bandwidth) to better capture high-frequency color transitions, combined with a residual adaptive gating structure that uses a 2-layer KAN backbone, a context-dependent gating vector, a channel-wise adaptive operator β, and a learnable modulation factor α to regulate feature flow. During recoloring, a palette-adaptive reconstruction loss (L_Palette) assigns all viewpoint-dependent effects (specular highlights, shadows) to the weight maps while keeping the palette view-invariant, enabling multi-view-consistent editing by simply swapping palette colors. The entire pipeline is weakly supervised, requiring only the coarse SAM masks and the LoCoPalette-derived initial palettes/weights as training signals.

## Results

On the primary local color editing task, KaRF achieves a mean MSE of 0.0002 across LLFF, NeRF-Synthetic, and Mip-NeRF 360, substantially outperforming LAENeRF (0.0028), PaletteNeRF+LSeg (0.0038), and ICENeRF (0.0075). For the segmentation stage, KaRF attains a mean IoU of 94.7% and mean accuracy of 99.5%, surpassing SA3D (93.7% IoU, 99.2% Acc) and SPIn-NeRF/MVSeg (90.7% IoU, 98.8% Acc). In multi-view consistency evaluation, KaRF also leads with the lowest short-range LPIPS (0.173) and long-range LPIPS (0.362) after recoloring.

## Baselines & Comparisons

KaRF decisively outperforms all four local color editing baselines on the MSE metric for non-edited regions: it reduces error by ~14× over LAENeRF (0.0002 vs. 0.0028 mean), ~19× over PaletteNeRF+LSeg (0.0038), and ~37× over ICENeRF (0.0075). In segmentation, KaRF beats SA3D by 1.0 IoU points (94.7% vs. 93.7%) and SPIn-NeRF/MVSeg by 4.0 IoU points (94.7% vs. 90.7%), with the largest gap on the Fork scene (84.4% vs. 87.9% for MVSeg, though KaRF is slightly lower here at 84.4% vs. SA3D's 89.8%). For multi-view consistency, KaRF achieves the best short-range LPIPS (0.173 vs. 0.182 for LAENeRF, 0.183 for PNF) and long-range LPIPS (0.362 vs. 0.377 for LAENeRF, 0.372 for PNF), confirming superior view coherence. In the user study (528 votes), KaRF is consistently preferred over both PaletteNeRF and LAENeRF for visual quality and consistency.

## Relation to Prior Work

KaRF sits at the intersection of two active research threads: NeRF-based local color editing (PaletteNeRF, ICENeRF, LAENeRF, RecolorNeRF) and the recent introduction of Kolmogorov-Arnold Networks as an alternative to MLPs. It departs from prior editing methods by replacing their MLP-based color and segmentation heads with a KAN architecture that uses Gaussian Radial Basis Functions instead of B-splines, and by unifying segmentation and recoloring into a single two-stage pipeline that inherits geometric priors from a frozen pre-trained NeRF. The palette extraction and layer decomposition strategy is borrowed from LoCoPalette, while the weakly-supervised mask generation (1–3 SAM prompts) follows the spirit of SPIn-NeRF and SA3D but replaces their feature-alignment or 2D-model-distillation mechanisms with a dedicated KAN-based segmentation radiance field.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work demonstrates that KANs can serve as a drop-in replacement for MLPs in NeRF editing pipelines with measurable gains in boundary precision and multi-view consistency, opening a path toward more interpretable and parameter-efficient radiance-field editing. However, the evaluation is limited to three standard datasets with a small number of scenes, no error bars are reported, and the code was not yet publicly available at submission, making independent verification difficult. Open questions include whether the KAN advantage persists at larger scene scales or with more complex material models, how the method handles dynamic or multi-object scenes with overlapping color palettes, and whether the two-stage dependency (segmentation must precede recoloring) limits real-time or interactive use.
