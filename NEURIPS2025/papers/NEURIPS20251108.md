## Problem

Neural solvers for NP-hard routing problems cannot find optimal solutions in a single attempt, yet existing inference-time adaptation methods fail to fully exploit the available computational budget. Policy-gradient fine-tuning (e.g., EAS) is constrained by the learning rate, limiting how much each collected transition can influence the update, while diversity-based search (e.g., COMPASS) can only select among pre-trained policies without updating them using newly gathered data. The paper asks whether a learned, memory-based mechanism can adapt action distributions more effectively than back-propagation-based updates within a fixed inference budget.

## Input / Output

The method takes a routing problem instance (node coordinates for TSP or CVRP), a frozen pre-trained base policy (e.g., POMO or COMPASS), and a growing memory of past transitions. Each memory entry stores eight features per transition: the node visited, the action taken, the base model's log-probability, the trajectory return, the remaining budget at collection time, the previously suggested MEMENTO logit, the full-trajectory log-probability, and the remaining-trajectory log-probability. At each decision step, the module retrieves all past transitions recorded at the current node, processes them through a small MLP, and outputs a vector of correction logits that are added to the base policy's logits before sampling the next action. The final output is a complete tour or set of vehicle routes.

## Method

MEMENTO introduces a lightweight memory-and-processing module that learns an expressive update rule for the action distribution at inference time. During rollout, every transition is stored in a node-keyed memory; when the policy visits a node, all previously recorded transitions at that node are retrieved and their features (logp, return, remaining budget, trajectory logp, age, prior MEMENTO logit) are concatenated, normalized, and passed through a small MLP that outputs a scalar weight per past action. The correction logits are computed as a weighted sum of one-hot action vectors, then added to the base policy's logits to form the updated action distribution. Training uses a multi-shot objective inspired by ECO-DQN: the reward for each new trajectory is the ReLU of its improvement over the best return so far, weighted logarithmically by budget consumption, encouraging the module to learn when to explore versus exploit. The resulting update rule is architecture-agnostic (operating on logits rather than parameters), can be zero-shot transferred to unseen solvers like COMPASS, and empirically learns asymmetric, budget-aware rules that outperform REINFORCE-style policy gradients.

## Results

On the standard benchmark (10k instances, n=100), MEMENTO achieves a 0.045% optimality gap on TSP and 0.066% on CVRP, outperforming EAS (0.161% and 0.081%) and POMO sampling (0.185% and 0.399%) across all 8 in- and out-of-distribution tasks. When zero-shot combined with COMPASS, MEMENTO reaches −0.082% gap on CVRP100 (beating LKH3) and 1.507% gap on TSP500 (high budget), surpassing the previous SOTA MOCO (1.72%). On TSP500, MEMENTO(COMPASS) reduces the gap to optimality by over 10% relative to MOCO, and on CVRP500 low-budget it achieves 0.215% gap vs. COMPASS's 0.287%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MEMENTO outperforms EAS (policy-gradient fine-tuning) on all 8 standard benchmark tasks for both TSP and CVRP; on TSP100 the improvement over sampling is 60% for MEMENTO vs. only 6% for EAS. Against SGBS (tree-search), MEMENTO achieves lower gaps on all reported settings, though SGBS results carry a setup bias (different POMO checkpoint, augmentation trick). The zero-shot combination MEMENTO(COMPASS) beats COMPASS alone on every task, reaching −0.082% gap on CVRP100 (below LKH3) and 1.808% on CVRP200. On TSP500, MEMENTO(COMPASS) (1.507% high-budget) outperforms the previous SOTA MOCO (1.72%) and all other RL methods (DIMES 7.55%, DIFUSCO 4.08%, DeepACO 13.23%), while remaining behind the exact solver Concorde (0.000%). On CVRP500 low-budget, MEMENTO(COMPASS) achieves 0.215% gap, beating COMPASS (0.287%) and EAS (0.525%), though EAS slightly edges out MEMENTO on CVRP500 high-budget with large parallel batches (>80).

## Relation to Prior Work

MEMENTO sits at the intersection of two lines of work: (1) RL-based construction solvers for routing (POMO, Attention Model) and (2) inference-time adaptation methods that exploit a compute budget beyond a single rollout. It departs from the dominant policy-gradient fine-tuning approach (EAS) by replacing backpropagation-based updates with a learned, memory-conditioned logit correction, drawing conceptual inspiration from retrieval-augmented generation in NLP and memory-augmented RL. It is explicitly positioned as orthogonal to diversity-based solvers like COMPASS, and the paper demonstrates this orthogonality empirically through zero-shot combination. Unlike prior learned-improvement methods (MOCO, MARCO, FER) that are tied to heatmap-based policies, MEMENTO operates directly on action logits, making it architecture-agnostic.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first RL-trained POMO/COMPASS checkpoints at 500-node scale and demonstrates that memory-based adaptation can match or exceed policy-gradient fine-tuning while being more data-efficient under low budgets and scaling better in wall-clock time as instance size and decoder depth grow. The zero-shot transfer of a POMO-trained memory module to the population-based COMPASS solver (achieving SOTA on 11/12 tasks without retraining) suggests a modular 'adaptation layer' paradigm that could be attached to future solvers. Open questions include whether the 50%-budget activation heuristic for COMPASS is optimal, whether the approach extends beyond routing to other CO domains (scheduling, graph partitioning), and whether the learned update rule can be distilled into a closed-form expression to eliminate the MLP overhead.
