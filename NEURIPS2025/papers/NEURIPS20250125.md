## Problem

Majority voting across multiple LLMs improves annotation reliability but incurs high computational cost, and prior LLM routing or ensembling methods require pre-trained models or ground-truth labels. No existing approach adaptively selects a cost-effective subset of LLMs per instance in a fully online, zero-shot setting without any held-out training data.

## Input / Output

The method processes an unlabeled text dataset sequentially, one instance per round. For each instance, it generates a 384-dimensional contextual embedding via all-MiniLM-L6-v2 (used internally as the bandit context), queries a dynamically selected subset of K LLMs (each with a known per-token cost in dollars), and produces a predicted class label via weighted majority vote. The output also implicitly determines which subset was queried, and cost is tracked as average dollars per million input tokens. All LLM queries are zero-shot with no task-specific fine-tuning, and the method requires no ground-truth labels at any point.

## Method

CaMVo combines a LinUCB-style contextual bandit with a Bayesian Beta-mixture confidence estimator to select the minimum-cost LLM subset per instance. For each LLM, it maintains a LinUCB matrix A_i and vector b_i, computing a lower confidence bound (LCB) on correctness probability conditioned on the input embedding; a Beta-mixture posterior then converts this LCB into a calibrated correctness estimate using the LLM's empirical relative accuracy as a prior. Laplace smoothing with a log(t) term regularizes early estimates to prevent compounding errors. An Oracle solves a combinatorial subset-selection problem (approximated via a Beta-CDF) to find the cheapest subset whose aggregated majority-vote confidence exceeds a user-specified threshold δ, subject to a minimum vote count k_min. The key design choice is using the LCB rather than a UCB, which deliberately expands the candidate set to encourage exploration; parameters update online after each round using agreement with the assigned label as a proxy reward.

## Results

On MMLU (14,042 instances), CaMVo at δ=0.97 and k_min=1 achieves 88.35% accuracy at a cost of $6.67 per million tokens, surpassing both the Majority Vote and Baseline Method (88.18% at $9.14) while reducing cost by ~27%. On IMDB Movie Reviews (50,000 instances), CaMVo at δ=0.997 and k_min=1 attains 95.45% accuracy at $2.83, a 0.17% accuracy drop relative to the baseline (95.61%) while halving query expenditure from $6.29. The best individual LLM on MMLU (o3-mini) reaches only 85.92%, confirming that selective ensembling via CaMVo captures ensemble gains at a fraction of the full-ensemble cost.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares CaMVo against three baselines: (1) individual LLMs, (2) full-ensemble Majority Vote using ground-truth accuracy weights, and (3) an online weighted majority voting baseline (Algorithm 2) that queries all K LLMs every round. On MMLU, CaMVo at δ=0.97 beats both ensemble baselines on both accuracy (88.35% vs. 88.18%) and cost ($6.67 vs. $9.14), a ~27% cost reduction with a slight accuracy gain. On IMDB, where individual models already achieve >94% accuracy and ensembling provides minimal benefit, CaMVo at δ=0.997 incurs only a 0.17% accuracy drop (95.45% vs. 95.61%) while cutting cost by 55% ($2.83 vs. $6.29). On AG News, CaMVo similarly achieves less than half the baseline cost while meeting its target accuracy. The key finding is that CaMVo tracks or surpasses the Pareto frontier of all possible LLM subsets without any pre-training or ground-truth labels, whereas the baselines are locked into querying all models at full cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

CaMVo repurposes the LinUCB contextual bandit framework (Li et al., 2010) from single-item recommendation to multi-LLM subset selection, combining it with a novel Bayesian Beta-mixture confidence estimator that has no direct precedent in the LLM ensemble literature. It departs from prior LLM ensemble work (Chen et al. 2024, Yang et al. 2023) by operating fully online without ground-truth labels or offline training, and from LLM routing work (Nguyen et al. 2024, Ding et al. 2024) by selecting a cost-minimizing subset for weighted majority voting rather than routing to a single model. The paper positions itself as the first formulation where both vote weights and the queried LLM subset are adapted in real time per instance, bridging the gap between contextual bandits, Bayesian confidence estimation, and ensemble aggregation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for organizations performing large-scale LLM-based annotation, where per-token costs accumulate quickly across thousands of instances; CaMVo offers a principled, user-tunable mechanism to cut costs by 40–50% while meeting a specified accuracy floor. The key open question is the independence assumption among LLM outputs, which is visibly violated on easier tasks (IMDB) and causes the method to lag the Pareto frontier in higher-cost regimes; the correlated extension (CCaMVo) yields only marginal gains, leaving principled handling of inter-model correlation unresolved. Extensions to regression, ranking, and iterative relabeling are outlined but not yet validated, and the single-run experimental protocol (no error bars, one dataset shuffle per main result) limits confidence in the generality of the reported trade-offs.
