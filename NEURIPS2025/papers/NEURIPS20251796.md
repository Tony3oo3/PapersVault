## Problem

Multimodal Alzheimer's disease diagnosis in clinical practice is hindered by two intertwined challenges: (1) modalities are frequently missing due to acquisition cost, radiation risk, or patient withdrawal, yet existing methods either discard incomplete cases or introduce noisy imputed data; and (2) 3D CNNs cannot efficiently capture long-range spatial dependencies while Transformers incur quadratic complexity on high-resolution 3D brain volumes. No prior framework simultaneously addresses heterogeneous modality incompleteness and efficient 3D spatial modeling for AD diagnosis.

## Input / Output

The framework accepts any non-empty subset of four heterogeneous modalities per subject: 3D T1-weighted MRI and 3D 18F-AV45 PET volumes (skull-stripped, MNI-normalized, cubic resolution 2^N × 2^N × 2^N), a small set of CSF biomarker values (Aβ, Tau, p-Tau), and 29 clinical cognitive examination scores. The 3D images are serialized into 1D sequences via multi-view Hilbert curves before SSM processing, while tabular modalities pass through MLPs. The output is a three-class diagnostic probability vector over CN, MCI, and AD, produced by a hybrid late-fusion that combines a cross-modal MoE decoder with direct modality-specific classifiers.

## Method

The HAD framework has three core components. First, the HMamba block serializes 3D voxels along L differently-oriented Hilbert curves (exploiting the locality-preserving property of space-filling curves to minimize spatial tearing), applies selective state space models to each 1D view, and fuses the results via learnable voxel-wise dynamic weighting. Second, the HSFE module stacks HMamba blocks with 3D residual convolutions at K progressively downsampled resolutions, concatenating multi-scale outputs to capture both local texture and global dependencies with linear complexity. Third, a mutual information maximization objective (max I(g;y) + α·I(g;z)) drives training: the first term is a cross-entropy loss on a hybrid late-fusion prediction that blends a dynamic MoE cross-modal decoder with direct per-modality classifiers (preventing modality collapse), while the second term is decomposed into intra-modal and inter-modal reconstruction losses that enforce semantic consistency across heterogeneous modalities without erasing modality-specific information.

## Results

On the ADNI dataset, HAD is evaluated on three binary classification tasks (AD vs. CN, AD vs. MCI, MCI vs. CN) under 10%, 30%, and 50% modality missing rates using five-fold cross-validation. The full HAD model achieves an AUC of 0.838, ACC of 0.732, and F1 of 0.740 on the most challenging MCI vs. CN task at 50% missing rate (Table 1). The paper reports that HAD consistently achieves the best performance on the most representative metrics (AUC, ACC, F1) across all three tasks and all missing rates, with performance degrading gracefully as more modalities are removed.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HAD is compared against seven state-of-the-art incomplete multimodal learning methods: LMVCAT [35], Adapted [36], DMRNet [37], ShaSpec [38], GMD [39], CM3T [40], and TriMF [41]. HAD outperforms all seven baselines on the majority of metrics (AUC, ACC, F1) across all three diagnostic tasks and all three missing-rate settings (10%, 30%, 50%), as shown in Figure 3. The advantage is most pronounced on the MCI vs. CN task, where early-stage discrimination is hardest and all methods show the lowest discriminative capability. All methods, including HAD, exhibit a consistent performance drop as the modality missing rate increases from 10% to 50%, confirming the negative impact of modality incompleteness. The ablation study (Table 1) further confirms that removing any single component (late-fusion, dynamic weighting, HMamba, or HSFE) degrades performance below the full HAD, with the largest drop observed when replacing HMamba with SegMamba or VMamba scanning strategies (AUC drops from 0.838 to 0.805 and 0.800 respectively).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the selective state space model (Mamba) paradigm from 1D sequences through 2D visual data (V-Mamba) to 3D medical volumes, introducing a multi-view Hilbert curve serialization strategy that preserves spatial locality better than the simple scanning approaches used in prior 3D Mamba methods like SegMamba. The hierarchical spatial feature extraction module (HSFE) builds on the fractal self-similarity of Hilbert curves (following Grela et al.) to enable multi-scale processing, while the multimodal fusion objective departs from prior incomplete-modality methods that rely on GAN-based imputation or subspace learning by instead maximizing mutual information between modality-specific features and a shared semantic space with a hybrid late-fusion mechanism. The dynamic MoE weighting with reversed temperature during training is a targeted response to the modality collapse problem observed in heterogeneous AD data, where easy-to-fit modalities (CSF, clinical scores) dominate gradient flow over harder 3D imaging branches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a clinically critical gap: AD diagnosis in settings where expensive or invasive modalities (PET, CSF) are unavailable, which is the norm rather than the exception in real-world practice. The linear-complexity SSM backbone is practically important for 3D medical imaging where Transformer-based approaches become prohibitively expensive at typical voxel resolutions. However, the evaluation is limited to a single dataset (ADNI) and a single disease, and the paper acknowledges open questions around interpretability of the multimodal fusion and further computational efficiency. Whether the Hilbert-curve serialization advantage generalizes beyond cubic 3D volumes (e.g., anisotropic MRI slices) or to other neurodegenerative conditions remains untested.
