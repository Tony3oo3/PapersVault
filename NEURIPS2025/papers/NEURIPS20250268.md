## Problem

Video diffusion transformers (VDiTs) incur quadratic attention cost over sequences of up to 100K tokens, making a single 5-second 720p video take ~1000 seconds on an H100. Existing sparse-attention accelerators handle local attention well but fail on long-range heads: imposing local windows on inherently non-local heads degrades quality, while falling back to dense computation for those heads caps the overall speedup. The paper identifies that long-range attention heads exhibit high intra-sequence token redundancy, which prior methods do not exploit efficiently.

## Input / Output

VORTA takes a text prompt and produces a 5-second video at 720p resolution (117 frames at 24 FPS for HunyuanVideo; 77 frames at 15 FPS for Wan 2.1). The method is plug-and-play on pretrained VDiTs: it requires only ~100 steps of lightweight router training on the Mixkit dataset (one day on two H100s) and adds 0.1% parameters. It is compatible with arbitrary SDE/ODE solvers and step counts (demonstrated with 50-step UniPC and 30-step DPM++), and can be stacked with orthogonal accelerators such as feature caching (PAB) and step distillation (PCD) for up to 14.41× combined speedup.

## Method

VORTA introduces two sparse attention variants and a signal-aware router. For local attention, it uses 3D sliding tile attention (a block-wise dense mask that avoids the cubic computation-bubble problem of sliding windows in video). For long-range attention, it proposes core-set attention with Bucketed Core-set Selection (BCS): tokens are partitioned into 3D buckets, and within each bucket only the center token's similarities to its neighbors are computed (O(L) total), pruning the top-k most similar tokens and merging their information into the center. A lightweight linear router (0.1% of model parameters) takes the diffusion timestep embedding and outputs three gate values via softmax, dynamically selecting full, sliding-tile, or core-set attention per transformer block. The router is trained self-supervised with a distillation loss (MSE between the routed output and the frozen VDiT's original output) plus the conditional flow matching loss and L2 regularization on gate values; only the router weights are updated. At inference, the branch with the highest gate value is activated (hard selection), with full attention retained for the ~0.2% of heads classified as 'pivotal' where small perturbations cause visible quality loss.

## Results

On HunyuanVideo (720p, 5s, 50 steps), VORTA achieves a 1.76× end-to-end speedup (594.23s vs. 1043.85s baseline) while improving the VBench score from 82.26 to 82.59 and reducing LPIPS to 0.185. Combined with PCD step distillation, VORTA reaches a 14.41× speedup (72.46s) with only a 0.78-point VBench drop (81.49 vs. 82.26). On Wan 2.1 (14B), VORTA delivers a 1.52× speedup (856.50s vs. 1304.82s) with a VBench improvement from 82.36 to 82.85.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among sparse attention methods, VORTA outperforms both ARnR (1.32×) and STA (1.54×) in speedup on HunyuanVideo, achieving 1.76× while also scoring higher on VBench (82.59 vs. 82.39 and 82.33) and LPIPS (0.185 vs. 0.211 and 0.201). Against the caching-based PAB, VORTA is faster (1.76× vs. 1.28×) and uses substantially less peak memory (51.15 GB vs. >80 GB), making PAB impractical without CPU offloading. Compared to step distillation (PCD, 8.29×), VORTA alone is slower but preserves quality better (VBench 82.59 vs. 81.17); the two are orthogonal and combine to 14.41× with only a 0.78-point VBench drop. In the appendix comparison with SVG, VORTA achieves lower latency and higher attention sparsity while VBench scores are statistically indistinguishable, and SVG incurs significantly higher memory due to online profiling. On Wan 2.1 (14B), VORTA improves VBench from 82.36 to 82.85 while delivering a 1.52× speedup, confirming generalizability across MMDiT and DiT backbones.

## Relation to Prior Work

VORTA builds on the sliding tile attention mechanism of Zhang et al. [55] for local interactions and extends it to 3D video data, while departing from prior sparse-attention approaches (STA, ARnR, SVG) that rely on predefined patterns or costly O(L²) online profiling. Its core-set attention draws on token-merging ideas from Bolya & Hoffman [4] and ARnR [40] but replaces global pairwise similarity with a bucketed selection strategy (BCS) that achieves O(L) complexity. The routing mechanism is inspired by conditional computation and mixture-of-experts paradigms [37, 49], adapted here to use diffusion timestep embeddings as the routing signal rather than input-dependent gating.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VORTA provides a practical, plug-and-play acceleration layer for video diffusion transformers that generalizes across schedulers, step counts, and model backbones without re-profiling—a significant practical advantage over heuristic sparse-attention methods. Its compatibility with orthogonal techniques (feature caching, step distillation) positions it as a composable building block, enabling up to 14.41× combined speedup. Key limitations include its focus on attention (dominant only at high resolution/long sequences), restriction to bidirectional generation, and dependence on the quality of the underlying pretrained model. Open questions include adaptation to autoregressive video generation and whether the routing paradigm extends to other modalities or shorter-sequence settings where attention is less dominant.
