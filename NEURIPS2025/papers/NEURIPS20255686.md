## Problem

While adversarial fine-tuning of CLIP image encoders (e.g., FARE) has been explored, the robustness of CLIP text encoders to character-level perturbations remains unexplored. Since CLIP text encoders are embedded in downstream pipelines like text-to-image diffusion models and multimodal retrieval systems, small text perturbations can cause significant embedding shifts and degrade generation quality or retrieval accuracy. This paper addresses the gap by studying adversarial fine-tuning specifically for the text encoder.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255686/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes text sentences as input, where adversarial perturbations are constrained to character-level edits bounded by Levenshtein distance k (typically k=1 or k=2) and semantic constraints that prevent the creation of new English words. The output is a drop-in replacement text encoder that produces embeddings close to the original CLIP clean embedding under perturbation, enabling seamless integration into existing pipelines (Stable Diffusion, SDXL, FLUX) without retraining the diffusion backbone. On the image side, the companion FARE encoder handles ℓ∞ perturbations with radius ε=2/255, and both encoders are trained independently.

## Method

LEAF extends the FARE objective to the text domain by minimizing the L2 distance between the original CLIP text embedding f_θ_CLIP(S) and the fine-tuned encoder's output f_θ(S') under the worst-case character perturbation S' within Levenshtein distance k. The key innovation is an efficient training-time attack that evaluates a constant number ρ of perturbations per sentence in two sequential batched steps: first, ρ random positions are tested with a whitespace character to select the highest-loss position; second, ρ random characters are tried at that position to select the final perturbation. This reduces per-batch cost from O(|S|·|Γ|) (as in Charmer) to O(ρ), enabling an order-of-magnitude speedup (3.23s vs. 118s per batch) with minimal loss in adversarial accuracy. Semantic constraints from Chanakya et al. (2024) are enforced during both training and evaluation to prevent new English words from appearing, which is critical for preserving image-domain performance. The resulting text encoder can be combined with a FARE-robust image encoder to yield bimodal robustness without joint training.

## Results

On AG-News zero-shot text classification at k=1 (one character change), LEAF improves adversarial accuracy from 44.7% (standard CLIP-ViT-L/14) to 63.2% when combined with a FARE robust image encoder. On ImageNet, the combined model achieves 46.0% adversarial accuracy (vs. 0.0% for standard CLIP) while retaining 72.6% clean accuracy. In MS-COCO text-to-image retrieval, LEAF models achieve ~10 points higher Recall@5 under k=2 character perturbations compared to non-robust CLIP. In text-to-image generation (SDXL), LEAF text encoders yield significantly higher CLIPScore under adversarial noise at k≥1, and the efficient LEAF attack trains an order of magnitude faster than Charmer (3.23s vs. 118.19s per batch) with comparable adversarial accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against standard CLIP (Radford et al., 2021), LEAF dramatically improves adversarial robustness: on AG-News at k=1, adversarial accuracy rises from 44.7% to 63.2% (ViT-L/14), and on ImageNet from 0.0% to 46.0% when paired with FARE. Against FARE (Schlarmann et al., 2024), which only hardens the image encoder, LEAF adds the missing text-domain robustness: FARE alone (✓/✗) yields 44.5% AG-News adversarial accuracy, while LEAF alone (✗/✓) reaches 60.1%, and the combination (✓/✓) achieves 63.2%—showing that robustness in both domains requires both encoders. Against Charmer (Abad Rocamora et al., 2024) as a training-time attack, LEAF (ρ=50) achieves 59.80% adversarial accuracy in 3.23s per batch versus Charmer-20's 60.17% in 118.19s, an order-of-magnitude speedup with only 0.37 points of accuracy loss. In MS-COCO retrieval, LEAF models maintain Recall@5 of 69.35% at k=2 versus 62.67% for non-robust CLIP-ViT-L/14, and in SDXL text-to-image generation, LEAF encoders produce significantly higher CLIPScore under k=2 perturbations, where the original model generates completely wrong images for some captions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends FARE (Schlarmann et al., ICML 2024), which made CLIP image encoders robust by preserving clean embeddings under perturbations, to the text domain via an analogous loss (TextFARE). The training-time attack is a streamlined variant of Charmer (Abad Rocamora et al., ICML 2024), reducing per-sentence evaluations from O(|S|) to a constant ρ to enable batched parallelism. Semantic constraints from Chanakya et al. (2024) are adopted to prevent the attack from introducing new English words, which the authors show is critical for preserving image-domain performance during joint training. The overall positioning is that image-side CLIP robustness was addressed (TeCoA, FARE) while the text side remained unexplored, and this work closes that gap with a modular, drop-in replacement for the text encoder.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CLIP text encoders are embedded in safety-critical pipelines (NSFW filters, content moderation, text-to-image generation), and a single character perturbation can flip outputs; this paper provides the first systematic recipe for hardening that component without retraining downstream models. The modular swap-in design means practitioners can upgrade existing SDXL or FLUX deployments by replacing one encoder, which has immediate practical value. Open questions include whether joint image-text training yields further gains, whether the approach extends to token-level attacks (which the authors avoid due to semantic drift), and whether it generalizes to non-CLIP text encoders in RAG or other retrieval-augmented settings. The finding that robust text encoders also improve embedding inversion quality is a nice conceptual bridge to interpretability but remains preliminary.
