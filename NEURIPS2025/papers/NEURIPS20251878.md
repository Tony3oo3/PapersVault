## Problem

Existing MoE expert pruning methods impose a uniform sparsity budget across all layers, ignoring the well-documented variation in expert redundancy between shallow and deep layers. Non-uniform pruning would be more effective, but the discrete combinatorial search space grows exponentially (e.g., C(64,8) ≈ 4×10⁸ configurations for a single 64-expert layer), making global optimization infeasible. The paper asks whether expert pruning can be reformulated as a continuous, gradient-based optimization that adaptively determines per-layer pruning rates without exhaustive enumeration.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-trained MoE model (e.g., Mixtral 8×7B with 32 layers × 8 experts, or DeepSeek-MoE-16B with 28 layers × 64 experts) and a small calibration set of 128 text sequences from C4. It produces a structurally pruned MoE model where entire experts are removed at non-uniform rates per layer, along with learned intra-layer importance logits α ∈ ℝ^{NL} and inter-layer scalars β ∈ ℝ^L. At inference, an additional adaptive skipping mechanism dynamically bypasses redundant experts per token based on routing-weight ratios and CKA-based expert-output similarity, yielding a 1.2–1.3× speedup with negligible accuracy loss.

## Method

DiEP relaxes the binary expert-selection mask into a continuous space by introducing two sets of learnable parameters: intra-layer importance scores α_i^(l) (softmax-normalized within each layer) and inter-layer importance scalars β^(l) (one per layer). The pruned model output is computed as y^(l+1) = β^(l) Σ_i α̅_i^(l) · FFN_i(x^(l)), and is trained against the original model via a combined objective of cross-entropy loss plus a Frobenius-norm reconstruction regularizer. Optimization uses an alternating block-coordinate descent scheme with a 3:1 update ratio favoring α over β, which the authors justify theoretically (convergence to first-order stationary points under standard smoothness assumptions) and empirically. After training, a single global ranking s_i^(l) = α_i^(l) · β^(l) across all N×L experts determines which K = NLr experts to permanently remove, yielding a non-uniform, depth-aware sparsity pattern without per-layer threshold tuning. An online expert-skipping rule at inference further skips the lower-weighted of two selected experts when w_{e1} < γ·w_{e0}, where γ is calibrated from routing-weight medians and CKA similarity ratios.

## Results

On Mixtral 8×7B at 50% expert sparsity, DiEP achieves an average score of 59.9 (retaining ~92% of the full model's 65.1), outperforming the next-best method S-MOE (55.9) by 4.0 points and NAEE (54.6) by 5.3 points. On the challenging MMLU benchmark at 50% sparsity, DiEP reaches 57.9 vs. S-MOE's 50.8, a 7.1-point margin. On Mixtral 8×7B-Instruct at 25% sparsity, DiEP achieves 65.8 average with only a 0.6-point drop from the full model (66.4), and on the larger Mixtral 8×22B at 50% sparsity it retains 94% of full-model performance (62.9 vs. 67.0).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiEP consistently outperforms all four baselines across every model and task. Against NAEE (exhaustive per-layer expert combination search), DiEP gains 5.3 points in average score on Mixtral 8×7B at 50% sparsity (59.9 vs. 54.6) and 2.1 points on Mixtral 8×7B-Instruct at 25% (65.8 vs. 63.8). Against S-MOE (similarity-based pruning and merging), DiEP leads by 4.0 points on Mixtral 8×7B at 50% (59.9 vs. 55.9) and by 1.6 points on Mixtral 8×22B at 50% (62.9 vs. 61.3). Expert Trimming (activation-frequency-based removal) is the weakest baseline at high sparsity, dropping to 51.2 average on Mixtral 8×7B at 50% versus DiEP's 59.9. M-SMoE (routing-policy-based expert merging) suffers catastrophic degradation on Mixtral 8×22B, collapsing to 37.3 average at 50% sparsity compared to DiEP's 62.9. The largest single-task margin is 7.1 points on MMLU (Mixtral 8×7B, 50% sparsity: DiEP 57.9 vs. S-MOE 50.8), confirming that DiEP's joint intra-layer/inter-layer differentiable optimization captures redundancy patterns that uniform or layer-wise heuristics miss.

## Relation to Prior Work

DiEP extends the continuous-relaxation paradigm of differentiable architecture search (DARTS and its variants) from dense convolutional/transformer architectures to the sparsely-activated MoE setting, where the search space is combinatorial over expert subsets rather than over architectural building blocks. It departs from prior MoE pruning methods (NAEE, M-SMoE, Expert Trimming, S-MoE) that either enforce uniform per-layer sparsity or perform exhaustive within-layer enumeration, by introducing a two-level decomposition—learnable intra-layer expert scores (α) and inter-layer importance scalars (β)—optimized via alternating gradient descent with a reconstruction regularizer. The online expert-skipping mechanism at inference, which combines routing-weight ratios with CKA-based expert similarity, builds on the observation from NAEE that not all top-k experts contribute equally per token, but adds a similarity-aware threshold that prior skipping methods lacked.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As MoE models scale to hundreds of billions of parameters (DeepSeek V3: 671B, 256 experts/layer), efficient compression is a practical bottleneck for deployment in resource-constrained settings, and DiEP's non-uniform, depth-aware pruning directly addresses the observation that shallow layers encode more diverse low-level features and thus require more experts than deeper layers. The method's 0.01% parameter overhead and sub-hour pruning time (vs. ~94,000 days for exhaustive search on 64-expert layers) make it scalable to architectures where prior methods are computationally infeasible. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to multimodal MoE (e.g., MoE-LLaVA), whether it generalizes to the 256-expert-per-layer regime of DeepSeek V3, and whether the alternating α/β optimization can be further accelerated or made fully parallelizable.
