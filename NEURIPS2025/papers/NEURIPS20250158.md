## Problem

While RLVR has unlocked powerful reasoning in LLMs through the emergence of cognitive behaviors like backtracking and subgoal decomposition, it remains unclear how these linguistic behaviors transfer to MLLMs for visual reasoning. Prior multimodal RL efforts either relied on learned reward models (RLHF) or lacked a systematic, large-scale analysis of how cognitive patterns emerge, transfer, and scale across modalities.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250158/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts an RGB image paired with a textual prompt (or a text-only prompt) and generates a reasoning chain-of-thought of up to 48k tokens, concluding with a final answer enclosed in \boxed{}. The reward is a strict binary match between the extracted boxed answer and the reference, making it verifiable and resistant to reward hacking. During RL, each prompt yields 16 sampled responses at temperature 1.0 for on-policy PPO updates.

## Method

The paper introduces a two-stage pipeline on Qwen2.5-VL-7B: a linguistic cold start that SFTs the LLM module on ~2M language-only reasoning traces distilled from DeepSeek-R1 (5 epochs, batch 640, 64k context, LR 2×10⁻⁴), followed by ~900 iterations of multimodal PPO with GAE (γ=1, λ=1) over ~300k mixed-modality examples using a sequence-length curriculum (24k→32k→48k). The key mechanism is that DeepSeek-R1's linguistic reasoning contains implicit mental imagery (e.g., 'let me visualize…'), which, once the model encounters actual images, rapidly grounds into visual-specific behaviors such as visual reflection and divide-and-conquer. The paper reveals a division of labor: cold start broadly memorizes diverse cognitive patterns, while large-scale RL strategically discerns and amplifies high-utility behaviors (backtracking transfer rate grows from 2.5% to 17.3%, while verification shows near-zero transfer). The final model is a uniform average of several intermediate checkpoints to balance performance across benchmarks.

## Results

OVR-7B achieves 51.8% on MathVision (first 7B post-trained Qwen2.5-VL model to surpass 50%), 54.6% on MathVerse vision-only, 33.5% on DynaMath (worst), 54.8% on LogicVista, and 50.2% on MMMU-Pro, setting new state-of-the-art among 7B open-source models on all these benchmarks. On language reasoning, OVR reaches 63.5% on AIME 2024, 52.1% on AIME 2025, and 95.3% on MATH500, surpassing all other 7B open-source models by over 10% on AIME. On perception benchmarks, the full pipeline (cold start + RL) recovers and slightly exceeds the base Qwen2.5-VL-7B on MMBench (86.6 vs 85.3) and BLINK (54.1 vs 53.7), confirming that multimodal RL mitigates the perceptual degradation introduced by linguistic cold start.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OVR-7B decisively outperforms all 7B open-source RL-based baselines on MathVision (51.8% vs. 48.8% for ReVisual-R1, 28.4% for VL-Rethinker, 28.1% for MM-Eureka), MathVerse (54.6% vs. 53.6% for ReVisual-R1), DynaMath (33.5% vs. 27.5% for ReVisual-R1), LogicVista (54.8% vs. 53.0% for WeThink), and MMMU-Pro (50.2% vs. 43.0% for X-REASONER). On MathVista, OVR (72.1%) is slightly behind VL-Rethinker-7B (73.7%) and MM-Eureka (72.6%), indicating that the advantage of cognitive behavior transfer is most pronounced on harder, more reasoning-intensive benchmarks rather than general visual QA. Against the larger 38B Skywork-R1V, OVR wins on MathVision (51.8% vs. 42.1%), MathVerse (54.6% vs. 40.4%), and LogicVista (54.8% vs. 50.6%) despite being roughly 5× smaller. On language benchmarks, OVR (63.5% AIME 2024, 95.3% MATH500) surpasses ReVisual-R1 (53.3%, 89.2%) and DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B (55.5%, 92.8%), approaching the 32B QwQ-32B-Preview (50.0%, 90.6%) and even exceeding it on AIME 2024. Among close-source models, OVR remains competitive with Gemini-2.0-Flash on MathVision (51.8% vs. 47.8%) and MathVerse (54.6% vs. 43.6%), though it trails doubao-1.5-vision-pro-32k on most visual benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 'RL with a cold start' paradigm from DeepSeek-R1 [34] and the minimalist PPO+GAE framework from Open-Reasoner-Zero [40] into the multimodal domain, applying them to Qwen2.5-VL-7B. It departs from prior multimodal RL efforts (Perception-R1, R1-OneVision, VLAA-Thinking) that either relied on learned reward models or complex distillation pipelines, instead using a language-only cold start followed by large-scale rule-based multimodal RL. The key conceptual departure is the systematic investigation of how linguistic cognitive behaviors (backtracking, verification, subgoal setting, backward chaining) transfer to their visual counterparts, a question left unanswered by prior work that treated cold start as a mere initialization step rather than a mechanism for cross-modal behavior transfer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first large-scale empirical evidence that linguistic cognitive behaviors transfer to visual reasoning, with the surprising finding that this transfer emerges during the cold start phase itself—driven by mental imagery in purely linguistic reasoning—rather than requiring explicit visual supervision. The practical two-stage recipe (2M linguistic SFT examples + ~1000 steps of multimodal RL) is likely to become a standard template for training open-source multimodal reasoners. Key open questions remain: whether the perceptual degradation from cold start can be fully eliminated, why RL is unscalable for pure perception tasks (reward grows but response length stagnates), and whether the observed behavioral correlations are causal. The authors point toward multi-turn and agentic RL with visual manipulation as the next frontier.
