## Problem

Deep learning optical flow models have not been applied to in vivo microvascular blood flow estimation, where fast-moving blood cells (~30 px/frame at 200 FPS) in long OBM video sequences (12,000+ frames) demand temporally stable, region-specific flow predictions. Existing OF architectures are limited to two-frame or short-window processing, compute flow over all pixels including irrelevant background, and lack a mechanism for users to focus estimation on diagnostically relevant vessel segments.

## Input / Output

SAM2Flow consumes consecutive 512×512 grayscale OBM frames in an online streaming fashion, optionally conditioned by sparse point prompts (x, y; label ∈ {1,0}) that the user provides in the first few frames to designate target capillary ROIs. It outputs per-ROI 2D displacement vector fields (H×W×2) at each frame, which are then averaged over fixed subregions to yield velocity-over-time curves for physiological analysis. The model processes one frame at a time with a FIFO memory bank of 7 prior frames, rather than ingesting a fixed temporal window, enabling unbounded sequence length at constant per-frame cost (~49 ms on 512×512).

## Method

SAM2Flow fuses a SEA-RAFT ResNet feature encoder (pre-trained on six OF datasets) with a SAM2 ViT context encoder (adapted via SAM2-adapter to the OBM domain) and a ConvNeXt-RNN iterative flow-update module. Two key mechanisms differentiate it from prior OF models: (1) prompt-guided ROI estimation, where user point prompts condition the context encoder via cross-attention and an ROI decoder produces binary masks that restrict correlation lookup to foreground pixels (reducing lookup complexity from O(hwK) to O(N_R·K)); and (2) a dual memory attention module that maintains separate motion memory (encoding prior flow maps fused with motion features) and context memory (encoding binarized flow masks fused with unprompted context) in a 7-frame FIFO bank, queried via alternating self/cross-attention to stabilize both flow regression and ROI identity tracking over long sequences. Training proceeds in three stages—backbone domain adaptation, prompt-conditioned module fusion on image pairs, and full dual-memory training on 8-frame sequences—using MoL loss with a multi-stage learning-rate schedule.

## Results

On the Microvascular test set, SAM2Flow achieves the best whole-image EPE of 1.14 (SD 0.92) and foreground EPE (FEPE) of 5.84 (SD 4.86), outperforming the next-best single-frame model SEA-RAFT (EPE 1.28, FEPE 6.60) and all multi-frame baselines. On the Spring public benchmark, SAM2Flow attains the lowest FEPE of 1.23 (SD 4.21), a 15% improvement over SEA-RAFT (1.45), along with the best foreground 1px accuracy (87.13%) and weighted AUC (84.57). Inference speed is 48.78 ms/frame on 512×512 inputs, competitive with other multi-frame models but slower than the lightweight SEA-RAFT (21.14 ms).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAM2Flow outperforms all seven baselines on the primary Microvascular FEPE metric (5.84 vs. 6.60 for SEA-RAFT, 10.13 for StreamFlow, 12.47 for MemFlow, and >27 for RAFT/GMA/VideoFlow), and also achieves the best whole-image EPE (1.14 vs. 1.28 for SEA-RAFT). On Sintel, SAM2Flow leads in foreground FEPE on both Clean (3.17 vs. 3.26 SEA-RAFT) and Final (3.39 vs. 4.08 SEA-RAFT, 4.27 MemFlow), showing notably smaller degradation (ΔFEPE = 0.22) under added texture and illumination compared to SEA-RAFT (Δ = 0.82) and MemFlow (Δ = 0.50). On Spring, SAM2Flow achieves the best FEPE (1.23 vs. 1.45 SEA-RAFT, 1.54 StreamFlow, 1.56 MemFlow), best 1px accuracy (87.13%), and best WAUC (84.57), though it trails SEA-RAFT by a narrow margin on flow outlier rate (5.29% vs. 5.18%). The main trade-off is speed: SAM2Flow runs at 48.78 ms/frame, roughly 2.3× slower than SEA-RAFT (21.14 ms) due to the dual-memory attention, but remains competitive with other multi-frame models (MemFlow 43.98 ms, StreamFlow 60.07 ms) and far faster than FlowFormer++ (133.95 ms) and VideoFlow (112.67 ms).

## Relation to Prior Work

SAM2Flow sits at the intersection of two research threads: prompt-based video segmentation (SAM2) and iterative optical flow estimation (RAFT/SEA-RAFT). It repurposes SAM2's ViT encoder, memory encoders, and prompt-conditioned cross-attention as a context backbone for flow, while retaining SEA-RAFT's ConvNeXt-RNN update module and correlation-volume machinery. The key conceptual departure from MemFlow (the closest prior work on memory-based online flow) is the split into a dual memory—motion memory for temporal smoothness of the flow field and context memory for persistent ROI identity—rather than a single unified memory bank. The ROI-guided correlation lookup, which restricts the expensive 4D correlation search to foreground pixels only, is a novel efficiency mechanism not present in prior RAFT-family architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first deep-learning optical flow model purpose-built for in vivo microvascular blood flow, and it demonstrates that prompt-guided, ROI-centric estimation can produce clinically interpretable velocity waveforms (systolic peak, diastolic trough) over 25-second sequences—something prior semiquantitative methods (MFI, ST diagrams) could not automate end-to-end. The 306,800-frame microvascular dataset is substantially larger than standard public flow benchmarks and will support future work. Open questions include generalization to pathological conditions (sickle cell, sepsis), whether the ST-diagram-derived ground truth (spatially/temporally smoothed) limits measurable accuracy gains, and whether the reliance on robust ROI detection becomes a bottleneck in complex or occluded vascular scenes.
