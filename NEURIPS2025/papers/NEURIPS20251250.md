## Problem

Standard constrained RL formulates objectives and constraints as expected discounted returns, which ignores tail risk and catastrophic events critical in high-stakes applications. Existing risk-aware RL methods either handle only unconstrained settings with reward-based risk measures or use return-based (aggregate) risk measures that do not capture per-stage risk. This paper is the first to handle reward-based risk constraints (via OCEs) in a constrained setting while establishing an exact partial Lagrangian relaxation with convergence guarantees.

## Input / Output

The method takes bounded reward functions r_i for the objective and m constraints, a discount factor γ ∈ (0,1), a CVaR level β ∈ (0,1), constraint thresholds c_i, and operates in continuous state-action spaces (S ⊂ R^n, A ⊂ R^d). It produces a risk-aware policy π_θ (parameterized, e.g., a neural network) that maximizes a per-stage CVaR objective while satisfying per-stage CVaR constraints, along with converged dual multipliers λ and auxiliary certainty-equivalent variables t. The risk measure is applied to the occupancy measure rather than the discounted return, meaning robustness is enforced jointly over reward values and time at each stage.

## Method

The key insight is to apply OCEs (specifically CVaR^β) to the occupancy measure, yielding a reward-based formulation where the risk-averse objective becomes a maximization over an auxiliary variable t with a modified reward r'(s,a,t) = t − (1/β)(t−r(s,a))_+. Under a constraint qualification (Slater's condition holding on a neighborhood of the optimal t), the paper proves a parameterized strong duality that makes the partial Lagrangian relaxation exact. The resulting problem is a stochastic minimax optimization over (t, λ): the outer loop uses SGDA to update the dual multiplier λ (minimization) and the auxiliary variable t (maximization), while the inner policy optimization is a standard unconstrained RL problem with a linear combination of modified rewards, solvable by any black-box algorithm such as PPO. The paper establishes O(ε^{-6}) iteration complexity for reaching an ε-stationary point of the Moreau envelope, accommodating inexact policy oracles with bounded gradient bias δ.

## Results

On Safety-Gymnasium (Point agent, level 1), the proposed method achieves strictly zero constraint violations (cost = 0.0) across all four navigation environments (Button, Circle, Goal, Push), while unconstrained PPO incurs costs of 38–207. The trade-off is a reward reduction in three of four environments (e.g., Circle: 39.19 vs. PPO's 60.18; Button: 2.58 vs. 24.29), though in the Push environment the constrained method actually achieves higher reward (2.42 vs. 0.93). On safe-velocity tasks (Hopper, Swimmer, Walker2d), the converged auxiliary variable t matches the empirical β-upper quantile of the post-training velocity distribution, confirming correct CVaR behavior, and the dual variable λ stabilizes to enforce the constraint.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against a single baseline: unconstrained, risk-neutral PPO. The proposed method decisively wins on constraint satisfaction, achieving zero violations in all four navigation environments where PPO accumulates costs of 38.48 (Push), 45.09 (Goal), 150.76 (Button), and 206.74 (Circle). On reward, PPO outperforms the proposed method in three of four environments (e.g., Circle: 60.18 vs. 39.19; Button: 24.29 vs. 2.58), reflecting the expected trade-off of enforcing safety constraints. Notably, in the Push environment the constrained method achieves higher reward (2.42 vs. 0.93), suggesting the constraint guides the agent toward more effective task behavior. The authors note that their method is the only one achieving strictly zero violations compared to other PPO-based constraint learning algorithms reported in the Safety-Gymnasium benchmark [19].

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the reward-based risk measure framework of Bonetti et al. [7] (which applied OCEs to the occupancy measure in unconstrained RL) to the constrained setting, and departs from return-based risk-constrained RL methods like Chow et al. [12] by placing the risk measure on per-stage rewards rather than the aggregate discounted return. The strong duality results build on Paternain et al. [33]'s zero-duality-gap theorem for risk-neutral constrained RL, while the OCE parameterization follows Ben-Tal and Teboulle [4]. The key conceptual departure is that the partial Lagrangian relaxation is shown to be exact under a constraint qualification (Slater's condition on a neighborhood of the optimal t), whereas prior risk-constrained RL work (e.g., Chow et al.) could not establish any meaningful primal-dual relation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides the first principled treatment of reward-based risk constraints (not just objectives) in RL, with a modular algorithm that wraps around any black-box RL solver and comes with non-asymptotic convergence guarantees via reduction to stochastic minimax optimization. The empirical demonstration of strictly zero constraint violations (vs. 45–207 for PPO) and the convergence of the auxiliary variable t to the empirical CVaR quantile validate the risk-management properties in practice. An open question the authors flag is whether full strong duality holds unconditionally (without Assumption 3.4), and the computational overhead of requiring an inner policy solve at each outer iteration remains a practical limitation for large-scale deployment.
