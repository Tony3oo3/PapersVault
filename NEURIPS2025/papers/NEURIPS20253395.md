## Problem

Causal discovery from multimodal unstructured data (e.g., text + images) remains largely unaddressed: traditional CD methods require predefined variables in structured data, and naive adaptation of unimodal LLM-based CD (e.g., COAT) to multimodal settings fails to identify implicit causal factors hidden in cross-modal interactions and cannot resolve structural ambiguities inherent to observational data. The paper tackles both gaps simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253395/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes raw multimodal unstructured samples—each comprising a text description and an image (e.g., apple review + apple photo, or clinical note + CT scan)—along with a scalar target variable value per sample. No variable names or structured schema are provided; the method must discover what the causal variables even are. Output is a structured table of identified factors with per-sample annotated values plus a partial ancestral graph (from FCI) encoding directed, undirected, and bidirected edges representing causal, ambiguous, and latent-confounded relationships.

## Method

MLLM-CD is a three-module iterative framework. First, a Contrastive Factor Discovery (CFD) module uses CLIP embeddings to select semantically maximally-dissimilar sample pairs within each modality (intra-modal) and across modalities (inter-modal, scored by embedding distance plus target-value difference), then prompts an MLLM to extract and consolidate causal factors from these contrastive pairs—revealing implicit variables that simple prompting misses. Second, a standard FCI algorithm infers the causal graph from the resulting structured dataset. Third, an Iterative Multimodal Counterfactual Reasoning (MCR) module targets uncertain edges by prompting the MLLM to generate 'what-if' counterfactual samples (revising text and generating images via a diffusion model), validates them for semantic plausibility (embedding similarity threshold) and causal consistency (non-descendant invariance check), and appends validated counterfactuals to the dataset for the next FCI round. The loop iterates, progressively reducing Markov-equivalence-class ambiguities by injecting MLLM world knowledge as complementary evidence beyond the observational data.

## Results

On the MAG dataset (200 samples, 9 factors), MLLM-CD achieves an average Node F1 of 0.89 and Adjacency F1 of 0.57 across four MLLMs (GPT-4o, Gemini 2.0, LLaMA 4, Grok-2v), with the lowest Extended Structural Hamming Distance (ESHD) of 13.42. On the real-world Lung Cancer dataset (60 samples, 5 factors), MLLM-CD reaches Node F1 of 0.90, Adjacency F1 of 0.68, and ESHD of 5.33, substantially outperforming all baselines. The contrastive factor discovery module drives factor identification gains (e.g., Node Recall 0.86 vs. 0.39 for COAT on MAG), while the counterfactual reasoning module primarily improves structure discovery (Adjacency F1 0.57 vs. 0.52 without CR in ablation).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MLLM-CD consistently outperforms COAT (the prior state-of-the-art LLM-driven CD method) on both datasets: on MAG, Node Recall jumps from 0.39 to 0.86 and Adjacency F1 from 0.29 to 0.57; on Lung Cancer, Node F1 improves from 0.52 to 0.90 and ESHD drops from 9.42 to 5.33. Against META (zero-shot LLM factor/structure proposal), MLLM-CD achieves substantially better factor identification (NF 0.89 vs. 0.52 on MAG) and lower ESHD (13.42 vs. 21.25), though META occasionally matches or slightly exceeds MLLM-CD on Adjacency Precision (0.74 vs. 0.72 on MAG) due to its reliance on general world knowledge for certain edges. Pairwise and Triplet prompting methods, which only infer pairwise/triplet causal relationships without factor discovery, are dominated by MLLM-CD on Adjacency F1 (0.40/0.39 vs. 0.57 on MAG) and ESHD (34.08/39.58 vs. 13.42), confirming that the integrated pipeline of contrastive factor discovery plus statistical structure learning plus counterfactual refinement is necessary for competitive multimodal CD performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LLM-driven causal discovery line initiated by COAT (Liu et al., 2024), which first demonstrated that LLMs can perform factor identification from unstructured text and then hand off to statistical CD algorithms (FCI/PC). MLLM-CD generalizes this pipeline to the multimodal setting by introducing a contrastive factor discovery module that probes intra- and inter-modal interactions via semantically contrastive sample pairs, and an iterative multimodal counterfactual reasoning module that leverages MLLM world knowledge to resolve structural ambiguities that purely observational statistical methods cannot. It also builds on the broader LLM-for-CD literature (Kiciman et al. 2023, Vashishtha et al. 2025) that uses LLMs as imperfect experts for pairwise or triplet causal relationship identification, but departs from those works by integrating LLM reasoning into the full discovery loop rather than using it as a post-hoc refinement step.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework and benchmark for causal discovery from multimodal unstructured data, a setting that is increasingly prevalent in domains like medical diagnosis where clinical notes, images, and lab results jointly inform causal understanding. The key open questions include scalability beyond the small evaluation datasets (200 and 60 samples), the reliability of MLLM-generated counterfactuals in domain-specific or cross-domain scenarios where hallucination risk is higher, and whether the contrastive sampling strategy generalizes to more than two modalities. The finding that the counterfactual reasoning module yields disproportionately large gains on the smaller Lung Cancer dataset (AF: 0.38→0.87) versus the larger MAG dataset (AF: 0.52→0.60) suggests a practical pathway for data-scarce settings, but the overall evidence base remains limited by dataset size and the dependence on a single statistical CD algorithm (FCI).
