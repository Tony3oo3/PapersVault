## Problem

Building state-of-the-art vision-language captioners typically requires training on billions of high-quality image-text pairs and millions of GPU hours, making the field inaccessible to most research groups. Although text-to-image diffusion models implicitly encode rich cross-modal semantic relationships necessary for coherent image generation, their potential as a knowledge source for understanding tasks like captioning has remained largely untapped. The paper asks whether a frozen diffusion decoder can serve as a self-supervised teacher that distills its generative knowledge into a lightweight vision encoder without any paired text data.

## Input / Output

The model takes a single RGB image (shorter side > 512 px, aspect ratio 0.5–2) and produces two outputs: (1) a continuous caption embedding of 77 tokens in the CLIP text-encoder space (the same conditioning space consumed by the frozen Stable Diffusion 2.1 U-Net), and (2) a variable-length natural-language caption (mean ≈ 227 tokens) generated autoregressively by a Qwen-2.5 3B LLM. The embedding is the primary learned representation; the caption is a downstream decoding of it, so the same embedding can also be fed directly to a diffusion model for image reconstruction or concatenated across images for compositional generation.

## Method

VLV is a two-stage auto-encoder that uses a frozen text-to-image diffusion model as its decoder. In Stage 1, a Florence-2 vision backbone plus a lightweight multimodal Transformer encoder maps the image to 77 learnable query tokens, which an MLP projects into the CLIP text-embedding space; the frozen Stable Diffusion 2.1 U-Net then reconstructs the image from this embedding via the standard denoising loss, forcing the encoder to distill all reconstruction-relevant semantics into the compact continuous bottleneck without any text supervision. In Stage 2, the same embedding is passed through the frozen CLIP text encoder, a trainable MLP projector, and a Qwen-2.5 3B LLM that autoregressively decodes it into a fluent caption, trained on 6 M image–caption pairs (captions synthesized by Gemini 2.0 Flash). The key design choices—continuous (rather than discrete/Gumbel-Softmax) latent tokens, a frozen diffusion decoder as the reconstruction signal, and progressive unfreezing of encoder modules during Stage 2—yield captioning quality within 0.5 FID of GPT-4o at a total training cost under $1,000 USD.

## Results

On the primary caption-quality benchmark (FID↓ via Stable Diffusion 3.5 Medium reconstruction on 30K MS-COCO 2014 val images), VLV achieves FID 6.64 at guidance scale 2.0, within 0.44 of GPT-4o (6.20) and the best among all open-source models. In the human/VLM captioner arena (0–6 scale), VLV scores 5.18 on average, matching GPT-4o (5.23) within 0.05 points and surpassing Qwen-2.5-VL-7B (5.03) by 0.15. On text-only VQA (DeepSeek-V3, 32-shot), VLV reaches 63.60% on VQAv2 and 60.25% on OK-VQA, within 1 percentage point of the overall leader (Gemini 2.0 Flash) at both benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GPT-4o, VLV is essentially matched: FID gaps are ≤0.5 across all guidance scales (e.g., 6.64 vs 6.20 at GS 2.0), and in the human/VLM arena VLV (5.18) trails GPT-4o (5.23) by only 0.05 on a 0–6 scale. Against Gemini 2.0 Flash, VLV is slightly behind on FID (6.64 vs 5.87 at GS 2.0) and human rating (5.18 vs 5.25), but Gemini is a closed-source model trained on far more data. VLV clearly outperforms Qwen2.5-VL-7B on FID at every guidance scale (e.g., 6.64 vs 6.98 at GS 2.0) and by 0.15 in human rating. Against Florence-2 Large, VLV wins at GS 2.0–4.0 (6.64 vs 7.51, 8.56 vs 9.95, 9.90 vs 11.35) but Florence-2 retains a narrow edge at GS 1.0 (10.61 vs 11.47). VLV substantially outperforms both LLaVA-v1.5-7B (6.64 vs 9.99 at GS 2.0) and Recap-7B (6.64 vs 7.70 at GS 2.0) across all guidance scales, confirming that the diffusion-distilled continuous embeddings carry richer visual semantics than prior open-source captioners of similar parameter count.

## Relation to Prior Work

VLV extends the 'analysis-by-synthesis' paradigm introduced by De-Diffusion [69] and ViLex [66], which used frozen text-to-image diffusion models to supervise visual embedding learning. The key departure is replacing De-Diffusion's discrete CLIP text tokens (optimized via Gumbel-Softmax) with a continuous embedding space, enabling more stable training and finer-grained semantic retention. The two-stage pipeline—first distilling semantics through a frozen diffusion decoder, then aligning an LLM to decode those embeddings into captions—repurposes the diffusion model as a self-supervised teacher rather than a generative tool, a framing that prior work had not fully exploited for captioning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The most practically significant finding is that GPT-4o-comparable captioning quality can be achieved at under $1,000 training cost using primarily single-modal images, dramatically lowering the barrier for vision-language research. Emergent properties—scalable 3D spatial awareness and zero-shot multi-image compositionality via embedding concatenation—suggest the learned representations encode richer structure than text alone would imply. Open questions include whether re-distilling from newer diffusion backbones (SD 3.5, FLUX) lifts the current ceiling, whether the approach extends to video, and how to address the OCR weakness introduced by aesthetic-score filtering of training data.
