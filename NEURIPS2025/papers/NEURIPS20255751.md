## Problem

Existing conformal aggregation methods for ensembles merge the separately conformalized prediction regions of individual predictors, forgoing the shared structure present in their conformal scores and producing overly conservative prediction regions. The paper asks whether aggregation can instead be performed directly in score space to automatically exploit inter-predictor structure while retaining distribution-free coverage guarantees.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255751/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes K non-negative score functions s_k(x,y) (one per ensemble predictor), a calibration set D_C, and a target miscoverage level α. It produces a prediction region C(x) defined implicitly as the set of y whose K-dimensional score vector s(x,y) falls inside a convex acceptance region Q̂ in R^K_+, parameterized by M projection directions and their quantile thresholds rather than a single scalar. In the predict-then-optimize variant, the output is an optimal decision w* obtained by solving a robust optimization whose inner maximization over C(x) is reduced to a finite collection of convex subproblems indexed by sample tuples from the generative predictors.

## Method

CSA extends split conformal prediction by replacing the scalar score with a K-dimensional vector score and the scalar quantile with a quantile envelope. The calibration set is split into S_C^(1) and S_C^(2): the former defines a data-driven pre-ordering via the 1-α quantile envelope (intersection of half-planes from M uniformly sampled positive-orthant directions, with a binary-searched per-direction quantile level β* ensuring joint coverage), and the latter yields the final threshold t̂ as the (1-α) quantile of the max-projection-ratio scores. This two-stage design preserves exchangeability and yields a finite-sample coverage guarantee via a direct corollary of the standard conformal argument applied to the induced scalar transformation T(s) = max_m(u_m^T s / q̃_m). For predict-then-optimize, the GPCP score structure ensures each per-index-tuple subproblem is convex, so the robust inner max is solved by enumerating the (typically small) product of per-predictor sample counts and taking the worst-case objective value.

## Results

On ImageNet classification (α=0.10), CSA achieves a prediction set size of 34.0, substantially smaller than VFCP (46.1), C^M (87.3), C^R (82.7), and the direct ensemble conformalization (69.1), while maintaining the target 90% coverage. On OpenML-CTR23 regression (α=0.05, task 361234), CSA yields an interval length of 7.69 versus 8.70 for C^R and 17.76 for the ensemble score. In the Manhattan traffic predict-then-optimize task, CSA significantly reduces the expected suboptimality gap proportion relative to both STEPS (p=3.61×10⁻⁴) and LDM (p=9.50×10⁻⁴) while achieving 96.8% coverage at α=0.05.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against VFCP [40] (single weighted score projection), CSA achieves 26% smaller prediction sets at α=0.10 (34.0 vs. 46.1) and 24% smaller at α=0.05 (59.6 vs. 78.1) on ImageNet, though VFCP marginally wins at α=0.01 (194.7 vs. 201.3); additionally, VFCP is restricted to classification while CSA extends to regression and predict-then-optimize. Against the majority-vote and randomized-thresholding aggregation methods C^M, C^R, C^U [29], CSA consistently produces 2.5×–6× smaller prediction sets across all α levels on ImageNet and 1.1×–2.3× smaller intervals on OpenML-CTR23 regression, while all methods maintain valid coverage. In the predict-then-optimize traffic routing task, CSA significantly outperforms both individually conformalized STEPS [45] and LDM [46] predictors on the suboptimality gap metric (paired t-test p-values of 3.61×10⁻⁴ and 9.50×10⁻⁴ respectively), demonstrating that score-space aggregation yields less conservative robust decisions than conformalizing each view independently. The 'Single-Stage' ablation (omitting the two-stage calibration split) matches or slightly beats CSA in interval length but fails to maintain coverage, confirming the necessity of the proposed two-stage procedure.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the split conformal prediction framework (Shafer & Vovk, 2008) from scalar to multivariate score functions, leveraging the quantile envelope construction from multivariate quantile theory (Hallin, Paindaveine & Šiman, 2010) to define data-driven acceptance regions in score space. It departs from existing conformal aggregation methods (Gasparin & Ramdas, 2024; Yang & Kuchibhotla, 2024; Luo & Zhou, 2024) that operate by merging or thresholding separately conformalized prediction regions, instead performing aggregation directly in the multivariate score space via a pre-ordering induced by the quantile envelope. The predict-then-optimize application extends Patel et al. (2023), which used scalar conformal prediction, to the multivariate CSA setting, showing the resulting regions remain amenable to convex optimization for robust decision-making.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CSA provides a principled, fully data-driven mechanism for ensemble uncertainty quantification that avoids the need for a priori knowledge of relative predictor uncertainties, while retaining finite-sample distribution-free coverage guarantees. The two-stage calibration (splitting the calibration set to define the pre-ordering and then calibrate the threshold) is shown to be necessary for validity, at the cost of statistical efficiency in data-sparse regimes. Open questions include whether a full-conformal analogue can avoid the data split, whether end-to-end training can discover more optimal score comparisons than the quantile envelope ordering, and how the method extends to robust control settings where sensor fusion is prevalent.
