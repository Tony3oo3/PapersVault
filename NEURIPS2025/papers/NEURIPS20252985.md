## Problem

Existing 3D Gaussian Splatting GANs (GSGAN, GGHead) stabilize training and improve steep-view rendering by conditioning the generator's latent code on the camera position, but this causes significant identity and geometry changes when re-synthesizing the head from different viewpoints, breaking 3D consistency. Removing view-conditioning typically destabilizes GAN training to the point of collapse, leaving a gap between high per-view quality and a single coherent 3D scene that can be rendered from arbitrary angles.

## Input / Output

The generator takes a single random latent vector (no camera conditioning) and produces a fixed explicit 3D Gaussian Splatting scene of up to ~1M primitives, which can be rendered at any viewpoint up to 2048² resolution and directly imported into game engines or VR environments. Training consumes 2D face images with known camera poses from FFHQ or the newly curated FFHQC dataset; the camera pose is fed only to the discriminator, never to the generator. Unlike NeRF-based 3D GANs that output a 2D rendering of an implicit volume, the output here is an explicit point-based scene with per-Gaussian position, color, scale, rotation, and opacity attributes.

## Method

CGS-GAN builds a hierarchical transformer generator on a fixed 512-point backbone with positional encoding, where each output layer applies an Adaptive Transformer Block (AdaIN + self-attention + MLP) followed by per-layer point upsampling (×4 per layer), enabling linear memory scaling to 2048² without the quadratic blowup of GSGAN's in-backbone upsampling and graph convolutions. Training stability without view-conditioning is achieved through a multi-view regularization that renders 4 views per training step and averages gradients before backpropagation, combined with replacing GSGAN's contrastive pose loss with a StyleGAN-style conditional embedding that injects camera parameters into discriminator features rather than applying an explicit contrastive term. A random per-sample background color prevents the generator from exploiting a fixed white background to create surface holes. The authors also curate FFHQC by removing ~15k occluded images, recropping full heads from 4K sources, and rebalancing smiling bias and extreme camera poses to reduce view-dependent artifacts.

## Results

On the standard FID metric (view-conditioned rendering), CGS-GAN achieves 4.94 on FFHQ-512 and 4.53 on FFHQC-512, competitive with GGHead (4.34 on FFHQ) and superior on the curated FFHQC dataset. The headline result is on the proposed FID₃D metric (fixed frontal conditioning, rendered from random views), where CGS-GAN achieves 4.94 (FFHQ-512) and 4.53 (FFHQC-512), dramatically outperforming GSGAN (10.50 / 7.68) and GGHead (7.90 / 7.78). CGS-GAN is the only method evaluated at 1024² (FID₃D = 5.25) and 2048² (FID₃D = 7.8) resolutions, demonstrating strong scaling. Synthesis speed reaches 152.5 FPS at 512² and 61.3 FPS at 2048² on a single RTX 4090.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GSGAN [15], CGS-GAN wins decisively on FID₃D (4.94 vs. 10.50 on FFHQ-512; 4.53 vs. 7.68 on FFHQC-512), reflecting GSGAN's heavy reliance on view-conditioning that degrades quality when the conditioning view does not match the rendering view. On standard FID, CGS-GAN is slightly worse on FFHQ (4.94 vs. 5.02) but better on FFHQC (4.53 vs. 5.17), and is the only method that scales to 1024² and 2048². Against GGHead [23], CGS-GAN loses on standard FID for FFHQ (4.94 vs. 4.34) but wins on FFHQC (4.53 vs. 5.37) and on FID₃D by a wide margin (4.94 vs. 7.90 on FFHQ; 4.53 vs. 7.78 on FFHQC), since GGHead's template-mesh design is optimized for frontal views and degrades sharply for side views. In the appendix, CGS-GAN also outperforms the NeRF-based EG3D (FID 4.94 vs. 3.28, though EG3D uses view-conditioning and 2D super-resolution) and Mimic3D (4.94 vs. 4.27, also view-conditioned), and substantially outperforms Gaussian Shell Maps (4.94 vs. 28.19). The key takeaway is that CGS-GAN's FID and FID₃D are identical because it produces a single 3D-consistent scene, whereas all baselines show a 2–6 point gap between the two metrics due to their dependence on view-conditioning.

## Relation to Prior Work

CGS-GAN builds directly on GSGAN's hierarchical transformer-based 3DGS generator, retaining the adaptive transformer blocks and AdaIN style injection from StyleGAN2, but departs in three key ways: (1) it removes the backbone point-upsampling and graph-convolution layers in favor of per-layer upsampling with fixed-size transformer blocks, enabling scalable resolution; (2) it eliminates the contrastive pose loss in favor of StyleGAN-style camera embedding in the discriminator; and (3) it drops view-conditioning entirely, replacing it with a multi-view regularization that renders the same 3DGS scene from multiple cameras per training step. The method also introduces a curated FFHQ subset (FFHQC) that removes occluders, recrops to full heads at 4K, and rebalances pose and expression distributions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in 3D head synthesis: prior 3DGS GANs (GSGAN, GGHead) produce identity-inconsistent scenes when the camera changes, limiting their use in game engines and VR where a single fixed 3D asset is required. By demonstrating that view-conditioning can be fully removed with stable training, CGS-GAN opens a path toward directly importable 3D head assets from a single 2D photo. The proposed FID_3D metric is a useful diagnostic that exposes how much prior methods' apparent quality relies on view-specific rendering. Open questions include extending to full 360° synthesis (back of head), reducing the remaining occluder artifacts, and whether the multi-view regularization strategy generalizes to other 3D generative tasks beyond heads.
