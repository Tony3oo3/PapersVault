## Problem

Current shared representation learning methods (e.g., CLIP) require massive paired datasets—hundreds of millions of image-text pairs—to learn a common embedding space, making them infeasible in domains where pairing is costly, rare, or impossible (e.g., medical imaging). This paper asks whether a meaningful cross-modal shared space can be learned almost exclusively from unpaired data, with only a minimal number of paired samples for alignment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251552/cropped_page1_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes pre-trained unimodal embeddings (e.g., DINOv2 ViT-B/14 for images, Sentence-BERT MiniLM-L6 for text) from large unpaired pools, plus a very small set of paired samples (as few as 50–500 pairs) used only for the CCA alignment step. It outputs a low-dimensional universal embedding vector (dimension r ≤ k) per sample, enabling direct cross-modal comparison via cosine similarity for retrieval, vector arithmetic for generation, and cosine-similarity-based classification for zero-shot and cross-domain tasks.

## Method

SUE (Spectral Universal Embedding) exploits the observation that random walks on semantically rich unimodal representations are structurally similar across modalities, so the leading eigenfunctions of the corresponding diffusion operators are modality-invariant. Concretely, two independent SpectralNet models are trained on unpaired data from each modality to parametrically approximate the k-dimensional Spectral Embedding (leading eigenvectors of the random walk Laplacian), yielding a modality-invariant feature space up to sign and basis ambiguity. A small set of paired samples is then used for Canonical Correlation Analysis to resolve this ambiguity and provide linear cross-modal alignment. Finally, an MMD residual network (trained without any paired data) applies a near-identity non-linear shift to minimize the distributional discrepancy between the two aligned SE spaces, producing the final universal embedding. Ablations confirm that SE is the pivotal component—replacing it with an auto-encoder collapses performance to near-random—while CCA and MMD are necessary for practical alignment.

## Results

On MSCOCO with only 100 paired samples, SUE achieves R@10 of 34.25 (I2T) and 33.25 (T2I), outperforming contrastive learning (13.00/12.20) by over 250% and CSA (3.00/2.25) by more than 10×. On Flickr30k (500 pairs), SUE reaches R@10 of 32.00 (I2T) and 32.75 (T2I) versus 16.20/15.00 for contrastive. The method also generalizes beyond vision-language: on Edges2Shoes (50 pairs) it achieves R@10 of 25.25 (E2S), and on the tabular Handwritten dataset (100 pairs) it reaches R@10 of 79.00 (K2P), a 283.6% improvement over contrastive.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SUE dominates all four baselines across every dataset and task. Against the CLIP-style contrastive baseline [60] (trained on the same small number of pairs), SUE achieves a 257% average improvement on MSCOCO, 103% on Flickr30k, and 284% on Handwritten; the paper further shows that contrastive learning requires an order of magnitude more pairs to match SUE's performance with 100 pairs. Against CSA [48], designed for small paired datasets, SUE outperforms by 10–30× on R@10 across all datasets (e.g., 34.25 vs. 3.00 on MSCOCO I2T). Against SAIL [83], a method that incorporates unpaired data alongside paired data, SUE achieves roughly 4–5× higher R@10 (e.g., 34.25 vs. 7.25 on MSCOCO I2T). Against MMD/domain confusion [66], which requires no paired data at all, SUE achieves 8–10× higher R@10 (e.g., 32.00 vs. 4.00 on Flickr30k I2T), confirming that the small number of pairs used for CCA alignment is critical. In the zero-shot setting, SUE (88.31% on 7-class ImageNet) trails CLIP (99.26%) but does so with 500 pairs versus CLIP's 400 million, and in cross-domain classification SUE (66.02% Amazon→DSLR) nearly doubles the MMD baseline (36.78%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on SpectralNet (Shaham et al., 2018) for parametric spectral embedding and MMD residual networks (Shaham et al., 2017) for distribution matching, combining them with CCA into a three-stage pipeline (SUE) that extracts modality-invariant structure from unimodal data alone. It departs fundamentally from the CLIP-style contrastive learning paradigm, which requires massive paired corpora, by arguing that the eigenfunctions of diffusion operators on each modality's representation are already approximately universal (modality-invariant) due to shared underlying semantic structure. Prior work on incorporating unpaired data (SAIL, domain confusion, MACK) either still relies heavily on paired supervision or does not construct a true shared embedding space; SUE positions itself as the first to learn a cross-modal embedding almost exclusively from unpaired samples, with only a minimal number of pairs used for linear alignment via CCA.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper challenges a core assumption in multimodal representation learning—that paired data is the essential supervisory signal for constructing shared embedding spaces—and demonstrates that spectral structure in unimodal representations carries enough cross-modal information to enable retrieval, generation, arithmetic, and zero-shot classification with as few as 50–500 pairs. However, absolute performance remains well below CLIP-scale models (e.g., 88% vs. 99% zero-shot accuracy), and the method is validated on a limited set of modalities and tasks, leaving open whether the universality assumption holds for more complex domains like video, speech, or scientific data. The observation that stronger unimodal encoders improve SUE performance without additional pairs suggests a promising direction: as foundation models scale, the unpaired-data regime may become increasingly viable.
