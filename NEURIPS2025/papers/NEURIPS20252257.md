## Problem

Existing VLA models for autonomous driving insert discrete textual chains-of-thought (e.g., language descriptions, bounding box coordinates) as intermediate reasoning steps, which compresses rich visual information into symbolic representations that blur spatio-temporal relations and create cross-modal gaps between perception and planning. This paper asks whether autonomous vehicles should instead 'think visually'—constructing mental images of future scenarios—rather than relying on text-based logical deduction, and how to equip existing MLLMs with the image generation capability needed for such visual reasoning without sacrificing their semantic understanding.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252257/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes N surround-view RGB images at the current timestep along with optional navigation commands and ego status (velocity, acceleration) as input. It first autoregressively generates a single unified future front-view frame at 128×192 resolution (via MoVQGAN visual tokens) that overlays red lane dividers and 3D detection boxes on the predicted scene, serving as the spatio-temporal CoT. The same model then autoregressively outputs a sequence of BEV trajectory waypoints (x, y) conditioned on both the current observations and the generated visual CoT, and can also produce free-form scene understanding answers via VQA prompts.

## Method

FSDrive builds on Qwen2-VL-2B by expanding its vocabulary with MoVQGAN visual tokens, enabling the LLM to autoregressively predict image tokens that are decoded back to pixels—no architectural changes beyond vocabulary expansion. A unified pre-training paradigm jointly optimizes VQA (preserving semantic understanding) and future-frame prediction (activating visual generation) using only ~0.3% of the data required by prior unified models. A progressive easy-to-hard scheme first trains the model to predict lane dividers (static physical constraints) and 3D boxes (dynamic constraints) before generating full future frames, enforcing physical plausibility. At inference, the VLA serves a dual role: as a world model it generates the unified spatio-temporal CoT frame (future scene + lane dividers + 3D boxes in one image), and as an inverse-dynamics model it plans the trajectory autoregressively conditioned on current observations and this visual CoT, eliminating the cross-modal conversion gap inherent in text-based CoT.

## Results

On nuScenes, FSDrive (without ego status) achieves 0.53 m average L2 error and 0.19% collision rate under ST-P3 metrics, and 0.96 m L2 / 0.40% collision under UniAD metrics, outperforming all prior autoregressive VLA methods. With ego status, FSDrive* reaches 0.28 m L2 and 0.10% collision (ST-P3), surpassing BEV-Planner* (0.35 m / 0.34%). On NAVSIM, FSDrive attains a PDMS of 85.1, exceeding the best camera-only baseline LAW (84.6) and DiffusionDrive-Cam (83.6). For future-frame generation at 128×192, FSDrive achieves FID 10.1, competitive with the much higher-resolution GEM (10.5 at 576×1024) and substantially better than Doe-1 (15.9 at 384×672).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FSDrive outperforms Doe-1 [95], the closest comparable method that also enables vision generation, by a clear margin: L2 drops from 0.70→0.53 m (ST-P3) and 1.26→0.96 m (UniAD), while collision rate improves from 0.21%→0.19% and 1.19%→0.40%, respectively, indicating that VQ-VAE-based discrete visual tokens in Doe-1 limit understanding. Against OmniDrive [64], FSDrive reduces ST-P3 L2 from 0.84→0.53 m and collision from 0.94%→0.19%, a substantial safety gain. On NAVSIM, FSDrive (PDMS 85.1) beats LAW [33] (84.6), DiffusionDrive-Cam [36] (83.6), PARA-Drive [69] (84.0), and UniAD [21] (83.4) using camera-only input. For scene understanding on DriveLM, FSDrive achieves a final score of 0.57, marginally ahead of OmniDrive (0.56) and SimpleLLM4AD (0.53). In future-frame generation, FSDrive's FID of 10.1 at 128×192 surpasses Doe-1 (15.9 at 384×672) and approaches GEM (10.5 at 576×1024), demonstrating that minimal-data autoregressive generation can rival specialized diffusion models.

## Relation to Prior Work

FSDrive extends the VLA-for-driving line (EMMA, OmniDrive, Doe-1) by replacing their textual chain-of-thought intermediates with a unified visual frame that overlays future lane dividers and 3D boxes on a predicted future image. For the visual generation capability, it departs from prior unified understanding-generation models (Show-o, VILA-U, Janus) that train VQ-VAE token predictors from scratch on billion-scale data; instead, it expands the vocabulary of an off-the-shelf MLLM (Qwen2-VL) with MoVQGAN tokens and activates generation with roughly 0.3% of the data those methods require. The progressive easy-to-hard generation schedule (lane dividers → 3D boxes → full frame) draws on visual prompting engineering and VLIPP's physical-prior idea, while the inverse-dynamics planning step follows the standard VLA formulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The core conceptual contribution—letting a driving VLA reason in pixels rather than in text—addresses a real cross-modal gap that prior text-CoT methods introduce, and the ablation (31% collision reduction over text CoT) provides concrete evidence. Practically, the finding that existing MLLMs can be given image-generation ability with minimal data and no architectural changes lowers the barrier for future work. Key limitations remain: only front-view future frames are generated (not surround views), the autoregressive generation runs at a modest 128×192 resolution, and all trajectory results are open-loop, leaving closed-loop robustness and real-time latency unaddressed.
