## Problem

Current deep time series imputers rely entirely on end-to-end data-driven learning to implicitly discover temporal patterns, overlooking the strategic integration of domain-specific inductive biases. This paper asks whether explicitly encoding the two most salient time-series properties—non-stationarity and periodicity—as structured guidance can substantially improve imputation accuracy across diverse architectures.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a multivariate time series X ∈ R^{T×C} with missing entries and a binary observation mask M, and produces imputed values at the missing positions. A key design constraint is channel independence: all guidance matrices and learned scalars are computed per-channel, so the module scales linearly with the number of channels. The Meta Guidance module is architecture-agnostic and is injected at two specific points in the host model—the input embedding layer (via Hadamard product and concatenation) and the normalization/denormalization stage (via guidance-weighted mean and variance)—making it a drop-in addition to Transformer, CSDI, TimesNet, SAITS, or iTransformer without modifying their core computation.

## Method

Meta Guidance (MG) constructs two interpretable guidance matrices and adaptively fuses them. Non-Stationary Guidance (NSG) assigns Gaussian-decayed weights to observed neighbors within a fixed radius r of each missing position, scaled by a per-channel scalar learned from an MLP over the interpolated input, encoding the proximity principle. Periodic Guidance (PG) first extracts the top-k dominant frequencies per channel via FFT, then places Gaussian-weighted influence at offsets that are integer multiples of each period length, with per-channel, per-frequency scaling scalars also learned by MLPs; the per-frequency PG vectors are fused using amplitude-proportional weights. A lightweight meta weighting network (MLP with sigmoid output) takes the interpolated input, NSG, and PG as features and produces a per-channel scalar λ_c ∈ [0,1] that blends the two guidances: g^Meta = λ_c · g^NS + (1−λ_c) · g^Per. The resulting guidance matrix is injected into the host model at the embedding stage (Hadamard product with X, then concatenation) and at the normalization stage (guidance-weighted mean and variance for RevIN-style normalization/denormalization), adding only O(T) computational overhead.

## Results

Integrating Meta Guidance into five deep imputation architectures (Transformer, CSDI, TimesNet, SAITS, iTransformer) yields an average 27.39% reduction in MAE across nine real-world datasets. The largest gains appear on the highly non-stationary HD dataset (43.76% MAE reduction) and TCPC (34.61%), while stationary/periodic datasets like Electricity and Traffic show more modest but consistent improvements (17.91% and 9.88%, respectively). CSDI+MG achieves the best absolute MAE on most datasets, e.g., 0.041 on HD and 0.022 on Weather at 25% missing rate, substantially outperforming all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

All five MG-enhanced architectures consistently outperform their unmodified counterparts across every dataset and missing rate, confirming MG's model-agnostic benefit. CSDI+MG achieves the lowest absolute MAE on most datasets (e.g., 0.041 on HD, 0.022 on Weather, 0.021 on TCPC at 25%), making it the strongest overall imputer. SAITS+MG shows the largest relative gain on the non-stationary HD dataset (MAE drops from 0.095 to 0.044, a 53.7% reduction), while on the highly periodic Electricity dataset the improvement is more moderate (0.321→0.209, 34.9%). Among the non-MG baselines, TimesNet and CSDI are the strongest, with CSDI excelling on Weather and TCPC but struggling on HD (MAE 0.166 at 25%), where MG-enhanced methods close the gap dramatically. The paper also compares against eight additional baselines (M-RNN, GAIN, BRITS, TIDER, ImputeFormer, ModernTCN, PSW-I) in the appendix, all of which are outperformed by the MG-enhanced methods, with PSW-I being the closest non-MG competitor on TCPC (MAE 0.039 vs. CSDI+MG's 0.021 at 25%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in deep time series imputation—where models like CSDI, TimesNet, SAITS, and iTransformer rely entirely on end-to-end data-driven learning to implicitly discover temporal structure—by explicitly encoding two domain-specific inductive biases (non-stationarity via a Gaussian-weighted proximity principle, and periodicity via FFT-based period detection) as interpretable guidance matrices. Rather than proposing a new architecture, Meta Guidance is a lightweight, model-agnostic plug-in that injects these biases into existing backbones through modified embeddings and guidance-weighted normalization (extending RevIN). The adaptive per-channel weighting (λ) learned by a small MLP distinguishes it from fixed-prior approaches, allowing the module to shift emphasis between local continuity and periodic alignment depending on the input segment's characteristics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 27.39% average MAE reduction across nine datasets and five architectures, with only O(T) additional overhead, makes Meta Guidance a practically attractive drop-in enhancement for any deep imputation pipeline. The ablation and λ-analysis confirm that both biases contribute independently and that the module automatically adapts to dataset characteristics (higher λ on non-stationary HD, lower λ on periodic Electricity), suggesting the approach captures genuine structural properties rather than overfitting. A key limitation is that Meta Guidance is restricted to deep learning imputers and cannot augment statistical methods; an open question is whether the same explicit-bias injection strategy transfers to other time series tasks (forecasting, anomaly detection) or to settings with structured (non-MCAR) missingness beyond the brief MAR/MNAR experiments in the appendix.
