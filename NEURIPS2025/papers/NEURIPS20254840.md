## Problem

Existing personalized VLMs treat understanding and generation as isolated tasks with separate concept tokens, preventing cross-task information transfer. This makes it impossible to perform personalized attribute-reasoning generation—producing images that incorporate textual knowledge about a concept (e.g., 'bo wearing its hat') when that knowledge exists only in the understanding data and not in the generation training set.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254840/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Given 3–10 images of a target concept plus 1–2 textual descriptions containing non-visually-inferable attributes (e.g., 'C's favorite hat is red'), the model produces three types of outputs: personalized textual responses to VQA/QA queries, concept-consistent generated images at 512×512, and attribute-reasoning images that fuse the textual knowledge into the generated scene. The concept is encoded as 32 learnable tokens (16 shared, 8 generation-specific, 8 understanding-specific) prepended to the prompt, requiring only ~10 training images per concept versus ~1,000 for prior unified methods.

## Method

UniCTokens fine-tunes a single set of unified concept tokens on Show-o (1.3B) via a three-stage progressive strategy that explicitly promotes mutual enhancement. Stage 1 (understanding warm-up) trains shared tokens on recognition and conversational VQA to encode high-level concept semantics. Stage 2 (bootstrap generation) adds generation-specific tokens initialized from the understanding tokens (or ArcFace embeddings for human concepts) and trains text-to-image generation, leveraging the learned semantics to retain concept fidelity with only 3–10 images. Stage 3 (deepen understanding from generation) analyzes intermediate diffusion steps to identify 'hard regions' via k-means on pixel differences between timesteps, then initializes new understanding tokens from these fine-grained visual patches and re-trains recognition, injecting low-level detail that the understanding task alone misses. This design achieves state-of-the-art attribute-reasoning generation while maintaining competitive understanding and generation scores with far fewer parameters and training images than Yo'Chameleon.

## Results

On UnifyBench, UniCTokens (1.3B, 32 learnable tokens, ~10 training images) achieves a recognition weight of 0.790, VQA GPT score of 0.523, and QA GPT score of 0.603 for personalized understanding. For generation, it reaches Pure Gen. CLIP-I of 0.750, CLIP-T of 0.282, and People Gen. Face-Similarity of 0.334. Most notably, UniCTokens achieves a PARG (Personalized Attribute-Reasoning Generation) score of 0.359 with CLIP-I of 0.749, setting a new state-of-the-art among unified models and substantially outperforming Yo'Chameleon (0.266 / 0.698).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends Yo'Chameleon, the first unified personalized VLM, by replacing its separate concept tokens for understanding and generation with a single set of unified tokens, and by introducing a three-stage progressive training strategy (understanding warm-up → generation bootstrapping → understanding deepening from generation). It also draws on MC-LLaVA's token initialization and VQA data construction for the understanding warm-up stage, and on DreamBooth-style prompt design for the generative stage. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of 'personalized attribute-reasoning generation'—a task requiring the model to transfer textual knowledge learned during understanding into image generation—along with UnifyBench, the first benchmark specifically designed to evaluate cross-task information transfer in unified personalized models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides empirical evidence that understanding and generation in a unified VLM can mutually reinforce each other through shared tokens, offering a design principle for future unified model personalization. The UnifyBench benchmark and the attribute-reasoning generation task fill a gap in evaluation: prior work assessed understanding and generation in isolation, making it impossible to quantify cross-task transfer. However, the evaluation is limited to 20 concepts on a single 1.3B backbone (Show-o), facial similarity remains below the practical recognition threshold (~0.33 vs. 0.4–0.5), and the method inherits Show-o's style-control limitations. Open questions include whether the three-stage strategy generalizes to larger backbones, multi-concept settings, and domain-shift scenarios.
