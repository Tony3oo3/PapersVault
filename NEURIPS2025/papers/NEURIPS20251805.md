## Problem

Amortized Bayesian inference and amortized data acquisition have advanced in parallel but remain disconnected: inference methods assume passive data collection, while acquisition methods lack integrated, instant posterior updates. Moreover, existing amortized design methods are inflexible, optimizing information gain over all parameters or a fixed predictive target without the ability to dynamically focus on specific parameter subsets or predictive tasks at runtime.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251805/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

ALINE consumes three structured input sets in a single forward pass: a context set of historical (x, y) observations, a discrete query set of N candidate points from a pre-defined pool, and a target set encoding either parameter index subsets or target input locations for predictive queries. The model simultaneously outputs a probability distribution over the query set (selecting the next point to acquire) and a 10-component Gaussian Mixture Model parameterizing either the approximate posterior over targeted parameters or the posterior predictive distribution at target inputs. The target specifier can be changed at runtime without retraining, enabling mid-experiment switching of acquisition goals.

## Method

ALINE employs a single Transformer Neural Process backbone with 3 layers and 4 attention heads, processing context, query, and target sets through shared embedding layers and specialized attention masks that include a query-target cross-attention mechanism allowing each candidate to attend to the current inference goal. Two output heads operate on the shared representations: an inference head (10 MLPs producing GMM parameters) for posterior/predictive estimation, and an acquisition head (2-layer MLP + softmax) for the policy. Training is joint: the inference network is supervised via negative log-likelihood over all T acquisition steps, while the policy is trained with policy gradient RL using a dense per-step reward equal to the one-step improvement in log-probability of the approximate posterior over the selected targets. This reward is proven to be a variational lower bound on the total Expected Information Gain (or Expected Predictive Information Gain), making the objective principled and tractable without requiring explicit likelihood evaluation. A warm-up phase trains only the inference network with random queries before joint policy-inference training begins.

## Results

On the Location Finding BED benchmark, ALINE achieves an EIG lower bound of 8.91±0.04, surpassing the best baseline RL-BOED (7.70±0.06) by 1.21 points, while requiring only 21.20h of training (vs. 63.29h for RL-BOED) and deploying in 0.03s (vs. 146.59s for VPCE). On the CES benchmark, ALINE scores 13.50±0.15, competitive with RL-BOED (14.60±0.10) but outperforming vsOED (12.12±0.18) and DAD (10.77±0.15). In out-of-distribution active learning, ALINE outperforms GP-based baselines (GP-US, GP-VR, GP-EPIG) on 3 of 4 benchmark functions (Higdon, Goldstein-Price, Ackley). On the psychometric model, ALINE matches Psi-marginal's targeted inference quality while delivering a 10× speedup.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ALINE decisively outperforms all amortized BED baselines on Location Finding: it exceeds RL-BOED (7.70) by 1.21 EIG points, DAD (7.33) by 1.58, and vsOED (7.30) by 1.61, while also reducing training time by 3× compared to RL-BOED. On CES, ALINE (13.50) trails RL-BOED (14.60) by 1.10 points but beats vsOED (12.12) and DAD (10.77), with training time 5× lower than RL-BOED. Against non-amortized VPCE, ALINE achieves substantially higher EIG on both tasks (8.91 vs. 5.25; 13.50 vs. 9.40) and is ~5000× faster at deployment (0.03s vs. 146.59s). In out-of-distribution active learning, ALINE surpasses GP-US, GP-VR, and GP-EPIG on 3 of 4 benchmark functions, and outperforms ACE-US in both predictive RMSE and hyperparameter posterior log-probability. On the psychometric model, ALINE matches Psi-marginal when targeting guess/lapse rates (where QUEST+ fails due to dominance by threshold/slope), and is comparable to both baselines on threshold/slope, while offering a 10× speedup over these non-amortized methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

ALINE unifies two previously parallel research threads—amortized Bayesian inference (neural processes, neural posterior estimation) and amortized Bayesian experimental design (DAD, RL-BOED, vsOED)—into a single transformer-based architecture. It builds directly on Transformer Neural Processes for the backbone, ACE for flexible target-conditioned inference via a GMM head, and the sequential EIG/sPCE framework from DAD for the policy training objective. The key conceptual departure is the self-rewarding RL loop where the policy's reward is the improvement in its own integrated inference network's log-probability, eliminating the need for separate contrastive sampling or external posterior estimators used by DAD and RL-BOED.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By jointly amortizing inference and acquisition in one model with runtime-switchable targets, ALINE addresses a practical gap where prior methods required separate pipelines for data selection and belief updating, or were locked into a fixed inference goal. The 10× speedup over non-amortized methods (QUEST+, Psi-marginal) in the psychometric task and the ability to dynamically redirect querying toward nuisance parameters or specific predictive targets have direct implications for clinical trials, psychophysics, and materials science. Key limitations remain: fixed priors require retraining, only marginal posteriors are estimated, the design space is discrete, and input dimensionality is fixed—each identified as a concrete avenue for future work (prior amortization, autoregressive joint posteriors, continuous design, dimension-agnostic generalization).
