## Problem

Vision-language models struggle in real-world smart-home scenarios involving occlusions, poor lighting, or privacy constraints, yet integrating alternative sensing modalities (LiDAR, mmWave radar, WiFi, infrared) into MLLMs is hindered by two compounding challenges: extreme data scarcity for modality-text alignment (only thousands of lab-collected samples versus millions for RGB) and the physical heterogeneity of these signals, which makes standard transformer encoders ineffective at extracting discriminative features.

## Input / Output

HoloLLM consumes 5 or 10 evenly sampled frames from synchronized multimodal sequences (LiDAR/mmWave point clouds, WiFi-CSI time-series, infrared/depth images, video, RFID signals) alongside a text instruction. For Action QA, the text includes a question plus a list of all action categories as options, and the model outputs the correct category; for Action Caption, the model generates a free-form description of the observed action. The same text prompt is shared across all modalities of a given sample, enabling direct cross-modality comparison.

## Method

The core contribution is the Universal Modality-Injection Projector (UMIP), which decouples text-alignment from feature-discrimination to avoid the data overhead of aligning raw heterogeneous features from scratch. A frozen CLIP ViT-L encoder produces pre-aligned initial embeddings for every modality without additional training, while modality-specific tailored encoders (ResNet-18, PointNet, 1D Temporal ResNet-18, MetaFi) capture fine-grained discriminative features. UMIP downsamples the CLIP embeddings into a fixed set of coarse queries and uses the tailored-encoder features as keys and values in an iterative coarse-to-fine cross-attention loop (8 blocks of self-attention → cross-attention → FFN), progressively injecting modality-specific detail into the text-aligned queries before projecting them into the LLaMA2-7B token space. Training is two-stage: tailored encoders are first pre-trained with a classification objective, then frozen while UMIP and modality tokenizers are fine-tuned with a combined classification and next-token-prediction loss. A human-VLM collaborative pipeline (GPT-4o rewriting + LLaVA-Video in-context captioning) generates the paired textual annotations needed for training.

## Results

On the MM-Fi dataset (Random split), HoloLLM achieves 86.5% average Action QA accuracy and 28.4% METEOR for Action Caption, substantially outperforming the strongest baseline ImageBind (46.2% QA, 19.9% METEOR). Under the more challenging Cross-Environment split, HoloLLM still reaches 56.4% QA accuracy and 22.6% METEOR, while ImageBind drops to 16.7% and 17.3% respectively. On XRF55 (Random split), HoloLLM scores 63.5% QA accuracy and 26.6% METEOR versus ImageBind's 35.8% and 16.3%. Projector-based baselines (Tokenpacker, Honeybee, OneLLM) perform near chance level (1–5% QA accuracy) on sensing modalities, highlighting the difficulty of aligning rare sensor data with text.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HoloLLM decisively outperforms all four baselines across every setting and modality. The three projector-based methods—Tokenpacker, Honeybee, and OneLLM—achieve near-chance QA accuracy (1–5%) on sensing modalities like mmWave, LiDAR, and WiFi, indicating that standard multimodal projectors cannot effectively align rare sensor signals with text without large-scale paired data. ImageBind, the strongest baseline, leverages dedicated per-modality encoders and contrastive alignment, reaching 46.2% QA accuracy on MM-Fi (Random) but still trailing HoloLLM by 40.3 percentage points. The gap widens under Cross-Environment evaluation, where ImageBind drops to 16.7% while HoloLLM retains 56.4%, demonstrating that UMIP's coarse-to-fine cross-attention preserves modality-specific discriminability better than contrastive embedding alignment. On XRF55, HoloLLM's advantage over ImageBind is 27.7 points (63.5% vs. 35.8% QA, Random), with the largest per-modality gains on Video (+32.3 pts) and Infrared (+13.6 pts), while both methods struggle on RFID and WiFi under cross-subject/cross-environment splits.

## Relation to Prior Work

HoloLLM extends the OneLLM universal-encoder-plus-projector architecture (Han et al., CVPR 2024) by replacing its generic projector with the Universal Modality-Injection Projector (UMIP), which leverages CLIP's pre-aligned image-text embeddings as coarse queries and injects fine-grained features from modality-specific convolutional encoders via iterative cross-attention. This departs from prior MLLM projectors (Q-Former, Tokenpacker, Honeybee) that require large-scale modality-text pairs for pre-training—impractical for rare sensing modalities like mmWave radar and WiFi-CSI with only thousands of lab-collected samples. On the sensing side, the paper builds on the MM-Fi and XRF55 multimodal human-sensing datasets but is the first to attach language-grounded reasoning (QA, captioning) to these sensing inputs, bridging the gap between the human-sensing literature and the MLLM community.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

HoloLLM establishes the first multisensory, language-grounded benchmark for human perception, providing a concrete evaluation framework that the sensing and MLLM communities can build upon. The UMIP design offers a practical recipe for integrating data-scarce, heterogeneous modalities into LLMs without expensive pre-training, a pattern likely transferable to other rare sensor types. However, WiFi and RFID performance collapses under cross-subject and cross-environment splits, and naive multimodal fusion fails to improve QA/captioning—highlighting that generalization and adaptive fusion remain open problems. The authors note that extending beyond action recognition to task planning and agent action generation is needed for real-world embodied deployment.
