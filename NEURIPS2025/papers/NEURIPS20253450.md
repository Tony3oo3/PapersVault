## Problem

Existing brain atlases are predefined, group-level templates with fixed resolution and cortical-only coverage, overlooking substantial inter-individual variability and subcortical/white-matter structures. Standard clustering algorithms applied to fMRI fail to produce parcels that are simultaneously functionally coherent and spatially contiguous, especially at high resolutions where the low signal-to-noise ratio and tens of thousands of voxels make full connectivity matrices intractable.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253450/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 4D resting-state fMRI volumes (96×96×96 spatial × 300 temporal frames at 2 mm isotropic resolution, smoothed to 3 mm FWHM) along with an arbitrary binary ROI mask and a user-specified parcel count K. It outputs a voxel-wise label map assigning each ROI voxel to one of K spatially contiguous, functionally coherent parcels; a group-level atlas is then derived by aggregating individual parcellations via template matching and connected-component enforcement. The framework generalizes to any brain structure (cortex, subcortex, white matter) by simply changing the ROI mask.

## Method

DCA first self-supervised pretrains a Swin-UNETR autoencoder via masked 4D reconstruction (80% spatiotemporal masking) to produce 256-dim voxel-level embeddings capturing both local and global fMRI context. For parcellation, it builds a 26-neighborhood graph over ROI voxels with edge weights set to cosine similarity of demeaned embeddings, then runs sparse spectral clustering on this graph to obtain hard auxiliary labels that enforce spatial contiguity. A KL-divergence loss aligns soft assignments (derived from Euclidean distances to K learnable, L2-normalized centroids) with these auxiliary targets, backpropagating gradients only to the centroids and the encoder's final projection layer while freezing all other weights. The procedure iterates—refining embeddings, re-weighting graph edges, and updating cluster centers—with Hungarian-algorithm label alignment across iterations to preserve cluster identity. A group atlas is constructed by selecting top-K template label vectors, assigning voxels by maximum label agreement, and enforcing contiguity via largest-connected-component retention with iterative neighbor-based reassignment.

## Results

Across 100 HCP subjects and parcel counts from 41 to 800, DCA achieves a mean Homogeneity of 0.1004 ± 0.0216 and Silhouette Coefficient of 0.0304 ± 0.0068 at 100 parcels, outperforming the strongest baseline (Schaefer) at every resolution. On average across all tested scales, DCA improves Homogeneity by 98.8% and Silhouette by 29% over the best existing atlas. At 200 parcels, DCA reaches Homogeneity 0.1127 vs. Schaefer's 0.0634 (+77.7%) and Silhouette 0.0417 vs. 0.0349 (+19.5%). In downstream tasks (gender, fluid intelligence, 7/24-way cognitive decoding, ASD, AD/MCI), DCA consistently matches or exceeds the top baseline at each resolution, with a task-specific atlas yielding up to +12% accuracy gain on gender classification.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DCA outperforms all seven compared atlases (Yeo, Brodmann, Schaefer, AAL, MMP, MUSE, GIANT) on both Homogeneity and Silhouette at every matched resolution. The largest gap is against Schaefer, the strongest functional baseline: at 200 parcels DCA gains +77.7% Homogeneity and +19.5% Silhouette; at 360 parcels the gap widens to +79.3% Homogeneity (0.1266 vs. 0.0706) and +27.9% Silhouette (0.0545 vs. 0.0426). Anatomical atlases (AAL, Brodmann, MUSE) show the weakest functional coherence, with Homogeneity values roughly 3–5× lower than DCA at comparable parcel counts. On downstream tasks, DCA200 and DCA360 achieve top or near-top accuracy on cognitive decoding (7-way and 24-way) and ASD classification, while Schaefer500 marginally edges DCA on individual tasks but DCA generalizes more consistently across domains. The task-specific variant DCA₁₀₀^gender (individual) reaches 0.82 accuracy with a 1-D CNN and 0.825 with k-GNN, surpassing the group-level DCA100 (0.70/0.650) and Schaefer100 (0.65/0.725) by up to 12%.

## Relation to Prior Work

DCA sits at the intersection of two research threads: (1) deep embedding clustering (Xie et al. 2015; Cai et al. 2022), which jointly refines encoder parameters and cluster centroids via KL-divergence between soft assignments and auxiliary targets, and (2) traditional brain atlas construction using spatially constrained spectral clustering, k-means, or community detection on functional connectivity matrices. The key departure is the first application of deep clustering to fully continuous, voxel-level brain parcellation, where a pretrained Swin-UNETR encoder (originally designed for 3D medical image segmentation) provides spatiotemporal embeddings and a 26-neighborhood KNN graph with cosine-similarity edge weights supplies the spatial prior that enforces anatomical contiguity. Unlike prior spatial regularization strategies (Markov random field priors, distance penalties) that require careful tuning and can compromise functional coherence, DCA's iterative KL-driven refinement alternates between updating graph weights, encoder parameters, and cluster centers, avoiding the trade-off between spatial smoothness and functional homogeneity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DCA addresses a long-standing practical limitation: existing atlases are fixed-resolution, group-level templates that cannot be adapted to individual subjects or arbitrary anatomical scopes. The released AtlaScore benchmarking platform (17 standardized evaluations spanning internal metrics and downstream tasks) gives the community a common yardstick for comparing parcellations, which has been lacking. Open questions include whether the fixed 26-neighborhood KNN graph suppresses long-range functional relationships, how the method scales to whole-brain (including white matter) parcellation at high resolution without prohibitive compute, and whether multimodal integration (structural MRI, diffusion, electrophysiology) can further ground the parcellations biologically. The finding that a task-specific fine-tuned encoder yields a meaningfully better atlas for that task (+12% on gender classification) suggests a broader principle: atlas quality is task-dependent, and no single parcellation is universally optimal.
