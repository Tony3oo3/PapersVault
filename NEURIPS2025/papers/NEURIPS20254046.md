## Problem

Existing post-hoc probabilistic adaptation methods for pretrained VLMs (e.g., ProbVLM, BayesVLM) treat text and image uncertainty symmetrically and operate in Euclidean space, ignoring two structural facts: (1) text-to-image ambiguity is driven by high aleatoric uncertainty from linguistic abstraction, while image-to-text ambiguity stems from lower-variance descriptive diversity, and (2) meaningful embeddings from contrastive-pretrained VLMs reside on the unit hypersphere, not in ℝ^d. This leads to suboptimal uncertainty estimates and degraded retrieval performance.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a frozen pretrained VLM (CLIP ViT-B/32 or SigLIP) and a batch of image-text pairs. Text inputs pass through the frozen text encoder followed by a trainable 4-layer MLP adapter that outputs a mean direction μ ∈ S^{d-1} and a scalar concentration κ > 0 (decomposed from the unnormalized output as z' = κμ). Image inputs pass only through the frozen image encoder, yielding deterministic unit-norm vectors. At inference, retrieval is performed by maximizing the directional log-likelihood (not raw cosine similarity), and a scalar uncertainty estimate u(t) = 1/κ(t) is produced for each text embedding.

## Method

AsymVLM introduces an asymmetric probabilistic adapter that models only text embeddings as random variables on the unit hypersphere using directional distributions (von Mises-Fisher or power spherical), while retaining deterministic image embeddings. The training objective is InfoNCE applied to a B×B log-likelihood matrix whose (m,n) entry is ln p_{z_m^T}(z_n^I), yielding kernels of the form κ(t_r)·μ(t_r)^⊤ z_s^I + F_d(κ(t_r)) for vMF or κ(t_r)·ln(1+μ(t_r)^⊤ z_s^I) + ln C_d(κ(t_r)) for PS. This loss is shown to be a monotonic extension of CLIP's cosine-similarity kernel with an additional concentration parameter, preserving alignment with the pretrained geometry while capturing text variance. The intractable vMF log-normalizer is approximated via tight Bessel-function bounds from Scott et al. (2021). Ablations confirm that both the asymmetric architecture (probabilistic text only) and the spherical (vs. Gaussian) distribution are necessary for optimal uncertainty quantification and retrieval accuracy.

## Results

On MS-COCO, AsymVLM_vMF achieves 0.561 Recall@1 for text-to-image and 0.392 for image-to-text retrieval, outperforming all baselines (next best: BayesVLM at 0.506/0.323). On Flickr-30k, AsymVLM_vMF reaches 0.688/0.504 Recall@1 (T2I/I2T), and on CC-200k it achieves 0.395/0.383. For uncertainty quantification, AsymVLM achieves near-perfect Spearman correlation (S = −1.000) and R² ≥ 0.94 between uncertainty and retrieval error across all datasets, with Recall@1 decreasing strictly monotonically with uncertainty—behavior unique among all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AsymVLM outperforms all five baselines on Recall@1 across all three datasets in both retrieval directions. PFE* and PCME++* (adapted post-hoc) only predict embedding variance while keeping directions fixed, so their retrieval performance is identical to the frozen CLIP backbone (e.g., 0.500 T2I on MS-COCO); AsymVLM_vMF improves this to 0.561 by also adapting directions. ProbVLM, which does adapt directions, achieves reasonable uncertainty estimates (R² up to 0.979) but consistently underperforms the pretrained CLIP in recall (0.480 vs. 0.500 T2I on MS-COCO), whereas AsymVLM improves both recall and uncertainty quality simultaneously. BayesVLM provides moderate uncertainty estimates (R² ≈ 0.88–0.92) but lags AsymVLM by 5–9 points in Recall@1 on MS-COCO. On the CC-200k dataset, the gap is most pronounced: AsymVLM_vMF achieves 0.395 T2I Recall@1 with a perfect Spearman correlation (S = −1.000), while the next-best method (ProLip) reaches only 0.351 with S = −0.990, and PFE*/PCME++* show near-zero correlation (S = −0.521 / −0.148), indicating their uncertainty estimates are essentially uninformative for retrieval quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

AsymVLM extends the post-hoc probabilistic adaptation paradigm introduced by ProbVLM and BayesVLM, which train lightweight adapters on frozen pretrained VLM embeddings to produce probability distributions. The key departure is twofold: (1) it introduces an asymmetric architecture where only text embeddings are modeled probabilistically (via vMF or power-spherical distributions on the unit hypersphere) while image embeddings remain deterministic, grounded in the argument that text carries high aleatoric uncertainty (one abstract concept maps to many objects) whereas images carry primarily epistemic uncertainty reducible by data coverage; and (2) it operates natively on the unit hypersphere using directional distributions rather than in Euclidean space, making it a direct, monotonic extension of CLIP's cosine-similarity kernel with an added concentration parameter κ. The vMF log-normalizer approximation F_d(κ) is inherited from Scott et al. (2021), and a SigLIP variant is derived by swapping the kernel in the sigmoid objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, lightweight mechanism for uncertainty quantification in pretrained VLMs that is directly compatible with the cosine-similarity inference pipeline, enabling practical applications such as 'none-of-the-above' rejection (achieving ~55–60% negative-sample accuracy vs. ~1–12% for deterministic baselines) and more robust fine-tuning that mitigates catastrophic forgetting. The asymmetry argument—text uncertainty is irreducible (aleatoric) while image uncertainty is data-dependent (epistemic)—offers a conceptual lens that prior symmetric methods overlooked. A key limitation is that only text uncertainty is modeled; extending probabilistic treatment to image embeddings (e.g., via augmentation-based epistemic estimation) remains open, as does exploring whether the asymmetry framing generalizes to other multimodal pairs beyond vision-language.
