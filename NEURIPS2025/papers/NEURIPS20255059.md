## Problem

The grokking phenomenon—where training loss rapidly reaches zero while test loss plateaus for an extended period before suddenly improving—lacks a rigorous, general optimisation-based explanation. Prior accounts either attributed the delayed generalisation to stochastic effects (SGD noise) or remained informal, and no prior work identified a deterministic mechanism linking weight decay to a slow drift along the interpolation manifold. This paper fills that gap by proving, under mild regularity assumptions, that small weight decay induces a two-timescale dynamics whose slow phase is a Riemannian gradient flow minimising the ℓ₂ norm on the manifold of stationary points.

## Input / Output

The framework takes an arbitrary smooth training loss F: ℝᵈ → ℝ₊ (satisfying C³ regularity, o-minimal definability, and bounded unregularised flow), a weight decay parameter λ > 0, and an initialisation w₀ independent of λ. It produces a full trajectory decomposition: on finite intervals the regularised flow w^λ tracks the unregularised gradient flow (fast phase), and after a timescale of order 1/λ the trajectory drifts along the stationary manifold M following a Riemannian gradient flow of ‖w‖₂²/2. The ultimate output is a characterisation of the double limit lim_{λ→0} lim_{t→∞} w^λ(t) as a KKT point of the constrained problem min_{w∈M} ‖w‖₂², with the additional guarantee (Proposition 4) that this limit is a strict local minimum of the norm on M when the Riemannian flow converges to one.

## Method

The core result decomposes the regularised gradient flow into two coupled dynamics using singular perturbation theory. In the fast phase (Proposition 1), a Grönwall argument shows w^λ converges uniformly to the unregularised flow on any finite interval [0,T] as λ→0. In the slow phase (Proposition 2), after rescaling time via t̃ = t/λ, the trajectory is shown to converge to a Riemannian gradient flow on the stationary manifold M, where the Riemannian gradient of the squared norm is identified with the orthogonal projection onto Ker(∇²F(w)) via the identity DΦ_w = P_Ker(∇²F(w)) from Li et al. [2021]. The junction between phases is handled by a timeshift at t(λ) = −λ ln λ / 2c (Lemma 2), and the proof of the slow-phase convergence is self-contained, building on Falconer's C² regularity of the limit mapping Φ near M rather than invoking Katzenberger's stochastic differential equation machinery. The Morse-Bott property (Assumption 2) ensures M is a smooth manifold with local exponential attraction, enabling the stability arguments that close the proof.

## Results

The main theoretical contribution is a rigorous two-timescale decomposition of gradient flow with small weight decay λ: (i) on finite intervals [0,T], w^λ → w^GF uniformly (Proposition 1), and (ii) on the rescaled time t/λ, the trajectory converges to the Riemannian gradient flow of ‖w‖²/₂ on the stationary manifold M (Proposition 2). Proposition 3 further shows that the limit points of w^λ as t→∞ are KKT points of min_{w∈M} ‖w‖², and Proposition 4 pins down the exact limit under a strict local minimum assumption. Empirically, the two-phase grokking pattern is confirmed on matrix completion (rank-3, 20×20, 50% entries, λ=10⁻³) where singular values of UVᵀ decay from 10 large values to the 3 true singular values by t≈10⁴, and on a two-layer ReLU network (m=100, n=10, λ=10⁻³) where the weight norm drops steadily after t≈1/λ while test loss improves.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no quantitative baseline comparisons; the 'results' are theorems rather than benchmark numbers. Conceptually, the paper positions itself against Lyu et al. [2023], who characterize grokking as a lazy-to-rich regime transition but require homogeneous parameterisation, large initialisation scales, and polynomially coupled limits (scale→∞, λ→0), and do not identify the slow drift phase along the interpolation manifold. The present framework is more general: it applies outside the NTK regime, accommodates arbitrary initialisation, and provides a self-contained proof (building on Falconer [1983] rather than Katzenberger [1990]) that characterises the full long-term limit of w^λ via KKT points. Compared to Li et al. [2021], who study stochastic drift on the manifold after SGD reaches zero loss, this paper identifies a deterministic mechanism (weight decay) driving the second phase and links it explicitly to the grokking phenomenon. The empirical experiments (matrix completion, ReLU networks, diagonal linear networks) serve as validation rather than competitive benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the drift-on-interpolation-manifold framework of Li et al. [2021] and the singular-perturbation analysis of Fatkullin et al. [2010], but identifies a deterministic mechanism (weight decay) for the slow phase rather than stochastic noise. It departs from Lyu et al. [2023], which also theorises grokking as a lazy-to-rich transition, by removing the need for homogeneous parameterisation, large initialisation, or specific architectures, and by explicitly characterising the slow Riemannian drift along the critical manifold. The regularity of the limit mapping Φ relies on Falconer [1983], and the Morse-Bott/PL equivalence on Rebjock and Boumal [2024].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides the first rigorous, architecture-agnostic optimisation-theoretic explanation for grokking as a two-timescale process, showing that the 'sudden' generalisation jump is actually a gradual norm reduction over O(1/λ) time that only appears abrupt on log-scale plots. Key limitations include the asymptotic λ→0 regime (fixed-λ analysis is deferred to a heuristic appendix), the restriction to regression settings with bounded dynamics (classification is left open), and empirical validation limited to small synthetic tasks. Open questions include extending the analysis to classification losses where the stationary manifold lies at infinity, and to non-smooth architectures like ReLU networks where the manifold structure may have singularities.
