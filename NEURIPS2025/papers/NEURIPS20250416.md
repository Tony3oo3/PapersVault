## Problem

Extracting shape descriptors from 2D object contours that remain invariant to translation, rotation, scaling, reflection, and arbitrary point indexation has been a persistent challenge. Classical statistics-based and Fourier-descriptor methods discard fine contour detail, while existing deep-learning approaches operating on segmentation masks require complex alignment or equivariant architectures to achieve geometric invariance. No prior method had solved the indexation-invariance problem (sensitivity to the choice of starting point and traversal direction) within a generative VAE framework for closed contours.

## Input / Output

The method takes binary segmentation masks as input, extracts simply-connected contours via marching squares, uniformly samples N points (default 64, up to 512 for highly irregular shapes), and converts them into an N×N Euclidean distance matrix normalized by its Frobenius norm. The VAE produces a 128-dimensional latent shape descriptor invariant to all shape-preserving transformations, and the decoded distance matrix can be inverted via multi-dimensional scaling to recover a 2D outline (up to arbitrary rotation, translation, and reflection). A variant (ShapeEmbed+Sz) optionally concatenates the pre-normalization matrix norm to the latent code when object size is biologically relevant.

## Method

ShapeEmbed encodes normalized distance matrices (rather than raw masks) with a modified ResNet-18 VAE, exploiting the fact that pairwise Euclidean distances are inherently invariant to translation and rotation. Indexation invariance is achieved by replacing standard padding with circular (repeated-tiling) padding in every convolutional layer, making the encoder shift-equivariant to the diagonal shifts induced by different contour starting points, combined with a novel reconstruction loss that takes the minimum MSE over all 2N equivalent indexations of the input matrix. Reflection invariance is enforced by processing each distance matrix both in its original form and horizontally/vertically mirrored, then summing the two encoder outputs. Three additional regularization terms (zero diagonal, non-negativity, and symmetry) encourage the decoder to produce valid Euclidean distance matrices, and a small KL coefficient (10⁻¹⁰) balances reconstruction fidelity against latent-space smoothness for generative sampling.

## Results

ShapeEmbed achieves the highest F1-score among all methods on both benchmark datasets: 0.96 ± 0.01 on MNIST and 0.75 ± 0.02 on MPEG-7. On the four biological imaging datasets, ShapeEmbed attains F1-scores of 0.67 (MEF), 0.83 (BBBC010), 0.80 (HeLa Kyoto), and 0.68 (MOC), consistently outperforming all classical and self-supervised baselines. Adding size information back (ShapeEmbed+Sz) further improves performance to 0.76, 0.87, 0.85, and 0.70 respectively, demonstrating the flexibility of the framework in handling scale as either a nuisance or a signal.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ShapeEmbed outperforms all five baseline families across nearly all datasets. Against the direct competitor O2VAE, ShapeEmbed wins by a wide margin on every dataset (e.g., 0.83 vs 0.61 on BBBC010, 0.80 vs 0.63 on HeLa Kyoto), confirming that the distance-matrix-based approach is superior to rotation-equivariant convolutional encoders on raw masks. Against the strongest MAE variant (ViT-L pretrained), ShapeEmbed leads on all four biological datasets (0.67 vs 0.64 on MEF, 0.83 vs 0.77 on BBBC010, 0.80 vs 0.76 on HeLa Kyoto, 0.68 vs 0.66 on MOC). The one notable exception is MEF, where Region Properties (0.72) edges out ShapeEmbed (0.67) because that dataset contains experimentally-induced size differences that summary statistics capture directly; ShapeEmbed+Sz (0.76) recovers and surpasses this gap. On the standard benchmarks, ShapeEmbed achieves 0.96 F1 on MNIST (beating MAE ViT-B's 0.95 and O2VAE's 0.86) and 0.75 on MPEG-7 (beating Region Properties' 0.70 and O2VAE's 0.13), with EFD and SimCLR performing poorly across the board.

## Relation to Prior Work

ShapeEmbed builds on the VAE framework (Kingma & Welling, 2014) but departs fundamentally from prior shape representation learning methods (Chan et al. 2020, Ruan & Murphy 2019, Vadgama et al. 2022/2023, O2VAE/Burgess et al. 2024) that all encode raw segmentation masks and must explicitly learn or enforce geometric invariance through complex training strategies or equivariant architectures. Instead, ShapeEmbed encodes Euclidean distance matrices of object contours, which are inherently invariant to translation, rotation, and (after normalization) scaling, reducing the invariance problem to only indexation and reflection. The indexation invariance is achieved through circular padding in a ResNet-18 encoder combined with a novel min-over-permutations reconstruction loss, while reflection invariance comes from processing each matrix and its mirror image. This approach is conceptually related to AlphaFold's use of distance matrices for protein structure, but extends to closed 2D contours where the indexation invariance problem is unique.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ShapeEmbed provides a clean, self-supervised solution to a practical problem in biological imaging where objects appear at arbitrary positions, orientations, and scales, and shape differences are often subtle. The distance matrix representation elegantly eliminates three of five invariance requirements by construction, making the remaining architectural modifications (circular padding, dual-pathway mirroring) relatively simple. Key limitations include the requirement for simply-connected contours, the 2D restriction, and the lack of strict guarantees that decoded outputs are valid Euclidean distance matrices. Open directions include 3D extension via shape signatures, reflection-equivariant convolutions to avoid the double-processing overhead, and parameterizing outputs to guarantee valid EDMs by construction.
