## Problem

Drone-based multi-object tracking is severely challenged by small targets, high density, and cluttered backgrounds where RGB spatial appearance cues (color, texture) become indistinct. While multispectral imagery offers complementary per-pixel spectral reflectance that enhances object discriminability, no dedicated multispectral UAV MOT dataset existed, and existing MOT algorithms assume RGB input with axis-aligned boxes, leaving both the data gap and the architectural gap unaddressed.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 8-channel multispectral video at 1200×900 resolution (395–950 nm, visible to near-infrared) or a 3-channel RGB proxy synthesized from bands 5, 3, and 2 of the MSI cube. It produces oriented bounding boxes (center, width, height, angle) with persistent unique identity IDs across frames, forming multi-object tracking trajectories over 8 fine-grained categories (pedestrian, car, van, truck, bus, tricycle, bike, awning-bike). The OBB format is essential because aerial viewpoints yield arbitrary object orientations that axis-aligned boxes cannot represent without significant geometric error.

## Method

The paper introduces a unified adaptation scheme with three components. First, a Spectral 3D-Stem replaces the standard 2D input convolution: a 3D conv with spectral kernel 3 captures local inter-band variations producing 8 feature-map groups, then a depthwise 3D conv with spectral kernel 8 folds the spectral axis back to a single spatial feature map, adding only 8×D parameters while preserving direct reuse of RGB-pretrained weights. Second, an orientation-aware Kalman filter extends the motion state to [u, v, s₁, s₂, θ, ẋ, ẏ, ṡ₁, ṡ₂, θ̇] and replaces standard IoU with oriented IoU (rIoU) for data association in tracking-by-detection methods. Third, for tracking-by-query (DETR-based) methods, an angle prediction head parallel to the box head predicts a normalized orientation, refined iteratively across decoder layers using a le135-style regression, with L1 loss on the 5-D OBB parameters and rIoU loss replacing standard IoU loss. The scheme is applied uniformly to eight representative trackers (SORT, ByteTrack, OC-SORT, BoT-SORT, MOTR, MOTRv2, MeMOTR, MOTIP) and a YOLOv11 detector, enabling fair cross-paradigm and cross-modality comparison on the new MMOT benchmark.

## Results

On the MMOT dataset with MSI input, BoT-SORT achieves the best overall tracking performance with class-averaged HOTA of 53.6, MOTA of 46.2, and IDF1 of 61.0, and detection-averaged HOTA of 60.7, MOTA of 59.4, and IDF1 of 69.4. MOTRv2 ranks second with class-averaged HOTA of 49.2 and detection-averaged HOTA of 54.5. MeMOTR achieves the highest detection-averaged AssA of 70.9, indicating superior multi-frame association capability. Across all methods, MSI input consistently improves class-averaged HOTA by 3.5–4.1 points over RGB, with the largest gains in the HUMAN category (+7.0 to +7.3 HOTA).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among tracking-by-detection methods, BoT-SORT dominates all other TBD baselines (SORT, ByteTrack, OC-SORT) by a wide margin, achieving 53.6 class-averaged HOTA versus 40.5 for ByteTrack, 29.5 for OC-SORT, and 27.2 for SORT, largely due to its optical-flow module compensating for strong drone ego-motion. Among tracking-by-query methods, MOTRv2 leads with 49.2 class-averaged HOTA, outperforming MOTR (39.0), MeMOTR (42.3), and MOTIP (39.0), though MeMOTR achieves the best association quality (AssA 70.9 vs. 68.8 for MOTRv2). The paper further demonstrates that MSI input consistently outperforms RGB across all query-based trackers: MOTRv2 gains +4.05 HOTA (44.48→48.53), MeMOTR gains +3.94 (40.75→44.69), and MOTR gains +3.48 (38.16→41.64) in superclass-averaged HOTA. The Spectral 3D-Stem ablation confirms its contribution over a naive 2D stem, with the largest gains in TBQ methods (+3.1 HOTA for MOTR, +3.8 for MeMOTR). Finally, detector quality is shown to be a critical bottleneck for TBD methods: replacing YOLOv11-L (mAP50=73.4) with Deformable-DETR (mAP50=62.1) drops BoT-SORT's class-averaged HOTA from 53.6 to 39.2.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three established research threads: UAV-based MOT datasets (UAVDT, VisDrone), multispectral visual tracking (HOT, MUST), and generic MOT benchmarks (MOT20, DanceTrack, SportsMOT). It departs from prior UAV MOT work by introducing multispectral (8-band) input and oriented bounding boxes, and from prior multispectral tracking work by scaling to multi-object tracking with identity consistency. Methodologically, it adapts eight existing MOT frameworks (SORT through MOTIP) via a lightweight Spectral 3D-Stem for channel-compatible feature extraction, an orientation-aware Kalman filter, and an angle prediction head inspired by Deformable-DETR's iterative refinement, rather than proposing a fundamentally new tracking architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By providing the first dedicated benchmark for drone-based multispectral MOT with OBBs, this work fills a clear gap that has limited progress in spectral aerial tracking. The consistent MSI-over-RGB gains (especially +7 HOTA for pedestrians) validate that spectral cues are a practical remedy for the small-target, low-texture regime dominant in aerial views. Open questions include whether the spectral advantage generalizes to other sensor configurations or platforms, how to reduce the 5,000+ hour annotation cost for OBBs, and whether tracking-by-query methods can close the gap with tracking-by-detection once their detection capacity is improved. Privacy and surveillance concerns around robust small-object tracking from the air are acknowledged but left for future work.
