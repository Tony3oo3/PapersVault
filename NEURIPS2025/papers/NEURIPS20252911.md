## Problem

Existing quaternion-valued Hopfield neural networks are confined to unsupervised associative-memory paradigms with fixed attractor dynamics, lacking explicit target tracking, structural control, or error-driven learning. While continuous-time modern HNNs have been introduced, they do not operate in the quaternionic domain with the algebraic constraints needed for 3D rotation representation. This paper addresses the gap by formulating a supervised, continuous-time quaternionic Hopfield network with provable asymptotic stability and smooth trajectory guarantees suitable for robotic control.

## Input / Output

The network takes N target quaternion states (each with four real components sampled uniformly from [-1,1]) as training targets and initial joint configurations as runtime inputs. It produces a trained 4N×4N real weight matrix whose every 4×4 block lies on the quaternion left-multiplication submanifold, along with smooth continuous-time quaternion trajectories whose curvature is uniformly bounded by κ ≤ 4. The network is a fully connected system of N quaternion neurons (4N real-valued Hopfield neurons), and the output at convergence is a unique equilibrium state matching the target within MSE < 10⁻⁶.

## Method

The QSHNN is a continuous-time autonomous dynamical system governed by the quaternionic ODE q̇ = −γq + μW∘φ(q) + μb, where each quaternion neuron integrates four real-valued Hopfield neurons and the weight matrix W is constrained so that every 4×4 block is a quaternion left-multiplication matrix. Training uses strict gradient descent derived via sensitivity analysis of the steady-state equation, with the gradient computed through the inverse of the sensitivity matrix S = I − (μ/γ)W·J_φ(q). The key innovation is a periodic projection step: every K = 5–10 gradient iterations, each 4×4 weight block is orthogonally projected onto the quaternion left-multiplication submanifold L = span{L(1),L(i),L(j),L(k)} under the Frobenius inner product, restoring quaternionic structure without requiring full GHR-calculus-based descent at every step. Two weight-norm constraints (μ/γ Σ Lᵢ|wᵢⱼ| < 1 for existence/uniqueness and μ/(2γ) Σ(|wⱼᵢ|+|wᵢⱼ|) < 1 for asymptotic stability) are enforced via ∞-norm normalization during training, and the bounded Lipschitz constant of tanh combined with normalized weights yields the curvature bound κ ≤ 4 on all trajectories.

## Results

The paper reports preliminary experiments with a 4-quaternion-neuron QSHNN trained on randomly generated target sets (components sampled uniformly from [-1,1]). Training converges to a mean square error below 10⁻⁶ within T_max = 30,000 iterations, with the periodic projection (every P=10 steps) preserving the 4×4 quaternion block structure of the weight matrix. The trajectory curvature is provably bounded by κ ≤ 4 for all components, ensuring smooth evolution suitable for robotic joint control. A preliminary PyBullet simulation demonstrates that the trained QSHNN can drive a 4-DOF robotic manipulator from arbitrary initial joint configurations to a specified end-effector posture. No formal benchmark comparison against baselines is conducted in this paper; such evaluation is deferred to a follow-up publication.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not include quantitative comparisons against baselines. Four baseline methods are listed in Appendix F (Damped Least-Squares IK, CHOMP, STOMP, and RRT-Connect + B-Spline Smoothing) and three benchmark environments are described in Appendix E (Robosuite PandaReach, OpenAI Gym FetchReach, and Random Workspace Target Alignment in MuJoCo), but the authors explicitly state that the complete evaluation against these baselines is planned for a follow-up publication. The only comparative analysis provided is qualitative: the paper argues that QSHNN's continuous-time dynamics with provable global convergence and bounded curvature offer advantages over static regression (QSNN), fixed-attractor QHNNs, and the listed trajectory optimization baselines, which suffer from initialization sensitivity, lack of global convergence guarantees, or high online computation cost. No numerical head-to-head results are reported.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the continuous-time Hopfield neural network (HNN) formulation into the quaternionic domain, building directly on the classic HNN evolution equation and the Generalized HR (GHR) calculus for quaternionic differentiation. It departs from prior quaternion-valued HNNs (which are discrete-time, unsupervised, and rely on Hebbian/outer-product weight encoding) by introducing a supervised learning paradigm with continuous-time dynamics and an explicit target-tracking objective. The key technical departure is the periodic Frobenius projection onto the quaternion left-multiplication manifold, which replaces the computationally expensive full GHR gradient descent with a practical alternating scheme of standard gradient steps and structure-preserving projections.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first rigorous Lyapunov-based stability guarantees (existence, uniqueness, asymptotic stability, bounded trajectory curvature) for a supervised quaternion-valued Hopfield network, filling a gap where prior QHNNs lacked convergence proofs under supervised training. The periodic projection technique offers a general recipe for maintaining algebraic structure in hypercomplex neural networks, potentially applicable beyond quaternions. However, experimental validation is limited to 4-neuron networks with random targets and a preliminary PyBullet simulation; the full robotic benchmark comparisons (Robosuite, FetchReach, MuJoCo) and baseline evaluations (CHOMP, STOMP, RRT-Connect, DLS-IK) are deferred to a follow-up publication, leaving the practical advantage over established motion planners unverified.
