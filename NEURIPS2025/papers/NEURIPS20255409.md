## Problem

Existing RAG benchmarks evaluate models under clean retrieval (gold-standard documents) or synthetically perturbed settings (artificially injected noise), neither of which reflects real-world conditions where retrieved evidence is naturally misleading, conflicting, or polarized. This leads to overestimation of RAG system performance, particularly in high-stakes domains like political fact-checking where misinformation is embedded in discourse rather than stated outright.

## Input / Output

The system takes a political claim (averaging 17.6 words) from PolitiFact and a set of retrieved Reddit documents (averaging 6.2 per claim, 161 words each) as context, and produces a binary TRUE/FALSE verdict. Documents are categorized not by objective truth content but by their functional effect on an LLM's prediction: supporting (helps the model reach the correct label), misleading (causes an incorrect prediction through subtle framing or omission), or unrelated (topically relevant but uninformative). Evaluation spans five settings from zero-context to oracle retrieval of only misleading documents, with two-shot in-context examples provided in all prompted settings.

## Method

RAGUARD is constructed in three stages: (1) scraping 2,648 political claims from PolitiFact (2000–2024 US presidential candidates) and binarizing the six-point truth scale into true/false; (2) using GPT-4 to generate keyword variants per claim, then issuing Google Search restricted to Reddit to collect top-10 posts per claim, yielding 16,331 documents; (3) LLM-guided annotation where GPT-4 is prompted to classify each claim using only a single document as context, and the document is labeled supporting, misleading, or unrelated based on whether its presence helps or hurts the model's prediction relative to the gold verdict. The key novelty is defining 'misleading' relative to model behavior rather than human judgment, enabling scalable annotation that targets LLM-specific reasoning vulnerabilities. Cross-model agreement (Cohen's κ = 0.789 with Claude 3.5, 0.650 with Gemini 1.5) confirms labels are not GPT-4-specific artifacts. Evaluation of eight LLMs (open-source, closed-source, and reasoning-oriented) across all settings reveals that every model performs worse than its zero-shot baseline when exposed to misleading retrievals, with Oracle Retrieval (Misleading) causing an average 46.5% accuracy drop.

## Results

On RAGUARD, all eight evaluated LLMs perform worse than their zero-shot (no-retrieval) baselines in every RAG setting, with the most dramatic degradation in Oracle Retrieval (Misleading), where average accuracy drops by 46.5% and every model falls below 50% on a binary task. Claude 3.5 Sonnet achieves the highest zero-context accuracy (74.51%) but suffers a 51.7% relative drop under misleading retrieval, while GPT-4o is the most robust overall (31.7% drop). In Standard RAG, adding more documents (RAG-5 vs. RAG-1) often worsens performance, e.g., DeepSeek drops from 66.88% to 57.81% and OLMo-1B from 52.68% to 49.74%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's central finding is that no RAG configuration outperforms the zero-shot baseline for any model: all eight LLMs (OLMo-1B, Llama 3, Mistral, Gemini 1.5, GPT-4o, Claude 3.5, DeepSeek R1, o4-mini) lose accuracy when given retrieved context. GPT-4o is the most robust under misleading retrieval (45.97%, a 31.7% relative drop), while Mistral suffers the steepest decline (26.88%, a 58.0% drop). In Standard RAG, o4-mini is the least affected (only 1.4% drop in RAG-1), whereas DeepSeek drops 17.4% in RAG-5. The robustness-oriented CRAG method (Llama 2-based) also fails, dropping from 50.57% zero-shot to 37.24% under Oracle Retrieval, confirming that existing noise-mitigation strategies do not address naturally occurring misleading evidence. Human annotators, by contrast, maintain or improve accuracy with additional context, highlighting a significant human–machine gap in handling misleading retrievals.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of fact-checking datasets and RAG robustness evaluation. Prior fact-checking benchmarks (FEVER, FEVEROUS, Mocheg, AVeriTeC) assume gold-standard or curated evidence, while noisy-RAG datasets (Power of Noise, RAAT, NoiserBench, QACC) introduce synthetic perturbations or binary conflicts. RAGUARD departs from both by sourcing naturally occurring misleading content from Reddit political discussions and defining 'misleading' behaviorally—relative to LLM prediction outcomes rather than human judgment—enabling scalable annotation that targets model-specific vulnerabilities. The paper also unifies fragmented terminology across prior work into a structured taxonomy of document types (supporting, misleading, unrelated, fabricated, unambiguous).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central finding—that every tested RAG system, including reasoning models and the purpose-built CRAG method, performs worse than its zero-shot baseline when exposed to misleading retrievals—challenges the foundational assumption that retrieval augmentation improves accuracy. This has direct practical implications for deploying RAG in high-stakes domains like fact-checking, legal, and medical applications where real-world retrieval will inevitably include biased or misleading content. Open questions include whether adversarial retrieval training or multi-step reasoning can close the human-LLM gap, whether the political domain generalizes to other fact-checking settings, and how to scale the small human study (4 annotators, 64 instances) into a more rigorous evaluation protocol.
