## Problem

Existing Lorentz-equivariant neural networks for high-energy physics rely on specialized layers (geometric algebra channels, scalar/vector message passing) that restrict architectural design and impose large computational overheads (e.g., L-GATr uses 10× more FLOPs and 4× more training time than LLoCa). No general framework existed to render arbitrary backbone architectures exactly Lorentz-equivariant, and no local canonicalization method had been adapted to the non-compact Lorentz group.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a variable-sized set of particles (typically 1–128 per jet, or 4–6 for amplitude regression), each carrying a four-momentum, discrete particle type, and scalar attributes such as charge and trajectory displacement. For jet tagging, the output is a 10-class label; for amplitude regression, it is a scalar logarithmic QFT amplitude. In both cases the output must transform correctly under Lorentz transformations (equivariant), and the architecture is permutation-equivariant over the particle set. Kinematic features like Δη and log p_T are only SO(2)-invariant, so symmetry breaking is handled explicitly via reference particles and non-invariant scalars fed to the frame-prediction network.

## Method

LLoCa makes any backbone Lorentz-equivariant in three steps: (1) a small equivariant Frames-Net predicts three four-vectors per particle via a softmax-weighted sum of pairwise four-momenta, which are then orthonormalized via polar decomposition (boost from v₀, rotation from v₁,v₂) to yield a local reference frame Lᵢ satisfying LᵢᵀgLᵢ = g; (2) all particle features are expressed in their local frames, becoming Lorentz-invariant and processable by any architecture (transformer, GNN, etc.); (3) outputs are mapped back to the global frame via Lᵢ⁻¹, guaranteeing equivariance. The key expressivity gain comes from tensorial message passing: messages sent from particle j to i are transformed by the invariant matrix LᵢLⱼ⁻¹, enabling exchange of scalar and vector (or higher-rank tensor) features rather than only scalars. For attention, the Minkowski product replaces the Euclidean dot product, which is Lorentz-invariant and allows keys/queries to be evaluated in the global frame, enabling use of optimized FlashAttention-style implementations. Data augmentation emerges as the special case where a single global reference frame is sampled from a distribution of Lorentz transformations, enabling controlled comparison with exact equivariance.

## Results

On the JetClass jet-tagging benchmark (125M jets, 10 classes), LLoCa-Transformer achieves 86.4% accuracy and 0.9882 AUC, matching the SOTA L-GATr (86.6% accuracy) while training 4× faster (31h vs. 166h) and using 10× fewer FLOPs (301M vs. 2060M). On QFT amplitude regression for Z+4g production, LLoCa-Transformer achieves 1.2±0.2 MSE (×10⁻⁵), outperforming L-GATr (1.8±0.2) with 16.3M vs. 528M FLOPs. LLoCa consistently improves non-equivariant backbones: a vanilla transformer jumps from 85.5% to 86.4% accuracy on JetClass, and a GNN improves from 10.5 to 5.0 MSE on amplitude regression.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LLoCa-Transformer (86.4% accuracy, 0.9882 AUC) matches the SOTA L-GATr (86.6%, 0.9885) within 0.2% accuracy while training 4× faster (31h vs. 166h) and using 10× fewer FLOPs (301M vs. 2060M). It outperforms the domain-specific ParT (86.1%, 0.9877) and MIParT-L (86.1%, 0.9878) by 0.3% accuracy. The vanilla transformer baseline (85.5%, 0.9867) is elevated to competitive SOTA performance by LLoCa without any domain-specific architectural changes. On QFT amplitude regression (Z+4g), LLoCa-Transformer achieves 1.2±0.2 MSE, beating L-GATr (1.8±0.2) by 33% while using 1/32nd the FLOPs (16.3M vs. 528M) and training 4× faster (2.1h vs. 8.3h). LLoCa also improves the non-equivariant GNN from 10.5 to 5.0 MSE and the non-equivariant transformer from 8.3 to 1.2 MSE on the same task, demonstrating that the equivariance inductive bias is the primary driver of improvement rather than architectural changes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the local canonicalization framework of Lippmann, Gerhartz et al. [30] and Gerhartz et al. [20] from compact groups (SO(3)) to the non-compact Lorentz group SO⁺(1,3), introducing the first local canonicalization scheme for Lorentz-equivariant deep learning. It departs from prior Lorentz-equivariant architectures (LorentzNet, CGENN, L-GATr, PELICAN) that rely on task-specific specialized layers by instead wrapping any off-the-shelf backbone with equivariantly predicted local reference frames and tensorial message passing. The Minkowski-product attention mechanism and the polar-decomposition-based frame construction are novel adaptations that address the non-compactness of the Lorentz group, which has no direct analogue in the 3D rotation literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LLoCa provides a practical, low-overhead path to exact Lorentz equivariance that is agnostic to backbone architecture, enabling rapid transfer of new deep learning designs (e.g., new transformer variants) into HEP without redesigning equivariant layers. The 4× training speedup and 10× FLOP reduction relative to the prior SOTA (L-GATr) significantly lower the barrier to deploying equivariant models at LHC scale. Open questions include extension to the full Poincaré group and O(1,d), the role of frame averaging for additional symmetries, and whether the data-augmentation-vs-equivariance crossover observed at small dataset sizes persists in other physics tasks. A limitation is that full equivariance requires N≥3 particles, and numerical stability for extremely boosted configurations remains an active concern.
