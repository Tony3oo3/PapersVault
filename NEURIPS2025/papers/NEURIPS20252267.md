## Problem

Existing category-level 6D pose estimation methods are predominantly discriminative, making them ill-suited for multi-hypothesis predictions arising from object symmetries and reliant on specialized pose-sensitive architectures that hinder integration into modern VLA frameworks. The prior generative attempt (GenPose) uses score matching but requires a separate energy-based model for likelihood estimation, breaking end-to-end training, and cannot resolve symmetry-induced pose multiplicity. This paper addresses both gaps by formulating pose estimation as Riemannian Flow Matching on SE(3) with built-in symmetry handling and end-to-end likelihood computation.

## Input / Output

The method takes a partially observed point cloud (subsampled to 1,024 points via Farthest Point Sampling) and a cropped RGB image as dual input streams. RGB features are extracted by a frozen DINOv2 backbone and spatially concatenated with PointNet++ point-cloud features to form cross-modal conditioning. The output is a single deterministic 6D pose (rotation R ∈ SO(3) and translation T ∈ R³), obtained by generating multiple pose candidates via ODE integration and aggregating them with likelihood-weighted averaging on SO(3) and R³ respectively.

## Method

RFMPose learns a velocity field v_θ that transports a prior distribution to the target pose distribution along deterministic ODE trajectories on the SE(3) manifold. Rotations are interpolated via geodesic paths on SO(3) using Lie algebra exp/log maps, while translations follow Euclidean linear interpolation, with a bi-invariant Riemannian metric (Killing form + Euclidean) defining the loss geometry. For symmetric objects, a Riemannian Optimal Transport formulation redistributes probability mass across equivalent ground-truth poses by minimizing weighted geodesic cost, eliminating the need for symmetry-specific architectures. Likelihood of generated candidates is computed end-to-end via Hutchinson trace estimation (Jacobian-vector products with random probes) to approximate the divergence of the velocity field, replacing GenPose's auxiliary energy model. Training is fully supervised: the network minimizes the squared Riemannian distance between predicted and conditional flow velocities, and at inference an ODE solver integrates from t=0 to t=1 to produce pose samples.

## Results

On the Omni6DPose ScanNet++ synthetic test set (149 categories), RFMPose achieves IoU₂₅ = 48.0, IoU₅₀ = 28.9, and 5°2cm AUC = 12.8, surpassing the strongest generative baseline GenPose++ by +4.1 in IoU₂₅ and +2.4 in 5°2cm. On the real-world ROPE set (trained exclusively on synthetic data), the method reaches IoU₂₅ = 42.1 and 5°5cm = 15.7, outperforming all deterministic and probabilistic baselines. For pose tracking, RFMPose attains 5°5cm = 16.1 and mIoU = 54.1 at 11.3 FPS, exceeding GenPose++ (15.9 / 53.4 at 8.7 FPS).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RFMPose outperforms all eight baselines across every reported metric. Against the closest generative competitor GenPose++ [42], it gains +4.1 IoU₂₅ (48.0 vs. 43.9) and +2.4 in 5°2cm AUC (12.8 vs. 10.4) on the synthetic set, and +3.1 IoU₂₅ (42.1 vs. 39.0) on the real-world ROPE set, while also being end-to-end trainable (GenPose++ requires a separate energy-based model). Among deterministic methods, SecondPose [10] is the strongest (IoU₂₅ = 31.5), yet RFMPose still leads by +16.5 IoU₂₅, demonstrating the advantage of probabilistic generative modeling. HS-Pose [45] (31.1) and AG-Pose [20] (29.9) trail further behind. On the real-world ROPE set, NOCS [36] scores 0.0 across all metrics, SGPA [7] reaches only 10.5 IoU₂₅, and IST-Net [25] achieves 28.7 IoU₂₅ — all substantially below RFMPose's 42.1. The earlier GenPose [43] (point-cloud only) reports 6.6 in 5°2cm AUC, far below RFMPose's 12.8, highlighting the benefit of the Riemannian flow matching formulation over score matching.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper transplants the Riemannian Flow Matching paradigm (Chen & Lipman, ICLR 2024) and SE(3) measure decomposition (Bose et al., ICLR 2024) into category-level 6D pose estimation, replacing the score-matching + energy-based-model two-stage pipeline of GenPose (Zhang et al., 2024) with a single end-to-end velocity-field learning objective. The geodesic interpolation on SO(3) via Lie algebra and the bi-invariant metric are drawn directly from the Riemannian flow matching literature, while the Hutchinson trace estimator (Grathwohl et al., ICLR 2019) is repurposed here to compute log-likelihoods without an auxiliary network. The Riemannian Optimal Transport formulation for symmetry-induced multi-hypothesis poses is a novel application of OT on SE(3) that departs from prior symmetry-handling strategies (e.g., SAR-Net, RBP-Net) which relied on dedicated network architectures or custom losses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By unifying pose generation, likelihood scoring, and symmetry resolution within a single differentiable Riemannian framework, RFMPose removes the architectural and training complexity that has limited the adoption of generative pose estimators in robot learning pipelines (e.g., VLA models). The demonstrated sim-to-real transfer without domain adaptation and the minimal-modification extension to pose tracking suggest the framework could serve as a drop-in perception module for embodied AI systems. Key open questions remain: performance on articulated objects (e.g., laptops) is acknowledged as unsatisfactory, and the practical integration into end-to-end VLA architectures is left as future work. The reliance on a frozen DINOv2 backbone and PointNet++ also raises questions about whether the Riemannian flow matching gains would persist with more powerful or task-specific backbones.
