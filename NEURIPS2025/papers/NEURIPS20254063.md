## Problem

Motion simulation, trajectory prediction, and ego planning in autonomous driving are typically addressed by specialized models with task-specific architectures, despite sharing fundamental capabilities in multi-agent interaction modeling and temporal-spatial motion reasoning. This fragmentation prevents cross-task knowledge transfer, limits system scalability, and overlooks the mutual benefits that joint training could provide among these inherently related tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254063/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes 11-frame historical agent states (sampled at 10 Hz) and HD map polylines, both discretized via clustering into 2,048 agent tokens (each spanning 0.5 s) and 1,024 map tokens (each ~0.5 m), compressing the original 91-step trajectories into 18 frames. Outputs are task-dependent: simulation produces K diverse multi-agent rollouts (32 per scenario) via autoregressive token generation; prediction yields 6 multi-modal future trajectories per agent over 80 frames in a single forward pass; and planning generates the ego trajectory token-by-token conditioned on predicted surrounding-agent futures.

## Method

UniMotion is a decoder-only Transformer with relative positional embeddings (invariant to coordinate transforms) and factorized attention—temporal self-attention, agent-map cross-attention, and agent-agent self-attention—that jointly trains on two objectives: Next-Token Prediction (cross-entropy) for diverse motion generation and Long-range Future Regression (smooth-L1) for accurate trajectory forecasting, unified under task-specific attention masks. The key architectural insight is abstracting all three motion tasks into two fundamental components (diverse generation and long-range forecasting), enabling weight sharing and mutual benefit during joint training. Task-specific fine-tuning then specializes the unified model: RL-based fine-tuning with GRPO-style group rollouts and kinematic/collision rewards for simulation, a lightweight multi-modal decoder with intention anchors for prediction, and a Pred2Gen strategy that fine-tunes ego generation on predicted (rather than ground-truth) surrounding-agent tokens to close the train-test distribution gap for planning.

## Results

On the WOMD Prediction leaderboard, UniMotion achieves the best minFDE (1.1643) and mAP (0.4534), tying EDA for best minADE (0.5718), while trailing RMP-YOLO slightly on Soft mAP (0.4642 vs. 0.4673). On the Sim Agents Challenge, UniMotion attains the top Realism Meta Metric (0.7851) and best Kinematic (0.4943) and Map-based (0.9187) scores. On Waymo Planning, UniMotion reduces planning error @5s to 2.246 m (vs. 3.335 m for DIPP) and collision rate to 1.565 (vs. 1.802), though it lags on red-light violations (1.309 vs. 1.235) due to the absence of explicit traffic-rule constraints.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On prediction, UniMotion outperforms all seven baselines on minFDE and mAP, with the closest competitor being RMP-YOLO (minFDE 1.1697, mAP 0.4523) and EDA (minADE 0.5718, Soft mAP 0.4596); UniMotion edges out RMP-YOLO on minADE (0.5718 vs. 0.5737) but trails by 0.0031 on Soft mAP. On simulation, UniMotion surpasses CATK [18] and UniMM [34] on the Realism Meta Metric (0.7851 vs. 0.7846 and 0.7829) and Kinematic score, but UniMM retains a slight minADE advantage (1.2949 vs. 1.3036), reflecting the tokenization-based design's trade-off between diversity and point-accuracy. On planning, UniMotion dominates DIPP [40] on every metric except red-light violations (1.309 vs. 1.235), with particularly large gains at @1s (0.083 vs. 0.227 m) and @5s (2.246 vs. 3.335 m), which the authors attribute to the two-stage predict-then-generate inference and Pred2Gen fine-tuning mitigating distribution mismatch.

## Relation to Prior Work

UniMotion extends the GPT-style motion modeling lineage initiated by BehaviorGPT and SMART, which established tokenized next-token prediction for multi-agent simulation, by unifying simulation, prediction, and planning under a single decoder-only Transformer. It adapts GRPO (from DeepSeekMath) for RL-based simulation fine-tuning and borrows the intention-point-based multi-modal decoder from MTR/EDA for prediction specialization. The key conceptual departure from prior work is the abstraction of all three tasks into two fundamental components—diverse motion generation (NTP) and long-range trajectory forecasting (LFR)—enabling joint training with shared representations rather than task-specific architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified framework offers a practical path toward scalable multi-task motion models, where joint training yields mutual benefits (e.g., NTP's diverse generation directions improve prediction accuracy) and targeted fine-tuning recovers task-specific SOTA. However, the work is limited to a single dataset (WOMD), and the tokenization-based design constrains the model from leveraging broader LLM techniques (e.g., in-context learning, chain-of-thought). Open questions include whether cross-dataset joint training further improves generalization, whether the two-component abstraction generalizes to additional motion tasks (e.g., scene-level forecasting), and how the diversity-accuracy trade-off inherent in tokenization affects downstream closed-loop performance.
