## Problem

Existing visual tokenizers like VQGAN are trained from scratch and optimized for pixel reconstruction, producing latent spaces that are rich in low-level detail but poor in high-level semantics and laden with spatial redundancy. This flawed latent structure prolongs autoregressive model training and necessitates classifier-free guidance (CFG) for high-fidelity class-conditional generation. The paper investigates whether frozen pre-trained vision foundation models (DINOv2, CLIP, SigLIP2) can directly serve as effective tokenizers for AR image generation, and how to further improve their token efficiency.

## Input / Output

The tokenizer consumes 336×336 RGB images and produces 256 discrete region-adaptive tokens (compared to 576 in standard VQGAN), using a codebook of 16384 vectors in 12-dimensional space. For conditional generation, a class label is prepended to the token sequence. The 256 tokens are decoded back into 256×256 images via a VQGAN decoder. Unlike fixed 2D grid tokenizers, the tokens correspond to irregular, semantically coherent regions identified adaptively per image, and the AR model uses 2D RoPE to encode their spatial structure.

## Method

VFMTok uses a frozen DINOv2-L as encoder, extracting multi-level features from layers 6, 12, 18, and 24 to capture both low-level detail and high-level semantics. A set of 256 learnable anchor queries (initialized as a 2D grid) are iteratively refined through deformable cross-attention layers that predict data-dependent sampling offsets at each feature level, producing region-adaptive tokens that aggregate information from semantically coherent irregular regions rather than a fixed grid. After vector quantization, a shared lightweight ViT with causal self-attention performs dual reconstruction: decoding to image pixels via a VQGAN decoder and reconstructing the frozen VFM's final-layer features via cosine similarity loss. The training objective combines standard VQGAN reconstruction loss (with a DINOv1-S discriminator replacing PatchGAN) and the feature reconstruction loss (L = α·L_AE + λ·L_sim). For AR generation, a standard LLaMA-based Transformer predicts the 256-token sequence via next-token prediction, and the rich semantic content in the tokens enables high-fidelity CFG-free synthesis.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256 class-conditional generation, VFMTok-XXL (1.4B params, 256 tokens) achieves a gFID of 2.19 and gIS of 278.0 with CFG, surpassing LlamaGen-XXL (same 1.4B scale) which reports gFID 2.34 and gIS 253.9. When integrated into the RAR framework, RAR-XXL(VFMTok) (1.5B params) achieves a new state-of-the-art gFID of 1.36 and gIS of 301.3, outperforming diffusion models such as DiT (gFID 2.27) and SiT (gFID 2.06). Notably, without classifier-free guidance, VFMTok-XXL still achieves gFID 1.95, whereas LlamaGen-3B degrades to gFID 9.38. For image reconstruction, VFMTok achieves rFID 0.89 and rIS 215.4 using only 256 tokens, outperforming VQGAN (rFID 0.95, 576 tokens) and TiTok (rFID 1.05, 256 tokens).

## Baselines & Comparisons

At the same model scale, VFMTok consistently outperforms LlamaGen: VFMTok-B (111M) achieves gFID 3.43 vs. LlamaGen-B's 6.09, and VFMTok-XXL (1.4B) reaches gFID 2.19 vs. LlamaGen-XXL's 2.34, while also using fewer tokens (256 vs. 576). VFMTok-XXL (1.4B) matches LlamaGen-3B (3.1B) on gFID (2.19 vs. 2.19) but with less than half the parameters and 2.25× fewer tokens. When plugged into the RAR framework, RAR-XXL(VFMTok) (1.5B) improves upon RAR-XXL (1.5B) from gFID 1.48 to 1.36, and surpasses all diffusion baselines (DiT 2.27, SiT 2.06, FasterDiT 2.03) by a wide margin. Without CFG, the advantage is even more pronounced: VFMTok-XXL achieves gFID 1.95 while LlamaGen-3B degrades to 9.38. For reconstruction, VFMTok (256 tokens, rFID 0.89) outperforms VQGAN (576 tokens, rFID 0.95), VAR (680 tokens, rFID 0.92), and TiTok (256 tokens, rFID 1.05), while achieving the highest rIS (215.4) among all compared tokenizers.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the VQGAN/LlamaGen tokenizer-AR pipeline by replacing the learned encoder with a frozen vision foundation model (DINOv2, CLIP, SigLIP2), departing from prior work [61, 63] that used VFM features only to initialize codebooks or as auxiliary branches. The key architectural departure is a region-adaptive quantization module built on deformable cross-attention (from Deformable DETR [62]), which replaces the fixed 2D grid with learnable anchor queries that sample semantically coherent irregular regions, reducing token count from 576 to 256. A novel semantic reconstruction loss aligns the tokenizer's latent tokens with the frozen VFM's own feature outputs, a training objective absent from standard VQGAN training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a 3× convergence speedup for AR models, 4× inference speedup from fewer tokens, and—most notably—high-fidelity class-conditional generation without classifier-free guidance (gFID 1.95 vs. 9.38 for LlamaGen-3B without CFG), which substantially reduces inference cost. The SOTA gFID of 1.36 on ImageNet 256×256 (via RAR+VFMTok) demonstrates that semantic structure in the latent space can replace the guidance mechanism that AR models have relied on. Open questions include whether this approach generalizes to higher resolutions, text-to-image generation, and video, and whether the frozen-VFM-as-encoder strategy remains effective when the VFM is not aligned with the target domain.
