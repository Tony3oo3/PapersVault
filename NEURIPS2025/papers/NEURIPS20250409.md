## Problem

Existing Dempster-Shafer evidence theory methods assume uniform splitting of mass functions, ignoring the a priori importance differences between subsets, and directly applying Dempster's combination rule can amplify conflicts when fusing BBAs from heterogeneous sources. Additionally, data imbalance in practical tasks causes models to under-attend to key regions, and no prior work has applied DSE theory to low-light image enhancement.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250409/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For pattern classification, the method consumes tabular feature vectors or features extracted via ResNet-18/CNN (with the final linear layer removed) from CIFAR-10/100 images, producing class labels. For low-light image enhancement, it takes a low-light image and its normal-light counterpart, where the image is partitioned into blocks and the prior information is the per-region degradation degree; the output is an enhanced image. The TTS module is plug-and-play and can be attached to any existing LLIE backbone without architectural modification.

## Method

The framework comprises three co-optimized modules: (1) Adaptive Diffusion Probability Transformation (ADPT) performs iterative nonuniform splitting of BBAs using a diffusion factor D(Aᵢ,Aⱼ)=(|Aᵢ|/|Aⱼ|)^(1/τ) that preserves the relative importance of subsets with larger initial mass, incorporating data a priori information; (2) Probabilistic Reliability Assessment (PRA) computes a High-Order Dynamic Maximum Mean Difference (HODMMD) in a Gaussian-kernel Hilbert space to quantify the discrepancy between BBAs, which degenerates to standard MMD for probability distributions and to pignistic-probability MMD as τ→∞; (3) Collaborative Decision Optimization (CDO) uses HODMMD to solve for reliability weights via SLSQP, applies them as discount factors, and fuses the discounted BBAs via Dempster's rule. For LLIE, a Targeted Training Strategy multiplies the low-light image by a per-region degradation weight map derived from the DSE framework, forcing the network to allocate more learning capacity to severely degraded regions.

## Results

On pattern classification, the proposed method achieves the best accuracy on 6 out of 7 UCI datasets, with headline results of 98.67% on Iris, 95.97% on Heart, and 99.96% on Segment, outperforming the strongest baseline DMA by up to 11.9% (Heart). On CIFAR-100, the method reaches 79.78% accuracy with ResNet-18, surpassing the best baseline APL (78.90%), while on CIFAR-10 it achieves 95.61%, slightly below APL's 96.00%. For low-light image enhancement, the plug-and-play TTS module consistently improves PSNR across all tested networks, e.g., boosting Retinexformer from 24.18 to 25.16 dB on LOL-v1 and LLFlow-S from 21.84 to 24.06 dB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On pattern classification, the proposed method outperforms the strongest evidence-theory baseline DMA [58] on 6 of 7 datasets, with the largest margin of +11.9% on Heart (95.97% vs. 84.07%) and +2.67% on Iris (98.67% vs. 96.00%); it loses only on Parkinsons to kNN [12] (82.50% vs. 83.02%), which the authors attribute to near-identical feature distributions. On CIFAR-100, the method (79.78% with ResNet-18) surpasses APL [70] (78.90%) and all other baselines, but on CIFAR-10 it trails APL by 0.39% (95.61% vs. 96.00%). For low-light image enhancement, the TTS module consistently improves PSNR over every baseline network it is attached to: +0.98 dB for Retinexformer [3], +2.22 dB for LLFlow-S [52], and +1.71 dB for LLFlow-L [52] on LOL-v1, while SSIM gains are smaller and occasionally slightly negative (e.g., LLFlow-L SSIM drops by 0.01). The ablation study confirms that removing ADPT costs 1.05% accuracy and removing HODMMD costs 2.06% on CIFAR-10, validating both modules' contributions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Dempster-Shafer evidence theory framework by replacing the uniform splitting assumption in prior BBA allocation methods (Huang et al., 2023) with a nonuniform splitting mechanism (ADPT) that incorporates data a priori information through a diffusion factor. For measuring differences between BBAs before fusion, it departs from Euclidean-space or simple statistical metrics used in prior evidence-theoretic classifiers (Tong et al., 2021; Xiao et al., 2022) by introducing HODMMD, which operates in a Gaussian-kernel Hilbert space. The discounting-based fusion pipeline follows Shafer's classical framework, and the Targeted Training Strategy for LLIE represents the first application of DSE theory to that domain, building on standard LLIE backbones (Retinexformer, LLFlow, SNR) as plug-and-play baselines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The first introduction of DSE theory into low-light image enhancement opens a new direction for uncertainty-aware enhancement, though the PSNR/SSIM gains over strong baselines are modest (typically 0.1–2.2 dB). The nonuniform splitting mechanism and Hilbert-space BBA comparison address genuine limitations in existing evidence-theoretic classifiers, but the paper reports no error bars or statistical significance tests, and the method underperforms on the Parkinsons dataset, suggesting sensitivity to feature separability. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger-scale classification (e.g., ImageNet) or more complex enhancement tasks, and whether the TTS module's added computational overhead (~25–30% FLOPs increase) is justified in deployment settings.
