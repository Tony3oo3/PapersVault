## Problem

Self-speculative decoding methods are training-free but achieve limited speedups, while cascade speculative decoding offers greater acceleration but requires training and maintaining multiple separate draft models. The paper asks whether training-free self-speculative strategies can be composed into an effective cascade hierarchy without any additional model training, and whether adaptive scheduling can further unlock their potential.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252182/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single target LLM (e.g., Vicuna-7B/13B/33B, Llama-2-7B) and a text prompt, producing generated text tokens that are bit-identical to standard autoregressive decoding (lossless guarantee). Draft models are constructed on-the-fly by applying dynamically switchable inference acceleration (DSIA) strategies—such as skipping every other transformer layer or applying activation quantization—to the target model's own inference path, with Prompt Lookup Decoding (PLD) serving as the fastest bottom-level draft. The draft tokens are organized in a tree structure (bounded by M_tree_max) rather than a single linear sequence, enabling parallel verification of multiple candidate paths.

## Method

CAS-Spec constructs a multi-level draft model hierarchy from the target model itself using Dynamically Switchable Inference Acceleration (DSIA) strategies (layer sparsity, early exiting, activation quantization/sparsity), eliminating the need to train separate draft models. The key scheduling component is Dynamic Tree Cascade (DyTC), which at each decoding step selects the best draft model configuration and draft length by maximizing an objective function (Eq. 5) that balances the predicted local speedup against the estimated least future speedup from the bottom draft model—an admissible-heuristic-inspired correction to avoid the suboptimality of pure greedy search. DyTC maintains online acceptance rate estimates via exponential moving average (H=20, λ=0.7) and hardware-aware latency predictions via Bayesian linear regression, and uses tree-based parallel generation to draft tokens for multiple sibling leaf nodes simultaneously. The algorithm adaptively stops expansion when the accumulated acceptance rate along a path falls below a threshold, and the entire pipeline is training-free and lossless.

## Results

On Spec-Bench, CAS-Spec (training-free, using layer sparsity + PLD) achieves overall speedups of 1.578×, 1.524×, and 1.481× over autoregressive decoding on Vicuna-7B, 13B, and 33B respectively, with per-task speedups ranging from 1.10× to 2.27×. When augmented with Kangaroo's early-exiting adapter (CAS-Spec†), overall speedups reach 1.696× (7B) and 1.673× (13B). The DyTC scheduling algorithm improves average speedup by 47% over the CS-Drafting cascade baseline and 48% over the SWIFT tree-based baseline on Vicuna-7B.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CAS-Spec consistently outperforms all training-free baselines across all model sizes and tasks. Against PLD (the strongest single training-free method), CAS-Spec gains +0.039 overall on 7B (1.578 vs 1.539), +0.066 on 13B (1.524 vs 1.458), and +0.096 on 33B (1.481 vs 1.385), with the gap widening for larger models. Against SWIFT (layer sparsity alone), CAS-Spec achieves roughly 1.5× the speedup (e.g., 1.578 vs 1.064 on 7B), confirming that cascading multiple DSIA strategies is far more effective than a single strategy. Against Lookahead decoding, CAS-Spec wins by a wide margin (1.578 vs 1.274 on 7B). Against the trained Kangaroo method, plain CAS-Spec is competitive (1.578 vs 1.534 on 7B), while CAS-Spec† (incorporating Kangaroo's adapter) surpasses it (1.696 vs 1.534). Against trained methods like EAGLE-2 (4.36× on Vicuna-7B), CAS-Spec remains substantially behind, reflecting the inherent gap between training-free and trained draft models. The DyTC algorithm specifically improves average speedup by 47% over CS-Drafting's static VC+HC cascade and 48% over SWIFT's static tree algorithm, validating the benefit of dynamic routing.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the cascade speculative decoding framework of CS-Drafting (Chen et al., NeurIPS 2024) by replacing the requirement for multiple separately trained draft models with Dynamically Switchable Inference Acceleration (DSIA) strategies—layer sparsity, activation quantization, early exiting—applied to the target model itself, creating 'virtual' draft models on the fly. It also builds on the tree-based speculative decoding paradigm from SpecInfer and EAGLE-2, but introduces the Dynamic Tree Cascade (DyTC) algorithm that adaptively routes through the draft hierarchy and assigns draft lengths using online acceptance-rate and latency heuristics, going beyond the static vertical/horizontal cascade scheduling of prior work. The positioning is explicitly against training-free self-speculative methods (SWIFT, Lookahead) and retrieval-based drafting (PLD), which the authors show individually fall short of the cascade potential.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CAS-Spec removes the primary practical barrier to cascade speculative decoding—the need to train and maintain multiple draft models—making multi-stage acceleration accessible to any LLM without additional training infrastructure. The 47–48% speedup from DyTC over static cascade scheduling demonstrates that adaptive, hardware-aware routing is a meaningful and underexploited dimension. Key limitations include sensitivity to batch size (dynamic scheduling gains diminish at large batch), a warm-up period for acceptance-rate estimation, and incompatibility with hidden-state-dependent methods like EAGLE-3. Open questions include whether the framework scales to larger models (tested up to 33B), how it interacts with emerging training-free SSD techniques, and whether hardware co-design can further reduce DSIA switching overhead.
