## Problem

In Partially Relevant Video Retrieval, standard contrastive training treats all text queries paired with the same video as positives and all others as negatives, causing semantically unrelated queries and video segments from the same video to collapse together while semantically similar ones from different videos are pushed apart. Prior work (GMMFormer, GMMFormer-v2) only enforced uniform separation among same-video queries without preserving their true semantic relationships, and the analogous collapse in video embeddings remained unaddressed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250783/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural-language text query and untrimmed videos (e.g., 128 frames per video) as input, producing a ranked list of videos with text-video similarity scores. Text is encoded via CLIP into a single pooled query token, while each video is encoded through a dual-branch transformer into two parallel token sequences: one per frame and one per clip (32 clips by default). Retrieval selects the single most similar video token per branch and fuses frame and clip scores (0.6/0.4 weighting) to rank the database.

## Method

The paper introduces two complementary losses to counter semantic collapse. Text Correlation Preservation Learning (TCPL) distills pairwise Euclidean distances and triplet angular distances from CLIP's [EOS] tokens into the retrieval embedding space via Huber loss, preserving the foundation model's semantic geometry among queries rather than merely separating same-video queries. Cross-Branch Video Alignment (CBVA) applies a bidirectional contrastive objective between frame-level and clip-level tokens at matching timestamps, pulling temporally aligned representations together and repelling misaligned ones to disentangle distinct events within a video. To make CBVA effective, Order-Preserving Token Merging (OP-ToMe) constructs clips by merging only adjacent-frame tokens (preserving playback order), and an adaptive variant uses bipartite token merging to reduce the clip count per video based on its internal similarity structure, avoiding unnecessary fragmentation of homogeneous content.

## Results

On QVHighlights, the method achieves SumR of 234.6, outperforming the previous best (ProtoPRVR, 226.6) by 8.0 points and GMMFormer-v2 (225.2) by 9.4 points. On TVR, ActivityNet Captions, and Charades-STA, the method achieves SumR of 263.1, 202.1, and 99.7 respectively, setting new state-of-the-art on all three benchmarks. The largest relative gains appear on QVHighlights, where videos contain the most diverse and rapidly transitioning events, making semantic collapse most severe.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method achieves state-of-the-art SumR on all four benchmarks, beating every compared baseline. On QVHighlights (Table 4), it surpasses ProtoPRVR (226.6) by 8.0 points and GMMFormer-v2 (225.2) by 9.4 points in SumR, with the largest gap over MS-SL (222.5) at 12.1 points. On TVR, it leads ARL (260.1) by 3.0 points and GMMFormer-v2 (258.1) by 5.0 points; on ActivityNet Captions, it exceeds GMMFormer-v2 (196.9) by 5.2 points and BGM-Net (190.3) by 11.8 points; on Charades-STA, it outperforms BGM-Net (96.7) by 3.0 points and GMMFormer-v2 (94.2) by 5.5 points. The gains are most pronounced on QVHighlights, which the authors attribute to its high diversity of events per video making it most susceptible to semantic collapse. Inference efficiency is comparable to GMMFormer-v2 (2.70 ms vs. 2.78 ms at 474 videos), while training time is higher (62.6 s/epoch vs. 17.2 s/epoch) due to the additional CBVA and TCPL objectives.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the PRVR line initiated by MS-SL's dual-branch (frame/clip) architecture and refined by GMMFormer/GMMFormer-v2, which introduced the concept of semantic collapse in text embeddings and proposed query-diverse losses to push apart same-video queries. The key departure is twofold: (1) it replaces the blind separation with relational knowledge distillation from CLIP's structured text space (TCPL), preserving actual semantic relationships rather than merely enforcing distance, and (2) it identifies and addresses the previously unexplored semantic collapse in video embeddings via Cross-Branch Video Alignment, leveraging the dual-branch structure and token merging (OP-ToMe, bipartite merging from ToMe) to construct context-coherent clips. The method is architecturally a drop-in addition to the GMMFormer-v2 baseline, requiring no new inference modules.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By diagnosing semantic collapse as a dual-modality problem and providing targeted fixes for each, the paper offers a principled recipe that any PRVR pipeline can adopt at training time without altering inference cost. The 8-point SumR gain on QVHighlights and consistent SOTA across four benchmarks demonstrate that preserving fine-grained semantic structure (rather than just enforcing separation) is critical when videos contain multiple diverse events. Limitations include inheriting CLIP's weaknesses on fine-grained spatial/directional queries and a ~4× increase in training time; open questions include whether the approach transfers to video-native foundation models (e.g., VideoCLIP) that could enable video-side relational distillation analogous to TCPL.
