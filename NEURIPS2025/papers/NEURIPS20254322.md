## Problem

Existing spectral exploration methods (eigen-options, cover options) push agents toward geometrically distant regions of the state space, but those regions may already be heavily visited, making them poor exploration targets. Count-based methods identify novelty but lack explicit gradient guidance, relying on slow value propagation to direct the agent. Moreover, prior Laplacian-based approaches are restricted to undirected transition graphs, excluding the many real-world environments that are inherently directed.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an incrementally built directed transition graph (with row-stochastic transition matrix P) and per-state visitation counts accumulated over H-step rollouts. It produces a library of up to 64 temporally-extended options, each defined by an initiation set (all known states), a hill-climbing policy that selects the neighbor maximizing the eigenvector magnitude, and a termination condition triggered at local maxima of that magnitude. Options are regenerated every H = 5×|V| decision stages, with the oldest four discarded when capacity is exceeded.

## Method

NEO constructs a source Laplacian L_ζ = I + Γ − P by adding a diagonal matrix Γ of scaled visitation counts (via F(N(s)) = δ·(N(s)/max N)^{1/k}) to the random-walk Laplacian, which pulls eigenvector values at well-visited (sink) nodes toward zero and concentrates energy at novel (source) nodes. The paper proves three key theorems: eigenvalue bounds for L_ζ, maximality of eigenvector magnitude at source nodes for eigenvalues below the minimum sink weight, and guaranteed directed gradient paths from high-visitation to low-visitation states for any eigenvalue with real part below the maximum visitation value—even in directed graphs where complex eigenvalues may arise. The Z smoothest eigenvectors (smallest real eigenvalues) are used to instantiate Z options whose orthogonality ensures distinct novel regions are targeted simultaneously. In directed settings, the sink weights stabilize the spectrum, making eigenvalues predominantly real in practice, and the method avoids the symmetric Hermitian restriction of prior polar-decomposition approaches.

## Results

In large undirected domains (office, hex, large maze), NEO agents achieve the maximum sparse reward of 100 on average by the end of training, while all competing methods (eigen-options, cover options, shortest-path-novelty, count-based Q-learning) fail to exceed a reward of 40. In terms of exploration coverage, NEO approaches full unique-node coverage in hex and large maze, achieving roughly twice the unique-node count of the best competing approach. In directed domains (directed four rooms, directed hex, directed large maze, NYC, torus), both NEO variants consistently outperform all alternatives, and no complex eigenvalues were encountered in practice across all 4 smoothest eigenvalues computed at every update.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NEO outperforms all five baselines across the full suite of undirected and directed domains. Against eigen-options [1,2] and cover options [3], NEO achieves substantially higher sparse reward in large undirected domains (100 vs. <40) and roughly 2× the unique-node coverage in hex and large maze; the paper attributes this gap to the Fiedler vector's tendency to place one boundary in a heavily visited region, causing the signed option pair to undo exploration progress (e.g., lifting the agent from mean visitation 68.5 to 822.6 in hex). Against shortest-path-novelty [8], NEO learns faster, with the shortest-path method exhibiting notably slower convergence on Tower of Hanoi. Against the count-based Q-learning baseline (intrinsic bonus β/√n(s,a)), NEO's explicit gradient-guided options propagate exploration signals without the lag inherent to value-function propagation. In directed domains, the polar-decomposition eigen-options baseline [9] is outperformed by NEO, and on the 10,000-node NYC street graph the polar-decomposition solver crashed entirely, whereas NEO completed all runs successfully.

## Relation to Prior Work

NEO extends the spectral option-discovery lineage (eigen-options, cover options) by replacing the standard random-walk Laplacian with a 'source Laplacian' whose diagonal is weighted by scaled visitation counts, thereby converting novelty into energy that concentrates on under-explored states. Unlike prior Laplacian-based methods that assume undirected graphs and push agents toward geometrically distant (but potentially well-visited) regions, NEO provides explicit gradient guarantees toward novel states and extends the construction to strongly connected directed graphs via a Schur-complement analysis of the non-Hermitian source Laplacian. The paper also identifies a systematic failure mode of the Fiedler vector used by cover options: one boundary tends to anchor in the most-visited region, causing the signed option pair to undo exploration progress.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The source Laplacian framework is general—any real-valued state function (reward, prediction error, competence) can replace visitation counts—making it a flexible template for novelty-directed temporal abstraction. The directed-graph extension is practically important since many real-world transition structures are inherently asymmetric, yet prior spectral methods were inapplicable. Key open questions include extending eigenvector computation to continuous or high-dimensional state spaces (currently limited to discrete graphs up to ~10K nodes), learning exploration policies that generalize across environments, and understanding when the sink-weighting mechanism guarantees real eigenvalues in practice beyond the empirically observed cases.
