## Problem

Two questions about DDPMs remain open: (i) what is the effect of additive noise in each score evaluation (e.g., from communication, compression, or stochastic rounding) on the quality of generated samples, and (ii) what is the optimal number of score queries K needed to achieve Wasserstein-2 error ε? Prior bounds achieved rates as poor as D⁴/ε² in Wasserstein distance, while the Gaussian case suggests √D/ε is achievable, but this had not been proven for broader distribution classes.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes a standard Gaussian vector ξ₀ ∈ R^D, a sequence of K+1 time steps, and K+1 queries to a randomized score estimator  s̃(t,x) that may return a noisy, compressed, or weight-perturbed version of the true score at each call. It outputs a synthetic sample Z_{K+1} ∈ R^D whose distribution P^DDPM approximates the target P*. The theoretical contribution is a Wasserstein-2 upper bound that decomposes into three terms: a discretization error linear in the maximum step size h_max, a score-bias term linear in ε_score^b, and a score-variance term scaling as √(h_max)·ε_score^v that vanishes as K grows.

## Method

The core technique is a recursive analysis of the squared Wasserstein distance between the continuous-time backward diffusion (driven by the true score) and the discrete-time DDPM (driven by the noisy score), coupled on a common probability space via shared Brownian increments. The key insight is that recursing on W₂² rather than W₂ yields significantly tighter triangle-inequality control, producing a discretization error linear in h_max (optimal, matching the Gaussian lower bound) instead of the h_max^{1/2} or h_max^{1/4} rates in prior work. A two-regime time discretization (arithmetic grid followed by geometric grid) handles the singularity of the score near t=0. Conditions on the target P* are expressed through a conditional-variance bound (Assumption 1), which covers strongly log-concave, semi-log-concave with compact support, and their convolutions, including distributions supported on low-dimensional subspaces. The variance of the noisy score enters only through a √(h_max)·ε_score^v term, explaining the empirical robustness to constant-level score noise.

## Results

The paper establishes that DDPMs achieve a Wasserstein-2 error rate of O(√D/ε) with K = O(√D/ε) score queries, matching the optimal rate known for Gaussian targets and improving upon all prior non-Gaussian bounds. The discretization error is linear in the step size h_max, compared to h_max^{1/12} in [CCL+23], h_max^{1/4} in [CLL23], and h_max^{1/2} in [BZL+23, GNZ25, YY25, SOB+25]. Empirically, on CIFAR-10 and CelebA-HQ (8192 images each), FID remains nearly unchanged under constant-variance score noise up to σ ≈ 2 (CIFAR-10) and σ ≈ 1 (CelebA-HQ), with the shape of the noise distribution (Gaussian, Uniform, Laplace, Student-t) having negligible impact—only the scale matters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper's theoretical bound of √D/ε strictly improves upon all prior Wasserstein-distance results: it halves the dimension dependence compared to [BZL+23, GNZ25, YY25, SOB+25] (D/ε²) and [SO25] (√D/ε²), and dramatically improves upon [CLL23] (D⁴/ε²) and [CCL+23] (D⁵/ε¹²). The discretization error is linear in h_max, which is provably optimal per the Gaussian lower bound in [GNZ25], whereas prior works achieved only h_max^{1/2} or worse. The noise-variance term in the bound scales as σ_ζ√(DT₁/K) and vanishes as K grows, unlike all prior bounds which contain a constant-order noise term independent of K. Empirically, the paper does not benchmark against competing generative models but rather demonstrates that the standard DDPM pipeline is robust to injected score noise, with FID degrading only mildly up to moderate noise levels before a sharp threshold effect appears.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the recursive analysis framework from Langevin Monte Carlo (Dalalyan & Karagulyan, 2019) to the DDPM setting, applying it to the *squared* Wasserstein distance rather than the distance itself, which yields tighter recursive bounds and recovers the optimal √D/ε rate. It departs from prior Wasserstein analyses (Chen et al. 2023, Bruno et al. 2023, Gao et al. 2025) that achieved at best D/ε² by exploiting the smoothness structure of the Gaussian-smoothed score more fully. A key conceptual departure is the treatment of *randomized* (noisy) score estimators, where prior bounds either ignored the noise or produced a constant-order error term that does not vanish with more queries; here the noise contribution scales as √(DT₁/K)·ε_score^v and disappears asymptotically.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper closes the optimality gap for DDPM convergence in W₂ distance, matching the Gaussian lower bound of √D/ε for a broad class of targets including semi-log-concave measures on low-dimensional subspaces. The robustness-to-noise result has direct practical implications for compressed inference, remote score evaluation, and stochastic rounding in deployment. Open questions remain: whether the exponential dependence on support diameter (e^{2bM}) is intrinsic or an artifact of the proof, whether kinetic Langevin variants can improve the discretization error, and whether Hessian-based corrections can further accelerate convergence. The uniform-in-x assumption on the score error (Assumption 2) is stronger than what prior work requires and may be relaxable.
