## Problem

Contrastive learning for multimodal embedding models relies heavily on large batches of high-quality in-batch negatives, but explicit hard negative mining across the full dataset becomes computationally prohibitive in multimodal settings where positives include high-resolution images. Existing approaches either omit hard negatives entirely or add individually mined negatives to randomly sampled batches, leaving the strong negative signals residing outside the immediate batch largely unexploited. The paper asks whether one can construct batches from the full dataset such that examples within each batch are inherently strong negatives for one another, eliminating the need for separately mined hard negatives.

## Input / Output

The method operates on (query, positive) pairs where both can be images, text, or image-text pairs across 20 MMEB training datasets spanning retrieval, classification, VQA, and grounding. Images are encoded at a maximum resolution of 1000 (yielding ~1200 tokens for Qwen2-VL) or 3k pixels (for InternVL3), and the model produces a single multimodal embedding vector from the last hidden state of the last token. The batch mining itself is an offline preprocessing step: a teacher model scores all N×N query-positive pairs to produce a rank matrix, from which pre-mined batches are stored on disk and simply sampled during training.

## Method

B3 constructs training batches via graph-based community detection over teacher-model rankings. A pretrained teacher embedding model ranks all positives relative to each query; after excluding the top-p ranks (false-negative filtering), the ranks in [p, p+m] define a sparse directed graph where edges denote mutual preference as strong in-batch negatives. Retaining only bidirectional edges yields an undirected graph on which METIS community detection identifies clusters of fixed size K, each containing mutually strong negatives. Batches of size |B| are then assembled by sampling |B|/K such clusters, ensuring every example in a batch has multiple strong negatives co-located with it. The approach is theoretically grounded by upper-bounding the gap between global and batch-level InfoNCE loss, showing that maximizing within-cluster edge density (i.e., H_{B_i,i}^K) tightens this bound. An optional B3++ variant adds h hard negatives sampled from a unified preference distribution aggregated over all queries in the batch, and instruction prompts are applied to positive targets to decouple stylistically diverse tasks during training.

## Results

On the MMEB benchmark (36 tasks), B3++ (Qwen2-2B) achieves 68.1 average accuracy, surpassing the previous best 2B model LLaVE (65.2) by +2.9 points. At 7B scale, B3++ (Qwen2-7B) reaches 72.0 average, outperforming the prior best UniME (70.7) by +1.3 points. Notably, B3 with a batch size as small as 64 already exceeds the 2B state-of-the-art, and B3 beats a random-batch baseline augmented with 5 hard negatives (67.14) by 0.7 points while using only half the training compute.

## Baselines & Comparisons

At 2B scale, B3++ (Qwen2-2B) achieves 68.1 average, beating LLaVE (65.2) by +2.9, UniME (64.2) by +3.9, and its own teacher VLM2Vec (59.3) by +8.8. At 7B scale, B3++ (Qwen2-7B) reaches 72.0, outperforming UniME (70.7) by +1.3, LLaVE (70.3) by +1.7, mmE5 (69.8) by +2.2, and VLM2Vec (65.8) by +6.2. The B3 variant (without hard negatives) using InternVL3-7B backbone achieves 71.8, already surpassing all prior 7B baselines. On image caption retrieval (Table 3), B3++ (Qwen2-7B) achieves the best overall scores on Flickr (85.5/95.9), COCO (62.8/77.6), and Urban1k (98.1/98.0), outperforming EVA-CLIP (8B) and UniME (7B). For text-only generalizability, B3 trained on SNLI data improves over SimCSE by +0.76 average on STS benchmarks (81.62 vs 80.86) and over SimCSE+HN by +0.67 (84.24 vs 83.57). B3 also consistently outperforms the GritLM-based batch mining (GBM) baseline across all tested batch sizes (Figure 3).

## Relation to Prior Work

B3 departs from the dominant paradigm in contrastive learning where hard negatives are individually mined from teacher rankings and appended to IID-sampled batches (as in NV-Retriever, SFR-Embedding, NV-Embed). Instead, it repurposes the same teacher-ranking signal to build a sparse preference graph and applies METIS community detection to form batches whose members are mutually strong in-batch negatives, eliminating the need for separately mined hard negatives. This connects to a smaller line of batch-mining work (NGAME, GCBS, BatchSampler, Morris & Rush) but is the first to combine rank-thresholded false-negative filtering with graph community detection in the multimodal embedding setting, and it provides a theoretical bound (Theorem 1) linking batch composition to the gap between global and batch-level InfoNCE loss.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: B3 matches or exceeds state-of-the-art multimodal embedding performance at batch sizes 4–16× smaller than prior methods, and it outperforms random batches augmented with five hard negatives while using half the compute. This lowers the hardware barrier for training competitive embedding models and makes the approach accessible in resource-constrained settings. The finding that a weak CLIP teacher yields nearly identical results to a same-scale VLM2Vec teacher suggests the method is robust and not bottlenecked by teacher quality. Open questions include whether the offline O(n log n) ranking cost remains tractable at much larger dataset scales, how the method interacts with the growing trend of synthetic data generation for multimodal embeddings, and whether the community-detection step can be made adaptive during training rather than fixed at initialization.
