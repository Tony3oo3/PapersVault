## Problem

Few-Shot Class-Incremental Learning (FSCIL) requires models to learn new classes from only a handful of examples while retaining prior knowledge, but current methods are constrained by data scarcity and the stability-plasticity dilemma. While diffusion models offer a path for data augmentation, their direct application in FSCIL suffers from semantic misalignment (generated images deviate from class semantics or lack diversity) and inefficient feedback (the generator is blind to the classifier's current weaknesses and confusion patterns). This paper addresses both gaps by establishing a closed-loop, reward-aligned co-evolution between the diffusion model and the classifier.

## Input / Output

The diffusion model (Stable Diffusion 3.5 Medium) takes a class-name text prompt (e.g., 'a photo of {class name}') and produces 512×512 RGB images, which are resized to the dataset's native resolution before being fed into a frozen ResNet-12/18 encoder. Only a small number of images are generated per class per session (~30 for new classes, ~10 for old classes), making the approach practical under FSCIL resource constraints. The classifier outputs class labels via Nearest Class Mean (NCM) using cosine similarity between image features and class prototypes, and its intermediate features and logits serve as the reward signal that steers subsequent image generation.

## Method

DCS establishes a mutual boosting loop where a classifier-derived, multi-faceted reward function guides the diffusion model via the training-free DAS (Diffusion Alignment as Sampling) algorithm, which uses Sequential Monte Carlo to sample from a reward-aligned target distribution without retraining the diffusion model. The reward operates at two levels: at the feature level, a Prototype-Anchored MMD reward (R_PAMMD) enforces semantic coherence and intra-class diversity, while a Dimension-Wise Variance Matching reward (R_VM) aligns per-dimension feature spread with limited real data; at the logits level, a Recalibrated Confidence reward (R_RC) uses adaptive temperature scaling to encourage exploratory samples, and a Cross-Session Confusion-Aware reward (R_CSCA) dynamically weights classes by their confusion severity to generate hard samples targeting the classifier's weakest inter-class boundaries. Generated images fine-tune the classifier, which in turn updates its prototypes and logits to produce more informative reward signals, creating a co-evolutionary cycle. Notably, DCS requires no architectural modifications to the baseline classification network—all gains come from the quality and strategic targeting of the generated training data.

## Results

On miniImageNet (8-way 5-shot, 9 sessions), DCS achieves 68.14% average accuracy, surpassing the previous best method SAVC (67.05%) by 1.09 points. On CUB-200 (10-way 5-shot, 11 sessions), DCS reaches 69.73% average accuracy, outperforming SAVC (69.35%) and ADBS (67.12%). On CIFAR-100 (8-way 5-shot, 9 sessions), DCS attains 66.36% average accuracy, slightly trailing ALFSCIL (66.75%) but exceeding all other baselines by a wide margin (next best: ADBS at 63.77%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DCS outperforms all nine baselines on miniImageNet, achieving 68.14% average accuracy versus SAVC's 67.05% (the strongest prior method) and OrCo's 66.90%, with consistent per-session gains of 1–3 points across all nine sessions. On CUB-200, DCS leads with 69.73% average accuracy, edging out SAVC (69.35%) and substantially surpassing ADBS (67.12%) and ALFSCIL (67.00%). On CIFAR-100, DCS (66.36%) narrowly trails ALFSCIL (66.75%) by 0.39 points but exceeds the next-best ADBS (63.77%) by 2.59 points. Notably, DCS achieves these gains without modifying the baseline classification network architecture, relying solely on reward-aligned diffusion-generated data, whereas competing methods employ additional self-supervised learning, distribution calibration, or network expansion techniques. The largest margins over weaker baselines (TOPIC, CEC, FACT) exceed 20 points in average accuracy, underscoring the benefit of diffusion-based data augmentation over purely network-centric approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Diffusion Alignment as Sampling (DAS) framework [21], which provides a training-free SMC-based mechanism for reward-aligned diffusion sampling, but replaces DAS's generic image-quality rewards (HPSv2, TCE) with a multi-faceted, classifier-derived reward system tailored to FSCIL. It departs from prior diffusion-based data augmentation for classification [4, 35, 28] that treats the diffusion model as a 'blind teacher' unaware of the student classifier's state, and from existing FSCIL methods (TOPIC, CEC, FACT, SAVC, etc.) that rely solely on network architecture or feature-space manipulation without generative augmentation. The key conceptual departure is the mutual boosting loop: the classifier's evolving state produces reward signals that steer generation, and the generated images in turn refine the classifier, creating a co-evolutionary process absent in prior unidirectional augmentation pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a small number of strategically generated images (<50 per class) guided by classifier feedback can outperform both vanilla diffusion augmentation at the same scale and prior SOTA FSCIL methods that employ additional self-supervised or distribution-calibration tricks, suggesting that reward alignment is more important than generation volume in data-scarce settings. However, the approach's practical adoption is constrained by its dependence on Stable Diffusion 3.5 Medium (a large, compute-intensive model) and the per-session image generation cost, which may be prohibitive in truly resource-limited FSCIL deployments. Open questions include whether the mutual boosting loop generalizes to video or 3D modalities, whether smaller diffusion models (e.g., SDXL-Turbo) can serve as adequate generators, and how the method scales beyond 10 incremental sessions where confusion accumulates across many old classes.
