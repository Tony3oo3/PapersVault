## Problem

Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) suffer from 'overthinking,' generating excessively long reasoning chains filled with redundant self-verification steps that dramatically inflate inference costs. Existing CoT compression methods rely on synthesizing concise target reasoning chains (via model merging, truncation, or extensive sampling) and then fine-tuning with SFT or RL, making them computationally expensive and prone to accuracy degradation on complex problems. The paper asks whether effective CoT compression is possible without any synthetic target chains.

## Input / Output

During training (SVFT), the model receives a question paired with a short-CoT solution (generated by small non-reasoning models like Qwen-2.5-7B-Instruct) and must output a fixed binary judgment string indicating whether the solution is entirely correct or contains mistakes; only the response tokens contribute to the loss. During inference, the model takes a mathematical problem and produces a compressed reasoning chain with a final answer. In the speculative reasoning extension, a short-CoT LLM first drafts a solution, and the SVFT-enhanced LRM either accepts it directly or triggers full long-CoT reasoning, with the overall output being the verified final answer.

## Method

The core contribution is Supervised Verification Fine-Tuning (SVFT), which fine-tunes the LRM exclusively on an auxiliary binary CoT-verification task rather than on the reasoning task itself. The key insight is that overthinking arises because the LRM's hidden state fails to accurately encode whether prior reasoning steps are correct, causing it to trigger unnecessary self-reflection; by explicitly training the model to classify solution correctness, SVFT increases the probability p(acc|h) of correct classification, thereby suppressing redundant verification of correct steps while preserving (or slightly increasing) verification of incorrect ones. Training uses LoRA with loss computed only on the fixed verification response tokens, effectively performing contrastive learning between correct and incorrect solutions without requiring the model to learn explicit correctness semantics. This mechanism is validated empirically: the SVFT model shows a 41% token reduction on correctly answered questions but only 8% on incorrectly answered ones, confirming adaptive rather than uniform compression. The verification capability further enables Solution-wise Speculative Reasoning, where a short-CoT LLM drafts solutions and the LRM verifies them, achieving 3–7× throughput gains with minimal accuracy loss.

## Results

On the average across MATH500, AIME2024, and AIME2025, VeriThinker reduces reasoning tokens by 30–34% while improving accuracy: R1-Distill-Qwen-7B goes from 10,407 tokens / 62.3% to 7,264 tokens / 64.0% (+1.7%), R1-Distill-Qwen-14B from 9,554 / 71.2% to 6,327 / 74.3% (+3.1%), and R1-Distill-Llama-8B from 10,928 / 55.1% to 8,265 / 55.5% (+0.4%). On the hardest benchmark (AIME2025), the 7B model drops from 14,321 to 10,287 tokens while gaining 2.1% accuracy (38.7%→40.8%). In speculative reasoning, using Qwen-2.5-Math-7B as draft model, the 7B LRM activates long-CoT for only 14.6% of MATH500 problems, yielding 4.2× throughput with 93.4% accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CoT-Valve [41], VeriThinker achieves comparable or better token reduction (e.g., 7,264 vs. 8,187 average tokens on the 7B model) while substantially outperforming on accuracy: +10.0% on the 7B average (64.0% vs. 54.0%) and +21.7% on the 14B average (74.3% vs. 52.6%), with the gap widening on harder AIME problems. Against the SFT baseline [58], which uses synthesized concise chains as targets, VeriThinker achieves similar token compression (7,264 vs. 6,864 on 7B) but avoids the severe accuracy degradation SFT suffers (64.0% vs. 57.5% on 7B; 74.3% vs. 64.8% on 14B), because SFT uniformly suppresses self-reflection even for genuinely incorrect steps. Against Qwen-2.5-7B-Instruct [66] as a verification-accuracy baseline (Appendix D), VeriThinker-7B achieves 90.4% accuracy and 0.972 precision versus Qwen's 74.8% and 0.714, and also surpasses GPT-4o (85.0%) and GPT-4.1 (86.4%) on the same task. Truncation and Fast-Prompting baselines consistently lose on both axes: they reduce tokens by a similar margin but sacrifice 3.8–9.3% accuracy on average, confirming that simple length constraints cannot preserve the self-verification steps that complex problems require.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant CoT compression paradigm—where LRMs are fine-tuned on synthetic concise target chains via SFT or RL with length penalties—by instead training on an auxiliary binary verification task (Supervised Verification Fine-Tuning). Conceptually, it reframes overthinking as a failure of the model's internal correctness classifier and directly optimizes that classifier rather than implicitly shaping the output distribution toward shorter chains. The approach also repurposes the learned verification capability for a novel solution-wise speculative reasoning pipeline, extending beyond the compression task itself into inference acceleration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for expensive synthetic target chain generation, VeriThinker offers a simpler and more scalable path to efficient LRM deployment, and its accuracy-preserving compression (unlike SFT baselines that degrade on hard problems) makes it practically attractive for production settings. The speculative reasoning extension demonstrates a concrete throughput win (3–7×) with minimal accuracy loss. Key limitations include poor transfer to smaller models due to catastrophic forgetting, and the moderate compression ratios (~24–34%) suggest the method may not suffice when extreme brevity is required. Open questions include whether the verification signal can be refined for step-level (rather than solution-level) granularity and whether the approach generalizes beyond mathematical reasoning.
