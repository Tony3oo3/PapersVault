## Problem

Existing rehearsal learning methods for Avoiding Undesired Future (AUF) only address single-round decisions, yet real-world tasks require coordinating multiple future outcomes over a time horizon. The long-term AUF objective is intractable because temporal dependencies among target variables complicate the probability characterization, small per-round deviations accumulate into substantial degradation, and the decision dimensionality grows with the horizon. This paper generalizes AUF to a long-term aggregated form and makes the resulting probabilistic optimization tractable.

## Input / Output

The method consumes observed variables x_t, the previous full state v_{t-1}, structural rehearsal model parameters Θ (estimable from historical observational data via least squares without interaction), and a centrally symmetric convex desired region S. It outputs a closed-form alteration z_t^ξ per decision round, computed as a linear function of the observed inputs with pre-computed invariant matrices. Only the current round's alteration is executed; future alterations in the sequence are discarded, making the approach naturally iterative and online in execution despite being offline in parameter estimation.

## Method

The paper derives an explicit decomposition of the time-averaged target variable into a mean component (affine in the alteration sequence) plus an aggregated noise term, showing the noise preserves log-concavity under symmetric log-concave distributions. This enables a quadratic programming reformulation that minimizes the squared distance between the shifted mean and the center of S, which is proven optimal under mild assumptions (linear system, unique targets, symmetric S) via Anderson's lemma for symmetric unimodal distributions. Two algorithms are built on this: GMuR (greedy, solving the QP with T=0 each round) and FarMuR (far-sighted, reweighting matrices by remaining horizon T and subtracting realized outcomes from the target center each round). FarMuR achieves a 1/(L+1)² variance reduction in the AUF objective versus 1/(L+1) for GMuR, yielding substantially higher AUF probability over long horizons. An O(1/√N) excess risk bound guarantees reliable performance when parameters are estimated from finite observational data.

## Results

The paper reports AUF probability P(Ȳ ∈ S | A_{t:t+L}) (%) as the primary metric, evaluated on Synthetic and Bermuda datasets under both Gaussian and Laplace noise at horizon lengths L = 0, 4, 8. The FarMuR method achieves dramatic gains over all baselines as the horizon grows: on Synthetic (Gaussian) at L=8, FarMuR reaches 95.0% AUF probability versus 32.4% (GMuR), 32.2% (QWZ23), and 31.3% (MICNS). On the real-world Bermuda dataset (Gaussian) at L=8, FarMuR achieves 74.3% compared to 28.2% (GMuR), 20.8% (QWZ23), and 20.2% (MICNS). At L=0 (single-round), all methods perform comparably, confirming that the advantage of FarMuR stems from its variance-reduction property over longer horizons.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two prior rehearsal learning methods: QWZ23 (Qin et al., NeurIPS 2023), which uses a MILP/sampling approach, and MICNS (Du et al., NeurIPS 2024), which uses contour-line-based search. At L=0 (single-round), all methods including the proposed GMuR perform comparably, with GMuR slightly outperforming both baselines on Bermuda (10.3% vs 7.3%/7.2% Gaussian; 14.1% vs 7.9%/7.5% Laplace), consistent with the theoretical guarantee in Thm. 3.4. The critical differentiation emerges at longer horizons: at L=8 on Bermuda (Gaussian), FarMuR achieves 74.3% AUF probability, exceeding QWZ23 (20.8%) by 53.5 percentage points and MICNS (20.2%) by 54.1 points. On Synthetic (Gaussian) at L=8, FarMuR reaches 95.0% versus 32.2% (QWZ23) and 31.3% (MICNS). The GMuR method also consistently outperforms both baselines at L=4 and L=8 across all four dataset-noise combinations, validating the QP reformulation's optimality guarantee. The paper notes that RL-based methods are excluded from comparison due to fundamental incompatibility with the AUF setting (distribution shift between observational and interventional data), and three other rehearsal methods (Du et al. 2025, Qin et al. 2025, Tao et al. 2025) are excluded because they address different problem settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the rehearsal learning framework for Avoiding Undesired Future (AUF) from single-round to long-term multi-round decision-making, building directly on the Structural Rehearsal Model (SRM) of Qin et al. (NeurIPS 2023) and the cost-aware formulation of Du et al. (NeurIPS 2024). The key conceptual departure is replacing the constraint-based reformulation (P_AUF ≥ τ) used by prior methods with a quadratic programming reformulation that directly optimizes the AUF objective, yielding a provably optimal solution under mild assumptions. Additionally, the paper generalizes the noise assumption from Gaussian to the broader symmetric log-concave family, and extends the SRM to capture cross-round temporal dependencies in a multivariate time series setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The long-term AUF formulation addresses a practical gap: real-world decisions (portfolio management, environmental control) inherently involve temporal coupling that single-round methods ignore. The QP reformulation provides both computational tractability and optimality guarantees that prior constraint-based methods lacked, while the FarMuR algorithm's L/(L+1) variance reduction offers a principled mechanism for improving long-horizon performance. Limitations include the requirement for linear structural equations, first-order Markov dynamics, and a symmetric desired region S; the unique-target assumption can be restrictive, and the paper's own counterexample (Appx. A) shows the QP can be far from optimal when assumptions fail. Open questions include extending to nonlinear structural equations, non-symmetric regions, and adaptive horizons where the end time is not fixed in advance.
