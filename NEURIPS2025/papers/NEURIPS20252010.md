## Problem

Prior work on online DR-submodular maximization required manually designing and analyzing new algorithms for each function class and feedback type, with no systematic way to transfer results from the simpler convex setting. The earlier linearizable framework of Pedramfar and Aggarwal (2024a) could only handle deterministic first-order algorithms, excluding stochastic, zeroth-order, and bandit-feedback settings. This paper asks: when and how can we adapt arbitrary OCO algorithms (and their regret proofs) into algorithms for online quadratizable optimization?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252010/cropped_page14_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework takes a base online optimization algorithm (e.g., ZO-FTRL or OGA) and a uniform wrapper—a time-independent, one-to-one tuple of action, function, and query-oracle maps—and produces a new algorithm for a more general function class. The wrapper transforms the agent's actions via a map K→K and the adversary's query oracles, so the base algorithm operates on a 'wrapped' problem while the actual actions played are the transformed ones. A novel cone-bounded stochastic oracle model is introduced, where oracle noise vanishes linearly near a reference point, enabling tighter guarantees than fully stochastic oracles.

## Method

The core mechanism is the uniform wrapper W = (W^action, W^function, W^query), a meta-algorithm that translates every action and feedback signal between the base algorithm and the adversary in a time-independent manner. The key proof-conversion guideline has two steps: (1) rewrite the base algorithm's regret proof to isolate a single use of the concavity inequality f(y)−f(x) ≤ ⟨∇f(x), y−x⟩ − (μ/2)‖y−x‖², and (2) replace that inequality with the upper-quadratizability condition αf(y) − f(W(x)) ≤ β(⟨∇W(f)(x), y−x⟩ − (μ/2)‖y−x‖²). Applied to ZO-FTRL (a zeroth-order FTRL variant using self-concordant barriers and random perturbations), the framework yields Õ(T^{2/3}) α-regret for three DR-submodular function classes under zeroth-order feedback, improving over the prior SOTA of Õ(T^{3/4}), and Õ(T^{3/4}) under bandit feedback via the SFTT meta-algorithm. The online-to-batch conversion further gives Õ(1/ε³) sample complexity for offline optimization, beating the prior Õ(1/ε⁴).

## Results

The paper achieves O(T^{2/3}) α-regret for online zeroth-order optimization across all three DR-submodular function classes (Theorem 5), improving upon the previous SOTA of O(T^{3/4}). For online bandit feedback, Theorem 6 yields O(T^{3/4}) α-regret, beating the prior O(T^{4/5}) bound for non-monotone functions over general convex sets. In the offline setting, Theorem 7 establishes O(1/ε^3) sample complexity for zeroth-order feedback, improving over the previous O(1/ε^5) for stochastic oracles. The framework also recovers all prior first-order and semi-bandit results (marked with * in Tables 1–2) as special cases.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's ZO-FTRL base algorithm with uniform wrappers achieves O(T^{2/3}) zeroth-order regret, beating the O(T^{3/4}) of Pedramfar et al. 2024a (ICLR) across all three function classes. For bandit feedback on non-monotone functions, the O(T^{3/4}) bound of Theorem 6 improves upon the O(T^{4/5}) of Zhang et al. 2024 and matches the O(T^{3/4}) of Wan et al. 2023 (whose algorithm is shown to be a special case SFTT(W_0^{M0}(ZO-FTRL))). In the offline setting, Theorem 7's O(1/ε^3) sample complexity beats the O(1/ε^5) of Pedramfar et al. 2023/2024b for stochastic zeroth-order oracles in all three classes. The framework recovers (as special cases) the results of Chen et al. 2018 (semi-bandit, O(T^{1/2})), Zhang et al. 2022 (full-info, O(T^{2/3})), Pedramfar and Aggarwal 2024a (NeurIPS, first-order), and Hassani et al. 2017/2020 (offline first-order, O(1/ε^2)), confirming that all non-Frank-Wolfe algorithms in the literature fit within the uniform wrapper framework.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalizes the linearizable/quadratizable optimization framework of Pedramfar and Aggarwal (NeurIPS 2024), which was restricted to deterministic first-order algorithms with semi-bandit feedback, by introducing 'uniform wrappers' — a class of meta-algorithms that can convert any OCO algorithm (including stochastic, zeroth-order, and non-deterministic ones) into an algorithm for quadratizable functions. The base algorithm ZO-FTRL is adapted from Saha and Tewari (AISTATS 2011), a bandit convex optimization method, with a key modification that decouples the query point from the action point to enable the wrapper conversion. The paper demonstrates that all non-Frank-Wolfe results in the continuous DR-submodular optimization literature (including those of Wan et al. 2023, Zhang et al. 2024, and Pedramfar et al. 2024a) are special cases of its framework, positioning itself as a unifying meta-theory rather than a single new algorithm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The uniform wrapper framework provides a systematic, reusable recipe for converting OCO algorithms and their regret proofs to the quadratizable setting, replacing the ad-hoc algorithm design that has characterized prior work in this area. The concrete improvements — T^{2/3} regret for zeroth-order feedback (vs. T^{3/4} SOTA) and T^{3/4} for bandit feedback (vs. T^{4/5} SOTA for non-monotone functions) — are meaningful and the introduction of cone-bounded stochastic oracles opens a new axis of analysis. However, the proof conversion is a 'guideline' rather than a single theorem, meaning each new base algorithm still requires case-by-case analysis, and the framework is specific to quadratizable functions, leaving open whether similar wrapper constructions exist for other non-convex classes (e.g., general non-convex or non-concave functions without the up-concavity structure).
