## Problem

Cross-view geo-localization (CVGL) must match street-level panoramas to aerial images despite extreme viewpoint, scale, and appearance differences. Existing methods struggle with confounding factors (lighting, weather, background) that create spurious correlations and degrade generalization, and with the large geometric gap between ground and bird's-eye perspectives. This paper is the first to formalize CVGL within a Structural Causal Model and to fuse BEV road topology into street features at the feature level.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252621/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 384×384 street panorama and a set of 384×384 aerial reference images, both encoded by a shared ConvNeXt-B backbone. The street panorama is additionally projected into a BEV representation via geometric back-projection (no depth estimation or camera intrinsics required), which serves as the topology source for fusion. The output is a ranked list of aerial images by dot-product similarity, evaluated with Recall@K (K ∈ {1, 5, 10}) and Recall@1% on benchmarks such as CVUSA, CVACT, and VIGOR.

## Method

CLGT integrates three modules. (1) A Causal Feature Extractor (CFE) applies DCT to the street image, uses a Content-aware Mask with three gradient-adaptive concentric radii to isolate mid-frequency causal components (roads, buildings) from low/high-frequency non-causal signals (style, lighting), and randomizes the non-causal band with Gaussian noise—implementing a do-operation that approximates back-door adjustment. (2) A Geometric Topology Fusion (GT Fusion) module injects BEV road topology into street features via cross-attention with Overlapping Spatial Reduction for global context, followed by a Dual Dynamic Fusion gate that adaptively blends original and geometry-enhanced features. (3) A Data-Adaptive Pooling module combines max, average, and GeM pooling through a learned gate to produce the final descriptor. Training uses a weighted sum of three InfoNCE losses: the primary street-to-aerial matching loss, a causal supervision loss aligning causally enhanced features with fused features, and a geometric consistency loss between street and BEV features.

## Results

On the standard CVUSA benchmark, CLGT achieves 98.73% Recall@1 (98.85% with γ=0.5), surpassing the strongest prior method Sample4Geo (98.68%) and the EP-BEV baseline (97.41%). On CVACT_val, CLGT reaches 91.61% R@1, outperforming EP-BEV by 1.00 points and Sample4Geo by 0.80 points. On the more challenging CVACT_test set, CLGT achieves 73.03% R@1, a 1.62-point gain over EP-BEV. Under 16 real-world corruptions (CVUSA-C-ALL), CLGT achieves 92.64% R@1, a 6.42-point improvement over EP-BEV, demonstrating strong robustness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CLGT outperforms all 13 compared baselines on the primary CVACT_val and CVACT_test benchmarks. Against the direct baseline EP-BEV [28], CLGT gains +1.00 R@1 on CVACT_val (91.61 vs 90.61) and +1.62 R@1 on CVACT_test (73.03 vs 71.41). Compared to the strongest contrastive-learning method Sample4Geo [1], CLGT improves by +0.80 R@1 on CVACT_val and +1.52 R@1 on CVACT_test, while matching it on CVUSA (98.73 vs 98.68). The gap widens substantially against geometric-layout methods: CLGT surpasses GeoDTR† [33] by +5.40 R@1 on CVACT_val and +8.51 R@1 on CVACT_test, and TransGeo [37] by +6.66 R@1 on CVACT_val. On the corruption-robustness benchmark CVUSA-C-ALL, CLGT achieves 92.64% R@1, exceeding EP-BEV by +6.42 points and L2LTR [25] by +4.71 points, confirming that the causal intervention and BEV topology fusion provide meaningful robustness gains under 16 types of visual degradation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the BEV-based cross-view geo-localization line initiated by EP-BEV [28] and HC-Net [21], adopting their geometric back-projection for BEV generation and dual-branch architecture as the foundation. Its primary departure is the introduction of causal inference into CVGL: the authors formalize the first Structural Causal Model for the task and implement a back-door adjustment via DCT-based frequency-domain masking (the Causal Feature Extractor), a mechanism not previously applied to cross-view matching. The Geometric Topology Fusion module also modifies the spatial attention paradigm from TransXNet [11], replacing non-overlapping Spatial Reduction Attention with Overlapping Spatial Reduction to preserve boundary coherence during cross-attention between street and BEV features.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a relatively lightweight frequency-domain intervention (DCT masking with Gaussian perturbation) can serve as a practical causal do-operation, yielding the most substantial gains under real-world corruptions (up to 3.2% R@1 on CVACT_test-C-ALL) where prior methods degrade most. This suggests that confounding factors (lighting, weather, background) are a primary source of cross-view failure and that explicitly suppressing them is more effective than purely geometric alignment. A key limitation acknowledged by the authors is that the geometric BEV transformation introduces considerable noise, capping further improvements; future work on learned or diffusion-based BEV generation combined with richer causal interventions (e.g., front-door adjustment with unobserved confounders) could close this gap. The approach also opens a broader question of whether causal feature extraction via spectral manipulation generalizes beyond geo-localization to other cross-domain matching tasks.
