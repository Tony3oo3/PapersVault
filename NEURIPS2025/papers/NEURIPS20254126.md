## Problem

Open-source large vision-language models (LVLMs) lack the sophisticated chain-of-thought reasoning (planning, reflection, verification) demonstrated by text-only models like DeepSeek-R1. Naively distilling R1 reasoning traces into LVLMs via SFT degrades performance due to imprecise visual grounding, while launching RL from scratch forces the model to search a prohibitively large action space, making reflective behaviors slow or impossible to emerge in smaller (7B) models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254126/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives an RGB image (up to 262,144 pixels) paired with a text question and produces a structured chain-of-thought reasoning trace enclosed in think tags, followed by a final answer in a designated answer tag. The reasoning trace explicitly exhibits inference-time actions—planning, self-reflection, and verification—triggered by specific linguistic markers such as 'wait', 'first', and 'check'. Inference uses near-greedy decoding (top_p=0.001, temperature=0.01) with a maximum of 2048 new tokens.

## Method

OpenVLThinker-7B is trained by iterating three cycles of lightweight SFT followed by curriculum RL, restarting from the Qwen2.5-VL-7B base model each iteration to prevent overfitting. In SFT, the first iteration distills reasoning traces from text-only QwQ-32B using image captions as visual proxies (with rejection sampling to select the shortest correct trace), while subsequent iterations use the previous iteration's own model to generate 3K CoT demonstrations. The RL stage applies GRPO in two sequential rounds—first on medium-difficulty data (3K examples from 5 sources) then on hard-difficulty data (6K examples from 3 geometry-heavy sources)—forming a smooth curriculum. The key insight is that SFT acts as an inductive prior that surfaces latent reasoning tokens (e.g., 'wait') and narrows the RL search space, while RL refines reasoning quality and produces higher-quality SFT data for the next cycle, creating a self-improving loop that achieves state-of-the-art results with only 12K training examples per iteration.

## Results

OpenVLThinker-7B achieves an average accuracy of 47.9% across six benchmarks (MathVista, MathVerse, MathVision, MMMU-Pro, EMMA, HallusionBench), a 2.3% absolute gain over its base model Qwen2.5-VL-7B (45.6%). Headline scores include 72.3% on MathVista, 50.3% on MathVerse, and 69.1% on HallusionBench, all the best among open-source models at comparable scale. Notably, these results are obtained with only 12K training examples per iteration, roughly 1/10 the data used by concurrent single-iteration SFT-RL methods. On the expanded benchmarks MM-Star and WeMath, OpenVLThinker scores 61.9% and 64.1% respectively, outperforming both the base model and VLAA-Thinker.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against its base model Qwen2.5-VL-7B, OpenVLThinker-7B improves average accuracy by 2.3 points (45.6→47.9), with the largest gains on MathVista (+3.8) and HallusionBench (+1.6), while also improving EMMA by +2.4. Compared to the strongest concurrent reasoning model VLAA-Thinker-7B (trained on 150K data from the same base), OpenVLThinker wins on every benchmark—MathVista 72.3 vs 70.0, MathVerse 50.3 vs 48.6, EMMA 26.8 vs 25.5, HallusionBench 69.1 vs 68.4—while using only 12K samples (1/12.5 the data). The gap widens dramatically against R1-Onevision-7B (165K data, avg 41.4) and R1-VL-7B (270K data, avg 33.2), the latter of which suffers severe degradation on MMMU-Pro (7.8) and EMMA (8.3). Among proprietary models, OpenVLThinker's 47.9% average slightly exceeds GPT-4o (47.3%) but falls 0.5 points short of Claude-3.5-Sonnet (48.4%), with the gap driven primarily by EMMA (26.8 vs 35.1) and MathVision (25.9 vs 33.5). The 3B variant (OpenVLThinker-3B) also outperforms VLAA-Thinker-3B and Qwen2.5-VL-3B on MathVista (63.4 vs 61.0 vs 62.3), confirming the approach transfers to smaller scales.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two recent lines of work: (1) RL-based reasoning in text-only LLMs (DeepSeek-R1, GRPO, S1) and (2) vision-language reasoning models that attempt to transfer R1-style capabilities to LVLMs (R1-VL, R1-OneVision, VLAA-Thinker). It departs from prior approaches in two key ways: first, it shows that naive SFT distillation from text-only R1 models to LVLMs actually degrades performance due to imprecise visual grounding, and second, it reframes SFT's role not as direct capability transfer but as 'action highlighting'—surfacing latent reasoning tokens (e.g., 'wait', 'check') that narrow the RL search space. The iterative SFT-RL cycle draws on ReST-EM-style self-improvement but applies it to the multimodal setting with a curriculum RL component.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides one of the first concrete, reproducible recipes for achieving R1-style reasoning in open-source LVLMs, demonstrating that 12K training examples with three SFT-RL iterations outperform concurrent single-iteration methods using 150K–270K data. The conceptual insight that SFT's primary value is narrowing RL's exploration space (rather than directly improving accuracy) offers a useful lens for designing future multimodal reasoning pipelines. Key limitations include validation only on a 7B model and six benchmarks, leaving open whether the iterative cycle scales to larger backbones or generalizes beyond math/geometry-heavy visual tasks. The initial SFT performance drop before iterative recovery is a practical concern for practitioners who may not have the compute budget for multiple training cycles.
