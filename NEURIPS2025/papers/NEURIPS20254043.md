## Problem

Detecting structural change in dynamic networks requires choosing a temporal resolution, but existing methods fix this resolution a priori, creating an inherent tradeoff between temporal localization and statistical stability analogous to the time-frequency tradeoff in signal processing. In domains like cybersecurity or community detection, anomalous or structural changes can emerge at multiple time scales simultaneously, making any single fixed resolution inadequate. The paper addresses this gap by formulating change detection as a multi-resolution intensity estimation problem that automatically adapts to the relevant time scales.

## Input / Output

The method takes as input a dynamic network represented as an ordered sequence of interaction events (u, v, t) encoded as an N×N adjacency counting measure, a latent rank D (selected via scree plot of singular values), and a significance level α (typically 0.05). It produces a D-dimensional subspace matrix Û ∈ ℝ^{N×D} capturing cross-sectional node structure, a multi-resolution intensity estimate Λ̂(t) reconstructed from thresholded wavelet coefficients, and a binary significance mask of size B×D×D indicating which (scale, location, factor-pair) coefficients are statistically significant. The intensity estimate is inherently low-rank and piecewise-structured, with resolution controlled by the maximum wavelet level J.

## Method

ANIE operates in two stages under a Common Subspace Independent Poisson Process (COSIP) model. In the first stage, the adjacency measure is projected onto an orthonormal functional basis (Haar wavelets), yielding B coefficient matrices of size N×N; these are concatenated into a single N×NB matrix and decomposed via truncated SVD to recover the D-dimensional subspace Û, with consistency guaranteed under incoherence and sparsity conditions (Theorem 4.1). In the second stage, empirical affinity coefficients Ŝ(φ^b) = Ûᵀ Y(φ^b) Û are computed for each basis function, producing D×D matrices that quantify interaction intensity changes between latent factor pairs at each scale and location. A key theoretical result (Theorem 4.2) establishes asymptotic normality of these coefficients via the Lyapunov CLT, enabling Z-score computation and Benjamini-Hochberg FDR-controlled multiple testing to separate signal from noise. The final intensity estimate is reconstructed using only statistically significant coefficients, yielding an adaptive, multi-resolution denoised representation that captures both sharp and gradual structural changes without overfitting to edge-level Poisson noise.

## Results

On synthetic E-blocks and SBM datasets (50–2500 nodes), ANIE-Haar achieves lower MISE than both IPP-Hist and IPP-KDE across all network sizes, with the gap widening as N increases. ANIE captures intensity perturbations at multiple temporal resolutions while remaining robust to noise, whereas IPP-Hist and IPP-KDE produce spurious oscillations in flat regions due to overfitting. On the UCI Messages dataset (1,899 users, 196 days), ANIE identifies the two major structural changes (end of spring term at day 60, start of fall term at day 150) at multiple resolution scales, while Tensorsplat's score largely tracks raw message volume and LAD yields only a single aggregated anomaly score.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against IPP-Hist [29] (histogram-based intensity estimation without thresholding), ANIE-Haar wins on MISE for both E-blocks and SBM by avoiding overfitting to noise in flat intensity regions while still localizing sharp change points. Against IPP-KDE [29] (kernel density estimation with low-rank denoising), ANIE similarly achieves lower MISE, as the fixed KDE bandwidth cannot simultaneously resolve fine-scale jumps and suppress noise. On the UCI Messages real-world task, ANIE outperforms Tensorsplat [26], whose PARAFAC-based score is dominated by interaction rate (message count) rather than structural change, and LAD [20], which collapses all temporal scales into a single Laplacian-spectral anomaly score. ANIE's multi-resolution output distinguishes coarse-scale community formation/dissolution from fine-scale academic-calendar fluctuations, a capability neither baseline provides.

## Relation to Prior Work

ANIE carves new ground by integrating two previously separate research threads: wavelet-based multiresolution intensity estimation for point processes (Taleb & Cohen, Kolaczyk, Donoho & Johnstone) and low-rank cross-sectional network structure estimation (COSIE, UASE, Intensity Profile Projection). Prior change-detection methods for dynamic networks (LAD, TensorSplat) operate at a single fixed temporal resolution, while wavelet intensity estimators handle only single point processes without exploiting network-level low-rank structure. ANIE is explicitly positioned as the first work to combine multiresolution wavelet decomposition with a global low-rank subspace estimate, enabling statistical testing of structural change across scales.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method addresses a fundamental practical limitation in dynamic network analysis: the inability of fixed-resolution approaches to simultaneously capture rapid bursts and gradual structural drift. The UCI Messages case study demonstrates that real-world events manifest at different time scales, validating the multiresolution design. Open questions include the sensitivity of thresholding to variance estimation error at high resolutions, the extension to smoother bases (Daubechies, B-splines), and whether tensor factorization or hybrid Tucker-KL decompositions could further sharpen the theoretical and empirical guarantees.
