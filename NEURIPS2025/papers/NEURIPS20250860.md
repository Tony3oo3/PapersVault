## Problem

Existing deep learning methods for learning Koopman operator singular subspaces (VAMPnet, DPNet) require backpropagation through numerically unstable operations—SVD and matrix inversion—on empirical second-moment matrices during objective computation. This introduces biased gradient estimates, causes training divergence especially with small mini-batches, and hinders scalability to high-dimensional systems. The paper seeks a training objective that provably recovers the correct singular subspaces while remaining amenable to standard mini-batch gradient descent.

## Input / Output

The method consumes consecutive state-transition pairs (x_t, x_{t+1}) collected from N independent trajectories of a stochastic dynamical system, where states can range from 1D scalar processes to high-dimensional molecular configurations or 28×28 MNIST images. The constant singular mode (σ₁=1) is handled by fixing the first component of both encoder outputs to 1 (centering), so the network only learns the remaining k−1 non-trivial modes. At inference, the learned basis supports two downstream pipelines: CCA+LoRA (whitening followed by SVD of the joint moment matrix) for ordered singular functions and eigen-analysis, and EDMD (operator regression) for multi-step forward and backward prediction of observables.

## Method

The core contribution is the LoRA loss, which directly minimizes the Hilbert-Schmidt approximation error ‖K − Σᵢ fᵢ⊗gᵢ‖²_HS, yielding the simple objective L_lora = −2·tr(T[f,g]) + tr(M_ρ₀[f]·M_ρ₁[g]). By the Eckart-Young-Mirsky theorem, the global optimum of this objective characterizes the top-k singular subspaces of the Koopman operator, and because the loss is a polynomial in second-moment matrices, its gradient is unbiasedly estimable from mini-batches without any SVD or matrix inversion during training. The authors further employ a nesting technique (joint or sequential) that simultaneously solves LoRA sub-problems for all dimensions i∈[k], encouraging ordered learning of singular functions. For continuous-time reversible dynamics (e.g., Langevin), the objective simplifies to a self-adjoint form involving the generator L, and the method requires only a spectral-gap assumption rather than full compactness. Two inference procedures are provided: CCA+LoRA, which whitens the learned basis and performs SVD on the joint moment matrix to extract ordered singular functions and approximate Koopman matrices, and EDMD, which performs least-squares operator regression on the learned basis for prediction.

## Results

On the Chignolin molecular dynamics benchmark (300 K dataset), LoRA_seq achieves the highest VAMP-E score of 42.44±0.35 in the low-rank (k=16, H=128) setting, outperforming VAMPnet-1 (34.76±0.50) and DPNet-relaxed (7.85±0.24). In the high-rank (k=64) setting, LoRA variants maintain scores above 37 while VAMPnet-1 drops to 19.71 and DPNet-relaxed to 6.97, demonstrating superior robustness under restricted batch sizes. On the Ordered MNIST task, LoRA with joint nesting achieves the lowest multi-step prediction RMSE across all prediction horizons t∈{−15,…,15}. On the 1D Langevin dynamics, LoRA_seq reliably recovers the first eight eigenfunctions and eigenvalues, while the DPNet objective fails to converge.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against VAMPnet-1 [24] and DPNet-relaxed [16] as the primary baselines (VAMPnet-2 and DPNet diverge and are omitted). LoRA variants consistently outperform both baselines on the Chignolin VAMP-E metric across all three configurations: in the low-rank H=128 setting, LoRA_seq (42.44) exceeds VAMPnet-1 (34.76) by ~7.7 points and DPNet-relaxed (7.85) by ~34.6 points. In the high-rank k=64 setting, the gap widens further, with LoRA (37.74) surpassing VAMPnet-1 (19.71) by ~18 points and DPNet-relaxed (6.97) by ~31 points, which the authors attribute to numerical instability in VAMPnet-1's matrix inverse operations under small batches. On Ordered MNIST, LoRA with joint nesting achieves the lowest multi-step prediction RMSE across all horizons, while VAMPnet-1 and DPNet show comparable but inferior performance. On the 1D Langevin dynamics, LoRA_seq successfully recovers ground-truth eigenfunctions and eigenvalues, whereas the DPNet objective fails to produce successful results under the recommended configuration. The authors also note that VAMPnet-1 is sensitive to PyTorch version and diverges under certain train-test splits, further highlighting the stability advantage of the LoRA framework.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper applies the low-rank approximation (LoRA) framework from Ryu et al. [32] to the specific problem of learning singular subspaces of the Koopman operator for stochastic dynamical systems. It departs from the VAMPnet [48] and DPNet [16] line of work by replacing their variational objectives—which require backpropagation through numerically unstable operations like SVD and matrix inversion of empirical second-moment matrices—with a simple polynomial loss in those matrices. The inference procedures (CCA+LoRA and EDMD) are adapted from the VAMPnet and DPNet pipelines respectively, but the training objective is fundamentally different in both form and optimization properties.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The LoRA objective eliminates the primary practical barrier to scaling Koopman operator learning: biased and unstable gradients from backpropagating through decompositions of small-batch moment matrices. This makes the method robust across batch sizes and model dimensions where VAMPnet and DPNet diverge, as demonstrated on the high-dimensional Chignolin molecular dynamics benchmark. Open questions remain for highly non-normal or chaotic systems, and the quality of learned dynamics is inherently limited by the temporal resolution of the data. The finding that DPNet's metric distortion regularization provides no benefit under the LoRA framework suggests the field may be over-parameterizing its training objectives.
