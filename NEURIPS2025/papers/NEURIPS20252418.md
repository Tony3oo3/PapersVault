## Problem

The Quality of Service Degradation (QoSD) problem seeks minimal edge-weight perturbations that push all shortest paths between critical node pairs beyond a performance threshold, but is NP-complete and becomes intractable under nonlinear edge cost functions (quadratic-convex, log-concave). Classical approximation algorithms handle only linear variants and treat source-target pairs independently, while recent ML-based ILP solvers are restricted to linear formulations and small-to-medium graphs. No prior method directly tackles QoSD at scale with general nonlinear edge-weight dynamics.

## Input / Output

The method takes a directed graph with per-edge non-decreasing weight functions f_e(x_e) (linear, quadratic-convex, or log-concave), a set of critical source-target pairs K, per-edge integer budget upper bounds b, and a scalar QoS threshold T. It produces an integer perturbation vector x ∈ ℕ^|E| (bounded by b) minimizing total budget ||x||_1 subject to every shortest path in K exceeding T. Unlike standard ILP formulations, the path cost is determined by the composed edge functions f_e(x_e) rather than x_e directly, so the problem is not an integer program in general and cannot be handed to solvers like Gurobi for nonlinear cases.

## Method

Hephaestus is a three-phase self-reinforcing generative pipeline. **Forge** trains SPAGAN to predict shortest-path costs, then runs a Predictive Path-Stressing (PPS) greedy algorithm that selects edge-increment pairs maximizing a gain-to-cost ratio over a soft potential function, yielding feasible solutions with a provable (1 + h·ln n + ln T + ln(1/ε̄))·OPT/a approximation bound. **Morph** trains an Energy-Based Model on the solution set and uses it to guide a Mixture of Conditional VAEs via a normalization-free minimax objective (min_q max_Ω KL(p‖q) − KL(Ω‖q)), dynamically adding new CVAE experts wherever the log-density ratio χ(x) = log(q(x)/Ω(x)) exceeds a threshold δ, with a theorem guaranteeing monotone KL reduction per expert. **Refine** deploys an RL agent in the Mix-CVAE latent space that modifies latent codes using a differentiable reward combining a sigmoid feasibility score over all pairs and a log-budget penalty; gradient ascent on this reward provides a warm-start before standard RL exploration, and top-k decoded solutions are fed back into the solution dataset to periodically retrain the EBM and Mix-CVAE, closing the self-reinforcement loop.

## Results

On the hardest threshold (T = 260% of baseline shortest-path length) under linear edge weights, Hephaestus achieves the lowest total perturbation budget across all four real-world networks. The advantage is most pronounced on large-scale graphs: Hephaestus reduces total cost by 28.1% on Skitter (2,199,372 vs. 3,012,839 for DIFFILO) and 16.8% on RoadCA (27,699 vs. 32,976) compared to the best ML-based baseline. On smaller networks the gap narrows—Hephaestus improves over DIFFILO by 4.5% on Gnutella and 1.0% on Email at 260%, while being marginally behind (by 7 units) on Gnutella at the easiest threshold (140%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the classical approximation baselines from Nguyen & Thai (2019), Hephaestus consistently outperforms Adaptive Trading (AT), Iterative Greedy (IG), and Sampling Algorithm (SA) on all four datasets at the 260% threshold; the gap is largest on RoadCA (27,699 vs. 32,935 for AT, a 16% saving) and Skitter (2.2M vs. 3.0M for AT, a 27% saving), because PPS exploits shared edges across source-target pairs while AT/IG treat each pair independently. Among ML-based ILP solvers, DIFFILO is the strongest competitor on small-to-medium graphs (Email, Gnutella), where Hephaestus leads by 1.0% and 4.5% respectively at 260%, but trails by a negligible 7 units on Gnutella at 140%. Predict-and-Search and L-MILPOPT are uniformly worse than DIFFILO across all datasets, with L-MILPOPT performing worst on Gnutella (11,772 vs. 10,495 for Hephaestus at 260%). The exact Gurobi solver achieves the lowest cost on Email (9,318) and Gnutella (10,073) but is infeasible on RoadCA and Skitter within the 6000s timeout, making direct comparison impossible on the largest instances where Hephaestus's advantage is most significant.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the QoSD problem introduced by Nguyen and Thai (2019), which previously had only approximation algorithms (AT, IG, SA) guaranteeing performance solely under linear edge functions. It departs from the ML-based ILP solver line (DiffILO, Predict-and-Search, L-MILPOPT) that is restricted to linear formulations and small-to-medium instances, and from classical hard network interdiction that removes edges rather than perturbing weights. The method lineage draws on SPAGAN (Yang et al., IJCAI 2019) for learned shortest-path estimation and the minimax EBM training paradigm (Du & Mordatch, NeurIPS 2019), but combines them with a novel EBM-guided dynamic mixture-of-CVAE and latent-space RL refinement into a unified self-reinforcing pipeline for the first time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to solve QoSD under nonlinear (quadratic-convex, log-concave) edge weight functions at large scale, a regime where exact solvers like Gurobi fail entirely and prior approximation methods lose their guarantees. The normalization-free minimax formulation for EBM-guided mixture training and the dynamic expert augmentation with a provable KL-reduction bound (Theorem 2) are reusable techniques for generative modeling over combinatorial spaces. Key limitations include dependence on the quality of PPS-generated initial solutions and SPAGAN's generalization to unseen graph topologies; open questions include whether the architecture transfers to other graph combinatorial optimization tasks and whether graph foundation models could replace the hand-crafted SPAGAN estimator.
