## Problem

Orientation maps—spatially organised arrangements of neurons by preferred edge orientation—are canonically associated with the layered architecture of mammalian visual cortex. Despite the Drosophila brain lacking cortical lamination and large-scale recurrent loops, it contains feature-selective neurons, raising the question of whether coherent, map-like orientation representations can emerge from purely connectome-constrained dynamics in a non-cortical invertebrate visual system. No prior study had computationally demonstrated such spatially organised orientation maps in Drosophila.

## Input / Output

The simulation takes the complete FAFB connectome (138,639 neurons, 1,508,983 synapses) as its structural backbone and drives it with oriented OFF-bar stimuli encoded as Poisson spike trains injected into L1–3 lamina neurons, where each neuron's input rate is a nonlinear function of the distance from its ommatidium to the bar (calibrated to calcium imaging data). Outputs include per-neuron preferred orientation angles extracted via circular Gaussian fits (R² ≥ 0.7, RSS_norm ≤ 0.4), smoothed 2D orientation maps in the Dm and Pm medulla sublayers, cross-layer orientation columns anchored on Tm3 neurons, and pinwheel-like topological singularities identified in the smoothed maps.

## Method

The core approach integrates a conductance-based leaky integrate-and-fire (LIF) spiking model with the full adult Drosophila connectome, propagating stimulus-evoked activity from L1–3 lamina inputs through all downstream synapses to monitor orientation tuning in the medulla. Stimuli are spatially structured OFF-bars whose per-ommatidium firing rate follows a square-root distance profile (A_x·√(1−d²)·max(fr)/10) with layer-specific gains (A₃ > A₁ > A₂) fitted to Ketkar et al. calcium imaging data. Orientation selectivity is quantified by fitting a circular Gaussian (accounting for 180° periodicity) to each neuron's firing-rate-vs-angle curve, and spatial organisation is revealed by projecting 3D neuron morphologies onto layer-specific 2D planes, applying circular-mean smoothing over a ~5 µm radius, and analysing the relationship between each neuron's position angle α and preferred orientation β in polar coordinates. A structural prediction method—drawing a best-fit ellipse through the spatial positions of each neuron's upstream columnar partners—provides an independent, connectivity-based estimate of preferred orientation for validation. Targeted ablation (silencing Tm or Mi neuron populations) and orientation-based synaptic connectivity analysis (excitatory diagonal vs. inhibitory off-diagonal structure) further dissect the circuit mechanisms underlying the emergent maps.

## Results

The LIF simulation over the full FAFB connectome (138,639 neurons, 1,508,983 synapses) reveals that Drosophila medulla neurons exhibit robust orientation selectivity with a mean absolute difference of 13.7° between LIF-simulated preferred orientations and structurally predicted angles from dendritic geometry. Spatial analysis of 1,710 Dm-layer neurons and 143 Pm-layer neurons uncovers pinwheel-like singularities and a systematic tangential alignment (β ≈ α + 90°) in peripheral regions. Across 858 extracted orientation columns (avg. ~158 neurons in Dm, ~30 in Pm), the Dm layer shows significantly sharper within-column orientation coherence than the Pm layer relative to a shuffled null baseline (p < 10⁻²⁰, Mann-Whitney U). Targeted ablation confirms Mi neurons as essential upstream drivers: silencing Mi reduces well-fit orientation-selective neurons to 2.4% (Dm) and 3.9% (Pm), whereas silencing Tm neurons preserves 90.7% in Dm but drops Pm to 10.4%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not benchmark against competing computational methods; instead, it validates its LIF simulation against two independent references. First, the simulated preferred orientations are compared to Seung's [45] structural (dendritic-geometry) predictions, yielding a mean absolute angular difference of 13.7° across the population, confirming that wiring alone largely determines orientation preference. Second, column coherence is validated against a null model in which preferred orientations are randomly shuffled while preserving spatial positions; the Dm layer's observed circular standard deviation distribution is significantly separated from this baseline (p < 10⁻²⁰), whereas the Pm layer's distribution overlaps more closely with the null, indicating weaker but still present columnar structure. The paper also cross-checks stimulus-driven lamina responses against calcium imaging data from Ketkar et al. [29] and verifies that known OFF-cells (Tm1, Tm2, Tm4) and ON-cells (Mi1, Mi4, Tm3) respond in the expected direction to OFF-bar stimuli, confirming biological plausibility of the simulation pipeline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the connectome-driven whole-brain simulation framework established by Shiu et al. (2024) to a specific visual computation question: whether spatially organized orientation maps can emerge in Drosophila. It builds directly on Seung's (2024) structural prediction of orientation selectivity from dendritic geometry, using the same FAFB connectome and LIF dynamics, but goes beyond per-neuron tuning to demonstrate population-level spatial organisation (pinwheel singularities, tangential alignment, cross-layer columns). Prior physiological work (Fisher et al., 2015) established T4/T5 orientation selectivity, and Seung predicted selectivity for Dm3/TmY types from wiring alone, but no prior study had computationally demonstrated coherent orientation maps in this non-cortical system.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstration that pinwheel-like orientation maps and columnar structure emerge purely from connectome-constrained dynamics in a non-layered invertebrate brain suggests that such maps reflect convergent computational principles rather than cortical-specific architecture, with implications for bio-inspired vision and understanding the minimal circuit requirements for feature-selective maps. Key limitations include the absence of in vivo validation, the use of a simplified LIF model without nonlinear dynamics or neuromodulation, and static bar stimuli that may not capture the full range of natural visual input. Open questions include whether the observed tangential alignment pattern has a direct functional role in flight control (e.g., horizon detection), how the maps change under dynamic stimuli, and whether the R7/R8 recurrent-loop finding generalises to other sensory neurons.
