## Problem

Best-of-N (BoN) sampling improves LLM performance by generating N parallel candidates and selecting the best, but faces two unaddressed efficiency bottlenecks simultaneously: (1) full generation of all N samples causes severe GPU memory overhead from KV cache accumulation, and (2) reward models needed for scoring add extra memory, latency, and require costly domain-specific training. No prior method eliminates both the full-generation cost and the reward-model dependency at once.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255415/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard text prompt/question and generates N parallel autoregressive samples using top-k (k=20), top-p (p=0.95), and temperature (T=0.7) sampling. It requires white-box access to the model's hidden states at every layer and position to compute Chain-of-Embedding features, which limits applicability to open-source models. The output is a single selected text response: after identifying the most consistent sample at an early decoding time c plus a buffer window τ, the remaining N−1 samples are truncated and only the best-estimated sample continues generation to [EOS].

## Method

ST-BoN first proves a probabilistic monotonicity theorem showing that smaller early-decoding distances between samples imply a higher lower bound on final consistency, establishing theoretical feasibility for early truncation. It then defines the earliest estimation time c as the first step where all N samples become pairwise textually distinct, and uses Chain-of-Embedding (CoE) features—computed from mean hidden states across all layers—as a continuous distance metric to score each sample's consistency with the others. A buffer window of length τ = mc (with m=1 in main experiments) accumulates τ+1 self-estimations, and the final selection is the most frequently chosen sample across these estimates, mitigating noise from minimal early differences. This eliminates both full generation (reducing peak GPU memory by >80%) and reward models entirely, achieving the same accuracy as Full-BoN at 70–80% lower computational cost, or 3–4 points higher accuracy at equal cost.

## Results

ST-BoN reduces peak GPU memory by over 80% and wall-clock inference latency by ~50% compared to Full-BoN, without requiring any reward model. On objective tasks (MATH, TheoremQA, GPQA, MMLU) with Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, ST-BoN matches Full-BoN w/o RM accuracy at 70–75% lower computational cost (e.g., ST-BoN at N=20–30 matches Full-BoN at N=20), and matches Full-BoN w/ RM at 75–80% lower cost (ST-BoN at N=40–80). Under a fixed computational budget, ST-BoN scales to N≈80 versus N=10 (Full-BoN w/o RM) or N=3 (Full-BoN w/ RM), yielding a 3–4 point accuracy gain across all four objective datasets. These gains hold across model scales (7B to 72B) and extend to subjective tasks (CNN/DM summarization, AlpacaFarm instruction-following).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Full-BoN w/o RM (self-consistency / majority voting [47]), ST-BoN achieves equivalent accuracy at 70–75% lower cost on all four objective datasets, and under the same cost budget gains 3–4 points by scaling N from ~10 to ~80. Against Full-BoN w/ RM (process reward model), ST-BoN matches performance at 75–80% lower cost, and avoids the additional memory and latency overhead of loading and running a separate reward model. Against the three efficient-BoN baselines (SBoN [41], CARDS [25], TreeBoN [36]) compared in Appendix B (Figure 9), ST-BoN clearly outperforms on the MATH reasoning benchmark, where all three methods perform poorly because their reward models cannot reliably score arbitrarily segmented text fragments. On AlpacaFarm (preference alignment), where those methods were originally validated, ST-BoN performs comparably or slightly better, confirming domain generalizability. Overall, ST-BoN is the only method that is simultaneously free of reward models, free of domain-specific priors, and applicable to both objective (reasoning, knowledge) and subjective (generation, preference) tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the self-consistency paradigm [47] from full-generation answer matching to early-decoding latent-space consistency estimation, using the Chain-of-Embedding (CoE) representation [49] as its core distance metric. It departs from prior efficient BoN methods (SBoN, CARDS, TreeBoN) that still rely on reward models for scoring partial sequences, and from breadth-pruning approaches (ASC, ESC) that sacrifice parallelism. The key conceptual departure is that no prior method simultaneously eliminates both the full-generation overhead and the reward-model dependency; ST-BoN achieves this by leveraging the model's own internal hidden states as a self-contained consistency signal in early decoding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 80%+ GPU memory reduction and 50% latency reduction make BoN sampling practically deployable under resource constraints, and the 70–80% cost savings at matched performance (or 3–4 point accuracy gains at matched cost) offer a concrete efficiency lever for production inference pipelines. Two limitations remain open: the method requires white-box access to hidden states (excluding closed-source APIs), and the buffer window length τ is a fixed hyperparameter whose adaptive tuning based on task complexity is identified as future work. The theoretical guarantee (Theorem 1) provides a probabilistic lower bound on early-to-final consistency propagation, but the bound is loose (exponential in T−t), leaving room for tighter analyses or data-driven calibration.
