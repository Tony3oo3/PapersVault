## Problem

Existing adversarial attacks on aligned LLMs optimize for exact affirmative text patterns (e.g., 'Sure, here is...'), creating brittle objectives that suffer from limited convergence, produce semantically incoherent prompts, and incur high computational costs. This forces a fundamental trade-off between attack efficacy and prompt naturalness that no prior method resolves.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252437/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a malicious user query token sequence q, a semantic representation Φ (identified via a fine-tuned Llama 2 13B from HarmBench acting as the mapping function R), and chat template tokens s1 and s2. It outputs a set of adversarial prompts A, each of which, when concatenated between the query and template, induces the target LLM to generate a response y* whose semantic representation matches Φ. The search is constrained by a perplexity threshold τ (default 20) and a top-k sampling parameter (k=50) to manage the discrete token space.

## Method

The core insight is reconceptualizing the adversarial objective from maximizing probability of a single exact response to maximizing probability mass over an entire semantic equivalence class Y_Φ of harmful responses, formalized in Definition 3.1. The Semantic Representation Heuristic Search (SRHS) algorithm performs incremental tree search over the prompt space with two coupled components: a Harmfulness Representation step that selects candidate prompts eliciting responses matching Φ under a probability threshold 1/τ^|y|, and a Semantic Coherence step that extends each candidate with tokens satisfying P(v|x) > 1/τ. Theorem 3.1 (Coherence-Convergence Relationship) proves that maintaining low perplexity on the full context-response sequence guarantees a lower bound on the probability of generating any semantically equivalent response, while Theorem 3.3 provides a sufficient condition for coherence preservation during token extension. This bounds the branching factor at each node to at most τ tokens (via the probability sum constraint), reducing complexity from O(V^N) to O(N·min(τ^l, η)) and enabling practical generation within seconds on a single GPU.

## Results

SRA achieves an average attack success rate (ASR) of 89.41% across 18 LLMs, reaching 100% ASR on 11 of them (including DeepSeek R1 8B, Vicuna 7B/13B, Koala 7B/13B, Orca 2 7B/13B, Mistral 7B, OpenChat 7B, and Starling 7B). This represents a 17.81 percentage-point improvement over the strongest baseline (AutoDAN at 71.60%). Under a 15-second compute budget, SRA achieves 95.77% ASR on Vicuna 7B with a PPL of only 24.21, compared to GCG's 43.85% ASR and PPL of 753.39, while maintaining 95.96% ASR under PPL-based defense (intensity 5).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SRA outperforms all 15 baselines on 16 of 18 LLMs in Table 1. Against GCG (Zou et al., 2023), SRA improves average ASR from 69.79% to 89.41%, with the largest gap on Llama 3.1 8B (45.00% vs 15.67%) and R2D2 7B (42.00% vs 10.50%). Against AutoDAN (Liu et al., ICLR 2024), the closest competitor at 71.60% average, SRA gains 17.81 points and achieves 100% on 11 models where AutoDAN peaks at 96.75%. Against BEAST (Sadasivan et al., ICML 2024), SRA dominates on efficiency: at 15 s it reaches 95.77% ASR on Vicuna 7B vs BEAST's 77.12%, with PPL of 24.21 vs 82.47, and retains 95.96% ASR under PPL defense vs BEAST's 67.31%. Against TAP (Mehrotra et al., 2024) and PAIR (Chao et al., 2023), SRA leads by 21.14 and 27.63 percentage points on average, respectively. On transferability (Table 4), SRA maintains 79–100% ASR across cross-model transfers, while BEAST drops to as low as 3.46% (Guanaco→Vicuna 13B) and AutoDAN to 27.31% (Vicuna 7B→Vicuna 13B), confirming that semantic-targeting prompts generalize far better than model-specific token-level optimizations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the gradient-based optimization lineage (GCG, PEZ, AutoDAN) and the search-based lineage (BEAST, TAP) by reconceptualizing the adversarial objective: rather than maximizing the probability of a single target string like 'Sure, here is...', it targets an entire semantic equivalence class of harmful responses. This reframing replaces complex gradient descent over discrete tokens with a simpler heuristic tree search (SRHS) constrained by a perplexity threshold, drawing on information-theoretic coherence arguments to guarantee convergence. Conceptually, it repurposes the idea of semantic equivalence (well-studied in NLP) as the optimization target rather than a post-hoc evaluation criterion, which prior methods like AutoDAN only partially addressed through perplexity regularization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 100% ASR on 11 of 18 models and the dramatic PPL reduction (24 vs. 753 for GCG) suggest that the efficacy-naturalness trade-off plaguing prior attacks may be an artifact of targeting exact strings rather than a fundamental constraint. However, the method requires white-box logit access, limiting applicability to closed-source LLMs, and depends on accurate semantic representation modeling that may not transfer across architectures. Open questions include whether the semantic targeting framework can be adapted to black-box settings, how it performs against more sophisticated defenses beyond PPL filtering, and whether the coherence threshold τ can be set automatically without per-model calibration.
