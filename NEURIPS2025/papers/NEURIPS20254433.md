## Problem

BIQA models trained on synthetic distortion datasets exhibit limited generalization to authentic and algorithmic distortions. The paper identifies the root cause as a discrete, clustered feature distribution induced by the synthetic data itself—high-quality features cluster by reference image while low-quality features cluster by distortion type—rather than by model architecture. This stems from two data-level issues: insufficient content diversity due to limited reference images, and excessive redundancy from uniform combinations of references, distortion types, and intensities.

## Input / Output

The method operates at the data level: it reshapes the synthetic training distribution (KADID-10k) before model training, so the downstream IQA model (ResNet-50, ViT-B/16, or Swin-T) consumes standard 224×224 RGB patches and outputs a continuous quality score in [0,10]. During training, one random patch per image is used with horizontal flipping; during testing, predictions from five patches are averaged. The DDCUp module additionally requires an unlabeled candidate reference set (KADIS-700k) and a pre-trained feature extractor (ImageNet or CLIP ViT-B/16) for content-aware reference selection, but adds no inference-time cost.

## Method

SynDR-IQA is a data-distribution reshaping framework grounded in a theoretical generalization bound (Theorem 3.1) that decomposes error into terms governed by the number of diverse i.i.d. samples m and a redundancy heterogeneity coefficient η. The first strategy, DDCUp, selects new reference images from KADIS-700k that are distributionally similar (within the median pairwise distance of training references) yet content-diverse (exceeding that median distance from all existing references), then synthesizes distorted images at levels 1, 3, and 5 with pseudo-labels computed as softmax-weighted averages of k nearest neighbors' labels in feature space. The second strategy, DRCDown, is applied before each training round: it identifies similar sample pairs using both feature distance (threshold 0.1) and label distance (threshold 1 on [0,10] MOS), groups them via disjoint-set union, and randomly downsamples clusters exceeding 2T_u members to max(⌊N_u/2⌋, T_u) samples. Together, DDCUp increases m while DRCDown decreases η, jointly tightening the generalization bound without modifying the model architecture or adding inference overhead.

## Results

On the synthetic-to-authentic setting (KADID-10k → LIVEC, KonIQ-10k, BID), SynDR-IQA achieves an average SRCC of 0.743 and PLCC of 0.737, outperforming the next-best method DGQA (0.716 SRCC, 0.710 PLCC) by 2.7% on both metrics. On the synthetic-to-algorithmic setting (KADID-10k → PIPAL algorithmic distortions), SynDR-IQA improves average SRCC from 0.4789 (DGQA) to 0.5110 (+3.21%). On the synthetic-to-synthetic setting, SynDR-IQA raises in-dataset KADID-10k SRCC from 0.8528 to 0.8922 and cross-dataset SRCC on TID2013 from 0.7077 to 0.7147.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SynDR-IQA outperforms the strongest prior method DGQA (CVPR 2024) by 2.7% average SRCC and 2.7% average PLCC across all three authentic test sets, with per-dataset gains of +1.7% SRCC on LIVEC, +4.6% on KonIQ-10k, and +1.8% on BID. Against FreqAlign (TMM 2023), the gap widens to +6.3% average SRCC, though FreqAlign retains a narrow edge on KonIQ-10k SRCC (0.748 vs. 0.727). StyleAM, the best UDA-based method, trails SynDR-IQA by 10.3% average SRCC. On the synthetic-to-algorithmic setting, SynDR-IQA beats DGQA by +3.21% average SRCC, with the largest gains on PSNR-originated SR (+4.26%) and SR with kernel mismatch (+4.53%), while showing a slight −0.31% drop on the SR-and-Denoising-Joint category. The method also surpasses the ResNet-50 baseline on the synthetic-to-synthetic setting by +3.94% SRCC on KADID-10k and +1.0% on TID2013, confirming that the data-distribution reshaping benefits generalize beyond the synthetic-to-authentic transfer scenario.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant unsupervised domain adaptation (UDA) paradigm in synthetic-to-real IQA (DANN, StyleAM, FreqAlign, DGQA), which adapts model features at training time to bridge the domain gap. Instead, it takes a data-centric approach: reshaping the synthetic training distribution before any model training, motivated by a theoretical generalization bound for clustered synthetic data. It builds directly on DGQA's training pipeline (ResNet-50 backbone, L1 loss, distortion-type selection) as its baseline, but replaces the UDA mechanism with two data-level strategies—distribution-aware diverse content upsampling (DDCUp) and density-aware redundant cluster downsampling (DRCDown). The pseudo-labeling strategy for newly synthesized images draws on the assumption that similar content under identical distortion yields similar quality, connecting to broader self-supervised and semi-supervised labeling ideas applied here to the IQA setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a previously underappreciated structural issue in synthetic distortion datasets—discrete feature clustering driven by limited reference diversity and redundant distortion combinations—and provides a theoretical bound linking this structure to generalization error. Because the approach is purely data-level, it is model-agnostic (validated on ResNet-50, ViT-B/16, and Swin-T) and adds zero inference cost, making it composable with any existing IQA architecture. A key open question, acknowledged by the authors, is the persistent gap on algorithmic distortions (e.g., GAN-based super-resolution), where synthetic degradation patterns fundamentally differ from learned artifacts; closing this gap likely requires new synthetic distortion generators that emulate deep-learning-based processing. The finding that indiscriminate data expansion can hurt performance (ablation row b vs. a) also cautions against naive scaling of synthetic datasets.
