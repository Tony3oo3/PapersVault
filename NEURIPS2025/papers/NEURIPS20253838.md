## Problem

Despite Adam's widespread success in training transformers, no comprehensive theoretical explanation exists for its advantage over SGD. The paper identifies that Adam is not rotation-equivariant—its performance depends on the coordinate basis—yet existing convergence proofs rely on rotation-invariant assumptions that are agnostic to basis choice and thus cannot capture this critical dependency. The central question is: which rotation-dependent properties actually govern Adam's empirical behaviour across different bases?

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard neural network training pipelines (GPT-2 124M on OpenWebText, ViT/S 22M and ResNet-50 on ImageNet-1K) but injects rotation matrices into the gradient flow: gradients are rotated before being fed to Adam, and the resulting update is rotated back to the standard basis. To handle the intractability of full d×d rotations, the paper uses a composite of block-diagonal n×n rotation matrices (n=768) with random permutations, reducing memory from O(d²) to O(n²+d). Outputs include training/validation loss curves under each rotation scope, the coefficient of variation (CV) of singular values of per-layer updates as an orthogonality metric, sampled Hessian row distributions partitioned by neuron/layer/other-layer indices, and empirical estimates of L∞ gradient bounds and (1,1)-norm Hessian surrogates.

## Method

The paper conducts a systematic empirical study of Adam's sensitivity to four scopes of random parameter-space rotations (global, layer-wise, output-wise, input-wise), showing that broader rotations cause greater performance degradation (16% slowdown for GPT-2, 96% for ViT under global rotation), while output-wise rotations preserve or slightly improve performance. It then demonstrates that structured SVD-based rotations (decomposing each layer's gradient as G=USVᵀ and rotating via G→UᵀGV, updated every 250 steps) improve Adam's convergence, linking this to the Muon optimizer's orthogonalized updates. To explain these observations theoretically, the paper evaluates three existing rotation-dependent assumptions—L∞ bounded gradients, Hessian block-diagonality, and L∞-smoothness—and shows each fails to consistently predict Adam's performance across rotation types. Finally, it identifies the scaled orthogonality of per-layer updates, measured by the coefficient of variation of their singular values, as a rotation-dependent quantity that closely tracks Adam's empirical performance: SVD rotations yield the most orthogonal (lowest CV) updates and best performance, while global random rotations yield the least orthogonal updates and worst performance.

## Results

Adam's performance degrades significantly under random parameter space rotations: GPT-2 (124M) experiences a 16% training slowdown under global rotations, while ViT/S (22M) suffers a dramatic 96% slowdown. Performance degradation scales with rotation scope—global > layer-wise > input-wise > output-wise—with output-wise rotations showing no degradation (GPT-2 even slightly improves) and ResNet-50 exhibiting minimal sensitivity overall. SVD-based rotations improve Adam's convergence with low computational overhead, and the coefficient of variation (CV) of singular values of layer updates closely tracks performance across all rotation types, with SVD rotations yielding the most orthogonal (lowest CV) updates.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not propose a new optimizer to benchmark against existing methods; instead, it compares different rotation scopes and theoretical assumptions. Among rotation types, global random rotations cause the most severe degradation (16% for GPT-2, 96% for ViT), while output-wise rotations are essentially neutral and SVD-based rotations actively improve performance. Among the three rotation-dependent assumptions examined, L∞-bounded gradients fail to predict performance (the bound decreases under global rotations, suggesting better performance, yet Adam actually degrades). Hessian block-diagonality is partially supported—diagonal blocks contain larger values—but off-diagonal elements collectively dominate gradient evolution, invalidating the strict block-diagonal approximation. The (1,1)-norm of the Hessian correlates with performance under global and SVD rotations but not output-wise, making it an incomplete indicator. The CV of singular values of layer updates emerges as the most consistent predictor, aligning with performance across all rotation types and connecting to the Muon optimizer's orthogonalized update mechanism.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of Adam convergence theory (Kingma & Ba, Reddi et al., Defossez et al.) and rotation-based optimization methods (Shampoo, SOAP, Muon), taking an empirical-first stance to systematically study how parameter space rotations affect Adam's performance across multiple scopes. It builds on GaLore's SVD-based gradient compression by repurposing full-rank SVD as a diagnostic rotation tool, and directly engages with three rotation-dependent assumptions from the literature (L∞-smoothness, L∞-bounded gradients, Hessian block-diagonality) to test their adequacy. Unlike prior works that propose a single theoretical explanation for Adam's advantage (heavy-tailed noise, directional sharpness, block-diagonal Hessian), this paper takes a broader diagnostic approach, evaluating multiple hypotheses simultaneously against a spectrum of rotation types.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper challenges the adequacy of existing rotation-dependent theoretical assumptions for explaining Adam's practical advantage, showing that the L∞ gradient bound and strict block-diagonal Hessian approximation both fail to predict performance under rotation. The identification of update orthogonality (measured via coefficient of variation of singular values) as a quantity that closely tracks Adam's performance across rotation types provides a concrete, testable hypothesis for future theoretical work and offers a principled connection to Muon's orthogonalized updates. Key open questions remain: a formal theoretical analysis of why more orthogonal updates lead to better convergence, why SVD rotations produce more orthogonal updates than the canonical basis, and whether the orthogonality metric generalizes beyond the GPT-2 setting studied here. The single-seed nature of the large-scale experiments and the absence of theoretical guarantees for the orthogonality finding limit the strength of the conclusions.
