## Problem

Standard LoRA methods designed for Transformers fail when applied to ConvNets because adjacent convolutional layers exhibit strong gradient correlations during fine-tuning, resulting in full-rank weight updates that cannot be captured by independent per-layer low-rank matrices. This paper identifies this full-rank learning bottleneck and asks whether an effective low-rank PEFT strategy can be designed that explicitly models inter-layer dependencies in ConvNets.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254254/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on pre-trained ConvNets (ResNet-50, ConvNeXt-S/B) initialized from ImageNet-22K and fine-tunes them on downstream RGB image tasks. For classification (VTAB-1k, CUB-200-2011, FGVC-Aircraft), it produces class labels; for semantic segmentation (ADE20K at 512×512 via UPerNet), pixel-wise masks; and for object detection (MS-COCO via Faster R-CNN), bounding boxes with class labels. Only ~5% of parameters are trainable (e.g., 2.6M for ConvNeXt-S), and the bilateral filtering module operates on intermediate feature maps during the forward pass without adding trainable parameters.

## Method

CoLoRA decomposes the weight update of each pair of adjacent 1×1 convolutional layers into a shared correlated component and layer-specific independent components, yielding ΔW₁ = sA_sB_sW₂ᵀ + s₁A₁B₁ and ΔW₂ = sW₁ᵀA_sB_s + s₂A₂B₂, where A_s, B_s are shared low-rank matrices that emulate the correlated gradient structure revealed by backpropagation. A variance-matching scaling factor s_var is used for the shared matrices to prevent divergence on domain-shifted tasks, while expectation-matching factors handle the layer-specific parts. A parameter-free edge-preserving bilateral filter (using a trigonometric range kernel) is applied to features compressed by the low-rank matrix A to enlarge the effective receptive field and suppress gradients from irrelevant local regions. CoLoRA is applied bottom-up to pairs of adjacent convolutions within each residual block, with vanilla LoRA on any unpaired layer, and optionally combined with periodic LoRA weight merging to further increase effective rank.

## Results

On the VTAB-1k classification benchmark, CoLoRA with ConvNeXt-S surpasses full fine-tuning performance, while with ResNet-50 it achieves average top-1 accuracy gains of 8.71%, 5.06%, and 7.54% over Conv-Adapter, PiSSA, and HiRA, respectively. On ADE20K semantic segmentation, CoLoRA (ConvNeXt-S) reaches 48.28 mIoU with only 2.6M trainable parameters, closing the gap to full fine-tuning (49.87) to just 1.59 mIoU; the stronger CoLoRA† variant (ConvNeXt-B) achieves 50.65 mIoU, nearly matching full tuning (50.99). On MS-COCO object detection, CoLoRA (ConvNeXt-S) attains 43.6 AP_box and 40.3 AP_mask, outperforming all PEFT baselines by 1.7–2.4 AP points. Overall, CoLoRA uses only ~5% trainable parameters while consistently outperforming state-of-the-art PEFT methods across classification, segmentation, and detection.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoLoRA consistently outperforms all three baselines across every task and backbone. Against Conv-Adapter [32], CoLoRA gains +1.77 mIoU on ADE20K (ConvNeXt-S: 48.28 vs 46.51) and +1.7 AP_box on COCO (43.6 vs 41.9), while using fewer parameters (2.6M vs 2.8M). Against PiSSA [7], the margins are similar: +1.72 mIoU on ADE20K and +1.8 AP_box on COCO for ConvNeXt-S. Against HiRA [22], CoLoRA leads by +1.26 mIoU on ADE20K and +2.4 AP_box on COCO (ConvNeXt-S), with the gap widening for ConvNeXt-B where HiRA drops to 43.5 AP_box versus CoLoRA's 47.0. On the VTAB-1k classification benchmark, CoLoRA achieves average accuracy gains of 5.06–8.71% over the three baselines with ResNet-50, and even surpasses full fine-tuning with ConvNeXt-S. When extended to ViT-B for fine-grained classification, CoLoRA (75.61 on CUB-200-2011, 72.79 on FGVC-Aircraft) substantially outperforms HiRA (51.97, 34.32) and slightly edges out PiSSA (74.70, 72.40), confirming the correlated-update strategy generalizes beyond ConvNets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LoRA lineage (Hu et al. 2022, PiSSA, HiRA) from Transformers to ConvNets, but departs fundamentally from the per-layer independent low-rank assumption. The key conceptual departure is identifying that backpropagation through sequential convolutional layers creates a shared gradient term (X^T ∂L/∂Y₂), making adjacent layer updates inherently correlated — a property absent in Transformer self-attention. CoLoRA exploits this by binding adjacent layer updates through shared low-rank matrices, and adds a parameter-free bilateral filtering step to handle the spatial receptive-field issue unique to convolutional kernels, distinguishing it from Conv-Adapter which inserts adapters without modeling inter-layer correlation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that ConvNet fine-tuning is essentially a full-rank process (unlike Transformers where low-rank suffices) reframes why naive LoRA fails on ConvNets and provides a principled explanation grounded in gradient structure. This matters practically because ConvNets remain the dominant architecture for dense prediction tasks (segmentation, detection) where PEFT is most needed, and CoLoRA's correlated update strategy offers a template for any hierarchical architecture with strong inter-layer dependencies. Open questions include whether the correlation modeling generalizes to 3D convolutions, vision-language models, and whether the full-rank property persists under different pre-training regimes (e.g., self-supervised vs. supervised).
