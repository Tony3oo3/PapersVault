## Problem

Existing imaging system metrics (resolution, SNR, sampling rate) characterize individual aspects separately and cannot compare systems making different trade-offs, while decoder-based evaluation requires ground truth and conflates encoder and decoder performance. No practical method existed to directly quantify how much object information survives in noisy measurements without assuming object properties or ignoring encoder physical constraints.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253291/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The estimator consumes only noisy measurement patches (~20×20 pixels, 1,000–10,000 patches) and a noise model (e.g., Poisson shot noise or Gaussian), requiring no clean reference images or object labels. It outputs an upper bound on mutual information I(X;Y) between noiseless images and measurements. For IDEAL design, the method additionally takes a differentiable encoder model and a dataset of objects, and outputs optimized encoder parameters (e.g., color filter array layout) that maximize the information estimate.

## Method

The key decomposition is I(X;Y) = H(Y) − H(Y|X), where the deterministic encoder assumption (X = f(O)) justifies replacing object-measurement information with image-measurement information. H(Y|X) is computed analytically from the known noise model under independent-pixel noise, reducing a high-dimensional integral to a sum of single-pixel entropies. H(Y) is upper-bounded by fitting a parametric density model (stationary Gaussian, full-covariance Gaussian, or autoregressive PixelCNN) to training patches and computing cross-entropy on held-out test patches, so the information estimate is always an upper bound on the true value. For design, IDEAL performs gradient ascent on this information estimate with respect to differentiable encoder parameters, avoiding the memory and backpropagation challenges of jointly training a neural network decoder.

## Results

Across four imaging domains (color photography, radio astronomy, lensless imaging, and LED-array microscopy), mutual information estimates consistently predicted decoder reconstruction quality (MSE, PSNR, SSIM) without requiring ground truth or reconstruction algorithms. IDEAL (Information-Driven Encoder Analysis Learning) achieved comparable information content and decoder reconstruction error to end-to-end joint encoder-decoder optimization [61] while optimizing only the encoder. The three measurement models span a practical trade-off: Stationary Gaussian trains in ~0.1–10 s with lowest expressivity, full-covariance Gaussian in ~0.1 s with medium expressivity, and PixelCNN in ~100 s with highest expressivity (lowest entropy bounds).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is end-to-end joint encoder-decoder optimization (Chakrabarti, NeurIPS 2016 [61]), which simultaneously trains color filter array parameters and a neural-network demosaicing decoder. IDEAL matches this baseline on both downstream reconstruction error and measurement mutual information for color filter design (Fig. 3c), while avoiding the memory overhead and backpropagation challenges of training a large decoder network. The paper also validates its information estimates against decoder-based evaluation across all four imaging applications: in each case, higher mutual information estimates consistently corresponded to better reconstruction accuracy (lower MSE, higher PSNR/SSIM) or classification accuracy, confirming that the information metric captures the same performance ordering as task-specific decoders. A secondary comparison among the three measurement models shows PixelCNN consistently achieves the lowest entropy bounds (most accurate estimates) on real data, while Gaussian models plateau at higher values due to their inability to capture non-Gaussian statistics; however, for approximately Gaussian data the performance gap narrows, making simpler models reasonable under compute constraints.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from two prior lines of information-theoretic imaging analysis: communication-theoretic approaches that model imaging as unconstrained channels (overestimating capacity by ignoring physical encoder constraints) and object-model-based approaches that require unverifiable assumptions about scene statistics. It also contrasts with the prevailing decoder-based evaluation paradigm and end-to-end joint optimization, both of which require ground truth or a trained decoder. The key methodological repurposing is applying density estimation (Gaussian processes, PixelCNN) to upper-bound measurement entropy H(Y) from noisy measurements alone, combined with a tractable noise-model term H(Y|X), to obtain a decoder-free mutual information estimate. IDEAL then uses this estimate as a differentiable objective for encoder-only gradient ascent, avoiding the backpropagation-through-decoder challenges that limit end-to-end methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes mutual information as a universal, decoder-free performance metric validated across four diverse imaging domains (color photography, radio astronomy, lensless imaging, microscopy), enabling evaluation in real-world settings where ground truth is unavailable. IDEAL's ability to match end-to-end optimization on color filter design suggests a path to designing previously intractable systems by eliminating decoder memory and training complexity. Key open questions include: whether the upper-bound nature of the estimate (from cross-entropy) introduces systematic bias that affects optimization, how well the framework extends to stochastic encoders and task-specific objectives, and whether the IDEAL result generalizes beyond the single application demonstrated. The task-specific information gap (Section 4.1) is a notable limitation for classification-oriented imaging tasks.
