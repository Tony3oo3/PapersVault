## Problem

Current VLMs struggle with fine-grained spatial reasoning that requires multi-step logic and precise spatial alignment, particularly in scenarios with occlusions and relative orientations. Standard DPO applies a single global trade-off parameter uniformly across all reasoning segments, causing the model to overfit to simpler descriptive responses while under-optimizing complex multi-hop reasoning paths. Additionally, high-quality LongCoT supervision for spatial reasoning is scarce, making it difficult to train models that produce interpretable, step-by-step spatial deductions.

## Input / Output

The model (built on Sa2VA/InternVL2.5, 4B and 8B variants) takes an RGB image, a text query specifying the spatial task, and visual prompt tokens that explicitly point to target objects or regions, producing a structured LongCoT trace with markdown-style section headers (Description, Rationale, Step N, In Conclusion) that decompose the answer into descriptive grounding and logical reasoning segments. Depth maps generated by Depth Anything are used during reward evaluation and data curation but are not fed as model input. The final output includes both the full reasoning chain and a concrete spatial answer (e.g., a distance in meters, a clock-face direction, or a qualitative relation).

## Method

The core contribution is fDPO, which segments each LongCoT response into a description component (R_desc) and a reasoning component (R_reason), then assigns each segment its own dynamically tuned trade-off parameter (β_desc, β_reason) computed from segment-specific preference differentials via a softmax weighting scheme, so that harder-to-learn reasoning segments receive amplified learning signals. Training data is generated by M3CTS, a Multi-Model Monte Carlo Tree Search that expands candidate reasoning states using multiple VLMs concurrently, evaluates them with a fine-grained spatial reward (Visual Consistency, Depth-Guided Spatial, and Logical Coherence scores), prunes low-quality paths, and selects the next state via UCB. The resulting preference pairs (400K from the Open Spatial Reasoning dataset plus 100K conclusion-perturbed negatives) are used to train the model in two stages: SFT followed by fDPO, with the composite reward score(R_desc) = R_vc + R_sp,desc and score(R_reason) = R_lc + R_sp,reason driving the segment-wise preference margins in the fDPO loss.

## Results

On SPATIALRGPT-BENCH, SpatialReasoner-R1 fDPO 8B achieves 95.59% qualitative accuracy and 77.30% quantitative accuracy, setting a new state-of-the-art. This represents a 9.4% average accuracy improvement over the strongest baseline (SpatialRGPT-8B: 92.69% qual., 61.42% quan.). The fDPO variant also outperforms its DPO counterpart by 4.1% on qualitative tasks and 9.0% on quantitative tasks, demonstrating the benefit of segment-specific preference granularity. On general vision-language benchmarks (Table 2), fDPO 8B surpasses SpatialRGPT-8B across all eight datasets, e.g., 76.21 vs. 67.00 on SEED-Bench and 55.43 vs. 43.98 on MMStar.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SpatialReasoner-R1 fDPO 8B outperforms all nine baselines on both qualitative and quantitative spatial reasoning. Against the strongest spatial-specialized baseline SpatialRGPT-8B (92.69% qual., 61.42% quan.), fDPO 8B gains +2.9% qualitative and +15.8% quantitative accuracy. Among general large VLMs, the gap is even larger: Gemini 2.0 Flash scores only 44.29% qual. and 22.43% quan., while ChatGPT-4o achieves 66.67% qual. but refuses quantitative answers entirely. The parameter-efficient fDPO 4B variant (87.37% qual., 66.76% quan.) surpasses the much larger InternVL2.5-78B (72.29% qual., 35.25% quan.), demonstrating that the fine-grained preference training strategy is more effective than raw model scale. On general vision-language benchmarks, fDPO 8B also dominates SpatialRGPT-8B across all eight datasets (e.g., +9.21 on SEED-Bench, +11.45 on MMStar, +10.30 on HallusionBench), indicating that spatial reasoning training does not degrade general capabilities.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023) by introducing segment-specific preference granularity (fDPO) that applies distinct optimization strengths to descriptive grounding versus logical reasoning within LongCoT traces, departing from prior token-, step-, and sentence-level DPO variants that treat semantic roles uniformly. The base architecture builds on Sa2VA (Yuan et al., 2025), which itself combines SAM2 with InternVL2.5 for dense grounded understanding. For data generation, M3CTS adapts multi-LLM Monte Carlo Tree Search (as in Mulberry and MoSA) to the spatial reasoning domain, adding a fine-grained spatial reward mechanism that evaluates visual consistency, depth-verified spatial grounding, and logical coherence simultaneously.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Spatial reasoning remains a critical bottleneck for VLMs in safety-sensitive applications like autonomous driving and robotics, and this work demonstrates that decomposing preference optimization by semantic segment (description vs. reasoning) yields meaningful gains—particularly on quantitative spatial tasks where multi-step arithmetic is required. The 4B model outperforming 78B baselines suggests that targeted preference training can compensate for parameter scale in narrow reasoning domains. Key limitations include reliance on explicit region prompts for object disambiguation, dependence on depth maps (via Depth Anything) for reward computation, and restriction to 2D spatial reasoning; extending to implicit grounding and 3D/embodied settings remains open.
