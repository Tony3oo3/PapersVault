## Problem

Graph pre-training methods struggle to scale data across domains because they require in-domain validation or task-specific data selection, and heterogeneous features/structures across graphs cause negative transfer. This paper asks how to effectively leverage the increasing scale of graph data across diverse domains without being constrained by feature or structural alignment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254120/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pre-training phase consumes only graph adjacency matrices (structure) from thousands of unlabeled graphs, using node degrees as the sole node feature to sidestep feature heterogeneity. In the downstream phase, the method takes a target graph's original node features plus a task-specific guidance objective (graph labels, node labels, node degrees, Common Neighbors, or a link-prediction score) and produces a synthetic adjacency matrix that is spliced with the original features to form the augmented graph. The augmented graph is then fed to a plug-and-play downstream GNN (GIN, GCN, NCN, etc.) for the actual prediction task.

## Method

UniAug pre-trains a self-conditioned discrete (Bernoulli) diffusion model on graph structures using a Graph Transformer denoiser conditioned on node degrees, diffusion timestep, and self-assigned cluster labels derived from graph-level properties (density, entropy, degree variance, scale-free exponent). The self-conditioning via clustering on unlabeled graphs enables the model to learn diverse structural patterns without requiring annotations. For downstream augmentation, an MLP guidance head is trained on task-specific objectives, and the NOS gradient-based guidance method steers the reverse diffusion via Langevin dynamics to align generated structures with downstream requirements. The key design choice is operating exclusively on structure (not features), which eliminates cross-domain feature incompatibility, while the guided generation bridges the gap between the broad pre-training distribution and the specific downstream data distribution.

## Results

On graph classification across 9 datasets, UniAug achieves an average rank of 1.11, consistently outperforming the GIN baseline by 2.3–5.5 points (e.g., 78.13% vs 75.81% on DD, 71.50% vs 66.00% on Enzymes, 92.28% vs 90.85% on Reddit-B). On link prediction, UniAug-GCN improves MRR over GCN on all 7 datasets (e.g., 35.36 vs 30.26 on Cora, 39.67 vs 35.71 on Erdos), and UniAug-NCN similarly boosts the NCN backbone. On heterophilic node classification, UniAug achieves an average rank of 2.00 versus GCN's 4.50, with the combination UniAug+Half-Hop reaching 72.43% on Cornell and 79.61% on Wisconsin.

## Baselines & Comparisons

All seven pre-training baselines (AttrMask, CtxtPred, EdgePred, InfoMax, JOAO, D-SLA, GraphMAE) suffer severe negative transfer when pre-trained on the same cross-domain graph collection, collapsing to ~22% on Enzymes versus GIN's 66.00% due to feature heterogeneity. GDA methods (S-Mixup, GraphAug, FLAG) produce inconsistent results: GraphAug achieves the best GDA average rank (3.67) but still trails UniAug by 2–5 points on most datasets, while FLAG degrades on IMDB-B (68.83% vs GIN's 71.10%). On link prediction, UniAug-GCN outperforms GCN on all 7 datasets (e.g., +5.1 MRR on Cora, +3.96 Hits@10 on Erdos) and also improves the stronger NCN backbone (e.g., 40.30 vs 38.26 MRR on Pubmed). On heterophilic node classification, UniAug beats GCN on 5 of 6 datasets (e.g., +8.70 on Cornell, +4.73 on Chameleon), and combining UniAug with Half-Hop yields the best results overall (72.43% Cornell, 79.61% Wisconsin). UniAug's plug-and-play design is validated by consistent gains when paired with both GCN and NCN for link prediction, and with PIN and PolyGCL for graph/node classification, confirming task-agnostic applicability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the discrete diffusion framework of D3PM (Austin et al., 2021) to model graph adjacency matrices as Bernoulli processes, and adapts the NOS guided-generation technique (Gruver et al., 2024) from protein design to graph structure augmentation. It departs fundamentally from existing graph pre-training methods (GraphMAE, JOAO, D-SLA) that require in-domain data filtering and from task-specific GDA methods (GraphAug, CFLP, Half-Hop) that cannot transfer across domains. The key conceptual departure is a structure-only, cross-domain pre-training paradigm where a self-conditioned diffusion model learns universal graph topology patterns, then performs plug-and-play augmentation via guided generation—decoupling the upstream generative model from any downstream inductive bias.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that graph data scaling across domains is feasible through a structure-only diffusion augmentor, showing consistent gains on 25 datasets spanning 7 domains and three task granularities. The finding that all standard pre-training methods suffer negative transfer when pre-trained on the same cross-domain collection, while UniAug consistently improves, challenges the assumption that more pre-training data always helps for graphs. Limitations include the absence of model-parameter scaling analysis, reliance on iterative Langevin sampling (computationally expensive), and the restriction to structure-only augmentation that cannot capture feature-level patterns. Open questions include fast sampling for discrete graph diffusion, whether the approach extends to truly large-scale graphs, and how to incorporate heterogeneous features without the current dimension-mismatch issues.
