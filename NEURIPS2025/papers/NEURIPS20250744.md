## Problem

Existing VLM benchmarks rely on well-specified inputs, closed-set taxonomies, and short-form answers, failing to capture the underspecified, context-rich, decision-centric nature of real-world expert consultations. In agriculture, farmers present ambiguous, visually grounded queries where success requires causal reasoning, handling missing context, and deciding whether to seek clarification or provide actionable guidance—capabilities untested by current evaluation frameworks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250744/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each MMST instance pairs a natural-language question (often elliptical or underspecified) with 1–3 real user-submitted photos of varying quality and lighting, plus optional location and timestamp metadata; the model must produce a structured response containing identified biological entities, a causal explanation of observed symptoms, and management recommendations. The MMMT variant additionally takes multi-turn dialogue history and requires the model to jointly predict a clarify-or-respond decision and generate the corresponding expert utterance, with outputs scored by an ensemble of three reasoning LLMs on accuracy, relevance, completeness, and diagnostic parsimony (0–4 scale).

## Method

MIRAGE is constructed from 218K real user-expert consultations on AskExtension through a four-step curation pipeline: filtering incomplete or uncertain responses, GPT-4.1-based categorization into seven agronomic categories with entity extraction, reformatting with PII removal and URL-content integration, and stratified splitting into 8,184 Standard, 3,934 Contextual, and 17,532 training samples spanning 7,000+ biological entities. Evaluation uses an ensemble of three reasoning-capable LLMs (DeepSeek-R1-Distilled, Qwen3-32B, Phi-4-Reasoning) as judges, each scoring three independent model generations for nine total evaluations per sample, with inter-rater reliability validated via Fleiss' κ (0.75–0.88) and Kendall's W. For the MMMT decision task, the model must infer a latent goal state (known vs. missing information) from dialogue history and select between Clarify and Respond, a formulation the authors show generalizes to other consultative domains. They further demonstrate the benchmark's utility as a development suite by LoRA-fine-tuning Qwen2.5-VL models (3B–32B) on the training split, revealing a persistent ~14-point accuracy gap between seen and unseen entities that underscores the open-world generalization challenge.

## Results

On MIRAGE-MMST-ID, GPT-4.1 achieves the highest Identification Accuracy at 43.9% and a Reasoning Score of 3.01/4, while the best open-source model (Qwen2.5-VL-72B) reaches only 29.8% accuracy and 2.47 reasoning. On the MMST-MG management task, GPT-4.1 attains a composite W-Sum of 0.82 (Accuracy 3.24, Parsimony 3.01), compared to 0.71 for the best open-source model (Qwen2.5-VL-32B). On MIRAGE-MMMT, GPT-4o achieves 62.98% Decision Accuracy in zero-shot and 65.5% with CoT, while the best open-source model (LLaMA 4 Maverick) reaches 53.75%/59.8%. After LoRA fine-tuning, Qwen2.5-VL-3B improves seen-entity accuracy from 22.3% to 28.4% but only reaches 14.6% on unseen entities, revealing a persistent 14-point generalization gap.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPT-4.1 dominates all 21 competing models on both MMST tasks, achieving 43.9% ID accuracy versus 29.8% for the best open-source model (Qwen2.5-VL-72B), a 14.1-point gap, and a W-Sum of 0.82 versus 0.71 for Qwen2.5-VL-32B on the management task. Among proprietary models, GPT-4.1-mini (34.6% ID) and Claude 3.7 Sonnet (33.9% ID) trail GPT-4.1 by roughly 10 points in identification accuracy, while GPT-4o (39.3%) is closer but still 4.6 points behind. Within open-source families, Qwen2.5-VL shows the strongest scaling behavior (17.2%→29.8% from 3B to 72B), outperforming Gemma-3 (10.4%→18.9%) and InternVL3 (9.0%→22.4%) at comparable scales, though none approach GPT-4.1. On the multi-turn MMMT task, GPT-4o leads with 62.98% Decision Accuracy (zero-shot), ahead of LLaMA 4 Maverick (53.75%) and LLaMA 4 Scout (49.81%), with CoT prompting yielding the largest gains for Claude 3.7 Sonnet (+10.5% Clarify Relevance) and LLaMA 4 Scout (+6.19% Decision Accuracy). Notably, even after LoRA fine-tuning on MIRAGE training data, Qwen2.5-VL-3B reaches only 28.4% on seen entities versus 14.6% on unseen entities, confirming that open-world generalization over rare biological entities remains unsolved across all evaluated model families.

## Relation to Prior Work

MIRAGE synthesizes ideas from multimodal VQA (MMMU, AgMMU), fine-grained biological classification (TreeOfLife, iNat21), multi-turn dialogue (CROP, MultiWOZ), and LLM-as-a-Judge evaluation (AlpacaEval, G-Eval) into a single benchmark grounded in real expert consultations. It departs from prior agricultural benchmarks by replacing synthetic MCQs with open-ended, underspecified queries and by introducing a joint clarify-or-respond decision task drawn from dialogue state tracking literature. The evaluation protocol borrows the ensemble-judge paradigm from long-form QA evaluation but applies it to a domain where ground truth is expert-authored long-form text rather than a single correct answer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MIRAGE exposes a concrete and actionable failure mode: current frontier VLMs essentially ignore location and time metadata that is critical for agricultural reasoning, and fine-tuning yields a persistent 14-point seen/unseen entity gap that suggests memorization over generalizable visual reasoning. The clarify-or-respond formulation generalizes beyond agriculture to healthcare triage, technical support, and legal advising, making the benchmark a template for evaluating consultative AI in any knowledge-intensive domain. Key open questions include whether retrieval-augmented or domain-adaptive pretraining can close the open-world gap, and whether dynamic multi-turn interaction (beyond the current static dialogue snapshots) reveals additional failure modes.
