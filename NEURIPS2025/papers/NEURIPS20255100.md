## Problem

Feed-forward 3D reconstruction models (DUSt3R, MASt3R, CUT3R, VGGT) consistently produce over-smoothed fine structures, blurred object boundaries, and inaccurate transparent/glossy surfaces, lagging behind state-of-the-art monocular geometry estimators. The paper identifies two root causes: (1) scarcity of high-fidelity depth and pose supervision in existing training data, and (2) inherent geometric misalignment from multi-view pointmap regression, including coupled pose-depth prediction, progressive drift, and scale uncertainty that erodes foreground detail along epipolar lines.

## Input / Output

The method operates on the same inputs as the base models—single RGB images or multi-view sequences at 512/518 pixel width—and produces 3D pointmaps in a reference-frame coordinate system, depth maps, camera poses, and cross-view correspondences. The fine-tuning adds only a tiny LoRA adapter (rank 8, alpha 8) to the encoder, leaving test-time memory and latency virtually unchanged. During training, the encoder is distilled using MoGe pseudo-labels generated on 20,000 SA-1B images per epoch, supplemented by 1,000 Hypersim and 1,000 TartanAir samples for multi-view replay.

## Method

Fin3R freezes the decoder (which handles cross-view matching) and fine-tunes only the image encoder via a lightweight LoRA adapter, distilling fine geometric detail from the MoGe monocular teacher on the diverse SA-1B dataset. The key technical challenge is that naive encoder distillation causes a continuous increase in feature L2 norms, pushing activations outside the range expected by the frozen decoder and degrading multi-view matching. To counteract this, the paper embeds a re-normalization operation within each LoRA block that rescales the combined weight (W + ΔW) to preserve the original norm ‖W‖₂, dynamically correcting feature drift. Multi-view data replay with a pointmap regression loss (gated by an indicator function) further anchors the encoder to its original multi-view distribution. Training runs for 10 epochs on four NVIDIA L20 GPUs over a single day, using a combined objective of monocular distillation loss (with aleatoric uncertainty weighting) and pointmap regression loss.

## Results

On monocular depth estimation (Table 1), Fin3R consistently reduces average relative error across all four baselines: VGGT+Ours achieves 4.29 Rel / 96.7 δ₁ (vs. VGGT 5.77 / 94.0), DUSt3R+Ours reaches 5.58 / 94.8 (vs. 7.03 / 92.3), and CUT3R+Ours reaches 5.59 / 94.7 (vs. 6.46 / 92.9), approaching the MoGe teacher (4.14 / 96.9). On ScanNet relative pose (Table 2), fine-tuned VGGT jumps from 28.40 to 35.21 AUC@5, surpassing the dedicated pose-regression model Reloc3r (34.79). Multi-view performance is preserved or improved: on RealEstate10k, VGGT+Ours gains +1.0 RRA@5 and +3.4 RTA@5 over the baseline, and on 7-Scenes pointmap regression, VGGT+Ours reduces mean accuracy from 0.017 to 0.012.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Fin3R is applied as a plug-in fine-tuning step to four feed-forward reconstruction baselines (DUSt3R, MASt3R, CUT3R, VGGT) and consistently improves all of them. Against the MoGe teacher, fine-tuned VGGT nearly closes the gap (4.29 vs. 4.14 average Rel), while DUSt3R and CUT3R remain ~1.4 Rel behind the teacher but improve by 1.4–1.5 Rel over their own baselines. On ScanNet relative pose, VGGT+Ours (35.21 AUC@5) outperforms Reloc3r (34.79), a model purpose-built for pose regression, and also beats MASt3R (37.60) at AUC@10/20. On multi-view pose (RealEstate10k), all four fine-tuned models improve over their baselines by 0.3–3.4 points on RTA@5, confirming that encoder distillation does not degrade the decoder's matching capability. On pointmap regression (7-Scenes), VGGT+Ours achieves the best mean accuracy (0.012) among all methods tested, outperforming both the VGGT baseline (0.017) and DUSt3R+Ours (0.024). The concurrent Pi3 model is matched or slightly outperformed on DTU/ETH3D pointmap metrics while requiring far less fine-tuning compute.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as a lightweight fine-tuning layer atop the family of feed-forward 3D reconstruction models (DUSt3R, MASt3R, CUT3R, VGGT), which share a common encoder-decoder architecture for pointmap regression. It departs from prior monocular-prior approaches (Align3R, Pow3R, Mono3R) that inject external depth estimators at inference time, and from test-time optimization methods (LoRA3D, Test3R) that require per-scene adaptation, by instead performing a single universal training-time distillation from MoGe into the encoder only. The technical core builds on LoRA but introduces a re-normalization constraint on the combined weight matrix to counteract feature norm drift, a modification not present in standard LoRA or prior 3D fine-tuning work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method offers a practical, low-cost recipe (one day on 4 GPUs, tiny LoRA weights) that consistently sharpens geometry across four architecturally distinct feed-forward models without altering test-time memory or latency, making it immediately adoptable by practitioners working with any of these popular backbones. Key limitations remain: boundary accuracy still trails the MoGe teacher, the re-normalization LoRA cannot be merged into the base weights (unlike vanilla LoRA), and the approach is capped at 512/518 resolution. Open questions include whether mixed-teacher strategies or higher-resolution training could close the remaining gap to dedicated monocular estimators, and whether the encoder-only constraint is truly optimal or whether a carefully regularized decoder update could yield further multi-view gains.
