## Problem

No prior work has attempted to watermark autoregressive image generation models at the token level, despite the growing deployment of such models and their potential for misuse. The key technical obstacle is the lack of reverse cycle-consistency (RCC): re-tokenizing a generated image yields a token sequence that differs substantially from the original (only ~66% token match), which would effectively erase any token-level watermark. This stands in sharp contrast to text, where BPE tokenizers maintain near-perfect RCC (~99.5% match).

## Input / Output

The autoregressive model receives a text prompt (CHAMELEON, 512×512 images) or an ImageNet class label (TAMING, RAR-XL, 256×256 images) and generates interleaved text and image tokens. During generation, the KGW watermark biases logits of green tokens by δ=2 with γ=0.25 green proportion. For detection, a suspected image is re-tokenized (after optional RCC-finetuned encoder) and scored; the output is a single p-value computed via an exact binomial test, which can be aggregated across multiple samples and modalities. The watermarked image is imperceptibly altered (FID increase <0.6) while the p-value provides a theoretically grounded confidence measure.

## Method

The paper adapts the KGW green/red LLM watermark to image tokens and addresses the RCC challenge with two complementary mechanisms. First, a lightweight finetuning procedure jointly optimizes the VQ decoder D and an encoder replica E′ (initialized from the original encoder) to minimize the distance between original hard latents and re-encoded latents after detokenization, with value-metric and weak geometric augmentations sampled during training to build robustness; a regularization loss (MSE + LPIPS) preserves decoder quality. Second, a post-hoc watermark synchronization layer embeds four 32-bit localized messages (via Sander et al.) into the four image quadrants; at detection time, an algorithm searches over rotation angles and fits separating lines to identify and revert geometric transformations (flips, rotations, crops) before applying the original KGW detector. The two components are complementary: RCC finetuning handles value-metric distortions and adversarial attacks (diffusion purification, neural compression), while synchronization handles geometric transformations that would otherwise scramble token positions.

## Results

On TAMING (256×256), the full method (FT+AUGS+SYNC) achieves TPR of 0.98 (unmodified), 0.83 (valuemetric), 0.82 (geometric), 0.69 (adversarial purification), and 0.80 (neural compression) at 1% FPR. Generation quality is preserved: FID remains at 17.3 vs. 16.7 for the unwatermarked model. On CHAMELEON (512×512) and RAR-XL (256×256), the method similarly achieves TPR ≥ 0.76 across all transformation categories, demonstrating transferability across model architectures and tokenizer codebook sizes (1024 to 16384).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against four post-hoc image watermarking baselines (CIN, MBRS, TRUSTMARK, WAM) applied on top of generated images. The proposed method (FT+AUGS+SYNC) is the only approach that maintains TPR above 0.6 across all four transformation categories simultaneously on TAMING; every post-hoc baseline collapses below 0.4 in at least one category. On adversarial purification, the proposed method (0.69) substantially outperforms TRUSTMARK (0.40), MBRS (0.36), WAM (0.06), and CIN (0.03). On neural compression, the proposed method (0.80) is competitive with TRUSTMARK (0.86) but far exceeds MBRS (0.31), WAM (0.02), and CIN (0.02). On valuemetric transformations, post-hoc methods (CIN 0.96, MBRS 0.98, TRUSTMARK 0.98) slightly outperform the proposed method (0.83), and on geometric transformations WAM (0.98) and TRUSTMARK (0.75) are competitive with the proposed method (0.82). The key advantage of the proposed method is its balanced robustness profile and theoretically grounded p-values, whereas post-hoc methods rely on neural extractors that may introduce bias in their p-value estimators.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the KGW green/red LLM watermarking scheme (Kirchenbauer et al., ICML 2023) to autoregressive image token generation, and repurposes localized watermarking (Sander et al., ICLR 2025) as a geometric synchronization signal. It departs from prior LLM watermarking by identifying reverse cycle-consistency (RCC) as a fundamental obstacle: image VQ tokenizers, trained for forward cycle-consistency, do not reliably re-tokenize their own detokenized outputs, causing ~34% token mismatch even without any transformation. Unlike post-hoc image watermarks (Trustmark, MBRS, WAM) that operate on pixels, this is a generation-time, token-level method that inherits the theoretical p-value guarantees of LLM watermarking while being specific to the autoregressive image generation pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first token-level watermark for autoregressive image generation, a setting that is increasingly important as models like Chameleon, Janus, and AnyGPT unify text and image generation in a single transformer. The RCC insight is a practical warning for anyone attempting to port LLM watermarking to other discrete-token modalities. Key open questions include robustness to combined geometric+removal attacks, support for arbitrary crops, improving audio time-frequency robustness, and whether the approach extends to continuous-representation or hybrid autoregressive-diffusion models. The method is also limited to VQ-based tokenizers and requires access to the tokenizer for detection, which constrains its deployment in fully black-box settings.
