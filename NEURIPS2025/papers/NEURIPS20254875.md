## Problem

Existing vision-language connectors (MLPs, Perceiver Resampler, convolutional projectors) lack inductive bias to keep projected visual features within the region spanned by the LLM's pretrained text embeddings, causing cross-modal misalignment, out-of-distribution inputs, and poor data efficiency. This is especially problematic for document understanding tasks where visual and textual content are tightly coupled, and under low-resource training regimes where connectors must learn alignment from limited data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254875/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts high-resolution document images (up to 2304×2304) that are tiled into up to 9 aspect-ratio-matched tiles, each further partitioned into 14×14 pixel patches processed by a SigLIP-400M vision encoder, alongside a free-form text prompt. The LLM (Llama 3.2-1B/3B or Llama 3.1-8B) generates answers auto-regressively. A notable design detail is that each visual patch is represented not as a free-form vector but as a probability distribution over the LLM's 128K-token vocabulary, which is then collapsed into a single embedding via a weighted sum of text embeddings.

## Method

The ALIGN connector replaces direct feature projection with a two-step mapping: a linear layer W₁ projects vision-encoder features into the LLM's token-embedding dimensionality, and a second linear layer W₂ (initialized from the LLM's own language-model head) followed by a softmax produces a probability simplex over the full vocabulary. The aligned visual feature is then computed as the convex combination of the LLM's text embeddings weighted by this simplex, guaranteeing that every visual token lies inside the convex hull of the text embedding space. This initialization of W₂ from the LM head injects an inductive bias that recycles existing token semantics into the visual pathway. Training proceeds in three stages—CC-12M image-text pretraining for alignment, BigDocs-7.5M for document-specific capabilities, and DocDownstream for instruction tuning—with the full model trained in stages 1–2 and only the LLM plus connector in stage 3. The paper further shows that ALIGN effectively relies on only ~3.4K high-probability vocabulary tokens, enabling potential embedding pruning without performance loss.

## Results

ALIGNVLM achieves state-of-the-art performance on multimodal document understanding, with the 8B model (ALIGNVLM-Llama-3.1-8B) reaching an average score of 62.88% across 9 document benchmarks (DocVQA, InfoVQA, DeepForm, KLC, WTQ, TabFact, ChartQA, TextVQA, TableVQA). The 3B model (ALIGNVLM-Llama-3.2-3B) achieves 58.81% average, outperforming the 8B-parameter DocOwl1.5-8B base model (57.56%) and the 11B-parameter Llama3.2-11B base model (60.26%) by 1.25% and 2.62% respectively under identical training conditions. In the low-resource regime (779K samples), ALIGN achieves 59.32% average on document tasks versus 48.56% for Ovis and 40.61% for MLP, demonstrating a 10.76% advantage over the next-best connector. The ALIGN connector also shows exceptional noise robustness, with only a 1.67% performance drop under Gaussian noise (σ=3) compared to a 25.54% drop for the MLP connector.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Base VLMs trained on identical data, ALIGNVLM-Llama-3.2-3B (58.81% avg) outperforms Qwen2-VL-2B base (49.59%) by 9.22%, DocOwl1.5-8B base (57.56%) by 1.25% despite having 5B fewer parameters, and Llama3.2-11B base (60.26%) by 2.62% with 3B fewer parameters. Against SOTA Instruct VLMs, ALIGNVLM-Llama-3.2-3B beats Qwen2-VL-2B instruct (55.84%) by 2.97% and InternVL-3-2B (57.64%) by 1.17%, while trailing Qwen2.5-VL-3B (65.10%) by 6.29% (though the latter uses undisclosed proprietary training data). The 8B ALIGNVLM model surpasses InternVL-2.5-8B (62.58%) by 0.30%, Pixtral-12B (58.03%) by 4.85%, and Llama3.2-11B instruct (34.04%) by 28.84%, while remaining competitive with GPT-4o (64.91%) and outperforming GeminiPro-1.5 (58.46%) by 4.42%. In the connector ablation (Table 2), ALIGN (58.81%) outperforms MLP (53.06%), Perceiver Resampler (50.68%), and Ovis (54.72%) under identical high-resource training. Under low-resource conditions (Table 3), the advantage widens: ALIGN achieves 59.32% on document tasks versus 48.56% (Ovis), 47.16% (HoneyBee), 40.61% (MLP), 36.23% (H-Reducer), and 33.64% (Perceiver), confirming its superior data efficiency.

## Relation to Prior Work

ALIGNVLM departs from the standard shallow-fusion connector paradigm (MLP, Perceiver Resampler, convolution-based projectors) by constraining visual features to the convex hull of the LLM's pretrained text embeddings rather than projecting them into an unconstrained or newly-learned space. Conceptually, it is closest to Ovis's softmax-over-embeddings mechanism, but replaces Ovis's separate visual embedding table with the LLM's own text embeddings, making W2W1 a low-rank factorization of Ovis's projection while adding the critical geometric constraint that outputs remain within the text embedding simplex. This positions the work as a targeted inductive-bias intervention within the well-established VLM connector design space, rather than a new fusion paradigm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The convex-hull constraint provides a simple, parameter-light mechanism that yields dramatically better noise robustness (1.67% vs. 25.54% performance drop) and stronger low-data efficiency, making it practically relevant for resource-constrained deployments. The finding that only ~3.4K of 128K vocabulary embeddings carry meaningful probability mass suggests further computational savings through embedding pruning. However, the evaluation is heavily skewed toward document understanding benchmarks, and generalization to broader visual reasoning (e.g., natural image VQA, video) remains underexplored; the paper itself notes future work on more diverse instruction-tuning data.
