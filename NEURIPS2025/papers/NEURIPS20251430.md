## Problem

Off-policy evaluation (OPE) algorithms such as FQE and importance sampling each carry their own hyperparameters, creating a chicken-and-egg problem: who selects among the OPE instances themselves? Prior selectors like BVFT address the double-sampling issue but suffer a slow n^{-1/4} convergence rate and require heuristic discretization. Additionally, existing experimental protocols for benchmarking selectors rely on unstable FQE training to generate candidate Q-functions, limiting controllability and reproducibility.

## Input / Output

The selector receives an offline dataset of n i.i.d. transition tuples (s, a, r, s'), a finite set of m candidate Q-functions (model-free) or candidate MDPs (model-based), and a target policy π, and outputs the index of the best candidate along with the performance estimate J(π). A key constraint is that the selector must be hyperparameter-free: the feature map is constructed entirely from the candidates themselves (φ_{i,j} = [Q_i(s,a), Q_j(s,a)]^T), requiring no external function class or tuning. In the model-based setting, the selector additionally accesses Bellman-operator quantities (T_{M_j}^π Q_{M_i}^π)(s,a) computed via Monte-Carlo rollouts across model pairs.

## Method

LSTD-Tournament converts the LSTDQ linear estimator into a pairwise-comparison subroutine following the BVFT recipe: for candidates Q_i and Q_j, the feature φ_{i,j} = [Q_i(s,a), Q_j(s,a)]^T makes Q^π linearly realizable with θ* ∈ {[1,0]^T, [0,1]^T}, so the LSTDQ loss ‖Aθ − b‖_∞ evaluated at each basis vector yields a double-sampling-free Bellman-violation score. Generalizing to m candidates via a tournament (taking the max over all opponents) gives the final loss L(Q_i) = max_k |E_μ[Q_k(s,a)(Q_i(s,a) − r − γQ_i(s',π))]|, where the other candidates act as instruments that bypass the double-sampling problem without auxiliary function approximation. This yields an n^{-1/2} rate (vs. BVFT's n^{-1/4}) and a simple, closed-form implementation. For the model-based setting, the paper further develops a regression-based selector that auto-constructs the helper class G_i = {T_{M_j}^π Q_i} from the candidate models, and a novel sign-flip selector replacing squared loss with absolute value. A new experimental protocol generates candidate models by varying environment parameters (gravity, noise) in Gym Hopper and computes all required Q-values via cached Monte-Carlo rollouts, eliminating the need for unstable FQE optimization.

## Results

The paper evaluates selectors on Gym Hopper (Hopper-v4) with n=3200 transition tuples, 15 candidate environments (varying gravity or noise), and 15 target policies from DDPG checkpoints. The primary metric is OPE error |J_M(π) − J_M*(π)|, reported as box plots across different groundtruth environments M*. LSTD-Tournament achieves the most consistently low OPE error across both gravity-grid (MF.G) and noise-grid (MF.N) settings, while BVFT's performance degenerates to match TD-squared, and the naive model-based selector fails catastrophically in high-noise environments (σ=55, 100). In the model-based comparison (m=5), LSTD-Tournament also outperforms the more complex regression-based and sign-flip model-based selectors, contrary to the expectation that additional model information should help.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LSTD-Tournament outperforms all four baselines across the main experiments. Against BVFT [64], LSTD-Tournament is more reliable: BVFT's OPE error mostly coincides with the naive TD-squared estimator, which the authors attribute to degeneration when n is insufficient for meaningful state-space discretization. Against the average Bellman error baseline [25] (equivalent to LSTD-Tournament with a trivial constant discriminator), LSTD-Tournament's use of candidate Q-functions as discriminators provides a clear advantage, particularly when the true environment shifts. In the model-based setting (m=5), LSTD-Tournament outperforms both the Zitovsky et al. [65] regression-based selector and the Antos et al. [6] regression variant, despite those methods having access to additional structural information from the candidate models. The paper notes this is surprising, as the model-based selectors were expected to benefit from the extra information in M, but LSTD-Tournament's simplicity and computational efficiency (O(m) vs. O(m²) rollouts) give it a practical edge.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on BVFT (Xie & Jiang 2021; Zhang & Jiang 2021), the only prior selector that addresses the double-sampling problem without additional function approximation, by replacing BVFT's base algorithm (Q^π-irrelevant abstractions) with LSTDQ to achieve a faster n^{-1/2} rate versus n^{-1/4}. For the model-based setting, it adapts regression-based ideas from Antos et al. (2008) and Zitovsky et al. (2023) by showing the helper function class G can be constructed automatically from the candidate model set, removing the need for user-specified hyperparameters. The experimental protocol departs from prior work (e.g., Nie et al. 2022, Paine et al. 2020) that uses FQE to generate candidate Q-functions, instead using Monte-Carlo rollouts from parameterized environment variants for optimization-free, controllable evaluation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a fundamental chicken-and-egg problem in offline RL deployment: if OPE methods have their own hyperparameters, who selects them? The LSTD-Tournament selector is hyperparameter-free and achieves standard statistical rates, making it a practical tool for the model-selection-for-OPE pipeline. Key open questions remain: the coverage assumption σ_min(A) inherited from LSTDQ is poorly characterized outside on-policy settings, and the protocol's candidate Q-functions (induced by uniform parameter shifts) may not capture the structured errors of real learning algorithms. The finding that simpler model-free methods outperform more information-rich model-based selectors challenges the intuition that additional structural knowledge should help, though the evidence is limited to a single environment family.
