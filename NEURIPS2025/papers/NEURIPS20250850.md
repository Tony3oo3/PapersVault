## Problem

Most GNN-based weather forecasting methods operate on fixed, regionally limited station networks, ignoring the fact that global station distributions are spatially irregular (concentrated in North America and Western Europe) and dynamically changing over time. This limits both prediction accuracy in sparse regions and the ability to generalize to previously unobserved stations, which is critical for practical deployment as new stations are added or decommissioned.

## Input / Output

The model consumes daily scalar weather observations (temperature, dew point, sea-level pressure, wind speed) from an irregularly distributed set of ~10,000 NOAA GSOD stations, each with longitude/latitude coordinates. The station set varies across time steps—different days have different numbers and locations of active stations—so the input graph topology is non-stationary. Output is the next-day scalar prediction at each station location, with the constraint that test-time stations may not have appeared during training.

## Method

MIGN follows an encoder–processor–decoder architecture built around a fixed HEALPix mesh (refinement level 3, ~768 nodes). The encoder performs one round of message passing from irregular station nodes to their nearest mesh nodes, effectively interpolating the sparse, uneven station data onto a uniform spherical grid. The processor then runs standard multi-layer GNN message passing on the fixed mesh, where spatial adjacency is well-defined and consistent across time steps. The decoder reverses the interpolation, aggregating mesh hidden states back to the (potentially different) station nodes for prediction. A key differentiator is the parametric spherical harmonics location embedding: instead of treating coordinates as fixed positional features, the model learns shared coefficients w_n^m over precomputed real spherical harmonic basis functions (degree 2, 9 basis functions), which are concatenated to node features in both encoder and decoder. This parametric encoding allows the model to represent weather as a learnable function on the sphere, enabling generalization to coordinates never seen during training.

## Results

On the NOAA GSOD dataset (2017–2024, ~10,000+ stations/day), MIGN achieves the lowest MSE across all six daily weather variables: 8.47 (MAX TEMP), 8.01 (MIN TEMP), 7.92 (DEWP), 20.09 (SLP), 8.38 (WDSP), and 19.73 (MXSPD). Relative to the strongest baseline, MIGN yields 13–15% MSE reductions on temperature and pressure variables and 3% on wind speed variables. In the multistep (3-day input → 4-day output) setting, MIGN maintains its lead, e.g., 15.47 total MSE on MAX TEMP vs. 16.82 for the best baseline. In the global generalization experiment (unseen test stations), MIGN achieves 8.55/8.05 MSE on MAX/MIN TEMP versus 9.81/9.52 for the closest competitor (HD-TTS).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MIGN outperforms all 13 baselines on every variable. The closest competitors are DualCast (2025) and HD-TTS (2024): DualCast leads on DEWP (9.42), SLP (23.83), and WDSP (8.63), while HD-TTS is strongest on MXSPD (20.25) and TTS-AMP on MIN TEMP (9.80). MIGN's advantage is largest on SLP (20.09 vs. 23.83, a 15% reduction) and MIN TEMP (8.01 vs. 9.80, 15%), and smallest on wind speed variables (WDSP 8.38 vs. 8.63; MXSPD 19.73 vs. 20.25, ~3%). MPNNLSTM is a clear outlier with MSE >40 on temperature variables, indicating it fails to learn meaningful spatial patterns. In the generalization-to-unseen-stations experiment (Table 4), MIGN's lead widens: 8.55 vs. 9.81 MSE on MAX TEMP and 20.90 vs. 23.87 on SLP, confirming that baselines overfit to fixed station sets while MIGN's mesh + spherical-harmonics design transfers to new locations. In the multistep autoregressive setting (Tables 8–9), MIGN's advantage grows with horizon, e.g., total MAE of 2.72 vs. 2.96 (STGCN) on MAX TEMP over 3 steps.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends GNN-based weather forecasting from the regional, fixed-station setting to a global, spatially irregular, and dynamically varying one. It adapts two ideas from different lineages: mesh interpolation from earth science (IDW, Kriging) and GraphCast's multi-mesh message passing, repurposed here to project irregular station data onto a uniform HEALPix grid; and spherical harmonics location encoding from Rußwurm et al. (ICLR 2024), applied as a learnable input embedding rather than a full function approximation. The encoder-processor-decoder architecture is a natural GNN design, but the key conceptual departure is treating the mesh as an intermediate regular space that decouples spatial modeling from the irregularity of station placement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: real-world station networks are global, unevenly distributed, and change over time, yet most data-driven forecasting models assume either regular grids or fixed regional station sets. The demonstrated ability to generalize to previously unseen stations is practically important for deployment in new or expanding observation networks. Key limitations include the exclusion of marine observations (sparse station coverage over oceans) and the single-step (next-day) prediction focus, leaving open whether the mesh+SH approach scales to multi-day horizons, additional variables, or truly global coverage including oceanic regions.
