## Problem

Existing graph embedding methods (GNNs, pooling layers) produce opaque, high-dimensional representations that hinder interpretability and direct visualization, while Persistent Homology from Topological Data Analysis offers graph-centric structural insight but is limited by cubic-time complexity in computing persistence diagrams. No prior method combines topological grounding with low-dimensional, interpretable, and scalable graph embeddings suitable for both classification and cross-dataset visualization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252968/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an undirected graph G=(V,E), a filtration function f (node-based such as degree, closeness, popularity, or edge-based such as Forman/Ollivier-Ricci curvature), and a threshold set {ε_i}_{i=1}^n, and produces a 2D vector (a, b) per graph where a is the pivot (y-intercept) and b is the growth (slope) of the best-fit line through (|V_i|, |E_i|) pairs across the filtration. Combining k filtration functions yields a 2k-dimensional embedding; for example, the full TopER model uses up to 26 features (13 functions × 2 coefficients) after Lasso-based feature selection. The embedding is training-free and can be directly plotted as a 2D scatter for visualization, or fed into a small MLP for classification.

## Method

TopER replaces the expensive persistence-diagram computation in Persistent Homology with a simple linear regression on the filtration sequence: given a filtration G_1 ⊆ G_2 ⊆ … ⊆ G_n = G induced by f, it records (x_i, y_i) = (|V_i|, |E_i|) at each threshold and fits L(x) = a + bx via least squares, yielding the TopER vector (a, b). The slope b quantifies the rate at which edges grow relative to nodes during the filtration (capturing topological complexity change tied to the Euler characteristic), while the intercept a reflects initial graph connectivity. Multiple filtration functions are applied independently and their (a, b) pairs concatenated, with statistically significant functions retained via t-test and Lasso regularization. A single-hidden-layer MLP with Adam optimization is then trained on the combined TopER vectors for classification. The overall complexity is O(n × (|V| + |E|)), making it over 10× faster than PH on large graphs, and stability theorems guarantee that small perturbations to the filtration function induce bounded changes in the TopER vector.

## Results

TopER achieves the best average deviation from the per-dataset best (1.60%) across eight benchmark graph classification datasets, outperforming the closest competitor TopoGCL (1.76%). It attains the highest accuracy on BZR (90.13%) and REDDIT-B (92.70%), and ranks second on MUTAG (90.99%) and PROTEINS (74.58%). On the large-scale OGBG-MOLHIV dataset (41K graphs), TopER reaches 80.21% AUC, second only to Graphormer (80.51%) which requires 119.5M parameters. In graph clustering, TopER embeddings outperform Spectral Zoo on Davies-Bouldin for all eight datasets and on Silhouette/Calinski-Harabasz for five of eight.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest SOTA competitor TopoGCL (topological graph contrastive learning), TopER wins on BZR (90.13 vs. 87.17), MUTAG (90.99 vs. 90.09), and REDDIT-B (92.70 vs. 90.40), but trails on PROTEINS (74.58 vs. 77.30), IMDB-B (73.20 vs. 74.67), and IMDB-M (50.00 vs. 52.81); TopoGCL does not report REDDIT-5K. Against the broader GNN and pooling baselines (DiffPool, SAGPool, Top-k, MinCutPool, 6 GNNs), TopER achieves the best or near-best accuracy on every dataset where those methods report results, with the largest margins on BZR (+6.2 over DiffPool) and REDDIT-B (+13.7 over DiffPool). In the direct comparison with Persistent Homology (Table 4), TopER outperforms PH on all six benchmarks (e.g., 73.2 vs. 69.5 on IMDB-B, 56.5 vs. 54.1 on REDDIT-5K) while being over 10× faster on large graphs. For clustering, TopER embeddings dominate Spectral Zoo on Davies-Bouldin across all eight datasets (e.g., 1.93 vs. 7.25 on BZR, 2.78 vs. 25.74 on REDDIT-5K) and on Silhouette for five of eight datasets. On OGBG-MOLHIV, TopER (80.21 AUC) narrowly trails Graphormer (80.51) but surpasses all other baselines including PNA (79.05), DGN (79.70), and Graphormer's lighter variants, while using a training-free 2D embedding rather than a 119.5M-parameter model.

## Relation to Prior Work

TopER builds directly on the filtration step of Persistent Homology from Topological Data Analysis, but deliberately replaces the expensive persistence diagram computation (cubic time complexity) with a simple least-squares linear fit on node-edge counts across the filtration sequence. It departs from prior topological graph methods (FC-V, DMP, EMP, MP-HSM) that still compute Betti numbers or vectorize persistence diagrams, and from GNN-based approaches (pooling, contrastive learning, data augmentation) that require node-level embeddings before graph-level aggregation. The stability theorems anchor TopER in the existing Wasserstein stability framework for persistence diagrams and Betti curve stability, while the introduction of the Popularity filtration function extends the degree-based ranking literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TopER's core practical contribution is producing interpretable 2D graph embeddings (pivot and growth) that enable direct visualization and cross-dataset comparison—something no prior graph embedding method achieves without learned high-dimensional encodings. The method's O(n×(|V|+|E|)) complexity makes it viable for large-scale graphs where PH is infeasible, and the stability guarantees provide theoretical grounding for robustness under perturbation. Open questions include whether the linear fit remains sufficient for non-monotone or domain-specific quantities, how TopER embeddings integrate into graph foundation models, and whether the filtration function selection can be automated to reduce domain-knowledge dependence.
