## Problem

Image-to-video diffusion models produce visually faithful clips but struggle to generate physically plausible and semantically coherent motion, as motion involves complex physical constraints and domain-specific dynamics that are difficult to capture through end-to-end training or text descriptions alone. The paper addresses this by explicitly retrieving and transferring motion priors from real reference videos, enabling cross-domain motion transfer (e.g., a person riding a horse → an astronaut riding a horse on the moon) without per-instance fine-tuning.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (h×w×3) and a text prompt as input, and produces a short video clip of 16 frames at 1024×576 (SVD, DynamiCrafter) or 17 frames at 720×480 (CogVideoX-5b). During inference, the text prompt is encoded via GTE-v1.5 to retrieve the top-9 most semantically relevant videos from a pre-indexed database in ~40 ms, and the entire retrieval-plus-adaptation pipeline adds less than 4 seconds to base-model inference time.

## Method

MotionRAG is a three-stage retrieval-augmented pipeline: (1) text-based video retrieval via cosine similarity over GTE caption embeddings; (2) Context-Aware Motion Adaptation (CAMA), where VideoMAE extracts high-level motion tokens and DINOv2 extracts appearance tokens from retrieved first frames and the target image, both compressed to 25×1024 tokens by Perceiver resamplers, then a 4-layer causal transformer (Motion Context Transformer) processes examples in reverse-similarity order with block-causal attention to predict target-compatible motion features via in-context learning; (3) motion-guided diffusion synthesis, where lightweight cross-attention Motion-Adapter modules (Q from visual tokens, K/V from motion features, residual addition) are inserted after every text cross-attention layer in the frozen base model. Training is two-stage: first the adapter and motion resampler are trained with MSE denoising loss, then the MCT and image resampler are trained with L2 loss on motion features. The key design insight is formulating motion transfer as an in-context learning problem, which enables zero-shot generalization to new domains simply by swapping the retrieval database without retraining any parameters.

## Results

On the OpenVid-1K test set, MotionRAG consistently improves motion quality across all three base models. The Action score (semantic motion correctness) increases by +5.9 for CogVideoX (59.9→65.8), +8.6 for DynamiCrafter (53.5→62.1), and +2.5 for SVD (57.5→60.0). FVD reductions are particularly striking for CogVideoX (87.1→80.2, −8.0%) and DynamiCrafter (88.4→69.0, −22.0%), while CLIP and DINO scores improve across all models, confirming better semantic alignment and identity preservation. The added inference overhead is negligible, only 0.01–0.06 minutes per video.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MotionRAG is evaluated as a plug-and-play module on three state-of-the-art image-to-video models: CogVideoX-5B, DynamiCrafter, and Stable Video Diffusion. Against CogVideoX, RAG wins on every metric—Action (+5.9), CLIP (+0.9), DINO (+1.6), FVD (−6.9), and FID (−0.4)—with only a 0.06 min increase in inference time. Against DynamiCrafter, the gains are even larger: Action improves by +8.6 points and FVD drops by 22.0%, the most substantial improvement among all comparisons. Against SVD, RAG improves Action (+2.5), CLIP (+4.6), DINO (+6.0), and FID (−2.3), but notably degrades FVD from 98.0 to 167.1, suggesting the motion injection may introduce temporal inconsistencies that SVD's architecture handles poorly. Overall, the method achieves the largest relative gains on the weaker base models (DynamiCrafter, SVD) while still delivering meaningful improvements on the strongest baseline (CogVideoX), confirming its value as a universal motion-enhancement module.

## Relation to Prior Work

MotionRAG adapts the retrieval-augmented generation paradigm from NLP to image-to-video generation, specifically targeting motion realism. It departs from prior video motion customization methods (MotionDirector, VMC, DreamVideo, Customize-A-Video) that require per-video fine-tuning by instead formulating motion transfer as an in-context learning problem via a causal transformer, drawing on LLM-style few-shot paradigms. The motion injection mechanism is directly inspired by IP-Adapter's cross-attention adapter pattern, repurposed to inject high-level semantic motion tokens (from VideoMAE) rather than appearance features. Compared to search-T2V, which performs retrieval-based text-to-video with expensive per-query fine-tuning, MotionRAG operates in the image-to-video setting with zero per-instance training, and compared to Motion-I2V, it uses high-level semantic motion features rather than low-level optical flow.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play design that works across UNet-based (SVD, DynamiCrafter) and DiT-based (CogVideoX) architectures with negligible inference overhead (<4s) makes this a practically deployable enhancement for existing video generation pipelines. The zero-shot domain transfer capability—simply swapping the retrieval database to handle instructional videos or out-of-distribution corpora without retraining—opens a path toward extensible, domain-adaptive video generation systems. Key open questions remain: the motion cancellation failure mode when retrieved references contain contradictory movements, the lack of robust automated metrics for physical plausibility in open-domain videos, and the limitation to short clips constrained by base model temporal capacity. Fine-grained motion (subtle hand gestures) would require higher-resolution spatiotemporal encoders and more specialized retrieval corpora.
