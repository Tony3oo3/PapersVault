## Problem

Foundation vision encoders aggressively downsample spatial information (14×–16×), producing semantically rich but spatially coarse feature maps that bottleneck dense prediction tasks requiring pixel-level accuracy. Existing upsampling solutions either depend on task-specific supervision, are restricted to fixed scaling factors, or produce over-smoothed outputs, leaving no lightweight, task-agnostic method that generalizes to arbitrary output resolutions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253316/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

JAFAR takes a high-resolution RGB image (downsampled to 224×224 for the guidance stream) and a low-resolution feature map from a frozen vision encoder (e.g., 32×32 from DINOv2 ViT-S/14 on 448×448 input) and produces an upsampled feature map at any arbitrary target resolution. Unlike fixed-ratio methods, the output resolution is determined at inference time via adaptive average pooling of the query stream, so the same trained model handles 56×56 through 896×896 outputs without retraining. No high-resolution ground-truth features or task labels are required at any stage.

## Method

JAFAR formulates upsampling as a global cross-attention interpolation where queries and keys are constructed asymmetrically from a shared lightweight image encoding. Queries retain high-resolution low-level details (color, texture) and are downsampled only during training via adaptive average pooling to bridge the abstraction gap; keys are hybrid representations that combine the same low-level spatial cues with high-level semantics injected through SFT modulation (K = γ_F ⊙ K̃ + β_F) from the encoder's deep features. RoPE positional embeddings on both streams provide spatial grounding that eliminates the need for arbitrary neighbor selection and enables generalization to unseen resolutions. Training is fully task-agnostic: multi-resolution views of the same image (downsampled by δ∈[2,4]) through the frozen encoder yield a simple cosine-similarity + L2 alignment loss, and training at low upsampling factors (≤4×) at low resolutions generalizes to much larger scales (e.g., 32×32→448×448). The attention weights directly interpolate the encoder features without a learned value projection, preserving original feature content and keeping the module to ~700K parameters.

## Results

On linear-probing semantic segmentation with DINOv2-ViT-S/14, JAFAR achieves the highest mIoU across all four benchmarks: 60.78 (COCO-Stuff), 84.44 (Pascal VOC), 40.49 (ADE20K), and 61.47 (Cityscapes), delivering an average +1.63 mIoU over the next-best method. On depth estimation (COCO), JAFAR reaches 62.18 δ₁ and 0.62 RMSE, ranking second overall. In zero-shot open-vocabulary segmentation (MaskCLIP, CLIP-ViT-B/16), JAFAR attains 35.70 mIoU on VOC and 13.61 on ADE20K, outperforming FeatUp by +3.43 and +0.58 mIoU respectively. On BeV vehicle segmentation (nuScenes), JAFAR yields up to +5 mIoU over the no-upsampling baseline across SimpleBeV, PointBeV, and BeVFormer architectures.

## Baselines & Comparisons

JAFAR outperforms all task-dependent upsamplers (CARAFE, SAPA, DySample, ReSFU) on every segmentation benchmark despite not using any task-specific labels during training; the largest gap is on Cityscapes where JAFAR leads DySample by +1.76 mIoU and SAPA by +11.35 mIoU. Among task-agnostic methods, JAFAR beats FeatUp by an average +2.78 mIoU (peak +5.41 on Cityscapes) and LiFT by +4.14 mIoU on VOC, confirming that the attention-based architecture with SFT-modulated keys generalizes far better than LiFT's fixed 2× CNN or FeatUp's stacked ×2 JBU stages. The sole exception is depth estimation, where ReSFU achieves a higher δ₁ (66.14 vs. 62.18) and lower RMSE (0.56 vs. 0.62), though JAFAR still surpasses all other task-dependent and task-agnostic baselines on that metric. Against LoftUp [54], JAFAR outperforms at lower output resolutions (56²: +3.79 mIoU on Cityscapes; 112²: +2.54) and matches at 224²/448², while using only 700K parameters versus LoftUp's 4.3M. In Grad-CAM faithfulness evaluation, JAFAR achieves the highest ADCC score (73.3), outperforming the second-best method (Strided, 65.3) by 8 points, and in BeV segmentation it improves mIoU by +2.9 to +4.8 over the no-upsampling baseline across three architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

JAFAR builds on two key prior ideas: the SFT (Spatial Feature Transform) modulation from Wang et al. (CVPR 2018), repurposed here to inject encoder semantics into attention keys rather than to modulate decoder features, and the multi-resolution view training objective from LiFT (ECCV 2024), which regresses features from a higher-resolution encoder pass. The critical departure from LiFT is replacing its fixed-scale CNN with a cross-attention mechanism whose queries and keys are derived from a shared low-level image encoding, enabling arbitrary-resolution upsampling without iterative stacking. Unlike FeatUp, which requires per-image optimization for its implicit variant or multi-stage ×2 stacking for its JBU variant, JAFAR achieves direct upsampling to any target resolution in a single forward pass with a simpler single-stage training pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

JAFAR provides a practical, drop-in upsampling module that consistently improves dense prediction tasks (segmentation, depth, CAM, BeV) without task-specific supervision, addressing a real bottleneck in foundation-model pipelines where 14×–16× spatial downsampling limits pixel-level accuracy. The finding that training at 8×8→32×32 generalizes to 32×32→448×448 upsampling is practically significant, reducing training cost while maintaining quality at inference. Key limitations remain: a separate upsampler must be trained per backbone, and the global attention mechanism becomes computationally prohibitive at very high key resolutions (64×64+), motivating future work on localized attention variants and backbone-independent inference.
