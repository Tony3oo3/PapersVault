## Problem

Message-passing GNNs suffer from over-squashing and over-smoothing when deepened to capture long-range dependencies, prompting costly workarounds like graph rewiring or attention-based Graph Transformers that sacrifice the O(|E|) scalability of local message passing. ChebNet, the original spectral GNN that generalizes GCN via higher-order Chebyshev filters, was largely abandoned under the assumption it would not scale or perform well on long-range tasks, despite its natural K-hop receptive field. This paper revisits ChebNet from first principles, showing it is already competitive on long-range benchmarks but identifying a critical training instability that emerges as the polynomial order grows.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a graph's normalized Laplacian (derived from the adjacency matrix) and per-node feature vectors, applying K-order Chebyshev polynomial filters without any eigendecomposition. Outputs are node embeddings at each layer that can be read out directly for node-level tasks (classification, regression) or pooled for graph-level predictions. A distinguishing detail is that the antisymmetric weight constraint (W_k^T = -W_k) means the model's Jacobian has purely imaginary eigenvalues, so node representations remain sensitive to distant inputs without exponential amplification or decay across depth.

## Method

Stable-ChebNet reformulates the ChebNet update as a continuous-time ODE dX/dt = Σ_k T_k(L) X W_k, then imposes antisymmetric weight matrices (W_k^T = -W_k) combined with the symmetric normalized Laplacian to guarantee the graph-wise Jacobian has purely imaginary eigenvalues (Theorem 3). The ODE is discretized via forward Euler with a small step ε and a damping hyperparameter γ, yielding the update X^(l+1) = X^(l) + ε Σ_k T_k(L) X^(l)(W_k − W_k^T − γI). This construction achieves second-order stability (‖J^(l)‖₂ = 1 + O(ε²)), preventing the exponential gradient growth/decay that plagues vanilla ChebNet at high K. The result is a non-dissipative propagator that preserves information over many hops without requiring eigendecompositions, positional encodings, or graph rewiring, while retaining O(K|E|) per-layer complexity.

## Results

On ogbn-arxiv, Stable-ChebNet achieves 75.73% test accuracy, outperforming all 10 baselines including ChebNet (73.27%), GCN (71.74%), and GraphGPS (70.92%). On ogbn-proteins, it reaches 79.55% accuracy, matching SGFormer (79.53%) and trailing only Spexphormer (80.65%). On the LRGB peptides-func benchmark, Stable-ChebNet attains 70.32 AP, competitive with S2GCN (72.75) and DRew+PE (71.50) while requiring no positional encodings or eigendecompositions. On the Graph Property Prediction tasks, Stable-ChebNet reduces log₁₀(MSE) from −1.22 to −2.10 on Eccentricity and from −1.85 to −2.21 on SSSP relative to vanilla ChebNet.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On ogbn-arxiv, Stable-ChebNet (75.73%) decisively outperforms every baseline, including ChebNet (73.27%), GCN (71.74%), GraphGPS (70.92%), and Exphormer+GCN (72.44%), by 2.5–4.8 percentage points. On ogbn-proteins, it matches SGFormer (79.55% vs. 79.53%) and beats ChebNet (77.55%), GCN (72.51%), and NodeFormer (77.45%), but trails Spexphormer (80.65%) by ~1.1 points. On LRGB peptides-func, Stable-ChebNet (70.32 AP) is competitive with but slightly below S2GCN (72.75) and DRew+PE (71.50), while clearly surpassing Exphormer (65.27), Graph ViT (69.42), and Graph Mamba (67.39); notably, S2GCN requires a full Laplacian eigendecomposition and DRew+PE requires precomputed positional encodings, neither of which Stable-ChebNet uses. On the Graph Property Prediction tasks, Stable-ChebNet dominates all 9 baselines across Diameter, SSSP, and Eccentricity, with the largest gap on Eccentricity (−2.10 vs. −1.22 for ChebNet, −0.66 for GRAND). On Barbell graphs at N=70, K=10, Stable-ChebNet achieves MSE 0.06 versus 1.08 for vanilla ChebNet, confirming that the antisymmetric Euler step prevents the over-squashing collapse that plagues the original formulation at larger graph sizes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper revisits ChebNet (Defferrard et al., 2016), the original tractable spectral GNN that was largely superseded by GCN's first-order simplification and subsequently forgotten in the GNN literature. The core methodological contribution combines ChebNet's Chebyshev polynomial filters with the antisymmetric weight parameterization introduced by Gravina et al. (2023) for stable deep graph networks, discretized via forward Euler to yield Stable-ChebNet. Conceptually, the paper departs from the dominant MPNN/Transformer/rewiring paradigm for long-range propagation by demonstrating that a purely spectral, edge-respecting architecture can match or exceed these approaches without positional encodings, attention, or topology modification. The theoretical analysis (exact Jacobian singular-value distributions via Marchenko-Pastur, sensitivity bounds) provides a principled explanation for why vanilla ChebNet destabilizes at high polynomial order and why antisymmetry restores stability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper challenges a widely held assumption in the GNN community—that spectral methods are inherently impractical for large-scale or long-range tasks—by showing ChebNet is competitive out-of-the-box and that a minimal antisymmetric+Euler modification yields near-SOTA results across LRGB, OGB, and synthetic benchmarks. This offers a theoretically grounded, O(K|E|)-scalable alternative to quadratic-complexity Transformers and cubic-time rewiring, which is practically significant for large graphs. Limitations include the restriction to Chebyshev polynomials (generalization to other spectral bases is left as future work), the expressivity cost of the antisymmetric constraint, and the fact that on some benchmarks (e.g., ogbn-proteins) Stable-ChebNet still trails the best Transformer baselines by a small margin. Open questions include whether the ODE-based stability framework extends to other polynomial families and whether the antisymmetric constraint can be relaxed while preserving the second-order stability guarantee.
