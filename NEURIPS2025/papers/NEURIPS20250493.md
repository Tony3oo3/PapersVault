## Problem

Bilevel optimization is widely used in machine learning (hyperparameter tuning, meta-learning, instrumental variable regression), but existing generalization theory is restricted to finite-dimensional inner-level parameters with strong convexity. When the inner variable lives in an infinite-dimensional function space such as an RKHS—as in kernel methods—the dimension of the inner parameter grows with sample size, making prior stability-based or convergence-based analyses inapplicable. This paper provides the first finite-sample generalization bounds for Kernel Bilevel Optimization (KBO), closing this gap for the nonparametric setting.

## Input / Output

The method consumes n i.i.d. samples from distribution P (inner-level) and m i.i.d. samples from distribution Q (outer-level), a bounded measurable kernel K, point-wise loss functions ℓ_in and ℓ_out that are C³ in (ω,v), and a fixed regularization parameter λ > 0. It produces the optimal outer parameter ω* ∈ R^d, the optimal inner function h*_ω in the RKHS, and uniform generalization bounds of order O(1/√m + 1/√n) on both the value function and its gradient over any compact subset of R^d. A key structural detail is that the gradient estimator ∇̂F(ω) requires only pointwise evaluations of the inner solution ĥ_ω and the adjoint solution â_ω (both in the fixed RKHS), rather than the growing-dimensional coefficient vector γ̂_ω ∈ R^n, which is what makes the statistical analysis tractable.

## Method

The paper derives an explicit expression for the total gradient ∇F(ω) via functional implicit differentiation in an RKHS, reformulating the intractable Jacobian ∂_ω h*_ω as the solution of a strongly convex adjoint quadratic problem in the RKHS. A central technical result (Proposition 3.1) establishes that the gradient of the plug-in value function estimator ∇F̂(ω) is exactly equal to the plug-in estimator of the population gradient ∇̂F(ω), i.e., differentiation and empirical estimation commute in this setting. For the generalization bounds, the authors decompose the uniform error into terms involving empirical processes (controlled by classical maximal inequalities) and terms involving RKHS-valued derivatives (which cannot be handled by standard empirical process theory). They resolve this by exploiting the reproducing property to express squared RKHS-norm errors as real-valued degenerate U-processes of order 2, then applying Sherman's maximal inequalities for U-processes. The resulting bounds are then applied to bilevel gradient descent, yielding sub-optimality rates that combine a statistical component O(1/√m + 1/√n) with an optimization component O(1/√t).

## Results

The main theoretical result (Theorem 4.1) establishes that both the value function error sup_ω|F(ω) − F̂(ω)| and the gradient error sup_ω‖∇F(ω) − ∇̂F(ω)‖ are bounded in expectation by C(1/√m + 1/√n), where n and m are the inner and outer sample sizes respectively. Corollary 4.2 further shows that bilevel gradient descent achieves E[min_i ‖∇F(ω_i)‖] ≤ c̄(1/√m + 1/√n + 1/√(t+1)), combining statistical and optimization error. Numerical experiments on synthetic instrumental variable regression (n = m ranging from 100 to 5,000, Gaussian kernel) confirm that the observed generalization error at initialization and at convergence closely follows the predicted O(1/√n) slope on log-log plots, averaged over 50 runs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper that does not benchmark against competing bilevel optimization methods. Instead, it provides the first finite-sample generalization bounds for kernel bilevel optimization, filling a gap left by prior work that was restricted to finite-dimensional, strongly convex parametric settings. The numerical experiments serve to validate the paper's own theoretical predictions (the O(1/√n) rate) rather than to compare against alternative algorithms. The paper notes that existing stability-based generalization results (e.g., Bao et al. [7], Zhang et al. [78]) require fixed-dimensional parameters and cannot capture the growing-dimensionality behavior of RKHS solutions, while spectral filtering approaches for minimax rates (e.g., Meunier et al. [51]) require quadratic losses in ω and do not apply to the general non-quadratic inner objectives considered here.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the functional bilevel optimization framework of Petrulionyté et al. (NeurIPS 2024) to the specific case where the inner function space is an RKHS, enabling the use of the representer theorem to obtain finite-dimensional formulations. It departs from prior bilevel generalization results (Bao et al., Zhang et al., Oymak et al.) that require fixed-dimensional inner parameters, by handling the nonparametric setting where the inner parameter dimension grows with sample size. The core proof technique leverages Sherman's maximal inequalities for degenerate U-processes, repurposing a tool from semiparametric statistics to control infinite-dimensional error terms that arise from the RKHS structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first learning-theoretic treatment of bilevel optimization in a nonparametric (RKHS) setting, filling a gap the authors explicitly identify as 'essentially lacking.' The O(1/√m + 1/√n) generalization rate has direct practical implications for hyperparameter optimization, where the validation set is typically much smaller than the training set, suggesting both sample sizes matter equally. Key open questions include extending to stochastic bilevel methods, obtaining minimax-optimal rates for the un-regularized problem (λ→0), handling non-smooth losses, and quantifying the dependence of constants on the regularization parameter.
