## Problem

Semantic segmentation requires dense per-pixel annotations that are prohibitively expensive, and existing active learning methods struggle under extreme pixel budgets: uncertainty-based selection picks spatially redundant pixels, while representation-based methods miss informative boundary regions. This paper formalizes a low-budget regime (annotating only ~0.1 pixels per image per round) and asks how to select the most informative pixels when the labeling budget is orders of magnitude smaller than the total pixel count.

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes 256×256 RGB images and a frozen pre-trained DDPM backbone, extracting per-pixel multi-scale features by concatenating activations from 4 decoder blocks at 3 denoising timesteps (yielding 6,144-dim vectors). A lightweight 2-layer MLP segmentation head maps these features to class probabilities. At each of 10 AL rounds, the method outputs a set of b = 0.1N pixels (across all images) for annotation, progressively building a labeled set that trains the segmentation head; the final output is a per-pixel class prediction over the full image.

## Method

The core contribution is a two-stage selection pipeline that decouples diversity from uncertainty. Stage 1 applies MaxHerding (generalized coverage with an RBF kernel) in a local-then-global fashion: K representative pixels are greedily selected per image, merged into a global pool, then refined to M candidates, reducing the search space from N×H×W to a manageable size. Stage 2 computes eDALD, an entropy-augmented diffusion-based active learning by disagreement score: mutual information between stochastically noised multi-scale features (from M independent noise draws at the diffusion timesteps) and predicted labels, plus a single-sample entropy term to capture absolute prediction confidence. The diffusion backbone remains frozen throughout; only the segmentation head is trained via cross-entropy on the growing labeled set. This architecture ensures that disagreement identifies perturbation-sensitive pixels while entropy recovers consistently low-confidence regions, and the prior MaxHerding filtering prevents the redundant clustering that plagues pure uncertainty sampling.

## Results

Under an extreme low-budget regime (0.1 pixels per image per round, 10 rounds total), the proposed 2-Stage eDALD with a DDPM backbone achieves an average mIoU of 37.14% across four benchmarks, outperforming the strongest single-stage baseline (Margin, 34.66%) by 2.48 mIoU and the best DeepLabV3-based method (PixelPick, 22.85%) by 14.29 mIoU. On Pascal-Context, the method reaches 47.98% mIoU versus 45.11% for Margin and 26.28% for PixelPick. The two-stage pipeline also converges to 90% of fully supervised mIoU within 21–47 rounds, labeling only 0.003%–0.007% of all pixels.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against PixelPick [41] (margin sampling with DeepLabV3), 2-Stage eDALD wins on all four datasets by margins of 6.19 (CamVid), 22.77 (ADE-Bed), 6.52 (Cityscapes), and 21.70 (Pascal-Context) mIoU, with the largest gap on ADE-Bed where the DeepLabV3 backbone struggles with indoor scenes. Didari et al. [42] (BalEntAcq + DeepLabV3) is outperformed even more decisively, by 13.65–29.83 mIoU across datasets. Among DDPM-based single-stage baselines, Margin is the closest competitor (34.66% avg vs. 37.14%), while BalEntAcq [30] underperforms significantly (23.49% avg), suggesting that balanced-entropy scoring is poorly suited to the extreme low-budget pixel-level setting. Core-set [9] as a diversity selector is consistently weaker than MaxHerding [16] in the first stage (e.g., 16.19 vs. 31.83 mIoU on CamVid with DDPM), and the two-stage MaxHerding→eDALD pipeline outperforms Core-set→eDALD on three of four datasets. The single-stage eDALD (without MaxHerding) scores only 30.17% avg, confirming that the representation-diversity stage contributes a critical +6.97 mIoU gain over uncertainty-only selection.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper combines two previously separate threads: the LEDM framework (Baranchuk et al., ICLR 2022), which showed that multi-scale features from a pre-trained diffusion model are effective for label-efficient segmentation, and MaxHerding / generalized coverage (Bae et al., ECCV 2024), a representation-based active learning method designed for low-budget regimes. The key departure is the two-stage decoupling of diversity (Stage 1: local-then-global MaxHerding) and uncertainty (Stage 2: a new eDALD criterion), which had not been combined in prior pixel-level segmentation AL. The eDALD criterion itself is novel: it computes BALD-style mutual information over stochastic diffusion features and augments it with an independent single-sample entropy term, a combination motivated by the observation that pure disagreement can misrank candidates once diversity is already enforced.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper tackles a practically important bottleneck—pixel-level annotation cost in semantic segmentation—by showing that near-supervised quality (90% of fully supervised mIoU) is reachable with under 0.01% of pixels labeled, which could make dense segmentation feasible in resource-constrained domains like medical imaging or remote sensing. A key limitation is that DALD is inherently tied to diffusion backbones (it relies on stochastic multi-timestep features), so the uncertainty component does not transfer to CNN or Transformer backbones without substitution (e.g., MC-Dropout-based eBALD, which performs worse). Open questions include whether the two-stage decomposition is optimal versus joint diversity-uncertainty scoring, how the pipeline scales to higher resolutions or video, and whether other generative models (e.g., score-based SDEs, latent diffusion) offer comparable or superior stochastic features for the same role.
