## Problem

Machine unlearning has been studied for 2D image generation but remains entirely unexplored for 3D generation, where privacy risks extend to geometric structures, textures, and spatial layouts. The multi-view nature of 3D synthesis compounds the difficulty: unlearning must be enforced consistently across all viewpoints, making the process computationally prohibitive when naively applied to every angle. This paper is the first to formulate and solve unlearning for zero-shot image-to-3D view synthesis models.

## Input / Output

The framework operates on a single input RGB image fed to a zero-shot image-to-3D model (Zero123/Zero123XL), conditioned on a set of viewpoint angles spanning the full 360° horizontal range (sampled every 10°, yielding 40 views). Unlearning is specified by three image sets: a forget set (target to erase), a remain set (content to preserve), and an override/retarget set (replacement distribution). The output is an updated model f_u that, given the same input image, produces multi-view images and 3D mesh reconstructions where the forget-set content has been replaced or removed while remain-set generation quality is maintained.

## Method

The framework has two coupled components. First, a dynamic skipping mechanism caches the full reverse-diffusion trajectory for a sparse set of 3–8 reference viewpoints; for each of the remaining sample angles, the two most similar reference views are identified via CLIP-based cosine similarity, their intermediate latents are linearly interpolated, and early denoising steps are skipped with the skip depth dynamically adjusted by angular proximity to the nearest reference (threshold τ=20°). Second, unlearning is performed in two stages: a fake score network S_f (initialized from the pretrained score network) is trained with a remain loss (matching ground-truth noise on the remain set) and a forget loss (aligning noise predictions with the override distribution), then the generator f_u is updated under a fixed S_f using corresponding diffusion remain and forget losses. This score-guided formulation avoids full supervision of every target view and, combined with the interpolation-based acceleration, achieves a 30% speedup while improving unlearning quality over the non-accelerated baseline.

## Results

On the Min10 dataset with 40 viewpoints, the best accelerated configuration (4 reference angles, 12 steps skipped) achieves SSIM 0.783, LPIPS 0.136, and ΔPSNR +1.093 dB, outperforming the non-accelerated baseline (SSIM 0.766, LPIPS 0.160) while reducing training time from 226.5 s to 180.9 s (1.22× speedup). The maximum speedup reaches 1.32× (4 ref angles, 16 steps skipped, 167.4 s). Across 10 diverse unlearning tasks, the forget-set LPIPS drops substantially (e.g., 0.250→0.066 for Yellow Car Transformation) while remain-set SSIM stays within ±0.04 of the original, confirming targeted forgetting without degrading general generation quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

As the first work to explore machine unlearning in 3D generation, the paper does not compare against external unlearning baselines; the primary comparison is between the proposed perspective-coherent accelerated framework and a non-accelerated baseline that processes all 40 viewpoints exhaustively. The accelerated method wins on both axes: the best configuration (4 ref angles, 12 skips) improves SSIM by +0.017 and reduces LPIPS by −0.024 relative to the baseline while cutting training time by 20%. Inference speedup reaches up to 2.12× (16 steps skipped) in the supplementary ablation. Across the 10 unlearning tasks in Table 2, the forget-set LPIPS consistently drops (average reduction ≈0.15) while remain-set SSIM fluctuates by less than 0.05, indicating that unlearning is selective. The only task where remain-set quality degrades noticeably is 'Football to Phone' (SSIM 0.788→0.744, LPIPS 0.277→0.328), suggesting that semantically distant retargeting pairs are harder to handle without collateral damage.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the machine unlearning paradigm—well-studied in 2D classification and diffusion-based image generation (e.g., SFD, GUIDE, meta-unlearning)—to the 3D multi-view generation setting, where unlearning must enforce consistency across all viewpoints simultaneously. The unlearning mechanism itself is adapted from Score Forgetting Distillation (Chen et al., 2024), which trains a fake score network to guide a generator toward forgetting, here applied to the Zero123 image-to-3D view synthesis architecture. The dynamic skipping acceleration draws conceptually from diffusion acceleration techniques (DeepCache, S²-DMs) but repurposes them for a different goal: exploiting inter-view geometric coherence to reduce redundant denoising rather than merely speeding up a single generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As 3D generative foundation models (trained on Objaverse-scale datasets) become more prevalent in gaming, film, and digital design, the privacy and IP risks of memorized proprietary geometry or biometric shapes become increasingly urgent, yet no prior work addressed unlearning in this domain. The finding that multi-view-consistent acceleration can simultaneously reduce training time by ~30% and marginally improve unlearning quality is practically useful, though the evidence is confined to Zero123/Zero123XL on small datasets (Min10, ~4,200 images). Key open questions include whether the framework generalizes to NeRF- or mesh-based 3D generators, whether unlearning is robust to adversarial re-injection or model-inversion attacks, and how to automate identification of privacy-sensitive content without manual forget/remain set specification.
