## Problem

Novel view synthesis methods (NeRFs, 3DGS) are evaluated under inconsistent protocols—differing in image downscaling, background color, LPIPS variant, and metric computation details—making published numbers incomparable and potentially misleading. The paper demonstrates that even a single protocol change (e.g., manual downscaling vs. pre-downscaled JPEGs) can shift a method's ranking by several places, undermining the validity of quantitative progress claims in the field.

## Input / Output

The framework consumes scene images with camera intrinsics/extrinsics (from datasets like Mip-NeRF 360, Blender, Tanks and Temples, Photo Tourism) and produces novel view renderings, per-scene and averaged PSNR/SSIM/LPIPS metrics, downloadable checkpoints, and rendered camera trajectory videos. Predictions are stored in uint8 for reproducibility; evaluation uses dataset-specific LPIPS backbones (VGG for Blender/Mip-NeRF 360/LLFF, AlexNet for newer datasets) and fixed SSIM parameters (kernel=11, σ=1.5, k₁=0.01, k₂=0.03) on [0,1]-normalized float32 images without per-image min-max normalization.

## Method

NerfBaselines wraps each official method implementation with a thin unified API (train_iteration, render, save, optimize_embedding) rather than reimplementing methods, preserving original behavior while enabling cross-dataset evaluation. Each method runs in an isolated environment (Docker, Apptainer, or Conda) with frozen source code and dependencies to ensure long-term reproducibility, since 12 of 19 tested methods could not be installed via their original instructions. The framework standardizes one evaluation protocol per dataset (e.g., pre-downscaled JPEGs for Mip-NeRF 360, white background blending for Blender, left-half appearance optimization for Photo Tourism) and provides a web platform with downloadable checkpoints, predictions, and an interactive 3D viewer with a camera trajectory editor for off-trajectory qualitative assessment.

## Results

Under the unified NerfBaselines protocol (P1) on Mip-NeRF 360, Zip-NeRF leads at 28.55±0.01 PSNR, followed by Scaffold-GS (27.71), Mip-Splatting (27.49), and 3DGS (27.43). The paper's key finding is that switching to manual downscaling (P2) shifts Gsplat from rank 6 to rank 5 and reorders several 3DGS methods, demonstrating that protocol differences can exceed inter-method performance gaps. On Blender, Gaussian Opacity Fields leads at 33.45 PSNR, with Mip-Splatting (33.33) and 3DGS (33.31) close behind; using a black background (P2) instead of white inflates 3DGS-based scores by ~0.4–0.5 dB. NerfBaselines reproduces published numbers within <1% for most methods, with exceptions (Instant-NGP, Mip-Splatting) attributable to protocol mismatches in the original papers.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Under the unified P1 protocol on Mip-NeRF 360, Zip-NeRF outperforms all 3DGS-based methods by 0.8–1.1 dB PSNR, while among 3DGS methods the spread is only 0.9 dB (Scaffold-GS 27.71 vs. 2DGS 26.81). The critical finding is that switching to P2 (manual downscaling) compresses the 3DGS gap to 0.6 dB and reorders Gsplat and Gaussian Splatting, showing that a single method adopting P2 can appear to jump 3 ranks. On Blender, the top four 3DGS/NeRF methods (GOF, Mip-Splatting, 3DGS, Scaffold-GS) are within 0.4 dB under P1, but using a black background (P2, as Instant-NGP did) inflates 3DGS scores by ~0.45 dB and flips the Mip-Splatting/3DGS ordering. On Photo Tourism, the NeRF-W half-image protocol (P1) gives WildGaussians a 0.9 dB lead over Gsplat, but optimizing on full test images (P2) narrows this to 0.32 dB and reorders the top three. For reproducibility, NerfBaselines matches published PSNR within 0.1 dB for Zip-NeRF, 3DGS, and TensoRF, while Instant-NGP (black vs. white background) and Mip-Splatting (downscaling) show 0.3–1.2 dB gaps fully explained by protocol differences. On Tanks & Temples, Zip-NeRF dominates the Training Data and Intermediate splits (e.g., 29.26 vs. 27.51 PSNR on barn), but no single method wins all Advanced scenes, with 3DGS and Mip-Splatting leading on larger scenes like ballroom and courtroom.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground in the evaluation infrastructure for novel view synthesis rather than building on a specific method. It departs from existing development frameworks like NerfStudio and SDFStudio, which reimplement methods within a unified codebase, by instead wrapping original implementations with a thin API and standardizing only the evaluation protocol. The empirical study on protocol sensitivity (downscaling strategy, background color, appearance-embedding optimization scope) is novel in systematically quantifying how these 'minor' choices shift method rankings across Mip-NeRF 360, Blender, and Photo Tourism. The paper also extends the evaluation scope to Tanks and Temples, a dataset not commonly used by the 3DGS methods it benchmarks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a seemingly innocuous protocol change (manual downscaling vs. pre-downscaled JPEGs) can shift a method's rank by three places on Mip-NeRF 360 directly challenges the validity of many published comparisons in the NVS literature, where evaluation details are often under-specified. NerfBaselines addresses a real infrastructure gap: 12 of 19 integrated methods could not be installed following their original instructions, and NerfStudio's reimplementation of Instant-NGP scores 15.24 PSNR versus 36.39 in the original, illustrating the fragility of reimplementation-based benchmarks. The main open question is community adoption — the authors acknowledge this as a key limitation, and the framework's long-term value depends on whether new methods are submitted under its protocol. The web platform with downloadable checkpoints and online 3D viewers lowers the barrier for qualitative comparison beyond test-set metrics.
