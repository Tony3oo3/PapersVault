## Problem

Variational Score Distillation (VSD) provides a principled KL-divergence framework for distilling diffusion models into one-step generators, but its gradient requires the intractable student marginal score ∇_{x_t} log p_{φ,t}(x_t), which must be approximated via auxiliary networks or conditional-score shortcuts. These approximations introduce bias, cause training instability from alternating optimization, or yield incomplete distributional matching that focuses on conditional means rather than full distributional characteristics. The paper asks whether a distillation objective can be constructed that matches the full noisy marginal distributions without ever computing or approximating the student score.

## Input / Output

The student generator g_φ(z) takes a latent vector z (drawn from a standard Gaussian) and, in conditional settings, a text prompt or class label, producing a single RGB image in one forward pass (NFE=1). For large text-to-image models (SD1.5, SDXL, SD3.5), VarFlow is applied via LoRA fine-tuning, so the input is a text embedding plus latent noise and the output is a 512×512 or 1024×1024 image. During training, the method consumes paired batches of K noisy student samples (from g_φ outputs passed through the forward noising process) and K noisy teacher samples (from real data passed through the same process) at a randomly sampled noise level t.

## Method

VarFlow operationalizes a Score-Rule Variational Distillation (SRVD) framework by minimizing the energy distance D_Energy^{(β)}(p_{φ,t}, q_t) between the student's induced noisy marginal and the teacher's target noisy marginal, averaged over noise levels t. The key insight is that the strictly proper energy score yields a loss whose Monte Carlo estimate depends only on pairwise distances between sampled points from the two distributions—specifically, a cross-term E‖x_t^S − x_t^T‖^β and a self-term E‖x_t^S − x_t^{S'}‖^β—completely bypassing the intractable student score. The gradient is computed by backpropagating through the student generator as it appears in the noisy student samples, with no auxiliary score network or stop-gradient trick required. A consistency theorem establishes that the loss is minimized if and only if p_{φ,t} = q_t for almost all t, and ablations show β=1, VSD-like time weighting w̃(t)=σ_t², and batch size K=16 with a paired cross-term estimator yield optimal performance.

## Results

In the diffusion distillation setting (1-step, 296M params), VarFlow achieves FID 1.46 on ImageNet 64×64, 2.42 on ImageNet 256×256, 2.22 on CIFAR-10, and 7.40 FID / 31.2 CLIP on MS COCO 512×512. On large-scale text-to-image models (Table 2), SDXL-VarFlow achieves FID 4.11 / CLIP 31.2 at 1-step and FID 3.65 / CLIP 32.1 at 8-step on COCO-10k, outperforming all few-step baselines including DMD2, Lightning, and TCD. VarFlow also achieves the best FID among from-scratch one-step models on ImageNet 64×64 (3.19) and MS COCO (9.26 FID, 30.2 CLIP).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VarFlow achieves the best FID on ImageNet 64×64 (1.46) and ImageNet 256×256 (2.42) among all distillation methods, narrowly beating SMD (1.48 / 2.45) and clearly outperforming SiD (1.52 / 3.15) and DMD2 (1.51 / 3.10). On CIFAR-10, VarFlow (FID 2.22) trails SiD (1.92) and SMD (2.08) but surpasses DMD2 (2.43) and DMD w/o reg (5.58). On MS COCO, VarFlow achieves the best CLIP score (31.2) and near-best FID (7.40 vs. SMD's 7.42), while DMD2 lags at 8.17 FID / 28.7 CLIP. In the large-scale text-to-image setting (Table 2), VarFlow consistently outperforms all baselines (PeRFlow, LCM, TCD, Lightning, Hyper-SD, DMD2, EMD) across SD1.5, SDXL, and SD3.5-Medium at every NFE from 1 to 8 steps, with the largest margins over DMD2 (e.g., SDXL 8-step: 3.65 vs. 3.88 FID) and TCD (e.g., SDXL 1-step: 4.11 vs. 4.53 FID). The from-scratch one-step results also show VarFlow beating SMT (3.23 FID on IN64) and iCT-deep (3.25 FID) while matching or exceeding ECT and iCT.

## Relation to Prior Work

VarFlow directly extends Variational Score Distillation (VSD) by Wang et al. (2024), which minimizes KL divergence between student and teacher noisy marginals but requires approximating the intractable student score. The key departure is replacing the KL objective with an energy distance derived from strictly proper scoring rules (Gneiting & Raftery 2007; Szekely et al. 2004), making the loss estimable purely from samples and eliminating the student score entirely. Conceptually, this adapts the scoring-rule-based distributional matching used in Distributional Diffusion Models (DDM) for conditional posteriors to the one-step marginal-matching setting, while also drawing on the IPM-based generative modeling tradition (MMD GANs) applied here specifically to diffusion distillation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By removing the need for student score approximation, VarFlow offers a simpler and potentially more stable training procedure for one-step generation, addressing a persistent source of bias and instability in VSD-based methods. The consistent SOTA or near-SOTA results across U-Net and DiT architectures (SD1.5, SDXL, SD3.5) with LoRA fine-tuning suggest broad practical applicability. However, the theoretical contribution is relatively thin (Theorem 4.1 essentially restates that energy distance is a metric), and open questions remain about whether energy distance is the optimal IPM choice, how the sample-based approach scales to very high-resolution or video generation, and whether the modest margins over strong baselines like DMD2 and SiD will hold as teacher models grow larger.
