## Problem

Auto-regressive LLMs must serialize all input into flat token sequences, introducing positional bias, quadratic attention complexity, and rapid context window consumption when processing data with native structural dependencies (e.g., citation graphs, multi-hop RAG). Existing parallel encoding methods eliminate serialization but discard inter-segment interactions entirely, making them unable to model the relational structure critical for multi-hop reasoning. The paper asks how to inject structural inductive biases directly into the LLM's attention mechanism without resorting to full sequential encoding.

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural language query, a set of text chunks (each up to L tokens), and a directed graph G=(V,E) specifying source→target structural dependencies between chunks. For RAG tasks, the graph is a bipartite structure built from retriever similarity scores (top-m chunks as sources); for ARXIV-QA and node classification, it is the native citation ego-graph. Output is a free-form natural language answer (constrained to 256 tokens) for QA tasks or a discrete class label for topic classification. A key constraint is that all source chunks share one PE range [0,L) and all target chunks share another [L,2L), so the total positional span scales as T·L (T = number of propagation rounds) rather than n·L.

## Method

Graph-KV operates in two phases. First, all text chunks are independently encoded in parallel to produce initial KV caches, matching the O(|V|L²) cost of standard parallel encoding. Second, a graph-structured block mask is applied: each target chunk's KV cache is updated by computing softmax attention only over the KV caches of its designated source chunks (its in-neighbors in G), effectively performing one round of message passing within the LLM at O(|E|L²) cost. Positional encodings are strategically shared—source chunks all occupy [0,L) and target chunks all occupy [L,2L)—which eliminates positional bias from chunk ordering and reduces context window consumption by a factor proportional to the number of chunks. The backbone is llama-3.1-8B post-trained with independent attention blocks (Block-RAG) to handle the distributional shift from parallel encoding. At query time, generated tokens attend to both source and target chunk representations, and the entire pipeline requires no fine-tuning of the LLM with the graph-structured mask itself.

## Results

On multi-hop RAG benchmarks (MultiHop-RAG, MoreHopQA, LongBench-V2), Graph-KV Full achieves 46.41, 44.90, and 37.5 average accuracy respectively, surpassing the best sequential encoding baseline (8B-RAG: 44.27, 37.38, 32.6) by 2–10%. On standard RAG tasks (2Wiki, HotpotQA), Graph-KV Top-3 reaches 75.15 and 75.19 average accuracy, approaching but not exceeding the best sequential baseline (77.29, 77.43). On ARXIV-QA with distractors, Graph-KV maintains correct answers while all sequential baselines fail entirely with two distractors. On paper topic classification, Graph-KV achieves 71.03% on Cora and 84.61% on Pubmed, outperforming Block-RAG (69.55%, 83.24%) and sequential encoding (67.09%, 79.79%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Graph-KV consistently outperforms both tuning-free parallel encoding baselines (PCW and APE) across all 10 evaluation datasets, with gaps of 10–30+ points on multi-hop tasks (e.g., MoreHopQA: Graph-KV Full 44.90 vs. APE 14.13 vs. PCW 11.35). Against Block-RAG (the strongest parallel baseline sharing the same 8B-Block backbone), Graph-KV Top-3 wins on 8 of 10 sub-tasks, and Graph-KV Full dominates on all multi-hop reasoning sub-tasks (e.g., MoreHopQA Hop-1: 64.86 vs. 56.75; LongBench-V2 Long: 33.3 vs. 16.7). The headline result is that Graph-KV Full surpasses the best sequential encoding baseline on all three multi-hop/long-document benchmarks (MultiHop-RAG: 46.41 vs. 44.27; MoreHopQA: 44.90 vs. 37.38; LongBench-V2: 37.5 vs. 32.6), while maintaining sparse O(|E|L²) computation instead of O(n²L²). On simpler single-hop tasks (2Wiki, HotpotQA, NarrativeQA), Graph-KV Top-3 approaches but does not exceed sequential encoding (75.15 vs. 77.29 on 2Wiki), suggesting that serialized order already encodes the needed dependencies in those settings. On ARXIV-QA with two distractors (~264k tokens), all sequential baselines produce no correct answers, while Graph-KV remains robust due to its shared PE strategy and structural attention masking.

## Relation to Prior Work

Graph-KV builds directly on Block-RAG (Ma et al., ICLR 2024), which post-trains LLMs with independent attention blocks to enable parallel encoding of text chunks. It departs from the broader parallel-encoding line (PCW, APE, Block-RAG) by introducing a graph-structured block mask that allows target chunks to selectively attend to designated source chunks, effectively performing one step of message passing within the LLM's attention mechanism. The paper positions itself as the first approach to directly modify the LLM's attention mechanism for structured data integration, contrasting with prior strategies that either serialize graph structure into text or use adapter modules to project graph data into token space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The PE-sharing strategy (source chunks share one positional range, target chunks share another) is an elegant mechanism that simultaneously reduces positional bias and context window consumption, making it practical for long-context structured reasoning. However, the work is limited to one-hop dependencies, a single 8B model family, and requires post-training with block attention to avoid distribution shift; multi-hop propagation and fine-tuning with Graph-KV are left as future work. The ARXIV-QA benchmark, while valuable, is small (60 questions), and the modest gains over Block-RAG on standard RAG benchmarks (1–5%) suggest the structural bias injection is most impactful in settings with strong native graph structure rather than implicit retrieval-order dependencies.
