## Problem

Existing LLM-based approaches to combinatorial optimization rely on intermediate steps such as code generation or solver invocation, requiring substantial domain expertise and limiting generality. End-to-end LLM solvers that directly map natural language problem descriptions to solutions remain highly limited, with even state-of-the-art models achieving only ~50% feasibility on small instances and large optimality gaps. This paper asks how to enable a lightweight LLM to serve as a unified, end-to-end CO solver across diverse NP-hard problems without code execution or problem-specific architectural customization.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a Text-Attributed Instance (TAI) prompt consisting of a natural language instruction describing the optimization objective and constraints, instance-specific attributes (node coordinates, customer demands, processing times, graph edges), and problem-specific heuristic features (e.g., top-2 nearest neighbors with distances for routing problems, shortest-processing-time jobs for scheduling) that bias the model toward promising solution regions without requiring explicit chain-of-thought. The output is a natural language solution string (a route sequence, vertex set, or job permutation) followed by the objective value, which is parsed back into the original solution space via regular expressions. During inference, Best-of-N sampling generates N candidates (default N=8) and selects the best feasible one.

## Method

The core contribution is a two-stage fine-tuning pipeline applied to Qwen2.5-7B via LoRA. Stage 1 (SFT) trains the model on 500K problem-solution pairs per problem, where solutions are generated by domain-specific solvers (LKH-3, QIG, Gurobi, OR-Tools CP-SAT), teaching the LLM to map TAI prompts to solution text via standard next-token prediction. Stage 2 (FOARL) addresses the over-greedy constraint violations observed after SFT by applying GRPO-based reinforcement learning with a composite reward: a feasibility-aware component that weights individual constraint satisfaction (with higher weights on easily-violated constraints like distance limits or vehicle capacity) plus an optimality-aware component based on the optimality gap. At inference, Best-of-N sampling with top-p decoding provides test-time exploration to compensate for the autoregressive model's inability to backtrack. The approach requires no problem-specific architecture changes and achieves 100% feasibility with 1.03–8.20% average optimality gap across seven NP-hard problems.

## Results

The fine-tuned Qwen2.5-7B (SFT+RL+BoN) achieves 100% feasibility on 6 of 7 CO problems (TSP, OP, CVRP, MVC, PFSP, JSSP) and 94% on MIS, with average optimality gaps of 1.03–8.20% across the seven problems. Average inference time is 9.8s per instance. On the Taillard JSSP benchmark (20×20), the method achieves a 20.01% optimality gap with N=64, outperforming GA (42.47%), L2D (31.96%), and StarJob (33.12%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method decisively outperforms all general-purpose LLMs (GPT-4o, Claude-Sonnet, DeepSeek-V3, Llama3.3-70B, Qwen2.5-72B) on both feasibility and optimality; for example, on TSP, GPT-4o achieves only 39% feasibility with a 33.79% gap versus 100% feasibility and 1.07% gap for the proposed method. Among reasoning models, DeepSeek-R1 (48% feasibility, 70.99% gap on TSP) and GPT-o1 (54% feasibility, 276% gap on TSP) are far surpassed, and their inference times (3.2–6.5 min) are 20–40× longer than the 9.8s of the proposed method. Among prompt-based strategies, SGE achieves the highest feasibility (84–98%) but still lags in optimality (e.g., 29.66% gap on TSP vs. 1.07%), while OPRO and PHP remain far behind on constrained problems like CVRP (21–33% feasibility). Against domain-specific heuristics (Table 2), the method outperforms OR-Tools, ACO, nearest-neighbor, farthest-insertion, Tsili, sweeping, parallel-savings, greedy, degree-based, Palmer's, NEH, SPT, FIFO, and ATC on most problem scales, particularly on small and medium instances where it achieves Gap@10 of 94–100% versus 0–99% for baselines. On the Taillard JSSP benchmark, the method (20.01% gap at N=64) beats GA (42.47%), L2D (31.96%), and StarJob (33.12%), confirming generalizability beyond the training distribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the text-attributed instance (TAI) framework from Jiang et al. (2024) for representing CO problems in natural language, and adopts GRPO (from DeepSeek-R1) as the RL training algorithm. It departs from prior LLM-for-CO work that relies on intermediate steps—code generation for heuristics (OPRO, LMEA, SGE) or solver invocation (Chain-of-Experts, LLMOPT)—by treating CO purely as a next-token prediction task. The key methodological departure is the two-stage SFT+FOARL pipeline, where SFT learns solution patterns from domain-specific solvers and RL corrects the over-greedy constraint violations that SFT alone introduces.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work demonstrates that a lightweight 7B-parameter LLM, fine-tuned with LoRA, can serve as a unified end-to-end solver across seven NP-hard CO problems without code execution or problem-specific architecture changes, outperforming frontier general-purpose and reasoning models. The practical implication is that non-experts could access CO solving through natural language, reducing reliance on domain-specific algorithm design. Key limitations include solving efficiency that lags behind traditional heuristics, and evaluation restricted to relatively small instances (≤100 nodes, ≤20×20 scheduling). Open questions include scaling to larger instances, more efficient textual representations, and hybrid approaches combining LLM solvers with classical heuristics.
