## Problem

Despite growing interest in ML surrogates for CFD, no standardized benchmark exists that simultaneously evaluates predictive accuracy, physical consistency (lift/drag fidelity), out-of-distribution generalization, and computational efficiency under realistic industrial data-scarcity constraints. This paper addresses that gap by organizing the NeurIPS 2024 ML4CFD competition (240+ teams) and providing a retrospective analysis of results, evaluation methodology, and the design principles underlying top submissions.

## Input / Output

Each sample is an unstructured point cloud of 250–300k nodes (from a structured C-mesh with y⁺≈1) cropped to [−2,4]×[−1.5,1.5] m, carrying per-node features of 2D inlet velocity, distance to the airfoil surface, and unit normals zeroed off-surface. The model must predict the full RANS solution—velocity (u_x, u_y), reduced pressure (p/ρ), and turbulent viscosity (ν_t)—plus a binary surface mask for force reconstruction. Training is deliberately scarce (103 NACA 4/5-digit airfoil samples at Re 3–5×10⁶), while evaluation spans 200 in-distribution and 496 out-of-distribution samples (Re 2–3 and 5–6×10⁶), making extrapolative generalization a core challenge.

## Method

The paper's core contribution is a multi-criteria evaluation framework on the LIPS platform that scores submissions on per-simulation normalized MSE accuracy, log-scaled speed-up, physics compliance (Spearman rank correlation and mean relative error for C_L and C_D), and OOD generalization, aggregated via a weighted linear combination. The four winning methods span distinct paradigms—mesh morphing with PCA and Gaussian Processes (MMGP), offset-based graph convolution on kNN-augmented meshes (OB-GNN), conditional neural fields with multiscale Fourier features and hypernetwork shift-modulation (MARIO), and surface-to-volume message passing (GeoMPNN)—yet all share the critical design principle of explicitly encoding geometric structure. Notably, the top entry (MMGP) outperformed the OpenFOAM solver on aggregate metrics, while deep learning methods achieved 300–600× speedups, revealing a persistent accuracy–efficiency trade-off that no single method fully resolves.

## Results

The top-ranked solution MMGP (Mesh Morphing Gaussian Process) achieved a global score of 84.68% with a 162.7× speed-up over OpenFOAM, outperforming the physical solver on the aggregate multi-criteria metric. Among deep learning methods, OB-GNN achieved 77.45% global score with 318.9× speed-up, MARIO reached 71.20% with 617.2× speed-up, and GeoMPNN scored 50.60% with 513.3× speed-up. The fully connected neural network baseline scored only 32.85%, establishing a clear performance gap. The evaluation combines ML accuracy (40%), physics compliance via lift/drag coefficients (30%), and out-of-distribution generalization (30%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MMGP (Rank 1, 84.68%) is the only method that surpasses the OpenFOAM physical solver on the aggregate global score, demonstrating that a classical PCA + Gaussian Process pipeline with mesh morphing can outperform the reference CFD solver under the competition's tailored multi-criteria evaluation. OB-GNN (Rank 2, 77.45%) achieves the best balance among deep learning methods, combining 318.9× speed-up with strong physics and OOD scores. MARIO (Rank 3, 71.20%) delivers the highest speed-up among the top three (617.2×) but trails MMGP and OB-GNN in accuracy, reflecting the trade-off between inference speed and field-level fidelity. GeoMPNN (Rank 4, 50.60%) achieves 513.3× speed-up but lags significantly in physics compliance and OOD generalization compared to the top three. All four winning methods substantially outperform the fully connected baseline (32.85%), with the gap driven primarily by geometric inductive biases (mesh morphing, graph connectivity, or coordinate-based neural fields) that the baseline lacks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper organizes and retrospectively analyzes the NeurIPS 2024 ML4CFD competition, a benchmark for learning surrogates of steady-state RANS aerodynamics over 2D NACA airfoils. It builds directly on the AirfRANS dataset (Bonnet et al., NeurIPS 2022) and the LIPS evaluation framework (Leyli-Abadi et al., NeurIPS 2022), while the top submissions apply and extend prior architectures: MMGP (Casenave et al., NeurIPS 2024) for mesh-morphing Gaussian Process regression, MARIO (Catalani et al., 2025) for modulated neural fields, and GeoMPNN (Helwig et al., 2024) for geometry-aware message passing. The competition situates itself within the broader SciML benchmarking effort (PDEBench, The Well, AhmedML) but is distinguished by its explicit multi-criteria scoring that jointly rewards predictive accuracy, physical consistency (lift/drag coefficients), and out-of-distribution generalization under data scarcity (~103 training samples).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The competition provides the first large-scale, standardized benchmark (240+ teams, 650 submissions) for industrial-relevant CFD surrogate modeling with a transparent, multi-criteria evaluation protocol, filling a gap between academic PDE benchmarks and deployment-ready industrial validation. A notable open question is whether the data-scarce regime (103 samples) that favored classical ML (MMGP) over deep learning would shift with more data, and whether boundary-layer fidelity can be improved sufficiently to enable differentiable aerodynamic shape optimization. The paper also highlights unresolved evaluation-methodology issues—training cost accounting, hardware fairness between CPU solvers and GPU inference, and the risk of overfitting to a single public dataset—that will shape future SciML benchmarking design.
