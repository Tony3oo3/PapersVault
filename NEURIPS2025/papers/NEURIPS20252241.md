## Problem

Denoiser-based iterative schemes such as Plug-and-Play and Cold Diffusion are widely used to solve MAP estimation in inverse problems, but they replace the intractable proximal operator of the negative log-prior with a pretrained denoiser that has no guarantee of matching that operator. This substitution breaks the variational interpretation of the algorithm, leaving practitioners without convergence guarantees or a clear understanding of what is being optimised. The paper asks whether a simple denoising-based recursion can be shown to provably converge to the true proximal operator, and at what rate.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes a degraded image y ∈ ℝ^d, a pretrained MMSE denoiser (or its neural network approximation) parameterised by noise level σ, and a convex data fidelity term f. It produces an approximation of the proximal operator prox_{-τ ln p}(y) after k inner iterations, which is then plugged into an outer proximal gradient descent loop to yield the MAP estimate. A key practical property is that the step sizes α_k = 1/(k+2) and noise levels σ_k² = τ/(k+1) are parameter-free, depending only on the regularisation parameter τ and requiring no knowledge of the prior's smoothness or condition number.

## Method

The core insight is that the MMSE Averaging recursion — a weighted average between a denoised version of the current iterate and the target y — can be rewritten via the Tweedie identity as gradient descent on a sequence of smoothed proximal objectives F_{σ_k}(x) = ½‖y−x‖² − τ ln p_{σ_k}(x), where p_{σ_k} is the prior convolved with a Gaussian of variance σ_k². This smoothing dramatically improves conditioning: the condition number of F_{σ_k} is 1 + τ/σ_k², which stays bounded even when the unsmoothed prior has arbitrarily large L-smoothness. Under log-concavity of p and a bound on its third derivative, the iterates converge to the true proximal point at rate Õ(1/k), with the bound depending only on the third-derivative constant M rather than the (potentially unbounded) second-derivative constant. The approximate proximal operator is then integrated into proximal gradient descent, yielding O(1/n) convergence in MAP objective value when the inner loop length grows as k_n = ⌊c·n^{1+η}⌋.

## Results

The main theoretical result (Theorem 1) establishes that the MMSE Averaging recursion converges to the true proximal operator at rate Õ(1/k), i.e., ‖x_k − prox_{−τ ln p}(y)‖ ≤ ((ln k) + 7)/(k+1) · [‖y − prox(y)‖ + τ²M√d], where M bounds the third derivative of ln p. Crucially, this rate is independent of the L-smoothness constant of −ln p, which can be arbitrarily large. In the 2D Gaussian toy example (Section 5), naive gradient descent on F with condition number κ=500 stagnates, while gradient descent on the smoothed objectives (F_σk) converges rapidly, clearly exhibiting an O(1/k) rate. For the special case of a Gaussian prior (M=0), Proposition 5 shows the exact identity x_k − prox(y) = (y − prox(y))/(k+1), confirming the rate is tight up to the log factor.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is against naive gradient descent applied directly to the unsmoothed proximal objective F. For a Gaussian prior with Hessian eigenvalues in [1, L], naive GD requires O(L·log(1/ε)) iterations to reach ε-accuracy, with the rate degrading as L→∞. In contrast, the MMSE Averaging (smoothed) approach achieves Õ(1/k) regardless of L, since the convergence bound depends only on the third-derivative bound M (which is 0 for Gaussians). Compared to Cold Diffusion (Bansal et al., 2023), which uses a similar averaging structure but with α_k = k/N, the proposed method uses α_k = 1/(k+2) and σ_k² = τ/(k+1), yielding provable convergence whereas Cold Diffusion lacks guarantees and can diverge for large N. Against PnP-SGD (Laumont et al., 2023), which performs SGD on a fixed-smoothing F_σ, the proposed method uses a decreasing noise schedule σ_k→0, ensuring convergence to the true proximal point rather than the smoothed one, and avoids dependence on the (potentially unbounded) smoothness constant of F_σ. Standard PnP methods replace the proximal step with a generic denoiser without guaranteeing it matches the correct proximal operator, whereas this work provides a constructive approximation with explicit error bounds.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper provides the first rigorous convergence guarantee for a denoiser-based iterative scheme (MMSE Averaging) that is closely related to the heuristic Cold Diffusion algorithm of Bansal et al. (2023). It departs from the broader Plug-and-Play literature, which replaces the intractable proximal operator with a pretrained denoiser without theoretical justification, by showing that a specific denoising recursion can be reinterpreted—via the Tweedie identity—as gradient descent on a sequence of smoothed proximal objectives. The approach is conceptually closest to Laumont et al. (2023, PnP-SGD), but differs critically in using a decreasing noise schedule σ_k → 0 rather than a fixed σ, which is what enables convergence to the true (unsmoothed) proximal operator rather than a smoothed surrogate.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result provides a principled theoretical foundation for a large class of empirically successful but previously heuristic denoising-based inverse problem solvers, bridging the gap between practical PnP/Cold Diffusion methods and classical proximal optimization theory. The key practical insight is that the convergence rate is independent of the L-smoothness of −ln p (which can be arbitrarily large for ill-conditioned priors), depending only on a bound on the third derivative—a significant advantage over naive gradient descent. However, the guarantees require strong assumptions (log-concavity, bounded third derivatives, full support on R^d) that are far from satisfied by realistic image priors, and the paper acknowledges that extending to non-convex priors or low-dimensional manifold support remains open. The approximate-score extension (Proposition 6) partially addresses the practical gap of using a trained network rather than the exact MMSE denoiser, but the uniform error bound ξ is a simplification of real denoiser behavior.
