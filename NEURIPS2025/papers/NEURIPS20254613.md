## Problem

Most deepfake detectors are general-purpose and ignore the prior knowledge of a known target identity, while existing identity-aware methods rely primarily on global facial features and neglect fine-grained semantic details such as eye shape, lip thickness, and skin textures. This paper reformulates personalized deepfake detection as a fine-grained face recognition problem for specific VIP identities, requiring only a small number of authentic reference images per user and assuming source identities used in forgeries are unseen during training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254613/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes a single facial image to verify (resized to 448×448 if larger) and a pre-trained lightweight VIP token (a 32×d learnable vector) that encodes the target user's identity-specific features. The VIP token substitutes for the query image's visual features in the cross-attention module, so no reference images are needed at test time. The model outputs a binary real/fake decision (Yes/No) along with a human-readable textual explanation pinpointing specific facial attribute discrepancies (e.g., eye size, lip thickness, glabella wrinkles, moles) between the suspect image and the VIP's known appearance.

## Method

VIPGuard builds on Qwen-2.5-VL-7B through a three-stage progressive fine-tuning pipeline. Stage 1 uses LoRA to fine-tune the MLLM on ~30K high-resolution facial attribute VQA pairs, grounding the model in fine-grained facial semantics. Stage 2 introduces a cross-attention module that captures fine-grained differences between visual token sequences of two face images, conditioned on global similarity scores from a pre-trained face recognition model (TransFace), and trains the MLLM to reason over identity discrepancies across arbitrary face pairs. Stage 3 freezes all MLLM parameters and trains only a learnable VIP token (32×d) on VIP-specific face pairs, which replaces the query image's visual features during inference, enabling per-user customization without retraining. The key architectural insight is that combining global identity priors (face similarity scores) with local attribute priors (MLLM-learned facial descriptions) through cross-attention and semantic reasoning enables detection of subtle forgeries that global-feature-only methods miss.

## Results

On VIPEval face-swapping detection, VIPGuard achieves AUC/EER of 99.48/0.81 (BlendFace), 99.63/0.61 (HifiFace), and 99.69/0.26 (UniFace), substantially outperforming the best general detector Effort (97.16/8.52 on SimSwap) and the best ID-aware detector ICT-Ref (88.67/13.37 on BlendFace). On entire-face synthesis, VIPGuard reaches 99.69/0.45 (ConsistentID) and 99.76/0.23 (TongYi), while the strongest baseline RECCE only achieves 83.63/22.73 on GPT-4o-generated faces. In the MLLM comparison (Table 3), VIPGuard attains 95.51–96.71% ACC across face-swapping methods and 95.91% on ConsistentID, surpassing FFAA (88.60–91.36% / 63.53%) and GPT-4o (71.85–94.35% / 46.70%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VIPGuard dominates all 11 baselines across every face-swapping method, with the largest gaps over general detectors: Effort (best general method) trails by 2.3–13.2 AUC points (e.g., 97.16 vs. 99.43 on SimSwap), while older detectors like Xception and EfficientNet fall 35–65 AUC points behind. Among ID-aware methods, ICT-Ref (best prior ID-aware) lags VIPGuard by 10.8–17.0 AUC points (e.g., 88.67 vs. 99.48 on BlendFace), and DiffID is even further behind (83.33 vs. 99.48). In the MLLM comparison (Table 3), VIPGuard outperforms FFAA by 6.9–32.4 ACC points (e.g., 95.91 vs. 63.53 on ConsistentID) and GPT-4o by 23.7–49.2 points on the same metric. On the one-shot CelebDF/DF40 evaluation (Table 4), VIPGuard (Stage 2 only) edges out DiffID and ICT-Ref by 1.3–11.3 AUC points, confirming gains even with a single reference image. The only setting where VIPGuard's advantage narrows is GPT-4o-generated EFS faces (89.03/16.14 AUC/EER), where RECCE achieves 83.63/22.73 — still a 5.4 AUC gap but the smallest margin in the paper.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends identity-aware deepfake detection (ICT-Ref, DiffID) by reformulating the problem as fine-grained face recognition over an MLLM, rather than relying on global identity embeddings or reconstruction distances. It adapts the personalized token mechanism from Yo'LLaVA to encode VIP-specific identity priors in a lightweight, swappable form, and introduces an open-world assumption (source identities unseen at training) that departs from the closed-world setting of prior ID-aware methods. The three-stage training pipeline (facial attribute learning → general identity discrimination → user-specific customization) builds on standard LoRA fine-tuning of Qwen-2.5-VL-7B but integrates pre-trained face recognition scores (TransFace/ArcFace/CosFace) as explicit global priors fed into cross-attention, a design not seen in prior MLLM-based detectors like FFAA.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The VIPBench benchmark (80K images, 22 identities, 14 SOTA generators including commercial APIs like GPT-4o and KlingAI) fills a genuine gap in identity-aware evaluation, and the annotation-free Stage 3 result (~3% AUC drop) makes the system deployable without expensive per-user labeling. However, the approach is scoped to protecting a small set of known VIPs with available reference photos, and the authors acknowledge sensitivity to large pose changes (>50°) and age gaps between training and test images. Open questions include whether the fine-grained attribute reasoning generalizes to video/temporal settings, whether audio-visual fusion would close the remaining gap on GPT-4o-generated faces (89% AUC vs. ~99% on other methods), and whether the open-world assumption holds when attackers specifically mimic the VIP's unique attributes.
