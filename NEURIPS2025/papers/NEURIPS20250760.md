## Problem

Existing EEG foundation models (BIOT, LaBraM, CBraMod) are pretrained on fixed electrode montages—often the TUH 19/21-channel layout—and rely on rigid positional encodings that cannot accommodate arbitrary spatial configurations. This restricts their generalization to unseen electrode layouts, bipolar montages, or varying channel counts, forcing costly full fine-tuning for transfer and limiting performance under linear probing.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250760/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes multi-channel EEG signals (C×T matrix) paired with 3D electrode coordinates (C×3), segmented into overlapping 1-second temporal patches (0.1 s overlap) and resampled to 200 Hz with a 0.5–99.5 Hz band-pass filter. It handles arbitrary channel counts and sequence lengths (minimum 1 s, in 1 s multiples), and during inference produces a fixed-dimensional EEG representation embedding per input window that can be fed to a linear probe or fine-tuned classifier to yield a class label.

## Method

REVE introduces a 4D Fourier-based positional encoding that projects each token's (x, y, z, t) coordinates—augmented with Gaussian spatial noise—into a multi-frequency sinusoidal space, eliminating any dependence on a fixed montage or learned position table. Pretraining uses a masked autoencoder with a novel spatio-temporal block masking strategy that masks contiguous regions in both spatial (radius R_s around a channel) and temporal (radius R_t around a token) dimensions, plus a channel-level dropout, making reconstruction harder than random masking. A secondary loss applies attention pooling across all multi-head attention layers to form a single global token that must also reconstruct masked patches, forcing the encoder to distribute useful information across depth rather than concentrating it in the final layer. The transformer backbone uses RMSNorm, GEGLU activations, Flash Attention v2, and bias-free linear layers, and is pretrained on 60,000+ hours of EEG from 92 datasets and 25,000 subjects; downstream adaptation uses a two-step protocol (frozen linear probe followed by LoRA fine-tuning) combined with model souping.

## Results

REVE-Base achieves a mean balanced accuracy of 71.50% across 10 downstream EEG tasks, outperforming the strongest prior foundation model CBraMod (68.98%) by 2.5 percentage points on average. In linear probing (frozen encoder), REVE-Large reaches 65.4% mean balanced accuracy versus 50.1% for CBraMod, a gap of ~15 points, demonstrating substantially richer pretrained representations. On individual tasks, REVE-Base leads on 8 of 10 benchmarks, with the largest margins on BCIC-IV-2a motor imagery (63.96% vs. 51.38% for CBraMod) and mental stress detection (76.60% vs. 72.56%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

REVE-Base outperforms all three prior EEG foundation models (BIOT, LaBraM-Base, CBraMod) on 8 of 10 tasks, with the most dramatic gap on BCIC-IV-2a motor imagery where it achieves 63.96% versus 51.38% for CBraMod and 48.69% for LaBraM. Against non-foundation baselines, REVE-Base surpasses the strongest (ST-Transformer, 60.71% avg) by over 10 points, and EEGNet (59.41%) by 12 points. In the frozen-encoder (linear probing) setting, REVE-Large achieves 65.4% mean balanced accuracy versus 50.1% for CBraMod and 27.2% with pooling, confirming that REVE's pretrained features are substantially more informative without any fine-tuning. The pretraining ablation (Table 3) reveals that REVE gains 11% from pretraining (54.09%→64.80% on PhysioNet-MI) while CBraMod gains only 2% (61.96%→64.17%), indicating REVE's architecture is more receptive to self-supervised learning. REVE-Base slightly trails CBraMod on ISRUC sleep staging (78.19% vs. 78.65%), the only task where it does not lead, though the difference is within one standard deviation.

## Relation to Prior Work

REVE extends the masked autoencoder framework (He et al., 2022) to EEG by replacing the fixed positional encoding with a 4D Fourier-based scheme derived from actual electrode 3D coordinates and temporal indices, building on the 2D Fourier encoding of Defossez et al. (2023). It departs from prior EEG foundation models (BIOT, LaBraM, CBraMod) that rely on learned embedding tables or convolutional spatial encodings tied to fixed montages, instead generating position encodings on-the-fly to support arbitrary electrode layouts and sequence lengths. The spatio-temporal block masking strategy extends spatial masking (Mohammadi Foumani et al., 2024; Guetschel et al., 2024) into the temporal domain, and a novel secondary loss with cross-layer attention pooling addresses the over-specialization of the final encoder layer in standard MAE.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

REVE addresses the core fragmentation problem in EEG research—models trained on one montage or device fail on another—by natively supporting arbitrary electrode configurations, demonstrated by generalization to unseen bipolar montages (TUEV) and 30-second inputs beyond the 10-second pretraining window. The 60,000-hour, 92-dataset, 25,000-subject corpus is the largest non-invasive EEG pretraining effort to date, and the released open-source weights in three sizes lower the barrier for the community. Key limitations include demographic homogeneity (predominantly North American/European subjects), a minimum signal length of one second, and reliance on a relatively simple MAE objective; open questions include whether more advanced SSL objectives, scaling laws for EEG, and extension to MEG/iEEG/OPM-MEG will yield further gains.
