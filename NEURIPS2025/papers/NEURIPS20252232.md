## Problem

Existing 3D object annotation methods rely on single vision-language models that struggle simultaneously with spatial complexity, occlusion, cross-view inconsistency, and hallucination. No prior framework decomposes the annotation task into specialized collaborative agents with adaptive decision-making, leaving a gap in balancing accuracy, completeness, consistency, and throughput for large-scale 3D datasets.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252232/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes six standard-view 2D renderings (512×512 RGB) of a 3D mesh plus a Poisson-downsampled 10,000-point cloud as input. The VLM agent is invoked via a remote Qwen2.5-VL-72B-Instruct API (consuming no local GPU memory), generating 5 candidate descriptions per view at temperature 0.7. The output is a single coherent global caption (front/back core sentence concatenated with supplementary side/top/bottom details) and a binary quality flag: samples with point-cloud–text cosine similarity below α=0.557 are routed to manual review.

## Method

Tri-MARF is a three-agent pipeline. Agent 1 (VLM Annotation) uses a structured multi-turn prompting protocol with Qwen2.5-VL-72B-Instruct—viewpoint-aware identification, systematic attribute elicitation, and contextual integration—to produce M=5 stochastic descriptions per view, each scored by average token log-likelihood. Agent 2 (Information Aggregation) first clusters candidates via RoBERTa embeddings and DBSCAN, weights survivors by a softmax over CLIP image–text similarity combined with VLM confidence, then applies a UCB1 multi-armed bandit to adaptively select the optimal description per view, balancing exploration and exploitation through a composite reward of VLM confidence and CLIP alignment; selected view descriptions are fused into a global caption with front/back prioritization. Agent 3 (Gating) encodes the 3D point cloud with Uni3D-L and the caption with CLIP-B/16, computing cosine similarity against a derived threshold (α=0.557) to suppress hallucinated annotations. The key architectural insight is replacing static aggregation rules with a reinforcement-learning-based bandit that learns per-object-type selection policies, combined with geometric-semantic gating to exploit intrinsic 3D structure that 2D-only methods overlook.

## Results

On Objaverse-LVIS (1k), Tri-MARF achieves a CLIPScore of 88.7 and ViLT R@5 of 45.2/43.8 (I2T/T2I), surpassing the best prior method (ScoreAgg: 80.1 CLIPScore, 37.8/36.0 ViLT R@5) by +8.6 CLIPScore and +7.4 ViLT R@5. On Objaverse-XL (5k) and ABO (6.4k), Tri-MARF maintains its lead with CLIPScores of 86.1 and 82.3 respectively. The framework also achieves the highest throughput at 12,000 objects/hour on a single NVIDIA A100 GPU, compared to 4k–9k for competing methods and 0.12k for human annotation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Tri-MARF outperforms all automated baselines across all three datasets and all metrics. Against the strongest competitor ScoreAgg, Tri-MARF gains +8.6 CLIPScore and +7.4/+7.8 ViLT R@5 on Objaverse-LVIS, with similar margins on Objaverse-XL (+7.6 CLIPScore) and ABO (+6.1 CLIPScore). Cap3D, 3D-LLM, ULIP-2, PointCLIP, and GPT4Point trail further behind, with GPT4Point and PointCLIP lagging by 20+ CLIPScore points. Notably, Tri-MARF also surpasses human annotation in CLIPScore (88.7 vs. 82.4 on Objaverse-LVIS; 82.3 vs. 78.9 on ABO) and ViLT retrieval, while being ~100× faster (12k vs. 0.12k objects/hour). In the cross-dataset generalization test (Table 2), Tri-MARF maintains its lead on ShapeNet-Core, ScanNet, and ModelNet40 with the smallest performance drop (−7.2% CLIPScore) compared to 9.8–15% drops for other methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant single-model paradigm in 3D captioning (Cap3D, 3D-LLM, PointCLIP, ULIP) by decomposing the annotation pipeline into three specialized agents that process tri-modal inputs (multi-view 2D images, text, and 3D point clouds). It repurposes the multi-armed bandit (UCB) framework—previously applied to content selection and viewpoint planning in vision systems—as an adaptive description-selection mechanism within an annotation workflow, a use not seen in prior 3D captioning work. The point cloud gating stage borrows from Uni3D's cross-modal alignment but applies it as a post-hoc hallucination filter rather than a primary encoding pathway, distinguishing it from PointCLIP or ULIP where point clouds drive the captioning process directly.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework's practical impact is significant: it has annotated approximately 2 million 3D models at 12k objects/hour on a single A100, addressing a real bottleneck in 3D dataset construction for autonomous driving and robotics. However, the ablation isolating the MAB component shows only a +0.7 CLIPScore gain over a simple max-combined-score heuristic, suggesting the multi-agent architecture's value lies more in the multi-view VLM pipeline and point cloud gating than in the RL-based selection per se. Open questions include whether the object-centric Uni3D gating can be extended to full scenes (acknowledged as a limitation), how agent communication strategies might reduce the 6–18 s VLM API latency, and whether the framework's gains persist when the underlying VLM is swapped for a smaller or locally-deployed model.
