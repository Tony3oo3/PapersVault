## Problem

Continual fine-tuning of LoRA-based LLMs on sequential tasks suffers from catastrophic forgetting, as updates to the low-rank matrices progressively erode the representations needed for previously learned tasks. Existing remedies—direct model editing (e.g., ROME, MEMIT) or stacking additional adapter modules—are either computationally expensive to locate the right parameters or impose persistent inference overhead. The paper asks whether one can design a fine-tuning method that maintains strong performance across all tasks while continuously adapting with small-scale datasets, without extra inference cost.

## Input / Output

The method consumes instruction-formatted text prompts (e.g., 'What is the topic of the following passage? Choose one from the options.') paired with input text, processed through a LoRA-adapted LLM (LLaMA-3.1-8B or T5-Large-Instruct) with a maximum source length of 512 tokens. Outputs are single class labels drawn from task-specific option sets (e.g., sentiment categories, entity types, NLI labels), with a maximum generation length of 50 tokens. Evaluation uses both Average Accuracy across sequential tasks and ROUGE-1 F1 for free-form label decoding.

## Method

DEAL decomposes continual LoRA fine-tuning into two coupled modules. First, a wavelet kernel-based knowledge retention module applies truncated SVD to the LoRA matrices A and B, modeling each as a sum of a core feature matrix X and a noise component D; a multi-layer wavelet neural network with heat-kernel filters (acting as low-pass filters over singular values) then extracts the denoised core representation X̂, with an inverse-free update rule that avoids costly matrix inversions. Second, a controlled knowledge updating module uses an MLP to superimpose new-task features onto the preserved core, producing updated matrices A′ or B′. Training minimizes a task loss plus asymmetric regularization: a higher-order ℓ₅-norm penalty on the wavelet retention parameters and a lower-order ℓ₂-norm penalty on the MLP adaptation parameters, ensuring retention parameters change less than adaptation parameters. At inference, the updated low-rank matrix simply replaces the original LoRA weights, so inference latency and memory remain identical to standard LoRA.

## Results

On the 4-task standard continual learning benchmark with T5-Large, DEAL achieves 78.5% Average Accuracy (AA) and 82.5% ROUGE-1, outperforming O-LoRA (71.2%/73.3%) by 7.3 and 9.2 percentage points respectively, and SeqLoRA (44.6%/44.6%) by over 33 points. On the 15-task large-scale benchmark with LLaMA-3.1-8B, DEAL reaches 74.6% AA, surpassing O-LoRA (73.2%) and SeqLoRA (45.2%) while approaching the PerTaskFT oracle (77.1%). In the broader comparison (Appendix G), DEAL attains 78.5% AA on the standard CL benchmark, exceeding the next-best method LB-CL (76.5%) and ProgPrompt (76.0%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DEAL consistently outperforms all eight compared baselines. Against O-LoRA [31], the closest LoRA-based competitor, DEAL gains 7.3 pp in AA on the 4-task benchmark (78.5% vs. 71.2% with T5) and 1.4 pp on the 15-task benchmark with LLaMA (74.6% vs. 73.2%), while also improving ROUGE-1 by 9.2 pp on the 4-task setting. In the broader non-LoRA comparison (Table 10), DEAL (78.5% AA) surpasses LB-CL [52] by 2.0 pp, ProgPrompt [51] by 2.5 pp, and LFPT5 [50] by 7.3 pp. The rehearsal-based Replay [46] (52.0%) and regularization-based EWC [47] (45.3%) and LwF [48] (52.9%) baselines trail DEAL by 26–33 pp, reflecting the difficulty of memory-free or distillation-only strategies in long task sequences. Prompt-based L2P [49] (60.5%) also falls well short at 18 pp below DEAL. Notably, DEAL also exceeds the PerTaskFT oracle on the 4-task benchmark (78.5% vs. 70.0% AA with T5), suggesting that its shared-representation strategy enables beneficial cross-task transfer that task-isolated fine-tuning cannot achieve.

## Relation to Prior Work

DEAL builds directly on LoRA's low-rank decomposition (Hu et al., 2021), treating the A and B matrices as the sole locus of continual adaptation. It departs from the subspace-isolation paradigm of O-LoRA (Wang et al., 2023), which enforces orthogonality in first-order gradient space, by instead applying a wavelet-kernel filter over the SVD of the LoRA matrices to separate task-relevant core features from redundant noise. The heat-kernel low-pass filtering draws on classical signal-processing ideas (large singular values as macro trends, small ones as high-frequency disturbance) and repurposes them for structured knowledge retention in a parameter-efficient fine-tuning context, while the asymmetric regularization (a ≥ b) between retention and adaptation branches is a new design choice not present in prior LoRA-CL methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DEAL offers a practical path for organizations that must sequentially adapt a single LoRA adapter to new tasks without retraining or storing past data, achieving near-oracle accuracy (within ~2–3 points of PerTaskFT) while keeping inference latency identical to vanilla LoRA. However, the ~43% training-throughput penalty, the restriction to classification-style tasks, and the assumption of a fixed task order and static adapter rank limit immediate deployment. Open questions include whether the wavelet-based retention generalizes to generative or multi-turn settings, how to handle ambiguous task boundaries, and whether dynamic capacity allocation could close the remaining gap to the oracle baseline.
