## Problem

Prior methods for polarized community discovery (PCD) in signed networks typically optimize polarity, which tends to produce highly size-imbalanced solutions with multiple empty clusters. This paper addresses that limitation by proposing a new objective that simultaneously penalizes large/sparse clusters and cluster-size imbalance, while still allowing vertices to remain neutral (unassigned).

## Input / Output

The method takes a signed network as a sparse adjacency matrix A ∈ {-1,0,+1}^{n×n} (extensible to arbitrary real-valued similarities), a user-specified number of non-neutral clusters k, and a regularization parameter β controlling the trade-off between cluster density and size. It outputs k disjoint non-neutral clusters S_1,…,S_k and a neutral set S_0 = V \ ∪S_m. The algorithm is fully unsupervised and requires no ground-truth labels; α is fixed at 1/(k−1) throughout.

## Method

The core contribution is a novel objective (Eq. 3) that augments the standard polarity terms with a −β∑|S_m|² regularization, which shifts intra-cluster similarities by −β and simultaneously penalizes both sparsity and size imbalance. The key technical insight is connecting this discrete optimization to block-coordinate Frank-Wolfe (FW) optimization: each vertex is a block constrained to a (k+1)-dimensional simplex (soft membership across k clusters plus neutral), and Theorem 2 proves that starting from a discrete solution, each FW step reduces exactly to a local search reassignment (Alg. 2). Theorem 3 then establishes an O(1/t) linear convergence rate by extending the multilinear analysis of [43] to this non-multilinear objective. For scalability, Algorithm 3 precomputes M = 2AX and updates it incrementally per reassignment, reducing per-iteration cost to O(n+k) and total complexity to O(kn² + T(n+k)), enabling the method to handle networks with 100K+ vertices in seconds to minutes.

## Results

On seven real-world signed networks (BTC, WikiV, REF, SD, WikiC, EP, WikiP) with k=4, LSPCD achieves polarity scores of 23.3–139.2 while maintaining imbalance factors of 0.27–0.58, consistently outperforming or matching all baselines in the polarity–balance trade-off. For k=2 on BTC, LSPCD reaches polarity 29.0 with IF 0.65, compared to SCG-MA's 28.8 with IF 0.16 and N2PC(γ=1)'s 29.6 with IF 0.02. On synthetic m-SSBM graphs, LSPCD is the only method that recovers ground-truth clusters (F1 > 0.9) for noise levels η > 0.2 when k ≥ 4. KOCG and the spectral methods BNC/SPONGE are far behind in polarity (e.g., 8.4 vs. 23.3 on BTC, k=4) and BNC/SPONGE exceed memory limits on datasets with >10K vertices.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SCG (Tzeng et al., NeurIPS 2020), LSPCD matches or exceeds polarity on most datasets while achieving substantially higher imbalance factors (e.g., IF 0.47 vs. 0.22 on BTC k=4; IF 0.54 vs. 0.25 on SD), indicating SCG's higher polarity on some datasets comes at the cost of near-empty clusters. Against KOCG (Chu et al., KDD 2016), LSPCD dominates by a wide margin in polarity (e.g., 139.2 vs. 15.0 on REF, k=4), confirming that the constrained quadratic optimization approach is far less effective than local search for this problem. Against BNC (Chiang et al., CIKM 2012) and SPONGE (Cucuringu et al., AISTATS 2019), LSPCD outperforms on all datasets where both methods run, and BNC/SPONGE fail entirely on the four largest datasets (SD, WikiC, EP, WikiP) due to memory limits in their k-means step. Against N2PC (Gullo et al., ML 2024), which is restricted to k=2, LSPCD achieves comparable polarity (29.0 vs. 29.6 on BTC) but with a much healthier IF (0.65 vs. 0.02 at γ=1); increasing N2PC's γ toward 2.0 improves IF to 1.00 but drops polarity to 24.1, illustrating the trade-off that LSPCD handles more gracefully through its β regularization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the local search paradigm for correlation clustering (CC) to the polarized community discovery (PCD) setting, where neutral (unassigned) vertices are permitted. It builds on block-coordinate Frank-Wolfe optimization [29] and the non-convex convergence analysis of Thiel et al. [43] for CC, adapting both to handle the neutral-set constraint. The key departure from prior PCD work (SCG [44], KOCG [12], N2PC [21]) is replacing the standard polarity objective (which normalizes by cluster size) with an additive squared-size regularization term, drawing on the similarity-shift idea from Haghir Chehreghani [10] for unsigned networks. This is the first local search algorithm for PCD that explicitly handles neutral vertices while scaling to large graphs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a well-documented practical failure mode of prior PCD methods: optimizing polarity alone frequently yields degenerate solutions with empty or near-empty clusters, making results hard to interpret. The O(1/t) convergence guarantee for a non-concave, non-multilinear objective is a theoretically clean result that strengthens the case for local search in this setting. However, no real-world PCD datasets with ground-truth neutral assignments exist, so evaluation relies on polarity as a proxy metric, leaving open the question of whether the balance-polarity trade-off the paper optimizes truly matches user expectations. Parameter sensitivity (α, β) also remains a practical concern, though the paper shows robustness to β in experiments.
