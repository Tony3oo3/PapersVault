## Problem

Robotic manipulation policies fail to generalize when encountering unexpected environmental variations (novel objects, obstacles, barriers) because they memorize surface-level patterns rather than capturing invariant relational structures. Existing representation learning methods either focus on cross-modal alignment without temporal hierarchy or model temporal structure without explicitly encoding cross-modal correlations, leaving a gap in learning transferable manipulation concepts that bridge low-level actions to high-level goals.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253520/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-modal robot demonstrations consisting of 128×128 agent-view RGB, 128×128 eye-in-hand RGB (both compressed to 1024-dim vectors via Stable Diffusion VAE), a 9D proprioceptive state, and natural language task descriptions. It produces 256-dimensional L2-normalized manipulation concept latents at each timestep, which are continuous (not codebook-discrete) and organized into hierarchical sub-processes via spherical-distance coherence thresholds. The enhanced policy outputs both action chunks and predicted concept chunks from a shared backbone representation.

## Method

The framework operates in two stages. Stage 1 (concept discovery) uses a 12-layer transformer encoder to map multi-modal observation sequences into concept latents, trained with two joint objectives: (a) a Cross-Modal Correlation Network that stochastically masks random subsets of modalities and reconstructs all observations from the unmasked subset plus the concept, maximizing conditional mutual information across modality bipartitions; and (b) a Multi-Horizon Future Predictor that predicts terminal observations of sub-processes derived from concept latent coherence (spherical distance below threshold ε), with ε sampled uniformly per iteration to cover the full spectrum from short-horizon to long-horizon sub-goals. Stage 2 (policy enhancement) integrates concepts via joint prediction: the policy backbone produces a shared representation from which separate heads predict both ground-truth actions and corresponding concepts, using concept prediction as a regularizer that enforces the policy to encode conceptual understanding alongside action planning. This design requires no human annotation, no language supervision for concept learning, and is architecture-agnostic (validated with both ACT and Diffusion Policy).

## Results

On LIBERO-90 (original training tasks), HiMaCon-enhanced Diffusion Policy achieves 89.6% success rate, outperforming the best baseline XSkill (87.7%) by 1.9 points, while ACT reaches 74.8% vs. XSkill's 73.4%. On the more challenging LIBERO-LONG transfer setting (novel long-horizon compositions), DP achieves 89.0% vs. the next-best Next baseline at 83.0%, and ACT reaches 63.0% vs. RPT's 59.0%. On LIBERO-GOAL (entirely unseen environments), DP achieves 95.7% vs. DecisionNCE-task's 93.0%, and ACT reaches 81.0% vs. DINOv2's 77.3%. HiMaCon consistently outperforms all baselines across all three evaluation settings and both policy architectures.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HiMaCon outperforms all six baselines across every evaluation setting and both policy architectures. Against XSkill (the strongest baseline on L90-90), HiMaCon gains +1.4 pts (ACT) and +1.9 pts (DP) on original tasks, but the gap widens dramatically on transfer: +8.0 pts (ACT) and +16.0 pts (DP) on LIBERO-LONG, and +4.0/+2.7 pts on LIBERO-GOAL. Against RPT, the advantage is +6.0/+5.3 pts on L90-90 and a striking +27.7 pts (DP) on L90-L, suggesting RPT's temporal masking does not capture the hierarchical sub-goal structure. Against DecisionNCE (which uses language supervision), HiMaCon wins by +5.8/+6.9 pts (ACT/DP) on L90-90 without any language input, and by +10.3/+30.3 pts on L90-L. Against DINOv2 and CLIP (pretrained visual features), HiMaCon leads by +2.9/+10.2 pts (ACT/DP) on L90-90 and by +6.0/+26.0 pts on L90-L. Against InfoCon, the gap is largest on transfer settings: +7.5/+14.0 pts (ACT/DP) on L90-L, confirming that single-hierarchy concept discovery is insufficient for long-horizon generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper synthesizes two previously separate threads in robotic representation learning: cross-modal alignment (as in RPT, XSkill) and temporal/hierarchical structure (as in time-contrastive methods and skill discovery). Rather than building on a single prior architecture, it repurposes the mask-and-predict paradigm from MAE-style pretraining to enforce cross-modal conditional mutual information, and introduces a coherence-threshold-based sub-process derivation that organizes concept latents into multi-horizon sub-goals without explicit segmentation labels. For policy enhancement, it adopts the joint prediction approach (predicting concepts alongside actions) over the more common two-module architecture, positioning itself as a drop-in regularizer compatible with diverse policy backbones like ACT and Diffusion Policy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a practical, annotation-free recipe for extracting interpretable manipulation structure from raw multi-modal demonstrations, with validation on both simulation (LIBERO) and a real Mobile ALOHA robot. The key open question is whether the threshold-based hierarchy derivation (Eq. 4) scales beyond the proof-of-concept setting, as the authors themselves acknowledge alternative derivation methodologies warrant investigation. Additionally, the inference-time strategy for labeling concepts (sliding window with fixed context length) is acknowledged as suboptimal, and the approach's dependence on multi-modal data (particularly proprioception) limits applicability to vision-only settings. The work opens a path toward more interpretable and transferable manipulation policies, but the generalization to truly open-world, unstructured environments beyond the tested scenarios remains unexplored.
