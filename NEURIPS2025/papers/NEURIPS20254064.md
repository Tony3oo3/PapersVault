## Problem

Most self-supervised learning methods for time series rely on handcrafted, aggressive data augmentations to generate diverse training views, but designing effective augmentations for signals (PPG, IMU, ECG, EEG) is particularly challenging due to signal-specific semantics. These augmentations impose task-specific inductive biases, can suppress critical features, and risk model collapse when transformations are too strong. The paper asks whether principled, information-preserving geometric transformations can replace augmentations entirely for contrastive representation learning.

## Input / Output

The method consumes real-valued time series of length L with C channels (e.g., wrist PPG, 3-axis IMU, 12-lead ECG, single-channel EEG at 100 Hz). During training, each sample is projected into three domains: the original time domain, the discrete Fourier transform (fed in polar coordinates as separate magnitude and angle channels), and a Gabor wavelet transform with 48 logarithmically spaced scales. At inference, only the time-domain encoder and two lightweight latent-space mappers (<1k parameters each) are used to produce a 128-dim representation per domain, which are then concatenated or individually probed with a linear classifier/regressor.

## Method

The core idea is to replace data augmentations with two fixed, information-preserving projections: the DFT (an orthonormal basis) and the Gabor wavelet transform (an overcomplete frame), which are complementary in capturing global periodic structure versus localized time-frequency content. Three separate ResNet encoders process the time, Fourier, and wavelet views, and a normalized temperature-scaled cross-entropy (NT-Xent) instance discrimination loss is computed across all three pairwise domain combinations. The paper proves (Proposition 2.1) that unintended invariances cannot minimize this loss, and (Proposition 2.2) that representations of the same sample across domains concentrate near orthogonality while pairwise angular differences span the full range, justifying the use of non-linear latent-space mappers rather than affine alignments. Two lightweight 1D convolutional mappers (Φ) are jointly trained to translate the time-domain embedding into the Fourier and wavelet latent spaces, enabling the complementary manifold geometries to be exploited at inference without running the auxiliary encoders or transformations.

## Results

Across nine datasets spanning five temporal tasks, the method achieves up to 20–30% improvements on individual tasks, with an average gain of 10–15% over both general SSL and sequence-specific baselines. On USC-HAD activity recognition, the method reaches 52.21% accuracy versus 35.40% for the best prior method (TS2Vec), a ~47% relative improvement. On DaLiA heart rate estimation, it achieves ρ = 63.72 (vs. 62.77 for TS-TCC) and MAE = 9.13 (vs. 10.23), while on Sleep-EDF it reaches 77.30% accuracy and κ = 69.16 (vs. 74.69% and 65.31 for SimMTM). The method uses only 128-dim encoders per domain (384 total) versus 384-dim encoders for baselines, achieving these gains with roughly half the parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all ten baselines on the majority of datasets. Against general SSL methods (SimCLR, BYOL, VICReg, Barlow Twins, CLIP), the gains are substantial: on USC-HAD, the method achieves 52.21% accuracy versus 23.75–29.16% for these baselines, and on HHAR it reaches 70.67% versus 38.05–49.64%. Among temporal-specific methods, TS2Vec is the strongest competitor on HHAR (67.13% vs. 70.67%) and Clemson (MAPE 5.92 vs. 5.16), while TS-TCC is closest on Clemson step counting (MAE 2.70 vs. 2.50). On Chapman CVD classification, the method achieves 87.21% accuracy and 96.50 AUC, surpassing TF-C (85.84%, 96.10) and SimMTM (84.29%, 95.87). The paper further shows that simply scaling backbone size by 2× for SimCLR, BYOL, and Barlow Twins does not close the gap, and adding a third augmented view to prior methods still leaves a ~10% deficit, confirming that the gains stem from the principled frame-projection views rather than model capacity or view count.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant augmentation-based SSL paradigm (SimCLR, BYOL, VICReg, Barlow Twins) by replacing handcrafted data augmentations with fixed, principled mathematical transformations—specifically a discrete Fourier transform (orthonormal basis) and a Gabor wavelet transform (overcomplete frame)—to generate views for instance discrimination. It builds on the multiview contrastive learning line (CMC [68], TF-C [47]) but eliminates the need for task-specific augmentations entirely, and extends latent-space mapping work [62, 63] by introducing non-linear mappers motivated by a concentration-of-measure argument (Proposition 2.2) showing that pairwise geometry, not point-wise alignment, is what must be preserved across domain-specific manifolds.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper challenges the widely held assumption that strong, asymmetric augmentations are necessary for effective self-supervised representation learning, showing that unitary and frame-based projections—which preserve all information—can outperform augmentation-based methods by 10–20% across nine time-series datasets. This is practically significant because it removes the need for domain-specific augmentation design, a major bottleneck for signals where semantic relevance of transformations varies across tasks. Key limitations include the absence of a formal theoretical explanation for the performance gains, the restriction to time-series/physiological signals (no evidence for images or audio), and the computational cost of wavelet transforms. Open questions include whether the same augmentation-free principle extends to other modalities and whether the method can be adapted for forecasting tasks.
