## Problem

Video relighting for long, highly dynamic sequences remains unsolved: existing generative relighting models are either restricted to portrait domains, struggle with temporal flicker when naively extended from image models, or incur prohibitive computational costs (OOM) on videos exceeding a few hundred frames. The core gap is achieving physically plausible, temporally coherent illumination and texture re-rendering at low computational overhead when camera motion is complex and foreground objects frequently enter and exit the scene.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251715/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes an RGB video (averaging ~256 frames at resolutions from 640×480 to 1280×720), a free-form text prompt specifying target illumination, and optionally per-frame depth maps. Optical flow is estimated on-the-fly via MemFlow. The output is a relighted video at the same resolution and frame count, where the two-stage post-optimization operates per-scene (no training) and the UVT representation compresses the video into a 1D RGB vector of shape (N, 3) indexed by flow IDs and 7-bit quantized colors, enabling the entire optimization to complete in roughly 2 minutes with ~14 GB peak VRAM on a single A100.

## Method

TC-Light first inflates the image relighting model IC-Light to video space in a zero-shot manner by combining VidToMe's intra/inter-chunk token merging (for spatiotemporal attention) with a decayed multi-axis denoising scheme that adaptively blends xy-plane (prompt-conditioned) and yt-plane (unconditional) noise predictions, using exponential decay of the mixing weight and Adaptive Instance Normalization to prevent the unconditional branch from biasing texture toward the source. The core contribution is a two-stage per-scene post-optimization: Stage I introduces a per-frame 3×4 affine appearance embedding optimized with photometric and flow-warp losses to align global exposure across adjacent frames; Stage II compresses the Stage I output into a Unique Video Tensor (UVT)—a 1D vector where each element aggregates all pixels sharing the same spatiotemporal index (flow ID + quantized RGB, optionally depth voxel)—and optimizes this tensor with total variation, SSIM, and flow-warp losses to refine fine-grained texture and lighting while enforcing cross-frame consistency. The UVT is differentiable, reconstructs the source video with near-zero loss (PSNR > 46 dB), and reduces the optimization target from full video to a compact canonical representation, making the entire pipeline complete in ~10 minutes with minimal VRAM overhead.

## Results

On the established long-video benchmark (58 clips, ~256 frames each), Ours-full achieves the best temporal consistency with Motion-S of 97.80% and Warp-SSIM of 91.75, while Ours-light reaches 97.39% and 88.53 respectively. On Virtual KITTI 2 with ground-truth relighting, Ours-full attains the highest SSIM (0.5910) and lowest LPIPS (0.3971), outperforming all baselines. In terms of efficiency, Ours-light completes a 256-frame clip in 771 s with 14.36 GB VRAM, adding only ~2.4 min and 2.7 GB over the VidToMe baseline, whereas Cosmos-Transfer1 requires 2543 s and 34.87 GB. Three training-based baselines (VideoDirector, Light-A-Video, RelightVid) fail entirely with out-of-memory errors on long clips.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TC-Light outperforms all seven baselines on temporal consistency metrics. Against the closest zero-shot baseline Slicedit, Ours-full improves Warp-SSIM from 85.37 to 91.75 (+6.4) and Motion-S from 96.48% to 97.80%, while also reducing runtime from 2101 s to 1255 s. Against Cosmos-Transfer1 (the strongest training-based method that does not OOM), Ours-full gains +8.3 in Warp-SSIM (91.75 vs 83.47) and +0.97% in Motion-S, while using less than half the VRAM (14.37 vs 34.87 GB) and completing in roughly half the time. The per-frame IC-Light* baseline, while achieving the highest CLIP-T (0.2743), suffers severe flicker (Warp-SSIM only 71.22) and the lowest user preference among non-OOM methods (10.97%). Three dedicated video relighting methods—VideoDirector, Light-A-Video, and RelightVid—fail entirely with OOM on 256-frame clips, highlighting the computational bottleneck of training-based approaches on long, dynamic videos. On Virtual KITTI 2 with ground truth, Ours-full leads in both SSIM (0.5910 vs 0.5359 for VidToMe) and LPIPS (0.3971 vs 0.4237 for Slicedit), confirming superior structural and perceptual fidelity to the target relighting.

## Relation to Prior Work

TC-Light builds on the image relighting model IC-Light and inflates it to video space using zero-shot strategies borrowed from VidToMe (token merging/unmerging) and Slicedit (multi-axis denoising), then introduces a two-stage post-optimization framework as its core contribution. The first stage (per-frame affine appearance embedding) follows the exposure-alignment idea from 3D Gaussian splatting literature, while the second stage proposes the Unique Video Tensor (UVT)—a 1D canonical representation that deliberately departs from the heavier NeRF/3DGS-based canonical representations used by CoDeF and Video-3DGS. The paper positions itself against training-based video relighting methods (Light-A-Video, RelightVid) and world-generation models (Cosmos-Transfer1), arguing that an optimization-based paradigm avoids both domain restriction and prohibitive compute on long videos.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck: existing video relighting methods either fail on long, highly dynamic videos (OOM) or produce temporally inconsistent results, limiting their use for sim2real data scaling in embodied AI. The UVT representation offers a lightweight, differentiable alternative to 3D scene representations for video editing, and the established 58-video benchmark (averaging 256 frames) fills a gap in evaluation for this setting. Key limitations remain: the method inherits IC-Light's weaknesses on hard shadows and low-light scenes, depends on optical flow quality (degrading under very fast motion or in textureless regions), and the temporal consistency loss can over-smooth fine texture. Open questions include whether the optimization-based paradigm scales to multi-minute videos and whether the UVT can be made more expressive without losing its efficiency advantage.
