## Problem

One-step diffusion sampling methods (distillation, consistency models) achieve fast inference by learning direct noise-to-image mappings, but they discard the temporally coherent denoising trajectory that makes diffusion models interpretable and controllable. This paper asks whether one can obtain one-step generation while retaining full analytical access to intermediate generative states, enabling spectral interpretation and mid-trajectory intervention.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252921/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single Gaussian noise sample x_T (drawn from the forward diffusion process of a pretrained EDM backbone) and produces a near-clean image x_ε in one forward pass, with no iterative denoising. The encoder operates on images at the dataset resolution (32×32 for CIFAR-10, 64×64 for FFHQ) and produces a pyramid of multi-scale latent features; the decoder reconstructs the full-resolution image from the evolved latent set. Because the Koopman evolution is closed-form, any intermediate state x_t for t ∈ [ε, T] is analytically recoverable from the same noise input, which is the key property enabling interpretability and editing.

## Method

HKD lifts the nonlinear Probability Flow ODE dynamics into a hierarchical Koopman space: a U-Net encoder extracts features at L spatial resolutions, and at each level l and spatial position (i,j), the latent vector evolves under a spatially-varying 2×2 block-diagonal linear operator A^(l)(i,j) with complex-conjugate eigenvalues α±iβ. The closed-form solution z_t = e^{A(t−s)} z_s replaces iterative integration with a single matrix exponential, yielding one-step sampling. A trajectory consistency loss supervises all intermediate states by requiring that encoding x_t, evolving it to ε via the Koopman dynamics, and decoding yields the ground-truth x_ε, using a combined MSE + LPIPS metric. The encoder, decoder, and all Koopman matrices are trained end-to-end (initialized from pretrained EDM weights), and a theoretical result (Thm. 3.1) shows the HKD estimation error is bounded above by the best one-step network error plus a small Koopman approximation term, providing a provable expressiveness advantage over black-box noise-to-image mappings.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, HKD achieves a FID of 3.30 with a single NFE, outperforming all distillation-based one-step methods (best: 2-Rectified flow++ at 3.38) and matching CD(LPIPS) (3.55) and ECM (3.60) within 0.3 FID. On FFHQ 64×64, HKD reaches FID 5.70 at 1 NFE, beating ECM (5.99) and substantially outperforming multi-step baselines like EDM at 15 NFE (9.85) and DDIM at 10 NFE (18.30). The method trains in 2–3 days on 8×V100 GPUs, compared to nearly a week for state-of-the-art consistency training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On CIFAR-10, HKD (FID 3.30) outperforms all distillation-based one-step methods, beating 2-Rectified flow++ (3.38) by 0.08, CD-LPIPS (3.55) by 0.25, DMD (3.77) by 0.47, and the Rectified Flow variants (4.85–6.18) by 1.5–2.9 FID. It also surpasses the consistency model ECM (3.60) by 0.30 FID. However, HKD trails the strongest consistency-model baselines iCT (2.83) and iCT-deep (2.51) by 0.47 and 0.79 FID respectively, though the authors note these methods require significantly longer training (~1 week on 8 GPUs) and are more sensitive to hyperparameters. On FFHQ 64×64, HKD (5.70) beats ECM (5.99) by 0.29 FID and substantially outperforms multi-step baselines at comparable or fewer NFE (EDM-15: 9.85, DDIM-10: 18.30). Overall, HKD positions itself as the best interpretable one-step generator, trading a small FID gap to iCT-deep for full trajectory access and spectral controllability that no competing method provides.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of Koopman operator theory (Mezić, 2005) and score-based diffusion models (Song et al., 2020), applying the former's linearization of nonlinear dynamics to the latter's Probability Flow ODE. It departs from the dominant one-step generation paradigms—distillation (KD, PD, DMD, Rectified Flow) and consistency models (CT, iCT, ECM)—which learn implicit black-box noise-to-image mappings, by instead learning an explicit linear trajectory in a lifted Koopman space with closed-form solutions. The hierarchical design with scale-specific Koopman subspaces and the trajectory consistency loss are novel architectural and training contributions not present in prior one-step methods. The authors claim to be the first to introduce Koopman theory into image generation, repurposing a tool from dynamical systems analysis for generative modeling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine gap: one-step diffusion methods sacrifice the interpretability and intermediate-state controllability that make diffusion models attractive for editable generation. By providing spectral analysis of the generative trajectory and enabling frequency-specific interventions at intermediate stages, HKD opens a path toward explainable and controllable one-step synthesis. However, the experiments are limited to CIFAR-10 and FFHQ 64×64, the FID (3.30) is competitive but not SOTA (iCT-deep achieves 2.51), and the theoretical guarantees rely on structural assumptions (isotropy, level disentanglement, spectra near 1) whose practical validity is not thoroughly validated. Open questions include scaling to high-resolution generation, integrating adversarial training, and extending spectral interventions to text-guided or attribute-specific editing.
