## Problem

Current MLLMs struggle with spatial reasoning from video due to two bottlenecks: uniform temporal sampling produces visually homogeneous keyframes that lack salient spatial structure, and the absence of explicit ego-motion cues forces models to rely on commonsense priors rather than grounded visual evidence for inferring object displacement and scene layout. While prior work has incorporated depth maps or point clouds, purely vision-only spatial understanding under these constraints remains largely unexplored.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a video clip (sampled at one frame per N frames, with N=4 as default), a text spatial-reasoning question, and a camera intrinsics matrix (shown to be robust to arbitrary values including identity or random matrices). It produces a set of K augmented keyframes (default K=8) each bearing a color-coded circular marker and frame index, a top-down BEV trajectory image, a 3D trajectory plot, and a structured text prompt containing relative spatial coordinates — all fed into the MLLM in a single forward pass to yield a spatial reasoning answer.

## Method

SEE&TREK is a training-free, GPU-free spatial prompting framework built on two complementary mechanisms. First, Maximum Semantic Richness Sampling runs YOLOv8-Tiny on uniformly subsampled frames and applies a Balanced-TopK strategy that greedily selects K keyframes minimizing category overlap with an accumulated pool while maximizing detected-class count and temporal spread, ensuring both semantic richness and diversity. Second, Motion Reconstruction estimates relative camera poses between consecutive sampled frames via ORB feature matching, essential matrix decomposition (RANSAC + SVD + cheirality), and recursive global pose accumulation, then renders the resulting trajectory as BEV and 3D visualizations. A Spatiotemporal Encoding step overlays each selected keyframe with a color from a continuous colormap (matching the trajectory) and its frame index, explicitly linking semantic content to motion progression. These augmented frames, trajectory images, and a text prompt with relative coordinates are jointly fed to the MLLM as a plug-and-play, single-forward-pass enhancement requiring no model modification.

## Results

On VSI-BENCH, SEE&TREK consistently improves all 10 evaluated open-source MLLMs, with average accuracy gains ranging from +1.0% (Qwen2.5-VL-3B, Qwen2.5-VL-32B) to +3.5% (InternVL3-1B). The most notable task-specific improvements include +10.8% on Approach Order for LLaVA-OneVision-7B and +9.1% for InternVL3-8B. On STI-BENCH, gains are more modest but consistent (+1.0% to +1.7% average), with standout improvements in dynamic understanding tasks such as +16.2% on Ego-Centric Orientation for Qwen2.5-VL-7B and +8.6% on Trajectory Description for InternVL3-14B. The method requires only a single forward pass and no GPU, with N=4 sampling achieving the best accuracy (43.2% Avg.) in just 82 seconds per video.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is against each MLLM's own baseline (without SEE&TREK), where the method achieves consistent positive gains across all 10 open-source models on VSI-BENCH (+1.0% to +3.5% Avg.) and all 5 on STI-BENCH (+1.0% to +1.7% Avg.), with smaller models benefiting most. Against other visual prompting methods, SEE&TREK (43.2% Avg.) outperforms Set of Mark (SOM, 41.3%) and Coarse Correspondence (CC, 40.9%) on VSI-BENCH with InternVL3-8B, demonstrating superior spatial cue injection. Compared to proprietary models (GPT-4o at 34.0%, Gemini-1.5 Pro at 45.4% on VSI-BENCH), the best SEE&TREK-enhanced open-source model (InternVL3-14B at 45.6%) slightly surpasses Gemini-1.5 Pro, while the 8B model (43.2%) approaches it. On STI-BENCH, SEE&TREK-enhanced Qwen2.5-VL-32B (41.7%) approaches Gemini-2.5-Pro (40.9%) and surpasses GPT-4o (34.8%). The method also shows gains on OPENEQA (+1.8% to +2.0%), confirming generalizability beyond spatial-specific benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of enhancing MLLM spatial understanding by injecting auxiliary modalities (depth maps, point clouds, explicit camera poses, 3D priors) into model training or inference pipelines. Instead, SEE&TREK repurposes two well-established computer vision components—YOLO-based object detection and ORB/RANSAC visual odometry—into a purely visual, training-free prompting strategy that operates entirely in the pre-processing stage. It also extends the line of query-based and heuristic frame-selection work for long video understanding by introducing the Balanced-TopK criterion, which jointly optimizes for semantic richness, temporal spread, and category diversity. Compared to prior visual prompting methods like Set-of-Masks (SOM) and Coarse Correspondence (CC), SEE&TREK adds an explicit motion-trajectory channel (BEV + 3D plots) and spatiotemporal color encoding that those static-label approaches lack.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical value lies in offering a zero-cost, plug-and-play module that can be bolted onto any existing MLLM (open-source or API-based) without retraining or GPU access, which is attractive for deployment in resource-constrained or proprietary-model settings. A notable open question is whether the gains (modest +1–3.5%) scale with stronger perception backends; the paper's own ablation with Grounding DINO and YOLO-World shows only marginal additional improvement, suggesting the bottleneck may shift to the MLLM's internal spatial representation rather than input quality. Limitations include dependence on YOLO's detection recall in cluttered scenes, the use of a fixed KITTI intrinsic matrix (though shown to be non-critical), and evaluation restricted to indoor/semi-structured video benchmarks, leaving outdoor and highly dynamic settings untested.
