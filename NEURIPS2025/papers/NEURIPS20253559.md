## Problem

While DiLoCo reduces communication costs in distributed LLM training via periodic synchronization across model replicas, prior work has not characterized how its behavior changes with model size. Without scaling laws, it is unclear whether DiLoCo's advantages over data-parallel training persist at frontier scales, and its additional hyperparameters (outer learning rate, replica count, synchronization cadence) make manual tuning infeasible at large model sizes.

## Input / Output

The framework takes a Chinchilla-style decoder-only transformer of size N (35M–10B parameters), a replica count M ∈ {1,2,4,8}, and a token budget D (Chinchilla-optimal or overtrained), and produces trained models along with predicted evaluation loss and optimal hyperparameters (inner learning rate, global batch size, outer learning rate) as closed-form power-law functions of N and M. Models are trained on C4 (or Dolma for overtraining ablations) at sequence length 2048, and evaluated on C4 validation loss plus zero-shot accuracy on HellaSwag, Piqa, and Arc-Easy.

## Method

The paper develops scaling laws for DiLoCo by performing extensive hyperparameter sweeps (inner learning rate, global batch size, outer learning rate) across 7 model sizes from 35M to 2.4B parameters and 4 replica counts, then fitting two-variable power laws L(N,M) ≈ AN^α M^β to the best-performing loss, inner learning rate, and batch size. They compare independent per-M fits against joint fits, finding the joint fit extrapolates better for inner learning rate prediction. Key empirical findings include: DiLoCo with M=1 (an enhanced Lookahead optimizer) outperforms data-parallel at all scales without reducing communication; the optimal outer learning rate η is constant with N and depends only on M and synchronization cadence H; and optimal batch size increases with M, granting greater horizontal scalability. The scaling laws are validated by training 4B and 10B models using predicted hyperparameters, confirming DiLoCo M=2 outperforms data-parallel while reducing total communication by over 100×.

## Results

At 4B parameters, DiLoCo with M=1 achieves 2.219 evaluation loss (−0.22% vs. Data-Parallel's 2.224), and DiLoCo with M=2 achieves 2.220 (−0.18%). At 10B parameters, both DiLoCo M=1 and M=2 reach 2.086 (−0.19% vs. Data-Parallel's 2.090), while DiLoCo M=4 slightly underperforms at 2.096 (+0.29%). The scaling laws fitted on models up to 2.4B parameters accurately predict these larger-scale results, with joint-fit power laws extrapolating slightly better than independent fits for inner learning rate prediction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is standard distributed Data-Parallel training. DiLoCo with M=1 (a single-replica variant equivalent to an enhanced Lookahead optimizer) outperforms Data-Parallel at every model scale tested (35M through 10B), achieving lower evaluation loss and higher zero-shot accuracy on HellaSwag, Piqa, and Arc-Easy. DiLoCo with M=2 also outperforms Data-Parallel at 4B and 10B scales while reducing total communication by over 100×. DiLoCo with M=4 and M=8 underperforms Data-Parallel at larger scales, suggesting a sweet spot at M=1–2. A key differentiator is batch size tolerance: Data-Parallel's performance degrades sharply when batch size is doubled or quadrupled, whereas DiLoCo (especially M=1) maintains or improves performance, enabling greater horizontal scalability and significantly reduced wall-clock training time even when communication is not the bottleneck.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DiLoCo algorithm (Douillard et al. [11]) by developing the first comprehensive scaling laws for it, building on the scaling law frameworks established for data-parallel training by Kaplan et al. [26] and Hoffmann et al. [20]. While Jaghouar et al. [24] demonstrated DiLoCo's viability at moderate scales and He et al. [18] performed a preliminary scaling analysis with fixed hyperparameters, this work systematically varies the number of replicas M, fits parametric scaling laws for both loss and optimal hyperparameters (inner learning rate, batch size, outer learning rate), and validates predictions at 4B and 10B scales. The bi-level optimization structure of DiLoCo (inner AdamW + outer SGD with Nesterov momentum) introduces hyperparameters absent from data-parallel scaling laws, requiring new joint fitting strategies over both model size N and replica count M.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The scaling laws provide a practical tool to predict optimal hyperparameters for DiLoCo at scales where exhaustive tuning is infeasible, and the finding that DiLoCo with M=1 outperforms data-parallel training even without any communication reduction (attributed to the outer Nesterov-momentum step acting as an enhanced Lookahead optimizer) reframes DiLoCo's value beyond mere bandwidth savings. The increased optimal batch size and robustness to batch size changes have direct implications for horizontal scalability and wall-clock training time. Key limitations include validation only on dense Chinchilla-style transformers, two pretraining datasets (C4, Dolma), and an idealized wall-clock model; open questions include whether findings transfer to MoE architectures, multi-modal settings, and practical heterogeneous compute environments.
