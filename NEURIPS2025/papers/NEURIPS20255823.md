## Problem

Decentralized model-parallel training over consumer-grade networks is infeasible because existing communication compression techniques (sparsification, quantization, low-rank projection) are designed for data-parallel weight-gradient exchange and do not extend to compressing activations and activation gradients between pipeline stages. Unlike weight gradients, activations lack inherent redundancy, and approximation errors accumulate exponentially across layers, causing severe convergence degradation. This communication bottleneck restricts large-model training to datacenter clusters with 100Gbps interconnects, excluding resource-constrained participants.

## Input / Output

The method operates on per-layer activations of shape b×n×d (batch × sequence × embedding dim) and their corresponding gradients, along with locally available positional embeddings and a fixed high-rank token embedding table T_fixed. It produces compressed representations of shape b×n×k (with k=40, d=4096, yielding 100× compression) for both forward activations and backward gradients, transmitted between adjacent pipeline stages. Reconstruction at the receiving node is exact: the full b×n×d activation is recovered by multiplying the compressed tensor by U_k^T and adding back the locally computed PE and T_fixed lookup, introducing zero approximation error.

## Method

The key insight is that transformer projection matrices (W_p1, W_p2) naturally undergo rank collapse under AdamW training, with their gradients converging to low-dimensional subspaces. The paper explicitly enforces this by constraining the row spaces of all projection matrices to a shared k-dimensional subspace S spanned by an orthonormal basis U_k, which forces residual activations (layer output minus PE minus TE) to also lie in S, making projection onto U_k lossless. The token embedding is decomposed into a fixed high-rank component T_fixed (transmitted once at initialization) and a trainable low-rank component T_S = T_fixed U_k U_k^T, preserving representational capacity while enabling compression. In the backward pass, gradients flowing through the constrained projection matrices are automatically confined to S, so the same U_k projection achieves lossless gradient compression. The subspace U_k is periodically refined (every 500 iterations) via Riemannian gradient descent on the Grassmann manifold, minimizing the Frobenius norm of out-of-subspace gradient energy at the final layer, allowing the subspace to slowly track evolving gradient directions without disrupting convergence.

## Results

On an 8-layer (2B) LLaMA model trained for 12 hours, the decentralized compressed model over 80 Mbps links achieves perplexity of 46.75 (OWT), 17.63 (BC), and 23.01 (WT), matching or slightly surpassing the centralized 100 Gbps baseline (47.22, 18.35, 23.08) while delivering 592 TPS versus 36 TPS for the uncompressed decentralized model — a ~16× throughput gain. At 100× compression (k=40 out of d=4096), the method achieves lossless reconstruction with no convergence degradation. On an 8B-parameter LLaMA model across 64 L4 GPUs in 4 geographic regions (60–350 Mbps), the compressed decentralized setup matches the wall-clock time and TPS of a centralized 16 Gbps setup, whereas the uncompressed decentralized baseline is 13× slower.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against three lossy compression families at 100× compression rate (Figure 6): Top-K sparsification [Wangni et al., 2018], low-rank SVD projection (PowerSGD) [Vogels et al., 2019], and quantization [Alistarh et al., 2017]. All three fail to converge at 100× compression in the model-parallel setting, while the proposed subspace method matches the uncompressed baseline exactly. In the complementary DDP overlay experiment (Table 2), the proposed method is stacked on top of Top-K(10%) and PowerSGD: adding the subspace compression boosts TPS from 32 to 599 (Top-K) and from 29 to 532 (PowerSGD) while simultaneously improving perplexity (52.98→52.45 and 31.18→30.26), demonstrating that the two compression axes are orthogonal and jointly beneficial. Quantization is additionally limited to 16× compression by numerical precision (16-bit), making it infeasible at the 100× target.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the well-documented rank-collapse phenomenon in transformer projection matrices (observed in prior work on gradient structure) for a fundamentally different purpose: instead of compressing weight gradients as in DDP, it exploits the recursive skip-connection structure of transformers to confine residual activations to a shared low-dimensional subspace, enabling lossless compression of inter-layer activations in pipeline parallelism. It departs from Pufferfish [50], which also targeted low-rank MP communication but required architectural modifications, by imposing only a subspace constraint on projection matrices while preserving the standard transformer architecture. The Grassmann-manifold-based subspace drift is a practical addition that allows the fixed subspace to slowly adapt, bridging the gap between rigid projection and unconstrained training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that billion-parameter model-parallel training is feasible over consumer-grade internet (60–80 Mbps), matching datacenter-grade (100 Gbps) convergence — a result with direct implications for democratizing large-scale training beyond well-resourced organizations. Open questions include why compressed models sometimes outperform uncompressed baselines (possibly implicit regularization from the subspace constraint), whether the approach extends to non-transformer architectures or tensor parallelism, and whether tighter convergence guarantees can be derived for adaptive optimizers like AdamW under the subspace constraint. The method's reliance on a single shared subspace U_k across all layers and its specificity to pipeline parallelism are notable limitations for broader adoption.
