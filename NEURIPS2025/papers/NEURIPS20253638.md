## Problem

Existing LLM agents for social deduction games like Werewolf decouple strategic decision-making from language expression (e.g., RL policy network + LLM expressor), which the authors argue contradicts Wittgenstein's language game theory that language, intention, and action are intrinsically unified. This separation compresses the language strategy space into rigid structured representations, limiting generalization and cross-environment strategy transfer. The paper asks whether LLMs can learn strategic reasoning directly through in-context multi-agent interaction without such architectural separation.

## Input / Output

The model receives a structured prompt containing the player's role assignment, objective game information (surviving players, action records, voting history, night events), and subjective information (all players' speeches, which may contain deception). Outputs are structured JSON with role-specific keys: for night actions, a target player number plus reasoning; for daytime speeches, an identity-to-present field, per-player identity labels, a vote target, and the full speech text; for voting, consolidated notes, a voting reason, and the target player number. The 'think-before-respond' format requires the model to output its reasoning (e.g., identity predictions, alliance analysis) before the final action, with thinking tokens kept shorter than long-CoT reasoning models.

## Method

MaKTO is a two-stage training pipeline. Stage 1 is behavior cloning (SFT) on 25k samples from expert-annotated Werewolf games (331 games by 17 experienced players) plus game terminology and strategy guides, using a 'think-before-respond' format with chain-of-thought. Stage 2 is Multi-agent KTO: the SFT model plays 9-player Werewolf games against a diverse pool of opponents (GPT-4o, Claude-3.5, other SFT models, and an RL+LLM Mix agent) rather than self-play, generating 20k unpaired preference samples. The key technical choice is KTO over DPO because multi-agent dialogues make paired chosen/rejected data intractable, while game rules and opponent feedback can label individual outputs as desirable or unacceptable. Preference data is selected stepwise (per action) rather than by trajectory win/loss, using three complementary criteria: heuristic rules (e.g., werewolves targeting special roles is desirable), staged voting outcomes (e.g., a Seer receiving majority villager votes is unacceptable), and LLM-based verification of speech consistency with game facts. This stepwise selection addresses the credit-assignment problem where a team victory does not guarantee all individual actions were optimal.

## Results

In 9-player Seer-Witch-Guard Werewolf tournaments, MaKTO-72b achieves a 61.0% average win rate, outperforming GPT-4o (49.6%) by 23.0% relative improvement and the two-stage RL Mix agent (55.0%) by 10.9%. Against 14 expert human players (1000+ games each), MaKTO-72b reaches a 60% head-to-head win rate and a 61.8% ± 8.3% win rate in random competitions. In Turing-style blind detectability tests, human judges identify MaKTO as AI only 48.9% of the time (below random chance), compared to 76.6% for GPT-4o.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Mix agent [21] (a Cicero-like two-stage system combining AlphaStar-style MARL for decision-making with GPT-4o for speech generation), MaKTO-72b achieves a 0.610 average win rate versus Mix's 0.550, a 10.9% relative improvement. In direct head-to-head matchups, MaKTO-72b beats Mix 53% of the time, with the gap widening when Mix plays as werewolves due to its overly aggressive RL policies. Against DeepSeek-R1 distilled reasoning models [41], MaKTO-14b achieves a 74% win rate over R1-Distill-Qwen-14b and MaKTO-72b achieves 84% over R1-Distill-Llama-70b, demonstrating that long-chain-of-thought reasoning trained on math/coding tasks does not transfer to social deduction games where strategic deception and persuasive communication are critical. MaKTO also generalizes to unseen game settings (new Hunter role, 10- and 12-player scales) where the Mix agent's policy network degrades significantly, while MaKTO maintains top performance across all configurations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on KTO (Ethayarajh et al., ICML 2024) for unpaired preference optimization, adapting it to a multi-agent language game setting where paired chosen/rejected data is infeasible to obtain. It departs from the dominant two-stage RL+LLM paradigm (Wu et al. 2024, Xu et al. 2024) that decouples strategic decision-making from language expression, instead training a single LLM end-to-end through direct multi-agent interaction. The stepwise preference selection (heuristic, staged voting, verifier-based) extends prior trajectory-level reward approaches by attributing quality to individual actions rather than whole-game outcomes, and the multi-agent model pool draws on population-based training ideas to avoid the rigidity of self-play.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that integrated language-decision training via interaction-based preference optimization can match or exceed both strong API models (GPT-4o) and decoupled RL+LLM agents in social deduction, while also passing a Turing-style detectability test at below-chance accuracy. The generalization to unseen roles (Hunter) and larger game scales (10/12-player) without retraining suggests the learned strategies are transferable rather than overfit. Key open questions include whether the approach extends to free-form (non-turn-based) multi-agent communication, whether online multi-agent RL could further improve performance, and whether the findings generalize beyond Werewolf to other social reasoning domains. The contribution of a large-scale expert-annotated dataset with chain-of-thought reasoning is a practical resource for the community.
