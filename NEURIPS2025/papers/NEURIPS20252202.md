## Problem

Existing robust Federated Graph Learning methods rely on coarse-grained Euclidean representations that fail to capture fine-grained topological differences among intra-class nodes, and they neither constrain edge-mediated noise propagation nor account for the fact that client-level local perspectives cause certain noisy nodes to appear normal locally while being anomalous globally. This leads to noise-valid signal coupling and degraded generalization of the global model under label noise.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252202/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each client provides a local subgraph with node feature vectors (d-dimensional), an adjacency matrix, and noisy one-hot labels over C classes. All representations are projected onto a unit hypersphere, so the method operates entirely in angular geometry: node embeddings are unit-norm vectors, class prototypes are also unit vectors on the same sphere, and similarity is measured via cosine/spherical angles rather than Euclidean distances. The server outputs a global model, global prototype clusters (K prototypes per class), and a pruned node set after Mahalanobis-based outlier removal.

## Method

HYPERION unifies three components in a single hyperspherical space. At the client level, TP-HSL allocates K prototype clusters per class (modeled with a von Mises-Fisher distribution) and optimizes two regularization losses: a structural loss that maximizes the minimum spherical angle between inter-class prototypes, and a semantic loss that minimizes the average spherical angle between node embeddings and their assigned prototype centers. HS-CNC then constructs a perturbed graph view via random edge dropping and feature masking, and trains only on nodes whose predicted class is consistent across both views, filtering perturbation-sensitive noisy nodes. At the server level, GA-HSP first uses a 2-component GMM on uploaded prototype clusters to partition clients into clean and malicious sets, then performs Wasserstein-distance-driven negative distillation (treating malicious prototypes as teachers to suppress anomalous directions in the global prototype), and finally applies Mahalanobis-distance-based neighborhood sparsification using inter-class and intra-class prototype covariance to prune the top η% highest-risk nodes from each client's training set.

## Results

Under 50%-pair noise on Cora, HYPERION achieves an F1-macro of 51.15%, surpassing the second-best method (39.41%) by 11.74 percentage points. At 50%-uniform noise on CiteSeer, HYPERION reaches 47.11% accuracy, outperforming the best baseline RTGNN (41.93%) by 5.18 points. Across 20% noise settings, HYPERION leads on all datasets (e.g., 62.18% on Cora-uniform vs. 58.15% for CRGNN), and under severe 70% noise it maintains an average 8.1–10.1% gain over all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HYPERION outperforms all 13 baselines across every dataset and noise setting evaluated. Among traditional FL methods (FedAvg, FedNova, FedProx, MOON), the gap is largest under high noise: at 70%-pair noise on Cora, HYPERION (27.19%) exceeds FedAvg (21.31%) by 5.88 points. Among FGL methods (FGSSL, FedTAD, FedGTA), HYPERION leads by 4–11 points at 20% noise and 3–10 points at 70% noise. The closest competitors are the robust graph learning methods: CRGNN (NN 2024) achieves 58.15% on Cora-20%-uniform vs. HYPERION's 62.18% (a 4.03-point gap), and RTGNN (WWW 2023) is the best baseline at 50%-uniform CiteSeer (41.93% vs. HYPERION's 47.11%). Among robust FL methods, FedNed (AAAI 2024) and FedNoRo (IJCAI 2023) are the strongest but still trail HYPERION by 5–15 points under moderate-to-high noise. FedCorr (CVPR 2022) performs worst among robust FL baselines, sometimes falling below standard FedAvg, highlighting that label-correction strategies alone are insufficient for graph-structured noisy data.

## Relation to Prior Work

HYPERION synthesizes ideas from three research threads—federated graph learning (FedGTA, FedTAD, FGSSL), robust federated learning under label noise (FedNed, FedCorr, FedNoRo), and robust graph learning (CRGNN, RTGNN, CLNode)—into a unified hyperspherical embedding framework. It extends prototype-based learning from single-prototype-per-class to multi-prototype clusters modeled with the von Mises-Fisher distribution, and adapts negative distillation (from FedNed) by replacing standard angular loss with a Wasserstein-distance-driven formulation. The Mahalanobis-based node pruning and GMM-based client classification are repurposed from OOD detection and robust aggregation literature, respectively, but are the first to be applied jointly within a federated graph learning pipeline operating entirely on the hypersphere.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap: existing robust FL methods ignore graph topology, and existing robust GNN methods assume centralized access, leaving federated graph learning under noisy labels largely unsolved. The unified hyperspherical design is elegant in that all three components (prototype learning, consistency filtering, server purification) share a single geometric space, reducing architectural complexity. However, the authors acknowledge the framework only handles class label noise and does not address feature noise or adversarial edge perturbations, and scalability to very large graphs (beyond ~34K nodes) remains untested. Open questions include whether the multi-prototype hyperspherical approach generalizes to other noise models and whether the Wasserstein distillation step is necessary at scale or can be approximated more cheaply.
