## Problem

Existing test-time scaling methods for LLMs (CoT, ToT, GoT) accumulate historical dependency information at every inference step, causing redundant computation and mutual interference between the current reasoning step and the growing context. This paper asks whether a reasoning process can be designed that is fundamentally memoryless—where each step depends only on the immediately preceding state—while still supporting effective test-time scaling and integration with structured exploration methods.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252481/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts a text question or problem, optionally accompanied by context passages (for multi-hop QA) or test cases and function signatures (for code generation). It produces a final answer along with an intermediate sequence of Markov states—each a self-contained, answer-equivalent reformulation of the original question with monotonically decreasing reasoning complexity—and the temporary DAG dependency structures used during each transition. Each state is independently solvable without access to prior states, and the chain terminates when an LLM-as-a-judge determines no further meaningful simplification is possible.

## Method

AOT models reasoning as a Markov chain of states Q_0 → Q_1 → … → Q_N, where each Q_i is a self-contained question answer-equivalent to the original Q_0 but with reduced test-time complexity. Transitions between states use a two-phase mechanism: (1) decomposition, where the current state is broken into a DAG of sub-questions with explicit dependency edges, and (2) contraction, where independent nodes (no incoming edges) are solved and their results are absorbed into the problem statement, while dependent nodes are reformulated into a new, simpler, self-contained question. A quality-aware termination strategy applies an LLM-as-a-judge to select the best answer from {solve(Q_i), solve(G_i), solve(Q_{i+1})}, implicitly enforcing answer equivalence and preventing error propagation. Because each state is a valid entry point for any external solver, AOT integrates modularly with tree search (ToT) and reflective refinement, and under these scaling-up procedures the reasoning trajectories converge to irreducible, low-complexity 'atomic' units that require no further decomposition.

## Results

AOT achieves the highest scores across all benchmarks and both model categories. For non-reasoning LLMs, GPT-4o-mini with AOT scores 83.6 on MATH, 95.0 on GSM8K, 75.2 on MBPP, and 68.5 on LongBench (F1); DeepSeek-V3 with AOT reaches 96.5, 98.2, 79.6, and 71.0 respectively. For reasoning LLMs, O3-mini with AOT attains 83.0 on AIME, 32.2 on LiveCodeBench, and 65.3 on LongBench; DeepSeek-R1 with AOT achieves 81.7, 30.9, and 67.9. The largest margin over the strongest baseline (AFlow) appears on LongBench, where AOT leads by 7.5 points (GPT-4o-mini) and 7.3 points (O3-mini).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AOT outperforms all eight baselines (CoT, CoT-SC, SR, AR, AFlow, ToT, GoT, FoT) on every benchmark and model combination tested. The margins are most pronounced on LongBench (multi-hop QA), where AOT leads the best baseline AFlow by 7.5 points with GPT-4o-mini (68.5 vs. 61.0) and by 7.3 points with O3-mini (65.3 vs. 58.0), suggesting the Markovian state decomposition is particularly effective for tasks with complex dependency chains. On LiveCodeBench, AOT achieves a 4.4-point advantage over FoT (32.2 vs. 27.8) with O3-mini, the largest gap on that benchmark. On MATH and GSM8K, AOT edges out the strongest baselines (AFlow and FoT) by 0.4–1.4 points, indicating consistent but smaller gains on already well-saturated benchmarks. AR (Analogical Reasoning) is the weakest baseline across all settings, trailing AOT by 10–18 points, while CoT-SC and SR remain competitive on MATH/GSM8K but fall behind on LongBench and LiveCodeBench.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the memoryless property of Markov processes for LLM reasoning, departing from the dominant paradigm of accumulating historical context (CoT, ToT, GoT) by enforcing that each reasoning state is a self-contained, answer-equivalent reformulation of the original problem. It draws on Least-to-Most prompting's subquestion framing and on prior task-specific Markov/atomic reasoning attempts (Markov Chain of Thought, AtomThink, AtomR), but generalizes these into a domain-agnostic two-phase decomposition-contraction mechanism using temporary DAGs. The key conceptual move is treating the DAG as a disposable scaffold that is discarded after each transition, thereby achieving true memorylessness rather than the partial context reduction seen in prior structured reasoning methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The modular integration property—where any intermediate Markov state can serve as an optimized entry point for ToT, FoT, or other frameworks—offers a practical plug-in mechanism for improving cost-efficiency across existing reasoning pipelines without architectural changes. However, the 'emergent atomic structure' claim rests primarily on statistical token-count convergence analysis rather than a formal characterization, and the framework operates purely at inference time with no training-time alignment, leaving open whether models can internalize Markovian/atomic reasoning patterns. The modest but consistent gains (typically 1–3 points over strong baselines, larger on LongBench) suggest the approach is a useful efficiency tool rather than a paradigm shift, and its practical impact will depend on whether the added LLM invocations for decomposition, contraction, and judging are justified by the token savings in downstream reasoning.
