## Problem

Regularized Newton methods for nonconvex optimization face a fundamental trade-off: achieving the optimal global complexity O(ε^{-3/2}) typically requires a regularizer proportional to √g_k, which limits local convergence to at most order 3/2, while quadratic local convergence demands a much smaller regularizer. Whether a parameter-free algorithm of this type can simultaneously attain optimal global complexity and quadratic local convergence remained an open question.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes a smooth nonconvex function φ: ℝⁿ → ℝ with a globally L_H-Lipschitz continuous Hessian and an initial point x₀, requiring no prior knowledge of L_H or the tolerance ε. It produces an ε-stationary point (‖∇φ(x*)‖ ≤ ε) in O(ε^{-3/2}) second-order oracle calls, and when the Hessian at the solution is positive definite, the entire sequence converges quadratically to a local minimum. Each iteration accesses second-order information only through Hessian-vector products (via capped CG), avoiding full Hessian construction, which keeps peak memory at ~6 GB versus ~74 GB for trust-region methods on the largest CUTEst problem.

## Method

The key insight is a new class of adaptive regularizers ρ_k = √(M_k) · ω_k^f · δ_k^θ, where δ_k = min(1, g_k^θ/g_{k-1}^θ) is the ratio of consecutive gradient norms raised to a power θ > 1, and M_k is an online estimate of L_H. Outside the local convergence region, δ_k ≈ 1 so the regularizer behaves like √g_k (preserving the optimal global rate); inside the local region where superlinear convergence occurs, δ_k decays rapidly, shrinking the regularizer and enabling quadratic convergence. The regularized Newton equation is solved via a capped conjugate gradient method with a negative curvature monitor that detects indefinite Hessians without computing minimal eigenvalues, and a fallback step prevents sharp gradient drops from degrading the global complexity. An adaptive linesearch with a secondary bounded-stepsize criterion and a TERM termination state in the CG subroutine ensure non-degenerate oracle complexity. The result is a fully parameter-free algorithm achieving O(ε^{-3/2}) global complexity (matching the Carmon et al. lower bound up to an additive Õ(1) term from L_H estimation) and quadratic local convergence for θ > 1.

## Results

The proposed parameter-free regularized Newton-CG method achieves an optimal global iteration complexity of O(L_H^{1/2} ε^{-3/2}) in second-order oracle calls (matching the Carmon et al. lower bound up to an additive Õ(1) term from L_H estimation) and Õ(ε^{-7/4}) Hessian-vector products. When θ > 1 and the Hessian at the solution is positive definite, the method exhibits quadratic local convergence (g_{k+1} ≤ O(g_k²)). On the CUTEst benchmark (124 problems, ε = 10^{-5}), the method is comparable to ARC_qK and slightly faster than CAT and AN2CER, while using ~6 GB peak memory versus ~74 GB for CAT on the largest problem (123,200 variables). On PINN training (81,201 parameters), ARNCG_g achieves test L2RE of 1.38×10^{-5} (Convection), 4.36×10^{-4} (Reaction), and 3.29×10^{-3} (Wave), outperforming NNCG by 1–2 orders of magnitude.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Theoretically, the proposed method strictly improves upon all prior RNMs: it reduces the L_H dependence from L_H³ (Royer et al.) or L_H² (He et al., Zhu and Xiao) to the optimal √L_H, eliminates the logarithmic factor present in Gratton et al. (achieving O(ε^{-3/2}) vs. O(ε^{-3/2} log(1/ε))), and is the first RNM to simultaneously achieve optimal global complexity and quadratic local convergence without requiring prior knowledge of L_H or minimal eigenvalue computations. In the CUTEst benchmark, the method (without the fallback step) is comparable to ARC_qK and slightly faster than both CAT and AN2CER in wall-clock time, since each iteration uses only a few HVPs whereas CAT relies on multiple Cholesky factorizations and AN2CER involves minimal eigenvalue computations; memory usage is ~6 GB vs. ~74 GB for CAT on the largest problem. On PINN training, ARNCG_g outperforms NNCG (Rathore et al.) by 1–2 orders of magnitude in both training loss and test L2RE across all three PDE problems (e.g., 2.72×10^{-11} vs. 1.38×10^{-8} training loss on Convection), while requiring no hyperparameter tuning of the regularizer and using at most 10.2 GB GPU memory.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the capped conjugate gradient (CG) framework of Royer et al. [49] and the adaptive Newton-CG methods of He et al. [29, 30], which already achieved near-optimal global rates for nonconvex optimization. The key departure is the introduction of a new class of regularizers built from the ratio of consecutive gradient norms (δ_k^θ = min(1, g_k^θ/g_{k-1}^θ)), which resolves a long-standing trade-off: prior methods either used a fixed √ε regularizer (optimal global rate but only linear local convergence, as in Royer et al.) or a √g_k regularizer (near-optimal global rate with a logarithmic factor and 3/2 local rate, as in Gratton et al. [24]). The paper also introduces an adaptive Hessian Lipschitz constant estimator M_k and an ε_k-based variant that eliminates the residual log factor, making the algorithm fully parameter-free.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper affirmatively answers an open question in the regularized Newton method literature: whether a parameter-free algorithm can simultaneously achieve the optimal O(ε^{-3/2}) global iteration complexity and quadratic local convergence. This is significant because it unifies two previously competing design goals and provides a principled, implementable regularizer that adapts automatically to the local geometry. Limitations include the requirement of a Lipschitz continuous Hessian (excluding ReLU-based networks), the need for multiple CG iterations per step, and the fact that numerical results are preliminary and show competitiveness rather than dominance over strong baselines like CAT. Open directions include extension to Riemannian optimization, Hölder continuous Hessians with unknown exponents, and inexact or stochastic settings.
