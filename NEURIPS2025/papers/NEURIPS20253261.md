## Problem

Deep Image Prior (DIP) methods solve inverse imaging problems without training data but require optimizing all network parameters independently for each measurement, making them slow and prone to noise overfitting. Data-centric alternatives (diffusion models, supervised networks) demand large clean datasets that are often unavailable in medical and scientific settings. UGoDIT addresses the open question of whether a fully unsupervised model can learn transferable weights from only a very small number (M < 10) of degraded (sub-sampled) measurements, enabling faster and more robust reconstruction of unseen images at test time.

## Input / Output

During training, the method takes M degraded measurement vectors y_i (e.g., multi-coil k-space data with 4×/8× Cartesian undersampling for MRI, downsampled images for SR, or nonlinearly blurred images for NDB) along with their corresponding forward operators A_i, and produces a set of shared encoder weights φ̂. At test time, given a single unseen measurement vector y and its forward operator A, the method outputs a reconstructed image x̂ by optimizing only the decoder parameters while keeping the encoder frozen. Network inputs are initialized via the adjoint operator (z ← A^H y), and the architecture uses a U-Net backbone for MRI (320×320, 15 coils) and a Skip-net for natural image tasks.

## Method

UGoDIT employs a shared encoder h_φ jointly trained with M disentangled decoders g_{ψ_i} (one per training image), optimizing a combined measurement-consistency and autoencoding objective via alternating updates: N gradient steps on all parameters followed by K input updates through forward passes (z_i ← g_{ψ_i}∘h_φ(z_i)). The autoencoding term (λ‖g∘h(z_i) − z_i‖²) implicitly enforces input-output consistency to mitigate noise overfitting, while the multi-decoder design encourages the encoder to extract generalizable features by receiving supervision from M independent reconstruction tasks. At test time, the pre-trained encoder is frozen and only the randomly initialized decoder is optimized using the same alternating scheme, reducing the number of free parameters and accelerating convergence. The multi-decoder architecture is justified through multi-task representation learning theory, which shows that shared encoders with task-specific decoders reduce generalization error and promote transferable features compared to a single shared decoder.

## Results

On fastMRI knee MRI at 4× acceleration, UGoDIT-6 achieves 36.05 dB PSNR and 0.960 SSIM using only 6 sub-sampled training images, outperforming all data-centric baselines except SITCOM-MRI (36.33 dB) while requiring no clean ground-truth images. On super-resolution (FFHQ, 4×), UGoDIT-6 reaches 30.75 dB PSNR and 0.871 SSIM, surpassing STRAINER-6 (29.45 dB) and matching SITCOM (30.68 dB). On non-linear deblurring, UGoDIT-6 achieves 30.19 dB PSNR, outperforming STRAINER-6 (27.41 dB) and SITCOM (30.12 dB). UGoDIT also converges significantly faster than standalone DIP methods, reaching 30 dB in ~10 iterations for MRI versus ~450 for aSeqDIP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UGoDIT-6 outperforms VarNet (33.06 dB), TTT (33.81 dB), and DDS (34.95 dB) by 3–3.1 dB on 4× MRI, and narrowly beats Recurrent VarNet (35.88 dB) by 0.17 dB, all while using only 6 sub-sampled images versus 8000 fully sampled images. Against the strongest diffusion-based baseline SITCOM-MRI (36.33 dB), UGoDIT-6 trails by 0.28 dB but is substantially faster at inference (48 s vs. 76 s per image). On super-resolution, UGoDIT-6 (30.75 dB) surpasses STRAINER-6 (29.45 dB) by 1.3 dB and marginally edges out SITCOM (30.68 dB), while on non-linear deblurring it leads STRAINER-6 (27.41 dB) by 2.8 dB and SITCOM (30.12 dB) by 0.07 dB. Compared to standalone DIP methods, UGoDIT gains ≥2 dB over aSeqDIP on MRI, ≥2 dB over DRP and Self-Guided DIP on SR, and ≥1 dB over aSeqDIP on NDB, while also converging 4–45× faster in terms of iterations to reach 30 dB. Against measurement-splitting self-supervised methods (SSDU, ZS-SSL), UGoDIT-6 leads by 2.6–2.8 dB on fastMRI and 0.43 dB on Cor-PD.

## Relation to Prior Work

UGoDIT extends the Deep Image Prior (DIP) lineage by learning transferable encoder weights from a small set of degraded images, drawing its input-adaptive autoencoding objective and sequential input updates directly from aSeqDIP. It departs from prior transferable-weight DIP methods (MetaDIP, STRAINER) in three key ways: it requires no clean ground-truth images during training, it introduces a shared-encoder-with-M-disentangled-decoders architecture motivated by multi-task representation learning theory, and it freezes the encoder at test time rather than merely initializing it. The multi-decoder design is explicitly grounded in the multi-task learning literature (Maurer et al., 2016), where shared representations with task-specific heads provably reduce generalization error.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

UGoDIT addresses a practically critical gap in medical and scientific imaging where large clean datasets are infeasible to acquire, offering a path to near-SOTA reconstruction quality using only 4–6 degraded measurements. The method's significant convergence speedup (e.g., ~45× fewer iterations than aSeqDIP for MRI) makes it more practical for clinical deployment. Key limitations include degraded out-of-distribution performance for MRI (knee→brain), reliance on full knowledge of the forward operator, and restriction to 2D reconstruction; future work targets 3D extension, blind inverse problems, and test-time adaptation for distribution shift.
