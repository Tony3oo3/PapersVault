## Problem

Existing VICL prompt selection methods, notably Partial2Global and VPR, rely on an unchallenged similarity-priority assumption that visually similar images make better in-context examples, yet this heuristic lacks statistical justification and its influence on performance remains unexplored. Additionally, Partial2Global constructs its global ranking from randomly sampled pairwise preferences, which can produce incomplete coverage and redundant comparisons, degrading ranking accuracy.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a query image, a pool of candidate training examples (image-label pairs), and a pretrained inpainting model (e.g., MAE-VQGAN). For each candidate, it computes quality scores (task-specific metrics like IoU) by using that candidate as a prompt for all other training samples, alongside visual similarity scores from CLIP/DINOv2 features. The output is a refined alternative set (the intersection of a globally reliable set and the query-specific top-K similarity pool) that feeds into a meta-learned list-wise ranker, ultimately yielding a global ranking from which the final in-context examples are selected for downstream segmentation, detection, or colorization.

## Method

RH-Partial2Global introduces two enhancements to the Partial2Global framework. First, a jackknife conformal prediction-guided selection strategy computes, for each training sample, a conformity score (e.g., negative KL divergence or Spearman correlation) quantifying the alignment between its prompt quality across all other samples and its visual similarity to them; the (1−α)-quantile of these scores defines a reliability threshold, and the resulting globally reliable set is intersected with the query-specific similarity-based candidate pool to retain only high-confidence examples. Second, a covering design-based sampling strategy replaces random shuffling in the local ranking stage: a precomputed optimal C(K′,k,2) covering design guides sequential sampling of k-length sub-sequences from a shuffled alternative set, guaranteeing exhaustive coverage of all pairwise preferences with a minimal number of sub-sequences (e.g., 130 for K=50, k=5 per the Schönheim lower bound) while eliminating redundant comparisons. An auxiliary filling strategy supplements the refined set with the most similar candidates from the reliable global set when the conformal filter reduces the pool below the ranker length.

## Results

On the Pascal-5i segmentation task, RH-Partial2Global achieves an average mIoU of 39.02 across four folds, outperforming Partial2Global (38.40) by 0.62 points. On Pascal VOC 2012 single-object detection, it reaches 30.94 mIoU versus 30.66 for Partial2Global, and on ILSVRC2012 colorization it achieves 0.56 MSE versus 0.58. With test-time voting, RH-Partial2Global+voting further improves to 43.08 average mIoU (segmentation) and 33.28 mIoU (detection), surpassing Partial2Global+voting (42.69 and 32.52 respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RH-Partial2Global consistently outperforms Partial2Global [6] across all three tasks: +0.62 average mIoU on segmentation, +0.28 mIoU on detection, and −0.02 MSE on colorization, with gains present in every individual fold. Against the VPR variants [2], the improvements are substantially larger (e.g., +3.46 average mIoU over SupPR on segmentation, +2.72 over SupPR on detection), confirming that the conformal prediction-guided selection strategy generalizes beyond the Partial2Global framework. On segmentation, prompt-SelF [11] reports a higher average mIoU (41.02 vs. 39.02), but RH-Partial2Global surpasses it on detection (30.94 vs. 29.83 mIoU) and is the only method evaluated on colorization. When test-time voting is applied, RH-Partial2Global+voting extends its lead over Partial2Global+voting by +0.39 mIoU (segmentation) and +0.76 mIoU (detection), indicating the gains are not an artifact of the ensemble. Overall, the method wins or ties on every reported metric against its direct predecessor and all VPR/MAE-VQGAN baselines, while trailing prompt-SelF only on the segmentation-specific ensemble comparison.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Partial2Global (NIPS'24), which itself builds on VPR (NIPS'23) for visual in-context learning prompt selection, by addressing two specific weaknesses: the unexamined similarity-priority assumption inherited from VPR, and the reliance on random shuffling for pairwise preference coverage. It imports jackknife conformal prediction from the uncertainty-quantification literature to construct a statistically grounded reliability filter, and draws on covering designs from combinatorics to replace ad-hoc random sampling with a structured, exhaustive pairwise coverage scheme. Unlike prior VICL prompt-selection methods (MAE-VQGAN, VPR, prompt-SelF) that treat visual similarity as a sufficient proxy for prompt quality, this work explicitly tests and partially refutes that proxy, then layers a distribution-free reliability criterion on top.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Providing the first statistical evidence that the similarity-priority assumption—foundational to most VICL prompt-selection methods—yields only weak monotonic correlations (ρ ≈ 0.03–0.05) opens a concrete direction for rethinking how in-context examples are chosen beyond raw visual similarity. The conformal prediction filter and covering design sampler are lightweight, model-agnostic add-ons that could be grafted onto other VICL frameworks (demonstrated on VPR), though the paper acknowledges sensitivity to calibration set size limits gains on smaller folds. Open questions include whether the reliability criterion generalizes to tasks without a natural quality metric (e.g., open-ended generation), whether the modest 0.6% average improvement scales with larger candidate pools or more diverse training sets, and whether the jackknife CP threshold can be adapted per-query rather than globally.
