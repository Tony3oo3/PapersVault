## Problem

LLMs are inherently probabilistic while formal verification demands deterministic guarantees, creating a critical gap when LLMs autoformalize natural language into SMT-LIB programs: existing uncertainty quantification techniques (token-level entropy, semantic entropy via clustering) fail to identify formalization errors in these structured artifacts. This paper asks how to systematically capture and exploit the uncertainty inherent in ensembles of LLM-generated formal specifications to enable reliable selective verification.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251149/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For each reasoning question, the method consumes an ensemble of N=100 LLM-generated SMT-LIB programs (sampled at temperatures uniformly distributed between 0.1 and 2.0) and the SMT-LIB v2 context-free grammar. Programs are parsed into ASTs and production rule usage frequencies are extracted to induce a question-specific PCFG, making the approach instance-level with no cross-question training. The output is a 25-dimensional vector of uncertainty metrics spanning five categories, which feeds a lightweight fusion model producing a correctness prediction score and a selective verification decision (accept or abstain).

## Method

The core mechanism models the LLM's implicit distribution over SMT-LIB programs via a PCFG whose rule probabilities are estimated by MLE (with Laplace smoothing) from parsed ensembles, then derives uncertainty metrics from the resulting grammar structure. Five metric families are computed: static grammar properties (non-terminal/rule counts, branching factor), information-theoretic measures (Shannon and Rényi entropy per non-terminal, overall grammar entropy, perplexity, KL divergence from uniform, and a novel NSUI composite linking entropy ratio to spectral radius), spectral properties (spectral radius of the grammar's mean matrix capturing recursive complexity), self-consistency scores (text majority-vote and SMT solver agreement across the ensemble), and ensemble predictors (simple, weighted, and logistic-regression fusion). A theoretical coverage guarantee theorem proves that N samples from a distribution with Shannon entropy H(μ) cover all regions of mass ε with miss probability bounded by exp(−Nε/2^H(μ)), with the critical ε scaling as ln(N)/N via the Lambert W-function. The approach is model-agnostic, requiring only black-box LLM access and a standard SMT solver (Z3), and reveals task-dependent signal dominance—grammar entropy achieves AUROC > 0.93 on ProofWriter while cross-modal consistency dominates on StrategyQA—enabling selective verification that reduces error rates by 14–100% with minimal abstention.

## Results

On ProofWriter (premise-explicit logic), PCFG-derived grammar entropy achieves AUROC=0.9301 (o3-mini) and AURC=0.0008, while the Ensemble ML predictor reaches AUROC=0.9892 with AURC=0.0003, enabling near-complete error filtering with minimal abstention. On StrategyQA (knowledge-intensive reasoning), self-consistency and cross-modal agreement metrics dominate, with Ensemble ML achieving AUROC=0.7850 (o3-mini) and 0.8517 (DeepSeek-v3). Across all four datasets, the lightweight ensemble fusion reduces error rates by 14–100% via selective verification, substantially outperforming individual token-level or semantic-entropy baselines whose AUROCs remain near-random (0.50–0.70).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper benchmarks two families of prior UQ methods against its PCFG framework. Token-level baselines (entropy, perplexity, kurtosis of LLM logprobs; Table 2) achieve at best AUROC≈0.70 (FOLIO entropy) and typically 0.51–0.62 on StrategyQA/ProntoQA/ProofWriter, with AURC values of 0.22–0.56 indicating poor risk-coverage tradeoffs. Semantic-entropy baselines (Kuhn et al. 2023; Farquhar et al. 2024; Table 3) using discrete/continuous entropy over entailment-based clusters yield AUROC 0.50–0.76 and AURC 0.24–0.56, with near-zero relative error reduction (RelErrRed < 0.03) in most configurations. The proposed PCFG approach decisively outperforms both: on ProofWriter, grammar entropy alone (AUROC=0.9301) surpasses the best token-level metric (0.5893 perplexity) by ~34 points and the best semantic metric (0.5966 RAO) by ~33 points, while the Ensemble ML predictor (AUROC=0.9892, AURC=0.0003) achieves near-perfect discrimination. On StrategyQA, the PCFG ensemble (AUROC=0.7850) still exceeds token-level perplexity (0.6179) and semantic discrete entropy (0.5341), though the gap narrows because knowledge-intensive tasks are better captured by self-consistency signals. The PCFG method also offers a computational advantage: it requires only parsing valid SMT outputs (no O(M²) undecidable entailment comparisons needed by semantic clustering).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends PCFG inference (De la Higuera, 2010) from grammar learning to uncertainty quantification, applying it to the distribution over LLM-generated SMT-LIB programs rather than natural language parses. It departs from constrained decoding approaches (Sengottuvelu, 2023; Melcer et al., 2024) that use CFGs to enforce syntax at inference time, instead using PCFGs post-hoc to quantify uncertainty among already-valid but potentially semantically wrong formal artifacts. The self-consistency framework (Wang et al., 2022) is adapted from text-level majority voting to SMT-solver-based agreement, and the work positions itself against semantic entropy methods (Kuhn et al., 2023; Farquhar et al., 2024) by showing those fail to capture formalization-specific errors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that no single UQ signal generalizes across reasoning tasks—grammar entropy excels for deductive logic (AUROC 0.93 on ProofWriter) while cross-modal agreement dominates for knowledge-intensive QA—challenges the assumption that one uncertainty metric suffices and motivates task-aware or fused verification pipelines. The demonstration that standard token-level and semantic-entropy UQ methods systematically fail to detect autoformalization errors (AUROC ≈ 0.5–0.6) is a practical warning for anyone deploying LLMs in formal verification workflows. Open questions include whether the PCFG approximation captures sufficient structure for richer formal languages (beyond SMT-LIB), whether the instance-level (per-question) PCFG induction scales to large-scale deployment, and whether the observed asymmetric self-consistency (distinct formal vs. textual reasoning pathways) can be addressed through joint training.
