## Problem

Hybrid neural PDE solvers that apply learned corrections directly to coarse solver outputs suffer from severe autoregressive error accumulation, particularly in chaotic regimes, because local perturbations are amplified by the full error amplification matrix G(u^n) = I + Δt·J(u^n) at each step. Existing stabilization strategies (noise injection, multi-step rollouts) only marginally mitigate this intrinsic sensitivity without providing formal error-reduction guarantees, limiting the practical deployment of hybrid solvers for long-term simulations.

## Input / Output

The neural corrector G_θ receives the current coarse-resolution PDE state u^n (plus positional grid and, for Navier-Stokes cases, Reynolds number and geometry parameters) and outputs a correction term that is added to the PDE's right-hand side before the temporal integrator advances the solution. Unlike direct methods that produce a state update u^{n+1} = G_θ(u^n*), INC's output is a source-term perturbation of magnitude O(Δt) that the solver integrates internally, meaning the correction's influence on the state is filtered through the time-discretization operator. The framework is resolution-agnostic and operates at whatever coarse grid the underlying solver uses (e.g., 64 points for 1D KS, 128×32 for 2D BFS, 64×32×32 for 3D TCF).

## Method

INC embeds the learned correction as a right-hand-side source term within the PDE's time integration (u^{n+1} = T[u^{n+1}, u^n, N(∂_x u^n, …) + G_θ(u^n)]) rather than applying it as a post-hoc state update. The theoretical analysis proves that this indirect embedding reduces worst-case perturbation amplification by a factor R_k ~ Δt^{-1} + L compared to direct corrections, where L is the Lipschitz constant of the dynamics, because indirect perturbations scale only with Δt while direct ones are amplified by the full Jacobian-based matrix I + Δt·J(u^n). The method imposes no architectural constraints: it integrates seamlessly with arbitrary neural backbones (FNO, DeepONet, ResNet, U-Net) and solver types (WENO finite-difference, pseudo-spectral ETDRK, PISO finite-volume), with the correction fused into the source term, nonlinear term, or momentum equation respectively. Training uses multi-step unrolled L² loss over autoregressive rollouts of length ~0.04·N (where N = t_c/Δt is steps per characteristic timescale), with an ℓ₂ regularization on θ to enforce Lipschitz continuity, and the entire solver-plus-network pipeline is differentiated end-to-end in PyTorch.

## Results

On the chaotic KS equation (5000-step rollout), INC improves long-term R² by up to 158.7% over SITL (UNet backbone) and by 35–37% for DeepONet, while the FNO backbone shows only ~3% gain due to its architectural similarity to the pseudo-spectral solver. On the Burgers equation with spatial downsampling (4000 steps), INC reduces MSE by 39–99% relative to SITL/SITL*. In stability-critical regimes (KS2 with Δt=0.5, Karman with CFL=2.0), INC achieves R²=0.78 and 0.80 respectively, versus 0.65/0.74 for SITL and 0.58/0.71 for SITL*, while the uncorrected solver blows up entirely. For the 3D turbulent channel flow, INC reduces statistical MSE by 80.7% over SITL and delivers a 330× speedup over a high-resolution No Model baseline at equivalent accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

INC consistently outperforms both direct-correction baselines (SITL and SITL*) across every test case and neural architecture. On the chaotic KS1 rollout, INC achieves up to 158.7% higher R² than SITL (UNet) and 35–37% higher than SITL/SITL* (DeepONet); the FNO backbone shows only ~3% gain because its spectral architecture already mimics the pseudo-spectral solver. In the stability-critical KS2 and Karman cases where the uncorrected solver blows up, INC attains R²=0.78 and 0.80 versus 0.65/0.74 for SITL and 0.58/0.71 for SITL*, representing 20–27% relative MSE reductions. The solver-free RNN (FNO-based) baseline consistently underperforms all hybrid methods on every metric, confirming the value of embedding a numerical solver. For the 3D TCF, INC's stabilizing effect enables a 2× larger timestep (CFL=1.6 vs. 0.8), yielding an 80.7% MSE reduction over SITL and a 330× speedup over a 256×128×128 No Model at matched statistical accuracy. CSM (Appendix I), which scales the correction by Δt, is also outperformed by INC, confirming that the indirect RHS embedding is the critical design choice rather than mere timestep scaling.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the solver-in-the-loop (SITL) paradigm of Um et al. [23] and the differentiable unrolled training framework of List et al. [18] by relocating the learned correction from a post-hoc state update to an indirect right-hand-side term within the PDE solve. While prior hybrid solvers (SITL, CSM, DPM) all apply corrections as direct state modifications, INC embeds the neural correction inside the temporal integrator, a change that the authors show is not merely architectural but fundamentally alters the error amplification dynamics. The theoretical framework connecting the error dominance ratio R_k to the timestep and Lipschitz constant is novel; no prior hybrid solver work provided a formal perturbation-propagation analysis distinguishing direct from indirect corrections.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 330× speedup for 3D turbulent channel flow at equivalent statistical accuracy, combined with a provable O(R_k) error reduction, makes INC a practically compelling drop-in modification for existing hybrid PDE solvers with no architectural constraints. The formal error framework gives practitioners a principled understanding of why direct corrections blow up in chaotic regimes, guiding design choices beyond ad-hoc stabilization. Limitations include diminished benefits for well-damped problems or unconditionally stable implicit schemes, and the optimal rollout length remains an open tuning question. Broader impact spans climate modeling, aerodynamic design, and plasma physics where long-horizon stable simulation is critical.
