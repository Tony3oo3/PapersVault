## Problem

Modern RL algorithms require probabilistic models that support exact likelihood computation, efficient sampling, and variational inference simultaneously, but popular choices like diffusion models and autoregressive transformers each support only a subset of these capabilities, forcing researchers to add complex auxiliary machinery. Normalizing flows (NFs) possess all three capabilities natively, yet have been largely overlooked in RL due to the prevailing belief that they lack sufficient expressivity. This paper challenges that assumption by demonstrating that a single, simple NF architecture can serve as a policy, Q-function, and occupancy measure across diverse RL settings, matching or exceeding state-of-the-art generative models with significantly fewer parameters and hyperparameters.

## Input / Output

The NF architecture learns a conditional distribution p_θ(x|y) where the conditioning variable y is a state observation (for policies), a state-action pair (for Q-functions in GCRL), or absent (for marginal goal density estimation in UGS). For policy tasks, the output is a continuous action vector sampled via the inverse flow; for GCRL, the output is a density over future goal states used both as a Q-value and for unsupervised goal selection. The model operates on continuous state and action spaces without discretization, and uses a masking scheme (with probability 0.1) to share a single network between conditional and unconditional density estimation in the UGS setting.

## Method

The proposed NF block combines two components: a fully connected coupling network (from NICE/RealNVP) that splits the input in half and applies learned shift and scale transformations conditioned on y, followed by a linear flow (a 1D adaptation of GLOW's generalized permutation) that mixes dimensions via a fixed permutation and learnable triangular matrices. The architecture uses LayerNorm in the coupling network weights instead of ActNorm, and employs a denoising trick at sampling time to reduce noise from the MLE training objective. The key insight is that because NFs support both MLE (for BC/GCBC) and VI (for MaxEnt RL policy optimization) through straightforward backpropagation through the forward and inverse passes, the same architecture plugs directly into existing RL algorithms—TD3+BC, GCBC, and UGS—without requiring distillation, importance resampling, or other auxiliary components that diffusion- and flow-matching-based policies necessitate. Across 82 tasks in five RL settings, this single architecture with 6–12 blocks and 10M parameters outperforms or matches diffusion policies (65M+ params), flow-matching policies, and autoregressive transformers while using 3× fewer hyperparameters.

## Results

Across 82 tasks in five RL settings, NF-based methods achieve competitive or superior performance with significantly fewer parameters (10M vs. 65.78M for Diffusion Policy) and faster inference (0.127s vs. 1.28s). In conditional imitation learning, NF-GCBC outperforms the closest flow-matching baseline (FM-GCBC) by 77% across 45 OGBench tasks and beats all dedicated offline RL algorithms. In offline RL, NF-RLBC outperforms all baselines on 15/30 tasks and achieves 230% higher performance than the next-best method on puzzle-3x3-play. In unsupervised goal-conditioned RL, NF-UGS surpasses the CRL-oracle (which uses test-time goals) in asymptotic success rate.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In imitation learning, NF-BC outperforms Diffusion Policy (both CNN and Transformer variants) [15], VQ-BET [53], and BeT [76] on PushT and Kitchen while using 6× fewer parameters and 10× faster inference than DiffPolicy. In conditional imitation learning, NF-GCBC beats the Gaussian-policy GCBC [22] on 40/45 tasks, outperforms FM-GCBC (flow-matching policy) [57] by 77%, and surpasses all offline RL baselines including GCIQL [48], QRL [88], and CRL [25] despite being a pure BC algorithm. In offline RL, NF-RLBC outperforms ReBRAC [84], IDQL [37], IFQL, and FQL [68] on 15/30 tasks and achieves top-2 on 10/30, with a 230% margin on puzzle-3x3-play; notably, all diffusion/flow-based baselines require additional distillation or resampling machinery to leverage policy expressiveness, while NF-RLBC uses direct gradient backpropagation. In unsupervised GCRL, NF-UGS outperforms CRL-oracle [25] (which has access to test-time goals), CRL-uniform, and CRL-minmax, demonstrating that a single NF can estimate both the Q-function and coverage density without the contrastive learning assumptions of prior methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the RealNVP coupling network (Dinh et al., 2017) and GLOW's generalized permutation (Kingma & Dhariwal, 2018), combining them into a single NF block with LayerNorm, and adopts the denoising trick from Zhai et al. (2024) for sampling. Rather than proposing a new NF architecture, the paper's contribution is empirical: it demonstrates that this straightforward combination of existing NF components, when plugged into standard RL algorithms (BC, GCBC, MaxEnt RL+BC, UGS), matches or exceeds diffusion policies, autoregressive transformers, and flow-matching baselines across 82 tasks. The paper departs from the prevailing RL-community assumption that NFs are too restrictive or unstable for high-dimensional control, positioning NFs as a generally capable alternative to the diffusion/transformer paradigm that has dominated recent RL work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: NF-based policies require 6× fewer parameters than diffusion policies, use fewer hyperparameters, and support end-to-end training with a single loss function, making them substantially easier to implement and tune for RL practitioners. The finding that a BC-based NF policy (NF-GCBC) outperforms dedicated offline RL algorithms that learn value functions challenges the assumption that value function estimation is necessary for strong conditional imitation. Key limitations include the invertibility constraint on NF architectures (restricting the class of representable distributions) and the fact that no new NF architecture is proposed; the paper leaves open whether more expressive NF designs (e.g., continuous-indexed flows) could further close remaining gaps on the hardest tasks.
