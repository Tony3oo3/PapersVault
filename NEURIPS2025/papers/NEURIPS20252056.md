## Problem

Generative models for real-world image super-resolution (both GAN- and diffusion-based) frequently distort textual structures, producing malformed strokes or unrecognizable characters, especially in complex-script languages like Chinese. Existing text-specific SR methods require multi-stage pipelines (detection, regional SR, fusion) and degrade on full-image scenarios with long, vertical, or multi-line text. Additionally, no existing dataset simultaneously provides accurate full-image text segmentation masks and high-quality background details needed for joint text-aware SR training.

## Input / Output

The model takes a degraded low-resolution RGB image and a free-form text prompt (which must contain the keyword "text") as input, and produces a 4× super-resolved high-resolution image together with a pixel-level text segmentation mask in a single forward pass. The LR image is encoded by a VAE encoder into latent space, while the prompt is processed by a text encoder; the token slice corresponding to the keyword "text" is identified dynamically within the conditioning vector. Inference follows a tile-based strategy with a fixed diffusion timestep of t=200, and the model is built on the Kolors variant of LDMs.

## Method

TADiSR introduces two key components on top of a pre-trained LDM: (1) Text-Aware Cross-Attention (TACA), where LoRA fine-tuning with a joint SR+segmentation task guides the U-Net's cross-attention layers to focus on text regions, and the attention maps responding to the "text" keyword token are extracted from all M intermediate layers, concatenated, and linearly projected to match the latent dimension; (2) Joint Segmentation Decoders (JSD), which replace the standard VAE decoder with a symmetric pair of image and text segmentation decoders that interact via Cross-Decoder Interaction Blocks (CDIB) in a multi-scale, dual-stream fashion—each CDIB splits features after a 1×1 conv, exchanges half the channels between branches through a Sigmoid-gated Hadamard product (GLU-like), and applies GroupNorm + SiLU + 1×1 conv with a zero-initialized skip connection. Training uses a composite loss combining MSE, LPIPS, and a Modified Focal Loss (which up-weights edge pixels where segmentation confidence is low) for the SR branch, plus MSE, Focal, and Dice losses for the segmentation branch, enabling mutual reinforcement between the two tasks. A scalable data synthesis pipeline constructs the FTSR dataset (50K triplets) by pasting OCR-filtered, SR-restored text patches from CTR onto text-free LSDIR backgrounds with aligned segmentation masks, then applying Real-ESRGAN degradation.

## Results

On the FTSR-TE test set (×4), TADiSR achieves the best scores across all five metrics: PSNR 25.49, SSIM 0.736, LPIPS 0.152, FID 32.13, and OCR-A 0.662. On the Real-CE validation set (Aligned, ×4), TADiSR reaches PSNR 24.02, SSIM 0.829, LPIPS 0.100, FID 38.01, and OCR-A 0.882, outperforming the second-best method (HAT) by a margin of 18.7% in OCR-based recognition accuracy. The method consistently leads on both pixel-fidelity and perceptual-quality metrics as well as text-specific legibility across both synthetic and real-world benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TADiSR outperforms all seven baselines on every metric across both datasets. Among GAN-based methods, HAT [4] is the strongest general SR baseline (PSNR 24.29 on FTSR, 23.05 on Real-CE), but TADiSR surpasses it by 1.2 dB PSNR and 18.7% OCR-A on Real-CE. Among diffusion-based general SR methods, OSEDiff [34] is the closest competitor on FTSR (PSNR 24.49, FID 32.47), yet TADiSR still leads by 1.0 dB PSNR and 6.6% OCR-A; on Real-CE, SeeSR [35] achieves the best FID (43.17) among diffusion baselines but TADiSR improves it to 38.01. Text-specific SR methods MARCONet [12] and DiffTSR [46] show the weakest full-image performance (OCR-A 0.467 and 0.543 on FTSR respectively) due to their reliance on cropped-region inference and hard length constraints, with TADiSR exceeding them by 19.5% and 11.9% OCR-A on FTSR. SupIR [41] and R-ESRGAN [32] trail all other methods on most metrics, confirming that neither large-scale scaling nor adversarial training alone addresses text structural fidelity.

## Relation to Prior Work

TADiSR builds on the Latent Diffusion Model framework (Rombach et al., 2022) instantiated via the Kolors variant, and departs from two prior lines of work: (1) generic diffusion-based real-world SR methods (SeeSR, OSEDiff, SupIR) that lack text-specific structural awareness, and (2) text image SR methods (MARCONet, DiffTSR) that operate on cropped text patches and require multi-stage detection–SR–fusion pipelines. The key conceptual departure is using LoRA fine-tuning to redirect cross-attention toward the 'text' token in the prompt, coupled with a joint segmentation decoder that shares features with the VAE image decoder via Cross-Decoder Interaction Blocks, unifying SR and text segmentation in a single end-to-end pass. The data synthesis pipeline also departs from prior practice by compositing OCR-filtered text patches from recognition datasets onto general-purpose SR backgrounds, rather than relying on manually annotated text-rich HR images.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Text distortion in super-resolved images is a practical bottleneck for downstream OCR, document processing, and signage restoration, and this paper demonstrates that a single diffusion pass can simultaneously restore text structure and general image quality without the patch-boundary artifacts of multi-stage pipelines. The multi-task coupling insight—that text segmentation supervision meaningfully improves text SR fidelity and vice versa—suggests a broader template for handling other under-attended semantic categories in generative restoration. Open questions include whether the synthetic FTSR distribution adequately captures real-world text degradation (e.g., extreme stroke fusion, motion blur), whether the approach generalizes beyond the 'text' keyword to other rare structural elements, and whether the tile-based inference strategy scales to very high-resolution scenes without quality loss.
