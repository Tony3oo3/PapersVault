## Problem

Current 3D molecular representation learning models encode individual conformers as rigid, static structures, ignoring the conformational flexibility that governs many molecular properties. Existing conformer ensemble approaches either produce unphysical averaged structures or require restrictive molecular alignment, and none simultaneously preserve both conformer-permutation symmetry and per-conformer geometric equivariance without alignment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252291/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a molecular graph (2D bond topology with atomic numbers) together with a set of up to 20 3D conformers (atomic coordinate matrices in R^{|V|×3}) sampled from the thermodynamically accessible conformational space; Boltzmann weights are used only for ground-truth computation, not as model input. Outputs are scalar molecular property predictions: regression values (MAE) for quantum mechanical properties (ionization potential, electron affinity, electronegativity, Sterimol steric descriptors) and binary classification scores (ROC-AUC) for SARS-CoV-2 inhibition tasks, with a fixed latent dimension of 128.

## Method

SPiCE is a hierarchical H-equivariant network (H = S_n × G^n) composed of stacked interaction blocks, each containing three components: (1) weight-shared 3D GNN layers (PaiNN, Equiformer, ClofNet, or ViSNet) that process each conformer independently while preserving geometric equivariance; (2) a Geometric Mixture-of-Experts (GMoE) module that routes scalar (type-0) and vector (type-1) features through separate invariant experts (MLPs) and equivariant experts (linear transforms gated by scalar features), using top-k selection with Gumbel-Sigmoid sampling and sparse upcycling for stable training; and (3) a hierarchical ensemble encoder that mean-pools across conformers, applies a GIN layer over 2D topology, and integrates molecular-level context back into conformer-level features via gated cross-attention. The architecture guarantees joint equivariance to conformer permutations and independent geometric transformations of each conformer through composition of symmetry-preserving operations, eliminating the need for alignment or unphysical intermediate structures.

## Results

SPiCE achieves superior performance in 34 out of 36 experimental configurations across 9 tasks and 4 backbone architectures. On Drugs-7.5K (MAE↓), SPiCE with PaiNN achieves IP=0.5281, EA=0.4929, and χ=0.2792, representing relative MAE reductions of 2.79%, 7.89%, and 5.78% over the second-best strategy. On Kraken (MAE↓), improvements reach up to 8.78% (L target), and on the highly imbalanced CoV2-3CL classification task (ROC↑), SPiCE with ClofNet achieves a perfect ROC of 1.0000, while on CoV2 (ROC↑) SPiCE with ViSNet reaches 0.6890, an 8.41% improvement over the best baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPiCE consistently outperforms all 1D/2D baselines (Fingerprint+RF, E3FP+RF, GIN, GIN-VN, GraphGPS) across every task, with the largest gaps on Kraken steric descriptors where 2D models struggle with 3D geometric information. Against single-conformer 3D GNNs (PaiNN, ClofNet, Equiformer, ViSNet with random conformer sampling), SPiCE with the same backbone always wins, confirming the value of explicit ensemble processing over random sampling. Among conformer ensemble aggregation strategies (Mean, DeepSets, Attention), SPiCE dominates in 34/36 configurations, with the Attention baseline performing worst in most settings. Against the specialized ensemble model ConfNet, SPiCE wins on all 9 tasks with the best backbone. The closest competitor is ConAN-FGW (ICML 2024), which SPiCE surpasses on all 9 tasks when using the optimal backbone (e.g., Kraken B5: 0.2178 vs. 0.3242; CoV2-3CL ROC: 1.0000 vs. 0.9180), though ConAN-FGW remains competitive on individual backbone configurations (e.g., CoV2-3CL with PaiNN: 0.8880 vs. 0.9180). The two configurations where SPiCE does not achieve the absolute best result are minor and task-specific, consistent with the known task-dependency in conformer ensemble modeling.

## Relation to Prior Work

SPiCE extends the DSSNet framework (Maron et al., ICML 2020) for S_n-equivariant set processing to the joint symmetry group H = S_n × G^n, where G is the geometric transformation group acting independently on each conformer. The type-0/type-1 feature decomposition and Equivariant Layer Normalization follow Equiformer (Liao & Smidt, ICLR 2023), while the Geometric Mixture-of-Experts module adapts standard MoE routing (Fedus et al., 2022; Lepikhin et al., 2021) and SAGPool-style attention (Lee et al., 2019) to handle scalar and vector features through separate invariant and equivariant expert networks. Unlike prior conformer ensemble methods (ConfNet, ConAN-FGW) that either produce unphysical averaged structures or require restrictive molecular alignment, SPiCE maintains exact joint equivariance throughout, and unlike simple pooling or DeepSets baselines, it captures cross-conformer interactions through a hierarchical GIN + gated attention mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SPiCE addresses a fundamental gap in 3D molecular representation learning: the treatment of molecules as rigid static structures when many experimentally observable properties depend on conformational distributions. The plug-and-play design (compatible with PaiNN, Equiformer, ClofNet, ViSNet, and even invariant backbones like DimeNet++) makes it broadly applicable, and the consistent 2–8% MAE improvements across 34/36 configurations suggest the symmetry-preserving MoE architecture is a robust inductive bias for conformer ensembles. Key limitations include the computational overhead of the hierarchical architecture relative to simple pooling, the absence of explicit thermodynamic priors (Boltzmann weights are used only for ground-truth computation, not as model input), and the restriction to at most 20 conformers per molecule. Open questions include whether the approach scales to very large ensembles, whether incorporating statistical-mechanical distribution information would further improve representations, and whether the type-separated MoE design generalizes to higher-order tensor features beyond type-0 and type-1.
