## Problem

Transformers have proven theoretical limitations in length generalization on certain retrieval and copying tasks, but it remained unclear whether large-scale pretraining on trillions of tokens could overcome these architectural constraints in practice. The paper asks whether pretraining selectively amplifies some transformer capabilities while leaving fundamental inductive biases intact, and which specific limitations persist in modern LLMs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253191/cropped_page2_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

For synthetic tasks, inputs are sequences of spaced characters (letters with diacritics and digits, ensuring one-token-per-character) of length 10–100, with a query token appended after a separator for retrieval or a separator for copying; outputs are a single retrieved token or a full copied/reversed sequence. For naturalistic validation, inputs are ~500-token Lorem Ipsum paragraphs (for copying) or truncated git commit logs (for direction-aware list reversal). Fine-tuning experiments use GPT-2 1.5B with APE, trained on sequences of length [ℓ_min, 100] and evaluated out-of-distribution on [101, 200], with a positional offset trick to cover all position embeddings.

## Method

The paper combines a C-RASP[pos] expressibility analysis (proving which of eight retrieval/copying variants length-generalize) with large-scale empirical evaluation of Llama-3 and Qwen2.5 models via in-context learning, revealing a pretraining-induced Directional Bias (right/forward tasks consistently outperform left/backward) and a persistent Uniqueness Bias. To disentangle architectural limits from pretraining artifacts, the authors fine-tune GPT-2 1.5B on each task and show that fine-tuning eliminates the Directional Bias but not the Uniqueness Bias, confirming the latter is architectural. Mechanistically, patching experiments demonstrate that induction heads are causally responsible for forward/right tasks while anti-induction heads handle backward/left tasks; pretraining creates an imbalance favoring induction circuits, and targeted fine-tuning can rebalance them. Naturalistic experiments on Lorem Ipsum copying and git commit manipulation validate that these biases produce real reliability risks in deployment.

## Results

Across all 8 pretrained LLMs (Llama-3.1-8B/70B, Qwen2.5-7B/32B, completion and instruction-tuned), a persistent Directional Bias is observed: rightward/forward retrieval and copying tasks (UR, NRFirst, NRLast, UF, NF) consistently outperform their leftward/backward counterparts (UL, NLFirst, NLLast, UB, NB) across all sequence lengths (10–100 tokens), prompt templates, and model sizes. After targeted fine-tuning of GPT-2 1.5B, all tasks provably in C-RASP[pos] (UL, UR, UF, UB, NLFirst, NRFirst) achieve 100% accuracy on out-of-distribution sequences (lengths 101–200), while tasks outside C-RASP[pos] (NLLast, NRLast, NF, NB) suffer significant accuracy drops, confirming that the uniqueness bias is architectural and not removable by fine-tuning. In the Lorem Ipsum copying stress test (~500 tokens), copying 'glitches' originate almost exclusively at ambiguous transition indices (tokens with multiple possible continuations), and in the Git commit history task, Llama-3 models reproduce forward commit lists near-perfectly but accuracy drops sharply when the order is reversed.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not benchmark against competing methods but instead compares three conditions to disentangle architectural limits from pretraining effects. (1) Pretrained LLMs vs. from-scratch transformers: from-scratch models (trained on the same tasks with APE or RoPE) show no directional bias, perfectly aligning with C-RASP[pos] symmetry predictions (Theorem 2), confirming the left-right asymmetry is an artifact of pretraining rather than the architecture. (2) Pretrained vs. fine-tuned models: fine-tuning GPT-2 1.5B eliminates the directional bias entirely (induction and anti-induction head strengths become balanced), but the uniqueness bias persists—tasks outside C-RASP[pos] still fail to length-generalize, matching theoretical impossibility results. (3) C-RASP[pos] theory vs. empirical results: the theoretical expressibility predictions (which tasks are in C-RASP[pos]) perfectly track empirical length-generalization outcomes in both from-scratch training (Figures 8–9) and fine-tuning (Figure 6), validating the framework as a predictor of transformer capabilities. The mechanistic patching experiments further confirm that induction heads are causally responsible for forward copying and anti-induction heads for backward copying, with fine-tuning amplifying the relevant head family to restore symmetry.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the C-RASP[pos] length-generalization framework of Huang et al. (2025) to a systematic family of retrieval and copying tasks inspired by the flip-flop setup of Liu et al. (2024a) and the algorithmic learning analysis of Zhou et al. (2024). Mechanistically, it builds on the induction-head circuit of Elhage et al. (2021) by hypothesizing and empirically validating a symmetric 'anti-induction' circuit for backward retrieval. The key departure from prior work is the explicit question of whether large-scale pretraining overcomes the length-generalization limits that are well-documented in small from-scratch transformers — a question that prior theoretical and empirical studies have not addressed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides practitioners with a concrete reliability warning: pretrained LLMs exhibit a left-right copying/retrieval asymmetry and persistent glitches on non-unique copying that can corrupt critical outputs (commit hashes, identifiers, DNA sequences) in production systems. The clean separation between pretraining-induced biases (fixable by fine-tuning) and architectural limits (not fixable without external tools) gives a principled decision framework for when fine-tuning suffices versus when tool-augmented approaches are needed. Open questions include whether these findings transfer to recurrent or hybrid architectures, whether the formal C-RASP[pos] guarantees extend to RoPE, and whether sufficiently large in-distribution context windows during pretraining could shift the out-of-distribution boundary to practically irrelevant lengths.
