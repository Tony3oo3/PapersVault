## Problem

RCTs often have small sample sizes, motivating the use of external controls to improve treatment effect estimation. However, existing borrowing methods (e.g., adaptive lasso-based approaches) rely on average-level bias measures that overlook individual-level heterogeneity and are sensitive to outliers, leading to suboptimal comparability and biased estimates when the exchangeability assumption is violated.

## Input / Output

The method takes RCT data (covariates X ∈ R^p, binary treatment A, outcome Y) and external control data (covariates X, outcome Y only, no treatment assignment) as input. It produces per-sample influence scores for each external control, a nested sequence of candidate borrowed subsets ranked by influence score, and a final estimated ATE using the optimally selected top-K external samples. The influence scores are computed via a closed-form Hessian-based expression, avoiding costly model retraining for each candidate sample.

## Method

The core mechanism uses influence function theory to quantify how much each external control sample perturbs the outcome model fitted on the RCT's control group: IF_loss(z) ≈ Σ|∇_θ L(Z_i, θ̂)ᵀ H_θ̂⁻¹ ∇_θ L(z, θ̂)|, where H_θ̂ is the Hessian of the empirical loss. This yields individual-level comparability scores that are invariant to other external samples, making the approach robust to outliers. From the ranked scores, nested top-K subsets are constructed, and a semiparametric efficient AIPW-type estimator τ̂_S is derived under a weaker exchangeability assumption (only for the borrowed subset). The paper then analyzes the asymptotic bias and variance of τ̂_S when exchangeability is violated, showing bias is proportional to E_S[R/q · {μ_0(X) − m_0(X)}], and selects the optimal K by minimizing the estimated MSE (bias² + variance) across all candidate subsets.

## Results

On the real-world NSW & PSID dataset, the proposed influence-based estimator (τ_if) achieves a standard deviation of 0.0127–0.0132 and a bias of 0.0013–0.0123 across Top-K values, outperforming the AIPW-only baseline (std=0.0134) and the adaptive lasso-based borrowing (std=0.0136–0.0145, bias=0.0106–0.0181). On the synthetic Linear dataset at Top-K=100, τ_if achieves std=0.0841 and bias=0.0185, compared to τ_lasso's std=0.1031 and bias=0.0965, and τ_aipw's std=0.1075. The full-borrowing estimator (τ_full) is excluded from further comparison due to its substantially larger bias (e.g., 0.4492 on Nonlinear, 0.0951 on Linear).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against three baselines: (1) τ_aipw, the standard AIPW estimator using only RCT data; (2) τ_full, AIPW with full borrowing of all external controls; and (3) τ_lasso, the adaptive lasso-based borrowing approach of Gao et al. (2025). The proposed τ_if consistently achieves the lowest standard deviation across all three datasets, reducing variance by approximately 18–22% relative to τ_aipw on the synthetic Linear data (0.0841 vs. 0.1075 at K=100) and by about 4–5% on the real-world NSW&PSID data (0.0128 vs. 0.0134 at K=20). Against τ_lasso, τ_if wins on both std and bias in the Linear setting (std 0.0841 vs. 0.1031; bias 0.0185 vs. 0.0965 at K=100) and in the real-world setting (bias 0.0078 vs. 0.0152 at K=20). In the Nonlinear setting, τ_if and τ_lasso are closer in std (0.2136 vs. 0.2218 at K=100), but τ_if still maintains a lower bias (0.0459 vs. 0.0648). The full-borrowing baseline τ_full is dominated by all other methods due to its large bias (0.4492 on Nonlinear), confirming that indiscriminate borrowing of all external controls is harmful.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the AIPW/doubly-robust estimation framework (Bang & Robins, 2005) to a combined RCT + subset-of-external-controls setting, deriving a new semiparametric efficient estimator under a weaker exchangeability assumption that applies only to the borrowed subset rather than the full external pool. It departs from the most recent adaptive lasso-based borrowing approach (Gao et al., 2025) by replacing global bias penalization with individual-level influence-function scores that measure how much each external sample perturbs the RCT outcome model. The influence function machinery is borrowed from Koh & Liang (2017) and Cook & Weisberg (1980), repurposed here from model interpretability to sample-selection for causal inference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The influence-based selection criterion is robust to outliers in external controls because each sample's score is computed independently, whereas the lasso approach depends on a global outcome model that can be distorted by extreme points. The bias-variance trade-off analysis (Theorem 1) provides a principled, data-driven rule for how much to borrow, addressing a practical concern that the exchangeability assumption is rarely fully satisfied. A key open limitation is computational: the Hessian matrix required for influence scores scales poorly with model complexity, making the approach less straightforward for deep neural networks with millions of parameters. Future work could explore approximate influence functions (e.g., via conjugate gradient or random projections) to scale to high-dimensional settings.
