## Problem

Evaluating PDE operators (Laplacian, biharmonic, etc.) on neural networks via nested first-order AD scales exponentially in derivative order, while vanilla Taylor mode AD, though polynomial, is empirically slower than nested backpropagation in practice. No unified forward-propagation scheme existed for general linear PDE operators beyond the Laplacian, and the relationship between specialized forward schemes (e.g., the forward Laplacian) and Taylor mode remained unclear.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a PyTorch neural network's computational graph (captured via torch.fx), an input point x_0, and a set of direction vectors (standard basis vectors for exact computation, or i.i.d. random vectors for stochastic estimation). It produces the value of a linear PDE operator at x_0 by propagating a reduced set of Taylor coefficient vectors through the graph: instead of 1+KR vectors (standard Taylor mode), it propagates only 1+(K−1)R+1 vectors, where K is the derivative order and R the number of directions. The output is the sum of highest-order Taylor coefficients, which equals the desired PDE operator value.

## Method

The key insight is that in Faà di Bruno's formula, the highest-degree Taylor coefficient's propagation rule is linear in the input's highest coefficient, so the sum across all R directions can be pulled inside the propagation rather than computed after. This 'collapsing' is implemented as two graph rewrites: (1) a forward traversal that pushes replicate nodes down to eliminate redundant computations on shared lower-order coefficients, and (2) a backward traversal that propagates the final sum node up through the graph, so each node receives a single summed highest-order vector instead of R separate ones. For operators with mixed partial derivatives (e.g., the biharmonic), the paper leverages Griewank et al.'s interpolation formula to decompose the coefficient tensor into a linear combination of same-direction K-jets, each of which is independently collapsible. The collapsed scheme recovers the forward Laplacian as a special case (K=2, R=D) and extends naturally to stochastic variants and higher-order operators, achieving ~2× speedup over nested AD and ~50% reduction over standard Taylor mode.

## Results

On a 5-layer MLP (D→768→768→512→512→1) with tanh activations, collapsed Taylor mode reduces per-datum runtime for the exact Laplacian from 0.61 ms (nested 1st-order AD) to 0.29 ms (0.48×), and for the exact biharmonic operator from 1.2 ms to 0.69 ms (0.55×). In the stochastic setting, collapsed Taylor mode achieves up to 9× speedup over nested AD for the biharmonic operator (4.9 ms vs. 44 ms per sample) and 5× memory reduction (38 MiB vs. 210 MiB, differentiable). Peak memory for the exact Laplacian drops from 4.4 MiB (nested) to 2.1 MiB (collapsed), a 0.47× reduction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares collapsed Taylor mode against two baselines: nested 1st-order AD (the standard practice in ML libraries) and standard Taylor mode (as implemented in JAX by Bettencourt et al., 2019). Collapsed Taylor mode consistently outperforms nested 1st-order AD across all operators and settings: roughly 2× faster for (weighted) Laplacians in both exact and stochastic modes, 1.7× faster for the exact biharmonic, and up to 9× faster for the stochastic biharmonic, while using 40–50% less memory for Laplacians and up to 5× less for the stochastic biharmonic. Against standard Taylor mode, collapsed Taylor mode achieves an additional ~2× speedup (e.g., 0.29 ms vs. 0.56 ms per datum for the exact Laplacian), matching the theoretical vector-counting ratio. The forward Laplacian of Li et al. (2024) is shown to be a special case of collapsed Taylor mode (K=2, exact), so it is subsumed rather than separately benchmarked; the paper's JAX experiments confirm that the forward Laplacian and collapsed Taylor mode yield equivalent performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Taylor mode AD framework (Griewank & Walther 2008; Bettencourt et al. 2019) and the interpolation formula for mixed partials (Griewank et al. 1999), identifying that the highest-order coefficient's propagation rule is linear in the input's highest coefficient—a structural property of Faà di Bruno's formula that had not been exploited for graph-level optimization. It subsumes the forward Laplacian (Li et al. 2024) as a special case and extends the stochastic Taylor derivative estimator (Shi et al. 2024) by collapsing the summation over random directions. The key departure from prior work is reframing the optimization as a simple graph rewrite (pushing a sum up the computational graph) rather than a new AD scheme, positioning it as something a JIT compiler could absorb transparently.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper resolves a practical gap: vanilla Taylor mode, despite its favorable polynomial scaling, was empirically slower than nested backpropagation for common PDE operators like the Laplacian, undermining its utility in PINNs and VMC. Collapsing Taylor mode closes this gap (≈2× speedup over nested 1st-order AD, ≈50% over standard Taylor mode) while remaining applicable to general linear differential operators and stochastic variants. Limitations include a PyTorch implementation with limited operator coverage and no use of the more efficient Griewank–Walther schemes; the authors note these are promising future efforts. A key open question is whether the graph-rewriting optimizations can be integrated into mainstream ML compilers (JAX, PyTorch) without user-facing API changes, which would make the technique broadly accessible.
