## Problem

Existing feature transformation methods face a fundamental trade-off: discrete search over feature-operator combinations is intractable due to the enormous combinatorial space, while continuous gradient search in embedding space (e.g., MOAT) is highly sensitive to initialization and step size, frequently converging to local optima. The paper asks how to learn a generalized, global representation of high-performing feature transformations and then generate task-optimal features in a way that avoids both combinatorial explosion and local-trap vulnerability.

## Input / Output

The method takes a raw tabular feature matrix and a discrete operator set (e.g., log, +, *, /) as input. Feature sets are tokenized into postfix expressions (e.g., 'f1 f2 *, f3 log, f4 f5 +') to reduce sequence length and ambiguity. The output is a transformed feature token sequence that, when applied to the raw data, yields an augmented tabular dataset for downstream classification or regression. A GCN-derived graph embedding of the transformed table's feature-correlation structure serves as a conditioning signal throughout generation, tying the output to the specific dataset's topology and semantics.

## Method

DIFFT reformulates feature transformation as a reward-guided generative task with three components. A VAE with a Transformer encoder maps feature token sequences into a compact latent space, while a hierarchical semi-autoregressive decoder first predicts the number of features (inter-feature level) and then autoregressively generates each feature chunk in parallel (intra-feature level), preserving intra-feature dependencies without redundant cross-feature attention. A Latent Diffusion Model (Transformer-based denoiser, not UNet) learns the distribution of effective feature embeddings, conditioned on a GCN graph embedding of the transformed table. During inference, a lightweight regression evaluator provides gradient signals that are injected into the DDIM reverse process (following RCGDM), steering the denoising trajectory toward high-reward regions of the latent space. Training data for the embedding space is collected via an RL-based multi-agent system that explores feature transformations and scores them by downstream performance improvement.

## Results

DIFFT achieves the best F-1 score (classification) or 1-RAE (regression) on all 14 benchmark datasets. Headline margins include 0.757 on Messidor Features (vs. 0.699 for ELLM, the second-best), 0.632 on Openml_618 (vs. 0.477 for MOAT), and 0.971 on Ionosphere (vs. 0.942 for ELLM). On the large-scale Ap-omentum-ovary dataset (10,936 features), DIFFT reaches 0.885, outperforming MOAT and ELLM (both 0.849). Training time per epoch is 5–27× faster than MOAT (e.g., 19.64 s vs. 524.4 s on Ap-omentum-ovary), and inference is up to 5.4× faster (0.43 s vs. 2.33 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DIFFT outperforms all 10 baselines on every dataset. The strongest continuous-search baseline, MOAT (Wang et al., NeurIPS 2023), is the closest competitor on classification datasets (e.g., 0.848 vs. 0.877 on SpectF; 0.849 vs. 0.885 on Ap-omentum-ovary) but degrades sharply on harder regression tasks, reaching only 0.162 on Openml_616 versus DIFFT's 0.335. ELLM (Gong et al., AAAI 2025), the strongest generative baseline, is competitive on classification (0.837 on SpectF, 0.942 on Ionosphere) but trails DIFFT by 5.8 points on Messidor Features (0.699 vs. 0.757) and 15.6 points on Openml_618 (0.476 vs. 0.632). GRFG (Wang et al., KDD 2022) is the best RL-based discrete-search method, reaching 0.796 on SpectF and 0.928 on Ionosphere, yet still lags DIFFT by 2–4 points. Traditional linear methods (PCA, LDA) and heuristic pipelines (AFAT, NFS, TTG) are consistently 5–20 points behind DIFFT, with PCA and LDA producing negative 1-RAE on several regression datasets (e.g., LDA: −0.174 on Openml_616), indicating they actively harm downstream performance. In the robustness check (Table 4), DIFFT leads across all five downstream models (DT, KNN, LR, SVC, RF) on SpectF, while MOAT drops to 0.836 under SVC and ELLM falls to 0.837 under RF, confirming DIFFT's superior model-agnostic generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

DIFFT extends the generative feature transformation paradigm established by MOAT [27] and ELLM [6], which frame feature sets as token sequences and search for optimal transformations in a continuous embedding space via gradient ascent. The key conceptual departure is replacing this static gradient search with a reward-guided latent diffusion process: a VAE learns the embedding distribution, a Transformer-based LDM models the global structure of effective feature embeddings, and evaluator gradients steer the reverse denoising trajectory toward high-performing regions. The semi-autoregressive decoder draws on the NMT literature (Wang et al. [25], Stern et al. [23]) to exploit the compositional structure of feature token sequences—parallel across features, autoregressive within each feature—rather than treating the entire sequence as a flat autoregressive stream.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that reframing feature transformation as a guided generative task (rather than a search problem) yields consistent improvements across 14 diverse tabular datasets while simultaneously reducing training and inference time by 5×+ compared to the strongest continuous-search baseline (MOAT). The ablation finding that the diffusion model's learned distribution alone (NoR) outperforms continuous search (CS) suggests the global structure captured by the LDM is itself a valuable inductive bias beyond what local gradient ascent can exploit. Open questions include scaling to ultra-wide feature spaces (beyond ~10K features), extending to task-agnostic foundation models for feature generation, and whether the reward-guided diffusion formulation generalizes to other structured discrete generation problems beyond feature engineering.
