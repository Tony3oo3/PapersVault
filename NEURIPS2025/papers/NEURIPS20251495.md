## Problem

Modern linear RNNs and SSMs like Mamba rely on channel-wise (diagonal) state transitions, which provably lack the expressivity needed for state-tracking tasks that dense, input-dependent transition matrices can solve. The central challenge is how to obtain the full expressivity of dense linear RNNs while retaining the parallelizability and stable parametrization that make diagonal RNNs practical at scale.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a standard sequence of token embeddings and produces a sequence of output vectors, but internally maintains a matrix-valued hidden state H_t ∈ R^{d_state × d_inner} (as in Mamba) rather than a vector. Unusually, the number of sequential computation steps ℓ* is not fixed at training time but adapts per sequence based on a convergence criterion (‖y^ℓ − y^{ℓ−1}‖∞ / ‖y^ℓ‖∞ < 0.1), meaning the model automatically allocates more compute to harder inputs. At test time, ℓ* can exceed the training-time cap ℓ_max, enabling length generalization beyond the training sequence length.

## Method

The paper factorizes a dense transition matrix A_t = Q_t^{-1} Λ_t (following Helfrich et al., 2018) and reformulates the dense RNN recurrence as a fixed-point equation h* = f_θ(x, h*) where f_θ is a diagonal linear RNN that interleaves channel mixing (via structured Q_t: Householder reflections, Kronecker products, or diagonal-plus-low-rank) with sequence mixing (via diagonal Λ_t). Convergence is guaranteed by Banach's fixed-point theorem under the conditions ‖Λ‖₂ < 1 and ‖I − Q‖₂ < 1, which are enforced through Mamba-style input gating and L2-normalized mixer vectors. For the Mamba extension, the fourth-order tensor mixer is avoided by passing the contracted output y_t between iterations, reducing each fixed-point step to a standard Mamba forward pass with an adjusted input x̃_t = Q_t(x_t − y_t^{ℓ−1}) + y_t^{ℓ−1}. Shifted hidden-state dependence (parameters conditioned on y_{t-1}^{ℓ−1}) is critical for memory/copying capabilities, and implicit differentiation at the fixed-point ensures zero additional memory overhead in the backward pass relative to a single diagonal RNN layer.

## Results

On the A5 and S5 state-tracking benchmarks (trained at sequence length 16), a single-layer FP-Mamba with Kronecker or Householder mixers achieves length generalization well beyond the training length, while 2-layer Mamba and Mamba-2 fail to solve the task even at the training length. On the copying task at ×2 length generalization, 1-layer FP-Mamba-H matches 2-layer Gated DeltaNet (the strongest baseline), achieving 0.94 ± 0.03 character accuracy with full hidden-state dependence (Table 1). On language pretraining (FineWeb, 2B tokens, context 2048), FP-Mamba improves validation perplexity over an identically-sized Mamba, while the effective number of fixed-point iterations remains below 10, indicating the model adapts its sequential compute to task difficulty.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Mamba and Mamba-2 (2-layer baselines), FP-Mamba (1 layer) decisively wins on both state-tracking (A5, S5) and copying: Mamba variants cannot learn state-tracking even at the training sequence length of 16, whereas FP-Mamba generalizes to lengths >3× the training length. On copying at ×2 generalization, FP-Mamba-H matches the strongest baseline Gated DeltaNet (Yang et al., 2025), while both Mamba 1 and Mamba 2 struggle significantly. Against LSTM (the theoretical upper bound for state-tracking), FP-Mamba approaches but does not fully match LSTM's performance on A5/S5, though it achieves this with a single layer versus LSTM's inherent non-linearity. In the long-range setting (train length 128, eval up to 512), FP-Mamba with Kronecker mixers slightly outperforms DeltaProduct (Siems et al., 2025) on A5, attributed to the non-linearity introduced by the fixed-point dynamics. On language modeling, FP-Mamba improves perplexity over Mamba with identical hyperparameters, and the Transformer (Vaswani et al., 2017) serves only as a lower bound in Fig. 1, confirming that the fixed-point framework unlocks capabilities unavailable to both attention and diagonal SSMs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) diagonal linear RNNs/SSMs like Mamba, which are parallelizable but provably lack state-tracking expressivity (Merrill et al., 2024; Cirone et al., 2024b); (2) explicit dense-transition RNNs such as DeltaNet and its Householder-based extensions (Yang et al., 2024b; Grazzi et al., 2024; Siems et al., 2025), which add non-diagonal structure but require careful hardware-aware design; and (3) implicit/equilibrium models (Bai et al., 2019; Ghaoui et al., 2021), which parametrize networks as fixed-points. The key departure is that rather than explicitly parameterizing a dense transition matrix, the paper shows that a dense linear RNN can be *implicitly* recovered as the fixed-point of a diagonal RNN, with the Banach contraction theorem guaranteeing convergence. This contrasts with concurrent work by Schöne et al. (2025), which applies implicit iteration to multi-layer architectures for language modeling, whereas here the fixed-point defines a single dense RNN layer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a principled, adaptive mechanism to trade parallelism for expressivity: the number of fixed-point iterations ℓ* scales with task difficulty rather than being fixed a priori, and the backward pass incurs no additional cost via implicit differentiation. This is practically significant because it means a single-layer model can match or exceed multi-layer baselines on state-tracking (A5, S5) and copying without increasing parameter count. Open questions include: (1) whether the forward-pass sequential overhead (O(ℓ*) per token) becomes prohibitive at scale, (2) convergence guarantees weaken for the time-varying, hidden-state-dependent case used in practice, and (3) the language modeling evidence is preliminary (2B tokens, small model). The paper explicitly identifies GPU kernel fusion and convergence-rate improvements as key future directions for practical deployment.
