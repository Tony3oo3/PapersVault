## Problem

Greedy Equivalence Search (GES) recovers the true Markov equivalence class in the sample limit but suffers from high computational cost and finite-sample inaccuracy, largely because its strictly greedy insertion step introduces excess adjacencies between variables that are conditionally independent. The paper asks whether relaxing the greedy choice—applying any score-increasing insertion rather than always the highest-scoring one—can improve both speed and accuracy while preserving asymptotic correctness.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254104/cropped_page3_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes i.i.d. observational samples from a linear-Gaussian (or multinomial) distribution, a decomposable and consistent scoring criterion (e.g., BIC), and optionally a set of prior assumptions expressed as required and forbidden edges (directed or undirected). It outputs a Markov equivalence class represented as a CPDAG; when interventional data is additionally provided, I-ORIENT refines the CPDAG into a smaller I-MEC by orienting undirected edges. The search operates over CPDAGs rather than individual DAGs, and the scoring criterion must satisfy local consistency for the theoretical guarantees to hold.

## Method

LGES replaces GES's strictly greedy insertion step with two less-greedy strategies: CONSERVATIVEINSERT discards all INSERT(X,Y,·) operators for a pair (X,Y) as soon as any single score-decreasing INSERT(X,Y,T) is found (implying conditional independence under Prop. 1), while SAFEINSERT picks an arbitrary DAG G from the current MEC and discards all INSERT(X,Y,·) if G∪{X→Y} scores lower than G. SAFEINSERT is proven sound—it returns a valid score-increasing INSERT if and only if one exists (Prop. 2)—yielding the asymptotic correctness guarantee of Corollary 1. Prior knowledge is incorporated via a prioritization scheme (GETPRIORITYINSERTS) that ranks candidate insertions by consistency with expert constraints, rather than initializing the search with those edges, making the algorithm robust to misspecified knowledge. For interventional data, I-ORIENT orients each undirected edge X–Y by comparing local scores s(y,x) vs. s(y) under interventions that include X but not Y, then propagates orientations via Meek's rules, providing the first asymptotically correct score-based interventional refinement that scales beyond 100 nodes.

## Results

On Erdős-Rényi graphs with 150 variables and 300 edges (n=10⁴ samples), LGES (Conservative) achieves approximately 30 SHD, roughly 2× more accurate than GES, while running an order of magnitude (up to 10×) faster. For interventional data, LGIES (Conservative) is up to 10× faster and up to 1.5× more accurate than GIES. NoTears, by contrast, achieves only ~125 SHD on 100-variable graphs, far worse than all GES-family methods. CONSERVATIVEINSERT yields better accuracy than SAFEINSERT, though both strategies produce similar runtimes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LGES dominates GES [9] on both axes: it is up to 10× faster and up to 2× more accurate (lower SHD) across all graph sizes tested, with the accuracy gap driven by fewer excess adjacencies and incorrect orientations. Against XGES-0 and XGES [36], LGES variants (LGES-0, LGES, LGES+) consistently improve on their respective greedy counterparts in both runtime and SHD, confirming that the less-greedy insertion strategy is beneficial independent of the deletion/restart heuristics. PC [54] is the fastest algorithm overall but is less accurate than GES for n=10⁴; it only becomes competitive in the many-variables/few-samples regime. NoTears [61] has substantially worse accuracy (SHD ≈ 125 at p=100 vs. ~45–60 for GES-family methods) though its runtime scaling appears somewhat better. For interventional data, LGIES (Conservative) outperforms GIES [24] by up to 10× in speed and 1.5× in accuracy, while LGES (Safe) + I-ORIENT—though the only combination proven asymptotically correct—matches GIES on ER-3 but lags slightly on ER-2 because it uses only observational data to learn the initial MEC.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends Greedy Equivalence Search (GES, Chickering 2003) by challenging its core assumption that the highest-scoring insertion should always be applied. It builds on the XGES heuristics (Nazaret & Blei 2024) for operator ordering and on the PC algorithm's philosophy of leveraging conditional independence, but repurposes these ideas within a score-based framework via CONSERVATIVEINSERT and SAFEINSERT. For interventional data, it departs from GIES (Hauser & Bühlmann 2012), which was shown to be asymptotically inconsistent, by introducing I-ORIENT as the first score-based orientation procedure with correctness guarantees that scales beyond moderate dimensions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 10× speedup and 2× accuracy gain over GES, combined with robustness to misspecified expert knowledge, make LGES a practically important upgrade for the causal discovery community, particularly in high-dimensional settings where GES struggles. The asymptotic correctness of I-ORIENT fills a notable gap: prior score-based interventional methods were either inconsistent or infeasible at scale. Open questions include the unresolved soundness of CONSERVATIVEINSERT, the sensitivity of SAFEINSERT to the choice of DAG within an MEC, and the restriction to Markovian (causally sufficient) settings, which limits applicability to real-world systems with unobserved confounders.
