## Problem

UAV 6-DoF localization in resource-limited settings (no GNSS, no internet) currently relies on either large posed image databases or memory-intensive 3D models, both impractical for field deployment. Lightweight orthographic geodata (digital orthophotos and surface models) are increasingly available from government geoportals and require ~30× less memory than 3D models, yet no existing dataset or benchmark addresses 6-DoF UAV localization using these 2.5D rasters as the sole reference, and the perspective-to-orthographic domain gap remains unexplored.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a UAV query image (1024×682 or 1024×767 px), a paired orthophoto raster (DOP, 1024×1024 px), and a paired elevation raster (DSM, 1024×1024 px at ~5 cm/pixel), all pre-aligned via ray-casting so no image retrieval step is needed. It outputs a georeferenced 6-DoF camera pose in SE(3) and, optionally, refined camera intrinsics (3×3 matrix). The paired structure is the key design choice: it decouples localization accuracy from retrieval errors, enabling isolated evaluation of matching and pose-estimation components.

## Method

The pipeline matches 2D features between the query image and DOP using a pluggable state-of-the-art matcher (e.g., GIM+DKM, SuperGlue, LoFTR), lifts each DOP match to 3D via the corresponding DSM elevation value, and solves for pose via RANSAC-EPnP with a 5-px inlier threshold; calibration additionally runs a Levenberg-Marquardt joint refinement of intrinsics and extrinsics. The core contribution, AdHoP (Adaptive Homography Preconditioning), estimates a homography from the initial 2D-2D correspondences using normalized DLT with RANSAC, warps the DOP to reduce the perspective disparity, performs a second round of feature matching on the warped orthophoto, maps new matches back via H⁻¹, re-lifts to 3D, and refines the pose—accepting the result only if mean reprojection error decreases. This exploits the approximate planarity of common aerial surfaces (roads, building roofs, fields) and is entirely method-agnostic, requiring no training or dataset-specific assumptions. The framework is benchmarked across 13 matcher configurations, with GIM+DKM+AdHoP achieving 75.4% recall at 1 m–1° and up to 95% matching improvement and 63% translation error reduction over the unrefined baseline.

## Results

On the OrthoLoC test set, GIM+DKM with AdHoP achieves the best overall localization: 1.3 px matching error, 0.32 m translation error, 0.12° rotation error, and 75.4% recall at the 1 m–1° threshold. Sparse matchers SP+SG and SP+LG also achieve sub-40 cm translation error (0.35 m and 0.37 m) but with lower recall (~64%). AdHoP yields dramatic improvements for previously weak methods: XFeat* matching error drops by 95.9% (222.2→9.2 px), DKM translation error drops by 63% (3.83→1.40 m), and RoMa 1 m–1° recall increases by 23 percentage points (44.4%→54.6%). Semi-dense matchers (LoFTR, eLoFTR, XoFTR) remain severely degraded, with translation errors exceeding 100 m and recall below 26%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GIM+DKM (Shen et al., 2024) is the clear winner, achieving 75.4% recall at 1 m–1° and 0.32 m translation error with AdHoP, outperforming all other matchers by a wide margin. SP+SG (Sarlin et al., 2020) and SP+LG (Lindenberger et al., 2023) achieve comparable translation accuracy (~0.35–0.37 m) but with ~11 percentage points lower recall, indicating they successfully localize fewer images. MASt3R (Leroy et al., 2024) is the second-best dense matcher (63.5% recall, 0.60 m TE) but trails GIM+DKM by ~12 points in recall. RoMa (Edstedt et al., 2024) benefits substantially from AdHoP, halving its translation error (1.47→0.75 m) and gaining 23 points in recall, yet still lags GIM+DKM. Semi-dense matchers—LoFTR (Sun et al., 2021), eLoFTR (Wang et al., 2024), and XoFTR (Tuzcuoğlu et al., 2024)—fail catastrophically on this cross-domain task, with translation errors of 100–125 m and recall below 26%, suggesting their training distributions do not cover the perspective-to-orthographic gap. DUSt3R (Wang et al., 2024) performs surprisingly poorly (6.4% recall at 1 m–1°), while XFeat* (Potje et al., 2024) with rotation-invariant matching shows the largest relative AdHoP gain (95.9% matching error reduction) but still trails the top methods in absolute accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from both dominant paradigms in UAV localization—image-retrieval-based methods (NetVLAD, Dislocation) and 3D-model-based approaches (SfM meshes, dense depth maps)—by repurposing widely available orthographic geodata (DOPs and DSMs) as the sole reference for 6-DoF pose estimation. The AdHoP refinement applies classical DLT+RANSAC homography estimation in a new context, exploiting the quasi-planarity of aerial scenes to reduce perspective disparity before a second matching round. The benchmarking framework is deliberately backbone-agnostic, integrating existing feature matchers (SuperGlue, LoFTR, DKM, RoMa, GIM, MASt3R, DUSt3R, XFeat) without architectural modifications, positioning the contribution as a new evaluation paradigm rather than a new matcher.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

OrthoLoC fills a clear gap as the first large-scale paired dataset enabling decoupled evaluation of localization and calibration using lightweight orthographic references, which is practically critical for resource-constrained UAV operations (no GNSS, no internet, no 3D models). The finding that domain shift causes 3×–7× degradation in translation error highlights a major open challenge: current matchers lack robustness to temporal appearance and structural changes between geodata acquisition and UAV flight. Calibration remains fundamentally limited by the geometric ambiguity between focal length and translation, and the 20% covisibility requirement constrains real-world deployment where upstream retrieval may yield incomplete reference crops. Future work on end-to-end learned localization and more robust homography estimators (particularly learned ones) could address these limitations.
