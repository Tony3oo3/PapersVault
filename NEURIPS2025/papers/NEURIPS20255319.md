## Problem

Existing 3D object detection methods require a priori metric camera poses and build explicit global 3D representations (point clouds or dense voxel grids) that overparameterize the desired output of oriented 3D boxes. The paper asks whether objects themselves, represented as 3D boxes, can serve as the fundamental geometric primitive for both camera localization and semantic mapping, eliminating the need for point-based representations and pre-existing poses.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255319/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes an unordered collection of RGB or RGB-D images (no temporal ordering or poses required), processing each at high resolution (e.g., 876×584) through Cubify Transformer. It produces metric camera poses, a global 3D object map of oriented boxes with per-object semantic class labels, and persistent 3D object tracks. The representation is inherently sparse, scaling with the number of objects in the scene rather than scene geometry, and no intermediate point cloud or voxel grid is ever constructed.

## Method

Rooms from Motion replaces the 2D keypoint pipeline of structure-from-motion with an object-centric pipeline: (1) CuTR independently detects metrically-scaled oriented 3D boxes with feature embeddings per image; (2) a LightGlue-style matching network (3 self/bi-directional attention blocks) establishes object-level correspondences between image pairs using box-derived positional encodings; (3) a second matching network operates on the 8 corners of each matched box to produce 3D point correspondences; (4) relative poses are estimated via Kabsch alignment treated as 4-DoF (yaw + translation) with circular regression, verified by exhaustive minimal-set sampling with IoU3D-based inlier testing; (5) global poses are obtained through rotation and translation averaging (glomap) with outlier filtering; (6) object tracks are formed via union-find over inlier matches, and a partial bundle adjustment differentially optimizes each track's box parameters (center, dimensions, yaw) through corner reprojection error using PyCeres.

## Results

On CA-1M with ground-truth depth and poses, Rooms from Motion (Un-posed) achieves AP15 of 39.6 and AR15 of 47.2, substantially outperforming FCAF3D (AP15 28.7, AR15 32.5). In the monocular RGB setting, RfM with partial bundle adjustment reaches AP15 of 31.3 and AR15 of 40.0 on CA-1M, a 60–70% improvement over ImGeoNet (AP15 18.6, AR15 23.1). For camera pose estimation on ScanNet++ RGB-D, RfM achieves a median rotation error of 1.1° and translation error of 3.5 cm, compared to 58.2° and 104.7 cm for DROID-SLAM with 500 frames and ground-truth depth.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against point-based 3D detectors, RfM (Un-posed) outperforms FCAF3D on CA-1M by +10.9 AP15 and +14.7 AR15, and on ScanNet++ by +4.8 AP15, though FCAF3D retains a recall advantage on ScanNet++ (AR15 55.7 vs. 42.9) due to its dense per-voxel box prediction. On ScanNet++, RfM (Posed) surpasses UniDet3D in precision (AP15 38.8 vs. 33.3) but trails in recall (AR15 48.6 vs. 48.1 is close; AR25 45.0 vs. 46.0). In the monocular RGB setting, RfM (Posed+BA) dominates both ImVoxelNet and ImGeoNet by large margins: on CA-1M, AP15 improves from 18.6 (ImGeoNet) to 31.3, and on ScanNet++ from 18.8 to 30.5. For localization, RfM (RGB) achieves 2.5° ARE and 12.7 cm ATE on CA-1M, dramatically outperforming CUT3R (10.7°, 35.9 cm) and Colmap (18.5°, 38.5 cm). On ScanNet++ RGB-D, RfM (1.1°, 3.5 cm) is an order of magnitude better than DROID-SLAM (58.2°, 104.7 cm), which struggles with the non-continuous DSLR trajectories. Mast3R-SLAM (25.8 cm ATE on ScanNet++ RGB) is also clearly outperformed by RfM (15.3 cm ATE). Overall, RfM wins on precision and localization across all settings, while point-based methods retain a recall edge in the GT-depth mapping setting on ScanNet++.

## Relation to Prior Work

Rooms from Motion restructures the classic structure-from-motion pipeline (detect → match → relative pose → global pose → tracks → optimization) by replacing 2D keypoints with metric 3D oriented boxes as the fundamental geometric primitive for both localization and mapping. It builds directly on Cubify Transformer (CuTR) for per-image 3D box detection, adapts LightGlue's attention-based matching to operate on object embeddings and box corners, and uses glomap-style rotation/translation averaging with Kabsch alignment for relative pose. Conceptually, it departs from point-based SLAM (DROID-SLAM, COLMAP) and from global 3D object detectors (FCAF3D, ImVoxelNet, ImGeoNet) that require a priori poses and explicit point clouds or dense voxel volumes, instead treating the object itself as the landmark and the map.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a sparse, object-centric representation (scaling with the number of objects rather than scene geometry) can simultaneously solve metric camera localization and produce a global semantic 3D object map from un-posed RGB(-D) images, outperforming both point-based SLAM and volume-based multi-view detection methods by large margins. This opens the previously infeasible setting of 3D object detection without any a priori pose or depth, and the partial bundle adjustment over object tracks offers a principled way to refine boxes beyond what single-view detection provides. Key limitations include restriction to indoor scenes, dependence on a strong per-image 3D box detector, and inability to register object-free images; open questions include extending to outdoor environments, online map updates, and whether the object-centric paradigm generalizes to scenes with very few or very many objects.
