## Problem

Aggregating 2D foundation model knowledge into 3D for part segmentation remains an open problem: label-aggregation methods ignore 3D geometric structure, while feature-aggregation methods rely on simple KNN propagation that cannot encode complex geometric patterns and discard SAM's high-quality grouping cues. This leads to under-segmentation of small parts and inconsistent labels at part boundaries.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250435/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D point cloud (rendered into M multi-view images with occlusion culling following PartSLIP) and a small set of labeled examples (8-shot) as input. Point features are extracted by pooling DINOv2 image features (upsampled from W/14×H/14 to full resolution, projected from 768 to 96 channels) at each point's visible views. SAM segmentation masks are decomposed into non-overlapping regions per view, and 3D points sharing a 2D mask form a segment. The output is a per-point part label produced by a 2-layer MLP segmentation head.

## Method

SegGraph constructs a segment graph where nodes are 3D SAM segments and edges encode two spatial relationships: overlap (mIoU > 10% across views, implying shared part labels) and adjacency (min inter-point distance < 0.01, delineating part boundaries). Segment features are computed via adaptive geometric encoding—each point's contribution is weighted by an MLP over its normalized position relative to the segment centroid and its surface normal, followed by max pooling and additive attention over point features. Features are then propagated through a 3-layer GATv2 with separate parameter sets for overlap and adjacency edges, enabling global geometric structure learning without point downsampling. Finally, a view-quality-aware unpooling module fuses segment features back to points, weighting each segment's contribution by the alignment between the point normal and the camera viewing direction (|n_j · (p_j − c_i)/‖p_j − c_i‖) refined by a learnable MLP, thereby attenuating unreliable segments from challenging viewpoints. The network is trained end-to-end with cross-entropy loss on few-shot examples.

## Results

On PartNet-E (45 categories, 8-shot), SegGraph with DINOv2+SAM achieves 72.8% mIoU overall, outperforming the best prior few-shot method PartField (66.3%) by 6.5% and PartDistill (65.9%) by 6.9%. On the 17 overlapping categories, SegGraph reaches 70.1% mIoU vs. 65.7% for PartField; on the 28 non-overlapping categories, it reaches 74.4% vs. 66.7%. On ShapeNetPart (16 categories, 8-shot), SegGraph achieves 62.6% mIoU, surpassing PointCLIPv2 (57.0%) by 5.6%. The method shows particularly strong gains on small parts, improving small-component mIoU by over 20% (e.g., 49.8% vs. 28.9% for PartSTAD on six representative categories).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SegGraph outperforms all 11 baselines on PartNet-E. Against fully supervised methods (PointNet++ 36.5%, SoftGroup 38.5%, PointNext 50.6%), SegGraph leads by 22–36% mIoU, confirming the advantage of foundation-model features even in the few-shot regime. Among few-shot label-aggregation methods, SegGraph beats PartSLIP (59.4%) by 13.4%, PartSLIP++ (62.1%) by 10.7%, PartSTAD (65.0%) by 7.8%, and 3-by-2 (64.2%) by 8.6%. Against distillation-based methods, SegGraph surpasses PartDistill (65.9%) by 6.9% and PartField (66.3%) by 6.5%, with the largest per-category gains on Door (+8.2% over PartField) and Lamp (+4.7%). On ShapeNetPart, SegGraph (62.6%) exceeds PointCLIPv2 (57.0%) by 5.6% and PointCLIP (52.1%) by 10.5%, despite lower-quality renderings due to sparse, colorless point clouds. The method also demonstrates extensibility: replacing DINOv2 with GLIP, CLIP, Diffusion, or PartField features still yields 4–10% improvements over the corresponding MLP-only baselines (Table 5).

## Relation to Prior Work

SegGraph sits at the intersection of three prior lines: (1) multi-view rendering pipelines from PartSLIP and 3-by-2, which project 3D shapes into 2D images for foundation model inference; (2) SAM-based segmentation, whose high-quality masks are repurposed here not as final labels but as structural primitives (graph nodes); and (3) graph-based 3D feature learning, where the key departure is replacing KNN or superpoint-based propagation with an explicit segment graph whose edges encode two distinct spatial relations—overlap (shared part identity) and adjacency (part boundaries). The paper's conceptual contribution is framing this segment graph as an atlas in the differential-geometry sense, where each SAM segment is a chart and edges are transitions, providing a principled structure for geometrically consistent feature propagation that neither label-voting nor distillation-based methods exploit.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 6.9% mIoU gain over PartDistill, with particularly large improvements on small components (+20%), addresses a persistent weakness in few-shot 3D part segmentation where boundary and small-part errors dominate. The method's demonstrated extensibility across five different foundation models (DINOv2, GLIP, CLIP, Diffusion, PartField) suggests the segment-graph propagation is a general-purpose 3D feature refinement module rather than a DINOv2-specific trick. However, the rendering-view paradigm fundamentally limits handling of occluded or internal structures, and the fixed segmentation scale precludes multi-scale or hierarchical part granularity—both open directions the authors acknowledge. For practitioners, the segment graph construction (overlap/adjacency thresholds) and view-quality-aware unpooling are concrete, adoptable components that could be plugged into other 2D-to-3D transfer pipelines.
