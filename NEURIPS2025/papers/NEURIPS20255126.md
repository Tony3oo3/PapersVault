## Problem

Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning methods like LoRA reduce the number of trainable parameters but still require a separate gradient-based optimization run for every downstream dataset, making large-scale deployment computationally prohibitive. While prior parameter-generation work (e.g., RPG) can synthesize network weights in a single pass, it relies on binary or simple embeddings for conditioning and fails to generalize to the rich semantic variation of linguistic task prompts. The paper asks whether a lightweight generator can learn the prompt-to-LoRA-weights mapping directly, eliminating per-task training entirely.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255126/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a batch of unlabeled task prompts (128 for common-sense, 16 for coding/math, 32 for multimodal) as its sole input—no answers or labels are required. A frozen Sentence-BERT encoder compresses these prompts into condition embeddings of shape [B, N, L, C], which the decoder expands into the full set of LoRA A and B matrices for every transformer layer of the target model. Generation completes in a single forward pass taking 0.11 s (0.5B) to 0.73 s (7B) on one A100, with peak memory of 9.6–20.5 GB, compared to hours of multi-GPU LoRA training.

## Method

DnD pairs a frozen text encoder with a cascaded hyper-convolutional decoder that maps prompt embeddings directly to tokenized LoRA weight matrices. Each decoder block contains five 2D convolution modules operating along three tensor axes—width (C,L), height (L,N), and layer-wise (N,L)—progressively transforming the embedding shape from [B,N,L,C] to the target weight shape [B,N_w,L_w,C_w]. Training is supervised with MSE loss between generated and ground-truth tokenized checkpoints, where checkpoints are collected by LoRA-fine-tuning on diverse datasets and saving at each iteration; prompt batches are randomly paired with checkpoints from the same dataset to establish the data-parameter correlation. At inference, prompts from a completely unseen dataset are encoded and passed through the decoder in one forward step, producing task-specific LoRA weights that can be plugged into the frozen base model without any gradient updates. This direct-generation design replaces the iterative denoising of diffusion-based parameter generators (RPG, p-diff) with a single-pass convolutional expansion, achieving up to 12,000× lower overhead than full fine-tuning while outperforming the training LoRAs by up to 30% on unseen benchmarks.

## Results

On common-sense reasoning (Qwen2.5-0.5B), DnD achieves an average 21.0-point accuracy improvement over training LoRAs across seven unseen datasets, with standout gains on PIQA (40.5→87.9) and BoolQ (13.5→44.9). On coding (HumanEval, Qwen2.5-1.5B), DnD improves pass@1 by 15.1, pass@5 by 26.7, and pass@10 by 30.9 over training LoRAs. On math (gsm8K/MATH), gains are +23.4 and +9.1 respectively, and on multimodal (Math-Vision/Math-Vista) DnD still leads by +1.3 and +0.8. Inference takes 0.11–0.73 seconds on a single A100, yielding up to 12,000× lower overhead than full LoRA fine-tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against **training LoRAs** (standard LoRA fine-tuning on each dataset), DnD wins on every benchmark: average +21.0 accuracy on common-sense reasoning, +15.1 to +30.9 pass@k on coding, +23.4 on gsm8K, and +9.1 on MATH, while also surpassing the foundation LLM's zero-shot performance (e.g., HumanEval 64.1 vs. 32.3 for Qwen2.5-1.5B). Against **RPG** (the prior state-of-the-art parameter generator), both methods perform comparably on close-set generation (reproducing seen checkpoints), but RPG fails entirely on open-set generation for novel datasets while DnD maintains strong zero-shot performance, validating the prompt-conditioning design. Against **foundation LLMs** (unmodified Qwen2.5), DnD-generated weights outperform the base model on all four task families (ARC-c 51.6 vs. 38.3, HumanEval 64.1 vs. 32.3, gsm8K 66.3 vs. 64.4, Math-Vision 24.3 vs. 22.7), demonstrating that the generated LoRA adapters add genuine task-specific capability beyond pre-training. DnD also beats full-shot LoRA tuning (300 iterations on ARC-c) in accuracy while being 2,500–12,000× faster, and outperforms few-shot tuning and in-context learning before 256 shots without requiring any answer labels.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the parameter-generation line of work (RPG, COND P-DIFF, SANE, ORAL) from vision classifiers conditioned on binary embeddings to LLM LoRA adapters conditioned on rich, unlabeled text prompts. It departs from diffusion-based generators (p-diff, RPG) by replacing the iterative denoising process with a single-pass cascaded hyper-convolutional decoder, motivated by the decoder-only efficiency paradigm in LLMs. The method also positions itself against the entire PEFT family (LoRA, DoRA, VeRA, DyLoRA), which all require a separate optimization run per dataset, by reframing LoRA adaptation as a direct prompt-to-weight mapping that eliminates gradient descent entirely.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The prompt-to-weights paradigm, if it scales, would collapse the dominant 'data → gradients → weights' loop into a single forward pass, making per-task LLM customization nearly free in wall-clock time. The paper demonstrates this across four task families and model sizes up to 7B, but critical open questions remain: whether the approach generalizes to 70B+ models, whether internet-scale pre-trained checkpoints can serve as training data, and whether the hyper-convolutional decoder can handle the vastly larger parameter spaces of production LLMs. The finding that zero-shot generated adapters can outperform fully-trained LoRAs on the target task challenges the working assumption that gradient descent on target data is necessary for optimal adaptation, though this result is currently limited to a single model family and relatively small backbones.
