## Problem

Imitation learning from videos (ILV) requires agents to extract policies from action-free expert videos without access to rewards or action labels. Existing ILV methods—whether inverse RL or supervised action prediction—either demand extensive environmental exploration or hit performance ceilings, failing to simultaneously achieve expert-level policy quality and high sample efficiency. This paper asks whether videos alone can serve as the sole supervisory signal for extremely sample-efficient visual policy learning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255251/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes action-free expert video frames (64×64×3 RGB, with 1-frame stacking for Procgen and 3-frame stacking for DMControl) alongside a small budget of reward-free environmental transitions (100k steps for Procgen/DMControl, 50k for Metaworld). It outputs a composite visual policy (feature encoder f, latent policy π, and latent action decoder d) that produces discrete 15-dimensional actions for Procgen or continuous actions for DMControl/Metaworld. Notably, the video dataset is substantially smaller than the interaction budget used by RL baselines (8M video steps vs. 4M–25M RL steps in Procgen; 200k video frames vs. 100k online steps in DMControl).

## Method

BCV-LR operates in two stages. Offline, it first pre-trains a self-supervised visual encoder via contrastive learning plus reconstruction (Procgen) or prototype-based temporal association with Sinkhorn-Knopp clustering (DMControl), then trains a latent action predictor and world model jointly under a dynamics-based reconstruction objective, where continuous latent actions are discretized via vector quantization to prevent trivial copying. Online, the latent action predictor is fine-tuned on collected reward-free transitions using a cross-entropy (or MSE) action prediction loss combined with a world-model reconstruction constraint that preserves expert dynamics, while a decoder aligns latent actions to the real action space. A latent policy is simultaneously trained via behavior cloning on the predicted latent actions; this policy collects higher-quality experience, which in turn improves latent action fine-tuning, creating an iterative improvement loop. The key architectural insight is that the same feature encoder, latent policy, and action decoder are shared across all stages, so the final deployable policy is simply the composition of these three components.

## Results

On 16 Procgen discrete control tasks (100k steps), BCV-LR achieves a mean score of 13.8 (79% of expert video performance), outperforming all ILV baselines (UPESV 9.0, BCO 4.8, ILPO 2.1, LAIFO 2.2) and RL baselines (LAPO 6.8, PPO 2.3). On 8 DMControl continuous control tasks (100k steps), BCV-LR reaches a mean score of 604 (78% of expert), surpassing TACO (310), DrQv2 (232), BCO (336), LAIFO (158), and UPESV (18). On 4 Metaworld manipulation tasks (50k steps), BCV-LR achieves 84% mean success rate versus 7% for BCO and 16% for DrQv2.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BCV-LR (using only expert videos) outperforms all four ILV baselines across every task: it leads UPESV (13.8 vs 9.0 mean on Procgen; 604 vs 18 on DMControl), BCO (13.8 vs 4.8; 604 vs 336), ILPO (13.8 vs 2.1), and LAIFO (13.8 vs 2.2; 604 vs 158). Against RL baselines that have access to ground-truth environmental rewards, BCV-LR still achieves higher mean scores: 13.8 vs 6.8 (LAPO) and 13.8 vs 2.3 (PPO) on Procgen, and 604 vs 310 (TACO) and 604 vs 232 (DrQv2) on DMControl, winning 6/8 DMControl tasks individually. The largest margins appear on tasks with complex dynamics (e.g., Starpilot: 54.8 vs 15.0 for UPESV; reacher_hard: 900 vs 154 for TACO). BCV-LR reaches expert-level performance (≥90% of video score) on tasks like Maze (9.9/10.0), Bigfish (35.9/36.3), and finger_spin (942/981), while the closest ILV competitor (UPESV) plateaus at 58% of expert on Procgen and 3% on DMControl.

## Relation to Prior Work

BCV-LR builds on the supervised imitation learning from observation line (BCO, ILPO) that recovers missed actions for behavior cloning, but departs from inverse RL approaches (LAIFO, GAIL) that the authors argue suffer from poor sample efficiency due to the dual burden of reward prediction and value estimation. It integrates self-supervised visual pre-training (CURL-style contrastive learning, DreamerPro-style temporal association) with vector-quantized latent action prediction via a world model, drawing on the latent action pretraining ideas from LAPO and LAPA. The key conceptual departure is framing the entire pipeline—feature extraction, action prediction, and policy cloning—as an unsupervised, dynamics-based process that requires neither expert actions nor environmental rewards, positioning it as the first method to make supervised ILV (behavior cloning) competitive with or superior to RL in sample efficiency.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work demonstrates that action-free videos alone can serve as a supervisory signal for extremely sample-efficient visual policy learning, outperforming both ILV baselines and reward-based RL methods on 24/28 tasks with only 100k interactions—a regime where prior methods fail. The practical implication is significant for real-world robotics and manipulation where collecting action labels or designing rewards is expensive or infeasible, while videos are readily available. However, the method inherits the covariate shift limitation of all behavior cloning approaches, which restricts its applicability to sequential decision-making tasks like complex locomotion; the authors identify combining BCV-LR with inverse RL as a natural future direction. Open questions include improving video data efficiency (performance degrades below ~20k transitions) and scaling to larger, more diverse video corpora from the internet.
