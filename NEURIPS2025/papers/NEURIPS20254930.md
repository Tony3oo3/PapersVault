## Problem

Existing zero-shot TTS systems support only utterance-level emotional expression, where a single reference governs the entire sentence, whereas humans naturally shift emotion and speaking rate within a single utterance. Achieving word-level control is further hampered by the extreme scarcity of publicly available datasets containing time-aligned intra-sentence emotion or speed transitions, making it unclear whether fine-grained expressive control is feasible without such data.

## Input / Output

The system takes a text string pre-segmented into 2–4 word-level spans, each annotated with a target emotion label and a speaking-rate ratio (0.5–2.0), along with one emotional speech prompt per segment (all from the same speaker) and a separate target-speaker reference clip. The 1st-stage teacher consumes these via multi-round continuation-style generation, while the 2nd-stage student processes them in a single autoregressive pass with emotion indicator tokens inserted between prompt segments. Output is a continuous waveform in which emotion and speaking rate vary across the sentence while speaker identity remains consistent.

## Method

WeSCon is a two-stage self-training framework built on the CosyVoice2 LM + flow-matching backbone. In Stage 1, a lightweight non-causal content aligner (trained on ASR data) predicts token-level text alignments, enabling a tail-to-head linkage that appends the previous round's final tokens to the next prompt for smooth cross-segment transitions; speaking rate is controlled per segment by nearest-neighbor interpolation or downsampling of the prompt speech tokens. In Stage 2, the Stage-1 teacher synthesizes pseudo-labels from GPT-4o-generated emotion-transition texts paired with ESD prompts, and the student (sharing the frozen backbone) is fine-tuned on the top-50% filtered samples. The key architectural addition is a Dynamic Emotional Attention Bias (DEAB): a two-layer causal Transformer predicts token-level emotion labels, which are projected through an MLP to produce a 7-dim weight vector that linearly combines seven predefined attention-bias templates, dynamically constraining each generation step to attend only to the emotion-consistent prompt region and preventing cross-emotion attention drift.

## Results

On the ESD-based word-level emotion and speaking rate control task, WeSCon (2nd-stage) achieves the best scores on all expressive metrics in both English and Chinese: DNSV of 4.361 (English) and 4.210 (Chinese), S-SIM of 0.532/0.599, AutoPCP of 2.707/2.663, Emo2v. of 0.882/0.872, and Aro. of 0.468/0.556. The most dramatic improvement is in transition smoothness (DNSV), dropping from 7.894/7.612 for CosyVoice2 to 4.361/4.210, a reduction of over 45%. Subjective evaluation confirms these gains, with WeSCon scoring 3.70 EMOS, 3.89 SPMOS, 3.96 SMOS, and 3.93 NMOS, outperforming all baselines by 0.1–0.7 points on each metric.

## Baselines & Comparisons

WeSCon (2nd-stage) outperforms all four baselines (Index-TTS, F5-TTS, Spark-TTS, and CosyVoice2) on every expressive metric—DNSV, S-SIM, AutoPCP, Emo2v., and Aro.—in both English and Chinese. The largest margin is on transition smoothness (DNSV), where WeSCon achieves 4.361/4.210 versus 7.894/7.612 for CosyVoice2 and 8.5–9.1 for the other baselines, reflecting the effectiveness of the transition-smoothing and self-training mechanisms. On speaker similarity, WeSCon leads by 0.01–0.15 over CosyVoice2 and by 0.08–0.22 over Index-TTS/Spark-TTS. The only metric where WeSCon does not lead is WER/CER: Index-TTS (2.611/1.834) and Spark-TTS (2.787/1.897) achieve lower error rates, though WeSCon's values (3.192/2.122) remain comparable to its backbone CosyVoice2 (3.185/2.119). On the out-of-domain CASIA dataset, WeSCon (2nd) maintains its lead on DNSV, S-SIM, and emotion metrics, with only a slight gap to the 1st-stage teacher on AutoPCP, confirming generalization to unseen speakers and emotions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on CosyVoice2's LM + flow-matching architecture for zero-shot TTS, extending it from utterance-level to word-level control of emotion and speaking rate. It departs from prior intra-utterance emotion control work (ELaTE, EmoCtrl-TTS) that required large-scale datasets with time-aligned emotion transitions, and from text-only phoneme-level emotion prediction methods (ED-TTS, EmoQ-TTS) that lack acoustic cues. The key conceptual departure is a two-stage self-training framework where a multi-round inference teacher (with transition smoothing and dynamic speed control via prompt token resampling) generates pseudo-labels to train a simplified student model equipped with a dynamic emotional attention bias, eliminating the need for any transition-annotated training data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in expressive TTS: the near-total absence of publicly available datasets with intra-sentence emotion or speed transitions. By demonstrating that word-level control can be acquired through self-training on untransitioned data, it opens a path to fine-grained expressive synthesis without expensive annotation pipelines. However, limitations remain: the model is restricted to five discrete emotions with no compositional or blended expressions, transitions are predefined by GPT-4o rather than contextually adaptive, and the approach is validated on a single backbone (CosyVoice2) with a small speaker pool (20 in ESD, 4 in CASIA). Open questions include whether the self-training paradigm generalizes to other TTS architectures, whether gradual/intermediate emotional states can be modeled, and whether the dynamic attention bias mechanism transfers to other multi-prompt conditioning scenarios.
