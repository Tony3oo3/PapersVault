## Problem

Corporate LLMs trained on sensitive organizational data are vulnerable to information leakage through memorization, and existing access-control mechanisms (RAG, separate models per permission zone) are either too slow, too expensive (exponential in the number of permission zones), or fail to enforce strict isolation. The paper addresses how to serve a single base model with strict, per-user access control over multiple sensitive knowledge domains without requiring exponential model maintenance or additional training for routing.

## Input / Output

The system accepts a user query (text, or text+image for multi-modal variants) paired with a User-ID credential. It produces a text response (or a generated image for text-to-image) synthesized from the intersection of top-k retrieved LoRAs and the user's permitted set, weighted by normalized cosine similarity scores. Additionally, it can emit an 'access hint' listing metadata of relevant-but-unpermitted LoRAs, guiding users to request the correct permissions. The retrieval operates over 100-token document chunks embedded with ALL-MPNET-BASE-V2, and the mixing occurs at every MLP layer of the base model.

## Method

AC-LoRA fine-tunes one isolated LoRA per permission zone on non-overlapping datasets, then maintains two vector databases: LORA-DOC EMBED (mapping each LoRA to its fine-tuned document embeddings) and LORA-PERMISSION (an n-dimensional binary vector per user indicating access to each LoRA). At inference, the query is embedded and a top-k cosine-similarity search yields candidate LoRAs O; the user's permission vector yields permitted LoRAs P; the active set is L = O ∩ P. The outputs of LoRAs in L are combined at every MLP layer via a training-free weighted sum Y = W + Σ S_i · y_i, where S_i are the normalized similarity scores, requiring no learned gate or router. This design supports O(1) dataset removal, O(1) permission updates, and scales to an exponential number of permission combinations without retraining, while the hint mechanism (H = O − L) optionally informs users about information they lack access to.

## Results

On FlanV2, AC-LoRA (k=3, fetch_k=10) matches or exceeds the best baseline in 8 out of 10 task domains, achieving e.g. 65.0 on Commonsense (vs. 62.5 Perfect Selection), 13.6 BLEU on Translation (vs. 13.1 Perfect Selection), and 90.0 on Sentiment (tied with Perfect Selection). On RepLiQA, the retriever achieves >90% accuracy in retrieving the correct LoRA (never exceeding 3 retrieved LoRAs), and AC-LoRA's mixed-LoRA inference grades closely match or occasionally exceed the single-LoRA perfect-selection baseline. On the CS-COMBI knowledge-combination dataset, 7.71% of test queries show improvement over both individual LoRAs when their outputs are merged. AC-LoRA's time-to-first-token latency is lower than an equivalent RAG setup (260–10K context) due to the shorter effective context.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LoRARetriever [44] (Selection, Fusion, Mixture baselines), AC-LoRA matches or outperforms in 8 of 10 FlanV2 domains without any trained routing gate; for example, it achieves 65.0 on Commonsense vs. 55.5 (Selection IID+) and 61.5 (Mixture IID+), and 13.6 BLEU on Translation vs. 12.8 (Selection IID+). Against MoLE [18] (MoE Top1/Top3/Soft columns), AC-LoRA consistently wins: 90.0 vs. 70.0–75.0 on Sentiment, 65.0 vs. 52.0–54.5 on Commonsense, and 13.6 vs. 9.5–10.7 on Translation, while requiring no trained gate parameters. Against SMEAR [43] (SME-AR column in Table C.1), AC-LoRA outperforms on the majority of fine-grained tasks (e.g., WebNLG Rouge-1: 69.8 vs. 51.1; StoryCloze: 86.0 vs. 62.0; SST-2: 98.0 vs. 74.0). AC-LoRA trails Perfect Selection on a few tasks (e.g., Reading Comp. 55.3 vs. 67.3; Closed-book QA 39.0 vs. 45.0), which is expected since Perfect Selection assumes oracle LoRA access, but it remains the best training-free retrieval-based method overall.

## Relation to Prior Work

AC-LoRA carves new ground at the intersection of LoRA-based parameter-efficient fine-tuning and access control, repurposing embedding-based retrieval (analogous to RAG) to select LoRA adapters rather than documents, and replacing trained MoE-style gates (MoLE, MoELoRA) with a training-free similarity-weighted mixing scheme. It departs from access-controlled RAG by encoding knowledge in adapter weights rather than external context, and from separate-adapter-per-permission-zone approaches by avoiding the O(2^n) combinatorial explosion through dynamic retrieval and merging. The permission-vector database and hinting mechanism are novel system-level additions not present in prior LoRA mixing or retrieval literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As corporate LLMs proliferate, the demonstrated verbatim memorization leakage (tens of thousands of words) from even small LoRA fine-tunes underscores that post-hoc censorship is insufficient, making structural access control by construction a practical necessity. The training-free mixing and O(1) update operations make the system deployable in real organizations without prohibitive retraining costs. Key open questions remain: the hinting mechanism introduces a membership-inference-like attack vector, cross-LoRA knowledge combination succeeds on only ~8% of queries (limiting its utility for multi-hop reasoning), and the multi-modal evaluation is explicitly proof-of-concept. Scaling to very high-privilege users requiring many simultaneous LoRAs also remains an unresolved GPU-memory bottleneck.
