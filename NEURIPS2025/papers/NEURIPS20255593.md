## Problem

Existing mechanistic interpretability methods treat attention heads and MLP layers as indivisible atomic units, probing or ablating entire components to assess their functional contribution. This monolithic view overlooks the possibility that a single head or MLP multiplexes multiple independent subfunctions along distinct internal directions, obscuring the fine-grained structure of computation within these modules.

## Input / Output

The method takes frozen pretrained GPT-2 Small weights and constructs augmented matrices (weights with biases folded in via a constant input dimension) for every attention head's QK and OV projections and every MLP's input/output projections. It produces learned diagonal masks in [0,1] over each component's singular directions, identifying which low-rank axes are task-relevant; it also yields fixed 'logit receptors' (right-singular vectors projected through the unembedding) that define stable token-preference axes in logit space, and causal intervention logit differences from scalar ablations of those directions. A notable design choice is the asymmetric treatment of QK matrices, where only the masked (not complementary) subspace is retained to avoid creating multiple incompatible attention kernels under a single softmax.

## Method

The core contribution is a unified linear formulation that expresses all transformer subcomponents (QK, OV, MLP-in, MLP-out) as augmented matrices amenable to a single SVD, yielding orthogonal singular directions as a natural coordinate system for direction-level interpretability. Learnable diagonal masks scale each singular direction and are optimized end-to-end via a joint objective combining KL divergence (faithfulness to the original model's predictions) and L1 regularization (sparsity), with the model's parameters frozen and only the masks updated. To improve robustness, the optimization feeds both clean and corrupted activations jointly, so masks must capture directions that are task-relevant under perturbation. The resulting masks are extremely sparse (~91–97% of directions pruned) yet faithfully reproduce model behavior, and they align with previously identified circuit components (e.g., Name Mover heads in IOI). The paper further shows that right-singular vectors of OV projections, when projected through the unembedding, form fixed 'logit receptors' whose scalar activations can be causally intervened upon to flip predictions, providing mechanistic evidence that computation is concentrated along a small set of interpretable, low-rank subfunctions within each component.

## Results

Across all three tasks, the learned directional masks achieve high-fidelity reconstruction with extreme sparsity: IOI retains only ~9% of singular directions (91.32% relative sparsity) while achieving KLD of 0.21±0.02 and 77% exact match; GP prunes 96.81% of directions with KLD of 0.13±0.01 and 86% exact match; GT prunes 95.21% with KLD of 0.23±0.03. Pruned-model accuracy closely matches full-model accuracy (e.g., IOI: 0.70 vs 0.79; GP: 0.75 vs 0.77), confirming that a small subset of singular directions suffices to reproduce task behavior. Causal interventions on identified OV singular directions in the Gender Pronoun task achieve 100% prediction reversal at moderate amplification scales (σ_scale ≥ 2), with logit differences shifting from +2.5 to −42 (masculine prompts) and +2.8 to −41 (feminine prompts).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not benchmark against named external methods in a head-to-head numerical comparison; instead, it validates its directional masking approach against two internal selection baselines: top-k magnitude SVD (retaining directions with the largest singular values) and random SVD (retaining a random subset of directions). The learned masks achieve substantially lower KL divergence than both baselines, indicating that task-relevant computation is concentrated along specific, semantically meaningful directions rather than the largest singular modes. The method is also validated against prior circuit-level analyses (Wang et al. 2022, ACDC/Conmy et al. 2023), showing that heads previously identified as circuit components (e.g., Name Mover, Backup Name Mover, S-Inhibition) exhibit consistently higher average mask values than non-circuit heads, confirming alignment with established mechanistic findings. The full-model (unpruned) baseline serves as the upper bound on accuracy, and the pruned model retains within 1–9% accuracy of the full model across tasks, demonstrating that the sparse directional decomposition captures nearly all task-relevant computation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the low-rank singular-direction perspective introduced by Merullo et al. (2024), which characterized inter-head communication channels, to the intra-component level by applying SVD to augmented weight matrices of both attention (QK, OV) and MLP layers. It builds on the transformer circuit formulation of Elhage et al. (2021) and departs from the dominant paradigm in mechanistic interpretability (Wang et al. 2022, Conmy et al. 2023, Bhaskar et al. 2024) that treats attention heads and MLPs as atomic, indivisible units of computation. The key conceptual shift is from component-level circuit discovery to direction-level attribution, where a single head or MLP is decomposed into orthogonal singular subfunctions that can be independently masked and interpreted.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that 91–97% of singular directions can be pruned while preserving model behavior (KL divergence 0.13–0.23) challenges the working assumption that transformer components are monolithic and that their full rank is functionally necessary. The introduction of 'logit receptors'—fixed, input-independent directions in logit space that can be causally steered via scalar interventions—provides a concrete mechanistic bridge between internal representations and token-level predictions. However, the analysis is confined to GPT-2 Small and three synthetic tasks, uses axis-aligned diagonal masks that may limit expressiveness, and lacks formal justification for equating singular directions with causally meaningful subroutines. Open questions include whether this directional structure persists in larger or instruction-tuned models, how cross-component interactions shape emergent behavior, and whether the approach generalizes to open-ended reasoning tasks.
