## Problem

Flow matching models, now dominant in image generation, rely on deterministic ODE sampling that is fundamentally incompatible with online RL, which requires stochastic exploration to learn from rewards. While RL has been applied to diffusion models and offline preference optimization has been adapted to flow models, the potential of online policy gradient RL (e.g., GRPO) for flow matching remains unexplored, and the high denoising step count of flow models makes RL data collection prohibitively expensive.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251362/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt as input and produces a group of G=24 generated images at 512×512 resolution via a flow matching backbone (SD3.5-M or FLUX.1-Dev). During training, each image is sampled using only 10 SDE denoising steps (versus the standard 40 ODE steps at inference), and rewards are computed on the final denoised image. The trained output is a LoRA-adapted flow model (α=64, r=32) that retains the full 40-step ODE schedule at test time, so the deployed model's inference cost is unchanged.

## Method

Flow-GRPO integrates GRPO into flow matching via two key mechanisms. First, an ODE-to-SDE conversion rewrites the deterministic flow ODE as a reverse-time SDE whose marginal distribution matches the original at every timestep; for rectified flow, the score term is expressed analytically in terms of the learned velocity field, yielding a closed-form isotropic Gaussian policy and a tractable KL divergence. Second, a Denoising Reduction strategy uses only T=10 SDE steps during training data collection (vs. T=40 at inference), achieving a >4× speedup without degrading final reward. The GRPO objective uses group-relative advantage normalization over G samples per prompt with a clipped importance ratio, and a KL penalty against the reference policy prevents reward hacking (quality or diversity collapse) while still reaching the same task-specific reward as the KL-free variant.

## Results

On GenEval, Flow-GRPO improves SD3.5-M from 63% to 95% overall accuracy, surpassing GPT-4o (84%) and all prior flow matching models (SANA-1.5: 81%, SD3.5-L: 71%, FLUX.1 Dev: 66%). For visual text rendering, OCR accuracy rises from 59% to 92%, and for human preference alignment, PickScore increases from 21.72 to 23.31. Critically, with KL regularization, image quality metrics on DrawBench (Aesthetic, DeQA, ImageReward, UnifiedReward) remain essentially unchanged, confirming minimal reward hacking.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On GenEval, Flow-GRPO (95%) decisively outperforms all diffusion-based baselines (DALLE-3: 67%, SD-XL: 55%), autoregressive models (Janus-Pro-7B: 80%, GPT-4o: 84%), and other flow matching models (SANA-1.5: 81%, SD3.5-L: 71%, FLUX.1 Dev: 66%). Among alignment methods, Flow-GRPO surpasses online DPO, SFT, Flow-RWR, DDPO, ReFL, and ORW on both GenEval and PickScore tasks; notably, DDPO's reward eventually collapses while Flow-GRPO trains stably, and ORW degrades from 29.17 to 23.05 PickScore by step 960 while Flow-GRPO continues improving to 29.89. On T2I-CompBench++, Flow-GRPO achieves the best 2D-Spatial (0.5447 vs. FLUX.1 Dev's 0.2863) and 3D-Spatial (0.4471 vs. 0.3866) scores, confirming generalization beyond GenEval-style training. The only setting where Flow-GRPO does not lead is on the Colors sub-task of T2I-CompBench++ (0.8379 vs. SD3.5-M's 0.7994 baseline already being strong), though it still improves over the base model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts GRPO (group relative policy optimization from DeepSeekMath) to flow matching models, a setting where prior RL work was limited to diffusion-based backbones (DDPO) or offline preference methods (Flow-DPO, RWR). The key technical bridge is an ODE-to-SDE conversion derived from the Fokker-Planck equation and the forward/reverse SDE relationship established in score-based generative modeling, which injects the stochasticity that GRPO's exploration requires while preserving the original marginal distribution. The MDP formulation of iterative denoising follows Black et al.'s DDPO, and the SDE/ODE unification framework draws on Albergo et al. and Domingo-Enrich et al., but no prior work had combined online policy gradient RL with rectified flow models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Flow matching is the backbone of current state-of-the-art T2I and video generation systems (SD3.5, FLUX, Kling, Wan), so a practical online RL recipe for these models has broad applicability. The denoising reduction finding (10 training steps vs. 40 inference steps) is a practical efficiency win that makes online RL feasible for large flow models, and the KL-regularization analysis clarifies that it is not merely early stopping but a genuine diversity-preserving constraint. Open questions include extending to video generation (multi-reward balancing, temporal consistency, scalability), designing better reward models beyond rule-based or single-signal approaches, and whether the ODE-to-SDE noise schedule can be further optimized for different model scales.
