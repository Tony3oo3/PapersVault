## Problem

Spiking neural networks offer biological plausibility and energy efficiency but suffer from pixel-wise information loss due to discrete binary spiking and suboptimal membrane potential dynamics, limiting their representation capacity for pixel-level regression tasks. These challenges are compounded in remote sensing image super-resolution, where large-scale scenes demand both global context modeling and fine-grained texture recovery, yet SNNs remain largely unexplored for this task.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252187/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a low-resolution RGB remote sensing image (generated by bicubic downsampling) and replicates it along a temporal dimension (T=4 time steps) before processing. The output is a super-resolved high-resolution image produced by a fusion block that converts discrete spike sequences into continuous values, followed by pixel-shuffle and a 3×3 convolution. All experiments target ×4 super-resolution on 64×64 patches, with evaluation on 160×160 LR inputs for FLOPs measurement.

## Method

SpikeSR stacks Spiking Attention Groups (SAGs), each containing Spiking Attention Blocks (SABs) that integrate three key components: (1) Spiking Convolution Blocks (SCB) that interleave CNN layers with LIF neurons to mitigate information loss from binary spiking; (2) Hybrid Dimension Attention (HDA) that performs temporal-channel joint attention to recalibrate membrane potentials and spike responses across both dimensions simultaneously; and (3) Deformable Similarity Attention (DSA) that builds a multi-scale feature pyramid, computes patch-level self-similarity via Gumbel-Softmax argmax, aligns matched patches with deformable convolution, and fuses them through cross-attention to capture global self-similar priors. A Fusion Block with temporal and spatial attention adaptively aggregates the discrete spike sequences into continuous features before reconstruction. The full model uses 4 SAGs with 2 SABs each and T=4 time steps, trained end-to-end with supervised learning on the AID dataset.

## Results

SpikeSR (full, 1042K params, 33.05G FLOPs) achieves average PSNR of 31.95 dB and SSIM of 0.8339 across AID, DOTA, and DIOR, outperforming all compared ANN-based efficient SR methods. The lightweight SpikeSR-S variant (472K params, 15.21G FLOPs) reaches 31.88 dB average PSNR, surpassing SwinIR-light (897K, 23.56G) at 31.87 dB while using roughly half the parameters and FLOPs. On individual datasets, SpikeSR leads by 0.08 dB (AID), 0.04 dB (DOTA), and 0.10 dB (DIOR) over SwinIR-light in PSNR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SpikeSR (full) achieves the highest average PSNR (31.95 dB) and SSIM (0.8339) across all three remote sensing benchmarks, beating the strongest transformer-based baselines HiT-SR (31.90 dB) and Omni-SR (31.91 dB) by 0.05 and 0.04 dB respectively, while using fewer parameters (1042K vs. 792K and 2803K). The lightweight SpikeSR-S (472K params, 15.21G FLOPs) outperforms SwinIR-light (897K, 23.56G) by 0.01 dB in average PSNR with roughly half the model size and FLOPs, and surpasses SPIN (555K, 18.91G) by 0.06 dB. Against CNN-based efficient methods, SpikeSR-S leads CARN (31.65 dB) by 0.23 dB, IMDN (31.71 dB) by 0.17 dB, and RLFN (31.70 dB) by 0.18 dB in average PSNR. On the per-scene breakdown (Table 2, AID 30 categories), SpikeSR achieves the best average PSNR of 30.91 dB, exceeding HiT-SR (30.87 dB) and SwinIR-L (30.83 dB), with particularly notable gains on complex scenes like Church (+0.06 dB over HiT-SR) and D-Residential (+0.00 dB tie but higher SSIM). The ablation on AID-tiny confirms that both HDA (+0.12 dB) and DSA (+0.21 dB) contribute meaningfully, with their combination yielding an additional 0.08 dB over either alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two research threads: (1) spiking neural networks for vision tasks, where prior work has focused on classification, detection, and optical flow but has largely neglected pixel-level regression tasks like super-resolution, and (2) efficient SR architectures (CARN, IMDN, SwinIR, SPIN, HiT-SR) that reduce computational cost through architectural design. The method directly builds on TCJA-SNN's temporal-channel joint attention [72] and the broader attention-SNN literature [65, 61], adapting these mechanisms to regulate membrane potentials in a SR context. It also repurposes non-local self-similarity priors [47] and deformable convolution [4] into a patch-level deformable similarity attention module, departing from exhaustive non-local operations by operating at the patch level with Gumbel-Softmax for differentiable matching.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first SNN-based framework for remote sensing image SR, opening a path toward energy-efficient inference for large-scale Earth observation where computational budget is a genuine constraint. However, the practical efficiency gain is not fully demonstrated: the paper compares FLOPs but does not report actual energy consumption on neuromorphic hardware, and the T=4 time-step requirement partially offsets the per-step sparsity advantage. The authors acknowledge that representational capacity remains limited and that attention mechanisms introduce additional overhead, leaving open whether SNNs can truly match or exceed ANN-based SR quality at equivalent or lower energy cost. A key open question is whether the observed 'active learning state' of LIF neurons in degraded images translates to a fundamental representational advantage for pixel-level tasks, or is merely an artifact of threshold dynamics.
