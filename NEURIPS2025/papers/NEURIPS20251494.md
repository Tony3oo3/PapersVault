## Problem

Existing backdoor defenses for LLMs rely on prior knowledge of trigger patterns or target labels, and most are designed for classification rather than generative tasks. More fundamentally, they fail to address the model's inherent reliance on superficial, non-robust activation patterns rather than generalizable semantic features, leaving models vulnerable to adaptive and stealthy attacks.

## Input / Output

The method consumes text instructions (potentially containing hidden triggers) and operates on the LLM's intermediate hidden representations (H ∈ R^{B×L×D}) rather than raw inputs. It produces a per-sample, per-token adaptive mask M_i ∈ [0,1]^{L×D} that soft-partitions each hidden state into backdoor-targeted and benign subspaces, which are then fed back into the model to generate a safe text response. The mask is learned jointly with the model during fine-tuning on potentially poisoned data.

## Method

RepGuard introduces a dual-perspective feature localization strategy that computes local consistency (variance of each token's hidden vector across dimensions) and sample deviation (L2 distance from the batch-mean activation) to heuristically flag suspicious backdoor patterns without knowing the trigger. A learnable weighted fusion of these two metrics produces a per-element score, passed through a sigmoid to yield a soft mask that adaptively separates backdoor features from benign semantic features. Three optimization objectives refine the mask: an orthogonality constraint enforcing mutual exclusivity between the two subspaces, a sparsity regularizer preventing the mask from over-capturing benign features, and a task-driven utility loss that maximizes generation quality from the benign subspace while penalizing the backdoor subspace. This representation-level intervention is trigger-agnostic and operates during fine-tuning, requiring no reference model or knowledge of the attack target.

## Results

On the Target Refusal task, RepGuard reduces the average attack success rate on backdoored inputs (ASR_w/t) to 10.58–12.36% across four LLMs, compared to 58–93% without defense and 33–48% with DeCE, while keeping clean-input ASR_w/o below 2%. On the Jailbreak task, RepGuard achieves an average ASR_w/t of 21.18–27.81%, a ~69% reduction over undefended models (~93–95%), and consistently outperforms both DeCE (~45–53%) and INT4 quantization (~77–92%). The paper reports an overall ~80% average ASR reduction on poisoned data for Target Refusal while maintaining near-original task performance on clean data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RepGuard substantially outperforms both baselines on both tasks. Against DeCE (deceptive cross-entropy loss, Yang et al. 2025), RepGuard reduces ASR_w/t by roughly 20–37 percentage points on Target Refusal (e.g., 10.58% vs. 48.02% on Mistral-7B) and by 24–25 points on Jailbreak (e.g., 21.18% vs. 45.26% on Llama-2-13B). Against INT4 quantization (Khalid et al. 2019), the gap is even wider: RepGuard achieves 10–12% ASR_w/t on Target Refusal versus 51–91% for quantization, and 21–28% on Jailbreak versus 77–92%. Notably, quantization often *increases* clean-input ASR_w/o (e.g., 18.01% vs. 14.17% on DeepSeek-R1), indicating it degrades normal model behavior, whereas RepGuard keeps clean-input ASR below 2% on Target Refusal and within ~3% of the undefended baseline on Jailbreak. DeCE provides moderate protection but is constrained by its static loss adjustment, particularly under clean-label settings where it cannot distinguish trigger-driven activations from benign invariance.

## Relation to Prior Work

RepGuard carves new ground at the intersection of representation learning and backdoor defense by repurposing activation-variance analysis (previously used for interpretability in [27–29]) as a mechanism for trigger-agnostic feature localization in LLMs. It departs from the dominant paradigm of optimization-based defenses (trigger inversion, adversarial training) and external mitigations (trigger removal, data augmentation) that require prior knowledge of triggers or targets, instead operating entirely in the hidden-representation space. The dual-perspective scoring (local consistency + sample deviation) and the adaptive soft-mask with multi-objective optimization (orthogonality, sparsity, utility) are novel formulations, but each component draws on established ideas from feature disentanglement and representation engineering. The paper explicitly positions itself as the first to address backdoor defense for LLM *generative* tasks, where prior work has focused on vision or text classification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Backdoor attacks on LLMs in generative tasks (jailbreak, target refusal) represent a growing and practically urgent threat as models are fine-tuned on unverified data, yet prior defenses were designed for classification and rely on trigger knowledge. RepGuard's trigger-agnostic, representation-level intervention offers a deployable defense that does not require the defender to know the attack pattern, which is critical for real-world supply-chain security. Key open questions remain: the method is validated only on clean-label attacks and four mid-scale models (7B–13B), its Jailbreak defense is notably weaker (~69% vs. ~80% ASR reduction), and it is untested against label-modifying attacks, multi-backdoor scenarios, or larger frontier models. Whether the adaptive mask generalizes to multi-layer or cross-attention interventions, and whether the approach composes with other safety mechanisms (RLHF, constitutional AI), are important directions for follow-up work.
