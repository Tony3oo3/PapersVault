## Problem

The LLM community has been steadily expanding pretraining context windows (from 512 to 8K+ tokens) on the assumption that longer contexts improve model quality, yet no prior work has conducted a fair, controlled comparison of context window sizes under a fixed token budget. This paper reveals that shorter-context models consistently outperform longer-context counterparts on standard benchmarks, exposing a fundamental trade-off between long-context capability and pretraining effectiveness that the field has overlooked.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard packed text sequences of fixed length L (documents concatenated with EOS markers) and a scalar training step t that determines the current effective context window. It produces a decoder-only transformer pretrained from scratch that natively supports the full target context window (up to 32K tokens) while having been trained predominantly on shorter effective contexts. The key constraint is that the attention mask is dynamically reshaped per training step via block-wise causal masking, so the same packed sequence is processed with progressively larger receptive fields over the course of training without any change to the data pipeline.

## Method

SkyLadder schedules the effective context window from short to long during pretraining by applying a local block-wise causal mask M_w to each packed sequence, where a token at position i can attend only to tokens j in the range [floor(i/w)·w, i]. The window size follows a linear schedule w(t) = min(w_e, w_s + floor(αt)), starting from a small w_s (e.g., 8 tokens) and expanding at rate α per step until reaching the target L. This mask is orthogonal to the packing strategy and can be seamlessly combined with IntraDoc masking to preserve document boundaries. The paper shows that this scheduling yields up to 3.7% gains on standard benchmarks and up to 22% faster training (via reduced attention computation in early steps), while the mechanism behind the gains is a more concentrated attention pattern with lower entropy, delayed attention-sink emergence, and more stable optimization dynamics (lower loss volatility and gradient norms) compared to constant long-context pretraining.

## Results

On 1B-parameter models pretrained on 100B tokens, SkyLadder improves average accuracy across nine standard benchmarks from 46.3% (Random) to 50.0% (+3.7%) and from 47.4% (IntraDoc) to 49.3% (+1.9%). On reading comprehension tasks, the Random baseline improves from 25.5% to 30.2% (+4.7%), while long-context performance (MDQA + RULER) is maintained or slightly improved. At 3B scale, SkyLadder yields +3.5% on both standard and long-context benchmarks, and at 32K context the gain reaches +3.6% standard / +3.8% long. Training is 13% faster (8K) and 22% faster (32K) compared to constant long-context baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the IntraDoc baseline (Zhao et al., ACL 2024), SkyLadder consistently improves standard benchmark accuracy by +1.9% (47.4% → 49.3%) and reading comprehension by +0.4% (28.7% → 29.1%), while matching or slightly exceeding long-context scores (13.0% → 13.2% on MDQA+RULER). Against the constant-long-context Random baseline, SkyLadder achieves a larger +3.7% gain on standard tasks and +4.7% on reading comprehension, though RULER scores drop slightly (12.8% → 10.3%), which the authors attribute to the synthetic benchmark's small size and high variance. Compared to Dataset Decomposition (Pouransari et al., NeurIPS 2024) on FineWeb-Pro, SkyLadder outperforms both DD with 1 cycle (53.9% standard / 12.3% long) and DD with 8 cycles (54.5% / 13.5%), achieving 54.8% / 13.9% respectively, while avoiding the domain bias and loss spikes that cyclic data reordering introduces.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on progressive context window expansion during pretraining, building on Li et al. [28] (sequence length warmup for training stability) and Jin et al. [21] (GrowLength for accelerating 400M-model pretraining). While those earlier efforts demonstrated stability or speed benefits at small scale, SkyLadder is the first to show that short-to-long context scheduling yields consistent downstream performance gains at 1B–3B parameter scale. The method also reinterprets the success of IntraDoc masking [64, 13] through the lens of effective context window distribution, and contrasts with Dataset Decomposition [38] by avoiding domain bias from length-based data reordering.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that shorter context windows consistently outperform longer ones under a fixed token budget directly challenges the prevailing assumption in the field that expanding context during pretraining is always beneficial, and provides a simple, drop-in scheduling recipe (start at 8 tokens, linearly expand over ~60% of training) that delivers up to 22% compute savings alongside better benchmark scores. Key limitations include the absence of experiments beyond 3B parameters and 32K context, and the lack of a theoretical account for why shorter contexts help. Open questions include whether optimal schedules depend on model size or data distribution, and how the approach interacts with other recent pretraining innovations such as hybrid attention or multi-stage training.
