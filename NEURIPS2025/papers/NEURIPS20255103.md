## Problem

Existing visual foundation models (DINO, CLIP, MAE) learn semantic class-level features but fail to capture the concept of a semantic instance—a distinct, coherent object entity—which is fundamental for physical-world understanding. Drawing on developmental psychology findings that infants acquire object unity through coherent motion cues, this paper asks whether motion boundaries can serve as a scalable, label-free supervisory signal for learning instance-level visual representations.

## Input / Output

The network consumes a single 224×224 RGB image and produces a dense, L2-normalized feature map at 1/4 spatial resolution (56×56) with 64 channels, upsampled 2× via bilinear interpolation. Pseudo-labels are generated offline from ~2,700 hours of driving video (OpenDV-YouTube + nuPlan) using VideoFlow optical flow and BFS clustering (θ_f=1.5, θ_s=100), yielding 48 million images with at least one foreground instance mask. The method requires no camera intrinsics or calibration parameters.

## Method

The core insight is a geometric argument that under rigid ego-motion, optical flow discontinuities (motion boundaries) coincide with depth discontinuities (object boundaries), since flow is a function of depth, camera pose, and intrinsics. The pipeline extracts optical flow via VideoFlow, clusters pixels with similar flow vectors using BFS to form pseudo-instance masks, and trains a Swin/ResNet backbone + FPN encoder (following Panoptic FPN architecture) with a pairwise contrastive loss: same-instance pixels are pulled together, different-instance pixels are pushed apart with margin m=1.0, and background-background pairs contribute zero loss. Training uses 200 sampled pixel pairs per image over 200 epochs with batch size 2048, and the resulting features are complementary to DINO-style class-level features, yielding further gains when concatenated.

## Results

On nuScenes 3D object detection (BEVFormer V2), the proposed method achieves 55.80% NDS and 47.29% mAP with Swin-L, outperforming SimMIM (54.98% NDS) and ImageNet-22K supervised pretraining (54.59% NDS). On KITTI monocular depth estimation (DCDepth), Swin-L achieves 6.558 SILog and 1.929 RMSE, surpassing SimMIM (6.542 SILog, 1.941 RMSE) and DINOv2 ViT-S (7.119 SILog, 2.153 RMSE). On nuScenes 3D occupancy (SparseOcc), Swin-L reaches 38.7 RayIoU, beating SimMIM (38.6) and ImageNet-22K (37.6), though DINOv2 ViT-L (39.0) remains slightly ahead. The method also generalizes to unseen indoor scenes (Ego4D, RT-X) without retraining.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method consistently outperforms all self-supervised baselines (SimMIM, MoCo v3, DINOv2) and supervised pretraining (ImageNet-22K, COCO) on 3D object detection across all backbone sizes, with the largest margin over ImageNet-22K (+1.21 NDS at Swin-L) and SimMIM (+0.82 NDS at Swin-L). On monocular depth estimation, the method beats Semantic-SAM (weakly supervised) by 0.16 SILog at Swin-T and DINOv2 ViT-S by 0.56 SILog, though SimMIM Swin-L edges it out by 0.016 SILog. On 3D occupancy, the method surpasses SimMIM and ImageNet-22K at every scale but trails DINOv2 ViT-L by 0.3 RayIoU, suggesting DINO's semantic features remain slightly stronger for this particular task. On the KITTI online leaderboard, the method (DCDepth + Ours, Swin-L) achieves 9.54 SILog, outperforming New CRFs (10.39), VA-DepthNet (9.63), IEBins (9.84), and NDDepth (9.62). The paper also shows that concatenating the proposed features with DINOv2 features yields further gains (6.796 SILog vs. 6.991 alone), confirming the two representations capture complementary information—instance-level structure vs. semantic category.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself against the dominant self-supervised pretraining paradigms (DINO/DINOv2, MAE/SimMIM, CLIP), arguing that they capture class-level semantics but miss the instance-level abstraction fundamental to physical-world understanding. It draws on developmental psychology (Kellman & Spelke) for the conceptual claim that motion coherence is the primary cue infants use to bootstrap object unity, and on Marr's geometric analysis of flow discontinuities for the formal justification that motion boundaries proxy object boundaries under rigid motion. Architecturally, it follows the Panoptic FPN design [28] for multi-scale feature merging, but the key departure is the supervision signal: instead of self-distillation, reconstruction, or text alignment, it uses pseudo-instance masks derived from off-the-shelf optical flow (VideoFlow) and BFS clustering, trained with a pixel-level contrastive loss. Unlike prior motion-based object discovery methods that require camera calibration or complex 3D point tracking, this approach is calibration-free and scales to ~2,700 hours of unstructured video.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a deliberately simple motion-based pipeline can produce object-centric features that are both competitive with and complementary to state-of-the-art foundation models (DINOv2, SimMIM) across depth estimation, 3D detection, and occupancy prediction, with consistent gains across Swin-T through Swin-L and ResNet-50. The finding that fusing these features with DINO yields the best results suggests the two capture genuinely orthogonal information (instance structure vs. semantic category), opening a path for hybrid foundation models. Key limitations include dependence on optical flow quality (which constrains extension to ego-centric or unconstrained video), inability to distinguish sub-object parts (limiting keypoint-level tasks), and training restricted to outdoor driving scenes. Open questions include whether temporal extensions can yield world-model capabilities, whether synthetic data can improve the optical flow bottleneck, and whether the approach transfers to non-rigid or deformable object scenarios.
