## Problem

Sound and complete neural network verification methods are NP-hard and fail to scale to large models (hundreds of millions of parameters), large inputs (ImageNet-scale), or geometric perturbations, leaving a critical gap in certifying the robustness of modern vision and video classifiers against realistic distortions like rotation, scaling, and translation. Existing geometric robustness tools either cannot handle such model sizes or, as the paper demonstrates, produce unsound results due to underestimation of Lipschitz constants in their global optimisation procedures.

## Input / Output

The method takes a Lipschitz-continuous neural network, a single input image (e.g., 3×224×224 or 3×299×299) or video (up to 32×3×256×256 frames), the predicted class label, and 4-dimensional geometric perturbation bounds (rotation angle, isotropic scaling factor, horizontal and vertical translation). Geometric transforms are applied via Spatial Transformer Networks with bilinear interpolation, and for video the same transformation is applied uniformly across all frames. The output is a three-valued verdict (robust, non-robust with a counterexample perturbation, or unknown) after an iterative optimisation loop bounded by a time budget and maximum query count.

## Method

H²V reduces the multi-dimensional geometric robustness problem to a one-dimensional global optimisation by mapping the N-dimensional perturbation hypercube onto a Hilbert space-filling curve, exploiting the fact that geometric perturbations have very low dimensionality (4 parameters) relative to the input space. Along the resulting 1D curve, the method performs Hölder optimisation: it iteratively splits intervals at points determined by adaptively estimated Hölder constants (combining local and global components), computes piecewise lower bounds for each interval, and selects the interval with the lowest bound for refinement until an optimisation budget is reached. A calibration step accounts for both the Hilbert curve approximation error and the finite optimisation budget before producing the final verdict. Theoretical soundness is guaranteed when the reliability parameter r exceeds a threshold or when the Hölder constant is overestimated; in practice, post-convergence heuristics that iteratively increase the global Hölder constant and neighbourhood size mitigate the risk of converging to a local minimum, and no unsound results were observed across 460 benchmark queries.

## Results

On ImageNet with 500 samples and combined geometric perturbations (R(20°)+S(10%)+T(10%)), H²V achieves robust accuracy up to 47.0% (ResNet101) and 46.8% (ResNet152) with timeouts as low as 0.6–2.2%, scaling to 300M-parameter Vision Transformers. On video classification (Kinetics-400), H²V validates 3D-NNs up to 32.45M parameters with 1–3% timeouts and robust accuracy up to 47.0% (Slow-R50). On SoundnessBench (460 queries), H²V produces zero unsound results while resolving the highest number of queries among all tested verifiers, including 19/19 on CNN Tanh where αβ-CROWN resolves only 0.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GeoRobust [22] on ImageNet, H²V finds significantly more counterexamples on ResNet50 (40.8% vs 31.1% robust accuracy) and ResNet152 (46.8% vs 46.2%), while reducing timeouts from 22.9% to 1.8% and 7.2% to 2.0% respectively; the paper identifies 8–30 specific queries per model where GeoRobust incorrectly reported robustness. On SoundnessBench, H²V resolves more queries than both αβ-CROWN and PyRAT (e.g., 27 vs 19 robust on CNN1; 19 vs 0 on CNN Tanh) with zero unsound results across all 460 queries, whereas prior work reported unsound answers from αβ-CROWN. On TLL Verify Bench (VNN-COMP), H²V matches αβ-CROWN exactly (15 robust, 17 non-robust, 0 unknown) but is 26% faster (27.86s vs 37.93s). On UCF101 video classification, H²V outperforms DeepGO [18] on robust accuracy across all perturbation combinations (e.g., 43.07% vs 45.05% for rotation alone, but 22.77% vs 25.25% for R+T+S where DeepGO's unsound upper bound inflates its apparent robustness), and substantially outperforms PGD [65] in finding counterexamples. On MNIST/CIFAR-10, H²V achieves the highest robust accuracy among all baselines including TSS, GSmooth, DeepG, and GeoRobust (e.g., 98.2% vs 95.7% on MNIST rotation; 74.8% vs 70.6% on CIFAR-10 R(10°)).

## Relation to Prior Work

H²V couples two established mathematical tools—Hilbert space-filling curves for dimensionality reduction and Hölder-based global optimisation (Lera & Sergeyev)—into a unified framework specifically tailored for geometric perturbation verification of neural networks. It departs from the dominant symbolic interval propagation and branch-and-bound verification methods (αβ-CROWN, Marabou, PyRAT) by operating in the low-dimensional perturbation space (4 parameters for rotation/scale/translation) rather than the high-dimensional input space, and from prior geometric robustness tools (GeoRobust, DeepG, TSS, GSmooth) by using adaptive Hölder constant estimation rather than requiring a known Lipschitz bound. The paper explicitly positions itself as a 'validation' rather than 'verification' method, acknowledging that outside certain parameter regimes it lacks the unconditional soundness guarantee of interval-propagation approaches, while noting that this distinction is blurred in practice by floating-point errors in all existing tools.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: H²V is the first method to validate geometric robustness of 300M-parameter image classifiers (ViT, ResNet152) on ImageNet and, to the authors' knowledge, the first to validate large 3D video classifiers (up to 32×3×256×256 inputs) against geometric perturbations, opening a previously intractable verification regime. A key open question is why Vision Transformers exhibit lower geometric robustness than ResNets despite their attention mechanisms, which the paper flags but does not resolve. The method's theoretical soundness is conditional on the reliability parameter r exceeding an unknown threshold r*, and the second calibration term in the lower bound grows with perturbation dimensionality, limiting applicability to very high-dimensional perturbation spaces. The empirical finding that GeoRobust produced 8–30 unsound 'robust' verdicts per model on ImageNet raises broader questions about the reliability of existing geometric verification tools.
