## Problem

Existing Gaussian-based asymptotic theories (NNGP, Tensor Programs) cannot characterize the infinite-width limit of attention layers under realistic conditions: finite heads and standard 1/√n scaling. Prior work resorted to either an infinite-head limit (which does not approximate practical 1–128-head Transformers) or a non-standard 1/n scaling (which collapses all similarity scores to zero, erasing the attention mechanism's core function). This paper derives the exact infinite-width limit distribution under the standard 1/√n scaling with a fixed, finite number of heads.

## Input / Output

The analysis takes a single multi-head attention layer whose s input vectors in R^n are generated within a NETSOR feedforward program (e.g., via a clipping or ReLU nonlinearity applied to MatMul outputs), together with independently Gaussian-initialized Q, K, V, O weight matrices scaled as N(0, σ²/n). It produces the exact infinite-width limit law of the attention output, which is a hierarchical Gaussian: conditionally Gaussian given the random dot-product similarity scores, with the scores themselves following a joint Gaussian whose covariance is determined by the fourth moments of the input limiting variables. Crucially, the output variance depends on a realization of the score variables rather than on their moments, a structural departure from standard NNGP/Tensor Programs results.

## Method

The paper extends the Tensor Programs (NETSOR) framework by introducing a new class of limiting random variables for scalar dot-products (pre-softmax attention scores), defined as a zero-mean Gaussian vector that is statistically independent of all NETSOR program variables. The proof of the main theorem (Theorem 3.1) proceeds in two stages: first, a CLT for exchangeable sequences (via Theorem 2 of Blum et al.) establishes weak convergence of the score vector to its Gaussian limit; second, a conditional weak convergence argument shows the attention output converges to a pseudo-Lipschitz function of both the NETSOR limiting variables and the score variables. The key insight is that the 1/√n scaling preserves non-degenerate variability in the scores (unlike 1/n scaling), and this residual randomness induces a hierarchical structure: the output is Gaussian conditional on the scores but non-Gaussian marginally. The result is validated numerically by showing that finite-width attention outputs converge to the predicted limit in KL divergence as n grows, and that the distribution approaches the infinite-head Gaussian of Hron et al. as H increases.

## Results

The paper's main theoretical result (Theorem 3.1) establishes that under standard 1/√n-scaling and finite heads, the attention output converges to a hierarchical Gaussian distribution — Gaussian conditional on the random dot-product scores, which are themselves jointly Gaussian — rather than a single Gaussian. Empirically, the log-KL divergence between the finite-width attention output y₁¹ and its infinite-width limit Z^{y¹} decreases monotonically as width n grows from 16 to 1024 (Figure 1b), confirming rapid convergence. The 1/√n-scaled dot-product scores retain a nondegenerate spread at n=256, while 1/n-scaled scores collapse to zero (Figure 2a). The theory remains accurate even when the number of heads H grows to 256 (comparable to n), and extends to low-rank attention with n_H=64 fixed and (n,H)∈{(64,1),(256,4),(1024,16)} (Figure 3).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two existing regimes that yield Gaussian limits for attention. Against the 1/n-scaling regime of Tensor Programs I [Yang 2019], the proposed 1/√n-scaling preserves nondegenerate dot-product score variability (Figure 2a), whereas the 1/n-scaling collapses all scores to zero in the infinite-width limit, effectively erasing the similarity measurement that defines attention. Against the infinite-head regime of Infinite Attention [Hron et al. 2020], the proposed finite-head analysis produces a non-Gaussian (hierarchical Gaussian) output distribution, while the infinite-head limit yields a single Gaussian. Remarkably, the variance of the proposed finite-head non-Gaussian distribution exactly matches the variance of the infinite-head Gaussian (Remark 3.2), and as H grows (Figure 2b, H=256), the non-Gaussian density visibly converges toward the infinite-head Gaussian curve (black solid line). Thus the proposed framework subsumes the infinite-head result as a special case while capturing the non-Gaussian structure present at practical head counts (H=1–128).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Tensor Programs framework (Yang 2019, 2020) — which rigorously characterizes infinite-width limits of feedforward and recurrent networks as Gaussian processes — to the attention mechanism under the standard 1/√n-scaling and finite number of heads. Prior work on attention in this setting was limited to two special regimes: the 1/n-scaling (Yang 2019b), which trivially collapses dot-product scores to zero, and the infinite-head limit (Hron et al. 2020), which smooths out non-Gaussian effects by averaging over infinitely many heads. The paper's key departure is introducing a new class of limiting random variables for scalar dot-products that are independent of the NETSOR program variables, enabling a rigorous treatment of the realistic finite-head, 1/√n-scaling regime that had remained unresolved.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that the infinite-width limit of a single attention layer is fundamentally non-Gaussian (a hierarchical Gaussian conditioned on random similarity scores) exposes a structural limitation of the Gaussian-based frameworks (NNGP, Tensor Programs) that underpin much of modern theoretical deep learning. This means that extending these tools to deep Transformer architectures will require new mathematical machinery beyond standard Gaussian process analysis. The paper is limited to a single attention layer with constant sequence length, and the authors explicitly note that extending to stacked layers, growing sequence lengths, and training dynamics remain open. The practical implication is that second-order statistics (kernels) may be insufficient to characterize attention behavior, and higher-order structure from the hierarchical limit could affect signal propagation, rank collapse, and optimization landscape geometry in deep Transformers.
