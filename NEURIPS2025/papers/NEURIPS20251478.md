## Problem

Existing infinite-width theory predicts that under Standard Parameterization (He initialization with a global learning rate), networks become unstable at learning rates above O(1/n) and feature learning vanishes at O(1/n), yet practical networks trained in SP exhibit stable feature learning with optimal learning rates decaying only as O(1/√n). This paper asks why SP remains stable and effective at large learning rates despite these predictions, and whether a more faithful infinite-width proxy for practical finite-width networks exists.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251478/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The analysis takes neural network architectures (MLPs up to width 16384, Pythia-GPT Transformers up to 1.4B parameters) trained with SGD, Adam, or AdamW under CE or MSE loss, and produces width-scaling exponents for stability regimes, maximal stable learning rates, and per-layer feature learning rates. A key diagnostic tool is the refined coordinate check (RCC), which decomposes each layer's pre-activation change into an effective update (ΔW·x) and a propagating update (W₀·Δx), then measures their alignment ratios in operator norm to track whether infinite-width alignment predictions hold at finite width.

## Method

The paper performs a fine-grained analysis of the regime previously dismissed as unstable under SP, showing that under cross-entropy loss it splits into a catastrophically unstable sub-regime (α < 1/2) and a benign 'controlled divergence' sub-regime (1/2 ≤ α < 1) where logits diverge but gradients and activations remain stable. At the edge of this regime (η_n = n^{-1/2} for deep MLPs), a well-defined infinite-width limit exists where hidden-layer features evolve width-independently, providing the first practical infinite-width proxy in the feature-learning regime for SP. The mechanism is that CE loss makes the effective output σ(f) bounded, so logit blowup does not destabilize the backward pass, unlike MSE loss where residuals grow unboundedly. The authors validate these predictions using refined coordinate checks across widths, ruling out finite-width explanations (catapult dynamics, edge of stability) and confirming that alignment exponents predicted by Tensor Program theory hold at moderate width. Empirically, the predicted maximal stable learning rate exponents closely match observed optimal learning rate exponents for both MLPs on vision data and GPTs on language data, and the analysis clarifies when layerwise learning rate scalings (SP-full-align) succeed or break down.

## Results

Under CE loss in SP with SGD, the maximal stable learning rate for hidden layers scales as O(n^{-1/2}), whereas under MSE loss it scales as O(n^{-1}), a factor of √n improvement. At the edge of the controlled divergence regime (α = 1/2), hidden layers learn features width-independently (‖Δx_t^l‖_RMS = Θ(1)) while logits diverge as Θ(n^{1-α}), providing the first practical infinite-width limit in the feature-learning regime for SP. Empirically, optimal learning rate exponents for GPT (SGD) and deep MLPs saturate at the predicted max-stable exponent of -1/2 at realistic widths ≥ 16384. On image datasets (MNIST, CIFAR-10, Fashion-MNIST), CE loss substantially outperforms MSE loss under SP, but this gap reverses under μP, where MSE consistently outperforms CE at large widths.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to the standard infinite-width theory of Sohl-Dickstein et al. (2020) and Yang & Hu (2021), which predicts that SP with η_n = ω(1/n) is unstable and η_n = O(1/n) yields only kernel (lazy) training, this paper shows the 'unstable' regime under CE loss actually contains a benign controlled divergence sub-regime (α ∈ [1/2, 1)) where training remains stable and feature learning persists in all hidden layers. Against Everett et al. (2024)'s SP-full-align parameterization, the paper refutes the hypothesized mechanism (lack of alignment between W_0^{L+1} and Δx_t^L) via refined coordinate checks showing alignment exponents remain width-independent, and demonstrates that SP-full-align's learning rate transfer breaks on all image datasets (CIFAR-10, MNIST, Fashion-MNIST) due to width-dependent logit divergence through the last layer, while its success on language data is explained by the large output dimension (d_out ≫ n) suppressing the divergent term. Compared to μP (Yang et al., 2022), the paper shows that CE loss's practical dominance over MSE loss is a scaling artifact of SP: under μP's width-independent dynamics, MSE loss consistently outperforms CE loss on image classification at large widths, suggesting MSE deserves renewed consideration as a simpler alternative under stable parameterizations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Tensor Program (TP) width-scaling framework of Yang and Hu (2021) and Yang and Littwin (2023) to re-examine the regime in Standard Parameterization (SP) that Sohl-Dickstein et al. (2020) classified as simply 'unstable.' It departs from Everett et al. (2024), who questioned whether infinite-width alignment predictions hold at finite width and proposed SP-full-align as the best parameterization, by validating alignment with refined coordinate checks and providing an alternative theoretical explanation for SP-full-align's success. The paper also rules out finite-width mechanisms (catapult dynamics, edge of stability) as explanations for large-LR stability in SP, positioning its controlled divergence regime as the correct resolution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This provides the first well-defined infinite-width limit for SP in the feature-learning regime, resolving a long-standing disconnect between theory and practice. The practical implications are significant: it gives a principled n^{-1/2} scaling prediction for maximal stable learning rates under CE loss, explains why CE loss dominates in practice (a scaling-theoretic rather than heuristic argument), and clarifies when SP-full-align transfer breaks on image datasets. Open questions include the effect of logit divergence on generalization and calibration, multi-epoch training dynamics, and whether the controlled divergence regime persists under deeper architectures or longer training.
