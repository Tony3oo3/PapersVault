## Problem

Online RL with complex function approximations still lacks practical exploration schemes backed by theoretical guarantees. The MEX framework unifies estimation, planning, and exploration in a single objective but requires bilevel optimization (computing the optimal value function from a target Q-function as a regularizer), making it computationally intractable and difficult to ablate. This paper asks whether a sample-efficient model-free online RL algorithm can optimize a unifying objective without resorting to such complex bilevel optimization.

## Input / Output

The method consumes transition tuples (s, a, s') collected sequentially from an episodic MDP, along with a known feature map φ_h(s,a) ∈ ℝ^d. It produces a log-linear policy π_ω(a|s) = exp(φ_h(s,a)ᵀω) / Σ exp(φ_h(s,a')ᵀω) and a linear Q-function Q_h(s,a) = φ_h(s,a)ᵀθ. A key structural detail is that the critic loss is policy-dependent: the Bellman residual is evaluated by sampling a' ~ π_{h+1}(·|s'), so the critic and actor are jointly optimized rather than the critic targeting a fixed optimal value as in MEX.

## Method

VAC derives its objective by formulating a value maximization problem over both the Q-function and policy, constrained by the Bellman *consistency* equation (rather than the Bellman *optimality* equation used in MEX), and writing the regularized Lagrangian of this constrained problem. Applying the dual reparameterization trick (λ_h(s,a) = (Q_{f,h}(s,a) − g_h(s,a))/β) eliminates the dual variables and yields a single non-bilevel objective: maximize V_f^π(ρ) − α·L_t(f,π), where L_t is a policy-dependent Bellman residual loss with an infimum over a reference function g_h. In practice this decomposes into an actor-critic loop: the critic is updated by solving a saddle-point problem that includes a value-incentivized term V_f^π(ρ) (approximated by a single Monte Carlo policy sample), and the actor is updated via standard policy gradient. Under linear MDPs with log-linear policies, VAC achieves Õ(dH²√T) regret, near-optimal up to a √H factor, and extends to infinite-horizon discounted and general function approximation settings.

## Results

The paper evaluates VAC (SAC with a value-incentivized critic term) against vanilla SAC on two MuJoCo continuous-control benchmarks: Ant-v4 and Walker2d-v4, over 10^6 training iterations with 3 random seeds. VAC uses 1/α = 2000 for Ant-v4 and 1/α = 1000 for Walker2d-v4, while the SAC baseline corresponds to 1/α = 0. The paper reports that VAC improves sample efficiency compared to SAC, as evidenced by higher best and average returns across seeds in the learning curves (Figures 1 and 2), though no specific final return numbers are stated in the text.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares VAC against a single baseline: vanilla Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) implemented in Stable-Baselines3, where the value-incentivized term is disabled (1/α = 0). On both Ant-v4 and Walker2d-v4, VAC with the value-incentivized regularization achieves higher returns than SAC across all three seeds, indicating improved sample efficiency. The improvement is visible in both the best-return and average-return curves over the 10^6-iteration training horizon. No other baselines (e.g., MEX, optimistic UCB-style methods, or other actor-critic variants) are included in the empirical evaluation, so the comparison is limited to the ablation of the value-incentivized term on top of SAC. The theoretical contribution (near-optimal Õ(dH²√T) regret under linear MDPs) is not empirically validated against competing theoretical methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reinterprets the MEX (Maximize to Explore) framework [Liu et al., 2024] through a primal-dual optimization lens, showing that its optimistic regularization term arises naturally as a regularized Lagrangian of a constrained value-maximization problem. The key departure from MEX is replacing the Bellman *optimality* constraint (which requires a max-over-actions in the loss, creating a bilevel structure) with the Bellman *consistency* constraint (policy-dependent), yielding a single-level actor-critic objective. The derivation leverages the dual-variable reparameterization trick from SBEED [Dai et al., 2018] and the Fenchel-Rockafellar duality framework [Nachum and Dai, 2020], extending the primal-dual perspective—previously applied to offline RL and off-policy evaluation—into the online RL setting for the first time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, single-objective actor-critic algorithm with near-optimal regret guarantees (within √H of the minimax lower bound) that is directly implementable with standard first-order methods, bridging a gap between theoretically efficient exploration and practical deployment. However, the empirical validation is limited to two MuJoCo continuous-control tasks with only three seeds, and no code is released, making it difficult to assess the practical magnitude of the improvement over vanilla SAC. Open questions include whether the value-incentivized term provides meaningful gains in more complex or high-dimensional settings, how it interacts with modern foundation-model-based RL (e.g., LLM post-training), and whether the near-optimal regret extends to the general function approximation regime without restrictive assumptions like low GEC.
