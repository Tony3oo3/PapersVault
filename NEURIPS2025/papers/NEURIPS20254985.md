## Problem

Standard classifier-free guidance (CFG) relies on linear extrapolation between conditional and unconditional velocity fields, which works for stochastic diffusion models but provokes severe off-manifold drift in deterministic rectified-flow (RF) models, producing oversaturation, warped geometry, and hyperparameter sensitivity. Existing guidance variants (CFG++, APG, CFG-Zero*) were designed for diffusion SDEs and lack a principled, flow-specific solution that preserves the geometric structure of learned transport paths.

## Input / Output

The method is a training-free, drop-in replacement for the guidance step inside the ODE solver of any pretrained flow-based text-to-image model (Flux, SD3/3.5, Lumina-Next). It consumes a text prompt and a Gaussian noise sample, and produces a 1024×1024 RGB image, requiring no additional networks, fine-tuning, or architectural changes. The only tunable parameter beyond the base model is the guidance schedule α(t) = λ_max(1−t)^γ, which controls the interpolation strength over the sampling trajectory.

## Method

Rectified-CFG++ replaces CFG's single extrapolative velocity with a two-stage predictor-corrector update at each ODE step. The predictor step advances the latent state along the conditional RF velocity v_t^c, anchoring the sample on the learned transport manifold; the corrector step then evaluates the guidance difference (v^c − v^u) at this intermediate predicted point and adds it scaled by a time-decaying weight α(t), converting the update from extrapolation to controlled interpolation. This design ensures the effective velocity remains a convex combination of tangent directions to the data manifold, and the paper proves that trajectories stay within a bounded tubular neighborhood of the conditional flow path, with per-step deviation controlled by α(t)·B·Δt. The approach is parameter-free beyond the guidance scale, requires no extra network evaluations beyond the standard conditional and unconditional forward passes, and achieves better FID, CLIP, and human-preference scores than CFG and competing guidance strategies across four large-scale flow-based backbones.

## Results

On MS-COCO 10K, Rectified-CFG++ consistently reduces FID across all four flow-based backbones: Lumina (26.93→22.49), SD3 (23.89→23.39), SD3.5 (20.29→20.22), and Flux-dev (37.86→32.23). Human-preference metrics improve substantially, with PickScore gains of up to +0.35 (Flux-dev: 0.32→0.68) and HPSv2 gains of +0.03–0.04 across models. On T2I-CompBench, Rectified-CFG++ improves all four compositional dimensions (Color, Shape, Texture, Spatial) over CFG for every backbone, e.g. Flux-dev Color rises from 0.61 to 0.77 and Shape from 0.42 to 0.50.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against standard CFG [13], Rectified-CFG++ wins on FID for all four models (most dramatically on Flux-dev, 37.86→32.23) and on PickScore/HPSv2 across the board, though it trades small CLIP or ImageReward drops on Lumina and Flux-dev. Against APG [33] on MS-COCO 1K (SD3.5), Rectified-CFG++ achieves the best FID (67.15 vs 67.23), best ImageReward (1.085 vs 1.075), and best HPSv2 (0.296 vs 0.294), while CLIP is marginally lower (0.3506 vs 0.3513). Against CFG-Zero* [8], Rectified-CFG++ leads on FID (67.15 vs 68.39), ImageReward (1.085 vs 0.995), and HPSv2 (0.296 vs 0.288), again with a negligible CLIP gap (0.3506 vs 0.3458). Overall, Rectified-CFG++ is the only method that simultaneously improves FID, human-preference scores, and compositional alignment (T2I-CompBench) without sacrificing text-image CLIP alignment, whereas APG and CFG-Zero* each trade off one dimension for another.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends classifier-free guidance (CFG) [13] to the deterministic rectified-flow setting, where the absence of stochastic regularization makes CFG's linear extrapolation particularly destabilizing. It borrows the intermediate-point velocity evaluation from APG [33] and the flow-specific motivation from CFG-Zero* [8], but reframes guidance as a predictor-corrector ODE step: the predictor follows the conditional velocity to stay on the learned transport manifold, and the corrector applies a time-scheduled interpolation (rather than extrapolation) between conditional and unconditional fields. This departs from prior diffusion-centric fixes (CFG++ [5], dynamic thresholding [34], characteristic guidance [43]) that either cannot be translated to flow ODEs or destabilize the deterministic trajectory.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As flow-based models (Flux, SD3/3.5, Lumina) become the dominant T2I paradigm, a guidance method that is training-free, drop-in, and manifold-aware is practically important for practitioners who want stronger prompt alignment without the oversaturation and structural artifacts of naive CFG. The paper's main limitation is the absence of error bars or statistical significance testing, and the theoretical bounds (Lipschitz constants, bounded velocity) are loose enough to be more suggestive than tight. Open questions include whether the predictor-corrector structure generalizes to video or 3D flow models, and whether the time-scheduled weight α(t) can be learned rather than hand-tuned per model.
