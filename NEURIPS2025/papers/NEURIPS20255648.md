## Problem

Existing WiFi sensing datasets are collected in controlled laboratory settings using homogeneous hardware (predominantly Intel 5300 NICs), with fragmented, pre-scripted session-based recordings that fail to capture natural daily activity. This limits model generalization to new users, devices, and environments, and prevents the development of unified multi-task sensing systems suitable for real-world deployment in homes and healthcare settings.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255648/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The input is a WiFi CSI amplitude tensor of shape C×K×T (channel × subcarrier × time), where phase is eliminated due to cross-platform instability and subcarriers are zero-padded or clipped to a fixed dimension to handle hardware heterogeneity (14–256 subcarriers across 1×1 to 2×2 MIMO configurations). Samples are segmented into 5-second windows (10-second for breathing at 30 Hz), normalized per-sample by mean/std, and the output spans seven classification labels: fall/non-fall, breathing present/absent, motion source (human/pet/robot/fan), room location (6 classes), activity (5 classes), user identity (6 classes), and proximity distance (4 classes at 0.5/1.5/2.5/3.5 m).

## Method

CSI-Bench introduces a continuous in-the-wild data collection pipeline using 16 commercial WiFi edge devices spanning five chipset vendors (Qualcomm, Broadcom, Espressif, MediaTek, NXP), coordinated via router-side CSI extraction and cloud upload, with a lightweight Google Spreadsheet annotation tool for user-logged activity timestamps. For benchmarking, the paper establishes a multi-task learning framework using a shared Transformer backbone with LoRA adapters (rank=8, α=32) and task-specific classification heads, reducing total parameter count by over 60% and wall-clock training time by ~3× compared to separate single-task models. Evaluation is stratified by difficulty tiers (Easy/Medium/Hard) for single-task datasets and by three OOD axes (cross-user, cross-environment, cross-device) for the multi-task dataset, revealing that while in-distribution accuracy is strong (e.g., 88% for HAR), OOD performance drops substantially (e.g., ~30% F1 for proximity under cross-device), exposing a critical generalization gap that motivates future domain-adaptive approaches.

## Results

On the four single-task CSI-Bench datasets, transformer-based architectures lead: PatchTST achieves the best Breathing Detection accuracy (98.84%), while ResNet-18 and TimeSformer-1D both reach 100% on Room-Level Localization. For Fall Detection, LSTM (94.93%) and ResNet-18 (94.88%) are the strongest, with MLP trailing at 92.16%. In the multi-task setting, joint training with a shared Transformer backbone and LoRA adapters improves HAR accuracy by +12.66% and Proximity Recognition by +8.89% over task-specific training, while reducing total parameter count by over 60% and wall-clock training time by ~3×. Under OOD splits (cross-device, cross-environment, cross-user), all models degrade substantially—HAR drops from ~88% to ~55–62% and Proximity from ~86% to ~28–31% F1—exposing a significant generalization gap.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ResNet-18 [16] is the strongest overall single-task model, achieving 100% on Localization and the best Motion Source Recognition accuracy (99.56%), while also being competitive on Fall Detection (94.88%). PatchTST [30] edges out all other models on Breathing Detection (98.84% Acc) and is near-perfect on Localization (99.91%). LSTM [17] leads on Fall Detection (94.93%) but lags on Motion Source Recognition (98.42%). TimeSformer-1D [8] matches ResNet-18 at 100% on Localization but shows the highest variance on Fall Detection (±1.16). ViT [11] is a solid mid-range performer, reaching 99.94% on Localization but trailing on Fall Detection (93.58%). The standard Transformer [36] sits in the middle across all tasks without clearly leading any. MLP [34] is consistently the weakest baseline, particularly on Localization (87.14%) and Fall Detection (92.16%), confirming that temporal and spatial modeling are critical for CSI-based sensing.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs fundamentally from prior WiFi sensing datasets (Widar3.0, SignFi, MM-Fi, XRF55, WiAR) that were collected in controlled laboratory settings using homogeneous Intel 5300 NICs with session-based, manually-triggered recordings. CSI-Bench instead leverages 16 heterogeneous commercial edge devices across five chipset vendors (Qualcomm, Broadcom, Espressif, MediaTek, NXP) deployed in real homes and offices, enabling continuous in-the-wild recording over 461+ hours. The CSI quality verification pipeline builds directly on the multilayered evaluation framework from Hu et al. (MobiCom 2024), while the multi-task learning setup with LoRA adapters on a shared Transformer backbone follows standard parameter-efficient fine-tuning practice. The paper carves new ground primarily through scale, hardware diversity, and the co-labeled multi-task annotation scheme rather than through novel sensing algorithms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CSI-Bench fills a critical gap by providing the first large-scale, in-the-wild, multi-task WiFi sensing benchmark with standardized OOD evaluation splits (cross-user, cross-environment, cross-device), enabling rigorous generalization studies that prior controlled datasets could not support. The finding that OOD performance collapses dramatically (e.g., proximity recognition drops to ~30% F1, HAR to ~47% F1) while in-distribution multi-task learning achieves strong results with 60% parameter reduction highlights both the promise and the persistent domain-shift challenge for deployable WiFi sensing. Key limitations include the use of amplitude-only CSI (excluding phase/AoA information), restriction to classification tasks (no regression or long-horizon tracking), and a relatively small multi-task user pool (6 users). Open questions include whether domain-adaptive or calibration-free methods can close the OOD gap, and whether phase information, if stabilized across heterogeneous hardware, would substantially improve performance.
