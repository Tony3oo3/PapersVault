## Problem

3DGS-based surface reconstruction methods are bottlenecked by two issues: their reliance on MVG-derived point cloud initialization that inevitably contains inaccuracies and uncovered regions, and the geometric ambiguity of Gaussian primitives that lack clearly defined edges. While sparse voxels offer completeness and geometric clarity as an alternative, they suffer from absent scene constraints causing heavy geometric distortion and extreme locality that hinders accurate surface formation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253629/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view RGB images (downsampled 2× for DTU/TnT, 2× or 4× for Mip-NeRF 360), camera poses, and monocular depth estimates from DepthAnythingV2. It produces a 3D mesh extracted via TSDF (voxel size 0.002 for DTU) and novel view renderings. The sparse voxel scene is organized in an adaptive octree with trilinear density fields at each voxel's eight corners, and the monocular depth is applied at pixel level through a rendered octree-level map for uncertainty-weighted supervision.

## Method

GeoSVR builds on SVRaster's sparse voxel rasterization with adaptive octree subdivision, adding two key mechanisms. First, a Voxel-Uncertainty Depth Constraint computes per-voxel geometric uncertainty from octree level and density (U_base = w_s / β(l+l₀), scaled by 1−exp(−v_geo)), renders this as a pixel-wise level map, and uses it to modulate a patch-wise monocular depth loss so that confident regions rely on photometric supervision while uncertain regions lean on external depth cues. Second, Sparse Voxel Surface Regularization combines three components: Voxel Dropout (randomly dropping a fraction of voxels during homography patch-warping geometry regularization to force each tiny voxel to maintain global geometric consistency), Surface Rectification (penalizing density at the ray-voxel entry point while encouraging it at the exit point for surface voxels crossing an alpha threshold, aligning the rendered surface to the true density peak), and a Scaling Penalty (penalizing voxels whose sampling distance exceeds the minimum voxel size to suppress geometrically inaccurate large voxels from surface formation).

## Results

On the DTU dataset, GeoSVR achieves a mean Chamfer distance of 0.47, outperforming the strongest 3DGS-based baseline PGSR (0.52) and the monocular-cue-based MonoGSDF (0.65) while training in 0.8h (vs. >12h for SDF methods). On Tanks and Temples, GeoSVR attains the best mean F1-score of 0.56, surpassing PGSR (0.52) and MonoGSDF (0.47). On Mip-NeRF 360, GeoSVR maintains competitive rendering quality (outdoor PSNR 24.83, indoor PSNR 30.46) comparable to its SVRaster backbone and other surface reconstruction methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GeoSVR outperforms all 18 baselines on DTU Chamfer distance, with the largest margins over SVRaster (0.76→0.47, −0.29), GS2Mesh (0.68→0.47, −0.21), and MonoGSDF (0.65→0.47, −0.18). Against the closest competitor PGSR (0.52), GeoSVR improves by 0.05 while also beating the SDF-based Geo-NeuS (0.51) by 0.04. On Tanks and Temples F1-score, GeoSVR (0.56) leads PGSR (0.52) by 0.04 and MonoGSDF (0.47) by 0.09, and substantially outperforms 2DGS (0.30) and SVRaster (0.40). On Mip-NeRF 360 rendering, GeoSVR is competitive but slightly trails PGSR on indoor LPIPS (0.172 vs. 0.147) and GOF on outdoor SSIM (0.738 vs. 0.750), indicating a minor trade-off in photometric quality for geometric accuracy. Notably, GeoSVR achieves these gains without requiring SfM point cloud initialization, unlike all 3DGS-based baselines, and trains faster than all SDF-based methods (0.8h vs. >12h).

## Relation to Prior Work

GeoSVR departs from the dominant 3DGS-based surface reconstruction paradigm (2DGS, GOF, PGSR) by adopting sparse voxel rasterization (SVRaster) as its geometric representation, eliminating the reliance on SfM point cloud initialization that inherently limits coverage and accuracy in 3DGS methods. It builds on the patch-wise depth regularizer from DNGaussian and integrates monocular depth cues (via DepthAnythingV2) through a novel voxel-uncertainty weighting mechanism, distinguishing itself from prior monocular-cue methods (MonoSDF, VCR-GauS, MonoGSDF) that apply depth constraints uniformly or conservatively. The sparse voxel surface regularization—combining voxel dropout, surface rectification, and scaling penalty—addresses the extreme locality of tiny voxels in a way that has no direct analogue in the Gaussian or SDF literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that sparse voxels, long under-explored for surface reconstruction, can match or exceed 3DGS-based SOTA while offering inherent advantages in completeness (no initialization gaps) and geometric clarity (bounded primitives vs. unbounded Gaussians). The voxel-uncertainty depth constraint provides a principled mechanism for integrating imperfect external geometry cues without degrading already-accurate regions—a problem that has resisted clean solutions in the 3DGS setting. Open questions remain around handling varying illumination, textureless regions, and scalability to very large unbounded scenes, which the authors acknowledge as future directions. The work also highlights a broader limitation of 3DGS: even with external depth cues, the SfM initialization bottleneck caps achievable quality in challenging regions.
