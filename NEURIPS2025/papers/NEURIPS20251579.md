## Problem

Standard Classifier-Free Guidance applied to iterative masked diffusion language models relies on a static unconditional input (e.g., a fully masked sequence or null prompt), which fails to adapt to the model's dynamically varying uncertainty across token positions and denoising steps. This context-agnostic unconditioning can produce guidance that is too diffuse, too weak, or misaligned with the specific points of ambiguity the model faces at a given generation step, limiting the effectiveness of conditional control.

## Input / Output

At each denoising step k, A-CFG consumes the current partially masked token sequence x^(k) (containing the conditioning prompt plus already-filled and still-masked tokens) and a scalar re-masking proportion ρ. It produces guided logits L_guided^(k) over the vocabulary for the [MASK] positions, which are then used to sample or select tokens for the next iteration. The method requires two forward passes of the base model per step (one conditional, one on the adaptively re-masked sequence), and the number of re-masked tokens scales with the fraction of non-masked content present at that step.

## Method

A-CFG replaces the static unconditional input in CFG with a dynamically constructed one based on the model's own predictive confidence. At each generation step, after computing conditional logits, it evaluates the maximum softmax probability for every non-[MASK] token position, then selects the bottom ρ fraction of tokens by confidence and re-masks them to [MASK] to form the unconditional input x_uncond^(k). The model is run a second time on this modified sequence to obtain L_uncond^(k), and the standard CFG interpolation formula is applied using this adaptively derived baseline. The key insight is that re-masking only the tokens where the model is most uncertain makes the guidance signal (L_cond − L_uncond) more discriminative, concentrating corrective influence precisely on ambiguous positions rather than applying it uniformly across the entire sequence. The re-masking proportion ρ (set to 0.7 in experiments) controls the intensity of this adaptive intervention, and the guidance scale w is tuned per model but held fixed across benchmarks.

## Results

On LLaDA 8B, A-CFG achieves 33.3 on GPQA (+3.9 over Std CFG's 29.4), 42.0 on Sudoku (+8.0 over Std CFG's 34.0), and 73.5 on GSM8K (+2.7 over Std CFG's 70.8). Compared to unguided LLaDA 8B, A-CFG yields +7.2 on GPQA (33.3 vs. 26.1) and +7.0 on Sudoku (42.0 vs. 35.0). The method also generalizes to Dream-7B, improving Sudoku by +8.0 (80.0 vs. 72.0) and ARC-C by +1.0 (60.8 vs. 59.8).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LLaDA 8B with Standard CFG, A-CFG wins on 9 out of 10 benchmarks, with the largest margins on Sudoku (+8.0), GPQA (+3.9), and GSM8K (+2.7); the sole exception is MMLU where the difference is negligible (66.1 vs. 65.8). Compared to unguided LLaDA 8B, A-CFG improves on 8 of 10 tasks, most notably GPQA (+7.2) and Sudoku (+7.0). Against the Dream-7B baseline, A-CFG applied to Dream-7B improves Sudoku by +8.0 (80.0 vs. 72.0) and ARC-C by +1.0, confirming cross-model generalizability. Versus LLaMA3 8B (AR), LLaDA 8B with A-CFG substantially outperforms on GSM8K (73.5 vs. 53.1), GPQA (33.3 vs. 25.9), and Sudoku (42.0 vs. 0.0), but trails on Hellaswag (72.6 vs. 79.1) and PIQA (76.1 vs. 80.6). Against LLaMA2 7B, A-CFG-equipped LLaDA dominates on nearly all benchmarks. Qwen2 7B remains the strongest AR competitor, leading on MMLU (70.3 vs. 66.1), Hellaswag (80.7 vs. 72.6), and GSM8K (80.2 vs. 73.5), though A-CFG narrows the gap considerably on reasoning tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG), originally formulated for continuous image diffusion [15], to the setting of iterative masked diffusion language models (MDMs) such as LLaDA [26] and Dream-7B [43]. Prior work on CFG in text diffusion [23, 25] typically employs a static unconditional input (e.g., a null prompt or fully masked target), which the authors identify as suboptimal when model uncertainty varies dynamically across tokens and denoising steps. A-CFG departs from this by constructing the unconditional input adaptively: it re-masks the lowest-confidence non-masked tokens at each step, localizing the guidance signal to regions of genuine ambiguity rather than applying a uniform, context-agnostic unconditioning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

A-CFG demonstrates that a simple confidence-based re-masking heuristic can meaningfully improve conditional generation in masked diffusion LMs, with consistent gains across reasoning (GPQA +3.9, GSM8K +2.7) and planning (Sudoku +8.0) benchmarks over standard CFG. However, the method is validated only on two 7–8B diffusion models and adds an extra forward pass per denoising step, raising open questions about computational cost at scale and generalizability to larger or architecturally different diffusion models. The ablation revealing that overly aggressive re-masking (ρ=0.9) degrades performance also suggests the technique is sensitive to hyperparameter tuning, and whether the confidence signal remains informative in longer or more complex generation settings remains unexplored.
