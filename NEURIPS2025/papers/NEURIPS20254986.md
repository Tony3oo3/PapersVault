## Problem

Offline RL methods have extensively explored data constraints, policy regularization, and uncertainty estimation to handle distributional shift, yet the role of neural network architecture in promoting generalization remains largely unexplored. In particular, there has been limited effort to structurally guide the optimization process toward flatter regions of the loss landscape, which are associated with better OOD generalization and reduced Q-value overestimation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254986/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes state vectors from a fixed offline dataset D = {(s, a, r, s')} and produces a deterministic policy π(a|s) along with ensemble Q-value estimates. Inputs are standardized to zero mean and unit variance before entering the network. The critic network uses a Gaussian activation (exp(-u²)) with bounded output in (0,1], while the actor retains standard ReLU; residual connections are applied exclusively in the critic to avoid constraining the action space. The ensemble of M independently parameterized critics aggregates via averaging for the actor loss but uses the minimum across target critics for bootstrapped targets.

## Method

FANS is a flatness-aware architectural framework comprising four modules integrated into a standard TD3 actor-critic without modifying the objective function: (1) pre-activation residual blocks (LayerNorm → Linear → Gaussian → Linear + skip connection) that stabilize gradient flow and promote low-frequency value functions; (2) a Gaussian activation φ(u) = exp(-u²) that provides smooth, non-monotonic, bounded nonlinearity encouraging flatter local loss landscapes; (3) post-final-block Layer Normalization that enforces per-sample feature scale invariance and reduces sensitivity to aberrant activations; and (4) an ensemble of M critic networks whose min-based target computation suppresses overestimation on OOD actions. An NTK analysis reveals that FANS maintains significantly lower kernel values k_φ(s̄,ā,s,a) compared to baselines, directly suppressing the propagation of in-distribution TD errors into OOD Q-value estimates. Ablation studies confirm that residual blocks and the ensemble are the most critical components, with the Gaussian activation and LayerNorm providing additional but smaller gains.

## Results

On the D4RL MuJoCo locomotion benchmark (9 tasks, 5 seeds, normalized scores), TD3+FANS achieves the best average score of 96.0, outperforming the strongest baselines EDAC (92.9), ReBRAC (89.7), and IQL (81.6). The method wins on 6 of 9 individual tasks, with particularly large margins on hopper-medium (104.6 vs. 102.3 for ReBRAC) and walker2d-medium (101.0 vs. 93.4 for EDAC). In the generalization-focused comparison (Table 3), TD3+FANS again achieves the best average (96.0) versus CQL+ADS (92.3) and PRDC (90.1). Under a 5% data regime, FANS trained on limited data even surpasses baselines trained on the full dataset on hopper and walker2d tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TD3+FANS (avg 96.0) outperforms all 15 baselines on average. Against the closest competitors, it beats EDAC (92.9) by 3.1 points and CQL+ADS (92.3) by 3.7 points, while leading ReBRAC (89.7) by 6.3 and PRDC (90.1) by 5.9. The margin is largest against AWAC (67.7, +28.3) and DT (73.8, +22.2), indicating that the architectural design compensates for the absence of offline-specific constraints like behavior cloning. On individual tasks, TD3+FANS is best on 6 of 9 locomotion tasks (all hopper and walker2d variants), but trails EDAC on halfcheetah-medium (66.6 vs. 68.2), halfcheetah-medium-replay (55.9 vs. 62.1), and halfcheetah-medium-expert (102.8 vs. 104.8). In the generalization-focused comparison (Table 3), TD3+FANS also achieves the top average, edging out CQL+ADS (92.3) and PRDC (90.1), while DOGE (81.0) and TSRL (77.5) trail by 15+ points.

## Relation to Prior Work

FANS positions itself as a structural/architectural intervention in offline RL, deliberately departing from the dominant paradigm of policy constraints (TD3+BC, CQL), value regularization (IQL, CQL), and uncertainty estimation (EDAC, SAC-N). It draws on the broader deep learning literature on loss-landscape flatness—residual connections (SimBa, BRO), layer normalization (SEEM, Lyle et al.), and smooth activations (GELU)—and repurposes these individually well-studied components as a unified inductive bias specifically for the distributional-shift challenges of offline RL. The NTK-based generalization analysis builds directly on the authors' prior work (Ma et al., NeurIPS 2023) on representation distinction in offline RL, extending it from a representation-learning perspective to an architectural-design perspective. The ensemble component is related to EDAC and SAC-N but is framed here as a variance-reduction structural choice rather than an algorithmic uncertainty mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that purely architectural modifications—without any offline-specific objective changes like behavior cloning or conservative penalties—can match or exceed methods that explicitly constrain the policy, suggesting that the field has underinvested in the model-architecture dimension of offline RL. The NTK analysis provides a mechanistic explanation (suppression of the generalization amplifier) for why flatter architectures reduce OOD extrapolation error, offering a principled design guideline beyond ad-hoc tuning. Key limitations include evaluation restricted to MuJoCo locomotion tasks, per-task tuning of ensemble size M, and the unusual Gaussian activation exp(-u²) whose specific form may not transfer trivially to other domains. Open questions include whether FANS generalizes to discrete-action or high-dimensional continuous tasks, how it interacts with offline-to-online transfer, and whether the flatness objective can be made explicit rather than implicit through architecture.
