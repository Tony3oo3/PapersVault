## Problem

Existing algorithms for quasi-self-concordant (QSC) optimization in the overdetermined regime (n ≫ d) have iteration complexity that scales with the number of data points n rather than the intrinsic dimension d, making them poorly suited to modern high-sample settings. The prior state-of-the-art by Adil-Bullins-Sachdeva (NeurIPS 2021) achieved Õ(n^{1/3}) linear system solves per oracle call, and its complex parameter tuning has prevented a practical implementation. This paper asks whether one can design a QSC optimization algorithm whose iteration complexity is nearly independent of n.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes as input an n×d matrix A, an m×d constraint matrix N, vectors b and v, a C-quasi-self-concordant function f, an initial feasible point x^(0), a diameter bound R on the ℓ∞-norm of the level set, a lower bound B on h, and a target precision ε. It outputs an ε-additive approximate minimizer x satisfying Nx = v. Each iteration requires solving linear systems with structured matrices of the form A^T DA and N(A^T DA)^+ N^T where D is a positive diagonal, so the dominant cost is d×d linear algebra rather than n×n operations. The ℓ∞ regression subroutine additionally accepts a guess M for the optimal objective and returns either a primal solution with ‖Ax‖∞ ≤ (1+ε)M or a dual certificate proving infeasibility.

## Method

The paper employs a trust-region Newton method whose convergence rate depends on the ℓ∞ diameter R of the level set (rather than the ℓ2 diameter, which can be larger by √n), yielding Õ(CR log(CR) log²((h(x⁰)−h(x*)))/ε)) outer iterations. Each outer iteration solves a quadratic subproblem over an ℓ∞ ball via a binary search on the objective value, and the key technical contribution is a lightweight IRLS-based residual solver that requires only Õ(d^{1/3} log n) linear system solves per subproblem. The IRLS solver initializes the dual resistances using ℓ∞ Lewis weight overestimates (computed via the fixed-point iteration of Cohen et al., COLT 2019) rather than the uniform initialization used by Ene-Vladu (ICML 2019), which is what breaks the n-dependence. The update rule distinguishes two regimes: in the high-width regime (‖AΔ‖∞ > d^{1/3}/C), a single coordinate is incremented, while in the low-width regime, all coordinates exceeding a threshold are multiplicatively updated, with the telescoping energy-invariant guaranteeing convergence in Õ(d^{1/3}) steps.

## Results

The main theoretical contribution is reducing the iteration complexity for QSC optimization from Õ(n^{1/3}) to Õ(d^{1/3}) linear system solves, a substantial improvement in the overdetermined regime n ≫ d. Empirically, on large instances (up to 1.8M × 11), the proposed algorithm runs in 0.3–7.5 seconds with objective gaps of at most 4.5×10⁻⁹ relative to Newton's method, confirming high-precision solutions at ε=10⁻¹⁰. On all tested datasets, both the proposed method and Newton's method are significantly faster than CVX, which was only feasible on instances of up to 2500 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two baselines: Newton's method with line search [Karimireddy et al., 2018] and the CVX solver. Against Newton's method, the proposed algorithm is consistently slower in wall-clock time (e.g., 7.5s vs 2.7s on Song Year Prediction, 6.5s vs 3.0s on Consumption of Power), but achieves comparable objective values with gaps below 5×10⁻⁹. The key advantage is theoretical: the proposed method provides a provable Õ(d^{1/3}) iteration complexity with poly-log(1/ε) convergence, whereas Newton's method has a significantly weaker theoretical guarantee. Against CVX, the proposed method is dramatically faster—CVX was infeasible on the full large instances and could only be run on random subsets of up to 2500 points, where it was still slower than both the proposed method and Newton's method. The paper positions its contribution as a proof-of-concept that a theoretically stronger algorithm can achieve near-competitive practical performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: the trust-region framework for quasi-self-concordant (QSC) optimization from Adil-Bullins-Sachdeva (NeurIPS 2021), the IRLS approach to ℓ∞ regression from Ene-Vladu (ICML 2019), and the Lewis weight techniques for p-norm regression from Jambulapati-Liu-Sidford (STOC 2022). The key departure is combining ℓ∞ Lewis weight overestimates with an IRLS scheme, which breaks the uniform-initialization assumption that limited the prior IRLS analysis to n^{1/3} iterations. Unlike the Monteiro-Svaiter acceleration used in [JLS22], the resulting algorithm is a simple lightweight IRLS method, making it significantly more practical than the state-of-the-art QSC solver of [ABS21], which currently lacks a working implementation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The reduction from Õ(n^{1/3}) to Õ(d^{1/3}) linear system solves is a substantial theoretical improvement in the overdetermined regime (n ≫ d) that dominates modern data analysis, and the algorithm's simplicity makes it implementable where prior methods were not. However, the empirical results show Newton's method from [KSJ18] remains faster in practice, and the algorithm requires knowledge of the level-set diameter R and lower bound B, which may be hard to obtain. Open questions include whether the Lewis-weight-IRLS combination extends to other norm regimes or non-decomposable objectives, and whether the practical gap with Newton's method can be closed through implementation refinements.
