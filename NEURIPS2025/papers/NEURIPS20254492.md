## Problem

Diffusion models offer superior accuracy and uncertainty quantification for physics emulation but are computationally expensive at inference due to iterative sampling. While latent-space generation has resolved this cost in image and video synthesis, it remains unclear whether the same strategy transfers to dynamical-systems emulation and how sensitive emulation accuracy is to the compression rate. This paper systematically investigates that question across three challenging fluid-dynamics datasets at compression rates up to 1000×.

## Input / Output

The emulator takes an initial physical state x⁰ (e.g., 512×512×5 scalar/vector fields for Euler, 512×128×4 for Rayleigh-Bénard, 64×64×64×6 for TGC) and simulation parameters θ (heat capacity, Rayleigh/Prandtl numbers, or density/temperature/metallicity), encodes x⁰ into a latent token grid via a convolutional autoencoder (spatial downsampling r=32 for 2D, r=8 for 3D), and autoregressively generates n=4 future latent states per step. Outputs are decoded back to pixel space, yielding 16 distinct trajectory rollouts per initial condition for diffusion models (a single deterministic rollout for neural solvers), evaluated over lead times of 100–200 simulation steps.

## Method

The pipeline has two stages: (1) a convolutional autoencoder with near-identity initialization, a deterministic saturating activation z/√(1+z²/B²) replacing the standard KL penalty, and a PSGD preconditioned optimizer compresses pixel states into latent tokens of 4, 16, or 64 channels; (2) a 16-block transformer denoiser with RoPE, query-key normalization, and value residual learning is trained via denoising score matching under a rectified-flow schedule to predict n=4 future latent states given the current state and θ, using MCVD-style binary masks for variable-length context. Sampling uses a 3rd-order Adams-Bashforth ODE integrator with only 16 NFEs. The key empirical finding is that diffusion-based emulation accuracy is remarkably robust to compression rate—even at 1000× compression where autoencoder reconstruction degrades severely, latent-space emulators match or exceed pixel-space baselines while using fewer parameters and less training compute.

## Results

On the Euler dataset, latent diffusion emulators achieve VRMSE of 0.070–0.093 (÷80 to ÷1280) at lead time 1:20, matching or exceeding the pixel-space neural solver (0.138) despite using fewer parameters. For Rayleigh-Bénard, diffusion accuracy actually *improves* with compression (VRMSE 0.171 → 0.141 → 0.146 from ÷64 to ÷1024), while autoencoder reconstruction degrades from 0.023 to 0.070. Inference time for latent diffusion is 84 ms per state versus O(1 s) for pixel-space diffusion and 56 ms for the pixel-space neural solver, enabling a full 100-step Euler trajectory in ~3 s on a single A100 GPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares latent diffusion emulators against three baselines: (1) latent neural solvers (same transformer architecture, deterministic mean-regression objective), (2) a pixel-space neural solver (2× more parameters, 16 H100 GPUs), and (3) autoencoder reconstruction quality as a reference. Diffusion emulators consistently outperform latent neural solvers across all three datasets and all compression rates—for example, on Euler at ÷320 and lead time 61:100, diffusion achieves VRMSE 0.371 versus 0.476 for the neural solver. Both latent emulators (diffusion and neural solver) surpass the pixel-space neural solver (0.138 at 1:20) even at the highest compression (÷1280: 0.093 diffusion, 0.137 neural solver). The headline finding is that increasing compression from ÷80 to ÷1280 (a 16× reduction in latent channels) degrades autoencoder reconstruction by ~5× (0.011→0.060) but changes diffusion VRMSE by only ~25% (0.075→0.093) on Euler, and actually *improves* it on Rayleigh-Bénard (0.171→0.146). The spread-skill ratio of diffusion ensembles stays near 1 (well-calibrated uncertainty), while it drops slightly at higher compression, hinting at mild overfitting in the reduced latent space.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the latent diffusion paradigm from image/video generation (Rombach et al. 2022, Esser et al. 2024) to physics emulation, building on recent applications of latent diffusion to dynamical systems (Gao et al. 2023, Du et al. 2024, Li et al. 2025). It adopts the rectified flow noise schedule, MCVD-style binary mask conditioning, and temporal bundling from the diffusion and neural PDE solver literatures, respectively. The key departure is a systematic empirical study of compression rate as the primary variable, rather than proposing a new architecture or training objective, and a controlled comparison of generative (diffusion) versus deterministic (neural solver) emulators within the same latent space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that emulation accuracy is robust to compression rates up to 1000×—even when autoencoder reconstruction degrades substantially—provides practitioners with a strong practical signal that aggressive latent compression is viable, reducing memory and compute costs without sacrificing fidelity. The consistent superiority of diffusion-based emulators over neural solvers in both accuracy and statistical plausibility (power spectrum, spread-skill ratio) reinforces the case for generative approaches in long-horizon rollout. Open questions include whether the compression robustness generalizes beyond fluid dynamics, whether a theoretical explanation exists (the paper invokes 'usable information' theory but offers no formal result), and whether the slight degradation in spread-skill ratio at high compression signals overfitting that could be mitigated with larger datasets or auxiliary losses.
