## Problem

Autoregressive language models are constrained by causal attention to reason in a single, sequential direction, forcing even their Chain-of-Thought reasoning to follow a rigid left-to-right order. Diffusion language models naturally support bidirectional, non-linear token generation, but existing RL approaches for DLMs only optimize the final output while ignoring the intermediate denoising steps as potential reasoning actions. This paper asks: can the entire reverse diffusion trajectory be treated as a chain of lateral thought and reinforced end-to-end to improve reasoning?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255105/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt (math question or code task description) and initializes a fully masked token sequence of fixed length (e.g., 256 tokens for LLaDA). Generation proceeds over N denoising steps (64–256), where at each step a subset of masked tokens is unmasked in a learned non-linear order, and the output is a complete text response containing the final answer (a boxed number for math, or a Python function for code). Unlike autoregressive models, the generation length is controlled by the number of initial mask tokens rather than an end-of-text token, enabling flexible response lengths at inference time.

## Method

DCoLT treats the full sequence of intermediate diffusion states x₀:ₙ as a chain of lateral thought actions and applies outcome-based RL (GRPO) with a binary reward on final-answer correctness, without any supervision on intermediate steps. For the continuous-time SEDD model, the predicted concrete score yields a closed-form transition probability that serves as the per-step policy. For the discrete-time masked LLaDA model, the paper introduces an Unmasking Policy Module (UPM) — a single transformer block with adaptive layer normalization encoding the step index and mask indicators — that assigns ranking scores to each masked token; a Plackett-Luce model then samples the top-K tokens to unmask at each step, and the full per-step policy is the product of this unmasking policy and the token prediction policy. Gradients are back-propagated at each denoising step and accumulated over the entire reverse diffusion trajectory before a single parameter update, enabling joint optimization of the whole reasoning chain.

## Results

On the 8B LLaDOU model (LLaDA + DCoLT), the method achieves 88.1% on GSM8K, 44.6% on MATH, 59.1% on HumanEval, and 51.6% on MBPP in zero-shot evaluation, using only 15K public training samples and 16 H800 GPUs. On the 400M SEDD model, DCoLT reaches 96.2% on Sudoku 4×4 and 57.0% on GSM8K-Aug, substantially outperforming the SFT-trained DoT baseline (79.4% / 53.5%). The DCoLT-reinforced LLaDA improves over the LLaDA 8B baseline by +9.8% on GSM8K, +5.7% on MATH, +19.5% on HumanEval, and +11.4% on MBPP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among diffusion language models, LLaDOU decisively outperforms all baselines: it beats Dream 7B by +7.0% on GSM8K and +1.7% on MATH, and surpasses the d1-LLaDA (SFT+RL) pipeline by +6.0% on GSM8K and +4.4% on MATH without any supervised reasoning traces. On the 400M SEDD model, DCoLT (96.2% Sudoku, 57.0% GSM8K-Aug) far exceeds DoT (79.4%, 53.5%) and GPT2+CoT (71.5%, 43.9%), confirming the advantage of RL over SFT for lateral reasoning. Against auto-regressive models, LLaDOU matches DeepSeekMath-RL on GSM8K (88.1% vs 88.2%) despite using ~50× less RL data (15K vs 144K questions), but trails on MATH (44.6% vs 51.7%) where DeepSeekMath benefits from 776K annotated SFT pairs. On code generation, LLaDOU (59.1% HumanEval, 51.6% MBPP) outperforms all DLMs and mid-size auto-regressive models (CodeLLaMA, DeepSeek 7B) but falls short of DeepSeekCoder-Instruct (66.1%, 65.4%), which leverages 2B tokens of proprietary instruction data (~150× more than LLaDOU's ~12M tokens).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RL-for-reasoning paradigm (pioneered by DeepSeek-R1 and DeepSeekMath on auto-regressive models) to diffusion language models, where the key conceptual shift is treating the entire reverse diffusion trajectory as a learnable 'chain of lateral thought' rather than optimizing only the final output. It builds directly on SEDD's concrete score framework for the continuous-time case and on LLaDA's masked diffusion architecture for the discrete-time case, adding a novel Unmask Policy Module parameterized by the Plackett-Luce model to make the unmasking order itself a learnable action. This departs from prior DLM RL work (d1, policy-gradient discrete diffusion) that treated intermediate denoising steps as fixed and only reinforced the terminal sample, and from DoT which still imposed supervised step-by-step CoT structure on diffusion models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that diffusion language models can achieve competitive reasoning performance using only 15K public training samples and outcome-based rewards, without any CoT supervision or proprietary data, suggesting a more data-efficient path to reasoning in DLMs. The finding that the unmasking order is a critical learnable component (UPM alone lifts accuracy from 47% to 69% on GSM8K) opens a new axis of optimization for masked diffusion models beyond token prediction. Key open questions include whether DCoLT generalizes to tasks without verifiable rewards (requiring learned reward models), how it scales with larger models and longer sequences, and whether the 'lateral thinking' framing yields qualitatively different reasoning patterns that transfer across domains.
