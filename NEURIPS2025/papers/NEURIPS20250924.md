## Problem

CLIP models trained with global-level image-text contrastive learning capture coarse semantics but fail to distinguish fine-grained visual details, leading to poor performance on dense prediction and vision-centric multimodal tasks. Existing remedies either modify the encoder at inference time (task-specific), add auxiliary encoders, or require costly region-text paired data for retraining. This paper asks how to improve an existing pretrained CLIP image encoder's detail-capturing ability using image data alone, without retraining from scratch or collecting new paired annotations.

## Input / Output

The method consumes only RGB images (no text pairs) during finetuning, processing them through the CLIP image encoder to produce a 768-dim (or backbone-specific) embedding. This embedding is fed into a frozen unCLIP diffusion generator (operating in a 96×96×4 or 64×64×4 latent space) that attempts to reconstruct the original image. The output is an improved CLIP image embedding that preserves alignment with the original text encoder while encoding substantially more pixel-level detail, usable downstream for segmentation, classification, retrieval, and MLLM tasks.

## Method

The core insight is that unCLIP—a diffusion model conditioned on CLIP image embeddings—naturally provides the probability model p_G(x|E(x)) needed to maximize the mutual information I(x; E(x)) between an image and its CLIP embedding, subject to a language-alignment constraint. The paper formalizes this as minimizing the conditional entropy H(x|E(x)), which is equivalent to the unCLIP diffusion denoising objective. During finetuning, the pretrained unCLIP generator G is frozen while only the CLIP image encoder E is updated via the diffusion loss, effectively inverting the generator to transfer its rich visual-distribution knowledge into the encoder. Because G's input space is already aligned with CLIP's image-text embedding space, no projection layer is needed, and freezing G prevents the encoder's outputs from drifting away from the original text-aligned embedding space—addressing a key limitation of DIVA, which uses a misaligned text-to-image model with a trainable projector that can absorb knowledge meant for the encoder.

## Results

On the MMVP-VLM benchmark (9 visual patterns, 15 pairs each), un²CLIP achieves the best average score across all CLIP backbones: 31.9 (OpenAI ViT-L-14@224), 30.4 (ViT-L-14@336), 33.3–36.3 (OpenCLIP ViT-H-14@224), and 41.5 (SigLIP ViT-SO-14@384), versus 19.3 for the original CLIP. On open-vocabulary semantic segmentation, un²CLIP yields the largest gains: ClearCLIP average mIoU improves from 30.8 to 34.3, SCLIP from 16.1 to 23.0, and MaskCLIP from 12.7 to 15.3. On MLLM vision-centric benchmarks (LLaVA-1.5 + Vicuna-7B), un²CLIP achieves the best scores on MMVP (31.3), NaturalBench Q-Acc (68.7), CV-Bench2D COCO (53.9), and CV-Bench3D (65.1). Zero-shot classification accuracy drops (e.g., IN-1K: 75.5→62.4), which the authors attribute to the model correcting background-as-foreground errors rather than losing image-text alignment.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the original CLIP, un²CLIP delivers large gains on all detail-sensitive tasks: +12.6 avg on MMVP-VLM (31.9 vs 19.3 for ViT-L-14@224), +7.9 mIoU on SCLIP segmentation (23.0 vs 16.1), and +3.5 mIoU on ClearCLIP (34.3 vs 30.8). Against DIVA (which uses a text-to-image generator with a trainable projector), un²CLIP consistently wins: DIVA shows only marginal improvements over original CLIP (e.g., SCLIP 16.2 vs 16.1; ClearCLIP 30.7 vs 30.8), while un²CLIP achieves substantially larger gains, confirming that the CLIP-embedding-aligned unCLIP generator provides a stronger training signal. Against the contemporaneous GenHancer (which trains a small generator from scratch), un²CLIP clearly outperforms on segmentation (e.g., SCLIP 23.0 vs 8.6; ClearCLIP 34.3 vs 21.1) where GenHancer actually degrades performance below the original CLIP, and matches or slightly exceeds GenHancer on MMVP-VLM (31.9 vs 25.9 for ViT-L-14@224). On zero-shot classification, both un²CLIP and GenHancer drop relative to original CLIP, but un²CLIP's drop is smaller (IN-1K: 62.4 vs GenHancer's 40.2) and un²CLIP improves retrieval (Flickr30K I2T@5: 90.1 vs 87.3 original; T2I@5: 65.5 vs 61.0).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on CLIP's contrastive image-text pretraining and unCLIP's CLIP-embedding-conditioned diffusion generator, proposing to invert the latter to improve the former. It departs from DIVA, which uses a text-to-image model (Stable Diffusion) whose input space mismatches CLIP's output, requiring a trainable projector that the authors argue steals knowledge from the generator. It also differs from the contemporaneous GenHancer, which trains small generative models from scratch rather than leveraging a large pretrained one. The key conceptual contribution is recognizing that unCLIP's input space is already aligned with CLIP's image-text embedding space, enabling a projector-free finetuning objective derived from mutual information maximization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method offers a simple, image-only upstream improvement to CLIP that consistently benefits dense prediction and vision-centric multimodal tasks across multiple CLIP backbones, without requiring region-text annotations or architectural modifications at inference time. However, the notable zero-shot classification drop (e.g., 75.5→62.4 on ImageNet-1K) raises open questions about the trade-off between detail capture and category-level discrimination, and whether this is acceptable for downstream users. The requirement of a pretrained unCLIP model (itself trained on ~200K A100 hours) is a practical dependency that limits immediate adoption for novel CLIP variants. Future work could explore whether the approach extends to text-side improvements or whether the classification drop can be mitigated with auxiliary losses.
