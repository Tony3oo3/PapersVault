## Problem

Model quantization has not been explored for video super-resolution (VSR), despite being critical for deploying VSR networks on resource-constrained edge devices. Existing image SR quantization methods cannot be directly transferred to VSR due to three fundamental challenges: temporal error propagation in recurrent architectures, compounded quantization effects from shared temporal parameterization, and the inability of conventional image quality metrics to capture temporal consistency degradation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254449/cropped_page2_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a low-resolution video clip (4× downsampling, with bicubic or blur-downsampling degradation) as input and produces the corresponding high-resolution video frames. Calibration requires only 100 unlabeled LR video clips sampled from REDS or Vimeo-90K—no ground-truth HR videos are needed. During inference, the quantized model dynamically adjusts activation bit-widths per video based on a computed spatiotemporal complexity score, while layer-wise bit-widths remain fixed after calibration.

## Method

QBasicVSR is a post-training quantization framework with two adaptive bit-allocation modules. The Flow-Gradient Video Bit Adaptation (FG-VBA) module computes a spatiotemporal complexity metric combining per-frame spatial gradient magnitudes with bidirectional optical-flow (SPyNet) magnitude and Jacobian-based consistency terms, then maps the score to a global bit factor (+1/0/−1) via learned thresholds. The Temporal Shared Layer Bit Adaptation (TS-LBA) module computes per-layer spatial sensitivity (channel-wise standard deviation) and temporal sensitivity (temporal standard deviation) from calibration activations, assigning layer-wise bit factors via joint 2-D thresholding. The final per-layer bit-width is b_base + b_v + b_L^k. A three-stage fine-tuning procedure then optimizes weight clipping, activation clipping, and bit-adaptation parameters sequentially using a Charbonnier-inspired reconstruction loss and a temporal-aware L2-normalized feature transfer loss, with the full-precision model serving as a teacher (knowledge distillation) and STE/tanh approximations handling non-differentiable quantization operations.

## Results

On REDS4, the 6-bit partial-quantized model (Ours-PQ, 1.3M params) achieves 31.26 dB PSNR / 0.8879 SSIM, surpassing the full-precision BasicVSR-lite (30.56 dB) by 0.70 dB while using only 6-bit weights/activations. On UDM10 (BD), the 4-bit fully-quantized model (Ours-FQ, 0.7M params) reaches 37.59 dB, a 2.53 dB improvement over the best static PTQ baseline (Percentile, 35.06 dB) and 1.02 dB over DBDC+FT (36.82 dB). The method requires only 100 unlabeled video clips and 90 minutes of processing on 1 GPU, compared to 370 hours on 8 GPUs for SSL, yielding a ~247× speedup in tuning time and 8 ms runtime per frame.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against efficient VSR methods, Ours-PQ (6-bit) outperforms SSL [42] by +0.20 dB on REDS4 (31.26 vs 31.06) and +0.25 dB on Vimeo-90K-T BI (37.07 vs 36.82), while also exceeding KSNet [21] by +0.12 dB on REDS4 (31.26 vs 31.14) with fewer parameters (1.3M vs 1.6M). Against static PTQ baselines at 4-bit, Ours-FQ achieves 30.26 dB on REDS4, surpassing MinMax+FT (29.21 dB) by +1.05 dB, Percentile+FT (28.30 dB) by +1.96 dB, and DBDC+FT (29.24 dB) by +1.02 dB. On UDM10 (BD), the 4-bit FQ model reaches 37.59 dB, a 2.53 dB gain over Percentile (35.06 dB) and 0.77 dB over DBDC+FT (36.82 dB). The method also outperforms the full-precision BasicVSR-lite [42] (30.56 dB on REDS4) by 0.70 dB at 6-bit PQ, demonstrating that adaptive quantization can exceed the accuracy of a larger FP model. In terms of efficiency, the method requires only 90 min on 1 GPU versus 370 hrs on 8 GPUs for SSL [42], a ~247× reduction in tuning time, while achieving 8 ms per-frame runtime versus 54 ms for SSL.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the post-training quantization (PTQ) paradigm from image super-resolution to video super-resolution, building directly on DBDC [38] (the first PTQ method for image SR) and standard calibration techniques like MinMax [20] and OMSE [8]. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of temporal awareness: two novel modules (flow-gradient video bit adaptation and temporal shared layer bit adaptation) that account for inter-frame error propagation and parameter sharing across time steps—challenges absent in single-image quantization. The fine-tuning stage repurposes the pixel transfer loss from [38] by adding a temporal dimension, and uses the full-precision model as a teacher signal rather than ground-truth labels, drawing on knowledge-distillation-style supervision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As the first quantization framework for VSR, this work opens a practical path to deploying high-quality video restoration on resource-constrained edge devices (mobile, embedded), where 32-bit FP inference is infeasible. The ×200 reduction in tuning cost and 1/8 GPU requirement compared to SOTA efficient VSR training (SSL) make model compression dramatically more accessible. However, the method is validated only on BasicVSR and its unidirectional variant; generalization to more complex architectures (BasicVSR++, VRT, transformer-based VSR) remains untested. Additionally, the adaptive bit-width mechanism (FAB varying from ~4 to ~7.5 bits) complicates hardware deployment compared to fixed-precision quantization, and the reliance on SPyNet for flow estimation introduces additional inference overhead that may limit real-time applicability on very constrained devices.
