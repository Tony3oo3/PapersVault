## Problem

Pretrained text-to-image models can be misused to generate deepfakes, imitate copyrighted art styles, or produce harmful content, yet existing unlearning methods either require full retraining, modify the entire diffusion pipeline, or operate only at inference time. This paper asks whether a target concept can be precisely erased by unlearning only the foundational text-encoder artifact that produces the conditioning embedding, using exclusively negative samples and no additional training data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255182/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single text prompt describing the target concept and the pretrained CLIP text encoder (from Stable Diffusion 2.1) as input. It produces updated encoder parameters that no longer yield a coherent embedding for that prompt, while the diffusion UNet, VAE, and all other model components remain frozen. No external corpus, anchor concept, or label is required; negative samples are bootstrapped in a dropout-like fashion from the embedding vector itself, and the entire unlearning process runs for 200 epochs at 512×512 resolution with a learning rate of 1.5×10⁻⁵.

## Method

Clipout decouples the text-to-image system and optimizes only the CLIP text encoder using a negative-only contrastive loss derived from InfoNCE with all positive-sample terms removed. Given the target embedding z, the method randomly masks a fraction p (the clipout rate, default 0.25) of its dimensions to zero, producing N masked variants z⊙mᵢ that serve as negative samples; the loss maximizes dissimilarity among these variants so the embedding can no longer 'identify itself.' Theoretically, minimizing this loss is equivalent to maximizing the alignment loss for the target concept (scattering its masked variants to opposite directions on the feature hypersphere) while Lemma 3.8 and Proposition 3.11 guarantee that the uniformity of the global feature distribution is preserved, limiting side effects on unrelated concepts. Convergence is shown at O(1/√N) via the Law of Large Numbers and a bounded-support argument, and the approach requires no anchor concept, no knowledge of downstream generator layers, and no inference-time safety guidance.

## Results

On VGGFace2 with Textual Inversion, Clipout achieves a CLIP Score difference of 6.12 between the target (sks) and related (male) concepts, more than double the best baseline (SLD at 2.85), while maintaining a FDFR difference of 0.69 and near-zero ISM difference (0.00). Across DreamBooth and LoRA personalization, Clipout similarly leads with CLIP Score differences of 3.92 and 4.45, and FDFR differences of 0.91 and 0.81, respectively. On the I2P benchmark (Sexual category), Clipout reduces the unsafe proportion to 11.06%, outperforming SLD (14.97%) and the clean Stable Diffusion baseline (28.54%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Clipout outperforms all six baselines across every personalization method and metric. Against the three primary diffusion-model unlearning baselines, ASD (Kumari et al., 2023) achieves the weakest CLIP Score separation (0.24–2.21) and often fails to reduce ISM meaningfully; ESD (Gandikota et al., 2023) shows moderate CLIP Score separation (1.51–2.23) but poor FDFR separation (0.01–0.11), indicating side effects on related concepts; SLD (Schramowski et al., 2023) is the strongest of the three with CLIP Score differences up to 2.85, yet still trails Clipout by 2–3×. The unlearnable-data defense FSMG (Le et al., 2023) performs worst overall, with CLIP Score differences below 1.80 and inconsistent FDFR/ISM results. In the supplementary comparison (Table 7), the continual-learning method SA (Heng et al., 2023) and the unified editing method UCE (Gandikota et al., 2024) achieve FDFR differences of only 0.18 and 0.25 respectively, far below Clipout's 0.91, confirming that encoder-level unlearning via contrastive masking is substantially more effective than full-model fine-tuning or inference-time guidance approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

Clipout departs from prior concept-erasure methods for diffusion models (ASD, ESD, SLD) that fine-tune the full generative pipeline, instead decoupling the system and modifying only the CLIP text encoder. Conceptually, it inverts the InfoNCE contrastive objective by discarding positive samples entirely and treating randomly masked variants of the target embedding as negatives, thereby pushing the concept's representation apart from itself. The theoretical grounding draws on the alignment/uniformity framework of Wang & Isola (2020) and Huang et al. (2023a), but applies it in the opposite direction: maximizing alignment loss for the target while preserving uniformity globally.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The decoupling strategy yields a substantial efficiency advantage (only the text encoder is optimized, not the full diffusion model), making targeted unlearning practical for large VLMs without retraining. However, the unlearning direction is uncontrollable—erased concepts degrade into random landscapes rather than being redirected to a benign alternative—and adversarial prompting (Ring-A-Bell) reduces FDFR from 0.94 to 0.58, revealing a meaningful robustness gap. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond CLIP-based encoders, how sequential unlearning of many concepts accumulates drift, and whether the negative-only objective can be made directionally controllable.
