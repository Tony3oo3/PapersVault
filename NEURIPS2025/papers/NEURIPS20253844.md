## Problem

Existing generative depth estimation methods (e.g., Marigold) require full-parameter fine-tuning of pre-trained text-to-image diffusion models, which catastrophically degrades the original image generation capability. The paper asks whether a fixed T2I model can be extended to depth estimation without any degradation of its inherent generation ability, using minimal additional parameters.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253844/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For depth estimation, the model takes an RGB image as conditioning input (processed through a doubled-channel patchify layer in the VAE latent space) and produces a depth map via iterative denoising; for generation, it takes a text prompt and produces an image. The same architecture switches between modes by simply skipping or running the pluggable converters, with no architectural change required. The model also generalizes to surface normal estimation by retraining the converters on normal map data.

## Method

MERGE introduces a play-and-plug framework that inserts learnable converter blocks (initialized from the corresponding T2I block) before each transformer layer of a fixed pre-trained DiT-based T2I model, transforming latent features from the generation task into the depth estimation task. A Group Reuse Mechanism (GRE) divides T2I blocks into groups and shares a single converter within each group, halving the additional parameters with minimal performance loss. Empirical analysis further simplifies converters by removing Cross-Attention (redundant with empty prompts) and reducing FFN expansion, cutting another ~25% of parameters. Only the converters are trained (12–18% of the base model size) on 74K synthetic depth samples for 30K iterations, while the pre-trained T2I weights remain frozen, preserving generation quality exactly.

## Results

MERGE-L (FLUX-based, 1.4B params, 12% additional) achieves 5.9 A.Rel and 95.4% δ1 on NYUv2, 7.1 A.Rel and 94.0% δ1 on ScanNet, and 31.4 A.Rel and 76.3% δ1 on DIODE, using only 74K training samples. MERGE-B (PixArt-based, 110M params, 18% additional) achieves 7.5 A.Rel and 94.2% δ1 on NYUv2. Notably, MERGE-L surpasses OneDiffusion (trained from scratch on 100M samples) by 0.9 A.Rel on NYUv2 and 1.1% δ1 on DIODE while requiring only ~12% additional parameters and 1/1350th the training data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among unified generation+depth methods, MERGE-B substantially outperforms JointNet (7.5 vs. 13.7 A.Rel on NYUv2) and UniCon (7.5 vs. 7.9 A.Rel), while MERGE-L surpasses OneDiffusion by 0.9 A.Rel on NYUv2 (5.9 vs. 6.8) and 1.1% δ1 on DIODE (76.3% vs. 75.2%), all with far fewer parameters and 1/1350th the training data. Against full-parameter fine-tuning (Marigold-P), MERGE-B achieves nearly identical NYUv2 performance (7.5 vs. 7.4 A.Rel) with only 18% additional parameters, and MERGE-B-28 (37% params) actually beats Marigold-P (7.0 vs. 7.4 A.Rel). Compared to low-rank fine-tuning (LoRA, DoRA) at matched parameter count (110M), MERGE-B wins on all three benchmarks (e.g., 7.5 vs. 8.7 A.Rel on NYUv2). However, against the strongest discriminative method DepthAnything v2, MERGE-L still trails significantly on DIODE (31.4 vs. 6.5 A.Rel) but narrows the gap on NYUv2 (5.9 vs. 4.4 A.Rel). For normal estimation (Table 4), MERGE-L achieves 20.1° mean angular error on NYUv2, competitive with Lotus (16.5°) and StableNormal (18.6°) while uniquely preserving T2I generation.

## Relation to Prior Work

MERGE departs from the Marigold paradigm of full-parameter fine-tuning a T2I diffusion model for depth estimation (which irreversibly destroys generation ability) and from parallel dual-model architectures like JointNet and UniCon that couple two separate diffusion models. Instead, it introduces a play-and-plug framework where lightweight, learnable converters are inserted before frozen T2I blocks (PixArt or FLUX), enabling seamless switching between generation and depth estimation modes. The Group Reuse Mechanism, motivated by observed feature similarity between adjacent T2I blocks, further reduces parameter overhead by sharing converters within groups. This positions MERGE as a parameter-efficient alternative to both full fine-tuning (Marigold) and data-hungry from-scratch training (OneDiffusion, 100M samples).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MERGE offers a practical, low-cost recipe (~12% additional parameters, 74K training samples) for extending any frozen DiT-based T2I model with depth estimation while preserving its original generation quality — a property no prior generative depth method achieves. The play-and-plug converter design is architecturally simple and could generalize to other dense prediction tasks (the paper demonstrates normal estimation), making it a reusable building block for unified generation-perception models. However, the method still trails strong discriminative baselines (DepthAnything v2) on challenging outdoor benchmarks like DIODE, and the authors acknowledge that extending the approach to tasks requiring discrete outputs (e.g., semantic segmentation) remains an open challenge due to the stochastic nature of diffusion sampling.
