## Problem

LLM-based anonymization methods that defend against inference-based privacy attacks rely on proprietary models (e.g., GPT-4) at inference time, incurring high costs and exposing sensitive text to untrusted external systems. Prior distillation attempts remain limited and still depend on GPT-4 for adversarial feedback. The paper addresses how to train small, locally deployable language models that can both anonymize text and iteratively refine their own outputs without any external model at inference time.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255520/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural-language text (e.g., a social media comment) and a set of target private attributes (age, gender, location, occupation, etc.) as input, and produces an anonymized text along with structured outputs: inferred private attributes with confidence scores and utility scores across readability, semantic preservation, and hallucination. During self-refinement, the model consumes its own previous anonymization plus its own privacy and utility evaluations as conditioning signals for the next rewrite, iterating until a desired privacy-utility trade-off is reached or a fixed step limit is hit.

## Method

SEAL is a two-stage distillation framework. First, adversarial trajectories are synthesized by alternating an LLM anonymizer, an attribute-inference adversary, and a utility evaluator over multiple steps, collecting tuples of (text, inferred attributes, utility scores). In the SFT stage, a small language model (e.g., Llama3-8B via LoRA) is jointly trained on three tasks: anonymization (mapping a text to a more private rewrite), adversarial inference (predicting private attributes from text), and utility evaluation (scoring readability, meaning preservation, and hallucination). In the DPO stage, preference pairs are constructed from trajectory steps where later anonymizations achieve strictly better privacy and non-worse utility, and the model is fine-tuned with direct preference optimization to prefer higher-quality anonymizations. At inference, the single trained model performs iterative self-refinement by alternating between generating a refined anonymization and evaluating its own output, eliminating the need for any external proprietary model.

## Results

On the main SynthPAI dataset, Llama3-8B trained with SEAL achieves a privacy score of 0.391 (lower is better) after a single anonymization step, outperforming GPT-4o adversarial anonymization (0.434) while maintaining comparable utility (0.931 vs. 0.947). With three iterations of self-refinement, SEAL reduces privacy to 0.263 and achieves an overall privacy-utility trade-off score of 0.441, substantially surpassing GPT-4o (0.253). On the harder dataset with contextually embedded PII, SEAL surpasses all adversarial anonymization baselines after the second refinement iteration, reaching an overall score of 0.298 versus GPT-4o's 0.272.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SEAL (Llama3-8B) outperforms all three adversarial anonymization baselines (GPT-4o, GPT-4o mini, Gemini 2.5 Flash) on privacy after just one anonymization step (0.391 vs. 0.424–0.434) while maintaining comparable utility (0.931 vs. 0.927–0.947), and widens the privacy gap substantially with self-refinement (0.263 at iter 3 vs. 0.434 for GPT-4o). The overall privacy-utility trade-off score for SEAL at iter 3 (0.441) is nearly 1.7× that of GPT-4o (0.253). On the harder dataset with contextually embedded PII, SEAL slightly lags GPT-4o after one iteration (0.609 vs. 0.568 privacy) but surpasses all baselines by iteration 2 (0.540 vs. 0.568) and iteration 3 (0.505 vs. 0.568). The rule-based Azure PII detector and the Dipper paraphrasing model are largely ineffective, providing minimal privacy gains (0.587 and 0.555 vs. 0.625 original) with Dipper incurring a substantial utility loss (0.868). Ablations confirm that combining SFT with DPO, joint training on anonymization and critique tasks, adversarial feedback, and confidence-aware privacy scoring each contribute incremental gains, with the full configuration achieving the best privacy of 0.379 on the main set.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the adversarial anonymization framework of Staab et al. (ICLR 2025), where an LLM anonymizer and an adversary model iteratively refine text to prevent attribute inference, and on DPO (Rafailov et al., NeurIPS 2023) for preference-based alignment. The key departure is a distillation framework (SEAL) that transfers both the anonymization and the adversarial-inference/critique capabilities from a frontier LLM into small language models via SFT followed by DPO, so that at inference time a single local model performs both generation and self-evaluation without any external API calls. Prior distillation attempts (e.g., Yang et al., 2024) still relied on GPT-4 for adversarial feedback at inference; SEAL eliminates that dependency entirely.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for privacy-sensitive deployments: an 8B model running on a single GPU achieves a privacy-utility trade-off comparable to (and with self-refinement, surpassing) GPT-4o at roughly 1% of the inference cost, while keeping all data local. A notable finding is that models trained on 3-step trajectories generalize to 5+ self-refinement iterations, suggesting the learned critique capability is not merely memorizing a fixed procedure. Open questions include whether the approach scales to longer or more complex inputs, whether the self-refinement loop can be made more efficient (e.g., via generative reward modeling), and whether the distill-and-self-critique paradigm transfers cleanly to other multi-objective text tasks like summarization or code generation.
