## Problem

Deepfake detectors face a perceived trade-off between demographic fairness and cross-domain generalization: prior work shows that pushing for fairness compromises generalizability and vice versa. This paper challenges that assumption by formally establishing, via causal inference, that fairness causally improves generalization once confounders (data distribution and model capacity) are properly controlled.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251753/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 224×224 RGB face crops and outputs a binary real/fake probability. During training, each sample is annotated with a demographic subgroup (gender × race, yielding six intersectional groups) that drives reweighting and feature alignment; at inference, no demographic information is required. DAID is plug-and-play: it inserts two lightweight modules between the backbone encoder and the existing classification head, preserving the original architecture and adding under 5% training overhead.

## Method

DAID operationalizes a back-door causal intervention on fairness to boost generalization by controlling two confounders. The first module (Demographic-aware Data Rebalancing) applies inverse-propensity sample weighting to equalize subgroup contributions to the loss and performs per-subgroup feature normalization to remove distributional shifts, thereby neutralizing the data-distribution confounder. The second module (Demographic-Agnostic Feature Aggregation) enforces cosine-similarity alignment between same-label samples from different demographic groups through a trainable low-rank orthonormal projection, with a Frobenius-norm orthogonality regularizer to prevent trivial collapse, thereby suppressing the model-capacity confounder. The total training objective is a weighted binary cross-entropy plus the alignment and orthogonality losses, optimized end-to-end with AdamW. The causal analysis (Section 3) stratifies data by demographic intersection and model architecture, then estimates the Average Causal Effect of fairness on AUC via the back-door adjustment formula, yielding a statistically significant positive ACE of 2.35 percentage points.

## Results

DAID achieves the best performance on both fairness (Skew↓) and generalization (AUC↑) across all three cross-domain benchmarks. On DFDC, DAID reaches 66.85% AUC with a Skew of 1.460; on DFD, 91.15% AUC with Skew 0.263; and on Celeb-DF, 84.39% AUC with Skew 0.289. Notably, on Celeb-DF, DAID improves fairness by 26% over the best-performing baseline while simultaneously achieving the highest AUC, demonstrating that fairness and generalization need not be in conflict when confounders are properly controlled.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DAID outperforms all 13 baselines on both Skew and AUC simultaneously across all three datasets. Against the strongest generalization baselines, DAID beats ProDet (65.89% AUC on DFDC, 89.18% on DFD, 82.71% on Celeb-DF) by 0.96, 1.97, and 1.68 AUC points respectively, while also achieving substantially lower Skew (e.g., 1.460 vs. 2.306 on DFDC). Against VLFFD, the best AUC on DFD (90.08%), DAID gains 1.07 AUC points and reduces Skew from 0.669 to 0.263. The fairness-oriented baselines DAW-FDD and FG improve Skew over vanilla detectors but sacrifice generalization severely (e.g., FG drops to 68.30% AUC on Celeb-DF vs. DAID's 84.39%), confirming the trade-off that DAID's causal framework resolves. On Celeb-DF, DAID's Skew of 0.289 represents a 26% improvement over the best baseline (CADDM at 0.391), while its AUC of 84.39% exceeds CADDM's 81.75% by 2.64 points.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the prevailing view in deepfake detection that fairness and cross-domain generalization are in inherent trade-off, instead proposing a causal framework (back-door adjustment) that positions fairness as a treatment variable whose intervention improves generalization. Methodologically, it repurposes standard causal inference tools (Pearl's back-door criterion) to a novel ML context, combining inverse-propensity reweighting and demographic-conditioned feature normalization (borrowed from fair representation learning) with a cosine-alignment loss in a low-rank projected subspace. Unlike prior fairness-oriented detectors (DAW-FDD, FG) that optimize fairness directly at the cost of AUC, DAID is plug-and-play on existing CNN backbones (Xception, EfficientNet, F3-Net, CADDM) and frames fairness as the mechanism through which generalization is achieved rather than a competing objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper reframes demographic fairness from a purely ethical constraint into a strategic lever for robustness, offering a theoretically grounded (causal) explanation for why fairness interventions can help generalization. However, the causal argument rests on the assumption that data distribution and model capacity constitute an exhaustive set of confounders, which the authors explicitly acknowledge as a limitation and flag for future comprehensive proof. The reliance on demographic annotations (gender × race) also limits applicability to settings where such labels are unavailable or where fairness must be defined along other axes. Open questions include whether the causal pathway holds under additional confounders, whether it extends to video-level or multi-modal deepfake detection, and whether the modest ACE (~2.35 pp) scales to more challenging cross-domain gaps.
