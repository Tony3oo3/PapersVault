## Problem

Existing cross-domain gaze estimation methods rely solely on domain-invariant representation learning (via domain generalization or unsupervised domain adaptation), but GLS theory proves this is insufficient when label shift is present. The paper identifies that CDGE inherently involves both label shift (differing gaze range and concentration across domains) and conditional shift (differing appearance given the same gaze), and that neither shift is addressed by prior work.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250179/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes labeled face images from a source domain (ETH-XGaze or Gaze360, with wider gaze distributions) plus 10% unlabeled face images from a target domain (MPIIFaceGaze or EyeDiap, with narrower gaze distributions). It outputs a 2D continuous gaze direction (azimuth, elevation) in degrees. The key distinction from classification-based label shift methods is that the gaze variable is continuous with compact support, requiring a parametric distribution model rather than per-class probability estimation.

## Method

GLSGE models the target gaze label distribution as a bivariate truncated Gaussian estimated from target pseudo labels, yielding a continuous importance weight function ω(y) that reweights the source label distribution to match the target. For conditional alignment, the paper derives a Probability-aware Conditional Operator Discrepancy (PCOD) that embeds the reweighted source conditional distribution P_{Z|Y^ω}^s into RKHS, replacing the standard empirical estimation with a weighted version that correctly accounts for the reweighted label distribution. Training alternates between estimating the reweighted label distribution and optimizing the feature extractor and predictor with a reweighted L1 loss plus PCOD conditional alignment and a DAGE-GRAM marginal alignment term. The framework is backbone-agnostic and plug-and-play, validated on ResNet-18, ResNet-50, GazeTR (ViT), and FSCI, achieving 12–27% average error reduction over baselines.

## Results

On four standard cross-domain gaze estimation tasks (ETH-XGaze→MPIIFaceGaze, ETH-XGaze→EyeDiap, Gaze360→MPIIFaceGaze, Gaze360→EyeDiap), GLSGE achieves the best average angular error of 6.06° with ResNet-18 and 6.01° with ResNet-50, outperforming all 12 baselines. The method attains the best per-task results on both MPIIFaceGaze transfer tasks (5.31° and 5.43° with ResNet-18) and reduces the baseline ResNet-18 error by 27.2% on average. When plugged into other backbones, GLSGE reduces prediction error by 26.3% (ResNet-50), 19.1% (GazeTR/ViT), and 12.1% (FSCI, a SOTA DG method).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GLSGE achieves the best average angular error across all four tasks with both ResNet-18 (6.06°) and ResNet-50 (6.01°), outperforming the strongest baselines PnP-GA+ (6.20°/6.46°) and DCUA (6.31°/6.24°). On the two MPIIFaceGaze transfer tasks, GLSGE is the clear winner (5.31° and 5.43° with ResNet-18), beating PnP-GA+ by 0.03° and 0.67° respectively. However, GLSGE underperforms on both EyeDiap transfer tasks: PnP-GA+ achieves 5.73° on D_E→D_D (vs. GLSGE's 6.21°) and DCUA achieves 6.40° on D_G→D_D (vs. GLSGE's 7.30°), suggesting the label shift correction is less effective when the target domain has a very narrow gaze range. As a plug-and-play module, GLSGE further reduces FSCI's (a SOTA DG method) error by 12.1% on average and GazeTR's (ViT-based) error by 19.1%, demonstrating broad applicability beyond its native ResNet backbone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reframes cross-domain gaze estimation (CDGE) from the dominant domain-invariant representation learning paradigm (encompassing both DG and UDA methods) to a Generalized Label Shift (GLS) correction problem, building directly on the GLS theory of Tachet des Combes et al. (NeurIPS 2020) and Luo & Ren (PAMI 2024), which prove that invariant representation learning is insufficient when label shift exists. The technical core extends COD (Yang et al., ECCV 2024) by deriving a probability-aware variant (PCOD) that embeds a reweighted source distribution into conditional alignment, and introduces a truncated Gaussian importance reweighting strategy to make label shift correction feasible for the continuous gaze variable—a gap left by classification-oriented GLS methods. The paper positions existing DG and UDA CDGE methods as partial realizations of the GLS framework that omit the label shift correction step.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean theoretical justification for why existing CDGE methods plateau: they correct conditional shift but ignore label shift, and GLS theory shows this is provably insufficient. The plug-and-play nature of GLSGE (applicable to ResNet, ViT, and SOTA DG backbones with 12–34% error reduction) makes it practically useful for the gaze estimation community. Key limitations include the requirement for unlabeled target samples (ruling out pure DG settings), the computational cost of kernel matrix operations, and the reliance on a parametric truncated Gaussian for label distribution modeling that may not capture multimodal gaze patterns (e.g., driver gaze). Open questions include whether more efficient non-kernel conditional distribution metrics can replace PCOD, and whether the GLS correction framework transfers cleanly to other continuous regression tasks like pose estimation.
