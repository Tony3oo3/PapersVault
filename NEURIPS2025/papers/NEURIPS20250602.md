## Problem

Standard attention's quadratic complexity makes long-context LLMs prohibitively expensive, yet existing efficiency solutions either impose rigid structural biases (sink/window attention) that limit generalizability or replace attention with linear approximations whose performance on complex reasoning remains unvalidated. The paper asks how to design an attention architecture that retains the Transformer framework, lets the model autonomously decide where to attend without predefined biases, and seamlessly transitions between full and sparse attention modes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250602/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

MoBA consumes standard text token sequences (up to 1M+ tokens in production) and produces generated text tokens, operating as a drop-in replacement for the attention layer within a Transformer. Internally, the KV matrices are partitioned into fixed-size blocks (e.g., 4096 tokens per block at 1M context), and each query token is routed to only a top-k subset of blocks (e.g., top-12 out of 256 blocks), yielding up to 95.31% sparsity. The method supports two inference configurations: MoBA throughout both prefill and decode, or MoBA for prefill with full attention for decode, allowing practitioners to trade off speed against generation quality.

## Method

MoBA applies Mixture-of-Experts routing to the attention mechanism: the KV context is partitioned into n blocks of size B, and a parameter-free gate computes each query's affinity to every block via the inner product between the query and the mean-pooled keys of that block. A top-k selection (with causal masking to prevent future-block attention) determines which blocks each query attends to, while the current block is always included with a causal mask—functioning analogously to shared experts in MoE. The implementation leverages FlashAttention's variable-length kernel and online softmax to combine outputs from the current block and selected historical blocks in a single pass. A key design property is that MoBA introduces no new parameters and can be swapped with full attention at any layer or training stage, enabling hybrid training recipes (e.g., MoBA for 90% of tokens then full attention for the final 10%) that match full-attention performance while substantially reducing compute.

## Results

On 16 downstream benchmarks, Llama-8B-1M-MoBA (with 95.31% attention sparsity) achieves performance highly comparable to the full-attention baseline Llama-8B-1M-Full, with most scores within 0.01–0.03. Notably, MoBA-Prefill-MoBA-Decode outperforms Full on GSM8K (0.7324 vs 0.7142), CEval (0.6298 vs 0.6165), and MMLU (0.4968 vs 0.4904), while showing a modest gap on RULER@128K (0.7690 vs 0.8031). In terms of efficiency, MoBA achieves a 6.5× prefill speedup over Flash Attention at 1M tokens and a 16× speedup at 10M tokens, demonstrating sub-quadratic scaling. Scaling law experiments confirm that MoBA and full attention follow nearly identical loss curves (within 1e-3) across model sizes from 545M to 2.1B parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is the full-attention model (Llama-8B-1M-Full), trained with identical hyperparameters and data but using standard softmax attention throughout. MoBA matches or slightly exceeds Full on the majority of benchmarks: it wins on GSM8K (+0.018), CEval (+0.013), MMLU (+0.006), and CompetitionMath (+0.013) under the MoBA-Prefill-MoBA-Decode configuration, while showing small losses on HumanEval (−0.012) and RULER@128K (−0.034). The MoBA-Prefill-Full-Decode configuration (using full attention only during generation) narrows the RULER gap and improves on code tasks like MBPPSanitized (0.6926 vs 0.6615). For efficiency, MoBA is compared against Flash Attention: at 1M tokens it achieves a 6.5× prefill speedup, and at 10M tokens a 16× speedup, confirming sub-quadratic complexity. The scaling law experiments further show that MoBA's validation loss tracks full attention within 1e-3 across all five model sizes tested, indicating no systematic performance degradation from sparsity.

## Relation to Prior Work

MoBA transplants the top-k routing paradigm of Mixture of Experts (Shazeer et al., 2017; DeepSpeed-MoE, Rajbhandari et al., 2022) from FFN layers to the attention mechanism itself, using mean-pooled block keys as 'expert' descriptors and a parameter-free inner-product gate to select which KV blocks each query attends to. It builds its high-performance kernel on FlashAttention (Dao et al., 2022) and MoE token-arrangement tricks, while deliberately departing from static sparse patterns (sink/window attention) and inference-only dynamic sparsity (Quest, Minference) by being natively trainable and seamlessly switchable to full attention. The closest concurrent work, DeepSeek NSA, also performs dynamic block selection, but MoBA's framing as an MoE analogue and its hybrid full/sparse training recipe distinguish its positioning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MoBA is already deployed in production at Moonshot AI for 1M-token workloads, and its 16× prefill speedup at 10M tokens makes it a practical path to million-token contexts without re-architecting the model. The seamless MoBA↔full-attention transition (no loss spikes, hybrid 90/10 training recipe matching full-attention loss) lowers the barrier for existing pre-trained models to adopt sparse attention. Open questions include whether the parameter-free router can be made learnable to capture more nuanced long-range patterns, whether the approach extends to multimodal inputs, and how it performs on long chain-of-thought reasoning where the paper's evidence is still limited.
