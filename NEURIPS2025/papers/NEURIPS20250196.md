## Problem

Transformer-based LLMs incur high inference costs due to the quadratic complexity of self-attention, and existing structured pruning methods (SLEB, DROP, SliceGPT) that remove attention layers or blocks entirely cause substantial accuracy degradation. The paper addresses the gap of lacking a principled, theoretically grounded strategy to *replace* redundant attention layers with simpler approximations rather than deleting them, while providing a quantitative error bound to guide which layers are safe to substitute.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250196/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained LLM (e.g., Mistral-7B, Llama-3.1-8B/70B, DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-8B) and a small calibration dataset (256 sequences from C4 or WikiText-2, context length t). During calibration, input-output activation pairs are extracted layer-by-layer from each self-attention module. The output is a compressed LLM of identical architecture where m selected attention layers are swapped for single linear layers (weight W ∈ R^{h_out×h_in} and bias b), reducing KV-cache memory by a factor of (K−m)/K and lowering prefill complexity from O(K·n²d) to O((K−m)·n²d + m·nd). No fine-tuning or gradient updates are performed after substitution.

## Method

NBL frames attention-layer replacement as a channel estimation problem: for each self-attention layer, it collects input X and output Y activations from a calibration set, then computes the closed-form LMMSE estimator W = C_YX·C_XX^{−1} and b = E[Y] − W·E[X] to produce a linear surrogate. To decide *which* layers to replace, the paper derives a CCA-based upper bound on the normalized MSE of the linear approximation: NMSE ≤ (h_out − r) + Σᵢ(1 − ρᵢ²), where ρᵢ are the canonical correlations between X and Y. Layers with the smallest bound (i.e., highest canonical correlations, indicating strong linear input-output alignment) are selected for substitution. The residual connection is preserved in the compressed layer, and the CCA bound is preferred over direct NMSE because it provides a variance-agnostic, mode-wise indicator that empirically yields more stable layer selection. The entire procedure is calibration-only (no backpropagation), with per-layer runtime scaling as O(d³ + s·t·d²), enabling 8B-model compression in under an hour.

## Results

On DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-8B, applying NBL to 12 attention layers (Attn NBL-12) achieves 62.4 average accuracy (vs. 63.6 baseline, <1% loss) while improving throughput by 32% (1.32×) and prefill by 24% (1.24×). On Mistral-7B, Attn NBL-8 retains the full baseline accuracy of 70.0 (vs. 70.2 baseline) with 1.20× throughput, and Attn NBL-16 scores 58.8 average accuracy with 1.37× throughput. On Llama-3.1-70B (4-bit AWQ quantized), Attn NBL-32 matches the baseline accuracy of 76.0 exactly while delivering 1.24× throughput. Calibration runtime scales linearly with model size: ~0.23 hr for 8B, ~1.75 hr for 70B, and ~13 hr for 405B parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NBL consistently outperforms all four baselines at comparable compression levels. On Mistral-7B, Attn NBL-8 (70.0 avg) exceeds Attn DROP-8 (69.4), SLEB-8 (51.8), SliceGPT-25% (55.0), and PruneNet-25% (55.0) by 0.6–18.2 points. The gap widens at higher compression: Attn NBL-16 (58.8) beats Attn DROP-16 (52.9) by 5.9 points, SLEB-12 (43.5) by 15.3 points, SliceGPT-35% (46.4) by 12.4 points, and PruneNet-35% (34.2) by 24.6 points. On DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-8B, Attn NBL-12 (62.4) outperforms Attn DROP-12 (60.7) by 1.7 points while achieving higher throughput (1.32× vs. 1.34× prefill). On Llama-3.1-70B, Attn NBL-32 (76.0) matches the quantized baseline exactly and surpasses SLEB-16 (70.4) by 5.6 points at comparable throughput (1.24× vs. 1.24×). The key advantage of NBL over DROP is that it replaces rather than removes layers, preserving residual information and avoiding the abrupt performance cliffs seen in layer-dropping methods at moderate compression.

## Relation to Prior Work

NBL sits at the intersection of two classical estimation-theory tools—LMMSE (Kay, 1993) and Canonical Correlation Analysis (Hotelling, 1992)—and repurposes them for a modern deep-learning problem: replacing self-attention layers in pretrained LLMs with closed-form linear maps. It departs from the dominant structured-pruning line (SLEB, DROP, SliceGPT, PruneNet), which *removes* layers or blocks and often suffers sharp accuracy drops, by instead *substituting* them with linear approximations that preserve the residual pathway. It also differs from subquadratic linear-attention architectures (Katharopoulos et al., Mercat et al., Zhang et al.), which require architectural redesign and retraining, because NBL operates post-hoc on frozen weights with no gradient-based optimization. The CCA-derived NMSE upper bound (Theorem 3.2) is a new theoretical contribution that provides a variance-agnostic, mode-wise criterion for layer selection, going beyond the cosine-distance or output-norm heuristics used by DROP and SLEB.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

NBL offers a training-free, theoretically grounded compression path that is straightforward to deploy on any pretrained transformer and composes naturally with quantization (demonstrated on AWQ-quantized Llama-3.1-70B) and speculative decoding (EAGLE-3, up to 4.07× combined speedup). The CCA-based bound outperforming direct NMSE for layer selection (68.3% vs 39.1% average accuracy on Mistral-7B after 12-layer replacement) is a practically important finding: it shows that a variance-agnostic, per-mode criterion is more reliable than a single scalar error for identifying safely linearizable layers. Open questions include reducing sensitivity to calibration distribution, extending the criterion to account for QKV structural properties or residual-connection norms, and exploring hybrid designs that retain a subset of critical attention heads within a linearized layer. The method's linearity assumption inherently limits how aggressively one can compress, and indiscriminate application to all layers would degrade performance substantially.
