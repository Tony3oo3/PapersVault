## Problem

Existing safe meta-RL methods (Meta-CRPO, Meta-CPO) fail to guarantee anytime safety during the meta-test phase—only the final or post-adaptation policies are provably safe, while intermediate exploration policies may violate constraints. They also lack provable near-optimality bounds for the adapted policies and incur high computational cost from bilevel optimization with Hessian and Hessian-inverse computations. This paper addresses all three gaps simultaneously.

## Input / Output

The method takes a learned softmax meta-policy (neural network with two hidden layers of size 64), a new task's CMDP parameters (reward, cost functions, and constraint thresholds), and a single batch of state-action data collected by the meta-policy on that task. It produces a task-specific safe policy in closed form (no iterative optimization) and, when applied iteratively, a sequence of policies each guaranteed to satisfy all safety constraints. The key structural property is that the policy adaptation reduces to a simple reweighting of the meta-policy's log-probabilities by the advantage functions, making the output directly computable from the input data without solving a constrained optimization problem.

## Method

The framework has two modules. First, a one-step safe policy adaptation problem (inspired by CPO but replacing √D_KL with D_KL) admits a closed-form softmax solution parameterized by Lagrangian multipliers, which are recovered by solving a low-dimensional convex dual via projected gradient descent; this guarantees monotonic reward improvement and zero constraint violation at every adaptation step. Second, the safe meta-policy training maximizes the expected reward of the one-step-adapted policy subject to safety constraints on the meta-policy itself, using a Hessian-free meta-gradient derived from the closed-form solution (approximating ∇_φ u* = 0 to avoid second-order terms) and TRPO for stable updates with a CRPO-style constraint-handling loop. The critical technical insight is that the D_KL (rather than √D_KL) substitution in the trust-region constraint is what unlocks the closed-form policy update, which in turn eliminates the Hessian computations that make Meta-CPO expensive, reducing meta-training complexity to that of single-level policy gradient.

## Results

Across seven locomotion and navigation benchmarks, the proposed method achieves approximately 50% higher accumulated reward than the best-performing baseline during both meta-training and meta-test phases. In terms of computational efficiency, the method reduces meta-training time by ~70% and meta-testing time by ~50% relative to meta-CPO. Critically, the method achieves zero constraint violation (anytime safety) for every policy in the adaptation sequence, whereas all baselines exhibit positive constraint violations at various adaptation stages. The optimality-safety trade-off is confirmed empirically: increasing the allowable constraint violation δ_{c_i} from 0 to 3.0 improves reward but increases constraint violation, consistent with the theoretical bound in Theorem 1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against three baselines: MAML with constraint penalty, meta-CPO [12], and meta-CRPO [24]. Against meta-CPO, the proposed method wins decisively on all three axes: it achieves ~50% higher accumulated reward, guarantees zero constraint violation (vs. positive violations for meta-CPO), and is ~70% faster in meta-training and ~50% faster in meta-testing due to the closed-form policy adaptation and Hessian-free meta-gradient. Against meta-CRPO, the proposed method achieves substantially higher accumulated reward (meta-CRPO has the worst experimental optimality per Table 1) and provides anytime safety, whereas meta-CRPO only bounds constraint violation for the final convergent policy. Against MAML with constraint penalty, the proposed method outperforms on both optimality and safety, as MAML's penalty-based approach cannot guarantee constraint satisfaction at any intermediate adaptation step. Notably, meta-CPO's meta-training is ~10× slower than the proposed method on the high-dimensional Humanoid task, making repeated runs impractical.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the CPO trust-region framework for safe policy optimization, the MAML inner-outer loop structure for meta-learning, and the CRPO constraint-handling mechanism, but makes a key conceptual departure: by replacing CPO's sqrt(D_KL) trust region with a linear D_KL penalty, the authors obtain a closed-form softmax solution for the one-step safe policy adaptation. This closed-form expression then enables a Hessian-free meta-gradient (Proposition 3), fundamentally distinguishing the approach from Meta-CPO, which requires solving a constrained bilevel optimization with Hessian inverses at every meta-training step. The paper also departs from Meta-CRPO and Meta-CPO by imposing safety constraints directly on the meta-policy and proving zero constraint violation for every intermediate policy during adaptation, rather than only bounding violation for the final convergent policy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The anytime safety guarantee (zero constraint violation for every exploration policy, not just the final one) is the first such result in safe meta-RL and is critical for real-world deployment where every intermediate policy interacts with the environment. The near-optimality bound (Theorem 1) and the explicit optimality-safety trade-off parameterized by δ_ci provide practitioners with a principled knob to balance reward maximization against strict constraint enforcement. Limitations include evaluation only on relatively low-dimensional Gym and Safety-Gymnasium benchmarks, reliance on the task variance Var(P(Γ)) being small for tight bounds, and the approximation ∇_φ u* = 0 in the meta-gradient whose error is not formally bounded. Open questions include whether the closed-form adaptation extends to non-softmax policies or to the stricter 'stay-in-safe-region' safety metric, and whether the framework scales to high-dimensional continuous control with many constraints.
