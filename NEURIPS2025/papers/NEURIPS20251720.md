## Problem

Actor-Critic algorithms parameterize policies with DNNs and improve them via gradient-based operators, which are inherently slow (low greedification) compared to operators like argmax in Q-learning. While slower updates aid training stability, they limit how quickly the policy can improve, creating a direct tradeoff between greedification and stability that existing two-step decoupling approaches (e.g., SAC, PPO) do not fully resolve because the parameterized acting policy still relies on gradient-based optimization.

## Input / Output

The method consumes continuous state observations from dm_control environments and produces actions via a DNN-parameterized acting policy (stochastic for SAC, deterministic for TD3). The key structural difference is that the critic evaluates a separate, greedier policy rather than the acting policy itself: this evaluated policy can be produced by an explicit operator (e.g., deterministic policy gradient with multiple gradient steps, or Best-of-N sampling) or an implicit operator (e.g., asymmetric expectile loss), and is discarded after each use without being stored as a separate network.

## Method

Value-Improved Actor Critic (VIAC) decouples the acting policy from the evaluated policy, applying a slow gradient-based improvement operator (I₁) to the acting policy and a greedier operator (I₂) to the evaluated policy whose value is used to bootstrap the critic's targets. Theoretically, the paper proves that policy improvement alone is insufficient for convergence of Policy Iteration with stochastic policies (Theorem 2), then identifies necessary and sufficient conditions—lower-bounded greedification and limit-sufficient greedification—that guarantee convergence of both standard and Value-Improved Generalized Policy Iteration in finite-horizon MDPs. Empirically, VI-TD3 and VI-SAC incorporate value improvement via the asymmetric expectile loss (implicit, tunable via parameter τ) or Best-of-N sampling (explicit), and significantly outperform or match their baselines across all tested dm_control environments with negligible additional compute cost.

## Results

Across all DeepMind continuous control (dm_control) environments tested, VI-TD3 and VI-SAC with implicit value improvement (expectile loss, τ=0.75) significantly outperform or match their respective baselines (TD3 and SAC) with negligible additional compute and implementation cost. Performance increases monotonically with the greedification parameter τ up to τ=0.8, after which it degrades, confirming a tunable tradeoff between greedification and stability. A Best-of-N (N=64) explicit improvement operator yields similar gains to the expectile operator, demonstrating that the performance improvement is attributable to value improvement rather than the specific operator used.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three baselines. Against TD3 (Fujimoto et al., 2018), VI-TD3 with expectile loss (τ=0.75) significantly outperforms the baseline across all tested dm_control environments, with the gap widening in more complex tasks. Against SAC (Haarnoja et al., 2018b), VI-SAC similarly significantly outperforms or matches baseline SAC in all environments. Against TD7 (Fujimoto et al., 2023), the paper reports that incorporating value improvement into TD7 yields similar performance gains, suggesting the benefit generalizes beyond TD3 and SAC. The BoN variant (N=64) of VI-TD3 provides comparable gains to the expectile-based variant, ruling out the possibility that gains are unique to the expectile operator. In all cases, the additional compute cost is negligible since value improvement is achieved through a single modified loss function rather than additional gradient steps.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Actor-Critic framework by decoupling the acting policy from the evaluated policy, going beyond prior work (SAC, MPO, Gumbel MuZero) that only decoupled policy improvement into a target-production and regression step while still improving the acting policy via gradient descent. Theoretically, it builds on the Generalized Policy Iteration analysis framework (Tsitsiklis, 2002; Smirnova & Dohmatob, 2019) but extends convergence guarantees from deterministic to stochastic policies by identifying necessary and sufficient conditions on greedification operators. Empirically, it repurposes the asymmetric expectile loss from IQL (Kostrikov et al., 2022) and the Best-of-N operator as value-improvement mechanisms within the online off-policy algorithms TD3 and SAC.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical result that policy improvement alone is insufficient for convergence (Theorem 2) challenges a common implicit assumption in RL and provides the first necessary-and-sufficient conditions for designing greedification operators that guarantee convergence with stochastic policies. Practically, the finding that value improvement via expectile loss or BoN significantly improves TD3/SAC with negligible compute overhead offers a drop-in enhancement for a wide range of off-policy AC algorithms. Key limitations include the restriction of convergence proofs to finite-horizon MDPs with finite action spaces, and the empirical evaluation is confined to dm_control continuous control tasks, leaving open whether the approach transfers to discrete-action, high-dimensional, or infinite-horizon settings.
