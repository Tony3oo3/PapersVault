## Problem

In the symmetric stochastic block model, polynomial-time algorithms achieve constant correlation (weak recovery) above the Kesten-Stigum threshold ε²d ≥ k², but no such algorithms are known below it. Prior low-degree hardness results only ruled out algorithms succeeding with 1−o(1) probability, leaving open whether constant-probability recovery algorithms might exist below the threshold. This paper provides the first rigorous evidence (conditional on the low-degree conjecture) for a sharp transition in the achievable recovery *rate* at the KS threshold, ruling out even n^{-0.49} correlation for sub-exponential-time algorithms below it.

## Input / Output

The method takes an n×n binary adjacency matrix Y of a random graph along with model parameters (n, d, ε, k) and produces a binary testing statistic f(Y) ∈ {0,1} that separates the SBM distribution from the Erdős-Rényi null distribution. The statistic is constructed by splitting edges into two subgraphs, running a hypothetical recovery algorithm on one, and evaluating its correlation with the other. The final output is a computational lower bound: no exp(n^0.99)-time algorithm can achieve recovery rate n^{-0.49} (or estimation error below 0.99kd/4 for the edge probability matrix) when ε²d ≤ 0.99k².

## Method

The core technique is a cross-validation reduction from hypothesis testing to weak recovery. The graph's edges are split into G₁ and G₂ by independent subsampling; a hypothetical recovery algorithm is run on G₁ to produce an estimator M̂₀, which is then regularized via the correlation-preserving projection from Hopkins–Steurer (implemented as an SDP) into a convex set K with bounded entries and nuclear norm. The test statistic is ⟨M̂, Y₂ − ηd/n · 11ᵀ⟩, where M̂ is the projected estimator. A key technical step is an approximate-independence lemma (Lemma E.3) showing that Y₂ and M̂ are nearly independent, enabling Bernstein concentration: the statistic is Ω(n^{0.51}) with constant probability under the SBM but o(n^{0.51}) with high probability under Erdős-Rényi. This yields R_{P,Q}(f) ≥ exp(n^{0.001}), contradicting the low-degree conjecture's O(1) bound and establishing the computational lower bound without proving new low-degree polynomial bounds.

## Results

The paper establishes the first rigorous evidence for a sharp computational transition in recovery rate at the Kesten-Stigum (KS) threshold ε²d = k². Below the threshold (ε²d ≤ 0.99k²), assuming the low-degree conjecture, no exp(n^0.99)-time algorithm can achieve recovery rate n^{-0.49} with constant probability, whereas above the threshold, polynomial-time algorithms achieve Ω(1) correlation (weak recovery). For learning the edge connection probability matrix, no exp(n^0.99)-time algorithm can achieve Frobenius error ≤ 0.99kd/4, matching the guarantees of known efficient algorithms (k-SVD) up to constant factors. For graphon estimation, no exp(n^0.99)-time algorithm can achieve Gromov-Wasserstein error ≤ d/(3n)·√(k/d), providing the first evidence of a computational-statistical gap in graphon learning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines; comparisons are against prior theoretical lower bounds. Prior work [Hop18, BBK+21a] rules out polynomial-time algorithms that distinguish SBM from Erdős-Rényi with 1-o(1) success probability, but does not rule out algorithms achieving constant correlation with only constant probability. The concurrent work [SW25] provides the first unconditional low-degree recovery lower bound below the KS threshold, ruling out degree-n^6 polynomial estimators for weak recovery, but does not extend to sub-exponential time algorithms. The paper's result is stronger in scope (sub-exponential time, recovery rate n^{-0.49}) but weaker in that it is conditional on the low-degree conjecture rather than unconditional. For learning the SBM parameters, [LG24] gives degree-ℓ polynomial lower bounds with error O(kd/ℓ^4), which only matches efficient algorithm guarantees up to logarithmic factors when k = O(log n); the paper's result matches the k-SVD guarantee (O(kd)) up to constant factors under the extended low-degree conjecture. The paper also provides the first computational lower bound for graphon learning, whereas [LG24] does not address this task since their hard instance uses fixed distribution parameters.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the low-degree likelihood ratio framework of Hopkins (2018) and Bandeira et al. (2021), which established lower bounds for distinguishing SBM from Erdős-Rényi graphs with 1-o(1) success probability, and on the correlation-preserving projection of Hopkins and Steurer (2017) for regularizing estimators. The key technical departure is a cross-validation reduction that converts a hypothesis-testing lower bound into a recovery-rate lower bound, exploiting the extended low-degree conjecture of Moitra and Wein (2023) to extend the argument from low-degree polynomials to all sub-exponential-time algorithms. Prior work either ruled out only 1-o(1) success probability or gave degree-poly(k) polynomial lower bounds for learning, leaving the sharp transition in recovery rate at the KS threshold unaddressed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first rigorous evidence (conditional on the low-degree conjecture) that polynomial-time algorithms face a sharp transition in achievable recovery rate at the Kesten-Stigum threshold, ruling out even n^{-0.49} correlation below the threshold while constant correlation is achievable above it. The results also provide evidence for a computational-to-statistical gap in learning SBM parameters (edge probability matrix and graphon), matching known algorithmic guarantees up to constants. Open questions include whether one can boost from n^{-0.49} to constant correlation in polynomial time given a good initialization, and whether the graphon learning gap extends to k ≤ √n. The results are conditional on conjectures and limited to constant or sub-polynomial k, leaving the general regime open.
