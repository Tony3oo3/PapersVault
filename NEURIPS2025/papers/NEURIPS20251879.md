## Problem

Gradient clipping in differentially private SGD introduces a bias between the true batch gradient and the aggregation of clipped gradients, manifesting as both direction deviation (unequal scaling of samples with different norms) and magnitude deviation (excessive reduction of the aggregation norm). Existing clipping methods such as PSAC reduce direction deviation by widening the linear region of the clipping curve, but their linear region span is fixed throughout training and cannot adaptively balance the direction–magnitude trade-off as gradient statistics evolve across iterations.

## Input / Output

The method consumes per-sample gradient vectors computed at the current model parameters, a clipping threshold C (set to 1 w.l.o.g.), and a saturation slope α that is itself a learnable parameter. It outputs clipped gradient vectors whose ℓ2-norm is bounded by C, updated model parameters via a standard DP-SGD step, and an updated α via a differentially private exponential update. Notably, the auxiliary sensitivity term r(θ,α;B) used in the α-update has an inherent ℓ2-sensitivity bound of 0.448/α, so no explicit per-sample clipping is needed for that term—only Gaussian noise is added.

## Method

AdaSig defines the clipping function ψ_α(x) = C(2/(1+e^{−αx}) − 1), a scaled and shifted sigmoid whose saturation slope α controls the span of the linear region: decreasing α widens the linear region (better equal scaling, less direction deviation, but smaller aggregation magnitude), while increasing α narrows it (preserves small-gradient magnitudes, less magnitude deviation, but more direction deviation). The key novelty is treating α as a learnable parameter updated each iteration via a differentially private exponential rule α_{t+1} = α_t · exp(λ_α · sign(ŝ^T r̂)), where ŝ and r̂ are noisy private estimates of the batch gradient aggregation and the sigmoid-derivative term, respectively. The two parallel Gaussian mechanisms per iteration are shown to be equivalent to a single Gaussian mechanism with combined noise multiplier σ = (σ_r^{−2} + σ_s^{−2})^{−1/2}, preserving the standard DP-SGD privacy accounting. A full convergence analysis under non-convex loss (smoothness, bounded gradient, symmetric per-sample gradient distribution) establishes that DP-SGD-AdaSig achieves the asymptotic rate O(√(d log(1/δ))/(Nε)), matching the best-known privacy-utility bound for vanilla clipping.

## Results

AdaSig achieves the highest average test accuracy across all eight evaluated datasets (six image, two text) under matched privacy budgets. The largest gains appear on ImageNette (64.65% vs. 62.30% for PSAC, +2.35 pp) and SST-2 (88.44% vs. 87.48% for PSAC, +0.96 pp), while improvements on MNIST and CelebA-Smiling are modest (≤0.05 pp). Statistical significance (p < 0.05 via t-test) is confirmed on 4 of 6 image datasets and both text datasets. Cosine-similarity analysis further shows AdaSig concentrates the distribution of direction alignment closer to 1 than any baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AdaSig outperforms all four baselines on every dataset. Against vanilla clipping [1], gains range from +0.03 pp (MNIST) to +2.63 pp (ImageNette), with the largest absolute improvements on ImageNette and SST-2. Compared to the adaptive-threshold method of Andrew et al. [3], AdaSig leads by up to +2.58 pp (ImageNette) and +0.98 pp (SST-2). Against Auto-S [4], which normalizes gradients and suffers from direction deviation, AdaSig is consistently better by 0.1–3.2 pp, with the widest gap on ImageNette (+3.21 pp). Versus PSAC [36], the closest competitor that also targets direction deviation, AdaSig still wins on all eight datasets; the margin is largest on ImageNette (+2.35 pp) and SST-2 (+0.96 pp), while on MNIST and CelebA-Smiling the advantage is within one standard deviation. Statistical significance (p < 0.05) is confirmed on FashionMNIST, CIFAR-10, ImageNette, CelebA-multi-label, SST-2, and QNLI.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DP-SGD framework (Abadi et al., 2016) by replacing the standard hard-clipping operation with a sigmoid-based clipping function whose saturation slope is adaptively learned during training. It directly addresses a limitation identified in PSAC (Xia et al., 2023) and Auto-S (Bu et al., 2023): while those methods reduce direction deviation by widening the linear region of the clipping curve, they cannot adaptively balance the resulting increase in magnitude deviation as gradient statistics evolve across iterations. The key conceptual departure is treating the clipping nonlinearity's shape parameter as a learnable variable with its own differentially private update rule, rather than fixing the clipping function's form and only adjusting a threshold.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The direction-magnitude trade-off is a fundamental source of bias in all DP-SGD variants, and this paper provides the first principled mechanism to balance it adaptively while preserving the best-known convergence rate. The consistent (though modest) accuracy gains across 8 datasets and two modalities suggest the trade-off is a real bottleneck in practice. Open questions include whether the adaptive slope mechanism extends to other privacy-preserving optimizers (e.g., DP-Adam), how it behaves under non-symmetric gradient distributions that violate Assumption 7.4, and whether the additional privacy noise for the slope update becomes a bottleneck at tighter privacy budgets. The method's negligible computational overhead makes it a practical drop-in replacement for existing clipping methods.
