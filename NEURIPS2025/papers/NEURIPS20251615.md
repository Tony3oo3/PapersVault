## Problem

Exact Shapley-value computation for weighted kNN classifiers has remained an open challenge: the unweighted case admits a closed-form solution in near-linear time, but the weighted case either requires pseudo-polynomial dynamic programming O(Wk²n²) or Monte Carlo approximation over n! permutations. This paper addresses the gap by introducing a new variant of weighted kNN-SV that is computable in near-linear time while retaining desirable axiomatic properties.

## Input / Output

The method takes a training dataset D of n tabular points (x ∈ R^d, y ∈ Y), a test dataset D_test, a distance-based weight function w(z|z_test) (e.g., Gaussian kernel with tunable width σ), and a neighbor parameter k. It produces a data valuation score φ(z) for each training point, defined as the sum of Shapley values over all copies of z in a conceptually duplicated dataset, averaged across test points. Weights are assumed to be positive integers (achievable by scaling), and the duplicated dataset D' is never materialized—only the original n points are processed.

## Method

The core idea is data duplication: each point z is replicated w(z|z_test)−1 additional times to form D', and unweighted kNN on D' with an adaptive parameter k' = Σᵢ₌₁ᵏ w(zᵢ|z_test) is shown to be equivalent to weighted kNN on D (Proposition 2). The DkNN-SV of z is the sum of the Shapley values of all its copies, which by symmetry (Lemma 5) equals w(z|z_test)·s'(z|z_test). The key efficiency insight is that all copies of z share the same Shapley value, so the recursive formula from the unweighted case (Theorem 1) can be applied once per original point using weighted cumulative ranks i' = Σⱼ₌₁ⁱ w(zⱼ|z_test), avoiding materialization of D'. This yields Algorithm 1 with O(dn + n log n) time, dominated by sorting. The resulting group values are axiomatically characterized (efficiency, symmetry, dummy player, additivity) and related to Owen values, though they differ from the standard weighted kNN-SV in general (Proposition 4).

## Results

On the noisy label detection task (F1 score, top-500 lowest-valued points), the proposed DkNN-SV (dup) achieves the best F1 on 9 out of 11 datasets, with notable gains such as 0.565 on cpu (vs. 0.507 for unweighted kNN-SV), 0.795 on apsfail (vs. 0.746), and 0.305 on poker (vs. 0.150). Running time is near-linear: dup takes ~1.6–637 s across datasets, only slightly above unweighted kNN-SV, while Monte Carlo weighted kNN-SV with 1000 samples is ~1000× slower (e.g., 874 s vs. 1.6 s on phoneme) and still does not exceed unweighted kNN-SV. The hard-label weighted kNN-SV (dynamic programming) from Wang et al. [10] performs only marginally above random guessing (e.g., F1 ≈ 0.07 on most datasets).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the unweighted kNN-SV (Jia et al. [8]), DkNN-SV wins on 9 of 11 datasets in F1 score, with the largest margin on poker (0.305 vs. 0.150, a 2× improvement) and cpu (0.565 vs. 0.507), while losing narrowly on phoneme (0.283 vs. 0.318) and vehicle (0.126 vs. 0.140). Against the Monte Carlo weighted kNN-SV (mc-1000, also from [8]), DkNN-SV matches or exceeds performance on all 11 datasets while running roughly 1000× faster (e.g., 1.6 s vs. 874 s on phoneme). Against the hard-label weighted kNN-SV via dynamic programming (Wang et al. [10]), DkNN-SV substantially outperforms: the dp baseline achieves F1 scores near random (≈0.036–0.107) on most datasets, whereas DkNN-SV reaches 0.23–0.89, indicating that the hard-label utility function is poorly suited for noisy label detection. The scaled unweighted kNN-SV (a simple post-hoc weighting of unweighted values) is consistently outperformed by DkNN-SV, confirming that the duplication-based approach captures weighted structure that naive scaling misses.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the exact Shapley value computation for kNN classifiers established by Jia et al. [8] and refined by Wang and Jia [13] from the unweighted to the weighted setting. The key conceptual move is a data-duplication reduction: replicating each point proportionally to its distance-based weight transforms the weighted kNN utility into an unweighted one, allowing direct reuse of the closed-form recursive formula. This connects to Owen values [14] from cooperative game theory, where a data point and its copies form a natural coalition, though the paper relaxes the group-compatibility constraint to enable an efficient algorithm. It departs from Wang et al. [10], whose dynamic-programming approach for hard-label weighted kNN-SV requires pseudo-polynomial time O(Wk²n²), by achieving near-linear O(n log n) time without materializing the duplicated dataset.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first near-linear-time exact algorithm for a principled variant of weighted kNN-SV, closing a significant efficiency gap over both the quadratic-time DP method and Monte Carlo sampling. The axiomatic characterization (efficiency, symmetry, dummy player, additivity) gives the DkNN-SV values a solid theoretical foundation, though the authors are transparent that these are not the 'true' weighted kNN-SV values but a new variant. A notable empirical finding is that the hard-label DP baseline [10] performs near-randomly on noisy label detection, suggesting the soft-label utility used here is more informative for downstream tasks. Open questions include extending the duplication technique to kNN regression and other distance-weighted models, and understanding when DkNN-SV and the original weighted kNN-SV diverge most significantly.
