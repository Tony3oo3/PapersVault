## Problem

Training LLMs with low-rank gradient projection reduces optimizer memory but introduces three coupled challenges: subspace updates via SVD are computationally expensive and noise-sensitive, Adam's internal momentum states become misaligned when the projection subspace shifts, and the gradient components discarded by the low-rank projection are simply thrown away. Existing methods (GaLore, LDAdam, Fira) address these issues individually but none simultaneously achieves SOTA convergence, minimal wall-time, and reduced memory without trade-offs.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an m×n gradient matrix (with m ≤ n w.l.o.g.) and the current weight matrix at each training step, projecting the gradient into an r-dimensional orthonormal subspace (r ≪ m) where the Adam optimizer operates. The output is an updated weight matrix computed from two terms: the projected-back optimizer output (S_t · G̃_t^O) and a recovery scaling correction Λ_t that rescales the orthogonal complement of the gradient using column-wise norm ratios from the low-rank optimizer. Optimizer states (first and second moments) are stored in the reduced r×n space rather than full m×n, matching GaLore's memory footprint.

## Method

SubTrack++ replaces periodic SVD-based subspace resets with continuous tracking on the Grassmannian manifold: at each update interval, it computes the tangent vector ∇F from the least-squares residual between the current gradient and its projection, takes a rank-1 SVD of ∇F, and moves along the corresponding geodesic with a fixed step size η, preserving orthonormality while making controlled, small adjustments. A projection-aware optimizer then transforms Adam's first and second moment estimates through the change-of-basis matrix S_t^T S_{t-1} at each subspace update, ensuring momentum statistics remain consistent under coordinate changes. Finally, a recovery scaling mechanism computes a column-wise scaling factor φ_t(G_t) = ‖G̃_t^O‖/‖G̃_t‖ from the low-rank optimizer output and applies it to the discarded orthogonal gradient components (G_t − S_t G̃_t), with a gradient-clipping-inspired limiter ζ to stabilize training. The overall subspace update has O(mnr) time complexity versus O(nm²) for SVD, and the convergence proof (Theorem 3.2) shows that the rank-1 geodesic updates prevent the drastic subspace changes that would break the constant-subspace assumption required for convergence.

## Results

On pre-training Llama-based architectures (60M–7B) on C4 over 10k iterations, SubTrack++ achieves the lowest evaluation loss in nearly every configuration, reaching 4.52 on the 1B model (vs. 4.61 full-rank, 4.70 LDAdam, 6.53 GaLore) and 4.50 on the 3B model (vs. 4.52 full-rank, 4.39 LDAdam, 6.57 GaLore). In wall-time, SubTrack++ is 43% faster than LDAdam on the 1B model and 67% faster on the 3B model, while maintaining the same optimizer-state memory footprint as GaLore (mr + 2nr parameters). On fine-tuning Llama-2-7B-chat-hf on Alpaca, SubTrack++ matches GaLore's evaluation loss (0.88) while running 36% faster (117 min vs. 178 min) and 65% faster than LDAdam (342 min).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SubTrack++ outperforms all six baselines on evaluation loss across nearly all model scales. Against GaLore (SVD-based subspace updates), SubTrack++ achieves substantially lower loss (e.g., 4.52 vs. 6.53 on 1B) while being significantly faster by avoiding expensive SVD computations. Against LDAdam (the strongest prior baseline using PowerSGD + projection-aware optimizer), SubTrack++ wins on 5 of 6 model sizes (losing only marginally on 3B: 4.50 vs. 4.39) and is 43–67% faster in wall-time. Against Fira (norm-based recovery scaling with GaLore), SubTrack++ dominates on all sizes (e.g., 4.52 vs. 6.31 on 1B). Against BAdam (block coordinate descent) and Online Subspace Descent (online PCA tracking), SubTrack++ achieves dramatically lower loss (e.g., 4.52 vs. 7.28 and 6.79 on 1B). Notably, SubTrack++ occasionally matches or exceeds full-rank training (e.g., 3.24 vs. 3.25 on 130M; 4.50 vs. 4.52 on 3B), attributed to implicit regularization from low-rank projections.

## Relation to Prior Work

SubTrack++ unifies three threads of recent low-rank LLM training research: GaLore's SVD-based gradient projection, LDAdam's projection-aware Adam update rules, and Fira's recovery scaling for discarded gradient components. Its key departure is replacing the expensive SVD (or PowerSGD) subspace re-estimation with rank-1 geodesic updates on the Grassmannian manifold, a technique drawn from the classical subspace-tracking literature (Balzano et al., 2011; Blocker et al., 2023) but not previously applied to LLM optimizer design. This geometric formulation yields controlled, incremental subspace shifts rather than the discrete resets inherent in periodic SVD, and the convergence proof in Theorem 3.2 is adapted from GaLore's analysis while exploiting the smaller per-update perturbation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 65% wall-time reduction over LDAdam at matched memory footprint is practically significant for teams training LLMs under tight compute budgets, and the method's plug-and-play Grassmannian tracking module could be combined with other optimizers beyond Adam. However, the convergence guarantee still assumes a constant subspace (a simplification shared with GaLore), and the authors acknowledge that extending projection-aware optimization to non-Adam families and validating at scales beyond 7B parameters remain open. The observation that low-rank projection can occasionally outperform full-rank training (implicit regularization) is noted but not deeply analyzed, leaving room for follow-up work on the regularization mechanism.
