## Problem

Existing diffusion-based adversarial purification methods treat adversarial perturbations as generic Gaussian noise, ignoring their structured, intentional nature. Adversarial perturbations exploit specific non-robust directions in input space and exhibit both local (gradient-based) and global (shared-structure) regularities that differ fundamentally from random corruptions, yet no prior purification framework explicitly models this adversarial landscape in its likelihood term.

## Input / Output

The method takes a potentially adversarial RGB image (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 64×64 for TinyImageNet) along with a frozen pretrained EDM diffusion prior and a lightweight UNet perturbation score model, and produces a purified image after 5–20 iterative gradient-based refinement steps, which is then passed to a standard classifier for prediction. The perturbation model is trained once offline on residuals between clean and adversarial examples generated with a fixed set of attack norms and budgets, making the defense plug-and-play across different classifiers at test time.

## Method

AAOpt formulates adversarial purification as a MAP estimation problem where both the data prior p(x) and the adversarial likelihood p(x̂|x) are parameterized by score-based diffusion models. The prior is a frozen pretrained EDM diffusion model, while the likelihood is a lightweight UNet trained as a standard DDPM on the residual γ = x − x̂ between clean and adversarial images, explicitly capturing the distribution of attack perturbations. At test time, the method iteratively refines the input by minimizing a combined surrogate loss that blends the prior score term and the perturbation score term (weighted by λ), using stop-gradient on both diffusion networks to avoid backpropagating through them. This optimization-based approach replaces the expensive multi-step reverse diffusion sampling of DiffPure with a small number of gradient updates, while the explicit perturbation likelihood term provides a more targeted purification signal than the generic denoising prior alone.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 under PGD-EOT (ℓ∞, ε=8/255), AAOpt achieves 86.75% robust accuracy with WRN-28-10 and 86.24% with WRN-70-16, substantially outperforming the best adversarial training baseline (66.01%) and the best diffusion purification baseline Score-Opt-N (65.62%). Under the stronger BPDA-EOT attack, AAOpt reaches 91.36% robust accuracy on CIFAR-10, surpassing Score-Opt-N (90.07%). On TinyImageNet (64×64, ResNet-50), AAOpt achieves 53.75% robust accuracy versus 21.2% for Score-Opt-O, demonstrating strong scaling to higher-dimensional data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AAOpt decisively outperforms all adversarial training baselines: on CIFAR-10 ℓ∞ (WRN-28-10), it achieves 86.75% robust accuracy versus 66.01% for the best AT method (Gowal et al. 2021), a gap of over 20 points. Among diffusion purification methods, AAOpt surpasses Score-Opt-N (65.62%) by 21.1 points under PGD-EOT and Score-Opt-N (90.07%) by 1.3 points under the stronger BPDA-EOT attack. On CIFAR-100 (BPDA-EOT), AAOpt achieves 66.56% robust accuracy, narrowly beating Score-Opt-O (66.11%) while maintaining comparable standard accuracy (69.98% vs. 70.53%). A notable trade-off is that AAOpt's standard accuracy (91.59–91.91%) is slightly below Score-Opt-N (94.43–95.11%) on WRN-28-10/70-16, though the robust accuracy gains far exceed this cost. On the CLIP zero-shot classifier, AAOpt (using only a WRN-28-10-trained perturbation model) reaches 91.63% robust accuracy at ε=4/255, outperforming Score-Opt-N (89.87%) and adversarially trained FARE⁴ (34.6%), confirming strong cross-architecture transferability. On TinyImageNet, AAOpt's 53.75% robust accuracy dwarfs Score-Opt-O's 21.2%, highlighting the advantage of the explicit perturbation model on more complex data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the score-based optimization purification framework of Zhang et al. (Score-Opt, NeurIPS 2023) by adding an explicit adversarial perturbation likelihood term to the MAP objective. The surrogate loss derivation follows Mardani et al. (RED-Diff, ICLR 2024), and the perturbation score model is trained as a standard DDPM (Ho et al., 2020) following the continuous-time score-matching framework of Song et al. (ICLR 2021). The key departure from prior diffusion purification methods (DiffPure, Score-Opt, etc.) is that adversarial perturbations are no longer treated as generic Gaussian corruption; instead, a lightweight score-based diffusion model explicitly learns the distribution of attack residuals, making the purification process adversary-aware while retaining the plug-and-play, classifier-agnostic nature of unsupervised purification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AAOpt achieves robust accuracy gains of 15–25 points over prior diffusion purification baselines on CIFAR-10 under fully adaptive PGD-EOT and BPDA-EOT attacks, without retraining the classifier, which is practically significant for deployed models where retraining is infeasible. The perturbation model's transferability across architectures (WRN-28-10 → WRN-70-16, and even to CLIP) and across norms (ℓ∞ ↔ ℓ₂) suggests the learned attack distribution captures universal geometric structure rather than classifier-specific artifacts. However, performance degrades substantially on TinyImageNet (53.75% robust vs. 91%+ on CIFAR-10), and the method still requires a one-time adversarial-example generation step for perturbation model training plus test-time optimization iterations, leaving open whether the approach scales to high-resolution or large-scale benchmarks.
