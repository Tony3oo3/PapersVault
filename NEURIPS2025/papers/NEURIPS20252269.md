## Problem

Previous learning-augmented online bidding work assumed deterministic prediction oracles and deterministic algorithms, leaving two major gaps: (1) no Pareto-optimal strategies existed for distributional predictions beyond the special case r=4, and (2) no results characterized the power or limitations of randomized learning-augmented bidding. This paper addresses both by computing the full consistency/robustness Pareto frontier under distributional predictions and establishing tight upper and lower bounds for randomized strategies.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes a discrete distributional prediction μ with k support points (μ_i, p_i) and a constant robustness requirement r, and outputs an infinite r-robust bidding sequence that is Pareto-optimal in consistency. The strategy is structured as a finite partial sequence (bids within the support of μ) followed by a tight r-extension that guarantees infinite r-robustness. For general (non-discrete) distributions, an exponential quantization rule reduces the problem to polynomial time in max{M/m, log m} with a (1+1/c)-approximation guarantee on consistency.

## Method

The core mechanism is a family of linear programs parameterized by 'configurations' — vectors specifying which bid interval each prediction point falls into. For each valid configuration, the LP minimizes the expected cost over μ subject to r-robustness constraints, a critical extendability constraint (sum of bids / last bid ≤ (r+√(r(r−4)))/2, from Lemma 6), and monotonicity. The key insight is that unlike the deterministic setting where simple geometric strategies suffice, the stochastic optimum requires searching over a much larger space of non-geometric partial strategies, which the LP formulation handles by restricting to tight r-extendable partial strategies (Lemma 5). For the randomized setting, the paper proves lower bounds via scalarization: it lower-bounds a weighted combination of robustness and consistency for any deterministic strategy against a carefully constructed target distribution, then applies Yao's principle to lift the result to randomized algorithms, circumventing the difficulty that r-robust randomized strategies lack the clean structural characterization available in the deterministic case.

## Results

For the distributional prediction setting, Algorithm 1 computes a Pareto-optimal r-robust strategy via a family of LPs, with a quantization-based approximation guarantee of e^{1/c} on consistency (Theorem 8). In the randomized setting, the strategy R* achieves consistency ≈ 1.724 at r=4 (with δ*≈0.9), improving upon the deterministic Pareto-optimal consistency of 4 at the same robustness level. However, the benefit of randomization rapidly dissipates: at r=4.5, δ*=0.95, and at r=5, δ*≈0.99, meaning the strategy nearly degenerates to the deterministic one. Experimentally, Algorithm 1 outperforms all three geometric heuristics across all robustness values r∈[4,12], with its consistency approaching 1 for large r while the best heuristic (ρ=ζ₂) plateaus near 2.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares Algorithm 1 (LP-based Pareto-optimal strategy) against three geometric heuristics of the form (λ·ρ^i)_{i≥0} from [8], with ρ chosen as ζ₁=(r−√(r(r−4)))/2, r/2, and ζ₂=(r+√(r(r−4)))/2. Algorithm 1 achieves strictly better consistency than all three heuristics for every r∈[4,12] and every dataset tested (six datasets with k=4 support points in [1,10⁴]). The ρ=ζ₁ heuristic is the weakest, with consistency increasing near-linearly in r due to very slow bid growth. The ρ=ζ₂ heuristic (which optimizes consistency for a single-valued prediction) is the strongest baseline but still lags behind Algorithm 1, with the gap widening as the prediction distribution becomes more spread (σ=4000 vs. σ=2000). For large r, Algorithm 1's consistency monotonically approaches 1 while all heuristics converge to approximately 2, meaning the geometric strategies pay essentially the full cost of finding the largest support point and gain no benefit from the distributional prediction. The paper also shows (Figure 3) that for simple two-point equiprobable predictions, the heuristics can be arbitrarily worse than Algorithm 1 as r grows.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the deterministic Pareto-optimal bidding framework of Angelopoulos et al. [8] and the distributional-prediction contract scheduling of [7] (which only handled the special case r=4) to general robustness requirements and full Pareto frontiers. It departs from prior work in two key ways: (1) it shows that the simple geometric strategies optimal in the deterministic setting can be arbitrarily inefficient under distributional predictions, necessitating an LP-based search over a much larger strategy space; and (2) it introduces the first randomized learning-augmented bidding analysis, building on the randomized competitive ratio e of Chrobak et al. [27] and Gal's [35] distributional lower-bound technique, adapted via scalarization and Yao's principle to the consistency/robustness tradeoff.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first complete characterization of the consistency-robustness Pareto frontier for distributional predictions and the first upper/lower bounds for randomized learning-augmented bidding, closing a gap that left the stochastic dimension of this classic problem unexplored. A notable negative result is that randomization's benefit over deterministic strategies rapidly vanishes as robustness r grows, suggesting that in learning-augmented settings the value of randomness is fundamentally limited compared to standard competitive analysis. Open questions include whether the LP-based approach scales to larger support sizes, whether similar scalarization techniques apply to other learning-augmented problems, and the paper's own suggestion of studying 'barely random' algorithms (e.g., one random bit) for bidding and search.
