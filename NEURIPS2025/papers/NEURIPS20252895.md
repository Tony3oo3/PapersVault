## Problem

Mainstream recommender systems model item topics and user preferences over those topics but neglect user social roles and the logical relationships between roles and item types, limiting expressiveness and interpretability. Correlations like the classic diaper-beer co-purchase are better explained by a shared user role ("dads with newborns") than by item-item statistics, yet no scalable framework exists to automatically identify such roles and model the U2I/I2U behavioral logic at industrial scale.

## Input / Output

The system consumes multi-modal item features (title, ASR transcript, OCR text, audio, image) through an MLLM to produce a semantic embedding and 8–10 user-role tags plus 8–10 item-topic tags per item, and consumes user interaction histories as sequential ID and tag embedding sequences. At inference it outputs a top-N ranked list scored by a combined utility score (SASRec dot-product) plus explicit tag-logic matching scores, alongside the maintained U2I and I2U logic graphs over cover sets of tags. A key design constraint is that the MLLM can only process ~500K new items per day, so a distilled classifier handles the remaining millions via the multi-modal embedding alone.

## Method

TagCF has three stages: (1) an MLLM (M3) extracts user-role and item-topic tags from each item's multi-modal content, with a greedy dynamic cover-set reduction algorithm keeping a stable, semantically diverse tag subset and a distilled model scaling tag prediction to all items; (2) an LLM (Qwen2.5-7B) iteratively generates U2I and I2U logic edges between tags, which are distilled into scoring models P_φ(c|t) and P_φ(t|c) to produce top-20 logic graphs over the cover sets; (3) the tag-logic knowledge is integrated into a SASRec-based recommender via three augmentations: an attention-pooled tag-based item encoder feeding a dual-branch user encoder (ID + tag sequences), contrastive learning losses that align user/item embeddings with the tag embedding space, and an explicit tag-logic inference score that adds utility-based and exploration-based tag matching at ranking time. The paper empirically shows user-role tags (TagCF-ut) outperform item-topic tags (TagCF-it) on accuracy, and that the logic graphs transfer across datasets without re-inference.

## Results

On the industrial video dataset, TagCF-ut achieves NDCG@10 of 0.0201 and MRR@10 of 0.0132, outperforming the best baseline (SAID) by +8.06% and +6.35% respectively, while also improving item coverage (Cover@10) by +12.78% over SAID. On the public Books dataset, TagCF-ut reaches NDCG@10 of 0.1881 (+10.3% over SAID) and on Movies achieves NDCG@10 of 0.1255 (+3.72% over SAID). In the online A/B test on a platform serving over 500M daily users, TagCF-util improves interaction rate by +0.946% (statistically significant) and TagCF-expl improves diversity by +0.102% (statistically significant) over a 4-year-developed production baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TagCF-ut consistently outperforms all nine baselines on both accuracy (NDCG, MRR) and diversity (Cover, Gini) metrics across all three datasets. Against the strongest sequential baselines (SASRec, LRURec, Mamba4Rec), TagCF-ut gains 8–10% in NDCG@10 on Industry and Books, while also substantially improving item coverage (e.g., Cover@10 of 0.3832 vs. LRURec's 0.3558 on Industry). The LLM-enhanced baselines (RLMRec, SAID, GENRE) generally improve over traditional sequential models but are still outperformed by TagCF on both accuracy and diversity; for instance, on Books, TagCF-ut achieves NDCG@10 of 0.1881 versus SAID's 0.1705 (+10.3%) and GENRE's 0.1674. BPR is a notable exception: it achieves the best Gini (diversity) scores (e.g., 0.5511 on Books) but at a severe accuracy cost (NDCG@10 of only 0.0633 vs. TagCF-ut's 0.1881), confirming that the tag-logic approach achieves a superior accuracy–diversity trade-off. In the online A/B test, TagCF-util delivers a statistically significant +0.946% interaction lift while TagCF-expl provides +0.102% diversity improvement without sacrificing accuracy, and a 40-day extended test confirms a +0.037% long-term retention (LT7) gain.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the collaborative filtering paradigm by introducing two new sub-tasks—user role identification and behavioral logic modeling—that complement the traditional item-topic-centric formulation. It builds on SASRec as the sequential backbone and departs from prior LLM-enhanced recommendation work (LLMRG, LLMHG, SAGCN, LLM-KERec) by shifting focus from item-side meta-feature enrichment to user-side social role modeling and the logical connections between user roles and item topics. The use of MLLMs (M3) for open-world tag extraction and LLMs (Qwen2.5-7B) for iterative logic graph construction, combined with cover-set reduction and distillation for industrial scalability, represents a practical integration strategy not seen in prior LLM-for-recommendation literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The empirical finding that user role tags are more stable and yield greater accuracy gains than item topic tags challenges the dominant item-topic-centric design philosophy in recommender systems, suggesting a potentially better modeling axis. The demonstrated transferability of the tag-logic graph across tasks (video → books/movies) positions this knowledge as a reusable asset rather than a task-specific artifact. Open questions include the long-term stability of the logic graph as tag sets evolve, the privacy implications of inferring user social roles from behavior, and whether the approach generalizes beyond the Kuaishou video domain to other recommendation settings with different interaction patterns.
