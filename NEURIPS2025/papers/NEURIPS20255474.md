## Problem

Standard 3D Gaussian Splatting representations do not scale to large-scale scenes because all Gaussians must reside in GPU memory simultaneously, making real-time rendering infeasible on mobile and low-end devices. Existing LOD methods for 3DGS improve rendering speed but still require all Gaussians from every LOD level in memory and recompute the active Gaussian set at every frame, adding per-frame overhead and preventing deployment on memory-constrained hardware.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained 3DGS representation (built from RGB images and camera poses) and produces a hierarchical LOD structure with multiple Gaussian sets at varying detail levels, partitioned into spatial chunks with precomputed active Gaussian sets per chunk. At render time, given a camera pose, the system selects the two nearest chunks, loads only their active Gaussians into GPU memory, and produces a rendered novel view image. The chunk partitioning is determined by K-means clustering of training camera positions, with chunk radius empirically around 5 m for outdoor scenes.

## Method

LODGE constructs each LOD level by applying a depth-aware 3D smoothing filter (inspired by Mip-Splatting's Nyquist-based anti-aliasing) to the base Gaussians, followed by iterative importance-score pruning and fine-tuning to maintain visual fidelity. To eliminate per-frame overhead and limit memory, the scene is partitioned into spatial chunks via K-means on camera positions, and each chunk stores a fixed set of 'active Gaussians' representing the entire scene at appropriate detail levels (not merely Gaussians within the chunk boundary). At render time, the two nearest chunks are loaded and their Gaussians are combined with an opacity-blending scheme that modulates the alpha of non-shared Gaussians using a projection-based weight along the line connecting the two chunk centers, producing smooth transitions without artifacts. Depth thresholds between LOD levels are selected automatically via a greedy strategy that minimizes the average number of visible Gaussians per 16×16 rasterization tile, and per-chunk visibility filtering with random orientation perturbations further reduces the loaded Gaussian count.

## Results

On the Hierarchical 3DGS dataset (SmallCity and Campus), LODGE achieves the best rendering quality (PSNR 26.57 / 24.75, SSIM 0.815 / 0.803, LPIPS 0.325 / 0.394) while loading only 877K / 1464K Gaussians in GPU memory and rendering at 257 / 219 FPS. On the Zip-NeRF dataset (Alameda, London, NYC), it achieves PSNR 22.41 / 26.34 / 27.40 and SSIM 0.741 / 0.818 / 0.849 at 230–280 FPS. The method also runs in real-time on mobile devices (41 FPS on iPhone 13 Mini, 35 FPS on iPhone 15 Pro), where H3DGS crashes entirely due to memory constraints.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LODGE outperforms the previous SOTA on Hierarchical 3DGS, H3DGS [11], on all quality metrics (PSNR +0.15 on SmallCity, +0.15 on Campus; SSIM +0.008 / +0.005; LPIPS −0.006 / −0.002) while using 8× fewer Gaussians in memory (877K vs 7093K on SmallCity) and rendering 6.8× faster (257 vs 38 FPS). Against OctreeGS [28], LODGE gains +0.59 PSNR and +0.008 SSIM on SmallCity at 2× the speed (257 vs 120 FPS). FLOD [29] is the only method that loads fewer Gaussians (497K) but sacrifices significant quality (PSNR 24.82 vs 26.57) due to its coarse-to-fine training discarding fine details. CityGS [18] is slower (114 FPS) and lower quality (PSNR 25.29) while loading 3× more Gaussians (2615K). On the Zip-NeRF dataset, LODGE matches or exceeds H3DGS on PSNR (27.40 vs 27.28 on NYC) and SSIM across all three scenes while rendering 8× faster (280 vs 33 FPS on NYC). Vanilla 3DGS [10] and Mip-Splatting [37] are competitive in quality but render at only 75–95 FPS with no memory reduction, whereas LODGE achieves 257 FPS with 877K Gaussians.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the 3D Gaussian Splatting representation, incorporating Mip-Splatting's 3D smoothing filter for LOD construction, RadSplat's importance pruning for Gaussian removal, and H3DGS's modified densification strategy for the base model. It departs from prior LOD-3DGS methods (H3DGS, FLOD, Octree-GS, CityGS) in two key ways: rather than dynamically selecting active Gaussians per frame (which requires all Gaussians in GPU memory), it pre-computes fixed active Gaussian sets per spatial chunk via K-means clustering of camera positions; and rather than using a coarse-to-fine training pipeline, it constructs LOD levels post-hoc on top of an existing reconstruction. The opacity blending between two nearest chunks is a novel mechanism not present in prior LOD-3DGS work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is enabling real-time large-scale 3DGS rendering on memory-constrained mobile devices (iPhone, Chromebook) where prior SOTA methods like H3DGS simply crash due to memory limits. The simultaneous improvement in quality, speed, and memory footprint (877K vs. 7093K Gaussians for H3DGS on SmallCity) makes this a deployable solution for AR/VR and interactive maps. The main open question is efficient streaming of Gaussians across chunk boundaries in production settings, which the authors acknowledge requires optimized servers and compression protocols. The assumption that two-chunk opacity blending suffices for arbitrary camera trajectories (including diagonal traversal) is empirically validated but not formally guaranteed.
