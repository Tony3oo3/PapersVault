## Problem

Many popular sequence models, including Transformers, Mamba, and linear RNNs, fail to learn state tracking tasks (simulating finite automata) on sequences longer than those seen in training. The paper identifies that the missing inductive bias is a multiplicative (bilinear) interaction between hidden states and inputs, rather than the additive input-dependent transitions used in prior work, and that additive terms in the recurrence are actively detrimental for constrained model variants.

## Input / Output

Models consume sequences of integer tokens drawn from Z_m (optionally interleaved with arithmetic operators +, ×, −), framed by [BOS] and [EOI] special tokens, and produce a single target class label at the [EOI] position (e.g., the sum modulo m, the final FSM state, or the arithmetic result). Training uses sequences of length 2–10 while evaluation tests length generalization at length 500, with all multi-digit integers tokenized as single tokens. The loss is computed only on the final target prediction, not on intermediate positions.

## Method

The core architecture replaces the standard linear recurrence h^t = A h^{t-1} + Bx^t + b with a pure bilinear update h^t = A_x h^{t-1}, where the transition matrix A_x is a linear function of the input embedding x, parameterized by a 3-way tensor W ∈ R^{H×H×D}. Critically, all additive terms (bias, input-dependent additive contributions) are removed, making hidden states scale-invariant and enabling normalization during inference without train/test mismatch. The paper establishes a hierarchy of model variants—full bilinear, CP-factored bilinear, block-diagonal bilinear, R₂ rotation-block, and real-diagonal—each corresponding to a progressively narrower class of learnable regular languages (arbitrary FSMs → abelian groups → parity). Three theoretical results are proven: full bilinear models can simulate any finite state machine, orthogonal block-diagonal models with a shared eigenbasis can simulate any abelian group, and a frozen random real-diagonal network with a trained linear readout learns parity from just two examples with probability 1−2^{−H}.

## Results

The full bilinear RNN achieves perfect (1.00) OOD accuracy at sequence length 500 across all three state-tracking tasks (modular addition, random state machine, modular arithmetic) for all tested moduli/state sizes up to 50. Even the most constrained variant that retains full expressivity (block-diagonal with block size 64) achieves 1.00 OOD accuracy on all tasks. In contrast, Mamba (4 layers) drops to near-zero OOD accuracy (≤0.03) on modular addition and state machine for m≥5, and Transformer (4 layers) similarly fails to generalize beyond m=2 on all three tasks. A frozen random bilinear network with only a trained linear readout layer solves parity (m=2 modular addition) with 1.00 OOD accuracy from just 2 training examples.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The full bilinear RNN dominates all baselines on OOD length generalization (length 500 vs. training length 2–10), achieving 1.00 accuracy on modular addition and state machine simulation for all moduli up to 50, where Mamba (4 layers) collapses to ≤0.03 and Transformer (4 layers) to ≤0.04. LSTM is the strongest baseline, matching bilinear on modular addition up to m=10 and on state machine up to m=10, but degrades sharply at m=25 (0.00 on modular addition, 0.64 on state machine) and m=50. The Elman RNN follows a similar pattern to LSTM but is consistently weaker on OOD (e.g., 0.37 vs. 1.00 on modular addition m=10). Mamba and Transformer both achieve near-perfect in-distribution accuracy but fail to generalize to longer sequences on most tasks, confirming the paper's central claim that linear RNNs with positive diagonal transitions and Transformers lack the inductive bias for state tracking. The block-diagonal bilinear with block size 64 matches the full bilinear on modular addition and state machine but degrades on modular arithmetic (0.21 vs. 0.99 at m=50), illustrating the hierarchy of expressivity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper revisits bilinear state transitions in RNNs originally explored by Sutskever et al. (2011), Downey et al. (2017), and Wu et al. (2016), but makes a critical departure by removing all additive terms to create purely multiplicative (not affine) transitions. It extends the recent line of work by Grazzi et al. (2025), Sarrof et al. (2024), and Fan et al. (2024), which established that input-dependent transition matrices are necessary for state tracking in linear RNNs, by showing that a specific bilinear (multiplicative) form of input-dependence is both sufficient and, for constrained architectures, necessary. The paper also connects the frozen-random-weight result to echo state networks (Jaeger, 2007) and positions popular architectures like Mamba at the lowest-complexity center of a proposed hierarchy of bilinear models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clear theoretical and empirical framework explaining why many popular linear RNNs (including Mamba) fundamentally cannot perform state tracking, and identifies the specific architectural property (pure bilinear, non-affine transitions) that enables it. The open question of parameter efficiency is significant: the full bilinear model's parameter count grows cubically in hidden dimension, making it impractical at scale, and whether the large parameter count is actually beneficial or counterproductive in multi-task settings like language modeling remains unresolved. The finding that additive terms are actively detrimental for constrained models (diagonal, 2D block-diagonal) challenges common design intuitions and has direct implications for how future sequence architectures should be constructed.
