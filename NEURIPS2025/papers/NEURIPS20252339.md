## Problem

Private hypothesis selection under differential privacy has been limited to algorithms with quadratic time complexity or suboptimal approximation factors. Bun et al. (NeurIPS 2019) posed the open question of whether an ideal private algorithm exists combining logarithmic sample complexity, nearly-linear time, and the optimal approximation factor α=3. This paper resolves the time complexity gap, achieving nearly-linear runtime with the optimal α=3 at the cost of a polylogarithmic (rather than logarithmic) sample complexity.

## Input / Output

The algorithm receives s i.i.d. samples from an unknown distribution P over domain X, a finite set of n public candidate distributions H = {H₁,...,Hₙ}, and parameters ε (privacy), β (confidence), and σ (additive error). It operates under a query access model where it can draw samples from P, compare PDFs of hypothesis pairs at points in X, and query probability masses of Scheffé sets. The output is a single hypothesis Ĥ ∈ H satisfying ‖Ĥ − P‖_TV ≤ 3·OPT + σ with probability at least 1−β, where OPT is the minimum TV distance from P to any hypothesis in H.

## Method

The algorithm privatizes the nearly-linear time minimum distance estimate (MDE) framework of Aliakbarpour et al. (NeurIPS 2024) by replacing its discrete bucketing scheme with a continuous exponential mechanism distribution Q over hypotheses, which favors those with low proxy distances. To identify prompting hypotheses (those that significantly improve proxy distances) without incurring high sensitivity, the paper introduces a score function that returns a quantile of lift values over a sampled list K, reducing sensitivity from O(1) to O(1/s). The sparse vector technique (SVT) is then applied to this low-sensitivity score to find a prompting hypothesis with privacy cost independent of n. Each round adds one prompting hypothesis to the set A and updates all proxy distances; the algorithm halts after O(log n) rounds when no further prompting hypothesis exists, at which point it samples the final output from Q. Privacy is enforced at each component via basic composition, yielding the stated ε-DP guarantee.

## Results

The paper's main result (Theorem 3) establishes the first nearly-linear time algorithm for private hypothesis selection under central differential privacy, achieving the optimal approximation factor α=3 with sample complexity s = Θ(log³(n/β)/(β²σ²ε)) and time complexity Õ(n/(β⁴σ³ε)). This resolves an open question posed by Bun et al. (NeurIPS 2019) about the existence of such an algorithm. The sample complexity is polylogarithmic in the number of hypotheses n with no dependence on the domain size, though it incurs an O(log²n/σ) overhead compared to prior private algorithms. The algorithm uses the exponential mechanism and sparse vector technique to privatize the iterative prompting-hypothesis selection process from the non-private MDE framework.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against three prior private hypothesis selection algorithms in Table 1. Against the Private Scheffé tournament of Bun et al. (Thm 3.6, α=9, quadratic time), this work strictly improves the approximation factor (3 vs. 9) and reduces time from quadratic to nearly-linear, at the cost of a larger sample complexity (log³n vs. log n + n²log n/(σε)). Against Bun et al. (Thm 3.5, α>54, quadratic time), this work dramatically improves the approximation factor (3 vs. >54) and time complexity, while maintaining comparable polylogarithmic sample complexity. Against the Minimum Distance Estimate of Aden-Ali et al. (α=3, quadratic time), this work matches the optimal approximation factor and achieves a strict improvement in time complexity (nearly-linear vs. quadratic), but pays an O(log²n/σ) penalty in sample complexity. The key trade-off is that the nearly-linear time guarantee comes at the expense of a modest increase in sample complexity, which the authors attribute to enforcing differential privacy at each algorithmic component rather than analyzing the full computation holistically.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the nearly-linear time minimum distance estimate (MDE) framework of Aliakbarpour, Bun, and Smith (ABS24) to the differentially private setting, preserving the optimal approximation factor α=3. The key technical departure from the non-private algorithm is replacing the discrete bucketing scheme—which is highly sensitive to individual data changes—with the exponential mechanism to maintain a distribution over hypotheses, and introducing a quantile-based 'score' function in place of exact lift counts to reduce sensitivity for the sparse vector technique. Prior private algorithms (Bun et al. 2019, Aden-Ali et al. 2021) achieved logarithmic sample complexity but required quadratic time; this work trades a modest polylogarithmic sample overhead (log³n vs. log n) for nearly-linear runtime.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This resolves an open question posed by Bun et al. (2019) about the existence of a nearly-linear time private hypothesis selection algorithm with optimal approximation factor, providing the first such algorithm. The trade-off in sample complexity (polylogarithmic rather than logarithmic) and the polynomial dependence on 1/β (rather than log(1/β)) are acknowledged limitations, with two concrete open directions: achieving O(log n/σ² + log n/(σε)) sample complexity and improving the confidence parameter dependence. The quantile-based score function and the normalization-term analysis bounding rounds to O(log n) are reusable techniques for privatizing iterative selection algorithms more broadly.
