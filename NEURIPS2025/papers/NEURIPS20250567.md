## Problem

Existing GFlowNet-based VRP solvers (AGFN, GFACS) rely exclusively on Trajectory Balance (TB) for global optimization, causing them to overlook fine-grained local transition signals between successive states. While Detailed Balance (DB) addresses local optimization, it alone cannot capture the holistic trajectory structure that VRPs inherently require. The paper tackles the absence of a principled mechanism to jointly exploit both TB and DB within a single GFlowNet training objective for vehicle routing.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a VRP instance as a complete graph with node coordinates, customer demands, and a depot location, which is sparsified into a k-nearest-neighbor graph for scalability. A message-passing GNN (12–16 layers) encodes node and edge features into embeddings that yield an edge probability distribution for route construction. The output is a set of feasible vehicle routes (trajectories) with total routing cost; for CVRP each route starts and ends at the depot respecting capacity constraints, while for TSP a single tour is produced. During inference, the depot-guided strategy applies stochastic sampling only at the depot node and greedy argmax selection at all customer nodes.

## Method

HBG introduces a VRP-specific Detailed Balance loss computed at each state transition, using step-wise forward probabilities from the GNN edge distribution and a backward probability derived from the trajectory's sub-trajectory composition (multi-node vs. single-node segments), yielding the closed-form P_b = 1/(2a+j) at the depot and 1 elsewhere. The total training objective sums the original TB loss (capturing global trajectory quality) with the per-step DB loss (capturing local transition quality), enabling the model to reinforce high-quality local segments even within globally suboptimal trajectories. A depot-guided inference mechanism exploits the structural asymmetry that only the depot has multiple valid predecessor candidates during backward destruction, applying sampling at the depot for exploration and greedy decoding at customers for exploitation. The framework is plug-and-play, integrated into both the construction-based AGFN and the improvement-based GFACS solvers without modifying their underlying architectures.

## Results

On synthetic CVRP instances, HBG-AGFN achieves optimality gaps of 9.95%, 10.44%, and 9.34% on CVRP200, CVRP500, and CVRP1000 respectively, improving over the original AGFN (11.48%, 12.21%, 11.15%). HBG-GFACS with local search reaches gaps as low as 1.96%, 2.81%, and 2.75%, approaching the LKH reference (28.04, 63.32, 120.53 objective values). On the real-world CVRPLIB-XXL benchmark, HBG-AGFN achieves up to 91.51% gap reduction over AGFN, and HBG-GFACS achieves up to 98.52% improvement over GFACS. Inference overhead is minimal (0.01–0.04 s additional), preserving the fast inference advantage of the underlying GFlowNet solvers.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HBG-AGFN consistently outperforms the original AGFN across all CVRP sizes, reducing the gap by 1.53 pp (200), 1.77 pp (500), and 1.81 pp (1000). HBG-GFACS shows larger gains over GFACS, with gap reductions of 6.63 pp (200), 10.30 pp (500), and 13.21 pp (1000), indicating the DB loss is particularly beneficial for the ACO-augmented solver. Against classical heuristics, HBG-GFACS (local search) nearly matches LKH (gap 1.96% vs. 0% on CVRP200), while HBG-AGFN outperforms POMO (gap 9.95% vs. 5.03% on 200, but 9.34% vs. 92.38% on 1000) and GANCO (9.34% vs. 21.00% on 1000). Both HBG variants substantially outperform ACO (gaps >150%) and NeuOpt (gaps >37%) at all scales. On TSP (Table 5), HBG-AGFN reduces the gap from 11.58% to 10.45% (200 nodes) and from 19.71% to 18.47% (1000 nodes), while HBG-GFACS (local search) achieves a 7.67% gap on TSP1000, competitive with POMO*8 (43.74%) and GANCO (32.14%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GFlowNet framework for VRPs by introducing Detailed Balance (DB) into a context where only Trajectory Balance (TB) has been used. It builds directly on AGFN (adversarial GFlowNet, ICLR 2025) and GFACS (ant colony sampling with GFlowNets, AISTATS 2025), both of which rely exclusively on TB for global trajectory optimization. The key departure is a VRP-specific DB formulation with a novel backward probability structure derived from the sub-trajectory decomposition of CVRP routes (multi-node vs. single-node segments), combined with a forward-looking credit assignment technique from Pan et al. (2023). The depot-guided inference strategy further leverages the structural asymmetry between depot and customer nodes identified in the backward policy analysis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete gap in GFlowNet-based VRP solvers: TB-only training can penalize high-quality local transitions embedded in globally suboptimal trajectories, and the Hybrid-Balance objective provides a principled fix that is general enough to plug into both construction-based (AGFN) and improvement-based (GFACS) solvers. A key limitation is that HBG's performance is bounded by the underlying GFlowNet solver, and the authors acknowledge that integration with stronger generative policies remains future work. Open questions include whether the adaptive weighting schedule (TB:DB ratio) generalizes beyond the tested 1:1 setting, and whether the depot-guided inference principle extends to other structured routing variants with multiple depots or heterogeneous node roles.
