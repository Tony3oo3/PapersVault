## Problem

Most research on deep learning for accelerated MRI focuses on improving neural network architectures, while the role of training data curation remains largely unexplored. This paper investigates whether filtering a large, heterogeneous pool of raw k-space data can improve reconstruction performance of existing state-of-the-art models, drawing an analogy to data curation strategies that have proven effective in vision-language and NLP domains.

## Input / Output

The reconstruction models take retrospectively undersampled multi-coil k-space measurements (2D Cartesian undersampling with 4–8% central lines fully sampled) and produce complex-valued image reconstructions evaluated against magnitude MVUE ground truths. The filtering pipeline operates on magnitude MVUE reconstructions normalized per volume, divided into non-overlapping 128×128 patches, embedded via DreamSim, and compared against a curated validation set. The training pool comprises 1.1M slices from 18 public k-space sources spanning diverse anatomies, contrasts, vendors, and field strengths, with 3D volumes converted into axial, sagittal, and coronal 2D slices.

## Method

The paper proposes and compares two classes of filtering: heuristic filtering (energy-ratio and Canny edge-density thresholds to remove dark or blurry slices) and alignment-based filtering, which retrieves training images whose DreamSim embeddings are k-nearest neighbors of validation-set embeddings in perceptual space. The best-performing variant, weighted alignment filtering, omits deduplication and instead uses the square root of raw retrieval frequencies as per-sample training weights, so that images more similar to the validation distribution are sampled more often. Near-duplicate removal within volumes (DreamSim similarity > 0.9) is applied before retrieval to preserve diversity. The key finding is that weighted alignment filtering consistently improves SSIM over no filtering across VarNet, U-net, ViT, and diffusion-model-based reconstruction, with gains comparable to a 3-fold increase in unfiltered data, and the benefit is largest when the unfiltered pool contains a low fraction of in-distribution data.

## Results

At 4-fold acceleration with a 120k-slice unfiltered dataset (1% in-distribution), weighted alignment filtering on just 40k slices achieves 40.60 dB PSNR and 0.108 LPIPS (mean over 48 test sets), outperforming no filtering on the full 120k slices (39.99 dB PSNR, 0.111 LPIPS). The performance gain from filtering is comparable to a 3-fold increase in unfiltered training data (i.e., 360k slices). Weighted alignment filtering improves on 46 out of 48 test sets, and the gains are statistically significant (95% confidence intervals via bootstrapping). The same trend holds for U-nets, Vision Transformers, and diffusion-model-based reconstruction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary baseline is training on the full 120k-slice unfiltered dataset. Weighted alignment filtering on only 40k slices (one-third the data) outperforms this baseline by +0.61 dB PSNR on the mean over 48 test sets, and by +0.80 dB on in-distribution data. Alignment filtering (without weighting) achieves +0.39 dB PSNR over no filtering, while heuristic filtering (retaining 80k slices) yields +0.20 dB. Combining heuristic then alignment filtering does not improve over alignment filtering alone. The paper also shows that increasing the fraction of in-distribution data in the training set provides a larger single-factor gain than filtering, but applying weighted alignment filtering on top of an already improved dataset still yields additional smaller gains. For diffusion-model-based reconstruction, weighted alignment filtering consistently improves performance across both variational optimization and decomposed diffusion sampling, and across different dataset compositions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper transplants the data-curation paradigm that has proven effective for vision-language models (DataComp, DataComp-LM, FineWeb) into the accelerated MRI reconstruction setting, where prior work has overwhelmingly focused on network architecture improvements (VarNet, HUMUS-Net, ViT-based models) rather than training data quality. The core filtering mechanism—retrieving training samples similar to a validation set via a perceptual similarity metric—directly adapts Gadre et al.'s alignment filtering, with DreamSim replacing CLIP as the embedding backbone. The paper also introduces the Fréchet DreamSim Distance as a train-test similarity metric, extending the FID lineage to a perceptually grounded embedding space, and demonstrates that the same filtering principles transfer from end-to-end unrolled networks to diffusion-model-based reconstruction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that data curation is a meaningful lever for accelerated MRI reconstruction, providing practitioners with a concrete, reproducible filtering pipeline (weighted alignment filtering with DreamSim) that yields consistent gains across architectures, accelerations, and dataset compositions. However, the improvements are modest (comparable to a 3× increase in unfiltered data) and the authors note that medical imaging data is already relatively high quality, limiting the headroom for filtering. Key open questions include whether these techniques extend to 3D reconstruction, motion-compensated imaging, and other modalities, and whether the gains persist when in-distribution data is abundant. The finding that filtering is most beneficial when in-distribution data is scarce suggests a practical guideline: invest in collecting more in-distribution data before relying on filtering.
