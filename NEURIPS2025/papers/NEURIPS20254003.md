## Problem

Most Schrödinger bridge methods assume Brownian or scalar OU reference dynamics, implicitly restricting the modelled system to potential-driven (gradient) evolution. Biological and active-matter systems, however, naturally operate out of equilibrium with non-conservative drift forces, and existing simulation-based solvers for general reference processes are computationally expensive and lose accuracy in high dimensions. This paper asks whether a family of analytically tractable non-equilibrium reference processes can yield both exact solutions (Gaussian case) and efficient simulation-free learning (general case).

## Input / Output

The method consumes discrete empirical samples from source and target marginals (e.g., single-cell snapshots binned by fluorescence) together with mvOU reference parameters: a drift matrix A ∈ R^{d×d} (which may be asymmetric), an equilibrium point m, and a diffusion matrix σ (assumed WLOG diagonal by Lemma 1). It produces two neural networks — a probability flow field u_t^θ(x) and a score field s_t^φ(x) — whose combination defines the Schrödinger bridge SDE dX_t = (u_t^θ(X_t) + D s_t^φ(X_t))dt + σ dB_t. For Gaussian endpoints, no neural network is needed: closed-form mean, covariance, and SDE drift are available directly from Theorem 3. The static entropic OT subproblem is solved with all available samples (no minibatching), which is a practical constraint for very large N.

## Method

The core contribution is MVOU-OTFM, a two-stage simulation-free algorithm. First, the static SBP is solved via Sinkhorn-Knopp iterations using a Gaussian cost C(x₀,x_T) = ½(x_T − μ_T^{x₀})ᵀ Σ_T⁻¹(x_T − μ_T^{x₀}) derived from the mvOU transition kernel, where Σ_T is a one-off 1D matrix integral independent of the endpoints. Second, neural networks for the flow and score are trained by conditional matching: at each step, a pair (x₀,x_T) is sampled from the OT plan, a time t is drawn uniformly, and a point z is sampled from the closed-form Gaussian mvOU bridge p_{t|(x₀,x_T)} (Theorem 2); the loss regresses u_t^θ(z) and s_t^φ(z) against the analytically known bridge flow and score. The key insight enabling simulation-free training is that the mvOU bridge control Λ_t depends only on t (not on endpoints), so all bridge statistics reduce to cached matrix exponentials and a single precomputed integral. For Gaussian endpoints, the full GSB is characterised exactly (Theorem 3) by transforming to a standard Gaussian entropic OT problem via Σ_T^{-1/2}, yielding explicit mean, covariance, and SDE drift without any neural approximation.

## Results

On Gaussian Schrödinger bridges with an mvOU reference, MVOU-OTFM achieves the lowest marginal error (Bures-Wasserstein) across all tested dimensions: 0.19±0.17 at d=2, 0.59±0.36 at d=10, and 6.84±0.78 at d=100, outperforming NLSB (1.21, 1.36, 17.40 respectively) and IPML (5.55, 3.00, 14.38). Force error (L2) follows the same pattern, with MVOU-OTFM at 3.56 (d=2) and 10.45 (d=100) versus NLSB at 7.59 and 16.49. On the repressilator leave-one-out task, MVOU-OTFM after 4 iterations reaches EMD 1.40±0.57 and energy distance 0.89±0.55, beating both SBIRR(mvOU) (EMD 2.10) and SBIRR(MLP) (EMD 1.67). Training is simulation-free and takes 1–2 minutes on CPU for d=50, versus 15+ minutes on GPU for NLSB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MVOU-OTFM outperforms all four baselines on both marginal error and force error across every dimension tested (d=2 to 100). Against BM-OTFM (the Brownian-reference version of the same algorithm, i.e. Tong et al. 2023), the gap is dramatic: at d=10, MVOU-OTFM achieves 0.59 BW error versus 8.93 for BM-OTFM, confirming that the mvOU reference is critical when the true dynamics are non-gradient. Against NLSB (Koshizuka & Sato 2022), MVOU-OTFM wins on both metrics at all dimensions, with the advantage widening in high dimensions (e.g. at d=100: 6.84 vs 17.40 BW error). Against IPML (Vargas et al. 2021), MVOU-OTFM is consistently better, though the gap is smaller at intermediate dimensions (d=10: 0.59 vs 3.00). On the repressilator task, MVOU-OTFM (iterated, 4 steps) beats both SBIRR variants (Shen et al. 2024): EMD 1.40 vs 2.10 for SBIRR(mvOU) and 1.67 for SBIRR(MLP), and energy distance 0.89 vs 1.39 and 1.10 respectively. MVOU-OTFM also trains in 1–2 minutes on CPU, whereas NLSB requires 15+ minutes on GPU and IPML/SBIRR require iterative SDE simulation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the simulation-free Schrödinger bridge framework of Tong et al. [52] (which assumed a Brownian reference) and the closed-form Gaussian bridge results of Bunne et al. [5] (which covered Brownian and scalar OU references) to the multivariate Ornstein-Uhlenbeck (mvOU) setting with a generic asymmetric drift matrix A. The key conceptual departure is that asymmetric A yields non-gradient, non-equilibrium dynamics, moving beyond the potential-driven systems that dominate the SBP literature. The bridge characterisation (Theorem 1) is adapted from the control-theoretic results of Chen and Georgiou [8], while the training objective generalises the conditional score-and-flow matching loss of [52] by substituting the mvOU bridge score and flow for the Brownian bridge counterparts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Biological systems (gene circuits, cell differentiation, active matter) are naturally non-equilibrium and non-gradient, yet most existing SBP solvers implicitly assume potential-driven dynamics; this work provides the first simulation-free, analytically grounded method for the non-gradient case. The practical speed advantage (1–2 min on CPU vs. 15+ min on GPU for NLSB) makes iterative reference-refinement pipelines feasible on standard hardware. Key limitations include the O(d³) matrix operations restricting reliable use to d ≤ 100, the linearity assumption on the reference process (a tradeoff between tractability and expressiveness), and the requirement for an exact static OT coupling that becomes problematic in large-scale or streaming settings where minibatch OT introduces bias.
