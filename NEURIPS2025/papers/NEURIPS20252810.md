## Problem

Computing exact Shapley values for biological sequence models requires exponential model evaluations per query, making large-scale interpretation (thousands of sequences) intractable—e.g., explaining ~1,000 guide RNA sequences in TIGER took one day, and third-order Faith-Shap interactions via SHAP-IQ would take over 80 days. The paper asks whether the cost of Shapley explanations can be amortized across queries by exploiting reusable structure in the model's global representation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252810/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a black-box sequence model f: Z_q^n → R with only query access (no architecture internals) and a set of Q query sequences of length n over an alphabet of size q (q=4 for DNA/RNA, q=20 for proteins). It produces per-feature SHAP values and Faith-Shap interaction indices up to order ℓ (typically ℓ≤5) for each query sequence. The one-time sketching phase requires O(sn²) model evaluations, after which each additional query incurs near-zero marginal cost that does not scale with sequence length n.

## Method

SHAP zero is a three-step amortized pipeline. First, it recovers the top-s Fourier coefficients of f via structured subsampling (inspired by sparse-graph codes) that aliases Fourier coefficients into buckets, followed by a peeling decoder on a sparse bipartite graph to resolve multi-ton bins—requiring O(sn²) samples and O(sn³) runtime. Second, it maps these global Fourier coefficients to a localized q-ary Möbius transform around each query sequence using the Fourier shifting property, with complexity O(s²(2q)^ℓ). Third, it converts the q-ary Möbius transform to the set Möbius transform and then to SHAP values and Faith-Shap interactions in O(q^ℓ) per query. The key theoretical contribution is proving the formal connection between Shapley explanations, the Möbius transform, and the sparse Fourier transform, showing that the Fourier basis provides a query-agnostic global sketch that can be localized per-query without additional model evaluations.

## Results

SHAP zero achieves high agreement with KernelSHAP (ρ = 0.83 on TIGER, ρ = 0.84 on inDelphi, ρ = 0.97 on Tranception) while reducing amortized per-query runtime by 17×, 1.4×, and 3× respectively. For high-order Faith-Shap interactions, SHAP zero correlates with SHAP-IQ at ρ = 0.79 (TIGER), ρ = 0.74 (inDelphi), and ρ = 0.61 (Tranception), while running up to >1000× faster (6.5 s vs. 2 h on TIGER). The recovered Fourier coefficients outperform linear and pairwise surrogate models in predicting held-out experimental scores (e.g., ρ = 0.74 vs. 0.37/0.47 on inDelphi), confirming that SHAP zero captures high-order (ℓ > 2) feature interactions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against KernelSHAP, SHAP zero achieves strong correlation (ρ = 0.83–0.97) across all three models while being 1.4×–17× faster in amortized per-query time, with the largest speedup on TIGER (6.5 s vs. 109 s). Against SHAP-IQ for Faith-Shap interactions, SHAP zero maintains reasonable correlation (ρ = 0.61–0.79) but delivers a dramatic speedup: >1000× on TIGER (6.5 s vs. 2 h), ~55× on inDelphi (40 min vs. 37 h), and 7× on Tranception (5.2 min vs. 38.1 min). Against FastSHAP, SHAP zero is decisively superior: FastSHAP shows near-zero or negative correlation with KernelSHAP (ρ = −0.39 on TIGER, ρ = 0.08 on inDelphi, ρ = 0.03 on Tranception), which the authors attribute to the difficulty of training a reliable surrogate when model outputs are highly sensitive to specific masked positions (seed regions, cut sites, epistatic positions). SHAP zero also recovers more biologically relevant interactions: 52% of its TIGER interactions fall within the seed region vs. 34% for SHAP-IQ, and 55% of its inDelphi interactions are within three nucleotides of the cut site vs. 17% for SHAP-IQ.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate threads: sparse Fourier recovery for q-ary functions (Erginbas et al., ISIT 2023; Tsui et al., IEEE ITW 2025) and the Möbius transform formulation for Shapley interaction detection (Kang et al., NeurIPS 2024). The key conceptual departure from prior SHAP estimation methods (KernelSHAP, SHAP-IQ, FastSHAP) is the shift from per-query model evaluation to a global-to-local framework: a one-time sparse Fourier sketch of the model is computed, then localized to any query via the q-ary Möbius transform, yielding SHAP values and Faith-Shap interactions with near-zero marginal cost. Unlike surrogate-based amortization (FastSHAP), which the authors show fails empirically on sequence models, SHAP zero relies solely on the sparsity of the model's Fourier representation, a property grounded in the limited number of biologically relevant epistatic interactions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The >1000× amortized speedup for high-order Faith-Shap interaction estimation makes previously intractable global interpretability analyses (e.g., discovering microhomology motifs in DNA repair or context-dependent epistatic interactions in protein fitness) computationally feasible, opening a path to principled, scalable explanation of black-box sequence models in genomics and protein design. A key open question is the marginalization assumption: SHAP zero uses a uniform distribution over absent features, whereas KernelSHAP and other methods use the empirical training distribution, leading to slightly different quantities (notably, more conservative negative SHAP estimates for rare amino acids). Extending the sparse Fourier framework to account for non-uniform feature distributions, and validating the sparsity assumption on models where it may not hold (e.g., highly redundant or dense interaction landscapes), are natural next steps.
