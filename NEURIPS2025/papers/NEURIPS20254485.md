## Problem

Open-set (open-vocabulary) detectors require a human-provided category list at inference and perform poorly on rare objects, while open-ended detectors can discover novel objects without any category input but suffer from significantly lower overall accuracy, especially on frequent and crowded objects. No existing framework simultaneously achieves strong detection performance across both rare and frequent categories while retaining the ability to discover unseen object categories automatically.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254485/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and an optional predefined object category list; when the list is empty, the model operates in open-ended mode and discovers all objects autonomously via a vision-language model (InternVL-2.5-8B). The VL-SAM pipeline converts VLM attention maps into point prompts that seed general queries, while the open-set model (LLMDet) produces specific queries from the category list. Outputs are bounding boxes with category labels, and when combined with SAM, pixel-level segmentation masks are also produced.

## Method

VL-SAM-V2 fuses two types of object queries through a general-and-specific query fusion module embedded in each transformer decoder layer: general queries derived from VL-SAM point prompts (open-ended) and specific queries from an open-set detector (LLMDet). A self-attention mechanism allows the variable-length general queries to interact with the fixed-length specific queries, followed by shared query-to-text and query-to-image cross-attention and FFN, with unshared box heads predicting per-query box offsets. To convert unordered point prompts into structured queries, the paper introduces ranked learnable queries that sort points by classification score and match them one-to-one with N fixed-order learnable queries. A denoising points training strategy replaces expensive VL-SAM inference during training with randomly sampled positive and negative noisy points (following DINO's coordinate-noise formulation), reducing training cost by 50% while stabilizing convergence. Only the self-attention and box-head parameters are fine-tuned on GroundingCap-1M using combined grounding and generation losses from LLMDet.

## Results

On LVIS minival (zero-shot open-set detection), VL-SAM-V2 with Swin-T achieves 45.7 AP (41.2 AP_r, 41.1 AP_c, 50.7 AP_f), surpassing the LLMDet baseline by 1.0 AP and 3.9 AP_r. With Swin-L, it reaches 51.7 AP (47.2 AP_r), improving over LLMDet by 0.6 AP and 2.1 AP_r. In open-ended mode (empty category list), VL-SAM-V2 achieves 31.8 AP and 30.5 AP_rare with Swin-L, outperforming GenerateU (27.9 AP, 22.3 AP_rare) by 3.9 AP and 8.2 AP_rare. On CODA, VL-SAM-V2 reaches 42.3 mAP versus 38.5 for LLMDet and 12.6 for GroundingDINO.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VL-SAM-V2 outperforms all open-set baselines on LVIS minival overall AP: it beats LLMDet (its direct baseline) by 1.0 AP (Swin-T) and 0.6 AP (Swin-L), T-Rex2 by 2.9 AP, DetCLIPv2 by 5.3 AP (Swin-T) / 7.0 AP (Swin-L), and GroundingDINO by 18.3 AP (Swin-T). The most notable gain is on rare objects: AP_r improves from 37.3 to 41.2 (Swin-T) and 45.1 to 47.2 (Swin-L) over LLMDet. DetCLIPv3, which uses additional private data, reports higher AP_r (49.9) and AP_c (49.7) than VL-SAM-V2 (47.2, 46.7) with Swin-L, but VL-SAM-V2 still leads on overall AP (51.7 vs. 48.8). In open-ended mode, VL-SAM-V2 surpasses GenerateU by 2.7 AP and 9.8 AP_rare (Swin-T) and by 3.9 AP and 8.2 AP_rare (Swin-L), and outperforms VL-SAM by 6.5 AP and 7.1 AP_rare (Swin-L). On CODA, VL-SAM-V2 (42.3 mAP) substantially exceeds GroundingDINO (12.6) and YOLO-World (16.1), confirming gains on corner-case objects.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established open-world detection paradigms: open-set (open-vocabulary) detection, exemplified by LLMDet and GroundingDINO, and open-ended detection, exemplified by VL-SAM and GenerateU. It departs from prior work by treating these not as competing approaches but as complementary sources of queries—specific queries from an open-set model that excel on frequent objects, and general queries from a VLM-driven pipeline that can discover novel categories. The denoising points strategy is a direct adaptation of DINO's denoising anchor boxes to the point-prompt setting, and the ranked learnable queries introduce a sorting-based matching mechanism not present in prior DETR-style detectors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical unified framework that can operate in either open-set or open-ended mode simply by controlling the category list, which is a useful deployment property for real-world systems like autonomous driving. The consistent gains across multiple backbones, VLMs, and open-set baselines (Table 6) suggest the fusion module is a broadly applicable component rather than a one-off trick. However, the approach inherits VLM limitations (hallucination, slow inference) and requires SAM for segmentation, leaving the end-to-end open-world perception pipeline still incomplete. An open question is whether the reliance on a large VLM at inference time can be reduced without sacrificing the novel-object discovery capability.
