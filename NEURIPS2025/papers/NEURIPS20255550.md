## Problem

Conventional time series forecasting relies on a single monolithic model to handle all forecasting scenarios, which struggles to generalize across heterogeneous temporal patterns and nonstationary signals. Different time series may require distinct modeling strategies—some benefit from multi-scale temporal awareness while others need heterogeneous signal perception—yet no principled framework exists for decomposing the forecasting task into specialized sub-tasks and enabling structured collaboration among dedicated models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255550/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a historical multivariate time series X ∈ R^{T×M} (T time steps, M variables) and produces a future multivariate forecast Ŷ ∈ R^{L×M}. All experiments use a symmetric prediction setting where the input length equals the forecasting horizon, tested at horizons from 96 to 720. Internally, each agent produces a sub-task-specific output (e.g., a partial-horizon prediction or a signal-component estimate) that is communicated and aggregated before the final forecast is emitted.

## Method

MAFS decomposes forecasting into N specialized agents (each an iTransformer encoder) assigned either homogeneous sub-tasks (predicting progressively longer portions of the future horizon) or heterogeneous sub-tasks (trend, seasonality, spectral energy, or statistical descriptors). Agents exchange hidden representations at every encoder layer via a Graph Convolutional Network over a predefined topology (Ring, Star, Chain, or Fully-Connected). Training is two-stage: Stage 1 independently optimizes each agent with fixed uniform communication weights; Stage 2 freezes agent parameters and jointly learns a Topology-aware Weight Adjuster (sigmoid-gated edge weights modulated by the adjacency mask) and an Agent-rated Voting Aggregator (AVA). The AVA combines a self-assessment gating mechanism (Agent Confidence Estimator) with an MLP-based Global Voter that assigns adaptive collaboration weights, producing the final forecast via a weighted sum of confidence-adjusted agent embeddings projected through a linear head.

## Results

MAFS achieves the best MSE and MAE on all 11 datasets, ranking first on 16 out of 22 metrics and top-2 on 20 out of 22. On average, MAFS reduces MSE by 6.35% and MAE by 4.03% over its iTransformer backbone, and by 3.24% MSE and 1.71% MAE over the strongest SOTA baseline TimeMixer. The largest single-dataset gain is on Temp, where MAFS reduces MSE by 19.08% and MAE by 10.28% relative to the single-agent model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MAFS outperforms all nine baselines on both MSE and MAE across all 11 datasets. Against its iTransformer backbone, MAFS achieves an average 6.35% MSE reduction and 4.03% MAE reduction, demonstrating that the multi-agent framework compensates for the backbone's individual limitations. Compared to the strongest SOTA baseline TimeMixer, MAFS still leads by 3.24% MSE and 1.71% MAE on average, with the closest margins on ETTm1 (0.366 vs. 0.381 MSE) and AQShunyi (0.701 vs. 0.719 MSE). The gaps widen substantially against older architectures: MAFS beats PatchTST by ~5–8% MSE, TimesNet by ~4–10% MSE, and DLinear by ~5–15% MSE depending on the dataset. The largest absolute improvements are over Autoformer and Informer, where MAFS reduces MSE by 10–25% on most datasets (e.g., ETTh2: 0.356 vs. 0.450 for Autoformer and 4.431 for Informer). Notably, on AQWan and ZafNoo, TimeMixer and PatchTST respectively achieve slightly lower MAE than MAFS (0.499 vs. 0.503 and 0.440 vs. 0.451), but MAFS still leads on MSE for both, confirming its overall dominance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the multi-agent system paradigm—previously applied to robotics, QA, and sequential decision-making—into time series forecasting, drawing a deliberate distinction from Mixture-of-Experts approaches (Time-MoE, MOIRAI-MoE) that route inputs through fixed expert networks without explicit agent-level communication or coordination. Each agent uses iTransformer as a pluggable backbone, and inter-agent information exchange is implemented via graph convolutional networks over predefined topologies (ring, star, chain, fully-connected), while a two-stage voting aggregator (confidence estimator + global voter) replaces the implicit softmax routing of MoE. The two-stage training protocol—first specializing agents on distinct sub-tasks, then freezing them and learning only communication weights and aggregation—borrows the curriculum-learning spirit of staged training but applies it to agent collaboration rather than model capacity scaling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MAFS offers a modular, interpretable alternative to monolithic forecasting architectures, where each agent's role (short-term vs. long-term horizon, trend vs. seasonality vs. spectral energy) is explicitly defined and inspectable. The finding that a star topology consistently outperforms ring, chain, and fully-connected structures provides a practical design guideline, and the ablation showing the voting aggregator contributes the largest single-component gain (7.24% MSE) highlights that adaptive decision fusion matters more than raw communication. Open questions remain around training instability at scale (beyond 16 agents), the reliance on manually chosen topologies rather than learned ones, and whether the framework generalizes to non-stationary or distribution-shift scenarios beyond the 11 benchmarks tested.
