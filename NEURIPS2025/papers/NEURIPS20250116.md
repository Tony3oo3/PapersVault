## Problem

LLMs exhibit significant knowledge deficiencies in domain-specific areas like medicine, yet existing synthetic data generation methods produce redundant samples that skew toward high-frequency pretraining distributions rather than targeting the model's actual knowledge gaps. The core challenge is that LLM knowledge is implicitly encoded in parameters, making it difficult to delineate known from unknown information, and the combinatorial explosion of knowledge graph paths makes exhaustive uncertainty detection computationally infeasible.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250116/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes the SPOKE biomedical knowledge graph (42M nodes, 160M edges across 28 entity types and 91 relation types), a target LLM (Llama-3-8B or Qwen2-7B), and a set of seed disease entities as starting points. The LLM acts as an agent that traverses the KG via cloze-style triplet completion (predicting object given subject-relation context), producing probability estimates that feed into the structural entropy reward. The output is a set of filtered synthetic QA pairs (26k for Llama-3, 128k for Qwen2) where each question targets a specific high-uncertainty knowledge path and the answer explains inter-entity relationships grounded exclusively in that path, followed by a fine-tuned model.

## Method

SENATOR employs Monte Carlo Tree Search over the knowledge graph with a structural entropy (SE) reward function that combines triplet-level self-information (negative log-probability from cloze completion) with topological structure via weighted node degrees, so that paths traversing highly connected but uncertain entities receive higher reward. The MCTS loop uses PUCT for node selection, expands leaf nodes by adding all neighbor relations, simulates random rollouts to terminal depth, and backpropagates SE-based value estimates. High-SE trajectories are then converted into synthetic QA pairs via prompt templates that constrain the answer to explain only relationships present in the identified path. A multi-tiered quality filter (regex for format, LLM judgment for logical coherence, evidence-grounding check for hallucination) removes ~38% of generated samples. The filtered data is used in a two-stage SFT pipeline: first a knowledge injection stage on the synthetic deficiency data, then a medical instruction tuning stage on 514k domain instruction samples.

## Results

On the four medical benchmarks (MedQA, MedMCQA, PubMedQA, GPQA Genetics/Mol Bio), SENATOR improves Llama-3-8B's average score from 42.31 to 47.38 (+11.98%) and Qwen2-7B's from 48.32 to 52.74 (+9.15%), using only 26k and 128k synthetic samples respectively. The most dramatic gains appear in GPQA Genetics, where Llama-3-8B jumps from 20.0 to 27.5 (+37.50%) and Qwen2-7B from 32.5 to 40.0 (+26.08%), indicating targeted repair of the most deficient knowledge areas. Notably, instruction tuning alone yields negligible or negative gains (e.g., Qwen2-7B drops from 48.32 to 47.67), confirming that the improvement stems from deficiency-targeted synthetic data rather than simply adding more general instructions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SENATOR-enhanced Llama-3-8B (Avg 47.38) and Qwen2-7B (Avg 52.74) substantially outperform all dedicated medical LLMs: PMC-LLaMA-13B (40.56), HuatuoGPT-II-13B (36.77), and Med-Alpaca-13B (28.12), despite using far fewer training tokens (0.8M–3.6M vs. 79B for PMC-LLaMA). On GPQA Genetics specifically, Qwen2-7B w/ SENATOR reaches 40.0%, surpassing even the strongest medical baseline (Llama-2-13B at 35.0%) and all 13B medical models by 15–30 points. In the appendix comparison (Table 3), Llama-3-8B w/ SENATOR beats Llama-3-8B-UltraMedical on MedQA (58.29 vs. 56.75) and PubMedQA (64.8 vs. 52.12), while Qwen2-7B w/ SENATOR dominates BioMistral-7B across all three benchmarks (59.70 vs. 44.93 on MedQA, 60.70 vs. 42.17 on MedMCQA, 63.2 vs. 56.4 on PubMedQA). The one exception is Qwen2-7B's PubMedQA score, which slightly decreases from 64.6 to 63.2 (−2.17%), suggesting the model already had strong coverage of that sub-domain and SENATOR's exploration allocated fewer resources there.

## Relation to Prior Work

SENATOR sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) knowledge-boundary detection in LLMs, where prior work relies on labeled data, confidence calibration, or refusal training (R-tuning), whereas SENATOR performs active, label-free exploration over a knowledge graph; (2) LLM self-improvement via synthetic data (Self-Instruct, STaR), which typically generates data without targeting specific gaps, while SENATOR uses structural entropy as an intrinsic MCTS reward to navigate toward high-uncertainty regions; and (3) structural entropy from network science (Li & Pan, 2016), repurposed here as a path-level uncertainty metric that captures topological importance beyond per-triplet self-information. The key conceptual departure is framing knowledge deficiency detection as a search problem in KG space guided by an information-theoretic reward, rather than a classification or scoring problem over pre-existing probes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a principled, label-free mechanism for identifying *where* an LLM is weak and generating targeted repair data, which is more efficient than indiscriminate synthetic data generation. The swap experiment—showing that synthetic data produced by one model's deficiency profile can repair another—suggests common knowledge blind spots across LLMs, pointing toward reusable domain-specific data assets. However, the approach is constrained by its dependence on a high-quality, domain-specific KG (SPOKE), limiting immediate applicability to domains lacking such structured resources. The 37.92% error rate in generated QA pairs (despite multi-tiered filtering) and the single-domain (medical) evaluation leave open questions about generalizability and the cost-benefit of KG curation at scale.
