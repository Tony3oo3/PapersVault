## Problem

While Kadkhodaie et al. (2024) identified geometry-adaptive harmonic bases as the inductive bias driving generalization in locally linear UNet-based diffusion models, the eigen-decomposition analysis that reveals these bases breaks down for transformer-based DiTs due to nonlinear operations like softmax and normalization. This leaves the question open: what architectural inductive biases actually enable the strong generalization observed in DiTs, particularly when training data is limited?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251066/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The DiT operates in pixel space at 32×32 resolution (equivalent to 512×512 latent space with 2×2 patchify), taking a noisy image and diffusion timestep as input and producing an estimated noise vector. The key architectural modification replaces global self-attention in the first 6 of 12 DiT blocks with local attention windows of sizes 3×3 through 13×13 (stride 2), constraining each output token to attend only within a spatially bounded neighborhood. All experiments use 300 test images for PSNR gap computation and evaluate on CelebA, ImageNet, MSCOCO, and four LSUN categories.

## Method

The paper identifies attention map locality as the pivotal inductive bias for DiT generalization: DiTs trained with more images develop sparse diagonal attention patterns in early layers, where each output token is influenced primarily by spatially neighboring input tokens, whereas under-trained DiTs exhibit position-invariant attention. To verify and exploit this bias, the authors replace global attention in early DiT layers with local attention windows, which enforce the diagonal structure by construction. They further show that placement matters critically—local attention in early layers reduces the PSNR gap most effectively, while interleaved or late placement degrades generalization—and that smaller effective window sizes strengthen generalization at the cost of data fitting. Across seven datasets and two model sizes (DiT-XS/1, DiT-S/1), these modifications consistently reduce the PSNR gap and improve FID, IS, and FD-DINOv2 when training with 10⁴ images, while having minimal effect at 10⁵ images where the model naturally develops local attention patterns.

## Results

Applying local attention windows (sizes 3×3 to 13×13) to the first six layers of a DiT consistently reduces the PSNR gap across all seven datasets. For DiT-XS/1 trained on 10⁴ images, the PSNR gap drops from 0.80 to 0.57 on CelebA (Δ=−0.29) and from 1.08 to 0.74 on ImageNet (Δ=−0.31). The corresponding FID improves from 9.69 to 8.46 on CelebA and from 52.57 to 43.87 on ImageNet at N=10⁴. At N=10⁵ (sufficient data), PSNR gaps are already near zero for both variants, and FID changes are negligible, confirming the benefit is specific to the data-limited regime.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is between a vanilla DiT and a DiT augmented with local attention in its first six layers, evaluated via PSNR gap and FID on seven datasets. Local attention wins on PSNR gap across all datasets and both model sizes (DiT-XS/1 and DiT-S/1) at N=10⁴, with reductions ranging from −0.18 (MSCOCO) to −0.45 (LSUN Church). On FID at N=10⁴, local attention also improves generation quality (e.g., CelebA: 9.69→8.46 for DiT-XS/1; ImageNet: 52.57→43.87), while at N=10⁵ the two variants are essentially tied, as the vanilla DiT naturally develops local attention patterns with sufficient data. A secondary comparison (Fig. 2) shows that a pixel-space DiT outperforms a hybrid UNet (Nichol & Dhariwal, 2021) with equivalent FLOPs on PSNR gap when trained on only 10³–10⁴ images, establishing DiT's inherent generalization advantage over UNet-based denoisers in the data-limited regime.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the inductive-bias analysis of Kadkhodaie et al. (2024), who showed that locally linear UNets generalize through geometry-adaptive harmonic bases visible in Jacobian eigenvectors, to the transformer-based DiT architecture. The key departure is that the eigen-decomposition framework breaks down entirely for DiTs due to nonlinear operations (softmax, normalization), prompting the authors to identify a different mechanism: locality of self-attention maps in early layers. The practical intervention—restricting attention windows in early DiT layers—repurposes local attention techniques originally developed for computational efficiency (Longformer, Neighborhood Attention) as a generalization control knob for diffusion models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a concrete, minimal architectural modification (local attention in early layers) that improves DiT generalization and generation quality under data scarcity, which is directly relevant to scientific and medical imaging domains where training data is limited. The conceptual finding that DiTs generalize through attention locality rather than harmonic bases clarifies a previously opaque architectural difference between UNets and DiTs. Open questions include whether the analysis extends to hybrid architectures and conditional transformers (e.g., MMDiT), and what other factors beyond attention locality contribute to generalization—the paper notes that non-local layers show increased deviation when early layers are restricted, suggesting additional mechanisms at play.
