## Problem

Existing overfitted INR-based codecs fit a single neural network to the entire image, limiting adaptation to local statistical variations and preventing fine-grained rate-distortion control across regions. This monolithic design also precludes flexible single-object compression, layered progressive reconstruction, and efficient handling of arbitrary-shaped regions without incurring high contour overhead.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251314/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a set of region masks (from DAVIS annotations or SAM2 segmentation) that partition the image into N mutually exclusive regions of arbitrary shape. The encoder produces a bitstream comprising quantized LSN parameters per region, a shared GMN, a shared ARM, per-region latent modulations, and C*-coded contour descriptions; the decoder reconstructs the image region-by-region and merges the results. For single-object compression, only the target region's LSN and contour are transmitted while the background is assumed known, enabling sub-800 MACs/pixel decoding.

## Method

MoRIC partitions the image into N regions and overfits a dedicated 4-layer Local Synthesis Network (LSN) to each, with a key design choice of sharing the modulator MLP parameters across regions to reduce bitrate and enable implicit local-global fusion. A shared Global Modulation Network (GMN) generates hierarchical multi-scale modulation vectors from per-region latent vectors, which are injected into each LSN layer via Progressive Concatened Modulation (PCM) in a coarse-to-fine manner, allowing global context to progressively refine local representations. Region contours are compressed using the proposed C* adaptive chain coding, which combines angle matching, global resetting, offset adaptation, and selective dilation to balance semantic coverage, curvature robustness, and RD trade-off. Training uses a two-stage quantization-aware procedure with soft-rounding and Kumaraswamy noise on latent modulations, while network parameters are quantized with a greedy-searched step size and entropy-coded under a non-learned Laplace distribution. The shared ARM provides autoregressive entropy coding for all latent modulations, and the overall RD loss is minimized jointly across regions with a common λ.

## Results

For single-object compression, MoRIC achieves BD-rate improvements of −10.43% (DAVIS) and −10.73% (Kodak) over VTM-19.1, outperforming the leading neural codec MLIC++ (−9.65% and −10.33% respectively) while using fewer than 800 MACs/pixel for decoding. For standard full image compression, MoRIC surpasses VTM-19.1 on Kodak (−2.79% BD-rate) and CLIC2020 (−10.58%), and achieves −5.0% over C3 on Kodak and −5.51% over COOL-CHIC v4 on DAVIS. MoRIC is the first neural codec to surpass VTM on Kodak in RD performance while maintaining decoding complexity below 2000 MACs/pixel, over 100× lower than MLIC++.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MoRIC outperforms all six baselines on the single-object compression task, beating MLIC++ (the strongest neural codec) by 0.78% BD-rate on DAVIS and 0.40% on Kodak, while achieving substantially lower decoding complexity (<800 vs. >10,000 MACs/pixel). On full image compression, MoRIC surpasses VTM-19.1 on Kodak (−2.79%) and CLIC2020 (−10.58%), improving over the previous overfitted codec SOTA LotteryCodec (−0.66% and −9.79% over VTM respectively). Against other overfitted codecs, MoRIC achieves −5.0% BD-rate over C3 on Kodak, −5.51% over COOL-CHIC v4 on DAVIS, and −5.48% over C3 on CLIC2020. MoRIC still trails MLIC++ in raw RD performance on full image compression, but does so with over 100× lower decoding complexity, making it the most efficient codec to surpass VTM on Kodak. All EVC variants (S/M/L) are significantly outperformed by MoRIC on both tasks, with gaps exceeding 13% BD-rate on Kodak.

## Relation to Prior Work

MoRIC extends the overfitted codec lineage (COIN → COMBINER → COOL-CHIC → C3 → LotteryCodec) by replacing the single whole-image INR with a modular, region-wise design where each region gets a dedicated local synthesis network (LSN). The region partitioning draws on INR literature on local latent vectors and modulated activations (Chabra et al., Mehta et al.), while the contour compression adapts adaptive chain coding from the 1990s (Chang et al.) into a new C* coding scheme tailored for INR-based codecs. The Global Modulation Network with Progressive Concatened Modulation (PCM) introduces a coarse-to-fine mechanism for injecting multi-scale global context into local overfitting, a design not present in prior overfitted codecs that used fixed architectures across all images.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MoRIC is the first overfitted codec to support semantically meaningful layered compression, where a background LSN produces a recognizable coarse reconstruction that is progressively refined by region-specific LSNs — a capability with direct implications for adaptive streaming and bandwidth-constrained deployment. The finding that local INRs exhibit cross-region semantic generalizability (inferring structure of untrained regions) opens a new direction for scalable, modular INR-based codecs. However, the high per-image encoding complexity remains a significant practical limitation, restricting near-term use to scenarios with one-time encoding and repeated decoding (e.g., surveillance). Open questions include whether region selection can be integrated into the coding loop, whether the approach extends to video via temporal contour correlation, and whether the semantic generalizability finding holds at larger scales with more regions.
