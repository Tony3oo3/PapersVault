## Problem

Coarsened PDE fields used by fixed-resolution neural emulators discard fine-scale information that could reduce emulation error, while unstructured latent representations lack the spatial structure needed to enforce physical constraints like incompressibility or conservation laws. No existing approach simultaneously exploits high-resolution input features, supports hard physical constraints, and enables efficient autoregressive rollouts at coarse resolution.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251285/cropped_page4_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The encoder consumes high-resolution PDE fields (512²) coarsened only to an intermediate encoding resolution (r' < r), while the processor operates on the hybrid representation at a lower rollout resolution (e.g., 32² or 64²). Outputs are coarsened future PDE fields at the rollout resolution, and an optional physics-constrained decoder reconstructs fields at a target resolution (e.g., 512²) by combining fixed interpolation with a learned correction conditioned on both the rolled-out coarsened and latent fields. The coarsening operator A_r is chosen to respect conservation laws (face-averaging for staggered grids, non-overlapping averaging for collocated grids).

## Method

The hybrid representation augments coarsened PDE fields x_t with n learned latent variable fields c_t extracted by a spatially structured encoder from higher-resolution inputs, so the processor (an mUnet with extra channels) advances both components autoregressively at the coarse rollout resolution. The training loss is computed only on the coarsened PDE field channels, avoiding costly losses on non-physical latent dimensions while still encouraging the encoder to extract useful fine-scale features. Physical constraints are enforced as hard constraints: incompressibility via a vector-potential curl layer on velocity channels, and conservation via flux-based updates, applied to both the processor output and the decoder. A separate decoder, trained with frozen encoder/processor weights, reconstructs high-resolution fields as fixed interpolation plus a physics-constrained learned correction from both x_t and c_t. Training uses an unrolled curriculum (K = 1,2,4,8,16) with early stopping, and the HREs benefit more from longer training rollouts than mUnet baselines, suggesting the encoder requires extended rollouts to learn features useful for long-horizon prediction.

## Results

At 32² rollout resolution after 64 autoregressive steps, HREs achieve RMSE 0.71±0.23 and correlation 0.68±0.21 on KF, and RMSE 0.29±0.08 and correlation 0.96±0.03 on ID, outperforming the strongest baseline (mUnet) by 30–50% in RMSE. On the 1D Kuramoto-Sivashinsky task at r=64, HREs achieve near-perfect predictions after 16 steps while mUnet's correlation drops to 0.5. HREs also continue to benefit from longer unrolled training (up to 16+ steps), whereas mUnet plateaus after 8-step training rollouts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HREs outperform mUnet (Gupta & Brandstetter, TMLR 2023) by a wide margin on KF (RMSE 0.71 vs 1.07, correlation 0.68 vs 0.35) and ID (RMSE 0.29 vs 0.58, correlation 0.96 vs 0.78) at 32² rollout resolution, though the gap narrows at 64² where mUnet is comparable on ID. Against FNO (Li et al., ICLR 2021), HREs win decisively on all tasks (e.g., KF correlation 0.68 vs 0.12), with FNO being the fastest but least accurate FRNE. DilResNet (Stachenfeld et al., 2021) is sometimes competitive for shorter rollouts but falls well behind HREs at 64 steps (ID RMSE 1.77 vs 0.29). FactFormer (Li et al., NeurIPS 2023) matches mUnet on ID (RMSE 0.58) but is clearly outperformed by HREs on KF (correlation 0.28 vs 0.68). DINo (Yin et al., ICLR 2023) and DPOT-S (Hao et al., ICML 2024) are the weakest baselines, with DPOT-S achieving the highest RMSE on both tasks (1.85 on KF, 1.65 on ID). HREs also maintain their advantage under out-of-distribution initial conditions (varying peak wavenumber), confirming the learned representation does not become a liability on unseen data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper synthesizes two established PDE emulation paradigms: fixed-resolution neural emulators (FRNEs) that operate autoregressively on coarsened PDE fields (mUnet, FNO, DilResNet, DPOT), and unstructured latent space emulators (ULSEs) that roll out compact latent vectors (FactFormer, DINo). The key departure is the hybrid representation, which retains spatially structured coarsened fields (enabling hard physical constraints like incompressibility and conservation) while augmenting them with learned latent variables extracted from higher-resolution inputs via a trained encoder. Architecturally, the processor closely mirrors mUnet, so the contribution is in the representation and the physics-constrained decoder that jointly uses both field types, rather than in a new network design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

HREs offer a practical path toward emulators that are simultaneously fast (coarse-resolution rollouts), accurate (fine-scale information from latent channels), and physically consistent (hard constraints on conservation and incompressibility), which is critical for climate and weather applications. The finding that HREs benefit more from unrolled training than FRNEs (which plateau) suggests the encoder needs long-horizon supervision to extract useful latent features, a useful practical insight. Open questions include extension to 3D systems, non-periodic boundaries, generative losses for chaotic dynamics, and which processor architectures benefit most from the hybrid representation. Limitations include testing only on 2D periodic domains and the observation that discontinuous fields (compressible flow) reduce the benefit of fine-scale latent information.
