## Problem

Contextual online pricing with offline data has been studied under the assumption that offline and online data share the same distribution, but distributional shifts are ubiquitous in practice. The degenerate K-armed bandit case with biased offline data has tight bounds, yet extending those results to contextual pricing with continuous action spaces and pricing-specific structure is non-trivial and a naive extension yields sub-optimal regret. This paper formulates the Contextual Online Pricing with Biased Offline Data (CB-OPOD) problem and provides the first minimax-optimal, instance-dependent regret guarantees in this setting.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes a pre-existing offline pricing dataset of N samples (context features, historical prices, observed demands) treated as deterministic with no distributional assumptions, plus a known bias bound V on the parameter shift ‖θ′−θ*‖. At each of T online periods it receives a context vector (x_t ∈ R^{d1}, y_t ∈ R^{d2}) and outputs a single price p_t ∈ [l,u]. The regret is defined conditional on the realized offline data, and the theoretical guarantees depend on the offline Gram matrix dispersion λ_min(Σ̂), sample size N, and the instance-dependent distance δ² between the offline empirical price policy and the unknown optimal price.

## Method

The CO3 algorithm (scalar elasticity, d₂=1) constructs a confidence set as the intersection of three ellipsoids at each time t: a classical online-safety ellipsoid, a Euclidean-norm ellipsoid that leverages offline data to sharpen the parameter estimate, and a combined online-offline ellipsoid that enables aggressive exploitation when the offline data is far from the optimum. A novel offline test phase decides whether the empirical offline price policy p̂(x,y)=Âᵀx/y suffices on its own (yielding O(δ²T) regret) or whether optimistic pricing over the three-ellipsoid set is needed. For general elasticity (d₂>1), GCO3 uses a two-ellipsoid confidence set and achieves a minimax-optimal worst-case rate Õ((d₁+d₂)√T ∧ (V²T + (d₁+d₂)T/λ_min(Σ̂))). When the bias bound V is unknown, RCO3 runs a uniform-pricing test phase of length T^α to estimate the true bias and adaptively switches between offline-informed pricing and the pure-online baseline, guaranteeing sub-linear regret for all V_true and strictly improving on online-only methods when V_true is small.

## Results

For the scalar price elasticity setting, CO3 achieves a minimax-optimal, instance-dependent regret of $\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(d_1\sqrt{T} \wedge (V^2T + d_1T/\lambda_{\min}(\hat{\Sigma}) + (N\wedge T)\delta^2))$, which tightens to $\mathcal{O}(\delta^2 T)$ in a corner regime. For general price elasticity, GCO3 attains the worst-case minimax rate $\tilde{\mathcal{O}}((d_1+d_2)\sqrt{T} \wedge (V^2T + (d_1+d_2)T/\lambda_{\min}(\hat{\Sigma})))$. In experiments with $T=1000$ and $V_{\text{true}}=\Theta(T^{-5/16})$, CO3/GCO3 decisively outperform all four baselines under a tight bias bound ($V=1.1V_{\text{true}}$) and match pure-online UCB under a loose bound ($V=10V_{\text{true}}$), confirming the never-worse safety property.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against pure-online UCB [2], CO3/GCO3 strictly outperform it whenever the bias bound is small ($V^2 \in \mathcal{O}(T^{-1/2})$) and the offline data is well-dispersed ($\lambda_{\min}(\hat{\Sigma}) \in \Omega(\sqrt{T})$); under a loose bias bound ($V=10V_{\text{true}}$), CO3/GCO3 match UCB's regret exactly, demonstrating the never-worse guarantee. Against UCB-Offline [7,31], which naively combines offline and online data in a single confidence ellipsoid, CO3/GCO3 substantially outperform it in the presence of bias: UCB-Offline accumulates regret faster than even pure-online UCB because it uncritically trusts the shifted offline data. The paper also compares against Thompson-sampling baselines (TS and TS-Offline); TS-Offline suffers the same degradation as UCB-Offline under distributional shift, while TS matches pure-online UCB. RCO3 (robust variant, unknown $V$) outperforms UCB when $V_{\text{true}}^2 \lesssim T^{-3/4}$ and reverts to UCB-level regret for larger biases, with the transition peak near $V_{\text{true}}^2 \asymp T^{-1/4}$ as predicted by Theorem 5.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the contextual online pricing literature (Ban & Keskin [2], Bu et al. [7], Zhai [31]) by incorporating biased offline data, a setting previously studied only in the degenerate K-armed bandit case (Cheung & Lyu [10]). The key methodological departure is the three-ellipsoid confidence set in CO3, which replaces the single ellipsoid used in unbiased offline-data pricing [7, 31] and the two-interval construction of [10], enabling the algorithm to simultaneously guarantee online safety, exploit offline dispersion, and adapt to the instance-dependent distance δ². The paper also shows that the K-armed bandit technique of [10] does not transfer directly to contextual pricing (yielding a suboptimal VT term instead of V²T), motivating the new construction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to provide minimax-optimal, instance-dependent regret guarantees for contextual online pricing with biased offline data, resolving an open gap between the K-armed bandit and full contextual settings. The impossibility result (Corollary 1) clarifies that some information about the distributional shift is necessary, while the robust RCO3 algorithm handles the practically important case where the bias bound is unknown. Open questions include extending to nonlinear demand models, analyzing model misspecification, and defining an appropriate instance-dependent distance metric for the general d₂ > 1 case, where only a worst-case rate is currently available.
