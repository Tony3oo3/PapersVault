## Problem

Bogomolnaia and Moulin introduced competitiveness (CP) as a fairness criterion for congested assignments—requiring both envy-freeness and non-wastefulness—but left open whether determining the existence of a CP assignment is computationally tractable. This paper resolves that open question affirmatively and further establishes the NP-hardness of two natural relaxations: finding envy-free top-guaranteed assignments and maximally competitive top-guaranteed assignments.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254805/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The input consists of m posts, n agents, and for each agent a weak order over all m×n tuples (post, congestion level), subject to the congestion-aversion constraint that for any fixed post, lower congestion is always strictly preferred. The primary algorithm outputs either a competitive assignment (a partition of agents into posts satisfying both envy-freeness and non-wastefulness) or a certificate of non-existence, running in O(m²(n+m)²) time. The optimization variants output envy-free top-guaranteed assignments or maximally competitive top-guaranteed assignments, with the latter minimizing the number of unsatisfied agents.

## Method

The polynomial-time CP algorithm has two key components. First, it reduces the general problem to the restricted case where every post is non-empty by guessing the number k of empty posts and augmenting the instance with k dummy agents, two auxiliary agents, and two fallback posts (Construction 1), forcing any CP assignment to fill previously empty posts with distinct dummy agents. Second, for the restricted problem, it iteratively refines a congestion table T (initially all ones) by constructing a flow network where the source connects to post-nodes with capacity T[a], post-nodes connect to agent-nodes if the agent's most-preferred tuple at the current congestion levels is that post, and agent-nodes connect to the sink with unit capacity. If the max flow equals |V|, a CP assignment is derived directly; otherwise, an obstruction (analogous to a Hall-violation set) is identified via BFS in the residual graph, and the congestion entries for posts in the obstruction are incremented. For the NP-hard variants, hardness is shown via reductions from X3C and CLIQUE, complemented by FPT algorithms parameterized by the number of agents or posts.

## Results

The paper's main result (Theorem 1) is a polynomial-time algorithm running in O(m²(n+m)²) time that decides whether a competitive (CP) assignment exists, resolving an open question posed by Bogomolnaia and Moulin [6]. For the optimization variants, the paper proves NP-hardness of finding a top-guaranteed and envy-free (EF+TG) assignment (Theorem 2, via reduction from X3C) and W[1]-hardness of finding a top-guaranteed and maximally competitive (MAXCP+TG) assignment (Theorem 4, via reduction from CLIQUE). Complementing these hardness results, the paper provides FPT algorithms for EF+TG with respect to both the number of agents n and posts m (Theorem 3), and for MAXCP+TG with respect to n (Theorem 5), as well as XP algorithms with respect to m and t (the number of unsatisfied agents).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical experiments, datasets, or baseline comparisons. The primary 'comparison' is against the open question left by Bogomolnaia and Moulin [6], who introduced the concepts of competitiveness and top-guarantees but left the computational complexity of determining CP assignments unresolved. The paper resolves this affirmatively by providing the first polynomial-time algorithm. The hardness results (NP-hardness for EF+TG, W[1]-hardness for MAXCP+TG) establish that the natural relaxations of CP are computationally intractable in general, while the parameterized algorithms (FPT in n or m) provide tractable avenues for instances with small numbers of agents or posts. No prior algorithmic baselines exist for these specific decision problems, as the complexity was previously unknown.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Bogomolnaia and Moulin's (2025) introduction of competitiveness (CP) and top-guarantees (TG) as fairness criteria for congested assignments, resolving their open question about the computational complexity of determining CP. The broader framework draws on congestion games (Rosenthal, Milchtaich) for Nash stability and on group activity selection (GAS) for the general structure of agent preferences over (resource, group-size) pairs. Methodologically, the paper departs from the best-reply improvement approach used for Nash stability by employing a maximum-flow formulation with iterative congestion-table updates, combined with b-matching for the optimization variants.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The polynomial-time algorithm for competitiveness is practically significant for fair resource allocation in settings like school choice, traffic management, and cloud computing, where one needs to verify whether a fair and non-wasteful assignment exists. The NP-hardness and W[1]-hardness results for the relaxation problems (EF+TG, maxCP+TG) delineate the boundary of tractability, while the parameterized algorithms offer practical tools when the number of agents or posts is small. Open questions include extending the analysis to weighted agents, heterogeneous congestion responses, and control strategies (e.g., minimum posts to remove for CP), as well as empirical validation of the algorithms on real-world instances.
