## Problem

While layer-wise selective updates for LLM training have been explored, fine-grained intra-layer parameter selection lacks a systematic methodology with convergence guarantees. Cautious Optimizers introduced momentum-gradient alignment as a selection criterion but provide no stochastic convergence proof, and AdaBelief's second-moment-based adaptation is incompatible with optimizers like Lion and Muon. The paper asks whether momentum-gradient alignment can serve as a truly plug-and-play selective update mechanism in stochastic optimization, and what design choices are necessary to preserve convergence.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250691/cropped_page3_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

At each iteration, MGUP consumes the current stochastic gradient g_t, the momentum vector m_t (or the normalized update vector u_t for Adam variants), and the base optimizer's per-parameter step size η_t. It produces a binary-valued per-parameter mask φ_t ∈ {α, γ}^d that multiplies element-wise with the base update, where α = 1/τ amplifies the top-K most aligned parameters and γ = τ attenuates the rest. The mask is recomputed every iteration via a full sort of the d-dimensional alignment score vector, adding O(d log d) overhead per step.

## Method

MGUP computes an alignment score s_{t,i} = m_{t,i}·g_{t,i} (or u_{t,i}·g_{t,i} for Adam-type optimizers) for each parameter, sorts all d parameters by this score in decreasing order, and assigns the top K = ⌊τ·d⌋ parameters an amplified step size α·η_t while the remaining parameters receive a reduced but non-zero step size γ·η_t. The critical design choice over Cautious Optimizers is that γ > 0: the paper proves via a counterexample that setting γ = 0 (nullifying misaligned updates) causes divergence in stochastic settings, and provides formal convergence guarantees (O(log T / √T) in expectation and high probability) for MGUP-AdamW under standard non-convex assumptions. The mechanism is plug-and-play, requiring only a one-line modification to any momentum-based optimizer's update rule, and is demonstrated with AdamW, Lion, and Muon. The default hyperparameter τ = 1/2 maximizes the average update magnitude factor (1 + τ − τ²), providing an intuitive acceleration effect while maintaining stability.

## Results

On the GLUE benchmark (RoBERTa-base fine-tuning), MGUP-AdamW achieves the best average score of 85.15, outperforming standard AdamW (84.74), C-AdamW (83.69), and all other baselines. On GSM-8K (LLaMA2-7B fine-tuning), MGUP-AdamW reaches 34.96% validation accuracy with the lowest training loss (0.056), surpassing AdamW (34.53%) and LDAdamW (34.88%). In LLaMA2-71M pretraining on WikiText-103, MGUP-AdamW delivers a 1.6× speedup over AdamW, MGUP-Lion achieves a 2.5× speedup over Lion, and MGUP-Muon yields a ~1.2× speedup over Muon, all while maintaining or improving generalization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MGUP-AdamW outperforms standard AdamW by 0.41 points on average GLUE score (85.15 vs. 84.74) and by 0.43% on GSM-8K accuracy (34.96% vs. 34.53%), while also achieving the lowest training loss. Against the Cautious Optimizer variants, MGUP-AdamW leads C-AdamW by 1.46 points on GLUE average and 0.28% on GSM-8K, with C-AdamW showing notably weaker performance on RTE (71.12 vs. 75.81) and CoLA (57.29 vs. 59.83). MGUP-Lion and MGUP-Muon both surpass their cautious counterparts (C-Lion: 83.23, C-Muon: 83.98) and their base optimizers (Lion: 83.39, Muon: 81.65) on GLUE, with MGUP-Lion gaining 0.79 points over Lion and MGUP-Muon gaining 2.52 points over Muon. Against memory-efficient baselines, MGUP-AdamW leads GaLore (82.25), LDAdamW (83.58), and Adam-mini (80.44) by 2.9, 1.57, and 4.71 points respectively on GLUE average. In pretraining, MGUP variants consistently achieve faster convergence (1.2×–2.5× speedup) than their base optimizers while maintaining or improving validation loss, whereas Cautious variants (especially C-Lion) exhibit training instability with early loss spikes.

## Relation to Prior Work

MGUP directly refines the Cautious Optimizers framework [10], which applies a binary mask to skip updates when momentum and gradient signs disagree, by replacing the all-or-nothing mask with a top-K selection that assigns amplified (α·η) or reduced (γ·η) but always non-zero step sizes. Conceptually, it also inherits the 'trustworthy momentum' intuition from AdaBelief [11] and the broader selective-update lineage (SIFT, LISA, BAdam, AutoFreeze), but operates at a finer intra-layer granularity. The theoretical analysis builds on the Adam convergence literature [32, 35, 37, 38], extending it to handle the non-uniform step-size mask introduced by the top-K mechanism, which prior convergence proofs for Adam do not cover.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first stochastic convergence guarantee for a masked Adam variant, filling a gap left by Cautious Optimizers, and the 1D counterexample concretely demonstrates why γ=0 (binary masking) can cause divergence—a practical warning for anyone using C-Adam. However, the theoretical coverage is limited to MGUP-AdamW without weight decay, and the empirical validation relies on small models (71M–150M for pretraining, 7B for fine-tuning), leaving open whether the 1.6×–2.5× speedups and stability gains persist at the 7B+ scale where most LLM training occurs. The pre-selection of τ (default 0.5) without an adaptive schedule is a notable limitation that the authors flag for future work.
