## Problem

Existing end-to-end multimodal GUI models are trained exclusively on nearly error-free offline trajectories, leaving them unable to recognize mistakes, backtrack, or recover from failures in real-world interactions. Moreover, the standard offline SFT process focused solely on successful examples can inadvertently suppress the base MLLM's inherent reflection and verification abilities, making subsequent online RL or bootstrapping ineffective at activating these behaviors.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253409/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a task instruction, the last 4 screenshots (past steps downsampled to 448×448, current at full resolution), a persistent memory bank, and the complete action history. Output is a three-part structured response: a free-form action thought (3–4 sentences integrating observation, reflection, and rationale without rigid labels), a natural-language action description following fixed per-action templates, and a grounded atomic action with coordinates normalized to integers in [0, 999]. The action space includes 14 atomic actions, notably Memorize (storing information into the memory bank) and Answer (retrieving/communicating information), which are frequently omitted in prior GUI agent formulations.

## Method

GUI-Reflection integrates self-reflection and error correction across three training stages. In GUI pre-training, a novel GUI-Reflection Task Suite (Action Verification, Action Reversal, Mistake-informed Reattempt) explicitly trains reflection-oriented atomic capabilities that standard GUI grounding data neglects. In offline SFT, a fully automatic pipeline constructs realistic error scenarios from existing successful trajectories via two strategies: (a) modifying the task goal so a correct action becomes a natural mistake, then generating reflection and rollback annotations, and (b) inserting an ineffective no-op action before the correct step and augmenting the thought with recognition of the failed attempt. In the online stage, an iterative reflection tuning algorithm operates in a distributed Android environment with 215 task templates across 11 apps; for unsuccessful rollouts, it automatically annotates pre-error correction (the correct action at the failure step) and post-error reflection (recognition plus informed reattempt), while successful trajectories undergo step-wise correctness filtering. Curriculum learning progressively introduces harder tasks, and sampling weights are dynamically adjusted by success rate across iterations.

## Results

On the AndroidWorld benchmark, GUI-Reflection-8B achieves a 34.5% success rate using only image input, outperforming the same-scale UI-TARS-7B (33.0%) and significantly exceeding OS-Genesis-8B (16.9%) and Aguvis-72B (26.1%). On the GUI-Reflection Task Suite, GUI-Pretrain-Ref-8B reaches 87.56% accuracy on Action Verification and 93.81% on Action Reversal, matching or approaching top closed-source models (Gemini-2.5-Pro: 88.22% and 95.71% respectively). On the Mistake-informed Reattempt task, GUI-Pretrain-Ref achieves 89.61% on ScreenSpot and 90.50% on ScreenSpotv2 at the 3rd attempt, surpassing its own pass@3 scores (87.35% and 88.84%), demonstrating effective error-informed reattempt capability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among 8B-scale end-to-end models, GUI-Reflection-8B (34.5%) outperforms UI-TARS-7B (33.0%) by 1.5 points and OS-Genesis-8B (16.9%) by 17.6 points on AndroidWorld, while also surpassing the much larger Aguvis-72B (26.1%) by 8.4 points. Against agent-based frameworks, GUI-Reflection-8B exceeds GPT-4o+UGround (32.8%) despite using only image input (vs. image + accessibility tree), but trails GPT-4o+Aguvis-7B (37.1%) and GPT-4o+Aria-UI (44.8%). On the reflection-specific tasks, GUI-Pretrain-Ref-8B (87.56% Action Verification, 93.81% Action Reversal) matches Gemini-2.5-Flash (87.85%, 95.24%) and approaches Gemini-2.5-Pro (88.22%, 95.71%), while dramatically outperforming the base InternVL2.5-8B (62.76%, 48.33%) and InternVL3-8B (60.11%, 63.80%), confirming that standard GUI pre-training degrades reflection abilities and the proposed task suite restores them. The ablation (Table 2) shows that adding reflection data to offline SFT lifts AndroidWorld success from 14.58% to 23.61%, and further applying iterative online reflection tuning pushes it to 34.72%, validating the multi-stage design.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the end-to-end multimodal GUI agent paradigm (UI-TARS, Aguvis, OS-ATLAS, SeeClick) by explicitly integrating self-reflection and error correction as first-class training objectives across all stages, rather than treating them as emergent or post-hoc behaviors. The closest prior work is UI-TARS, which also targets reflection/correction but confines it to a final online bootstrapping stage and relies on human-annotated feedback; GUI-Reflection departs by decomposing reflection into atomic pre-training tasks (Action Verification, Action Reversal, Mistake-informed Reattempt), generating reflection data automatically from error-free trajectories, and introducing iterative online reflection tuning in a distributed Android environment. The work is also informed by LLM research showing that base-model verification and reflection abilities are prerequisites for successful RL, highlighting that standard GUI SFT on error-free data can inadvertently suppress these capabilities.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that standard GUI pre-training degrades reflection capabilities (e.g., Action Verification accuracy drops from 62.76% to 57.95% after GUI pre-training) challenges the assumption that domain-specific pre-training is neutral for all base-model abilities, and motivates explicit reflection-oriented training signals. The fully automated data pipeline (no human annotation for reflection scenarios) and the 215-template distributed Android environment make the framework practically adoptable for the GUI agent community. Key limitations include the mobile-only scope, focus on visual/action-level errors rather than high-level planning failures, and reliance on a single base model family (InternVL), leaving open whether the reflection degradation and recovery findings generalize across architectures and platforms.
