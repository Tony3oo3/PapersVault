## Problem

Per-neuron functional embeddings from predictive models of mouse V1 have not yielded well-separated clusters that could correspond to discrete cell types, leaving it unclear whether this reflects model limitations or a genuine continuum of neuronal functions. The paper asks whether an explicit clustering inductive bias can reveal hidden discrete structure, framing the approach as a model-driven hypothesis test: if distinct functional cell types exist, the bias should improve embedding structure and cluster consistency.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254602/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes grayscale natural images or videos (with optional behavioral channels such as pupil size, locomotion speed, and eye position concatenated as extra input channels) and produces per-neuron predicted firing rates via a shared convolutional core and neuron-specific Gaussian readouts. The clustering component outputs soft cluster assignments, learned cluster centers μ_j, and per-cluster diagonal scale matrices Σ_j. The per-neuron embeddings z_i are the readout weight vectors (dimension K, the number of feature channels), representing each neuron's full stimulus-response function rather than a single activation.

## Method

DECEMber augments the standard Poisson regression loss of a core-readout predictive model with a KL-divergence clustering loss on the per-neuron readout embeddings, weighted by a strength parameter β. Instead of learning cluster centroids by gradient descent (as in vanilla DEC), it fits a multivariate Student's t-mixture model with per-cluster diagonal scale matrices Σ_j and updates both centers and scales via a closed-form EM step after each minibatch. The t-distribution with learned, non-isotropic scales is critical: vanilla DEC's fixed unit-scale t-distribution causes cluster-center collapse in regression settings where embedding magnitudes are constrained by the data-fitting loss. Training proceeds in two phases—pretraining the predictive model for m epochs, then jointly optimizing the model loss and clustering loss with the EM updates interleaved with SGD steps on the core and readout parameters. The approach is architecture-agnostic and generalizes across Gaussian and attention readouts, static and dynamic cores, and multiple species and visual areas.

## Results

On marmoset retinal ganglion cells with known cell-type labels, DECEMber achieves ARI = 0.96 ± 0.01 across 3 seeds for 4 clusters while maintaining predictive performance of 0.81 ± 0.07 (Pearson correlation). On mouse V1 (SENSORIUM 2022), DECEMber doubles the ARI compared to the GMM baseline (Fig. 4H) but shows no sharp peak at any specific cluster count, suggesting a functional continuum rather than discrete cell types. Predictive performance is preserved across all datasets: mouse retina correlation stays at 0.4727 ± 0.0008 and macaque V4 at 0.305 ± 0.003, matching baseline within standard deviation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against vanilla DEC (Xie et al. [23]), DECEMber decisively wins in the toy example: DEC's fixed unit-scale t-distribution causes cluster centers to collapse to a single point within a few iterations, while DECEMber's EM-updated multivariate t-mixture with learned diagonal scale matrices converges to the true cluster means (‖μ₁‖ ≈ 11.3, ‖μ₂‖ ≈ 17). Against k-means (MacQueen [39]), DECEMber achieves higher ARI on marmoset RGC (Fig. 3A) and is more robust to initialization, whereas k-means is sensitive to seed choice (Suppl. Fig. 12). Against the GMM post-hoc clustering baseline from Turishcheva et al. [13], DECEMber doubles ARI on mouse V1 and achieves comparable consistency on the rotation-equivariant model (App. A.8, Fig. 16) while eliminating the need for a post-hoc alignment step and improving predictive performance from ~38.1% to ~39.5%. On macaque V4, DECEMber doubles embedding consistency over the GMM baseline (Fig. 7B), confirming generalization across the attention-readout architecture.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Deep Embedding Clustering (DEC, Xie et al. 2016) from the autoencoder setting to neural response prediction, replacing DEC's fixed unit-scale Student's t-distribution with a multivariate t-mixture model whose per-cluster scale matrices are updated via the EM algorithm (McLachlan & Peel 2000). It builds on the core-readout predictive model architecture from the Sensorium competition (Willeke et al. 2022) and the Gaussian/attention readouts (Lurz et al. 2020, Pierzchlewicz et al. 2023), and directly addresses the embedding consistency problem identified by Turishcheva et al. (2024), who showed that post-hoc clustering of neuronal embeddings yields low ARI scores. The key methodological departure is that cluster centers and shapes are updated via closed-form EM steps rather than gradient descent, avoiding the degenerate collapse of DEC's fixed-scale centroids in regression-constrained embedding spaces.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled tool (DECEMber) for testing whether neuronal populations contain discrete functional cell types, and its application to mouse V1 yields a negative result consistent with prior morphological and electrophysiological evidence: no clear ARI peak emerges across cluster counts, supporting the continuum hypothesis for excitatory V1 neurons. The method's generality across species (mice, marmosets, macaques), visual areas (retina, V1, V4), and readout architectures (Gaussian, attention) makes it a reusable component for future multi-modal cell-type definition efforts. Open questions include whether the approach can be integrated with contrastive or generative embedding methods, how to automatically determine the number of clusters without expensive multi-run ARI sweeps, and whether the t-distribution assumption is appropriate for all embedding geometries.
