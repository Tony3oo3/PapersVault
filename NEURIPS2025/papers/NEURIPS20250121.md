## Problem

Standard softmax attention has quadratic Θ(N²d) complexity in sequence length, a major bottleneck for long-sequence tasks. Existing sub-quadratic alternatives are either not transferable (requiring retraining or fine-tuning of pre-trained models) or fail to deliver practical speed-ups on modern GPUs due to hardware incompatibility. MonarchAttention addresses both gaps simultaneously by providing a zero-shot, hardware-efficient attention substitute that directly replaces softmax attention with minimal quality loss.

## Input / Output

Given query, key, and value matrices Q, K, V ∈ R^{N×d} for a single attention head, the method produces an approximate output O = M·V where M is a Monarch matrix (row-permuted block rank-one) with block size b that approximates softmax(QK^T). The number of alternating maximization steps T (typically 1–3) and block size b jointly control the FLOP-accuracy tradeoff, with T=1 already closing most of the approximation gap. Critically, the Monarch factors L and R are never materialized in high-bandwidth memory; only O(Nd) intermediate states (α and c vectors) are stored, with all intermediate computations fused in on-chip SRAM.

## Method

The core insight is to reframe softmax attention as an optimization problem via the variational form of softmax (maximizing ⟨A, QK^T⟩ + H(A) over the simplex), then constrain A to have Monarch matrix structure. This yields closed-form alternating maximization updates for the L and R factors, where each update reduces to a softmax over a batched matmul result. The rank-one block structure makes the entropy term separable across L and R, reducing per-block computation from Θ(mb) to Θ(m+b) and achieving overall Θ(N√N·d) time complexity. The implementation fuses each update step into FlashAttention-like Triton kernels that exploit tensor cores for batched dense matmuls; because the effective sequence length per kernel is only √N, sequence-length tiling is unnecessary, yielding optimal Θ(Nd) IO complexity and up to 8.2× wall-time speed-up over FlashAttention-2 at N=16K.

## Results

MonarchAttention achieves 1.4×, 4.5×, and 8.2× wall-time speedups over FlashAttention-2 at sequence lengths N=256, 4K, and 16K respectively on an NVIDIA A40 GPU. On ViT/ImageNet-1K, it loses only 5% top-5 accuracy while reducing attention FLOPs by 80%, and matches softmax performance at 50% fewer FLOPs. On DiT-XL/ImageNet, replacing the first half of attention layers yields FID 0.39 and sFID 0.66 (vs. Nyströmformer's 8.17 and 19.01) at comparable FLOPs. On BART/BookSum at N=8192, MonarchAttention improves ROUGE-1 by 0.75 and ROUGE-L by 0.5 over the N=2048 softmax baseline with fewer FLOPs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MonarchAttention consistently outperforms all compared baselines across every task. Against Nyströmformer, the gap is most dramatic on DiT image generation: replacing the first half of layers yields FID 0.39 vs. 8.17 and sFID 0.66 vs. 19.01, while on BART summarization at N=8192 MonarchAttention achieves ROUGE-1 34.60 vs. 32.86 (zero-shot) and 35.38 vs. 33.94 (post-swap fine-tuned). On ViT/ImageNet and RoBERTa/SQuAD (Figure 2), MonarchAttention dominates Performer, linear attention, CosFormer, and Nyströmformer across the entire FLOP budget, achieving higher accuracy at every compute level. Against FlashAttention-2, MonarchAttention is a speed comparison rather than a quality one: it achieves up to 8.2× speedup at N=16K and 1.4× at N=256, with the advantage growing with sequence length due to its Θ(N√N·d) complexity versus FlashAttention-2's Θ(N²·d). The paper notes that sparsity/LSH-based methods (Reformer, Scatterbrain, HyperAttention) were excluded from direct comparison because they lack efficient GPU implementations, and learnable low-rank methods (Linformer, Hedgehog) were excluded because they require retraining, incompatible with the zero-shot setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

MonarchAttention sits at the intersection of three prior threads: the Monarch structured matrix family (Dao et al., 2022a), the IO-aware streaming computation paradigm of FlashAttention (Dao et al., 2022b), and the variational/entropic formulation of softmax (Blondel et al., 2019). Unlike prior sub-quadratic attention methods that approximate the attention matrix as low-rank (Performer, linear attention) or sparse (Reformer, Scatterbrain), MonarchAttention projects softmax attention onto Monarch matrices via alternating maximization of the variational objective, inheriting the data-dependent, non-fixed-support expressiveness of sparse methods while retaining the tensor-core-friendly batched matmul structure of low-rank methods. The paper explicitly positions itself as a generalization of block-sparse FlashAttention to the broader Monarch class, and distinguishes itself from Monarch Mixer (Fu et al., 2023) by being data-dependent (attention-based) rather than a fixed token-mixing operator.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The zero-shot, no-retraining conversion of attention layers is the paper's most practically significant contribution: it means existing pre-trained Transformers can be made sub-quadratic without any fine-tuning, a property that pure low-rank or fixed-sparse methods lack. The hardware efficiency via tensor-core batched matmuls and the strictly better worst-case IO complexity versus FlashAttention make this a viable drop-in replacement rather than a theoretical curiosity. Key open questions include extending MonarchAttention to autoregressive decoding (where no full attention matrix exists), integrating causal masks, and generalizing to other structured matrix classes or sparser nonlinearities like α-entmax that might better match the heterogeneous structure of real attention maps.
