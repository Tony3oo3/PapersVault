## Problem

Existing parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) methods such as LoRA, adapters, and prompt tuning underperform when naively applied to large-scale 3D point cloud transformers because they treat points as orderless tokens, ignoring the irregular spatial structure and geometric distribution shifts between pre-training and downstream domains. Moreover, the local-attention design prevalent in 3D transformers prevents these methods from capturing scene-wide context, further limiting their adaptation capacity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255616/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes large-scale 3D point cloud scenes containing on the order of millions of unordered, sparse points in R³, processed through a frozen pre-trained point cloud transformer backbone (Sonata or PTv3-PPT). It outputs per-point semantic class labels for scene segmentation. The GEM module is inserted at each transformer block and updates only ~1.6% of total parameters (1.8M out of 124.8M), with the spatial adapter operating on local voxel neighborhoods (k=3) and the context adapter using m=4 latent tokens that attend to all points globally.

## Method

GEM (Geometry Encoding Mixer) comprises two complementary adapters inserted into each transformer block of a frozen point cloud backbone. The Spatial Adapter refines the pre-trained positional encoding via a lightweight 3D convolutional bottleneck (down-project, neighborhood convolution over k³ voxels, up-project) that learns fine-grained local spatial details specific to the target domain. The Context Adapter introduces m learned latent tokens (m≪n) that first attend to all points to aggregate global scene context, then broadcast that context back to every point through a second attention, effectively bypassing the local-attention constraint. The latent tokens are updated dynamically (L←L+L_c) and shared across all transformer layers, yielding scene-specific prompts rather than static prefixes. Together, the two adapters bridge local geometry and global context, achieving full fine-tuning-level performance while updating only 1.6% of parameters.

## Results

On ScanNet validation, GEM achieves 78.3 mIoU while updating only 1.6% of parameters (1.8M), matching the standard full fine-tuning baseline Sonata(ft.) at 78.3 mIoU and approaching Sonata(full.) at 79.4 mIoU. On ScanNet++, GEM reaches 46.6 mIoU, surpassing Sonata(full.) (43.7) and all PEFT baselines. On S3DIS 6-fold, GEM attains 77.9 mIoU versus 77.4 for LoRA and 76.4 for Adapter. Under supervised pre-training (PTv3-PPT), GEM further improves to 79.1 mIoU on ScanNet, exceeding the full fine-tuning reference of 78.6 mIoU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GEM outperforms all four standard PEFT baselines (BitFit, Adapter, LoRA, Prompt Tuning) across every dataset, with the largest margins on ScanNet++ (+4.0 mIoU over LoRA, +5.4 over Prompt Tuning) and S3DIS 6-fold (+0.5 over LoRA, +4.2 over Prompt Tuning). GEM matches Sonata(ft.) full fine-tuning on ScanNet (78.3 vs 78.3) and S3DIS Area 5 (75.1 vs 72.4, actually exceeding it), while using only 1.6% of parameters versus 100%. On the supervised pre-training setting (Table 3), GEM improves PTv3-PPT from 78.6 to 79.1 mIoU, whereas all other PEFT methods degrade or plateau. On ShapeNetPart (Table 8), GEM achieves 84.02 Cls. mIoU, narrowly beating PointLoRA (83.98) and PointGST (83.98) while approaching ReCon full fine-tuning (84.52). On the convolutional SparseUNet backbone (Table 9), GEM gains +13.7 mIoU over linear probing (15.2 vs 1.5) and outperforms Adapter (9.5) and LoRA (13.2), though it remains below full fine-tuning (32.0). Notably, Prompt Tuning underperforms even linear probing on S3DIS 6-fold (73.7 vs 76.5), highlighting the cost of ignoring spatial structure in 3D.

## Relation to Prior Work

GEM extends the PEFT paradigm (adapters, LoRA, prompt tuning) from NLP and 2D vision to large-scale 3D point cloud transformers, but departs fundamentally by treating the unordered, sparse geometry of point clouds as a first-class signal rather than an afterthought. It builds on the local-attention architecture of PTv3/Sonata (which caps complexity at O(np) per point) and on the bottleneck-adapter design pattern, yet replaces channel-space MLPs with a 3D convolutional spatial adapter for positional encoding and a dynamic latent-token context adapter that bypasses the local-attention constraint. Prior 3D PEFT work (PointLoRA, PointGST, dynamic adapters) targets object-level inputs at ShapeNetPart scale; GEM is the first to address scene-level inputs with millions of points, where geometric distribution shift between pre-training and target domains is most acute.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By matching or exceeding full fine-tuning with only 1.6% updated parameters, GEM makes it practical to deploy large pre-trained 3D backbones across many downstream scene datasets without storing a full model copy per task, which is critical for multi-tenant or edge deployments. The finding that a single shared latent token can express scene-wide context (Tab. 6b) suggests the global-attention bottleneck in 3D PEFT can be far tighter than previously assumed, opening a direction for even more compact context modules. Key open questions remain: the persistent indoor-to-outdoor domain gap (Tab. 5), the sequential-processing overhead at large batch sizes, and whether the geometry-aware design principle transfers to other 3D tasks (detection, reconstruction) beyond segmentation.
