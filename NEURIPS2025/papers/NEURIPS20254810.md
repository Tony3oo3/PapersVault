## Problem

Existing mathematical Chain-of-Thought (CoT) datasets frequently contain 'Thought Leaps'—omitted intermediate reasoning steps that experts consider trivial but that create structural gaps in the reasoning chain. Unlike factual errors, these completeness gaps are understudied yet significantly undermine model training effectiveness, with severe leaps causing up to 27.83% accuracy reduction. The paper formalizes this phenomenon and proposes a dedicated task to automatically detect and repair such leaps.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254810/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

CoT-Bridge takes an incomplete reasoning chain with steps explicitly labeled (Step 0 through Step N, where Step 0 is the problem statement) and produces a structured response identifying each leap position (e.g., 'between Step Y and Step Y+1') along with the full text of the missing intermediate step(s). The bridged output is a complete, coherent reasoning chain that is then used as training data for downstream SFT, distillation, or RL pipelines. Step delimiters are adapted per dataset ('\n' for MetaMathQA, '\n\n' for NuminaMath-CoT).

## Method

The authors construct ScaleQM+ (588k samples) by systematically removing 1–3 intermediate steps from the structurally complete ScaleQuestMath dataset, pairing each incomplete chain with its original steps as ground-truth bridging content. CoT-Bridge, fine-tuned from Qwen2.5-Math-7B via instruction tuning, learns a two-stage mapping: first localizing all adjacent step pairs that violate a completeness criterion, then generating the missing intermediate steps to restore logical coherence. A key design choice is that 20% of training examples retain the complete chain unchanged, teaching the model to recognize when no bridging is needed. The trained model is then applied as a plug-and-play data augmentation module to MetaMathQA and NuminaMath, and the resulting bridged datasets consistently improve downstream SFT (+5.87% on NuminaMath), knowledge distillation (+3.02%), and RL cold-start (+3.1%) performance, with ablations confirming that accurate leap localization (vs. random insertion) is essential for gains.

## Results

On the main mathematical reasoning benchmarks, CoT-Bridge achieves average accuracy improvements of +5.87% (Meta-Llama3.1-8B on NuminaMath, 49.74% vs 43.87% Direct SFT) and +3.36% (Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B on MetaMathQA, 45.91% vs 42.55%), with the largest gains on competition-level tasks (+15.63% on AMC23). As a plug-and-play module, bridging distilled data yields +3.02% average improvement, and using bridged data as an RL cold start provides +3.10% over unbridged RL training (63.98% vs 60.88% on the 6-benchmark average). Out-of-domain logical reasoning also improves by +2.99% (Meta-Llama3.1-8B) and +0.99% (Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B) on five OOD benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against zero-shot bridging with Qwen2.5-Instruct-7B/72B (cited as QwenBridger-S/L), CoT-Bridge consistently outperforms on average: on Meta-Llama3.1-8B + NuminaMath, CoT-Bridge reaches 49.74% vs 48.41% (72B) and 46.75% (7B); on MetaMathQA, the 72B zero-shot model actually degrades performance (31.11% vs 33.09% Direct SFT) due to noisy insertions, while CoT-Bridge improves to 34.44%. Against CoT-Bridge-Random (which inserts steps at random positions rather than detected leaps), CoT-Bridge shows the importance of accurate gap localization: on Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B + NuminaMath, the random variant drops MathOdyssey by 3.68% and only gains +0.64% average, whereas CoT-Bridge gains +3.39% with no benchmark regressions. In the RL comparison (Table 9), the bridged cold-start model (Qwen2.5-Math-NuminaBridge + GRPO) achieves 63.98% average, surpassing the unbridged RL model (60.88%, +3.10%), the official Qwen2.5-Math-Instruct-1.5B (63.12%), and Oat-Zero-1.5B with Dr.GRPO (61.79%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by identifying and formalizing the 'Thought Leap' phenomenon—omitted intermediate steps in CoT reasoning chains—as a distinct data quality problem separate from factual errors. While related to fill-in-the-middle tasks (e.g., MathFimer) and process supervision literature, the paper's two-stage formulation (detecting gap locations AND generating missing content) is positioned as a new task. Methodologically, it repurposes structured dataset construction (ScaleQuestMath) to create training pairs, and positions CoT-Bridge as a plug-and-play data augmentation module compatible with existing distillation and RL pipelines rather than a standalone reasoning method.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical value lies in CoT-Bridge's plug-and-play nature: it can be inserted into any existing SFT, distillation, or RL pipeline to improve data completeness without architectural changes, yielding consistent 3–6% gains. The finding that structural completeness (bridging gaps) matters independently of factual accuracy, and that random step insertion can actively degrade performance, reframes how practitioners should think about CoT data quality. Key limitations include evaluation only on 1.5B–8B models, restriction to the mathematical domain, and residual noise in generated steps; open questions include whether the effect persists or amplifies at 32B+ scale and whether the approach transfers to non-mathematical multi-step reasoning (law, medicine, science).
