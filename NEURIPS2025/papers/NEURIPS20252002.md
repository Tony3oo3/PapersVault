## Problem

Existing RL methods for LLM reasoning depend on external supervision (ground-truth labels or pretrained reward models), limiting scalability to open-ended tasks. Prior self-rewarding approaches (TTRL, EMPO) assign rewards solely from final answers, making them vulnerable to reward hacking and ignoring where errors actually originate in intermediate reasoning steps. The paper asks whether intermediate reasoning states themselves can yield reliable self-supervised reward signals without any external labels.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252002/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt (e.g., a math word problem or multiple-choice question) and generates N sampled reasoning trajectories, each decomposed into T discrete intermediate states and a final answer. During training, a distance matrix D ∈ R^{T×K} is computed per trajectory, measuring the negative log-likelihood of each candidate final answer y_k given each intermediate state s_i. The reward is then derived from the geometric relationship between consistency and volatility features extracted from this matrix, with no access to ground-truth labels at any point.

## Method

CoVo constructs a self-rewarding RL framework built on two trajectory features: Consistency (fraction of intermediate states whose nearest candidate answer is the trajectory's own final answer) and Volatility (the last state index where the trajectory deviates from its own answer, normalized by total steps). These features are encoded per trajectory as a 2D vector v_i = Con(τ_i)·[cos(Vol(τ_i)), sin(Vol(τ_i))], summed within groups of trajectories sharing the same final answer, with the group vector's magnitude serving as the intrinsic reward r_int^V — a formulation proven more robust to outliers than linear aggregation. A curiosity bonus r_cur based on average token log-probability with a KL-from-uniform penalty encourages diverse exploration. The total reward r_covo = r_int + r_cur drives policy updates via Reinforce++ with clipped advantages, and the paper provides a variational inference interpretation showing the reward is grounded in latent reasoning states, plus a convergence bound dependent on the base policy's initial likelihood of correct trajectories.

## Results

On Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct, CoVo achieves 68.2% on MATH-500, 88.7% on GSM8K, and 47.5% on AMC-23, surpassing the best supervised RL baseline (Reinforce++ at 67.6% MATH-500, 45.0% AMC-23) and all self-rewarding methods. On Llama3.2-3B-Instruct, CoVo reaches 51.2% on MATH-500 and 32.1% on GPQA, matching or exceeding GRPO (51.8% MATH-500, 31.5% GPQA) while requiring no external labels. CoVo also demonstrates strong domain generalization: trained solely on math data, it achieves competitive gains on commonsense (MMLU-Pro, CommonsenseQA) and science (GPQA) benchmarks, outperforming all in-context learning baselines by 1.5–5.5 points across domains.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoVo outperforms all in-context learning baselines (Zero-shot-CoT, Few-shot-CoT, CoT+SC@4) by 1.6–7.5 points across benchmarks, confirming that RL training yields substantial gains over prompting alone. Against supervised RL methods (GRPO, RLOO, Reinforce, Reinforce++), CoVo matches or exceeds performance on most benchmarks: on Qwen2.5-3B it leads on MATH-500 (68.2 vs. 67.6 Reinforce++), AMC-23 (47.5 vs. 45.0), and GPQA (31.7 vs. 31.5), while on Llama3.2-3B it ties GRPO on MATH-500 (51.2 vs. 51.8) and wins on GPQA (32.1 vs. 31.5) and CommonsenseQA (74.4 vs. 74.2). Among self-rewarding baselines, CoVo consistently beats TTRL and EMPO: on Qwen2.5-3B it gains +0.4 over TTRL on MATH-500 and +2.5 on AMC-23, and on Llama3.2-3B it leads TTRL by +0.2 on MATH-500 and +0.4 on GPQA. The key differentiator is that CoVo achieves these results without any ground-truth labels or external reward models, whereas GRPO/RLOO/Reinforce++ require verifiable answer labels. Ablation results further show that the vectorial intrinsic reward (r_int^V) combined with curiosity reward yields the best overall performance, with the curiosity component contributing +2.0 to +5.0 points over intrinsic-only variants on math benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoVo builds directly on the distance-metric framework from Zhou et al. [72] (Landscape of Thoughts) to quantify how intermediate reasoning states relate to candidate final answers, and adapts the intra-group relative reward structure from GRPO [42] to organize trajectories by shared answers. It departs from prior self-rewarding RL methods (TTRL, EMPO) that assign rewards solely based on final-answer frequency or semantic clustering, by instead extracting reward signals from the *internal structure* of reasoning trajectories via consistency and volatility features. The theoretical grounding via variational inference over latent reasoning states connects the self-rewarding objective to the standard RLHF optimization, providing a principled justification that prior heuristic self-rewarding approaches lacked.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CoVo offers a practical pathway to improve LLM reasoning without costly human annotations or external reward models, which is a significant scalability advantage for domains where ground-truth labels are unavailable or expensive. However, the performance gains over supervised RL are modest (comparable rather than substantially superior), and the 24.6% per-step computational overhead from distance-matrix computation limits immediate deployment. Open questions include whether the consistency-volatility signal generalizes to multimodal reasoning (VLMs), semi-supervised settings with partial labels, and whether the method can truly elicit novel reasoning capabilities beyond what the base model already possesses, as the authors themselves acknowledge RL primarily amplifies pre-existing abilities.
