## Problem

Existing parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) methods such as LoRA are designed for dense models and, when applied to pruned LLMs, overlook the specific information lost during pruning, leading to suboptimal performance recovery. The paper identifies that pruning-induced information loss is directly reflected in attention head activations and that selectively reintroducing the lost components can substantially close the performance gap between pruned and dense models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250225/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pruned LLM (with all attention and FFN weight matrices zeroed out), the corresponding dense model's attention head activations for a set of calibration samples, and contrastive sample pairs (question, positive response, most-similar negative response) constructed via a sentence encoder. It produces a restored pruned LLM in which a small number of selected attention heads each receive a learned constant bias vector c_learned ∈ R^{d_h} (where d_h is the per-head dimension, e.g., 128 for LLaMA-7B). Because c_learned is absorbed as a bias in the multi-head attention block, it adds less than 0.05% parameters and introduces no additional inference computation, preserving the pruned model's sparsity ratio and speed.

## Method

RestoreLCC operates in two stages. First, contrastive probing constructs contrastive tuples (q, r⁺, r⁻) using a MiniLM sentence encoder to find the most similar incorrect response, then applies activation editing (adding the SVD-derived lost component to the pruned question activation) and trains a lightweight linear NLI classifier per head to rank attention heads by their discriminative power between correct and incorrect sequence activations. Second, lost component compensation (LCC) applies SVD to the activation difference matrix ΔZ = Z_dense − Z_pruned for each selected head, yielding orthogonal unit directions v_i; the method then learns a scalar magnitude β_i for every direction (not just the top-K) plus a hedging bias vector b, forming c_learned = Σ β_i v_i + b. This constant vector is injected additively into the pruned head's output activation. Unlike LoRA, which learns unguided low-rank weight updates, RestoreLCC fixes the information directions from SVD and only optimizes their magnitudes, explicitly compensating for the specific components lost during pruning while keeping the model sparse.

## Results

On LLaMA-7B under unstructured pruning at 50% sparsity (Wanda), RestoreLCC achieves 58.83% mean accuracy across seven commonsense reasoning benchmarks and 6.93 PPL on WikiText, outperforming the best baseline LoFiT (56.82%) by +2.01% and approaching the dense model's 59.99%. Under semi-structured pruning (SparseGPT, N:M=2:4), RestoreLCC reaches 55.00% mean accuracy (+2.65% over DoRA) and 8.99 PPL. Under structured pruning (SlimGPT, 20% sparsity), RestoreLCC attains 59.76% mean accuracy (+1.25% over DoRA) and 7.53 PPL, consistently the best across all three pruning regimes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RestoreLCC consistently outperforms all five baselines across all three pruning regimes. Against LoRA, RestoreLCC gains +2.56% (unstructured), +3.34% (semi-structured), and +1.31% (structured) in mean accuracy. Against DoRA, the gains are +2.25%, +2.65%, and +1.25% respectively. Against LoFiT (the strongest representation-engineering baseline), RestoreLCC leads by +2.01%, +2.89%, and +1.34%. EoRA, a training-free eigenspace method, is outperformed by +2.95% (unstructured) and +3.79% (semi-structured). FLAP, a bias-compensation method evaluated only in the semi-structured setting, trails RestoreLCC by a large margin of +5.02%. Notably, RestoreLCC also achieves the lowest PPL in every setting (6.93, 8.99, 7.53), indicating superior language modeling recovery alongside task accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself against the dominant paradigm of using generic PEFT methods (LoRA, DoRA, LoFiT, EoRA) to restore pruned LLMs, arguing that these methods are designed for dense models and ignore the specific information loss introduced by pruning. It repurposes ideas from activation editing and representation engineering (function vectors, steering vectors) but applies them in a targeted way: SVD decomposition of the dense-minus-pruned activation difference to extract 'lost components,' combined with a contrastive NLI-style probing classifier to select which attention heads to compensate. The method is explicitly plug-and-play across structured (SlimGPT), semi-structured (SparseGPT), and unstructured (Wanda) pruning, filling a gap where prior restoration methods treat all pruning types uniformly without modeling the specific information that was removed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that RestoreLCC restores pruned model performance while preserving sparsity and inference speed (the learned component is absorbed as a constant bias), unlike LoRA without masking which reintroduces non-zero weights at pruned positions. The finding that minor SVD components can outperform the top principal component for restoration challenges the standard assumption that dominant directions of information loss are the most recoverable. Open questions include whether the approach extends to even higher sparsity ratios (>60%), whether it can be combined with quantization more deeply (only 4-bit tested), and whether the contrastive probing module can be made fully training-free. The authors also note potential overfitting of unimportant component magnitudes as a limitation.
