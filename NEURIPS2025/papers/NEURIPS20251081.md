## Problem

Diffusion inversion—recovering the latent generative trajectory of a pretrained diffusion model from a real image—remains challenging under few-step inference and on out-of-distribution (novel) samples. Existing approaches either modify the denoising trajectory (weakening generative fidelity) or solve a fixed-point equation via numerical methods (e.g., Anderson acceleration), both of which suffer from slow convergence and a performance bottleneck that limits reconstruction quality, especially when only 2–4 inference steps are available.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251081/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single real RGB image x_0 (and optionally a source text prompt for editing) and produces a parameterized noise embedding ε_T* that, when fed through the frozen diffusion model's denoising trajectory, reconstructs x_0. Inversion requires only 3.43 GB of GPU memory on SD v1.4 regardless of the number of inference steps T, and operates in as few as 2 steps. For prompt-driven editing, the inverted latent is reused with classifier-free guidance (target prompt as positive, source prompt as negative) to generate an edited image while preserving non-edited attributes.

## Method

PreciseInv reformulates diffusion inversion as a sequence of T local learning subproblems rather than a global root-finding problem, exploiting the Markov property of the diffusion process. A dynamic programming-inspired bottom-up strategy recursively estimates a parameterized noise embedding ε_t* at each timestep t by minimizing a single-step reconstruction loss ‖μ(x_t*) − x_{t−1}*‖² via gradient descent, where μ is the DDIM (or Euler) denoising update. Because backpropagation is performed over only one timestep per subproblem, memory usage stays constant regardless of T. The paper proves monotonic convergence to a stationary point under Lipschitz continuity assumptions and derives a geometric upper bound δ/(1−L_m) on the accumulated reconstruction error. The framework is model-agnostic, extending naturally to stochastic DDPM samplers (by jointly optimizing the stochastic noise z_t*) and continuous-time rectified flow models like SD3 (via Euler-discretized velocity field integration).

## Results

On COCO 2017 with SD v1.4, PreciseInv (η=10⁻⁵) achieves LPIPS 0.078, SSIM 0.756, and PSNR 25.86 dB, outperforming all baselines while running in 23.29 s (vs. 41.30 s for DDIM Inversion). Under the relaxed threshold η=10⁻², it still leads with LPIPS 0.104 and PSNR 25.13 dB at only 5.33 s per image. On the challenging few-step setting (T=2), PreciseInv achieves LPIPS 0.077 and PSNR 25.93 dB, substantially surpassing the next-best method ReNoise (LPIPS 0.199, PSNR 22.92 dB). Gains are most pronounced on the out-of-distribution Cartoon dataset, where PreciseInv reduces LPIPS by 0.127 and increases PSNR by 7.92 dB over DDIM Inversion.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PreciseInv outperforms all six baselines across every dataset and metric. Against forward-optimization methods, it beats DDIM Inversion by 0.040 LPIPS and 1.19 dB PSNR on COCO, and by a dramatic 0.127 LPIPS and 7.92 dB PSNR on the out-of-distribution Cartoon set; ReNoise and EasyInv are even further behind, with EasyInv particularly weak on DarkFace (LPIPS 0.210 vs. 0.052). Among invertible samplers, EDICT, BDIA, and BELM cluster together with LPIPS ≈ 0.43 on COCO and PSNR ≈ 14 dB, far below PreciseInv's 0.078 / 25.86 dB, though BELM is the fastest (9 s) and BDIA the slowest (172 s). In the few-step regime (T=2), the gap widens further: PreciseInv achieves LPIPS 0.077 while the best baseline ReNoise reaches only 0.199, and BELM degrades to 0.832. On LCM-SD v1.5 and SDXL (Table 2), PreciseInv similarly dominates, e.g. LPIPS 0.083 vs. 0.626 for DDIM Inversion on LCM, confirming generality across architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the forward-optimization line of diffusion inversion (DDIM Inversion, ReNoise, AIDI, EasyInv), which formulates inversion as a fixed-point equation solved via Anderson acceleration or iterative renoising. PreciseInv departs from this numerical root-finding paradigm by reformulating inversion as a sequence of local gradient-based optimization subproblems connected by a dynamic programming recursion over the Markov chain structure of the diffusion process. It also contrasts with invertible samplers (EDICT, BDIA, BELM) that modify the sampling procedure itself, and with backward-optimization methods (Null-Text Inversion, Eta Inversion) that alter the denoising trajectory; PreciseInv preserves the original generative path while learning a parameterized noise embedding at each step.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ability to achieve faithful inversion in as few as two inference steps with only 3.43 GB of GPU memory makes precise inversion practical for real-time editing pipelines and resource-constrained settings, where prior methods required 50–1000 steps. The demonstrated generality across architectures (SD v1.4, LCM, SDXL, SD3/Rectified Flow) and the theoretical convergence guarantee with a geometric error bound position PreciseInv as a strong default inversion module for downstream editing tasks. Open questions include the high inference cost on large models like SD3 (403 s), the absence of statistical significance reporting, and whether the per-image test-time optimization can be amortized or distilled into a learned inversion network.
