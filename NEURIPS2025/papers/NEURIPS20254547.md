## Problem

Existing unsupervised domain adaptation methods for adverse-scene semantic segmentation rely exclusively on visual modality to learn domain-invariant features, but severe visual degradation under fog, snow, rain, and night (texture loss, low visibility, color distortion) makes consistent cross-domain semantic learning difficult and prone to hallucinations. No prior unified framework introduces a complementary modality to compensate for this degraded visual information.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254547/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images at 512×512 resolution from a source domain (Cityscapes) and unlabeled target-domain images under adverse conditions, alongside learnable text prompts (context words + class names, length M=5) processed through a CLIP text encoder. It produces a pixel-wise semantic segmentation mask over 19 Cityscapes classes. Unlike purely visual UDA methods, the text modality is generated internally from prompts rather than provided as external input, and all modules (CPTG, RCTVF) remain active at inference.

## Method

CroPe introduces cross-modal semantic compensation via two modules. The Complementary-Perceptive Text Generation (CPTG) module decouples CLIP text embeddings into domain-invariant and domain-specific components using two MLPs with an orthogonality constraint; the domain-specific branch interacts with multi-scale visual features via attention to capture scene-aware semantics, while the domain-invariant branch is softly aligned to hand-crafted prompts for generalization, and a gated fusion combines both into multi-level text embeddings. The Reverse Chain Text-Visual Fusion (RCTVF) module then fuses these text embeddings into FPN multi-scale visual features through a unified attention (deformable + cross-modal) and propagates the compensated deep semantics to shallower layers via a reverse decoding chain, maximizing semantic density gain. Training follows a self-training paradigm with pseudo-labels, optimizing cross-entropy plus orthogonality and alignment losses, using CLIP ViT-B/16 or ViT-L/14 as the frozen or fine-tuned backbone.

## Results

CroPe achieves state-of-the-art mIoU on 9 out of 10 adverse scene benchmarks, with headline improvements of +6.5 mIoU on ACDC-Night (66.8 vs. PASS's 60.3) and +1.2 mIoU on ACDC-All (72.0 vs. PASS's 70.8). Across all ten scenarios, CroPe surpasses the previous best (PASS) by an average of 2.1 mIoU. The method also demonstrates strong generalization, e.g., a model trained on CS→DZ transfers to ND and BD with +4.6 and +4.5 mIoU over PASS respectively, and achieves 62.3 mIoU on DZ (+19.8 over the best scene-specific method MCGDA).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest scene-agnostic baseline PASS [13], CroPe wins on 8 of 10 scenarios, ties on ACDC-Fog (70.6 vs. 70.6), and is marginally behind on ACDC-Rain (74.4 vs. 74.6); the largest gap is +6.5 mIoU on ACDC-Night. Against MIC [9], CroPe outperforms on every reported scenario, with gains ranging from +1.4 mIoU (ACDC-Snow) to +9.6 mIoU (ACDC-Night). Compared to DAFormer [7], the improvement is substantial across all ten benchmarks (e.g., +21.9 mIoU on ACDC-Night, +16.6 on ACDC-All). CroPe also dominates all scene-specific methods where they overlap: on foggy scenes it beats DAEN [32] by +5.0 mIoU on ACDC-Fog, and on nighttime scenes it surpasses MCGDA [16] by +19.8 mIoU on DZ. Against HRDA [8] and SePiCo [19], CroPe leads by +3.0 and +12.5 mIoU respectively on ACDC-All, confirming that cross-modal semantic compensation provides a consistent advantage over purely visual domain-invariant learning strategies.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on DAFormer's self-training UDA framework and CLIP backbone, but departs from the purely visual paradigm that dominates scene-agnostic adverse-condition segmentation (PASS, MIC, HRDA, SePiCo). The key conceptual shift is introducing text modality as a semantic compensation signal for degraded visual features, drawing on prompt learning (learnable + hand-crafted prompts) and cross-modal attention mechanisms. The CPTG module's decoupling of domain-invariant and domain-specific text embeddings, combined with the RCTVF's reverse decoding chain, represents a novel architectural combination not seen in prior UDA segmentation work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CroPe provides the first cross-modal semantic compensation framework for UDA under adverse scenes, demonstrating that text semantics can effectively compensate for visual degradation (fog, night, rain, snow) without requiring scene-specific models. The practical efficiency gains—achieving SOTA at 512×512 resolution without FD loss, and offering LoRA/frozen-backbone variants that reduce GPU memory to under 10 GB—make the approach more deployable than prior SOTA methods. Open questions include whether the text compensation generalizes beyond driving scenes to other domain gaps, and whether the parameter overhead in full fine-tuning can be further reduced while maintaining performance.
