## Problem

Optimistic FTRL (OFTRL) achieves constant social regret and last-iterate convergence to Nash equilibrium in multi-agent games, but real-world applications (e.g., Cournot markets, recommender systems) inherently observe rewards with time delays. This paper proves that even a single-step delay degrades OFTRL's social regret from O(1) to Ω(√T) and causes strategies to diverge from equilibrium rather than converge, fundamentally breaking the algorithm's guarantees.

## Input / Output

Each player receives only the reward vector from m steps ago (u_i^{t-m}) rather than the current reward, with rewards before time 0 set to zero. The game payoff structure is multi-linear in the joint strategy profile, with each player's gradient depending only on the other players' strategies. The output is a mixed strategy (probability distribution over a finite action set) updated each round via a regularized optimization step, with the learning rate η chosen as O(1/(m²L)) to balance prediction error against exploration.

## Method

The paper proposes Weighted OFTRL (WOFTRL), which multiplies the optimistic prediction term by n∈ℕ instead of 1, so the update uses m_i^t = n·u_i^{t-m}. A Taylor expansion of the dynamics reveals that the coefficient of the first-order reward difference term is (n−m), meaning the optimistic weight n and time delay m cancel each other out; when n = m+1, the residual prediction error is only O(η²) and negligible. The regret analysis shows WOFTRL satisfies the RVU property with a degradation factor λ=(m+1)(m+2)/2, yielding constant social regret O(m²) with learning rate η=1/(2λL). For convergence, the paper extends the Lyapunov function technique from Mertikopoulos et al. to construct a monotonically decreasing divergence V^t(x*) that incorporates n−1 delayed Bregman divergence terms, proving last-iterate convergence to Nash equilibrium in poly-matrix zero-sum games for η≤1/(√8·n²L).

## Results

The paper establishes that any time delay m≥1 causes OFTRL (n=1) to degrade to Ω(√T) social regret (Thm. 6) and diverge from the Nash equilibrium (Thm. 7) in Matching Pennies. The proposed WOFTRL with optimistic weight n=m+1 recovers constant social regret of O(m²) independent of T (Cor. 11) and achieves last-iterate convergence to the Nash equilibrium in poly-matrix zero-sum games (Cor. 13). The key mechanism is the exponential rate α = m − n + 1/2: when n > m the radius of the strategy dynamics shrinks (convergence), while when n ≤ m it grows (divergence). Experiments on Matching Pennies, Sato's game, and Rock-Paper-Scissors confirm a sharp phase transition at n = m, with O(1) regret for n > m and O(√T) regret for n ≤ m.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Vanilla FTRL | 0 | 0 | +½ | O(√T) | Diverges |
| OFTRL (no delay) | 0 | 1 | −½ | O(1) | Converges |

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not benchmark against external baselines but instead compares algorithmic variants under a unified framework. Vanilla FTRL (n=0, m=0) and OFTRL with delay (n=1, m≥1) both exhibit the same worst-case Ω(√T) social regret and divergence, showing that the standard optimistic prediction is completely negated by even a single-step delay. WOFTRL with n=m+1 strictly outperforms OFTRL under delay, reducing regret from Ω(√T) to O(m²) (constant in T) and restoring last-iterate convergence. The experiments in Figs. 1–3 confirm a sharp phase transition: for m=10, regret is O(1) for n=11,13,15 but oscillates at O(√T) for n=1,3,5,7,9. In the convergence experiments (Fig. 3, m=4), trajectories converge to the Nash equilibrium for n=5,6 but diverge for n=3,4, with larger n yielding faster convergence.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the optimistic learning-in-games framework of Syrgkanis et al. (OFTRL, NeurIPS 2015) and Mertikopoulos et al. (optimistic mirror descent, ICLR 2019) by introducing a time-delayed observation of past rewards. While delayed feedback has been extensively studied in single-agent online learning (Weinberger & Ordentlich, Zinkevich et al., Quanrud & Khashabi) and time-varying games (Zhang et al., Anagnostides et al., Duvocelle et al.), the specific interaction between optimistic prediction and observation delay in multi-agent games had not been analyzed. The paper's WOFTRL algorithm is a minimal modification of OFTRL—weighting the optimistic term by n—whose analysis draws on the Lyapunov function technique from Mertikopoulos et al. and the RVU regret framework from Syrgkanis et al., extended to handle the accumulated prediction error introduced by the delay.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that even a single-step delay (m=1) degrades OFTRL from O(1) social regret to Ω(√T) and from convergence to divergence is practically significant for domains like Cournot competition and multi-agent recommender systems where reward observation is inherently lagged. The WOFTRL fix (n = m+1) is elegant but carries a cost: regret scales as O(m²) and the learning rate must shrink by a factor of λ = (m+1)(m+2)/2, which becomes prohibitive for large delays. Open questions include extending the theory to stochastic or adversarial delays, obtaining explicit convergence rates, and analyzing the constrained (simplex) setting where boundary effects may alter the dynamics.
