## Problem

Existing RGB-to-hyperspectral reconstruction methods suffer from a metameric dilemma: distinct spectral distributions that map to nearly identical RGB values become indistinguishable, leading to unreliable reconstructions. This stems from three compounding limitations—data poverty (datasets lack diverse metameric examples), spectral manifold blindness (no explicit modeling of real-world spectral distributions), and architecture myopia (treating spectral radiance as a monolithic entity that conflates illumination and chrominance).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254484/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard 3-channel sRGB image as input and produces a full hyperspectral cube as output (31 spectral bands at 482×512 for ARAD-1K, 103 bands at 1300×1392 for ICVL). A key design choice is that the spectral response function (SRF) matrix A is treated as a learnable parameter rather than a fixed sensor calibration, allowing the network to adapt to variations across cameras and compression artifacts. During inference, the coarse HSI estimate is further refined via a test-time inner-loop optimization (K=5 gradient steps per diffusion timestep, S=50 total steps), making the output depend on per-sample adaptation rather than a single forward pass.

## Method

Diff-Spectra is a semi-supervised framework with three components trained in three stages. First, an Adaptive IllumiChroma Decoupling (AICD) module factorizes RGB inputs into illumination and chrominance subspaces via a trainable Retinex decomposition followed by an orthogonal UV transform (cos/sin of reflectance), producing distinctive features that separate metameric counterparts. Second, these features feed into a learnable SRF-guided HSI Initial Estimation (SRF-guided HIE) mechanism that mimics physical image formation by inverting the SRF projection (X·Aᵀ(AAᵀ)⁻¹) through a UNet, injecting physics-aware constraints into the reconstruction. Third, a lightweight 1D MLP-based UNet Spectral Diffusion Module (SDM) is pre-trained on a metameric-augmented dataset (synthesized via null-space perturbation of spectra to preserve RGB colorimetry) and then used as a spectral prior: during inference, the coarse HSI from SRF-guided HIE is treated as learnable parameters and refined through K inner-loop gradient steps at each diffusion timestep, bridging the domain gap between the estimated and learned spectral distributions.

## Results

On ARAD-1K, Diff-Spectra achieves the best spectral quality (ERGAS 4.63, SAM 3.96) and highest PSNR (35.47 dB) among all 9 baselines, with 2.49M parameters. On ICVL, it similarly leads in ERGAS (2.84), SAM (2.39), and PSNR (34.71 dB). In the metameric dilemma evaluation, Diff-Spectra degrades far less than all competitors on metamer RGB inputs (PSNR 31.61 vs. 26.63–28.59 for baselines), and achieves the best HSI classification accuracy on Pavia University (OA 89.02%, κ 0.8349) and Indian Pines (OA 78.14%, κ 0.7571).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Diff-Spectra outperforms all 9 baselines on ERGAS, SAM, and PSNR across both ARAD-1K and ICVL, with the largest margins over older methods (e.g., 12.8 dB PSNR gain over AWAN on ARAD-1K). The closest competitor is CESST [8] (AAAI'24), which trails by 3.22 ERGAS and 0.73 dB PSNR on ARAD-1K, and by 1.25 ERGAS and 1.75 dB PSNR on ICVL. PADUT [49] (ICCV'23) is the only method that beats Diff-Spectra on SSIM (0.946 vs. 0.940 on ARAD-1K; 0.948 vs. 0.941 on ICVL), though it uses 2.6× more parameters (6.38M vs. 2.49M). In the metameric dilemma stress test, all baselines suffer catastrophic degradation (PSNR drops of 5–7 dB), while Diff-Spectra retains PSNR 31.61 vs. the next-best CESST at 28.59, confirming its robustness to metamer ambiguity. On downstream HSI classification, Diff-Spectra achieves the best OA (89.02%) and κ (0.8349) on Pavia University, surpassing PADUT (85.48%, 0.8039) by 3.54% OA, and the best OA (78.14%) and κ (0.7571) on Indian Pines, ahead of SPECAT (77.32%, 0.7088).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself against the dominant paradigm of direct RGB-to-HSI mapping (MST++, CESST, PADUT, SPECAT) by arguing that these methods suffer from a 'metameric dilemma' where distinct spectra collapse to identical RGB values. It builds on DDPM for the spectral diffusion prior, borrows orthogonal decoupling from ODCR (CVPR 2024) for the AICD module, and leverages the metameric black concept from color science (Alsam & Lenz, 2006) for data augmentation. The key departure is formulating reconstruction as a semi-supervised problem that explicitly separates a physics-constrained supervised estimation stage (SRF-guided HIE) from an unsupervised diffusion-based spectral regularization stage, rather than treating the mapping as a single end-to-end regression.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The metameric dilemma evaluation (Table 2a) provides a useful diagnostic protocol showing that existing SOTA methods degrade catastrophically on metamer inputs (e.g., MST++ SAM jumps from 6.05 to 50.92), a failure mode previously underappreciated in the literature. The ablation revealing that naively adding the SDM without inner-loop optimization actually *hurts* performance (Variant 3 vs. Variant 2) highlights a non-trivial domain gap between physics-guided estimation and learned spectral priors that remains only partially resolved. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to sensors with different SRF configurations, whether the 1-D spectral diffusion can be extended to capture spatial-spectral correlations, and how to more principledly bridge the distribution gap the authors acknowledge in their conclusion.
