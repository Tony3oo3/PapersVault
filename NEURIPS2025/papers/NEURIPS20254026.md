## Problem

Existing low-rank training methods for LLMs (LoRA, GaLore) constrain optimization to a low-rank subspace, inevitably discarding gradient or weight information outside that subspace and yielding sub-optimal performance. The paper asks whether full-rank training—using full-rank gradients to update full-rank weights—can be achieved while consistently maintaining a low-rank constraint on optimizer states for memory efficiency.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254026/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Fira consumes full-rank weight matrices W_t ∈ R^{m×n} and their full-rank gradients G_t, decomposing each gradient via an SVD-based projection matrix P_t ∈ R^{m×r} into an in-subspace component R_t = P_t^T G_t and an out-of-subspace component (I − P_t P_t^T)G_t. It produces updated full-rank weights while storing only low-rank optimizer states (mr + 2nr + 1 scalars per matrix versus 2mn for full-rank Adam), adding just one scalar ‖S_{t−1}‖ per weight matrix for the norm-growth limiter. The projection matrix is refreshed every T steps (typically 200), and the method is plug-and-play, requiring only ~3 additional lines of code over a standard GaLore implementation.

## Method

Fira's core insight is that the scaling factor φ_t(R_t) = ‖ψ_t(R_t)‖/‖R_t‖—the ratio of corrected to raw gradient norm produced by a low-rank Adam—exhibits high cosine similarity (≥0.92) and low MSE to its full-rank counterpart at both matrix and column granularity. Leveraging this, the norm-based scaling step applies the low-rank scaling factor to the out-of-subspace gradient: W_{t+1} = W_t − η P_t ψ_t(R_t) − η φ_t(R_t)(G_t − P_t R_t), effectively approximating full-rank Adam correction without storing full-rank optimizer states. A second component, the norm-growth limiter, prevents loss spikes by capping the relative increase in the corrected gradient norm: if ‖S_t‖/‖S_{t−1}‖ > γ, then S_t is rescaled so its norm equals γ‖S_{t−1}‖, converting abrupt gradient jumps into gradual rises. Theoretical analysis provides an error upper bound κ(r) ≤ Δ_{φ²}·(1 − ‖G_r‖/‖G‖) for the scaling-factor approximation and proves Var[φ] ≤ Var[ψ], showing the low-rank scaling is no noisier than full-rank Adam's element-wise rates.

## Results

For pre-training LLaMA models on C4, Fira achieves validation perplexity of 31.06 (60M), 22.73 (130M), 16.85 (350M), and 14.31 (1B), surpassing full-rank Adam (34.06, 25.08, 18.80, 15.56) while using 61.1% less optimizer-state memory at 1B scale. For fine-tuning LLaMA-7B on eight commonsense reasoning tasks, Fira achieves 76.9% average accuracy, outperforming GaLore (62.7%), LoRA (74.7%), and full-rank training (58.6%). On LLaMA-7B pre-training, Fira uses 8× smaller rank (optimizer-state memory) than GaLore while achieving significantly lower validation perplexity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Fira consistently outperforms GaLore across all pre-training model sizes (e.g., 14.31 vs. 15.64 perplexity at 1B) and achieves 76.9% vs. 62.7% average accuracy in fine-tuning, where GaLore suffers severe degradation on HellaSwag (32.2%) and WinoGrande (18.0%). Against LoRA, Fira wins by large margins in pre-training (14.31 vs. 19.21 at 1B) and fine-tuning (76.9% vs. 74.7%), and similarly dominates ReLoRA (14.31 vs. 18.33 at 1B; 76.9% vs. 72.2% fine-tuning). In fine-tuning, Fira also surpasses Flora (76.9% vs. 64.9%). Against the concurrent memory-efficient optimizers Adam-mini, SlimAdam, and FRUGAL, Fira achieves the best perplexity at every model size in pre-training (e.g., 14.31 vs. 16.72/15.42/15.73 at 1B) and the highest fine-tuning average accuracy (76.9% vs. 69.4%/73.9%/74.7%). On MMLU with Gemma-7B and Mistral-7B, Fira is competitive with APOLLO, trailing slightly on Gemma-7B (33.26 vs. 33.81) but matching or exceeding it on Mistral-7B (61.72 vs. 61.58).

## Relation to Prior Work

Fira extends the gradient-projection line of work initiated by GaLore, which reduced optimizer-state memory by projecting full-rank gradients into a low-rank subspace but discarded gradient information outside that subspace. It also departs from the LoRA/ReLoRA family of weight-decomposition methods, which constrain both parameters and gradients to a low-rank subspace. The key conceptual departure is the observation that the norm-scaling behavior of adaptive optimizers (e.g., Adam) is nearly identical at the matrix level between low-rank and full-rank training, enabling low-rank optimizer states to approximate full-rank gradient correction. This positions Fira as the first method to simultaneously maintain full-rank weights, full-rank gradients, and a low-rank constraint on optimizer states.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 8× reduction in optimizer-state memory for LLaMA 7B pre-training while outperforming GaLore makes Fira a practically attractive option for large-scale LLM training where optimizer states dominate memory budgets. The plug-and-play design (3 additional lines of code, one insensitive hyperparameter γ) lowers the adoption barrier significantly. Limitations include evaluation primarily on LLaMA-family architectures and the C4 dataset, with the authors noting future extension to MLLMs and diffusion models. An open question is whether the scaling-factor similarity observation generalizes to non-Transformer architectures or to training regimes with very different loss landscapes (e.g., RLHF, instruction tuning).
