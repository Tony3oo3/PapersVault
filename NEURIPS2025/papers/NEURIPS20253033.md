## Problem

Estimating whole-brain electrophysiological networks (WBEN) from EEG/iEEG data is hampered by the ill-posedness of the source localization inverse problem, which causes two-step methods (localize then connect) to produce highly diffuse source estimates and dense, unreliable connectivity maps. While one-step state-space approaches have been proposed for EEG-only settings, no principled framework existed for jointly integrating simultaneously recorded scalp EEG and intracranial EEG (iEEG) to exploit the complementary spatial coverage of both modalities for whole-brain network inference.

## Input / Output

The method consumes simultaneous scalp EEG (128-channel, 10-20 montage) and iEEG (ECoG/sEEG) time series, a BEM-derived lead field matrix L mapping N source regions to M EEG electrodes, a binary selection matrix C identifying which of the N regions are directly observed by O iEEG electrodes, and known noise covariance matrices P and S. The iEEG provides direct (but spatially limited) observation of a subset of source regions, while EEG provides indirect whole-brain observation. The output is a set of state transition matrices Φ_k (k=1,…,K) encoding directed region-to-region connectivity at the Harvard-Oxford atlas level (69 regions: 48 cortical, 21 subcortical), along with smoothed source state estimates x_t for all regions and time points.

## Method

The core contribution is a unified Bayesian state-space model in which scalp EEG and iEEG are treated as conditionally independent observations of shared latent source dynamics governed by a vector autoregressive (VAR) process. An EM algorithm alternates between an E-step that computes the posterior mean and covariance of source states via Fixed Interval Smoothing (forward Kalman filtering followed by backward Kalman smoothing on the merged observation y_t^+ = [y_t; z_t] with block-diagonal noise covariance) and an M-step that maximizes the resulting Q-function with l1 row-wise sparsity regularization on Φ, solved efficiently via the FASTA algorithm. The VAR(K) model is converted to an equivalent VAR(1) form through an augmented state vector to enable standard Kalman filtering. The key architectural insight is that the iEEG selection matrix C provides direct, high-SNR observation of a subset of source regions, which anchors the otherwise ill-posed inverse problem and dramatically reduces false-positive connectivity predictions—achieving this within a single joint estimation step rather than the traditional two-step localize-then-connect pipeline.

## Results

On synthetic data (BBCB-generated, 10 active regions, in-degree 2), the proposed one-step EM framework with 30% iEEG observation achieves Sen=0.639±0.120 and Acc=0.545±0.224 at SNR=−5 dB, compared to the best two-step baseline eLORETA (Sen=0.383, Acc=0.010) and the one-step method without iEEG (Sen=0.461, Acc=0.189). At the lowest tested SNR (−10 dB), integrating 30% iEEG observation yields Acc=0.595±0.167 while the 0%-iEEG variant collapses to Acc=0.034±0.023, demonstrating robustness to high noise. On real simultaneous EEG/iEEG data (15 epilepsy patients, Sternberg verbal WM task), the framework reveals significantly stronger cortical-to-subcortical information flow during encoding than maintenance (group t-test: t=3.359, p<0.001 for connectivity strength; t=5.487, p<0.001 for cortical outgoing connections).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method dominates all seven two-step baselines (MNE, dSPM, sLORETA, eLORETA, ALCMV, ASTAR, VSSI-ARD) on both sensitivity and accuracy across every SNR level tested. At the standard SNR=−5 dB setting, the best two-step method (eLORETA) achieves only Sen=0.383 and Acc=0.010, while the proposed one-step method with 30% iEEG observation reaches Sen=0.639 and Acc=0.545 — a ~67% relative improvement in sensitivity and a >50× improvement in accuracy. Even the one-step variant without any iEEG (0% observation) already surpasses all two-step baselines on sensitivity (Sen=0.461 vs. best 0.448 for VSSI-ARD), confirming the advantage of joint source-localization-and-connectivity estimation. The critical finding is that adding just 30% iEEG coverage dramatically reduces false positives: accuracy jumps from 0.189 to 0.545 at SNR=−5 dB and from 0.034 to 0.595 at SNR=−10 dB, with the false positive rate dropping from ~0.028 to ~0.003 (Table 5). At high SNR (5 dB), the 0% and 30% iEEG variants perform comparably on sensitivity, but the 30% variant maintains superior accuracy at lower SNRs, confirming that iEEG integration provides the most benefit under noisy recording conditions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the one-step state-space modeling paradigm for brain connectivity (Yang et al., 2016; Soleimani et al., 2022) by introducing a principled multimodal fusion of scalp EEG and intracranial EEG (iEEG) within a unified EM + Fixed Interval Smoothing framework. Prior one-step approaches treated EEG/MEG as the sole observation modality, while iEEG was used only for validation or as a separate analysis channel. The key departure is modeling both modalities as complementary Gaussian observations of shared latent source dynamics, with iEEG providing direct (zero-leadfield) access to a subset of source regions, thereby partially resolving the ill-posed inverse problem that limits EEG-only estimation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides the first unified computational tool for whole-brain electrophysiological network estimation from simultaneous scalp EEG and iEEG, a data configuration that is increasingly available in epilepsy monitoring but has lacked a principled inference method. The finding that ~30% iEEG coverage yields a step-change in accuracy (Acc jumping from ~0.19 to ~0.55) and that performance remains stable at low EEG SNR has direct practical implications for clinical settings where scalp EEG is noisy. Open questions include whether the linear VAR assumption captures non-linear cortical dynamics, how the method scales beyond atlas-level parcellation, and whether the framework can be extended to handle the partial and heterogeneous electrode coverage typical of real iEEG implants without requiring simultaneous full-head EEG.
