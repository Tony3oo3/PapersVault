## Problem

Multimodal models tend to over-rely on dominant modalities while neglecting weaker ones, leading to degraded performance that can be worse than unimodal baselines. While prior work addresses this through gradient modulation, masking, or re-weighting objectives, data-centric solutions that treat misaligned samples as informative training signals rather than noise remain largely unexplored.

## Input / Output

The method operates on pairs of modalities (audio+video, image+text, or optical flow+RGB frame) and produces a single class label per sample. Unusually, it also generates synthetic misaligned samples by swapping one modality from a different source instance with a different label, and assigns these soft labels as weighted averages of two one-hot vectors rather than a single hard label. The soft label weights are derived from unimodal classifier confidence scores, making the supervision signal sample-specific and modality-aware.

## Method

MIDAS generates misaligned samples within each mini-batch by randomly pairing modalities from two source instances with different labels, then supervises the multimodal classifier with a confidence-based soft label computed from unimodal classifier outputs. Two weighting mechanisms enhance learning from these difficult samples: weak-modality weighting dynamically increases the loss contribution of the least-confident modality (identified per batch) when the multimodal model under-predicts its class, and hard-sample weighting scales the loss by the cosine similarity between the swapped-in feature and the original feature it replaced, prioritizing semantically ambiguous swaps. The total training objective combines standard cross-entropy on aligned samples, unimodal classification losses, and the weighted misaligned-sample loss, with a warm-up phase to stabilize unimodal confidence estimates before misaligned training begins.

## Results

MIDAS achieves the best or near-best performance across all four primary benchmarks: 74.88% top-1 accuracy (67.18% F1) on Kinetics-Sounds, 74.99% (73.82% F1) on CREMA-D, 95.20% (91.66% F1) on UCF-101, and 93.46% (89.02% F1) on Food-101. The largest gains over the strongest baseline (MCR) are +3.13%p accuracy on Kinetics-Sounds and +4.08%p on CREMA-D. On the trimodal CMU-MOSI dataset, MIDAS reaches 74.00% accuracy, outperforming joint training (71.13%) by nearly 3%p.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MIDAS outperforms all six imbalance-specific baselines on three of four datasets (Kinetics-Sounds, CREMA-D, Food-101) and is essentially tied with SMV on UCF-101 (95.20% vs. 95.24% accuracy, with MIDAS showing lower variance). The most notable margins are over MCR (+3.13%p on Kinetics-Sounds, +4.08%p on CREMA-D) and over AMCo (+5.08%p on CREMA-D), confirming that supervised misaligned-sample learning with explicit confidence-based labels is more effective than unsupervised/contrastive misalignment (MCR, LFM) or feature-masking strategies (AMCo). Against the data augmentation baselines (Mixup, PowMix, LeMDA) in Table 3, MIDAS leads by a wide margin on CREMA-D (+11.33%p over PowMix) and by a smaller but consistent margin on Food-101 (+3.55%p over PowMix), while PowMix and LeMDA actually underperform plain joint training on Food-101. SMV, which generates ~3× more augmented data, remains the closest competitor on UCF-101, suggesting that data volume can partially compensate for the lack of targeted misalignment. Overall, MIDAS is the only method that simultaneously achieves top accuracy and F1 on all four benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant optimization-based and feature-masking approaches to imbalanced multimodal learning (e.g., AGM, OGM, AMCo, SMV) by reframing misaligned samples—previously treated as noise or outliers—as informative supervised training signals. It also distinguishes itself from prior misaligned-pair methods (LFM, MCR) that use such pairs only in unsupervised or contrastive settings, instead assigning explicit soft labels derived from unimodal confidence scores. The confidence-based labeling draws on prior work estimating modality reliability from unimodal outputs [11, 30], while the two weighting mechanisms (weak-modality and hard-sample) are novel contributions that have no direct precedent in the multimodal augmentation literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MIDAS offers a practical, modality-agnostic data augmentation recipe that practitioners can plug into existing multimodal training pipelines without architectural changes, and the Shapley-value analysis convincingly shows the gains come from genuine rebalancing (video utilization jumping from 19.2% to 49.9%) rather than generic regularization. The finding that standard multimodal augmentation methods (Mixup, PowMix, LeMDA) can actually underperform joint training on imbalanced tasks highlights a gap in the augmentation literature that this work addresses. Key limitations include restriction to classification tasks and reliance on a warm-up phase for unimodal classifiers; extending the approach to generative or retrieval tasks, and to settings with more than three modalities, remains open.
