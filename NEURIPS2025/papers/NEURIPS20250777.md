## Problem

Unsupervised Domain Generalization (UDG) requires disentangling semantic content from domain-specific variations in self-supervised representations, but existing methods either depend on domain labels that are unavailable in practice or lack a principled information-theoretic criterion for what constitutes an optimal semantic representation. This paper formalizes the UDG objective as learning a Minimal Sufficient Semantic Representation that preserves all shared semantic information while maximally removing semantically irrelevant factors, and provides a theoretical guarantee that optimizing toward this representation reduces out-of-distribution risk.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250777/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 224×224 RGB images from multiple source domains and produces two disentangled representations per view: a semantic representation s and a variation representation v, both derived from a shared ViT-S/16 encoder. Positive pairs are constructed via Fourier-based augmentation (swapping amplitude spectra between images), and a decoder reconstructs images from cross-domain (s, v) combinations to enforce variation capture. No category or domain labels are used during representation learning; downstream classification is performed via linear probing (1%, 5% labels) or fine-tuning (10%, 100% labels).

## Method

MS-UDG trains a ViT-S/16 encoder with three complementary losses: (1) an InfoNCE loss (L_suff) that ensures the sufficient representation z captures all shared information between augmented views; (2) a mixed InfoNCE loss (L_min) that simultaneously maximizes I(s₁;s₂) and minimizes I(s₁;v₁) by treating the variation representation as an additional negative in the contrastive denominator; and (3) a cross-domain reconstruction loss (L_max) that forces the variation v to encode all input information not captured by semantics, thereby maximizing I(v;x|S). An Information Disentanglement Module (IDM) uses two MLP heads to split z into s and v, while a Semantic Representation Optimizing Module (SROM) applies cross-attention (inspired by Stable Diffusion) to fuse s and v for reconstruction. The theoretical contribution proves that minimizing I(s;v) and maximizing I(v;x|S) jointly minimize I(s;x|S), yielding the minimal sufficient semantic representation whose Bayes error rate has the tightest upper bound among all sufficient representations.

## Results

On PACS, MS-UDG achieves average accuracies of 45.26%, 63.02%, 68.35%, and 72.89% at 1%, 5%, 10%, and 100% label fractions, respectively, outperforming the strongest SSL baseline SimCLR V2 by +19.33%, +22.34%, +26.71%, and +22.83%. On DomainNet, MS-UDG reaches overall accuracies of 32.45%, 41.33%, and 53.37% at 1%, 5%, and 10% label fractions, surpassing the best SSL method MAE by +17.04%, +18.45%, and +14.52%. Against the strongest UDG baselines, MS-UDG improves over BSS/SimCLR by +0.35% (PACS 1%) to +6.62% (PACS 5%) and over CycleMAE by +5.48% (PACS 10%) to +2.59% (DomainNet 10%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MS-UDG outperforms all 11 baselines across both PACS and DomainNet. Against standard SSL methods, the gains are largest: SimCLR V2 (best SSL on PACS) is surpassed by +19.33% to +26.71% across label fractions, while MAE (best SSL on DomainNet) is beaten by +14.52% to +17.04%. Among UDG-specific methods, MS-UDG edges out BSS/SimCLR (the strongest UDG baseline) by a narrow +0.35% on PACS at 1% labels but widens to +6.62% at 5% and +11.31% at 100%; on DomainNet the margin over BSS grows from +0.18% (1%) to +11.79% (10%). Against CycleMAE, the previous SOTA UDG method, MS-UDG leads by +5.48% on PACS at 10% labels and +2.59% on DomainNet at 10% labels. DiMAE and BrAD, which rely on domain-specific decoders or style alignment, are consistently outperformed by 5–15% depending on the dataset and label fraction. In the no-ImageNet-pretraining setting (Table 6), MS-UDG also surpasses DisMAE by +0.77% (1%) to +2.65% (100%) overall accuracy on DomainNet.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Unsupervised Domain Generalization (UDG) task introduced by Zhang et al. (DARLING, CVPR 2022) by grounding it in the information-theoretic framework of minimal sufficient representations from contrastive learning (Wang et al., CVPR 2022). While prior UDG methods (DiMAE, CycleMAE, DisMAE) rely on domain labels to disentangle semantics from style, and augmentation-based methods (BSS, BrAD, FDA) standardize style via Fourier operations, MS-UDG formulates the problem as learning a representation that is simultaneously sufficient (preserving all shared semantic information across augmented views) and minimal (removing semantically irrelevant variation), without any domain labels. The implementation builds on InfoNCE-based contrastive learning (He et al., CVPR 2020) and Fourier-based augmentation (Xu et al., CVPR 2021), adding a mixed InfoNCE disentanglement loss and a cross-attention reconstruction decoder inspired by Stable Diffusion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first theoretical link between minimal sufficient semantic representations and out-of-distribution generalization risk in the unsupervised setting, offering a principled justification for why removing semantically irrelevant information improves downstream performance on unseen domains. Practically, it achieves state-of-the-art UDG performance without domain labels, which are often unavailable or expensive in real-world deployment. However, the authors acknowledge the method is primarily effective under pronounced domain discrepancies and may offer limited gains when covariate shifts are subtle, leaving open the question of how to adapt the minimality objective for fine-grained or continuous domain variation.
