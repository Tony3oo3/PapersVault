## Problem

Existing differentially private (DP) algorithms for finding second-order stationary points (SOSP) in non-convex stochastic optimization suffer from two critical flaws: their saddle-point escape analysis overlooks gradient variance, yielding incorrect error bounds, and they rely on auxiliary private model selection procedures that degrade utility—especially in distributed settings where sharing high-dimensional second-order information is costly. Moreover, no prior work provides formal DP-SOSP guarantees for distributed learning with heterogeneous data across multiple clients.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes a dataset of n i.i.d. samples (or m clients each holding n local samples from potentially different distributions in the federated variant), a DP budget (ε, δ), and initial model parameters x₀. It directly outputs model parameters constituting an α-SOSP (or α-DP-SOSP under inter-client record-level DP), with α = Õ(1/n^{1/3} + (√d/(εn))^{2/5}) in the centralized case and Õ(1/(mn)^{1/3} + (√d/(√mn·ε))^{2/5}) in the distributed case. Crucially, the output is produced during the optimization loop itself—no post-hoc private selection over iterates is required.

## Method

The paper introduces Gauss-PSGD, a generic perturbed SGD framework that injects Gaussian noise into a general stochastic gradient oracle and uses model drift distance (‖x_t − x̃‖ ≥ R) rather than function-value decrease as the saddle-point escape criterion, enabling the algorithm to certify and output an SOSP directly during training. The gradient oracle is instantiated as Ada-DP-SPIDER, an adaptive variance-reduced estimator that dynamically switches between a full-batch oracle O₁ and a low-variance incremental oracle O₂ based on cumulative model drift, avoiding the redundant noise injection and frozen-state tracking of prior work. In the distributed extension, each client independently computes privatized gradient estimates with client-specific Gaussian noise and sends them to a server for averaging, achieving ICRL-DP without a trusted server. The key analytical correction is properly accounting for gradient variance in the escape analysis, which tightens the error exponent from 3/7 to 2/5 in the privacy-dependent term.

## Results

The paper's primary results are theoretical convergence rate bounds for finding α-DP-SOSP. In the centralized setting, the proposed Gauss-PSGD with Ada-DP-SPIDER achieves an error rate of Õ(1/n^{1/3} + (√d/(εn))^{2/5}), correcting the previously reported suboptimal bound of Õ(1/n^{1/3} + (√d/(εn))^{3/7}) from Liu et al. (2024). In the distributed setting with m clients and n samples per client, the first formal DP-SOSP guarantee under heterogeneous data is Õ(1/(mn)^{1/3} + (√d/(√mn·ε))^{2/5}). Numerical experiments on CIFAR-10 and MNIST (detailed in Appendix F) validate the theoretical predictions, though specific accuracy figures are deferred to the supplementary material.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Liu et al. [30] (NeurIPS 2024), the paper achieves a strictly tighter privacy-dependent error exponent: the term (√d/(εn))^{2/5} decays faster than the previously reported (√d/(εn))^{3/7} (since 2/5 < 3/7), correcting an analytical error that arose from overlooking gradient variance in the saddle-point escape analysis. Additionally, the proposed framework eliminates the need for an auxiliary private model selection procedure that [30] requires to identify a DP-SOSP among iterates, which is particularly problematic in distributed settings where sharing high-dimensional second-order information incurs significant communication overhead. Against DIFF2 [37] (ICML 2023), the paper provides a strict improvement: DIFF2 only guarantees convergence to a DP-FOSP (first-order stationary point) under homogeneous data, whereas the proposed method guarantees convergence to a DP-SOSP (second-order stationary point) under heterogeneous data, yielding the first formal DP-SOSP bound in the distributed federated learning setting. The paper further demonstrates (Theorem 4) that any private model selection procedure in the distributed setting introduces additional error terms scaling as d/√(mnε) and d²/(mn²ε²), which degrade the overall guarantee in high-dimensional regimes—reinforcing the advantage of the proposed framework that inherently outputs an SOSP without selection.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the DP-SPIDER variance-reduction framework [2] and the perturbed-SGD saddle-point-escape analysis of Jin et al. [24], but departs from both in critical ways. Its most immediate predecessor is Liu et al. [30] (NeurIPS 2024), which first attempted DP-SOSP by injecting extra Gaussian noise near saddle points and relying on a post-hoc private model selection step; this paper corrects [30]'s convergence error rate (from (√d/(εn))^{3/7} to (√d/(εn))^{2/5}) and replaces the function-value-decrease escape criterion with a model-drift-distance criterion, eliminating the need for auxiliary selection. The distributed extension generalizes DIFF2 [37], which only guarantees DP-FOSP under homogeneous data, to DP-SOSP under heterogeneous data via an adaptive SPIDER variant.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The correction of [30]'s error bound matters because it changes the practical privacy-utility tradeoff for DP non-convex optimization, and the distributed extension provides the first formal DP-SOSP guarantees in federated settings with heterogeneous data—a gap that has no prior formal treatment. A notable open question highlighted by the paper is that private model selection in distributed high-dimensional settings imposes a dimension constraint (d ≤ (√m·n·ε)^{6/13}) that degrades utility, suggesting that selection-free algorithms like Gauss-PSGD may be essential as model dimensions grow. The framework's reliance on unbiased gradient oracles (Assumption 1) and its separation from practical DP-SGD implementations with clipping bias remain limitations that future work must address.
