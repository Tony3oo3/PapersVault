## Problem

Current embodied world models optimize RGB pixel fitting without explicit physical awareness, producing videos with geometric inconsistencies and unrealistic motion in contact-rich robotic scenarios (e.g., deformable objects, cloth manipulation). Existing approaches to injecting physics—regularization on 3D representations, cascaded physics simulators, or object-level material fields—are either domain-limited, computationally expensive, or inapplicable to scene-level generation.

## Input / Output

The model takes a 16-frame video clip (sampled at 2 Hz) and a corresponding robot action sequence (concatenated end position, end orientation, and effector position) as input, and autoregressively generates 15 future RGB frames plus depth maps. Both RGB and depth are tokenized via MAGVIT-2 into discrete latent tokens at reduced spatial resolution (H/α × W/α), and the model is conditioned on the first frame to roll out subsequent frames. Pre-computed depth maps (from Video Depth Anything) and keypoint trajectories (from SpatialTracker) serve as supervision signals during training but are not required at inference.

## Method

RoboScape employs a dual-branch co-autoregressive Transformer (DCT) where parallel RGB and depth branches each consist of 32 spatial-temporal Transformer blocks with causal temporal attention and bidirectional spatial attention. Two physics-informed auxiliary losses are jointly trained with the RGB prediction: (1) a temporal depth prediction branch whose intermediate features are injected into the RGB branch via learnable linear projections at every ST-Transformer block, enforcing 3D geometric consistency; and (2) an adaptive keypoint dynamics loss that selects the top-K most motion-active keypoints (by cumulative displacement) and penalizes token inconsistency at those locations across frames, implicitly encoding material properties like rigidity and softness. A keypoint-guided attention mechanism further upweights the cross-entropy loss in spatiotemporal regions intersected by keypoint trajectories. The final multi-task objective combines RGB cross-entropy, depth cross-entropy, keypoint L2 consistency, and attention-weighted cross-entropy with tunable coefficients (λ₁=1, λ₂=0.01, λ₃=1, γ=5).

## Results

On the AgiBotWorld-Beta test set (1.2K clips), RoboScape achieves the best performance across all six metrics: LPIPS 0.128, PSNR 21.730, AbsRel 0.378, δ₁ 0.608, δ₂ 0.814, and ΔPSNR 3.442. For downstream robotic policy learning, synthetic data from RoboScape improves π₀ success rate on LIBERO from 65.2% (real data only) to 79.1% (with 800 synthetic trajectories), and as a policy evaluator, RoboScape achieves a Pearson correlation of 0.953 with ground-truth simulator outcomes, far exceeding IRASim and iVideoGPT.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RoboScape outperforms all four baselines on every reported metric. Against the two embodied world models, the gains are most dramatic: IRASim (LPIPS 0.663, PSNR 11.38) and iVideoGPT (LPIPS 0.501, PSNR 16.23) both struggle with long-horizon motion consistency, while RoboScape achieves LPIPS 0.128 and PSNR 21.73. Against Genie, the strongest general world model baseline, RoboScape improves ΔPSNR from 1.868 to 3.442 (nearly 2× better action controllability) and reduces AbsRel from 0.446 to 0.378. CogVideoX, a text-to-video diffusion model, cannot follow action commands (ΔPSNR not applicable) and trails RoboScape on all visual and geometric metrics. In the policy-learning transfer experiment (Table 5), RoboScape-generated data yields 79.1% average LIBERO success versus 69.8% for CogVideoX and 64.8% for IRASim, confirming that the physics-informed generation translates into practically better downstream policies.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the autoregressive embodied world model lineage (Genie, iVideoGPT, IRASim) by integrating physics knowledge directly into the training objective rather than relying on external physics engines or narrow regularization constraints. It departs from prior joint RGB-depth approaches (Aether, Tesseract) by operating at the temporal level with a dual-branch co-autoregressive Transformer and cross-branch feature fusion, and introduces keypoint dynamics learning as a self-supervised proxy for material properties—avoiding the cascaded pipeline complexity of simulator-based methods and the domain restrictions of Gaussian-splatting regularization. The MAGVIT-2 tokenizer and ST-Transformer backbone are inherited from the video generation literature, while the adaptive keypoint sampling via SpatialTracker and curriculum-organized data pipeline from AGIBOT-World are novel integrations specific to robotic manipulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RoboScape demonstrates that physics-informed world models can simultaneously serve as high-quality synthetic data generators (matching real-data policy performance on RoboMimic and improving LIBERO scores) and reliable policy evaluators (0.953 Pearson correlation with ground-truth simulator), addressing two critical bottlenecks in scalable robotic learning. Key limitations remain: the 48-frame rollout cap restricts long-horizon task simulation, the physical modeling is limited to geometric and kinematic properties without force/dynamic interactions, and validation is confined to single- and dual-arm manipulation. Open questions include whether the keypoint-dynamics-as-material-proxy assumption generalizes beyond contact-rich manipulation, how the approach scales to diverse embodiments (quadrupeds, humanoids), and whether richer physical laws (e.g., momentum conservation) can be incorporated without the computational cost of explicit simulators.
