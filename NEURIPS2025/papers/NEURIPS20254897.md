## Problem

Randomized exploration methods in contextual bandits (Thompson Sampling, Perturbed History Exploration) consistently outperform deterministic OFU-based algorithms in practice, yet provably incur a suboptimal Õ(d^{3/2}√T) regret bound versus the near-optimal Õ(d√T) of OFU methods. Hamidi and Bayati showed this √d inflation is inherent to parameter-space randomization, creating a fundamental tension between empirical performance and theoretical guarantees. The paper asks whether a different locus of stochasticity can retain the practical benefits of randomization while recovering the tighter regret bound.

## Input / Output

At each round t, the algorithm receives a set of d-dimensional feature vectors {x_ti} for all available arms, the full history of past (feature, reward) pairs, a regularization parameter λ, and a tuning parameter c_t (set to the confidence width β_t). It outputs a single selected arm index i_t and the corresponding perturbed feature vectors {x̃_ti}. A key structural detail is that a single shared Gaussian vector ζ_t perturbs all arms simultaneously, coupling their scores and eliminating any explicit dependence on the number of arms K in the regret bound. The MLE θ̂_t is computed via SQP or IRLS since no closed form exists for general GLMs.

## Method

GLM-FP injects randomness into the feature inputs rather than the model parameters or rewards: at each round it computes the MLE θ̂_t, samples a shared ζ_t ~ N(0,I), and perturbs each arm's feature as x̃_ti = x_ti + c_t · ‖x_ti‖_{Ĥ_t^{-1}} / ‖θ̂_t‖ · ζ_t, then selects the arm maximizing μ(x̃_ti^T θ̂_t). The perturbation magnitude is governed by a curvature-aware weighted Gram matrix Ĥ_t = λI + ∇²L_t(θ̂_t), which adapts to the local geometry of the link function via the Hessian of the negative log-likelihood. The critical theoretical advantage is that the concentration analysis only requires bounding a one-dimensional projection u^T ζ_t along a fixed direction, rather than the full d-dimensional norm ‖ζ_t‖₂ required by TS; this avoids a union bound over d coordinates and eliminates the extraneous √d factor. The result is the first randomized algorithm for generalized linear bandits achieving Õ(d√(κ*T) + d²/κ) regret with no dependence on K, matching the minimax-optimal d-scaling of deterministic OFU methods while benefiting from the instance-dependent constant κ*. The framework extends naturally to non-parametric and neural network models (DeepFP), where independent arm-wise Gaussian noise with 1/t variance decay replaces the GLM-specific scaling.

## Results

GLM-FP achieves the lowest cumulative regret across all tested feature dimensions in both linear and logistic contextual bandit settings, outperforming TS, PHE, UCB, RandUCB, and ε-greedy over 100 independent runs. In the neural bandit setting, DeepFP (the feature-perturbation extension) outperforms NeuralUCB, NeuralTS, and FTPL on all three UCI benchmarks (shuttle: 9 features/7 classes, isolet: 617 features/26 classes, mushroom: 112 features/binary). Theoretically, GLM-FP achieves Õ(d√(κ*T) + d²/κ) regret, matching the minimax-optimal linear d-dependence of deterministic OFU methods while retaining the instance-dependent κ* benefit, compared to Õ(d^{3/2}√T) for TS and PHE. A dedicated experiment (T=200,000, K=50, varying d) confirms the linear d-scaling of GLM-FP versus the superlinear d^{3/2} scaling of TS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GLM-FP consistently outperforms Thompson Sampling (TS) [3] and Perturbed History Exploration (PHE) [32] in both linear and logistic bandit settings, achieving lower cumulative regret across all dimensions; the key advantage is the elimination of the √d factor in the regret bound (linear d vs. d^{3/2}). Against UCB/GLM-UCB [17], GLM-FP wins by avoiding the over-exploration typical of deterministic optimistic methods while maintaining comparable theoretical guarantees. In the linear bandit case, RandUCB [46] is the closest competitor (both achieve Õ(d√T)), but GLM-FP demonstrates superior robustness in fixed-arm and non-stationary arm-set settings, and in the logistic setting GLM-FP clearly outperforms RandUCB due to its curvature-aware perturbation (benefiting from κ* rather than being penalized by 1/κ). In the neural bandit experiments, DeepFP outperforms NeuralUCB [52], NeuralTS [51], and FTPL [25] on all three UCI datasets (shuttle, isolet, mushroom), achieving the lowest cumulative regret without requiring posterior approximations or gradient-based confidence intervals. Overall, GLM-FP/DeepFP wins or ties on every comparison across all experimental settings reported in the paper.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in randomized contextual bandit exploration—Thompson Sampling (TS) and Perturbed History Exploration (PHE), which randomize model parameters or observed rewards—by instead perturbing the input feature vectors at decision time. Theoretically, it builds on the unified confidence sequences for GLMs from Lee et al. [37] and the instance-dependent analysis framework of Abeille et al. [4], while its linear variant (LinFP) is shown to coincide with RandUCB [46], differing only in interpretation. The key conceptual departure is that feature-space perturbation reduces the concentration analysis from a d-dimensional Gaussian norm (required in TS) to a single 1D projection, thereby eliminating the √d penalty that Hamidi and Bayati [21] proved inherent to parameter-based randomization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first randomized algorithm for generalized linear bandits to achieve Õ(d√(κ*T) + d²/κ) regret—matching the tight OFU guarantees while retaining the empirical robustness of randomized exploration—and it does so with no dependence on the number of arms K. The extension to non-parametric models (DeepFP) suggests feature perturbation is a broadly applicable exploration principle that avoids the instability of parameter sampling in overparameterized settings (p ≫ d). Open questions include whether the approach extends to structured RL settings (Linear MDPs, value-function approximation), non-stationary environments, and whether the shared-perturbation design (coupling all arms with one random vector) is essential or merely sufficient for the tight bound.
