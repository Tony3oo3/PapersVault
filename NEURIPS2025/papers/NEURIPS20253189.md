## Problem

Astronomy lacks a unified framework for joint modeling across its highly diverse data modalities, as existing approaches treat each modality in isolation or rely on contrastive objectives limited to single physical phenomena. The fundamental challenge is integrating heterogeneous observations from different instruments, measurement protocols, and noise sources into a single model that captures cross-modal relationships without hand-crafted fusion schemas.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253189/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

AION-1 ingests 39 modalities per astronomical object, including 96×96 multiband images (4-band Legacy Survey, 5-band HSC), optical spectra on fixed pixel grids (DESI 7081-pixel, SDSS variable, Gaia 110 Gauss-Hermite coefficients), scalar photometry/astrometry, shape descriptors, and segmentation maps. At inference, any arbitrary subset of these modalities can be provided as context, and the model produces either a pooled object embedding (via mean or attentive pooling over the encoder's token sequence) for downstream probing, or conditional samples of any target modality (e.g., high-resolution DESI spectra from coarse Gaia BP/RP coefficients) through iterative MaskGIT-style decoding.

## Method

AION-1 uses a two-stage pipeline: modality-specific tokenizers (ResNet+FSQ for images, ConvNeXt-V2+LFQ for spectra, CDF-uniform binning for scalars) homogenize heterogeneous observations into discrete token sequences, followed by a T5-style transformer encoder-decoder trained with a multimodal masked-prediction objective over all modalities simultaneously. A key architectural choice is assigning a unique modality embedding to each modality-source pair (e.g., Legacy Survey g-band and HSC g-band get distinct embeddings), implicitly encoding provenance, resolution, and data quality. The masking strategy uses a fixed global input budget of 256 tokens and output budget of 128 tokens, with Beta-distributed sampling for output tokens to align training with iterative inference-time decoding. Trained on 200M+ objects from five surveys (Legacy Survey, HSC, DESI, SDSS, Gaia) using pairwise cross-survey associations rather than requiring all modalities at once, the model family spans 300M to 3.1B parameters and is trained for 205k steps with AdamW at batch size 8096.

## Results

On galaxy property estimation (Table 1), AION-1-XL with photometry+imaging+spectra achieves R² of 0.99 (redshift), 0.95 (stellar mass), 0.53 (age), 0.62 (metallicity), and 0.73 (sSFR), matching or exceeding dedicated supervised baselines. On Galaxy Zoo 10 morphology classification (Table 4), AION-1-L reaches 87.2% accuracy with a simple MLP head on frozen embeddings, surpassing EfficientNet trained from scratch (80.0%) and DINOv2 (71.4%), while approaching ZooBot (89.6%) trained on 100M human annotations. On APOGEE stellar property prediction from Gaia XP coefficients (Table 3), AION-1-B outperforms the task-specific pretrained transformer of Leung et al. [34] on all three parameters (e.g., 94.6 K vs 99.1 K scatter in T_eff). In the low-data regime (Figure 6), AION-1 matches or surpasses supervised models trained from scratch with an order of magnitude more labeled data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DINOv2 [47], AION-1-XL substantially outperforms on all galaxy properties with photometry-only input (e.g., R² 0.79 vs 0.57 for redshift, 0.76 vs 0.55 for stellar mass), demonstrating that astronomy-specific multimodal pretraining yields far more informative embeddings than generic vision pretraining. Against AstroCLIP [48], AION-1 matches or exceeds performance on photometry-only inputs (R² 0.79 vs 0.78 for z) and on imaging-only inputs (0.94 vs 0.78 for z), while being competitive on spectra-only (0.99 vs 0.99 for z). Against supervised baselines, AION-1-XL with photometry alone (R² 0.79 for z) outperforms XGBoost on photometry (0.71), and with imaging added (0.94) exceeds ConvNeXt on imaging (0.86); with full spectra, AION-1 matches the dedicated Conv+Att spectrum network (0.99 vs 1.00 for z) while substantially outperforming it on stellar mass (0.95 vs 0.85). On morphology classification, AION-1-L (87.2%) beats EfficientNet trained from scratch (80.0%) and DINOv2 (71.4%) but falls slightly short of ZooBot (89.6%), which was trained on ~100M citizen-science labels. On APOGEE stellar parameters from Gaia XP coefficients, AION-1-B outperforms the task-specific pretrained transformer of Leung et al. [34] on all three targets (94.6 K vs 99.1 K for T_eff; 0.206 vs 0.229 dex for log g; 0.115 vs 0.143 dex for [Fe/H]), despite never having seen this task during pretraining. For rare-object retrieval (Figure 7), AION-1 achieves competitive or superior nDCG@10 compared to AstroCLIP and Stein et al. [62] on spirals, mergers, and strong lenses, with the largest gains on the rarest class (strong lenses at ~0.1% prevalence).

## Relation to Prior Work

AION-1 builds directly on the 4M massively multimodal masked modeling framework (Mizrahi et al., 2023), adopting its encoder-decoder architecture, masked-token objective, and ROAR inference scheme, while extending it to handle arbitrary modality subsets via pairwise cross-survey associations rather than requiring all modalities simultaneously. It departs from the dominant contrastive paradigm in prior astronomy-specific multimodal work (AstroCLIP, Stein et al.) by using generative masked prediction, which the authors argue can capture information beyond the mutual information between modalities. The bespoke tokenization strategies—FSQ for images, LFQ for spectra, and CDF-based binning for scalars—represent a novel engineering layer that addresses the heterogeneity of real-world scientific instruments, a challenge absent from the original 4M formulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AION-1 provides the first open-source, billion-parameter template for multimodal scientific foundation models, demonstrating that a single masked-modeling backbone can unify 39 modalities across galaxies, stars, and quasars and serve as a frozen feature extractor competitive with task-specific supervised models. The low-data regime advantage—matching or exceeding supervised baselines with orders of magnitude fewer labels—is practically critical in astronomy where reliable annotations are scarce and expensive. Key open questions include whether the approach generalizes to time-series data, how survey selection biases propagate into the latent space, and whether physics-aware objectives could further constrain representations toward physically consistent manifolds.
