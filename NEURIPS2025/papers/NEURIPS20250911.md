## Problem

LLMs trained with RL from outcome rewards exhibit 'overthinking,' generating excessively long responses even for trivial problems, and existing token compression methods apply a uniform penalty that ignores the natural relationship between problem difficulty and appropriate verbosity. A second, underexplored issue is that naively combining weighted difficulty-dependent penalties with outcome rewards before GRPO's group normalization distorts the intended penalty strength due to interaction with outcome variance, undermining difficulty-aware adaptation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250911/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt containing a mathematical problem and produces a concise step-by-step reasoning chain ending with a boxed final answer. During training, the model samples N=8 responses per prompt at temperature 0.6 and top-p 0.95 (max 8192 tokens), and the on-the-fly difficulty estimate is computed as 1 minus the fraction of correct samples among these N rollouts—incurs no extra cost since GRPO already requires multi-sample advantage estimation. At inference, evaluation uses 10 samples per question (32 for AIME 2024) with majority voting via sympy-based LaTeX equivalence checking.

## Method

DIET integrates on-the-fly difficulty estimation into the RL objective through two complementary mechanisms: an adaptive trade-off parameter α_ada = α_base · Ĉ that scales penalty strength linearly with correctness (stronger compression pressure on easy problems), and a dynamic length target t(x,π) sampled from a difficulty-conditioned uniform distribution that shifts the penalty from minimizing absolute length to minimizing length relative to a difficulty-aware budget. A key enabling technique, Advantage Weighting, normalizes the outcome and penalty advantages separately by their per-prompt standard deviations before combining them with the adaptive weight, avoiding the signal distortion that arises when the combined reward's normalization factor is confounded by difficulty-dependent outcome variance. Training is further stabilized by Cyclical Compression Pressure, a cosine-modulated schedule that periodically relaxes the penalty to prevent premature convergence on brevity. The full method is implemented within GRPO on the R1-Distilled Qwen 1.5B model trained on DeepScaleR, achieving 40.7% token reduction while improving macro-average Pass@1 from 48.6% to 50.2%.

## Results

DIET achieves a Macro Average Pass@1 of 50.2% with only 6,097 average tokens, a 40.7% reduction from the Base Model (48.6% P@1, 10,280 tokens) while simultaneously improving accuracy by +1.6%. The Adaptive Weighting variant reaches 49.9% P@1 at 5,723 tokens, matching Kimi 1.5 DPO's performance with 32% fewer tokens. On individual benchmarks, DIET leads on MATH 500 (83.0%), AIME 2024 (31.8%), AMC 2023 (65.4%), and Minerva (26.9%), while maintaining competitive performance on Olympiad Bench (43.7%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DIET outperforms all RL-based baselines on Macro Average P@1: it beats CosFn (50.2% vs. 46.8%) and O1-Pruner (50.2% vs. 45.9%) by 3.4 and 4.3 points respectively, while also using fewer tokens than CosFn (6,097 vs. 6,798). Against Kimi 1.5 DPO, the strongest SFT/DPO baseline, DIET achieves a higher P@1 (50.2% vs. 49.9%) with 28% fewer tokens (6,097 vs. 8,441). TokenSkip achieves extreme compression (1,697 tokens) but suffers a catastrophic 17.7-point P@1 drop (30.9% vs. 48.6% base), confirming that non-difficulty-aware compression severely degrades reasoning. Kimi 1.5 RL shows the worst performance-efficiency trade-off among RL methods (35.0% P@1 at 4,050 tokens), while DIET's combined adaptive penalty and dynamic target strategy dominates the Pareto frontier across all five benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of RL-based token compression for reasoning LLMs (Kimi 1.5 RL, CosFn, O1-Pruner, L1) by replacing uniform length penalties with difficulty-adaptive ones, drawing on the observation that LLMs naturally allocate more tokens to harder problems. It builds directly on GRPO's group-normalized policy gradient framework and the Kimi penalty formulation, but departs from prior work in two key ways: (1) it introduces on-the-fly difficulty estimation to modulate both the penalty strength (adaptive α) and the target length (dynamic target), and (2) it provides a theoretical analysis showing that naively combining weighted penalties with outcome rewards before GRPO normalization distorts the intended penalty effect, proposing Advantage Weighting as a fix. The cyclical compression pressure schedule is a minor training-dynamics addition inspired by annealing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical relevance is clear: a 40–45% token reduction with maintained or slightly improved accuracy directly lowers inference costs for deployed reasoning models, and the inference-scaling benefit (better majority-voting accuracy at fixed compute budgets) addresses a deployment concern that prior compression work largely ignored. However, the evaluation is confined to mathematical reasoning on a single model family (R1-Distilled Qwen at 1.5B and 7B) trained on one dataset (DeepScaleR), leaving open whether difficulty-aware compression generalizes to coding, science, or other reasoning domains where the difficulty-length relationship may differ. The Advantage Weighting technique is a broadly applicable methodological fix for any weighted-reward GRPO training, which could have utility beyond token compression. A key open question is whether combining DIET's difficulty-aware objectives with other compression strategies (e.g., latent-space reasoning, step-skipping) yields further gains.
