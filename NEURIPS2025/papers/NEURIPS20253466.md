## Problem

Visual Autoregressive (VAR) models use a next-scale decoding paradigm that progressively generates token maps from coarse to fine, which breaks the semantic stability and monotonic locality assumptions underlying conventional early-exit and layer-compression methods. The paper identifies through wavelet-based analysis that VAR's intermediate representations are highly dynamic (cosine similarity between consecutive layers stays between 0.2–0.6) and that high-frequency details emerge only in later decoding steps, rendering existing dynamic inference strategies ineffective for this architecture.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253466/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For unconditional generation, the model takes a single ImageNet class label and produces a 256×256 RGB image through 10 autoregressive steps, each outputting a full token map at increasing spatial resolution. For zero-shot inpainting, it additionally accepts a masked image as spatial conditioning. A distinctive aspect is that the number of active transformer layers can vary per generation step (e.g., 20 layers for early coarse steps, 10 layers for later fine steps), enabling per-step computational budget control within a single model without architectural modification.

## Method

FreqExit is a training-only framework (no architecture change) that fine-tunes a pretrained VAR-d20 checkpoint for 80 epochs (~30% of original training cost) using three synergistic loss components. First, a curriculum-based early-exit supervision applies depth-aware layer dropout (deeper layers dropped more frequently), cross-entropy loss at intermediate layers with quadratic depth weighting, and layer-adaptive knowledge distillation with temperature annealing from 4.0 to 1.0, all scheduled via a rotational round-robin curriculum. Second, a progressive high-frequency consistency loss aligns student and teacher token maps in the Haar wavelet domain, with step-aware resolution weighting and delayed activation to avoid destabilizing early training. Third, a lightweight Frequency-Gated Self-Reconstruction (FGSR) module applies learnable exponential gates over four wavelet sub-bands (LL, LH, HL, HH) to provide frequency-aware reconstruction supervision that adaptively shifts emphasis from low-frequency structure to high-frequency detail across decoding stages. At inference, the model simply truncates at chosen layers per step, achieving up to 2× speedup with FID 3.58 or 1.3× speedup with FID 2.72 (near-lossless).

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256, FreqExit achieves up to 2.00× inference speedup (10 layers, FID 3.58) and 1.30× speedup with near-zero quality loss (14 layers, FID 2.90). At the most conservative setting (16 layers, 1.27× speedup), FreqExit actually surpasses the full VAR-d20 model on FID (2.72 vs. 2.78). The method also improves throughput to 7.72 it/s at the 2× setting, compared to 3.80 it/s for the full model, while reducing active parameters from 600M to 300M.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FreqExit consistently outperforms LayerSkip at every matched layer count: at 16 layers, FreqExit achieves FID 2.72 vs. LayerSkip's 3.25; at 14 layers, 2.90 vs. 3.99; and at 12 layers, 3.16 vs. 4.78 — a gap that widens as fewer layers are used. FreqExit also extends to a 10-layer configuration (2.00× speedup, FID 3.58) for which no LayerSkip equivalent is reported. Against CALM (FID 8.20) and CoDe without training (FID 9.35), FreqExit is dramatically superior, confirming that training-free or two-model approaches are poorly suited to VAR's next-scale paradigm. Even CoDe with training (FID 5.86) lags behind FreqExit at all comparable settings. Notably, FreqExit(a) at 16 layers achieves a lower FID (2.72) than the unmodified full VAR-d20 (2.78), indicating that the frequency-aware training actually improves generation quality rather than merely preserving it.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends early-exit inference (CALM, LayerSkip, BERxiT) from token-wise autoregressive models to the next-scale generation paradigm of VAR, where entire token maps are produced per step. It departs from prior dynamic inference by identifying that VAR's step-wise frequency progression and lack of layer redundancy invalidate the semantic-stability and monotonic-locality assumptions underlying existing early-exit and layer-compression methods. Rather than modifying the architecture, FreqExit introduces training-time frequency-aware losses (wavelet-domain consistency and a frequency-gated self-reconstruction module) to reshape intermediate representations so that shallow exits become viable, building on wavelet-based analysis techniques previously used in image compression and generative model studies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FreqExit is the first method to enable early-exit acceleration in next-scale VAR models, achieving up to 2× speedup with only a modest FID increase (2.78→3.58) and 1.3× speedup with essentially no perceptible quality loss, which is practically significant for deploying VAR in resource-constrained settings. The frequency-domain diagnostic (showing that high-frequency accuracy drives quality and that no layer is redundant) provides a principled explanation for why prior dynamic inference fails on VAR, a framing that could guide acceleration of other hierarchical generative models. Limitations include the need for 80-epoch fine-tuning, manual tuning of multiple loss weights, evaluation only on ImageNet 256×256, and the absence of error bars or multi-seed validation; open questions include generalization to higher resolutions, other next-scale architectures, and whether the frequency-aware supervision can be automated to reduce tuning burden.
