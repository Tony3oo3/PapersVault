## Problem

The theory of universal online learning (Bousquet et al., STOC'21) characterizes when a hypothesis class admits a learner making finitely many mistakes, but treats the learner as an abstract mathematical function without addressing whether it can be implemented as a computer program. This paper bridges that gap by asking when universal online learning is achievable by a computable learner, and shows that the answer is not always yes—even for recursively enumerable representable (RER) and decidable representable (DR) classes.

## Input / Output

The learner receives a finite sample S = ((x_0,y_0),…,(x_n,y_n)) of previously revealed pairs and a new query x ∈ N, and must output a binary prediction ŷ ∈ {0,1}. The critical constraint beyond standard online learning is that the learner must be a total computable function (or an almost-total randomized computable function in the agnostic setting), i.e., a program that halts on every input. The domain is restricted to N, justified by the observation that all practical machine learning operates over countable domains such as images or binary strings.

## Method

The paper introduces computability constraints into the universal online learning framework and provides exact characterizations for realizable, agnostic, and proper variants. The central technical insight is that the topological closure of a class (functions locally consistent with H after finitely many steps) may contain uncomputable functions even when H consists solely of computable functions, which is precisely what obstructs computable learning (Theorem 1). For agnostic learning, Theorem 2 characterizes learnability as the existence of an RER superset of the closure, with the proof converting a realizable learner to an agnostic one via prediction with expert advice (Weighted Majority Algorithm with a doubling trick over countably many experts). Separation results are established by diagonalization: Theorem 3 constructs a class using 'evil sequences' that defeat every total computable learner in the agnostic setting, while Theorem 6 employs a priority construction to build a RER class where no computable learner can be proper, even though a non-proper computable learner exists.

## Results

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical results or quantitative metrics. The main contributions are six theorems establishing separations and characterizations in computable universal online learning. Theorem 1 shows that universal online learnability does not imply computable universal online learnability, even for RER (recursively enumerable representable) classes that are decidable representable. Theorem 2 provides an exact three-way characterization of computable agnostic universal online learnability (equivalent to total computable realizable learnability and to the existence of a RER superset of the closure). Theorems 3–4 establish that the standard realizable-to-agnostic equivalence breaks without computability constraints but is restored for RER classes. Theorems 5–6 characterize and separate proper from improper computable universal online learning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare empirical methods or report benchmark numbers. Instead, it establishes theoretical separations between different notions of learnability. The key separation is between universal online learnability (Bousquet et al., STOC'21) and computable universal online learnability: Theorem 1 shows the former does not imply the latter, even for DR classes, in sharp contrast to the finite Littlestone dimension case where RER classes are always computably learnable (Assos et al., 2023; Kozachinskiy and Steifer, 2024). A second separation (Theorem 3) shows that computable universal online learnability does not imply computable agnostic learnability for arbitrary classes, whereas for RER classes the two are equivalent (Theorem 4). A third separation (Theorem 6) shows that proper computable universal online learning is strictly harder than non-proper computable universal online learning, even among RER classes. These results collectively show that adding computability constraints to the universal online learning framework of Bousquet et al. (2021) yields a strictly more restrictive and more nuanced theory.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of universal online learning (Bousquet et al., STOC'21), which characterizes learnability via ordinal Littlestone dimension, and the growing line of work on computable analogs of learning (Agarwal et al. 2020; Hasrati and Ben-David 2023; Assos et al. 2023). Prior computability results covered PAC learning and uniform online learning (finite Littlestone dimension), where RER classes are always computably learnable. The key departure here is extending the computability analysis to the universal (non-uniform, ordinal-dimension) setting, where the authors show the reassuring equivalence breaks down: universal online learnability no longer guarantees the existence of a computable learner, even for RER or DR classes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides exact characterizations (via RER supersets of the closure) for when computable universal online learning, agnostic learning, and proper learning are possible, giving a more realistic and implementable picture of the existing theory. A notable open problem is characterizing realizable computable universal online learning for arbitrary (non-RER) classes, and the question of how complex the learners for Theorem 1's classes must be is deferred to a companion paper. For practitioners, the practical takeaway is that the standard theoretical guarantee of universal online learnability does not automatically yield a deployable algorithm, and the RER + closure condition (Theorem 4) is the operative criterion for classes of practical relevance.
