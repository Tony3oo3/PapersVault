## Problem

CASSI hyperspectral reconstruction is a severely ill-posed inverse problem where spatial and spectral information are entangled in a single 2D measurement, and existing methods lack an interpretable decomposition that separates the physical roles of spatial and spectral components. Additionally, captured radiance inherently couples intrinsic surface reflectance with scene illumination, making reconstruction sensitive to lighting variations—a challenge that intrinsic image decomposition has addressed in RGB but has never been incorporated into CASSI reconstruction.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 2D CASSI compressed measurement (H × (W + d·(N_λ−1))) and a known intensity image from a co-located PAN or RGB camera in a dual-camera setup. It outputs a chromaticity cube (H × W × N_λ) representing the normalized, lighting-invariant spectral signature per pixel; the full hyperspectral image is recovered by element-wise multiplication with the known intensity. The intensity is approximated as the PAN image under the proven PAN-Intensity Equivalence, and the chromaticity is supervised with an ℓ₂ loss against ground-truth chromaticity derived from the decomposition.

## Method

CIDNet is a K-stage deep unfolding network built on a chromaticity-intensity decomposition (X = C ⊙ I) that reformulates the CASSI forward model as a linear inverse problem y = Hc + n on the chromaticity variable, with the intensity absorbed into an intensity-guided mask M' = I ⊙ M. Each unfolding stage alternates between a data-consistency gradient projection with spatially-varying anisotropic noise covariance Σ^(k) and a learned proximal denoiser, where both Σ^(k) and the denoising strength ω^(k) are estimated per-iteration by a lightweight Dual Noise-Estimation Module (DNEM) CNN. The proximal denoiser is an asymmetric UNet with a Hybrid Spatial-Spectral Transformer (HSST): the encoder uses Swin-style window-based local spatial attention (Spa-LWSA) to capture high-frequency textures, while the decoder employs a sparse TopK spectral attention (Spec-TKSA) that restricts each spectral token to its K most relevant channel neighbors within local spatial windows, with multi-ratio TopK fusion to capture both dominant and complementary spectral correlations. The entire network is trained end-to-end with supervised ℓ₂ loss on chromaticity patches, using Adam with cosine annealing over 300 epochs.

## Results

On the KAIST simulation dataset (10 scenes, 28 spectral bands), CIDNet-9stg achieves an average PSNR of 44.12 dB and SSIM of 0.991 for HSI reconstruction, outperforming the closest dual-camera baseline In2SET-9stg (43.69 dB / 0.990) and the best single-camera unfolding method SSR-9stg (40.69 dB / 0.978). For chromaticity reconstruction, CIDNet-9stg reaches 35.81 dB PSNR and 0.93 SSIM, substantially exceeding In2SET (28.03 dB / 0.80) and SSR (33.47 dB / 0.91). The method uses only 4.19M parameters and 74.16 GFLOPs for the 9-stage variant, compared to 9.69M / 59.40 GFLOPs for In2SET.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CIDNet outperforms all seven baselines on average HSI PSNR across the 10 KAIST scenes. The closest competitor is In2SET-9stg [27] (43.69 dB), a dual-camera CASSI method; CIDNet-9stg edges it by 0.43 dB while using roughly half the parameters (4.19M vs. 9.69M). Among single-camera unfolding methods, SSR-9stg [38] (40.69 dB) and DAUHST-9stg [4] (38.36 dB) trail by 3.43 and 5.76 dB respectively. The end-to-end transformer MST-L [3] (35.18 dB) and the optimization-based GAP-Net [19] (33.26 dB) and DeSCI [17] (25.27 dB) are further behind. In the chromaticity domain (Table 3), the gap widens: CIDNet-9stg achieves 35.81 dB PSNR versus 28.03 dB for In2SET and 33.47 dB for SSR, confirming that the chromaticity-intensity decomposition yields a more faithful reflectance estimate than direct HSI reconstruction. On the real-world DCCHI dataset, CID-TV (the optimization-based variant) also visually surpasses PIDS [5] in spectral band fidelity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the deep unfolding paradigm for CASSI reconstruction (DAUHST, SSR, GAP-Net) by introducing a physically motivated chromaticity-intensity decomposition borrowed from RGB intrinsic image analysis and Retinex theory, which had not previously been applied to compressive spectral imaging. It departs from prior dual-camera CASSI methods (PIDS, In2SET) that directly reconstruct the full hyperspectral cube, instead reformulating the inverse problem around the lighting-invariant chromaticity with the intensity treated as a known mask. The asymmetric hybrid spatial-spectral Transformer (Swin-based spatial attention in the encoder, sparse TopK spectral attention in the decoder) builds on the windowed attention ideas of Swin Transformers and the spectral self-attention of SSR, but reorganizes them into an encoder-decoder split motivated by the distinct spatial-texture vs. spectral-sparsity structure of chromaticity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The chromaticity-intensity decomposition provides a physically interpretable, lighting-invariant reconstruction target that could enable more robust hyperspectral recovery across varying illumination conditions, and opens a path toward low-light enhancement and shadow removal in the spectral domain. A key limitation is the reliance on a dual-camera setup to obtain the intensity a priori; the authors note that a single-camera variant (training a separate intensity network first) is left as future work. The intensity-guided mask yields very large gains when applied to existing solvers (e.g., +12 dB for ADMM), suggesting that prior single-camera methods may be wasting significant capacity on recovering illumination structure that could be provided for free. Open questions include whether the decomposition generalizes to non-uniform illumination, whether the TopK spectral sparsity assumption holds for all material classes, and how the framework scales to higher spectral resolutions.
