## Problem

Existing rectified-flow (RF)-based inversion methods for text-guided image editing approximate the noisy latent at each timestep using the previous step's latent, causing accumulated drift that degrades reconstruction accuracy and editing fidelity. These methods inherit the iterative re-noising paradigm from diffusion models, overlooking RF's unique property that noisy latents are constructed via direct linear interpolation between Gaussian noise and the clean image, which opens the door to a fundamentally different inversion strategy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254147/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a source RGB image (encoded into the latent space of an RF model such as FLUX-dev or SD3.5-medium), a source text prompt describing the original image, and a target text prompt specifying the desired edit. It outputs a single edited RGB image that preserves the background and non-target regions of the source while modifying the specified regions according to the target prompt. The method is training-free and model-agnostic, requiring no architectural modifications to the underlying MM-DiT backbone, and operates with a fixed MVG coefficient η=0.8 across all editing tasks.

## Method

DNAEdit comprises two training-free components. First, Direct Noise Alignment (DNA) replaces the conventional iterative re-noising inversion: starting from a random Gaussian noise sample, it constructs each noisy latent via direct linear interpolation with the clean image, computes the predicted velocity field from the RF model, and corrects the noise by feeding back the difference between the expected linear-path velocity and the predicted velocity, iteratively shifting the noise from S_T to S_0 to converge on a structured noise that faithfully reconstructs the reference image. Second, Mobile Velocity Guidance (MVG) guides the re-denoising phase by computing the velocity difference between source- and target-conditioned fields, applying it to a 'mobile' reference image that evolves toward the target, and blending the resulting mobile velocity with the target-conditioned velocity via a weighting coefficient η to balance background fidelity against editability. A residual offset (Δx_DNA) computed during DNA is reused during re-denoising to ensure exact alignment of preserved regions, and the source velocity is cached from the DNA pass to reduce NFEs.

## Results

On PIE-Bench, DNAEdit (SD3.5) achieves the best overall rank (2.50), with the lowest structure distance (14.19), highest PSNR (26.66 dB), and lowest MSE (32.76) among all methods, while maintaining competitive CLIP similarity (Whole: 25.63, Edited: 22.71). On the proposed DNA-Bench (long-prompt), DNAEdit (SD3.5) again ranks first (2.08) with the highest CLIP Whole score (28.90) and CLIP Edited score (23.66). For reconstruction, DNA achieves the lowest MSE (0.010) and LPIPS (0.110) with only 56 NFEs, outperforming FireFlow (MSE 0.015, LPIPS 0.200) and RFEdit (MSE 0.022, LPIPS 0.244).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DNAEdit outperforms all 10 baselines on overall rank on PIE-Bench. The closest competitor is FTEdit (SD3.5, rank 3.50), which achieves a slightly lower structure distance (18.17 vs. 14.19 for DNAEdit-SD3.5) and higher SSIM (91.50 vs. 88.63), but DNAEdit wins on PSNR (26.66 vs. 26.62), MSE (32.76 vs. 40.24), and CLIP Edited (22.71 vs. 22.27). FlowEdit-SD3 achieves the highest CLIP Whole score (26.53) but suffers from weaker background preservation (PSNR 22.22 vs. 26.66) and over-saturation artifacts. Among RF-based methods, RFEdit (rank 6.42) and FireFlow (rank 7.42) both use higher-order ODE solvers but still accumulate drift, resulting in 1.5–2× higher MSE than DNAEdit. RF-Inversion (rank 12.42) and InsP2P (rank 13.42) perform the worst, with structure distances of 42.29 and 58.13 respectively, indicating severe reconstruction failure. On DNA-Bench (long prompts), DNAEdit-SD3.5 leads with the best CLIP scores (Whole 28.90, Edited 23.66) while RFEdit-FLUX ranks second (2.92) but with lower CLIP Edited (22.99 vs. 23.66).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Rectified Flow (RF) formulation (Liu et al., ICLR 2023) that models generation as a straight linear trajectory between Gaussian noise and clean images, but departs fundamentally from the iterative re-noising inversion paradigm inherited from diffusion models. Prior RF-based editing methods (RF-Inversion, RFEdit, FireFlow, FTEdit) still reverse the ODE step-by-step, approximating the unknown latent at each timestep and accumulating drift; DNAEdit instead exploits RF's linear interpolation property to directly refine the noise sample in the Gaussian domain by aligning predicted and expected velocity fields. The Mobile Velocity Guidance component extends the velocity-difference guidance idea from RF-Inversion by replacing a fixed reference image with a moving one that evolves during denoising, and the method is explicitly model-agnostic across MM-DiT architectures (FLUX, SD3, SD3.5) without requiring cross-attention injection or AdaLN manipulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The conceptual reframing—treating inversion as a noise-alignment problem rather than a trajectory-reversal problem—offers a cleaner and more efficient path to high-fidelity editing that avoids the NFE overhead of higher-order solvers or iterative averaging. The introduction of DNA-Bench, a long-prompt benchmark (avg. 33 words vs. 9.5 in PIE-Bench), addresses a real evaluation gap for RF-based models that handle detailed textual guidance better than diffusion models. However, as a training-free method, DNAEdit is bounded by the pretrained model's prior and may struggle with edits outside that distribution; open questions include whether the direct noise alignment principle extends to video, 3D, or multi-modal generation, and whether the modest performance margins over FTEdit widen at higher resolutions or with more aggressive editing tasks.
