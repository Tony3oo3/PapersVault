## Problem

Existing feedforward planar 3D reconstruction methods require expensive 3D plane annotations for training, limiting their zero-shot generalization, while per-scene optimization methods depend on accurately posed multi-view captures. This paper addresses the gap of achieving metric-scale, pose-free, zero-shot planar 3D reconstruction from unposed stereo pairs without any explicit plane-level supervision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255758/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes two unposed RGB images (512×384) with known camera intrinsics and produces, in a single forward pass, a sparse set of 3D planar primitives (each parameterized by depth, 2D radii, and a quaternion) at two resolutions (H/16×W/16 and H/8×W/8 per view), a 6-DoF relative camera pose in metric scale, and as side outputs, rendered depth/normal maps and instance-level 3D plane segmentation. Notably, the model does not perform per-pixel prediction; instead, a deconvolution network upsamples decoder features to generate primitives at the higher resolution, and a gradient-threshold heuristic selectively merges low- and high-resolution primitives to keep the total count as low as ~1400 per view.

## Method

PLANA3R uses a Siamese ViT encoder and cross-attention transformer decoder (initialized from DUSt3R weights) to jointly predict sparse planar primitives and relative pose. The key training innovation is a two-stage loss: a patch-level warm-up loss that stabilizes primitive depth and orientation, followed by a full-resolution rendering loss that backpropagates through PlanarSplatting's CUDA-based differentiable rasterizer to refine primitive radii and geometry against GT depth and normal maps—eliminating the need for plane annotations entirely. A Hierarchical Primitive Prediction Architecture (HPPA) predicts primitives at both H/16 and H/8 resolutions, then uses the gradient magnitude of predicted low-res normal patches to selectively activate high-res primitives only in geometrically complex regions, yielding a compact representation. The model is trained on ~4M metric-scale image pairs from four indoor datasets, preserving true metric scale in both depth and pose supervision to enable direct metric reconstruction at inference.

## Results

On ScanNetV2, PLANA3R achieves a Chamfer distance of 0.11 and F-score of 92.52 for two-view planar reconstruction, with median translation error of 0.07 m and median rotation error of 2.01° for relative pose estimation. On the unseen Matterport3D dataset, it attains Chamfer 0.32 and F-score 56.63, outperforming methods trained directly on that dataset. For zero-shot monocular depth on NYUv2, PLANA3R reports RMSE 0.463 and δ₁ 86.4%, surpassing MASt3R (RMSE 0.51, δ₁ 83.0%). On Replica, single-view plane segmentation yields RI 0.89, VOI 1.62, and SC 0.63, with plane recall at 0.6 m depth reaching 30.74% versus 13.46% for PlaneRecTR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PLANA3R outperforms all prior planar reconstruction baselines (SparsePlanes, PlaneFormers, NOPE-SAC) by large margins on both ScanNetV2 and Matterport3D in reconstruction quality (Chamfer, F-score) and pose accuracy. Against the dense-point-cloud foundation model MASt3R, PLANA3R wins decisively on ScanNetV2 (Chamfer 0.11 vs. 0.21; F-score 92.52 vs. 74.92; translation median 0.07 m vs. 0.11 m) and on Matterport3D reconstruction (Chamfer 0.32 vs. 0.49; F-score 56.63 vs. 30.01), though MASt3R retains a slight edge in rotation median on Matterport3D (0.98° vs. 2.00°). For zero-shot monocular depth on NYUv2, PLANA3R beats all five single-view plane methods (PlaneNet, PlaneAE, PlaneRCNN, PlaneTR, PlaneRecTR) on Rel, RMSE, and δ₁, and marginally surpasses MASt3R on Rel (0.132 vs. 0.152) and RMSE (0.463 vs. 0.51). On Replica single-view plane segmentation, PLANA3R improves over PlaneRecTR on all metrics (RI 0.89 vs. 0.85; VOI 1.62 vs. 1.81; SC 0.63 vs. 0.58) and dramatically on plane recall (e.g., 30.74% vs. 13.46% at 0.6 m depth), despite never using plane annotations during training.

## Relation to Prior Work

PLANA3R builds on two key lineages: the DUSt3R/MASt3R family of feed-forward stereo 3D vision models (adopting their Siamese ViT encoder + cross-attention decoder architecture and initializing from DUSt3R weights), and the PlanarSplatting differentiable renderer for planar primitive representation. The critical departure is from both: rather than predicting dense point clouds (DUSt3R, MASt3R), it predicts sparse 3D planar primitives; rather than requiring explicit plane-level annotations for supervision (SparsePlanes, PlaneFormers, NOPE-SAC), it uses only depth and normal maps via differentiable planar splatting, enabling training on ~4M pairs from large-scale datasets. The hierarchical primitive prediction architecture (HPPA), which uses normal-gradient thresholds to selectively combine low- and high-resolution primitives, is a novel architectural contribution not present in prior planar or point-based methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for plane annotations and camera poses, PLANA3R removes the two major bottlenecks that have constrained planar 3D reconstruction to small, carefully annotated datasets, opening the door to scalable training and zero-shot deployment in AR/VR and robotics. The finding that a compact set of ~1,400 planar primitives per view matches the quality of 3,072 primitives (and outperforms dense point clouds from MASt3R on indoor scenes) suggests structured representations are underexploited in feed-forward 3D vision. Key limitations include the restriction to indoor scenes, the pairwise (not single-pass) multi-view formulation, and the lack of reliable out-of-domain benchmarks for plane segmentation, which limits quantitative evaluation of the semantic side of the method.
