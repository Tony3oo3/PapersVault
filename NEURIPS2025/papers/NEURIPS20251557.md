## Problem

The sampled softmax, which replaces the full n-class denominator with a sum over k sampled classes, is known to be biased. However, it remained an open question whether some other function operating on the same k sampled logits could serve as an unbiased estimator of the full softmax. This paper proves that no such function exists, establishing a fundamental impossibility boundary for all sampled-estimator approaches.

## Input / Output

The analysis considers an arbitrary function s: R^{k+1} → R that receives one positive logit ŷ⁺ and k sampled negative logits (ŷ⁻_{i₁}, …, ŷ⁻_{i_k}), where the k indices are drawn without replacement from n negative classes. The key constraint (Assumption 3.2) is that the sampling distribution must be fixed before logits are computed, excluding distributions that depend on the logits themselves. The output is a proof that for every such s and every k < n, there exist logit configurations where E[s(·)] ≠ Softmax(·), i.e., unbiasedness is impossible.

## Method

The proof proceeds by contradiction after reformulating softmax estimation as estimating F(a) = 1/(1 + Σa_i) over positive reals. Using symmetrization to reduce the estimator to a symmetric function f_k, the authors evaluate the unbiasedness condition at logit vectors with at most two distinct values (b₁ repeated n−m times and b₂ repeated m times, for m = 0, …, k). Strong induction on m then forces f_k to take the specific form g(m) = Σ_{l=0}^{m} c(m,l)/(1+(n−l)b₁ + lb₂) with combinatorially defined coefficients c(m,l). A final bounding argument shows that choosing b₁ = 2(k+1)c*−1/(n−k) and b₂ = 1/n makes the upper bound on |g(k)| equal to 1/2, contradicting the required value g(k) = 1/(1+nb₂) = 1/2, thereby proving no unbiased estimator can exist.

## Results

This is a pure theory paper with no experiments or quantitative benchmarks. The main result (Theorem 3.1) proves that for any k < n, no function s: R^{k+1} → R can be an unbiased estimator of the full softmax when it has access to only k sampled negative logits. Corollary 3.3 extends this impossibility to any sampling distribution that is fixed before computing logits (Assumption 3.2), ruling out the possibility that a cleverly designed sampling scheme or alternative estimator could eliminate the bias.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper contains no experimental comparisons or baselines, as its contribution is a theoretical impossibility proof rather than a new method. The closest 'comparison' is with Blanc & Rendle (ICML 2018) [3], who demonstrated that the specific sampled softmax formula (Eq. 4) is biased but did not rule out the existence of some other unbiased function. This paper generalizes that finding: it shows that no function whatsoever—regardless of its form—can be unbiased when restricted to k < n logits, thereby closing the door on an entire class of potential solutions. The authors note that prior works (e.g., [3], [16]) that use sampling distributions dependent on the logits themselves (violating Assumption 3.2) are excluded from the theorem, as such distributions would require computing the full softmax to construct, defeating the purpose of sampling.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the result of Blanc & Rendle (ICML 2018), which demonstrated that the specific sampled softmax function is biased, to a universal impossibility statement: no function whatsoever operating on k < n sampled logits can be an unbiased estimator of the full softmax. Prior work on reducing sampled softmax bias (better sampling distributions, hard negative mining, moving averages) implicitly assumed no unbiased alternative existed but never formally justified this. The paper also positions itself relative to alternative approaches (hierarchical softmax, spherical softmax, problem reformulations) that avoid sampling entirely, noting those are outside its scope.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This impossibility theorem provides a definitive theoretical boundary that redirects research effort away from searching for unbiased sampled estimators and toward bias-minimization strategies or fundamentally different formulations (e.g., the reformulation approaches in [10, 11, 15]). The Assumption 3.2 (sampling distribution must not depend on logits) is the key constraint that makes the result meaningful; without it, trivially unbiased but computationally infeasible estimators exist. An open question is whether the impossibility extends to settings where the estimator has access to additional structure (e.g., pairwise similarities between classes) beyond individual logits, which could potentially circumvent the proof.
