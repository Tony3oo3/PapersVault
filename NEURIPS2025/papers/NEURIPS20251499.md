## Problem

Existing point cloud completion models perform static inference at test time, relying on fixed inductive biases learned from biased synthetic training data. This rigidity causes them to produce generic completions that follow training priors rather than adapting to the unique structural patterns and sensor-induced distortions of each input, particularly under novel occlusions. No prior work has applied meta-auxiliary test-time adaptation to point cloud completion.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251499/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a partial, unordered point cloud of M points and produces a complete point cloud of N > M points via a hierarchical encoder-decoder with coarse-to-fine refinement. At test time, the shared encoder is refined in-place through K=3 gradient steps on self-supervised auxiliary losses (stochastic masked reconstruction and Gaussian-noise artifact denoising), while the decoder remains frozen. The auxiliary branches generate pseudo-labels from the input itself—no ground truth is required during adaptation—making the approach applicable to real-world scans like KITTI where paired supervision is unavailable.

## Method

PointMAC introduces Bi-Aux Units (Stochastic Masked Reconstruction and Artifact Denoising) that share the primary encoder and a Token Synergy Integrator to produce self-supervised signals simulating structural incompleteness and sensor noise. A meta-auxiliary training strategy based on first-order MAML aligns these auxiliary adaptations with the primary completion objective: the inner loop adapts auxiliary branch parameters on a single sample, while the outer loop updates shared parameters via the primary Chamfer Distance loss. Adaptive λ-Calibration dynamically balances gradient contributions between the two auxiliary tasks through softmax-normalized, jointly-optimized weighting coefficients, preventing negative transfer. At inference, the model performs sample-specific test-time adaptation by minimizing the calibrated auxiliary loss on the shared encoder alone, enabling per-input refinement without any supervision or retraining.

## Results

On the PCN dataset, PointMAC achieves a CD-ℓ1 average of 6.33 (×1000), outperforming the strongest baseline CRA-PCN (6.39) and EINet (6.63). On ShapeNet-55, it attains CD-ℓ2-Avg of 0.83 and F1 of 0.490, surpassing CRA-PCN (0.85 / –) and ProxyFormer (0.93 / 0.483). On the simulated-scanning MVP benchmark, PointMAC records CD-ℓ2 of 5.24 (×10⁴) and F1 of 0.537, and on real-world KITTI LiDAR scans it achieves Fidelity of 0.135 and MMD of 0.477, the best among all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PointMAC achieves the best CD-Avg on every evaluated dataset, consistently outperforming the strongest recent baselines. On PCN, it beats CRA-PCN [19] by 0.06 in CD-ℓ1-Avg (6.33 vs 6.39) and EINet [39] by 0.30 (6.33 vs 6.63), with the largest margins over older methods like PoinTr [7] (8.38) and FoldingNet [18] (14.31). On ShapeNet-55, PointMAC improves CD-ℓ2-Avg from 0.85 (CRA-PCN) to 0.83 and F1 from 0.483 (ProxyFormer [8]) to 0.490; on the unseen 21 categories of ShapeNet-34 it leads with CD-Avg 1.22 vs 1.24 (CRA-PCN). On the simulated-scanning MVP benchmark, PointMAC edges out CRA-PCN [19] with CD-ℓ2 5.24 vs 5.33 and F1 0.537 vs 0.529. On real-world KITTI LiDAR (no ground truth), PointMAC reduces Fidelity to 0.135 (vs 0.151 for SeedFormer [6]) and MMD to 0.477 (vs 0.508 for ProxyFormer [8]), demonstrating that the meta-auxiliary test-time adaptation generalizes to sparse, noisy real scans without paired supervision.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the test-time adaptation (TTA) paradigm—previously applied to image deblurring, optical flow, and point cloud upsampling/registration—into the point cloud completion domain, which had been exclusively addressed via static encoder-decoder inference. Architecturally, it builds on the coarse-to-fine decoder from CRA-PCN [19], the SpatialRefiner from Dis-PU [35], and the dual-masked self-attention from PointGPT [34], while its core training procedure is a direct adaptation of MAML [17] to a multi-task setting with self-supervised auxiliary objectives. The key departure from prior TTA work is the use of meta-learning to align auxiliary gradient updates with the primary completion loss, addressing the negative-transfer problem that plagues naive auxiliary-loss optimization at test time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that a few self-supervised gradient steps at inference can meaningfully improve completion quality without any ground-truth supervision, this work opens a path toward deployment in safety-critical 3D perception (robotics, AR, autonomous driving) where test-time labels are unavailable and sensor conditions vary. The framework is currently limited to single-object completion and a small number of adaptation steps (K=3), and the per-sample optimization introduces non-trivial inference latency that the paper does not fully quantify. Open questions include whether the meta-auxiliary TTA strategy transfers to scene-level completion, whether more adaptation steps yield diminishing returns, and how the approach behaves under severe domain shifts (e.g., indoor-to-outdoor) beyond the cross-dataset KITTI evaluation.
