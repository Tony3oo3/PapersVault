## Problem

RL-based quantum architecture search (QAS) suffers from severe scalability bottlenecks: the action space grows exponentially with qubit count, and each episode demands costly quantum circuit simulations, restricting prior methods to at most 8-qubit noiseless and 4-qubit noisy systems. No existing framework efficiently combines the expressivity of RL-driven circuit discovery with the compactness and physical insight of tensor network methods to push QAS beyond these limits.

## Input / Output

The method takes a molecular quantum chemistry Hamiltonian (e.g., 6-BEH2, 8-H2O, 10-H2O, 12-LiH) and a bond dimension parameter χ (typically 2) that governs the entanglement capacity of the tensor network approximation. It produces a hardware-native parameterized quantum circuit in the {RX, RY, RZ, CNOT} gate set with a brickwork structure, along with the corresponding ground-state energy estimate. The output circuit is characterized by its depth, CNOT count, and rotation count, with the fixed TN-init variant reporting only the gates added by the RL agent (the warm-start circuit is retained as a statevector and not counted).

## Method

TensorRL-QAS is a three-stage pipeline: (1) DMRG finds a matrix product state (MPS) approximation of the Hamiltonian's ground state at a chosen bond dimension χ; (2) the MPS is mapped to a shallow brickwork quantum circuit via Riemannian optimization on the Stiefel manifold, using a Cayley-transform-based adaptation of Adam to preserve unitarity of the 2-qubit gates while maximizing state overlap; (3) a DDQN-based RL agent then sequentially appends gates from the {RX, RY, RZ, CNOT} pool to the warm-started circuit, guided by a VQE energy-based reward. Two variants are studied: TensorRL (trainable TN-init), where the MPS circuit structure and parameters are encoded in the RL state and jointly optimized, and TensorRL (fixed TN-init), where the MPS is used as a fixed statevector initialization that is invisible to the agent, reducing the binary-encoding tensor size and cutting per-episode simulation time by up to 98%. The key insight is that the TN warm-start narrows the RL search to physically meaningful circuits, reducing classical optimizer function evaluations by up to 100× and enabling chemical accuracy at 10-qubit with 50% success probability versus <1% for baselines.

## Results

On molecular ground-state problems from 6 to 12 qubits, TensorRL (fixed TN-init) consistently produces the most compact circuits: for 8-H₂O it achieves 8.9×10⁻⁴ energy error with only 6 depth and 9 CNOTs (vs. 75–100 depth and 85–117 CNOTs for CRLQAS/Vanilla RL), and for 12-LiH the trainable variant reaches 1.0×10⁻² error with 37 CNOTs (vs. 321–390 for Vanilla RL/SA-QAS). TensorRL reduces classical optimizer function evaluations by up to 100× and per-episode execution time by up to 98% compared to CRLQAS. Under depolarizing noise on 8-H₂O, TensorRL (fixed) achieves 100% success rate at chemical accuracy with depth 7 and 5 CNOTs, versus 30% success for CRLQAS at depth 22 and 11 CNOTs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TensorRL (fixed TN-init) dominates all baselines on circuit compactness: for 8-H₂O it uses 9 CNOTs and depth 6 versus 85–117 CNOTs and depth 75–100 for CRLQAS and Vanilla RL, while maintaining competitive error (8.9×10⁻⁴ vs. 1.7×10⁻⁴). Against SA-QAS and RA-QAS, TensorRL wins on both accuracy and compactness across all four molecules (e.g., 12-LiH: 1.0×10⁻² error with 37 CNOTs vs. 2.5×10⁻² with 390 CNOTs for SA-QAS). Versus quantumDARTS on 8-H₂O, TensorRL (fixed) uses 9 CNOTs and depth 6 compared to 68 CNOTs and depth 64, with lower error. Against TF-QAS on 6-BEH₂, TensorRL achieves 6.8×10⁻⁵ error (100× better than TF-QAS's 1.8×10⁻³) in 1.2 h training time versus 2.5 h. Under depolarizing noise, TensorRL (fixed) reaches 100% success at chemical accuracy with half the CNOTs of CRLQAS (5 vs. 11), while CRLQAS achieves only 30% success. The trainable TN-init variant matches or slightly exceeds StructureRL in final accuracy (e.g., 6.0×10⁻⁵ vs. 5.9×10⁻⁵ on 6-BEH₂) but converges faster, confirming that the MPS warm-start accelerates rather than replaces the RL search.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the tensor-network pre-training idea of Rudolph et al. [12] by embedding the MPS warm-start directly into an RL-based quantum architecture search loop, rather than appending a fixed PQC structure and optimizing classically. The RL framework (DDQN agent, binary circuit encoding, reward function) is inherited from Ostaszewski et al. [21] and Patel et al. [35], while the MPS-to-circuit mapping uses Riemannian optimization on the Stiefel manifold following Luchnikov et al. [15]. The key conceptual departure from [12] is that the RL agent dynamically explores which additional gates to add, balancing expressivity against circuit compactness, rather than committing to a predetermined ansatz topology.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 100× reduction in classical optimizer function evaluations and 98% per-episode speedup make RL-based QAS practical for 12-qubit systems on CPU, a regime where prior methods were intractable. However, the gate set is limited to {RX, RY, RZ, CNOT} with a fixed brickwork topology, and no real-hardware training was performed (only amplified-noise simulation and post-hoc IBMQ-Brisbane evaluation). Open questions include whether richer action spaces (Hamiltonian-inspired gates, gadgets) and alternative tensor network layouts can push beyond 12-qubit, and whether the fixed-vs-trainable TN-init distinction can be refined into intermediate variants that capture the best of both.
