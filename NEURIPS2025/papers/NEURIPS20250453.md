## Problem

Existing streaming video LLMs and benchmarks fail to jointly support proactive coherence (answering questions that depend on future visual evidence), just-in-time responsiveness (responding at the earliest reliable moment without over- or under-responding), and synchronized efficiency (maintaining real-time perception while generating answers). Current online models are confined to narrow single-turn tasks, exhibit poor timing behavior, and lack the capacity for continuous multi-turn understanding with temporal precision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250453/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a continuous ego-streaming video (first-person frames arriving at a fixed frame rate) alongside a sequence of text queries that emerge at specific timestamps. At each timestep, the model outputs an action from a three-way set {silence, response, ask_high}; the ask_high action triggers acquisition of a high-resolution frame for fine-grained scrutiny before committing to a response. When responding, the model generates an open-ended textual answer, and the entire perception-reasoning loop must operate with zero latency so that new visual input is never missed during answer generation.

## Method

VideoLLM-EyeWO builds on the VideoLLM-Online (LIVE) architecture (LLaMA3 + SigLIP, LoRA fine-tuning) and introduces three key components. First, a data engine generates 60K single-turn and 20K multi-turn QA pairs via a three-stage pipeline: LVLM captioning to extract initial QA with one answer interval, RAG-based expansion into multiple valid intervals, and compositional multi-turn question assembly. Second, a multi-stage training strategy progressively learns: Stage-1 applies a weighted response supervision (linear decrease function over the valid interval) to resolve training conflicts from visually similar adjacent frames; Stage-2 introduces the ask_high action with a dedicated loss so the model proactively requests high-resolution frames at uncertain timestamps to pinpoint both the correct response moment and accurate answer; Stage-3 fine-tunes on multi-turn questions for contextual coherence. Third, a Proactive Dynamic Compression mechanism inserts a special compression token (<ct>, initialized from the EOS embedding) after response segments, leveraging causal self-attention to compress preceding visual content into a single KV-cache entry, achieving roughly one-tenth the original token usage while preserving high-resolution frames at lower compression rates.

## Results

On ESTP-Bench, VideoLLM-EyeWO achieves an overall ESTP-F1 of 34.7, substantially outperforming the best offline MLLM baseline (MiniCPM-V with Polling Strategy, 22.9) by +11.8% and the best online MLLM baseline (LIVE at threshold=0.9, 15.5) by +19.2%. On OVO-Bench (online task), the model scores 36.20 vs. 29.80 for VideoLLM-Online, and on the COIN benchmark it reaches 55.5 Proc+ accuracy vs. 53.6 for VideoLLM-online-8B-v1+. The model also demonstrates strong contextual question performance (43.6 ESTP-F1), where all baselines score below 27.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VideoLLM-EyeWO outperforms all 12 baselines across every evaluation setting. Among offline MLLMs, MiniCPM-V (Polling, 22.9) and Qwen2-VL (Polling, 21.3) are the strongest, yet both trail the proposed model by over 11 points in overall ESTP-F1; notably, all offline models score below 27 on contextual questions where EyeWO reaches 43.6. VLM-based detectors (CLIP 10.1, LaViLa 14.3, EgoVLP 15.5) are limited by their inability to generate open-ended answers (reply score fixed at 0), capping their overall scores well below 16. Among online MLLMs, MMDuet (17.8) achieves the highest implicit proactive score (34.2) but suffers from low precision (high false positives), while LIVE at threshold=0.9 (15.5) shows the best recall-precision balance among baselines yet still lags EyeWO by 19.2 points overall. On the COIN benchmark, EyeWO (LLaMA3.1) improves over VideoLLM-online-8B-v1+ by +1.9 on Step, +2.5 on Next, and +1.9 on Proc+, confirming architectural generalizability beyond the ESTP task.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the VideoLLM-Online (LIVE) framework [5] by expanding its binary action space (silence/response) to a three-action space that includes a proactive high-resolution frame request, and by replacing single-stage binary supervision with a three-stage curriculum (passive interval responsiveness → proactive just-in-time accuracy → multi-turn coherence). It departs from prior streaming benchmarks (VStream, StreamingBench, OVO-Bench, MMDuet) by unifying three previously disconnected evaluation axes—proactive coherence, just-in-time responsiveness, and synchronized efficiency—into a single task formulation specific to egocentric video. The proactive dynamic compression mechanism builds on KV-cache compression ideas from Video-XL [43] and related work but introduces response-likelihood-driven, model-decided compression timing rather than fixed schedules.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ESTP-Bench and ESTP-F1 metric fill a genuine gap: no prior benchmark simultaneously evaluates whether a streaming model answers correctly, answers at the right time, and does so without sacrificing perception speed. The finding that contextual (multi-turn) questions cause a sharp performance drop even when temporal grounding is easy suggests that dialogue-history tracking is a distinct and underexplored bottleneck for streaming video LLMs. Limitations include the relatively small benchmark (2,264 questions from Ego4D validation), low absolute performance (34.7 ESTP-F1), and evaluation restricted to a single egocentric dataset, leaving open whether the multi-stage training and compression techniques transfer to longer videos, third-person settings, or real-time deployment at scale.
