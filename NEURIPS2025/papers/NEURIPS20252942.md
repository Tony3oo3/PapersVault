## Problem

Classical tensor decomposition for CNN compression minimizes the Frobenius norm of the weight-space error, which ignores how the actual data distribution shapes each layer's output. This mismatch between the optimization criterion and the network's functional behavior causes significant accuracy drops that typically require costly post-compression fine-tuning on the original training data. The paper asks whether a data-informed, function-space norm can guide tensor decomposition to preserve layer behavior without fine-tuning.

## Input / Output

The method consumes the kernel tensors of a trained CNN (ResNet-18/50, GoogLeNet, or AlexNet) along with a dataset used to estimate the input covariance matrix Σ for each layer via forward passes; the first convolutional layer is always left uncompressed. The output is a compressed network where each target convolutional layer is replaced by a sequence of smaller 1×1 and spatial convolutions parameterized by CP or Tucker-2 factor matrices. Notably, the dataset used to compute Σ need not be the original training set, enabling compression when training data is unavailable.

## Method

The paper derives a distribution-aware (Sigma) norm ‖(K − K̃)₍₁₎ Σ^{1/2}‖_F that measures the L² functional error of a convolutional layer under the input data distribution, proving it equals the expected output difference (Proposition 1). It then designs two new Alternating Least Squares algorithms—CP-ALS-Sigma and Tucker2-ALS-Sigma—that directly minimize this norm by incorporating Σ^{1/2} into the closed-form least-squares updates for each factor matrix, solved via MINRES for numerical stability. Rank selection uses VBMF with a tunable ratio α. The key practical advantage is that the Sigma norm is transferable: covariance statistics estimated from a related dataset (e.g., FGVC-Aircraft or CIFAR-100) yield compressed models with accuracy close to that obtained using the original training data, often matching or exceeding fine-tuned Frobenius-norm baselines without any additional training.

## Results

On ResNet18 (ImageNet), Tucker2-ALS-Sigma achieves 66.8% top-1 accuracy at 1.4× compression, compared to 58.9% for standard Tucker2-ALS and 54.9% for the activation-guided baseline (AG-LRA). On GoogLeNet (ImageNet), the method reaches 66.3% at 1.24× compression versus 60.2% for Tucker2-ALS. For CP decomposition on ResNet18, CP-ALS-Sigma attains 67.9% top-1 accuracy at 2.07× compression, dramatically outperforming greedy tensor deflation (35.2%) under the same Sigma norm. The advantage grows with compression rate: at 1.77× on ResNet18, Tucker2-ALS-Sigma retains 63.3% while Tucker2-ALS drops to 30.2%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two primary tensor-decomposition baselines: standard Tucker2-ALS (Frobenius-norm Tucker decomposition, Kim et al. 2016) and standard CP-ALS (Frobenius-norm CP decomposition, Lebedev et al. 2015). Tucker2-ALS-Sigma consistently outperforms Tucker2-ALS by 7–33 percentage points across all tested architectures and compression rates (e.g., 66.8% vs. 58.9% at 1.4× on ResNet18; 66.3% vs. 60.2% at 1.24× on GoogLeNet), and the gap widens sharply at higher compression ratios. CP-ALS-Sigma similarly dominates CP-ALS and greedy tensor deflation (67.9% vs. 35.2% at 2.07× on ResNet18). Two additional data-aware baselines—Fisher-weighted (FW-LRA) and activation-guided (AG-LRA) low-rank approximation—also lose to the proposed method at every compression rate, with FW-LRA degrading most severely (e.g., 4.2% vs. 63.3% at 1.77× on ResNet18). Notably, Tucker2-ALS-Sigma matches or exceeds the accuracy of fine-tuned Tucker2-ALS models without any post-compression fine-tuning, and the Sigma matrix computed from a different dataset (e.g., CIFAR-100 or FGVC-Aircraft) retains most of the benefit, confirming transferability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the data covariance norm introduced by Denton et al. (2014) for SVD-based compression to the tensor decomposition setting (CP and Tucker-2), where prior work (Lebedev et al. 2015; Kim et al. 2016) has exclusively minimized the Frobenius norm in weight space. The key methodological contribution is deriving new Alternating Least Squares algorithms (CP-ALS-Sigma, Tucker2-ALS-Sigma) that directly optimize the distribution-aware Sigma norm rather than the isotropic Frobenius norm, with VBMF (Astrid et al. 2018) retained for rank selection. The paper also positions itself against data-free compression literature (zero-shot quantization, data-free pruning) by being the first to address data-free high-order tensor approximation via cross-dataset covariance transfer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: the method often eliminates the need for post-compression fine-tuning, which is a major bottleneck in deployment pipelines and requires access to the original training data. The transferability of the Sigma matrix across datasets (e.g., using FGVC-Aircraft statistics to compress ImageNet models) opens a path to compressing proprietary or privacy-restricted models without their training data. Key limitations include the functional norm only accounting for the upstream network (not downstream layers), the VBMF rank heuristic assuming isotropic noise, and the absence of a theoretical performance guarantee for the compressed network. Open questions include whether a complete functional norm incorporating downstream layers would further improve results, and whether anisotropic VBMF could yield better rank selection.
