## Problem

Existing fair federated learning methods suffer from two overlooked issues: (1) full-parameter aggregation introduces conflicts between redundant and critical parameter updates, degrading global model performance, and (2) persistent protection of weak clients throughout the entire training cycle skews the optimization trajectory, causing both lower accuracy and unfairness. The paper asks whether fairness can be promoted while simultaneously improving the global model rather than trading one for the other.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250630/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

Clients process local image batches (28×28 grayscale for Fashion-MNIST/Digits, 32×32 RGB for CIFAR-10/100, variable for Office-Caltech) with corresponding class labels, and send parameter update vectors (Δw) to the server rather than raw data. The server outputs an updated global model (a 2-layer CNN for single-domain or ResNet-10 with 512-dim features for cross-domain) that maps images to class labels. The method operates entirely at the aggregation step, requiring all clients to share identical network architecture.

## Method

FedPW modifies the server-side aggregation with two coupled modules. Parameter Adjustment first applies a client-specific binary mask that discards the smallest-magnitude parameter updates (mask rate inversely proportional to each client's smoothed loss), then rescales the most cross-client-consistent parameters (lowest standard deviation) by a factor that restores the original gradient magnitude, reducing inter-domain conflicts while preserving critical updates. Adaptive Weighted Aggregation computes two per-client weight sequences—gradient-alignment scores (dot products with other clients' updates) for generalization and loss-proportional scores for fairness—each with momentum smoothing, then blends them using a ratio determined by the relative standard deviations of the two weight distributions, automatically shifting from consensus-driven weighting in early rounds to fairness-driven weighting in later rounds.

## Results

On single-domain benchmarks, FedPW achieves the best average accuracy across all three datasets: 88.2% (σ=7.1) on Fashion-MNIST Dir(0.1), 75.3% (σ=11.1) on CIFAR-10 Dir(0.1), and 41.2% (σ=6.6) on CIFAR-100 Dir(0.1), while simultaneously attaining the lowest standard deviation among all compared methods. On cross-domain Digits, FedPW reaches 79.52% average accuracy with σ=21.00, improving over FedAvg (75.85%, σ=24.67) by +3.67 in accuracy and −3.67 in variance. On Office-Caltech, FedPW achieves 63.24% (σ=4.83) versus FedAvg's 58.60% (σ=10.85), a +4.64 accuracy gain and −6.02 variance reduction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedPW outperforms all 12 baselines on average accuracy across every single-domain setting (Table 1) and every cross-domain setting (Table 2). On CIFAR-10 Dir(0.1), FedPW (75.3%) surpasses the strongest baseline FedAA (73.8%) by 1.5 points and FedAvg (68.1%) by 7.2 points, while also reducing the standard deviation from 15.0 to 11.1. On the cross-domain Digits benchmark, FedPW integrated with FedAvg achieves 79.52% average accuracy (σ=21.00), beating FedAvg alone (75.85%, σ=24.67) and the next-best +FedHEAL variant (78.18%, σ=22.29); on Office-Caltech, FedPW+FedAvg reaches 63.24% (σ=4.83) versus FedAvg's 58.60% (σ=10.85). Notably, fairness-oriented baselines AFL and q-FFL degrade global accuracy relative to FedAvg on CIFAR-10 (64.3% and 67.8% vs. 68.1%) and on SVHN in Digits (75.42% and 77.93% vs. 79.13%), confirming the paper's claim that persistent protection of weak clients harms the global model. FedPW also improves the weakest domain (SYN in Digits: 49.43% vs. 40.02% for FedAvg; Webcam in Office-Caltech: 58.62% vs. 45.92%), demonstrating that its adaptive weighting lifts disadvantaged clients without sacrificing overall performance. The compatibility study shows FedPW consistently improves FedProx, MOON, and FedDyn as well, with the largest gains on Office-Caltech (+6.27 over FedProx, +2.97 over MOON).

## Relation to Prior Work

FedPW builds directly on the FedAvg aggregation framework and positions itself at the intersection of two well-studied FL sub-areas: heterogeneous-data handling (FedProx, MOON, SCAFFOLD, FedDyn) and performance fairness (AFL, q-FFL, FedHEAL, Ditto, FedFV, FedAA, FedGCR). Its key conceptual departure is twofold: (1) it argues that full-parameter aggregation introduces redundant-update conflicts that existing fairness methods ignore, and (2) it challenges the prevailing assumption that weak clients should be persistently protected throughout training, instead proposing a phase-adaptive shift from consensus-driven to fairness-driven weighting. Unlike prior fairness methods that modify only aggregation weights or client selection, FedPW operates at the parameter level (masking and rescaling) in addition to the client level (adaptive weights).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a practical, plug-in-compatible technique for FL practitioners who need to balance global-model quality with cross-client fairness, and its phase-adaptive weighting principle (consensus early, fairness later) provides a reusable design heuristic for future aggregation strategies. However, the method requires all clients to share an identical network architecture, limiting applicability in heterogeneous-architecture settings, and the hyperparameter c governing mask ratio is sensitive to tuning. Open questions include whether the two-phase training dynamics assumption generalizes to deeper architectures, larger client populations, or non-convex objectives beyond image classification, and whether the parameter-masking step remains beneficial as model capacity grows.
