## Problem

All existing online RLHF algorithms, whether using passive or active exploration, suffer from a sample complexity that scales exponentially with the reward range R_max (i.e., O(exp(R_max)/ε²)), making their guarantees vacuous in practical settings where one response can clearly dominate another. This exponential dependency arises because the Bradley-Terry preference signal becomes uninformative when the reward gap between two responses is large, requiring exponentially many samples to distinguish quality. The paper answers the open question raised by Xie et al. [2024] by showing that this exponential scaling is not inherent to the problem but is an artifact of prior algorithmic design.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes a reference SFT policy π_ref, a prompt distribution ρ, and binary preference labels (win/lose) generated by a Bradley-Terry oracle over pairs of responses. It operates in K = ⌈R_max⌉ intervals, each running T iterations of the POPO subroutine, where the second response in each pair is sampled from a self-updating sampler π_sam rather than the fixed reference policy. The output is a single aligned policy π̄ that achieves ε-optimal suboptimality gap with Õ(d·R_max^8·log|ℛ|/δ / ε²) total preference queries, replacing the prior O(exp(R_max)/ε²) bound.

## Method

SE-POPO introduces two key mechanisms to eliminate the exp(R_max) dependency. First, it replaces the reward-based optimistic exploration bonus used in prior work (XPO, VPO, SELM) with a preference-based bonus G(π) that directly measures the expected win rate of the current policy over the sampler, optimizing preference regret rather than reward regret. Second, it employs a self-updating sampler: across K intervals, the output policy of each POPO run becomes the sampler for the next, gradually closing the reward gap between the two sampled responses so that the critical condition Δ(x,y,y') ≤ 1 holds with high probability. The theoretical backbone is a Preference-to-Reward reduction lemma showing that the exponential blow-up in the sigmoid derivative only activates when the reward gap exceeds 1, so a sufficiently good sampler converts the preference regret bound into a polynomial reward regret bound. In practice, the bilevel optimization reduces to a single-level DPO-like loss (equation 12) with an additional exploration term that only requires sampling from the fixed sampler, making it a one-line modification to existing iterative DPO pipelines.

## Results

On the 3-iteration online RLHF setting with Llama-3-8B-SFT, SE-POPO-iter3 achieves 73.3% win rate and -2.03 average reward on IID data, 92.4% win rate and +0.61 average reward on Alpaca data, 40.12 on AlpacaEval 2.0 LC, and 8.39 on MT-Bench. These results surpass the industry-level Llama-3-8B-Instruct (22.92 AE2 LC, 8.16 MT-Bench) and approach Llama-3-405B-Instruct (39.30 AE2 LC), a model with two orders of magnitude more parameters. Across 9 academic benchmarks, SE-POPO performs best in 4 tasks, DPO in 3, and XPO in 2.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SE-POPO outperforms both baselines across all evaluation settings. Against iterative DPO (passive exploration), SE-POPO-iter3 leads by 0.9 pp in IID win rate (73.3% vs. 72.4%), 1.1 pp in Alpaca win rate (92.4% vs. 91.3%), 4.02 points on AE2 LC (40.12 vs. 36.10), and 0.11 on MT-Bench (8.39 vs. 8.28). Against XPO (active exploration), the margins are smaller but consistent: +0.3 pp IID WR, +0.6 pp Alpaca WR, +1.89 AE2 LC, and +0.18 MT-Bench. Notably, SE-POPO's advantage over DPO is most pronounced on the out-of-distribution Alpaca setting and public benchmarks, suggesting the preference-based exploration bonus is particularly effective when the prompt distribution shifts. The ablation study further confirms that the self-updating sampler mechanism (using π_t for both responses rather than π_ref) is critical: fixing the second sampler to π_ref inflates the mean reward gap from 2.90 to 7.14 at iteration 3, directly manifesting the exp(R_max) pathology the paper aims to eliminate.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of optimistic online RLHF algorithms (XPO, VPO, SELM) that augment the DPO loss with a reward-based exploration bonus to encourage out-of-distribution exploration. The key departure is replacing the reward-based bonus with a preference-based bonus and introducing a self-updating sampler that iteratively improves the second response distribution, rather than fixing it at the reference policy. The implementation builds directly on the iterative DPO codebase from Dong et al. (2024), requiring only a one-line modification to the loss function. The theoretical framework inherits the linear reward oracle and KL-regularized objective from prior works but introduces a novel preference-to-reward reduction lemma that is the core of the polynomial scaling guarantee.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first online RLHF algorithm to break the exp(R_max) sample complexity barrier, resolving an open question explicitly raised by Xie et al. (2024) who conjectured the exponential dependence might be unavoidable. The practical significance is that in real LLM alignment, reward gaps can be large (e.g., objectively correct vs. incorrect answers), making prior guarantees vacuous; SE-POPO provides meaningful guarantees in this regime. Limitations include restriction to the Bradley-Terry model (non-monotonic preferences are excluded), a noted length-exploitation artifact in the simplified objective (12), and a gap between theoretical preference for small β and empirical instability at very small β. Open directions include extending to token-level MDPs, multi-turn settings, and general preference models.
