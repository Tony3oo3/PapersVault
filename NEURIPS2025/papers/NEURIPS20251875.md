## Problem

Existing medical vision-language models underutilize the rich semantics of radiology reports by aligning at the word or token level rather than at semantically meaningful sentence units, and they rely on attention maps for explainability that only indicate where the model focuses without explaining why, since softmax activation forces non-zero values even for irrelevant regions. This limits both the quality of vision-language alignment and the interpretability needed for clinical adoption.

## Input / Output

The model takes a chest X-ray image (processed at 518×518, yielding 37×37 = 1369 patches via a 14×14 patch size) and a text prompt formatted as a finding-sentence (e.g., 'There is [finding] of [location]'). At inference, it produces a scalar similarity probability (sigmoid of the global logit) for classification, and a pixel-level VL similarity map obtained by reshaping patch-level cosine similarities into a 37×37 grid, bilinearly interpolating to original resolution, and applying element-wise sigmoid. This map supports grounding (argmax localization), segmentation (thresholding), and open-vocabulary semantic segmentation (single fixed threshold, e.g., 0.7 for findings, 0.4 for anatomical regions).

## Method

RadZero's core mechanism, VL-CABS, replaces learned cross-attention with direct cosine similarity between ℓ2-normalized text embeddings and individual image patch embeddings, producing similarity scores bounded in [-1, 1] and centered at zero. The architecture freezes a pre-trained XrayDINOv2 vision encoder and appends two trainable 768-dim Transformer layers, while a fine-tuned MPNet (Sentence-BERT) encodes each finding-sentence extracted from radiology reports by Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct. Training uses a multi-positive NCE (MP-NCE) loss that independently evaluates all N finding-sentences per image against all other images in the batch, combined with a symmetric InfoNCE loss, on 352,875 MIMIC-CXR images. Because the patch-level similarity map is derived directly from cosine similarity without softmax normalization, unrelated image-text pairs yield near-zero values, enabling interpretable visualizations and single-threshold open-vocabulary segmentation without additional supervision.

## Results

On zero-shot classification (AUROC), RadZero achieves 0.847 on Open-I, 0.841 on PadChest, and 0.871 on PadChest20, setting new state-of-the-art on all three. For zero-shot grounding (pointing game accuracy), RadZero reaches 0.622 mean on ChestXDet10 and 0.844 on MS-CXR phrase grounding, outperforming the previous best (CARZero) by 0.079 and 0.095 respectively. On zero-shot segmentation (Dice), RadZero scores 0.171 on SIIM pneumothorax (vs. 0.100 for CARZero) and 0.546 on RSNA pneumonia, while also achieving the highest pixel-wise AUC of 0.947 on SIIM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest competitor CARZero [19], RadZero wins on Open-I (+0.9), PadChest (+3.1), PadChest20 (+3.4), and RSNA (+8.7) in classification, ties on SIIM (0.924), but trails on ChestXray14 (−0.7), CheXpert (−2.3), and ChestXDet10 (−0.9). In grounding, RadZero surpasses CARZero by 0.079 mean pointing-game accuracy on ChestXDet10 and by 0.095 on MS-CXR phrase grounding. For segmentation, RadZero improves SIIM Dice from 0.100 to 0.171 (+71% relative) and RSNA Dice from 0.540 to 0.546, while also achieving a substantially higher Pix-AUC (0.947 vs. 0.856). Against earlier methods, RadZero dominates GLoRIA [12] and BioViL-T [1] across all reported metrics by wide margins (e.g., +0.145 AUROC on Open-I over GLoRIA). MedKLIP [37] remains competitive on CheXpert (0.879 vs. 0.900) and RSNA classification (0.869 vs. 0.834), while KAD [42] is outperformed on every shared dataset. The ablation in Table 5 further shows that VL-CABS with cosine similarity and two Transformer layers outperforms the cross-attention decoder used in CARZero on most metrics even without multi-positive training.

## Relation to Prior Work

RadZero sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) the CLIP/LiT paradigm of contrastive vision-language alignment with a frozen pre-trained vision encoder, (2) the dino.txt extension that adds lightweight trainable Transformer layers to enable patch-level text-image similarity, and (3) the UniCLIP multi-positive NCE loss for handling multiple text descriptions per image. Its key departure from prior medical VLMs (CARZero, MedKLIP, KAD) is replacing learned cross-attention with softmax by direct cosine similarity between text embeddings and local patch features (VL-CABS), which yields well-defined, zero-centered similarity scores rather than normalized attention weights. This design choice is what enables the paper's explainability claims and its open-vocabulary segmentation via simple thresholding, neither of which is achievable with standard attention maps.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine gap in medical VLM interpretability: attention maps show where a model looks but not why, and their softmax-normalized values cannot be compared across different image-text pairs. VL-CABS provides a principled fix by making the similarity score itself the explanation, which is a meaningful step toward clinically deployable explainability. However, the work is validated only on chest X-rays, the absolute segmentation performance remains low (Dice ~0.17 on SIIM), and the reliance on a pre-trained XrayDINOv2 encoder limits domain transfer. Open questions include whether the cosine-similarity-based approach generalizes to 3D modalities (CT, MRI), whether the open-vocabulary segmentation capability scales to richer anatomical vocabularies, and whether the LLM-based finding-sentence extraction bottleneck can be removed for real-time clinical use.
