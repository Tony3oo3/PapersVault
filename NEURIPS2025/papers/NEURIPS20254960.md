## Problem

Meta-World, the de facto benchmark for multi-task and meta-reinforcement learning, has undergone undocumented reward function changes (V1→V2) that silently altered per-task reward scales by over two orders of magnitude, rendering cross-paper algorithm comparisons invalid. This paper quantifies the resulting inconsistencies across published results and releases a standardized, fully reproducible version of the benchmark with explicit reward versioning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254960/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The agent consumes a 39-dimensional observation vector (end-effector XYZ, gripper openness, two object positions and quaternions, goal XYZ, stacked with the previous timestep) plus a one-hot task descriptor, and produces a 4-dimensional continuous action (3D end-effector displacement and gripper finger position). In multi-task RL the goal location is visible in the observation, whereas in meta-RL it is zeroed out to test generalization. Evaluation reports mean success rate over 50 episodes per task (multi-task) or 3 episodes per held-out task after 10 adaptation episodes (meta-RL), aggregated with interquartile mean over 10 seeds.

## Method

The paper re-engineers Meta-World around the Gymnasium API and MuJoCo Python bindings, eliminating dependencies on the deprecated OpenAI gym and Mujoco-Py packages, and exposes both V1 (geometric-primitive-based, reward scale up to ~1200) and V2 (fuzzy-logic constraint-based via Hamacher product, reward scale 0.1–10) reward functions as selectable options through a unified gym.make interface rather than introducing a third reward set. Two new intermediate task sets (MT25, ML25) are added, and users can construct arbitrary custom multi-task or meta-RL task sets of any size. Empirically, the paper shows V2 rewards consistently improve multi-task RL success rates (e.g., MTMHSAC on MT10: 46.9→77.3) because their similar per-task scales facilitate Q-function learning, while meta-RL methods (MAML, RL²) are less sensitive due to their policy-gradient baselines that normalize advantage magnitudes. The evaluation protocol follows Agarwal et al. (2021) with 10 random seeds and interquartile mean reporting with 95% confidence intervals.

## Results

On MT10, V2 rewards consistently yield higher mean success rates than V1 across all multi-task RL methods: PCGrad improves from 70.5% to 85.5%, SM from 71.4% to 84.9%, MOORE from 61.4% to 83.4%, and PaCo from 26.2% to 72.3%. On MT50 the gap widens further (e.g., PaCo: 18.6% V1 vs. 58.9% V2; PCGrad: 45.8% vs. 69.5%). For meta-RL on ML10, MAML shows no statistically significant difference between V1 (39.6%) and V2 (30.1%), while RL² drops sharply on V1 (6.5%) compared to V2 (25.7%), and AMAGO-2 remains low in both settings (3% V1, 6% V2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates eight baselines—five multi-task RL methods (MTMHSAC, SM, MOORE, PaCo, PCGrad) and three meta-RL methods (MAML, RL², AMAGO-2)—across two reward function versions (V1 and V2). For multi-task RL, all five methods perform substantially better under V2 rewards: PCGrad leads on MT10-V2 (85.5%) and MT50-V2 (69.5%), while SM is competitive (84.9% MT10-V2, 65.8% MT50-V2). PaCo shows the largest V1-to-V2 swing (26.2%→72.3% on MT10), suggesting its parameter-compositional architecture is particularly sensitive to reward scale. For meta-RL, MAML performs comparably under both reward versions (39.6% V1 vs. 30.1% V2 on ML10; 26.7% vs. 32.4% on ML45), while RL² is severely degraded on V1 (6.5% ML10, 9.7% ML45) but recovers on V2 (25.7% ML10, 36.8% ML45). AMAGO-2 remains weak across all settings (3–11%), indicating that even state-of-the-art transformer-based meta-RL struggles with the compositional task diversity of ML10/ML45. The central finding is that published results in the literature are not directly comparable because different methods were evaluated on different reward versions, with the V2 rewards enabling higher absolute performance due to more consistent cross-task reward scaling.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper is a direct re-engineering of Meta-World (Yu et al., CoRL 2020), the standard benchmark for multi-task and meta-RL robotic manipulation. Rather than proposing a new algorithm, it identifies that undocumented reward function changes (V1→V2) between Meta-World versions have silently invalidated cross-paper comparisons, and empirically demonstrates this by re-running MTMHSAC, SM, PaCo, MOORE, PCGrad, MAML, and RL² under both reward sets. The re-engineered version modernizes the infrastructure to Gymnasium and MuJoCo Python bindings, adds MT25/ML25 task sets and custom task-set creation, and preserves both V1 and V2 rewards as selectable options for full backward compatibility.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: any paper that reported Meta-World results without specifying the reward version may have produced non-comparable numbers, and the dramatic gaps (e.g., PaCo dropping from 85.4 to 26.2 on MT10 depending on reward version) show this is not a minor concern. The finding that consistent reward scaling across tasks (V2's design principle) uniformly improves multi-task performance echoes Hessel et al. (2019) but is now empirically grounded in a widely-used benchmark. Open questions remain for meta-RL, where MAML and RL² still achieve only ~30% success on ML10/ML45, suggesting the compositional and non-parametric task diversity in these settings is far from solved. The paper also highlights a broader methodological lesson: benchmarks need explicit versioning, and researchers should re-run baselines rather than copying published numbers.
