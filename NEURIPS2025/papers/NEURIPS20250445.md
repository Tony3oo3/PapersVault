## Problem

Existing inference-time alignment methods for text-to-video diffusion models (greedy search, best-of-N) suffer from error propagation caused by noisy one-step posterior mean estimates used for reward evaluation, leading to sub-optimal generation. Additionally, individual video quality metrics (e.g., subject consistency, dynamic degree) do not reliably correlate with human or VLM preferences, so it is unclear which composite reward should be optimized. The paper addresses both the 'how' (a more robust search algorithm) and the 'what' (reward calibration) of inference-time video alignment.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt as input and produces a generated video as output, operating entirely in the latent space of a pre-trained text-to-video diffusion model (Latte 1.1B, CogVideoX-5B, or Wan 2.1-14B) without any parameter updates. At each denoising step, the reward function is evaluated on a decoded video estimate (via VAE decoder), and the search selects among KB candidate latents. The calibrated reward is a weighted linear combination of six frame-level and temporal metrics (DINO subject consistency, AMT motion smoothness, RAFT dynamic degree, LAION aesthetic score, MUSIQ imaging quality, ViCLIP text-video consistency), with weights tuned per prompt dynamics grade.

## Method

Diffusion Latent Beam Search (DLBS) replaces the greedy top-1 argmax at each denoising step with a beam search that maintains B beams and samples K stochastic candidates per beam, selecting the top-B highest-rewarded latents from KB candidates to mitigate error accumulation from noisy reward estimates. A Lookahead (LA) estimator further reduces reward-estimation noise by running T' steps (typically 2–6) of deterministic DDIM from the current latent to obtain a cleaner z_0 approximation before scoring, with theoretical guarantees that the reward-approximation error decreases as O(1/T') for DDIM. The reward itself is a calibrated weighted linear combination of six perceptual video quality metrics, where weights are determined by brute-force search to maximize Pearson correlation with Gemini or GPT-4o preference scores, and vary by prompt dynamics grade (e.g., higher weight on dynamic degree for high-motion prompts). The approach is grounded in stochastic optimal control: the optimal drift term is approximated via Jensen's inequality, Tweedie's one-step posterior mean, and conversion of the reward gradient into a zeroth-order argmax, with DLBS-LA providing a practical correction to the resulting approximation errors.

## Results

DLBS-LA (KB=8, T'=6, NFE=2500) consistently outperforms BoN (KB=64, NFE=3200) in human pairwise evaluation across all models and prompts, requiring ~22% fewer function evaluations. On the DPO-finetuned VideoCrafter2, combining DPO with DLBS yields 0.359 on DEVIL-high and 0.576 on MSRVTT-test (calibrated reward), versus 0.337/0.555 for the base model and 0.335/0.556 for DPO alone. DLBS-LA with a small search budget (KB=8, T'=6) achieves comparable or superior calibrated reward to DLBS with KB=32, and the LA estimator adds only marginal computational overhead. On 1,003 diverse Movie Gen Video Bench prompts, DLBS-LA (KB=8, T'=6) delivers substantially higher alignment rewards than greedy search (KB=1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Greedy Search (Kim et al., Free²Guide [15]), DLBS outperforms GS across all search budgets (KB=1–32), with the gap widening at KB=16 and 32 where GS saturates; DLBS-LA further amplifies the advantage with only marginal overhead. Against Best-of-N Sampling (Ma et al. [17]), DLBS achieves higher calibrated reward at equivalent or lower NFE, and in human pairwise evaluation DLBS-LA (KB=8, NFE=2500) is preferred over BoN (KB=64, NFE=3200) by all three human raters regardless of model or prompt. Against VideoDPO (Liu et al. [58]), DPO alone yields negligible improvement over the base VideoCrafter2 (0.335 vs. 0.337 on DEVIL-high), but combining DPO with DLBS produces a clear gain to 0.359 (DEVIL-high) and 0.576 (MSRVTT-test), demonstrating that inference-time search is complementary to fine-tuning rather than redundant. The paper also shows that increasing diffusion steps T (DLBS-T with T=100, 200) provides only marginal improvement over the LA estimator + larger search budget, establishing a practical guideline to prioritize search budget and lookahead over denoising steps.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies existing inference-time alignment methods for diffusion models (classifier guidance, greedy search, twisted SMC) under a stochastic optimal control framework, then departs from them by replacing the single-beam argmax with a multi-beam search (DLBS) and introducing a lookahead estimator that runs a short deterministic DDIM rollout to reduce reward-estimation noise. It builds directly on DDIM sampling [42], the Tweedie posterior-mean approximation [36], and the argmax-based latent selection of Huang et al. [11], but addresses their core weakness—error accumulation from noisy one-step reward estimates—through beam diversity and multi-step lookahead. A second contribution, reward calibration via weighted linear combinations of VBench-style metrics, is positioned as a necessary prerequisite that prior inference-time alignment work largely overlooked.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a concrete, actionable guideline for inference-time compute allocation in video generation: prioritize the lookahead estimator and search budget over additional denoising steps, a finding supported across Latte, CogVideoX-5B, and Wan 2.1-14B. The reward-calibration analysis reveals that no single perceptual metric correlates well with VLM or human preference, and that optimal metric weights shift with prompt dynamics grade, which has implications for how future alignment pipelines should be designed. Limitations include the assumption that VLMs (Gemini, GPT-4o) serve as reliable proxies for human judgment, the prompt-set-dependent nature of calibrated weights, and the fact that the method still requires multiple reward evaluations per step, which constrains its applicability to very long or high-resolution videos. Open questions include whether physics-aware or task-specific reward models could replace the current multi-metric calibration, and whether the approach extends naturally to interactive or streaming video generation.
