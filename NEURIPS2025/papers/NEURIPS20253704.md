## Problem

When Euclidean diffusion models are applied directly to data constrained on a known manifold, the score function exhibits a multiscale singularity: its normal component diverges as O(1/σ) while the tangential component remains O(1) as the noise scale σ→0. This scale discrepancy causes the quadratic training loss to disproportionately weight the normal direction, leading the model to capture the manifold geometry rather than the data distribution on it (manifold overfitting), ultimately degrading generation accuracy.

## Input / Output

The method takes a dataset of samples drawn from an unknown density p_0 on a known d-dimensional submanifold M ⊂ R^n (specified by a smooth constraint function ξ: R^n → R^{n-d} with full-rank Jacoboid), and produces new samples on M. The manifold can be a hyperplane, a polyhedral mesh, the special orthogonal group SO(10) (45-dim in R^100), or a level set of dihedral angles in a molecular system. Generation is performed via either a reverse SDE with a final projection onto M, or a two-stage annealed Langevin dynamics constrained to M.

## Method

The paper first provides a rigorous asymptotic decomposition of the perturbed score function into tangential and normal components (Theorem 3.1), showing the normal component scales as O(1/σ) while the tangential component is O(1), and establishing a novel connection between the ambient-space score and the Riemannian score on M. Two mitigation strategies are proposed: (1) Niso-DM replaces isotropic Gaussian noise with non-isotropic noise that adds extra variance σ^{2α} along the normal direction (α∈(0,1)), reducing the normal score scale from O(1/σ²) to O(1/σ^{2α}) and narrowing the tangential–normal gap (Theorem 4.1); (2) Tango-DM trains only the tangential component of the score via a projected loss ℓ_tango when σ is below a threshold, entirely bypassing the singular normal component. For sampling, a two-stage Annealing SDE refines coarse reverse-SDE samples using Langevin dynamics projected onto the tangent space, whose invariant distribution converges to the target p_0 as t→0.

## Results

On the 45-dimensional SO(10) manifold embedded in R^100, Niso+DM with rescaling achieves a sliced 1-Wasserstein distance of 4.60e-3 (Reverse SDE) and 6.00e-3 (Annealing SDE), substantially outperforming the vanilla Iso-DM (1.76e-2 / 1.86e-2) and the Riemannian baseline RSSM (7.14e-3). On mesh data (Stanford Bunny, Spot the Cow), Niso+res and Tango+res reduce the JS divergence to 3.48e-1 and 3.84e-1 respectively, compared to 7.00e-1 for RSSM. On the alanine dipeptide level set, Niso-DM achieves a 2-Wasserstein distance of 8.24e-2 (Annealing), improving over Iso-DM's 8.83e-2, though the rescaling variant does not further help in this case.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary external baseline is Riemannian Score-based Generative Models (RSSM) [De Bortoli et al., 2022; Huang et al., 2022], which explicitly leverages manifold geometry via Riemannian logarithmic maps or Laplace-Beltrami eigenpairs. On SO(10), Niso+res (4.60e-3) outperforms RSSM (7.14e-3) by ~36% under Reverse SDE, and Niso+res (6.00e-3) still beats RSSM (7.14e-3) under Annealing SDE. On mesh data, the gap is even larger: Niso+res achieves 3.48e-1 JS divergence on Stanford Bunny versus 7.00e-1 for RSSM, a roughly 2× improvement. The internal baseline is the vanilla isotropic diffusion model (Iso-DM); both Niso-DM and Tango-DM consistently reduce the tangential score error and improve sample quality over Iso-DM across all four test manifolds. On the alanine dipeptide, improvements over Iso-DM are more modest (8.24e-2 vs 8.83e-2 for Niso under Annealing), and the rescaling trick actually hurts performance, suggesting that simultaneously rescaling tangential and normal components can discard critical distributional information in this setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the VESDE score-based generative framework of Song et al. (2021) and the denoising score matching objective of Vincent (2011), but departs from prior manifold diffusion approaches (De Bortoli et al. 2022, Huang et al. 2022, Lou et al. 2023) that require expensive Riemannian geometric computations such as heat kernels or Laplace-Beltrami eigenpairs. While prior singularity analyses (Lu et al. 2023, Pidstrigach 2022, Chen et al. 2023) identified the score function blowup under the manifold hypothesis, this paper provides the first rigorous decomposition of the singularity into tangential O(1) and normal O(1/σ) components under VESDE, and translates this structural insight into two practical training modifications (non-isotropic noise and tangential-only loss) that avoid the need for manifold-specific diffusion operators entirely.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a practical, geometry-light alternative to Riemannian diffusion models: one only needs the manifold's defining equations (for the projection matrix) rather than expensive spectral or geodesic computations, making it applicable to complex meshes and high-dimensional Lie groups where prior methods struggle. Key limitations include the requirement of a known, smooth manifold definition (excluding the common case of implicitly structured data like images) and the incompatibility of Tango-DM with standard Reverse SDE sampling. Open questions include extending the approach to unknown or implicitly represented manifolds and designing efficient Reverse SDE-style samplers for the tangential-only training paradigm.
