## Problem

Word embeddings from models like Word2Vec and GloVe exhibit a striking linear analogy structure (e.g., king − man + woman ≈ queen), yet the theoretical origin of this geometric regularity remains unclear. Prior theoretical attempts relied on assumptions—such as spherical symmetry of a latent semantic space—that are inconsistent with the observed broad eigenvalue spectrum of the PMI matrix and with the fact that top eigenvectors already carry semantic content correlated with analogy relations. This paper asks: what generative mechanism for word co-occurrences naturally produces linear analogies in the eigendecomposition of the co-occurrence or PMI matrix?

## Input / Output

The method takes a word co-occurrence matrix P(i,j) (or its rescaled form M(i,j) = P(i,j)/(P(i)P(j)) and its log, the PMI matrix) derived from a text corpus, and produces word embeddings W_i ∈ R^K via truncated eigendecomposition. In the generative model, the input is a set of d binary semantic attributes per word (a point on the {−1,+1}^d hypercube) plus per-attribute signal strengths s_k and asymmetry parameters q_k; the output is an exact closed-form eigendecomposition of M and log M, from which embeddings of dimension K are read off. The analogy evaluation uses the Mikolov et al. benchmark of 19,544 four-word quadruples across 13 semantic families, scored by top-1 nearest-neighbor accuracy in embedding space.

## Method

The paper introduces a generative model in which each word is a d-dimensional binary attribute vector and co-occurrence probabilities factorize independently across attributes, yielding an exact Kronecker-product structure M = ⊗_k P^(k) for the rescaled co-occurrence matrix. This structure allows the full eigenspectrum and eigenvectors of M to be computed analytically: eigenvalues are products of 2×2 per-attribute eigenvalues, and eigenvectors are Kronecker products of per-attribute eigenvectors. For the PMI matrix (log M), the key result is that log M has rank at most d+1 with eigenvectors that are affine functions of the attribute vectors, so the parallelogram relation W_A − W_B + W_C = W_D holds exactly whenever the attribute vectors satisfy the corresponding relation—regardless of the magnitude or distribution of the signal strengths s_k. In contrast, for the raw M matrix, linear analogies hold only under restrictive conditions (small, narrowly distributed s_k and K ≤ d+1), because higher-order interaction eigenvectors (products of multiple attributes) contaminate the embedding at larger K. The paper further proves robustness via random-matrix-theory arguments: additive noise, vocabulary sparsification (retaining as few as ~15% of words), and removal of all word pairs encoding a specific analogy relation all leave the semantic eigenspace of log M essentially unchanged in the large-d limit.

## Results

In the synthetic binary-attribute model (d=8), the PMI (log M) target achieves 100% top-1 analogy accuracy for all K ≥ d, regardless of the distribution of semantic strengths s_k, while the raw M target achieves high accuracy only when complete eigenbands are fully included and degrades at higher K for broadly distributed s_k. On Wikipedia co-occurrence data (10,000-word vocabulary, Mikolov et al. analogy benchmark), log(M) strictly outperforms M across all embedding dimensions and saturates at high K. The model is robust to multiplicative noise (σ_ξ ≈ O(1)), vocabulary sparsification (retaining only f=0.15 of words, removing >97% of the co-occurrence matrix), and removal of all word pairs of a given analogy relationship, with analogy breakdown under noise occurring only at K ≈ (1/σ_ξ)·2^(d/2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper is primarily theoretical and does not benchmark against competing embedding methods; instead, it compares two embedding targets (raw co-occurrence matrix M vs. PMI log M) and validates its generative model against Wikipedia statistics. The central empirical finding is that log(M) consistently outperforms M as an embedding target: on Wikipedia, log(M) achieves strictly higher analogy accuracy at every embedding dimension and saturates earlier (Figure 2b). In the synthetic model, log(M) reaches 100% accuracy at K = d for any s_k distribution, whereas M requires narrowly distributed s_k and complete eigenband inclusion to achieve perfect accuracy, and degrades at higher K when s_k are broadly distributed (as in real text). The paper further shows that the PMI-based embedding is robust to three perturbation types—additive noise, vocabulary pruning, and removal of all pairs of a given relationship—whereas the M-based embedding is more fragile under the same conditions. The model's eigenvalue spectrum predictions (broadly distributed for M, concentrated at d+1 values for PMI) are confirmed against empirical Wikipedia co-occurrence data (Figure 1c).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the attribute-based view of word semantics from cognitive psychology (Rumelhart & Abrahamson, 1973) and the PMI-based embedding framework of GloVe (Pennington et al., 2014), but departs sharply from prior theoretical explanations of linear analogies. Earlier works (Arora et al., 2016; Hashimoto et al., 2016) posited a 'true' Euclidean semantic space with spherical symmetry to derive analogy relations, while Ethayarajh et al. (2019) and Allen et al. (2019) relied on approximate ratio-of-conditional-probabilities arguments without specifying when or why the approximation holds. Here, the authors instead assume words are defined by independent binary attributes, which yields an exact Kronecker-product structure in the co-occurrence matrix and makes the eigendecomposition analytically tractable, providing a mechanistic account of how each embedding dimension contributes to analogy structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first fully analytical model that simultaneously explains four previously unconnected empirical observations about linear analogies (emergence in top eigenvectors, saturation with dimension, superiority of PMI over raw M, and persistence after removing all pairs of a given relation), giving practitioners a principled understanding of why GloVe-style log-M targets outperform raw co-occurrence targets. The model's limitations—binary attributes, no polysemy, no hierarchical structure, and the assumption that all 2^d attribute combinations exist—mean it captures the skeleton of the phenomenon but not its full complexity. Open questions include extending the framework to hierarchical and correlated attributes, polysemous words, and testing whether the same attribute-based mechanism underlies the linear subspaces observed in modern large language models.
