## Problem

Scaling test-time compute via RL for VLMs requires effective policy exploration, yet existing approaches such as increasing rollout temperature introduce only superficial decoding diversity without meaningfully directing policies toward more robust or informative behaviors. Additionally, VLMs suffer from imperfect visual perception that degrades downstream reasoning, but recent RL-based VLM training methods largely ignore this perceptual bottleneck when adapting techniques from the LLM domain.

## Input / Output

Each training sample consists of an RGB image and a text query (converted from multiple-choice to free-form to prevent reward hacking). During rollout collection, the image is additionally passed through a distortion function (Gaussian noise or rotation) to produce a noisy variant, but the policy update is conditioned exclusively on the clean image-query pair. The model outputs a free-form text response (reasoning trace plus final answer), scored by a binary rule-based reward (1 if the parsed answer is correct, 0 otherwise).

## Method

NoisyRollout augments standard GRPO with a hybrid rollout strategy: for each input, the old policy generates n₁ rollouts from the clean image and n₂ rollouts from a moderately distorted version, and all rollouts are pooled into a single group for computing the shared reward baseline and normalized advantage. Crucially, the policy gradient update is conditioned only on the clean input, so the noisy rollouts serve purely as exploration signals that inject perceptual diversity into the group statistics without altering the optimization target. A sigmoid-shaped noise annealing schedule progressively reduces the distortion strength α_t over training, balancing early-stage exploration with late-stage on-policy stability and preventing distributional mismatch. The method requires no additional training cost, no changes to the RL objective, and no architectural modifications—only a distortion function applied at rollout time.

## Results

Trained on only 2.1K samples from Geometry3K with Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct, NoisyRollout achieves 53.2% on MathVerse, 28.5% on MathVision, 72.6% on MathVista, 69.6% on WeMath, and 72.1% on HallusionBench, outperforming vanilla GRPO (50.8/27.3/70.5/67.4/69.8) on all five benchmarks. On the larger MMK12 dataset (6.4K samples), NoisyRollout further improves to 53.0/30.6/74.5/70.3/72.2. For the 32B model, NoisyRollout reaches 59.3% MathVerse, 41.6% MathVision, 77.4% MathVista, 77.6% WeMath, and 73.2% HallusionBench on MMK12, surpassing the vanilla GRPO baseline by up to 1.6 points on MathVision.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NoisyRollout consistently outperforms vanilla GRPO (its direct baseline) across all five benchmarks by 1.2–2.4 points, using the same total rollout budget (12). Among open-source RL-tuned models, NoisyRollout with only 2.1K training samples surpasses OpenVLThinker-7B (48.0% MathVerse, 71.5% MathVista; trained on 35K SFT + 15K RL), MM-Eureka-Qwen-7B (50.5/71.5; 15K RL), ThinkLite-7B-VL (50.2/72.7; 11K RL), and VLAA-Thinker-7B (49.9/68.8; 25K RL) on MathVerse, while matching or exceeding them on MathVista. On HallusionBench (perception), NoisyRollout achieves 72.1%, surpassing ThinkLite-7B-VL (71.0%), OpenVLThinker-7B (70.8%), and MM-Eureka-Qwen-7B (68.3%), and even exceeding GPT-4o (71.4†). For the 32B model, NoisyRollout improves over vanilla GRPO by 0.7 points on MathVision (39.9 vs 39.2) and 1.2 points on HallusionBench (73.5 vs 72.3), though gains on MathVerse are minimal (58.9 vs 58.9), likely because the 32B base model was already RL-fine-tuned. The method also outperforms the much larger MM-Eureka-Qwen-32B (56.5/39.8/76.7/76.7/71.4) on MathVision and HallusionBench while using far less training data (2.1K vs 17K).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on GRPO (Group Relative Policy Optimization) from DeepSeekMath, which has been adapted from LLMs to VLMs by recent works (MM-Eureka, Vision-R1, OpenVLThinker, R1-VL). The key conceptual departure is importing the data-augmentation-for-exploration principle well-established in robotics RL (domain randomization, data-augmented RL) into the VLM reasoning RL setting, where prior work has largely ported LLM RL recipes without addressing perceptual limitations. The hybrid rollout strategy—mixing clean and distorted-image trajectories into a single GRPO group while anchoring policy updates to clean inputs—is a novel design choice that distinguishes this from standard sample-level augmentation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

NoisyRollout offers a drop-in, zero-extra-cost modification to existing GRPO pipelines that consistently improves out-of-domain generalization across model sizes (7B, 32B), datasets (Geometry3K, MMK12), and augmentation types, making it practically attractive for the VLM RL community. The noise annealing schedule addresses a real training-stability issue (distributional mismatch causing divergence), and the gradient projection analysis provides quantitative evidence that noisy rollouts drive more of the policy update than clean ones. Open questions include whether the approach extends beyond math reasoning to broader visual tasks, whether the optimal noise schedule and strength transfer across domains, and whether the modest 1–3 point gains persist at much larger data scales (tens of thousands of samples).
