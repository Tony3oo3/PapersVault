## Problem

Data sharing platforms (marketplaces, consortia, federated learning) reward agents for contributing data, but naive quantity-based incentives are vulnerable to fabrication. Prior mechanisms that compare an agent's submission against others' to detect untruthfulness rely on strong distributional assumptions (Gaussian, Bernoulli, exponential families) or narrow models of misreporting, leaving the general case—arbitrary data distributions with arbitrary strategic manipulation—unsolved.

## Input / Output

Each agent submits a dataset Y_i (possibly a manipulated version of their true data X_i), and the mechanism designer specifies a collection of K feature maps φ^k: X → R (e.g., DistilBERT 768-dim embeddings for text, DeiT-small 384-dim embeddings for images) plus, in the Bayesian variant, a prior Π over data distributions. The mechanism outputs a per-agent loss L_i ∈ [0,1] that serves as a negative reward; in applications this loss is converted into bounded payments (marketplace) or a data-allocation size for the agent's ML task (federated learning). The bounded [0,1] range is critical for guaranteeing budget feasibility and individual rationality without distributional assumptions.

## Method

The core loss replaces one empirical CDF in the classical two-sample Cramér-von Mises statistic with a conditional expectation E[F_{Z_i}(T_i) | X_i = Y_i, T_i], so the loss measures how well the agent's own data predicts the ECDF of others' data at a randomly sampled evaluation point. In the Bayesian version (Algorithms 1–2), this conditional expectation is computed under a known prior Π and applied across K feature maps, yielding an exact Nash equilibrium for truthful reporting under a mild non-degeneracy condition on Π. The prior-agnostic version (Algorithm 3) replaces the intractable conditional expectation with an ECDF computed from the agent's submission augmented by a subset W_i of others' data, trading exact truthfulness for an ε-approximate NE where ε = (1/4)(1/(|X_i|+|W_i|) + 1/|Z_i|) vanishes as data grows. The loss is provably bounded in [0,1] and strictly decreases with the quantity of truthful data submitted (MIB property), enabling direct use in payment and data-allocation mechanisms without Gaussian or finite-support assumptions.

## Results

In synthetic experiments, the proposed Bayesian mechanism (Algorithm 1) always penalizes fabrication, while all three baselines (KS-test, direct CvM-test, Mean-diff) yield lower loss under at least one fabrication strategy, confirming their susceptibility to manipulation. On real-world SQuAD text data (500 sentences), Algorithm 3 achieves an average truthful loss of 0.0003 versus 0.0011 for LLM-fabricated data, a 3.7× gap; on Oxford Flowers-102 images (100 images), the gap is 0.0015 vs. 0.0040 (2.7×). The prior-agnostic mechanism (Algorithm 3) is provably (1/4)(1/(|X_i|+|W_i|) + 1/|Z_i|)-approximately truthful, with the approximation parameter vanishing as data volume grows.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its CvM-inspired mechanism against three standard two-sample tests used as losses: the Kolmogorov-Smirnov test, the direct Cramér-von Mises test, and the mean-difference statistic (used in prior Gaussian mean-estimation work [7, 23]). In the synthetic beta-Bernoulli experiment, Mean-diff and KS-test both yield *lower* loss under fabrication (adding Bern(1/2) or Bern(p̃) samples), meaning an agent can reduce its loss by fabricating data—directly violating truthfulness. In the normal-normal synthetic experiment, KS-test again rewards fabrication (inserting fake points between real observations), while the direct CvM-test and Mean-diff happen to penalize it. On real-world SQuAD and Flowers-102 data, all four methods show higher loss for fabricated submissions, but the paper emphasizes that only Algorithm 3 carries a provable ε-approximate truthfulness guarantee; the baselines may still be vulnerable to more sophisticated or distribution-specific fabrication strategies not tested here.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the Cramér-von Mises two-sample test statistic into a mechanism-design loss function for incentivizing truthful data sharing, departing from prior work that relied on strong distributional assumptions (Gaussian [7,11,23], finite support or exponential families [10], binary data [22]) or restricted models of untruthful behavior (duplication [13], scalar shifts [12]). The key conceptual move is replacing one empirical CDF in the CvM statistic with a Bayesian conditional expectation, which provably makes truthful reporting a Nash equilibrium for general distributions. The prior-agnostic variant (Algorithm 3) further removes the need for a prior by using an augmented ECDF as a plug-in estimate, connecting the approach to the peer prediction literature [33–38] while handling the critical case where agents can change the number of data points they submit.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides the first general-purpose, distribution-agnostic mechanism that simultaneously guarantees truthfulness and rewards agents for contributing more data, removing the Gaussian/exponential-family restrictions that limited prior data-sharing mechanisms. The prior-agnostic version is computationally simple and validated on real text and image data, making it practically deployable in data marketplaces and federated learning consortia. Key limitations include the need for application-specific feature map selection (no universally optimal choice), the computational cost of Bayesian posterior inference in the exact version, and the fact that the ε-approximate guarantee in the prior-agnostic version only vanishes asymptotically with data size. Open questions include whether the mechanism is robust to more sophisticated adversarial strategies beyond simple fabrication, and how to automatically select feature maps that maximize the truthfulness gap.
