## Problem

Standard Transformers only support discrete, quantized positional information, making them ill-suited for irregularly sampled time-series and other modalities with continuous positions. Existing solutions require task-specific architectural modifications (e.g., neural ODEs, modified feedforward layers) that add complexity and prevent leveraging the broader Transformer ecosystem. The paper asks whether a standard positional embedding method—Rotary Positional Embeddings (RoPE)—can be extended to continuous positions without any time-series-specific architectural changes.

## Input / Output

RoMAE accepts multidimensional inputs (images, Mel spectrograms, multivariate irregular time-series) of shape d₁×d₂×…×d_D, where some dimensions may be irregularly sampled, along with a corresponding sequence of continuous positional coordinates s ∈ ℝ^D per patch. Inputs are N-D patchified into a flat token sequence; irregular dimensions must use patch size 1, while regular dimensions can use larger patches (e.g., 16×16 spatial patches with patch size 1 along time). During pre-training the model outputs reconstructed patch values for masked positions; during fine-tuning it produces class labels, interpolated values, or regression targets from the encoder's intermediate representations.

## Method

RoMAE combines MAE's asymmetric encoder/decoder pre-training with Axial RoPE applied to continuous positions: the input embedding is split into D subspaces (one per positional dimension), and p-RoPE (keeping only the 75% smallest-frequency rotations) is applied to each subspace using the real-valued coordinate m ∈ ℝ rather than an integer index. The encoder uses modern NLP components (SwiGLU, RMSNorm) and an optional learned [CLS] token; the decoder is a small shallow Transformer that reconstructs masked patches. A key theoretical finding is that the [CLS] token breaks RoPE's relative-position invariance, enabling the encoder to reconstruct absolute positions, while without it the encoder is translationally invariant. For datasets with many irregular dimensions (e.g., 6-channel ELasTiCC light curves), one axial dimension is reserved to encode the channel index, reducing the positional dimensionality from 6 to 2.

## Results

On the DESC ELasTiCC Challenge (irregular multivariate light-curve classification), RoMAE-tiny achieves an F-score of 0.8029 without the alert mask, surpassing the specialized ATAT architecture (0.6270) by ~0.17. On the Pendulum regression task, RoMAE attains MSE 3.32×10⁻³, outperforming S5 (3.41) and ContiFormer (4.63). For interpolation, RoMAE-tiny achieves RMSE 0.0183 on the 2D Spirals benchmark—a 26× improvement over ContiFormer (0.49)—and MSE 0.467 on PhysioNet-2012, beating HeTVAE (0.562). On audio (ESC-50), RoMAE-small reaches 84.7% accuracy when pre-trained on AudioSet-20k, exceeding SSAST (82.2%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RoMAE consistently outperforms all 12 baselines on their respective primary tasks. On ELasTiCC, RoMAE-tiny (F-score 0.8029) beats the specialized ATAT Transformer (0.6270) and the vanilla Transformer (0.5256) by large margins. On Pendulum regression, RoMAE (MSE 3.32×10⁻³) surpasses S5 (3.41), CRU (3.94), ContiFormer (4.63), RKN-Δt (5.09), and ODE-RNN (7.26). On interpolation, RoMAE-tiny dramatically outperforms ContiFormer on Spirals (0.0183 vs 0.49 RMSE, ~26×) and LatentODE (2.09), while matching or beating HeTVAE on Synthetic (0.233 vs 0.223 MSE) and PhysioNet (0.467 vs 0.562). On UEA time-series classification, RoMAE-tiny ties or exceeds TST, mTAN, S5, and ContiFormer on 4 of 5 datasets, with the closest margin on HB (0.7447 vs mTAN's 0.7789). On audio, RoMAE-small outperforms SSAST by 3.2 and 2.5 percentage points when pre-trained on Librispeech and AudioSet-20k respectively. On Tiny ImageNet, RoMAE without [CLS] (F-score 0.500) slightly edges out both the [CLS] variant (0.475) and the absolute-positioning MAE-style baseline (0.479), confirming no performance loss from using RoPE.

## Relation to Prior Work

RoMAE combines two well-established Transformer components—Masked Autoencoder (MAE) pre-training and Rotary Positional Embeddings (RoPE)—in a way that neither had previously been applied to irregular time-series. It extends RoPE from its original discrete-position formulation in RoFormer to continuous, real-valued positions via Axial RoPE, and adopts p-RoPE (truncated RoPE) for robustness. Conceptually, it departs from the line of specialized irregular time-series architectures (ContiFormer, Timer, mTAN, S5) by demonstrating that no task-specific architectural modifications are needed, and it introduces a new theoretical insight: learned input embeddings such as the [CLS] token invalidate RoPE's relative-position guarantee, a property previously assumed to hold universally.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical takeaway is that a single, unmodified Transformer encoder with RoPE and MAE pre-training can serve as a strong, task-agnostic baseline across irregular time-series, images, and audio, reducing the need for modality-specific architectural engineering. The finding that [CLS] tokens break RoPE's relative-position property has implications well beyond RoMAE, affecting any RoPE-based model (including RoFormer and LLMs) that uses learned prefix tokens. Open questions include whether linear-attention variants can address the O(n²) scaling limitation, how RoMAE extrapolates to unseen temporal ranges, and whether the continuous-position overhead can be further reduced through optimized RoPE implementations.
