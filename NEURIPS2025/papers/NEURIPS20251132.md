## Problem

Widely adopted pixel-wise metrics (PSNR, SSIM) for sparse-view CT reconstruction are insensitive to the presence or absence of small but clinically critical anatomical structures, since these structures occupy less than 0.01% of the scan volume. The paper demonstrates through a 21-radiologist reader study and quantitative benchmarks that state-of-the-art neural reconstruction methods (NeRF, TensoRF, InTomo) achieve high PSNR/SSIM while completely failing to preserve small organs, vessels, and intestinal structures, exposing a critical disconnect between algorithmic scores and clinical utility.

## Input / Output

CARE takes a sparse-view reconstructed CT volume (512×512 in-plane resolution, with three adjacent axial slices stacked as 3 channels to preserve inter-frame consistency) produced by any upstream reconstruction method (FDK, NeRF, TensoRF, R²-GS, etc.) along with a fixed text embedding encoding the contrast phase (arterial or portal venous). It outputs an enhanced CT volume at the same resolution with improved anatomical completeness. The method is model-agnostic and patient-agnostic: it is trained once on a population dataset and applied at inference to unseen reconstructions without per-case fine-tuning, requiring no additional scanning hardware or projection data.

## Method

CARE is a three-stage latent diffusion framework. First, a KL-regularized VAE (initialized from Stable Diffusion v1.5) is fine-tuned on 6,212 CT scans to produce well-behaved latents. Second, a latent diffusion UNet is pretrained with a super-resolution scheme (4× bicubic downsample-then-upsample) to learn intrinsic enhancement. Third, the UNet is fine-tuned on paired (ground-truth, sparse-view reconstruction) CT scans with a composite loss: standard noise-prediction loss L_n, an L1 pixel-space reconstruction loss L_p (weight 1), and a novel anatomy-guidance loss L_s (weight 0.001) that applies cross-entropy between a frozen nnU-Net segmentator's predictions on the decoded reconstruction versus the ground truth. The key mechanism is that the denoising UNet receives the noisy ground-truth latent concatenated with the reconstruction latent (doubled channel dimension), so the reconstruction provides structural anchoring while the diffusion process generates anatomically complete details. At inference, CARE starts from pure Gaussian noise concatenated with the unseen reconstruction's latent and iteratively denoises over 1,000 steps, producing an anatomically enhanced CT without modifying the underlying reconstruction pipeline.

## Results

On the 61-scan Reconstruction Benchmark Dataset, CARE-enhanced reconstructions achieve up to +32% improvement in large-organ NSD (e.g., InTomo: 2.4→30.9), +22% in small-organ NSD (NeRF: 0.2→25.3), +40% in intestinal clDice (InTomo: 6.3→33.5), and +36% in vessel clDice (InTomo: 4.7→47.9) over the unenhanced baselines. Critically, these anatomy-aware gains are invisible to pixel-wise metrics: SSIM and PSNR change by less than 1 point for most methods, confirming the paper's central claim that PSNR/SSIM are unreliable indicators of structural completeness. The anatomy segmentator (nnU-Net) achieves median Dice of 96.9% (liver), 97.7% (kidneys), and 92.1% (aorta) on the 1,958-scan JHH test set, outperforming or matching MedNeXt, MedFormer, and STU-Net-B across most structures.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper benchmarks nine CT reconstruction methods (InTomo, NeRF, TensoRF, R²-GS, NAF, FDK, SART, ASD-POCS, SAX-NeRF) on 61 CT scans. A striking finding is that pixel-wise and anatomy-aware rankings are nearly inverted: InTomo achieves SSIM 82.7 but NSD-small of 0.0 and clDice-vessel of 18.0, while FDK/SART/ASD-POCS with comparable SSIM (~92.5) achieve NSD-large of 84–85 and clDice-vessel of 76–79. Among neural renderers, SAX-NeRF leads on both pixel-wise (SSIM 93.7, PSNR 29.7) and anatomy-aware metrics, while R²-GS is second on pixel-wise but lags on small-organ NSD (53.0 vs. 65.8 for SAX-NeRF). CARE consistently improves all nine baselines on anatomy-aware metrics, with the largest absolute gains on the weakest methods (InTomo, NeRF, TensoRF) and smaller but still significant gains on already-strong methods (SART, SAX-NeRF). For the anatomy segmentator, CARE's nnU-Net outperforms MedNeXt, MedFormer, and STU-Net-B on liver (96.9 vs. 96.5/96.7/97.2), kidneys (97.7 vs. 97.2/97.1/96.9), and aorta (92.1 vs. 91.3/90.9/91.5), though it trails slightly on pancreas (86.5 vs. 88.1 for MedNeXt) and gallbladder (90.9 vs. 91.7 for STU-Net-B).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the Stable Diffusion v1.5 latent diffusion architecture as a plug-and-play enhancement module for CT reconstruction, adapting it with a segmentation-guided loss that has no direct precedent in the CT reconstruction literature. It builds on nnU-Net as a frozen anatomy segmentator, using its predictions both as an evaluation oracle and as a differentiable training signal—a dual role that departs from prior work where segmentation models serve only as post-hoc analysis tools. Conceptually, the paper positions itself against the dominant evaluation paradigm (PSNR/SSIM) in sparse-view CT, arguing that these metrics are structurally blind to small anatomical features, and introduces anatomy-aware metrics (NSD, clDice) as both a new benchmark and a training objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that widely adopted pixel-wise metrics (SSIM, PSNR) show near-zero correlation with anatomical completeness (Pearson ≈ 0.16–0.30) challenges a core assumption in CT reconstruction research and suggests that many published comparisons may be misleading. The 61-scan benchmark is the largest in the field, and the plug-and-play CARE module offers a practical path for existing pipelines to improve structural fidelity without retraining. However, CARE sometimes degrades pixel-wise scores, its gains are marginal when the baseline already preserves anatomy well (e.g., SAX-NeRF), and the single-institution JHH dataset limits generalizability claims. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to other modalities (MRI, ultrasound), whether segmentation model errors propagate into the enhancement, and whether the anatomy-aware metrics can be extended to pathology detection rather than just structural completeness.
