## Problem

Egocentric hand-object motion generation is hindered by unstable viewpoints, self-occlusions, perspective distortion, and noisy ego-motion inherent to first-person capture. Existing methods either depend on predefined 3D object priors that fail on novel objects, or rely on complex contact-map pipelines and open-loop prediction that accumulate errors over time. The paper tackles how to generate physically plausible, fine-grained hand trajectories from egocentric RGB and text without object-specific models or intricate hand-object contact modeling.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a free-form text instruction, a single egocentric RGB frame (resized to 224×224), and an initial MANO hand pose (109-dim vector comprising 15×6 finger rotations, 10 shape parameters, 1×6 wrist rotation, and 3D wrist translation, all in the camera frame). A monocular depth map is estimated on-the-fly from the RGB frame via UniDepthV2 rather than being provided as a separate input. The output is a trunk of l=16 future MANO parameter frames, where wrist pose is predicted as a relative transformation to the initial pose and the shape parameter β is repeated from the initial configuration at each timestep.

## Method

MEgoHand adopts a bi-level architecture. The high-level 'Cerebrum' uses a frozen Eagle-2 VLM (SmolLM2 + SigLIP-2) to infer motion priors from visual-textual context, augmented by a monocular metric depth estimator (UniDepthV2) whose output is encoded by a ResNet-50 depth encoder; visual and depth features are additively fused and interact with text embeddings via cross-modal attention to produce a unified multimodal representation. The low-level 'Cerebellum' is a DiT-based flow-matching policy that generates the 16-frame MANO motion trunk conditioned on this representation and the initial hand pose, trained with a conditional flow matching loss using a beta-distributed timestep sampling biased toward noisier values. A training-free Temporal Orthogonal Filtering (TOF) decoding strategy queries overlapping motion chunks, averages predictions at each timestep, and projects the convolved rotation onto SO(3) via SVD to suppress high-frequency jitter. For data, the authors build an Inverse MANO Retargeting Network (two-stage iterative training with a gating signal) to recover MANO parameters from raw joint coordinates, and a Virtual RGB-D Renderer to synthesize aligned depth maps for datasets lacking them, yielding a unified 3.35M-frame corpus.

## Results

On the in-domain evaluation (average across TACO, HOI4D, H2O, HOT3D, OakInk2), MEgoHand achieves MPJPE of 5.425 cm, MPJPE-PA of 0.425 cm, and MRE of 0.123 rad, reducing mean wrist translation error by 86.9% and joint rotation error by 34.1% over the strongest baseline LatentAct. After Procrustes alignment, joint and mesh vertex errors drop to 0.424 cm and 0.409 cm, representing 71.2% and 71.9% relative improvements. On cross-domain datasets, MEgoHand achieves 33.9% and 29.8% MPJPE improvements over LatentAct on ARCTIC (7.358 vs 11.65 cm) and HOLO (5.775 vs 8.341 cm), respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is LatentAct (and its diffusion variant LatentAct-Diff), which predicts 3D hand poses and contact maps from a single RGB image, text description, and 3D contact points. MEgoHand outperforms LatentAct on every metric across all five in-domain datasets, with the most dramatic gain in MRE (0.123 vs 0.937 rad, an 86.9% reduction) and substantial gains in Procrustes-aligned errors (MPJPE-PA: 0.425 vs 1.478 cm; MPVE-PA: 0.409 vs 1.453 cm). Removing the contact map from LatentAct further degrades its performance (e.g., MPJPE rises from 7.726 to 8.523 cm), confirming its heavy reliance on this auxiliary input, whereas MEgoHand-TI (without depth) already achieves 5.683 cm MPJPE—50% lower than LatentAct. On cross-domain ARCTIC and HOLO, MEgoHand maintains its lead with 33.9% and 29.8% MPJPE improvements, respectively, while also reducing MRE by roughly 75% (0.398 vs 1.577 on ARCTIC; 0.271 vs 1.112 on HOLO). Inference efficiency also favors MEgoHand: 74 ms per 16-frame sequence at 5.8 GB VRAM versus 156 ms at 10.8 GB for LatentAct on a single RTX 4090.

## Relation to Prior Work

MEgoHand extends the egocentric hand-object interaction (HOI) motion generation line initiated by SIGHT and LatentAct, but departs from both by eliminating reliance on explicit 3D object models or hand-crafted contact maps. Architecturally, it repurposes a frozen VLM (Eagle-2 with SigLIP-2 and SmolLM2) as a high-level 'cerebrum' for task and spatial reasoning, paired with a monocular depth estimator (UniDepthV2) to supply object-agnostic 3D geometry, and a DiT-based flow-matching policy as a low-level 'cerebellum' for trajectory generation. The Temporal Orthogonal Filtering decoding strategy and the Inverse MANO Retargeting / Virtual RGB-D Renderer data pipeline are novel contributions that address practical gaps (temporal jitter, dataset heterogeneity) not tackled by prior HOI predictors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that a VLM-plus-depth formulation can replace expensive contact-map pipelines while achieving 2× faster inference and 50% less VRAM, MEgoHand offers a more deployable path toward real-time AR/VR and robotic imitation from egocentric video. The curated 3.35M-frame, 1.2K-object unified dataset lowers the barrier for future egocentric HOI research. Key open questions remain: the method is limited to the right hand and rigid objects (ARCTIC's articulated objects expose this gap), metric depth degrades under cross-domain camera variation, and scaling to in-the-wild videos with modern pose detectors is left as future work.
