## Problem

Optimization-based MTL methods that manipulate task gradients often fail to outperform simple linear scalarization, while Pareto Front Learning (PFL) approaches that use mode connectivity to approximate the entire Pareto front suffer from linear-path degeneracy in 2-task settings and poor scalability to many tasks. This paper asks whether mode connectivity can be leveraged more efficiently for MTL by optimizing the connecting curve itself rather than the endpoints, to identify a single desired trade-off point on the Pareto front.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (CityScapes at 2048×1024, NYUv2 at 480×640, CelebA at 200×200, MultiMNIST at 28×28) and produces task-specific outputs: segmentation masks, depth maps, surface normal maps, or class labels depending on the benchmark. Unusually, the trained model is not a single weight vector but a parameterized curve C(t) over t ∈ [0,1]; the final MTL solution is extracted at a chosen t (t=1/2 for scene understanding, validation-selected for classification), and different t values yield different task trade-offs along the same curve.

## Method

EXTRA is a two-stage procedure: (1) train single-task or task-group models to obtain endpoint weights, then (2) optimize a NURBS curve connecting those endpoints with a small number of trainable control points. The NURBS representation (via Cox-de Boor basis functions) decouples the number of control points from the curve order, enabling local adjustments that Bezier curves cannot provide. An order-aware objective R_o penalizes configurations where both task losses move in the same direction along t, explicitly encouraging the curve to traverse the Pareto front. For many-task scenarios (e.g., 40 tasks on CelebA), a warmup-based spectral clustering on gradient cosine similarity partitions tasks into three groups, with an alignment objective anchoring the third group's loss at the curve midpoint t=1/2, keeping memory and storage constant regardless of task count.

## Results

On CityScapes (2 tasks), EXTRA-F achieves the best Δm% of 1.63% and MR of 2.00, outperforming FairGrad (5.18%, 2.50) and Nash-MTL (6.82%, 4.00). On NYUv2 (3 tasks), EXTRA-F attains Δm% of −6.30% and MR of 1.56, surpassing FairGrad (−4.66%, 3.56) and Nash-MTL (−4.04%, 4.11). On CelebA (40 tasks), EXTRA-F achieves Δm% of −0.11% and MR of 3.98, substantially outperforming the next-best methods FAMO (1.21%, 6.35) and FairGrad (1.15%, 7.05). EXTRA also demonstrates plug-and-play capability, improving LS, CAGrad, and FairGrad endpoints on CityScapes by 17.7, 2.97, and 3.55 Δm% points respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EXTRA-F dominates all 14 baselines on the primary Δm% metric across all three benchmarks. On CityScapes, EXTRA-F (1.63%) beats the strongest gradient-based method FairGrad (5.18%) by 3.55 points and Nash-MTL (6.82%) by 5.19 points; even the simplest LS baseline (22.60%) is outperformed by over 20 points. On NYUv2, EXTRA-F achieves −6.30% Δm% versus FairGrad's −4.66% and Nash-MTL's −4.04%, while also attaining the best MR (1.56 vs. 3.56 and 4.11). On CelebA (40 tasks), EXTRA-F (−0.11% Δm%, MR 3.98) substantially outperforms FAMO (1.21%, 6.35), FairGrad (1.15%, 7.05), and IMTL-G (0.84%, 6.45), confirming the scalability of the task-grouping strategy. Among PFL baselines, PaMaL achieves competitive results only in the 2-task CityScapes setting but degrades on NYUv2 (3 tasks) and causes OOM on CelebA (40 tasks), whereas EXTRA maintains consistent gains. The plug-and-play experiments (Table 5) show EXTRA improves LS by 17.67 Δm% points, CAGrad by 2.97 points, and FairGrad by 3.55 points on CityScapes, confirming it is orthogonal to existing gradient-combination strategies.

## Relation to Prior Work

EXTRA repurposes mode connectivity (Garipov et al., 2018) for multi-task learning by optimizing the curve between two single-task endpoints rather than the endpoints themselves, drawing on NURBS (Piegl & Tiller, 2012) for flexible curve representation. It explicitly departs from two prior paradigms: gradient-combination MTL (PCGrad, CAGrad, Nash-MTL, FairGrad), which recent work has shown to be no better than linear scalarization, and Pareto Front Learning (PaMaL, COSMOS, HPN), which optimizes endpoints to reconstruct the entire Pareto front at prohibitive memory cost. The paper positions itself as a 'curve-based' alternative to both, requiring only two endpoints plus a few control points regardless of task count.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

EXTRA offers a practical plug-and-play layer that can be stacked on top of existing MTL methods (demonstrated with LS, CAGrad, and FairGrad), making it immediately useful for practitioners who want Pareto-front-aware trade-offs without redesigning their optimization. The key open limitation is that user-preference MTL (selecting a specific point on the Pareto front) degrades beyond two tasks, since the curve-based formulation naturally encodes a single trade-off axis; extending this to higher-dimensional preference spaces remains unresolved. The finding that NURBS's local flexibility is the dominant contributor (with the order-aware objective providing only marginal additional gain in ablation) suggests the curve representation itself is the critical design choice, a useful signal for future work on loss-landscape navigation.
