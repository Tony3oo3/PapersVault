## Problem

Policy optimization is increasingly deployed in privacy-sensitive domains (healthcare, LLM alignment), yet no formal differential privacy definition or sample complexity theory exists for PO. Standard DP does not directly apply because on-policy learning has no fixed dataset and changing one sample cascades into all future samples through the evolving policy, making the privacy unit ill-defined.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes a stream of N unique 'users' (patients, prompts, or contexts), each providing a state x drawn from ρ; the learner then generates actions y ~ μ(·|x) and y' ~ π_θt(·|x) on the fly and observes reward r(x,y). The output is a trained policy π_θ together with a closed-form sample complexity bound N = m·T (batch size × iterations) as a function of target accuracy α, privacy parameters (ε,δ), and problem-specific constants. The privacy unit is per-user rather than per-sample, and the one-pass design means each user is interacted with exactly once.

## Method

The paper introduces a unified batched one-pass meta-algorithm (Algorithm 1) that, at each of T iterations, collects m fresh trajectories from new users, computes advantage estimates, and calls a pluggable PrivUpdate oracle to produce the next policy. This meta-algorithm instantiates three private PO methods: DP-PG adds Gaussian noise to a REINFORCE-style gradient estimate; DP-NPG reduces each update to a private least-squares regression (solved via the exponential mechanism or ISSP) over the NPG regression subproblem; and DP-REBEL similarly privatizes the relative-reward regression of REBEL. The one-pass structure ensures that (ε,δ)-DP in the standard sense for PrivUpdate automatically yields (ε,δ)-DP in PO (Definition 2) via adaptive parallel composition. For DP-NPG and DP-REBEL, the reduction to private regression allows direct import of state-of-the-art private estimation results (ISSP for low-dimension, high-dimension private linear regression, private SGD), and the key theoretical finding is that the privacy cost typically appears as a lower-order additive term in the overall sample complexity bound.

## Results

The paper establishes the first sample complexity bounds for differentially private policy optimization. For DP-PG, the FOSP convergence rate is E[‖∇J(θ_U)‖²] ≤ O_δ(1/√N + (√d/(Nε))^{2/3}), yielding a total sample complexity of Õ_δ(1/α⁴ + √d/(α³ε)) for α-accuracy. For DP-NPG with a log-linear policy in the low-dimension regime, the sample complexity is Õ_δ((d/α⁴ + d/(α³ε) + d/(α²ε²)) · B²W²). The key finding is that the privacy cost (terms involving ε) consistently appears as a lower-order additive term relative to the non-private statistical term, for constant ε and d.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no experimental baselines; comparisons are made against the corresponding non-private sample complexity bounds from prior work. For DP-PG FOSP, the non-private term 1/α⁴ exactly matches the bound in Yuan et al. [6], while the privacy overhead √d/(α³ε) is a strictly lower-order term for constant ε and d. For the tabular softmax global convergence (Theorem 5), the non-private term 1/α⁶ matches Yuan et al. [6], and the privacy cost √d/(α^{9/2}ε) is again lower-order. For DP-NPG with log-linear policies, the non-private d/α⁴ term in the low-dimension case matches the standard linear regression rate, while the two privacy terms d/(α³ε) and d/(α²ε²) are lower-order. A notable subtlety identified is that the weight bound W in DP-NPG can scale as √d due to Gaussian noise in the private regression oracle, introducing an additional d-dependence in the non-private term that is absent in the DP-PG analysis.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the non-private sample complexity analyses of policy gradient methods (Yuan et al. 2022, Agarwal et al. 2021, Ding et al. 2022) into the differentially private setting, while also drawing on the 'user'-level dataset formulation from private online bandit/RL work (Vietri et al. 2020, Chowdhury & Zhou 2022). It departs from prior private RL work, which studied optimistic PPO/NPG from the exploration/regret perspective in tabular or linear settings, by taking the optimization perspective with coverage conditions and general reward/policy function classes. A key conceptual departure is the redefinition of the privacy unit from 'one (x,y) sample' to 'one user,' which the authors argue is necessary because on-policy learning makes the standard adjacent-dataset notion incoherent. The reduction of private NPG and REBEL to sequences of private regression problems allows the paper to import results from private linear regression (ISSP, Chen et al. 2025) and private stochastic optimization (Bassily et al. 2019).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic theoretical treatment of sample complexity for differentially private policy optimization, providing concrete bounds for DP-PG, DP-NPG, and DP-REBEL that quantify the privacy cost. The encouraging finding that privacy costs are typically lower-order additive terms (e.g., O(√d/(α³ε)) vs. O(1/α⁴) for the statistical term) suggests that DP can be incorporated into PO without catastrophic sample overhead, which is practically relevant for sensitive domains like healthcare and LLM alignment. Open questions include whether the bandit formulation generalizes cleanly to full MDPs, whether the dimension-dependent W in the NPG/REBEL analysis can be removed, and whether computationally efficient private oracles exist for the general function class case beyond the exponential mechanism. The paper is purely theoretical with no empirical validation beyond a proof-of-concept appendix, leaving the practical tightness of the bounds untested.
