## Problem

Existing 3D structure-based drug design models produce molecules that fit protein pockets well but are often chemically unreasonable—featuring improper ring conjugation, partially unsaturated rings, and rare polycyclic systems that deviate from drug-like chemical space. The paper introduces the Molecular Reasonability Ratio (MRR) to quantify this gap, showing that models like TargetDiff (37.8%) and MolCRAFT (58.5%) fall far below the 85.9% MRR of FDA-approved drugs, while pure LLMs achieve high MRR (97.5%) but poor binding affinity. CIDD aims to close this gap by unifying 3D interaction modeling with LLM-based chemical reasoning.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D protein pocket as input. The SBIG component (e.g., MolCRAFT) generates an intermediate 3D interaction-focused molecular scaffold, which is then re-docked into the pocket and fragmented via BRICS to extract non-covalent interaction profiles. The LEDD component outputs a final 2D molecular structure (not a 3D conformation) that is chemically valid, synthetically accessible, and preserves key binding interactions. Ten molecules are generated per pocket, with the Selection Module picking the best candidate based on interaction quality and chemical viability.

## Method

CIDD decomposes generation into two stages: a 3D generative model (SBIG) produces interaction-oriented intermediates, and an LLM pipeline (LEDD) refines them into drug-like molecules through four specialized modules. The Interaction Analysis Module docks the molecule, fragments it via BRICS, detects non-covalent interactions (H-bonds, π-stacking, salt bridges), and uses LLM_I to produce a structured fragment-level interaction profile. The Design Module uses LLM_D with a four-stage Chain-of-Thought prompt—identifying interaction-critical fragments, flagging unfavorable substructures, planning strategic modifications, and generating candidate molecules—guided by medicinal chemistry priors. The Reflection Module (LLM_R) compares successive designs against the original scaffold and provides feedback, while the Selection Module (LLM_S) ranks all N candidates by balancing interaction quality and chemical coherence. This modular, CoT-guided architecture mirrors real-world medicinal chemistry workflows and enables interpretable, stepwise molecular design without requiring supervised training data for the interaction-to-drug mapping.

## Results

On CrossDocked2020, CIDD achieves the best score across all evaluated metrics: Vina docking score of −8.496, QED of 0.576, SA of 0.735, MRR of 81.74%, success ratio of 34.59%, and QikProp pass ratio of 35.22%. The headline improvement is the success ratio (molecules satisfying Vina < −8.18, QED > 0.25, SA > 0.59), which more than doubles from 15.72% (DecompDiff, the best prior baseline) to 34.59%. MRR shows the most dramatic gain, rising from 37.81% (TargetDiff) to 81.74%, approaching the 85.9% observed in FDA-approved drugs. CIDD also generates larger molecules (mean MW 336.70) while maintaining drug-likeness, demonstrating that its gains are not an artifact of producing trivially small structures.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CIDD outperforms all nine baselines on every metric except SA, where Pocket2Mol edges ahead (0.758 vs. 0.735). Against the strongest prior baseline DecompDiff, CIDD improves Vina from −8.260 to −8.496, QED from 0.444 to 0.576, MRR from 62.60% to 81.74%, and success ratio from 15.72% to 34.59%. Compared to its own SBIG backbone MolCRAFT, CIDD lifts MRR by 23.3 percentage points (58.47%→81.74%) and success ratio by 20.9 points (13.72%→34.59%), confirming the LLM refinement stage drives most of the gain. Gradient-guided TAGMol improves QED over TargetDiff (0.563 vs. 0.474) but still lags CIDD on MRR (37.31% vs. 81.74%) and success ratio (3.23% vs. 34.59%), illustrating that predictor-guided optimization overfits individual scoring functions rather than producing broadly drug-like molecules. IPDiff, which uses interaction-guided sampling, achieves a competitive Vina score (−7.745) but the worst MRR among diffusion models (29.83%), reinforcing that binding-affinity-focused guidance does not ensure structural reasonability.

## Relation to Prior Work

CIDD carves new ground by repurposing existing 3D SBDD generative models (MolCRAFT, TargetDiff, DecompDiff, etc.) as 'interaction scaffold generators' and layering an LLM-based Chain-of-Thought design pipeline on top, rather than modifying the 3D model itself. This departs from gradient-guided approaches like TAGMol that optimize within the generative process, and from pure-LLM molecular editing (e.g., ChatDrug) that lacks spatial reasoning. The proposed MRR metric also breaks from the QED/Lipinski paradigm by targeting a specific structural failure mode (inconsistent ring hybridization) that prior evaluation frameworks miss entirely.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The MRR metric exposes a previously underappreciated gap: even state-of-the-art 3D SBDD models produce molecules that are structurally implausible by medicinal-chemistry standards, despite competitive docking scores. CIDD's plug-and-play architecture (works with any 3D SBDD backbone) and its interpretable, modular CoT design process make it a practical template for integrating domain-knowledge LLMs into geometric generation pipelines. Key open questions include whether the LLM's 'chemical knowledge' generalizes beyond the training distribution, whether the MRR metric is too narrow to capture all structural pathologies, and whether the computational improvements translate to wet-lab-validated candidates. The framework's dependence on proprietary LLM APIs and its lack of experimental validation remain significant limitations.
