## Problem

Diffusion and flow matching models have a well-explored design space that limits further improvements, while continuous autoregressive generation has not yet matched non-causal methods in quality. The paper introduces Transition Matching (TM), a discrete-time, continuous-state generative paradigm that unifies and generalizes both diffusion/flow and continuous AR generation by allowing expressive non-deterministic transition kernels and arbitrary non-continuous supervision processes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254144/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes text prompts of 1–128 tokens (embedded via a Flan-UL2 encoder) and produces 256×256×3 RGB images. Images are encoded into a 32×32×4 latent space via SDXL-VAE, and all generation occurs in this latent space. Text conditioning is injected through cross-attention layers in the DiT architecture or as a prefix token in the LLM architecture variant. The output is a sequence of 256 latent tokens (each 2×2×4) that can be decoded back to pixel space.

## Method

Transition Matching frames generation as learning a Markov process with probability transition kernels p_{t+1|t} between consecutive states, trained by matching the kernel to a supervising process. Three variants explore different design choices: (i) DTM uses the linear (Cond-OT) process and directly learns the difference Y = X_T − X_0 as a stochastic kernel, generalizing flow matching to discrete time with a backbone-head architecture that generates all tokens in parallel per step; (ii) ARTM uses an independent linear process (each noise sample drawn independently per timestep) with an autoregressive kernel that generates tokens sequentially within each transition; (iii) FHTM conditions on the full history of states X_0,…,X_t, enabling fully causal generation compatible with standard LLM architectures. All variants use flow matching loss for kernel modeling with a 1.7B DiT backbone and a 40M-parameter MLP head, and DTM provably converges to Euler-step flow matching as the number of transition steps grows.

## Results

On PartiPrompts, DTM achieves the best overall performance with only 32 backbone NFE (vs. 256 for FM), scoring ImageReward 0.53 (vs. 0.23 for FM), UnifiedReward 5.12 (vs. 4.78), Aesthetic 5.42 (vs. 5.29), and DeQA 2.65 (vs. 2.55), while matching MAR's CLIPScore of 26.8. FHTM-3 with an LLM architecture (the first fully causal model) matches or surpasses FM on all metrics (CLIPScore 27.0, PickScore 21.0, ImageReward 0.43, UnifiedReward 5.02, Aesthetic 5.30, DeQA 2.54). On GenEval, DTM leads with an overall score of 0.54 vs. 0.47 for FM and 0.52 for MAR. DTM also achieves a ~7× sampling speedup over FM (1.6s vs. 10.8s per image on H100) while maintaining or improving quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against FM (Esser et al. 2024), DTM wins on every metric while using only 32 backbone NFE versus FM's 256 — an 8× reduction in backbone forwards — with the largest gap on ImageReward (0.53 vs. 0.23) and UnifiedReward (5.12 vs. 4.78). Against MAR (Li et al. 2024), DTM matches CLIPScore (26.8 vs. 27.0) but substantially outperforms on image quality: Aesthetic 5.42 vs. 4.95, DeQA 2.65 vs. 2.36, and ImageReward 0.53 vs. 0.33. Against MAR-Fluid (Fan et al. 2024), DTM dominates across all metrics (e.g., ImageReward 0.53 vs. 0.07, UnifiedReward 5.12 vs. 3.82). Against discrete-token baselines (AR-discrete from Yu et al. 2022 and MAR-discrete from Chang et al. 2022), DTM leads on all quality and preference metrics. The fully causal FHTM-3 (LLM) matches or exceeds FM on all six PartiPrompts metrics, establishing it as the first fully causal model to reach flow-matching-level text-to-image quality in continuous domains. On GenEval, DTM achieves 0.54 overall vs. 0.47 (FM), 0.52 (MAR), and 0.44 (MAR-Fluid), with the largest gains on Position (0.20 vs. 0.12) and Color Attribute (0.46 vs. 0.34).

## Relation to Prior Work

Transition Matching (TM) unifies two previously separate lines of generative modeling—diffusion/flow matching and continuous autoregressive generation—under a single discrete-time Markov transition framework. DTM generalizes flow matching (Lipman et al. 2022; Liu et al. 2022) by replacing the deterministic Euler step with a learned stochastic transition kernel, while ARTM/FHTM extend the single-step continuous AR approach of Li et al. (2024) to multi-step generation with a novel independent linear supervising process. The paper also shows that standard diffusion models (ε-prediction, x-prediction) are special cases of TM, positioning the framework as a strict generalization of both paradigms. The key conceptual departure from prior work is the explicit separation of three design axes—supervising process, kernel parameterization, and kernel modeling—that had been conflated in diffusion and flow matching literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: DTM achieves a ~7× sampling speedup over flow matching while improving image quality and text adherence, and FHTM becomes the first fully causal model to match or surpass flow-based methods on text-to-image in continuous domains, opening a path toward seamless integration with LLM-based multimodal systems. The controlled large-scale comparison (fixed 1.7B DiT architecture, 350M training pairs, identical hyperparameters) provides a rare apples-to-apples benchmark across 12 methods and 27 metrics. Open questions include whether the speed advantage of DTM persists at higher resolutions, whether FHTM can be effectively scaled within a full multimodal AR pipeline, and how different time schedulers or distillation strategies might further close the gap between causal and non-causal generation quality.
