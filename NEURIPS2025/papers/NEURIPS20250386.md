## Problem

Association reasoning—a cornerstone of human cognition underlying creative thinking and knowledge integration—remains largely unexplored in LVLM evaluation. Existing benchmarks like The Labyrinth of Links rely on closed-ended, multiple-choice formats that introduce option bias and cannot capture the complexity of open-ended, multi-step associative reasoning required for real-world applications such as scientific discovery and creative ideation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250386/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The RIA task presents a pair of seemingly unrelated images (or text-image pairs) and requires the model to produce a free-form response identifying a meaningful conceptual bridge, while the ICA task presents four images where three establish a relational pattern and the model must abstract and transfer that pattern to generate the appropriate complement for the fourth. Evaluation yields two complementary score sets: a holistic 0–4 cascading rubric score (aggregated as SR, HR-4, HR-3, and ΔHR to capture convergent accuracy and divergent creativity) and a per-step process-reward score decomposing each reasoning hop into Reasonableness, Distinctiveness, and Knowledgeability. Reference answers serve as heuristic quality benchmarks rather than rigid correctness criteria, since multiple valid associative explanations may exist.

## Method

MM-OPERA introduces 11,497 open-ended instances across two tasks—Remote-Item Association (RIA) and In-Context Association (ICA)—spanning 13 hierarchical ability dimensions, 3 relationship types, 15 languages, and 22 topic domains. For evaluation, the paper designs a dual LLM-as-a-Judge framework: a Regular Scoring engine applies a unified 0–4 cascading rubric prioritizing depth, coherence, and insight over mere correctness, while a Process-Reward Judge (PR-Judge) reformats model responses into structured association paths and scores each hop via s_t = αR_tD_t + (1−α)K_t, then aggregates with a cognitive decay factor δ^t to favor efficient reasoning chains. A dual-judge protocol (GPT-4o and DeepSeek-V3) mitigates self-enhancement bias, and the framework is validated against 8 human evaluators with strong alignment (r = 0.72 for Reasonableness, 83.5% accuracy for Knowledgeability). The benchmark is further probed through sensitivity analyses (multi-image substitution, text-image substitution, order sensitivity) and multi-dimensional breakdowns across ability levels, hop counts, relationship types, domains, and languages.

## Results

On the RIA task, the best proprietary model (o4-mini) achieves a Score Rate of 60.33% and HR-4 of 19.86%, while the human baseline reaches 61.88% SR and 22.84% HR-4. On the more challenging ICA task, the gap widens substantially: Gemini-2.5-Pro-Preview attains 63.09% SR and 12.85% HR-4 versus the human baseline of 68.69% SR and 31.65% HR-4. Open-source models trail significantly, with the best (Yi-VL-34B) reaching only 45.25% SR on RIA and 54.39% SR on ICA. The ΔHR metric (divergent thinking) shows humans at 26.13% (RIA) and 29.82% (ICA), while the best LVLMs reach 18.87% and 28.30% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

As a benchmark paper, MM-OPERA evaluates 16 LVLMs against a human baseline rather than proposing a new method. Among proprietary models, Gemini-2.5-Pro-Preview leads on RIA HR-4 (23.89%) and ICA SR (63.09%), while o4-mini achieves the highest RIA SR (60.33%); both remain 2–19 points below the human baseline on HR-4. GPT-4o (59.72% RIA SR, 58.26% ICA SR) and Gemini-2.0-Flash-Thinking (59.11%, 61.42%) cluster closely behind, but all lag humans by 10–20 points on HR-4 for ICA. Open-source models are substantially weaker: the best (Yi-VL-34B at 45.25% RIA SR, 54.39% ICA SR) still trails the top proprietary models by 15+ points on RIA, and GLM-4V (26.92% RIA SR) is the weakest overall. Notably, Gemini-1.5-Flash outperforms the larger Gemini-1.5-Pro on RIA SR (55.86% vs. 45.34%), revealing that conservative factuality constraints in Pro suppress associative flexibility. On ICA, most models underperform relative to RIA, but exceptions like Yi-VL-34B and GLM-4V show relatively stronger ICA performance, suggesting architecture-dependent pattern-transfer strengths.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Remote Associates Test (RAT) paradigm from cognitive psychology into the LVLM evaluation setting, departing from The Labyrinth of Links (Li et al., 2024) by replacing closed-ended multi-choice association with open-ended, multi-hop, multimodal free-form reasoning. It introduces two novel task formulations—Remote-Item Association (RIA) and In-Context Association (ICA)—that go beyond the single-hop, adjective-verb scope of prior work. The evaluation methodology draws on process-reward models (Lightman et al., 2023; Wang et al., 2023) to score intermediate reasoning steps, adapting this idea from math reasoning to associative cognition via a stepwise Reasonableness-Distinctiveness-Knowledgeability rubric inspired by Guilford's divergent thinking framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals a persistent and quantifiable gap between state-of-the-art LVLMs and humans on association reasoning, with even o4-mini and Gemini-2.5-Pro-Preview falling well short on high-score rates, particularly on ICA where pattern abstraction and transfer are required. The finding that conservative, factuality-oriented models (Gemini-1.5-Pro) underperform speed-optimized variants (Gemini-1.5-Flash) on creative association tasks highlights a tension between reliability and associative flexibility that is underappreciated in current model design. Open questions include whether the 'distinctiveness gap'—models producing plausible but conceptually vague connections—can be addressed through training, and whether the process-reward evaluation framework generalizes to other open-ended reasoning benchmarks beyond association.
