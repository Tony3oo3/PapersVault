## Problem

Graph classification has long assumed a closed-world setting where all test categories are known during training, but real-world scenarios (e.g., novel protein structures, unseen malware behaviors) require discovering entirely new graph categories without any supervision from those classes. While Novel Category Discovery (NCD) is well-studied for images and node-level graphs, graph-level NCD (GLNCD) remained unexplored. Moreover, straightforward adaptation of image NCD methods to graphs suffers significant performance degradation because existing graph SSL and ranking-statistics pseudo-labeling both fail to exploit graph structural information.

## Input / Output

The method consumes attributed graphs of varying sizes (node feature matrices, edge feature matrices, and adjacency matrices), preprocessed with Random Walk Structural Encoding (RWSE) at max path length 32. A key design constraint is that the K learnable prototype graphs must have uniform node count, since the differentiable BAPG layer requires the second group of graphs to be size-homogeneous for parallel computation. The output is dual: a class prediction for labeled old-category samples (Old ACC) and a cluster assignment for unlabeled novel-category samples (New ACC), evaluated jointly in a single training process rather than the multi-stage pipeline of AutoNovel.

## Method

ProtoFGW-NCD replaces the Euclidean-vector-based SSL and pseudo-labeling of AutoNovel with structure-aware Fused Gromov-Wasserstein (FGW) distance computations against K learnable prototype graphs of uniform size. ProtoFGW-CL computes FGW distance vectors between each graph (and its node-dropped augmentation) and the prototypes, then enforces cross-view consistency of these FGW codes via a symmetric cross-entropy contrastive loss. FGW-RS feeds the same FGW codes into classic ranking statistics to generate pairwise binary pseudo-labels that now encode structural relationships, training the novel-class head with binary cross-entropy. The critical enabling component is a parallel, differentiable BAPG layer that solves b₁×b₂ FGW optimal-transport problems simultaneously on GPU by arranging variable-sized sparse graphs into a block-diagonal giant matrix and broadcasting over dense prototype graphs, achieving up to 2070× speedup over the POT library's nested-loop implementation. The entire pipeline—SSL, supervised old-class learning, and novel-class discovery—runs in a single unified training loop with three loss terms (L_CE, L_BCE, L_SSL), and the prototype graphs are updated via backpropagation through the BAPG layer to capture representative structural patterns.

## Results

With a GIN+ encoder, ProtoFGW-NCD achieves the best average rank (2.25) across four GLNCD datasets, with standout New ACC of 74.51% on MalNet-Tiny and 44.04% on CIFAR-10 (graph). With a GCN+ encoder, the advantage is more pronounced: ProtoFGW-NCD attains the best average rank (1.625) and leads on Old ACC for three of four datasets (ENZYMES 72.17%, MalNet-Tiny 80.95%, REDDIT12K 69.43%) and New ACC for three of four (ENZYMES 44.84%, MalNet-Tiny 63.35%, REDDIT12K 40.81%). The largest single-dataset gain over the strongest baseline is +5.4 points on MalNet-Tiny New ACC (74.51% vs. 69.76% for NCL) with GIN+.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ProtoFGW-NCD is compared against three adapted visual NCD baselines (AutoNovel, NCL, DualRS) and K-means on four graph datasets. The most consistent wins are on New ACC for MalNet-Tiny, where ProtoFGW-NCD reaches 74.51% (GIN+) / 63.35% (GCN+), surpassing NCL (69.76% / 62.23%), AutoNovel (68.93% / 62.43%), and DualRS (69.09% / 49.53%). On CIFAR-10 (graph), ProtoFGW-NCD leads in New ACC (44.04% GIN+, 38.92% GCN+) over AutoNovel (41.67% / 41.26%) and NCL (38.21% / 39.64%), though it trails NCL on Old ACC (56.44% vs. 67.54% GIN+). On ENZYMES, the method is competitive but does not always lead: NCL achieves the best New ACC (45.81%) with GIN+, while ProtoFGW-NCD wins with GCN+ (44.84% vs. 39.71% for NCL). On REDDIT12K, all methods cluster within ~3 points on New ACC, with ProtoFGW-NCD marginally ahead (39.94% GIN+, 40.81% GCN+). Overall, ProtoFGW-NCD achieves the best average rank in both encoder settings, confirming that structure-aware FGW-based comparison provides a consistent but moderate advantage over Euclidean-readout baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the visual Novel Category Discovery (NCD) pipeline—originally built on AutoNovel's ranking-statistics pseudo-labeling and contrastive SSL—to the graph domain, but identifies that straightforward adaptation (swapping CNN for GNN, RotNet for GraphCL) fails due to insufficient exploitation of graph structure in both the SSL and pseudo-labeling stages. The key methodological departure is replacing Euclidean comparisons on readout vectors with Fused Gromov-Wasserstein (FGW) distances computed via a novel parallel differentiable BAPG layer, using learnable prototype graphs as computational intermediaries. The FGW formulation draws on Vayer et al.'s optimal transport for structured data and Li et al.'s BAPG solver, while the overall NCD architecture mirrors AutoNovel's three-stage design (SSL → supervised → discovery) collapsed into a single training loop.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By formalizing GLNCD as a distinct task and providing four cross-domain benchmarks (bioinformatics, program analysis, social networks, graph-based vision), this paper opens a previously unexplored direction in open-world graph learning that prior work has addressed only at the node level. The diagnosis that ranking-statistics pseudo-labels are fundamentally unsuited to graph data—even when encoder representations improve—challenges the assumption that better embeddings automatically yield better pseudo-labels, and points toward structure-aware comparison as a necessary ingredient. However, FGW-based computation remains expensive relative to Euclidean operations, and the paper itself notes that more efficient structural comparison methods are needed for large-scale GLNCD; the modest performance gains over baselines (best average rank but not dominant on every dataset) suggest the approach is a proof-of-concept rather than a definitive solution.
