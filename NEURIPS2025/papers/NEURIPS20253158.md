## Problem

Existing MLLMs for ECG interpretation suffer from two critical gaps: they process only a single non-text modality (either raw time series or rendered images), missing the complementary strengths that clinicians exploit when jointly analyzing dynamic signal trends and spatial waveform patterns; and they provide limited explainability, failing to explicitly link diagnostic conclusions to granular, measurable waveform evidence such as per-heartbeat QRS or PR intervals.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253158/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

GEM consumes three modalities simultaneously: a 12-lead ECG time series (C×L), a corresponding 12-lead ECG image (H×W×3, rendered via ECG-image-kit), and a free-form text query. The output is a ~300-word natural-language interpretation that explicitly cites lead-specific, heartbeat-level physiological measurements (e.g., 'QRS duration of 134 ms in Lead I') as evidence for each diagnostic claim, structured to mirror a clinician's step-by-step reasoning from rhythm assessment through lead-by-lead analysis to final diagnosis.

## Method

GEM employs a dual-encoder architecture using a pre-trained ECG-CoCa model for time series and a CLIP encoder (from LLaVA) for images, whose outputs are aligned via a two-stage projection: an MLP first maps time-series features to image-feature dimensionality, then a shared MLP maps both modalities into the LLM's text embedding space, where they are concatenated with the query embedding for next-token prediction. A key data-side innovation is knowledge-guided instruction data generation: a parameter-free FeatureDB extracts 14 physiological feature sequences (heart rate, RR intervals, P/QRS/T amplitudes and durations, ST/QT intervals, etc.) per heartbeat across all 12 leads, and a cardiology-specific Diagnosis Guider constructs tailored prompts that activate GPT-4o's latent medical knowledge to produce 30,000 heartbeat-level grounded instruction pairs (ECG-Grounding) without manual annotation. Training is a single-step SFT (one epoch on 8 A100 GPUs) that freezes both encoders and jointly optimizes the two projectors and the LLM, applied to two base models—unmodified LLaVA-7B and the ECG-pretrained PULSE—demonstrating that the multimodal grounding framework transfers effectively regardless of prior ECG exposure.

## Results

On the novel Grounded ECG Understanding task, GEM (SFT LLaVA) achieves 87.24% Diagnosis Accuracy on in-domain MIMIC-IV-ECG (vs. 81.14% for PULSE) and 73.53% on out-domain PTB-XL (vs. 59.24% for PULSE), a 14.3-point gap. ECG Feature Grounding improves from ~50 (PULSE) to ~75 (GEM), and Lead Assessment Coverage jumps from 7.11% to 71.07% on MIMIC-IV-ECG. On ECG-Bench abnormality detection, GEM (SFT LLaVA) reaches 92.6% accuracy on CSN (vs. 85.2% PULSE, +7.4%) and 81.8% on G12EC (vs. 78.2%), while report generation scores 65.0/100 on PTB-XL (vs. 61.3 for PULSE).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GEM is compared against PULSE (Liu et al., 2024), the state-of-the-art ECG MLLM trained on 1M+ ECG image instructions, and GPT-4o as a general-purpose LLM baseline. Against PULSE, GEM wins decisively on all Grounded ECG Understanding metrics: Diagnosis Accuracy improves by 6.1 points in-domain and 14.3 points out-domain, ECG Feature Grounding rises by ~25 points (50→75), and Lead Assessment Coverage increases by ~64 points (7→71). On ECG-Bench abnormality detection, GEM (SFT LLaVA) outperforms PULSE on CSN (+7.4% ACC) and G12EC (+3.6% ACC), while GEM (SFT PULSE) leads on PTB-XL Super F1 (75.8 vs. 74.8) and CODE-15% F1 (86.4 vs. 85.4). GPT-4o performs poorly on ECG-specific tasks (e.g., 57.5% CSN accuracy, 50.2/100 report score), confirming that domain-specific multimodal training is essential. Notably, GEM (SFT LLaVA), trained from scratch on ECG data for only one epoch, already matches or exceeds PULSE on several detection benchmarks, underscoring the effectiveness of the dual-encoder multimodal design.

## Relation to Prior Work

GEM extends the multimodal LLM paradigm for ECG by unifying two previously isolated non-text modalities: raw 12-lead time series (via the ECG-CoCa contrastive encoder) and rendered ECG images (via CLIP/LLaVA), whereas prior ECG MLLMs like PULSE and ECG-Chat process only one of these. The cross-modal alignment strategy follows LLaVA's projector-based mapping to text embedding space, but introduces a two-stage projection (dimensional alignment followed by shared text-space mapping) to handle the architectural mismatch between time series and image encoders. The knowledge-guided instruction data generation departs from PULSE's report-derived ECG-Instruct by using a grounding feature extractor and a cardiology-specific diagnosis guider to prompt GPT-4o with heartbeat-level physiological sequences, producing feature-grounded interpretations rather than report paraphrases.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine gap in medical AI: existing ECG MLLMs lack the ability to explicitly link diagnoses to measurable waveform parameters (e.g., QRS duration per heartbeat), which is essential for clinician trust. The ECG-Grounding dataset (30K samples) and the Grounded ECG Understanding benchmark provide reusable resources for the community. Key limitations include GPT-4o's occasional misalignment with cardiologist interpretations in generated training data, the model's tendency to overstate severity without patient context, and the reliance on a single-epoch SFT protocol whose robustness across architectures remains untested. Open questions include whether human-in-the-loop feedback can close the remaining gap with expert judgment and whether the grounding approach generalizes to other physiological time series beyond ECG.
