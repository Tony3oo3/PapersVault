## Problem

Large Reasoning Models produce intermediate thinking drafts before generating final answers, but it remains unclear whether these drafts faithfully reflect the model's actual computation. Prior faithfulness evaluations for LRMs rely on input-level manipulations (e.g., inserting hints into prompts) and observe correlations with final answers, but they do not test whether individual reasoning steps causally drive the draft conclusion or whether the answer stage genuinely depends on the draft rather than performing independent reasoning. This gap undermines the reliability of monitoring and controlling LRM reasoning through their verbalized drafts.

## Input / Output

The framework takes a multiple-choice task prompt, a thinking draft (sourced from DeepSeek-R1, Qwen3-32B, or the evaluated model itself), and applies targeted counterfactual interventions—either inserting a misleading reasoning step at a specific position within the draft or modifying the draft's concluding statement. Outputs are binary faithfulness indicators aggregated into rates: an intra-draft faithfulness score (whether the draft conclusion correctly integrates or rejects the inserted step), a draft-reliance rate (whether the answer is identical under standard vs. immediate answering), and a draft-answer consistency rate (whether the final answer matches the modified draft conclusion). An LLM-based classifier (Qwen2.5-Instruct-32B) labels each model response as Explicit Correction or Step Following, with Cohen's κ of 0.83 for step labeling and 0.62 for behavior classification validated against human annotators.

## Method

The paper introduces a two-dimensional counterfactual intervention framework. For Intra-Draft Faithfulness, it inserts counterfactual steps targeting restating moments in the draft (where the model re-enumerates options), using two intervention types—Shift Mapping (reassigning option labels) and Corrupt Option (altering the selected choice's content)—each constructed as either a CONTINUE step (mistaken forward reasoning) or a BACKTRACK step (explicit revision). The model continues generating the draft, and an LLM judge classifies whether it explicitly corrects or follows the insertion; faithfulness is scored by checking if the draft conclusion changes according to a predefined mapping φ. For Draft-to-Answer Faithfulness, it appends a counterfactual conclusion to the draft (either a Direct Alternation with an unsupported answer or a Plausible Alternation with a GPT-4o-mini-generated coherent justification) and measures two sub-metrics: Draft Reliance (consistency between standard and immediate answering) and Draft-Answer Consistency (alignment between the final answer and the modified draft conclusion). The framework is evaluated across six models (R1-7B/8B/14B/32B, QwQ-32B, OR1-32B) on GPQA Diamond and MMLU Global Facts, using both self-generated and externally sourced drafts to ensure cross-model generalizability.

## Results

On Intra-Draft Faithfulness (Table 1), average faithfulness rates range from 45.53% (R1-7B, GPQA) to 68.22% (OR1, MMLU), with BACKTRACK-step interventions consistently outperforming CONTINUE-step interventions by 10–20 percentage points. On Draft-to-Answer Faithfulness, Draft Reliance reaches up to 94.54% (R1-32B, MMLU) but drops to 48.65% (R1-7B, GPQA), while Draft-Answer Consistency is dramatically lower for RLVR-tuned models: QwQ achieves only 19.54% (GPQA) and 13.39% (MMLU), and OR1 achieves 29.94% (GPQA) and 49.63% (MMLU), compared to 40.82% (R1-32B, GPQA) and 81.75% (R1-32B, MMLU). Immediate answering (suppressing answer-stage explanation) consistently improves Draft-Answer Consistency over standard answering, confirming the answer stage introduces new reasoning that deviates from the draft conclusion.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's sole external comparison is with Chen et al. [6], which evaluates faithfulness via counterfactual simulatability (inserting hints into the user prompt and checking whether the model verbalizes them in the thinking draft). On the same GPQA and MMLU traces, Chen et al.'s metric shows RLVR-tuned models (OR1-32B: 48.4% GPQA, 72.5% MMLU) achieving *higher* faithfulness than distilled counterparts (R1-32B: 44.4% GPQA, 58.3% MMLU). In stark contrast, this paper's intervention-based Draft-Answer Consistency metric shows RLVR-tuned models achieving *lower* faithfulness (QwQ: 19.54% GPQA, 13.39% MMLU; OR1: 29.94% GPQA, 49.63% MMLU vs. R1-32B: 40.82% GPQA, 81.75% MMLU). The authors argue the two metrics are complementary: Chen et al. captures whether models verbalize provided hints, while this paper's metrics verify whether the final answer causally depends on the draft conclusion. This divergence highlights that RLVR tuning strengthens latent answer-stage computation, making models less responsive to explicit draft guidance even when they appear to 'follow' prompt-level hints.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the counterfactual editing paradigm for CoT faithfulness (Lanham et al. 2023; Yee et al. 2024) from single forward reasoning trajectories to the non-linear, multi-path thinking drafts of Large Reasoning Models. It departs from concurrent LRM faithfulness work (Chen et al. 2025; Arcuschin et al. 2025; Marjanović et al. 2025), which relies on input-level manipulations such as prompt-hint insertion or option rearrangement, by directly intervening on the draft's internal structure—inserting counterfactual steps at specific positions and modifying the draft's concluding logic. The two-dimensional decomposition (Intra-Draft vs. Draft-to-Answer Faithfulness) is novel relative to prior simulatability-based metrics, which conflate whether a model verbalizes a hint with whether its final answer causally depends on the draft.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The findings that the answer stage introduces substantial new reasoning beyond the draft and that RLVR-tuned models exhibit *lower* draft-answer consistency than their distilled counterparts challenge the assumption that thinking drafts are reliable proxies for internal computation, with direct implications for monitoring and control pipelines. The paper's framework provides a reusable benchmark for future LRM evaluations, but open questions remain: whether faithfulness can be explicitly targeted via RLVR reward shaping, how faithfulness correlates with downstream safety-critical capabilities, and whether the counterfactual intervention approach generalizes to open-ended (non-multiple-choice) reasoning. The evaluation is limited to multiple-choice tasks and a small set of model families, and the absence of error bars or multiple seeds limits statistical confidence in the reported differences.
