## Problem

Video LLMs incur substantial inference costs from the high volume of video tokens, yet existing pruning methods fail to exploit spatiotemporal redundancy effectively. Spatial-only compression (e.g., FastV, VisionZip) ignores temporal dependencies across frames, while static segmentation strategies either group visually dissimilar frames together or disrupt temporal order. No prior method simultaneously preserves temporal structure and visual context under aggressive token reduction.

## Input / Output

The method takes a video uniformly sampled into F frames (32 for LLaVA-OneVision, 64 for LLaVA-Video, up to 768 for Qwen-VL series) and a text query. Each frame is encoded by the vision encoder into a token grid, which is then projected and pooled into a video token sequence. FastVID compresses this sequence to a retention ratio r (e.g., 9.7%–25%) before the LLM prefill stage, producing a reduced token set that is concatenated with query tokens for generation. The method is plug-and-play at the prefill stage, compatible with FlashAttention, KV cache reuse across multi-turn conversations, and preserves RoPE/M-RoPE positional encodings of retained tokens.

## Method

FastVID is a training-free, inference-time pruning framework with two stages. First, Dynamic Temporal Segmentation (DySeg) computes cosine transition similarities between adjacent frame features and adaptively selects segment boundaries as the union of the c−1 most dissimilar transitions and all transitions below a threshold τ, yielding temporally ordered segments with high intra-segment similarity. Second, Density Spatiotemporal Pruning (STPrune) operates within each segment via two complementary modules: Density-based Token Merging (DTM) selects density-peak tokens as anchors from anchor frames (sampled at interval p), assigns remaining tokens to the nearest anchor by cosine similarity, and merges them via anchor-centric weighted aggregation (β·anchor + (1−β)·mean) while preserving original positional information; and Attention-based Token Selection (ATS) reintegrates a lightweight pretrained SigLIP head to extract [CLS] attention scores and selects the top salient tokens per frame. A hyperparameter d controls the token allocation between DTM (segment visual context) and ATS (salient details), with d=0.4 yielding optimal performance.

## Results

On LLaVA-OneVision-7B, FastVID prunes 90.3% of video tokens (9.7% retention), reducing FLOPs to 8.3% (4.04 vs. 48.82 TFLOPs) and accelerating the LLM prefill stage by 7.1×, while maintaining 98.0% of the original average accuracy (58.1 vs. 59.3). At 25% retention, FastVID retains 99.0% accuracy; at 14.8%, it retains 98.3%. Across LLaVA-Video, Qwen2-VL, and Qwen2.5-VL, FastVID consistently outperforms all baselines at comparable compression ratios, achieving 97.5–98.1% accuracy on LLaVA-Video and 93.3–96.2% on the Qwen-VL series.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FastVID outperforms all seven baselines across every retention ratio tested. Against the strongest image-compression baseline VisionZip* (which applies pruning after pooling), FastVID leads by 8.4 percentage points in average accuracy at ~10% retention (98.0% vs. 89.6%) and by 2.7 points at 14.8% (98.3% vs. 95.6%). Against the video-specific baseline PruneVID*, FastVID gains 2.6 points at ~10% retention (98.0% vs. 95.4%) while also being 6.1× faster in compression (5.6 ms vs. 37.2 ms). DyCoke, which retains all tokens from the first frame of each 4-frame segment, drops to 86.2% at 25% retention versus FastVID's 99.0%. FastV, which prunes inside the LLM using attention scores, degrades sharply under extreme compression (90.2% at 10% vs. FastVID's 98.0%). On the Qwen-VL series, FastVID beats PruneVID* by 3.5 points on Qwen2.5-VL (93.3% vs. 88.1%) and by 6.8 points on Qwen2-VL (96.2% vs. 89.4%). In additional comparisons (Table 11), FastVID surpasses PyramidDrop (57.3 vs. 51.8 on VideoMME) and SparseVLM (57.3 vs. 53.3) at substantially lower FLOPs (4.04 vs. 14.70 and 7.06 TFLOPs respectively), and outperforms LLaVA-PruMerge by 7.4 points (57.3 vs. 49.9) at the same 9.7% token budget.

## Relation to Prior Work

FastVID extends the token compression line of work (FastV, VisionZip, ToMe) from spatial-only redundancy reduction to explicit spatiotemporal redundancy exploitation in Video LLMs. Its core mechanism—selecting density peak tokens as anchors—directly adapts the density peaks clustering algorithm (Rodriguez & Laio, 2014) to the token selection problem, departing from the content-agnostic uniform sampling used by VisionZip. For video segmentation, it replaces the clustering-based approach of PruneVID with a simpler transition-similarity criterion that preserves temporal order, and unlike cluster-based merging methods, it retains positional information of merged tokens to maintain RoPE/M-RoPE compatibility.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 7.1× prefill speedup at 98% accuracy retention across four architecturally distinct Video LLMs (LLaVA-OneVision, LLaVA-Video, Qwen2-VL, Qwen2.5-VL) makes Video LLM deployment practical on consumer hardware, and the query-agnostic design enables KV cache reuse across multi-turn conversations—a property query-aware methods lack. The main open question is whether query-guided keyframe selection can close the remaining accuracy gap on long-frame models (768 frames) where aggressive pruning discards query-relevant content. The length extrapolation result (more frames + compression > fewer frames + no compression) suggests a promising direction for scaling video context without proportional compute growth.
