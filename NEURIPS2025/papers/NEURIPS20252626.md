## Problem

Existing time-series generative models (VAEs, GANs, diffusion) require task-specific training from scratch, struggle with long-term dependencies and multivariate coupling, and cannot leverage pretrained foundation models. Adapting LLMs to time-series generation is non-trivial because long temporal windows inflate inference costs and temporal/multivariate correlations demand structured modeling beyond what raw text encoding provides.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252626/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts univariate or multivariate time series (optionally a single long sequence that is split via periodicity-aware segmentation into I windows of length L) and optional textual conditions (e.g., channel identity). It produces synthetic time series of the same length and channel count as the input, with the key constraint that the embedding dimension k (typically 3–7 per channel) decouples computational cost from sequence length, enabling efficient generation of arbitrarily long signals.

## Method

SDForger projects each time-series channel onto a small set of basis functions (FastICA or FPC, typically k=3 components), yielding a compact tabular embedding whose dimension is independent of sequence length. These embedding rows are converted into structured text via a Fill-In-The-Middle template with random feature-order permutation to enforce order independence, then used to fine-tune a lightweight autoregressive LLM (GPT-2, 124M) with early stopping. At inference, the LLM generates new embedding rows via multinomial sampling, which are filtered online (missing values, duplicates, norm-based outlier rejection) and decoded back to the time-series domain by linear combination of the learned basis functions. The ICA variant outperforms FPC because independent, unordered components give the LLM a more balanced joint distribution to model, and the entire pipeline achieves 1–2 orders of magnitude faster generation than GAN or diffusion baselines.

## Results

In the multisample setting, SDF-ICA₃ achieves the best normalized distance-based score (0.074) and a competitive feature-based score (0.224), yielding an average rank of 3.143 across 7 models. In the univariate setting, SDF-ICA₃ attains the best overall rank (2.429) with normalized scores of 0.149 (feature) and 0.070 (distance). For downstream forecasting utility, SDF-ICA-generated data alone achieves the best average rank (2.00) among all synthetic-only methods, and combining it with original data (SDF-ICA+OD) yields the best RMSE on bikesharing (0.487) and etth1 (0.642), matching or exceeding original-data-only performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SDForger (SDF-ICA₃) does not dominate any single metric but achieves the most balanced profile across feature-based and distance-based evaluations. TimeVAE wins on feature-based metrics (normalized score 0.000 multisample) but lags on distance-based (0.094 vs. SDF-ICA₃'s 0.074), while TimeVQVAE excels on distance (0.000) but is weak on features (0.873). RtsGAN is competitive on both (0.231/0.058) and achieves a slightly better multisample rank (2.86 vs. 3.14), but degrades in the univariate setting (rank 4.00 vs. SDF-ICA₃'s 2.43). SdeGAN and LS4 are consistently the weakest, with normalized distance scores of 0.69–0.98 and average ranks of 5.1–6.1, indicating poor temporal fidelity. In the utility evaluation, SDF-ICA alone outperforms all other synthetic-only baselines (avg. rank 2.00 vs. TimeVQVAE 2.67, RtsGAN 4.67, SdeGAN 4.00, LS4 5.89), and SDF-ICA+OD matches or beats original-data-only performance on bikesharing RMSE (0.487 vs. 0.495) and etth1 RMSE (0.642 vs. 0.658).

## Relation to Prior Work

SDForger bridges two previously separate research threads: LLM-based tabular data generation (Borisov et al., 2022, from which it adopts the random feature-order permutation and fill-in-the-middle prompting) and functional data analysis (FastICA, FPC). Unlike prior LLM-for-time-series approaches (GPT4TS, Time-LLM, LLMTime) that encode raw numerical sequences as text tokens, SDForger first projects time series into a compact low-dimensional basis, decoupling generation cost from sequence length. It also departs from traditional generative models (TimeVAE, TimeVQVAE, RTSGAN, SDEGAN, LS4) by leveraging pretrained LLMs with minimal fine-tuning rather than training from scratch, while retaining the ability to condition generation on textual prompts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practical efficiency advantage: generation time scales with embedding dimension k rather than sequence length, and a 124M-parameter GPT-2 matches or exceeds 2B–3.8B models, making synthetic time series generation accessible on modest hardware. The text-conditioned generation capability (Section 6) opens a path toward multimodal time series synthesis where natural language guides structure and semantics. Key limitations include reliance on linear embeddings (FastICA/FPC), full fine-tuning rather than PEFT, and support only for autoregressive LLMs; extending to nonlinear embeddings, encoder-only architectures, and broader downstream tasks (classification, anomaly detection) are natural next steps.
