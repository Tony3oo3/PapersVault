## Problem

Orientation-rich images such as fingerprints, textures, and directional fields exhibit coherent angular patterns that are poorly captured by standard isotropic Euclidean diffusion models, which ignore the periodic geometry of angular data and destroy structural coherence too rapidly during forward noising. This paper asks whether biologically inspired synchronization dynamics (Kuramoto oscillator coupling) can serve as a structured inductive bias that preserves orientation coherence during diffusion and enables faster, more faithful generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250143/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

Input images (3×32×32 CIFAR-10, 1×96×96 SOCOFing, 1×32×32 Brodatz patches, 128×128 ground terrain) are linearly mapped from pixel range [-1,1] to phase range [-0.9π, 0.9π] to avoid boundary collapse under wrapping. For Navier-Stokes velocity fields, the input is decomposed into log-magnitude and phase channels, with the phase evolving under Kuramoto dynamics and the magnitude under a standard VP-SDE. The model outputs generated images or, in the conditional forecasting setting, predicted velocity fields given a two-step history, with all phase variables wrapped into [-π, π] after every SDE step.

## Method

The forward diffusion replaces the linear drift of standard SDEs with stochastic Kuramoto dynamics: each pixel's phase evolves under sinusoidal coupling to neighboring (local) or all (global) oscillators plus attraction to a fixed reference phase, with Gaussian noise, driving the distribution toward a low-entropy von Mises state. The reverse process desynchronizes phases using a learned score network that takes sinusoidal embeddings [sin(θ), cos(θ)] as input and outputs two Cartesian components projected back to the angular domain, ensuring circular-geometry consistency. Because the nonlinear drift makes the marginal score intractable, training uses denoising score matching with Monte Carlo samples (M=5) from a truncated wrapped Gaussian transition kernel (K=3 terms) to estimate local scores. The locally coupled variant restricts sinusoidal coupling to a 5×5 spatial neighborhood, introducing diffusion-like blurring that aligns with image spatial correlations while the reference phase still enforces global synchronization. Coupling strengths follow K_ref(t) > D_t > K(t) to balance structural preservation against noise injection, and the U-Net backbone with self-attention layers is shared with the SGM baseline for fair comparison.

## Results

On the Brodatz texture dataset, the locally coupled Kuramoto model achieves FID 18.47 at 100 steps, outperforming the SGM baseline's 1000-step FID of 20.37, demonstrating a 10× sampling efficiency gain. On SOCOFing fingerprints, the locally coupled variant reaches FID 67.49 at 100 steps versus SGM's 104.92. On CIFAR-10, the method achieves FID 28.17 at 100 steps (vs. SGM's 38.04) but trails SGM at 1000 steps (10.79 vs. 3.17), indicating the synchronization bias is most beneficial in low-step regimes and on orientation-dense data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is the standard VP-SDE score-based generative model (SGM) [60], compared across all image datasets. On orientation-dense benchmarks (Brodatz, SOCOFing, Ground Terrain), both Kuramoto variants consistently outperform SGM at every step count, with the locally coupled model achieving the largest margins (e.g., FID 18.47 vs. 38.33 at 100 steps on Brodatz). On CIFAR-10, Kuramoto wins at 100 steps (28.17 vs. 38.04) and 300 steps (24.86 vs. 25.76) but loses at 1000 steps (10.79 vs. 3.17), suggesting the synchronization inductive bias trades off global expressiveness for local coherence on general natural images. On Earth/climate spherical datasets (Table 8), the method achieves competitive NLL but does not clearly dominate: Riemannian flow matching [10] leads on Volcano (−7.93) and Fire (−1.86), while Riemannian diffusion [26] leads on Earthquake (−0.40). On Navier-Stokes velocity fields, the coupled Kuramoto + log-VP model reduces MSE from 0.0260 (SGM Cartesian) to 0.0188, confirming gains on intrinsically angular data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the Kuramoto model of coupled oscillator synchronization—a canonical framework from nonlinear dynamics—as the forward diffusion process in a score-based generative model, replacing the standard linear drift of VP/VE SDEs with nonlinear sinusoidal phase coupling on a periodic domain. It builds directly on the SDE-based score matching framework of Song et al. (ICLR 2021) and the denoising score matching training procedure of Vincent (2011), but departs from both by introducing a non-Gaussian, wrapped-Gaussian transition kernel and a quasi-equilibrium von Mises terminal distribution. The approach is conceptually distinct from Riemannian diffusion models (which handle non-Euclidean geometry but retain standard diffusion dynamics) and from blurring diffusion models (which use heat-equation drifts), positioning itself at the intersection of biologically-inspired synchronization dynamics and structured generative priors for angular data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that biologically-inspired synchronization dynamics can serve as effective structured inductive biases in generative modeling, yielding substantially better sample quality on orientation-rich data (fingerprints, textures, terrain) with fewer diffusion steps—100-step Kuramoto models match or exceed 1000-step SGM baselines on Brodatz textures. The hierarchical coarse-to-fine generation pathway (global coherence first, local details later) offers improved interpretability over standard diffusion. Key limitations include O(T) training cost from explicit forward-chain simulation (mitigated by pre-computation), degraded performance on general natural images at high step counts where the synchronization bias becomes restrictive, and the need for careful schedule tuning. Open questions include whether the framework extends to neural spiking data and whether the coupling mechanism can be made adaptive to data complexity.
