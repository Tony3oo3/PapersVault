## Problem

In current MLLMs, visual comprehension and generation remain largely independent capabilities that do not synergize, leaving the LLM's reasoning mechanisms unintegrated into image generation. The paper asks whether these two capabilities can be collaboratively co-evolved to enable a genuine, self-driven chain-of-thought for visual generation that triggers 'Aha moments'—iterative self-correction where the model reflects on its own outputs and regenerates improved images.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252007/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For text-to-image generation, the model takes a text prompt and autoregressively produces 576 VQ visual tokens per image, alternating between an image head (generation) and a text head (self-evaluation) for up to 3 rounds of generate-reflect-regenerate cycles. For image editing, it takes an input image (encoded via the generation encoder) plus a text editing instruction and outputs a modified image while preserving unedited regions. The model also functions as a semantic evaluator, taking an image-text pair and outputting a Yes/No consistency judgment with a natural-language rationale.

## Method

The method builds on Janus-Pro-7B (which uses separate SIGLIP and VQ-tokenizer encoders for understanding and generation) and introduces a two-stage training paradigm. Stage 1 (SFT) decomposes visual-generation CoT into three sub-tasks—text-to-image generation, self-evaluation of text-image consistency, and image regeneration—trained with mixed ratios (0.2/0.3/0.5) over 200K prompts with 18 generated images each, teaching the model to imitate an introspective reasoning chain. Stage 2 (RL) applies GRPO with a bi-level QA-based reward: a generation reward that weights the final image more heavily (T−K+1 multiplier) and a comprehension reward that penalizes disagreement between the model's self-assessment and an external InternVL2.5-26B evaluator. The key insight is that SFT alone only produces imitation, while RL with exploration-exploitation incentives transforms this into genuine reasoning, enabling the model to autonomously detect errors and trigger Aha moments for self-correction.

## Results

On GenEval, Janus-Pro-R1 (with Aha moments) achieves an overall score of 0.86, surpassing GPT-4o (0.85) and the backbone Janus-Pro-7B (0.80). On T2I-CompBench, it reaches an average of 85.57 (vs. 84.17 for Janus-Pro and 84.70 for Sana-1.5), and on DPG-Bench it scores 85.57 (vs. 84.17 for Janus-Pro). For image editing on PIE-Bench, Janus-Pro-R1-Edit achieves the best structure distance (35.87) and PSNR (22.81) among all baselines, outperforming the AR-based EditAR (39.43 / 21.32).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Janus-Pro-R1 outperforms GPT-4o on GenEval overall (0.86 vs. 0.85) and all diffusion-based baselines (Sana-1.5 at 0.81, SD3 at 0.74, FLUX at 0.66) as well as all MLLM-based baselines (Janus-Pro at 0.80, Infinity at 0.73, Show-o at 0.68). On T2I-CompBench, it consistently beats the concurrent CoT-based work T2I-R1 (85.57 avg vs. 81.3/58.5/72.4 sub-scores) and the backbone Janus-Pro (85.57 vs. 84.17), with the largest gain on the Color attribute sub-metric (83.4 vs. 63.6). On DPG-Bench, it edges out Sana-1.5 (85.57 vs. 84.70) and SD3 (85.57 vs. 84.08). For image editing on PIE-Bench, Janus-Pro-R1-Edit beats the strongest AR-based competitor EditAR on structure distance (35.87 vs. 39.43), PSNR (22.81 vs. 21.32), LPIPS (114.96 vs. 117.15), and MSE (123.30 vs. 130.27), while EditAR retains a slight edge on CLIP Whole similarity (24.87 vs. 24.78). The ablation without Aha moments (0.83 GenEval) still surpasses Janus-Pro (0.80), confirming that both the SFT/RL training and the introspective self-correction contribute independently to the gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Janus-Pro, a unified autoregressive MLLM for visual comprehension and generation, by introducing a two-stage training paradigm (SFT followed by GRPO-based RL) that explicitly collaborates these two capabilities rather than treating them as independent functions. It departs from prior CoT-in-generation work (MINT, GOT, T2I-R1) that impose forced textual planning, arguing instead that genuine CoT must emerge spontaneously from the model's self-driven reasoning, where comprehension and generation form an interleaved text-image chain. The RL stage directly adapts the GRPO framework from DeepSeekMath/DeepSeek-R1 to the visual generation domain, using a bi-level QA-based reward that incentivizes both image quality and self-evaluation accuracy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that RL can transform SFT's surface-level imitation of sub-skills into genuine introspective reasoning for visual generation, evidenced by the counterfactual generation experiment where only the RL-trained model correctly identifies and corrects semantic mismatches. A notable side effect is that the collaborative training enhances the model's visual comprehension, making it a competitive image semantic evaluator. However, the findings are limited to a single backbone architecture (Janus-Pro), the 1B variant fails to benefit (suggesting a scaling threshold), and the authors acknowledge that current experiments are insufficient to conclusively validate genuine unification of comprehension and generation—large-scale RL remains an open question.
