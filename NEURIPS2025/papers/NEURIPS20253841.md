## Problem

Long-context supervised fine-tuning (Long-SFT) requires training on datasets with highly heterogeneous sequence lengths (e.g., 99.89% under 1K tokens and 0.11% around 37K tokens in Llama3's data). Existing distributed training systems must configure context parallelism and memory-reduction strategies to accommodate the longest sequences, which severely degrades efficiency for the dominant short sequences through unnecessary communication, GPU underutilization, and load imbalance in the quadratic-complexity attention module.

## Input / Output

Skrull consumes a global batch of mixed-length token sequences along with model configuration (hidden dimension, KV heads) and parallelism settings (DP degree, CP degree, batch size, BucketSize C derived from offline profiling). It produces a two-level scheduling plan: GDS assigns each sequence to a specific micro-batch on a specific DP rank, and DACP further classifies each sequence as local (assigned entirely to one CP rank) or distributed (sharded across all CP ranks), outputting a per-sequence assignment array. The scheduling executes online within the DataLoader with near-zero overhead, and the roll-back mechanism guarantees no OOM violations.

## Method

Skrull introduces a two-level dynamic data scheduler operating at different granularities. At the fine-grained level, Distributed-Aware Context Parallelism (DACP) selectively keeps short sequences entirely on a single CP worker to avoid sharding overhead while distributing long sequences across CP workers, and exploits the independence between local and distributed sequences to overlap attention communication with local computation. At the coarse-grained level, Global Data Scheduling (GDS) uses bin-packing to balance FLOPs across DP ranks and interleaves long and short sequences within micro-batches to expand the valid scheduling space for DACP. The joint scheduling is formulated as a min-max optimization over execution time subject to per-rank memory (BucketSize) constraints, and solved at runtime by lightweight greedy heuristics with a roll-back mechanism that reverts local assignments to distributed ones when memory pressure is detected, achieving near-zero scheduling overhead.

## Results

Skrull achieves an average end-to-end training speedup of 3.76× (peak 7.54×) over DeepSpeed (Zero-2) and 3.45× (peak 6.85×) over the sorted-batching strategy from LongAlign, measured as average iteration time reduction across three real-world Long-SFT datasets. The speedup is model-size dependent: Qwen2.5-0.5B benefits more (5.50× average) than Qwen2.5-7B (2.03× average), attributed to the larger BucketSize (26K vs. 13K) providing a wider valid scheduling space for the smaller model. On the bimodal ChatQA2-Long-SFT dataset, gains are smaller because long sequences dominate and often exceed the BucketSize, limiting the room for selective sharding.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Skrull is compared against two baselines: (1) DeepSpeed with Zero-2 (the default distributed training configuration) and (2) the sorted-batching strategy from LongAlign, which groups sequences by length to reduce padding. Against DeepSpeed, Skrull wins by a large margin—3.76× average speedup with a peak of 7.54×—across all model/dataset combinations tested. Against LongAlign's sorted batching, Skrull still leads by 3.45× on average (peak 6.85×), indicating that dynamic per-sequence scheduling (DACP + GDS) substantially outperforms static length-based grouping. The gap between the two baselines is notable: sorted batching already improves over naive DeepSpeed, but Skrull's fine-grained, computation-aware scheduling captures additional gains that length-sorting alone cannot. On the bimodal ChatQA2-Long-SFT dataset with Qwen2.5-7B, the speedup over DeepSpeed shrinks because most sequences exceed the 13K BucketSize, leaving little room for the selective-sharding optimization that drives most of Skrull's gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

Skrull builds on DeepSpeed's Zero-2 as its implementation substrate and positions itself against two prior lines of work: (1) fixed batching strategies like LongAlign's sorted batching and Chunkflow's fixed-size chunking, which cannot adapt to heterogeneous sequence lengths within a batch, and (2) dynamic parallelism switching approaches that reconfigure DP/CP degrees at runtime. The key conceptual departure is that Skrull keeps parallelism settings fixed and instead introduces a two-level data scheduling mechanism—DACP (selective sequence sharding within a fixed CP group) and GDS (global micro-batching across DP ranks)—that treats the scheduling problem as a joint optimization over both granularities. This is orthogonal to PEFT/LoRA methods and to specific CP implementations (Ring Attention, etc.), making it a composable system-layer optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 3.76× average speedup (up to 7.54×) over DeepSpeed is practically significant for teams running long-SFT at scale, where training time directly translates to compute cost. The approach's orthogonality to PEFT and its applicability to other mixed-length training scenarios (e.g., RLHF) broadens its relevance beyond the immediate Long-SFT setting. Key limitations include the need for offline profiling to determine BucketSize, the fixed-parallelism assumption (which may not suit all workloads), and the observation that speedup diminishes when dominant sequence lengths exceed BucketSize. An open question is whether the heuristic scheduling can be replaced by learned or adaptive policies that eliminate the offline profiling dependency.
