## Problem

Underwater scene understanding requires multi-task perception across image, region, and object granularities, but no large-scale multi-task instruction-tuning dataset exists for this domain, and general-purpose LMMs fail due to domain shift and image degradation from light scattering and absorption. Existing underwater LMMs like MarineGPT address only image-level understanding, neglecting hierarchical scene information such as detection, grounding, and counting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250843/cropped_page0_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts an underwater RGB image (processed at 336×336 resolution by CLIP ViT-L/14) paired with a free-form text instruction, and additionally extracts monocular depth features via a frozen Depth Anything V2 encoder as auxiliary input. Outputs span eight task types: coarse/fine-grained class labels, bounding boxes for detection and grounding, numerical object counts, image and region captions, and short VQA answers. Both the original and VFE-enhanced vision features are projected through a shared MLP and fed in parallel to the LLM, so the model simultaneously reasons over degraded and restored representations.

## Method

The core contribution is a plug-and-play Vision Feature Enhancement (VFE) module grounded in the physical underwater imaging model I_c = J_c·exp(−β_c(z)·z) + B_c. It operates in two steps in feature space: (1) backscattering removal, where the k-th image patch with the lowest mean RGB value is identified as a dark token, a cross-attention layer with a learnable query (initialized with global average pooling) isolates global semantics from this token, and the residual is broadcast-subtracted from all vision features; (2) light-absorption restoration, where a lightweight MLP maps depth features to per-patch absorption weights W, and the enhanced feature is computed as (v − s) ⊘ exp(−W). The VFE module is integrated into LLaVA-1.5 and Qwen2.5-VL with LoRA (rank 128) fine-tuning on the 1.45 M-pair NautData, training only the projector, LoRA adapters, and VFE parameters for one epoch on four A800 GPUs.

## Results

On the NautData test set, NAUTILUS(Qwen2.5-VL) achieves the best performance on four of eight tasks: fine-grained classification (93.8% acc, +5.6 over baseline), image caption (0.223 METEOR), grounding (53.8 mIoU, +1.3), and detection (45.3 mAP@0.5, +3.6). NAUTILUS(LLaVA-1.5) improves over its baseline on seven of eight tasks, with the largest gains in grounding (+2.7 mIoU) and detection (+1.9 mAP@0.5). Zero-shot commercial LMMs (GPT-4o, Qwen2.5-VL-72B, Gemini 2.0 Flash) score below 56% on classification and below 0.19 METEOR on captioning, far behind fine-tuned open-source models. On the IOCfish5k counting benchmark, NAUTILUS(LLaVA-1.5) reduces MAE from 59.2 to 51.2 and RMSE from 151.9 to 136.0.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against zero-shot commercial LMMs, NAUTILUS dominates across all tasks: GPT-4o scores only 55.2% coarse classification and 0.3 mAP on detection, while NAUTILUS(Qwen2.5-VL) reaches 90.3% and 25.8 mAP respectively. Among instruction-tuned open-source baselines, NAUTILUS(Qwen2.5-VL) outperforms Qwen2.5-VL on fine-grained classification (+5.6 acc), image caption (+0.001 METEOR), grounding (+1.3 mIoU), and detection (+3.6 mAP@0.5), but trails mPLUG-Owl3 on coarse classification (90.3 vs. 91.9) and region caption (0.199 vs. 0.207 METEOR). NAUTILUS(LLaVA-1.5) improves over LLaVA-1.5 on 7 of 8 tasks, with the largest gains in grounding (+2.7 mIoU) and detection mAP@0.5 (+1.9), but slightly underperforms InternVL-2.5 on detection (11.1 vs. 18.3 mAP). In the image-enhancement ablation (Table 6), the VFE module outperforms all three SOTA restoration methods (Reti-Diff, SMDR-IS, CCL-Net) on coarse classification, image caption, region caption, and detection, confirming that feature-space enhancement preserves more information than pixel-space restoration.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the LLaVA-1.5 and Qwen2.5-VL instruction-tuning frameworks, extending them to the underwater domain by introducing a physics-motivated Vision Feature Enhancement (VFE) module that operates in feature space rather than pixel space. It departs from prior underwater LMMs (MarineGPT, MarineInst) that addressed only image-level or object-level understanding by unifying eight tasks across image, region, and object granularities within a single model. The VFE module draws on the classical underwater imaging model (dark pixel prior for backscattering, depth-based exponential absorption) but applies these priors to enhance vision features inside the LMM rather than pre-processing images, a design choice validated against pixel-space enhancement methods like Reti-Diff and CCL-Net.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

NautData (1.45M QA pairs, 8 tasks, 3 granularities) fills a critical gap as the first multi-task underwater instruction-tuning dataset, and the plug-and-play VFE module offers a reusable component for any underwater LMM. The paper's key open question is generalization to unseen species and environments: the authors acknowledge the need for open-vocabulary or few-shot capabilities that remain unexplored. The finding that pixel-space enhancement degrades downstream LMM performance while feature-space enhancement helps is a practical insight that could redirect how degradation is handled in domain-specific multimodal pipelines.
