## Problem

Most federated learning theory assumes clients draw i.i.d. samples from fixed datasets, yet real-world streaming data from health and environmental monitoring is generated by non-stationary Markov processes with temporal dependencies between consecutive samples. Whether FL can still achieve linear speedup (sample complexity proportional to 1/M) under such Markovian data streams, and at what cost in sample complexity relative to i.i.d. sampling, remained an open question for smooth non-convex objectives.

## Input / Output

Each of M clients maintains a memory cache of K samples drawn sequentially from its own time-homogeneous Markov chain (initially non-stationary, converging to a unique stationary distribution π_m); the cache is refreshed every communication round, so samples within and across rounds are statistically dependent. In the empirical validation, clients are virtual partitions of the Beijing Multi-Site Air-Quality dataset (hourly measurements from 12 Chinese weather stations, 2013–2017), where contiguous windows of 6 or 12 months serve as each client's data stream and the prediction target is seasonality-adjusted PM2.5 concentration from air-quality and meteorological features. The output is a global parameter vector w minimizing the average of M client objectives, evaluated via gradient-norm convergence and PM2.5 prediction accuracy.

## Method

The paper provides convergence guarantees for Minibatch SGD, Local SGD, and a momentum-based variant (Local SGD-M) under smooth non-convex objectives, using only a bounded Radon-Nikodym derivative assumption (C_∞) on the transition kernel rather than the stronger uniform geometric ergodicity common in prior work. A key technical device is a change-of-measures argument that handles the non-stationarity of Markovian samples when taking conditional expectations, revealing that gradient noise is amplified by a factor C_∞/ν_ps (the pseudo spectral gap of the system-level product chain) and produces a persistent bias that cannot be eliminated by shrinking the step size. Local SGD-M mitigates client drift without any bounded-gradient-dissimilarity assumption by computing local updates as a convex combination of the current gradient and the previous round's aggregated update, yielding a recursive bound on gradient error that matches Minibatch SGD's rate up to a 1/β constant. All three algorithms achieve linear speedup in M, but the sample complexity carries an extra C_∞/ν_ps factor over the i.i.d. case, quantifying the cost of temporal dependence.

## Results

The paper establishes convergence bounds for three FL algorithms under Markovian data streams with smooth non-convex objectives. All three algorithms (Minibatch SGD, Local SGD, and Local SGD-M) achieve a sample complexity of O(C∞σ²/(νps·M·ε⁴)), demonstrating linear speedup with the number of clients M. The key Markovian overhead is the C∞/νps factor (bounded Radon-Nikodym derivative divided by the pseudo spectral gap), which amplifies gradient noise compared to i.i.d. sampling. Local SGD-M achieves the i.i.d. communication complexity lower bound O(LΔ₀/(β·ε²)) without any heterogeneity assumption, while Local SGD requires the BGD assumption and incurs an additional (δ² ∨ (θ²+σ²)/(δ²ε²)) factor in communication rounds.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The main experimental baseline is SCAFFOLD [27], the first i.i.d.-setting algorithm designed to mitigate client heterogeneity via control variates. On the Beijing Multi-Site Air-Quality dataset (120 clients, 12 months of data each), Minibatch SGD and Local SGD-M consistently improve their gradient norm as K increases from 10 to 100, while both Local SGD and SCAFFOLD show little to no improvement — confirming the theoretical prediction that heterogeneity limits Local SGD and that SCAFFOLD's advantage is more apparent in partial-participation settings. Theoretically, Local SGD-M matches the i.i.d. communication complexity lower bound [42] up to a constant 1/β without any heterogeneity assumption, whereas SCAFFOLD requires a bounded-heterogeneity-around-optimum condition and SCAFFLSA [37] needs more samples per round than its vanilla version. Compared to centralized Markovian SGD methods [3, 15, 17, 14], the paper's results avoid the uniform geometric ergodicity assumption (replacing it with the milder bounded Radon-Nikodym derivative condition) and avoid requiring a lower bound on T proportional to the mixing time, while achieving the same O(1/ε⁴) sample complexity scaled by 1/M.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the momentum-based Local SGD variant introduced for i.i.d. federated learning [9, 61] to the Markovian data stream setting, and generalizes prior FL-with-streaming work [38, 35] (which assumed i.i.d. samples) and Federated Reinforcement Learning with Markovian sampling [28, 37, 59] (which was restricted to convex/linear objectives) to smooth non-convex objectives. A key technical departure from the centralized Markovian SGD literature [3, 15, 14, 17] is the use of a bounded Radon-Nikodym derivative (Assumption 3.2) and a simple change-of-measures argument, avoiding the commonly imposed uniform geometric ergodicity assumption that prior works rely on to handle non-stationarity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first convergence guarantees showing that linear speedup (sample complexity ∝ 1/M) is achievable in FL with Markovian data streams for non-convex objectives, quantifying the cost of temporal dependence through the C_∞/ν_ps factor. The finding that Local SGD-M matches Minibatch SGD without heterogeneity assumptions even under Markovian data is practically significant for edge/streaming deployments where client drift is a major concern. Open questions include extending the analysis to partial client participation, incorporating variance reduction to match the i.i.d. sample complexity lower bound, and handling more complex (e.g., non-time-homogeneous or interacting) Markov structures.
