## Problem

In multimodal sensor fusion for autonomous driving, sensor information becomes entangled within the fusion network, making it impossible to determine how each modality (camera, radar, LiDAR) contributes to a specific prediction. Existing interpretability methods either require architectural modifications (GAMs, NAMs) or fail to scale to high-dimensional perception models (fANOVA, LIME), leaving no post-hoc approach that can attribute predictions of a pretrained fusion model to individual input modalities across all network layers.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254816/cropped_page6_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

LMD operates on a pretrained fusion model (e.g., SimpleBEV with ResNet-101 backbone) and its multimodal inputs: six surround-view camera images at 224×400, radar point clouds, and LiDAR point clouds, all projected into a shared BEV coordinate space (200×8×200). The method requires two forward passes — the first caches activation masks and normalization statistics, the second runs the fully linearized network — and produces an exact additive decomposition of the prediction into per-modality maps (camera, radar, LiDAR) plus a bias component at every layer, such that their sum equals the original model output exactly.

## Method

LMD linearizes every non-linear layer in the fusion network so that the superposition principle holds, enabling exact separation of modality contributions. Activations are replaced by element-wise multiplication with a cached output-to-input ratio (a binary mask for ReLU), BatchNorm is linearized by routing its bias term exclusively to the bias component (identity rule), and LayerNorm is linearized via a ratio rule that fixes the variance from a single forward pass while centering each modality independently. Formulated within the Deep Taylor Decomposition / Layer-Wise Relevance Propagation framework, the method is post-hoc and model-agnostic: no architectural changes are made, and the linearized network reproduces the original output exactly at the operating point. The paper further explores bias-splitting variants (identity, uniform, ratio, sum, and ratio-splitting rules) and shows that the identity rule for BatchNorm combined with the ratio rule for LayerNorm yields the strongest modality separation under perturbation-based evaluation metrics.

## Results

On the nuScenes benchmark, LMD (Ratio Rule) achieves near-perfect modality separation: when the radar input is perturbed, the radar-specific prediction drops to a Pearson correlation of 0.05 (Radar+Camera) / 0.09 (LiDAR+Camera) with its original output, while the camera-specific prediction remains at 1.00 correlation with zero cross-modality MSE. The best baseline (fixing both activation and normalization) still shows residual entanglement with Rp/R = 0.50 and cross-modality MSE of 0.48–0.74. In the LMD+SHAP combination, modality-replacement stability improves from 0.69 (SHAP alone) to 0.94 (Rp/C) for Radar+Camera, confirming that LMD's decomposition enhances downstream attribution fidelity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares LMD against two main baselines. Against LRP (Bach et al., 2015), LMD requires only 2 forward passes with O(1) auxiliary memory, whereas LRP needs 2 forward + 1 backward pass under gradient checkpointing with O(√Nℓ) activation storage; LMD also avoids the numerical instabilities that prior LRP variants encountered with LayerNorm. Against SHAP (Lundberg & Lee, 2017), LMD+SHAP substantially outperforms SHAP alone on modality-replacement stability: for Radar+Camera, Rp/C rises from 0.69 (SHAP) to 0.94 (LMD+SHAP), and Cp/R from 0.67 to 0.89; for the three-modality setting, cross-modality correlations improve from ~0.70 to 0.88–0.96. In the linearization ablation (Table 1), the best baseline that fixes both activation and normalization still leaves Rp/R at 0.50 and cross-modality MSE at ~0.5, indicating residual entanglement, while LMD drives these to 0.05–0.09 and 0.00 respectively, demonstrating that full linearization with the ratio rule is essential for clean modality separation.

## Relation to Prior Work

LMD extends the Deep Taylor Decomposition (DTD) and Layer-Wise Relevance Propagation (LRP) lineage by shifting the attribution target from individual input features (pixels, tokens) to entire sensor modalities (camera, radar, LiDAR) in a fusion network. The linearization strategy for activation layers (caching output-to-input ratios) and the ratio rule for LayerNorm are directly inherited from prior LRP work (notably Ali et al., ICML 2022), but are repurposed here to enforce a modality-separation property rather than pixel-wise conservation. Unlike SHAP or fANOVA, which operate at the feature level and face combinatorial or scalability barriers, LMD exploits the structural decomposition of a fusion network into modality-specific branches to achieve exact, layer-wise separation with only two forward passes and O(1) auxiliary memory.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

In safety-critical autonomous driving, knowing which sensor drove a particular perception decision is essential for failure diagnosis and system validation, yet prior interpretability tools could not disentangle modality contributions once features were fused. LMD provides the first model-agnostic, layer-wise modality attribution for sensor-fusion perception, with a clean O(1)-memory two-pass implementation that is practical for deployment. Key open questions remain: bilinear cross-modal terms in attention-based fusion (e.g., CRN) cannot be fully assigned to a single modality under the current linearization, and the bias component's non-trivial perceptual content (capturing high-order interactions) suggests the decomposition, while exact, may not be the most semantically natural partition. Extending the framework to handle these interaction terms and to more complex fusion topologies is a natural next step.
