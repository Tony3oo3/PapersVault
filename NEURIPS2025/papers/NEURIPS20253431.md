## Problem

Next-token prediction (NTP) constrains LLMs to a short sequential context and one-token-per-step inference, while existing multi-token prediction (MTP) extends only to adjacent positions, leaving long-range dependencies underexploited and inference speedup limited. The paper asks whether strategically skipping intermediate tokens during prediction can simultaneously broaden the effective training signal and further accelerate decoding.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253431/cropped_page1_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

Given a prefix of input tokens x_{≤t}, L-MTP produces predictions at non-adjacent (leaping) positions—e.g., with stride k=2 and n=4 heads, it predicts tokens at offsets 1, 3, 5, 7 in a single forward pass. The complete output sequence is reconstructed at inference time by a 'looking backward' strategy that retrieves the skipped tokens from previously cached predictions, so the final generated text is a contiguous sequence identical in distribution to standard autoregressive decoding.

## Method

L-MTP retains the multi-head architecture of MTP but reassigns each head to predict tokens at a fixed stride k apart rather than at consecutive positions, yielding a broader supervision signal that captures longer-range dependencies. Training proceeds in two stages: (1) head warm-up, where new heads are trained via self-distillation on the frozen backbone using the leaping supervision pattern, and (2) full model tuning with LoRA (rank 32) on curated math/code/general data. At inference, the 'looking backward' decoding fills the k−1 gaps between leaping predictions by retrieving tokens already produced in prior steps (mod-k indexing), and the method integrates with tree-attention speculative decoding for parallel verification. A theoretical analysis formalizes an attenuation property (marginal probability decreases with prediction horizon) and proves that L-MTP's longer effective range k(n−1)+1 yields a higher expected accepted length than vanilla MTP when the attenuation coefficient γ = O(1/n²).

## Results

Across six base LLMs (Llama 3.2-3B, Llama 3.1-8B, Qwen 2.5-3B/7B, Gemma 3-4B/12B), L-MTP achieves the best average score on 5 out of 6 models, with the most dramatic gains on Gemma 3-12B (49.58 avg vs. 45.17 for MTP and 46.87 for NTP) and Llama 3.2-3B (26.68 vs. 25.46). On inference speed, L-MTP with self-speculative decoding delivers up to 2.43× speedup on GSM8K (Vicuna-13B), a 22% relative improvement over standard MTP decoding (2.24×). When extended to Medusa models, L-MTP decoding yields up to 1.3× additional speedup over MTP decoding with the same number of heads.

## Baselines & Comparisons

L-MTP consistently outperforms MTP (Gloeckle et al., 2024) on average across 5 of 6 base models, with the largest margin on Gemma 3-12B (+4.41 avg: 49.58 vs. 45.17) and Llama 3.2-3B (+1.22 avg: 26.68 vs. 25.46). The gains are most pronounced on math tasks (e.g., GSM8K: 26.38 vs. 5.61 on Gemma 3-12B; 5.91 vs. 3.87 on Llama 3.2-3B) and general instruction-following (IFEval: 22.18 vs. 19.42 on Llama 3.1-8B). Against NTP, L-MTP also wins on average for 5 of 6 models, though NTP occasionally matches or exceeds L-MTP on individual code benchmarks (e.g., MBPP on Qwen 2.5-7B: 78.31 vs. 78.04). On inference speed, L-MTP decoding achieves 2.32×/2.43× speedup on GSM8K for Vicuna-7B/13B, surpassing Medusa's original MTP decoding (1.83×/2.24×) by up to 22% relative improvement, confirming that the leaping strategy accelerates generation without sacrificing output quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the multi-token prediction (MTP) paradigm introduced by Gloeckle et al. [39] and the multi-head inference acceleration framework of Medusa [48] by reassigning prediction heads to non-adjacent (leaping) positions rather than consecutive ones. The 'looking backward' decoding strategy, which retrieves previously computed hidden states to fill gaps between leaping positions, is a novel inference mechanism that complements the existing speculative decoding and tree-attention verification frameworks [49, 50, 48]. The theoretical analysis formalizes when leaping outperforms adjacent prediction via an attenuation coefficient condition, building on the draft-then-verify losslessness guarantee of speculative decoding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

L-MTP offers a drop-in modification to existing MTP/Medusa-style models (reassigning head positions + a backward-lookup cache) that simultaneously broadens the training signal and accelerates inference, with up to 22% additional speedup over MTP at the same head count. The observed 'myopia' phenomenon—where larger models exhibit worse multi-token prediction accuracy—challenges the assumption that scaling inherently improves long-range token forecasting and motivates training from scratch with leaping objectives. Open questions include adaptive selection of stride k and head count n based on local entropy, integration with reinforcement fine-tuning, and validation at scales beyond 12B parameters where computational constraints limited the current study.
