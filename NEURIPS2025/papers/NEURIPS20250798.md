## Problem

Forward prediction of neural population activity—especially across multi-session, spontaneous calcium imaging recordings—remains underexplored relative to behavioral decoding and latent interpretation. Existing population dynamics models are largely single-session, and prior forecasting work is confined to short trial-based spiking data or single-animal benchmarks, leaving open how to build scalable, generalizable forecasters that leverage shared neural motifs across individuals and species.

## Input / Output

The model consumes C=48 time steps of z-scored calcium traces from N_j neurons (ranging from 126 in C. elegans to ~77K in zebrafish) plus a session identifier, and produces P=16 predicted time steps for all neurons. A key constraint is that N_j varies across sessions and neurons have no one-to-one correspondence between animals, so the architecture must handle variable population sizes without assuming aligned units. For large zebrafish datasets, the input can be reduced to the first 512 principal components to make computation tractable.

## Method

POCO combines a univariate MLP forecaster (which predicts each neuron from its own C-step history, capturing neuron-specific autocorrelation) with a population encoder that generates per-neuron FiLM conditioning parameters (γ, β) to modulate the MLP's hidden activations. The population encoder adapts POYO's Perceiver-IO architecture: each neuron's trace is split into tokens of length T_C=16, embedded with learnable unit and session embeddings, processed through 8 learnable latents and L self-attention layers, then queried by unit embeddings in a final cross-attention layer to extract FiLM parameters. This design ensures linear scaling with neuron count while allowing the model to capture how global brain state contextually modulates each neuron's dynamics. Multi-session training shares the MLP and encoder weights across sessions while maintaining per-session unit and session embeddings, enabling rapid adaptation to new recordings via embedding-only fine-tuning in under 15 seconds.

## Results

Multi-session POCO achieves the highest prediction score (1 − L_model/L_copy, analogous to R²) on 4 of 5 calcium imaging datasets: 0.525 on Zebrafish (Deisseroth, 512 PCs), 0.440 on Zebrafish (Ahrens, 512 PCs), 0.420 on Mice, and 0.364 on C. elegans (Zimmer). On C. elegans (Flavell), the larger MLP baseline (MS_MLP_L, 0.276) slightly outperforms POCO (0.213). POCO also benefits from multi-session training, improving from 0.466 to 0.525 on Deisseroth zebrafish and from 0.079 to 0.213 on Flavell C. elegans, and rapidly adapts to new sessions with only ~200 fine-tuning steps of its unit embeddings (<15 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

POCO outperforms all eight baselines on Zebrafish (Deisseroth), Zebrafish (Ahrens), Mice, and C. elegans (Zimmer), with the largest margins over AR_Transformer (which yields negative scores on three datasets) and NLinear/DLinear (which plateau around 0.17–0.35). The strongest competitor is MS_MLP_L, which is within 0.01–0.06 of POCO on Mice (0.412 vs. 0.420) and actually surpasses POCO on C. elegans (Flavell) by 0.063 (0.276 vs. 0.213), likely because the small 136-neuron population offers limited benefit from population-level conditioning. TexFilter is the next-closest baseline, trailing POCO by 0.085 on Deisseroth zebrafish and 0.091 on Ahrens zebrafish but remaining competitive on Mice (0.389 vs. 0.420). NetFormer and Latent_PLRNN, which model inter-neuron interactions or latent dynamical states, underperform POCO by 0.1–0.25 across most datasets, suggesting that explicit connectivity modeling is less effective than FiLM-based population conditioning for calcium traces. On the Zapbench benchmark (single zebrafish, 71,721 neurons), POCO matches the volumetric UNet at short context (C=4) but underperforms it and TSMixer at longer context (C=256).

## Relation to Prior Work

POCO adapts the POYO/Perceiver-IO architecture—originally designed for behavioral decoding of primate spiking data—into a forecasting framework by repurposing the unit embedding as a query for FiLM conditioning parameters rather than as a tokenization aid. The univariate MLP forecaster component draws on the time-series forecasting literature showing that simple per-variable predictors are surprisingly competitive, while the population encoder introduces cross-neuron context that prior single-session dynamical system models (PLRNN, switching linear systems, sequential VAEs) handled through explicit latent state evolution. The multi-session, multi-species calcium imaging setting extends the foundation-model paradigm (Neuroformer, POYO+) from spiking data in controlled tasks to spontaneous, whole-brain recordings across species, a regime where no prior systematic forecasting benchmark existed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

POCO provides the first comprehensive cross-species, multi-session benchmark for neural population forecasting, establishing that population-level conditioning yields consistent gains over univariate baselines and that pre-trained models adapt to new sessions in under 15 seconds—enabling real-time closed-loop neurotechnology. The discovery that multi-species training does not improve performance (and can hurt it) challenges the straightforward scaling narrative of neural foundation models and suggests that distributional alignment across species remains an open problem. Key limitations include the unclear generalizability of learned unit embeddings across species, the sensitivity to preprocessing choices (filtering, calcium indicator dynamics), and the gap between simulated and real-data performance that hints at unmodeled non-stationarities in biological recordings.
