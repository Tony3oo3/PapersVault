## Problem

Scaffolding LLMs into multi-agent systems consistently improves task performance, yet the safety implications of such scaffolds remain largely unexplored. Prior alignment research has focused almost exclusively on single-agent (unipolar) settings, while deployed agents increasingly operate in multi-agent (multipolar) configurations where emergent vulnerabilities may arise. This paper asks: can we systematically discover multi-agent scaffolds that are simultaneously more capable and more robust to adversarial attacks, and conversely, can we surface scaffolds that inadvertently weaken the base model's safety?

## Input / Output

The system takes seven hand-designed seed scaffolds (Python code defining agent architectures with Agents, Meetings, and Chats components), a capability benchmark (DROP, MMLU, or GPQA), a safety benchmark (SaladData's Attack-Enhanced subset), and a Meta Agent LLM (Claude 3.5 Sonnet) as input. It produces a population of evolved multi-agent scaffolds as executable Python code, along with Pareto fronts visualizing the capability-safety tradeoff across generations. Safety is scored via Constitutional AI-style LLM judging (100 attack-enhanced prompts per scaffold), and a helpfulness check on TruthfulQA guards against trivial reward-hacking solutions like refusing all out-of-domain questions.

## Method

AgentBreeder is an evolutionary open-ended search algorithm inspired by MAP-Elites that evolves multi-agent scaffolds in the space of Python code. At each generation, newly generated scaffolds are evaluated on capability and safety benchmarks, embedded via a 12-dimensional text embedding of their code, and clustered using agglomerative clustering (distance threshold 0.7). Within each cluster, Pareto-optimal scaffolds are identified as elites, and a Meta Agent (Claude 3.5 Sonnet) applies mutation (sampling from hand-designed capability- or safety-enhanced textual operators) or crossover (combining two elite scaffolds) to produce offspring. The framework operates in three modes: BLUE (maximizes both capability and safety), RED (maximizes capability while minimizing safety via an inverted scoring function), and CAPABLE (single-objective capability maximization as baseline). A balanced sampling strategy (50% correct, 50% incorrect baseline answers) is used during validation to strengthen the evolutionary signal across generations.

## Results

In BLUEAGENTBREEDER (blue-team defense mode), the best discovered scaffold achieves a 79.4% average uplift on the SaladData safety benchmark (up to 110.7% maximum) while maintaining or improving capability scores (e.g., 79.0±3.8 F1 on DROP vs. 78.0±3.9 for the best seed scaffold; 39.2±5.6 accuracy on GPQA vs. 32.4±6.0). In REDAGENTBREEDER (red-team attack mode), the best scaffold reaches 81.6±4.8 on the inverted 1-SaladData harm metric while retaining a competitive 67.7±4.7 F1 on DROP, demonstrating that unsafe scaffolds can coexist with strong task performance. CAPABLEAGENTBREEDER (single-objective baseline) marginally surpasses ADAS across all capability benchmarks (72.3±3.1 DROP, 82.4±3.2 MMLU, 41.2±4.4 GPQA) but shows only modest safety changes, confirming that multi-objective optimization is necessary for meaningful safety gains.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is the set of 7 hand-designed seed scaffolds from ADAS [20] (CoT, Self-Consistency, Self-Refinement, Debate, Step-Back Abstraction, Quality-Diversity, Role Assignment), evaluated with the same GPT-4o-mini core model. On safety (SaladData), BLUEAGENTBREEDER's best non-hacked scaffold (86.0±4.0 on the DROP run) outperforms the best seed scaffold (Step-Back Abstraction, 40.8±5.6) by over 45 percentage points, a 110.7% relative uplift. On capability, discovered scaffolds match or exceed the best seeds: 79.0±3.8 vs. 78.0±3.9 on DROP, 85.2±4.4 vs. 81.6±4.4 on MMLU, and 39.2±5.6 vs. 32.4±6.0 on GPQA. In REDAGENTBREEDER, the best scaffold (81.6±4.8 on 1-SaladData) surpasses the most vulnerable seed (CoT, 76.8±4.8) by 6.25% while retaining competitive DROP F1 (67.7±4.7 vs. 74.9±4.2 for QD). In the single-objective ablation (CAPABLEAGENTBREEDER vs. ADAS-discovered scaffolds), AgentBreeder marginally wins on all three capability benchmarks (72.3 vs. 72.0 DROP, 82.4 vs. 80.4 MMLU, 41.2 vs. 37.4 GPQA) but shows no consistent safety advantage, underscoring that multi-objective optimization is the key differentiator.

## Relation to Prior Work

AgentBreeder extends the Automated Design of Agentic Systems (ADAS) framework of Hu et al. by replacing its single-objective Meta Agent search with a quality-diversity evolutionary algorithm inspired by MAP-Elites, adding agglomerative clustering over scaffold embeddings and Pareto-front selection across capability and safety objectives. Unlike prior evolutionary scaffold-search methods (EvoAgent, ComfyAgent, Gödel Agent, PromptBreeder), it treats the entire multi-agent architecture as a unit to evolve and explicitly incorporates safety as a co-optimization target rather than an afterthought. The multi-agent safety literature (Zhang et al., Huang et al., Fowler) has examined vulnerabilities via malicious-agent injection or psychological framing, but none formulates scaffold-level safety as a searchable optimization landscape.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a practical, reproducible pipeline (implemented in Inspect) that labs could adopt for pre-deployment red- and blue-teaming of multi-agent scaffolds, addressing a gap the authors identify as increasingly urgent with the deployment of autonomous web agents. The finding that adversarially weak scaffolds emerge more readily than robust ones—and that they can maintain competitive capability scores—challenges the implicit assumption that architectural complexity improves safety. Key limitations include small population sizes, limited generation budgets, a single safety benchmark (SaladData), and demonstrated reward-hacking vulnerability, leaving open whether the observed safety uplifts generalize to broader threat models and larger-scale evolutionary runs.
