## Problem

Human annotations for evaluating LLM performance are often incomplete, and interactions from the same user exhibit strong within-cluster correlation that violates the i.i.d. assumptions underlying standard missing-data methods. While the doubly robust estimator is well-characterized under independent sampling, its asymptotic properties under cluster dependence—especially with unbounded cluster sizes—were not previously established, leaving practitioners without valid inference procedures for clustered missing-response problems.

## Input / Output

The method takes cluster-level covariates (e.g., conversation language), individual-level covariates (e.g., message embeddings via BAAI/bge-small-en-v1.5 and role), binary missingness indicators, and observed outcomes. In the sequential setting, a fixed-length summary variable S_gt (e.g., an embedding of the concatenated conversation history) replaces the growing-dimensional history, keeping nuisance function arguments bounded regardless of conversation length. Nuisance functions are estimated via SuperLearner on a separate sample split, and the output is a point estimate of the population mean accompanied by a cluster-robust confidence interval whose width adapts to the degree of within-cluster dependence.

## Method

The paper establishes asymptotic normality of the doubly robust estimator under cluster dependence, showing the convergence rate is √(n/Ω_n) where Ω_n captures the within-cluster correlation structure of influence functions—yielding rates faster than the conventional √G when dependence is weak. A key extension over prior work (Park and Kang, 2021) is allowing cluster sizes to diverge, governed by a moment condition linking cluster sizes to total sample size. For sequential data, the authors introduce Assumption 3, which posits the existence of a fixed-dimensional summary S_gt of the conversation history such that both the propensity score and outcome regression depend on (X_g, S_gt) in a cluster- and time-invariant way, enabling unified nonparametric nuisance estimation. A consistent cluster-robust variance estimator is derived from cluster-level sums of influence functions, and the framework is validated through simulations and a real-data analysis on the OpenAssistant Conversations dataset.

## Results

In the simulation study (n=10,000, cluster sizes n_g = n^α for α ∈ {0.1, ..., 0.5}), the cluster-robust variance estimator achieves the nominal 95% coverage probability, while the i.i.d.-based variance estimator systematically undercovers, with coverage dropping well below 0.95 as within-cluster correlation (ρ=0.8) and cluster size increase. On the OpenAssistant Conversations dataset (9,808 trees, 81,937 messages), unadjusted estimates ignoring missing annotations are biased and their CIs fail to cover the ground-truth average for all four annotation types (quality, creativity, humor, toxicity). The doubly robust estimator with cluster-robust variance produces CIs that are at least 10% wider than i.i.d.-based CIs and correctly cover the true average, whereas the i.i.d.-based DR CIs undercover (e.g., humor in Figure 2c). In the misspecification simulation (Appendix E, n=10,000, n_g=100), the DR estimator achieves MSE of 0.0563 when either μ or π is correct, versus 2.0356 (regression) or 2.7023 (IPW) when the corresponding model is misspecified.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three estimation approaches on the OpenAssistant Conversations dataset: (1) a naive i.i.d.-based estimator that simply discards missing annotations, (2) a doubly robust estimator with i.i.d.-based variance, and (3) a doubly robust estimator with cluster-robust variance (Eq. 4). The naive estimator is biased for all four annotation types because covariates confound both missingness and outcome. The DR estimator with i.i.d. variance corrects the bias but produces CIs that are too narrow and undercover (e.g., humor), while the cluster-robust DR estimator yields valid CIs at the cost of ≥10% wider intervals. In the misspecification simulation (Appendix E), the DR estimator matches the regression estimator's MSE (0.0563 vs 0.0562) when μ is correct regardless of π, and matches IPW's MSE (0.0572 vs 0.0563) when π is correct regardless of μ, confirming the double-robustness property. When both models are misspecified, DR (MSE 2.5765) performs slightly better than IPW (2.6896) but worse than regression (2.0603), consistent with the product-of-errors bias structure. In the sequential simulation, modeling missingness with the correct historical summary S_gt achieves lower RMSE than using only current information W_gt or ignoring missingness entirely.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the doubly robust estimator framework of Robins et al. (1994) to clustered data, building directly on Park and Kang (2021) who established asymptotic normality for the DR estimator under multilevel sampling but required bounded cluster sizes. The key departure is leveraging the central limit theorem for clustered samples from Hansen and Lee (2019) to allow unbounded cluster sizes and derive adaptive convergence rates that depend on within-cluster correlation. The paper further introduces a sequential sampling framework with summary statistics (Assumption 3) to handle temporal dependence within clusters, a setting not formally treated in prior DR literature for clustered data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first theoretical justification for using doubly robust estimators in clustered missing-data problems with unbounded cluster sizes, showing the convergence rate adapts to within-cluster dependence and can exceed the conventional √G rate. This has direct practical implications for LLM evaluation, where human annotations are costly, incomplete, and naturally clustered by user. Open questions include the stability of DR estimation when propensity scores approach zero under clustering, the extension to covariate shift for evaluating different models without new annotations, and the lack of theoretical guarantees for nonparametric nuisance estimation under arbitrary within-cluster dependence structures.
