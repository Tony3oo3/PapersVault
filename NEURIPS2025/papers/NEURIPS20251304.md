## Problem

Existing frame-based methods for crystal property prediction face a fundamental trade-off: global frames are too coarse to capture local structural heterogeneity, while equivariant local frames inadvertently break crystal symmetry by aligning symmetry-equivalent atoms (e.g., those at the same Wyckoff position) to identical orientations, making them indistinguishable to the model. No prior method simultaneously achieves SO(3) invariance, local expressivity, and crystal symmetry preservation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251304/cropped_page3_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a crystal structure as a triplet of atomic feature vectors, 3D Cartesian coordinates (or fractional coordinates), and a 3×3 lattice matrix, organized into a crystal graph with both SO(3)-invariant edge features (interatomic distances) and SO(3)-equivariant edge features (directional vectors). It produces scalar property predictions (formation energy, bandgap, bulk modulus, shear modulus, energy above hull) via global average pooling followed by an MLP regression head. The equivariant edge features are embedded into spherical harmonics of orders 0, 1, and 2 before being processed by tensor product layers.

## Method

SPFrame decomposes the per-atom frame into a product of an invariant local frame F_INV,i and a shared equivariant global frame F_global. The invariant local frame is constructed by predicting a 4D quaternion per atom via SO(3)-invariant transformer-based message passing (using only invariant edge features), then converting it to a rotation matrix; because the construction is invariant, symmetry-equivalent atoms receive identical local frames, preserving their relative structural differences. The global frame is computed non-parametrically from the lattice matrix (e.g., via QR decomposition with a determinant constraint) and is shared across all atoms, enforcing SO(3) invariance without disrupting crystal symmetry. This local-global associative design is integrated into eComFormer's node-wise equivariant updating layers, where equivariant edge vectors are transformed by F_global^T F_INV,i^T before spherical harmonic embedding and tensor product aggregation. A mutual information argument in the appendix shows SPFrame retains strictly more task-relevant information than equivariant local frames, and the quaternion-based construction is more parameter-efficient (6.3M vs. 8.5M) than Gram-Schmidt alternatives.

## Results

On JARVIS-DFT, eComFormer with Quaternion-based SPFrame achieves the best MAE across all five prediction tasks: 0.0261 eV/atom (formation energy), 0.0276 eV/atom (total energy), 0.107 eV (bandgap OPT), 0.239 eV (bandgap MBJ), and 0.042 eV (E hull). On the Materials Project dataset, SPFrame achieves the best MAE on formation energy (0.0171 eV/atom) and bandgap (0.181 eV), while Crystalframer leads on bulk modulus (0.0338 log(GPa)) and iComFormer on shear modulus (0.0637 log(GPa)). The quaternion-based SPFrame consistently outperforms both the Gram-Schmidt-based SPFrame variant and the SO(3)-equivariant local frame baseline across all tasks on both datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On JARVIS-DFT, Quaternion-based SPFrame outperforms the strongest baseline Crystalframer on all five tasks, with the largest margin on bandgap MBJ (0.239 vs. 0.242 eV) and E hull (0.042 vs. 0.047 eV). It also surpasses iComFormer (0.0272 eV/atom formation energy) and eComFormer without any frame (0.0284 eV/atom) by clear margins. On Materials Project, SPFrame wins on formation energy (0.0171 vs. 0.0172 for Crystalframer) and bandgap (0.181 vs. 0.185), but trails Crystalframer on bulk modulus (0.0371 vs. 0.0338 log(GPa)) and iComFormer on shear modulus (0.0672 vs. 0.0637 log(GPa)). The ablation comparing frame types shows that the SO(3)-equivariant Gram-Schmidt local frame is consistently worse than both SPFrame variants, confirming that symmetry preservation is the key factor. The quaternion-based SPFrame also uses fewer parameters (6.3M vs. 8.5M) and trains faster (143.75s/epoch vs. ~235s/epoch) than the Gram-Schmidt variants, making it both more accurate and more efficient.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the frame-based approach to crystal property prediction by identifying a critical flaw in equivariant local frames: they inadvertently break crystal symmetry by aligning symmetry-equivalent atoms (same Wyckoff position) to identical orientations, making them indistinguishable to the network. It builds directly on Crystalframer [19], which first introduced local frames to crystal modeling, and on the equivariant local frame concept from Wang and Zhang [50], but departs from both by replacing the equivariant local frame with an invariant one (identical for symmetry-equivalent atoms) while retaining an equivariant global frame for SO(3) invariance. The quaternion-based rotation representation and the eComFormer backbone [57] are adopted from prior work, but the local-global associative decomposition (Eq. 4) is the novel architectural contribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The symmetry-breaking diagnosis of equivariant local frames is a subtle but important insight for the crystal ML community, as it explains why simply adding per-atom frames to crystal models can degrade performance despite increasing expressivity. The mutual information argument (Appendix A.1) provides a clean theoretical justification for why preserving Wyckoff-position distinctions matters. However, the practical impact is tempered by modest performance gains (e.g., 0.0261 vs. 0.0263 MAE on JARVIS formation energy), evaluation on only two datasets without error bars, and the absence of released code. Open questions include whether the symmetry-preserving frame design generalizes to tensor property prediction or crystal generation tasks, and whether the quaternion-based construction advantage over Gram-Schmidt holds at larger scale.
