## Problem

Existing Marked Temporal Point Process (MTPP) models overlook that event mark distributions are highly imbalanced in real-world applications, with some marks frequent and others rare. This imbalance severely degrades next-event prediction accuracy for rare marks, which are often the most critical (e.g., major earthquakes or celebrity retweets). No prior MTPP method addresses this imbalance, and the standard predict-time-then-mark strategy is incompatible with thresholding-based imbalance correction because mark probabilities conditioned on time require different thresholds at each time point.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a sequence of past events (each with a categorical mark and arrival time) encoded by an LSTM, along with the current time t > t_l. It outputs a predicted mark for the next event (selected via argmax over thresholded, prior-normalized mark probabilities r_m - ε_m) and a predicted arrival time (obtained by drawing N samples from the conditional CDF F*(t|m) via inverse transform sampling and averaging). Unlike most MTPP methods that predict time first and then mark, this approach reverses the order, which is essential for applying time-independent thresholds.

## Method

The key insight is unifying two separate improper integrations (for mark probability p*(m) and conditional time CDF F*(t|m)) into a single function Γ*(m,t) = ∫_t^∞ p*(m,τ)dτ, which is monotonically decreasing in t. IFNMTPP approximates Γ*(m,t) without numerical integration: an LSTM encodes event history, per-mark vectors v_m (with non-negative components) are combined with elapsed time (t - t_l) to form inputs to an Integral Estimation Module (IEM) of fully-connected layers with non-negative weights and monotonic-increasing activations, terminated by a sigmoid to enforce monotonic decrease. A normalization module divides by the partition function Z = Σ_m s*(m, t_l) to yield valid probabilities. The model is trained end-to-end with the standard negative log-likelihood loss, and Proposition 3.1 guarantees that the output equals Γ*(m,t) when the gradient is -p*(m,t). At inference, per-mark thresholds ε_m are learned on the training set to maximize pairwise F1, and the next mark is selected by argmax(r_m - ε_m) before time is sampled from F*(t|m).

## Results

On mark prediction (macro-F1), IFNMTPP with thresholding achieves 0.4750 on Retweet, 0.1776 on StackOverflow, 0.4190 on Taobao, and 0.1382 on USearthquake, outperforming all baselines. For rare marks specifically, the improvement is most dramatic: e.g., on Retweet, rare-mark macro-F1 jumps from 0.0266 (SAHP) to 0.2010 (ours), a ~7.5× gain. On time prediction (MAE, lower is better), ours achieves 2515.1 on Retweet, 0.5212 on SO, 0.3324 on Taobao, and 0.6856 on USearthquake, beating SAHP (3320.0, 0.8010, 23.409, 0.7608) and AttNHP (3551.1, 7.9305, 5.4038, 6.4583) across all datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IFNMTPP with thresholding outperforms all five baselines on both mark and time prediction across all four real-world datasets. On mark prediction (macro-F1 for all marks), the largest gains are over SAHP (0.4211→0.4750 on Retweet; 0.0616→0.4190 on Taobao) and AttNHP (0.4100→0.4750 on Retweet; 0.2930→0.4190 on Taobao). For rare-mark prediction, the advantage is most pronounced: on Retweet, rare-mark macro-F1 improves from 0.0266 (SAHP) and 0.0373 (AttNHP) to 0.2010, and on USearthquake from 0.0037 (SAHP) to 0.0339. On time prediction (MAE), ours beats SAHP by ~24% on Retweet (2515 vs 3320) and by ~99% on Taobao (0.33 vs 23.41), and dominates AttNHP on SO (0.52 vs 7.93) and USearthquake (0.69 vs 6.46). Marked-LNM is the closest competitor on time prediction for Taobao (0.21 vs 0.33) and USearthquake (0.76 vs 0.69), but still loses on mark prediction across all datasets. FullyNN and THP are consistently the weakest baselines, often predicting only a single mark class (micro-F1 = 0.0000 on several datasets).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the neural MTPP line of work (SAHP, THP, AttNHP, FullyNN, Marked-LNM) by introducing a thresholding mechanism borrowed from imbalanced classification literature and a novel integration-free architecture (IFNMTPP) that unifies two separate improper integrations into a single monotonically-decreasing function Γ*(m,t). It departs from the standard MTPP prediction pipeline (time-then-mark) by reversing the order to mark-then-time, which is necessary to make thresholding feasible since mark probability p*(m) is time-independent. The integration-free design draws on FullyNN's idea of modeling the integral of the CIF directly, but adds architectural constraints (non-negative weights, monotonic activations) to guarantee the output is a valid survival function.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Mark imbalance is a practical and previously overlooked issue in MTPP applications where rare events (major earthquakes, celebrity retweets) are often the most critical to predict. The thresholding approach requires minimal external knowledge compared to cost-sensitive methods, making it broadly applicable. Open questions include whether the approach extends to continuous marks, whether incorporating domain knowledge about mark importance (beyond learned thresholds) would further improve rare-mark prediction, and whether the mark-first strategy generalizes to settings where mark and time are more tightly coupled. The paper acknowledges it does not deeply investigate resampling or cost-sensitive alternatives in the MTPP context.
