## Problem

Prior-fitted networks like TabPFN are confined to a fixed, pre-defined number of target dimensions because they lack target equivariance: permuting the order of target components changes predictions. The paper identifies this as a fundamental architectural deficiency that introduces an irreducible 'equivariance gap' in the training objective, and shows that existing workarounds (ensembling over permutations, hierarchical classification) incur prohibitive computational cost.

## Input / Output

The model receives training covariate-target pairs (with targets as one-hot vectors of arbitrary dimension q) and test covariates, and outputs predicted class labels as one-hot vectors of the same dimension q. Unlike TabPFN which requires q to be fixed at pre-training time (≤10 classes), EquiTabPFN handles any q at inference without architectural modification or ensembling. The test targets are unavailable at inference and are replaced by a shared trainable prediction token repeated across all test samples and target components.

## Method

EquiTabPFN enforces target permutation equivariance through three coordinated mechanisms: (1) a target-equivariant encoder that embeds each target component via a 1×1 convolution (preserving equivariance) and replaces missing test targets with a shared prediction token; (2) an alternating bi-attention backbone that applies self-attention across target/covariate components within each sample (with masking so target tokens attend only to the covariate token) and across samples (with masking so test tokens attend only to training tokens); and (3) a non-parametric equivariant decoder that computes predictions as a softmax-weighted average of training targets using the processed embeddings as keys/queries, followed by a point-wise MLP residual correction. The paper further proves that under standard assumptions (permutation-invariant convex loss, permutation-invariant data distribution), the equivariance gap is non-negative and vanishes only for equivariant functions, establishing that optimal pre-training solutions must be target-equivariant.

## Results

On 10 datasets with more than 10 classes (ranging from 11 to 100), EquiTabPFN achieves a median relative accuracy improvement of 27.9% over KNN, substantially outperforming TabPFNv2 (6.0%) and TabPFNv1 (18.3%), while running in 0.4s vs. 2.8s for TabPFNv2. On 76 datasets with fewer than 10 classes, EquiTabPFN achieves 6.3% median relative accuracy improvement, competitive with TabPFNv2 (9.1%) despite being trained on the same publicly available prior. The paper also demonstrates that EquiTabPFN's predictions are fully invariant to class ordering, whereas TabPFN and TabPFNv2 show significant prediction instability when target dimensions are permuted.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EquiTabPFN is compared against TabPFNv1 (Hollmann et al., ICLR 2023) and TabPFNv2 (Hollmann et al., Nature 2025), the two prior-fitted network baselines. On the critical setting of datasets with more than 10 classes (where TabPFNv2 requires ECOC ensembling to function), EquiTabPFN achieves 27.9% median relative accuracy over KNN versus only 6.0% for TabPFNv2 and 18.3% for TabPFNv1, while also running 7× faster than TabPFNv2 (0.4s vs. 2.8s). On the 76 datasets with fewer than 10 classes, EquiTabPFN (6.3%) is slightly behind TabPFNv2 (9.1%) but ahead of TabPFNv1 (5.8%); the gap to TabPFNv2 is attributed to TabPFNv2's superior (but non-public) training prior. To isolate the architectural contribution, the authors also retrain TabPFNv2's architecture with the same public prior (TabPFNv2*), which EquiTabPFN consistently outperforms on both dataset splits (27.9% vs. 3.9% on >10 classes; 6.3% vs. 4.3% on <10 classes), confirming that the equivariant architecture itself drives the improvement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the prior-fitted network (PFN) lineage initiated by TabPFN (Hollmann et al., 2023) and the covariate-equivariant bi-attention mechanism of GAMFormer (Müller et al., 2024) by enforcing a previously unaddressed symmetry: target-permutation equivariance. The key architectural departure is that, unlike covariate equivariance which can be achieved by alternating row/column attention, target equivariance is complicated by the unavailability of test targets, requiring the introduction of prediction tokens and a non-parametric decoder that generalizes the Nadaraya-Watson estimator. The paper also provides a theoretical foundation absent from prior PFN work, proving that the optimal solution to the pre-training objective must be target-equivariant and quantifying the irreducible error (equivariance gap) incurred by non-equivariant architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By natively handling arbitrary class counts without ensembling or hierarchical decomposition strategies (ECOC, hierarchical classification), EquiTabPFN removes a significant practical bottleneck for deploying PFNs on real-world datasets with many classes, while also reducing inference cost. The theoretical result on the equivariance gap provides a clean, general argument for why equivariant architectures are not merely convenient but necessary for optimal PFN pre-training, which could inform future foundational model design beyond tabular data. Key limitations include quadratic scaling with the number of target dimensions and the assumption that targets are unordered (inapplicable to ordinal data), leaving open questions about efficient attention for very high-dimensional targets and how to adapt the prior when target ordering carries semantic meaning.
