## Problem

Existing online Video-LLMs rely heavily on EOS tokens to decide when to respond versus stay silent, causing response-silence imbalance, consecutive-frame inconsistency, pre-training misalignment, and vocabulary pollution that degrade both training effectiveness and base video understanding. Additionally, current benchmarks cover only narrow scenarios (e.g., first-person Ego4D) and single-task evaluation (video QA), failing to capture the diversity of real-world streaming applications.

## Input / Output

The model ingests a continuous video frame stream at 1–4 FPS, with each frame encoded into 16 visual tokens by InternViT and processed within an 8K-token context window. Unlike offline Video-LLMs that receive the full video at once, LiveStar processes frames incrementally and autonomously decides when to emit a response versus remain silent. Outputs include real-time narration segments, QA answers, and temporal grounding intervals, all triggered at semantically appropriate moments determined by the model's own verification mechanism rather than fixed decoding points.

## Method

LiveStar is built on InternVideo2.5 (InternViT encoder + MLP projector + InternLM2.5-7B) and introduces two key mechanisms. For training, Streaming Causal Attention Masks (SCAM) construct interleaved frame-caption sequences where consecutive frames from the same semantic clip share paraphrased captions, with a custom mask matrix that blocks attention to non-terminal caption tokens to prevent trivial copying while preserving scene-transition signaling. For inference, Streaming Verification Decoding (SVeD) determines response timing via a single forward pass: it recomputes the perplexity of the last generated caption under the updated context and triggers new decoding only when PPL exceeds a tunable threshold (α=1.03) times the reference PPL, otherwise moving the caption to the context tail without generating new tokens. For long-video efficiency, a peak-end memory compression strategy probabilistically prunes older frames based on relative PPL and elapsed time, combined with a dual-level streaming KV cache that achieves 1.53× faster inference.

## Results

On the OmniStar benchmark (5 online tasks), LiveStar achieves the best SemCor across all tasks: 3.19 (RNG), 6.44 (FDQ), 5.85 (COQ), and 5.78 (MIQ), while also attaining the lowest TimDiff of 1.91 (RNG) and 1.80 (FDQ). It runs at 3.82 FPS on 5-minute videos, the fastest among all online Video-LLMs. On the Ego4D Narration Stream benchmark, LiveStar achieves the best PPL (1.97), TimeDiff (1.76), and TokAcc (61.1%), outperforming the second-best LION-FS by 8.7% in TokAcc. Overall, LiveStar delivers a 19.5% average SemCor improvement, 18.1% TimDiff reduction, and 12.0% FPS increase over the second-best online model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LiveStar consistently outperforms all three online Video-LLM baselines (VideoLLM-online, VideoLLM-MoD, MMDuet) across all five OmniStar tasks in both SemCor and TimDiff. Compared to VideoLLM-online, LiveStar improves RNG SemCor from 1.68 to 3.19 (+90%) and reduces TimDiff from 2.67 to 1.91 (−28%); on FDQ, SemCor jumps from 2.35 to 6.44. MMDuet is the closest competitor on COQ (5.71 vs. 5.85) and MIQ (5.62 vs. 5.78), but LiveStar still leads and runs 4.2× faster (3.82 vs. 0.91 FPS). On Ego4D Narration Stream, LiveStar achieves 61.1% TokAcc versus 52.4% for LION-FS and 48.9% for VideoLLM-MoD, with the lowest PPL (1.97) and TimeDiff (1.76). On SVBench, LiveStar's zero-shot performance trails MiniCPM-V 2.6 (AVG 53.15) and Qwen2.5-VL (AVG 55.21), but after fine-tuning on SVBench it surpasses all open-source Video-LLMs with a 15.37% average improvement, closing the gap with GPT-4V (AVG 59.12).

## Relation to Prior Work

LiveStar builds on the InternVideo2.5 foundation model but fundamentally departs from the EOS-token-based response-silence paradigm established by VideoLLM-online, VideoLLM-MoD, and LION-FS, which the authors argue causes training instability, pre-training misalignment, and vocabulary pollution. Instead, it introduces SCAM (Streaming Causal Attention Masks) for training and SVeD (Streaming Verification Decoding) for inference, replacing explicit silence tokens with perplexity-based verification gates. The OmniStar benchmark extends prior online video understanding evaluation (SVBench, OVO-Bench, StreamBench) from single-task VQA on narrow domains (Ego4D, SoccerNet) to 15 real-world scenarios and 5 diverse tasks, while the peak-end memory compression draws on Kahneman's cognitive psychology literature for long-video context management.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap in deploying Video-LLMs as always-on streaming assistants, where the EOS-based approach's 1:35 response-to-silence imbalance and frame-level inconsistency make real-time deployment unreliable. OmniStar provides the first comprehensive multi-task, multi-scenario benchmark for online video understanding, which should become a standard evaluation resource. However, the model's 16-token-per-frame compression limits fine-grained visual reasoning, and the absence of audio modalities constrains applicability to scenarios where sound is critical (e.g., music, sports commentary). Open questions include whether the SVeD perplexity-based gating generalizes to non-narration tasks without task-specific tuning, and whether peak-end compression remains optimal for videos with uniformly distributed salient events.
