## Problem

Existing Video LMMs treat video referring understanding (comprehending designated regions) and video grounding (segmenting objects from language) as isolated tasks, preventing unified, human-like referential interaction with dynamic video content. This fragmentation is compounded by the absence of high-quality, unified video instruction data that jointly supervises both capabilities, and the lack of a comprehensive benchmark for evaluating integrated referential grounded video dialogue.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251568/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a video (sampled into N frames via a ViT encoder), a multi-turn text dialogue, and an object prompt (bounding box, point, or mask) that specifies the referential target. Keyframe features are flattened directly as fine-grained visual tokens, while all frames pass through the temporal aggregator to produce a compact context representation. The LLM generates free-form text responses; when it emits a special [SEG] token, the corresponding hidden state is routed to SAM2's decoder to produce a pixel-level segmentation mask for the referred object.

## Method

SAMA builds on Sa2VA and introduces a three-stage Versatile Spatial-Temporal-Context Aggregator (STC): a Spatial Aggregator (Q-Former-style cross-attention condensing each frame's patch features into 32 tokens), a Temporal Aggregator (sliding-window Q-Former whose learnable queries are concatenated with the question and object embeddings to make aggregation query-aware), and a Context Aggregator (cross-attention that injects the temporal representation back into frame features, followed by adaptive average pooling to one token per frame). This design preserves fine-grained keyframe detail for precise grounding while capturing long-range temporal dynamics for coherent multi-turn dialogue. The model is instruction-tuned on SAMA-239K (172K multi-turn grounded QA pairs and 67K object descriptions) generated by an automated pipeline using HQ-SAM for pseudo-masks and Gemini-1.5 Pro for dialogue synthesis, with only the STC module and SAM2 decoder parameters made trainable.

## Results

On the proposed SAMA-Bench, SAMA-8B achieves an mIoU of 0.70 and Recall of 0.55 on SAMA-Bench^G, surpassing the strongest image-level baseline Ferret-13B+SAM2 (mIoU 0.64, Recall 0.43) by 6% and 12% respectively, and outperforming Sa2VA-8B (mIoU 0.64, Recall 0.17) by a wide margin. On SAMA-Bench^C, SAMA-8B attains CIDEr 0.32 and CLAIR 0.50, exceeding Gemini-1.5 Pro (CIDEr 0.27) and all specialist models. On standard video referring segmentation, SAMA-8B sets new SOTA with 53.7 J&F on MeViS, 77.3 on Ref-DAVIS17, and 59.1 on ReVOS, improving over Sa2VA-8B by 2.2, 1.4, and 1.5 points respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAMA-8B decisively outperforms all image-level specialist models (GLaMM, Shikra, Ferret) on SAMA-Bench^G grounding metrics, achieving mIoU 0.70 vs. Ferret-13B+SAM2's 0.64 and GLaMM+SAM2's 0.28, while also dominating on dialogue quality (CIDEr 0.69 vs. Ferret's 0.20). Against the closest video-level baseline Sa2VA-8B, SAMA-8B improves mIoU from 0.64 to 0.70 and Recall from 0.17 to 0.55 on SAMA-Bench^G, and lifts video segmentation J&F by 1.4–2.2 points across MeViS, Ref-DAVIS17, and ReVOS. On SAMA-Bench^C, SAMA-8B surpasses Gemini-1.5 Pro on CIDEr (0.32 vs. 0.27) but trails slightly on CLAIR (0.50 vs. 0.62), indicating Gemini retains an edge in captioning fluency. On general chat benchmarks (Table 3), SAMA-8B scores slightly below Sa2VA-8B (e.g., MMBench 80.8 vs. 84.4, Video-MME 51.8 vs. 54.3) but remains competitive with generalist video LMMs like InternVL2-8B (MMBench 81.7) and mPLUG-Owl3-8B (Video-MME 53.5), confirming that the grounding-focused training causes only a modest dialogue regression. On image referring segmentation (Table 2), SAMA-8B achieves the best RefCOCO (82.7), RefCOCO+ (78.1), and RefCOCOg (80.6) scores, edging out Sa2VA-8B and all prior methods including VISA-13B and GLaMM-7B.

## Relation to Prior Work

SAMA builds directly on Sa2VA, which first married SAM2's segmentation decoder to an LLaVA-style video LMM for dense grounded understanding, and on BLIP-2's Q-Former cross-attention mechanism, which is repurposed here as a three-stage spatio-temporal context aggregator. The key conceptual departure from prior work is the unification of video referring understanding (Artemis, VideoRefer, OmniRGPT) and video grounding (VISA, VideoLISA, VideoGLaMM) into a single multi-turn referential grounded video chat framework, whereas all prior approaches treat these as isolated tasks. The data pipeline is also novel in using Gemini-1.5 Pro to synthesize multi-turn dialogues that explicitly link conversational entities to spatio-temporal masks, going beyond the single-turn QA format of existing video instruction datasets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SAMA-Bench fills a genuine evaluation gap by being the first benchmark to jointly assess referential understanding and grounded dialogue in video, and SAMA-239K provides the first large-scale dataset enabling joint training of these capabilities. However, the paper acknowledges several open questions: the model only handles box-based prompts (not points or masks natively), joint training on grounding and dialogue causes measurable interference (evidenced by slight drops in general chat metrics), and the dataset is limited to short clips, leaving long-range temporal understanding underexplored. The finding that the STC aggregator helps dialogue quality but slightly hurts standard video segmentation suggests a fundamental tension between holistic temporal reasoning and frame-level precision that the field has not yet resolved.
