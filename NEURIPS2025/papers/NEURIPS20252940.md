## Problem

Classical Bayesian neural networks for sequential decision making (e.g., Thompson sampling) require sampling from high-dimensional parameter-space posteriors, which is computationally expensive and often relies on misspecified priors and likelihoods that degrade predictive performance. In fully online, streaming settings—recommender systems, adaptive control, financial forecasting—these costs are prohibitive because updates and decisions must be made in constant time without replay buffers. The paper asks whether one can decouple Bayesian decision-making from parameter-space inference entirely, working directly with the one-step-ahead posterior predictive.

## Input / Output

At each timestep the agent receives a context vector c_t (possibly empty) and a set of candidate actions A, and observes a scalar or vector-valued reward y_t from the environment. The method maintains a belief state b_t = (ℓ_{t|t}, h_{t|t}, Σ̂_{ℓ,t}^{1/2}, C_t) consisting of point estimates for last-layer and hidden-layer parameters plus a Cholesky factor (full-rank) for the last-layer covariance and a low-rank factor C_t ∈ R^{d_h × D_h} for the hidden-layer covariance. For each candidate action, the method evaluates the neural network to obtain a Gaussian posterior predictive (mean and variance), samples one outcome per action, and returns the argmax action along with the updated belief state—no replay buffer or multiple inner iterations are required.

## Method

The core idea is a predictive-first framework inspired by martingale posteriors: instead of maintaining a posterior over parameters and sampling from it (as in Thompson sampling), the method parameterizes the one-step-ahead posterior predictive directly with a neural network and updates its parameters via single-pass frequentist extended Kalman filter recursions. The neural network is linearized around the current estimate, and a random-walk state-space model with per-layer dynamics noise (q_{ℓ,t}, q_{h,t}) yields closed-form predict/update equations. Three structured covariance strategies control the tradeoff between expressiveness and cost: HiLoFi keeps a full-rank Cholesky factor for the last layer and a low-rank factor for hidden layers; LoLoFi uses low-rank factors for both; LRKF uses a single low-rank factor for all parameters. The covariance update avoids forming the full D_θ × D_θ matrix by exploiting QR decompositions of stacked Cholesky factors (for the full-rank block) and truncated SVD of stacked low-rank factors (for the low-rank block), giving per-step cost O(D_ℓ(D_ℓ+D_y)² + D_h(d_h+D_y)²). Decision making then samples once per action from the resulting Gaussian predictive in outcome space (dimension D_y, typically 1), which is 10–100× faster than sampling from the high-dimensional parameter posterior.

## Results

On the MNIST contextual bandit, HiLoFi achieves the highest cumulative reward (31,167.7) with a runtime of 8.44 minutes, outperforming LoLoFi (30,038.5, 3.67 min) and LRKF (28,354.9, 2.51 min). Predictive sampling (pBayes) is 10–100× faster than classical Thompson sampling: for LoFi, pBayes runs in 4.76 min vs. 38.90 min for TS, and for LLL, 1.36 min vs. 191.55 min. On the Kuairec recommender system, HiLoFi achieves the highest average daily reward with the second-lowest running time. In Bayesian optimization, HiLoFi sits on the Pareto frontier between runtime and performance across seven benchmark functions spanning 2D to 200D.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LoFi [8] (diagonal-plus-low-rank precision), HiLoFi achieves substantially higher cumulative reward on MNIST (31,167.7 vs. 23,184.5) at a modest runtime cost (8.44 vs. 4.76 min), while both methods use the same linearization scheme. Against LLL [11] (last-layer Laplace with FIFO buffer), HiLoFi more than doubles the reward (31,167.7 vs. 13,922.3 under pBayes) and avoids the 191.55-min runtime that LLL incurs under TS. Against VBLL [5] (variational Bayesian last layer), HiLoFi matches or exceeds performance on the Kuairec recommender system with lower runtime, and in Bayesian optimization VBLL achieves slightly higher final values but at greater compute cost, placing HiLoFi on the Pareto frontier. Against GP [62], HiLoFi is competitive in low-dimensional BO (2D–6D) but clearly dominates in high-dimensional settings (50D, 200D DrawNN) where GPs become intractable. Overall, the paper's methods consistently win on the performance–speed tradeoff, with the 10–100× speedup over TS being the most striking practical advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper synthesizes two previously separate threads: the martingale posterior framework [20, 26], which advocates a predictive-first view of Bayesian uncertainty, and low-rank extended Kalman filtering for online neural network learning [8, 48]. The key conceptual departure from classical Bayesian neural networks and Thompson sampling [52, 51] is that uncertainty is maintained and updated entirely in the one-step-ahead posterior predictive via frequentist Kalman-filter recursions, rather than through a (approximate) posterior over high-dimensional model parameters. Structurally, the method inherits its covariance approximations from the diagonal-plus-low-rank (DLR) and low-rank Kalman filter literature [8, 36, 47], while the decision-making step (predictive sampling) is a novel reframing of Thompson sampling that sidesteps parameter-space sampling altogether.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 10–100× inference speedup over classical Thompson sampling, achieved while matching or exceeding decision performance, makes principled uncertainty-aware sequential decision making viable in latency-sensitive online settings (recommender systems, adaptive control, high-frequency trading) where replay-buffer or multi-step variational methods are impractical. Limitations noted by the authors include sensitivity to the linearization step, lack of fixed-lag smoothing, and dependence on hand-tuned dynamics covariances (q_ℓ, q_h); extending the framework to fully-online reinforcement learning and to robust/outlier-resistant filtering are identified as natural next steps. The clean separation between frequentist parameter tracking and Bayesian predictive decision-making also opens the door to combining these Kalman-filter updates with arbitrary acquisition functions beyond Thompson sampling.
