## Problem

Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) has proven effective for improving LLM reasoning, but it depends on large-scale instruction datasets with ground-truth labels that are prohibitively expensive to collect in specialized domains like clinical diagnostics or aerospace engineering. Existing self-instruction methods generate static datasets unsuitable for online RL, and treating model-generated responses as labels introduces unreliable reward signals. This paper asks: can an LLM bootstrap its own RL training from only 500 seed instructions, without any external annotations or expert-level teacher models?

## Input / Output

The method consumes a small seed set of 500 math word problems (uniformly sampled from the MATH training set) and, at each training step, generates a rollout batch of new instructions via few-shot prompting (mixing 1/4 seed examples with 3/4 previously generated ones). For each generated instruction, the model is prompted to produce a chain-of-thought reasoning trace followed by a final answer in the format 'The final answer is: [answer]'. Generated instructions are filtered to 3–150 words, must not contain visual-content keywords, must have ROUGE-L < 0.7 against all existing instructions, and must yield a majority-answer ratio between 0.2 and 0.8 across 16 sampled responses. The final output is an updated LLM that, at inference time, takes a text math problem and produces a single greedy-decoded CoT response with a final answer.

## Method

SeRL comprises two tightly coupled modules operating in an iterative self-play loop. The self-instruction module generates a fresh batch of instructions at every training step using few-shot prompting, then applies a four-criteria online filter (ROUGE-L diversity, keyword exclusion, length bounds, and dual-end difficulty clipping at γ_diff=0.2 and γ_easy=0.8) to ensure quality, diversity, and appropriate difficulty matched to the model's current capability. The self-rewarding module replaces verifiable labels with a majority-voting mechanism: for each instruction, n_vote=16 responses are sampled, the majority final answer is identified (ties broken by shortest length), and each response receives reward 1 if it matches the majority, 0 otherwise. These (instruction, response, reward) triplets drive standard policy-gradient RL (Reinforce++ by default, also validated under GRPO and RLOO) for one gradient step per batch. The entire generate-filter-reward-update cycle repeats across multiple iterations, with the few-shot context progressively incorporating newly generated instructions so that data difficulty tracks the model's evolving competence. The dual-end difficulty filter is critical: removing it causes reward hacking where the model converges to outputting a single incorrect answer (e.g., '0') that receives unanimous majority-vote reward.

## Results

On LLaMA-3.2-3B-Instruct, SeRL after 3 iterations achieves 52.6% on MATH-500, 23.7% on MATH-Hard, 89.0% on ASDiv, 37.7% on College Math, and 70.6% on TabMWP (Pass@1, greedy decoding), surpassing the RL-GT baseline (49.7/24.1/88.9/36.4/69.9) on 4 of 5 benchmarks. On Qwen-2.5-7B-Instruct, SeRL (iter3) reaches 75.8/50.4/94.4/55.1/79.4, outperforming RL-GT (74.8/50.9/94.3/55.5/72.7) on MATH-500, ASDiv, and TabMWP. On the general MMLU-Pro benchmark, LLaMA-3.2-3B-Instruct improves from 34.0% (initial) to 35.1% (iter3), surpassing RL-GT (36.1%) only marginally, while Qwen-2.5-7B-Instruct remains roughly flat around 57.3–57.5%. In the medical domain, SeRL with only 500 seed instructions matches or exceeds RL-GT trained on the full 10.2k MedQA dataset (e.g., 56.9 vs 57.2 on MedQA, 27.5 vs 26.1 on NephSAP for LLaMA-3.2-3B).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SeRL consistently outperforms both iterative baselines (SR-DPO and I-RPO) across all three iterations on both model backbones. On LLaMA-3.2-3B-Instruct, SeRL (iter3) leads SR-DPO by 20.1 points on MATH-500 (52.6 vs 32.7) and I-RPO by 10.1 points (52.6 vs 42.5); both baselines show progressive degradation over iterations, with SR-DPO dropping to 29.5 on TabMWP and I-RPO to 52.2. On Qwen-2.5-7B-Instruct, the gap narrows (SeRL 75.8 vs SR-DPO 71.6 vs I-RPO 72.8 on MATH-500 at iter3), but SeRL still wins on all five benchmarks. For reward estimation quality (Table 11), the proposed majority-voting reward achieves 0.75 cosine similarity with ground-truth rule-based rewards, substantially outperforming the entropy-based reward from Zhang et al. (0.65) and the CAI reward model from Bai et al. (0.01), explaining why SR-DPO's model-based reward underperforms especially on the smaller LLaMA backbone. Against RL-GT (the oracle baseline using full MATH training data with verifiable labels), SeRL matches or exceeds it on most benchmarks after 2–3 iterations, validating that self-generated data with majority-voting rewards can substitute for large-scale labeled data.

## Relation to Prior Work

SeRL builds on the Self-Instruct paradigm (Wang et al., ACL 2023) for few-shot instruction generation, but repurposes it from an offline SFT pipeline into an online, iterative RL loop where instructions are regenerated at each training step to track the model's evolving capability. On the reward side, it departs from model-based self-rewarding methods (Yuan et al. 2025; Bai et al. 2022) that rely on the model to score individual responses, instead adopting a majority-voting mechanism over multiple sampled answers—a simpler strategy that the paper shows is more stable and accurate. The closest concurrent work, TTRL (Zuo et al. 2025), also uses majority voting in unsupervised RL but operates on a fixed test set in a single round, whereas SeRL generates diverse training data and supports multi-round iterative self-improvement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that SeRL enables effective RL-based reasoning improvement in specialized, data-scarce domains (e.g., clinical diagnostics, aerospace) where expert-annotated data with verifiable labels is prohibitively expensive to obtain, matching the performance of full-data RL-GT with only 500 seed instructions. A key open question is the self-iteration ceiling: Maj@K does not improve across iterations, meaning the method converts latent Pass@K ability into Pass@1 but cannot fundamentally expand the model's reasoning capacity. The critical dependence on difficulty filtering to prevent reward hacking (where the model converges to outputting '0') highlights that naive self-rewarding is fragile, and designing robust curriculum-like mechanisms for unsupervised RL remains an active area.
