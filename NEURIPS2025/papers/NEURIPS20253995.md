## Problem

In conditional diffusion policy training for VLA models, when generative conditions are difficult for the network to distinguish, the flow-matching objective degenerates into modeling the marginal action distribution—a failure mode the authors term loss collapse. This creates a destructive feedback loop where the policy increasingly ignores conditional inputs, producing actions disconnected from observations. The paper provides the first theoretical characterization of this phenomenon and a principled fix.

## Input / Output

The policy consumes multi-view RGB images (two views in LIBERO/SO100, one 3rd-person view in xArm), a natural-language instruction, and robot joint angles (current plus previous 10 steps on real robots), all encoded by frozen DINOv2-Base and T5-Base encoders. It outputs action chunks of target absolute joint angles—length 16 for simulation (8 steps executed per chunk) and length 64 for real robots (40 steps executed per chunk). The key structural detail is that the diffusion source sample x₀ is drawn from a condition-dependent Gaussian rather than a standard N(0,I), making the initial noise itself carry semantic information about the task.

## Method

Cocos replaces the standard Gaussian source distribution in conditional flow matching with a condition-conditioned Gaussian q(x₀|c) = N(x₀; αF_φ(E(c)), β²I), where F_φ is a single-layer Transformer autoencoder trained to preserve condition semantics via a negative cosine-similarity reconstruction loss. The theoretical contribution (Theorem 1) proves that under the standard independent sampling q(x₀)q(x₁,c), gradient differences between conditions are bounded by O(ε), forcing the network to converge to a condition-independent function; under the conditional sampling q(x₀|c)q(x₁,c), no such bound exists, preventing collapse. The policy backbone is a compact 40M-parameter RDT (12 layers, hidden dim 384) using cross-attention for condition injection and AdaLN-Zero for diffusion timestamps, with a linear-interpolation flow-matching schedule. The autoencoder can be trained in two stages (pretrain then freeze) or jointly with an EMA target network for stability. Setting α=0, β=1 recovers the standard Gaussian prior, making Cocos a strict generalization.

## Results

On the LIBERO benchmark (40 tasks, 4 suites), DP-DINOv2 + Cocos achieves 94.8% average success rate, improving over DP-DINOv2 (86.5%) by 8.3% and over DP-scratch (64.4%) by 30.4%. Cocos reaches >95% on Spatial (97.5%) and Object (99.1%) suites within 5–10K training steps, converging 2.14× faster than the vanilla model. On MetaWorld (30 tasks), Cocos yields a 25.7% average gain over the no-Cocos counterpart (74.8% vs. 59.5% on the first 10-task block). The method matches π₀ performance on LIBERO with only 30K gradient steps and ~40M parameters, versus 100–250K steps for π₀.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against π₀ (fine-tuned, ~3B params, 100–250K gradient steps), DP-DINOv2 + Cocos (~40M params, 30K steps) achieves comparable LIBERO success rates, demonstrating that proper condition integration can substitute for model scale. Against OpenVLA-OFT (fine-tuned, 50–150K steps), Cocos matches performance at roughly one-third to one-fifth the training budget. On the challenging LIBERO-Long suite, Cocos (88.7%) outperforms Seer, a non-end-to-end predictive model with a larger parameter count. Within the DP family, Cocos improves over DP-DINOv2 by 8.3% on average across all four LIBERO suites and by 25.7% on MetaWorld, with the largest gains on the Long suite (+6.0%) where multi-step condition sensitivity is most critical. On real-world SO100 and xArm platforms, Cocos consistently improves success rates across all 20 tasks, with particularly pronounced gains on SO100 where hardware jitter amplifies condition-misinterpretation failures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the conditional flow matching framework (Lipman et al., ICLR 2023) as applied to diffusion policies for robot manipulation (Chi et al., RSS 2023; Liu et al., ICLR 2025). Prior VLA training-efficiency work has focused on improving condition encoders, action tokenizers, or conditioning injection strategies within the policy network, but has not questioned the source distribution itself. Cocos departs from this lineage by modifying the generative process rather than the network: it replaces the standard Gaussian prior with a condition-conditioned Gaussian, a change that is architecturally minimal but theoretically consequential. The formal identification of 'loss collapse'—where the CFM objective degenerates into an unconditional marginal when conditions are indistinguishable—is a new theoretical contribution not present in prior flow-matching or diffusion policy literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Cocos offers a plug-and-play, architecture-agnostic fix for a previously uncharacterized failure mode in VLA training, with the practical implication that compact (~40M parameter) policies can match large-scale pre-trained VLAs (π0, OpenVLA-OFT) on task-specific benchmarks with 2.14× fewer gradient steps. The finding that the policy network actively *omits* conditions rather than merely using them suboptimally reframes how practitioners should diagnose poor VLA performance. Open questions include whether Cocos transfers to large-scale multi-task pretraining, whether more expressive condition-dependent priors (flow-based, attention-guided) yield further gains, and whether the fixed-variance Gaussian is sufficient for highly heterogeneous action distributions.
