## Problem

Despite numerous debiasing techniques for VQA, the fundamental question of how language bias actually forms in multimodal models remained unexplored. This paper identifies that inherent data heterogeneity between modalities causes modality-specific gradient imbalances, which propagate into skewed feature fusion and biased classifier weights, and provides the first systematic empirical and theoretical account of this bias formation pipeline.

## Input / Output

The method operates on the UpDn architecture, consuming RGB images (encoded into 36 object vectors of dimension 2048 via Faster R-CNN) and natural language questions (encoded into 300-dimensional GloVe embeddings aggregated by a single-layer GRU). The two unimodal representations are fused via Hadamard product, and the output is a class label over the VQA answer vocabulary. Auxiliary question-only and image-only classifiers are also maintained to compute confidence-aware margins during training.

## Method

MMCD reshapes decision boundaries on the unit hypersphere through two complementary mechanisms. First, Multi-Grained Adaptive Margins (MAM) impose three adaptive angular penalties: frequency-aware margins (larger offsets for underrepresented answers, regularized with Gaussian noise), confidence-aware margins (derived from a weighted hybrid of multimodal, question-only, and image-only logits), and difficulty-aware margins (computed from a per-instance difficulty model combining learning speed via Jensen-Shannon divergence and classification margin gaps). Second, Difficulty-aware Contrastive Learning (DCL) integrates the same difficulty model into a supervised contrastive loss, up-weighting hard samples to enhance intra-class compactness and inter-class separation. The training objective transitions from standard supervised contrastive loss during a warm-up phase to DCL afterward, and the paper provides gradient-level and SVD-based theoretical proofs showing that margin penalties rebalance modality gradients, uniformize fused feature composition, and equalize the classifier's singular value spectrum.

## Results

On VQA-CP v2, MMCD achieves 61.34% overall accuracy, surpassing the second-best method CVIV (60.08%) by 1.26 points and the prior SOTA GGD (59.37%) by 1.97 points. On VQA-CP v1, MMCD reaches 63.62% overall accuracy, outperforming GENB (62.74%) by 0.88 points. The gains are most pronounced on the 'number' category (55.68% on VQA-CP v2, a 2.79-point improvement over the next best), indicating strong mitigation of language priors in numerically-biased questions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MMCD outperforms all 12 compared baselines on overall accuracy for both VQA-CP v2 and VQA-CP v1. On VQA-CP v2, it leads CVIV (60.08%) by 1.26 points and GGD (59.37%) by 1.97 points; on VQA-CP v1, it surpasses GENB (62.74%) by 0.88 points and AdaVQA (61.20%) by 2.42 points. The advantage is largest on the 'number' subcategory (55.68% on CPv2, 52.67% on CPv1), where language priors are strongest, while on the 'Others' subcategory MMCD trails GENB (47.03%) and GGE-iter (49.77%) on CPv1/CPv2 respectively, suggesting the method's gains are concentrated in bias-heavy question types. Across alternative architectures (SAN, S-MRL, LXMERT), MMCD consistently adds 18–33 points over the unmodified baselines, confirming architecture-agnostic effectiveness.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the margin-based debiasing line (ArcFace, CosFace, AdaVQA, RMLVQA) from fixed or single-grained angular penalties to an adaptive multi-grained scheme that jointly considers answer frequency, prediction confidence, and instance-level learning difficulty. It departs from prior VQA debiasing work (ensemble-based like RUBi/GGE, augmentation-based like counterfactual synthesis) by providing the first systematic theoretical analysis of *why* margin losses reduce language bias—showing they rebalance modality-specific gradients, homogenize fused feature composition, and equalize classifier weight singular spectra. The difficulty-aware contrastive learning component adapts supervised contrastive learning (Khosla et al.) with a novel instance-difficulty estimator built from Jensen-Shannon divergence of prediction trajectories and classification margin gaps.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical decomposition of bias formation (gradient imbalance → feature fusion skew → classifier directional bias) gives practitioners a principled diagnostic framework for understanding *where* in the pipeline language priors entrench, which can inform debiasing strategies beyond VQA (e.g., shortcut learning, long-tail recognition). However, the practical gains are modest (1.26% over the second-best method on VQA-CP v2), the ablation reveals DCL contributes only ~0.25% over the full margin stack, and the difficulty model's added value over simpler confidence-based margins is marginal. Open questions include whether the gradient-imbalance analysis generalizes to transformer-based fusion (beyond Hadamard product) and whether the adaptive margins transfer to open-ended VQA or other multimodal tasks without re-derivation.
