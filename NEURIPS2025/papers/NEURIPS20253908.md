## Problem

Plant trait prediction from hyperspectral data is severely constrained by label scarcity and substantial domain shifts across sensors, ecosystems, and acquisition conditions, causing supervised models to generalize poorly. No large-scale, multi-source unlabeled spectral dataset existed to support semi- or self-supervised pretraining for this task, and no prior work applied such methods to multi-output trait regression.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253908/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 1D canopy surface reflectance spectra spanning 400–2450 nm (1721 bands, full-range) or 400–900 nm (500 bands, half-range), harmonized to at-surface reflectance from proximal, airborne, and spaceborne platforms. It produces simultaneous regression estimates of seven plant functional traits (leaf mass per area, protein content, equivalent water thickness, chlorophyll, carotenoids, anthocyanins, and LAI) via a multi-output head. The pretraining corpus (GreenHyperSpectra) contains over 140,000 unlabeled spectra, while the labeled evaluation set comprises 7,900 spectra with co-located trait measurements aggregated from 50 field campaigns.

## Method

The paper benchmarks three semi-/self-supervised frameworks for 1D spectral multi-output regression, all pretrained on GreenHyperSpectra: (1) SR-GAN, a 1D convolutional GAN where the discriminator performs trait regression while a feature-contrasting loss pulls real spectra together and pushes generated spectra apart in embedding space; (2) RTM-AE, an autoencoder whose decoder is replaced by the non-learnable PROSAIL-PRO radiative transfer model, constraining the latent space to physically meaningful trait parameters and adding a learnable correction layer to bridge the simulation–observation gap; and (3) a 1D masked autoencoder (MAE) with a transformer encoder that reconstructs randomly masked spectral tokens using a composite cosine-similarity + MSE loss, followed by fine-tuning with a multi-output regression head. The MAE with full fine-tuning (MAE-FR-FT) achieves the best performance across full-range, half-range, and out-of-distribution settings, improving average R² by ~9% over the supervised EfficientNet-B0 baseline, and demonstrates strong cross-spectral transfer when pretrained on full-range spectra and applied to half-range inputs.

## Results

On the full-range (400–2450 nm) trait prediction benchmark, the fine-tuned 1D Masked Autoencoder (MAE-FR-FT) achieves the best average R² of 0.641 (±0.020) and lowest average nRMSE of 12.78 (±0.36) across seven plant functional traits, outperforming the supervised EfficientNet-B0 baseline (R² = 0.587, nRMSE = 13.70) by ~9% in R² and ~6% in nRMSE. In the out-of-distribution cross-dataset evaluation, MAE-FR-FT maintains the highest average R² (0.311) versus the supervised baseline (0.243). In the half-range (VNIR-only, 400–900 nm) setting, semi- and self-supervised methods outperform the supervised baseline by 100–200% in R² and 8–27% in nRMSE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper benchmarks four methods against a supervised EfficientNet-B0 multi-output regression baseline [Cherif et al. 2023]. MAE-FR-FT (fine-tuned masked autoencoder) decisively outperforms all baselines on full-range spectra, achieving 0.641 average R² versus 0.587 for the supervised baseline, 0.592 for SR-GAN [Olmschenk et al. 2019], and 0.592 for RTM-AE [She et al. 2024]. The RTM-AE, which constrains the latent space via the PROSAIL-PRO radiative transfer model, consistently ranks second but lags MAE-FR-FT by ~5% in average R², particularly on anthocyanins (0.337 vs. 0.598). SR-GAN shows modest gains over the supervised baseline (+0.5% R²) but is outperformed by both MAE variants. Critically, the linear-probing variant (MAE-FR-LP) underperforms the supervised baseline (0.466 vs. 0.587 R²), demonstrating that fine-tuning the pretrained encoder is essential for trait-specific spectral feature extraction. In the OOD cross-dataset setting, MAE-FR-FT retains a clear advantage (0.311 vs. 0.243 R²), while in the half-range VNIR-only setting, all semi/self-supervised methods surpass the supervised baseline by 100–200% in R².

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts three distinct semi/self-supervised frameworks—1D masked autoencoders (from Thoreau et al.'s hyperspectral classification work), feature-contrasting semi-supervised GANs (from Olmschenk et al.'s regression GAN), and RTM-constrained autoencoders (from She et al.)—to the 1D spectral domain for multi-output plant trait regression. It departs from the dominant supervised paradigm (PLSR, GPR, and the EfficientNet-based multi-trait CNN of Cherif et al.) by leveraging a newly curated 140K+ sample multi-source pretraining corpus (GreenHyperSpectra) spanning proximal, airborne, and spaceborne sensors, rather than relying on RTM-simulated spectra or single-sensor benchmarks. The paper positions itself as the first to apply semi/self-supervised pretraining to multi-output trait regression, filling a gap it identifies in the literature where prior semi/self-supervised trait work was limited to single traits or single sensor configurations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The GreenHyperSpectra dataset addresses a critical bottleneck in plant trait remote sensing: the scarcity of labeled, ecologically diverse spectra needed to train generalizable models. By demonstrating that MAE-based pretraining yields consistent gains over supervised baselines—especially in low-label regimes and under sensor noise—the paper provides a practical pathway toward operational, label-efficient trait monitoring across heterogeneous sensor platforms. Open questions remain about whether the modest OOD improvements (R² 0.31 vs. 0.24) will hold as labeled data from underrepresented biomes grows, whether the stratified-splitting protocol generalizes beyond the current 20-subset design, and how learned representations transfer across spatial resolutions (drone → satellite). The dataset and pretrained checkpoints are released as community resources, which should accelerate follow-up work on cross-domain spectral representation learning.
