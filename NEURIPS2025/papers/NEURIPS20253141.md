## Problem

Standard Concept Bottleneck Models require a pre-specified pool of candidate concepts, creating a fundamental tradeoff: a large pool risks missing relevant concepts (hurting accuracy) while a small pool is cheap but incomplete. Moreover, existing LLM-assisted CBM methods either rely on a fixed concept list or use 'soft' continuous concepts that leak label information and resist human audit. This paper asks whether concepts can be discovered iteratively and data-adaptively from a potentially infinite space while preserving the interpretability and statistical rigor of a transparent model.

## Input / Output

The method takes raw observations (RGB images, clinical note text, or tabular features) and a supervised target Y, with no pre-specified concept list required. An LLM first extracts free-form keyphrases from each observation, then iteratively proposes and annotates K binary/categorical concept questions (e.g., 'Does the note mention the patient smoking?'). The output is a transparent logistic-regression CBM, a full posterior distribution over K-concept sets (enabling concept-level uncertainty quantification), and calibrated predictive probabilities. Critically, the method annotates only O(KT) concepts per observation (typically K≈5, T≈5) rather than the hundreds or thousands required by standard CBMs.

## Method

BC-LLM runs Metropolis-within-Gibbs over a space of K-concept sets, where the key innovation is a split-sample Metropolis update: the LLM proposes candidate concepts conditioned on a random data subset S (serving as a 'partial posterior' proposal), and the accept-reject step evaluates the candidate against the held-out complement S^c via a partial Bayes factor, correcting LLM hallucinations and miscalibrations. A multiple-try variant batches M candidate proposals per iteration to amortize LLM annotation costs. Initialization uses LLM-extracted keyphrases to fit a bag-of-words logistic model, from which the LLM brainstorms an initial K-concept set. The paper proves that the resulting Markov chain converges to the true concepts asymptotically even when the LLM prior is poor or self-inconsistent, and that the Bayesian framework provides calibrated uncertainty that improves out-of-distribution performance.

## Results

On the CUB-birds family-level classification task (37 tasks), BC-LLM achieves 68.0% accuracy, 0.874 AUC, and 0.428 Brier score, outperforming all interpretable CBM baselines and the black-box ResNet50 (66.4% accuracy, 0.853 AUC, 0.457 Brier). On the MIMIC-IV simulation with known true concepts, BC-LLM matches the oracle Human+CBM with only 400 observations and recovers true concepts more accurately than all comparators. On the real-world heart-failure readmission task, BC-LLM improves AUC from 0.60 (original tabular model) to 0.64, with clinicians rating its concepts at 2.5/3 for clinical relevance versus ≤2 for all other methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BC-LLM outperforms all five baselines on the primary CUB-birds task. Against LLM+CBM (Benara et al., NeurIPS 2024), BC-LLM gains +4.0 pp accuracy (0.680 vs 0.640) and +0.064 AUC, with the gap widening as training data increases because BC-LLM iteratively refines concepts. Against Boosting LLM+CBM (Ludan et al., 2023), the advantage is largest: +14.2 pp accuracy and +0.152 AUC, as the boosting approach struggles to hypothesize relevant concepts from few misclassified examples. BC-LLM also beats Human+CBM (Koh et al., ICML 2020) by +2.2 pp accuracy and +0.039 AUC, likely because it can explore concepts beyond the 312 pre-annotated CUB features. Against the LLM+CBM (No keyphrases) variant using 370 pre-specified concepts from Oikarinen et al. (ICLR 2023), BC-LLM gains +12.5 pp accuracy, demonstrating the benefit of data-adaptive concept search over a fixed pool. Notably, BC-LLM also surpasses the black-box ResNet50 (He et al., CVPR 2016) by +1.6 pp accuracy and +0.021 AUC, while providing calibrated uncertainty and fully interpretable concepts; ResNet50 only edges out BC-LLM on OOD entropy (0.914 vs 0.865), reflecting its greater capacity to express uncertainty without concept constraints.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Concept Bottleneck Model (CBM) framework of Koh et al. by replacing the standard pre-specified concept pool with an iterative, data-adaptive search over a potentially infinite concept space. The core sampling procedure builds on Metropolis-within-Gibbs (Gilks et al.) and multiple-try Metropolis-Hastings (Liu et al.), with the key novelty being a 'split-sample' update where the LLM proposes concepts from a partial posterior (seeing only a fraction of data) and the remaining data corrects LLM errors via a fractional Bayes factor acceptance ratio (O'Hagan). This departs from prior LLM+CBM approaches (Benara et al., Ludan et al.) that either propose concepts non-iteratively or use boosting-style heuristics without statistical guarantees, and from soft-concept CBMs that suffer from information leakage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BC-LLM addresses the fundamental interpretability-accuracy tradeoff in CBMs by showing that a small number of iteratively discovered, fully interpretable concepts (K≈5) can match or exceed black-box models on small-to-medium datasets while providing calibrated uncertainty and OOD robustness. The theoretical guarantee that convergence holds even under a miscalibrated or inconsistent LLM prior is important for deploying LLM-assisted methods in regulated settings. Limitations include the restriction to K≤20 concepts for interpretability, dependence on LLM annotation quality, and the computational cost of O(nTK) LLM queries; open questions include scaling to larger concept sets via mini-batching and extending beyond logistic regression to more complex transparent predictors.
