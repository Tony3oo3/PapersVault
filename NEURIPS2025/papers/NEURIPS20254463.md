## Problem

Unsupervised domain adaptive hashing suffers from two intertwined issues: pseudo-labels generated by the adapted model carry noise that misguides target-domain learning (especially early in training), and existing alignment strategies either operate in a single space or tightly couple the high-dimensional feature space with the low-dimensional Hamming space, restricting the flexibility needed to exploit each space's complementary strengths.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images from a labeled source domain and an unlabeled target domain, processed through a VGG feature encoder and a frozen CLIP ViT-B/16 image encoder. The CLIP text encoder encodes class descriptions of the form 'a photo of a [CLASS_c]' to provide zero-shot class predictions. The output is a compact binary hash code of configurable length (16–128 bits) per image, enabling Hamming-distance-based retrieval in both cross-domain (query from target, retrieval from source) and single-domain (both from target) settings.

## Method

VPDS introduces a decoupled dual-space adaptation strategy that performs prototype-based global domain alignment independently in the feature space and the Hamming space, each using momentum-updated memory banks of class prototypes, rather than enforcing strict cross-space structural consistency. For pseudo-labeling, a frozen CLIP model provides zero-shot class predictions that are blended with the adapted model's predictions via a linearly increasing weight β = e/E, so that early training relies more on the VLM's generalization while later training trusts the progressively adapted model. The training objective combines a supervised cross-entropy loss on source data (through separate classifiers in each space), a self-supervised loss on target hash codes guided by the VLM-calibrated pseudo-labels, information maximization in both spaces to prevent class collapse, and the dual-space prototype alignment loss. During training, the non-differentiable sign function is replaced by tanh for gradient flow, while inference uses the exact sign operation.

## Results

On the Office-Home and Office-31 cross-domain retrieval tasks with 64-bit codes, VPDS achieves an average MAP of 75.56%, outperforming the second-best method CPH (68.31%) by 7.25%. On the Digits dataset (MNIST↔USPS), VPDS reaches 85.83% average MAP across all code lengths, surpassing the runner-up COUPLE (68.53%) by 17.30%. In single-domain retrieval, VPDS also leads all baselines by substantial margins (e.g., 59.45% vs. 51.40% on Office-Home P→R at 128 bits). The method also demonstrates strong scalability (unsupervised setting) and generalization (out-of-sample setting), maintaining competitive performance even when retrieval data is excluded from training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VPDS outperforms all 14 baselines on the average MAP across Office-Home and Office-31 cross-domain retrieval. The closest competitor is CPH (68.31%), which uses a hypersphere-based joint alignment; VPDS gains 7.25% over it, with particularly large margins on Office-31 tasks (e.g., 87.22% vs. 68.37% on A→D, 91.40% vs. 60.61% on A→W). Among the joint-alignment methods, COUPLE (60.29%) and IDEA (57.03%) trail VPDS by 15.27% and 18.53% respectively. The earlier domain-adaptive hashing methods (PEACE 51.22%, DANCE 51.31%, DHLing 46.54%) are surpassed by 24–29 percentage points. Unsupervised hashing baselines (ITQ 30.24%, DSH 15.74%, SGH 26.94%, OCH 24.17%, GraphBit 24.49%) are all far below VPDS, confirming the benefit of domain adaptation. On the Digits dataset, VPDS (85.83%) leads COUPLE (68.53%) by 17.30% and CPH (65.81%) by 20.02%, with the largest gap at 16-bit codes (86.21% vs. 64.55% for CPH on MNIST→USPS). VPDS also wins on single-domain retrieval (Table 3), e.g., 59.45% vs. 51.40% (CPH) on Office-Home P→R at 128 bits and 89.30% vs. 72.46% on MNIST→USPS at 128 bits.

## Relation to Prior Work

VPDS positions itself within the domain adaptive hashing retrieval literature, departing from prior joint-alignment methods (PEACE's feature-hash concatenation, CPH's hypersphere consistency) by proposing decoupled, independent alignment in the feature and Hamming spaces. It repurposes the zero-shot generalization of frozen CLIP as an external pseudo-label calibrator, a role not previously exploited in hashing-based retrieval. The pseudo-labeling and information-maximization components draw on standard UDA techniques (DAMP-style mutual prompting, self-augmented training), while the prototype-based global alignment with momentum-updated memory banks extends the class-prototype alignment paradigm to a domain-level formulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The decoupled dual-space strategy offers a clean conceptual alternative to the tight cross-space coupling that dominates prior domain adaptive hashing, and the VLM-assisted pseudo-labeling is explicitly model-agnostic, making it a plug-in component for other unsupervised or semi-supervised retrieval frameworks. However, the method is limited to two-domain adaptation (source→target), and the reliance on a frozen CLIP model means it inherits CLIP's blind spots for fine-grained or out-of-distribution classes. Open questions include whether the approach extends to multi-source or source-free settings, whether the VLM component remains beneficial when the adapted model has already converged, and how the method scales to larger code lengths or higher-dimensional feature backbones beyond VGG.
