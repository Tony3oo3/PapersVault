## Problem

Non-contrast chest CT scans routinely capture the breast region but are underexploited for breast cancer analysis due to the subtle appearance of lesions without contrast enhancement. Existing approaches treat lesion detection and malignancy classification as separate tasks and process each breast independently, overlooking the bilateral asymmetry cues that radiologists rely on. This paper addresses the need for a unified framework that jointly models both tasks while leveraging cross-breast context on NCCT.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250857/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes a full non-contrast chest CT volume (1×D×512×512, D varying by acquisition) and first crops bilateral breast regions to a uniform 56×160×192 size via a pre-trained nnUNet-based Pre-Stage. The Main-Stage consumes both cropped breasts simultaneously as a tandem bilateral input, producing a voxel-wise three-class segmentation mask (background, breast, lesion) at full resolution and a single breast-level malignancy probability score. During inference, a sliding-window strategy with 8-way mirroring TTA is applied, and the final classification score is the maximum across windows.

## Method

DRT-M3D builds on Mamba-3D (selective SSM with linear complexity) and introduces three key mechanisms. First, a dual-resolution architecture splits features into a high-resolution path (patch 2×4×4, C=192) for voxel-level segmentation and a low-resolution path (downsampled 2×4×4, C'=384) for breast-level classification, with mutual fusion modules that downsample HR features into the LR path and upsample LR features back into the HR path at every block. Second, a tandem input mechanism organizes left and right breast tokens into a bilateral pseudo-3D space, flattens them with dimension-priority permutations (HWD, DWH, DHW), and concatenates them end-to-end so that Mamba-3D scans all voxels of one breast before the other, guaranteeing a roughly fixed sequence distance between anatomically symmetric locations and enabling cross-side feature interaction. Third, the model is trained in two stages: 500 epochs of masked autoencoder pre-training (masking ratio 0.8, with mask sizes matched to the HR-to-LR downsampling factors to prevent information leakage) followed by 50 epochs of joint fine-tuning using a weighted sum of Dice+CE segmentation loss and CE classification loss (λ=0.1).

## Results

On the three internal datasets, DRT-M3D achieves AUC of 0.9909 (Inst. 1), 0.9743 (Inst. 2), and 0.9471 (Inst. 3) for breast-level malignancy classification, with Dice scores of 0.6608, 0.6750, and 0.6056 for lesion segmentation. On the external dataset (Inst. 4, a distinct institution), DRT-M3D attains AUC 0.9371 and sensitivity 0.8762 at a fixed specificity of 0.9083, alongside a Dice of 0.5948 and FROC-Score of 0.8766. The tandem input mechanism (TI) contributes the largest classification gain: adding TI to DR-M3D raises external sensitivity from 0.7207 to 0.8762 and AUC from 0.9082 to 0.9371.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DRT-M3D outperforms all 13 baselines on every metric across both internal and external datasets. Against the strongest general-purpose segmentation baseline nnUNet, DRT-M3D improves Dice by ~2.8 points (0.5948 vs 0.5668) and AUC by 4.8 points (0.9371 vs 0.8889) on the external set. The most directly comparable NCCT-specific method, Sun et al. 2025 [56], is surpassed by DRT-M3D on Dice (0.5948 vs 0.5624), FROC-Score (0.8766 vs 0.8246), and AUC (0.9371 vs 0.8827) externally. Among Mamba-based competitors, DRT-M3D leads U-Mamba-Bot by 5.3 Dice points and 3.9 AUC points externally, and SegMamba by 5.6 Dice points and 2.9 AUC points. The single-task nnUNet-CLS variant achieves the second-best AUC (0.9156) but with substantially lower sensitivity (0.7238 vs 0.8762), confirming that the multi-task dual-resolution design yields complementary gains in both detection and classification.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Mamba-3D block (from Mamba-ND) and the MAE pre-training strategy for Mamba-3D, extending them into a dual-resolution multi-task architecture for 3D medical imaging. It departs from prior Mamba-based segmentation methods (U-Mamba, SegMamba, EM-Net) by introducing a tandem bilateral input mechanism and a high/low-resolution split with mutual fusion, addressing the joint detection-classification problem that prior single-task approaches ignore. The work also extends the limited prior literature on breast cancer analysis from non-contrast chest CT (Sun et al. 2025, Koh et al. 2022) by incorporating bilateral context, which radiologists routinely use but which has been largely absent from 3D deep learning pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The opportunistic screening angle—extracting breast cancer signals from routine chest CTs without additional radiation or cost—has clear clinical value, and the bilateral tandem input concept is a transferable design principle applicable to other paired organs (lungs, kidneys). However, the study is limited to a single country's data, uses only NCCT (not contrast-enhanced), and the Pre-Stage cropping introduces a dependency on a separate segmentation model. Open questions include whether the bilateral modeling benefit persists with larger, more diverse multi-ethnic datasets, and whether the tandem input strategy generalizes to other 3D bilateral organ tasks beyond breast.
