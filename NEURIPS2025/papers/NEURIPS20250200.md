## Problem

Despite the widespread use of graph-structured systems in power grids, traffic networks, and communication infrastructure, no standardized open-source benchmark suite exists for cooperative multi-agent RL problems with inherent network topology that supports scalability to 10²–10⁴ agents, arbitrary graph structures, and partial observability. Existing MARL benchmarks either target toy navigation/communication tasks or realistic single-agent simulators, leaving a gap between theoretical DecPOMDP research and practical evaluation of distributed learning methods on large networked systems.

## Input / Output

Each environment is instantiated with a user-defined weighted adjacency matrix (defining agent connectivity and influence probabilities) and a configurable number of agents N. At each timestep, agents receive noisy local observations (e.g., fire level at their house with probability-dependent accuracy, or votes of graph neighbors) and emit discrete or continuous actions; the environment returns per-agent rewards, termination flags (e.g., consensus reached, fire extinguished), and truncation signals. All environments are implemented as self-contained PettingZoo parallel environments, and the joint state/action spaces grow exponentially (2^N, 9^N, θ^N, ρ_max^(k×E)), making centralized model-free approaches intractable beyond small N.

## Method

COGNAC provides four cooperative DecPOMDP environments—Firefighting Graph (1D/2D), Binary Consensus, SysAdmin Network, and Multi-commodity Flow—each adapted from classical theoretical problems and implemented as modular PettingZoo environments with graph-agnostic initialization (for Consensus and SysAdmin) and configurable dynamics. The empirical benchmark compares independent learning (IPPO, IDQL), CTDE methods (MAPPO, Q-Mix), fully centralized PPO, and a GNN-based aggregator (InforMARL) across varying agent counts (10–10,000) and network densities (Erdős-Rényi graphs). The key finding is that IPPO, a fully decentralized independent actor-critic method, consistently achieves the highest-quality joint policies and scales robustly to 10⁴ agents with minimal hyperparameter tuning, while CTDE methods suffer from the curse of dimensionality in their centralized critic and fail to generalize to larger or more interdependent networks. This suggests that standard CTDE paradigms require additional inter-agent communication or structural inductive biases (e.g., GNN aggregators) to capture network topology effectively.

## Results

Across all four COGNAC environments, IPPO consistently achieves the highest-quality joint policies with minimal hyperparameter tuning, rapidly converging to near-optimal performance on SysAdmin (100 agents) and scaling to 10,000 agents (~10⁶ edges) without any retuning. Centralized PPO fails entirely on Binary Consensus at N=100 agents (action space 2¹⁰⁰), while IPPO learns a working policy on the same instance. On Multi-Commodity Flow, IPPO outperforms a centralized linear-program heuristic, and on Grid Firefighting it surpasses a random-selection baseline. CTDE methods (MAPPO, QMIX) converge more slowly, exhibit greater variance, and fail to generalize to larger or more interdependent network instances.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IPPO [36] is the strongest performer across all environments, beating IDQL on convergence speed (IDQL requires substantially more training steps on Binary Consensus and underperforms on other discrete tasks), beating MAPPO [39] and QMIX [38] on both convergence speed and final policy quality (CTDE methods are slower, less stable, and fail to scale beyond ~100 agents with strong inter-agent dependencies), and beating fully centralized PPO which collapses at N=100 on Binary Consensus due to the combinatorial action space. InforMARL [41] (GNN-based aggregator) matches IPPO on SysAdmin but requires a centralized critic with full state knowledge and additional neighborhood information, whereas IPPO needs neither. On the 10,000-agent SysAdmin instance, IPPO still converges to an efficient policy while IDQL is stopped early due to prohibitively slow convergence, and CTDE methods are excluded entirely due to infeasible runtime from the centralized value function.

## Relation to Prior Work

COGNAC adapts four well-known theoretical graph-based DecPOMDP problems (Firefighting Graph, Binary Consensus/voter model, SysAdmin Network, Multi-commodity Flow) into standardized, open-source PettingZoo environments for the first time, filling a gap between theoretical MARL literature and practical benchmarking. It positions itself against existing MARL suites (MPE, SMAC, POGEMA, Grid2Op) that either focus on toy navigation/coordination tasks or lack the modularity and graph-agnostic scalability needed for network-structured cooperative control. The empirical evaluation draws on standard independent learning (IPPO, IDQL) and CTDE (MAPPO, Q-Mix) baselines from CleanRL, plus the GNN-based InforMARL aggregator, situating the benchmark within the broader CTDE-vs-independent-learning debate.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

COGNAC provides the first modular, graph-agnostic benchmark suite for cooperative MARL at scale (up to 10⁴ agents), enabling systematic study of how network topology, density, and size affect learning dynamics — a question that has been largely unaddressed due to the absence of standardized environments. The finding that simple independent actor-critic (IPPO) outperforms CTDE and fully centralized methods at scale, while CTDE methods fail to generalize beyond ~100 agents, suggests that centralized critics face the curse of dimensionality even in cooperative settings and motivates future work on communication-augmented or GNN-based decentralized methods. Open questions include whether GNN aggregators (e.g., InforMARL) can close the gap, whether theoretical guarantees exist for specific graph structures, and whether the Python-only implementation limits practical deployment to truly large-scale systems.
