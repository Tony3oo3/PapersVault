## Problem

Existing diffusion-based inverse problem solvers either enforce measurement consistency through heuristic projection steps that require manual tuning, or approximate the likelihood p(y|x) via Tweedie-based gradient corrections that distort posterior geometry under high noise. Because these approaches modify data-space and measurement-space dynamics in an uncoordinated manner, generated samples can drift off the learned data manifold or fail to match the observed measurements, producing artifacts and instability.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a noisy measurement vector y ∈ R^m, a known linear forward operator A ∈ R^{m×d}, the measurement noise covariance Σ_n, and a pre-trained score network s_θ(x_t, t) (typically a U-Net or Transformer). It outputs a reconstructed signal x_0 ∈ R^d, evaluated on 256×256 RGB images from FFHQ and ImageNet under five corruption models: random/box inpainting, Gaussian/motion deblurring, and 4× super-resolution. A latent-space variant (LC-DPS) operates entirely within the latent representation of a pretrained generative model for improved efficiency on high-resolution data.

## Method

C-DPS introduces a parallel forward Markov chain in the measurement space {y_t} that starts from the known observation y_0 = Ax_0 + n and progressively injects Gaussian noise using the same variance schedule {β_t} as the data-space diffusion {x_t}. This symmetric coupling allows the authors to express the conditional p(y_{t-1}|x_{t-1}) in closed form via Tweedie's formula, then freeze the score network at the current iterate x_t (justified empirically by high cosine similarity between s_θ(x_{t-1},t-1) and s_θ(x_t,t)) to render the joint posterior p(x_{t-1}|x_t, y_{t-1}) exactly Gaussian. The resulting posterior precision Λ_t = c_t I + A^⊤ Σ_{y|x}^{-1} A is solved matrix-free using a pre-whitened conjugate gradient (PW-CG) solver that constructs a synthetic right-hand side z with cov(z) = Λ_t from two standard Gaussians, avoiding any dense Cholesky factorization. This eliminates both heuristic projection tuning and likelihood gradient approximation, yielding a principled Bayesian update at every reverse diffusion step with runtime comparable to standard DPS.

## Results

On FFHQ 256×256, C-DPS achieves FID of 20.14 (Inpaint Random), 26.33 (Inpaint Box), 32.24 (Deblur Gaussian), 27.29 (Deblur Motion), and 28.41 (SR 4×), matching or exceeding the best baseline DMPlug (20.12, 27.12, 32.44, 27.55, 28.55) while running ~4× faster. On ImageNet 256×256, C-DPS achieves FID of 32.37, 33.24, 56.36, 52.06, and 47.30 across the same five tasks, consistently outperforming DMPlug (32.85, 34.28, 57.42, 53.13, 48.96) and all other baselines. The method also improves measurement fidelity, maintaining lower ‖y − Ax_t‖₂² throughout the sampling trajectory compared to DPS and ReSample.

## Baselines & Comparisons

C-DPS is most competitive with DMPlug, the strongest baseline: on FFHQ the two methods are within 0.02–0.8 FID on most tasks (DMPlug edges C-DPS by 0.02 FID on Inpaint Random, while C-DPS wins by 0.79 FID on Inpaint Box and 0.14–0.29 FID on the deblurring/SR tasks); on ImageNet, C-DPS consistently beats DMPlug by 0.5–1.7 FID across all five tasks. Against DPS, C-DPS achieves substantially lower FID (e.g., 26.33 vs. 33.12 on FFHQ Inpaint Box; 32.37 vs. 35.87 on ImageNet Inpaint Random), confirming that the closed-form coupled posterior outperforms DPS's Tweedie-based likelihood gradient approximation. ReSample, the next-strongest projection-based baseline, is also clearly outperformed (e.g., 27.29 vs. 31.19 FID on FFHQ Deblur Motion; 47.30 vs. 49.19 on ImageNet SR). Older projection methods (DDRM, MCG, ILVR) and variational approaches (Score-SDE, PnP-ADMM, ADMM-TV) are decisively outperformed, often by 10–50+ FID. The paper notes that DMPlug, while close in accuracy on FFHQ, runs approximately 4× slower than C-DPS (550 s vs. 152 s per image), making C-DPS the more practical choice.

## Relation to Prior Work

C-DPS extends the diffusion posterior sampling framework introduced by DPS (Chung et al., ICLR 2023), which approximates the likelihood gradient ∇_{x_t} log p(y|x_t) via Tweedie's formula to steer the reverse diffusion. Rather than retrofitting measurement information through heuristic projections (ILVR, DDRM, MCG) or likelihood approximations (DPS, IIGDM, Score-SDE), C-DPS introduces a parallel forward stochastic process in the measurement space {y_t} that evolves in lockstep with the data-space diffusion {x_t}. This coupling defines a joint generative model over (x, y) and yields a closed-form Gaussian posterior p(x_{t-1}|x_t, y_{t-1}), eliminating both the need for ad-hoc constraint tuning and the intractable likelihood gradient. The efficient sampling algorithm builds on standard DDPM (Ho et al., NeurIPS 2020) reverse dynamics augmented with a pre-whitened conjugate gradient solver for the posterior precision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By providing a principled, closed-form posterior that integrates the measurement model directly into the diffusion dynamics, C-DPS removes two major sources of instability in diffusion-based inverse problem solving: heuristic projection tuning and likelihood approximation error. The method is practically efficient (comparable runtime to DPS, ~4× faster than DMPlug) and extends naturally to latent diffusion and locally-linear nonlinear operators. Open questions include whether the shared noise schedule between data and measurement spaces is truly optimal, how the method behaves under severely ill-conditioned or highly nonlinear forward operators beyond local linearization, and whether the score-freezing approximation (Eq. 14) can be tightened without sacrificing the Gaussian closed form. The paper's validation is limited to 256×256 images and standard linear operators, leaving high-resolution and complex imaging pipelines unexplored.
