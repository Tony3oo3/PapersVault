## Problem

MPC-based private LLM inference suffers from severe latency and communication overhead for long sequences, with attention computation (especially Softmax) dominating the cost. Existing KV cache eviction methods designed for plaintext inference introduce MPC-unfriendly operations—top-k ranking, token gathering, and per-token similarity computation—that actually increase overhead rather than reduce it in the MPC setting. The paper addresses the gap of designing a training-free, MPC-friendly KV cache eviction algorithm that reduces private LLM inference cost without sacrificing model performance.

## Input / Output

The method takes a long text prompt (sequence length up to 2048+ tokens) and model weights distributed as secret shares across 2 or 3 MPC parties (using BumbleBee 2PC or PUMA 3PC protocols). During prefill, the full prompt is processed to build the KV cache; during decoding, only a small subset of cached tokens participates in attention at each step. The output is standard autoregressive text generation, but the attention computation operates on a compressed KV cache (e.g., 5–20% of original tokens), reducing per-step attention complexity from O(Td) to O(kd) where k ≪ T. All intermediate values (indices, similarity scores, selected KV entries) remain as ciphertexts throughout the protocol.

## Method

MPCACHE combines a look-once static eviction (during prefill, discarding ~60% of tokens identified as unimportant-to-all via accumulated attention scores) with a query-aware dynamic selection (during decoding, activating only ~20% of remaining tokens per step). The dynamic selection is made MPC-friendly through three key optimizations: (1) hierarchical KV cache clustering that groups adjacent tokens into clusters, reducing top-k ranking and token gathering complexity proportionally to cluster size; (2) an MPC-friendly similarity approximation that replaces the expensive max operation with a linearized weighted sum of per-dimension max/min key values (α·q_i·r_i^max + (1−α)·q_i·r_i^min), further reordered to halve multiplications; and (3) cross-layer index-sharing where adjacent layers reuse the same selected token indices, exploiting the observed high commonality of top-k rankings between neighboring layers. The framework is training-free and builds directly on established 2PC/3PC cryptographic primitives without introducing new protocols.

## Results

On LongBench with LongChat-7B-V1.5-32K, MPCACHE achieves comparable or superior F1 scores to full-cache inference while using only 5–10% KV cache budget (e.g., 17.23 vs 17.02 on NarrativeQA at 5%, 82.45 vs 82.67 on TriviaQA at 10%). In efficiency evaluation with 3PC protocols, MPCACHE achieves 1.8–2.01× decoding latency reduction and 3.39–8.37× communication reduction over full KV cache across sequence lengths of 512–2048. On LLaMA-3.1-8B-Instruct (Table 5), MPCACHE matches or exceeds full-cache performance on most LongBench tasks at a 1024-token budget (e.g., 91.84 vs 91.74 on TriviaQA, 55.38 vs 55.53 on HotpotQA), outperforming all baselines including Quest and SnapKV.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against dynamic eviction baselines, MPCACHE dominates on both performance and efficiency: on NarrativeQA at 5% budget, MPCACHE achieves 17.23 F1 (exceeding full-cache 17.02) with 36.13s latency, versus InfLLM's 12.80 F1 at 47.74s and LongCache's 14.65 F1 at 86.42s — a 1.32× and 2.39× latency reduction respectively. On TriviaQA at 10%, MPCACHE reaches 82.45 F1 (nearly matching full-cache 82.67) at 50.75s, while LongCache drops to 60.56 F1 at 123.1s. Against static and fixed-pattern baselines (H2O, StreamingLLM, SnapKV, TOVA), MPCACHE consistently outperforms across all LongBench sub-tasks; for example, on LLaMA-3.1-8B-Instruct at 1024 budget, MPCACHE scores 91.84 on TriviaQA vs. StreamingLLM's 88.84 and SnapKV's 90.02. In the efficiency benchmark (Figure 10), MPCACHE achieves 3.85× latency and 19.47× communication reduction over LongCache, and outperforms both head merging [62] and ArkVale [8] due to its clustering and cross-layer index-sharing optimizations. On the Needle-in-a-Haystack benchmark, MPCACHE matches full-cache retrieval accuracy across all needle positions, while StreamingLLM fails beyond ~2048 context length.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two well-developed lines of work: MPC-based private LLM inference (PUMA, BumbleBee, CipherGPT) and plaintext KV cache eviction (H2O, SnapKV, InfLLM, LongCache). It departs from prior MPC work, which focuses on protocol-level optimizations or model architecture changes requiring retraining, by introducing a training-free algorithmic layer that reduces the number of tokens participating in attention. It also departs from prior KV cache eviction methods, which assume plaintext computation and rely on MPC-unfriendly operations (top-k ranking, token gathering, cosine similarity), by redesigning the eviction pipeline around MPC cost constraints—specifically through hierarchical clustering, linearized similarity approximation, and cross-layer index sharing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that naively applying dynamic KV cache eviction to MPC inference is counterproductive, increasing both latency and communication over the full-cache baseline—a non-obvious finding that reframes how sparsity must be designed under cryptographic constraints. The resulting 1.8–2.01× latency and 3.39–8.37× communication reductions are meaningful for a domain where even modest overhead multipliers are prohibitive. Key limitations remain: absolute latency is still orders of magnitude above plaintext, static eviction can fail on tasks requiring recall of initially 'unimportant' tokens, and the method is validated only on 2PC/3PC protocols, leaving open whether the same design principles transfer to HE- or TEE-based private inference.
