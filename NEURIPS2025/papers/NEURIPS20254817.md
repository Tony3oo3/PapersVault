## Problem

Standard RL relies on handcrafted reward functions that are hard to specify and prone to reward hacking, while IRL and preference-based RL infer surrogate rewards that suffer from ambiguity and distribution shift. Imitation learning avoids reward modeling but behavior cloning aligns only individual state-action pairs, failing to preserve the coherence of long-horizon expert trajectories. This paper tackles the largely unexplored problem of directly maximizing the probability of reproducing complete expert trajectories without any reward signal, and establishes both its computational hardness and tractable special cases.

## Input / Output

The method takes a finite-horizon MDP (discrete state space, finite action space, transition kernel, horizon H, initial distribution μ) and a small expert-labeled trajectory set T_sel of M complete H-step trajectories (M ≪ |T|). In the unknown-model variant, the transition kernel is not given and must be learned from environment interaction. The output is a deterministic time-indexed policy π = {π_h}_{h=1}^H that maximizes the total probability mass assigned to the expert trajectories, with the intermediate solution being a maximum-weight independent set of mutually non-conflicting trajectories (where conflict means two trajectories require different actions at the same state).

## Method

The core insight is that direct trajectory alignment reduces to a Maximum-Weight Independent Set (MWIS) problem on a conflict graph: vertices are expert trajectories weighted by their realization probability, and edges connect pairs that demand contradictory actions at a shared state. The paper proves this problem is NP-complete in general (via a BFS-based reduction from MWIS), then provides two polynomial-time solutions under structural assumptions: (1) under ε-realizability (all trajectory weights ≥ ε₀), an enumeration oracle over subsets of size at most ⌊1/ε₀⌋ yields the exact optimum in O(M²H + M^{1/ε₀}); (2) under a Tree MDP (no state revisits, no successor merging), a backward pruning/dynamic-programming algorithm (TGL-PrunedTree) computes the optimal policy in O(H·M·|A|). For unknown dynamics, TGL-UCB frames the problem as a combinatorial multi-armed bandit over the conflict graph, using UCB confidence bounds on trajectory realization probabilities and an MWIS oracle each round, achieving sub-linear regret O(M³ log K · Δ_max / Δ_min²).

## Results

On a 4×4 FROZEN LAKE grid-world (H=10, 10% stochastic action noise), TGL-UCB achieves trajectory match probability ranging from 0.634 to 0.814 across 14 expert-labeled trajectory set compositions, evaluated over 10,000 episodes with 95% Wald confidence intervals. The best result (0.814 ± 0.008) occurs with 10 deterministic and 5 stochastic demonstrations, where BC reaches 0.793 and the PPO expert reaches 0.803. TGL-UCB consistently outperforms behavioral cloning by 1.5–3.5 percentage points across all configurations, with the largest gaps appearing under scarce or highly mixed demonstration sets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TGL-UCB is compared against two baselines: Behavioral Cloning (BC, from the open-source `imitation` library [Gleave et al., 2022]) and a PPO-trained expert policy used to generate the deterministic demonstrations. Against BC, TGL-UCB wins in all 14 trajectory-set compositions, with margins ranging from 0.1 pp (3 det. only: 0.672 vs. 0.672, essentially a tie) to 3.5 pp (5 det. & 5 stoch.: 0.740 vs. 0.713). Against the PPO expert, TGL-UCB wins in 12 of 14 configurations; the two near-ties (5 det. & 10 stoch.: 0.779 vs. 0.780; 10 stoch. only: 0.738 vs. 0.739) fall within the reported 95% confidence intervals. The advantage over BC is most pronounced when demonstrations are scarce (≤5 total) or highly stochastic, where trajectory-level conflict resolution provides the greatest benefit over state-by-state action matching.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the three dominant paradigms for learning from expert behavior—inverse RL, preference-based RL, and standard imitation learning (behavior cloning)—by introducing 'direct trajectory alignment,' which maximizes the probability of generating complete expert trajectories rather than matching state-action pairs or inferring surrogate rewards. Methodologically, it builds on the UCB exploration framework of Auer et al. (2002) and the combinatorial multi-armed bandit analysis of Chen et al. (2013) to handle the unknown-dynamics setting, while casting the core optimization as a Maximum-Weight Independent Set problem over a trajectory-induced conflict graph. The key conceptual departure from behavior cloning is the shift from per-state action matching to whole-trajectory likelihood maximization, which the authors argue is necessary to preserve long-horizon coherence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By proving NP-completeness of the general trajectory alignment problem and then providing polynomial-time algorithms under practical structural assumptions (bounded realizability, tree-structured MDPs), the paper establishes a clear theoretical boundary for when reward-free trajectory alignment is tractable. The sub-linear regret guarantee for the unknown-model setting (TGL-UCB) is a meaningful step toward practical deployment without requiring a known transition model. However, the empirical validation is confined to a single 4×4 FROZEN LAKE grid world with modest gains over behavior cloning (typically 1–3 percentage points), and the MWIS oracle remains a computational bottleneck that limits scalability. Open questions include extending the framework to continuous MDPs, partially observed settings, and larger-scale environments where the conflict graph becomes intractable.
