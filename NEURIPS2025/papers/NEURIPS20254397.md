## Problem

Existing safety mitigation methods for LLM finetuning practice 'static safety shaping': they treat each training example as an atomic unit and make a binary keep-or-drop decision based on a guardrail's full-response judgment. This is suboptimal because safety context can shift within a single response (e.g., a refusal followed by harmful content followed by another refusal), so updating the model equally on both safe and unsafe segments allows harmful content to influence training. The paper identifies this vulnerability as 'context entanglement' and shows that even strong guardrails can be fooled by misleading suffixes, causing safety to collapse.

## Input / Output

The method consumes user-uploaded prompt-response training pairs, a guardrail model (e.g., Granite Guardian-3.1-2B or Llama Guard-3-8B), and a reference safety-aligned model (e.g., Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct). The guardrail is applied not just to full responses but to partially completed responses at every M-token boundary, producing a cached sequence of STAR scores per training example. The output is a finetuned LLM that preserves the reference model's safety alignment while acquiring the intended task capability, with no requirement for a separate curated safe dataset.

## Method

The key insight is repurposing guardrail models to evaluate partially completed responses, yielding the STAR score—a continuous, token-level safety signal that tracks how risk evolves across a response. The ★DSS loss uses these STAR scores as per-chunk weights to dynamically interpolate between cross-entropy (imitation of the target response) and KL divergence (regularization toward the reference model): when STAR ≈ 1 the loss reduces to standard SFT, and when STAR ≈ 0 the KL term dominates, suppressing unsafe learning. Smaller chunk size M improves granularity at higher computational cost, and STAR scores are precomputed and cached to keep training overhead minimal. A theoretical bound shows the finetuned model's harmfulness is at most the reference model's harmfulness plus a KL-controlled term and a guardrail false-negative term that shrinks with finer chunking or a more accurate guardrail.

## Results

Under the worst-case scenario (malicious user, no trusted safe data), ★DSS achieves 72.12% HEx-PHI and 89.42% AdvBench safety scores, outperforming the strongest baseline (Rejection Sampling) by 20.41%, while maintaining capability at 47.34% MMLU and 59.31% ARC-C. In the ideal-case scenario (benign user with access to safe data), ★DSS reaches 86.06% HEx-PHI, 97.50% AdvBench, and 35.10% GSM8K accuracy, matching or exceeding all baselines on both safety and capability. The method generalizes across six LLMs (1B–8B parameters), four guardrail models, and multiple harmful datasets (PureBad, BeaverTails, HH-RLHF) without degrading task performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

★DSS decisively outperforms all eight baselines on safety in both evaluation scenarios. Against the strongest static baseline, Rejection Sampling (RS), ★DSS improves AdvBench safety from 79.23% to 89.42% (worst case) and from 94.23% to 97.50% (ideal case), while matching RS on capability metrics. Deep Token, the closest prior dynamic method, is surpassed by ★DSS on both safety (89.42% vs. 51.54% AdvBench) and capability (59.31% vs. 55.97% ARC-C), with the gap attributed to Deep Token's fixed KL schedule on only the first five tokens. Vaccine and Safe LoRA fail to restore safety in the worst case (≤11% and ≤4% respectively) and Vaccine catastrophically degrades capability (MMLU drops to 9.39%). In the ideal case, ★DSS also beats Safe Instruct (86.06% vs. 73.94% HEx-PHI), LISA (97.50% vs. 96.54% AdvBench, 35.10% vs. 16.68% GSM8K), and SEAL (86.06% vs. 13.03% HEx-PHI), demonstrating that token-level STAR-guided shaping is more effective than sample-level filtering or bilevel data selection.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies the fragmented landscape of LLM finetuning safety defenses under a 'safety shaping' framework, distinguishing static methods (Rejection Sampling, Vaccine, SafeLoRA, SEAL, LISA) that make binary keep-or-drop decisions per example from a proposed dynamic approach that re-weights loss at the token level. Its closest conceptual predecessor is Deep Token, which applies hand-crafted KL penalties to the first five tokens; ★DSS generalizes this by replacing the fixed schedule with a guardrail-derived STAR score computed on partial responses. The key repurposing is treating guardrail models not as post-hoc classifiers but as trajectory evaluators that track evolving safety risk token by token, drawing a loose analogy to state-value functions in RL.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for finetuning-as-a-service providers: ★DSS offers a drop-in loss modification that requires no external safe data, no architectural changes, and only a single hyperparameter (λ_KL), while the theoretical bound gives interpretable guarantees linking safety to guardrail error and chunk granularity. Limitations include the computational overhead of STAR precomputation (mitigated by caching and pipelining), dependence on guardrail quality (smaller models like Granite Guardian-3.1-2B work well, but adversarial adaptation of guardrails remains an open question), and evaluation primarily on 1B–8B models, leaving open whether the approach scales to 70B+ deployments. A broader open question is whether the static-vs-dynamic framing generalizes beyond text to multimodal finetuning or to settings where the guardrail itself is under adversarial pressure.
