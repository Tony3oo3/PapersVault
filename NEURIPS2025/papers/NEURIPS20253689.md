## Problem

Deep residual networks achieve strong performance but introduce computational redundancy: their short skip connections create exponentially many gradient paths that can act as shortcuts, leaving some layers underutilized or prone to overfitting. The paper asks whether an alternative connectivity pattern can preserve the stable gradient flow and multi-pathway benefits of residual architectures while naturally eliminating redundant depth and improving generalization.

## Input / Output

The method accepts standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10, 224×224 for ImageNet-1K) or tokenized text sequences and produces class labels via a shared classification head. Unusually, the network output is the element-wise sum of all intermediate layer representations plus the input embedding (y = Σ x_i), which structurally yields L+1 valid sub-networks: any prefix of layers can serve as a complete inference point without additional heads or auxiliary supervision.

## Method

ACNs replace the short residual connection (layer i → layer i+1) with a long feedforward connection (layer i → final output), so the network output is the sum of all layer outputs plus the input. This simple topological change produces a distinctive gradient structure: a single forward propagation path (identical to a feedforward network) combined with multiple backward paths (linear in depth, unlike the exponential count in ResNets), with a strong Direct Gradient component that connects the loss directly to each layer. The resulting implicit layer-wise training dynamic causes early layers to receive disproportionately strong gradients, pushing representational information into the bottom layers while deeper layers converge toward identity mappings (auto-compression). No auxiliary losses, intermediate supervision, or hyperparameter tuning are required; the compression emerges purely from the architecture's interaction with standard gradient descent, and the degree of compression adapts to task difficulty (easier tasks use fewer layers).

## Results

AC-ViT achieves 70.76% top-1 accuracy on ImageNet-1K with 51M parameters (19.7 GFLOPs), matching Res-ViT's 70.74% (86M params, 33.7 GFLOPs) while using 40% fewer parameters. AC-BERT matches or slightly exceeds Res-BERT on all three GLUE tasks (SST-2: 86.68% vs 86.63%; QQP: 87.30% vs 87.20%; QNLI: 83.07% vs 83.14%) with 58% fewer parameters (46M vs 110M). On CIFAR-10, AC-Mixer reaches 92.0% accuracy using only 75% of its layers versus 90.3% for Res-Mixer using all layers. In continual learning on Split CIFAR-100, AC-Mixer reduces average forgetting by up to 18% (32% vs 50% with Synaptic Intelligence at L=15).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Res-ViT [Dosovitskiy et al., ICLR 2021], AC-ViT matches top-1 accuracy on ImageNet-1K (70.76% vs 70.74%) while using 40% fewer parameters and 41% fewer GFLOPs, and shows substantially better noise robustness (e.g., 51.89% vs 45.46% at Gaussian σ=0.4). Against ResMixer [Tolstikhin et al., NeurIPS 2021], AC-Mixer achieves higher CIFAR-10 accuracy (92.0% vs 90.3%) using only 75% of its layers, and reduces catastrophic forgetting by up to 18% in continual learning (32% vs 50% average forgetting with SI at L=15). Against DenseNet-Mixer [Huang et al., CVPR 2017] and DenseFormer-Mixer [Pagliardini et al., NeurIPS 2024], AC-Mixer outperforms both on CIFAR-10 (92.0% vs 90.3% and 90.4% respectively) while also achieving earlier layer cutoff. In transfer learning (CIFAR-100→CIFAR-10), AC-Mixer (85.38%) clearly outperforms the regularization-based Aligned method [Jiang et al., ICLR 2025] (82.9%) and LayerSkip [Elhoushi et al., ACL 2024] (79.0%). Against Res-BERT [Devlin et al., NAACL 2019], AC-BERT matches or marginally exceeds accuracy on all three GLUE tasks while using 58% fewer parameters (46M vs 110M) and 75% fewer GFLOPs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the residual connection paradigm (He et al., 2016) by replacing short skip connections with long feedforward connections from every layer directly to the output, creating a distinct connectivity pattern from DenseNets (which concatenate all preceding layers) and DenseFormer (which uses learned weighted averaging). It is positioned against the growing body of evidence that residual architectures contain substantial layer redundancy (Veit et al., 2016; Gromov et al., 2025) and against regularization-based compression methods like Deeply Supervised Nets and LayerSkip that impose explicit intermediate losses. The key conceptual departure is that ACNs achieve compression purely through architectural design and the resulting gradient dynamics—specifically a strong direct-gradient component that drives implicit layer-wise training—rather than through auxiliary objectives or hyperparameter-tuned regularization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a simple connectivity change (short residual → long feedforward) organically produces 30–80% layer redundancy while simultaneously improving noise robustness, transfer learning, and continual learning (up to 18% less forgetting) suggests that architectural design can serve as implicit regularization in a way that is hardware-friendly and requires no specialized software. However, all experiments are conducted at relatively small scale (CIFAR-10/100, ImageNet-1K with ViT-S, BERT-base), and ACNs train noticeably longer than residual baselines (e.g., 700 vs. 300 epochs for ViT), which limits immediate practical adoption. Open questions include whether auto-compression persists at the scale of billion-parameter foundation models, whether it extends to self-supervised and generative settings, and whether per-sample adaptive depth can be exploited at inference without retraining.
