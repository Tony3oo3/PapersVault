## Problem

N:M sparsity patterns are not preserved under matrix transposition, so they accelerate only forward passes but not backward passes during training. While transposable N:M sparsity was proposed to resolve this, existing mask-finding methods either rely on minimum-cost flow (which does not scale to billion-parameter LLMs) or are restricted to M=4 via exhaustive search, leaving no efficient solver for arbitrary and especially large N:M values where the accuracy cost of transposability is smallest.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained weight matrix W and N:M sparsity parameters, partitioning W into M×M blocks. For each block it produces a binary mask S where every row and column contains exactly N ones (the transposable constraint), and the mask is applied element-wise to W to yield the pruned layer. Because the problem is separable across blocks, the solver processes millions of M×M submatrices simultaneously as a single batched tensor, with the final output being a full-size binary mask of the same shape as W.

## Method

TSENOR reformulates the per-block binary mask selection as a capacitated optimal transport problem, where S is a transport plan between rows and columns with N units of mass per marginal and unit capacity per entry. The relaxed problem is solved via entropy regularization (making it a Bregman projection under KL divergence) and Dykstra's algorithm, which alternates closed-form projections onto row-sum, column-sum, and box constraints using only matrix-vector products and element-wise operations. A novel rounding procedure then converts the fractional solution to a feasible binary mask: a greedy phase assigns entries in descending order subject to row/column capacity, followed by a local search that performs swap-based insert-remove triplets to fix unsaturated rows and columns. The entire pipeline is implemented as batched PyTorch tensor operations (with log-space arithmetic for numerical stability), enabling GPU-parallel processing of all blocks and achieving up to 100× speedup over existing methods with only 1–10% relative error versus the optimal mask. The solver plugs into Wanda, SparseGPT, and ALPS as a drop-in mask generator, with new ADMM convergence guarantees established for the ALPS integration.

## Results

TSENOR achieves 1–10% relative error versus the optimal transposable N:M mask (vs. up to 50% for Bi-NM and Max1000 baselines) while running up to 300× faster than the optimal Network Flow solver (1.06 s vs. 350 s on an 8192×8192 matrix on H100). On LLaMA3.2-3B pruned to transposable 16:32 sparsity, TSENOR+ALPS attains C4 perplexity 18.03, WikiText2 13.08, and zero-shot average 58.06, closely matching standard (non-transposable) ALPS (16.74 / 12.06 / 59.30) while enabling backward-pass acceleration. The performance gap between transposable and standard N:M sparsity shrinks by ~90% as M increases from 4 to 32, and transposable 16:32 models outperform standard 2:4 models on multiple zero-shot benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Network Flow method (Hubara et al., 2021) which guarantees optimal masks, TSENOR achieves 1–10% relative error at up to 300× lower runtime (1.06 s vs. 350 s on 8192×8192, H100). Compared to the 2-Approximation greedy heuristic from the same paper, TSENOR reduces relative error by roughly 10× while being 3× faster. The GPU LP solver cuPDLP (Lu & Yang, 2023) is 16× slower than TSENOR on H100 for the same matrix size. Bi-NM (Zhang et al., 2023) and Max1000 baselines exhibit up to 50% relative error, far exceeding TSENOR's 1–10%. In end-to-end LLM pruning, TSENOR+ALPS at 16:32 sparsity on LLaMA3.2-3B achieves zero-shot average 58.06, slightly below standard ALPS (59.30) but above standard SparseGPT (58.25) on several individual tasks (ARC-C: 38.05 vs. 34.81; BoolQ: 72.32 vs. 71.53), while additionally providing backward-pass acceleration that standard N:M methods cannot offer.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the transposable N:M sparsity framework introduced by Hubara et al. (2021a), who formulated mask generation as a minimum-cost flow problem, and on Hu et al. (2024), who solved the special case of transposable 2:4 masks via exhaustive search. The key departure is a novel reformulation of the mask selection problem as a capacitated optimal transport problem, enabling the use of entropy-regularized Dykstra's algorithm (rooted in Sinkhorn-type methods from Cuturi, 2013) for massively parallel GPU solving. The paper also extends the layer-wise pruning literature (Wanda, SparseGPT, ALPS) by showing how to plug in transposable mask solvers, with new ADMM convergence guarantees for the ALPS integration that account for approximate (non-exact) D-updates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Transposable N:M sparsity is the only fine-grained structured sparsity pattern that accelerates both forward and backward passes during training, yet prior solvers were either too slow (minimum-cost flow) or restricted to M=4 (exhaustive search), leaving the practical potential unrealized. The finding that transposable 16:32 sparsity outperforms standard 2:4 sparsity on LLaMA3.2-8B challenges the assumption that transposable constraints are always a net accuracy cost, and suggests that larger M values are the key to making transposable sparsity practically competitive. Open questions include whether an end-to-end training pipeline can fully realize the theoretical speedup, how this compares to parameter-efficient fine-tuning (LoRA, SPP), and whether channel permutations can further improve transposable mask quality.
