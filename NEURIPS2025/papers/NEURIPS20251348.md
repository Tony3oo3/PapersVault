## Problem

Existing context compression methods for LLMs fail to simultaneously preserve semantic information across multiple dimensions: explicit methods (e.g., LongLLMLingua) sacrifice fine-grained local detail for global coherence, while implicit methods (e.g., Activation Beacon, ICAE) suffer from positional bias, progressive information degradation in recursive compression, and an inability to capture long-range dependencies. No prior approach balances breadth (coverage across the full context) and depth (granularity of detail) while maintaining inference efficiency.

## Input / Output

The method takes a long text context (up to 32K tokens) paired with a task instruction as input and produces a generated response conditioned on a compressed semantic tree representation. Compression ratios are adaptive to context length (×2 for 4K–8K, ×4 for 8K–16K, ×8 for 16K–32K tokens with LLaMA-2), and the tree structure is built incrementally so that new context can be integrated without recompressing the entire history. The backbone LLM remains frozen throughout; only gist-token embeddings, gist-token attention projections, and a single-layer tree aggregator are trained.

## Method

AdmTree performs hierarchical compression in three stages. First, the input is uniformly partitioned into segments, then adaptively re-segmented using an entropy-adjusted perplexity score that allocates more gist tokens to information-dense regions (top 25% of segments receive 2× the base budget, bottom 50% receive half), yielding variable-length sub-segments each followed by a dedicated ⟨GT⟩ leaf token. Second, a binary semantic tree is constructed bottom-up from the leaf gist token hidden states using a lightweight single-layer self-attention aggregator followed by averaging, which enables bidirectional information flow between sibling nodes and mitigates the unidirectional degradation of causal compression. Third, each sub-segment is encoded by the frozen LLM with a dual-branch attention mechanism—separate trainable projection matrices for gist tokens versus text tokens—conditioned on the flattened preceding tree context, so that the current segment's representation integrates both local text and global hierarchical summary. Training uses standard next-token prediction with only the gist-token parameters and aggregator updated, keeping the backbone frozen.

## Results

On LongBench, AdmTree achieves an average score of 44.1 on LLaMA-2-7B and 49.7 on Qwen-2-7B, outperforming the strongest compression baseline Activation Beacon (40.1 and 47.2) by 10% and 5% respectively. The gains are most pronounced on Multi-Document QA (36.3 vs. 27.5 on LLaMA-2; 45.9 vs. 40.3 on Qwen-2), where AdmTree surpasses Beacon by over 20 points. AdmTree is the only compression-based method that consistently outperforms the full fine-tuning baseline (Original LLM-FT: 39.8 and 47.4) across both backbone models, while maintaining comparable inference latency (7.8s vs. 8.0s on LLaMA-2) and a 3.3× speedup over the original LLM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AdmTree outperforms all seven baselines across both backbone LLMs on LongBench. Against the strongest compression baseline Activation Beacon [58], AdmTree gains +4.0 points (44.1 vs. 40.1) on LLaMA-2 and +2.5 points (49.7 vs. 47.2) on Qwen-2, with the largest margin on Multi-Document QA (+8.8 and +5.6 respectively). AdmTree also surpasses the full fine-tuning baseline (Original LLM-FT) on both models, whereas Beacon does not consistently beat it. Among other implicit methods, SnapKV [38] is the closest competitor (34.6 / 45.0 AVG) but trails AdmTree by ~10 points on LLaMA-2; ICAE [21] and AutoCompressor [11] perform substantially worse (21.8 / 20.5) due to their fixed gist-token budgets. The explicit method LongLLMLingua [26] (31.5 / 34.4) is competitive on summarization but lags significantly on QA tasks. Retrieval-based methods (BM25 [48], SBERT [47], OpenAI) achieve the highest speedup (4.0×) but sacrifice 10–20 points in average accuracy, particularly on multi-hop QA.

## Relation to Prior Work

AdmTree extends the gist-token compression paradigm introduced by Mu et al. (NeurIPS 2023) and the recursive compression framework of ICAE (Ge et al., ICLR 2024), both of which use a frozen backbone LLM with a small set of trainable parameters for compression tokens. The key departure is replacing the flat or linearly-recursive compression structure with an adaptive binary semantic tree: segments are dynamically sized by an entropy-adjusted perplexity score, and a lightweight self-attention aggregator performs bottom-up hierarchical pooling. This contrasts with prior recursive methods (AutoCompressor, ICAE, Activation Beacon) that use fixed-size segments and unidirectional sequential compression, and with explicit methods (LongLLMLingua) that discard tokens outright.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and well-motivated failure mode of existing compressors—positional bias and progressive semantic degradation in linear/flat schemes—by introducing a hierarchical structure that preserves both global coherence and local detail simultaneously. The finding that a compression method can outperform full fine-tuning of the same backbone (Original LLM-FT) on LongBench is practically significant, suggesting that structured compression can act as an implicit retrieval mechanism. Open questions include whether the fixed binary tree depth and the top-25%/middle-25%/bottom-50% budget allocation generalize to much longer contexts (beyond 32K) or to non-English settings, and whether the tree structure can be made fully adaptive in depth. The authors also note the potential of integrating MoE architectures to specialize compression across task types.
