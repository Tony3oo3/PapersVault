## Problem

Contrastive learning for text-video retrieval suffers from optimization tension: the modality gap causes positive and negative gradients on each text anchor to be nearly collinear but reversed, largely canceling and trapping representations in in-place optimization. Additionally, false negatives inject noisy repulsion gradients that further distort anchor semantics. Existing methods address neither the gradient-cancellation structure nor the pair-level noise propagation.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 32- or 64-word text captions and 12- or 64-frame video clips (depending on dataset), encoded by a frozen CLIP ViT-B/32 dual-encoder with a 4-layer Temporal Transformer for video. At inference, a lightweight cross-attention module computes a pair-specific increment Δ_ij for every text-video candidate pair, adding it to the text (or video) embedding before cosine scoring, so retrieval requires evaluating Δ for each query-gallery pair rather than a single precomputed embedding.

## Method

GARE introduces a pair-specific learnable increment Δ_ij that locally adjusts the relative positioning of each text-video pair, derived via a multivariate first-order Taylor expansion of the InfoNCE loss under an ℓ₂ trust-region constraint around a nonzero coupled state. A cross-attention module ψ, conditioned on the semantic gap (v_j − t_i) as query and frame/word features as key-value, amortizes the iterative descent into a single forward pass, so each Δ_ij absorbs only the gradient from its own pair rather than the full batch. A Jensen-relaxed variational information bottleneck (aggregating posteriors along the video dimension) regularizes Δ toward compact, semantically grounded corrections, while norm-diversity and directional-diversity losses structure the trust-region radii and update directions. The net effect is gradient redistribution: repulsion from false negatives is redirected into their respective increments, and the anchor retains only global positive-pair supervision, releasing the in-place optimization trap.

## Results

On MSR-VTT 1k-A, GARE achieves R@1 of 49.1 (T2V) and 48.6 (V2T), outperforming the strongest baselines DiffusionRet (49.0/47.7) and HBI (48.6/46.8). On DiDeMo, GARE reaches R@1=47.6 (vs. 46.9 for HBI); on ActivityNet Captions, R@1=42.6 (vs. 42.2 for HBI); and on MSVD, R@1=46.4 with MnR=10.6 (vs. 46.6/15.7 for DiffusionRet). GARE also improves mean rank substantially, e.g., MnR drops from 12.6 (baseline) to 12.0 (T2V) and from 9.6 to 8.5 (V2T) on MSR-VTT.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GARE outperforms all 13 baselines on MSR-VTT in both retrieval directions: T2V R@1 of 49.1 edges out DiffusionRet (49.0) and HBI (48.6), while V2T R@1 of 48.6 surpasses DiffusionRet (47.7) by a clear 0.9-point margin. On DiDeMo, GARE leads with R@1=47.6 over HBI (46.9) and DiffusionRet (46.7); on ActivityNet Captions, it achieves R@1=42.6 vs. HBI's 42.2. On MSVD, GARE (R@1=46.4) is marginally behind DiffusionRet (46.6) on R@1 but substantially improves MnR (10.6 vs. 15.7) and R@5 (76.1 vs. 75.9). In the hard-negative comparison (Appendix C.5), GARE (R@1=49.1) is competitive with NeighborRetr (49.5, CVPR 2025) while using only a single cross-attention layer versus NeighborRetr's 8 MLP modules and large memory banks, and trains in ~1.5 h versus ~4.5 h. Overall, GARE consistently wins or ties on R@1 across all four benchmarks and delivers the best V2T results on every dataset.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the contrastive-learning paradigm for text-video retrieval (CLIP, CLIP4Clip, TS2-Net, HBI, etc.) by introducing a pair-specific learnable increment Δ_ij that offloads optimization tension from the shared anchor, rather than modifying the loss or reweighting negatives as prior false-negative methods (Debiased CL, DMAE) do. The theoretical grounding draws on the Information Bottleneck principle (Tishby et al., Alemi et al.) applied not to the main representation but to the auxiliary increment variable, and on trust-region optimization via multivariate first-order Taylor expansion of InfoNCE. The modality-gap analysis (Liang et al. 2022) motivates the problem, but GARE departs from prior gap-mitigation strategies by redistributing gradients across pair-level variables instead of projecting or aligning the two modality subspaces.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a concrete optimization pathology—near-complete gradient cancellation at the anchor due to the modality gap—that has been observed empirically but not previously addressed through gradient redistribution. The lightweight single cross-attention module and the relaxed VIB regularization offer a practical, low-overhead remedy (1h34min training vs. ~4.5h for NeighborRetr) that could be plugged into existing CLIP-based retrieval pipelines. However, the authors acknowledge that the root modality gap persists and that Δ_ij lacks a generalized supervision signal beyond InfoNCE gradients, leaving open whether the approach transfers to non-CLIP backbones or benefits from stronger pair-level supervision. The modest but consistent gains (~2–3% R@1) across four benchmarks suggest the technique is useful but not transformative.
