## Problem

Multimodal large language models (MLLMs) suffer significant performance degradation under distribution shifts such as visual/textual perturbations and long-tail queries, because standard instruction tuning causes them to overfit to data-specific patterns rather than learning invariant representations. Existing remedies—scaling instruction data or enlarging model architectures—are resource-intensive, and the information bottleneck principle, which explicitly trades off task-relevant information against input-specific redundancy, has not been applied to end-to-end autoregressive multimodal instruction tuning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254301/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single RGB image (processed by CLIP ViT-L/14-336px into visual token embeddings via a two-layer MLP projector) paired with a free-form text instruction, and produces a text response for open-ended QA, a yes/no label for object hallucination detection (POPE), or a multiple-choice selection for closed-form QA. The bottleneck operates at the token-embedding level inside the LLM backbone (default: layer 24 of 32 in a 7B model), so the input format is identical to standard LLaVA instruction tuning; no additional input channels or resolution constraints are introduced.

## Method

Vittle inserts a lightweight per-token MLP bottleneck layer inside the LLM backbone that maps each intermediate d-dimensional token embedding to the mean and variance of a diagonal Gaussian posterior, enabling stochastic sampling via the reparameterization trick. The training objective is a novel variational lower bound of the information bottleneck tailored to autoregressive multimodal models: it maximizes the expected log-likelihood of the response (standard cross-entropy) while penalizing KL divergences between the visual and textual posterior distributions and their respective priors (either a fixed N(0,I) or a learnable N(μ,σ²·I)). The sampled bottlenecked representation is interpolated with the pre-bottleneck representation using a cosine-scheduled coefficient α (up to 0.5), and at inference the deterministic mean is used. Theoretically, the paper shows that minimizing this objective reduces the effective mutual information difference (EMID), an information-theoretic robustness metric, by simultaneously lowering output entropy and the Jensen-Shannon divergence between clean and perturbed representation distributions.

## Results

On LB-COCO under joint visual-textual perturbations, Vittle (F) achieves 64.4 relative preference score versus 62.3 for the standard LLaVA-v1.5 baseline, while LoRA drops to 39.7. On long-tail QA (LB-Wilder), Vittle (L) reaches 168.8 versus 156.9 for the baseline. On closed-form benchmarks (ScienceQA, MMMU, MME, MMStar), Vittle maintains competitive average scores (50.9–51.1) with the baseline (50.9), confirming no in-distribution performance loss. Across 30 distribution shift scenarios spanning 45 datasets, Vittle consistently improves robustness with only ~20% additional training time and negligible inference overhead.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the standard LLaVA-v1.5 baseline [70], Vittle (F) improves joint-perturbation performance by +2.1 points (64.4 vs. 62.3) and textual-perturbation by +1.9 points (74.1 vs. 72.2), while sacrificing only 1.7 points on clean data (76.1 vs. 77.8). Against LoRA [83], Vittle (F) is dramatically superior under joint perturbations (64.4 vs. 39.7, a +24.7 gap) and also wins on clean data (76.1 vs. 73.4), showing that weight-space compression fails to preserve robustness. Against weight decay [83], Vittle (F) wins on all perturbation settings (74.2/74.1/64.4 vs. 72.1/73.0/59.5) while maintaining comparable clean performance. Against information-maximization objectives ROSS [84] and LIT [85] (Figure 6), Vittle achieves better open-ended QA performance under perturbations, whereas those methods are stronger on hallucination detection, indicating Vittle offers a more balanced robustness profile across task types.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Information Bottleneck (IB) framework from its traditional classification and small-scale settings to end-to-end instruction tuning of autoregressive multimodal LLMs. While prior IB work (Alemi et al., 2017; Tishby et al., 2000) established variational lower bounds for supervised learning, and a recent study (Bai et al., 2025) applied IB to a frozen-projector setting for MLLMs, this is the first to derive a variational bound tailored to the sequential, multimodal structure of MLLMs and to modify the LLM backbone itself. The paper also departs from the dominant data-scaling and model-scaling paradigms for MLLM robustness, instead taking a representation-centric view grounded in the EMID information-theoretic robustness metric.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Vittle offers a lightweight (1.5% parameter overhead), model-agnostic training objective that consistently improves MLLM robustness across 30 distribution shifts without sacrificing in-distribution performance, providing a practical alternative to expensive data or model scaling. The theoretical connection between IB optimization and reduced EMID gives a principled explanation for why compressive representations improve robustness. However, the method's reliance on gold response quality, its slight degradation of OCR capability, and its untested applicability to counterfactual or cross-domain generalization remain open questions that limit its universality.
