## Problem

Grid cells in the medial entorhinal cortex and place cells in the hippocampus form complementary spatial codes, but it remains unclear how their interaction supports internally driven navigation—recalling goals from partial sensory cues, planning novel routes through unvisited locations, and reconstructing expected sensory experiences along planned paths. Existing frameworks (successor representations and pure grid-based planning) each capture only part of this capability, leaving unresolved how context-dependent memory integrates with context-independent spatial metrics.

## Input / Output

The model receives partially occluded sensory observations (SMC responses with masking ratios up to 0.9), speed and direction signals, and grid cell patterns at the start location; for recall, the network state is initialized to zero and only SMC inputs are provided. Planning outputs a sequence of speed and direction actions that drive the network through imagined trajectories, while intermediate GC states passively trigger HPC-mediated reconstruction of SMC embeddings. In the Habitat-Sim experiments, panoramic images are encoded to 1024-dimensional vectors via a modified Masked Autoencoder (BtnkMAE with ViT-Large), and decoded back to pixel-space images that should match expected views at planned locations.

## Method

The core architecture is a single-layer RNN partitioned into subpopulations: partially supervised grid cells (GCs) for path integration, supervised spatially modulated cells (SMCs) for sensory autoencoding, and unsupervised hidden units that spontaneously develop place-cell-like activity by autoassociating SMC and GC patterns. A planning subnetwork is added that receives only GC representations (current state plus target pattern via a learnable projection) and outputs speed/direction actions, ensuring planning occurs on the context-independent grid manifold while sensory details are reconstructed passively through HPC autoassociation. Theoretically, the paper shows that decoding displacement within each grid module and averaging across scales reliably reduces distance to the target when the number of scales m exceeds k + (1−s^{−k})/(s−1), where k indexes undercovered scales. For realistic navigation, simulated SMC rate maps are replaced with 1024-dim visual features from panoramic Habitat-Sim images, and a DETR-style cross-attention decoder reconstructs images from intermediate SMC states during planning, demonstrating that the framework generalizes from abstract sensory patterns to photorealistic visual input.

## Results

The path-integration RNN, trained on 5-second trajectories, accurately path-integrates over 10-second trials (L=100.50±8.49 cm) and 120-second trials (L=1207.89±30.98 cm), with stable grid fields across all durations. In the recall experiment, all nine networks (masking ratios 0.1–0.9) successfully recalled goal locations from partial sensory cues within 5 seconds, with higher masking ratios yielding more place cells (up to 512) and improved spatial decoding accuracy at the cost of reduced sensory reconstruction fidelity. The planning subnetwork, trained on only 1-second local transitions (128 start-end pairs over 100 steps), generalizes to plan paths over 10-second and 20-second horizons, taking shortcuts through unvisited locations. In the Habitat-Sim experiment, intermediate SMC states decoded during planning via BtnkMAE produce panoramic images that closely match the expected views at corresponding planned locations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not benchmark against competing navigation or planning methods in a head-to-head comparison; instead, it validates a unified theoretical framework through a series of progressive experiments (path integration → recall → planning → sensory reconstruction). The authors position their approach relative to two existing frameworks discussed in the introduction: the successor representation (SR) model [39,40], which discretizes space and requires experience at many locations, and pure grid-cell vector navigation [44], which provides context-independent metrics but cannot easily capture context-specific constraints. REMI's key advantage over SR is that grid-based planning permits shortcuts through unvisited locations and generalizes local transitions to long-range paths without requiring pairwise experience. Compared to the direct displacement decoding in [44], REMI proposes a sequential planning mechanism (transition matrix over phase bins) that decomposes long-range navigation into executable local steps, and adds the HC-MEC auto-associative loop to reconstruct sensory experience along planned routes—something neither SR nor pure grid-cell models provide. The Habitat-Sim experiment further demonstrates that the framework generalizes beyond simulated rate maps to photorealistic visual inputs, a capability not tested in prior grid-cell planning models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate lines of computational neuroscience work: RNN-based grid cell path integration models (Cueva & Wei 2018, Banino et al. 2018, Sorscher et al. 2019, Schaeffer et al. 2023) and place cell autoassociation of sensory inputs (Wang et al. 2024). It departs from the successor representation framework (Stachenfeld et al. 2017), which discretizes space into learned transition states, by proposing that planning occurs on the continuous grid cell manifold with sensory details recovered through hippocampal pattern completion. The grid-based displacement decoding formalism from Bush et al. 2015 is reframed to support multi-scale sequential planning via local transition matrices, and a modified MAE (BtnkMAE) with a single CLS-token bottleneck and DETR-style cross-attention decoder is introduced to bridge the gap between population-level SMC representations and pixel-space image reconstruction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a concrete, testable wiring theory for how the HC-MEC loop supports internally driven navigation, making three specific experimental predictions (cue-triggered grid reactivation, look-forward MEC sweeps during planning, and asymmetric disruption effects for MEC→HC vs. HC→MEC lesions) that could be directly tested in rodent experiments. Key limitations include the supervised (non-emergent) grid cells, absence of boundary cell representations, and restriction to mid-range planning within a single environment scale. Open questions include whether grid and place cells can co-emerge without supervision, how the framework extends to 3D or multi-scale global planning, and whether the autoassociative smoothing effect generalizes to more complex sensory modalities.
