## Problem

Most unified multimodal large language models (MLLMs) rely on autoregressive architectures that impose raster-scan order for image generation, restrict reasoning to causal context, and prevent self-correction of earlier tokens. This paper challenges the AR paradigm by asking whether a purely discrete flow matching framework can match or exceed AR-based MLLMs on both visual understanding and text-to-image generation while enabling iterative refinement and bidirectional context integration.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252720/cropped_page1_idx8.webp)

## Input / Output

For text-to-image generation, the model takes a text prompt and produces a 384×384 image encoded as 576 discrete tokens (vocabulary size 16,384) via a LlamaGen-style pixel encoder/decoder. For visual understanding, it takes an image (processed by SigLIP) plus a text question and outputs a text response of up to 500 tokens (vocabulary size 102,400). Notably, the model uses full (non-causal) attention and applies a one-position shift to output logits to inherit next-token prediction capabilities from its AR initialization, while omitting explicit time-embedding layers since the model implicitly infers the corruption level from the input.

## Method

FUDOKI replaces the standard mask-based corruption process with metric-induced probability paths where the conditional distribution at time t is softmax(−β_t · d(x^i, x_1^i)), with d measuring L2 distances between normalized token embeddings and β_t = 3(t/(1−t))^0.9. The probability velocities are derived by minimizing kinetic energy subject to the continuity equation, yielding a closed-form expression where probability mass flows only from states farther from the target to states closer to it. The architecture is initialized from Janus-1.5B and trained in two stages: first fine-tuning only the transformer to adapt to the flow matching paradigm, then full fine-tuning on ~13M samples. At inference, an Euler solver simulates the continuous-time Markov chain over 32 steps, allowing any token to be revised at each step—unlike mask-based diffusion where unmasked tokens are frozen—enabling self-correction and bidirectional reasoning.

## Results

On GenEval, FUDOKI achieves an overall score of 0.77, surpassing all prior unified multimodal models (Janus-Pro-1B: 0.73, D-DiT: 0.65, ILLUME: 0.61) and approaching the best generation-only model SD3-Medium (0.74). On DPG-Bench, FUDOKI scores 83.63 overall, outperforming the best unified baseline Janus-Pro-1B (82.63) and all other unified models. On multimodal understanding benchmarks, FUDOKI (1.5B) achieves MME-P 1485.4, MMBench 73.9, SEED-Bench 68.2, and MM-Vet 38.0, matching or exceeding AR-based MLLMs of similar or larger scale (e.g., ILLUME 7B, TokenFlow-XL 13B). With test-time inference scaling, FUDOKI further improves to 0.88 on GenEval and 55.5 on MM-Vet.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On GenEval, FUDOKI (0.77) outperforms all unified multimodal baselines: Janus-Pro-1B (0.73, +0.04), D-DiT (0.65, +0.12), Transfusion (0.63, +0.14), ILLUME (0.61, +0.16), and Janus (0.61, +0.16), while also exceeding the best generation-only AR model Emu3-Gen (0.54) and approaching SD3-Medium (0.74). On DPG-Bench, FUDOKI (83.63) beats Janus-Pro-1B (82.63) and Janus (79.68) among unified models, and is competitive with top generation-only models like SD3-Medium (84.08) and DALL-E 3 (83.50). On understanding benchmarks, FUDOKI (1.5B) is broadly competitive with Janus-Pro-1B (1.5B): it leads on MME-P (1485.4 vs. 1444.0) and SEED-Bench (68.2 vs. 68.3, near-tie) but trails slightly on MMBench (73.9 vs. 75.5), MMMU (34.3 vs. 36.3), and MM-Vet (38.0 vs. 39.8). FUDOKI also surpasses larger AR unified models like ILLUME (7B) on MME-P (1485.4 vs. 1445.3) and MMBench (73.9 vs. 65.1), and TokenFlow-XL (13B) on MMBench (73.9 vs. 68.9). With test-time inference scaling, FUDOKI's MM-Vet score jumps from 38.0 to 55.5, surpassing all baselines including TokenFlow-XL (40.7), and its GenEval score reaches 0.88, exceeding even SD3-Medium (0.74). The self-correction experiment (Table 4) further shows FUDOKI improves baseline MM-Vet scores by +0.55, while AR-based Janus-Pro-1B actually degrades by -1.65 under the same correction protocol.

## Relation to Prior Work

FUDOKI builds directly on the Janus-1.5B architecture as its initialization, adapting an AR-based MLLM to a discrete flow matching paradigm via a logit-shifting operation from Gong et al. Its core methodological departure is from masking-based discrete diffusion (used in D-DiT, UniDisc, LLaDA, Dream) by adopting metric-induced probability paths with kinetic-optimal velocities from Gat et al. and Shaul et al., which replaces the binary mask/unmask corruption with a continuous, semantically-aware interpolation over token embedding space. This positions FUDOKI as the first unified multimodal model to use the full discrete flow matching framework rather than the simpler masked construction, while retaining the decoupled visual encoding (SigLIP for understanding, LlamaGen VQ tokens for generation) established by Janus.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FUDOKI demonstrates that a non-autoregressive, iterative-refinement paradigm can match AR-based MLLMs on both understanding and generation at 1.5B scale, challenging the assumption that causal left-to-right processing is necessary for strong multimodal reasoning. The self-correction capability—where tokens can be revised across inference steps in a way that mask-based diffusion cannot—opens a path toward RL-based test-time scaling that AR models struggle with. Key limitations include the 384×384 generation resolution, single-model-size evaluation, and the fact that 'comparable' performance has not yet been shown to scale to 7B+ parameters or video. Open questions include whether the kinetic-optimal velocity formulation provides consistent advantages at larger scale, how the approach extends to video or audio, and whether RL fine-tuning can close the remaining gap to the best AR systems.
