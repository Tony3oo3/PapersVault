## Problem

Quantification learning on graph-structured data has previously only been addressed via node clustering methods, and the standard Adjusted Classify & Count (ACC) framework relies on a prior probability shift assumption that is often unrealistic for graphs, where structural covariate shift (e.g., localized sampling of neighborhoods) is the more natural distribution shift. Additionally, non-homophilic edges in graphs can make class confusion profiles collinear, rendering the ACC system of equations underdetermined.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a graph with vertex features, a pre-trained node classifier (MLP, GCN, GAT, or APPNP), a set of labeled training vertices D_L, and a set of unlabeled test vertices V_U. It produces a single label prevalence distribution over the test set (one K-dimensional probability vector per quantification task), not per-instance predictions. The confusion matrix is estimated from D_L and reweighted by structural importance ratios computed via kernel density estimation over the graph topology.

## Method

The paper proposes two complementary extensions of ACC to graphs. Structural Importance Sampling (SIS) generalizes ACC from prior probability shift to (structural) covariate shift by reweighting training vertices in the confusion matrix estimate using a density ratio ρ_V(v) = q_V(v)/p_V(v) computed via kernel density estimation with a Personalized PageRank (PPR) kernel that captures structural proximity between vertices. Neighborhood-aware ACC (NACC) addresses class identifiability by augmenting the confusion matrix from K×K to K²×K, where each entry tracks the joint distribution of a vertex's predicted label and its neighbors' majority predicted label, thereby breaking symmetries between confused classes that arise from non-homophilic edges. The two methods are orthogonal and can be combined (SIS + NACC), and the final prevalence estimate is obtained by solving a constrained least-squares optimization over the probability simplex.

## Results

Under PPS with an APPNP classifier, SIS PACC achieves an average rank of 1.6 across five datasets (CoraML AE: .0203, CiteSeer AE: .0171, Amazon Photos AE: .0130, Amazon Computers AE: .0249, PubMed AE: .0154), outperforming standard PACC (avg rank 2.6) and PCC (avg rank 5.0). Under structural covariate shift (BFS), SIS PACC reduces CoraML AE from .0457 (PACC) to .0380 and RAE from 1.881 to 1.729, achieving the best average rank of 1.6. On the real-world Twitch Gamers dataset with natural covariate shift, SIS ACC with APPNP achieves AE .008 on German and .080 on Russian partitions, compared to .011 and .089 for standard ACC, respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SIS PACC consistently outperforms standard PACC under all three shift types: under PPS it reduces CoraML AE from .0217 to .0203 (avg rank 1.6 vs 2.6), and under BFS/RW covariate shift it reduces AE from .0457 to .0380 (avg rank 1.6 vs 2.8). NACC provides additional gains when classes are confused due to non-homophilic edges (e.g., Amazon Photos where label 7 is confused with labels 1 and 6), but can slightly underperform SIS PACC alone when the confusion matrix is already well-conditioned. The naive PCC baseline is consistently worst (avg rank 5.0 under PPS, 4.2 under covariate shift), confirming that classifier error correction is essential. The prior graph quantification method ENQ (Ego-network Quantification, Milli et al. 2015) performs worst among all methods, with AE .1839 under BFS on CoraML versus .0378 for SIS NACC. On the real-world Twitch Gamers dataset, SIS ACC with APPNP outperforms standard ACC on four of five language partitions (e.g., German AE .008 vs .011, Russian AE .080 vs .089), though it underperforms on the dominant English partition (AE .011 vs .021) where distribution shift is minimal.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Adjusted Classify & Count (ACC) framework from Forman [8] and Bunse [3] to graph-structured data, where prior graph quantification work (Milli et al. [22]) relied solely on node clustering. The key conceptual departure is demonstrating that the prior probability shift (PPS) assumption underpinning ACC is often inapplicable to graph quantification, where structural sampling biases induce covariate shift instead. SIS generalizes ACC to this covariate shift setting via kernel density estimation of vertex distributions, while NACC addresses a separate identifiability problem arising from non-homophilic edges by augmenting the confusion matrix with neighborhood majority labels.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that PPS is frequently violated in graph quantification challenges a default assumption in the quantification literature and motivates a more careful analysis of which shift assumption holds in a given domain. The absence of true graph quantification benchmarks (the authors resort to synthetic shifts on node classification datasets) limits the practical impact and leaves open whether the gains observed under synthetic BFS/RW shifts translate to real-world structural biases. Future directions include extending distribution-matching quantifiers (e.g., DMy, KDEy) to the graph setting and constructing genuine graph quantification benchmarks from social media or other relational data.
