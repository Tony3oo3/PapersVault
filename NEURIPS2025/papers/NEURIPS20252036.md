## Problem

No single, simple pretraining objective has been shown to produce state-of-the-art features for all downstream vision tasks; contrastive, captioning, and spatially self-supervised losses each excel in different domains, and combining them scales poorly in complexity. This paper asks whether contrastive vision-language training alone can suffice, and if so, why its strong general features are not accessible at the network output.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252036/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The encoder consumes RGB images at up to 448×448 (progressive 98→448 during training) or video clips represented as 8 uniformly sampled frames, alongside text prompts or captions. For video, each frame is encoded independently and the per-frame CLIP embeddings are average-pooled into a single video embedding aligned with the caption. The core model outputs a 1280-dim global CLIP embedding (G scale) plus per-token features; after alignment tuning, the last-layer token features are directly usable by an MLLM decoder (PE_lang) or a spatial decoder for detection, depth, and tracking (PE_spatial), eliminating the need to select intermediate layers.

## Method

PE_core is a ViT-based contrastive vision-language encoder (up to 2B params) trained with a robust recipe: progressive resolution, 131K global batch, LAMB optimizer at high LR, 2D RoPE, attention-pooling CLIP token (retaining the class token), heavy data augmentation, and mask regularization. A video data engine—using an early PE checkpoint as a frame encoder with a Llama decoder to generate captions, refined by 120K human-annotated videos—produces 22M aligned video-text pairs for a short finetuning stage that boosts both image and video performance. The key empirical finding is that intermediate layers of the contrastive encoder contain features matching DINOv2 on spatial tasks and AIMv2 on language tasks, but the CLIP token construction at the output obfuscates them. Two lightweight alignment procedures then lift these latent features to the last layer: PE_lang finetunes with next-token prediction against a Llama 3.2 3B decoder on 70M MLLM samples, while PE_spatial distills from its own frozen layer-41 features combined with SAM 2.1 mask-logit supervision to restore spatial locality, both using heavy regularization (DropPath, LayerScale, 75% masking) to avoid overwriting the pretraining knowledge.

## Results

PE_core G (1.9B params, 448px) achieves 86.6% average zero-shot image classification (85.4% ImageNet val), 78.5% average fine-grained classification, and 75.8% average image-text retrieval, outperforming SigLIP2-g-opt (86.2/76.3/75.9) across all categories. On video, PE_core G reaches 74.8% average zero-shot classification and 58.7% average retrieval using only 22M videos, surpassing InternVideo2 (70.7/59.9) which uses 102M videos. After language alignment, PE_lang G scores 72.4% average OCR/Doc QA, 78.1% average VQA, and 71.3% average grounding, exceeding AIMv2-3B (48.9/73.0/36.1) by large margins. After spatial alignment, PE_spatial G achieves 61.5 DAVIS tracking, 49.3 ADE20K mIoU, 0.262 NYU depth error, and 66.0 COCO AP_box with a simple DETA decoder, matching or exceeding DINOv2-g and EVA02-L.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PE_core G outperforms SigLIP2-g-opt on average zero-shot classification (86.6 vs 86.2) and fine-grained classification (78.5 vs 76.3), while retrieval is nearly tied (75.8 vs 75.9); it also beats the much larger EVA 18B (17.5B params) by 3.0 points on average classification. On video, PE_core G surpasses InternVideo2 (74.8 vs 70.7 avg classification) using only 22M videos versus 102M, and significantly outperforms SigLIP2-g-opt (74.8 vs 68.2). For MLLM tasks, PE_lang G dominates AIMv2-3B by 23.5 points on OCR QA (72.4 vs 48.9) and 35.2 points on grounding (71.3 vs 36.1), and beats DINOv2-g by 42.4 points on OCR QA (72.4 vs 30.0) and 17.1 on VQA (78.1 vs 61.0). On spatial tasks, PE_spatial G exceeds DINOv2-g on DAVIS tracking (61.5 vs 58.5), ADE20K segmentation (49.3 vs 48.7), and NYU depth (0.262 vs 0.279, lower is better), and achieves 66.0 COCO AP_box with DETA, matching EVA02-L (65.9) and beating InternImage-G (65.3) and SwinV2-G (62.5) with a simpler decoder.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the CLIP/SigLIP contrastive vision-language pretraining lineage by demonstrating that a single contrastive objective, when trained with a robust recipe (progressive resolution, 2D RoPE, attention pooling, mask regularization, LAMB optimizer), produces intermediate-layer features that match or exceed specialized pretraining methods—DINOv2 for spatial tasks and AIMv2 for language tasks—without any additional objectives. It departs from the growing trend of combining multiple pretraining losses (CoCa, RADIO, TIPS) by showing that the apparent need for captioning or spatial self-supervision is actually an alignment problem: the features already exist but are not at the network output. The alignment tuning strategy (MLLM finetuning for language, SAM 2 mask-logit distillation plus self-distillation for spatial) draws on PerceptionLM's MLLM recipe and SAM 2's segmentation capabilities, but repurposes them as lightweight feature-lifting steps rather than primary training objectives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a pure global contrastive loss encodes spatial, linguistic, and grounding features in intermediate layers challenges the field's working assumption that different downstream tasks require different pretraining objectives, potentially simplifying the pretraining landscape to a single scalable recipe plus task-specific alignment. The practical impact is substantial: PE achieves SOTA across zero-shot classification, retrieval, MLLM tasks, detection, depth, and tracking with one encoder family, and the released PE Video Dataset (1M videos, 120K human-refined captions) addresses a critical data gap for video understanding. Open questions include whether the alignment tuning generalizes to new task categories without additional teachers, whether the last-layer stagnation observed even with the robust recipe can be fully resolved, and whether the scaling behavior of intermediate-layer features continues beyond 2B parameters.
