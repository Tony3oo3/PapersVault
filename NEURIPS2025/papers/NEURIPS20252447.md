## Problem

Existing vision encoders exhibit an inherent bias toward either spatial modeling (e.g., CLIP) or temporal modeling (e.g., V-JEPA), preventing them from jointly capturing fine-grained spatial details and structured temporal dynamics. Prior cluster-discrimination methods handle only single modality or treat video events in isolation, lacking the ability to model structured multi-event temporal relations and spatial object co-occurrences within a shared representation space.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252447/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts RGB images (H×W×3) and variable-length video clips (T×H×W×3) at 224 or 336 resolution. Videos are segmented into variable-length frame sequences of 1, 2, 4, 8, or 16 consecutive frames via the VS² strategy, and all inputs are patchified and encoded by a shared ViT backbone. The output is a set of D-dimensional spatiotemporal token embeddings plus a class-level representation produced by attentive pooling, which serves as the query for contrastive discrimination against 1M offline semantic centroids.

## Method

UniViT employs a two-stage cluster-discrimination paradigm: first, offline K-means clustering generates 1M shared semantic centroids at two granularities—object-level (pooled patch features from images/single frames) and multi-granularity event-level (concatenated frame features from variable-length video sub-clips of 1–16 frames). Second, a joint multi-label contrastive objective (Eq. 4) uses these centroids as pseudo-labels, assigning 8 positive labels per sample and enforcing structural constraints (temporal event adjacency, spatial object co-occurrence) beyond isolated category separation. Two key architectural components enable unified spatiotemporal modeling: (i) U-RoPE decomposes rotary position embeddings into spatial (high-frequency) and temporal (low-frequency) components via a frequency-aware allocation that avoids the periodic oscillations of M-RoPE, supporting position-invariant learning; and (ii) VS² dynamically assigns positional encodings to variable-length frame sequences, eliminating the rigidity of fixed-input approaches. The entire framework is trained without pixel-level or feature-level reconstruction supervision, relying solely on cluster-discriminative contrastive loss.

## Results

On the linear probing protocol (Table 2), UniViT (ViT-L/14 @336) achieves 94.9% average accuracy across 8 image benchmarks and 72.0% across 6 video benchmarks, without any language supervision. On the attentive probing protocol (Table 1), the same model reaches 92.8% image average and 76.3% video average. Notably, UniViT outperforms the strongest unified baseline PE-Core (ViT-G/14 @448, 1882M params) on video tasks (72.0% vs 68.6%) while using only 334M parameters, and matches or exceeds image-only models like SigLIPv2 (94.7%) and MLCD (94.9%) on image tasks. On the most temporally demanding benchmark SSV2, UniViT achieves 27.4% (linear probe) vs 23.2% for PE-Core and 23.2% for SigLIPv2.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniViT (ViT-L/14 @336, 334M) achieves the best video average (72.0%) among all compared methods, outperforming the strongest unified baseline PE-Core (ViT-G/14 @448, 1882M) by 3.4 points on video average while using 5.6× fewer parameters. Against image-only models, UniViT matches SigLIPv2 (94.9% vs 94.7% image avg) and MLCD (94.9% vs 94.9%) on image tasks while substantially exceeding them on video (72.0% vs 66.5% and 62.2% respectively). Compared to video-only models, UniViT surpasses V-JEPA (72.0% vs 57.0%), LanguageBind (72.0% vs 63.6%), and VideoMAEv2 (72.0% vs 35.3%) on video average, while also reporting competitive image scores that these video-only models cannot produce. The largest single-benchmark gap is on K400, where UniViT reaches 85.3% vs 80.8% (PE-Core) and 78.6% (SigLIPv2). On SSV2, the most temporally complex benchmark, UniViT leads all methods at 27.4%, ahead of PE-Core (23.2%) and SigLIPv2 (23.2%). The only margin where UniViT trails is image average vs PE-Core (94.9% vs 95.2%), a 0.3-point gap attributable to PE-Core's larger model (1882M vs 334M) and higher resolution (448 vs 336).

## Relation to Prior Work

UniViT extends the multi-label cluster discrimination paradigm of MLCD from image-only to a unified image-and-video setting, introducing event-level clustering with multi-granularity temporal segments alongside object-level spatial clustering. For positional encoding, it departs from M-RoPE (Qwen2-VL) by reassigning rotary frequencies—placing temporal structure in low-frequency components and spatial detail in high-frequency ones—to avoid the periodic oscillations that destabilize dense label discrimination. The variable spatiotemporal stream design generalizes fixed-frame video encoders (V-JEPA, VideoMAEv2) to handle arbitrary frame lengths, while the overall framework unifies the previously separate image-centric (CLIP, DINOv2) and video-centric (V-JEPA, I-JEPA) pretraining paradigms into a single encoder trained without language supervision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

A single encoder that handles both static images and variable-length videos without language supervision is practically valuable for MLLM pipelines (demonstrated via LLaVA-NeXT-Video with Qwen2.5-7B), reducing the need for separate image and video backbones. The U-RoPE frequency-allocation strategy and VS² variable-frame mechanism are modular techniques that could be adopted in other spatiotemporal transformer architectures. Key limitations include reliance on offline clustering (introducing bias from the initial feature extractor and preventing adaptive cluster updates during training) and computational cost for long or high-resolution sequences. Open questions include whether online or iterative clustering could close the gap, and whether the approach generalizes to longer-form video understanding beyond 16-frame clips.
