## Problem

Finding paths on Cayley graphs of large finite groups (e.g., 4×4×4 and 5×5×5 Rubik's cubes with state spaces of 10^45 and 10^74 nodes) remains intractable for standard pathfinding algorithms, and prior ML solvers (DeepCubeA, EfficientCube) were limited to the 3×3×3 case with modest optimality (60–70%). No effective tool existed to find even non-optimal paths on such large permutation-group graphs, leaving a gap between the 3×3×3 benchmark and all larger puzzles.

## Input / Output

The method takes a scrambled puzzle state encoded as a permutation vector (e.g., the sequence (p(0), p(1), …, p(l−1)) describing the current arrangement of l symbols) and produces a sequence of moves (edges in the Cayley graph) leading to the solved state. Training data is generated on-the-fly via non-backtracking random walks from the solved state, yielding (node_vector, step_count) pairs; no human-annotated solutions or domain-specific features are required. The approach is group-agnostic: the same pipeline applies to any permutation group, from 2×2×2 cubes to Globe 6×10 puzzles of size 10^145.

## Method

The core architecture is a 10-layer ResMLP (multilayer perceptron with residual blocks and batch normalization, ~4M parameters) trained in a supervised fashion to predict the diffusion distance — the number of non-backtracking random-walk steps from the solved state — for any given node vector. At inference time, a beam search of configurable width W uses the network's predictions as a heuristic to greedily expand the W most promising neighbors at each step until the solved state is reached. The key novelty is multi-agency: many independently trained agents (each seeing a different random-walk training set) are run in parallel on the same scramble, and the shortest path among all agents is selected, exploiting the diversity of learned distance estimates. A surprising empirical finding is that training-set size saturates around 8B examples (further training yields negligible gains and even degrades half-precision inference), while beam width and agent count robustly reduce solution length, making the approach highly scalable via distributed computing without requiring larger or longer-trained models.

## Results

On the DeepCubeA 3×3×3 dataset (1000 scrambles, QTM), the multi-agent ensemble (26 agents, 10-layer ResMLP, W=2²⁴) achieves 20.67 average solution length with 98.4% optimality, surpassing DeepCubeA (21.50, 60.3%) and EfficientCube (21.26, 69.6%). On the 2023 Kaggle Santa Challenge, the method beats every individual best submission: 46.51 avg for 4×4×4 (vs. 53.49 Santa best) and 92.16 avg for 5×5×5 (vs. 96.58 Santa best). In terms of speed, the solver is ~25.6× faster than EfficientCube (9.16 s vs. 237.9 s per scramble) while requiring up to 8.5× less training time (3 h 2 min vs. 25 h 50 min).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DeepCubeA [3] on the 3×3×3 DeepCubeA dataset, the proposed multi-agent solver reduces average solution length from 21.50 to 20.67 and raises optimality from 60.3% to 98.4%, while using a model with 3.7× fewer parameters (4M vs. 14.7M) and 1.25× less training data (8B vs. 10B). Against EfficientCube [46], the method achieves a comparable average solution length (20.67 vs. 21.26) but with 98.4% vs. 69.6% optimality, and is ~25.6× faster at inference (9.16 s vs. 237.9 s per scramble) with 8.5× less training time. Against the aggregated best results from the 2023 Kaggle Santa Challenge (1000+ teams), the method beats every single submission on 3×3×3 (19.5 vs. 21.8), 4×4×4 (46.51 vs. 53.49), and 5×5×5 (92.16 vs. 96.58) in UQTM. Against the genetic algorithm baseline [49], the improvement is dramatic: e.g., 20.67 vs. 238 moves on 3×3×3 and 92.16 vs. 1761 moves on 5×5×5. The paper also notes that even a single-layer MLP (0.34M params) with W=2²⁴ reaches 90.4% optimality on 3×3×3, already surpassing both DeepCubeA and EfficientCube, highlighting that the multi-agent ensemble and beam width—not model capacity—drive the gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the neural-network-guided search paradigm established by DeepCubeA (Agostinelli et al., 2019) and EfficientCube (Takano, 2023), but departs from both in key ways: it replaces reinforcement learning and self-supervised objectives with simple supervised regression on diffusion distance estimates from random walks, and it introduces a multi-agent ensemble of small ResMLP models rather than a single large network. The beam search component is shared with EfficientCube, but the training signal (random-walk step count as a proxy for graph distance) and the multi-agent selection strategy are novel. The approach is positioned as a general method for pathfinding on Cayley graphs of arbitrary finite permutation groups, going well beyond the Rubik's cube-specific formulations of prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first ML approach to robustly solve 4×4×4 and 5×5×5 Rubik's cubes, beating all 1,000+ Kaggle Santa 2023 submissions, and it generalizes to arbitrary permutation groups up to 10^145 elements with minimal domain-specific tuning. The demonstrated stagnation of performance with training set size (beyond ~8B examples) and the superiority of multiple small agents over a single large model challenge the standard scaling intuition and suggest a more compute-efficient path for similar combinatorial search problems. Open questions include the lack of theoretical guarantees on solution quality, the inability to solve 6×6×6 cubes with current parameters, and whether the approach's success on Cayley graphs reflects a general principle or is specific to the high symmetry of these structures.
