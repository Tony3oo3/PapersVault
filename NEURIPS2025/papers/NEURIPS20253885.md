## Problem

Recent advances in Mixed Integer Programming have enabled solving non-private Best Subset Selection at scale (millions of variables in minutes), but extending these algorithmic advances to the differential privacy setting has remained largely unexplored. Existing DP variable selection methods either require enumerating all C(p,s) feasible supports (as in the exponential mechanism) or rely on approximate DP via MCMC, limiting their scalability and the strength of their privacy guarantees. This paper bridges the gap by introducing pure DP estimators that leverage MIP to avoid exhaustive combinatorial search.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a bounded data matrix X ∈ R^{n×p} and response vector y ∈ R^n (clipped to satisfy sup|y| ≤ b_y and sup‖x‖_∞ ≤ b_x), along with a privacy budget ε, sparsity budget s, and ridge radius r. It outputs a single sparse support set S ⊆ [p] with |S| = s, drawn from a distribution that approximates the exponential mechanism over the combinatorial space of all size-s subsets. The framework is loss-agnostic: the objective can be least squares, hinge loss, or any convex function of β, with the MIP formulation requiring only convexity of the loss.

## Method

The paper introduces two pure DP estimators built on a truncated exponential mechanism. The top-R method enumerates the R best supports via a sequence of MIPs (each excluding previously found supports), then samples from a distribution where the top R supports receive their true exponential-mechanism weights while all remaining supports share a single bucket weighted by the R-th best objective; a rejection-sampling subroutine (M_0) draws uniformly from this bucket without enumerating it. The mistakes method instead partitions all supports by Hamming distance from the optimal support and assigns each partition a single weight based on the best objective within it, requiring only s+1 MIP solves. To obtain the top-R supports at scale (p up to 10,000), the paper formulates each MIP with a ridge-regularized objective and solves it via a cutting-plane outer approximation algorithm that iteratively adds linear underestimators of the strongly convex objective, converging to the optimal binary solution.

## Results

On synthetic data with p=10,000, s=5, SNR=5, ρ=0.1, and ε=1, both the top-R and mistakes methods achieve near-perfect support recovery (proportion of correct draws approaching 1.0) as n increases, outperforming the MCMC baseline [29] and the Samp-Agg baseline [23] across all tested sample sizes. The mistakes method consistently outperforms top-R, aligning with its milder identifiability margin requirement (τ ≳ max{s log p/n, s log p/(nε)} vs. τ ≳ max{log p/n, s² log p/(nε)}). The methods scale to p=10,000 variables with runtime comparable to MCMC (100,000 iterations), and extend to sparse classification with hinge loss where they again outperform Lasso-based Samp-Agg.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two baselines: (1) the approximate-DP MCMC algorithm of Roy and Tewari [29], which is the state-of-the-art for private BSS, and (2) the resample-and-aggregate (Samp-Agg) algorithm of Kifer et al. [23] using Lasso as the support subroutine. Both proposed methods (top-R and mistakes) outperform MCMC [29] in support recovery proportion for sufficiently large n in the least squares setting (Figure 1a), with the gap widening as n grows. Against Samp-Agg [23], both methods also achieve superior support recovery for both least squares and hinge loss objectives (Figures 1a and 1b). The mistakes method is the strongest performer overall, recovering the correct support at a higher rate than top-R, MCMC, and Samp-Agg across the full range of n tested. A key practical advantage is that the proposed methods achieve pure (ε,0)-DP guarantees, whereas MCMC [29] provides only approximate DP and requires Markov chain mixing assumptions that hold only with high probability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the exponential mechanism (McSherry & Talwar) for pure DP and on modern MIP-based Best Subset Selection solvers (Bertsimas et al., Hazimeh & Mazumder), combining them in a way neither line of work had previously attempted. It departs from the exponential-mechanism-based DP variable selection of Lei et al. (which requires enumerating all C(p,s) supports) and from the approximate-DP MCMC approach of Roy & Tewari, by showing that only the top-R supports are needed and that a cutting-plane outer-approximation algorithm can enumerate them efficiently. The truncation idea is analogous in spirit to the Restricted and Truncated Exponential Mechanisms in the DP literature, but applied here to combinatorial support selection under pure DP rather than mean estimation or metric-DP on text.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that pure (not approximate) DP variable selection can scale to p=10,000 features while outperforming the state-of-the-art approximate-DP MCMC method, opening the door to private sparse modeling in sensitive domains like medicine and finance. Two open questions remain: whether the mistakes method's additional objective-gap assumption can be weakened to yield unconditional pure-DP guarantees, and whether the identifiability-margin conditions are tight (i.e., whether necessary conditions can be established). The gap between the top-R and mistakes theoretical conditions (an extra factor of s) also suggests room for tighter analysis.
