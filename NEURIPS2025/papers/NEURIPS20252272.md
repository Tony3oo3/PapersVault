## Problem

Despite growing interest in applying LLMs to biology, no comprehensive benchmark exists to evaluate whether LLMs can genuinely reason over single-cell multi-omics data. Existing single-cell analysis paradigms suffer from lack of unification across omics types and tasks, poor user-friendliness requiring domain expertise, and limited interpretability of black-box predictions. CELLVERSE addresses this by providing the first large-scale, language-centric QA benchmark that systematically probes LLM understanding across hierarchical cell biology tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252272/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Input is raw single-cell data (scRNA-seq, CITE-seq, ASAP-seq, scATAC-seq) encoded into natural language via cell2sentence (C2S), which represents each cell as a sentence of the top-n most highly expressed gene names in descending order, or via gene regulatory network (GRN) statements describing pairwise gene interactions. All questions are converted to closed-set multiple-choice format (e.g., candidate cell types, sensitive/resistant, yes/no, up/down) because open-ended prompting causes all evaluated models to fail. Context length is controllable by varying n in C2S, and the benchmark spans three difficulty levels: cell-level (18 or 7 or 9 classes), drug-level (binary), and gene-level (binary significance and direction).

## Method

CELLVERSE constructs a unified QA benchmark by (1) curating five sub-datasets across four omics modalities, (2) translating them into natural language using C2S for cell-level encoding and GRN for gene-level interaction encoding, and (3) reformulating three representative tasks—cell type annotation, drug response prediction, and perturbation analysis (significance + direction)—as multiple-choice QA problems with post-processing for redundancy filtering and class balancing. The evaluation protocol tests 14 LLMs (ranging from 160M to 671B parameters, including specialist C2S-Pythia models and generalist Qwen, Llama, DeepSeek, and GPT families) under both zero-shot and few-shot settings using vLLM for open-source models and official APIs for closed-source models. Key findings include: specialist models fail across all tasks due to limited capacity, generalist models show preliminary but insufficient understanding (best accuracy 42–61% on cell type annotation, ~55% on drug response—near random), performance scales with model size, and few-shot in-context learning does not consistently help due to inherent noise in single-cell data.

## Results

On the CELLVERSE benchmark, the best-performing model (DeepSeek-R1) achieves 42.38% accuracy on scRNA-seq cell type annotation, while GPT-4.1 leads on CITE-seq (61.43%) and ASAP-seq (29.33%). For drug response prediction, the top model (GPT-4.1-mini) reaches only 55% accuracy—barely above the 49.38% random-guess baseline. On perturbation significance analysis, Qwen-2.5-7B/32B and DeepSeek-V3/R1 tie at 76.67% accuracy, while perturbation direction analysis peaks at 62.96% (DeepSeek-R1), with most models failing to beat random guessing (44.44%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Specialist models (C2S-Pythia-160M/410M/1B), despite being trained specifically on single-cell language tasks, consistently fail across all sub-tasks, producing hallucinated predictions and performing near or below random baselines. Among generalist models, the DeepSeek family (V3, R1) and GPT-4 family (GPT-4, 4o, 4.1) dominate the leaderboards, securing 4 and 2 first-place rankings respectively across the six task-specific leaderboards. Qwen-2.5 models (7B–72B) show a clear scaling trend but remain 10–20 points below the top performers on most tasks. Llama-3.3-70B performs mid-pack, outperforming Qwen-2.5-7B but trailing DeepSeek and GPT-4.1 on accuracy. A critical finding is that on drug response prediction, no evaluated LLM—including GPT-4.1-mini (55%)—achieves statistically significant improvement over the 49.38% random baseline, and GPT-4 outright refuses to answer most DRP and perturbation questions (0% accuracy). Few-shot in-context learning does not consistently help and often degrades performance, suggesting sample quality matters more than quantity in this noisy domain.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the cell2sentence (C2S) encoding framework [34], which maps single-cell gene expression profiles to ranked gene-name sentences, and extends it with gene regulatory network (GRN) language for gene-level perturbation queries. It departs from the dominant single-cell analysis paradigm—purpose-built deep learning classifiers and foundation models (e.g., scGPT)—by reframing all tasks as multiple-choice QA problems solvable by general-purpose LLMs, thereby unifying multi-omics and multi-task evaluation under a single language interface. In the broader LLM-for-science landscape, it fills a gap left by existing science QA benchmarks (SciBench, PubMedQA, MathVista), none of which handle the high-dimensional, sparse, data-intensive nature of single-cell measurements.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CELLVERSE provides the first systematic, large-scale evidence that current LLMs—despite impressive general reasoning—remain far from reliable on single-cell biology: the best drug-response accuracy (55%) is statistically indistinguishable from random guessing, and cell-type annotation peaks at only ~61% on the most favorable modality. The finding that domain-specialist C2S-Pythia models underperform generalist LLMs across all tasks challenges the assumption that task-specific pretraining on small single-cell corpora is sufficient, and the observation that few-shot prompting can degrade performance highlights the noise sensitivity of single-cell data. Open questions include whether scaling model size, improving the language encoding (beyond top-n gene lists), or introducing structured multi-modal inputs could close the gap, and whether the benchmark's English-only, single-dataset-per-task design limits generalizability.
