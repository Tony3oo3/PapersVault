## Problem

Mean aggregation is the default choice in practice for GNNs used in knowledge graph completion, yet no prior work has analyzed the logical expressivity of mean-GNNs or extracted sound logical rules from them. While sound Datalog rules and FOL characterizations exist for GNNs with max or sum aggregation, the mean-aggregation case remains unexplored, leaving the most widely deployed GNN variant without provable explanations for its predictions.

## Input / Output

The method takes a finite knowledge graph dataset (a set of ground unary and binary facts over a fixed signature) and a trained MAGNN with L layers, and produces three types of output: predicted facts via the standard GNN forward pass, a finite set of sound monotonic rules of the restricted form ∃P₁.⊤ ∩ … ∩ ∃Pⱼ.⊤ ∩ A₁ ∩ … ∩ Aₖ ⊆ Aₖ₊₁, and per-prediction explanatory rules in a new Ω fragment of FOL that uses a novel ∃ₙ (exists-n-unique) operator to capture exact neighbourhood structure. The soundness check for any candidate rule reduces to a single forward pass of the MAGNN on a small base dataset, making verification computationally tractable.

## Method

The paper restricts mean-GNNs to non-negative weights (MAGNNs) to isolate non-monotonicity to the aggregation function, then proves two main theoretical results. First, Theorem 3 shows that any sound ELUQ rule for a MAGNN is subsumed by a finite set of very simple rules containing only existential predicates and atomic concepts, with a practical soundness check (Proposition 4) that requires evaluating the MAGNN on a single base dataset. Second, Theorem 6 defines a restricted FOL fragment Ω (using a new ∃ₙ operator for exact-unique-existence) and proves that for any MAGNN prediction on any dataset, a sound Ω-rule can be constructed that entails that prediction, by inductively encoding the L-hop neighbourhood structure. Empirically, the paper validates that clamping weights to be non-negative preserves or improves performance on seven inductive benchmarks (WN18RRv1, FB237v1, NELLv1, LUBM, and three LogInfer variants), and demonstrates that the extracted sound rules can expose nonsensical patterns learned by the model (e.g., predicting everything is a Publication).

## Results

On the inductive benchmark WN18RRv1, the non-negative-weight MAGNN achieves 95.5% accuracy (F1 95.3), outperforming the standard mean-GNN (93.7% accuracy, F1 93.4). On NELLv1, the restriction to non-negative weights yields a substantial improvement from 75.2% to 93.4% accuracy (F1 from 65.7 to 93.8). On FB237v1, the non-negative variant improves accuracy from 68.7% to 71.8% and F1 from 55.7 to 69.7. The only notable performance drop occurs on LUBM (97.1% → 91.5% accuracy) and the deliberately non-monotonic WN-hier_nmhier dataset (86.2% → 71.6%), where the monotonicity constraint is expected to hurt.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against external methods; instead, it compares the standard (unrestricted-weight) mean-GNN against the non-negative-weight MAGNN on the same architecture and datasets. The non-negative restriction improves or matches performance on 5 of 7 datasets: WN18RRv1 (+1.8% acc), NELLv1 (+18.2% acc), FB237v1 (+3.1% acc), WN-hier (+0.3% acc), and WN-sym (+0.6% acc). The two exceptions are LUBM (−5.6% acc) and WN-hier_nmhier (−14.6% acc), where the latter is explicitly constructed with non-monotonic rules that a monotonic model cannot capture. The authors note that the performance gains on benchmark datasets are consistent with prior findings for sum-GNNs (Morris et al. 2024) and max-GNNs (Tena Cucala et al. 2021), suggesting the non-negative weight constraint acts as a regularizer that does not sacrifice—and often improves—generalization on inductive tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the sound-rule extraction framework for GNNs from max and sum aggregation (Tena Cucala et al. [35, 37]; Morris et al. [25]) to mean aggregation, which is the default in widely-used architectures like R-GCN and GCN but had no prior logical expressivity or soundness analysis. It builds on the non-negative-weight restriction that prior work showed preserves performance for max/sum GNNs, and on the ALCQ/FOL expressivity results of Barceló et al. [7], but isolates the non-monotonicity source to the mean aggregation function itself. The key departure is proving that the class of sound monotonic rules (ELUQ) for MAGNNs is far more restricted than for max/sum GNNs, and providing a dedicated FOL fragment (Ω) for explaining individual predictions when no equivalent FOL program exists.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Mean aggregation is the de facto standard in practice, so the absence of soundness guarantees for the most commonly used GNN variant was a significant gap; this paper closes it by showing both what can and cannot be soundly extracted. The finding that sound monotonic rules are extremely limited (reducible to bare existentials plus unary predicates) and that a dataset explicitly populated with monotonic rules (WN-sym) yields almost no recoverable sound rules raises important questions about whether mean-GNNs are the right architecture when provable explainability is required, potentially favouring max- or sum-GNNs. Open questions include whether ELUQ is a maximal monotonic fragment of ALCQ, and how to encourage MAGNNs to recover the underlying dataset rules rather than learning spurious ones like 'everything is a publication.'
