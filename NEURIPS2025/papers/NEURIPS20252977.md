## Problem

Existing behavioral datasets suffer from a fragmentation problem: they capture isolated aspects of human movement (full-body pose, hand articulation, eye gaze, or multi-view video) but fail to integrate all of these with goal-directed action annotations and visual attention data. No prior dataset simultaneously provides synchronized egocentric and exocentric video, 3D body and hand poses, eye gaze, IMU signals, and dense hierarchical action labels in a naturalistic setting, leaving a critical gap for studying ecologically valid human behavior.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252977/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The dataset provides 29.7 hours of synchronized data from 10 camera views (9 Azure Kinect RGB-D exocentric + 1 HoloLens 2 egocentric), 8 IMU sensors, and derived 3D SMPL body meshes and MANO hand meshes in a globally calibrated coordinate system. For the benchmarks, inputs vary by task: action recognition and segmentation consume subsets of video frames, 3D pose sequences, and eye gaze rays; motion generation takes verb-noun text prompts plus CLIP-encoded egocentric visual features; and Lemonade takes 8-frame egocentric video clips paired with closed-ended text questions. Outputs range from 763-class fine-grained action labels and per-frame segment boundaries to 327-dimensional full-body motion sequences (joint locations, angles, and gaze) and free-text QA answers.

## Method

The core contribution is a multi-view, multi-modal kitchen dataset with a hierarchical annotation scheme (33 verbs × 79 nouns = 763 fine-grained actions with temporal overlaps, plus 6 coarse activities yielding 33.78 segments/minute), paired with four benchmarks. The 3D pose pipeline extracts 2D poses via RTMPose/DeepLabCut, 3D body tracklets via the Kinect SDK, and hand poses via HoloLens 2, then lifts to 3D and fits SMPL/MANO meshes by minimizing joint, reprojection, temporal smoothing, and regularization losses (6.22 cm body / 3.30 cm hand error). For benchmarks, the authors adapt VideoMAE into a multi-modal masked autoencoder for action recognition, use the DLC2Action toolbox (MS-TCN3, C2F-TCN, EDTCN) on kinematic and video features for segmentation, train T2M-GPT, MARDM-SiT, and MoMask on the 327-dim full-body representation for situated motion generation, and evaluate four VLMs (InternVL2.5, LLaVA-OneVision, Qwen2.5-VL, Gemini 2.0 Flash) via lmms-eval on 36,521 auto-generated Lemonade QA pairs spanning perception, reasoning, summarization, session properties, physical attributes, and kinematics.

## Results

On the novel Lemonade VLM benchmark (36,521 QA pairs), the best model (LLaVA-OneVision) achieves 40.85% overall accuracy versus 54.75% for humans, with kinematics questions being the hardest (21.7–34.1% for models vs. 38.34% for humans; chance = 25%). On the fine-grained action recognition benchmark (763 classes), the best multi-modal configuration (egocentric + exocentric + hand pose) reaches 40.03% Top-1 action accuracy and 60.80% Top-1 verb accuracy, a 21.6% improvement over egocentric-only when trained from scratch. For action segmentation, the best F1 scores are 35.0% (verbs, C2F-Transformer + video) and 35.2% (nouns, C2F-TCN + video). In full-body motion generation, MoMask achieves the best R-Precision T1 of 0.372 and FID of 0.930 under text+action conditioning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the Lemonade benchmark, all four evaluated VLMs (Gemini-2.0-Flash, LLaVA-OneVision, InternVL2.5-8B, Qwen2.5-VL) fall 10–17 points below human performance (54.75%), with LLaVA-OneVision leading at 40.85% overall; the largest gap appears on kinematics questions (21.7–34.1% vs. 38.34% human), indicating that current VLMs cannot reliably estimate joint angles or velocities from video alone. For action recognition, simple concatenation of all modalities yields only marginal gains over single-modality inputs, but a carefully designed multi-modal fusion (egocentric + multi-exocentric + hand pose) boosts Top-1 action accuracy by 21.6% over egocentric-only when trained from scratch, and transfer learning from EPIC-KITCHENS-100 helps visual-input models while hurting pose-only models. In action segmentation, video features (VideoMAE) consistently outperform pure pose inputs, with C2F-TCN achieving the best noun F1 (35.2%) and C2F-Transformer the best verb F1 (35.0%), both well below the activity-level F1 (~72%), highlighting the difficulty of fine-grained temporal boundaries. For motion generation, MoMask consistently outperforms T2M-GPT and MARDM-SiT across all metrics (e.g., T1: 0.372 vs. 0.320 vs. 0.271; FID: 0.930 vs. 1.230 vs. 2.378 under text+action conditioning), and adding egocentric visual conditioning further reduces FID to 0.627.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself against the 'fragmentation problem' in behavioral datasets: existing cooking and action datasets (EPIC-KITCHENS-100, EgoExo4D, HoloAssist, Assembly101) each capture subsets of modalities but lack integrated multi-view video, full-body and hand 3D poses, eye gaze, and dense hierarchical annotations simultaneously. Methodologically, it adapts VideoMAE into a multi-modal masked autoencoder for action recognition and leverages the DLC2Action toolbox (MS-TCN, EDTCN, C2F-TCN) for pose-based action segmentation, while extending text-to-motion generation (T2M-GPT, MARDM-SiT, MoMask) to include hands and eye gaze. The Lemonade benchmark departs from prior VLM evaluation suites (MVBench, Video-MME, NExT-QA) by specifically probing fine-grained kinematic reasoning—joint angles, trajectory velocities, distances—that existing video-QA benchmarks do not address.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dataset's 33.78 action segments per minute and 763 fine-grained action classes provide a level of behavioral granularity unmatched by prior cooking datasets, enabling studies of motor synergies, attention-action coupling, and procedural behavior that were previously infeasible. The benchmark results reveal that current VLMs achieve only ~37–41% accuracy on kinematic and reasoning questions (vs. 55% for humans) and that action segmentation F1 scores hover around 35%, indicating substantial headroom for multi-modal fusion methods. The authors note future plans to collect data from older and neurologically impaired participants, which could extend the dataset's utility to clinical motor-function assessment. A key open question is whether the modest gains from multi-modal integration (21.6% with clever fusion vs. near-zero from naive concatenation) will scale to more complex or longer-horizon behavioral tasks.
