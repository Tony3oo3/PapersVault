## Problem

Existing membership inference attacks (MIAs) designed for text and image data in multimodal LLMs fail to generalize to video understanding LLMs (VULLMs), achieving negligible true positive rates at low false positive rates and deteriorating as more frames are sampled. This is because prior methods adopt a static view of MIA that ignores the inherent temporal variations of video frames and the model's shifting memorization behavior across different frame conditions.

## Input / Output

The method takes uniformly sampled video frames (varying counts from 2 to 32) paired with a text instruction context, and queries the target VULLM twice—once with frames in natural order and once in reversed order. From each forward pass, it extracts only the next-token probability distributions at video-frame token positions (located via model special tokens), computes per-position Sharma-Mittal entropy, and outputs a single scalar membership score derived from the element-wise entropy difference between the two passes, thresholded to yield a binary member/non-member decision.

## Method

Vid-SME adaptively parameterizes the two free parameters (q, r) of Sharma-Mittal entropy per video: q is inversely scaled by normalized optical-flow variance (motion complexity) so that fast-moving videos receive smaller q, increasing sensitivity to low-probability tokens; r is scaled by normalized brightness standard deviation (illumination variation) so that videos with large lighting shifts receive larger r, amplifying nonlinearity in the entropy calculation. The model is then run on both the natural and temporally-reversed frame sequences, and the per-token SME values are extracted for each. The final membership score is the mean of the smallest K% of the element-wise differences ΔS = S_nat − S_rev, exploiting the observation that training-set videos exhibit a larger entropy gap between natural and reversed orderings because the model is more confident in natural order but shows more pronounced confidence degradation when frames are reversed. This design is training-free and operates under a grey-box setting requiring only access to output logits and the tokenizer.

## Results

On the most challenging target (Video-XL-CinePile-7B), Vid-SME (Min_90%) achieves AUC 0.830, accuracy 76.5%, and TPR@5% FPR of 0.305, substantially outperforming the best baseline (ModRényi α=2, AUC 0.703). On LLaVA-NeXT-Video-34B, Vid-SME (Min_0%) reaches AUC 0.757 and TPR@5% FPR of 0.204, representing a 28.3% AUC improvement, 18.1% accuracy gain, and 293% TPR@5% FPR boost over the strongest baseline (Max_60% Rényi α=0.5, AUC 0.590). Across all five target VULLMs, Vid-SME consistently achieves the highest TPR@5% FPR, maintaining values above 0.05 even in the hardest settings where all baselines drop to near 0.001.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Vid-SME dominates all four baseline families across every target model. Against Perplexity [59] (loss-based), Vid-SME (Mean) improves AUC by 7.4–161 percentage points depending on the model, with the largest gap on Video-XL-CinePile-7B (0.840 vs. 0.679). Against Min-K% [43] (probability-based), Vid-SME (Min_90%) achieves AUC 0.830 vs. 0.680 on CinePile-7B and 0.538 vs. 0.461 on NExT-QA-7B, while TPR@5% FPR jumps from 0.004 to 0.305 on CinePile. Against MaxRényi/ModRényi [24] (Rényi-entropy-based), the advantage is most dramatic on LLaVA-NeXT-Video-34B where Vid-SME (Min_0%) reaches AUC 0.757 and TPR@5% FPR 0.204 versus the best Rényi variant's 0.590 and 0.045. Against Modified Entropy [50], Vid-SME consistently outperforms by 5–16 AUC points. Notably, all baselines exhibit AUC values both above and below 0.5 across different models, indicating unreliable and inconsistent discrimination, whereas Vid-SME maintains AUC ≥ 0.496 across all five targets with TPR@5% FPR never dropping below 0.035.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the membership inference attack (MIA) literature from text LLMs (Min-K% [43]) and image-based MLLMs (MaxRényi-K%, ModRényi [24]) to the video domain, where existing methods fail due to their static treatment of frame sequences. The key methodological departure is the use of Sharma-Mittal entropy with video-specific adaptive parameters (motion complexity via optical flow, illumination variation) combined with a natural-versus-reversed frame sequence comparison to capture temporal asymmetry in model confidence. The paper also builds on the Video-XL and LongVA training pipelines to construct controlled self-trained VULLM benchmarks, addressing the difficulty of isolating task-type and distribution effects in open-sourced models trained on heterogeneous multi-source data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic study of MIA risk in VULLMs, revealing that all prior MIA methods essentially fail on video data (AUC ≈ 0.5, TPR@5% FPR ≈ 0.001), which is a significant gap given the rapid deployment of video understanding models on potentially sensitive footage. The non-linear relationship between frame count, model memorization, and attack effectiveness (attacks degrade at intermediate frame counts before improving) is a useful characterization that could inform both attack design and defensive strategies. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to longer videos (beyond 32 frames), whether defenses such as differential privacy or temporal augmentation can mitigate the identified risk, and whether the grey-box assumption (access to logits) holds in real-world API settings.
