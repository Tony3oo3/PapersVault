## Problem

Practitioners increasingly use LLMs to generate entirely new synthetic samples (e.g., simulated survey responses), but naively combining such synthetic data with real data for downstream inference produces biased estimates with no statistical guarantees. Prior prediction-powered inference methods address proxy labels on existing text but leave open how to validly incorporate fully synthetic samples where both the text and its covariates/outcomes are model-generated.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255424/cropped_page7_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a small labeled dataset (text, covariates, outcomes), a large unlabeled text corpus, LLM proxy predictions for all texts, and LLM synthetic samples generated conditionally on each individual real text via in-context prompting. It produces a point estimate of the target regression coefficient along with asymptotically valid confidence intervals. Unlike competing debiasing methods (e.g., PPI++Synth), the estimator is hyperparameter-free, requiring no cross-fitting or tuning of mixture weights.

## Method

The paper proposes a two-step GMM estimator that augments the original moment conditions (evaluated only on labeled data) with auxiliary moments for separate parameter vectors η, each evaluated on both labeled and pooled (labeled + auxiliary) data. The key mechanism is that the optimal GMM weight matrix in the second step captures off-diagonal covariances between real-data moment residuals and synthetic-data moment residuals, enabling information sharing when the synthetic residuals are predictive of the real ones. Synthetic samples are generated by conditioning the LLM on each individual real text, introducing a deliberate correlation structure between paired real and synthetic observations. Theoretically, when synthetic and real residuals are independent, the asymptotic variance reduces exactly to the optimal real-data-only variance (no harm); when correlated, the variance is lower-bounded by the residual variance of regressing real residuals on synthetic residuals, yielding tighter confidence intervals.

## Results

GMM-Synth achieves the lowest MSE across all 8 downstream tasks (4 tasks × 2 model types: logistic regression and OLS), with reductions exceeding 50% in very low-label regimes (e.g., 10% labeled data). The method maintains valid asymptotic coverage and produces tighter confidence intervals in 7 out of 8 tasks. In terms of effective sample size, GMM-Synth saves up to more than 50% of human annotations while maintaining equivalent MSE compared to a classical estimator using only labeled data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares GMM-Synth against three adapted debiasing baselines: RePPI, PPI++Proxy, and PPI++Synth (with oracle α selection). GMM-Synth outperforms all baselines on MSE across all 8 task-model combinations. The most striking comparison is with PPI++Synth: while GMM-Synth consistently benefits from adding synthetic data (GMM-Synth > GMM-Proxy on all tasks), PPI++Synth shows no MSE improvement over PPI++Proxy in 5 out of 8 tasks, demonstrating that the GMM framework extracts substantially more information from synthetic samples. RePPI, which uses a learned mapping from proxy/synthetic losses to real losses, also underperforms GMM-Synth on MSE, though it retains valid coverage. All methods maintain approximately valid coverage, but GMM-Synth achieves the narrowest confidence intervals in 7/8 settings, translating to the largest effective sample size gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the prediction-powered inference (PPI) and debiasing literature (Angelopoulos et al. 2023, Ji et al. 2025), which handles model-predicted labels on real text (proxy data), to the setting of fully synthetic samples where the text itself is LLM-generated. Methodologically, it departs from the debiasing paradigm by adopting a two-step GMM framework (Hansen 1982; Newey & McFadden 1994), where the optimal weighting matrix automatically determines how to combine real, proxy, and synthetic data moments without requiring explicit bias-correction terms or hyperparameter tuning. The key conceptual departure is that the target parameter θ appears only in moments evaluated on labeled data, yet the GMM weighting mechanism allows auxiliary parameters defined solely on synthetic data to reduce the variance of θ's estimate through cross-moment correlations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This provides the first principled, hyperparameter-free mechanism for incorporating fully synthetic LLM-generated samples into statistically valid inference, addressing a gap that the PPI/debiasing literature has not addressed. The worst-case guarantee (synthetic data never hurts asymptotically) and the characterization of when it helps (residual predictability) give practitioners clear guidance for pipeline design. Limitations include reliance on asymptotic guarantees (potential undercoverage in very small samples), dependence on LLM generation quality, and validation on only four computational social science tasks. Open questions include whether the framework extends to more complex generative pipelines (multi-turn simulations, agent-based models) and whether the conditional distribution matching condition is realistically achievable with current LLMs.
