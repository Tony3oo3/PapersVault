## Problem

Estimating multiple quantiles under differential privacy has been limited by a rank error bound of O(log(b)·log²(m)/ε) under pure DP (Kaplan et al., ICML '22), and by log(1/δ) factors under approximate DP. This paper closes the gap by achieving O((log(b)+log²(m))/ε) rank error under pure DP and an error bound completely independent of δ under (ε,δ)-DP, at the cost of a mild minimum-gap assumption between queried quantiles.

## Input / Output

The mechanism consumes a dataset of n real numbers in [a,b] with minimum pairwise separation g (so the effective domain ratio is ψ=(b−a)/g), a set of m quantile queries q₁<…<qₘ, and privacy parameters (ε,δ). It outputs m quantile estimates z₁,…,zₘ whose accuracy is measured by maximum rank error |rank_X(zᵢ)−⌊qᵢn⌋|. A critical precondition is that the quantile gaps satisfy |qᵢ−qᵢ₋₁| ≥ Ω(m·log(m)·log(ψ)/(nε)) for pure DP (or a milder bound for approximate DP); if this fails, the protocol halts without accessing private data. The output is discretized to integers in [0,b] for the pure-DP guarantee, introducing at most 1 unit of additional rank error.

## Method

SliceQuantiles splits the problem into m independent single-quantile subproblems by first perturbing the target ranks r₁,…,rₘ with correlated noise from a continual counting (binary-tree) mechanism, then slicing the sorted dataset into disjoint windows Sᵢ = x₍r̃ᵢ₋ₕ₎,…,x₍r̃ᵢ₊ₕ₎ around each noisy rank, and finally applying the exponential mechanism to each slice. The key insight is that the correlated noise structure of continual counting guarantees that a single-point change in the dataset shifts at most one (addition) or two (removal) slices, enabling a parallel-composition privacy analysis rather than the sequential composition that would inflate error by a factor of poly(m). A novel injective mapping F⁺/F⁻ between noisy-rank vectors for adjacent datasets makes the privacy proof work despite the non-trivial overlap structure. Pure DP is recovered from the (ε,δ) guarantee by mixing in a uniformly random output with probability γ = δ|Y|/(e^ε−1), which is negligible when the output space is large. The slice size h is set via the exponential mechanism's concentration bound, and the continual counting error w is optimized numerically via a Chernoff bound on the discrete Laplace noise.

## Results

Under pure ε-DP, SliceQuantiles achieves a maximum rank error of O((log(ψ) + log²(m))/ε), improving over the prior best bound of O((log(ψ)log²(m) + log³(m))/ε) by a factor of min{log(ψ), log²(m)}. Under (ε,δ)-DP, the same error bound holds with no dependence on δ, unlike prior methods that incur log²(1/δ) factors. In experiments on AdultAge and AdultHours (n≈500,000, ε=1, δ=10⁻¹⁶), SliceQuantiles outperforms the AQ baseline by a factor of 2 in maximum rank error when estimating 200 quantiles under substitute adjacency. The required quantile gap for these parameters is only 0.005, allowing up to 200 equally-spaced quantiles to be answered.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is Approximate Quantiles (AQ) by Kaplan, Schnapp, and Stemmer [18], which achieves O(log(b)log²(m)/ε) rank error under pure DP. SliceQuantiles consistently outperforms AQ across all tested numbers of quantiles (m = 10 to 200) on both AdultAge and AdultHours, with the advantage most pronounced under substitute adjacency (where the theoretical bound is tighter) and for large m. Specifically, at m=200, n=500,000, ε=1, δ=10⁻¹⁶, SliceQuantiles reduces the maximum rank error by a factor of 2 compared to AQ. The histogram-based method of Lalanne et al. [19] (Hist) performs substantially worse, with error approximately one order of magnitude higher than both SliceQuantiles and AQ, due to its linear approximation of the data distribution. Notably, AQ under approximate DP performs worse than AQ under pure DP at δ=10⁻¹⁶, while SliceQuantiles is unaffected by δ in its error guarantee, giving it a clear practical advantage at small δ values.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the continual counting (binary tree) mechanism of Dwork et al. (STOC 2010) and the k-ary tree variant of Andersson et al. (2024), repurposing them to introduce correlated noise across multiple quantile rank estimates simultaneously. It departs from the divide-and-conquer approach of Kaplan, Schnapp, and Stemmer (ICML 2022) by instead partitioning the dataset into disjoint slices around each quantile and applying the exponential mechanism independently to each slice, with the continual counting step ensuring privacy under parallel composition rather than sequential composition. The key technical departure is an injective mapping between noisy rank vectors for adjacent datasets that handles the asymmetry between point addition and removal, enabling a tighter privacy analysis than prior slicing-based approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The error bound improvement (saving a factor of min(log(b), log²(m)) over the prior best pure-DP mechanism) is most impactful when estimating many quantiles, a common practical scenario. The approximate-DP variant's error being independent of δ is practically significant since prior methods incur log²(1/δ) factors that dominate for small δ. The main limitation is the quantile gap assumption, which restricts applicability to sufficiently spaced quantiles; the authors note that tighter analysis or alternative techniques may relax this. An open question is whether the gap requirement can be eliminated entirely while preserving the improved error bound, and whether the approach extends to streaming or local DP settings.
