## Problem

Existing video understanding benchmarks evaluate motion at the event level, are limited to third-person perspectives, or lack open-ended assessment, leaving fine-grained temporal motion comprehension largely unmeasured. This gap is critical for applications like embodied imitation learning and text-to-video generation that require precise understanding of detailed temporal dynamics.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254791/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark consumes 1,776 video clips (average 20.6 s) from ego-centric and third-person perspectives, sampled at model-specific frame rates (8–64 frames or 1 fps; proprietary APIs capped at 16 frames for videos >16 s). Close-ended tasks present four-option MCQA prompts targeting six motion sub-tasks, while open-ended tasks instruct models to describe temporal dynamics. Outputs are scored as MCQA accuracy, GPT-4o correctness/detailedness (1–10 scale), and a novel LLM-free score derived from structured sequence comparison.

## Method

FAVOR-Bench is built via a semi-automated pipeline: DeepSeek-R1 generates 20,402 initial MCQA pairs from structured motion annotations (subjects, per-subject timestamped action lists, camera motions), then blind filtering (Qwen2-72B answers without video) and single-frame filtering (GPT-4o with 5 sampled frames) remove questions solvable by language priors or isolated frames, leaving 8,184 verified pairs across six tasks. For open-ended evaluation, the paper proposes an LLM-free framework that uses an NLTK-based hierarchical parser to extract camera motion, subject lists, and action sequences from model responses, then scores them against ground-truth annotations via Sentence-BERT semantic similarity matrices, similarity-weighted precision/recall with a length-penalty factor, and Kendall's Tau for temporal ordering. To improve model performance, FAVOR-Train (17,152 annotated videos) is used for supervised fine-tuning of Qwen2.5-VL, yielding consistent gains on FAVOR-Bench, TVBench, and MotionBench.

## Results

On FAVOR-Bench, Gemini-1.5-Pro achieves the highest close-ended MCQA accuracy (49.77%), while Qwen2.5-VL-72B leads among open-source models (47.96%). For open-ended evaluation, Tarsier2-Recap-7B attains the best GPT-assisted score (4.60/4.38) and LLM-free score (56.58%), yet all models remain far below the human baseline (90.92% MCQA, 7.29/6.24 GPT, 74.25% LLM-free). Fine-tuning Qwen2.5-VL-7B on FAVOR-Train yields consistent gains: +1.07% MCQA, +0.27/+0.12 GPT-assisted, and +7.87% LLM-free, with the LLM-free score (56.33%) surpassing even Qwen2.5-VL-72B (49.72%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among proprietary models, Gemini-1.5-Pro leads on MCQA (49.77%) and GPT-assisted detailedness (4.68), while GPT-4o and Claude-3.7-Sonnet trail by 8.6 and 6.4 MCQA points respectively; all three proprietary models outperform open-source counterparts on MCQA but are surpassed by Tarsier2-Recap-7B on open-ended metrics. Among open-source models, Qwen2.5-VL-72B achieves the best MCQA (47.96%), exceeding VideoChat-Flash-Qwen2-7B (43.52%) and LLaVA-Video-72B-Qwen2 (45.81%) by 4.4 and 2.1 points. Tarsier2-Recap-7B, designed specifically for captioning, dominates open-ended evaluation with 4.60/4.38 GPT scores and 56.58% LLM-free, outperforming Gemini-1.5-Pro by 0.08/0.30 GPT points and 3.67 LLM-free points. The FAVOR-Train fine-tuned Qwen2.5-VL-7B (41.56% MCQA, 3.55/3.53 GPT, 56.33% LLM-free) surpasses the 72B variant on both GPT-assisted and LLM-free scores despite being 10× smaller, and exceeds all other 7B-scale models on LLM-free by at least 7.7 points. On external benchmarks, FAVOR-Train improves TVBench average from 45.2% to 46.1% and MotionBench-Dev overall from 46.2% to 47.9%, with the largest gain in Motion Recognition (+4.0%). Most models perform better on ego-centric than third-person MCQA, but reverse on open-ended tasks, indicating a description gap for first-person motion.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the video understanding benchmarking line (MVBench, TVBench, MLVU, EgoSchema) by narrowing focus to fine-grained motion and spanning both ego-centric and third-person perspectives, a combination absent from prior benchmarks. It departs from concurrent MotionBench by adding ego-centric videos, open-ended evaluation, and a novel LLM-free scoring framework that avoids the cost and opacity of GPT-assisted grading. The QA generation pipeline repurposes DeepSeek-R1 for semi-automated question construction with blind and single-frame filtering, building on the single-frame bias literature (Huang et al. 2018; Lei et al. 2023) to ensure temporal reasoning is genuinely required.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The quantified gap between the best model (~50% MCQA) and human performance (~91%) provides a concrete target for the field, and the asymmetry—models handle ego-centric MCQA better but describe third-person motion better in open-ended form—reveals distinct failure modes that prior event-level benchmarks masked. The LLM-free evaluation framework (Pearson r=0.86 with GPT-assisted) offers a reproducible, cost-effective alternative that lowers the barrier for community-wide benchmarking. Open questions include whether the modest FAVOR-Train gains (+1.07% MCQA, +7.87% LLM-free) scale with larger models or more diverse training data, and whether the cultural/regional under-representation in the dataset limits generalizability to global motion vocabularies.
