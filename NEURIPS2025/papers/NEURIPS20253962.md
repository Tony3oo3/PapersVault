## Problem

The Sinkhorn bridge method for estimating Schrödinger bridges previously lacked statistical guarantees in the two-sample setting, where only finite samples from both source and target distributions are available. Pooladian and Niles-Weed (2024) provided bounds only for the one-sample case with full access to the source distribution, and no analysis existed for intermediate Sinkhorn iterations that practitioners actually use. This paper fills both gaps by establishing convergence rates in the squared total variation distance for the two-sample estimator and for finite-iteration Sinkhorn bridges.

## Input / Output

The method consumes m i.i.d. samples from the source distribution μ and n i.i.d. samples from the target distribution ν (both supported in a ball of radius R), along with an entropic regularization parameter ε > 0. The output is a path measure P_{m,n}^{k} on C([0,1]; R^d) defined as the law of an SDE with drift b_{m,n}^{(k)}(z,t) and volatility √ε, restricted to the time interval [0, τ] with τ < 1 to avoid terminal-time degeneration. The drift can be evaluated exactly at O(n) cost per SDE step, or approximated by a 4-layer neural network (512-512-512 hidden units) trained via SGD on the Markovian projection loss.

## Method

The core contribution is a two-part error decomposition: a statistical term O(1/m + 1/n) from finite sampling (Theorem 1) and an algorithmic term r^{4k} from finite Sinkhorn iterations (Theorem 2), yielding the combined rate O(1/m + 1/n + r^{4k}). The proof introduces an auxiliary entropic plan coupling the true source μ with the empirical target ν_n, then applies Girsanov's theorem to convert path-measure KL divergence into integrated drift L² differences, which are bounded via Hilbert metric contraction of Sinkhorn iterates (with contraction factor tanh(R²/ε)). A key structural result (Propositions 1–2) shows that [SF]²M, lightSB(-M), DSBM-IMF, and BM2 all produce the same optimal drift as the converged Sinkhorn bridge, and that DSBM-IMF/BM2 with reference-process initialization exactly replicate intermediate Sinkhorn iterations, thereby extending the convergence guarantees to all these methods simultaneously.

## Results

The paper establishes two main theoretical convergence rates for the Sinkhorn bridge estimator in the two-sample setting. Theorem 1 gives a statistical bound E[TV²(P*_{∞,∞}, P*_{m,n})] = O(1/m + 1/n), improving upon the prior one-sample result of Pooladian and Niles-Weed (2024) which had rate n^{-1/2} in the target sample size. Theorem 2 gives an algorithmic bound E[TV²(P*_{m,n}, P^k_{m,n})] ≲ (τ/(1−τ))(R⁶/ε²)(tanh(R²/ε))^{4k}, showing exponential convergence in the number of Sinkhorn iterations k. Empirically, heatmaps (Figure 1) confirm the (m^{-1}+n^{-1}) scaling of drift MSE across multiple time points, and convergence plots (Figure 2) confirm exponential decay in k for ε=0.005, m=n=1000.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical analysis paper without traditional baseline comparisons. The key comparison is with Pooladian and Niles-Weed (2024), whose one-sample bound had rate n^{-1/2} in the target sample size; the present work sharpens this to n^{-1} at the cost of slightly worse dependence on ε, R, and ambient dimension d. The paper's main practical contribution is showing that its convergence guarantees directly apply to four existing Schrödinger bridge solvers — [SF]²M (Tong et al., 2023), DSBM-IMF (Shi et al., 2023), BM2 (Peluchetti, 2024), and lightSB(-M) (Korotin et al., 2023) — by proving their optimal estimators coincide with the Sinkhorn bridge (Proposition 1) and that DSBM-IMF/BM2 with reference-process initialization match the Sinkhorn bridge at every intermediate iteration k (Proposition 2). No head-to-head numerical benchmarking between these methods is performed; instead, the paper provides the first unified finite-sample generalization error analysis covering all of them simultaneously.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Sinkhorn bridge framework of Pooladian and Niles-Weed (2024) from the one-sample setting (where the source distribution is fully known) to the two-sample setting, and further analyzes intermediate Sinkhorn iterations rather than only the converged solution. The analysis draws on the Hilbert-metric convergence theory of the Sinkhorn algorithm (Franklin and Lorenz, 1989; Cuturi, 2013) and the minimum-intrinsic-dimension scaling results for entropic optimal transport (Stromme, 2023b). A notable conceptual contribution is showing that four independently developed Schrödinger bridge solvers—[SF]²M, DSBM-IMF, BM2, and lightSB(-M)—all produce the same optimal estimator as the Sinkhorn bridge, unifying their theoretical analysis under a single framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first finite-sample statistical guarantees for intermediate Sinkhorn iterations in the two-sample Schrödinger bridge setting, giving practitioners concrete guidance on how many samples (m, n) and iterations (k) are needed to achieve a target accuracy. The unification of [SF]²M, DSBM-IMF, BM2, and lightSB(-M) as special cases of the Sinkhorn bridge means that the convergence rates transfer directly to these widely-used methods, which previously lacked such guarantees. Open questions include removing the linear dependence on ambient dimension d, extending to more general reference processes beyond Brownian motion, and analyzing alternative EOT solvers beyond Sinkhorn iterations.
