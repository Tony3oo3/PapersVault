## Problem

Activation steering in LLMs requires selecting which layers to intervene on, but multi-layer selection faces combinatorial explosion (e.g., C(32,5)=187,488 subsets for a 32-layer model), making exhaustive evaluation on held-out data impractical. Existing heuristics (consecutive layers, top-performing single layer) are brittle and can severely degrade output fluency, especially for complex behaviors. The paper asks whether a principled, data-free criterion can identify the most steerable layers directly from the activations already computed during steering vector extraction.

## Input / Output

The method consumes the 2N layer-wise activations (N positive and N negative contrastive prompt pairs) that are already computed during steering vector generation, requiring zero additional forward passes or held-out evaluation data. It outputs a per-layer steerability score S_l = D_l + C_l, from which the top-K layers are selected for intervention; the steered model then produces text aligned with the target behavior. A key constraint is that the score is a relative ranking within a given extraction algorithm (MD or PCA) and is not comparable across different extraction methods.

## Method

LayerNavigator scores each layer's steerability as the sum of two components computed from Z-score-normalized activations. The discriminability score D_l is a normalized Fisher discriminant ratio that measures how well the steering direction v_l separates positive from negative activations by balancing between-class variance against within-class variance along v_l. The consistency score C_l is the mean cosine similarity between v_l and each individual contrastive pair's difference vector, capturing whether the steering direction is a stable signal rather than noise across examples. By jointly requiring both a strong directional signal (D_l) and cross-example stability (C_l), the method identifies layers where steering is both informative and reliable. The approach is extraction-agnostic (works with both Mean Difference and PCA vectors), requires no extra data or inference, and achieves over 550× speedup over grid-search baselines while matching or exceeding their alignment performance.

## Results

On Llama-3-8B-Instruct with K=5 steering layers, LayerNavigator achieves the highest alignment probability on 5 of 6 persona behaviors: 92.05% (Conscientiousness), 83.27% (Religion Following), 89.56% (Self-Aware), 84.06% (Self-Improvement), 92.88% (Alliance Building), and 85.74% (Impact Maximization), while maintaining low perplexity (14.2–46.5). In contrast, the Top baseline degrades catastrophically at K=5 (e.g., Self-Improvement drops to 51.68% with PPL 318.7; Alliance Building to 55.27% with PPL 838.8). LayerNavigator requires only 0.6 s on GPU for layer selection versus 347.8 s for Top/Around Top 1, a >550× speedup, and needs no held-out validation data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LayerNavigator is compared against four baselines: Random, Random Consec, Top (exhaustive single-layer evaluation on validation data), and Around Top 1 (consecutive layers centered on the best single layer). At K=5, LayerNavigator decisively outperforms all baselines on 5 of 6 behaviors, with the largest margins over Top (e.g., +41.2% on Alliance Building, +8.1% on Self-Improvement) and Around Top 1 (+37.6% on Alliance Building, +2.3% on Self-Improvement). The Top and Around Top 1 baselines suffer severe perplexity blow-ups at K=5 (PPL 318–839 vs. LayerNavigator's 18–47), indicating that their layer selections, while optimal for single-layer steering, produce incoherent multi-layer interference. At K=1, Top slightly edges out LayerNavigator on Conscientiousness (85.55% vs. 82.83%) and Self-Aware (84.69% vs. 80.67%), but LayerNavigator matches Top on Religion Following (80.36%) and maintains lower perplexity. On the full 135-task Anthropic Persona benchmark, LayerNavigator trails Top by ~1–2% on average but does so with zero additional inference passes and without requiring a held-out validation set, making it the only method that scales to multi-layer steering without combinatorial search.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits within the activation steering literature (ActAdd, ITI, CAA, ICV, CAST, ACT), where the layer selection problem has been addressed via held-out evaluation, trained probes, or fixed heuristics (e.g., consecutive middle layers). LayerNavigator departs from these by repurposing the Fisher discriminant ratio [7] and a cosine-alignment consistency measure, both computed from the same contrastive activations already used to extract the steering vector (MD or PCA, following Li et al. [9] and Rimsky et al. [18]). The key conceptual departure is that no additional forward passes, validation data, or auxiliary probes are needed—layer selection becomes a byproduct of the vector extraction step itself.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The combinatorial explosion of multi-layer steering (C(32,5) ≈ 187K evaluations) makes exhaustive search impractical, and LayerNavigator's zero-overhead scoring offers a practical path to principled multi-layer selection that scales to 64+ layer models. The finding that incorrect layer choices can catastrophically degrade fluency (PPL > 300) underscores why a reliable selection criterion matters beyond raw alignment scores. A key limitation is that steerability scores are not comparable across extraction algorithms, and the method is scoped to contrastive-pair-based steering; whether the discriminability-consistency framework extends to non-contrastive or conditional steering settings remains open.
