## Problem

Existing hypergraph neural networks for few-shot node classification rely on complex, nonlinear architectures with numerous parameters, making them prone to overfitting and poor scalability when labeled data is scarce. Despite the known advantages of linear models in few-shot settings, no prior work has explored whether a fully linear, parameter-free HNN can capture the high-order structural richness of hypergraphs while remaining efficient and generalizable.

## Input / Output

ZEN takes a hypergraph incidence matrix H, a node feature matrix X ∈ R^{|V|×d}, and a small set of k labeled nodes per class (5-shot in main experiments) as input, and directly outputs predicted class labels for all nodes via a single closed-form matrix product. No iterative training is performed; the only tunable hyperparameters are three propagation coefficients (α₀, α₁, α₂) constrained to the probability simplex, searched over 55 uniform samples. The model is entirely parameter-free in the sense that the weight matrix W is computed analytically from the labeled training nodes rather than learned via gradient descent.

## Method

ZEN first unifies five representative HNNs (HGNN, HNHN, UniGCNII, AllDeepSets, ED-HNN) into a single linear form Ŷ = PXW, where P is a propagation matrix built from l-hop adjacency matrices derived from H, and W is the sole d×c weight matrix. Two key innovations make this tractable and effective: (1) a Tractable Closed-form Solution (TCS) that, under assumptions of unit-norm embeddings and near-orthogonal inter-class structure, approximates the pseudoinverse in the optimal W* as a scalar multiple of the identity, reducing W* to a simple product (PX)ᵀD_trn Y with row/column L2 normalization; and (2) Redundancy-Aware Propagation (RAP) that explicitly subtracts the diagonal (residual self-information) from each l-hop adjacency matrix before combining them, preventing redundant self-signals from accumulating across hops and enabling precise control over self- versus neighbor-influence. The final classifier is a single forward pass: Ŷ = g_row(P*X) · g_col(g_row(P*X)ᵀ D_trn Y), restricted to 2-hop propagation, achieving up to 696× speedup over the fastest baseline while attaining the best average rank across 11 hypergraph benchmarks.

## Results

On 5-shot node classification across 10 real-world hypergraphs, ZEN achieves the best average rank (1.7) among 9 methods, with standout accuracies of 62.6% on Pubmed, 60.0% on Cora-CA, 97.6% on ModelNet40, and 73.2% on House. ZEN is up to 696× faster than the fastest baseline (HNHN on Senate) and over 1,700× faster than AllDeepSets on House, while requiring no iterative training. The model uses only 3 hyperparameters (α₀, α₁, α₂) searched over 55 simplex combinations, compared to 72 configurations for baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ZEN outperforms all 8 main baselines in average rank (1.7 vs. 3.6 for the next-best AllSetTransformer). Against the strongest competitor AllSetTransformer, ZEN wins on 7 of 10 datasets (e.g., +4.3% on Citeseer, +2.2% on Pubmed, +6.9% on House) but trails on 20News (68.6% vs. 70.3%) and Congress (87.0% vs. 88.2%). Against ED-HNN, ZEN gains substantial margins on Pubmed (+6.1%), Cora-CA (+1.2%), and House (+10.4%), though ED-HNN edges ahead on Congress (89.1% vs. 87.0%). Simpler baselines like HGNN and UniGCN remain competitive on 20News and Congress respectively, suggesting that architectural simplicity aids generalization in few-shot settings, but ZEN surpasses them on the majority of datasets. In terms of speed, ZEN is 2.5×–696× faster than all baselines, with the largest gaps on dense hypergraphs (Senate, House) where iterative training-based methods incur the highest overhead.

## Relation to Prior Work

ZEN sits at the intersection of two research threads: (1) the linearization of graph neural networks (SGC, APPNP, S²GC, SlimG), which showed that removing nonlinearities and decoupling propagation from transformation yields competitive, interpretable models, and (2) hypergraph neural networks (HGNN, HNHN, UniGCNII, AllDeepSets, ED-HNN), which capture higher-order interactions but rely on complex nonlinear architectures. The paper's key conceptual move is to transplant the linear-GNN paradigm into the hypergraph domain and then go one step further by eliminating even the single remaining weight matrix via a closed-form solution, yielding a truly parameter-free model. The redundancy-aware propagation mechanism is a hypergraph-specific contribution that addresses self-information entanglement across multi-hop walks, a problem less acute in pairwise graphs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that a zero-parameter linear model matches or beats eight nonlinear HNNs in few-shot hypergraph node classification challenges the prevailing assumption that nonlinearity is necessary to capture high-order structure, and the up-to-696× speedup makes hypergraph learning practical at scale. The fully interpretable decision process (weight matrix entries directly encode feature-to-class importance) is a rare property among HNNs and valuable for domains like biology or recommendation where trust matters. A key limitation is that ZEN is restricted to node classification and 2-hop propagation; extending the framework to hyperedge prediction, clustering, or deeper propagation (where RSI computation becomes intractable) remains open. The approximation in Theorem 1 also degrades as the number of classes grows, suggesting a ceiling for very fine-grained classification.
