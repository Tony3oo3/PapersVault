## Problem

Existing low-rank PEFT methods (LoRA, PiSSA, AdaLoRA) assume that weight updates, weights, or gradients lie in a single low-dimensional subspace, creating a fundamental trade-off: a small rank limits expressiveness while a large rank inflates trainable parameters and memory. The paper identifies that pretrained network weights are actually distributed across multiple linear subspaces rather than one, and that this multi-subspace structure has been overlooked by prior single-subspace approaches.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained weight matrix W₀ ∈ ℝ^{d×n} from any transformer layer (ViT, RoBERTa, LLaMA, Mistral, or Gemma) and produces an updated weight matrix via a compact block-diagonal low-rank representation. The initialization requires a truncated SVD of W₀ (rank R), a closed-form LRR coefficient matrix Z₀* = V₀V₀ᵀ, and a subspace clustering step (NCut) to assign columns to K subspaces. Downstream, the frozen A^(k) matrices and trainable B^(k), C^(k) pairs produce task-specific outputs: class labels for image classification, correlation/accuracy scores for NLU, and generated text for NLG.

## Method

AdaMSS first computes the truncated SVD of W₀ and applies the closed-form Low-Rank Representation (LRR) to obtain Z₀* = V₀V₀ᵀ, which empirically exhibits an approximate block-diagonal structure revealing K distinct subspaces. Subspace segmentation (via NCut on the affinity matrix) partitions the weight columns into K blocks, yielding a strictly block-diagonal representation where each block (Z₀*)^(k) captures the local low-rank structure of its subspace. For each subspace k, the update is parameterized as W^(k) = W₀^(k) + A^(k)B^(k)C^(k), where A^(k) is frozen (from the SVD of the block) and B^(k) ∈ ℝ^{R_k×r_k}, C^(k) ∈ ℝ^{r_k×n_k} are trainable with C^(k) initialized to zero. An adaptive budget allocation mechanism, adapted from PLATON's importance scores, progressively freezes less important subspaces during training via a cubic decay schedule, reducing trainable parameters from all K subspaces down to K_target. Theoretically, under the same total rank, AdaMSS achieves a lower Gaussian complexity bound (∑√(r_k·n_k) ≤ √(r·n) by Cauchy-Schwarz) than any single-subspace method, providing a stronger generalization guarantee.

## Results

On ViT-Large across seven image classification tasks, AdaMSS (r_k=3) achieves 4.7% higher average accuracy than LoRA (r=16) while using only 15.4% of the trainable parameters. On RoBERTa-Large (GLUE), AdaMSS (r_k=1) outperforms PiSSA (r=1) by approximately 7% in average accuracy while reducing trainable parameters by ~94.4%. On LLaMA 2-7B, AdaMSS* (r_k=3) achieves 50.80% on GSM8K and 7.22% on MATH with only 4M trainable parameters, surpassing LoRA* (42.30/5.50 with 320M) and PiSSA* (44.11/5.84 with 19M). On Gemma-7B, AdaMSS* reaches 76.41% on GSM8K with 4M parameters, exceeding LoRA* (74.90% with 200M).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LoRA, AdaMSS consistently wins on GSM8K and MATH for LLaMA 2-7B (50.80 vs. 42.30, 7.22 vs. 5.50) using only 1.25% of LoRA's trainable parameters, and achieves 4.7% higher average accuracy on ViT-Large with 15.4% of LoRA's parameters. Against PiSSA, AdaMSS outperforms by ~7% average accuracy on RoBERTa-Large GLUE tasks while using ~94.4% fewer parameters, and leads on LLaMA 2-7B GSM8K (50.80 vs. 44.11 for PiSSA*), though PiSSA* slightly edges AdaMSS* on Mistral-7B GSM8K (71.00 vs. 70.74). Against LoRA-PRO, AdaMSS wins on LLaMA 2-7B (50.80 vs. 46.61 GSM8K) and Gemma-7B (76.41 vs. 75.90 GSM8K) with fewer parameters. Against LoRETTA, AdaMSS achieves substantially higher accuracy on all NLG tasks (e.g., 50.80 vs. 37.86 on LLaMA 2-7B GSM8K) while using ~13x more parameters (4M vs. 0.3M). Against AdaLoRA and DyLoRA on GLUE, AdaMSS achieves comparable or higher average accuracy with fewer trainable parameters. Against WeGeFT (DFT-based PEFT), AdaMSS outperforms on most image classification and NLU benchmarks while using fewer parameters.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the low-rank adaptation lineage (LoRA, PiSSA, AdaLoRA, LoRA-GA) by replacing the single-subspace assumption with a multi-subspace decomposition derived from Low-Rank Representation (LRR) subspace segmentation. While LoRA and its variants assume weight changes or gradients lie in one low-dimensional subspace, AdaMSS observes that pretrained weight columns are approximately distributed across multiple linear subspaces (evidenced by the block-diagonal structure of the LRR coefficient matrix) and applies independent low-rank updates within each subspace. The adaptive budget allocation mechanism borrows the importance-scoring idea from AdaLoRA/PLATON but operates at the subspace granularity rather than the individual parameter level, progressively freezing less relevant subspaces during training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a core tension in PEFT: single-subspace methods must trade expressiveness against parameter count by choosing a global rank r, whereas AdaMSS's per-subspace rank allocation (r_k) and adaptive freezing allow finer-grained control. The 94.4% parameter reduction on RoBERTa-Large while matching or exceeding PiSSA/LoRA performance is practically significant for deployment-constrained settings. Open questions include whether the multi-subspace structure is universal across architectures and pretraining regimes, how the method scales to models with tens of billions of parameters (the SVD + clustering initialization cost is non-trivial), and whether the theoretical Gaussian complexity advantage translates to measurable generalization gains in deeper networks beyond the shallow-network analysis provided.
