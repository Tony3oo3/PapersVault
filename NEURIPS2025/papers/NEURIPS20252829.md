## Problem

State Space Models like Mamba offer efficient long-sequence modeling, but scaling their expressive power via Mixture of Experts remains unsolved. Naive application of MoE to Mamba's individual projection layers (Conv, Gate, Output) consistently degrades performance rather than improving it, as the interdependent roles of these layers resist independent expert routing. No prior work has successfully integrated Mamba layers as MoE experts to scale total model parameters.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252829/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a text sequence X ∈ R^{L×D_m} (sequence length L up to 16K, hidden size D_m) and produces next-token probability distributions for autoregressive language modeling. At each time step t, a lightweight router projects the token embedding through W_r ∈ R^{D_m×N} to select K experts (typically Top-1 out of 8), and the same routing decision is shared across the Conv, Gate, and Output projection experts. Smaller sub-modules (x Proj, dt Proj, 1D Conv) are shared across all experts, reducing per-expert parameter overhead.

## Method

Routing Mamba (RoM) selectively converts Mamba's three major projection layers—Conv Proj, Gate Proj, and Out Proj—into sparsely activated linear projection experts under a single shared router. The key insight is that a unified routing decision (TopK over Softmax(X_t·W_r)) applied simultaneously to all three projection layers preserves the functional coherence of the Mamba block, whereas independent routers per layer fragment specialization and degrade performance. Smaller components (x Proj, dt Proj, depthwise Conv) are shared across experts following a Multi-Query Attention analogy, and no explicit load-balance loss is needed as the routing naturally balances expert utilization. The formulation replaces the dense projections with weighted sums over selected experts: H_t = Σ 1_{i∈TopK} · X_t W_{in,i} for the input path, G = SiLU(Σ 1_{i∈TopK} · X_t W_{g,i}) for the gate, and O_t = Σ R_i(X_t) · (Y_t ⊙ G W_{out,i}) for the output, achieving up to 2.3× parameter efficiency over dense Mamba at matched perplexity.

## Results

At 1.3B active parameters (10B total), RoM achieves a validation PPL of 8.52 at 4K context length, matching a dense Mamba model that requires over 2.3× more active parameters (3B). On the Samba 511M hybrid model, RoM (Conv, Gate, Out) achieves PPL of 9.28 / 8.92 / 8.85 at 4K / 8K / 16K context lengths, outperforming the Samba (expand=4) baseline (9.78 / 9.39 / 9.31) while saving 23% FLOPs. RoM also demonstrates consistent perplexity across context lengths (4K to 16K), with minimal degradation, confirming strong length extrapolation. On downstream tasks, the hybrid RoM + FFN-MoE (8top1) configuration achieves 50.1% average accuracy, closely matching the FFN-MoE (16top1) baseline at 50.2%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against dense Mamba, RoM consistently achieves lower PPL at every scale tested (115M–1.3B active params), with the dense Mamba requiring 2.0–2.3× more active parameters to reach equivalent perplexity (e.g., RoM 115M active / 710M total achieves PPL 12.33 at 4K vs. Mamba 115M at 14.31). Against Samba (expand=4), RoM (Conv, Gate, Out) on Samba 511M improves PPL from 9.78 to 9.28 at 4K while reducing FLOPs by 23% (6.13T vs. 6.13T for the same active params but fewer total compute). Against SwitchHead and MoA (both adapted to Samba's attention layers with matched total parameters of 1.1B), RoM (Conv, Gate, Out) at 1.0B total parameters outperforms both across all context lengths (9.77/9.38/9.31 vs. 9.84/9.45/9.37 for SwitchHead and 9.94/9.54/9.46 for MoA). Against MoE-Mamba (the prior naive MoE integration), RoM delivers a substantial improvement: at Samba 421M with 1.0B total params, RoM achieves PPL 9.31 at 16K versus MoE-Mamba's 9.77, confirming that independent per-layer routing degrades performance while shared routing is essential.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Mixture-of-Experts paradigm—previously applied almost exclusively to Transformer FFN layers (Switch Transformers, GShard, Mixtral) and attention projections (SwitchHead, MoA)—into the State Space Model domain by converting Mamba's Conv, Gate, and Output projection layers into sparsely activated linear experts. It directly departs from prior failed attempts to integrate MoE with SSMs (MoE-Mamba, BlackMamba), which either degraded performance or relied on non-scalable rule-based sparsity. The key conceptual departure is the shared routing strategy: a single router selects a unified expert pathway across all major projection layers within a Mamba block, rather than assigning independent routers per layer, drawing an analogy to holistic expert selection in standard MLP-MoE.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RoM establishes the first successful and scalable framework for sparse MoE integration into SSMs, demonstrating that the 2.3× active-parameter efficiency gain achievable with FFN-MoE in Transformers can be replicated in the SSM setting. The negative result—that naive per-layer MoE application to Mamba consistently degrades perplexity—provides important guidance for future SSM architecture design. Open questions remain around optimal expert configurations, generalization to the rapidly evolving landscape of SSM variants (Mamba-2, Gated DeltaNet, Hymba), and whether the shared routing principle extends cleanly to more complex hybrid or linear-attention architectures. The 23% FLOPs saving over dense Mamba scaling for hybrid models suggests a practical path toward more cost-efficient long-context language models.
