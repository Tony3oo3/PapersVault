## Problem

LoRA's low-rank updates exhibit a stable rank far below their algebraic rank (as low as 1.06 for rank-32 adapters), causing all neuron update directions to collapse onto a single axis. This directional diversity collapse means the allocated rank capacity is severely underutilized, limiting the adapter's effective expressiveness despite theoretical results showing LoRA can approximate any target model under mild rank assumptions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254996/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained linear layer weight W₀ ∈ ℝ^{m×n} and a fine-tuning dataset, and produces an additive low-rank update ΔW = XΘYᵀ where X ∈ St(m,r) and Y ∈ St(n,r) are column-orthogonal direction matrices on Stiefel manifolds, and Θ ∈ ℝ^{r×r} is an unconstrained scale matrix. During training, iterates are not strictly on the manifold (infeasible optimization), but an infeasibility penalty drives them to satisfy XᵀX = I_r and YᵀY = I_r in the limit. At inference, Θ can be merged into X or Y, making storage and runtime identical to standard LoRA.

## Method

PoLAR reparameterizes the LoRA update via polar decomposition: each low-rank factor Z₁, Z₂ is factored into a Stiefel-constrained direction matrix (orthonormal columns) and a PSD magnitude matrix, with the two magnitude matrices merged into a single unconstrained Θ ∈ ℝ^{r×r}. This enforces orthogonality among the r learned directions, directly preventing the directional diversity collapse observed in vanilla LoRA. For optimization, PoLAR employs a landing field Γ(X) = ψ(X)X + λ∇N(X) that combines the Riemannian gradient with an infeasibility penalty, avoiding expensive SVD-based retractions and requiring only matrix multiplications (≥3× faster on GPU). Theoretically, on a canonical overparameterized matrix factorization problem, PoLAR with Riemannian gradient descent achieves global convergence with a κ⁴ condition-number dependence, exponentially improving over the κ^κ or κ¹⁵ dependence of GD on the Burer-Monteiro parameterization, while also ensuring last-iteration convergence without early stopping.

## Results

On Llama-2-7B commonsense reasoning (8 tasks), PoLAR achieves 73.26–74.00% average accuracy across ranks 4–32, consistently outperforming LoRA (72.18–72.76%) and DoRA (72.39–72.55%), with the gap widening as rank increases. On Gemma-3-27B mathematical reasoning, PoLAR improves over LoRA by +0.30 on GSM8K (85.67 vs 85.37) and +0.76 on MATH (42.70 vs 41.94). On GLUE with DeBERTa-V3-base, PoLAR achieves 88.94% average versus 88.18% for LoRA, with notable gains on SST-2 (+0.78), CoLA (+1.72), and RTE (+2.34).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PoLAR consistently outperforms LoRA across all three benchmark suites. On Llama-2-7B commonsense reasoning, PoLAR leads LoRA by 0.75–1.24 points in average accuracy, with the advantage growing from +0.75 at rank 4 to +1.24 at rank 16 before slightly narrowing at rank 32 (+1.53); notably, PoLAR's accuracy increases with rank while LoRA's plateaus or declines, confirming that PoLAR better exploits the allocated subspace. Against DoRA, PoLAR wins by 0.71–1.61 points on average, with the largest gap at rank 16 (+1.61). On Gemma-3-27B math reasoning, PoLAR beats LoRA by +0.30 on GSM8K and +0.76 on the harder MATH benchmark. On GLUE (DeBERTa-V3-base), PoLAR improves over LoRA by +0.76 on average, with the largest gains on RTE (+2.34) and CoLA (+1.72), though QQP and MRPC show comparable results. The paper also notes that PoLAR's stable rank of the learned update (5.49) is substantially higher than LoRA's (1.06) at the same nominal rank of 32, providing a mechanistic explanation for the accuracy gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

PoLAR reparameterizes the LoRA low-rank update ΔW = Z₁Z₂ᵀ by applying polar decomposition to each factor, yielding Stiefel-constrained direction matrices and an unconstrained r×r scale matrix. This places it in the lineage of direction-magnitude decompositions (DoRA, AdaLoRA) but distinguishes itself by enforcing column orthogonality via manifold constraints rather than per-column normalization or diagonal scaling. On the optimization side, it adopts the retraction-free landing field from Ablin & Peyré (2022) and Gao et al. (2022), which had previously been applied to LoRA fine-tuning (Zhang et al., 2024) but not to a polar-decomposed parameterization. The theoretical analysis positions PoLAR against the Burer-Monteiro matrix factorization literature, showing that the Stiefel constraint fundamentally changes the convergence landscape compared to unconstrained BM factors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The empirical demonstration that LoRA updates collapse to a stable rank of ~1 despite nominal rank 32 provides a concrete diagnostic for why simply increasing LoRA rank yields diminishing returns (consistent with Kalajdzievski, 2023), shifting the bottleneck narrative from expressiveness to optimization dynamics. The κ⁴ convergence guarantee (vs. κ^κ for overparameterized GD) is a meaningful theoretical advance, and the landing-field implementation makes the method a practical drop-in for existing LoRA pipelines with 3×+ speedup over retraction-based alternatives. Limitations include evaluation restricted to NLP/transformer architectures up to 27B parameters, single-seed runs for large models, and modest absolute gains (0.5–1.5% on commonsense, ~0.3–0.8% on math). Open questions include whether the stable-rank collapse phenomenon and its remedy generalize to non-transformer architectures, vision tasks, and whether the spectral dynamics of low-rank fine-tuning can be further characterized.
