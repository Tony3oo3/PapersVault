## Problem

Decision Transformer and similar attention-based sequence models for offline RL exhibit attention 'dispersal' across timesteps, failing to focus on the short-range local dependencies that Markovian decision-making actually requires. The paper identifies the root cause—inconsistent state-action-reward distributions within each timestep—and asks whether simpler, non-attention operators can replace self-attention while better preserving the Markov property.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250093/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a restructured trajectory sequence where each timestep's (return-to-go, state, action) triplet is shifted so the model predicts a_t conditioned on (a_{t-1}, s_t, R_t), with context length K=20 for D4RL and K=16 for real-world. For the Franka Emika Panda robot, raw inputs are RGB images from three cameras plus 7D joint positions, encoded via ResNet50 and fused with FiLM into a 512-dimensional state vector before entering the sequence model. The output is a continuous action vector (10-dimensional for the robot, task-specific dimensions for D4RL).

## Method

DHi decomposes sequence modeling into two hierarchical stages: a convolutional Token Merger that fuses each <R,s,a> triplet into a single unified token (reducing sequence length by one-third and enforcing modality alignment), and a parameter-free average-pooling Token Mixer with window size 2 that captures inter-step dependencies without any learned parameters. The design is motivated by an attention entropy analysis showing DT's failure is inter-step (dispersed attention across timesteps) rather than intra-step, and by Theorem 3.1 proving that triplet grouping strictly reduces attention entropy. A theoretical result (Appendix A/B) further proves that a single-layer pooling model can exactly represent first-order Markov transition kernels, justifying the choice of average pooling over more complex mixers. The final architecture uses 3 layers with GELU activation, trained via supervised next-token prediction on offline trajectories, and achieves 73.6% faster inference and 9.3% higher D4RL scores than DT with 32% fewer parameters.

## Results

On the D4RL benchmark, DHi achieves an average normalized score of 83.3 on Gym (vs. DT 76.2, DC 82.2, GDT 79.1), 50.1 on Adroit, 65.0 on Kitchen, and 85.5 on AntMaze, representing a 9.3% overall improvement over DT. In real-world robotic manipulation on a Franka Emika Panda arm, DHi achieves a 100% success rate across all four tasks (Lift, Selective Lift, Place, Selective Place) while DT drops to 40% on Selective Lift. DHi also requires 32% fewer parameters (6.6M vs. 9.7M) and achieves 35% faster inference (1.7s vs. 2.3s for 400 actions) compared to DT, with a 73.6% overall inference speedup on the D4RL benchmark.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DHi outperforms DT by 9.3% on the overall D4RL Gym average (83.3 vs. 76.2) and by a wide margin on hopper-medium-replay (97.7 vs. 82.7) and walker2d-medium-replay (81.2 vs. 79.4). Against the strongest sequence-modeling baseline DC, DHi wins on Gym (83.3 vs. 82.2) and Kitchen (65.0 vs. 62.5) but trails slightly on Adroit (50.1 vs. 52.5), where DC's convolutional local filtering gives it an edge on dexterous manipulation. DHi substantially outperforms GDT on Gym (83.3 vs. 79.1) and AntMaze (85.5 vs. 72.5), and beats FCNet on Gym (83.3 vs. 75.1) while FCNet remains competitive on AntMaze (83.0 vs. 85.5). Among value-based methods, DHi surpasses CQL (80.6), IQL (77.0), and BCQ (53.8) on the Gym average. In real-world robotic manipulation, DHi achieves a perfect 100% success rate on all four tasks including the challenging Selective Lift, whereas DT drops to 40% on that task, while also using 32% fewer parameters and running 35% faster.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Decision Transformer (DT) lineage for offline RL by adopting the MetaFormer architectural principle of separating token feature operations (Token Merger) from token mixing operations (Token Mixer). It builds directly on Decision ConvFormer (DC), whose codebase is used as the implementation foundation, and on the observation from DC and StARformer that DT's global self-attention is poorly suited to the local, Markovian dependencies in trajectory data. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of attention entropy as a diagnostic metric to explain *why* DT's attention disperses, followed by the finding that a parameter-free average pooling operation suffices as the inter-step Token Mixer, eliminating the need for any learned mixing operator.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled explanation (via entropy decomposition) for a long-noted weakness of DT and demonstrates that the complex attention machinery in sequence-based offline RL is largely unnecessary for capturing the short-range dependencies that actually drive decision-making. This has practical implications for real-time robotic control, where the 73.6% inference speedup and 32% parameter reduction matter. However, the performance gains over the strongest baseline (DC) are modest and inconsistent across domains (e.g., Adroit), and the authors acknowledge the lack of evaluation on transferability to other RL paradigms. Open questions include whether the Markov-property assumption generalizes to non-Markovian or partially observable settings, and whether the pooling-based approach scales to longer-horizon or multi-agent tasks.
