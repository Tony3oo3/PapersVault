## Problem

DPO's binary win-or-lose sequence-level supervision provides limited signal for small language models with constrained capacity, making it difficult to assess fine-grained preference quality. Prior preference distillation methods focus on mimicking teacher behavior through KL divergence or behavior cloning, overlooking the teacher's internal reward modeling. This paper addresses how to transfer the teacher's reward structure to a student without introducing conflict with the original DPO objective.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-collected offline DPO dataset of (prompt, preferred response, dispreferred response) triplets along with precomputed teacher logits over those sequences, requiring no additional rollouts from either teacher or student during training. The teacher's soft value function is derived from its token-level logits and used as a fixed shaping signal. The output is a trained student policy that generates text responses, with the teacher's value function providing soft sequence-level reward margins that go beyond the binary preference label.

## Method

TVKD extracts the soft value function V_φ(s) = β log Σ_a exp(Q_φ(s,a)/β) from a DPO-trained teacher, where Q_φ are the teacher's token-level logits interpreted as soft Q-values under MaxEnt RL. This value function constructs a potential-based shaping term ψ_φ(s,a) = V_φ(s') − V_φ(s) that satisfies Ng et al.'s PBRS conditions, theoretically guaranteeing that the optimal policy of the original DPO MDP is preserved. The shaping term modifies the DPO loss by dividing the student's log-probability ratio by exp((α/β)ψ_φ(s,a)), effectively adding a soft reward margin that encodes how much better the teacher considers one trajectory over another. Because the shaping term telescopes over a trajectory, it naturally handles variable-length sequences without explicit normalization, and the gradient flows solely through the student's log-probabilities since the teacher's value function is fixed.

## Results

Using LLaMA-3.2-1B as student and LLaMA-3.1-8B as teacher, TVKD achieves the best scores across all four benchmarks on both DPO-MIX and HelpSteer2 datasets. On DPO-MIX, TVKD scores RM −1.15, MT-Bench 3.97, AlpacaEval 52.18%, and OLL 41.61; on HelpSteer2, it scores RM −1.18, MT-Bench 3.98, AlpacaEval 54.85%, and OLL 41.35. Notably, TVKD is the only method that surpasses ADPA (the strongest prior distillation baseline) on AlpacaEval, gaining +2.18 and +4.85 win-rate points on DPO-MIX and HelpSteer2 respectively. The method requires no additional rollouts and operates fully offline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TVKD outperforms all 10 baselines on RM and MT-Bench across both datasets, with the largest margins over KL-divergence-based KD methods (DCKD, DPKD, GKD) which trail by 0.4–0.6 RM points. Against the strongest prior distillation method ADPA, TVKD gains +0.24 RM and +0.24 MT on DPO-MIX, and +0.25 RM and +0.41 MT on HelpSteer2, while also being the only method to beat ADPA on AlpacaEval (52.18 vs 50.00 on DPO-MIX; 54.85 vs 50.00 on HelpSteer2). Compared to standard DPO, TVKD improves RM by 0.26 and AlpacaEval by 5.15 points on DPO-MIX, showing that the teacher value signal adds meaningful preference granularity beyond binary supervision. On the Open LLM Leaderboard, TVKD achieves the best average (41.61 on DPO-MIX) and is competitive on HelpSteer2 (41.35 vs SimPO's 41.33), indicating that the method does not sacrifice general capability. The advantage over DDPO (which aligns reward margins between teacher and student) suggests that value-based shaping provides a more effective proxy for human preference than direct reward-matching.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the DPO framework (Rafailov et al., 2023) and its interpretation as a soft Q-function (Rafailov et al., 2024), repurposing the teacher's value function as a potential-based reward shaping signal. It departs from prior preference distillation methods (DCKD, DPKD, ADPA, DDPO) that focus on mimicking teacher behavior via KL divergence or logit matching, instead distilling the teacher's internal reward model. The key conceptual departure is the use of Ng et al.'s (1999) potential-based reward shaping to guarantee that the teacher signal augments rather than conflicts with the DPO objective, a connection not previously drawn in the LLM alignment literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TVKD offers a theoretically grounded, fully offline mechanism to transfer a teacher's reward modeling to small models without the computational overhead of rollouts or the policy-conflict issues of naive KL-based distillation. The PBRS guarantee is practically important: it means practitioners can add teacher guidance without risking misalignment with the human preference data. However, the method assumes the teacher's value function is reliable, and robustness to weak or mismatched teachers remains untested. Open questions include whether the value-function interpretation generalizes to non-DPO-trained teachers, whether the approach extends to online or multi-turn settings, and whether the modest but consistent gains (e.g., ~0.3 MT-bench points) scale to larger student models or more diverse preference datasets.
