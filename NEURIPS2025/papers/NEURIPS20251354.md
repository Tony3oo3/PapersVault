## Problem

The optimal regret for OCO on ℓ_p-balls (p>2) shifts between high-dimensional (d>T) and low-dimensional (d≤T) regimes, requiring different regularisers (uniformly-convex vs. strongly-convex) for optimality in each. When the horizon T is unknown, the learner cannot determine which regime applies, raising the question of whether a single fixed regulariser can achieve anytime optimality. The paper answers this negatively for separable regularisers, proving that adaptive regularisation is necessary.

## Input / Output

The learner selects points x_t from the unit ℓ_p-ball in R^d (p>2) and suffers L-Lipschitz convex losses in ℓ_p-norm, with both the dimension d and time horizon T potentially unknown at the start of the game. The algorithm outputs an action point per round and a regret guarantee that must hold simultaneously in both dimension regimes without prior knowledge of which applies. A key constraint is that the regulariser must be chosen before knowing whether d>T or d≤T, making the anytime requirement the central design challenge.

## Method

The anytime-optimal algorithm uses FTRL with a time-varying regulariser that switches from the uniformly-convex φ_p(x)=(1/p)||x||_p^p to the strongly-convex φ_2(x)=(1/2)||x||_2^2 at threshold t_0=3^{-2p/(p-2)}·d, with step-sizes tuned to each regime so that the switch incurs no regret cost. The main negative result (Theorem 4.6) proves no separable regulariser ψ(x)=Σg(x_i) can be anytime optimal, using a two-phase adversarial loss construction where gradients cancel in the first half of rounds and push the competitor to a corner of the ℓ_p-ball in the second half, creating a bias-variance trade-off that no fixed step-size can resolve. The proof hinges on Lemma 4.7 (achieving √T regret in 1D requires quadratic growth of the regulariser) combined with Lemma 4.4 (any regulariser with quadratic growth suffers linear regret in sufficiently high dimensions). The paper further establishes that any strongly-convex regulariser is provably sub-optimal in high-dimensions (Theorem 4.2) and that φ_p is sub-optimal in low-dimensions (Proposition 4.5), and for the bandit feedback variant shows sub-linear regret is unachievable when d is large enough for all ℓ_p-balls with p≥1.

## Results

The paper establishes that the optimal regret for OCO on ℓ_p-balls (p>2) is O(L√(Td^{1-2/p})) in the low-dimensional regime (d≤T) and O(LT^{1-1/p}) in the high-dimensional regime (d>T). FTRL with adaptive regularisation (switching from uniformly-convex φ_p to strongly-convex φ₂ at threshold t₀≈d) achieves these optimal rates in both regimes simultaneously without knowing T in advance. The main negative result (Theorem 4.6) proves that no fixed separable regulariser can be anytime optimal: any such regulariser is provably sub-optimal in at least one dimension regime. For the linear bandit problem, the paper shows sub-linear regret is unachievable when d is sufficiently large (Theorem 5.1), with any learner suffering Ω(LT) regret.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares FTRL with three regularisation strategies on the adversarial loss construction from Section 4 (Figure 1, T=40, L=1, p=10, varying d). FTRL-φ₂ (strongly-convex, building on [28]) achieves the optimal O(√(Td^{1-2/p})) regret in low dimensions but its regret grows polynomially with d and becomes linear in T in high dimensions, making it provably sub-optimal there (Proposition 4.1). FTRL-φ_p (uniformly-convex of degree p, building on [40]) achieves the optimal O(T^{1-1/p}) regret in high dimensions but is provably sub-optimal in low dimensions (Proposition 4.5), where its regret is bounded below by Ω(T^{1/p_★}) rather than the optimal O(√(Td^{1-2/p})). The adaptive procedure from Theorem 3.1 (switching regularisers at t₀) matches the optimal rate in both regimes at no cost, and the experiment confirms it outperforms either fixed regulariser across the full range of dimensions. The paper further shows (Theorem 4.6) that this adaptivity is not merely sufficient but necessary for any separable regulariser, ruling out the existence of a single fixed regulariser that is simultaneously optimal in both regimes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work characterising optimal regret for OCO on ℓ_p-balls, building on Levy & Duchi [28] for p∈[1,2], Sridharan & Tewari [40] and Bhaskara et al. [4] for uniformly-convex regularisation in high dimensions, and Orabona [36] for the FTRL analysis framework. The key departure is that prior work treated the high-dimensional (d>T) and low-dimensional (d≤T) regimes separately, each with its own optimal regulariser, whereas this paper asks whether a single fixed regulariser can be optimal across both regimes simultaneously. The result connects to the universality question for mirror descent raised by Sridharan [39], providing a negative answer for separable regularisers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central negative result—that no fixed separable regulariser (including all ‖x‖_r^r for r≥1 and diagonal Adagrad-style step-sizes) can be anytime optimal—establishes a fundamental limitation on the design of online learning algorithms for ℓ_p-balls with p>2. This means practitioners cannot rely on a single 'universal' regulariser and must either use adaptive switching or accept sub-optimality in one regime. The open question of whether non-separable regularisers could circumvent this limitation, and the extension of the quadratic-growth lemma (Lemma 4.7) beyond one dimension, are identified as key directions. The bandit result showing linear regret for all ℓ_p-balls (p≥1) in sufficiently high dimensions further underscores the difficulty of the bandit setting compared to full-information OCO.
