## Problem

Existing end-to-end autonomous driving methods rely on Imitation Learning, which suffers from causal confusion (learning correlations rather than true causal factors) and an open-loop-to-closed-loop gap (compounding errors in deployment that are never encountered during training). Closed-loop RL training could address both issues, but requires a photorealistic, interactive driving environment that previous game-engine simulators fail to provide for end-to-end sensor-level training.

## Input / Output

The policy consumes multi-view camera image sequences along with navigation commands and ego-vehicle kinematic state (position, heading, velocity). It outputs a decoupled pair of discrete actions over a 0.5-second horizon: a lateral displacement chosen from 61 uniformly spaced options in [-0.75 m, +0.75 m] and a longitudinal displacement from 61 options in [0, 15 m]. These discrete displacements are converted into linear velocity and steering angle via a kinematic bicycle model to update the ego pose in the 3DGS environment at each 0.1 s step.

## Method

RAD constructs a photorealistic closed-loop driving environment by extending StreetGaussian with mesh-constrained road geometry, separate sky modeling, and depth/normal-consistency supervision for foreground objects, enabling realistic sensor rendering from arbitrary ego poses. Training follows three stages: (1) perception pre-training with ground-truth map/agent supervision, (2) planning pre-training via focal-loss imitation on 2000 hours of expert demonstrations, and (3) reinforced post-training where PPO with decoupled lateral/longitudinal clipping alternates with IL regularization in a 4:1 ratio. The reward function combines four safety-critical penalties (dynamic collision, static collision, positional deviation, heading deviation) propagated via GAE, while four directional auxiliary objectives provide dense gradients that adaptively shift action probability mass toward safe behaviors. The architecture uses a BEV encoder, instance-level map and agent tokens, and an image encoder feeding a cascaded Transformer decoder that outputs both the action distributions and value functions for the decoupled action dimensions.

## Results

On the proposed 3DGS-based closed-loop evaluation benchmark (337 unseen scenes), RAD achieves a Collision Ratio (CR) of 0.089, which is approximately 3× lower than the best IL-based baseline VADv2 (0.270). RAD also leads on all other safety and consistency metrics: Dynamic Collision Ratio (DCR) 0.080, Static Collision Ratio (SCR) 0.009, Deviation Ratio (DR) 0.063, and Average Deviation Distance (ADD) 0.257. In terms of driving smoothness, RAD records the lowest Lateral Jerk (0.082) and competitive Longitudinal Jerk (4.495) among all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RAD outperforms all four IL-based baselines on every reported metric. Against TransFuser [27], RAD reduces CR from 0.320 to 0.089 (3.6× improvement) and cuts Lateral Jerk from 0.142 to 0.082. Compared to VAD [2], RAD achieves a 3.8× lower CR (0.089 vs 0.335) and a 6.8× lower Lateral Jerk (0.082 vs 0.550), indicating substantially smoother and safer driving. Versus GenAD [3], RAD lowers CR by 3.8× (0.089 vs 0.341) and Longitudinal Jerk by 2.5× (4.495 vs 11.37), showing GenAD's generative planning produces highly jerky motions. Against the strongest baseline VADv2 [6], RAD still achieves a 3.0× lower CR (0.089 vs 0.270), a 3.9× lower DCR (0.080 vs 0.240), and a 2.1× lower Lateral Jerk (0.082 vs 0.171), confirming that closed-loop RL training in 3DGS environments yields a general collision-avoidance capability that pure imitation learning cannot match.

## Relation to Prior Work

RAD extends the 3DGS-based dynamic scene reconstruction line (StreetGaussian, HUGS) from closed-loop *evaluation* into the RL *training* loop, making photorealistic digital twins a viable exploration environment for policy learning. It departs from the dominant IL paradigm in end-to-end AD (UniAD, VAD, VADv2, TransFuser, DiffusionDrive) by introducing closed-loop PPO with a decoupled lateral/longitudinal action space and GAE-based advantage propagation. The IL+RL co-optimization strategy builds on prior joint-training work (CADRE, CIRL, Lu et al.) but is the first to do so within a photorealistic 3DGS environment rather than non-photorealistic simulators like CARLA or structured BEV representations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that large-scale trial-and-error RL can be conducted in photorealistic 3DGS reconstructions of real driving scenes, opening a path toward data-driven closed-loop policy improvement without game-engine simulators. The 3× collision-rate reduction over strong IL baselines is compelling, but the evaluation rests on a self-constructed 337-clip 3DGS benchmark with no reported error bars, and the 8-second clip horizon with log-replayed agents limits generalization claims. Key open questions include whether the approach scales to longer horizons and truly novel (out-of-distribution) scenarios, and how 3DGS rendering fidelity for non-rigid agents, unobserved viewpoints, and low-light conditions will affect policy robustness at scale.
