## Problem

Existing Generalized Category Discovery methods dynamically optimize cluster prototypes or classification weights, creating an inconsistent optimization objective between labeled known categories and unlabeled novel categories. This inconsistency, combined with the lack of geometric constraints on feature distributions, leads to feature overlap and degraded novel-category accuracy. The paper asks whether pre-assigning a fixed optimal geometric structure can enable consistent, equally well-separated learning for all categories.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254369/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images processed by a pre-trained DINO ViT-B/16 backbone (768-dim embeddings) along with a small set of labeled known-category samples and a larger unlabeled set containing both known and novel categories. It requires the total number of categories K (either ground-truth or estimated via clustering) to construct the ETF prototype set. Output is a per-sample category assignment: known categories receive their true labels, while novel categories receive discovered cluster indices, with the feature space organized so that all K categories occupy vertices of a Simplex ETF.

## Method

NC-GCD pre-assigns a fixed Simplex Equiangular Tight Frame (ETF) prototype set before training, so that both known and novel categories share the same geometric alignment target, eliminating the inconsistent optimization objective of prior methods. Unsupervised ETF Alignment periodically clusters all unlabeled features (every T epochs), selects the top α% most confident samples per cluster, and pulls them toward their corresponding ETF prototype via a dot-regression loss; Supervised ETF Alignment similarly aligns labeled features to their assigned prototypes. A Consistent ETF Alignment Loss (weighted by γ) unifies both alignment objectives. To handle clustering instability and label-prototype mismatches, a Semantic Consistency Matcher (SCM) computes an optimal bijective permutation between consecutive clustering iterations and between predicted clusters and ground-truth labels, enforcing one-to-one correspondence that stabilizes pseudo-labels across iterations. The full training objective combines the ETF alignment loss with a contrastive representation learning loss (adapted from CMS) that encourages intra-sample similarity and inter-sample separation.

## Results

With the ground-truth number of categories K and a DINOv1 backbone, NC-GCD achieves 74.8% overall accuracy on CUB-200, 59.9% on Stanford Cars, 60.0% on FGVC Aircraft, 46.4% on Herbarium19, 82.7% on CIFAR-100, and 88.4% on ImageNet-100. Averaged across all six datasets, NC-GCD reaches 68.7% overall accuracy and 64.9% novel-category accuracy, surpassing the strongest prior method (SPT) by +3.0% and +4.1% respectively. Without GT K, NC-GCD still leads with 64.7% overall and 59.7% novel accuracy, outperforming CMS by +3.1% and +4.4%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NC-GCD outperforms all 12 baselines on every dataset and every metric (All, Old, New) under both GT-K and estimated-K settings. Against the closest competitor SelEx (ECCV'24), NC-GCD gains +1.2% on CUB (74.8 vs 73.6) and +4.3% on FGVC Aircraft (60.0 vs 57.1), with the largest novel-category margin of +10.0% on Herbarium19 (40.7 vs 30.7 implied from CMS's 26.4). Versus SPT (ICLR'24), the strongest prior method on generic datasets, NC-GCD improves ImageNet-100 overall accuracy by +3.0% (88.4 vs 85.4) and novel accuracy by +4.1% (85.5 vs 81.4). Against CMS (CVPR'24), NC-GCD leads by +6.6% on CUB (74.8 vs 68.2) and +10.0% on Herbarium19 (46.4 vs 36.4), confirming that the fixed-ETF geometric constraint is particularly effective for fine-grained categories with high intra-class variance. On FGVC Aircraft, NC-GCD achieves 61.2% novel accuracy, surpassing SPT (58.1%) by +3.1% and CMS (52.3%) by +8.9%, the largest gap among all baselines on that dataset. The consistent wins across both fine-grained and generic benchmarks, and under both known and estimated K, indicate that the advantage is not dataset-specific but stems from the unified supervised-unsupervised ETF alignment objective.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Generalized Category Discovery (GCD) line of work by replacing the dynamic prototype learning paradigm (used in GCD, DCCL, PromptCAL, SimGCD, CMS, SPT, and others) with pre-assigned fixed Equiangular Tight Frame (ETF) prototypes derived from Neural Collapse theory (Papyan et al., 2020). The closest prior work is TRAILER (CVPR 2024), which also employs a fixed NC-inspired classifier, but NC-GCD departs by unifying supervised and unsupervised ETF alignment under a single consistent objective and introducing the Semantic Consistency Matcher to resolve clustering-induced label instability. The representation learning component follows CMS (CVPR 2024), while the overall framework is positioned as a geometric-structure-first alternative to the contrastive-learning and GMM-based approaches that dominate the GCD literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The consistent optimization objective for known and novel categories addresses a fundamental asymmetry in prior GCD methods, where labeled data drives prototype learning while novel categories are optimized purely unsupervised, leading to feature overlap. The fixed ETF structure provides a theoretically grounded geometric guarantee (maximal inter-class separation, minimal intra-class variance) that is backbone-agnostic, as demonstrated across DINOv1, DINOv2, and CLIP. Key limitations include the reliance on accurate (or near-accurate) estimation of the total category count K, the periodic clustering overhead, and evaluation restricted to image classification benchmarks. The authors identify extension to incremental/continual GCD settings as a natural next step, and the robustness to ±20% K estimation error, while encouraging, leaves open how the method behaves under larger deviations or in truly open-world scenarios where K is unknown a priori.
