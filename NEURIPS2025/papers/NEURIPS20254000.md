## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting methods that fuse semantic features into the scene representation face an inherent contradiction: the anisotropic rasterization pipeline required for RGB rendering conflicts with the isotropic, viewpoint-independent distribution that semantic features demand for cross-view consistency. Existing approaches treat features as fully differentiable and co-optimize them with appearance, which disrupts the self-attention structure of pre-trained transformer features and introduces noise that degrades both segmentation quality and geometric reconstruction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254000/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view RGB images (downsampled to 1000 px max side) and frozen SAM image embeddings as non-differentiable semantic supervision, initialized from a COLMAP sparse point cloud via a spatial hash table that maps each 3D point to its 2D feature across visible views. Output is a set of 2D Gaussian primitives each carrying a frozen high-dimensional feature vector alongside standard geometric and appearance parameters, from which the system renders RGB images, depth maps, normal maps, extractable meshes, and a queryable 3D feature field at ≥60 FPS.

## Method

FHGS augments each 2D Gaussian primitive with a frozen, non-differentiable semantic feature vector extracted from SAM, so that gradients from the feature loss propagate exclusively through the rasterization weights w_i to optimize geometry (position, rotation, scale, opacity) without ever modifying the feature vectors themselves. A physics-inspired dual-drive mechanism governs optimization: an External Potential Field Constraint (L_gt) applies a sigmoid-activated cosine-similarity loss between each primitive's feature and the ground-truth feature along the viewing ray, while an Internal Clustering Driving term (L_cf) performs an O(N) cumulative front-to-back traversal that penalizes feature incoherence among primitives sharing a pixel, suppressing inter-cluster noise and tightening intra-cluster semantic coherence. The forward pass computes both losses directly from the sorted primitive list without rendering a feature image, and the backward pass reuses the same cumulative prefix sums, preserving the O(N) complexity of the original rasterizer. Built on a 2DGS backbone with custom CUDA kernels, the framework trains in ~5 minutes per scene—roughly 15× faster than Feature3DGS—while achieving superior cross-view feature consistency (FE metric) and comparable feature similarity (FL1).

## Results

On indoor scenes (DTU-24, DTU-37, MN360-kitchen), FHGS achieves FE scores of 0.15–0.23, substantially lower than Feature3DGS (0.52–0.88) and 2DGS (1.31–1.35), indicating far stronger cross-view feature consistency. FL1 feature loss is 0.18–0.22 for FHGS versus 0.24–0.31 for Feature3DGS, while training time drops from 73–113 minutes (Feature3DGS) to 5.1–5.7 minutes (FHGS), a ~15× speedup. On outdoor scenes, FHGS maintains FE of 0.21–0.25 and FL1 of 0.18–0.41, and is the only method that successfully completes TnT-Caterpillar (Feature3DGS fails due to GPU memory exhaustion). PSNR is competitive in indoor settings (30.8–30.9 dB) but slightly lower than Feature3DGS in outdoor scenes (26.5–30.6 vs. 28.2–31.6 dB), attributed to filtering of low-semantic regions like soil and shadows.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against 2DGS (the base rendering framework), FHGS improves FE by 83–88% (e.g., 0.15 vs. 1.35 on DTU-24) and FL1 by 64–73%, while training time is comparable or slightly faster (5.2m vs. 6.1m). Against Feature3DGS (the primary feature-fusion baseline), FHGS achieves substantially lower FE (0.15–0.23 vs. 0.52–0.88 indoor; 0.21–0.25 vs. 0.55–0.65 outdoor) and lower FL1 (0.18–0.22 vs. 0.24–0.31 indoor), while reducing training time by approximately 15× (5–8 min vs. 73–181 min). However, FHGS trails Feature3DGS on PSNR in both indoor (30.8–30.9 vs. 31.1–31.7 dB) and outdoor (26.5–30.6 vs. 28.2–31.6 dB) settings, a gap the authors attribute to aggressive filtering of low-semantic regions. Feature3DGS also fails entirely on TnT-Caterpillar due to GPU memory exhaustion, whereas FHGS completes it in 5.2 minutes. In Table 3 (DTU geometric metrics), FHGS achieves the best Chamfer distance (0.75 vs. 0.83 for 2DGS, 1.85 for Feature3DGS) with the fewest primitives (196k) and smallest storage (183 MB vs. 745 MB for Feature3DGS).

## Relation to Prior Work

FHGS builds directly on the 2D Gaussian Splatting framework for geometric reconstruction and departs from the dominant paradigm in GS-based feature fusion (exemplified by Feature3DGS and LangSplat) where per-primitive feature embeddings are learned end-to-end alongside appearance. Instead, it freezes high-dimensional pre-trained features (SAM, CLIP) as non-differentiable attributes on each primitive and routes all optimization through the rasterization weights, resolving what the authors frame as a fundamental anisotropy-isotropy conflict between Gaussian rendering and semantic representation. The physics-inspired dual-drive loss (external potential field + internal cumulative clustering) is a novel optimization strategy that replaces the auxiliary networks or contrastive losses used by prior external-fusion methods (SAGA, Semantic Gaussians, OmniSeg3D) with a lightweight, self-contained mechanism operating entirely within the rasterization loop.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 15× training-time reduction over Feature3DGS while matching or exceeding feature-fusion quality makes semantic-aware Gaussian Splatting practically viable for real-time applications such as VLN and interactive 3D editing, where the baseline's 80–180 minute training is prohibitive. The conceptual shift—treating semantic features as fixed constraints rather than learnable parameters—offers a cleaner separation between geometry optimization and semantic consistency that could generalize to other frozen-feature settings. However, the method remains sensitive to manually tuned sigmoid parameters (λ, k), incurs significant GPU memory in large scenes due to hash-table and cumulative-weight buffers, and the evaluation relies on a single primary baseline and a self-proposed FE metric, leaving open questions about robustness across diverse feature backbones and truly large-scale outdoor environments.
