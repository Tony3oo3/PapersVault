## Problem

GNN training on large-scale graphs is dominated by non-computation overheads (mini-batch construction, host-device data transfer) rather than GPU computation itself, and existing sampling-based methods form a Pareto frontier where reducing these overheads inevitably degrades accuracy. No prior approach simultaneously achieves high accuracy and substantially lower training time without resorting to aggressive neighborhood pruning or complex variance-reduction schemes.

## Input / Output

The method takes a graph G=(V,E,A,X) with adjacency matrix A∈R^{N×N} and node feature matrix X∈R^{N×d}, and produces full N-dimensional node embeddings H^{(L)}∈R^{N×d_out} for downstream node classification (cross-entropy) or link prediction (cosine-margin loss). Crucially, all intermediate GNN layers operate on a compressed M×d representation (M≪N) rather than the full N×d matrix, and the N-dimensional output is recovered only at the final layer via a compressed-sensing optimization, so the model never materializes full-size embeddings during forward or backward passes.

## Method

YOSO performs a single learnable orthonormal spectral projection U_ℓ^⊤X at the input (avoiding O(N³) eigendecomposition by learning U_ℓ via backpropagation on the Stiefel manifold), then applies a fixed sensing matrix Φ=S_struct⊗Σ_rand to compress the spectral coefficients to M≪N rows. All L GNN layers then execute message passing entirely in this M-dimensional compressed space using a propagation operator A_Φ=Φ Ã_norm Φ^⊤ that injects original graph structure. At the output layer, full N-dimensional embeddings are reconstructed by solving a bounded ℓ_{2,1}-regularized CS recovery problem (via FISTA), and the total loss jointly optimizes task accuracy and reconstruction fidelity. The theoretical guarantee shows stable recovery when M=O(k log(N/k)) under the Restricted Isometry Property, and empirically YOSO achieves ~74% average training time reduction while matching SOTA accuracy across five benchmark datasets.

## Results

On node classification, YOSO achieves 0.720 accuracy on ogbn-arxiv (tied with GraphSage), 0.967 Micro-F1 on Reddit (tied with GraphSAINT), and 0.787 accuracy on ogbn-products (within 0.005 of the best, GraphSAINT-EG at 0.792). On link prediction, YOSO reaches 0.223 Hits@100 on ogbl-ppa and 0.802 MRR on ogbl-citation2, within 0.003 of the best baselines. Across all five datasets, YOSO achieves an average 74% reduction in total training time (node classification) and 72% (link prediction) compared to all baselines, while reducing non-computation time by approximately 95.7% on average.

## Baselines & Comparisons

YOSO matches or nearly matches the best accuracy across all five datasets while achieving the shortest training time in every case. On ogbn-arxiv node classification, YOSO ties GraphSage at 0.720 accuracy; on Reddit it ties GraphSAINT at 0.967 Micro-F1; on ogbn-products it trails GraphSAINT-EG by only 0.005 (0.787 vs. 0.792). For link prediction, YOSO is within 0.003 of the best on both ogbl-ppa (0.223 vs. GraphSAINT-RW's 0.226) and ogbl-citation2 (0.802 vs. 0.805). The efficiency gains are substantial: YOSO reduces total training time by an average of 74% (node classification) and 72% (link prediction), with non-computation time dropping by ~95.7%, while sampling-based baselines like FastGCN and LADIES sacrifice 5–30% accuracy for speed. Against graph condensation methods (GCond, GC-SNTK), YOSO achieves 0.7169 accuracy vs. 0.6172/0.6219 with 6–12× less preprocessing time; against linearization (SIGN, iSVD), YOSO delivers higher accuracy at comparable or lower training cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

YOSO sits at the intersection of three research threads: Graph Fourier analysis (Sandryhaila & Moura), compressed sensing recovery (Candes & Tao), and GCN-based message passing (Kipf & Welling). It departs from the dominant GNN acceleration paradigm—sampling-based mini-batch construction (GraphSage, FastGCN, LADIES, Cluster-GCN, GraphSAINT)—by eliminating neighborhood sampling entirely and instead operating in a compressed M-dimensional spectral subspace. Unlike prior spectral GNNs that require O(N³) eigendecomposition of the graph Laplacian, YOSO replaces the fixed basis with a learnable orthonormal matrix U_ℓ optimized jointly with GNN weights, and unlike layer-wise spectral approaches, it performs the spectral projection only once at input and the CS-based reconstruction only once at output.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that non-computation time (host-side sampling, data transfer) rather than GPU computation dominates GNN training time is well-supported by the paper's breakdown analysis and reframes where optimization effort should be directed. The 74% average training time reduction with competitive accuracy is practically significant for large-scale graph learning. However, the method is validated only on two-layer GCN across five datasets, the RIP proof contains acknowledged scaling gaps, and no error bars are reported, leaving open whether the approach generalizes to deeper architectures, heterogeneous graphs, or settings where spectral sparsity is weaker. A key open question is how the learnable basis U_ℓ behaves as graph size grows to billions of nodes, where even the O(N²) storage of U_ℓ becomes prohibitive.
