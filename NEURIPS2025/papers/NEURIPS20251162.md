## Problem

Vision-language models frequently produce spatial hallucinations—incorrect descriptions of object positions—because training data contains very little explicit spatial language (only 1.7%–7.3% of captions in common datasets include spatial cues). This modality asymmetry, where images encode rich spatial structure but paired text is spatially sparse, limits the model's ability to ground spatial relations during training.

## Input / Output

SiTe consumes pairs of existing image-caption samples from standard datasets (e.g., 558K, Flickr30K) and produces stitched composite images (two images concatenated horizontally or vertically) paired with structured spatial captions or binary Yes/No spatial QA pairs. The stitched images preserve original resolution of each half on a shared canvas, and the generated text uses 35 horizontal and 29 vertical caption templates plus 20 QA templates per stitching direction. In the fine-tuning stage, noun entities are extracted from captions via Qwen2.5-72B-Instruct to instantiate the QA templates, requiring no manual annotation.

## Method

SiTe operates in two steps: (1) IMAGE-SPATIAL STITCHING combines two images along a chosen axis onto a shared canvas, with two pairing strategies—SiTe_rand (random pairing) and SiTe_ratio (aspect-ratio-bucketed pairing for geometric balance); (2) TEXT-SPATIAL TELLING generates spatially-aware supervision by inserting original captions into spatial templates (e.g., 'The left part shows T₁, while the right part displays T₂') and by extracting noun entities to form binary spatial QA pairs whose answers are determined automatically by the known layout. The method is annotation-free and plug-and-play: structured captions are injected during pretraining (default 1:3 stitched-to-raw ratio), QA pairs are added during instruction tuning, and spatial-expression swaps (e.g., 'left'↔'right') yield contrastive negatives for hallucination-mitigation training as in HALVA. No architectural changes or generative models are required, and the reduced sample count from stitching actually cuts pretraining time by ~23%.

## Results

On LLaVA-v1.5-7B pretraining (558K), SiTe-rand improves MME_Position by +5.50% (127.83→133.33) and Spatial-MM by +1.76% (42.02→43.78), while SiTe-ratio further lifts COCO-QA_Spat to 70.06% (+2.34%). In the supervised fine-tuning stage, adding 5K spatial QA samples (SiTe-ratio) boosts MME_Position to 141.00 (+13.17%) and Spatial-MM to 48.58% (+6.56%) over the LLaVA baseline. For the smaller Qwen2-1.5B model, SiTe-rand during pretraining gains +5.01% on MMBench (61.67→66.68) and +2.25% on MME_Position, while 5K SiTe-ratio fine-tuning achieves +5.33% on MMBench and +4.38% on MM-Vet_Spat. General vision-language benchmarks (COCO-QA, VQA-v2, MMBench, MM-Vet) are largely maintained or slightly improved, confirming that spatial supervision does not degrade general capabilities.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the VSR spatial-supervision baseline (Liu et al., TACL 2023), SiTe-rand on LLaVA-v1.5-7B wins on COCO-QA_Spat (68.75 vs. 67.85), Spatial-MM (43.78 vs. 42.65), and MME_Position (133.33 vs. 121.67, a +11.66 gap), while slightly trailing on MM-Vet_Spat (28.43 vs. 29.42). SiTe-ratio extends this lead to all four spatial metrics (70.06 / 44.15 / 132.80 / 28.68), confirming that structured stitching outperforms simply mixing in a dedicated spatial dataset. Against HALVA (Sarkar et al., 2024), which performs phrase-level contrastive alignment for hallucination mitigation, SiTe-augmented HALVA improves COCO-QA_Spat (+1.61), Spatial-MM (+1.08), and MM-Vet_Spat (+0.40) but drops MME_Position by 11.67 points (135.00→123.33), suggesting that swapping only spatial expressions is less effective than HALVA's full object/relation/attribute perturbation on that particular benchmark. Compared to traditional augmentations (Rotate, Crop), SiTe consistently outperforms on spatial benchmarks: Rotate helps marginally on 558K but degrades on Flickr30K (COCO-QA_Spat drops 0.93%), while Crop is unstable due to potential removal of key objects. Overall, SiTe achieves the best spatial gains without sacrificing general vision-language performance, whereas VSR and traditional augmentations either underperform or introduce misalignment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves out a distinct niche within multimodal data augmentation by targeting spatial structure specifically, departing from prior augmentation methods (MixGen, StableLLaVA, ALIA) that either assume semantic compatibility across samples or rely on expensive generative models for caption rewriting. It also contrasts with dedicated spatial supervision datasets like VSR, showing that a simple stitching-and-template approach can match or exceed their effect without requiring curated spatial annotations. Conceptually, SiTe repurposes the well-known image concatenation operation as a spatial grounding signal, paired with template-based text generation, rather than treating spatial understanding as requiring new architectural components (e.g., coordinate embeddings) or additional modalities (depth, 3D).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SiTe offers a practically important, annotation-free recipe for injecting spatial supervision into VLM training pipelines at negligible computational cost, making it broadly adoptable across model scales and training stages. The key open question is generalization beyond binary left-right/top-bottom layouts to richer spatial topologies (diagonal, 3D, relative distance), which the authors acknowledge as a limitation. For embodied AI and robotics applications where spatial reasoning is critical, this lightweight augmentation could serve as a default pretraining step, though its ceiling on complex spatial reasoning remains to be tested.
