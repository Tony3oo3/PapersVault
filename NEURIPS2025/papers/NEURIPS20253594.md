## Problem

Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) achieve strong performance through extended chain-of-thought sequences, but the resulting long trajectories impose prohibitive inference costs, especially in resource-constrained settings. Existing long-to-short methods either assume all extra reasoning is 'overthinking' and compress aggressively (causing performance drops) or rely on simple prompting heuristics that do not systematically identify which steps are truly essential. The paper tackles the problem of automatically discovering a compact, high-information-density reasoning path from an LRM's full trajectory without degrading accuracy.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a question q, the full CoT trajectory t = {t^(1), ..., t^(N)} (segmented by double newlines into individual thinking steps), and the ground-truth solution s. It outputs a compressed trajectory t' ⊆ t — a subset of the original steps (each represented as a 3-step span ⟨t^(n-1), t^(n), t^(n+1)⟩ to preserve local context) — that is then used as training data for supervised fine-tuning of the LRM. The compressed trajectories are constrained by a token budget (512–4096 tokens) and a maximum search depth k_max, and the verification model s1.1-32B is used both during search and as the fine-tuning target.

## Method

A*-Thought formulates CoT compression as a best-first search over a tree of reasoning spans, guided by two complementary signals. At the step level, a Bidirectional Importance Score (BIS) combines attention weights and negative log-likelihood (computed with GPT-2) from both the question and the solution to rank individual thinking steps, providing a principled initialization order for the search. At the path level, an A* search algorithm expands the tree using a cost function f = g + h, where g(·) measures current path quality via the verification model's log-probability of the partial trajectory, and h(·) estimates future cost as the conditional self-information of the solution given the current path. A verification model checks whether the current path already yields the correct solution (with a minimum depth k_min to avoid unreliable short-path verification), and the search terminates when verification succeeds or the maximum depth k_max is reached. The resulting compressed trajectories are used to fine-tune the LRM, achieving up to 2.39× accuracy improvement at 512-token budgets and ~33% length reduction at 4096 tokens with minimal accuracy loss.

## Results

On QwQ-32B with a 512-token budget, A*-Thought achieves 29.4% average accuracy across MATH500, AMC23, OlympiadBench, and GSM8K, a 2.39× improvement over the fine-tuned baseline (12.3%) and 2.29× over the strongest prompt-based baseline (BtC Effective Shortcut, 13.4%). The ACU (Accuracy per Computation Unit) score rises from 2.41 to 5.99 in this low-budget setting. At the 4096-token budget, A*-Thought reduces average response length by 33.6% (from 2826 to 1877 tokens) while maintaining 69.3% average accuracy (vs. 70.6% for the fine-tuned baseline), and achieves the highest ACU of 3.69. The method generalizes across backbones (R1-Distill-32B, s1.1-32B) and out-of-domain tasks (LiveCodeBench, MMLU).

## Baselines & Comparisons

A*-Thought decisively outperforms all three baselines across every budget setting. Against Chain-of-Draft (CoD), which constrains each thinking step to ≤5 words via prompting, A*-Thought achieves 29.4% vs. 12.2% average accuracy at 512 tokens (2.4×) and matches CoD's 69.3% accuracy at 4096 tokens while using 33% fewer tokens (1877 vs. 2804). Against Break-the-Chain (BtC), the strongest prompt-based shortcut method, A*-Thought leads by 16.0 points at 512 tokens (29.4% vs. 13.4%) and by 0.7 points at 4096 tokens (69.3% vs. 68.6%) with substantially shorter outputs. Against TokenSkip, the training-based compression baseline that uses prompt compression to shorten CoT data before fine-tuning, A*-Thought achieves 29.4% vs. 10.9% at 512 tokens (2.7×) and 69.3% vs. 64.9% at 4096 tokens, while also compressing training data to 31.3% of the original size (vs. TokenSkip's 85.4%), reducing training time by ~24%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the importance-estimation mechanism from LongLLMLingua (Jiang et al., 2024), which uses unidirectional ATTN/NLL scores to rank tokens by question relevance, into a bidirectional formulation that also incorporates solution relevance. Methodologically, it departs from prior CoT compression approaches—TokenSkip (token-level filtering), Chain-of-Draft (prompt-based brevity), and Break-the-Chain (shortcut prompting)—by reframing compression as a combinatorial search problem over reasoning spans, solved via A* with custom cost functions (current path quality via a verification model and future cost via conditional self-information of the solution). The use of a small proxy model (GPT-2) for importance scoring and a separate verification model (s1.1-32B) for path evaluation distinguishes it from end-to-end training-based compression methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Efficient inference for LRMs is a critical bottleneck for on-device and resource-constrained deployment, and this paper demonstrates that a search-based compression framework can simultaneously improve accuracy (up to 2.39× at 512 tokens) and reduce output length (~34% at 4096 tokens) across multiple backbone models. Key limitations include reliance on a strong verification model, validation primarily on mathematical reasoning tasks, and confinement to supervised fine-tuning (the authors note RL extension as future work). Open questions include whether the A* search formulation generalizes to open-ended generation tasks, whether the GPT-2-based importance scoring can be replaced by lighter or learned alternatives, and how the method interacts with reinforcement learning training paradigms that are becoming standard for LRMs.
