## Problem

Standard continuous RL algorithms rely on deterministic or unimodal Gaussian actors that cannot represent complex multimodal decision distributions, making them brittle in diversity-critical scenarios like multi-goal navigation or generative tasks. Existing multimodal RL methods based on diffusion or amortized actors are intractable—lacking closed-form decision probabilities—which prevents direct application of policy gradient and entropy-based diversity regularization. The paper addresses how to train such intractable multimodal actors while simultaneously optimizing expected return and decision diversity.

## Input / Output

The method takes a continuous environment state s and produces a continuous action vector a. The stochasticity is introduced via a latent variable z drawn from a fixed distribution p_z: for amortized actors, z is concatenated with s and fed through a single MLP; for diffusion actors, z is a sequence of T i.i.d. Gaussian noise vectors consumed iteratively in a denoising chain. The resulting action distribution π_θ(·|s) is multimodal but has no closed-form density, which is precisely what makes standard RL objectives inapplicable without the reparameterization-based approach proposed here.

## Method

DrAC unifies amortized and diffusion actors under a stochastic-mapping formulation π_θ = {f_θ, p_z} where a = f_θ(s, z), z ~ p_z, and shows that the policy gradient for any such actor can be estimated via the reparameterization trick as ∇_a Q(s, f_θ(s,z)) · ∇_θ f_θ(s,z), bypassing the need for decision probabilities entirely. For diversity, the paper replaces entropy with a distance-based regularization D^π(s) = E[log δ(x,y)] over pairs of actions sampled from the policy, using the geometric mean (via log) of pairwise L2 distances to avoid overestimating diversity from sparse clusters. The actor loss is L_θ = -Q(s, f_θ(s,z)) - αD̃_θ(s), while the critic bootstrap target incorporates the diversity term, and α is auto-tuned via a dual-optimization loss that matches achieved diversity to a target value D̂ = log(β√|A|). This yields a practical off-policy actor-critic algorithm compatible with any stochastic-mapping actor, demonstrated with both amortized (DrAmort) and diffusion (DrDiffus) instantiations.

## Results

In multi-goal PointMaze, DrAmort reaches the most goals across all three maze difficulties and shows the best few-shot robustness against both goal removal and obstacle insertion, while DrDiffus and DACER learn fast and stably but with less diversity. In game level generation, DrAmort achieves the best episodic return (quality) for both MarioPuzzle and MultiFacet styles and generally dominates the quality-diversity Pareto front. On MuJoCo-v4, DrAmort achieves the best performance in 3 of 6 locomotion tasks and surpasses SQL in 5 of 6. Computationally, DrAmort trains at 21.0 s per 1000 steps (faster than SAC's 22.7 s) with 392 MB peak GPU memory, while diffusion-based methods (DrDiffus, DACER) are 3–4× slower and S²AC is ~10× slower with 3.5 GB memory.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SQL (amortized actor + SVGD), DrAmort consistently outperforms on diversity, goal coverage, and OOD robustness in multi-goal PointMaze, and achieves higher episodic return in game level generation; SQL occasionally shows marginally better diversity under MultiFacet style but at the cost of poor return. Against DACER (diffusion actor + noise-scaling diversity), DrDiffus is comparable in MuJoCo but DrAmort/DrDiffus clearly outperform in multi-goal PointMaze, where DACER fails to learn distinct multimodal behaviors due to its noise-scaling approach. Against S²AC (energy-based + SVGD sampling), DrAC variants are dramatically faster (21–85 s vs. 220 s per 1000 steps) and use far less memory (392–608 MB vs. 3.5 GB), while S²AC performs worst on all tasks. Against SAC (unimodal Gaussian baseline), DrAmort is competitive in MuJoCo (best in 3/6 tasks) but clearly superior in multi-goal and generative settings where multimodality is critical; SAC struggles to reach multiple goals and generalizes poorly to OOD perturbations despite high temperature settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two families of intractable multimodal actors—amortized actors (from SQL) and diffusion actors (from DACER)—under a single 'stochastic-mapping actor' formulation π_θ = {f_θ, p_z}, and shows that the reparameterization trick provides a general policy gradient estimator for all such actors, extending what DACER demonstrated only for diffusion actors. It departs from the maximum-entropy RL lineage (SAC, SQL, S²AC) by replacing entropy regularization—which requires analytical decision probabilities and does not specifically encourage multimodality—with a distance-based geometric-mean diversity regularization that operates purely on sampled actions. The actor-critic training machinery (double critics, target smoothing, automatic temperature) is inherited from SAC, and the general policy regularization framework from Wang et al. (2024) provides the theoretical scaffolding for plugging in the new diversity term.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical finding that a simple amortized actor (state concatenated with a random latent vector) achieves superior multimodal expressivity and faster training than diffusion actors challenges the prevailing assumption that more complex generative models are needed for expressive policies, and positions the amortized actor as a promising, efficient policy class for diversity-critical applications. The demonstrated few-shot robustness gains in out-of-distribution scenarios (obstacle insertion, goal removal) provide concrete evidence that decision diversity is not merely an aesthetic property but a functional robustness mechanism. Open questions include whether the advantage of amortized actors persists with more diffusion steps or larger networks, how to schedule the diversity temperature over training, and whether the geometric-mean diversity metric generalizes to high-dimensional or structured action spaces.
