## Problem

Existing generative models for proteins either predict single static conformations or sample ensembles at the backbone/coarse-grained level, failing to capture the full all-atom conformational diversity—including side-chain rearrangements—that governs function in dynamic targets like GPCRs. No prior framework has demonstrated the ability to learn and generate system-specific, complete all-atom conformational ensembles directly from MD simulation data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250848/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes Kabsch-aligned MD trajectory frames (12,241 snapshots of the 2191-heavy-atom D2R in a POPC membrane) represented as k-NN graphs (k=4) with 3D coordinates as node features, plus a fixed reference structure (the first aligned frame) whose frozen ChebNet embedding conditions all generation. Output is a set of independently sampled all-atom Cartesian coordinate sets (backbone + side chains) expressed as deformations from the reference, with no temporal correlations between generated frames. The diffusion model operates on a compact pooled latent (d_p ≈ 60–100 for blind/sequential; ~1.1k effective dimensions for residue-based), not on the full per-atom embedding space.

## Method

LD-FPG factors generation into two coupled components: a ChebNet encoder (4 layers, Chebyshev order K=4) that maps each aligned frame's k-NN graph to per-atom latent embeddings Z^(t) ∈ R^{N×d_z}, and a DDPM trained on a compact pooled latent h_0 derived from Z^(t) via one of three strategies—blind (global 2D adaptive average pooling), sequential (backbone-then-sidechain two-stage decoding), or residue-based (per-residue local pooling into N_res context vectors). A decoder, conditioned on the frozen reference latent C = Z_ref rather than raw coordinates, maps (h_0, C) back to full Cartesian coordinates, with training losses on coordinate MSE and optional dihedral-angle terms. The key architectural insight is that generation is framed as learning a score function over a low-dimensional deformation space relative to a fixed chemical frame, guaranteeing all samples remain anchored to the same topology while the diffusion model explores conformational diversity. Training proceeds in three phases: encoder pretraining on coordinate reconstruction, decoder training on ground-truth latents, and DDPM training on the pooled latent distribution.

## Results

On the D2R-MD benchmark, LD-FPG achieves a backbone dihedral JSD of 0.007, improving over all baselines by 3–5×, while matching MD-level dynamic flexibility (Cα RMSF 1.22 nm vs. MD reference 1.34 nm). Its all-atom lDDT is ~0.80 and TM-score ~0.96, with side-chain χ-angle JSD of ~0.022 and all-atom RMSF of 1.36 nm (vs. MD 1.60 nm). Among the three internal pooling strategies, sequential pooling delivers the best backbone geometry (JSD_bb 0.0029) and balanced global scores (lDDT_All 0.712), while residue pooling achieves the best side-chain distributional fidelity (JSD_sc 0.0224) and lowest clash count (1145.6).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LD-FPG decisively outperforms all three baselines on local geometric accuracy: its backbone dihedral JSD of 0.007 is roughly 3× better than BioEmu (~0.022) and AlphaFlow (~0.023), and nearly 5× better than Boltz-2 (~0.034). On dynamic flexibility, LD-FPG (RMSF 1.22 nm) closely matches the MD reference (1.34 nm), whereas BioEmu (0.09 nm) and Boltz-2 (0.07 nm) produce nearly static structures with lDDT ≈ 1.0, and AlphaFlow (0.84 nm) still under-represents the true conformational spread. The lower lDDT of LD-FPG (~0.80 vs. 0.997–0.999 for BioEmu/Boltz-2) is by design: as an ensemble generator, its score against a single reference frame naturally decreases with realistic conformational diversity. Unlike all backbone-centric baselines, LD-FPG additionally generates full side-chain detail, enabling side-chain validation (χ-angle JSD ~0.022, all-atom RMSF 1.36 nm) that none of the compared methods provide.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of latent diffusion generative modeling (DDPM) and graph-based molecular encoding (ChebNet), extending the MD-informed ensemble generation line (EnsembleVAE, AlphaFlow, BioEmu, DiG) from backbone or coarse-grained representations to complete all-atom conformational ensembles. It departs from prior latent-space protein models (EnsembleVAE, idpSAM) that produce Cα ensembles requiring a separate all-atom refinement step, by jointly learning a compact latent and decoding directly to all heavy-atom coordinates. The systematic comparison of three decoder pooling strategies (blind, sequential, residue-based) as a design axis for balancing global fold fidelity against local side-chain detail is a new contribution not explored in prior ensemble generators.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GPCRs are targeted by roughly 50% of marketed drugs, and capturing their system-specific, all-atom conformational landscapes is essential for understanding biased agonism and designing allosteric modulators—yet current methods either produce static structures or backbone-only ensembles. LD-FPG demonstrates that a single-protein, MD-trained latent diffusion model can match MD-level dynamic flexibility (RMSF 1.22 vs. 1.34 nm) while achieving 3–5× better local dihedral geometry than baselines, offering a practical route to ensemble-based drug design on dynamic targets. Key open questions include generalization beyond a single GPCR in one membrane environment, the need for multi-epoch sampling in residue pooling, and whether the framework scales to larger or multi-state systems without per-protein retraining.
