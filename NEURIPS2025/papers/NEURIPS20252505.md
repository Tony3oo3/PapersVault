## Problem

Despite the practical success of contrastive vision-language models like CLIP, no prior work provides a theoretical analysis of how jointly training two nonlinear encoders with contrastive loss behaves under realistic data misalignment (spurious image-text correlations and missing features). In particular, the mechanism by which text recaptioning provably improves zero-shot generalization remained unexplained, and existing training dynamics results were limited to single encoders or linear networks.

## Input / Output

The theoretical model generates image-text pairs via sparse coding: each image x_p = Mz_{x_p} + ξ and text y_p = Hz_{y_p} + ξ, where z vectors are sparse (Bernoulli(C_z/d) active coordinates) and noise can dominate signal energy. For downstream evaluation, out-of-domain test images and K text prompts are used for zero-shot classification, with the prediction being argmax_k ⟨f(x), g(y_k)⟩. In practical experiments, CLIP's 12-layer transformer encoders produce 768-dim features projected into a shared 512-dim embedding space, evaluated on standard benchmarks (Food-101, CIFAR-10/100, Caltech-101, Pets, STL-10, ImageNet).

## Method

The paper analyzes two one-hidden-layer ReLU networks (image encoder f and text encoder h) trained jointly via SGD on a spectral contrastive loss, proving convergence to near-optimal loss in T = Θ(d² log d) steps. The core four-stage pipeline is: (S1) contrastive pre-training on raw data, (S2) fine-tuning an image-grounded text decoder G on high-quality pairs to generate synthetic captions, (S3) filtering by replacing noisy captions whose image-text cosine similarity is lower than the synthetic caption's, and (S4) re-training on the filtered dataset. The key theoretical insight is 'feature purity': on raw misaligned data, each neuron pair inevitably entangles a true feature M_j with its spuriously correlated counterpart M_{j'}, yielding constant zero-shot error; after recaptioning, spurious correlation drops from constant C_s to Θ(1/d), allowing neurons to learn purified single-feature representations and achieving 1−o(1) zero-shot accuracy. The analysis is validated empirically by comparing CLIP vs. LaCLIP across multiple datasets and by controlled neuron-pruning and caption-shuffling experiments on a CC3M-pretrained CLIP model.

## Results

On the simulated experiments, ITCP trained on filtered (recaptioned) data learns purified representations for nearly all d=50 features even at high spurious correlation (C_s=0.3), while raw-data training degrades sharply. On practical benchmarks, LaCLIP (with LLM-rewritten captions) consistently outperforms CLIP across all six zero-shot classification datasets: e.g., on CC12M, Food-101 accuracy improves from 50.8% to 60.7% (+9.9 pp) and CIFAR-10 from 64.9% to 75.1% (+10.2 pp). On ImageNet (CC12M), Top-1 accuracy rises from 35.04% to 42.62% (+7.58 pp). The theoretical analysis (Theorem 4.5) proves that raw-data training yields a constant classification error while recaptioned-data training achieves 1−o(1) accuracy on out-of-domain zero-shot classification.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares CLIP (Radford et al., 2021) against LaCLIP (Fan et al., 2023), which shares the same architecture and pre-training data but replaces a portion of raw web captions with LLM-generated rewrites. LaCLIP wins on every dataset and every metric: on CC12M, the gains are largest (e.g., +9.9 pp on Food-101, +10.2 pp on CIFAR-10, +7.58 pp Top-1 on ImageNet), while on the larger LAION-400M corpus the absolute gains shrink (e.g., +1.0 pp on Food-101, +0.5 pp on CIFAR-10, +3.93 pp Top-1 on ImageNet) but remain consistent. Silhouette Scores also improve uniformly, confirming that recaptioning yields more orthogonal, purified feature embeddings. The theoretical contribution goes beyond these empirical comparisons by proving *why* the gap exists: Theorem 4.2 shows raw-data training entangles true and spurious features in every neuron pair, while Theorem 4.4 shows recaptioned-data training recovers purified single-feature neurons, directly explaining the downstream accuracy gap in Theorem 4.5.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the training dynamics analysis of contrastive learning from single-encoder or linear-network settings (Wen & Li 2021; Chen et al. 2024; Nakada et al. 2023) to the joint training of two nonlinear ReLU encoders under a spectral contrastive loss, while introducing a data misalignment model with spurious and missing features. It builds on the sparse coding data model from Allen-Zhu & Li (2022) and the multimodal spectral loss framework of HaoChen et al. (2021) and Zhang et al. (2023a), but departs from all prior theory by incorporating modality misalignment and providing the first theoretical justification for why text recaptioning (as practiced in LaCLIP and BLIP) provably improves zero-shot generalization. The paper also extends the zero-shot analysis of Chen et al. (2024) from aligned, linear settings to misaligned, nonlinear ones.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies 'feature purity'—the degree to which each neuron aligns to a single semantic direction—as the central mechanism governing VLM generalization, and proves that without recaptioning, spurious correlations cause neurons to entangle true and irrelevant features, leading to a provable constant classification error. This provides the first rigorous explanation for a widely adopted but previously unexplained practical technique. Key limitations include the restriction to one-hidden-layer ReLU networks (not transformers), a simplified linear text decoder G, and a single spurious feature pair per feature; extending the analysis to deep architectures, multiple spurious correlations, and tasks like retrieval or VQA remains open.
