## Problem

Existing volumetric video compression methods either require separate models for each bitrate (incurring high storage costs and limiting adaptability) or suffer from high decoding latency that precludes real-time playback on mobile devices. No prior approach simultaneously supports multiple quality levels within a single bitstream, handles large-motion sequences without error accumulation, and achieves real-time decoding and rendering on lightweight hardware.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253376/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes multi-view video sequences (e.g., 81 synchronized Z-CAM cinema cameras at 3840×2160, downsampled by 2× for training) and produces a single progressive H.264 bitstream containing hierarchical 4D Gaussian representations across L=6 quality layers. Each layer's Gaussian attributes are quantized (uint16/uint32 for positions, uint8 for others), flattened into 2D single-channel maps preserving spatial continuity, and encoded with x264 (I/P-frames only, YUV4:4:4, qp=10 or 20). Clients selectively decode subsets of layers (L1–L6) via hardware video codecs and shaders, enabling adaptive quality from ~0.19 MB to ~1.31 MB per sequence.

## Method

4DGCPro constructs a perceptually-weighted hierarchical keyframe representation by sorting 3D Gaussians (initialized via NeuS2 mesh extraction) in descending order of a significance metric Ψ = α + λ_Ψ·S (opacity plus weighted spatial volume), partitioning them into L=6 progressive detail layers. Inter-frame motion is decomposed into rigid transformations (translation and rotation predicted by lightweight MLPs conditioned on multi-resolution hash grid features) followed by residual deformations (scaling, opacity, and spherical-harmonic color residuals), with group boundaries adaptively triggered when average Gaussian translation exceeds a threshold τ_μ to prevent error accumulation. Training employs layer-wise rate-distortion supervision: keyframes use FFT-accelerated KDE for attribute entropy estimation, inter-frame residuals exploit their validated Gaussian distribution for simplified entropy modeling, and a temporal L1 regularization enforces inter-frame consistency. After training, the multi-layer representation is differentially quantized, flattened into 2D single-channel image sequences, and compressed with a standard H.264 encoder, yielding a single progressive bitstream decodable at any quality level in real time.

## Results

On the 4DGCPro dataset, the high-quality setting achieves 29.47 dB PSNR and 0.963 SSIM at 1.31 MB, while the low-quality setting retains 27.69 dB at only 0.33 MB — all from a single model. On HiFi4G, the method reaches 36.38 dB (High) down to 34.62 dB (Low) at 0.19 MB, and on N3DV it achieves 31.64 dB (High) at 0.64 MB. BD-PSNR gains over ReRF are 4.20 dB (4DGCPro), 7.87 dB (HiFi4G), and 2.07 dB (N3DV), with decoding as fast as 12 ms and rendering at 2.2 ms per frame, enabling real-time playback on mobile devices.

## Baselines & Comparisons

4DGCPro outperforms all six baselines on every dataset where they are jointly evaluated. Against the closest NeRF-based competitor HPC [80], it gains 1.79 dB PSNR on 4DGCPro (29.47 vs 27.68) and 2.24 dB on HiFi4G (36.38 vs 34.14) while using less storage. Against the strongest 3DGS-based baseline V³ [69], it improves by 1.36 dB on 4DGCPro (29.47 vs 28.11) and 0.12 dB on HiFi4G (36.38 vs 36.26) at a smaller size (0.75 vs 0.92 MB). The rigid-motion-only methods 3DGStream [61] and 4DGC [20] suffer severe degradation on large-motion sequences (21.08 and 21.48 dB on 4DGCPro vs 29.47 dB for 4DGCPro), confirming the importance of residual deformation modeling. HiCoM [16] is outperformed by 4.82 dB on 4DGCPro and 7.01 dB on HiFi4G. A critical differentiator is that 4DGCPro delivers three quality tiers (High/Mid/Low) from a single bitstream, whereas all baselines require separate models per bitrate; even the Low tier (27.69 dB, 0.33 MB) matches or exceeds the single-rate quality of ReRF and HPC on 4DGCPro.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) line of work for dynamic scene compression, building directly on 4DGC [20] and V³ [69] for rate-aware 4D Gaussian modeling, while departing from their fixed-group, single-bitrate designs. It introduces a perceptually-weighted hierarchical keyframe representation (inspired by V³'s NeuS2 initialization) combined with motion-aware adaptive grouping that replaces the rigid fixed-length groups used in 3DGStream [61] and 4DGC [20]. The entropy-optimized training with layer-wise RD supervision and attribute-specific KDE/Gaussian entropy modeling generalizes the joint RD optimization of 4DGC to a multi-level progressive setting, and the final compression step repurposes standard H.264 video codecs (x264) rather than learned neural codecs, distinguishing it from NeRF-based compressors like ReRF [66] and HPC [80].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in demonstrating that progressive volumetric video streaming with multiple bitrates in a single compact model can achieve real-time decoding and rendering on mobile devices (44 FPS on tablet, 58 FPS on desktop) while outperforming SOTA by 3× in compression ratio over HPC without quality loss. This opens a path toward bandwidth-adaptive free-viewpoint video delivery on consumer hardware using off-the-shelf video codecs. Key open questions remain: training still requires several minutes per sequence due to hierarchical supervision, sparse-view reconstruction is unaddressed, and scalability to spatially extensive scenes is limited. The reliance on multi-view input (81 cameras) also constrains near-term deployment beyond controlled capture environments.
