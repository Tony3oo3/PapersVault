## Problem

Prior theoretical analyses of Neural Collapse (NC) treat last-layer features as unconstrained free variables, showing that global optima of matrix-factorization-like problems exhibit NC, but they do not explain how training a real neural network actually produces NC. This paper asks how gradient flow on a genuine two-layer ReLU network—where features are constrained by the input data and the ReLU activation—provably converges to NC, and what role the implicit bias of the training dynamics plays in facilitating this emergence.

## Input / Output

The method takes orthogonally separable training data (same-class pairs positively correlated, cross-class pairs negatively correlated) and a width-h two-layer ReLU network with ε-small balanced initialization. The output is the asymptotic weight direction of the trained network, whose last-layer features exhibit three NC properties: intra-class directional collapse (features of each class lie in a 1D subspace rather than collapsing to a singleton), orthogonal class-mean directions (rather than a simplex ETF), and projected self-duality (classifier weights align with class means after subtracting the global mean). The directional collapse rather than singleton collapse is a direct consequence of the ReLU nonlinearity and the limited expressiveness of the shallow architecture.

## Method

The paper analyzes gradient flow (continuous-time gradient descent) on a two-layer ReLU network under small balanced initialization, decomposing training into two phases. In the early alignment phase, the directional dynamics of input neuron weights (governed by a Riemannian flow on the sphere) cause neurons to align with class-mean directions, achieving inter-class feature separation. In the late asymptotic phase, the max-margin bias of gradient flow on the resulting decoupled linear sub-problems drives intra-class directional collapse and self-duality. For multi-class problems, the authors extend these binary results to handle the cross-entropy loss and multi-dimensional output, identifying pseudo-labels (projected one-hot vectors) as attractors for output neuron directions. The key insight is that the implicit bias of GF—small initialization enabling feature learning followed by max-margin convergence—is the mechanism that produces NC, and the ReLU activation reshapes NC from singleton collapse to directional collapse and from simplex ETF to orthogonal class means.

## Results

The paper's main contribution is theoretical: Theorem 1 proves that gradient flow on a two-layer ReLU network with small initialization converges to a Neural Collapse solution on orthogonally separable data, exhibiting intra-class directional collapse, orthogonal class means, and projected self-duality. In validation experiments on MNIST digits {0,1,2} with a width-50 two-layer ReLU network, PCA of the last-layer features achieves ~0.2% relative approximation error (vs. ~61% for raw input data), confirming the dramatic feature compression predicted by the theory. In ResNet18/50 experiments on MNIST and CIFAR-10, adding an RMSNorm layer before the ReLU classifier significantly reduces the NC1 metric (intra-class variability), validating the theoretical prediction that normalization promotes stronger collapse.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is primarily a theoretical paper without traditional method-vs-baseline comparisons. The empirical section serves to validate the theoretical NC characterizations rather than benchmark against competing methods. The closest to a comparison is the ablation over normalization layers (Identity/None, LayerNorm, RMSNorm) in modified ResNet18 and ResNet50 on MNIST and CIFAR-10, where RMSNorm consistently yields the lowest NC1 (intra-class variability) metric, supporting the paper's conjecture that normalization layers promote stronger neural collapse. The paper also contrasts its NC characterizations with those from prior Unconstrained Feature Model (UFM) works, noting that the ReLU nonlinearity and input data structure lead to directional collapse (rather than singleton collapse) and orthogonal class means (rather than simplex ETF), but these are qualitative distinctions rather than quantitative benchmark comparisons.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant Unconstrained Feature Model (UFM) line of NC theory (Mixon et al., Zhu et al., Tirer & Bruna, Sukenik et al.), which treats last-layer features as free optimization variables, by instead analyzing NC emergence through the actual training dynamics of gradient flow on a two-layer ReLU network with structured input data. It builds directly on the implicit bias literature for ReLU networks—Phuong & Lampert's alignment dynamics on orthogonally separable data, Ji & Telgarsky's max-margin bias in linear networks, Min et al.'s early neuron alignment, and Lyu & Li's margin maximization—extending these binary-classification results to multi-class settings with cross-entropy loss. The key conceptual departure is showing that NC is not merely a property of the optimization landscape but is actively produced by the two-phase implicit bias of GF: early directional alignment creates inter-class separation, and late max-margin bias drives intra-class directional collapse and self-duality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides the first rigorous bridge between NC characterization and the implicit bias of training dynamics, showing that the specific NC structure (directional collapse into a 1D subspace, orthogonal class means, projected self-duality) is determined by the interplay of input data geometry and ReLU nonlinearity rather than being an artifact of unconstrained feature optimization. The practical observation that RMSNorm significantly sharpens intra-class collapse suggests a concrete architectural lever for promoting NC in deeper networks. However, the analysis is limited to two-layer networks and requires the strong orthogonal separability assumption plus non-trivial initialization conditions (especially the semi-local initialization for multi-class), leaving open whether these characterizations persist in deeper architectures or on less structured data. Extending the ROA analysis to remove the additional separability condition X_max²/(X_min² μ_d μ_s²) < 2K−3 and relaxing the initialization shape assumptions are identified as key future directions.
