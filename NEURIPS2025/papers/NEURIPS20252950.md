## Problem

Conventional design-optimization pipelines pair a generative model with a discriminative scorer to select or guide samples toward high-property designs, but discriminative models are unreliable in the data-scarce, tail-of-distribution regimes typical of scientific applications (e.g., protein engineering). This paper eliminates the need for any external predictor by recasting optimization as conditional density estimation over matched (low-value, high-value) sample pairs.

## Input / Output

The method takes a seed sequence (one-hot encoded over 20 amino acids for proteins, or a continuous vector for images) and a matched dataset of (seed, improved) pairs constructed by pairing samples where the improved member has strictly higher property value and satisfies a Levenshtein/Euclidean distance constraint. It outputs a population of candidate designs (same format as input) that collectively exhibit higher property values than their seeds while respecting the distance constraint; designs are generated iteratively, with each round's outputs serving as the next round's seeds. The matched dataset is typically much larger than the original dataset, amplifying effective training signal in low-data settings.

## Method

GPE defines a target conditional distribution p⁺(x′|x) ∝ p(x′)·𝕀(g(x′)>g(x) ∧ C(x,x′)) and proves (Theorem 1) that minimizing negative log-likelihood on matched pairs uniquely recovers this distribution, bypassing any explicit property predictor. Four off-the-shelf generative architectures are adapted to the conditional setting: mPropEn (discrete NLL on logits), mVAE (conditional VAE trained via ELBO), mFM (conditional discrete flow matching with a linear probability path), and mWJS (a conditional denoiser trained by denoising MSE, used with Langevin MCMC walk steps and a Tweedie-Miyasawa jump step to produce clean designs). Iterative sampling chains K rounds where each round's designs condition the next, progressively pushing property values upward. An optional iterative self-training loop merges model-generated pseudo-matches back into the training set each round, enabling extrapolation beyond the original property range without any oracle or discriminator.

## Results

On the AAV protein fitness benchmark, the best GPE variant (mWJS) achieves a median fitness of 0.53 on the medium split and 0.54 on the hard split, matching or exceeding the strongest baseline GGS (0.51/0.60). On the GFP benchmark, GPE variants clearly outperform all baselines: mVAE reaches 0.84 fitness on the medium split (vs. GGS at 0.76) and mPropEn reaches 0.88 on the hard split (vs. GGS at 0.74). In the iterative self-training experiment on HER2 antibodies starting from only 400 sequences, all three GPE variants surpass a model trained on 1,000 true examples by round 2, with the property ceiling rising each round until saturating around round 4.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The strongest baseline, GGS (smoothed Gibbs sampling) [14], is the only method that competes closely with GPE: it wins on AAV hard (0.60 vs. mWJS 0.54) but is outperformed on AAV medium (0.51 vs. 0.53), GFP medium (0.76 vs. mVAE 0.84), and GFP hard (0.74 vs. mPropEn 0.88). All other baselines are clearly outperformed by GPE variants on fitness across both datasets: GFN-AL [72] (0.20/0.10 AAV, 0.09/0.10 GFP), CbAS [7] (0.43/0.36 AAV, 0.14/0.18 GFP), AdaLead [8] (0.46/0.40 AAV, 0.56/0.18 GFP), BO-qei [73] (0.38/0.32 AAV, 0.20/0.00 GFP), CoMS [9] (0.37/0.26 AAV, 0.00/0.00 GFP), PEX [28] (0.40/0.30 AAV, 0.47/0.00 GFP), and GWG [40] (0.43/0.33 AAV, 0.10/0.00 GFP) all trail the best GPE variant by at least 0.05–0.78 in median fitness. A key architectural distinction is that every baseline requires a trained discriminative fitness predictor during sampling or selection, whereas GPE operates entirely without one, relying solely on the matched-pair conditional density. The ablation in Table 3/4 further confirms that the matched conditioning (vs. unconditional) is what drives the fitness gains: e.g., on GFP medium, unconditional WJS scores −0.02 fitness while mWJS scores 0.76.

## Relation to Prior Work

GPE extends the implicit guidance mechanism of PropEn [Tagasovska et al., NeurIPS 2024] by shifting from a point-estimate optimization perspective to a sampling-based conditional density estimation framework, enabling the generation of diverse populations of improved designs rather than single trajectories. The matched conditioning is instantiated across three off-the-shelf generative architectures—conditional VAEs, flow matching, and walk-jump sampling—each adapted to treat the seed as a conditioning variable. The iterative self-training loop draws on semi-supervised pseudo-labeling (FixMatch-style) but applies it to generative design optimization, a setting where prior work (e.g., Padmakumar et al., Kirjner et al.) still relies on learned scorers at each refinement step. Unlike classifier-guided or classifier-free diffusion approaches, GPE requires no external predictor at any stage of training, sampling, or selection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the discriminative model entirely, GPE addresses a core practical bottleneck in scientific design optimization: reliable property prediction is infeasible in the data-scarce, tail-of-distribution regimes where optimal designs typically reside. The iterative self-training result—where 400 true examples plus four rounds of pseudo-matching surpass a model trained on 1,000 true examples—suggests a path to extrapolating property values beyond the training manifold, which is critical for early-stage drug and antibody discovery. Key limitations remain: the framework currently handles only single-property optimization, validation is in silico (though wet-lab data is reported in the appendix), and the iterative sampling process requires some hyperparameter tuning. Open questions include extending to multi-objective settings, integrating domain-specific structural constraints, and validating at scale in wet-lab pipelines.
