## Problem

Static tabular models assume a fixed feature-to-label mapping, while naive adaptive models risk overfitting to transient patterns, creating a dilemma between generalizability and adaptability under temporal distribution shifts. The paper identifies that evolving feature semantics—where the same raw value carries different subjective meaning (e.g., 'high income') across time—drives concept drift that neither static nor simple temporal-embedding approaches adequately address.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255050/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a numerical feature vector x ∈ R^m and a scalar timestamp t, which is encoded into a temporal embedding ψ(t) ∈ R^d (fixed dimension 128 for periodic components plus a trend component). The modulated features are fed into a standard tabular backbone (MLP, MLP-PLR, or TabM), producing a class label (AUC-evaluated) or regression value (RMSE-evaluated). A key design constraint is that the modulation is incompatible with PLR's arcsine-distributed embeddings, so for PLR-based models the modulation is applied only once at the raw feature level rather than at multiple network stages.

## Method

The core mechanism is a lightweight hypernetwork-style modulator that maps the temporal embedding ψ(t) to three per-feature parameter vectors: γ (scale), β (bias), and λ (Yeo-Johnson power coefficient). The modulation function x̃_i = γ_i(ψ(t))·YJ(x_i; λ_i(ψ(t))) + β_i(ψ(t)) applies a nonlinear Yeo-Johnson transformation followed by affine rescaling, enabling the model to dynamically reshape each feature's distribution (bias, scale, and skewness) according to temporal context. This aligns subjective feature semantics across time so that the backbone learns a stable, temporally invariant decision boundary. The modulation can be applied at the raw input, intermediate representations, and output logits; ablations show input-level modulation alone captures ~87% of the full gain, making the mechanism easy to plug into existing architectures. Unlike FiLM's linear scaling-and-shifting, the Yeo-Johnson component explicitly accommodates nonlinear evolution of feature distributions, and unlike full hypernetworks, only three small parameter vectors are generated per feature, keeping the approach computationally efficient.

## Results

On the TabReD benchmark (8 datasets, 4 classification + 4 regression), TabM with the proposed temporal modulation achieves the best average rank of 3.50 across all methods, outperforming the strongest GBDT baseline CatBoost (rank 4.375) and the temporal-embedding baseline TabM+Embedding (rank 5.125). The modulation yields a 2.09% average improvement over the static MLP backbone, with input-level modulation alone capturing 87.4% of the full multi-stage gain. Notably, TabM+Modulation is the first deep tabular method to consistently outperform GBDT-based models (XGBoost, CatBoost, LightGBM) under temporal distribution shifts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TabM with temporal modulation (rank 3.50) outperforms all GBDT baselines: CatBoost (rank 4.38), XGBoost (rank 6.38), and LightGBM (rank 7.38), marking the first consistent deep-tabular-over-GBDT result under temporal shift. Against the temporal-embedding baseline [6], TabM+Modulation improves the average rank from 5.13 to 3.50 and gains on 6 of 8 datasets (e.g., HD AUC 0.8457 vs. 0.8363; CT RMSE 0.4773 vs. 0.4791). Among static deep methods, TabM+Modulation surpasses ModernNCA (rank 9.13), TabR (rank 13.75), and FT-Transformer (rank 15.13) by a wide margin. The simplest MLP backbone with full modulation (rank 11.00) still beats FT-Transformer and TabR in their static form, though it trails CatBoost and XGBoost. Statistical significance tests (Nemenyi) confirm that TabM+Modulation significantly outperforms both static TabM (p=0.063) and TabM+Embedding (p=0.29), while the embedding-only improvement over static is not significant (p=0.73). The method also generalizes across training protocols: under random splits (Table 7) and original temporal splits (Table 8), TabM+Modulation retains the best or near-best rank (3.50 and 3.63 respectively).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the temporal embedding approach of Cai & Ye (ICML 2025), which concatenates a learned temporal vector to model inputs, by instead using temporal context to generate per-feature modulation parameters (scale, bias, and a Yeo-Johnson nonlinear coefficient) via a lightweight hypernetwork-style modulator. Conceptually, it sits between FiLM's linear feature modulation and full hypernetwork weight generation, borrowing the conditioning idea from FiLM but replacing the linear affine transform with a nonlinear Yeo-Johnson transformation to capture skewness shifts. The paper also draws on the hypernetwork literature for the architectural pattern of a small network producing parameters for a larger one, while explicitly avoiding the computational cost of generating full weights.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that a simple MLP with input-level temporal modulation consistently outperforms GBDTs (XGBoost, CatBoost, LightGBM) under temporal distribution shift is a notable milestone, as deep tabular methods have historically struggled to match tree ensembles in this regime. The practical takeaway is strong: a single modulation layer at the input achieves ~87% of the full multi-stage modulation gain, making the technique easy to bolt onto existing tabular models without architectural changes. Key limitations include incompatibility with PLR embeddings (which produce arcsine-distributed features that undermine the semantic-alignment rationale) and evaluation on a single benchmark (TabReD), leaving open whether the approach generalizes to other temporal shift patterns or domains. The objective-vs-subjective feature semantics framing provides a useful conceptual lens, but a formal characterization of when and why distributional statistics suffice as semantic proxies remains open.
