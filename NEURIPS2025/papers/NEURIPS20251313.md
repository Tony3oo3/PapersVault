## Problem

IMU-based human activity recognition suffers from poor interpretability—raw multi-channel inertial signals are inherently opaque, making it difficult to determine whether models learn meaningful activity structure or merely exploit superficial correlations. This opacity compounds cross-dataset generalization failures, since distributional shifts in sensor type, body placement, and subject characteristics cause models trained on one dataset to degrade on another. No prior method provides a discrete, human-inspectable vocabulary of fundamental movements that both drives recognition and enables transparent analysis of model decisions.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-channel IMU windows of shape T×C (resampled to 100 Hz, 500-sample windows) along with per-channel sensor metadata strings (e.g., 'body_part: Chest, sensor: acc, axis: x') that are converted to fixed-length vectors via Google's text-embedding-004 API. Each channel is independently partitioned into 50-sample (0.5 s) segments, which are instance-normalized and vector-quantized into indices from a 1024-entry codebook; the final token sequence interleaves [CLS], [START], per-channel primitive embeddings, and [END] tokens. Downstream, the model outputs a single activity class label via a linear head on the [CLS] representation, while the intermediate VQ indices constitute the interpretable motion-primitive sequence.

## Method

MoPFormer tokenizes continuous IMU signals into discrete 'motion primitives' via a Vector Quantization codebook (K=1024, L=50, D=256) trained with a commitment loss, then feeds the resulting index sequence through a 5-layer Transformer encoder (8 heads) whose input embeddings fuse four components: VQ lookup, linearly projected statistical features (mean/variance), a metadata embedding adapter that maps pre-trained text embeddings of sensor descriptors into model space, and shared-across-channels positional encodings. Pre-training uses a Masked Auto-Encoding objective that randomly masks 25% of primitive tokens and reconstructs their VQ indices via cross-entropy, while a separate [CLS] head supports supervised fine-tuning. During fine-tuning, the Motion Primitive Module and Context-Aware Embedding Module are frozen and only the classification head is updated, enabling efficient transfer across heterogeneous sensor configurations. The key design insight is that the discrete primitive vocabulary makes model behavior directly inspectable—primitives can be ranked by frequency, clustered by cosine similarity, and mapped to activity classes—something continuous representations cannot offer.

## Results

MoPFormer achieves the highest average accuracy (90.17%) and macro-F1 (89.23%) across six HAR benchmarks, outperforming the strongest supervised baseline TSLANet (89.28% Acc, 88.13% F1) and the best self-supervised baseline ModCL (86.88% Acc, 85.98% F1). The largest individual gains are on MHealth (+3.38% Acc over TSLANet) and USC-HAD (+1.53% Acc), while TSLANet retains a slight edge on PAMAP2 (+0.68%) and DSADS (+0.52%). An ablation removing pre-training drops average accuracy from 90.17% to 86.45%, with the steepest loss on UCI-HAR (−13.0 percentage points), confirming the value of the masked motion-primitive pre-training stage.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest supervised baseline TSLANet (ICML 2024), MoPFormer wins on 3 of 6 datasets (MHealth +3.38%, Realworld +2.02%, USC-HAD +1.53% accuracy) but trails slightly on PAMAP2 (−0.68%), DSADS (−0.52%), and UCI-HAR (−0.44%), yet still secures the best overall average (90.17% vs 89.28%). Against CALANet (NeurIPS 2024), MoPFormer leads on every dataset, with the largest margin on MHealth (+7.17% Acc) and USC-HAD (+4.09% Acc). Among self-supervised baselines, MoPFormer outperforms ModCL by 3.29% average accuracy and BYOL+Mixup by 4.17%, with the gap widening on USC-HAD (+5.87% over ModCL) where sensor heterogeneity is most pronounced. The w/o-pretrain ablation (86.45% avg) confirms that the masked motion-primitive pre-training contributes roughly 3.7 percentage points on average, with a dramatic 13-point drop on UCI-HAR, indicating that the learned primitive vocabulary is especially valuable for small, complex-activity datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

MoPFormer draws a direct conceptual lineage from language modeling, repurposing Vector Quantization (Van Den Oord et al., NeurIPS 2017) to discretize continuous IMU segments into a learnable 'vocabulary' of motion primitives, then applying a Transformer encoder over these discrete tokens. This departs from the dominant self-supervised HAR paradigm (BYOL, ModCL, TS2Vec, TSLANet) that learns continuous latent representations via contrastive or reconstruction objectives, instead learning a symbolic, interpretable token space. The motion-primitive concept itself has roots in robotics (Sanzari et al., Calvo et al.), but this is the first systematic application of VQ-based primitive discovery to multi-channel wearable sensor HAR. The Metadata Embedding Adapter, which maps sensor descriptors through a pre-trained text embedding model, introduces a cross-modal bridge not seen in prior HAR architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By providing a discrete, inspectable vocabulary of fundamental movements, MoPFormer addresses a long-standing interpretability gap in IMU-based HAR that attention-based methods only partially address. The metadata embedding mechanism enables unified pre-training across heterogeneous sensor configurations (different body placements, sensor types, sampling rates), which is practically important for deploying HAR systems in real-world settings where sensor placement varies. Key limitations include the computational overhead of VQ plus multi-layer Transformer inference on resource-constrained wearables, and the high variance observed in multi-seed runs on PAMAP2 (±4.10 accuracy). Open questions include whether the learned primitives transfer meaningfully to new activity domains and whether alignment with large language models (as the authors propose) can yield a truly general-purpose motion understanding system.
