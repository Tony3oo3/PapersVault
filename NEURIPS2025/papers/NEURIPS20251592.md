## Problem

Dynamic novel-view synthesis from monocular videos is addressed by two extremes in prior work: test-time optimization of 4D representations (physically plausible but slow, taking hours per video) and large feed-forward view synthesis models (fast but lacking 3D geometric accuracy and requiring multi-view training data that is scarce for dynamic scenes). No prior method combines data-driven robustness from large-scale pretraining with per-scene geometric fidelity, and none specifically targets the hallucination of occluded regions in extreme novel views.

## Input / Output

The method takes a casually captured monocular video (49 frames at 480×720) of a dynamic scene along with a target camera trajectory, and produces a photorealistic novel-view video of the same scene from that trajectory. The input video is first processed by an off-the-shelf SLAM system (e.g., MegaSAM) to extract 3D scene geometry and camera odometry; the partially rendered novel view (containing only co-visible pixels) serves as the conditioning signal for the diffusion model. A single novel-view sequence is generated in approximately 5 minutes on an A6000 Ada GPU after 140 minutes of test-time finetuning.

## Method

CogNVS decomposes dynamic view synthesis into three stages: (1) reconstruct the scene with off-the-shelf SLAM, (2) render the reconstruction from the target novel view and inpaint all non-co-visible regions using a video diffusion model, and (3) test-time finetune the inpainter on the target video to close the domain gap. The inpainter is built by adapting CogVideoX-5B from an image-to-video to a video-to-video setting, where a 3D causal VAE encodes both the partial novel-view conditioning and the target into latents that are concatenated and processed by a self-attention transformer with 3D-RoPE and adaptive layer normalization under a score-matching objective. The key training insight is that self-supervised pairs are generated by reconstructing 2D videos with SLAM, sampling arbitrary SE(3) camera trajectories via spherical-coordinate interpolation, and using the rendered co-visible masks as structured inpainting targets—avoiding the need for any ground-truth 3D supervision. This allows pretraining on ~10,000 in-the-wild 2D videos (SA-V, TAO, Youtube-VOS, DAVIS) and the same self-supervised procedure to be applied at test time (200–400 gradient steps) to adapt CogNVS to the specific scene's lighting, appearance, and motion distribution.

## Results

On Kubric-4D, CogNVS achieves 22.63 PSNR, 0.760 SSIM, and 0.232 LPIPS, outperforming all baselines on photometric metrics while also attaining the best KID (0.008). On ParallelDomain-4D, CogNVS reaches 24.34 PSNR and 0.797 SSIM, again leading on photometric quality. On the real-world DyCheck benchmark, CogNVS (with MoSca reconstruction) achieves 16.94 PSNR and 92.83 FID, substantially outperforming all test-time optimization and feed-forward baselines on generative quality metrics (FID/KID).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CogNVS outperforms GCD [73] on PSNR (+4.04 on Kubric-4D, +2.57 on ParallelDomain-4D), SSIM, LPIPS, and KID, though GCD achieves a lower FID on ParallelDomain-4D (90.58 vs. 102.43) since it was trained on that dataset. Against Gen3C [61] on Kubric-4D, CogNVS leads on PSNR (+3.22), SSIM (+0.130), LPIPS (−0.058), and KID, while Gen3C has a slightly better FID (98.58 vs. 102.47). The closest competitor, TrajectoryCrafter [92], is beaten by CogNVS on PSNR (+1.70 on Kubric, +2.88 on ParallelDomain-4D), SSIM, and LPIPS across both synthetic datasets, and on KID for Kubric-4D (0.008 vs. 0.024), but TrajectoryCrafter achieves lower FID on both datasets (130.20 vs. 102.47; 95.38 vs. 102.43). On the real-world DyCheck benchmark, CogNVS (MoSca) surpasses Shape-of-Motion [74] and MoSca [37] on PSNR, SSIM, FID, and KID, and dramatically outperforms TrajectoryCrafter [92] on FID (92.83 vs. 140.35) and KID (0.031 vs. 0.059), demonstrating that test-time finetuning is critical for handling distribution shift on in-the-wild videos. CAT4D [82] is competitive on masked photometric metrics (mLPIPS 0.341 vs. 0.530) but is not directly comparable on full-frame FID/KID since it requires multi-view video diffusion plus 4D-GS fitting.

## Relation to Prior Work

CogNVS sits at the intersection of two established lines of work on dynamic novel-view synthesis: test-time 4D reconstruction methods (Shape-of-Motion, MoSca) that are geometrically faithful but slow and cannot hallucinate occluded content, and feed-forward generative approaches (GCD, Gen3C, TrajectoryCrafter) that are fast but sacrifice 3D consistency. The paper's key conceptual move is reformulating the problem as structured video inpainting: it leverages off-the-shelf monocular reconstruction (MegaSAM) to identify co-visible pixels, then fine-tunes CogVideoX into a video-to-video inpainter conditioned on those partial observations. The self-supervised training paradigm—using 3D reconstruction to generate co-visible masks from arbitrary 2D videos—borrows from prior 3D-aware inpainting work (NeRFfiller) but extends it to the dynamic video setting, and the test-time finetuning stage positions the method as a 'best-of-both-worlds' hybrid between data-driven robustness and per-scene optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that CogNVS demonstrates a simple, modular pipeline (reconstruct → inpaint → test-time finetune) that achieves state-of-the-art zero-shot dynamic view synthesis without requiring multi-view dynamic training data, which is notoriously difficult to obtain. The self-supervised training on ~10K in-the-wild 2D videos is a significant practical advantage over methods needing posed multi-view sequences. Key limitations include dependence on reconstruction quality (a ~3 PSNR gap between MegaSAM and ground-truth depth), the inability of the spherical-coordinate trajectory sampler to capture real-world camera diversity, and the relatively small training corpus. Open questions include whether scaling to 3D/4D datasets and data-driven trajectory sampling would close the remaining gap, and whether the test-time finetuning paradigm generalizes to longer sequences or more extreme viewpoint changes beyond ~90 degrees.
