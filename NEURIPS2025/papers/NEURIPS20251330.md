## Problem

While chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning has been extensively applied to LLMs and visual understanding tasks, its role in autoregressive text-to-image generation remains largely unexplored. Current unified multimodal models (ULMs) that combine understanding and generation lack explicit textual planning before image synthesis, and no prior work has identified or jointly optimized the distinct reasoning levels needed at different stages of image generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251330/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single text prompt as input and produces two outputs: a semantic-level CoT (a free-form textual reasoning passage describing the planned scene, object attributes, and spatial layout) followed by a 384×384 RGB image generated autoregressively patch-by-patch. The semantic-level CoT is generated first and then fed back as an additional conditioning signal alongside the original prompt for the subsequent token-level image generation, making the two stages sequentially dependent within a single forward pass of the ULM.

## Method

T2I-R1 introduces BiCoT-GRPO, a reinforcement learning framework that jointly optimizes two levels of CoT within a single training step on a ULM (Janus-Pro-7B). In step 1, the model generates a semantic-level CoT (textual planning of objects, attributes, and spatial relationships); in step 2, it generates image tokens patch-by-patch conditioned on both the prompt and the semantic-level CoT, with the sequential token generation itself serving as the token-level CoT. Both CoT segments form a single response whose policy gradient is optimized via GRPO with group-relative advantages over G=8 sampled images per prompt. The reward is an ensemble of three vision experts—HPS (human preference), GroundingDINO (object detection with spatial/numerical checks), and GIT (VQA for attribute verification)—whose scores are averaged to prevent reward hacking. The paper shows that direct RL outperforms SFT-then-RL, and that jointly optimizing both CoT levels yields superior results and higher image diversity compared to optimizing either level alone.

## Results

On T2I-CompBench, T2I-R1 achieves 0.8130 (Color), 0.5852 (Shape), 0.7243 (Texture), 0.3378 (Spatial), 0.3090 (Non-Spatial), and 0.3993 (Complex), representing a 13% average improvement over the Janus-Pro-7B baseline. On the WISE benchmark, T2I-R1 reaches an overall score of 0.54, a 19% improvement over the baseline (0.35), surpassing FLUX.1-dev (0.50). On GenAI-Bench, T2I-R1 achieves the highest overall scores of 0.88 (basic) and 0.73 (advanced), outperforming FLUX.1-dev (0.87 and 0.64 respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

T2I-R1 outperforms the Janus-Pro-7B baseline by 13% on T2I-CompBench and 19% on WISE, with the largest gains in attribute binding (Color +0.177, Shape +0.232, Texture +0.231) and spatial reasoning (Spatial +0.132). Against the state-of-the-art diffusion model FLUX.1, T2I-R1 leads in 5 of 6 T2I-CompBench subtasks (e.g., Spatial 0.3378 vs. 0.2863, a >5% margin) but trails slightly on Non-Spatial (0.3090 vs. 0.3127). On WISE, T2I-R1 surpasses FLUX.1-dev by 4% overall (0.54 vs. 0.50), excelling in Cultural (0.56 vs. 0.48) and Biology (0.54 vs. 0.42) but losing on Time (0.55 vs. 0.58) and Chemistry (0.30 vs. 0.35). Among autoregressive models, T2I-R1 achieves the best scores across all T2I-CompBench subtasks, beating Show-o+PARM (the strongest prior AR method) by 5.8% on Color and 14.1% on Spatial. On GenAI-Bench, T2I-R1 achieves the highest advanced-prompt score (0.73) among all models tested, surpassing FLUX.1-dev (0.64) by 9%, with the gap driven by superior performance on Count (0.81 vs. 0.75) and Negate (0.60 vs. 0.45) subtasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on CoT-enhanced autoregressive image generation (Guo et al., 'Can we generate images with CoT?') by identifying a second, higher-level reasoning stage—semantic-level CoT—that precedes the token-level patch-by-patch generation already recognized in prior work. It builds on the GRPO reinforcement learning framework from DeepSeekMath and the Janus-Pro unified multimodal model (ULM) as the base architecture, but departs from prior prompt-rewriting approaches that rely on an external LLM by generating the semantic plan from the generation model itself. The key methodological departure is the joint optimization of both CoT levels within a single GRPO training step using an ensemble of vision-expert reward models, rather than the rule-based rewards used in text reasoning tasks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that RL-based CoT reasoning can elevate a 7B autoregressive image generator to surpass a much larger diffusion model (FLUX.1) on compositional and world-knowledge benchmarks, suggesting that reasoning capability—not just model scale or architecture type—can be a decisive factor in generation quality. The finding that SFT-then-RL underperforms direct RL, and that token-level CoT optimization alone reduces output diversity, provides practical guidance for future training pipelines. Open questions include whether the paradigm extends to video or 3D generation (where reward design and inference latency become significantly harder), whether it generalizes beyond Janus-Pro to other ULMs, and how it scales to higher resolutions.
