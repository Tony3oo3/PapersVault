## Problem

Conditional image generation models struggle to capture the inherent uncertainty in input conditions, where a single prompt (e.g., 'sunset scene' or '65-year-old male') maps to many plausible outputs. Existing diversity strategies either rely on random seed variation (producing non-meaningful differences) or verbal prompt diversification via LLMs (limited to text conditions and introducing external biases), leaving a gap for a general-purpose, condition-type-agnostic method that produces diverse yet faithful images.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single condition—text prompt (encoded to 77×1024 via CLIP), demographic vector (256-dim for age/sex), or input image plus prompt—and simultaneously produces a set of M diverse images (M=40 for 512×512 natural images, M=8 for 160×192×176 3D brain MRI volumes, M=10 for 512×512 chest X-rays). Unlike standard single-sample generation, the output is a structured set where each image reflects a distinct interpretation of the same condition, with the pretrained generative backbone remaining frozen.

## Method

Rainbow inserts a latent graph (N nodes, sparsity ρ) into the condition representation pipeline and uses a GFlowNet to sample M diverse edge-addition trajectories over this graph, each trajectory encoding a different 'reading' of the condition. A graph decoder (RNN + projection pooling) maps each trajectory to a condition embedding, which is blended with the original condition via a convex combination (γ=0.5) before being fed to a frozen LDM. Training uses the Detailed Balance Objective to align trajectory sampling with a reward defined as exp(−MSE) of the diffusion denoising error, jointly optimizing the GFlowNet generator (diversity + reward alignment) and the graph decoder (fidelity) while keeping the Unet, VAE, and condition encoder frozen. This plug-and-play design requires training only two small modules from scratch, making Rainbow applicable to any pretrained conditional generative model regardless of condition type.

## Results

On 3D brain MRI synthesis, Rainbow achieves a FID of 0.3149 (vs. 0.3288 for LDM and 2.3329 for GAN), 79% sex classification accuracy (vs. 68% LDM, 63% GAN), and 13.73 years age MAE (vs. 21.52 LDM, 29.23 GAN). On natural images (COCO validation, 60 prompts × 40 images), Rainbow outperforms SD2-1, SD3.5, CADS, and PAG on both Vendi Score (diversity) and Inception Score (quality), while all methods achieve similar CLIP scores (~30.3). On chest X-rays, Rainbow achieves higher Vendi Score and lower FID than the finetuned SD1.5 baseline, and both outperform RadEdit.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Rainbow consistently outperforms all seven baselines across the three domains. On natural images, Rainbow surpasses SD2-1, SD3.5, CADS, and PAG in both Vendi Score (diversity) and Inception Score (quality); SD3.5 produces repetitive backlit-mountain layouts, CADS yields unclear or winter-dominated images, and PAG with diversified prompts fails to introduce meaningful seasonal variation. On 3D brain MRIs, Rainbow reduces FID from 0.3288 (MONAI LDM) to 0.3149, improves sex classification accuracy from 68% to 79%, and lowers age MAE from 21.52 to 13.73 years, while the GAN baseline (Kwon et al.) lags far behind on all metrics (FID 2.33, 63% sex accuracy, 29.23 age MAE). On chest X-rays, Rainbow achieves higher Vendi Score and lower FID than the finetuned SD1.5 (PRISM) baseline, and both Rainbow and SD1.5 outperform RadEdit, which was trained from scratch on multiple radiology datasets. Notably, even with M=1 trajectory (ablation), Rainbow still outperforms the LDM baseline, confirming that the latent graph mechanism provides benefit beyond simple multi-sample generation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper integrates Generative Flow Networks (GFlowNets), previously applied to molecule generation, gene regulatory networks, and dropout masks, into the condition-encoding pipeline of Latent Diffusion Models — a combination the authors note is, to their knowledge, the first. It departs from prior diversity strategies that rely on random seed variation or LLM-based prompt diversification by instead decomposing the condition embedding into diverse latent graph trajectories sampled via the Detailed Balance Objective. The framework is model-agnostic, building on frozen pretrained LDMs (Stable Diffusion, MONAI 3D LDM) and only training a lightweight graph generator and decoder, positioning it as a plug-in module rather than a new generative architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Rainbow addresses a practical gap in conditional generation: capturing the inherent multi-modality of ambiguous prompts or conditions without external LLMs or seed-hacking, and it does so across text and non-text conditions (age/sex for brain MRI, disease labels for chest X-rays). The interpretability of latent graph edges (e.g., edges corresponding to seasonal features or medical devices) opens a path toward controllable, explainable diversity. Key limitations include the higher computational cost of training M parallel trajectories, the still-limited automatic interpretability of the latent graph, and the need for anatomical plausibility validation before clinical use. Open questions include scaling to larger graphs, extending to text generation and recommendation, and whether the approach generalizes to fundamentally different condition spaces.
