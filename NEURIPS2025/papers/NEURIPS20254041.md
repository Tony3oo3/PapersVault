## Problem

Current frame-event fusion trackers rely on ANNs that struggle to model sparse, asynchronous event data and incur high computational cost, while existing SNN-based trackers are limited to unimodal event streams or hybrid ANN-SNN designs that sacrifice energy efficiency. No fully spiking network has been applied to unified frame-event object tracking, leaving the natural compatibility between SNNs and event streams unexploited for multimodal tracking.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254041/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The network consumes paired image frames and event streams, where events are first converted to time-integrated images (3D tensors with B temporal bins) to match the frame modality. Template patches (128×128) and search patches (256×256) are extracted from both modalities, then randomly reorganized spatially by the RPM before entering the backbone. At inference, two decoupled spiking tracking heads produce separate response maps for frame and event modalities, which are fused via a weighted sum (λ=0.5) to yield the final target localization.

## Method

SpikeFET adopts a dual-single-dual architecture entirely in the spiking domain: dual-branch ConvFormer Spike Blocks extract modality-specific features, a shared Transformer Spike Block with Cross-Shaped Windows Spiking Self-Attention (CSWin-SSA) performs cross-modal fusion, and dual decoupled heads predict separate response maps constrained by a Gaussian-Weighted Focal similarity loss. The Randomized Patchwork Module (RPM) randomly concatenates template and search images horizontally or vertically before feature extraction, breaking padding-induced positional bias while a learnable type encoding preserves logical patch identity. The Spatial-Temporal Regularization (STR) strategy applies an MSE loss between fused features of two adjacent template frames to enforce spatio-temporal consistency and suppress boundary asymmetry. All spiking neurons use Spike Firing Approximation (SFA) for integer-based training and spike-driven inference, and the model is pre-trained on frame datasets (COCO, LaSOT, TrackingNet, GOT-10K) before fine-tuning on frame-event data.

## Results

On FE108, SpikeFET-Tiny achieves 68.5% AUC and 96.5% PR, matching the previous SOTA HRMonTrack (68.5% AUC) while using only 29.33M parameters and 18.36 mJ power (39× less than SDSTrack's 719.10 mJ). On COESOT, SpikeFET-Base reaches 68.5% AUC and 81.7% PR, surpassing the best ANN-SNN hybrid MMHT by 2.7% AUC and 7.7% PR. On VisEvent, SpikeFET-Base (59.0% AUC) is slightly below ViPT (59.2%) and SDSTrack (59.7%), which the authors attribute to VisEvent's less challenging scenarios where event-camera advantages are less pronounced. The event-only variant SpikeET also sets new SOTA on both event benchmarks, improving AUC by 4.5% over SDTrack on FE108.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SpikeFET-Tiny outperforms all frame-event baselines on FE108 by a wide margin: +2.7% AUC over SDSTrack (65.8%), +3.3% over ViPT (65.2%), and +5.5% over MMHT (63.0%), while consuming 39× less power than SDSTrack and 7.3× less than ViPT. On COESOT, SpikeFET-Base leads all methods with 68.5% AUC, beating MMHT (65.8%) by 2.7% and SDSTrack (63.7%) by 4.8%. On VisEvent, SpikeFET-Base (59.0% AUC) trails ViPT (59.2%) and SDSTrack (59.7%) by small margins, which the authors attribute to VisEvent's simpler scenarios where event-camera advantages are less critical. The event-only SpikeET variant also dominates event-based tracking, surpassing SDTrack by 4.5% AUC on FE108 (64.4% vs. 59.9%) and 2.0% on VisEvent (39.4% vs. 37.4%) with only 8.80 mJ power. Overall, SpikeFET achieves the best accuracy-efficiency trade-off across all three benchmarks, with the Tiny variant delivering near-Base accuracy at a fraction of the computational cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

SpikeFET extends the one-stream tracking paradigm of OSTrack into the fully spiking domain, adopting its training procedure and overall architecture while replacing all ANN components with spiking equivalents. The spiking tracking head design is inherited from SDTrack, the first transformer-based spike-driven tracker, but SpikeFET generalizes it from unimodal event-only input to unified frame-event tracking. The spiking neuron training relies on the Spike Firing Approximation from E-SpikeFormer, and the cross-modal fusion leverages a spiking adaptation of CSWin Transformer's cross-shaped window attention. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of RPM and STR, which address padding-induced translation invariance degradation and asymmetric template boundary features—problems that prior spiking trackers (SDTrack, STNet) did not explicitly tackle.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work demonstrates that a fully spiking network can match or exceed ANN-based frame-event trackers on challenging scenarios while consuming 39× less theoretical power, making it a strong candidate for neuromorphic edge deployment in autonomous driving and surveillance. The RPM finding—that random spatial reorganization of input patches dramatically mitigates padding-induced positional bias—offers a transferable insight applicable beyond spiking networks. However, the evaluation is limited to DAVIS346 camera data across three datasets, no dynamic template update is employed, and performance on less challenging datasets (VisEvent) lags behind strong ANN baselines like ViPT and SDSTrack, leaving open whether the fully spiking advantage generalizes to broader sensor modalities and more diverse tracking scenarios.
