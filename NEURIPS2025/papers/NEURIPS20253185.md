## Problem

The precise cortical pathways along which tau aggregates propagate in Alzheimer's disease, and how amyloid-beta modulates those flows, remain open questions. Existing models either assume a pre-defined physics law without identifying the governing principle, operate at coarse anatomical regions that discard fine-grained cortical geometry, or produce black-box predictions lacking mechanistic interpretability. This paper seeks to simultaneously learn the propagation dynamics and the explicit tau-amyloid interaction law from longitudinal PET data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253185/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates directly on the FreeSurfer pial surface mesh (~163,842 vertices, average degree ~6) rather than volumetric parcels, consuming tau PET SUVR at time t, amyloid PET SUVR at time t, and the mesh-derived graph topology as input. It produces a predicted tau SUVR map at t+1, a per-vertex optimal transport flow field ν ∈ ℝ^{N×d}, and a per-vertex symbolic algebraic expression R(u,v) encoding the tau-amyloid reaction law. The surface-based resolution preserves sulcal and gyral geometry that region-based graphs (typically <100 nodes) discard.

## Method

MFG4AD formulates tau spread as a reaction-diffusion PDE on the cortical graph, then proves this PDE is the Euler-Lagrange optimality condition of a potential Mean Field Game (MFG) coupling HJB and Fokker-Planck equations. Exploiting the Kantorovich-Rubinstein dual, the MFG saddle point reduces to a Wasserstein-1 Lagrangian GAN: a generator G_θ (a GNN for graph-constrained diffusion, a symbolic regression module for the explicit tau-amyloid reaction term, and a lightweight subnetwork for the advection flow field) predicts the next-time tau density, while a 1-Lipschitz critic C_φ (enforced via spectral normalization) estimates the Earth-Mover gap between predicted and true distributions. Training alternates 5 critic updates per generator update, with the generator loss combining the adversarial term and an L1 reconstruction penalty (λ=10). The symbolic regression module replaces a black-box MLP to yield a human-readable reaction law, and learnable scalars μ₁, μ₂, μ₃ automatically balance advection, diffusion, and reaction contributions.

## Results

On ADNI (tau+amyloid input), MFG4AD achieves MAE 0.064±0.004 and RMSE 0.093±0.009, outperforming the closest competitor DSM (MAE 0.083±0.004, RMSE 0.129±0.019) by ~23% in MAE. On OASIS, MFG4AD reaches MAE 0.435±0.066 and RMSE 0.619±0.061, again the best among all methods. All improvements over baselines are statistically significant (p<0.01, paired t-test). The learned scaling factors (μ₁=0.94, μ₂=0.97, μ₃=1.33) indicate the amyloid-tau reaction term carries the greatest weight in the propagation dynamics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MFG4AD outperforms all 10 baselines on both datasets with tau+amyloid input, with all differences statistically significant (p<0.01). On ADNI, the closest competitor is DSM (Deep Symbolic Model, MAE 0.083), which MFG4AD beats by 0.019 MAE (23% relative improvement); Ridge regression (0.088) and NDM (0.101) follow. On OASIS, the margin narrows: MFG4AD (0.435) edges out DSM (0.447) and Ridge (0.451) by ~0.01–0.016 MAE, while NDM achieves a lower RMSE (0.589) but with much higher variance (±0.246). Graph-based methods (GCN, GCNII, GRAND, GREAD) and vanilla GAN consistently underperform, with GCNII and GRAND showing the weakest ADNI results (MAE 0.127–0.214). The ablation confirms both the diffusion (GNN) and reaction (symbolic regression) components are necessary, with removing the reaction term causing the larger performance drop, underscoring amyloid as a key modulator of tau dynamics.

## Relation to Prior Work

MFG4AD unifies three previously separate threads: (1) the reaction-diffusion PDE formulation for tau propagation (Iturria-Medina et al., 2014), (2) the equivalence between such PDEs and potential mean-field games (Li et al., 2022), and (3) the Kantorovich-Rubinstein dual of the Wasserstein-1 distance, which naturally yields a Lipschitz-constrained GAN (Goodfellow et al., 2020). The key conceptual departure is that prior tau-propagation models (NDM, GREAD, GCN-based methods) either assume a fixed physics model or learn black-box dynamics on coarse anatomical parcels, whereas MFG4AD derives the governing law from the MFG saddle-point structure and operates on a 163,842-vertex cortical surface mesh. The symbolic regression module (Petersen et al., 2021) replaces the typical MLP reaction term, yielding an explicit, human-readable tau-amyloid interaction law rather than an opaque function.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a theoretically grounded, interpretable framework for predicting tau progression that simultaneously recovers biologically meaningful propagation pathways and an explicit reaction law linking amyloid burden to tau accumulation, consistent with Braak staging. This addresses a core gap in AD research: most computational models are either mechanistically opaque (deep learning) or lack data-driven discovery of the interaction kernel (hand-tuned PDEs). Key limitations include the small cohort sizes (134 ADNI, 77 OASIS subjects), the absence of long-range structural connectivity (tractography), and the computational cost of fitting per-vertex symbolic laws. Open questions include whether the learned reaction laws generalize across more diverse cohorts, whether incorporating neuroinflammation or synaptic-loss biomarkers improves predictions, and whether the reaction-diffusion engine can be used for in-silico intervention testing before clinical trials.
