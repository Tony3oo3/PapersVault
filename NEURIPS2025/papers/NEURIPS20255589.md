## Problem

Diffusion-based structure prediction models such as AlphaFold 3 and Boltz-1 are trained to predict static structures, and their default sampling produces highly concentrated outputs that fail to capture the full conformational landscape. Even a hypothetical model that perfectly reproduced the Boltzmann distribution would require enhanced sampling to efficiently explore rare states (e.g., unbound conformations of tight-binding proteins at ~10⁻¹⁴ probability). The paper asks: given a pretrained generative structure model, what inference-time strategy can efficiently and accurately sample its hidden conformational landscape without prior knowledge of relevant degrees of freedom?

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained diffusion-based structure prediction model (Boltz-1 or BioEmu) and standard biological inputs (amino acid sequence, optionally MSA and structural templates), requiring no additional training or architectural modification. It produces a structured set of conformational samples as 3D atomic coordinates, organized by target RMSD distance (0–20 Å) from the default prediction, with physically implausible samples filtered out using a 10-residue sliding-window pLDDT criterion and clash detection. Optionally, MBAR reweighting of the conditional samples yields free energy estimates across the sampled conformational space.

## Method

ConforMix applies Twisted Diffusion Sampling—a sequential Monte Carlo particle filter—to perform conditional generation at inference time, requiring only a time-independent guidance function evaluated on the fully denoised state estimate at each denoising step. The key insight is that diffusion models learn richer energy landscapes than default i.i.d. sampling reveals, and these hidden states can be accessed by steering the reverse diffusion process toward regions of interest. ConforMixRMSD, the automated exploration variant, constructs a series of quadratic RMSD-based guidance potentials that progressively push sampling away from the default prediction, with a rigid-element mask restricting the RMSD metric to secondary-structure residues to avoid trivially sampling disordered loop fluctuations. The particle filter simultaneously denoises multiple particles and periodically resamples them, providing an asymptotic guarantee of correct conditional sampling. For free energy estimation, the multistate Bennett acceptance ratio (MBAR) algorithm is applied to the mixture of conditional samples to jointly estimate partition functions and reconstruct the unconditional distribution, a statistically optimal reweighting approach applied to diffusion models for the first time.

## Results

On the harder task of recovering the worst-matched (alternate) reference conformation, ConforMixRMSD-Boltz achieves 0.69 coverage on domain motion proteins (n=38), 0.33 on membrane transporters (n=15), and 0.45 on cryptic pocket proteins (n=31), substantially outperforming default Boltz sampling (0.33, 0.13, 0.15) and all MSA-perturbation baselines. On the easier best-matched task, ConforMixRMSD reaches 0.97 on domain motion and 0.79 on transporters. ConforMix sampling costs ~3× the wall-clock time of default sampling but generates far more informative samples, and PCA analysis shows 96% of variance in ConforMixRMSD ensembles aligns with a single biologically relevant principal component for domain motion proteins.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ConforMixRMSD-Boltz clearly outperforms all three MSA-perturbation baselines (AFCluster, CF-random, AFsample2) and default Boltz sampling on the harder worst-matched conformation recovery task for domain motion (0.69 vs. 0.44–0.51), membrane transporters (0.33 vs. 0.19–0.20), and cryptic pockets (0.45 vs. 0.33–0.39). However, on fold-switching proteins, AFCluster (0.27) and CF-random (0.20) outperform ConforMixRMSD (0.13), suggesting discrete input modulation better captures large-scale fold rearrangements. On the easier best-matched task, ConforMixRMSD leads on domain motion (0.97) and transporters (0.79) but is competitive rather than dominant on cryptic pockets (0.94 vs. 0.97 for AFCluster/CF-random) and fold switching (0.67 vs. 0.70 for AFsample2). PCA analysis further shows ConforMixRMSD concentrates structural variance along experimentally relevant transition directions, whereas MSA-based methods produce diffuse, less interpretable sampling. For free energy estimation in BioEmu, ConforMix with MBAR reweighting converges significantly faster than unbiased default sampling while reaching comparable estimates.

## Relation to Prior Work

ConforMix builds directly on Twisted Diffusion Sampling (Wu et al., 2024), a sequential Monte Carlo method for asymptotically exact conditional sampling in diffusion models, and on the MBAR free energy estimator (Shirts & Chodera, 2008), applying both to biomolecular structure prediction for the first time. It departs from the dominant prior approach of perturbing model inputs (MSA subsampling in AFCluster, AFSample2, CF-random) to elicit conformational diversity, instead operating purely at inference time on a fixed input. The method is orthogonal to and complementary with existing conformational generation models like BioEmu, which it also enhances for faster free energy estimation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical implication is that diffusion models trained for static structure prediction (e.g., Boltz-1, AlphaFold 3) may encode richer conformational information than their default sampling reveals, and ConforMix provides a general, training-free way to access it without prior knowledge of relevant degrees of freedom. Limitations include dependence on the underlying model's landscape quality, non-asymptotic SMC approximation error, and weaker performance on discrete rearrangements like fold switching. Open questions include whether combining ConforMix with input-modification methods yields further gains, how the approach scales to larger complexes, and whether the hidden landscape information persists as models are retrained with explicit dynamics data.
