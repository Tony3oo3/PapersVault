## Problem

Learning neural controllers for continuous-time nonlinear systems with formal stability guarantees remains challenging because existing methods suffer from severe conservatism in region-of-attraction (ROA) estimation, driven by fixed training domains and unbalanced training data selection. Additionally, verifying the continuous-time Lyapunov condition (involving Jacobian-vector products) via SMT solvers like dReal is prohibitively slow, scaling poorly beyond low dimensions.

## Input / Output

The method takes a continuous-time nonlinear state-space model (e.g., cartpole, quadrotor, PVTOL), a desired equilibrium state x*, its corresponding control input u*, and control input bounds. It outputs a tanh-parameterized neural controller (ensuring u(x*)=u* and respecting input limits), a sigmoid-parameterized Lyapunov function V: R^n → [0,1], and a formally verified ROA defined as the sublevel set V ≤ c₂ with a small unverifiable hole V ≤ c₁ excluded. The controller and Lyapunov function are standard feedforward MLPs trained jointly via an actor-critic scheme.

## Method

The core contribution is a two-stage training pipeline. Stage 1 (ROA Estimation) uses a Zubov-inspired sampling strategy that performs projected gradient descent to select balanced batches of points both inside the current ROA estimate (V_θ ≤ c) and near its boundary (V_θ ≈ 1), combined with dynamic domain expansion that periodically simulates trajectories and grows the training box to include all states along convergent paths. The training loss combines the Zubov PDE residual, a Bellman-type data loss (avoiding full trajectory integration), a Sontag-style controller loss, and boundary/zero regularization. Stage 2 (CEGIS Refinement) applies gradient-based PGD attacks to find counterexamples violating the Lyapunov condition in the band V ∈ [c₁, c₂], then fine-tunes with a hinge loss on those counterexamples plus regularization to preserve the levelset structure. For verification, the paper extends α,β-CROWN with novel tight linear relaxations for the Jacobians of tanh and sigmoid (treating them as 'bell-shaped' functions with case-specific tangent/chord bounds), and introduces an adaptive branch-and-bound algorithm that dynamically shrinks c₁ or c₂ when counterexamples are found in the worst-bounded subdomain, eliminating the need for expensive bisection while preserving soundness of already-verified subproblems.

## Results

Across 11 benchmark systems (2D to 12D), the proposed two-stage Zubov-based training achieves 100% success rate on all systems, yielding ROA volumes 5–1.5×10⁵ times larger than the best baseline. On the 4D Cartpole, the method produces an ROA of 306.1±54.2 (vs. 0.9266 for the best baseline Shi et al., a ~330× improvement), and on the 6D 2D Quadrotor it reaches (6.64±4.67)×10⁶ (vs. 44.53 for Shi et al., a ~150,000× improvement). For formal verification, the extended α,β-CROWN verifier is 40–10,000× faster than the SMT solver dReal on 2D systems, and dReal fails to terminate on Cartpole entirely.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method dominates all four training baselines on every system where they overlap. Fossil 2.0 [13] is limited to 2D systems and is outperformed on ROA volume by 1.8× (Van der Pol) to 5.4× (Pendulum Small), while also suffering from an incorrect equilibrium condition (u(x*)≠u*) on some systems. DITL [44] only covers 2D systems and is beaten by 5–14× on ROA volume; it also fails entirely on Cartpole (ROA ≈ 0.021 vs. 306.1 for ours). Yang et al. [48] covers 2D and 4D systems but is outperformed by 5× on Path Tracking and is the only baseline that cannot handle Cartpole at all. Shi et al. [35] is the strongest baseline, covering 2D through 6D systems, yet is still beaten by 2.9× on Van der Pol, 6× on Pendulum Big, 330× on Cartpole, 380× on PVTOL, and ~150,000× on 2D Quadrotor. For verification, the extended α,β-CROWN verifier achieves 40–10,000× speedup over dReal [15] on 2D systems (e.g., 3.94s vs. 39,265s on Van der Pol), and dReal fails to terminate on Cartpole where α,β-CROWN completes in ~76,000s.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of neural Lyapunov control work (NLC, DITL, Yang et al., Shi et al.) by introducing a two-stage training framework that decouples ROA estimation from formal certification. It draws on the Zubov theorem for both the physics-informed PDE loss and a principled data-sampling strategy (mixing interior and boundary-near points via PGD), and on the Liu et al. (2025) ground-truth Zubov value for the data loss. For verification, it departs from the SMT-solver-based approach (dReal) used in prior continuous-time work by extending α,β-CROWN with novel linear relaxations for Jacobian operators (tanh', sigmoid') and an adaptive levelset-adjustment scheme that avoids expensive bisection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The two-stage design addresses a practical bottleneck: CEGIS alone fails without a good initialization (0% success in ablations), and the Zubov-guided ROA estimation stage provides that initialization while simultaneously yielding a much less conservative domain through trajectory-based expansion. The 40–10,000× verification speedup over dReal makes formal certification of continuous-time neural controllers tractable where it was previously prohibitive. However, formal verification still does not scale beyond ~6 dimensions due to bound-tightness issues on Jacobian-vector products, leaving high-dimensional systems (e.g., 12D quadrotor) reliant on empirical trajectory checks. Open questions include whether verifier-friendly training objectives can close this gap and whether the framework extends to discrete-time, hybrid, or perturbed systems.
