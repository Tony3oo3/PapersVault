## Problem

4D Gaussian Splatting achieves high-fidelity dynamic scene reconstruction but suffers from two forms of temporal redundancy: a large fraction of Gaussians have very short temporal spans (70% have Σt < 0.25), inflating storage to ~2 GB per scene, and during rendering only ~15% of Gaussians are actually active at any frame, yet all are processed, wasting computation. No prior method systematically exploits both redundancies to simultaneously compress storage and accelerate rasterization.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view video of a dynamic scene with calibrated camera parameters and per-frame timestamps, evaluated at half-resolution (1352×1014) with 300 frames on N3V. It outputs a compressed 4D Gaussian representation (reduced from ~3.3M to ~667K Gaussians, ~418 MB or as low as 50 MB with post-processing) and renders novel-view RGB images at over 1000 FPS rasterization speed. The temporal filter stores per-key-frame visibility masks (compressible to bits) that gate which Gaussians participate in each frame's rasterization.

## Method

4DGS-1K applies a two-stage compression pipeline on top of vanilla 4DGS. First, a Spatial-Temporal Variation Score ranks each Gaussian by combining a spatial score (aggregated alpha-blending contribution across all training views) with a temporal score derived from the second derivative of the Gaussian's temporal opacity function (mapped via tanh and scaled by normalized 4D volume); the lowest-scoring 80–85% of Gaussians are pruned and the remainder fine-tuned for 5000 iterations. Second, a key-frame temporal filter selects sparse key-frames at fixed intervals (Δt = 20 frames on N3V), computes visibility masks by rendering from all training views at each key-frame, and at test time restricts rasterization to only the Gaussians marked active by the two nearest key-frames, exploiting the observed >80% IoU of active sets across adjacent frames. An optional post-processing stage applies vector quantization to spherical harmonics and bit-packs the masks for further storage reduction.

## Results

On the N3V dataset, 4DGS-1K achieves PSNR 31.88, SSIM 0.946, and LPIPS 0.052 with only 418 MB storage and 1092 raster FPS, compared to vanilla 4DGS which requires 2085 MB and 118 raster FPS — a 41× storage reduction and 9× speedup at a mere 0.03 dB PSNR cost. On D-NeRF, the method reaches PSNR 33.34, SSIM 0.97, LPIPS 0.03 with 42 MB storage and 2482 raster FPS, actually surpassing the original 4DGS by 0.35 dB in PSNR while using 40× less storage. With post-processing (vector quantization + bit-packed masks), storage drops further to 50 MB (N3V) and 7 MB (D-NeRF) with negligible quality loss.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary baseline 4DGS [1], 4DGS-1K matches quality almost exactly (PSNR 31.88 vs 31.91 on N3V; 33.34 vs 32.99 on D-NeRF) while reducing storage by 41×/40× and accelerating rasterization by 9×/4×. Compared to the fastest prior Gaussian method Dynamic 3DGS [37], 4DGS-1K gains 1.2 dB PSNR (31.88 vs 30.67) while using only 15% of its storage (418 MB vs 2764 MB) and running 1.75× faster (805 vs 460 FPS). Against the concurrent compression methods MEGA [42] and Compact3D [29], 4DGS-1K is 10× and 4× faster in rasterization respectively, at comparable or slightly higher storage. On D-NeRF, deformation-based methods Deformable3DGS [23] (PSNR 40.43) and SC-GS [44] (PSNR 40.65) still outperform 4DGS-1K (33.34) in reconstruction quality, but 4DGS-1K is 20× faster (1462 vs 70 FPS) and uses less storage. Among 4DGS-based methods specifically, 4DGS-1K achieves the best raster FPS (1092 on N3V, 2482 on D-NeRF) by a wide margin over 4D-RotorGS [41] (277 FPS) and 4DGS [1] (118 FPS).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on 4D Gaussian Splatting (4DGS) by Yang et al., which represents dynamic scenes as collections of 4D Gaussian primitives with a 4×4 covariance matrix. It departs from 4DGS by diagnosing temporal redundancy—specifically, the prevalence of short-lifespan Gaussians and the waste of computing on inactive Gaussians—as the root cause of 4DGS's storage and speed problems, then applies a two-step compression pipeline (spatial-temporal variation score pruning followed by key-frame temporal filtering). The pruning criterion draws on 3DGS compression literature (LightGaussian, Mini-Splatting) but extends it with a temporal component based on the second derivative of the temporal opacity function, while the temporal filter exploits the empirically observed high IoU of active Gaussians across adjacent frames—a property specific to the 4D setting that has no direct analogue in 3DGS compression.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 41× storage reduction and 1000+ FPS rasterization make 4DGS practically deployable in real-time applications (VR, AR, interactive media) where vanilla 4DGS's ~2GB per scene and ~118 FPS rasterization are prohibitive. The temporal redundancy analysis (70% of Gaussians having Σt < 0.25, 85% inactive per frame) provides a useful diagnostic framework that could inform future 4D representation designs. Limitations include the need for post-pruning fine-tuning, a fixed key-frame interval hyperparameter, evaluation on only two datasets with limited scene diversity, and the absence of environment maps on some scenes. Open questions include whether the pruning criterion generalizes to longer or more complex dynamic sequences, and whether the temporal filter's shared-mask assumption holds under rapid scene changes.
