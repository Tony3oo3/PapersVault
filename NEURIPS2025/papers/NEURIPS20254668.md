## Problem

Reasoning LLMs generate extremely long intermediate reasoning paths (often exceeding 32K tokens), causing KV cache size to grow linearly and severely degrading inference throughput and memory usage. Existing KV cache compression methods such as SnapKV, H2O, and TOVA are designed for long input prompts rather than long generated sequences, and they struggle to preserve accuracy on reasoning tasks where token relevance is unpredictable and shifts over the course of generation. The paper identifies and exploits the semantic sparsity inherent in reasoning traces—repeated re-derivations and redundant logical steps—as a principled basis for compressing the KV cache of generated tokens.

## Input / Output

RPC operates entirely at inference time on the KV cache of a standard autoregressive decoder, requiring no model weights modification or additional training. At each compression trigger (every P generated tokens), it reads the query vectors of the R most recent tokens (the selector window) and computes attention-based importance scores for all previously retained and newly generated tokens across all layers and heads. The output is the same generated reasoning text as full-KV inference, but with the KV cache bounded to approximately (total_generated_tokens / c) + R entries, where c is the target compression ratio (default 4×), enabling generation at lengths that would otherwise cause out-of-memory failures.

## Method

RPC periodically compresses the KV cache every P decoding steps rather than at every step, reducing computational overhead compared to step-wise eviction methods. At each compression cycle, it scores the importance of all candidate tokens (previously retained plus newly generated) by aggregating attention weights from a selector window of R recent query tokens across all attention heads, then applies local average pooling with window size w to encourage contiguous retention of semantically related tokens. Only the top P/c highest-scoring tokens are retained, and because the full candidate set is re-evaluated at every cycle, outdated tokens naturally fade out as the reasoning context evolves. The method is entirely training-free and model-agnostic, requiring only access to attention scores during decoding, and integrates directly into existing inference pipelines built on FlashAttention-2. Ablations show that layer-wise aggregation of attention scores, a selector window of R=32, and compression intervals of P=1024 or P=4096 provide the best accuracy–efficiency trade-off, achieving up to 1.60× throughput improvement and over 50% peak memory reduction with ≤1.2% accuracy drop on AIME 2024.

## Results

On QwQ-32B, RPC (P=4096) achieves 78.3% pass@1 on AIME 2024 (vs. 79.5% full KV, a 1.2% drop), 62.2% on LiveCodeBench (vs. 63.4%), and 82.6% on IFEval (vs. 83.9%). On DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B, RPC (P=4096) scores 52.9% on AIME 2024 (vs. 55.5% full KV, a 2.6% drop) and 35.9% on LiveCodeBench (vs. 37.6%). RPC delivers up to 1.60× throughput improvement and over 50% peak memory reduction, enabling QwQ-32B to generate 32K-token reasoning paths that would otherwise cause out-of-memory failures.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RPC substantially outperforms both KV cache compression baselines. On DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B AIME 2024, RPC (P=4096) achieves 52.9% pass@1, beating H2O (42.5%) by 10.4 points and TOVA (42.5%) by 10.4 points; on QwQ-32B AIME 2024, RPC scores 78.3% versus H2O's 75.0% (+3.3) and TOVA's 70.0% (+8.3). The gap widens further on LiveCodeBench, where RPC (62.2% on QwQ-32B) exceeds H2O (54.2%) by 8 points and TOVA (43.8%) by 18.4 points. The training-based baseline LightThinker performs catastrophically, scoring only 6.7% on AIME 2024 and 0.7% on LiveCodeBench for DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B, confirming that length-aware training objectives conflict with reasoning quality. RPC also slightly exceeds full KV cache on IFEval for both models (56.6% vs. 55.1% for DeepSeek; 82.6% vs. 83.9% for QwQ-32B), suggesting that compression does not harm—and may even help—lighter instruction-following tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the KV cache compression literature (SnapKV, H2O, TOVA) from its traditional focus on long input prompts to the underexplored regime of long generated reasoning sequences. It departs from training-based approaches like LightThinker that attempt to shorten reasoning paths via length-aware objectives, instead operating purely at inference time with no model modification. The key conceptual departure is the 'selector window' mechanism: rather than scoring token importance relative to input tokens (SnapKV) or all preceding tokens (H2O), RPC uses only the most recent R generated queries as importance indicators, exploiting the observation that recent reasoning steps encode what the model currently needs from earlier context. The periodic (rather than step-wise) compression schedule is a practical design choice that amortizes the scoring overhead.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As reasoning LLMs (o1, R1, QwQ) become the default for complex problem-solving, their 16K–32K token generation paths create severe memory and throughput bottlenecks that block practical deployment; RPC offers a drop-in, training-free solution that resolves OOM for 32B models at 32K tokens while preserving accuracy within ~1–2%. The semantic sparsity finding, quantified via n-gram entropy, provides a principled justification for why aggressive compression is viable specifically for reasoning traces (unlike general long-form generation). Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to non-reasoning long generation, multi-turn conversations, or interacts well with speculative decoding and other inference accelerators; the 8× compression results also suggest a hard ceiling beyond which reasoning fidelity degrades substantially.
