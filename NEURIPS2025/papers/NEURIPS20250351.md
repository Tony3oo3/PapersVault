## Problem

Standard CATE estimation assumes the same confounders are observed at both training and inference time, but in clinical practice (e.g., telemedicine, medical chatbots), models are trained on rich structured patient data while inference relies only on free-text symptom descriptions. This discrepancy—formalized here as inference time text confounding—causes naive text-based estimators to produce biased treatment effect estimates due to residual confounding, and no existing method addresses this specific setting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250351/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At training time, the method consumes structured clinical confounders X (e.g., blood pressure, lab values, demographics) along with binary treatment A and continuous outcome Y. At inference time, it takes only a free-text symptom description (≈150–200 tokens of self-reported language) and outputs a scalar CATE estimate τ̂^t(t) = E[Y(1)−Y(0)|T=t]. The text is embedded via a pretrained BERT encoder (768-dim mean-pooled token representations), and the final predictor is a 3-layer MLP regressing doubly-robust pseudo-outcomes onto these embeddings.

## Method

TCA (Text Confounding Adjustment) operates in three stages: (1) estimate nuisance functions (outcome surfaces μ_a^x and propensity score π^x) on the structured training data and construct doubly-robust pseudo-outcomes; (2) generate text surrogates T̃ = g(X) using an LLM (GPT-4o mini) to simulate the inference-time text modality, so that the training data links structured confounders to their textual projections; (3) train a text-conditioned regression f_θ on BERT embeddings of T̃ to predict the pseudo-outcomes. The key theoretical insight is the reformulation τ^t(t) = E[τ^x(X)|T=t], which decouples treatment effect estimation (performed on X) from inference-time adaptation (performed on T), making the target estimand identifiable despite X being unobserved at test time. The estimator inherits double robustness: it remains consistent if either the outcome model or the propensity score is correctly specified. Crucially, the LLM is used solely as a semantically rich text generator—not as a causal reasoner—bridging the modality gap between structured training data and textual inference inputs.

## Results

On the IST dataset, TCA achieves a PEHE of 0.116 ± 0.02, approximately 40% lower than the best baseline (TBE-CFR at 0.192 ± 0.03). On MIMIC-III, TCA achieves 0.179 ± 0.02, roughly 35% lower than the best baseline (TBE-CFR at 0.274 ± 0.02). The advantage is consistent across demographic subgroups (gender, age) and across different LLMs (GPT-4o mini, GPT-3.5 Turbo) and prompt strategies used for text surrogate generation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

All five baselines are text-based estimators (TBE) that train standard CATE learners directly on text embeddings without adjusting for residual confounding. TCA outperforms every baseline on both datasets by a wide margin. Against the meta-learner baselines (TBE-S-learner and TBE-T-learner from Künzel et al., PNAS 2019), TCA reduces PEHE by roughly 41% on IST (0.116 vs. 0.198/0.196) and 37% on MIMIC-III (0.179 vs. 0.283/0.280). Against the representation-learning baselines (TBE-TARNet and TBE-CFR from Shalit et al., ICML 2017), the gap is similar: 0.116 vs. 0.193/0.192 on IST and 0.179 vs. 0.275/0.274 on MIMIC-III. Against the doubly-robust baseline (TBE-DR from Kennedy, EJS 2023; Table 5), TCA still leads by a large margin (0.116 vs. 0.187 on IST; 0.179 vs. 0.266 on MIMIC-III), confirming that the core contribution is the confounding-adjustment framework rather than the choice of DR estimation itself.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the doubly-robust CATE estimation framework of Kennedy (2023) and the meta-learner literature (Künzel et al., 2019; Shalit et al., 2017), but departs from the standard assumption that identical confounders are observed at both training and inference time. It also distinguishes itself from proxy-based text confounding methods (Veitch et al., 2020; Chen et al., 2024; Roberts et al., 2020), where text is available at both times as a noisy proxy for latent confounders, and from runtime confounding (Coston et al., 2020), which requires a known fixed subset of covariates at inference. The key conceptual departure is the reformulation τ^t(t) = E[τ^x(X) | T=t], which decouples treatment effect estimation on structured confounders from inference-time adaptation to text, and the use of LLMs purely as semantically rich text generators rather than causal reasoners.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses a practically critical gap in personalized medicine: telemedicine chatbots, emergency triage systems, and medical LLMs routinely receive only free-text symptom descriptions at inference time, yet are trained on structured clinical data. The identifiability result and the three-stage TCA framework provide a theoretically grounded path to unbiased CATE estimation under this modality shift. Key limitations include reliance on semi-synthetic benchmarks (counterfactuals are unobservable in real data), dependence on LLM quality for surrogate text generation, and the assumption that the text generation mechanism satisfies conditional independence (Assumption 3.2). Open questions include extension to multi-treatment or continuous-treatment settings, handling cases where partial structured data is also available at inference, and validation on fully observational (non-RCT) clinical deployments.
