## Problem

While data imbalance and model hyperparameters are known to affect group fairness in deep learning, the role of the optimization algorithm itself has remained unexplored. This paper asks whether and how the choice between stochastic (SGD) and adaptive (RMSProp, Adam) optimizers influences the fairness of the learned model, particularly under severe demographic imbalance in training data.

## Input / Output

The method operates on RGB images resized to 224×224 pixels, spanning facial images (CelebA, FairFace) and general images (MS-COCO). It produces binary class labels for facial expression recognition (happy/not-happy) and gender classification, or multi-label predictions over 75 human-associated object categories for MS-COCO. Fairness is then evaluated by computing equalized odds, equal opportunity, and demographic parity gaps across demographic subgroups (gender, race, age) defined by the sensitive attribute.

## Method

The paper derives SDE approximations for SGD and RMSProp under a Noisy Gradient Oracle framework, then solves the resulting Fokker-Planck equations analytically in a two-subgroup quadratic loss setup to show that RMSProp has a strictly higher probability of converging to the fair (demographic-parity-optimal) minimum than SGD when sampling imbalance exceeds a threshold. Two further theorems establish that RMSProp's coordinate-wise gradient normalization (via exponentially decaying squared-gradient averages) shrinks the parameter-update disparity between subgroups relative to SGD, and that the worst-case single-step increase in the demographic parity gap under RMSProp is upper-bounded by that of SGD. Empirically, the authors train ResNet-18/50, VGG-16, and TinyViT backbones on CelebA, FairFace, and MS-COCO using SGD, RMSProp, and Adam with Bayesian-optimized hyperparameters, and additionally test complementarity with a fairness-enhancing batch-selection method on tabular data, consistently finding that adaptive optimizers achieve better fairness scores at comparable accuracy.

## Results

On CelebA with ViT (gender attribute, hyperparameter-tuned), Adam achieves F_EOD of 65.21 and F_DPA of 73.50, outperforming SGD (62.66 and 60.80) by 2.6 and 12.7 points respectively, while RMSProp is nearly on par with Adam (65.18, 73.68). Accuracy remains comparable across optimizers: Adam reaches 92.08% on CelebA vs. SGD's 91.23%, and 92.20% vs. 89.41% on FairFace. The fairness gap between adaptive and stochastic optimizers is largest on the most imbalanced dataset (FairFace, minority-to-all ratio ~0.9%), where RMSProp outperforms SGD by up to 9% on F_EOD and 6% on F_EOP. Wilcoxon signed-rank tests confirm statistical significance (p ≤ 0.007) across all fairness metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against standard SGD, both Adam and RMSProp consistently achieve higher fairness scores across all three metrics (F_EOD, F_EOP, F_DPA) on all five dataset-attribute combinations, with the largest margins on the most imbalanced settings (FairFace with age: +9% F_EOD for RMSProp, +6% F_EOP for Adam). Against SGD with momentum (Liu et al., 2020), AdamW achieves 64.90 F_EOD (gender) and 71.11 (age) versus 61.12 and 67.25 for SGD w/ momentum, confirming that momentum alone does not recover the fairness benefit of second-moment normalization. Against AdaBound (Luo et al., 2019), AdamW again leads (64.90 vs. 61.10 F_EOD gender), while AdaBound performs comparably to SGD w/ momentum (61.10 vs. 61.12) because AdaBound deliberately clips its second-moment estimates to behave like SGD, neutralizing the adaptive normalization mechanism that drives the fairness advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper applies the SDE approximation framework for optimization dynamics—established for SGD by Li et al. (ICML 2017) and extended to adaptive methods like RMSProp by Malladi et al. (NeurIPS 2022)—to a new domain: group fairness analysis. It connects to the broader fairness literature on pre/in/post-processing interventions and to Liu et al.'s (2019) work on implicit fairness criteria of unconstrained learning, but departs by showing that the optimizer itself, a component present in every training pipeline, systematically shapes fairness outcomes. The paper also builds on the observed robustness-fairness tradeoff noted by Ma et al. (2023), who showed SGD-trained models are more robust to perturbations, and formalizes the complementary fairness advantage of adaptive methods through Fokker-Planck analysis and two new theorems.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical implication is significant: switching from SGD to an adaptive optimizer like Adam or RMSProp yields a 'free' fairness improvement without any additional fairness-specific modifications, regularization, or data preprocessing—addressing a key limitation of existing fairness methods that are rarely adopted due to computational overhead. The theoretical results (Theorems 1–3) provide a mechanistic explanation—gradient normalization suppresses subgroup-specific update disparities—rather than merely an empirical observation. Limitations include the 1D analytical setup for the SDE analysis, the single-step scope of Theorem 3, and the isotropic noise assumption; open questions include whether these fairness properties hold in highly non-convex, high-dimensional landscapes and how they interact with other fairness interventions at scale.
