## Problem

Existing RPM solvers suffer from two fundamental limitations: their reasoning modules are shallow and cannot be easily scaled up without overfitting (deepening the network increases overfitting risk on concise, intuitive rules), and they rely on static CNN/MLP weights that fail to adapt to diverse task-specific rules in novel or out-of-distribution scenarios. This paper addresses both the scalability and adaptability gaps simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252238/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an 80×80 pixel image matrix (3×3 or 2×3 grid with the last slot missing) and a set of 8 candidate answer images as input, producing a predicted answer index. Visual features are extracted via four ResNet blocks, then processed row-wise through the reasoning pyramid; the same reasoning network is shared across all three rows to enforce rule consistency. For the RVP video prediction task, input consists of two historical frames sampled every 15 frames from traffic videos, with the goal of selecting the correct future frame from 8 candidates.

## Method

DSRF introduces a Multi-View Reasoning Pyramid (MVRP) that widens rather than deepens the reasoning network: features are mapped into multiple views (pyramid structures of 2-1, 4-2-1, or 8-4-1 views per layer), each focusing on distinct attribute combinations, enabling scalable coverage of complex rules without overfitting. Within each view, a Dynamic Domain-Contrast Prediction (DDCP) block implements a predict-and-verify paradigm using a Gated Attention Reasoning Block (GARB) that models context and target feature distributions via Gram matrices, applies learnable weights and input-dependent gating to dynamically contrast domain differences, and highlights relevant features for rule construction. A Multi-granularity Rule Mixer (MRM) further refines reasoning by exploring rule combinations through parallel channel-wise, token-wise, row-wise, and sample-wise feature mixing. The entire framework is trained end-to-end with supervised learning (Adam, lr=1e-3, batch size 128) and can be plugged in as a drop-in reasoning module replacing the reasoning component of other RPM solvers.

## Results

On the three RAVEN datasets (O-RVN, I-RVN, RAVEN-FAIR), DSRF-M and DSRF-L achieve 99.2% average accuracy, outperforming the previous best HP²AI (98.9%) by 0.3%. Under OOD settings (train on one RAVEN, test on another), DSRF-L reaches 97.7% average, surpassing the second-best PredRNet (94.3%) by 3.4%. On the Unicode Analogies dataset, DSRF-L achieves 73.8% (normal) and 37.3% (OOD), a 16.1% gain over PredRNet in the normal setting. On PGM, DSRF-L sets a new SOTA of 54.9% average (vs. 51.9% for Slot Abstractors), and on RVP it reaches 88.8% (vs. 71.6% for TRIVR).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DSRF-M and DSRF-L consistently outperform the previous SOTA HP²AI [19] by 0.3% average on the three RAVEN datasets, with the largest gain (0.5%) on RAVEN-FAIR where the network widening captures more complex rules. Under OOD transfer (train on one RAVEN, test on another), DSRF-L beats the second-best PredRNet [15] by 3.4% average, and specifically achieves 95.9% when trained on O-RVN and tested on I-RVN versus PredRNet's 89.2%. On Unicode Analogies, DSRF-L surpasses PredRNet [15] by 16.1% in the normal setting (73.8% vs. 57.7%) and HP²AI [19] by 7.1% in the OOD setting (37.3% vs. 30.2%), where most baselines drop to near-random performance (~25%). On PGM, DSRF-L outperforms Slot Abstractors [42] by 3.0% average (54.9% vs. 51.9%), with particularly large gains on held-out regimes (e.g., 82.3% on HTP vs. 78.3%). On RVP, DSRF-L achieves 88.8%, a 17.2% improvement over TRIVR [41] (71.6%), demonstrating the scalability advantage of MVRP in real-world video prediction. The lighter DSRF-S remains competitive with HP²AI (98.7% vs. 98.9%) while using fewer parameters (11.64M vs. 22.10M for DSRF-L).

## Relation to Prior Work

DSRF builds on the predict-and-verify paradigm introduced by Li et al. (IJCAI 2024) for the DDCP block, borrows the Gram-matrix-based feature distribution modeling from Zhu et al. (CVPR 2024), and adapts the convolutional gating mechanism from TransNext (Shi, CVPR 2024). Conceptually, it departs from the dominant trend of deepening reasoning networks (as in PredRNet and HP²AI, which stack residual reasoning blocks) by instead widening the network through a Multi-View Reasoning Pyramid, and replaces static CNN/MLP reasoning modules with dynamic, input-dependent GARB blocks that model domain differences between context and target features. This positions DSRF as a response to two specific limitations identified in prior work: overfitting from deep reasoning stacks and poor OOD generalization from fixed-weight reasoning modules.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that widening (more parallel views) rather than deepening (more stacked blocks) is a more effective scaling strategy for abstract visual reasoning, and that dynamic, input-conditioned reasoning modules substantially improve OOD generalization—DSRF-L outperforms the prior best by 3.4% on average across six OOD settings on RAVEN variants, with the largest gap (6.7%) in the hardest cross-dataset transfer. The plug-and-play experiments showing consistent gains when DSRF replaces reasoning modules in four different architectures suggest the dynamic reasoning component is broadly applicable. Limitations include evaluation restricted to RPM-style tasks (the RVP video prediction dataset has only 3K samples), reliance on supervised training without auxiliary losses, and the fact that in-distribution accuracy gains over the strongest baseline (HP²AI) are modest (~0.3–0.5%), with the real advantage concentrated in OOD and compositional generalization regimes.
