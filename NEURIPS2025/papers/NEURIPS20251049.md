## Problem

Molecular relational learning (MRL) methods have relied exclusively on 2D topological graphs, ignoring the 3D geometric information that governs how molecules recognize, interact, and bind in their environment. Obtaining true 3D interaction geometry via quantum mechanical or molecular dynamics simulations is prohibitively expensive, and no pre-training strategy has been designed specifically for MRL—unlike molecular property prediction, where 3D geometric pre-training is well established.

## Input / Output

At inference, the model consumes only a pair of 2D molecular graphs (atom attribute matrices and adjacency matrices) and outputs a scalar interaction property (solvation free energy, absorption/emission wavelength, excited-state lifetime) or a binary drug-drug interaction label. During pre-training, 3D conformer pairs are additionally used to construct a virtual interaction geometry, but this 3D information is never required at downstream task time, making the approach applicable in settings where 3D structures are unavailable.

## Method

3DMRL constructs a virtual interaction environment by placing n randomly oriented copies of a smaller molecule around selected non-aromatic atoms of a larger molecule, using Gaussian-noise-scaled translations and random rotations to mimic explicit solvent configurations. Two complementary pre-training objectives are jointly optimized: (1) an SE(3)-invariant global geometry loss that aligns the 2D MRL interaction representation (concatenated Set2Set graph embeddings) with a geometric GNN encoding of the virtual environment via NT-Xent contrastive learning, and (2) an SE(3)-equivariant local relative geometry loss that predicts, using local frames, the unit direction vector from each atom of the small molecule to its assigned atom on the large molecule. The local-frame formulation converts equivariant predictions into invariant edge features for GNN processing, then re-projects through the frame to recover equivariant outputs, guaranteeing physical consistency under arbitrary rotations and translations. The total pre-training loss is L_glob + α·L_local, and after task-agnostic pre-training the 2D encoders are fine-tuned on labeled downstream data without any 3D input.

## Results

3DMRL achieves consistent RMSE reductions across all 40 molecular interaction and DDI tasks, with a maximum improvement of 24.93% (CIGIN on CompSol: 0.369→0.277). On the DDI scaffold-split task, 3DMRL-pretrained CIGIN reaches 74.85/78.56 AUROC on ZhangDDI/ChChMiner, surpassing the no-pre-training baseline (70.96/75.81) by ~4–3 points. The method also improves weaker architectures substantially, e.g., MPNN on Abraham (0.640→0.550, 14.06% reduction) and AttentiveFP on CompSol (0.350→0.271, 22.57% reduction).

## Baselines & Comparisons

3DMRL consistently outperforms all single-molecule MPP pre-training baselines (3D Infomax, GraphMVP, MoleculeSDE) on the CIGIN architecture across nearly all molecular interaction tasks; for example, on CompSol 3DMRL achieves 0.277 RMSE versus 0.321 (3D Infomax), 0.301 (GraphMVP), and 0.308 (MoleculeSDE). On the DDI scaffold-split task, 3DMRL leads by 2.9–4.0 AUROC points over the no-pre-training baseline and by 1.5–3.7 points over the best MPP method (GraphMVP), with the gap widening in the harder scaffold-split setting. When applied to other architectures, 3DMRL yields the largest relative gains on weaker models: MPNN improves by up to 14.06% (Abraham) and AttentiveFP by up to 22.57% (CompSol), while the already-stronger CGIB and CGIB_Cont see more modest but still consistent 1–15% improvements. Notably, MPP pre-training methods sometimes cause negative transfer (e.g., MoleculeSDE drops ZhangDDI molecule-split AUROC from 71.75 to 70.07), whereas 3DMRL never degrades performance relative to training from scratch.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: molecular relational learning (MRL) methods like CIGIN, CGIB, and Delfos that model molecule-pair interactions using only 2D topological graphs, and 3D geometric pre-training methods like 3D Infomax, GraphMVP, and MoleculeSDE that inject 3D conformer information into single-molecule encoders. The key departure is that prior 3D pre-training targets single-molecular property prediction (MPP), while MRL requires modeling the *relative* geometry between two molecules in an interaction environment—a fundamentally different geometric problem. The virtual interaction geometry construction (random placement of smaller molecules around a larger one) draws on explicit solvent modeling from molecular dynamics and stochastic initialization strategies from protein-ligand docking, but repurposes them as a data-agnostic, cost-free substitute for expensive quantum mechanical or metadynamics-based interaction structure generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that pre-training strategies designed for single-molecule 3D geometry (MPP pre-training) do not transfer well to molecular interaction tasks and can even cause negative transfer, motivating task-specific pre-training for MRL. The virtual interaction environment offers a practical, real-time-generable alternative to prohibitively expensive 3D interaction structure computation, making 3D-informed MRL pre-training accessible without quantum chemistry infrastructure. Open questions include whether the random placement strategy can be replaced by physics-informed generators (e.g., MD trajectory samplers), whether the approach scales to protein-ligand binding where the 'larger molecule' has vastly more complex geometry, and whether the SE(3)-equivariant local geometry prediction can be extended to predict actual interaction forces rather than direction vectors.
