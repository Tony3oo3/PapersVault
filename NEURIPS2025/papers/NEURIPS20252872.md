## Problem

Diffusion-based flexible docking methods require multi-step iterative sampling and external scoring functions for pose selection, making them slow (tens of seconds per prediction) and difficult to scale in drug discovery workflows. Regression-based approaches offer one-shot efficiency but have historically underperformed in flexible docking because a single forward pass struggles to capture induced-fit conformational changes without iterative correction. The paper asks whether a regression framework can match or exceed diffusion-based accuracy while retaining its speed advantage.

## Input / Output

The model takes unbound (apo) protein structures predicted by ESMFold and apo ligand conformations generated by RDKit as input, and directly outputs the bound (holo) complex structure including both ligand pose and protein pocket side-chain conformations in a single forward pass. Atom types are distinguished by structural role (e.g., backbone carbonyl C vs. side-chain CB, CE1, etc.) to explicitly encode side-chain identity. The intermediate conditional pair representation encodes inter-molecular distance patterns between pocket and ligand atoms, serving as an explicit structural condition rather than a generative latent variable.

## Method

FIGRDock uses a two-stage training pipeline: first, a conditional pair representation is learned by pre-training an interaction network on 5.34M SIU computational complexes to predict inter-molecular distance matrices from noisy (perturbed dihedral) apo-like conformations, with a pocket encoder pre-trained via a side-chain denoising objective on ProFSA data; second, a regression-based docking module is fine-tuned on 17K PDBbind crystal complexes to directly predict coordinate displacements and distance matrices for ligand, pocket, and interface. The key architectural insight is that interaction-aware distance patterns distilled from coarse docking poses serve as explicit structural conditions that guide the regression head, compensating for the lack of iterative refinement. The regression module (a Transformer-based encoder following Uni-Mol's design) takes apo structures plus the frozen pair representation and outputs coordinate offsets and intra/inter-molecular distance matrices in one pass, achieving ~100× speedup over diffusion baselines while improving %RMSD<2Å by 4–7%.

## Results

On PDBbind, FIGRDock achieves 57.2% RMSD<2Å (holo) and 46.6% RMSD<2Å (apo) with a median RMSD of 1.6Å and 2.3Å respectively, while completing inference in just 0.4 seconds per complex. This represents a ~145× speedup over the best diffusion-based method ReDock (58s) and a ~650× speedup over GNINA (1575s), while simultaneously improving RMSD<2Å by 3.3–3.7 percentage points over ReDock. On PoseBusters V2, FIGRDock achieves 99.5% PBValid (apo) and 96.7% PBValid (holo), outperforming FlexDock and search-based methods GOLD and VINA under holo input.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FIGRDock outperforms all diffusion-based flexible docking baselines on PDBbind: it beats ReDock by +3.3 pp (holo) and +3.7 pp (apo) on RMSD<2Å, and DiffDock-Pocket by +7.4 pp (holo) and +4.9 pp (apo), while running 145× faster than ReDock and 152× faster than DiffDock-Pocket. Against the all-atom flexible model FlexDock, FIGRDock improves RMSD<2Å by +6.9 pp under apo input (46.6% vs. 39.7%) with a 27× speedup (0.4s vs. 11s). Search-based methods (SMINA, GNINA) are dominated on both accuracy and speed; even their rigid variants (GNINA rigid: 42.7% holo RMSD<2Å) fall well short of FIGRDock's 57.2%. On PoseBusters V2, FIGRDock with holo input surpasses GOLD and VINA, and with apo input slightly trails them but tackles a harder task (predicting protein conformational changes). AlphaFold3, a co-folding method trained on far more data, still outperforms FIGRDock on PoseBusters, but FIGRDock achieves competitive accuracy at a fraction of the computational cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

FIGRDock extends the regression-based docking tradition (EquiBind, TANKBind) from rigid to flexible docking, a setting previously dominated by diffusion-based generative models (DiffDock-Pocket, ReDock, FlexDock). Its closest predecessor, FABFlex, also uses regression for flexible docking but omits side-chain flexibility and lacks explicit interaction modeling. The key departure is the two-stage training design: a conditional pair representation learned on 5M+ SIU computational complexes provides interaction-aware guidance to the regression head, replacing both the iterative sampling of diffusion models and the generic protein language model embeddings (ESM2) used by prior generative methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 100× inference speedup (0.4 s vs. ~58 s) while matching or exceeding diffusion-based accuracy makes flexible docking practical for large-scale virtual screening, where per-pose cost is the bottleneck. The finding that a single forward pass guided by interaction-specific representations can rival multi-step generative sampling challenges the field's working assumption that flexible docking inherently requires iterative refinement. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to novel protein families outside the PDBbind distribution, how performance scales with even larger pre-training corpora, and whether the dependence on ESMFold-predicted apo structures limits robustness for poorly characterized targets.
