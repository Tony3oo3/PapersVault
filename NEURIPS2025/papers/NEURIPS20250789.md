## Problem

Conventional patching in time series forecasting partitions the look-back window into fixed, adjacent patches, creating a rigid representation space that assumes useful information is evenly distributed. In practice, changeable periods, regime shifts, and anomalies break this assumption, causing fixed patching to either split semantic patterns or inject harmful noise into the representation. The paper asks whether an adaptive, selective patching mechanism can construct a more informative representation space for forecasting.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a multivariate time series X ∈ R^{N×T} (N channels, T timesteps) as a look-back window, processed with instance normalization and a channel-independent strategy. It produces a future forecast Ŷ ∈ R^{N×L} for L steps ahead (tested at L ∈ {96, 192, 336, 720}). The SRS module internally scans all K = (n−1)·s + 1 candidate patches at stride 1 from the padded series, selects n of them (with replacement), reorders them, and fuses their embeddings with those of the conventional adjacent patches before feeding a lightweight MLP head.

## Method

The SRS module replaces fixed adjacent patching with two learnable, gradient-based stages. First, Selective Patching uses an MLP Scorer^s to assign n scores to each of K candidate patches (scanned at stride 1), then retrieves the top-scoring patches with replacement; a detach-reciprocal trick (E^s = S_max^s ⊙ detach(1/S_max^s)) preserves gradient flow through the otherwise non-differentiable Argmax. Second, Dynamic Reassembly applies a second MLP Scorer^r to the selected patches, sorts them by score to determine their sequence, and applies the same gradient-preserving trick. Finally, Adaptive Fusion computes a convex combination (with learnable α ∈ [0,1]^{n×d}) between the embeddings of the selected patches and those of the conventional adjacent patches, then adds sinusoidal position embeddings. The resulting SRSNet pairs this module with a ≤2-layer MLP head trained end-to-end with MSE loss, and the SRS module can also be plugged into existing patch-based backbones (PatchTST, Crossformer, xPatch, PatchMLP) with ~10% overhead in memory and time.

## Results

SRSNet (SRS module + MLP head) achieves state-of-the-art average MSE/MAE across 8 forecasting benchmarks with horizons F∈{96,192,336,720}. It attains the best MSE on 6 out of 8 datasets: ETTh1 (0.404), ETTh2 (0.334), ETTm2 (0.252), Electricity (0.161), Solar (0.183), and Traffic (0.392). On Solar, SRSNet improves MSE by 7.6% over the next-best TimeKAN (0.198) and by 8.5% over PatchTST (0.200). The model is notably lightweight, requiring only ~1,012 MB GPU memory on ETTh1 versus 2,837 MB for PatchTST and 4,011 MB for Crossformer.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SRSNet outperforms all 12 baselines on 6 of 8 datasets in average MSE, with the largest margins on Solar (0.183 vs. 0.198 for TimeKAN, 7.6% improvement) and ETTh2 (0.334 vs. 0.347 for TimeMixer, 3.7% improvement). Against the closest patch-based competitors, SRSNet beats PatchTST on ETTh1 (0.404 vs. 0.419), ETTh2 (0.334 vs. 0.351), ETTm2 (0.252 vs. 0.256), Electricity (0.161 vs. 0.171), Solar (0.183 vs. 0.200), and Traffic (0.392 vs. 0.397). SRSNet trails iTransformer slightly on ETTm1 (0.351 vs. 0.347 MSE) and Weather (0.226 vs. 0.224 MSE), where the inverted-attention design captures inter-channel dependencies more directly. Against non-patch baselines, SRSNet dominates DLinear on all 8 datasets (e.g., 0.404 vs. 0.430 on ETTh1) and Crossformer on 7 of 8 (Crossformer degrades severely on ETTh2 with MSE 0.894). The ablation confirms SRS as a plug-in improves PatchTST by ~5% average, Crossformer by ~5%, xPatch by ~2.4%, and PatchMLP by ~3%, validating its generality across patch-based architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the patching paradigm introduced by Triformer and PatchTST for time series forecasting, where fixed-stride adjacent patches create a rigid representation space. It departs from this by proposing the Selective Representation Space (SRS) module, which uses learnable Selective Patching (inspired by Mamba's sample-wise scoring) and Dynamic Reassembly to adaptively choose and reorder patches, replacing the assumption that all information is uniformly distributed. The SRS module is positioned as a plug-and-play enhancement orthogonal to multi-scale techniques (Pathformer, PatchMLP) and channel-independence strategies, while SRSNet (SRS + MLP) demonstrates that a well-constructed representation space can substitute for complex Transformer or CNN backbones.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play SRS module offers a practical, low-overhead (~10% compute) way to improve any existing patch-based forecaster, which is valuable for practitioners already invested in such architectures. The paper's core insight—that investing in representation space quality (which patches to include and in what order) can be more effective than designing increasingly complex backbones—challenges the prevailing trend of architectural complexity in TSF. Key open questions include whether SRS scales to foundation-model pretraining, whether the selected patches are interpretable, and how to make the module environment-aware rather than purely data-driven. The limitation that SRS is inapplicable to non-patch models (e.g., iTransformer's whole-series embedding) constrains its universal applicability.
