## Problem

Most non-invasive speech decoding datasets are 'broad but shallow,' offering only 1–2 hours per subject, which limits the ability to train deep within-subject models. Prior evidence shows that deep single-subject data outperforms broader multi-subject data when matched for total training hours, yet no MEG dataset exceeded ~10 hours per participant. LibriBrain addresses this gap by providing over 50 hours of within-subject MEG recordings, enabling systematic study of how decoding performance scales with data volume in a controlled, single-participant setting.

## Input / Output

Input consists of 306-channel MEG time series (102 magnetometers + 204 planar gradiometers) downsampled to 250 Hz (4 ms/sample) and bandpass-filtered to 0.1–125 Hz, stored as per-session HDF5 files with paired TSV event files providing time-locked word and phoneme onsets/durations. The three benchmark tasks produce: (1) a binary speech/non-speech label per time window, (2) one of 39 ARPAbet phoneme labels per phoneme-aligned MEG segment, and (3) a top-10 ranked set of words from a 250-word vocabulary per word-onset-aligned window. Validation and test splits are drawn from sessions recorded on a separate day from all training data to prevent leakage.

## Method

The primary contribution is the dataset itself, but baseline models are provided for three tasks. Speech detection and phoneme classification use a lightweight SEANet-inspired 1D-CNN: 306→128 channels via a 7-kernel conv, two ResNet blocks (3-kernel + 1-kernel), a strided 50-kernel conv for temporal pooling, then a 7-kernel conv, flatten, and two 512-dim linear layers with dropout, trained with Adam (lr=1e-4) and cross-entropy loss. Word classification replicates Jayalath et al. (2025a): MEG windows pass through a spatial attention module (projecting 306 channels to 270-dim latent space using sensor x,y coordinates), dilated convolutions for temporal encoding, and a 16-layer bidirectional transformer (1024-dim, 16 heads, rotary positional embeddings) trained with a SigLIP contrastive loss against middle-layer T5 word embeddings. Across all tasks, the key empirical finding is that decoding accuracy improves approximately logarithmically with training data volume, and that averaging multiple instances of the same phoneme (simulating repeated BCI inputs) boosts balanced accuracy above 60% at 100 repetitions.

## Results

Across three decoding tasks, LibriBrain demonstrates strong baseline performance: speech detection achieves F1 of 0.899 and AUROC of 0.864; phoneme classification (39 ARPAbet classes) achieves micro F1 of 0.117 and micro AUROC of 0.636; and word classification (top-250 vocabulary) achieves Top-10 Balanced Accuracy of 0.3621. All metrics significantly exceed random baselines (p < 0.01). Performance improves approximately logarithmically with increasing training data volume across all three tasks, consistent with scaling laws observed in other ML domains.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LibriBrain outperforms the previous largest within-subject MEG dataset (Armeni et al., 2022) on word classification, achieving 0.3621 Top-10 Balanced Accuracy versus 0.3261 (p < 0.01, two-sample t-test). Scaling experiments (Figure 3c) further show that LibriBrain scales better than Armeni et al. at like-for-like training data volumes and continues to improve beyond the point where the Armeni dataset plateaus, owing to its ~5× larger data volume (52.32 hrs vs. ~10 hrs per subject). The paper also reports that LibriBrain is the best-performing dataset among existing large non-invasive speech datasets for this task. For speech detection and phoneme classification, the only direct comparison is against a frequency-matched random baseline, which LibriBrain's models significantly exceed on all reported metrics (F1, AUROC, Jaccard, Cross Entropy Loss).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 'deep within-subject' direction established by Armeni et al. (2022), which demonstrated that ~10 hours of MEG from a single participant outperforms broader multi-subject datasets at matched total data volume. Methodologically, the baselines adapt existing architectures: a SEANet-inspired CNN (Tagliasacchi et al., 2020) for speech detection and phoneme classification, and a transformer encoder with T5 embedding targets and SigLIP contrastive loss (Defossez et al., 2023; Jayalath et al., 2025a) for word classification. The key conceptual departure is scaling within-subject MEG data to over 50 hours—5× the previous deepest dataset—while maintaining the same single-narrator, single-genre stimulus design to control phonetic variance, analogous to single-speaker ASR corpora.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LibriBrain is likely to become the standard benchmark for non-invasive speech decoding, much as LibriSpeech became standard for ASR, given its scale, standardized splits, Python library, and public leaderboard infrastructure. The confirmed logarithmic scaling of decoding accuracy with training data volume provides a clear roadmap for the field: more within-subject data will continue to improve performance, but the absolute numbers remain modest (phoneme balanced accuracy ~4%, word top-10 accuracy ~36%), leaving a large gap to clinically useful brain-to-text systems. Key open questions include whether these scaling trends hold across subjects, whether listening-trained models transfer to covert or overt speech decoding, and whether the single-author, single-narrator design limits generalization to diverse linguistic content.
