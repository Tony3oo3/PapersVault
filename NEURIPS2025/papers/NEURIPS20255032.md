## Problem

Existing circuit discovery methods for language models predominantly use noising-based interventions, which fail to fully recover OR gates in the circuit, resulting in incomplete, non-transitive, and run-variable circuits that omit key mechanisms. The paper identifies that this incompleteness is fundamentally caused by the logical structure of circuits containing AND, OR, and ADDER gates, and that no single intervention strategy can recover all three gate types simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255032/cropped_page4_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on a Transformer computational graph (GPT-2 small) with paired clean and corrupted text inputs for a specified task (IOI, GT, or SA). It produces a circuit subgraph whose edges are explicitly partitioned into AND, OR, and ADDER gate categories via set operations between the Ns and Dn circuits, along with faithfulness (KL divergence, task accuracy) and completeness (performance drop after circuit removal) scores. A granularity alignment constraint requires the Ns and Dn circuits to contain approximately equal numbers of edges to avoid misclassifying ADDER edges as AND or OR edges.

## Method

The paper defines three logic gates based on how sender nodes affect a receiver node's output: AND (all senders required), OR (any sender suffices), and ADDER (each sender contributes independently). It proves that noising-based intervention (Ns) fully recovers AND and ADDER gates but only partially recovers OR gates, while denoising-based intervention (Dn) exhibits the complementary pattern. The proposed Ns+Dn framework runs both interventions in parallel on any existing circuit discovery algorithm (greedy search, linear estimation, or differentiable mask), then separates gates via set operations: C_Ns ∩ C_Dn = ADDER, C_Ns \ C_Dn = AND, C_Dn \ C_Ns = OR. This adds at most a 2× computational overhead and is compatible with all three method families. A misalignment score is introduced to verify that the Ns and Dn circuits are at comparable granularity, ensuring correct gate classification.

## Results

On the IOI task with GPT-2-small, the Ns+Dn framework improves completeness (measured as KL divergence after circuit removal) by 5–22% over Ns-only circuits across all three baseline methods at 5000 edges (e.g., EAP: 1.21→1.48, ACDC: 1.34→1.46, EdgePruning: 1.62→1.73). The reduction in circuit randomness (Hamming distance between independently discovered circuits) is substantial: at 5000 edges on IOI, EdgePruning shows a 509.5±164.7 reduction, ACDC 127±28.5, and EAP 14.5±6.9. Faithfulness is preserved: Ns+Dn circuits match Ns circuits in KL divergence and accuracy (C_Ns+Dn ≈ C_Ns > C_Dn), confirming that adding OR gate recovery does not sacrifice the model's ability to reproduce outputs in isolation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three circuit discovery families—ACDC (greedy search), EAP (linear estimation), and EdgePruning (differentiable mask)—each run under Ns-only versus the proposed Ns+Dn framework. EAP shows the largest completeness gap between Ns and Ns+Dn (e.g., +0.27 KL at 5000 edges on IOI) because linear estimation completely fails to detect any OR gate edges under Ns, making it the most incomplete baseline. ACDC and EdgePruning partially recover one OR edge under Ns (depending on search order or mask initialization), so their completeness improvement from Ns+Dn is more moderate (+0.12 and +0.11 KL respectively at 5000 edges). In terms of circuit stability, EdgePruning benefits most from Ns+Dn in reducing randomness (509.5±164.7 Hamming distance reduction at 5000 edges), followed by ACDC (127±28.5) and EAP (14.5±6.9). Faithfulness is maintained across all three methods: Ns+Dn circuits match Ns circuits in KL and accuracy, while Dn-only circuits are consistently worse, confirming that the AND gate recovery (which Ns handles but Dn does not) is essential for faithfulness.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Ns/Dn intervention framework from Heimersheim & Nanda [17], which established that noising-based and denoising-based interventions recover different subsets of circuit edges. It extends the circuit discovery literature (ACDC, EAP, EdgePruning) by showing that all existing methods, which rely solely on Ns, are fundamentally incomplete due to OR gates, and proposes a constant-overhead Ns+Dn combination to close this gap. The systematic decomposition of circuits into AND, OR, and ADDER gates is a new theoretical lens that unifies prior scattered observations about logical structure in circuits [16, 2] into a formal framework with provable faithfulness and completeness requirements (Corollary 1).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Ns+Dn framework offers a drop-in modification to any existing circuit discovery method that restores completeness without sacrificing faithfulness, which is practically important for interpretability researchers who need reliable, reproducible circuits. The graph study revealing that OR gates encode redundant backup paths while AND gates combine distinct functional requirements provides a structural vocabulary for understanding how language models organize computation. However, the evaluation is confined to GPT-2 small and three tasks, leaving open whether the gate decomposition and the 1:1 Ns/Dn edge-count alignment assumption hold at scale. The authors also acknowledge that other gate types (e.g., XOR) exist but are not captured by the simple intersection of C_Ns and C_Dn, limiting the framework's generality.
