## Problem

In massively parallel on-policy RL, the common practice of using short rollouts (K << H) to maximize the update-to-data ratio, combined with synchronous full-episode resets, causes each training batch to contain data from only a narrow temporal slice of the episode. This induces cyclical batch nonstationarity that destabilizes value estimation, triggers catastrophic forgetting, and prevents learning of long-horizon behaviors—yet this interaction between the data collection protocol and learning stability has been overlooked when adapting algorithms like PPO to GPU-parallel simulation.

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard state-vector observations from GPU-parallel simulation environments (ManiSkill3, IsaacGym) and produces trained continuous or discrete action policies. The key additional inputs are the task horizon H and rollout length K, which determine the staggering granularity (N_B ≈ H/K groups, each offset by j·K steps). The technique is algorithm-agnostic: it modifies only the environment reset/initialization protocol, leaving the policy gradient update (PPO, SAPG, etc.) unchanged, and requires no architectural modifications to the actor-critic networks.

## Method

Staggered resets desynchronize the effective starting points of N parallel environments across the task horizon before training begins. Environments are divided into N_B ≈ H/K groups, and each group j is advanced by j·K simulation steps (using random actions or the initial untrained policy) so that at any PPO update, the aggregated batch of N×K transitions spans the full range of task stages rather than a single K-step window. During training, environments that reach H elapsed steps or terminate early are reset to t=0 at scheduled 'reset gates' aligned with group boundaries, preserving the staggered coverage while minimizing unbatched reset calls. This yields a temporally diverse, more stationary data distribution within every gradient update, closely emulating the state visitation coverage of much longer rollouts. The approach is validated to be algorithm-agnostic (effective for both PPO and SAPG), scales better with increasing parallelism (avoiding the performance saturation seen with naive resets beyond ~1024 environments), and is most beneficial for tasks with long horizons, deterministic resets, and weak intrinsic skill gating.

## Results

On StackCube-v1 (H=100), staggered resets with K=8 achieve a final reward of 0.74±0.00 versus 0.62±0.01 for naive synchronous resets, and reach >75% success in 16.2M environment steps (16.6 min wall-clock) versus 35.8M steps (26.2 min) for naive — a 2.2× sample-efficiency and 1.6× wall-clock speedup. In the quantitative nonstationarity analysis (Appendix E), staggered resets reduce average catastrophic forgetting by 14× (0.015 vs. 0.21 relative accuracy drop) and keep value-function MSE below 2.5, whereas naive resets produce MSE spikes exceeding 80 at every mass-reset cycle. Across six ManiSkill3 PPO tasks and two SAPG AllegroKuka tasks, staggered resets consistently improve convergence speed, final success rate, and run-to-run variance, with the sole exception being MS-HumanoidWalk where natural desynchronization from stochastic falls makes the two methods comparable.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is the standard synchronous (naive) reset protocol, where all N parallel environments reset together after completing H steps. Staggered resets outperform naive resets on every evaluated task except MS-HumanoidWalk. On PPO/ManiSkill3 tasks (StackCube-v1, PushT, TwoRobotPushCube, AnymalC-Reach, Unitree G1 Transport Box), staggered resets yield substantially higher final success rates, faster convergence, and lower variance across 10 seeds; the gap is largest on long-horizon manipulation tasks (e.g., StackCube-v1: 0.74 vs. 0.62 final reward at K=8). On the SAPG/AllegroKuka dexterous manipulation tasks (AllegroKukaThrow, AllegroKukaRegrasping), staggered resets again produce dramatic gains in sample efficiency and final reward despite frequent early resets and domain randomization that partially desynchronize environments naturally. On MS-HumanoidWalk, both methods perform comparably because stochastic falls and shorter effective skill horizons induce natural desynchronization, reducing the severity of cyclical nonstationarity. In the scaling experiment (Figure 5), naive PPO's wall-clock convergence saturates or degrades beyond N≈1024–6144 environments, while staggered PPO continues to improve even beyond 6000 parallel environments.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on PPO (Schulman et al., 2017) and the massively parallel RL literature (IMPALA, A3C, PPO variants for GPU simulation), identifying a specific data-collection pathology—cyclical batch nonstationarity—that prior work on parallel RL overlooked when adopting short rollouts (K ≪ H) to maximize update-to-data ratios. While Rudin et al. (2022) and some ManiSkill implementations already employed a form of reset staggering in practice, this paper is the first to formally characterize the problem, analyze the conditions under which it is most severe (via toy environments), and demonstrate its algorithm-agnostic benefits across PPO and SAPG. The work is positioned as an environment-protocol modification rather than an algorithmic change, distinguishing it from prior efforts that focused on improving the RL algorithm itself for scalability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As GPU-accelerated simulation (IsaacGym, ManiSkill3, Brax) enables thousands of parallel environments, the short-rollout regime becomes the default operating point, making this technique broadly applicable to the current wave of sim-to-real robotics research. The finding that naive synchronous resets cause performance to *degrade* with increasing parallelism (beyond ~1024 environments) while staggered resets continue to improve is practically critical for practitioners scaling up training. Limitations include reduced benefit in locomotion tasks with natural desynchronization, the absence of formal theoretical guarantees, and the heuristic nature of the N_B granularity choice. Open questions include whether the technique interacts with off-policy methods, experience replay, or curriculum learning in ways that could further amplify or diminish its benefits.
