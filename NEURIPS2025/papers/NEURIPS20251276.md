## Problem

Computing exact data Shapley values for KNN models requires sorting all training points for each test point, yielding O(n_test · n log n) time that becomes quadratic when the test set size is proportional to the training set (as needed for stable valuations). This paper addresses the gap by exploiting the distance-based local structure of the data space to decompose the global valuation into smaller, localized computations, achieving provable speedups for a threshold-based KNN variant.

## Input / Output

The method takes a training dataset D of n points in R^d (features normalized to [0,1]), a test dataset D_test of n_test points (typically 0.2%–1% of |D|), and KNN hyperparameters K and threshold radius τ. It outputs exact data Shapley values for every training point, averaged over all test points. The threshold τ defines a ball around each test point beyond which training points have zero influence, and the ratio τ/d controls the effective neighborhood size.

## Method

The core mechanism is a landmark-based near neighbor search that partitions the data space via farthest-first traversal (FFT) into clusters whose maximum radius is bounded by τ. For each test point, the algorithm expands a rank-based ball in the landmark-sorted order until the triangle inequality certifies all remaining unvisited points lie beyond distance τ, then applies the recursive Shapley formula only to the collected subset S. Under 3-perturbation resilience for metric k-center clustering, this guarantees that all points within distance τ of a test point reside in the same cluster, yielding a runtime of O(n_L · n(d + log n) + n_test · s(d + log s)) where s is the largest cluster size. With n_L = √n and comparable cluster sizes, this improves the baseline from O(n²(d + log n)) to O(n^1.5(d + log n)), while the worst-case running time never exceeds the baseline.

## Results

Algorithm 1 achieves up to 25× speedup over the recursive KNN-SV baseline (e.g., 488.95 s vs. 12,289.43 s on poker) and up to 11× speedup over the TNN method (e.g., 488.95 s vs. 5,542.69 s on poker). Across all eight datasets, Algorithm 1 with τ/d = 0.05 consistently outperforms the baseline, with speedups ranging from ~1.8× (dota2) to ~25× (poker). The performance advantage grows with dataset size, and the worst-case running time of Algorithm 1 never exceeds that of the baseline even at τ/d = 0.2.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the recursive KNN-SV baseline (extending Jia et al. [11]), Algorithm 1 wins on all eight datasets, with speedups from ~1.8× (dota2: 249 s vs. 445 s) to ~25× (poker: 489 s vs. 12,289 s). Against the state-of-the-art TNN method (Wang et al. [14]), Algorithm 1 wins on five of eight datasets—most dramatically on poker (489 s vs. 5,543 s, an 11× advantage), covtype (1,919 s vs. 5,010 s), and skin (157 s vs. 229 s)—while TNN is faster on the three smaller or higher-dimensional datasets (magic, cifar10, dota2). The paper notes that Algorithm 1 excels on lower-dimensional datasets with well-defined cluster structure, where the landmark-based ball expansion prunes the search space most effectively. In the worst case (large τ/d), Algorithm 1's running time approaches but never exceeds the baseline, providing a robust fallback guarantee.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the KNN-SV framework of Jia et al. [11], which first provided an O(n log n) recursive formula for exact data Shapley values in KNN models, by introducing a localized computation strategy that exploits the threshold-based KNN (TKNN) variant from Wang et al. [14]. The key algorithmic novelty is a landmark-based near neighbor search that repurposes farthest-first traversal (FFT) from k-center clustering to partition the data space, drawing on perturbation resilience results from Balcan et al. [22] and Chekuri & Gupta [23] to prove that the search space per test point is bounded by cluster size under mild structural assumptions. Unlike prior KNN-SV methods that sort all n training points for each of n_test test points (yielding O(n²) total), this approach restricts computation to a localized ball around each test point, achieving a provable O(n^1.5) runtime when clusters are balanced.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The quadratic scaling of KNN-SV has been a practical bottleneck for data valuation on datasets with hundreds of thousands of points; the demonstrated 25× speedup (and 11× over TNN) makes exact Shapley computation feasible at scale for the TKNN setting. However, the perturbation resilience assumption required for the provable speedup is difficult to verify in practice, and the landmark-based search may degrade under the curse of dimensionality, limiting applicability to high-dimensional feature spaces. Open questions include extending the localization strategy to standard (non-threshold) KNN, regression tasks, and more general utility functions, as well as developing practical diagnostics for when the clustering structure assumption holds.
