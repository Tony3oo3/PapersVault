## Problem

Spiking Neural Networks offer energy-efficient, multiplication-free computation but have not been successfully applied to neural radiance field rendering. When binary SNNs are naively adapted to NeRF, the binary spike activation maps carry insufficient information capacity (only C×N bits vs. 32×C×N bits for real-valued ANNs), causing severe performance degradation (e.g., 11.93 vs. 31.15 average PSNR on synthetic scenes). This paper asks whether SNNs can match ANN-based NeRF quality while retaining their efficiency advantages.

## Input / Output

The model consumes 3D spatial coordinates p=(x,y,z) and viewing directions v=(θ,φ) sampled along camera rays, with 64 points for the coarse network and 128 for the fine network. Each spiking MLP layer processes inputs over T=2 timesteps using LIF neurons, and the temporal spike outputs are averaged before volumetric integration. The final output is a rendered 400×400 RGB image per novel view, produced by alpha-compositing the time-averaged density and color predictions across all sampled points on each ray.

## Method

Spik-NeRF replaces the standard binary LIF neuron with a ternary spike neuron that fires in three states {0,1,2} based on two thresholds (V_th and V_th+Δ_v), increasing theoretical information capacity by ~58.5% (from C×N to 1.585·C×N bits). At inference, a re-parameterization step shifts spikes to {-1,0,1} via an affine transform, converting weight-activation multiplications into additions and preserving the event-driven, multiplication-free property of SNNs. The model is directly trained (not converted from an ANN) using the Differentiable Spike surrogate gradient over 300K iterations with Adam. An isomorphic knowledge distillation loss further guides the spiking student by matching its density and color predictions to a pre-trained ANN-based NeRF teacher via MSE, with the ternary {0,1,2} activation being more compatible with this distillation than {-1,0,1} due to its ReLU-like non-negativity.

## Results

On the NeRF Synthetic dataset (8 scenes), Spik-NeRF achieves an average PSNR of 30.23, SSIM of 0.942, and LPIPS of 0.045 with only 2 timesteps, approaching the ANN-based NeRF baseline (31.15 PSNR, 0.953 SSIM, 0.032 LPIPS). On the NeRF Real-World dataset (8 scenes), Spik-NeRF reaches 26.26 PSNR, 0.817 SSIM, and 0.121 LPIPS, compared to 26.88 PSNR for the ANN baseline. The vanilla binary-spike Spiking NeRF baseline achieves only 28.95 PSNR (synthetic) and 25.54 PSNR (real-world), confirming the benefit of the ternary spike formulation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Spik-NeRF is compared against two external baselines and one internal ablation. Against the ANN-based NeRF [31], Spik-NeRF trails by 0.92 dB PSNR on synthetic scenes (30.23 vs. 31.15) and 0.62 dB on real-world scenes (26.26 vs. 26.88), while achieving 98.8% and 97.7% of the ANN's SSIM respectively — a notable gap given it uses only 2 timesteps and ternary spikes. Against Spiking-NeRF [27], an ANN-to-SNN conversion method requiring 8 timesteps, Spik-NeRF outperforms by a large margin: +18.30 dB PSNR on synthetic (30.23 vs. 11.93) and +13.17 dB on real-world (26.26 vs. 13.09), while using 4× fewer timesteps. The internal vanilla binary-spike baseline (Spiking NeRF) is outperformed by Spik-NeRF by 1.28 dB PSNR on synthetic and 0.72 dB on real-world, validating the ternary spike design. In SSIM and LPIPS, Spik-NeRF consistently narrows the gap to the ANN baseline compared to the binary variant, e.g., LPIPS drops from 0.066 to 0.045 (synthetic) and 0.162 to 0.121 (real-world).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the original NeRF framework [31], replacing its MLP layers with spiking neurons to create the first directly-trained SNN-based NeRF. It departs from prior SNN-NeRF efforts (Spiking-NeRF [27], Spiking NeRF [28], SpikingNerf [43]) that relied on ANN-to-SNN conversion, hybrid ANN-SNN architectures, or modified NeRF backbones (DVGO, TensoRF). The key conceptual departure is the entropy-based argument that binary spikes are fundamentally insufficient for NeRF's feature embeddings, motivating the ternary spike neuron and a train-infer re-parameterization that preserves multiplication-free inference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work demonstrates that SNNs can approach ANN-level rendering quality with only 2 timesteps, opening a path toward energy-efficient neural rendering on neuromorphic hardware. However, the paper does not discuss limitations (marked NA in the checklist), does not report error bars, and does not explore whether the approach extends to more modern NeRF variants (e.g., Instant-NGP, Mip-NeRF) or scales to larger/more complex scenes. Open questions include whether the ternary spike advantage persists at greater depths or with positional encoding, and whether the distillation dependency on a pre-trained ANN teacher is a practical bottleneck for deployment.
