## Problem

Existing methods for aligning diffusion models to rewards or composing multiple pretrained models rely on weighted sums with ad hoc hyperparameters, making it difficult to guarantee that all desired properties are simultaneously satisfied. When multiple competing rewards or models are involved, poorly chosen weights cause the result to overfit one objective while neglecting others, and there is no principled way to determine appropriate trade-offs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250628/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For alignment, the method takes a single pretrained diffusion model (Stable Diffusion v1.5), a set of differentiable reward functions (e.g., MPS, HPS, PickScore, ImageReward, aesthetic score), and user-specified constraint thresholds indicating minimum acceptable reward levels. For composition, it takes multiple pretrained models (e.g., LoRA variants finetuned on different rewards or text-to-image models conditioned on different prompts) and a text prompt. The output is a finetuned score function (via LoRA) that generates images satisfying all constraints simultaneously, with the optimal trade-off weights determined automatically by the dual variables rather than manually specified.

## Method

The paper formulates both alignment and composition as constrained distribution optimization problems: alignment minimizes reverse KL divergence to the pretrained model subject to expected reward constraints, while composition minimizes the maximum KL divergence to each pretrained model. Strong duality is established for both problems, yielding closed-form solutions in distribution space—a reward-tilted distribution for alignment and a tilted product of distributions for composition. Training proceeds via a primal-dual algorithm: the primal step minimizes the Lagrangian (using path-wise KL for alignment and a new point-wise KL formula for composition), while the dual step performs sub-gradient ascent on Lagrange multipliers that automatically determine optimal reward weights or composition weights. For composition, the primal step requires sampling from the tilted product distribution via annealed MCMC, and the point-wise KL is computed using a new formula (Lemma 2) that expresses it as a weighted sum of score differences over diffusion timesteps plus a discretization error.

## Results

For concept composition of two text-to-image diffusion models, the constrained approach achieves a minimum CLIP score of 22.9 and minimum BLIP score of 0.268, outperforming equal-weight composition (22.7 / 0.252) and combined prompting (22.1 / 0.204). In the alignment experiments, the constrained method achieves a 50% improvement in MPS reward satisfaction compared to equal-weight regularization, while simultaneously yielding a smaller KL divergence to the pretrained model. For product composition of multiple reward-finetuned models, equal weights cause drops of up to 30% in some reward scores, whereas constrained composition maintains proximity to each individual model and improves rewards across all models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its constrained approach against two baselines: (1) equal-weight composition/alignment, which uses fixed uniform weights across models or rewards, and (2) combined prompting, which concatenates both prompts into a single input. On the concept composition task (Table 1), constrained composition wins on both metrics: it improves minimum CLIP by 0.2 over equal weights (22.9 vs. 22.7) and by 0.8 over combined prompting (22.9 vs. 22.1), while improving minimum BLIP by 0.016 over equal weights (0.268 vs. 0.252) and by 0.064 over combined prompting (0.268 vs. 0.204). In the multi-reward alignment experiments, the constrained method outperforms equal-weight regularization by achieving a 50% improvement in MPS reward while producing a smaller KL divergence to the pretrained model, indicating less deviation. For product composition of multiple reward-finetuned LoRA models, equal weights cause up to 30% drops in individual reward scores, while the constrained approach finds optimal weights that preserve closeness to each model and improve all rewards simultaneously. The paper does not report a single unified benchmark table; most alignment results are presented as training trajectories and qualitative image comparisons rather than fixed-metric tables.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two established lines of diffusion model adaptation—reward-based alignment (DPOK [17], AlignProp [35]) and multi-model composition (Du et al. [14], Diffusion Soup [1], Superposition [40])—under a single constrained optimization framework, building on the constrained learning theory of Chamon and Ribeiro [6,7] and the authors' own prior work on constrained diffusion via dual training [22]. The key conceptual departure is replacing ad-hoc weighted sums of KL divergence and rewards (or weighted products of score functions) with explicit constraint thresholds, which yields closed-form solutions in distribution space and strong duality guarantees that weighted formulations lack. The DDIM score-matching parameterization [42,43] and path-wise KL divergence [17] are adopted as the computational substrate, while annealed MCMC [14] is repurposed for sampling from the tilted product distribution in composition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a principled, interpretable alternative to heuristic weight tuning in both alignment and composition, with the practical benefit that specifying reward thresholds is more intuitive than choosing regularization weights, and that constraint satisfaction is guaranteed rather than approximate. Theoretical contributions—closed-form optimal solutions (reward-tilted and product-tilted distributions), strong duality, and a tractable point-wise KL formula for diffusion models (Lemma 2)—provide a foundation for future work on convergence and sample complexity of the primal-dual algorithms. Open questions include scalability of MCMC-based composition to high-dimensional settings, extension to mixed alignment-composition constraints, and validation beyond image generation (e.g., video, audio, molecular design).
