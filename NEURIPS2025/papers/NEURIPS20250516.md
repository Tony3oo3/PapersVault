## Problem

Physics-driven deep learning (PD-DL) MRI reconstruction achieves state-of-the-art acceleration but requires raw k-space data for training, which is only accessible at specialized academic centers with vendor research agreements. Rural and under-resourced clinical sites can only export final DICOM images reconstructed via parallel imaging, creating a critical access gap that prevents fine-tuning or deployment of fast MRI where it is most needed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250516/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

CUPID takes as input a parallel imaging–reconstructed DICOM image (x_PI) at the target acceleration rate (e.g., R=4), which inherently contains noise and aliasing artifacts, along with the multi-coil encoding operator E_Ω (undersampling pattern Ω and coil sensitivity maps). The encoding operator is derived entirely from acquisition parameters and low-resolution calibration scans in DICOM format, eliminating any need for raw k-space. The output is a single reconstructed MRI image per slice, with the method operating on 320×320 2D slices (knee, brain) or 320×300×224 3D volumes (prospective brain study).

## Method

CUPID trains a VS-QP unrolled network (10 unrolls with a 15-block ResNet regularizer and CG-based data fidelity) using a novel two-term unsupervised loss that requires no raw k-space. The first term is a reweighted ℓ₁ compressibility loss over dual-tree complex wavelet transform (DTCWT) coefficients of the network output, adapted from prior work but reformulated to operate on the DICOM input rather than k-space. The second term enforces parallel imaging fidelity via perturbation equivariance: K carefully designed perturbations (randomly rotated/positioned letters, shapes, or card suits with varying intensities) are added to x_PI, and the network is penalized if its output does not shift by the same perturbation amount, preventing collapse to an all-zeros solution. The perturbations are constructed to avoid fold-over aliasing at the target acceleration rate, ensuring they remain resolvable by parallel imaging. This formulation enables both database-level training on 300 slices and zero-shot subject-specific fine-tuning on a single slice without any modification to the loss.

## Results

On four fastMRI datasets (coronal PD, coronal PD-FS knee; axial FLAIR, axial T2 brain) at R=4 equidistant undersampling, CUPID achieves PSNR of 38.82, 35.04, 36.49, and 35.31 dB respectively, with SSIM of 0.952, 0.880, 0.957, and 0.921. These results are within ~1.6 dB PSNR of supervised PD-DL (which requires raw k-space) while outperforming all methods that also lack raw k-space access. In the zero-shot subject-specific setting, CUPID closely matches ZS-SSDU quality despite not using raw data or self-validation masks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CUPID outperforms the parallel imaging baseline (CG-SENSE) by 3.3–5.5 dB PSNR and the compressed sensing baseline by 1.9–2.3 dB PSNR across all four datasets, confirming substantial artifact and noise reduction. Against the diffusion-based methods, CUPID beats ScoreMRI by 0.97–1.48 dB PSNR and DDS by 0.18–1.33 dB PSNR, with the largest gaps on the knee PD-FS and brain T2 datasets. CUPID also surpasses EI-trained PD-DL by 0.75–1.98 dB PSNR, despite EI having access to raw k-space during training. The closest competitors are supervised PD-DL and SSDU, which both require raw k-space; CUPID trails them by 1.62–0.64 dB PSNR (Supervised) and 0.82–0.55 dB PSNR (SSDU), a notable gap given CUPID's strictly more restricted input. In the zero-shot setting, CUPID matches ZS-SSDU in visual quality while eliminating the need for raw k-space and self-validation masks, and outperforms CS, CG-SENSE, ScoreMRI, and DDS on both artifact suppression and noise reduction.

## Relation to Prior Work

CUPID adapts the compressibility-inspired unsupervised loss from Alçalar et al. (ISBI 2024), which previously required raw k-space data for stabilization, by replacing that fidelity term with a novel parallel imaging consistency constraint built on perturbation equivariance. The equivariance framework is inspired by Chen et al.'s Equivariant Imaging (ICCV 2021), but CUPID introduces a new transformation group specifically tailored to parallel imaging in MRI, where perturbations are designed to avoid fold-over aliasing. The method departs from self-supervised approaches (SSDU) and diffusion-based generative priors (ScoreMRI, DDS) by operating entirely on artifact-laden DICOM images without any raw k-space access, positioning it as the first PD-DL training strategy compatible with standard clinical scanner exports.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CUPID addresses a concrete and significant access gap: rural and under-resourced MRI sites that lack vendor agreements for raw k-space export can now fine-tune or zero-shot train PD-DL reconstruction using only routine DICOM images, potentially democratizing fast MRI beyond specialized academic centers. Key limitations include the need for the encoding operator E_Ω (undersampling pattern and coil sensitivities, the latter requiring a separate calibration scan), performance degradation at very high acceleration (R=8), and zero-shot runtimes of ~7–8 minutes per slice. Open questions include robustness to vendor-specific filtering/processing applied to DICOMs, extension to 3D reconstruction, and whether the perturbation-equivariance principle generalizes to other inverse problems beyond MRI.
