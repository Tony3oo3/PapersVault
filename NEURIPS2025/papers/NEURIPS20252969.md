## Problem

Existing text-to-image systems are restricted to fixed text encoders and cannot produce explicit reasoning (chain-of-thought) for tasks requiring world knowledge, mathematical logic, or spatiotemporal perception. While unified multimodal LLMs address the multimodal input limitation, they still lack the ability to generate interpretable reasoning that guides image synthesis, and prior reasoning-driven generation methods (e.g., GoT) are constrained to spatial relations with inflexible CoT templates.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252969/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

MindOmni accepts text prompts, image-plus-text instructions, or reference image-plus-text instructions, and produces generated images (up to 1024×1024), text responses, explicit chain-of-thought reasoning in plain text (enclosed in think/answer tags), and edited images. For vision editing, VAE features from the reference image are concatenated with ViT semantic features to preserve low-level details such as texture, posture, and spatial layout that would otherwise be lost.

## Method

MindOmni builds a unified model from Qwen2.5-VL (vision language backbone) and OmniGen (decoder-only diffusion module) bridged by a two-layer LLM decoder connector, trained in three stages: (1) pretraining the connector with rectified-flow diffusion loss and KL distillation, (2) supervised fine-tuning on coarse-to-fine CoT instruction data, and (3) the proposed RGPO algorithm. RGPO extends GRPO by generating multimodal feedback signals from both image and text features, combining a format reward (CoT structure adherence) with a CLIP-based consistency reward (visual-linguistic alignment), and introducing two independent KL divergence regularizers—one for text rollouts and one for visual rollouts—to stabilize policy updates and mitigate knowledge forgetting in each modality separately.

## Results

On the WISE reasoning generation benchmark, MindOmni* (thinking mode) achieves a state-of-the-art overall score of 0.71, surpassing BAGEL (0.70), MetaQuery-XL (0.55), and FLUX (0.50). On GenEval, MindOmni* reaches 0.83 overall (vs. Janus-Pro 0.80, OmniGen baseline 0.70, FLUX 0.67), and on DPG-Bench it scores 83.0 (vs. MetaQuery-XL 82.1). For vision understanding, MindOmni achieves 51.6 on MMMU (boosting Janus-Pro by 10.6%) and 83.2 on MMBench (boosting MetaMorph by 9.8%), while maintaining near-parity with the Qwen2.5-VL backbone (51.1/83.4).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the WISE reasoning generation benchmark, MindOmni* (0.71) edges out the strongest unified baseline BAGEL (0.70) and substantially outperforms MetaQuery-XL (0.55), Janus-Pro (0.35), and all generation-only models (FLUX 0.50, PixArt 0.47, SDXL 0.43). On GenEval, MindOmni* (0.83) surpasses Janus-Pro (0.80) and the OmniGen baseline (0.70), while on DPG-Bench MindOmni (83.0) slightly leads MetaQuery-XL (82.1). For vision understanding, MindOmni (51.6 MMMU, 83.2 MMBench) significantly outperforms Janus-Pro (41.0, 79.2) and MetaMorph (41.8, 75.2), while remaining within 0.2–0.7 points of the Qwen2.5-VL backbone (51.1, 83.4), confirming that the RL stage preserves understanding capability. The ablation (Table 5) shows that removing the CoT SFT stage (Stage 2) before RL drops WISE from 0.60 to 0.49 and GenEval from 0.81 to 0.72, highlighting that supervised CoT initialization is essential for RGPO to be effective.

## Relation to Prior Work

MindOmni sits at the intersection of three research threads: unified MLLMs for joint understanding and generation (Janus, Show-o, OmniGen), RL-based reasoning in language models (DeepSeek-R1, GRPO from DeepSeekMath), and reasoning-driven image generation (GoT, PARM, RPG-DiffusionMaster). Its core architectural contribution is a lightweight two-layer decoder connector bridging Qwen2.5-VL and a decoder-only diffusion module (OmniGen), while its algorithmic contribution is RGPO — a modification of GRPO that replaces text-only reward signals with multimodal feedback (CLIP-based consistency reward) and introduces two independent KL regularizers for text and visual rollouts. Unlike GoT, which uses a fixed structured CoT template limited to spatial relations, MindOmni learns open-ended CoT reasoning via RL, extending the reasoning scope to mathematics, physics, chemistry, and temporal reasoning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to apply RL to reasoning generation in a unified MLLM, and the 71% WISE score (vs. 55% for MetaQuery-XL and 50% for FLUX) demonstrates that CoT-guided RL can substantially improve world-knowledge-driven image generation beyond what SFT alone achieves. The ablation showing that skipping CoT SFT before RL degrades both GenEval and WISE performance is a practically important finding for the training pipeline design. Key limitations include plain-text-only CoT (no multimodal reasoning traces), restriction to image (not video) generation, and reliance on CLIP for the consistency reward, which may not capture fine-grained semantic alignment. Open questions include whether multimodal CoT (interleaving images in the reasoning chain) would further improve performance, and whether the RL gains transfer to other generation modalities.
