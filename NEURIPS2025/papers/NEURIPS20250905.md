## Problem

Existing 3D visual grounding benchmarks are predominantly object-centric, evaluating only the localization of named objects within scenes. Grounding free-form referring expressions to regions beyond objects—unoccupied space for placing new items, functional areas for activities, or fine-grained object parts—remains entirely unexplored. This paper asks whether current LLMs, MLLMs, and 3D grounding specialist models can ground expressions to precise 3D positions and dimensions across four hierarchical levels of granularity.

## Input / Output

The benchmark takes a free-form natural language referring expression plus a 3D scene graph (ground-truth object bounding boxes and Qwen2.5-VL-generated captions) as textual input; MLLMs additionally receive a bird's-eye-view image and eight uniformly sampled video frames with object markers, while 3D grounding specialist models receive point clouds. The output is a single 3D bounding box parameterized as [center_x, center_y, center_z, size_x, size_y, size_z]. Evaluation uses a level-adaptive IoU: 2D horizontal-plane IoU for area-level (where vertical extent is inherently ambiguous), 2D IoU with a 0.05 m thin-axis threshold for objects with small dimensions along one axis, and standard 3D IoU otherwise, reported at k=0.25.

## Method

The core contribution is Anywhere3D-Bench, a dataset of 2,886 expression–3D bounding box pairs spanning four grounding levels (area: 189, space: 1,209, object: 954, part: 534) across 276 scenes from ScanNet, MultiScan, 3RScan, and ARKitScenes. Referring expressions are generated by GPT-4o conditioned on scene graphs (following SceneVerse) with level- and type-specific prompts, then iteratively refined and verified by human annotators using a custom 3D bounding box editor with a distance measurement tool. The space level is subdivided into five expression types (size, distance, situation, commonsense, trajectory) and the part level into three (movement, relationship, function), each targeting distinct spatial reasoning capabilities. The paper evaluates three model families—text-only LLMs, vision-augmented MLLMs, and specialized 3D grounding models—under a unified protocol where ground-truth object locations are provided to decouple detection from grounding. To probe improvement potential, the authors introduce two input enhancements: key video frame selection for visual perception and BEV axis overlay plus object orientation discretization for relational reasoning, both of which yield modest gains but leave a large gap to human performance (95% overall vs. 38.9% for the best model, o4-mini).

## Results

On Anywhere3D-Bench (Acc@0.25IoU), the best-performing model o4-mini (MLLM, thinking) achieves 76.19% on area-level, 23.00% on space-level, 55.82% on object-level, and 31.46% on part-level grounding, for an overall accuracy of 38.90%. Space-level and part-level tasks are the most challenging, with even the strongest model falling far short of human performance (92.00% and 97.00% respectively). 3D visual grounding specialist models (e.g., Chat-Scene at 22.90% overall, PQ3D at 16.68%) consistently underperform MLLMs, particularly on space-level tasks where they score only 6.55–8.36%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

o4-mini (MLLM, thinking) is the overall best model at 38.90% Acc@0.25IoU, outperforming GPT-4.1 (MLLM, non-thinking) by 3.00 points overall and by 3.97 points on space-level (23.00% vs. 19.03%) and 5.61 points on part-level (31.46% vs. 25.85%). Among LLMs, GPT-4.1 (32.34%) and o4-mini (32.80%) lead, with DeepSeek-R1-671B (30.49%) close behind; open-source Qwen2.5-72B trails at 19.90%. MLLMs consistently outperform their LLM counterparts when visual inputs (BEV + video frames) are added: GPT-4.1 gains 3.56 points overall, o4-mini gains 6.10 points, and Qwen2.5-VL-72B gains 6.14 points, with the largest gains on object-level (+8.19% avg) and part-level (+4.76% avg). 3D visual grounding specialist models (Chat-Scene 22.90%, Grounded 3D-LLM 20.10%, PQ3D 16.68%, 3D-VisTA 16.26%) are substantially outperformed by all MLLMs and most LLMs, with the gap most pronounced on space-level (6.55–8.36% vs. 17–23% for top MLLMs). Notably, GPT-4.1 (MLLM) achieves the highest area-level score (81.48%), surpassing o4-mini (76.19%), suggesting that non-thinking models can leverage commonsense reasoning for functional-area identification without the overhead of chain-of-thought.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 3D visual grounding task, previously confined to object-level localization (ScanRefer, Nr3D, Sr3D), to a four-level hierarchy: area, space, object, and part. It builds on SceneVerse for scene graph generation and adopts GPT4Scene's visual input protocol (bird's-eye view + video frames) for MLLM evaluation. The key departure is the introduction of space-level grounding—localizing referring expressions to unoccupied regions in 3D space—which no prior benchmark addresses, and a more open-ended part-level formulation compared to SceneFun3D's predefined functional elements.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals a stark capability gap: even o4-mini drops from 55.82% on object-level to 23.00% on space-level grounding, and specialized 3D visual grounding models (trained on point clouds) underperform general MLLMs, particularly on space-level tasks. This challenges the assumption that 3D-native architectures are necessary for spatial reasoning and highlights that interpreting 3D spatial relationships from 2D visual inputs remains a fundamental bottleneck. The substantial gap to human performance (95% overall) and the limited gains from spatial/visual input enhancements suggest that closing this gap will require more than incremental prompting improvements, pointing toward the need for genuinely new spatial reasoning capabilities.
