## Problem

Volumetric novel view synthesis methods (e.g., 3DGS) produce semi-transparent, geometrically inaccurate reconstructions on flat textureless surfaces like walls and ceilings, while surface reconstruction methods fix geometry at the cost of visual fidelity. No prior approach jointly optimizes planar constraints during photometric reconstruction, instead relying on post-hoc plane detection or hard-to-tune regularizers that fail when planar assumptions are violated.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255190/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes N posed RGB images (downsampled 2× for ScanNet++ training), per-view binary segmentation masks for M planar surfaces (generated by PlaneRecNet + SAMv2 video propagation), and an optional SfM sparse point cloud for initialization. It produces a hybrid scene of 2D planar Gaussians and 3D freeform Gaussians, from which novel view renderings, expected-ray-termination depth maps, and per-plane meshes (via ray-plane intersection, voxel downsampling, Marching Squares, and ear-clipping triangulation) can be extracted. The method operates without requiring a specific camera model, generalizing across iPhone and DSLR captures.

## Method

The core contribution is a hybrid 2D/3D Gaussian representation where flat surfaces are modeled by 2D Gaussians rigidly locked to learned planes (parameterized by 3D origin and normal), while the rest of the scene uses standard 3D Gaussians. Training proceeds in two phases: a 3DGS warm-up (3500 iterations), followed by iterative planar rounds where RANSAC fits planes to Gaussians within semantic masks, inliers are snapped to 2D, and block-coordinate descent alternates 10 iterations of plane-parameter optimization with 100 iterations of joint Gaussian optimization. A stochastic planar relocation mechanism (governed by a Bernoulli distribution over perpendicular and parallel distances) densifies planes by migrating nearby freeform Gaussians onto them, addressing the slow densification of compact-support planar Gaussians. The loss combines photometric L1, a mask consistency term that renders binarized planar/3D Gaussians, total depth variation, and scale/opacity regularizers.

## Results

On ScanNet++ (dense iPhone captures), the method achieves the best depth accuracy across all baselines: RMSE 0.27, MAE 0.18, AbsRel 0.10, and δ<1.25 of 0.88, while maintaining near-parity rendering quality with fully 3D methods (PSNR 27.01 vs. 27.09 for 3DGS and 27.23 for 3DGS-MCMC). On the sparser ScanNetv2, it further improves to RMSE 0.40 and PSNR 21.75, outperforming both 3DGS-MCMC (RMSE 0.46, PSNR 20.18) and 2DGS (RMSE 0.60, PSNR 21.44) on both depth and image metrics. The method uses 2.43M primitives with 27.8% being planar Gaussians, and on ETH3D sparse-view scenes it achieves PSNR 18.72–22.57, surpassing all baselines including GOF and DNSplatter.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against fully 3D baselines (3DGS, 3DGS-MCMC), the method achieves substantially better depth (RMSE 0.27 vs. 0.44/0.49) at a negligible PSNR cost (27.01 vs. 27.09/27.23), confirming that planar constraints improve geometry without sacrificing photometric quality. Against 2D surface methods (2DGS, PGSR, RaDe-GS), it wins on depth by a wide margin (RMSE 0.27 vs. 0.35–0.65) while recovering the rendering quality that surface methods sacrifice (PSNR 27.01 vs. 25.56–25.78 for 2DGS/PGSR, and 20.13 for RaDe-GS). On the sparser ScanNetv2, the advantage over 3DGS-MCMC widens in both depth (RMSE 0.40 vs. 0.46) and image quality (PSNR 21.75 vs. 20.18), and it also beats 2DGS on both metrics. For planar mesh extraction, the method outperforms PlanarRecon on both DSLR (Chamfer 12.12 vs. 37.96) and iPhone (F1 50.24 vs. 36.93), and beats AirPlanes on DSLR (Chamfer 12.12 vs. 26.60) while being competitive on iPhone. On ETH3D sparse-view scenes, it surpasses GOF and DNSplatter in both rendering (PSNR up to 22.57) and mesh quality (Chamfer 0.148–0.406 vs. 0.21–0.65). The key trade-off is a ~0.2 dB PSNR gap versus 3DGS-MCMC, which the authors attribute to constrained geometry preventing view-dependent overfitting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two well-established lines of work: volumetric 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS, 3DGS-MCMC) for high-fidelity rendering, and 2D surface reconstruction (2DGS, PGSR) for geometrically accurate planar surfaces. It departs from prior hybrid approaches (Kim & Lim's mesh+3DGS, Zanjani et al.'s tree-structured planar Gaussians) by using a simpler mixture of 2D and 3D Gaussians that remains fully compatible with the standard 3DGS rasterization pipeline. Unlike post-hoc plane detection methods (PlanarRecon, AirPlanes) or regularizer-based approaches (Manhattan world, plane normal consistency), the method jointly detects and optimizes planes end-to-end via RANSAC fitting and block-coordinate descent, allowing geometry to benefit from planar constraints during training rather than as a fixed penalty.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The hybrid 2D/3D representation offers a practical, drop-in solution to the well-known flat-surface artifact in 3DGS-based reconstruction, achieving SOTA depth accuracy on ScanNet++ and ScanNetv2 while preserving rendering quality comparable to pure 3D methods. The zero-shot planar mesh extraction that generalizes across camera modalities (DSLR and iPhone) without retraining addresses a real deployment limitation of prior plane-detection methods. Open questions include whether the dependence on external 2D semantic masks (SAMv2 + PlaneRecNet) can be eliminated, whether stronger appearance models (beyond weak spherical harmonics) would further reduce spurious geometry, and whether the RANSAC-based plane initialization can be made more efficient to reduce the ~1.5× training overhead over 3DGS-MCMC.
