## Problem

Multi-modal image generation models (e.g., GPT-4o, Gemini 2.0 Flash) are currently evaluated by disjoint toolkits: text-to-image benchmarks that ignore image conditioning, and customization benchmarks that lack compositional semantic assessment. No existing benchmark unifies both tasks under a single framework with fine-grained, interpretable metrics that capture prompt-image alignment beyond coarse prompt-following scores.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254818/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark consumes 4,850 densely annotated text prompts (covering compositionality, style, realism, and common sense) paired with 1,750 multi-view reference images across 386 subject groups (animals, humans, objects, styles). Evaluation produces a three-level score vector: low-level metrics (CLIP-Text/CLIP-Image/DINOv2/CUTE for identity, PAL4VST for artifacts), a mid-level Aspect Matching Score computed via VQA over aspect-specific question-answer pairs, and high-level metrics (Aesthetic, HPSv2, PickScore). The AMS specifically leverages Qwen2.5-VL-72B as a VQA oracle to answer aspect-level questions about each generated image, yielding a per-aspect interpretable alignment score.

## Method

The core technical contribution is the Aspect Matching Score (AMS), a VQA-based mid-level metric that decomposes prompt-image alignment into four fine-grained aspects (Objects, Relations, Attributes, Counting). For each prompt, GPT-4o extracts aspect phrases via FineMatch-style graph parsing, then generates corresponding VQA pairs through in-context learning with manually curated exemplars. At evaluation time, Qwen2.5-VL-72B answers each question about the generated image, and AMS is the proportion of exactly correct answers. The benchmark data is curated by extracting 207 frequent entities from existing T2I datasets, generating diverse prompts via eight GPT-4o instruction templates (controlling structure, style, and semantic complexity), and collecting 3–5 multi-view object-centric images per entity from Pexels. The framework is validated with 32,400 human ratings from over 8,000 AMT workers, showing AMS achieves the highest Spearman correlation (ρ=0.699) with human judgment among all tested VQA-based metrics.

## Results

On the T2I task (Table 1), HiDream-I1-Full (17B) achieves the best overall performance with AMS 89.65, human rating 83.18, and PickScore 0.450, followed by FLUX.1-dev (12B) at AMS 84.44 and SD 3.5-large at AMS 85.33. On the customization task (Table 2), GPT-4o leads in semantic alignment (AMS 90.90) and human preference (PickScore 0.550), while DreamBooth dominates identity preservation (CLIP-I 88.76, DINOv2 38.96). The proposed Aspect Matching Score (AMS) achieves the highest Spearman correlation with human judgments (ρ=0.699), outperforming DSG (ρ=0.692), VQ2 (ρ=0.399), and BLIPVQA (ρ=0.147) across 32.4k human ratings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In the T2I task, HiDream-I1-Full (17B) outperforms all other models across nearly every metric, with a 5.2-point AMS advantage over the next-best FLUX.1-dev (84.44) and a 6.1-point human-rating gap. FLUX.1-dev and SD 3.5-large are closely competitive (AMS 84.44 vs. 85.33), while GPT-4o and Gemini-2.0-Flash show strong human-preference scores (81.02 and 81.98) but trail in AMS. Among autoregressive models, Janus-Pro-7B (AMS 85.35) is competitive, but JanusFlow suffers severe artifact issues (PAL4VST 365.66 vs. ~2–15 for diffusion models). In the customization task, GPT-4o achieves the highest AMS (90.90) and PickScore (0.550), far exceeding OmniGen (73.52) and MS-Diffusion (71.40) in semantic alignment, yet DreamBooth remains the strongest in identity preservation (CLIP-I 88.76, DINOv2 38.96, CUTE 79.78), outperforming GPT-4o (CLIP-I 75.15, DINOv2 25.17) by a wide margin. RealCustom and OmniGen rank second in generation quality but show unsatisfactory identity scores, while BLIP-Diffusion and IP-Adapter-XL lag in both semantic alignment (AMS 41.59 and 51.10) and overall quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously disjoint evaluation paradigms—text-to-image benchmarks (PartiPrompts, Gecko, T2I-CompBench++, GenEval, GenAI-Bench) that assess compositional semantics without image conditioning, and customization benchmarks (DreamBench++, DreamBooth evaluations) that focus on identity preservation but lack fine-grained semantic metrics—into a single multi-modal framework. The proposed Aspect Matching Score (AMS) builds directly on FineMatch's aspect graph parsing for fine-grained text-image alignment and extends the VQA-based evaluation lineage (TIFA, DSG, VQ2, BLIPVQA) by generating aspect-specific QA pairs validated by humans. The three-level (low/mid/high) scoring structure is a novel organizational contribution that addresses the gap where T2I metrics overlook visual fidelity and customization metrics overlook compositional semantics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MMIG-Bench provides the first unified evaluation platform where 17 state-of-the-art models (spanning diffusion, autoregressive, and API-based architectures) can be compared side-by-side on both pure T2I and image-conditioned generation, enabling insights into how architecture and scale affect different quality dimensions. A key open question it surfaces is the identity-semantic tradeoff: GPT-4o achieves the best semantic alignment (AMS 90.90) yet fails at identity preservation worse than early encoder-based models, suggesting that frontier multimodal models have not yet solved reference-faithful generation. The authors note that human ratings do not yet cover visual quality, and the benchmark's 207-entity scope, while broad, may not capture long-tail or culturally specific content. As a comprehensive, openly released benchmark with 32k human ratings, it is likely to become a standard reference for multi-modal image generation research.
