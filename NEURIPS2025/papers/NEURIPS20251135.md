## Problem

Masking diffusion, which uses the simplest possible forward process (uniformly replacing tokens with a mask), consistently outperforms discrete diffusion models with structured forward processes that incorporate inductive biases. No prior work explained why this trivial process dominates, leading to a mistaken belief that masking is optimal and stalling progress on forward-process design. The paper identifies the missing ingredient: masking diffusion implicitly conditions on the jump schedule (when transitions occur), while other models must learn both when and where to transition.

## Input / Output

The model takes a noised discrete sequence x_t (e.g., 32×32×3 CIFAR-10 pixels with B=256 values, UniRef50 protein sequences with B=31 tokens, or LM1B text with B=30522 tokens) together with a per-dimension event-count vector s_t ∈ ℕ^D recording how many corruption events have struck each position. It outputs a prediction of the clean sequence x_0. Unlike classical discrete diffusion which conditions on a scalar time t, SCUD conditions on the full D-dimensional schedule, which is injected via FiLM layers at every network layer. For large-vocabulary language, the forward process uses a sparse 10-nearest-neighbour graph over the top 2000 tokens to keep K^{s_t^d} computationally tractable.

## Method

SCUD (Schedule Conditioned Diffusion) decomposes the discrete diffusion ELBO into a 'where to transition' term and a 'when to transition' term, then eliminates the latter by conditioning the backward process on the known event schedule S from the forward process. The forward process is parameterized as a continuous-time Markov chain with infinitesimal generator L = r(K−I), where K is a stochastic matrix encoding the inductive bias and r is the event rate; a parameter γ ∈ (0,1] controls how much schedule information is baked in (γ=1−1/D with uniform L recovers masking diffusion exactly, γ→0 recovers classical SEDD/τLDR). The backward model q_θ predicts the previous state at each event given the current state and the per-dimension event count s_t, trained with a weighted cross-entropy loss (Eqn. 6) where each dimension d is weighted by s_t^d · β_t / ∫₀ᵗ β_s ds. In practice, the scalar time embedding in standard architectures is replaced by FiLM layers that inject the D-dimensional s_t vector, adding no extra parameters. This framework unifies masking diffusion and classical discrete diffusion as special cases and enables structured forward processes (Gaussian for images, BLOSUM for proteins, nearest-neighbour graphs for language) to finally outperform masking.

## Results

On LM1B (language), SCUD with a nearest-neighbour graph forward process achieves 37.63 perplexity, outperforming SCUD with masking (37.82) and substantially beating D3PM masking (76.9*, different tokenizer). On UniRef50 (proteins), SCUD BLOSUM achieves 37.63 perplexity versus 76.9* for D3PM masking and 149.5* for D3PM graph. With pre-trained ESM2 weights on proteins, SCUD BLOSUM reaches 10.04 perplexity, beating DPLM masking (10.61) and classical BLOSUM (10.34). On CIFAR-10 (images), SCUD with a Gaussian structured forward process achieves state-of-the-art bits-per-dimension among discrete diffusion models, outperforming both masking and classical structured diffusion.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SCUD substantially outperforms D3PM (Austin et al., NeurIPS 2021) across all three modalities: on LM1B, SCUD masking (37.82) and SCUD graph (37.63) both far exceed D3PM masking (76.9*, different tokenizer), and on UniRef50 proteins SCUD BLOSUM (37.63) beats D3PM masking (76.9*) by a factor of ~2. On CIFAR-10, SCUD with a Gaussian forward process outperforms both τLDR (Campbell et al., NeurIPS 2022) and MD4 (Shi et al., 2024) in bits-per-dimension, achieving state-of-the-art among discrete diffusion models. For protein generation with pre-trained ESM2 weights, SCUD BLOSUM (10.04 perplexity) beats DPLM (Wang et al., ICML 2024) masking (10.61) and classical BLOSUM diffusion (10.34). The key finding is that in classical discrete diffusion, masking consistently outperforms structured processes (e.g., D3PM graph at 149.5* vs. masking at 76.9* on LM1B), but SCUD reverses this: once schedule conditioning is applied, structured forward processes (graph, BLOSUM, Gaussian) consistently beat their masking counterparts, unlocking the inductive biases that were previously wasted.

## Relation to Prior Work

SCUD unifies and extends the continuous-time discrete diffusion framework established by τLDR (Campbell et al., 2022) and SEDD (Luo et al., 2022), which parameterize the backward process via an infinitesimal generator learned end-to-end. The key departure is that SCUD conditions the backward process on the forward process's known event schedule, so the model only learns where to transition rather than both when and where. This single modification provably recovers masking diffusion (Shi et al., 2024) as a special case when the forward process is uniform, and recovers classical discrete diffusion (SEDD, τLDR) in the limit of no schedule conditioning, while also enabling structured forward processes (Gaussian for images, BLOSUM for proteins, nearest-neighbour graphs for language) that were previously outperformed by masking.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper resolves a long-standing empirical puzzle in discrete diffusion—why the simplest masking forward process consistently outperformed structured alternatives—by showing the advantage comes from implicit schedule conditioning rather than from masking being inherently optimal. This reframing opens a design space where inductive biases in the forward process and schedule conditioning are complementary rather than competing, yielding state-of-the-art ELBOs on CIFAR-10 and UniRef50. Open questions include how much information can be packed into the schedule S before the denoising task becomes intractable, whether the benefits transfer to sampling quality (the paper focuses on likelihood), and how to extend the framework to continuous diffusion where the analogue of S (distance traveled) has non-convergent marginals.
