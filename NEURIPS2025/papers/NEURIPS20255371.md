## Problem

While the asymptotic arm-pulling behavior of the canonical UCB algorithm has been precisely characterized, the analogous analysis for the variance-aware UCB-V algorithm remained unexplored. The incorporation of variance estimates introduces a new quantity beyond reward gaps that governs arm-pulling rates, and it was unclear whether UCB-V retains the stability properties of canonical UCB or exhibits fundamentally different behavior. Additionally, the best-known regret bound for UCB-V did not account for the optimal arm's variance, making it conservative in regimes where the optimal arm has high variance.

## Input / Output

The algorithm operates on K arms with unknown reward distributions supported on [0,1], parameterized by means μ_a and variances σ_a², over a time horizon T with exploration coefficient ρ > 1. The analysis allows Δ, σ₁, σ₂ to be T-dependent, which is essential for capturing the phase-transition regime. The key output is a deterministic fixed-point equation (Eq. 6) whose unique solution n_{1,T}^* predicts the optimal arm's pulling count, along with high-probability confidence regions for n_{a,T} and a refined regret bound of form O(σ₂²/(σ₁∨σ₂) · √(T log T)).

## Method

The core technique is a unified concentration result (Proposition 1) that sandwiches a variance-aware ratio involving φ_{1,T} = (σ̄₁ ∨ n_{1,T}^{-1/2})/√n_{1,T} between (1±ε)⁴, where ε → 0 at rate O(log log T / log T). This yields a deterministic fixed-point equation f(φ) = 1 whose solution characterizes asymptotic arm-pulling behavior. A perturbation analysis (Lemma 1) then shows the solution is stable unless σ₁ = o(σ₂) and σ̄₂²/(TΔ̄₂²) → 1 simultaneously, at which point a phase transition occurs: the optimal arm's pulling count shifts from Ω(T) to O(√T/σ₂). The instability at the critical point is rigorously established via a Bernoulli bandit construction and a time-uniform anti-concentration argument using Donsker's principle. As an application, the high-probability arm-pulling bounds yield a refined regret of O(σ₂²/(σ₁∨σ₂) · √(T log T)), which is the first to reveal the effect of the optimal arm's variance and is shown optimal via a matching lower bound.

## Results

The paper establishes that UCB-V achieves a refined worst-case regret of O((σ₂ ∧ 16σ₂²/σ₁)·√(ρT log T)) + √(ρ log T) (Theorem 2), improving upon the previously known O(σ₂√(T log T)) bound when the optimal arm's variance σ₁ exceeds the suboptimal arm's variance σ₂. A matching lower bound (Theorem 3) confirms optimality. The asymptotic analysis reveals a phase transition in the optimal arm's pulling count n_{1,T}: when σ₁ = o(σ₂), the pulling rate shifts from Ω(T) (linear) to O(√T/σ₂) (sublinear) at the critical point Λ_T = σ₂√(ρ log T)/(√T·Δ) = 1, and an unstable instance exists at this transition (Proposition 2). In the homogeneous variance regime (σ₁, σ₂ = Ω(1)), the small-gap arm-pulling allocation is n_{1,T} ~ σ₁²/(σ₁²+σ₂²)·T, generalizing the canonical UCB's equal T/2 split.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares UCB-V against the canonical UCB (Auer, 2002) in arm-pulling behavior: while canonical UCB is stable for all gap values Δ > 0 (as shown in [21, 23]), UCB-V exhibits instability in the heterogeneous variance regime σ₁ = o(σ₂) at the critical point Λ_T = 1, where the optimal arm's pulling count fluctuates between O(√T log T) and Ω(T/√log T) with constant probability (Proposition 2). In terms of regret, the paper's bound O(σ₂²/σ₁ · √T) in the σ₂ = o(σ₁) regime strictly improves upon the previous best-known UCB-V regret O(σ₂√(T log T)) from Audibert et al. (2009) [4], and surpasses all other known variance-aware bandit algorithms [41, 40, 33, 9, 10] which achieve only O(σ₂√T) in the two-armed setting. The improvement is first to reveal the effect of the optimal arm's variance σ₁ on regret, which was previously unaccounted for. In the small-σ₁ regime (σ₁ = o(σ₂)), the new bound recovers the classical O(σ₂√T) rate, so no degradation occurs. The K-armed extension (Theorem 5) gives O(√(Σ_{a≥2} σ_a²) ∧ (Σ_{a≥2} σ_a²/σ₁) · √(T log T)), improving upon the previous O(√(KT log T)) worst-case bound.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the precise asymptotic characterization of arm-pulling rates, recently developed for the canonical UCB algorithm by Kalvit & Zeevi (2021) and Khamaru & Zhang (2024), to the variance-aware UCB-V algorithm introduced by Audibert et al. (2007, 2009). The analysis framework builds directly on the concentration and perturbation techniques from [23], but must handle the additional complexity introduced by variance estimates in the UCB bonus. The refined regret bound departs from all prior variance-aware regret analyses (which achieve O(σ₂√T)) by revealing a dependence on the optimal arm's variance σ₁, a quantity previously unexploited in worst-case guarantees.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The discovery that UCB-V can exhibit instability in arm-pulling rates (unlike canonical UCB, which is always stable) has direct implications for post-policy statistical inference: the standard Z-statistic CLT may fail for data collected by UCB-V in the critical regime, necessitating new inference methods. The refined regret bound O(σ₂²/(σ₁∨σ₂)·√T) with a matching lower bound establishes the first variance-adaptive worst-case guarantee that accounts for the optimal arm's variance, a result previously unknown for any variance-aware algorithm. Open questions include extending the stability characterization to the general σ₁=o(σ₂) regime, refining the K-armed stability condition, and developing new variance-aware algorithms with stronger stability guarantees.
