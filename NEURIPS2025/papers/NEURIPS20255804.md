## Problem

LM-based embodied agents in dynamic, resource-constrained environments cannot rely on online symbolic planners or large-scale multi-step inference due to latency and connectivity limits. Existing agentic frameworks incur heavy computational overhead, neurosymbolic methods require continuous rule-base updates and suffer from increasing solve times, and memory-augmented LMs lack the procedural structure needed for complex multi-step embodied tasks. The paper asks whether multi-step symbolic reasoning can be compiled into single-step LM inference that remains adaptive to changing environments.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255804/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At training time, the agent receives symbolically defined problem instances (PDDL-style observations and goals) paired with domain knowledge (action rules) and plans generated by a symbolic planner (e.g., Fast Downward). At test time, the agent receives only the current observation and goal with no access to any symbolic tool, and must produce a complete action plan in a single inference step. The setting is continual: the agent solves a sequence of tasks where both the goal and the transition function may vary, and it receives only binary task-level success/failure feedback between tasks with no gradient updates.

## Method

NeSyPr performs neurosymbolic proceduralization in two phases inspired by ACT theory's knowledge compilation. During training (compositional NeSy procedure learning), a symbolic tool generates task-specific plans whose implicit production rules are encoded via vector quantization into discrete procedure-units stored in a learnable procedure-book; these units are composed into runtime procedures that are injected into the LM's decoder blocks through a gated cross-attention mechanism, with the procedure-book updated via EMA. During testing (NeSy procedure contrastive planning), the agent maintains two procedure banks labeled by environmental feedback (success/failure), reconstructs positive and negative versions of the current runtime procedure by similarity matching, and applies contrastive decoding that reshapes the next-token distribution by subtracting the log-probability under failure-conditioned procedures from that under success-conditioned procedures, thereby suppressing failure patterns without any online symbolic guidance.

## Results

On closed-loop continual embodied tasks, NESYPR achieves 61.1% CSR on VirtualHome unseen tasks and 59.7% CSR on ALFWorld unseen tasks, outperforming the strongest baselines (DT-Mem at 48.7% and Optimus-2 at 49.1%) by 12.4% and 10.6% respectively. On PDDLGym open-loop tasks, NESYPR reaches 65.2% CSR on Minecraft and 73.5% on Rearrangement, surpassing the best baseline LongMem by 13.6% and 15.3%. In the proceduralization analysis, NESYPR with a 3B LM achieves 83.6% CSR with 3.5s latency, outperforming PlaSma (80.5% with 8B LM, 18.4s) and reducing inference latency by over 90% compared to BoT (75.1s with 70B LM).

## Baselines & Comparisons

NESYPR outperforms all 17 baselines across every benchmark and evaluation split. Against single-step planners (ZSP, RAP, LLM-Planner), NESYPR achieves 13–65% higher CSR on unseen tasks, as these methods lack structured multi-step reasoning. Agentic workflows (CoT, ToT, GoT, ReAct, Reflexion) improve over single-step methods but still trail NESYPR by 25–55% CSR on unseen tasks, reflecting their reliance on iterative large-model inference without procedural structure. Among memory-augmented LMs, NESYPR surpasses the strongest embodied baselines DT-Mem (48.7% → 61.1% CSR on VirtualHome unseen) and Optimus-2 (49.1% → 59.7% on ALFWorld unseen) by 12.4% and 10.6% respectively. In the proceduralization comparison (Table 3), NESYPR with a 3B LM (83.6% CSR, 3.5s latency) outperforms PlaSma with an 8B LM (80.5%, 18.4s) and reduces latency by 95% versus BoT-70B (75.1s). On dynamic-condition robustness (Figure 3b), NESYPR achieves 62.1% higher success rate than the Fast Downward symbolic planner, which fails entirely when input information is incomplete.

## Relation to Prior Work

NESYPR extends the memory-augmented LM line (LM2, Recurrent Memory Transformer) by replacing generic key-value memory with a vector-quantized procedural memory that encodes discrete production rules, drawing its conceptual framing from ACT theory's knowledge compilation. It departs from prior neurosymbolic methods (LLM+P, CLMASP) that require online access to symbolic solvers at test time by internalizing declarative knowledge into the LM's architecture during training, enabling single-step inference. The contrastive planning mechanism at test time adapts the autocontrastive decoding strategy of Gera et al. to the embodied setting, using success/failure-labeled procedure banks to guide generation without gradient updates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical deployment gap: LM-based embodied agents in latency-sensitive, resource-constrained physical systems cannot rely on large-scale inference engines or online symbolic planners. The demonstrated 90%+ latency reduction and 70x model-size reduction relative to strong baselines make this relevant for real-world robotics and edge deployment. A key limitation acknowledged by the authors is that performance partially depends on the pretrained LM's knowledge, and the proposed future direction of combining knowledge distillation with proceduralization remains untested. Open questions include whether the vector-quantized procedure-book generalizes beyond PDDL-style symbolic domains to continuous or partially observable real-world settings.
