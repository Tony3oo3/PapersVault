## Problem

Large multimodal models (LMMs) perform poorly on egocentric (first-person) video understanding because frequent head movements cause rapid viewpoint shifts that break the visual continuity assumptions of third-person video models. Existing egocentric instruction-tuning datasets are either inaccessible or rely on coarse-grained text-only event labels, overlooking the fine-grained visual cues (object attributes, spatial context, motion patterns) that first-person scenarios demand.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250868/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes egocentric video clips of varying lengths (10 s to 5 min) sampled at up to 192 frames resized to 384×384, with a maximum visual token budget of 13,440 tokens after compression, paired with a natural-language question. Outputs are text answers in multiple-choice, open-ended, or detailed-description formats. The training data (OME10M) spans 9.2M QA pairs covering atomic actions, goal-step reasoning, spatial-temporal reasoning, self-motion analysis, and object interaction, while OMEBench provides 4,000 multiple-choice questions over 372 hold-out videos for evaluation.

## Method

OpenMMEgo combines three innovations. First, a dual semantic-aware token compression (DuaSTC) plugs between the vision tower and LLM: Spatial-redundant Token Merging (STM) builds a graph of spatially adjacent tokens weighted by cosine similarity, retains top-k% edges, finds connected components via union-find, and fuses each component into a single semantic token; Temporal-irrelevant Token Pruning (TTP) then applies a SlowFast-inspired scheme where slow frames retain all tokens while fast frames keep only tokens whose cross-frame similarity falls within a [r_l%, r_h%] percentile band, discarding both view-shift noise and temporal redundancy. Second, a dual curriculum learning strategy organizes training into three offline stages of increasing difficulty (easy→medium→hard, quantized by forward loss from reference LMMs) and adds an online per-batch dropout that excludes the highest-loss samples from the backward pass with probability p_i = Clip{α(l_i − l̄), 0, 1}. Third, the OME10M dataset provides multi-level supervision by synthesizing both behavior-based QAs (from human annotations) and vision-centric QAs (from Gemini-1.5-Pro structured visual annotations of 10-second subclips at 1 FPS), bridging the gap between high-level behavioral semantics and low-level perceptual grounding.

## Results

OpenMMEgo(Qwen2.5-VL) achieves 69.3% on EgoSchema, 50.2% on EgoPlan, 65.6% on QAEgo4D, 56.2% on EgoTaskVQA, 65.7% on OMEBench(beh.), and 63.2% on OMEBench(vis.), setting new SOTA among 7B-scale models on all six egocentric benchmarks. On general video understanding, it maintains or slightly improves base-model performance (Video-MME 65.0%, MVBench 70.8%, PerceptionTest 71.2%), confirming no catastrophic forgetting. On additional benchmarks beyond Ego4D, OpenMMEgo(Qwen2.5-VL) reaches 42.4% on HD-EPIC, 60.2% on OpenEQA, and 49.7 mAP on EP-100 MIR, outperforming all open-source baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OpenMMEgo(Qwen2.5-VL) outperforms all open-source 7B video MLLMs on every egocentric benchmark, with the largest gains on OMEBench(beh.) (+10.4 over Qwen2.5-VL) and OMEBench(vis.) (+13.8). Against the strongest general video MLLM, VideoChat-Flash, it leads by +5.0 on EgoPlan, +3.2 on QAEgo4D, +8.6 on OMEBench(beh.), and +8.7 on OMEBench(vis.). Compared to the only other egocentric-oriented MLLM, MM-EGO (built on LLaVA-OneVision), OpenMMEgo(Qwen2.5-VL) edges ahead on EgoSchema (69.3 vs. 69.0) and substantially surpasses it on all other reported benchmarks. Against the closed-source Gemini-2.0-Flash, OpenMMEgo wins on 5 of 6 egocentric benchmarks (EgoPlan 50.2 vs. 51.5 is the sole loss, by 1.3 points) and narrows the EgoSchema gap to 1.8 points. On general video understanding, OpenMMEgo maintains or slightly improves base-model scores (e.g., MVBench 70.8 vs. 69.6 for Qwen2.5-VL), whereas MM-EGO drops 1.3 points on Video-MME, highlighting the benefit of the dual curriculum learning strategy. On non-Ego4D benchmarks (HD-EPIC, OpenEQA, EP-100 MIR), OpenMMEgo beats all open baselines by +8.2, +4.5, and +7.6 respectively over Qwen2.5-VL.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) egocentric video understanding, which has progressed from dataset construction (Ego4D, Ego-Exo4D, Epic-Kitchens) and retrieval models (EgoCLIP, R-VLM) to the first EgoLMM (MM-Ego, Ye et al. 2025, but inaccessible); (2) visual token compression for efficient video LMMs, drawing on Token Merging (Bolya et al. 2023) and SlowFast architectures (Feichtenhofer et al. 2019); and (3) curriculum learning for stable training (Bengio et al. 2009). The key departure from prior work is the systematic combination of multi-level data synthesis (behavior-based + vision-centric QAs via Gemini-1.5-Pro), a dual spatial-temporal token compression specifically motivated by egocentric viewpoint shifts, and a dual offline/online curriculum strategy—all released openly, in contrast to the closed MM-Ego. The paper repurposes SlowFast's dual-pathway idea not for feature extraction but for token-level temporal pruning, and extends token merging from global similarity matching to adjacency-constrained spatial grouping.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As the first fully open-source EgoLMM with 8.2M training QA pairs, a dedicated benchmark (OMEBench), and reproducible training code, this work substantially lowers the barrier for egocentric video research that was previously gated behind inaccessible models (MM-Ego) or coarse-grained annotations. The ablation finding that vision-centric QAs are the most critical data component—removing them drops performance below the base model on EgoPlan—challenges the assumption that high-level behavioral annotations alone suffice for egocentric understanding, suggesting low-level visual grounding is a prerequisite for behavioral reasoning. Open questions include whether the approach scales beyond 7B models, whether the Gemini-1.5-Pro-dependent synthesis pipeline introduces systematic biases, and whether the token compression generalizes to non-egocentric long videos. The lack of error bars and single-seed evaluation limits confidence in the reported margins.
