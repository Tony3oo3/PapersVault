## Problem

Commuting OD flows are essential for urban planning and sustainability research, but obtaining them is prohibitively expensive and privacy-sensitive via travel surveys or individual trajectories. Existing computational OD generation models require fine-grained, hard-to-collect inputs (sociodemographics, POIs, land use, infrastructure data), restricting their use to a small number of data-rich developed cities. This paper asks whether globally accessible public data—satellite imagery and population statistics—can substitute for these costly inputs to enable OD flow generation for any city worldwide.

## Input / Output

The method takes as input (1) region-cropped satellite imagery, obtained by downloading and stitching tiles from platforms like Google Earth or Esri World Imagery, then masking to exact polygon boundaries with zero-padding outside the region, and (2) a single scalar population count per urban region. The output is a full N×N directed weighted matrix where entry (i,j) represents the number of people living in region i and working in region j. The satellite imagery is consumed at native resolution through the RemoteCLIP vision encoder, producing a fixed 1024-dimensional feature vector per region that is concatenated with the population scalar to form the node attribute vector.

## Method

GlODGen operates in three stages: (1) satellite imagery preprocessing via tile download, stitching, and boundary masking; (2) urban semantic feature extraction using the frozen vision encoder of RemoteCLIP, a vision-language geo-foundation model pre-trained with natural language supervision, yielding 1024-dim features that capture mobility-relevant urban semantics (residential density, commercial structures, built-up patterns); and (3) OD flow generation via WEDAN, a graph denoising diffusion model where urban regions form nodes and commuting flows form directed weighted edges. The concatenated feature-population vector serves as the conditioning signal for a graph transformer denoising network that iteratively removes Gaussian noise from the OD flow graph. The key insight is that satellite imagery encodes over 98% of the urban semantic expressiveness of traditional multisource data, and the frozen foundation model preserves generalizable representations while only the MLP and diffusion parameters are trained on US data (3,333 urban areas) before zero-shot transfer to other continents.

## Results

In the cross-continental transfer experiment (trained on US, evaluated on UK), GlODGen achieves CPC 0.485, RMSE 72.68, and NRMSE 1.253, outperforming the best baseline (GMEL) by 46.5% on CPC and 63.5% on RMSE/NRMSE. In the US same-continent experiment, public satellite imagery + population data fed into WEDAN achieves 98.3% of the CPC performance (0.623 vs. 0.634) and 94.1% of the RMSE performance (67.88 vs. 64.06) compared to traditional hard-to-collect sociodemographic and POI features. Spearman rank correlations between generated and observed OD flows range from 0.361 (London) to 0.816 (Rio de Janeiro) across six cities on four continents.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GlODGen decisively outperforms all six baselines on every metric in the cross-continental UK evaluation. The strongest baseline, GMEL (graph-based geo-contextual embeddings + random forest), achieves CPC 0.362 and RMSE 149.1, while GlODGen reaches CPC 0.485 and RMSE 72.68 — a 46.5% and 63.5% improvement respectively. DeepGravity (deep MLP gravity model) is the second-best baseline (CPC 0.359, RMSE 157.0), still trailing GlODGen by a wide margin. The classical physical models (Gravity and Radiation) perform worst, with the Radiation Model actually underperforming the Gravity Model on RMSE (211.6 vs. 101.6), highlighting their inability to capture urban semantic heterogeneity. Random Forest and NetGAN occupy the middle range (CPC ~0.33–0.33), confirming that without rich urban semantic inputs, data-driven models degrade to near-physical-model performance in cross-continental settings. In the same-continent US experiment (Table 1), GlODGen's underlying WEDAN model with public data (CPC 0.623) nearly matches WEDAN with traditional features (CPC 0.634, 98.3%), while still substantially outperforming all other models regardless of input type.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on WEDAN, a graph denoising diffusion model for OD flow generation, and repurposes RemoteCLIP, a vision-language geo-foundation model originally designed for remote sensing tasks, as a frozen feature extractor for urban regions. Conceptually, it departs from the established OD flow generation literature (gravity/radiation models, DeepGravity, NetGAN, GMEL) by replacing the traditional hard-to-collect inputs—sociodemographics, POIs, land use, infrastructure—with publicly available satellite imagery and population data. The paper also connects to the broader line of work on urban representation learning from satellite imagery (poverty mapping, land cover classification, commercial activeness), but is the first to use such representations as conditioning signals for a generative OD flow model at global scale.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: GlODGen provides an open-source, single-line-of-code tool that can generate commuting OD flows for any city worldwide using only publicly available data, lowering the barrier for urban planning, transportation research, and SDG-related studies in data-poor regions. Key limitations include reliance on US-trained models for cross-continental transfer and the restriction to static commuting flows, which limits applicability to time-dependent traffic analysis. Open questions include whether training on multi-continental data would further improve robustness, whether the approach extends to non-commuting mobility patterns, and how performance scales with satellite imagery resolution and region granularity.
