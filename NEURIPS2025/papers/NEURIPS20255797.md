## Problem

Behavior cloning for visuomotor policies is vulnerable to covariate shift: small deviations from expert trajectories compound into out-of-distribution states and task failure. Existing mitigations—human-in-the-loop corrections (DAgger) or synthetic data augmentation—are labor-intensive, rely on strong task assumptions, or risk injecting suboptimal demonstrations that degrade imitation quality, exposing a fundamental tension between precise expert imitation and robustness to diverse states.

## Input / Output

The base policy consumes a window of T_o=2 RGB wrist-camera images (140×140, center-cropped to 128×128) plus proprioceptive state and outputs a T_p-step action sequence, of which the first T_a steps are executed before replanning. At inference, the dynamics model additionally takes the current observation and a candidate noisy action sample to predict a future latent state, whose L2 distance to the nearest expert latent neighbor serves as the OOD score that triggers or suppresses gradient-based steering.

## Method

LPB decouples precise imitation from OOD recovery into two modules: a base diffusion policy (ResNet-18 encoder + U-Net denoiser) trained exclusively on high-quality expert demonstrations, and an action-conditioned visual latent dynamics model (decoder-only ViT) trained on a broader mixture of expert data and automatically collected policy rollouts from intermediate training checkpoints. At inference, the frozen shared encoder maps observations to a latent space where the L2 distance to the nearest expert latent defines an OOD score; when this score exceeds a threshold τ, LPB applies classifier-guidance-style gradient steering during the final K_guide DDPM denoising steps, back-propagating the OOD score of the dynamics model's predicted future latent state through to the noisy action sample to bias the denoised action toward the expert manifold. This treats the expert distribution as an implicit barrier (inspired by Control Barrier Functions) without requiring explicit safety sets, rewards, or additional human correction.

## Results

Under the limited-demonstration setting (20% expert demos), LPB achieves the highest success rate on all five simulated benchmarks: 0.65 on Square, 0.85 on Transport, 0.39 on Tool-Hang, 0.65 on Push-T, and 0.75 on LIBERO-10. The largest gains appear on long-horizon, precision-sensitive tasks: Tool-Hang improves from 0.29 (Filtered BC) to 0.39, and Transport from 0.79 to 0.85. On the real-robot Belt Assembly task, LPB raises success from 0.55 (base BC) to 0.75, and on Cup Arrangement it matches the base policy on in-distribution poses while substantially outperforming it on out-of-distribution initial poses.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LPB outperforms or matches all five baselines across every simulated task. Against Expert BC (Diffusion Policy trained solely on expert data), LPB gains 0.04–0.12 in success rate, with the largest margin on Tool-Hang (0.39 vs. 0.27). Against Filtered BC (Chen et al., which augments expert data with successful rollouts), LPB wins on all tasks, most notably on Tool-Hang (0.39 vs. 0.29) and Transport (0.85 vs. 0.79), indicating that even 'successful' rollouts contain suboptimal actions that degrade BC. Against CCIL (Ke et al., continuity-based corrective augmentation), LPB dominates on every task, with CCIL actually underperforming Expert BC on Tool-Hang (0.14 vs. 0.27). CQL (Kumar et al.) fails catastrophically on Robomimic and LIBERO-10 (0.0 success), likely due to the absence of dense rewards, and also trails LPB on Push-T (0.29 vs. 0.65). On the real-robot Cup Arrangement task, LPB matches the UMI pretrained policy (Chi et al.) on in-distribution initial poses but substantially outperforms it on out-of-distribution poses, where the base policy drifts to joint-limit failure while LPB recovers to in-distribution views and completes the task.

## Relation to Prior Work

LPB builds on Diffusion Policy as its base visuomotor architecture and adapts the classifier guidance mechanism from diffusion models (Dhariwal & Nichol, 2021) to steer action denoising in latent space. Conceptually, it draws an analogy to Control Barrier Functions from control theory, but replaces the analytically defined safety set with a learned latent expert distribution, eliminating the need for explicit dynamics or safety constraints. The key departure from prior covariate-shift mitigations (DAgger, synthetic augmentation, offline RL) is the explicit decoupling of precise expert imitation from OOD recovery into two separate modules, with the dynamics model trained on automatically collected policy rollouts rather than human corrections or reward-annotated data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LPB offers a practical, plug-and-play mechanism to improve the robustness of off-the-shelf pretrained visuomotor policies without retraining or additional human annotation, which is increasingly relevant as generalist robot policies (π₀, OpenVLA) become available. The finding that suboptimal, unlabeled rollout data is more effective for dynamics model training than filtered successful rollouts challenges the common assumption that only high-quality data is useful. Open questions include whether the approach can handle long-horizon deviations (currently limited to local corrections), whether per-task dynamics models can be replaced by multi-task or zero-shot variants, and whether the latent OOD score can be computed without access to expert training data.
