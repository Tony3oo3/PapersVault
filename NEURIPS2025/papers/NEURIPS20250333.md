## Problem

Existing kernel-based online change point detection methods suffer from two fundamental limitations: they are not genuinely online (requiring historical data known to be from the pre-change distribution) and they require a user-specified window parameter whose choice is notoriously difficult and directly affects detection power. This paper addresses both gaps simultaneously by proposing a fully non-parametric, window-free online change detector with provable minimax optimality.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes a sequential multivariate data stream X_1, X_2, ... in R^d one observation at a time, along with a pre-specified sequence of thresholds {λ_n}. It produces an extended stopping time N (the detection time) and, upon flagging a change, an estimate of the most likely change point location. Crucially, no reference or training data from the pre-change distribution is required, and no window size must be chosen by the user. The algorithm maintains only O(r log n) memory after n observations, where r is the number of random Fourier features.

## Method

Online RFF-MMD performs sequential two-sample tests based on the kernel Maximum Mean Discrepancy (MMD), approximated via Random Fourier Features (RFFs) derived from Bochner's theorem. The RFF approximation converts the MMD into a Euclidean norm of mean feature vectors, enabling linear-time computation and constant-time updates per new observation. These local tests are embedded on a dyadic grid of candidate change point locations {n − 2^j : j = 0, …, ⌊log₂ n⌋ − 1}, which eliminates the window parameter while requiring only O(log n) tests per step. A dyadic window-merging data structure (Algorithm 1) maintains the grid in O(r log n) time and space overall. The paper proves that the detection delay is minimax optimal up to logarithmic factors, and that the threshold sequences guarantee either a target average run length or a target uniform false alarm probability without any knowledge of the pre-change distribution.

## Results

On the HASC dataset (10 walking-to-staying sequences, d=3), Online RFF-MMD achieves an average detection delay of 21.86 with 2 too-early flags and 1 miss, outperforming NewMA (34.25 delay, 5 misses) and ScanB (31.20 delay). On synthetic 20-dimensional data and MNIST (d=784), the method achieves the lowest expected detection delay (EDD) among all kernel-based competitors across all tested post-change distributions. The algorithm's O(r log n) per-observation runtime is empirically verified for streams up to n=250,000 with r up to 1000 random Fourier features.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Scan B-statistics [Li et al., 2019], Online RFF-MMD reduces the average detection delay from 31.20 to 21.86 on HASC and achieves lower EDD on both synthetic and MNIST benchmarks, while also being genuinely online (no reference samples needed) and window-free. Against NewMA [Keriven et al., 2020], the proposed method cuts the average delay from 34.25 to 21.86 and reduces misses from 5 to 1 on HASC. Against Online kernel CUSUM [Wei & Xie, 2022], OKCUSUM achieves a slightly lower delay (17.44 vs. 21.86) on HASC, but it requires 1,596 pre-change reference samples and a window parameter, whereas Online RFF-MMD needs neither. Against the density-ratio-based RuLSIF [Liu et al., 2013], RuLSIF reports a lower raw delay (4.50) but flags 8 of 10 sequences too early; with a practical offset of 30, RuLSIF's delay (20.38) is comparable to Online RFF-MMD (21.86) with the same number of too-early flags. Overall, Online RFF-MMD is the only method that is simultaneously genuinely online, window-free, and competitive in detection delay across all three datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper combines three established ingredients—kernel MMD two-sample testing (Gretton et al.), random Fourier feature approximation (Rahimi & Recht), and Lai's exponential/dyadic grid for sequential testing—into a unified online change point detection algorithm. It departs from prior kernel-based detectors (Scan B-statistics, Online kernel CUSUM, NEWMA) by eliminating both the need for reference data from the pre-change distribution and the window parameter, achieving this through the RFF approximation (which makes the MMD statistic linear-time updatable) and the dyadic grid (which replaces a fixed window with a logarithmic set of candidate split points). The result is the first kernel-based online change point detector that is simultaneously genuinely online, window-free, and comes with minimax optimality guarantees.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The minimax optimality result (Theorem 4) is the first of its kind for kernel-based online change point detection, closing a gap between the offline non-parametric and parametric online settings. Practically, the O(r log n) per-observation cost with no window tuning makes the method attractive for high-dimensional streaming applications where prior methods either require reference samples or suffer from window-size sensitivity. Open questions include extending to non-i.i.d. (mixing) data, adapting kernel selection to the sequential setting, and whether the distribution-dependent thresholds (Corollaries 1–2) can be made fully adaptive without any pre-change information.
