## Problem

Existing full-duplex speech LLMs either rely on modular pipelines with auxiliary components (VAD, dialogue controllers) that suffer from error propagation, or inject discrete audio codec tokens into the LLM vocabulary (as in Moshi), which causes significant performance degradation on speech modality and demands massive training data to prevent catastrophic forgetting. No prior work achieves standalone full-duplex speech interaction from a single LLM backbone without codec injection while maintaining competitive accuracy on spoken QA and open-domain dialogue.

## Input / Output

The model consumes two concurrent 25 Hz streaming speech channels per 80 ms time block: an environment stream (user speech, background noise, and the assistant's own echo) and a dual-channel assistant stream (the model's prior generated speech). Output is a single autoregressive token sequence that interleaves text response tokens with special state-transition tokens (think, shift); every 4 generated text tokens trigger 12 speech tokens from the CosyVoice2 synthesizer, yielding 480 ms of audio with a 320 ms time-to-first-audio latency. The model must autonomously decide when to emit shift tokens to transition between listening and speaking, with no external VAD or dialogue controller.

## Method

SALMONN-omni integrates a Mamba-based streaming speech encoder (knowledge-distilled from Whisper-large-v3 at 50 Hz, downsampled to 25 Hz), a Llama-3-8B-Instruct backbone (fine-tuned with LoRA rank 32), and a CosyVoice2-0.5B streaming synthesizer, all connected through hidden-embedding linear projections so that no audio codec tokens enter the LLM vocabulary. The key architectural insight is interleaving environment-stream embeddings, assistant-stream embeddings, and text tokens into one autoregressive sequence segmented into fixed 80 ms blocks, enabling a single LLM to listen and speak simultaneously. For dialogue-state control, the paper proposes an explicit 'thinking' strategy where the LLM generates special tokens (think for listening/waiting, shift for state transitions) as part of its normal output sequence, effectively treating the LLM as a 'secretly a full-duplex predictor' rather than adding a separate state classifier. Training proceeds in three stages: (1) connect the streaming encoder via LoRA on ASR/QA data, (2) connect the streaming synthesizer and train end-to-end on multi-turn dialogue including barge-ins and backchanneling, and (3) apply DPO reinforcement learning to correct the SFT model's bias toward being over-interrupted, improving context-dependent barge-in F1 from 0.86 to 0.90.

## Results

Under the predicted turn-taking setting (where the model autonomously decides when to speak), SALMONN-omni achieves 79.3% S2T accuracy on Llama Questions, 49.7% on Web Questions, 63.6% on TriviaQA, and a GPTScore of 4.01 on AlpacaEval, delivering an average 35.9% relative improvement over the best prior open-source full-duplex models (Moshi and Freeze-Omni). Under the oracle turn-taking setting, SALMONN-omni attains the best S2T accuracy across all four datasets (80.0%, 50.5%, 66.0%, 4.05) and the best S2S on Web Questions (45.4%) and TriviaQA (58.8%), while maintaining a high average UTMOS of 4.261. The model also achieves a 92.8% turn-taking success rate on TriviaQA and 92.0% on AlpacaEval, substantially outperforming Moshi (37.1% and 83.4%) and Freeze-Omni (72.0% and 87.9%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

In the predicted turn-taking (full-duplex) setting, SALMONN-omni substantially outperforms both full-duplex baselines: it surpasses Moshi by 18.5 points on Llama Questions S2T (79.3 vs. 60.8), 26.3 points on Web Questions S2T (49.7 vs. 23.4), and 38.0 points on TriviaQA S2T (63.6 vs. 25.6), and it beats Freeze-Omni by 5.1, 8.9, and 18.5 points on the same three datasets respectively. In the oracle turn-taking (half-duplex) setting, SALMONN-omni achieves the best S2T accuracy on all four benchmarks, narrowly edging out Kimi-Audio on Llama Questions (80.0 vs. 79.7) and miniCPM-o on TriviaQA (66.0 vs. 65.4), while Qwen2.5-Omni slightly leads on Llama Questions S2S (75.7 vs. 75.0). Notably, SALMONN-omni achieves these results using only ~80k multi-round conversation samples and ~730k QA samples, compared to baselines like Kimi-Audio trained on up to 13 million hours of audio. On full-duplex-specific tasks, SALMONN-omni achieves a 0.88 F1 on context-independent barge-in/backchanneling discrimination (vs. Moshi's 0.80 and Freeze-Omni's 0.17 with echo), and a 92.8% turn-taking success rate on TriviaQA where Moshi drops to 37.1% and Freeze-Omni to 72.0%.

## Relation to Prior Work

SALMONN-omni departs from two dominant paradigms in full-duplex speech LLMs: (1) codec-injection models (Moshi, SyncLLM, OmniFlatten) that embed discrete audio tokens into the LLM vocabulary, and (2) multi-process 'model-as-a-server' systems (VITA, Freeze-Omni, MinMo) that run separate LLM instances for listening and speaking. Instead, it integrates a Mamba-based streaming encoder (distilled from Whisper-large-v3 following the MT2KD approach) and a CosyVoice2-based streaming synthesizer around a single Llama-3-8B backbone via hidden embeddings, with no audio codecs in the token space. The key conceptual departure is the 'thinking' strategy, which treats dialogue state transitions (listen/speak) as special tokens generated autoregressively by the LLM itself, rather than relying on an external VAD or a separate full-duplex predictor.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that a single, standalone LLM can handle full-duplex speech interaction without codec injection, removing the modality-gap penalty and catastrophic-forgetting issues that plague codec-based approaches. The first application of DPO to full-duplex dialogue dynamics (barge-in, backchanneling) opens a path toward preference-aligned conversational timing. Key limitations remain: the 3-minute context window, the model's inability to generate backchanneling responses during user speech, and inconsistent emotional expression. Open questions include whether the codec-free standalone paradigm scales to longer conversations, whether the 'thinking' strategy generalizes to other LLM backbones, and how to reduce the 405 ms time-to-first-audio latency further.
