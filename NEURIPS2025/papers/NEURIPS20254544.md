## Problem

Prior time-series forecasting research has been predominantly encoder-centric, treating the decoding (prediction) mechanism and training strategy as secondary or independent concerns. The standard formulation assumes fixed-length, continuous lookback windows predicting fixed-length future horizons, which constrains the model to a unidirectional temporal flow and limits its ability to capture the full range of temporal dynamics. This paper argues that architectural design, prediction mechanism, and training objective should be jointly aligned rather than optimized in isolation.

## Input / Output

The model consumes an arbitrary (potentially discontinuous) subset of multivariate time series patches as input context, each patch being a fixed-length window (P ∈ {12,16,24,48} timesteps) across all C channels, augmented with learnable temporal and channel positional embeddings. The target is any complementary set of patches along the temporal axis—future (extrapolation), past (interpolation), or missing (imputation)—so the input and target segments need not be contiguous or in a fixed past-to-future arrangement. Output is a per-channel, per-patch reconstruction projected from query representations back to the original signal resolution.

## Method

TIMEPERCEIVER generalizes the forecasting task to jointly encompass extrapolation, interpolation, and imputation by randomly sampling arbitrary input and target patch indices during training, eliminating the need for a separate pretraining-finetuning pipeline. The encoder uses a latent bottleneck: a small set of M learnable latent tokens first cross-attends to all input patch tokens to compress information, then refines itself through K self-attention layers, and finally cross-attends back to update the input representations—reducing attention complexity from O(N²) to O(NM) while jointly capturing temporal and cross-channel dependencies. The decoder employs learnable query tokens (constructed from shared temporal and channel positional embeddings for target patches) that cross-attend to the encoded input, enabling selective retrieval of relevant context for each target position. A final linear projection maps query outputs to patch-level predictions, and RevIN handles distribution shift. The entire framework is trained end-to-end with MSE loss over the sampled target patches.

## Results

TimePerceiver achieves 55 best and 17 second-best scores out of 80 evaluation settings (8 datasets × 5 prediction-length entries × 2 metrics), recording the best average rank of 1.375 (MSE) and 1.550 (MAE). Averaged across all datasets, it improves MSE by 5.6% and MAE by 4.4% over the strongest channel-dependent baseline CARD, and by 8.5% (MSE) and 7.3% (MAE) over iTransformer. On the Weather dataset, TimePerceiver reaches an average MSE of 0.227 and MAE of 0.265; on Electricity, 0.161/0.254; and on ETTm1, 0.347/0.375.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TimePerceiver outperforms all nine baselines on the majority of dataset–metric combinations, with the most consistent margins over MLP-based DLinear (e.g., 0.161 vs. 0.187 MSE on Electricity) and CNN-based TimesNet (0.407 vs. 0.593 MSE on Traffic). Against the strongest Transformer baselines, it beats CARD by 5.6% average MSE and iTransformer by 8.5% average MSE. Two notable exceptions exist: on Traffic, S-Mamba achieves a lower average MSE (0.387 vs. 0.407) and MAE (0.270 vs. 0.273), and on ETTh2, DeformableTST records a substantially lower average MSE (0.336 vs. 0.444), though TimePerceiver still wins on individual horizons H=192 and H=720. On ETTh1, DeformableTST edges out TimePerceiver on MAE (0.413 vs. 0.426) while TimePerceiver leads on MSE (0.410 vs. 0.413). Overall, the paper reports an average rank of 1.375 (MSE) and 1.550 (MAE) across all 80 settings, confirming broad superiority with only a handful of narrow losses.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant encoder-centric paradigm in time-series forecasting, where prior work (PatchTST, iTransformer, CARD, DeformableTST, etc.) has focused almost exclusively on designing better encoders while treating decoding as a simple linear projection and training as a separate concern. It repurposes the Perceiver-style latent bottleneck and query-based decoding from vision/NLP into the time-series setting, and draws on BERT's masking-reconstruction idea to motivate a generalized training formulation that unifies extrapolation, interpolation, and imputation. Rather than building on a single prior architecture, it synthesizes patch tokenization (from PatchTST), cross-attention bottleneck compression (from Perceiver IO), and learnable positional queries into a unified encoder-decoder framework tightly coupled with its training objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper makes a compelling case that aligning architectural design (encoder, decoder) with the training formulation is more important than any single encoder innovation, and the generalized formulation eliminates the need for a separate pretraining-finetuning pipeline. The consistent SOTA across 8 diverse datasets (55 best, 17 second-best out of 80 settings) with a computationally efficient latent bottleneck (O(NM) vs O(N²)) makes this practically relevant for practitioners. Open questions include how the optimal target-patch sampling strategy (contiguous vs. disjoint vs. mixed) should be selected for new domains, whether the approach scales to very high-channel-count or ultra-long-sequence settings, and whether the generalized formulation's benefits persist when applied to non-patch-based architectures beyond the PatchTST variant tested.
