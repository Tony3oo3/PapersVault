## Problem

Despite RL-based training (e.g., GRPO) producing large accuracy gains on mathematical reasoning benchmarks, it remains unclear which specific reasoning capabilities RL actually enhances and which remain fragile. Prior evaluations track only end-to-end accuracy, providing no signal into whether RL improves planning, knowledge retrieval, step-by-step execution, or problem decomposition. This paper asks: beyond accuracy, what does RL change about how a model reasons?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252235/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The SPARKLE benchmark augments 2,564 mathematical problems (from AIME24, AMC23, MATH500, GSM8K, OlympiadBench) with three annotation layers: a high-level planning skeleton, relevant knowledge components (theorems, definitions, lemmas), and a chain of self-contained subproblems with answers. During evaluation, the model receives the problem text optionally combined with one or more of these auxiliary inputs; during Stage-2 training, it additionally receives 0–4 semantic chunks of a reference solution as partial-step scaffolding. The model outputs a full reasoning trace ending in a boxed numerical answer, and the framework scores it along three axes: plan-following accuracy, knowledge-integration gain, and subproblem success rate (SSR).

## Method

The paper contributes two complementary components. First, SPARKLE is a three-axis analytic framework that isolates plan following (comparing performance with vs. without a human-crafted planning skeleton), knowledge utilization (comparing performance with vs. without explicitly provided theorems and facts), and subproblem resolution (scoring whether the model solves every constituent subproblem in a decomposed chain). Second, SparkleRL-PSS is a two-stage GRPO training pipeline: Stage 1 trains on 40K diverse math problems from DeepScaleR-Preview, and Stage 2 continues from the Stage-1 checkpoint on 5.7K identified hard problems, each augmented with partial solution scaffolding—reference traces are split into four semantic chunks and 0–4 chunks are prepended as context so the model must complete the remaining reasoning. This avoids generating new data while providing graded guidance on the hardest examples. Key empirical findings include: RL-tuned models are more robust to externally imposed plans but perform best with self-generated strategies; knowledge augmentation yields consistent gains that grow with difficulty (up to +42.5% at level 8); and all models still struggle with solving every subproblem in a chain, indicating a persistent weakness in compositional decomposition.

## Results

Using Avg@8 as the primary metric, SparkleRL-Stage 1 (GRPO on 40K problems) lifts Qwen-2.5-Math-7B from 35.23% average to 65.01% across five benchmarks (AIME24, AMC23, MATH500, GSM8K, OlympiadBench), a 29.78-point gain. The best Stage 2 variant, SparkleRL-Stage 2-pss (hard problems with partial step scaffolding), further improves to 67.03% average and reaches 50.42% on AIME24—matching SoTA pure-RL 32B models (50% on AIME24) with only 7B parameters. Fine-grained SPARKLE analysis reveals that RL-tuned models gain +4.2–4.3% from external knowledge injection while the base model drops 5.4%, and that externally imposed human plans degrade base-model performance on 4/5 benchmarks but leave RL-tuned models largely stable.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is the unmodified Qwen-2.5-Math-7B-Base model. Single-stage GRPO (Stage 1) outperforms the base by 29.78 points on average, with the largest relative gains on GSM8K (+49.24) and MATH500 (+35.97). Among the three Stage 2 strategies, partial step scaffolding (pss) consistently dominates: it beats hard-only by +3.43 average and mix by +3.91 average, and is the only variant that improves on Stage 1 across all five benchmarks. On the hardest benchmark (AIME24), Stage 2-pss reaches 50.42%, which the authors note is comparable to SoTA pure-RL 32B models (e.g., DAPO at 50%), while the hard-only and mix variants actually underperform Stage 1 on AIME24 (41.67 and 40.00 vs. 46.67). In the fine-grained SPARKLE analysis, RL-tuned models outperform the base on knowledge integration (+4.2–4.3% with external knowledge vs. −5.4% for base) and show greater robustness to externally imposed plans, though all models still struggle with full subproblem-chain resolution (e.g., 17.5% subproblem success rate on AIME24 for Stage 2-pss vs. 50.4% full-problem accuracy).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the GRPO algorithm from DeepSeekMath (Shao et al., 2024) and the rule-based reward design from DeepSeek-R1 (Guo et al., 2025), applying them in a two-stage curriculum-style pipeline. It departs from prior RL-for-reasoning work—which typically reports only aggregate accuracy on standardized benchmarks—by introducing SPARKLE, a fine-grained analytic framework inspired by cognitive science (Newell & Simon; Wason & Johnson-Laird) that decomposes reasoning into plan following, knowledge integration, and subproblem resolution. The multi-stage SparkleRL-PSS pipeline also contrasts with the common practice of filtering out hard problems that yield no positive reward signal (Xiong et al., 2025), instead reusing them with partial step scaffolding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The SPARKLE framework provides the first systematic, multi-axis diagnostic for understanding *what* RL actually changes in a model's reasoning behavior, moving beyond the black-box accuracy metric that dominates the field. The practical takeaway that targeted external knowledge injection outperforms continued RL fine-tuning on knowledge-intensive tasks offers a simple, low-cost lever for practitioners. Key limitations include the focus on a single model family (Qwen-2.5-Math) and a single domain (mathematics), and the absence of theoretical grounding for why RL reshapes plan-following flexibility. Open questions include whether the subproblem-resolution weakness persists at larger scales and how to systematically incorporate auxiliary hints (plans, knowledge, subproblem cues) into the RL objective itself.
