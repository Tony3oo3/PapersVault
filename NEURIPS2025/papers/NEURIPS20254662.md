## Problem

Floorplanning in EDA is NP-hard with a vast solution space, and existing methods—SA-based, analytical, and RL-based—suffer from insufficient exploration and get trapped in local optima. No prior work has designed a collaborative framework that synergizes the global search strength of evolutionary algorithms with the fine-grained learning capability of reinforcement learning for this complex combinatorial optimization task.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254662/cropped_page4_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of rectangular modules (each with length, width, and orientation), fixed port positions on the die boundary, and a netlist describing connectivity between modules and ports. It produces a compact, gap-free floorplan encoded as a B*-Tree, from which module positions and orientations are derived. The action space at each step is 4n_m², comprising the choice of which module to insert, which target module to attach to, whether to place it as left or right child, and whether to rotate it, with the episode terminating after all n_m modules are placed.

## Method

CORE is the first evolutionary reinforcement learning (ERL) framework for floorplanning, integrating three processes: (1) Clustering-based Diversified Evolutionary Search (CDES), which clusters the population by layout features via K-Means, scores individuals by a weighted combination of quality (negative HPWL) and novelty (negative top-2 cosine similarity), and mutates elites using B*-Tree operators (rotation, exchange, deletion-insertion); (2) a B*-Tree-based MDP solved with PPO, where a GNN (2-layer SAGEConv) and Transformer (2-layer, 2-head) encode module, port, and netlist states, and a hierarchical MLP with action masks selects the insertion module, target module, and orientation/child-type; (3) two collaboration mechanisms—the Reinforcement-Driven Mechanism injects RL-constructed layouts into the EA population to accelerate evolution, and the Evolutionary-Guided Mechanism converts superior EA layouts from an archive into state-action sequences for an imitation learning loss added to the PPO objective, enabling bidirectional knowledge transfer.

## Results

On the MCNC and GSRC benchmarks (8 cases ranging from 10 to 300 modules), CORE achieves an average 12.9% improvement in Half Perimeter Wire Length (HPWL) over the best baseline across all cases, with gains of 22.6% on n300 and 29.5% on ami49. CORE maintains comparable Area Utilization (AU ≈ 0.90–0.95) while substantially reducing wirelength. In the HPWL-only optimization setting (Table 6), CORE further extends its lead, e.g., 461,219 vs. 621,663 (B*-Tree SA) on n300 and 627,620 vs. 900,802 on ami49.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CORE outperforms all five floorplanning baselines (Corblivar, SP-FP, B*-Tree SA, GoodFloorplan, CBL-RL) on every case in HPWL, with the largest margins against SA-based methods (e.g., 44.5% lower HPWL vs. B*-Tree SA on ami49: 656,068 vs. 1,180,549). Against the two RL-based baselines, CORE still leads by 10–20% on larger instances (n100–n300), demonstrating that the EA-RL collaboration adds value beyond RL alone. Compared to the same-representation baseline B*-Tree SA, CORE also achieves higher area utilization (e.g., 0.915 vs. 0.741 on n100), confirming that the advantage is not merely from the B*-Tree encoding. In the macro-placement comparison (Table 2), CORE dramatically outperforms MaskPlace and ChipFormer (e.g., 469,242 vs. 960,487 and 693,501 on n300), validating that compact structural representations are essential for floorplanning and that macro-placement algorithms are ill-suited to this task. The ablation (Table 3) further shows that CORE (461,219 on n300) beats both its CDES-only (574,579) and PPO-only (468,089) components, confirming the synergy of the collaboration mechanisms.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the B*-Tree floorplan representation (Chang et al., DAC 2000) as its structural foundation, extending it from a simulated-annealing search space to a sequential decision (MDP) formulation for the first time. It departs from prior RL-based floorplanning methods (GoodFloorplan, CBL-RL) that use corner-block-list or graph representations by adopting B*-Tree, and from prior SA-based B*-Tree methods by replacing stochastic local search with a learned policy. The broader ERL literature has explored EA-RL hybrids for problems like TSP and locomotion, but CORE is positioned as the first such framework applied to constrained combinatorial optimization in EDA, introducing bidirectional collaboration mechanisms (RL-injection into EA populations and EA-guided imitation learning for RL) that go beyond the unidirectional assistance seen in prior ERL work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CORE demonstrates that synergistic EA-RL collaboration can substantially outperform either component alone on floorplanning, with a 12.9% average wirelength improvement over the best prior methods and a widening gap on larger instances. The clustering-based diversity maintenance (CDES) and the bidirectional collaboration mechanisms offer a template for other constrained combinatorial optimization problems in EDA. Limitations include higher runtime than pure heuristic methods (mitigated by a parallel framework but still 2-3× slower than B*-Tree SA), evaluation limited to MCNC and GSRC benchmarks with up to 300 modules, and the absence of open-source implementations for the RL baselines (GoodFloorplan, CBL-RL), making direct reproduction difficult. Open questions include whether the collaboration mechanisms generalize to other EDA stages (placement, routing) and whether the framework scales to industrial-scale problems with thousands of modules.
