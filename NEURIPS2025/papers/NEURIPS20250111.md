## Problem

Existing nighttime deraining datasets synthesize rain by linearly adding uniform rain masks to dark backgrounds, ignoring that nighttime rain is only visible near artificial light sources and invisible in low-illumination regions, creating a large domain gap with real scenes. Additionally, prior nighttime deraining methods still operate in RGB space, overlooking the observation that nighttime rain produces far more pronounced contrast in the Y (luminance) channel of YCbCr than in individual RGB channels due to light reflections against dark backgrounds.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250111/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a nighttime rainy RGB image (H×W×3) and produces a derained RGB image of the same dimensions. Internally, the input is split into Y, Cb, and Cr channels via a learnable converter; only the single-channel Y image (H×W×1) is processed for rain removal while Cb and Cr bypass to the color refinement stage. Training uses 128×128 patches with batch size 4 on the proposed HQ-NightRain dataset (11,200 pairs across rain-streak, raindrop, and mixed subsets), and evaluation extends to real-world benchmarks (RealRain-1k, RainDS-real) and a public synthetic set (GTAV-NightRain).

## Method

CST-Net is a two-stage Transformer-based framework. In the degradation removal stage, a learnable Color Space Converter (CSC) replaces the fixed 3×3 RGB→YCbCr matrix with nine learnable scalars passed through an MLP, enabling adaptive luminance-chrominance separation; the Y channel is then fed into a 4-level Restormer-style encoder-decoder for rain removal, augmented by an Implicit Illumination Guidance (IIG) module that uses an MLP to map pixel coordinates and locally weighted Y values to predicted luminance, implicitly encoding spatially varying illumination. In the color refinement stage, the derained Y channel is concatenated with the untouched Cb and Cr channels, converted back to RGB via the CSC, and refined by a second Transformer encoder-decoder. The companion HQ-NightRain dataset is synthesized by modulating rain masks with an illumination coefficient matrix (extracted from the HSV V channel, thresholded to exclude very dark and overexposed regions) and applying defocus blur to raindrops, producing non-uniform rain distributions that better match real nighttime appearance.

## Results

On the proposed HQ-NightRain dataset, CST-Net achieves an average PSNR of 39.07 dB, SSIM of 0.9778, and LPIPS of 0.0477 across the RS, RD, and SD subsets, outperforming the strongest baseline NeRD-Rain (38.51 dB / 0.9754 / 0.0588) by 0.56 dB in PSNR. On the public GTAV-NightRain benchmark, CST-Net reaches 38.94 dB PSNR, surpassing NeRD-Rain by 1.12 dB. On real-world benchmarks (RealRain-1k and RainDS-real), CST-Net also leads all baselines, e.g., achieving 27.31 dB PSNR on RealRain-1k-L and 25.05 dB on RainDS-real RS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CST-Net outperforms all 11 baselines across every evaluated dataset and metric. Against the closest competitor NeRD-Rain (CVPR 2024), CST-Net gains 0.81 dB PSNR on the most challenging SD (streak+drop) subset of HQ-NightRain and 1.12 dB on GTAV-NightRain, while also reducing LPIPS from 0.0588 to 0.0477 on average. DRSformer (CVPR 2023) is the second-strongest baseline at 38.74 dB average PSNR, still 0.33 dB behind CST-Net. On real-world data (RealRain-1k, RainDS-real), CST-Net leads by 0.10–0.24 dB PSNR over the next-best methods (DRSformer, NeRD-Rain), confirming generalization beyond synthetic data. Nighttime-specific baselines RLP and MSGNN lag significantly (34.12 and 28.67 dB average), indicating that prior nighttime deraining approaches struggle with the illumination-aware rain distribution captured by HQ-NightRain. The ablation in Table 4 further confirms that the YCbCr color space + learnable CSC + IIG combination is essential: removing any single component drops PSNR by 0.5–5.4 dB.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from prior nighttime deraining methods (RLP, NightRain, NeRD-Rain) that perform rain removal in the RGB color space by arguing that the Y channel in YCbCr better isolates rain artifacts under low illumination, and introduces a learnable color space converter to replace the fixed linear transformation matrix. The implicit illumination guidance module adapts the implicit neural representation technique from NeRD-Rain (Chen et al., CVPR 2024) to encode spatially-varying illumination cues for guiding deraining. On the data side, the paper moves beyond the linear superposition model used by GTAV-NightRain and RoadScene by incorporating an illumination coefficient matrix and defocus blur to synthesize non-uniform, physically-motivated nighttime rain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The HQ-NightRain benchmark (11,200 pairs with illumination-aware synthesis) addresses a genuine gap in nighttime deraining evaluation, and the t-SNE and real-world generalization results suggest it reduces the synthetic-to-real domain gap more effectively than prior datasets. The Y-channel deraining strategy is a practical, low-cost architectural choice that could be adopted by other restoration pipelines. However, the paper acknowledges it cannot handle the veiling effect under low-light conditions, and the method's gains over strong baselines like NeRD-Rain are modest (~0.8–1.1 dB), leaving open whether the color-space reformulation offers fundamental advantages beyond the specific nighttime setting.
