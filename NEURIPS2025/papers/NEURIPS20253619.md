## Problem

Existing graph backdoor defenses rely on detecting structural anomalies or distributional outliers, making them ineffective against subtle feature-based attacks (e.g., SPEAR) that preserve graph topology. The paper identifies a previously unexplored 'class-wide loss drift' phenomenon where early loss convergence of target nodes spreads to nearby clean nodes through message passing, causing distribution overlap that renders fixed thresholds and global ratios—standard in image-domain training-time defenses—unreliable for graphs.

## Input / Output

The defender receives only the trigger-embedded training graph (node features, adjacency matrix, and labels that may include a malicious label) without knowledge of the malicious label, the number or positions of target nodes, or trigger patterns, and has no access to clean validation data. The method operates entirely during the training phase (training-time defense), producing a defended GNN model along with an identified target node set and clean node set, where target nodes are decoupled from the malicious label while clean nodes retain correct predictions.

## Method

LoSplit is a two-stage training-time defense. Stage 1 (Early-Stage Dynamic Split) trains a GCN for a few epochs under Reverse Cross-Entropy (RCE) loss to amplify early loss divergence, identifies the malicious label via maximum intra-class loss variance, selects the optimal split epoch by maximizing inter-cluster loss expectation difference, and applies GMM clustering on z-scored losses to adaptively separate target nodes from clean nodes within the target class. Stage 2 (Decoupling-Forgetting) trains the model with a unified objective: target nodes receive random label reassignment (excluding the malicious label) combined with gradient ascent w.r.t. the malicious label to break the shortcut association, while clean nodes are trained normally to preserve accuracy. This differs from image-domain approaches by using RCE instead of CE loss, dynamic epoch selection instead of fixed early epochs, and GMM-based adaptive thresholds instead of fixed isolation ratios, all motivated by the unstable loss dynamics induced by graph message passing.

## Results

LoSplit achieves near-zero Attack Success Rate (ASR ≤ 0.56%) across all six datasets (Cora, CiteSeer, PubMed, Physics, Flickr, OGB-arXiv) and all four attacks (GTA, UGBA, DPGBA, SPEAR), while maintaining or improving Clean Accuracy (CA) over the undefended GCN baseline (e.g., 84.81% vs 82.96% on Cora under GTA; 65.74% vs 63.12% on OGB-arXiv under GTA). Under the most challenging feature-based SPEAR attack, where structural defenses like Prune and OD retain ASR of 100% on Cora, LoSplit reduces ASR to 0.00% with CA of 84.44%. On the large-scale OGB-arXiv dataset, LoSplit achieves ASR of 0.00–0.20% with CA of 65.24–66.68% across all attacks, outperforming RIGBD (ASR up to 97.09% under SPEAR) by a wide margin.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the undefended GCN and RobustGCN baselines, LoSplit reduces ASR from 93–100% to ≤0.56% across all settings while matching or exceeding clean accuracy. Structural defenses Prune and OD are completely ineffective against the feature-based SPEAR attack (ASR remains 100% on Cora and CiteSeer), whereas LoSplit achieves 0.00% ASR; OD also fails under DPGBA (96.31% ASR on Cora) due to its reliance on distributional outliers. The image-domain training-time defense ABL, adapted to GCNs, suffers from under-detection (e.g., only 12.5% Recall on Cora under SPEAR) and uses a fixed isolation ratio with CE loss, yielding ASR of 30.26% on Cora-SPEAR versus LoSplit's 0.00%. RIGBD, the closest graph-specific competitor, achieves competitive ASR under structural attacks (e.g., 2.22% on Cora-DPGBA) but degrades severely under SPEAR (97.78% ASR on Cora, 97.09% on OGB-arXiv) due to its heuristic threshold and random edge dropping. In the recovery-strategy comparison (Appendix E, Table 6), LoSplit's Decoupling-Forgetting outperforms SCRUB (a machine unlearning framework) on both ASR and CA under all four attacks on PubMed, while being substantially faster (42–85% less runtime). GNNGuard and RobustGCN, designed for general adversarial robustness, provide only partial backdoor mitigation (e.g., GNNGuard retains 53.51% ASR under SPEAR on Cora) and are clearly outperformed by LoSplit.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the training-time backdoor defense paradigm from the image domain (ABL, HARVEY, PIPD) to graphs, where the message-passing mechanism introduces a previously unexplored 'class-wide loss drift' that invalidates the fixed isolation ratios and global thresholds used in vision. It departs from existing graph backdoor defenses (Prune, OD, RIGBD) that rely on structural anomaly detection, which are ineffective against feature-based attacks like SPEAR that preserve topology. The method combines Reverse Cross-Entropy loss (borrowed from noisy-label learning and HARVEY) with GMM-based adaptive clustering for target node identification, and introduces a Decoupling-Forgetting recovery strategy (random label reassignment + gradient ascent) that is graph-specific and distinct from prior robust-training or machine-unlearning approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LoSplit fills a clear gap as the first training-time defense for graph backdoor attacks, offering a unified framework that handles both structural and feature-based triggers without requiring knowledge of the malicious label or trigger patterns. However, the method's practical utility is limited by its sensitivity to three hyperparameters (T_S, η_S, γ) that require dataset- and attack-specific tuning, as acknowledged by the authors. An open question is whether the Early-Stage Dynamic Split can be made fully adaptive (e.g., via automatic epoch selection or learned thresholds) to eliminate this manual tuning burden, particularly for large-scale graphs where the loss overlap between target and clean nodes becomes severe at high poisoning rates.
