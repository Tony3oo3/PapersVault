## Problem

Existing distribution matching methods for dataset distillation operate in Euclidean space, treating all samples as independent and identically distributed points. This overlooks the hierarchical (tree-like) structure inherent in real datasets, where lower-level samples near the root better represent category prototypes while higher-level samples carry noisier, less representative information. The paper asks whether embedding the distillation process in hyperbolic space can naturally encode this hierarchy and improve synthetic data quality.

## Input / Output

The method takes an original dataset (e.g., 32×32 RGB CIFAR-10, 28×28 grayscale Fashion-MNIST) and a small synthetic dataset (IPC = 1–50 images per class), extracts features via a frozen three-module ConvNet encoder, and maps them into the Lorentz hyperbolic space. It outputs an updated synthetic dataset whose hyperbolic centroid aligns with that of the original, with the training signal being the Lorentzian geodesic distance between the two Riemannian centroids. HDD is designed as a drop-in loss module compatible with existing DM, IDM, and Dance frameworks, requiring only the addition of the hyperbolic distance term alongside the original matching objective.

## Method

HDD encodes features from both original and synthetic samples into the Lorentz model of hyperbolic space via the exponential map at the origin, then computes the Riemannian (Karcher) centroid of each set using a closed-form Euclidean-average approximation (Law et al.). The distillation loss is the Lorentzian hyperbolic distance between these two centroids, scaled by a gradient factor λ to compensate for the small distances near the origin. The key geometric insight is that negative curvature causes the centroid to be naturally biased toward lower-level (prototype) samples: the weight function w(r) = √|K|·d / sinh(√|K|·d) is strictly decreasing in hyperbolic radius, so peripheral samples contribute less to the centroid. This hierarchical weighting is derived formally via tangent-space approximation of the Fréchet mean condition, and a gradient analysis in Appendix D confirms that the pull of each sample on the centroid decays monotonically with distance. The method also enables hierarchical pruning by sorting samples by their Lorentz time component and retaining only the 20% closest to the root, which preserves performance while improving training stability.

## Results

IDM with HDD achieves the best distillation accuracy across all four main benchmarks: 78.5% on Fashion-MNIST, 67.8% on SVHN, 47.0% on CIFAR-10, and 25.3% on CIFAR-100 at IPC=1, outperforming the IDM baseline by 1.1–3.2 percentage points. At IPC=10, IDM with HDD reaches 61.3% on CIFAR-10 (+4.0% over IDM) and 45.4% on CIFAR-100 (+0.7% over IDM), while DM with HDD improves over DM by 2.3–3.1 points on SVHN and CIFAR-10. The method also shows consistent gains at IPC=50 (e.g., 69.7% on CIFAR-10, 48.9% on CIFAR-100) and extends to the hybrid Dance architecture, where Dance with HDD reaches 77.1% on CIFAR-10 (IPC=50) and 53.9% on CIFAR-100 (IPC=10).

## Baselines & Comparisons

IDM with HDD consistently outperforms the IDM baseline across all four datasets and all IPC settings, with the largest gains at IPC=1 (e.g., +3.2% on CIFAR-100, +2.5% on SVHN) and IPC=10 on CIFAR-10 (+4.0%). DM with HDD similarly beats DM on every dataset, with improvements of 2.3–3.1% on SVHN and CIFAR-10 at IPC=1. Against optimization-based methods, IDM with HDD surpasses DSA (52.1% → 61.3% on CIFAR-10 IPC=10) and DCC (54.4% → 61.3%), and also outperforms the decoupling method G-VBSM (46.5% → 61.3% on CIFAR-10 IPC=10). On CIFAR-100 IPC=1, IDM with HDD (25.3%) leads all baselines including G-VBSM (16.4%) and CAFE+DSA (14.0%). The method also extends to the hybrid Dance architecture, where Dance with HDD improves over Dance by 0.8% on CIFAR-10 (IPC=50) and 1.5% on CIFAR-100 (IPC=1), and gains 0.4–0.5% on ImageWoof at IPC=1 and IPC=10.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends distribution-matching-based dataset distillation (DM, IDM, Dance) by replacing the Euclidean/Hilbert-space distributional discrepancy metric with a geodesic distance between Riemannian centroids computed in the Lorentz hyperbolic model. It draws on the centroid approximation of Law et al. [25] and the exponential/logarithmic map machinery of hyperbolic geometry, but departs from prior distribution-matching work (which relies on MMD or per-sample MSE) by exploiting the negative curvature to induce a natural hierarchical weighting: samples near the hyperbolic origin (prototype-like) dominate the centroid, while peripheral samples contribute less. The method is explicitly designed as a plug-in compatible with existing DM frameworks rather than a standalone pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The core practical contribution is a lightweight, architecture-agnostic loss term that can be added to any distribution-matching DD method with ~30% runtime overhead and negligible memory cost, yielding consistent 1–4% accuracy gains across five benchmarks. The hierarchical pruning result (20% of original data suffices) suggests a new axis for data reduction beyond IPC selection. However, the improvements are modest relative to the conceptual novelty, the curvature ablation shows the method is insensitive to K (raising questions about how much the hyperbolic geometry is truly doing versus a reparameterization effect), and the paper leaves open whether richer hyperbolic distributional metrics (KL divergence, Wasserstein distance on the hyperboloid) could yield larger gains.
